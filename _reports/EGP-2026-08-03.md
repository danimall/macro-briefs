## Executive Summary

EastGroup's second-quarter print was unambiguously good on operations. FFO of $2.36 per share rose 6.8% year over year, property net operating income rose 10.6%, same-store cash NOI accelerated to +8.3%, and management raised FY2026 FFO guidance to $9.52–$9.66 (midpoint $9.59, +6.8%), lifted same-store cash NOI guidance to 6.3%–7.3% from 5.7%–6.7%, raised occupancy guidance, and raised development starts for the second time this year to $325M from $265M in April against $179M actually delivered in 2025. Consensus current-year EPS estimates have moved up 3.9% over thirty days. Nothing in the quarter suggests deterioration.

The balance sheet is the second pillar and it is genuinely exceptional: $1,615.0M of unsecured debt entirely fixed or swapped at a 3.43% weighted average, Debt/EBITDAre of 3.0x, debt-to-total-market-capitalization of 12.9%, interest and fixed-charge coverage of 15.1x, zero drawn on $675M of credit facilities, roughly $917.6M of total liquidity, and a Moody's upgrade to Baa1 in February 2026. The debt carries a fair value of $1,539.1M against $1,615.0M carrying, meaning below-market coupons are an economic asset, and the swap book expects to reclassify $7.3M out of OCI as a *reduction* to interest expense over the next twelve months.

The problem is what an investor pays for that. At $209.01 the stock is 21.8x guided 2026 FFO, carries a 2.97% dividend yield against a 4.1% median for its industrial-REIT comparables, and implies a portfolio cap rate of roughly 4.25%–4.45% — at or modestly inside the high-4% range management says private buyers are bidding for similar assets. There is no NAV discount to arbitrage. The engine generating 6–8% same-store NOI growth is visibly slowing: straight-line releasing spreads fell from 45.8% in 1H2025 to 35.2% in 1H2026, cash spreads are reportedly near 19%, and rent concessions of $11,307K in the first half already exceed the full-year 2025 total of $10,894K, with concessions per square foot up 44%. Independent industry data does not corroborate the tight-supply framing at the national level. We maintain **HOLD** with a 12-month base case of $220 (+5.3%, ~8% total return with the dividend), a bull case of $248 and a bear case of $168.

## Company Overview

EastGroup is a self-managed industrial REIT structured as an UPREIT through EastGroup Properties, L.P., headquartered in Ridgeland, Mississippi, and a constituent of the S&P MidCap 400. At 6/30/2026 the portfolio comprised 557 industrial properties across 12 states, approximately 65.8M square feet including development and value-add projects, with a stabilized operating portfolio of 62,523,000 square feet.

The differentiating feature is product type. Of the portfolio, 517 properties and 59.8M square feet are "business distribution" — shallow-bay multi-tenant space in the 20,000 to 100,000 square foot range — versus 19 bulk distribution properties totaling 5.1M square feet. Roughly 91% of square footage is shallow-bay, deliberately distinct from the big-box single-tenant fulfillment product that dominates the industrial REIT sector's largest players.

That product choice produces extreme tenant granularity. As of 2/10/2026 the company had approximately 1,700 leases with no single tenant above approximately 1.5% of annualized base rent, and no individual property at 10% or more of gross revenue or book value. Management stated on the Q2 call that top-ten tenants represent 6.6% of rents; that figure comes from a third-party call summary rather than a filing, but the filing-level 1.5% cap independently establishes the point. Geographically the concentration is Sunbelt: Dallas 10.9% and Houston 9.5% of annualized base rent at 12/31/2025, with emphasis on Texas, Florida, California, Arizona and North Carolina.

Roughly 50% of the portfolio by square footage was internally developed. Management states a preference for development over acquisition on risk-adjusted return grounds, and the arithmetic supports that preference: 7.6% projected stabilized development yields against an implied portfolio cap rate in the mid-4s is a 310–330 basis point value-creation spread.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and per-share reality.** Total revenue grew from $487.0M (2022) to $570.6M, $640.2M and $721.3M — 17.2%, 12.2% and 12.7% annual growth. Revenue per diluted share, however, grew only 10.4%, 4.0% and 4.3%, because diluted weighted-average shares compounded 7.32% annually from 42,712K to 52,814K. Roughly two-thirds of the reported 2024–2025 top-line growth was funded by equity issuance rather than asset productivity.

The material change is that this has been throttled. FY2026 guidance assumes 53,726K diluted shares, up only 1.7% from 2025. EastGroup raised $479.9M under its prior ATM through 12/5/2025; approximately $720.4M of the new $1.0B ATM remained available at 7/21/2026, implying roughly $280M used in seven months, most of it in unsettled forward contracts rather than settled shares. Guided 2026 FFO/share growth of 6.8% is therefore of substantially higher quality than 2025's 7.5% — nearly all organic. This is the single most underappreciated positive in the file, and we weight it.

**Same-store performance and the cash/GAAP crossover.** Same-property NOI excluding lease terminations grew +6.2% on a straight-line basis and +8.3% on a cash basis in Q2 2026, versus +6.6% and +6.4% in Q2 2025. The relationship inverted: cash now grows faster than straight-line, the reverse of last year. The mechanism is visible in the reconciliation — straight-line rent adjustments for same properties fell to $(1,274)K from $(3,391)K, and straight-line rent income *decreased* $1,264K in 1H2026 after *increasing* $5,777K in FY2025. The favorable reading is that cash rents are catching up to previously accrued amounts, reducing GAAP dependence on non-cash accruals. The less favorable reading is a maturing lease book signing fewer leases with steep embedded escalators. Straight-line rents receivable still rose to $107,672K from $100,949K, so the accrual is growing, just decelerating. Both readings fit the data.

**Occupancy — decomposition matters.** Total operating portfolio occupancy fell to 95.6% at 6/30/2026 from 96.0% a year earlier, and leased fell to 96.8% from 97.1%. But same-property average occupancy *rose* to 96.9% in Q2 from 96.3%, and to 97.1% in 1H2026 from 96.2%. The total-portfolio decline is arithmetic dilution: development projects transfer into the operating portfolio at the earlier of 90% occupancy or one year post-completion, so partially leased buildings enter the denominator. The 2025 transfers included weak lease-ups (SunCoast 9 at 32% leased; Eisenhauer Point 10-12 at 48%); the 1H2026 transfers were 1,231,000 square feet at 95% leased, with the four Q2 transfers at 100%. The stabilized base is tightening. The leased-to-occupied gap widened to 120bp from 50bp at year-end — roughly 750,000 square feet of signed-not-occupied space — but only 10bp versus 110bp a year earlier, so most of the increase is seasonal and should not be booked as a large 2027 tailwind.

**Cash flow and distribution coverage.** Operating cash flow was $480.7M in FY2025 and $301,940K in 1H2026, up 9.0% year over year. EastGroup does not report AFFO. A conservative proxy for FY2025 — FFO attributable to common of $474,253K less $75,653K of real estate improvements and $35,585K of capitalized leasing costs — yields $363,015K, or $6.87 per diluted share, against $313,183K of declared dividends for an 86.3% payout. That deduction includes first-generation and value-add repositioning spend that is not recurring maintenance, so treat $6.87 as a floor and 86% as a ceiling on the payout. Against reported FFO the 2026 annualized $6.20 dividend is 64.6% of the $9.59 guidance midpoint. The dividend was raised 10.7% ($1.40 to $1.55 quarterly) against 6.8% guided FFO growth, deliberately lifting the payout ratio from 62.4% to 64.6%. Q2 2026 marked the 186th consecutive quarterly distribution, with the dividend increased or maintained for 33 consecutive years and increased in each of the last 14.

**Two quantified headwinds.** First, near-term maturities carry unusually low coupons: $475.0M rolling through 2028 at a 2.73% weighted average against November 2025 issuance at 4.13% effectively fixed. Refinancing at 4.20% costs approximately $475M × 1.47% = $6.98M annually, or roughly $0.13 per share — about 1.4% cumulative FFO drag spread across three years. Real, not thesis-changing. Second, FY2025 capitalized interest was $21,730K, 40.4% of $53,843K gross interest, equal to roughly $0.41 per diluted share of FFO support; 1H2026 ran at 39.0%. With starts guided to $325M this shield is expanding near-term, but it is a direct function of development volume. A sustained development pullback converts into reported interest expense on top of the refinancing drag. The accounting is standard; the sensitivity is worth holding in mind.

## Growth Analysis

Guided FY2026 FFO/share growth of 6.8% decomposes into three sources of very different durability.

**Mark-to-market roll-up (largest, finite).** Straight-line releasing spreads were +40.1% in FY2025, +45.8% in 1H2025, and +35.2% in 1H2026 — roughly 1,000 basis points of compression year over year. Management reports cash spreads near 19%. The gap between the two methodologies is mechanical and important: straight-line averages rent across the full new lease term and therefore captures contractual escalators, while cash compares the first full rent month to the last month of the prior lease. An investor anchoring on 34% is capturing future escalators, not current mark-to-market. Spreads can stay positive for years purely because leases signed in 2019–2021 roll to today's rents even if market rents are flat, but that is amortization of a legacy stock, and it converges toward zero. Same-property average rental rate was $9.30/sf in 1H2026.

**Concession inflation (the unresolved question).** Rent concessions in 1H2026 totaled $11,307K across 77 leases and 3,283,000 square feet — $3.44/sf — versus FY2025's $10,894K across 156 leases and 4,555,000 square feet, or $2.39/sf. Concessions rose from 1.37% of income from real estate operations in 1H2025 to 2.95% in 1H2026. Neither spread methodology captures this: EastGroup's own definition states that where free rent exists, "the first positive full rent value is used." So reported spreads are structurally insulated from concession inflation. There are two consistent explanations. If concessions are concentrated in same-store renewals, effective rents are softening materially faster than the headline implies. If they are concentrated in development and first-generation space — excluded from both the spread calculation and the same-store pool — then this is a development-yield cost, consistent with 16 development/first-generation leases totaling 1,101,000 square feet signed between 4/1 and 7/21/2026 and with same-store cash NOI simultaneously accelerating to +8.3%. EastGroup does not disaggregate. This is the single most consequential open item in the file, and its irreducibility is a reason we do not raise the growth score despite the guidance raises.

**Development (most durable).** The pipeline at 6/30/2026 carried $486.8M of projected total cost with $175.1M still to invest, implying roughly $37.0M of stabilized PNOI at the 7.6% projected yield — a 6.5% uplift to annualized Q2 PNOI. Guided 2026 starts of $325M at 7.6% generate approximately $24.7M of stabilized PNOI; capitalized at a 4.5% exit cap that is $549M of value against $325M of cost, or roughly $224M / $4.17 per share of annual NAV creation. At a 5.5% exit cap the spread narrows to $124M / $2.31 per share. The sensitivity to exit cap rates is the entire argument, and exit caps move with long rates.

**Demand vector to be precise about.** Management identifies data center *suppliers* — component vendors, electrical distributors, staging and logistics providers — as roughly 40% of Q1 2026 and 20% of Q2 2026 development leasing. EastGroup is the landlord; these suppliers are tenants. This is conventional industrial leasing to a cyclically strong vertical, not data center exposure, and it should not be valued as such. It also carries a concentration question if AI capital expenditure decelerates. The figures come from a third-party call summary, not a filing.

Estimate revisions corroborate near-term momentum without signalling any change in trend: current-year EPS consensus at $5.60 (+3.9% over thirty days, +1.9% over ninety), next quarter +3.2% over thirty days. Next-year consensus of $5.55 sits *below* current-year, which is consistent with $0.56/share of 1H2026 real estate sale gains not recurring rather than with earnings deterioration — GAAP EPS is a poor lens here.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of 36.7x and the peer median of 29.2x are both distorted by REIT depreciation and, in EGP's case, by $30,074K of 1H2026 gains on sale. Use FFO, yield, and implied cap rate.

| Metric | EGP at $209.01 | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| P/FFO on 2026 guidance midpoint $9.59 | **21.8x** | vs ~18.7x a year ago at $167.78 on $8.96 guidance |
| P/FFO on FY2025 actual $8.98 | 23.3x | — |
| Dividend yield ($6.20) | **2.97%** | Peer median 4.1%; lowest in the comparable set |
| Implied cap rate (Q2 PNOI annualized) | 4.46% | Net of guided G&A: **4.25%** |
| Private-market bids cited by management | high-4% | EGP at or modestly inside |

Three observations follow.

First, the multiple has already re-rated. The proxy pay-versus-performance table shows EGP's total-return index at 148.98 at year-end 2025 versus 179.73 in 2021, while FFO/share rose 47.5% over the same span — substantial multiple compression across 2021–2025. That compression has now largely reversed: forward P/FFO expanded roughly 300 basis points over the past twelve months, from ~18.7x to ~21.8x. The re-rating is the return; the stock's ~24% year-over-year gain came predominantly from multiple, not FFO.

Second, there is no NAV discount. An implied cap rate of 4.25%–4.45% against high-4% private bids means the public market is paying at least as much as, and plausibly more than, private capital. This annualization is a sensitivity rather than a forecast: property tax payable swung from $14,707K at 12/31/2025 to $63,973K at 6/30/2026, so quarterly PNOI is an imperfect base. A normalized alternative — FY2025 PNOI of $528,345K grown at the guided ~7% cash rate plus development contribution — lands in the same mid-4% zone.

Third, the yield is the cleanest relative signal. Within the industrial comparables (recognizing CUBE is self-storage and the set is heterogeneous), EGP's 2.97% is the lowest against FR 3.2%, TRNO 3.1%, STAG 4.1% and REXR 5.0%. REXR sits 24% below its 52-week high with a 5.0% yield while EGP sits 7.8% below its high at 2.97% — the market is discriminating within the group and awarding EastGroup a premium for balance-sheet quality and shallow-bay product. That premium is defensible, not a mispricing.

Analyst consensus of $228.25 mean / $230.50 median implies +9.2% and, with the dividend, roughly a 12% total return. On a stock at 21.8x forward FFO with decelerating same-store growth, consensus targets here reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of upside. We do not treat them as a floor.

The macro overlay matters: the discount rate applied to a 4.25% implied cap rate portfolio is a dominant swing factor in the equity, and a widening in exit caps mathematically erodes both the NAV and the development spread. That rate risk is partially expressed in the 7.8% retracement from the July high, and it is why we score valuation at 5.0 rather than higher.

## Competitive Landscape

The bull argument rests on product-type divergence. National statistics are dominated by big-box, and the post-pandemic construction wave was overwhelmingly large-format; if shallow-bay 20,000–100,000 square foot space is structurally tighter, EastGroup's spreads and same-store growth can persist while national aggregates soften.

Independent data does not corroborate the tight-supply framing at the national level. Colliers' Q2 2026 U.S. industrial statistics (read via secondary reporting) show national vacancy at 7.3%, essentially flat year over year at an elevated level; construction underway up 7% in the quarter to 312M square feet, the largest pipeline since Q3 2024; average warehouse/distribution asking rents flat at $10.34/sf and down 1.6% year over year; and "several Sun Belt markets" reporting elevated vacancy after strong development cycles. Landlords are described as prioritizing occupancy over pricing. Management's framing on the Q2 call was materially more constructive — permitting friction "act[s] as a natural governor on overbuilding," with expected "continued upward pressure on rents."

Three reconciliations are plausible and not mutually exclusive: genuine shallow-bay tightness (credible but unverifiable — no size-cohort-decomposed vacancy series was obtainable); stock-versus-flow, under which legacy leases roll up regardless of market direction (this requires zero market rent growth and fully accommodates both datasets, but implies further spread compression); and geographic mix, with Colliers attributing rent declines mainly to coastal corrections against EGP's 5–6% Southern California exposure. The second explanation is sufficient on its own, and it is the least favorable for growth durability.

On trajectory — which matters more than level — the evidence is mildly positive. Same-property occupancy is rising, quarterly leasing was a record 3.9M square feet, development transfers improved from 32–48% leased in 2025 to 95–100% in 1H2026 at an unchanged 7.6% stabilized yield ex-redevelopment, and the pipeline's crude lease-up bucket improved from roughly 34% at year-end to roughly 52%. Capital recycling has also stepped up: after selling one property for $3.6M in all of 2025, EastGroup sold 444,000 square feet for $42,901K net in 1H2026, redeploying from Fresno (sold ~$91/sf) into Jacksonville (~$215/sf), Phoenix (~$193/sf) and a contracted five-building Austin asset 92% leased at ~$214/sf. Dispositions guidance of $75M against $4M in 2025 signals genuine pruning. That is market-quality upgrading, though it also means buying at prices that embed the same low cap rates being paid in the public market.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate-driven multiple compression (largest, partially priced).** The equity is a duration instrument at its current multiple and yield. A rising-long-rate environment is the primary path to the bear case. Partially expressed in the 7.8% retracement from $226.71.

**Spread convergence.** 1,000 basis points of annual straight-line spread compression, cash spreads near 19%, and national asking rents down 1.6% together argue 6–8% same-store growth is not steady state. Deceleration toward 3–4% is not priced at the current multiple.

**Concession opacity.** $11.3M in one half versus $10.9M for all of 2025, with $/sf up 44%, and a methodology that structurally excludes free rent from reported spreads. Unresolvable from disclosure.

**Speculative development.** The 10-K states plainly: "We also routinely develop properties with no pre-leasing." The pipeline was 21.7% leased at 7/21/2026 on 3,175,000 square feet, with $175.1M still to invest. Mitigants are real — a Board-approved land cap, flexibility on start timing, and a 2025 record of starts coming in at $179M against $215M guided.

**Non-earning land.** $379,821K of prospective development land, 6.9% of $5,522,866K total assets, generating no income with 10,992,000 square feet of potential building. Optionality with a carrying drag.

**Sunbelt supply concentration.** Dallas plus Houston exceed 20% of annualized base rent in precisely the region Colliers flags for elevated vacancy.

**Construction inputs.** Tariff-driven cost language appears through multiple risk factors, and management flagged switchgear and transformer lead times that "can delay project deliveries by several months."

**Dilution mechanics.** 1,040,457 shares under unsettled forward sale agreements at a $201.45 weighted average initial forward price, settling March–June 2027: 1.94% of shares outstanding, or 1.90% ownership dilution post-issuance. Separately and not part of current capitalization, open performance awards may range from zero to 167,472 shares (0.31%) contingent on multi-year FFO/share, cash same-store NOI, leverage, coverage and relative TSR outcomes, plus 86,156 unvested restricted shares. With $720M of ATM capacity remaining, issuance can reaccelerate if acquisitions exceed the raised $215M guidance.

**What is absent.** No material litigation. No impairments in FY2025, 1H2025 or 1H2026. No material risk factor changes in the Q2 10-Q. Tenant concentration is the lowest in the sector. There is no identifiable binary event risk, which is why risk scores 6.0 rather than lower, despite meaningful cyclical and rate exposure.

**Governance and management.** Capital allocation is credible: leverage is an explicit compensation metric and the outcome is 3.0x Debt/EBITDAre; 2025 starts came in below guidance; equity issuance has been throttled from 7.3% to 1.7% annual share growth. Against that, CEO total compensation rose 32.5% to $9,437,394 in 2025 against 7.5% FFO/share growth; the dividend was raised 10.7% against 6.8% guided FFO growth; and across the 25 most recent Form 4 filings we identified no open-market purchases by any executive or director, with one small director sale (504 shares at $195.77 on 6/4/2026). Grants and tax withholding dominate the filings, so absence of buying is weak evidence rather than a bearish signal, but it provides no offsetting alignment support. The December 2025 promotion of four executives, including Reid Dunbar to President (corroborated by the Q2 10-Q signature block; the underlying 8-K text was not retrievable), reads as succession bench-building under a CEO who remains in place. G&A rose 36% year over year to $7,207K, consistent with either succession layering or span-of-control growth. Charter provisions are shareholder-friendly relative to typical Maryland REITs — opt-outs from both the Control Share Acquisition and Business Combination Acts, with bylaws requiring stockholder approval to repeal the former. Management quality scores 6.0: sound capital stewardship, no insider-buying confirmation, and compensation growth well ahead of per-share results.

The absence of stress signals in options is consistent with our view that the risk here is gradual multiple compression, not a discrete event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($248, +18.7%).** Shallow-bay tightness proves genuine and distinct from national big-box statistics; cash spreads stabilize near 19–20% while concession inflation proves confined to development lease-up rather than same-store renewals; the $325M of 2026 starts and the $486.8M pipeline deliver at 7.6% yields, adding roughly $4/share of annual NAV creation; long rates retreat, exit caps hold in the mid-4s, and 2027 FFO of roughly $10.10–$10.20 is capitalized at 24.5x. Balance sheet capacity — 3.0x leverage against a sector that generally runs higher — lets EastGroup accelerate acquisitions if private pricing cracks.

**Bear case ($168, −19.6%).** Colliers is right and the shallow-bay divergence is thinner than management asserts. Construction underway at 312M square feet delivers into Sunbelt markets already showing elevated vacancy; asking rents stay negative; cash spreads converge toward 10% by 2027 and concession per square foot keeps climbing, revealing that effective rents were softening while face rents held. Same-store growth decelerates to 3–4%. Simultaneously long rates hold high, exit caps widen to 5.25%+, the development spread compresses toward $2.30/share, and the forward multiple reverts to 17x on roughly $9.90 of 2027 FFO. The 52-week low of $159.37 becomes the reference. The low analyst target is $185, so the bear case sits below the published range — appropriate, because sell-side targets on a 21.8x REIT reflect the same rate assumptions embedded in the price.

**Base case ($220, +5.3%; ~8% total return with the dividend).** FFO/share growth decelerates modestly to roughly 5.5% in 2027 as spread compression and the $0.13/share refinancing drag offset development contribution and the near-elimination of share issuance, producing approximately $10.12. The multiple holds near 21.8x because balance-sheet quality and a 33-year distribution record command a premium, but does not expand because the implied cap rate is already inside private-market bids. Return equals dividend plus per-share FFO growth, minus a small multiple give-back.

**Trajectory.** This is the fourth report and the third consecutive HOLD, following scores of 6.8 → 7.0 → 6.7 → 6.4. Prior declines were located in growth prospects (7.0 → 6.0) and competitive position (8.0 → 7.0). The current data stabilizes rather than extends that decline: the Q2 beat-and-raise, the acceleration in same-store cash NOI to +8.3%, the improvement in development transfer lease-up from 32–48% to 95–100%, and positive estimate revisions are all confirming. The ~5.6% decline from $221.34 on 7/21 to $209.01 is a price move on improving fundamentals, which improves risk/reward at the margin. It is not enough to change the call, because the binding constraint is a 21.8x multiple and a 2.97% yield against a 4.1% peer median in a rising-long-rate environment, not fundamental deterioration. Financial health remains the strongest dimension at 8.0; valuation at 5.0 is what caps the score.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026), which should show whether concessions per square foot decline from the $3.72 Q2 level and whether cash releasing spreads hold near 19%. Secondary checkpoints: the long-end response to macro data, and any Q4 guidance reset for 2027.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price at or below $185** — approximately 18.3x our $10.12 2027 FFO estimate, a 3.35% dividend yield, and an implied cap rate near 4.8% that would sit at the low end of cited private bids. At that level the roughly $4/share of annual development NAV creation is compensated rather than pre-paid.
2. **Concession normalization with spread stabilization** — full-year 2026 concessions per square foot at or below $3.00 while cash releasing spreads hold at 19% or better, which would resolve the central open question in favor of the development-cost explanation and support same-store durability at 6%+ into 2027.
3. **Exit-cap confirmation** — a disposition or acquisition print at a sub-4.5% cap on stabilized shallow-bay assets, validating the NAV and widening the measured development spread.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Same-store cash NOI guidance cut below 5%** for 2026 or initial 2027 guidance below 4%, which would mark the mark-to-market runway as effectively closed.
2. **Cash releasing spreads below 12%** on a reported basis alongside another year-over-year increase in concessions per square foot — the combination that would indicate face rents were being held with escalating free rent.
3. **Development starts guidance cut by more than 30%** from $325M, which would simultaneously remove the value-creation engine and reverse roughly $0.40/share of capitalized-interest FFO support into reported interest expense on top of the $0.13/share refinancing drag.
4. **Equity issuance reacceleration above 4% annual share growth**, which would return the company to the 2024–2025 pattern where headline growth outran per-share growth by roughly two-thirds.

## Conclusion

**HOLD at $209.01.** EastGroup is a high-quality operator: the shallow-bay niche is defensible, tenant granularity is the best in the sector, the balance sheet at 3.0x Debt/EBITDAre with 15.1x coverage and all-fixed debt at 3.43% is a genuine competitive asset in a higher-for-longer environment, and the shift from 7.3% to 1.7% annual share growth makes 2026's guided 6.8% FFO/share growth materially higher quality than 2025's. Management raised guidance twice on development starts and lifted FFO, same-store and occupancy guidance in July.

What is missing is price. At 21.8x guided FFO, a 2.97% yield against a 4.1% peer median, and an implied cap rate of 4.25%–4.45% at or inside cited private-market bids, the stock pre-pays for the development spread it is expected to create. The growth engine is visibly amortizing: 1,000 basis points of annual straight-line spread compression, cash spreads near 19%, and concessions per square foot up 44% with disclosure insufficient to determine whether that reflects same-store rent softening or development lease-up cost. Independent industry data shows construction underway at its largest since Q3 2024 and national asking rents down 1.6%, which places the burden of proof on the shallow-bay divergence argument that has not been independently verified.
