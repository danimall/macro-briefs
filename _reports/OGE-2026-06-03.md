**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

OGE Energy is a pure-play regulated electric utility holding company operating through Oklahoma Gas and Electric (OG&E), the largest electric utility in Oklahoma, serving roughly 913,000–915,000 customers across a 30,000-square-mile territory. Since divesting its midstream Energy Transfer stake in 2022, the entire investment case rests on the regulated rate base, which has been reshaped by a structural load-growth story. Commercial MWh sales rose roughly 46% over two years (8.5M in 2023 to 12.4M in 2025), and the company has captured ~24–25% cumulative system growth over five years. The defining catalyst is the April 30, 2026 announcement of Electric Service Agreements to power three Google data centers in Muskogee and Stillwater, structured so Google funds 100% of grid-connection costs plus minimum take-or-pay charges, exit provisions, and credit support. This contract design spreads fixed costs over a larger base and reduces stranded-asset risk that has become a political flashpoint elsewhere.

The fundamentals are improving and the trajectory is intact: 2025 net income of $470.7M (+6.6% YoY), diluted EPS of $2.32 (+5.9%), and 2026 guidance of $2.38–$2.48 reaffirmed at the Q1 print despite a weak Q1 ($0.24 vs $0.31 prior on mild weather). Favorable Oklahoma legislation (SB 998 CWIP recovery and 20-year deferral mechanism) and Arkansas Act 373 materially reduce regulatory lag, and Moody's upgraded its outlook back to stable in April 2026 after the November 2025 equity raise. A director-level $500K open-market purchase in February 2026 adds a genuine insider conviction signal.

The constraint is price. At ~$46.61, OGE trades around 20.8x trailing and forward EPS against a multi-utility peer median of 18.8x, while offering a 3.6% dividend yield that sits below the peer median of 3.8%. The growth premium is partly earned, but the stock is not cheap, the $7.285B 2026–2030 capex plan requires continued equity issuance (dilution), and S&P placed OG&E on negative outlook in November 2025 citing elevated wildfire exposure. With analyst consensus mean near $50 and the stock close to it, the near-term risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. This is a HOLD with a constructive growth lean.

## Company Overview

OG&E is vertically integrated (generation, transmission, distribution) and operates within the Southwest Power Pool Integrated Marketplace. The 2025 revenue mix is approximately 92% Oklahoma and 8% Arkansas, regulated by the Oklahoma Corporation Commission (88% of revenue), the Arkansas Public Service Commission (7%), and FERC (5%). Generation capability is ~6,921 MW, weighted 65.9% natural gas, 22.5% coal, 4.6% dual-fuel, 6.5% wind, and 0.5% solar. The 2025 generation mix shifted toward coal (34% vs 18% in 2024) as natural gas prices rose, which is an economic dispatch decision rather than a strategic one.

The central competitive asset is rate level. Oklahoma residential rates run roughly 19% below the regional average and ~34% below the national average. Management has built its economic-development and load-growth strategy around this advantage, positioning Oklahoma as a low-cost destination for hyperscale data center demand. The company targets 5–7% consolidated EPS growth through 2030 (top half of range in 2027–2028) with a 60–70% dividend payout ratio.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from $2.61B (2023) to $2.92B (2024) to $3.19B (2025), +9.4% YoY in 2025. A meaningful portion of that growth is pass-through fuel and purchased-power recovery, which is revenue-neutral to earnings, so the cleaner signal is volume: system sales grew +7.7% in 2024 and +5.0% in 2025. Net income progressed $416.8M → $441.5M → $470.7M. Operating margin is ~25%, net margin ~14.8%, ROE ~9.5%, ROA ~3.3%, a standard regulated-utility return profile.

The balance sheet carries the leverage typical for the model: debt/equity ~1.89, long-term debt $5.37B, short-term debt $292M, stockholders' equity $4.98B. The Altman Z-Score of 1.19 ("distress zone") and current ratio of 0.78 look alarming in isolation but are structural artifacts of capital-intensive regulated utilities with stable rate-based cash flows and continuous capital-markets access. Investment-grade ratings (OG&E A-/A3/A; parent BBB/BBB+/Baa1) and the April 2026 Moody's upgrade to stable confirm this is not a genuine distress signal.

Cash flow is where the growth model shows its cost. Operating cash flow was strong at $1.14B in 2025 (+40% YoY on fuel recoveries), but free cash flow was only $82.7M because capex was $1.05B. The 2026–2030 capital plan is $7.285B ($1.385B in 2026 rising toward ~$1.5B/year), funded by operating cash, debt, and equity. The November 2025 ~$397M equity offering (4.6M shares plus 4.6M via forward sale agreements settleable through May 2027, with ~$193M of dry powder remaining) and the April 2025 $350M of 5.80% 30-year notes funded the ramp. Per the Q1 2026 call, the 2026 financing plan is complete, which de-risks near-term funding but does not remove the multi-year dilution path.

The earnings surprise record over the last four quarters is mixed: 1 beat, 1 in-line, 2 misses, including a 5.5% Q1 2026 miss ($0.24 vs $0.25). Utility quarterly EPS is heavily weather-driven and concentrated in Q3 cooling season, so a soft Q1 on mild weather is a swing factor rather than a thesis break, and full-year guidance was reaffirmed.

## Growth Analysis

The load-growth shift is visible in reported numbers, not just guidance. Commercial MWh sales rising ~46% over two years and ~24–25% cumulative system growth over five years are the strongest data points. The 2026 guidance assumes 4–6% retail load growth.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not the +30% inflection that signals a step-change re-rating. Current-quarter consensus rose +12.7% over 90 days (to $0.56), but current-year ($2.43) and next-year ($2.61) estimates have been essentially flat (+0.0% to +0.5%). This is steady, low-double-digit revision momentum consistent with a regulated utility executing on plan, not an accelerating earnings breakout.

The Google contract is the headline catalyst, with "Customer X" representing roughly ~1 GW by 2031 against a ~7 GW system. Google funds 100% of connection costs, pays minimum charges regardless of usage and its fair share of generation, and makes 600 MW of solar capacity available via Capacity Purchase Agreements. Oklahoma's Data Center Consumer Ratepayer Protection Act (signed ~May 15, 2026) and HB 2992 legislatively reinforce this posture; OG&E plans to file a stand-alone large-load tariff with the OCC by July 1, 2026. The agreements still require OCC approval (filed May 2026), which is a near-term gating item.

The most important nuance for forward upside is that the ~1 GW Google load is already within the 2026 Integrated Resource Plan, so it is not yet incremental to the published 5–7% framework. The genuine upside levers, the pending generation RFP, the Seminole-to-Shreveport SPP transmission line, and the 300 MW Frontier battery storage project (pre-approval expected August 2026), are not yet in the capital plan. Management has signaled these will extend the EPS growth runway beyond the current through-2030 framework once approved, with communication expected in 2H 2026/early 2027. This "layer in approved projects, then re-rate the plan" playbook is the path to upside, but it is contingent on regulatory approvals rather than confirmed.

The estimate-revision momentum here is the steady utility kind, not the cheap-relative-to-rising-estimates pattern that would justify treating consensus targets as stale floors. The absence of a >30% revision velocity, combined with a forward P/E that is not depressed, means the standard cycle-trough valuation override does not apply. The valuation supports a HOLD: not stretched enough to AVOID, not cheap enough to issue a high-conviction long at a premium-to-peer multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

OG&E is a regulated monopoly in its franchised territory with limited retail competition (some pressure from co-ops and municipals; Oklahoma law forbids exclusive franchises). The competitive trajectory is improving because the industry's dominant dynamic, the AI/data-center load supercycle, converts a historically low-growth utility into a growth platform, and OG&E's nation-leading low rates are the structural magnet drawing hyperscalers to Oklahoma. Management explicitly contrasts OG&E with markets where large-load demand has driven price escalation, stating they have not seen comparable escalation and have the regulatory framework to prevent it, positioning the company as a disciplined, ratepayer-protective version of the theme.

The relative disadvantage is the coal-heavy fleet under any future GHG regime, though the current deregulatory federal posture (EPA rule reconsiderations, proposed Endangerment Finding rescission in February 2026) reduces near-term compliance pressure. The favorable Oklahoma SB 998 (CWIP recovery for new gas generation plus a 20-year regulatory-asset deferral mechanism the company began using in September 2025) and Arkansas Act 373 reduce regulatory lag, a structural positive that strengthens the competitive position relative to peers in less constructive jurisdictions.

## Risk Assessment

**Wildfire liability** is the newest and most company-specific financial risk. S&P revised OG&E's outlook from stable to negative on November 18, 2025, explicitly citing elevated wildfire exposure. Oklahoma's tornado, windstorm, and drought conditions create catastrophic tail risk, and the PG&E and Hawaiian Electric precedents show how a single event under inverse-condemnation or negligence frameworks can overwhelm insurance. This is the risk the market is least focused on and the one to monitor for dedicated mitigation capex and any state liability-cap legislation.

**Regulatory cost recovery** is the perennial constraint. A rate review is to be filed in 2026 with new rates targeted for 2027, the single most important regulatory event ahead. The Google ESAs require OCC approval (filed May 2026), and the large-load tariff filing is due by July 1, 2026.

**Capex and financing risk**: the $7.3B plan plus incremental generation/transmission for load growth requires sustained equity and debt issuance, with continued dilution. Moody's lowered the parent FFO/debt downgrade threshold to 17%, leaving credit metrics tight despite the April 2026 upgrade to stable.

**Environmental compliance**: OG&E earlier estimated $2.4–$2.8B in potential Good Neighbor FIP NOx compliance costs for coal units. Multiple EPA rules are in flux under litigation and the deregulatory administration, creating uncertainty in both directions.

**Holding-company structure**: the parent depends entirely on OG&E dividends, which regulators could restrict.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 26.6% against 15.9% historical volatility (rich, +10.7pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (26.6% near-term vs 17.2% at six months), signaling an imminent catalyst window. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.1% (above-average put demand), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.1%. The catalyst window aligns with the OCC approval process for the Google contracts, the July 1 large-load tariff filing, and the broader rate-case timeline. The elevated put demand is a modest caution flag for a long position, though it is consistent with event hedging around a known regulatory cluster rather than a directional bearish bet (put/call OI ratio is only 0.10). IV has compressed since early May (34.2% to 30.5%), suggesting some uncertainty is resolving.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$59):** OCC approval of the Google contracts is clean, the July 1 large-load tariff establishes a replicable framework, and management layers the generation RFP, Seminole-to-Shreveport transmission, and Frontier battery into the capital plan in 2H 2026/early 2027, extending EPS growth above the 5–7% framework toward the top of the range. The market re-rates OGE toward the higher end of the data-center-utility cohort (MDU 22.9x, NWE 25.9x) on a durable growth runway, supporting ~$59 on ~22x a rising forward number.

**Bear case (~$40, roughly -14% from current):** a wildfire event or worsening wildfire-liability framing pressures the credit narrative further, the 2026 rate case delivers less timely recovery than hoped, equity issuance to fund the capex ramp dilutes EPS, and the premium-to-peer multiple compresses to the peer median (~18.8x) on a flat-to-modestly-growing earnings base. Combined with rate-path uncertainty pressuring utility yields, this maps to the low-$40s, near the $44 analyst floor with downside extension.

**Base case (~$50):** the company executes on guidance, the Google contracts clear regulatory approval without material concessions, and the growth-plan extension is communicated but not yet fully in numbers. The stock holds its modest premium and tracks toward consensus mean of ~$50, a ~7% gain plus the 3.6% dividend for a low-double-digit total return over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment points are the OCC ruling on the Google ESAs (filed May 2026), the July 1, 2026 large-load tariff filing, the 2026 rate-case filing, and the Q3 2026 earnings print (the seasonally important quarter), followed by the expected 2H 2026/early 2027 capital-plan update.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- OCC approval of the Google contracts plus a formal extension of the EPS growth framework above 7% with the RFP/transmission/battery projects added to the capital plan, supported by accelerating estimate revisions (+10%+ on next-year EPS).
- A pullback toward the low-$40s that compresses the forward multiple to or below the peer median while the load-growth fundamentals remain intact, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material wildfire event or a credit downgrade (rather than outlook change) reflecting realized wildfire liability.
- A 2026 rate case that delivers an inadequate authorized ROE or denies timely cost recovery, or OCC rejection/material adverse modification of the Google ESAs.
- Deterioration in FFO/debt toward the 17% Moody's threshold alongside accelerated equity issuance that dilutes EPS below the guided range.

## Conclusion

OGE has transitioned from a low-growth regulated utility into a credible data-center load-growth story, and the evidence is in the numbers rather than only in guidance. The Google contract structure is best-in-class, the Oklahoma legislative framework is unusually constructive, the Moody's upgrade to stable removes a near-term overhang, and a director's $500K open-market purchase signals insider conviction. The trajectory has improved, even as scores have drifted down modestly over recent reviews on financing and wildfire considerations.

The constraint is valuation. At ~20.8x earnings with a below-peer-median yield, the stock already prices in a meaningful share of the visible upside, the real growth-plan extension is still pending regulatory approvals not yet in estimates, and the $7.3B capex ramp implies continued dilution. With the stock near consensus mean and options pricing a near-term catalyst window, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate OGE a HOLD with a constructive growth lean and would upgrade on either confirmed growth-plan extension with accelerating revisions or a pullback to the low-$40s.
