**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Innovex International is a recently-merged oilfield-equipment company built around a "small ticket, big impact" model: engineered consumable products that represent 2-3% of total well cost but are mission-critical to well performance. The September 2024 reverse merger that folded legacy Dril-Quip's subsea franchise into Legacy Innovex created an entity with a net-cash balance sheet, expanding margins (record 21% adjusted EBITDA in Q1 2026), and a capital-light operating profile that is differentiated within a capital-intensive, cyclical sector. The fundamentals are stronger than the GAAP headlines suggest: 2024 net income was inflated by an ~$85.8M bargain-purchase gain, and the Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $(0.24) was entirely driven by a $48.8M legal accrual. Underlying adjusted EBITDA grew from ~$139M (2024) to ~$188M (2025).

The stock has lagged its peer set badly (indexed TSR 73.84 vs 184.92 for the peer group), and the gap is largely explained by two overhangs rather than operational deterioration. First, the Impulse patent litigation: a jury found acquired subsidiary Downhole Well Solutions willfully infringed, with a $47.6M award and live treble-damages and injunction tail risk pending post-trial motions and appeal. Second, Amberjack Capital, the legacy PE sponsor, is methodically exiting a once-42% stake (6.6M+ shares sold in a February 2026 secondary, continued activity flagged in a June 2026 8-K, ~16.9M shares still held). Both are real, neither is operational, and both could clear over the next 12-18 months. Citi initiated at Buy with a $35 target in May 2026.

We rate INVX a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The balance sheet quality and margin trajectory are clear positives that warrant a higher financial-health score; the litigation tail and the absence of a dated near-term catalyst keep us from a BUY. The score has declined modestly over recent reports (6.4 in April to 5.7 in June), driven by valuation and growth-prospect resets, and the most recent data — a decline since the late-May high on no fundamental deterioration plus continued sponsor selling — leaves the risk/reward more balanced than asymmetric. We would upgrade on resolution of the Impulse case at or below the accrual and a clearing of the Amberjack stake.

## Company Overview

Innovex designs, manufactures, sells, and rents engineered consumable products across the full well lifecycle (construction, completion, production, and intervention) for both onshore and offshore wells globally. It operates as a single reportable segment from its Humble/Houston, Texas base. The strategic core is the "Big Impact, Small Ticket" positioning: because its products are a small fraction of total well cost but critical to reliability, purchasing decisions favor performance over price, which partly insulates the company from commodity-style price competition.

On September 6, 2024, legacy Dril-Quip (the historical public company, ticker DRQ) completed a merger with Innovex Downhole Solutions, with Legacy Innovex identified as the accounting acquirer. The combined company was renamed Innovex International and re-tickered to INVX. All pre-merger financials reflect only Legacy Innovex, with Dril-Quip's subsea/offshore business consolidated from September 2024 forward. This explains the apparent revenue step-ups: ~$377M (2023) to ~$661M (2024) to ~$978M (2025) are driven by the merger and bolt-on acquisitions, not pure organic growth.

Geographically, North America (US/Canada onshore) was ~57% of Q1 2026 revenue and International & Offshore ~43%. Management's stated strategy is to grow the international and offshore mix (longer-cycle, less cyclical) with Saudi Arabia and the broader Middle East as a key growth vector. That strategy is currently running in reverse on a near-term basis, with NAM doing the heavy lifting while international declined.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet is the standout feature and a genuine rarity in oilfield services. As of Q1 2026, Innovex held $200.7M of cash, no bank debt (only $24.2M of finance leases), and $155M of undrawn revolver availability. The Altman Z-Score sits around 5.85, well into the safe zone, with a current ratio near 5x and total equity of ~$1.03B. This is a net-cash position in a sector where peers typically carry meaningful leverage through cyclical troughs.

Profitability requires careful reading because GAAP net income is misleading in both directions. The 2024 net income of ~$140M was inflated by the one-time bargain-purchase gain plus a ~$39.8M gain on the Eldridge facility sale. The 2025 net income of ~$83M is a cleaner figure and reflects genuinely improved operations.

Q1 2026 shows the same pattern. Revenue was $239.0M, down 1% YoY and 13% sequentially. The GAAP net loss of $(16.7)M was driven entirely by the Impulse legal accrual; the business was operationally profitable. Adjusted EBITDA reached a record $49.3M at a 21% margin, up from 19% in both Q4 2025 and Q1 2025, beating guidance. The margin gain split roughly evenly between favorable product mix and manufacturing efficiency from the Eldridge facility exit. Free cash flow was $14.0M in the seasonally weakest quarter, with management guiding to 50-60%+ EBITDA conversion through-cycle.

The earnings-surprise record reflects accounting noise and should be read with caution: per the Phase 1 surprise data, Q1 2026 posted a reported EPS of $0.55 against a $0.27 estimate (a 101.5% "beat") alongside a -62.5% "miss" in Q3 2025, producing 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters. [Note: the vendor surprise table shows a positive $0.55 Q1 2026 EPS actual, which conflicts with the brief's GAAP net loss of $(0.24); the discrepancy likely reflects an adjusted-EPS basis in the vendor feed.] This volatility is a reason to anchor on adjusted EBITDA and revenue rather than GAAP EPS. Capital allocation is disciplined: a $100M buyback authorized in February 2025 has ~$76.6M remaining, with $14.1M repurchased in Q1 2026 at $24.59, partly absorbing Amberjack's secondary. Capex intensity is low at ~2.4% of revenue, validating the capital-light claim. ROCE of 12% is depressed by the net-cash balance sheet, with management targeting high-teens.

The financial-health profile (net cash, ample liquidity, no refinancing cliff, disciplined capex) supports a strong score and functions as a reliable downside filter against the sector's cyclicality.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth picture is muted, which separates Innovex from a true inflection story. Rystad projects only ~2%/year upstream spending growth through 2028, with global wells drilled flat-to-modestly-down (~32k to ~31k to ~32k). In that backdrop, share gains and M&A — not market growth — are the primary levers.

Estimate revisions are mixed. Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates have ticked up (+3.6% and +5.5% over 30 days), and next-year estimates have risen meaningfully (+10.0% over 90 days to $1.91). But current-year estimates were cut 11.3% over 30 days (to $1.40), reflecting the Q1 GAAP accrual and softer Q2 mix. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a systematic underestimate of a fundamental turn; it is a business with improving margins and a low-growth top line working through litigation and logistics noise.

The near-term operational concern is that geography is moving opposite to the stated strategy. International & Offshore fell 24% sequentially (15% YoY) in Q1 2026, partly on Subsea delivery lumpiness (~$15M pulled into Q4 2025) but also on conflict-related disruption in Saudi Arabia and the offshore Middle East. Q2 2026 guidance ($235-245M revenue, $43-48M adjusted EBITDA) bakes in higher air-freight costs as the company substitutes air for sea freight, and the adjusted EBITDA midpoint steps down from Q1's record. Management expects "significant Subsea momentum in H2 2026," a claim that requires verification in the next two quarters before it can anchor a growth thesis.

The more durable growth elements are margin expansion and M&A cadence. The Dril-Quip-legacy subsea business, historically a low-margin drag, now posts >20% EBITDA margins under Innovex's cost discipline, a credible hidden margin story if sustained. The serial bolt-on strategy (DWS, SCF, Citadel, DIS) continues, with the April 2026 DIS acquisition (~$16M at ~4x TTM EBITDA) fitting the capital-light model and adding US offshore products. Management notes a buyer-favorable private M&A market. Commercial wins include two Asia project awards each >$20M and a first Southeast Asia subsea wellhead order under the OneSubsea alliance. The growth case rests on margin improvement and accretive M&A rather than secular demand, which justifies a middling growth score.

## Valuation Assessment

Innovex trades at a forward P/E of ~34x on trailing/forward $1.20 EPS, but the trailing GAAP EPS is distorted by the litigation accrual, so the multiple needs adjustment. On the next-year consensus EPS of $1.91, the forward multiple compresses to ~13.4x, a far more reasonable figure for a net-cash, margin-expanding business. On the more conservative current-year $1.40 estimate, it is ~18x.

Peer comparison is unusually noisy because the small-cap oilfield-services group is heterogeneous and several peers carry distorted multiples. The peer-median P/E of 76.6 (RES at 76.6, HLX at 100.5, AESI negative) is not a usable benchmark; these reflect trough or depressed earnings rather than a coherent sector multiple. Against the cleaner comps (CLB at 21.5x, VTOL at 11.2x), Innovex's ~18x current-year / ~13.4x next-year sits in a reasonable-to-cheap range, and the company carries a materially better balance sheet than any of them. The discount is largely technical: the Amberjack overhang and the Impulse litigation, not operational weakness.

Citi's $35 target (~38% upside from the $25.41 reference price) is the clearest external bull marker; the consensus mean target of $32.8 (median $34.0) sits well above the current price, with a low target of $26.0 near spot. We treat these targets with appropriate caution given the analyst sentiment trend is characterized as degrading and the coverage set is thin (four analysts). The cheapness is real but conditioned on overhangs clearing and Subsea/international recovering. The lessons of this market regime caution against rewarding apparent cheapness without an independent catalyst, and here the catalysts (Impulse resolution, Amberjack exit completion) are unscheduled binaries. We score valuation as moderately positive: the discount is defensible and the downside is cushioned by net cash, but there is no dated near-term unlock to convert the discount into a return.

## Competitive Landscape

Innovex competes against larger integrated players (SLB, Halliburton, Baker Hughes, Weatherford) by occupying niche, high-margin consumable-product segments where it can out-innovate and respond faster than scale incumbents. The "small ticket, big impact" niche partially insulates it from price-based competition because customers prioritize reliability over cost on products that are a small share of well spend but critical to performance.

The OneSubsea alliance (the SLB/Subsea7/Aker integrated-subsea grouping) is a channel through which Innovex's Dril-Quip-legacy subsea wellheads participate in integrated offshore projects. Innovex supplies equipment into alliance-led projects; it does not own the alliance.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. In NAM, the company is gaining share (aided by Citadel) and the subsea margin recovery suggests it is extracting value that prior Dril-Quip management could not. Against that, International & Offshore weakness — even if partly timing — shows the international-growth thesis is not yet executing, and the muted industry backdrop caps the addressable opportunity. The moat is real but narrow, and the recent score decline (6.5 to 6.0 over prior reports) reflects that the competitive story is steady rather than improving. We hold competitive position at a modestly above-neutral level.

## Risk Assessment

**Impulse patent litigation (dominant near-term risk).** A 2023 jury found subsidiary Downhole Well Solutions willfully infringed Impulse Downhole Solutions' patents on directional-drilling friction-reduction tools, awarding $47.6M. Innovex recorded a $48.8M accrual in Q1 2026. No judgment has been entered. The willful-infringement finding raises the possibility of enhanced (treble) damages and a potential injunction on the relevant product line. Management strongly disagrees and plans post-trial motions and a Federal Circuit appeal. The accrual roughly matches the award, but management explicitly warns the ultimate loss may differ materially. This is M&A-inherited tail risk (DWS was only fully acquired in November 2024) and it is the single most material idiosyncratic exposure, with an asymmetric (treble/injunction) downside skew.

**Amberjack PE overhang.** The February 2026 secondary priced at $25.75, and a June 15, 2026 8-K indicates continued equity activity. This is a structural technical headwind that likely explains a meaningful portion of the TSR underperformance versus peers. The company is partially absorbing it via buybacks. As the stake winds down, the overhang could clear and de-suppress the multiple, but the timing is uncertain, and continued selling pressures the stock in the interim (the decline since the late-May high coincides with elevated volume and the June 8-K).

**International concentration and Middle East disruption.** Saudi Aramco and the broader Middle East are key growth customers; offshore/project revenue is inherently lumpy, and the Q1 2026 international decline plus higher Q2 air-freight costs show the exposure is live. The brief attributes the international softness to conflict-related disruption in Saudi Arabia / the offshore Middle East.

**M&A integration risk.** Growth depends on a steady acquisition cadence, each carrying integration, purchase-accounting, and inherited-liability risk, with Impulse being the cautionary example. Goodwill/intangibles total ~$212M.

**Commodity cyclicality.** A low-growth upstream backdrop makes the company reliant on share gains and M&A rather than market tailwinds.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 65.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 41.5% — rich by ~24 points, with an IV percentile of 77%. The term structure is in backwardation (65.3% near-term vs 41.7% long-term), indicating the market expects an imminent catalyst or stress event. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±13.2%. ATM IV jumped 10.5 points (54.9% to 65.3%) over the past two weeks while the price fell 10.2%, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.12 to 0.32. The put/call skew remains balanced (2.8%), so this is rising two-sided uncertainty rather than a one-directional bearish bet, but the combination of rising IV and falling price is a caution flag that aligns with the unresolved litigation and sponsor-selling overhangs. The rich near-term IV makes this an unattractive moment to pay up for short-dated optionality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($35-40).** The Impulse case resolves at or below the accrued amount with no injunction, removing the most material tail risk. Amberjack completes its exit, clearing the technical overhang and allowing the multiple to re-rate toward where the net-cash, 21%-margin fundamentals belong. Subsea momentum materializes in H2 2026 as management guides, international/offshore recovers, and margins push consistently above 20% toward the high-teens ROCE target. On $1.91 next-year EPS at ~18-20x — justified by the balance sheet quality and margin trajectory — the stock reaches the mid-$30s, matching Citi's $35 target and the $34 consensus median.

**Bear case ($16-18).** The Impulse judgment escalates to enhanced damages and/or an injunction on the relevant product line, forcing a larger charge and removing a revenue stream. Amberjack's continued selling keeps a lid on the stock and the overhang persists into 2027. International/offshore weakness proves structural rather than timing-driven, and the low-growth industry backdrop caps share-gain-driven growth. The stock retraces toward the low end of its 52-week range ($14.65-$18), a ~30-40% decline. The net-cash balance sheet limits genuine distress, which separates this from a value trap.

**Base case ($26-29).** The litigation grinds through appeal without near-term resolution, Amberjack continues a measured exit, and margins hold around 20% while organic growth stays muted. The stock trades roughly sideways-to-modestly-higher in a band near current levels, with the discount to fair value persisting until the overhangs clear. This is the most likely 12-month outcome given the binary catalysts are unscheduled.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Impulse litigation resolves at or below the accrued $48.8M with no injunction, or post-trial motions materially reduce the award, removing the asymmetric tail.
- Amberjack completes its exit or reduces its stake below ~5%, clearing the technical overhang (monitor 8-K/Form 4 filings).
- Two consecutive quarters of international/offshore recovery confirming management's H2 2026 Subsea momentum claim, with adjusted EBITDA margin sustained above 20%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Impulse judgment with enhanced (treble) damages or an injunction on the directional-drilling product line.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin reverting below 18% on adverse mix, signaling the margin-expansion thesis is breaking.
- A large bolt-on acquisition at an undisciplined multiple (>6-7x EBITDA) or one carrying fresh inherited liabilities.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, followed by H2 2026 results that will test the Subsea-momentum claim. Any Impulse post-trial ruling or Amberjack secondary should trigger immediate reassessment. We would revisit the rating fully within two quarters.

## Conclusion

Innovex is a fundamentally sound business: net cash, record 21% adjusted EBITDA margins, capital-light, disciplined on capital allocation, and trading at a defensible discount (~13.4x next-year EPS) to where its quality would otherwise place it. The discount is overwhelmingly technical and litigation-driven rather than operational, which is the bull case for a re-rating. But the catalysts that would close the gap — Impulse resolution, Amberjack's exit, the international/offshore recovery — are unscheduled binaries, not dated near-term unlocks, and the options market is pricing rising stress alongside continued sponsor selling that has pressured the stock off its late-May high.

We rate INVX a HOLD (uncertain). The financial strength earns a high score and limits genuine downside; the litigation tail and lack of a near-term catalyst prevent a BUY. We view the modest score decline over recent reports (6.4 to 5.7) as appropriate: the valuation reset reflects the overhangs, not a deterioration in the underlying business, which remains intact. The recent price action on unchanged fundamentals modestly improves the risk/reward, but not enough to overcome the unresolved binaries. We would move constructive on resolution of the Impulse case at or below the accrual and a clearing of the Amberjack stake; we would move negative on enhanced damages, an injunction, or margin reversion below 18%.
