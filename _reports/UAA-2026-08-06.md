## Executive Summary

Under Armour ended fiscal 2026 (year to March 31, 2026) with net revenues of $4,966.4M, down 3.8% and 15.9% below the FY2023 peak of $5,903.6M. The rate of decay improved, but the composition of that improvement is unfavorable: the derived Q4 revenue decline of 0.8% — the best top line of the year — coincided with a gross margin of 42.0%, down 464 basis points and the worst quarter of the year. Full-year gross margin fell 240bp to 45.5%, of which 155bp is attributed to tariffs. Adjusted operating income, the company's own selected performance measure in its proxy, fell 53% to $100.3M, a 2.0% margin, against $518.2M in FY2021.

The cash statement is the harder constraint. Operating cash flow was -$75.1M in FY2026 after -$59.3M in FY2025; free cash flow was -$162.2M. Capex of $87.1M is 1.75% of revenue, below management's own stated 2–4% principle, which reads as cash preservation rather than discipline in a business with an aging distribution network and a global ERP program in flight. Sitting in front of FY2027 cash flow is a $98.5M accrual covering the approximately $90M of D&O insurance proceeds the Fourth Circuit ordered repaid to carriers on January 20, 2026, with the carriers' prejudgment-interest claim still open. That is roughly 32% of the March 31, 2026 cash balance of $309.2M, and management expects to fund it from cash and/or the revolver — which raises the numerator on a total-debt-to-EBITDA covenant capped at 3.25x where our derived headroom is 12% on a conservative EBITDA definition and 28% on a more permissive one.

Two things have changed since our June review, and they point in opposite directions. The stock is up 11.6% from $6.06 to $6.76 while 90-day consensus revisions have gone the other way: current-year EPS cut from $0.23 to $0.11 (-53.6%) and next-year from $0.37 to $0.24 (-35.7%). Rising price against falling estimates is the configuration we treat as a warning rather than momentum. Offsetting that, UBS Evidence Lab data reported July 29 shows U.S. website traffic up 23% year over year in the June quarter versus +3% the prior quarter, with average discounting down roughly 250bp — the first indication in this cycle of demand improving without price concession, and the exact inverse of the Q4 FY2026 pattern. That data is secondary and unverified against issuer disclosure, and the Q1 FY2027 print scheduled for August 7 will settle it. With 37.6% of float short and near-term implied volatility at 80.8% pricing a ±13.7% move, the risk into that print is genuinely two-sided. We stay AVOID on the business and explicitly decline the short.

## Company Overview

Under Armour designs, markets and distributes branded performance apparel, footwear and accessories from Baltimore, reporting in four geographic segments plus a corporate bucket that carries centrally managed FX hedge results.

FY2026 revenue architecture:

| Line | FY2026 | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Total net revenues | $4,966.4M | -3.8% |
| Wholesale | $2,831.8M (~57%) | -4.9% |
| Direct-to-consumer | $2,054.1M | -1.7% |
| License | $107.4M | +13.5% |
| Apparel | $3,395.1M | -1.6% |
| Footwear | $1,076.4M | -10.8% |
| Accessories | $414.5M | +0.9% |
| North America | $2,859.4M (58%) | -7.9% |
| EMEA | $1,180.5M (24%) | +8.6% |
| Asia-Pacific | $719.1M (14%) | -4.8% |
| Latin America | $234.2M (5%) | +8.7% |

Forty-eight percent of revenue is generated outside the United States. The owned-and-operated fleet was 442 doors as of June 30, 2025, of which 356 are Factory House outlets and only 86 are full-price Brand House stores. An 80%-outlet fleet sits awkwardly against a stated strategy of premiumization and higher average selling prices, and it is one reason wholesale still carries 57% of revenue at a lower realized margin than a DTC-weighted peer.

Sourcing is concentrated and contractually thin: ten manufacturers produce approximately 69% of apparel and accessories, seven produce substantially all footwear, and the 10-K states there are no long-term contracts with suppliers. That combination limits cost negotiation leverage precisely when tariff pass-through is the dominant margin variable.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.**

| FY (to 3/31) | Revenue | YoY | Gross margin | GAAP op income | GAAP net income |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $5,903.6M | — | 44.9% | $283.8M | $386.8M |
| FY2024 | $5,701.9M | -3.4% | 46.1% | $229.8M | $232.0M |
| FY2025 | $5,164.3M | -9.4% | 47.9% | -$185.2M | -$201.3M |
| FY2026 | $4,966.4M | -3.8% | 45.5% | -$163.1M | -$495.6M |

**The FY2026 net loss is mostly non-cash, and that matters for interpretation but not for the conclusion.** Pretax loss was $200.7M; income tax *expense* was $294.8M, an effective rate of -146.9%, driven by valuation allowances established against previously recognized U.S. federal deferred tax assets. The balance-sheet trace is visible: the non-current deferred tax asset fell from $286.2M at 3/31/2025 to $68.4M at 12/31/2025. The charge did not consume liquidity and does not affect covenant EBITDA. It mechanically distorts net margin (-10.0%) and ROE (-35.0%). It also creates asymmetric future optics: profitable foreign segments (EMEA $191.5M, APAC $84.5M, LatAm $29.9M of segment operating income) generate tax expense while U.S. losses generate no offsetting benefit, so the reported effective rate will stay inflated until U.S. profitability returns — at which point a valuation-allowance release would produce a large one-time non-cash EPS gain that should not be capitalized.

**Quality of earnings is the central analytical problem.** The bridge from GAAP to adjusted is now larger than the adjusted profit itself: GAAP operating loss -$163.1M, plus $171.5M of FY2026 restructuring-and-related charges, plus the $98.5M litigation reserve, arrives at approximately the disclosed $100.3M of adjusted operating income. Add-backs of roughly $270M against $100.3M of adjusted profit means anyone using the adjusted number is underwriting the non-recurrence of charges that have recurred and expanded for two consecutive years:

- May 2024 announced plan: $140–160M total.
- Increased November 14, 2025 (8-K Item 2.05).
- May 11, 2026: Board approved a further $50M, taking the plan to approximately $305M and pushing completion to December 31, 2026 from the prior end-of-FY2026 target. $260.7M recorded through 3/31/2026; $44.3M remaining.

Roughly double the original high end, nine months later. There is an identifiable one-time cause inside the FY2026 expansion — $69.7M of non-cash contract-termination cost primarily relating to the Curry Brand separation, plus the $15.9M Rialto distribution-facility exit — so this is not purely a cost-parking exercise. But with the plan now running through the end of calendar 2026, GAAP operating income carries the weight in our base case.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow of -$75.1M in FY2026 after -$59.3M in FY2025, with FCF of -$162.2M (-3.3% of revenue). The derived Q4 operating outflow of approximately -$332.2M is seasonal, not a solvency signal: accounts payable fell from $664.5M at 12/31/2025 to $420.1M at 3/31/2026 as post-holiday payables unwound, and supply-chain-finance obligations within AP were $265.3M at 12/31/2025 versus $143.8M a year earlier. No single quarter of UAA cash flow should be annualized. The decision-relevant number is the full-year -$75.1M, two years running.

**Balance sheet and covenants.** Cash $309.2M (from $501.4M); inventories $914.8M; receivables $681.9M; goodwill $492.8M with no impairment; total equity $1,414.4M; retained earnings $217.4M (from $746.3M).

Debt structure is better managed than the operating results:
- $600M 3.25% notes due June 2026 were satisfied and discharged in August 2025 via an irrevocable deposit. Because this is an in-substance defeasance, both the notes and ~$601.2M of restricted investments remained on the 3/31/2026 balance sheet and come off together at repayment. Netting them is the correct treatment.
- $400M 7.25% notes due July 2030, issued June 2025. The coupon is itself a market read on the credit.
- $200M drawn on the $1.1B revolver at a weighted-average 4.83%, versus zero at 6/30/2025, plus $45.5M of letters of credit. The facility matures June 2030 and is secured by a first-priority lien on substantially all assets excluding real property.

Post-defeasance indebtedness is therefore $600M. The financial covenants are consolidated EBITDA/interest of at least 3.50x and total indebtedness/consolidated EBITDA of no more than 3.25x, with the eighth amendment excluding the defeased notes. Using disclosed adjusted operating income of $100.3M plus $109.6M of depreciation gives EBITDA of $209.9M and leverage of 2.86x — implying EBITDA could fall only 12%, to $184.6M, before the test binds. Permitting a stock-compensation add-back of roughly $46M lifts EBITDA to about $256M, leverage to 2.34x and headroom to roughly 28%. We have not read the credit agreement's EBITDA definition, so treat the 12–28% band as a sensitivity, not a forecast; the company states it was in compliance at both 12/31/2025 and 3/31/2026. Interest coverage is comfortable at roughly 5.4x against the 3.50x test.

The practical implication is that liquidity (~$309M cash plus roughly $855M undrawn revolver) is only useful while the covenant holds, and drawing revolver to fund the $90M+ insurance claw-back raises the numerator. Repaying the revolver to zero would push the breach threshold down to roughly $123M of EBITDA. Deleveraging, not buybacks, is the highest-value use of cash here, and the company repurchased $25M of Class C stock in FY2026 while FCF was -$162M.

Recomputing Altman Z from the FY2026 balance sheet — using retained earnings/total assets of 0.0492 rather than the equity/assets substitution a standard screen applies — gives Z of 1.64 on book equity to 1.92 on market equity, versus 2.11 on the same book basis a year earlier. At or below the 1.81 distress boundary, deteriorating about 0.47 points year over year.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze at the consolidated level; there is a question about whether contraction has ended.

**Evidence that it has slowed:** derived Q4 FY2026 revenue was -0.8% against -4.2% in Q1 and -5.2% in Q3, with the Q4 operating loss narrowing to -$33.7M from -$72.1M on a 14.7% SG&A reduction. EMEA grew 8.6% for the year with segment operating income up 30.1% to $191.5M, a ~16.2% margin that now exceeds North America's. License revenue grew 13.5%. Latin America grew 8.7%.

**Evidence that the mix of the business is still degrading:** footwear fell 10.8% to $1,076.4M (-14.3% in Q1, -12.0% in Q3), and Under Armour no longer has a marquee basketball signature athlete after the November 2025 Curry separation. North America, 58% of revenue, fell 7.9% with segment operating income down 29.7% to $442.5M — an implied margin decline from roughly 20.3% to 15.5%. Asia-Pacific fell 4.8%. Within DTC, Q1 FY2026 owned-store revenue grew 1% while eCommerce fell 12%, with eCommerce at 31% of DTC. A shrinking direct digital channel is the least ambiguous demand signal a brand produces, because it is not mediated by wholesale buyers' order decisions.

**Consensus has capitulated on FY2027.** Current-year EPS estimates fell from $0.23 to $0.11 over 90 days (-53.6%) and next-year from $0.37 to $0.24 (-35.7%). The 30-day changes are essentially flat (-2.4% and +0.6%), so the cutting happened around the May 12 print and has since stabilized rather than continued. The near-quarter estimate rose 61.8% over 90 days but off a $0.01 base, which is not informative. The earnings-surprise record is genuinely mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses in four quarters, including a -69.5% miss in the March 2026 quarter on a $(0.02) estimate.

Two forward items carry real optionality but cannot be modeled. First, the IEEPA tariff position: the Supreme Court invalidated certain IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026, new tariffs were initiated under alternative authorities, the Court of International Trade ruled those illegal on May 7, 2026 while leaving them in effect pending appeal, and a refund process opened in April 2026 with Under Armour stating it is pursuing reimbursement of certain previously paid IEEPA tariffs. Amount and timing are undisclosed. Second, FX moves against the company in FY2027: FX added 45bp to FY2026 gross margin, but the cash-flow-hedge component of AOCI moved from +$6.7M to -$43.7M between 3/31/2025 and 12/31/2025 on $1,425.9M of designated notional, and deferred hedge losses reclassify into COGS as the underlying transactions occur.

We could not verify the company's own FY2027 guidance in issuer material. The $0.08–$0.12 adjusted EPS range in circulation is UBS's characterization as of July 29, 2026, and should be treated as unconfirmed.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline market capitalization of $1.28B corresponds to Class A shares alone (188.8M × $6.76 = $1,277M). Under Armour has three classes: Class A 188,834,386, Class B 34,450,000, Class C 202,605,615 — 425,890,001 shares total, consistent with FY2026 weighted-average diluted shares of 426,575K.

Valuing all classes at the Class A price gives equity of $2,879M. Applying an illustrative 7% discount to B and C (the 10-K acknowledges Class C trades at a discount) gives $2,767M. Adding $600M of post-defeasance debt and deducting $309.2M of cash gives enterprise value of **$3,058–3,170M**, before the $98.5M litigation accrual and before capitalizing $880.7M of undiscounted operating lease commitments.

Against a peer set trading at a median 16.7x (CRI 16.7x, GIII 12.5x, CPRI 32.1x, with OXM and the UA Class C line negative), Under Armour at 28x next-year non-GAAP earnings carries a ~1.7x premium to the median while contracting revenue and generating negative free cash flow. Any screen using Class A market cap alone will show this name as roughly half as expensive as it is. At 14.6–15.1x adjusted EBITDA for a business with a 2.0% adjusted operating margin, four years of revenue decline and -$162M FCF, there is no statistical value case.

The bull case is entirely a margin-recovery option. FY2021 adjusted operating income was $518.2M; FY2024 was $350.8M. Restoring $350M on roughly $5B of revenue against $110M of D&A would put EV/EBITDA near 6.7x at today's price, worth about $11 per share at 11x. That is a genuine multi-bagger path, and it is what UBS's $10 target (roughly 14x an FY2029 EPS estimate of $0.70) prices. The problem is the sequencing: the recovery has to run through gross margin, and gross margin fell 464bp in the most recent quarter.

The price also matters. At $6.76 the stock sits 6.3% above the consensus mean of $6.36 and 22.9% above the median of $5.50, having risen 11.6% since mid-June and 63.7% off the November 2025 low of $4.13, while out-year estimates were cut by a third or more. The guidance about treating analyst targets as floors applies to inflection stocks that are cheap relative to rising estimates. Here, estimates are falling and the multiple is above peers.

## Competitive Landscape

Under Armour is a distant third-or-lower Western athletic brand competing against materially larger and better-capitalized peers, and the FY2026 segment data localizes the pressure precisely.

North America is where the brand is contested: -7.9% revenue, -29.7% segment operating income, an implied margin compression of roughly 480bp, and -10.3% in Q3 alone. This is the domestic wholesale channel where shelf space and price/value positioning are decided, and where an 80%-outlet owned fleet undercuts a premiumization message.

EMEA is the strongest counter-evidence: +8.6% revenue, +30.1% segment operating income, a ~16.2% margin now above North America's, with Q1 FY2026 at +9.6% (+6.0% currency-neutral). The brand can grow where it is under-penetrated and less associated with discount distribution. Asia-Pacific is shrinking (-4.8%) but doing so margin-accretively, with segment operating income up 15.4%.

Footwear is the structural hole. The category fell 10.8% and Under Armour recorded $69.7M of non-cash contract-termination costs primarily relating to the Curry Brand separation. Press reporting consistently indicates Curry parted ways in November 2025, retained the Curry Brand, and signed with Li-Ning in June 2026; the ten-year characterization is unconfirmed in any filing. What filings do confirm is that the company has no marquee basketball signature athlete, and that total sponsorship-and-other contractual commitments are $248.8M — a modest war chest for re-entering a signature-athlete category.

One item in the obligation schedule deserves emphasis. Total contractual obligations at 3/31/2026 are $3,074.5M, of which **product purchase obligations of $1,214.6M are all due within one year**. Committed product buys of that size against declining revenue is the mechanism by which apparel companies end up in clearance cycles, which is precisely what compressed Q4 gross margin.

The macro backdrop is unhelpful. Under Armour's mid-market, promotion-sensitive positioning leaves it exposed if discretionary spending weakens.

## Risk Assessment

**Insurance claw-back — near-term cash, growing.** The Fourth Circuit reversed on January 20, 2026, requiring Under Armour to repay approximately $90M of D&O insurance proceeds previously funded by carriers for the Consolidated Securities Action. A $98.5M accrual sits in accrued expenses. A rehearing petition is pending; if denied, the matter returns to the District Court including the carriers' anticipated prejudgment-interest claim. Under Armour is the payer. Funding is expected from cash and/or revolver — roughly 32% of the cash balance, in a year of negative free cash flow.

**Covenant, not liquidity.** Derived headroom of 12–28% on the 3.25x leverage test. If FY2027 adjusted EBITDA slipped to $150M with $600M drawn, leverage would be 4.0x. This is the constraint that would force asset sales or an equity raise, and it is the reason buybacks are the wrong use of cash here.

**Tariffs, two-sided.** 155bp of the FY2026 gross margin decline; approximately $80M of FY2026 COGS impact. The legal position is unsettled in both directions, with an unquantified IEEPA refund possibility offsetting continued exposure.

**FX reversal.** FY2026's 45bp tailwind is likely to invert as deferred hedge losses reclassify into COGS.

**Sourcing concentration.** Ten manufacturers for 69% of apparel/accessories, seven for substantially all footwear, no long-term contracts.

**Control environment.** Material weaknesses at both 3/31/2024 and 3/31/2025, and disclosure controls concluded not effective at 12/31/2025. The FY2026 10-K states remediation was complete at 3/31/2026 and controls effective. Genuine progress, freshly earned.

**Governance.** Kevin Plank holds all Class B shares at ten votes each, giving majority voting control; Class A and Class C holders cannot effect governance change. Retained earnings of $217.4M means another loss of FY2026's magnitude turns cumulative retained earnings negative.

**Stale information.** The most recent 10-Q available covers the quarter ended 12/31/2025. Balance sheet, hedge positions, revolver balance and covenant position are as of 3/31/2026 at best.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $10–11 per share (+48% to +63%), 24–36 months

Revenue decline has effectively stopped (Q4 -0.8%). EMEA compounds at high single digits with a 16% segment margin. SG&A came down 14.7% in Q4 and the restructuring program completes December 2026, removing $44.3M of remaining charges and, more importantly, the add-back mechanism. The UBS Evidence Lab reading — U.S. traffic +23% with discounting down 250bp — indicates demand recovering at better price, which if confirmed reverses the Q4 margin pattern. Tariffs are exogenous and possibly partly refundable; a valuation-allowance release would add a large non-cash EPS credit. Restoring FY2024's $350.8M of adjusted operating income on roughly $5B of revenue supports approximately $11 per share at 11x EV/EBITDA. Fairfax bought 1.18M Class A shares at ~$5.00 immediately after the disappointing May 12 print, and 37.6% short interest means any confirmation forces covering into a thin float.

### Bear Case — $4.00–4.25 per share (-37% to -41%), 12–18 months

Gross margin is the binding variable and it went the wrong way in Q4 (42.0%, -464bp). FX flips from tailwind to headwind. $1.2B of product purchase obligations due within twelve months meets a decelerating U.S. consumer, forcing another clearance cycle. The $90M+ insurance repayment lands, drawing the revolver and pushing leverage toward the 3.25x test on 12% derived headroom. North America continues to lose share with no basketball franchise and a fleet that is 80% outlet. Adjusted EBITDA drifts toward $170M, and at 12x EV/EBITDA the equity is worth roughly $4.10 per share — consistent with the $4.13 fifty-two-week low and the $4.00 low analyst target.

### Base Case — $5.75 (-15%)

FY2027 adjusted EBITDA roughly flat at $210M, capitalized at 13x, gives $2,730M of EV; net of $600M debt and $309M cash, equity is $2,439M, or $5.73 across 425.9M shares. This sits between the consensus median of $5.50 and the mean of $6.36. Base case below spot is the operative fact for the recommendation.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Q1 FY2027 results on August 7, 2026 — one day forward of this report. Secondary catalysts: the rehearing decision on the ~$90M insurance repayment, and Q2 FY2027 results in early November, by which point FX hedge reclassification and holiday inventory positioning become visible.

**Timing window.** The primary catalyst resolves within 24 hours, which is too tight to establish a position on this analysis, and the secondary catalysts are undated or three months out.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 37.6% of float short, 53.8M shares, 5.8 days to cover. Fairfax holds ~23.4% of Class A. This is among the most crowded shorts in mid-cap consumer discretionary.

**Borrow and options signal.** Near-term IV of 80.8% against 54.8% realized means protection and directional puts are expensive; the 26-point premium is a direct carrying cost. Put/call OI of 4.53 confirms the bearish side is already heavily populated, while the near-term skew reads call-heavy — the options market is not offering shorts a favorable entry. UBS's public stance ahead of the print ("we doubt the 1Q report is a catalyst," in-line expected, traffic +23%) is the opposite of what a short needs.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $4.25 (-37%). Squeeze upside on a clean print with confirmation of the traffic and discounting data plausibly reaches the 52-week high of $8.15 (+21%) or beyond toward $9–10 given the short base. Reward:risk is roughly 2.51:1.74, or 1.44:1 — below the 2:1 threshold, before an 80% IV borrow-equivalent cost.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The fundamental thesis — negative FCF, contracting revenue, thin covenant headroom, add-backs exceeding adjusted profit — is intact, but 37.6% short interest, a 23.4% strategic holder in the Class A float, an imminent binary with 80.8% implied volatility, and secondary evidence pointing toward an in-line-or-better quarter make the mechanics adverse. Avoid the equity; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (any two would move us to HOLD; three-plus toward BUY):**

1. **Full-year operating cash flow positive.** FY2027 OCF above zero after -$59.3M and -$75.1M. This is the single most important test.
2. **Gross margin recovery above 46% for two consecutive quarters** with the improvement not attributable to FX or one-time tariff refunds. The Q4 FY2026 42.0% print is the level to beat.
3. **Revolver drawn balance back to zero** and leverage below 2.0x on the disclosed EBITDA definition, which would move the covenant breach threshold down to roughly $123M of EBITDA.
4. **Insurance claw-back resolved** at or below the $98.5M accrual, with no material prejudgment interest.
5. **Restructuring program closed at ~$305M on December 31, 2026** with no further expansion, and GAAP operating income positive without add-backs.
6. **Footwear returning to growth**, or a credible signature-athlete signing with disclosed economics, reversing the -10.8% trajectory.
7. **Price.** At $4.50–5.00 — Fairfax's May entry zone, roughly 0.50x EV/revenue and 11–12x adjusted EBITDA — the margin-recovery option becomes worth underwriting even with the risks unresolved.

**Immediate downgrade-severity triggers:** covenant leverage above 3.0x on the company's own disclosed basis; any further restructuring-plan expansion beyond $305M; a third consecutive year of negative operating cash flow; retained earnings turning negative; or reinstatement of a material weakness.

**Timeline.** We expect the headwinds to persist through FY2027 (to March 2027). Tariff resolution is in litigation into FY2027, FX hedge losses reclassify into COGS through the year, the restructuring runs to December 2026, and the U.S. consumer backdrop is deteriorating at the margin. The natural reassessment points are the August 7 Q1 FY2027 print (which will settle the traffic-versus-discounting question and, if the company issues one, the FY2027 outlook) and the November Q2 print.

## Conclusion

**AVOID. Overall score 3.0 / 10, a modest decline from 3.4 in June.**

The decline is not a reaction to price action — the stock is up 11.6% — but to the arithmetic of that price against the fundamentals. On a full-capitalization basis the stock trades at 14.6–15.1x FY2026 adjusted EBITDA and roughly 28x next-year consensus non-GAAP EPS, versus a peer median P/E of 16.7x, for a business whose revenue has fallen 15.9% in three years, whose adjusted operating margin is 2.0%, whose operating cash flow has been negative for two consecutive years, and whose GAAP-to-adjusted add-backs are 2.7x its adjusted profit. Out-year consensus EPS has been cut 35.7% over 90 days while the shares rose, leaving the price above both the mean and median analyst target. Derived leverage-covenant headroom of 12–28% against an imminent $90M+ insurance repayment is the specific mechanism by which a bad FY2027 becomes a financing event rather than merely a disappointing year.
