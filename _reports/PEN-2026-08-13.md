## Executive Summary

Penumbra signed a definitive merger agreement with Boston Scientific on January 14, 2026 at a headline $374.00 per share. Shareholders approved the transaction in May 2026, management withdrew FY2026 guidance and stopped holding earnings calls, and the expected close is by year-end 2026. From that point forward, PEN's share price has been determined by merger mechanics rather than by thrombectomy market share.

The mechanics have moved materially against holders. Consideration is elective — $374.00 in cash or 3.8721 Boston Scientific shares — subject to proration to roughly 73% cash and 27% stock. The exchange ratio is fixed with no disclosed collar, set against a BSX reference price of $96.59 ($374 ÷ 3.8721, the ten-day VWAP through January 13, 2026). Boston Scientific now trades at $51.32 after cutting its 2026 outlook in late July on WATCHMAN and electrophysiology softness, announcing a restructuring, and absorbing roughly a dozen sell-side target reductions. The blended consideration is therefore 0.73 × $374.00 + 0.27 × (3.8721 × $51.32) = $273.02 + $53.65 = **$326.67**. PEN at $326.19 sits 0.15% below that figure. Any screen anchored to the $374 headline — including the $361.83 consensus target, which requires BSX near $85 to be reachable on deal terms — misreads this as a double-digit opportunity.

The distribution of outcomes is unattractive from here. If the deal closes at today's BSX price, the return is approximately zero before discounting and negative after: the cash leg discounted at 4% over 0.375 years is worth $268.99, so the present value of the total consideration is roughly $322.64, about 1.1% below the current price. If BSX rallies 22% to its own $62.69 consensus, PEN is worth $338.56, or +3.8% — a transmission factor of about 0.165x, which makes PEN a poor instrument for expressing a BSX recovery view. If the deal breaks, a standalone framework on ~$1.62B of FY2026E revenue plus $637M net cash lands between $180 and $263 per share, with up to $22.85 recoverable if the $900M reverse termination fee is triggered; the midpoint of roughly $250 implies **−23%**. Probability-weighting at even a 97-99% completion assumption produces an expected return around zero to slightly negative, against a Treasury alternative yielding roughly 1.5% over the same window. That combination — no spread, negative carry, and an unhedged 23% left tail — is the basis for moving from HOLD to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Penumbra manufactures mechanical thrombectomy and embolization devices, operating as a single segment with two reported product categories. FY2025 revenue of $1,403.7M split $947.9M thrombectomy (67.5%) and $455.7M embolization and access (32.5%).

The thrombectomy franchise is built on CAVT (Computer Assisted Vacuum Thrombectomy), a microprocessor-controlled aspiration platform sold into peripheral indications (Indigo, Lightning Flash, Lightning Bolt) and neurovascular stroke (Penumbra System, and now THUNDERBOLT). Embolization and access spans coil platforms (Ruby, SMART COIL, SwiftPAC), microcatheters, and access sheaths. Manufacturing is substantially in-house across two California campuses — Alameda (~610,000 sq ft) and Roseville (~260,000 sq ft) — with a 330,000 sq ft Costa Rica facility under construction. Direct sales were roughly 90% of FY2025 revenue, with distributor dependence deliberately reduced from 16.7% (2023) to 10.0% (2025). China is served through a licensing arrangement in which Penumbra transfers manufacturing technology to a local partner in exchange for fixed payments and certain royalties; $18.0M of contract assets sit against these agreements, with no new tranches signed in 2025 or H1 2026.

The intellectual-property structure is worth isolating: 125 issued patents globally (66 U.S.), with the 22 patents covering CAVT running to 2039–2044 while legacy coil and 3D-device patents expire 2029–2037. The durable protection sits under the faster-growing platform, and the nearer expiries sit under the more commoditized franchise.

The company frames its opportunity as penetration rather than share gain: roughly 2.15 million annual U.S. clot incidences, of which the 10-K states the vast majority receive no mechanical intervention. The binding constraint is clinical-protocol change at the hospital level, which the filing acknowledges "takes years."

### Earnings quality

Reported operating income — $73.6M (2023), $9.3M (2024), $189.2M (2025) — overstates the improvement. FY2024 carried $115.3M of charges from exiting the immersive-healthcare (VR rehabilitation) business: $33.4M of inventory written down through cost of goods sold, $58.9M of intangibles, $18.0M of PP&E, $5.0M of severance. That equals 9.6% of FY2024 revenue, and the inventory portion is why FY2024 gross margin printed 63.2% versus 67.1% in FY2025. Adding the $115.3M back gives normalized FY2024 operating income of roughly $124.6M, or 10.4% of revenue, against 13.5% in FY2025 — underlying expansion of about 310 basis points, not the ~1,270 the reported series implies.

FY2025's 13.4% effective tax rate is likewise not repeatable. MD&A attributes it to excess stock-based-compensation tax benefits, which scale with share-price appreciation at vest and exercise. At a 24% rate, FY2025 pretax income of $205.1M produces roughly $155.9M net income (~$3.97 diluted EPS) versus $177.7M and $4.52 reported — approximately $0.55 per share of tax tailwind. H1 2026 normalized to a 23.3% ETR. Anyone valuing PEN standalone off $4.52 is working from an inflated base.

## Growth Analysis

Operating growth remains mid-teens, and the clinical pipeline had its strongest year on record — after the price was fixed:

- **STORM-PE 90-day results (April 13, 2026):** randomized evidence that CAVT plus anticoagulation produced significantly better functional outcomes than anticoagulation alone in acute intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism. The 10-K identifies protocol adoption as the binding constraint; randomized functional-outcome data is the specific instrument that moves guidelines, applied against roughly 150,000 U.S. high/intermediate-risk PE patients.
- **THUNDERBOLT FDA clearance (June 11, 2026) and CE Mark (June 15, 2026)** — first CAVT platform with modulated aspiration for acute ischemic stroke, the payoff of an IDE study begun in 2022.
- **THUNDER results (July 22, 2026)** and **FORWARD enrollment (July 7, 2026)** in distal ischemic stroke.

All four are company-issued releases; effect sizes and endpoint definitions are unverified here.

Held to close at today's BSX price, the position underperforms Treasury bills.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names Boston Scientific, Medtronic, Stryker (explicitly "now including Inari Medical"), and Terumo as most notable competitors, conceding they have greater name recognition, deeper GPO and payor relationships, the ability to bundle and rebate, and more resources for development, sales, and patent litigation.

The Stryker/Inari combination placed a bundling-capable owner behind the leading pure-play venous thrombectomy franchise. Penumbra's own acquisition completes the consolidation of mechanical thrombectomy into three or four strategics. Penumbra's structural counterargument — that this is a penetration market where protocol adoption, not competitive displacement, binds — is credible and is why STORM-PE and THUNDER matter. It also cuts both ways: if the treated population expands, resource-rich incumbents with bundling leverage capture a disproportionate share.

The IP shape is favorable for an acquirer: CAVT protection to 2039-2044 under the growth engine, with the 2029-2037 expiries sitting under the slower, more commoditized coil and access lines.

The acquirer's condition is now part of the picture. Boston Scientific cut its 2026 outlook to 5-6% organic revenue growth and 7-8% adjusted EPS growth, guided Q3 adjusted EPS to $0.80-0.82 against $0.84 consensus, announced a restructuring, and pointed to 2028 for recovery. The cash leg alone requires roughly $10.7B (0.73 × 39.389M × $374) from a company now capitalized at $74.4B and simultaneously restructuring. Merger agreements of this type are binding, specific performance is customarily available, the shareholder vote is complete, the $900M reverse fee cuts against walking, and BSX has publicly maintained the deal is on track. The tension is worth naming without inferring intent from deal structure.

**Competitive position score: 6.0.** Leading position with durable IP under the growth platform and improving clinical evidence, against a halving of the core franchise growth rate and a consolidating competitive set.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Deal-break risk is the dominant idiosyncratic exposure.** Downside to roughly $250 (−23%) against essentially zero upside on close. The most decision-relevant unresolved item is regulatory status: no confirmation of HSR clearance, a second request, EU phase status, or divestiture commitments could be located. The only external corroboration that the transaction is on track is Morningstar's June 22 characterization and the market's 0.15% spread. Penumbra's own 10-K names the acquirer as a most notable competitor; the specific product overlap is unverified, and the unusually large 6.11% reverse fee (versus 3.56% on the target side) is consistent either with counsel pricing regulatory risk or with routine large-cap medtech convention.

**2. BSX price risk is unhedgeable inside the security.** With no collar, 16.4% of PEN's value floats with BSX at a 0.165x transmission factor. A holder wanting BSX exposure gets a badly diluted version; a holder not wanting it cannot remove it.

**3. Timing risk.** A close later than year-end 2026 widens the discount to consideration mechanically, with no change in deal risk.

**4. Operating constraints during pendency.** Material contracts, indebtedness, unbudgeted capex, asset transactions, and share repurchases all require BSX consent. The $637M net cash position cannot be deployed.

**5. Information vacuum.** Guidance withdrawn, no earnings calls for either 2026 quarter, no analyst Q&A. In a break scenario, the market would be repricing a standalone company on which no management commentary has existed for a year.

**6. Operating risks that matter chiefly on a break:** substantially all manufacturing at two California campuses with named earthquake and wildfire exposure, Costa Rica incomplete and 32% over budget; dependence on a single third-party ethylene oxide sterilizer with disclosed EPA exposure; single- and limited-source suppliers; 332 days of inventory against self-cannibalizing product generations; fixed MS-DRG reimbursement that makes devices a cost input to a fixed-payment procedure; GPO/IDN contracts terminable on 60-90 days; unhedged 22% international revenue; China technology-transfer IP risk with no new tranches since 2024.

**7. Litigation is clean.** The California wage-and-hour class action and PAGA claim settled for $4.6M with final approval in February 2026. A 2021 securities case was voluntarily dismissed. January 2026 plaintiff-firm solicitation is routine around any announced merger.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating the 99% completion probability implied by the spread. ATM IV rose from 16.5% to 40.6% between July 27 and August 13, and put/call open interest jumped from 2.47 to 14.42. Near-term ATM IV of 37.2% sits 7.9 points rich to 29.3% one-year realized vol, with a flat term structure (37.2% near, 35.9% at six months) and implied moves of ±22.5% at three months and ±32.5% at six months. For a security in which BSX moves transmit at only 0.165x, implied moves of that magnitude are pricing break risk, not deal-adjusted BSX beta. The 14.42 put/call open-interest ratio indicates protection held in size. Near-term skew reads call-heavy (−11.2%) while the six-month tenor reads 10.4%, consistent with a small near-term pin plus longer-dated downside demand.

The divergence favors caution: the cash-equity spread says near-certainty; the options structure says holders are paying up to insure the tail. When the spread offers 0.15% and the tail costs real premium to hedge, the trade is not compensating.

**Risk factors score: 3.5.** A single binary event with 23% asymmetric downside, unverified regulatory status, an unhedgeable acquirer-price exposure, and no management disclosure channel.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($339-346, +3.8% to +6.1%)

Boston Scientific's July guidance cut proves to be a reset rather than a structural break. WATCHMAN and electrophysiology headwinds stabilize, the restructuring is received as credible, and BSX recovers toward its $62.69 consensus or beyond by the close. Each dollar of BSX recovery adds $1.045 to PEN's blended value: BSX at $62.69 gives $338.56 (+3.8%); BSX at $70 gives $346.20 (+6.1%). Regulatory clearance is confirmed, the deal closes before year-end 2026, and the arithmetic pays out cleanly. Requires a 22-36% move in a de-rating large-cap medtech name in under five months, delivered to PEN holders at a 0.165x rate.

### Base Case ($328, +0.6%)

The deal closes by year-end 2026 with BSX in the $51-55 range. Blended consideration lands $326.67-$330.85. On a present-value basis the holder realizes slightly less than the current price, and the position underperforms a Treasury bill over the same window. The realistic base case is not loss of capital but forgone return of roughly 100-200 basis points versus cash, plus the 23% tail.

### Bear Case ($250, −23.4%)

The transaction fails — most plausibly on an antitrust path, given that Penumbra names Boston Scientific as a most notable competitor and that regulatory clearance status is unverified, or on a financing/conditions dispute at a restructuring acquirer facing a $10.7B cash outlay. PEN reprices to standalone: $1,617M FY2026E revenue at 4-6x EV/sales plus $637M net cash gives $180-263, with up to $22.85 recoverable if the $900M reverse fee triggers (conditions unverified). Midpoint approximately $250. Standalone repricing would occur into an information vacuum — no guidance for a year, no earnings calls, a core franchise whose growth has halved, and FY2026 estimates falling — which argues the low end of that range is as likely as the midpoint. Penumbra's 52-week low of $221.26 corresponds to roughly 5x on the same framework and is a plausible landing zone.

### Probability-weighted expectation

At the market-implied 99% completion probability: 0.99 × (+0.1%) + 0.01 × (−23.4%) ≈ −0.13%. At a more conservative 95%: 0.95 × (+0.1%) + 0.05 × (−23.4%) ≈ −1.1%. Adding the BSX distribution shifts the close-case return modestly positive but not enough to change the conclusion. Against a 4.20% two-year yield, the opportunity cost over 4.5 months is roughly 150 basis points on top.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Horizon to resolution: approximately 4.5 months (expected close by December 31, 2026).

**Base case target: $328** — the blended consideration at a BSX price of $52-53, or +0.6% from $326.19. Arithmetic: $273.02 cash + 1.04547 × BSX.
**Bull case target: $346** — BSX at $70.00, +6.1%.
**Bear case target: $250** — deal break, standalone $200-263 plus conditional $22.85 fee, −23.4%.

**De-risking plan.** Trim now rather than around a catalyst. There is no dated event between here and close that improves the entry — the spread is already inside 20 basis points and the cash leg accrues no yield to the holder. Investors who want to retain exposure to a possible BSX recovery should convert: sell PEN and buy BSX directly, obtaining roughly 6x the sensitivity per dollar of capital without the break tail. Investors holding for the cash leg should recognize that a Treasury bill dominates it on both return and risk. Residual exposure, if any, should be sized as a position one is willing to see marked down 23%.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD.**
1. Confirmed regulatory clearance (HSR expiry or clearance, EU Phase I clearance) filed on an 8-K, which would compress the break probability and make the 0.15% spread a fair-value hold rather than a negative-carry one.

3. A deal amendment adding a collar, a price increase, or a firm accelerated closing date.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. A second request, EU Phase II referral, or any disclosed divestiture requirement — each would materially raise break probability against a price offering zero cushion.
