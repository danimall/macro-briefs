**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Entegris supplies high-purity materials, filtration, and contamination-control products that sit inside the semiconductor manufacturing process flow. The model is structurally more attractive than capital-equipment peers because revenue tracks wafer volumes and content-per-wafer rather than the more violent WFE capex cycle, and because products are spec'd into customer process recipes (high switching cost). The company is mid-recovery from a soft 2024-2025: Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 5.0% YoY to $811.9M, gross margin recovered to 46.9%, and non-GAAP EPS rose 28% to $0.86. Consensus EPS estimates are rising (current-year +6.7% over 90 days to $3.64; next-year +6.0% to $4.62), and the sell-side recommendation mix has drifted toward more buys.

The problem is price. The stock has roughly doubled off its November 2025 low of $67.97 and trades around $157 (the company-profile field shows a stale $180.74; the most recent traded close in the price series is $157.34 on June 17). On forward consensus of $3.64 CY EPS, that is roughly 43x, and meaningfully higher (~101x) on the trailing GAAP $1.55. A material slice of the recent margin and EPS improvement is non-operating in origin: a January 2026 useful-life extension on PP&E that cuts FY2026 depreciation by ~$73M (~$0.05 of the Q1 EPS benefit), plus an abnormally low 1.1% Q1 effective tax rate. Normalized for both, underlying operating momentum is solid but below what the headline GAAP suggests.

The investment case is balanced. On one side: a real consumable moat, content-per-wafer tailwinds (molybdenum interconnects, HBM, advanced packaging, 2nm purity), rising R&D (10.3% of sales), de-leveraging ($5.6B to $3.7B debt since the 2022 CMC deal), and an April 2026 revolver upsize that pushes the maturity wall to 2031. On the other: a premium multiple against low-single-digit organic growth, a 21%-of-sales China exposure that export controls are actively converting into domestic competitors, a full CEO-and-CFO turnover within twelve months, and proxy-disclosed TSR underperformance (89.3 vs. peer 268.2). We rate ENTG HOLD with a growth tilt.

## Company Overview

Entegris operates two segments generating $3.20B of FY2025 revenue:

- **Advanced Purity Solutions (APS)** — ~$1.80B (56% of sales): filtration, purification, microcontamination control, FOUPs/wafer carriers, fluid handling. FY25 segment profit $426M (~24% margin), the higher-margin half of the business.
- **Materials Solutions (MS)** — ~$1.41B (44% of sales): CMP slurries and pads, deposition precursors, specialty and implant gases (SDS), selective etch and clean chemistries. FY25 segment profit $277M (~20% margin). This is largely the inherited CMC Materials franchise.

The company's differentiation is breadth. No single product platform exceeds 3% of net sales (unusual diversification for a materials supplier), and the strategic narrative is cross-segment co-optimization: supporting an industry transition (e.g., to molybdenum interconnects) with precursors, CMP, cleans, and filtration simultaneously. No single competitor spans the full deposition-to-CMP-to-etch-to-clean-to-contamination-control portfolio; the segment-by-segment rivals are Pall/Danaher, Shin-Etsu Polymer, Cobetter (APS), and Merck KGaA, Air Liquide, Linde, Anji Microelectronics (MS).

David Reeder is now CEO; long-tenured CEO Bertrand Loy has moved to Executive Chair. CFO Linda LaGorga departed in January 2026, with Sukhi Nagesh hired as permanent CFO (offer letter April 2026) after an interim period.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2023 $3.52B → FY2024 $3.24B → FY2025 $3.20B (-1.4% YoY). The decline is mostly portfolio pruning (divestitures of QED, the Electronic Chemicals business to Fujifilm, and the PIM business); organic volume was only modestly down (~$14M in FY25). Q1 FY2026 inflected positive at $811.9M, +5.0% YoY, with APS +7% and MS +3% on improved wafer-start demand.

**Margins.** FY25 gross margin was 44.4% (down ~1.5pts on plant performance and higher depreciation); GAAP operating margin 14.3%, net margin 7.4%. Q1'26 gross margin recovered to 46.9% and operating margin to 17.4%. The caveat: the January 2026 useful-life extension reduced Q1 depreciation by ~$19M (~$8M net income), so part of the sequential margin step-up is a non-cash accounting tailwind rather than operating leverage. Adjusted EBITDA margin remains genuinely strong at ~28%.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow $695M, capex $299M, FCF $396M (12.4% margin); 2026 capex is guided down to ~$250M, which should lift FCF. Cash rose to $443M at Q1'26. Total par debt was $3.75B at FY25, down from $5.6B in 2022, the residue of the CMC acquisition. Maturities are laddered 2028-2030 with no near-term wall, and the April 2026 revolver was upsized to $750M (from $575M) and extended to 2031. Current ratio is a healthy 3.35. The Altman Z-Score of 1.68 ("distress zone") is a leverage-and-EBIT/assets artifact, not a liquidity or going-concern signal; FCF is positive and the company repaid $300M of term loan in FY25 plus $50M in Q1'26.

**Earnings quality flags.** Two items inflate GAAP optics: (1) the depreciation estimate change above, and (2) an unusually low tax rate (FY25 effective rate 7.1%; Q1'26 1.1%, helped by OBBBA provisions and release of unrecognized tax benefits). Both flatter EPS and are unlikely to persist at these levels. We lean on Adjusted EBITDA and a normalized tax rate when judging the trajectory.

**Capital allocation.** $0.10/quarter dividend (since 2017, ~$61M/yr, well covered, ~0.26% yield). No active buyback. Stated priority is debt paydown plus R&D and capacity. R&D has risen to 10.3% of sales from 7.9% in 2023, a constructive technology-leadership signal.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on content-per-wafer expansion rather than wafer-count cyclicality. Each node transition (2nm, GAA), HBM stacking, and advanced-packaging step adds process steps, new materials, and tighter purity requirements, all of which raise Entegris's dollar content per wafer. This is a durable, multi-year secular driver and the part of the thesis we weight most.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum but not an explosive re-rating. Current-year EPS consensus rose +6.7% over 90 days to $3.64 and next-year +6.0% to $4.62; the current-quarter estimate is up +5.4% over 30 days. The earnings surprise record is good but modest (3 beats, 1 miss across four quarters; Q1'26 beat by 13%). This is solid mid-single-digit-to-low-double-digit forward earnings growth, not the +30%-plus revision velocity that would mark a trough-to-recovery deserving aggressive re-rating. The signal is real but does not clear the bar at which we would override a premium multiple.

The structural drag on growth is China and peer-relative lag. Entegris's consumable model meant it largely sat out the AI-driven WFE rally that lifted equipment peers in 2025, visible in the proxy's TSR underperformance (89.3 vs. peer 268.2). The bull case is content-per-wafer; the bear case is that growth keeps lagging peers while China erodes the base.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$157 (or $178.77 on the analyst-target reference price), ENTG trades at roughly 43x forward CY EPS of $3.64, ~34x next-year $4.62, and ~9x sales on ~$3.2B revenue with ~$0.4B FCF (~70x FCF). Trailing GAAP P/E is ~101x on $1.55, partially a trough artifact, but even forward-normalized the multiple is rich for low-single-digit organic revenue growth.

Peer comparables in semiconductor materials and equipment trade at a wide and elevated range: MKSI 72x, AEIS 70x, ONTO 141x, FORM 150x (peer median ~72x). On a headline P/E basis ENTG looks cheaper than several peers, but those peers carry higher near-term growth (WFE-levered) against which Entegris's mid-single-digit revenue growth compares unfavorably on a growth-adjusted basis. The PEG is unattractive: ~43x forward on roughly 7-10% earnings growth is well above 1.5. The sell-side makes this point directly: recent notes rate Hold and argue the premium is not supported by the growth and margin profile relative to WFE-exposed alternatives.

The analyst price-target set (mean $160.6, median $162.5, high $205, low $115) brackets the current price almost exactly, which tells us the consensus already views the stock as fairly valued rather than mispriced upward. Given the earnings-quality flags, the case that the market is materially underpricing ENTG is weak. We do not treat the consensus target as a floor here, because this is a quality name at a full multiple, where targets and price reflect the same content-per-wafer optimism and are vulnerable to any disappointment. Valuation scores 4.5: the franchise is real, but there is no identifiable mispricing that argues for upside from here.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is genuine and trajectory-stable to slightly improving. Products are spec'd into customer recipes, switching risks customer yields, and the consumable revenue base is structurally less cyclical than equipment. Portfolio breadth spanning deposition, CMP, etch, clean, and contamination control is a real differentiator that no single rival matches, and rising R&D supports continued content gains at each node transition.

The trajectory caveat is China-driven competitive displacement. The 10-K is explicit that U.S. export controls are incentivizing Chinese customers to localize and develop domestic competitors (Anji Microelectronics named), and that China imposed retaliatory critical-materials restrictions in 2025. This is a slow-burn erosion of one-fifth of the revenue base and the formation of future competitors in MS chemistries. Customer concentration is also rising: top-10 customers are 50% of sales (up from 43% in 2023), with TSMC alone at 16%, and contracts generally lack long-term purchase commitments. Net, the competitive position is strong in the West and durable in APS, but with a structural Chinese headwind that caps the score short of high-conviction.

## Risk Assessment

1. **China / export controls (highest conviction).** ~21% of FY25 sales, with controls actively reducing sales and manufacturing domestic competitors. Structural, not headline.
2. **Customer concentration.** Top-10 at 50%, TSMC at 16%, no long-term commitments; bargaining power skewed toward customers.
3. **Earnings quality.** GAAP momentum is inflated by a ~$73M annual depreciation reduction and a ~1% effective tax rate; both are non-recurring tailwinds that overstate underlying operating improvement.
4. **Leadership turnover.** New CEO (Reeder) and new CFO (Nagesh) within roughly twelve months, against documented TSR underperformance; strategy-continuity risk that could cut either way.
5. **Leverage.** $3.7B debt (D/E 2.11), variable-rate term-loan exposure into a higher-for-longer rate path; mitigated by the 2031 revolver extension and steady paydown.
6. **Tariffs and trade.** ~82% of sales international, a large share of US-made product ships to China; the trade regime is actively evolving (IEEPA invalidation, Section 301 investigations).
7. **Goodwill.** $3.95B (47% of assets), mostly CMC; no impairment to date but a large carrying value.

Insider activity is neutral-to-cautious: predominantly programmatic option-exercise-and-sell (10b5-1 driven), with no open-market conviction buying at current levels after the rally. This is informational noise rather than a bearish signal given rising estimates, but the absence of any insider buying removes a potential bullish confirmation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 81.6% against 59.2% one-year historical vol (rich by ~22 points) and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 81.6% > six-month 78.4%), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress or a catalyst (next earnings). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.8% (and ±18.1% on the front monthly chain). Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-0.4% near term) and volume/OI ratios are call-heavy (P/C OI 0.06), so positioning is not bearish, but the elevated, backwardated vol indicates the market expects a large move and is charging for it. The reported jump in ATM IV to ~255% in the delta snapshot looks like a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. Practically: rich IV argues against chasing here and means option premium favors sellers over buyers near term.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$205-220).** Content-per-wafer expansion (molybdenum, HBM, advanced packaging, 2nm purity) drives sustained mid-to-high-single-digit organic revenue growth; margin recovery continues toward FY24 levels on operating leverage and lower capex; de-leveraging continues and the 2031 revolver removes refinancing as a concern; the new management team executes and closes the peer-relative TSR gap. On $4.62+ next-year EPS at a ~45x multiple sustained by the secular story, the stock reaches the low-$200s. Matches the analyst high target of $205.

**Bear case (~$115-120).** China erosion accelerates as localized competitors take MS share; the depreciation and tax tailwinds roll off and reveal flatter underlying earnings; growth continues to lag WFE peers; a higher-for-longer rate path and any AI-capex deceleration compress the premium multiple toward the equipment-peer growth-adjusted range. On normalized EPS at ~25-28x, the stock falls toward the analyst low of $115, roughly 27% below current levels.

**Base case.** Mid-single-digit revenue growth, gradual margin recovery, and continued deleveraging keep the franchise healthy, but the premium multiple caps upside and the China overhang plus earnings-quality flags cap re-rating. The stock trades in a wide band around fair value, consistent with consensus mean/median of $160-162.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-tilted), 12-month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to ~$120-130 (≈26-28x forward $4.62) that restores asymmetric upside without requiring a fundamental change.
- Organic revenue growth accelerating durably above ~8% YoY for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating content-per-wafer gains outpace the China drag.
- Evidence the new management team is closing the peer-relative growth/TSR gap, plus clustered open-market insider buying.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- China revenue declining materially (e.g., toward mid-teens % of sales) as localization accelerates, with MS segment profit compressing.
- Underlying (depreciation- and tax-normalized) operating margin deteriorating, confirming the GAAP improvement was largely accounting-driven.
- A guidance cut, a CMC-related goodwill impairment, or covenant pressure from the variable-rate debt in a higher-rate scenario.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the front-month options backwardation signals the market expects a large move around it), plus any 8-K on strategy from the new CEO/CFO.

## Conclusion

Entegris is a genuinely high-quality semiconductor materials franchise with a defensible consumable moat, a recovering margin profile, a real revenue inflection in Q1 FY2026, and a clean deleveraging path now extended to 2031. The forward estimate trend is positive. What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a premium ~43x forward multiple against only mid-single-digit organic growth, GAAP earnings flattered by a depreciation estimate change and a sub-2% tax rate, a 21%-of-sales China exposure that export controls are converting into domestic competitors, and a full CEO-and-CFO turnover against documented peer underperformance. Consensus targets bracket the current price almost exactly, confirming there is no identifiable upward mispricing to exploit at these levels. The score trajectory has improved appropriately (AVOID → HOLD as the franchise quality and growth re-accelerated), and we hold it there: own it for the secular content-per-wafer thesis if already in, but the current price does not offer asymmetric upside. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the $120-130 range or on confirmation that organic growth is durably outpacing the China headwind.
