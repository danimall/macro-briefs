**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Southside Bancshares is a Tyler, Texas-based regional bank holding company with roughly $8.8 billion in total assets, operating almost entirely within Texas markets (East Texas, DFW/Fort Worth, Austin, Houston). Its defining structural feature is a larger-than-peer securities portfolio (approximately $2.87 billion, or a third of assets), heavily weighted to agency MBS and Texas municipal securities and partly funded with wholesale borrowings. Management calls this its "balance sheet strategy": deliberately leveraging capital into securities to enhance profitability. The tradeoff is a structurally lower net interest margin than loan-dominant peers, in exchange for a more liquid, more rate-sensitive book.

The near-term story is one of recovery. Fiscal 2025 was a down year, with net income falling roughly 22% to ~$69M, driven mainly by a ~$32M pre-tax securities-repositioning loss and one large restructured CRE credit. Both items have now cleared. Q1 2026 showed a clear recovery: net income of $23.3M (+8.1% YoY), diluted EPS of $0.78 versus $0.71, FTE net interest margin expanding to 3.01% from 2.86% as funding costs fell faster than asset yields, and the $27.5M restructured CRE loan paid off in full, collapsing nonperforming assets 74.6% to $9.7M (0.11% of assets). Consensus estimates have risen 7-10% over 90 days, confirming positive earnings momentum. Credit is currently pristine.

The problem is price. The stock trades at $34.54, near its 52-week high of $36.20 and up roughly 12% since a year ago, at approximately 10.1x forward consensus EPS ($3.41) and around 15.1x trailing ($2.29). That is a modest premium to the peer median P/E of 13.6 on trailing earnings, but the peer group is uniformly cheap, and much of SBSI's recovery is now visible in the price. The five covering analysts are unanimously at Hold, with targets clustered tightly at $37-38, only 7-10% above spot. The recovery is real, the balance sheet is well-capitalized, and the ~4.1% dividend is well-covered, but the appreciation case is thin and the earnings profile carries an unusual downside skew to further Fed rate cuts. Hold this for income rather than for appreciation near its high.

## Company Overview

Southside operates through Southside Bank, a Texas state-chartered community/regional bank serving roughly 178,800 deposit accounts with an average balance near $34,000. The ~$4.95B loan book is heavily real-estate-weighted (~83% of loans):

- Commercial real estate: ~$2.75B (including ~$1.69B non-owner-occupied and ~$690M multifamily)
- 1-4 family residential: ~$717M
- Construction: ~$642M (+40% YoY, the fastest-growing category)
- Commercial & industrial: ~$457M
- Municipal loans: ~$337M — a genuine differentiator, direct lending to Texas municipalities and school districts at yields above comparable municipal securities

The company's competitive niche rests on deep East Texas community relationships and Texas municipal lending. Its structural disadvantage is the lower NIM produced by the securities-heavy strategy. Texas market health — population and economic growth — supports the ~8% YoY loan growth. Southside is one of the early adopters of the NYSE Texas dual listing following its Texas reincorporation; the November 2024 "Notice of Delisting" 8-K that appears in the filing history is a technical artifact of that re-listing, not financial distress.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Pretax income by fiscal year: $119.6M (2022) → $101.1M (2023) → $107.4M (2024) → $82.6M (2025). The 2025 dip was largely self-inflicted and non-recurring: a securities-repositioning loss taken to clean up the AFS book, NIM compression, and one elevated restructured CRE credit. A naive screen showing "declining net income" mistakes a deliberate balance-sheet cleanup for deterioration.

**Q1 2026 detail.** NII rose 7.1% to $57.7M. The mechanism is straightforward: the Fed cut 75 bps in late 2025, and Southside's funding costs fell faster than its asset yields (cost of total deposits down to 2.13% from 2.26%, FHLB borrowing costs down sharply). ROA of 1.10% and ROE of 10.96% (annualized) are improving but still modest for the sector. Loans grew 2.7% QoQ and 8.3% YoY, led by construction and CRE.

**Capital and balance sheet.** Total shareholders' equity of $854.9M is 9.7% of assets. Regulatory ratios are solid: consolidated CET1 12.68%, Total capital 16.95%, Bank-only CET1 16.01%, comfortably well-capitalized. Two embedded overhangs deserve attention. AOCI carries a -$102.6M drag from unrealized securities and pension losses; the company opted out of including AOCI in regulatory capital, so this does not impair ratios but depresses tangible book. Separately, HTM securities carry a ~$158M unrealized loss (fair value $1.06B versus $1.22B carrying). Neither impairs solvency, but combined they represent ~$260M of embedded losses that would crystallize only under forced-sale liquidity stress. Contingent liquidity is ample at ~$2.68B (FHLB $2.22B, Fed discount window $384M, plus correspondent lines). Uninsured deposits are 38.4% of total, or a more defensible 21.9% excluding collateralized public funds and affiliate deposits.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is steady at $0.36/quarter, roughly a 46% payout and about a 4.1% yield. The buyback authorization was increased to 2.0M shares in October 2025, but no repurchases were executed in Q1 2026 despite the stock trading below its highs, a missed opportunity that tempers the capital-allocation grade. The August 2025 issuance of $150M of 7.00% subordinated notes and February 2026 redemption of $100M of 3.875% notes terms out debt to 2035 but locks in higher interest cost.

**Earnings quality note.** The beat/miss record is choppy: Q1 2026 beat by 4.2%, but Q4 2025 missed by 12.9% and Q3 2025 missed by 78.1% (the repositioning-loss quarter). The volatility reflects the securities strategy and one-time items rather than core deposit-franchise instability, but it argues for measured confidence in the smooth-looking forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is a cyclical recovery, not a structural re-rating. NIM expansion is the primary driver — funding costs are repricing down faster than assets in the current easing cycle — supplemented by healthy 8% loan growth in Texas markets. Consensus estimate revisions confirm the momentum: current-quarter EPS revised +10.3% over 90 days to $0.86, current-year to $3.41 (+7.1%), next-year to $3.48 (+3.9%). The 30-day revisions have flattened (+0.1% to +0.6%), suggesting the bulk of the upward revision cycle following the CRE-loan resolution and NIM recovery has already occurred.

This magnitude of revision — 7-10% over 90 days on a mid-single-digit revenue growth base — falls well short of the sharp profile (>30% revisions plus >30% revenue growth) that would flag a systematic underestimation of fundamentals. The recovery is real and consensus is catching up, but this is a bank normalizing off a self-inflicted trough. Forward EPS growth of ~2% year over year (from $3.41 to $3.48) is consistent with a mature, deposit-constrained regional bank operating in a healthy but not explosive Texas market.

## Valuation Assessment

On tangible book — equity of $854.9M less ~$202M goodwill/intangibles, roughly $653M, or ~$21.9/share against 29.75M shares — the stock trades near 1.6x tangible book, elevated for a bank generating a ~11% ROE, and higher still if one marks the AOCI/HTM embedded losses more fully.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The Regional Banks peer set (CFFN, CPF, TRST, HAFC, HFWA) shows a median trailing P/E of 13.6 and yield of 3.3%. SBSI's ~15.1x trailing sits above that median, though the forward multiple of ~10.1x on rising estimates is more comparable. The peer group is uniformly cheap (11.7x-16.1x), reflecting the negative-alpha reality of small-cap regional banks in this environment rather than SBSI-specific mispricing.

The valuation dimension does not offer asymmetric upside here. The five covering analysts carry a tight target range of $37-38 (mean $37.33, median $37), just 7-10% above the current price, with a unanimous Hold rating. On a stock with a compressed multiple and rising estimates, analyst targets can function as floors, but SBSI is a mature bank whose targets reflect the same modest forward-growth extrapolation as the price, and whose recovery is already visible in the numbers. The ~4.1% dividend at a 46% payout provides genuine yield support and a valuation floor, but the equity itself offers little room above the analyst ceiling. A high valuation score requires a specific reason the market is wrong; I do not see one. The market appears to be pricing this bank about right.

## Competitive Landscape

Southside competes against larger Texas regionals (Frost, Prosperity), national banks, credit unions, and fintechs. Its durable advantages are relationship depth in East Texas and its municipal-lending niche, which earns yields above comparable municipal securities. Its structural disadvantage is a below-peer NIM, an acknowledged and permanent consequence of the securities-heavy balance-sheet strategy — safer, more liquid assets that yield less than a loan-dominant peer would earn.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable. Loan growth of ~8% YoY, led by construction (+40%) and CRE, tracks or modestly exceeds Texas market growth, suggesting the bank is holding or slightly gaining share in its footprint. There is no evidence of erosion, but neither is there evidence of moat-widening or the kind of structural share gain that would warrant a high competitive score. The municipal niche is defensible but small (~$337M of loans). This is a stable community-banking franchise operating in a favorable geography.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate-cut asymmetry — the defining risk.** Unusually for a post-2023 regional bank, SBSI's larger earnings risk is rates falling too far, not rising. Its own simulation shows NII +1.86% under +100bps but -2.62% under -100bps and -5.14% under -200bps. The downside sensitivity worsened materially year over year (from -1.61% for -100bps). With the Fed in an easing cycle, and the broader rate path two-sided but skewed toward eventual cuts if the consumer weakens, this is the number to watch. Near-term, modest cuts are helping NIM as funding reprices; deep cuts would reverse that benefit and compress the securities-book spread.

**CRE concentration.** CRE is ~67% of real-estate loans; substandard-rated CRE was ~$264M at 3/31/2026. Multifamily (~$690M) and fast-growing construction (~$642M) bear watching. Current credit metrics are excellent — nonaccruals of 0.19% of loans, NPAs at 0.11% of assets, ACL/NPAs of 472% — but the concentration is the structural credit vulnerability if Texas CRE softens.

**Balance-sheet-strategy execution.** Reliance on wholesale funding rose to 22.4% of deposits (from 16.0%), and the strategy embeds MBS prepayment, spread, and duration risk. Combined with the ~$260M of AOCI/HTM embedded losses, these are the levers that would turn a liquidity event ugly, though the deep $2.68B contingent liquidity cushion makes an actual event unlikely.

**Geographic and energy concentration.** Texas-only footprint; modest energy exposure at 1.47% of loans.

Most of these risks are known and reasonably priced, which is why I score risk factors near neutral rather than low. The rate-cut asymmetry is the least-appreciated item and the one that could genuinely surprise a market conditioned to worry about the opposite exposure.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility jumped from 40.6% to 89.0% between late May and early July, a 48.5-point increase, while the stock rose only 5.9%. For a small-cap regional bank this magnitude of IV likely reflects thin, illiquid options rather than a large informed positioning event, and should be treated with caution as a signal. Sharply rising IV against a stable-to-rising price often precedes a catalyst or reflects growing uncertainty; here it most plausibly captures event risk around earnings and the broader rate-path debate. Short interest is low and uncrowded at 2.8% of float (4.9 days to cover), consistent with the absence of a strong bear thesis. Neither the options nor the short-interest data materially alters the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The 2025 earnings decline was self-inflicted and non-recurring; both that item and the problem CRE loan have cleared. NIM is expanding as funding costs reprice down faster than assets, and Q1 2026's 3.01% margin has room to run if the Fed delivers measured cuts. Credit is pristine (NPAs 0.11%), capital is strong (CET1 12.68%), the ~4.1% dividend is well-covered, and Texas loan growth of ~8% provides a steady volume tailwind. If NIM continues expanding and the bank puts its 2.0M-share buyback to work, EPS could push toward the high end of consensus, supporting a move to the $38 analyst ceiling. Bull case ~$40 (roughly 12x on ~$3.55 EPS with buyback support), or about +16% plus the dividend.

**Bear Case.** The stock is near its 52-week high with the recovery already priced. Deep Fed cuts would trigger the -5.1% NII sensitivity, compressing the very margin the bull case relies on. The securities-heavy strategy produces a structurally below-peer NIM and choppy earnings (Q3 2025 missed by 78%), and the ~$260M of embedded AOCI/HTM losses limit tangible-book support. Wholesale funding reliance is rising (22.4% of deposits). If NIM rolls over or Texas CRE softens, the multiple compresses toward the peer median with no growth to offset. Bear case ~$28-29 (roughly 8.5x on flat/pressured earnings, toward the 52-week low), or about -16%.

**Base Case.** A well-run, conservatively-capitalized regional bank in recovery mode, fairly valued near consensus targets. Base case ~$36-37, in line with the analyst floor and roughly +5-7% plus a ~4.1% dividend — a total return in the high single digits driven mostly by yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The durable return here is the ~4.1% dividend at a sustainable 46% payout, supplemented by modest appreciation toward analyst targets. The equity does not offer asymmetric upside at $34.54 near its high, and the earnings profile carries an under-appreciated downside skew to further rate cuts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$30s or below (toward ~9x forward, closer to the peer discount), which would restore an asymmetric entry with the ~4.1% yield intact.
- Evidence that NIM expansion is durable through additional Fed cuts — i.e., Q2/Q3 2026 FTE NIM holding above 3.05% while funding costs continue repricing down — which would falsify the rate-cut-asymmetry concern.
- Meaningful execution of the 2.0M-share buyback at current or lower prices, signaling capital-allocation discipline and accelerating per-share growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A reversal in NIM (FTE margin declining below ~2.85%) as deep rate cuts trigger the -100/-200bps NII sensitivity.
- Deterioration in CRE credit — substandard CRE rising materially above ~$264M, or nonaccruals climbing back toward 2025 levels.
- Wholesale funding reliance climbing further above ~25% of deposits, or a forced crystallization of the HTM/AOCI embedded losses under liquidity stress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings (roughly late July), with the FOMC rate path as the key ongoing macro input given the balance sheet's rate-cut sensitivity.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory has been declining (6.0 → 5.3) with the recommendation held at HOLD, driven by valuation and management-quality markdowns. The current data confirms rather than reverses that read. Fundamentally, the picture has genuinely improved — the CRE loan resolution and NIM recovery are real positives, and estimate revisions are positive. But the stock has moved with those fundamentals, and near its 52-week high, above ~1.6x tangible book, and only 7-10% below a unanimous-Hold analyst target range, the risk/reward is balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable. The self-inflicted 2025 trough was correctly identified as non-recurring, but the recovery is now the consensus, not a mispricing.

Management quality earns a middling score: the CEO succession from Gibson to Donahoe was orderly and internally groomed, but there is no open-market insider buying, the buyback sat idle while the stock traded below its highs, TSR lags peers (121.0 vs. 130.1 over five years), and ROATCE has declined steadily from 17.0% to 11.2%. This is competent, conservative stewardship of a recovering bank rather than aligned, opportunistic capital deployment.

I maintain a HOLD, classified as an income hold. Own SBSI for the ~4.1% covered dividend and the stability of a well-capitalized Texas franchise in recovery; do not chase it near its high for appreciation. The clearest path to a more constructive view is a pullback into the low-$30s or hard evidence that NIM expansion survives the next leg of Fed easing.
