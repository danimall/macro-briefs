**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EchoStar is now best understood as a levered, discounted claim on a SpaceX equity stake it does not yet legally hold. The company's ~$30B market cap is underpinned less by its cash-burning DISH/Sling/Boost/Hughes operations than by a ~262M-share position in SpaceX — valued by New Street at roughly $42B post-IPO — that will only be received on completion of the ~$40B spectrum sales to SpaceX (65 MHz) and AT&T (50 MHz). The FCC approved those sales on May 12, 2026, but the AT&T leg's closing delay directly triggered prepackaged Chapter 11 filings for DISH DBS and DISH Wireless on June 30/July 1, 2026, when the company could not cover ~$2B of debt due July 1. The crown-jewel assets (Hughes, Boost, Gen Mobile, and the SpaceX stake) sit outside the filing.

Underneath the special-situation optics, the operating business is deteriorating. FY2025 revenue fell 5.2% to $15.0B on subscriber erosion across all three segments; the headline $14.5B net loss (-$50.41 EPS) is dominated by ~$17.6B of non-cash impairments tied to abandoning the 5G build, but the ex-impairment adjusted loss of ~$1.05B (worse than 2024's ~$664M) and free cash flow of roughly -$1.07B are real and worsening. Stockholders' equity collapsed from $20.2B to $5.77B in a single year; the current ratio sits near 0.41.

Fundamentals are secondary to a binary sequence of deal closings, SpaceX's volatile share price, and Ergen's undisclosed intentions for the stake. The estimate revisions look strongly positive (current-year EPS revised from -$3.61 to +$1.71 over 90 days), but they reflect the swing from impairment-crushed prints toward gain-on-sale accounting rather than durable operating momentum. We rate SATS HOLD (uncertain), recognizing genuine upside if the AT&T deal closes and SpaceX holds its IPO price, against 40-55% downside if the closing slips and the restructuring turns disorderly. The prior report's move from AVOID to HOLD reflected the same event-driven asymmetry; we sustain that call while emphasizing the timeline is not in the company's control.

## Company Overview

EchoStar is a Colorado-based telecom and satellite holding company controlled by co-founder and chairman Charlie Ergen. Its December 2023 all-stock merger with DISH Network explains the revenue jump from ~$2.0B (FY2022) to ~$17.0B (FY2023). The business collects several legacy and transitional franchises:

- **Pay-TV:** DISH satellite TV (~4.8M subs) plus Sling streaming (~1.79M), ~7.0M combined at Q4 2025.
- **Retail wireless:** Boost Mobile / Gen Mobile, ~7.51M subscribers.
- **Broadband/satellite:** Hughes Satellite Systems, ~739K broadband subscribers.
- **Spectrum portfolio:** AWS-3, AWS-4, H-block, 600 MHz and other licenses accumulated to build a nationwide 5G Open RAN network — a plan now abandoned.

The competitive moat was never operational scale. It was the spectrum bank and Ergen's regulatory and dealmaking acumen. That thesis has now been monetized: EchoStar is selling the spectrum to AT&T and SpaceX and pivoting into an asset-holding vehicle whose dominant value driver is a passive minority stake in a private rocket company, with no operational involvement in SpaceX.

Four points matter.

First, the FY2025 loss is largely optical. Roughly $17.6B of non-cash impairments — including a ~$7B PP&E write-down (network assets from $9.19B to $2.24B) plus intangible/spectrum charges — drove the -$50.41 EPS. Isolate those and the story is more mundane but still negative.

Second, the underlying business is loss-making and getting worse. Ex-impairment adjusted net loss was ~$1.05B in 2025 versus ~$664M in 2024. Operating deterioration, not accounting noise, is the trend to watch.

Third, revenue is in secular decline, falling 5.2% YoY on subscriber losses across all segments (pay-TV -168K in Q4 alone, wireless -9K, broadband -44K). Cord-cutting and competitive wireless pressure are structural.

Fourth, the balance sheet is stressed. Retained earnings swung from +$11.6B to -$2.88B. Current liabilities of $12.4B exceed current assets of $5.13B (current ratio ~0.41) — the near-term liquidity squeeze that materialized in the July 2026 subsidiary bankruptcies. Standardized health screens (Altman Z, Piotroski) returned insufficient data, consistent with a company mid-restructuring whose ratios are distorted by impairments and merger accounting.

The prepackaged Chapter 11 of DISH DBS and DISH Wireless is a deliberate liability-management maneuver, designed to repay debt early without penalty and enabled by incoming spectrum cash. Creditors holding most DISH DBS notes and >$8.8B of DISH Wireless debt support the plan, with emergence targeted by end of September 2026. It reflects a genuine July 1 liquidity shortfall rather than an accidental blow-up, but it is still a bankruptcy filing, and the outcome depends on deal closings the company does not control.

## Growth Analysis

Conventional growth analysis does not apply cleanly here. Operating revenue is shrinking with subscriber declines in every segment; there is no organic growth engine.

The estimate revisions look dramatic — current-year EPS revised from -$3.61 (90 days ago) to +$1.71, next-year from -$3.97 to +$0.97, and current-quarter from +$0.11 (90 days ago) to +$0.17. Normally, revision velocity of this magnitude combined with revenue growth would be a powerful BUY signal. It is not here, for two reasons. Revenue is falling, not growing, so the revenue-plus-revisions confirmation the strongest setups require is absent. And the EPS swings are driven by the transition from impairment-crushed comparisons toward gain-on-sale and post-restructuring accounting, not by improving operating economics. The next-quarter estimate whipsawed from +$8.10 (90 days ago) to +$0.78 (now) — a -90.4% revision — because these forecasts are dominated by deal-timing assumptions rather than run-rate earnings. On only two-to-four covering analysts, the estimate series is noisy and low-confidence.

The genuine "growth" thesis is a one-time step-up in equity value: receipt of the ~262M SpaceX shares and the spectrum-sale cash, offset by debt repayment and the $2.4B FCC escrow. That is a balance-sheet event, not an earnings-growth trajectory. Whether it accrues to equity holders depends on the holdco discount and Ergen's disposition plans.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are meaningless. Forward P/E screens at -911; there is no dividend; trailing P/E is undefined given the loss. Peer comparables (LYV -97x, BIDU -735x, OMC 285x, ROKU 90x; median ~90x) tell us nothing about a company whose value is a sum-of-the-parts on a private-equity stake plus distressed telecom operations.

The only relevant valuation framework is sum-of-the-parts anchored on the SpaceX stake:

- **Bull (New Street, David Barden):** $161 target. ~262M SpaceX shares at ~$161 IPO = ~$42.1B, with a 20% holdco discount, implies SpaceX value in SATS of ~$86/share, roughly 46% below where the stake is marked. AWS-3 revalued down to $3.00/MHz-POP (from $3.62), cutting that piece to ~$8.3B.
- **Bear (Seeking Alpha):** deal is "already priced in"; fundamentals weak; 42x TTM / 32x forward EV/EBITDA against declining operations; downside 40-55% if the AT&T leg terminates and the restructuring turns disorderly.

At the current $92.03, SATS sits below the entire published analyst target range (low $120, median $131, mean $137.6, high $161). The stock has retraced from ~$147 (52-week high) and ~$124 in late May. Trading below every target with rising consensus looks like an entry, but the targets are not independent confirmation of value here — they are direct functions of two assumptions the market is actively repricing: SpaceX's post-IPO share price and the size of the holdco discount, which New Street itself widened from 10% to 20% on Ergen's opacity. A >30% drawdown from the high, into a period when the AT&T deal delay forced a subsidiary bankruptcy, indicates sophisticated holders are discounting closing risk, not that the market has simply been volatile.

We score valuation 5.5 — modestly above neutral. There is a credible, quantified path to meaningful upside if the deal closes and SpaceX holds its price, and the current price embeds a wide discount to the marked stake. But the "cheapness" is contingent on a binary event outside management's control, which is exactly the kind of setup that punishes a high valuation score. We do not treat proximity-to-target as a floor here because the targets and the price both move with the same SpaceX variable.

## Competitive Landscape

EchoStar's operating businesses are competitively disadvantaged and losing share. Pay-TV faces structural cord-cutting; DISH and Sling are subscale relative to streaming incumbents. Retail wireless (Boost/Gen Mobile) competes as an MVNO/challenger against T-Mobile, Verizon, and AT&T in a market the FCC itself has cited as intensely competitive. Hughes broadband faces Starlink and fiber overbuilders. All three segments lost subscribers in Q4 2025.

The spectrum bank — the strategic asset — is being sold. The failed bid to become the 4th nationwide 5G carrier, a legacy of the T-Mobile/Sprint remedy, proved economically unviable, forcing the write-off and the sale. AT&T emerges as a clear winner acquiring low/mid-band spectrum; SpaceX gains spectrum for direct-to-cell and hands EchoStar equity that now dominates its value. Broader spectrum comps are softening (New Street's AWS-3 cut; Verizon's $3.2B Auction 113 spend as a reference point), which pressures the residual spectrum valuation.

Competitive trajectory is deteriorating, which is why we lowered this dimension to 3.5 from 5.0 across recent reports. The company is exiting its one durable competitive advantage and retaining declining, share-losing operations.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by binary, largely uncontrollable factors:

1. **Deal-closing / regulatory execution (the swing risk).** The equity thesis hinges on completing the spectrum sales. If the AT&T deal terminates, the SpaceX-stake-plus-cash edifice loses its funding mechanism.
2. **Bankruptcy/restructuring.** Emergence from the prepackaged Chapter 11 is targeted end-Sept 2026 but not guaranteed. Disorderly outcomes cascade.
3. **Leverage.** ~$18.7B long-term debt against a shrinking, cash-burning operation.
4. **FCC escrow overhang.** The $2.4B escrow covers disputes over the abandoned terrestrial buildout — a condition of approval and a cash claim on proceeds.
5. **SpaceX-stake concentration and illiquidity.** The dominant value driver is shares the company does not yet legally hold ("we do not actually have that equity yet" — management, Q4 call), tied to a volatile post-IPO price.
6. **Key-man/controlled-company risk.** Ergen's voting control and undisclosed intentions for the stake (sell, hold, reinvest?) drove New Street to widen its discount to 20%. Minority holders have limited say.
7. **Secular decline.** Every operating segment loses subscribers.

These are specific, dated, asymmetric catalysts. Risk_factors scores 4.0: not lower, because much of the distress (bankruptcy, impairment, going-concern signals) is now known and partly priced, with real recovery upside if deals close; not higher, because the downside catalysts are live and the timeline is not in management's hands.

### Options Market Signal

Between May 23 and May 26, 2026, ATM implied volatility collapsed from 142.5% to 77.2% (-65.3pp) while the price barely moved (-0.9%). The put/call OI ratio eased from 0.42 to 0.33 — light put demand, not a bearish tilt. The IV crush suggests the market had priced an imminent binary (the FCC approval window) that then resolved, draining event premium. Post-crush IV near 77% is still elevated in absolute terms, consistent with a name where deal-closing and restructuring uncertainty remain. The modest P/C ratio argues the options market is not aggressively positioned for collapse.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($131–161)

The AT&T and SpaceX spectrum sales close on the revised timeline. EchoStar receives ~$40B in cash and equity, repays/retires the restructured DISH debt, funds the escrow, and emerges from Chapter 11 by late September 2026 as a holding company whose primary asset is the SpaceX stake. SpaceX holds or exceeds its ~$161 IPO price. The market narrows the holdco discount from 20% as Ergen provides capital-allocation clarity. On New Street's math, that supports up to $161, roughly 75% above the current $92.03. Even a more conservative discount and a modestly lower SpaceX mark lands in the $120–131 published-target zone.

### Bear Case ($40–55)

The AT&T leg slips further or terminates, removing the funding mechanism and turning the prepackaged restructuring disorderly. The escrow, debt, and negative operating FCF claim proceeds ahead of equity. SpaceX's post-IPO price fades from IPO-day levels, cutting the stake's mark. Ergen's opacity keeps the holdco discount wide or wider. The operating business continues to bleed subscribers and cash. Equity absorbs 40-55% downside toward $40-55.

The gap between these cases is enormous because it hinges almost entirely on non-operating variables: deal closing (binary), SpaceX's share price (volatile, private-market-derived), and the holdco discount (governance-driven). This is why the position resists a confident call in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (uncertain).** This is a hold-to-catalyst on an event-driven special situation, not a conviction growth or income hold. There is no dividend, and the growth thesis is a one-time balance-sheet step-up, not durable earnings compounding.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AT&T spectrum leg closes and cash is received on the revised timeline, with clear post-restructuring parent capital structure disclosed.
- Ergen provides explicit capital-allocation guidance on the SpaceX stake (monetization plan, distribution, or reinvestment), narrowing the holdco-discount uncertainty.
- SATS re-rates toward the low end of the published target range ($120) while the SpaceX mark holds near IPO levels — confirming the discount is closing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- AT&T deal terminates or is materially renegotiated, removing the funding mechanism.
- The prepackaged Chapter 11 slips past the end-September 2026 emergence target or converts to a contested process.
- SpaceX's implied share price falls materially below IPO levels, or the escrow/creditor claims prove larger than modeled.

**Reassessment timeline:** The AT&T closing and the September 2026 restructuring emergence are the decisive events. Monitor for confirmation of deal completion, receipt of the SpaceX shares, and post-emergence consolidated capitalization. Reassess immediately on any AT&T-deal news.

## Conclusion

SATS is a levered, discounted claim on a not-yet-received SpaceX stake wrapped around a secularly-declining, cash-burning telecom operation whose two largest subsidiaries are in bankruptcy. The bull case ($161) and bear case (~$40-55) diverge by a factor of three-to-four because the value hinges on binary, uncontrollable events: closing the AT&T leg, SpaceX's post-IPO price, and Ergen's intentions for the stake. The rising estimate revisions are accounting-driven, not evidence of operating recovery, and the balance sheet is genuinely stressed. Trading below the full analyst target range embeds a wide discount to the marked stake, which is the source of the upside, but that discount is contingent on a deal outside management's control.

We rate SATS **HOLD** with an overall score of 4.05, sustaining the prior report's AVOID-to-HOLD transition. This is an uncertain hold to catalyst, not a fundamentals-based conviction call. Position holders should size the position as a binary event bet and revert to AVOID if the AT&T deal terminates or the restructuring loses its September emergence path.
