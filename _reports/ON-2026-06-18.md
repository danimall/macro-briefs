**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

onsemi enters mid-2026 having confirmed the early stages of a cyclical recovery, but the stock has discounted that recovery aggressively and then some. Q1 2026 marked the first year-over-year revenue growth (+5% to $1,513M) after a 28% peak-to-trough revenue decline from $8.33B (2022) to $5.99B (2025). Power Solutions Group grew 14% YoY on automotive recovery plus an emerging AI-data-center vector, and reported gross margin recovered to 38.5% from a charge-depressed 20.3% a year earlier. The replacement growth narrative — AI data center power via the new GaNEXUS gallium-nitride portfolio, with that revenue line growing >30% sequentially and guided to double in 2026 — is real but small, sitting inside the 21% "Other" end-market on a $299M base.

The problem is price. The shares have roughly doubled in 2026 (from a ~$45 low in November 2025 to a ~$135 high in early June, currently ~$113), leaving a ~$47.8B market cap resting entirely on normalized-earnings recovery. FY2025 net income was only $121M (down 92% YoY), and Q1 2026 still posted a GAAP net loss of $(33M) due to $329M of fresh restructuring charges. Trailing P/E is ~80x. The bull case requires earnings to return toward the ~$2.2B peak achieved in 2023; consensus FY2026 EPS sits at $3.09 and FY2027 at $4.28, implying ~26-37x forward on estimates that have risen only modestly (+6% over 90 days, now roughly flat over 30 days).

We rate ON a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The cyclical inflection and the FCF engine ($1.42B in FCF during the trough year) are genuine and argue against shorting or avoiding. But the asymmetry at current prices is unattractive: the SiC franchise that was the prior growth story is decelerating amid a securities class action alleging misleading SiC statements, image sensing faces intensifying Sony competition, and the CFO and CEO have been selling into the rally with zero open-market buying. The stock prices a convincing recovery; the evidence shows an uneven one.

## Company Overview

onsemi supplies intelligent power and intelligent sensing semiconductors across three segments:

- **Power Solutions Group (PSG)** — 47% of 2025 revenue. Discrete and module power devices, MOSFETs, and the strategic silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) franchises. Vertically integrated in SiC (boules in Hudson NH, wafers in the Czech Republic and South Korea). Lowest-margin segment at 24.5% gross margin in 2025, and the focal point of both the prior SiC growth thesis and the new AI-power thesis.
- **Analog & Mixed-Signal (AMG)** — 38% of revenue, the profit anchor at 51.1% gross margin in 2025. Houses the Treo scalable analog platform.
- **Intelligent Sensing (ISG)** — 15% of revenue. CMOS image sensors with market-leading automotive ADAS share. Took the brunt of 2025 inventory write-offs and now faces Sony in automotive CIS.

End-market exposure is automotive 51%, industrial 28%, other (including AI data center) 21%. With 79% of revenue tied to automotive and industrial, both deeply cyclical, the defining feature of onsemi's situation is operating leverage in both directions. Competitors include Infineon, STMicroelectronics, Wolfspeed, ROHM and Nexperia in power; TI, ADI, Renesas, NXP and Monolithic Power in analog; and Sony, Samsung and Omnivision in sensing.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story is a cyclical trough followed by an apparent Q1 2026 inflection that remains partly engineered.

**The downturn was severe.** Revenue fell from $8.25B (2023) to $7.08B (2024) to $5.99B (2025). Net income collapsed from $2.18B (2023) to $1.57B (2024) to $121M (2025). Operating income fell from $2.54B to $84M. Gross margin contracted ~1,230 bps to 33.1% from 45.4%, driven by volume declines, factory underutilization, and $268M of excess/obsolete inventory charges (mostly $230M of ISG work-in-process from strategy changes). The company also booked $666.9M of restructuring and impairment charges in 2025 (vs. $134M in 2024) under the 2025 Manufacturing Realignment Program, including $496M of non-cash equipment impairments and 2,400 layoffs.

**Q1 2026 inflection.** PSG grew 14% YoY; ISG gross margin swung from −55.6% to +39.4% on the absence of write-offs. The segment posted a small net loss driven entirely by continuing restructuring under a new 2026 Manufacturing Realignment Program (~650 more layoffs, $147M equipment impairments in South Korea and the Czech Republic, $162M accelerated D&A). Underlying operations are profitable; the headline GAAP loss is restructuring-driven.

At ~38.5%, underlying gross margin remains well below the ~45-47% peak. Much of the optical YoY recovery owes to the absence of prior-year charges rather than fresh operating strength. The path back toward peak gross margin is the single most important metric to track, and it is not yet evident at scale.

**The balance sheet and cash flow are the genuine strengths.** Current ratio 4.52; cash and short-term investments ~$2.4B; D/E ~0.63; Altman Z 2.75 (grey zone, dragged by depressed EBIT, not leverage). The trough-year FCF held at a 23.7% margin despite the earnings collapse, achieved by slashing capex from 19% of revenue (2023) to 6% (2025), guided to ~5% in 2026. Operating cash flow was $1.76B. Debt is cheap and well-laddered: $805M 0% notes (May 2027), $1.5B 0.50% notes (2029), $700M 3.875% notes (2028), weighted-average rate ~1.7-2.1%. The May 11-12, 2026 8-K (material agreement, new financial obligation, and unregistered equity sales) is most plausibly a new convertible offering with note hedges and warrants to pre-fund the $805M 0% notes maturing May 2027, a manageable refinancing given the strong cash position. *(This interpretation was not directly verified against the filing text and should be confirmed against the primary 8-K.)*

**Capital allocation:** $1.375B of buybacks in 2025 (27.9M shares), plus $349M in Q1 2026. A fresh $6.0B authorization (Nov 2025) leaves $5.7B remaining through 2028. The timing was poor in hindsight, with much of 2025's repurchase executed in the ~$50-60 range against a stock now near $113, but the firepower and FCF support remain real.

## Growth Analysis

Two growth vectors are in tension. The prior engine, SiC, is decelerating amid pricing pressure and Chinese capacity shifting to 8-inch wafers. The replacement, AI data center power, is early: the line is guided to double YoY in 2026 but sits inside the 21% "Other" bucket on a small base. GaNEXUS (gallium nitride, launched June 2026) extends the AI-power thesis beyond SiC and shows genuine product momentum, complemented by tuck-in M&A — Qorvo's SiC JFET business ($118.8M, Jan 2025) and Aura's Vcore power IP (up to $144M, mostly contingent).

Estimate revisions are positive but modest. Current-year EPS estimates rose +6% over 90 days and next-year +6.4% over 90 days, but both are roughly flat over the trailing 30 days (current year +0.0%, next year +0.4%). The current quarter consensus of $0.71 was revised +8.1% over 90 days yet +0.0% in the last 30. Revenue growth of +5% YoY, while a directional turn, is well short of the 30%+ thresholds that mark a true earnings inflection. The earnings track record is mixed — two beats, one in-line, one miss over four quarters — consistent with a company that is recovering but not yet running ahead of expectations.

The LTSA backlog of ~$6.5B in remaining performance obligations provides some visibility, and the broader analog/auto-chip cycle appears to be bottoming in early 2026. The bull case is content gains (more silicon per EV, AI power density) outgrowing cyclicality; the bear case is that SiC growth underwhelms while the auto/industrial recovery is gradual. The data so far supports a measured recovery, not a step-change.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the stock fails to clear the bar. At ~$113 against FY2025 EPS of $0.29 (and a GAAP-loss Q1), trailing P/E is ~80x, a number that is meaningless at a cyclical trough and should not anchor the analysis. On a forward basis the picture is fairer but not cheap: ~37x consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.09 and ~26x FY2027 EPS of $4.28. The PEG on FY2027 growth is acceptable, but only if the recovery delivers cleanly.

The peer set offers limited help because semiconductor multiples are distorted by trough earnings across the cohort — MCHP at 422x, LSCC at 1,012x, MTSI at 160x, with a peer median P/E of 160x. ON's ~80x trailing is "cheap" relative to that distorted median, but that is an artifact of where everyone sits in the cycle, not evidence of mispricing. On normalized earnings power, onsemi earned ~$2.2B at the 2023 peak; capitalizing a recovery toward that level at a reasonable multiple gets to a defensible value, but the current market cap already discounts most of a convincing recovery.

The analyst price-target distribution is telling: mean $107 and median $105 sit below the current price, with a low of $68 and high of $150. Sentiment is degrading — buy ratings fell from 13 to 10 over three months while holds rose from 19 to 23. When consensus targets sit below the market price on a stock that has doubled, the burden of proof shifts to the bull, and the burden is not met here. A "cheap" headline multiple driven by trough earnings, with no independent catalyst beyond a recovery that is already priced, is a value-trap configuration rather than an asymmetric opportunity. We score valuation 3.5.

## Competitive Landscape

onsemi is a top-tier power-discretes/modules player and the #1 or #2 automotive image-sensor supplier, but it is fighting a two-front transition. In SiC, the franchise that defined its growth story two years ago is decelerating under pricing pressure and Chinese 8-inch capacity, and a securities class action (*Hubacek v. ON Semiconductor*, D. Ariz.) alleges management made misleading statements about that business; the court dismissed the second amended complaint in July 2025 but plaintiffs filed a third amended complaint in August 2025. In image sensing, Q1 2026 commentary explicitly cites intensifying competition from Sony in automotive CIS, pressuring the historically dominant franchise.

The offset is AI data center power, where onsemi claims one of the more comprehensive portfolios (SiC + GaN + analog power tree) and the GaNEXUS launch demonstrates real product velocity. The trajectory is mixed: gaining ground in AI power, defending eroding ground in SiC and CIS. The AMG analog segment (51.1% gross margin) remains a stable profit anchor. On balance the competitive position is roughly stable rather than clearly improving, which warrants a neutral 5.0: the AI-power gains are real but not yet large enough to offset the deceleration in the two franchises that historically defined the company.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality and concentration.** With 79% of revenue in automotive and industrial, earnings swing violently, with operating income moving from $2.5B to $84M across the downturn. A stalled or false recovery is the central risk, and it is the scenario the current price is least prepared for.

**Customer/channel concentration.** Distributors are 54% of revenue, and one distributor alone was ~11-12% of total revenue and rising. Loss of a large customer is flagged as potentially material.

**SiC litigation and credibility.** The securities class action, combined with the now-visible SiC slowdown, suggests the prior growth story was over-promised. Investors should scrutinize whether the AI-data-center narrative is similarly aspirational given its small current base.

**Restructuring execution.** Two consecutive years of major realignment programs, with further impairments explicitly flagged as likely. Continued held-for-sale equipment write-downs suggest footprint right-sizing is not finished.

**Geopolitical/manufacturing.** 73% of employees in Asia-Pacific/Japan; fabs in China (including the 80%-owned Leshan JV), Malaysia and South Korea; ~34% of manufacturing input costs from third-party contractors. Tariff and export-control exposure is meaningful in the current US-China environment.

**Refinancing.** The May 2027 0% notes are being addressed by the likely May 2026 convertible financing, manageable but worth confirming.

Given the binary SiC litigation overhang, the rising customer concentration, and a price that assumes a clean recovery, the idiosyncratic risk not fully reflected in valuation is elevated. We score risk factors 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 118.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 54.6% — rich by a 63.7pp spread, in the 89th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (118.4% near-term vs. 75.4% long-term), and put/call skew is elevated at 11.2%, signaling above-normal put demand. Longer-dated ATM IV has fallen from 89.0% to 54.1% since early May even as the price rose 15.8%, but the front-end spike points to a near-dated catalyst. Combined with 11.0% short interest (2.4 days to cover) and degrading analyst sentiment, the positioning corroborates a cautious stance: the market is hedging downside on a stock that has run hard, not pricing complacent continuation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$150).** The auto/industrial cycle bottoms convincingly in 2026; PSG content gains and AI data center power (doubling YoY, with GaNEXUS adding GaN exposure) compound; gross margin retraces toward 45%; and normalized EPS approaches $5+, supporting a ~30x multiple. The trough FCF and $6B buyback authorization provide downside support and per-share accretion. Reaching the bull case implies ~33% upside from current levels.

**Bear case (~$68).** SiC continues to disappoint, Sony erodes the image-sensor franchise, the auto/industrial recovery proves gradual rather than V-shaped, and the multiple compresses as the market realizes it paid a peak multiple on still-recovering earnings. Restructuring charges persist longer than expected, keeping GAAP profitability depressed. The analyst low of $68 implies ~40% downside from current levels, the asymmetry that makes the current price unattractive.

The base case sits in between: a measured recovery delivers FY2027 EPS near consensus, the market settles on ~24-26x as the cycle normalizes, and the stock holds roughly current levels with modest upside. That is a hold, not a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The cyclical recovery is real enough to preclude an AVOID, but the price/risk asymmetry is too poor and the evidence too mixed to support a BUY. We lack the evidence for a stronger directional call in either direction at the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Underlying (charge-adjusted) gross margin recovers above 42% for two consecutive quarters, confirming operating leverage rather than charge-absence optics.
- AI data center revenue scales to a quantified, material share of total revenue (e.g., >10%) with sequential growth sustained above 20%, demonstrating the replacement growth thesis is more than aspirational.
- A meaningful pullback toward the high-$70s to low-$80s, where forward P/E (~20-22x FY2027) would offer genuine asymmetry — close to where the CFO was selling.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 revenue growth reverses to negative YoY, signaling a stalled recovery.
- SiC litigation advances unfavorably (third amended complaint survives motion to dismiss with material disclosed exposure), or a major customer/distributor (the ~11-12% concentration) is lost.
- Gross margin fails to advance and a third consecutive realignment program is announced, indicating the footprint problem is structural.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected early August). The near-term options backwardation suggests the market expects a catalyst on or around that print.

## Conclusion

onsemi is a quality cyclical franchise with a genuine FCF engine and a credible early-stage recovery, but the stock has already priced a convincing recovery while the underlying evidence shows an uneven one. The SiC franchise that justified the prior re-rating is decelerating under a litigation cloud, image sensing faces Sony, AI data center power is promising but small, and management is monetizing the rally rather than adding to positions. At ~37x forward FY2026 earnings on estimates that have flattened over the past 30 days, with analyst targets sitting below the current price and options markets hedging near-term stress, the asymmetry favors patience. We rate ON a HOLD and would revisit constructively either on confirmed underlying margin expansion above 42% or on a pullback into the low-$80s where the forward multiple would compensate for the cyclical and competitive risks.
