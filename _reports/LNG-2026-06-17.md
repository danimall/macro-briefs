**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cheniere is the largest U.S. LNG exporter and the second-largest LNG operator globally, running two coastal terminals (Sabine Pass via subsidiary CQP, and 100%-owned Corpus Christi) with total expected capacity above 60 mtpa. The business is structured as an infrastructure toll: roughly 90% of anticipated production is contracted through the mid-2030s under take-or-pay Sale & Purchase Agreements that pay a fixed liquefaction fee whether or not cargoes are lifted, with a ~15-year weighted-average remaining contract life and ~$111.4B of unsatisfied contracted transaction price as of Q1 2026. This contracted base produces durable cash flow that is largely insulated from Henry Hub feedstock moves, which are passed through to customers.

The headline financials require careful interpretation. FY2025 revenue was $19.98B (+27% YoY) with net income of $5.33B ($24.13 diluted EPS), but Q1 2026 printed a GAAP net loss of $(3.5)B / $(16.65) EPS driven entirely by a $4.8B non-cash mark-to-market on IPM-related derivatives. Operating cash flow stayed positive at $1.08B and adjusted Q1 EPS of $4.77 beat consensus by ~22%, prompting a full-year guidance raise. Both the FY2025 gain and the Q1 loss are derivative noise; true earnings power sits between them and is best tracked through contracted fixed fees plus cash flow. FY2025 generated $5.54B operating cash flow and $2.46B FCF, funding a $2.7B buyback and a dividend raised >10%.

We maintain a HOLD. The franchise quality, contracted visibility, and expansion pipeline are real, and the stock at $229 sits ~24% below its March 2026 high of $300.89 with fundamentals intact and consensus estimates rising — an improved entry point. We stop short of BUY on valuation honesty: the stock trades at ~34x trailing GAAP EPS, and while a forward consensus EPS line ($17.91 CY) implies a much lower multiple, that consensus is set by only 3–4 analysts and leans materially on uncontracted/spot economics that Cheniere itself flags as entering a supply-growth phase. We rate this a growth-oriented HOLD with a bias to accumulate toward $200–215 and upgrade on either a clean expansion FID with attached contracts or further multiple compression on intact fundamentals.

## Company Overview

Cheniere shipped its first cargo in 2016 and by May 2026 had delivered more than 4,760 cumulative cargoes (>325M tonnes) to 40+ countries. The asset base:

- **Sabine Pass (Louisiana):** >30 mtpa operational, held through publicly traded subsidiary Cheniere Energy Partners (CQP), in which Cheniere owns the 100% general-partner interest, a 48.6% LP interest, and 100% of the incentive distribution rights. Blackstone, Brookfield, and the public hold the remaining LP interest.
- **Corpus Christi (Texas):** 100%-owned, >30 mtpa including the Stage 3 Project (7 midscale trains, ~96.5% complete as of Q1 2026, 5 of 7 in operation; Train 5 completed March 2026) and Midscale Trains 8 & 9 (~37% complete, target 2H 2028).

About 8 mtpa was under construction as of Q1 2026. The commercial model layers long-term SPAs (fixed liquefaction fee plus a variable fee indexed to ~115% of Henry Hub) and IPM agreements over a low-cost, brownfield-expandable platform with a fixed-price lump-sum Bechtel EPC relationship. Uncontracted volumes are sold by Cheniere Marketing into the global spot market. The MLP structure at CQP creates related-party complexity and restricts cash upstreaming: SPL and CCH must clear a 1.25x debt service coverage ratio before distributions.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue grew 27% off higher Henry Hub-linked pricing (+$2.9B) and the Corpus Christi Stage 3 ramp (+$1.2B). Net income attributable to Cheniere was helped by a $3.6B non-cash derivative gain. Revenue has been volatile year to year (2022: $33.4B; 2023: $20.4B; 2024: $15.7B; 2025: $20.0B) because of commodity swings and the gross-up of procured LNG volumes; the underlying contracted fixed-fee base is steadier and growing. FY2025 margins: operating 45.6%, net 34%, ROE 85.8% (flattered by a thin equity base typical of project finance), ROA 14.2%.

**The Q1 2026 loss is a non-cash artifact.** The GAAP loss came entirely from the $4.8B unfavorable mark on IPM-related derivatives as international-vs-Henry Hub spreads widened amid Middle East volatility. These marks swing by billions every quarter (a $3.6B gain in FY2025, a multi-billion loss in Q1 2026) and largely net out over time. The cash story was positive: $1.08B operating cash flow, record loadings, the adjusted EPS beat, and a raised full-year guide. Any screen anchored on GAAP net income or trailing EPS will misrepresent this business in either direction.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Capex was $3.08B against fixed-price Bechtel contracts; FCF was 12.3% of revenue. Shareholder returns are aggressive: $2.7B repurchased in 2025 (~12.1M shares at ~$221.55 average), a dividend lifted >10% to $2.22/share annualized, and a February 2026 buyback authorization expanded to ~$10B through 2030 (~$9.7B remaining). Share count has fallen from 251M (2022) to ~210M (Q1 2026).

**Balance sheet.** $23.9B total debt at a ~4.65% weighted-average coupon, structural D/E ~4.4x typical of project-financed infrastructure, target deleveraging toward ~4x. Liquidity is solid at ~$8.3B (cash plus $6.6B undrawn). The Altman Z-Score of 1.41 ("distress zone") is a false positive here — its drivers (low equity/assets, negative working capital, 0.94 current ratio) reflect the financing model, not insolvency. The contradicting evidence is direct: 2025–2026 upgrades from all three agencies (S&P BBB+, Fitch BBB, Moody's Cheniere unsecured to Baa2 in Feb 2026), all stable. Tax tailwinds add support: OBBBA's 100% bonus depreciation deferred cash taxes in 2025, the new FDDEI export regime delivers an effective ~14% rate on eligible export income from 2026, a $380M CAMT refund landed in December 2025, and a $370M excise-tax credit reduced Q1 2026 cost of sales.

## Growth Analysis

Three layers of growth: (1) the in-flight Corpus Christi Stage 3 ramp, the primary near-term volume/earnings driver; (2) sanctioned CCL Midscale Trains 8 & 9 (FID June 2025, ~$2.9B Bechtel EPC, target 2H 2028); and (3) the pre-FID expansion optionality — SPL Expansion (~20 mtpa, with Phase 1 Train 7 >6 mtpa now under a ~$4.69B Bechtel lump-sum EPC and limited notice to proceed signed May 27–28, 2026 at the CQP/SPLV level) and CCL Expansion (up to 24 mtpa, FERC application filed Feb 2026). Both large expansions are pre-FID and require additional capital, contracts, and regulatory approvals.

Commercial momentum continues: JERA SPA (~1 mtpa, 2029–2050), CPC Corporation Taiwan SPA (~1.2 mtpa, 2026–2050), and the Canadian Natural Resources IPM. The macro backdrop is constructive long-term — Wood Mackenzie projects global LNG demand +64% to ~671 mtpa by 2040 with a ~104 mtpa supply gap — and Europe is a durable U.S. LNG sink (2025 imports +27% to ~125 mtpa, EU vote to ban residual Russian gas including LNG by 2027).

**On the estimate-revision signal.** Consensus EPS revisions are rising: CY +20.4% over 90 days (to $17.91), next year +31.5% (to $19.51). Ordinarily this magnitude of upward revision plus volume growth would be a strong forward indicator. Two caveats temper its weight here. First, the estimate panel is thin (3–4 analysts), so revision percentages are less robust than for a widely-covered name. Second, and more important, a portion of the forward earnings improvement reflects spot/uncontracted economics and IPM marks rather than the fixed-fee base. The 10-K explicitly flags the market "transitioning from multi-year tight conditions into a period of rapid growth," with ~20 mtpa of global supply added in 2025 and more coming, which pressures realizations on uncontracted volumes and the terms available on new/replacement SPAs. The ~90% contracted, take-or-pay base (~$107.7B of contracted fixed fees) is the durable anchor; the marginal uncontracted ~10% is precisely the slice most exposed to the glut that the rising estimates partly capitalize. The revision tailwind is therefore real but less durable than the headline number implies; consensus is not structurally behind a contracted reality.

## Valuation Assessment

The multiple drives the rating, so it must be stated precisely.

**Reconciling the multiple.** The reported trailing/forward P/E is 34.3x on $24.13 GAAP EPS (the FY2025 figure, which was *inflated* by the $3.6B non-cash derivative gain). The lower ~12.8x current-year / ~11.7x next-year figures circulating come from dividing the $229 price by the 3–4 analyst consensus EPS of $17.91 (CY) and $19.51 (NY). These are not the same earnings base. The $24.13 trailing figure is GAAP and derivative-flattered; the $17.91 forward consensus is an adjusted/normalized estimate set by a thin panel. Neither is a clean read on underlying earnings power, which sits between the derivative-distorted extremes and is best proxied by contracted fixed fees plus cash flow.

We will not lean the thesis on the ~13x figure. Taken at the reported 34x trailing or ~13x thin-consensus forward, the honest conclusion is that the multiple is *ambiguous*, not obviously cheap. On FCF, the $2.46B FY2025 FCF against a ~$48.1B market cap is a ~5.1% FCF yield — reasonable for an investment-grade infrastructure compounder but not a screaming discount, and FCF is depressed during the heavy expansion-capex phase. The ~13x forward case requires the thin-panel consensus to be both accurate and durable, and the marginal earnings it captures are the most glut-exposed. A PEG-well-under-1 framing does not survive that scrutiny; we therefore decline to use it as the load-bearing pillar it was previously made to be.

**Peer and target context.** The analyst configuration is constructive (7 strong buy / 15 buy / 1 hold) with a mean target of $303.41 (median $304.50), low $259, high $340 — roughly 13–48% above the $229 price. On a high-momentum infrastructure name, consensus targets can lag, but a $303 mean against a 34x trailing multiple is best read as the Street capitalizing the volume ramp and expansion optionality, not as independent confirmation of cheapness. The stock's ~24% pullback is on no fundamental deterioration (estimates rose through the decline, guidance was raised, ratings were upgraded), which improves risk/reward and is why we hold rather than trim. At a full multiple to where pure midstream tolls trade, the burden is on the bull case, and the contracted floor supports the stock here.

Net: valuation is fair-to-slightly-rich on reported earnings, defensible on contracted cash-flow visibility, and not a source of asymmetric upside on its own. Hence a 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's moat is genuine and stable-to-improving: first-mover scale, the lowest-cost U.S. platform with brownfield expansion on owned land at both sites, a fixed-price Bechtel EPC relationship that de-risks construction cost, and ~90% contracted volumes with ~15-year average life. No single customer exceeds 10% of FY2025 revenue (though Customer A is 18% of receivables). Against new global LNG projects, Cheniere competes on installed cost and contracted volume and is positioned to capture a share of the structural 2040 supply gap.

The trajectory caveat is the supply-growth phase. As ~20+ mtpa of new global capacity comes online, spot margins on uncontracted volumes compress and replacement-SPA economics soften when current contracts roll (mid-2030s+). This does not erode the contracted base now in place, but it caps the upside on the marginal volume and is why we mark competitive position at 7.0 (strong, stable franchise) rather than the 8.5 of prior reports — the moat is intact, but the industry tailwind has shifted from tightening to loosening.

## Risk Assessment

- **Derivative/IPM earnings volatility:** Billions of non-cash GAAP swings each quarter on international-vs-domestic gas spreads (~12,199 TBtu notional, much Level 3). Creates headline risk and complicates valuation; not a cash or solvency issue.
- **LNG supply glut / re-contracting risk:** The most substantive forward risk — pressures uncontracted realizations and new-SPA terms as the market loosens.
- **Trade policy:** USTR Section 301 rules mandate an escalating share of U.S. LNG exports on U.S.-built vessels (1% by 2029 → 15% by 2047), a structural cost/logistics risk; China-linked vessel fees deferred one year (Nov 2025).
- **Property-tax incentive roll-off:** Ad valorem incentives at both terminals begin expiring 2026/2027 (FY2025 ad valorem ~$89M); a future cost headwind of uncertain magnitude.
- **EPC/financing execution** on the large pre-FID SPL (~20 mtpa) and CCL (~24 mtpa) expansions.
- **Counterparty concentration:** ~30 long-term third-party customers; Customer A = 18% of receivables; default/force-majeure termination is a tail risk.
- **Regulatory dependency:** FERC/DOE gate all expansion; methane charge deferred to 2034 under OBBBA; EU methane import rules loom.

Risk factors were not materially changed in the Q1 2026 10-Q. We score risk 5.5 — the supply-glut and re-contracting risks are real and partly underappreciated relative to the contracted-floor narrative, and the unconfirmed April 6, 2026 Item 5.02 departure remains an open governance item.

### Options Market Signal

The options chain is pricing near-term stress that does not match the fundamental picture, suggesting it is largely event/expiry-driven. Near-term ATM IV is 43.7% vs 27.1% one-year HV (rich, 16.6pp spread, 63rd percentile), and the term structure is in backwardation (43.7% near vs 30.3% long). ATM IV jumped from 31.4% to 43.7% over three days while the price fell 5.0%, and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.43 to 0.92. The near-week skew is sharply call-heavy (-12.6% at the 1-week tenor; -41.0% chain-wide) with a P/C volume ratio of 0.68, so the elevated near-term vol looks like a one-day expiry artifact (1-day tenor, 2.77 P/C vol ratio) rather than directional put accumulation; the 1m–6m skews are only mildly negative (-1.6% to -4.3%). Net: the market is pricing a short-term move, not a structural bearish tilt, and the call-heavy bias is mildly constructive. The rich near-term IV argues against buying premium here; for equity holders it does not change the thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300–340).** Corpus Christi Stage 3 fully ramps, SPL Train 7 reaches FID with attached long-term contracts, the ~90% contracted base compounds with a shrinking share count (~$9.7B buyback authorization), FDDEI lowers the cash-tax rate to ~14%, and Europe's structural pull on U.S. LNG plus the 2040 supply gap keep replacement contracts economic. Consensus targets ($303 mean, $340 high) are realized as volume and expansion optionality re-rate the franchise.

**Bear case (~$185–200, roughly -13% to -19%).** The supply-growth phase deepens, spot margins on uncontracted volumes compress, new SPAs sign on weaker terms, expansion FIDs slip on financing/regulatory friction, ad valorem incentives roll off, and the U.S.-built-vessel mandate adds cost. At a 34x trailing multiple, multiple compression toward the 52-week low ($186.20) is the natural landing if growth optionality is discounted.

The asymmetry is modest in both directions: a contracted floor limits downside, but a full-ish multiple and glut-exposed marginal economics limit clean upside without an FID catalyst.

**On management alignment (audit point).** The signals are mixed and we treat them as net-neutral, not net-constructive. The company repurchased stock at a ~$221.55 average in 2025, below the current price. Against that, three C-suite officers (CFO Davis ~29,000 shares at ~$300; CCO Feygin ~40,000 at ~$291; CLO Markowitz ~22,000 at ~$291) sold near the March highs. The buy-low/sell-high contrast is exactly what a skeptic flags: the company bought back stock while insiders personally lightened into strength. These appear to be planned/diversification sales (no clustered open-market buying offsets them, and the stock has since fallen ~24% from those levels), and CFO Davis signed the May 2026 10-Q, so he remains in place. But the April 6, 2026 Item 5.02 departure is unconfirmed, and "no open-market insider buying" plus C-suite selling near the top is a reason to read the buyback as capital-return policy rather than a high-conviction internal valuation signal. We therefore mark Management Quality at 5.8 — competent, aligned on pay-for-performance (CEO comp-actually-paid fell from $62.0M to $24.6M as TSR marked down), but with insider behavior that argues caution rather than confidence.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).** Reassessment timeline: next earnings and the SPL Expansion FID decision expected 2026/2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. SPL or CCL Expansion reaches full FID with attached long-term, take-or-pay SPAs at economics that hold up in the supply-growth environment.
2. Price pullback to $200–215 on intact fundamentals (rising estimates, raised guidance, IG ratings stable), improving the contracted-cash-flow yield to a level that creates asymmetric upside.
3. Broader analyst coverage confirms the forward EPS path on the contracted base (rather than the thin 3–4 analyst panel), validating that the rising estimates are durable, not spot-driven.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A confirmed re-contracting event at materially below-historical liquefaction fees, signaling the glut is impairing the take-or-pay base.
2. Expansion FID slippage combined with a cut to full-year guidance.
3. Credit-rating downgrade or a covenant/DSCR distribution restriction at SPL/CCH that interrupts cash upstreaming.

**Trajectory note.** Our score has drifted from 7.0 (May 29) to 6.1 here, driven by Growth Prospects (-1.7), Competitive Position (-1.5), and Valuation (-1.2). New data confirms rather than reverses that drift: the franchise is unchanged, but the supply-growth phase the 10-K flags and the recognition that the cheap-multiple framing rests on a thin, partly-spot-driven consensus both warrant the lower marks. The recommendation has been HOLD four times; we keep it, but the bar to upgrade is now an attached-contract FID or a cheaper price.

## Conclusion

Cheniere is a high-quality, investment-grade LNG infrastructure franchise with a deeply contracted, take-or-pay cash-flow base, a credible volume-growth pipeline, and disciplined capital return. The Q1 2026 GAAP "loss" is non-cash derivative noise, cash generation is positive and growing, and the ~24% pullback has come on improving fundamentals, which improves the entry.

We rate it HOLD rather than BUY for a specific reason: the valuation is not the slam-dunk a ~13x forward-multiple framing suggests. At ~34x reported trailing earnings and a ~5.1% FCF yield, the stock is fair-to-slightly-rich, supported by the contracted floor rather than by cheapness, and the rising-estimate tailwind leans partly on the uncontracted/spot economics most exposed to the LNG supply glut the company itself flags. The $303 mean analyst target offers meaningful upside if the volume ramp and expansion optionality re-rate the name, and a disciplined investor is well-served accumulating toward $200–215 or upgrading on a contracted-FID catalyst. Until one of those arrives, the balance of contracted strength against a full multiple and glut-pressured marginal economics keeps this a HOLD.
