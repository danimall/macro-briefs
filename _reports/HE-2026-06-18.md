**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hawaiian Electric Industries (HEI) is a special-situation utility working through the financial aftermath of the August 2023 Lahaina wildfires, which killed 102 people and triggered a ~$1.92B pre-tax settlement liability against a company that, at the time, had a market value barely larger. Over the past 18 months HEI sold American Savings Bank, wound down its Pacific Current renewables portfolio, suspended its common dividend, and raised dilutive equity, transforming itself into a pure-play vertically integrated electric utility serving ~95% of Hawaii's population. The investment question has shifted from whether the company survives the litigation, which is substantially answered, to the per-share cost of financing the remaining settlement obligations.

The de-risking has accelerated. The first $479M settlement installment was paid April 10, 2026; the global release became effective and the ~20,000+ tort suits are being dismissed; Moody's upgraded HEI to Ba2 (from Ba3) and HECO to Ba1 (from Ba2), each a one-notch move, on April 21, 2026, with S&P at B+/Positive; utility book ROACE recovered to ~10% versus 9.5% authorized; and HECO resumed upstream dividends to the parent ($11M in Q1 2026). The legal overhang of a $47.8M securities class action and $100M derivative settlement is being cleared at near-zero net cash cost, both funded by D&O insurance. Three more equal ~$479M installments are due in April 2027, 2028, and 2029, and HEI has not yet secured the financing, which management frames in going-concern language. The company holds $1.51B of total available liquidity, which more than covers the first installment, and has ~10 months until that deadline plus credible non-dilutive levers (Act 258 securitization, MRP2 rate relief, state co-funding under Acts 191/301).

At $13.11 the stock carries a ~$2.26B market cap against $1.64B of book equity that already reflects the full ~$1.9B wildfire charge. The most-feared outcomes — bankruptcy, or another deeply dilutive raise — are substantially priced in. That is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID: the bearish thesis is not a fresh, unpriced catalyst, and the trajectory of every observable milestone is improving. The setup is binary and catalyst-dependent, which is why it is not yet a BUY. We rate HE a HOLD (uncertain rationale) and would upgrade on a confirmed non-dilutive financing path.

## Company Overview

HEI is now a single-segment regulated electric utility. The operating subsidiary, Hawaiian Electric Company (HECO), and its subsidiaries Hawaii Electric Light and Maui Electric serve roughly 475,000 customer accounts across five island grids (Oahu, Maui, Hawaii Island, Molokai, Lanai). The utility is HEI's sole reportable segment; an "All other" bucket contains corporate, a residual 9.9% stake in American Savings Bank, the GLST1 wildfire-payment SPV, and Pacific Current's last asset (Mahipapa biomass, held for sale).

The divestiture program is essentially complete: American Savings Bank sold December 31, 2024 (HEI retains 9.9%); Hamakua Energy sold March 2025; the PC Opco solar/BESS portfolio sold August 2025; Mahipapa pending. The March 23, 2026 PUC approval suspending Affiliate Transaction Requirements, conditioned on HEI divesting all remaining non-utility assets and not pursuing new diversification, formalizes the pure-play structure and enabled the "dual-hatted executive" leadership consolidation effective June 1, 2026.

HECO operates as a regulated monopoly under a Performance-Based Regulation framework with revenue decoupling, which insulates the utility from volume declines. The only real demand-side competition is customer self-generation: rooftop solar already covers ~44% of single-family homes on the major islands and ~24% of total customers. Hawaii carries a statutory 100% renewable portfolio standard by 2045 (36.8% achieved in 2025, ahead of interim targets) and the nation's highest electricity costs given heavy oil dependence.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Q1 2026 consolidated revenue was $746.4M, essentially flat versus $744.1M in Q1 2025 (utility revenue +$5.7M on rate adjustments, offset by the shrinking "All other" segment). Net income for common was $30.5M ($0.18/sh diluted) versus $26.7M ($0.15), but the improvement came from reduced losses in the wind-down segment — utility net income actually fell from $47.8M to $35.3M on higher O&M, storm, and insurance costs. Trailing utility book ROACE recovered to ~10%, above the 9.5% authorized, signaling operational normalization. Maui Electric remains a laggard at 2.66% book ROACE on under-recovered investment.

**Balance sheet — the dominant story.** Total assets are $8.91B against total equity of $1.64B (up from $1.48B at YE 2024 as the retained-earnings deficit narrows). The wildfire liability stands at ~$1.92B pre-tax, split into $479M current (now paid) and $1.44B noncurrent. Total long-term debt is $2.41B; the consolidated capital structure is 59% debt / 41% equity. Book equity already absorbs the full wildfire charge — the balance sheet reflects the worst-case economic hit, not an unrecognized contingency.

**Liquidity.** Cash of $453M plus $479M restricted (held in GLST1 for the first installment, now disbursed) and total available liquidity of **$1.51B** (cash + $518M utility credit + $535M HEI credit/ATM). This figure is decisive for the central thesis and is frequently omitted in bearish framings of the name. Against ~$479M per annual installment, existing liquidity covers a full installment before any securitization, equity issuance, or operating cash flow, with the first deadline ~10 months out (April 2027).

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $61M; capex was $103.5M and rising. The common dividend remains suspended since Q3 2023. HECO resumed upstream dividends to HEI ($10M/quarter in 2025, $11M in Q1 2026), a signal of subsidiary health, though the parent does not yet pass this through. The capex program runs ~$2B over 2026-2028 (2026 guided $550M-$700M), centered on the ~$908M Waiau repower (253 MW of fuel-flexible firm generation; six GE Vernova turbines contracted April 28, 2026; in-service 2029-2033, with PUC-approved cost recovery up to $847M plus inflation via EPRM).

A note on data quality: standardized screening tools (Altman Z, Piotroski) returned insufficient data, and XBRL pulls are unreliable here because of the bank divestiture distorting historical comparability and the non-recurring wildfire liability. The financials must be read from the filings directly.

## Growth Analysis

HEI's growth is a rate-base story constrained by balance-sheet repair. The ~$2B 2026-2028 capex program (Waiau repower, grid hardening, wildfire resilience) is the engine of future rate-base earnings, supported by an unusually constructive regulatory posture. The MRP2 rate re-basing filed March 6, 2026 seeks a $170M target-revenue increase ($125M year one, $45M year two) for the period starting January 1, 2027. Waiau cost recovery is approved through the EPRM mechanism. With decoupling protecting revenue from volume declines and a 100% RPS mandate driving a multi-decade investment cycle, the underlying utility has a credible mid-single-digit rate-base CAGR once the financing question is settled.

The constraint is per-share growth, not enterprise growth. The ~$1.44B of remaining settlement installments competes directly for capital with the growth program at the moment the company most needs to deploy capital into rate base. If the installments are funded with equity, per-share earnings growth gets diluted even as rate base expands. If funded via Act 258 securitization (recovering wildfire/resilience costs through a non-bypassable customer charge), the per-share trajectory is materially better. The growth thesis is therefore real but conditional on the financing path, which is why the score sits in the middle of the range rather than higher.

## Valuation Assessment

With company profile, price history, and analyst target data unavailable from standard feeds, valuation must be anchored to balance-sheet and asset-based reference points. At $13.11 on ~172.6M shares, the equity is worth ~$2.26B versus $1.64B of book equity. The stock trades at roughly 1.4x a fully-impaired book value, a modest premium that prices in the going-concern tail while still crediting the franchise as a going concern. This is the single most important valuation observation and it cuts against the bear case: the worst-case outcomes (bankruptcy wiping out equity, or another deeply dilutive raise near book) are substantially discounted, not unrecognized.

On earnings, the picture is muddied by the wildfire charges and bank divestiture, but a normalized utility earning ~10% book ROACE on ~$1.64B of equity implies normalized utility earnings power in the rough range of ~$160-180M, which against the current cap is a low-double-digit multiple on stabilized earnings — not demanding for a regulated utility, but appropriate given the financing overhang.

We deliberately avoid treating the depressed multiple as a buy signal in isolation. A compressed valuation on a name with a genuine going-concern disclosure can be a value trap if the dilution scenario plays out near current prices. The asymmetry here is two-sided: securitization and rate relief offer a path to re-rating toward a normalized utility multiple (meaningful upside), while a forced, dilutive raise of the magnitude of the September 2024 precedent (62.2M shares at a depressed price) would pressure per-share value (meaningful downside). With no clear catalyst yet confirming which path wins, the valuation supports a HOLD — the price reflects the uncertainty rather than mispricing it in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

HECO is a regulated monopoly with no direct competitor for grid service. The relevant competitive dynamic is customer self-generation, where rooftop solar penetration erodes volume, but revenue decoupling under PBR neutralizes the earnings impact. The trajectory of the competitive position is improving rather than deteriorating: the PUC's 2024 "Inclinations" explicitly endorse utility ownership of new generation "when such ownership stabilizes utility finances," and the suspension of Affiliate Transaction Requirements simplifies the corporate structure.

The state functions as a partner, not just a regulator. Act 191 provides a state "step-in" mechanism for utility financial distress; Act 258 authorizes securitization and directs study of a wildfire relief fund and liability cap; Act 301 funds the state's share of the settlement. JERA's non-binding partnership with the State (October 2025) and its March 2026 proposal for a ~500 MW LNG-supported hybrid plant on Oahu, with potential equity participation, represents a source of generation capital outside HE's balance sheet (though JERA is a potential developer/investor, not a HECO subsidiary). This constructive regulatory and statutory backdrop differentiates HE favorably from PG&E's adversarial pre-bankruptcy environment.

## Risk Assessment

**Financing the remaining $1.44B (central risk).** Three more ~$479M installments are due April 2027, 2028, and 2029, and HEI has not yet secured the financing, instead working with advisors on a plan. Management explicitly frames failure as a going-concern risk that could trigger default, debt acceleration, and bankruptcy absent lender waivers. This is the risk that justifies the depressed multiple. We weight it as material but not terminal: available liquidity covers the first installment, the runway is ~10 months, full-year operating cash flow accrues in between, and the financing toolkit (securitization, rate relief, asset sales, capex deferral, equity) is broad.

**Dilution.** The $250M ATM is registered but undrawn. Funding installments materially above that figure likely means further equity issuance, with the September 2024 raise (62.2M shares, ~$558M net at a low price) as the dilutive precedent. This is the most credible path to per-share value impairment short of bankruptcy.

**Residual wildfire liability.** ~80 plaintiffs have not signed releases; a $500M holdback fund is reserved for opt-outs. Liabilities exceeding settlement amounts "could be material" and could breach covenants.

**Insurance recovery shortfall.** Only ~$9M of excess-liability coverage remains; HEI is litigating its own property insurers (suit filed February 13, 2026; mediation set July 8, 2026). Recoveries are uncertain.

**Cost-recovery uncertainty.** PUC authorized deferral (an $80.5M regulatory asset) of incremental wildfire costs but has not approved recovery — a separate future proceeding. The Utilities have committed customers will not bear the $1.9B settlement, so shareholders absorb it.

**Concentration and macro.** Single-state exposure to Hawaii tourism (Q1 2026 passenger counts -2.7% y/y; Japanese visitors -38.6% vs. 2019), high fuel dependence, and extreme-weather risk (FEMA reclassified part of Waiau into a flood zone effective June 10, 2026).

**Improving credit trajectory (a mitigant the bear case understates).** The April 21, 2026 upgrades are evidence that financing access is widening, not narrowing, into the funding window. These upgrades remain within the below-investment-grade range; the credit profile is improving but has not crossed into investment grade. Treating below-investment-grade as a static negative misses the directional signal; rating agencies are pricing improving, not deteriorating, fundamentals.

### Options Market Signal

The options snapshot shows the put/call open-interest ratio rising from 0.65 to 0.82 as the price fell from $15.06 to $13.11 (-12.9%) between early May and mid-June 2026. The increase in put positioning is consistent with hedging into the unresolved financing question and reflects two-sided uncertainty rather than one-directional bearish conviction. There is no extreme skew or unusual volume that would override the fundamental read; it confirms the market is pricing elevated uncertainty around the 2027 funding catalyst, which the valuation already reflects.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** HEI executes a non-dilutive or low-dilution financing path — primarily Act 258 securitization recovering wildfire/resilience costs via a non-bypassable customer charge — funding the remaining installments without a PG&E-style equity event. The MRP2 $170M rate increase lands, utility ROACE holds at ~10%, the common dividend is eventually reinstated, and the credit ratings continue to climb toward investment grade. The stock re-rates from ~1.4x impaired book toward a normalized regulated-utility multiple, with the ~$2B capex program driving rate-base earnings growth. In this scenario the equity is worth meaningfully more than the current price as the going-concern discount unwinds.

**Bear case.** Securitization stalls or proves insufficient, and HEI funds the 2027-2029 installments through dilutive equity at depressed prices, expanding the share count materially and capping per-share value even as the enterprise survives. Residual wildfire liabilities exceed the settlement, insurance recoveries disappoint, or covenant pressure forces emergency action. In the tail scenario, financing failure triggers the going-concern outcome management has disclosed. Downside is bounded toward — and potentially through — book value, but the existence of $1.51B of liquidity and state backstops makes the catastrophic tail less probable than the disclosure language alone implies.

The decisive point for the recommendation: the bear case is largely discounted at $13.11. The market cap sits only ~38% above a book value that already absorbs the full charge, the most-feared dilution precedent is known and partly priced, and every observable milestone (installment paid, suits dismissed, ratings upgraded, ROACE normalized, upstream dividend resumed) points toward de-escalation. An AVOID rating would require demonstrating that the downside is unpriced and that the business faces structural impairment, secular decline, or governance breakdown — none of which is present. This is a resolving crisis with a measurable downside floor and credible non-dilutive paths, consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The next checkpoints are: the Q2 2026 10-Q (financing-plan progress, insurer-litigation/mediation outcome from July 8), the MRP2 rate-case decision path into the January 1, 2027 rate period, and any announcement of the financing structure for the April 2027 installment. The ~10-month runway to the first deadline means the catalyst window is concrete and monitorable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY).**
- A confirmed non-dilutive or low-dilution financing path for the 2027-2029 installments — most decisively, executed or PUC-approved Act 258 securitization covering a material share of the remaining $1.44B.
- MRP2 awarded at or near the $170M target, combined with utility ROACE sustained at or above 9.5% authorized.
- A further credit upgrade or reinstatement of the common dividend, signaling the parent views the balance sheet as stabilized.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).**
- An announced equity raise of a magnitude comparable to or exceeding the September 2024 precedent (≥50M shares) at prices near or below current levels, without offsetting securitization.
- Material adverse development in residual wildfire liability (opt-out claims exceeding the $500M holdback) or a covenant breach.
- A renewed going-concern emphasis in filings, or a credit downgrade reversing the April 2026 trajectory.

## Conclusion

HEI is a post-crisis utility whose existential risk is resolving faster than its depressed valuation implies. The first installment is paid, the tort suits are being dismissed, ratings have been upgraded one notch each at the parent and the utility, ROACE is back above authorized, and HECO is again sending cash upstream. The remaining ~$1.44B in 2027-2029 settlement payments are not yet financed and likely entail some dilution, but available liquidity covers the first installment, the runway is ~10 months, and credible non-dilutive levers exist. At $13.11 — ~1.4x a book value that already absorbs the full charge — the going-concern fear is substantially priced, leaving a two-sided, catalyst-dependent setup. The asymmetry is not clear enough to warrant a BUY before the financing path is confirmed, and the discount is too well-established and the trajectory too clearly improving to justify an AVOID. We rate HE a **HOLD** and would move to BUY on confirmation of a non-dilutive financing structure or a favorable MRP2 outcome.
