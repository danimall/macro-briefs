**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Arch Capital is a Bermuda-domiciled specialty (re)insurer running three underwriting engines — insurance (~44% of net premium written), reinsurance (~43%), and U.S./international mortgage insurance (~13%) — backed by ~$26.9B of total capital and a conservatively positioned, AA-rated investment portfolio. The franchise is high quality: debt/total capital is only 10.1%, FY2025 operating ROAE was ~17.1%, book value per share grew 22.6% in 2025 to $65.11 and reached $66.19 at Q1 2026, and the company generated $6.2B of operating cash flow in 2025. Management repurchased $783M of stock in Q1 2026 and authorized a fresh $3.0B buyback in April, with a director adding to his stake in the open market near $94.

The complication is timing. Arch is rolling off a multi-year hard market, and the data confirm a turn. Reinsurance net premium fell ~6% in Q1 2026 and property-catastrophe premium dropped ~36% as the company walked away from inadequate January-renewal pricing (property-cat rates down an estimated 10–20%). Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every horizon — current year revised down 1.2% and next year down 2.2% over 90 days — and Q1 2026 produced the first EPS miss (-1.1%) after three consecutive beats of 9–21%. The headline 81.7% consolidated combined ratio was flattered by a benign catastrophe quarter (reinsurance cat load 5.4 points vs 21.7 a year earlier) and $220M of favorable prior-year reserve development, neither of which is a repeatable earnings source. Underlying ROE is normalizing from the 18–20% peak toward mid-teens.

We move ACGL to HOLD after four prior BUY ratings. The downgrade reflects a deterioration in forward earnings momentum, while business quality is unchanged. At ~9.6x current-year forward EPS ($9.27) and ~1.35x book, the stock trades roughly in line with its P&C peer median (9.2x), so there is no specific mispricing for a BUY to exploit while near-term revisions point down. We retain a constructive medium-term view on book-value compounding and the buyback, and would upgrade on stabilizing estimates or a more attractive entry.

## Company Overview

Arch operates across Bermuda, the U.S., U.K., Europe, Canada, and Australia. The three segments:

- **Insurance (~44% of NPW):** Specialty commercial lines — E&S casualty, financial/professional, property and short-tail specialty, workers' comp, commercial auto/multi-peril, construction, and programs. The book was materially expanded by the August 2024 acquisition of Allianz's U.S. MidCorp & Entertainment business (MCE, $450M cash).
- **Reinsurance (~43% of NPW):** Worldwide casualty, specialty, property cat, property ex-cat, and marine/aviation.
- **Mortgage (~13% of NPW):** U.S. primary MI, GSE credit-risk transfer, and international MI (Australia/Europe). This is a high-margin, diversifying earnings engine that ran a ~22% combined ratio in Q1 2026.

The durable competitive trait is disciplined cycle management: reallocating capital toward the best risk-adjusted lines and shrinking where pricing is inadequate. The leadership is underwriting-centric and long-tenured (CEO Nicolas Papadopoulo, CFO François Morin). Arch was added to the IAIG (Internationally Active Insurance Group) list in 2024, which raises group-wide regulatory scrutiny.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year hard-market growth, now inflecting.** Revenue rose from $9.6B (2022) to $13.6B (2023), $17.4B (2024), and $19.9B (2025). Net income to common was ~$4.36B in 2025 versus ~$4.27B in 2024. BVPS growth, management's primary value metric, was +22.6% in 2025.

**Profitability is excellent but normalizing.** FY2025 operating ROAE was ~17.1% (down from 18.9% in 2024); net income ROE ~20.1%. Q1 2026 net income was $1,047M ($2.88 diluted EPS, up from $1.48), with a consolidated combined ratio of 81.7% and segment combined ratios of Insurance 96.5% (improved from 100.1%), Reinsurance 75.9% (vs 91.8%), and Mortgage 22.3%.

Two tailwinds flattered Q1 2026 and should not be extrapolated:
1. **Low catastrophe quarter** — reinsurance cat load of 5.4 points vs 21.7 points in Q1 2025 (California wildfires). A normal-to-heavy cat year would add significantly to the reinsurance loss ratio.
2. **Favorable prior-year reserve development** — $220M total, with reinsurance booking 8.3 points of favorable development. This reflects conservative reserving but is a one-time benefit, and reinsurance casualty already showed $20M of *adverse* long-tail development.

**Balance sheet is a genuine strength.** Total assets $81.4B, shareholders' equity $24.2B, debt/total capital just 10.1% (debt + preferred 13.2%), senior notes $2.73B. Investable assets ~$46.8B at a short 3.43-year duration and AA-/Aa3 average quality; net reserves $25.0B. Net investment income rose to $408M in Q1 2026 (pre-tax book yield ~3.99%), with $160M of lumpy equity-method fund income on top. Operating cash flow was $1.2B in Q1.

A screening Altman Z-Score in the "distress" zone is a false signal here: the model is calibrated for industrial firms and treats insurance reserves/float as distress-level leverage. Arch's actual condition (low debt, AA portfolio, ~18% ROE, $6B+ operating cash flow) is robust.

**Capital allocation has pivoted from growth to return.** Arch repurchased $1.9B in 2025, and the Q1 2026 buyback plus April authorization signal management prefers buying its own stock at ~$89–94 to writing softening business. Cumulative repurchases since 2007 total 463M shares for $8.6B. No common dividend (2024 featured a $1.9B special dividend instead).

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has shifted from "expand into a hard market" to "shrink with discipline and compound book value." That shift is the core reason for the rating change.

Forward indicators are negative:
- **Estimate revisions are down across every period.** Current quarter -3.9% (90d), current year -1.2%, next year -2.2%. Consensus current-year EPS is $9.27 and next-year $9.90 — both drifting lower, not rising. This is the opposite of the revision velocity that flags a genuine inflection-buy.
- **Top line is contracting by choice.** Property-cat NPW fell to $307M from $477M, and insurance is roughly flat as Arch prunes weaker MCE program business.
- **The earnings beat streak broke.** After +9.3%, +20.5%, and +12.7% surprises, Q1 2026 EPS came in at $2.50 vs $2.53 consensus.

The positives that keep this above a pure value-trap: BVPS still compounds at a healthy mid-teens rate, the buyback shrinks the share count and supports per-share metrics, and the mortgage segment provides steady ~$200M+ quarterly underwriting income as cat-exposed lines soften. The near-term earnings trajectory is down, and the company itself is signaling that the best risk-adjusted use of capital is buying stock rather than growing premium. We score Growth Prospects 5.0, consistent with the decline already visible in the score trajectory (7.5 → 6.0).

## Valuation Assessment

ACGL trades at ~6.85x trailing EPS ($11.60), which overstates cheapness because trailing earnings reflect a cyclical peak. The relevant metrics are forward and book-based:
- **Forward P/E ~9.6x** on current-year consensus; ~9.0x on next year.
- **Price-to-book ~1.35x** on Q1 2026 BVPS, for a business with mid-teens-and-falling ROE.

Against the P&C peer set, this is roughly fair rather than cheap:

WRB's 13.8x premium reflects its specialty mix, but the broader cohort is also at cycle-peak earnings, so a multiple in line with peers does not embed asymmetric upside.

The analyst range is $93 (low) to $125 (high), mean $108.92, median $110 — about 22% above the current $89.16. Normally we treat consensus targets as floors on cheap, inflecting stocks. Here the targets are anchored to estimates that are *falling*, and the stock trades near peer-fair value, so the target gap reflects optimism about a soft-landing in the cycle rather than a clear mispricing. The honest read is fair value with modest upside if estimates stabilize, downside if the cycle softens faster. Valuation scores 6.0 — reasonable for the quality, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Arch competes with global specialty (re)insurers (RenaissanceRe, Everest, Axis, Markel, the Bermuda cohort, large reinsurers) and, in mortgage, with MGIC, Radian, Essent, NMI, and the FHA. The defining industry dynamic is the exit from a multi-year hard market: abundant traditional and alternative (ILS) capital is pushing property-cat and short-tail rates down 10–20%, while casualty pricing remains more disciplined.

Arch's structural edges are real:
- **Three-engine diversification.** The mortgage segment's ~22% combined ratio and steady underwriting income are genuine ballast as cat lines soften.
- **Demonstrated discipline.** Cutting property-cat premium ~36% into a soft market is the behavior of a franchise willing to defend margins over share — historically the right call through a cycle.
- **Underwriting-led culture and conservative reserving.** Repeated favorable development supports the quality of the book.

The trajectory is stable-to-defensive rather than share-gaining, which is appropriate at this point in the cycle but does not constitute the "improving competitive position the market hasn't priced" that would warrant a higher score. Competitive Position scores 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for this company at this point in the cycle:

1. **Cyclical inflection (central thesis risk).** Management states conditions "have become more competitive." As the hard market fades, the 18–20% peak ROE is likely to compress toward mid-teens, and falling estimates are already tracking that path.
2. **Catastrophe volatility.** Peak modeled exposure is a Florida Tri-County windstorm with net PML ~$1.9B (8.2% of tangible equity); the 1-in-250-year zonal cap is ~25% of tangible equity. A heavy cat year would sharply lift the currently benign loss ratios.
3. **Long-tail reserve adequacy.** Net reserves are $25B. Recent development is favorable overall, but the 2022–2024 casualty underwriting years carry social-inflation risk, and Q1 already showed $20M of adverse reinsurance casualty development.
4. **Tax regime.** The Q1 2026 effective rate of 8.6% (from 17.4%) is a near-term tailwind from Bermuda refundable tax credits and should not be extrapolated. The Bermuda 15% CIT and OECD Pillar II structurally raise Arch's tax burden over time, with explicit risk of a partial write-off of the ~$1.4B net deferred tax asset under evolving OECD guidance.
5. **Mortgage/GSE policy risk.** PMIERs sufficiency was 175% (pro-forma ~173% under phasing-in deductions). GSE-privatization discussion, FHA premium competition, Basel III Endgame, and Australia's expanded First Home Guarantee Scheme all threaten MI volumes; persistency slipped to 80.7%.
6. **Intercompany concentration and governance.** Somers is Arch's largest reinsurance recoverable (~$2.0B), with Greysbridge put/call mechanics settling in 2026. A classified board, supermajority provisions, and a 9.9% U.S. voting cap entrench the board and could deter a takeover premium.

Most of these are known and at least partially priced; the active risk is the cyclical earnings normalization. Risk Factors score 6.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 26.5% versus 21.0% trailing realized — rich by ~5.4 points, with a flat term structure (near 26.5% ≈ far 27.6%), indicating the market prices evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a single dated catalyst. The shift is in positioning: the put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.26 to 4.23 as the stock fell 7.4% over the past week, and put/call volume sits at 6.75. This is meaningful put accumulation into a declining price. It corroborates the cautious near-term read and argues against chasing the stock here; it does not signal a fundamental break, but it confirms that informed flow is hedging downside rather than positioning for a bounce. The implied ±4.5% move into the June expiry is modest.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110–125)
Arch's combined ratio holds in the low-to-mid 80s as casualty discipline offsets property-cat softening, the mortgage book continues delivering ~$200M+ quarterly underwriting income, and net investment income keeps rising on a $46.8B portfolio. The $3.0B buyback shrinks share count over time, supporting double-digit EPS compounding even with mid-teens ROE. BVPS compounds higher over 12–18 months; at a stable book multiple or normalized earnings multiple, the stock reaches $110–125, matching the high end of the analyst range. Falling estimates prove to be conservative, and the next two quarters re-establish the beat pattern.

### Bear Case ($75–82)
The soft market accelerates, with property-cat and short-tail rates falling faster than expected and casualty discipline eroding industry-wide. A normal-to-heavy catastrophe year adds materially to the reinsurance loss ratio, the favorable reserve-development tailwind reverses as social inflation catches up to the 2022–2024 casualty years, and the Bermuda DTA takes a partial write-down. ROE compresses below mid-teens, estimates keep falling, and the multiple de-rates toward ALL/TRV levels (~8.5–9x on lower earnings), pushing the stock to $75–82 (roughly -8% to -16% from current, near the 52-week low of $82.44).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth_hold**: a quality compounder we want to own at the right price and with stabilizing estimates, but not one to add to today given negative near-term earnings momentum and a peer-fair multiple.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive (current-year and next-year estimates rising rather than falling over a 60–90 day window), confirming the cycle is normalizing rather than deteriorating.
- The stock retraces to ~$80–82 (the 52-week low / ~1.20x book), restoring asymmetric upside to the $108–110 analyst mean.
- Two consecutive quarters of underlying combined-ratio strength that are not reliant on below-normal catastrophe loads or outsized favorable reserve development.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Reinsurance/insurance combined ratios deteriorate materially (consolidated combined ratio above ~95% ex-catastrophe) as soft-market pricing flows through.
- Adverse long-tail casualty reserve development expands beyond the isolated $20M Q1 figure into a sustained trend.
- A material write-down of the ~$1.4B Bermuda deferred tax asset, or a GSE-policy change that structurally reduces Arch MI volumes.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, late July). The flat options term structure suggests no single near-term catalyst, so the natural reassessment is the next earnings print and the trajectory of estimate revisions into it.

**Current-price context:** Base-case fair value ~$95–100 (12-month), roughly +6–12% from $89.16; bull case $110–125 (+23–40%); bear case $75–82 (-8% to -16%). The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, which is the quantitative basis for HOLD over BUY.

## Conclusion

Arch Capital is one of the better-run specialty (re)insurers — fortress balance sheet (10.1% debt/total capital, AA portfolio), ~17% operating ROAE, 22.6% BVPS growth in 2025, disciplined cycle management, and a management team returning capital aggressively while a director buys in the open market. None of that is in dispute, and it is why the stock has earned a premium-quality reputation.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: forward earnings momentum has turned negative just as the hard market fades. The Q1 2026 EPS miss was the first in four quarters, and the headline combined-ratio strength was flattered by the benign catastrophe quarter and non-repeatable favorable reserve development. Arch walked away from softening pricing, and ROE is normalizing toward mid-teens. At a forward multiple at or slightly above the 9.2x peer median with no common dividend, there is no identifiable market error for a BUY to exploit; the market is correctly pricing a softening cycle. Surging put open interest into the decline confirms cautious near-term positioning.

We would turn more constructive on stabilizing estimates, two clean underlying-margin quarters, or a pullback toward $80–82. Until then, HOLD: own it for the book-value compounding and buyback, but do not pay up at a cyclical peak with revisions pointing down.
