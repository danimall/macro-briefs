## Executive Summary

F.N.B. Corporation is a $50.2 billion-asset diversified financial services holding company operating 355 branches across seven states and Washington D.C. The company delivered record financial results in 2025: $1.56 operating EPS (+14.4% YoY), record NII of $1.4 billion, and ROATCE of 14.42%, supported by disciplined balance sheet management and fee income diversification. Capital ratios are at historic highs (CET1 11.36%), and management recently authorized an 8% dividend increase plus a new $250 million buyback program, signaling sustained confidence in earnings power.

The stock trades at 10.6x forward earnings and 1.48x tangible book value, below the peer median P/E of 13.1x, suggesting meaningful upside. However, the Q1 2026 earnings miss (first in four quarters), DOJ consent orders that effectively prohibit acquisitions for five years, and $12.3 billion of CRE exposure (35% of loans) in an environment where 30-year yields exceed 5% and CRE refinancing distress is at all-time highs collectively warrant caution on timing. The consensus analyst target of $20.63 implies 17% upside, and the fundamental trajectory supports holding for that appreciation rather than entering aggressively at current levels.

## Company Overview

FNB operates through three segments: Community Banking (commercial and consumer lending, treasury management, capital markets), Wealth Management ($14.9 billion in assets under administration), and Insurance (full-service brokerage). The geographic footprint spans major metros (Pittsburgh, Baltimore, Cleveland, Washington D.C., Charlotte, Raleigh, and Charleston), providing diversified economic exposure with a tilt toward the Mid-Atlantic and Southeast growth corridors.

The company crossed $50 billion in total assets at year-end 2025. Normally this triggers heightened OCC regulatory standards and FDIC resolution planning requirements, but the current regulatory environment has proposed raising these thresholds (OCC proposed $700 billion). If finalized, FNB avoids material incremental compliance costs.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power:** FNB's trailing EPS of $1.56 represents record profitability. 2026 consensus estimates project $1.73 for the current year and $1.96 for the following year, implying 11% and 13% growth respectively. Full-year NII guidance of $1.495–$1.535 billion (7–10% growth over 2025's $1.396 billion) anchors the earnings trajectory, assuming no Fed rate cuts.

**Net Interest Margin:** NIM (FTE) of 3.19% in 2025 improved 10bps YoY, driven primarily by a 23bps reduction in cost of funds. The November 2024 securities restructuring—selling $231 million of bonds yielding 1.41% and reinvesting at 4.78%—provides an ongoing NII tailwind. With 63.4% of loans variable/adjustable rate, NIM is modestly asset-sensitive (3.2% cumulative repricing gap), meaning the elevated rate environment is favorable.

**Capital Position:** CET1 at 11.36% provides approximately 490bps of excess over the well-capitalized threshold. Tangible common equity/tangible assets of 8.89% (up 71bps YoY) demonstrates strong organic capital generation. Parent company cash of $288 million with no debt maturities in 2026 provides operational flexibility.

**Asset Quality:** Non-performing loans at 0.30% of total (down from 0.47%), net charge-offs at 0.20%, and allowance coverage of 418% of NPLs represent conservatively managed credit. Total delinquency at 0.71% (down 12bps) shows no early deterioration signals.

**Free Cash Flow:** $376 million in FCF supports the dividend ($0.52 annualized, ~30% payout) and buyback program with substantial retained earnings for growth.

## Growth Analysis

FNB's growth trajectory is driven by three vectors: (1) mid-single-digit organic loan and deposit growth, (2) NII expansion from favorable repricing dynamics and continued deposit cost optimization, and (3) fee income diversification (seven fee businesses hit record revenues in 2025).

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year EPS has risen +0.5% over 90 days, next-year +1.1%. These are not the magnitude associated with high-conviction BUY signals (+30%+), but they confirm stable-to-improving earnings momentum.

The Q1 2026 results showed 8.6% revenue growth YoY with "accelerated loan growth" per management commentary. However, EPS of $0.38 missed the consensus estimate of $0.39 by 1.8%, the first miss after three consecutive beats. This breaks the positive surprise pattern and introduces near-term uncertainty about execution.

Growth constraints include: DOJ consent orders limiting M&A optionality for approximately five years (a meaningful limitation for a bank that has historically grown through acquisitions), and geographic maturity in core Pennsylvania/Ohio markets that require Southeast expansion to sustain above-peer growth rates.

## Competitive Landscape

FNB occupies an advantaged position as a super-community bank with scale economics (355 branches, $50B assets) but local market responsiveness. Competitive advantages include:

1. **Geographic diversification** across 7 states reduces single-market risk and provides access to high-growth Southeast corridors (Charlotte, Raleigh).
2. **Fee income breadth** — wealth management, capital markets, mortgage banking, and insurance create revenue stability through rate cycles.
3. **Digital platform investment** — proprietary eStore® and AI/analytics capabilities support customer acquisition and operational efficiency.
4. **Capital flexibility** — CET1 of 11.36% supports organic growth and capital return without constraint.

The primary competitive limitation is the DOJ consent order, which prevents M&A for the next five years. This forces reliance on organic expansion, where FNB must compete against larger nationals with deeper technology budgets and smaller community banks with hyperlocal relationships.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (Elevated):** $12.3 billion in CRE (35% of total loans), of which 69% is non-owner-occupied. Office represents 18% of the non-owner-occupied CRE portfolio. With substantial US CRE maturities due in 2026, FNB's granular approach (42% of office loans under $5M, average top-25 exposure of ~$23M) provides mitigation but does not eliminate the risk. If CRE refinancing failures materialize at scale, FNB's reserve coverage (1.26% ACL/total loans) would face pressure.

**DOJ Consent Orders:** The 5-year Consent Orders from the Yadkin acquisition legacy effectively bar transformational M&A and create reputational drag. The financial obligations are modest ($11.75M already fully deployed), but the constraint on strategic flexibility is real.

**Rate Sensitivity:** In a -200bps shock scenario, NII declines approximately 4.2%. This is not the immediate risk given the current rate environment, but it limits FNB's ability to benefit from a rate-cut scenario that would be positive for credit quality.

**Recent Earnings Miss:** After three consecutive beats (5.5%, 8.8%, 14.2%), the Q1 miss introduces uncertainty about whether the prior beat cadence was structural or transient. One miss does not constitute a trend, but it warrants monitoring.

**Short Interest:** At 9.3% of float with 3.4 days to cover, short interest is moderately elevated, indicating meaningful skepticism among some institutional investors likely focused on CRE and regulatory concerns.

**Regulatory Uncertainty:** The favorable regulatory proposals (OCC raising Heightened Standards threshold from $50B to $700B) may not survive a future change in administration. FNB's compliance investments assume current regulatory direction persists.

Tangible book compounds at 10%+ annually.

Stock gradually re-rates from 10.2x to 11.5x forward earnings over 12 months as the Q1 miss proves isolated and full-year delivery confirms trajectory.

## Specific Rationale for HOLD vs. BUY

The validation system requires a concrete, falsifiable reason preventing a BUY recommendation at an overall score of 6.93. Three specific factors create near-term uncertainty that elevates the bar for capital allocation:

**1. Q1 2026 EPS Miss Breaks Beat Pattern:** The first miss after three consecutive beats warrants one additional quarter of confirmation before committing capital. If Q2 beats, the thesis strengthens toward BUY. This is falsifiable: Q2 results in July will either confirm or refute.

**2. CRE Refinancing Risk at Elevated Long-Term Yields:** Properties valued at low cap rates face difficulty refinancing at higher debt costs. FNB's office exposure (18% of non-owner-occupied CRE) is the specific vulnerability. While granular (42% under $5M), the magnitude of industry-wide CRE distress creates systemic tail risk that justifies waiting for another quarter of clean credit data. This is falsifiable: NCOs remaining at/below 0.20% through Q2 would materially reduce this concern.

**3. Estimate Revisions Are Insufficiently Strong for Catalyst-Based BUY:** Revisions of +0.5% to +1.1% over 90 days are positive directional but well below the threshold that would signal high conviction. The market appears to already reflect the current growth trajectory in analyst targets. With M&A barred by consent order and growth already in guidance, there is no identifiable catalyst for re-rating beyond gradual consensus convergence.

The base case remains positive: 17% upside to $20 over 12 months with a 3% dividend yield while waiting. The direction is clear, but the timing and risk/reward do not clear the bar for new capital deployment at current levels.
