**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kratos is a defense-technology company executing an "affordability is a technology" strategy: building low-cost, mass-producible systems (jet-powered attritable drones, small turbojet/turbofan engines, hypersonic rocket motors, satellite ground software) to disrupt or subcontract for legacy primes. The growth story is genuine and accelerating. Q1 FY26 revenue rose 22.6% YoY to $371.0M, the Unmanned segment grew 30.9%, management raised FY26 revenue guidance to $1.7–1.76B, and the company has beaten EPS estimates four straight quarters. The demand backdrop is among the strongest in the sector: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act added $156B for defense, FY26 national-security spend approaches $1 trillion, and the FY27 request seeks ~$1.5T with explicit priorities in missile defense, unmanned systems, hypersonics, and propulsion, matching Kratos's product map.

The problem is what you pay for that story and how the growth is financed. At ~$10.9B market cap on ~$1.35B trailing revenue (~8x sales) and a 342x trailing P/E, the stock prices flawless execution of a multi-year production ramp that has not yet inflected to positive free cash flow. Operating cash flow was negative $27.4M in Q1 FY26 (negative $29.2M a year earlier), and the growth is funded almost entirely by serial equity issuance: three secondary offerings in 15 months ($18 → $38.50 → $84 per share) lifting share count ~24% to 187.4M. The balance sheet looks fortress-like ($1.46B cash, zero long-term debt, Altman Z ~4.7), but that cash is the product of dilution, and the equity is the financing mechanism because the business consumes cash.

The defining near-term signal is that the stock fell on a beat-and-raise quarter in May, with at least six analysts cutting price targets, while the shares now sit at $57.75, down 57% from the $134 January high. Estimate revisions are negative (current-quarter EPS revised down 22.9% over 90 days), GAAP EPS ($0.07) was less than half the non-GAAP figure ($0.16), and there is no insider buying at these levels, only programmatic selling. The growth thesis is intact; the question is whether the market has already paid for it. We rate KTOS HOLD.

## Company Overview

Kratos operates two reportable segments:

- **Kratos Government Solutions (KGS)** — ~78% of revenue ($288.4M of $371M in Q1 FY26, +20.4% YoY; ~11.8% organic with the balance from acquired Nomad and Orbit revenue). Includes microwave electronics, space/satellite/cyber ground systems, training, C5ISR, turbine technologies (jet engines), and Defense & Rocket Support Services (DRSS), which houses the fast-growing hypersonics/rocket business (Zeus and Oriole solid rocket motors, Erinyes hypersonic flight systems).
- **Unmanned Systems (US)** — ~22% of revenue ($82.6M, +30.9% YoY). Built around the XQ-58A Valkyrie jet-powered attritable drone, plus unmanned ground/seaborne and tactical drone systems.

The strategy is to be first-to-market with proven (not bleeding-edge) engineering at low cost, winning on volume rather than pricing power. The company recognizes most revenue over time on a cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion basis. U.S. Government customers represent ~69% of revenue including foreign military sales; foreign customers ~21%, with Israel now material at ~16% of long-lived assets following the Orbit acquisition.

CEO Eric DeMarco is long-tenured and serves as the chief operating decision maker; Deanna Lund is CFO. Fiscal year ends the last Sunday of December.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth is real and broad-based.** FY2025 revenue grew ~18.5% to ~$1.35B; net income rose from $16.4M to $22.0M. The earnings-surprise record is consistent (10–17% surprises). Backlog reached $2.011B ($1.457B funded) at Q1 FY26, up from $1.508B a year earlier, with ~$184M added via acquisitions and ~37% expected to convert in FY26.

**Margins are structurally thin, and cash flow is negative.** Q1 FY26 gross margin was 24.2% (flat YoY), operating income only $4.7M (~1.3% operating margin) after $72.3M SG&A (19.5% of revenue) and $15.0M stock compensation. GAAP net income of $11.9M ($0.07 diluted) versus $4.5M a year earlier was boosted by a $7.3M stock-comp tax benefit and $4.5M net interest income on the cash pile, neither operational. The negative operating cash flow is structural: working capital is consumed by advance long-lead inventory purchases (inventory $225.7M; the company pre-bought 60 Oriole and 60 Zeus SRMs), rising unbilled receivables ($334.1M), and DSO climbing from 124 to 130 days. Capex remains significant, with the Unmanned segment alone guided to ~$35–40M.

**The balance sheet is funded entirely by equity.** Zero long-term debt after the $177.5M Term Loan A was extinguished in July 2025; the new February 2026 facility is an undrawn $300M PNC revolver with $31.2M of LCs outstanding. Cash reached $1,464.3M, up from $560.6M, after the February 2026 equity raise. Altman Z ~4.7 places it in the safe zone. Solvency is not the risk here; dilution and valuation are.

**Capital allocation is serial equity issuance.** Three large offerings in two years — February 2024 (19.2M shares at $18.00, ~$331M net), June 2025 (14.9M at $38.50, ~$556M net), February 2026 (16.4M at $84.00, ~$1.35B net) — lifted share count from ~151M at end-2024 to 187.4M. Management has raised into strength to pre-fund growth and M&A, including two Q1 FY26 acquisitions (Nomad ~$148.8M, Orbit ~$352.7M).

## Growth Analysis

The demand environment is exceptionally favorable and well-documented. FY26 appropriations of $838.7B plus OBBBA and DOE national-security funds push FY26 national-security spend toward ~$1 trillion; the April 2026 FY27 request seeks ~$1.5T, with explicit priorities in missile defense (Golden Dome), space/satellite comms, unmanned systems, hypersonics, and the nuclear triad (Sentinel ICBM). This maps directly onto Kratos's product portfolio.

Three growth engines stand out. First, Valkyrie production is targeted to scale from ~8 to ~40 units per year by early 2028 (currently a 24-unit lot build); a $447M space-related award was also referenced. Second, hypersonics inside DRSS (Zeus/Oriole SRMs, Erinyes) led KGS organic growth in Q1 and is structurally supply-constrained, exactly where the DoD is racing to add capacity. Third, propulsion: the GE Aerospace teaming agreement (a 50/50 ASC 808 risk/reward arrangement to co-develop engines for next-gen UAS and CCA-type aircraft, $33.7M revenue at zero margin to date) and the Prometheus JV with RAFAEL (~50/50, up to $175M combined committed capital, production targeted 2027) position Kratos in the munitions/propulsion supply chain.

The qualifier on growth is margin character. The affordability model implies thin gross (~24%) and low-single-digit operating margins; Kratos must win on volume and scale. Estimate revisions are moving the wrong way: current-quarter EPS has been revised down 22.9% over 90 days, next-quarter down 6.2%, and current-year down 1.4%. Next-year estimates are flat-to-marginally-up (+0.7%). The revenue ramp is real but earnings momentum is decelerating as costs, dilution, and the GAAP/non-GAAP gap weigh on the bottom line.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the thesis breaks down. KTOS trades at 342x trailing P/E, ~8x trailing sales, and roughly 76x forward earnings on the FY26 consensus of $0.76. Peer medians in Aerospace & Defense sit near 29.5x P/E (SARO 29.5x, PSN 27.4x, AIR 28.2x, HXL 64.7x; AVAV is unprofitable). On a sales basis, Kratos at ~8x is several multiples above the diversified-defense norm.

The cycle-adjusted valuation framework does not rescue this. Kratos is not at a trough in earnings recovering toward a normalized level — its margins are structurally thin by design, and the EPS estimate trend is negative. The forward P/E of ~76x already prices several years of flawless production scaling. On next-year consensus of $1.08, the stock trades at ~53x, still roughly double the peer group for a business with low-single-digit operating margins and negative free cash flow.

The valuation score must also account for ongoing dilution: even strong revenue growth gets divided across a rising share count, which may rise further if management continues its raise-into-strength pattern. The bull case requires the market to keep paying a premium-of-premiums multiple while FCF remains negative.

Analyst targets span a wide range (low $75, mean $112.2, median $112.5, high $150), all well above the current price. We treat these with caution. On a stock at ~53–76x forward earnings, analyst targets reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation; the guardrail against treating targets as floors applies squarely here. That six analysts cut targets after a beat-and-raise quarter shows even the bullish sell side is repricing the multiple downward. We score valuation 3.5: the multiple is stretched against fundamentals with no near-term FCF to justify it.

## Competitive Landscape

Kratos occupies a genuinely differentiated position as the low-cost, fast, attritable-warfare disruptor. Valkyrie, cheap mass-producible drones, small turbojet/turbofan engines "designed in" on cruise missiles and loitering munitions, and hypersonic rocket motors place it in supply-constrained niches the legacy primes are slower to address. The drone/autonomy warfare shift is a structural tailwind that favors challengers like Kratos and AeroVironment over incumbents (RTX, NOC, GD, LMT) at the margin, and the segment growth rates (Unmanned +30.9%, KGS organic ~11.8%) outpace the broad defense market.

The competitive trajectory is improving via share gains in attritable systems, expanding backlog, and partnerships (GE, RAFAEL) that embed Kratos in propulsion supply chains. The constraint is that the same affordability model that wins volume caps pricing power, leaving margins structurally thin. Kratos must execute a high-fixed-cost capacity build-out (Indiana hypersonics fab, Oklahoma turbofan plant, Michigan turbojet relocation, Israeli microwave expansion, Sentinel facility) speculatively, ahead of contracts. If awards land, operating leverage is substantial; if they slip, these become stranded costs. Competitive position is a 7: improving and structurally advantaged, but with margin and execution caveats.

## Risk Assessment

**Valuation/dilution risk (dominant).** The stock prices flawless execution. Continued equity raises dilute holders, and the equity is the financing mechanism precisely because the business burns cash.

**Negative operating cash flow is structural.** Growth consumes cash via working capital and pre-positioned inventory. The bull case requires inflection to positive FCF as production scales over the next 4–6 quarters; if the ramp slips, cash burn continues, and management explicitly flags covenant compliance on the (currently unused) revolver as a downside scenario.

**Government/budget dependency (~69% of revenue).** The MD&A details government shutdowns (Oct–Nov 2025, Feb 2026), continuing resolutions, debt-ceiling risk, and executive orders reshaping DoD procurement and the FAR with "unknown" impact. Programs can be delayed, de-funded, terminated for convenience, or awards protested.

**Speculative capacity build-out.** Multiple facilities funded ahead of contracts; if anticipated awards don't materialize, multi-year drag on cash and margins.

**Fixed-price production margin risk.** Skilled-labor shortages, wage inflation, and supply-chain disruption pressure margins on long-term firm fixed-price production contracts, the contract type dominating as Valkyrie/hypersonics move to production.

**Acquisition integration and purchase-accounting.** Goodwill jumped to $884M, intangibles to $220M; purchase-price allocations for Nomad and Orbit are provisional, and future reallocation to finite-lived intangibles will raise amortization. Orbit is an Israeli entity not historically on U.S. GAAP.

**Israel concentration.** Orbit adds ~16% of long-lived assets ($72.8M) in Israel, a fresh geopolitical exposure amid an active regional conflict.

**DCAA cost-claim exposure.** DCAA is "closely examining and questioning certain established and disclosed practices it had previously audited and accepted," worth monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and does not corroborate acute downside fear. Near-term ATM IV is 66.2% versus 71.9% one-year historical volatility (cheap, -5.7% spread), sitting at the 6th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (2.0%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.36 and OI ratio 0.50, both call-tilted. The term structure is in contango (66.2% near vs. 74.0% long-term), a normal/complacent shape. The longer-dated tenors do show mild put skew building (-5.5% at 3m, -7.0% at 6m), consistent with some hedging of the production-ramp/FCF uncertainty further out, but there is no near-term distress signal. The 226.9% → 66.2% IV collapse between snapshots reflects post-event normalization, not new information. Net read: the options market is not pricing an imminent binary, which is consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The defense recapitalization is generational and Kratos is positioned across the highest-priority categories (drones, hypersonics, propulsion, missile defense, space). Valkyrie scales toward ~40 units/year by early 2028, hypersonics production ramps, the GE and RAFAEL partnerships convert to volume, and operating leverage emerges as speculative capacity fills with awards. FCF inflects positive, the dilution stops, and the multiple is validated by a much larger earnings base. Backlog and FY26 guidance support the trajectory. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the $112–150 analyst range over 18–24 months.

**Bear Case.** The valuation already embeds the bull case. Estimate revisions are negative, the company burns cash, GAAP earnings are a fraction of non-GAAP, and management funds the business by issuing equity at ever-higher prices, diluting per-share value even as revenue grows. Speculative facilities become stranded if awards slip; fixed-price production margins compress under labor and supply-chain inflation; a government shutdown or budget reshaping delays awards. The stock fell on a beat-and-raise and has lost 57% from its January high, and six analysts cut targets; the de-rating may not be complete. Bear target $40–45 (near the prior 52-week low of $39), roughly 22–31% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY26, early August 2026) is the natural checkpoint, with the FCF inflection question carrying a 4–6 quarter horizon into 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating cash flow turns sustainably positive (two consecutive quarters), demonstrating the production ramp converts to cash rather than consuming it.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions turn positive (>0% over 90 days) alongside maintained revenue growth >20%, confirming earnings momentum is re-accelerating rather than decelerating.
- A meaningful price pullback toward the low-$40s on unchanged fundamentals, compressing the forward multiple toward the high-end peer range while leaving the growth thesis intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or revenue growth decelerating below ~10% YoY, signaling the ramp is stalling.
- Another large equity raise without a corresponding step-up in funded backlog, confirming the dilution-to-finance-burn dynamic.
- Gross margin compression below ~22% on fixed-price production scaling, or a goodwill/intangible impairment from the Nomad/Orbit deals.
- Operating cash burn widening rather than narrowing through FY26.

## Conclusion

Kratos is a real growth business in the right place at the right time: accelerating revenue, a debt-free balance sheet, a backlog at record levels, and a product map aligned with the most-funded defense priorities. None of that is in dispute. The dispute is price. At ~8x sales, 342x trailing and ~76x forward earnings, with negative free cash flow and ~24% dilution in 15 months, the stock already pays for the bull case. Earnings estimates are falling, not rising; the stock dropped on a beat-and-raise; analysts are cutting targets; and insiders are selling, not buying. The 57% drawdown from the January high reflects multiple compression on a business whose fundamentals are intact, which improves the risk/reward versus the peak but does not make ~53x next-year earnings cheap.

We rate KTOS **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale. The fundamental momentum justifies retaining a position for those who own it as a thematic long-term defense-disruptor holding, but the valuation and negative cash-flow profile do not offer the asymmetric entry required for new capital. The proof point to watch is the conversion of the Valkyrie/hypersonics ramp into positive free cash flow over the next four to six quarters. Until that inflection is visible, the price embeds an outcome the financials have not yet delivered.
