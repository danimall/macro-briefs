## Executive Summary

L3Harris Technologies is delivering on its multi-year transformation at precisely the moment the defense spending environment has shifted to its most favorable posture since the Cold War. Q1 2026 results showed 12% revenue growth, 33% EPS growth, and a 1.4x book-to-bill ratio that pushed total backlog to a record $40.7B — up 19% year-over-year. The completion of LHX NeXt (multi-year cost restructuring) is now flowing directly to operating margins, which expanded from 9.0% in FY2024 to 11.4% in Q1 2026.

The stock has pulled back 20% from its March 2026 high of $379.23 to $302.65, largely driven by general market weakness and the Iran peace deal framework reducing near-term defense sentiment. This pullback occurs against strengthening fundamentals: raised full-year guidance ($11.40-$11.60 EPS), a $1B Department of War equity investment validating the missile business, and classified program wins driving record backlog. The 21 sell-side analysts covering LHX have a consensus target of approximately $365-$384 with zero sell ratings.

The primary risk is valuation at approximately 33x trailing twelve-month P/E (using updated TTM EPS of ~$9.21 incorporating Q1 2026), but this reflects a transitional earnings base that meaningfully understates forward earnings power. On FY2027 consensus EPS of $13.66, the stock trades at 22x, reasonable for a defense company delivering mid-teens earnings growth with $40.7B in contracted backlog visibility.

## Company Overview

L3Harris Technologies operates as a "Trusted Disruptor" in defense electronics, space systems, and missile propulsion, serving customers across 100+ countries with ~45,000 employees (including 18,000 engineers/scientists). The company reorganized into three segments effective FY2026:

- **Space & Mission Systems (SMS):** 52% of Q1 revenue ($2.99B), 10.5% operating margin, $21.1B backlog. ISR, space systems, maritime, mission networks.
- **Communication & Spectrum Dominance (CSD):** 32% ($1.86B), 25.1% margin, $9.1B backlog. Tactical radios, electronic warfare, night vision.
- **Missile Solutions (MSL):** 17% ($990M), 12.5% margin, $10.5B backlog. Solid rocket motors, propulsion, guidance systems.

The business model is differentiated from pure primes (Lockheed, Northrop) through a "platform-agnostic" merchant supplier approach. L3Harris provides critical subsystems — radios, sensors, propulsion — across many platforms, giving diversified exposure to defense spending without platform-specific concentration risk. Revenue split: 75% US Government, 22% international, 3% commercial.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2022 $17.1B → FY2023 $19.4B → FY2024 $21.3B → FY2025 $21.9B → FY2026E $23.0-23.5B (guidance). The step-up from FY2022-2023 reflects the Aerojet Rocketdyne acquisition; organic growth has been running 5-7% annually, accelerating to 12% in Q1 2026.

**Earnings acceleration:** Q1 2026 EPS of $2.72 beat consensus by $0.15 (5.9% surprise), following four consecutive quarterly beats. The earnings growth rate has accelerated from low-single-digits in FY2024 to 33% YoY in Q1 2026, driven by margin expansion from LHX NeXt cost savings, operating leverage on higher volumes, and mix shift toward CSD (25%+ margins).

**Margin expansion:** Operating margin trajectory of 9.0% (FY2024) → 9.7% (FY2025) → 11.4% (Q1 2026) reflects a combination of structural improvement and certain favorable non-recurring items. Structural drivers include the elimination of LHX NeXt implementation costs (zero in FY2026 vs. $167M in FY2025), volume leverage, and mix improvement. However, Q1 2026 also benefited from a $20M favorable legal settlement in CSD and a $39M gain from monetizing legacy assets in MSL, partially offset by a $31M unfavorable EAC adjustment on a legacy naval sensor program. Segment operating margin rose 10 bps year over year (15.6% to 15.7%), while GAAP operating margin rose more substantially due to corporate cost reductions and LHX NeXt cost removal. CSD's 25.1% margin remains among the highest in defense and reflects genuine structural pricing power.

**Balance sheet:** Approximately $11.4B in total debt (including $350M commercial paper and $1.8B current portion of long-term debt, with $9.2B in long-term debt). Based on Q1 2026 reported total equity of $19.7B, debt/equity is approximately 0.6x — conservative for a defense company with $40.7B backlog and $3.1B annual OCF. The $20B goodwill balance (49% of assets) is a legacy of the L3-Harris merger and AR acquisition — all reporting units showed >40% clearance in impairment testing. Net pension overfunding of $1.2B and $657M environmental liabilities (73% government-recoverable) round out the key balance sheet items.

**Free cash flow:** FY2025 FCF of $2.68B (12.3% FCF margin) is excellent for defense. Q1 2026 OCF was -$95M, but this is a consistent seasonal pattern (billings/collections timing). Full-year FCF should be $2.5B+, supporting the $5.00/share dividend and continued buybacks.

**Capital allocation:** ~$2.1B returned to shareholders in FY2025 ($1.2B buybacks + $903M dividends) plus $600M debt reduction. The 25th consecutive annual dividend increase to $5.00/share (1.65% yield) demonstrates commitment.

## Growth Analysis

**Backlog provides strong revenue visibility.** $40.7B total backlog equals approximately 1.7–1.8x FY2026 guided revenue, with ~40% expected to convert within 12 months and ~65% within 24 months. Q1 2026 book-to-bill of 1.4x means backlog is growing faster than revenue — the growth pipeline is expanding.

**SMS backlog surge (+45% YoY to $21.1B)** is the most significant growth signal. This reflects large classified program wins that will drive SMS revenue growth for 3-5 years. The opacity of classified programs makes precise modeling difficult, but the magnitude of the backlog increase is unprecedented for this segment.

**Defense budget tailwinds are favorable and expected to be multi-year.** The US FY2027 budget request of approximately $1.5T represents the largest proposed YoY increase since WWII. Key programs aligned with L3Harris include: Golden Dome missile defense ($185B proposed program), reconciliation munitions funding ($155B proposed), and NATO's commitment to increase defense spending toward 5% of GDP. However, these remain budget requests and political commitments subject to congressional authorization, appropriations, and potential continuing resolutions. L3Harris itself notes in SEC filings that U.S. government budget and appropriations decisions are outside its control and that shutdowns or CRs can have material adverse consequences. Nonetheless, the bipartisan consensus favoring higher defense spending provides a supportive backdrop for multi-year revenue growth.

**Missile Solutions IPO creates a value-unlock catalyst.** L3Harris confidentially submitted a draft S-1 on April 29, 2026 for a proposed IPO of Missile Solutions, with expected H2 2026 timing depending on market conditions. The number of shares and price range have not yet been determined publicly. Combined with the $1B DoW equity investment, this positions MSL as a separately valued entity. L3Harris is expected to retain majority ownership, capturing the bulk of any re-rating while using IPO proceeds for capacity expansion.

**International growth (22% of revenue) has significant runway.** NATO allies have committed to significantly increasing defense spending toward 5% of GDP (up from 2%), which is expected to drive substantial incremental demand for tactical radios and electronic warfare systems over the next decade. L3Harris tactical communications and EW products are interoperable with US forces, giving them a natural advantage in allied procurement.

**Consensus estimates:** FY2026E EPS $11.71, FY2027E $13.66 — implying 37% and 17% growth respectively. Estimate revision trends are modestly negative (-1.6% current quarter, -0.4% full year over 30 days), but this appears to reflect macro anxiety rather than fundamental deterioration given the Q1 beat and guidance raise.

## Valuation Assessment

The approximately 33x trailing twelve-month P/E (using updated TTM EPS of ~$9.21 after substituting Q1 2026 for Q1 2025) appears expensive in isolation but misrepresents the opportunity. Alternatively, on FY2025 GAAP EPS of $8.53, the trailing P/E is approximately 35.5x, reflecting a transition year where LHX NeXt costs were still being absorbed and revenue growth hadn't yet fully accelerated. On forward estimates:

- FY2026E P/E: 25.8x ($11.71 EPS)
- FY2027E P/E: 22.2x ($13.66 EPS)

Peer context: Based on available data, defense prime forward P/E multiples vary significantly. LHX's 22x forward FY2027 multiple for a company delivering 33% Q1 EPS growth with record backlog and accelerating margins appears reasonable within the peer group, though direct comparisons require current data given rapid sector re-rating.

Analyst consensus target of approximately $365-$384 implies 20-27% upside. The range of analyst targets is wide, reflecting uncertainty around defense spending durability and MSL IPO valuation.

On a DCF basis, with $2.5B+ annual FCF growing at 12-15% for 3 years then moderating to 5%, a 9% discount rate and 15x terminal multiple supports a $340-370 fair value range.

The valuation is not cheap on an absolute basis — this prevents a higher score — but the market is pricing LHX as though the growth acceleration and margin expansion are temporary rather than structural. With ~65% of backlog converting within 24 months, the growth duration risk is lower than what 22x FY2027 implies.

## Competitive Landscape

L3Harris's "merchant supplier" positioning creates both an advantage and a vulnerability. The advantage: diversified exposure across dozens of platforms and programs without single-program concentration risk. The vulnerability: lower barriers to displacement on any individual subsystem contract.

In practice, switching costs are high in defense electronics (radios, sensors, EW) because of integration, qualification, and security clearance requirements. CSD's 25%+ margins reflect genuine pricing power from installed base dominance in tactical communications.

L3Harris is a key U.S. missile propulsion supplier in a highly concentrated domestic supply base, with Northrop Grumman and Anduril also relevant competitors depending on motor class and program. The DoW's $1B direct equity investment validates the strategic criticality of L3Harris's missile propulsion position, signaling the Pentagon's intent to strengthen and expand this critical production capacity. This structural positioning insulates MSL from competitive displacement for the foreseeable future.

Non-traditional entrants (Anduril, SpaceX, Palantir) represent a medium-term competitive threat in software and space, but L3Harris's "platform-agnostic" approach means they can partner with these companies as readily as compete. The company explicitly names partnership with non-traditional entrants as part of its strategy.

## Risk Assessment

**Fixed-price contract risk (75% of revenue):** Development programs under fixed-price terms create material cost overrun exposure. FY2025 saw $63M in negative EAC adjustments; Q1 2026 had another $31M on a legacy naval sensor program. While manageable relative to $5.7B quarterly revenue, a concentrated cost overrun on a large classified program could cause a material earnings miss.

**Iran peace deal framework (outcome highly uncertain):** If a durable deal is confirmed, it could reduce near-term defense sector sentiment. However, structural spending drivers (NATO commitments, Golden Dome, reconciliation, arsenal replenishment) are proposed multi-year programs with bipartisan support, operating largely independently of any single conflict. LHX's backlog is contracted revenue, not optionality on future conflict. This risk is largely one of multiple compression rather than earnings deterioration. Various press reports describe both fragile progress and significant obstacles; we treat this as a scenario variable rather than an assignable probability.

**Missile Solutions IPO execution:** H2 2026 timing depends on market conditions. The DoW's novel equity structure (conversion at 80% of IPO price, warrants for 3% of diluted equity) creates complexity. Final ownership percentages and pricing have not been publicly determined. If the IPO is delayed or priced poorly, the value-unlock catalyst evaporates and stranded costs accumulate.

**Government shutdown/sequestration:** 75% US Government revenue concentration. The October 2025 43-day shutdown caused payment delays. While defense has historically been shielded from sequestration's worst effects, budget uncertainty can delay new awards.

**Management transition:** New CFO (Kenneth Sharp, Feb 2026) and CEO Kubasik's 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 189,501 shares through October 2026. While 10b5-1 plans are pre-programmed, the magnitude (essentially his full post-vesting stake) warrants monitoring.

**Estimate revision trend is modestly negative:** -1.6% on current quarter, -2.0% on next quarter over 30 days. This contradicts the Q1 beat and guidance raise, suggesting analysts may be incorporating peace deal/spending uncertainty that could reverse if the deal fails.

### Options Market Signal

At 27x FY2027 EPS of $13.66 = $369; at 30x = $410.

**Bear Case ($250-275):** Peace deal holds durably, defense spending narrative shifts, multiple compresses to 18-20x forward. Classified program delays or cost overruns cause margin disappointment. MSL IPO delayed. Non-recurring items in Q1 prove that margin trajectory is less sustainable. 18x × $13.66 = $246; 20x × $13.66 = $273.

MSL IPO provides incremental catalyst. 26x × $13.66 = $355; 28x × $13.66 = $382.

## Conclusion

L3Harris at $302.65 represents a fundamentally sound defense company in the midst of an earnings acceleration, trading at a 20% discount to its March high on macro fears (peace deal, general risk-off) rather than fundamental deterioration. The Q1 2026 beat, guidance raise, and $1B DoW investment all confirm the growth trajectory is intact. At 22x FY2027 EPS with 17% earnings growth, 12.3% FCF margins, and a favorable defense spending environment supported by bipartisan consensus, the risk/reward favors continued ownership.

The specific concern that prevented a BUY at prior price levels ($340+) was valuation stretched at 25-26x FY2027 EPS during a period of peak defense sentiment. At $302.65, the multiple has compressed to 22x FY2027 while the fundamental case has strengthened (beat, guidance raise, backlog acceleration). The uncertain Iran peace deal outcome creates near-term sentiment risk, but the structural independence of key defense spending drivers from any single geopolitical scenario makes the current pullback an opportunity. BUY with a 12-month base target of $365.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Q1 2026 margin expansion included identifiable non-recurring items ($20M legal settlement, $39M legacy asset gain); defense budget figures are proposals subject to congressional action, not enacted multi-year authorizations; and MSL IPO ownership retention percentage is not yet publicly confirmed — these corrections reduce certainty around margin durability, spending tailwind permanence, and MSL value-unlock math that underpin the target price.
