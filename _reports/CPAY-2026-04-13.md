## Executive Summary

Corpay is a $20.8B global corporate payments company executing a deliberate portfolio rotation from its legacy Vehicle Payments segment toward higher-growth Corporate Payments and cross-border FX. The stock trades at ~12x FY2026E EPS of $25.86, a meaningful discount to the 26.2x peer median P/E, despite guiding for 22% adjusted EPS growth and 10% organic revenue growth in FY2026. Mastercard's $300M investment in the cross-border business — implying a standalone $13B valuation for that unit alone — provides a concrete valuation anchor that suggests the market is materially underpricing the sum of Corpay's parts.

The primary risks are significant: $10B in total debt (6.8x D/E), four consecutive quarterly EPS misses (Q1-Q4 2025), a June 2027 term loan maturity requiring refinancing, and complex structured obligations from the AvidXchange JV. CEO Ron Clarke (age 70) has driven strategy since 2000, and succession planning is opaque. These risks explain a discount to peers but do not justify the current magnitude of that discount, particularly given $1.30B in free cash flow, 92% revenue retention, and $1-1.3B in pending divestitures that will further delever the balance sheet and fund buybacks.

## Company Overview

Corpay operates across four segments serving business payment needs globally. **Vehicle Payments** (47% of FY2025 revenue, $2.14B) includes fuel cards, electronic tolling (Sem Parar in Brazil with 7.5M+ tagholders), and fleet management. **Corporate Payments** (36%, $1.64B) encompasses cross-border FX in ~200 countries/145 currencies, AP automation, and virtual card solutions. **Lodging Payments** (10%, $0.47B) provides discounted hotel bookings for workforce travelers, airline crews, and insurance displacement housing. **Other** (6%, $0.29B) covers gift card management and payroll cards.

The geographic mix is 49% U.S., 16% Brazil, 14% U.K., and 21% other international markets. Approximately 51% of revenue is non-USD denominated, creating meaningful FX exposure. The company has completed over 100 acquisitions since 2002, most recently the $2.4B Alpha Group (cross-border FX) and AvidXchange JV (AP automation) in October 2025, both reinforcing the Corporate Payments pivot.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** FY2025 revenue reached $4.53B, up 14% reported and 10% organic — the fourth year out of five with 10%+ organic growth. Corporate Payments drove the acceleration at +34% reported (+17% organic), while Vehicle Payments grew +6.5% reported (+9% organic). Lodging declined 4% on lower FEMA-related volumes.

**Profitability:** Adjusted EBITDA margin remained strong at 56.6% (slight compression from 57.1% due to lower-margin acquisitions). Operating margin was 44.0%. FCF of $1.30B represents a 28.7% FCF margin, with cash conversion well above net income levels, a strong quality-of-earnings indicator.

**Balance Sheet:** This is where the risk concentrates. Total debt is approximately $10.0B ($1.8B short-term, $6.7B long-term credit facility, $2.3B securitization). Stockholders' equity of $3.88B produces a D/E of 6.8x. Management's net leverage ratio of 2.8x is more operationally meaningful given the securitization-heavy model, but the absolute debt burden is real: $404M in annual interest expense consumes roughly 38% of operating income. The company maintains $4.5B notional in interest rate swaps (fixed at 3.2-4.2%), providing meaningful hedge benefit, and reported $4.0B in total liquidity. The Altman Z-Score of 0.7 is a known false positive for payment companies carrying customer receivables on-balance-sheet.

**Capital Allocation:** Corpay has returned $8.6B via buybacks since inception (no dividend), including $0.8B in FY2025. Shares outstanding have declined at a 2.6% CAGR. The company announced $450M in proceeds from the PayByPhone divestiture (February 2026), with management indicating two additional vehicle payment divestitures could generate $1-1.3B in additional liquidity, earmarked primarily for repurchases and deleveraging.

**Guided FY2026:** Revenue of $5.265B (+16%), adjusted EPS of $26.00 (+22%). Q1 2026 guided at $1.21B revenue (+20%) and $5.45 adj. EPS (+21%).

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. Corporate Payments organic growth at 17%.** This segment's addressable market is $145 trillion in global B2B transactions, most still processed via manual methods. Corpay's cross-border platform processes transactions in 200 countries across 145 currencies. The Alpha Group acquisition adds ~$300M in incremental revenue and strengthens the financial institution distribution channel.

**2. Mastercard partnership opens institutional distribution.** Mastercard's $300M investment for 2.3% of the cross-border business creates a joint go-to-market motion targeting banks and financial institutions. Management reported two joint deals closed and 50-70 opportunities in pipeline. This channel was previously inaccessible to Corpay as a standalone operator.

**3. Portfolio rotation accelerates the earnings growth rate.** Vehicle Payments dropped from 51% to 47% of revenue in one year. Corporate Payments rose from 31% to 36%. Each percentage point of mix shift toward Corporate Payments raises the blended organic growth rate by approximately 0.3-0.5 points, because Corporate Payments grows at roughly double the rate of Vehicle Payments.

New sales bookings grew 29% YoY with 92% revenue retention, indicating the installed base is expanding and churning minimally. The EV transition adds complexity to fleet management — mixed-fleet solutions (fuel + EV charging) may actually increase demand for Corpay's services rather than erode them.

FY2026 consensus EPS of $25.86 has been revised up 4.2% over the past 90 days, though the 30-day trend is flat. The revision trajectory is modestly positive but not accelerating, consistent with a company delivering solid growth into a cautious macro environment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $302.83, Corpay trades at approximately 11.7x FY2026E EPS of $25.86 and 10.2x FY2027E of $29.66. The peer median P/E of 26.2x implies a **55% discount** to comparable payment processors. Even the cheapest peer, Fiserv at 10.6x, is in the same range rather than materially cheaper.

The discount reflects three factors: (1) leverage, (2) four consecutive EPS misses, and (3) acquisition integration risk from $3B+ in 2025 deals. However, the magnitude of the discount appears excessive given:

- **Mastercard's cross-border valuation signal.** The $300M for 2.3% implies $13B for the cross-border unit alone. Corpay's total market cap is $20.8B. Corporate Payments (which includes cross-border) generated $1.64B in FY2025 revenue. This implies the rest of the business — $2.89B in Vehicle, Lodging, and Other revenue — is valued at roughly $7.8B, or approximately 2.7x revenue. That is cheap for a portfolio of businesses generating 44% operating margins and 57% EBITDA margins.

- **Sum-of-parts math.** Cross-border at $13B + Vehicle Payments at 10x EBITDA (~$12B) + Lodging/Other at 8x EBITDA (~$4B) yields an enterprise value of ~$29B. Subtract $10B net debt = ~$19B equity, or $287/share. This is a floor estimate using conservative multiples; applying even modest premia to the Sem Parar toll franchise or AvidXchange JV upside pushes fair value above $350.

- **Buyback yield.** At $0.8B annual buybacks on a $20.8B market cap, the buyback yield is ~3.8%. Combined with 10% organic growth and financial leverage, EPS growth can sustain low-20s even without margin expansion.

Analyst consensus targets range from $340 (low) to $430 (high), with a median of $380. The current price of $302.83 represents 25% upside to the median target.

## Competitive Landscape

Corpay occupies a distinct position in the payments ecosystem: it does not compete directly with Visa or Mastercard on general-purpose payments but rather owns specialized networks and proprietary data capabilities within vertical markets.

**Vehicle Payments:** The closest pure competitor is WEX. Corpay's Sem Parar toll franchise in Brazil (7.5M+ tagholders) has significant network effects — every new toll road that integrates with Sem Parar increases the switching cost for existing customers. The U.S. fuel card business faces FTC oversight (consent order since June 2023) but maintains strong positions with over-the-road trucking fleets.

**Corporate Payments / Cross-Border:** The competitive moat here derives from curated bank relationships in 200+ countries and the complexity of compliance in multi-currency environments. The Mastercard partnership provides distribution at zero incremental cost. The 10-K's newly added stablecoin/blockchain risk factor is worth monitoring: management acknowledged "no demand" currently but is building pilots. If stablecoins commoditize FX transfer costs, the cross-border margin structure (currently opaque but likely 10-30 bps per transaction) could compress.

**Lodging:** A niche business with limited competition but also limited growth — management called it a "problem child" alongside U.S. Vehicle on the Q4 call.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and Refinancing (HIGH).** Term Loan A and revolving credit facility mature in June 2027, requiring refinancing within the next 14 months. In the current rate environment (SOFR 3.64%+, with hawkish Fed posture and no rate cuts expected), refinancing costs will likely increase. The interest rate swap book ($4.5B notional at 3.2-4.2% fixed) provides partial insulation, but swaps themselves mature and must be rolled.

**Consecutive EPS Misses (MEDIUM-HIGH).** Four straight quarterly misses ranging from -0.4% to -2.0% signal either persistent operational headwinds or systematic guidance over-optimism. While the misses are small in absolute terms, the pattern undermines confidence in FY2026 guidance. The flat 30-day estimate revision trend suggests the Street has not yet decided whether to give management the benefit of the doubt on 2026.

**AvidXchange JV Contingent Obligations (MEDIUM).** The structured deal terms include a minimum 1.6x return guarantee to TPG and a call option at 2.5x invested capital ($578M base). If Corpay exercises the call in ~33 months, the total outlay would be ~$1.45B. If it doesn't exercise and TPG seeks an exit, Corpay may need to fund the 1.6x minimum (~$925M). These are material future cash obligations that are difficult to model precisely.

**CEO Succession (MEDIUM).** Ron Clarke, age 70, has led Corpay since 2000. His deal-making capability drives the acquisition strategy. CFO Peter Walker joined only in July 2025. The board has been refreshed (multiple 5.02 filings), but no public succession plan exists. The company's 5-year TSR of 110.3 vs. peers at 132.1 (22-point underperformance) indicates the stock has not fully rewarded Clarke's strategy, which could pressure any successor to pursue operational execution over transformative deals.

**FX Exposure (MEDIUM).** With 51% non-USD revenue and ~8% revenue directly fuel-price linked, Corpay's reported results are subject to meaningful currency and commodity volatility. The Brazil real and British pound are the two largest exposures.

**Macro Sensitivity (MEDIUM-HIGH).** In a recessionary scenario, cross-border transaction volumes, corporate travel (Lodging), and fleet utilization (Vehicle) would all decline. Consumer sentiment at record lows and recession probability at 45-50% means this risk is elevated. The counter-argument is that B2B payment digitization is a secular trend with counter-cyclical adoption characteristics — economic pressure often accelerates the shift from paper checks to digital payments.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 44.5% is running 9 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 35.5%, placing the options market in a moderately rich posture. The term structure is in backwardation (44.5% near-term vs. 35.0% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing near-term event risk. Put skew of 16.7% shows elevated put demand, though the OI put/call ratio at 0.63 is not extreme. The backwardated term structure likely reflects residual post-earnings positioning and broader macro uncertainty rather than company-specific catalyst pricing. For a new buyer, the elevated near-term IV suggests waiting for short-term options to decay or using longer-dated entries.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($380-430)

Corpay delivers on FY2026 guidance ($26.00 adj. EPS, +22%). Corporate Payments organic growth sustains at 15%+ driven by Mastercard FI channel ramp. Alpha Group integration hits synergy targets, contributing $300M+ revenue at improving margins. Two additional vehicle payment divestitures close for $1-1.3B, deployed primarily into buybacks, reducing the share count by 5-7% over 18 months. Refinancing of Term Loan A (June 2027) proceeds at acceptable rates given strong FCF coverage. The market re-rates CPAY toward 15x forward earnings as the EPS miss pattern breaks, implying $390-430. Sum-of-parts recognition of the $13B cross-border valuation drives institutional reappraisal.

### Bear Case ($225-260)

EPS misses extend into FY2026. Recession compresses cross-border volumes and fleet utilization simultaneously. Refinancing in 2027 occurs at 100-150 bps wider spreads, adding $100-150M to annual interest expense and pressuring free cash flow to $1.0-1.1B. The AvidXchange JV triggers the 1.6x minimum return guarantee, consuming ~$925M in cash. CEO Clarke retires without a clear successor, creating strategic uncertainty. The stock de-rates to 9-10x depressed earnings of $23-25, implying $225-260.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $370.** 14.3x FY2027E EPS of $29.66, which represents a partial re-rating from 10.2x current forward toward the lower end of peer multiples. This implies 22% upside from $302.83.

**Bull case target: $430.** 14.5x FY2027E on upside earnings of $29.66+, plus additional re-rating on sum-of-parts recognition. 42% upside.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months.** Key catalysts: Q1 2026 earnings (~May 2026, first test of FY2026 guidance), completion of pending vehicle divestitures (H1-H2 2026), refinancing visibility on Term Loan A (by early 2027).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. FY2026 quarterly EPS misses continue (2+ more misses), indicating systemic guidance credibility problems.
2. Net leverage ratio rises above 3.5x, signaling balance sheet deterioration.
3. Cross-border organic growth declines below 10% for two consecutive quarters, undermining the Corporate Payments pivot thesis.

## Conclusion

Corpay at ~12x forward earnings is priced for stagnation while delivering 10% organic growth, 22% guided EPS growth, and executing a strategically sound portfolio rotation. The four consecutive EPS misses, heavy leverage, and complex acquisition structures are real risks that prevent maximum conviction. However, the Mastercard cross-border valuation ($13B implied for a unit within a $20.8B market cap company), $1.30B in FCF, pending divestitures, and consistent 29% new bookings growth provide concrete, measurable catalysts. The Wall Street consensus — 11 Buy/Strong Buy, 3 Hold, zero Sells, median target $380 — aligns with the thesis that the current discount is overdone. The risk-reward at current levels favors ownership with appropriate position sizing to account for the leverage and refinancing overhang.
