## Executive Summary

Oshkosh Corporation is a $9.5B industrial technology company whose largest segment (Access, 43% of revenue) is entering a cyclical downturn compounded by a $200M tariff headwind, while its Vocational segment (36%) is experiencing secular margin expansion and its Transport/Defense segment (20%) benefits from the largest U.S. defense budget request in decades. The stock has rallied 67% from its May 2025 low of $87.54, currently trading at $149.87, still 17% below its 52-week high of $180.49.

At ~13.4x the company's own 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $11.50 (consensus $11.21), the valuation reflects the near-term tariff headwinds and Access weakness. The $14.2B backlog (1.4x annual revenue) provides unusual revenue visibility for an industrial company. However, the current quarter (reporting ~May 8) is guided to approximately $0.85 EPS, roughly half of the year-ago period, creating a near-term sentiment overhang. The bull thesis (Vocational margin expansion to 16%+, NGDV ramp, defense spending) requires patience through 2-3 quarters of compressed earnings before proving out.

The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty: 60.9% near-term IV vs. 36.7% historical volatility, in steep backwardation. This structure reflects the imminent Q1 earnings catalyst and confirms elevated binary risk around tariff/pricing execution.

## Company Overview

Oshkosh operates through three segments:

- **Access (43% of FY2025 revenue):** JLG-branded aerial work platforms, telehandlers, and towing/recovery vehicles. Customers are primarily equipment rental companies (United Rentals, Sunbelt) and construction contractors. Highly cyclical, tied to non-residential construction spending.

- **Vocational (36%):** Fire apparatus (Pierce Manufacturing), airport ground support (AeroTech, acquired 2023 for $805M), refuse/recycling vehicles (McNeilus). Municipal and commercial customers. More defensive demand characteristics with multi-year backlogs.

- **Transport/Defense (20%):** Military tactical vehicles (JLTV, FHTV, FMTV) for the U.S. DoD, plus the USPS Next Generation Delivery Vehicle (NGDV) program — a contract for up to 165,000 vehicles over 10 years with only 51,500 ordered so far.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:**
- FY2022: $8.28B → FY2023: $9.66B (+17%) → FY2024: $10.73B (+11%) → FY2025: $10.42B (-3%)
- FY2026 guided at ~$11.0B (+5.5%)

The FY2025 decline was driven by Access (-13% organic, including loss of Caterpillar telehandler agreement worth ~$659M annually) and Transport (-2.7% from JLTV completion), partially offset by Vocational (+12.6%).

**Profitability:**
- Gross margin: 17.5% (FY2025), down 90bps from 18.4%
- Operating margin: 9.0% (FY2025), down from 9.4%
- Segment margins diverging sharply: Access compressed from 15.6% to 11.2% (guided to 9.7% in 2026), while Vocational expanded from 12.0% to 14.7% (guided to 16.1%)

**Cash flow and balance sheet:**
- FCF: $618M in FY2025 (5.9% yield on revenue; ~6.5% FCF yield on market cap)
- Cash: $480M; Total debt: $1.1B; Debt/capitalization: 19.5%
- Investment grade ratings (BBB/Baa3)
- $1.51B unused revolver capacity
- Current ratio: 1.94x

The balance sheet is conservatively managed for an industrial company. The Altman Z-Score of 2.54 (grey zone) is an artifact of high goodwill/intangibles from acquisitions ($2.2B combined) rather than a genuine distress signal.

**Capital allocation:** Management accelerated buybacks to $278M in FY2025 (from $116M prior year) and raised the dividend 11.8% — the 12th consecutive year of double-digit increases. 7.9M shares remain under the repurchase authorization. CapEx declining from $281M to $165M as major facility investments complete.

## Growth Analysis

Growth visibility is bifurcated:

**Positive vectors:**
- Vocational: $6.6B backlog (nearly 2x annual segment revenue), expanding margins from 14.7% toward 16.1%, $150M capacity investment still partially deploying. Fire apparatus demand driven by aging fleet replacement.
- Transport: 19% guided growth in FY2026 from NGDV ramp (5,000+ units produced; contract allows 165,000) and international JLTV orders. Defense budget at $1.5T request for FY2027.
- Innovation pipeline: Electrification (Volterra), autonomy (FMAV for Army), robotics (CES 2026 awards).

**Negative vectors:**
- Access: Backlog down 30.4% YoY ($1.3B). Non-residential construction spending flat in 2026. Rental company CapEx budgets being trimmed. Chinese competitors gaining share through aggressive pricing. Margins heading toward single digits (9.7% guided).
- Tariffs: $200M impact in FY2026 (470% increase from FY2025's $35M), ~75% hitting Access. Management expects pricing to fully offset by H2 but H1 will be severely compressed.
- Estimate revisions: Current quarter estimates collapsed 56% over 90 days ($2.36 → $1.04). Full-year consensus down 9.6% over 90 days. This is materially negative earnings momentum.

Net assessment: low-single-digit organic growth with segment mix shifting toward higher-margin Vocational and higher-revenue-but-lower-margin Transport. Total company margin profile flat to slightly down near-term, with potential expansion in FY2027 as tariff offsets fully deploy.

At 13-14x current-year earnings for an industrial company with 6.5% FCF yield and investment-grade balance sheet, the market is pricing in the tariff headwinds and Access cyclicality but not giving full credit to the Vocational transformation or NGDV optionality. A re-rating would require: (1) demonstrated tariff offset execution in Q2-Q3, (2) USPS follow-on order confirmation, or (3) Access stabilization/recovery.

The 10.5x FY2027 multiple, if consensus estimates hold, would represent clear value. But estimates have been falling consistently, and the track record of 2 beats/2 misses in the last 4 quarters provides limited confidence in forward estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

**Access:** JLG maintains leading market share but faces structural headwinds. The Caterpillar agreement loss ($659M annual revenue) is permanent. Chinese competitors (XCMG, Dingli) are applying pricing pressure internationally. Rental company consolidation increases customer bargaining power. JLG holds ~78% of the segment's $1.4B in goodwill — a prolonged downturn could create impairment risk.

**Vocational:** Strongest competitive positioning. Pierce Manufacturing is the leading U.S. fire apparatus brand with multi-year backlogs and pricing power (300bps gross margin benefit from pricing alone in FY2025). The $150M capacity investment positions Oshkosh to reduce lead times and capture share. AeroTech adds airport sector exposure with limited competition.

**Defense:** JLTV was a market-defining franchise but domestic production is complete — the transition to sustainment and international sales introduces execution risk. The DoD's push for intellectual property rights and competition could pressure future programs. New executive orders emphasizing "speed and affordability" create margin headwinds.

**NGDV:** Sole-source contract with the USPS provides protected revenue but at thin margins. The deferred cost overhang ($135M if follow-on orders don't materialize) creates binary risk.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Execution (Critical, Near-Term):** If pricing actions trigger volume loss in a competitive market, the offset may not fully materialize.

**2. Access Cyclical Downturn:** Backlog down 30%, margins compressing from 15.6% to 9.7% in two years. If non-residential construction weakens further (rate-sensitive), Access could undershoot even conservative estimates.

**3. NGDV Binary Risk:** $135M deferred cost write-down if USPS doesn't place follow-on orders beyond 51,500 units. The USPS can terminate for convenience at any time.

**4. Defense Budget Execution vs. Announcement:** The $1.5T budget request is favorable in principle, but the January 2026 executive order could limit share buybacks/dividends for defense contractors and pressure margins through "affordability" mandates.

**5. UAW Arbitration:** Labor dispute at Oshkosh Defense heading to arbitration (March 2026). Contract expires September 2027. Disruption risk is real but manageable.

**6. Rate Environment Impact:** Elevated long-term rates create headwinds for non-residential construction that directly reduce Access equipment demand.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175-195):**
- Vocational margins reach 16%+ and sustain, with fire apparatus backlog providing 2-3 years of visibility
- USPS places follow-on NGDV order, triggering favorable catch-up adjustment and derisking the $135M overhang
- Tariff offsets execute successfully in H2, restoring Access margins toward 11-12%
- $1.5T defense budget translates into new program wins (FMAV, international JLTV)
- FY2027 consensus of $14.24 EPS proved achievable; stock re-rates to 13-14x = $185-199

**Bear Case ($115-125):**
- Tariff pricing actions accelerate market share losses in Access; margins stay below 10%
- USPS fails to place follow-on order; $135M write-down taken
- Non-residential construction weakens further as rates remain elevated; Access backlog declines another 20%+
- Executive order restricts buybacks/dividends, removing capital return support
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $9.50-10.00 vs. $11.50 guidance; stock de-rates to 12x = $114-120

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q1 2026 earnings (expected ~May 8) demonstrate tariff pricing acceptance without material volume loss; Q2 guidance confirms trajectory toward H2 offset. Stock pulls back to $130-135 range on soft H1 numbers, creating entry point at 12x forward EPS.
- USPS formally places follow-on NGDV order, removing $135M deferred cost overhang.
- Access backlog stabilizes or grows sequentially, indicating cyclical trough.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q1 earnings reveal tariff costs above $200M run-rate OR pricing actions driving >10% incremental volume loss in Access.
- USPS signals no follow-on order or program restructuring.
- Management cuts full-year guidance below $10.00 EPS.
- UAW dispute escalates to work stoppage at defense facilities.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 2026 earnings (~May 8, 2026) is the critical catalyst. Tariff offset execution will begin to show in Q2 results (July-August). NGDV follow-on order timing is uncertain but expected in 2026.

## Conclusion

Oshkosh is a classic industrial transition story trading at fair value: 13-14x a depressed earnings year that should recover if management executes on tariff offsets. The Vocational segment's secular improvement and the defense spending backdrop provide genuine long-term quality. However, the negative estimate revision trajectory, 2-for-4 recent earnings track record, and binary NGDV risk prevent a higher-conviction call at current prices.

The stock has already re-rated substantially from the May 2025 low (~$88) and sits 17% below its February 2026 high. At $150, the market is pricing approximately fair value for the visible risks. A better entry point would be $130-135 (12x adjusted EPS), where the risk/reward tilts more decisively positive. Alternatively, demonstrated execution on tariff offsets in Q2-Q3 would support buying at current levels with higher conviction. Until one of those conditions materializes, HOLD.
