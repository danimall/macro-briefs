**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer and the largest North American nitrogen fertilizer producer, with a structural cost advantage anchored in low-cost US/Canadian natural gas. The current investment question concerns how much of the company's earnings power is durable; asset quality is not in question. Q1 2026 produced $3.98 diluted EPS (+115% YoY) on $1.99B revenue (+19%), and consensus current-year EPS has been revised from $8.47 to $18.23 over 90 days (+115.2%). This presents as a textbook earnings inflection, but the bulk of the move is a geopolitical supply premium plus one-time items, neither of which carries a clean multiple.

The pricing strength traces directly to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the curtailment of Middle East nitrogen and Qatari LNG, which removed roughly 25-30% of globally traded ammonia and 35-40% of traded urea from the marginal-cost-setting pool. Average selling price jumped 28% to $424/ton. Management's own MD&A attributes the surge to the Iran conflict and characterizes the supply tightness as near-term. On top of the price effect, Q1 net income absorbed a ~$170M Orica litigation settlement, a $25M insurance recovery, and $24M of 45Q carbon-capture tax credits. Normalizing for the geopolitical premium and the one-timers leaves a substantially lower run-rate than the 9.9x trailing multiple implies.

The balance sheet is genuinely strong (net debt ~$1.2B against ~$2.9B EBITDA, current ratio ~3.5x, $2B cash, undrawn $750M revolver), and the company retired ~25% of its shares since 2022. Three things argue against pressing a long here: the central earnings driver is explicitly transient and tied to a binary geopolitical event with no verified resolution path; an escalating Sherman Act class action and a December 2025 federal fertilizer-pricing task force create asymmetric downside as margins peak; and the buyback that defined the equity story has slowed sharply (Q1 2026 repurchases of $15M vs $434M a year earlier) as cash pivots to the $3.7B Blue Point build. We rate CF a HOLD with an overall score of 5.8, continuing a flat trajectory that reflects deteriorating risk and valuation scores against an intact financial profile.

## Company Overview

CF's entire business rests on the Haber-Bosch process, fixing atmospheric nitrogen with hydrogen derived from natural gas to produce anhydrous ammonia, then upgrading it into granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate, and industrial products. The asset base is concentrated and high-quality: six US plants including Donaldsonville (the world's largest ammonia complex), Port Neal, Yazoo City, Verdigris, Woodward, and Waggaman; two Canadian plants; a UK plant at Billingham; a 50% Trinidad JV; and a 40% interest in the Blue Point low-carbon ammonia JV.

Q1 2026 segment gross margins show the mix: Ammonia $227M (36.2%), Granular Urea $255M (43.2%), UAN $250M (42.9%), AN at a -$11M loss due to the Yazoo City outage, and Other $25M. The competitive position rests on three pillars: scale plus a North American gas cost advantage against higher-cost European and disrupted Middle Eastern marginal producers; an extensive owned terminal and distribution network in the US Midwest; and early-mover positioning in carbon-capture low-carbon ammonia. Five of the six US plants are held via CF Industries Nitrogen, LLC, ~90% owned by CF with CHS holding ~10%.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings stream is highly cyclical and commodity-driven. Revenue peaked near $11.2B in FY2022 during the post-invasion price spike, normalized to ~$5.9B in FY2024, then recovered. FY2025 delivered $1.80B net income and ~$2.88B adjusted EBITDA.

Q1 2026 was strong on every reported line: net sales $1.99B (+19% YoY), gross margin $746M (37.6% vs 34.4%), net earnings to common $615M (+97%), diluted EPS $3.98 (+115%). The composition matters more than the headline. Underlying gross-margin improvement of roughly $174M was real, but a meaningful share of the bottom-line growth is non-recurring. Partly offsetting, natural gas cost rose 24% to $4.57/MMBtu on a late-January Henry Hub spike, cutting gross margin by $76M. Gas is ~39% of production cost and hedging is light (9.0M MMBtu of basis swaps).

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case. Cash is $2.04B (including $254M trapped in the Blue Point VIE), long-term debt is $3.25B across four senior-note tranches due 2034-2044 at ~5% coupons, the $750M revolver is undrawn, and the current ratio is ~3.5x. Net debt of ~$1.2B against ~$2.9B EBITDA is comfortable leverage (~0.4x). The Altman Z-Score "distress" flag is a false positive here, a known artifact for asset-heavy, low-asset-turnover commodity producers with large PP&E; it does not reflect genuine solvency risk.

Q1 operating cash flow of $496M was down YoY on a $95M Canadian tax deposit and a working-capital build. The capital-allocation story is the key change. Share count fell from ~203M (2022) to ~154M (Q1 2026), roughly a quarter of the company retired. The 2022 $3B program completed in October 2025; a new $2B program through 2029 is underway with ~$1.7B remaining. The buyback pace collapsed in Q1 2026 as cash redirects toward Blue Point. The dividend held flat at $0.50/quarter, a ~1.8% yield.

A reporting caveat: noncontrolling interests are large (~$2.9B equity, $61M of Q1 net earnings, ~$201M distributed to CHS in Q1) due to the CFN stake and the Blue Point partner stakes. Consolidated figures overstate CF-attributable economics, so per-share normalization should be done on an attributable basis.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate revision data is striking on its face: current-quarter EPS revised +48.5% (30d) and +173.6% (90d); next-quarter +92.0% and +286.1%; current-year +18.0% and +115.2%. Under normal circumstances, revision velocity of this magnitude alongside revenue growth would be a strong forward signal. Here it is not, for a specific reason: the revisions are tracking a commodity-price spike caused by a discrete geopolitical event, not a durable demand or margin expansion. The next-year revision is telling — next-year went only +66.0% over 90 days and was flat over the last 30 days (-0.2%), which is the consensus already pricing partial mean reversion as the Hormuz premium fades. The market is treating this as a peak.

The durable growth vectors are narrower. Low-carbon ammonia is the genuine multi-year thesis: the Donaldsonville CCS project (online July 2025, ~$200M, up to 2M tonnes/yr CO₂ sequestered via ExxonMobil, generating $24M of 45Q credits in Q1 alone) and the $3.7B Blue Point JV (first production 2029, 60% funded by JERA/Mitsui, both certified low-carbon hydrogen suppliers by Japan's METI). These are real, but the addressable market for ammonia as a power-generation and marine fuel is nascent and depends on government incentives (45Q, METI support, EU CBAM). Blue Point also carries multi-year execution and imported-equipment cost risk, and contributes nothing to earnings before 2029.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and consistent with low forward visibility on commodity prices: +8.2% (Q1 2026), +24.9% (Q4 2025), +0.7% (Q3 2025), -10.3% (Q2 2025). Three beats and one miss across four quarters does not support high confidence in any single forward estimate when the swing factor is selling price.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline 9.9x trailing P/E looks cheap against the Materials peer median of 24.2x. That gap is not a mispricing to exploit; it reflects the market correctly discounting peak-cycle, geopolitically-contingent earnings. The right way to value CF is on normalized earnings power, and that requires stripping out the Hormuz premium and the one-timers.

Peer comparables are heterogeneous and of limited use here: IP and ALB show negative P/Es, RGLD (24.2x) is a royalty business, AMCR (25.2x) and IFF (22.6x) are packaging/ingredients names with different cyclicality. None is a clean nitrogen comparable. The more useful anchor is CF's own through-cycle earnings. FY2024 revenue of ~$5.9B and ~$1.8B FY2025 net income suggest mid-cycle EPS power closer to the $8-11 range than the $18 consensus current-year figure. Consensus next-year EPS of $11.16 implies the Street already expects normalization; at $111.50 that is ~10x next-year, which is reasonable but not compelling for a commodity producer at what may be a cyclical top.

The valuation hinges entirely on what happens to nitrogen prices. If the Strait reopens and Qatari LNG/Middle East nitrogen capacity returns, prices likely normalize and the stock de-rates toward a normalized ~10x on ~$10 EPS, roughly $100, near the analyst low of $100. If the disruption grinds on (the more probable near-term path given verification has been absent), high prices persist and the $130 median target and $150 high are achievable. Analyst consensus has degraded (sentiment trend: degrading), and the recommendation mix is 5 buy/strong-buy, 13 hold, 3 sell/strong-sell, a cautious distribution. The valuation score of 5.5 reflects a stock fairly priced for its cycle position with symmetric, event-dependent outcomes rather than asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive dynamic is structurally favorable at present. Nitrogen prices are set by the marginal high-cost producer, and the marginal producers — Europe on high gas, Middle East/India/Pakistan/Bangladesh dependent on now-disrupted LNG — are curtailed. CF's North American gas advantage plus owned logistics positions it as a low-cost survivor. Reuters framed nitrogen producers (CF, Nutrien) as beneficiaries of the Middle East gas disruption while phosphate-heavy Mosaic faces headwinds.

The longer-term competitive edge depends on low-carbon ammonia, where CF is among the most advanced incumbents (Donaldsonville CCS live, Blue Point under construction, first low-carbon premium sales to Europe/Africa in 2025). This is a genuine moat-widening vector if the hydrogen/ammonia-as-fuel market develops, but it is policy-dependent and early. The score of 6.5 reflects a strong, improving competitive position whose near-term advantage is cyclical (gas-cost spread) and whose durable advantage (low-carbon) is real but not yet earnings-relevant.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile has deteriorated, and this is where the thesis is most constrained.

**Geopolitical premium reversal.** The core earnings driver is a Strait of Hormuz closure with no verified reopening. Diplomatic signals around the conflict have repeatedly failed to translate into physical resolution. The disruption could persist (sustaining margins) or resolve abruptly (collapsing them). Qatar has said full LNG restoration will take multiple years, which cuts both ways. This is a binary overhang on the central earnings line.

**Antitrust and political crosscurrent.** A March 2026 Sherman Act class action alleges CF, Mosaic, Nutrien, Koch, Yara, and Canpotex conspired to restrain NPK trade; a JPML consolidation hearing was set for May 28, 2026. Compounding this, a December 2025 Trump Executive Order created DOJ/FTC Food Supply Chain Security Task Forces specifically targeting fertilizer price-fixing. The unusual feature is that commercial success — high prices — directly invites legal and political scrutiny. Potential losses are not yet estimable, which is itself an open-ended risk.

**Yazoo City outage.** A November 2025 incident idled the entire site; production is not expected to resume until late Q4 2026 at the earliest, turning the AN segment loss-making (-19% margin) and dragging DEF/Other volumes. The rebuild is insured for property and business interruption ($75M of initial proceeds received April 2026), which limits the financial drag but extends the operational impairment.

**Capital-return downshift.** The buyback that anchored the equity story slowed as cash pivots to Blue Point. Investors who owned CF for the return-of-capital cadence are getting a different profile now.

**Leadership transition.** CEO Tony Will stepped down to Director; CFO Cameron exited under a separation agreement; Andrew Scribner was appointed EVP & CFO in May after an interim period. Scribner is an internal hire; Bohn's elevation to President & CEO is an internal promotion (continuity-oriented), but clustered CEO+CFO turnover during a peak-margin, high-litigation period warrants monitoring.

**Other:** gas-price volatility (~39% of cost, light hedging), tariff/trade-policy uncertainty (ammonia not exempt from the 10% US tariff; steel/aluminum tariffs raise Blue Point costs), and single-site concentration risk demonstrated by Yazoo.

The risk score of 4.5 reflects multiple specific, partially-unpriced downside catalysts: the binary geopolitical reversal, the open-ended antitrust/regulatory probe, and the capital-return change.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 74.8% against 41.9% one-year historical vol (rich by 32.9 points, 92nd percentile within the chain), with a backwardated term structure (74.8% near-term vs 45.9% long-term) and a 13.9% put/call skew showing elevated put demand. ATM IV jumped 21 points (53.8% to 74.8%) over the three days into this snapshot while the stock rose 1.9%, and the put/call OI ratio ticked up to 1.04. The near-dated spike is concentrated in the 1-week tenor and likely reflects event-hedging around the live geopolitical binary rather than a fundamental tell, but the elevated put demand corroborates the asymmetric downside in the central pricing driver. For a long, the rich near-term IV is unfavorable entry timing; the structure supports patience over fresh exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$150):** The Strait of Hormuz disruption persists, Qatari LNG restoration takes the multiple years management indicates, and Middle East nitrogen stays curtailed. Nitrogen prices hold near current levels through 2026-2027, sustaining ~$18 current-year EPS power. The antitrust suit is dismissed or settled cheaply, Yazoo restarts on schedule, and the low-carbon ammonia premium (45Q credits, CBAM, METI offtake) begins to contribute. At a normalized ~8x on sustained-premium earnings, the stock reaches the $150 analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$90-100, roughly -10% to -19%):** The Strait reopens, Qatari and Middle East nitrogen capacity returns, and prices normalize toward mid-cycle. Stripped of the geopolitical premium and one-timers, EPS reverts toward $8-10. The antitrust class action gains traction or produces a material settlement, the federal task force imposes pricing constraints, and the buyback stays throttled by Blue Point funding. The stock de-rates to ~10x on normalized earnings near $100, with downside toward $90 if both pricing normalization and a litigation overhang hit together.

The base case sits between these: the disruption grinds on through much of 2026, supporting elevated prices, while the market continues to discount the earnings as peak-cycle and refuses to award a durable multiple. That holds the stock in a $105-130 range, consistent with the analyst median of $130 and the cautious 13-hold consensus.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The fortress balance sheet and demonstrated downside support argue against an AVOID, but the transient nature of the core earnings driver and the rising regulatory/legal overhang preclude a BUY at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward $90-100 with the geopolitical premium still intact, compressing the risk/reward; or
- Evidence the supply tightness is structurally durable (confirmed multi-year Qatari LNG restoration timeline plus Middle East capacity staying offline) such that elevated nitrogen prices warrant a higher normalized multiple; or
- Favorable resolution of the Sherman Act class action and the federal task force inquiry, removing the asymmetric legal overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Physical reopening of the Strait of Hormuz with 72+ hours of sustained, verified commercial transit, which removes the central pricing support;
- An adverse antitrust development (class certification, an unfavorable JPML consolidation outcome, or a quantified material-loss disclosure);
- A guidance cut or material gas-cost spike that compresses margins faster than prices normalize.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) for normalized run-rate margin and updated price realization, plus ongoing monitoring of the Hormuz situation and the antitrust docket. The clean tactical exit remains a verified physical reopening of the Strait.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a high-quality, low-cost commodity producer with a strong balance sheet, trading at a low headline multiple on earnings that are largely a function of a temporary geopolitical supply shock and one-time items. The estimate revision velocity is real but is tracking a price spike the market correctly refuses to capitalize at a high multiple, evidenced by next-year estimates already flattening as consensus prices mean reversion. The deterioration since prior reviews is driven by the antitrust/regulatory escalation, the throttled buyback, and the increasingly binary dependence on the Hormuz closure, against an unchanged and healthy financial base.

We rate CF a HOLD with an overall score of 5.8. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric and event-dependent: meaningful upside if the disruption persists, comparable downside if it resolves, with an open-ended legal overhang on top. We would become buyers on a pullback toward $90-100 with the premium intact or on confirmation of durable supply tightness, and would exit on verified physical reopening of the Strait or an adverse antitrust development.
