## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin trades at $512.76 after a 27% decline from its March 2026 high of $692, driven by a Q1 2026 earnings miss ($6.44 vs $6.79 estimate), continued classified program losses, and the overhang from Executive Order 14372. The company maintains a record $193.6B backlog (2.6x annual revenue) and is positioned at the center of a defense spending upcycle ($901B NDAA, proposed 85 F-35s in FY2027), but profitability has deteriorated meaningfully: net income has fallen 27% from $6.9B (2023) to $5.0B (2025) despite 11% revenue growth over the same period. The Missiles & Fire Control segment represents the clearest growth catalyst, with backlog growing 45% to $46.7B, but the loss of NGAD/F-47 to Boeing and the systemic pattern of fixed-price development losses ($1.76B in 2025 alone) represent structural headwinds that the current ~24x trailing P/E does not adequately discount.

The defense budget environment is the strongest in a generation, but LMT-specific execution problems and policy risks (EO 14372 targeting capital returns of "underperforming" contractors) create a company-specific discount that may widen before it narrows. We maintain HOLD pending evidence that margin compression has troughed and classified program losses have been fully recognized.

## Company Overview

Lockheed Martin is the world's largest defense contractor by revenue ($75.0B in 2025), operating across four segments: Aeronautics (40% of sales, anchored by the F-35), Missiles and Fire Control (19%, the primary growth vector), Rotary and Mission Systems (23%), and Space (17%). Customer concentration is extreme — 72% of revenue comes from the U.S. Government, with another 28% from international customers largely routed through Foreign Military Sales.

The F-35 program alone accounts for 27% of total company revenue, creating meaningful single-program concentration. The company's competitive position rests on monopoly/duopoly positions across critical defense categories (F-35, THAAD, Aegis, Trident II), but the NGAD loss to Boeing in March 2025 signals that this dominance is not permanent.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth has been solid but margins have compressed significantly:**

| Year | Revenue | Op. Margin | Net Income | EPS |
|------|---------|-----------|-----------|-----|
| 2022 | $66.0B | 12.7% | $5.7B | $21.66 |
| 2023 | $67.6B | 12.6% | $6.9B | $27.55 |
| 2024 | $71.0B | 9.9% | $5.3B | $22.31 |
| 2025 | $75.0B | 10.3% | $5.0B | $21.49 |

Revenue grew 13.6% over three years while net income declined 27%. The primary drivers: $1.76B in reach-forward losses on fixed-price programs in 2025 (classified Aeronautics: $950M; CMHP: $570M; C-130: $140M; TUHP: $95M), an $874M pension swing (from +$62M income in 2024 to -$874M expense in 2025), and rising interest expense ($623M in 2022 → $1.12B in 2025).

**Segment margin divergence is instructive:**
- Aeronautics: 6.9% (down from 10.3% in 2023) — classified losses dominate
- MFC: 13.8% (recovered sharply from the 3.3% trough caused by a $1.4B classified loss in 2024)
- RMS: 7.6% (down from 11.5% in 2023) — CMHP/TUHP headwinds
- Space: 10.3% (stable/improving)

Free cash flow of $6.9B (9.2% FCF margin) is robust and improved from $5.3B in 2024, but Q1 2026 showed negative FCF due to working capital headwinds. The balance sheet carries $21.7B in debt against $6.7B in equity (D/E of 3.2x), which is characteristic of defense primes that have executed aggressive buyback programs rather than a sign of distress. Operating cash flow of $8.6B covers debt service comfortably, and the government-backed revenue stream reduces refinancing risk.

Capital allocation has been aggressive: $3.0B in buybacks + ~$3.2B in dividends in 2025, totaling ~$6.2B against $5.0B in net income. The company is returning more than it earns, funding the gap partly through debt issuance ($2.0B in 2025).

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers (12-18 months):**
- MFC production ramp: PAC-3 MSE, JASSM, LRASM, PrSM multi-year framework agreements. Management has signaled potential to grow missile sales 3-4x. MFC backlog grew 45% from $32.2B to $46.7B (2023-2025).
- F-35 volume: FY2027 budget proposes 85 aircraft, nearly doubling prior year requests. Lot 18/19 definitized for 296 aircraft.
- FY2026 NDAA: $901B authorized (+$8B over request). "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" provides $150B+ in mandatory defense funding through 2029.

**Longer-term headwinds:**
- NGAD/F-47 loss to Boeing eliminates what could have been a multi-decade Aeronautics franchise program.
- F-35 sustainment cost pressure: The $2T+ lifetime program cost (75% sustainment) draws Congressional scrutiny and "repair rights" legislation that would erode LMT's maintenance monopoly.
- Management guidance targets ~$80B in 2026 sales with FY2026 consensus EPS at $29.88, implying margin recovery that has not yet been demonstrated.

Consensus estimates reflect ~39% EPS growth from FY2025's $21.49 to FY2026's $29.88, but this requires the cessation of reach-forward losses. Estimate revisions over 90 days are essentially flat (+0.2% for current year, +1.1% for next year), reflecting genuine uncertainty about whether margins have troughed.

If FY2026 EPS comes in at $25-27 (reflecting continued program charges), the stock trades at 19-20x actual earnings — no longer cheap relative to NOC.

The 2.7% dividend yield provides modest support. The analyst consensus mean target of $637.60 (median $645) implies ~24% upside, but the 14 HOLD ratings (vs. 7 BUY/Strong Buy and 1 SELL) signal limited conviction.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengthening:** MFC's missile portfolio is in the strongest demand position in decades. PAC-3 deployed across 17 nations, with multi-year production agreements locking in volume. The global munitions shortage and conflict-driven replenishment create a multi-year tailwind with few substitutes available at scale.

**Stable:** F-35 production franchise remains intact with Lot 18/19 definitized and FY2027 requesting 85 jets. The "fifth-gen-plus" upgrade path extends the platform's relevance.

**Deteriorating:** The NGAD loss signals the Pentagon's explicit desire to diversify away from Lockheed dominance in tactical aviation. The Air Force chose Boeing specifically to "diversify the defense industrial base." LMT's Aeronautics segment faces a ceiling on next-generation programs. Additionally, the EO's encouragement of "innovative acquisition pathways" and OTAs favor non-traditional contractors and erode the incumbency advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Classified Program Losses (HIGH — Ongoing, Unquantifiable):** Three consecutive years of major reach-forward losses totaling $3B+ across multiple segments. Management explicitly warns of potential future charges. This reflects a structural issue with fixed-price development bidding that the market has only partially priced.

**2. Executive Order 14372 (MODERATE-HIGH — Binary, Unclear Timeline):** If the Secretary of Defense designates LMT as "underperforming" (plausible given documented delivery issues), the capital return model (~$6.2B/year) could be curtailed. The bipartisan Hawley-Warren bill would make this statutory.

**3. Earnings Quality Concerns:** The $874M pension swing, $1.76B in program charges, and capital returns exceeding net income create an earnings profile that is both volatile and partially non-recurring. FCF ($6.9B) exceeds net income ($5.0B), but Q1 2026 showed negative FCF.

**4. Tariff Exposure ($485M cash impact in 2025):** Near-term margin headwind on fixed-price contracts with imported components.

**5. Rare Earth Supply Chain:** China's export controls create vulnerability for defense systems requiring specialized materials.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($620-650, ~21-27% upside):**
- FY2026 EPS of $29-30 materializes as classified program losses don't recur and MFC continues its margin recovery
- MFC production ramp accelerates, driving segment revenue toward $18-20B within 2-3 years
- EO 14372 is not applied to LMT, preserving the capital return model
- Defense budget momentum continues with bipartisan support, providing multi-year revenue visibility
- International F-35 and missile sales expand amid the current geopolitical environment

**Bear Case ($420-450, ~12-18% downside):**
- Additional reach-forward losses on classified Aeronautics program ($500M-$1B incremental)
- EO 14372 designation restricts buybacks and dividends
- F-35 sustainment reforms erode Lockheed's maintenance revenue stream
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $25-27, and the stock de-rates to 18x forward (NOC parity)
- Q2/Q3 2026 FCF remains weak, raising questions about dividend sustainability vs. debt load

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**

2. EO 14372 designation by the Secretary of Defense, restricting capital returns
3. FY2026 guidance cut below $27 EPS (from current ~$29.88 consensus)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected July 2026). The next 90 days should provide visibility on whether classified losses have been fully recognized and whether the MFC ramp is translating to consolidated margin improvement.

## Conclusion

Lockheed Martin presents genuinely uncertain risk/reward at current levels. The 27% decline from March highs reflects real fundamental deterioration — recurring program losses, margin compression, the NGAD franchise loss, and EO policy risk — rather than a temporary mispricing. At 17.2x forward earnings, the stock is reasonably priced if $29.88 materializes, but there is meaningful probability (30-40%) that it does not. The defense spending environment is highly supportive, and the MFC growth story is compelling, but these positives are offset by execution risks specific to Lockheed that have persisted for three consecutive years. The score trajectory decline from 7.3 to 5.9 over four months accurately reflects deteriorating fundamentals, and today's data does not warrant a reversal. HOLD until margin recovery is demonstrated.
