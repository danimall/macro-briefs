**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kirby is the largest domestic tank-barge operator in the United States (~28% of the inland tank-barge fleet) paired with a Distribution & Services segment increasingly levered to data-center power generation. The investment case mixes durable and fragile elements. The durable side: a fortress balance sheet (22.3% debt-to-cap against a 60% covenant limit), tightening marine supply from an aging fleet with minimal newbuild activity, and secular growth in KDS power-gen equipment (revenue +45% YoY in Q1 2026). The fragile side: the stock trades near its 52-week high at roughly 21x forward EPS with estimate revisions that have gone flat, while a March 2026 Jones Act waiver — the broadest in history, triggered by the Iran/Hormuz conflict — directly attacks the regulatory moat underpinning the marine franchise and expires 8/16/2026.

The scoring trajectory captures the tension. KEX moved from 6.5 (Apr) to 7.2 (May, BUY) to 6.2 (Jun, HOLD), with Competitive Position marked down two full points (8.0 → 6.0) as the Jones Act waiver crystallized as a live threat. The current data does not reverse that concern. Coastal marine is booming (+23% revenue, +19–21% term pricing on scarcity), but that strength is masking softening inland spot pricing (-4% to -6%) in the larger part of the marine business, and KDS margin compressed to 6.7% from 7.3% even as revenue grew. The recent price decline from ~$157 to ~$149 does not reflect fundamental deterioration; the setup no longer offers asymmetric upside at ~21x forward on flat estimates.

We rate KEX a HOLD (uncertain). The business is high quality and financially sound, but at the current price the risk/reward is roughly balanced: the 8/16 waiver decision is a two-sided catalyst that can move the stock in either direction, near-term options pricing (46.6% IV in backwardation, 3.9% put skew) signals the market expects volatility, and clustered C-suite selling into strength (COO O'Neil zeroing his direct holdings) argues against paying up here. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$120s, on a favorable waiver expiry, or on an inland spot pricing inflection.

## Company Overview

Kirby runs two segments. **Marine Transportation (KMT, ~59% of revenue)** operates 1,124 inland tank barges (25.1M barrels capacity), 284 inland towboats, and a coastal fleet of 27 tank barges and 24 tugboats, moving petrochemicals (47% of KMT), black oil (27%), refined products (22%), and agricultural chemicals (4%) along the Mississippi River System, the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway, and all three U.S. coasts. The core competitive protection is the Jones Act, which reserves U.S. coastwise trade for U.S.-flagged, -built, and -owned vessels, combined with scale-driven network density.

**Distribution & Services (KDS, ~41% of revenue)** spans Commercial & Industrial (46% of KDS — marine engine repair and MTU/Allison/EMD/Daimler distributorships), Power Generation (44% of KDS), and Oil & Gas (10% of KDS). KDS manufactures power-generation and electrical distribution/control equipment sold into the data-center buildout and behind-the-meter/backup power markets. Kirby is a supplier of power infrastructure to data centers, not an operator of them, a distinction that matters for both the growth ceiling and the margin profile.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year operating-leverage story is genuine. Revenue rose from ~$2.78B (2022) to ~$3.36B (2025); operating income more than doubled ($193M → ~$496M); net income roughly tripled. Adjusted EBITDA climbed $410M (2022) → $557M (2023) → $708M (2024) → $781M (2025).

Q1 2026 confirmed continued progress: total revenue $844.1M (+7.4% YoY), net earnings to Kirby $81.2M ($1.50 diluted EPS) versus $76.0M ($1.33), EPS +12.8% aided by a ~6% lower share count. Operating income was $107.7M (+2%) at a ~12.8% consolidated margin. Operating cash flow surged to $97.7M from $36.5M (+168%), helped by working-capital timing and the absence of a $24.4M tax payment that hit Q1 2025. The one-off tax component flatters the year-over-year comparison.

The segment detail carries the nuance. KMT revenue was $497.2M (+4%) at an 18.0% margin (vs 18.2%). Within that, coastal revenue jumped +23% ($83.7M → $103.1M) on term-contract renewals of +19% to +21%, while inland spot pricing was negative (-4% to -6%) and inland term was flat-to-+2%, with inland utilization slipping to low-90s% from low-to-mid-90s%. KDS revenue was $346.9M (+12%) but operating income only +3%, with margin compressing to 6.7% from 7.3%; power generation grew +45% ($104.5M → $151.8M), C&I was roughly flat, and oil & gas fell -25% on the conventional-to-electric-frac shift.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story. Total debt is ~$986M against $3.42B equity, giving debt-to-cap of 22.3% versus a 60% covenant limit, with interest coverage comfortably above the 2.5x minimum. On 3/26/2026 Kirby refinanced into a new $750M revolver maturing 2031 (with a $500M accordion, ~$570M available) and removed the term loan. Senior notes are $500M at 4.2% (2028) and $300M at 3.46–3.51% (2033), with no near-term maturity wall. 2026 capex is guided to $220–260M. Capital return is buyback-only (no dividend): $52.7M repurchased in Q1 2026 at an average $123.18, following $101.5M in Q1 2025, cutting diluted share count from ~57.3M to ~54.0M, with ~6.7M shares remaining authorized.

The earnings-surprise record is consistent but modest: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with the largest surprise a 6.6% Q1 2026 beat. That pattern supports confidence in consensus but does not signal an inflection that would justify a re-rating.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two legs of unequal quality. The marine leg rests on structurally tight supply: no coastal ATBs are under construction industry-wide, the coastal fleet in Kirby's size class is aging (22 barges over 25 years old), and inland newbuilds (~60–70 for 2026) barely offset retirements from a fleet with ~600 barges aged 30+. That scarcity is real and is driving coastal term pricing. But inland (79% of KMT revenue) is showing negative spot renewals and slipping utilization, so the marine growth engine is running on the smaller coastal segment right now.

The more attractive leg is KDS power generation, tied to the data-center power buildout. Kirby's exposure is genuine and diversifies it away from cyclical marine and declining oil & gas. The caveat is margin: power-gen growth is flowing through at lower margins on an equipment-delivery-heavy mix with OEM engine supply delays pushing projects into H2 2026. Higher-margin service and parts should follow equipment installs, but the current growth is margin-dilutive.

Estimate revisions temper the enthusiasm. Consensus is essentially flat: current-quarter estimates were cut -4.9% over 90 days (partially offset by a +0.7% 30-day nudge), while current-year (+1.6%) and next-year (+1.6%) revisions are marginally positive. This is not the +30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine inflection. Consensus sees current-year EPS ~$7.02 and next-year ~$8.26, implying mid-teens growth, which is respectable for a capital-intensive operator but does not, on its own, support paying a premium multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

At $148.65, KEX trades at ~22.9x trailing and ~21.2x forward (on $7.02 current-year consensus), near the top of its 52-week range ($79.52–$157.69). Against the Industrials peer set — Federal Signal 25.1x, Simpson 22.7x, Terex 43.3x, Booz Allen 11.2x, Builders FirstSource 29.8x, median ~25.1x — KEX screens at a modest discount to the median. But that peer group is heterogeneous, and the more relevant benchmark is the company's own cycle position and growth rate. At ~21x forward on a mid-teens EPS growth outlook, the PEG is roughly 1.3–1.5, fair rather than cheap.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the case. Kirby is not at a trough: earnings have already tripled off 2022, EBITDA has nearly doubled, and coastal pricing is at cyclical highs. There is no earnings inflection signal (revisions are flat, not +30%), so forward P/E is the right metric, and it points to a stock that is fairly valued for its growth rather than mispriced. Analyst targets ($158 low, $170.5 median, $182 high) sit 6–22% above the current price, offering some cushion, but on a stock that has already run 28% YTD to near its high with flat estimates, we treat those targets as fair-value anchors rather than floors, since the guardrail for treating targets as floors (cheap relative to accelerating estimates) is not met here.

The valuation score reflects genuine caution. A high valuation multiple in a sector where "cheap" has historically been a value-trap warning does not automatically earn a low score, but here the issue is the reverse: the stock is not cheap, the growth does not clearly justify a premium, and the one identifiable catalyst (the Jones Act waiver) is two-sided. We score valuation 4.8; the multiple is defensible but offers no asymmetric upside at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Kirby's marine moat is Jones Act protection plus dominant scale, and its KDS moat is OEM distributorship relationships (MTU/Allison/EMD/Daimler) and manufacturing capability in power-gen equipment. The structural supply picture in marine is favorable: industry consolidation of secondhand tonnage (Kirby is a serial acquirer, having spent $95.8M on 23 barges + 3 towboats in March 2026 and $97.3M the prior year) simultaneously grows share and removes capacity from a tight market. Petrochemical demand is supported by cheap U.S. Gulf Coast natural-gas feedstock.

The competitive-position trajectory is the reason for the score markdown from prior reports. The Jones Act waiver of March 2026 is the single most important development. On 3/17/2026, DHS (at DoD request) issued one of the broadest Jones Act waivers in history following the Operation Epic Fury strikes on Iran and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, opening coastwise trade to foreign-flagged/built/owned vessels across 659 (later 671) commodity categories. The waiver has been extended and expanded. Kirby's coastal moat — the segment currently enjoying the strongest term pricing — is directly exposed. To date, foreign-vessel use has been small and the independent estimate of the gasoline-price benefit is negligible ($0.000157/gal), and 52 House Republicans (including Speaker Johnson and Leader Scalise) have urged letting the waiver expire on schedule (Washington Post, 7/1/2026; Altoona Mirror, 7/8/2026). But usage is growing (63 waiver instances by 5/26 versus 30 on 5/4), and the precedent that the moat can be suspended by executive action erodes the competitive premium regardless of the near-term financial impact. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating in trajectory, which we score at 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Jones Act waiver (the defining binary).** This is the standout, non-standard risk and it resolves around 8/16/2026. A broadened, repeated, or precedent-setting waiver would erode the coastal pricing power that is doing the heavy lifting in the marine segment. Kirby's own Q1 10-Q added a new risk-factor update acknowledging that an extension "could increase competition from non-U.S. vessels." The political outcome is genuinely uncertain, making this a two-sided catalyst with outcomes not fully priced.

**Inland spot softening.** Inland posted negative spot renewals and slipping utilization. The bull case depends on limited newbuild supply eventually tightening inland the way it has tightened coastal, but the near-term data shows the inland cycle is not yet hot.

**KDS margin compression and fuel lag.** KDS margin fell on mix; management flagged Q2 2026 margin compression from rising diesel via the 30–120 day fuel-escalator lag. Both are near-term headwinds to reported earnings.

**Customer concentration.** Five KMT customers account for ~17% of 2025 revenue; three KDS customers ~9%, with growing power-gen/data-center concentration a newly disclosed dependency.

**Other.** Aging inland lock-and-dam infrastructure (>50% of locks are 50+ years old), mariner labor cost inflation, weather/spill exposure, KDS input-cost tariffs, and the still-open Canadian Nathan E. Stewart spill litigation (believed insured, unquantified).

We score risk 4.5. The combination of a binary catalyst in the near term, concentration, and inland softness represents idiosyncratic downside not fully reflected in a stock near its 52-week high.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. ATM IV has jumped from 32.8% to 46.6% over the past month (rich versus 36.8% one-year historical vol, 9.8pp spread), the term structure is in backwardation (46.6% near-term vs 34.2% at six months) signaling imminent event risk, and there is elevated put demand (3.9% put skew at the front). The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.1%. This aligns with the 7/29/2026 Q2 earnings date and the 8/16/2026 waiver expiry: the market expects the stock to move on those catalysts, and the put skew leans toward downside protection. For a BUY thesis this would be a divergence to flag; for our HOLD it corroborates the two-sided, event-driven setup and argues against chasing before the catalysts resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($173–$182).** The Jones Act waiver expires without extension, removing the overhang and reaffirming coastal pricing power. Inland spot pricing inflects positive as tight newbuild supply catches up with demand. KDS power-gen growth continues at 40%+ with margins recovering as higher-margin service/parts follow equipment installs. Buybacks continue reducing share count ~6% annually. Coastal term renewals hold. In this scenario, ~$8.50+ EPS power supports a ~21x multiple toward $180, consistent with the analyst high target.

**Bear Case ($115–$125).** The waiver is extended or broadened, foreign-vessel competition compresses coastal pricing back toward flat, and the precedent triggers a re-rating of the entire marine moat's value. Inland spot pricing continues softening, KDS margins stay compressed on OEM delays, and rising diesel squeezes reported earnings through H2. In that case, the multiple compresses toward ~16–17x on flat-to-down EPS, taking the stock into the mid-$110s to mid-$120s, roughly 16–23% below the current price.

**Base Case ($150–$160).** The waiver expires or is only modestly extended with limited practical impact, coastal pricing holds while inland stabilizes, KDS grows with gradual margin recovery, and EPS lands near consensus. The stock roughly holds current levels, appreciating in line with buyback-driven EPS accretion. This yields a fair value modestly above the current price but not enough to compensate for the binary risk of chasing here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The business is high quality and financially sound, but at ~21x forward on flat estimates, near its 52-week high, ahead of a two-sided regulatory catalyst, the risk/reward is balanced. The re-rating from prior reports is largely complete, and the current price does not offer asymmetric upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Jones Act waiver expires without meaningful extension, removing the overhang, combined with a pullback below ~$135.
- Inland spot pricing inflects positive (renewals turn to +2% or better) with utilization recovering to mid-90s%, confirming the broader marine cycle is tightening.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (+5% or more over 90 days) on KDS margin recovery and continued coastal strength, supporting a higher forward earnings base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Jones Act waiver is extended and broadened materially, with foreign-vessel usage accelerating enough to visibly compress coastal term pricing on renewal.
- Inland spot renewals deteriorate further (worse than -6%) alongside falling utilization, signaling a marine downcycle rather than a coastal/inland divergence.
- KDS margin compresses below ~6% or power-gen revenue growth decelerates sharply, undermining the diversification thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release on 7/29/2026 and the Jones Act waiver expiry on 8/16/2026, both within three weeks. These two events will substantially resolve the current uncertainty.

## Conclusion

Kirby is a well-run, conservatively capitalized operator with a genuine data-center power-generation growth vector and a structurally tight marine market. But those qualities do not, at $148.65, make it a compelling entry. The stock has re-rated 28% YTD to near its 52-week high, estimate revisions are flat, coastal strength is masking inland softness, and a live Jones Act waiver poses a two-sided binary directly at the regulatory moat, the kind of idiosyncratic, near-term catalyst that the elevated, backwardated options structure and clustered C-suite selling are flagging. The prior BUY (May) has since been walked back to HOLD, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that caution. We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale, preferring to reassess after the 7/29 earnings print and 8/16 waiver decision, and would look for a pullback toward the mid-$120s or a favorable waiver outcome before turning constructive.
