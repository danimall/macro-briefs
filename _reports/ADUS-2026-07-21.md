**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Addus HomeCare is a multi-state in-home care provider whose economics run almost entirely through government payors. Roughly 77% of revenue comes from personal care, funded predominantly by state Medicaid/HCBS programs and managed care organizations; hospice (~18%, mostly Medicare) and a deliberately shrinking home health segment (~5%) round out the mix. The company has executed a consistent consolidation strategy (organic census/hours growth plus disciplined tuck-in acquisitions) that doubled net income from $46M (2022) to $96M (2025) and lifted revenue 23% in 2025, largely on the Gentiva personal care acquisition closed December 2024. The business is asset-light (capex ~$7.7M against $111.5M operating cash flow in 2025) and has deleveraged rapidly, cutting long-term debt from $218M at end-2024 to $91M by Q1 2026.

The core tension is that this is a policy-sensitive name with essentially no pricing power. Economics hinge on state/federal rate-setting and the pace at which reimbursement rates offset caregiver wage inflation. Two structural overhangs define the bear case: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted July 2025), which is expected to reduce Medicaid spending and restrict federal matching funds, and the CMS "80/20" rule requiring ≥80% of Medicaid personal care payments flow to direct-worker compensation by ~2030, which could compress margins in the largest segment. Against those, the 2026 setup carries a concrete tailwind: Illinois raised its personal care rate to $30.80/hr effective January 1, 2026, and a softening labor market in key states is easing the caregiver shortage that had capped billable hours.

The stock has recovered from a June low near $88 to roughly $111, tracking stable-to-rising consensus estimates (current-year EPS revised +1.6% over 90 days to $7.00) and continued M&A cadence (HomeCourt/Indiana closed May 2026). At ~16x forward EPS the multiple is undemanding for a mid-single-digit compounder with expanding margins, but the policy binaries are unresolved, insiders provide no open-market conviction signal, and Illinois represents ~32% of total revenue. We rate ADUS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: there is a credible path to appreciation on rate tailwinds and continued deleveraging, but the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric until the Medicaid-policy overhangs clarify.

## Company Overview

Addus operates through 263 offices across 23 states (200 personal care, 40 hospice, 22 home health) as of Q1 2026. Its consumers are predominantly dual-eligible (both Medicare and Medicaid), and its value proposition is being a lower-cost alternative to institutional care, aligned with the secular shift toward home-based delivery and the migration of Medicaid business to managed care organizations (38.3% of revenue in Q1 2026, up from 36.4%).

The three segments have distinct economics. Personal care is the growth engine: non-medical assistance with activities of daily living, paid largely hourly by state Medicaid/HCBS programs and MCOs. Hospice is the highest-margin segment (~46% gross margin), ~94% Medicare-funded on daily rates. Home health is skilled nursing/therapy on Medicare episodic payments; management is deliberately shrinking it, culling unprofitable volume to lift gross margin from ~40% to 45.3% while revenue fell 7% YoY.

The competitive frame is a fragmented personal care market being consolidated by a scaled operator. Addus's ability to offer all levels of in-home care in a given market differentiates it from mom-and-pop operators, and its balance sheet and integration capability let it acquire smaller providers accretively.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue progressed $951M (2022) → $1,059M (2023, +11%) → $1,155M (2024, +9%) → $1,423M (2025, +23%). The 2025 step-change reflects Gentiva plus tuck-ins (Helping Hands, Gold Horses). Q1 2026 revenue was $363.6M (+7.7% YoY), with operating income up 11.7% on that top-line growth, a clean read on operating leverage. Operating margin has climbed from 7.2% (2022) to 9.7% (2025); gross margin sits near 32%. Net income progression ($46M → $63M → $74M → $96M) represents a doubling in three years, and Q1 2026 net income of $25.1M (+18% YoY) at a 6.9% net margin continued the trend.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $111.5M against FCF of $103.8M; the ~7% capex intensity confirms an asset-light model with high FCF conversion (well above 100% of net income). Q1 2026 OCF surged to $52.4M from $18.9M on faster AR collections, with DSO improving to 36 days. Only ~$94M of a $650M revolver (with a $150M incremental) is drawn, leaving ~$548M of dry powder, net leverage well under the 3.75x covenant, and $103M cash. Interest expense halved YoY in Q1 2026 ($2.2M vs $4.0M), directly aiding EPS.

**Balance sheet composition.** Equity is $1.12B, but goodwill ($997M) plus intangibles ($100M) equal roughly 76% of total assets, the natural consequence of the roll-up model and the source of impairment risk should reimbursement deteriorate materially. This is not a solvency concern given the modest debt and strong FCF, but it is a quality caveat on book value.

**Earnings quality and estimate momentum.** The beat/miss record over four quarters is mixed (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss), with recent surprises small (Q1 2026 +2.4%). Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year EPS +1.6% over 90 days to $7.00, next-year to $7.48. This is steady positive momentum, not the >30% revision velocity that flags an earnings inflection. We treat it as confirmation of a durable mid-single-digit compounding profile rather than a signal of underappreciated acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm has three components. First, organic census/hours growth in personal care, currently supported by the Illinois rate increase and a softening labor market; the swing variable is whether Addus can convert available labor into hours faster than in 2025, when personal care census was roughly flat and a soft Q1 2026 revenue print drew a negative market reaction. Second, disciplined tuck-in M&A: Jacksonville (Jan 2025), Great Lakes (Mar 2025), Helping Hands/PA ($21.4M, Aug 2025, entering PA hospice/home health), Gold Horses/TX ($7.4M, Oct 2025), and HomeCourt/Indiana (~$12.5M, May 2026). With ~$548M of revolver capacity and strong FCF, the acquisition engine is well-funded. Third, margin-driven earnings growth as operating leverage and deleveraging compound.

The secular backdrop is favorable: aging demographics and payor preference for lower-cost home-based care support long-term volume. But the ceiling is set by reimbursement rates, not by demand. Growth here is a function of state Medicaid budget health and the wage-reimbursement spread, which makes the top line more policy-dependent than execution-dependent. Consensus of mid-single-digit organic growth plus M&A supports ~7% current-year EPS growth ($7.00 vs a $6.89 base 90 days ago) and ~7% into next year ($7.48). That is a solid but unspectacular trajectory, and the near-term risk is that OBBBA-driven state budget pressure caps organic growth even as rate increases arrive.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$111, ADUS trades at ~20.6x trailing EPS ($5.22) and roughly 15.8–16x forward EPS. The peer table is not directly useful: the health care services comps span profitless names (NEO, AMN, SDGR) and a high-growth outlier (PGNY at 33.6x), producing a distorted "peer median" of 33.6x that overstates a fair multiple for a low-growth, government-payor provider. The more relevant frame is Addus's own history and its cash-flow profile: ~5% FCF yield ($103.8M FCF on a ~$2.02B market cap) and a self-funding M&A model at 16x forward earnings for a mid-single-digit compounder.

The valuation is not stretched, and prior appreciation from the June low is not a reason to mark it down. But cheapness alone does not earn a high valuation score, and here the compressed multiple reflects genuine policy risk rather than a clean mispricing. There is no identified near-term catalyst that forces a re-rating: estimate revisions are positive but modest, and the two structural overhangs are precisely the kind of uncertainty that keeps a policy-sensitive name at a discount. Analyst targets (mean $133, median $135, range $96–$155) imply ~20% upside to the mean, but those targets embed a benign policy resolution that is not yet visible in the data. We score valuation 5.5: undemanding but appropriately discounted for the binary policy overhang, with no independent catalyst to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

Addus's position is stable-to-improving within its niche. As a scaled consolidator in a fragmented market, it gains share through accretive tuck-ins and benefits from the payor shift to MCOs, which prefer coordinated, multi-service providers. The three-service model in a single market is a modest structural advantage over single-line operators. The New York exit (deconsolidated October 2024 over CDPAP-program uncertainty) demonstrates disciplined portfolio pruning rather than distress.

The competitive caveat is that Addus competes for reimbursement dollars, not customers, so its "moat" is operational scale and state-relationship depth rather than pricing power or brand. Personal care census was roughly flat in Q1 2026, which suggests share gains are incremental and dependent on labor availability. The deliberate shrinkage of home health is quality-over-quantity management, not deterioration. On balance the trajectory is stable with a slight positive tilt from M&A share capture, warranting a middling competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by policy and concentration, and several of these are binary and not fully priced.

1. **Medicaid funding / OBBBA (dominant risk).** The Act imposes greater restrictions on expansion states. Because personal care is overwhelmingly Medicaid/HCBS-funded, state budget cuts, eligibility restrictions, or waiver changes threaten the core segment directly. Block-grant/per-capita-cap proposals are a tail risk.

2. **Extreme payor and geographic concentration.** Illinois is ~32% of total revenue; the Illinois Department on Aging alone is ~18% of revenue and 23% of net receivables. Any Illinois budget disruption, payment delay, or rate reversal is material. Personal care is further concentrated in IL/TX/NM.

3. **The 80/20 rule.** If implemented as written, this structurally compresses margins in the largest segment. Timing is uncertain due to litigation and administration changes, but it is a real long-term margin ceiling.

4. **Wage-reimbursement mismatch.** Caregiver shortages and rising minimums (Chicago $16.60, IL direct-worker minimum $18.75) require offsetting rate increases. The 2026 IL rate helps, but there is no guarantee of future offsets; any year rates lag wages compresses margin.

5. **Smaller-segment rate pressure.** Home health Medicare payments are estimated to fall 1.3% in CY2026; the hospice per-beneficiary cap ($35,361 FY2026) can trigger repayments.

6. **Goodwill/impairment.** With ~76% of assets in goodwill and intangibles, a sustained reimbursement deterioration could force impairment.

The combination of a dated structural threat (80/20 by 2030), a near-term unquantified funding threat (OBBBA), and single-state concentration at ~32% justifies a below-average risk score. These are specific, asymmetric catalysts that could move the stock and are only partially reflected at 16x forward.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Illinois rate increase and a loosening labor market let Addus convert available caregivers into billable hours, reaccelerating organic personal care growth from the flat Q1 2026 base. Continued deleveraging and operating leverage push EPS toward and beyond the $7.48 next-year consensus, while dry powder funds accretive tuck-ins. OBBBA implementation proves manageable at the state level, the 80/20 rule is delayed or softened by litigation, and the multiple re-rates toward the mid-$130s analyst target as policy risk clears. Bull case ~$135–$155.

**Bear case.** OBBBA-driven state Medicaid budget pressure caps organic growth and pressures Illinois specifically, rate increases fail to keep pace with wage inflation, and the 80/20 rule advances as written, compressing personal care margins into 2027–2030. A soft revenue print or an Illinois payment disruption drives a de-rating toward the low end of the target range. Bear case ~$85–$96, roughly 14–23% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The base case is a mid-single-digit compounder with a clean balance sheet and a real 2026 rate tailwind, offset by unresolved policy binaries that keep the risk/reward balanced.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Personal care census/hours reaccelerating to mid-single-digit organic growth in Q2/Q3 2026 prints (evidence the IL rate + labor easing is converting to volume), OR
- Concrete OBBBA implementation clarity showing minimal impact on Illinois/HCBS funding, removing the dominant overhang, OR
- Price pullback toward the low-to-mid $90s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetry (forward P/E toward ~13x).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An Illinois budget/payment disruption or rate reversal affecting the Illinois Department on Aging exposure, OR
- 80/20 rule finalized on schedule with quantified margin impact management cannot offset, OR
- Two consecutive quarters of falling estimate revisions alongside declining personal care census.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings print (early August), with the trajectory of federal Medicaid rulemaking and any Illinois state budget developments as the ongoing policy watch items.

## Conclusion

Addus is a well-run, asset-light home-care consolidator that has doubled earnings in three years, deleveraged aggressively, and enters 2026 with a genuine Illinois rate tailwind and M&A firepower. At ~16x forward earnings the stock is not expensive for its quality and cash generation, and the ~27% recovery from the June low reflects stable-to-rising estimates rather than any fundamental deterioration. The reason to hold rather than buy is that the thesis hinges on Medicaid policy that Addus does not control. OBBBA funding pressure and the 80/20 rule are specific, asymmetric overhangs against single-state concentration, and there is no open-market insider buying to signal conviction near current levels. An upgrade to BUY is warranted on either confirmed organic-volume reacceleration, OBBBA clarity, or a pullback that restores asymmetry. Until one of those arrives, ADUS is a HOLD.

The declining score trajectory (6.9 → 5.8) reflects the progressive crystallization of these policy risks rather than a business that is weakening; financials, in fact, improved through the deleveraging. Our 5.9 overall marks a stabilization: financial health is genuinely strong, but valuation and risk remain appropriately capped by the policy binaries.
