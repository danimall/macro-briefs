## Executive Summary

UnitedHealth has done what a broken managed-care franchise is supposed to do after an underwriting failure: reprice, shed unprofitable membership, cut benefits, and let earnings recover ahead of revenue. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance has been raised twice, to $19.50–$20.00 from an initial ">$18.25", and consensus for the current year has moved up 7.7% in thirty days to $19.81. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $6.38 beat the ~$4.91 consensus by roughly 30%. The buyback target was doubled to at least $5B, with $4B already deployed at a derived average of ~$351 per share, and Q1 forward repurchase contracts were struck at $285.68, both meaningfully below today's price. On the reported numbers, this is a company two quarters into a credible turn.

The problem is what sits underneath the reported numbers. Note 4 of the Q1 2026 10-Q decomposes medical costs, and the arithmetic is unambiguous: current-year medical costs of $74,726M on premiums of $87,561M produce an accident-period MCR of 85.34%, versus 73,731/86,534 = 85.20% in Q1 2025. The current-year pick got 14 basis points *worse*. The entire reported 90bp MCR improvement, and more, came from $1,050M of favorable prior-year development plus a $187M loss-reserve release, $917M of incremental credits equal to 1.05% of quarterly premiums. Q2 repeated the pattern with $860M of favorable development. The CFO's own characterization is that medical costs remain "elevated over historical levels" and that recovery is a "multi-year journey." An alternative reading deserves weight: a company that badly under-reserved in 2024–2025 would rationally set conservative initial picks, which would suppress the current-year comparison even if true trend improved. I cannot distinguish these from outside the reserve triangles. The data does not support the claim that H1 2026 demonstrated underlying cost improvement.

At $405.51 the stock trades at 20.5x FY2026 guidance, 18.1x FY2027 consensus of $22.44, and 30.6x trailing GAAP EPS of $13.23, with a 2.18% dividend yield. Revenue is guided to at least $439B against FY2025 actual of $447.6B, a decline. Guided adjusted EPS returns the company only to roughly its 2021 level ($19.02) on about 20% more revenue and a smaller share count. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on this name. What justifies repeating rather than upgrading is that the recovery's foundation remains unverified while its price is now largely paid. Entry arithmetic has improved: the forward multiple compressed from roughly 23x to 20.5x since mid-July as the price fell 5% while estimates rose 7.7%. That is a better risk/reward on unchanged fundamentals, not a thesis change.

## Company Overview

Two businesses, four reportable segments. **UnitedHealthcare** generated $344.9B of FY2025 segment revenue: Employer & Individual $79.2B (29.7M people), Medicare & Retirement $171.3B (8.4M Medicare Advantage members plus 4.3M Supplement and 2.8M stand-alone Part D), and Community & State $94.4B (~7.4M Medicaid lives across 32 states plus D.C.). Total medical membership was 49.76M at 12/31/2025, 49.05M at 3/31/2026, and 48.5M at 6/30/2026.

**Optum** generated $270.6B of gross segment revenue: Optum Health $102.0B (care delivery, value-based arrangements, ~95M consumers served), Optum Insight $19.4B (analytics, revenue cycle, the acquired Change Healthcare business), and Optum Rx $154.7B (PBM managing $188B of pharmaceutical spend including ~$87B specialty).

Two structural features dominate the investment case. First, Optum's scale is largely internal. Consolidated FY2025 revenue of $447.6B reflects $168.0B of intersegment eliminations, 37.5% of gross segment revenue. In Q1 2026, Optum's $63.7B of revenue comprised $38.3B affiliated and $24.8B unaffiliated, 60.1% internal. UnitedHealthcare pays Optum for pharmacy, care delivery and administrative services; when UnitedHealthcare membership shrinks, Optum shrinks with it. Optum Rx adjusted scripts fell to 383M from 408M in Q1 2026 (-6.1%) and segment margin to 3.3% from 3.8%, which the 10-Q attributes explicitly to "contraction in people served at UnitedHealthcare."

Second, government payer concentration. CMS premium revenue was **44% of total consolidated revenue** in FY2025. Add state-funded Medicaid and government-sourced revenue exceeds half. Risk-based products are "nearly 80% of total consolidated revenues." In economic substance UNH is a leveraged position on the adequacy of administratively set reimbursement rates relative to medical cost trend, and the 10-K states that rate notices "for numerous years have resulted in industry base rates well below the industry forward medical cost trend."

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 reset was severe and is masked by headline revenue growth.

| (FY, $M except EPS) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenues | 371,622 | 400,278 | 447,567 |
| Earnings from operations | 32,358 | 32,287 | 18,964 |
| Net earnings to shareholders | 22,381 | 14,405 | 12,056 |
| Diluted EPS | $23.86 | $15.51 | $13.23 |
| Medical care ratio | 83.2% | 85.5% | **89.1%** |
| Operating margin | 8.7% | 8.1% | **4.2%** |
| Cash from operations | 29,068 | 24,204 | 19,697 |

Revenue grew 11.8% in 2025 while operating earnings fell 41.3%. The MCR deteriorated 590bp over two years. Management's own 10-K language is blunt: pricing and health-status assumptions were "well-short of the medical cost trends incurred."

Segment damage was concentrated but broad. Optum Health swung from $7.77B of operating earnings in 2024 to a **$278M operating loss** in 2025, an $8.05B reversal. Optum Insight's margin halved from 22.5% (2023) to 13.5% (2025), reflecting the Change Healthcare cyberattack aftermath and business-services attrition. UnitedHealthcare's margin fell to 2.7% from 5.8% in 2023.

**Earnings quality carried heavy one-time traffic in both directions.** Q4 2025 contained +$568M net portfolio divestiture gains, –$2.5B of restructuring and other actions (including a **$623M loss-contract reserve for anticipated 2026 losses** on certain value-based contracts, $137M of which was reversed into Q1 2026 earnings), and –$799M of additional Change Healthcare provider-loan reserves. Netting these, roughly $2.7B of Q4 2025 charges were non-recurring or forward-shifted, which flatters the 2026 base. Loss-contract accounting is standard and was disclosed; the mechanical effect is nonetheless to move income from 2025 into 2026.

**Favorable prior-year reserve development collapsed before it reversed.** It ran $840M (2023), $700M (2024), $140M (2025), then $1,050M in Q1 2026 alone and $860M in Q2. That swing is the single most important item in the file. The 10-K quantifies the sensitivity: a 1% difference between estimated and actual medical costs payable would have moved 2025 net earnings by roughly $300M, or 2.5% of FY2025 earnings.

**Cash flow improved genuinely but should not be annualized.** FY2025 OCF was $19,697M against $12,056M of net earnings (1.63x); capex $3,622M implies FCF of $16,075M, a 3.59% FCF margin and a 4.4% FCF yield on today's market capitalization. Dividends of $7,916M plus buybacks of $5,545M consumed 83.7% of that FCF. Q1 2026 OCF was $8,912M versus $5,456M, and Q2 was reported at $11.1B, so H1 2026 OCF of roughly $20.0B already matches all of FY2025. Management attributes the Q1 improvement to Inflation Reduction Act pharmacy-rebate timing and working-capital changes, and UNH separately operates a $3.3B uncommitted 364-day receivables facility (it sold $585M of receivables in Q1 2026 while remitting $2.0B related to 2025 sales). As a labeled sensitivity only: annualizing H1 would approach $40B; a normalized figure stripping rebate timing and receivables mechanics would be materially lower, and the disclosure is insufficient to quantify it.

**Consolidated leverage is unremarkable; holding-company liquidity is the binding constraint.** At 3/31/2026: total debt $77.9B ($6,477M current plus $71,440M long-term) against shareholders' equity of $97.9B, 0.80x debt/equity, 42.9% debt/total capital. That is normal for a scaled managed-care holding company. The screening current ratio of 0.79 and Z-Score of 2.7 are structurally expected here (medical costs payable of $39.7B are current; $56.8B of the offsetting portfolio is classified long-term) and do not indicate distress.

The disclosure that matters is different. Of $28.0B of cash at 3/31/2026, **only $1.1B was available for general corporate use**; the remainder sits in regulated subsidiaries under statutory capital minimums and dividend approval. And the direction reversed: U.S. regulated subsidiaries received **net capital infusions of $535M in 2025** versus paying **$9.2B of net dividends upstream in 2024**, a roughly $9.7B swing in parent-level cash availability. The 10-K then adds that because of the higher MCR, required statutory capital "is expected to increase." Against annual dividends of roughly $8.0B and a raised ≥$5B buyback target, that is the mechanism by which a margin problem becomes a capital-return problem. All three major agencies (Moody's A2, S&P A+, Fitch A) carry Negative outlooks. Goodwill of $110.5B plus $20.1B of other intangibles equals 42% of total assets, sitting against a segment that posted an operating loss in 2025; the October 1, 2025 impairment test concluded all reporting units had fair values "substantially in excess" of carrying value, which is management's audited conclusion reached before the Q4 charges and 2026 VBC contraction were fully known.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is a revenue contraction paired with an earnings recovery.

FY2026 revenue guidance of ≥$439B against FY2025 actual of $447.567B, set against H1 2026 revenue of $223.75B, implies H2 of ≥$215.3B, a lower second half than first, consistent with membership attrition through the year. That follows +11.8% in 2025 and +7.7% in 2024.

Membership is the driver. Q1 2026 medical membership of 49.05M was down 1,075K year over year: Medicare Advantage –690K (–8%), commercial risk-based –685K (–8%), Medicaid –410K (–5%), partly offset by fee-based commercial +750K. By 6/30/2026 membership was 48.5M, with MA down 965K since year-end. Management guides to roughly 1.1M MA and 500K exchange losses for the full year. Optum Health served 93M at 3/31/2026 versus 95M a year earlier, with value-based-care patients down about 700K.

Because 60% of Optum revenue is affiliated, that attrition flows directly into Optum Rx script volume and Optum Health VBC lives. Optum Insight's external franchise is separately eroding: disclosed backlog fell from $32.8B to $31.1B while the affiliated portion rose from $12.5B to $12.9B, implying third-party backlog down from $20.3B to $18.2B (–10.3%). UNH's "backlog" includes "anticipated contract renewals based on historical experience"; the ASC 606 remaining performance obligation is a different and much smaller $11.0B. These are not interchangeable measures.

Earnings growth off the 2025 trough is real but bounded. Guided FY2026 adjusted EPS of $19.50–$20.00 (midpoint $19.75) compares with proxy-disclosed adjusted EPS of $16.35 (2025), $27.66 (2024), $25.12 (2023) and $19.02 (2021). From 2023 to guided 2026, revenue grows roughly 18–20% while adjusted EPS falls about 21%, on a smaller share count (950M diluted in 2022 to ~910M in Q1 2026). A reproducible bridge: $19.75 × ~905M diluted ≈ $17.9B adjusted net income; add ~$0.8B NCI, gross up at ~20% tax to ~$23.4B pretax, add ~$3.8B interest expense → ~$27.2B adjusted operating earnings on ~$443B revenue = **~6.1% adjusted operating margin**, versus 8.7% GAAP in 2023. Treat 6.1% as an approximation using assumed tax and NCI rather than a company figure. The recovery restores growth *rates* without restoring the prior margin *level*.

FY2027 consensus of $22.44 implies +13% off 2026, consistent with management's reaffirmed 13–16% long-term target "to be reached by 2028," with the CFO saying Optum Health revenue growth should "fully return in 2028."

**Estimate revisions are positive but do not clear the inflection threshold.** Current year +7.7% over 30 days and +7.9% over 90 days; next year +7.2% and +8.1%. That is genuine positive earnings momentum, well short of the +30% velocity that reliably precedes sustained re-rating, and it accompanies declining revenue rather than accelerating revenue. The next-quarter estimate was cut 17.6% (from $2.59 to $2.14) while the current quarter rose 10.4% (to $4.12). Analysts are pulling earnings forward into Q3 and marking Q4 down to roughly flat against Q4 2025's $2.11. The full-year recovery is front-half loaded, and Q4 2026 is where a reserve-release-dependent story would first crack.

Cost trend itself is not improving. UnitedHealthcare's CEO put commercial medical cost trend "modestly above 11%" on the Q2 call and described commercial costs as "stubbornly high... across the sector"; the commercial unit head attributed roughly 0.5 percentage points to No Surprises Act billing-dispute arbitration and stated: "Simply put, we're not yielding the full margin expansion for which we planned in 2026." That is a management admission inside a quarter the market read as a clean beat.

Elevance at 17x on a similar-quality government-weighted book is the only clean read, and UNH trades at a modest premium to it on FY2027 estimates, defensible given UNH's scale and integration, not obviously cheap.

The case for the stock being mispriced upward rests on 2028: if the 13–16% target holds from a $22.44 2027 base, adjusted EPS approaches $25–26 in 2028, and 20x that is $500–520. Analyst mean of $475.23 and median of $490 sit inside that band; the high of $529 essentially prices that path with confidence. The case against is that revenue is shrinking, the adjusted operating margin remains roughly 2.6 points below 2023, and two of the three raised-guidance quarters lean on reserve credits totalling $1.91B, approximately 10% of guided full-year adjusted net income.

Where I come out: 20.5x forward on guided non-GAAP earnings for a business with declining revenue, 44% CMS concentration, three negative rating outlooks and an unquantified tax claim is neither distressed nor stretched. It is a fair multiple on an earnings base whose durability is unproven. The dimension score of 5.8 reflects an improvement from the prior cycle (the multiple compressed roughly 12% since mid-July as price fell 5% and estimates rose 7.7%) without reaching the threshold where I would argue the market is wrong.

## Competitive Landscape

UNH remains the largest MA carrier, the largest PBM by managed spend, and the only payer with a care-delivery arm at 95M-consumer scale. Level of position is high. Trajectory is negative, and that matters more.

Involuntary erosion is visible in three places. Provider bargaining power has shifted: the 10-K names hospitals and multi-specialty physician groups with "significant market positions which could diminish our bargaining power" and cites "increases in provider pricing and additional services bundled per visit." The No Surprises Act independent dispute resolution process is quantified by management as adding ~0.5pt to commercial trend and accounting for at least one full point of the total commercial increase. Optum Insight's third-party backlog is down 10.3% with margin halved since 2023, consistent with lasting customer attrition after the February 2024 cyberattack.

Voluntary contraction is the rest of it. Shedding 1.1M MA and 500K exchange members to repair margin is rational; it is also share ceded to competitors who may keep it. The shift toward fee-based commercial membership (22.34M at 3/31/2026 from 21.59M, while risk-based fell to 7.73M from 8.41M) reduces insurance risk per member but does not reduce payer concentration: CMS remains 44% of revenue, and MA plus Medicaid remain the revenue engine.

UNH's own disclosures identify company-specific aggravators of the 2025 failure: "the member profile of newly added patients under value-based care arrangements," "additional people served by our Medicare Advantage plans in markets where other plans exited," and "market morbidity changes on our individual exchange offerings." Reading those together, UNH grew into adverse selection in 2023–2024, absorbing members competitors priced away from and onboarding VBC patients whose risk it had not characterized. That is a discipline failure layered on a sector headwind, and it is the portion management can actually fix, which is consistent with the 2026 recovery coming from repricing and exits rather than from cost trend abating.

## Risk Assessment

**Reserve-development dependence.** Two consecutive quarters of large favorable development follow three years of collapsing development. A 1% miss on medical costs payable moves earnings ~$300M; a 1pt change in the two-month PMPM trend factor moves the reserve ~$501M. Q4 2026, where consensus now sits at $2.14 after the cut, is the natural test.

**Medicare Advantage rate adequacy.** The 10-K said the 2027 Advance Notice was "far below" expected forward trend; the Q1 10-Q softened this to the Final Notice having "moved towards" trend but remaining "below" it. Combined with "substantial revisions to the risk adjustment model" that have "resulted and will continue to result in reduced funding," this is a compounding multi-year headwind against 44%-of-revenue exposure.

**Unquantified legal and tax exposure.** On March 6, 2026 UNH received IRS Notices of Proposed Adjustment covering 2017–2020 intercompany transfer pricing with a foreign subsidiary; the IRS "is seeking to significantly increase taxable income for each of the applicable periods and could also seek similar adjustments for subsequent years after 2020." No amount is disclosed. The 2011 False Claims Act whistleblower case (DOJ intervened 2017) has a Special Master recommendation in UNH's favor from March 2025 that DOJ moved to reject in April 2025; UNH "cannot reasonably estimate the outcome." Filings additionally reference ongoing subpoenas and reviews of "compliance with coding and other requirements under the Medicare risk-adjustment model." Reporting of a parallel criminal investigation could not be verified from filings or accessible sources and should be treated as unresolved rather than assumed benign or assumed active.

**Holding-company liquidity.** $1.1B of $28.0B usable; subsidiary flow reversed by ~$9.7B between 2024 and 2025; statutory capital requirements rising with the MCR; remaining buyback authorization was 19.3M shares (~$7.8B) at 3/31/2026 against 11.4M already repurchased through mid-July. Sustaining the pace requires board re-authorization and improved subsidiary dividend capacity.

**Self-imposed earnings reduction.** UNH "voluntarily pledged to rebate 2026 profits on our individual exchange products to customers." That forgoes profit on a book of business for political reasons.

**Governance churn.** Eight Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks in under eleven months, plus an unexplained Chief Accounting Officer transition inferred from the mismatch between the 10-K officer table (Tom Roos) and the Q1 10-Q signature page (Dennis Stankiewicz).

Offsetting: consolidated leverage is moderate, the balance sheet is investment grade, and the reputational-remediation program (Public Responsibility Committee, 23 completed action plans, prior-authorization reductions, a transparent fee-based PBM model) is substantive process work even if the FTI HouseCalls review, 200 visits against 3.1M performed in 2025 and issuer-commissioned, is evidence of remediation rather than exoneration.

### Options Market Signal

The headline reads are misleading. Near-term ATM IV of 32.6% against 38.5% one-year realized volatility is **cheap**, not stressed. The "backwardation" (32.6% at 1 day versus 30.3% at six months) and the 8.9% put skew both come from the August 7 one-day expiry, where 8,458 contracts traded the $408 call against 186 open interest, which is same-day pinning flow rather than informed positioning. At meaningful tenors the skew flattens and then inverts: 1.4% at one month, 0.0% at three months, **–2.4% at six months**. Put/call volume of 0.23 is heavily call-tilted. Put/call open interest rose from 0.50 to 0.64 since July 19, so some protection is being accumulated, but at 0.64 the book remains call-weighted. Short interest is 2.1% of float at 2.9 days to cover, not a crowded bearish position. The six-month tenor prices a ±14.0% move, which brackets my base and bear cases and is consistent with two-sided uncertainty around 2027 guidance rather than an imminent event. Nothing here contradicts the fundamental read; cheap IV relative to realized volatility does argue that hedging a position via options is inexpensive at present.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($525, +29%)

The conservative-pick hypothesis is correct: management, having been burned, is setting initial current-year picks high, and the reserve releases are genuine over-correction rather than the exhaustion of a finite lever. Repricing and benefit redesign carry MA margins above 3% as guided; UnitedHealthcare delivers ≥$12B and Optum Health ≥$2.2B of operating profit in 2026; the 2027 Final Notice damage proves absorbable. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands at or above $22.44 and 2028 approaches $25–26 on the reaffirmed 13–16% growth path. Membership stabilizes in 2027 as competitors reprice too, restoring Optum's captive volumes. Buybacks continue at scale, having been executed 13–29% below current price. Twenty times $26 of 2028 earnings, discounted, supports $500–530, roughly the analyst high of $529.

### Bear Case ($305, –25%)

Prior-period development normalizes toward zero in H2 2026 and Q4 misses the already-cut $2.14. Commercial trend stays above 11% and the No Surprises Act arbitration drag persists, so the 2027 MA rate notice, still below forward trend, combines with a lapping reserve tailwind to produce initial 2027 guidance nearer $19 than $22.44. Membership attrition overshoots, taking Optum Rx scripts and Optum Health VBC lives with it and pushing Optum Health's margin recovery past 2028. Rising statutory capital requirements keep subsidiary cash trapped, buybacks are throttled again as they were in 2025 ($5.5B from $9.0B), and one of the three Negative outlooks converts to a downgrade. The IRS NOPAs or a risk-adjustment enforcement outcome adds an unquantified charge. Sixteen times $19 = $304, near the $313 analyst low.

The two cases are roughly symmetric in probability, which is why this is a HOLD. The asymmetry that would make it a BUY is a demonstrated decline in the current-year loss pick, which has not appeared in two quarters of data.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $445.** Nineteen point eight times FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $22.44. That multiple sits between today's 18.1x and the 21.2x embedded in the $475.23 consensus mean, and reflects a business with recovering earnings on declining revenue. **+9.7% price appreciation, +11.9% total return with the $8.84 dividend, over 12 months.**

**Bull case target: $525** (+29.5%). Twenty-one times a 2028 adjusted EPS trajectory near $25, requiring confirmation that 2027 guidance lands at or above $22.44.

**Bear case target: $305** (–24.8%). Sixteen times FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$19 if reserve tailwinds lapse and 2027 guidance disappoints.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to three dated checkpoints: the Q3 2026 print (mid-October), the Q4 print and initial FY2027 guidance (mid-January 2027), and the CMS 2028 Advance Notice (typically late January/early February).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Current-year MCR improves on a like-for-like basis.** Specifically, a quarter in which current-year medical costs divided by premiums (the Note 4 decomposition, not the reported MCR) falls at least 50bp year over year with prior-period development below $400M. This is the single decisive test.
2. **Initial FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance at or above $22.50** with revenue guided to growth rather than decline, accompanied by MA membership stabilization.
3. **Price below $340**, which would put the stock at roughly 15x FY2027 consensus and restore asymmetry even without resolution of the reserve question.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Q3 or Q4 2026 prior-period development turns unfavorable, or reported MCR exceeds 88% in any quarter**, either of which would indicate the 2024–2025 under-reserving was not fully corrected.
2. **Buyback suspension or a failure to obtain incremental board authorization** beyond the 19.3M shares outstanding at 3/31/2026, which would confirm that statutory capital absorption is binding at the holding company.
3. **A rating downgrade from any of Moody's, S&P or Fitch**, or quantification of the IRS NOPAs at a level material to the ~$1.1B of usable holding-company liquidity, or an adverse turn in the DOJ risk-adjustment matter.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report in mid-October, when the third data point on reserve development becomes available.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted from 6.0 in February to 5.35 now. The drift is not a reaction to price (the stock is up roughly 64% from its $239.50 low and 12% below its $461.62 high) but to the accumulating evidence that the visible recovery rests on levers with finite runway. Repricing, benefit reduction, member exits and back-book reserve releases have delivered a 30% earnings beat and two guidance raises. None of them constitutes evidence that medical cost trend is falling, and management says as much: costs remain "elevated over historical levels," commercial trend is "modestly above 11%," and the company is "not yielding the full margin expansion for which we planned in 2026."

Against that, the shares now cost 20.5x guided 2026 adjusted earnings on revenue guided to decline, with 44% of revenue set by CMS, three negative rating outlooks, $1.1B of usable holding-company cash, $110.5B of goodwill and an unbounded new tax claim. Capital allocation has been good: repurchases at $285.68 and ~$351 are well below today's price, and the CEO's SEC-defined Compensation Actually Paid went to negative $7.13M in 2025, which is real alignment. But no insider bought stock in the open market between April and July 2026, and consensus estimate revisions of +7.7%, while positive, are the ordinary product of a guidance raise rather than the kind of velocity that precedes re-rating.

Base case $445 versus bear case $305 gives roughly 1.2:1 on the bull tail and only about 0.4:1 against the bear, insufficient for new capital. Existing holders are compensated for waiting by a 2.18% yield, an in-the-money buyback and a credible if unproven path to 13–16% growth by 2028. The decisive datapoint arrives in October: whether the current-year loss pick, stripped of prior-period development, finally improves.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Own it if you do; add only on a like-for-like MCR improvement, initial FY2027 guidance at or above $22.50, or a price below $340.
