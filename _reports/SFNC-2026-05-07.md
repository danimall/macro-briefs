## Executive Summary

Simmons First National Corporation completed a balance sheet restructuring in Q3 2025, crystallizing $625.6 million in after-tax losses on low-yielding securities and raising $326.9 million in dilutive equity to reset its earnings trajectory. GAAP EPS was -$2.95 for FY2025, but the restructuring has produced measurable results: net interest margin expanded from 2.74% (FY2024) to 3.84% (Q1 2026), a 110bp improvement that translates directly to earnings power. Q1 2026 delivered $0.47 adjusted EPS with management guiding to 9-11% NII growth and double-digit PPNR growth for full year 2026.

The stock trades at approximately 10.3x forward earnings ($21.36 vs. consensus $2.07 for FY2026), a modest discount to the peer median of 16.1x. This discount is partially justified by below-peer ROE, $1.32 billion in goodwill (43% of market cap), elevated CRE concentration, and a unique IRS tax treatment risk on the securities losses. Estimate revisions are positive (+7-9% over 90 days) but insufficiently aggressive to warrant a high-conviction buy call. The 4.0% dividend yield provides income support while the organic growth thesis plays out under new CEO Jay Brogdon.

## Company Overview

Simmons First National Corporation operates Simmons Bank, an Arkansas state-chartered institution with $24.5 billion in assets and 222 financial centers across Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas. The bank completed 21 whole bank acquisitions since 1990, most recently Spirit of Texas Bancshares ($3.1B assets, April 2022), building significant scale in secondary and tertiary markets across the Mid-South. Management has signaled a pivot from acquisition-driven growth to organic expansion, cost optimization, and balance sheet efficiency. The company operates three segments: Community Banking (retail and small business), Commercial Banking (larger and more complex relationships), and Wealth Management ($39.4M in annual fees, growing 8.4% YoY).

## Financial Analysis

### Balance Sheet Repositioning

In Q3 2025, management reclassified $3.59 billion from held-to-maturity to available-for-sale and sold $3.16 billion in low-yielding securities at a gross loss of $801.5 million ($625.6M after-tax). Proceeds retired high-cost wholesale deposits and FHLB advances. Simultaneously, SFNC raised $326.9 million through an equity offering (18.65M shares at $18.50) and issued $325 million in 6.25% subordinated notes.

The dilution was significant: share count rose 15% from 125.7M to 144.8M. The earnings power improvement:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 (Adj.) | Q1 2026 Ann. |
|--------|--------|--------|---------------|--------------|
| NIM | 2.78% | 2.74% | 3.32% | 3.84% |
| Net Interest Income (FTE) | $675.6M | $654.3M | $738.7M | ~$800M+ |
| Adjusted EPS | — | $1.41 | $1.73 | ~$1.88+ |

### Capital Position

Post-repositioning capital ratios are well above regulatory minimums: CET1 at 11.63% (min 4.50%), total risk-based capital at 14.45% (min 8.00%), tangible common equity/tangible assets at 8.71%. Book value per share stands at $23.62; tangible book value at $13.91. The stock at $21.36 trades at 90% of book value and 1.54x TBV.

### Credit Quality

NPLs/total loans stand at 0.64%, with allowance coverage at 199% of non-performing loans. Net charge-offs in 2025 were elevated at 0.47% due to two specific relationships (a downtown St. Louis hotel and a fast-food operator), but management guides to 25 bps normalized charge-offs for 2026. The $17.49 billion loan portfolio is 78.7% real estate (including $8.29B other CRE and $2.87B construction & development), representing meaningful concentration.

### Funding & Deposits

Total deposits of $20.18 billion declined $1.70 billion in 2025 as management intentionally ran off high-cost wholesale/brokered deposits to replace expensive funding with securities sale proceeds. Noninterest bearing deposits have shrunk from $5.2 billion (2023 average) to $4.4 billion (2025 average), a secular challenge that affects NIM sustainability. Q1 2026 showed 6% annualized deposit growth, a positive reversal.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Trajectory

Management's 2026 guidance:
- NII growth: 9-11% (expects high end)
- NIM target: mid-3.80% range by year-end
- Loan growth: low to mid-single digits
- Operating leverage: >5% full year
- PPNR growth: double-digit

Q1 2026 delivered 10% annualized loan growth with the commercial pipeline at $1.54 billion (up from $1.26 billion a year prior). Analyst estimates have risen modestly: FY2026 EPS from $1.93 (90 days ago) to $2.07 currently (+7.3%), and FY2027 from $2.06 to $2.19 (+6.3%). Positive but below the +30% threshold for a strong momentum signal.

### Medium-Term Growth Drivers

1. **NIM normalization** — the full-year benefit of the repositioning hasn't been fully reflected in consensus estimates; NIM could stabilize in the high-3.80s range
2. **Fee income expansion** — Wealth management growing 8.4% YoY; potential for further penetration in existing customer base
3. **Buyback activation** — $175M authorization represents 5.6% of market cap, likely deployed in 2H 2026 or 2027 once capital rebuilds
4. **Organic loan growth** — management pivoting from M&A to organic expansion in existing footprint

### Constraints

Growth is capped by: (a) geographic concentration in slower-growth Mid-South markets, (b) competitive pressure from larger banks and fintech in digital banking, (c) potential NIM pressure if Fed cuts rates (60% of loans reprice within one year — asset sensitivity cuts both ways), (d) limited ability to pursue transformative M&A given recent dilution and goodwill levels.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.36 with consensus FY2026 EPS of $2.07, SFNC trades at 10.3x forward earnings. This compares to:
- Peer median P/E: 16.1x (PRK 16.1x, BOH 16.3x, SBCF 21.1x, BANC 15.6x, FFBC 10.8x)
- SFNC 5-year TSR: 105 vs. peer group TSR of 153

The 36% discount to peer median P/E is partially justified by: (a) lower historical returns on equity due to the repositioning drag, (b) 15% share dilution in FY2025, (c) elevated goodwill ($1.32B, 43% of market cap), (d) unique IRS tax risk, and (e) below-peer revenue growth trajectory (Mid-South secondary markets vs. higher-growth Sun Belt/coastal peers).

At consensus $2.07 FY2026 EPS, the stock would need to re-rate to 13x P/E to reach $26.90 — roughly 25% upside if the company delivers on guidance and the market recognizes the earnings improvement. At peer-median 16.1x, the stock would be $33.30, but this multiple seems unwarranted given SFNC's risk profile and track record.

The analyst consensus target range is $23.00-$25.00 (mean $23.57), implying 8-17% upside. The 4.0% dividend yield provides incremental return.

The stock at 0.90x book value is not expensive for a bank with improving profitability, but at 1.54x TBV, there is meaningful franchise premium already reflected. If ROA reaches the mid-80s bps range (annualized Q1 2026 implies roughly 1.1%), the current price is reasonable.

## Competitive Landscape

SFNC operates primarily in secondary and tertiary markets across its six-state footprint, where competition is less intense than in major metropolitan areas. The 222 financial centers represent significant deposit-gathering infrastructure. The company faces persistent competitive threats from: (a) national banks with superior digital capabilities and brand recognition, (b) credit unions offering below-market rates, (c) fintech companies capturing younger demographics, and (d) other regional banks that executed balance sheet repositionings earlier or avoided the HTM trap entirely.

The 5-year TSR underperformance of 48 points vs. peers (105 vs. 153) reflects the cost of SFNC's acquisition-heavy strategy and subsequent HTM portfolio losses. While the repositioning is complete and go-forward metrics should improve, the company needs to demonstrate sustained organic growth and efficiency gains to close this performance gap.

Wealth management ($39.4M, +8.4% YoY) is an emerging differentiator but remains a small contributor to overall revenue.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **IRS Tax Treatment (Unique, Material):** The 10-K explicitly discloses that the IRS could determine the $801.5M in securities losses should not receive ordinary tax treatment. If reclassified as capital losses, SFNC could face material additional tax obligations. This is an unusual and specific risk disclosure that markets may underappreciate.

2. **CRE Concentration:** With 78.7% of the $17.49B loan portfolio in real estate (including $8.29B other CRE), SFNC carries meaningful exposure to commercial real estate stress. In the current environment of elevated rates and potential CRE maturities industry-wide at significantly higher rates than origination, this could produce credit losses beyond the guided 25 bps.

3. **Goodwill Impairment:** $1.32 billion in goodwill from 21 acquisitions. The 10-K acknowledges that a "significant and sustained decline" in stock price could necessitate write-downs. While the stock has recovered from October 2025 lows (~$17.00), it remains well below historical trading ranges. Any retest of those lows could trigger impairment testing.

4. **Interest Rate Sensitivity:** 60% of loans reprice within one year. If the Fed cuts rates (low probability near-term but possible in late 2026/2027), asset yields could compress faster than liability costs decline, particularly given the secular shift toward more expensive deposits.

5. **Geographic/Economic Concentration:** Operations concentrated in Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas — economies with energy, agriculture, and local growth dependencies.

6. **Leadership Transition:** New CEO Jay Brogdon assumed the role January 2026. While planned and orderly, execution risk under new leadership during the organic growth pivot cannot be dismissed.

### Mitigating Factors

- Capital ratios well above minimums provide cushion
- 199% allowance coverage of NPLs
- The repositioning loss and dilution are already realized
- Improving operational metrics reduce probability of sustained stock price decline that would trigger goodwill impairment

## Conclusion

SFNC has completed the restructuring: crystallizing massive losses and diluting shareholders to fix a broken balance sheet. NIM has expanded 110 bps, earnings are recovering, and the commercial pipeline is growing. At 10.3x forward earnings with a 4% yield, the stock offers reasonable value for a bank with an improving trajectory.

However, this requires continued execution under new leadership. The 48-point TSR underperformance vs. peers over five years creates a trust deficit that one quarter of strong results cannot overcome. The IRS tax risk, goodwill overhang, and CRE concentration limit multiple expansion potential. At current prices, SFNC is fairly valued for its risk-adjusted return profile — offering limited near-term upside to consensus targets ($23-25) but supported by dividend income and improving fundamentals.

The appropriate stance is HOLD with a growth orientation: the earnings trajectory is positive, the dividend is well-covered, and a pullback to $18.50-19.00 would create a more compelling entry point. Two consecutive quarters of NIM stability >3.85% and organic loan growth >5% would build the conviction necessary for an upgrade.
