**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

IDACORP is the holding company for Idaho Power, a vertically integrated regulated electric utility serving ~668,000 customers across southern Idaho and eastern Oregon. It combines two features that are rare in the same name: one of the strongest organic load-growth backdrops in the US electric-utility sector (2.3% trailing customer growth, semiconductor/industrial in-migration, and a 2025 IRP assuming 8.3% annual retail MWh growth over five years) and a genuinely protective Idaho regulatory construct, the ADITC accelerated-amortization mechanism that supports a minimum ~9.12% Idaho ROE, plus power-cost (PCA), decoupling (FCA), and wildfire-deferral mechanisms that compress regulatory lag. Net income has compounded from $246M (2021) to $324M (2025), roughly 11% in each of the last two years, and Q1 2026 EPS rose 10% to $1.21 on the January base-rate increase.

The offsetting reality is that this is a heavy-capex, negative-free-cash-flow growth utility financing itself through debt and equity in a higher-for-longer rate environment. Capex is guided to ~$1.3-1.5B in 2026 rising toward $3.6-4.1B cumulative in 2028-2030, against roughly $600M of operating cash flow at the Idaho Power level. Long-term debt rose to $3.68B in Q1 2026, long-term-debt interest expense climbed 25% YoY, and Moody's cut IDACORP to Baa3 in March 2026, the lowest investment-grade rung. Outstanding forward sale agreements total ~6.3M shares, more than 11% of the ~55.4M share count, which will dilute headline net-income growth at the per-share level as they settle (the $575M May 2025 block by November 2026).

At $148.57 the stock trades below its 52-week high of $154.91 at ~24.9x trailing / ~23.3x consensus forward EPS ($6.38), a ~18% premium to the ~19.7x peer median. The consensus mean target of $158.9 implies ~7% upside, the low target ($144) sits below the current price, and the sentiment trend is degrading with current-quarter estimates revised down 3.6% over 90 days. The load-growth quality is real and largely appreciated by the market; the dilution and financing drag cap per-share upside. This is a growth-oriented HOLD: own it for the durable rate-base compounding, but current price offers no asymmetric entry, and the fourth consecutive HOLD reflects a genuinely balanced setup rather than indecision.

## Company Overview

IDACORP operates a single material business: Idaho Power, a regulated monopoly electric utility covering ~24,000 square miles. The "All Other" segment (IFS affordable-housing tax credits, Ida-West small hydro) is immaterial. Three structural features distinguish it from typical peers:

**Hydro-heavy generation.** Hydropower is over half of nameplate capacity, anchored by the Hells Canyon Complex, which is in FERC relicensing (new license expected 2027+). This makes power-supply costs and earnings sensitive to Snake River Basin hydrology. Management guides 2026 hydro generation to 5.5-7.0 million MWh versus a 20-year average of ~7.3M, a below-average water year that is the primary driver of the $51.6M April 2026 PCA increase filing.

**A favorable Idaho regulatory construct.** The 2018/2023/2025 settlement stipulations include the ADITC accelerated-amortization mechanism, which lets Idaho Power pull forward tax credits to support a minimum ~9.12% Idaho ROE. The 2025 Settlement caps this at $55M/year for 2026+ and refreshed the eligible pool; ~$159.6M remained available at Q1 2026. This is a real downside shock-absorber most peers lack. Q1 2026 usage was only $6.3M (versus $19.3M in Q1 2025) because underlying earnings were strong enough not to need it, though it caused tax expense to jump ~$13M YoY.

**Cost-recovery mechanisms that reduce regulatory lag:** the PCA (95% of power-supply cost variances passed through), FCA revenue decoupling for residential/small commercial, wildfire-cost deferrals, and current recovery of HCC relicensing AFUDC (~$38.5M/yr).

The recently announced sale of the Oregon distribution and certain transmission assets to Oregon Trail Electric Consumers Cooperative for a $154M base price (expected to close within 12 months, pending OPUC/IPUC/FERC approval) simplifies the regulatory footprint and concentrates the business in the higher-growth Idaho jurisdiction.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Net income attributable to IDACORP: $246M (2021) → $260M (2022) → $262M (2023) → $290M (2024) → $324M (2025). Diluted EPS reached ~$5.90 in 2025. Q1 2026 net income was $68.0M (EPS $1.21) versus $59.6M (EPS $1.10), +10%, driven by the January 1 base-rate increase (+$18M operating income), customer growth (+$5M), and FCA (+$19.1M), partly offset by weather-driven usage declines (-$10.7M) and higher O&M/D&A.

**The central financial dynamic is deliberate negative free cash flow.** Capex is guided to $1.4-1.6B (2027), with Q1 2026 capex of $361M in cash against only ~$60M operating cash flow at the Idaho Power level. Major projects include the Boardman-to-Hemingway (B2H) transmission line (~$1.5-1.7B total, IDA ~45%, in-service late 2027), Gateway West Segment E-8 (~$900M-1.1B IDA share), SWIP-North, three new natural-gas plants (167 MW by 2028, 222 MW by 2029, 430 MW by 2030), 250 MW+ of battery storage, and 625 MW of solar PPAs. For a regulated utility this outspending is how shareholder value is created, since every prudently incurred dollar enters rate base and earns a regulated return, but it requires continuous external financing.

**Leverage and financing cost.** Long-term debt rose to $3.68B (Q1 2026) from $3.33B at YE2025, including a new $350M 4.85% first-mortgage bond in February 2026. Total equity is ~$3.65B. Covenant leverage sits at IDACORP 54% / Idaho Power 53% debt-to-cap against a 65% cap, comfortable but trending up. The clearest earnings headwind is long-term-debt interest expense of $47.8M in Q1 2026 versus $38.2M a year earlier, +25% YoY. First-mortgage-bond issuance headroom (~$1.7B under the aggregate cap) is adequate for near-term needs.

A note on screening artifacts: any automated model showing a ~0.06 debt/equity or an Altman Z-Score distress flag is mismapping regulatory line items. The real capital structure is ~54% debt / 46% equity, and negative FCF plus leverage are normal features of the rate-regulated model, not distress signals. The credit signal that matters is the ratings agencies.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.88 from $0.86 (Q1 dividends $49.2M), a ~2.4% yield. Growth is funded heavily through equity issuance, the crux of the per-share debate below.

Earnings-surprise history is genuinely mixed: two beats and two misses over the last four quarters (Q1 2026 +7.9%, Q4 2025 +5.6%, Q3 2025 -0.8%, Q2 2025 -2.0%), consistent with a weather- and hydrology-exposed utility rather than a reliable serial beater.

## Growth Analysis

The organic demand backdrop is the best part of the story and among the strongest in the US electric-utility space. Idaho's demographic in-migration plus large-load industrial customers (heavily implied to include the Micron "Triton" semiconductor fab in Boise, whose special-rate contract remains pending at the IPUC) underpin an IRP assuming 8.3% annual retail MWh growth and 5.1% annual peak-demand growth over five years. All-time system peaks keep rising (summer 2024: 3,793 MW; winter 2024: 2,719 MW). Regional transmission constraints force Idaho Power to build owned generation and transmission rather than import, which directly expands rate base.

A crucial attribution point: Micron and other industrial loads are retail electricity customers of Idaho Power. IDACORP's upside is regulated rate-base investment to serve that load, not any AI or memory-product exposure. The load-growth thesis is real and durable, corroborated by data-center and industrial power demand appearing in regulated-utility hard data across the sector, but it converts to shareholder value only through prudently recovered rate base, and only after dilution.

That dilution is why headline growth overstates per-share growth. Net income compounding ~11% will translate to materially lower per-share growth once the ~6.3M FSA shares settle. This shows in the estimates: consensus current-year EPS is $6.38 and next-year $6.96, roughly 9% forward EPS growth, below the net-income growth, reflecting the share-count drag. Current-quarter estimates were revised down 3.6% over 90 days and next-quarter down 3.2%, while full-year figures are essentially flat. The 30-day trend is mildly negative for the current quarter (-1.7%). This is not the >30% positive revision velocity that would signal a mispriced move; it is a modest downward drift consistent with a weak water year and dilution. The growth thesis is durable but decelerating at the per-share level.

## Valuation Assessment

At $148.57, IDACORP trades at ~24.9x trailing EPS ($5.90) and ~23.3x consensus forward EPS. Against the Electric Utilities peer median P/E of 19.7x, this is a ~18% premium. The peer set is heterogeneous (TXNM at 42.1x, POR at 22.4x, OTTR at 13.3x, MGEE at 19.7x, HE at 17.9x) but IDA sits clearly above the median.

The premium is partly earned: IDA's load-growth backdrop and ADITC downside protection are genuinely above-average, and a modest premium to the median is defensible. The question is whether ~23x forward on ~9% forward EPS growth (PEG ~2.6) offers asymmetric upside. It does not. The 2.4% dividend yield is below the peer median of 2.6% and well below POR (4.3%), providing limited absolute support. The consensus mean target implies ~7% upside, the median is $158.5, and the low target of $144 sits below the current price, meaning the least optimistic analyst already sees downside. This stock trades near the top of its analyst range below a 52-week high, not one where targets are stale floors waiting to be revised up.

I am not treating the ~21% price appreciation over the trailing year as evidence of overvaluation in itself; that move reflected real rate-base and earnings growth. But the current multiple already capitalizes the growth story, the forward multiple exceeds the growth rate, and there is no identified catalyst for further multiple expansion. The higher-for-longer rate path is a direct headwind to a negative-FCF utility that must finance a $6.3-7.2B capex plan: rising interest expense and a rising cost of equity both compress the model. The valuation score reflects a modestly stretched multiple relative to growth-adjusted fundamentals with the dilution and financing drag not fully offset by the quality premium. Framing IDA as "cheap on load-growth optionality" would be a value-trap warning here rather than a buy signal, because the optionality is already in the price.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated monopoly, IDACORP faces no direct retail competition; its "competitive" dynamics are regulatory and macro. Its trajectory is favorable on several axes:

- **Above-average organic load growth** driven by Idaho demographics and semiconductor/industrial in-migration, a structural tailwind few peers can match.
- **Transmission constraint as a rate-base tailwind:** constrained regional import capability forces owned-generation and transmission buildout, the mechanism by which regulated utilities grow earnings.
- **Coal transition largely de-risked:** Jim Bridger units converting coal-to-gas, Valmy winding down, reducing stranded-asset and environmental overhang. Federal EPA deregulation (Feb 2026 GHG endangerment-finding rescission, MATS rollback) is a modest incremental positive.
- **Regulatory quality:** the ADITC floor, PCA/FCA mechanisms, and the Idaho Wildfire Standard of Care Act (effective July 2025, WMP approved April 2026) collectively give IDA one of the more favorable regulatory relationships in the sector.

The prior score trajectory marked Competitive Position down from 7.5 to 6.5, reflecting appreciation that the load-growth advantage, while real, is contingent on large-load ramp timing (the Micron special-contract case is unresolved) and that hydrology introduces earnings variability peers with thermal-heavy fleets do not face. I hold the score at 6.8: the competitive trajectory is improving on load growth and de-risking on coal, but the double-edged nature of large-load concentration and the hydro dependence prevent a higher mark. The trajectory is stable-to-improving, not deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

**Equity dilution — the most concrete, underappreciated risk.** IDACORP is pre-funding capex with forward sale agreements: a May 2025 registered offering of 5.18M shares (~$575M, ~$107.67 forward price, settleable by November 9, 2026), plus a fully utilized $300M ATM program with additional FSAs (~1.1M shares at ~$139.61). Q1 2026 diluted count already reflects ~1.17M incremental FSA shares. Total potential issuance is ~6.3M shares, >11% of shares outstanding. This is the crux of the bull/bear debate: rate-base growth versus dilution plus rising interest expense.

**Credit-rating pressure — active and recent.** Moody's downgraded IDACORP to Baa3 (issuer) / P-3 (commercial paper) and Idaho Power to Baa2 / A3 in March 2026, both stable outlook. Baa3 is the lowest investment-grade rung. S&P remains BBB/BBB. Further downgrades would raise financing costs and could trigger up to ~$32M of additional derivative collateral if below investment grade.

**Large-load ramp uncertainty.** The 10-Q explicitly flags "rapid addition of new industrial customer load, uncertainty of forecasted power usage ramp rates or volumes… resulting in increased risks of power demand potentially exceeding available supply and of revenue, cash flow, and earnings volatility." The Micron special-contract case at the IPUC is unresolved, a catalyst to monitor in both directions.

**Wildfire liability**, partly mitigated by the Idaho Wildfire Standard of Care Act (WMP-defined duty of care); Idaho WMP deferral balance was $89.6M at Q1 2026. **Hydrology risk** (below-average 2026 water year expected). **Regulatory-lag/prudence risk** on the very large capex program, where every project must be deemed prudent for rate-base inclusion. **Transmission execution risk**, as B2H faces ongoing (so-far-unsuccessful) legal challenges in Oregon and Gateway West segments still require CPCNs.

Many of these are partially priced (the Moody's cut is public, the FSA overhang is disclosed), which supports a mid-range risk score rather than a low one. The unpriced tail is the interaction: a weak water year plus a slower-than-modeled large-load ramp plus higher-for-longer financing costs would compress earnings and per-share growth simultaneously.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$173-180).** The large-load ramp (Micron plus additional industrial customers) proceeds on or ahead of schedule; the IPUC approves the special-rate contract on favorable terms; rate-base growth of the $6.3-7.2B capex plan compounds EPS at the high end of a 7-9% target; the ADITC floor and PCA/FCA mechanisms deliver on downside protection through a weak water year; the Oregon sale closes cleanly with a modest gain; and the market awards a premium multiple (~25-26x) to one of the best organic-growth stories in the sector. On ~$7.00 EPS in 2028 at 25x, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range ($171) and beyond.

**Bear case (~$118-125).** A below-average water year combines with a slower-than-modeled large-load ramp, missing near-term estimates; rising interest expense and a Moody's Baa3 profile keep financing costs elevated in a higher-for-longer environment; the ~11% share dilution drags per-share growth to mid-single digits; and the multiple compresses toward the peer median (~19-20x) as the growth premium deflates. At 19x on ~$6.40 forward EPS the stock trades near $121-122, roughly the 52-week low and ~18% below the current price.

**Base case (~$155-160).** EPS grows toward $6.38 (2026) and $6.96 (2027) with the FSA dilution absorbing part of the net-income growth; the multiple holds a modest premium to peers (~22-23x); the dividend compounds ~2-3%; and total return is roughly the dividend plus mid-single-digit EPS growth minus modest multiple normalization. This lands near the consensus mean/median target and implies limited price appreciation from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale: the 12-month thesis is positive on rate-base and load-growth compounding, but current price offers no asymmetric entry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $130s (below ~21x forward, closer to the peer median and to the FSA forward-sale reference prices), where the load-growth quality would offer asymmetric upside.
- IPUC approval of the Micron/large-load special-rate contract on terms that de-risk the ramp, combined with a turn in estimate-revision velocity (current-quarter and next-quarter estimates stabilizing or rising).
- Evidence that the FSA settlements are absorbed without meaningful per-share dilution surprise, plus a stable-to-improving credit trajectory (S&P/Moody's outlook reaffirmed with no further downgrade).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further Moody's or S&P downgrade below Baa3/BBB-, which would raise financing costs and risk derivative collateral calls.
- A materially worse hydro year or a large-load ramp delay that cuts current-year EPS estimates by more than ~5%.
- Multiple expansion above ~26x forward without commensurate estimate upgrades, signaling the growth premium has become stretched and risk/reward has turned negative.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (hydro/PCA impact and reaffirmed guidance), IPUC action on the Micron special contract, and the November 2026 settlement of the $575M FSA block. The next several quarters will resolve the two swing variables: large-load ramp timing and hydrology.

## Conclusion

IDACORP is a high-quality regulated utility with a best-in-class organic load-growth backdrop and a genuinely protective Idaho regulatory framework, being appropriately de-risked through the coal transition and the Oregon divestiture. The offsets are equally concrete: a massive multi-year capex program driving negative free cash flow, 25% YoY growth in interest expense, a March 2026 Moody's downgrade to the lowest investment-grade rung, and >11% potential equity dilution from outstanding forward sale agreements that will hold per-share growth below headline net-income growth. Estimate revisions are drifting modestly lower, sentiment is degrading, and the low analyst target already sits below the current price.

At ~23x forward EPS on ~9% per-share growth (PEG ~2.6) below a 52-week high, the market has priced the load-growth story and the ~7% upside to consensus does not compensate for the dilution and financing drag in a higher-for-longer rate environment. The fourth consecutive HOLD is warranted on genuinely balanced merits rather than inertia: own IDA for the durable rate-base compounding and the ADITC downside cushion, but wait for a pullback toward the low-to-mid $130s or a de-risking of the Micron ramp before adding. **Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).**
