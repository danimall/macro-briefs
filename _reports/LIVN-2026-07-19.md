**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

LivaNova is a UK-domiciled medical technology company with two franchises: Cardiopulmonary (heart-lung machines, oxygenators, perfusion consumables; ~57% of revenue) and Neuromodulation (VNS Therapy for drug-resistant epilepsy, plus development-stage depression and obstructive sleep apnea programs; ~43%). The operating business is inflecting. FY2025 revenue grew 10.7% to $1.39B and Q1 2026 revenue rose 14.3% YoY to $362M, with Cardiopulmonary up 18.3% on Essenz perfusion-system share gains and Neuromodulation up 9.3%. Underlying operating income grew 54% to $199.4M in FY25 and free cash flow reached $173M (12.5% margin). The headline FY25 net loss of $242.5M is almost entirely the product of a one-time, non-cash $365.6M SNIA environmental charge, not operational deterioration; the company returned to GAAP profitability in Q1 2026 ($22.3M net income, $0.40 diluted EPS).

Two binary catalysts define the next twelve months. The first is the SNIA environmental liability: an Italian Supreme Court ruling in March 2025 found LivaNova jointly and severally liable for a predecessor's environmental damages, producing a $389.5M current liability. A June 24, 2026 Court of Appeal hearing was scheduled to explore an out-of-court settlement, so resolution could either remove the overhang or crystallize a large cash obligation. The second is the March 2026 FDA PMA approval of the aura6000 hypoglossal nerve stimulation device for OSA, supported by OSPREY/PolySync data lifting AHI response rates from 65% to 84.5%. This opens a third neuromodulation growth vector, but into a market dominated by Inspire Medical.

The complicating factor is price. The stock has risen roughly 90% from its 52-week low of $41.02 to $80.70, and now trades at approximately 19x current-year consensus EPS ($4.25) and ~17.4x next-year ($4.63). That multiple is defensible for a low-double-digit grower with expanding margins, but it no longer embeds the discount that made the earlier buy case compelling, and it sits below the analyst mean target of $84.80 by only a modest margin. Estimate revisions have flattened after a strong beat streak. Given the SNIA binary, an unproven OSA launch, and a valuation that has largely caught up to fundamentals, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate LivaNova a HOLD.

## Company Overview

LivaNova operates two reportable segments. Cardiopulmonary ($785M FY25 revenue) supplies heart-lung machines, oxygenators, autotransfusion systems, and perfusion consumables used in cardiopulmonary bypass procedures (CABG, valve surgery). The flagship Essenz Perfusion System, a next-generation HLM with embedded patient monitoring, is the primary share-gain engine. Manufacturing is concentrated in Italy and Germany. Neuromodulation ($593M FY25) centers on the VNS Therapy System (implantable vagus nerve stimulation generators and leads for drug-resistant epilepsy) plus development programs in difficult-to-treat depression (RECOVER) and OSA (aura6000, from the 2018 ImThera acquisition). Neuromodulation is manufactured in the U.S.

The competitive structure differs by segment. Perfusion is a consolidated oligopoly (Terumo, Getinge/Maquet) where Essenz is currently winning share and price. Epilepsy VNS is a durable near-monopoly with recurring "razor/razorblade" economics: generator swaps do not require lead removal, generating a recurring implant stream from an installed base. The company has exited non-core lines (Advanced Circulatory Support wound down) and reduced depression-program R&D by $18.9M in 2025, redirecting capital toward the OSA opportunity, a disciplined portfolio focus.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth is accelerating and broad-based: $1.02B (2022) → $1.15B (2023) → $1.25B (2024) → $1.39B (2025), with Q1 2026 at $362M. The reported FY25 net loss is an accounting artifact of the non-cash SNIA charge. Stripping it out, the business is healthy:

- Operating income $199.4M in FY25 vs. $129.1M in FY24, up 54%
- Segment income (management's core metric) $323.8M, +19%, with Cardiopulmonary segment income +40.9% and Neuromodulation +10.3%
- Gross margin ~67.7%; SG&A leverage improving to 39.5% of revenue from 40.6%
- Free cash flow $173M (12.5% FCF margin); operating cash flow $254M (+39% YoY)

The balance sheet has been materially de-risked. Total debt fell from $627.6M (2024) to $376.7M (2025), then to ~$287.8M after the January 2026 full repayment of the term facilities ($95.9M). Remaining debt is essentially the $345M 2.50% convertible notes due 2029 (effective rate 9.84% due to a bifurcated embedded derivative). Cash was ~$540M at Q1 2026, with a $225M undrawn revolver and a current ratio of 1.36. The Altman Z-Score of 1.82 ("grey zone") is misleading: the EBIT-to-assets input reflects the SNIA charge and the current-liability ratio is distorted by the $389M SNIA liability being classified as current. On a normalized basis this is a profitable, cash-generative, de-leveraging business.

The earnings surprise record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 +7.1%, Q4 2025 +3.4%, Q3 2025 +18.4%, Q2 2025 +20.3%). The convert's embedded derivative and capped calls do create GAAP volatility in the other-income line (from +$47.8M in 2024 to -$2.7M in 2025), which will continue to add noise to reported quarterly EPS.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth story rests on Essenz. Cardiopulmonary's Q1 2026 growth combines unit share gains against Terumo and Getinge, favorable pricing, and consumables pull-through as the installed base expands. This is the highest-visibility part of the portfolio. Epilepsy VNS provides a durable, high-margin annuity with recurring generator-swap revenue, growing at a steadier high-single-digit clip.

The optionality is aura6000. FDA PMA approval for moderate-to-severe OSA patients who fail or cannot tolerate CPAP unlocks a large addressable market, and the OSPREY/PolySync data provides a credible clinical differentiator for the launch. The offset is Inspire Medical, the incumbent hypoglossal nerve stimulation leader with established physician relationships, scale, and reimbursement infrastructure. A new entrant with better programming still has to build a sales force, secure reimbursement, and prove differentiation in the field. Whether OSA becomes a meaningful third leg or a costly launch drag will not be clear for several quarters.

Consensus reflects steady rather than explosive growth: roughly 9% forward earnings growth. Revision trends have flattened: current-year +1.0% over 90 days, next-year +1.3%, and next-quarter down 4.5%. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags an underappreciated inflection; consensus has largely caught up to the operating improvement. We weight growth prospects positively given accelerating revenue, expanding segment margins, and a genuine new product cycle, but the absence of accelerating estimate revisions caps the score below the level a true inflection would warrant.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.70 the stock trades at ~19x current-year EPS and ~17.4x next-year. The trailing 41.5x P/E is meaningless because it is measured against a SNIA-distorted loss base; forward multiples are the correct lens. The peer set in Health Care Equipment shows a median P/E of 27.2 (ITGR 23.2, LMAT 34.0, UFPT 27.2; TFX and TMDX distorted). On forward earnings, LivaNova at ~17-19x sits below that peer median, which superficially argues for upside.

The discount is partly justified and partly a warning. It reflects the SNIA overhang (reserved current liability with a live settlement process), UK-plc capital-action constraints, and an unproven OSA launch. Cheapness in this system's experience is a poor standalone buy signal; it sorts the wrong way absent a specific catalyst, and here the catalysts (SNIA resolution, OSA ramp) are genuinely two-sided. The stock has re-rated ~90% off its low on the back of the operating turnaround, and the analyst mean target of $84.80 (median $84.00) sits only ~5% above current price, with a range of $67 to $95. That is not the wide margin of safety the prior buy thesis relied on.

The forward multiple is defensible for a low-double-digit grower with expanding margins and net-cash operating leverage, but it prices in continued execution. There is no clean case that the market is materially mispricing the stock today. We score valuation modestly below neutral: the multiple is reasonable rather than cheap, and the discount to peers is offset by the binary SNIA exposure and launch risk.

## Competitive Landscape

LivaNova's competitive trajectory is improving in its two core franchises and untested in its growth optionality. In perfusion, Essenz is taking share and price against Terumo and Getinge in a consolidated oligopoly, the clearest positive trend, evidenced by the Q1 segment growth and FY25 segment income gains. In epilepsy VNS the company holds a near-monopoly with recurring installed-base economics and a high moat, though this is a mature, slower-growth franchise. Both are stable-to-improving.

The OSA battleground is where competitive position is most uncertain. Inspire Medical is the entrenched incumbent with physician relationships, reimbursement coverage, and scale. aura6000's PolySync programming is a genuine differentiator on the OSPREY data, but market entry against a dominant player is difficult and the outcome is binary. The depression program has been de-prioritized on CMS reimbursement and RECOVER-study challenges, a sensible capital-allocation choice. On balance the competitive position is solid and improving in the core, but the growth leg carries real displacement risk.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is SNIA. The Court of Appeal referral carries a June 24, 2026 hearing to explore settlement; the Court disallowed €157.3M of the prior award, while Public Administrations push for at least €108.8M. Original claims sought ~$4.0B, so the tail is wide in both directions. Management terminated the €270M litigation guarantee and released the collateral, and states it has resources to satisfy the liability, but the outcome is not in the company's control.

Secondary risks are being wound down but persist: 3T Heater-Cooler product liability (~60 claims, provision declining to $12.6M), Saluggia site remediation (~$41M, up to $54.6M), and the Italian MedTech payback measure (~$13.3M reserve). Supply-chain concentration (oxygenator fiber, rare-earth magnets) and tariff exposure are relevant given manufacturing in Italy/Germany for global distribution. The convertible note dynamics add GAAP earnings volatility. The company also flags shareholder activism as an explicit risk factor alongside board turnover, a potential catalyst for capital return but also a source of distraction. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 52.3% is rich against 1-year historical volatility of 37.0% (a 15.3-point spread, 80th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (52.3% near-term vs. 39.8% at six months), consistent with the market pricing the SNIA hearing and OSA launch cadence as imminent binary events. The 1-month implied move is ±13.2%. Put/call skew of +3.5% at the front month signals elevated put demand, a caution flag against complacency. Short interest is moderate at 8.2% of float (4.6 days to cover), not a squeeze setup. For a holder, the rich near-term IV and backwardation reinforce that the next few weeks carry event risk; for a prospective buyer, waiting for post-event IV normalization would be a more favorable entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The operating turnaround is real and underappreciated. Essenz continues taking perfusion share, driving Cardiopulmonary to sustained mid-teens growth; VNS epilepsy compounds steadily; and aura6000 becomes a credible third leg, capturing meaningful OSA share on PolySync differentiation. The SNIA hearing produces a manageable settlement well below the reserved liability, removing the largest overhang and freeing capital. Debt paydown continues, FCF compounds, and the stock re-rates toward its healthcare-equipment peer multiple of ~27x forward. On $4.63 next-year EPS at ~22-24x, the stock reaches $100-110, roughly the $95 analyst high.

**Bear case.** The SNIA settlement crystallizes a large cash obligation or the case reverts to a worse outcome, forcing a capital event. The aura6000 launch stalls against Inspire's entrenched position, turning the third growth leg into an expense drag. Estimate revisions, already flat, roll over as the beat streak normalizes. The stock de-rates back toward the low end of the analyst range ($67), roughly 17% below current price, as the ~90% run from the low proves to have front-run the fundamentals.

The base case sits between these: continued high-single-to-low-double-digit revenue growth, a SNIA outcome absorbed without a capital crisis, and a gradual OSA ramp. On ~$4.63 next-year EPS at ~19-20x, that supports a price of roughly $88-93, modestly above current and consistent with the analyst mean. The upside to base is limited relative to the downside if the SNIA binary breaks unfavorably.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD. The operating trajectory is favorable, but valuation has caught up and two binary catalysts sit directly ahead, making the near-term risk/reward balanced.

**Reassessment timeline:** The SNIA Court of Appeal settlement process and the next 1-2 quarterly prints tracking the aura6000 launch cadence are the natural reassessment points. Q2/Q3 2026 earnings will show whether OSA revenue is contributing and whether Essenz momentum holds.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- SNIA resolved via settlement materially below the reserve, removing the overhang without a capital event
- aura6000 delivers evidence of a successful launch — quantified OSA revenue contribution and reimbursement traction against Inspire in two consecutive quarters
- A pullback toward the mid-$60s (near the $67 analyst low), restoring a clear discount to forward earnings and to the peer multiple

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- SNIA outcome exceeds the reserve materially or reverts toward the original ~$4.0B claim scale, forcing dilutive financing
- Cardiopulmonary revenue growth decelerates below high-single digits, signaling Essenz share gains have plateaued
- Consensus current-year EPS estimates fall more than 5% over a 90-day window, reversing the current stability
- aura6000 launch metrics disappoint, confirming the OSA vector as an expense drag

## Conclusion

LivaNova has executed a genuine operating turnaround: accelerating revenue, 54% operating-income growth in FY25, $173M of free cash flow, and a balance sheet de-leveraged to essentially net-cash on operating debt. The headline net loss is a SNIA accounting artifact, and the underlying business is healthier than a screening pull would suggest. The score trajectory captures this: financial health has improved (5.8 → 6.8) even as valuation compressed (6.8 → 4.5) on the price run.

The reason to hold rather than buy is that the stock now reflects most of this improvement. At current forward multiples, trading only ~5% below the analyst mean target, with flattened estimate revisions and rich near-term implied volatility pricing imminent event risk, the asymmetry that justified the earlier buy calls is gone. The SNIA settlement is a live binary and the aura6000 launch is unproven against a dominant incumbent. We would upgrade on a favorable SNIA resolution, evidence of OSA traction, or a pullback toward the mid-$60s. For now, the risk/reward is balanced. HOLD.
