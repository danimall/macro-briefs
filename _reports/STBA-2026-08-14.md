## Executive Summary

S&T Bancorp is a $9.94bn-asset Pennsylvania community bank that has spent the last nine months converting excess capital into a smaller share count while its balance sheet stopped growing. The Q2 2026 print was operationally clean: net income $36.6m (+14.9% YoY), diluted EPS $1.02 (+22.9%), net interest margin 3.99% on an FTE basis, noninterest expense up only 0.9% year over year, nonaccruals down to 0.50% of loans, and every dollar of brokered deposits retired. Consensus responded: current-year EPS estimates rose 5.3% over 30 days to $3.94 and next-year to $3.99, and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters.

The composition of that improvement matters more than the headline. Management's own rate/volume table attributes the $3.8m year-over-year gain in quarterly net interest income to +$1.5m volume and +$2.3m rate — but within the rate component, interest *earned* fell $0.9m while interest *paid* fell $4.7m. Cost of interest-bearing liabilities dropped 34bp year over year against a 12bp decline in earning-asset yield. That tailwind is finite: 88.9% of the $1.95bn CD book matures during 2026 at an average cost already down to 3.64%, brokered deposits are at zero with nothing left to retire, and the received-fixed swap portfolio that dampened rate sensitivity has fallen from $350m to $250m notional. Separately, diluted weighted shares fell 6.8% year over year, which accounts for roughly 8 of the 22.9 points of Q2 EPS growth. Portfolio loans declined $13.6m in the first half because, in management's words, of "increased competition and higher CRE payoffs."

The unresolved item is credit, and it cuts against the headline. Special mention loans more than doubled from $69.0m to $143.0m on downgrades of four C&I and three CRE relationships; total criticized and classified balances rose 23.8% to $242.4m, or 3.01% of loans from 2.42%. Payment defaults on previously modified loans were $13.7m in the first half versus $3.9m a year earlier, entirely C&I. The allowance was unchanged at $93.3m (1.16% of loans). Management's explanation — that a mix shift out of substandard offset the special-mention increase — is technically sound under CECL, but the reserve ratio has declined from 1.41% in FY2023 to 1.16% while gross criticized dollars are now rising. At 13.0x forward earnings, 1.76x tangible book and 4% below its 52-week high, the stock does not pay you to carry that ambiguity. HOLD.

## Company Overview

S&T Bank operates from Indiana, Pennsylvania across Pennsylvania and Ohio, with contiguous lending in New York, West Virginia, New Jersey, Delaware and Maryland. It reports a single Community Banking segment; the 10-Q states explicitly that the chief operating decision maker uses consolidated net income and reviews no lower-level revenue or expense detail. Out-of-footprint CRE and construction exposure was 3.5% of those combined portfolios and 1.7% of total loans at year-end 2025.

Revenue is overwhelmingly spread-based: FY2025 net interest income of $350.1m against noninterest income of $52.0m puts fee income at 12.9% of total revenue. The fee lines are small and stable — card income $18.3m, deposit service charges $16.4m, wealth management $12.4m on $2.1bn of assets under administration. At $9.9bn of assets the bank sits below the $10bn threshold that triggers Durbin interchange caps and direct CFPB supervision, which is relevant given $18.3m of card revenue.

The loan book is 68.9% commercial as of 6/30/26: CRE $3,485.9m (43.3%), C&I $1,590.1m (19.7%), commercial construction $475.5m (5.9%), with consumer real estate at $2,426.9m (30.1%). Management's four stated 2026 priorities are growing the deposit franchise, improving core profitability, maintaining asset quality and retaining talent. Loan growth is not among them, and neither is M&A.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings arc.** Diluted EPS peaked at $3.74 in FY2023, fell to $3.41 in FY2024 as deposits repriced (interest expense $128.5m → $181.1m), and recovered only to $3.49 in FY2025. ROTCE compressed 453bp from 17.15% to 12.62% over the same span. The 2026 recovery has been real: Q2 ROTCE 14.15% annualized, ROA 1.49%, 1H net income $71.7m (+9.8%) on EPS of $1.96 (+16.0%).

**Where the margin came from.** Certificates repriced from 4.07% to 3.64% on a $1.99bn average balance; money market from 3.04% to 2.57% on $2.19bn. Total deposits reached $8,086m at 6/30/26, but the composition change carries the story: customer deposits grew $307.7m (+4.0%), which funded the retirement of the remaining $180.4m of brokered money market balances (brokered CDs had already gone to zero during 2025). Average short-term borrowings fell from $257.5m in FY2024 to $90.3m in 1H26. Replacing 4.43%-cost brokered money with 2.57% customer money market is straightforwardly accretive and is the mechanism behind the 3.99% NIM, which is high for a $10bn commercial bank.

**Capital and payout.** This is the dominant capital-allocation fact. Across Q4 2025 through Q2 2026 the company retired approximately 3.17m shares (~8.3% of the prior share base) for about $134m at a weighted average near $42.19. Shares outstanding fell from 37,402,705 to 35,264,936 in six months. Regulatory capital funded it: Bancorp CET1 fell 68bp to 13.64%, Tier 1 leverage 60bp to 11.58%, and Tier 1 capital in dollars from $1,154.7m to $1,102.2m. Total equity declined $60.1m despite $71.7m of net income. First-half dividends of $26.7m plus $98.3m of repurchases equal a 174% total payout ratio against earnings, funded from the capital base.

At 13.64% CET1 versus a 7.00% minimum-plus-buffer, the balance sheet can absorb this. But 68bp of CET1 in two quarters is not a repeatable annual rate, and the new $100m authorization approved 22 July 2026 (effective 27 July, expiring 31 August 2027) equals roughly 5.5% of shares at the current price and would consume more than the year's retained earnings.

**A change in the buyback arithmetic that deserves emphasis.** Repurchases at $38.20 (Q4 2025) were struck at roughly 1.30x tangible book. At $51.36 the stock is 1.76x tangible book, so the same program is materially more dilutive to tangible book value per share and less accretive to earnings. A full $100m execution at current prices retires ~1.95m shares and removes $100m of tangible equity against roughly $87m of full-year retained earnings — TBV per share still grinds higher, but the margin is thin. The earnings yield being repurchased has fallen from ~9.2% at $38 to 7.7% at $51.36.

**Balance sheet quality.** The securities portfolio is entirely available-for-sale at $1,013m, so there is no unrecognized held-to-maturity loss; the net unrealized loss widened from $34.9m to $45.8m in the first half as rates rose. Composition is agency-dominated (CMOs $641.9m, commercial MBS $277.4m). Management continues to reposition — $34.8m sold and reinvested into longer-duration, higher-yielding paper in Q2 at a $1.7m loss, offset by a $1.9m gain on Visa Class B-2 conversion. The disclosure decomposes this transparently, but the "net gain on sale of securities" caption is not a clean read, and with both Class B tranches now converted, that offset is largely spent.

Uninsured deposits rose from 33.7% to 36.0% of total deposits ($2.9bn) while total deposits grew only 1.6% — the growth is concentrated in larger balances. Available borrowing capacity of $3.84bn covers uninsured deposits about 1.3x, and highly liquid assets were $916m (9.2% of assets).

**Rate positioning has flipped.** The 1–12 month NII sensitivity moved from +1.7% to +6.6% under +400bp and from –6.8% to –12.3% under –300bp between December and June, which management attributes to floating-rate loan mix and swap maturities. Having harvested the deposit-repricing benefit of the easing cycle, the bank has repositioned into a stance that needs rates to stay put or rise; that is the mirror image of the FY2025 narrative in which management credited a more interest-rate-neutral balance sheet for holding NIM up during easing. Note also that the runoff of the received-fixed swaps removes a current drag: $489k was reclassified out of AOCI as a reduction to Q2 interest income, with a further $1.5m scheduled over the next twelve months.

## Growth Analysis

There is very little organic growth to underwrite. Portfolio loans fell $13.6m (–0.2%) in 1H26; commercial grew $25.2m while consumer fell $50.5m. CRE contracted $140.9m (–3.9%) on payoffs and competitive refinancing, partly replaced by commercial construction, which grew $95.4m (+25.1%) to $475.5m. Management named the cause of the Q1 decline as increased competition. Commercial loan yields fell from 6.19% to 6.02%, with C&I down sharply from 6.71% to 6.18% — the bank chose runoff over price matching, which is defensible but caps the top line.

Fee income is sub-scale and roughly flat. Wealth AUA went from $2.0bn to $2.1bn on a $9.9bn balance sheet. Card income is exposed to the $10bn Durbin threshold if the bank ever crosses it.

Consensus reflects this. Current-year EPS of $3.94 and next-year of $3.99 imply 1.3% growth. Given ~5% of potential buyback-driven share reduction, that consensus embeds a mid-single-digit decline in net income — consistent with a peaked NIM, normalizing provisions and flat balances. Estimate revisions have been positive (+5.3% for the current year over 30 days, +5.7% over 90 days), and the beat record (three of four, average surprise ~6.6% on the beats) supports confidence in the near-term numbers. But these are 5% revisions on a low-growth base, not the 30%+ revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection. The 2027 EPS bridge depends almost entirely on repurchase and expense control.

Growth prospects score 4.6.

The forward P/E sits about 11% below the regional-bank peer median while STBA earns a 1.49% ROA and 14.15% quarterly ROTCE — both above typical for the group. That is the bull's valuation argument, and it is not trivial. The peer set is heterogeneous (TFIN at 51.5x, CASH at 10.9x), so the median carries limited precision.

On capital, the picture is fuller. Backing out an implied cost of equity from 1.76x tangible book at 13.7% ROTCE and 3% terminal growth gives roughly 9.1% — a reasonable, not generous, discount rate for a $1.8bn bank with a 4.6% risk-free rate. The multiple already assumes ROTCE holds near 13.5–14%, which requires the 3.99% NIM to persist after the deposit-repricing benefit is exhausted and provisions to stay near 0.07% of loans annualized.

The market's judgment does not look wrong. Base case: 13.5x FY2027 consensus $3.99 = $53.87. Cross-checked on capital, 1.77x projected TBV of ~$30.48 = $53.95. Both land near **$54**, roughly 5% above the current price, or ~8% total return with the dividend over twelve months.

Two things prevent a higher valuation score. First, the stock is trading at the bottom of the analyst target range ($51.00 low, $53.33 mean), and while consensus targets lag on genuine inflection stocks, this is not one — 1.3% expected EPS growth does not generate upward target revisions. Second, the estimate revisions that would ordinarily support a higher multiple are modest in magnitude and rest largely on the liability-repricing benefit already captured. Cheapness relative to a peer median is not, by itself, a reason to expect re-rating.

Valuation score 4.8.

## Competitive Landscape

The funding side is genuinely improving and is the source of the above-peer margin. Noninterest-bearing demand deposits are approximately 28% of total deposits, blended interest-bearing deposit cost was 2.43% in Q2, and growing customer deposits $307.7m while retiring 100% of brokered funding is not the behaviour of a bank losing a deposit war. Within FY2025, however, personal deposits grew $301.2m (+6.6%) while business deposits fell $117.1m (–4.4%) — the retail franchise is doing the work, and commercial operating accounts, which are the stickiest and cheapest, are under pressure. Worth monitoring.

The asset side is losing ground. CRE payoffs to competitors, a 17bp decline in commercial loan yield, and flat total balances describe a lender being outbid. The response — shifting originations toward construction at 6.40% — improves spread but concentrates in the highest loss-severity commercial category. The entire construction book is currently risk-rated Pass, and the concentration test against total capital (38.8% versus a 100% threshold) is comfortably passed. The combined CRE-plus-construction figure of ~323% of total capital cannot be compared to the 300% interagency guidance, because that guidance excludes owner-occupied CRE and S&T does not disclose the owner-occupied split; I am not treating the bank as exceeding that threshold.

M&A posture is passive. The S-3 shelf remains entirely unused, no transaction appears in the two-year 8-K record, and strategic priorities omit acquisitions. A $9.9bn bank with a 28% noninterest-bearing deposit base and 13.7% ROTCE in a consolidating Pennsylvania/Ohio market is at least as plausible a target as an acquirer, though there is no evidence of any discussion.

Trajectory: improving funding franchise, eroding asset-side pricing power, sub-scale fee businesses. Net stable. Score 5.2, unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Criticized-asset migration with a flat allowance (highest priority).** Special mention rose from $69.0m to $143.0m on seven downgraded commercial relationships; substandard fell $28.1m on paydowns; total criticized rose 23.8% to $242.4m (3.01% of loans from 2.42%). The ACL was flat at $93.3m. The CECL mix-shift explanation holds arithmetically, but the reserve ratio has fallen from 1.41% (FY2023) to 1.16% while gross criticized dollars rise. The industries, property types and individual sizes of the seven relationships are not disclosed, so I cannot determine whether this is idiosyncratic or the leading edge of a commercial cycle. A single quarter of migration from special mention to nonaccrual with a catch-up provision would produce an earnings surprise the current 13x multiple does not contemplate.

**2. C&I modification re-defaults.** Payment defaults on previously modified loans were $10.3m in Q2 and $13.7m in 1H26 against $0.1m and $3.9m a year earlier. Of $36.8m modified in the trailing twelve months, $10.3m (28%) was 90+ days past due at quarter-end, all C&I. Twelve commitments exist to lend a further $1.7m to modified borrowers versus $0.1m a year prior. Absolute dollars are small (0.13% of loans) and the largest individually evaluated C&I credit already carries a 25% collateral adjustment, up from 10%, so these may be identified and reserved. The direction is the concern, not the level.

**3. Margin durability.** 88.9% of CDs mature during 2026 at 3.64%, brokered funding is at zero, and asset yields fell 12bp year over year. Forward NIM now requires rates to hold or rise; the –300bp NII sensitivity worsened to –12.3%.

**4. Buyback runway.** 68bp of CET1 consumed in two quarters at a 174% total payout. The new authorization is a maximum, not a commitment. If it is not executed, the 2027 EPS bridge weakens materially; if it is executed fully at 1.76x tangible book, capital declines with thinner accretion.

**5. Goodwill and stress sensitivity.** Goodwill of $373.4m equals 26.6% of equity, and the October 2025 annual test was qualitative only. Impairment risk has receded with the share price rather than for operational reasons. Separately, the 10-K discloses that under management's severely adverse scenario the ACL would rise approximately 107% — roughly $100m of incremental provision, about 1.1 years of pretax earnings, absorbable within capital but not within a single year's profit.

**6. Deposit composition.** Uninsured deposits rose 250bp to 36.0% in six months while total deposits grew 1.6%, implying larger-balance concentration and marginally higher rate and confidence sensitivity. Borrowing capacity of $3.84bn provides 1.3x coverage.

**7. Disclosure gaps.** Five 8-K Item 5.02 director/officer filings and two bylaw amendments (February and May 2026) are unread; the proxy has not been reviewed, so LTIP metrics, ownership guidelines and related-party detail are unverified. Legal Proceedings is "None" in the Q2 10-Q.

**8. Short interest.** 7.2% of float with 7.6 days to cover is elevated for a sleepy $1.8bn regional bank and is consistent with a credit-skeptic position. It also creates squeeze potential on any clean credit quarter.

Risk factors score 4.7 — the credit-migration channel is specific, monitorable and not obviously reflected in a stock 4% below its high with rising estimates.

### Options Market Signal

The supplied chain reads as low-quality data rather than a signal. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 99.5% against 23.2% one-year realized, with a ±20.3% implied move on the 21 August expiry, is implausible for a $1.8bn bank with no scheduled catalyst — earnings were released 23 July and the next print is late October. Corroborating the data-quality read: the entire chain is 23 contracts across four expirations, put/call open interest is reported as 0.00 (no puts open at all), and ATM IV has *fallen* from 73.0% to 62.5% since the early-July snapshot. The term structure normalizes to 21.0% at six months, roughly in line with realized volatility. I draw no directional inference. The absence of any accumulated put open interest is mildly inconsistent with the bearish credit view, but with a chain this thin it carries no weight.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $60 (+16.8%, ~19.7% with dividend)

The seven downgraded relationships cure without loss, as most special-mention credits do, and charge-offs stay near the current 0.07% annualized. A Fed that holds or hikes rewards the newly asset-sensitive balance sheet: the +200bp scenario adds 3.5% to net interest income. Securities remixing continues to lift the 3.83% portfolio yield toward loan yields, and the $1.5m swap drag rolls off. Commercial loan growth resumes at low single digits as the CRE payoff wave passes. The new $100m authorization is executed in full, retiring ~5.5% of shares, and the 2.9% dividend rises again. EPS reaches roughly $4.15–4.25 and the stock re-rates to ~14.5x — a market multiple for a bank earning 1.5% ROA. Optionality: a $9.9bn bank with a 28% noninterest-bearing deposit base is a credible target in a consolidating region.

### Bear Case — $40 (–22.1%)

The special-mention migration is the leading edge rather than noise. Two or three of the seven downgraded relationships move to nonaccrual over the next three quarters, forcing the allowance from 1.16% toward the 1.31–1.41% range of 2023–24 — roughly $12–20m of catch-up provision. Simultaneously, a Fed easing cycle removes 5.8% of net interest income under the –200bp scenario with the CD book already repriced and no brokered funding left to retire. Provisions normalize toward 0.45% of loans, EPS falls to ~$3.25, and the multiple compresses to 12.3x on a bank with no loan growth and a spent capital-return story. That produces ~$40, cross-checked at 1.35x tangible book. Construction, up 25% in six months and 55% originated in 2025, is where severity would show up.

### Probability-weighted view

55% base ($54, +8.0% total return) / 25% bull (+19.7%) / 20% bear (–19.2%) gives an expected twelve-month total return of approximately **+5.5%**, against a 4.6% ten-year Treasury. The implied equity risk premium is under one point. That is the arithmetic definition of a fairly-valued holding, not an accumulation candidate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income rationale).** The holding case is a 2.88% dividend covered at a ~38% payout, 13.64% CET1, and roughly 5% of potential annual share reduction — a defensible total-return package for existing owners at a modest discount to peer earnings multiples. New capital has no urgent entry signal 4% below the 52-week high and at the bottom of the analyst target range.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Q3 2026 disclosure showing special mention declining materially from $143.0m *without* migration to nonaccrual, and the ACL ratio stable or higher. This is the single most important data point in the next report.
2. Portfolio loans returning to ≥3% annualized growth with commercial yields stabilizing at or above 6.00%, evidencing that the CRE payoff wave has passed rather than that the bank is losing share.
3. A price below $44–45 on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore roughly 20% upside to the $54 base case and put the multiple back near 11x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A quarterly provision above $8m, or the ACL/loans ratio rising above 1.30% on new downgrades rather than mix — evidence the criticized migration is converting.
2. Nonaccruals returning above 0.75% of loans, or C&I modification re-defaults exceeding $15m in a single quarter.
3. NIM (FTE) falling below 3.85% for two consecutive quarters, which would confirm the liability tailwind is exhausted and asset yields are winning.
4. Buyback pace falling below ~$20m per quarter without a stated reason, which removes the primary EPS driver, or CET1 falling below 12.5%.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release, expected in the second half of October 2026, together with the subsequent 10-Q risk-rating tables. The credit question cannot be resolved before then from public disclosure.

## Conclusion

Score trajectory has moved 6.2 (May) → 5.4 (July) → 5.3 now, and the composition of that path is worth stating plainly. The July decline was driven by valuation and competitive position. The current, much smaller decline is driven by risk: the Q2 print and the estimate revisions that followed confirmed the operating improvement (NIM 3.99%, expenses +0.9%, brokered funding eliminated, EPS beat by 8.8%), while the 6 August 10-Q introduced a new credit flag — criticized assets up 23.8% with a flat allowance and C&I re-defaults up roughly 3.5x. Financial health holds up at 6.6 on genuinely strong capital and liquidity, tempered by a 174% payout drawing down CET1 by 68bp in two quarters and a reserve ratio that has fallen for three straight years. Management quality at 6.3 reflects buybacks executed at a $42.19 weighted average against today's $51.36, an 8.8% dividend increase, real pricing discipline on loans, and no open-market selling by any C-suite officer in the reviewed Form 4 record — every executive disposition was tax withholding on vesting. One non-executive director sold roughly 47% of his position on 11–12 August at $51.18–$51.43; that is a single data point and I do not read motive into it.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than something better is arithmetic, not doubt about the franchise. Consensus expects 1.3% EPS growth next year. The margin gain came from the liability side and is largely harvested. Loans have been flat for three quarters by management's own choice. The remaining EPS lever is a buyback that is now struck at 1.76x tangible book rather than 1.30x. At $51.36 the expected twelve-month total return works out near 5.5% probability-weighted, with a specific, unquantifiable credit risk attached and roughly 22% downside if it materializes. Existing holders are being paid a covered 2.9% to wait for the October risk-rating tables. Prospective buyers should wait for either those tables or a price in the mid-$40s.
