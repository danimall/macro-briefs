## Executive Summary

Viasat has undergone a genuine operational turnaround since its post-Inmarsat acquisition nadir. The December 2025 quarter delivered the first positive net income ($25M) since the merger, operating cash flow inflected sharply to $1.27B for the first nine months of FY2026, and the company is actively deleveraging ($800M of debt retired in nine months). The Ligado settlement adds a perpetual revenue stream worth ~$900M+ in present value. These are real, quantifiable improvements.

However, the stock has appreciated approximately 640% from its 52-week low of $8.61 to $74.56, far exceeding what the fundamental improvement supports. The company still carries $6.4B in gross debt at a blended ~8% interest rate ($380-400M annual interest), trades on a negative trailing P/E, and analyst EPS estimates for next year have declined 53% over the past 90 days. The options market is pricing 105% near-term implied volatility in backwardation, signaling institutional uncertainty about sustainability at these levels. With Starlink aggressively entering Viasat's core IFC, maritime, and broadband markets, the competitive moat is narrowing at precisely the moment the valuation demands it be widening.

The current price of $74.56 exceeds the analyst consensus mean target of $65.50 and median of $54.50 by 14% and 37% respectively. Speculative momentum has carried the stock well beyond what current earnings power and competitive positioning justify.

## Company Overview

Viasat is a global satellite communications company operating through two segments: Communication Services (~72% of revenue) and Defense and Advanced Technologies (~28%). The May 2023 acquisition of Inmarsat created a combined entity with 23 in-service satellites spanning Ka-, L-, and S-bands, providing near-global coverage.

Key revenue drivers include: In-Flight Connectivity (IFC) serving ~4,460 commercial aircraft and ~2,100 business jets; government satcom services (~16% of revenue from the U.S. government); maritime connectivity for ~13,400 vessels; and a deliberately shrinking fixed broadband business (~143,000 U.S. subscribers). The company differentiates through vertical integration — owning satellites, ground infrastructure, and terminals — and is pursuing a multi-orbit strategy combining GEO assets with LEO partnerships.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Growth Trajectory

Consolidated revenue grew 6% in FY2025 to $4.52B post-Inmarsat, with 9-month FY2026 revenue at $3.47B (+3% YoY). Growth is bifurcated: Aviation services grew 15% YoY to $304M in Q3 FY2026, while fixed broadband declined ~20% as bandwidth is deliberately reallocated. Defense & Advanced Technologies grew 9% YoY, benefiting from increased defense spending.

The 3% consolidated organic growth rate is insufficient to support the current enterprise value of ~$15.7B ($10.6B market cap + $5.1B net debt). At an annualized revenue run rate of ~$4.6B, the company trades at ~3.4x EV/Revenue — elevated for a business growing at low single digits.

### Profitability Inflection

The December 2025 quarter produced $25M in net income — the first positive quarter since the Inmarsat acquisition. Trailing twelve-month EPS remains -$4.48. The current quarter consensus is $0.32, and full-year FY2026 estimate is $1.09. On these numbers, VSAT trades at ~68x current-year forward earnings — a demanding multiple for a highly leveraged satellite operator facing secular competitive pressure.

### Cash Flow — The Strongest Part of the Story

Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow reached $1.27B, up $658M YoY. However, $420M of this came from the one-time Ligado settlement lump sum. Excluding Ligado, operating cash flow was approximately $850M for nine months (~$1.1B annualized), which is genuinely strong. Capital expenditures declined to $694M from $1.03B in the prior year as ViaSat-3 construction matures. This produces underlying free cash flow approaching $400-500M annualized — a meaningful inflection.

### Balance Sheet & Leverage

Total debt stands at $6.4B against $1.35B in cash (net debt ~$5.1B). At estimated annualized EBITDA of $1.6-1.7B, net leverage is ~3.1x. The blended interest rate of approximately 8% generates $380-400M in annual interest expense — consuming roughly 23% of EBITDA. The weighted-average cost of the variable-rate term loans (9.3-9.6%) makes refinancing a priority but also means rising rates would further compress margins.

No debt maturities arrive until 2027 ($600M at 5.625%), providing runway. The company has demonstrated deleveraging intent, retiring ~$800M in nine months. Total liquidity of ~$2.5B (cash plus revolver capacity) is adequate.

### The Ligado Stream

The Ligado settlement provides: $420M received (Oct 2025), $100M due March 2026, and perpetual quarterly payments of ~$16M (3% annual escalator) through 2107. At a 10% discount rate, the perpetual stream has a present value of approximately $900M-$1B. This is real, contracted, and growing. However, the market appears to have already capitalized this into the stock price given the 640% appreciation.

## Growth Analysis

Aviation IFC is the primary growth engine. The installed base grew from ~4,030 to ~4,460 aircraft (+11%) over twelve months, with ~1,100 additional aircraft contracted. At current IFC revenue run rates (~$1.2B annualized), this represents the highest-quality revenue line. ViaSat-3 F2 (launched November 2025, expected commercial service H1 FY2027) will unlock additional capacity, enabling both IFC growth and potential new fixed broadband customers.

Defense revenue ($202M/quarter) benefits from elevated defense spending globally. The D2D (Direct-to-Device) initiative represents optionality but lacks near-term revenue contribution.

Analyst estimates are deteriorating: next-year EPS consensus fell from $0.64 to $0.30 over 90 days (-53%). Current-year estimates fell from $1.28 to $1.09 (-15%). This negative revision momentum contradicts the stock's continued appreciation and represents a fundamental disconnect. The 30-day revision for the current quarter is flat at $0.32, but the longer-term trajectory is clearly down.

## Valuation Assessment

At $74.56, Viasat trades at:
- ~68x FY2026E EPS ($1.09)
- ~249x FY2027E EPS ($0.30)
- ~3.4x EV/Revenue
- Enterprise value ~$15.7B vs. EBITDA of ~$1.6-1.7B (~9.5x EV/EBITDA)

The stock price exceeds the analyst mean target ($65.50) by 14% and the median ($54.50) by 37%. Only the high-end target of $100 provides upside from current levels.

The valuation would require one of: (a) sustained 15%+ revenue growth (currently 3%), (b) margin expansion to generate $3+ EPS within two years, or (c) a take-private at premium to current levels. None of these scenarios appears probable given the competitive environment and capital structure constraints.

The stock's 640% appreciation from its 52-week low reflects a legitimate turnaround from near-distress levels ($8.61 represented genuine bankruptcy risk pricing). The move from $8 to $30 was the turnaround trade; the move from $30 to $75 reflects momentum and speculation beyond fundamental support.

## Competitive Landscape

The existential competitive question is Starlink. SpaceX's LEO constellation offers lower latency, rapidly expanding capacity, and aggressive pricing in aviation (IFC), maritime, and fixed broadband — all core Viasat markets.

Viasat's advantages: deep airline relationships (switching costs), vertical integration, multi-band capabilities (L-band for safety-critical maritime/aviation), and established ground infrastructure. The company is pursuing multi-orbit partnerships to supplement GEO capacity with LEO access.

Viasat's vulnerabilities: GEO satellites have higher latency (~600ms round-trip vs. ~20-40ms for LEO), longer replacement cycles (7-10 year build-deploy vs. Starlink's continuous refresh), and concentration risk (a single satellite failure can eliminate entire regions of coverage — as the ViaSat-3 F1 anomaly demonstrated).

Fixed broadband is in managed decline (subscribers down from ~160K to ~143K), which is rational but acknowledges Starlink's superiority in that market. Maritime is showing early pressure (-3.4% YoY). Aviation is holding and growing, but airlines are beginning to evaluate Starlink's IFC offering, and the switching window opens as contracts expire.

## Risk Assessment

### Leverage Risk (HIGH)
Any operational disappointment immediately threatens covenant compliance and equity value given the debt load and interest burden. The dual credit structure (Viasat parent vs. Inmarsat subsidiary) adds complexity.

### Competitive Displacement (HIGH)
Starlink's entry into IFC represents a generational competitive threat. Viasat's IFC contracts have finite terms — as they expire over the next 3-5 years, airlines will evaluate Starlink's lower-latency offering. The risk is not immediate but is existential over a 5-year horizon.

### Satellite Execution Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH)
ViaSat-3 F1 suffered anomalies resulting in a $1.67B impairment (partially offset by $770M in insurance). ViaSat-3 F2 launched November 2025 and is in commissioning. Any issues with F2 would eliminate the capacity expansion needed to sustain IFC growth.

### Estimate Deterioration (HIGH)
Negative revision momentum of this magnitude (-53% over 90 days) typically precedes further stock weakness with a 4-8 week lag, as sell-side models catch up to deteriorating fundamentals.

### CFO Transition
Shawn Duffy's departure during the turnaround adds execution uncertainty. Garrett Chase is now signing filings as SVP & CFO.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 105.4% against 79.3% historical volatility prices a 26pp risk premium. The term structure is in backwardation (105.4% near vs. 84.3% far), indicating the market expects a binary event or resolution within weeks. The put/call OI ratio of 0.07 is extremely call-skewed, suggesting speculative retail ownership — a contrarian negative signal when combined with deteriorating fundamentals. The implied ±21% move over the nearest expiry encompasses the full range between the analyst median target ($54.50, -27%) and the high target ($100, +34%).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($90-100)
- ViaSat-3 F2 enters service on schedule, unlocking capacity for IFC growth acceleration to 20%+
- Successful refinancing of high-cost Inmarsat debt ($1.975B at 9%) reduces interest expense by $50-80M annually
- Aviation IFC backlog of ~1,100 aircraft converts faster than expected
- Ligado perpetual stream + declining capex generates $600M+ sustainable FCF
- Defense spending increases benefit the D&AT segment
- Take-private interest from infrastructure/PE buyers at 12-14x EBITDA

### Bear Case ($35-50)
- ViaSat-3 F2 experiences anomalies similar to F1, delaying or impairing capacity expansion
- Airlines begin switching to Starlink IFC as contracts expire, eroding the core growth driver
- Rising rates increase variable-rate debt costs, compressing already-thin margins
- FY2027 EPS comes in at or below the declining $0.30 consensus
- The stock re-rates to 30-40x a normalized $1.00-1.50 EPS range = $30-60 range
- At 8-9x EV/EBITDA on stagnant EBITDA, equity value = $35-50 per share

### Base Case ($50-60)
ViaSat-3 F2 enters service successfully, IFC grows 12-15% annually, defense remains steady. The company generates $400-500M in annual FCF and pays down $500M-$1B in debt over 2 years, reducing leverage to ~2.5x. EPS reaches $1.50-2.00 by FY2028. At 30-35x those earnings, fair value is $45-70. The midpoint is roughly $55-60, closely aligned with the analyst median target of $54.50.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: FY2026 Q4 earnings (expected May-June 2026) will reveal whether the positive December quarter was sustainable or one-time-driven. The 53% decline in next-year EPS estimates suggests the sell-side is already modeling deterioration. ViaSat-3 F2 commissioning updates in the same timeframe could provide positive or negative news on the capacity expansion timeline.

**Timing window**: 30-60 days until next earnings release provides the natural resolution point. The stock's appreciation above all analyst targets creates vulnerability to any disappointment.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: SI at 9.6% of float with 6.2 days to cover is moderate — not crowded enough to prevent further shorting, but high enough (combined with elevated IV) to make timing painful. The 640% run has likely already squeezed most shorts out.

**Borrow and options signal**: Near-term IV of 105% makes put protection extremely expensive (17%+ for 1-month ATM puts). The extreme call-skew in OI (P/C ratio of 0.07) suggests speculative retail positioning — contrarian negative. Backwardation in the term structure indicates professional hedging demand is concentrated short-term.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Base case target of $55 represents -26% from current; bear case of $35-50 represents -33% to -53%. Upside risk to $90-100 represents +21% to +34%. Risk/reward from the short side at current levels: approximately 1.5:1 to base case, 2:1+ to bear case, against 1.3:1 upside risk to bull case. Moderately favorable.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE** — The thesis is directionally correct (stock has overshot fundamentals), but 105% implied volatility makes the cost of expressing this view prohibitive via options. Direct short carries 6.2 days-to-cover squeeze risk in a name with demonstrated momentum. A stock that has appreciated 640% can add another 30-50% on speculative flows before mean-reverting. The asymmetry is modestly favorable but not compelling enough to justify active shorting given the gamma risk. Better expressed as a "do not own" rather than an active short.

## Conclusion

Viasat's operational turnaround is real — the company has moved from near-distress to generating positive earnings and free cash flow. The Ligado settlement, declining capex, and IFC growth provide genuine fundamental improvement. However, at $74.56 the stock has priced in more than the turnaround delivers: it trades above all but the most optimistic analyst target, on declining forward estimates, with existential competitive risk from Starlink, and carrying $6.4B in high-cost debt. The 640% appreciation represents the market correctly identifying a turnaround but overshooting on its capitalization.

At 68x current-year earnings, 3.4x EV/Revenue, and with next-year estimates falling 53%, the risk/reward is asymmetrically unfavorable. The correct posture is to avoid ownership and monitor for a more reasonable entry point in the $45-55 range where the fundamental improvement is fairly compensated for the competitive and leverage risks.
