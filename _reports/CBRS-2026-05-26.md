## Executive Summary

Cerebras Systems completed the largest U.S. tech IPO of 2026 on May 14, raising $5.55 billion at $185/share. The stock surged to $386.34 intraday before settling at $311 on day one, and has since declined approximately 23% to ~$241. The company designs wafer-scale AI inference chips and operates cloud computing services, with $510M in 2025 revenue growing 76% year-over-year. However, 86% of that revenue came from two related UAE entities, the reported GAAP profit was entirely manufactured by a one-time $363.3M non-cash accounting gain, and the underlying business lost $145.9M at the operating level.

At approximately 87x estimated 2026 revenue and 587x trailing P/E (which itself reflects the artificial GAAP gain), Cerebras is priced for flawless execution across multiple dimensions simultaneously: converting a $24.6B backlog (80% OpenAI, 85% back-loaded to 2028+), scaling cloud operations from scratch against hyperscaler competition, navigating export control risk on its UAE revenue, and doing so while Nvidia's $20B Groq acquisition directly targets the same inference market niche. The technology is genuinely differentiated — the wafer-scale architecture has real inference speed advantages — but the gap between current fundamentals and current price requires an extraordinary degree of conviction in execution that cannot be supported by the available evidence.

## Company Overview

Cerebras Systems, founded in 2016 by Andrew Feldman (previously SeaMicro, acquired by AMD for $355M), designs the Wafer-Scale Engine 3 (WSE-3) — a 46,225 mm² chip occupying an entire silicon wafer, containing 4 trillion transistors and 900,000 compute cores with 44GB of on-chip SRAM. The core technical advantage: large language model weights never need to travel between chips during inference, eliminating inter-chip communication latency that plagues multi-GPU clusters.

The company has pivoted from chip sales to operating as a cloud inference provider, running its own data centers and selling AI compute as a service. This puts it in direct competition with AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, and Oracle — companies that are simultaneously customers (AWS partnership signed March 2026) and competitors building their own custom inference chips.

Key partnerships include a $20B+ Master Relationship Agreement with OpenAI (January 2026, 750MW of compute through 2028 with 1.25GW option through 2030) and a binding AWS term sheet for hardware deployment inside Amazon data centers. The company employs 708 people and manufactures exclusively at TSMC on a 5nm process node.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

2025 revenue of $510M represents 76% growth from $290M in 2024. However, the quality of this revenue requires scrutiny:

- **Customer concentration:** G42 contributed 24% and MBZUAI contributed 62% of 2025 revenue. These are related UAE entities. Combined: 86% from what is effectively a single customer relationship.
- **GAAP vs. reality:** The reported $237.8M GAAP net income includes a $363.3M one-time non-cash gain from extinguishing a G42 forward-contract liability. Excluding this, GAAP operating loss was $145.9M and non-GAAP net loss was $75.7M — a deterioration of 247% from 2024.
- **The trailing P/E of 587x is meaningless** as a valuation anchor because the "E" is an accounting artifact.

### Balance Sheet and Cash Position

Post-IPO, Cerebras has approximately $5.55B in proceeds plus existing cash, offset by:
- $1B OpenAI loan at 6% interest (with severe clawback provisions)
- $2.2B Canadian data center lease (10-year minimum payments)
- $344M in existing non-cancelable data center commitments
- $250M revolving credit facility from Morgan Stanley (expandable to $850M)

The cash position is strong near-term, but capital obligations are front-loaded while revenue recognition is back-loaded. Only 15% of the $24.6B remaining performance obligations (RPOs) convert in 2026-2027 combined. This creates a structural cash flow timing mismatch.

### Backlog Quality

The $24.6B RPO carries material execution risk:
- ~80% attributable to a single customer (OpenAI)
- OpenAI can terminate if delivery timelines slip or service falls below thresholds
- OpenAI's $1B loan includes provisions allowing fund seizure and immediate repayment upon termination
- Conversion depends on Cerebras successfully building and operating cloud infrastructure at hyperscaler scale — something it has never done

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. AI Inference Market Expansion:** The industry shift from training-dominated compute spend toward inference scaling theoretically favors Cerebras' architecture. Inference workloads reward low latency and cost-per-token, where wafer-scale SRAM provides measurable advantages over multi-GPU solutions.

**2. OpenAI Deployment Ramp:** The Master Agreement, if fully executed, transforms Cerebras' revenue trajectory from 2027 onward. OpenAI's expanded coding tools reportedly run on Cerebras hardware already.

**3. Hyperscaler Partnerships:** The AWS binding term sheet (March 2026) plus potential further cloud provider deployments could diversify beyond UAE concentration.

However, growth headwinds are equally concrete:
- Nvidia's Groq acquisition (December 2025, $20B) directly targets the SRAM-based inference market with Nvidia's manufacturing scale, software ecosystem (CUDA), and customer relationships
- Google TPU, Amazon Trainium/Inferentia, and Microsoft Maia represent custom inference silicon from companies with existing cloud infrastructure at scale
- DeepSeek's 75% inference price cut compresses the revenue opportunity per unit of compute deployed
- 5nm process vs. 2nm/3nm cutting-edge creates a transistor density disadvantage that will compound over chip generations

Revenue growth of 76% YoY is strong but from a small base. At $510M in 2025 revenue against an implied ~$56B enterprise value, Cerebras must reach multi-billion-dollar revenue within 2-3 years to justify current pricing. That requires flawless contract execution, successful cloud operations buildout, and maintaining competitiveness against better-capitalized competitors.

- ~87x estimated 2026 revenue (assuming ~$900M-$1B in revenue with continued strong growth)

- No meaningful FCF yet generated

**Peer comparison:**
- Nvidia: ~26x trailing revenue
- AMD: ~21x trailing revenue
- Marvell: ~14x trailing revenue

Even granting Cerebras a significant growth premium for 76% revenue growth, an 87x revenue multiple requires sustaining >60% annual growth for 5+ years while achieving 30%+ operating margins — both undemonstrated at scale.

**The backlog argument:** Bulls point to $24.6B in RPOs. But at 15% conversion rate in 2026-2027 and 80% concentration in OpenAI, the backlog is essentially an option on a single customer relationship executing over 4+ years. If OpenAI terminates, diversifies, or builds internal inference capacity, the RPO collapses.

**IPO overshoot:** The stock opened at $350 (89% above IPO price) and has corrected to $241. The IPO was 20x oversubscribed, suggesting retail and growth-fund enthusiasm that typically mean-reverts as lock-ups expire and fundamentals must carry the stock rather than scarcity value.

At $241, the market values Cerebras at approximately what Nvidia paid for Groq ($20B) scaled up by ~2.8x, despite Groq now having Nvidia's full manufacturing, software, and customer ecosystem behind it. This premium is difficult to justify.

## Competitive Landscape

Cerebras' wafer-scale architecture provides genuine technical differentiation for inference workloads. The 44GB on-chip SRAM eliminates memory bandwidth bottlenecks that constrain GPU-based inference. Independent benchmarks have shown Cerebras achieving significantly faster token generation on large models.

However, competitive dynamics are deteriorating:

**Nvidia + Groq (Critical Threat):** Nvidia's Groq acquisition gives it an SRAM-based inference architecture similar in concept to Cerebras', combined with Nvidia's manufacturing relationships, CUDA software ecosystem, and 500,000+ developer community. Groq-based products launched at GTC in March 2026. This is the most directly comparable competitive threat.

**Hyperscaler Internal Silicon:** Google TPU (inference-optimized versions deployed at scale), Amazon Trainium/Inferentia (integrated with Bedrock/SageMaker), and Microsoft Maia represent hundreds of billions of cumulative R&D investment targeting the same inference market. These companies are simultaneously Cerebras' potential customers and its competitors, and partnerships may evaporate once internal solutions mature.

**Process Node Gap:** WSE-3 runs on TSMC 5nm. Competitors are moving to 3nm and 2nm. While Cerebras' architectural advantage (massive die area, on-chip SRAM) may compensate for older process nodes in inference-specific workloads, the gap will widen with each generation unless Cerebras migrates.

**TSMC Single-Source Risk:** Manufacturing an entire defect-free wafer is extraordinarily difficult. Cerebras has no alternative supplier. Any TSMC capacity constraint, yield issue, or geopolitical disruption (Taiwan Strait) directly impacts supply.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

**1. Customer Concentration (Existential):** If export controls tighten, or the UAE political relationship with the U.S. shifts, the majority of current revenue disappears. The first IPO attempt in 2024 was withdrawn due to exactly this CFIUS concern.

**2. Lock-up Expiration (Near-Term, Quantifiable):** 60M+ shares becoming sellable within months against a 30M share IPO float. Early investors (Benchmark ~$3.3B, Foundation ~$2.8B, Fidelity ~$3.8B) have massive incentives to monetize. The tradeable supply could triple, creating persistent selling pressure.

**3. OpenAI Dependency:** OpenAI holds warrants worth potentially half the gross profit of the contract, can terminate on performance shortfalls, and its executives (Altman, Brockman) hold personal stakes creating conflicts of interest. OpenAI is also actively exploring custom silicon alternatives.

**4. Execution Risk in Cloud Operations:** Cerebras has never operated cloud infrastructure at hyperscaler scale. The business model has pivoted from chip design to cloud services without demonstrated competence in data center operations, network engineering, or customer support at scale.

**5. Competition Intensifying:** Nvidia's Groq acquisition directly targets Cerebras' niche. Hyperscalers building internal chips reduces addressable market. DeepSeek-style efficiency gains compress revenue per unit of deployed compute.

## Investment Thesis

- OpenAI deployment ramps on schedule, converting RPOs to revenue at attractive margins
- AWS and additional hyperscaler partnerships diversify revenue beyond UAE concentration
- First-mover advantage in dedicated inference hardware creates switching costs as customers optimize models for the WSE-3 architecture

### Bear Case
- Nvidia's Groq unit delivers comparable inference performance with superior software ecosystem and go-to-market capabilities, commoditizing Cerebras' advantage
- OpenAI terminates or scales back the Master Agreement as it develops internal capabilities or shifts to alternative suppliers
- Export controls tighten on UAE technology transfers, eliminating the majority of current revenue
- Lock-up expiration triples float, creating sustained selling pressure that overwhelms limited buy-side demand
- Cloud operations fail to scale efficiently, burning through IPO proceeds without achieving profitability

## Conclusion

Cerebras Systems possesses genuinely differentiated technology in AI inference computing. The wafer-scale architecture has measurable performance advantages, the $24.6B backlog provides theoretical revenue visibility, and the AI inference market is growing rapidly. CEO Feldman has a credible track record (SeaMicro exit to AMD).

However, at ~87x estimated 2026 revenue with 86% customer concentration in UAE entities, a misleading profitability narrative, $145.9M in operating losses, intensifying competition from Nvidia/Groq, unproven cloud operations, and 60M+ lock-up shares pending release against a 30M float, the risk/reward is deeply unfavorable. The stock must decline approximately 50-60% from current levels before the valuation begins to compensate for the concentration, execution, and competitive risks embedded in the business.

The technology may ultimately succeed. The current price assumes it already has.
