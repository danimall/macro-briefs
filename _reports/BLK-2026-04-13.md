## Executive Summary

BlackRock is the world's largest publicly traded asset manager with $14.0 trillion in AUM, generating $24.2 billion in revenue and $9.6 billion in adjusted operating income in FY2025. The company posted record $698 billion in net inflows — 9% organic base fee growth at this scale reflects the compounding power of the iShares ETF franchise ($527B in ETF inflows alone) and an accelerating pivot toward higher-fee private markets. Adjusted EPS grew 10% to $48.09. At approximately 18x adjusted forward earnings, BlackRock trades at a meaningful discount to asset management peers despite its unmatched competitive position.

The principal near-term risk is the private credit redemption wave hitting the newly acquired HPS business. The $26 billion HPS Corporate Lending Fund activated 5% quarterly redemption gates after $1.2B+ in withdrawal requests, and Bloomberg reported HPS funds posted their worst monthly losses in three years in February 2026. This is a legitimate concern, but it must be sized correctly: HPS private credit represents roughly $165B of $14.0T in total AUM — about 1.2%. The core business — iShares, Aladdin, institutional fixed income — is structurally unaffected. BlackRock's $16.9B in total liquidity, sub-1x net leverage, and $7.5B operating cash flow provide substantial capacity to absorb private credit volatility without systemic impairment.

The stock has declined approximately 8% YTD through late March 2026, creating an improved entry point. The private credit stress, while headline-damaging, reinforces BlackRock's long-term competitive advantage: weaker private credit managers will lose access to capital while BlackRock's institutional relationships and balance sheet allow it to weather the cycle and emerge with greater market share. We rate the stock BUY.

## Company Overview

BlackRock was founded in 1988 and has grown into the dominant global investment management franchise through organic growth and strategic acquisitions (most notably Barclays Global Investors/iShares in 2009). The company operates across four major segments:

**iShares ETFs ($5.5T AUM):** The world's largest ETF platform, generating $527B in net inflows in FY2025. This business provides massive operating leverage — incremental ETF inflows generate fees with minimal marginal cost. iShares spans equities, fixed income, commodities, and digital assets.

**Institutional ($4.8T AUM):** Serves pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, insurance companies, and endowments globally. Fixed income and multi-asset mandates dominate. The institutional index business experienced $119B in outflows in 2025, which bears monitoring.

**Aladdin Technology ($2.0B revenue, +16% organic ACV growth):** BlackRock's proprietary risk management and portfolio analytics platform serves over 200 institutional clients. Aladdin creates deep operational lock-in — once embedded in a client's investment process, switching costs are extremely high. The addition of Preqin (acquired for ~$3.2B in March 2025) extends this platform into private markets data and analytics.

**Private Markets ($323B AUM, up 136% from $137B in 2023):** The fastest-growing segment following the GIP ($70B infrastructure) and HPS ($165B private credit) acquisitions. Private markets generated 15% of base fees on just 3% of average AUM in 2025, illustrating the fee-rate uplift that makes this pivot strategically essential.

**Geographic Mix:** Approximately 70% Americas, 25% EMEA, 5% Asia-Pacific.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $24.2B grew 19% YoY, driven by market appreciation, record inflows, and full-year contributions from GIP. The revenue mix is shifting favorably:

| Revenue Source | FY2025 | FY2024 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Investment advisory & admin fees | $19.2B | $16.4B | +17% |
| Performance fees | $1.4B | $656M | +113% |
| Technology services | $2.0B | $1.8B | +11% |
| Distribution fees | $1.4B | $1.3B | +8% |

Performance fees more than doubled, driven by strong alternative investment returns and HPS contributions. This revenue line is inherently volatile, but the trajectory reflects the private markets buildout generating real fee income.

**Margin divergence is critical to understand.** GAAP operating margin compressed 800 basis points to 29.1%, while as-adjusted operating margin held at 44.1%. The $2.6B gap is entirely acquisition accounting: $775M intangible amortization, $738M acquisition-related compensation, $720M contingent consideration fair value changes, and $122M transaction costs. Investors should track adjusted margins as the better indicator of underlying business economics — but should also recognize that the acquisition-related compensation ($738M) represents real cash leaving the business to retain HPS and GIP personnel.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow (excluding consolidated investment products) was $7.5B in FY2025. Free cash flow after capex was approximately $3.6B (operating cash flow of $3.6B on a GAAP basis including CIP effects). Capital expenditures were $438M. The company returned $4.9B to shareholders ($3.3B in dividends/distributions plus $1.6B in buybacks), funded partially by balance sheet cash.

FCF conversion relative to adjusted net income (~$7.4B) is approximately 50% on a GAAP basis but closer to 100% when excluding CIP consolidation effects and non-cash acquisition charges. The underlying cash generation engine is healthy.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet requires careful parsing. Total GAAP assets of $170B include substantial separate account assets and consolidated investment products that belong to clients, not BlackRock. Adjusted assets (excluding these) are approximately $98.8B.

**Key balance sheet considerations:**

- **Goodwill + intangibles of $63.3B (113% of equity):** This is the direct consequence of the acquisition strategy. Goodwill impairment testing is performed annually and depends on continued AUM growth and fee generation from acquired businesses. If the private credit stress deepens materially, GIP/HPS goodwill impairment becomes a non-trivial risk, though it would be non-cash.

- **Contingent consideration liabilities of $8.4B:** Split between GIP ($4.8B) and HPS ($3.5B), these are performance-based and payable in BlackRock stock/Subco Units. The $720M mark-to-market charge in FY2025 illustrates the P&L volatility this creates. Up to 13.8M additional shares (~9% potential dilution) may be issued from HPS-related commitments, depending on performance milestones.

- **Debt of $12.8B** is well-laddered from 2027 to 2055 with an average coupon of approximately 3.8%. Net debt-to-EBITDA is below 1.0x. Liquidity of $16.9B ($11.0B cash plus $5.9B revolver) is more than adequate. The April 3, 2026 8-K filing indicating a new material financial obligation suggests incremental borrowing, which bears monitoring but is not alarming given existing leverage metrics.

- **Altman Z-Score of 1.05 is a false positive** — the metric was designed for manufacturing firms and systematically misprices asset-light financial services companies with large balance sheets relative to revenue.

### Capital Return

BlackRock raised its quarterly dividend to $5.73/share (annualized ~$22.92, approximately 2.1% yield at current levels). Share repurchases of $1.6B in FY2025 were supplemented by a new 7M share authorization in January 2026. Total capital return of ~$4.9B represents approximately 66% of adjusted operating cash flow — a balanced approach that funds both shareholder returns and acquisition integration.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Engine

BlackRock's 9% organic base fee growth on a $14T base is the single most important metric in the report. To generate $698B in net inflows at this scale — more than many asset managers' total AUM — reflects the structural advantages of the iShares franchise and the deepening of the Aladdin technology moat.

Breaking down the inflow quality:
- **ETFs ($527B):** The core engine. BlackRock captures a disproportionate share of ETF industry inflows due to first-mover advantage, liquidity advantages in its largest funds, and the breadth of the iShares product suite.
- **Retail ($107B vs. $25B prior year):** A 4x acceleration that reflects improved distribution and the maturation of model portfolio adoption by wealth advisors using BlackRock products.
- **Active strategies ($136B vs. $62B):** Doubling of active inflows challenges the "passive-only" narrative. Active fixed income and multi-asset strategies are driving this.
- **Private markets ($40B vs. $9B):** Still early but accelerating rapidly. This is where the GIP/HPS acquisitions provide distribution leverage.
- **Digital assets ($35B):** Primarily the iShares Bitcoin ETF (IBIT), which captured enormous share in 2024-2025.

The $119B institutional index outflow is the one negative data point. This likely reflects rebalancing by large institutions (reallocating from passive equity to fixed income or alternatives) rather than competitive loss, but if this outflow pace persists it would signal share erosion in the most price-sensitive segment.

### Private Markets Pivot

The strategic logic is sound: private markets generate approximately 5-7x the fee rate of index products. Moving from 3% of AUM to even 5% of AUM in private markets would meaningfully lift the blended fee rate across the entire platform.

The execution risk is elevated. Three major acquisitions in 18 months (GIP October 2024, Preqin March 2025, HPS July 2025) create integration complexity at a time when the underlying private credit market is experiencing its first real stress cycle since 2020. The $935M in RSU retention grants to HPS employees (vesting over 5 years) indicates significant key-person risk if the private credit business underperforms and retention becomes difficult.

BlackRock's target of $400B in private markets fundraising by 2030 (from a $323B base) implies sustained 5-7% annual organic growth in private markets. This is achievable in a normal environment but will be tested by the current redemption cycle.

### Technology

Aladdin's 16% organic annual contract value growth is underappreciated. At $2.0B in revenue and growing at mid-teens rates, the technology platform alone would be valued at 15-25x revenue as a standalone SaaS business ($30-50B). The Preqin acquisition extends this into private markets data, creating the potential for a unified public-private analytics platform that has no direct competitor.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $1,050-1,100 per share (incorporating the ~8% YTD decline through late March), BlackRock trades at:
- ~18x FY2025 adjusted EPS ($48.09)
- ~22x FY2025 GAAP EPS ($35.31)
- Approximately 30x GAAP trailing P/E including full acquisition charges

The appropriate valuation lens is the adjusted P/E of ~18x. Peer context: traditional asset managers (T. Rowe Price, Invesco, Franklin Templeton) trade at 10-14x, while alternative asset managers (KKR, Apollo, Ares) trade at 25-40x. BlackRock occupies a unique position — it has the scale and stability of a traditional manager with an increasing alternative asset mix.

At 18x adjusted earnings, BlackRock is trading closer to traditional asset managers than alternative managers despite its $323B private markets business, $2.0B technology platform, and dominant ETF franchise. If the private markets pivot succeeds and reaches $400B+ by 2030, a re-rating toward 22-25x adjusted earnings (midway between traditional and alternative multiples) would imply significant upside.

The valuation discount relative to its strategic positioning is the core reason we maintain BUY despite the near-term headwinds. The market is pricing BlackRock as if the private credit stress invalidates the entire pivot, when it affects less than 2% of total AUM.

## Competitive Landscape

BlackRock's competitive moat is among the strongest in financial services:

**Scale economics:** At $14T, BlackRock can spread fixed costs (technology, compliance, distribution) across a vastly larger base than any competitor. The marginal cost of managing an additional $1B in iShares is effectively zero.

**iShares franchise:** The ETF business benefits from a liquidity flywheel — the largest funds attract the most trading volume, which tightens bid-ask spreads, which attracts more assets. This creates a self-reinforcing advantage that is nearly impossible to dislodge. Vanguard is the primary competitor but operates as a mutual structure without public market accountability.

**Aladdin lock-in:** Once Aladdin is integrated into a client's portfolio management, risk, and trading infrastructure, switching costs are measured in years and tens of millions of dollars. The platform serves as both a revenue source and a distribution channel for BlackRock investment products.

**Distribution depth:** BlackRock has relationships with every major wirehouse, private bank, and institutional allocator globally. This distribution infrastructure took decades to build and cannot be replicated quickly.

**Threat assessment:** Fee compression in core index products will continue. Vanguard's fee leadership puts structural pressure on ETF margins. State Street's SPDR franchise competes for institutional ETF business. In private markets, established players (Apollo, Ares, KKR, Blackstone) have deeper track records and established investor relationships. BlackRock's competitive advantage in private markets is distribution access, not investment track record — which is why HPS retention is critical.

## Risk Assessment

### Private Credit Stress (Near-term, High Impact)

The HPS redemption gating is the most pressing risk. Poor performance drives redemption requests, which force selling to meet redemptions, which causes further losses and more redemptions — a negative feedback loop.

**Sizing the risk:** HPS private credit represents approximately 7% of base fee revenue (due to higher fee rates). Even a severe stress scenario — 15-20% AUM decline in HPS over 12 months — would reduce total BlackRock AUM by ~0.2% and base fee revenue by ~1.0-1.5%. This is meaningful but not existential.

The secondary risk is reputational: if BlackRock's first major private credit product cycle ends with gated redemptions and losses, institutional allocators may question the broader private markets thesis, slowing the path to $400B.

### Acquisition Integration

Three acquisitions totaling $15B+ in 18 months creates organizational strain. The $63.3B in goodwill/intangibles and $8.4B in contingent consideration liabilities are balance sheet manifestations of execution risk. If the acquired businesses underperform their earnout targets, BlackRock overpaid but reduces future share issuance. If they overperform, the contingent consideration dilution (~9% potential from HPS alone) is the cost.

### Market Sensitivity

With $7.8T in equity AUM, every 10% equity market decline reduces AUM by ~$780B and base fees by approximately $800M-$1.0B annually. The current macro environment — with a naval blockade pushing oil above $102, consumer sentiment at record lows, and recession probability at 45-50% — creates a credible scenario for equity market declines of 15-25% from recent highs. This would mechanically compress revenue by $1.2-2.5B annually.

Partially offsetting: fixed income AUM ($3.3T) benefits from flight-to-quality flows during risk-off periods, and BlackRock's cash management business ($1.1T) benefits from elevated short-term rates.

### Political/Regulatory

The ESG political risk is a two-sided squeeze: some US states restrict business with BlackRock over perceived anti-fossil-fuel policies, while European regulators push for more aggressive sustainability standards. This creates compliance costs and potential AUM losses in both directions. The appointment of Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser to the board signals a deliberate rebalancing of the ESG narrative.

### Options Market Signal

With the stock down ~8% YTD and private credit headlines generating negative sentiment, we would expect elevated put demand and rich implied volatility relative to realized. The credit-equity divergence across the broader market — HYG OI put/call ratios at multi-year highs while equity options are repricing — suggests institutional investors are hedging credit exposure broadly, which captures BlackRock indirectly through its private credit business. This options positioning aligns with our fundamental view that private credit stress is a near-term headwind but not a structural impairment.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$1,350, ~25-30% upside from ~$1,050)

- Private credit stress proves cyclical rather than structural; HPS redemptions stabilize by Q3 2026 as markets normalize
- Organic base fee growth sustains at 7-9% as iShares captures continued ETF industry share growth
- Aladdin technology platform re-rates to a higher multiple as investors recognize its standalone value
- Private markets AUM reaches $400B by 2028-2029, driving blended fee-rate expansion
- Multiple re-rates from ~18x to 22-24x adjusted earnings as the business mix shifts toward higher-fee products
- Math: 22x × $55 FY2027E adjusted EPS = $1,210; with continued growth trajectory, $1,350 by late 2027

### Bear Case (~$830, ~20% downside from ~$1,050)

- Private credit losses deepen; HPS AUM declines 25%+ triggering goodwill impairment review
- Equity market correction of 20%+ reduces AUM by $1.5T+, compressing fees by $1.5-2.0B annually
- Institutional index outflows accelerate from $119B to $200B+ as competitors (Vanguard, State Street) gain share
- Integration of GIP/HPS proves more costly than expected; acquisition-related compensation exceeds projections
- Multiple compresses to 15x adjusted earnings on private markets disappointment
- Math: 15x × $42 depressed adjusted EPS = $630 (extreme bear); 15x × $55 = $830 (moderate bear)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $1,200** — 20x FY2027E adjusted EPS of ~$55-58 (reflecting 10-12% adjusted EPS growth from $48.09 FY2025 through continued organic growth and acquisition integration). This represents approximately 14-15% upside from ~$1,050.

**Bull case target: $1,350** — 22-24x adjusted earnings on successful private markets execution and technology platform re-rating.

**Bear case target: $830** — 15x adjusted earnings on private credit stress deepening, equity market correction, and multiple compression. Approximately 20% downside.

**Upside/downside ratio: ~1.4:1** (base case) to **~1.5:1** (bull/bear).

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: Q1 2026 earnings (expected late April), which will reveal HPS redemption dynamics and organic flow trends; Q2 earnings providing full-quarter HPS post-stress data; and any resolution of broader credit market stress.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Organic base fee growth declines below 3% for two consecutive quarters (would indicate structural franchise deterioration)
2. HPS AUM declines more than 25% from acquisition-date levels (would indicate private credit strategy failure beyond cyclical stress)
3. Total net outflows in any quarter (BlackRock has never experienced total quarterly outflows; this would represent a fundamental break)

## Conclusion

BlackRock's competitive position in public markets asset management is durable. The $14T AUM platform, iShares ETF franchise, and Aladdin technology moat generate high-quality fee income with strong operating leverage. The strategic pivot toward private markets through GIP, HPS, and Preqin is directionally correct — the fee-rate uplift is mathematically compelling — even as the execution carries near-term integration and credit cycle risk.

At ~18x adjusted earnings, the market is pricing BlackRock as if private credit impairment will permanently damage the franchise, when the affected business represents approximately 1.2% of total AUM.

The insider selling (particularly COO Goldstein's $56M sale) and absence of open-market purchases temper our management quality score. CEO compensation growth outpacing TSR over 5 years is a governance concern. These issues reduce our confidence but do not override the fundamental value proposition at the current valuation.

We rate BlackRock BUY with a base case target of $1,200 over 12-18 months. The stock offers competitive durability, organic growth, and a valuation discount to its strategic positioning that outweighs the near-term private credit and macro headwinds.
