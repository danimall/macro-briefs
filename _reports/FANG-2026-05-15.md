## Executive Summary

Diamondback Energy is a Permian Basin pure-play that has consolidated its position through the Endeavor and Double Eagle acquisitions into one of the largest independent U.S. oil producers, running at ~980 MBOE/d in Q1 2026. The company reported a blowout quarter on May 4 (adjusted EPS $4.23 vs. approximately $3.55–$3.75 consensus estimate) and raised production and capex guidance for 2026. Based on our scenario analysis at sustained elevated oil prices, we estimate 2026 adjusted free cash flow potential of approximately $8.3B, implying the stock trades at roughly 6.9x estimated FCF at a $57B market cap, materially below its peer median P/E of 13.2x.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) FANG is among the lowest-cost large-scale Permian producers at $10.23/BOE full-year 2025 cash operating costs ($11.26/BOE in Q1 2026), meaning it generates substantial cash flow assuming oil prices remain above approximately $60; (2) the oil supply disruption from Hormuz-related geopolitical tensions provides a potential multi-quarter tailwind to realizations that consensus has been slow to fully capitalize; and (3) estimate revisions are accelerating at an extraordinary pace (+144% on current-quarter EPS over 90 days), confirming the fundamental improvement is still being underappreciated. The primary risk is commodity mean-reversion if conflict resolution occurs, which would compress cash flows by 30-40% from current run-rate. The breadth of insider and former-owner selling is an additional concern, though it primarily reflects Endeavor liquidation dynamics rather than fundamental pessimism.

## Company Overview

Diamondback Energy operates approximately 869,036 net acres exclusively in the Permian Basin (774,645 in the Midland Basin, 94,391 in the Delaware Basin). Following the $26B Endeavor acquisition (closed September 2024) and the Double Eagle acquisition (closed April 2025), the company has consolidated a position with approximately 8,854 gross economic drilling locations at $50 WTI, representing over a decade of inventory runway at current development pace.

The company's production grew 54% from 598 MBOE/d in 2024 to 921 MBOE/d in 2025, with Q1 2026 reaching 979,400 BOE/d (521,000 b/d oil). The production mix is approximately 54% oil, 22% natural gas, and 24% NGLs. FANG owns a publicly traded subsidiary, Viper Energy (VNOM), which holds mineral and royalty interests across ~86,600 net royalty acres in the Permian following the Sitio acquisition.

Beginning in Q2 2026, management removed the company's prior minimum 50% return-of-capital quarterly commitment, opting instead for flexibility to allocate excess free cash flow among base dividends, share repurchases, debt reduction, or cash-building as market conditions warrant. The remaining share buyback authorization is approximately $2.1B as of May 1, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Cash Flow:** Full-year 2025 total revenues were $15.0B (oil, natural gas, and NGL revenues of $13.5B), up from $11.1B in total revenues in 2024, driven by the production step-up from Endeavor. Operating cash flow grew to $8.76B. Capital expenditures (excluding acquisitions) were $3.5B, implying free cash flow of approximately $5.3B. For 2026, management has raised CapEx guidance to ~$3.9B (adding 2-3 rigs for Barnett/Woodford development) and raised production guidance to 520+ MBO/d oil / 972+ MBOE/d. Based on our scenario analysis assuming sustained oil prices near current levels (~$100+ WTI), we estimate 2026 adjusted free cash flow potential of approximately $8.3B. Q1 2026 adjusted FCF was $1.737B.

**Per-BOE Economics:** Full-year 2025 total cash operating costs were $10.23/BOE (LOE of $5.55/BOE, cash G&A of $0.62/BOE, production taxes of $2.53/BOE, gathering/transport of $1.53/BOE). Q1 2026 total cash operating costs rose to $11.26/BOE (LOE of $6.21/BOE, production/ad valorem taxes of $3.04/BOE, gathering/processing/transport of $1.36/BOE, cash G&A of $0.65/BOE). Even at the higher Q1 run-rate, FANG maintains a strong cost position among large-scale Permian producers. Management's revised 2026 plan targets generating meaningful free cash flow per share assuming WTI averages over $60 for the remainder of the year.

**Balance Sheet:** As of March 31, 2026, consolidated total debt was approximately $14.1B (net debt of $13.9B). Following April 2026 debt repayments, pro forma gross debt was approximately $12.7B. Debt-to-equity is approximately 0.76x. Near-term maturities as of year-end 2025 were $763M in 2026 and $1.9B in 2027 (subsequent repayments have reduced these balances). Investment-grade ratings (BBB/BBB+/Baa2) and $2.6B of liquidity (including $2.5B undrawn revolver) provide adequate cushion. The company previously reduced its return commitment from 75% to 50% to accelerate deleveraging, and beginning Q2 2026 removed the formulaic minimum commitment entirely, providing maximum flexibility for debt reduction.

**Q1 2026 Results:** Production of 979,400 BOE/d beat the high end of guidance. Revenue of $4.24B beat estimates by approximately 8%. Adjusted EPS of $4.23 beat consensus of approximately $3.55–$3.75 (GAAP diluted EPS was $0.08, depressed by a $1.4B non-cash ceiling-test impairment). Management raised full-year oil production guidance to 520,000+ b/d as "the new baseline" and committed to growth spending into what CEO Van't Hof described as a "legitimate supply-demand imbalance."

**GAAP vs. Cash Earnings:** The trailing P/E of 206x reflects the $3.7B non-cash ceiling test impairment taken in Q4 2025 and an additional $1.4B impairment in Q1 2026, both under full-cost-method accounting (using backward-looking 12-month SEC prices that averaged $64.73 during a period before the current oil price surge). On a forward EPS basis, the stock trades at approximately 10.5x 2026 consensus ($19.28) and 12.0x 2027 ($16.96).

## Growth Analysis

**Production Growth:** Management is actively growing — adding 2-3 rigs, a 5th completion crew, and allocating $150M in additional capex for 2026. Most peers (Chevron, ConocoPhillips, ExxonMobil) explicitly stated they would remain "cautious" on growth after Q1 results. FANG's willingness to grow into elevated prices, combined with its cost advantage, creates a volume upside path that differentiates it from the capital discipline consensus.

**Barnett/Woodford Delineation:** The successful 2025 confirmation of reservoir continuity in the Barnett/Woodford zone across the Midland Basin adds meaningful incremental drilling locations to an already deep inventory. Management allocated 3-4% of the 2026 budget to advancing this zone — small enough to be low-risk but significant enough to signal confidence.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-quarter EPS estimates have risen 143.6% over 90 days (from $2.36 to $5.75). Full-year 2026 estimates have risen 96.2% (from $9.82 to $19.28). This magnitude of revision velocity — substantially exceeding the +30% threshold — is among the strongest forward-looking indicators of continued outperformance. The 30-day revisions (+29.6% current quarter) remain strongly positive, indicating the upward trajectory has not plateaued.

**Structural Tailwind:** The oil supply environment has moved decisively in FANG's favor. Strait of Hormuz–related disruptions and geopolitical tensions have tightened global supply, with WTI trading around $103/bbl as of mid-May 2026. In our assessment, the probability of a sustained blockade scenario is elevated, while diplomatic resolution probability remains low. FANG's Q1 2026 realized oil price was $73.47/bbl unhedged ($72.53 hedged), reflecting pre-disruption Q1 averages; forward-looking realizations at current spot prices would be materially higher.

## Valuation Assessment

**Forward P/E:** At $202.97 and $19.28 consensus 2026 EPS, FANG trades at 10.5x forward earnings. Peer median P/E is 13.2x (Devon at 13.0x, EOG at 13.2x). Applying the peer median to FANG's 2026 EPS yields a fair value of $254. The premium over peers is justified by FANG's lower cost structure, deeper inventory, and higher growth rate (actively adding rigs while peers hold flat).

**FCF Yield:** Based on our estimated 2026 adjusted FCF of approximately $8.3B (a scenario assumption reflecting sustained $100+ WTI, not company-issued guidance) and $57B market cap, the implied FCF yield is approximately 14.6%. Investors should note that Diamondback removed its minimum 50% quarterly return-of-capital commitment beginning Q2 2026. Management has indicated it will maintain the base dividend and may deploy excess cash flow toward buybacks, debt reduction, or cash-building depending on market conditions. The actual magnitude of 2026 shareholder returns will depend on management's capital allocation decisions and oil price outcomes.

**Analyst Consensus:** Median target $227, mean $230, range $195-$277. Current price of $203 implies 12% upside to median and 36% upside to the high target. Given the acceleration in estimate revisions (+96% on FY2026 over 90 days), consensus targets likely lag the fundamental improvement — analysts have not yet fully marked to the elevated FCF potential at sustained $100+ oil.

**Specific Mispricing:** The market is pricing FANG as though oil reverts to $75-80 within 2-3 quarters. The estimate revision trajectory (still accelerating) and the persistence of geopolitical supply disruptions suggest this assumption is too aggressive. At sustained $100+ oil, normalized FCF power could reach $8-9B, supporting a valuation of $240-260.

## Competitive Landscape

FANG occupies a differentiated position within the Permian Basin competitive hierarchy:

- **Cost Leadership:** $10.23/BOE total cash operating cost in FY2025 ($11.26/BOE in Q1 2026) positions FANG as one of the lowest-cost producers among large-scale Permian operators. This provides margin protection across commodity cycles — at $60+ WTI, FANG still generates meaningful positive cash flow while higher-cost producers face greater pressure.

- **Inventory Depth:** ~8,854 gross economic locations at $50 WTI provides over a decade of development runway. The Barnett/Woodford delineation success extends this further.

- **Scale Advantage Post-Consolidation:** With ~869,000 net acres and ~980 MBOE/d production, FANG is one of the two or three largest pure-play Permian operators. This scale enables operational efficiencies (shared infrastructure, optimized logistics, water recycling via Deep Blue) that smaller operators cannot replicate.

- **Growth Differentiation:** While ConocoPhillips, Chevron, and ExxonMobil signaled caution on growth after Q1, FANG committed to adding rigs. This positions the company to capture disproportionate value from elevated prices while competitors remain disciplined.

- **Competitive Position Trajectory:** Improving. The Endeavor and Double Eagle acquisitions consolidated Tier 1 acreage positions. The Barnett/Woodford success extends inventory life. Active growth at low cost strengthens market position.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Concentration (HIGH):** FANG's entire value proposition depends on oil prices. A diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict would likely send oil toward $85-95, reducing estimated 2026 FCF from ~$8.3B to approximately $5.0-5.5B. A more severe demand destruction scenario combined with conflict resolution could compress FCF further. The company's decision to add rigs at currently elevated pricing increases this exposure. Geopolitical probability estimates and oil-price scenarios referenced in this report represent the author's assumptions, not verified facts.

**Insider and Former-Owner Selling (MODERATE):** SGF FANG Holdings (Endeavor-related) sold 12.65M shares at $170.19/share (~$2.15B) in a March 2026 secondary offering, and Diamondback repurchased 3.0M shares from SGF for approximately $509M. Endeavor equityholders held approximately 30.2% of Diamondback common stock as of March 31, 2026 (down from ~35.8% at year-end 2025), and further monetization sales are expected. Separately, executive Form 4 filings from March 2026 (Van't Hof, Wesson, others) primarily reflect tax-withholding transactions on vesting stock awards (coded "F"), not discretionary open-market sales. At least one open-market executive sale occurred (Matt Zmigrosky, 20,899 shares on March 13 at ~$180). While the Endeavor overhang creates ongoing selling pressure, executive transactions are largely non-discretionary and should not be conflated with bearish signaling.

**Debt Level (MODERATE):** Pro forma gross debt of approximately $12.7B (as of end-April 2026) is elevated relative to history. At estimated $8.3B FCF in our high-price scenario, net debt/FCF would be below 2.0x, and the company is actively deleveraging with full flexibility following the removal of the formulaic return commitment. Investment-grade ratings provide refinancing flexibility.

**Geographic Concentration (LOW-MODERATE):** 100% Permian exposure means regulatory risk (water disposal restrictions, seismic regulations from Texas Railroad Commission) and infrastructure constraints (Texas grid reliability amid AI data center demand) are concentrated rather than diversified.

**Impairment Risk (NON-CASH):** The company recorded $3.7B in Q4 2025 and $1.4B in Q1 2026 in non-cash ceiling test impairments. These do not affect cash flow or covenant compliance but create GAAP earnings noise and reduce book equity. Management has stated it does not expect additional impairment in Q2 2026 given the recovery in oil prices. The impairments reflect full-cost-method accounting using backward-looking 12-month SEC prices, not operational or asset-quality deterioration.

## Conclusion

Diamondback Energy at $203 offers compelling risk/reward at approximately 7x estimated free cash flow (scenario-based, assuming sustained $100+ oil) with one of the lowest cost structures in its peer group, a decade of Tier 1 inventory, and estimate revisions accelerating at a pace (+96% FY2026 over 90 days) that strongly signals continued upward re-rating. The specific non-consensus view is that the oil supply disruption is more persistent than market pricing implies, meaning current estimated FCF power (~$8.3B) may represent a sustainable level rather than a cyclical peak.

The Endeavor overhang and removal of the formulaic return commitment deserve monitoring, but the fundamental picture remains strong: a company beating estimates materially, raising guidance, growing production, and trading at a discount to peers with higher cost structures and shallower inventories. The recommendation is BUY with a 6-9 month horizon targeting $235, with thesis invalidation tied to oil below $85 for an extended period or operational deterioration in production delivery.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The $8.3B 2026 FCF figure is an author scenario estimate (not company-issued guidance), and Diamondback has removed its minimum 50% return-of-capital commitment beginning Q2 2026 — both corrections reduce certainty around the shareholder-yield pillar of the BUY thesis.
