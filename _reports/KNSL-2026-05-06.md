## Executive Summary

Kinsale Capital Group has experienced a significant de-rating over the past year, declining from $512 (July 2025) to $307 — a 40% drawdown driven primarily by the deceleration of gross written premium growth from ~50% in 2023 to -0.5% in Q1 2026. The market is pricing this as a cyclical peak for Kinsale's growth story, and the growth rate has objectively weakened. However, the decline has overshot fundamentals in several measurable ways: the combined ratio remains elite (77.4% in Q1 2026), operating ROE is 24%+ annualized, operating cash flow hit $1.04B in FY2025, and EPS growth remains solidly positive (+37.7% operating EPS in Q1 2026) driven by favorable loss experience and compounding investment income.

The prior BUY recommendation issued April 21 at ~$359 has lost approximately 14.5%. Two material changes since the prior call: (1) Q1 2026 GWP came in at -0.5%, confirming the property headwind is more severe than expected (commercial property GWP -28.3%); (2) The stock has fallen an additional ~14% on essentially unchanged forward earnings estimates (consensus FY2026E $20.80, FY2027E $21.85 per sell-side aggregators). The first development is negative; the second is mechanically positive for risk/reward. Net assessment: the core thesis (structurally advantaged operator in a cyclically pressured market, being valued on near-term growth rather than through-cycle earnings power) has strengthened on price, modestly weakened on near-term growth trajectory.

At 14.8x FY2026E earnings (consensus) with 24%+ ROE and a 75-78% combined ratio, Kinsale offers asymmetric upside if the E&S cycle stabilizes or turns, while being protected on the downside by exceptional profitability metrics and aggressive capital return at higher prices.

## Company Overview

Kinsale Capital is a pure-play excess and surplus (E&S) lines insurer, founded in 2009 and IPO'd in 2016 at $16/share. The company exclusively underwrites hard-to-place, small-to-medium commercial risks through ~227 independent broker relationships. Key structural features:

- **Technology-first underwriting model:** A proprietary platform built from scratch enables 24-hour response times to brokers, automated workflows, and data-driven risk selection. ~140 IT employees support a workforce of only 720 total.
- **Expense ratio structurally below peers:** 20.8% in FY2025 vs. industry averages above 30%. This advantage compounds over time — lower expenses mean Kinsale can profitably underwrite business that competitors cannot.
- **Discipline over volume:** In 2025, the company processed ~988,000 submissions, quoted 72%, and bound only 7.6%. This selectivity produces combined ratios (75.9% FY2025) that are among the best in the P&C industry.
- **Small-account focus:** Average premium of $13,400 per policy means less competition from large-market E&S carriers.

The E&S market historically grows at 10% annually (vs. 4.7% for total P&C) and is currently in the early stages of a cyclical softening in property lines after a hard market that ran from 2019-2024.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has clearly decelerated, and this is the primary driver of the stock's decline:

| Period | GWP Growth | Driver |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| FY2023 | ~50% | Hard market pricing + new business |
| FY2024 | +19.2% | Growth moderating |
| FY2025 | +5.7% | Property softening begins |
| Q1 2026 | -0.5% | Property (-28.3%) offset by casualty (+6.0%) |

The growth deceleration is almost entirely attributable to the Commercial Property Division, which has shrunk from 26.3% of GWP in 2023 to approximately 13.6% of GWP in Q1 2026. Total property exposure (including Small Business Property, homeowners, and other property-related lines) was 24.0% of Q1 2026 GWP — down from 29.3% in FY2025 — so the company retains meaningful property exposure beyond the Commercial Property Division alone. Management is letting business walk away as rates become inadequate — standard carriers are re-entering territories they abandoned during the hard market. CRC Group reports 20-30% rate cuts on catastrophe-exposed wind accounts.

Excluding property, the rest of the book grew 6.0% in Q1 2026 and 13.3% in full-year 2025. Specific casualty lines growing well: Excess Casualty (+7.2%), General Casualty (+11.8%), Allied Health (+15.3%), Entertainment (+29.1%), Inland Marine (+26.1%).

Forward estimates (per sell-side consensus) reflect modest growth: FY2026E EPS of $20.80 and FY2027E of $21.85. The 90-day estimate revision is -4.8% on FY2027, reflecting analyst concerns about sustained competitive pressure. However, current-year estimates have revised up +2.1% over 30 days and +1.0% over 90 days.

The growth story for the next 12-18 months: (a) mid-single-digit casualty premium growth, (b) investment income compounding as the $5.2B portfolio rolls into higher yields (net investment income grew 28% in FY2025 and 27% in Q1 2026, though future growth rates will depend on reinvestment yields and portfolio growth), (c) buyback accretion from remaining $187.5M authorization (representing ~2.6% of shares at current prices if fully deployed), (d) optionality on E&S cycle turn that reignites property opportunity.

## Valuation Assessment

At $307 (as of report date) with FY2025 EPS of $21.65, KNSL trades at 14.2x trailing earnings. On FY2026E of $20.80 (consensus), that's 14.8x forward P/E. Against specialty P&C peer medians near 9.8x, this represents a ~50% premium.

**Why the premium is justified:**
1. Combined ratio of 75.9% vs. closest comparable RLI at 83.6% (FY2025) and broader specialty peers generally in the high-80s to mid-90s — Kinsale earns significantly more per dollar of premium than competitors
2. Operating ROE of 26.4% vs. peer ROEs typically 10-15% — more than 2x peer capital efficiency
3. Expense ratio advantage of 10+ points is structural (technology-driven, not cyclical)
4. Strong operating cash flow generation relative to market cap
5. Book value per share ~$85 growing at 20%+ annually — at current pace, book doubles in ~4 years

**Why the premium has compressed (from 25-30x historically to 14.2x now):**
1. GWP growth went from ~50% to flat — the market priced a growth premium and growth evaporated on the top line
2. P&C sector-wide cycle concern — capital flooding into reinsurance/E&S compressing rates
3. Short interest at 10.2% of float reflects active institutional bearishness

**Valuation framework:** At 14.8x forward on 24%+ ROE, the PEG ratio is approximately 1.0x (using normalized mid-cycle growth of 12-15% which Kinsale has delivered over its history). For a company with this level of profitability and structural competitive advantage, a 15x forward P/E represents a reasonable entry point for long-term holders. The stock would need to de-rate to ~11x forward (~$230) to approach fair value for a "normal" P&C insurer without Kinsale's structural advantages — that scenario requires the combined ratio to deteriorate significantly, which would be a genuine thesis-breaker.

Management deployed $62.5M in buybacks during Q1 2026 at an average price of ~$378.64. While buyback programs are discretionary, the willingness to deploy significant capital at prices 23% above current levels is a data point regarding management's view of intrinsic value.

## Competitive Landscape

Kinsale occupies a distinctive niche: small-account E&S with technology-enabled operating leverage. Direct competitors include RLI, Markel, W.R. Berkley, and portions of AIG and Chubb's specialty divisions. Key observations:

- RLI Corp (comparable pure-play specialty insurer) reported similar property headwinds (property premiums -9%) and posted an 83.6% combined ratio in FY2025 — approximately 8 points worse than Kinsale
- Kinsale's expense ratio advantage (20.8% vs. RLI's 38.6% and other peers above 30%) creates a permanent cost-of-capital advantage that allows profitable underwriting at rates competitors must reject
- The company's small-account focus ($13,400 average premium) provides natural protection from larger competitors who find these risks uneconomical to underwrite at scale without Kinsale's technology stack
- 1.5% market share of a $130B E&S market leaves substantial runway regardless of cycle conditions

The competitive position is stable to improving. The proprietary technology platform represents a durable advantage that competitors with legacy systems would find difficult to replicate, though this assessment reflects analytical judgment rather than a quantifiable certainty.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **E&S Cycle Softening (HIGH — partially priced):** The commercial property market is in clear softening mode, and casualty could follow within 12-24 months. If the combined ratio deteriorates from 76% to 85%+, the valuation premium evaporates. Estimated probability of combined ratio exceeding 82% in any of the next 4 quarters: approximately 15-20% (subjective scenario assumption based on historical variability and current competitive dynamics; recent combined ratios were 75.9% FY2025 and 77.4% Q1 2026).

2. **Construction Liability Reserves (MODERATE):** Recurring adverse development on 2017-2019 construction defect claims exposed to inflation. A 5% change in net IBNR reserves would impact net income by ~$90M (~18% of annual earnings). The 91.2% IBNR ratio is conservative, but social inflation in long-tail casualty lines remains a persistent industry headwind.

3. **Concentration Risk (MODERATE):** 49% of premiums from CA/FL/TX, 47.6% through top 3 brokers. A major catastrophe in these states or disruption at a key broker relationship could meaningfully impact results.

4. **Key Person Risk (MODERATE):** CEO Michael Kehoe (founder) has consolidated authority, now serving as Chairman, President and CEO following President Brian Haney's retirement in 2026. While the proxy discloses Board oversight of succession planning, the company's long-term CEO transition timeline remains unclear and could weigh on the multiple over time. Multiple management transitions in recent months (including CIO Diane Schnupp's retirement) reflect organizational change, though the company characterizes these as planned transitions rather than restructuring.

5. **Short Squeeze / Momentum Risk (TACTICAL):** 10.2% short interest with 8.4 days to cover creates potential for violent short squeezes on positive catalysts. The crowded short could trigger rallies but also reflects genuine institutional skepticism.

### Options Market Signal

## Conclusion

Kinsale Capital at $307 represents an exceptional franchise trading at a cyclically depressed multiple. The 40% drawdown from highs prices in a severe growth deceleration that has already occurred — the forward question is whether profitability deteriorates from here. With a 75-78% combined ratio, 24%+ ROE, $1B+ annual cash flow, and management actively deploying buyback capital at prices 23% above current levels, the risk/reward skews favorably. The elevated short interest (10.2%) creates both tactical risk and potential catalyst if stabilization data emerges. This remains a **BUY** for investors with 12-18 month time horizons who can tolerate near-term volatility associated with E&S cycle uncertainty.
