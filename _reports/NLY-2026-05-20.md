## Executive Summary

Annaly Capital Management is the largest mortgage REIT in the United States, managing a $132 billion investment portfolio across Agency MBS (62% of capital), residential credit (19%), and mortgage servicing rights (19%). The company delivered a 20.2% total economic return in 2025 driven by mortgage basis tightening and declining rate volatility, with book value per share recovering to $20.21 from $19.15. Q1 2026 EAD of $0.76/share beat consensus and covers the $0.70/quarter dividend at 108%.

The investment case centers on a ~12.3% dividend yield that appears sustainable in the near term, supported by favorable technicals (Trump-directed $200B GSE MBS purchases) and a diversified business model (MSR providing natural rate hedging). However, the current macro environment presents material headwinds: 30Y Treasury yields above 5.12%, two FOMC members endorsing rate hikes, confirmed foreign central bank selling of Treasuries, and PPI at 6% suggesting further inflation pressure. These conditions directly threaten Annaly's core business: a +25 bps MBS spread widening reduces NAV by 8.6%, and the company rolls $81.9 billion in repo financing with a weighted average maturity of only 35 days. The stock trades at approximately 1.12x book value, offering limited margin of safety against the rate/spread environment.

## Company Overview

Annaly Capital Management operates three integrated business segments designed to generate net interest income from leveraged positions in mortgage-related assets:

**Agency Group (62% of capital, 89% of portfolio):** Invests in government-guaranteed residential MBS. Pure spread/carry business with zero credit risk but significant duration and prepayment exposure. Economic leverage of 5.6x (conservative relative to 10x policy maximum).

**Residential Credit Group (19% of capital):** Non-agency whole loans and securitizations through Onslow Bay Financial, the largest non-bank Prime Jumbo/Expanded Credit MBS issuer. Priced 29 securitizations totaling $15.2 billion in 2025 (+38% YoY). Average borrower FICO: 761, LTV: 67%.

**MSR Group (19% of capital):** 6th largest non-bank Agency servicer, 2nd largest conventional MSR buyer in 2025. MSR values rise with rates, providing a natural hedge against the Agency portfolio. Weighted average note rate of 3.28% creates 250 bps refinancing buffer.

The company operates with 212 employees managing a $135.6 billion balance sheet — G&A/assets of 0.17% reflects exceptional operating leverage from scale.

## Growth Analysis

Annaly's growth prospects are constrained by the nature of the mREIT business model — "growth" comes primarily from portfolio expansion funded by equity issuance and leverage, not organic revenue compounding. The relevant growth vectors:

**Onslow Bay expansion:** Non-agency securitization volume grew 38% in 2025 to $15.2 billion. This business generates fee income and NIM that is less rate-sensitive than Agency carry. Q1 2026 saw management increase capital allocation to non-agency "citing compelling relative value." This is the most attractive growth vector — building a durable earnings stream beyond the Agency spread trade.

**MSR portfolio build-out:** 2nd largest MSR buyer in 2025. MSR provides natural hedge income and servicing cash flows. Growth here improves portfolio balance and reduces net rate sensitivity.

**Portfolio scale:** Total assets grew 31% in 2025, but this was funded by 23% share dilution and increased leverage. Per-share book value grew only 5.5%. The company's scale creates competitive advantages (counterparty access, operating efficiency), but size alone does not compound shareholder value.

**Structural constraints:** mREITs do not compound in the traditional sense. Since founding in 1997, Annaly has accumulated a $13.2 billion retained earnings deficit — cumulative dividends paid have significantly exceeded cumulative economic earnings. Total returns depend overwhelmingly on dividend collection minus book value erosion.

Growth prospects score reflects the Onslow Bay and MSR diversification thesis partially offset by the structural inability to compound book value and ongoing dilution.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current price:** ~$22.70
**Book value per share:** $20.21
**Price/Book:** ~1.12x
**Dividend yield:** ~12.3%
**EAD yield:** ~12.9% (on $2.92 FY2025 EAD)

**Valuation context:** NLY historically trades between 0.85x-1.15x book value. At 1.12x, it sits near the upper end of this range. The premium to book is justified by: (a) EAD coverage of the dividend, (b) Onslow Bay growth, (c) MSR hedging benefits, and (d) the $200B GSE purchase mandate supporting spreads. BofA has a Neutral rating with a $23 price target (lowered from $23.50 in April 2026).

**What the market may be mispricing:** The $200 billion GSE MBS purchase directive creates substantial near-term demand support for Agency MBS that is not fully reflected in NLY's modest premium to book. Additionally, the Section 199A permanent extension makes the 12%+ yield more valuable on an after-tax basis.

**What offsets the bull case:** At 1.12x book with 30Y Treasuries above 5%, the margin of safety is thin. If MBS spreads widen 25 bps, NAV falls 8.6% — that eliminates the premium and more. The stock's recent appreciation from the $19 area already reflects the favorable 2025 environment. Buying at 1.12x book in an environment where rate hikes are being discussed means accepting asymmetric downside.

**Peer comparison:** AGNC (closest peer) trades at similar P/B but with higher leverage and less diversification. NLY's MSR/credit business warrants a modest premium, and the premium currently embedded appears to roughly reflect this.

The valuation is neither compelling nor prohibitive. At 0.90-0.95x book ($18.20-$19.20), the risk/reward would be attractive. At 1.12x with 30Y above 5%, the dividend yield compensates for risk but offers limited upside beyond income.

## Competitive Landscape

Annaly's competitive position is genuinely strong within the mREIT universe:

**Scale advantages:** $135.6 billion balance sheet, 38 repo counterparties (North America, Europe, Japan), direct FICC access through Arcola Securities broker-dealer. No other mREIT has this breadth of funding relationships.

**Capital markets access:** ATM programs with 13 banks; raised $2.6 billion in 2025 at prices above book value. This accretive issuance capability is a competitive moat — smaller mREITs cannot raise equity as cheaply or frequently.

**Operating efficiency:** 0.17% G&A/assets with 212 employees. This is a fixed-cost business where scale directly benefits margins.

**Diversification:** The three-segment model (Agency + Credit + MSR) is unique among pure-play mREITs. AGNC is Agency-only. Two Harbors has MSR but less credit. The MSR hedge reduces NAV volatility relative to peers.

**GIC partnership:** Strategic relationship with Singapore's sovereign wealth fund provides capital and credibility.

**CEO pedigree:** Finkelstein's background running the Fed's MBS purchase program provides unique insight into central bank demand dynamics — relevant given the current GSE purchase mandate.

The company is the clear industry leader with durable structural advantages. Competition comes primarily from the broader fixed-income market (why own leveraged Agency MBS when you can own Treasuries at 5%?), not from smaller mREITs.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Rate/Spread Risk (HIGH):**
A +75 bps rate shock reduces NAV by 4.0%. With confirmed foreign central bank selling of Treasuries (Japan and China at 18-year lows) creating structural long-end pressure, the probability of 10Y breaking 5% within 60 days is estimated at 55-60%.

**2. Repo Rollover Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):**
In a market dislocation, margin calls could force fire sales at 5.6x leverage. The 3% unencumbered assets/total assets ratio provides limited buffer. The company's 38-counterparty diversification and FICC access mitigate but do not eliminate this risk.

**3. GSE Reform Tail Risk (LOW PROBABILITY / HIGH IMPACT):**
If Agency guarantees are eliminated or reduced, it undermines the business model. KBW believes privatization is unlikely before 2026 midterms. The $200B purchase directive is near-term bullish but creates political uncertainty about the GSE structure.

**4. Perpetual Dilution:**
23% share dilution in 2025. New $2.5B ATM program will fund further dilution in 2026-2027. While accretive above book, this creates persistent selling pressure and reduces per-share compounding.

**5. Prepayment Acceleration:**
CPR increased to 8.5% from 7.4%. If rates decline significantly (unlikely near-term), prepayment speeds could accelerate, compressing net interest margins.

**6. Macro Regime:**
PPI at 6%, CPI at 3.8%, and multi-channel inflation through H2 2026 create an environment where the Fed cannot cut rates. If rate hikes materialize, mREIT book values face substantial pressure. The Frankfurt CRE deal collapse demonstrates that 5%+ yields create real-world threshold effects for leveraged real estate strategies.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:**
- $200B GSE MBS purchase mandate creates sustained demand tailwind, compressing Agency MBS spreads and supporting book value
- Onslow Bay growth (38% YoY securitization volume) builds durable fee-based earnings
- MSR hedge provides portfolio balance in rising rate scenarios
- 12.3% yield at 108% coverage is attractive income with favorable tax treatment (Section 199A)
- If rate volatility declines and spreads tighten further, 15-20% total economic return is achievable again
- Book value could reach $22-23 by YE2026, supporting stock price of $24-26

**Bear Case:**
- 30Y above 5% + potential rate hikes → MBS spread widening → 8-10% NAV decline → stock trades at $17-18
- Repo market stress during dislocation forces fire sales at leveraged 5.6x
- Continued share dilution ($2.5B ATM program) limits per-share recovery even if macro improves
- GSE reform creates political uncertainty that widens Agency MBS spreads regardless of fundamentals
- Accumulated deficit of $13.2 billion reflects structural inability to compound book value — dividend stream is compensation for periodic capital destruction

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to 0.90-0.95x book value ($18.20-$19.20) while EAD coverage remains above 100%
2. 30Y Treasury declines below 4.75% with declining rate volatility
3. MBS spreads widen to 120+ bps (from current ~87 bps) without corresponding credit impairment — would indicate a buying opportunity for NLY's portfolio

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Book value declines below $18.50 (indicating spread widening/rate moves are impairing NAV materially)
2. EAD coverage falls below 90% of dividend — would signal unsustainability of the payout
3. Economic leverage exceeds 7.5x — would indicate management reaching for yield in a deteriorating environment
4. Confirmed rate hike by Fed — would accelerate both repo costs and book value erosion simultaneously

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (likely late July). The FOMC meeting in mid-June (Warsh's first) and any movement in 30Y yields toward or beyond 5.25% would warrant earlier reassessment.

## Conclusion

Annaly Capital Management is the best-positioned operator in the mREIT space with genuine competitive advantages in scale, diversification, and management expertise. The 12.3% dividend yield is well-covered and tax-advantaged. However, the current macro environment is directionally hostile for leveraged fixed-income portfolios. Trading at 1.12x book value provides insufficient margin of safety against a +25 bps spread scenario.

The appropriate posture is HOLD for existing positions collecting the dividend, with readiness to upgrade at lower prices (0.90-0.95x book) or if rate conditions improve materially. The $200B GSE purchase mandate is a genuine near-term positive, but it operates against the headwind of structural long-end selling by foreign central banks and potential Fed hawkishness. Income investors should maintain positions; new capital should wait for a more favorable entry point.
