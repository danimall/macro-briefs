**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Affirm reached its first full-year GAAP profit in FY2025 (+$52.2M, reversing a -$517.8M FY2024 loss) and accelerated that to $102.9M net income in Q3 FY2026 ($0.30 EPS against a ~$0.20 consensus). Revenue less transaction cost (RLTC) grew 41% to $498M while GMV grew 35%, meaning unit economics are improving rather than the company simply buying volume. Funding costs fell 126bps year-over-year to 5.8% as rate easing and ABS access repriced the liability stack. The Affirm Card is now a distinct growth engine, with card GMV up 146% and 4.4M active cardholders, shifting the business from a checkout button toward a recurring consumer-payments relationship.

The debate on this stock is valuation and risk, not solvency. A naive screen misreads Affirm badly: the Altman Z-Score reads as "distress" because the model is built for industrial firms, not a balance-sheet lender that deliberately funds receivables with debt and securitizations, and standardized revenue tags capture only the network-fee line (~$1.1B) rather than total revenue net (~$4.2B FY2026E). On corrected figures the company is profitable and growing fast. At ~21x the $3.10 current-year consensus EPS (the headline 67x trailing P/E reflects a barely-positive trailing base, not forward earnings), the multiple is reasonable for a 30%+ grower but already discounts continued execution. The muted-to-negative reaction to a clean Q3 beat-and-raise indicates the bar is high.

We rate AFRM a HOLD with a positive growth lean. The fundamental story is improving and the stock has recovered 34% from its February-March trough, but three things keep us off a BUY: near-term EPS estimate revisions are negative (current quarter -6.1%/30d, next quarter -10.7%/30d), the idiosyncratic risk set is concentrated and largely unpriced (Amazon ~22% of GMV, full credit retention untested through a downturn), and a softening consumer would land directly on Affirm's borrower base. We would own this through execution but not chase it at a level where the market already assumes the execution continues.

## Company Overview

Affirm operates a U.S.-centric point-of-sale "buy now, pay later" platform built around a three-sided network connecting consumers, merchants, and funding sources. Its differentiation versus pure pay-in-4 competitors is a hybrid product set spanning 0% APR merchant-subsidized installments and interest-bearing longer-term loans, individually underwritten in seconds by a proprietary risk model, with no late fees and no compounding/deferred interest. Loans are reported to credit bureaus, which positions Affirm as the more transparent, less predatory end of the BNPL spectrum.

Revenue comes from five streams: merchant network fees, interest income on owned loans, gain-on-sale on loans sold to investors, servicing income, and virtual-card/interchange including the Affirm Card. Substantially all loans are originated through partner banks Celtic Bank and Lead Bank and then purchased by Affirm; the Affirm Card is issued via Evolve Bank & Trust and Stride Bank, and the savings product runs through Cross River. Affirm is not itself the chartered originating bank for most volume, a structural feature that carries both cost and legal-challenge risk.

Scale as of Q3 FY2026 (ended 3/31/26): GMV $11.6B (+35% YoY), 26.8M active consumers (+22%), 6.7 transactions per active consumer (+20%), and 4.4M active Affirm cardholders. The company operates in the U.S., Canada, U.K., and is expanding into Western Europe and Australia. Distribution relationships with Amazon, Shopify, and Apple Pay are commercial partners, not owners, and underpin much of the volume.

## Financial Analysis

The progression — net loss of $985.3M (FY2023), $517.8M (FY2024), to net income of +$52.2M (FY2025) and +$102.9M in a single quarter (Q3 FY2026) — is the central fact. Q3 FY2026 produced $1.039B total revenue (+33%), operating income of $88M (8.5% margin), and adjusted operating income of $281M (27% margin). RLTC equal to 4.31% of GMV, growing faster than GMV itself, is the cleanest evidence that this is operating leverage rather than financial engineering.

The funding-cost decline was helped by ABS issuance and favorable repricing, with $28.2B funding capacity per management. The accumulated deficit of ~$3.1B is roughly flat year-over-year, consistent with a return to profit. The capital structure includes 0% convertible notes due 2026 (a near-term refinancing/dilution watch item) and 0.75% convertibles due 2029, alongside heavy reliance on warehouse facilities, securitizations, and forward-flow loan sales.

A critical caveat for interpreting this company: the standard distress screens do not apply. The Altman Z-Score reads below 1.8 because it penalizes the debt-funded receivables that are the entire point of a lending business model. It is not a bankruptcy signal here. Likewise, the network-revenue tag understates the company's total revenue net. Judged on the metrics that matter for a lender — RLTC margin, funding cost, credit performance, and capital-markets access — the financial health picture is solid and improving, though not fortress-grade given the inherent funding-market dependence.

The one degradation to flag sits in the estimate revision table. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 6.1% over 30 days and next-quarter consensus 10.7% over 30 days (and 12.9% over 90 days). Current-year ($3.10) and next-year ($3.78) consensus are roughly stable. The pattern suggests near-term margin or volume conservatism even as the longer arc holds, which should be weighted against the otherwise positive trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

Affirm has now posted ten consecutive quarters of 30%+ GMV growth, and FY2026 guidance (raised in Q3) calls for GMV of ~$49.3-49.6B and revenue of ~$4.175-4.205B, implying roughly 35% GMV growth for the year. Street models point to FY2027 revenue exceeding $5B. The four-quarter beat record is strong and consistent: +53.5%, +39.1%, +110.4%, +84.0% surprises, which lends credibility to the forward consensus even as near-term revisions soften.

The primary growth vector is the Affirm Card. Direct-to-consumer GMV up 48% to $3.7B converts Affirm from a transactional checkout integration into a recurring payments relationship with higher repeat usage. This is the mechanism that justifies a growth multiple rather than a legacy-BNPL multiple: repeat frequency drives the operating leverage now showing up in RLTC.

The growth durability question rests on whether 30%+ GMV growth persists as the law of large numbers and a moderating BNPL TAM (still ~20%+ CAGR but off pandemic peaks) bite, and whether the consumer base holds up. International expansion into Western Europe and Australia provides incremental runway but was explicitly delayed in H2 FY2025 when the company reallocated resources to fix platform-stability problems — a self-disclosed operational drag worth monitoring.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline 67x trailing P/E is an artifact of a barely-positive trailing earnings base and is not the right anchor. On the current-year consensus, AFRM trades around 21x forward earnings; on the next-year consensus, roughly 19x. For a company compounding GMV and revenue in the mid-30s with expanding unit economics, that is not stretched, with PEG well below 1.5. Forward metrics govern here, and on forward metrics the valuation is fair-to-reasonable rather than cheap or expensive.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $55.1, mean $83.15, median $85.0, high $100.0. With the stock at $71.01, the median implies ~20% upside and the mean ~17%. On a sub-30x forward grower with mostly stable current-year and next-year estimates, those targets carry more weight as a floor than they would on a premium-multiple name. BofA's $88 (Buy) explicitly framed the post-print dip as a buying opportunity while conditioning upside on durable GMV growth and unit economics.

What keeps the valuation score at a 5 rather than higher is the combination of two offsetting forces. The multiple is not demanding, but the market reaction to a clean beat-and-raise was muted to negative, which signals that execution is already priced. And the negative near-term estimate revisions sit against the inflection thesis — the strongest version of a "buy the dip on rising estimates" case requires rising estimates, and on the near-term horizon they are falling. The asymmetry is therefore roughly balanced: real upside if growth and credit hold, but no margin of safety if the consumer weakens or Amazon renegotiates.

## Competitive Landscape

Affirm is one of the scaled leaders in U.S. BNPL, competing against Klarna, Block/Afterpay, PayPal, and increasingly against incumbent card issuers (Synchrony, Chase, Citi, Capital One, Bread, Amex) and networks (Visa/Mastercard) rolling out installment features. Its moats are scale, marquee enterprise integrations (Amazon, Shopify, Apple Pay), credit-bureau transparency, conservative underwriting, and diversified revenue. The competitive position is improving on the product side: the Affirm Card and rising repeat usage widen the relationship beyond a single-transaction checkout button.

The structural disadvantage is funding cost. Bank competitors funded by low-cost insured deposits can cross-subsidize installment products in a way Affirm, dependent on capital markets, cannot. That gap is narrowing as rates ease but remains a permanent competitive variable that turns against Affirm in any rate-up or credit-stress regime. AI-driven underwriting and omnichannel presence are becoming table stakes rather than differentiators, which means Affirm must keep widening its data and integration advantages to hold share.

## Risk Assessment

The risk set is concentrated and largely not reflected in the valuation, which is why this dimension scores below 5.

**Customer concentration.** Top 5 commercial partners ≈ 47% of GMV; Amazon alone ≈ 22%. Most partner agreements are non-exclusive, 12-36 months, and terminable on 30-90 days' notice. Loss or unfavorable renegotiation of Amazon or Apple Pay would be highly material and is the single most important idiosyncratic risk.

**Credit and macro sensitivity.** Affirm now retains more loans on its balance sheet and bears full default risk on those, plus risk-sharing on sold loans. The model has not been tested through a full recession. A softening U.S. consumer — rising unemployment or delinquencies — would land directly on Affirm's borrower base. Management says credit is tracking expectations; that claim is most valuable precisely when it is least testable.

**Funding-market dependence.** The capital-efficient model lives on warehouse lines, securitizations, and forward-flow buyers, with covenants tied to default rates. Credit deterioration could reflexively curtail funding. The $28.2B capacity and strong recent ABS access are real mitigants but do not eliminate the reflexivity.

**Regulatory overhang.** Now under CFPB supervision, with FTC, state AG, and ECOA fair-lending scrutiny of black-box AI underwriting, plus Holder Rule and state rate/licensing exposure tied to the originating-bank structure. A legal challenge to the rent-a-bank model would be material.

**Governance.** Founder/CEO Max Levchin holds Class B super-voting shares (15 votes each) and controls outcomes — key-man risk plus minority disenfranchisement. The July 2025 Delaware-to-Nevada reincorporation added anti-takeover provisions and a Nevada exclusive-forum clause, reducing minority-holder leverage. A securities class action (Kusnier) and three derivative suits are outstanding.

**Near-term notes.** The 0% convertibles due 2026 are a refinancing/dilution watch item, and the H2 FY2025 platform-stability resource reallocation is an operational red flag to monitor for recurrence.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent event risk but not directional fear. Near-term ATM IV at 74.8% is rich versus 60.0% one-year historical vol (an 83rd-percentile reading within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (74.8% near-term vs 66.6% long-term), which signals the market is pricing a near-term catalyst. The skew is call-heavy (-13.1%) with a put/call OI ratio of 0.89 and put/call volume ratio of 0.79, so positioning is not bearish. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±4.4%. The market expects volatility but is not betting on a downside break, consistent with a stock where the debate is upside magnitude rather than solvency. The rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Affirm sustains 30%+ GMV growth, the Affirm Card keeps compounding into a recurring payments franchise, RLTC margin continues expanding as funding costs fall further with rate easing, and credit holds through a softer consumer. FY2027 revenue clears $5B, GAAP profitability scales, and the market re-rates toward the BofA/median target zone. Bull target ~$100 (the 52-week high and Street high), implying ~41% upside, requiring flawless execution on Amazon retention, credit, and card monetization.

**Bear case.** A weakening consumer materializes, delinquencies rise on the now-larger retained book, funding covenants tighten reflexively, and/or Amazon renegotiates terms. Near-term estimate cuts (already underway) accelerate, the growth multiple compresses, and the stock retraces toward its early-2026 lows. Bear target ~$48-50 (the February-March 2026 trading range), implying roughly 30-32% downside.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced from $71. The fundamental trajectory favors the bulls, but the concentrated, unpriced risk set and the negative near-term revisions cap the conviction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q4 FY2026 earnings (reporting in August 2026, fiscal year ends June 30), which will confirm or break the FY2026 guidance and provide initial FY2027 framing. The 0% convertibles due 2026 maturity is a secondary near-term event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Near-term EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current-quarter and next-quarter consensus stabilizing or rising over 30 days), reversing the current -6% to -11% trend.
- Q4 confirms credit metrics holding (delinquencies stable-to-improving) through a visibly softening consumer, demonstrating the retained book is performing as underwritten.
- A pullback toward the high-$50s/low-$60s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a meaningful margin of safety to the median ~$85 target.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Amazon or Apple Pay relationship renegotiation/loss, or any disclosure of unfavorable terms on a top-5 partner.
- A quarter where RLTC growth falls below GMV growth (unit economics rolling over) combined with rising delinquencies.
- Funding-market stress: ABS spreads widening materially, warehouse-covenant pressure, or curtailed securitization access.

## Conclusion

Affirm has earned the right to be valued as a profitable growth fintech rather than a perpetual cash-burner. The GAAP-profit inflection is real, the unit-economics improvement is genuine (RLTC outgrowing GMV), the Affirm Card gives the business a credible second act, and falling funding costs are a structural tailwind in the current rate environment. The forward multiple is reasonable for the growth on offer, and analyst targets sit comfortably above the current price.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of negative near-term estimate revisions, a valuation that the muted reaction to a clean beat-and-raise shows already prices strong execution, and a concentrated, largely unpriced risk set landing into a macro backdrop where the consumer is the most exposed variable. Amazon at ~22% of GMV and a full-default retained loan book untested through a recession are the risks that do not show up in trailing data and would move the stock hard if they break. We would own this through continued execution and upgrade on positive revision turn or credit confirmation; we would not chase it here, and we would become more constructive on weakness toward the low-$60s.
