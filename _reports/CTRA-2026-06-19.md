**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Coterra Energy is no longer an independent public company. On February 2, 2026 Coterra and Devon Energy (DVN) announced a definitive all-stock merger; the transaction closed on May 7, 2026. Under fixed terms with no collar, each Coterra share converted into 0.70 Devon shares, and former Coterra holders own roughly 46% of the combined entity, which now operates as Devon Energy on the NYSE under ticker DVN. The CTRA ticker stopped trading on May 7, 2026; the price history in the data set shows volume falling to zero on that date at a closing print of $32.56. Any "CTRA" investment decision is now mechanically a Devon decision, and the standalone $24.7B market cap, 0.30 beta, and standalone forward P/E of 10.8x describe a company that has been absorbed.

The analysis below treats the question honestly: a holder of CTRA today holds 0.70 DVN shares per former Coterra share, so the relevant frame is whether to hold that converted position. The combined company is one of the largest US independent shale producers, anchored in the Delaware Basin with the stated largest sub-$40 breakeven inventory there, and is run on a return-of-capital model (Devon's June 2026 outlook targets returning up to 70% of free cash flow via a $0.32/quarter fixed dividend plus an $8B buyback, alongside $1.25B of 2026 debt reduction). Standalone Coterra entered the merger financially sound: 2025 revenue rose 34% to $7.29B, net income rose 53% to $1.72B, free cash flow was $1.73B, and leverage was modest at debt/equity ~0.24x.

The conversion changed the risk profile of the position. Coterra's low-beta, gas-balanced, multi-basin diversification was swapped for a more oil-weighted Permian story with higher capex ($4.8–5.0B for FY26, >60% to the Permian) and conservative realization assumptions (gas guided to 40–50% of Henry Hub, oil floors as low as ~$47–56/bbl). Three overhangs cloud the near term: an activist (Toms Capital built a major Devon stake, disclosed June 2026), an explicit portfolio review that has produced an ~$8B offer from Stone Ridge for the legacy Marcellus gas franchise, and $1.0B of synergy targets that carry execution risk through year-end 2027. We rate the converted position HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the return-of-capital base is attractive, but the strategic picture is unsettled and the entity is meaningfully different from the one a CTRA holder originally underwrote.

## Company Overview

Coterra Energy was formed in October 2021 from the merger of Cabot Oil & Gas and Cimarex Energy. As a standalone company it operated a diversified, multi-basin US E&P portfolio: the Delaware Basin in west Texas and New Mexico (oil-weighted growth engine, sub-$40 breakevens on core inventory), the Marcellus Shale in northeast Pennsylvania (legacy Cabot dry-gas position, low-cost natural gas), and the Anadarko Basin in Oklahoma (legacy Cimarex oil/gas/NGL). That commodity mix and a 0.30 beta gave Coterra a cycle-resilient, gas-balanced profile relative to pure-play oil producers.

The corporate structure changed on May 7, 2026. The combined Devon/Coterra company has a combined enterprise value of roughly $58B, pro-forma production above 1.6 MMBoe/d (≈550k bopd of oil, 4.3 Bcf/d of gas at announcement), and is anchored in the Delaware Basin with secondary positions across the Anadarko, Eagle Ford, Rockies, and Marcellus. Devon holders control 54% of the combined entity and Clay Gaspar serves as President and CEO; legacy Coterra CEO Thomas Jorden's role in the go-forward company is diminished. Devon has stated it intends to "concentrate the portfolio around our premier Permian position," which signals the legacy Coterra gas-diversification thesis is being unwound.

2025 was a strong rebound year, with revenue and net income gains driven by higher volumes and a recovery from depressed 2024 gas prices. Free cash flow against $4.02B of operating cash flow reflects a capital-disciplined program. Leverage was conservative — long-term debt of $3.57B against $4.02B of operating cash flow and $14.8B of equity, typical of an investment-grade E&P.

The largest balance-sheet movement is the 2025 acquisition footprint: net PP&E jumped ~$4.2B (from $17.9B to $22.1B) while cash collapsed from $2.04B to $114M. This is consistent with Coterra's early-2025 Delaware Basin bolt-on acquisitions in New Mexico. The deal identities (reported as Franklin Mountain Energy and Avant Natural Resources) are inferred from the balance-sheet change rather than re-confirmed line-item by line-item, but the cash drawdown and PP&E surge confirm a large cash-funded acquisition closed just months before Coterra agreed to be acquired itself. The near-zero post-acquisition cash balance is the standalone watch item, mitigated by strong recurring free cash flow.

The earnings track record is a caution. Coterra missed consensus EPS in three of its last four reported quarters: Q4 2025 actual $0.39 vs $0.49 estimate (−20.6%), Q3 2025 $0.41 vs $0.45 (−9.0%), Q1 2025 $0.80 vs $0.81 (−1.4%), with only Q2 2025 beating ($0.48 vs $0.46, +4.1%). The misses temper confidence in near-term consensus, though commodity-price swings are the dominant driver for an E&P and these are largely revenue-line, not cost-line, misses.

Post-close, Devon plans to retire $1.25B of debt in 2026 and return up to 70% of free cash flow, so the combined balance sheet is being managed conservatively into a soft-price environment.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone estimate revisions show a positive shift: current-year consensus EPS rose from $2.11 ninety days ago to $3.00 (+41.8%), with the current quarter up +62.6% over 90 days to $0.66, and the next quarter up +42.5%. Revision velocity of this magnitude is normally a strong forward signal. Two qualifications matter here. First, these estimates describe an entity in transition — the conversion to Devon means the relevant forward earnings stream is now Devon's combined, more oil-levered book, not standalone Coterra's. Second, the underlying commodity backdrop is muted: Devon's FY26 guidance assumes natural gas realizing only 40–50% of Henry Hub and oil hedge floors as low as ~$47–56/bbl, and the IEA projects a 2027 oil surplus. Management's heavy hedging and conservative realization assumptions read as a defense against a soft-price environment rather than a bet on upside.

The combined company's growth posture has shifted from per-share volume growth toward capital efficiency and return of capital, consistent with the broader Permian consolidation logic that at sub-$60 oil, scale and free cash flow drive returns rather than production growth. Devon targets $1.0B of run-rate pre-tax synergies by year-end 2027 ($600M realized in 2027), which provides a self-help growth lever independent of commodity prices, but execution on a transformational combination is unproven. The growth case is real but capped by the realization assumptions and the unsettled portfolio.

## Valuation Assessment

On standalone metrics CTRA screened cheap: forward P/E of 10.8x and trailing 15.0x against an energy peer-median P/E of 21.9x. The relevant comparison set is mixed — gas-weighted peers EQT (9.7x) and Expand Energy (6.5x) trade in single digits, while royalty and services names (TPL 50.7x, Halliburton 21.9x) skew the median higher — so the headline "half the peer multiple" discount is partly a function of business model rather than pure mispricing. A low absolute multiple on an E&P at mid-cycle commodity prices is not by itself a buy signal; it more often reflects the market's discount for commodity-price risk and reinvestment needs.

The analyst targets ($28 low, $46 high, $37.65 mean, $38.50 median) are now stale relative to the corporate reality — they were set against a standalone CTRA frame and the security no longer trades. Post-close, Devon traded around $42 in June 2026 (and was down on the merger-close day), so on the 0.70 exchange ratio a former Coterra share is worth roughly $29 of Devon equity at that level. The valuation question now reduces to Devon's: a scaled Permian producer trading at a return-of-capital yield with a conservative commodity assumption set. The combination is reasonably valued for the cash it returns, but there is no identifiable catalyst that argues the converted position is materially mispriced to the upside; the cheap headline multiple is offset by the soft realization guidance and the strategic uncertainty. We score valuation moderate and decline to treat the cheapness as an asymmetric opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

The merger is part of a sweeping Permian consolidation wave (ExxonMobil–Pioneer, Diamondback–Endeavor, and others). The strategic logic is that at sub-$60 oil, scale and capital efficiency, not volume growth, are the path to investor returns. The combined Devon/Coterra is one of the largest independent US shale producers, anchored in the Delaware Basin plus Anadarko, Eagle Ford, Rockies, and Marcellus exposure. Coterra brought top-tier per-basin capital efficiency, validated by its compensation metric (PVI-10) and its TSR (187.8) tracking close to its peer group (185.3).

The competitive trajectory is two-sided. Scale and a deep low-breakeven Delaware inventory strengthen the combined company's structural position and cost of capital. But the explicit plan to concentrate around the Permian, combined with the ~$8B Stone Ridge offer for the Marcellus assets, means the legacy Cabot/Coterra gas diversification may be sold. That unwind concentrates the combined book in oil-weighted Permian exposure, raising beta and reducing the cycle-balancing benefit that Coterra's gas weighting historically provided. Whether that improves or weakens competitive position depends on one's commodity view; it changes the risk character of the position a former CTRA holder owns.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger conversion (realized).** The fixed exchange ratio carried no collar, so former Coterra holders bore full exposure to Devon's share price between announcement and close. The conversion is complete; the residual risk is the go-forward Devon equity, which is more oil-levered and higher-capex than the position a CTRA holder originally underwrote.

**Portfolio divestiture uncertainty.** Devon's stated intent to concentrate around the Permian, plus the reported Stone Ridge bid for the Marcellus assets, leaves the legacy gas franchise's status open. A sale could simplify the story and fund buybacks, but it also concentrates commodity exposure and removes a diversifying cash-flow stream.

**Activist overhang.** Toms Capital's disclosed Devon stake introduces governance and strategic uncertainty over capital allocation and portfolio decisions.

**Integration and synergy execution.** The synergy target is unproven on a combination of this scale.

**Commodity realizations.** The gas and oil-floor assumptions, set against an IEA-forecast 2027 surplus and in-basin gas weakness (WAHA basis ~−$1.85), cap upside and signal management bracing for softer prices.

**Standard E&P risks** persist: midstream/takeaway constraints, federal-lands and regulatory exposure, reserve-estimation uncertainty, and trade-policy cost impacts.

### Options Market Signal

Around the merger-close window, CTRA's ATM implied volatility spiked from 60.0% to 92.7% (up 32.7 points) as the price fell 8.6% to $32.56 on May 6–7, 2026. The put/call open-interest ratio was negligible (~0.02). This reflects the deal-close repricing and the conversion event rather than directional positioning on a going concern; with the ticker no longer trading, the options series carries no forward signal for the converted position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The combined Devon/Coterra is a scaled, low-cost Permian operator generating substantial free cash flow that funds the dividend, buyback, and debt reduction. If the synergy target is delivered, the Marcellus sale closes near ~$8B and is redeployed into buybacks and debt paydown, and oil holds in the mid-$70s, the converted position compounds through return of capital with per-share accretion. On the 0.70 ratio, a Devon recovery toward the high-$40s implies former-CTRA-equivalent value in the mid-$30s.

**Bear case.** Oil moves toward the IEA-projected 2027 surplus, gas realizations stay at 40–50% of Henry Hub, synergies underdeliver, and the activist forces a value-destructive strategic shift or the portfolio review unsettles the dividend/buyback cadence. Combined with the higher-capex, more oil-levered profile, this drives Devon back toward the high-$30s, implying former-CTRA-equivalent value near $26–28.

On balance the return-of-capital base provides downside support, but the strategic picture is genuinely unsettled and the conversion altered the position's character. There is no clean catalyst that makes the converted equity asymmetrically attractive today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (uncertain)** — applied to the converted Devon position, since CTRA no longer trades.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY on DVN):**
- The Marcellus divestiture closes at or above the reported ~$8B and proceeds are committed to buybacks/debt reduction, simplifying the story without diluting the FCF-return model.
- Devon delivers measurable progress toward the synergy target (e.g., ≥$600M run-rate confirmed in 2026 reporting) while maintaining the 70%-of-FCF return commitment.
- Oil stabilizes above the mid-$70s with gas realizations improving above the 40–50% Henry Hub guide, lifting the combined free-cash-flow base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID on DVN):**
- The activist pushes a value-destructive strategic shift, or the board cuts/suspends the fixed dividend or pauses the buyback to fund capex or acquisitions.
- Integration costs or capex overruns erode free cash flow below the level needed to sustain the 70% return target.
- A materially weaker commodity path (oil sustained below the ~$47–56/bbl hedge-floor range unhedged, or the 2027 surplus arriving early) compresses cash flow.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next Devon quarterly report and any 8-K on the Marcellus sale or activist agreement, whichever comes first. The portfolio review and synergy cadence over the next two to four quarters will determine whether this converts to a confident hold, an upgrade, or an exit.

## Conclusion

The defining fact for any CTRA position is that Coterra ceased to exist as an independent equity on May 7, 2026; holders now own 0.70 Devon shares per former Coterra share. The combined company is a credible, scaled Permian producer with a disciplined return-of-capital framework and a conservative balance-sheet posture, and standalone Coterra entered the deal financially sound with strong 2025 free cash flow and rising estimates. Against that, the conversion swapped a low-beta, gas-balanced book for a more oil-levered, higher-capex profile, and three unresolved overhangs — the activist stake, the open-ended portfolio review including a possible Marcellus sale, and unproven synergy delivery against a soft commodity outlook — leave the forward picture uncertain. We rate the converted position HOLD. The yield-and-buyback base limits downside, but there is no asymmetric upside catalyst that warrants adding, and the strategic questions need to resolve before conviction in either direction is justified. Hold the converted DVN shares and reassess at the next quarter and on any divestiture or activist news.
