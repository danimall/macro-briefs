**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RPM International is a diversified specialty-coatings and construction-products manufacturer that runs a decentralized portfolio of operating companies across three reorganized segments: Construction Products (CPG), Performance Coatings (PCG), and Consumer. The investment case rests on a self-help margin story (the now-concluded MAP 2025 program plus a freshly launched 2026 restructuring action), a serial-acquirer growth model, and a fortress balance sheet supporting a long dividend-increase record. Roughly half of revenue is tied to repair-and-maintenance demand, which dampens cyclicality relative to new-construction peers.

The current setup is a tug-of-war between two genuinely healthy segments and one weak one. CPG and PCG are compounding nicely: Q3 FY2026 CPG sales rose 10.5% with income before taxes nearly tripling to $22.9M, and PCG grew 8.4% with the highest segment profitability ($61.0M IBT). The Consumer segment posted reported growth of 7.9% entirely from acquisitions while organic volume declined 2.4% in the quarter and 3.4% over nine months, reflecting soft DIY/home-improvement spending amplified by RPM's heavy big-box retail concentration. Headline net income optics are also distorted: FY2025's ~17% net-income gain was substantially a tax artifact (effective rate of ~13% vs. ~25%), and the now-normalizing ~24% rate is a structural earnings headwind that makes any FY2025-anchored model overstate run-rate profitability.

We rate RPM a HOLD with an income-hold rationale. At ~$110 the stock trades around 20x trailing and forward earnings, which is fair, not cheap, for a business delivering low-single-digit organic growth. The balance sheet (net leverage 1.77x against a 3.75x covenant, $1.02B liquidity, ~$657M nine-month operating cash flow) is investment-grade and supports a 2% dividend that has grown for decades. We do not see an asymmetric entry here: the commercial-construction momentum is partly offset by Consumer drag, a $110.8M Oregon judgment that is materially under-reserved, ongoing executive turnover, and tariff-driven cost inflation flagged into FY2027. Our scoring has been gently improving (5.6 → 6.1 over four reports), driven mostly by financial-health and management-quality reassessments, but the underlying business remains a steady compounder rather than a re-rating candidate.

## Company Overview

RPM manufactures specialty coatings, sealants, adhesives, roofing systems, flooring, and building/construction products through a decentralized network of operating companies. A June 1, 2025 reorganization collapsed the prior four-segment structure (dissolving the Specialty Products Group) into three:

- **Construction Products Group (CPG):** Roofing systems, sealants, concrete admixtures, building-envelope and weatherproofing/restoration solutions, sold to contractors, distributors, and institutions. The largest IBT contributor; now includes the Legend Brands disaster-restoration unit and the recently acquired Kalzip metal-roof/facade business.
- **Performance Coatings Group (PCG):** High-performance flooring, corrosion-control and fireproofing coatings, infrastructure repair, and food/pharma coatings. The highest-margin segment, with meaningful international and emerging-market exposure (India, Middle East).
- **Consumer:** DIY and professional paints, caulks, adhesives, cleaners, abrasives, and wood stains sold to mass merchants and home centers.

The business is highly seasonal, with the fiscal third quarter (December–February) the weakest. RPM competes against far larger players (Sherwin-Williams, PPG, Akzo Nobel, Axalta, Sika, Carlisle, H.B. Fuller, Masco) but in fragmented niches where no single competitor spans all its lines. The Sullivan family has long controlled the company; Frank C. Sullivan is Chairman and CEO, and Russell L. Gordon is VP and CFO. Market capitalization is approximately $14.0B.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended 5/31/25).** Revenue was approximately $7.37B, essentially flat (+0.5%). Net income of $688.7M rose ~17% YoY, but the gain was overwhelmingly tax-driven: the effective rate fell to ~12.9% from ~25.2%, reflecting roughly $22.1M of foreign-tax-credit valuation-allowance reversals plus a ~$21.8M deferred-tax adjustment. Underlying operating gains came primarily from the MAP 2025 efficiency program, which concluded on 5/31/25. The headline growth materially overstates run-rate earnings power.

**Q3 FY2026 (ended 2/28/26).** Net sales were $1.608B, up 8.9% YoY (organic +3.0%, acquisitions +3.5%, FX +2.4%); nine-month sales reached $5.63B, up 6.4%. Q3 net income of $51.6M was roughly flat vs. $52.3M. Nine-month net income fell to $440.9M from $464.3M, but the decline is a tax optic: the prior-year period carried large one-time credits, pushing the nine-month effective rate up to 23.8% from 14.7%. Diluted nine-month EPS was $3.43 vs. $3.59.

Gross margin in Q3 expanded 110 bps to 39.5% on volume leverage, pricing, and MAP savings, though nine-month gross margin was flat at 41.0% as inflation, unfavorable mix, and plant-consolidation inefficiencies offset the gains. The segment divergence is the story: CPG IBT surged from $8.1M; PCG IBT led on profitability; Consumer grew reported but contracted organically, with growth coming entirely from acquisitions.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Total assets were $7.88B, total debt ~$2.56B against $3.15B of equity. The net leverage ratio sits well below the covenant ceiling, with available liquidity and $294M cash. Nine-month operating cash flow rose to $656.7M from $619M; capex was $159.6M, dividends $202.8M (quarterly payout raised to $0.54), and buybacks $52.5M with ~$139.8M authorization remaining. The revolving credit facility ($1.35B) was amended and extended to February 2031. Goodwill and intangibles of ~$2.4–2.5B carry impairment risk if reporting units deteriorate.

This is a financially healthy, investment-grade profile with high-quality cash conversion (nine-month OCF of $657M against $441M net income implies conversion well above 100%) and ample flexibility to fund M&A, dividends, and shock absorption. Financial Health is the standout dimension, scored 7.0, consistent with our recent upward reassessment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed and best understood at the segment level rather than the consolidated line. CPG and PCG are genuinely compounding: combined, they are delivering high-single-digit reported growth with healthy organic contribution and meaningful margin expansion in CPG. The drivers are durable — commercial and industrial roofing, wall systems, concrete admixtures, protective and fireproofing coatings, and emerging-market infrastructure demand in India and the Middle East. RPM appears to be gaining share in these fragmented commercial/industrial niches.

The Consumer segment is the drag, reflecting soft DIY spending against a backdrop of elevated mortgage rates discouraging renovation. RPM's heavy big-box concentration (Home Depot, Lowe's, Walmart, Ace collectively ~65% of Consumer sales, ~22% of total) amplifies the exposure. Management is masking the weakness with deals such as Star Brands, which is itself underperforming: the $12.7M earn-out reduction recognized in FY2026 signals the acquired business is tracking below the targets that set its purchase price.

Estimate revisions are modestly supportive. Current-year consensus EPS has risen to $5.48 (from $5.27 ninety days ago, +4.1%), and current-quarter and current-year figures have inched up over thirty days. But next-quarter (−4.5%) and next-year (−1.0%) ninety-day revisions are negative, so this is not the kind of revision velocity that flags an earnings inflection. The earnings track record is uneven: the most recent quarter beat by 60% off a low seasonal base, but the prior quarter missed by 16%. Combined with low-single-digit organic growth, an unfavorable Consumer mix, and a normalizing tax rate, we score Growth Prospects 5.3. The growth is real in two of three segments but consolidated organic momentum is modest, and growth_prospects is the dimension we weight most heavily; there is no acceleration here to justify a higher mark.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$110, RPM trades near 20x trailing and forward earnings (forward P/E ~20.4 on the reported EPS of $5.35; ~20.0x on current-year consensus of $5.48). That is a reasonable absolute multiple for a stable specialty-chemicals compounder, but it does not embed asymmetric upside.

The peer table is heterogeneous and of limited use as a literal benchmark: the listed Specialty Chemicals comps show distorted multiples (Solstice 69.3x, Element Solutions 69.9x, DuPont 147.7x, Westlake negative on cyclical losses). The peer median P/E of ~70x reflects depressed or transitioning earnings at several names rather than a clean read on what investors will pay for steady coatings earnings. The more relevant comparison is the larger, higher-quality coatings group (Sherwin-Williams, PPG, Sika), which typically commands a premium to RPM on superior scale and pricing power. RPM's ~20x sits below those premiums and above commodity-chemical names, which is appropriate for its niche-leader-but-subscale position.

We are deliberately cautious about treating RPM's multiple as "cheap." There is no identified catalyst to drive a re-rating, organic growth is low-single-digit, and the most recent earnings reaction (an ~8.6% post-print decline) reflected the Consumer drag and tax normalization rather than a transient misunderstanding. Cheapness without a catalyst is a value-trap risk, and the headline FY2025 net income overstates underlying earnings power because of the tax artifact. Analyst targets (mean $128.86, median $126.00, low $115.00, high $148.00) sit well above the current price, but the sentiment trend is degrading and these targets are anchored on a fully-priced steady-growth name rather than an accelerating inflection, so we do not treat them as floors. We score Valuation 5.0: fairly valued, no asymmetry in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

RPM occupies defensible niches in fragmented specialty-coatings and construction-products markets. Roughly half of revenue is repair-and-maintenance, which smooths cyclicality relative to pure new-construction names. CPG and PCG are demonstrating share gains in commercial, industrial, and infrastructure end-markets, supported by the Kalzip metal-roof/facade acquisition (closed 3/31/26, ~€75M CY2024 sales) extending the building-envelope strategy.

The competitive weakness is concentrated in Consumer/DIY, where the softness is largely a sector-wide phenomenon (weak home-improvement spending and rate-driven renovation deferral) rather than purely company-specific. RPM's big-box retail concentration amplifies it. Against giant peers, RPM is smaller and more acquisitive, relying on its serial-acquirer model and successive MAP efficiency programs to drive margin expansion rather than on scale economics. The trajectory is improving in two segments and deteriorating in one; the net read is a stable-to-modestly-improving competitive position, scored 5.8. Competitive position is a useful secondary discriminator but we weight it with some caution given its softer recent predictive strength.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Consumer organic decline.** The clearest concern. Reported growth is acquisition-driven while underlying DIY demand contracts. High retailer concentration (~22% of total sales through a handful of big-box accounts) means softness or shelf-space loss at any large customer is material.

2. **Under-reserved litigation.** A Consumer subsidiary lost a September 2024 Oregon jury verdict of $190M (trade-secret/contract/fraud), reduced by the court to $110.8M plus 9% prejudgment interest. RPM has accrued only the low end ($0.5M) of a $0.5M–$152.5M range and is appealing. An adverse appellate outcome could force a charge of up to ~$152.5M+, equal to roughly 22% of FY2025 net income. This binary contingency does not appear in headline financials.

3. **Tax normalization.** The rising effective rate is a structural earnings headwind that removes the FY2025 tailwind. Models anchored to FY2025 net income overstate run-rate profitability.

4. **Tariff and raw-material inflation.** Management repeatedly flags tariff-related cost inflation persisting into FY2027; many inputs are petroleum derivatives, minerals, and metals. RPM pre-bought safety stock to mitigate.

5. **Restructuring and execution.** MAP 2025 just ended and a "2026 Restructuring Action" (precursor to the next multi-year MAP) was immediately launched, with ~$29M total expected cost. The back-to-back cadence indicates demand and margins are being defended via self-help rather than top-line momentum.

6. **Management churn.** SG&A across FY2026 periods repeatedly cites "higher executive departure costs," and there is a cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks (June 2026, April 2026, October 2025, January 2025). With CEO succession a named risk factor and the Sullivan family central, this leadership-transition phase warrants monitoring.

7. **Standard exposures.** FX (28.9% of sales foreign), pension volatility, and goodwill-impairment risk on ~$2.4–2.5B of intangibles.

Several of these, particularly the under-reserved Oregon judgment and the tax normalization, are specific, partly off-balance-sheet, and not fully reflected in the headline multiple. We score Risk Factors 5.0, reflecting genuine binary and concentration risks balanced against a strong balance sheet that cushions downside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** RPM's commercial and protective-coatings momentum proves durable: CPG and PCG continue high-single-digit growth with margin expansion from the new restructuring program, the Kalzip and other bolt-ons accrete, Consumer DIY demand stabilizes as rates ease, and the Oregon appeal succeeds in materially reducing the judgment. Operating leverage plus continued buybacks below the current price drive mid-single-digit EPS growth, the multiple holds at ~20x, and the stock re-rates toward the analyst high near $148.

**Bear case.** Consumer organic decline accelerates, acquisitions continue masking shrinking underlying volume, the Oregon judgment is upheld at the full ~$152.5M+ on appeal, tariff inflation compresses margins into FY2027, and the management-transition uncertainty weighs on the multiple. EPS stalls, the multiple compresses toward 16x, and the stock revisits the 52-week low near $93.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an income-hold rationale. The thesis for owning is the ~2% growing dividend, high-quality cash conversion, and balance-sheet resilience, not near-term appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consumer segment organic growth returns to positive for two consecutive quarters, confirming the DIY trough has passed and removing the acquisition-mask concern.
- A favorable Oregon appellate ruling that caps the litigation liability near the accrued low end, removing a nine-figure binary overhang.
- Forward P/E compresses below ~16x (roughly $88) on stable or rising estimates, restoring asymmetric upside, or evidence of accelerating consolidated organic growth (>5%) with margin expansion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Consumer organic decline deepens beyond −5%, or a major big-box account is lost or materially reduces shelf space.
- The Oregon judgment is upheld at the full range and forces a charge approaching $150M+ without offsetting strength elsewhere.
- Net leverage rises above ~2.5x to fund M&A or litigation, or a dividend-coverage strain emerges (current coverage is comfortable).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q4/full-year FY2026 report (expected July 2026), which will reset the MAP-program savings run-rate and provide updated Consumer organic and tax-rate trajectories. The Oregon appeal outcome is an event-driven catalyst to monitor separately.

## Conclusion

RPM is a well-run, financially sound specialty-chemicals compounder with genuine momentum in commercial construction and protective coatings, a fortress balance sheet, and a multi-decade dividend-growth record. Those strengths are offset by persistent Consumer/DIY organic decline being papered over with acquisitions, a tax rate that has normalized away the FY2025 earnings tailwind, an under-reserved nine-figure litigation contingency, and ongoing executive turnover. At ~20x earnings with low-single-digit organic growth and no identifiable re-rating catalyst, the stock is fairly priced. The scoring trajectory has improved modestly over four reports, but the improvement reflects balance-sheet and management reassessments rather than a step-change in the growth or valuation case.

We maintain a **HOLD** (overall score 5.6) with an income-hold rationale. Own RPM for the quality of its cash flows and its growing dividend, with the recognition that the next leg of appreciation requires either a Consumer organic recovery, resolution of the litigation overhang, or a cheaper entry. Materials BUYs as a group have historically struggled to generate alpha against the market, which reinforces the bar for any constructive call here; RPM does not yet clear it.
