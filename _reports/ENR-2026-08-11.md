## Executive Summary

Energizer's reported financial trajectory over the past two years has been distorted by two government-driven items, and the Q3 FY2026 report (filed 8/4/2026) makes the underlying trend visible. FY2025 GAAP net earnings of $239.0M versus $38.1M in FY2024 looked like an operating turnaround; it was substantially the recognition of $112.4M of Section 45X Advanced Manufacturing Production Credits in a single quarter, $78.5M of which was a retroactive catch-up to January 2023. In FY2026, company-adjusted nine-month gross margin fell to 39.2% from 41.9%, and that 39.2% still contains 1.69pp of 45X credits ($36.5M) and 2.97pp of a one-time IEEPA tariff refund ($64.1M) recognized in cost of goods sold. Underlying gross margin is closer to 34.5%. Nine-month adjusted EPS fell to $1.99 from $2.47, a 19% decline, and management narrowed FY2026 guidance to the low end of $3.30–$3.60.

The operating picture beneath the accounting is a mature, price-competitive portfolio defending share. Nine-month organic sales fell 2.3% (volume −2.1%, price −0.2%), with North America down 2.9%. Q3's apparent shift to +2.7% organic decomposes into roughly +4.1pp of volume against −1.4pp of price; within Batteries & Lights, organic growth was only +0.3% because 2.5pp of volume was offset by 2.2pp of promotional investment. Advertising and promotion spend was simultaneously cut to 5.1% of sales from 5.5%. Auto Care grew sales 10.4% in Q3 while segment profit fell 14.1%, and nine-month Auto Care profit fell 26.8%, because the growth is concentrated in low-margin R-134a refrigerant distribution. Management's July 1, 2026 annual intangible test concluded that the $337.0M Rayovac trade name now has a 30-year definite life "based on decreases in forecasted demand, increased competition and other economic factors," a disclosed reduction in management's own long-term forecast for an asset equal to 22.5% of market capitalization.

The balance sheet converts modest operating disappointment into meaningful equity risk. Equity is 32% of enterprise value; book equity is $203.2M, or 4.5% of $4,466.0M of assets, against $2,015.2M of goodwill and intangibles. Derived net debt to FY2026E adjusted EBITDA is roughly 5.7x, up from ~5.4x, and ~6.5x excluding the non-cash IEEPA benefit, despite a $90.0M term-loan prepayment and near-zero buybacks; EBITDA is falling faster than debt. $2,117.2M of notes at a 4.17% weighted coupon mature in FY2028–FY2029 against a 6.00% most-recent new issue; refinancing at 6.25% would cost roughly $0.51 per diluted share, about 15% of guided EPS, and the $500M swap at 1.042% terminates December 22, 2027 with $100M annual step-downs.

## Company Overview

Energizer operates two reportable segments. **Batteries & Lights** was $1,682.6M of $2,156.3M nine-month net sales (78%), spanning household, specialty and hearing-aid batteries under Energizer, Eveready and Rayovac, plus a licensed Varta mark in Latin America and Asia Pacific. Portable lighting is small and shrinking: $54.2M versus $62.6M, −13.4%. **Auto Care** was $473.7M (22%), covering Armor All, STP, A/C Pro and related appearance, performance-chemical and DIY refrigerant lines.

The company employs roughly 6,050 people across 34 countries, with long-lived assets concentrated in the U.S. ($612.6M of $851.0M at 6/30/2026). The moat is trademark- and shelf-based (over 2,900 battery and 1,900 auto-care trademarks) rather than technological. In adjacent categories such as solar, power banks, generators and light bulbs, Energizer is the licensor rather than the manufacturer.

Customer concentration is lower than the category stereotype: Wal-Mart was the only customer above 10% of FY2025 sales, at 12.8%. That is also the co-defendant in the consolidated antitrust class actions discussed below.

The current capital structure originates in the 2018–19 acquisition of Spectrum Brands' battery and auto-care businesses, which produced ~$3.3B of debt and ~$2.0B of goodwill and intangibles against a 2015 spin-off from Edgewell that carried minimal leverage.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue quality.** Nine-month net sales rose 1.7% to $2,156.3M, composed entirely of the APS acquisition (+2.3%) and currency (+1.8%) against organic of −2.3% and highly inflationary markets of −0.1%. Geographic detail: United States sales −2.5% to $1,192.6M; North America market −2.9%; international Modern Markets +9.1% to $388.6M and Distributor Markets +28.8% to $176.6M (largely APS). Global Professional (OEM, industrial, medical, hearing aid) fell 2.1% to $205.0M.

**Margin composition.** Reported nine-month gross margin fell to 36.9% from 43.8%; company-adjusted, to 39.2% from 41.9%. The adjusted figure includes $36.5M of 45X credits (1.69pp) and $64.1M of IEEPA refund (2.97pp) in COGS. Management's own gross-margin bridge shows net tariff impact at +0.4pp for nine months, implying roughly $55M of FY2026-incurred IEEPA duties against the $64.1M refund; the split of the refund by period of original assessment is not disclosed, so the size of any out-of-period element is an estimate. Adjusted SG&A deleveraged to 17.7% of sales from 17.3% despite ~$14M of Project Momentum savings, on APS-related costs (+$9.8M), digital investment, and higher legal and recycling fees.

**Earnings.** Nine-month adjusted EPS $1.99 versus $2.47. Q3 adjusted EPS $0.75 versus non-GAAP consensus of $0.83–$0.86 (a 9–13% miss; the two consensus figures conflict, so treat magnitude as approximate). Full-year adjusted EPS has been essentially flat for five years: $3.48 (FY21), $3.08, $3.09, $3.32, $3.52 (FY25).

The FY2026 guide arithmetic is demanding. Low end $3.30 less nine-month actual $1.99 requires $1.31 in Q4, against $1.05 in Q4 FY2025, a 25% year-over-year increase, consistent with management guiding Q4 gross margin "north of 40%" versus 39.2% for nine months. If Q4 merely matched last year at $1.05, full-year adjusted EPS would be $3.04, roughly 8% below the low end. That is a bracket on risk rather than a forecast; Q4 seasonality, 45X recognition timing and Momentum phasing are not reconciled. The guidance range and "low end" language are sourced to secondary reporting of the 8/4/2026 release, not to a filing I can quote.

**Cash flow is the genuinely improved line.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $156.0M versus $85.6M; capex $52.1M versus $69.1M; free cash flow $103.9M versus $16.5M. The cash-flow statement removes the tariff benefit (−$67.1M add-back for the refund receivable), so this is clean of the $64.1M non-cash item. Two recognized-but-uncollected inflows remain: roughly $53M of the IEEPA refund (timing "still unclear") and $83.8M of production-credit receivables, which historically convert with a lag ($50.7M received shortly after FY2025 year-end).

The composition matters. Finished goods fell $69.1M ($429.9M → $360.8M) while WIP rose $27.2M and raw materials $8.5M; the inventory balance fell $33.4M in total. Most of the working-capital benefit is finished-goods destocking that does not repeat. Full-year capex is guided to $60–70M including $25–35M for Momentum, implying a Q4 step-up. A reasonable FY2026 FCF estimate is $140–155M (9–10% of market cap), of which ~$82M funds the dividend, leaving roughly $60–70M for debt reduction, about 2% of net debt per year. At that pace, leverage falls only if EBITDA grows, and EBITDA is currently declining.

**Leverage (derived; not a covenant calculation).** Total debt face $3,357.8M less cash $173.4M equals net debt $3,184.4M. Using segment profit less general corporate plus segment D&A, nine-month adjusted EBITDA was $384.4M versus $429.7M, −10.5%. Two independent full-year builds (bottom-up from Q4 FY2025 comparables, and top-down from the $3.30 guide grossed up for tax, interest and D&A) both land at roughly $550–565M. Against $3,184.4M net debt, that is ~5.7x, versus ~5.4x at FY2025 year-end; excluding the $64.1M non-cash IEEPA benefit, ~6.5x. Interest coverage is roughly 3.5x on this proxy. The credit-agreement definition almost certainly permits add-backs my proxy excludes (restructuring, run-rate savings, possibly the pension settlement), so the company's reported leverage is likely lower; the 10-Q confirms covenant compliance at 6/30/2026 and expected compliance for twelve months, but does not state thresholds. Headroom is unknown.

**Refinancing.** The maturity table shows $8.6M in year one, $592.3M in year two (4.750% Notes due 2028), $1,542.1M in year three (4.375% Notes 2029 and 3.50% Euro Notes 2029), and $1,118.4M thereafter. The three note tranches total $2,117.2M at a 4.17% weighted coupon. Refinancing at 6.25% adds $44.0M pre-tax, $35.1M after tax at the 20.3% adjusted rate, or $0.51 per diluted share on 69.2M shares, 15% of the $3.30 guide. Current interest expense annualizes near $158M. AOCI reclassifications into interest expense have already fallen to $11.5M from $16.9M as the swap notional steps down.

**Data note.** The supplied profile shows a trailing and forward P/E of 18.97 alongside EPS of $3.32. Those are inconsistent at $21.90 ($21.90 / $3.32 = 6.6x). The $3.32 EPS figure matches FY2024 adjusted EPS and is stale, and the 18.97 P/E is a vendor field that cannot be reproduced from either the $3.32 EPS or GAAP TTM earnings of $81.5M ($21.90 / ($81.5M / 68.5M) = 18.4x). I use explicit earnings bases below rather than the composite.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth engine in evidence. Nine-month organic sales fell 2.3% on volume of −2.1%. Management indicated on the 8/4/2026 call that the battery category is softening by 200–300 basis points (externally reported; not verified in a filing). Q3's volume recovery cost 2.2pp of price in Batteries & Lights, so the incremental revenue arrived at reduced margin, and it arrived while brand spend was cut 40bp. Substituting promotional price for advertising in a business whose only durable asset is brand equity improves the current quarter's volume line and erodes the pricing architecture that supports it.

Auto Care's growth is the clearest example of low-quality mix: Q3 sales +10.4% with segment profit −14.1%, and nine-month segment profit −26.8% ($79.8M → $58.4M) on +2.0% sales, attributed to "the lower margin profile of the refrigerant business." R-134a faces a long-dated regulatory phase-down (EU new-vehicle phase-out 2017, Canada from 2021, U.S. AIM Act 2020, state-level restrictions often bundled with PFAS limits). The counterargument is real: DIY aftermarket recharge serves the existing car parc, which ages for years after new-vehicle rules change, so the practical runway likely exceeds what the dates imply. But the segment is growing where it earns least.

The APS acquisition is currently dilutive to profit (a $5.1M segment loss in Q3 and $1.9M for nine months), and the expiry of the acquired brand license on 12/31/2025 removed $17.2M of Q3 sales (−3.2% in Batteries & Lights).

Consensus is moving the wrong way. Current-year EPS estimates fell 5.9% and next-year 7.4% over 90 days, with the current quarter down 11.7% to $1.30, the entire revision occurring in the last 30 days, i.e., on the Q3 print. Next-year consensus of $3.38 sits only 2.4% above the FY2026 low end and does not obviously incorporate the Rayovac amortization now beginning: $337.0M over 30 years is $11.2M per year, roughly $9.0M after tax, about $0.13 per diluted share. Because intangible amortization is not among the items excluded from Energizer's adjusted EPS reconciliation, this is a direct headline headwind from FY2027.

The one structural positive is Project Momentum's fourth year plus U.S. manufacturing efficiency work, targeting $20–25M of savings and $10–15M of tariff mitigation. The incremental economics are poor: $80–95M of P&L cost plus $25–35M of capex for that benefit, versus roughly dollar-for-dollar over the first three years ($215.8M cost, ~$206M of savings). Cumulative Momentum cost of ~$292.6M equals 19.5% of market capitalization, all excluded from adjusted EPS. Some of the FY2026 increase is non-cash machinery write-off following the decision to outsource certain battery raw materials, which sits in tension with expanding qualifying 45X production.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.9027, ENR trades at 6.64x the $3.30 FY2026 guide low end and 6.48x next-year consensus of $3.38, roughly a quarter of the 23.5x household-products peer median. The dividend yield is 5.48% versus a 1.8% peer median. On GAAP TTM earnings of $81.5M, the multiple is 18.4x.

The equity multiple is cheap because the enterprise is levered, not because the enterprise is cheap. EV/EBITDA is $4,684M / ~$557M = **8.4x**, and 9.5x on the ~$493M that excludes the non-cash IEEPA benefit. For a business with flat five-year adjusted EPS, −2.3% organic sales, declining segment profit in its growth segment, and a disclosed reduction in the long-term forecast for a $337M brand, 8.4–9.5x EV/EBITDA is not a discount. Peer comparison at the equity level is uninformative here: Edgewell prints a negative P/E, and WD-40 at 35.7x and Inter Parfums at 23.5x are structurally different businesses with negligible leverage.

Composition of the earnings being capitalized matters more than the multiple. Of ~$228M of FY2026E adjusted net earnings, $55–65M (24–28%) is refundable Section 45X credit, available through calendar 2032 with phase-out beginning in calendar 2030. The nine-month result additionally contains a $64.1M one-time tariff refund that flows through adjusted earnings. A multiple applied to that earnings stream is a multiple on a statute as much as on a franchise.

Because equity is 32% of EV, small EBITDA changes swing the share price hard. Holding today's 8.4x EV/EBITDA constant, a 9% EBITDA decline to ~$505M implies equity of roughly $1,058M, or $15.45 per share. That is the mechanical reason to treat the bear case as severe rather than the price as safe.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names the pressure directly: competition on "brand perceptions, product performance, price, retail execution and customer service," intensifying "as consumers shift consumption between channels such as e-commerce, discounters and private label brands." The portfolio spans price (Rayovac), premium (Energizer) and performance tiers. The Rayovac life reassessment is the first hard filing-level disclosure that the value tier specifically is losing ground, and it is consistent with nine-month pricing of −0.2% and 2.2pp of Q3 promotional investment in Batteries & Lights. The reading that fits both facts is store brands taking the price segment while Energizer defends premium with promotion.

Positive elements exist. Walmart at 12.8% of FY2025 sales is modest concentration for the category. International Modern and Distributor Markets are growing at 9.1% and 28.8%. The U.S. manufacturing footprint has become a tax-advantaged asset through 45X, which is unusual among staples peers, and the company is investing to expand qualifying production. Commodity exposure, however, is essentially unhedged relative to scale: 15 zinc contracts at ~$24M notional and 34 FX contracts at ~$87M against a ~$1.8B annual COGS base, extending only into FY2027. Cost recovery therefore depends on pricing, which the current promotional environment constrains.

On trajectory, share is being held with price rather than with product or brand investment, and the value brand's forecast has been formally reduced. Competitive position score: 4.0, consistent with the decline recorded in the prior review.

## Risk Assessment

**Section 45X dependence.** A risk factor newly added in the FY2025 10-K (referencing the July 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act) and repeated in Q3 FY2026: the benefit "is dependent upon the federal government's ongoing implementation, guidance, regulations, or rulemakings," and amounts recognized "are based on Management's judgment and best estimate." Expected future credits of $55–65M annually equal 24–28% of adjusted profit. Two distinct exposures: adverse regulatory or IRS treatment of primary alkaline cells and components (the company only reached "reasonable assurance" after December 2024 final regulations), and the scheduled 2030 phase-down.

**Tariff regime instability.** The IEEPA risk factor was added in Q3 FY2026. The Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs on 2/20/2026; CBP opened a refund portal 4/20/2026; ENR recognized ~$64M and has received ~$11.0M with "no timeline yet established." Meanwhile a new 10% global tariff under Section 122 and 10%/12.5% Section 301 tariffs on sixty-plus partners reconstitute the forward cost under different authority. The refund is one-time; the cost is being rebuilt.

**Thin equity versus intangibles.** Goodwill plus intangibles of $2,015.2M against book equity of $203.2M means an impairment of roughly 10% of the intangible base would eliminate essentially all book equity. FY2024 already carried a $110.6M impairment, and management has just disclosed a reduced Rayovac forecast. Tangible book equity is approximately −$1,812M. A screening Altman Z near 1.0 reflects this post-acquisition capital structure rather than an operating collapse, but it correctly flags the absence of balance-sheet cushion.

**Antitrust litigation.** Three 2023 class actions in N.D. California, consolidated, against Energizer and Wal-Mart, alleging a conspiracy to inflate prices charged to other retailers and to prevent below-Walmart consumer pricing. Three putative classes; damages, fees and injunctive relief sought. **No accrual** — loss neither probable nor estimable. Sherman Act damages are trebled, and injunctive relief could constrain pricing to a customer representing 12.8% of sales. Both SG&A and corporate expense cite increased legal fees.

**Refinancing into a higher-rate regime.** Covered above: $0.51 per share of potential interest headwind, arriving as the swap benefit amortizes to zero. This is the central medium-term question.

**Cash location.** Roughly 93% of the $173.4M cash sits outside the U.S. against predominantly USD debt service; the 10-Q acknowledges repatriation "could have adverse tax consequences."

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 61.5% against 46.2% one-year realized (rich by 15.3 points), sits in the 96th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (61.5% at 9 days versus 43.7% at 128 days). The August 21 tenor prices a ±11.1% move. The unusual feature is direction: put/call skew is −31.3% (call-heavy), put/call volume ratio 0.05 and open-interest ratio 0.16, the most call-tilted structure I would expect on a name with 10.2% short interest and 6.7 days to cover. Earnings are already behind (8/4/2026), so the near-dated positioning is not an earnings hedge. Two readings are consistent with the data: speculation on a strategic or governance development following Aqua Capital's accumulation to 11.7%, or thin-chain noise (28 contracts across 4 expirations makes the skew reading low-confidence). ATM IV also collapsed from an implausible 838.1% at the prior snapshot to 61.5%, so the level series should be treated cautiously.

The practical implication for a REDUCE: exit exposure in an orderly way rather than aggressively, because a 10.2% short base plus call-tilted near-dated positioning creates real squeeze risk on any strategic headline. This is a reason to trim into strength, not a reason to change the fundamental call.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($28.00, +27.8%).** The Q3 volume recovery is genuine category recovery rather than purchased share, promotional intensity normalizes as competitors pull back, and Q4 gross margin lands "north of 40%," validating the $3.30 guide. Momentum's fourth year delivers $20–25M of savings plus $10–15M of tariff mitigation, the ~$53M IEEPA receivable and $83.8M of credit receivables convert to cash, and free cash flow above $150M funds both the $82M dividend and accelerating debt paydown. 45X qualification stands and the company expands qualifying U.S. production, holding $55–65M annually through the pre-phase-down period. Rates fall enough that the FY2028–29 refinancing costs less than the 6.25% assumption. Aqua Capital's 11.7% stake, accumulated almost daily between $16.18 and $21.54, becomes an active catalyst for capital-allocation change or strategic action. On ~$570M EBITDA at 8.8x, the equity is worth $28, with the 5.5% dividend on top.

**Bear case ($15.00, −31.5%).** The 200–300bp category softening persists, promotional investment stays in the P&L, and the pricing architecture erodes further after two years of reduced brand spend. Q4 delivers something closer to last year's $1.05, taking FY2026 adjusted EPS to roughly $3.04 and confirming that consensus at $3.38 for FY2027 is too high before the $0.13 Rayovac amortization. EBITDA falls toward $505M, taking derived leverage above 6.3x; a further intangible reassessment converts to impairment and eliminates most of the $203.2M book equity. The IEEPA refund stalls in collection while Section 122 and 301 tariffs raise forward cost. Refinancing prices above 6.25% and the swap expiry removes the offset. At 8.4x on $505M, the equity is $15.00, near the $15.75 52-week low, and dividend sustainability comes into question given GAAP TTM earnings of $81.5M against ~$85M of dividends.

**Base case ($22.50, +2.7%).** Volumes stabilize at roughly flat, promotion remains a permanent feature of the category, and adjusted EPS holds near $3.10–$3.30 in FY2027 as Momentum savings offset the Rayovac amortization and modest price mix erosion. 45X holds. Leverage drifts sideways near 5.5x as ~$60–70M of annual post-dividend free cash flow is applied to debt. Fair value near $22.50, essentially spot, with the 5.5% dividend the dominant component of return.

**Trajectory assessment.** Prior reviews scored 4.4 → 4.2 → 4.8 with AVOID → AVOID → HOLD. Since the June review, the price is roughly unchanged ($21.52 → $21.90) while several fundamental items moved against the thesis: a 10.2% Q3 EPS miss, guidance narrowed to the low end, consensus cut 5.9%/7.4%, Auto Care nine-month segment profit −26.8%, the Rayovac forecast reduction with a new adjusted-EPS amortization headwind, and derived leverage up roughly half a turn. That is specific deterioration on an unchanged price, which worsens risk/reward. I also part company with the prior management-quality upgrade to 6.0: the strongest insider signal in the file is a third-party 11.7% holder, not management. No named executive officer made an open-market purchase in the reviewed window; five-year TSR indexed to 76.05 against a 117.42 peer index while CEO total compensation rose 84% from FY2021; and the company repurchased roughly 14x more stock at an average $22.40 than at $18.26. Offsetting positives — the $90.0M term-loan prepayment, halting buybacks to preserve cash, the disciplined $16.7M APS deal, the U.K. pension buy-out removing a legacy obligation, and specific voluntary disclosure of the Rayovac reassessment — support 4.5 rather than 6.0.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Action: REDUCE.** Trim exposure over the next one to two months, using strength toward the $23–24 area (the post-print rally high was $23.09 on 8/5/2026) rather than selling into weakness. Given 10.2% short interest, 6.7 days to cover and call-tilted near-dated options, avoid concentrated selling around potential strategic headlines. A residual position sized for income is defensible for holders who require the 5.48% yield and can tolerate a −31% bear case; new capital should wait.

**Targets and math from $21.9027:** base $22.50 (+2.7%), bull $28.00 (+27.8%), bear $15.00 (−31.5%). Including the $1.20 annual dividend, probability-weighted 12-month total return at 45/20/35 is approximately −0.4%.

**Timeframe:** twelve months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q4 FY2026 report (early November 2026, which resolves the $1.31 implied quarter and the "north of 40%" gross-margin guide) and the FY2027 guidance issued with it.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD:**
1. Q4 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $1.25 or better with gross margin at or above 40%, delivered without an increase in the 45X or tariff contribution — i.e., underlying gross margin above 35%.
2. Batteries & Lights organic growth positive for two consecutive quarters with promotional price drag of less than 1.0pp, indicating volume held without buying it.
3. Price in the $17–18 area on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the base case roughly 25% above spot and restore margin of safety.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE (all three required):**
1. A completed or announced refinancing of the FY2028 4.750% Notes at a coupon of 5.5% or below, removing the $0.51/share sensitivity.
2. Company-reported net leverage declining for two consecutive quarters on a consistent definition.
3. Consensus FY2027 EPS stabilizing or rising, plus disclosure of underlying gross margin excluding 45X and tariff items.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY2026 adjusted EPS below $3.10, or FY2027 guidance below $3.00, which would put derived leverage above 6.3x on my proxy.
2. Any adverse 45X development — legislative change, IRS position, or a reduction in the disclosed $55–65M annual expectation.
3. An intangible impairment above $200M, a dividend reduction, or a covenant amendment.
4. Class certification in the consolidated N.D. California antitrust matter, or the first accrual recorded against it.

**Re-entry criteria if the position is fully exited:** price near $16 with underlying gross margin above 35% and organic sales positive, or a refinancing completed at a coupon that removes the interest overhang.

## Conclusion

Energizer is a cash-generative but structurally slow-growing branded manufacturer whose reported earnings have been meaningfully supported by a production tax credit and a one-time tariff refund, and whose equity is a 32% sliver of a $4.68B enterprise carrying roughly 5.7x net leverage. The nine-month record — organic sales −2.3%, adjusted EPS −19%, Auto Care segment profit −26.8%, underlying gross margin near 34.5% — is not consistent with the 6.6x forward multiple being a bargain. It is consistent with a levered equity priced at 8.4x EV/EBITDA for an asset with declining EBITDA, a formally reduced brand forecast, a $2.1B refinancing wall at a 4.17% coupon maturing into a 6%+ market with the hedge expiring first, and roughly a quarter of adjusted profit dependent on a statute that phases down from 2030.
