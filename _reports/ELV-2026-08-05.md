## Executive Summary

Elevance is a margin-repair story priced at roughly 14.4x its own FY2026 adjusted earnings floor. The company raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to at least $27.00 on July 15 and framed 2027 as at least 12% growth off that base, and the stock fell about 9% that day. The market's reaction was arithmetically defensible: first-half adjusted EPS of $20.06 represents 74.3% of the full-year floor, implying second-half adjusted EPS of $6.94 or better against $9.19 in the prior-year second half, a 24.5% decline. Part of that gap is deliberate — management indicated roughly $0.80 per share of one-time second-quarter investment gains will be reinvested in technology and capabilities — and part is continued cost trend in the government books.

The underlying business is being deliberately shrunk. Every risk-based membership category declined year over year in the second quarter: Medicare Advantage −15.9%, Employer Group Risk-Based −5.5%, Federal Employee Program −5.4%, Medicaid −4.3%, Individual ACA −2.3%. Only fee-based administrative services grew, at +1.7%. Premiums were exactly flat at $41,279M versus $41,271M. Health Benefits operating margin has compressed from 4.6% in FY2023 to 2.5% in FY2025 to 2.1% in the second quarter of 2026, with Medicaid guided to a negative 1.75% full-year operating margin — roughly a $1.0B operating loss on ~$57B of annualized revenue. Each 100 basis points of Health Benefits margin is worth about $5.90 per share of adjusted EPS, so the leverage is extreme in both directions.

We move to HOLD from the prior BUY. The 3.9% price gain since the last review does not drive this — fundamentals are unchanged and out-year estimates have edged higher. The change reflects three analytical conclusions. First, the FY2025 base was flattered by roughly $2.65 per share of discrete tax benefit that was not adjusted out, so FY2026 guidance is approximately flat against a normalized ~$27.36 rather than down 10%, and the 2027 target of ≥$30.24 merely recovers reported 2025 — a two-year round trip. Second, at 14.4x adjusted EPS that excludes $3.49 per share of charges (amortization, transaction and integration, business optimization) that have recurred annually, the stock is mid-range for its cohort rather than materially mispriced. Third, the unaccrued contingency stack is unusually dense for a company at a 2.1% segment margin. Base case $435 (+12.3%), bull $492 (+27.0%), bear $300 (−22.6%).

## Company Overview

Elevance serves approximately 44.9 million medical members as of June 30, 2026, down from 45.2 million at year-end 2025. The core asset is exclusivity: Elevance holds the Blue Cross and/or Blue Cross Blue Shield license in fourteen states or partial states, including California (Blue Cross only), Georgia, Indiana, New York metro, Ohio and Virginia. The lock-in is quantified in the 10-K: approximately 34 million BCBS-branded enrollees at a contractual re-establishment fee of $98.33 each implies roughly $3.3 billion payable on license termination. That same structure imposes BCBSA capital, quality and best-efforts obligations.

Four reportable segments generated FY2025 operating revenue of $197,584M and operating gain of $7,199M (3.6% margin): Health Benefits $167,094M / $4,158M (2.5%), CarelonRx $43,400M / $2,418M (5.6%), Carelon Services $28,316M / $960M (3.4%), and Corporate & Other, offset by $41,689M of eliminations.

CarelonRx does not self-perform core pharmacy benefit functions, which is not typical for a company of this scale. Under the CVS Agreement, Elevance delegates claims adjudication, network administration, rebate administration and the back-end of its home-delivery pharmacy to CaremarkPCS Health, a CVS Health subsidiary. Elevance is the purchaser; CVS is the vendor. The agreement runs through December 31, 2027, with Elevance holding an election to extend three years on the same terms. Elevance's own forward-looking-statement list names non-compliance under that agreement as a discrete risk.

The Carelon diversification narrative warrants a caution: in the first half of 2026, CarelonRx ($21,850M) plus Carelon Services ($15,340M) reported $37,190M of segment operating revenue against $23,090M of consolidated eliminations. Eliminations are not disclosed by segment, so 62% is an upper bound rather than a measured figure, but it establishes that a large majority of Carelon's reported revenue is Elevance transacting with its own Health Benefits membership. Carelon's own volume indicators are also declining — adjusted scripts −2.9% to 80.9M, consumers served −5.0% to 92.4M. Revenue mix shift here is not evidence of external customer diversification.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth has stalled and what remains is price, not volume.** Total operating revenue: $170,209M (FY2023), $175,204M (FY2024), $197,584M (FY2025, +12.8%), $99,320M (1H 2026, +1.2%). Second-quarter growth was +0.8% with premiums flat. The FY2025 surge was Medicare repricing (+21.6% to $44,752M), Carelon Services (+57.7% on the CareBridge and Centers Plan acquisitions), and CarelonRx (+20.7%). Individual/ACA operating revenue rose 16.8% in the second quarter on 2.3% fewer members; Medicare revenue fell only 4.2% against a 15.9% MA membership decline. This is margin-over-growth executed deliberately.

**Margin erosion is the central fact.** The benefit expense ratio moved 87.0% → 88.5% → 90.0% across FY2023–FY2025, with 89.7% in the second quarter of 2026 (up 80bp year over year on government cost trend, partly offset by improved Individual ACA). Health Benefits operating gain fell 33.4% in FY2025 to $4,158M and a further 42.6% in the second quarter of 2026 to $896M. The adjusted operating expense ratio moved the wrong way too — 11.0%, up 100 basis points, on workforce and capability investment.

**The FY2025 earnings base requires normalization.** The effective tax rate was 15.6% versus 24.5% in FY2024 and 22.3% in FY2023, attributed in the 10-K to a discrete non-operating benefit from internal subsidiary restructuring plus resolution of uncertain tax positions. At FY2024's rate, FY2025 tax would have been $1,644M rather than $1,049M — a $595M benefit, or $2.65 per share on 224.6M average diluted shares. Because the non-GAAP bridge adjusts only for the tax effect *of* the add-backs and not for discrete rate items, that benefit flowed into adjusted EPS. Normalized FY2025 GAAP EPS is approximately $22.56 against $25.21 reported; normalized adjusted EPS approximately $27.36 against a derived $30.01. Treating the entire rate gap as non-recurring is a judgment, and 24.5% is only one reference rate, but the direction is clear: the 2025 comparison base is softer than the headline.

**The second-quarter beat was thinner than reported.** Adjusted diluted EPS of $7.45 beat the ~$6.18 non-GAAP consensus by roughly $1.27, but net investment income was $704M versus $486M, a $218M increase attributed to alternative investments. Management indicated approximately $0.80 per share of one-time gains would be reinvested in the second half. At 217.9M diluted shares, $0.80 is roughly $174M after tax, or about $232M pretax at the second-quarter rate — essentially the entire year-over-year investment-income increase. Elevance's reconciliation excludes net *losses* on financial instruments but does not exclude net investment income, so those gains sat inside the $7.45. Adjusting to approximately $6.65 reduces the beat to roughly $0.47. The counterargument is legitimate: alternative-investment marks in a $37B-plus portfolio are lumpy but recurring, and "one-time" is management's characterization rather than an accounting determination.

**Cash flow was genuinely weak in 2025 and is timing-flattered in 2026.** Operating cash flow: $8,061M (FY2023), $5,808M (FY2024), $4,290M (FY2025). FY2025 conversion of 0.76x net income is poor for a managed care organization, which normally runs above 1.0x on float growth; the $666M BCBSA provider settlement paid in September 2025 explains part of it. Free cash flow was $3,174M, 1.6% of revenue. First-half 2026 operating cash flow of $6,245M looks like a sharp recovery, but $2,700M came from changes in operating assets and liabilities (versus −$2,003M a year earlier), the second quarter's $1.9B reflected timing of a state Medicaid pass-through payment (implying roughly $4.3B in the first quarter alone), and $593M of the CMS accrual plus $114M of transformation liability remain accrued but unpaid. Stripping the working-capital swing puts the half roughly in line with net income plus depreciation and amortization. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast, but it argues against annualizing the reported figure.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $126,438M, cash $10,232M, investments $39,671M across current and long-term, goodwill and other intangibles $39,322M, long-term debt $30,669M plus $375M current, equity $44,883M. Debt to capital improved to 40.8% from 42.1% at year-end 2025 and 43.0% at year-end 2024. No commercial paper, revolver draw or FHLB borrowings outstanding; the revolver was upsized to $5.0B and extended to September 2030. Ratings are solidly investment grade but not high grade (S&P A−, Fitch BBB+, Moody's Baa2). The genuine constraints are two: goodwill and intangibles equal 87.6% of equity, leaving tangible book of only $5,561M ($25.65 per share); and parent-company cash and investments of approximately $2.1B against roughly $2,100M of expected 2026 subsidiary dividend capacity, versus FY2025 parent outflows of $4,134M in buybacks and dividends. Regulated subsidiary capital requirements, not consolidated cash, govern upstreaming.

A note on screens: the SEC XBRL "Revenues" concept returns roughly $32.9B for 2025 against actual operating revenue of $197,584M, because the tag captures CarelonRx product sales only. Any automated screen built on that field — including the Altman Z-Score of 1.33 that appears in some data sets — is corrupted. Z-Score is also calibrated on manufacturers and is inapplicable to a regulated insurer with statutory capital requirements. Debt to capital, subsidiary dividend capacity and RBC are the correct lenses.

**Reserves are conservatively posed.** Days in claims payable rose 2.9 days year over year to 45.4 (down 1.2 sequentially), and the current-year paid-to-incurred ratio fell 140 basis points to 78.9% — both consistent with a reserve build. Prior-year favorable development of $1,195M in the first half broke down as $636M trend and $559M completion factors, versus $1,013M trend and only $52M completion a year earlier. Large completion-factor redundancy suggests the year-end 2025 reserve was set conservatively, though the filings do not disaggregate cause and a methodology or processing-speed change would produce the same signature. Prior-year redundancy as a percentage of prior-year incurred has declined (1.4% → 1.4% → 1.0%), a normal consequence of faster-moving cost trend rather than evidence of inadequacy.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus and management are close but not identical, and the gap is informative. Current-year consensus of $27.16 sits 0.6% above the $27.00 floor; next-year consensus of $29.63 implies +9.1%, roughly 2% *below* the ≥$30.24 that management's ≥12% framing implies. Consensus is not fully underwriting the 2027 recovery.

Estimate revisions are split by tenor. Current-quarter consensus fell 8.5% in 30 days ($5.10 → $4.66) and next-quarter fell 17.7% ($3.00 → $2.47), summing to $7.13 for the second half against management's implied ≥$6.94 floor. Annual figures moved the other way: current year +0.9% over 30 days and +1.8% over 90 days; next year +1.3% and +1.7%. The pattern is consistent with analysts moving second-half earnings into reinvestment while leaving the annual and out-year picture intact. This is not the >+30% ninety-day revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection; it is stability.

The one large, filed, already-determined tailwind is Star Ratings. For the 2026 payment year, approximately 40% of MA members were in plans rated 4.0 stars or better; for the 2027 payment year, based on ratings released October 2025, that rises to approximately 59%. Star bonus revenue carries high incremental margin and is contractual rather than negotiated, which makes it the most credible mechanical support for the ≥12% 2027 framing. The offset is that the benefit applies to a 15.9% smaller MA base, so the dollar value is less than the percentage improvement implies.

The margin sensitivities frame the opportunity. On annualized first-half Health Benefits revenue of approximately $170.4B, each 100 basis points of margin is roughly $1.70B pretax, $1.29B after tax at 24.5%, or approximately $5.90 per share on 218M shares. Recovering the full 210 basis points to FY2023 levels would be worth roughly $12 per share of adjusted EPS. Separately, moving Medicaid from the guided −1.75% to +2.0% on $57.4B of revenue is a $2.15B pretax swing, roughly $7.45 per share. These hold revenue constant, which is the flaw: Elevance is shedding risk-based membership to get there, and margin recovery on a smaller book delivers less than the arithmetic suggests. Management also stated on the call that it expects to exit additional Medicaid markets over the next 12 to 18 months, which further shrinks the revenue base against which recovery multiplies. The Medicaid margin guidance and the exit commentary are secondary reporting of call remarks, not filed figures.

The counterweight to Star Ratings is regulatory sequencing. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act introduces work and community-engagement requirements, more frequent redeterminations, new cost-sharing and limits on state directed payments, with most Medicaid provisions effective 2027–2028. The mechanical effect is attrition concentrated among healthier members who are likelier to fail administrative requirements, worsening the residual risk pool. That risk sits precisely in the years management is guiding to recovery. Enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired December 31, 2025; Elevance's Individual book has held better than feared on revenue but membership fell 7.5% sequentially (1,424K at March 31 to 1,317K at June 30), and mid-year attrition typically leaves a sicker pool whose morbidity will not be legible until the fourth quarter.

## Competitive Landscape

The 2025–2026 managed care cycle is an industry margin reset, not an Elevance-specific failure; Molina, Centene and Oscar traded down in sympathy with the July 15 print. Elevance's distinguishing choice is to exit markets rather than defend share, which is available to a diversified carrier where Medicaid is roughly 29% of Health Benefits revenue and largely unavailable to Medicaid pure-plays. That is a structural advantage in a down-cycle.

On Medicare Advantage, Elevance shrank membership 15.9% while holding revenue nearly flat, and its Star Ratings improvement positions it ahead of peers still working through Star deterioration. On Commercial and ACA, +16.8% revenue on −2.3% membership indicates it priced adequately for 2026 while others chased share.

The clear structural weakness is pharmacy. The industry profit pool has migrated toward pharmacy and care delivery — Optum at UnitedHealth, Health Services at CVS/Aetna. Elevance rents its core PBM engine from a competitor's subsidiary under an agreement expiring December 31, 2027, one year before Part D delinking and the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2026 rebate pass-through and reporting requirements bite (approximately August 2028). CarelonRx's 5.6% operating margin faces direct statutory pressure regardless of who operates the function, and Elevance bears both the regulatory margin risk and the vendor-dependency risk. The extension election on the same terms mitigates renewal pricing risk but compresses the window for renegotiating or insourcing.

Trajectory is mixed. Share loss is voluntary and margin-motivated rather than competitive, and the Blue license structure is not erodible. But Carelon's internal volume indicators are declining, the diversification narrative is largely intercompany, and the vertical-integration gap versus UnitedHealth is widening rather than closing. We hold competitive position at 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The February 27, 2026 CMS matter concerning MA risk-adjustment diagnosis codes for dates of service 2015 through April 2023 has resolved favorably: $935M accrued at March 31, $342M paid May 27, $593M remaining, and on July 13 CMS confirmed all required remedial steps were completed, imposed no sanctions and closed the enforcement process. That removes one binary. What remains is unusually dense for a company running a 2.1% segment margin.

**U.S. v. Anthem** (S.D.N.Y., filed March 2020), a DOJ False Claims Act action on MA risk adjustment, has fact discovery closing **August 31, 2026** and expert discovery May 10, 2027. No accrual is disclosed. FCA damages can be trebled plus per-claim penalties. The CMS closure resolves the administrative track and establishes neither liability nor exoneration in the FCA case. The discovery calendar makes the next four weeks through 2027 an active catalyst window.

**Mosaic Health** is a concentrated, four-way, partly unrecognized exposure. Elevance holds approximately 40% of Augusta Topco Holdings, and put/call/true-up options could require it to purchase the majority owners' units as early as 2028 and no later than 2030, priced on multiples of Mosaic revenue and earnings. Elevance elected not to fair-value these options: Mosaic's carrying value is $1,330M against an estimated Level III fair value of $2,736M, a $1,406M unrecognized excess. Elevance is simultaneously a 40% equity holder, a lender ($200M term loan plus a $500M line with $100M drawn), a paying customer of Mosaic's care-delivery services ($386M in the first half, in benefit expense), a shortfall funder (up to $70M committed through year-end 2026, $34M provided), and the contingent forced buyer of the remaining ~60%. The Liberty Dental options are exercisable as early as the third quarter of 2026. The measurement election is permissible and the Level III estimate could prove high if Mosaic underperforms, but a probable multi-billion cash call in 2028–2030 carried at cost is a material off-balance-sheet claim on parent liquidity that is currently ~$2.1B.

**Goodwill and intangibles of $39.3B against $44.9B of equity.** FY2025 disclosure states fair values were substantially in excess of carrying values and no impairment was recorded. With Health Benefits at a 2.1% quarterly margin and Carelon Services margin having compressed from 4.8% to 3.4% across FY2023–FY2025, headroom disclosure is worth monitoring at each annual test.

**Blue Cross antitrust opt-outs** remain: Alaska Air, JetBlue, Bed Bath & Beyond, Hoover, and VHS Liquidating Trust/Prime Healthcare, the last with trial scheduled March 2027. No accrual amounts disclosed.

**Macro overlay.** Higher-for-longer policy rates support net investment income on a $37B-plus portfolio, and Elevance carries $31,044M of notes against a $29,068M estimated fair value — a rate effect rather than a credit signal. The offsetting exposures are policy rather than rate: a midterm-election year with health costs politically salient, OBBBA Medicaid provisions landing in 2027–2028, and PBM statutes converging on 2028.

**Unresolved item.** An 8-K filed February 26, 2026 carries Item 5.02 (departure or election of directors or officers) alongside Item 7.01, and its contents could not be retrieved. A December 10, 2025 8-K likewise carries Item 5.02. Boudreaux and Kaye were both in place in July 2026, so neither is a CEO or CFO change. No inference should be drawn from the one-day proximity to the February 27 CMS notice.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 36.1% sits essentially in line with 35.9% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is flat (36.1% near, 37.8% at six months), distributing uncertainty evenly rather than concentrating it at an event. The direction of change is meaningful: IV rose 4.3 points from 31.8% while the price rose 3.4%, and put/call open interest rose from 0.50 to 0.72. Rising implied volatility alongside a rising price indicates the market pricing an approaching catalyst rather than settling into complacency. The one-month tenor carries a 4.5% put/call skew characterized as elevated put demand and a 2.33 put/call volume ratio, against a −1.2% reading at three months and −4.3% at six months; the near-dated protection bid is concentrated in a window containing the August 31 FCA fact-discovery close and no scheduled earnings event. The ±6.0% implied move over 15 days is arithmetically consistent with 36% annualized volatility, so it is the level, not the move, that is notable for a managed care name.

Short interest of 3.1% of float with 3.5 days to cover indicates an uncrowded book with no squeeze dynamics in either direction. This positioning does not contradict a HOLD; it modestly cautions against the more aggressive read.

We score risk factors 4.5: the density of specific, dated, largely unaccrued exposures against a thin tangible equity base and a constrained parent liquidity position is not adequately compensated at 14.4x.

## Management Quality

Insider activity is the strongest forward signal in the file and it is constructive. CEO Gail Boudreaux purchased 2,725 shares on July 17, 2026 at $366.41 and $368.25 (~$1.0M), two days after the earnings decline. Director Ramiro Peru bought 1,000 shares at $366.05 the same day. Director Steven Collis bought 3,000 shares at $289.84 on March 5, 2026, near the year's low. Three insiders buying on the open market, with one well-timed at the trough, is a credible cluster. The caveat is scale: Boudreaux's purchase added roughly 1.6% to a 171,976-share position, and purchases of that size can reflect ownership-guideline compliance as much as conviction.

Capital allocation shows genuine discipline and genuine constraint, and the filings do not distinguish which dominates. Second-quarter buybacks were only $234M at a $344.62 weighted average price, against roughly $1,124M in the first quarter and a ~$651M quarterly FY2025 average, while the stock rose toward $426.79 by July 13. Reading that as price discipline is reasonable. Reading it as parent-liquidity preservation ahead of the $342M CMS payment, the $593M remaining accrual, roughly $1.0B of Medicaid operating losses and transformation cash outlays running through 2028 is equally reasonable. The quarterly dividend went from approximately $1.70–1.71 to $1.72, roughly +1%, after years of high-single to low-double-digit increases — effectively frozen. FY2025 combined returns of $4,134M consumed 96% of operating cash flow and 130% of free cash flow. Debt to capital nonetheless improved to 40.8% and the revolver is undrawn.

The pay-for-performance record cuts both ways. CEO "compensation actually paid" was negative $3,957,314 in 2024 and NEO average CAP was negative $256,544 — the equity-heavy structure genuinely destroyed prior-grant value when the stock fell, which is unusual and shareholder-friendly. Against that, indexed five-year TSR of 117 versus a peer index of 148 shows decisive relative underperformance, the company-selected metric (adjusted shareholders' net income) peaked at $7,838M in 2023 and fell 14.0% to $6,741M in 2025, and CEO grant-date target pay rose from $21.89M to $22.58M over the same period. There is no disclosed capital-efficiency or margin metric in the incentive set, and adjusted net income excludes the CMS accrual and optimization charges that shareholders bear in cash.

Two presentation choices also weigh here: the non-GAAP framework did not adjust out the ~$2.65 per share discrete tax benefit that flattered FY2025, and it does not exclude net investment income, so the one-time alternative-investment gains sat inside the reported $7.45. Neither is improper; both make the reported trajectory look better than the underlying one. We score 6.5, down from the prior 7.5 on the pay-versus-performance divergence and presentation choices, not on any change in the insider evidence.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $492 (+27.0%)

Medicaid margin inflects from the guided −1.75% toward breakeven and then positive as July 1 rate increases take effect and market exits remove the worst books; that swing alone is worth up to ~$7.45 per share of adjusted EPS on current revenue. The Star Ratings step-up to 59% of MA members in 4.0+ plans flows through 2027 revenue at high incremental margin, delivering management's ≥12% growth and beating the $29.63 consensus that currently sits 2% below the company's own framing. Individual ACA continues to perform on 2026 pricing. Health Benefits margin recovers toward 3.5–4.0%, and adjusted EPS reaches $32–33 in 2027. At 15x that range the stock is $480–495, consistent with the $492 analyst high. The $27.00 floor language proves conservative from a team that guided down once in 2025 and has since beaten four consecutive quarters by 6.5% to 21.0%. Insider buying at $366–368 marks the floor.

### Bear Case — $300 (−22.6%)

Second-half 2026 delivers the implied −24.5% and no more, and the market concludes the $27.00 floor was the ceiling. State Medicaid rate-setting continues to lag realized behavioral health, specialty pharmacy and outpatient trend, and the further market exits shrink the revenue base faster than margin recovers, so 2027 lands near $27–28 rather than $30+. OBBBA redeterminations beginning in 2027 remove healthier members and worsen the residual pool exactly when recovery was promised. ACA morbidity from the 7.5% sequential membership decline surfaces in the fourth quarter. The DOJ FCA action produces an adverse development in a matter with no accrual and treble-damages exposure. Liberty Dental options are exercised and the Mosaic purchase obligation crystallizes toward the $2.7B Level III fair value against ~$2.1B of parent cash and ~$2.1B of annual subsidiary dividend capacity, forcing buyback suspension. At 11x $27.50 the stock is $302, near the March 2026 trading range and above the $273.71 52-week low.

### Balance of Evidence

The bull case requires a rate-setting process Elevance does not control to cooperate on schedule; the bear case requires only that current trends persist. The Star Ratings tailwind is the one element that is contractual and already determined, and it is the reason this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID. The margin-recovery leverage is symmetric, which at 14.4x on adjusted earnings that exclude $3.49 of recurring charges does not constitute the asymmetry a BUY requires.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a specific, catalysed 12-month appreciation thesis — Star Ratings revenue in 2027, July 1 Medicaid rate increases, and consensus sitting below management's own out-year framing. Base case $435, derived as 14.7x the $29.63 FY2027 consensus (equivalently 14.4x management's implied ≥$30.24), or +12.3% from $387.47 plus a 1.78% yield. That sits between the $300 bear (−22.6%) and $492 bull (+27.0%) cases above and below the $449 analyst mean.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Third-quarter 2026 (mid-October) adjusted EPS at or above $4.66 consensus **with** Medicaid operating margin confirmed improving toward breakeven from −1.75%, and the benefit expense ratio flat or better year over year. That would convert the recovery from framing into evidence.
2. FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance at or above $30.24 issued with a Medicaid margin bridge, alongside restoration of buyback pace toward $600M+ per quarter — which would simultaneously resolve the parent-liquidity question.

## Conclusion

Elevance offers a credible margin-recovery story at a defensible price, with one filed and already-determined 2027 tailwind, four consecutive earnings beats, improving leverage, and open-market buying by the CEO and two directors. It also carries flat premiums, contraction in every risk-based membership line, a Health Benefits margin at 2.1%, a Medicaid book losing roughly $1.0B this year, a guidance floor embedding a 24.5% second-half decline, a pharmacy engine rented from a competitor through December 2027 ahead of 2028 delinking, an unaccrued False Claims Act action entering an active discovery window, and a probable multi-billion-dollar 2028–2030 purchase obligation carried at cost against $5.6B of tangible equity.

At $387.47 the stock trades at 14.4x the FY2026 adjusted floor and 13.1x next-year consensus — attractive against a normalized trailing base but mid-range for the cohort on forward adjusted earnings, and 15.7x once the recurring portion of the adjustment bridge is added back. With approximately 12% upside to base case against approximately 23% downside, near-dated protection bid up while implied volatility rose 4.3 points into a rising price, and a dense stack of dated contingencies that are not accrued, the risk/reward no longer supports the prior BUY. We move to HOLD and would become constructive again either on evidence that Medicaid margin is inflecting or on a price near $330–345.
