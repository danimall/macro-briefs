## Executive Summary

Vita Coco is the clear leader in a genuinely fast-growing beverage category, and the underlying operating improvement in the first half of 2026 is real. H1 revenue rose 32.1% to $395.9M, and after removing the $15.6M one-time tariff refund that ran through cost of goods sold, gross margin expanded roughly 430bps to 40.8%. The cleanest confirmation is Q1 2026, which carried no refund at all: derived gross margin of 39.95% versus 36.7% a year earlier, a 322bp improvement driven by net pricing, lower ocean freight, and lower finished-goods cost. The company holds greater than 40% US and 80% UK coconut water share (Circana, 52 weeks ended December 2025), carries no interest-bearing debt, and has repurchased ~1.30M shares at a ~$33.92 average cost against today's $62.51.

The reason the stock sits below its June 18 high of $85.83 is the arithmetic embedded in management's own raised guidance. FY2026 sales guidance of $790–805M and Adjusted EBITDA of $154–161M, less H1 actuals, implies second-half revenue of $401.6M (+29.5%) but second-half Adjusted EBITDA of only $51.6M (+11.0%), with implied H2 gross margin of 35.3% — below the 36.5% posted in H2 2025 and 547bps below H1's refund-adjusted 40.8%. Management names three causes: unquantified "current cost pressures," "adverse product mix," and a discretionary sales-and-marketing step-up to carry momentum into 2027. Two of the three are headwinds; one is a choice. The disclosures do not allow a confident adjudication between sandbagging and real deterioration, and that single question dominates the twelve-month return.

Two structural changes compound the uncertainty. The Copra acquisition, closed July 22 for $140M cash plus $35M stock plus a $45–100M 2029 earnout, converts a piece of the twenty-year asset-light sourcing network into an owned Thai factory with cold-chain, foreign-labor, fixed-cost, and direct coconut-price exposure the company had explicitly avoided; no Copra revenue or EBITDA has been disclosed, so no acquisition multiple can be computed. Separately, the earnings base itself is flattered: FY2025 carried $16M of tariff cost that has now lapsed, and FY2026 additionally banks a $15.6M refund that will not repeat. FY2027 comparisons therefore start from a normalized ~$138–145M Adjusted EBITDA base, not the guided $154–161M. At ~30.8x current-year and ~26.7x next-year consensus EPS against peer-median 30.3x, the price does not embed a margin of safety commensurate with those unknowns.

## Company Overview

Vita Coco pioneered packaged coconut water in 2004 and derived 96% of FY2025 revenue from coconut water. The adjacent-brand portfolio has been retired or is being rebuilt: Runa ceased selling in December 2023 with remaining assets impaired in September 2025; Ever & Ever ceased production in 2024; PWR LIFT is described in the 10-K as still lacking a viable retail approach and is being redesigned. The concentration is therefore not cosmetic — one category and one raw material carry essentially the entire P&L.

The business is organized in two segments: Americas ($508.8M, 83.4% of FY2025 net sales) and International ($101.0M, 16.6%), across three categories — branded Vita Coco Coconut Water, Private Label, and Other. Only 336 full-time employees supported $609.8M of FY2025 revenue.

The competitive asset is the supply chain rather than marketing spend. Coconut water must be packaged aseptically or converted to concentrate within hours of harvest, forcing processing adjacent to growing regions. Vita Coco sources from approximately 16 coconut water factories across six countries plus six co-packers in three countries, and until July 2026 owned none of them. The 10-K frames this asset-light structure as a competitive advantage providing the ability to reallocate sourcing in the event of supply, weather, or logistics disruption. Private Label, entered in 2016, adds scale to that network and captures the value segment without diluting the brand.

The company is a Delaware public benefit corporation and Certified B Corporation, with a charter obligation to consider stakeholders beyond shareholders.

**Separating one-time from underlying margin.** Following a February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that certain IEEPA tariffs were not statutorily authorized, Vita Coco filed refund claims in April and received $15.6M by June 30, recognized entirely as a reduction of cost of goods sold. Adding it back: Q2 2026 gross profit becomes $89.7M on $216.2M of sales, a 41.5% margin versus 36.3% a year earlier (+520bps). H1 becomes 40.8% versus 36.5% (+430bps). The Q2 EPS contribution was approximately $0.205 (($15.6M + $0.6M interest) × (1 − 23.16% effective rate) ÷ 60.64M diluted shares), so ex-refund Q2 diluted EPS was roughly $0.61 against $0.38.

Two distinct tariff effects must be kept apart. The refund is non-recurring. The elimination of the ~$16M annual tariff run-rate (2.6% of FY2025 sales, at a weighted-average rate estimated at 23% as of end-Q3 2025, relieved by the November 14 and 21, 2025 exemptions) is a permanent level shift already in the 2026 base — it flatters 2026 growth but does not compound. Only the pricing, freight, and product-cost improvement visible in the clean Q1 print grows from here.

**Cash conversion is the weakest link.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $47.2M against $71.3M of net income is a 0.66x conversion, with inventory up $27.9M and receivables up $18.1M. FY2025 free cash flow was $39.0M — a 6.4% FCF margin against a 16.1% Adjusted EBITDA margin. H1 2026 OCF of $96.5M looks like a decisive reversal, but the composition is mixed: roughly $28.3M came from finished-goods inventory drawdown ($106.1M to $78.6M, plausibly the unwind of a tariff-era pre-build) and $39.4M from a payables and accruals build, against a $50.6M receivables build. Accrued expenses and other current liabilities rose 42.3% ($89.5M to $127.3M) against 32.1% revenue growth; Q2 is the seasonal peak for trade promotion and incentive accruals, so this is the expected pattern, but it is the line to watch in Q3. Underlying working-capital efficiency is stable — Q2 2026 DSO of ~55.7 days against ~54.6 days a year earlier, and improved inventory days — the issue is that 30% growth absorbs cash regardless. I do not annualize H1 OCF; the inventory release cannot repeat at that magnitude.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30: cash $278.6M, total assets $572.9M, total liabilities $172.0M, equity $400.9M, interest-bearing debt effectively zero. The $60M Wells Fargo revolver, extended to February 2030 at SOFR + 1.00–1.75%, was undrawn and in covenant compliance. Pro forma for the $140M Copra cash payment, cash falls to roughly $138.6M, giving ~$199M of liquidity against a minimum $45M earnout payable in 2029 and ~$61M of remaining buyback authorization. Comfortable, but the cushion has halved.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend. The repurchase authorization moved from $40M (October 2023) to $65M (April 2025) to $105M (July 21, 2026). Execution has been price-sensitive: ~$44M for 1,297,067 shares at a ~$33.92 average through April 2026; H1 2026 repurchases of $20.0M at a $50.16 average, with the April tranche of 173,618 shares at $48.98 near the observable April low. No repurchases occurred July 1–22, when the stock traded materially higher; whether that reflects discipline or a pre-earnings blackout cannot be determined. Capex was $8.1M in FY2025 for new office leaseholds and only $0.6M in H1 2026 — a figure that will rise now that the company owns a factory.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth has accelerated sharply: +4.5% (FY2024), +18.2% (FY2025), +32.1% (H1 2026), with FY2026 guidance implying roughly +30%. The category tailwind appears genuine on both issuer and third-party evidence, and management characterizes growth as driven by both household penetration and consumption per household — the healthier decomposition than price or distribution alone. That characterization rests on management commentary plus a single third-party citation; the underlying Circana category growth rate could not be independently verified.

The composition of that growth is less favorable than the headline. In Q2 2026, branded Vita Coco Coconut Water case-equivalent volume grew only 6.6% in the Americas, while Private Label CE volume grew 81.7% in the Americas. Americas branded net sales still rose 14.5%, so roughly 8 points came from price and mix. Management attributes the branded volume softness partly to a retailer promotion that shifted into Q1; the cleaner H1 read is +16.0% branded Americas CE volume. Even so, the H1 spread between +16.0% branded and +44.1% Private Label Americas volume is a structural mix headwind: International segment gross margin was 36.7% in Q2 versus 51.8% in the Americas, and Private Label is by design the value segment.

The Private Label surge also requires discounting. The FY2025 10-K discloses that in 2023 the company began discontinuing Private Label supply to one significant customer on margin grounds, lost further regions in 2025, and in early 2026 restarted supply to one lost region at that customer's request. Americas Private Label fell 30.2% in FY2025 to $62.7M, making Q2 2025's $14.7M a trough. Q2 2026's $26.9M is an 83.2% increase against that trough but also above the FY2024 quarterly average of ~$22.5M — part easy-comp recovery of deliberately exited business, part genuine distribution gain. Seventy-eight percent Private Label volume growth is not a secular rate.

**The H2 guidance step-down is the crux.** Subtracting H1 actuals from FY midpoints:

| Metric | H1 2026 actual | Implied H2 2026 | H2 2025 actual | Implied YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $395.9M | $401.6M | $310.1M | +29.5% |
| Gross margin | 44.7% (40.8% ex-refund) | 35.3% | 36.5% | −119bps |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $105.9M | $51.6M | $46.5M | +11.0% |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 26.8% | 12.8% | 15.0% | −214bps |

Implied H2 SG&A of ~$94.8M is +26.2% in dollars but slightly lower as a percentage of implied H2 sales (23.6% versus 24.2%), so the compression is predominantly a gross-margin phenomenon rather than an SG&A blowout. Management's raise history argues for conservatism — the initial February guide was $680–700M, raised to $720–735M, then to $790–805M — and the incremental marketing spend is explicitly framed as discretionary and enabled by outperformance. Against that, the mix shift is measurable and worsening, the cost pressures are named but unquantified, the Q2 10-Q explicitly flags Strait of Hormuz disruption raising fuel and energy costs and creating fuel-availability uncertainty for suppliers, and H1's margin gain was itself partly attributed to lower ocean freight rates. Both readings remain live.

Estimate revisions reflect the same split: current-year consensus rose to $2.03 (+14.3% over 30 days, +25.8% over 90 days) and next-year to $2.34 (+14.3%, +22.3%), while next-quarter estimates fell 13.2% over 30 days and 20.5% over 90 days. That divergence is exactly the H2 step-down appearing in the sell-side model. Note also that only two analysts contribute to these estimates, and part of the current-year revision is mechanical, reflecting the banked refund rather than a forward operating view. The revision magnitude therefore sits below the threshold at which I would treat it as a standalone buy signal.

**Copra** adds a new growth vector into what management estimates is roughly 13% of US coconut water sales — the super-premium cold segment the company does not currently serve. Guidance "includes balance of year for Copra" without disclosing the split, which bounds organic FY2026 growth only between roughly +18% (if the entire $70M raise is Copra) and +32% (if none is).

## Competitive Landscape

Level of position is strong; trajectory is mixed. Vita Coco holds greater than 40% US and 80% UK coconut water share, competing against Goya, Harmless Harvest, Zico, and C20 within the category and against Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, and Nestlé across functional beverages. Coca-Cola owned Zico and exited it, which supports the view that the barrier is the near-source aseptic supply chain rather than marketing budget — twenty years of origin factory relationships, farmer networks, and Tetra Pak-based logistics.

Three developments qualify that moat. Supplier concentration moved adversely: suppliers exceeding 10% of purchases went from 15%/14%/9% (38% combined) in H1 2025 to 21%/11%/11% (43%) in H1 2026, and Tetra Pak remains the single source for the majority of packaging. At 21%, the largest supplier is now roughly as concentrated as the largest customer. This is a one-year move within a ~16-factory network and volume allocation can shift year to year without reducing available optionality, so I weight it as directional rather than established.

Second, the Copra acquisition converts part of the network from contractual flexibility to a fixed asset. The Q2 10-Q's new risk factor states the position plainly: the deal "represents a change to our operating model because we have not historically owned or operated any manufacturing facilities," and "the concentration of production in a single owned facility for the Copra acquisition products limits our flexibility to shift volume in the event of a disruption." The company also becomes a direct purchaser of whole coconuts, assuming raw-material price and local growing-condition risk previously borne by contract manufacturers. The strategic logic — owning the super-premium cold segment before a larger competitor builds it — is coherent, and vertical integration may be operationally necessary for a cold-chain product. Without Copra revenue, EBITDA, or capacity disclosure, the price cannot be assessed.

Third, category mix is broadening while measured customer dependence is not. International rose from 16.6% of FY2025 sales to 19.0% of H1 2026 and Private Label from 14.5% to 18.1%, but two customers accounted for 44% of H1 2026 net sales (45% in H1 2025) and 40% of receivables (39% at year-end). These measure different things, and the flat 44% is the figure bearing on negotiating leverage. If the Private Label surge routes partly through the same large retail accounts, effective dependence is unchanged or higher; if it reflects genuinely new accounts, as management's "distribution gains" language suggests, the base broadens over time. The disclosures do not resolve this, and the investment case should not assume the favorable reading.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration, three ways.** 96% of revenue from one category built on one raw material; 44% of sales from two undisclosed customers; 43% of purchases from three suppliers with a single-source packaging dependency. The 10-K documents that the largest Private Label relationship is negotiated hard — the company walked away in 2023 on margin grounds, lost regions in 2025, and was invited back in 2026.

**Newly assumed operating risks (added in Q2 2026).** Manufacturing ownership brings capex obligations, fixed costs not offset in periods of reduced demand, idle capacity, environmental and health-and-safety liability, foreign skilled-labor recruitment, and single-facility production concentration. Copra's super-premium products require continuous refrigeration from production through retail sale, introducing spoilage, recall, and food-safety exposure absent from the legacy shelf-stable aseptic model.

**Freight and energy.** The 10-Q explicitly names the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption as increasing fuel and energy costs and creating fuel-availability uncertainty for suppliers and their manufacturing operations. This is directly material for a company whose cost structure depends on ocean freight from Southeast Asia and Brazil, and whose H1 margin gain was partly attributed to lower ocean freight rates. My read of the broader shipping environment is that transit normalization remains unverified and contracted freight rates unwind slowly even if it occurs, so this is a live H2 headwind rather than a resolved one.

**Tariff reversal.** The refund is banked, but the IEEPA ruling closed one statutory avenue, not all of them, and the 10-Q notes announced intent to use other authorities. A return to a 23% weighted-average rate would cost $16M+ annually at FY2025 volumes and more at FY2026's.

**Unhedged FX through GAAP earnings.** $99.1M notional of forward contracts across BRL, EUR, CAD, GBP, and THB, none designated as hedges, so all marks run through income. Disclosed sensitivity is roughly $6.7M of P&L on a 10% USD move; history shows $8.2M of unrealized losses in FY2024 versus $4.7M of gains in FY2025. This is why the company declines to guide GAAP net income.

**Governance and key person.** Dependence on co-founder Michael Kirban with no key-person life insurance. The charter's DGCL §203 equivalent carves out Verlinvest Beverages regardless of ownership percentage, combined with a three-class staggered board, no cumulative voting, and no action by written consent. Three Item 5.02 filings within four months (January 8, April 10, and an April 22 amendment) could not be identified; Roper remains CEO and Baker remains CFO, so no top-two departure is indicated, but the thread is unexamined.

**Insider activity.** Roughly 836,000 shares for ~$57M were sold March–June 2026 with no open-market purchases. Composition matters: the CEO's ~204,456 shares and the COO's ~200,000 were predominantly exercise-and-sell of options struck at $10.178, with the CEO's direct holdings unchanged at 298,484 — routine low-strike monetization, weakly informative. The more substantive signal is co-founder Ira Liran selling 340,000 held shares on June 11–12 at $79.14–$80.32 (~$27.1M) and Kirban selling 50,000 held shares at $68.00 on April 30 (~$3.4M), both without accompanying exercises, into what proved to be a local price peak. The 10-Q confirms no 10b5-1 plans were adopted, amended, or terminated during Q2, so governing plans pre-date April — consistent with pre-scheduled diversification. Founder diversification after ~375% cumulative TSR (per the proxy) is ordinary, but seven insiders selling with zero buying, including held-share sales by both co-founders, is a cluster I weight negatively.

**Compensation design.** Adjusted EBITDA is both the company-selected pay metric and a PSU vesting metric, and management did not exclude the explicitly labeled one-time $15.6M refund from it — the "Other adjustments" line shows $0 for Q2 and H1 2026 while carrying $952K and $1,621K in the comparable 2025 periods for items as small as $0.4M of overlapping office rent. There is a defensible symmetry argument (the tariff costs were never excluded either), and I draw no conclusion about intent. What matters is arithmetic: guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $154–161M embeds ~$15.6M that will not recur. Stock-based compensation rose 60% to $8.2M in H1 alongside a disclosed PSU vesting re-assessment whose direction is not stated. A modest offset: the board declined its permitted 2% share-reserve increase for 2026.

**Unexplained late-June derating.** The most significant single-day move in the price history is June 26, when the stock opened at $83.52 and closed at $73.71 on 3.39M shares (roughly 3x average), followed by a further decline to $66.14 by June 30 — both before the Copra announcement and the Q2 print. No item in the available evidence explains this. A meaningful part of the drawdown from the high therefore has no identified cause, which is itself a reason for caution about declaring the market wrong.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $88 (+40.8%)

The clean Q1 gross margin of 39.95% versus 36.7% establishes that the margin expansion is operational, not a refund artifact. Pricing has held, freight and finished-goods costs have fallen, and the permanent removal of a ~$16M annual tariff burden is a durable level shift. Guidance has been raised twice from an initial $680–700M to $790–805M, and the second-half spending step-up is explicitly discretionary, which means the implied 35.3% H2 gross margin is more likely a conservative placeholder than a forecast — Q3 could beat comfortably against a 36.5% prior-year comparison. The category is compounding on both household penetration and consumption frequency, a durable growth signature, and Vita Coco's >40% US share plus twenty-year origin network faces no credible new entrant, as Coca-Cola's Zico exit demonstrated. Copra adds a platform in the ~13%-of-market super-premium cold segment funded from a debt-free balance sheet. At 26.7x FY2027 consensus versus a 30.3x peer median with roughly 28% implied earnings growth off a refund-adjusted base, the multiple is undemanding, and $61M of remaining buyback authorization from a management team that has repurchased at a $33.92 average cost provides a bid. Consensus targets range $68–$90.

### Bear Case — $44 (−29.6%)

The reported profit base is inflated twice — by a $15.6M refund inside the pay metric and by the lapse of $16M of prior-year tariff cost — so FY2027 must grow from a normalized ~$138–145M Adjusted EBITDA, not $157.5M. The mix shift is measurable and adverse: branded Americas volume grew 6.6% in Q2 while Private Label grew 81.7%, International segment margin is 36.7% against Americas at 51.8%, and Private Label rose to 18.1% of H1 sales from 14.5% in FY2025. The Private Label surge is partly recovery of business the company itself walked away from on margin grounds and cannot be extrapolated. Cost pressures are named but unquantified, with Hormuz disruption explicitly flagged as raising fuel and energy costs against a comparison base that benefited from low ocean freight. Free cash flow of $39.0M in FY2025 and a 6.4% FCF margin mean the stock trades at 45–60x normalized free cash flow, and Copra adds capex. The Copra deal converts the marketed asset-light advantage into single-facility, cold-chain, foreign-labor, and coconut-price exposure at an unassessable price with no disclosed target financials. Two customers remain 44% of sales and 40% of receivables with no measured improvement. Seven insiders sold and none bought over four months, including ~$30M of co-founder held-share sales within weeks of the June high. A meaningful part of the drawdown from the high has no identified cause.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (hold rationale: uncertain). Existing holders can maintain exposure; the balance sheet is unlevered, the category is growing, and the underlying margin improvement is verified in the clean Q1 print. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $62.51 with an expected return of roughly +8% against a −30% bear leg.

- **Base case: $70 (+12.0%)** — 30x peer-median forward multiple on FY2027 consensus EPS of $2.34, with H2 2026 gross margin landing between the implied 35.3% and H1's refund-adjusted 40.8%.
- **Bull case: $88 (+40.8%)** — H2 guidance proves conservative, gross margin sustains near 40%, FY2027 EPS ~$2.60 at 34x.
- **Bear case: $44 (−29.6%)** — mix and freight pressure persist, FY2027 EPS ~$1.90 at 23x.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 report (late October), which tests the H2 gross-margin guide against a 36.5% prior-year comparison and should provide the first Copra contribution disclosure.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Q3 2026 gross margin prints at or above 38% ex-refund, demonstrating the implied 35.3% H2 guide was conservative rather than predictive.
2. Copra revenue, EBITDA, and purchase-price allocation are disclosed at an implied multiple below ~15x EBITDA, with FY2027 guidance showing accretion net of integration capex.
3. Price reaches $50–53, where FY2027 consensus of $2.34 would trade at 21–23x and the base case would offer 32–40% upside. Note management repurchased 173,618 shares at a $48.98 average in April.
4. Branded Americas case-equivalent volume growth reaccelerates above 12% in a quarter, reducing dependence on Private Label mix.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if:**
1. Q3 2026 gross margin comes in below 35% and management attributes it to sustained cost or mix pressure rather than a discretionary spending choice, implying FY2027 EPS below $2.00.
2. Two-customer concentration rises above 48% of sales, or either customer's Private Label supply is reduced or repriced — the 10-K documents this relationship has been renegotiated adversely before.

## Conclusion

Vita Coco earns a HOLD at $62.51. The business is better than it was a year ago on the evidence that matters: 32.1% H1 revenue growth, roughly 430bps of genuine gross-margin expansion confirmed by a refund-free Q1 print at 39.95%, no interest-bearing debt, and a category leader's share position in a category growing on both penetration and frequency. Management has raised guidance twice and has repurchased stock at prices well below today's.

The offsetting evidence is equally concrete. Management's own raised guidance implies second-half Adjusted EBITDA grows 11% on 29.5% revenue growth, with gross margin below the prior-year second half, and the causes are a mix of unquantified cost pressure, measurably worsening product mix, and discretionary spending. The FY2026 earnings base embeds $15.6M that will not repeat and a $16M tariff lapse that will not compound, so FY2027 starts from a normalized ~$138–145M rather than $157.5M. Free cash flow of $39.0M in FY2025 means the equity trades at a multiple of cash generation several times its multiple of accounting earnings. The Copra acquisition, at $220–275M of total potential consideration with no disclosed target financials, converts a documented competitive advantage into single-facility fixed-asset, cold-chain, and coconut-price exposure. Seven insiders sold and none bought over four months, including both co-founders reducing held positions near the June high. A meaningful part of the drawdown from the high has no identified cause in the available evidence.

At 26.7x next-year consensus with an expected return near +8% and a −30% bear leg, the price compensates for neither the H2 uncertainty nor the model change. The Q3 report resolves the dominant variable, and a move toward $50–53 — where management itself was buying in April — would restore the asymmetry required for a more constructive rating.
