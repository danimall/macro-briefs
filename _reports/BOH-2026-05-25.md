## Executive Summary

Bank of Hawaii Corporation is a $3.1 billion market cap regional bank with a dominant franchise in Hawaii's geographically isolated banking market. The company has delivered strong earnings recovery through 2025 and into Q1 2026, driven by NIM expansion from 2.16% to 2.74% as deposit costs repriced lower following 175bps of Fed cuts. Credit quality is exceptional: net charge-offs of just 3bps annualized and NPAs at 0.09% of loans, reflecting Hawaii's tight real estate market and low unemployment.

The stock has appreciated 15% over the past year and now trades at approximately 16.8x trailing EPS ($4.63) and roughly 15x annualized Q1 2026 EPS (~$5.20). This is in line with the peer median P/E of 15.5x. The 3.6% dividend yield is well-covered at a 54% payout ratio, and management has accelerated buybacks ($15.1M in Q1 2026 vs. $5M in all of FY2025). However, the NIM tailwind from deposit repricing is approaching its limit, growth prospects are constrained by Hawaii's small economy, and the company faces elevated risks from federal government policy changes, defense spending uncertainty, and tariff impacts on an import-dependent island economy.

The investment thesis is income-oriented: a stable, well-capitalized bank with a protected franchise and reliable dividend, but without meaningful catalysts for multiple expansion or above-trend growth from current levels.

## Company Overview

Bank of Hawaii, founded in 1897, operates as the holding company for its principal subsidiary, Bank of Hawaii. The bank serves businesses, consumers, and government entities through 52 branches and 319 ATMs across Hawaii (93% of loans), Guam (4%), and other Pacific Islands. With $23.9 billion in total assets and 1,877 employees, BOH operates through three segments:

- **Consumer Banking**: Residential mortgages ($4.8B), home equity, auto loans, credit cards, deposits, trust, and wealth management
- **Commercial Banking**: CRE ($4.2B), C&I, construction loans, lease financing, and cash management
- **Treasury & Other**: Corporate ALM, investment securities portfolio, and interest rate risk management

The bank elected financial holding company status in January 2026, broadening its potential future activities. A CEO transition completed on April 1, 2026, with James C. Polk succeeding Peter S. Ho after 16 years.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Recovery**

BOH's earnings trajectory shows clear recovery from the 2023-2024 trough:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 (ann.) |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Net Income | $226M | $171M | $150M | $206M | ~$230M |
| Diluted EPS | ~$5.68 | ~$4.34 | ~$3.46 | $4.63 | $5.20 |
| NIM | — | — | 2.16% | 2.45% | 2.74% |
| ROE | ~17.8% | ~13.9% | ~10.9% | 11.9% | ~13.9% |

Q1 2026 net income of $57.4M represented 31% YoY growth, with NII of $151M up 20% YoY. The primary driver is the "scissor effect": deposit costs fell from 2.16% to 1.72% YoY while asset yields remained stickier due to fixed-rate loan repricing.

**Balance Sheet Strength**

Capital ratios are well above regulatory minimums: CET1 of 12.06% (minimum 6.5%), total capital ratio of 15.44%. Total deposits of $21.0B include $5.65B in noninterest-bearing deposits (27% of total), a valuable low-cost funding source. Uninsured deposits are just $1.39B (6.6% of total deposits), significantly reducing liquidity risk.

**Investment Securities Overhang**

The legacy securities portfolio carries $784.3 million in total unrealized losses ($170.6M AFS + $613.7M HTM), representing roughly 25% of current market capitalization. These are government-backed securities with no credit risk; the losses reflect duration mismatch from the low-rate era and will amortize to par over time. The losses are declining (from ~$1.1B gross in 2024) as rates have fallen and securities mature, but they depress tangible book value and constrain portfolio flexibility.

**Credit Quality**

Credit metrics are near-pristine: NPAs at $12.1M (0.09% of loans), net charge-offs of just $1.1M (3bps annualized) in Q1 2026, and provision expense of only $1.75M. The allowance for credit losses stands at 1.04% of total loans. Hawaii's 2.6% unemployment rate and tight real estate market support this quality.

**Capital Return**

The quarterly dividend of $0.70/share ($2.80 annualized) yields 3.6% at current prices with a 54% payout ratio. Share repurchases accelerated to $15.1M in Q1 2026 (194,096 shares at $77.84 average), with $105.9M of buyback authorization remaining.

## Growth Analysis

BOH's growth profile is modest and largely rate-dependent:

- **Loan growth**: 0.8% QoQ in Q1 2026, with commercial mortgage growth of 3.2% offset by flat consumer portfolios. Hawaii's small economy (population ~1.4M) inherently limits organic loan growth.
- **NIM expansion**: The 42bp YoY NIM improvement was the primary earnings driver, but sensitivity analysis shows limited further upside (+100bp rates = only +0.8% NII). The deposit repricing tailwind is largely exhausted unless rates fall further.
- **Fee income**: Declined after the Q4 2025 sale of merchant services (~$8-11M annual revenue sacrificed for a one-time $18.1M gain). Exchange & other service charges fell 24% YoY in Q1 2026.
- **Consensus estimates**: FY2026 EPS of $6.01 (+30% YoY), FY2027 EPS of $6.97 (+16% YoY). These imply continued NIM benefit but decelerating growth.
- **Estimate revisions**: Current quarter estimates revised down 0.9% over 30 days; next year revised up 1.5% over 30 days and 1.9% over 90 days. The revision trend is mixed and provides no compelling momentum signal.

Growth is constrained by geographic concentration, a static population base, and the near-completion of the NIM recovery cycle. The Maui fire rebuilding provides a modest multi-year construction lending opportunity but is not transformative for a $14.2B loan portfolio.

- Forward P/E (FY2027): ~11.2x (on $6.97 consensus)

**Peer comparison**: The peer median P/E of 15.5x and median yield of 2.6% place BOH slightly above fair value on trailing earnings but with a materially higher yield than peers, reflecting the income-oriented shareholder base. First Financial Bancorp (FFBC) trades at 10.8x with a 3.3% yield — cheaper but with less franchise quality. Seacoast Banking (SBCF) at 20.5x is more expensive, reflecting Florida growth premium.

The consensus analyst target range of $82-$95 (mean $86.67) implies 5-22% upside, with the median target of $86 offering ~11% total return including the dividend. Analyst coverage is split (1 Strong Buy, 1 Buy, 3 Hold, 1 Strong Sell), suggesting no consensus that the stock is materially mispriced.

The stock is not expensive on forward estimates, but the forward P/E compression assumes continued NIM expansion that may be nearing its ceiling. There is no clear catalyst for multiple expansion beyond current levels, and the market appears to be pricing the earnings recovery appropriately.

## Competitive Landscape

BOH benefits from one of the most defensible competitive positions among U.S. regional banks. Hawaii's geographic isolation creates natural barriers to entry — mainland banks lack physical presence, local knowledge, and the relationship infrastructure important in Hawaiian culture. The bank competes primarily with First Hawaiian Bank (FHB) and Central Pacific Financial (CPF) in a stable oligopoly.

Competitive advantages:
- 127+ year operating history and deep community relationships
- Extensive physical distribution (52 branches, 319 ATMs across islands)
- "Outstanding" CRA rating — important for government contracts
- Deposit franchise stability demonstrated through the 2023 regional bank crisis (minimal outflows when peer banks failed)
- Low-cost deposit base (27% noninterest-bearing)

Competitive risks:
- Limited growth opportunity within a confined geographic market
- Fintech encroachment on payments and lending (though slower in Hawaii than mainland)
- The merchant services divestiture reduces fee diversification
- Tourism-dependent economy subject to external shocks

The competitive position is stable to slightly improving — the bank's 5-year TSR underperformance (109% vs. peers' 153%) reflects rate-driven issues rather than competitive deterioration. Market share within Hawaii appears stable.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geographic and Economic Concentration (HIGH)**
93% of loans in Hawaii creates meaningful single-market dependency. Hawaii's economy relies on tourism (weakening as jet fuel costs rise), military spending (under potential fiscal pressure), and real estate (concentrated). Any defense budget cut, sustained tourism decline, or natural disaster would disproportionately impact BOH with no geographic diversification to absorb losses.

**2. Federal Government Policy Risk (ELEVATED/NEW)**
BOH's 10-K devotes unusually extensive new language to federal government risks. The October-November 2025 government shutdown strained the local economy. Federal workforce reductions directly impact Hawaii's employment base. Defense spending uncertainty clouds a major economic pillar. Tariffs increase costs for Hawaii's import-dependent economy.

**3. Interest Rate Sensitivity at Ceiling**
With NIM at 2.74% and sensitivity showing only +0.8% NII for +100bp rates, the bank has limited upside from further tightening. If rates resume cutting, NII would face headwinds (-300bp = -5.3% NII impact). The bank is modestly asset-sensitive, but the sensitivity is small at this point.

**4. Investment Portfolio Duration Risk**
The $784M in unrealized losses will take years to amortize. While not a solvency issue given strong capital and liquidity, it constrains balance sheet flexibility and creates ongoing mark-to-market noise. If rates rise further, unrealized losses could widen again.

**5. CEO Transition Execution**
James Polk assumed the CEO role April 1, 2026, after approximately 20 months as President. While the transition appears orderly, new leadership introduces execution uncertainty. The prior CEO's 16-year tenure created deep institutional relationships that take time to rebuild.

**6. Natural Disaster Tail Risk**
Maui fire recovery is ongoing nearly three years later. Hawaii faces unique vulnerability to hurricanes, volcanic activity, and sea-level rise. Insurance costs are rising; a major event could disrupt lending and deposit operations simultaneously.

### Options Market Signal

Historical volatility of 24.6% with implied moves of 6.3% (1-month) to 13.8% (6-month) appears reasonable for a stable regional bank. ## Investment Thesis

- NIM expansion continues if deposit costs fall further while loan yields stabilize, pushing FY2026 EPS above $6.01 consensus
- Hawaii tourism recovery accelerates, supporting loan growth and credit quality
- Maui rebuilding creates multi-year construction lending opportunity
- Buyback acceleration at $106M remaining authorization provides EPS accretion
- New management brings fresh capital allocation discipline; potential for special actions (portfolio restructuring, enhanced returns)
- The franchise's defensibility and 3.6% yield attract income investors as growth stocks decelerate

**Bear Case ($60-65, -17-23% downside)**
- Federal government spending cuts reduce Hawaii's military-dependent employment base, triggering credit deterioration
- Tourism declines further due to elevated travel costs (jet fuel + tariffs); Hawaii's import dependence raises local inflation above mainland levels
- If rates rise, the investment portfolio's unrealized losses widen, and the market penalizes BOH's duration mismatch
- New CEO struggles with strategic direction; institutional relationships built over 16 years erode
- Natural disaster (hurricane, volcanic activity) disrupts operations
- Stock re-rates to 10-11x forward earnings (in line with cheaper peers like FFBC), implying $60-66

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock declines to $68-70 range (providing 4%+ dividend yield and meaningful discount to peer median P/E)
- FY2026 EPS tracking above $6.50 with visible catalysts for continued expansion
- Material positive catalyst in Hawaii economy (new military investment, tourism breakthrough, major rebuilding program)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NIM compression below 2.50% for two consecutive quarters
- NPAs rise above 50bps of loans (from current 9bps), indicating credit stress
- Dividend coverage threatened (payout ratio above 80%)
- Hawaii unemployment rises above 4.5%, signaling economic deterioration

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, likely July 2026). Key monitoring items: NIM trajectory, deposit cost trends, loan growth, and any federal government policy actions affecting Hawaii's economy.

## Conclusion

Bank of Hawaii is a high-quality franchise in a defensible market, currently priced at fair value after a 15% appreciation over the past year. The earnings recovery is well-advanced, with NIM expansion approaching its ceiling and consensus estimates reflecting continued growth that may prove difficult to sustain. At 16.8x trailing earnings with a 3.6% yield, the stock offers reliable income but limited upside from current levels.

The appropriate posture is HOLD for income: collect the well-covered dividend, monitor NIM trajectory, and wait for either a price pullback to provide a better entry ($68-70 range) or evidence that growth can sustain above consensus to justify upgrading. The concentrated geographic exposure, federal government policy risks specific to Hawaii, and CEO transition create sufficient uncertainty that paying a full peer-median multiple lacks the asymmetric upside required for a BUY recommendation.
