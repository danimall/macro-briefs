## Executive Summary

Occidental completed the most consequential balance-sheet repair in its post-Anadarko history during the first half of 2026. The $9.7B sale of OxyChem to Berkshire Hathaway closed 2 January 2026 at a final $9,472M after adjustments, generating roughly $3.1B of after-tax gain, and management applied the proceeds almost entirely to debt: $8,609M of face-value principal retired in H1 at a $190M extinguishment cost, taking principal debt from $20,427M at year-end 2025 to $11,818M at 30 June 2026. That is $2.5B below the $14.3B target disclosed in the 10-K, the level at which management said cash would rotate to opportunistic repurchases. Fitch upgraded to BBB during 2026; the current ratio improved from 0.94 to 1.41; there are no debt maturities in 2026 and only $414M cumulatively through 2029 against an undrawn $4.15B revolver maturing June 2028.

What the deleveraging has not done is make the equity cheap or the risk profile clean. Three claims sit ahead of common shareholders that a headline P/E does not capture. First, $8.5B face of 8% Berkshire preferred consuming $679–680M of cash annually — 29.2% of 2025 net income after noncontrolling interests — which Occidental *cannot voluntarily redeem before August 2029*, and which carries a mandatory 10%-premium redemption trigger on distributions above $4.00/share trailing twelve months. Second, a retained and now parent-guaranteed environmental tail: $1,853M reserved across 149 sites at 30 June 2026 with up to $1.9B of reasonably possible additional loss and unreserved natural resource damages, with the appeal on EPA's 85% Passaic River allocation fully briefed and awaiting decision. Third, the Tronox tax matter, where closing arguments concluded May 2024 and the Tax Court "may issue an opinion at any time" — an adverse ruling requires roughly $2.4B to be paid or bonded within 90 days. The liability is already accrued, so this is a cash-flow event rather than an earnings event, but it would consume most of the $4,150M cash balance.

Simultaneously, the operating case has become a two-sided commodity bet. H1 2026 results were extraordinary — Q2 WTI averaged $92.79, OXY realized $96.78/bbl (104% of WTI), and continuing-operations EPS reached $2.75 in the quarter — but 30-day consensus revisions are negative in every period (current quarter -2.3%, next quarter -15.4%, current year -7.5%, next year -4.5%) even as 90-day revisions remain positive. The +31.7% 90-day revision to the current quarter is a price artifact rolling over, not an earnings inflection. Fifth consecutive HOLD; overall score eases to 5.3 from 5.5.

## Company Overview

Post-divestiture Occidental is a two-segment company. **Oil & Gas** produced 1,434 Mboe/d in 2025 (+8% on 1,328 in 2024), of which 1,202 Mboe/d was U.S. — Permian 786 (up from 664 following the August 2024 $11.1B CrownRock acquisition), Rockies & Other Domestic 284, Gulf of America 132 — and 232 Mboe/d international, principally Al Hosn (UAE) 90, Oman 71 and Dolphin 40. Proved reserves were 4,603 MMboe at 12/31/2025, 72% developed, implying an 8.8-year reserve life against 523 MMboe of annual production. **Midstream & Marketing** gathers, processes, transports and markets hydrocarbons, CO₂ and power, holds the Western Midstream stake (39.0% combined share of net income at 6/30/26, down from 43.1%), and houses the direct-air-capture venture.

A reporting discontinuity must be handled before any trend work: OxyChem is in discontinued operations with all prior periods retrospectively restated. On the continuing basis, net sales were $21,593M (2025), $22,019M (2024) and $23,156M (2023); the frequently cited ~$36.6B for 2022 includes chemicals and is not comparable. Income from continuing operations was $2,107M in 2025 against $2,866M in 2024, and net income to common $1,647M ($1.61 diluted).

One data note: the supplied profile reports trailing and forward P/E both at 72.74 on EPS of $1.35. That is inconsistent with reported results — trailing four-quarter EPS actuals were $0.64, $0.31, $1.06 and $2.40 ($4.41 TTM), and Q2 2026 continuing-operations EPS alone was $2.75. I treat the 72.74x figure as a stale or misconstructed field and anchor valuation to consensus forward estimates and EV/EBITDA.

## Financial Analysis

**Unit economics.** 2025 worldwide realized oil was $64.60/bbl (-14%) against WTI of $64.81. Cost control was genuinely good: lease operating expense fell to $8.94/Boe from $9.75 (2024) and $10.48 (2023), a 15% two-year reduction from a combination of CrownRock mix, the divestiture of higher-cost Rockies barrels, and operational work. Full-cycle 2025: revenues $39.89/Boe less LOE $8.94, transportation $3.11, other operating $2.88 and production taxes $1.93 leaves roughly $23.03/Boe of cash margin before DD&A, G&A, interest and tax; DD&A of $13.59/Boe leaves $8.97/Boe pretax.

**Cash flow, unbundled.** Continuing-operations operating cash flow was $9,606M in 2025 against $6,427M of capex, so continuing FCF was $3,179M — not the ~$4.1B implied by total OCF, the difference being OxyChem's $926M which is gone prospectively. Against $945M of common and $679M of preferred dividends, 2025 coverage was 1.96x. A worthwhile detail: dividends received from equity-method investees (predominantly Western Midstream) were $705M in 2025 while $619M of undistributed *losses* were recorded — cash received from those stakes materially exceeds accounting earnings, which flatters cash flow relative to reported income.

H1 2026 delivered $6,478M of continuing OCF against $3,143M capex, or $3,335M of half-year FCF. That figure should not be annualized. It benefited from $92.79 WTI in Q2, $908M of marketing derivative gains inside Q2 net sales (mark-to-market, and they reverse), and lower cash taxes ($514M paid versus $877M in H1 2025). As a labeled sensitivity rather than a forecast: at $70 WTI with 2026 capex guidance of $5.5–5.9B and roughly $300M of annualized interest savings from the paydown, continuing FCF plausibly runs $3.5–4.0B, leaving $2.8–3.3B after preferred dividends — a 5.0–5.9% FCF yield to equity.

**Leverage.** Total debt and finance leases were $13,743M at 6/30/26 against $4,150M cash, so net debt of approximately $9,593M. Using FY2025 continuing EBITDA of $11,740M (pretax $3,128M + interest $1,079M + DD&A $7,533M) — deliberately the $64.81-WTI year rather than the price-inflated recent quarters — net debt/EBITDA is 0.82x; adding the $8,489M preferred at face as a debt-like claim gives 1.54x. Stockholders' equity rose to $41,746M from $36,034M and retained earnings to $27,350M.

Screening flags keyed to the last 10-K materially misstate the current position. An Altman Z of 1.22 on the 12/31/2025 balance sheet uses inputs that predate the OxyChem close, the $8.6B paydown and the $3.1B gain; recomputed on the 6/30/2026 balance sheet with TTM flows it rises to roughly 1.94. The Z-score is in any case structurally depressed for capitalized-reserve E&Ps because asset turnover runs 0.26–0.30. The maturity ladder and the undrawn revolver are the load-bearing solvency facts, and both are benign.

**The offsets to a clean bill of health** are substantial and mostly off the income statement: $12.6B of take-or-pay purchase obligations with $3.0B due in 2026 alone and $4.7B in 2027–28; asset retirement obligations of $4,553M including $452M of upward revisions to prior estimates in 2025; $1,853M of environmental reserves with up to $1.9B of reasonably possible additional loss; and the contingent ~$2.4B Tronox cash call. S&P still rates the company BB+, one notch below investment grade. Financial health scores 6.5 — a real and confirmed improvement, held below 7 by the preferred, the contingent cash calls and the commodity-dependence of the cash flow that services them.

## Growth Analysis

There is little organic growth here, and consensus does not expect any. 2025 volume growth of 8% was CrownRock, acquired at a cycle-high deck. Q1 2026 production of 1.426 MMboe/d was roughly flat. Reserves were essentially unchanged at 4,603 MMboe versus 4,612 MMboe despite $6.4B of capital spending; organic reserve replacement was 107% and total replacement 98% including purchases and sales. Implied organic finding-and-development cost of roughly $10.93/Boe consumes nearly half the $23.03/Boe cash margin before corporate costs. Capex guidance of $5.5–5.9B for 2026 is below the $6,427M spent in 2025.

Reserve quality carries a flag: proved undeveloped reserves fell to 1,309 MMboe from 1,421, including a negative revision of 318 MMboe attributed to "management changes in development plans," of which 314 MMboe was Permian, offset by +238 MMboe of positive performance revisions. A further 185 MMboe of PUDs booked before 2021 remain undeveloped, 168 MMboe of it international and constrained by gas processing capacity. Roughly $8.4B over five years is required to develop Permian PUDs; $2.2B was spent in 2025 converting 31%.

Earnings "growth" is therefore a price function. Consensus for the current year is $5.41 and for next year $3.91 — a 28% decline that embeds normalization from the H1 2026 $82.36 WTI average toward roughly $71. That cross-check is worth making explicit: on approximately 717 Mbbl/d of oil, each $1/bbl of WTI is roughly $262M pretax, or about $0.175/share after tax at the 33% effective rate. The $1.50 gap between current-year and next-year consensus corresponds to about $8.6 of WTI, consistent with a normalizing deck.

The genuine per-share growth levers are financial rather than operational: roughly $300M of annualized interest savings, a 2.2%-of-shares repurchase authorization ($1,223M) not yet deployed, and the dividend raised 8% in August 2026 to $0.28 quarterly ($1.12 annualized, 1.99% at $56.32). Direct air capture is real optionality — STRATOS central processing construction complete, Class VI permits obtained, BlackRock's full $550M funded, OBBBA having raised credit values — but it currently carries negatively, and OXY has already written its 40.3% NET Power stake to zero with $401M of impairment while still carrying $954M of low-carbon intangibles and $668M of Carbon Engineering goodwill tested each April. Growth prospects score 4.6.

That preferred is the reason the apparent P/E discount is not a mispricing. The 8% coupon costs $680M of after-tax cash; refinancing $8.5B with debt at roughly 5.75% would cost $489M pretax, or $328M after tax — a saving of about $352M per year, roughly $0.35/share, or about 6% of current-year consensus EPS. That option does not exist until August 2029, and then only at a 5% premium (~$425M). Above that, every $1.00 returned to common requires a matching $1.10 preferred redemption — $2.10 of outlay per $1.00 delivered. The constraint is not binding at current distribution levels, but it removes the recapitalization lever that would otherwise justify a re-rating.

Consensus targets: low $55.00, mean $64.52, median $65.00, high $75.00, with a recommendation mix of 2 strong buy, 7 buy, 15 hold and no sells. The low target is essentially at spot. The mean requires roughly 6.4x EV/EBITDA on a $70-WTI deck, which is achievable but not conservative. Valuation scores 5.3: modestly cheap on headline earnings, fair-to-full once the preferred, the environmental run-off and the contingent tax cash call are treated as claims on enterprise value.

## Competitive Landscape

Three characteristics genuinely differentiate Occidental.

**Permian scale with midstream egress.** Permian gas is now a cost line, not a revenue line: OXY's U.S. realized gas price was *minus* $1.48/Mcf in Q2 2026 and minus $0.26/Mcf for H1, producing negative $250M of domestic gas revenue in the quarter, against a positive $1.88/Mcf in H1 2025. The 2025 U.S. realization of $1.58/Mcf was only 45% of NYMEX — in a good year. Operators holding contracted or owned takeaway capture the basis spread instead of paying it, and OXY does: Midstream & Marketing swung from a $33M H1 2025 pretax loss to $1,251M of H1 2026 pretax income, attributed in part to gas transportation capacity optimization and crude marketing. On the disclosed figures the roughly -$690M year-over-year domestic gas revenue swing was more than offset by the +$1,284M midstream swing. This is a structural partial hedge that a pure-play Permian producer without integration does not have. It is also volatile and partly non-recurring: Q1 2026 midstream was an $87M loss against Q2's $1,338M income, and $908M of Q2 net sales were mark-to-market marketing derivative gains. The offset is real; the H1 magnitude is not a run rate.

**International ballast.** International operations generated $17.69/Boe of pretax income versus $7.26/Boe domestically in 2025 on 16% of volumes, largely because international DD&A is $6.07/Boe against $15.05/Boe in the U.S. on long-lived, substantially depreciated Middle East positions. The 2025 Oman contract extension added 61 MMboe of positive revisions. Few U.S.-listed independents have this. It is also why the consolidated effective tax rate is 33% (2025) versus 29% (2024) and 28% (2023) — the OxyChem reclassification of a primarily domestic business mechanically raised it, and international statutory rates run "as high as 55%."

**Legacy CO₂ EOR infrastructure** underpins a DAC position further advanced than any major peer's, with the honest caveat that OXY is also the only major to have written a low-carbon investment to zero this cycle.

Against those: an 8.8-year reserve life is short for a company of this scale; reserves were flat on $6.4B of capital; the 318 MMboe PUD write-down on development-plan changes is a quality signal; and the standardized measure fell from $42,871M to $36,627M on a -$5,968M price-deck change. Competitive position scores 5.5 — differentiated in mix, not visibly gaining share, with capital intensity rising to hold volumes flat.

## Risk Assessment

**Tronox (undated, near-term).** Anadarko's $881M tentative refund plus roughly $500M of additional benefits was disallowed; trial was held May 2023 with closing arguments in May 2024, and the Tax Court "may issue an opinion at any time." Adverse outcome: roughly $1.4B of tax plus ~$0.9B of interest, approximately $2.4B at 6/30/26, already recorded in deferred credits — so earnings-neutral, but any appeal requires the full amount bonded or paid within 90 days of entry. Against $4,150M of cash, that would consume most of the liquidity buffer and almost certainly defer the buyback. Separately the IRS is reviewing the 2022 reorganization that produced a $2.7B benefit, with $1,944M of unrecognized tax benefits and $951M of accrued interest outstanding.

**Passaic River (undated, near-term).** The December 2024 Amended Consent Decree accepted EPA's determination that OXY bears approximately 85% of cleanup costs for OU2 (~$1.4B) and OU4 (~$440M). A $925M reserve increase was booked in Q4 2024; OXY appealed in February 2025 and all briefs were filed as of January 2026. If not reversed, the decree could bar contribution claims against 85 parties that settled for a combined $150M. Natural resource damages are being assessed separately and are not reserved. Note the structure: OXY retained legacy environmental liabilities through a subsidiary and Occidental *guaranteed* its subsidiaries' indemnification obligations in favor of Berkshire. The chemical business was sold; the liability tail was retained and formally backstopped, with roughly 30% of the balance expected to flow out over three to four years and the remainder over 10+ years.

**Commodity exposure, now less buffered.** The 10-K states plainly that post-OxyChem the business is more exposed to oil, NGL and gas prices; OxyChem contributed $926M of countercyclical operating cash flow. There were no commodity hedges at 12/31/2025. February 2026 collars cover 100 Mbbl/d for March–December at a $55.00 floor and $75.89 weighted-average ceiling — roughly 14% of oil volumes, so the floor protects little and the ceiling has already cost $234M in net sales with $183M of cash settlements paid against Q2 realizations near $96.78.

**Middle East is two-sided.** The same Hormuz disruption that drove Q2 realizations to 104% of WTI threatens 201 Mboe/d (14% of volumes) and $5,939M of net PP&E in the UAE, Oman and Qatar. The 10-Q attributes the Q2 price spike to conflict involving Iran and disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz; post-Q2 conditions and whether the disruption persisted are not established in the filings reviewed.

**DAC structure.** The STRATOS joint venture holds $1.3B of construction in progress; BlackRock's $635M noncontrolling interest carries preferential distributions and a liquidation preference; OXY may owe additional payments if construction or operational thresholds are missed and may be required to call the interest if commercial operations are not achieved.

**Preferred step-up.** If preferred dividends are ever unpaid, the rate steps to 9% compounded quarterly and remains at 9% permanently, and unpaid preferred bars any common dividend.

Risk factors score 4.2. Two undated legal binaries with combined cash exposure of roughly $2.4B plus an unreserved remediation tail, layered on an essentially unhedged commodity book with a geographically concentrated volume risk correlated to the price upside, is a dense stack of idiosyncratic exposure that the 10.4x current-year multiple does not compensate.

### Options Market Signal

The headline read — near-term ATM IV of 52.4% against 34.9% realized, term structure "in backwardation" — is largely a one-day-expiry artifact; the 1-week tenor has a single day to expiration. Excluding it, the curve is flat and close to fair: 35.2% at one month, 35.6% at three months, 36.7% at six months against 34.9% realized volatility. ATM IV rose from 37.2% to 52.4% between 19 July and 6 August alongside a 2.6% price gain, tracking the oil round trip rather than a company-specific catalyst.

Positioning is mildly bullish, not defensive. Put/call volume ratio is 0.34 and put/call open interest 0.44; skew is described as balanced at the front (-0.9%) with a modest tilt by three months (-3.1%). The single most notable print is 6,624 contracts of 14 August $60 calls against 576 open interest (11.5x) — short-dated speculation on further oil upside. Short interest is effectively nil at 0.0% of float (108,149 shares), so there is no bearish positioning to squeeze and no informed short base corroborating the risk concerns above. The six-month tenor is the one place hedging appears, with put/call volume at 1.20. Net read: the options market is pricing a two-sided commodity outcome with a call-side speculative tilt, consistent with a HOLD and offering no cheap protection.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $75 (+33%)

Hormuz disruption persists, sustained tension holds WTI in the $90s, and Occidental compounds a large-EBITDA year against $11.8B of principal debt. Continuing FCF exceeds $6B; the board launches the remaining $1,223M authorization at prices a new CEO has personally validated, retiring roughly 2.2% of shares; S&P moves OXY to investment grade, completing the ratings normalization Fitch began. The Passaic appeal succeeds — OXY's argument rests on the District Court's failure to weigh Supreme Court decisions restricting agency deference — reducing the 85% allocation and unfreezing stayed contribution claims against 85 settling parties. The Tax Court rules for OXY, releasing a ~$2.4B accrued liability. Midstream continues capturing Waha basis at scale. On elevated-cycle EBITDA at a cycle-peak-discounted multiple less net debt, preferred and noncontrolling interests, equity reaches the $75 analyst high.

### Bear Case — $42 (-25%)

Gulf flows normalize and WTI settles near $60. The $55 collar floor protects 14% of oil volumes; nothing protects the rest. EBITDA compresses toward $10.5B, next-year EPS toward $2.00. Domestic gas realizations stay negative while the midstream offset normalizes from the H1 2026 level. The Tax Court rules against OXY, requiring ~$2.4B paid or bonded within 90 days, and the appeal affirms the 85% Passaic allocation while barring contribution — pushing the environmental provision toward the disclosed $1.9B of additional reasonably possible loss. Buybacks are shelved, the dividend increase looks premature, and the market re-rates a company whose reserve life is 8.8 years and whose CROCE has fallen every year from 0.36 (2022) to 0.19 (2025). At 6.0x $10.5B EBITDA less $18.7B of claims, equity is roughly $44; a 5.5x multiple gives $39, near the $38.80 52-week low. Bear case $42.

### Base Case — $60 (+6.5%)

WTI averages $70–72. EBITDA of roughly $13.1B (FY2025's $11.74B plus 5.2 × $262M of oil-price sensitivity) at 6.0x EV/EBITDA gives $78.7B of enterprise value; less $9.6B net debt, $8.5B preferred at face and $0.6B noncontrolling interest leaves approximately $60.0B of equity, or $60/share on 1,000M shares. That is 15.3x next-year consensus $3.91, a modest premium to the 13.5x peer median justified by the international margin ballast and the midstream basis capture, and offset by the preferred. Continuing FCF of $3.5–4.0B covers the $1.12B dividend and $0.68B preferred with room to complete the authorization. Total return with the 1.99% dividend is roughly 8.5% over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (fifth consecutive). Hold rationale: uncertain — the dominant variable is an unresolved commodity binary, and two material legal outcomes have no disclosed decision date.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with three natural reassessment points: the Q3 2026 report (whether a repurchase program is finally launched now that principal debt is $2.5B below target and $1,223M of authorization remains unused); the Tax Court opinion, which may issue at any time; and the Passaic appeal decision, fully briefed since January 2026.

**Scenario summary from the $56.32 basis:** base $60 (+6.5%), bull $75 (+33.2%), bear $42 (-25.4%). Reward:risk on the base case is approximately 0.26:1; even bull-versus-bear is only about 1.3:1. That asymmetry does not support new capital.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. A repurchase program launched and executing at scale — repurchases materially above the 2.0M shares (average $58.09) reported in H1 2026, which the 10-Q characterized as largely savings-plan trustee activity rather than program execution. Full deployment of the $1,223M authorization retires roughly 2.2% of shares.
2. Favorable resolution of either the Tronox opinion or the Passaic appeal, removing roughly $2.4B of contingent cash exposure or reducing the 85% allocation.
3. Price near $46–48, which on the base-case $13.1B EBITDA implies roughly 5.1x EV/EBITDA and 12x next-year consensus — a level at which the preferred overhang and legal tail are adequately discounted.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. WTI sustaining below $60 for a quarter, at which point 86% of oil volumes are unprotected and the $1.12 dividend plus $679M preferred consumes most of FCF.
2. An adverse Tax Court ruling combined with an affirmed 85% Passaic allocation and a contribution bar — a combined cash and provision event that would push net debt plus preferred meaningfully above 2x normalized EBITDA.
3. Evidence that the H1 2026 midstream result was substantially non-recurring, specifically Midstream & Marketing pretax income reverting toward the H1 2025 loss run-rate while domestic gas realizations remain negative.
4. Further negative PUD revisions on development-plan changes, following the 318 MMboe (314 MMboe Permian) reduction in 2025, which would call reserve-life and F&D economics into question.

**Position guidance:** existing holders should hold. Do not add at $56.32 — note that this basis is already 4.66% above the prior official close, so the entry embeds a sharp single-session move on unresolved geopolitical news. Do not reduce on the basis of the oil-price pullback alone; the balance sheet and maturity ladder make forced action unnecessary.

## Conclusion

The score moves from 5.5 to 5.3, and the composition matters more than the level. Financial health holds at 6.5, confirmed and extended by the H1 deleveraging from roughly $15.1B at the 10-K filing date to $11,818M at 30 June — over-delivery against management's own $14.3B target, plus a Fitch upgrade to BBB, a current ratio of 1.41 and no maturity wall until 2030. The decline sits in the near-term earnings trajectory and the risk stack: 30-day estimate revisions are negative across all four periods, and the two undated legal binaries carrying roughly $2.4B of contingent cash exposure remain unresolved.

The prior report's competitive-position markdown from 6.5 to 5.5 is confirmed rather than reversed. The company is more commodity-levered than it was, having sold the one countercyclical earnings stream it owned while retaining and guaranteeing that business's environmental tail, and it holds reserves with an 8.8-year life that required $6.4B of capital merely to keep flat. Offsetting that is a real structural feature the market underweights: negative Permian gas realizations that cost the upstream segment roughly $250M in Q2 were more than recovered by the midstream segment's capacity optimization, and few pure-play Permian producers have that internal offset.

The reason this is not a BUY is arithmetic rather than narrative. The equity looks inexpensive at 10.4x a cyclically inflated current-year number, but at 14.4x normalized next-year consensus it trades above the peer median, and enterprise value including the $8.5B of 8% preferred is 6.4x FY2025 EBITDA. That preferred cannot be refinanced until August 2029, locking out roughly $0.35/share of annual value, and its mandatory-redemption mechanic caps practical shareholder returns at about $4.00/share. Management has earned credit for the balance sheet and for a new CEO putting $250K of personal capital in at $52.38 with no offsetting open-market sales, but the capital-return trigger it defined has been crossed and no program has started. Until either that gap closes or the price offers a wider margin — the mid-to-high $40s — the risk/reward is balanced. Hold.
