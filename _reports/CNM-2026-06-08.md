**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Core & Main is one of two national specialty distributors of water, wastewater, storm drainage, and fire-protection infrastructure products in North America, holding roughly 17% of a fragmented ~$44B addressable market. The business is a replacement-driven, picks-and-shovels play on aging U.S. water infrastructure, with ~44% of revenue from steady municipal demand and the balance from cyclical non-residential (38%) and residential (18%) construction. The structural story is intact: gross margin has expanded from 26.6% to 26.9% in FY25 and reached 27.2% in Q3, driven by private-label expansion, sourcing optimization, and data-driven pricing. Free cash flow conversion is high, with OCF of $650M against just $46M of capex producing $604M of FCF on an asset-light model.

The tension that prevents a more constructive call sits below the gross line. Despite the margin expansion, operating income was essentially flat ($722M vs $719M) and Adjusted EBITDA was flat ($931M vs $930M) because SG&A deleveraged to 15.1% of sales from 14.5% on integration costs, personnel, tech investment, and rising self-insured medical claims. Reported EPS growth of ~8% is being manufactured largely by buybacks and a $22M reduction in interest expense, not by operating leverage. The M&A engine, the primary growth lever in this consolidating market, downshifted from $769M deployed in FY24 to roughly $76M in FY25, and a Tax Receivable Agreement drains ~$40-44M of FCF annually to pre-IPO insiders, a claim standard screens miss.

The stock has fallen ~13% over the trailing twelve months and sits ~23% below its September 2025 high of $67.18, but estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative rather than collapsing, so this is a valuation reset rather than a fundamental break. At ~22.5x trailing and ~21x forward EPS, CNM trades at a discount to its distributor peer median (P/E ~29.9), which is partly justified by its leverage, the TRA leakage, and the absence of a dividend. With balanced operating momentum, flat consensus, and an options market pricing an 11% move into the imminent earnings print, the risk/reward is symmetric. This is a HOLD with a constructive 12-month growth lean, not a BUY.

## Company Overview

Core & Main distributes its products through ~370 branches across 49 U.S. states and Canada, carrying 225,000+ SKUs from 5,000+ suppliers to a base of 60,000+ customers. The product mix in FY25 was pipes/valves/fittings (67%), storm drainage (16%), meters (9%), and fire protection (8%), with roughly an even division between new construction and repair/replacement.

The company traces back to 1874, operated as HD Supply Waterworks, was acquired by Clayton, Dubilier & Rice in 2017 and rebranded, then IPO'd in July 2021. CD&R fully exited by January 2024, removing the sponsor overhang. The competitive structure is a national quasi-duopoly with Ferguson Waterworks atop hundreds of regional and local players, which sets up a durable roll-up. The moat rests on local technical expertise (field reps average 14 years of experience), knowledge of municipal product specs, breadth and availability, scale-based supplier access including exclusive and limited distribution rights, and proprietary pricing tools. The customer base is highly fragmented (top 50 customers ~12% of revenue, largest ~1%); the supplier base is more concentrated (top 10 ~45%, largest ~7%).

A management transition completed during the year: Mark Witkowski (former CFO) became CEO, founder-era leader Stephen LeClair moved to Executive Chair, and Robyn Bradbury became CFO. The succession was internal and orderly.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY23 $6,702M → FY24 $7,441M (+11%) → FY25 $7,647M (+2.8%). The headline FY25 figure was depressed by one fewer selling week; average daily sales rose 4.8%, driven by volume and acquisitions. Pricing has been roughly flat to slightly deflationary on certain product lines after the 2022 inflation spike normalized.

**Margins.** Gross margin expanded to 26.9% in FY25 from 26.6%, reaching 27.2% in Q3, a structural self-help lever from private label, sourcing, and pricing analytics. The problem is what happens below it: SG&A rose to 15.1% of sales from 14.5% on acquisition costs, personnel, inflation, tech investment, and unfavorable self-insured medical claims. The net result was flat operating income and flat Adjusted EBITDA. Net income attributable rose 7.3% to $441M; diluted EPS was $2.31 and Adjusted Diluted EPS $2.97. The gap between reported EPS growth and flat EBITDA is bridged by buybacks ($155M repurchased in FY25) and a $22M drop in interest expense.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** The FCF margin is ~8%, with working capital the main intra-year swing. Leverage reflects the LBO heritage: ~$2,166M term-loan debt, an undrawn $1,250M ABL with ~$1,226M available, D/E of 2.01, and net leverage of ~1.5x EBITDA, manageable for a stable distributor. Interest expense fell to $120M from $142M on 2024 repricing amendments and lower SOFR; swaps fix $700M through July 2026 and a $750M→$1,500M swap from July 2026-2028 at a 5.913% effective rate, with each 100bps move worth ~$22-34M of annual interest. Goodwill (32% of assets) plus intangibles (14%) equal 46% of total assets, the cost of the roll-up strategy and a source of impairment risk if growth disappoints.

**Earnings track record.** The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters, most recently by 22.9% (Q1 FY26, $0.52 vs $0.42) and 15.9% (Q4 FY25, $0.89 vs $0.77). The beat history supports confidence in near-term estimates, though it partly reflects conservative guidance from a management team that flexed comp negatively when the stock fell.

## Growth Analysis

The thesis rests on three legs: secular demand from aging U.S. water infrastructure (replacement-driven municipal demand is steady through cycles), share gains via acquisition in a fragmented market where CNM is the acquirer of choice, and structural gross-margin expansion from private label, sourcing, and pricing analytics. The first and third are working; the second has stalled.

The most important growth signal this cycle is the M&A downshift (FY24's $769M was headlined by Dana Kepner, 21 locations; FY25's ~$76M covered Canada Waterworks in September 2025 and Pioneer Supply in January 2026). Because acquisitions are the core organic-plus-inorganic growth engine in this fragmented market, the deceleration matters. It likely signals either pricing discipline (acquisition multiples have risen in a consolidating market) or a deliberate pivot toward buybacks and debt reduction. The board raised the buyback authorization to $1B (+$500M) on December 1, 2025, with ~$669M remaining at year-end, consistent with a capital-return pivot.

Estimate revisions confirm muted momentum: current-year EPS consensus of $2.50 is down 3.1% over 90 days; next-year $2.98 is down 0.7%; current-quarter $0.57 and next-quarter $0.78 are flat over 30 days (next-quarter +1.4% over 90 days). Rising revisions plus accelerating revenue would justify a more aggressive stance; the current data shows the opposite. The IIJA's $55B water allocation is a multi-year tailwind whose timing and political durability are uncertain; the January 2025 funding pause explicitly excluded water and road projects, so the core municipal tailwind appears intact even amid political noise, with the bulk of the allocation still unrealized as latent upside.

## Valuation Assessment

At $51.97, CNM trades at ~22.5x trailing EPS of $2.31 and ~21x forward consensus of $2.50. On Adjusted Diluted EPS of $2.97, the trailing multiple is ~17.5x. Against the Trading Companies & Distributors peer median P/E of ~29.9 (AIT 29.1, WSO 29.9, MSM 31.0, DXPE 28.7), CNM screens cheap.

The discount is partly warranted and partly opportunity. Warranted: CNM carries ~1.5x net leverage that the peer set largely does not, pays no dividend (peer median yield 0.7%, with WSO and MSM above 3%), and bleeds FCF to the TRA, a claim worth ~$720M, or ~7% of market cap, that standard FCF screens overlook and that depresses takeover appeal because it can accelerate on a change of control. Opportunity: the business generates a 23% ROE and $604M of FCF (~6.2% FCF yield on the equity), has a structurally expanding gross margin, and operates in a duopoly on a genuine secular theme.

Applying the principle that a price decline on stable fundamentals improves risk/reward, the >20% drawdown from the high requires a specific explanation rather than dismissal. The market is repricing two things: the M&A deceleration that removes the inorganic growth premium, and the SG&A deleverage that converts gross-margin gains into flat EBITDA. Both are real fundamental changes, not noise, which is why I treat the lower price as a fair reset rather than a clear bargain. A reasonable base case applies ~22x to ~$2.55 FY26 EPS, supporting roughly $56, modest upside from current levels. To justify the peer multiple (~28-30x), CNM would need to re-accelerate M&A or break the SG&A deleverage, neither of which is visible in current data.

## Competitive Landscape

CNM's competitive position is improving on the strength of its scale and margin initiatives, which is why this dimension scores well above the others. The national duopoly structure is durable: only Ferguson Waterworks competes at scale, and the fragmented base of local distributors gives CNM a long runway as the consolidator of choice. The private-label push, scale-based supplier access (including exclusive distribution rights), and proprietary pricing tools are widening the gross-margin advantage, evidenced by the 26.6% → 27.2% trajectory.

The principal competitive risk is disintermediation (large municipalities or contractors buying direct from suppliers, or suppliers expanding direct sales), and the private-label strategy could itself create channel conflict with the top-10 suppliers that account for 45% of purchases. Supplier rebate and term changes, plus product-cost deflation that shrinks rebate dollars, are recurring margin levers that can cut both ways. On balance, the moat is widening faster than these threats are eroding it, and tariff exposure is genuinely low (75%+ domestic sourcing), a differentiator versus import-heavy industrial peers.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality.** Roughly 56% of sales (non-residential 38%, residential 18%) are interest-rate and construction-cycle sensitive. A higher-for-longer rate environment is the primary cyclical risk, with residential lot development already pressured following the FY23 rate hikes.

**SG&A deleverage.** The flat FY25 EBITDA despite gross-margin gains shows this is a present, not hypothetical, headwind. Rising self-insured medical claims are flagged in the filings and are difficult to forecast.

**M&A deceleration.** The drop removes the visible inorganic growth premium and is the single most important trend to watch.

**TRA.** The $720M liability paid to pre-IPO insiders is a structural FCF leak that grows as the Management Feeder exchanges units and can accelerate on change of control.

**Governance.** A classified board, blank-check preferred, no action by written consent, supermajority amendment provisions, and Delaware exclusive forum form a shareholder-unfriendly anti-takeover posture, a legacy of the sponsor structure.

**Goodwill/intangibles** at 46% of assets create impairment risk if growth or cash flows disappoint. Asbestos product-liability litigation is ongoing but ordinary-course and not deemed material.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 79.1% against 41.7% one-year historical vol (a 37.4pp premium), in steep backwardation (79.1% at 1 week, 56.9% at 3 months, 49.8% at 6 months). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.0%, and near-term put/call skew is elevated at 10.8%, signaling demand for downside protection into the next print. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV jumped from 43.2% to 79.1% while the stock rose 14.8% and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.31 to 0.54, so the rally has been accompanied by event hedging rather than fundamental deterioration. The longer-dated skew flips negative (-7.2% at 6 months), suggesting the market sees the stress as concentrated around the imminent catalyst rather than a structural overhang. Short interest is modest at 4.8% of float (3.4 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The read: trust the structure (no durable bearish positioning) but recognize the near-term print carries unusually high binary risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$66, +27%).** M&A re-accelerates as acquisition multiples normalize, IIJA water funding begins flowing in scale, gross margin continues climbing toward 28%, and SG&A deleverage reverses as integration costs roll off. EBITDA resumes growth, the buyback compounds per-share value, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median (~28x) on ~$2.60+ EPS. This returns the stock toward its prior highs.

**Bear case (~$42, -19%).** Higher-for-longer rates deepen the construction slowdown, non-residential and residential volumes contract, SG&A deleverage persists or worsens on medical-claims inflation, EBITDA declines, and goodwill impairment risk surfaces. The multiple compresses toward the low-teens forward and the TRA continues draining FCF. This is consistent with the analyst low target of $40.

**Base case (~$56, +8%).** Modest average-daily-sales growth, continued gross-margin expansion partly offset by SG&A, low-single-digit EBITDA growth, and buyback-aided EPS reaching ~$2.55. The stock holds its ~22x multiple and drifts modestly higher, with total return roughly in line with EPS growth plus buyback.

The insider and alignment signals tilt the balance modestly positive: director James Hope made a ~$100K open-market purchase at $50.70 on April 7, 2026 (the strongest insider signal type), and the CEO's "compensation actually paid" went negative (-$1.77M) in FY25 on downward equity revaluation, evidence the pay structure genuinely flexes with the stock rather than extracting wealth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- M&A deployment re-accelerates to >$300M annualized at disciplined multiples, restoring the inorganic growth engine.
- Adjusted EBITDA returns to growth (>5% YoY) with SG&A as a percent of sales stabilizing or declining, demonstrating the gross-margin gains finally drop through.
- Forward EPS revisions turn positive (>+5% over 90 days) alongside evidence of IIJA water funding flowing into municipal orders.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of declining average daily sales, signaling the construction cycle is rolling over rather than plateauing.
- SG&A deleverage accelerates and Adjusted EBITDA declines YoY, with management guiding margins lower.
- Goodwill impairment charge or a covenant-relevant rise in net leverage above ~2.5x.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report is the immediate checkpoint, followed by the subsequent quarter to confirm whether the M&A pause is temporary or structural. The rate path and FOMC guidance tone bear directly on the cyclical 56% of revenue and should be monitored as a macro overlay.

## Conclusion

Core & Main is a high-quality, defensively positioned distributor on a genuine secular theme, with a structurally expanding gross margin, strong free cash flow, and a long roll-up runway in a fragmented duopoly market. Those qualities support the improvement in the financial-health and competitive-position scores over the past two quarters. The constraints on a stronger call are specific and falsifiable: operating income and Adjusted EBITDA were flat in FY25 because SG&A deleverage offset the gross-margin gains, the M&A engine that drives growth contracted ~90% year-over-year, consensus EPS revisions are flat-to-negative (current-year -3.1% over 90 days), and a $720M Tax Receivable Agreement drains FCF to insiders. With the stock at ~21x forward EPS, a discount to peers partly justified by leverage, the TRA, and the absence of a dividend, and an options market pricing an 11% move into the next print, the risk/reward is balanced. Base-case upside to ~$56 (+8%) roughly matches bear-case downside to ~$42 (-19%) adjusted for probability, which is symmetric rather than the asymmetric setup a BUY requires. The recommendation is HOLD with a constructive growth lean; the path to a BUY runs through re-accelerating M&A, EBITDA returning to growth, and positive estimate revisions.
