## Executive Summary

Par Pacific just printed the best quarter in its history. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $10.10 beat the $8.15 non-GAAP consensus by 23.6%, adjusted EBITDA reached $571.3M against $91.5M in Q1, and GAAP net income attributable to the company was $462.1M. None of it came from the assets. Hawaii throughput fell to 73 Mbpd from 88 Mbpd on a planned turnaround; the entire ~$548M year-over-year adjusted EBITDA increase came from spread. The Hawaii Index — management's bespoke measure of the Singapore 3.1.2 crack less Par's Hawaii crude differential — went from $8.57/bbl to $46.06/bbl. The Q1 10-Q names the cause: the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz in early March 2026, with Brent averaging $99.60/bbl that month.

The balance sheet response has been substantial. Total debt is $739.2M against $185.0M cash and $1.982B of equity, liquidity is approximately $1.4B after a $500M senior unsecured notes offering that retired over $130M of term debt, and a recomputation of Altman Z from audited 10-K inputs yields ~3.9 (the automated screen's ~3.1 reflects a mis-tagged EBIT input; the filing shows $538.8M of FY2025 operating income on $7.46B of revenue, a 7.22% margin). Management retired roughly 10% of the share count during 2025 — from ~55.2M to ~49.7M shares — inside a year whose disclosed trading range was $12.23 to $47.20, and the proxy's company-selected performance measure is Modified Free Cash Flow per Share, an unusually well-aligned metric for a capital-intensive cyclical.

The problem is the price. At $81.32 the stock sits 6.6% below its 52-week high after a 172% twelve-month advance, and the arithmetic bifurcates depending on which earnings base you accept. On trailing four-quarter adjusted EPS of ~$14.15 it trades at 5.7x; on FY2026 consensus of $16.10, 5.1x. On mid-cycle EBITDA — FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $633.5M stripped of the $202.6M small-refinery-exemption windfall, or the three-year average of $455M — the enterprise value of ~$4.59B is 10.1–10.7x, which is not cheap for a mid-cap refiner. Consensus itself models the reversion: the next-quarter estimate of $4.19 is 59% below the quarter just reported, and FY2027 at $11.34 is 30% below FY2026. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on this name, and the score trajectory has been declining (5.9 in May to 5.5 in July). Fundamentals improved materially while the multiple on normalized earnings stayed full.

## Company Overview

Par Pacific is a Houston-headquartered downstream operator with four refineries (187.8 Mbpd combined FY2025 throughput), an integrated logistics network, and a retail fuel/convenience chain, all oriented toward the western U.S. and Hawaii. FY2025 refinery detail:

| Refinery | Throughput (Mbpd) | Adj. Gross Margin/bbl | Ex-SRE Margin/bbl | Production cost/bbl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hawaii | 84.1 | $11.69 | $11.69 | $4.43 |
| Montana (Billings) | 51.7 | $15.83 | $12.78 | $11.11 |
| Washington (Tacoma) | 38.7 | $13.69 | $8.42 | $4.19 |
| Wyoming (Newcastle) | 13.3 | $30.93 | $16.41 | $14.24 |
| **Total** | **187.8** | **$14.60** | **$11.64** | **$6.92** |

Two features distinguish the portfolio. Hawaii is a logistically isolated, vertically integrated position: Par controls the refining capacity, single-point mooring, terminals, pipelines and retail, and competes against imported product rather than neighboring refineries. It is also the largest throughput contributor at the lowest production cost. The trade-off is that Hawaii's margin is set by the Singapore crack and the delivered cost of Brent-linked waterborne crude, which brings Asian refining volatility inside a U.S.-listed equity.

The three mainland refineries are small refineries eligible for Renewable Fuel Standard relief, which is the largest single swing item in FY2025. Alongside the refineries sit the Hawaii Renewables JV (Par 63.5%, Alohi — owned by Mitsubishi and ENEOS — 36.5%; Alohi contributed $100M cash of which $83M was distributed to Par; facility commenced operations April 2026), a $98.7M refining/logistics equity portfolio, and a non-operated 
stake in Laramie Energy carried at $45.0M at 3/31/26, roughly $57M below Par's share of Laramie's underlying net assets.

Working capital is financed through structures that distort the balance sheet's appearance: a Citigroup Inventory Intermediation Agreement under which Citi holds legal title to Hawaii crude and product ($161.5M obligation at 12/31/25), a Citi RIN financing facility (zero drawn at year-end, combined cap $450M), and a Wells Fargo Renewables Intermediation Agreement ($31.3M outstanding). Citi and Wells Fargo are financing counterparties and title holders, not customers.

## Financial Analysis

**Three-year GAAP trajectory ($000s):**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenues | 8,231,955 | 7,974,457 | 7,464,650 |
| Operating income | 680,006 | 47,628 | 538,758 |
| Net income attrib. PARR | 728,642 | (33,322) | 369,391 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 696,247 | 238,676 | 633,516 |
| Operating cash flow | 579,156 | 83,776 | 445,337 |

FY2025 derived metrics: operating margin 7.22%, net margin 4.92%, ROE 24.4%, ROA 9.6%, current ratio 1.61x, debt/equity 0.54x, FCF (OCF less $148.9M PP&E) $296.4M.

**The SRE distortion is the single most important adjustment.** In August 2025 the EPA granted Par's mainland refineries a mix of full (100%) and partial (50%) exemptions from the RFS for compliance years 2019 through 2024, producing a $199.5M GAAP net income gain and a **$202.6M adjusted EBITDA gain** (Washington $74.4M, Wyoming $70.5M, Montana $57.6M). Ex-SRE FY2025 adjusted EBITDA is therefore $430.9M, and refining adjusted gross margin was $11.64/bbl rather than $14.60. Anyone using FY2025 as a base year without this adjustment overstates recurring earnings power by roughly 47%. For Wyoming specifically, $14.52 of its $30.93/bbl margin — 47% — was the exemption.

**H1 2026.** Q1 delivered revenue of $1.824B, adjusted EBITDA of $91.5M and adjusted EPS of $0.78 (a 13.9% miss), with operating cash flow of −$40.7M as crude prices spiked into March. Q2 delivered revenue of $2.969B, adjusted EBITDA of $571.3M, GAAP EPS of $9.35 and adjusted EPS of $10.10. Summing the two: H1 revenue $4.793B, adjusted EBITDA $662.8M, adjusted EPS ~$10.88, operating cash flow $241.9M.

That last figure matters. H1 operating cash flow of $241.9M against $662.8M of adjusted EBITDA is a 36% conversion rate, driven by working-capital build. Q2 alone carried $312.2M of working-capital outflow; management states OCF excluding working capital and turnaround was $614.3M and expects "a substantial portion" of the outflow to reverse. That reversal is a real, identifiable source of cash in H2, but it has not happened yet, and the reported earnings have not yet become cash.

**Quality of the Q2 print.** GAAP operating income of $634.6M included a $41.2M environmental-obligation mark-to-market gain, a $35.7M inventory valuation gain, and a $28.3M unrealized derivative gain — roughly $105M of non-cash or timing benefit, all of which the company itself removes to reach adjusted figures (so the $571.3M adjusted EBITDA is not inflated by them). More consequential is the item that *is* inside adjusted results: Hawaii's $57.00/bbl adjusted gross margin includes a net price-lag benefit of $76.5M, or $11.49/bbl. The arithmetic checks: $76.5M ÷ (73 Mbpd × 91 days = 6.643 MMbbl) = $11.52/bbl. Strip it and Hawaii captured ~$45.51/bbl against a Hawaii Index of $46.06, approximately its benchmark and no more. The lag benefit arises because Hawaii buys waterborne crude on a delivery lag and sells at spot, so it is a *falling-crude* gain. It disappears if crude flattens and inverts if crude rises, which is precisely what depressed Q1 2026 despite a $31.11 Hawaii Index.

**Capital structure.** Long-term debt fell from $1,108.1M (12/31/24) to $797.9M (12/31/25) on $332.5M of net repayments; the term loan was repriced 50bps lower in December 2025. At 6/30/26: total debt $739.2M, term debt $505.7M, cash $185.0M, net term debt $320.7M. Leverage: 1.17x FY2025 reported adjusted EBITDA, 1.72x ex-SRE, 0.56x H1-annualized (a sensitivity, not a forecast). The 2026 capex plus deferred turnaround budget is $190–220M against $250.1M in FY2025, with $2.6B of non-cancelable crude purchase commitments for 2026 delivery.

**The under-modeled item: cash taxes.** The Q4 2023 release of most of the federal valuation allowance produced a $277.7M non-cash deferred tax benefit. FY2025 tax expense of $110.8M included $100.4M of non-cash deferred expense, implying current tax of ~$10.4M on $477.9M pretax income — a ~2.2% cash rate. Q2 2026 tax expense of $144.0M included $122.3M deferred, implying ~$21.7M current on ~$606M pretax (~3.6%). Cumulative deferred expense of roughly $233M against the $277.7M released implies the book shield is 80–85% consumed. Book pretax income is not taxable income, and I have not read the FY2025 tax footnote, so this is an approximation rather than a statement about remaining tax-basis carryforwards. Directionally, cash taxes converge toward statutory rates from here, and at H1-2026 pretax run-rates that is a multi-hundred-million-dollar annual cash cost that did not exist in 2025. It materially reduces the appeal of extrapolating current earnings into free cash flow.

A normalized alternative: ex-SRE EBITDA of $430.9M less ~$205M of capex/turnaround and $45–80M of cash interest implies roughly $150–180M of normalized free cash flow, a ~4% yield on the current market cap before cash taxes bite.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying volume growth is zero. Revenue declined for two consecutive years (FY2023 $8.23B → FY2024 $7.97B → FY2025 $7.46B) and 2026's revenue expansion is price, not throughput; Hawaii volumes fell 17% year over year in Q2 on a turnaround. There are no announced capacity additions. Growth from here comes from four narrower sources:

1. **Hawaii Renewables**, operating since April 2026, with Mitsubishi and ENEOS funding 36.5% of the equity and Par earning operating, construction-management and terminalling fees. Pre-startup it consumed cash — Q1 2026 NCI loss of $8.3M, of which ~$7.1M was the NCI share of non-GAAP adjustments (largely feedstock hedge marks under the Wells Fargo agreement), implying a modest ~$3.3M underlying adjusted operating loss on a pro-rata gross-up. Not yet a contributor.
2. **Per-share growth via buyback.** Retiring ~10% of the equity in one year is real accretion, and a $250M authorization dated 2026-02-21 remains available.
3. **The SRE option on 2025 and 2026 compliance years.** Par accrues 100% of its RFS obligation with zero relief assumed, stated explicitly in both the 10-K and the Q1 10-Q. The FY2025 precedent was $199.5M across six compliance years — roughly $33M per year at then-prevailing RIN prices. A single-year grant at current RIN prices could exceed that, but there is no filing basis for a specific number, and the option is genuinely two-sided: Par also carries a $272.7M RINs/environmental-credit asset against a $266.7M gross obligation, so RIN price moves cut both ways and the net sensitivity is not determinable from disclosure.
4. **Crack durability**, which is the whole question and belongs to the macro, not to Par.

The estimate revision data is large: current year $16.10 (+42.6% over 90 days), next year $11.34 (+62.0%). Revisions of this magnitude are ordinarily a strong forward signal. Here they are not, for two reasons. First, they are analysts marking a spot commodity spread to market rather than discovering structural earnings power — the same mechanical artifact visible across tanker and refining estimate sets this cycle. Second, and decisively, consensus has already built in the reversal: the next-quarter estimate of $4.19 is 59% below the quarter just reported, and FY2027 sits 30% below FY2026. The claim "the market is systematically underestimating the reversion" requires consensus to be extrapolating; instead it is discounting. The earnings surprise record — two beats and two misses in four quarters, including a 13.9% miss in Q1 2026 — confirms that consensus visibility on this name is poor in both directions.

One near-term nuance argues for caution on Q3 specifically. July product cracks were at records (Gulf Coast 3:2:1 above $62/bbl per contemporaneous reporting), which is supportive, but Brent rose roughly 20% during July. Rising crude turns Hawaii's price-lag from a $76.5M tailwind into a headwind — the same mechanism that produced a weak Q1 despite a $31.11 Hawaii Index. Q3 could show a high index and materially weaker capture.

## Valuation Assessment

Using ~49.3–50.1M shares at $81.32, market cap is $4.01–4.08B. Enterprise value = ~$4.03B + $739.2M debt − $185.0M cash ≈ **$4.59B**, treating inventory-financing obligations as working capital in nature.

| Basis | EBITDA | EV/EBITDA |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 as reported | $633.5M | 7.2x |
| FY2025 ex-SRE | $430.9M | 10.7x |
| Three-year average (FY23 / FY24 / FY25 ex-SRE) | $455.3M | 10.1x |
| H1 2026 annualized *(sensitivity only)* | $1,325.5M | 3.5x |

Earnings multiples at $81.32: 5.7x trailing four-quarter adjusted EPS of ~$14.15; 5.1x FY2026 consensus $16.10; **7.2x FY2027 consensus $11.34**. Price/book is ~2.0x against $1.982B of equity (~$40/share).

Peer context is of limited use — three of the five listed comparables carry negative P/Es and one (IEP) is a diversified holding company. PBF at 10.8x is the closest read, and the peer median of 15.3x is distorted upward by REX. The more useful benchmark is Par's own history and the sector's normal treatment of peak-cycle refining earnings: mid-cap refiners do not sustain double-digit EV/EBITDA on normalized numbers, and they do not hold 5x multiples on peak numbers either. Both readings are true simultaneously, and which one governs depends on crack persistence.

Consensus implies roughly $1.35B of cumulative net income over 2026–2027, about a third of today's market cap. Capitalize what follows at a mid-cycle $4.50–6.00 of EPS and 8x, and terminal value is $36–48/share plus retained cumulative earnings. That path arrives in the $70s. To justify $81.32 and more, mid-cycle cracks must be structurally higher than the 2024–25 baseline. There are arguments for that — damage to Russian refining capacity, global capacity attrition, robust demand — but I could not verify capacity-closure specifics from filings, and the two Reuters pieces closest to the event both explicitly frame the windfall as temporary, with the August 3 headline reading "Iran war ushers in oil refining golden era. It won't last."

What genuinely is unpriced: the 2025/2026 SRE option (perhaps $2–4/share at the FY2025 per-year precedent, with wide error bars), the ~$57M Laramie basis difference (~1.4% of market cap), and the working-capital reversal (cash, not earnings). Against those sit the cash-tax step-up, the reversing price-lag benefit, and a hedge book that is net short roughly 8.2 million barrels out to June 2027 with ~$52.5M of net derivative liabilities, meaning realized hedge losses offset part of any further physical windfall.

Analyst targets: low $65, high $100, mean $83, median $85. The stock is at the mean. Consensus targets function as floors only when a stock is cheap relative to *rising* forward growth; here forward growth is negative by consensus's own hand, so proximity to $83 is a genuine constraint rather than a stale anchor. Goldman's move to $92 from $77 (single source, note unread) is the most bullish datapoint and implies 13% upside.

**Valuation score: 4.8.** Cheap on trailing and current-year, full on any normalized basis, with roughly symmetric-to-negative asymmetry from here. A low headline multiple on peak-cycle commodity earnings is the archetypal value trap, and I am not treating it as a buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Par's advantage is logistical isolation, not scale or complexity. Its refineries run 13–84 Mbpd, and management's own 10-K language describes "logistically complex, niche markets" where each asset has "unique cost advantages and disadvantages as compared to their respective relevant market indices." The 2025 decision to construct four bespoke regional indices — incorporating local product pricing, lagged crude differentials, and management estimates of delivery, yield-loss, tax and tariff costs — is itself an admission that no standard benchmark describes these assets, and it means capture-rate comparisons against peers are not apples-to-apples.

Hawaii is the real moat: freight economics plus control of terminals, pipelines and retail insulate it from refinery competition. The offset is direct exposure to Asian product spreads and Brent-linked feedstock. Q2 illustrated the asymmetry: the ANS-minus-Brent differential widened to +$13.07 from +$3.67, a $9.40/bbl feedstock cost headwind, and was swamped by a ~$36/bbl improvement in the product crack.

The mainland assets serve inland and Pacific Northwest markets on discounted heavy slates (WCS Hardisty at −$11.34 to WTI in FY2025) and are where the SRE optionality lives. Wyoming is the highest-beta asset: $14.24/bbl production cost and 47% of its FY2025 margin from the exemption.

Trajectory is mixed. The renewables facility adds a capability with third-party capital funding the majority of remaining spend. Retail, which was the stabilizer in the FY2024 trough at $76.0M of adjusted EBITDA, moved backward in Q2 — $17.3M versus $23.3M a year earlier. That is the one segment going the wrong way, and it is the segment that matters most when cracks normalize. **Competitive position: 5.7.**

## Risk Assessment

**Crack reversal is the dominant risk and it is not diversifiable.** The entire 2026 step-up traces to one disclosed event. Hawaii Index moved 5.4x year over year with no change in the asset base.

**Specific, company-identified, unquantified items:**
- **EPA Consent Decree.** The Q1 2026 10-Q discloses an EPA letter alleging violations under the Hawaii Consent Decree and states resolution "will likely involve financial penalties or impose capital expenditure requirements that could be material."
- **Labor.** A collective bargaining agreement covering refinery employees expired January 31, 2026 and was operating on rolling 24-hour extensions as of the Q1 filing. No resolution has been disclosed in materials reviewed; I could not verify which bargaining unit or location.
- **Litigation.** Pacific Current (defective naphtha, November 2025), ExxonMobil (Yale Oil remediation, December 2025), a Hawaii foreign-trade-zone False Claims Act matter, and Honolulu property tax appeals.
- **Single-asset concentration.** The February 2025 Wyoming incident idled the smallest refinery for 66 days, cut logistics gross margin by $4.1M and pushed Wyoming production cost to $14.24/bbl from $8.10. Hawaii at 84.1 Mbpd is the far more consequential single point of failure, and it just completed a turnaround.
- **Market-structure cost.** Brent M1–M3 backwardation of $6.76 in Q2 (vs. $1.14 average in FY2025) raises the cost of the Citi intermediation structure; Q1 market-structure fees were $15.4M versus $4.5M a year earlier. Steep backwardation is a recurring cash cost of Par's financing model.
- **Feedstock policy.** The 10-K devotes unusual space to tariffs, including the February 2026 substitution of a Section 122 global tariff at 15% and a January 2026 announced 25% tariff on purchasers of Iranian oil. For a Hawaii importer of waterborne crude, this is not abstract.
- **Cash-tax step-up** as the deferred shield depletes, discussed above.
- **Hedge book** net short ~8.2 MMbbl to June 2027, capping upside participation.

**Verification gaps I am carrying explicitly:** all Q2 2026 figures are issuer-provided from the earnings release, not a filed 10-Q; the $500M notes' coupon, maturity and covenants are unknown, so pro forma interest expense cannot be computed; remaining buyback authorization could not be cleanly reconciled; and the remaining federal NOL balance is unverified.

**Risk factors score: 4.2.** The count and specificity of unquantified company-identified items, layered on a binary macro dependence, is unusual.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 61.8% against 56.3% one-year realized is rich (spread 5.5pp, 76th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is backwardated (61.8% at one month versus 56.7% at six) — the market is pricing near-term event risk with the August 21 tenor implying ±10.6%. Positioning is bullish, not defensive: put/call skew is call-heavy at −9.4% and open-interest put/call is 0.09, the most call-tilted reading I would expect to see on a name that just delivered a peak print. ATM IV has *fallen* 2.4pp since July 19 while the stock rose 7.1%, which is complacency rather than hedging.

Set against 12.7% short interest and 4.7 days to cover, the combination cuts both ways: crowded call ownership means a Q3 capture disappointment has little cushion, while the short base provides squeeze fuel if July's record cracks translate into another beat. The signal that matters for a fundamental view is the absence of downside protection at a cycle peak — the options market is not corroborating a mean-reversion thesis, and it is not hedged against one either.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Refining capacity attrition is structural, not episodic — Russian capacity damaged, global closures reducing supply — so mid-cycle cracks reset permanently above the 2024–25 baseline. FY2027 consensus of $11.34 proves conservative at $14–15. The 2025 compliance-year SRE is granted, delivering a discrete gain outside every model. The $312.2M working-capital outflow reverses in H2, net debt approaches zero, and management deploys the $250M authorization plus incremental cash into buybacks against a shrinking float. Hawaii Renewables contributes. At 8x $14 that is roughly **$112**, and analyst high is $100 with Goldman at $92. Call it **$110**, +35%.

**Base case.** Cracks retreat from July records but hold above the 2024–25 baseline through 2027 as demand stays firm and supply-side impairments partially persist. FY2027 lands near or modestly below consensus at $10.00–11.34. Working capital reverses, net debt falls, buybacks continue at a moderate pace, cash taxes step up. At 7.5–8x $10.50–11.00 that is **$84**, +3.3% from $81.32 — essentially the consensus median of $85.

**Bear case.** Hormuz normalization holds, OPEC+ completes its cut rollback, and the Singapore crack reverts toward the FY2025 environment during H1 2027. Hawaii's price-lag turns negative on rising crude, hedge losses realize against the net short book, the EPA Consent Decree lands with a capex mandate, and cash taxes arrive just as EBITDA falls. Mid-cycle EBITDA of ~$450M at 6x EV/EBITDA less ~$400M net debt yields roughly **$50**, near 1.25x book. That is −38%.

The distribution is not attractive at $81.32. The base case is flat, the bull case requires a macro judgment I cannot verify from filings, and the bear case is 38% down with a mechanism that is well understood and already flagged by the sell-side reporting closest to the event.

**What keeps this a HOLD rather than an AVOID.** The business is not impaired: it is delevering, generating cash, buying back stock at accretive prices historically, and carrying a live regulatory option worth real money. Near-term earnings momentum is positive and July cracks were at records, so the Q3 print (early November) could beat the $4.19 estimate. Shorting a company with $1.4B of liquidity, 12.7% short interest and call-heavy positioning into that setup is a poor trade.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** three dated checkpoints. (1) The Q2 2026 10-Q filing, to verify the release figures and read the $500M notes terms. (2) The EPA's 2025 compliance-year SRE determination, tied to the ~September 1 RFS compliance deadline. (3) The Q3 2026 print in early November, which is the first clean test of whether high cracks survive rising crude and what Hawaii captures against its index without a price-lag tailwind.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A granted 2025 compliance-year SRE with a quantified benefit of $100M or more, which would validate that exemptions are recurring economics for three of four refineries rather than a one-time windfall.
- Price in the $55–60 range with FY2027 consensus still above $10 — roughly 5.5x forward and near 1.4x book, where the mean-reversion downside is largely paid for.
- Verified evidence of structural refining capacity attrition (named, dated closures) sufficient to support a mid-cycle Hawaii Index above $20/bbl versus the $8.57 of Q2 2025.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Singapore 3.1.2 crack sustained below $20/bbl or Hawaii Index below $15/bbl for a full quarter.
- EPA Consent Decree resolution imposing material capital expenditure requirements, or a labor disruption at Hawaii.
- Aggressive buyback execution above $80 — repurchasing at ~2.0x book on peak-cycle cash flow would reverse the capital-allocation case that currently supports the management score — or re-leveraging above 2.5x ex-SRE EBITDA.
- An unplanned Hawaii outage of more than 30 days.

**Position guidance:** hold existing exposure through the Q3 print; do not add at $81.32. The specific reason not to add is Q3 capture risk — Brent rose roughly 20% during July, which converts Hawaii's $76.5M price-lag benefit into a headwind, the same mechanism that produced Q1's 13.9% miss against a $31.11 index.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Par Pacific has done nearly everything a cyclical operator should do at the top of a cycle: cut debt from $1.11B to $739M, refinance into unsecured notes, reprice the term loan, retire 10% of the share count at $12–47 per share, bring in Mitsubishi and ENEOS to fund 36.5% of a renewables project while taking $83M of cash out at closing, and hold the RFS obligation at 100% with no exemption assumed. Financial health (6.8) and management quality (6.5) are the strongest dimensions here and are the reason this is not an AVOID.

But the earnings that produced a 172% twelve-month return are a spread event, not an asset event, and the numbers that make the stock look cheap are the ones least likely to repeat: $10.10 of quarterly adjusted EPS including an $11.49/bbl Hawaii price-lag benefit that requires crude to keep falling, against a normalized ex-SRE EBITDA base of $430.9M that puts the enterprise at 10.7x. Consensus has already marked FY2027 down 30% from FY2026, the stock sits at the mean analyst target, and a 4% normalized free-cash-flow yield is about to face a cash-tax step-up as an 80–85%-consumed deferred shield runs out. Score of 5.4, continuing a declining trajectory (5.9 → 5.5 → 5.4), driven this cycle by better financials against a fuller price on normalized earnings, not by any deterioration in the business.

The place to buy Par Pacific was $12–47, when management was buying. The place to buy it again is the mid-$50s or a confirmed SRE grant, not 6.6% below an all-time high on the best quarter the company has ever reported.
