**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Intuit has fallen roughly 62% from its July 2025 high of $813.70 to about $290, a de-rating that stands in sharp contrast to its operating results. Over the first nine months of fiscal 2026 (year ends July 31), revenue grew 14% to $17.1B, operating income rose 18% to $5.4B, net income grew 20%, and diluted EPS rose 22% to $15.05. The collapse traces almost entirely to one line in the Q3 10-Q, "fewer TurboTax federal units," which confirmed that price-sensitive do-it-yourself tax filers defected during the 2026 season, and to a broader fear that generative AI will commoditize both tax preparation and bookkeeping.

The dislocation matters because the weakness is concentrated in the smallest, lowest-margin slice of the company. TurboTax DIY sits inside a Consumer segment that grew 10% in aggregate, buoyed by higher-value TurboTax Live and a 22% re-acceleration at Credit Karma. Global Business Solutions (QuickBooks Online, payments, payroll, mid-market Enterprise Suite, and Mailchimp) grew 17% at a 77% segment operating margin and now represents roughly 55% of revenue. The core compounder is intact and accelerating, while the market is pricing a thesis break that the consolidated financials do not yet show.

The counter-argument deserves respect. AI is a genuine, unresolved threat to Intuit's most contestable products, the DIY erosion may be structural rather than a one-season pricing misstep, an early-stage securities-fraud litigation cluster is unquantified, and ~$19B of goodwill and intangibles (48% of assets) faces its annual impairment test in fiscal Q4. At ~12x forward non-GAAP EPS the stock is cheap by its own history, but "cheap" software names losing their most contestable franchise to a structural shift have repeatedly proven to be value traps over the past two years. We rate INTU a HOLD with a constructive lean: the risk/reward is improving as price falls on intact-to-improving core fundamentals, but the structural AI question and litigation overhang keep us from a BUY until DIY stabilization is visible in reported results.

## Company Overview

Intuit is a financial-technology platform incorporated in 1984 and headquartered in Mountain View, California. Since 2019 management has repositioned the company as an "AI-driven expert platform" built on its proprietary GenOS generative-AI operating system. Effective August 1, 2025, Intuit reorganized into two reportable segments:

- **Global Business Solutions (GBS)** — ~55% of nine-month FY26 revenue. QuickBooks Online and Desktop, Intuit Enterprise Suite (mid-market), QuickBooks Payroll and Time, payments, QuickBooks Capital lending, and Mailchimp. This is the growth engine and highest-quality franchise, running a 77% segment operating margin.
- **Consumer** — TurboTax (DIY plus TurboTax Live assisted/full-service), Credit Karma personal-finance lead generation, and ProTax (Lacerte, ProSeries, ProConnect for accountants). Heavily seasonal, with revenue concentrated November–April.

Roughly 87% of revenue is recurring service revenue, and ~93% of sales are U.S. The competitive advantage is a large base of financial data, an integrated product ecosystem under a single customer identity, and a network of AI-enabled human experts. The main structural vulnerability is that the year's outcome is concentrated in a single tax season, which magnifies any single-season execution error, precisely what occurred in FY26.

## Financial Analysis

Operating performance remains strong despite the equity drawdown.

- **FY2025:** Revenue $18.8B (+16%), net income $3.87B, diluted EPS $13.67, FCF margin ~32%.
- **9-mo FY2026:** Revenue $17.1B (+14%); operating income $5.4B (+18%); net income $4.2B (+20%); diluted EPS $15.05 (+22%).
- **Q3 FY2026 (qtr ended 4/30/26):** Revenue $8.56B (+10%); operating income $4.02B (+8%); EPS $11.09 (+11%).

Segment detail for the nine months underscores where the health sits. GBS grew 17% to $9.44B, led by QuickBooks Online Accounting +24% and Online Services +17% (money +$327M, payroll +$204M). Consumer grew 10% to $7.65B: TurboTax +7% (growth in assisted/Live offset by fewer DIY federal units) and Credit Karma +22% to $1.9B, driven by personal loans, cards, and insurance.

Cash generation is a defining strength. Nine-month operating cash flow rose 29% to $7.5B, helped by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted 7/4/25) restoring immediate R&D expensing, which cut deferred-tax assets from $1.22B to $0.11B and boosted FY26 cash flow. The balance sheet carries $6.8B cash and investments against ~$6.2B total debt ($5.0B senior notes plus $1.2B non-recourse secured facilities), well inside the 4.0x gross-debt/EBITDA covenant. Leverage is appropriate for a recurring-revenue software franchise.

The caveat is asset composition: $14.0B goodwill plus $4.8B intangibles equals ~$18.8B, or roughly 48% of $39.3B total assets, the legacy of the ~$8B Credit Karma and ~$12B Mailchimp deals. Tangible book is thin, and the Altman Z of ~2.5 reflects that intangible-heavy structure rather than distress. Any future impairment of the Credit Karma or Mailchimp carrying values would produce a large non-cash charge, with the annual test occurring in fiscal Q4.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one narrow in-line print over the last four quarters, including an 11% beat in the March quarter and a 5.8% beat in December.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth thesis rests on GBS and Credit Karma, not consumer tax. QuickBooks Online Accounting growth and mid-market Enterprise Suite adoption indicate Intuit is still taking share in small-and-medium-business software, payments, and payroll, categories with far less regulatory and competitive fragility than consumer DIY tax. Credit Karma's re-acceleration reflects a friendlier consumer-lending backdrop for personal loans, cards, and insurance after prior macro-driven softness.

Estimate revisions are stable-to-modestly positive rather than deteriorating, which is the more telling signal given the price action. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been revised +13.5% over 90 days (to $3.59), current-year EPS +2.6% (to $23.83), and next-year +3.2% (to $27.33). This is a stable estimate picture against a collapsing multiple rather than the falling-estimate profile that typically accompanies a genuine fundamental break. The +13.5% current-quarter revision is meaningful, but it does not reach the signal threshold (>30% over 90 days with accelerating revenue) that would flip this to a high-conviction buy; revenue growth is healthy but decelerating from mid-teens, not accelerating off a trough.

The growth risk is durability at the consumer end. If AI self-service and free/low-cost or government-funded filing continue eroding DIY federal units, TurboTax's contribution flattens and the company becomes increasingly a GBS-plus-Credit-Karma story. Management is explicitly leaning into this by trading commodity DIY volume for higher-value TurboTax Live and opening physical TurboTax stores, an omnichannel push toward assisted filing. Whether that shift fully offsets DIY attrition is the central open question in the growth case.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$290 on trailing FY25 GAAP EPS of $13.67, INTU trades at ~21x trailing GAAP earnings; on consensus FY26 non-GAAP EPS of $23.83 it trades at ~12x forward, and ~11x FY27 non-GAAP of $27.33. Against Intuit's own multi-year history in the 30–40x range, this is a decade-low valuation for a business still compounding revenue in the mid-teens and non-GAAP EPS mid-to-high-teens.

Peer comparison is noisy because the "Application Software" cohort mixes EDA and observability names (Synopsys 108x, Cadence 89x, Datadog 614x) that are poor comps, alongside better analogues Adobe (12.8x) and Autodesk (30x). INTU at ~12x forward sits at the low end of that spread, near the level where the market has already priced structural stress into Adobe, another mature software franchise facing an AI-disruption debate.

The case for a high valuation score requires identifying why the market is wrong, and there is a specific, falsifiable argument: the de-rating is concentrated in the smallest, lowest-margin segment (consumer DIY) while the majority of enterprise value (GBS at 77% margins plus a re-accelerating Credit Karma) is growing double digits, estimates are rising not falling, and consolidated EPS grew 22%. The consensus median target of $443 and mean of $463 imply the sell-side views the multiple compression as excessive.

We temper this for two reasons. First, cheapness alone is not a buy signal, and mature application-software names caught in a structural AI narrative have repeatedly re-rated lower even when they looked inexpensive on forward earnings; the compressed multiple can reflect impairment rather than opportunity. Second, the AI-commoditization question is genuinely unresolved; if it is correct, forward estimates will fall and the multiple will not re-rate. On balance the reset is real and the mispricing thesis is specific, so valuation earns a moderately high score, but the structural tail prevents a maximum reading. We anchor a base case around $400 (roughly 15x FY27 non-GAAP), a bull case near $520 on DIY stabilization plus re-rating, and a bear case near $230 if AI erosion and DIY attrition confirm.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is bifurcating, and trajectory rather than level is what matters. In GBS, Intuit is widening its lead: QuickBooks Online growth, growing payments and payroll attach, and mid-market penetration via Enterprise Suite, all defended by data scale, an integrated ecosystem, and an expert network. This franchise is improving.

In consumer tax, the moat is compressing. Price-sensitive simple filers are the most contestable customers, exposed to free and low-cost private alternatives, potential government-funded filing (the IRS Direct File system was suspended but proponents persist, and IRS Free File remains), and AI self-service. The "fewer TurboTax federal units" admission confirms share loss at the commodity end. Intuit's response of migrating value toward TurboTax Live assisted/full-service and physical stores is a deliberate trade of low-margin volume for higher-value expertise, but it concedes the low end rather than defending it.

AI is the two-sided variable. The bear reading is that generative AI automates tax and bookkeeping, eroding both TurboTax and QuickBooks. The bull reading is that Intuit's proprietary data plus its human-expert network make it a net AI beneficiary through its GenOS platform. Both are plausible, and the market is currently pricing the bear reading. On net, the improving GBS trajectory is roughly offset by the deteriorating consumer-tax trajectory and the unresolved AI threat, leaving competitive position slightly below neutral.

## Risk Assessment

- **TurboTax DIY erosion (central issue).** The repeated 10-Q acknowledgment of fewer federal units is the trigger for the drawdown. If this proves structural rather than a one-season pricing error, the consumer-tax growth base flattens.
- **Securities-fraud litigation.** Multiple law-firm investigations and class actions (BFA Law and others, filed May–July 2026) allege Intuit misrepresented TurboTax's DIY price positioning. Early-stage and unquantified, but a genuine overhang; management calls the claims meritless.
- **AI commoditization.** The double-edged risk above; the 10-Q added extensive AI-specific risk language (model bias, IP validity, EU AI Act, state AI laws).
- **Public-sector competition.** Government-funded free filing remains a category risk even after the Direct File suspension.
- **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** ~$19B carrying value tested in fiscal Q4; a depressed valuation environment raises the odds of a non-cash Credit Karma/Mailchimp charge.
- **Regulatory.** The Fifth Circuit vacated the FTC "free advertising" order on 3/20/2026 and remanded, a procedural win, but the matter is unresolved. A 2014 FTC security order over Credit Karma runs through ~2034.
- **Seasonality.** Concentration in the November–April window magnifies any single-season misstep, exactly as FY26 demonstrated.

Offsetting these, the stock has already fallen 62%, so much of the bad news is priced. That creates asymmetric recovery upside if DIY stabilizes, but it does not neutralize the binary risks (litigation outcome, impairment charge, structural DIY decline).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 72.1% against 47.5% one-year historical volatility (rich, at the 79th percentile of the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (72.1% near-term versus 55.9% at six months), which typically flags an expected near-term catalyst. Skew is modestly call-heavy at the front (-3.5%) but turns put-heavy at the 3-month tenor (-7.4%), and put/call open interest sits at 1.19. ATM IV rose 6 points since early June even as the price fell 5.7%. The market expects a large move and is hedging into the next print and the litigation/impairment calendar. Rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry and favors selling premium over buying it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The sell-off is a narrative overshoot concentrated in the smallest, lowest-margin segment. A director bought ~$542K of stock in the open market at ~$309 (above today's price), executives terminated scheduled sale plans in March 2026, and the board authorized an incremental $8B buyback. If DIY stabilizes and the AI-beneficiary thesis holds via GenOS, the multiple re-rates toward the high-teens on ~$27 FY27 non-GAAP EPS, implying $490–550.

**Bear case.** The DIY erosion is structural: AI self-service and free/government filing permanently compress the most contestable segment, and AI eventually pressures QuickBooks bookkeeping as well. Estimates begin to fall, the litigation cluster produces a settlement and reputational damage in the DIY category, a goodwill impairment lands at the fiscal-Q4 test, and the multiple stays at 10–12x on flat-to-declining earnings. In that scenario the stock drifts to $230 or lower.

The base case sits between: DIY attrition slows but does not reverse, GBS and Credit Karma keep compounding, the multiple recovers modestly to ~15x FY27 non-GAAP, and the stock works back toward $400 over 12–18 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-lean). Reassessment timeframe: 6–12 months, anchored to the August FY26 Q4 print (annual impairment test and 2026-Plan restructuring completion) and the FY27 tax season starting November.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q4/Q1 disclosure showing TurboTax federal units stabilizing or returning to growth, confirming the DIY misstep was one-season rather than structural.
- Estimate revisions turning to +15% or more over 90 days on current/next-year EPS, or GBS growth sustaining at 17%+ with QBO above 20%.
- Resolution or dismissal of the securities-fraud actions without a material charge.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Consensus current-year non-GAAP EPS revised down more than 5%, signaling the erosion is spreading beyond consumer DIY.
- QuickBooks Online growth decelerating below ~15%, indicating AI/competitive pressure reaching the core compounder.
- A goodwill impairment materially above the low end of expectations, or a securities-fraud settlement with a large accrual.

**Base case target: ~$400 (≈ +38% from ~$290)** — 15x FY27 non-GAAP EPS of ~$27, a partial re-rating from ~12x that assumes DIY stabilization without full recovery to historical multiples.
**Bull case target: ~$520 (≈ +79%)** — high-teens multiple on confirmed DIY stabilization plus AI-beneficiary re-rating.
**Bear case target: ~$230 (≈ -21%)** — 10–12x on flat-to-declining estimates if structural erosion confirms.

## Conclusion

Intuit presents a dislocation between price and reported fundamentals: a ~62% drawdown against 14% revenue growth and 22% EPS growth, with the entire de-rating hinging on the weakest, smallest segment. The core franchise (QuickBooks/GBS at 77% margins and a re-accelerating Credit Karma) is compounding, estimates are stable-to-positive, the balance sheet is cash-rich, and insiders are buying in the open market. At ~12x forward non-GAAP earnings, the downside appears substantially priced and the risk/reward has improved as the stock has fallen on intact core results.

We stop short of a BUY because the two questions that drove the sell-off remain unresolved: whether the TurboTax DIY erosion is structural, and whether generative AI ultimately commoditizes Intuit's franchises rather than strengthening them. Both are genuine, and history shows mature software names caught in a correct AI-disruption theme can re-rate lower even while screening cheap. With an early-stage securities-fraud overhang and a fiscal-Q4 impairment test ahead, the prudent stance is a constructive HOLD. We would move to BUY on evidence that DIY units are stabilizing and estimates are turning higher; we would move to AVOID if the erosion spreads to QuickBooks or estimates begin falling.
