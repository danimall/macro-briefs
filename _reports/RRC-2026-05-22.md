## Executive Summary

Range Resources is a pure-play Appalachian natural gas and NGL producer trading at 10.9x trailing/forward earnings after reporting a Q1 2026 that saw net income surge 252% YoY to $341.6M. The company has reduced net debt from $2.7B (2021) to $834M (Q1 2026) while guiding for modest production growth to 2.35–2.40 Bcfe/day for full-year 2026. With NYMEX gas averaging $4.97/mcf in Q1 2026 and RRC's natural gas basis differential turning positive at +$0.21/mcf (before basis hedging) in Q1 — versus -$0.35 for full-year 2025 — the unit economics improved materially in the quarter.

The stock trades at a discount to gas-weighted peers, which may partly reflect geographic concentration risk (100% Pennsylvania) and commodity sensitivity. The market may also be underweighting the potential for improved Appalachian takeaway capacity to compress basis differentials over time. With current-year EPS estimates at $4.29 (revised up 23.5% over 90 days) and next-year at $4.67, the forward multiple on what could be a structurally improved gas market is attractive.

The primary risk remains binary commodity exposure — a return to sub-$3 gas would compress margins given $1.63/mcfe transportation costs — but the structural demand drivers (LNG exports, data center baseload power) and RRC's hedging strategy that leaves 2027 substantially unhedged create asymmetric exposure to continued gas price strength.

## Company Overview

Range Resources operates exclusively in the Marcellus Shale in southwestern Pennsylvania, where it was one of the original horizontal developers. The company's proved reserves total 18.1 Tcfe (PV-10: $11.6B), split approximately 65% natural gas, 34% NGLs, and 1% oil. Q1 2026 production was ~2.21 Bcfe/day, with management guiding full-year 2026 production of 2.35–2.40 Bcfe/day (up from 2.24 Bcfe/day in 2025) on a capital budget of $650-700M.

RRC differentiates through cost discipline. Direct operating expenses of $0.13-0.14/mcfe are among the lowest in U.S. E&P. The company has achieved MiQ A-grade certification on all Pennsylvania production and maintained net-zero Scope 1 & 2 GHG emissions since 2024. The operation is capital-efficient with decades of remaining inventory in a single concentrated basin — no portfolio management overhead, no wildcatting.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**
Revenue recovered from $2.35B (2024) to $2.99B (2025), driven by NYMEX rising from $2.27/mcf to $3.43/mcf. Q1 2026 production sales annualize to approximately $4.0B on NYMEX averaging $4.97/mcf. Net income jumped from $266M (2024) to $658M (2025) to $341.6M in Q1 2026 alone.

**Cash Flow Inflection:**
GAAP operating cash flow of $619M in Q1 2026 (vs. $330M Q1 2025) included a $79.5M working-capital inflow; cash flow before working-capital changes was $544.9M. Capital expenditures were $167.7M per the cash-flow statement ($139M per the earnings release, depending on definition), yielding Q1 free cash flow of approximately $377M–$452M depending on capex and working-capital treatment. Full-year 2025 FCF was approximately $530M ($1.17B operating CF less ~$640M in property additions, acreage, and field service capital per the cash-flow statement). Annualizing Q1 2026 FCF would imply approximately $1.5–1.8B, though this is best understood as a spot-quarter figure benefiting from elevated gas prices, a positive basis differential, favorable working-capital timing, and front-loaded NGL realizations — not a normalized run rate.

**Balance Sheet Transformation:**
- Net debt: $2.7B (2021) → $1.2B (Dec 2025) → $834M (Q1 2026)
- January 2026: Redeemed $600M of 8.25% senior notes, eliminating high-coupon debt and reducing the annual interest burden (gross coupon savings of ~$50M/year, partially offset by floating-rate borrowings on the credit facility used to fund the redemption; $334M was outstanding on the bank line at quarter-end)
- Remaining debt: $334M bank line + $500M 4.75% notes due 2030
- Liquidity: ~$1.5B available under $2.0B credit commitment
- Interest expense dropped from $0.15/mcfe to $0.10/mcfe, with management guiding 2026 net interest of $0.07–$0.09/mcfe

**Capital Returns:**
2025 shareholder returns totaled $316M ($231M buybacks + $86M dividends). The February 2026 authorization increase to $1.5B (with the full amount remaining) signals management's confidence. At current prices, full execution would retire ~37M shares (16% of outstanding). Q1 2026 repurchases were $27M (800,000 shares), relatively modest despite strong cash flow — buyback authorizations are discretionary and pace depends on management judgment, commodity prices, and competing capital needs.

**Unit Economics (Q1 2026):**
- All-in realized price: $3.21/mcfe
- Total cash costs (ex-interest): ~$2.00/mcfe
- Interest: $0.10/mcfe
- Breakeven (maintaining production): approximately $2.50-2.75/mcf NYMEX equivalent

## Growth Analysis

RRC is pursuing modest production growth — management guides 2026 production of 2.35–2.40 Bcfe/day versus 2.24 Bcfe/day in 2025, representing approximately 5–7% growth. Q1 2026 production of 2.21 Bcfe/day suggests a back-half ramp. Growth also accrues through:

1. **Potential gas price realization improvement:** Q1 2026 natural gas basis differentials turned positive (+$0.21/mcf before basis hedging, +$0.18/mcf including hedges), reflecting favorable seasonal dynamics and improving Appalachian takeaway infrastructure. Management's full-year 2026 guidance still projects natural gas differentials of NYMEX minus $0.35 to $0.45/mcf, suggesting Q1's positive basis may not persist through all quarters.

2. **Structural demand drivers:** LNG export capacity additions, AI/data center baseload power demand, and Pennsylvania's RGGI withdrawal (removing a carbon pricing headwind for in-state gas consumption) may support regional gas demand over time. RRC's 10-Q notes that longer-term gas futures remain constructive based on market expectations of LNG export expansion and increasing global power demand.

3. **Per-share growth via buybacks:** If $1.5B is deployed over 2-3 years, per-share metrics could grow 5-8% annually from share count reduction alone.

4. **Oil production surge:** Q1 2026 oil volumes jumped 75% YoY (8,239 bbl/day vs. 4,706), adding higher-value barrels from liquids-rich development.

5. **Interest savings:** The $600M high-cost note redemption materially reduces the interest burden, though the net benefit is partially offset by credit-facility borrowings used to fund the redemption.

EPS estimates have been revised up 23.5% over 90 days (current year from $3.47 to $4.29) and 10.3% for next year ($4.23 to $4.67). This positive revision velocity reflects the gas price environment and improved realizations, though the 30-day current quarter revision turned slightly negative (-8.7%), likely reflecting gas price softening from Q1 peaks.

## Valuation Assessment

At $40.90, RRC trades at:
- 10.9x trailing/forward P/E
- ~9.5x current-year earnings ($4.29)
- ~8.8x next-year earnings ($4.67)
- 0.83x market-cap-to-PV-10 of proved reserves ($11.6B total PV-10; on an EV basis including ~$834M net debt, EV/PV-10 is closer to ~0.90x)

**Peer comparison:** RRC's 10.9x P/E sits below certain gas-weighted peers. Direct peer valuation comparisons require careful normalization for gas/NGL mix, balance sheet leverage, hedge profile, inventory depth, and basis exposure — factors that may partially justify RRC's discount.

**Specific mispricing thesis:** The market may not be fully incorporating the potential for sustained basis differential improvement. RRC historically traded at a discount partly because Appalachian gas traded at persistent negative basis to Henry Hub (-$0.35 in 2025). Q1 2026 showed a positive differential for the first time, reflecting infrastructure buildout. Management's own full-year 2026 guidance still projects negative differentials of -$0.35 to -$0.45/mcf, suggesting the company itself views the Q1 result as potentially seasonal or transitory. If basis improvement proves durable over multiple quarters — driven by continued LNG and pipeline capacity additions — it would represent a meaningful rerating catalyst.

**Buyback support:** The $1.5B authorization at 16% of market cap provides potential per-share upside — management has economic incentive to buy aggressively below intrinsic value, particularly with strong FCF generation, though execution pace and timing remain discretionary.

The analyst consensus mean target of $47.14 (median $46) represents 15% upside, with a high target of $59.

## Competitive Landscape

RRC holds a structural cost advantage in Appalachian gas production:
- Direct operating costs of $0.13-0.14/mcfe are among the lowest in U.S. E&P
- Decades of remaining core drilling inventory (vs. inventory exhaustion concerns at many Permian/Haynesville operators)
- Diversified market access via extensive pipeline commitments (though these carry fixed-cost risk)
- ESG credentials (MiQ A-grade, net-zero since 2024) increasingly relevant for LNG export contracts requiring certified gas

The competitive position may be improving due to:
- Basis differential improvement as new takeaway infrastructure comes online (though sustainability is uncertain)
- Peer inventory depletion in other basins driving higher industry marginal costs
- Data center co-location opportunities with Pennsylvania power generators

The 545% five-year TSR vs. 319% for the peer group validates management execution over this period.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Binary (HIGH):** RRC is fundamentally a leveraged bet on natural gas prices. At $2.50/mcf NYMEX, the company barely covers all-in costs including transportation. A return to 2024 pricing ($2.27/mcf) would eliminate FCF entirely. The structural thesis (LNG + data centers) must hold.

**Geographic Concentration (MODERATE):** 100% Pennsylvania operations create regulatory tail risk. The Cecil Township setback expansion (500ft → 2,500ft from residences, 5,000ft from hospitals/schools) and statewide setback petition (accepted for rulemaking review Dec 2025) could constrain future drilling. A hydraulic fracturing ban — however unlikely — would render the entire reserve base uneconomic.

**Transportation Commitment Overhang (MODERATE):** $5.9B in minimum volume commitments (~9x annual FCF at 2025 rates) are largely fixed. If production were curtailed for any reason, these obligations become a cash drain. Rising electricity costs are also pushing per-unit transportation costs higher ($1.63/mcfe in Q1 2026, up from $1.55/mcfe).

**Hedging Drag (NEAR-TERM):** Q1 2026 hedges cost $49.3M. Remaining 2026 swaps at ~$4.06/mcf and collars with $5.06 ceilings on 614K Mmbtu/day cap near-term upside. 2027 is substantially unhedged (only 350K Mmbtu/day vs. ~1.5M Mmbtu/day production), creating significant positive asymmetry if prices hold.

**Short Interest (MODERATE):** Short interest is elevated (approximately 10.9% of float with approximately 5.2 days to cover based on recent exchange data), reflecting commodity sensitivity concerns. This creates potential for squeeze dynamics on positive catalysts.

### Options Market Signal

Based on recent options data, near-term IV sits modestly above historical vol, with the term structure in slight backwardation. Put/call volume ratios appear call-heavy, while open interest is more balanced. The overall picture is one of modest uncertainty premium without acute stress — consistent with a stock whose fate depends on commodity prices that could move materially either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($52-59, 27-44% upside):**
Natural gas prices sustain at $4.50-5.50/mcf through 2026-2027, driven by LNG export growth and data center demand. Basis differentials remain improved relative to historical levels. RRC generates $1.2-1.5B+ annual FCF, deploys $500M+/year in buybacks, and grows per-share earnings to $5.50-6.00 by 2027. Multiple re-rates to 10-11x forward as the market recognizes structural gas demand improvement.

**Bear Case ($28-33, 20-31% downside):**
Natural gas prices revert to $2.50-3.00/mcf on mild weather, oversupply from Haynesville, or demand disappointment. FCF collapses to near-zero. Buyback slows. Multiple compresses to 8x on lower earnings of ~$2.00-2.50/share. Geographic concentration or regulatory action in Pennsylvania creates additional discount. Transportation commitments become a cash drain.

**Base Case ($47-50, 15-22% upside):**
Gas prices average $4.00-4.50/mcf over the next 12 months. Basis differentials improve modestly from historical levels but remain negative per management guidance. RRC generates $900M-1.1B annual FCF, executes $300-500M in buybacks, and trades at 10-11x $4.50-4.70 EPS = $47-52.

## Conclusion

RRC merits a BUY recommendation at $40.90. The investment thesis centers on the view that Appalachian basis differentials are on a path toward structural improvement driven by LNG and pipeline infrastructure buildout — Q1 2026's positive differential of +$0.21/mcf offers early evidence, though management's own full-year guidance still projects negative differentials of -$0.35 to -$0.45/mcf. The thesis requires monitoring for confirmation over subsequent quarters.

The balance sheet transformation, $1.5B buyback authorization, positioning for structural natural gas demand growth, and guided production growth provide multiple avenues for value creation. The risks — commodity binary, geographic concentration, and elevated transportation obligations — are known and reflected in the peer-relative discount. At current prices, the stock offers 15-37% upside against 27% bear-case downside, with strong FCF generation providing capital return support.

Position sizing of 2-3% reflects commodity sensitivity and geographic concentration risk.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The report's central non-consensus thesis — that Appalachian basis differentials have structurally shifted positive — is not supported by RRC's own full-year 2026 guidance (which projects -$0.35 to -$0.45/mcf). The annualized Q1 FCF yield of ~18.7% reflects a seasonally strong quarter with $4.97/mcf NYMEX, positive basis, and working-capital tailwinds that management does not guide to persist. These corrections reduce confidence in the magnitude of near-term upside.
