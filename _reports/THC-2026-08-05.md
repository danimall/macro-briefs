## Executive Summary

Tenet reported Q2 2026 results on July 24 that were strong on almost every visible axis: net operating revenues of $5,628M (+6.8%), segment Adjusted EBITDA of $1,304M (+16.3% excluding contract-termination revenue), Hospital Operations margin expansion of 240bp to 18.0%, adjusted EPS of $6.12 against roughly $4.26 of consensus, and a FY2026 guidance raise of approximately $295M of Adjusted EBITDA and ~20% of adjusted EPS. HCA and Community Health Systems guided *down* in the same reporting window. The stock moved from $199.02 to a $263.60 high in ten sessions and now sits at $253.33.

Our prior report rated THC a BUY at 6.4 with the stock near $195. That call worked. We are stepping down to HOLD not because of the price move in isolation — a 30% advance accompanied by a 16-19% upward revision in consensus EPS means the forward multiple expanded only modestly, and a re-rating driven by rising estimates is not evidence against further appreciation. We are stepping down because of what the disclosures now reveal about *composition*. Of the $295M EBITDA raise, approximately $140M is additional supplemental Medicaid revenue; $92M of Q2's Medicaid revenue was out-of-period, tied to prior periods. Against a $139M year-over-year increase in Hospital Operations Adjusted EBITDA, that $92M represents 66% of the segment's reported growth. Supplemental Medicaid state-directed payments and provider taxes are precisely what the One Big Beautiful Bill Act caps beginning in 2027, alongside scheduled Medicaid DSH cuts from October 1, 2027 and a proposed increase in the 340B remedy offset from 0.5% to 3.0% per year for CY2027. Tenet's own 10-K states it "cannot estimate the OBBBA's impact."

The market is not ignoring this. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $20.62 sits *below* FY2026's $21.22 even though the buyback is shrinking the share count 7-8% per year, implying analysts already embed roughly a 10% decline in attributable net income. At 12.1x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, 7.2x EV/EBITDA including all noncontrolling interests, and an ~8.6% attributable free cash flow yield, the shares are reasonably valued rather than mispriced. Our base case of $285 offers +12.5%; a 2027 cliff scenario supports roughly $180, or -29%. That is a balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable payoff at a 52-week high, with the lowest published analyst target ($250) essentially at spot.

## Company Overview

Tenet operates two segments. **Hospital Operations and Services** comprised 50 acute care and specialty hospitals with 12,494 licensed beds across eight states at December 31, 2025, plus 132 outpatient facilities, and includes **Conifer Health Solutions**, a revenue-cycle-management business that became wholly owned effective January 1, 2026 when CommonSpirit's 23.8% interest was redeemed. Texas (15 hospitals) and Arizona (8) dominate; roughly 74% of the segment's outpatient centers sit in those two states.

**Ambulatory Care (USPI)** held interests in 538 ambulatory surgery centers and 26 surgical hospitals at June 30, 2026 across 37 states, of which 413 were consolidated and 151 equity-method. The majority are joint ventures with physicians and health systems, operated under management services contracts.

The economic split is the essential framing. In Q2 2026 Ambulatory produced $1,388M of $5,628M revenue (24.7%) but $542M of $1,304M segment Adjusted EBITDA (41.6%), at a 39.0% margin against Hospital Operations' 18.0%. Tenet is a high-margin, JV-structured ASC platform attached to a mid-margin, reimbursement-exposed acute-care portfolio, and because a material share of ASC economics belongs to physician partners, consolidated headline metrics overstate what accrues to THC shareholders. Roughly $800M per year of cash leaves the system as distributions to noncontrolling interests.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025:** net operating revenues $21,310M; operating income $3,508M; interest expense $821M; net income attributable to Tenet $1,407M (GAAP diluted EPS ~$15.49). Company-defined Adjusted EBITDA was $4,566M, up from $3,483M in 2021, a 31% cumulative increase achieved while the hospital count shrank through divestitures. That is genuine evidence the mix shift and portfolio pruning have created operating value.

**Q2 and 1H 2026.** Revenue $5,628M in the quarter and $10,996M in the half. GAAP diluted EPS of $9.84 (Q2) and $17.81 (1H) is inflated by "revenue from contract termination" of $413M and $826M respectively, tied to the CommonSpirit settlement. Adjusted EBITDA excludes that revenue: $1,304M in Q2 (+16.3%) and $2,466M in the half (+8.0%). Backing out the after-tax termination benefit gives underlying 1H adjusted EPS of roughly $10.55, consistent with reported Q2 adjusted EPS of $6.12 plus an implied Q1 of ~$4.45.

**Cost performance is the cleanest positive in the file.** Same-hospital salaries, wages and benefits fell 170bp to 44.7% of revenue; supplies fell 60bp to 14.5%; all-in operating expense per adjusted admission *declined* 0.3% to $15,941. Holding unit cost flat against legislated California healthcare minimum-wage step-ups through 2028 and persistent clinical labor scarcity is a real accomplishment that owes nothing to Medicaid timing. Tenet's ~5,600-employee Global Business Center in the Philippines is a structural cost lever not all peers operate at that scale.

**Cash flow.** 1H26 operating cash flow of $2,226M less $348M capex gives $1,878M of consolidated free cash flow. Two adjustments are required: $540M of that operating cash flow is the one-time CommonSpirit consideration received as an offset, and $398M of distributions went to noncontrolling partners. Attributable 1H free cash flow is therefore roughly $940M. On the same construction, FY2025 attributable free cash flow was approximately $1,760M, or $21.86 per share on the current count — an **8.3% attributable FCF yield at $253.33**.

**Balance sheet at June 30, 2026.** Long-term debt $13,248M; cash $2,170M; net debt $11,078M. Weighted average effective rate 5.7%; **all debt fixed-rate except an undrawn $1.900B revolver**; debt fair value at 99.8% of carrying value; in compliance with all covenants. Maturities: $45M in 2026, $1,643M in 2027, $2,422M in 2028, $1,457M in 2029, $3,488M in 2030. There is no near-term wall.

Leverage depends on how you treat the JV structure:

| Measure | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Net debt / FY2026E Adj. EBITDA | 11,078 / 4,930 | 2.25x |
| Net debt / Adj. EBITDA after NCI distributions | 11,078 / ~4,134 | 2.68x |
| (Net debt + redeemable NCI) / EBITDA after NCI | 13,221 / 4,134 | 3.20x |

The middle and right columns are the honest ones, because consolidated EBITDA captures 100% of JV profit while roughly $800M of cash exits annually, and $2,143M of redeemable noncontrolling interests in the mezzanine section carries put rights settleable in cash. Interest coverage is comfortable on any basis: FY2025 $4,566M / $821M = 5.6x; 1H26 6.0x.

An Altman Z-Score near 1.57 will screen this company into a distress zone. That reading is driven by low retained earnings relative to assets ($4,415M against $29,677M, a legacy of historical write-offs), asset turnover of ~0.72 against an $11.4B goodwill-heavy intangible base, and a market-equity-to-liabilities ratio depressed because roughly $4.0B of enterprise value sits in noncontrolling interests rather than THC's market cap. Against fixed-rate debt, an undrawn revolver, 5.6x coverage and debt at par, this is a signal about thin accounting equity and structural leverage — a real constraint on the ability to absorb a reimbursement shock while sustaining a $1.4B annual buyback — not a solvency warning. Separately, the fall in the current ratio from 1.76 to 1.41 is almost entirely deferred revenue rising from $88M to $963M, a liability extinguished by recognizing revenue rather than paying cash; reading it as liquidity deterioration would be an error.

**Financial Health: 6.4.** Strong cash generation and coverage, no refinancing cliff, all-fixed-rate debt, but honest leverage near 2.7-3.2x, thin book equity, and $2.1B of put-style claims limit the capacity to absorb 2027 policy compression while returning ~$1.4B per year to shareholders.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance was raised to approximately $22.2B of revenue, $4.83-5.03B of Adjusted EBITDA (midpoint $4.93B, +8.0% over FY2025's $4,566M) and roughly $21.00 of adjusted EPS. Estimate revisions have been strong and broad: current quarter +14.0% over 90 days, next quarter +16.6%, current year +19.8%, next year +16.1%. The earnings surprise record is four consecutive beats of 10.9%, 14.8%, 14.1% and 43.6%.

That revision velocity is a legitimate positive signal, and the *out-year* rose 16.1% — analysts did not simply pull earnings forward. But three qualifications keep our growth score below prior levels.

**First, composition.** The CFO attributed the $295M raise to ~$97M of first-half fundamental outperformance, ~$58M of expected second-half momentum and ~$140M of additional supplemental Medicaid revenue. Within Q2, Medicaid DSH and other supplemental revenue rose $96M ($351M to $447M) while managed care revenue rose 1.9%, and $92M of Medicaid revenue was out-of-period. Netting the out-of-period amount against the $139M segment EBITDA increase implies underlying Hospital Operations growth closer to $47M, or +7.5%, versus the headline +22%. Management's claim of "a clean beat even without these incremental Medicaid revenues" is a statement about beating *guidance* and is not inconsistent with our year-over-year arithmetic. Out-of-period supplemental settlements are also a recurring feature of hospital accounting — states routinely settle late — so some catch-up is normal. Both things are true, and the net read is that the operating improvement is real but roughly a third the size the headline suggests.

**Second, volume quality is deteriorating beneath the revenue line.** Q2 same-hospital admissions rose 2.3% but inpatient surgeries fell 1.9%, total surgeries fell 0.7%, total outpatient visits were +0.1% and *paying* outpatient visits were **-0.9%**. Charity and uninsured admissions rose 19.1%; uninsured and charity care costs were $167M against $146M; managed care fell to 67.3% of net patient service revenue from 70.2%. Management quantified a $65M Q2 EBITDA headwind from exchange turbulence, with exchange revenue down 17% and exchange admissions down 13.5%, and said lost exchange volume converts to uninsured "pretty much one-to-one." Volume is being held; payer mix is not.

In Ambulatory, same-facility cases were **-1.2% in Q2 and -0.6% in the half**, with all revenue growth coming from +6.3% revenue per case. Tenet ceased operations at eight ASCs in 1H26 while opening four de novo and acquiring eight. Segment margin was flat-to-down (39.0% vs 39.2%). Deliberately trading low-acuity for high-acuity cases mechanically reduces counts while raising revenue per case, and that is a legitimate strategy consistent with the data, but flat margin on +6% price/acuity means cost is absorbing the mix benefit, and the highest-multiple part of the company is not currently compounding on organic volume.

**Third, 2027 steps down.** The 10-Q's unsatisfied fixed-fee RCM performance obligations show $1,213M in 2H 2026 falling to $91M per year for 2027-2030, a stark illustration of the Conifer/CommonSpirit contract concluding by December 31, 2026 (originally scheduled through 2032). That table is fixed-fee only and is not Conifer backlog or total Conifer revenue, so the magnitude of the 2027 Conifer EBITDA loss cannot be quantified from primary filings. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $20.62 against FY2026's $21.22 implies a ~10% decline in attributable net income after a ~7% share-count reduction.

**Growth Prospects: 5.9.** Strong near-term momentum and revision velocity, tempered by policy-sensitive composition, negative organic volume in both segments' quality metrics, and a consensus out-year that declines.

At the authoritative $253.33:

| Attributable FCF yield | ~8.3% |
| Dividend | none |

Against peer medians of 17.6x P/E, THC looks discounted. That comparison needs care: the peer set is heterogeneous (Encompass 17.6x, Ensign 24.0x, National Healthcare 24.9x, Option Care 16.2x), and the closest structural comparable — Universal Health Services at 6.1x and 39% below its 52-week high — is the more informative reading. Acute-care hospital equities are being derated on exchange attrition and the 2027 statutory pipeline; UHS's multiple and the HCA and CHS guidance cuts are the market's expression of that. THC trades at roughly twice UHS's earnings multiple, which we think is justified by the 39%-margin ASC platform contributing 41.6% of EBITDA, but it means THC is the *expensive* name within its own sub-cohort, not the cheap one.

The bull arithmetic is straightforward: an 8.3% attributable FCF yield plus a 7-8% annual reduction in share count produces a mid-teens owner's return if EBITDA merely holds flat. The bear arithmetic is equally straightforward: if 2027 Adjusted EBITDA falls 12-15% on the combination of state-directed payment caps, DSH cuts, the 340B offset rising to 3.0%, the full-year lap of exchange attrition, and the loss of CommonSpirit RCM economics, adjusted EPS lands near $16.50-17.50 and a 10x multiple prints $165-185.

We do not think there is a specific, identifiable mispricing here. The market has already re-rated the shares 57% over twelve months and moved them 30% in ten sessions on the earnings event; consensus targets bracket spot narrowly (low $250, mean $279.86, median $281, high $309); and the 12x multiple is a rational discount for an unquantified legislated step-down rather than an error. Analyst targets tend to function as floors on stocks that are cheap relative to rising estimates, a condition partly met here (12x forward, estimates +16-19%), but the lowest target sitting exactly at spot argues the sell side sees this as fairly valued rather than lagging.

**Valuation: 6.2.** Reasonably priced on forward earnings and cash flow with genuine yield support; asymmetry has narrowed materially since our July report as the price rose 30% against a 16% estimate increase.

## Competitive Landscape

Tenet's disclosed advantages are facility density within operating areas, which strengthens managed-care network negotiation, and USPI's ability to recruit physician partners into JVs. The site-of-care shift is the durable tailwind: the proposed CY2027 rule contemplates +2.4% for ASCs and +7.4% average net OPPS increases for proprietary hospitals, consistent with continued CMS support for outpatient migration, and Tenet's 538-center ASC platform is well positioned for it.

The disadvantages Tenet names itself are substantial. Not-for-profit and government competitors carry tax-exempt financing, sales/property/income tax exemptions and 340B discounted drug pricing. Physician-owned freestanding ASCs, EDs, imaging centers and urgent care have "increased significantly" in Tenet's markets. The filing explicitly anticipates competition from health insurers and private equity acquiring providers, meaningful for a company deriving ~70% of Hospital Operations net patient service revenue from managed care, with the top 10 payers at 69% of that (66% in 1H26) and national payers at 50%. The revenue mix is shifting toward Ambulatory, but that is not payer diversification: concentration is roughly flat and both segments sell to the same national payers.

Site-neutral payment is the policy that would most directly compress the hospital-outpatient-department premium underpinning part of the ambulatory arbitrage, and the 10-K flags it. South Carolina's hospital certificate-of-need program sunsets January 1, 2027, easing Tenet's expansion at Piedmont and Fort Mill while opening those markets to entrants.

**Trajectory read:** Hospital Operations margin expanded 240bp on flat unit cost and Tenet raised guidance while two larger peers cut — evidence of relative operating outperformance. Offsetting that, payer mix is deteriorating, paying outpatient visits are negative, and ASC same-facility cases are negative with eight closures in six months. **Competitive Position: 6.0.**

## Risk Assessment

**The 2027 reimbursement cliff is legislated, multi-pronged, and unquantified by the company.** OBBBA's most consequential provisions — caps on state-directed payments, limits on provider taxes, Medicaid work requirements, stricter eligibility checks — take effect beginning 2027, and Tenet states it cannot estimate the impact. Medicaid DSH cuts are scheduled to commence October 1, 2027. The July 2026 proposed OPPS/ASC rule would raise the 340B remedy offset from 0.5% to 3.0% annually beginning CY2027 through CY2029. Partially offsetting proposed updates (+7.4% OPPS proprietary, +2.4% ASC, +1.0% IPPS) are proposals, not final rules. This is the dominant unpriced-magnitude risk, and it lands directly on the line item that carried the Q2 beat.

**Non-expansion-state concentration amplifies the ACA subsidy expiry.** Over half of licensed beds sit in Florida, South Carolina, Tennessee and Texas, none of which expanded Medicaid; enhanced premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025. The 1:1 uninsured conversion management described means the 2027 comparison laps a partial-year 2026 headwind and gets worse before stabilizing, absent congressional action management explicitly declines to predict.

**JV structure creates cash claims and legal exposure.** Redeemable NCI of $2,143M carries put rights that could require cash or additional debt. The 10-K discloses that some JV arrangements do not qualify for anti-kickback safe harbors and that penalties or exclusions imposed on USPI's partners could adversely affect Tenet.

**Counterparty credit on the CHI note.** The $1.36B non-interest-bearing promissory note (three ~$453M payments due January 2027, 2028, 2029; carried at $1,242M present value, Level 3) is unsecured obligation of a not-for-profit system operating in the same reimbursement environment. No collateral, guarantee or acceleration provisions were verified.

**Concentration and labor.** Fifteen of 50 hospitals in Texas; ~74% of segment outpatient centers in Arizona and Texas; unionization at 27 hospitals concentrated in California, Florida and Michigan; California nurse-ratio mandates that the 10-K says force admission limits at times; legislated California healthcare minimum-wage increases through 2028. The Desert Regional Medical Center lease expires May 30, 2027 with a new 30-year lease-purchase following.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (March 30 and April 10, 2026) and two Item 1.01/2.03 filings (November 2025) were not retrievable. The CEO and CFO both certified the Q2 10-Q, ruling out changes in those roles. Whether the FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance formally excludes contract-termination revenue was established to high confidence through three independent cross-checks but not read directly from the company's non-GAAP definition; if it in fact included termination revenue, the forward multiple and the entire 2027 bridge change materially.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 42.5% sits essentially on top of 42.0% trailing realized volatility (spread 0.4%, IV percentile 5% within the chain), and ATM IV has collapsed from 57.6% pre-earnings to 42.5%; the event risk has been discharged. Put/call skew is balanced at -0.3% near-term, widening modestly to -3.3% at the six-month tenor, and the term structure is in contango (42.5% near versus 45.4% at six months). The put/call open-interest ratio fell from 1.35 to 0.26 across the earnings event. Short interest is 4.3% of float at 2.2 days to cover.

The read: the options market prices no near-term dislocation and has removed downside protection following the beat, while the six-month tenor carries slightly richer vol and a more negative skew, consistent with the 2027 policy calendar being the market's identified risk window rather than the next print. Cheap near-term vol relative to a stock 3.9% off its high reflects complacency rather than a bullish signal; for a holder, replacing some equity exposure with longer-dated protection is inexpensive relative to realized vol.

**Risk Factors: 4.8.** Multiple dated, legislated 2027 catalysts whose magnitude the company itself declines to quantify, layered on payer concentration, a deteriorating payer mix already visible in Q2, and $2.1B of put-style JV claims.

## Management Quality

Capital allocation has been aggressive and buyback-dominated, and the execution has been good. Shares outstanding fell from 95,109K (12/31/24) to 80,507K (6/30/26) — **15.4% in eighteen months** — at an average of $158.03 in FY2025 and ~$193.70 in 1H26 against today's $253.33. The board added $2.0B of authorization in June 2026, the same month Tenet repurchased 1,644K shares at an average $167.20. We would not read that timing as demonstrated foresight; the pattern (nothing in January, February or April; heavy May-June) is equally consistent with scheduled 10b5-1/ASR execution and a calendar-driven authorization refresh. The compensation design deserves credit on one specific point: the free-cash-flow metric is defined *net of cash distributions to noncontrolling interests*, which is the economically correct measure for a JV-heavy operator and is not standard among peers. Five-year TSR of $497.67 per $100 invested versus $148.36 for the peer group is a genuine record.

Two offsets. CEO summary compensation of $43.1M for FY2025 (from $24.7M) and "compensation actually paid" of $149.4M at a $20-21B market cap is at the high end of the sector; the qualitative grant discussion was not reviewed, so whether a one-time grant drove the 75% increase is unverified. And every open-market insider transaction reviewed was a sale, with a cluster immediately after the July 24 pop: COO Foo sold 10,000 shares at $258.03 (≈24% of her position), CIO Arbour 10,878 at $243.90 (≈37%), and directors Bierman and Lynch sold on July 27. Insider selling is a weak signal in isolation and is routinely diversification-driven, particularly against +16-19% estimate revisions and record results; neither C-suite seller crossed 50% of holdings, and whether these executed under pre-existing 10b5-1 plans could not be verified. But four insiders selling within three days of a 20% single-day move, with no offsetting purchases, is relevant alongside a decision to deploy 61% of 1H operating cash flow into repurchases five months before a legislated reimbursement step-down.

**Management Quality: 6.2.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $320 (+26%)

Q2's cost performance is the durable core: SWB down 170bp, supplies down 60bp, expense per adjusted admission down 0.3% against California wage escalation. Apply that to an ASC platform at 39% margins contributing 41.6% of EBITDA and site-of-care migration supported by proposed CY2027 updates of +2.4% for ASCs and +7.4% for proprietary hospital outpatient payment, and the mix shift keeps compounding. Final CY2027 rules could land better than the proposals suggest, enhanced premium tax credits could be restored legislatively, and state-directed payment arrangements could be grandfathered or phased more gradually than feared. The $1.36B CHI note delivers ~$453M of cash in each of January 2027, 2028 and 2029 — roughly 26% of estimated FY2025 attributable free cash flow — that cushions the cliff and appears nowhere in EBITDA. Ambulatory M&A exceeding $300M in 2026 plus continued repurchase at 7-8% of shares annually compounds per-share value even on flat EBITDA. $320 is 15.2x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of $21.00, above the high published target of $309.

### Bear Case — $180 (-29%)

2027 absorbs four simultaneous hits: OBBBA caps on state-directed payments and provider taxes against a supplemental Medicaid run-rate that just carried two-thirds of Hospital Operations' EBITDA growth; DSH cuts from October 1; the 340B offset tripling to 3.0%; and a full-year lap of exchange attrition converting to uninsured at 1:1, with charity/uninsured admissions already +19.1% and paying outpatient visits negative. Simultaneously, the CommonSpirit RCM contract concludes, with fixed-fee obligations dropping from $1,213M in 2H26 to $91M per year. Adjusted EBITDA falls 12-15%, adjusted EPS lands near $16.50-17.50, and the multiple compresses to 10x as the market applies the treatment it has already given UHS (6.1x, -39% from high). $180 is roughly 10.5x $17.25. Balance-sheet capacity to defend the buyback through that is limited at honest leverage of 2.7-3.2x.

### Base Case — $285 (+12.5%)

Underlying operations continue improving at the demonstrated ~7-8% Hospital Operations pace; 2027 policy compression is real but partially offset by favorable final Medicare updates, the CHI cash inflows, ambulatory M&A and a 7% share-count reduction; adjusted EPS holds roughly flat near $20.50-21.00 in 2027. $285 is 13.6x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of $21.00 and 13.8x FY2027 consensus of $20.62, or ~7.7x EV/EBITDA on the all-NCI construction, consistent with the $279.86 consensus mean and $281 median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** We see modest appreciation to $285 over twelve months with dated catalysts, but not enough asymmetry to add at a 52-week high.

**Base case $285** (13.6x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS $21.00) — **+12.5%** from $253.33.
**Bull case $320** (15.2x $21.00) — **+26.3%**.
**Bear case $180** (10.4x a compressed ~$17.25) — **-29.0%**.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q3 2026 print (late October), the final CY2027 OPPS/ASC rule (typically early November 2026), the FFY2027 IPPS final rule, initial FY2027 guidance (early February 2027), and the ~$453M CHI note payment due January 2027.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**
1. Management issues FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance at or above $4.7B (i.e., a decline of less than ~5% from the $4.93B FY2026 midpoint), quantifying OBBBA/DSH/340B exposure as manageable; or
2. Final CY2027 rules preserve the proposed +7.4% OPPS / +2.4% ASC updates without a harsher 340B offset, *and* enhanced premium tax credits are restored in any form; or
3. The shares trade back toward $200-215 (roughly 9.5-10.2x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS) with FY2027 consensus holding above $20, restoring 30%+ upside to base case; or
4. USPI same-facility case volume returns to positive for two consecutive quarters alongside sustained 39%+ segment margin.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:**
1. FY2027 guidance implies Adjusted EBITDA below $4.3B (a >12% decline), or management quantifies OBBBA impact above ~$400M of EBITDA;
2. Supplemental Medicaid revenue declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters without offsetting operating improvement, or a subsequent quarter reveals that out-of-period Medicaid catch-ups were required to meet guidance;
3. The buyback is suspended or materially curtailed to preserve balance-sheet capacity — the clearest signal management sees the cliff as larger than disclosed;
4. Uninsured/charity care costs exceed ~$200M in a single quarter (vs $167M in Q2 2026), or managed care falls below 65% of net patient service revenue;
5. Any deterioration in CHI note collectibility, or an impairment/reserve against the $1,242M carrying value.

**Next natural reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report in late October, which will show a second full quarter of exchange attrition, whether supplemental Medicaid normalizes, and whether ASC same-facility cases stabilize.

## Conclusion

Tenet is a better-run company than it was three years ago, and the evidence for that is specific: Adjusted EBITDA up 31% from 2021 to 2025 on a smaller hospital footprint, unit cost per adjusted admission declining while California wages rise, a 39%-margin ASC platform now supplying 41.6% of segment EBITDA, and a 15.4% share-count reduction in eighteen months executed at prices well below today's. Guidance was raised while HCA and Community Health Systems cut theirs.

What we cannot underwrite at $253.33 is the 2027 bridge. Roughly $140M of the $295M guidance raise and 66% of the year-over-year Hospital Operations EBITDA increase trace to supplemental Medicaid revenue, the exact category OBBBA caps beginning in 2027, alongside DSH cuts in October 2027, a proposed tripling of the 340B offset, a full-year lap of exchange attrition converting to uninsured at 1:1, and the conclusion of the CommonSpirit RCM contract. The company states it cannot estimate the OBBBA impact; consensus has responded by modeling FY2027 EPS below FY2026 despite a shrinking share count. At 12.1x forward adjusted EPS, 7.2x EV/EBITDA and an 8.3% attributable free cash flow yield, the price embeds a rational discount for that uncertainty rather than an error we can exploit. With +12.5% to base case against -29% to a plausible bear case, the lowest published analyst target sitting at spot, near-term implied volatility at the 5th percentile of its own chain, and four insiders selling into the post-earnings move, the correct action is to hold what you own, decline to add, and revisit at the Q3 print or on a pullback toward $200-215.

### Valuation and Price Discipline

- Current price: $253.33
- Fair value: $285.00
- Margin of safety: +12.5%
- Initiate/add below: $215.00
- Reduce/exit above: $305.00
- Rationale: Base value 13.6x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of $21.00, consistent with the $281 consensus median; thin cushion against a 2027 reimbursement compression scenario near $180.

**Thesis:** Tenet is a 39%-margin ASC platform attached to a reimbursement-exposed acute-care portfolio, compounding per-share value via a 15% eighteen-month share-count cut and ~8.5% attributable FCF yield. At 12x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and 7.2x EV/EBITDA the market already discounts a legislated but unquantified 2027 step-down: OBBBA state-directed payment caps, DSH cuts, a tripled 340B offset and the CommonSpirit RCM roll-off. Fair, not mispriced.

**Variant view:** Consensus reads the guidance raise as operating leverage; roughly $140M of the $295M raise and 66% of segment EBITDA growth are supplemental Medicaid, so underlying hospital growth is ~7.5% not 22%. Conversely, ~$453M annual CHI note cash in 2027-2029 cushions the cliff outside EBITDA.

### Catalysts

- **High — 3-4 months:** Final CY2027 OPPS/ASC rule (typically early November) sets the 340B offset and outpatient updates, resolving part of the 2027 bridge.
- **Medium — ~3 months:** Q3 2026 print in late October shows a second full quarter of exchange attrition and whether supplemental Medicaid and ASC same-facility cases normalize.
- **Medium — 6-7 months:** First ~$453M CHI note installment in January 2027 plus initial FY2027 guidance in early February quantify the cliff and cash cushion.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **6-7 months:** FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance below ~$4.3B, or management quantifies OBBBA impact above ~$400M of EBITDA.
- **3-9 months:** Supplemental Medicaid revenue declines sequentially two consecutive quarters without offsetting operating gains, or out-of-period catch-ups are needed to meet guidance.
- **0-12 months:** Buyback suspended or materially curtailed to preserve balance-sheet capacity, signaling management sees a larger cliff than disclosed.
