## Executive Summary

Portland General Electric is an Oregon-based vertically-integrated electric utility serving approximately 960,000 customers in the Portland metropolitan area, including Hillsboro, one of the nation's top data center markets. The company is experiencing structural demand growth, with industrial energy deliveries growing 17.3% YoY in 2025 and five data center contracts totaling 430 MW executed in 2025-2026. Revenue grew 35% from 2022 to 2025, and management guides for $3.33-$3.53 adjusted EPS in 2026, representing 9-16% growth.

The investment case is complicated by three interrelated issues: (1) the $1.9B PacifiCorp Washington acquisition announced in February 2026, which transforms the company's risk profile and requires complex multi-party financing; (2) persistent equity dilution (shares outstanding grew 24% in three years) that has suppressed per-share returns despite strong operating growth; and (3) Oregon's lack of wildfire liability protection, which creates a material unquantified tail risk. At approximately 14.9x 2026E midpoint earnings with a 4.1% dividend yield, POR trades at a 32% discount to its peer median P/E of 22.2x, but this discount partially reflects legitimate structural risks that may not resolve quickly.

## Company Overview

PGE generates, transmits, distributes, and retails electricity across a 4,000 square mile service territory in northwestern Oregon. The company serves approximately two-thirds of Oregon's commercial and industrial activity. Its generation portfolio is diversified: 43% thermal (natural gas + coal), 28% hydro, 13% wind, and 5% solar, with a 20% ownership share in the Colstrip coal plant (fully depreciated as of December 2025).

Oregon's HB 2021 mandates 80% greenhouse gas reduction by 2030, 90% by 2035, and 100% by 2040, creating a mandatory multi-decade capital investment cycle. Simultaneously, the Portland metro's emergence as a leading data center market provides an organic demand catalyst that most utilities lack. PGE has partnered with GridCARE to deliver 80+ MW of data center capacity in 2026, targeting 400+ MW by 2029.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Operating Performance:** Revenue reached $3.56B in 2025, up from $2.64B in 2022 (10.5% CAGR). Operating income grew from $397M to $555M over the same period (11.8% CAGR). These are strong numbers for a regulated utility and reflect both rate increases and genuine demand growth from the industrial segment.

**Earnings Quality:** GAAP net income declined slightly in 2025 ($306M vs. $313M in 2024) due to $42M in business transformation expenses and the expiration of Tucannon wind production tax credits. Adjusted EPS was $3.05, down from $3.14. A record-warm December reduced Q4 earnings by approximately $0.17/share, contributing to the -26.1% Q4 earnings miss ($0.47 vs. $0.64 expected).

**Dilution Problem:** Shares outstanding grew from 89.3M to 110.7M over 2022-2025, a 24% increase driven by at-the-market equity offerings to fund capex. This is the single largest drag on per-share returns. With $300M more equity planned for 2026 and additional dilution from PacifiCorp acquisition financing, this trend will continue through at least 2028.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow improved significantly to $1.118B in 2025, up from $420M in 2023. Free cash flow improved from -$938M to -$71M over the same period. However, the five-year capex plan totals approximately $7.6B ($1.655B in 2026 alone), ensuring continued external financing needs.

**Balance Sheet:** Total assets of $13.2B, long-term debt of $4.66B, equity of $4.13B, producing a D/E ratio of 2.2x. The common equity ratio of 47% is below the 50% target. Moody's rates PGE at A3, S&P at BBB+ (both Stable). The Altman Z-Score of 1.12 is misleading for a regulated utility; the model systematically underscores asset-heavy, debt-intensive regulated businesses. The investment-grade ratings with stable outlooks are the relevant metric.

**Under-Earning:** Actual ROE of 7.4% versus the authorized 9.34% indicates PGE is under-earning relative to its allowed return. This is typical during heavy capex cycles where rate base grows faster than rate adjustments. As recent rate cases take effect and capex moderates (projected decline from $1.74B in 2027 to $1.35B in 2028), ROE should converge toward the authorized level.

## Growth Analysis

PGE's growth story rests on three pillars:

**1. Data Center Demand (Primary Driver):** Industrial energy deliveries grew 17.3% YoY in 2025. PGE has executed five data center contracts totaling 430 MW. The Hillsboro market is structurally advantaged by power availability, low natural disaster risk (relative to other Pacific Northwest locations), and fiber connectivity. Management targets 2.5-3.5% weather-adjusted energy delivery growth annually. Industrial demand has compounded at 10% CAGR since 2020.

**2. Rate Base Growth:** The $7.6B five-year capex program grows rate base, which directly drives allowed earnings for a regulated utility. Transmission investment ramps from $215M in 2026 to $525M by 2030. Each dollar of rate base earns the authorized ROE (currently 9.34%), providing mechanical earnings growth as projects enter service.

**3. PacifiCorp Acquisition:** The $1.9B acquisition adds 140,000 customers, 477 MW of gas generation, 328 MW of wind, and 4,500 miles of T&D lines. Management expects accretion in the first full year. If successfully integrated, this adds a second regulatory jurisdiction and diversifies the customer base.

**2026 Guidance:** $3.33-$3.53 adjusted EPS (9-16% growth). Long-term guidance: 5-7% EPS CAGR. Estimate revisions show current-year consensus at $3.41, essentially flat over 30 and 90 days, suggesting the market has absorbed the recent data.

**Counter-considerations:** The 5-7% EPS CAGR guidance must be evaluated against continued dilution. If shares outstanding grow at 5-7% annually (as they have), then per-share earnings growth is largely offset by share count expansion. The investment thesis requires capex eventually moderating relative to internally generated cash flows, allowing dilution to slow. The capex trajectory (declining from $1.74B peak to $1.35-1.45B in 2028-2030) supports this, but timing is uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

At $51.50 with 2026E midpoint EPS of $3.43, POR trades at approximately 15.0x forward earnings. This compares favorably to:
- Peer median P/E: 22.2x
- TXNM Energy: 39.9x
- Idacorp: 25.1x
- MGE Energy: 20.8x
- Otter Tail: 13.4x (lower-quality comp given diversified operations)

The 32% discount to peer median reflects legitimate concerns: acquisition execution risk, wildfire liability, persistent dilution, and an inconsistent earnings beat record (2 beats, 2 misses in last 4 quarters). However, PGE's data center demand tailwind is stronger than most peers', which have more mature service territories.

**Dividend:** $2.08/share annualized, yielding 4.1% at current price. This exceeds the peer median of 2.4%, providing income support. However, the payout ratio on adjusted EPS ($2.08/$3.05 = 68%) is moderate rather than conservative given the capex-intensive outlook.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target of $52.77, median $54.00, range $42.50-$62.00. The stock trades essentially at the consensus mean, suggesting limited near-term upside without a positive catalyst.

**Valuation verdict:** The discount to peers is partially justified by structural risks, but a 32% discount for a utility with 17% industrial demand growth and a dominant position in a top data center market appears excessive. If PGE executes the PacifiCorp acquisition cleanly and demonstrates convergence toward authorized ROE, a re-rating to 17-18x forward earnings is plausible ($58-64 range). Execution must be demonstrated first.

## Competitive Landscape

PGE's competitive position is defined by its geographic monopoly (regulated utility) and the quality of its service territory:

**Hillsboro Data Center Market:** The Portland/Hillsboro metro has emerged as a premier data center location. PGE is the sole electric provider. Data centers cannot easily relocate once interconnected, and high switching costs create durable demand. Five contracts for 430 MW already executed, with 400+ MW pipeline through 2029.

**Clean Energy Positioning:** Oregon's decarbonization mandate (HB 2021) creates a multi-decade investment cycle. PGE's 2025 RFP final shortlist is approximately 5,000 MW (targeting 2,500 MW selection). The company is well ahead of schedule on renewable procurement.

**Competitive Risks:** The OBBB legislation repealing/amending IRA tax incentives could increase renewable project costs, making PGE's energy transition more expensive. Oregon's HB 3546 specifically addresses data center cost allocation, potentially limiting the economic benefit of large-load customers if costs are not appropriately socialized.

**PacifiCorp Expansion:** Geographic diversification into Washington adds a second regulatory jurisdiction. While this creates integration complexity, it also reduces single-state regulatory concentration and adds incremental data center-adjacent load opportunities.

## Risk Assessment

**1. PacifiCorp Acquisition (HIGH):** $1.9B purchase price requiring bridge financing, permanent debt issuance, equity dilution, and Manulife partnership. Multi-jurisdictional regulatory approval needed. Integration of 140,000 customers and 4,500 miles of T&D infrastructure. Twelve-month approval timeline. Termination fee of $35M if deal fails.

**2. Wildfire Liability (HIGH/UNQUANTIFIABLE):** Oregon lacks California-style wildfire liability protections. PGE explicitly acknowledges that wildfire damages "may not be recoverable through prices or insurance." Annual wildfire mitigation spending of $120-134M (capital + O&M) provides some protection, but catastrophic wildfire exposure remains unhedged.

**3. Portland Harbor Superfund (MODERATE):** PGE is one of 100+ PRPs. Total remediation costs estimated at $1.9B-$3.5B. PGE's share is indeterminate but could be material.

**4. Regulatory Recovery (MODERATE):** Multiple large deferred cost balances ($48M storm costs, $90M reliability contingency, $57M distribution system costs) pending OPUC decisions. The OPUC has previously allowed lesser amounts than requested.

**5. Equity Dilution (MODERATE/PERSISTENT):** 24% dilution over 3 years, with more planned. This structurally limits per-share returns until capex moderates.

**6. Trade Tariffs (MODERATE):** Tariffs on steel, transformers, meters, and renewable components could increase project costs and cause delays, particularly for the 2025 RFP procurement cycle.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($58-62):** PacifiCorp acquisition closes on favorable terms with accretion confirmed in the first full year. Data center demand continues at 15%+ growth rates, driving rate base expansion. OPUC approves pending deferred costs in full. ROE converges toward 9.34% authorized level as rate cases take effect. Stock re-rates from 15x to 17-18x forward earnings on demonstrated execution. At 18x $3.50 FY2026E = $63.

**Bear Case ($39-42):** PacifiCorp acquisition faces regulatory complications or requires more equity than planned, driving dilution above expectations. A significant wildfire event creates material uninsured liability. Data center demand growth slows as hyperscalers diversify locations. OPUC denies or significantly reduces deferred cost recovery. Stock de-rates to 11-12x earnings. At 12x $3.41 = $41.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. PacifiCorp regulatory approval received with acquisition financing terms confirmed at or below the $1.9B bridge facility cost, demonstrating manageable dilution
2. Two consecutive quarters of earnings beats with realized ROE convergence toward 9%
3. Stock pullback to $45-46 range (13x forward) which would provide sufficient margin of safety against acquisition/wildfire risks

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any material wildfire liability event exceeding insurance coverage
2. PacifiCorp acquisition requires more than $500M in incremental equity beyond current plans
3. Data center contract cancellations or 3+ quarter sequential decline in industrial energy deliveries

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 2026 earnings (expected May 2026), at which point OPUC deferred cost decisions and acquisition regulatory filing progress should be available.

## Conclusion

Portland General Electric presents a genuine growth story uncommon among regulated utilities: the data center demand tailwind is structural and differentiated. The 4.1% dividend yield and 15x forward earnings discount to peers provide valuation support. However, the PacifiCorp acquisition represents a step-function increase in execution risk, financing complexity, and regulatory uncertainty. Combined with persistent equity dilution, unquantified wildfire liability, and an inconsistent recent earnings record, the risk/reward does not yet justify a full conviction position.

Rating: growth-HOLD. The thesis has identifiable catalysts that could unlock re-rating to 17-18x on rising earnings, but those catalysts carry binary execution risk that has not yet been resolved. Wait for acquisition clarity and ROE convergence before increasing conviction.
