**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carvana has executed one of the more complete operational recoveries in recent public-market memory. The company that flirted with insolvency in 2022 (negative equity of ~$518M) posted record Q1 2026 results: 187,393 retail units (+40% YoY, the sixth consecutive quarter of ≥40% growth), $6.43B revenue (+52%), $581M operating income, $405M net income, and $672M Adjusted EBITDA at a 10.4% margin that no other used-vehicle retailer approaches. Shareholders' equity is now +$4.6B, liquidity is ~$6.9B, and the company is in compliance with all covenants. The disruption thesis remains intact: a ~37M-unit/year used-car market where the top ten retailers hold under 10% combined share, and where Carvana is now the dominant surviving pure-play online model after Vroom and Shift exited.

The complication is that the reported profitability is more capital-markets-linked than a retail multiple implies. Roughly $354M of Q1 2026 gross profit came from 100%-margin "gain on loan sales," a genuine but credit-spread-sensitive activity that management itself flags as a dependency (spreads were already lower YoY in the quarter). 2025 net income was flattered by a ~$2.8B one-time deferred-tax valuation-allowance release. Operating cash flow was only $107M against $405M net income in Q1 2026, reflecting inventory build. Overlaid on this is an active SEC investigation (subpoena received June 2025) tied to a January 2025 short-seller report focused on loan-sale accounting, DriveTime related-party dynamics, and subprime credit quality, the single most important unresolved overhang.

The stock has fallen roughly 38% from its 52-week high of $97.38 to $60.46. On unchanged-to-improving operating fundamentals, that decline improves risk/reward mechanically. But the drawdown coincides with falling near-term estimates (current-quarter EPS cut 13.1% over 30 days), a July 29 earnings print the options market is pricing for stress, and a macro regime where a probable H2 consumer/credit deterioration would hit both used-car demand and the loan-sale spreads that carry Carvana's incremental margin. The prior two reviews carried HOLD; nothing in the current data warrants an upgrade to BUY, and the business is growing too fast and too profitably to warrant AVOID. We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale.

## Company Overview

Carvana is a vertically integrated e-commerce platform for buying and selling used cars in the U.S. Customers complete the entire transaction online (browse, finance, trade-in, purchase, delivery or pickup), supported by proprietary inventory and pricing software, in-house inspection and reconditioning centers, a company-owned "hub-and-spoke" logistics fleet, and the branded car vending machines. The business is managed as a single reportable segment; the CEO (founder Ernie Garcia III) runs to two north-star metrics: retail units sold and total gross profit per unit (GPU).

The footprint spans 316 MSAs serving over 80% of the U.S. population, with roughly 75% within 100 miles of a reconditioning or auction site. Cumulatively the company has sold 2.9M retail vehicles and generated $90.5B revenue since 2012. The 2022 ADESA (KAR) acquisition supplies the physical auction and reconditioning backbone that Carvana is converting into retail production capacity, the central lever behind current volume growth and the structural edge over online-only peers that lacked physical reconditioning scale.

Carvana monetizes each transaction through multiple channels: the vehicle itself, a gain on the associated auto loan, vehicle service contracts (VSC), GAP and insurance products, and wholesale on trade-ins. This multi-revenue model produces industry-leading unit economics but also embeds sensitivity to consumer credit and capital-markets conditions that a simple retail comparison misses.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating results.** Q1 2026 was an all-time record across every headline metric. Gross profit was $1.27B (reported GPU $6,783; non-GAAP $6,911). Net income margin was 6.3%; the 10.4% Adjusted EBITDA margin is the cleanest read on operations and stands well above CarMax and franchised-dealer peers. The multi-year revenue trajectory (~$13.6B in 2022 → $10.8B in 2023 contraction → $13.7B in 2024 → ~$20B+ run-rate in 2025 → reaccelerating) confirms the turnaround is not a one-quarter artifact.

**Earnings quality — the crux.** Three items separate reported profit from operating cash generation:
1. **Gain on loan sales** of $354M in Q1 2026 (up from $273M), recorded at ~100% margin in "Other" revenue. Loans are sold to Ally (MPSA up to $6B) and through securitizations and fixed-pool agreements ($12B through 2027). This is real, monetized activity, but management explicitly lists dependence on finance-receivable sales as a risk, and loan-sale spreads were already lower YoY in Q1.
2. **A ~$2.8B deferred-tax valuation-allowance release** in 2025 that put a ~$3.0B deferred tax asset on the balance sheet, a one-time GAAP boost, not operating cash. The effective tax rate is normalizing (8.1% in Q1 2026 vs 0.5% prior year) but remains below 21% because ~35% of Carvana Group income flows to non-controlling LLC unitholders.
3. **Root warrant fair-value swings** in "Other income/expense" (a $42M loss in Q1 2026 vs a $158M gain a year earlier), which introduce EPS noise unrelated to operations.

**Cash conversion.** Operating cash flow of $107M against net income (a 26% conversion rate) reflects growth-driven inventory build and loan-warehouse dynamics. Whether OCF scales with reported earnings or stays structurally below GAAP net income is the metric to watch across the next two quarters.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Cash of $2.41B and total liquidity resources of ~$6.9B against total debt of ~$5.0B, dominated by 9% Senior Secured Notes ($1.66B due 2030, $2.27B due 2031). Quarterly interest expense fell to $99M (from $139M) after the 2023 restructuring and repayment of the 2028 notes. Revolvers were extended and upsized into 2027; the company added a $250M Citizens transportation-fleet facility and a Stellantis floor-plan facility. Refinancing risk sits in 2030–2031, and the indentures restrict incremental capital raising and dividends.

**Earnings surprise record.** Over the last four quarters: 2 beats, 2 misses (Q1 2026 +12.0%, Q4 2025 -5.4%, Q3 2025 -19.9%, Q2 2025 +13.3%). The record is inconsistent enough that forward estimate confidence should be moderate, and the September 2025 miss (-19.9%) shows visibility is not airtight.

## Growth Analysis

The volume growth is genuine and durable-looking at the unit level. Forty percent unit growth in the sixth straight quarter, against a fragmented ~37M-unit market where Carvana runs at roughly 750K–800K units annually (low single-digit share), leaves a long runway if execution and the ADESA reconditioning conversion hold. Management guided Q2 to further sequential records and FY2026 to "significant growth."

The estimate-revision picture is mixed rather than the clean acceleration that would signal a re-rating:
- Current quarter: -13.1% (30d), +0.9% (90d)
- Next quarter: -4.7% (30d), +8.7% (90d)
- Current year: -4.0% (30d), +2.8% (90d)
- Next year: +3.8% (30d), +7.5% (90d)

Near-term estimates are being cut while out-year estimates rise modestly. Revisions are not +30% over 90 days, and the near-term trend is negative, so this does not meet the threshold of a high-conviction inflection. The out-year strength keeps the growth thesis alive, but the near-term cuts warn that Q2 could disappoint against the "record" framing management has set.

The credit-linked component of growth is the sensitivity to monitor. In a regime where used-car affordability is pressured by higher-for-longer mortgage and auto rates, and where a probable H2 consumer slowdown coincides with private-credit stress, both demand and loan-sale spreads face headwinds simultaneously. The retail growth engine can keep running while the profit-per-unit contribution from loan sales compresses, which would show up as decelerating gross-profit and Adjusted-EBITDA growth even if unit growth holds.

## Valuation Assessment

At $60.46, market cap is ~$43.3B. On consensus EPS the stock trades at ~40.9x current-year ($1.48) and ~28.7x next-year ($2.11); trailing P/E is ~35x on $1.69 EPS. The peer median P/E in automotive retail is 21.5, but the quality growth comparables sit higher — CarMax 30.7x, O'Reilly 29.3x, AutoZone 21.2x. Against 40% unit growth, the ~29x next-year multiple implies a PEG comfortably under 1.0, which is not a stretched multiple for the growth delivered.

The reason we do not score valuation as attractive is that the apparent discount reflects real, unpriceable risk rather than a clean mispricing. A "cheap" forward multiple here is anchored to earnings that are partly capital-markets-linked (loan-sale gains), partly boosted by a one-time tax event, and overhung by an active SEC investigation with unknown outcome and timing. Analyst targets (low $67, mean $90.6, median $90, high $120) all sit above the current price, but those targets extrapolate the operating trajectory without discounting the binary regulatory outcome or a credit-cycle hit to loan-sale economics. On a normalized-earnings basis that haircuts the loan-sale contribution for spread compression, the through-cycle multiple is meaningfully richer than the headline forward P/E suggests. The valuation is defensible for a growth compounder but does not offer asymmetric upside once the risk stack is priced.

## Competitive Landscape

Carvana's competitive position is improving on trajectory, which matters more than absolute share. The failure of Vroom and Shift left Carvana as the dominant surviving pure-play online used-car retailer, a moat established partly by attrition. Against CarMax (the largest used retailer, omnichannel), AutoNation, franchised dealer groups, and thousands of independents, Carvana's edge is vertical integration: self-sourced inventory that reduces auction fees, proprietary logistics and pricing software, national scale, and the ADESA reconditioning capacity that online-only peers never possessed. The result is a ~10%+ Adjusted EBITDA margin that no comparable used-auto retailer matches.

The company is taking share in a fragmented market with a long runway, and the multi-revenue monetization model (vehicle + loan + F&I products + wholesale) deepens the per-unit economics competitors struggle to replicate at scale. The offsetting consideration is that a portion of the margin advantage depends on the loan-sale and F&I channels, which are exposed to consumer-credit and capital-markets conditions rather than being purely operational. The moat around logistics, reconditioning, and brand is durable; the moat around the financing-driven margin is more cyclical.

## Risk Assessment

Carvana carries a concentration of company-specific risks that are not fully reflected in the current valuation:

1. **Active SEC investigation.** The subpoena is "primarily related to the allegations" of the January 2025 short-seller report. The company is cooperating and disputes the report, but the outcome and timing are unknown. This is a genuine binary overhang and plausibly explains much of the stock's extreme volatility and wide bull/bear divergence.

2. **Dependence on finance-receivable sales.** Gain-on-loan-sale is a large, high-margin profit driver reliant on funding capacity (Ally $6B, $12B fixed-pool through 2027), favorable spreads, and benign credit/capital-markets conditions. A credit downturn or spread widening would hit the P&L directly, a live risk in the current H2 macro setup.

3. **Governance and related-party concentration.** Carvana is a controlled company; the Garcia family holds super-voting Class B stock (10 votes/share). Carvana Co. owns only ~64.9% of the operating LLC, with 35.1% non-controlling interest absorbing ~$155M of quarterly income. DriveTime (Garcia-controlled) administers VSCs (~$111M Q1 2026 commission), the limited warranty, loan servicing, and leases reconditioning real estate to Carvana, placing the controlling shareholder on both sides of multiple economic relationships.

4. **Tax Receivable Agreement — $2.2B liability.** Carvana owes LLC unitholders (85% to Garcia parties, ~$1.7B) 85% of realized tax savings from basis step-ups. A change of control or early termination accelerates the liability, a structural constraint on strategic optionality and a growing cash drain (~$37M paid in Q1 2026).

5. **High-cost debt and refinancing.** $5B at up to 9–10.25% coupons, with maturities in 2030–2031 and restrictive indentures.

6. **Cyclicality.** Consumer discretionary exposure to used-vehicle prices (post-2021 normalization), interest rates, and consumer credit health, all of which are turning less favorable in the current regime.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($90–$120).** The operational turnaround is real and under-appreciated by a market fixated on the short-seller thread. Low-single-digit share of a 37M-unit market implies years of runway; ADESA reconditioning conversion sustains volume; Adjusted EBITDA margin expands past 10% as fixed costs lever. The SEC matter resolves without material findings, removing the overhang. Loan-sale spreads hold, F&I attach rates rise, and next-year EPS clears $2.11 with continued upward revisions. At a PEG below 1.0 the stock re-rates toward the ~$90 analyst mean and, in the upside scenario, the $120 high target as consensus catches up to a 30%+ compounder. Best-in-class margins in a category no competitor can match justify a premium multiple.

**Bear case ($40–$48).** The H2 consumer slowdown and credit-spread widening hit demand and compress loan-sale gains simultaneously, revealing how much of the margin is capital-markets-linked. Near-term estimate cuts foreshadow a Q2 miss against the "record" framing, and the September 2025 -19.9% surprise shows this is possible. The SEC investigation escalates or produces adverse findings on loan-sale accounting or DriveTime dealings, forcing restatement risk and a governance discount. Cash conversion stays structurally below GAAP earnings, undermining the quality of the reported profit. The heavily traded $46 puts prove prescient; the stock retraces toward the low $40s, a ~25–30% decline from current levels.

**Base case (~$60–$68).** The business keeps growing units at 30–40% while loan-sale-driven margin faces modest spread pressure, producing solid but not spectacular EPS. The SEC matter remains unresolved, capping the multiple. The stock trades in a wide band around current levels, with the July 29 print the near-term swing factor. This maps to the analyst low target of $67 and roughly the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings print on July 29, 2026 (a binary near-term event priced for a ±13% move), followed by any SEC investigation update and the trajectory of loan-sale spreads through H2.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The SEC investigation resolves without material findings or restatement, removing the primary overhang.
- Operating cash flow demonstrates it scales with reported net income (conversion sustained above ~60–70% for two consecutive quarters), confirming earnings quality.
- Next-year EPS estimates continue rising (currently +7.5% over 90 days) while unit growth holds ≥30% and loan-sale spreads stabilize, evidence the profit engine is not merely riding benign credit conditions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse SEC development (Wells notice, enforcement action, or restatement of loan-sale accounting).
- A Q2 unit-growth deceleration below ~20% YoY, or a gross-profit-per-unit decline signaling loan-sale spread compression is flowing through.
- Deterioration in credit metrics or funding capacity (loss of loan-sale spread, reduced Ally/fixed-pool capacity, or a covenant issue).

**What the market appears to be pricing.** The ~38% drawdown from the high is the market discounting a binary SEC outcome, capital-markets sensitivity of the earnings, and a governance structure that hands minority holders limited protection, not the market ignoring the operating growth. The falsifiable version of "the market is wrong" is: the SEC matter clears benignly and loan-sale spreads hold through the H2 slowdown, in which case ~29x next-year on 40% growth is too cheap. The falsifiable version of "the sellers are right" is: a Q2 disappointment or an SEC escalation exposes earnings-quality dependence, in which case the multiple compresses further. Until one of those resolves, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.

## Conclusion

Carvana is a genuine operational success: 40% unit growth, industry-leading 10.4% Adjusted EBITDA margin, and a balance sheet rebuilt from negative equity to +$4.6B. The disruption thesis in a fragmented $37M-unit market is intact, and the company has emerged as the dominant surviving online model. That is not enough for a BUY in the current setup. The reported profitability leans on capital-markets-sensitive loan-sale gains and a one-time tax benefit; cash conversion is weak (26% of net income); an active SEC investigation remains unresolved; the governance structure (super-voting stock, $2.2B insider-tilted TRA, deep DriveTime entanglement) disadvantages minority holders; near-term estimates are being cut; and the options market is pricing imminent stress into the July 29 print with heavy downside put buying. The macro regime of a probable H2 consumer/credit deterioration that would hit both used-car demand and loan-sale spreads cuts against a discretionary retailer whose incremental margin is credit-linked.

The score trajectory has improved (4.4 → 5.6 across four reviews) on the strength of the turnaround, and the current 5.4 reflects a modest step back for incremental near-term negatives (falling near-term estimates, more bearish options positioning, no insider dip-buying) rather than fundamental deterioration. We maintain **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale: the business is too strong operationally to avoid and too encumbered by unresolved binary risk to buy. The path to a BUY runs through SEC resolution and demonstrated cash-flow quality; the path to AVOID runs through an SEC escalation or evidence that the margin was riding benign credit conditions.
