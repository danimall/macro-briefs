## Executive Summary

PulteGroup's second-quarter 2026 report, filed July 22, contained the first evidence in six quarters that the operating deterioration has stopped. Home sale gross margin rose to 25.0% from 24.4% in Q1 2026 — the 10-Q explicitly frames this as an increase "after sequential quarterly declines since the beginning of 2025." Net new orders rose 6% in units, the cancellation rate fell to 13% from 15%, and backlog units at 10,966 were up 2% year over year, the first positive backlog unit comparison after FY2025 closed down 16%. Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of 28,500–29,000 closings and 24.5%–25.0% gross margin in the same week D.R. Horton cut its closings guidance to 83,800–84,300 from 86,000–87,000 and reported cancellations rising to 20%.

The offsetting evidence is equally concrete. The order recovery was purchased with price: orders rose 6% in units but 5% in dollars, and backlog dollars per unit fell from $634.9K to $620.6K. Earnings are still contracting — Q2 revenue fell 9.6%, pretax income 22.9%, diluted EPS 18.2% to $2.48. Operating cash flow in the first half collapsed 58% to $176.8M against an $807.3M inventory build, and the company returned $780.7M to shareholders (95% of net income) by drawing cash down $645.2M and lifting debt-to-capitalization from 11.2% to 12.3%. Land purchased at 2022–2024 prices continues to close at 2026 reset selling prices, and management projects roughly $5.4B of 2026 land spend that sets the 2028–2029 cost basis.

At $132.90 the stock trades at 13.1x the $10.18 FY2026 consensus, 11.8x the $11.24 FY2027 consensus, and 1.92x the $69.34 June book value — roughly at the 14.4x homebuilding peer median rather than below it, despite PulteGroup carrying the sector's widest gross margin (25.0% versus D.R. Horton's 20.7%) and its most conservative capital structure. Base case fair value is $145, or 9.1% above spot; the bear case at $106 is 20.2% below. That asymmetry, combined with a 30-year mortgage rate near 6.9% and total application demand running below year-ago levels, supports holding existing exposure rather than adding. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

PulteGroup operates 1,014 active communities across 47 markets in 26 states at December 31, 2025, expanding to 48 markets and an average 1,074 active communities in Q2 2026 (+8% year over year). FY2025 closings were 29,572 homes at a $566,000 average selling price. Homebuilding produced 98% of consolidated revenue in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025; the captive Financial Services segment (Pulte Mortgage, title, insurance agency) contributed $158.0M of FY2025 pretax income, only 5.4% of the consolidated total.

The structural differentiator is deliberate three-way buyer diversification. FY2025 closings split 38% first-time (Centex), 40% move-up (Pulte Homes), and 22% active adult (Del Webb). Active-adult buyers are materially less mortgage-rate-elastic: cash buyers were 21% of closings in both 2024 and 2025, and Pulte Mortgage originated only 64% of closings in 2025. In a 7%-mortgage environment, a 21% cash cohort is a genuine demand cushion that entry-level-concentrated peers do not have.

Geographic mix at FY2025 revenue: Florida $4.26B (25%), West $3.88B (23%), Southeast $2.96B (17%), Midwest $2.73B (16%), Texas $1.68B (10%), Northeast $1.24B (7%). Segment ASPs range from $385K in Texas to $753K in the Northeast, so consolidated ASP moves on geographic mix as much as on realized price. 83% of closings are single-family detached; 82% price between $250,000 and $750,000.

## Financial Analysis

### The two-year compression, and what actually caused it

| Net income | $2,602M | $3,083M | $2,219M |
| Diluted EPS | — | $14.69 | $11.12 |
| Home sale gross margin | — | 28.9% | 26.3% |
| SG&A % of home sale revenue | — | 7.6% | 9.4% |
| Diluted shares | 221.2M | 209.8M | 199.5M |

FY2025 revenue fell 3.5% while net income fell 28.0%. Two mechanisms explain the gap, and they should not be conflated.

**Gross margin.** 28.9% → 26.3% in FY2025, with a straight-line intra-year path from 27.5% in Q1 to 24.7% in Q4. Management attributes the decline to elevated sales incentives (spec discounts, closing-cost credits, rate buydowns), higher land acquisition and development costs flowing through with a multi-quarter lag, and margin drag from clearing completed spec inventory.

**SG&A, partly an accounting comparison.** FY2024 SG&A was reduced by a $333.9M favorable general-liability reserve reversal; FY2025 by only $42.3M. That $291.6M swing is roughly 165 bps of FY2025 home sale revenue and accounts for most of the 180 bps of SG&A ratio deterioration. Excluding reversals in both years, FY2025 pretax income was approximately $2,869M versus $3,672M in FY2024 — a 21.9% decline rather than the reported 27.3%. Roughly 5.4 percentage points of the headline pretax decline is reversal-comparison noise. The genuine deleverage is separate and visible in 2026: H1 SG&A dollars *fell* 3% to $763.3M, but home sale revenue fell 11%, so the ratio rose 90 bps to 10.7%. Community count grew 8% against an 8% closings decline. This is fixed-cost absorption failure, and volume is the only cure.

The quarterly gross margin sequence — 27.5% (Q1'25) → 27.0% (Q2'25) → 24.7% (Q4'25) → 24.4% (Q1'26) → 25.0% (Q2'26) — is the single most decision-relevant new datapoint, and it is a filing-confirmed sequential turn rather than an inference. It was helped by a 23% year-over-year reduction in completed unsold specs (1,865 → 1,427) and a mix shift toward build-to-order, which carries a lighter incentive load. One quarter of 60 bps improvement off a trough is not a trend, and management explicitly warns "we expect these factors to continue to impact our gross margins over the near term."

Geographic dispersion is now extreme. Q2 segment pretax income versus prior year: Florida flat, Midwest −14%, Texas −21%, Southeast −25%, Northeast −50%, West −56%. Florida orders rose 19% in units and 24% in dollars with backlog units up 9% — an inversion of FY2025, when Florida pretax fell 27%. The West is the clear negative, with closings −20% and backlog units −15%, management naming Las Vegas and Arizona. The Northeast collapsed on volume (closings −27%, ASP −8%) after being FY2025's best segment.

### Cash flow: the headline is alarming and partly misleading

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $176.8M versus $421.7M in H1 2025, a 58% decline, driven by an $807.3M inventory consumption of cash (versus $533.0M) as house and land inventory rose from $12,925.4M to $13,715.4M. Two cautions apply. First, the $807.3M is a period flow and the $13,715.4M an ending balance; they are not comparable magnitudes. Second, the H1 figure must not be annualized — the 10-Q's seasonality disclosure states revenues and operating cash flow increase in Q4, and guidance implies roughly 15,650 H2 closings against 13,099 in H1. A naive 2x of H1 would imply ~$354M against FY2025's actual $1,871.2M; the realistic FY2026 band, assuming inventory build moderates as the H2 closings wave converts work-in-process to cash, is closer to $0.9B–$1.4B. That is still materially below FY2025's $1,748.5M of free cash flow.

The honest statement about capital return: PulteGroup is currently funding buybacks partly from the balance sheet. H1 repurchases of $681.2M plus $99.5M of dividends equal $780.7M against $819.0M of net income (95% payout) and $176.8M of operating cash flow. Cash fell $645.2M to $1,335.7M, bridged by $794.8M of note issuance net of $600.7M of repayments. Retained earnings *declined* from $9,494.6M to $9,488.7M over the half; total equity was near-flat at $13,013.7M. Book value per share is growing through share-count shrinkage, not equity accumulation.

### Balance sheet: the strongest single attribute

- Debt-to-total-capitalization excluding Financial Services debt: 11.2% (12/31/25) → 12.3% (6/30/26).
- Total interest-bearing debt $2,298.2M against $13,013.7M of equity = 17.7%. Homebuilding net debt: $1,820.3M notes less $1,335.7M cash = **$484.6M, or 3.7% of equity** — effectively unlevered.
- **Maturity wall eliminated.** February 2026 issuance of $800M ($400M at 4.250% due 2031, $400M at 4.900% due 2036) funded repayment of $251.9M of 5.500% notes at maturity and full redemption of $337.3M of 5.000% notes due January 2027. No senior note maturity until March 2031; weighted average fixed rate 5.74%. Management issued $800M at lower rates than the $589.1M repaid.
- Revolver amended February 4, 2026: capacity $1.25B → $1.75B, maturity June 2027 → February 2031. No borrowings; $343.2M of letters of credit; $1.4B available. Covenants (minimum tangible net worth, maximum debt-to-cap) in compliance with very large headroom.
- The one live item: Pulte Mortgage's $625M master repurchase facility ($477.9M drawn at 5.42%) **matures August 12, 2026**. The company states it intends to refinance but warns there is no assurance of commercially reasonable terms. Collateral is agency-eligible loans turning in roughly 30 days against $1.4B of undrawn revolver; this reads as routine renewal risk, but it is dated and resolves within days.

Note a data discrepancy worth flagging: screening feeds show "debt-to-equity 0.39," which is total liabilities to equity, not interest-bearing leverage. For a builder whose liabilities include $512.3M of customer deposits and $459.6M of deferred taxes, the distinction is material.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 is a down year on every absolute measure. Closings guidance of 28,500–29,000 sits 2–4% below FY2025's 29,572. At the 24.75% gross margin midpoint, a $545K ASP, and H2 SG&A near 9.9% of home sale revenue on better absorption, FY2026 EPS lands near $9.84 — consistent with the $10.18 twelve-analyst consensus and 11–12% below FY2025's $11.12. The sensitivity is most exposed to ASP: a $10K error moves FY EPS roughly $0.30.

The forward growth case rests on four identifiable legs:

1. **Margin trough passed.** 24.4% → 25.0% sequentially, with guidance reaffirmed. If specs continue to clear and build-to-order backlog grows, FY2027 margin can hold 25%+ absent further land-cost shock.
2. **Community count and backlog.** Average active communities +8%, backlog units +2% after FY2025's −16%. Community growth guided at 3–5% for 2026. This is the volume engine for 2027 — but it is currently a margin cost, not a benefit.
3. **Share count.** 202.9M (12/31/24) → 192.7M (12/31/25) → 187.7M (6/30/26): a 7.5% reduction in 18 months, and 20.6% off the FY2022 diluted count of 236.2M. Q2 dilutive securities added only 0.6%; there are no convertibles or earn-outs creating contingent dilution. At the current pace, roughly 5% annual EPS accretion arrives independent of operating results.
4. **Consensus FY2027 $11.24**, +10.4% over FY2026 consensus.

Estimate revisions are modestly supportive rather than powerful: current-year +1.6% over 30 days and +1.5% over 90 days; next-year +1.2% and +0.7%. The current-quarter estimate was cut 4.8% while the next quarter was raised 6.1%, which reads as timing reallocation around the Q3 closings guide of 7,000–7,400. This is nowhere near the magnitude of revision velocity that would signal an underappreciated inflection; it is stabilization.

The binding constraint sits outside the company. The 30-year mortgage rate is near 6.9%, and national for-sale inventory is above 865,000 units, with total mortgage application demand running below year-ago levels and buyers actively pausing. Nothing in the current rate configuration delivers the rate relief on which a genuine volume recovery depends. PulteGroup's own 10-Q language — "elevated mortgage interest rates, higher housing costs, and general economic uncertainty" — describes latent-but-hesitant demand, which is a better setup than 2006–2011 but not a growth setup.

## Competitive Landscape

The margin premium is the crux. PulteGroup reported 25.0% home sale gross margin for calendar Q2 2026; D.R. Horton reported 20.7% for its fiscal Q3 ended the same date. The ~430 bps spread is primarily a product-mix and land-strategy outcome, not a pure execution verdict: PHM's 40% move-up and 22% active-adult mix at a $544K ASP structurally supports higher margin at lower inventory velocity (1.19x turn versus DHI's 2.0x historical norm), while DHI is optimizing the other variable.

More informative is that the two companies resolved the same price-versus-pace trade-off in opposite directions in the same quarter. DHI's CEO stated the company "did make the decision to hold margin a little more than push into the units," and consequently cut FY2026 closings and revenue guidance with cancellations rising to 20%. PulteGroup accepted a 3% ASP decline and 200 bps of margin compression, reaffirmed guidance, grew orders 6%, and cut cancellations to 13%. PHM's choice looks better on pipeline metrics and worse on near-term margin defense. Which proves correct depends entirely on 2027 demand: improving demand rewards PHM's community-count and backlog build; deteriorating demand rewards DHI's inventory discipline.

Land strategy places PulteGroup in the middle of the industry spectrum. At June 30, 2026 it controlled 102,323 owned lots (45%) and 125,918 optioned (55%). Over the first half, owned lots rose 1.2% while optioned fell 5.7%, moving the optioned share from 57% to 55% — the opposite direction from the 10-K's stated priority of "increasing our lot optionality." The most likely explanation is arithmetic rather than strategy reversal: exercising options moves lots from optioned to owned, and abandoning them ($11.5M of H1 write-offs) removes them entirely; both push the mix toward owned with no new outright purchases. Either way, 102,323 owned lots against a 28,750-closing midpoint is 3.6 years of owned supply versus DHI's roughly 80%-optioned structure — a real cyclical exposure DHI has largely engineered away, though Del Webb's large master-planned communities structurally require longer-dated owned positions.

Spec discipline is a genuine positive: completed unsold specs fell from 1,865 to 1,427 year over year, total homes in production fell 6% to 16,743, and sold homes in production held near flat at 8,342. Clearing spec is the heaviest incentive drag, so this mix shift is margin-accretive going forward. National share is only ~4% of U.S. new home sales, so share gain is not capacity-constrained.

## Risk Assessment

**Land cost lag (highest materiality).** Both MD&A documents use near-identical language: cost changes in land acquisition and development hit results with a lag due to development and construction cycle times. Land bought at 2022–2024 peak prices is closing in 2026 at reset prices, and no amount of incentive management fixes an embedded cost basis. The ~$5.4B of projected 2026 land spend sets the 2028–2029 basis at prices no one can yet assess.

**Land option deposits: $1.29B of quantified at-risk capital.** At June 30, deposits and pre-acquisition costs of $1,290.6M sat against $9,687.1M of remaining option purchase price, with only $15.8M of the $717.4M of pure deposits refundable. The 10-K states that in an extended slowdown the company could cancel a large portion of options, writing off related deposits. Write-offs are already scaling: $18.3M (FY2024) → $48.4M (FY2025) → $11.5M (H1 2026). The $1.29B is theoretical maximum exposure (9.9% of equity); realistic moderate-slowdown loss is a fraction.

**Impairment threshold approaching.** The 10-K's stated trigger is community-level projected gross margin in single digits. At 12/31/25 both realized and backlog margins exceeded 25% with "only a small minority" of communities below 10%. Consolidated margin is now 25.0% with 24.5%–25.0% guided, so the distribution's left tail has moved closer. The filing warns resulting impairments "could be material."

**Insurance reserve tailwind exhausted, with a cyclical mechanism.** FY2024 and FY2025 benefited from $333.9M and $42.3M of favorable general-liability reserve reversals, driven by actuarial reweighting toward recent low-cost policy years on improved construction techniques, **rising home values**, and greater subcontractor claim participation. Home values are no longer rising. Recorded reserves are $262.2M against a disclosed reasonably-possible range of $175M–$325M, and IBNR is 71–74% of general liability reserves, up from 68% at 12/31/24 — the reserve is increasingly composed of its least observable component. Mitigant: H1 2026 favorable adjustments were *larger* than H1 2025 ($13.5M vs $8.7M), and defect claims resolve over ten-plus years, so any deterioration is gradual.

**ERP implementation.** A dedicated FY2025 risk factor warns a multi-year ERP conversion "may cause inefficiencies until the ERP systems are stabilized," mandates modifications to disclosure controls and ICFR, and could impair the ability to produce timely and accurate financial statements. The Q2 10-Q reports controls effective with no material change during the quarter, so this is a watch item, not a finding.

**Florida concentration and insurance availability.** Florida is 25% of revenue. The 10-K notes homeowner, hazard and flood insurance costs have risen and in some cases constrained coverage availability. This is already visible in PHM's own P&L: insurance agency commissions fell 80% from $35.8M (FY2024) to $7.2M (FY2025) on "lower policy retention and commission rates as a result of the evolving environment for home insurance." Partial recovery to $7.7M in Q2 2026 from $1.9M.

**Input-cost policy.** The FY2025 10-K states recently imposed tariffs have not been material but flags new tariffs on Canadian and Mexican materials and immigration-enforcement effects on skilled trades. Management warned in October 2025 that tariffs could add roughly $1,500 per home from 2026 — about 28 bps of margin against a $544K ASP, small individually but additive to the land lag.

**Financing.** The August 12, 2026 mortgage repurchase facility renewal is the only dated financing item and appears routine given agency-eligible collateral and $1.4B of undrawn revolver. Otherwise there is no maturity wall, and covenant headroom at 12.3% debt-to-cap is very large.

Net read: the options market prices this as an ordinary cyclical with no near-term event risk, which is consistent with a HOLD and inconsistent with a high-conviction call either way.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($166, +24.9%)

The gross margin trough is confirmed at 24.4% and behind the company. Spec inventory is down 23% year over year, removing the heaviest incentive drag, and the mix is shifting to build-to-order. Community count is up 8% with 3–5% more guided, providing volume capacity when demand recovers. Orders grew 6% and cancellations fell to 13% in the same quarter the volume leader cut guidance and saw cancellations reach 20% — PulteGroup is taking share with a 430 bps margin advantage intact. The balance sheet has no senior maturity until 2031 at a 5.74% weighted rate, 12.3% debt-to-cap, and $1.4B of undrawn revolver, so the company can outspend distressed competitors on land in a downturn. Management is buying stock at prices below spot (Q2 average $119.42, with 1.46M shares in May at $114.83) and shrinking the count ~5% annually, which delivers EPS accretion independent of operating results. If mortgage rates move toward 6%, incentive load falls, margin recovers above 25.5%, and FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds $11.24 on 14.8x — $166, matching the high analyst target.

### Bear Case ($106, −20.2%)

The order recovery is a price cut, not a demand recovery: orders +6% in units versus +5% in dollars, backlog dollars per unit −2.3%. That lower realized price flows into H2 2026 and H1 2027 revenue and margin. The land-cost lag has years left — 2026's ~$5.4B of land spend prices 2028–2029 — while incentives cannot be withdrawn at 6.9% mortgage rates with 865,000 homes for sale. SG&A is deleveraging structurally: community count +8% against closings −8% means fixed-cost absorption failure that only volume fixes, and volume is guided down. The insurance reserve reversal that flattered FY2024 by $333.9M is over, and its cited driver (rising home values) has stopped, so favorable development could turn unfavorable against a $175M–$325M possible range. Consolidated margin 400 bps off the FY2024 peak has moved community-level margins toward the disclosed single-digit impairment trigger, with land charges already up from $34.6M to $126.9M. The West (−56% pretax) and Texas (−21% Q2 pretax) are impaired. Operating cash flow has fallen 58% while the company pays out 95% of earnings, so the buyback is drawing down the balance sheet at 1.9x book — capital not available for distressed land in 2027. Multiple compression to 1.5x book on a $9.50 trough EPS puts the stock at $106, near the 52-week low.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report was declining — 6.8 (February) to 5.6 (June) — driven by growth prospects falling from 6.5 to 4.0 and valuation from 7.0 to 5.0. The Q2 print partially reverses that direction. Gross margin turned sequentially higher for the first time in six quarters, orders and backlog units inflected positive, cancellations improved, and guidance was reaffirmed while the industry's volume leader cut. Those are real improvements, and they justify a modest upgrade to growth prospects and a marginally better overall score of 5.7.

They do not justify a rating change. The order recovery was funded by price concession, visible precisely in backlog dollars per unit falling 2.3%. Earnings are still down 18% year over year on the bottom line and FY2026 EPS will land 11–12% below FY2025. Operating cash flow fell 58% while the company paid out 95% of earnings, so the buyback — well executed at prices below spot — is drawing down cash and adding modest leverage at 1.9x book. Most importantly, at 13.1x FY2026 consensus and 1.92x book the stock is priced at the peer median, not below it, so an investor gets the sector's best margin, cleanest balance sheet and fastest share-count reduction without paying a premium, but also without a discount that compensates for the risk that FY2027 consensus proves optimistic. With the 30-year mortgage near 6.9% and application demand below year-ago levels, the rate relief the bull case requires is not in sight.

Base case $145 against a $106 bear leaves roughly 9% upside and 20% downside — insufficient asymmetry for new capital in a sector where the demand constraint is macro and unresolved. Existing holders should maintain exposure; the balance sheet removes solvency risk from the equation and the buyback provides a floor under EPS. New capital should wait for either $115–120, where the company's own repurchase activity clustered, or confirmation in the October print that the margin recovery is structural.
