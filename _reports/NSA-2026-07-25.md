**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National Storage Affiliates Trust is no longer an independent public company. On March 16, 2026, NSA signed a definitive all-stock merger agreement with Public Storage (NYSE: PSA); shareholders approved the transaction on July 14, 2026; and the deal closed on July 22, 2026, at which point NSA was delisted from the NYSE. Each NSA common share converted into 0.14 Public Storage shares. The transaction was valued at roughly $10.5 billion including assumed debt. The $43.41 quote and $3.4 billion market capitalization in the standard data feed are terminal, deal-driven marks on a security that has already been exchanged for PSA stock.

There is no going-concern NSA equity to underwrite. Any prior or current exposure to NSA is now, in economic substance, a position in Public Storage at a fixed 0.14:1 conversion ratio. A rational holder's decision is whether to hold or sell PSA, which requires a PSA-specific integration, synergy, and cyclical-recovery analysis rather than anything intrinsic to the NSA ticker. Because the corporate action is complete and irreversible, the honest classification is a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale: the decision has migrated to a different security.

The fundamentals that led to this outcome explain the sale price and the timing. NSA reported negative same-store NOI growth of −4.5% and negative Core FFO-per-share growth of −8.6% in 2025, both missing already-negative internal bonus thresholds. Three-year total shareholder return sat in roughly the 23rd percentile of the MSCI US REIT Index and trailed the storage-peer weighted average by about 919 basis points. Persistent underperformance, a diluted competitive differentiator after the 2024 PRO internalization, and a compressed valuation made NSA the natural consolidation target among the self-storage "big four."

## Company Overview

NSA was a Maryland-formed self-storage REIT (UPREIT structure via NSA OP, LP) headquartered in Greenwood Village, Colorado. At acquisition it owned more than 1,000 properties comprising over 550,000 units serving roughly 500,000 customers across 40+ states and Puerto Rico.

Its historical differentiator was the "PRO" (Participating Regional Operator) model, a decentralized structure in which regional operators such as SecurCare and Optivest contributed and managed properties in exchange for subordinated operating-partnership units. This distinguished NSA from the vertically integrated majors (Public Storage, Extra Space, CubeSmart). NSA internalized the entire PRO structure over 2024–2025, transitioning to a self-managed platform. That decision eliminated the founding differentiator and, in retrospect, set the table for the sale. Founder Arlen Nordhagen remained Vice Chairperson and CEO David Cramer also came out of SecurCare.

## Financial Analysis

The stitched XBRL series (revenue ~$146M FY2025, ~$137M FY2024) does not represent NSA's total revenue, which historically ran north of $800M; it appears to capture only a reclassified fee/JV subset and should be treated with caution. The meaningful signals sit elsewhere:

- **Operating fundamentals were contracting into the sale.** The 2025 bonus scorecard shows Same-Store NOI growth of −4.5% (below the −3% threshold) and Core FFO-per-share growth of −8.6% (below the −6% threshold). Management earned only 68% of target bonus. This reflects the post-COVID self-storage downcycle: falling street rates, normalized move-in demand, and higher financing costs.
- **Leverage was elevated.** Long-term debt of roughly $3.4 billion against total assets near $5.1 billion (FY2025). Book equity declined steadily from $1.65 billion (FY2022) to about $0.95 billion (FY2025) as the company repurchased units and absorbed the PRO internalization. Cash was thin at ~$23 million.
- **Cash generation held up.** Operating cash flow of roughly $338 million and free cash flow near $300 million (FY2025) confirm that self-storage remains a high-margin, cash-generative asset class even in a down-cycle.
- **Share count** fell from ~91 million (2022) to ~77.8 million (2025), consistent with buybacks and the late-2024 ATM/forward sale program.

The earnings surprise record (2 beats, 2 misses over the last four quarters) and the negative 90-day estimate revisions (current-quarter EPS revised −23.6%, next-quarter −13.9%) are consistent with the down-cycle picture, though these forward estimates are now largely irrelevant given the close.

## Growth Analysis

NSA's standalone growth profile was negative at the point of acquisition. The −4.5% SSNOI and −8.6% Core FFO-per-share prints represent contraction, not deceleration from a high base. Estimate revisions were negative across every near-term window before the merger closed. The forward growth story now belongs entirely to Public Storage, which framed the acquisition as its first major "PS4.0 Value Creation Engine" milestone. PSA guided the deal to be FFO-accretive within year one, building to $0.35–$0.50 per share of accretion from $110–$130 million of run-rate synergies over three to four years, most of it from eliminating NSA's corporate overhead (confirmed by the post-close Colorado HQ layoffs reported July 23, 2026). Whether that accretion materializes is a PSA execution question, not an NSA one.

## Valuation Assessment

The terminal NSA price is anchored mechanically to PSA's stock at the 0.14 exchange ratio, not to NSA's intrinsic REIT fundamentals. The 57.9x trailing P/E in the data feed is meaningless in an all-stock deal context: it reflects trough GAAP earnings in a REIT structure where FFO, not net income, is the relevant metric, and it says nothing about the exchange value.

Against the peer set (Real Estate median P/E ~36.2, median yield 2.3%), NSA's optical multiple looks stretched, but this comparison is moot because the security no longer trades on its own fundamentals. The analyst price targets (low $40, mean $44.13, high $53) reflect stale pre- and mid-deal sell-side marks; the median $43.00 essentially matches the closing exchange value. Options-implied volatility compressed from 51.0% to 37.7% between May and June, consistent with a market that had largely priced in a completed, low-uncertainty corporate action.

For a holder today, "valuation" reduces to whether PSA shares received at 0.14:1 are attractively priced, which requires underwriting PSA's balance sheet, synergy capture, and the timing of a self-storage cycle recovery. That is outside the scope of an NSA-specific valuation and is why the dimension scores as neutral rather than cheap or expensive.

## Competitive Landscape

Self-storage consolidation among the big four (Public Storage, Extra Space, which absorbed Life Storage in 2023, CubeSmart, and NSA) is the defining industry dynamic. NSA was the smallest and only decentralized operator, and after the 2023–2025 rate and occupancy downcycle compressed its valuation and its TSR fell to the ~23rd percentile of the MSCI US REIT Index, it became the natural target. The 2024 PRO internalization removed its structural differentiator without conferring the scale of the vertically integrated majors. Post-close, NSA's ~700+ wholly-owned properties fold into PSA's platform, and 313 properties across 28 states plus Puerto Rico were placed into a joint venture (~80% legacy LPs, ~20% PSA) to defer legacy unitholder tax gains. NSA's competitive position as an independent entity has been extinguished by absorption into the market leader.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant "risk" is that the situation is already resolved: the deal has closed, the stock is delisted, and shares have converted. There is no residual deal-break risk. The forward risks are PSA's: integration execution, realization of the synergy target, exposure to the self-storage cycle, and the ~$2 billion of secured mortgage debt plus $237 million PSA mezzanine financing taken on within the 313-property JV structure.

For a former NSA holder now holding PSA, the risks are those of a large-cap self-storage REIT in a soft-demand cycle: street-rate weakness, new-supply overhang in certain markets, and the pace of the demand recovery. These are moderate and broadly priced, which supports a mid-range risk score.

### Options Market Signal

The options data reinforce the completed-deal read. ATM implied volatility fell 13.3 points into June as the transaction became a near-certainty, and the put/call open-interest ratio drifted up modestly to 0.64. Short interest was negligible at 0.0% of float (14,648 shares). None of this reflects fundamental positioning; it reflects an event that had already largely resolved.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (for the residual PSA position):** PSA executes the integration cleanly, captures the synergy target, and the self-storage cycle troughs and recovers, driving the guided FFO accretion within three to four years. In that scenario the 0.14 PSA shares appreciate with PSA's re-rating.

**Bear case:** The self-storage down-cycle persists longer than expected, synergy capture slips, and PSA's larger balance sheet dilutes the accretion, leaving the converted shares dead money for an extended period.

Both cases are PSA cases. There is no NSA-specific bull or bear thesis remaining because the equity no longer exists.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Because this is a completed corporate action rather than a live standalone equity, standard BUY/AVOID exit mechanics do not apply. The practical framing:

- **Upgrade triggers (to a PSA BUY view):** Confirmation that PSA is tracking ahead of its year-one FFO-accretion guidance; visible stabilization or inflection in self-storage same-store NOI across the big four; PSA trading at a discount to its historical FFO multiple.
- **Downgrade triggers (to a PSA sell view):** Evidence that synergy capture is stalling, HQ integration costs are exceeding plan, or the self-storage demand recovery is pushed out beyond 2027; deterioration in PSA's leverage or dividend coverage.
- **Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is PSA's first post-close quarterly report, which will provide the initial read on integration and any updated synergy commentary. The NSA ticker itself requires no further monitoring.

## Conclusion

NSA is a completed, delisted acquisition, not an investable standalone equity. Any forward analysis should pivot to Public Storage's ability to execute the integration and capture the guided synergies.

The recommendation is **HOLD** with an **uncertain** rationale, reflecting that the investment decision has migrated to PSA rather than any conviction on NSA as a going concern. Holders should evaluate the position as PSA equity received at the 0.14 exchange ratio; there is no independent NSA thesis to act on. The scores reflect a mid-range, neutral profile appropriate to a resolved event where the standalone entity has ceased to exist and forward exposure carries ordinary large-cap REIT risk without a distinct, actionable edge.
