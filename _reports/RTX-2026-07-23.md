**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RTX has delivered one of the cleaner post-merger recovery stories in large-cap aerospace and defense. Revenue grew 9.7% to $88.6B in 2025 with organic growth across all three segments, operating margin expanded to 10.5% from 5.2% two years earlier, diluted EPS reached $4.96, and operating cash flow hit $10.6B. Backlog is at a record $268B at year-end 2025 and roughly $289B by Q2 2026, split $161B commercial and $107B defense, giving multi-year revenue visibility. Management raised 2026 guidance to $95-96B in sales and $7.10-7.25 in adjusted EPS — the only one of the three primes to lift its outlook after Q1 — and the balance sheet is deleveraging (total debt down to $37.9B, Moody's outlook upgraded to Baa1/stable). The Pratt & Whitney GTF powder-metal problem has transitioned from an engineering crisis to an MRO-throughput problem, with the accrued liability falling to $0.7B from $1.7B.

The complication is price. At roughly $209 (the reference level in the options and analyst data; the most recent trade in the price history is ~$195), RTX changes hands at ~30x trailing GAAP EPS and ~27-29x the 2026 adjusted EPS guide. The three engines of the bull thesis — the commercial-aftermarket annuity, the defense backlog conversion, and Golden Dome optionality — are consensus, and the shares have already re-rated ~30% over the trailing twelve months. Estimate revisions are flat (current-year +0.8% over 90 days, current-quarter -1.0%), which is not the accelerating-revision signal that would justify paying up. The company trades below the peer-median P/E of 41.3, but that median is distorted by GE Aerospace (41x) and Howmet (61x); against the more comparable defense primes Lockheed (26.6x) and General Dynamics (22.6x), RTX carries a premium that its slower-converting, GAAP-muddied earnings do not obviously earn.

Two genuinely new overhangs deserve weight beyond the standard risk boilerplate. First, a January 2026 Executive Order empowers the Secretary of War to restrict dividends, buybacks, and executive comp at contractors deemed to have underperformed, a novel capital-allocation risk RTX explicitly flags as potentially adverse to its share price, and one that lands as buybacks are already paused. Second, RTX operates under two Deferred Prosecution Agreements, an SEC order, and an ITAR consent agreement with an independent monitor installed in Q1 2026; a breach carries debarment risk and constrains M&A flexibility. Neither is priced. We rate RTX a HOLD (growth_hold), a high-quality compounder whose entry price offers no margin of safety.

## Company Overview

RTX (formerly Raytheon Technologies, the product of the 2020 Raytheon/United Technologies merger) is one of the "big three" U.S. defense primes and one of two large commercial-engine OEMs on the A320neo platform. It operates three segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B 2025 sales, 16.3% operating margin) — avionics, power and electrical systems, interiors, landing and communications/navigation systems. Boeing and Airbus together are ~16% of Collins sales.
- **Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B 2025 sales, 7.9% operating margin) — the GTF geared-turbofan family powering A320neo/A220/E-Jets E2, and the F135 that exclusively powers the F-35. Airbus is ~29% of P&W sales.
- **Raytheon** ($28.0B 2025 sales, 11.5% operating margin) — integrated air and missile defense (Patriot, LTAMDS, NASAMS), missiles (AMRAAM, Tomahawk, SM-3/SM-6, AIM-9X, Stinger, Javelin), radars (SPY-6), hypersonics.

The 2025 sales mix is 48% commercial aerospace, 38% U.S. government direct, 8% foreign military sales, and 7% foreign direct; 47% of total sales are international. The commercial aftermarket franchise (spare parts, MRO, long-term service agreements) is the durable, high-margin annuity at the core of the investment case.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year recovery is visible across every line:

|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $68.9B | $80.7B | $88.6B |
| Operating profit | $3.6B | $6.5B | $9.3B |
| Operating margin | 5.2% | 8.1% | 10.5% |
| Net income | $3.2B | $4.8B | $6.7B |
| Diluted EPS | $2.23 | $3.55 | $4.96 |
| Operating cash flow | $7.9B | $7.2B | $10.6B |
| Free cash flow | — | — | $7.9B |

Revenue grew $8.9B organically in 2025, with all three segments contributing (P&W +$4.8B, Collins +$2.6B, Raytheon +$1.7B). The 2023 trough was distorted by the $5.4B revenue / $2.9B pre-tax GTF powder-metal charge; the recovery since is real, driven by air-traffic-led aftermarket volume and defense backlog conversion. P&W swung from a negative operating margin in 2023 to +7.9%, Collins reached 16.3%, and Raytheon 11.5%.

The balance sheet is improving rather than stressed. Total debt fell from $41.3B, debt/total-capital improved to 36% from 40%, cash was $7.4B, and Moody's upgraded its outlook to Baa1/stable in March 2025 (S&P affirmed BBB+/stable in June 2025). The reported Altman Z-score of 1.45 ("distress") is a false signal here; it reflects the asset- and goodwill-heavy post-merger balance sheet (low EBIT/assets and revenue/assets ratios), not liquidity stress. With operating cash flow of $10.6B, an undrawn $5B revolver, investment-grade ratings, and no near-term maturity cliff, the balance sheet is a source of resilience. The current ratio is thin at 1.03, which is normal for a working-capital-intensive prime with large advance-payment liabilities.

Two earnings-quality caveats matter for how the multiple reads. Roughly $2.0B/year of acquisition-accounting amortization (~$1.15 of EPS drag) depresses GAAP results, while ~$1.2B/year of non-service pension income flatters them. Adjusted EPS, the guidance basis, strips both. This is why the trailing GAAP P/E of ~30x overstates the underlying multiple; on the 2026 adjusted guide the shares trade closer to 27-29x, which is still a full price.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in the forward estimates: four consecutive beats, ranging +4.6% to +19.5%, with the last two quarters at +12.6% and +16.1%. Estimate revisions are flat, not rising: next-year +0.7% over 90 days. This is a business delivering against expectations, not one where consensus is racing to catch up.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars, all of which are structurally supported but broadly recognized.

**Commercial aftermarket.** The narrowbody air-traffic recovery is driving high-margin aftermarket volume (P&W commercial aftermarket +$2.9B, Collins +$1.4B in 2025). With airframe production slots — not demand — the binding constraint, RTX's services annuity has multi-year runway. This is the highest-quality earnings stream in the portfolio.

**Defense backlog.** Defense bookings ran ~$61B in 2025 (Raytheon ~$40B), supporting the $107B defense backlog. Global demand is elevated on Ukraine, Middle East, and Indo-Pacific threats, and RTX's air-and-missile-defense and munitions franchises (Patriot, NASAMS, AMRAAM, SM-family) are directly in the demand path. Roughly 25% of total backlog converts to revenue within twelve months, which underpins the 2026 sales guide.

**Golden Dome optionality.** The July 2025 reconciliation act added $156.2B to defense through 2029, including $24.4B for the Golden Dome missile-defense shield and $25.4B for munitions and supply-chain resiliency. RTX is a leading integrated air-and-missile-defense supplier and well-positioned for share, but these awards are not yet booked; this is optionality, not a modeled contribution.

Recent commercial momentum reinforces the durability of the GTF franchise despite the powder-metal saga: Farnborough 2026 produced firm GTF wins from British Airways (33 firm + 30 option A320neos) and lessor Jackson Square Aviation, and the GTF Advantage received FAA/EASA certification in 2025 (up to 8% more thrust, ~1% better fuel burn). The July 2025 tax act, restoring full R&D expensing and immediate capex deductibility, is cash-tax positive.

The growth prospects are genuine and broad-based, which is why we score the dimension above midpoint. But mid-single-digit organic revenue growth with margin recovery that has largely already occurred is a solid compounder, and the flat estimate revisions confirm that consensus already models the recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

RTX is fully valued. The shares trade at ~30x trailing GAAP EPS and ~27-29x the midpoint of the 2026 adjusted EPS guide ($7.18). The analyst consensus mean target of $215.41 and median of $220 sit only 3-5% above the reference price, with a low of $180 and a high of $243, a range that implies roughly symmetric-to-modestly-favorable risk/reward, not the asymmetry that would warrant a BUY.

The peer table requires care. The stated peer-median P/E of 41.3 is inflated by GE Aerospace and Howmet (61.4x), both of which are pure commercial-aerospace plays riding the same aftermarket cycle at richer multiples. Against the defense-prime cohort that better matches RTX's government-weighted mix (46% of sales are U.S. government direct plus FMS), Lockheed and General Dynamics, RTX's forward multiple is a premium. That premium is partly defensible: RTX has the larger commercial-aftermarket franchise and stronger organic growth than either LMT or GD, and it was the only prime to raise 2026 guidance. But the premium is not obviously mispriced in RTX's favor, and the GAAP earnings are muddied by the amortization drag and pension-income benefit discussed above.

On the calibration point that cheapness has been a poor predictor and that analyst targets act as floors on inflection names trading below 30x with rising estimates, RTX does not qualify for that treatment. Its estimates are flat, not rising, so the consensus target is not stale in the way it would be for a genuine inflection stock. A stock at 27-29x forward with mid-single-digit organic growth and no accelerating revision velocity is priced for continued execution, which means beats are largely expected (limited re-rating) while any operational stumble (a GTF litigation surprise, a negative EAC adjustment, an EO-driven capital-return restriction) carries asymmetric downside. The absolute dividend yield of ~1.5% offers little support.

We assign a valuation score of 5.0 — neither a value trap nor a bargain, a quality business at a price that discounts the visible good news.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX's competitive position is strong and broadly stable, with a modestly improving trajectory in commercial aftermarket offset by procurement-side pressure in defense.

On the commercial side, the A320neo is a duopoly engine platform (P&W GTF vs. CFM LEAP). The powder-metal issue dented GTF's competitive perception, but CFM's LEAP has had its own durability problems (with lower grounding impact), and the new GTF wins from British Airways and lessors demonstrate that customers continue to select the platform. The GTF Advantage certification improves the competitive spec. The aftermarket annuity — spares, MRO, long-term service agreements — is a durable, high-margin moat that widens with the installed base.

On the defense side, RTX is one of three primes with sole-source or duopoly positions across air-and-missile defense and munitions. The trajectory here is mixed: demand is structurally elevated, but the Department of War is pushing more fixed-price development, Other Transaction Authority awards, and IP/data-rights demands, all of which pressure margins and shift risk onto the contractor. Non-traditional entrants (startups, commercial contractors) are increasingly competing for share in the autonomy and software layers. RTX's cumulative TSR of 287.6 vs. its peer index of 230.5 confirms it has outperformed the A&D peer group.

We score competitive position at 7.0 — a strong, wide-moat position with a slightly improving commercial trajectory, tempered by defense procurement headwinds and the residual reputational cost of the GTF saga. This is a step down from the prior 7.5-8.5 readings, reflecting the crystallization of procurement-reform pressure and the fixed-price risk shift rather than any loss of share.

## Risk Assessment

**GTF powder-metal matter.** The single largest idiosyncratic risk, now transitioning from crisis to throughput problem. At peak ~650 A320neo-family aircraft were grounded; RTX took the $2.9B pre-tax charge in 2023. Roughly $0.7B cash impact is expected in 2026. AOG levels remain elevated through 2026, and one operator has indicated the A320neo fix could persist 2-3 more years, with the bottleneck now MRO shop capacity and parts throughput. The residual risk has shifted to customer litigation: Italy's ITA Airways was weighing a lawsuit in June 2026 over grounded aircraft, which would be exposure beyond negotiated compensation.

**January 2026 Executive Order.** The Secretary of War may restrict dividends, share repurchases, and executive-comp incentives at contractors deemed to have underperformed or under-invested in U.S. government contracts. RTX explicitly warns this could adversely affect its stock price. It lands as buybacks are already effectively paused during deleveraging, removing what would normally be an EPS tailwind and placing the dividend under a policy shadow. This risk is not priced.

**Compliance monitorship.** RTX operates under two Deferred Prosecution Agreements (FCPA/Middle East bribery; defective-pricing fraud), an SEC administrative order, and a State Department consent agreement (ITAR violations), with an independent monitor installed in Q1 2026. A breach could trigger criminal prosecution, fines, or suspension/debarment from government contracting, and the terms constrain divestiture and M&A flexibility.

**Fixed-price development risk.** Negative EAC adjustments totaled -$386M in 2025 (improved from -$648M in 2023). Fixed-price development work carries cost-overrun risk RTX bears entirely, and the DoW's push toward more fixed-price contracting increases this exposure.

**Supply chain, tariffs, and concentration.** Persistent disruption in rare earths, castings/forgings, and titanium, with dependence on foreign single-source suppliers; U.S. tariffs and counter-tariffs since February 2025 are a live cost pressure on Collins and P&W margins. Customer concentration (Airbus ~29% of P&W, U.S. government 38% direct) and China sanctions on the Raytheon business (Taiwan arms sales) are ongoing overhangs.

We score risk factors at 5.5 — the GTF cash drag is now bounded and understood, but the novel EO and the compliance monitorship are genuine, unpriced, asymmetric-downside items that keep the score below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options profile is defensive. ATM IV is rich at 40.0% versus 24.8% one-year historical volatility (a 15.2-point spread, 72nd percentile within the chain), the put/call skew is elevated at 28.3%, and the term structure is in backwardation (40.0% near-term vs. 26.3% long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress consistent with an approaching earnings catalyst. IV has already compressed 7.7 points (47.7% → 40.0%) as the price rose 8.1% into the reference date, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 0.94 to 0.52, so positioning has turned less bearish. Unusual activity is concentrated in upside calls ($230, $215, $212 strikes) rather than protective puts, which tempers the bearish read from the elevated near-term skew. The options market is neither confirming a strong directional bull thesis nor flagging acute distress; it is pricing event risk around the next print, which argues for patience on entry rather than chasing into rich near-term vol.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($243, ~16% upside).** Commercial aftermarket compounds as narrowbody traffic and GTF fleet returns accelerate; the A220/E2 fleets recover on schedule and A320neo AOG normalizes faster than the 2-3 year worst case. Golden Dome awards begin converting to bookings, adding a visible growth leg to the defense backlog. Margins continue expanding toward pre-crisis P&W levels, 2026 adjusted EPS comes in at the high end ($7.25) with 2027 tracking toward $8+, and the deleveraging completes, freeing capital return. The multiple holds at ~30x forward on the improving mix.

**Bear Case ($180, ~8% downside from ~$195, ~14% from the ~$209 reference).** ITA Airways litigation opens a wider customer-claims front on GTF beyond negotiated compensation; MRO throughput disappoints and the A320neo grounding drags into 2028. The January 2026 EO is invoked or credibly threatened, forcing a dividend/buyback restriction that removes the capital-return support and triggers multiple compression toward the defense-prime cohort (22-27x). A negative EAC adjustment on fixed-price development work, or a compliance-monitor finding, adds an idiosyncratic shock. On these, EPS holds but the multiple derates.

The base case sits in between: RTX executes the 2026 guide ($7.18 adjusted EPS midpoint), the GTF cash drag runs off as accrued, and the shares track earnings growth without meaningful multiple change. That is a mid-single-digit total return including the dividend, respectable but not compelling against the risk profile.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).**

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release, with the GFY27 appropriations process and any Golden Dome award announcements as interim catalysts. Continued clarity on GTF MRO throughput (A220/E2 returns by end-2026) is the key operational milestone.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$180s (near the analyst-low target of $180), compressing the forward multiple below ~25x adjusted, would restore asymmetry.
- Golden Dome awards converting to booked backlog (adding a modeled growth leg beyond consensus), combined with a turn to positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year revisions moving above +5% over 90 days).
- Formal clearance of the compliance monitorship or explicit exemption from the January 2026 EO restrictions, removing the two unpriced overhangs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Invocation of the EO to restrict RTX's dividend or buybacks, or a compliance-monitor breach carrying debarment risk.
- ITA Airways (or a broader group of operators) filing GTF litigation that materially expands liability beyond the negotiated ~$0.7B/year cash impact.
- A material negative EAC adjustment (>$500M) on fixed-price development work, or evidence the A320neo grounding is extending beyond 2028.

**Base case target: $215** — approximately 30x the 2026 adjusted EPS midpoint, consistent with the current multiple applied to the guided earnings, implying roughly 3% upside from the ~$209 reference and ~10% from the most recent ~$195 trade. This sits between the bear ($180) and bull ($243) scenarios.

## Conclusion

RTX is a high-quality aerospace and defense compounder in a genuine multi-year recovery, with record backlog, all-segment organic growth, recovered margins, strengthening credit, and a management team that raised 2026 guidance when its peers did not. The fundamentals confirm the improving trajectory that six prior HOLD reads have tracked. The problem is entirely one of price and asymmetry: at ~27-30x forward adjusted EPS the market has priced the aftermarket annuity, the defense backlog, and Golden Dome optionality, estimate revisions are flat rather than accelerating, and consensus targets sit only 3-5% above the reference price. Layered on top are two unpriced, asymmetric-downside overhangs, the novel January 2026 Executive Order threatening dividends and buybacks, and the DPA/ITAR compliance monitorship, that cap the upside case and land as buybacks are paused.

The declining score trajectory (6.5 in late May to 6.1 here, with management quality, competitive position, and risk factors all easing) reflects the crystallization of these overhangs, not deterioration in the operating business. We continue to rate RTX a HOLD for growth, worth owning for the trajectory but offering no margin of safety at the entry price. We would become buyers in the low-$180s, on Golden Dome bookings converting with a positive revision turn, or on clearance of the compliance and EO overhangs.
