## Executive Summary

Cheniere Energy is the largest U.S. LNG producer and the second-largest globally, operating ~52 mtpa of liquefaction capacity across two Gulf Coast terminals. The business model approximates a toll road: 90%+ of production is contracted under long-term take-or-pay agreements with fixed fees and 115% Henry Hub pass-through, generating $6.94B in adjusted EBITDA and $5.29B in distributable cash flow in FY2025. The company has $290.6B in contracted future revenues extending through the mid-2030s.

The current geopolitical environment is a significant positive catalyst. With Qatari LNG supply severely disrupted by the Iran-Hormuz conflict, U.S. Gulf Coast LNG has emerged as among the most reliable sources of global supply. Cheniere is operating at maximum utilization, pricing power on uncontracted volumes is elevated, and negotiating leverage for future contracts has meaningfully improved. The stock has risen 43% YTD reflecting this, but at ~17x FY2026 forward earnings and ~11x adjusted EBITDA, the valuation still offers upside if the geopolitical disruption persists.

The primary risk is binary: a diplomatic resolution of the Iran conflict would reduce global LNG prices and unwind 15-25% of the stock's geopolitical premium. Structural leverage is appropriate for the project-finance model but requires ongoing capital market access for $11.8B in maturities due 2027-2030. Concentrated insider selling in March at ~$290-300 reflects rational diversification post-RSU vesting, not a fundamental concern.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates two liquefaction terminals:

- **Sabine Pass (Louisiana):** 30+ mtpa, 6 operational trains, 5 storage tanks, 3 marine berths
- **Corpus Christi (Texas):** ~30 mtpa expected total capacity; Stage 3 (7 trains, FERC/DOE-approved for 11.45 mtpa total, 4 operational as of Dec 2025, remaining 3 completing 2026) plus Midscale Trains 8 & 9 under construction (expected 2H 2028)

The company has delivered 4,610+ cumulative cargoes (315 million tonnes) to 40+ countries. Its customer base includes approximately 30 long-term counterparties (no single customer >10% of revenue), spanning major utilities and energy companies globally. The contracted revenue structure provides quasi-utility visibility: customers pay fixed fees regardless of whether they take delivery, with variable fees passing through 115% of feedstock costs.

**Growth pipeline:** Beyond current operations, Cheniere has an SPL Expansion Project (3 trains, ~20 mtpa, in FERC permitting) and a CCL Expansion Project (4 trains, ~24 mtpa, FERC application filed February 2026). Combined potential capacity exceeds 90 mtpa if all projects are sanctioned.

The GAAP income volatility (net income swung from $9.9B to $3.3B to $5.3B) is driven by mark-to-market on IPM derivative contracts — approximately $3.6B of unrealized derivative gains inflated FY2025 results. Adjusted EBITDA of $6.94B (+13% YoY) is the appropriate earnings measure, reflecting true operational performance.

**Key Profitability Ratios:**
- Operating Margin: 45.6%
- Net Margin (attributable to Cheniere): ~26.7% ($5.33B / $19.98B)
- ROE: approximately 67-78% (varies by methodology; extremely high due to leverage)
- ROA: 14.2%

**Cash Flow Generation:**

FY2025 operating cash flow was $5.5B. Free cash flow of $2.46B (12.3% margin) is depressed by $3.1B of growth capex for Stage 3 completion and Trains 8 & 9 construction. As capex normalizes post-2028, FCF could converge toward the $5B+ distributable cash flow figure, though future expansion project FIDs would extend the elevated capex period.

**Capital Structure:**

- Total debt: $23.0B (weighted average rate on senior notes: 4.65%, predominantly fixed-rate, with a small portion of variable-rate credit facility borrowings)
- Cheniere stockholders' equity: $7.9B; total stockholders' equity including noncontrolling interests: $12.9B
- Debt/Cheniere stockholders' equity: ~2.9x; debt/total equity including NCI: ~1.8x
- Available liquidity: $8.76B (cash + credit facilities)
- Credit ratings: BBB+ (S&P), BBB (Fitch) — investment grade, upgraded in 2025

The leverage is structural to the project-finance model. Each terminal subsidiary (SPL, CQP, CCH) operates as an independent capital structure with ring-fenced debt supported by contracted revenue. The Altman Z-Score of 1.41 is misleading here — the company's investment-grade ratings, $5.5B operating cash flow, and $8.8B liquidity confirm no actual distress.

**Maturities:** $11.8B due 2027-2030, requiring continued capital market access. Given investment-grade ratings and the contracted revenue profile, refinancing risk is manageable but non-trivial in a rising-rate environment.

**Capital Return:**

- $10B share repurchase authorization through 2030 (~17.5% of current market cap)
- FY2025: repurchased 12.1M shares for $2.7B at $221.55 average
- Target: ~175M shares outstanding (from ~210M currently), implying ~35M more shares to retire
- Annual dividend: $2.22/share (~0.8% yield) — nominal; shareholder return is primarily via buyback
- Run-rate DCF target: ~$30/share on reduced share count

- Trains 8 & 9 (31.8% complete) expected 2H 2028 → additional capacity
- 2026 EBITDA guidance: $6.75-$7.25B — set in February before geopolitical escalation, likely conservative

**Medium-term (2028-2032):**

- SPL Expansion (3 trains, ~20 mtpa) and CCL Expansion (4 trains, ~24 mtpa) in permitting
- Combined FID would roughly double current capacity over the next decade
- These projects benefit from brownfield economics (existing infrastructure, pipeline connections, permitting familiarity)

**Global LNG Demand Backdrop:**

WoodMac forecasts global LNG trade growing from ~410 mtpa (2025) to ~671 mtpa by 2040. Even without geopolitical disruption, the secular growth story is intact, driven by coal-to-gas switching in Asia and European energy security needs.

## Valuation Assessment

At $272.55, Cheniere trades at:

- ~11.3x trailing GAAP EPS ($24.13) — but trailing GAAP EPS is derivative-inflated and non-representative
- ~17.1x FY2026 forward consensus EPS
- ~11.5x Adjusted EBITDA ($6.94B) on enterprise value of ~$80B (using company-reported Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA)
- ~9.1x run-rate DCF/share target of $30

**Relative to peers:** Pure-play LNG infrastructure companies typically trade at 8-12x EBITDA. Cheniere's premium (at the upper end) reflects its scale, contract visibility, and expansion optionality. With the geopolitical tailwind, the premium is justified.

**Analyst consensus:** Based on available data, the mean analyst target is approximately $274-$276 with a high target around $301-$322. The majority of covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Overweight.

**The key valuation question:** Is the geopolitical premium sustainable? If we assume Cheniere can sustain $7.5B+ EBITDA in a world of elevated LNG prices (vs. $6.75-7.25B guidance set before the conflict), then ~10.5x EBITDA produces a $79B EV → ~$265/share equity value (roughly current). At 11x EBITDA on $7.5B, the equity value approaches $295. The upside comes from (a) EBITDA beating the high end of guidance, and (b) multiple expansion as the expansion FIDs demonstrate growth trajectory.

The current valuation offers reasonable asymmetry to the upside given the geopolitical shift in global LNG supply.

## Competitive Landscape

**Competitive Position — Strengthening:**

1. **Geographic insulation from Hormuz:** The single most important competitive advantage in the current environment. Cheniere's Gulf Coast facilities are completely insulated from the Strait of Hormuz disruption that has severely impaired Qatari deliveries.

2. **Scale and track record:** 10 years of continuous operations, 4,610+ cargoes, established customer relationships. Repeat contracts (CPC Taiwan through 2050) demonstrate customer confidence.

3. **Brownfield expansion economics:** Expanding at existing sites costs less per unit of capacity than greenfield projects, providing structural cost advantage for growth.

4. **Henry Hub structural advantage:** U.S. natural gas prices ($2-3/MMBtu) remain far below international pricing, creating attractive netbacks for U.S. LNG even when international prices moderate.

5. **Contract portfolio depth:** $290.6B in contracted future revenues provides visibility unmatched by any competitor. The take-or-pay structure means revenue is largely decoupled from commodity price cycles.

**Competitive threats:** Venture Global LNG is a primary domestic competitor, with its Plaquemines facility now in operation. Golden Pass LNG shipped its first cargo in April 2026 and, when fully complete, will be the third-highest nominal-capacity U.S. LNG export project behind Sabine Pass and Plaquemines. Canadian LNG projects (LNG Canada) are also entering the market. However, Cheniere's operational track record, contract depth, and brownfield expansion optionality remain differentiators.

The EIA estimates approximately 20% of global LNG trade normally transits the Strait of Hormuz, primarily from Qatar. This creates a potentially multi-year window of enhanced pricing power and contract negotiation leverage for U.S. Gulf Coast suppliers, though the duration depends on conflict dynamics and the degree to which Qatar can find alternative arrangements.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Binary Geopolitical Resolution (Primary Risk):**
A diplomatic resolution would imply downside of $40-70/share at current price. The probability and timing of such a resolution remain highly uncertain.

**2. Derivative Earnings Volatility:**
The $2.9B Level 3 derivative asset (IPM agreements) creates substantial GAAP earnings unpredictability. A reversal in forward curve shape could produce a multi-billion dollar mark-to-market loss in any quarter, creating headline risk even if underlying operations are stable. Cheniere's quarterly GAAP earnings history reflects this — large reported "beats" or "misses" versus normalized EPS consensus are frequently derivative-driven rather than operational.

**3. Leverage and Refinancing:**
At current investment-grade ratings and liquidity, the maturity wall is manageable. The risk elevates if rates continue higher (10Y approaching 5%) or if credit conditions deteriorate.

**4. Construction Execution:**
Trains 8 & 9 ($2.9B EPC with Bechtel) and later expansion projects carry typical construction risks — cost overruns, permitting delays, supply chain disruptions.

**5. Regulatory/Trade:**
The USTR's Section 301 investigation initially proposed mandating U.S.-built LNG vessels, though reports indicate that penalties on U.S. LNG companies were subsequently scrapped in October 2025 while promoting U.S. shipbuilding. The regulatory landscape remains fluid. EU tariff/trade tensions could affect European customer decisions on future contracts.

**6. Property Tax Escalation:**
Legacy incentives expiring 2026-2027 at both terminals will increase operating costs, though the magnitude is manageable relative to the $7B EBITDA base.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 58.3% is extraordinarily elevated relative to 1-year historical volatility of 26.4% — a 31.9pp premium. The term structure is in sharp backwardation (58.3% near-term vs. 31.4% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing a specific near-term event or stress window rather than sustained elevated uncertainty. Put/call ratios are skewed bullish (OI ratio 0.38, volume ratio 0.21), and from May 1 to May 4 the ATM IV declined 10.2pp while price rose 1.3%, suggesting resolution of some near-term uncertainty.

This options profile is consistent with heightened geopolitical event risk (the Iran naval confrontation) creating elevated short-term vol. The near-term vol premium decays quickly if no adverse catalyst materializes.

Short interest at 2.2% of float (1.1 days to cover) is minimal, indicating no meaningful bearish positioning by institutional investors.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($310-340):**
- Iran conflict persists or escalates, keeping global LNG prices elevated through 2027
- FY2026 EBITDA exceeds guidance high end ($7.5B+) on geopolitical premium
- Expansion project FIDs (SPL and/or CCL) de-risk the growth pipeline
- Accelerated buyback at elevated cash flows reduces shares toward 175M target faster
- Multiple re-rates toward 12-13x EBITDA on growth visibility
- Math: 12x × $7.5B EBITDA = $90B EV → ~$318/share equity

**Bear Case ($220-240):**
- Diplomatic resolution reduces global LNG prices to pre-conflict levels
- FY2026 EBITDA comes in at low end of guidance ($6.75B)
- Expansion FIDs delayed by permitting or market conditions
- Mark-to-market derivative reversal creates headline earnings miss
- Multiple compresses to 9x EBITDA
- Math: 9x × $6.75B = $60.75B EV → ~$180/share equity (extreme); 10x × $7.0B = $70B EV → ~$224/share equity (more likely bear)

**Base Case ($295-310):**
The conflict persists in some form (base case: sustained disruption with periodic partial openings), keeping LNG prices elevated. Cheniere delivers EBITDA near the high end of guidance ($7.1-7.3B). Share count reduces by 5-8M in 2026 via buyback. The market values the company at 10.5-11x EBITDA.

Math: 10.75x × $7.2B EBITDA = $77.4B EV → ~$260/share equity + $30-35/share for expansion optionality and continued buyback → $290-310/share.

## Conclusion

Cheniere Energy represents a high-quality expression of the LNG supply thesis in public markets. The combination of (1) contracted toll-road economics providing downside protection, (2) maximum utilization during a geopolitically-driven global supply shortage, (3) $10B buyback reducing float by ~20% through 2030, and (4) brownfield expansion optionality worth 40+ mtpa of potential capacity creates a multi-year compounding story.

At ~17x forward earnings and ~11.5x adjusted EBITDA, the valuation offers reasonable asymmetry if the Hormuz disruption persists and Cheniere captures enhanced pricing on uncontracted volumes and future contract negotiations. The primary risk is binary (diplomatic resolution), and the duration and severity of the geopolitical disruption remain the key variables for the investment case.

Maintain BUY. The competitive position score trajectory (8.5 → 9.0 in prior reports) reflects progressive recognition that the Iran conflict has elevated Cheniere's global market position. I hold this at 8.5 rather than 9.0 given that some of this advantage is conflict-dependent and impermanent.

These corrections reduce the quantified upside case relative to the original presentation.
