**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MGE Energy is among the highest-quality small-cap regulated utilities in the United States: a vertically integrated electric and gas monopoly serving an affluent, university-and-government-anchored territory in and around Madison, Wisconsin, backed by sector-leading credit ratings and one of the most constructive state regulators in the country. The 2026/2027 rate case was settled unanimously with intervenors, lifting the authorized ROE to 9.8% and growing authorized electric rate base from $1.24B (2025) to $1.54B (2027), an ~11% CAGR that forms the core earnings engine. Fifty consecutive years of dividend increases and a fortress balance sheet (58% common equity ratio, 41.1% covenant leverage vs. a 65% cap) round out a business whose quality is not in dispute.

The debate here is entirely about price. At $82.42 the stock trades at ~22x trailing and forward EPS versus a peer median of ~17.9x, and every published analyst target sits below the current price (high $81, mean $77.25, low $74). Near-term consensus is drifting lower — current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates have been cut roughly 5-6% over the past 90 days — and 2025's headline 11.7% EPS growth was materially weather-inflated (heating degree days +18%, gas volumes +14%), overstating the underlying high-single-digit trajectory that rate-base growth actually supports. The data-center rate-class initiative is a genuine but unquantified optionality, and management's ratepayer-protective posture (seeking a dedicated tariff, plus a Madison city moratorium on new large-scale data centers) means upside is uncertain and not yet in the numbers.

We maintain a HOLD (income_hold). The dividend yield of ~2.3% is well-covered, the defensive cash flows are reliable, and the regulatory relationship is a durable asset. The premium multiple offers no margin of safety, the FCF profile is turning negative on a heavy capex cycle that will require equity issuance, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of the financing model. This is a full-price holding appropriate to own for yield and stability but not a source of asymmetric return.

## Company Overview

MGE Energy is a Madison, Wisconsin holding company whose principal subsidiary, Madison Gas and Electric (chartered 1896), operates five segments:

- **Regulated electric** (~72% of regulated revenue): generation and distribution to ~170,000 customers across a compact 264-square-mile territory in Dane County, ~91% concentrated in Madison, Fitchburg, Middleton, and Monona.
- **Regulated gas** (~28% of regulated revenue): distribution to ~180,000 customers over 1,722 square miles in seven south-central Wisconsin counties.
- **Nonregulated energy**: MGE Power Elm Road (8.33% interest in two Oak Creek coal units) and MGE Power West Campus (UW-Madison cogeneration), consolidated as VIEs and leased to MGE. Together these contributed $24.8M of net income in 2025 (18% of total), earning a stable return via the lease structure.
- **Transmission investments**: a 3.6% equity stake in American Transmission Company (ATC) and 4.4% in ATC Holdco, generating $13.0M of other income (~6.6% of 2025 net income).

The moat is a textbook regulated monopoly: an affluent, stable, low-churn service territory, a historically constructive Public Service Commission of Wisconsin (PSCW), no wildfire exposure, minimal merchant-generation risk, and top-tier credit. The company's growth levers are rate-base expansion via a renewable-and-storage buildout and grid investment, plus potential new large-load (data-center) demand.

## Financial Analysis

MGE's financials reflect a steady, rate-base-driven compounder:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income ($M) | 111.0 | 117.7 | 120.6 | 135.9 |
| EPS ($) | 3.07 | 3.25 | 3.33 | 3.72 |
| Operating income ($M) | 137.7 | 146.4 | 146.3 | 170.7 |
| Total assets ($M) | 2,518 | 2,675 | 2,828 | 3,155 |
| Stockholders' equity ($M) | 1,082 | 1,140 | 1,230 | 1,304 |

Normalized underlying growth is closer to the high-single-digit rate that the ~11% authorized rate-base CAGR supports over time. Margins are strong and stable: 32.1% operating margin, 25.5% net margin, and 10.4% ROE against a 9.7-9.8% authorized return, meaning MGE is earning at or slightly above allowed.

Cash flow is where the pressure shows. Operating cash flow was $263M in 2025 versus $278M in 2024, while capex rose to $343M from $237M. The five-year plan calls for $395M (2026) and $580M (2027) before settling to ~$300-315M annually through 2030. This is a negative-FCF phase, and MGE plans to resume equity issuance via its DSPP/DRIP beginning March 2026 to help fund the program. The mechanism is straightforward: capex exceeds operating cash flow, external capital is required, some combination of debt and equity dilution follows, and per-share earnings growth runs below rate-base growth. A higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of both debt and the equity component, compressing the spread between authorized ROE and the cost of new capital.

The balance sheet is conservative for a utility. The regulatory capital structure is ~59% equity / 37% long-term debt / 4% short-term debt; covenant leverage is 41.1% (holdco) and 44.5% (MGE) against a 65% maximum. MGE's 13-month rolling common equity ratio of 58.0% sits well above the 55% dividend-restriction floor. In January 2026 MGE issued $90M of senior notes and fully redeemed its legacy first-mortgage-bond indenture, modernizing its capital structure.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.15 ("distress") is a false signal here: the model structurally penalizes the low asset turnover (~0.17) and high leverage inherent to regulated utilities without crediting the cost-recovery framework. With investment-grade credit and a 58% equity ratio, bankruptcy risk is negligible. The current-ratio flag reflects normal reliance on commercial paper, not liquidity stress.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth engine is rate base, which translated through equity issuance and the earnings-sharing mechanism supports high-single-digit EPS growth over a multi-year horizon: solid, dependable, but unremarkable relative to the current multiple.

Three factors temper the growth thesis. First, 2025's 11.7% print is not a run-rate; normalizing for weather brings underlying growth back toward the rate-base trajectory. Second, near-term estimate revisions have turned modestly negative: current-quarter EPS was cut 6.2% over 30 days ($0.81 → $0.76), next-quarter cut 5.0% ($1.21 → $1.15), while current-year and next-year estimates are roughly flat-to-slightly-lower ($3.93 and $4.14). This is not the +30% revision velocity that would signal an earnings inflection; it is stable-to-softening consensus. Third, the earnings-sharing mechanism caps upside — under the 2024/2025 order, ROE above 9.7% is largely shared with or refunded to customers (100% retained only on the first 15 bps, 50% deferred on the next 60 bps, 100% refunded beyond).

The largest swing factor is data-center demand. The 2025 10-K added new risk-factor language on "growing interest from large-load customers," and in July 2026 MGE was reported to be seeking a dedicated PSCW data-center rate class to prevent existing customers from subsidizing new infrastructure. This posture de-risks the downside but also means the upside is speculative and absent from current estimates. The City of Madison's temporary moratorium on new large-scale data centers further caps near-term realization. If a large load materializes with an appropriate tariff, it becomes a durable rate-base catalyst; if not, the premium is harder to justify.

MGE deserves a premium to the median for its clean regulatory jurisdiction, affluent territory, top-tier credit, and 50-year dividend record. But a ~23% premium to the peer median, paired with a below-median 2.3% dividend yield and high-single-digit normalized growth, leaves no asymmetric upside. The market is not obviously mispricing this stock; if anything, the analyst community sees it as slightly rich — every published target sits below spot (high $81, mean $77.25, low $74, median $77). On inflection stocks I treat analyst targets as stale floors, but that principle does not apply here: there is no earnings inflection, estimates are drifting down not up, and the multiple already reflects the quality. When a high-quality name trades above every analyst target with softening near-term estimates, the targets are more likely a ceiling than a floor.

Applying the calibration lesson that "cheap" screens are anti-predictive and premium multiples on stable-growth names are vulnerable, the valuation score is low — not because the business is impaired, but because the current price offers no margin of safety and modest downside to fair value. A fair multiple of ~19-20x on ~$3.95-4.00 of normalized EPS implies roughly $75-80, below the current price. The valuation score of 4.5 reflects a full-price defensive holding, not a value trap (the recovery mechanism, rate-base growth, is intact) and not an opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

MGE's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. As a vertically integrated monopoly with an exclusive franchise, it faces no direct customer competition. Its structural advantages relative to peers are real: no wildfire liability (unlike Western utilities), minimal merchant-generation exposure, sector-leading credit, and a regulator that resolved the latest rate case by unanimous settlement rather than the contentious battles seen in other jurisdictions. Steady residential customer growth in the Madison territory and the rate-base buildout support a widening asset base.

The trajectory is positive at the margin but not a source of surprise upside the market has failed to price. The one genuinely underappreciated lever is large-load/data-center demand, and management's conservative posture means that optionality is real but not yet bankable. The renewable-and-storage buildout (~252 MW solar + 125 MW battery through 2030) expands the rate base but is exposed to a fast-moving tariff and tax regime that is industry-wide, not company-specific.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for MGE specifically:

1. **Weather normalization.** 2025's strong result was partly a weather tailwind. A reversion toward normal weather in 2026 is a headwind to reported growth and is already visible in the softening near-term estimates.

2. **Capex execution and cost recovery.** The five-year plan hinges on timely PSCW approval and full recovery. Several projects (Darien, Koshkonong, Columbia Energy Dome, RockGen) are already flagged as expected to exceed prior approvals; overruns above approved levels may not be recoverable.

3. **Negative FCF and equity dilution.** Resuming DSPP/DRIP issuance in March 2026 dilutes per-share growth. A higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the financing model and can compress the spread between authorized ROE and cost of new capital.

4. **Solar tariff and tax overhang.** The August 2025 Court of International Trade ruling allowing retroactive tariff collection, new AD/CVD investigations, and a Section 232 polysilicon probe are flagged as a potential "new and potentially material financial liability." OBBBA (July 2025) accelerates PTC/ITC phase-out for projects in service after 12/31/2027 unless construction begins by 7/4/2026, pressuring the back half of the renewable plan and creating incentive to pull capex forward.

5. **Small-territory concentration.** All operations sit in south-central Wisconsin; local economic, weather, or policy shocks have outsized impact.

6. **Governance transitions.** EVP Lynn Hobbie (31-year veteran) retired 12/31/2025, and two additional officer/director 8-Ks were filed in April and June 2026 (primary documents unread; most likely routine succession). The bench is being refreshed, which is orderly but worth monitoring.

Most of these are known and partially priced. The most dynamic is the solar tariff/tax regime, which is fast-moving and could pull capex and financing forward. The risk score of 5.5 reflects a business with few binary catastrophic risks but with real, non-trivial regulatory-recovery and financing exposures that a premium multiple does not cushion. This is a step down from the 6.5 in March, consistent with the expanded tariff/OBBBA disclosures and the negative-FCF turn.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress that is not obvious in the fundamentals. Near-term ATM IV of 47.4% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 21.3% (a 26-point spread, 73rd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (47.4% at 1 month vs. 27.8% at 6 months), which typically signals an imminent event. The nearest expiry implies a ±9.1% move. The skew is call-heavy (-7.8% at 1 month, -18.2% at 3 months), which is unusual for a defensive utility and suggests positioning for upside, plausibly around the data-center rate-class decision or the next earnings print. For a fundamentally low-beta name, this elevated IV argues against chasing entry at current levels and rewards patience; if the implied event resolves benignly, IV compression and a pullback could offer a better income-oriented entry. Short interest is unremarkable at 5.7% of float (4.9 days to cover).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90-95).** Rate base compounds at ~11% through 2027, the PSCW remains constructive, and a large data-center load materializes with a dedicated tariff that converts unpriced optionality into durable rate-base growth. Weather normalizes without a sharp reversal, the renewable plan avoids material tariff surprises, and the market continues to pay a premium for a scarce, clean, small-cap defensive compounder. Multiple holds at ~23-24x on rising EPS. This case requires the data-center upside to become bankable, which management's own posture suggests is uncertain.

**Bear Case ($68-72).** Weather reverts sharply, cutting 2026 reported EPS below trend; the data-center opportunity fails to materialize or is diluted by the ratepayer-protective tariff structure; solar tariffs and OBBBA phase-outs impair renewable-plan economics and force capex/financing forward; and a higher-for-longer rate path compresses the multiple toward the peer median. On ~$3.90 EPS at 18x, the stock falls to roughly $70, ~15% below spot — approximately the 52-week low of $72.16.

**Base Case ($77-80).** Rate-base growth delivers high-single-digit normalized EPS growth, weather partially normalizes, and the multiple drifts modestly toward but not to the peer median as the market rationalizes the premium against the negative-FCF profile. This converges with the analyst consensus (mean $77.25) and implies modest downside from the current price, offset by the ~2.3% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis is dividend yield, defensive cash flows, and best-in-class regulated quality, not appreciation. The premium multiple, negative-FCF turn, softening near-term estimates, and every analyst target below spot together argue against a BUY, while the durable rate-base growth and fortress balance sheet argue against an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$70s (52-week low $72.16 / analyst low $74), where the yield exceeds ~2.6% and the multiple compresses toward the peer median — a genuine margin of safety.
- A confirmed large-load/data-center customer signed under an approved PSCW rate class, converting the current optionality into quantified rate-base growth and pushing EPS estimates higher.
- A durable turn in estimate-revision velocity to positive across current-year and next-year windows.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse solar-tariff/OBBBA outcome that impairs the renewable-plan economics or forces a large, dilutive equity raise above plan.
- Erosion of the regulatory relationship (a contested rate case or an ROE cut below 9.8%).
- Multiple expansion above ~24x forward on flat-to-declining estimates, which would make the risk/reward clearly negative.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report and the outcome of the PSCW data-center rate-class filing, whichever comes first. The elevated near-term IV suggests one of these events is being priced within the coming month.

## Conclusion

MGE Energy is a genuinely high-quality regulated utility with a constructive regulator, an ~11% rate-base growth path, a fortress balance sheet, and a 50-year dividend-growth record. None of that is in question. What is in question is price: at ~22x earnings, a ~23% premium to the peer median, a below-median 2.3% yield, and trading above every published analyst target with near-term estimates drifting lower, the stock offers no asymmetric upside. The 2025 EPS strength was partly weather; the negative-FCF phase will require dilutive equity issuance into a higher-for-longer rate environment; and the data-center growth lever is deliberately being managed conservatively and is not yet in the numbers.

We rate MGEE a HOLD for income. Own it for the reliable dividend and defensive characteristics, size it as a low-beta anchor rather than a return driver, and look to add only on a pullback toward the low-$70s or a confirmed data-center catalyst. The trajectory of prior reports (declining overall score, three consecutive HOLDs) is confirmed rather than reversed by the current data: growth prospects remain solid but not accelerating, and the valuation gap that has kept this a HOLD has not closed.
