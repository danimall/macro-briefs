## Executive Summary

GE Aerospace is the world's largest commercial jet engine manufacturer by installed base (~50,000 commercial engines), operating as a pure-play aerospace company since the April 2024 GE Vernova spin-off. FY2025 revenue grew 18% to $45.9B with adjusted EPS of $6.37 (+38% YoY) and free cash flow of $7.7B (+24% YoY). Q1 2026 continued the streak with an EPS beat ($1.86 vs. approximately $1.60 consensus), representing the fourth consecutive quarter of earnings outperformance.

The core investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) a structural LEAP aftermarket wave beginning in 2027-2030 as the ~5,000+ installed LEAP engines enter first shop visit cycles, (2) expanding margins as the services mix recovers from a period of elevated equipment deliveries, and (3) $24B in planned capital returns through 2026 on top of a new $20B buyback authorization. The $190.6B backlog provides revenue visibility measured in decades.

The stock trades at ~41-43x FY2026 guided EPS ($7.10-$7.40), a meaningful premium to the peer median of 32.4x. This premium is justified by GE's growth rate, services annuity model, and margin expansion trajectory, though it leaves limited margin for error if the geopolitical environment deteriorates further or if airline economics compress under sustained high oil prices.

## Company Overview

GE Aerospace operates through two segments following the GE Vernova and GE HealthCare separations:

**Commercial Engines & Services (CES) — 73% of revenue:** The company's profit engine, built on 50,000+ installed commercial engines generating recurring shop visit revenue. Key platforms include CFM56 (mature fleet, massive aftermarket), LEAP (production ramp phase, ~1,800 deliveries in 2025), GEnx (787), and GE9X (sole-source on Boeing 777X). CES margins expanded to 26.6% in FY2025 from 23.7% in FY2023.

**Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT) — 23% of revenue:** Military engines (F110, F414, T901), avionics, and advanced propulsion. Growing at 11% with margins improving from 10.1% (2023) to 12.3% (2025). Recent wins include a $5B IDIQ for F110 engines and $1.6B from Hindustan Aeronautics for F404 engines.

**Corporate & Other (~4%):** Legacy run-off insurance (long-term care, ~201,700 policies), Bank BPH (Polish mortgage litigation, $2.3B estimated losses), and environmental remediation ($250M annually).

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term drivers (12-18 months):**
- LEAP delivery ramp: 1,802 units in 2025 (+28% YoY), with monthly delivery records in Q1 2026 (+22% YoY). Powder metal disk supply has materially improved, though management notes supply-chain constraints continue across the industry.
- Internal shop visit growth: +24% in FY2025. CFM56 mature fleet driving high-margin services revenue.
- Defense orders: $5B F110 IDIQ, $1.6B F404 India, T901 development acceleration.
- Asia revenue surge: $10.8B in 2025 (+49% YoY), reflecting Southeast Asian and Chinese aviation expansion.

**Medium-term catalyst (2027-2030):**
The LEAP aftermarket wave represents the defining growth catalyst. With an installed base approaching 5,000+ engines and typical first shop visit occurring at 5-7 years post-delivery, the initial LEAP production cohort (2016-2020) begins generating meaningful aftermarket revenue in 2027-2030. GE's consolidated services business carries significantly higher margins than equipment (companywide services gross margin was approximately 45% in FY2025 versus negative equipment gross margins as production ramps). Services RPO of $163B provides unprecedented visibility into this revenue stream.

**Long-term optionality:**
CFM RISE (next-gen open fan, targeting 20%+ fuel burn improvement) positions the GE/Safran JV for the next narrowbody platform cycle in the 2030s. Ground and flight testing planned this decade.

**Estimate revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates revised up +2.4% over 90 days. The revision momentum is positive but modest — insufficient to qualify as a strong BUY signal on estimate velocity alone. The +2.2% 30-day revision for current quarter suggests continued upward momentum post-Q1 beat.

## Competitive Landscape

GE Aerospace operates in a tripartite duopoly with Rolls-Royce and Pratt & Whitney (RTX):

**Narrowbody (the majority of commercial aircraft deliveries by units):** LEAP (GE/Safran) vs. GTF (P&W). Both engines power the A320neo; LEAP is exclusive on 737 MAX. P&W's GTF has faced severe durability issues requiring accelerated inspections, creating a temporary competitive advantage for LEAP in new orders.

**Widebody:** GE9X (sole-source on 777X), GEnx (787), CF6/GE90 legacy fleet. Rolls-Royce competes primarily here (Trent family). Boeing's eventual 777X certification will unlock GE9X production revenue and long-term services.

**Defense:** Competitive with P&W (F119/F135) and Rolls-Royce on specific platforms. GE's F110, F414, and new T901 provide portfolio breadth. GCAP participation through Avio Aero extends European defense positioning.

**Competitive trajectory is positive:** P&W's GTF problems, improving GE supply chain performance, and the sheer scale of the installed base (first-mover advantage in aftermarket lock-in) all favor GE over the next 3-5 years. The 50-50 JV structure with Safran (CFM International) provides partnership stability uncommon in the industry.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geopolitical/Oil Price Pressure (HIGH — near-term):** Elevated oil prices above $100/bbl directly pressure airline operating economics, potentially reducing future flying hours and deferring shop visits. Reports indicate the IRGC threatened strikes on multiple U.S. companies, including GE, in March 2026. Management flagged "elevated oil prices, fuel supply constraints" as a backdrop risk. A sustained peace deal has a low probability of holding given current diplomatic signals, creating a binary overlay: if oil normalizes toward pre-crisis levels, airline profitability improves and GE's medium-term services outlook brightens; if tensions escalate further, oil could spike significantly and airline stress would intensify.

**2. Tariff Impact on Service Agreements (MODERATE — ongoing):** Management explicitly noted unfavorable profitability impact on long-term service agreements from tariffs in Q1 2026. While zero-for-zero arrangements cover EU/UK/Japan/Korea, residual exposure remains. This directly compresses the margin expansion narrative.

**3. Legacy Insurance Tail (~$2-5B potential liability):** 201,700 long-term care policies with assumptions about morbidity, mortality, and discount rates. Adverse changes could require multi-billion dollar capital contributions to insurance subsidiaries. The market appears to underweight this given the aerospace excitement.

**4. Boeing Dependency:** GE9X (777X) and GEnx (787) revenue are directly tied to Boeing's production capability and certification timelines. Boeing's ongoing challenges create delivery uncertainty that GE cannot control.

**5. Guidance Below Consensus Creates Sentiment Risk:** Q1 2026 saw a beat-and-sell pattern: EPS beat significantly but guidance of $7.10-$7.40 remained below consensus (estimated at approximately $7.46-$7.56 depending on source). If the company continues to guide conservatively while consensus drifts higher, repeated earnings reactions could be negative despite beats.

**6. Valuation Compression Risk at 41-43x:** The premium multiple requires continued 15%+ growth. Any deceleration to 10% growth would compress the appropriate multiple toward 28-32x (peer range), implying 20-30% downside from current levels.

## Conclusion

GE Aerospace warrants a BUY at $305.19 with a 12-month base case target of $350. The $190.6B backlog, 25%+ earnings growth, expanding margins, and the approaching LEAP aftermarket wave provide multi-year visibility unusual for an industrial company trading at 41-43x forward earnings. The pattern of consecutive quarterly earnings beats demonstrates management is guiding conservatively.

The primary risk is a geopolitical scenario where oil sustains at elevated levels for an extended period, simultaneously compressing airline economics (reducing GE's addressable market for services) and the earnings multiple (risk-off). Against this: even in the bear case, the installed base doesn't disappear and shop visits are largely non-discretionary for airworthiness.

Position sizing should reflect the binary geopolitical overlay: 2-3% portfolio weight is appropriate given the favorable long-term fundamentals offset by near-term uncertainty.
