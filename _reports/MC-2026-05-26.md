## Executive Summary

Moelis & Company is a pure-play independent advisory firm with an asset-light model, zero financial debt, and a demonstrated ability to generate exceptional free cash flow during strong M&A cycles (35.6% FCF margin in FY2025, $540M). The company achieved record revenue of $1.52B in 2025 (+27% YoY) and reported record Q1 2026 revenue of $320M, but growth decelerated sharply to just 4% YoY—a signal that the M&A market is cooling under the weight of geopolitical disruption, tariff uncertainty, and elevated interest rates.

The stock currently trades at ~22x trailing and forward earnings, roughly in-line with peer median (22.3x), while consensus estimates are declining—current year EPS revised down 11.2% over 90 days, from $3.48 to $3.09. This is a business with extreme operating leverage to M&A volumes (demonstrated by the 2023 operating loss when revenue fell to $855M), and the current macro regime presents multiple headwinds to deal-making. The $300M buyback program and ~3.9% dividend yield provide downside support, but the risk/reward at current levels is approximately symmetric.

## Company Overview

Moelis & Company is a global independent investment bank founded in 2007, focused exclusively on advisory services: M&A, restructurings, capital markets advisory, and private capital advisory. The firm employs 1,027 advisory bankers and 178 Managing Directors across 20+ offices globally. Unlike bulge-bracket banks, Moelis has no trading book, no lending operations, and no financial debt—making it a pure expression of M&A activity levels.

Revenue is entirely fee-based, earned at transaction close. There are no long-term contracted revenue sources. The top 10 transactions represented ~18% of 2025 revenues, indicating reasonable diversification. Kenneth Moelis retains ~37% voting control through super-voting Class B shares held via Partner Holdings.

## Financial Analysis

- Q1 2026 revenue: $320M (+4% YoY)—dramatic deceleration
- Compensation ratio improved from 84% (2023) to 67% (2025) to 66% (Q1 2026)
- Operating margin: 18.1% (FY2025); 12.7% (Q1 2026—seasonally weak)
- Trailing EPS: $3.17; consensus FY2026E: $3.09; FY2027E: $3.95

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**
- Operating cash flow: $576M (FY2025); Free cash flow: $540M
- Cash + investments: ~$354M as of Q1 2026 (seasonal decline from bonus payments)
- Zero financial debt (only lease liabilities and TRA obligation)
- ROE: 45.7% (reflects asset-light partnership structure)
- Negative retained earnings (-$817M) is structural—reflects cumulative distributions through partnership structure, not financial distress

**Capital Return:**
- Annual dividend: $2.60/share (~3.9% yield)
- $300M buyback authorized February 2026; $117M deployed in Q1 alone (1.91M shares at ~$61.40 avg)
- $248M remaining authorization (~5% of market cap)

The balance sheet is fortress-quality for a financial firm. The concern is the income statement trajectory: Q1 deceleration from 27% to 4% growth, with management explicitly citing AI disruption in software M&A and "evolving conditions" from geopolitics.

## Competitive Landscape

Moelis occupies a strong position in the independent advisory segment, which has gained M&A market share from bulge-bracket banks for over a decade. Key competitive advantages:

1. **Conflict-free advice**: No lending relationships that create conflicts in M&A advisory
2. **Restructuring countercyclicality**: Advisory fees from creditor/debtor restructurings partially offset M&A volume declines
3. **Private capital advisory**: Newer growth vector tapping ~$100B+ annual secondary market
4. **Global reach**: 20+ offices with particular strength in Middle East (Aramco IPO heritage)

The firm's 178 MDs represent its core competitive asset. Revenue per advisory banker (~$1.5M) is competitive with peers. However, Moelis has underperformed its peer TSR index (191 vs. 203 for the peer group through FY2025), suggesting the market assigns a structural discount for governance or cyclicality reasons.

**Competitive risks:** Non-compete enforceability is weakening nationally, making MD retention more challenging. The firm is smaller than Evercore (~195 MDs) and Lazard, potentially limiting its ability to handle the very largest transactions simultaneously.

## Risk Assessment

**1. M&A Cyclicality (HIGH):** The current macro environment—geopolitical uncertainty (tariffs, regional conflicts), potential rate changes—could produce a downturn similar to or worse than 2023. Zero long-term contracted revenue means the entire revenue base reprices quarterly.

**2. Falling Estimates:** The trajectory of declining consensus EPS is inconsistent with a BUY thesis.

**3. Q1 Miss + Deceleration:** The combination of a Q1 EPS miss (-11.5%) and growth deceleration to 4% breaks the prior 3-quarter beat streak. Management commentary about "near record pipelines" has not converted to closing revenue yet.

**4. Governance:** Kenneth Moelis's 37% super-voting control limits minority shareholder influence. The CEO compensation spike to $35.2M (4.6x prior year) raises alignment questions.

**5. Macro Sensitivity:** Higher rates discourage leveraged buyouts (a significant source of advisory fees). If rates rise further, M&A volumes face additional headwinds.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress: ATM IV of 42.9% vs. 34.4% historical volatility (+8.5pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 42.9% > 6-month 37.8%). Put/call skew of 8.9% in the near-term indicates elevated put demand.

The implied move of ±8.9% for the nearest expiry (~$60.25 to $72.01) suggests meaningful short-term uncertainty. The backwardated term structure typically resolves through either a negative catalyst or vol decay—the current setup is cautionary for long positions.

2. EPS estimate revisions turn positive by >5% over 30 days
3. Stock declines to $55-58 on macro fear (creating 15x FY2027 EPS entry point with 3.9% yield support)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 revenue declines YoY (would signal deal activity contraction, not just deceleration)
2. Management cuts or suspends dividend
3. MD departures exceed 10 in a quarter (talent flight)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). This report will reveal whether Q1 deceleration was timing or trend.

## Conclusion

Moelis & Company operates an exceptional business model—zero debt, 35%+ FCF margins at peak, and structural tailwinds from the independent advisory secular share gain story. However, growth has decelerated from 27% to 4%, estimates are declining, the most recent quarter missed, and the macro environment presents multiple headwinds to deal-making. At 22x earnings with falling estimates and no identifiable catalyst for re-acceleration, the risk/reward is approximately symmetric. The 3.9% dividend yield and $248M buyback provide meaningful downside support, preventing a more negative recommendation. Wait for either a cheaper price (creating margin of safety) or evidence that the pipeline is converting to revenue.
