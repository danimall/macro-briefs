**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Clean Harbors operates the largest commercial hazardous-waste infrastructure network in North America: 10 incinerators across 5 facilities, 7 landfills, 100+ disposal sites, and the continent's largest used-oil re-refining operation. The moat is genuine and difficult to attack: RCRA Part B permits take years and public hearings to obtain, generator liability under Superfund makes customers sticky, and no competitor matches the one-stop-shop breadth. This is a well-run, structurally advantaged infrastructure business, and the fundamentals continue to grind higher: adjusted EBITDA reached $1.170B in 2025 (+4.7%), operating cash flow climbed to $866.7M (+11%), and adjusted free cash flow rose 42% to $509.3M.

The problem is price, not quality. At $303.74 the stock trades at ~42x trailing and ~34x consensus forward EPS ($8.82), roughly double the peer-group median P/E of 21.3x. The market is paying full freight for the moat, the PFAS optionality, and the pricing power in tight incineration capacity. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (+5.8% current-year over 90 days), which supports the durability of the earnings trajectory, but the revision velocity is nowhere near the +30% threshold that would justify looking through a premium multiple. This is a stable, high-quality compounder priced as one.

We maintain a HOLD (growth_hold). The 12-month thesis is intact: Kimball incinerator ramping to full utilization by end-2026, PFAS destruction as a validated multi-year growth vector, disposal pricing gains recurring, and a stepped-up buyback ($574M remaining). But the risk/reward from current levels is roughly symmetric: the stock sits near its 52-week high with a consensus price target of $330 (only ~9% upside to mean) and a soft industrial-services cycle already surfacing in the numbers. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$250s or on evidence of accelerating disposal volumes and PFAS revenue.

## Company Overview

Clean Harbors is North America's leading environmental and industrial services company, organized into two segments:

- **Environmental Services (ES)** — ~86% of revenue ($5.19B in 2025), comprising Technical Services (incineration, landfill, TSDF disposal), Industrial Services, Field & Emergency Response, and Safety-Kleen branch services. Adjusted EBITDA margin of 25.9% and rising.
- **Safety-Kleen Sustainability Solutions (SKSS)** — ~14% ($837M), collecting and re-refining used oil (243M gallons collected in 2025) into base and blended oils. Commodity-exposed with margins around 16%.

The competitive moat rests on physical assets and regulatory barriers that are difficult to replicate. The 10 incinerators represent 631,721 tons of practical annual capacity, the largest such network on the continent. The company serves 350,000+ customers, with the top 10 industries representing ~80% of revenue and no dangerous single-customer concentration (general manufacturing 14%, chemical 14%, refineries 12%, automotive 10%). Regulatory intensity, which most companies would call a headwind, functions here as a barrier to entry and a driver of outsourcing.

Leadership is undergoing a generational transition. Founder Alan McKim, who built the company over 45+ years as founder, Executive Chairman, and CTO, announced his retirement from the board in May 2026, with Robert Willett appointed chairman in July 2026. The operating leadership is a co-CEO structure (Michael Battles and Eric Gerstenberg), with Eric Dugas as CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** has grown steadily with acquisition support: $5.17B (2022) → $5.41B (2023) → $5.89B (2024) → $6.03B (2025), a +2.4% increase in 2025. Q1 2026 revenue was $1.46B, up 1.9% YoY. This is low-single-digit organic growth augmented by tuck-in M&A, respectable for the sector but not spectacular.

**Profitability trends favorably at the cash level.** Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 19.4%; Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $247.9M (+5.5%), at a 17.0% margin. GAAP net income dipped 2.8% to $391.0M in 2025, but this is an optical decline: a $45.1M jump in D&A (HEPACO/Noble amortization plus new Kimball incinerator depreciation) drove the difference. Underneath, operating cash generation strengthened. Q1 2026 net income re-accelerated +7.7% to $63.2M ($1.19 diluted EPS versus $1.09). Operating margin runs ~11.2%, net margin ~6.5%, ROE ~14.2%.

**Cash flow quality is the standout.** Operating cash flow rose from $777.8M, and adjusted free cash flow from $357.9M, aided by working-capital collections, lower taxes, and reduced environmental spend. FCF margin of ~7.3% against a ~$16B market cap implies an FCF yield of roughly 3.2%, thin, and a reminder that the equity is priced for growth.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt sits at ~$2.79B (term loan due 2032 plus $1.545B senior notes maturing 2029/2031/2033); D/E is 2.78. The Altman Z-Score of 2.04 lands in the "grey zone," but this is a structural artifact of an asset-heavy, appropriately leveraged infrastructure model rather than a distress signal. Supporting evidence: current ratio of 2.33, full covenant compliance, cash and securities of $669M (Q1'26), an undrawn $600M revolver (~$455M available), and an effective interest rate of just 5.2%. $600M of the term loan is swap-fixed at 3.46% through September 2027. Piotroski score is 5/5 on available signals. The one genuine rate exposure: $660M of variable-rate term-loan debt remains unhedged. For a capital-intensive environmental services business, this is a sound, well-termed capital structure.

**Capital allocation is balanced.** Buybacks stepped up from $55M (2024) to $250M (2025), with a fresh $350M authorization in February 2026 leaving ~$574M remaining. No dividend. 2026 capex is guided at $460–520M, including strategic growth (SDA unit, fleet). The buyback ramp signals management confidence and a shift toward returning capital, not just deploying it into M&A.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters (Q3'25 missed by 9.0%). This tempers confidence in the precision of forward estimates and argues against paying up for beat-driven re-rating.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is durable but not explosive. Three vectors support the forward EPS trajectory:

1. **Incinerator utilization normalization.** Ex-Kimball utilization was a robust 89% in 2025, but the headline figure dipped to 80% in Q1'26 (versus 81%) as the new Kimball incinerator dilutes the average during ramp. Kimball reaches full utilization by end-2026, which converts a current earnings drag (depreciation without full revenue) into operating leverage.

2. **PFAS destruction optionality.** The EPA/DoD-partnered study concluded in September 2025 that CLH's RCRA-permitted high-temperature incineration achieves 99.9999% thermal destruction of PFAS. As PFAS disposal regulation tightens, this positions scarce, permitted incineration assets, which competitors cannot easily replicate, as the go-to solution. This is genuine multi-year optionality that current estimates likely under-weight.

3. **Disposal pricing and reshoring.** Tight incineration capacity (compounded by captive incinerator closures) supports recurring pricing gains, seen in both 2024 and 2025. North American manufacturing reshoring and government infrastructure spend support waste-volume growth.

The offsetting headwind is cyclical. Industrial Services turnaround revenue fell ~$50M in 2025 and ~$20M in Q1'26 on lower turnaround activity, an early signal of softening industrial capex. SKSS remains hostage to base-oil markets (a $47M revenue decline in 2025, a further 7.1% drop in Q1'26), though management's self-help (taking two re-refineries offline, higher collection charges, headcount actions) expanded SKSS margin 320bps in Q1'26 even against falling oil prices.

Estimate revisions are consistently positive: current quarter +7.0%, next quarter +8.8%, current year +5.8%, next year +3.5% over 90 days. This is healthy, confirming positive earnings momentum, but it is well below the +30% velocity that would flag a systematically underestimated turn. The growth thesis is one of steady mid-to-high-single-digit compounding, which we weight alongside the trajectory data showing Growth Prospects has cooled from 6.8 to 6.0 over recent reports, a decline driven by the industrial-services softening that the current data confirms rather than reverses.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the thesis stalls. CLH trades at ~42x trailing EPS ($7.28) and ~34x consensus forward EPS. Against a peer-group median P/E of 21.3x, this is a substantial premium. The peer set is heterogeneous (Rollins at 43.2x, a comparably high-quality compounder; Casella at 817.9x, distorted trough earnings; Veralto at 21.3x; ABM at 17.8x; GEO at 14.4x), but the median cleanly frames CLH as expensively valued relative to the space.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the multiple. This is not a trough-earnings turn story: revenue is growing 2–3%, EBITDA 4–5%, and estimate revisions are modest. There is no depressed base being normalized, so trailing P/E is a fair (if unflattering) representation of earnings power. Applying a growth-adjusted frame, ~34x forward on ~6–8% forward EPS growth implies a PEG well above 2.0x, expensive by any reasonable measure. The premium is justified partly by moat quality, cash-flow durability, and PFAS optionality, but those are already the consensus case, and the price reflects them.

Analyst targets reinforce the constrained upside: mean $330 (~9% above current), median $330, high $356 (~17%), low $305 (roughly at spot). On a premium-multiple stock like this, analyst targets are not floors that lag reality; they reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price and offer little independent confirmation of undervaluation. The 15 buy/hold analysts (2 strong buy, 9 buy, 4 hold, 0 sell) with a $305 low target signal a market that broadly likes the company but sees limited near-term room.

We score valuation 4.5. A high-quality moat at a full multiple with no identifiable mispricing catalyst offers limited asymmetry. The absence of a dividend removes any yield support to underpin the price.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is the strongest dimension and improving; the trajectory data shows Competitive Position rising from 7.5 to 8.0, and the current evidence confirms it. In commercial hazardous-waste incineration, CLH is the dominant #1, with fragmented rivals (Veolia, Ross Incineration) unable to match its scale. In re-refined base oil it competes against virgin base oil from Motiva and Chevron. Across the broader waste complex, Republic Services, Waste Management, GFL, and Enviri overlap in pieces but not in the full one-stop-shop offering.

The moat is widening rather than eroding. Rising regulation raises entry barriers and increases customer outsourcing. Tight incineration capacity, plus captive incinerator closures at industrial customers, supports pricing. The PFAS destruction capability leverages scarce permitted assets into a growth vector competitors cannot easily build. Generator liability under Superfund keeps customers auditing and limiting approved vendors, which favors the incumbent with the deepest compliance record. The disposal-network moat is currently offsetting the industrial-services cyclicality, doing exactly what a moat should do in a soft cycle.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are real but largely priced and non-binary:

1. **SKSS commodity exposure.** Base and blended oil pricing is structurally pressured (IMO low-sulfur rules shrank the used-oil-as-fuel market). Fixed re-refinery costs magnify volume/price declines. Management's active spread management (re-refineries offline, higher collection charges) mitigates but does not eliminate this. Ongoing, not binary.

2. **Industrial cyclicality.** Turnaround/high-value services revenue is already softening. If a broader industrial slowdown arrives, ES Industrial Services would feel it. This is the most relevant near-term watch item.

3. **Leverage and rate risk.** $660M of unhedged variable-rate debt in a higher-for-longer environment; covenant and cross-default provisions. Manageable given the 5.2% effective rate and swap protection through 2027, but not trivial.

4. **Environmental and remedial liabilities.** $229–231M recorded (mostly acquired), payable over decades; PFAS regulation could enlarge or accelerate them. 132 Superfund sites where CLH is a PRP (only 3 with likely >$1M exposure).

5. **Product liability.** ~83 pending Safety-Kleen parts-washer/solvent injury cases; currently insured, but punitive damages may be uninsurable.

6. **Execution on capital projects.** Kimball ramp and the $210–220M SDA unit (2028) carry ramp risk.

7. **Governance transition.** The founder's board exit ends 45+ years of founder control. The co-CEO structure provides continuity, and the transition appears orderly, but it is a genuine key-person consideration worth monitoring.

None of these is a catalyst for material downside that the market is ignoring, which supports a middling-to-favorable risk score of 6.2.

The term structure is in backwardation (37.5% near-term versus 28.3% at six months), signaling the market prices imminent uncertainty (likely the next earnings print). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.6%. IV collapsed from 113.9% to 37.5% since the prior snapshot, so the extreme reading has normalized. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.5%) and put/call volume ratio is low (0.38), so positioning is not directionally bearish. For a prospective buyer, the rich near-term IV argues for patience: better entry likely comes after the next earnings-driven volatility crush rather than before it.

Short interest is negligible at 2.3% of float (2.6 days to cover), indicating no informed bearish crowd.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$356–400):** Kimball reaches full utilization by end-2026, converting depreciation drag into operating leverage. PFAS destruction becomes a quantifiable revenue line as regulation tightens, and the market re-rates the incineration assets as strategic. Disposal pricing gains continue, industrial-services softness proves shallow, and SKSS self-help sustains margin expansion. Adjusted FCF compounds double-digits, funding accelerated buybacks. The stock holds its premium multiple on rising forward EPS toward $10+, reaching the high target of $356 and beyond.

**Bear Case (~$230–250):** A broader industrial slowdown deepens the Industrial Services decline, SKSS margins compress on falling oil prices, and the premium multiple compresses toward the peer median as growth decelerates. Kimball ramp slips, the SDA project runs over budget, and the founder transition surfaces execution friction. At ~28x on flat-to-down EPS, the stock falls to the mid-$200s, roughly 18–24% downside.

**Base Case (~$330):** Steady low-to-mid-single-digit revenue growth, high-single-digit EPS growth, and continued FCF strength support the current premium multiple with modest expansion. The stock tracks earnings and the consensus target of ~$330, delivering ~9% total return over 12 months, at a price that already reflects the compounding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)** — Own for the durable compounding thesis and moat, not for an entry-point mispricing. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the bar for continuing it is met by the combination of a genuinely improving competitive position and a full valuation that caps near-term asymmetry.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Pullback toward the mid-$250s (~34x → ~29x forward), restoring valuation asymmetry, OR
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +15% on the current year alongside evidence of accelerating disposal volumes, OR
- A quantified PFAS revenue ramp that materially raises forward EPS beyond current consensus.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Industrial Services revenue decline accelerating to double-digit YoY declines, signaling a genuine cyclical downturn, OR
- SKSS margin reversal alongside falling base-oil prices erasing the Q1'26 self-help gains, OR
- Kimball utilization failing to recover toward 89%+ by end-2026, indicating execution failure on the growth capex, OR
- Multiple compression toward peer median (~21x) without a corresponding fundamental catalyst.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (the options market is pricing a ±8.6% move). Monitor incinerator utilization, Industrial Services turnaround revenue, and SKSS margins each quarter through the Kimball ramp into end-2026.

**Upside/downside from current ($303.74):** Base case $330 = +8.6%; bull case $356+ = +17% or more; bear case $240 = -21%.

## Conclusion

Clean Harbors is a high-quality, moat-protected infrastructure business with strengthening cash flow, a widening regulatory advantage, and genuine PFAS optionality. The competitive position is improving and the balance sheet, despite an alarming-looking Z-Score that is really a capital-intensity artifact, is sound. The issue is entirely valuation: at ~34x forward earnings, roughly double the peer median, the market has fully priced the moat and the growth, leaving thin asymmetry against a softening industrial cycle and a founder-exit governance transition. Estimate revisions are positive but modest, well short of the velocity that would justify looking through the premium. We maintain HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. We would deploy capital more aggressively on a pullback toward the mid-$250s or on a demonstrable acceleration in disposal volumes and PFAS revenue.
