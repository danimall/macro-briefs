**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ryder System is a North American outsourced fleet-management and logistics provider that has spent the better part of a decade shifting its revenue mix toward contractual streams (ChoiceLease, supply-chain solutions, dedicated transportation) and away from transactional exposure (commercial rental, used-vehicle sales). That repositioning is doing its job: comparable EPS grew 8% in FY2025 and 3% in Q1 2026 even as the freight cycle stayed in an extended downturn, and the business threw off $946M of free cash flow in 2025 versus $133M the prior year. The company carries three investment-grade ratings and received a Moody's upgrade to Baa1 (stable) in April 2026, confirming improving rather than deteriorating credit. Governance is a relative strength: an orderly internal CEO handoff (Robert Sanchez to John Diez, effective March 31, 2026) and cumulative TSR of 353 versus a peer group at 149.

The complication is that the stock has already priced much of this. R is up 71% over the trailing twelve months, sits at $263 against a 52-week range of $149-$284, and trades at roughly 22x trailing/forward earnings — near the top of its own historical band and effectively at the consensus mean target of $269 with a high of $290. The free cash flow surge that anchors the bull case is partly a trough artifact: management guides FCF down to ~$800M in 2026 as lease-fleet capex resumes ($2.1B rising to ~$2.4B). Per-share earnings growth is being manufactured in meaningful part through buybacks (share count down to ~39.2M from 41.8M) and, in Q1, a one-time tax benefit (21% rate vs. 26.8%). Segment EBT was roughly flat-to-down.

We rate R a HOLD. The franchise is sound and the downside is reasonably protected by the contractual mix, investment-grade balance sheet, and residual values already sitting at historically low levels. But there is no near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple, organic growth is modest, and the asymmetric upside (a freight-cycle recovery flowing through rental and used-vehicle sales) has no clear date. After a 71% run into the multiple, the risk/reward is balanced. This continues a HOLD trajectory that has held at an overall ~6.0 across three prior reports; the current read confirms rather than reverses that view.

## Company Overview

Ryder operates three segments:

- **Fleet Management Solutions (FMS)** — roughly 38% of operating revenue. Full-service truck/tractor/trailer leasing (ChoiceLease), commercial rental, contract maintenance (SelectCare), fuel services, and used-vehicle sales. Ryder owns or leases ~183,000 vehicles across 789 locations. This is the asset-heavy core; ChoiceLease, ~60% of FMS revenue, carries 3-7 year contracts (up to 10 years for trailers).
- **Supply Chain Solutions (SCS)** — roughly 43% of operating revenue. Integrated logistics across omnichannel retail, automotive, CPG, and industrial verticals, including ~105M sq ft of warehousing, e-commerce/last-mile, and transportation management.
- **Dedicated Transportation Solutions (DTS)** — roughly 18% of operating revenue. Turnkey dedicated fleets with professional drivers, materially expanded via the 2024 Cardinal Logistics acquisition.

The integrating logic is the FMS maintenance and fleet infrastructure (789 locations, 401 fueling sites, 4,600 technicians) that underpins all three segments, generating $131M of cross-segment "Equipment Contribution" in 2025. The secular thesis: of roughly 11M U.S. commercial fleet vehicles, only about 1M are outsourced to third parties, and rising vehicle complexity, emissions regulation, capital intensity, and driver/technician shortages push private fleets toward outsourcing over time.

A measurement note that matters for anyone modeling the company: the structured XBRL "revenue" figure of $8.78B is a partial line. GAAP total revenue was $12.7B (flat YoY) and management's preferred operating revenue (ex-fuel and ex-subcontracted pass-throughs) was $10.4B (+1%). The fuel and subcontracted-transportation lines are low-margin pass-throughs that distort the top line.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings.** FY2025 GAAP EPS from continuing operations was $11.99 (+8%); comparable EPS $12.92 (+8%). Net income was $499M, comparable EBITDA $2.87B (+3%), and adjusted ROE 17% (up from 16%). The trailing EPS of $11.98 against a $263 price puts the trailing P/E at ~22x.

**Segment trajectory.** Contractual strength is masking transactional weakness:
- FMS EBT $501M (-3%): ChoiceLease pricing and maintenance savings offset by weak used-vehicle sales (used truck pricing -15%, tractor -11%) and soft commercial rental (utilization 70%, smaller fleet).
- SCS EBT $355M (+7%): omnichannel network optimization and record 2025 contractual sales.
- DTS EBT $140M (+12%): Cardinal synergies despite revenue falling on the freight downturn.

**Cash flow and capital discipline.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $2.6B, as capex was cut to $2.1B (from $2.7B/$3.3B prior years). This is countercyclical discipline — deliberately under-investing in the lease fleet during the downturn. Management is candid that this is partly a trough phenomenon: 2026 guidance is OCF ~$2.7B as lease-fleet investment resumes. Normalized FCF is therefore closer to $800M than $946M, which still represents a healthy ~8% FCF yield on the ~$10.2B market cap.

**Balance sheet.** The Altman Z-Score of 0.92 ("distress zone") is a false signal for a leasing model. Ryder intentionally runs ~250% debt/equity (269% at Q1 2026) because it finances ~$7.6B of revenue-earning equipment with debt, matching debt repricing life to asset life. The corroborating evidence runs the other way: three investment-grade ratings (S&P BBB+, Fitch BBB+, Moody's Baa1). The current ratio of 0.89 is normal given short-term commercial-paper funding. We disregard the Z-Score as inapplicable.

**Capital returns.** Ryder returned $664M in 2025 ($519M buybacks + $145M dividends), raised the dividend 12% to $3.64 annualized, then to $0.91/quarter in February 2026. Q1 2026 buybacks ran $233M (up from $167M). The ~1.4% dividend yield is well covered.

**Q1 2026.** Total revenue $3.1B (flat); EBT $118M (-12%) on weaker SCS automotive; comparable EPS $2.54 (+3%, buyback-driven and tax-aided at the 21% rate). The Q1 beat was +9.6% on EPS, but it was driven by tax timing and buybacks rather than operating outperformance.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses over the last four quarters, with the two misses (Q3 and Q4 2025, each -1.5%) clustered in the most freight-sensitive period. This argues against high confidence in upside surprises while the cycle stays soft.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is modest. Operating revenue was up only ~1% in FY2025 and flat in Q1 2026. Comparable EPS growth of 8% (FY) and 3% (Q1) leaned on buybacks and, in Q1, a non-recurring tax benefit; segment EBT was roughly flat-to-down. The honest characterization is a company grinding out low-single-digit per-share growth at the trough, with the contractual mix protecting earnings on the way down.

Estimate revisions are positive but not the inflection-grade signal that would force a re-rating. Current-year EPS estimates rose 3.5% over 90 days to $14.60; next-year estimates rose 5.6% to $17.55. These are constructive but well below the +30% velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection, and the 30-day revisions are essentially flat (+0.1% to +0.3%), meaning the positive trend has stalled in the most recent window. The implied trajectory — $14.60 this year, $17.55 next year — embeds roughly 20% EPS growth into FY2027, which is the cycle-recovery bet: rental utilization improving (up 200 bps YoY in Q1), used-vehicle pricing turning (tractors +6% in Q1 after being down double digits), and ~$70M of 2026 earnings benefits from lease pricing, maintenance savings, and acquisition synergies.

The secular outsourcing tailwind is real but slow-moving. Addressable markets are large and under-penetrated (~$234B outsourced 3PL of a $1.5T U.S. spend, ~$31B outsourced dedicated of ~$500B addressable, ~10M non-outsourced commercial fleet vehicles). These support multi-year penetration but do not generate near-term acceleration. Growth prospects score below the midpoint because the durable component is low-single-digit organic, and the higher-growth component (cycle recovery) lacks timing.

## Valuation Assessment

R trades at ~22x trailing and forward earnings. The stock sits at $263, essentially at the consensus mean target of $269 (median $269.5), with a high target of $290 (+10%) and a low of $245 (-7%). The risk skew at consensus is roughly symmetric.

Peer comparison is awkward because the listed Cargo Ground Transportation peers carry distorted or cyclically-depressed multiples — KNX at 394x, SAIA at 51x, LSTR at 62x, ARCB at 70x, RXO negative. The peer median of 70x reflects trough earnings across truckload/LTL carriers, not a clean comp set. On its own quality and through-cycle earnings, R at ~22x looks more reasonable than the headline peer median suggests, but it is also near the top of its own historical multiple band. A leasing/logistics business growing per-share earnings in the low-to-mid single digits organically, with FCF guided down in 2026, does not obviously warrant multiple expansion from here.

The estimate-revision-as-buy-signal logic does not clear the bar here. The revisions are positive but well short of the +30% inflection threshold, the 30-day revisions are flat, and the stock is not cheap relative to those rising estimates — it is fairly priced at ~22x against ~20% projected FY2027 EPS growth that is itself contingent on a freight recovery. Treating the $290 high target as a floor would be inappropriate: at ~22x with stalled near-term revisions and a substantial prior run, analyst targets here are more ceiling than floor.

The cleaner support for the stock is the absolute FCF yield (~8% on normalized $800M) and the contractual earnings floor, which limit downside more than they create upside. We score valuation below the midpoint: the multiple is full for the organic growth delivered, the cheapness case rests on a cycle recovery whose timing is unknown, and there is no identifiable near-term mispricing to exploit. A low or middling multiple, by itself, has historically been a poor predictor of forward returns absent a specific catalyst, and there isn't one dated here.

## Competitive Landscape

Ryder's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. The integrated network is a genuine structural advantage that smaller finance lessors, OEMs, and most 3PLs cannot replicate, and it generates real cross-segment economics. SCS posted record contractual sales in 2025 and FMS/DTS sales in Q1 2026 ran above prior year and ahead of expectations, evidence that the contractual book is growing through the trough.

The countervailing pressures are familiar and largely priced. Customers can self-perform (the perennial threat in outsourced fleet management); consolidation creates larger 3PL competitors; some rivals enjoy lower capital costs. SCS automotive exposure has been a recurring drag — plant shutdowns and lost business cut SCS EBT in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 — and the $1.2B of SCS/DTS goodwill depends on a few key customers and verticals. On balance, the trajectory is positive on the contractual side and neutral-to-soft on the transactional/automotive side, supporting a score modestly above the midpoint. This is industry beta as much as company-specific selection skill, which tempers conviction; Industrials BUYs as a group have not generated durable alpha, so the bar for an upgrade here is a demonstrated company-specific catalyst, not sector improvement.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Used-vehicle residual value / depreciation risk** is the most idiosyncratic financial-statement exposure. Residual estimates are at "historically low levels," and Ryder cut certain tractor residuals effective January 1, 2026. A hypothetical 10% residual reduction would add ~$340M of depreciation over the remaining fleet life. The flip side is asymmetry: with residuals already depressed, downside room is limited and Q1 already showed tractor used pricing +6%. This risk cuts both ways.
2. **Freight cycle / transactional exposure.** Commercial rental and used-vehicle sales remain cyclically depressed (utilization 68-70%, smaller fleet). The extended freight downturn is the explicit near-term headwind across FMS and DTS.
3. **Customer/vertical concentration in SCS and DTS,** particularly automotive softness, with goodwill recoverability tied to a few key relationships.
4. **Interest-rate / refinancing.** Effective rate rose to 5.2%, with large MTN maturities through 2026-2029 and heavy commercial-paper reliance. A two-notch downgrade would impair CP access, but the recent Moody's upgrade moves the company further from, not closer to, that line.
5. **Earnings quality.** Per-share growth is partly manufactured through buybacks and, in Q1, a one-time tax benefit. The contractual-growth narrative is real but modest; investors should not extrapolate 8% comparable EPS growth as organic.
6. **Pension** is underfunded (94%, up from 91%) with a $575M after-tax AOCI charge, but 2026 required contributions are only ~$10M — manageable and improving.
7. **CEO transition** is well-telegraphed and internal (Diez was CFO→COO→CEO with Sanchez staying as Executive Chair), which reduces rather than adds transition risk.

Most of these are priced and well-understood, which keeps the risk score near the midpoint. The residual-value lever is the one genuinely idiosyncratic item, but it is currently more asymmetric to the upside than the downside given how depressed residuals already are.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral and offers no strong directional tell. Near-term ATM IV of 35.8% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical vol of 34.0% (a fair 1.8% spread), and IV percentile within the current chain is low at 10%, so the market is not pricing an imminent shock. Put/call skew is balanced at -1.0% near-dated, widening only modestly to -3.3% at six months — mild hedging demand into the back half of the year, consistent with a cyclical name rather than an acute concern. The put/call OI ratio of 1.75 looks elevated, but it jumped from a near-zero prior reading (0.01), which reflects sparse prior open interest rather than a fresh bearish surge; with only 102 contracts across three expirations, this is a thinly-traded options name and the signal carries low weight. Implied move of ±7.4% into the July expiry is unremarkable for a freight-cyclical. Net read: no divergence from the fundamental HOLD view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300-320).** The freight cycle turns in 2026-2027. Rental utilization and used-vehicle pricing recover (tractors already +6% in Q1), driving FMS transactional earnings off a depressed base. The contractual book (SCS records, DTS Cardinal synergies, ~$70M of 2026 strategic benefits) compounds, FY2027 EPS reaches the ~$17.55 consensus, and the multiple holds at ~18-20x, yielding $315-350 on those estimates. Continued buybacks at ~$700M+/year and a rising dividend add to per-share returns. The investment-grade balance sheet and Moody's upgrade reduce the financing-risk discount.

**Bear case ($200-215, roughly -20% from current).** The freight downturn extends through 2026, residuals fall further (the $340M depreciation sensitivity bites), SCS automotive weakness persists, and FCF compresses toward the low end of guidance as capex resumes. Organic EBT stays flat-to-down, buyback-driven EPS growth decelerates, and the multiple compresses toward the low-to-mid teens as the market reprices the cycle-recovery optionality further out. At 15x on ~$14 of trough-ish EPS, the stock retraces to ~$210.

**Base case ($270-280).** Modest contractual growth plus early cycle-recovery signals support low-double-digit per-share growth, but the stock has largely discounted this already at 22x and at consensus targets. The multiple holds, EPS grinds higher, and total return tracks roughly the FCF-plus-dividend yield with limited multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July 2026), with the FY2026 guidance refresh and any update on residual-value assumptions and rental/used-pricing trends as the key inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed freight-cycle inflection: rental utilization sustained above 75% and used-vehicle pricing positive YoY for two consecutive quarters, which would convert the transactional drag into an earnings tailwind.
- EPS estimate revisions accelerating above +10% over 90 days (vs. the current +3.5%/+5.6%) alongside a pullback that compresses forward P/E below ~18x.
- A meaningful price decline (toward the low-$220s, the early-2026 base) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material residual-value write-down (a 10%+ residual cut, the ~$340M depreciation event) signaling the freight downturn is deepening.
- SCS automotive losses broadening into a goodwill-impairment review on the $1.2B SCS/DTS goodwill.
- A credit-outlook reversal that threatens commercial-paper access, or a guidance cut to FCF/comparable EPS.

## Conclusion

Ryder is a financially sound, investment-grade, cyclically-trough industrial with a de-risked contractual core, strong (if partly trough-inflated) free cash flow, disciplined capital returns, and a clean internal CEO succession. The franchise quality is real, the balance-sheet "distress" signal is a model artifact that should be disregarded, and the residual-value risk is currently more asymmetric to the upside than the downside. None of that is a near-term reason to buy at $263.

The stock has risen 71% over twelve months to ~22x earnings, sits at consensus targets, and is priced for the contractual story already in evidence. Organic growth is low-single-digit, per-share growth is leaning on buybacks and tax timing, and the higher-return scenario — a freight-cycle recovery flowing through rental and used-vehicle sales — has no clear date. Estimate revisions are positive but have stalled in the most recent 30-day window and fall well short of inflection grade. We rate R a HOLD, classified as uncertain rather than a conviction income or growth hold: the downside is reasonably protected and the cycle optionality is genuine, but the entry point after a 71% run leaves balanced risk/reward with no catalyst to force a re-rating. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$220s or on confirmed evidence the freight cycle has turned.
