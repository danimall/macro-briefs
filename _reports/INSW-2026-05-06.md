## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the largest independent tanker companies globally, operating approximately 66 vessels (as of late April 2026) across crude and product carrier segments, following recent vessel sales and newbuild deliveries. The company is experiencing a sharp earnings recovery driven by surging VLCC rates (Q4 2025 spot rates at $75,566/day, more than doubling sequentially) and geopolitical disruptions that have elevated ton-mile demand: Red Sea diversions, Russia sanctions, and Iran/Hormuz conflict dynamics.

The balance sheet is exceptionally strong: net debt-to-capital of 16.5%, $724M total liquidity, 31 unencumbered vessels, and an Altman Z-Score of 3.79. Management declared the largest quarterly dividend in company history ($2.15/share for Q1 2026), representing 87% of Q4 adjusted net income. The dividend is variable in nature, and its sustainability depends on continued elevated rate environments. The company has returned over $1 billion to shareholders since 2020 through dividends and buybacks. The January 2026 acquisition of 100% of Tankers International strengthens competitive positioning materially.

The primary risk is geopolitical normalization. With reported rate pullbacks toward pre-conflict levels in some segments, a sustained diplomatic resolution would compress tanker rates meaningfully. The company itself acknowledges in its SEC filings that normalization of conflicts could cause tanker rates to decline significantly.

## Company Overview

International Seaways operates a diversified tanker fleet. As of December 31, 2025, the company had 70 operating vessels (8.4M dwt) across all major asset classes: 12 VLCCs, 13 Suezmaxes, 4 Aframaxes, 1 LR2, 7 LR1s, and 33 MRs. Since year-end, the company has sold seven vessels, redelivered one chartered-in LR1, and taken delivery of two LR1 newbuilds, bringing the current fleet to approximately 66 total operating and newbuild vessels as of late April 2026. The fleet splits approximately 52/48 between crude tankers and product carriers by TCE revenue. The company operates predominantly in the spot market (82% of TCE revenues in 2025), primarily through six specialized commercial pools including Tankers International (now 100% owned), providing operational leverage to rate improvements.

The business model is capital-efficient: only 66 shoreside staff manage the entire operation through outsourced technical and commercial management with internal oversight. This hybrid model keeps G&A contained ($50.2M in 2025) while maintaining operational quality.

Two remaining LR1 newbuilds are scheduled for delivery in Q3 2026, financed at SOFR +125bps on 20-year amortization through an ECA facility. The company also completed $216M in vessel sales in Q1 2026 (seven older ships), generating estimated gains of ~$80M while reducing fleet age.

The Q4 earnings beat, combined with Jefferies initiating coverage with a Buy rating (per CNBC), suggests continued upward earnings momentum. The forward P/E compression relative to trailing reflects expectations of continued elevated rates.

## Competitive Landscape

INSW occupies a strong position among independent tanker operators:

1. **Scale and diversification:** ~66 current vessels across all major tanker classes provides revenue diversification unavailable to pure-play operators.
2. **Tankers International ownership:** 100% control of the world's leading independent VLCC pool is a unique competitive asset. The expansion into Suezmax pooling creates additional commercial leverage.
3. **Balance sheet superiority:** Net LTV of 13% vs. peers like Frontline (higher leverage) provides counter-cyclical acquisition capacity and lower cost of capital.
4. **Commercial pool relationships:** Six pools provide scheduling efficiency, market intelligence, and revenue optimization across vessel classes.
5. **Environmental positioning:** Three dual-fuel LNG VLCCs and LNG-ready LR1 newbuilds position for emerging carbon regulations (EU ETS, IMO CII).
6. **Lightering franchise:** U.S. Gulf lightering business provides asset-light complementary income (though declining from $23.3M to $7.8M in 2025).

**Competitive Risk:** The tanker orderbook at 17% of fleet is the primary structural concern. While nearly half the existing fleet will be 20+ years old by delivery time (creating replacement demand), net fleet growth could pressure rates in 2027-2028 if geopolitical disruptions normalize.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geopolitical Normalization (HIGH — Binary):**
The Iran peace deal framework and broader geopolitical normalization represent the single largest near-term risk. The company's 10-K explicitly states that "geopolitical instability and weather conditions have significantly benefitted the Company's financial results... There can be no certainty as to when such geopolitical instability and weather conditions will normalize, and any such normalization could cause tanker rates to decline significantly." If normalization occurs (Hormuz opens, Red Sea diversions end, Russia routing normalizes over time), VLCC rates could fall significantly from current levels. Historical patterns suggest geopolitical resolutions in this region tend to be protracted and partial, with multiple prior signals of diplomatic progress that did not result in sustained normalization.

**2. Rate Cyclicality (MEDIUM):**
82% spot exposure means quarterly earnings can swing dramatically. Q3 2025 was $0.44 EPS vs. Q4's $2.56—a 6x variance within adjacent quarters. This volatility depresses the multiple investors will pay.

**3. U.S.-China Port Fees (MEDIUM):**
INSW owns 13 Chinese-built vessels, of which nine are above the 55,000 dwt minimum threshold for U.S. port fees. Additionally, one Chinese-built time-chartered-in vessel and three non-Chinese-built vessels bareboat-chartered from a Chinese financial institution may be affected. The company also faces potential Chinese retaliatory fees given its U.S. incorporation. Both U.S. and Chinese orders were suspended for one year as of November 2025, meaning this risk re-emerges in late 2026. The financial impact per port call would depend on vessel net tonnage and the applicable fee schedule.

**4. Orderbook Supply (LOW-MEDIUM, longer-term):**
17% of fleet on order, deliveries concentrated 2026-2028. Partially offset by aging fleet retirements and sanctioned tonnage removal from effective supply.

**5. Tax/Regulatory (LOW):**
Bermuda redomiciliation addresses OECD Pillar Two, but Section 883 exemption depends on shareholder concentration tests. Failure would result in 4% gross tax on U.S.-source income.

**6. Dividend Sustainability (MEDIUM):**
The $2.15/quarter dividend represented 87% of Q4 adjusted net income. INSW's dividend policy includes a variable supplemental component tied to earnings. In a rate downturn, the dividend would be reduced materially, removing a key valuation support.

## Conclusion

INSW presents a compelling risk/reward at approximately 10x EV/EBITDA with a strong Q4 earnings recovery, a fortress balance sheet, and an approximately 9.8% annualized variable dividend yield. The stock is trading at its 52-week high, which limits the margin of safety, but the earnings recovery is supported by multiple structural factors: geopolitical disruptions (which historically tend to be protracted in this region), OPEC+ production increases requiring more tanker capacity, fleet aging, and INSW-specific catalysts (TI consolidation, newbuilds, profit-sharing economics).

The position carries meaningful binary risk from potential geopolitical normalization. INSW's low leverage, high liquidity, and variable dividend policy mean the company can navigate a rate downturn without existential risk. The key uncertainty is whether the stock trades at current multiples on elevated earnings or at higher multiples on depressed earnings in a bear scenario.

The Q4 earnings beat, Jefferies Buy initiation, and call-heavy options positioning all suggest positive momentum. Sizing should reflect the binary risk: this is a 2-3% position, not a core overweight, with the understanding that geopolitical outcomes will dictate whether to add or trim.
