## Executive Summary

Essential Utilities trades at $37.54, approximately 12% below its 52-week high of $42.37. The stock's investment thesis is dominated by its pending all-stock merger with American Water Works (AWK) at a 0.305 exchange ratio, which at AWK's recent price implies WTRG's value should be approximately $39-41 per share. The current spread reflects genuine regulatory uncertainty: Pennsylvania's Office of Consumer Advocate has formally recommended denial of the merger, and PA is WTRG's home state and largest market by revenue.

On standalone fundamentals, WTRG is a modestly attractive regulated utility trading at 17x forward earnings with a 3.7% dividend yield, both metrics representing a discount to water utility peers (median P/E 21.6x, median yield 2.8%). The company has beaten EPS estimates for four consecutive quarters by an average of 18%, reflecting successful rate case execution and a favorable weather year. However, chronic underperformance relative to peers (5-year TSR of 94 vs. peer group 164) and a leveraged balance sheet ($8.1B long-term debt, Moody's Negative outlook) limit enthusiasm for the standalone story.

The position is fundamentally a binary bet on regulatory approvals. With only Kentucky having approved so far and Pennsylvania hearings ongoing, the risk-reward is symmetric rather than asymmetric, warranting a HOLD pending clarity on the PA PUC decision.

## Company Overview

Essential Utilities operates regulated water, wastewater, and natural gas distribution services across nine U.S. states, serving approximately 5.5 million people through roughly 1.9 million customer connections. The business operates under two principal brands: **Aqua** (water/wastewater, ~1.14 million customers in 8 states) and **Peoples** (natural gas, ~747,000 customers, concentrated 95% in western Pennsylvania).

The company's growth model centers on acquiring municipal water and wastewater systems — typically aging infrastructure that municipalities lack the capital to upgrade. State-level "fair value" legislation in eight states allows WTRG to include acquired assets at appraised value in rate base, providing an economic incentive for tuck-in acquisitions. Since 2015, the company has added ~138,000 customers and approximately $570 million in rate base through this strategy.

In October 2025, WTRG entered a definitive merger agreement with American Water Works (AWK), the largest publicly traded U.S. water utility. The all-stock transaction at 0.305 AWK shares per WTRG share was overwhelmingly approved by shareholders of both companies in February 2026. Expected closing is Q1 2027, pending multi-state regulatory approvals.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue of $2.47 billion grew 18.6% YoY, driven by a 32.6% increase in the Regulated Natural Gas segment (weather-driven volumes from 25.5% more heating degree days + rate increases) and 8.6% growth in Regulated Water (rate surcharges). Diluted EPS of $2.20 grew modestly from $2.17 in 2024. The company guides to adjusted EPS CAGR of 5-7% off a 2024 adjusted base of $1.97.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue $861.8 million (+10% YoY). Adjusted EPS $0.83 (excluding $0.04/share merger costs). GAAP EPS $0.79, down from $1.03 in Q1 2025, which included a one-time gain on the sale of natural gas production assets.

**Operating Cash Flow:** $1.01 billion in FY2025, up from $770 million in FY2024. The improvement reflects new rates/surcharges and $46 million in PFAS settlement proceeds. Capital expenditures of $1.43 billion exceeded OCF by approximately $420 million, requiring external financing to bridge the gap. The 2026 plan calls for $1.7 billion in capex.

**Balance Sheet:** Long-term debt of $8.1 billion, representing 54.4% of total capitalization. Debt-to-equity of 2.84x. Weighted average cost of fixed-rate long-term debt was 4.16% as of March 2026, with recent issuances at 5.125-5.63%. The company maintains $1 billion in commercial paper capacity and investment-grade ratings (S&P A-/Positive; Moody's Baa2/Negative).

**Margins:** Operating margin of 37.2% and net margin of 24.9% are strong for the sector. ROE of 9.0% is adequate for a regulated utility but below the authorized returns in several jurisdictions (9.0-10.0% ROE authorized).

**Effective Tax Rate:** Remarkably low at 0.6% in 2025 (negative in 2023-2024) due to tax repair deductions on qualifying utility asset improvements. This is a genuine structural advantage but carries legislative risk.

**Dividend:** $1.37/share annually, yielding 3.7% at current price. Paid consecutively for 80 years. The payout ratio relative to adjusted EPS (~$2.26 FY2026E) is approximately 61%, providing adequate coverage with room for continued growth.

## Growth Analysis

WTRG's organic growth trajectory is tied to three mechanisms: (1) capital investment earning returns through rate base growth, (2) tuck-in acquisitions of municipal systems, and (3) new connections from population growth in service territories.

**Rate Base Growth:** Current rate base of ~$12.4 billion is projected to grow at 8% CAGR through 2029 (6% water, 11% gas). The five-year capital plan of $8.7 billion (2026-2030) funds infrastructure replacement, PFAS compliance ($450M+), and system expansion.

**Acquisition Pipeline:** Approximately 400,000 customers in active pursuit with $300 million in signed purchase agreements. The DELCORA acquisition ($276 million, ~198,000 EDUs in Philadelphia suburbs) remains pending and is excluded from current guidance.

**Estimate Revisions:** Modestly positive. Current-year EPS estimates rose 1.0% over 90 days (from $2.23 to $2.26). Next-year estimates stable at $2.42. This is consistent with utility-typical low single-digit earnings growth rather than any acceleration. The revision velocity does not meet the threshold for a strong BUY signal.

**Growth Constraints:** The primary limitation is regulatory lag — the time between capital investment and rate recovery creates a structural drag on returns. Additionally, growing affordability concerns could slow the pace of approved rate increases. The company has pending rate filings of ~$265 million, but there is no guarantee of full recovery.

**Earnings Surprise Pattern:** Four consecutive beats averaging +18% suggests the street is systematically underestimating results, likely because consensus does not fully account for weather impacts, surcharge mechanisms, and PFAS settlement proceeds. This provides a modest positive skew to forward estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

WTRG is the second-largest publicly traded U.S. water utility behind AWK. Its competitive advantages are scale-dependent: (1) access to capital markets at investment-grade rates, (2) regulatory expertise across multiple jurisdictions, (3) first-mover advantage in fair-value acquisition states, and (4) operational efficiencies from managing large systems.

The pending AWK merger would create a combined entity with unmatched scale (~4.7 million connections in 14+ states), potentially crowding out smaller acquirers (SJW, California Water, York Water) from the municipal acquisition pipeline. The combined company's ability to absorb PFAS compliance costs and infrastructure upgrades would be superior to any alternative acquirer.

**Competitive Position Trajectory:** Stable to improving, contingent on merger outcome. The standalone growth-through-acquisition strategy remains viable regardless of merger status, supported by a pipeline of 400,000 potential customer additions. The structural tailwind of aging municipal water infrastructure requiring billions in investment creates a durable runway for regulated water utilities broadly.

The natural gas segment adds diversification but also complicates the ESG profile and faces longer-term decarbonization headwinds. Post-merger, AWK management has indicated intent to maintain gas operations, but the political environment for gas utilities is deteriorating in certain jurisdictions.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Merger Failure (Primary Risk — 30-40% probability):** The PA OCA has formally recommended denial, and only 1 of multiple required state approvals has been granted. If the merger fails, the stock could decline 5-10%. Termination fee mechanics: AWK owes WTRG $370M if AWK terminates under specific conditions; WTRG owes AWK $370M if WTRG terminates under certain circumstances; AWK owes WTRG $835M under other termination scenarios.

**2. Regulatory/Affordability Headwind:** Management's own 10-K acknowledges "customer dissatisfaction reaching historic highs." Political pressure to limit rate increases could compress authorized ROEs below the current 9-10% range, impairing the rate-base-growth model.

**3. Interest Rate/Credit Risk:** Moody's Baa2/Negative outlook creates refinancing risk. The company needs approximately $1.7B in annual capex with OCF covering only ~60%. Recent debt issuances at 5.125-5.63% represent 100+ bps above the weighted average cost, creating gradual margin compression until rates decline.

**4. PFAS Compliance Uncertainty:** $450M+ in mandated capital investment with uncertain regulatory recovery timing across multiple jurisdictions. Annual operating costs of ~5% of installed capital create ongoing pressure.

**5. Weather Sensitivity:** Despite the WNA mechanism in PA gas (effective Oct 2024), the gas segment remains meaningfully seasonal. The 25.5% HDD increase that boosted 2025 results creates a difficult comparable for 2026.

**6. Tax Policy Risk:** Effective tax rate near zero relies on IRS repair deduction treatment. Any legislative change or IRS interpretive shift would materially increase cash tax obligations.

**Options Market Signal:** Short interest of 1.7% with 2.2 days to cover is negligible, indicating no meaningful bearish positioning. The stock's low beta and narrow 52-week range (~15%) suggest options markets price limited volatility — consistent with a regulated utility but potentially underpricing merger-binary outcomes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($41-45):**
- AWK merger receives all regulatory approvals by Q1 2027; spread closes to zero ($40-41)
- Standalone earnings growth of 5-7% re-rates toward peer multiples (21x × $2.26 = $47.50 in a full re-rate scenario)
- Rate case outcomes exceed expectations; DELCORA acquisition closes; acquisition pipeline accelerates
- Falling interest rates reduce financing costs and expand utility multiples broadly

**Bear Case ($33-36):**
- PA PUC denies merger or imposes unacceptable conditions; deal collapses; stock falls 5-10% on standalone revaluation
- Rising rates compress utility multiples further; Moody's downgrades to Baa3
- Rate case outcomes disappoint as affordability concerns dominate regulatory proceedings
- Gas segment faces accelerating decarbonization policy pressure

## Conclusion

Essential Utilities presents a modest merger arbitrage opportunity with adequate standalone fundamentals as a backstop. The 3.7% dividend yield, 17x forward P/E, and four consecutive earnings beats provide reasonable downside protection. However, the dominant variable is the PA PUC decision, which is genuinely uncertain given formal consumer advocate opposition.

At current prices, the risk-reward is balanced: ~8-10% upside to merger close vs. ~5-8% downside if the deal fails (with downside scenarios subject to termination fee mechanics that depend on which party terminates and under what conditions). The stock is neither cheap enough on standalone fundamentals to warrant a BUY (chronic underperformance vs. peers for structural reasons including gas exposure and leverage), nor sufficiently impaired to avoid. HOLD with reassessment pending regulatory clarity.
