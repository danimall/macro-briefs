**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Jackson Financial is a pure-play US retail annuity franchise, de-merged from UK Prudential plc in 2021, that has delivered a 506% five-year TSR versus a 176% peer group primarily by buying back shares aggressively while the stock traded below embedded value. The economic engine is spread- and fee-based income on a large in-force annuity block, wrapped in a hedging program that manages the equity- and rate-market risk in variable-annuity living-benefit guarantees. FY2025 produced record retail annuity sales of $19.7 billion (+10% YoY) and adjusted operating EPS of $22.67, even as GAAP net income printed a $17 million loss, illustrating that GAAP is dominated by non-economic mark-to-market noise, not underlying deterioration.

The valuation optics are misleading in both directions. Trailing GAAP EPS of -$0.24 makes screening metrics useless; on adjusted operating earnings, the stock trades near 5x current-year consensus of $23.66 and roughly 4.3x next-year $28.02, a discount that reflects the market's discomfort with the accounting volatility and the opacity of the captive-reinsurance structure (Brooke Re) that insulates statutory capital. Consensus estimates are rising across every window (+0.3% for the current year over 90 days, +7.6% for next-year over 90 days and +8.2% over 30 days), and the stock sits at $119.28 versus a consensus target range of $128-$140, placing analyst targets above the current price.

We rate JXN a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The setup has genuinely improving components: record sales, a strategic alts pivot via the 10-year TPG partnership, rising estimates, and a below-consensus price. Three things keep us from returning to the BUY we carried earlier this year: the March-quarter operating miss (-14.5% surprise, breaking a three-quarter beat streak), a CEO/CFO succession that lands October 1, and an equity/rate tail embedded in the VA-guarantee book that is not fully visible in trailing data. The score trajectory has declined from 7.0 to 5.5 across prior reports, driven mostly by valuation normalization as the stock re-rated; our updated read stabilizes the profile at 5.75.

## Company Overview

Jackson operates principally through Jackson National Life Insurance Company, distributing variable annuities (VAs), fixed index annuities (FIAs), registered index-linked annuities (RILAs), and fixed annuities through independent financial professionals: independent broker-dealers, banks, and RIAs. It is consistently a top-selling VA provider in the US, leveraging one of the industry's largest wholesaling forces. An in-house institutional asset manager, PPM America, rounds out the structure.

Two structural shifts define the forward story. First, Jackson is deliberately re-weighting new business toward RILAs and FIAs to reduce reliance on capital-intensive VA guarantees, a mix shift that lowers the equity-tail sensitivity of the incremental book over time. Second, the January 2026 TPG partnership marks Jackson's entry into the "insurance + alternative asset manager" model that Apollo/Athene and KKR/Global Atlantic pioneered: TPG will manage a minimum of $12 billion of Jackson's assets (long-term target $20 billion) over a 10-year term, with the strategic aim of widening general-account spreads through higher-yielding private credit. Jackson retains ownership of the assets and the liabilities; this is an outsourced investment-management arrangement, not a change of control. A related unregistered equity issuance in January 2026 strongly implies TPG received a JXN equity stake as part of the transaction.

## Financial Analysis

The single most important interpretive point is that standardized screening metrics are meaningless for this insurer. The balance sheet is dominated by separate-account assets (policyholder VA assets offset by matching liabilities) and large policyholder reserves; the resulting Altman Z-Score and current-ratio screens flag "distress" that does not exist. The correct lens is statutory capital, RBC ratios, and adjusted operating earnings.

GAAP net income has swung violently by design: $3.42B (2021), $6.19B (2022), $0.90B (2023), $0.90B (2024), and a -$17M loss (2025). The swings reflect mark-to-market movements on hedge assets and fair-value remeasurement of VA guarantee liabilities under LDTI / market-risk-benefit accounting. Hedges are calibrated to economic (statutory) exposure; GAAP marks the hedge assets to market while reserve movements follow a different path, producing accounting mismatch. The 2025 near-breakeven GAAP result alongside adjusted operating EPS and ~$1.0B of net cash flow to the holding company is the definitive example.

Capital return is the through-line. Management runs to "Net Cash Flow to JFI" (the holdco), which reached ~$1.0B in 2025 and funds dividends and repurchases. The TSR was driven predominantly by buybacks executed while the stock traded below book/embedded value, a genuinely accretive use of capital when the discount is wide. The current 2.9% dividend yield is modest; the total return case rests on the combination of buybacks plus dividend plus any residual re-rating.

The earnings-surprise record shows the recent shift clearly. Jackson beat by 3.9%, 11.8%, and 11.6% in the June, September, and December 2025 quarters, then missed by 14.5% in March 2026 ($5.15 actual versus $6.02 estimate). A single miss after a three-quarter streak warrants attention; it can reflect hedge-related operating drag, market-driven fee compression on the separate-account block, or a normalization of prior tailwinds. It is not by itself a thesis-breaker, but it is the reason we are not chasing the recent price rebound with a BUY.

Capital-structure activity has been elevated through 2026: multiple Item 2.03 financial-obligation 8-Ks (March, June, July) consistent with debt refinancing and funding of the captive reinsurance and TPG structures, plus the January equity issuance tied to TPG. Holdco leverage relies on subsidiary dividends, which are subject to insurance-regulator approval, so the cash-flow-to-holdco metric is the correct coverage gauge rather than consolidated GAAP.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case is more credible than the trailing GAAP number suggests. Record retail annuity sales confirm demand strength, driven by RILAs/FIAs as Jackson diversifies its mix. Rising rates over recent years have been a tailwind for annuity demand (attractive guaranteed rates) and for spread income on the general account.

Estimate revisions are the strongest forward signal here and they are broadly positive: current-quarter consensus rose +3.1% over 30 days (+7.6% over 90 days) to $5.76, next-quarter +3.7% over 30 days (+7.1% over 90 days) to $6.03, current-year to $23.66 (+3.1% over 30 days), and next-year to $28.02 (+8.2% over 30 days). The next-year figure implies ~18% growth over the current-year estimate, and the acceleration in the 30-day revision on next-year estimates (+8.2%) versus the 90-day (+7.6%) indicates momentum is building rather than fading. This does not carry the same weight as a classic trough-recovery story, since Jackson is not recovering from depressed earnings, but the direction and breadth of revisions support a constructive growth read.

The TPG partnership is the structural growth lever. Redirecting $12-20B toward alts-enhanced credit should lift the investment yield on the general account, widening spreads and supporting adjusted operating ROE. The offset is that it imports private-credit and illiquidity exposure onto the balance sheet at a time when rating agencies and regulators are increasingly focused on exactly that model. The economics (fee structure, asset-class mix, equity-stake size) should be confirmed against the definitive agreement.

## Valuation Assessment

On adjusted operating earnings, JXN is inexpensive on an absolute basis: roughly 5.0x current-year consensus and 4.3x next-year estimates. The reported trailing/forward P/E of -20 is a GAAP artifact and should be ignored. The peer set — PRI (11.7x), CNO (20.0x), GL (11.4x), UNM (19.9x), with a median of ~19.9x — trades at multiples that are not directly comparable because those peers do not carry Jackson's degree of VA-guarantee accounting volatility, but even the lowest-multiple life peers trade at more than 2x Jackson's operating multiple. Brighthouse (BHF), the closest structural comp with a VA-heavy legacy, screens at -21.6x on the same GAAP distortion.

The discount to peers and to absolute earnings power is real, but the equity lessons here counsel caution: cheapness has been an anti-predictive signal, and a low multiple without an independent re-rating catalyst is more often a value trap than an opportunity. Jackson's discount prices the GAAP unpredictability, the opacity of the Brooke Re captive, the holdco-dividend dependency, and the equity/rate tail. The re-rating catalyst that would compress the discount is a demonstrated ability to keep generating ~$1B of holdco cash flow and returning capital through a full market cycle, plus evidence that the TPG spread pickup flows through to operating ROE without incident.

Analyst targets sit at $128 low, $134.5 median, $140 high, all above the current price. With estimates rising and the stock cheap relative to those estimates, targets that lag reality tend to be revised upward as consensus catches up. We treat the $128-$140 band as a floor for the base case rather than a ceiling. We score valuation a 6.0: genuinely cheap on operating earnings with rising estimates, but discounted for structural reasons that require a catalyst to unwind rather than mechanical mean reversion.

## Competitive Landscape

Jackson's differentiation is scale in variable annuities and best-in-class advisor distribution, backed by one of the industry's largest wholesaling forces. It competes against Equitable/AXA, Brighthouse, Lincoln National, Prudential Financial, Corebridge, and Allianz Life. The competitive edge in this industry is shifting away from product features toward capital efficiency and investment sourcing, which is why the TPG deal and the captive reinsurance structure matter.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving. Record sales and continued share strength in RILAs/FIAs indicate Jackson is holding and diversifying its franchise rather than ceding ground, and the deliberate mix shift away from capital-intensive VA guarantees improves the capital efficiency of new business. The alts pivot puts Jackson on the same competitive footing as the Apollo/KKR-backed insurers who have been widening spreads through private credit. The risk to the competitive read is that everyone is running the same playbook, so the incremental spread advantage may compress as the model becomes ubiquitous, and the private-credit exposure it introduces is a growing focus of regulatory scrutiny. We score competitive position 6.0: a durable distribution moat with a sensible strategic pivot, tempered by an increasingly crowded model.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for JXN are specific and, in the tail, material:

**Equity market & rate sensitivity of VA guarantees** is the dominant risk. A sharp equity decline or rate move raises the value of living-benefit guarantee liabilities. Jackson hedges economically, but hedges are calibrated to statutory/capital exposure, creating GAAP mismatch and reported-earnings volatility. Hedge breakage or basis risk in a severe stress scenario is the core tail. This is not fully visible in trailing data and is the primary reason risk scores low.

**Statutory capital / captive reinsurance opacity.** Brooke Re and related captives are designed to insulate statutory capital from GAAP hedge-accounting volatility. Their effectiveness under severe market stress, and any regulatory reconsideration of captive structures, is the key monitorable. The 2026 financial-obligation 8-Ks suggest ongoing activity around these entities. This mechanism lets Jackson keep returning capital despite GAAP swings; its resilience is the linchpin of the thesis.

**Private-credit import via TPG.** Redirecting assets toward alts widens spreads but adds illiquidity and credit exposure to the balance sheet at a time when rating agencies and regulators are increasingly focused on the "insurance + private-asset-management" model.

**Policyholder behavior, reinvestment/spread risk, and distribution/regulatory dependence** (DOL fiduciary rules, SEC Reg BI) round out the specific-but-priced risks.

The recent March operating miss compounds the caution: it breaks the beat streak and raises the question of whether the prior tailwinds are normalizing. Governance risk is modest, since the succession is planned, internal, and orderly, with an actuary/hedging specialist (Walta) taking the CFO seat, which is the correct skill set for this business, but it does mark the end of the founding-era leadership. We score risk 4.5: the equity/rate tail and captive-structure opacity are genuine, asymmetric, and not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mixed. Near-term ATM IV of 46.9% is rich relative to 32.9% one-year historical vol (a 14-point spread, 73rd percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (46.9% near-term versus 43.7% at six months), pricing imminent stress consistent with the pending earnings and the October 1 leadership transition. The put/call skew is balanced (-0.9%), and while the put/call volume ratio spiked to 6.62, the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed to 0.58 from 5.41 a month earlier, indicating that a prior bearish positioning wave has largely unwound as IV fell 74 points from a June spike. The net read: elevated near-term event risk is priced, but there is no persistent structural bearish tilt in the options book. The rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry timing rather than chasing the current rebound.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($155-$175).** Jackson keeps generating ~$1B of holdco cash flow, sustains buybacks that continue shrinking the share count against a stock trading at 4-5x operating earnings, and the TPG partnership delivers a visible spread pickup that lifts adjusted operating ROE. Estimates continue their upward march toward and beyond $28 next year, and the discount to peers compresses toward 6-7x operating earnings as the market gains confidence in the captive-reinsurance apparatus through a full cycle. At 6.5x next-year $28.02, the stock is worth ~$182; a more conservative 6x on $28 is ~$168.

**Bear Case ($80-$90).** A sharp equity drawdown or disorderly rate move exposes hedge basis risk, statutory capital comes under pressure, and the captive structure proves less resilient than assumed, forcing a pause in capital return. The private-credit exposure imported via TPG marks down in a credit cascade. Operating EPS drops toward the mid-teens and the multiple compresses further on capital-return uncertainty, taking the stock back toward the 52-week low of $82.65.

The asymmetry is two-sided. The upside is a re-rating of a cheap, cash-generative franchise; the downside is a low-probability but high-severity tail tied to hedge integrity and statutory capital. That balance, rather than a clear edge, is what defines the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD; the reassessment cadence is quarterly, with the next natural checkpoint the upcoming earnings report and the October 1 leadership transition.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of operating EPS beats re-establishing the pre-March trend, confirming the March miss was a one-off rather than a normalization of tailwinds.
- Demonstrated statutory-capital / RBC stability through a market drawdown, or clear disclosure that materially improves visibility into the Brooke Re captive's stress capacity.
- A pullback toward the low-$100s that widens the discount to operating earnings without any fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A pause or cut in buybacks/dividend signaling holdco cash-flow stress.
- Hedge-breakage or basis-risk losses that flow through statutory capital in a market stress.
- A material adverse mark on the TPG-managed private-credit book, or regulatory action against the captive-reinsurance structure.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release and the October 1, 2026 CEO/CFO transition are the two near-term inputs; a full re-underwrite is warranted once the 10-K financial-statement notes clarify RBC ratios and captive capacity.

## Conclusion

Jackson Financial is a cheap, cash-generative annuity franchise with record sales, rising consensus estimates, a sensible strategic pivot toward alts-enhanced spread income, and a below-consensus price with analyst targets above the market. Those are real positives, and they argue against being bearish. But the equity is fundamentally a levered bet on the integrity of a VA-guarantee hedging program and an opaque captive-reinsurance apparatus, carrying an equity/rate tail that is not fully visible in trailing data; the March operating miss, the October leadership transition, and the imported private-credit exposure keep us from returning to the BUY we carried earlier this year. We rate JXN a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 5.75, stabilizing the recent decline from 7.0. Constructive investors should accumulate on weakness toward the low-$100s or on confirmation that operating earnings and statutory capital hold through the coming quarters; the current rebound to $119 offers a less compelling entry given rich near-term implied vol pricing event risk.
