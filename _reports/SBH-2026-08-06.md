## Executive Summary

Sally Beauty is a two-segment beauty retailer and distributor generating roughly $3.73B of revenue with essentially no growth: net sales have declined in each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025, cumulatively -3.0% from FY2022, and management guides FY2026 to $3.725-3.733B with comparable sales of about +0.5%. The company-selected compensation measure in the proxy, adjusted operating income, has fallen from $461.1M in FY2021 to $328.4M in FY2025, a 28.8% decline; FY2026 guidance of $329-335M does not begin to close that gap.

What the equity offers instead is a funded per-share compounding engine. Nine-month FY2026 adjusted operating earnings were $239.487M against $239.829M a year earlier — down $0.342M — while interest expense fell $6.962M and the diluted share count fell 4.59%. Those two items account for the entire 8.88% increase in adjusted diluted EPS. Management has been explicit that the FY2026 EPS guide "assumes 50% of Free Cash Flow goes towards share repurchases." At $15.79 the stock trades at 7.7x the $2.06 guided adjusted EPS midpoint, 7.1x the $2.24 FY2027 consensus, roughly 4.7x EV/adjusted EBITDA, and a ~13.4% free cash flow yield on the guided ~$200M. The remaining $395.9M repurchase authorization equals 26.8% of the market capitalization. Net leverage is 1.4x with no maturity before 2030 and $655.5M of liquidity.

The prior report on this name was an AVOID at $13.83 on June 19; the stock is 14.2% higher. That call underweighted two things: the durability of the interest-plus-buyback arithmetic, and the strength of the domestic Sally business, where U.S./Canada comps ran +3.5% in Q3 FY2026 against consolidated Sally comps of +1.6%. It got one thing right, and the Q3 print reinforced it: BSG comparable sales deteriorated from +0.5% to -2.1%, segment operating margin turned down 20 bps, and the company-employed salon business consultant force shrank 13.1% year over year. BSG carries $448.5M of the $538.4M consolidated goodwill and has not been through a quantitative impairment test since September 30, 2023. I am upgrading to HOLD at an overall 5.0 — the earnings mechanism is working and the balance sheet is comfortable, but 14% of the discount has been paid away since June and my base case now offers roughly 11% upside against a 24% bear case.

## Company Overview

Two segments with structurally different economics:

**Sally Beauty** — ~$2.09B FY2025 net sales, 3,066 stores at June 30, 2026, operating across the U.S., Canada, Mexico, Puerto Rico, the U.K., Ireland, Belgium, France, Germany, the Netherlands and Chile. Omni-channel retail to both DIY consumers and stylists. Average U.S./Canada store ~1,700 sq ft with ~7,000 SKUs, sited mostly in strip centers anchored by grocers and mass merchants. Owned and exclusive-label brands (Ion, Salon Care, Beauty Secrets, Bondbar, Inspired by Nature) were 35% of Sally sales in FY2025. Segment gross margin 60.8%.

**Beauty Systems Group** — ~$1.61B FY2025 net sales, 1,320 stores (1,189 company-operated Cosmo Prof plus 131 Armstrong McCall franchises). A professional-only wholesale distributor to licensed stylists and salons in the U.S. and Canada. Channel mix: company stores 69%, e-commerce 14.7%, salon business consultants 8.5%, franchise 7.6%. Segment gross margin 40.4% in 9M FY2026, about 20 points below Sally, reflecting the distribution model.

Hair color and hair care together are approximately 70% of consolidated sales and have been in each of the last three fiscal years. On the supply side the company is a concentrated buyer: Henkel, L'Oréal's Professional Products Division, Wella, John Paul Mitchell Systems and Kao accounted for roughly 48% of FY2025 consolidated merchandise purchases. These are vendors, not customers; on the demand side no single customer reaches 10% of revenue. Approximately 18% of FY2025 revenue was non-U.S., and management is shrinking that: Spain was divested in FY2025 and the Q3 FY2026 10-Q discloses a "strategic exit of the majority of our low-margin full-service operations in Europe," with associated inventory write-offs.

Strategic programs in flight: Fuel for Growth (cost and efficiency), Sally Ignited (store remodels — 30 completed at FY2025 year-end, 50 more planned in FY2026), Happy Beauty Co. (indie-beauty test concept, ~20 pilot stores), and a new fragrance category in select Sally stores.

Comparable sales were +0.3% in both FY2024 and FY2025, so the revenue decline is store-base contraction rather than same-store deterioration: Sally has gone from 3,439 units at the start of FY2023 to 3,066 in June 2026, including 294 closures under the FY2023 distribution-center and store optimization plan.

### Q3 and nine-month FY2026: where the earnings growth comes from

Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026): net sales $935.5M (+0.2%), consolidated comps flat, gross margin 52.4% (+90 bps), GAAP operating margin 9.2% (+80 bps), GAAP diluted EPS $0.55 (+25%), adjusted diluted EPS $0.55 (+8%). Because pre-tax adjustments totaled only $0.194M, the GAAP and adjusted figures are effectively identical this quarter, so the earnings beat is robust to whichever basis consensus used. Revenue was the soft line.

The nine-month decomposition, reproduced from the company's own non-GAAP reconciliation, is the central number set in this report:

| 9M ended June 30 | FY2026 | FY2025 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adjusted operating earnings | $239.487M | $239.829M | **-$0.342M** |
| Interest expense | $42.478M | $49.440M | **-$6.962M** |
| Adjusted pre-tax earnings | $197.009M | $190.389M | +$6.620M |
| Adjusted net earnings | $146.232M | $140.769M | +$5.463M |
| Diluted shares | 99.402M | 104.187M | -4.59% |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $1.4711 | $1.3511 | **+8.88%** |

-$0.342M + $6.962M = +$6.620M. The entire improvement in adjusted pre-tax earnings is interest savings. Of the 8.88% EPS growth, roughly 4.8 points come from the smaller share count (1/(1-0.0459)-1) and the remaining ~3.9 points from higher adjusted net earnings, itself interest savings plus a 29 bp reduction in the adjusted effective tax rate. On a GAAP basis nine-month operating earnings fell 5.5% to $234.3M and net earnings fell 2.5% to $142.3M, because the prior-year period included a $26.6M gain on the sale of the former Denton, Texas headquarters.

There is a legitimate counter-reading. Gross margin expanded 70 bps year to date and 90 bps in Q3 while the company absorbed growth spending: nine-month capex rose to $84.3M from $59.3M for the new Plano headquarters and Sally Ignited remodels, share-based compensation rose to $18.1M from $14.8M, and advertising increased. Flat adjusted operating earnings while gross margin expands and investment steps up is a different picture from flat operating earnings with flat gross margin. But the gross-margin gains are being consumed by SG&A rather than dropping through, and that has now been true for three consecutive quarters.

The segments crossed in Q3. Management attributes BSG's decline to hair-care softness and, year to date, pressure on stylist spending tied to the government shutdown early in the fiscal year. The 10-Q's category disaggregation supplies a cleaner mechanism: hair care fell as a share of sales in both segments (Sally 21.2% from 23.0%; BSG 40.6% from 41.6%) while hair color rose in both (Sally 45.2% from 42.9%; BSG 44.3% from 43.0%). Hair care is roughly 41% of BSG's mix versus 21% of Sally's, so a category-level weakness in hair care damages BSG about twice as hard, while the offsetting color strength is spread across both. Sally also has a channel BSG structurally cannot use — third-party marketplaces (Amazon, Walmart.com, DoorDash, Uber Eats, Instacart) — cited as a comp driver in every FY2026 filing, while BSG's professional-only anti-diversion obligations bar the equivalent.

Mix data cannot separate category weakness from share loss. That distinction is the most consequential unresolved question in this analysis, and no third-party channel data was obtainable to settle it.

### Cash flow

Nine-month operating cash flow was $247.5M against $154.0M, and company-defined free cash flow $163.2M against $138.3M (+18.0%). Both need adjustment. The prior-year FCF base adds back $43.6M of headquarters sale proceeds; on a clean OCF-less-capex basis 9M FY2025 was $94.7M, so the underlying improvement is larger than reported. But most of the OCF swing is timing rather than earnings — net earnings actually fell. The drivers were accounts payable and accrued liabilities (a use of $4.6M versus $74.6M, a ~$70M favorable swing), income taxes paid ($48.2M versus $75.4M), and collection of landlord receivables on the new headquarters ($14.9M source versus $6.8M use), partially offset by inventory (a $10.3M use versus a $34.2M source).

Management's own guidance implies reversal: FY2026 FCF of ~$200M with capex of ~$100M implies full-year OCF near $300M, which leaves Q4 OCF of roughly $52.5M and Q4 FCF of roughly $37M against $78.0M of clean Q4 FY2025 FCF. Annualizing the nine months would produce ~$330M of OCF, about 10% above what guidance implies. The normalized anchor is the guided ~$200M FCF.

### Balance sheet

At June 30, 2026: cash $173.1M; inventory $996.0M (-1% y/y); total assets $2,883.1M; equity $871.5M (up from $794.2M at September 30, 2025). Debt is $815.0M principal — $600.0M 2032 senior notes and $215.0M term loan B due 2030 — carried at $807.6M. No ABL borrowings, $482.4M available on the $500M facility, total liquidity $655.5M, and stated compliance with all covenants.

A definitional point matters here. A screened "debt/equity of 2.62" is total liabilities over equity, which sweeps in ~$700M of operating-lease liabilities and $224.5M of trade payables. Financial debt to equity is 0.93x. Company-stated net leverage is 1.4x, which cross-checks against net debt of $641.9M and implies credit-agreement EBITDA near $458M — consistent with a ~$450-455M FY2026 adjusted EBITDA estimate. The conservative rating-agency framing, adding $707.8M of discounted lease liabilities, is roughly 3.0x. For a company with 4,386 leased locations that lease-adjusted figure is the honest one, and it is still comfortable against no maturity before 2030.

The $200M notional swap at 3.705% matured in April 2026, leaving the $215M term loan floating and unhedged. At that size a 100 bp move is ~$2.15M pre-tax, about $0.016 per diluted share — immaterial, and shrinking as the loan is prepaid ($57.0M voluntary prepayment in 9M FY2026).

One structural disclosure is informative beyond the credit: the note guarantor group (largely domestic) holds 76.2% of consolidated assets, generated 80.9% of nine-month sales, but 90.3% of net earnings. Foreign non-guarantors are ~19% of sales and ~10% of earnings, which independently corroborates that international is materially less profitable and supports the European exit.

Altman Z of 2.59 places SBH in the grey zone, but two mechanical factors drive it: $646.7M of capitalized operating-lease assets inflate the asset base for every turnover ratio, and nine years of buybacks have taken additional paid-in capital to zero, thinning the equity base. That is a real observation about capital-structure thinness, not a near-term solvency signal against 1.4x net leverage and no revolver draw.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here at the consolidated level, and the guidance says so: revenue $3.725-3.733B (a midpoint trim of ~$8.5M from May), comps ~+0.5% (cut from flat-to-+1%), adjusted operating earnings $329-335M (midpoint cut from $335M to $332M), adjusted EPS $2.04-2.08 (midpoint held at $2.06), capex ~$100M, FCF ~$200M unchanged. Implied Q4 revenue of $943-951M against $947.1M last year is -0.4% to +0.4%; implied Q4 adjusted operating earnings of $89.5-95.5M against ~$88.6M is +1.0% to +7.8%.

The growth components that do exist:

- **Sally domestic**, +3.5% U.S./Canada comps in Q3, aided by marketplace expansion and 50 planned Sally Ignited remodels. Management reports qualitative gains from the first 30 remodels (dwell time, units per transaction, average ticket above the fleet) but discloses no comp lift or payback period, so the return on the FY2026 remodel capital is unverified.
- **Global e-commerce** at 12% of net sales ($110M, +11%) in Q3, up from 10.7% in FY2025 and 9.3% in FY2023. This is growth through a channel the 10-K itself flags as margin-dilutive, and the Q3 10-Q confirms marketplace commissions as a Sally SG&A driver.
- **Gross margin expansion** of 70 bps year to date, apparently more than offsetting tariff cost on the 35% of Sally sales that is directly sourced owned-brand product.
- **Per-share arithmetic**: nine-month share count reconciles precisely to a 3.9% net reduction (97.875M at September 30 plus 1.655M issued, less 4.920M repurchased and 0.518M withheld, equals 94.092M at June 30, then 93.622M at July 30). At $15.79, deploying 50% of a $200M FCF stream retires roughly 6.3M shares, or 6.8% of the count, per year.

Estimate revisions are flat to slightly negative: current quarter -2.0% over 90 days, current year -0.4%, next year -1.2% to $2.24. That is not the deterioration a falling-estimate screen implies, but it is not positive momentum either, and it is nowhere near the threshold at which revision velocity becomes a standalone buy signal. The offsetting evidence is delivery: four consecutive beats (+14.9%, +2.4%, +6.3%, +2.0%), a pattern of small, consistent outperformance that gives reasonable confidence in the $2.06 FY2026 guide and the $2.24 FY2027 consensus.

The critical constraint on the growth story is that the interest tailwind is bounded. Interest expense has already fallen from $76.4M in FY2024 to a ~$54.8M run rate. The $600M 2032 notes are the floor; only the $215M term loan remains as a source of further savings. After that, per-share growth depends entirely on buybacks funded by an FCF stream that management guides flat, and the buyback retires proportionally fewer shares as the price rises. The engine decays.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own framing is unflattering: "there are few significant barriers to entry into the marketplace for most of the products we sell." Sally competes with local and regional beauty supply stores, mass merchandisers, drug stores, supermarkets, online retailers and salons that resell product. BSG competes with wholesale distributors, online retailers, and manufacturers selling direct to salons — including manufacturers that are "vertically integrated through the acquisition of distributors and stores." Amazon and Shopify are named specifically.

The moat, described literally, has four components: scale purchasing across ~4,386 locations; exclusive and partially-exclusive distribution rights; a salon business consultant field force providing just-in-time service to capital-constrained salons; and hair-color category expertise across 15 million-plus active loyalty customers. The second is economically the strongest and legally the weakest — the 10-K discloses that supply arrangements are generally at-will or terminable "without cause upon 90 days or less notice," some with minimum purchase requirements that become live precisely when comps decline, as at BSG. The third is contracting: total SBCs fell to 558 from 611 year over year, and because 193 and 191 respectively are franchisee-employed, company-employed SBCs fell from 420 to 365, or -13.1%.

Two readings of the SBC reduction are available and the filings do not adjudicate. The favorable one is a deliberate Fuel for Growth channel migration from a high-cost field force to digital ordering, consistent with BSG's 80 bp year-to-date gross-margin expansion and the planned FY2026 rebuild of the BSG app and e-commerce platform (BSG e-commerce rose to 14.7% of segment sales from 13.9% while SBC fell to 8.5% from 9.4%). The unfavorable one is that cutting 13% of the direct salon relationship layer is a cause of the comp decline rather than a neutral efficiency action. No management commentary resolving this was verifiable — the Q3 earnings call Q&A was not obtainable.

The Amazon relationship deserves stating plainly: Amazon is disclosed as a competitor and simultaneously operates as a channel through which Sally sells, taking a commission that appears in Sally's SG&A. Marketplace expansion is cited as a Sally comp tailwind in each FY2026 filing. The same counterparties supply the growth and take part of the margin.

Externally, the comparison to Ulta is unfavorable on trajectory — higher revenue, an upward long-term path, and a widening gap — which is consistent with SBH's own four years of flat-to-down sales and a shrinking store base. SBH is a cash-generative, share-shrinking, low-growth asset rather than a growth retailer, and should be valued as such.

One mitigating point on the sector: hair color and care are low-ticket replenishment categories, roughly 70% of sales, which historically hold up better than big-ticket discretionary in a consumer slowdown. SBH's basket is closer to a consumable than to furniture or apparel. That limits the macro downside without changing the competitive trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**BSG goodwill.** Goodwill is $538.4M, or 62% of book equity, of which $448.5M (83%) sits at BSG. The last quantitative goodwill test was performed at September 30, 2023; the FY2026 annual test completed January 31, 2026 was qualitative for goodwill, with only indefinite-lived intangibles tested quantitatively. Trade-name impairments have already occurred ($4.5M in FY2025 on certain Sally names, $1.8M on a European name). If BSG comps stay negative through the FY2027 test, a quantitative test and possible write-down become live. The impairment itself would be non-cash — hitting GAAP earnings, book equity, ROE and the Altman equity ratio but not adjusted operating earnings, EBITDA, cash flow or covenant compliance — but it would be a sentiment event on a stock with 30% short interest.

**Supplier concentration and revocable exclusivity.** Five suppliers are ~48% of merchandise purchases on contracts terminable on ≤90 days' notice, and the 10-K explicitly warns that suppliers "may seek to decrease their reliance on distribution intermediaries… by promoting their own distribution channels." BSG's competitive position *is* its distribution rights. This is the risk most directly threatening the durability of the earnings base, and there is no FY2026 update to the concentration figure.

**Unquantified litigation tail.** Item 1A specifically flags chemical hair relaxer and straightener litigation against manufacturers and distributors as potentially material, while Item 3 and the 10-Q describe legal proceedings only in generic ordinary-course terms with no company-specific matter, accrual or exposure range. This is an unquantified tail, not a quantified liability, and Note 10 of the FY2025 statements was not read.

**Margin structure.** E-commerce at 12% of sales carries higher delivery and marketplace commission costs; Sally's SG&A rose to 45.4% of segment sales from 45.1% and segment operating margin fell 10 bps year to date despite +2.5% sales and +20 bps gross margin. Labor inflation across ~27,000 associates is a persistent rather than discrete headwind. Tariffs on directly sourced owned-brand product (35% of Sally sales) are being mitigated so far, but the 10-K concedes mitigation may not be full.

**Governance and controls.** The CFO seat turned over during FY2026: Marlo Platz appears as "Former SVP & CFO" by June 2026 with two Item 5.02 8-Ks filed March 17 and April 2, and Adrianne Lee signed the Q3 10-Q as SVP & CFO after a 98,280-unit new-hire RSU grant on May 13. Her prior role and the reason for the transition rest on a single secondary source and were not verified against the 8-K text; no adverse inference is drawn, and finance turnover during a cost-transformation program has many benign explanations. Separately, Oracle Retail Sales Audit was implemented during Q3 with new and modified controls over the revenue cycle; management concluded disclosure controls were effective. Routine, but a live change worth monitoring.

**Short interest.** 30.1% of float short, 20.05M shares, 12.5 days to cover. This cuts both ways: it signals a large cohort of investors underwriting structural deterioration, and it makes any positive surprise mechanically violent. The August 3 print produced a 14.99-to-17.20 intraday range on 2.98M shares, roughly 1.7x average volume, consistent with short covering into the beat.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 23 contracts across three expirations — so the readings deserve limited weight. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 91.2% against 45.0% one-year historical volatility, with a supplied put/call skew of 37.9% flagged as elevated put demand and an implied move of ±11.9% on the August 21 expiry, would normally indicate a priced catalyst. But earnings have already been reported, and the term structure collapses to 40.4% at three months (with the skew label reversing) and 46.5% at six months. The most likely explanation is a stale or wide-quoted near-term chain rather than a real imminent-stress signal; the reported change in ATM IV from 1,568.9% to 91.2% since the prior snapshot confirms this feed carries artifacts.

The one reading worth carrying is the put/call open interest ratio of 5.30, the kind of level that is consistent with a 30% short base using listed puts as part of the expression or hedging existing shorts. That does not confirm the bear thesis independently, and it does not contradict the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($21.00, +33%)

At 7.7x guided earnings and a 13.4% FCF yield, an equity that shrinks its share count 4-7% a year and pays down debt does not need revenue growth to compound. Nine-month FCF conversion is real, net leverage is 1.4x with no maturity before 2030, and $395.9M of authorization equals 26.8% of the market cap. The domestic Sally business is accelerating (+3.5% U.S./Canada comps), gross margin is up 70 bps year to date through a tariff cycle, and the European full-service exit removes operations that contribute ~19% of sales but ~10% of earnings. If the FY2026 investment phase (capex $84.3M year to date versus $59.3M, elevated advertising and stock comp) ends and the gross-margin gains drop through to operating earnings, adjusted operating income growth resumes on top of the per-share arithmetic. Four consecutive beats support the guide. With 30% of float short and 12.5 days to cover, two quarters of BSG stabilization would force covering into a re-rating toward Signet's 12.9x rather than Academy's 9.2x.

### Bear Case ($12.00, -24%)

Adjusted operating earnings have not grown: nine-month adjusted operating income is $0.3M below last year, and FY2026 guidance of $329-335M against FY2025's $328.4M adds 0.2-2.0% on a base already 28.8% below FY2021. Every dollar of reported EPS growth comes from interest expense that has a floor at the $600M 2032 notes and from buybacks that retire fewer shares as the price rises. Meanwhile the segment holding 83% of goodwill is deteriorating: BSG comps went from +0.5% to -2.1%, its operating margin turned down, and its company-employed field sales force shrank 13.1%. If hair-care weakness is share loss to manufacturer-direct and online channels rather than a category cycle, BSG's earnings base is impaired, the $448.5M of goodwill untested since September 2023 comes into question, and the multiple compresses rather than expands. Supply contracts terminable in 90 days sit under the distribution franchise. Guidance also implies Q4 FCF of only ~$37M against $78.0M clean last year, so the cash-flow momentum that supports the yield is partly working-capital and tax timing. Add a consumer slowdown into calendar H2 and 7.7x becomes 6x.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Overall score 5.0, up from 4.8. This is an upgrade from AVOID, and I state the reason plainly: the June AVOID at $13.83 underweighted the durability of the interest-plus-buyback engine and the strength of Sally domestic comps, and the stock is 14.2% higher. What the AVOID got right was the operating stagnation and BSG deterioration, both of which the Q3 print confirmed. The upgrade is not to BUY because the same guidance now costs 7.7x instead of 6.7x, base-case upside is roughly 11%, and Consumer Discretionary specialty retail has been an unforgiving place to underwrite cheapness without a catalyst.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**

2. **FY2027 guidance shows adjusted operating earnings growth of 4%+** on the FY2026 base of ~$332M, demonstrating that the 70-90 bp gross-margin gains are dropping through rather than being consumed by SG&A.

1. **BSG comps deteriorate beyond -3%** or a quantitative goodwill test is triggered on the $448.5M BSG balance.

## Conclusion

Sally Beauty is a competently run, cash-generative distributor with a stagnant operating business and a functioning financial engine. The engine is real: $200M of guided free cash flow against a $1,478M market capitalization, half of it committed to repurchase, on a balance sheet at 1.4x net leverage with no maturity before 2030. At 7.7x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and 7.1x FY2027 consensus, the arithmetic of a shrinking share count delivers mid-to-high single-digit per-share growth without any help from revenue.

The engine also has a fuel gauge. Nine-month adjusted operating earnings were $0.3M lower than last year, FY2026 guidance adds 0.2-2.0% to an adjusted operating income base that is 28.8% below FY2021, the interest tailwind is bounded by the $215M term loan, and the buyback becomes less accretive as the price rises. The segment carrying 83% of the goodwill is the one going backwards, its comps deteriorated 260 bps in Q3, its field sales force is 13% smaller, and the last quantitative test of its $448.5M of goodwill predates the deterioration by nearly three years. Whether hair-care weakness is a category cycle or share loss to manufacturer-direct channels is the question that determines which case is right, and it is not answerable from the available disclosures.

At $15.79 the stock prices roughly the middle of that uncertainty: 11% to the base case, 33% to the bull, 24% to the bear. HOLD. Own it if you own it; buy it at $13.50 or on two quarters of BSG stabilization; do not short it into a 30% short base and a 13.4% FCF yield.
