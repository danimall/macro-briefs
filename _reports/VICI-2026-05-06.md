## Executive Summary

VICI Properties is a $31B triple-net lease REIT trading at approximately 11.8x forward AFFO and yielding approximately 6.2% — a valuation that implies meaningful discount relative to REIT peers. The stock has declined 15% from its August 2025 highs on two identifiable concerns: (1) Caesars Entertainment's deteriorating regional gaming performance, which threatens the economics of a master lease covering 17 properties, and (2) rising long-term rates pressuring REIT valuations broadly.

The Caesars issue is genuine. Management disclosed "preliminary discussions" in the 10-K about potential lease modifications — language that is unusual for triple-net REIT filings and signals the problem is material. However, the Caesars Regional Lease represents approximately 17 properties within a portfolio of approximately 100 experiential assets (following the Golden Entertainment closing on April 30, 2026), backed by a parent guarantee with 9 years of term remaining. Even a 10-15% rent concession on those specific properties would reduce total AFFO by approximately 3-4%, which is already more than priced into the stock's discount versus peers.

The fundamental business continues to execute: Q1 2026 AFFO grew approximately 5.7% YoY, guidance was raised, and the company closed over $2.2B in new capital commitments across two consecutive quarters. The approximately 6.2% yield is covered at roughly 73-76% of AFFO, providing a meaningful buffer. At $28.85, the stock offers a risk/reward profile skewed to the upside given the median analyst target of $34 and the structural income protection of long-dated, inflation-indexed leases.

## Company Overview

VICI Properties owns approximately 100 experiential real estate assets (following the April 30, 2026 closing of the Golden Entertainment acquisition) across 26 U.S. states and Canada, totaling approximately 127 million square feet, ~60,300 hotel rooms, and over 500 restaurants/bars/venues. The portfolio is anchored by three of the most recognizable Las Vegas Strip properties (Caesars Palace, MGM Grand, The Venetian Resort) and diversified across regional gaming, resorts, wellness centers, bowling centers, and golf courses.

The operating model is maximally capital-light: 28 employees collect rent from tenants who bear all property-level costs (taxes, insurance, maintenance, capex). This produces net income margins near 70% and operating cash flow of $2.51B annually on $4.0B of revenue.

**Revenue composition (FY 2025):**
- Leasing revenue: $3.67B (91.6%)
- Income from real estate debt investments: $218M (5.4%)
- Other: $117M (2.9%)

**Tenant concentration:**
- Caesars Entertainment: 39% of annualized rent
- MGM Resorts: 35% of annualized rent
- Top two tenants combined: 74%

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability and Cash Flow:**

VICI's triple-net structure produces exceptional predictability. FY 2025 delivered $2.53B in AFFO ($2.38/share), up 6.6% from $2.37B in 2024. Operating cash flow was $2.51B (+5.4% YoY). The AFFO payout ratio of 75.6% leaves approximately $650M in annual retained cash flow for reinvestment, reducing equity issuance dependency.

Q1 2026 AFFO of approximately $651M (up ~5.7% YoY) implies an annualized run rate tracking ahead of the midpoint of raised full-year guidance ($2.665-$2.695B, or $2.44-$2.47/share).

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt outstanding is approximately $17.1B against $46.7B in total assets and $27.8B in stockholders' equity. Net leverage is approximately 5.0x EBITDA — within investment-grade REIT norms. All three major agencies rate VICI at BBB-/Baa3 with stable outlooks.

Near-term debt maturities ($1.75B in 2026, $1.5B in 2027) are manageable against $3.1B of total liquidity. However, refinancing costs are rising: the April 2025 offering repriced debt from 4.375-4.625% to 4.750-5.625%, adding approximately $10-15M in annual interest burden. Each subsequent refinancing at current rates will modestly compress AFFO growth by 10-20bps.

**CECL Allowance Increase:**

The credit loss provision rose 40% YoY to $178M in FY 2025 — a signal that management's internal credit assessment of certain tenants has deteriorated. VICI disclosed this was driven by changes in probability of default/loss-given-default for existing tenants/guarantors and macro model changes. While the increase likely relates to Caesars regional operations, VICI has not explicitly attributed it to a specific tenant. This is a non-cash charge that flows through GAAP earnings but not AFFO, yet it represents a leading indicator of potential future lease stress.

## Growth Analysis

AFFO/share growth has decelerated meaningfully: ~22% (2023, acquisition-driven) → 11% (2024) → 5.1% (2025) → ~4.5% guided (2026). The company is transitioning from a transformational acquisition phase to steady-state organic growth driven by:

1. **Contractual rent escalators:** 42% of rent is CPI-linked in 2025, expanding to ~90% over time. Fixed escalators at 1.5-2.0% provide the floor.
2. **Partner Property Growth Fund:** Funding tenant capital improvements (e.g., $700M Venetian investment) in exchange for incremental rent.
3. **Selective acquisitions:** Golden Entertainment ($1.16B, April 2026) adds $87M initial rent with 2% annual escalation. Management targets $1-2B annually in external growth.
4. **Real estate debt investments:** As of December 31, 2025, $2.58B outstanding at 9.1% weighted average yield, with $624M in unfunded commitments. The portfolio grew to approximately $2.71B by March 31, 2026, including a restructured $1.5B One Beverly Hills mezzanine loan.

At a steady state, VICI should grow AFFO/share 3-5% annually (2% escalators + 1-3% from redeploying retained cash flow + modest accretive acquisitions), supporting a total return profile of 9-11% (6.2% yield + 3-5% growth).

**Growth constraints:** The stock's depressed price ($28.85 vs. $34 consensus) makes equity issuance dilutive. Until the stock trades closer to NAV (estimated $33-35 based on 7.0-7.5% implied cap rates on gaming), external growth via equity-funded acquisitions is value-destructive. Management appears aware of this — hence the emphasis on self-funding through retained AFFO.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.85, VICI trades at:
- **~11.8x forward P/AFFO** (based on company guidance midpoint of $2.455/share FY2026 AFFO)
- **~6.2% dividend yield** (well-covered at ~73% forward payout)
- **Meaningful discount to net-lease REIT peers** on a P/AFFO basis

Three factors account for the discount:

1. **Caesars regional lease overhang:** Market prices in some probability of rent concessions.
2. **Rate sensitivity:** At ~5x leverage, long-term Treasury rates near 5% compress net asset values.
3. **Growth deceleration:** 4-5% AFFO growth is below the peer median, justifying some discount.

However, the magnitude of discount appears excessive. Applying a 15x P/AFFO multiple to $2.455 AFFO yields ~$36.83 fair value. A more conservative 13x multiple yields ~$31.92 — close to the analyst median target.

The market is treating the Caesars regional concern as a potential existential threat (pricing in broad rent reduction risk across the portfolio) when the actual exposure is contained to the Caesars Regional properties within a diversified portfolio, protected by a parent guarantee with 9 years remaining. A 10% rent concession on the Caesars Regional Lease specifically would reduce total AFFO by approximately $74M (~2.8% of 2026 AFFO guidance midpoint), which at 12x implies approximately $0.80/share of equity value impact — meaningful but far less than the $5+ discount embedded in the current price vs. consensus targets.

## Competitive Landscape

VICI holds a structural competitive advantage among gaming REITs: it is one of only two scaled players (alongside GLPI), owns irreplaceable Las Vegas Strip properties with regulatory barriers to new supply, and maintains investment-grade ratings that provide a cost-of-capital advantage in deal-making.

The portfolio's 100% occupancy rate since 2017 formation — maintained through COVID — demonstrates the mission-critical nature of these assets. Gaming properties cannot easily relocate, and replacement cost significantly exceeds acquisition cost.

Competitive risks exist primarily at the tenant level: regional casinos face margin pressure from online sports betting, iGaming expansion, and consumer discretionary spending normalization. Las Vegas Strip properties face fewer competitive threats given the destination-resort model's relative insulation from local/online gaming cannibalization.

VICI's expansion into non-gaming experiential assets (Canyon Ranch, Chelsea Piers, Great Wolf, Cabot) is strategically sound — diversifying tenant credit risk and expanding the addressable market — though these properties currently represent <15% of revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Caesars Regional Lease modification (estimated 50-60% probability of some concession within 24 months).** The 10-K's disclosure language is atypical and signals active negotiation. Likely outcome: modest rent reduction on a subset of properties or modified escalation terms, not lease termination. The parent guarantee and 9-year term provide meaningful structural protection.

2. **Interest rate environment (long-term rates near or above 5%).** Every 50bps increase in the 10Y compresses implied NAV by ~4-5% for leveraged REITs. VICI's fixed-rate debt structure (weighted avg 4.46%) provides short-term insulation, but refinancing cost will rise. $1.75B due in 2026 represents the immediate pressure point.

3. **Tenant credit deterioration beyond Caesars Regional.** Caesars carries sub-investment-grade ratings. A broader gaming downturn (recession, consumer spending pullback) would stress the parent guarantee that protects the regional lease. MGM's credit profile, rated B+ by S&P (speculative grade), benefits from stronger Las Vegas Strip cash flows, but the 35% rent concentration remains a key dependency.

4. **Real estate debt portfolio (growing, higher-risk).** The loan book includes development/mezzanine positions (One Beverly Hills, restructured to a $1.5B mezzanine loan in March 2026; North Fork Casino $510M) that carry fundamentally different risk profiles than triple-net leases. A severe downturn could produce impairments here.

5. **AFFO growth deceleration limits total return if yield compression doesn't occur.** If the stock remains at ~12x AFFO indefinitely, total returns are capped at ~9-11% (6.2% yield + 3-5% growth). The stock needs multiple expansion to outperform.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 24.9% sits 8.6pp above historical realized vol of 16.3%, with the term structure in backwardation (24.9% near-term vs. 18.2% at 6 months). This pricing structure suggests the market expects a near-term catalyst that could move the stock ±4.8% — likely related to Caesars earnings or rate movements. The put/call OI ratio at 0.35 and volume ratio at 0.22 suggest limited bearish positioning. Combined with 2.9% short interest, the options market data does not confirm the fundamental bear case at current prices, though inferring institutional hedging activity from aggregate put/call ratios has inherent limitations.

The IV compression from 29.8% to 24.9% over the past month (alongside a +1.9% price move) indicates uncertainty is declining post-Q1 earnings beat and guidance raise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($34-37, +18-28%):** The Caesars regional overhang resolves with modest concessions (5-10% rent reduction on a subset of properties), removing the primary stock overhang. Rate normalization (30Y declining toward 4.5%) triggers REIT sector re-rating. VICI re-prices to 14-15x forward AFFO. Golden Entertainment acquisition demonstrates external growth capability at accretive economics. Total return over 18 months: 25-35% (yield + appreciation).

**Bear Case ($24-25, -13-17%):** Caesars credit deteriorates further; lease restructuring involves 15-20% rent reduction across the full regional portfolio. Rates remain above 5%; no REIT sector re-rating occurs. Real estate debt portfolio produces a $100-200M impairment. AFFO/share growth stalls at 2-3%. Stock compresses to ~10x forward AFFO with no catalyst for multiple expansion.

**Base Case ($31-33, +7-14%):** Caesars regional situation stabilizes with minor adjustments; market gradually prices out worst-case scenario. AFFO grows 4-5% annually. Dividend grows 3-4% annually. Stock re-rates to 13x forward AFFO over 12 months as the overhang diminishes. Total return: 13-20% (yield + modest appreciation).

**Base case target:** $32.00 — approximately 13x forward P/AFFO on $2.455 FY2026E AFFO (guidance midpoint). This reflects partial multiple expansion from the current ~11.8x toward sector-appropriate levels as the Caesars overhang resolves.

**Bull case target:** $36.00 — approximately 14.7x forward P/AFFO, achievable if rates decline and Caesars situation resolves cleanly.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key resolution points: (1) Caesars Q2/Q3 2026 earnings demonstrating regional stabilization, (2) VICI management commentary on lease discussions at next investor day, (3) 30Y Treasury trajectory through upcoming FOMC meetings and beyond.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Caesars announces formal lease restructuring with >15% aggregate rent reduction (would reduce AFFO/share by >$0.10, compressing growth to <2%)

3. AFFO/share guidance cut below $2.35 for FY2026 (would signal growth trajectory broken)

## Conclusion

VICI Properties at $28.85 presents a compelling income-plus-growth opportunity where identifiable risks (Caesars regional lease, rate sensitivity) are more than reflected in the current valuation discount to REIT peers. The approximately 6.2% yield is well-covered, the balance sheet is investment-grade, and Q1 2026 results demonstrated continued operational execution.

The specific risk preventing a more aggressive stance is the Caesars situation, which management has acknowledged is under active discussion. However, the structural protections (parent guarantee, 9-year term, diversification across approximately 100 total experiential properties including many non-Caesars Regional assets) limit the downside, while the combination of income, organic growth (3-5% AFFO/share), and potential multiple re-rating provides a favorable total return profile. With 17 out of 24 covering analysts at Buy/Strong Buy and a median target of $34 (18% above current), the consensus view aligns with the fundamental case for appreciation.

The primary constraint on conviction is the rate environment: at long-term rates near 5%, REIT valuations face structural headwinds that may prevent multiple expansion even as company-specific concerns fade. Investors should size accordingly — this is an income compounder with upside optionality, appropriate for 2-3% portfolio weighting.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The corrected FY2026 AFFO/share ($2.44–$2.47 per company guidance vs. the originally cited $2.85) reduces the forward P/AFFO discount, narrows the upside to base-case target, and lowers the reward-to-risk ratio. Additionally, MGM is rated B+ (speculative grade) by S&P, not investment-grade, which modestly increases assessed tenant credit risk.
