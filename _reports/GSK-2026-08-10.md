## Executive Summary

GSK at $51.935 is a mid-single-digit revenue grower with a cash-generative, deleveraging balance sheet, trading at approximately **10.9x FY2026 and 10.1x FY2027 consensus core EPS per ADS** ($4.75 and $5.14 respectively), with a **3.40% dividend yield** that is covered roughly 2.7x on core earnings. The operational record over the last four quarters is good: four consecutive beats (+12.8%, +8.7%, +3.8%, +6.7%), Q2 2026 core EPS of $1.36 per ADS against a $1.27 non-GAAP consensus (+7.1%), core turnover and EPS delivered at the **upper end** of guidance, and core EPS growth of +9% year-over-year on both reported and constant-currency bases. Operating cash flow inflected to **£7,741m in FY2025, +18.1% year-over-year** and the highest since 2021, after three consecutive years of decline.

The bear case rests on terminal value and the durability of the earnings base rather than on operational deterioration. GSK's new CEO, Luke Miels, whose first reported period as announcing executive was FY2025 results in February 2026, committed within roughly four months to a **$10.6bn all-cash acquisition of Nuvalent at $124 per share (~40% premium)** for lung-cancer assets that were pre-approval at signing, and within roughly five months launched a **£1.9bn three-year cost programme whose savings are explicitly earmarked to fund late-stage pipeline and R&D**, not to expand margin. External reporting (Reuters, 11 June 2026) frames the deal as accelerating GSK's oncology "rebuild" to "fend off competition," and multiple outlets tie it to anticipated exclusivity losses. Both the size of the HIV cliff and GSK's new medium-term targets from the 28 July "Accelerate Growth" event are unquantified in the evidence available. Shares fell on the deal announcement.

The arithmetic supports **HOLD**. A base case of **$58** (11.3x FY2027 core, essentially the $58.08 analyst mean) implies +11.7% price appreciation plus 3.4% yield. The bear case of **$44** (-15.3%) reflects a further de-rating toward 8.5-9x forward core if the cliff quantifies worse than expected or the enlarged intangible base impairs. Probability-weighted, that is a high-single-digit total return — positive, but without the margin of safety or catalyst clarity that would justify initiating a full position. The reliable component of the return is the yield and £7.7bn of annual operating cash generation; the growth component is real but currently unverifiable.

## Company Overview

GSK plc is a UK-domiciled, NYSE- and LSE-listed biopharmaceutical company (SIC 2834), renamed from GlaxoSmithKline plc on 16 May 2022 in conjunction with the Haleon consumer-healthcare demerger. That demerger materially breaks the historical revenue series: the FY2021 20-F reported £34,114m of revenue, while the FY2022 20-F restates the FY2021 comparative to £24,696m. Pre-2022 headline figures are not comparable to today's company, and any long-run growth calculation that spans the demerger is invalid.

The continuing business is organised around **Specialty Medicines** — Respiratory, Immunology & Inflammation; Oncology; and HIV (conducted principally through majority-owned **ViiV Healthcare**, in which Pfizer and Shionogi hold minority equity stakes) — and **Vaccines** (Shingrix, Arexvy, meningitis). Late-stage assets of consequence include **bepirovirsen** in chronic hepatitis B, with Phase III B-Well 1 and B-Well 2 data presented at EASL on 28 May 2026, and the acquired Nuvalent oncology programmes: the ROS1 inhibitor **Jideytro**, approved as of 28 July 2026, and the ALK inhibitor **neladalkib**, guided for approval within 2026.

One structural feature matters for interpreting the accounts: GSK consolidates ViiV and books the Pfizer and Shionogi interests as non-controlling interests. Consolidated equity and consolidated profit therefore overstate what is attributable to GSK's own shareholders, by an amount not disclosed in the data available. Consolidated-basis ROE and net margin computed from those figures are not usable as stated.

## Financial Analysis

All IFRS figures below are as tagged in GSK's filed 20-Fs (SEC XBRL, GBP millions).

**Revenue — consistent, unremarkable, and FX-blended:**

| FY | Revenue (£m) | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 (restated) | 24,696 | — |
| 2022 | 29,324 | +18.7% |
| 2023 | 30,328 | +3.4% |
| 2024 | 31,376 | +3.5% |
| 2025 | 32,667 | +4.1% |

FY2022→FY2025 CAGR is 3.7%. These are *reported* sterling figures that blend commercial performance with translation. The constant-currency decomposition is not available, so 4.1% should not be quoted as an organic rate.

**Reported profit is close to uninformative on its own:**

| FY | Profit (£m) | Consolidated net margin |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 15,621 | 53.3% |
| 2023 | 5,308 | 17.5% |
| 2024 | 2,951 | 9.4% |
| 2025 | 6,289 | 19.3% |

FY2022 is inflated by demerger accounting and is not operating earnings. FY2024's £2,951m is a trough in which profit fell 44% while revenue *grew* 3.5%, a shape that points to large below-the-line charges rather than commercial deterioration. The composition of that charge is unverified in the available evidence; litigation provisioning is the most cited hypothesis, but intangible impairment, deferred tax, or pension remeasurement are all plausible and I do not assert one. The consequence is that FY2025's +113% "recovery" is largely a base effect. The defensible framing is **FY2025 profit +18.5% versus FY2023 on +7.7% revenue** — genuine but modest operating leverage.

**Operating cash flow is the series to anchor on, and it inflected:**

| FY | Operating CF (£m) | OCF margin | OCF/Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 7,952 | 32.2% | 1.56x |
| 2022 | 7,403 | 25.2% | 0.47x |
| 2023 | 6,768 | 22.3% | 1.28x |
| 2024 | 6,554 | 20.9% | 2.22x |
| 2025 | 7,741 | 23.7% | 1.23x |

Cash generation declined 17.6% cumulatively from 2021 to 2024, then rose **18.1% in FY2025** to a four-year high. FY2024's 2.22x OCF/profit ratio independently corroborates the interpretation that the profit trough was charge- or provision-driven while cash generation held; FY2025's 1.23x is a normal range for a pharma carrying heavy amortisation and indicates the profit figure is cash-backed. The caveat is that part of the FY2025 gain could be working-capital timing or the absence of a cash litigation outflow that fell in another year. Without the cash-flow bridge, £7,741m should not be extrapolated as a clean run rate.

**Balance sheet — thin equity, thickening quickly:**

| Period end | Equity (£m) | Total liabilities (£m) | Total assets (£m) | Equity ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 10,096 | 50,050 | 60,146 | 16.8% |
| 2023 | 12,795 | 46,210 | 59,005 | 21.7% |
| 2024 | 13,086 | 46,377 | 59,463 | 22.0% |
| 2025 | 15,956 | 45,162 | 61,118 | 26.1% |

Equity is up 58% from FY2022 while total liabilities fell from £50,050m to £45,162m. Return on total assets in FY2025 was 10.3% (6,289/61,118); asset turnover 0.53x. The deleveraging is genuine on both sides of the balance sheet, and it gives GSK more capacity to absorb the Nuvalent cash outlay than it had two years ago.

Two disciplines are required here. First, **45,162/15,956 = 2.83x is total liabilities to equity, not debt to equity** — it includes payables, provisions, pension obligations and deferred tax. GSK's financial-debt-to-equity ratio is materially lower and is not available in this evidence set. Second, **total borrowings and net debt are not retrievable here**, which is the single most important gap: a $10.6bn all-cash acquisition is funded from cash and/or new debt, so leverage after the transaction is unknown. No enterprise-value, EV/EBITDA, net-debt/EBITDA or interest-coverage conclusion appears in this report, and none should be drawn from it. Capital expenditure is likewise unavailable, so no free cash flow figure is presented.

Note also that £15,956m of equity supports £61,118m of assets and £32,667m of revenue. A multi-billion litigation charge or intangible impairment consumes a double-digit percentage of book value, and the Nuvalent transaction materially enlarges the impairable intangible balance.

**Earnings-basis conflict in the market-data feed.** The quote feed reports trailing and forward P/E both at 33.88x alongside EPS of $1.87105 — those two fields do not reconcile with each other ($51.935/$1.87105 = 27.7x), and neither reconciles with the consensus core estimates. The trailing EPS series in the surprise table ($0.55, $0.26, $0.47, $0.51 = $1.79) is on a different basis from the consensus estimate series (current quarter $1.34; FY2026 $4.75; FY2027 $5.14), which is the per-ADS core basis Zacks used for the $1.36-versus-$1.27 Q2 comparison. **I use consensus core EPS per ADS as the valuation anchor** and treat the 33.88x figure as an artefact of GSK's IFRS "total" earnings being dominated by episodic non-operating items. Financial health scores at 6.2: strong and inflecting cash generation and a clear deleveraging trend, offset by an unverified debt position into a large cash outlay, a thin equity base relative to asset scale, and no verifiable free cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is durable low-single-digit revenue growth converting into high-single-digit core EPS growth, with the medium-term profile unresolved.

**What is confirmed.** Reported revenue growth of 3.4%, 3.5% and 4.1% in the last three years. Q2 2026 core EPS +9% year-over-year on both reported and CER bases, at the upper end of guidance, with a 7.1% beat versus non-GAAP consensus. Four consecutive quarterly beats. One acquired oncology asset (Jideytro, ROS1) approved within roughly seven weeks of deal announcement, with neladalkib guided for approval in 2026 — a meaningful retirement of the regulatory risk GSK paid a control premium for.

**What is unresolved and material.** The **HIV exclusivity cliff** is corroborated in its existence by both external reporting and GSK's own capital allocation, but the affected molecules, expiry dates and revenue at risk are not quantified in the available evidence. That single input drives terminal value. Similarly, the medium-term financial targets set at the 28 July "Accelerate Growth" investor event — held alongside quarterly results in a new CEO's second reported quarter, which is how a company signals a formal re-articulation of medium-term ambition — are not obtained. Holding a capital-markets-day-style event and then not having its content available leaves the central growth question open.

**The cost programme does not add to earnings growth, by design.** GSK announced a three-year programme to reduce annual expenditure by £1.9bn ($2.5bn), explicitly "to fund investment in its late-stage pipeline and R&D initiatives." A separately reported £2.4bn figure from another outlet has an unconfirmed basis — plausibly a cumulative saving or a restructuring charge — and the two figures should not be summed, nor should £2.4bn be presented as annual savings. If savings are recycled into R&D, modelled operating margin expansion from this programme should be near zero; the return arrives, if at all, as pipeline optionality several years out.

**Optionality that is genuinely unpriced.** Bepirovirsen in chronic hepatitis B is the largest single upside item, and I do not know the B-Well 1 / B-Well 2 outcomes. GSK convened a same-day investor call with an external hepatology KOL, which is weakly consistent with a favourable narrative, but that is an inference from meeting format rather than from data and it is not actionable.

**Consensus momentum is mildly negative.** FY2026 estimates are down 0.8% over 30 days and 2.0% over 90 days; FY2027 is +0.2% over 30 days and -2.0% over 90 days; the current quarter is down 5.0% and 7.3%. The magnitude is small, the analyst count is 2-3, and some of the drift could be sterling translation rather than operational. This is not the estimate-revision profile that supports paying up, but it is also not deterioration of a kind that argues for exiting. Growth prospects score 5.5.

## Valuation Assessment

On the correct basis — consensus core EPS per ADS — GSK trades at:

- **10.9x FY2026** ($51.935 / $4.75)
- **10.1x FY2027** ($51.935 / $5.14)
- **3.40% dividend yield**, implying roughly $1.77 per ADS annually, a **~37% payout on FY2026 core EPS**

Implied FY2026→FY2027 core EPS growth is 8.2% ($5.14/$4.75), which puts the forward PEG at approximately **1.2x** on the FY2027 multiple. That is not expensive. Large-cap pharma forward core multiples in this market span a very wide range — from high-single-digit for names carrying visible patent cliffs to the high-20s for franchise compounders with volume-driven growth — and GSK sits at the low end of that band.

The question the valuation score has to answer is whether the discount is a mispricing or a correct assessment of terminal value. The evidence points more toward the latter than the former:

1. The discount is **persistent, not new**. GSK has traded in a high-single to low-double-digit forward core multiple for years. A cheap multiple that has been cheap through multiple operating cycles is usually pricing a structural question, and here the question is identifiable: exclusivity loss in HIV, plus a vaccines franchise where I cannot distinguish moat-driven durability from cohort-driven revenue that decays mechanically as the eligible 50+ population is vaccinated.
2. The **catalyst set is soft**. Neladalkib approval is guided but incremental. Bepirovirsen is genuinely large and genuinely unknown. The Accelerate Growth targets may be the re-rating catalyst, but their content is unavailable, so I cannot underwrite it.
3. External sell-side and independent commentary has converged on the same view: a 28 July downgrade argued that "forward P/E and dividend yield don't offer adequate support." That is a tier-3 contributor view without a captured target, but it is directionally consistent with the analyst distribution — 1 strong buy, 1 buy, 5 hold, 1 sell.
4. Analyst targets cluster close to the base case: **mean $58.08, median $56.00, low $47.00, high $70.00**. On a stock at 10x forward with mildly *negative* revisions, consensus targets are not stale floors; they reflect the same terminal-value uncertainty the multiple does.

**Scenario framework (all versus $51.935):**

- **Base $58.00 (+11.7%)** — 11.3x FY2027 core $5.14, effectively the analyst mean. Assumes mid-single-digit revenue growth continues, core EPS lands within guidance, neladalkib approves, and the cliff neither improves nor worsens versus current expectations. Total return with dividend ≈ 15%.
- **Bull $68.00 (+30.9%)** — 13.2x FY2027 core. Requires two or three of: bepirovirsen delivering a franchise-scale HBV opportunity, Accelerate Growth targets credibly bridging the cliff, oncology uptake exceeding expectations for Jideytro/neladalkib, and resolution of US pricing/tariff exposure on favourable terms. Sits just below the $70 analyst high.
- **Bear $44.00 (-15.3%)** — 8.6x FY2027 core, below the $47 analyst low. Triggered by cliff quantification worse than assumed, a multi-billion impairment against the enlarged intangible base, adverse US pricing action, or a renewed litigation provision cycle.

Probability-weighted at 45% base / 20% bull / 35% bear gives roughly **+6% price return and +9-10% total return** over twelve months. Positive, thin on margin of safety. Valuation scores 6.2 — cheap on forward core with a covered yield, but the discount has an identifiable structural cause and no dated catalyst to close it.

## Competitive Landscape

GSK's competitive trajectory is the weakest element of the case, and the score reflects trajectory rather than current standing.

**Oncology is being rebuilt from behind.** Reuters' framing — "rebuild of its cancer drug business" — implies the franchise was previously diminished. GSK is therefore re-entering biomarker-selected lung cancer as a relative latecomer against entrenched incumbents. The Nuvalent assets are attractive precisely because ROS1/ALK-driven NSCLC is a genomically defined niche requiring modest commercial infrastructure; the same logic makes those assets attackable by the next generation of entrants. Regulatory approval (Jideytro, secured) is necessary but not sufficient; commercial uptake against established competitors is unproven.

**HIV is where the scale is and where the cliff bites.** GSK, via ViiV, is one of very few scaled players. The strategic pivot toward long-acting injectables is the right defence, but it requires converting an existing patient base onto a new administration paradigm — a commercial-execution problem layered on a scientific one.

**Vaccines is the strongest position with the most ambiguous durability profile.** Shingrix has few credible global competitors; Arexvy faces at least one large peer in adult RSV. Whether revenue durability comes from a manufacturing/regulatory moat or from a large unvaccinated 50+ cohort is not resolvable from the available evidence, and the two have very different decay characteristics. This is the most consequential unresolved analytical fork in the name.

**Hepatitis B is the cleanest potential moat and the least verified.** A functional-cure therapy would be a new franchise with limited direct competition.

There is no product-level or customer concentration data available. A shifting therapeutic mix does not evidence reduced concentration — replacing one large franchise with another leaves concentration unchanged — so I make no diversification claim. Competitive position scores 5.3: strong absolute positions in vaccines and HIV, with the trajectory in two of four core areas requiring successful defence or successful catch-up rather than extension of an existing advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**1. HIV exclusivity loss (highest-impact, least quantified).** Corroborated in existence by external reporting and by GSK's own capital allocation; the molecules, dates and revenue at risk are unverified. This is the item that determines whether 10x forward is cheap or fair.

**2. Litigation and provision volatility against a thin equity base.** FY2024 demonstrated that GSK's accounting earnings can be halved by below-the-line items while revenue grows. With £15,956m of book equity, a repeat multi-billion charge is a double-digit percentage hit to book value.

**3. Impairment risk on an enlarged intangible base.** A $10.6bn all-cash purchase at a ~40% premium for assets that were pre-approval at signing creates a large impairable balance. One asset is now approved, which reduces but does not eliminate the risk — commercial underperformance impairs just as regulatory failure does.

**4. US pricing and tariff policy.** Whether GSK has entered a most-favoured-nation pricing arrangement, direct-to-consumer platform, or tariff accommodation with the US administration is **unconfirmed in both directions**. Several large peers reportedly did in this period. GSK's expansion of its Montgomery County, Pennsylvania footprint via Nuvalent is consistent with sector onshoring behaviour but does not establish motive. This is a live two-sided catalyst.

**5. Cost-programme execution with no margin benefit.** Savings recycled into R&D mean the programme carries restructuring charges and execution risk while delivering no modelled margin expansion. The programme's headline size is inconsistently reported across secondary sources (£1.9bn annual versus £2.4bn of unconfirmed basis) and unreconciled against GSK's primary announcement.

**6. Structural disclosure gaps.** GSK is a foreign private issuer: no 10-K, no 10-Q, no 8-K, no DEF 14A, and no Section 16 Form 4 insider filings (SEC data confirms `insiderTransactionForIssuerExists: 0` for CIK 0001131399). Altman Z and Piotroski F cannot be computed, and their absence is structural rather than evidence of weakness. The absence of insider selling here is not a positive signal — the data does not exist. Net debt, capex, free cash flow, and the FY2025 20-F risk-factor deltas are all unavailable.

**7. FX translation.** ADS-level earnings and estimates carry sterling translation risk; part of the modest negative revision drift may be currency rather than operations.

Risk factors score 5.5. Much of the cliff is arguably in the multiple already, and short positioning is negligible, but the unquantified terminal-value question, the litigation/impairment tail against thin equity, and unresolved US pricing policy are material and only partly priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($68, +30.9%)

GSK is a 10x-forward-core, 3.4%-yielding pharma with four consecutive beats, cash flow at a four-year high, and a balance sheet that has deleveraged by nearly £5bn of liabilities while adding 58% to book equity in three years. The market is pricing the cliff and ignoring the bridge. The bridge components are already in motion: Jideytro approved within seven weeks of a deal that was criticised for buying regulatory risk, neladalkib guided for 2026, bepirovirsen carrying franchise-scale HBV optionality that is entirely unpriced, and a £1.9bn cost programme funding the late-stage pipeline. Q1 2026 showed the market was priced for guidance *raises* rather than beats (shares -8.8% in a month on a beat with maintained guidance); Q2 then delivered at the upper end, validating management's conservatism. If the Accelerate Growth targets prove credible and one of bepirovirsen or oncology uptake surprises, an 11-13x forward core multiple is achievable and the stock re-rates toward the $70 analyst high, with the 3.4% yield paid throughout.

### Bear Case ($44, -15.3%)

The 10x multiple is correct. HIV exclusivity loss is larger than the market's working assumption and GSK's replacement portfolio is a latecomer entry into biomarker-selected oncology purchased at a 40% control premium, plus a hepatitis B asset whose Phase III outcome is unknown to outside investors. The cost programme delivers no margin because the savings are spent, and it carries restructuring charges instead. A £15,956m equity base against a materially enlarged intangible balance means one impairment cycle or one litigation provision resets book value by double digits, as FY2024 demonstrated. Consensus estimates are drifting down, not up. US pricing policy is unresolved and GSK's position is unconfirmed while peers reportedly secured arrangements. In that scenario forward core compresses to 8.5-9x, the stock trades through the $47 analyst low, and the dividend is the only return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income_hold).** The defensible support at $51.935 is a 3.40% dividend covered ~2.7x on FY2026 core EPS, backed by £7,741m of annual operating cash generation and an improving equity ratio. Existing holders are compensated for waiting; new capital has no urgent entry signal at roughly +11.7% to base case against -15.3% to bear.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Quantified cliff disclosure.** Publication of the Accelerate Growth medium-term targets with the exclusivity-loss revenue at risk and the specific replacement contribution by year — enough to test whether 10x forward is cheap or fair. Absent this, the terminal-value question cannot be closed.
2. **Positive consensus revision inflection.** FY2027 consensus rising above $5.24 (its 90-day-ago level) on a constant-currency-explained basis, ideally combined with a guidance raise rather than a maintained range.
3. **Bepirovirsen confirmed as a launchable franchise** — endpoints met at commercially meaningful magnitude with a regulatory filing timeline.
4. **Price weakness to $45-47** on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the stock at 8.8-9.1x FY2027 core with a ~3.8% yield and a materially better bear-case buffer.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Post-Nuvalent leverage disclosure materially worse than expected** — net debt/EBITDA rising to a level that constrains the dividend or the R&D reinvestment programme. This is currently unmeasurable and is the largest single evidence gap.
2. **A new multi-billion charge cycle** — litigation provision or intangible impairment consuming more than ~10% of book equity, which would confirm the FY2024 pattern as recurring rather than episodic.
3. **FY2027 consensus cut more than 8% from the current $5.14**, or a guidance cut, which would break the "beat-and-deliver-at-upper-end" record that underpins the base case.
4. **Adverse US pricing outcome** — an MFN or tariff regime applied to GSK on terms worse than peers who reportedly negotiated arrangements.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026) is the next scheduled decision point, with the neladalkib approval decision expected inside 2026 as an interim checkpoint. Any disclosure of post-transaction net debt or the Accelerate Growth target detail would prompt an off-cycle reassessment.

## Conclusion

GSK's operating performance is better than its multiple suggests and its accounting earnings are worse than its cash flow suggests. The reconciliation is that reported IFRS profit has been dominated by episodic non-operating items in three of the last four years, while operating cash flow moved in a tight band and inflected to £7,741m in FY2025. On the correct basis — consensus core EPS per ADS of $4.75 and $5.14 — the shares trade at 10.9x and 10.1x with a 3.4% dividend covered nearly three times, four consecutive beats behind them, and a balance sheet that has deleveraged materially.

What prevents a stronger rating is that the cheapness has an identifiable and unquantified cause. The HIV exclusivity cliff is corroborated in existence and unmeasured in size; GSK's answer to it is a $10.6bn all-cash entry into biomarker-selected lung cancer as an acknowledged latecomer, plus a £1.9bn cost programme whose savings are spent on R&D rather than margin, plus a hepatitis B asset whose Phase III outcome is not publicly resolvable from the evidence available. Consensus estimates are drifting down modestly, the analyst distribution is five holds against two positives and one sell, and the analyst mean of $58.08 sits close to my base case rather than above it. Net debt after the acquisition is unknown, which precludes any enterprise-value conclusion.

A base case of $58 with a $44 bear does not clear the bar for initiating a full position, and it does not warrant exiting either. **HOLD**, on the yield and the cash generation, with the Accelerate Growth target detail and the post-transaction leverage disclosure as the two items that would move this in either direction.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Q2 2026 non-GAAP beat is +7.1% ($1.36 vs $1.27), but the Phase 1 GAAP earnings-surprise feed shows the most recent quarter's surprise at +6.7% ($0.51 vs $0.47), not the reiterated four-quarter beat magnitudes used loosely in prose; and the base case leans on the analyst mean ($58.08), which sits only marginally above spot — the thin +6% probability-weighted price return should be re-examined against the four-quarter beat sequence, which per the feed is (+12.8%, +8.7%, +3.8%, +6.7%) and matches the report.
