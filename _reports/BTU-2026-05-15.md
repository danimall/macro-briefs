## Executive Summary

Peabody Energy, the world's largest private coal producer, is experiencing a severe earnings downcycle with 2025 operating income swinging to a loss of $80M from $445M the prior year. The company missed Q1 2026 consensus estimates badly (-$0.27 vs. +$0.11 expected), and forward estimates are collapsing: current-year EPS has been revised down 59% in 90 days, from $2.70 to $1.12. The Centurion metallurgical coal mine, central to the recovery thesis, has suffered repeated production shortfalls, with Q1 2026 output slashed from 700K tons to 250K tons.

The balance sheet remains solvent ($575M cash, $336M debt, $942M liquidity), but the surety bonding agreement expires December 31, 2026, and the 10-K explicitly states failure to renew "could result in its inability to continue as a going concern." Anglo American's ICC arbitration represents an unquantified but potentially material liability (deal size was $3.5B+). Multiple officer departures in 8 months add governance uncertainty.

At $24.26, the stock trades at a negative trailing P/E and forward P/E of roughly 22x FY2026 consensus ($1.12). While seaborne coal prices have recovered modestly from 2025 lows, the combination of execution failures, estimate deterioration, structural secular headwinds, and critical near-term risks (surety renewal, arbitration, Centurion ramp) creates an unfavorable risk/reward skew. Analyst targets of $30-$40 assume successful Centurion execution and coal price recovery — assumptions that recent data directly contradicts.

## Company Overview

Peabody Energy operates through four segments: Seaborne Thermal (Australian export thermal coal), Seaborne Metallurgical (Australian and Alabama coking coal for steel), Powder River Basin (Wyoming sub-bituminous for U.S. utilities), and Other U.S. Thermal (Illinois/Indiana/New Mexico/Colorado). The company sold 122 million tons in 2025 and controls approximately 2.0 billion tons of proven and probable reserves across ~335,000 acres.

The strategic narrative centers on the Centurion Mine — an underground longwall metallurgical coal operation in Queensland that achieved full-scale production in February 2026 but has underdelivered consistently. The company also holds a 50% interest in the Middlemount Mine and is exploring rare earth element extraction from Powder River Basin coal (early-stage pilot funded by Wyoming Energy Authority).

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars, all facing material challenges:

**1. Centurion Mine Ramp-Up:** Originally expected to produce ~700K tons in Q1 2026, actual output was ~250K tons. Full-year 2026 guidance was cut to 2.5M tons from earlier expectations of 3.5M. Longwall automation optimization is targeted for end of May 2026. The first longwall move has been pushed to early 2027. Repeated operational failures erode confidence in execution timelines.

**2. Coal Price Recovery:** Premium HCC was $242.50/tonne as of the February 2026 10-K filing (up from $218 at year-end). Newcastle thermal at $115/tonne. If sustained, these prices should improve 2026 results from 2025's trough. However, Chinese renewable buildout and weakening steel demand create price ceiling risk.

**3. Rare Earth Element Optionality:** Pre-commercial pilot stage. Should not be valued as a primary driver.

**Estimate Trajectory (Critical):** Current-year EPS consensus has collapsed from $2.70 to $1.12 in 90 days (-59%). Current-quarter estimates went from +$0.79 to -$0.25 in 30 days (-132%). Next-year estimates of $3.65 assume successful Centurion ramp and price recovery — both unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.26 with FY2026 consensus of $1.12 EPS, the stock trades at approximately 22x forward earnings — expensive for a coal company with a track record of missing estimates. On next-year consensus ($3.65), the multiple compresses to ~6.6x, but this requires trusting estimates that have consistently proven too optimistic.

Peers trade at median 7.0x P/E (PARR), though many coal/energy names carry negative earnings. On an EV/EBITDA basis, if 2026 EBITDA recovers to $600-700M (which requires Centurion execution and stable pricing), EV/EBITDA would be approximately 5-6x — reasonable for coal but not cheap given structural headwinds.

The critical valuation question: is 2025 a cyclical trough or the beginning of structural decline? If trough, buying at 6-7x normalized earnings is attractive. If decline, any positive P/E is too expensive for a terminally shrinking industry. The analyst consensus target range of $30-$40 embeds the trough assumption.

At $24.26, the stock has already declined 41% from its $41.14 high. This magnitude of decline with simultaneous 59% estimate cuts suggests the market is correctly repricing the asset, not irrationally overreacting. The burden of proof lies with the bull case to explain why estimates will stop falling.

## Competitive Landscape

Peabody remains the largest private coal producer globally with 2 billion tons of reserves and geographic diversification across U.S. and Australian operations. This scale provides:
- Exposure to both Atlantic and Pacific seaborne markets
- Cost diversification across underground and surface operations
- Optionality across thermal and metallurgical coal

However, the competitive position is eroding on multiple fronts:
- ESG restrictions are limiting banking and insurance access for coal producers, increasing cost of capital
- U.S. thermal coal's addressable market shrinks annually as utilities retire coal plants (~16% of generation, declining)
- Centurion execution failures mean the metallurgical coal volume growth that would differentiate Peabody from peers is delayed
- The terminated Anglo acquisition removes what would have been a transformative scale addition to the met coal portfolio

India's expanding steel industry and rising electricity demand in developing Asia provide a structural floor for seaborne demand, but Chinese domestic production and renewable capacity buildout limit upside.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Near-Term Risks:**

1. **Surety Bond Renewal (December 31, 2026):** Current liquidity ($942M) provides a buffer above the $487M minimum covenant, but continued operating losses would erode this margin. This is a hard deadline with going-concern implications per the 10-K.

2. **Anglo Arbitration (Unquantified):** Anglo American is pursuing ICC arbitration for damages from the terminated $3.5B+ acquisition. Potential exposure could be hundreds of millions of dollars. No reserve has been disclosed. Timeline is uncertain.

3. **Centurion Execution Risk:** Two production guidance cuts in 2026 already. If longwall optimization (target: end of May 2026) fails or is delayed, further downgrades to full-year production guidance are probable.

4. **Estimate Deterioration Momentum:** Three misses in four quarters with no visibility into stabilization. Consensus may still be too high for FY2026.

5. **Secular Decline:** Coal's share of U.S. generation continues falling. Customer concentration (25% from top 5) with contracts expiring 2025-2028 creates volume risk as utilities decommission plants.

6. **Officer Turnover:** Four 8-K officer departure filings in 8 months (Aug 2025 - May 2026). Unusual frequency suggests internal instability.

### Options Market Signal

The options market confirms stress positioning:
- Near-term ATM IV of 102.7% vs. 57.7% historical volatility — priced at nearly double realized vol
- Term structure in steep **backwardation** (102.7% near-term vs. 55.5% at 6 months), indicating the market expects imminent resolution of uncertainty (likely Centurion update or further earnings deterioration)
- Put/call OI ratio of 1.24 with elevated put demand (291% skew in nearest expiry)
- IV increased 8.9pp over two weeks alongside a 9.1% price decline — rising vol into falling prices is a classic stress signature

The options market is pricing material event risk in the near term, consistent with Centurion execution updates expected around end of May 2026.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Centurion longwall optimization was targeted for "end of May 2026." If this milestone is missed or production guidance is cut again, a further leg down is likely. Any negative Anglo arbitration development or surety bond renewal difficulties would also be catalytic. Next quarterly earnings (likely early August) will reveal whether FY2026 estimates need further cuts.

**Timing window:** May-August 2026. The Centurion optimization update should surface within weeks. Q2 results by early August. Surety negotiations by Q4.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 15.1% short interest with 4.1 days to cover. Elevated but below the 20%+ threshold for acute squeeze risk. However, low institutional ownership relative to mega-caps means a Centurion success announcement could produce a violent short squeeze of 20-30%. This is meaningful risk.

**Borrow and options signal:** Steep backwardation, put-heavy OI (1.24x), and IV at 102.7% vs 57.7% HV confirm bearish positioning. However, the high IV makes put protection expensive (paying nearly 2x realized vol). Short selling via equity is more capital-efficient but carries unlimited upside risk.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target $15-18 (-38% to -26%) vs. bull case $30-35 (+24% to +44%). The risk/reward is approximately 1:1 from current levels, which is insufficient for an active short given 15% SI and event risk from Centurion.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The thesis is correct (deteriorating fundamentals, execution failures, critical risks), but the stock has already declined 41% from highs, making the remaining downside limited relative to squeeze risk and event risk from potential Centurion success. At $24, much of the negative scenario is priced. Avoid owning rather than actively shorting.

## Conclusion

Peabody Energy presents an unfavorable risk/reward profile at $24.26. The company faces severe estimate deterioration, repeated execution failures at its most important growth project, an approaching surety bond renewal with going-concern implications, and an unquantified arbitration liability. The 15.1% short interest, extreme options backwardation, and 3-of-4 quarterly misses confirm broad institutional bearish positioning.

The bull case requires simultaneous success on Centurion execution, coal price recovery, and risk resolution — an intersection of favorable outcomes that recent data directly contradicts. While analyst targets of $30-$40 suggest upside, these targets assume a recovery that has not materialized. The score trajectory (4.5 → 4.3, declining) reflects continued deterioration.

Recommendation: **AVOID.** Wait for concrete evidence of operational stabilization before reconsidering.
