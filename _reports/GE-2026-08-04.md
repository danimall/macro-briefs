## Executive Summary

GE Aerospace is executing at the top end of what an aftermarket-led propulsion franchise can produce. H1 2026 adjusted revenue grew 26.6% to $24,248M, services revenue grew 27.3%, LEAP deliveries grew 41% to 1,030 units, free cash flow grew 31.2% to $4,685M, and management raised every 2026 guidance line on July 16 — adjusted EPS from $7.10–7.40 to $7.65–7.85, free cash flow to $8.9–9.2B (a $650M increase to the prior high end), total revenue growth from low double digits to high teens. Remaining performance obligations reached $210.8B, up $20.2B in six months, of which $178.7B is services. Moody's upgraded the long-term rating from A3 to A2 in February and S&P moved its A– outlook to positive in April. Consensus has followed: current-year EPS estimates are up 4.7% over 90 days to $7.91 and next-year up 5.7% to $9.72, and the company has beaten in each of the last four quarters by an average of roughly 9%.

None of that is the decision. The decision is whether a business generating a 2.31% free cash flow yield and trading at 47.6x the midpoint of guided 2026 non-GAAP earnings offers adequate compensation for the specific risks that remain unresolved. Three of those risks carry near-term dates and no disclosed quantification: the annual long-term-care reserve assumption review that management has said will complete in Q3 2026 against $25.5B of LTC future policy benefit reserves; the IRS audit of 2016–2020 returns, where March 2026 Acknowledgement of Facts IDRs (a stage that typically precedes a proposed adjustment) accompany language that the IRS "may assert a material amount of additional taxes"; and a proposed FAA airworthiness directive expanding ultrasonic and eddy-current fan-blade inspections on the CFM56-7B, with comments due August 31, 2026, three weeks after an NTSB-led investigation into an in-flight failure on Ryanair FR1879 that injured a passenger.

Our base case is $400, or 41.2x 2027 consensus, +8.4% from the close, against a bear case of $285 (-22.7%). That is roughly 1:2.7 against, and it is the same conclusion the company's own capital allocation supports: GE repurchased 14.1M shares in H1 2026 at an average of $299.50, 18.8% below the current price, and now holds $18.0B of authorization to deploy at a materially thinner arithmetic return. **HOLD.** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on this name; the underlying fundamentals have improved since the last review while the price has improved faster, which is the configuration that keeps a high-quality asset out of the buy column.

## Company Overview

GE Aerospace is what remained of General Electric after the 2023 GE HealthCare and April 2024 GE Vernova separations: a propulsion company where services represented 71.3% of FY2025 adjusted revenue ($30,163M of $42,322M). The installed base is approximately 50,000 commercial and 30,000 military engines — management cites 80,000 engines and 2.3 billion flight hours.

Two reportable segments. **Commercial Engines & Services (CES)** was roughly 73% of FY2025 revenue at $33,314M (+24%) with a 26.6% segment margin, covering LEAP, GEnx, GE90, GE9X, CF34 and legacy CFM56. **Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT)** was roughly 23% at $10,554M (+11%) and a 12.3% margin, covering F110, T408, F404 and T700-class engines plus Avio Aero. Corporate & Other carries the run-off insurance operations, the tax equity portfolio and legacy liabilities. Effective January 15, 2026, CES was expanded to the full commercial engine lifecycle and the Aeroderivative business moved to DPT, with prior periods recast in the Q2 10-Q; FY2025 segment figures from the 10-K are not directly comparable to recast H1 figures.

One structural point that materially affects how the economics should be read: LEAP and CFM56 are products of CFM International, a 50/50 joint venture with Safran Aircraft Engines accounted for under the equity method, not consolidated. GE books its own manufacturing and aftermarket activity plus JV equity income (FY2025 CES equity-method income $376M on $1,682M of investments). Program economics and program risk on the world's most widely installed narrowbody engine family are shared. Ryanair, United, Delta, American and Copa are customers; Safran is a 50/50 partner; Beta Technologies is a program partner and investee.

Non-U.S. revenue was 60% of FY2025 total, up from 55%, with Asia at $10,819M (+49%). U.S. Government revenue fell to 10% of total from 12%, but Defense & Systems revenue grew in absolute terms from $5,927M to $6,574M — the declining percentage is denominator-driven by faster commercial growth and does not evidence reduced dependence on U.S. defense budgets.

### The margin question

The most decision-relevant tension in the numbers is revenue accelerating while margin compresses. CES margin fell 190bps in H1 to 26.9% and 160bps in Q2 to 27.3%; total non-GAAP operating margin fell 160bps. Decomposing the CES movement from the segment expense disclosure: cost of revenue rose from 63.9% to 65.9% of segment revenue (−200bps), SG&A improved 40bps, R&D improved 20bps, and other segment income grew slower than revenue (−40bps), netting −180bps against the reported −190bps within rounding.

Essentially all of the compression sits in cost of revenue. Management attributes it to "install engine growth including GE9X investments and inflation." The reading most consistent with 41% LEAP delivery growth is that thin- or negative-margin new installs are diluting reported margin while enlarging the base that generates 25%-plus services margin for decades — the razor/razorblade model working as designed, with the equipment ramp effectively an investment expensed through the margin line.

The alternative reading cannot be excluded from outside the company: a 200bp deterioration in cost of revenue is also what genuine input inflation, under-priced legacy long-term service agreements, or thinner services margins look like. Segment disclosure does not split equipment from services profitability, and when asked directly by Morgan Stanley whether there was line of sight to a >30% CES margin, management declined to commit. The forward test is whether CES margin recovers as equipment growth decelerates in 2027, and whether the LEAP-1B durability-kit cutover in early 2027 lowers warranty and concession cost as expected.

### Cash flow quality

H1 CFOA of $5,126M versus $3,891M and FCF of $4,685M versus $3,572M are genuine improvements, with receivables a $0.5B favorable swing and inventories $0.8B favorable — working capital was a $(0.3)B use versus $(0.5)B, achieved while earnings grew 24%.

A meaningful slice of the improvement, however, is one accrual line. The sales discounts and allowances liability rose $956M in H1 2026 (to $4,993M) versus $447M in H1 2025; that $509M differential accounts for roughly 41% of the $1,235M year-over-year CFOA improvement. The 10-Q attributes it to "allowances on new engine installs and spare parts, as well as product durability reserves at CES." Accruing customer concessions builds a liability now and consumes cash later when credits are redeemed. This is standard engine-OEM concession accounting during a delivery ramp rather than an earnings-quality flag, but it means Q2's reported >140% net income conversion is not a run rate, and the CFO said as much.

Q2 FCF of ~$3.0B should not be annualized. Doing so implies ~$12B against management's own $8.9–9.2B full-year guide, because H2 comparisons are harder, the concession build reverses over time, cash taxes and working capital are seasonal, and Q2 included roughly $100M of one-time IEEPA tariff refunds. As a labeled sensitivity rather than a forecast: had concession-liability growth matched the prior-year rate, H1 FCF would have been approximately $4.2B, still up ~18%. Guided full-year FCF of $9.05B against $10.65B of operating profit implies roughly 85% conversion — the defensible base rate.

### Balance sheet

Total borrowings of $19,157M at June 30, 2026 were down $1,337M from year-end with no new issuance in H1. Cash, equivalents and restricted cash of $9,345M plus $1,000M of time deposits gives $10.3B of stated liquidity, alongside a fully undrawn $3.0B revolver maturing 2029. Net debt is therefore approximately $8.8B. Against a TTM EBITDA proxy of roughly $12.5B, that is 0.71x; against guided 2026 operating profit of $10.65B, roughly 0.83x. The revolver's net-debt-to-EBITDA covenant was satisfied at June 30.

Two presentational items warrant correction. First, the current ratio of 0.98 (39,697/40,430) is not a liquidity problem: current liabilities include $7,937M of progress collections and $10,159M of contract liabilities and deferred income, which are performance obligations rather than near-term cash claims. Second, any book-equity-based distress screen will misclassify this company — book equity is $17.6B against $382.7B of market equity, a consequence of decades of repurchases and accumulated OCI. Recomputing the classic five-factor Altman Z with market equity and TTM EBIT of roughly $11.2B on $127.7B of assets produces approximately 3.8, in the conventional safe zone. That metric is poorly specified for an issuer carrying a $37.9B run-off insurance portfolio and $36.2B of insurance liabilities on both sides of the balance sheet, so the load-bearing evidence is elsewhere: 0.71x net leverage and two ratings actions in 2026 (Moody's A3→A2 with positive outlook, February 2; S&P A– outlook to positive, April 16).

### Capital allocation

FY2025: 29.6M shares repurchased for $7.4B, with a new $20B authorization approved in December 2025. H1 2026: 14.1M shares for $4,223M at an average of $299.50, including $2,338M via accelerated share repurchase; $17,988M remained authorized at June 30. Diluted share count fell 2.2% year over year. The quarterly dividend rose 30.6% to $0.47, an annualized $1.88 — a 0.51% yield at the close.

Two observations. The H1 execution was good: $299.50 against a current $368.93. The remaining $18.0B would be deployed at 47.6x guided earnings and a 2.31% FCF yield, a materially less accretive proposition. And returns were partly balance-sheet funded in H1 — cash and restricted cash fell $3,415M while $4,527M went to treasury purchases and $873M to dividends. That is a deliberate drawdown from a strong position rather than strain, but it was not funded from H1 FCF alone.

### Earnings composition

Of the $0.74 H1 adjusted EPS increase, roughly $0.115 came from the adjusted tax rate falling to 15.7% from 18.2% (applying the 250bp delta to $4,845M of adjusted pretax income across 1,050M diluted shares) and roughly $0.08 from share shrinkage — together about 26%, leaving ~74% operational. Guidance for a sub-16.5% full-year rate means the tax tailwind persists through 2026 but is unlikely to repeat at this magnitude in 2027.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the installed base rather than on order intake, and the disclosures support it. Approximately 30% of the CFM56 fleet has not had a first shop visit and two-thirds have not had a second; 70% of the GE90 installed base has not seen shop visit two, which management describes as carrying a significant workscope step-up. LEAP installed base is expected to more than double between 2026 and 2030, GEnx to double between 2024 and 2030, and LEAP shop visits to compound at roughly a 25% CAGR to 2030. Most engines shipping now will not come in for a shop visit before 2030 — the 2030 services stream is largely a function of engines already on wing, which insulates it from a near-term delivery slowdown, though not from airline decisions about when and how deeply to service.

RPO of $210.8B (+11% in six months; services $178.7B, equipment $32.1B) measures long-duration annuity value, not near-term revenue. Only 12% of services RPO and 32% of equipment RPO convert within a year — roughly $20.6B combined against revenue running above a $51B annualized rate. The majority of near-term revenue comes from transactional spare parts and time-and-material shop visits that never appear in RPO. RPO growth is a good indicator of installed-base value and a poor predictor of next quarter.

The binding constraint has inverted from demand to supply. H1 CES services orders and spare parts orders each grew 34%; spare parts delinquency (GE's term for shipments delayed by material availability) rose 20% sequentially despite record shop-visit output; the MRO footprint is described as oversubscribed; GE entered Q3 with more than 95% of spare parts revenue already in backlog and engines off wing; and the Q3 pipeline of planned removals exceeds the full-year shop visit guide by more than 40%. That is a favorable position for visibility and pricing, but it means 2027 revenue is gated by GE's and its suppliers' throughput rather than by order intake — which is why FLIGHT DECK metrics (LEAP turnaround ~100 days, down more than two weeks year over year; CFM56 final assembly lead time down ~50% at Celma) matter more to the revenue line than bookings do.

**Estimate revision assessment.** Current-year consensus is up 4.9% in 30 days and 4.7% in 90 days to $7.91; next year up 2.9% and 5.7% to $9.72. This is consistent, broad-based positive momentum across 21 and 13 analysts respectively, and it corroborates the guidance raise. It is not the large-magnitude revision signal (>30% over 90 days) that would override valuation discipline — nor should it be, since GE is not recovering from a trough. Revisions of this size on a stock already at 47.6x guided earnings confirm the operating story without creating a re-rating case.

The offsetting growth considerations are real. Roughly a quarter of H1 EPS growth came from tax and buyback. Margin is compressing. And the aftermarket's decoupling from airline stress may be a lag rather than immunity: jet fuel roughly doubled after the Iran conflict began in late February, fell about 45% from April levels on the mid-June interim framework, then surged approximately 20% in July, forcing major U.S. carriers to cut earnings guidance. GE's own Q2 10-Q added language absent from the 10-K, monitoring "lower utilization and increased prices" with possible consequences including "lower volume related to shop visits, spare parts and spare engines and lower profitability of our long term contracts, as well as customer credit implications." Global departures were roughly flat in H1. Transmission from airline financial stress to deferred workscopes, thinner shop-visit content and spare-engine deferrals runs with a delay measured in quarters.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is the installed base, and the trajectory is improving. Switching costs are effectively absolute once an engine is on wing, and the disclosed fleet age structure means workscope intensity rises structurally rather than cyclically.

H1 2026 win record: United and Delta selected GEnx for 787 orders; American and Copa selected LEAP for narrowbody orders (Copa up to 120 LEAP-1B); Ryanair signed a long-term material services agreement covering its entire fleet of approximately 2,000 CFM56 and LEAP engines. Widebody deliveries rose 30% in Q2 with GEnx up significantly more. On defense, F110 deliveries grew over 50% year over year in Q2, book-to-bill was 1.0 in Q2 and 1.7 in H1, the XA102 adaptive-cycle engine moved from design into assembly and test, NAVAIR awarded T408 engines for the CH-53K, Turkish Aerospace selected the F404 for Hürjet, and the UK MoD selected GE for its New Medium Helicopter.

The operational focus is LEAP durability. The LEAP-1B durability kit including the upgraded HPT blade has been certified, expected to roughly double time-on-wing, with full MRO and new-make cutover in early 2027. Over 40% of the LEAP-1A fleet is now equipped with time-on-wing in line with the CFM56, and management states there are nearly zero grounded LEAP-powered aircraft due to engines. Culp noted the retrofit will not pull work forward.

The unresolved strategic tension is cost of ownership. RBC put the customer complaint on the call directly: new-generation cost of ownership is much higher than before and "in some ways maybe almost not sustainable" for airlines' legacy operating models. GE's answer is the durability program, a repair-intensity push (repair CAGR above 20% this year), fixed-cost leverage, and shifting shop-visit labor to third-party MROs while retaining the parts sale — external channel from under 10% of LEAP services to mid-teens today, targeted at roughly 30% by 2030, with Iberia added as seventh Premier MRO, expanded Delta TechOps capability and MTU's Fort Worth facility inducting its first LEAP-1B. Doubling time-on-wing is unambiguously correct competitively and reduces warranty and concession cost, but it lengthens the shop-visit interval per engine. GE's bridge is that installed-base growth and fleet aging more than offset the interval extension. That arithmetic is plausible given the disclosed age distribution; it remains management's projection.

Competitors are Safran (simultaneously the 50/50 CFM partner), RTX's Pratt & Whitney in narrowbody, and Rolls-Royce in widebody. No independent verification of competitor operational data was performed here, so no characterization of their positions is offered.

## Risk Assessment

**Long-term service agreement estimation (largest accounting exposure).** Net LTSA liability of $7.2B equals 4.1% of $174B of life-of-contract billings, and the contract portfolio is on average only 19.2% complete. GE discloses that a one-percentage-point change in estimated LTSA profitability translates to roughly $0.4B of profit impact. The mechanism is live quarterly: Q1 2025 carried an unfavorable change driven primarily by estimated tariff impact, Q1 2026 reversed $118M of it, and H1 2026 net unfavorable changes were $42M.

**Q3 2026 long-term-care reserve review (near-dated, unquantified).** LTC future policy benefit reserves were $25,468M at June 30 within $36,195M of total insurance liabilities, with 10.9-year weighted-average duration, a 5.5% discount rate and $60,456M of undiscounted expected future benefit payments. The 10-Q flags that the annual cash-flow assumption review completes in Q3, with any revision recognized retroactively in current-period earnings. LTC is the most assumption-sensitive liability class in U.S. insurance. The supporting portfolio carries $37,910M of fair value against $39,946M of amortized cost — a ~$2.0B net unrealized loss with $13,600M in a loss position for 12 months or more (stated as non-credit-related, with no intent to sell). Insurance profit is declining ($301M in H1 2026 versus $353M). Because insurance is excluded from adjusted EPS, an adverse revision would hit GAAP results and possibly capital requirements while leaving the headline adjusted guide untouched — a disclosure asymmetry worth tracking.

**IRS audit escalation.** The 2016–2020 consolidated returns are under audit, and the Q2 10-Q newly discloses March 2026 Acknowledgement of Facts IDRs relating to pre- and post-TCJA computations including the transition tax. GE believes accruals are sufficient but states the IRS "may assert a material amount of additional taxes," that no meaningful estimate is possible, and that resolution is unlikely within 12 months. AOF IDRs typically precede a proposed adjustment. Any obligation would be allocated with GE Vernova and GE HealthCare under the tax matters agreements, so GE Aerospace's share is less than gross.

**CFM56-7B failure, NTSB probe and proposed FAA AD.** On July 10, an apparent engine failure on Ryanair FR1879 (737-NG) resulted in cabin-window damage and a passenger injury; on July 16 the NTSB announced it would lead the investigation; on July 30 and August 4 reporting indicated the FAA proposed an AD amending AD 2018-26-01 to require initial and repetitive ultrasonic or eddy-current fan-blade inspections with conditional replacement, driven by CFM service-material updates, with comments due August 31. No SEC filing quantifies exposure; the 10-Q filed July 16 does not mention FR1879 in Legal Matters, and product warranty liability declined to $511M from $595M. Two important qualifications: the causal link between FR1879 and the proposed AD is not established (the AD text is attributed to CFM service-material updates, and CFM has iterated these requirements since 2018), and Ryanair's CEO publicly speculated that debris may have caused the failure, which if confirmed would place root cause outside CFM's design and manufacture. The event is genuinely two-sided — mandated repetitive inspections across a very large CFM56-7B fleet with conditional blade replacement mechanically generate incremental inspection, repair and parts demand flowing to CES services and equity-method income.

**Boeing 777X dependency in a $2.4B contract asset.** The 10-Q discloses $2,406M of capitalized nonrecurring engineering costs, primarily in DPT, with the most significant program relating to DPT contracts for the 777X, amortized once the aircraft enters service, with impairment required if recovery is no longer probable. The 777X share is not disclosed, so exposure is bounded above by $2,406M and otherwise unsized. GE has indicated no recoverability concern.

**Supply chain.** The 10-Q states global material availability continues to cause disruptions affecting production and delivery. Spare parts delinquency rose 20% sequentially. Nine consecutive quarters of double-digit input growth from critical suppliers is progress, but the constraint caps revenue conversion.

**Legacy items.** Bank BPH estimated borrower-litigation losses fell to $1,814M from $2,334M, though the decline came from utilization (settlements paid) and FX rather than a falling claim rate, and GE expects lawsuit counts to keep rising. EHS reserves stand at $2,190M with roughly $250M of annual expenditures guided for 2026–2027 and an explicit statement that exposure could exceed accruals. Approximately $7,000M of GE Vernova obligations remain behind GE Aerospace credit support, down more than 89% since 2023, with GE Vernova contractually obligated to reimburse and indemnify and paying a quarterly fee; GE carries a $63M stand-ready reserve and reports no historical instance of being required to perform. The Priest/Tola derivative case was dismissed with prejudice in July 2026, closing out 2018-vintage legacy shareholder litigation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.2% against 32.2% one-year realized is rich (5.0pp spread, 64th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation with 37.2% near-term versus 32.7% at six months — the market is paying up for near-dated protection. The 2-day tenor's ±2.7% implied move is largely expiry mechanics; the more informative reading is the September 11 tenor at ±8.6% and November at ±15.3%. The data source characterizes the near-term 5.3% put/call skew as elevated put demand, while the six-month tenor's skew reads negative and put/call volume ratios fall sharply with tenor (0.92 at one week to 0.08 at six months). Open interest is call-tilted at 0.63, and the two flagged unusual trades are both call buying — 410 contracts of September $440 calls at 3.4x open interest and 672 contracts of November $400 calls at 3.0x. Short interest is negligible at 1.4% of float with 3.0 days to cover.

The composite points to near-term event hedging (plausibly around the August 31 AD comment deadline and the Q3 reserve review) alongside longer-dated positioning that remains constructive. That is consistent with our own framing: the franchise is not in question, the next two quarters carry identifiable binary disclosure risk, and the price already reflects a good outcome. Rich near-term IV argues against buying premium here and modestly favors selling upside calls for holders rather than adding equity exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $455 (+23.3%)

Supply, not demand, is the constraint, and FLIGHT DECK is loosening it. Shop-visit turnaround improved about a week since end-2025 and LEAP turnaround fell more than two weeks to ~100 days; the Q3 removal pipeline exceeds the full-year shop visit guide by over 40%. If throughput converts, 2027 revenue growth stays high-teens with CES margin recovering toward 28%+ as equipment growth decelerates and the LEAP-1B durability cutover in early 2027 cuts warranty and concession cost. LEAP shop visits compound ~25% to 2030 off engines already on wing, external channel reaches ~30% of LEAP services (trading labor for higher-margin parts), and the Q3 LTC review, IRS audit and CFM56 AD all resolve without material cost — with the AD arguably a net positive through mandated inspection and parts demand. 2027 EPS reaches $10.00+ and the market pays 45x for a services annuity with $178.7B of RPO. Consensus high target is $455.

### Bear Case — $285 (−22.7%)

Two channels, either sufficient. First, the airline transmission lag closes: jet fuel up ~20% in July has already forced U.S. carriers to cut guidance, and deferred workscopes, thinner shop-visit content and spare-engine deferrals arrive with a two-to-four-quarter delay, decelerating services orders from +34% toward high single digits. Second, the multiple compresses independently of earnings — a re-rating to 30x 2027 consensus on unchanged $9.50 EPS produces $285 with no operational miss at all. Layer in an adverse Q3 LTC assumption revision against $25.5B of reserves, an IRS assertion the company itself calls potentially material, or an FAA AD that expands toward design remediation with GE bearing cost, and the downside compounds. Cost of revenue rising 200bps as a share of CES revenue turns out to reflect genuine inflation and under-priced LTSAs rather than install mix; management's refusal to affirm a >30% CES margin path proves informative.
