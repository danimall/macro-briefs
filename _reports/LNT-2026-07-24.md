**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Alliant Energy is a ~$19.4B Wisconsin-based regulated utility holding company operating two vertically integrated subsidiaries (Interstate Power and Light (IPL) in Iowa and Wisconsin Power and Light (WPL) in Wisconsin) plus a minority stake in American Transmission Company. The business is ~95%+ regulated, giving it a low-volatility, rate-base-growth earnings model. Alliant's service territories sit directly in the path of a hyperscale data-center load boom. A Wisconsin PSCW draft report projects state peak load rising ~40% by 2032 (14.2 GW to 20+ GW), driven almost entirely by AI data centers, and IPL/WPL have already signed electric service agreements for ~3 GW of aggregate peak demand. This underpins a ~$13B 2026-2029 capital plan, roughly double the historical pace.

The load-growth thesis is genuine and confirmed across multiple regulatory and corporate data points, and it is the reason the stock has advanced ~15% over the trailing year to near its 52-week high. The thesis converts to per-share earnings growth only after passing through two frictions: ~$2.4B of planned equity issuance through 2029 (plus a new $1B ATM program) that dilutes the ~258M share base by an estimated 7-8%, and a negative-free-cash-flow financing model that is directly exposed to a higher-for-longer rate environment. Interest expense rose from $449M (2024) to $512M (2025) and was up 19% YoY in Q1 2026. Consolidated 2025 EPS of $3.14 grew a solid mid-single-digit rate, but the utility operating segment's EPS declined in Q1 2026 (utility segment $0.83 vs $0.87), with the reported beat carried by a one-time $12M parent-level tax benefit.

At ~$74.42, LNT trades at ~24x current-year and forward EPS, a premium to the electric-utility peer median of ~21.7x, with a below-median ~2.9% dividend yield and analyst consensus targets (mean $79.27, median $80.00) implying only mid-single-digit upside. Current-quarter estimates were revised down 13.7% over the past 30 days while full-year estimates held flat. Alliant is a high-quality compounder in an attractive demand environment, but the entry point offers no asymmetry. We maintain a HOLD, classified as a growth hold, and would become constructive on a pullback toward the low-$60s or on evidence that data-center load is converting to approved, recoverable rate base ahead of the dilution.

## Company Overview

Alliant operates through two regulated utility subsidiaries. IPL is a regulated electric and gas utility in Iowa (~$12.6B total assets, regulated by the Iowa Utilities Commission), and WPL is a regulated electric and gas utility in Wisconsin (~$10.8B total assets, regulated by the PSCW). The parent also holds a minority equity interest in ATC Holdings (a steady ~$0.16/share contributor) and small non-utility operations (Travero) plus corporate services.

The competitive structure is the standard regulated-monopoly model: exclusive service territories, constructive regulatory frameworks (forward-looking test years, riders, advance rate-making principles in Iowa), and a large, tax-advantaged renewables fleet that generates transferable tax credits. Alliant transferred $285M of renewable credits to third parties in 2025 (up from $216M in 2024 and $98M in 2023) and holds ~$656M of federal credit carryforwards. Iowa's Major Economic Growth Attraction program and Iowa/Wisconsin data-center sales-tax exemptions actively court hyperscale load, and Wisconsin regulators ordered Alliant in May 2026 to create a standardized large data-center tariff tied to a Beaver Dam hyperscale project.

The hyperscalers are customers of IPL/WPL for electricity; Alliant is the infrastructure and energy provider, not an owner or partner in the data centers. This distinction matters for the risk profile: Alliant bears the capital cost of generation and transmission built ahead of load, and it is exposed to customer counterparty credit and project-cancellation risk.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and revenue.** Consolidated 2025 revenue was $4.36B, up 9.6% from $3.98B. Net income rose 17% to $810M from $690M, and diluted EPS advanced to $3.14 from $2.69. On an adjusted basis, the multi-year EPS progression runs $2.65 (2021) to $3.24 (2025), steady mid-single-digit growth consistent with the sector. Q1 2026 EPS was $0.87 versus $0.83 (net income $224M vs $213M). The utility segment itself declined ($0.83 vs $0.87) on higher O&M, depreciation, and interest, with the consolidated improvement driven by a one-time $12M state tax apportionment benefit at the parent. The underlying utility earnings trend was soft in the most recent quarter even as headline EPS beat.

**Margins and returns.** Operating margin runs ~27.7% and net margin ~21.9%, healthy and stable. ROE is ~11.0%, in line with authorized returns of ~9.8-11.25%. ROA of ~3.2% is low but normal for a capital-intensive utility.

**Cash flow and the FCF gap.** 2025 operating cash flow was $1.17B. Actual utility construction and acquisition expenditures were ~$1.9B, well above operating cash flow. Alliant is a structurally negative-free-cash-flow utility funding the gap with debt and equity. This is normal for a rate-base-growth utility, but it means per-share earnings growth is mechanically diluted by the financing required to build the rate base, and it makes the model more sensitive to interest rates than a self-funding business.

**Balance sheet and credit.** Total debt is ~$12.1B, with debt/total-cap of ~61-62% (60% on the rating-agency-adjusted basis). The screening-model Altman Z-Score of 0.85 ("distress zone") and D/E of 3.41 are false alarms; the Z-Score is not calibrated for rate-regulated utilities whose high leverage is supported by regulated cash flows. The credit story is favorable: S&P upgraded IPL to A- (from BBB+) in March 2026; WPL is A-/Baa1; the parent is BBB+/Baa2, all Stable. The Piotroski score of 4/5 is a more accurate read of financial health than the leverage-based distress flag.

**Capital allocation.** The ~$13B 2026-2029 capex plan (~$3.1-3.6B/year) is dominated by new gas generation, renewables/storage, and distribution. It is funded by ~$2.4B of planned equity issuance through 2029 via ATM programs with forward-sale components (~19.6M shares under forward agreements at ~$66 average as of Q1 2026), plus a new $1B 2026 ATM. This is sophisticated pre-funding at locked-in prices, but it represents ~7-8% dilution to the share base as forwards settle through 2028. The dividend was raised 5% to $2.14/share for 2026 (~2.9% yield), targeting a 60-70% payout.

The financial-health score of 5.5 reflects a business that is financially sound (upgraded credit, stable margins, covered dividend) but structurally negative-FCF and financing-dependent in a rising-rate environment, a profile that does not clear the bar for a high (7+) financial-health score in the current regime.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on data-center load, corroborated by regulator projections, standardized tariff mandates, and multiple signed agreements across two states. Recent regulatory approvals validate the pipeline: the IUC approved advance rate-making for up to 1,000 MW of new Iowa wind (cost cap $3,020/kW) and the 720 MW Bobcat Energy Center; the PSCW approved WPL rate increases of $69M electric + $7M gas effective 1/1/2026 and $75M electric + $5M gas effective 1/1/2027; and in April 2026 IPL signed a new ~370 MW data-center agreement and filed for a second ~720 MW simple-cycle gas plant. Alliant is pivoting toward dispatchable gas (~1,600 MW planned) alongside storage to serve this load. That breadth and durability support a growth-prospects score above the sector baseline.

Two qualifiers keep the score from the top tier. First, the load is mobile and contingent: in Q1 2026 a data-center customer that had signed with WPL in Wisconsin switched to a site in IPL's Iowa territory, terminating and re-signing the agreement (the source of the $12M tax benefit). This is a concrete illustration of the timing-and-location uncertainty the 10-K explicitly warns about. Second, the growth converts to per-share EPS only after passing through the ~7-8% equity dilution. Alliant targets rate-base-driven earnings growth, but the per-share reality is closer to the mid-single digits after dilution, consistent with consensus EPS of $3.42 (current year) rising to $3.69 (next year), roughly 8% but on a diluting share count.

Estimate revisions are neutral-to-mildly-negative: current-year EPS is flat over 90 days, next-year flat, but the current quarter was revised down 13.7% over 30 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity that would flag a strong buy signal; it is a stable-to-soft revision picture that argues against paying up.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$74.42, LNT trades at ~24x current-year and forward EPS. Against the electric-utility peer median of ~21.7x, this is a premium of roughly 10%. The peer set shows wide dispersion (EVRG at 22.1x, ES at 14.6x, PPL at 21.7x, FE at 25.4x, EIX at 7.9x, distressed on California wildfire exposure), but the median of quality regulated peers sits below Alliant's multiple. Alliant's ~2.9% dividend yield is also below the peer median of ~4.0%, meaning investors are paying a premium multiple for a below-median yield, justified only if the load-growth premium translates to superior per-share growth.

The core problem for the valuation score is that the premium is not asymmetric. Analyst consensus targets (mean $79.27, median $80.00, high $86.00, low $73.00) imply mid-single-digit upside to the median and downside to the low. The stock trades within ~$4.40 of its 52-week high of $78.81, having run ~15% over the trailing year as the data-center thesis re-rated the shares. Under this system's principle that analyst targets are floors on cheap inflection stocks, that guardrail does not apply here: the stock is neither cheap (24x above peer median) nor an inflection story (flat estimate revisions), so the consensus targets read more like a genuine ceiling than a stale floor.

There is no cycle-adjustment argument to make. This is not a trough-earnings situation where trailing P/E overstates the multiple; EPS is growing steadily and the trailing and forward multiples are identical at ~24x. The equity lessons reinforce the caution: in the current regime, a high valuation score ("cheap") has been anti-predictive, but the opposite problem applies here: this stock is not cheap, and paying a premium multiple for a below-median yield with no catalyst offering asymmetric upside merits a low valuation score. We assign 4.5: the multiple reflects a fully-appreciated growth story with the dilution drag not yet fully in the numbers.

## Competitive Landscape

Alliant's competitive position is structurally advantaged and improving on the demand side. It holds regulated monopoly service territories in two states with constructive regulatory frameworks and sits in one of the strongest structural demand environments in the regulated utility sector. Its signed agreements and load projection place Alliant in a superior load-growth position relative to most regulated peers, whose demand is flat-to-low-single-digit.

The competitive-position score of 6.5 reflects this favorable trajectory while accounting for two constraints. IPL operates under a retail electric base-rate moratorium through September 2029 (reopenable only if ROE falls 100+ bps below authorized for a year or 50+ bps for two years), and WPL has an earnings-sharing mechanism capping ROE above 10.05%. These constructive-but-constraining mechanisms limit the ability to over-earn on the growth. Execution also depends on third parties (interconnection and transmission via ITC and ATC), introducing delay risk outside Alliant's control. Within the regulated-utility complex, Alliant's differentiator is confirmed superior load growth; the offset is the dilution and negative-FCF financing that cap the conversion to per-share value.

## Risk Assessment

**Data-center concentration — the defining risk.** Alliant added an entirely new lead risk category to its 2025 10-K, "Risks Related to Data Center and Other Large Load Growth Customers." The company must deploy large upfront capital in generation and transmission before returns materialize. Customers may take less energy than contracted, delay, or cancel; concentration increases earnings volatility within a single industry; individual customer rates may not fully recover costs; and there is counterparty credit and stranded-asset risk. IPL and WPL have already posted ~$163M and ~$75M of guarantees to transmission providers for data-center infrastructure that would need reimbursement if projects terminate. The Q1 2026 customer state-switch demonstrates that this load is contingent.

**Tax-credit dependence and OBBB.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted July 2025) accelerates the phase-out of wind/solar PTCs/ITCs and tightens foreign-entity sourcing restrictions. Given Alliant's unusual reliance on transferable tax credits ($285M transferred in 2025), this threatens future renewables economics and credit monetization, partly mitigating the value of the accelerated pivot to gas.

**Regulatory lag and moratorium.** The IPL moratorium through September 2029 and the WPL earnings-sharing cap constrain upside during a period of heavy capital deployment.

**Interest-rate sensitivity.** As a negative-FCF utility funding the capex plan with debt and equity, Alliant is directly exposed to a higher-for-longer path. A hawkish rate outcome raises the cost of the financing model and pressures the equity multiple.

**Governance and labor.** A CEO transition (Larsen to Barton) is reflected in the 2026 proxy and a cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks, though the timeline was not fully corroborated by news. Pay-for-performance alignment looks reasonable (2025 TSR of 147.98 modestly beat the peer group's 143.83; CEO comp $9.0M/$10.8M CAP). Insider activity is routine (director grants and tax withholdings only, no meaningful open-market buying or selling). A WPL IBEW Local 965 contract covering 85% of WPL employees expired May 2026, a work-stoppage watch item. WPL also lacks an automatic fuel-cost adjustment clause (unlike IPL), an under-appreciated intra-company risk asymmetry partially addressed by Wisconsin Act 193 from 2027.

The risk-factors score of 5.0 reflects that the data-center concentration and OBBB tax-credit risks are specific, newly disclosed, and not fully priced, while the credit-rating strength and regulated cash flows cushion the downside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 27.5% versus 1-year historical volatility of 16.0% (rich by an 11.5-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (27.5% near-term vs 18.4% at six months), signaling the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, most likely the upcoming earnings print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.4%. Put/call skew is balanced (2.7%), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.14 is not defensive. The options market is pricing event risk into the near-term earnings window without a directional bearish tilt. For a HOLD, the rich near-term IV argues against establishing a position immediately ahead of the print; a better entry would follow the post-earnings volatility crush. Short interest of 12.4% of float (10.7 days to cover) is elevated for a utility, reflecting institutional caution on the dilution/rate-sensitivity story rather than a distressed-company thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$86, the analyst high).** Data-center load converts faster than the market expects; the signed agreements and additional pipeline (Beaver Dam, Linn County, the 370 MW April agreement) translate into approved, recoverable rate base with minimal cancellation. Regulators approve the large-load tariffs cleanly, DOE loan guarantees (~$3B aggregate for IPL/WPL) finalize and lower financing costs, and the rate-base growth flows through to per-share EPS at the high end. A dovish rate path re-rates the multiple. In this scenario Alliant earns a durable premium to peers.

**Bear case (~$60, roughly -19% from current).** The higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the financing plan, compressing the equity multiple toward the peer median or below. Data-center projects slip, relocate (as one already did), or partially cancel, forcing guarantee reimbursements and stranding some pre-built capital. OBBB erodes tax-credit economics faster than the gas pivot compensates. Equity dilution caps per-share growth in the low-single digits, and the below-median yield offers little support as investors reprice the premium.

**Base case (~$79-80, in line with consensus median).** Load growth proceeds broadly as planned but at a measured, regulator-gated pace; rate-base growth converts to mid-single-digit per-share EPS growth after dilution; the multiple holds near current levels. Total return is roughly the dividend plus mid-single-digit EPS growth, with the multiple providing little additional lift. This is a solid, low-volatility total return, but not one that requires buying at the 52-week high.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings print and the rolling cadence of large-load tariff approvals.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$60s (near the 52-week low of $63.28), which would restore asymmetric upside to the base case and bring the yield back above 3.2%.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity — full-year EPS revised up meaningfully (rather than the current flat-to-negative trend) alongside confirmed conversion of data-center agreements to approved, recoverable rate base ahead of the dilution.
- Finalization of the DOE loan guarantees (~$3B), which would materially lower the financing cost of the capex plan and de-risk the negative-FCF model.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cancellation or material relocation of one or more of the data-center agreements, triggering guarantee reimbursements and stranded-capital charges.
- A sustained hawkish rate outcome that pushes interest expense growth above the ~19% Q1 2026 pace while the equity multiple compresses below the peer median.
- OBBB-driven impairment of tax-credit economics not offset by the gas pivot, or a regulatory affordability backlash that constrains large-load cost recovery.

**Reassessment points:** The next quarterly earnings release (the options market is pricing a ±6.4% move around it), the WPL IBEW contract resolution, and the rolling docket of large-load tariff approvals in Iowa and Wisconsin.

## Conclusion

Alliant Energy is a high-quality regulated utility with a genuinely differentiated demand backdrop: its Iowa and Wisconsin territories sit in the path of a confirmed, multi-source data-center load boom that underpins a doubling of its capital plan and improving credit. The growth thesis is real and broad-based, not speculative. At ~$74.42, roughly $4 below its 52-week high and ~24x forward EPS versus a ~21.7x peer median, the stock has already re-rated to reflect that thesis, and the analyst consensus implies only mid-single-digit upside. The per-share conversion of rate-base growth is capped by ~7-8% equity dilution, and the negative-FCF financing model is directly exposed to a higher-for-longer rate path. The revision picture is stable-to-soft, not an inflection.

Alliant is a durable mid-single-digit compounder that we would own at a better price. The trajectory of prior scores (6.2 → 6.0 → 6.0) reflects a business whose story is sound but whose valuation has caught up to it; nothing in the current data reverses that read. We maintain HOLD as a growth hold, own it for the covered dividend plus steady EPS growth if already held, and would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$60s or on demonstrated, recoverable load conversion ahead of the dilution.
