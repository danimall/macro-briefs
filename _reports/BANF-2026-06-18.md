**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BancFirst is the dominant community and commercial bank in Oklahoma, running a multi-charter structure (flagship BancFirst plus Pegasus in Dallas and Worthington in Fort Worth) on top of a deposit franchise that few regional peers can match. Core deposits are 95.2% of total, noninterest-bearing deposits are roughly 32%, the company uses no brokered CDs, and long-term borrowings were paid to zero in Q1 2026. That funding base produced a 3.74% net interest margin, 1.71% ROA, 13.6% ROE, and a 54.1% efficiency ratio in the most recent quarter, top-decile metrics for a regional bank. Capital is strong at 20.2% total risk-based and 18.2% CET1, and the securities book is short-dated, so the AOCI hole that crippled duration-heavy banks in 2023 is trivial here (-$10.7M against $1.9B equity).

The investment question is whether that quality is mispriced today. At $110.67 the stock trades at 14.8x forward earnings, broadly in line with its own historical range and with high-quality regional peers. Consensus EPS estimates have risen +5.6% (current year) and +7.5% (next year) over 90 days, confirming positive earnings momentum but well short of the revision velocity that would justify looking through the multiple. The three covering analysts cluster targets at $123-125, about 12% above the current price, and all three rate the stock Hold with stable sentiment. There is no dated, quantifiable catalyst on the horizon: the SpiritBank acquisition is announced but its terms are undisclosed, so accretion cannot be modeled.

We rate BANF a HOLD (growth_hold). The score trajectory has improved (6.0 → 6.7 → current), driven by genuine upgrades to Financial Health, Competitive Position, and Management Quality, and we confirm those upgrades. What holds us back from a BUY is the combination of a full-but-fair multiple, the absence of a measurable near-term catalyst, and creeping office-CRE/OREO that is the one soft spot in an otherwise pristine book. This is a compounder to own through the cycle, not a stock with identifiable asymmetric upside over the next twelve months.

## Company Overview

BancFirst Corporation (~$3.7B market cap) is an Oklahoma City–headquartered bank holding company, the largest state-chartered bank franchise in Oklahoma. Its structure is unusual and central to the thesis:

- **BancFirst** (~83% of loans), the flagship, split into two reporting segments: Metropolitan Banks (Oklahoma City and Tulsa) and Community Banks (rural/non-metro Oklahoma). The Community segment is the largest by assets ($8.7B) and pre-tax income ($48.1M in Q1 2026), reflecting a sticky, low-cost deposit base in markets with limited competition.
- **Pegasus Bank** (~11% of loans), Dallas metro.
- **Worthington Bank** (~6% of loans), Fort Worth/Arlington/Denton, Texas.
- **Other Financial Services**, trust, insurance, SBA/guaranteed lending, residential mortgage, brokerage, and a differentiated oil & gas hedging desk that intermediates commodity swaps and options for energy customers, earning roughly 35% of the spread.

The franchise's defining feature is its rural-Oklahoma community-bank deposit base, which produces an extraordinarily cheap and stable source of funding. With 31% of assets held in cash and interbank deposits and no reliance on wholesale funding, BancFirst is structurally the opposite of the banks that failed in 2023.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability and growth.** FY2025 net income was $240.6M ($7.11 diluted EPS), up from $216.4M in FY2024 and $212.5M in FY2023, steady mid-single-digit to low-double-digit growth. Q1 2026 net income was $63.0M ($1.85 diluted EPS) versus $56.1M ($1.66) a year earlier, up 12% YoY. Net interest income grew 10.1% YoY to $127.6M, driven by earning-asset growth and a favorable funding shift: interest-bearing liability cost fell to 2.86% from 3.36%, faster than asset yields declined, lifting NIM to 3.74% from 3.70%.

**Returns and efficiency.** Q1 2026 annualized ROA was 1.71% and ROE 13.59%, both top-tier for the regional cohort. The efficiency ratio of 54.1% (improved from 55.9%) reflects disciplined cost control alongside revenue growth.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets were $15.1B, deposits $12.9B, loans $8.6B, and equity $1.9B at quarter-end. Loan growth was modest (+0.6% QoQ), concentrated in commercial real estate at the core bank. Capital ratios are exceptional: total risk-based 20.2%, CET1 18.2%, leverage 11.8%, all far above well-capitalized thresholds. This excess capital is the strategic engine funding the acquisition roll-up.

**Capital allocation.** Operating cash flow runs roughly $76M per quarter, comfortably covering the dividend. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.49 from $0.46, a ~26% payout ratio that leaves ample room for self-funded capital build. A buyback authorization (479,784 shares remaining) exists but is used sparingly; organic growth, dividends, and M&A take priority.

**Asset quality.** Nonaccrual loans are 0.72% of loans (flat), the allowance covers loans at 1.23% and nonaccruals at 169%, and net charge-offs are negligible at 0.02% annualized. The one creeping soft spot: other real estate owned and repossessed assets rose to $53.6M from $49.1M, net OREO expense is climbing, and nonaccrual CRE is 59% of total nonaccruals. Management explicitly flags commercial office property deterioration. The AOCI loss of just -$10.7M on a short-dated ($900M, mostly sub-5yr Treasuries) securities book confirms negligible duration risk.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is steady rather than rapid: loan growth of 0.6% QoQ and high-single-digit net interest income growth, supported by NIM expansion as deposit costs reprice down. The estimate revision picture is positive but moderate: current-year EPS consensus has risen to $7.39 and next-year to $7.67, with the 30-day next-year revision (+4.8%) showing the trend is firming rather than fading. This does not approach the +30% revision velocity that signals a true earnings inflection. The appropriate framing is a high-quality bank compounding earnings in the high-single digits, not a trough-to-recovery story.

The discretionary growth lever is M&A. BancFirst is deploying its ~20% capital ratio into an in-market consolidation strategy:

- **ABOK** (American Bank of Oklahoma, ~$414M assets) closed November 2025 for ~$33M and merged into BancFirst in February 2026, immaterial but accretive.
- **SpiritBank** (Tulsa-based, ~$939.6M assets, $618.4M loans, $847.2M deposits) was announced June 10, 2026, with a targeted Q4 2026 close. This is the second Tulsa-area deal in roughly seven months and would grow the company ~9% in combined assets with minimal balance-sheet strain.

The strategic logic is sound: BancFirst is the natural consolidator in a fragmented Oklahoma market and the acquirer of choice (the SpiritBank CEO chose it among alternatives). The caveat for valuation is that SpiritBank terms are undisclosed, so accretion and goodwill impact cannot be quantified yet. The growth from M&A is real but currently unmodelable, which limits how much credit it can earn in a price target today.

## Valuation Assessment

At $110.67, BANF trades at 14.8x forward and 15.2x trailing earnings, with a 1.72% dividend yield and a 0.60 beta. The estimate revisions do not clear the threshold for treating the stock as a trough-earnings inflection where the trailing multiple should be ignored. The earnings power is real and growing; the multiple already reflects it.

Peer comparison is not directly informative here: the provided same-sector comparables (Klarna at a negative P/E, Blue Owl at 81.5x) are not regional-bank analogs and tell us nothing useful about BANF's relative value. The more relevant benchmark is the high-quality regional-bank cohort, against which 14.8x forward for a 1.71% ROA / 13.6% ROE franchise with strong capital is fair-to-slightly-rich rather than cheap. BANF deserves a premium to the average regional for its deposit franchise, minimal duration risk, and capital strength; at ~15x it is collecting that premium, not offering it at a discount.

The stock is down from a 52-week high of $138.77 to $110.67, a ~20% drawdown that tracks the broader regional-bank de-rating on CRE and rate-path concerns rather than any BancFirst-specific deterioration; fundamentals are improving, not eroding. That moderate decline on intact fundamentals improves the risk/reward at the margin and is the reason we do not rate this lower. Improved risk/reward on a fairly-valued name does not by itself create asymmetric upside.

Consensus targets sit at $123-125 (mean $124.33), roughly 12% above current. Ordinarily we would treat analyst targets on a rising-estimate stock as stale floors. The guardrail applies here: with only three covering analysts, all rating Hold, stable sentiment, and a stock that is not cheap relative to growth (this is not a sub-1.5 PEG inflection), the targets are not a high-conviction signal of latent upside; they reflect the same fairly-valued-compounder view we hold. We score valuation a 5.0: not a value trap, not a bargain, fairly priced.

## Competitive Landscape

BancFirst's competitive position is improving, which supports a 7.0 score. Its differentiators are durable and partly underappreciated:

1. **Funding advantage.** A 95.2% core-deposit base, ~32% noninterest-bearing, no brokered CDs, and zero wholesale borrowings produce a stable, cheap funding cost that drives the 3.74% NIM despite a conservative, highly liquid balance sheet. Rural Oklahoma deposits face limited competition, making this base sticky.
2. **No duration trap.** The short-dated securities book left BancFirst structurally outside the 2023 regional crisis, the opposite profile of the failed banks. This is a genuine, under-recognized quality differentiator.
3. **Consolidator of choice.** In a fragmented Oklahoma market, BancFirst's statewide network and capital make it the natural acquirer, a position smaller community banks cannot match and out-of-state megabanks do not prioritize.
4. **Texas growth runway.** Pegasus and Worthington extend the franchise into the higher-growth Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, diversifying away from Oklahoma's energy and agriculture exposure.

The trade-offs are real: geographic concentration in Oklahoma and exposure to energy-sector cyclicality, including a growing oil & gas loan book and an energy-hedging derivative position that swings sharply with commodity prices.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic and energy concentration.** Earnings are heavily tied to the Oklahoma economy, sensitive to energy and agriculture. Direct oil & gas loans rose to $585.9M (~6.8% of loans). The energy-hedging derivative book swung roughly $60M in a single quarter (from a -$7.4M liability to a +$53.2M margin asset) as oil rose; while management states its ~35% net spread is unaffected by price moves, the gross notional and daily margin requirements create real liquidity and operational sensitivity.

**Commercial real estate / OREO.** Net OREO expense is rising and management specifically flags office-property risk. Overall credit metrics remain pristine, but this is the line to monitor and the most plausible source of negative surprise.

**Regulatory scaling costs.** Above the $10B asset threshold, BancFirst is subject to the Durbin Amendment (interchange caps, relevant given debit interchange is ~13% of fee income) and the Volcker Rule (a $4.4M expense in Q1 2025 to dispose of newly impermissible equity investments illustrates the cost of scale).

**Uninsured deposits ~34%** (~$4.4B), the structural post-2023 sensitivity for any regional bank, mitigated here by 31% liquid assets and $995M+ undrawn FHLB lines.

**Acquisition integration.** Two deals in seven months raise integration and goodwill-impairment risk (goodwill $183M), compounded by SpiritBank's undisclosed terms.

**Earnings consistency.** The recent track record is mixed, two beats and two misses over four quarters, including a -4.0% Q4 2025 miss, which tempers confidence in a clean upward trajectory and is why Financial Health, while high, does not pull the overall score higher.

We score Risk Factors 5.5: no single binary catastrophe is likely, but the combination of office-CRE creep, energy-book volatility, and integration risk are specific, monitorable downside vectors not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

The most recent options data show ATM implied volatility collapsing from 73.3% (mid-April) to 35.8% (early May) while the stock rose modestly, uncertainty resolving toward complacency rather than the market pricing an imminent catalyst. Short interest is low at 4.2% of float with 7.3 days to cover, consistent with a low-controversy, low-beta name. Neither signal contradicts the fundamental read; both reinforce the picture of a quiet compounder without a near-term binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($138, ~25% upside).** SpiritBank closes accretively in Q4 2026, the Tulsa and DFW expansion compounds organic growth, NIM holds above 3.7% as deposit costs continue repricing down, OREO stabilizes, and the market re-rates BancFirst back toward its prior $138 high on recognition of franchise quality and consolidation optionality. Rising estimates continue, pulling EPS toward $8 and the stock with it.

**Bear case ($90, ~19% downside).** Office-CRE deterioration accelerates, OREO and credit costs rise materially, energy prices reverse and pressure both the loan book and the regional economy, SpiritBank integration disappoints or its terms prove dilutive, and the regional-bank cohort de-rates further on rate or recession concerns. At ~12x trough EPS the stock retraces to the low $90s.

**Base case ($120, ~8% upside).** High-single-digit EPS growth continues, SpiritBank closes on reasonable terms, the multiple holds near 15x, and the stock drifts toward the lower end of the analyst target range. Total return is the mid-single-digit appreciation plus the 1.72% yield, a respectable but not index-beating outcome that defines the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q3 2026 earnings and any disclosure of SpiritBank deal terms, expected ahead of the targeted Q4 2026 close. The deal economics are the single most important unknown for re-rating the stock.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. SpiritBank terms disclosed and clearly EPS-accretive (e.g., guided accretion of 3%+ to forward EPS), converting the M&A optionality into a quantifiable catalyst.
2. Forward P/E compressing below ~13x on stable or rising estimates (a price below ~$96 against current $7.39 CY consensus), restoring genuine valuation asymmetry.
3. OREO stabilizing or declining for two consecutive quarters alongside continued NIM expansion above 3.75%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. OREO rising above ~$80M or nonaccruals breaching ~1.2% of loans, signaling office-CRE stress is material rather than creeping.
2. A NIM reversal below ~3.5% driven by a renewed shift into higher-cost deposits.
3. SpiritBank terms revealed as dilutive or integration costs materially exceeding expectations, or a goodwill impairment against the $183M balance.

## Conclusion

BancFirst is one of the higher-quality regional banks in the country: a cheap, sticky core-deposit franchise, strong capital, top-tier returns, negligible duration risk, an exceptionally shareholder-aligned cost structure (a $1.0M CEO package and TSR of 198 versus a 149 peer group), and a disciplined in-market consolidation strategy. The score trajectory has improved for good reason, and we confirm the upgrades to Financial Health (8.0), Competitive Position (7.0), and Management Quality (7.0).

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: at 14.8x forward earnings the stock is fairly valued for a high-single-digit grower, the consensus target implies only ~12% upside from three Hold-rated analysts, and the one catalyst that could drive a re-rating, SpiritBank accretion, is unquantifiable because terms are undisclosed. The estimate revisions are positive but moderate, not the +30% inflection magnitude that would justify looking through the multiple, and creeping office-CRE/OREO is a real, monitorable downside vector. This is a compounder to own through the cycle for its yield and steady earnings growth, with a clear path to upgrade once SpiritBank economics are disclosed or the multiple compresses. We would become buyers on confirmed deal accretion or a pullback toward the high $90s.
