## Executive Summary

Azenta reported a strong third quarter on August 4, 2026: revenue $161.2M (+12.0% reported, +9% organic), non-GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations of $0.16 against a $0.12 consensus (+37.8% surprise), and full-year guidance raised and narrowed to revenue of $613-618M with adjusted EBITDA of $59-62M. The shares are up 98% from the May 15 low of $15.93 and 19.5% from the $26.43 level of three weeks ago. That price move has done most of the work. Enterprise value has expanded from roughly $629M to $855M, taking EV/FY26E adjusted EBITDA from about 10.4x to 14.1x while the underlying guidance midpoint moved only modestly.

Underneath the beat, the operating picture is more ambiguous than the headline. Q3 GAAP operating loss widened to -$4.2M from -$1.9M as R&D rose to $8.9M and SG&A to $67.2M. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.16 was *below* the prior year's $0.17. Pre-tax income from continuing operations of +$0.846M was composed of a -$4.178M operating loss offset by $3.825M of net interest income and $1.199M of other income; the near-breakeven line is funded by an investment portfolio whose contribution has fallen from $43.5M (FY23) to $13.3M (9M FY26) as the cash was spent on buybacks. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA declined 14.6% to $38.9M on 4.6% revenue growth, reversing three years of margin repair. Sample Management Solutions, which carries $463.1M of the remaining $547.5M of goodwill, lost 745bps of gross margin and more than half its adjusted operating income on lower Automated Stores volumes, quality remediation and rework costs, and higher excess-and-obsolete inventory reserves.

The balance sheet is the genuine asset: $528.5M of cash, marketable securities and restricted cash, no debt, equity of $1,510.1M against a $1,384M market capitalization (0.92x book), and $200M remaining on a $250M authorization after repurchasing 2,344,002 shares at an average $21.33 in May-June 2026 (currently about 48% in the money). Against that: internal control over financial reporting remains ineffective with two of three material weaknesses outstanding (cash-flow statement review, open ~7 quarters; account reconciliations, open ~5 quarters); estimates have fallen 24% for the current quarter and 35% for the next quarter over 90 days; Q4 organic revenue is guided *down* low single digits; and FY26 free-cash-flow guidance requires a roughly $32-34M single-quarter year-over-year swing. Base case $34, bull $41, bear $23 from $31.59 produces an expected return near zero. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Azenta is the life-sciences remainder of the former Brooks Automation, which sold semiconductor cryogenics to Edwards Vacuum for $659.8M in 2019 and semiconductor automation to Thomas H. Lee Partners for $2.9B in cash on February 1, 2022. What remained was a life-sciences business roughly one-fifth the size of the proceeds just banked, and the deployment of those proceeds defines the company's record.

Two continuing segments:

**Sample Management Solutions** (9M FY26 revenue $250.8M). Sample Repository Services — outsourced biobanking, $111.2M for 9M, +16.6% — is the defensible franchise: relocating a biobank means physically moving material whose integrity is the product, which creates real switching costs. Core Products (Automated Stores, cryogenic systems, automated tubes, consumables, controlled-rate thawing) was $139.6M for 9M, *down* from $143.2M.

**Multiomics** (9M FY26 revenue $203.8M). GENEWIZ-branded next-generation and Sanger sequencing, gene synthesis and bioinformatics, competing against BGI Genomics, Novogene, GenScript, IDT, Eurofins and Twist Bioscience in a scale-and-price-per-base business. The segment has run an adjusted operating loss in every disclosed period (-$14.8M for 9M FY26, -$11.8M for 9M FY25, -$11.9M for FY25).

Approximately 2,900 employees, ~14,000 customers, sales in ~75 countries, 14 genomics labs (7 US, 3 China, 2 UK, 1 Japan, 1 Germany), storage sites across the US, Germany, Canada, Singapore and Beijing plus the newly acquired UK Biocentre. No customer exceeded 10% of revenue or receivables, which removes a risk that is common in this size bracket. Services were 74% of Q3 revenue, up from 73%.

## Financial Analysis

**A screening trap first.** Reported revenue fell $665.1M (FY23) → $656.3M (FY24) → $593.8M (FY25), which reads as a shrinking company. It is not the right series: B Medical Systems met held-for-sale criteria on November 12, 2024 and was reclassified for all periods. Continuing operations grew $551.5M → $573.4M → $593.8M, with gross margin improving 43.4% → 44.4% → 45.5% and adjusted operating margin going from -2.8% to +0.6% to +2.6%. Any screen using the reported line misclassifies this business.

**FY2026 broke that repair trend.** Nine-month FY26 versus FY25: revenue $454.6M vs $434.6M (+4.6% reported, ~+2% organic); gross margin 43.6% vs 45.6% (-200bps); adjusted gross margin 44.9% vs 46.9%; segment adjusted operating income -$1.7M vs +$6.5M; adjusted EBITDA $38.9M vs $45.6M (-14.6%). Revenue up, profit down.

**The segment crossover is the most important operating fact of the year.**

| Q3, adjusted | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| SMS revenue | $88.3M | $77.6M | +13.7% |
| SMS gross margin | 44.3% | 51.8% | **-745bps** |
| SMS adj. operating income | $4.9M | $10.7M | **-54%** |
| Multiomics revenue | $72.9M | $66.2M | +10.0% |
| Multiomics gross margin | 45.6% | 39.6% | **+600bps** |
| Multiomics adj. operating income | -$0.3M | -$4.0M | +$3.7M |

Multiomics, which absorbed $112.4M of goodwill write-off one quarter earlier, reached near-breakeven and out-earned SMS on gross margin for the first time in the disclosed periods. SMS, which holds 85% of remaining goodwill, halved. Management attributes the SMS decline to lower fixed-cost absorption on reduced Automated Stores volumes, "quality remediation and rework activities," and higher E&O reserves (inventory reserves $5.9M at 9/30/25 → $8.0M at 6/30/26). A cyclical volume decline explains absorption; it does not explain rework costs. Neither the duration nor the total cost of the rework has been quantified.

**Cash flow is the weakest line.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $35.8M against $70.0M, capex $20.2M against $26.0M, so derived free cash flow was $15.6M against $44.0M. Q3 alone: $1M of operating cash flow, $7M of capex, -$5M free cash flow. Management guides FY26 FCF to improve 10-15%. Reproducing the arithmetic from a derived FY25 base of $38.3M ($72.2M OCF less $33.9M capex) gives $42.1-44.0M, which against 9M actual of $15.6M requires Q4 FCF of roughly $26.5-28.4M versus a derived Q4 FY25 figure of -$5.7M, a $32-34M single-quarter swing. Q4 is seasonally the strongest collections quarter and B Medical's working-capital drag is gone as of July 1, so this is achievable; it is also the guidance line with the least margin for error, and the FY25 baseline is my derivation rather than a company-stated base.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $189.7M, short-term securities $136.1M, long-term securities $196.1M, restricted cash $6.7M, a company-stated $528.5M. No debt. Total assets $1,876.2M, liabilities $366.1M, equity $1,510.1M, current ratio ~2.5x. Two items merit attention: a $45.6M long-term income taxes receivable tied to a capital-loss/worthless-stock refund claim, against which the company has now booked $20.0M of unrecognized tax benefits ($2.5M in Q4 FY25, an additional $17.5M after filing the July 2026 federal return) and which it expects to be examined by the IRS and reviewed by the Joint Committee on Taxation; and a $28.4M derivative liability on a $260.0M/€250.0M cross-currency swap designated as a net investment hedge, extended to February 2, 2028, with fair-value changes running through OCI.

The honest read: essentially no solvency risk and essentially no demonstrated earnings power. The Altman Z of 4.81 derives from the equity-to-liabilities and working-capital terms; the EBIT/assets component is slightly negative.

**Tax is a structural drag.** Q3 tax expense of $2.375M on $0.846M of pre-tax income turned a small profit into a -$1.529M net loss, because a US valuation allowance denies benefit for US losses while foreign profits are taxed. Cash taxes paid were $7.0M for nine months.

*Data note:* the profile feed shows EPS of $0.53 alongside a trailing P/E of -12.50, which is internally inconsistent (the negative multiple implies roughly -$2.53 of trailing GAAP EPS, consistent with the $3.49 Q2 GAAP loss per share from the impairment). I have relied on the filings and the earnings release rather than either field.

## Growth Analysis

Q3 organic growth of +9% is the best print in years, but the composition and the forward guide temper it. FY26 organic growth is guided to flat-to-+1% (raised from -2% to +1%), which means **Q4 organic revenue declines approximately low single digits**. Nine-month organic growth was ~2%, with roughly 2pts of FX and 1pt of UK Biocentre in the reported 4.6%.

Geography tells the demand story: for nine months, United States revenue fell 3.4% to $262.0M (~58% of the total) while China rose 27.8% to $53.6M, the UK rose 29.7% to $32.5M and rest-of-Europe rose 9.8% to $82.4M. Ex-US mix rose to 42% from 38%. This is consistent with sector-wide pressure on US academic, government and pharma R&D budgets rather than idiosyncratic share loss. It is not evidence of diversification benefit and not evidence of share gain — part of the ex-US growth is FX and part is the acquisition.

**Estimate revisions are the clearest negative.** Consensus for the current quarter fell 15.5% in 30 days and 24.0% in 90 days; next quarter -16.7% and -35.2%; current year -16.5% over 90 days (though +2.5% over 30 days, reflecting the Q3 beat); next year -12.5% over 90 days. The 30-day current-year uptick is the only positive revision in the table. The four-quarter surprise record — +8.6%, -35.9%, -131.7%, +37.8% — shows genuinely poor forward visibility, which argues for wide error bars around the $0.39 current-year and $0.61 next-year consensus.

Structural growth drivers that are real: Sample Repository Services at +16.6% with high switching costs, and UK Biocentre positioned as a European operational hub complementing Griesheim. Structural drags that are also real: Sanger sequencing in decline (explicitly cited for reduced North America volumes), Core Products revenue falling, and the long-range plan timeline extended to 2029.

Remaining performance obligations were $64.0M at June 30 ($53.7M within one year), but the company defines RPO to cover only contracts with an original expected term over one year for which fulfillment has started. It is not backlog and should not be read as one.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry backdrop — pharma and biotech R&D discipline, weak instrument capex, pressure on US academic and government funding — is well documented and priced across the group. What is company-specific:

**Sample Repository Services is the franchise and is winning.** 16.6% nine-month growth, genuine switching costs, and the destination for acquisition capital (UK Biocentre, purchased March 4, 2026 for ~$27.5M net of cash acquired but only $11.15M of cash out the door, because $13.84M settled a pre-existing receivable; the allocation was asset-heavy with PP&E of $22.2M and goodwill of just $1.2M). UK long-lived assets went from $14.3M to $43.1M. The coherent strategy, if there is one, is becoming the outsourced biobank of record for global pharma.

**Automated Stores is the self-inflicted problem.** Quality remediation and rework is unusual language for a capital-equipment line, and it is the largest identified driver of a 745bp one-quarter gross-margin collapse in the segment holding $463.1M of goodwill. Core Products revenue is down 2.5% for nine months. Competitors named by the company in automation are Hamilton and Liconic; in storage and consumables, LabCorp and Thermo Fisher.

**Multiomics is structurally disadvantaged but improving.** At roughly $270M of annualized revenue it has never reached profitability against BGI, Novogene, GenScript, IDT, Eurofins and Twist. Q3's 600bp gross-margin gain and near-breakeven operating result is the first evidence that could change, and management's own explanation for prior weakness (fixed-cost absorption on lower North America Sanger volumes) supports a scale reading rather than a pure price-competition reading. One quarter is one data point, and management's full-year Multiomics guide of -1% to flat organic is more conservative than Q3's +8%.

**Regulation cuts both ways.** The BIOSECURE Act and the DOJ bulk sensitive personal data rule (28 CFR Part 202, final December 27, 2024) constrain cross-border genomic data flows and Azenta operates three China labs, but the same rules target BGI and Novogene, two of the four Multiomics competitors Azenta names. Whether Azenta is a net winner depends on how much China lab output serves US versus domestic Chinese customers, which is not disclosed.

Net trajectory: improving in the segment that matters most for long-term value (SRS), deteriorating in the segment carrying the goodwill (Core Products), and inflecting from a low base in the structurally weakest segment. Mixed, with the near-term direction negative in the highest-margin line.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Internal control over financial reporting is not effective, and has not been for two years.** Three material weaknesses were disclosed as of September 30, 2025; as of June 30, 2026 one is remediated and two remain — cash-flow-statement review (open since the FY2024 10-K, ~7 quarters) and account reconciliations (open since the Q2 FY25 10-Q, ~5 quarters). Both disclosure controls and ICFR are stated as not effective. The consequences were not hypothetical: the now-remediated classification weakness forced revisions to FY2023 annual, FY2024 interim and annual and FY2025 Q1-Q3, moving 9M FY25 cost of revenue +$4.07M, gross profit -$4.31M, R&D +$2.20M and SG&A -$5.98M. A separate correction restated disclosed contract-asset revenue for 9M FY25 from $49.4M to $30.5M. The company classifies these as immaterial-error revisions rather than restatements, and specific remediation steps are disclosed, so the direction of travel is positive. But the two areas where controls remain deficient — the cash-flow statement and account reconciliations — are precisely the two areas central to the investment case (segment margin detail and FCF conversion).

**2. The B Medical divestiture structure.** Closed July 1, 2026 for $63.0M total: $28.0M cash received before closing ($9.0M deposit in December 2025, $19.0M advance June 30, 2026) plus a $35.0M Vendor Loan Agreement in which Azenta Germany GmbH is the lender and Thelema S.à r.l. the borrower, at 6.0%, maturing three months after funding (around October 1, 2026), secured by a first-priority pledge over 100% of B Medical's equity. Azenta has pre-agreed to cooperate with prospective third-party lenders such that the pledge "may be subordinated to, or released" in connection with acquisition or refinancing debt. Thelema's majority owner is an individual who served as a Company Vice President and CEO of B Medical through closing; Azenta states he did not participate on the Company's side, that the Audit Committee approved under the related-person transaction policy, and that terms were negotiated through a competitive auction. The most likely reading is that an auction for a deteriorating asset produced one willing buyer whose permanent financing was not in place by the deadline, requiring a short seller bridge. The subordination provision is what keeps a less benign reading open. Magnitude is bounded at ~2.5% of market cap; resolution should be visible in the FY2026 10-K.

**3. Goodwill concentration.** The Q2 FY26 quantitative test — triggered partly by "a sustained decline in its stock price" as of March 31, 2026 — impaired Multiomics by $112.4M and SMS by $36.6M, total $149.1M, leaving $547.5M (29% of assets, 36% of equity), of which $463.1M sits in SMS. A stock-price-triggered test in the quarter the shares bottomed measures sentiment as much as asset quality; the forward risk is that the segment now showing an unresolved quality problem carries 85% of the remaining balance against 9M segment adjusted operating income of $13.0M. The April 1 annual test was qualitative and found no further impairment.

**4. Q4 execution.** The implied Q4 guide is revenue $158.4-163.4M (versus derived Q4 FY25 of $159.2M) and adjusted EBITDA $20.1-23.1M, a 12.6-14.1% margin — the best of the year — on organic revenue guided *down* low single digits. Cost actions must deliver peak margin on a shrinking base, and FCF must swing $32-34M year over year.

**5. Declining interest-income subsidy.** Net interest income has fallen $43.5M → $32.9M → $18.8M → $13.3M (FY23, FY24, FY25, 9M FY26), roughly $30M of annual pre-tax income versus FY2023. Part is rates, part is the balance decline from buybacks. Every dollar of repurchase shrinks the subsidy currently offsetting the operating loss, so underwriting AZTA requires believing operating income turns positive.

**6. Tax receivable.** $45.6M carried, with $20.0M of related claims already reserved and the claim expected to face IRS examination and Joint Committee review.

**Risk removed:** on July 7, 2026 the New York Supreme Court, Commercial Division, dismissed the Edwards Vacuum suit (which sought more than $13.0M) in its entirety, denied leave to amend, and marked the action disposed. Azenta released $1.8M of accrual. Clean.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($41, +30%)

Q3 marks the turn. Multiomics sustains its 600bp gross-margin gain as North America Sanger volumes stabilize and fixed-cost absorption improves, converting a segment that has never earned money at $270M of revenue into a low-single-digit-margin contributor. The SMS rework issue proves transient — a discrete quality remediation on a specific Automated Stores cohort, not a reset — and SMS gross margin returns toward the 50%+ range as Sample Repository Services (+16.6%) becomes a larger share of segment mix, with UK Biocentre operating as the European hub. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin moves from ~10% toward 15% on ~$650M of FY28 revenue, or ~$97M. At 13x EV/EBITDA plus ~$470M of net liquid assets on a share count reduced to ~41M by continued repurchase, that is ~$42/share. The $200M of remaining authorization, executed anywhere near the $21.33 average of the May-June tranche, is materially accretive, and 20.6% short interest amplifies the move. The activist-reconstituted board with a Value Creation Committee, a ROIC and FCF-weighted comp plan, and a director buying 10,000 shares at $16.38 provides the governance mechanism.

### Bear Case ($23, -27%)

Roughly 10% adjusted EBITDA conversion is the structural ceiling for this asset mix. Multiomics competes on price per base against Chinese providers with structurally lower costs and cannot earn an adequate return; Core Products faces a multi-year instrument capex trough with US academic and government funding under pressure; US revenue keeps shrinking (-3.4% for nine months) and the ex-US offset is partly FX. The SMS rework charge proves to be the leading edge of a durable margin reset, triggering a further impairment against $463.1M of segment goodwill. Q4 free cash flow misses the implied $26.5-28.4M requirement, and the FY27 guide lands below the current $0.61 consensus, which has already fallen 12.5% in 90 days. The interest-income subsidy continues to erode roughly $5-8M a year, so consolidated pre-tax income turns negative even on flat operations. At 8-10x on $58M of EBITDA plus ~$490M of net liquid assets, the equity is worth $22-25. The floor is the balance sheet, not the business.

### What Would Change My Mind

The single most decisive datapoint is whether SMS gross margin recovers in Q4 and Q1 FY27. Two consecutive quarters of SMS gross margin back above 48% with the rework cost quantified and closed out would convert the bull case from a hypothesis into a trend, and would substantially de-risk the $463.1M goodwill balance. Two more quarters in the mid-40s would confirm the bear reading.

Probability-weighting these (25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear) gives an expected return of roughly +1%. That is fair value, and it is why the rating is HOLD rather than the ACCUMULATE that would have been appropriate at $21-24 in May.

**Timeframe:** the next hard checkpoint is the Q4/FY2026 report and 10-K, expected in late November 2026. The vendor-loan maturity around October 1, 2026 is an earlier, smaller checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. SMS adjusted gross margin recovers above 48% in Q4 FY26 with the Automated Stores rework cost quantified and stated as complete, *and* Multiomics holds adjusted operating income at or above breakeven — two segment datapoints, both verifiable in the Q4 release.
2. FY26 free cash flow lands at or above $42M (the low end of the 10-15% improvement guide), demonstrating the Q4 conversion is real rather than a plug.
3. Price back toward $25-26 on unchanged or improving guidance, which would restore roughly 30% upside to base case against a bounded asset-value floor. This is the cleanest path to a higher rating.
4. Remediation of both remaining material weaknesses with tested controls, plus an FY27 guide at or above $0.61 of non-GAAP EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY26 free cash flow below $35M — a miss against the improvement guide — which would put the largest guidance credibility question into the 10-K.
2. SMS adjusted gross margin remaining below 46% in Q4, or any incremental goodwill impairment against the $463.1M SMS balance.
3. The $35.0M vendor loan not repaid at or near its ~October 1, 2026 maturity, or disclosure that the equity pledge has been subordinated or released without repayment.
4. A third material weakness identified, or any move from "revision" to "restatement" language.
5. FY27 revenue guidance implying organic decline, which would invalidate the margin-recovery-on-growing-revenue base case.

**Reassessment date:** Q4 FY2026 earnings and 10-K, late November 2026, with an interim check on vendor-loan repayment in early October.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory here has been 4.5 → 4.1 → 4.2 → 4.1 → 4.7, with three AVOIDs followed by a HOLD. The improvement was earned: financial health rose as the debt-free balance sheet and disciplined May-June buyback at $21.33 came into focus, and valuation improved when the shares traded in the low $20s. This cycle adds real positives — a Q3 non-GAAP EPS beat versus the $0.12 consensus (+37.8%), raised and narrowed guidance, the first near-breakeven quarter in Multiomics history, one material weakness remediated, and the Edwards litigation dismissed.
