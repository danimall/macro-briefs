**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bill Holdings is a cloud-based financial-operations platform for small and medium-sized businesses, serving roughly 493,800 direct customers and processing total payment volume equal to about 1% of U.S. GDP. The company is mid-transition from a growth-at-all-costs software model into a margin-and-cash-return business. The evidence on either side of the current ~$32 price is genuinely balanced, which is why this report carries a HOLD rather than a directional call.

The bear case is real and identifiable: direct customer count grew only +1% YoY (488,600 → 493,800), reported revenue growth has decelerated to +13% in the most recent quarter from the 30%+ rates of prior years, float income (~9% of revenue, very high margin) is rolling off as the Fed eases, and the company's single most important integration partner, Intuit, via native QuickBooks bill pay, is also its most dangerous competitor. Truist downgraded the stock on June 10 citing exactly these themes, and short interest sits at 14.7% of float. Severe relative underperformance (indexed TSR of 51 versus a peer-group TSR of 282) has drawn activist involvement and meaningful C-suite turnover, including the departure of the President/COO.

The offsetting bull case is equally concrete: the balance sheet is net-cash ($994.7M cash plus $1.18B short-term investments against $1.4B of 0% convertibles that don't mature until 2030 and are deeply out of the money); free cash flow is real at $258.1M over nine months of FY26 (roughly an 8% FCF yield on market cap); the board authorized a $1.0B buyback in April 2026 (~31% of market cap); the company has beaten EPS estimates by ~13-28% for four consecutive quarters; and next-year EPS estimates have been revised up +24.1% over 90 days. Demand-side revenue lines — subscription plus transaction fees (+16%) and Spend & Expense cards (+21%) — are growing despite the flat logo count. On ~12x forward EPS with the cost cuts not yet fully reflected, the bearish thesis appears largely priced. With fair value bracketing the current price, neither a buy nor an avoid is defensible.

## Company Overview

BILL operates a financial-operations platform that digitizes accounts payable, accounts receivable, spend/expense management, and payments for SMBs. It serves ~493,800 direct businesses plus 8M+ network members and processed roughly $273B of TPV over nine months of FY26. Revenue is organized into three lines:

- **BILL AP/AR** — the core accounts-payable/receivable platform (~$183M Q3 revenue, +10% YoY; the maturing, slowest-growing line).
- **BILL Spend & Expense** (formerly Divvy) — corporate cards generating interchange revenue (~$167M Q3, +21% YoY; the growth engine).
- **Embedded Solutions & Other** — partner channel (Paychex, Oracle NetSuite, Acumatica) plus Invoice2go (~$21M Q3).

The go-to-market moat rests on deep relationships with accounting firms (85+ of the top 100, 9,600+ firm partners, exclusive CPA.com partnership), financial-institution distribution, and integrations with the major accounting software stacks (QuickBooks, NetSuite, Sage Intacct, Xero, Dynamics). The company reports as one segment; under 2% of revenue is international.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (FY ends June 30):** $642M (FY22) → ~$1.06B (FY23) → ~$1.12B (FY24) → ~$1.46B (FY25). Growth has decelerated materially. Q3 FY26 grew +13% YoY to $406.6M; nine-month FY26 revenue was $1.217B (+13%).

**Mix and the float headwind.** Interest on funds held for customers was $35.4M in Q3 (-7% YoY), now 9% of revenue versus 11-12% a year ago, as the float yield compressed to 3.8-4.1% from 4.3-4.6%. This is the unwind of the 2022-23 rate tailwind, a high-margin line declining with no offsetting cost. The underlying demand-side lines are healthier than the headline: subscription plus transaction fees grew +16% and cards +21%. The reported +13% understates organic momentum because the float drag is dilutive to the total.

**Profitability.** BILL reached its first GAAP profitability in FY25 (+$23.8M net income). Q3 FY26 net income was $12.8M, though the operating result is barely breakeven (operating loss of -$0.4M in Q3; -$39.2M over nine months). Nine-month net income fell to $7.2M from $30.9M, but the prior-year period included a one-time $40.6M debt-extinguishment gain, so underlying operations are improving rather than deteriorating. Stock-based compensation remains very high (~$177M over nine months, ~$240M annualized) and is the main GAAP/non-GAAP gap; non-GAAP gross margin is ~85% versus ~81% GAAP.

**Cash flow is the strongest part of the story.** Operating cash flow was $304.8M and FCF $258.1M over nine months FY26, both up YoY and substantially in excess of GAAP net income — high cash-conversion quality. At ~$3.2B market cap this is roughly an 8% FCF yield.

**Balance sheet.** $994.7M cash plus $1.18B short-term investments. Debt is $1.4B of 0% convertible notes due 2030 (conversion price $119.45, far out of the money), $123.5M of 0% 2027 notes, and $330M drawn on receivables-backed facilities. Goodwill is $2.4B. The ~$4.0B of "funds held for customers" is matched by an equal customer-deposit liability (pure pass-through). The Altman Z-Score of ~1.36 ("distress") is a mechanical false signal — current liabilities are inflated by the pass-through customer deposits; the company is net-cash with no near-term solvency risk.

**Capital allocation.** ~$272M repurchased over nine months FY26, and in April 2026 the board authorized a new $1.0B buyback (~31% of market cap), signaling a maturing, cash-return posture. A possible release of the U.S. deferred-tax valuation allowance within 12 months (NOLs of $944M federal, $882M state) would deliver a one-time non-cash EPS benefit.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has two faces. The logo engine has stalled — direct customers grew +1% YoY — and net revenue retention has weakened over time, so growth now depends on monetizing the installed base (cards, ad-valorem payment take rate) rather than adding accounts. That is the legitimate core of the bear thesis and the reason the multiple has compressed.

Against that, the demand-side lines are still growing at double digits. The reported +13% total is depressed by the float roll-off, not by collapsing customer activity. Estimate revisions confirm the underlying momentum: next-year EPS estimates rose +24.1% over 90 days and current-quarter estimates +21.9%, and the beat record has held for four straight quarters. Much of this revision velocity reflects the profitability pivot — the up-to-30% workforce reduction announced May 2026 and the broader cost program flow straight to EPS — but the demand-side line growth means it is not purely cost-cutting. The mix is an efficiency-driven earnings inflection layered on a maturing top line, which justifies a constructive-to-neutral growth view rather than either extreme.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP the stock is meaningless (negative/near-zero EPS, hence the ~20,000x optical P/E). The relevant frame is forward earnings and FCF. Consensus FY (current-year) EPS is $2.64 and next-year $3.34; at ~$32 the stock trades at roughly 12x current-year and under 10x next-year EPS. FCF yield is ~8%. Application Software peers (DLB 21x, BOX 39.5x, APPF 38x, QTWO 38.2x, RNG 40.3x; median ~38x) trade at multiples of BILL's forward P/E. The discount reflects genuine concerns — flat logos, float roll-off, Intuit — but the gap is wide enough that the bearish thesis appears substantially in the price.

A reasonable normalized framing: a low-teens multiple on FY26 EPS of ~$2.64 supports roughly $33-38. That range brackets and slightly exceeds the current ~$32 price, which is the central reason this is a HOLD: the analyst fair-value floor is at or above spot, so the downside case is already discounted. The bull case (Seeking Alpha) of ~1.9x FY28e core revenue and ~8.1x adjusted FCF describes a depressed multiple, and the upside scenario keys off margin expansion plus the deferred-tax release. The valuation is fair, not stretched and not obviously cheap on a risk-adjusted basis given the deceleration. I am deliberately cautious about over-rewarding the optical multiple here, because a low forward P/E on a decelerating franchise can be a value trap absent a catalyst; the catalyst that exists (cost-out + buyback) is real but bounded.

## Competitive Landscape

BILL's moat is its accounting-firm distribution and its integrations into the major software stacks, reinforced by a two-sided network. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating at the margin, which is why this dimension scores below midpoint. Intuit launched native QuickBooks bill pay in October 2023; Brex and Ramp have entered bill pay and spend management. The structural vulnerability is that Intuit is simultaneously BILL's most important integration partner and its most dangerous competitor.

One point the bear case overstates: that displacement has been live for ~2.5 years, yet BILL grew to $1.46B FY25 revenue and reached GAAP profitability across that window. The competitive threat is real and the +1% logo growth is consistent with share pressure, but the franchise has not been impaired by it so far — the AP/AR core still grew +10% and cards +21%. AI cuts both ways: it lowers entry barriers for challengers but also powers BILL's own defensive product velocity (BILL AI agents for W-9 collection, reconciliation, invoice coding; BILL Travel; Cash Accounts). The competitive position is eroding slowly rather than breaking, which supports caution rather than an avoid.

## Risk Assessment

Key company-specific risks, several newly elevated in recent filings:

1. **Growth deceleration / flat logos** — +1% customer growth; monetization-dependent growth model.
2. **Float-income decline** — high-margin ~9% of revenue rolling off with falling rates.
3. **Intensifying competition** — Intuit native bill pay, Brex, Ramp; partner-as-competitor risk.
4. **Card credit risk** — BILL bears full loss on ~$819M of card receivables; provisions rose +26% YoY on growth and rising delinquencies, an early SMB-stress indicator.
5. **Funding/interchange concentration** — reliance on warehouse facilities and Issuing Banks (Cross River, WEX, WebBank); interchange exposed to regulatory/network litigation.
6. **Restructuring execution** — up-to-30% headcount cut risks talent and growth capability loss.
7. **Activist-driven strategy and governance churn** — dedicated activism risk factor, $9.1M advisory costs, COO departure, repeated Item 5.02 8-Ks.
8. **Convertible refinancing** — $1.4B due 2030; conversion price far above stock implies likely cash settlement.

These risks are largely known and reflected in the depressed multiple and 14.7% short interest. The offsetting downside floor — net cash, ~8% FCF yield, a buyback at ~31% of cap, and plausible takeout appeal — limits the tail. That is why risk factors score near the midpoint: meaningful idiosyncratic risks exist, but a solvency or terminal-decline scenario is not supported.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 58.3% against 1-year HV of 59.8% — options are fairly priced, not signaling an imminent catalyst from the front month. The put/call OI ratio rose to 1.81 (from 1.04 a month ago) and the put/call skew is elevated at 6.3%, consistent with the short interest and Truist downgrade — the market is positioned cautiously, confirming the bearish lean without implying a specific dated event. The 3-month tenor shows a steep -12.4% put skew, suggesting hedging into the Q4 FY26 print (restructuring charges, fresh guidance). The reported 291.5% ATM IV in the delta snapshot is a thin-options artifact and is treated as non-informative; the term-structure table (58-62% across tenors, contango) is the credible read and indicates normal/complacent vol pricing. None of this overrides the fundamental balance; it reinforces caution rather than conviction in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$45-55).** The profitability pivot works: the up-to-30% RIF (the stock rose +7.5% on the announcement) and broader cost-out drive operating margin expansion; the $1.0B buyback shrinks share count meaningfully at a depressed price; the deferred-tax valuation-allowance release adds a one-time EPS boost; cards and subscription/transaction fees continue +16-21% growth; and the multiple re-rates toward peers as the market credits the cash-return, margin story. Next-year EPS of $3.34 at a ~15x multiple supports the low-$50s, with analyst targets ranging to $77.

**Bear case (~$25-28).** Logo growth stays near zero and net revenue retention erodes further; Intuit/Brex/Ramp compress the AP/AR core; the float headwind persists; SMB card delinquencies rise into a consumer-cliff environment, forcing higher provisions; and cost cuts hollow out the growth capability needed to re-accelerate. The multiple stays at ~10x or de-rates further on revenue disappointment.

The two cases roughly offset, and the fair-value range ($33-38) sits at or above spot, which is a balanced setup.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4 FY26 earnings (expected early August 2026), which will show the restructuring charges ($30-60M), updated FY27 guidance, and the first clear view of post-RIF margin structure. The deferred-tax valuation-allowance decision (possible within 12 months) and any further activist-driven board/management changes are interim catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence the cost-out is structural margin expansion, not just one-time — e.g., FY27 guidance implying non-GAAP operating margin stepping up several hundred bps with stabilized revenue growth (≥13-15%).
- Re-acceleration in logo growth or net revenue retention, or a clear take-rate inflection that pushes total revenue growth back toward the high teens.
- A buyback execution pace that materially shrinks share count (e.g., >8-10% of float retired) at prices near current levels, plus the deferred-tax release confirmed.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Logo count turning negative or card-receivable delinquencies/provisions accelerating sharply (a credit-loss spike that consumes the FCF cushion).
- Revenue growth decelerating below ~8% YoY, signaling the demand-side lines are cracking rather than offsetting the float drag.
- Evidence the 30% RIF impaired the growth/product engine (product velocity stalls, further unplanned executive departures, guidance withdrawn).

## Conclusion

BILL is a maturing platform business in the middle of a deliberate shift from growth to cash generation and margin. The franchise is not impaired: it is net-cash, generates ~$258M of nine-month FCF, has bought back stock aggressively, beats estimates consistently, and is plausibly a takeout candidate. But growth has genuinely decelerated, logos are flat, float income is rolling off, and a powerful partner-competitor (Intuit) is encroaching. With the stock at ~$32 and a defensible fair-value range of $33-38 bracketing that price, the bearish thesis is already discounted and the bullish catalysts (cost-out, buyback, tax-asset release) are bounded. This is a HOLD: the evidence on both sides is real and roughly cancels, and the next earnings print is the natural point to resolve which side wins. The constructive lean comes from the cash and capital-return floor; the caution comes from the deteriorating competitive and growth trajectory.
