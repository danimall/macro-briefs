## Executive Summary

Insperity is a Professional Employer Organization (PEO) serving small and medium-sized businesses that has experienced a catastrophic four-year margin erosion, with operating income declining from $250M (2022) to a $10M loss (2025). The proximate cause is a pricing-cost mismatch in health insurance: benefits costs rose 9.2% in 2025 while client pricing increased only 3%. Q1 2026 showed modest improvement (5% benefit cost growth, $1.31 adjusted EPS beating consensus), but FY2026 guidance implies declining worksite employee counts (-1 to -2.3%) and full-year adjusted EPS of only $1.60-$2.60, reflecting a business still far from historical profitability norms.

The most compelling counterargument to the bearish case is the extraordinary insider buying: CEO Sarvadi purchased approximately $9M in open-market shares at $19-$30 in early 2026, joined by the CFO, General Counsel, and four independent directors. This is a textbook cluster buy signal. However, insider conviction alone cannot override the fundamental reality of declining scale (worksite employees guided down), heavily negative estimate revisions (-16% to -20% over 90 days), three consecutive quarterly EPS misses, and a business model structurally exposed to healthcare cost inflation with limited pricing power and 30-day client cancellation provisions. The options market is pricing near-term IV at 117% (vs. 67% historical) in steep backwardation, consistent with continued acute stress.

## Company Overview

Insperity operates a co-employment model where it becomes the employer of record for client worksite employees (WSEEs), providing payroll, health benefits, workers' compensation, compliance, and HR services. The company manages approximately 310,000 WSEEs across 73 offices in 44 markets. Revenue of $6.8B (FY2025) represents the gross markup portion of $45.6B in total billings. The business is concentrated in Texas (17% of WSEEs), California (15%), and New York (10%), with approximately 85% of health coverage through UnitedHealthcare under a partially self-funded arrangement.

The company recently launched HRScale (a Workday-based joint solution targeting larger middle-market clients) and renegotiated its UnitedHealthcare contract to reduce the pooling level from $1M to $500K per claimant (effective January 2026), which should partially mitigate benefits cost volatility.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory shows progressive deterioration:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|------|
| Revenue ($B) | 5.94 | 6.49 | 6.58 | 6.81 |
| Gross Profit ($M) | 1,011 | 1,037 | 1,052 | 900 |
| Operating Income ($M) | 250 | 219 | 117 | (10) |
| Gross Profit/WSEE/Month | ~$277 | ~$277 | ~$285 | $242 |

The progression from $250M operating income to a loss in four years reflects two distinct problems: (1) the 2024 decline was driven by $117M in incremental operating expenses, largely Workday partnership development costs; (2) the 2025 collapse was caused by the 9.2% health benefit cost surge against only 3% pricing growth.

**Balance sheet:** Stockholders' equity is only $46M, distorted by cumulative share repurchases (large treasury stock position). Debt stands at $369M against a $750M revolving facility. Adjusted cash (after removing pass-through payroll obligations) was only $57M at year-end 2025 and $36M at Q1 2026. Debt/equity at 47.9x appears alarming but is partially an artifact of the asset-light PEO model where massive payroll liabilities flow through the balance sheet.

**Q1 2026 recovery signals:** Revenue grew 2% YoY to $1.9B. Gross profit declined only 3% (vs. -21% in Q4 2025). Benefits cost trend moderated to 5%. Adjusted EBITDA of $103M grew 1% YoY. However, Q1 is seasonally Insperity's strongest quarter (payroll tax wage bases not yet reached, deductibles not yet met). Full-year EBITDA guidance of $170-$230M implies Q2-Q4 average of only $22-$42M per quarter.

**Operating cash flow:** The reported -$278M in FY2025 is distorted by $440M in Employee Retention Credit distributions to clients (a timing item from funds received late 2024). Normalized cash generation is positive but compressed.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are constrained across multiple dimensions:

**WSEEs declining:** The company guides for 303K-307K average WSEEs in 2026, down from 310K in 2025. This represents the core operating metric declining at a time when the business needs scale to absorb fixed overhead.

**Sales force reduced 20%:** Average Business Performance Advisors dropped from 769 to 612 (and trained BPAs from 698 to 599) in 2025. This constrains new client acquisition for 12-18 months.

**Estimate revisions severely negative:** FY2026 EPS consensus has fallen from $2.44 to $2.05 over 90 days (-16%). FY2027 estimates declined from $3.51 to $2.80 (-20%). Current quarter estimates were cut 21% in the last 30 days alone. This is active negative earnings momentum.

**HRScale is early-stage:** The Workday partnership product onboarded its first clients in Q1 2026. Even if successful, revenue contribution is minimal for 12-18 months. Cumulative development costs already exceed $49M (2024-2025).

**Industry context:** PEO penetration of eligible SMBs remains ~15-16%, offering long-term TAM expansion. But Insperity's current execution problems (pricing lag, WSEE attrition, sales force contraction) prevent it from capturing this opportunity in the near term.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.30 with ~38.2M shares, Insperity's market cap is approximately $1.23B. Adding $369M debt less ~$57M cash yields an EV of approximately $1.55B.

Against FY2026 EBITDA guidance midpoint of $200M, the stock trades at ~7.7x EV/EBITDA. Against consensus EPS of $2.05, it trades at ~15.8x forward earnings.

Peer context: Robert Half trades at 20.2x P/E, Korn Ferry at 13.1x, Paylocity at 24.1x. NSP's ~16x on recovering (but still depressed and declining) estimates is not cheap enough to compensate for the execution risk. Peers' earnings are relatively stable, while NSP's estimates are actively being revised downward.

If full margin recovery materializes and FY2027 EBITDA approaches $250-$300M (management's implicit aspiration based on historical levels), the stock is undervalued at 5-6x forward EBITDA. However, the earnings decline trajectory and multiple consecutive misses create a substantial probability-weighting problem: the recovery path requires benefits cost moderation AND pricing catch-up AND WSEE stabilization — three conditions that must hold simultaneously.

The stock traded at $120-$150 in 2023 before margin deterioration became apparent, representing a >75% decline. The magnitude of the drawdown reflects both cyclical earnings compression and a structural re-evaluation of the business model's risk profile.

## Competitive Landscape

Insperity maintains the highest gross profit per WSEE in the PEO industry (113% above average) and the highest staff support ratio (55% above average). These metrics reflect genuine service quality differentiation in an industry where the primary competitor is "do it yourself" internal HR.

However, several structural challenges are emerging:

1. **Low-cost PEO competition:** Rippling, Vensure, and others offer reduced-service PEO at lower price points. NSP's 16% Select Edition penetration acknowledges demand for cheaper options.

2. **AI disruption:** Newly disclosed as a risk factor — AI may reduce clients' employee counts (shrinking the WSEE pool) or enable competitors to offer comparable services at lower cost. This is a structural threat to the high-touch, high-staff-ratio model.

3. **Pricing power limitation:** The 2025 experience demonstrated that when healthcare costs spike, Insperity cannot immediately pass through increases due to competitive dynamics and annual renewal cycles. This creates inherent earnings volatility embedded in the business model.

4. **UnitedHealthcare concentration:** 85% of health coverage through a single insurer creates counterparty concentration. The Chubb workers' comp contract expires September 2026 and must be renewed or replaced.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Benefits cost re-acceleration (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** The moderation to 5% in Q1 2026 is encouraging but Q1 is seasonally favorable (deductibles not met). If H2 costs re-accelerate toward 7-8%, the margin recovery thesis fails.

2. **WSEE count decline accelerates (MODERATE probability, HIGH impact):** If macro deterioration causes SMB client failures or hiring freezes, WSEEs could decline beyond the guided -1 to -2.3%. The 30-day cancellation provision makes attrition rapid.

3. **Liquidity tightness (LOW-MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact):** $36M adjusted cash against $369M debt with a $750M revolver provides runway but no cushion for unexpected cost shocks.

4. **ERC clawback risk (LOW probability, MODERATE impact):** If Employee Retention Credit claims are disallowed, Insperity may face penalties or client recourse.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 116.9% is 49.6 percentage points above 67.3% historical volatility — the richest premium observed across most mid-cap industrials. The term structure is in steep backwardation (117% near-term vs. 71% long-dated), indicating the market expects an imminent resolution event (positive or negative) rather than prolonged stability. Put/call volume ratio of 3.50 and 21.7% put skew confirm elevated directional bearish positioning.

Combined with 13.1% short interest (3.5 days to cover), institutional positioning is decidedly bearish despite the insider buying. This divergence between management conviction and market positioning is significant — typically credit-informed participants (who see the balance sheet up close) are the ones buying.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($45-$54; +40-67%)
The Q1 2026 beat confirmed that the UnitedHealthcare contract renegotiation is working. Benefits cost growth moderates to 4-5% in H2 while pricing catches up. FY2026 EBITDA hits the high end of guidance ($230M). WSEE declines stabilize. HRScale generates early traction. The stock re-rates toward 10x EBITDA ($230M × 10 = $2.3B EV → ~$50 stock). CEO's $9M bet pays off.

### Bear Case ($15-$20; -38-54%)
Benefits costs re-accelerate in H2 as deductibles are met and large claims surge. WSEE count declines faster than guided as SMB clients face macro headwinds. Estimates continue falling. The Chubb workers' comp renewal comes at unfavorable terms. The stock re-tests March 2026 lows near $18-$20.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** H2 2026 earnings reports (likely August and November 2026). Q1 is seasonally best; Q3-Q4 are seasonally worst due to benefits cost stacking. If Q2-Q4 EBITDA averages only $22-42M per quarter (as implied by guidance), any negative surprise on benefits costs could produce operating losses in Q3/Q4. The Chubb workers' comp contract renewal (September 2026) is a secondary catalyst.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. Q2 results (August) will reveal whether benefits cost moderation persists beyond the favorable Q1 seasonal window.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 13.1% SI with 3.5 days to cover is elevated but not extreme. Squeeze risk is MODERATE — the CEO buying creates headline risk for shorts on any positive surprise. Low float liquidity (avg volume 853K shares) means short covering events can be sharp.

**Borrow and options signal:** Near-term IV at 117% makes put purchases expensive relative to realized volatility. The options market already prices the bearish thesis, reducing asymmetry for new shorts. A defined-risk put spread at the 71% longer-dated IV would be more capital-efficient than an outright short position.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $15-20 (-38 to -54% from $32.30). Upside risk (squeeze to $45-54, or +40-67%). Risk/reward approximately 1:1 to 1.5:1 at current levels — below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis (benefits cost re-acceleration, WSEE declines) is correct directionally but: (a) risk/reward at current levels is unfavorable due to 50% decline already realized and extraordinary insider buying creating headline squeeze risk, (b) options are too expensive to express the view cheaply, and (c) the Q1 2026 beat creates uncertainty about near-term trajectory. The correct expression is avoiding ownership, not actively shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Two consecutive quarters demonstrating benefits cost growth ≤5% AND gross profit per WSEE recovery toward $270/month (vs. $242 in 2025)
2. WSEE count stabilization or positive growth on a trailing 3-month basis
3. Estimate revisions turning positive (30-day revision trend > 0%)
4. Stock price below $25 on a confirmed benefit cost recovery (creating genuine valuation asymmetry)

**Timeline:** The headwinds are structural (healthcare cost inflation, sales force reduction, AI displacement risk) and will persist 12-18 months minimum. Reassess after Q3 2026 results (November 2026), which will provide the first seasonally challenging test of the margin recovery thesis.

## Conclusion

Insperity presents an investable situation in theory — a historically profitable business with a strong competitive position, temporarily distressed by a cost estimation failure, with extraordinary insider buying signaling management confidence. In practice, the thesis requires simultaneous resolution of: (1) healthcare cost moderation sustaining through unfavorable H2 seasonality, (2) WSEE count stabilization despite guided declines and reduced sales capacity, and (3) estimate revisions bottoming despite four consecutive downward revisions.

The stock has declined 55% from its 52-week high and the market has repriced it aggressively. The bull case must explain what sophisticated short sellers (13.1% SI) are missing. The answer — "management knows the insurance contract fix is working" — has merit but requires 2-3 more quarters of evidence to confirm. At current prices (~16x declining consensus estimates, ~7.7x guided EBITDA on a business with demonstrated earnings volatility), the stock does not offer sufficient margin of safety against continued estimate deterioration. The insider buying is compelling but does not override negative fundamental momentum, declining unit volumes, an extremely volatile cost structure, and an options market pricing acute near-term risk.

Rating: AVOID. Monitor for re-entry if H2 2026 confirms the margin recovery thesis.
