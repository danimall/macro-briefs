## Executive Summary

Sterling Infrastructure has completed one of the most successful corporate transformations in the mid-cap industrial space over the past decade: from a distressed, low-margin highway contractor with -4.9% operating margins in 2015 to a diversified infrastructure services company generating 16.3% operating margins and 27.9% ROE in fiscal 2025. The E-Infrastructure segment — focused on site development and now electrical/mechanical services for data centers — drove 59% revenue growth in 2025 and constitutes 75% of operating income, making STRL a leveraged play on the hyperscaler AI capex buildout.

The financial profile is compelling: $3.01B in signed backlog (up 78% YoY) with an additional $1B+ of future project phases not yet counted, a net cash balance sheet, $363M in free cash flow (14.6% FCF margin), and 2026 guidance implying 22-29% revenue growth with further margin expansion. Three of the last four quarters delivered earnings beats of 15-24%, and full-year estimates have been revised up 12.8% over 90 days.

The constraint is valuation. At ~$516, the stock trades at ~55x trailing EPS and ~38x FY2026E adjusted earnings, prices that embed multi-year growth perfection and leave minimal margin of safety. CEO Joseph Cutillo has sold $69M in stock since March 2026. Options markets price 84.5% near-term IV in steep backwardation, indicating the market perceives near-term event risk. The growth thesis is real and the business quality is high, but the current price reflects it.

## Company Overview

Sterling Infrastructure operates through three segments serving distinct end markets:

**E-Infrastructure Solutions (59% of 2025 revenue, 75% of segment operating income):** Large-scale site development and, following the September 2025 CEC Facilities Group acquisition ($562M), specialty electrical/mechanical contracting for mission-critical facilities. Customers include hyperscaler data center operators, semiconductor fabrication plants, advanced manufacturing, and e-commerce distribution. This segment grew revenue from $924M to $1,467M (+59%) in 2025, with 40% organic growth and $170M from CEC's 4-month contribution.

**Transportation Solutions (26% of revenue):** Highway, bridge, airport, port, and drainage projects funded primarily by federal/state IIJA appropriations ($643B through FY2026). Sterling has strategically reduced low-bid heavy highway from 79% of revenue in 2016 to 9% in 2025, shifting toward higher-margin alternative delivery and design-build contracts. Operating margins expanded from 6.5% to 12.1% in 2025.

**Building Solutions (15% of revenue):** Residential and commercial concrete foundations, plumbing, and surveying in DFW, Houston, Phoenix, and Oklahoma. Currently experiencing cyclical weakness: revenue declined 6.3% YoY and operating margin compressed 300bps to 10.2% on housing affordability headwinds.

The company is headquartered in The Woodlands, TX and trades on NASDAQ with approximately 30.7M shares outstanding and a $15.9B market capitalization.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from $1.77B (2022) → $1.97B (2023) → $2.12B (2024) → $2.49B (2025), with 2026 guidance of $3.05-3.20B implying 22-29% growth. Excluding the RHB deconsolidation ($236M removed from the 2024 base), organic+acquired growth in 2025 was 32%.

Gross margin expanded from 15.5% (2022) to 23.0% (2025). Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 20.2% (up from 16.3% in 2024), with 2026 guidance targeting 20-21%. The margin-in-backlog figure of 17.8% (up from 16.7%) indicates the current revenue mix is locked in at structurally higher margins than historical averages.

Operating cash flow of $440M and free cash flow of $363M (14.6% FCF margin) in 2025 demonstrate real earnings quality. The Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 on available signals confirms this — OCF exceeds net income, a positive quality indicator. ROE of 27.9% and ROA of 11.8% indicate efficient capital deployment.

Net cash of ~$98M at year-end 2025 after spending $482M on acquisitions (primarily CEC). Total debt of $293M against cash of $391M. The credit facility was amended in June 2025, extending maturity to 2028 with a $300M term loan and $150M revolver. The Altman Z-Score of 2.48 (grey zone) is misleadingly low, driven by thin working capital ratios common to construction companies with large contract liabilities and by goodwill/intangibles from the CEC acquisition ($1.14B, 43% of total assets).

**Earnings quality check:** Adjusted diluted EPS of $10.88 vs. GAAP of $9.38 in 2025 — a $1.50 gap driven primarily by amortization of acquired intangibles from CEC and transaction costs. The gap should narrow in 2026 as one-time acquisition costs fall away, though ongoing amortization of $228M in CEC intangibles (25-year useful life ≈ $9M/year pre-tax) will persist.

**2026 Guidance:** Revenue $3.05-3.20B, adjusted EBITDA $626-659M, GAAP net income $365-384M, adjusted EPS $13.45-14.05. This reflects CEC contributing a full twelve months vs. four in 2025.

## Growth Analysis

Sterling's growth thesis rests on three pillars with different durations and certainty levels:

**Pillar 1: Data Center / AI Infrastructure (high certainty, multi-year).** Hyperscaler capex grew 57% in 2025 per Dell'Oro Group. Sterling's data center customers have "multi-year capital deployment plans." The CEC acquisition extended Sterling's addressable scope from site development into electrical/mechanical fit-out, expanding revenue per project and creating integrated switching costs. The $3.01B signed backlog (78% YoY growth), with 70% expected to convert within 12 months, provides 12-month revenue visibility. The additional $1B+ of future project phases not yet in backlog provides medium-term visibility.

**Pillar 2: Transportation Infrastructure (moderate certainty, policy-dependent).** IIJA funding runs through FY2026, supporting near-term visibility. Sterling's shift toward higher-margin alternative delivery and design-build contracts (from 21% to 91% of transportation revenue since 2016) means each dollar of revenue is worth more. Post-IIJA funding remains uncertain, though strong state-level programs in Utah, Arizona, Colorado, and Nevada provide a partial floor.

**Pillar 3: Housing Recovery (low certainty, cyclical).** Building Solutions is a drag at present. Housing affordability constraints limit near-term recovery. However, Sterling operates in structurally undersupplied Sun Belt markets with population growth. This segment could inflect positively if mortgage rates decline, but recovery timing remains uncertain.

**Estimate revisions confirm momentum.** Current-year EPS consensus has been revised up 12.8% over 90 days ($12.04 → $13.59). Next-year estimates are up 8.1% ($14.75 → $15.95). The 30-day revisions are flattish (current quarter -1.7%, current year +0.5%), suggesting the bulk of the upward revision cycle from CEC's full-year contribution has been absorbed into estimates.

**Earnings surprise record supports confidence.** Three of the last four quarters exceeded estimates by 15-24%. The lone miss (Q1 2025, -7%) occurred in the seasonally weakest quarter. This pattern suggests management and Street estimates are conservatively set, providing a cushion.

To justify $516 at 25x EBITDA on FY2026E, one must assume: (a) the $640M midpoint EBITDA is achieved, (b) the multiple is sustained or expanded despite being elevated for a construction-adjacent business, and (c) growth continues at 20%+ beyond 2026. If growth decelerates to 10-15% post-CEC full integration (losing the acquisition-driven step-up), a 20x EBITDA multiple would be more typical, implying ~$410 (-20%). If the bull case plays out — sustained 20%+ growth through 2028 on continued data center buildout — 25x FY2027E EBITDA could support $600-650.

The 245% price appreciation over the trailing year reflects the market's rapid repricing of STRL from a construction company to an AI infrastructure beneficiary. That repricing is largely complete. From here, the stock needs to grow into its multiple, which requires sustained 20%+ earnings growth.

## Competitive Landscape

Sterling occupies a defensible mid-market niche, and the competitive position is improving for several reasons:

**Integrated capability post-CEC.** The ability to offer site development and electrical/mechanical services under one contract creates genuine switching costs. For hyperscaler clients building time-sensitive, mission-critical facilities, having a single contractor responsible for multiple phases reduces coordination risk and schedule uncertainty. This advantage was acquired through CEC and strengthens the moat.

**Execution track record on mission-critical projects.** Data center operators prioritize schedule assurance above cost — a day of delay on a multi-hundred-million-dollar facility costs far more than the contractor premium. Sterling's reputation for on-time delivery in this domain is an earned competitive asset that takes years for competitors to build.

**Strategic exit from low-bid markets.** The reduction of low-bid heavy highway from 79% to 9% of revenue has removed the structurally competitive segment where Sterling had no differentiation. This is a rare example of disciplined business portfolio management in the construction sector.

**Fragmented competition.** Sterling competes against a mix of small local contractors (lacking scale for large projects) and international companies (lacking local presence and mid-market focus). This fragmentation limits pricing pressure in Sterling's core segments.

The primary risk to competitive positioning is concentration: 59% of revenue and 75% of operating income from a single segment serving a narrow set of hyperscaler customers.

## Risk Assessment

**Data center capex cyclicality.** The E-Infrastructure segment's dependence on hyperscaler capital spending is the primary risk. While current capex plans span multiple years, any retrenchment in data center spending would hit Sterling's highest-margin, fastest-growing segment disproportionately. The fixed-price nature of contracts means Sterling bears cost overrun risk on increasingly complex, large-scale projects.

**Customer concentration within E-Infrastructure.** The top 4 E-Infrastructure customers represent 27% of segment revenue (~16% of total revenue). Loss of a major hyperscaler relationship would be material and difficult to replace quickly.

**CEC integration execution.** The $562M acquisition added $305M of goodwill and $228M of intangibles. Purchase price allocation is still preliminary. An $80M earn-out creates contingent liability (total company earn-out liabilities of $57M on the balance sheet). Integration of operations, systems, and personnel is in its early stages.

**CEO insider selling.** Joseph Cutillo sold 152,408 shares for approximately $69M between March 9 and April 23, 2026, at prices of $408-498. He retains ~290,593 shares (~$145M), so his alignment remains meaningful. However, $69M of selling during a rapid stock appreciation warrants monitoring. No open-market purchases from any insider were observed. The new CFO (Nicholas Grindstaff, appointed mid-2025) holds only ~6,600 shares.

**IIJA funding expiration.** Transportation Solutions (26% of revenue) depends on federal funding that runs through FY2026. The post-IIJA funding environment is uncertain.

**Labor and immigration risk.** The 10-K explicitly states reliance on immigrant labor with uncertainty about documentation status. Policy changes could constrain labor availability across all segments.

**Housing downturn persistence.** Building Solutions margin compression could deepen if mortgage rates remain elevated.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$600-650, +16-26%)
- Data center capex sustains 20%+ growth through 2028 as AI infrastructure buildout extends beyond current hyperscaler plans
- CEC integration delivers cross-selling synergies ahead of schedule; full-year CEC contribution lifts 2026 EBITDA above guidance
- The $1B+ of future project phases converts to signed awards, extending visibility to 2028+
- Building Solutions recovers as housing affordability improves, adding a third growth vector
- Valuation premium sustained as the market continues pricing STRL as an AI infrastructure pure-play

### Bear Case (~$350-380, -27-32%)
- Hyperscaler capex slows due to AI monetization concerns or macro deceleration; E-Infrastructure backlog growth decelerates
- CEC integration challenges surface (customer overlap, personnel departures, cost overruns on larger combined projects)
- Fixed-price contract margins compress as labor and material costs rise
- IIJA funding lapses without renewal; Transportation Solutions enters volume decline
- Multiple compresses from 25x to 18-20x EV/EBITDA as growth premium is reassessed

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers to BUY (any two of three):**

2. Q1 2026 earnings (expected May 2026) show E-Infrastructure backlog growth sustaining above 30% YoY on an organic basis, confirming that CEC cross-selling is driving incremental demand beyond the acquired base
3. Insider selling ceases and at least one C-suite officer makes an open-market purchase

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one):**
1. E-Infrastructure backlog declines sequentially in any reported quarter, signaling deceleration in data center demand
2. Two consecutive quarters of E-Infrastructure operating margin below 20%, indicating pricing pressure or cost overruns on larger integrated projects
3. Loss of a top-4 E-Infrastructure customer or announcement of hyperscaler capex cuts directly impacting Sterling's project pipeline

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q1 FY2026, expected late May 2026). The first full quarter with CEC fully integrated will be the most informative data point on both organic growth trajectory and integration execution.

## Conclusion

Sterling Infrastructure has built a genuinely differentiated business through disciplined strategic transformation and timely M&A. The E-Infrastructure segment's positioning within the data center buildout cycle is well-timed and structurally sound. The financial execution — 78% backlog growth, 20.2% EBITDA margins, $363M FCF on a net cash balance sheet — is difficult to criticize.

The issue is price. At ~$516 (~38x FY2026E adjusted earnings, ~55x trailing), the stock is priced for continued 20%+ growth with flawless integration execution. The analyst consensus median target of $500 is below the current price. CEO selling and options market backwardation both suggest elevated near-term risk relative to the stock's position just below its all-time high. The 2.3% FCF yield provides minimal downside protection if growth decelerates.

The correct posture is to hold existing positions for the continued growth trajectory while waiting for either a better entry point or incremental data confirming that growth can sustain 20%+ beyond the CEC integration tailwind. Initiating new positions at current levels offers an unfavorable risk/reward skew.

**Recommendation: HOLD** — Growth trajectory supports continued ownership; valuation prevents new entry.
