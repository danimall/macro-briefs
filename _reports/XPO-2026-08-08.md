## Executive Summary

XPO's Q2 2026 print was the best quarter in the company's history as a pure freight operator: revenue $2,355M (+13.2%), net income $162M (+52.8%), GAAP diluted EPS $1.36 versus $0.89, adjusted diluted EPS $1.70 against a non-GAAP consensus of $1.49, and a North American LTL adjusted operating ratio of 79.9% versus 82.9% a year earlier. Management guided Q3 LTL OR below 81% and reaffirmed at least 200 basis points of full-year margin improvement. Consensus has followed: current-year EPS has been revised from $4.85 to $5.44 over 90 days (+12.2%) and next-year from $5.97 to $6.41 (+7.4%), with four consecutive beats averaging roughly 11% in surprise magnitude.

The problem is the composition of that margin gain. Working from the Q2 10-Q LTL segment schedule, fuel-surcharge revenue rose $131M (from $183M to $314M, +71.6%) while the "fuel, operating expenses and supplies" line rose $53M, a $78M net spread against a $76M increase in LTL adjusted operating income. On a half-year basis the spread was $97M against $108M of improvement. Stripping fuel out, ex-fuel LTL revenue grew $57M in Q2 against $46M of higher salaries/wages/benefits and $8M of purchased transportation, leaving roughly $3M of incremental contribution. For H1 the fuel-independent improvement works out to approximately $12-24M, not the headline $108M. The bundled cost line prevents a precise measurement, and genuine productivity gains may be suppressing growth inside that same line, which is management's implicit framing, but the direction of the adjustment is unambiguous, and XPO does not disclose an ex-fuel operating ratio that would settle it.

At $202.58 the stock is 37.2x the $5.44 current-year consensus, 31.6x the $6.41 next-year consensus, and 19.0x LTM adjusted EBITDA on an enterprise value of $26.7B. That is a price at which the durable, non-fuel portion of the improvement must compound for several more years. XPO grew tonnage 1.0% and shipments 2.8% while Old Dominion's tonnage declined, which supports a share-gain thesis that is not fuel-dependent. It does not, at this multiple, produce compelling asymmetry. Base case $220 (+8.6%), bull $275 (+35.7%), bear $140 (-30.9%). Maintaining HOLD: the operating thesis is intact and improving, the entry price is not.

## Company Overview

XPO runs two reportable segments. North American LTL is the value driver: more than 300 service centers reaching 99% of U.S. ZIP codes, roughly 38,000 shippers, an in-house trailer manufacturing plant and company-operated CDL driver schools (400+ graduates in 2025). The FY2025 10-K sizes the U.S. LTL market at approximately $52 billion with XPO at roughly 9% share, ranked #4 in LTL by Transport Topics. European Transportation spans dedicated truckload, LTL/pallet networks, brokerage and managed transportation, with claimed #1 positions in French FTL brokerage and pallet networks, Iberian FTL and LTL, and UK warehousing. FY2025 revenue was $8,157M, split roughly 59% North America, 16% France, 14% UK, 11% rest of Europe.

Three structural assets matter for the thesis. First, deliberate excess capacity: XPO states it has built over 30% excess door capacity, adding more than 2,000 net doors since Q4 2021 plus 19,100+ trailers and 6,000+ tractors. In LTL, door count is the binding constraint on volume, so pre-built capacity converts incremental freight at high marginal margin. Second, the $870M purchase of 28 Yellow service centers (26 owned, 2 leased) in the first bankruptcy auction — irreplaceable real estate concentrated in the South, Southeast, Southwest, Nashville, Atlanta, Texas and the Midwest, roughly half relocations to larger terminals and half incremental market adds. Third, linehaul insourcing: outsourced linehaul fell to 5.1% of miles in Q4 2025, and LTL purchased transportation is now $40M on $1,428M of Q2 segment revenue (2.8%).

Customer concentration is genuinely low: top five customers approximately 8% of global revenue, largest under 3%; within LTL, top five under 11%. None of the North American workforce is unionized, with employees voting against Teamsters representation in 19 of 28 elections since 2014. In Europe, roughly 85% of employees are covered by collective bargaining, a cost-flexibility gap that partly explains the segment margin differential.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2025 was a trough; 2026 is a recovery of contested quality

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $7,744M | $8,072M | $8,157M (+1.1%) |
| Operating income | $438M | $660M | $656M (-0.6%) |
| Net income | $189M | $387M | $316M (-18.3%) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $996M | $1,266M | $1,272M (+0.5%) |
| Operating cash flow | — | $808M | $986M |
| Capex | — | $789M | $657M |

FY2025 LTL volumes were poor: tonnage per day -6.2%, shipments per day -4.1%, offset by ex-fuel yield +6.0%, with LTL revenue still down 1.4% to $4,832M. Insurance and claims rose 24.6% to $167M and D&A to $521M from $490M; the year also absorbed a $35M Pre-Con-way environmental charge and $59M of restructuring.

Q2 2026 reversed the volume trend. Revenue $2,355M (+13.2%), LTL revenue $1,428M (+15.2%), LTL adjusted OR 79.9% (-300bp), LTL adjusted EBITDA ex-real-estate gains $381M (+27%), tonnage/day +1.0%, shipments/day +2.8%, ex-fuel yield +4.4%. The volume and price statistics reconcile with the revenue lines (1.044 × 1.010 = +5.4% ex-fuel revenue growth, matching the segment schedule), so the operating data are internally consistent.

### Decomposing the margin gain

The single most important calculation in this report, reproducible from the Q2 10-Q LTL segment schedule:

| Line | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuel surcharge revenue | $183M | $314M | +$131M |
| Revenue ex-fuel surcharge | $1,057M | $1,114M | +$57M |
| Salaries, wages & benefits | $643M | $689M | +$46M |
| Purchased transportation | $32M | $40M | +$8M |
| Fuel, operating expenses & supplies | $222M | $275M | +$53M |
| Adjusted operating income | $211M | $287M | +$76M |

Fuel-surcharge revenue growth less the fuel/opex line growth = +$78M, versus +$76M of adjusted operating income improvement, approximately 103% coverage. The ex-fuel test: +$57M revenue less $46M SWB less $8M purchased transportation = +$3M, before a roughly $4M D&A headwind and $2M insurance tailwind. For H1: surcharge +$154M ($361M→$515M) less fuel/opex +$57M ($454M→$511M) = +$97M spread against +$108M of adjusted operating income (90% coverage); ex-fuel revenue +$91M less SWB +$72M and PT +$1M, plus $6M insurance, less $12M D&A, leaves roughly +$12M. The GAAP reconciliation ties in both periods, so this is not an artifact of adjustment definitions.

Three caveats deserve equal weight. The cost line bundles maintenance, facilities, IT and bad debt, so pure fuel cost cannot be isolated; the $78M is an upper bound on the fuel benefit. Higher volumes consume more fuel, so part of the $53M is volume-driven. And productivity gains may legitimately manifest as suppressed growth in that same line, in which case a portion of the "spread" genuinely is operating improvement. Management attributes results to yield, volume, productivity and fuel jointly. What is not in dispute: LTL adjusted OR is computed on total revenue including surcharge, so a 71.6% increase in surcharge revenue against a smaller cost increase improves the ratio mechanically, and XPO publishes no ex-fuel OR.

There is a further point that holds regardless of where diesel goes. Fuel surcharge contributed to earnings *growth* because it rose year-over-year. Even if diesel simply plateaus at the elevated 2026 level, the year-over-year delta compresses toward zero by mid-2027 and the growth contribution disappears. A diesel decline would additionally reverse it. This makes the fuel tailwind transient as a growth driver on a 12-18 month view, which is the window over which the market is capitalizing $6.41 of FY2027 EPS.

### Balance sheet: leverage is manageable; screening flags are artifacts

At 6/30/2026, total debt principal was $3,292M (Term Loan A $500M at SOFR+1.25% due 2029; Term Loan B $385M at SOFR+1.50% due 2031; $830M 6.25% secured notes 2028; $450M 7.125% 2031; $585M 7.125% 2032; $300M 6.70% debentures 2034; $242M finance leases). Cash $298M gives net debt of approximately $2,994M. Liquidity is $898M including a $600M undrawn revolver to April 2030. Europe runs a fully drawn €200M receivables securitization to March 2029 at 3.69%.

LTM adjusted EBITDA derives to $1,407M (FY2025 $1,272M less H1 2025 $618M plus H1 2026 $753M), and the segment build reconciles exactly to the audited proxy series. Net leverage is therefore 2.13x. Secured debt already carries investment-grade ratings, and Term Loan A covenants terminate with liens released upon IG ratings from two or more agencies, a concrete de-risking path. The 4.4x debt/equity figure circulating on screens is not reproducible: total liabilities/equity is 3.25x and total debt/equity 1.68x at 6/30/2026. Distress readings from Altman-style models reflect a working-capital/assets term of 0.009, which is structural for an inventory-free LTL carrier running a 1.05 current ratio by design.

Real constraints do remain: $1,962M of equity supports roughly $8.3B of assets, FY2026 interest expense is guided to $205-215M, and $830M of secured notes mature in 2028 in a cyclical business. Management repaid $200M of term loans year-to-date including $100M in July.

### Two cash-flow flags

Capex has fallen below depreciation. FY2025 capex was $657M against $521M D&A (126%); H1 2026 was $238M against $265M (90%), with the 10-Q citing "planned reductions in capital expenditures." Average tractor age rose from 3.7 to 4.0 years, total load factor fell 2.1% (22,287 versus 22,765 freight pound-miles per linehaul mile), and average weight per shipment fell 1.8% to 1,311 lbs while length of haul lengthened to 853.6 miles. H1 free cash flow of $253M (OCF $491M less capex $238M) versus -$6M a year earlier is therefore partly a timing benefit. The counter-argument is credible: with 30%+ excess doors built and Yellow absorbed, XPO may be in a harvest phase, a 4.0-year fleet is far younger than the 5.9 years at YE2022, and FY2026 guidance of $500-600M implies H2 spend of $262-362M, returning full-year capex to roughly D&A parity. Old Dominion raised 2026 capex to approximately $380M while XPO cut.

Receivables grew faster than revenue: AR rose from $1,035M at 12/31/25 to $1,267M at 6/30/26 (+22.4%) against 13.2% revenue growth, implying DSO of roughly 49.0 days versus approximately 47 at year-end. Part is seasonal and part follows revenue acceleration, but it is a $232M cash drag.

## Growth Analysis

The volume inflection is the genuine, non-fuel bull case. Tonnage per day went from -6.2% in FY2025 to +1.0% in Q2 2026 and shipments per day to +2.8%, at the same time Old Dominion reported *lower* tonnage with revenue growth of 10.4% driven entirely by yield, and Saia posted record revenue on "rate hikes and pricing power." Across the group the upcycle is pricing-led; XPO is the one gaining volume, which is what excess door capacity and newly commissioned Yellow terminals in previously constrained Southern and Southwestern markets should produce.

Pricing is supportive and forward-looking: the CFO described contract renewals up in the mid-to-high single digits, with shippers accelerating renewals to lock rates ahead of further increases. The CEO cited manufacturing demand rising for the first time in three years, amplified by truckload capacity constraints pushing marginal freight into LTL, and a customer survey showing double the proportion of customers expecting second-half acceleration versus the start of 2026.

The self-help runway is large and quantifiable. XPO's 79.9% LTL adjusted OR sits roughly 1,000 basis points above Old Dominion's near-70% level. On approximately $5.7B of annualized LTL revenue, each 100 basis points of OR is worth roughly $57M of operating income. Guidance for at least 200bp of full-year improvement implies roughly $115M of annualized benefit if sustained.

Set against this: +1.0% tonnage is thin, mix is deteriorating (weight per shipment -1.8%, load factor -2.1%), and management framed weight per shipment sequentially and versus seasonality rather than year-over-year, with the "flat" figure applying to July only. Europe is a drag on the consolidated growth rate: 41% of revenue producing roughly 11% of adjusted EBITDA, with FY2025 adjusted EBITDA falling to $147M from $158M (4.4% margin versus 5.0%), a Q2 2026 GAAP operating loss of $6M against $11M of income, and $27M of H1 restructuring. Consolidated growth is therefore an LTL story diluted by a low-margin European business consuming purchased transportation at roughly 46% of its revenue.

Estimate revisions are positive but below the threshold that would justify overriding valuation: current quarter +18.3% over 90 days, current year +12.2%, next year +7.4%. This confirms an operating recovery rather than a mispriced inflection at a low multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

Reproducible inputs at the $202.58 basis:

- Enterprise value = $23.72B market cap + $2.994B net debt = **$26.71B**; including $782M of operating lease liabilities, approximately $27.5B
- **EV/LTM adjusted EBITDA = 19.0x** ($26.71B / $1.407B); 19.5x with operating leases
- **P/E on current-year consensus $5.44 = 37.2x**
- **P/E on next-year consensus $6.41 = 31.6x**
- LTM GAAP diluted EPS derives to $3.41 (FY2025 $2.66 less H1 2025 $1.47 plus H1 2026 $2.22) → **59.4x trailing**
- Forward EBITDA sensitivity: H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $753M; H2 2025 was $654M. H2 growth of roughly 15% implies FY2026 near $1.50B and forward EV/EBITDA of approximately **17.7x**

Two data-quality notes. The quoted "forward P/E" of 57.92 is identical to the trailing figure and is not a genuine forward estimate; the $2.64 EPS field is also inconsistent with that ratio (202.58/57.92 implies $3.50, close to the derived $3.41 LTM figure). The consensus-derived multiples above are the correct basis.

Peer context is unusually unhelpful because the group is at a cyclical earnings trough: JBHT 44.8x, KNX 393.7x, SAIA 51.2x, R 23.2x, LSTR 62.2x, median 51.2x trailing. XPO's 59.4x trailing sits above the median; its 31.6x on next-year consensus is the more meaningful anchor, and it is not a discount to intrinsic value for a business whose incremental ex-fuel operating leverage in H1 was roughly $12-24M on $91M of revenue growth.

Analyst targets show extreme dispersion: low $115, mean $231.83, median $240, high $275, with post-print revisions in both directions (Stephens to $270, Oppenheimer to $238, TD Cowen to $228, Citi *down* to $221). The rating mix also shifted: sells fell from 2 to 1 while a strong-sell appeared where there was none. That dispersion is itself the signal: the sell side cannot agree on whether 79.9% is a new baseline or a fuel-flattered print. I do not treat the $231.83 mean as a floor here, because the multiple is not cheap relative to growth and the target range embeds the same unresolved question the price does.

My base case of $220 implies 34.3x the $6.41 FY2027 consensus, giving credit for continued OR improvement and share gains while assuming the fuel contribution to growth fades. That is +8.6% from spot, below the mean target, and it is the honest answer once the fuel decomposition is respected.

## Competitive Landscape

The LTL upcycle is broad and pricing-led. Old Dominion's Q2 revenue rose 10.4% with diluted EPS +32% and an operating ratio approaching sub-70, explicitly on higher yields offsetting lower tonnage; its shares fell on the print. Saia posted record revenue with operating income +26% from a 218-terminal network on rate increases. FedEx Freight now trades independently as FDXF, creating a newly incentivized scaled competitor for share.

XPO's relative position is improving on volume and lagging on margin. Growing tonnage while the best operator in the industry shrinks is the strongest evidence yet that the excess-door-capacity strategy and the Yellow terminals are converting. Yellow's 2023 liquidation permanently removed capacity in a market where terminal real estate is the scarce input, and XPO bought 28 of those terminals for $870M, a non-replicable advantage. Service quality supports pricing: the damage claims ratio improved from 1.2% in Q4 2021 to 0.3% for FY2025 and below 0.2% in Q2 2026.

The 1,000bp OR gap to Old Dominion cuts both ways. It is a large self-help runway, and it has persisted for years against a competitor that is not standing still. My decomposition suggests much of the recent narrowing is fuel-related rather than structural, which is why I score competitive position at 5.8 rather than higher despite the share-gain evidence, and why I would not lean on this dimension as a primary driver of the call.

Amazon is the unresolved structural variable. On June 10, 2026 Amazon opened its LTL service to any U.S. business shipping palletized freight to any destination, beyond marketplace sellers, leveraging roughly 80,000 trailers and 24,000 intermodal containers; the announcement moved FedEx Freight, Old Dominion and XPO shares lower. XPO's Q2 10-Q states there were no material changes to the FY2025 risk factors, which were filed February 5, 2026, four months before the launch. There is therefore no Amazon-specific risk factor. Existing generic competition and technology-entrant language may adequately cover it, and Amazon's initial offering may remain a narrow low-service option within a $52B market. A low-cost entrant monetizing backhaul density is the first credible threat to LTL pricing in a decade.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel-surcharge reversal or plateau (highest-impact).** Quantified above: 90-103% of the H1/Q2 LTL operating income improvement is covered by the surcharge-minus-fuel-line spread. FY2025 demonstrates the symmetry: LTL surcharge revenue *fell* from $785M to $731M on lower diesel. Even a plateau removes the growth contribution within roughly three quarters. A plausible underlying driver is a 2026 crude and diesel move; Transport Topics' concurrent topic taxonomy on the XPO earnings article referenced Iran, crude oil, the Strait of Hormuz and oil prices, but this is an inference from circumstantial evidence, not a company statement, and no EIA series was obtained to confirm magnitude or the specific mechanism. For a 12-month view, any diesel normalization is unverified, while the year-over-year benefit fades regardless.

**Europe.** Board authorization to divest remains in effect with no announced process, bidder or valuation found in any source. LTM Europe adjusted EBITDA derives to roughly $152M on a business earning a 5.2% adjusted EBITDA margin against LTL's 27%. Q2 delivered a $6M GAAP operating loss with $21M of restructuring. Escalating restructuring spend is equally consistent with preparing the asset for sale or with committing to fix and retain it; management intent cannot be inferred from the filings. Depressed margins, 85% collective-bargaining coverage and a fully drawn securitization argue against a premium multiple today.

**Capex deferral into an upcycle.** Sub-depreciation spending with the tractor fleet aging from 3.7 to 4.0 years, load factor down 2.1% and weight per shipment down 1.8% raises the question of whether service metrics and maintenance costs hold. FY2026 guidance restores roughly D&A parity, so this is a watch item rather than a thesis-breaker.

**Legacy Pre-Con-way environmental liability.** The Allianz/ACE matter (Multnomah County, Case No. 1204-04552) relates to Freightliner, a former Con-way subsidiary sold in 1981, with liability assumed via the 2015 acquisition. XPO booked $35M in Q3 2025 and $1M more in Q2 2026; final judgment was entered in May 2026 consistent with the accrual, but Notices of Appeal were filed in June 2026 by Allianz and multiple defendants with no appellate schedule set. Bounded but open.

**Governance.** Mario Harik became Chairman in addition to CEO effective January 2026, removing an independent-chair check, and also sits on the board of QXO (run by XPO founder Brad Jacobs); CFO Kyle Wismans sits on GXO's board. The board expanded to eight members with the July 27, 2026 appointment of Michael Kneeland, former United Rentals CEO — directly relevant asset-heavy cyclical experience, but eight directors is lean for a $23.7B company.

**Financing and rate exposure.** $3.29B of debt, $205-215M of FY2026 interest, $830M of 6.25% secured notes due 2028, unsecured coupons at 7.125%, and two floating-rate term loans. Manageable at 2.13x leverage; constraining if freight rolls over.

**Spin-off tax and Section 162(m).** Tax-free treatment of the GXO (2021) and RXO (2022) spin-offs remains a low-probability, high-severity contingency. Non-deductible executive compensation cost $12M of tax benefit in 2025; the FY2025 effective rate was 27.8% versus 18.1% in FY2024, with FY2026 adjusted guided to 23-24%.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 35.4% against 43.6% one-year realized — an 8.3-point discount and the 9th percentile within the current chain — with the term structure in contango (35.4% at one month, 39.4% at six months) and a call-heavy skew of -4.5%. Put/call open interest fell from 2.26 to 1.43 since July 22 as the price declined 5.1%, and ATM IV compressed 15.1 points post-earnings. The reading is complacency: the market has released the event premium and is not pricing a near-term dislocation. Cheap near-dated volatility is a favorable condition for owners who want to write covered calls or buy protection, and it is mildly inconsistent with my view that the fuel-comparison headwind arrives within three quarters. Short interest at 5.0% of float with 4.3 days to cover shows the bear case is uncrowded, leaving more room for a de-rating but also no informed short base validating it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $275 (+35.7%)

Manufacturing demand recovers as the CEO describes, truckload capacity constraints keep pushing freight into LTL, and XPO's excess doors and Yellow terminals convert 2-4% tonnage growth alongside mid-to-high-single-digit contract renewals. LTL OR reaches the mid-to-high 70s, delivering more than the guided 200bp with genuine operating leverage as ex-fuel revenue growth accelerates above the 5.4% Q2 rate. Europe is divested at 6-7x roughly $152M of EBITDA ($0.9-1.06B, about $7.80-9.10 per share gross), removing a 5%-margin dilutant and taking net leverage toward 1.5x, which triggers the two-agency investment-grade threshold that releases Term Loan A liens. FY2027 EPS reaches $7.25 and the market pays 38x for a re-rated LTL pure play. Diesel stays elevated, so the fuel comparison headwind never bites.

### Bear Case — $140 (-30.9%)

Diesel retraces, and the surcharge spread that covered 90-103% of the H1 margin improvement reverses. LTL OR backs up above 82% even with volumes intact, and the market discovers that ex-fuel incremental margins were roughly break-even. Amazon's nationwide LTL service takes low-single-digit share of a $52B market and caps industry pricing, ending the yield-led upcycle. Thin +1.0% tonnage stalls as manufacturing momentum proves a restocking blip; deteriorating mix (weight per shipment -1.8%, load factor -2.1%) compounds the margin problem. Capex must normalize above D&A, cutting free cash flow. Europe cannot be sold at an acceptable price and restructuring spend continues. FY2027 EPS lands near $5.00 and the multiple compresses to 28x.

### Base Case — $220 (+8.6%)

Volume growth persists at 1-3% with mid-single-digit ex-fuel yield, and XPO delivers the guided 200bp of full-year OR improvement, roughly half of which proves durable. Diesel plateaus, so the fuel contribution to growth fades through 2027 without reversing violently. FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $6.41; the market pays 34.3x for a business gaining share with a visible but slow-closing 1,000bp margin gap to the industry leader. Europe remains unresolved, contributing option value not embedded in the base multiple.

Probability-weighting 30% bear / 45% base / 25% bull gives an expected return near +4%, with a plausible range of +3% to +6% across reasonable weightings. That is a fair-value outcome, not an actionable one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Existing holders should stay long: the volume inflection, pricing power and share capture are real, and the European divestiture is unpriced optionality. New capital should wait for a better entry.

**Base case target: $220 over 12 months** — 34.3x the $6.41 FY2027 consensus, a modest premium to the 31.6x currently paid, justified by share gains and partial delivery of the OR runway. +8.6% from $202.58.

**Bull case: $275** (+35.7%) — 38x on $7.25 FY2027 EPS with Europe divested.

**Bear case: $140** (-30.9%) — 28x on $5.00 FY2027 EPS with the fuel spread reversed.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q3 2026 print (late October, tests the sub-81% OR guide against a harder fuel comparison), Q4/FY2026 results and FY2027 guidance (early February 2027), and any European transaction announcement.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price toward **$170-180** (28x FY2027 consensus) with estimates stable or rising — this would restore roughly 20-25% base-case upside against a bear case that has already partly played out.
2. Disclosure of an **ex-fuel LTL operating ratio, or a quarter in which ex-fuel revenue growth exceeds combined SWB and purchased-transportation growth by more than $30M** — direct evidence that operating leverage is structural rather than fuel-derived.
3. A **definitive European divestiture agreement at 6x or better** on approximately $152M of LTM adjusted EBITDA, with proceeds directed to debt reduction toward 1.5x net leverage.
4. **Tonnage per day accelerating above +3%** with weight per shipment flat or improving year-over-year, confirming share capture is broadening rather than mix-degrading.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **LTL adjusted OR above 82%** in any quarter, or Q3 OR above the guided 81% — the fastest confirmation that the margin print was fuel-flattered.
2. **Fuel-surcharge revenue declining year-over-year** while ex-fuel incremental contribution stays under $10M per quarter.
3. **Tonnage per day turning negative** for two consecutive quarters, particularly if accompanied by evidence of Amazon LTL pricing pressure in contract renewals falling below mid-single digits.
4. **Capex guidance cut again below $500M** while tractor fleet age exceeds 4.2 years — indicating harvest has become under-investment.
5. Net leverage rising above 2.75x, or FY2027 EPS revisions turning negative by more than 5%.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings, late October 2026. That print carries the first materially harder fuel comparison and is the cleanest available test of whether 79.9% is a baseline or a peak.

## Conclusion

XPO's fifth consecutive HOLD requires justification, and the justification is that the two things that have changed since the prior review push in opposite directions. Fundamentally, the picture improved: tonnage turned positive while the best operator in LTL shrank, shipments rose 2.8%, contract renewals are running mid-to-high single digits, consensus EPS is up 12.2% over 90 days, and net leverage of 2.13x with $898M of liquidity and no maturity before 2028 refutes the distress readings that appear on screens. The stock is also 5.1% cheaper than at the last review, which on unchanged-to-better fundamentals is an improvement in risk/reward.

Analytically, though, the margin story is thinner than the headline. A $131M increase in fuel-surcharge revenue against a $53M increase in the bundled fuel and operating cost line accounts for approximately 103% of the quarter's LTL operating income improvement, and the ex-fuel arithmetic leaves roughly $3M of incremental contribution in Q2 and $12-24M for the half. The bundled cost line prevents precision, and productivity gains may genuinely be embedded in it, but at 37.2x current-year and 31.6x next-year consensus earnings, and 19.0x LTM EV/adjusted EBITDA, the price already assumes the improvement is structural. Base-case upside of 8.6% against 30.9% downside if diesel retraces is not adequate compensation for that uncertainty, and Industrials selection generally requires a distinct company-specific catalyst to justify paying up.

The catalysts exist — a European divestiture at a credible multiple, a disclosed ex-fuel operating ratio, tonnage acceleration above 3% — and any of them would move this to ACCUMULATE, as would a price near $175-180. Until then, own it if you own it; the entry, not the franchise, is the constraint.

**Variant view:** Consensus treats 79.9% as a new baseline and pays 31.6x FY2027.
