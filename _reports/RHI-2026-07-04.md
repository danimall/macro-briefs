**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Robert Half is the world's largest specialized talent-solutions firm, and it is deep into the third year of a cyclical staffing downturn. Revenue has fallen from a 2022 peak of $7.24B to $5.38B in 2025 (-26%), net income has collapsed 80% to $133M, and reported operating margin has compressed to 1.4%. The core contract talent segment (56% of revenue) posted a reported operating loss of $25M in 2025 as hours worked fell 14.1%. The offset is a strong balance sheet: zero drawn debt, $464M cash, an Altman Z-Score of 3.39, and $267M of free cash flow that still comfortably covers the dividend given minimal capex.

The investment question has shifted since our prior three AVOID calls, all issued when the stock traded between $22 and $27. RHI has since rallied roughly 24% to $33.48 and sits about 53% above its 52-week low of $21.83. Two things changed. First, Q1 2026 showed the decline decelerating sharply: revenue fell only 3.8% reported versus 8.4% a year earlier, with management flagging a second consecutive quarter of positive sequential same-day constant-currency growth and international talent solutions returning to +4.5% growth. Second, the rally has removed most of the obvious downside, and a 28.5%-of-float short position makes an active bearish stance dangerous. Against those positives, consensus estimates are still being cut — current-quarter EPS down 27% over 90 days, full-year and next-year estimates each down about 10% — which contradicts the "confirmed trough" narrative that the price now partly embeds.

We upgrade to HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The ~7% dividend yield, funded by FCF though not by GAAP earnings, provides a floor, and the balance sheet removes solvency risk. But we cannot construct a confident 12-month appreciation case while estimates are still falling and the core segment is unprofitable, nor can we responsibly recommend AVOID/short into a 24% rally, a crowded short, and a decelerating decline. This is a two-sided setup where the cyclical-trough and AI-disruption debates are unresolved.

## Company Overview

Robert Half (founded 1948) operates three reportable segments:

- **Contract talent solutions** (~$2.99B, 56% of 2025 revenue): places contract "engagement professionals" in finance/accounting, technology, marketing/creative, legal, and administrative/customer support. Highest-volume and most cyclical; approximately 94,300 professionals placed in 2025.
- **Permanent placement** (~$440M, 8%): direct-hire recruiting at near-100% gross margin (no payroll pass-through) but the most economically sensitive line.
- **Protiviti** (~$1.95B, 36%): global consulting subsidiary (internal audit, risk, regulatory compliance, technology/AI advisory) that competes with the Big Four and has become the company's most stable and largest profit contributor.

The moat is modest but real: a global brand, an office network across 18+ countries, decades of proprietary two-sided candidate/client matching data, a proprietary AI matching engine, and a blended model where Protiviti staffs engagements with Robert Half contract talent (roughly $117M/quarter of intersegment revenue). The 10-K explicitly characterizes barriers to entry as "quite low," with local and regional firms the toughest competitors. There is no customer concentration; no client exceeds 1% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory is a textbook deep cyclical trough:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $7.24B | $6.39B | $5.80B | $5.38B |
| Net income | $658M | $411M | $252M | $133M |
| Operating margin (reported) | ~13.5% | ~7.3% | 4.2% | 1.4% |
| Diluted EPS | ~$6.03 | ~$3.88 | ~$2.44 | $1.33 |

The mechanism is negative operating leverage. In 2025, contract talent bill rates rose 3.5%, only partly offsetting the drop in hours worked and tipping the segment to its operating loss (versus +$64M in 2024). With headcount held roughly flat, further revenue declines flow directly to the bottom line, but the same operating leverage works powerfully in reverse on any recovery.

Protiviti is now the earnings anchor: $89M reported operating income (4.5% margin) versus combined talent-solutions operating income of roughly $12M, with segment revenue essentially flat (-0.1%). The consulting business has proven far more durable than staffing through this cycle.

The balance sheet is the key offset. There is no drawn debt (a $100M undrawn revolver maturing 2030 plus $10M of letters of credit), current ratio 1.53, and the Z-Score sits in the safe zone. The headline D/E of 1.24 is misleading: nearly all "debt" is a $772M employee deferred-compensation obligation fully offset by a matching trust, so the net economic exposure is essentially zero. Cash-flow quality holds up: 2025 operating cash flow was $320M and FCF a 5% margin even at trough earnings.

The central tension is capital allocation. In 2025, RHI paid $238M in dividends and repurchased $92M of stock ($330M total) against FCF — total returns exceeded FCF, funded partly from cash. The dividend was raised to $0.59/quarter (~$2.36 annualized) in February 2026. But 2025 EPS was only $1.33 and Q1 2026 EPS was $0.14, so the dividend is not covered by trailing GAAP earnings (payout ratio above 150% of EPS), though it remains covered by FCF given low capex. Management cut buybacks sharply (from $276M in 2024 to $92M in 2025, then to zero open-market repurchase in Q1 2026) to protect the dividend, revealing their priority ordering.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the weakest link, and they are the dimension we weight most heavily for winner selection. Two signals point in opposite directions.

The positive: Q1 2026 revenue fell only 5.6% adjusted YoY, a meaningful deceleration from the prior year. Technology contract talent turned positive (+0.8%), and management cited trends strengthening into early April. Third Avenue's framing — that temporary labor sits at a historically low share of the workforce after three years of muted activity — is a credible mean-reversion argument, and flat headcount means any volume recovery drops through at high incremental margins.

The negative, and the reason we cannot lean bullish: consensus estimates are still being cut, not raised. Current-quarter EPS estimates fell to $0.25, full-year estimates to $1.32, and next-year estimates to $2.07. This is the opposite of the estimate-revision inflection that would confirm a trough. The mechanism matters: for a business with no backlog, no long-term contracts, and revenue that tracks near-real-time hiring decisions, falling forward estimates mean analysts closest to the numbers are still marking demand down. On the macro side, management characterizes the backdrop as improving — rate cuts, easing inflation, 4.4% Q3 2025 real GDP growth, and college-educated unemployment near 2.8% — though the still-falling forward estimates suggest analysts have not yet embraced that recovery. Administrative and customer-support placements, the roles most exposed to AI automation, fell 9.8% in Q1.

The earnings track record is decent but modest: three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with beats of 1.8% to 8.8%. That supports confidence in the current (reduced) consensus but does not signal an inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.48, RHI trades at 25.8x trailing EPS of $1.33, a meaningless figure at a cyclical trough. On the forward $2.07 next-year consensus it trades at roughly 16x, and on any credible mid-cycle earnings power (a normalized $3.50-4.50 is plausible on recovery) the stock looks inexpensive. The dividend yield provides absolute support.

Peer comparison is muddy because most direct comps carry distorted multiples: Korn Ferry at 14.5x, ManpowerGroup at -94x, Insperity at -57x, Paylocity at 23.2x, Upwork at 10.6x. Peer median P/E of 14.5x and yield of 3.0% suggest RHI trades at a modest earnings premium and a large yield premium, consistent with a stock priced partway between "cyclical trough" and "structurally impaired."

We are deliberately restrained on the valuation score for two reasons. First, cheapness on normalized earnings without a confirmed catalyst is the classic value-trap signature, and estimates are still falling rather than inflecting. Second, and more concretely, the 24% rally has already consumed much of the margin of safety that existed at $22-27. The Barclays $25 price target (a bearish call that nonetheless argues "limited AI risk," implying cyclical rather than secular pressure) and Third Avenue's fresh contrarian long position bracket the debate: the stock currently embeds meaningful AI-disruption pessimism, but also a partial recovery. Analyst consensus sits at a $27 median / $29.89 mean with a $20-$50 range; the current price is already above both the median and the mean. On a premium-yield, still-falling-estimate name, we do not treat analyst targets as stale floors; the wide dispersion reflects genuine disagreement about whether this is a trough or a structural derating.

## Competitive Landscape

RHI's competitive level is high — it is the largest specialized staffing firm globally, with a proprietary matching engine, decades of two-sided performance data, and a differentiated Protiviti consulting arm using a configure-to-fit blended-team model against the Big Four. The trajectory, which matters more than the level, is ambiguous.

On the bear side, the 10-K concedes low barriers to entry and substantially expanded its AI risk language, warning that maturing AI tools "may enable clients to use advanced automation capabilities in lieu of" contract talent, a direct threat to the placeable-role base, most acutely in administrative/customer-support functions. On the bull side, RHI develops its own matching engine, Protiviti has a patent-pending enterprise AI platform (Atlas), and there is a reasonable argument that proprietary temp-performance data becomes more valuable in a world where verifying candidate quality is harder. Protiviti's resilience through the downturn demonstrates the value of the consulting diversification, though even there U.S. Protiviti declined 6.4% in Q1, a new soft spot masked by international strength.

Net, competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the core, with a credible offense in consulting. We do not over-weight this dimension, and its recent predictive signal has been weak in any case.

## Risk Assessment

- **Cyclicality (primary).** No backlog, no long-term contracts, short-duration placements; management states future results "cannot be reliably predicted." A double-dip or prolonged muted labor market hits earnings and dividend coverage directly.
- **AI disruption (the defining structural debate).** Expanded 10-K language, new detail on governance and EU AI Act compliance, and a two-sided outcome: threat to placement demand, opportunity for Protiviti advisory. This is only partly priced.
- **Dividend sustainability.** Covered by FCF but not by GAAP EPS (payout >150%). The February 2026 raise signals confidence but adds fragility if recovery stalls; a cut would be a specific downside catalyst.
- **Wage-and-hour litigation.** Certified/putative class actions and California PAGA claims alleging misclassification; outcomes unpredictable and could force compensation-plan changes.
- **Negative operating leverage.** With headcount flat, further revenue declines flow straight to margins, which are already near breakeven in the core segment.
- **Governance watch item.** Two Item 5.02 director/officer 8-Ks (April 21 and May 14, 2026) whose details were not retrievable, against a backdrop of long-tenured founder-era leadership and explicit succession-risk callouts. Warrants monitoring.
- **FX translation.** 22% of revenue is international; the dollar was a -1.8% Q1 headwind.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 54.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 52.4% — essentially fair, with IV sitting in the 12th percentile of the current chain. The nearest expiry prices a ±9.6% move. The near-term put/call skew is elevated at 7.8%, indicating some demand for downside protection into the next earnings print, but the term structure is in contango (long-dated IV of 59.6% above near-term), a normal, non-stressed configuration. The most notable shift is the put/call open-interest ratio collapsing from 2.57 to 0.05 as the stock rallied; bearish positioning has largely unwound. Combined with the short interest and 11.5 days to cover, the mechanical setup argues against pressing the short: an upside surprise (a strong labor print, as the July ADP-driven 7% pop illustrated) could force covering.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$45-50):** Q1 marks the confirmed cyclical trough. Contract talent hours re-accelerate, and with headcount flat the operating leverage snaps earnings back toward $3.50-4.50 over 18-24 months. A shrinking share count (down from 109M to ~100M since 2022) amplifies per-share recovery. Protiviti's AI-advisory offense grows, and Barclays' "limited AI risk" view proves correct — the pressure was cyclical, not secular. A 20x multiple on $2.50 recovering EPS supports $50. The yield pays investors to wait.

**Bear case (~$22-25):** The Q1 deceleration is a head-fake in a structurally weaker demand environment. AI genuinely displaces placeable roles (administrative/customer support already -9.8%), permanently lowering the earnings ceiling. Estimates keep falling, the core segment stays unprofitable, and the uncovered dividend is eventually cut, removing the yield floor and re-rating the stock toward peer staffing multiples on lower normalized earnings. This maps to roughly the Barclays $25 target and the $20 analyst low, or 25-35% downside from current levels.

The reason we land on HOLD rather than either extreme: the fundamentals genuinely support both cases, the estimate-revision data leans bearish while the sequential-revenue and balance-sheet data lean supportive, and the stock's rally plus its crowded short position have neutralized the asymmetry in both directions.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), which will test whether the sequential-growth inflection persisted into a full quarter and whether management reaffirms the dividend. The next-quarter consensus of $0.47 and the 90-day revision trend are the numbers to watch.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive — a stabilization or increase in current-year/next-year consensus over a 30-90 day window, reversing the -10% trend.
- Contract talent solutions returns to reported operating profitability, confirming operating leverage on a demand recovery.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive reported YoY revenue growth, validating the trough thesis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut or suspension, which would remove the yield floor and confirm the bear case on demand persistence.
- Q2 revenue re-accelerating to the downside (worse than the Q1 reported trend) or estimates resuming a sharp decline.
- Evidence that AI displacement is structurally, not cyclically, shrinking the placeable-role base (e.g., sustained double-digit declines in administrative/customer-support and technology-contract placements even as broader labor data improves).

## Conclusion

Robert Half is a well-capitalized franchise near the bottom of a severe cyclical downturn, with a strong balance sheet, an FCF-covered dividend, and the first tentative signs of a demand trough in Q1 2026. It is also a business whose core contract segment is currently unprofitable, whose forward estimates are still being cut 10-27% over 90 days, and whose central AI-disruption question remains unresolved. After a 24% rally from the levels at which we previously recommended AVOID, the stock trades above the analyst median target with much of the easy downside already priced out and a short position that makes an active bearish stance unattractive.

We upgrade to HOLD (uncertain). The scores have drifted higher over three reports (4.6 → 5.1 → 5.0 → 5.1), and the incremental improvement is driven by the Q1 deceleration and the removed downside, not by the price move alone. But the burden of proof for a BUY sits with confirmed estimate stabilization and a return to core-segment profitability, neither of which is yet in the data. Income-oriented investors are paid to wait via the yield, but that yield carries genuine sustainability risk if the downturn extends. We would move constructive on a positive estimate-revision turn at the Q2 print, and negative on any signal that the dividend is at risk.
