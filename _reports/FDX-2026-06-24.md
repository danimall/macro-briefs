**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FedEx enters a structurally different chapter following the June 1, 2026 spin-off of FedEx Freight (NYSE: FDXF). The remaining company is a focused global parcel/express operator anchored by the Federal Express segment, smaller and more cyclically exposed than the prior conglomerate but also generating its highest free cash flow profile in years on the lowest capital intensity in company history (capex ~4% of revenue, ~$4.7B FY2026 adjusted FCF). The DRIVE cost program hit its $1B structural-savings target, composite package yields rose 11%, and Federal Express segment operating margin improved to ~7.4% in Q3 FY26 from 6.7%. These operational wins support the bull thesis of a leaner, cash-generative parcel franchise.

The complication is that the market is not paying up for this. FedEx posted a double beat in Q4/FY2026 (revenue $25.01B vs. ~$24.18B consensus; adjusted EPS $6.31 vs. ~$5.91, a 4.9% surprise), yet the stock fell roughly 5-7% on the print. Two issues drove the reaction: consolidated adjusted operating margin slipped to 8.4% from 9.1% a year earlier (the loss of high-margin Freight, fuel and incentive-comp pressure), and the CY2026 outlook of $16.90-$18.10 adjusted EPS on ~11% revenue growth read as light to much of the desk. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen 12-14% across all forward periods over the last 90 days; the fiscal-year change muddies comparability, but the direction of revisions is unambiguously negative.

We maintain a HOLD. The valuation is not demanding at ~17x trailing earnings, and capital returns (up to $1B CY2026 buybacks, ~5% dividend raise) provide a floor. But falling estimates, an interim CFO during a complex transition, stranded costs that management says won't normalize until the 2027 exit rate, and a live Amazon-insourcing threat leave the risk/reward symmetric. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on FDX, and nothing in the post-spin data argues for a directional upgrade or downgrade.

## Company Overview

FedEx is one of the world's largest transportation and logistics companies, serving more than 220 countries and territories. Following the multi-year "One FedEx" consolidation (FedEx Ground/Services folded into Federal Express in June 2024) and the Freight spin-off, the post-spin company comprises:

- **Federal Express** — the dominant core: integrated express air and ground pickup-and-delivery, operating the world's largest express transportation network through a hub-and-spoke model anchored at the Memphis World Hub. FY2025 segment revenue was approximately $75.3B.
- **FedEx Logistics, FedEx Office, FedEx Dataworks** — supporting and specialty units.

FedEx Freight, the North American LTL leader and historically the highest-margin segment (FY25 operating margin ~16.7%), is no longer part of FDX. At separation FedEx received a $4.1B cash dividend from Freight.

FedEx's durable advantages are brand recognition and an integrated global air network that is genuinely hard to replicate, reinforced by deep B2B relationships in premium verticals: healthcare (~$10B of transportation revenue), automotive, aerospace, and a growing AI/data-center shipment franchise. The structural offset is a high fixed-cost air network that flexes poorly when volumes fall, which is precisely the condition the parcel industry has faced since late 2022.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Revenue has been flat-to-down off the COVID peak: $93.5B (FY22) → ~$87.7B (FY24) → ~$87.9B (FY25). FY2026 turned more constructive on price and mix. Q3 FY26 (ended Feb 28, 2026) delivered revenue of $24.0B (+8% YoY), net income of $1.06B (+16%), and diluted EPS of $4.41 (+17%). Q4/FY2026 saw Federal Express segment revenue up 14%, U.S. domestic package revenue up 13%, international export up 15%, and composite package yield up 11%. International domestic volume fell 9%, a deliberate shift toward higher-yield cross-border business.

**Margins — the central tension.** The Federal Express core margin is improving on DRIVE: segment operating margin rose to ~7.4% in Q3 FY26 (from 6.7%) and to ~7.0% on a nine-month basis (from 5.9%). But the consolidated Q4 contraction came against a backdrop of yield-driven revenue growth: fuel expense surged 66% (largely offset by surcharges), variable incentive comp rose, and the loss of high-margin Freight weighs on the consolidated mix. This is the proximate cause of the post-earnings selloff.

**Cash flow and capital intensity.** This is the clearest positive in the post-spin profile. Capex fell to ~$3.8B, or 4% of revenue, the lowest capital intensity in company history. A business long penalized for heavy aircraft and facility spending is now converting more revenue to cash. This supports the capital-return program and is, in our view, underappreciated relative to the margin narrative the market is fixated on.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt rose into the spin (~$22.8B at Feb 2026) as FedEx Freight Holding issued $3.7B in notes plus a revolver/term loan. Debt/EBITDA of ~1.9x sits well inside the 3.5x covenant; ratings are BBB/Baa2 stable. The reported post-spin cash balance of ~$13.3B includes the $4.1B Freight dividend and ~$800M of IEEPA tariff refunds held for customers; neither should be read as recurring operating cash, and the tariff money is not FedEx's economics. The Altman Z-Score near 1.24 ("distress" zone) is a structural artifact of an asset-heavy balance sheet (large PP&E and ~$17B operating-lease liabilities), not a genuine solvency signal given investment-grade ratings and positive FCF.

**Capital returns.** FY25 returned ~$3.0B in buybacks and ~$1.34B in dividends. FY26 buybacks were deliberately slowed (~$776M in nine months) to conserve cash for the spin and the InPost investment. Forward plan: up to $1B in buybacks for the rest of CY2026 and a roughly 5% dividend increase. At a ~1.5% yield, the dividend is well-covered but not the primary reason to own the stock.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed and, on a forward-looking basis, the weakest dimension here. Yield-led revenue growth is genuine, and the premium-vertical pivot (healthcare, automotive, aerospace, AI/data-center logistics) is a sensible strategy in a soft macro. Management frames the June-December 2026 transition period as implying ~20% adjusted EPS growth year-over-year, though the fiscal-year change makes this non-comparable to legacy estimates.

The problem is estimate direction. The 90-day cuts break down as: current quarter -14.5%, next quarter -12.3%, current year -12.3% ($22.24 → $19.50), next year -13.5%. Some of this reflects the Freight removal and fiscal-year change rather than pure deterioration, but the magnitude and breadth of the cuts are a negative momentum signal that we weight heavily.

The earnings beat record is strong — four consecutive beats, including a 25.7% surprise in the quarter ended Mar 2026 — which argues for some conservatism in the guide. But beats against a guidance bar that has itself been reset lower are a weaker signal than beats against rising estimates. The structural growth drivers (premium B2B verticals, Network 2.0 cost-out, capital-intensity inflection) are real but slow-burning, and they sit against a soft consumer-goods import environment, nearshoring (shorter hauls = lower yields), and an Amazon insourcing threat. On balance, growth prospects are below-average and the revision trend is the dominant near-term signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$317 and a ~$74.5B market cap, FedEx trades at roughly 17x trailing EPS of $16.81, and at the midpoint of CY2026 guidance implies a forward multiple in the high-teens. That is a discount to the Air Freight & Logistics peer median P/E of 26.9x, but the relevant comparable is UPS at 17.6x — the two integrated parcel carriers trade in line, and the higher-multiple peers (CHRW 38.7x, EXPD 26.9x, GXO 43.5x) are asset-light forwarders and contract-logistics players with different economics. Adjusted for that, FedEx is not obviously cheap relative to its closest structural peer.

We are deliberately cautious about reading the discount-to-median as a buy signal. The compression here reflects a real growth and margin question — falling estimates, lost high-margin Freight, stranded costs — rather than a clean mispricing. A low multiple on a business with negative estimate momentum and structural competitive pressure is more often a value trap than an opportunity, and we require an independent catalyst before rewarding cheapness. The catalysts on offer (DRIVE savings, capital-intensity inflection, capital returns) are incremental and slow, not the kind of identifiable trigger that re-rates a stock in the near term.

Analyst targets span an unusually wide range — low $160, high $442, mean ~$346, median ~$357 — reflecting genuine disagreement about the standalone parcel company's earnings power. The mean implies ~9% upside, but these targets straddle the fiscal-year transition and are not yet recalibrated to the new structure. We treat the wide dispersion as evidence of uncertainty, not of asymmetric upside. The valuation score sits at the midpoint: not stretched, not a clear bargain, with no catalyst to force a re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

The parcel/express industry is cyclical and currently pressured: weak U.S. consumer-goods imports since late 2022, a mix shift toward slower/cheaper deferred services, lower fuel surcharges, and nearshoring that compresses yields on shorter hauls. The expiration of the USPS air contract (Sept 29, 2024) removed a meaningful volume base FedEx is still adjusting its air network around.

Competition is multi-front: UPS and DHL globally, regional and crowdsourced players locally, and global air-freight forwarders. The most strategically significant threat is Amazon's vertical integration — the 10-K explicitly flags Amazon building its own hub, aircraft, and vehicle network, launching inbound LTL, and signaling intent to offer delivery to third parties. This is a structural overhang on both parcel volumes and pricing power, and it is the primary reason our competitive-position score has stepped down from the 7.5 of early 2026 toward 5.0 now.

FedEx's response — pivoting from volume-chasing to revenue quality via premium B2B verticals plus Network 2.0/Tricolor cost-out — is the right strategy, and early evidence (yields +11%, Federal Express margin up, $1B savings exceeded) supports it. But the company is now smaller and less diversified post-Freight, and the moat (network density, brand) is being tested by a well-capitalized insourcing competitor at the same time the cyclical backdrop is soft. The trajectory is roughly stable-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Loss of the highest-margin segment plus stranded costs.** Post-Freight, FDX is smaller and more exposed to lower-margin, cyclical parcel/express. Management expects to remove only ~$100M of stranded costs in CY2026 and frames stranded costs as a non-issue only by the 2027 exit rate, a real near-term margin drag.

**Tariff/trade-policy whipsaw.** The Aug 2025 removal of the de minimis exemption hurt low-value cross-border volumes. On Feb 20, 2026 the Supreme Court invalidated the IEEPA tariffs; FedEx sued for refunds Feb 23, and five class-action suits were filed against FedEx seeking tariff refunds for customers. The 10-K calls the financial impact "reasonably possible it could be material" — a live, hard-to-model overhang.

**DRIVE/Network 2.0/Tricolor execution.** The entire margin-recovery thesis depends on integrating historically separate Express and Ground networks, targeted complete by end CY2027, with attendant risks of higher costs, service disruption, and customer/service-provider loss.

**Amazon insourcing** (discussed above) remains the most significant structural competitive risk.

**Fleet and labor.** A Nov 2025 FAA emergency Airworthiness Directive grounded all Boeing MD-11s (being retired by ~2032), with continued aircraft impairment charges. Pilots (ALPA) have worked without a ratified contract since 2021, a cost overhang with no operational effect to date. "Nuclear verdict" self-insurance exposure persists across vehicle, cargo, and workers' comp lines.

**Governance flux.** A dense run of officer/director-change 8-Ks (Jan 29, Mar 12, Apr 13, May 8, May 18, Jun 1, Jun 8, 2026) and an interim CFO (Claude Russ) during the spin, fiscal-year change, and InPost deal concentrate execution risk in a thinly-bridged leadership team at a delicate moment.

These risks are partially but not fully priced. The estimate cuts capture some of the margin/stranded-cost concern, but the tariff litigation, the interim-CFO transition, and the Amazon threat are not cleanly reflected in a ~17x multiple. The risk score sits below average at 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 44.7% is rich versus 1-year historical vol of 29.2% (a 15.5-point spread, 70th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (44.7% near-term vs. 30.1% long-term), consistent with the market expecting an imminent event to resolve. Put/call volume (1.58) and OI (1.56) ratios are elevated and rose since the prior snapshot (OI ratio 1.26 → 1.56) as the stock fell 4.3%, with unusual put activity at the $298 strike (3.0x vol/OI). The signal is consistent with hedging into the post-earnings repricing and the fiscal-year transition rather than directional conviction. For a HOLD, the rich near-term IV and elevated put positioning reinforce the view that the market is paying up for protection.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$380-400).** A leaner, lower-capital-intensity parcel company executes Network 2.0 and DRIVE, drives Federal Express margins higher, sustains 11%+ yields, and converts the cash-generation inflection into accelerating buybacks. CY2026 EPS lands at or above the $18.10 top of guidance, estimates stabilize and turn up, and the stock re-rates toward the peer-adjusted upper end on a clean standalone-parcel multiple. The InPost minority stake adds European parcel-locker optionality. ~20-26% upside.

**Bear Case (~$240-255).** Stranded costs bite harder and longer than the $100M CY2026 guide, the soft import macro persists, Amazon's third-party delivery push compresses parcel pricing, and the consolidated margin stays below 9%. Estimates continue to fall, the interim-CFO transition surfaces execution missteps, and the tariff class actions or refund timing create a cash/legal drag. The stock derates toward the low end of the analyst range. ~19-24% downside.

The base case sits in between: incremental margin progress at Federal Express offset by stranded-cost drag and a soft macro, with the stock range-bound around the current level as falling estimates and capital-return support roughly cancel.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The first reported quarter under the new December fiscal-year-end (the June-December 2026 transition period) is the next natural reassessment point, alongside any update on stranded-cost removal and the InPost close (expected 2H 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward EPS estimates stabilize and turn positive over a 90-day window, reversing the current -12-14% trend.
- Consolidated adjusted operating margin recovers back above 9% with evidence that stranded costs are being removed ahead of the $100M CY2026 guide.
- A permanent CFO is appointed and management reaffirms or raises the CY2026 EPS range with improving margin commentary.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cut, or the company guides below $16.90.
- Federal Express segment margin reverses below ~6.5%, signaling DRIVE/Network 2.0 stalling.
- Material adverse development in the IEEPA tariff class actions, or evidence that Amazon's third-party delivery push is taking visible parcel share/pricing.

## Conclusion

FedEx is a fundamentally sound, cash-generative business at a fair-to-modestly-discounted multiple, but it is in the middle of a structural transition that has left forward estimates falling, the margin profile contracting on the loss of Freight, and the leadership team partially bridged. The post-spin parcel company has genuine positives — the lowest capital intensity in its history, ~$4.7B FCF, improving Federal Express margins, and a credible premium-vertical strategy — but these are offset by stranded costs, a soft cyclical macro, an aggressive insourcing competitor, and a messy tariff overhang. The fifth consecutive HOLD reflects a setup with no asymmetric edge in either direction: the ~17x multiple and capital-return support cap the downside, while negative estimate momentum and execution risk cap the upside. We would become buyers on evidence that estimates have bottomed and margins are recovering, and sellers on a guidance cut or visible competitive share loss. Until one of those resolves, the stock is a hold.
