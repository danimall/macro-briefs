**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Leonardo DRS is a mid-tier US defense-technology company sitting at the intersection of the fastest-growing Pentagon demand pools: counter-drone (C-UAS) and short-range air defense, integrated air and missile defense, naval electrification, and AI-enabled tactical sensing. Revenue has accelerated from $2,826M (2023) to $3,648M (2025, +12.8%), gross margin has climbed 230 basis points over two years to 25.1% in Q1 2026, and the company carries an $8.4B backlog with a 1.16x book-to-bill. The balance sheet was cleaned up in January 2026 when DRS retired its $191M term loan and replaced its facility with an undrawn $500M revolver, leaving net debt near zero. FY26 defense appropriations are set at ~$839B, and the FY27 request steps national-defense base funding to ~$1.15T.

The obstacle is price. At $45.46 the stock trades at roughly 35x current-year consensus EPS ($1.30) and ~42x trailing, a premium to the aerospace and defense peer median of 29.5x and well above the more comparable prime/shipbuilder cluster (HII 19.6x, Textron 17.9x). The business is executing well and the growth is real, but the multiple already embeds continued double-digit revenue growth and margin expansion. Estimate revisions have been stable rather than rising: current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates are flat over 30 days, with only modest +3.6% current-year and +2.2% next-year revisions over 90 days. That is a healthy but not inflecting picture, which matters because at ~35x forward the multiple prices in beats rather than rewarding them with re-rating.

We rate DRS a HOLD (growth_hold). The fundamental trajectory is intact and improving, and a genuine open-market director purchase in May provides a conviction signal against a backdrop of routine 10b5-1 executive selling. But the valuation offers limited asymmetric upside from here, the Leonardo S.p.A./FOCI control structure warrants a persistent governance discount and carries a hard 2030 proxy-renewal catalyst, and the 88% fixed-price book in an inflationary, supply-constrained environment (germanium) is producing recurring negative estimate-at-completion adjustments (-$59M in 2025). We would upgrade on a pullback toward the high-$30s or on a positive estimate-revision inflection tied to accelerating international radar/counter-drone bookings.

## Company Overview

Leonardo DRS designs and manufactures advanced sensing, network computing, force protection, and electric power and propulsion systems. It runs two segments:

- **Advanced Sensing & Computing (ASC)** — ~65% of revenue ($2,355M in 2025). Electro-optical/infrared (EO/IR) sensors, SIGINT, electronic warfare, tactical AESA radars (used in counter-drone and short-range air defense), dismounted-soldier and space sensing, and rugged AI-enabled edge computing via its SAGEcore open-architecture software.
- **Integrated Mission Systems (IMS)** — ~35% of revenue ($1,307M in 2025). Naval electric power and propulsion, where DRS is the leading provider for the Columbia-class ballistic-missile submarine (the first modern US electric-drive sub), plus force-protection C-UAS/short-range air defense ground-vehicle integration.

Management deliberately runs a diversified portfolio in which no single contract exceeds 10% of revenue, reducing single-platform risk. About 80% of 2025 revenue was US government (Navy 36%, Army 36%), with ~20% international/commercial and rising. 88% of 2025 revenue was fixed-price, and DRS acted as prime on 39% of revenue and subcontractor on 61%. The moat rests on embedded sole- and dual-source positions on long-running production programs, most notably Columbia-class naval power, where roughly 45% of remaining performance obligations run up to 11 years.

The defining structural feature is ownership. Leonardo S.p.A. (Italy) controls ~71% of voting power, and the Italian state owns ~30.2% of Leonardo, so DRS is deemed foreign-government-controlled. It operates under a DoD FOCI-mitigation proxy agreement (independent board, proxy holders vote the parent's shares) that expires in March 2030.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** $2,693M (2022) → $2,826M (2023) → $3,234M (2024) → $3,648M (2025, +12.8%). Q1 2026 revenue was $846M, up 5.9% YoY.

**Profitability, improving on both mix and execution:**
- Gross margin: 22.8% (2024) → 23.8% (2025) → 25.1% in Q1 2026 (+240 bps YoY), driven by favorable program mix, better Columbia-class execution, and a one-time quantum-cascade-laser license.
- Operating earnings: $348M in 2025 (+18.8%); Q1 2026 $77M (+30.5%). ASC operating margin reached 10.2% in 2025; IMS improved on naval-power execution.
- Net income $278M (2025), diluted EPS $1.03 (vs $0.80). Q1 2026 EPS $0.23 (vs $0.19). The 2022 net income of $405M contained one-time items and is not a clean comparison.
- Adjusted EBITDA grew to $453M (2025) from $400M (2024).

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** 2025 operating cash flow was $366M against $139M of capex (up 60% YoY on capacity build-out), yielding $227M of free cash flow (6.2% margin). Working capital is seasonally lumpy: Q1 is typically cash-negative (Q1'26 OCF -$66M), with the bulk generated in Q4. In January 2026 DRS repaid and terminated its $191M term loan without penalty and replaced its facility with a new, undrawn $500M JPMorgan revolver expiring 2031. Cash was $328M at Q1'26 (down from $647M mainly on the term-loan payoff), leaving net debt negligible. Total assets $4,486M; equity $2,730M. Goodwill and intangibles are ~30% of assets, an impairment vector if a business underperforms.

**Financial-health scores.** The Altman Z of 2.32 ("grey zone") is a false-positive artifact of a percentage-of-completion balance sheet (large contract assets, modest reported EBIT/assets) rather than distress. With near-zero net debt, an undrawn $500M revolver, a 1.89 current ratio, and positive free cash flow, real financial risk is low. The negative retained earnings (-$2.3B) is a legacy accounting artifact, not an operating concern.

**Backlog and orders.** Total backlog $8,448M at YE25; 2025 bookings $4,245M (book-to-bill ~1.16x; IMS 1.4x). Q1'26 backlog $8,382M. Q1'26 bookings fell 10.7%, with ASC down 35.9% against a tough prior-year comp that included a large ground-vehicle sensing award, while IMS rose 41.6% on naval power. The ASC decline is a comp effect rather than demand deterioration.

**Earnings track record.** Four consecutive beats: Q1'26 +25.9%, Q4'25 +12.1%, Q3'25 +2.9%, Q2'25 +6.5%. The pattern supports confidence in current consensus, though the magnitude of beats has been variable.

Weighing the resilience (clean balance sheet, positive FCF, strong liquidity) against the capital-structure inefficiency of a modest 6.2% FCF margin and the recurring negative EAC drag, we score Financial Health 6.8. This is the most reliable discriminator in our framework, and the evidence here is solidly above-average without being top-decile.

## Growth Analysis

DRS is levered to structurally growing demand pools. Counter-drone/C-UAS, integrated air and missile defense, naval electrification, and AI-enabled tactical sensing are all reinforced by Ukraine, Middle East, and Indo-Pacific dynamics and a rising DoD topline. The FY27 President's request includes a ~$350B reconciliation component on top of the base funding, a favorable multi-year backdrop that supports the broader defense-overweight thesis. CEO Baylouny describes "nearly insatiable" foreign demand, and international revenue rose to 12% of sales in Q1'26 on tactical-radar and counter-drone orders. The Columbia-class franchise provides a long-duration, high-visibility revenue anchor.

The growth durability is corroborated by multiple independent signals rather than a single-company story: a rising defense topline, robust IMS book-to-bill (>1.4x), expanding international mix, and management's capacity investment (new Charleston, SC naval facility for submarine components). This is meaningful in our scoring: a broad-based demand shift carries higher durability than an isolated backlog datapoint.

The caveat is the estimate-revision picture, which is stable rather than accelerating. Current-quarter EPS is flat over 30 days (+2.0% over 90d), next-quarter flat (-0.3% over 90d), current-year flat over 30 days, next-year flat over 30 days. Consensus does not show the +30%-in-90-days inflection velocity that would signal the market is systematically underestimating a turn. Growth is real and durable, but it is being delivered at roughly the pace already embedded in estimates. We score Growth Prospects 6.6: directionally strong, supported by a durable sector theme, but lacking the revision acceleration that would push it into top-tier territory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.46, DRS trades at ~35x current-year consensus EPS of $1.30 and ~42x trailing $1.03, a premium to the aerospace and defense peer median P/E of 29.5x and a substantial premium to the more directly comparable prime and shipbuilder cluster: Huntington Ingalls at 19.6x, Textron at 17.9x. Kratos (342.5x) and AeroVironment (-35.7x) are distorted by depressed or negative earnings and are not useful anchors; StandardAero at 29.5x is a services comp.

The premium is partially justified. DRS grows revenue faster than the primes (12.8% vs low-to-mid single digits for HII/TXT), carries a cleaner balance sheet post-refinancing, and has genuine margin-expansion momentum. On next-year consensus of $1.44, the forward multiple is ~31.5x. But even adjusting for growth, the PEG is well above 1.0: a ~11% forward EPS growth rate ($1.30 to $1.44) against a ~35x current-year multiple implies the market is already paying full price for the execution. DRS is a steady-grower being valued as though the growth will both persist and accelerate.

The estimate-revision framework reinforces caution. On inflection stocks with rapidly rising estimates, consensus targets lag reality and function as floors. That guardrail does not apply here: estimates are flat over the trailing 30 days, so the ~35x multiple is not being outrun by rising numbers. The analyst target band ($47 low, $59 high, $53.5 median) implies modest upside, but at this multiple the targets reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation.

We are also mindful that cheapness has been an unreliable buy signal in this environment and that a premium multiple without an independent catalyst is a warning, not a virtue. Here the multiple is elevated with a stable-not-rising estimate backdrop, which limits the case for asymmetric upside. We score Valuation 4.5: the price is stretched relative to the delivered growth, with no identifiable mispricing catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

DRS occupies a defensible mid-tier niche. Its moat rests on embedded sole- and dual-source positions on long-running production programs, most notably Columbia-class electric power and propulsion, where switching costs and qualification barriers are high and revenue visibility extends up to 11 years. The company is exposed to the highest-priority DoD growth areas — counter-drone, layered air and missile defense, naval electrification, and advanced IR sensing — and the trajectory is improving: expanding international mix, strong IMS bookings, and rising gross margins that signal pricing power and execution discipline.

The mid-tier position is double-edged. DRS is nimble and often sole-source on niche subsystems but is out-resourced on scale and R&D by the larger primes and can be undercut on emerging platforms by well-funded startups in the drone/autonomy space. The germanium supply constraint on IR optics is a competitive-cost pressure specific to its EO/IR franchise. On balance the competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: the company is gaining share in counter-drone and international radar while anchoring on a durable naval franchise. We score Competitive Position 6.7. This dimension has been a weaker recent predictor in our framework, so we weight it accordingly and lean more heavily on financial health and growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Foreign ownership / FOCI structure (the defining idiosyncratic risk).** Leonardo S.p.A. controls ~71% of voting power and, through the Italian state's ~30.2% stake in Leonardo, DRS is foreign-government-controlled. It operates under a DoD proxy agreement expiring March 2030. A material breach or failure to renew could trigger loss of facility security clearances, novation or termination of classified contracts, or debarment. The structure caps free float (~29%), gives the parent consent rights over M&A, new debt, dividends, and auditor changes, and subjects DRS's own acquisitions to CFIUS review. The proxy has functioned without sanction to date, but the 2030 renewal is a hard, dated catalyst/risk that is only partly priced.

**Government revenue concentration.** ~80% US government, exposing DRS to appropriations, continuing-resolution, and shutdown risk. This is mitigated near-term by fully appropriated FY26 funding and a favorable FY27 step-up.

**Fixed-price exposure.** 88% of revenue is fixed-price, concentrating cost-overrun and inflation risk. Negative EAC adjustments were -$59M in 2025 (~2% of revenue), up from -$25M and -$23M in prior years, driven by fixed-price development programs and a legacy ground-surveillance wind-down. This is the flip side of the margin story and warrants monitoring.

**Supply chain.** Germanium shortages pressure IR/optics program costs; sole-source components and tariff exposure add cost risk.

**Israel operations** amid regional conflict, and a **January 2026 Executive Order on defense-contractor executive compensation** that DRS flags as a potential constraint on retention.

The Q1'26 10-Q reported no material changes to 10-K risk factors. We score Risk Factors 5.5: the FOCI structure and 2030 proxy renewal are genuinely idiosyncratic and only partly discounted, and the recurring EAC drag is a live, quantifiable exposure, offset by the absence of near-term binary events and a strong liquidity cushion.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 55.3% against 40.5% one-year historical vol — rich, in the 89th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (55.3% near-term vs 41.6% at six months). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with an approaching earnings date and the implied ±12.4% one-month move. Put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-4.2%) and the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.39, so positioning is not defensively tilted despite the elevated vol. The P/C OI ratio rose from 0.31 to 0.47 since early June, a modest increase in hedging demand but nothing alarming. Rich near-term IV argues against chasing entry ahead of the print and favors patience for a post-event volatility crush. Short interest is negligible at 1.2% of float (3.1 days to cover), so there is neither a squeeze setup nor an informed bearish crowd.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($55–59).** International demand for tactical radars and counter-drone systems accelerates, pushing bookings above 1.2x and re-igniting positive estimate revisions. Gross margins hold above 25% on continued Columbia-class execution, FY27 budget growth lifts the DoD topline, and the market extends the ~35x multiple to next-year EPS approaching $1.50, supporting the high analyst target of $59. The clean balance sheet enables opportunistic capacity investment and buybacks.

**Bear case ($36–38).** Fixed-price cost overruns worsen (EAC adjustments deepen beyond the 2025 level on germanium and inflation), ASC bookings soften beyond the comp effect, or a defense-budget disruption (continuing resolution, appropriations delay) pressures the topline. The premium multiple compresses toward the prime peer group (~20x) as growth decelerates, and any noise around the 2030 FOCI proxy renewal adds a governance discount. A move to ~28x next-year EPS implies the low-$40s; a full de-rate toward peers implies the high-$30s.

**Base case ($48–50).** Revenue grows high-single to low-double digits, margins hold near current levels, estimates continue their measured upward drift, and the multiple stays broadly intact. The stock delivers mid-single-digit total return including the ~0.8% dividend, roughly in line with the analyst median of $53.5 discounted for the valuation risk we flag.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The fundamental trajectory supports ownership, but the valuation caps near-term asymmetry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the high-$30s (~$38–40), which would compress the forward multiple toward ~27x and restore asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Positive estimate-revision inflection: current-year or next-year consensus EPS rising >5% over a 90-day window on accelerating international/counter-drone bookings.
- Book-to-bill re-accelerating above 1.2x for two consecutive quarters, confirming demand durability beyond the naval franchise.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Deepening negative EAC adjustments (materially above the 2025 level) signaling fixed-price program deterioration.
- Concrete negative developments on the 2030 FOCI proxy renewal, or a security-clearance/compliance action.
- Gross margin reverting below ~23% or a guidance cut, alongside multiple compression that outpaces earnings.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural reassessment is the upcoming quarterly earnings report, followed by the FY27 appropriations process, which will confirm or challenge the budget tailwind. The 2030 proxy renewal is a longer-dated structural watch item.

**Current-level math.** Base case $48–50 implies ~6–10% upside; bull case $55–59 implies ~21–30% upside; bear case $36–38 implies ~16–21% downside. The roughly symmetric near-term risk/reward, with a rich multiple against stable-not-rising estimates, supports HOLD rather than BUY.

## Conclusion

Leonardo DRS is a high-quality, well-positioned defense-technology company delivering what the demand environment calls for: accelerating revenue, expanding margins, a durable naval franchise, and rising exposure to counter-drone, air-defense, and international radar demand. The balance sheet is clean, free cash flow is positive, and the four-quarter beat record supports confidence in consensus. A director's ~$1.07M open-market purchase in May adds a genuine conviction signal against otherwise routine 10b5-1 executive selling.

The constraint is price. At a premium to both its peer median and the more comparable primes, and with an estimate-revision backdrop that is stable rather than accelerating, the multiple is not being outrun by rising numbers. Combined with the persistent governance discount warranted by the Leonardo S.p.A./FOCI control structure and the 2030 proxy-renewal risk, plus the recurring fixed-price EAC drag, the setup offers limited asymmetric upside from current levels. We rate DRS a HOLD (growth_hold) and would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$30s or on a clear positive inflection in estimate-revision velocity.
