## Executive Summary

Terreno Realty is a pure-play coastal infill industrial REIT operating across six supply-constrained U.S. markets with 309 buildings totaling ~19.8 million square feet. The company delivered FY2025 FFO/share of $2.77 (+14.5% YoY), grew revenue to $476M (+24% YoY), and achieved cash rent spreads of +25.4% on renewals — all supported by the lowest leverage profile among industrial REIT peers at 2.3x net debt/EBITDA.

The stock has appreciated 17% over the past year and trades within 2% of its 52-week high at $66.54, against a consensus analyst target median of $70. The fundamental quality is clear: best-in-class balance sheet, supply-protected infill locations, disciplined capital recycling (12.7% unleveraged IRR on $1.1B dispositions since inception), and management whose compensation is tied exclusively to TSR. However, three specific risks constrain further near-term upside: (1) national industrial vacancy rising toward 8.6% by year-end 2026 is beginning to slow rent growth even in protected submarkets, (2) 42.4% floating rate debt exposure in a higher-for-longer rate environment creates earnings headwind, and (3) on an FFO basis at ~24x forward FFO, the premium to peers narrows substantially versus what trailing GAAP P/E (16.9x vs. peer median 29.9x) suggests. The risk/reward from near the 52-week high with only 5% upside to consensus median favors patience over accumulation.

## Company Overview

Terreno Realty was founded in 2010 with a focused strategy: acquire functional industrial properties in supply-constrained infill locations near major transportation infrastructure in six coastal markets (NYC/NJ, LA, Miami, SF Bay Area, Seattle, DC). The portfolio comprises 309 buildings, 46 improved land parcels, and 6 development/redevelopment projects, serving 683 tenants with no single tenant exceeding 4.9% of annualized base rent.

The company runs an extraordinarily lean operation — 47 employees managing $5.4B in assets by outsourcing property management to local third-party operators. Revenue mix is dominated by warehouse/distribution (80.5% of ABR), supplemented by improved land (10.1%), transshipment (6.0%), and flex/light industrial (3.4%). Geographic concentration is highest in NYC/NJ (26.6% of ABR), followed by LA, Miami, and SF Bay Area.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Growth:**
Revenue grew from $276M (2022) to $476M (2025), a ~20% CAGR driven by acquisitions ($683.5M deployed in 2025), rent escalations, and mark-to-market lease resets. FFO per diluted share grew from $2.42 (2024) to $2.77 (2025), a 14.5% increase that lagged portfolio-level growth due to dilution from ATM equity issuance (~7% share count growth YoY).

The FY2025 net income of $403M was materially inflated by $238.4M in property disposition gains — core FFO is the correct operating metric. Same-store cash NOI growth of 6.4% (excluding termination fees) reflects genuine organic momentum.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Net Debt/EBITDA: 2.3x (peer norm is 4-6x)
- Interest coverage: 10.3x
- Fixed charge coverage: 8.9x
- Weighted average interest rate: 3.9%
- Credit rating: BBB+ (Fitch, stable)
- Total debt/market cap: 13.4%

The balance sheet provides ~$1B+ of incremental debt capacity before reaching even 4.5x leverage, creating meaningful optionality for accretive acquisitions without equity dilution. The primary concern is the 42.4% floating rate composition (exceeding management's own 20% policy) and a somewhat short 2.8-year weighted average debt maturity ($50M due 2026, $150M 2027, $273M 2028).

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $272M (FY2025) comfortably covers the $2.08 annualized dividend ($221M at current share count). The dividend payout ratio of ~73% of FFO is sustainable. The 11.8% dividend CAGR since 2011 reflects genuine per-share growth discipline.

**Earnings Surprise Pattern:**
Four consecutive beats with extraordinary magnitude (29.8% to 166.0%). These largely reflect property disposition gains and timing of lease-up activity rather than systematic underestimation of recurring earnings. Forward estimates ($1.44 current year EPS from only 2 analysts) suggest limited coverage depth, reducing confidence in consensus accuracy.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current year EPS estimates revised +9.8% over 30 days but -9.9% over 90 days. This choppy pattern reflects lumpy property gains and timing uncertainty rather than fundamental momentum. Only 2 analysts cover estimates — insufficient for robust consensus signals.

## Valuation Assessment

TRNO trades at $66.54, or approximately 17x trailing GAAP EPS ($3.91, which includes disposition gains). On a more relevant FFO basis, using $2.77 FY2025 FFO/share, the stock trades at ~24x FFO.

The more relevant comparison is on P/FFO:
- TRNO: ~24x FY2025 FFO
- EGP: ~36x (higher growth Sunbelt markets)
- REXR: ~38x (similar coastal infill strategy, California focused)
- FR: ~24x (closest comparable)
- STAG: ~30x (secondary markets, higher yield)

At 24x FFO, TRNO trades at a discount to coastal infill peer REXR and growth peer EGP, which reflects TRNO's lower dividend yield (3.2% vs. 4.1% peer median) and more modest growth profile. The stock sits near the 52-week high ($67.55) with only 5% upside to the $70 consensus median target. The analyst high target of $80 implies ~20% upside, which would require a re-rating toward 29x FFO — possible but dependent on accelerating organic growth or a rate cut cycle.

The 3.2% dividend yield provides modest income support but is below the peer median of 4.1%, making TRNO relatively less attractive as a pure income vehicle.

**Why the market may be fairly pricing TRNO at current levels:** The stock has re-rated from $53 (August 2025 lows) to $66.54 (+25%), driven by strong Q4 earnings and the February 2026 results. Further re-rating requires: (a) demonstrable rent growth acceleration, (b) rate cuts that compress cap rates and benefit acquisition economics, or (c) development deliveries that prove accretive. None of these have defined near-term catalysts before the next earnings report.

## Competitive Landscape

Terreno's infill locations are genuinely supply-constrained — new competing industrial space cannot be built in urban cores surrounded by existing development. This creates a structural rent growth advantage vs. greenfield markets (Phoenix, Austin) where supply can and has overshot demand.

The small-bay focus (targeting <50K sq ft buildings) positions TRNO in the healthiest vacancy segment (5.2% vs. 11.5% for 500K+ sq ft). Last-mile delivery demand near population centers provides secular tailwind as e-commerce penetration continues.

**Competitive Risks:** (1) Tariff uncertainty specifically threatens coastal port markets — TRNO's entire portfolio is port-adjacent. Any sustained trade disruption would reduce throughput at these ports and depress demand for adjacent warehouse space. (2) Geographic concentration (26.6% in NYC/NJ) creates outsized regional risk.

**Asset Recycling Edge:** The 12.7% unleveraged IRR on $1.1B of dispositions demonstrates management's ability to acquire below replacement cost and sell above it — a genuinely differentiated capital allocation skill in the REIT sector.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-Term Risks (6-12 months):**
1. **Rising vacancy nationally** — same-store occupancy declined from 98.2% to 97.2% over 2025, confirming marginal pressure even in protected submarkets.
2. **Floating rate debt (42.4%)** — every 100bps increase in SOFR costs ~$4M annually, or ~$0.04/share. With rates potentially elevated through 2026, this is an ongoing earnings drag.
3. **Tariff/trade policy** — TRNO explicitly added tariff risk to the 10-K for the first time in 2025. Coastal port markets face direct trade disruption exposure.
4. **Refinancing risk** — $473M maturing by 2028 in a potentially higher-rate environment.

**Structural Risks:**
5. **Dilution** — 25% share count growth over 2 years. Management uses ATM issuance as primary equity funding, which erodes per-share growth.
6. **Key person dependency** — 47 employees; CEO Baird is central to the value-investing-in-real-estate approach.

**Options Market Signal:**
The near-term ATM IV of 97.0% vs. 21.5% historical vol represents an extreme dislocation — likely reflecting thin options liquidity and perhaps an upcoming corporate event. The steep backwardation (97% near-term vs. 19.3% 6-month) and zero put/call volume suggest the options market is illiquid rather than informationally rich. The 9.8% short interest (10.3 days to cover) is modestly elevated for a REIT, indicating some skepticism about the premium valuation or macro exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75-80, +13-20%):**
- Rate cuts materialize in late 2026, compressing industrial cap rates and accelerating acquisition accretion
- Rent growth re-accelerates as new supply tapering (8-year low in 2026 deliveries) tightens markets
- Development pipeline delivers on schedule and leases up at projected rents
- Tariff concerns prove manageable or benefit nearshoring demand for coastal distribution
- FFO/share growth accelerates to 10%+ on reduced equity issuance needs

**Bear Case ($53-56, -15-20%):**
- Rates remain elevated, compressing acquisition economics and increasing refinancing costs
- National vacancy overshoots 9%, pulling even infill markets below 95% occupancy
- Tariff disruption reduces port throughput and weakens tenant demand in coastal markets
- ATM issuance continues at 5-7% annual dilution, limiting per-share growth
- Short interest (9.8%) reflects fundamental concerns that materialize in weaker leasing spreads

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $58-60 (12-15% correction), restoring meaningful upside to consensus target and creating >4% FFO yield
2. Same-store occupancy stabilizes above 97% for two consecutive quarters AND cash rent spreads remain above 20%
3. Fed initiates rate cutting cycle, providing clear catalyst for cap rate compression and reduced floating rate debt burden

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Same-store occupancy falls below 94% or cash rent spreads compress below 10%
2. Management issues equity at prices below $58 (indicating capital needs at adverse prices)
3. Net debt/EBITDA exceeds 4.0x (would signal a fundamental strategy shift toward higher leverage)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (likely late April/early May 2026 for Q1), which should clarify occupancy trends, leasing velocity, and any tariff-related tenant softness.

## Conclusion

Terreno Realty is one of the highest-quality industrial REITs in the market — conservative leverage, infill supply barriers, disciplined capital allocation, and aligned management. These qualities justify a premium valuation, but the current price near the 52-week high with only 5% upside to consensus, rising vacancy rates nationally, elevated floating rate exposure, and tariff-related demand uncertainty for coastal port markets make the risk/reward modestly unfavorable for new capital deployment. The correct entry point is 10-15% lower ($58-60), where the yield, growth, and quality converge into a compelling BUY. At current levels, existing holders should maintain positions given the structural quality and 3.2% yield, but new capital should await better entry.
