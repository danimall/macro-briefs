## Executive Summary

SLB is the world's largest oilfield services company, generating $35.7B in FY2025 revenue across 100+ countries with clear leadership in international upstream services, subsea systems, and oilfield digitalization. The company produced $6.5B in operating cash flow and $4.1B in free cash flow despite a challenging year in which organic revenue declined approximately 6%, driven by activity cuts in Saudi Arabia, Mexico, and Sub-Saharan Africa. Management returned $4.0B to shareholders through dividends and buybacks, with a commitment to exceed $4.0B in 2026.

The investment case rests on whether the cyclical headwinds that compressed FY2025 earnings are genuinely "behind us," as management claims. The stock has rallied 53% from its October 2025 low of $31.64 to $52.77, pricing in a meaningful recovery in international activity that has not yet materialized in reported results. At 22.5x trailing earnings on a trough EPS year ($2.35 vs. $3.11 prior), the valuation is only cheap if earnings normalize upward — and that normalization depends on oil prices, OPEC+ discipline, and Middle East activity levels, all of which face elevated uncertainty given the current geopolitical environment.

The scoring trajectory has been declining steadily (6.7 → 6.1 → 5.6 over three months). Today's assessment stabilizes the decline: competitive position and financial health remain solid anchors, but the risk environment for a company with 82% international revenue and heavy Middle East/OPEC exposure has worsened, and the price rally has consumed the valuation margin of safety that existed at $34-38.

## Company Overview

SLB operates through four divisions: Well Construction (33% of revenue, $11.9B), Production Systems (37%, $13.3B including the ChampionX acquisition), Reservoir Performance (19%, $6.8B), and Digital (7%, $2.7B), plus an "All Other" segment that includes the fast-growing Data Center Solutions business and Asset Performance Solutions (Ecuador field projects).

The company's strategic framework identifies three growth engines: (1) the traditional oilfield services core, (2) digital platforms (Delfi/Lumi, autonomous drilling), and (3) "New Horizons" including modular data center infrastructure, carbon capture (SLB Capturi), and geothermal. International operations generated 82% of FY2025 revenue, with significant concentration in the Middle East, Latin America, and Sub-Saharan Africa.

The July 2025 completion of the $4.9B all-stock ChampionX acquisition added production chemicals and artificial lift capabilities to the Production Systems division, contributing approximately $1.5B in revenue for the five months of ownership.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability.** FY2025 revenue of $35.7B declined 2% reported but approximately 6% organically (excluding ChampionX). The decline was concentrated in Well Construction (-11% revenue, -220 bps margin to 19%) and Reservoir Performance (-5%, -191 bps margin to 18%), reflecting broad drilling activity reductions. Production Systems grew 12% reported but was flat organically. Digital stood out with 9% growth and margin expansion of 291 bps to 28%.

GAAP net income fell 24% to $3.4B ($2.35 EPS vs. $3.11 prior year), but $836M of this decline was attributable to one-time charges: $407M workforce reductions, $210M SLB Capturi goodwill impairment, $166M ChampionX purchase accounting, and merger/integration costs. Adjusted for these items, underlying earnings power was closer to $3.0B ($2.09 adjusted EPS impact from charges alone), suggesting core profitability declined modestly rather than dramatically.

**Cash Flow.** Operating cash flow of $6.5B was essentially flat versus FY2024 and FY2023 despite the revenue decline — a meaningful quality indicator. Free cash flow of $4.1B (11.5% FCF margin) modestly exceeded the prior year. FCF conversion relative to GAAP net income exceeded 120%, a strong signal that earnings quality is high and charges were largely non-cash.

**Balance Sheet.** Net debt of $7.4B is stable. The $11.6B total debt is well-laddered ($1.9B due 2026, $1.6B due 2027, $2.0B due 2028), and SLB retired $1.6B of debt in 2025 including all assumed ChampionX obligations. Shareholders' equity increased $5.0B to $26.1B, largely from the ChampionX share issuance. Current ratio of 1.33 is adequate. Goodwill and intangibles total $21.8B (40% of total assets), which is elevated but characteristic of acquisitive services companies. The Altman Z-Score of 1.99 places SLB in the "grey zone" — appropriate for a capital-intensive company with this leverage profile but not alarming given stable $6.5B annual cash flow.

**Capital Allocation.** FY2025 shareholder returns totaled $4.0B ($1.6B dividends, $2.4B buybacks), up from $3.3B in FY2024 and $2.0B in FY2023. The average buyback price of $40.23 vs. the current price of $52.77 means recent buybacks have been value-creating. The dividend was raised 3.5% in January 2026 to $1.18 annualized (2.2% yield). $4.1B remains on the $10B buyback authorization. Management has committed to >$4B returns in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

SLB's growth profile has three distinct components with different trajectories:

**Core OFS (negative near-term, uncertain medium-term).** Organic revenue declined 6% in FY2025. The Q1 2026 8-K warning of 6-9 cent EPS impact from tariff-related costs and below-expectations revenue suggests the headwinds have not fully abated. Recovery depends on: (a) Saudi Arabia resuming drilling activity — Saudi is the single most important swing factor for international OFS and timing remains uncertain; (b) OPEC+ production discipline — the current sustained blockade environment has supported crude prices near $100, but physical oil market dynamics are complex with structural infrastructure damage coexisting alongside OPEC+ oversupply; (c) broader international E&P spending, which faces competing pressures from elevated energy prices (supportive of spending) and energy transition mandates (constraining capital allocation).

**Digital (structural positive, 7-9% of revenue).** 9% revenue growth with 28% pretax margins and 291 bps of margin expansion make Digital the highest-quality segment. The Delfi/Lumi platform, autonomous drilling, and AI applications have a secular demand tailwind independent of commodity prices. However, at $2.7B (7% of revenue), Digital cannot offset meaningful weakness in the $32B core business.

**Data Center Solutions (emerging, high growth but small base).** 121% YoY growth to an estimated $460M run-rate. SLB doubled manufacturing capacity in Shreveport, LA. The modular data center infrastructure opportunity leverages SLB's engineering capabilities for hyperscaler customers. At <2% of revenue, this is a multi-year story with real strategic value but minimal near-term earnings impact.

**Carbon Capture (impaired).** The $210M SLB Capturi goodwill impairment plus "significant loss on one particular project" is a negative signal for what was positioned as a key New Horizons growth pillar. Aker Carbon Capture holds a put option to sell its 20% stake back to SLB. Commercialization is proving harder than expected.

**Net assessment:** Recovery in core OFS is the primary swing factor. If Saudi Arabia activity normalizes and international spending recovers to mid-single-digit growth, FY2026 EPS could reach $2.80-3.00. If current headwinds persist, EPS may be flat to modestly higher at $2.40-2.55. Digital and Data Center Solutions provide optionality but insufficient mass to drive the overall growth profile.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.77, SLB trades at 22.5x trailing EPS of $2.35, on a trough earnings year distorted by $836M in charges. The forward P/E of 22.5x (per Yahoo Finance data) aligns with the trailing multiple, suggesting consensus expects limited earnings recovery in FY2026.

**Peer comparison.** The peer median P/E is 23.2x, with KMI at 23.2x, WMB at 33.1x, VLO at 31.9x, and EOG at 14.7x. SLB's 22.5x is at the peer median, but HAL and BKR are more appropriate comparables. SLB has historically commanded a 10-20% premium to HAL on international exposure and digital differentiation, which appears roughly intact.

**Valuation framework.** On normalized mid-cycle earnings of $2.80-3.00, SLB trades at 17.6-18.8x — reasonable but not compelling for a cyclical company with significant geopolitical exposure. The 2.2% dividend yield is below the peer median of 3.0%.

**Price trajectory context.** The stock rallied 53% from its October low of $31.64 to the current $52.77, driven by the January 2026 earnings report and geopolitical premium on energy assets. At $34-38 in late 2025, SLB offered genuine value on trough earnings. At $52.77, the valuation assumes a recovery that earnings have not yet confirmed.

The analyst consensus price target range of $41-70 (median $56.55) implies 7% upside to the median, with the low target implying 22% downside. This asymmetry is unfavorable for a new position.

## Competitive Landscape

SLB's competitive position is the strongest dimension of this analysis. The company holds clear leadership as the #1 global oilfield services provider ahead of Halliburton and Baker Hughes, with several durable advantages:

- **International footprint:** Operations in 100+ countries with relationships spanning decades with NOCs across the Middle East, Latin America, and Africa. This breadth is extraordinarily difficult to replicate and provides resilience through geographic diversification of activity.
- **Technology moat:** Industry-leading IP portfolio, first-mover advantage in oilfield digitalization (10+ year head start with Delfi/Lumi), and autonomous drilling capabilities.
- **Subsea leadership:** The OneSubsea JV (70% SLB, 20% Aker Solutions, 10% Subsea7) is a dominant subsea production system franchise.
- **Customer diversification:** No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue across NOCs, IOCs, and independents.
- **Adjacent expansion:** Data Center Solutions leverages core engineering capabilities into a high-growth market where SLB has no traditional OFS competitor overlap.

Baker Hughes reported $27.7B in FY2025 revenue with a $32.4B backlog in its Industrial & Energy Technology segment, indicating the OFS competitive landscape remains intense. HAL reported Middle East operations impairment in its recent quarter, confirming the regional headwinds are industry-wide rather than SLB-specific.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical/commodity exposure (HIGH).** SLB's revenue and earnings are fundamentally tied to global upstream spending, which correlates with oil prices and OPEC+ decisions. A sustained decline in oil prices below $70 would likely trigger another round of customer budget cuts.

**Geopolitical concentration (HIGH).** The Hormuz Strait disruption has supported energy prices broadly, but SLB's Middle East operations face direct impairment risk if conflicts expand. Russian operations (~4% of revenue, $0.7B net assets) remain under sanctions with $0.2B in trapped cash.

**ChampionX integration (MODERATE-HIGH).** The $4.9B acquisition is SLB's largest integration challenge. The immediate divestiture of ChampionX Drilling Technologies ($286M) suggests the acquisition was targeted rather than comprehensive, but realization of synergies remains early-stage.

**Tariff exposure (MODERATE).** The March 2026 8-K disclosed 6-9 cent Q1 EPS impact from additional costs plus below-expectation revenue. For a company with global manufacturing and supply chains, sustained tariff regimes create ongoing margin pressure.

**Carbon capture impairment (NOTABLE).** The $210M SLB Capturi goodwill write-down and project loss raise questions about whether SLB's "New Energy" growth pillar can achieve commercial viability at scale.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in steep backwardation (68.5% near-term → 34.6% at 6-month tenor), characteristic of the market pricing an imminent catalyst or risk event. Put/call skew of 9.3% indicates elevated put demand at near-term expiries. The OI put/call ratio shifted from 0.36 to 0.44 over the past week while the stock was essentially flat — institutional put accumulation.

This options profile is consistent with positioning ahead of Q1 2026 earnings (likely late April) following the March tariff/revenue warning. The implied ±4.9% move on the nearest expiry translates to a ~$2.50 range. The market is pricing significant uncertainty about whether the Q1 warning represents a one-quarter issue or a prolonged margin headwind.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60-65).** International upstream activity recovers in H2 2026 as Saudi Arabia resumes drilling, sustained oil prices near $90-100 support customer budgets, ChampionX synergies begin materializing ($200-300M run-rate), Digital and Data Center Solutions contribute incremental growth. FY2026 EPS reaches $3.00+, placing the stock at 17-18x normalized earnings. The $10B buyback continues to support the share price. The energy transition creates long-duration demand for efficient production technology.

**Bear Case ($38-42).** OPEC+ oversupply resumes despite geopolitical disruption, Saudi Arabia delays activity recovery into 2027, tariff costs persist beyond Q1, ChampionX integration falters, and international budgets contract further. FY2026 EPS comes in at $2.30-2.50 (flat to trough), and the multiple contracts to 16-17x as the market prices in a prolonged downcycle. The SLB Capturi impairment extends to other New Energy ventures. Russian asset write-off materializes.

**Base Case (~$50-55).** Gradual activity stabilization with FY2026 EPS of $2.60-2.80 as ChampionX contributes a full year, Digital grows 8-10%, and core OFS is flat to modestly positive. The stock trades in a range as recovery remains gradual rather than sharp. 18-20x normalized earnings supports the current price but provides limited upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
- FY2026 Q1 earnings show organic revenue stabilization with sequential improvement in Well Construction margins above 19%
- Saudi Arabia rig count increases 10%+ from current levels (verifiable through Baker Hughes international rig count data)
- Stock pullback to $42-45 (15-16x normalized $2.80-3.00 EPS), restoring meaningful margin of safety

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
- Q1 2026 organic revenue declines exceed 5% sequentially, indicating the activity downturn is deepening
- Management reduces shareholder return commitment below $4B for 2026, signaling cash flow deterioration
- Oil prices sustain below $75 for 4+ weeks, which would likely trigger a new round of international budget cuts

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 2026 earnings (expected late April 2026) is the next critical checkpoint. The tariff-related 8-K warning makes this quarter a particularly important data point for assessing whether cost headwinds are one-time or structural.

## Conclusion

SLB is the global leader in oilfield services with $6.5B annual operating cash flow, emerging digital and data center growth engines, and a management team returning >$4B annually to shareholders. The competitive position is strong and durable.

The stock has rallied 53% from its October 2025 low, consuming the valuation discount that existed on trough earnings. At $52.77, the price implies a recovery that FY2025 results and Q1 2026 warnings have not confirmed. The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty (68.5% IV vs. 33% realized). The Middle East activity recovery that drives the bull case depends on Saudi Arabia drilling decisions and OPEC+ discipline — variables with elevated uncertainty in the current geopolitical environment.

The scoring trajectory has declined from 6.7 to 5.6 over three months, reflecting accumulated negative developments (organic revenue decline, Capturi impairment, tariff warning, ChampionX integration costs). Today's 5.8 score represents stabilization — the competitive position and cash flow generation provide a floor — but does not warrant upgrading the recommendation given the consumed valuation cushion and unresolved near-term headwinds. HOLD, with a clear path to BUY if the stock retraces to $42-45 or if Q1 2026 earnings demonstrate organic stabilization.
