**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Macy's has delivered the strongest operational stabilization in years. Comparable sales turned positive in FY2025 (+1.5% on an owned-plus-licensed basis) after declines of −0.9% and −6.0% in the prior two years, and accelerated to +3.0% in Q1 FY2026 — the best quarterly comp in four years, with all three nameplates and all channels positive. The balance sheet has materially de-risked: funded debt is down to $2.43B from $3.0B in FY2023, cash sits at $1.25B, the $2.1B ABL is fully undrawn, and ratings straddle investment grade (Moody's Ba1 / S&P BB+ / Fitch BBB−). Berkshire Hathaway's small new position (~3M shares, ~$55M, attributed to Greg Abel) added a value-investor endorsement that helped carry the stock to multi-year highs. The result is a four-quarter beat streak and a stock up 118% over twelve months.

The catch is earnings quality. FY2025's GAAP improvement was substantially driven by a one-time $328M interchange-fee litigation settlement, while adjusted EBITDA fell for a third consecutive year — $2,236M → $1,977M → $1,842M. The Q1 operating cash flow swing (+$292M versus −$64M a year ago) was likewise dominated by the settlement cash receipt. The comp turnaround is real and improving, but the absolute profit base continues to erode as the store count shrinks. This is a managed-contraction business: the "Bold New Chapter" strategy closes roughly 150 underproductive stores while reinvesting in a ~200-location "Reimagine" fleet. The underlying engine is luxury (Bloomingdale's +10.2% Q1 comps, Bluemercury's 20th straight positive quarter), which is structurally healthier than the core Macy's nameplate (+1.6%).

We move to HOLD from a prior AVOID. The prior stance reflected a stock trading at $11-14 where the bear case (secular decline, leverage) dominated. At $24.84 the picture has inverted: the operational improvement is now substantially in the price, the analyst mean ($21.55) and median ($22.00) targets sit below the current quote, and the deep-value margin of safety is gone. We do not see a fundamental case for either active accumulation or active shorting here. Consumer Discretionary is our weakest sector for selection alpha, and Macy's specific catalysts (real-estate monetization, continued comp acceleration) are not dated or high-conviction enough to override that caution.

## Company Overview

Macy's is a ~150-year-old omnichannel department-store retailer operating 665 stores plus digital channels under three nameplates that span the value-to-luxury spectrum. The flagship Macy's nameplate (~82% of net sales) is a mid-to-higher-income department store that includes the off-price Backstage concept, small-format stores, and a digital Marketplace. Bloomingdale's is the upscale/luxury full-line department store (plus Bloomie's small format and outlets), with licensed presence in Dubai and Kuwait. Bluemercury is the luxury beauty and spa retailer. The company serves roughly 40 million active customers annually, with revenue heavily weighted to the Q4 holiday quarter.

Beyond merchandise, Macy's earns higher-margin "other" revenue (~3.9% of sales) from a Citibank credit-card profit-share (Program Agreement to March 2030) and the Macy's Media Network retail-advertising business, recently augmented by an Amazon ad partnership. Most merchandise is sourced from Asia (China, Vietnam, India, Indonesia), with no single supplier exceeding 4% of purchases.

The "Bold New Chapter" strategy, now in its third year, has three pillars: close underproductive Macy's stores and reinvest in a "go-forward" fleet (the Reimagine program, expanding from First 50 → 125 → 200 locations); accelerate luxury through Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury; and simplify operations through automation (a new "China Grove" fulfillment center) and AI deployment ("Ask Macy's" shopping assistant). Tony Spring, a Bloomingdale's veteran, has served as Chairman and CEO since 2024 and is the architect of this strategy.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue: secular decline, stabilizing.** Net sales fell from $24.4B (FY2023) to $23.1B (FY2024) to $22.3B and finally $21.76B for the year ended January 2026 — roughly 11% erosion over three years, with about $700M of FY2025's decline attributable to closed stores. Store closures mechanically subtract revenue, but the surviving fleet is now comping positive, so the topline decline is increasingly a function of deliberate footprint reduction rather than demand collapse.

**Profitability: GAAP improved, underlying base still eroding.** Operating income rose to $1,030M (4.6% margin) in FY2025 from $909M the prior year, and net income reached $642M ($2.32 diluted EPS). The critical qualifier: FY2025 results were flattered by the one-time interchange-fee litigation settlement and partly offset by $230M of impairment/restructuring, a $67M pension settlement, and a $33M debt-extinguishment loss. On a cleaner basis, adjusted EBITDA fell to $1,842M, and adjusted EPS slipped to $2.32 from $2.64. The operating profit base is still contracting even as the comp trajectory improved and GAAP optics brightened.

**Margins.** Gross margin slipped 40 bps to 38.0% in FY2025 on tariff impact net of mitigation; Q1 FY2026 margin was 38.9%, down 30 bps, entirely tariff-driven. This is a watch item rather than a thesis-killer at current magnitudes.

**Cash flow and balance sheet — solid for a struggling retailer.** Operating cash flow was $1.43B for FY2025; free cash flow was positive at roughly $1.06B, though the Q1 figure was inflated by the settlement receipt. Cash stood at $1.25B with no ABL borrowings ($1.96B+ availability). Long-term debt fell via tender offers and redemptions, partly offset by a July 2025 $500M 7.375% notes issuance. The headline D/E of 3.34 is misleading: it embeds ~$2.7B of operating-lease liabilities, and financial debt to equity is closer to 0.5x. The Altman Z of 2.31 (grey zone) and partial Piotroski 5/5 reflect the lease load and thin margins, not imminent distress. Liquidity is genuinely sound.

**Q1 FY2026.** Net sales rose 1.8% to $4.68B (+2.7% excluding closed stores); net income was $63M versus $38M. Management raised full-year guidance to net sales of ~$21.5–21.75B, comps +0.5–1.2%, and adjusted EPS of $2.00–$2.20. The earnings surprise record is strong — four consecutive beats, including a 392% beat on the most recent quarter (against a near-zero estimate of $0.03).

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.1915/quarter (~$200M/year, current yield ~3.2%). Buybacks resumed — $251M in FY2025 at an average $14.21, and $50M in Q1 FY2026 at $18.92 — with $1.07B remaining authorized. The FY2025 repurchases at $14.21 look prescient against the current $24.84; the Q1 repurchases at $18.92 are merely fair. Real-estate monetization gains fell to $48M from $144M, a relevant deceleration given that real-estate value underpins the activist/value bull case.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is improving at the comp level and shrinking at the absolute level — both true simultaneously. Comps accelerated from −6.0% (FY2023) to +1.5% (FY2025) to +3.0% (Q1 FY2026). The luxury nameplates are the real engine: Bloomingdale's posted +10.2% Q1 comps (the highest Q1 in its 154-year history) and Bluemercury logged its 20th consecutive quarter of comp growth. The core Macy's nameplate (+1.6%) is stabilizing but is not a growth asset — it is being pruned. The Reimagine stores consistently outperform the rest of the fleet, validating the "invest in winners, close losers" approach.

Estimate revisions are mixed and modest. Current-year EPS estimates rose +4.8% over 30 days and +2.6% over 90 days; next-year estimates rose +5.5% / +9.0%. These are constructive but well short of the +30% velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection. The current-quarter estimate has actually slipped (−2.1% / −6.9%), and the next-quarter estimate has fallen to −$0.07 — a reminder of the seasonal earnings concentration in Q4 and the fragility of mid-year quarters. We weight growth prospects heavily as a discriminator, and here the signal is "stabilizing, low-single-digit comp, shrinking absolute base" — not a name with durable structural growth.

The forward-looking question is whether positive comps can outrun store-closure drag enough to grow absolute adjusted EBITDA. So far they have not: EBITDA has fallen three years running. Until that base turns positive, the growth case is "managed decline with a better customer trajectory," which does not earn a high score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.84, Macy's trades at ~9.9x trailing and forward earnings (EPS $2.32; FY2026 adjusted guidance $2.00-2.20). On its face this is cheap — below the Broadline Retail peer median P/E of 25.3 (ETSY 28.0, EBAY 25.3, AMZN 28.9, OLLI 20.7), and only KSS (7.3x) trades cheaper. But the relevant comp set for a low-growth, mall-anchored department store is the discount cohort (KSS), not the marketplace/off-price growth names. Against KSS, Macy's modest premium is justified by its luxury exposure and cleaner balance sheet.

We treat a cheap multiple here as a value-trap caution rather than a buy signal. The reasons: (1) the trailing EPS is inflated by the one-time settlement, so the "true" normalized multiple is higher than 9.9x; (2) adjusted EBITDA is still declining, so the cheapness reflects a shrinking earnings base; and (3) there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating. The classic Macy's bull case — real-estate "hidden value" (the Herald Square flagship, the 2024 Arkhouse/Brigade approaches) — remains embedded but underexploited, and monetization gains fell YoY. That option is real but unscheduled.

The stock is now near the top of the analyst range. The mean target is $21.55 and the median $22.00, both below the current $24.84; the high is $27.00 and the low $9.00. We normally treat analyst targets as floors on cheap, accelerating names, but that principle requires positive estimate revision velocity that the market is lagging. Here, estimate revisions are only modestly positive, the stock has already run 118% off its low, and Telsey reiterated Market Perform at a $23 target just below the price. The sell-side skepticism after a strong beat is informative: this is a re-rated turnaround, not an underappreciated inflection. The deep-value setup that existed at $11-14 (where Macy's executed buybacks) is gone. Risk/reward is roughly balanced — we score valuation at 4.5, recognizing the optically low multiple but the structural and earnings-quality offsets.

## Competitive Landscape

Macy's competes against department stores, off-price (TJX, Ross, Burlington), specialty retail, mass merchants, marketplaces (Amazon), and DTC brands. The structural headwind — share migration to digital and off-price — is real and ongoing, and the company's own strategy concedes it by closing ~150 stores.

The bull narrative, reflected in Berkshire's interest, is "shrinking competition": the Neiman Marcus/Saks consolidation and broad department-store retrenchment leave fewer full-line competitors, potentially benefiting survivors. There is something to this. Macy's barbell — value (Backstage), mainstream (Macy's), and luxury (Bloomingdale's, Bluemercury) — is more differentiated than a pure mid-tier department store, and luxury is structurally healthier. But the company's overall competitive trajectory is best described as stabilizing-while-contracting rather than share-gaining. The core nameplate is defending, not advancing. Tariff exposure is industry-wide, but Macy's heavy Asian sourcing makes it acutely sensitive; the Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs introduced a new 150-day 10% (potentially 15%) tariff regime that injects ongoing uncertainty into the cost base.

We score competitive position at 4.2 — a market participant whose moat is stable-to-eroding in its core, offset by genuine momentum in the smaller luxury segments. The trajectory is "managed defense."

## Risk Assessment

**Secular decline / managed contraction.** The core risk is structural: this is a shrinking business by design. The turnaround in comps does not change the fact that the strategy is to close roughly 150 stores. Absolute earnings have not yet turned positive.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 GAAP earnings were materially boosted by the non-recurring interchange settlement; the underlying adjusted EBITDA base is still declining. Investors anchoring on $2.32 trailing EPS are overstating normalized earnings power.

**Tariffs.** The most dynamic and recently modified risk. Asian sourcing concentration means tariff escalation flows directly to gross margin. The post-Supreme-Court tariff regime is unsettled.

**Credit-card revenue dependence.** ~3.1% of net sales, high-margin, tied to the Citibank program (expires 2030), and exposed to consumer credit deterioration, BNPL shift, and CFPB late-fee rule risk. A consumer-credit deterioration scenario would make this procyclical revenue stream vulnerable.

**Consumer macro.** Macy's mid-market exposure sits in the discretionary segment most exposed to consumer-spending softness. A genuine consumer slowdown would test the comp recovery.

**Lease/debt and change-of-control puts.** ~$6.0B operating-lease obligations plus $2.4B debt; a change-of-control-plus-downgrade put exists on ~$2.19B of notes at 101%. Liquidity is solid, so this is a structural rather than near-term risk.

**Governance.** A dual Chair/CEO role and a cluster of three Item 5.02 (director/officer) 8-Ks in six months (Dec 2025, Mar 2026, May 2026) signal ongoing board/management churn two-plus years into the turnaround. The proxy's own pay-versus-performance table shows cumulative TSR of 156.8 versus a 220.8 peer group — material underperformance despite the recent rally, with CEO comp-actually-paid of $20.5M.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 52.0% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 44.6% (a 7.3-point spread) and in the 70th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 52.0% above long-term 44.9%), which typically signals an anticipated near-term catalyst or elevated event risk. Put/call skew is balanced (1.2%) and the put/call volume ratio (0.61) and OI ratio (0.52) lean call-heavy, with unusual call buying at the $25 and $26 June strikes — consistent with momentum chasers riding the post-earnings, post-Berkshire move to multi-year highs rather than bearish positioning. ATM IV has come down from 57.1% to 52.0% over the past month even as the stock rose 20%, so some uncertainty has resolved. Short interest remains elevated at 14.5% of float (6.2 days to cover), which creates modest squeeze potential on continued momentum but also reflects sustained institutional skepticism. The net read: the options market sees a binary-ish near-term setup with no strong directional conviction, consistent with our balanced fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$30-34).** The comp recovery proves durable, luxury continues to compound, the Reimagine fleet keeps outperforming, and absolute adjusted EBITDA turns positive as closure drag fades. Real-estate monetization re-accelerates from the $48M FY2025 trough, surfacing the long-discussed flagship value. Berkshire's stake attracts further value-investor flows, and the dividend plus buyback authorization provide a yield-and-return floor. On normalized EPS approaching $2.50 and a modest re-rating to ~12-13x, the stock reaches the low-$30s. This requires the absolute earnings base to stop shrinking — the single most important unproven element.

**Bear case (~$15-17, roughly −33% from current).** A consumer slowdown arrives, comps roll back toward flat-to-negative, tariffs compress gross margin further, and the market re-focuses on the still-declining adjusted EBITDA and the one-time nature of the FY2025 settlement boost. With the deep-value cushion gone above $24, the stock reverts toward its FY2025 buyback range ($14-19) as the turnaround narrative loses momentum. The 14.5% short interest and below-current analyst targets are consistent with this downside being a live scenario.

**Base case (~$22-24).** Comps hold in the low-single-digit positive range per guidance, adjusted EPS lands in the $2.00-2.20 range, and the stock trades sideways-to-modestly-lower as it consolidates the 118% run, supported by the dividend and buyback but capped by the absence of a re-rating catalyst and the still-eroding profit base. This is roughly flat-to-down from $24.84 and is why we hold rather than buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale — we lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call in either direction at the current price. The operational stabilization is real, but the absolute earnings base is still shrinking and the valuation re-rating has consumed the margin of safety.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Absolute adjusted EBITDA turns positive YoY (reverses the $2,236M → $1,977M → $1,842M decline), confirming the comp recovery is translating to underlying profit growth.
- A pullback toward the high-teens (~$17-19) that restores a value margin of safety, with comps and guidance intact.
- A concrete, dated real-estate monetization event (flagship transaction or material redevelopment proceeds) that crystallizes the embedded balance-sheet value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comps turn negative again or full-year guidance is cut, signaling the recovery has stalled.
- Gross margin compression accelerates beyond ~75-100 bps on tariff escalation without pricing offset.
- Credit-card profit-share revenue deteriorates materially as consumer credit weakens, removing a high-margin earnings pillar.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 print (roughly late August/early September 2026), which will test whether the comp acceleration is holding through the seasonally weaker mid-year and whether the next-quarter consensus loss (−$0.07) materializes. The Q4 holiday quarter is the decisive test of the full-year guide.

## Conclusion

Macy's has earned credit for a genuine operational stabilization: positive and accelerating comps, a meaningfully de-risked balance sheet, a durable luxury growth engine, and disciplined capital allocation including well-timed buybacks. Those facts, plus Berkshire's small endorsement, justify the move off the lows and warrant retiring the prior AVOID. The score has improved modestly across financial health and risk, consistent with the data.

But the bull case demands two things that have not happened: a positive turn in absolute adjusted EBITDA (still falling three years running) and a re-rating catalyst (real-estate monetization is decelerating, not accelerating). FY2025 GAAP earnings were flattered by the one-time settlement, so the apparent ~9.9x multiple overstates the cheapness on normalized earnings. The stock now trades above the analyst mean and median targets, the sell-side held Market Perform after a strong beat, and Consumer Discretionary is the weakest sector for selection alpha — a higher bar for any constructive call. At $24.84, risk/reward is balanced: the deep-value setup that existed at $11-14 is gone, but the business is not deteriorating enough to short. We rate Macy's a HOLD and would look to upgrade on either a positive EBITDA turn or a pullback that restores a margin of safety.
