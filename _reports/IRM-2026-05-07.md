## Executive Summary

Iron Mountain has successfully transformed from a physical records storage company into a diversified data infrastructure platform, with its data center segment now driving exceptional growth (31.5% organic revenue growth in 2025, 51.8% EBITDA margins) alongside an emerging ALM (Asset Lifecycle Management) business growing at 46% year-over-year. Q1 2026 delivered record results across all metrics: revenue of $1.94B (+21.6%), AFFO/share of $1.43 (+22%). Management raised full-year guidance to $5.79-$5.86 AFFO/share, representing ~13% growth. The completion of Project Matterhorn eliminates ~$200M in annual restructuring drag.

The investment challenge centers on valuation and leverage. At $130.20/share, IRM trades at approximately 22.4x forward AFFO, a substantial premium to the 15-17x range typical for REITs and materially above peer median P/E of 22.9x (with peers offering 2-3x higher dividend yields). The company carries $16.5B in debt with $829M in annual interest expense (rising to ~$895M annualized in Q1 2026), and the CEO liquidated his entire common stock position at $105-$110 in March 2026. The growth trajectory is real and structural, but the current price appears to discount a significant portion of the data center upside, and the risk/reward is skewed unfavorably at this level.

## Company Overview

Iron Mountain operates as a specialized REIT across four segments: Global RIM (77% of revenue — physical records storage, shredding, media management), Global Data Center (12% of revenue, growing rapidly to 488MW leased capacity across 31 facilities), Corporate/Other (11%, primarily ALM), and emerging digital solutions. The company serves 240,000+ customers across 61 countries, including ~95% of the Fortune 1000. The REIT structure, adopted in 2014, requires distribution of 90%+ of taxable income.

The strategic pivot since 2019 has been aggressive investment in data center capacity. IRM now has 852MW under construction or held for development (total potential: 1,340MW), with five of the largest global hyperscalers as data center customers and weighted-average lease expiration of 10.3 years. The ALM business — decommissioning, data erasure, and disposition of IT hardware — grew from ~$550M to $807M in 2025 and is guided to $950M for 2026.

The concern is absolute leverage in a higher-for-longer rate environment: every 100bps of incremental rate on floating exposure is highly material to distributable earnings.

## Growth Analysis

Iron Mountain's growth story has three distinct engines:

**1. Data Centers (primary driver):** 488MW at ~97% leased with 852MW under construction. At current margins, each incremental 100MW represents roughly $150-180M in annual EBITDA once fully leased. The pipeline theoretically doubles the EBITDA contribution from this segment over 3-5 years. Five hyperscale customers provide demand visibility, and 10.3-year weighted average lease terms lock in revenue.

**2. ALM (emerging):** Growing from $807M to a guided $950M in 2026 (+18%). This business is a natural complement to data center relationships — as hyperscalers refresh equipment on AI-driven replacement cycles, IRM handles decommissioning and disposition. The TAM is expanding with accelerating hardware refresh rates.

**3. Global RIM (steady):** Growing at low-to-mid single digits through pricing power (contractual CPI escalators) on a 740M+ cubic feet installed base. Volume is mature but revenues are inflation-protected. Cross-sell into digital solutions creates incremental value.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year EPS estimates up +7.4% over 90 days, next year +6.7%. This is meaningful but below the +30% threshold that signals a strong BUY. The growth is real and visible but arguably consensus rather than under-appreciated.

## Valuation Assessment

At $130.20/share with $5.82 AFFO/share guidance midpoint, IRM trades at 22.4x forward AFFO.

- Peer median P/E: 22.9x (but peers offer 4.6-6.6% dividend yields vs. IRM's 2.6%)
- Pure-play data center REITs (EQIX, DLR): 25-30x AFFO typically
- Traditional storage/specialized REITs: 15-17x AFFO
- IRM's blended multiple reflects the market's recognition of data center growth but still sits below pure-play data center peers

A sum-of-the-parts framework might value: (a) Global RIM at ~15x EBITDA (~$30B EV), (b) Data Center at 25x EBITDA (~$15B EV), (c) ALM at 15x EBITDA (~$5B), less $16.5B debt = ~$33B equity value, or ~$111/share. The current market price of $130 implies either higher multiples on the growth segments or aggressive forward earnings capitalization.

The stock has rallied 67% from its December 2025 low of $77.77 and is now within 2% of its 52-week high of $134.09. The current multiple is pricing in sustained 15%+ AFFO growth and successful execution of the data center buildout. The analyst median target is $138 — only 6% above current price — suggesting limited near-term upside even under consensus bull-case assumptions.

The $44 analyst low target (likely from the lone Sell-rated analyst) reflects a bear-case scenario where leverage becomes untenable or data center demand disappoints.

## Competitive Landscape

**Physical Records (dominant, defensible):** IRM operates the world's largest physical storage network. Customer switching costs are extreme (moving physical records is expensive and risky). The business functions as a toll-road with contractual CPI escalators. Competitive pressure is minimal — the risk is secular decline, not competitive displacement.

**Data Centers (strong but contested):** IRM competes with Equinix, Digital Realty, and hyperscalers building their own capacity. IRM's differentiation lies in: (a) geographic diversity (21 markets vs. more concentrated peers), (b) existing customer relationships providing warm leads, (c) renewable energy credentials (100% renewable matching since 2017, Green Power Pass). However, IRM is not the cost or scale leader in any single market. The five hyperscale customer relationships are both an asset (demand visibility) and a concentration risk.

**ALM (first-mover advantage):** Growing rapidly in a nascent market. Named "ITAD Company of the Year" in 2025. Competitors include hyperscalers handling their own recycling in-house and smaller ITAD specialists. IRM's advantage is the trust relationship from data center services and the ability to handle global scale.

The competitive trajectory is improving. The cross-sell opportunity (only 5% of 240,000 customers buy from >1 business unit) represents significant embedded optionality that competitors cannot replicate.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage Risk (Critical):** $16.5B in debt with $829M in annual interest expense consuming nearly all operating income. In a sustained high-rate environment, refinancing risk is material. The weighted average rate of 5.6% is manageable, but incremental debt for growth capex must be funded at current elevated rates. A scenario where long-term rates rise further would likely compress IRM's valuation multiple while simultaneously increasing interest costs.

**Execution Risk (High):** 852MW under construction represents billions in committed capital. Construction costs, permitting delays, power availability constraints, and tariffs on equipment could increase costs or delay revenue recognition. The company has project-level financing on some developments where delays could trigger covenant issues.

**Customer Concentration in Data Centers:** Five hyperscalers represent a significant portion of data center revenue. Loss of any one, or a pause in hyperscaler capex expansion, would materially impact the growth narrative.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Even if the Fed holds rates flat, IRM's floating-rate exposure (Term Loans, revolving facility) and need for new issuance at current market rates creates a structural headwind vs. the 2020-2021 era when much of the expansion was initially funded.

**Secular Physical Records Decline:** While stable today, physical volumes face long-term headwinds. Any acceleration in digital conversion would erode the cash cow business that funds dividends and helps service debt.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($145-155, +10-19%):**
- Data center capacity buildout proceeds on schedule; margins hold at 51%+
- AFFO grows 15-20% annually through 2028 as 852MW comes online
- ALM reaches $1B+ by 2027
- Sum-of-parts re-rating as data center segment grows to 20%+ of revenue
- Cross-sell opportunity begins materializing (5% → 10% multi-product penetration)
- Rate environment moderates, easing refinancing pressure

**Bear Case ($90-100, -23% to -31%):**
- Hyperscaler capex cycle moderates or pauses
- Rising rates compress REIT multiples broadly; 22x → 17x AFFO = ~$99/share
- Construction cost overruns reduce IRRs on data center investments
- ALM client concentration (China exposure) creates revenue volatility
- Leverage covenant headroom narrows on earnings disappointment
- CEO's total liquidation of equity proves prescient

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback to $110-115 (19-20x forward AFFO), creating a more favorable risk/reward ratio
- Evidence of accelerating AFFO growth above 20% sustained for 2+ quarters beyond current guidance
- Meaningful insider buying by C-suite executives at current or higher levels
- Rate environment moderation reducing interest expense trajectory

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Data center leasing velocity declines (below 30MW/quarter run rate)
- Net leverage exceeds 5.5x (approaching covenant stress zone)
- AFFO growth decelerates below 10% for 2 consecutive quarters
- Loss of a hyperscale data center customer
- Further large-scale insider selling at or above current price levels

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely late July/early August 2026). Key metrics to watch: data center leasing pace, AFFO/share trajectory relative to $5.82 midpoint, and interest expense run-rate.

## Conclusion

Iron Mountain's transformation into a data center-powered growth REIT is genuine and well-executed. The Q1 2026 beat, raised guidance, and completion of Project Matterhorn confirm the operational thesis. However, the stock at $130.20 has already priced a substantial portion of this transformation, trading at 22.4x forward AFFO, near its 52-week high, and above the level where the CEO sold his entire equity stake. With the consensus mean analyst target at $128.55 (median $138), upside skew is limited unless growth re-accelerates beyond current guidance.

The combination of heavy leverage in a high-rate environment, concentrated insider selling at materially lower prices, premium valuation relative to REIT peers, and options market signals showing elevated put activity near highs suggests risk/reward is balanced to modestly unfavorable at $130. The growth story supports holding an existing position, but initiating or adding at current levels requires accepting meaningful downside if the rate environment or hyperscaler demand disappoints. HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis — reassess for BUY on a pullback to $110-115.
