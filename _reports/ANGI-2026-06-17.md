**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Angi operates a digital marketplace connecting consumers with home-services professionals across 500+ categories under the Angi, Angie's List, HomeAdvisor, and Handy brands, plus international properties. Since the March 2025 spin-off from IAC and a 1-for-10 reverse split, the company has pursued a deliberate "shrink-to-quality" pivot: the January 2025 "homeowner choice" change collapsed low-margin Network revenue (down 72% to $103M in 2025) in favor of higher-quality Proprietary channels (up 17% to $801M). The strategy expanded 2025 operating margin to 6.3% and produced $45M of free cash flow, but at the cost of a 45% three-year revenue decline and accelerating professional ("Pro") attrition.

The thesis broke down in Q1 2026. Revenue fell 3% to $238M, the company swung to a $9.5M operating loss and a $0.22 per-share net loss (from positive results a year earlier), Average Monthly Active Pros dropped 22% YoY to 105K, and management withdrew short-term guidance citing a "challenging March." The stock fell roughly 33% on the print and now trades near $5.39 against a 52-week high of $19.42, a 72% decline. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut more than 60% over 90 days across the current quarter and current year.

The setup is a deeply out-of-favor turnaround with a genuine deleveraging story (discounted note buybacks retiring ~$100M of 2028 debt below par) set against a shrinking top line, deteriorating unit economics, a specific AI/search-disruption risk, and a thin goodwill cushion. The forward P/E near 12x looks cheap, but it sits on a steeply falling estimate base and reflects structural impairment rather than mispricing. I maintain an AVOID, consistent with the prior two calls. The swing variable — whether the Proprietary model can stabilize Pro churn without permanently escalating advertising spend — has not yet resolved favorably, and Q1 evidence pushed it in the wrong direction.

## Company Overview

Angi runs a two-sided marketplace matching homeowners with service Pros. In Q4 2025 it served ~111K Average Monthly Active Pros and facilitated ~16M projects on a trailing-twelve-month basis. Revenue splits into two reportable segments after a 2025 relabeling: U.S. (~85–88% of revenue) and International (~12–15%, via HomeStars in Canada, MyBuilder in the UK, MyHammer in Germany/Austria, Travaux in France, Werkspot in the Netherlands/Italy). Monetization is increasingly framed as Proprietary (Angi's own consumer experiences and retail partners) versus Network (third-party affiliate traffic), with the shift toward Proprietary as the defining operational dynamic.

The corporate history matters for interpreting the financials. On March 31, 2025, IAC completed a full spin-off via special dividend; IAC now owns 0%, all Class B shares converted to Class A, and ANGI became a single-class independent company. A 1-for-10 reverse split executed March 24, 2025. Joseph Levin, former IAC CEO, is Executive Chairman with stock locked up through 2031. IAC transition services terminated March 31, 2026, requiring ANGI to self-provide finance, IT, and HR functions.

Management identifies its primary competitor as the offline status quo (word-of-mouth referrals) and acknowledges direct competition from Google/search, Thumbtack, Yelp, and home-improvement retailers. Switching costs are low on both sides of the marketplace, and the business is heavily dependent on Google for both paid and organic traffic.

The multi-year picture is a controlled contraction with a brief profitability improvement that has now reversed.

FY2025 revenue fell 13%, driven almost entirely by the homeowner-choice implementation that gutted Network revenue. Operating margin expanded as the company cut S&M 16%, G&A 18%, and headcount. But the margin improvement was partly mechanical: FY2025 G&A benefited from a $10.2M non-recurring reversal of stock compensation tied to Levin's forfeited IAC shares, and Adjusted EBITDA declined ~3% to $140M (from $145M). The reported profitability improvement rested on cost actions and a one-time SBC tailwind more than on operating leverage from a growing base.

Q1 2026 confirmed the underlying fragility. The company swung to an operating loss on three drivers: a $14.9M restructuring charge (part of a ~$30M program announced January 2026, cutting ~350 employees); a deliberate +$30M increase in advertising spend that pushed S&M to 59% of revenue from 48%; and continued top-line erosion. U.S. Adjusted EBITDA fell 30% to $15.1M and total Adjusted EBITDA fell 17% to $22.9M. The need to add $30M of ad spend just to feed the Proprietary channels while revenue still declined is the central red flag: the model is not yet self-sustaining.

The balance sheet is solid but levered. Cash was $244.6M in Q1 2026, down from $304M at YE2025 and $416M at YE2024, a drawdown reflecting aggressive buybacks and debt repurchases. The company carries $473M of 3.875% senior notes due August 2028, the key refinancing wall, and has been retiring them at a discount: $26.6M repurchased in Q1 2026 at a gain, another $73.4M in April–May 2026. A new $175M senior secured revolver (November 2025, JPMorgan, undrawn) carries a 4.0x net leverage covenant. Altman Z of 1.95 sits in the grey zone; D/E is ~1.8x; current ratio 1.65.

Capital allocation has been uneven. ANGI repurchased 10.5M shares in 2025 at an average ~$14.15 ($148.7M), against a current ~$5.39 price — capital largely destroyed in hindsight. The better recent use of cash has been the discounted note buybacks, which are accretive and de-risk the 2028 maturity.

A live impairment risk sits on the balance sheet: $890M goodwill plus $167M indefinite-lived intangibles equal ~63% of total assets. The U.S. reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only $109.7M at year-end 2025, a thin cushion, and the stock decline triggered an interim test. Further forecast deterioration could force a write-down.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are weak and the direction is negative. The top line has fallen 45% over three years by design, and the cuts are no longer producing offsetting quality gains in the metrics that matter. Pro churn has risen to roughly 5.0% per external analysis. U.S. Advertising revenue is being wound down and turned negative in Q1 2026. The Proprietary channel is now growing only by absorbing escalating ad spend, which undercuts the margin thesis that justified the strategy.

The estimate revision picture is decisively negative and is the most important forward signal here. Consensus EPS for the current quarter was cut 60.2% over 90 days (to $0.15 from $0.39), next quarter cut 57.0%, and current year cut 61.7% (to $0.31 from $0.82). The 30-day revisions remain negative (current quarter −10.6%, next quarter −9.1%). Estimates are collapsing, not rising, and the earnings track record (3 beats, 1 in-line over four quarters) was set before the guidance withdrawal that severed visibility.

The structural bull argument is real but distant: the home-services market is large, fragmented, and still mostly offline, so secular digital migration provides a long runway. That has been true for years and has not protected the company from declining engagement. There is no identifiable near-term catalyst to re-accelerate revenue; management itself withdrew guidance, signaling low confidence in its own forward visibility.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward P/E near 12x (trailing P/E identical at 12.0x on $0.94 reported EPS that includes one-time and below-the-line items) is the only metric that looks attractive, and it is misleading. The denominator is falling fast — current-year consensus dropped to $0.31 from $0.82 in 90 days — so a multiple computed against stale EPS overstates cheapness. Against the current-year $0.31 estimate, the stock trades at ~17x; against the next-quarter and current-quarter trajectory, the earnings power that supported the headline figure is no longer there.

Peer comparison offers no clean read because the cohort is heterogeneous and ANGI is not a true advertising comp. The Advertising peer median P/E is 49.6x (DV 30.8x, ZD 49.6x, TTD 21.3x, OMC 285x), but those are growing or scaled platforms, not a contracting marketplace mid-restructuring. A discount to that group is warranted.

The analyst target range of $5.00 (low) to $14.00 (high), mean $9.50, median $10.50, implies substantial upside on paper. I treat the high end with skepticism: those targets predate or lag the full impact of the guidance withdrawal and the estimate cuts, and on a name with falling estimates and structural top-line decline, consensus targets tend to be revised downward, as the recommendation mix already shows (a buy rating moved to hold over the past two months). The $5.00 low is effectively at the current price.

The valuation score reflects a value-trap profile: a compressed multiple that mirrors genuine structural impairment rather than a mispricing the market has overlooked. There is no specific catalyst to close the gap, and a low multiple alone — especially on collapsing estimates — is not a reason to own this. I assign a modestly below-neutral 4.5, recognizing the deleveraging optionality and the discounted-debt accretion, but the burden of proof for a re-rating sits squarely on Pro-metric stabilization that has not arrived.

## Competitive Landscape

ANGI's competitive position is deteriorating. The moat is structurally thin: low switching costs for both consumers (the service is free) and Pros, heavy dependence on Google for paid and organic traffic, and well-capitalized competitors in Thumbtack, Yelp, and home-improvement retailers. The 22% YoY decline in Active Pros and rising churn indicate the supply side of the marketplace is contracting, which over time degrades match quality and consumer value — a negative reflexive loop the homeowner-choice strategy was supposed to break but has not yet reversed.

The most underappreciated structural threat is AI-driven search disruption, and it cuts two ways. Generative AI could teach homeowners to DIY simple projects (reducing demand) and could reshape search so that Angi loses the organic and paid traffic it depends on (reducing supply of leads). Management is simultaneously betting on AI — deploying a fine-tuned LLM for service requests and citing "AI-driven efficiency improvements" as a rationale for the January 2026 headcount cuts. That dual exposure makes the company a potential disintermediation target rather than a clear beneficiary. For a marketplace whose primary moat is aggregating intent that increasingly originates at the search layer, control of that layer migrating to AI assistants is a direct threat to the core funnel.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and not fully discounted even at a depressed price:

- **Accelerating Pro attrition and a shrinking core.** Active Pros down 22% YoY with churn near 5% is the central bear case. If the Proprietary pivot fails to restore Pro ROI, the revenue base keeps eroding.
- **AI/search disruption.** Specific, articulated, and structural — both a demand and a traffic risk, layered on heavy Google dependence.
- **2028 debt maturity.** The notes must be refinanced or repaid; the revolver matures ahead. Discounted buybacks reduce the wall, but refinancing terms hinge on turnaround credibility.
- **Spin-off tax constraints through ~March 2027.** Tax-free-spin covenants restrict large stock issuances, M&A, asset sales, and certain repurchases, limiting strategic flexibility during the turnaround. A failed qualification could trigger material indemnification owed to IAC.
- **Goodwill impairment.** The ~$110M U.S. reporting-unit cushion is thin against $890M of goodwill; further deterioration risks a write-down.
- **Worker classification.** Reclassifying Pros as employees would create retroactive tax/labor liabilities.
- **Standalone execution and C-suite instability.** A new CFO (Julie Hoarau, March 2026), a departing COO (Bailey Carson, May 2026, who adopted a 10b5-1 sale plan), and the post-spin realignment add execution risk during a turnaround. IAC transition services ended March 2026.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing extreme uncertainty. ATM implied volatility surged from 72.8% to 240.9% (up 168 percentage points) between late May and mid-June, against a price down ~6%. That magnitude of IV expansion on a micro-cap signals the market is bracing for binary outcomes — further deterioration or a sharp turnaround reaction — rather than complacency. The put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.96 to 0.70, a less bearish skew than the headline short interest implies, suggesting some speculative call positioning at the low. Short interest sits at 13.3% of float with 2.9 days to cover — elevated bearish institutional positioning but not crowded enough to make a squeeze the dominant risk. For an existing holder, the rich IV makes covered-call overwrites attractive relative to outright exposure; for a new buyer, the IV surge says the market sees a wide outcome distribution and is not signaling a clear bottom.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The homeowner-choice strategy ultimately works: prioritizing quality leads over volume improves Pro ROI, stabilizes churn, and produces a higher-margin, more defensible marketplace. Proprietary revenue continues to grow, ad spend normalizes once the channel reaches scale, and Adjusted EBITDA re-accelerates. Discounted note buybacks de-risk the 2028 maturity, and positive free cash flow funds continued debt reduction. The AI bet pays off as Angi's LLM tooling improves match quality and lowers cost-to-serve. On a recovery to ~$0.70+ normalized EPS and a 15–18x multiple, the stock re-rates toward the $10–14 analyst high range. Optionality also exists in a take-private or strategic combination once spin-off tax constraints lapse in March 2027.

**Bear Case.** Pro attrition continues to accelerate, the marketplace's two-sided network degrades, and the Proprietary channel only grows by permanently escalating ad spend — meaning the model is structurally unprofitable at scale. AI search disruption erodes the lead-generation funnel faster than management can adapt. A goodwill impairment hits reported equity. The 2028 refinancing arrives with the turnaround unproven, forcing dilutive or high-cost capital. Estimates keep falling and the stock drifts toward $3–4 as the market prices a secular decliner. The estimate cuts and guidance withdrawal already point in this direction.

The weight of current evidence favors the bear case. Q1 2026's swing to an operating loss while Active Pros fell 22% and ad spend rose $30M directly contradicts the bull thesis that the model can grow profitably. The 33% post-earnings decline and estimate cuts are the market repricing a broken thesis, not an overreaction to a temporary stumble.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is a mediocre-to-impaired business in the middle of an unproven transformation, not an actively collapsing one with a clean short catalyst.

**Re-entry criteria** (any of the following would warrant moving to HOLD or reassessing toward BUY):
- **Pro metrics stabilize**: Average Monthly Active Pros declines flatten to single-digit YoY or return to sequential growth for two consecutive quarters, with churn falling back below ~4%.
- **Profitable growth without escalating ad spend**: revenue returns to YoY growth while S&M as a percentage of revenue declines toward the high-40s and Adjusted EBITDA stops contracting.
- **Estimate revisions inflect positive**: current-year consensus stops falling and posts net upward revisions over a 90-day window.
- **2028 maturity de-risked**: notes substantially refinanced or repurchased below par to a level that removes the refinancing overhang, with no goodwill impairment taken.
- **Valuation re-rates to clearly cheap on stabilized numbers**: forward P/E falls below ~10x on estimates that have stopped declining (a cheap multiple on a falling estimate base does not qualify).

**Timeline**: The headwinds — Pro attrition, AI/search disruption, and the spin-off strategic box — are likely to persist through at least the March 2027 expiration of tax-free-spin constraints, and the 2028 debt wall keeps refinancing risk live into that period. I would reassess at each quarterly print, with the next earnings release the natural checkpoint to see whether the Q1 ad-spend step-up is producing any Pro stabilization. Until the swing variable resolves favorably, the structural top-line erosion and collapsing estimates outweigh the optically low multiple.

## Conclusion

Angi has executed real, defensible actions: disciplined cost cuts, positive free cash flow, and accretive discounted debt repurchases that reduce the 2028 wall. But the core marketplace is shrinking faster than the quality pivot can restore it, the brief 2025 profitability improvement (partly powered by a one-time SBC reversal) reversed in Q1 2026, and consensus estimates have been cut more than 60% in 90 days with guidance withdrawn. The forward multiple near 12x is a value-trap signal, not a mispricing, because it rests on a rapidly falling estimate base and the model has not demonstrated it can grow profitably without escalating advertising spend. Layer in a specific AI/search-disruption risk that threatens the lead-generation funnel, a thin goodwill cushion, and a 2028 refinancing overhang, and the risk/reward remains unattractive. I maintain AVOID, consistent with the prior trajectory, and would only revisit on concrete evidence that Pro churn has stabilized and estimates have stopped falling.
