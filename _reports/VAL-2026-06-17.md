**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

On February 9, 2026 Valaris agreed to be acquired by Transocean (RIG) in an all-stock deal at a fixed ratio of 15.235 RIG shares per VAL share, with pro forma ownership of roughly 53% legacy Transocean and 47% legacy Valaris. The transaction is structured as a Bermuda scheme of arrangement, carries a February 9, 2027 outside date, and a $173M termination fee. From the announcement, VAL gapped from roughly $62 to $84 and subsequently traded to a 52-week high of $114 in May before retracing to the low $80s. VAL's price is now a function of two variables: Transocean's share price and the market's assessment of deal completion probability and timing. Standalone quarterly results matter only as a fallback in the break scenario.

The defining open question is the U.S. Department of Justice review, disclosed in May 2026, which is seeking additional information given that Transocean and Valaris are two of the largest floater operators. The combination is a horizontal-consolidation case for antitrust scrutiny, but both companies have reaffirmed confidence in a 2026 close, and VAL's shareholder vote appears to have been held (June 10, 2026 8-K, Item 5.07). The position resolves as a binary: clearance/close (shares converge to deal value and a squeeze of the elevated short base is plausible) versus break (VAL re-rates toward standalone value, pays $173M, and resumes trading on fundamentals).

I rate VAL a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale. The business is not weak — it is a premium-fleet driller with a clean balance sheet, rising backlog ($4.93B as of May 2026 versus $3.6B a year earlier), and forward consensus EPS of $7.27 that is being revised upward. The constraint is that the central arb spread cannot be established without Transocean's contemporaneous share price, and on a binary event with a live upside catalyst (DOJ clearance) and a contained downside floor, the risk/reward is two-sided. The score has drifted down from 6.6 in April to 5.2 in early June, driven largely by a valuation mark that leaned too heavily on stale pre-deal analyst targets.

## Company Overview

Valaris is one of the world's largest offshore contract drillers, operating on a day-rate basis across six continents. As of May 2026 it owns 45 rigs: 13 drillships (12 are seventh-generation ultra-deepwater units), 1 semisubmersible, and 31 jackups. It holds a 50% interest in ARO, a 50/50 joint venture with Saudi Aramco that owns 9 jackups and leases 7 more from Valaris. The company emerged from Chapter 11 in April 2021 under fresh-start accounting, shedding legacy Ensco/Rowan debt, and is now lightly levered for the sector.

The business runs four segments (Floaters, Jackups, ARO, and Other) with 86% of revenue non-U.S. The five largest customers were 49% of 2025 revenue, with Petrobras the largest (roughly 13–17%, and 17% in Q1 2026), alongside BP, Azule Energy, and Eni. The fleet's high seventh-generation drillship mix is the key competitive asset: customers preferentially contract high-spec units, and benign-environment floater supply has fallen roughly 45% from the 2014 peak (approximately 280 to 150 rigs) through attrition, with no economical newbuilds being ordered.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year recovery is real, but FY2025 net income is heavily distorted by accounting. Revenue progressed from $1,602M (2022) to $1,784M (2023) to $2,362M (2024) and was roughly flat at $2,369M in 2025. The cleaner signal is operating income, which rose 35% to $477M from $352M, driven by higher day rates and cost discipline despite fewer operating days. Reported net income of $982.8M is inflated by a $426.8M income tax benefit (release of a $523.2M deferred-tax valuation allowance), so the headline 44.3% net margin and ~31% ROE are not representative. Operating margin of roughly 21.6% is the honest figure.

Cash flow generation in 2025 was solid: operating cash flow of $546M against capex of $343.5M produced free cash flow of $203M. Capex is guided up to $425–475M in 2026, which will compress near-term FCF as the fleet reinvests. The balance sheet is a genuine strength: $599M cash at year-end 2025, a single debt instrument of $1.1B in 8.375% Second Lien Notes due 2030 with no principal due until then, and an undrawn $375M revolver. D/E of 1.67 and an Altman Z of 2.19 sit in the grey zone, which is typical for an asset-heavy cyclical rather than a distress signal here. A $1.33B net deferred tax asset (Bermuda domicile, low cash taxes) is a meaningful and often-overlooked source of value.

Q1 2026 showed clear standalone softening. Revenue of $465M fell 25% year-over-year; operating income collapsed to $20M from $143M; the company posted a net loss of $(16.4)M, or $(0.24) per share, a miss versus the $(0.12) estimate. The driver was floater idle time: active-fleet floater utilization dropped to 45% from 81% as several drillships went warm-stacked between contracts, with four units expected to return to work later in 2026. Pricing held: floater day rates rose to $436K/day from $384K, so the weakness was utilization-driven, not a pricing breakdown. The quarter also carried $13.6M of merger/integration expense and $7.5M of Middle East war-risk insurance cost.

The earnings-surprise record is volatile because of these moving parts: Q4 2025 printed $10.26 against a $0.63 estimate (the tax benefit), Q3 2025 beat by 126%, Q2 2025 by 27%, and Q1 2026 missed. This lumpiness is characteristic of a contract-driller in a recovery with idle gaps between awards.

## Growth Analysis

Backlog is the most important forward indicator and it is improving. Total Valaris backlog reached $4.93B in May 2026, up from $4.67B in February 2026, led by drillship awards and extensions (DS-4 added roughly $426M; approximately $2.1B of incremental floater backlog was booked in 2025). ARO carries a separate backlog of roughly $1.9–2.0B. The order book is filling even as the income statement temporarily sagged on idle time, which is the normal sequencing in an offshore up-cycle.

Estimate revisions support a constructive standalone earnings trajectory. Next-year consensus EPS is $7.27, revised +4.1% over 30 days and +4.3% over 90 days; current-year consensus is $3.50, up +5.2% over 30 days; and the current-quarter estimate has been revised +681.2% over 90 days off a near-zero base. The next-quarter estimate softened modestly (−2.3% over 30 days, −9.0% over 90 days), reflecting the idle-floater drag, but the longer-horizon trend is upward. These revisions anchor the break-scenario floor, which I address in valuation.

Industry conditions are constructive but choppy. Global drillship utilization is around 92% and jackups around 88%; roughly 80% of expected deepwater sanctioning carries breakevens below $60/bbl per Rystad. Near-term oil oversupply pressures sentiment, partially offset by Middle East-driven tightening and NOC energy-security spending — Saudi Aramco recalled seven suspended jackups for 2026. The structural floater-supply contraction underpins day rates.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation framework must be split into the two states of the world.

**Deal-close state.** VAL is worth 15.235 × RIG's share price, less an arb discount for time and deal risk. I cannot establish whether VAL currently trades at a premium, par, or discount to deal value because Transocean's contemporaneous share price is not in the data set. This is a material gap: the claim that VAL "has already discounted most of the deal premium" cannot be demonstrated either way. If VAL trades at or below parity, there is residual upside to close; if it trades at a meaningful premium, the spread is unfavorable. Without RIG's price, the arb spread is indeterminate.

**Break state.** Here the data argues against a deep re-rating. Standalone Valaris carries forward EPS of $7.27 (rising), $4.93B backlog, $599M cash, a single 2030 maturity, and a $1.33B DTA. At a 10–12x multiple on $7.27, reasonable for a premium-fleet driller in a tightening cycle, standalone value sits in roughly the $73–87 range. Even haircutting for the merger-expense overhang and 2026 capex step-up, a break floor in the high-$60s to mid-$70s is more defensible than the $55–65 a bear case would require. The pre-announcement trading range (low-to-mid $50s in late 2025) reflected both a weaker oil tape and a pre-recovery backlog; it is not the right anchor for a break in mid-2026.

The peer set (NE at 32.3x P/E, PTEN and HP at negative trailing P/E given tax/impairment items) shows wide dispersion and limited clean read-through, but the sector median P/E of 32.3x and 3.3% yield underscore that VAL's ~11x forward earnings is not stretched. On trailing P/E of 5.8x the stock looks cheap, but that figure is contaminated by the tax benefit and is not a usable metric.

Two caveats on the analyst marks. The $60 median and $69.82 mean targets, with a 0 buy / 8 hold / 1 sell / 1 strong sell distribution, are almost certainly stale pre-deal standalone marks — no analyst would carry a $60 target on a stock pinned to a live exchange ratio at $82.64. These are relics, not live valuations, and should not be used to corroborate a low standalone floor. I retain the $45 low and $99 high as the reported range but flag that the central tendency is non-operative. This is the primary reason I am marking valuation at 5.5 rather than the 4.5 of the prior report; the earlier mark over-weighted a stale $60 anchor.

## Competitive Landscape

Valaris holds a strong relative position: a high seventh-generation drillship mix, broad geographic diversification, and a 50% ARO stake providing Saudi exposure. The structural supply contraction benefits all surviving high-spec operators, Valaris among them. The day-rate strength in Q1 (+14% YoY despite the utilization dip) is evidence that pricing power is intact in the premium tier.

The merger reshapes the competitive dynamic. Combining Transocean and Valaris consolidates two of the largest floater fleets, which is the strategic rationale (scale, synergies, fleet optimization) and simultaneously the source of the antitrust scrutiny. If the deal completes, the combined entity becomes the dominant floater operator; if it breaks, Valaris remains a strong but sub-scale independent against Transocean, Noble, Seadrill, and Borr. Fleet rationalization continues either way — seven rigs retired/sold in 2025, DPS-1 sold for recycling in April 2026 — concentrating the portfolio on high-return assets.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by binary, idiosyncratic factors not fully priced, which is why I score risk factors at 4.5.

1. **Merger/regulatory risk (primary).** The DOJ inquiry seeking additional information is the key swing factor. A Second Request, divestiture conditions, or an outright block would re-rate the stock toward standalone value. The $173M break fee and a clear path to standalone trading limit but do not eliminate the downside.
2. **Stock-deal price risk.** Consideration is RIG shares at a fixed ratio, so holders bear full Transocean price and combined-company integration risk through close.
3. **ARO joint-venture complexity.** ARO has never paid a cash distribution; 2025 interest on the $400.7M notes receivable was paid in kind, not cash; the notes are Saudi-law governed, subordinated to ARO bank debt, and require Aramco consent to enforce. Valaris carries a contingent newbuild funding obligation of up to $1.1B (Kingdom 3 & 4 ordered at roughly $300M each). ARO income is fully Saudi Aramco-concentrated, and ARO cash was only $90.4M in Q1 2026.
4. **Customer concentration.** Petrobras at 13–17% with Brazilian regulatory aggressiveness.
5. **Near-term utilization gaps.** The Q1 floater utilization drop to 45% is evidence of the lumpy contract dynamic.
6. **Geopolitics.** Middle East conflict and Strait of Hormuz exposure (war-risk insurance, mobilization disruption for VAL and ARO rigs).
7. **Tax structure.** Bermuda domicile carries IRS Section 7874 challenge risk and Pillar Two minimum-tax exposure; a ~$29M Malaysia assessment is ongoing.
8. **Merger covenants** restrict buybacks ($175M authorized but paused), M&A, asset sales, and dividends until close.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is consistent with a market pricing an imminent binary, not a directional collapse. Near-term ATM IV of 66.4% sits rich to 1-year HV of 57.4% and at the 82nd percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (66.4% near-term versus 51.4% at six months), the signature of an event being priced. The put/call skew of −34.3% is call-heavy, and the put/call OI ratio of 0.50 leans bullish, which argues against reading the elevated near-term IV as a bearish tell. The IV step-up (+6.3pp since June 2) alongside the −12% price move reflects event-risk re-pricing around deal milestones rather than fundamental deterioration. Short interest is elevated at 13.2% of float with 7.7 days to cover, a meaningful squeeze risk if DOJ clears the deal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** DOJ clears the deal (with or without modest remedies) and it closes in 2026. VAL converges to deal value, the elevated short base is squeezed into the close, and holders end up owning the dominant floater operator with combined backlog and synergy upside in a tightening offshore cycle. Even absent the merger, standalone earnings power, backlog, and the DTA support a high-$70s-to-high-$80s standalone value. Bull target $95–99.

**Bear case.** The DOJ blocks the transaction or demands divestitures that the parties walk away from. VAL pays the $173M fee, the merger-arb base unwinds, and the stock re-rates to standalone value. The key correction to a prior bear framing: standalone value is not $55–65. On $7.27 forward EPS at 10–12x, with a clean balance sheet and rising backlog, the break floor is more plausibly in the high-$60s to mid-$70s — call it $68–75, roughly −10% to −18% from $82.64, not −25–35%. A deeper move would require both a deal break and a simultaneous oil/utilization shock. Bear target $68.

**Why this is HOLD, not AVOID.** An AVOID requires asymmetric downside. Three facts undercut that here. First, the break-downside is contained (high-$60s to mid-$70s, not mid-$50s), because forward earnings and backlog have recovered well above pre-announcement levels. Second, the arb spread cannot be shown to be unfavorable, since Transocean's price is not available, so the "premium already discounted" premise is unproven. Third, the position cannot be monetized on the short side (see below), and it faces a live upside catalyst in DOJ clearance. A binary with a supported floor and an un-shortable structure is a balanced HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on a binary event with resolution expected within 6–9 months (deal close targeted for 2026; outside date February 9, 2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- DOJ resolves the inquiry with clearance or de minimis remedies, materially raising close probability — converging VAL to deal value and likely squeezing the short base.
- Evidence that VAL trades at a meaningful discount to 15.235 × RIG's prevailing price (a positive arb spread), establishing a margin of safety to close.
- Continued backlog accumulation above $5B with floater utilization recovering toward the high-70s/low-80s, strengthening the standalone floor and reducing break-downside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- DOJ demands structural divestitures the parties signal they will not accept, or a deal termination, combined with a deteriorating oil/utilization backdrop that would push standalone value below the high-$60s.
- A sharp decline in Transocean's share price that drags the implied deal value well below VAL's standalone floor while the deal is still pending.
- Material ARO deterioration (a call on the $1.1B funding obligation, continued PIK with notes non-renewal risk) that impairs standalone value in a break.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are any DOJ resolution or divestiture disclosure, the next quarterly print (utilization trajectory of the four idle drillships), and confirmation of the shareholder-vote outcome. Monitor RIG's share price continuously, as it directly sets implied deal value.

## Conclusion

Valaris is a recovering, premium-fleet offshore driller with a clean balance sheet, rising backlog, and forward earnings being revised upward, but as a position today it is a Transocean merger-arbitrage instrument whose value turns on the DOJ antitrust process and RIG's share price. The prior trajectory marked the stock down on a stale $60 standalone anchor that is incoherent for a stock pinned to a live exchange ratio; on the company's own forward data, the break-downside is more contained than a bear case implies, sitting in the high-$60s to mid-$70s rather than the mid-$50s. With the arb spread indeterminate, a live upside catalyst in clearance, an un-shortable structure (expensive carry, squeeze-on-close risk), and a supported downside floor, the risk/reward is two-sided. The call is HOLD on a binary event. Existing holders should retain into the DOJ resolution; new capital lacks a demonstrable margin of safety to deploy until the spread or the regulatory path clarifies.
