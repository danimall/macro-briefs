## Executive Summary

ANI Pharmaceuticals is a three-legged specialty pharma company — Purified Cortrophin Gel (rare disease), the ILUVIEN/YUTIQ retina franchise, and a deliberately fragmented hard-to-make generics platform — that delivered $883.4M of FY2025 revenue (+43.8%) and is guiding to $1,080-1,140M for FY2026. On headline multiples it screens as one of the cheapest growth stories in the sector: at $82.63 the equity is $1,897M, enterprise value approximately $2,158M, and the stock trades at 8.8x the $9.44 midpoint of FY2026 adjusted non-GAAP diluted EPS guidance and 7.4x the $292.5M midpoint of adjusted EBITDA guidance, against guided revenue growth of 22-29%.

The discount is earned. ANI pays Merck a perpetual, tiered royalty on Cortrophin Gel net sales, and the FY2025 10-K states that in 2025 the company "surpassed the highest royalty tier for incremental net sales." The blended rate has gone from ~10% (2023) to the upper teens (2024) to the low twenties (2025), is guided to the high twenties in 2026, and moves "into the 30% range" as sales approach $1B. Working the guidance midpoints implies a top marginal tier of roughly 38-40%: approximately 40 cents of every incremental Cortrophin dollar leaves before manufacturing, distribution, or selling cost. Management's own FY2026 adjusted gross margin guidance of 59.9-60.9%, against H1 actual of ~61.7%, embeds continued compression. Cortrophin is a scaling asset with deteriorating incremental unit economics, which is the correct way to read the ~7x EV/EBITDA multiple.

The August 7 print sharpened the issue rather than resolving it. Revenue of $266.0M (+25.9%) and adjusted EPS of $2.21 both beat, and total revenue, EBITDA and EPS guidance were all held, but Cortrophin guidance was cut from $540-575M to $520-540M, a 4.9% reduction at the midpoint, with the gap filled by $39.3M of H1 Harmony pitolisant licensing revenue and generics strength. Excluding the non-recurring $15.0M upfront and $8.0M milestone, H1 adjusted EBITDA grew 6.5% rather than the reported 28.4%; excluding all Harmony revenue, Q2 adjusted EBITDA was essentially flat year over year on ~17% underlying revenue growth. Some of that flatness is a forward-investment artifact: the ~50% Rare Disease sales-force expansion (roughly 120 to 180 reps) only became fully operational at the end of June 2026. The reduced Cortrophin guidance still requires an H2/H1 ratio of 1.76x versus the 1.59x actually delivered in 2025. Repeating last year's seasonality produces roughly $497M, about $23M below the new guidance floor. That is the single most decision-relevant unknown, and it resolves at the Q3 report.

We move to a second consecutive HOLD, with the overall score easing to 5.6 from 5.5 in July on a slightly different composition (valuation marginally better on a de-risked guide, risk marginally worse on the flagship cut). Probability-weighted expected return from $82.63 is roughly +12%, which is positive but insufficient given weak management-quality and risk readings, an unresolved execution binary, and several verification gaps.

## Company Overview

ANI operates two reporting segments — Rare Disease and Brands, and Generics and Other — from three U.S. manufacturing sites (two in Baudette, Minnesota; East Windsor, New Jersey, acquired with Novitium in 2021). All three sites carry VAI or NAI classification, with East Windsor receiving NAI, the best available outcome, after a July 2026 FDA inspection.

**Cortrophin Gel** is the flagship: $347.8M of FY2025 net revenue (+75.6%), approximately 39% of total company revenue versus 32% in 2024. It is one of only two ACTH products marketed in the U.S., with five Orange Book–listed patents running to late 2043. H1 2026 revenue was $192.2M (+42.9%), Q2 $117.1M (+43.5%). The expanded sales force is being directed at an acute gouty arthritis flare indication unique to Cortrophin within the class.

**Retina (ILUVIEN and YUTIQ)** came via the September 2024 all-cash acquisition of Alimera Sciences ($5.50/share plus a CVR, plus $72.5M of debt repaid; approximately $401.3M total investing outflow). FY2025 revenue was $74.9M; FY2026 guidance is $78-83M globally across the U.S. and ~24 other countries. Q2 2026 revenue fell 16.1% to $18.7M, attributed by management to international shipment timing; H1 was down 1.2%.

**Generics** produced $384.1M in FY2025 (+27.6%) across roughly 135 product families, with 13 launches in 2025, 17 in 2024, and 12 already completed year-to-date 2026 against a 15+ annual target. Management states no single generic product will exceed 10% of Generics revenue in 2026, a structurally lower-concentration model than most generic peers. ANI ranks #2 in industry Competitive Generic Therapy filings.

A legacy **Brands** portfolio ($61.3M in FY2025, −5.3%; H1 2026 −37.0%) is in structural decline.

Two adjustments matter to the FY2025 figures. First, reported cost of sales improved from 40.7% to 38.6% of revenue, but the 10-K attributes essentially all of that to the non-recurrence of $13.6M of Alimera inventory step-up amortization in 2024; underlying mix moved the other way. Second, the $111.1M of operating income includes a $31.0M non-cash gain from contingent-consideration remeasurement (27.9% of operating income): $21.0M from writing EyePoint accrued licensor payments to zero, $7.6M from marking the Alimera CVR from $9.0M to $1.4M, and $2.6M from Novitium. Excluding it, operating income was $80.1M, a 9.1% margin against 12.7% reported. These are liability fair-value marks and affect GAAP income, not cash.

**Margin trajectory is the live issue.** GAAP gross margin was 62.4% in Q2 2026 versus 64.7% (−230bp) and 61.5% in H1 versus 63.9% (−240bp). FY2026 adjusted gross margin guidance of 59.9-60.9% carries an explicit footnote attributing the level to the high-twenties blended Merck royalty. Working the guidance: revenue midpoint $1,110M × 60.4% = ~$670M adjusted gross profit; H1 delivered ~$311M on ~61.7%; the implied H2 margin is ~59.3%, a further ~240bp of sequential compression. Accrued royalties corroborate the mechanism, rising from $22.6M (Dec 2024) to $48.5M (Dec 2025) to approximately $55.8M at June 2026.

**Cash generation is real but lumpy.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $185.2M against $13.8M of capex, so free cash flow of ~$171.4M (19.4% margin), and cash rose from $144.9M to $285.6M. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $115.0M versus $110.8M, only +3.8% despite far higher net income — the gap is working-capital timing (H1 2025 benefited from inflows in government rebates, returned-goods reserve and accrued royalties; H1 2026 saw outflows in the first two plus a ~$12.3M tax drag). This is a business whose rebate, chargeback and returns accruals swing materially: the 10-K discloses that a 10% change in the government rebate estimate alone would move FY2025 revenue by $7.8M. Half-year cash flow should not be annualized.

**Balance sheet at June 30, 2026.** Term Loan A of ~$304.7M (SOFR + 2.25-3.00%, ~6.33% contractual at Dec 2025, matures Sept 16, 2029, with a swap fixing $139.4M notional at ~2.313%); 2.25% convertible senior notes of $316.25M principal due Sept 1, 2029 (conversion price $74.11; capped calls struck at $114.02 covering ~4.3M shares); $74.9M undrawn on a $75M revolver; cash of $360.2M. Net debt is therefore ~$260.8M, or 1.0x TTM adjusted EBITDA of ~$260M. First-lien net leverage against a 3.00x covenant (steppable to 3.50x post-acquisition) and 3.00x minimum interest coverage are comfortable.

Full conversion of the notes would issue 4.267M shares — 18.6% of the existing 22.96M, equal to 15.7% post-issuance ownership dilution. Three qualifications: ANI must settle principal in cash and may elect cash or shares above it; the capped calls neutralize economics between $74.11 and $114.02; and the notes were not convertible at June 30 because the price trigger requires 130% of $74.11, or $96.34, for 20 of 30 trading days. At $82.63 the stock is 11.5% above the conversion price but 14.2% below the trigger.

A screening Altman Z-Score of ~2.1 sits in the grey zone. That reflects model fit against an intangible-heavy, acquisition-built balance sheet with modest retained earnings, not solvency stress — 1.0x net leverage, 2.7x current ratio, $171M of FY2025 FCF and an undrawn revolver are the relevant facts.

**Effective tax rate is a growing EPS drag.** FY2025 was 18.2%; H1 2026 ran ~29.9% (Q2 ~33.4%); FY2026 guidance assumes 26-28% GAAP. Drivers include state taxes and §162(m) non-deductible officer compensation. This compresses the EBITDA-to-EPS bridge in a way that is easy to miss.

Financial health scores 6.8: leverage and liquidity are genuinely strong, but the twin September 2029 maturities, the working-capital volatility, and the tax step-up temper the score.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance implies 22-29% revenue growth. The composition matters more than the rate.

**Cortrophin.** The guidance cut to $520-540M from $540-575M is the first negative datapoint on the flagship since re-launch. The required H2 of $337.8M at midpoint means 63.7% of the year lands in the second half, an H2/H1 ratio of 1.76x. Reconstructing 2025 from disclosed growth rates gives H1 2025 of $134.5M and H2 2025 of $213.3M, a ratio of 1.59x. ANI's seasonality is real and documented — Q1 2026 Cortrophin of $75.1M was 14.2% of the $530M midpoint, consistent with management's 13-14% framework driven by payer deductible resets and gross-to-net — but 2026 requires a steeper skew than 2025 produced. The bridge is the sales-force expansion completed at end-June plus the gout initiative. It is plausible, execution-dependent, and testable in roughly ninety days.

**Harmony pitolisant license.** Novitium granted Harmony an exclusive license expanding Harmony's pitolisant IP estate plus a co-exclusive license for a novel formulation, receiving $15.0M upfront, up to $10.0M in development milestones, and low-single-digit royalties on pitolisant product net sales. H1 2026 contribution: $15.0M upfront (Q1) + $16.3M royalties + $8.0M milestone (Q2) = $39.3M, at essentially no incremental cost of sales. Approximately $2M of remaining milestone is expected in Q3. Q2 royalties of $9.7M annualize to roughly $39M, but that is a one-quarter run rate offered as sensitivity only — WAKIX seasonality, gross-to-net, royalty tiering and the outcome of the underlying patent litigation are all unknown.

**The earnings-quality bridge:**

| | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reported adjusted EBITDA | $134.6M | $104.8M | +28.4% |
| Ex $23.0M one-time Harmony | $111.6M | $104.8M | +6.5% |
| Ex all $39.3M Harmony | $95.3M | $104.8M | −9.1% |

The honest reading is the middle line plus a growing recurring royalty: mid-single-digit underlying EBITDA growth on ~17% underlying revenue growth, with the royalty escalator and the sales-force investment consuming the operating leverage. Reported ~30% EBITDA growth is not a clean read.

**Estimate revisions are mixed rather than confirming.** Current-year consensus rose 3.8% over 90 days to $9.42 and next year 1.6% to $10.76, but next-quarter estimates fell 7.0%. The four-quarter beat record is unblemished (5.7%, 54.0%, 14.5%, 14.0% surprises), which supports confidence in the guided range. The forward momentum is modest and mixed, and does not justify overriding valuation and quality concerns.

**Retina and Brands are drags.** Brands fell 37.0% in H1. Retina guidance of $78-83M implies +4-11% for the year, but Q2 declined 16.1%, and three separate accounting marks say the franchise sits well below deal underwriting: the Alimera CVR went $9.0M → $1.4M → ~$0.5M with the company stating it does not expect to achieve the 2026 milestone (which requires 2026 net revenue above $140.0M, roughly 1.8x the current run rate), and the EyePoint accrued licensor liability — 30% of annual U.S. net sales of certain products above thresholds "beginning at $70.0 million in 2025, and increasing annually thereafter" — was written to zero with the 10-K stating no further payments are anticipated in fiscal 2026-2028. Contingent thresholds are frequently set above a buyer's base case, so missing them is not conclusive; the zero mark on EyePoint does, however, imply ANI's own forecast keeps U.S. retina sales below an escalating threshold that started at $70M. The SYNCHRONICITY open-label ILUVIEN data at a Q4 2026 medical conference is a defined near-term catalyst.

Growth prospects score 6.0: revenue growth is high and broad-based across generics and Cortrophin, but profit growth is materially thinner than the top line and the flagship's guidance just moved the wrong way.

## Competitive Landscape

**Cortrophin's franchise position is strong; its value capture is not.** ANI is one of two ACTH marketers in the U.S., protected by patents to 2043 and by the exceptional regulatory difficulty of an ACTH filing. Per the company's August 2026 presentation, the U.S. ACTH market went from ~$594M (2021) and ~$537M (2023) — a category then shrinking 4-6% annually — to ~$684M (2024), ~$1.0B (2025), and greater than $1.3B expected in 2026. Implied ANI share is ~34.8% in 2025 and ~41% at the FY2026 guidance midpoint. Both figures rest on ANI's internal market estimates, not independent data.

Cortrophin's growth has been substantially category expansion shared with the Acthar marketer, not pure share capture from a static pool. A two-player market growing 28%+ with a patent-protected asset is a good place to be. But the category's growth depends on both incumbents' promotional spending, and ANI's incremental economics are impaired by the royalty escalator in a way the top line conceals. The 5% guidance reduction is the first evidence that share and category growth together are running below management's own prior expectation.

**Generics positioning is genuinely differentiated and improving.** Roughly 135 product families, no product above 10% of segment revenue in 2026, 12-17 launches per year, and a #2 industry rank in CGT filings constitute a repeatable, low-concentration engine. Each individual 180-day exclusivity is short-lived by design — prucalopride's expired in late June 2025, creating a 2026 comparison headwind — but the pipeline cadence has held.

**Tariff posture is a relative advantage.** Approximately 95% of revenue derives from finished goods manufactured in the U.S., with API country of origin at 56% U.S., 20% India, 18% Europe and only 4% China. Under pharmaceutical tariff scenarios, ANI is better positioned than most generic manufacturers, and the FY2025 10-K indicates the issue remained live at filing.

**Retina trajectory is the negative.** Q2 down 16.1%, CVR marked to near zero, EyePoint threshold liability at zero, and the franchise at roughly half the scale the acquisition's contingent architecture assumed. The SWK royalty buyout for $17.3M in March 2025 permanently removed a 3.125% royalty and was a clean, accretive simplification, but it does not change franchise scale.

Competitive position scores 5.9: a durable duopoly and a differentiated generics platform, offset by compromised value capture on the flagship, a decelerating flagship guide, and a deteriorating retina franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**Merck royalty escalator (highest conviction).** Filing-verified. Success and margin compression are the same event. The obligation is perpetual and does not expire with a patent. Mitigating: it is a percentage obligation, never a solvency threat, and the blended rate rises asymptotically toward the marginal rate, so incremental compression decelerates. The ~38-40% marginal tier estimate depends on reading "low twenties" and "high twenties" as ~22% and ~28%; a different reading shifts the estimate several points.

**Cortrophin concentration and the H2 ramp.** Cortrophin is 39% of FY2025 revenue from one product whose guidance was just cut, requiring a steeper back-half skew than 2025 delivered. Supply is thin and specialized — long-term agreements with a single supplier of the primary raw material, a single API supplier, and a fill/finish CMO — and one domestic supplier accounted for 17% of raw-material purchases in 2025, up from 12%.

**Customer concentration is flat, not improving.** FY2025: three customers at 22%, 17% and 14% = 53% of revenue, with 64% of net AR. The comparable 2024 top-three figure was 53% as well; the disclosure format changed from four named customers to three, which can create a false impression of diversification. In Q2 2026, ≥10% customers summed to ~50% with the largest at ~24% — the single largest customer's share increased. Mix shift toward rare disease does not reduce channel concentration; these are the same national wholesalers.

**Litigation with economic content.** The CG Oncology royalty claim (reported as 5% on worldwide cretostimogene net sales) drew a no-damages jury verdict on July 29, 2025, and post-trial motions were denied July 16, 2026, with ANI intending to appeal to the Delaware Supreme Court. A material zero-cost option is close to extinguished; ANI separately holds CG Oncology equity carried at $9.1M at Dec 2025. Novitium is named in the California ranitidine JCCP with a bellwether trial reportedly set for October 2026. And in the pitolisant patent litigation where Novitium is now a co-plaintiff with Harmony, defendants have asserted antitrust counterclaims against both — unadjudicated allegations by adverse litigants, common in Hatch-Waxman practice, but a new class of exposure created directly by the license.

**Unapproved marketed products.** Four products without an approved NDA or ANDA contributed approximately $11.9M of H1 2026 revenue and can be required off the market at any time.

**Governance items.** Related-party payments to India-based development and service entities (Esjay, Nuray, SThree, Scitus) associated with Muthusamy Shanmugam — simultaneously an ANI executive and a board member — rose to approximately $19.6M in H1 2026 from ~$6.7M, with ANI also capitalizing ~$6.3M of intangibles acquired from Nuray. The plausible benign explanation is that this outsourced network came with the 2021 Novitium acquisition and the spend tracks a step-up in launch and 505(b)(2) activity. We are not attributing motive; the magnitude of the increase combined with the dual role and an asset purchase from the same network is nonetheless the governance item most worth monitoring. Separately, an April 7, 2026 Item 4.01 auditor change is unverified as to whether it was a dismissal or resignation and whether any disagreements were disclosed. No Item 4.02 non-reliance filing accompanied it, the 10-K was filed on time, and Q2 disclosure controls were reported effective — all consistent with routine rotation, but unconfirmed. Two Item 5.02 filings (Dec 1, 2025 and May 21, 2026) are likewise unread.

**Compensation design.** Performance measures are Revenue, Adjusted non-GAAP EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA growth and Relative TSR. No metric references GAAP earnings, cash flow, ROIC or gross margin. For a company whose flagship's defining economic feature is a royalty scaling with revenue, a plan weighted to top-line and adjusted EBITDA growth rewards expansion whose incremental margin is ~40 points below average. Adjusted EBITDA also excludes stock compensation. Relative TSR performance has been outstanding — cumulative $100 invested at $271.83 versus $71.73 for the peer group — so the realized outcomes are defensible on alignment; the design still points the wrong way for the next leg.

**Capital allocation.** A $100M buyback was authorized effective May 8, 2026 and left entirely unused, with FY2026 guidance explicitly assuming no repurchases, while management states it is exploring expansion of the Rare Disease business. At ~9x forward adjusted EPS, the opportunity cost is real. Investors should expect acquisitions, not buybacks. Conservatism ahead of the 2029 maturities and preserving first-lien covenant headroom for an acquisition are equally plausible readings.

**Insider activity.** Consistent net selling with no open-market purchases: the CEO sold 55,000 shares at ~$77.57 on May 11, 2026 (retaining ~349,889) and adopted a new 10b5-1 plan on May 26 for the August-December 2026 window; sales also appear from the CFO, General Counsel, segment heads, CHRO and a director. Following PSU vesting at 200% of target and a 172% relative TSR run, routine diversification through pre-scheduled plans is the most probable explanation, and blackout windows genuinely constrain opportunistic buying. Consensus estimates for the full year are rising, which is the dominant signal. But the absence of any open-market purchase in the high-$70s, including after a Cortrophin guidance reduction, provides no offsetting positive. Mildly negative.

Risk factors score 4.7. Management quality scores 4.4, consistent with the prior report's 4.5, with the related-party escalation and the unused authorization offsetting a strong TSR record and the accretive SWK buyout.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $122 (+48% from $82.63)

Cortrophin H2 delivers $340M+ as the 50%-larger sales force, fully operational from July 1, converts a two-player ACTH market guided above $1.3B; the acute gouty arthritis initiative opens an indication unavailable to the competitor and expands the category further. Generics compound at 15+ launches annually with no single product above 10% of segment revenue. The WAKIX royalty proves durable and scales — Q2's $9.7M annualizes near $39M at essentially zero incremental cost — permanently improving earnings mix. SYNCHRONICITY data in Q4 returns ILUVIEN to growth. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $11.50, the multiple re-rates to 10.5x SBC-burdened cash earnings as investors recognize that the blended royalty rate rises asymptotically and incremental compression decelerates, and 18.1% short interest amplifies the move. $122 also aligns with the $124 top analyst target.

### Bear Case — $62 (−25% from $82.63)

Q3 Cortrophin comes in near a 1.59x H2/H1 seasonal path, implying a full year around $497M and a second guidance reduction. The blended Merck royalty moves toward 30% while the sales-force expansion is a permanent cost step, so adjusted gross margin settles below 59% and adjusted EBITDA growth ex-Harmony stays flat to negative. The $15M upfront and $10M of milestones do not repeat in 2027, creating a ~$25M EBITDA headwind. Retina keeps declining, Brands keeps eroding 30%+, the effective tax rate holds at 27-28%, and the pitolisant antitrust counterclaims or the October 2026 ranitidine bellwether create a discrete liability. SBC-burdened recurring EPS proves closer to $7.50 and the multiple holds at 8x → ~$60-62. Governance concerns compound the discount.

### Base Case — $93 (+12.6% from $82.63)

Cortrophin lands near $520M, the low end of reduced guidance, on a modestly steeper-than-2025 skew. FY2026 adjusted EPS finishes near the $9.44 midpoint. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches ~$10.00 — below the $10.76 consensus, haircut for the royalty step and the loss of the one-time Harmony items, partly offset by full-year WAKIX royalties and generic launches. Applying 9.3x to $10.00 gives $93. Cross-check on enterprise value: FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of ~$310M at 7.5x is $2,325M, less ~$200M of net debt after another year of deleveraging, divided by 23.5M diluted shares = ~$90. We center the base at $93.

Probability weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear yields an expected return of approximately +12% from $82.63, and approximately +18% from the $78.20 last trade. Positive, but not with the confidence or margin of safety a stronger rating requires.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

## Conclusion

ANI is a well-run, cash-generative specialty pharma company with a genuinely differentiated asset base — a patent-protected ACTH product in a two-player market growing 28%+, a deliberately fragmented generics platform with no single product above 10% of segment revenue, 95% U.S.-manufactured finished goods in a tariff-exposed industry, and 1.0x net leverage against $360M of cash. It also carries a perpetual royalty on its flagship that has passed its top tier, so roughly 40 cents of every incremental Cortrophin dollar is committed before any cost of goods. That is why the stock trades at 7.4x guided EV/EBITDA, and it is why we do not treat the multiple as a mispricing.

The August print made the trade-off explicit. Revenue and adjusted EPS beat, total guidance held, and the flagship's guidance was cut 4.9% at the midpoint, with the difference made up by a $39.3M licensing contribution of which $23M does not repeat. Strip the one-time items and H1 adjusted EBITDA grew 6.5% on 17% underlying revenue growth; strip the recurring royalty too and Q2 was flat. Some of that is the cost of a sales-force expansion whose revenue arrives in H2 and 2027. Whether it is investment timing or margin erosion resolves at the Q3 report against a Cortrophin bar that requires a steeper seasonal skew than 2025 produced.

Adjusted for stock compensation and one-time licensing, the stock trades at roughly 11x recurring earnings with a 9% FCF yield, a probability-weighted expected return near +12%, and 25% downside if the H2 ramp disappoints. Every analyst target sits above spot, but sentiment is degrading and 18% of the float is short. Management quality and risk both read weak here, on tripled related-party payments to an executive-director's affiliated network, continuous insider selling with no offsetting purchase, an unused buyback at 9x forward earnings, and an unverified auditor change. **HOLD.** Existing owners are adequately compensated for waiting through one print. New capital should wait for Q3 Cortrophin above $155M, positive ex-Harmony EBITDA growth, or a price below $70.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: GAAP trailing P/E was overstated (21.2x vs. correct ~24.9x using vendor EPS of $3.32), placing ANIP above rather than at the peer median; the "cheap on GAAP" framing versus peers is accordingly weaker than the report implied.
