## Executive Summary

Atlassian reported one of the better-looking quarters in application software in Q3 FY26: revenue of $1,787.0M, up 31.7% year over year, non-GAAP operating margin of 34% (up 800bps), and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $1.75 against $0.97. The stock has rallied roughly 80% off its 10 April low of $56.01. It is also down 47% over twelve months and roughly 49% from the 52-week high of $199.09.

The central analytical problem is that the headline growth rate is not the underlying growth rate, and the company's own guidance says so. Nine-month deferred revenue additions — Atlassian's own rollforward flow — grew 16.0% ($4,736M versus $4,084M) while reported nine-month revenue grew 25.4%. The total deferred revenue balance *fell* from $2,481.3M at 30 June 2025 to $2,411.6M at 31 March 2026, a nine-month decline during a period of 25% reported revenue growth. Data Center revenue accelerated to +44% in a product Atlassian has announced it is winding down. And Q4 FY26 guidance of $1.653–1.661B implies a 7.3% *sequential* decline from Q3 and a deceleration to +19.7% year over year from +31.7%. The mechanism that reconciles all four observations is straightforward: Data Center term licenses recognize the license component upfront at delivery, existing customers are signing multi-year renewals ahead of the announced sales cutoff and support end, and future-period revenue is landing in current periods without corresponding billings or cash.

Two things follow. First, the 34% non-GAAP margin is probably partly transitory, since upfront license revenue carries near-zero incremental delivery cost. Second, FY27 is the year the tailwind laps, and the licenses already signed will not renew. Set against that, free cash flow has now been flat-to-declining for two consecutive years against 20–25% revenue growth: FY25 FCF of roughly $1.42B was essentially flat on FY24 despite $857M of incremental revenue, and nine-month FY26 FCF of $844.4M was down 20% from $1,055.2M. FCF margin fell from 27.5% to 17.6%, or 20.1% adding back $119.4M of disclosed restructuring cash. Stock-based compensation of roughly $1.6B annualized exceeds that free cash flow. On an SBC-expensed basis, economic owner earnings are near zero. We rate TEAM HOLD with a base case of $112.

## Company Overview

Atlassian sells collaboration, work-management and IT service management software. Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management constitute the majority of revenue per the FY25 10-K. Adjacent assets include Bitbucket, Trello, Loom, Compass and Rovo, the AI agent platform. Products are increasingly sold as bundled "Collections."

Revenue is reported by type rather than by segment. FY25 total revenue was $5,215.3M, up 20%, with Cloud at $3,447M (+28%) and Data Center at $1,467M (+21%). Server is discontinued; Data Center is the last self-managed offering and is itself now being wound down.

The distribution model carries a structural dependency that is frequently overlooked. Per the FY25 10-K, over 50% of FY25 revenue was derived from channel partners' sales efforts, and at times during FY25 one solution partner represented more than 10% of total accounts receivable. These partners are independent resellers and implementers — the counterparties that owe Atlassian cash. The agreements are non-exclusive, terminable on limited notice with little penalty, and partners may carry competing products simultaneously. No single end customer exceeded 10% of revenue.

**A required correction to the supplied data.** The $16.54B market cap in the data set divides by $103.60 to 159.6M shares, almost exactly the Class A count alone. Per the Q3 FY26 10-Q there were approximately 160.3M Class A and 94.1M Class B shares at 31 March 2026, roughly 254.5M total, with identical economic rights and differing only in voting (Class B carries ten votes). At the $101.015 basis, equity value is 254.5M × $101.015 ≈ **$25.71B**, and with net cash of roughly $150M, enterprise value is approximately **$25.56B**. Using the Class-A-only figure would understate the multiple by roughly 40%. All valuation work below uses the full count.

**Billings versus revenue.** The 9.4-point gap between deferred revenue additions growth (16.0%) and reported revenue growth (25.4%) is the single most important number in this analysis. It is a flow-to-flow comparison using Atlassian's own disclosure, and it is corroborated independently by the balance-to-balance decline in total deferred revenue across nine months of 25% reported growth. A subscription business booking 25% growth does not normally shrink its deferred revenue balance.

**Q4 guidance confirms the point without requiring any inference.** Q4 FY25 revenue derives as FY25 $5,215.3M less 9M FY25 $3,831.0M = $1,384.3M. The Q4 FY26 guide midpoint of $1,657M is +19.7% year over year and −7.3% sequentially from Q3. For a business that is overwhelmingly ratable subscription, a guided sequential decline is close to structurally impossible unless Q3 contained non-repeating upfront revenue. Implied FY26 revenue: $4,805.8M + $1,657M = $6,462.8M, +23.9%.

The counter-evidence deserves an honest accounting. Cloud grew 29% on its own, which is genuine demand. Remaining performance obligations were reported at $4.0B, up 37% — inconsistent with a pure pull-forward reading and suggestive that forward contracted commitments are building. RPO is not equivalent to total backlog and roughly 69% is expected within twelve months. Alternative explanations for the deferred-revenue behaviour also exist: a mix shift toward monthly or annually billed Cloud, or changed partner invoicing cadence given that more than half of revenue flows through intermediaries. None of these change the conclusion drawn from the guide itself.

**Margin quality.** The 800bp non-GAAP margin expansion is very likely flattered by the same effect, since upfront license revenue carries essentially no incremental delivery cost. Atlassian has not disaggregated margin by revenue type, so this is inference rather than disclosure, but it is the natural corollary of the revenue mechanism.

**Cash flow is the cleanest signal, because it is immune to the recognition question.** FY25 FCF of roughly $1.42B was flat against FY24 on $857M of incremental revenue; FCF margin compressed from ~32.5% to ~27.1%. Nine-month FY26 FCF of $844.4M was down 20% while revenue rose 25%; FCF margin fell from 27.5% to 17.6%. Adding back the disclosed $119.4M of restructuring cash gives 20.1%, still roughly 740bps below prior year. MD&A attributes the decline to higher cash paid to employees and vendors and higher cash taxes. Some drivers are timing-related: the FY24 Australian Taxation Office settlement of $117.4M, Pillar Two global minimum tax first applying in FY25, and heavy Q2/Q4 seasonality disclosed in the 10-K.

We deliberately do not annualize nine-month FCF. As a labeled sensitivity: Q4 FY25 FCF was $1,415.6M − $1,055.2M = $360.4M. If Q4 FY26 merely matches it, FY26 FCF lands near $1.20B, an 18.6% margin. A stronger seasonal quarter could push it to $1.25–1.30B. On any of these, FY26 FCF finishes below FY24's ~$1.42B on roughly 48% more revenue.

**Stock compensation exceeds free cash flow.** FY25 SBC was $1,362M, 26% of revenue; nine-month FY26 SBC was $1,212M, 25.2%, annualizing near $1.6B. Unrecognized RSU compensation is $2.7B and RSUs outstanding rose to 21.2M from 16.6M. Because grants are dollar-denominated, a lower share price mechanically issues more shares per dollar: the weighted-average FY26 grant-date fair value was $152.63 against today's $101.015, so FY27 grants will issue roughly 51% more shares per dollar of grant value. Stated precisely, 21.2M RSUs plus roughly 1.4M restricted share awards is 22.6M unvested awards, 8.9% of the 254.5M shares outstanding, or 8.2% post-issuance ownership dilution if all vested with no offsetting repurchase. These vest over years and are not current fully diluted capitalization. Buybacks are currently more than absorbing them — total shares fell from 262.98M at 30 June 2025 to roughly 254.5M at 31 March 2026, down 3.2% in nine months.

**The balance sheet cushion was largely spent.** Cash and marketable securities fell from roughly $2,935M to roughly $1,139M across nine months, a 61% drawdown. Debt is $989M carrying value ($500M of 5.250% notes due 2029 and $500M of 5.500% due 2034), leaving net cash near $150M against roughly $1.95B a year earlier. The $750M revolver was undrawn. Uses reconcile: $1,436.6M of repurchases plus $1,231.8M of cash acquisitions equals $2,668.4M against $844.4M of FCF, a $1,824M gap versus the roughly $1,796M decline in cash and securities. This is a deliberate allocation choice rather than distress: no maturity before 2029, and roughly $1.2B of annual FCF comfortably services $989M of fixed-rate debt. The optionality a $2B net cash position provided is nonetheless gone.

Separately, the Q3 FY26 10-Q discloses roughly $3.0B of *new* non-cancelable purchase commitments entered during the nine months, with terms of two to six years. Against $6.46B of FY26E revenue that is a large incremental fixed obligation. The 10-Q does not disclose counterparties or purpose; attribution to cloud infrastructure and third-party AI model capacity is inference from context, and multi-year committed pricing is typically cheaper per unit than on-demand, so the commitment may be margin-protective.

## Growth Analysis

Three separate growth questions matter, and they resolve differently.

**Data Center is a timing problem that becomes a level problem.** Per the Q3 FY26 10-Q, Atlassian has ceased selling Data Center term licenses to new customers, will stop selling to existing customers in March 2028, and will end maintenance and support in March 2029. (These specific months are as read in the filing; external corroboration attempts failed on search rate limits rather than on contradictory evidence, so the exact dates should be re-verified against Atlassian's end-of-life documentation. The existence and direction of the wind-down is independently corroborated by the +44% growth, the deferred revenue decline and the guided sequential drop.) The 10-K frames the economics plainly: revenue recognized from Cloud in the initial year is typically lower than from Data Center, and slower-than-anticipated migration would negatively affect revenue growth and profitability. FY27 therefore faces a double headwind: the recognition tailwind laps, and multi-year licenses already signed do not come up for renewal.

**Cloud is the real engine, and its new-logo motion weakened sharply in Q3.** Cloud grew 29%. Customers with more than $10,000 in Cloud ARR ran 50,715 (Mar-25) → 51,978 → 53,017 → 55,369 → 55,913 (Mar-26), up 10.2% year over year. But net additions collapsed to +544 in Q3 FY26 from +2,352 in Q2. With Cloud revenue up 29% against a 10.2% increase in this count, growth is coming predominantly from expansion and pricing rather than new logos. Caveats apply: only five quarters are disclosed, it is a threshold metric customers can cross in either direction, and Q3 is Atlassian's seasonally weakest quarter per its own disclosure. One quarter is not a trend, but it is the weakest point in the disclosed series.

**AI is a genuine two-sided question about the addressable market.** The bear case is mechanical: Atlassian charges per human seat, its core buyer is the software engineering organization, and the 10-K itself concedes that increased AI prevalence "may impact the work practices of software teams… and therefore our market opportunity." Atlassian's own March 2026 reduction of roughly 10% of the global workforce — about 1,600 roles, attributed to enterprise focus and the "AI era" — demonstrates the mechanism on its own P&L. The bull case is that the scarce asset shifts to the orchestration and context layer: who assigns work to which agent, with what permissions, against what enterprise knowledge, under what audit trail.

The most useful evidence cuts both ways. Per the CEO's reported remarks, before Atlassian acquired The Browser Company's Dia AI browser only four of 13,000 Atlassian employees were permitted to use it because of AI-in-browser security concerns, and he noted some customers take six months of queries before enabling Atlassian's AI platform. That establishes enterprise AI deployment is gated by governance and security rather than model capability, which supports durable value in the orchestration layer and slower seat erosion than the bear case implies. It equally establishes that Atlassian's own AI monetization will be slow. Rovo is bundled free with premium and enterprise Jira, Confluence and JSM, with the 10-K warning explicitly that the strategy "may further increase these hosting costs without corresponding revenue increases." Slow adoption plus free bundling plus $3.0B of new non-cancelable commitments is a margin issue before it is a revenue opportunity.

**Estimate revisions require careful reading.** Ninety-day revisions are strongly positive — current quarter +26.9%, current year +15.7%, next year +10.2% — reflecting the Q3 beat flowing through consensus. Thirty-day revisions have turned: next quarter −2.5%, next year −1.9%. The FY26 number ($5.51) and FY27 number ($6.10) imply only 10.7% non-GAAP EPS growth next year after a large step this year. That shape is exactly what a one-time recognition pull-forward produces, and the direction of the 30-day revision suggests the sell side has begun to model it.

Label discipline matters here. The 18.3x and 16.6x are non-GAAP multiples against non-GAAP EPS that exclude roughly $1.6B of annual SBC. GAAP EPS is negative (trailing −$0.98 per the market data). Comparing 18.3x to a GAAP P/E would be a category error.

Against the roughly 40x forward multiple typical of application software peers, 16.6x on FY27 looks like a substantial discount. Three considerations cut it down. First, the growth being bought is roughly 16% underlying and decelerating, not the 25–32% the reported line implies, and FY27 non-GAAP EPS growth of 10.7% puts PEG near 1.6 — no longer obviously cheap. Second, on an SBC-expensed basis owner earnings are approximately zero, so the discount is partly an artifact of which basis you choose. Third, a business with roughly 16% growth and an 18% FCF margin at 3.95x sales is priced near fair on a growth-plus-margin framework, not at a discount.

The freshly-revised sell side brackets the current price almost exactly. Cantor cut to $98 on 27 April, framing it as software multiple compression; BTIG cut to $110 on 28 April; Barclays raised to $112 on 7 May. The supplied consensus mean of $139.70 and high of $480 are contaminated by stale pre-drawdown targets — the $480 predates a 49% decline and should be disregarded. The median of $115 and the low of $95 are the informative ends. The freshly-updated cluster sits at or below the price, which is confirmation of fair value rather than of lagging targets.

On the 80% rally from the April low: prior appreciation is not a valuation input, and we do not penalize the stock for having recovered. What matters is that the recovery has already closed most of the gap to the recently-revised target cluster, so the mispricing that existed at $56 has largely been arbitraged. The remaining question is FY27 earnings power, which the 6 August print will address.

## Competitive Landscape

The FY25 10-K does not claim a moat. It says the markets are "fragmented, rapidly evolving, highly competitive" with "relatively low barriers to entry," concedes many competitors have greater resources, and notes that large organizations "have elected, and more may in the future elect, to develop or acquire their own internal collaboration and productivity software tools." That last clause has become more threatening than when it was drafted: AI code generation lowers the cost of building internal tooling, eroding the build-cost protection Atlassian's low end has historically enjoyed.

The defensible assets that emerge from the filings are installed-base entrenchment where Jira is the system of record for software development workflow, the Atlassian Marketplace third-party app ecosystem that deepens switching costs, an unusually low customer-acquisition cost structure inherited from the self-serve motion, and the emerging "Teamwork Graph" data asset. The Marketplace is double-edged: the 10-K discloses that third-party apps "have caused disruptions affecting multiple customers" and that Atlassian's ability to mandate security standards on those third parties "may be limited."

The competitive set is Microsoft (Azure DevOps, Teams, Loop, GitHub, bundled into existing enterprise agreements), ServiceNow in ITSM, GitLab, Asana and Monday.com. In AI coding, Cursor and Anthropic's Claude Code appear as integrated partners receiving work routed from Jira — they are owned and developed by those third parties, not Atlassian products. Atlassian is a purchaser of third-party frontier models, and the 10-K warns those providers "may terminate their relationships with us… or may make certain models or technologies more expensive for us to use." Positioning as the orchestration layer above suppliers who could disintermediate that layer is coherent strategy with real supplier-power risk.

Trajectory assessment: stable-to-improving at the enterprise end, where the Service Collection reportedly passed $1B ARR growing 30%+ with enterprise ARR inside it growing 50%+ and more than 600 customers above $1M ARR (these figures are company-asserted, relayed through a tier-3 aggregator, and were not verified in a filing). Weakening at the low end, where net logo additions in the >$10K Cloud ARR cohort collapsed to +544 in Q3. The most consequential competitive event is structural: the forced Data Center migration creates a re-evaluation moment for the entire self-managed installed base, precisely the customers most reluctant to move to cloud, often for regulatory or data-sovereignty reasons. Every one of those renewals becomes a competitive bid on a contract that previously auto-renewed.

One distinction to preserve: Atlassian's revenue *mix* is diversifying (Cloud share rising, expansion into IT and business teams), but the disclosed *counterparty* concentration did not improve — over 50% of revenue still originates through non-exclusive channel partners, one of which at times exceeded 10% of total accounts receivable. Mix shift is not concentration relief.

## Risk Assessment

**Data Center revenue cliff (highest conviction, near-dated).** The recognition tailwind laps in FY27, and forward licenses already signed will not renew. Reported growth must reset toward the ~16% underlying billings rate and could undershoot it if migration slips or customers defect. This is simultaneously the source of today's flattering optics and tomorrow's largest single risk.

**AI and seat-based pricing.** The 10-K names it directly. Atlassian's own 10% workforce reduction is corroborating evidence from inside the company.

**AI margin risk with a newly disclosed cost commitment.** Rovo is bundled free with premium and enterprise editions, with standard editions intended to follow, against roughly $3.0B of new two-to-six-year non-cancelable purchase commitments. The 10-K states explicitly that AI investments are expected "to continue to negatively impact our operating margins in the near term."

**Liquidity flexibility.** Cash and securities fell 61% in nine months; net cash is roughly $150M. Not a solvency issue given 2029/2034 maturities, an undrawn $750M revolver and roughly $1.2B of annual FCF, but the strategic buffer is gone. The Altman Z-Score near 1.42 on the FY25 balance sheet reads as distress, and its components deserve interpretation rather than either dismissal or alarm: the retained-earnings term is negative from SBC-driven accumulated deficits (GAAP net losses of $486.8M in FY23, $300.5M FY24, $256.7M FY25 per the proxy), the working-capital term is depressed because roughly $2.2B of current deferred revenue is a non-cash claim, and the EBIT term is near zero on a GAAP basis. The channel through which it *is* informative is liquidity, where the drawdown is real. The Z-Score was not recomputed on the March 2026 balance sheet.

**Channel and receivable concentration; dual-class entrenchment.** Class B holders control roughly 85% of voting power, substantially all held by entities affiliated with the co-founders. Class A holders cannot force strategic change or receive a control premium.

**Execution risk from near-total executive turnover.** The Q3 FY26 10-Q discloses that the President, CFO and CTO all stepped down from executive officer roles in that quarter — the quarter the stock bottomed and the 10% reduction was announced. Including the new CRO (January 2025) and Chief Product and AI Officer (November 2025), Atlassian replaced its President, CFO, CTO, CRO and product/AI leadership within roughly 20 months. Eight Item 5.02 filings appeared between April 2025 and March 2026, and the 11 March 2026 8-K carried both Item 2.05 (restructuring) and Item 5.02. No 8-K disclosed a dispute, restatement or material weakness, and the CRO and CPAIO hires look deliberate and additive; we do not infer cause from timing. The aggregate loss of institutional knowledge during a business-model transition is an execution risk regardless of cause.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($145, +43.5%).** The Data Center pull-forward is additive to genuine Cloud strength rather than a substitute for it — Cloud grew 29% independently, RPO of $4.0B rose 37%, net revenue retention is reportedly above 120%, and the Service Collection passed $1B ARR growing 30%+ with enterprise ARR inside it growing 50%+. The forced migration converts a lower-margin self-managed base into higher-lifetime-value Cloud contracts, and enterprise AI deployment being gated by governance and security rather than model capability means Jira's role as system of record and audit trail becomes more valuable, not less, as agents proliferate. Rovo at 5M monthly active users with AI credit usage growing over 20% month over month, plus the Dia and DX acquisitions, converts free bundling into consumption revenue in FY27. The 10% workforce cut plus a Cloud-revenue-linked incentive plan produces sustained margin expansion. At 16.6x FY27 non-GAAP EPS against roughly 40x for application software peers, modest re-rating on a credible FY27 guide produces significant upside. (Note that the ARR, NRR, RPO and Rovo figures are company-asserted, relayed through a tier-3 aggregator, and were not verified in a filing.)

**Bear case ($78, −22.8%).** FY27 guidance reveals underlying growth near or below 16% and the recognition tailwind reverses into a headwind, because the multi-year licenses signed in FY26 do not renew. The self-managed base uses the forced end-of-life as an exit rather than a migration, and Microsoft bundling and ServiceNow win a share of those contracts. Free Rovo bundling plus $3.0B of non-cancelable commitments keeps FCF margin near 18% while SBC stays at 25% of revenue, so owner earnings remain near zero and the 16.6x non-GAAP multiple is exposed as an accounting artifact. New logo additions stay near the +544 Q3 level, confirming that Cloud growth is expansion and pricing rather than market expansion, and AI-driven engineering headcount reduction begins to bite the seat base. The multiple compresses toward 3.0x EV/revenue in line with the broader application-software derating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (uncertain). We do not have sufficient evidence to make a confident directional call three days before the print that resolves the central question.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY27 revenue guidance implying at least 14% growth *with* an explicit reconciliation of the Data Center recognition effect, so that Cloud-only growth is verifiably at or above 25%.
2. Full-year FY26 free cash flow of at least $1.30B, with FY27 commentary indicating FCF margin expansion rather than further compression. This is the metric immune to the recognition question and the one that has failed for two consecutive years.
3. Net additions in the >$10K Cloud ARR cohort recovering above +1,500 per quarter, confirming the Q3 +544 was seasonal rather than structural.
4. Price near $80–85 with fundamentals unchanged, which would restore roughly 30% upside to the base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY27 revenue guidance below 10% growth, or a refusal to quantify the Data Center recognition lap.
2. FY26 FCF below $1.15B, or FY27 FCF guidance implying a margin below 18%, which would confirm structural rather than transitory cash-flow deterioration.
3. SBC rising above 27% of revenue while buyback capacity contracts, so share count begins rising again — the reflexive dilution mechanism turning against shareholders.
4. Any disclosure that the $3.0B of non-cancelable commitments carries take-or-pay economics materially above expected usage.

**Reassessment timeline.** 6 August 2026 (Q4 FY26 results and first FY27 guidance) is the primary date, with the ±14.7–18.1% implied move indicating the market expects it to be decisive. The second checkpoint is the Q1 FY27 print in late October, the first clean post-lap quarter. Existing holders should own through 6 August rather than add; new capital should wait for the FY27 guide. Given 177.7% near-term implied volatility, writing calls against an existing position into the print is well compensated.

## Conclusion

Atlassian is a genuinely important software franchise — inside 85% of the Fortune 500, with Jira as the system of record for software development workflow and a Marketplace ecosystem that raises switching costs — trading at 3.95x FY26E revenue and 16.6x FY27E non-GAAP EPS after a 49% decline from its high. That is not a demanding price for the asset.

It is close to a fair price for the economics currently on offer. The reported 31.7% growth rate overstates the business by roughly nine points on the company's own billings disclosure, and the Q4 guide implies a 7.3% sequential revenue decline that only a non-repeating upfront recognition effect explains. Free cash flow has been flat to declining for two consecutive years against 20–25% revenue growth, with margin falling from 27.5% to 17.6%. Stock compensation of roughly $1.6B exceeds free cash flow of roughly $1.2B, so the multiple is only defensible on a non-GAAP basis.
