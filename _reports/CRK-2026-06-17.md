**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Comstock Resources is a pure-play U.S. natural gas producer concentrated in the Haynesville and Bossier shales of North Louisiana and East Texas. The investment case sits at the intersection of two genuinely attractive structural tailwinds (Gulf Coast LNG export growth and emerging data-center power demand) and a balance sheet that leaves no room for execution error. At $13.34, the stock has fallen 49.6% over twelve months and roughly 38% from its March 2026 peak near $21, as the data-center euphoria around the NextEra/Texas Power Generation Hub faded and investors refocused on three uncomfortable facts: production is declining (-15% in 2025 and again in Q1 2026), free cash flow is negative, and the hedge book has flipped from a tailwind to a drag.

The core problem is the combination of commodity leverage and financial leverage; the Haynesville's proximity to Gulf Coast demand is a real cost advantage. With ~$3.0B of total debt, an Altman Z-Score of 1.48 (distress zone), a current ratio of 0.49, and only $14.8M of cash, CRK's equity functions as a high-beta call option on Henry Hub priced on a structure with meaningful interest drag (~$223M/year). The 2025 net income recovery to $396M was driven by gas prices recovering to $3.17/Mcf and a $292M asset-sale gain, not organic volume growth. Capex outspent operating cash flow in both 2025 (FCF roughly -$450M) and Q1 2026 ($405M capex vs. $272M operating cash flow).

We rate CRK AVOID, consistent with the prior four reports, and want to be precise about why. The estimate-revision picture is deteriorating: current-year consensus EPS has fallen 41.2% over 90 days and the current-quarter estimate is down 84% over 90 days. This is negative earnings momentum, the inverse of the signal that would justify a contrarian bid on a beaten-down name. The Sixth Street recapitalization (selling 27% of Pinnacle for $600M at a $2.2B EV while retaining 73% control) is a genuinely smart, equity-friendly deleveraging move and the strongest positive in the file, but not enough to offset the structural risk profile at the current setup. This is a name to avoid owning, not a name to short.

## Company Overview

Comstock operates one of the premier dry-gas positions in North America: 7.0 Tcfe of proved reserves (SEC pricing) with a PV-10 of $4.5B, a reserve life of roughly 16 years, and essentially 100% natural gas weighting. Only 41% of proved reserves are developed. The company holds ~1.07M gross acres (803K net) and has identified 1,848 legacy plus 3,343 Western Haynesville drilling locations, supporting management's "decades of inventory" framing. CRK operates 99% of its proved reserves with roughly 252 employees, using successful-efforts accounting.

Two growth engines define the strategy. First, the Western Haynesville, a deeper, higher-pressure and higher-temperature extension of the play being delineated (30 wells turned to sales through 2025). Second, the Texas Power Generation Hub, where the company's Western Haynesville acreage in Anderson County has been selected to host a 5.2-GW natural-gas-fired power complex tied to Japan's U.S. investment commitment, to be built and operated by NextEra Energy Resources. Comstock's role is gas supplier, with deliveries potentially approaching ~1 Bcf/d by 2031.

The company is a controlled entity. Dallas Cowboys owner Jerry Jones and affiliates hold the majority position; CEO M. Jay Allison has run the company since 1988 and President/CFO Roland Burns since 1990. This produces deeply aligned but highly concentrated control, with attendant key-man and succession risk.

## Financial Analysis

Comstock's financials trace a clean commodity cycle. Revenue ran $3.63B in the 2022 boom, collapsed to $1.25B in 2024 (a $230M net loss), and recovered to $2.22B in 2025 ($396M net income). The 2025 rebound is mostly price- and asset-sale-driven: realized gas rose to $3.17/Mcf (+60% YoY), and results included a $292M gain on the Shelby Trough/Cotton Valley divestitures plus a $62M unrealized derivative gain. Operating margin was roughly 29% and ROE about 16%.

Beneath the headline recovery, production is declining. Volumes fell 15% in 2025 to 450 Bcf and another 15% in Q1 2026 to 97.9 Bcf, as the company redirected capital toward exploratory Western Haynesville delineation and sold producing assets. This is a deliberate trade-off, but it means the revenue gain is price-driven, not volume-driven, and that near-term cash generation is being sacrificed to finance the growth option.

Q1 2026 illustrates the squeeze. Net income was $112.5M ($0.38/share) on realized gas of $4.27/Mcf (+19% YoY), but hedge settlements subtracted $0.82/Mcf, netting to $3.45. Capex again outspent operating cash flow. The hedge book has flipped from a 2024 tailwind (+$0.39/Mcf) to a 2026 headwind, and the collar ceilings ($4.35–$4.44) cap participation in exactly the gas-price spike the bull case depends on.

The balance sheet is the central risk. Total debt is ~$3.0B (6.75% notes due 2029 at $1.62B; 5.875% notes due 2030 at $965M; revolver ~$350M). Debt/equity is 1.53. The company is in compliance with its leverage covenant (<3.5x), but interest expense is a structural cash drain. Liquidity of ~$1.27B is available via the $1.5B revolver against a $2.0B borrowing base. No dividend is paid; the revolver restricts it.

A hidden cost: of $1.5B in federal NOLs, roughly $721M is expected to expire unused because of the 2018 Section 382 change-of-control limitation, reducing the realizable value of the tax shield.

## Growth Analysis

The structural demand case is legitimate. Gulf Coast LNG export build-out, Mexico exports, petrochemical demand, and now data-center power are converging on Comstock's basin. The NextEra hub is the cleanest expression of this: a federally-supported 5.2-GW gas-fired complex sited on CRK acreage with CRK positioned as feedstock supplier. If the ~1 Bcf/d expectation by 2031 is realized under a firm, priced contract, it would materially change the demand profile for the company's gas.

The mechanism that converts this into shareholder value is not yet in place. The hub is owned by Japan/U.S. interests and built and operated by NextEra; Comstock's gas-supply economics are an expectation, not a signed and priced agreement reflected in the financials. Investors should not credit data-center revenue until contracts exist. The 8-GW figure cited in some materials is an aspirational ceiling, not the concrete project.

The near-term growth signals run the other direction, and emphatically. Current-year consensus EPS has fallen from $0.92 (90 days ago) to $0.54, a 41.2% cut; the current-quarter estimate has fallen 84% over 90 days to $0.02. Next-year EPS is down 11.6% to $1.19. Comstock did beat in three of the last four quarters, but the most recent quarter (Q1 2026) missed by 42%, and the revision trend is uniformly negative across every horizon. This is the opposite of the earnings-inflection profile (rising estimates plus accelerating revenue) that would warrant a contrarian bid. Volumes are falling, the per-share earnings base is being marked down, and the growth that exists is exploratory optionality that consumes capital before it produces cash.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing metrics CRK screens cheap: forward and trailing P/E both ~6.3x, against peer-median P/E of 15.8x for the E&P group (CNX at 4.4x, MGY at 15.8x, several peers with negative earnings). On the surface this looks like deep value.

The screen is misleading for two reasons. First, the cheapness is the wrong kind. A low multiple on falling estimates is a value-trap signature, not a catalyst. The current-year and next-year EPS figures are themselves being revised down, so the "6.3x" is anchored to a number that has fallen 41% in 90 days. Second, the earnings here are commodity-cyclical and the balance sheet amplifies the risk. A distress-zone Z-score producer outspending cash flow deserves a discount to peers, not a re-rating toward the peer median.

The forward setup is genuinely two-sided. The bull arithmetic is straightforward: if Henry Hub strengthens structurally and the NextEra demand materializes, the equity, as a leveraged call on gas, can move sharply higher (the move from $13 to $21 in early 2026 on the hub announcement is the template). But the same leverage works in reverse, and the hedge ceilings cap participation in the spike that would drive the bull case. A Seeking Alpha note (6/16/2026) pegs fair value at $13–16 with a buy point near $10, consistent with our read that the stock trades roughly at fair value with rising costs and gas-price headwinds offsetting the Haynesville optionality.

We do not award a high valuation score. The compressed multiple reflects real structural risk (leverage, negative FCF, declining volumes, hedge drag) rather than a mispricing the market is missing. We score valuation modestly below neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Comstock's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-favorable in trajectory but undersized relative to the basin's leaders. It competes against larger, better-capitalized operators (Expand Energy/Chesapeake-Southwestern, Aethon). Its differentiators are real: lowest-delivered-cost basin proximity to Gulf Coast demand, long laterals (41 wells ≥15,000 ft), horseshoe-well innovation, and a company-owned midstream (Pinnacle) that de-bottlenecks Western Haynesville takeaway.

The Western Haynesville delineation could widen the moat if the deeper play proves out economically; it is also the source of greatest execution risk, as these are among the deepest, hottest, highest-pressure wells in the industry and remain less de-risked than legacy acreage. The future development cost to convert PUDs jumped to $4.7B (from $0.9B) as higher 2025 prices added 332 PUD locations, a capital commitment stretching to 2030 that the current cash flow base cannot self-fund. The company's strategic positioning into the AI/data-center power demand theme is a credible structural advantage, but it is a sector-wide narrative not yet captured in priced contracts specific to CRK.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the stacking of commodity leverage on financial leverage. As a ~100% gas producer with a distress-zone Z-score, CRK's equity is a high-beta call option on Henry Hub. A repeat of the 2024 price collapse drove a net loss and PUD write-downs; the same dynamic, on today's debt load, would be more punishing. PUD reserves swung +3.1 Tcf in 2025 purely on price after falling 1.2 Tcf in 2024, demonstrating how sensitive the inventory story is to gas prices.

Second is the negative-FCF / outspend dynamic. Continued outspend depends on higher gas prices or further asset monetizations; absent either, leverage rises or drilling is curtailed, which accelerates the production decline already underway.

Third is execution risk in the Western Haynesville and the unproven, uncontracted status of the data-center supply. The NextEra hub revenue is an expectation. Fourth is the hedge book working against the company, with collar ceilings capping the upside that the bull thesis requires.

Governance is a mixed picture. Insider alignment is strong (Jones affiliates bought $100M of stock in 2024), but the recent signal is mildly cautious: no open-market C-suite buying recently, one discretionary open-market sale (VP Operations McGough, 48,915 shares at $21.21 on 3/5/2026, near the peak), and CEO 2025 total comp of $21.9M (with "comp actually paid" of $34.5M), rich for a $3.8B-cap company and roughly doubled from $12.5M in 2024 as the stock ran.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 77.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 59.3% (a 17.8-point premium, 88th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (77.1% near-term vs. 59.3% long-term), which signals near-term event risk. ATM IV jumped 18.5 points (58.6% → 77.1%) over the prior two weeks as the price fell 4.2%, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 1.14 to 0.37. Put/call volume ratio of 1.41 with a roughly balanced skew (0.5%) suggests two-sided positioning rather than a directional collapse bet. The combination of rich near-term vol and backwardation argues against owning the stock here and also against shorting it cheaply; the market is charging a high premium for downside protection.

The 29.6% short interest (23.2M shares, 7.8 days to cover) is a flashing caution for any short. It signals either a crowded bearish consensus or genuine structural deterioration, but at that level squeeze risk on any positive catalyst (gas-price spike, a signed NextEra contract, another midstream monetization) is material.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Henry Hub strengthens structurally on LNG export growth and data-center power demand, lifting realized prices well above the $3.50 hedge floors. The NextEra hub converts to a firm, priced gas-supply agreement approaching 1 Bcf/d, giving Comstock a durable demand outlet at premium economics. Western Haynesville delineation de-risks the deeper play, validating the "decades of inventory" claim. The Sixth Street recapitalization template repeats, monetizing midstream at rising valuations to deleverage without diluting equity. In this scenario the leveraged equity re-rates sharply, plausibly back to the high $20s. The early-2026 move from $13 to $21 on the initial hub news shows the slope of that recovery. Bull case target: ~$25 (consensus high), roughly +87% from current.

### Bear Case
Gas prices soften, the hedge floors provide partial protection but the company keeps outspending cash flow, leverage rises, and drilling is curtailed, accelerating the volume decline. PUD reserves are written down on lower prices (a repeat of 2024). The NextEra contract is delayed or signed at less attractive economics, and estimate revisions continue lower. A distress-zone Z-score on a ~$3.0B debt load forces further asset sales at the worst time. The stock retests the 52-week low ($12.44) and below, toward $9–10. Bear case target: ~$10, roughly -25% from current.

The asymmetry is two-sided, which is why this is an AVOID rather than a short. The estimate-revision trend, the negative FCF, the declining production, and the hedge drag tilt the near-term probability toward the bear path, but the leverage that creates the downside also creates the violent upside on any gas-price spike or contract signing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria:**
- **Estimate revisions stabilize and turn positive.** Specifically, two consecutive quarters of flat-to-rising current-year EPS consensus. Falling estimates are the single clearest near-term signal to stay away.
- **Free cash flow turns positive.** A quarter in which operating cash flow covers capex, demonstrating the outspend is self-correcting rather than structural.
- **A signed, priced NextEra/data-center gas-supply contract** that converts the demand optionality into reflected financials, materially changing the volume and cash-flow outlook.
- **Deleveraging that moves the Altman Z-Score out of the distress zone**, whether through further midstream monetization (the Sixth Street template) or debt reduction.
- **Valuation reset.** A pullback toward the $9–10 area on intact fundamentals would tilt the risk/reward, given the leveraged upside to any gas-price recovery.

**Timeline:** The headwinds (negative FCF, declining volumes, falling estimates, hedge drag) are likely to persist through at least the next two quarters. The natural reassessment points are the Q2 2026 print and any 8-K confirming a firm NextEra supply agreement.

## Conclusion

Comstock owns a high-quality asset base in the right basin at the right time for structural gas demand, and management has shown real skill in financing the midstream without diluting equity. Those positives are offset by a balance sheet in the distress zone, production that is shrinking 15% per year, free cash flow that is negative, a hedge book now working against the company, and an estimate-revision trend that signals the market is marking down forward earnings power in real time. The data-center demand thesis is real but uncontracted; crediting it today is paying for an option that has not yet been written.

The trailing P/E looks cheap against the peer median, but the cheapness reflects genuine structural risk on falling earnings rather than a mispricing the market is overlooking. We maintain AVOID, the fifth consecutive such call, with an overall score of 4.3. This is a name to keep off the buy list until estimate revisions stabilize, free cash flow turns positive, or a firm NextEra contract reprices the demand outlook. It is not a short candidate: the elevated short interest, two-sided options positioning, and the upside leverage to any gas-price spike make the risk/reward on a short position unfavorable.
