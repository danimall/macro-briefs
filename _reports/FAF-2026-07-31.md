**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First American Financial reported its strongest quarter in the current cycle on July 23: Q2 2026 revenue of $2,117.3M (+15.0% y/y), pretax income of $283.9M (+45.4%), GAAP diluted EPS of $2.12 versus $1.41, and company-defined adjusted EPS of $2.08 (+36%). The market repriced the stock immediately — from a $68.96 close on July 23 to $79.16 on July 28, a 15% move in three sessions — before giving back to roughly $75. That price action is the central fact for this report. The fundamental improvement is real; the discount that supported four consecutive BUY recommendations at prices between $67.53 and $69.96 has substantially closed.

Two things in the quarter deserve more weight than the headline. First, the growth is narrowly sourced. Commercial title revenue rose 34% to $314M on a record average revenue per order of $19,980 (+31%), but CEO Mark Seaton volunteered on the call that "when you look at everything except for data centers, our commercial business is up 11%," with data-center revenue up roughly 140-147%. Data centers therefore account for approximately two-thirds of commercial growth, and each project generates up to three separate title events (land, construction loan, takeout refinance). FAF's earnings trajectory is now materially levered to AI data-center construction. Second, residential is not participating: domestic closed orders were 137,300, down 0.7%, with purchase orders per day down 3.4% in the quarter and 4.6% over six months. Purchase revenue rose only 2%, and the +6% purchase ARPO was attributed by the CFO to geographic mix (California), not price. With the 30-year fixed near 6.99% in late July and open purchase orders flat y/y through the first three weeks of July, the largest structural piece of the business remains in a trough.

We downgrade to HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 5.95, continuing a declining trajectory (6.8 → 6.9 → 6.7 → 6.4). The downgrade rests on two specific developments rather than on price action alone: (1) upside to our $84 base case has compressed from roughly 21% at the June 23 report price of $69.44 to roughly 12% today, and (2) the Q2 disclosure quantifies a concentration in the growth engine that was previously unmeasured. Offsetting these, consensus estimates are rising (current year +4.3% in 30 days to $7.15), the company has beaten four consecutive quarters by 12.9% to 37.4%, and the analyst target range of $83-99 sits entirely above spot. This is a well-run oligopolist at a fair price with genuine optionality, not a compelling entry.

## Company Overview

FAF is the second-largest U.S. title insurance underwriter, operating in 49 states plus DC and international markets (Canada, UK, Europe, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand). It reports three segments:

**Title Insurance and Services (~95% of revenue)** bundles several distinct businesses: title underwriting through direct and independent-agency channels; closing/escrow; proprietary title plant data and indexes carried at $690.2M; 1031 tax-deferred exchange services; appraisal, lien release, document custodial and default services; FirstFunding (mortgage warehouse lending); ServiceMac (residential mortgage subservicing); and First American Trust, FSB, a federal savings bank.

**Home Warranty** sells residential service contracts in 36 states plus DC. Q2 pretax margin was 21.3% on revenue up 1% — run for loss ratio (40% vs. 41%) rather than growth.

**Corporate** holds a venture capital portfolio, financing facilities and overhead.

The dual regulatory identity — insurance holding company and savings and loan holding company — is the engine of the current earnings story. FAF is using its banking charter to capture escrow, 1031 and subservicing float that competitors must park at unaffiliated banks. Average deposits at First American Trust reached $7.9B in Q2, up 30% y/y, with the balance-sheet deposit liability rising from $5,292.7M at 12/31/2025 to $7,877.9M at 6/30/2026. Management disclosed that 36% of deposits now originate outside captive title operations.

## Financial Analysis

### The trailing-earnings picture requires normalization

FY2024 pretax income of $165.4M embedded a $401.6M net investment loss tied to a portfolio "rebalancing project" plus impairments; FY2025's $826.2M pretax included a $20.9M net investment gain. Reported net income attributable went from $131.1M to $621.8M, a 374% increase. Stripping net investment gains and losses gives $567.0M for 2024 versus $805.3M for 2025 — roughly 42% core pretax growth. Both figures are accurate descriptions of different things; a screen anchored on net-income growth overstates the operating trend by roughly a factor of ten. We have not resolved whether the 2024 losses were elective repositioning or forced impairment; no disclosure we reviewed settles that, and we draw no inference about motive from the accounting treatment.

### Multiple bases in circulation — reconciled

The company profile reports a P/E of 10.47 at $74.99, which implies EPS of ~$7.16. That is not FY2025 GAAP EPS of ~$6.00. Reconciling: FY2025 diluted EPS of ~$6.01, less H1 2025 EPS of $2.12, plus H1 2026 EPS of $3.33, gives trailing-twelve-month diluted EPS of approximately $7.22 and a TTM P/E of ~10.4x. Against consensus, $75 is ~10.5x current-year $7.15 and ~10.1x next-year $7.40. Against FY2025 GAAP alone, it is ~12.5x. We use the consensus-forward and TTM figures throughout.

### Quality of the Q2 beat

Revenue lines: direct premiums and escrow fees $794.1M (+12.8%); agent premiums $819.7M (+14.4%); information and other $307.8M (+14.0%); net investment income $183.7M (+14.7%); net investment gains $12.0M versus −$9.7M.

Three items flatter the pretax-to-EPS conversion. The effective tax rate was 22.8% versus 24.6%, against a management-cited 24% normalized rate — roughly 1.2 points of the conversion was tax-driven. The title loss provision held at 3.0% of title premiums and escrow fees, but its composition was a 3.75% ultimate loss rate for the 2026 policy year *less* $11.3M of favorable prior-year reserve releases ($21.2M for six months, versus $9.9M/$18.3M in 2025). And reported margin expansion — title segment pretax margin 15.7% GAAP / 14.0% adjusted versus 12.6% — was carried by high-margin commercial mix, not unit economics: the company's "success ratio" (incremental personnel plus operating expense divided by incremental net operating revenue) came in at 66% against a 60% target, with personnel up 9% and other operating expense up 15%. The CFO attributed the overrun to ServiceMac investment and guided the ratio to stay "a little bit elevated." Incremental margins were therefore worse than target in the same quarter reported margins expanded.

### The bank: real, differentiated, and smaller than the headline

Gross title-segment investment income rose 11% despite three Fed cuts, an impressive result. But interest expense rose 33% to $30.4M, because paying depositors is the cost of the strategy. The CFO reframed it explicitly: "investment income, net of interest expense, grew 8% year over year. And I think that 8% is a good proxy for the growth that you'll see in the back half." Anyone modeling the gross line overstates the economics by roughly three points of growth.

The funding sources are genuinely new. ServiceMac contributed $1.7B of deposits (+76%) with its subservicing loan portfolio up 54%; 1031 exchange deposits averaged $827M from effectively zero (balance-sheet: $93.6M at 12/31/2025 to $833.5M at 6/30/2026, about one-third of $2.8B total 1031 balances); agent depositors reached 310, up 37%, against a cited addressable base of ~20,000 settlement agents. Off-balance-sheet fiduciary funds grew: escrow deposits $12.2B (from $9.3B), of which $4.9B at FA Trust; trust assets administered $6.0B; subservicing client cash $2.2B (from $1.6B), $1.5B at FA Trust.

The mix shift away from captive escrow does not establish reduced counterparty concentration. ServiceMac alone is $1.7B of the non-captive 36%, and FAF discloses no client-level concentration for that subservicing book. Risk factor #27 states that these custodial balances can leave on MSR transfers or subservicing terminations, with that likelihood *increasing as rates rise* — a live consideration given the long end at multi-year highs.

### Balance sheet and cash flow

Total assets grew to $18,941.0M from $16,228.8M, almost entirely the deposit-funded securities and cash build. Cash rose to $2,624.8M from $1,387.3M; debt securities to $9,774.7M fair value against $10,062.0M amortized cost.

Gross unrealized losses on debt securities were $326.2M at 6/30/2026, up from $269.4M at year-end, against $38.9M of gross gains, with $247.7M in positions underwater twelve months or longer on $1,922.5M of fair value. AOCI deteriorated from −$256.7M to −$348.0M. Credit quality supports the non-credit characterization: 92.3% rated A− or higher, 5.9% BBB, 1.8% ($172.1M) non-investment grade, 63% governmental agency MBS. This is duration, not default. The rising rate environment since issuance continues to weigh on the position.

Six-month operating cash flow was $362.2M (from $309.0M) with capex of $77.5M, down 18%; management cited $285M of six-month free cash flow, +32%. We do not annualize this. Management flagged Q1 as seasonally weakest, and warehouse-lending gross flows ($29.6B advances / $29.4B collections) plus deposit swings make quarterly cash figures volatile. As a labeled sensitivity only, 2× the half-year figure implies ~$570M (~7.4% of market cap); treat it as illustration, not forecast.

Equity of $5,620.8M on 102.0M shares gives BVPS of $55.11 and P/B of 1.36x at $75. Debt-to-capital is 31.4%, or 21.5% excluding the $1,036.5M of warehouse secured financings (company-defined), against a stated 20% target. Liquidity is comfortable: $239.4M holding-company cash, a fully undrawn $900M revolver maturing 5/17/2028, and $427.8M of statutory dividend capacity available without regulatory approval for the remainder of 2026. An Altman Z-Score is not a meaningful solvency metric here — there is no classified working capital and the asset base is an investment portfolio funded by deposits and escrow float.

**Financial health: 6.5.** Strong liquidity, disciplined leverage, well-covered dividend (~30% payout on consensus), high-quality bond portfolio by credit. Offset by growing unrealized losses, an AOCI drag, fiduciary-funded assets subject to counterparty withdrawal, and a venture book that produced a $41.7M internally-valued impairment in the quarter.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus estimates are rising across every horizon except next quarter: current quarter $1.89 (+2.5% in 30 days), current year $7.15 (+4.3% in 30 days, +4.0% in 90 days), next year $7.40 (+2.6%/+1.8%). Next quarter has been trimmed 0.7% in 30 days and 1.4% in 90 days — the only negative datapoint, and consistent with management's commentary that the Q1-end refinance surge (rates briefly at their lowest since 2022) has moderated as rates rose again. The company has beaten four consecutive quarters by 12.9%, 25.6%, 37.4% and 15.9%, which justifies above-average confidence in the current $7.15/$7.40 anchors.

The revision magnitudes do not reach the threshold that flags a genuine inflection (+30% in 90 days). This is steady upward drift on an oligopolist, not an estimate re-rating.

Composition matters more than magnitude here. Three growth vectors:

**Commercial title (the engine, concentrated).** +34% revenue, record $19,980 ARPO, 14 transactions with >$1M premium in National Commercial Services versus 11 a year ago. Management reports breadth — industrial 23% of premium mix, multifamily 16%, development sites 14%, retail 14% — but data centers drove roughly two-thirds of the growth and the *ARPO record* is data-center dependent. A construction slowdown would compress revenue per order and order volume simultaneously. Commercial open orders were +9% in the first three weeks of July with three $1M+ premium deals already closed, and Seaton described the pipeline as never stronger; his claim that "we're still in the early innings of the next commercial real estate cycle" is a hypothesis, not evidence. Office remains weak.

**The bank (structural, 8% net).** Long runway (310 of ~20,000 agents; two-thirds of 1031 balances still at third-party banks) but growing at 8% net of interest expense, and reversible on ServiceMac client attrition.

**AI automation (real, pre-scale).** Endpoint, the AI-native title/settlement platform, converted its first office (Spokane) with automation moving 30% → 34% → 39% in July; statewide Washington by year-end 2026, national through 2027. Sequoia decisioning expanded California refinance coverage from 8 to 41 counties with automation at 40%, and purchase instant-decisioning now covers ~16% of transactions at order opening. Exam Assist QC processed >50,000 orders with 92% requiring no human review. The target is ~70% of purchase and ~80% of refinance decisions automated in title-plant markets. Management says company-wide technology spend has been "relatively flat since 2022" and does not anticipate needing materially more. The productivity benefit is credible but not yet in the numbers — the success ratio miss argues it has not arrived.

**Residential (the drag).** MBA's June 2026 forecast projected Q2 originations +10.1% y/y with purchase originations −1.9%, all growth from refinance (+39.9%). Refinance is 5% of FAF's direct revenue. With the 30-year fixed near 6.99% in late July, the affordability constraint remains binding. Management is explicit: "we remain more cautious than the broader consensus on the residential purchase market."

**Growth prospects: 6.0.** Mid-teens revenue growth with rising estimates and a credible automation program, discounted for concentration in one construction cycle, an 8% (not 30%) bank spread, a declining core residential business, and earnings quality that includes reserve releases and a favorable tax rate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $75, FAF trades at ~10.5x current-year consensus $7.15, ~10.1x next-year $7.40, ~10.4x TTM diluted EPS of ~$7.22, 1.36x book value of $55.11, and yields 2.93% on the $2.20 annualized dividend (~30% payout).

Peer medians in the P&C group are 9.9x P/E and 1.9% yield (THG 9.9x, KNSL 13.7x, MCY 6.5x, ORI 9.4x, SIGI 12.6x). FAF is roughly at the peer median multiple with a 100bp yield premium. That comparison is imperfect — these are underwriting-cycle P&C names whose earnings are levered to a hard market that is rolling over, while FAF's cycle is housing and commercial construction. But it does mean the stock is not screening cheap against its sector.

The cycle-adjusted picture is genuinely two-sided, which is why we score valuation near the middle rather than high. Commercial title is at or near a cyclical peak on ARPO (record $19,980, data-center driven) while residential purchase volumes sit at multi-year lows (purchase orders per day −4.6% over six months). Normalized earnings power depends on which reverts first. If residential purchase volumes recover 15-20% from trough over two to three years at incremental margins, title earnings power plausibly reaches $9-10 per share; at 11x that is $100-110. If data-center construction decelerates while residential stays flat, EPS compresses toward $6.00-6.50 and the multiple compresses with it.

Two pieces of evidence bear directly on whether $75 is attractive. First, analyst targets — low $83, median $85.50, mean $87.67, high $99 — sit entirely above spot, with a buy-side ratings shift from 3 to 4 "buy" ratings in the last month and no holds or sells among six analysts. On a stock at 10x forward with rising estimates, consensus targets are more likely to be stale floors than ceilings. Second, and pulling the other way, management repurchased 330,405 shares in Q2 at an average $61.99, has $246.0M of a $300M authorization unused, and told the call: "at the moment here we're not in the market." Seaton added that leverage "doesn't weigh in on the buyback decision at all," so the pause is valuation-driven. Management, with better information than we have, is not buying at these levels after buying 20% lower. H1 repurchases were $54.0M versus $88.7M a year prior; the dividend was raised only 1.9%, to $0.55 quarterly.

Our base case is $84: 11.3x next-year consensus $7.40, cross-checked against 1.45x forward book value of roughly $58. That is +12% from $75, or ~15% including the dividend.

**Valuation: 5.8.** Fair rather than mispriced. A modest premium to peer median P/E is justified by the bank optionality and commercial franchise; the compressed gap to base case, the flattering components of trailing earnings, and management's own decision to stop buying keep this from scoring higher. We are deliberately skeptical of treating a 10x multiple as sufficient reason to own a housing-levered financial.

## Competitive Landscape

Title insurance is a concentrated oligopoly — FAF alongside Fidelity National Financial, Old Republic and Stewart — with barriers rooted in state-by-state title plant data, agent networks and licensing. FAF's $690.2M of capitalized title plants and indexes is the balance-sheet expression of that moat, and management's argument is that AI increases its value because Sequoia's decisioning accuracy depends on proprietary historical records new entrants cannot assemble. That argument is coherent and, in commercial, the deal evidence supports improvement: 14 deals above $1M in premium versus 11.

The competitive threat and the competitive opportunity are the same technology. Fannie Mae's title waiver pilot uses automated title review to *replace* a policy on low-LTV refinances; Sequoia uses automated title review to *produce* policies more cheaply. On June 23, FHFA Director Bill Pulte posted that "Fannie Mae is working actively to expand its title pilot program, especially on title insurance for home refinancings," with Freddie Mac "hyper focused on reducing costs up and down the home closing chain." The pilot currently covers certain refinance loans below 80% LTV in specific geographies, with Doma and Westcor as title vendors and UWM and Better among participating lenders. KBW's read is that "the title pilot remains very small, and it is only for refinances, which account for under 10% of revenues," corroborated by FAF's own disclosure that refinance is 5% of direct revenue. Seaton: "Haven't heard anything incremental... So we're kind of in wait-and-see mode." A reported April 2026 agreement for Opendoor to acquire Doma's closing and escrow operations is single-source and unconfirmed in any FAF filing.

The tail risk is scope creep into purchase transactions, which would be a different order of magnitude. Today it is not priced because it is not happening.

**Competitive position: 6.5**, unchanged from the prior report. Improving in commercial, stable in residential, with a data moat that AI plausibly deepens but a demonstrated case that a large enough counterparty can decide the product is optional in low-risk segments.

## Risk Assessment

**AI data-center concentration.** Roughly two-thirds of commercial growth and the record ARPO both depend on data-center construction. This ties FAF's earnings to the durability of hyperscaler capex — the least-resolved question in the current market. A construction pause would remove FAF's only growth engine while residential remains in a trough, and would hit volume and price per order together.

**Rate path, two-sided.** Higher short rates support float income on $7.9B of average deposits and $12.2B of escrow. Higher long rates deepen the $326.2M of unrealized bond losses, worsen AOCI, and — per risk factor #27 — raise the likelihood that ServiceMac custodial balances leave on MSR transfers. Higher mortgage rates suppress the residential business that is 95% of the segment's order volume. On net, a higher-for-longer path is unfavorable.

**Undisclosed regulatory inquiries and class actions.** Risk factor #18 states plainly that "the Company is the subject of regulatory inquiries" and is "a party to class action lawsuits," and Note 16 adds that governmental agencies are examining certain operations, with exams potentially resulting in business-practice changes that "could ultimately have a material adverse impact." The agencies, subject matter and exposure are not disclosed. This is an unquantifiable overhang.

**Bank model concentrates risk a third-party arrangement would not.** Risk factor #5 is candid: funds deposited at FAF's own bank are invested in fixed income and losses "would generally not occur if [funds] were deposited at an unaffiliated entity." The deposit growth driving the earnings beat is funded by fiduciary money FAF does not own, invested in a portfolio carrying $326.2M of unrealized losses, and withdrawable at counterparty discretion.

**Reserve pattern worth monitoring.** Total claims reserve fell from $1,169.6M to $1,151.6M, but known claims rose from $54.6M to $75.0M while IBNR fell from $1,095.9M to $1,053.9M. Rising known claims alongside continued IBNR releases is the pattern that precedes strengthening if sustained. A $20M move against a $1.15B reserve is small and could reflect normal reporting timing; the six-year emergence tail (only 65-75% of claims known within six years) means an automation-driven deterioration in underwriting quality would not surface for years.

**Venture portfolio.** A $41.7M impairment of a non-marketable equity investment on an internal valuation drove corporate-segment net investment losses of $36.2M. Carrying value fell from $253.1M to $214.4M. Cumulatively the book carries $404.3M of gross unrealized losses and impairments against $280.5M of gross gains. Bounded at ~3.8% of equity, but a recurring source of unpredictable charges.

**Governance.** The 5/14/2026 charter restatement begins board declassification that completes only at the 2029 annual meeting; until then directors may be removed only for cause. Six Item 5.02 filings between 4/15/2025 and 4/29/2026 indicate broader leadership turnover than a single seat. Mark Seaton's status as CEO is confirmed by the Q2 10-Q signature page and the March 2026 proxy, but the announcement date, effective date and rationale for Kenneth DeGiorgio's departure are unverified. Three Item 1.05 cybersecurity 8-Ks (December 2023 – January 2024) confirm a realized incident, referenced directly in risk factor #12.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 35.7% against 29.1% one-year realized, a 6.6-point premium, with the term structure in backwardation (35.7% at 20 days versus 26.6% at 167 days) and an implied move of ±7.0% into the August 21 expiry. Backwardation this close after an earnings release is unusual and suggests residual demand for near-dated protection or leverage rather than a resolved catalyst. Skew is call-heavy (−7.2% at one month, −4.0% at six months), and volume-based put/call ratios are effectively nil — thin, non-informative flow with 47 contracts across four expirations. The composite read is bullish positioning at expensive near-term volatility, which argues against paying up for short-dated upside exposure and is consistent with a HOLD. Short interest at 7.5% of float and 6.4 days to cover is uncrowded and creates no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

**Risk factors: 5.2**, down from 5.5. The reduction reflects the newly quantified data-center dependence, the rising-known-claims pattern, and the widening unrealized-loss position, against an undisclosed regulatory docket that cannot be sized.

### Management Quality

Insider activity is one-directional: across roughly 18 months of Form 4 data, no open-market purchases by any officer or director. Chief Accounting Officer Steven Adams sold 3,252.6 shares at $74.9937 on 7/24/2026 — at essentially the current price, one day after the print — disposing of roughly 24% of his pre-sale stake. Chief Legal Officer Lisa Cornehl sold 5,823.5 shares at $68.627 on 5/5/2026; director Margaret McCarthy sold 6,630 shares at $70.5894 on 4/24/2026 (~22% of her position). Dollar amounts are modest ($244K-$468K) and all retained meaningful holdings; Seaton still holds 232,081 shares (~$17.4M). This does not meet the bar for a materially bearish signal — it is not three C-suite insiders within 30 days, it does not exceed 50% of holdings, and fundamentals are improving. But the complete absence of buying alongside three discretionary sales into a rising price is not alignment either.

Capital allocation is disciplined and honest: the buyback pause at $75 after buying at $61.99, an explicit M&A stance ("the bar for acquisitions is higher today than it has been in many years... no interest in pursuing acquisitions simply for the sake of scale"), and flat technology spend since 2022. Against that, the dividend raise was 1.9%, leverage sits above the stated 20% target, and the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows five-year FAF TSR indexed at 142 versus a peer group at 211 — a 69-point shortfall that widened by 26 points during 2025, the strongest earnings year, while PEO compensation actually paid rose from $7.78M to $8.21M. The 2021 and 2022 columns ($17.7M and $2.86M actually paid) show the plan does flex, and the proxy peer group composition is not available to us, so comparability is unassessable. **Management quality: 5.3.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $99 (+32%)

Data-center title demand persists through 2027 as hyperscaler capex commitments convert to construction, with each project generating three title events; commercial ARPO holds near $20,000 and the pipeline management describes as "never stronger" delivers. Residential purchase volumes bottom and begin recovering as the Fed eventually eases, restoring the 95%-of-orders base at incremental margins on a cost structure already built. Endpoint and Sequoia scale past their current 39-40% automation toward the 70%/80% targets, converting the AI narrative into the success-ratio improvement that has not yet appeared. The bank compounds toward $10B+ of deposits as agent count grows from 310 and the remaining two-thirds of 1031 balances migrate in-house. EPS reaches $8.50-9.00; at 11.5x, $98-104. Management resumes buybacks with $246M of authorization at prices management itself deems attractive.

### Bear Case — $57 (−24%)

AI capex decelerates in late 2026 or 2027, and data-center title work slows. Commercial revenue per order reverts toward pre-2026 levels while order counts fall, hitting price and volume together. Residential remains suppressed with the 30-year above 6.5% through 2027 as the long end stays elevated; purchase orders per day continue their 4-5% annual decline. The success ratio stays at 66%, so mix reversion drives margins back toward 12%. Reserve strengthening becomes necessary as known claims continue rising while releases stop, removing $20-40M annually of pretax support. Custodial deposits leave on MSR transfers as rates rise. EPS compresses to $6.20; at 9x, $56, with 1.0x book on roughly $57 BVPS providing similar support.

### What tips the balance to HOLD

The bull case requires two independent things to go right (data centers persist *and* residential recovers), while the bear case requires only one to go wrong. At $75 the market pays 10.1x next-year for that configuration, roughly at peer median. Reward-to-risk on the base case is about 0.5:1 (+12% versus −24%), improving to 1.3:1 against the bull case. Financial-sector selection has been a persistent source of negative relative return, which raises the bar for a fifth consecutive BUY on the same thesis. The most decisive single piece of evidence is that management, holding $246M of unused authorization and stating that leverage is irrelevant to the decision, chose to buy at $61.99 and not at $75.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** We see a positive 12-month growth thesis with identifiable catalysts, but insufficient asymmetry at $75 to add.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Price at or below $66-68** with fundamentals intact — restoring roughly 25% upside to the $84 base case. Equivalently, a resumption of company repurchases disclosed in the Q3 10-Q would signal management sees value at prevailing prices.
2. **Ex-data-center commercial growth accelerating above 15%** in the Q3 (late October) print, demonstrating the commercial cycle is broader than one construction theme. Commercial open orders sustaining above +9% would corroborate.
3. **Success ratio improving to 60% or below** with title segment adjusted pretax margin above 15%, which would convert the automation story from narrative to unit economics. Watch Endpoint automation crossing 50% and Sequoia purchase instant-decisioning above 25%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Commercial ARPO falling below $16,000** or data-center revenue growth turning negative, indicating the growth engine has stalled while residential remains in trough.
2. **Reserve strengthening** — the title loss provision rising above 3.5% of premiums and escrow fees, or prior-year releases ceasing with known claims continuing above $75M.
3. **Deposit contraction** — average FA Trust deposits declining sequentially, particularly a ServiceMac subservicing loss, or investment income net of interest expense growing below 3%.
4. **GSE pilot scope extending to purchase transactions**, which would move the waiver risk from 5% of direct revenue to the core franchise.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results, expected late October 2026. Interim monitoring points: the 30-year fixed mortgage rate direction (near 6.99% in late July per HousingWire), any September FOMC decision, and any FHFA announcement on the title pilot, whose expiry date management referenced ambiguously as "November 27th."

## Conclusion

First American is a well-capitalized oligopolist executing a genuinely differentiated strategy — monetizing a banking charter to capture float competitors must outsource, and applying proprietary title-plant data to automated decisioning — that produced 15% revenue growth and a 36% increase in adjusted EPS in Q2 2026. Estimates are rising, the company has beaten four consecutive quarters, analyst targets of $83-99 sit entirely above spot, and at 10.1x next-year earnings and 1.36x book the valuation is undemanding in absolute terms.

The case for waiting is equally concrete. The entire growth engine reduces to data-center title work (two-thirds of commercial growth) and a bank spread compounding at 8% net, while the residential purchase business that generates most order volume declined 4.6% over six months against a 30-year mortgage rate near 6.99% in late July. Reported margin expansion came from revenue mix, not efficiency — the success ratio missed its target at 66% versus 60%. Earnings benefit from $21.2M of six-month reserve releases and a 22.8% tax rate against a 24% normalized assumption.
