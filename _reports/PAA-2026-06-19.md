**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Plains All American is one of the largest crude-oil midstream operators in North America, structured as a Delaware master limited partnership that pairs large-scale crude merchant/aggregation activities with ownership of pipelines, gathering systems, terminals, and storage. Over the past year the partnership has executed a deliberate portfolio reshaping: it spent roughly $2.8B on debt-funded Permian and Eagle Ford crude acquisitions (EPIC/Cactus III, Ironwood, JV deals) while agreeing to sell its Canadian NGL business to Keyera for approximately US$3.9B (~$3.2B net), with proceeds mandated to deleveraging. The transaction closed May 12, 2026, completing the transition toward a crude-oil pure-play.

The investment case is primarily an income case. The unit yields about 7.7% on a $1.67 annualized distribution covered roughly 1.7x by implied distributable cash flow, backed by an investment-grade balance sheet (S&P, Moody's, Fitch) and over $2B of liquidity. Estimate revisions have been broadly positive — current-year consensus EPS rose 15.7% over 90 days and next-year rose 23.4% — and management raised FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA midpoint guidance to roughly $2.88B while hiking 2026 growth capex to $400–450M on an improving crude macro. Goldman Sachs moved from Sell to Neutral on June 4, raising its target to $24.

The case against paying up here is structural rather than acute. PAA itself acknowledges that many of its operating areas, particularly the Permian, are overbuilt, producing persistent downward pressure on long-haul tariffs as favorable legacy contracts reset to market. ExxonMobil represents 31% of revenues. The Keyera deal closed under an active Canadian Competition Bureau challenge, leaving a post-closing regulatory tail. At 19.2x trailing earnings against a peer median near 19.2x and with the unit having risen 16% over the trailing year to within ~12% of its 52-week high, the asymmetric upside is limited. We rate PAA a HOLD on income merits.

## Company Overview

PAA operates 20,405 miles of active crude pipelines and gathering systems plus 76MM bbl of commercial storage. Its largest footprint is in the Permian Basin (9,490 system miles; 7,333 MBbl/d tariff volume, +9% YoY), the dominant driver of US production growth. The partnership owns the marquee Cushing, OK terminal (27MM bbl, the NYMEX WTI delivery hub) alongside facilities at St. James LA, Midland, and Patoka IL, and operates an extensive JV network (>25 arrangements) including the Permian JV (65%), Cactus II (70%, with Enbridge), Red River (67%), and equity stakes in BridgeTex, Capline, Diamond, Saddlehorn, and Wink-to-Webster.

The model integrates crude supply aggregation (merchant activities) with fee-based transportation and storage infrastructure. The merchant book makes the top line misleading: sales and purchases move together with crude prices, so the ~10% revenue decline in 2025 reflects lower oil prices (WTI averaged ~$65 vs ~$76 in 2024) rather than business deterioration. Adjusted EBITDA is the relevant gauge.

The strategic pivot to a crude pure-play, funded by selling the Canadian NGL business and buying Permian/Eagle Ford crude assets, concentrates PAA's exposure to the most active US basin while shedding a non-core segment. Two reportable segments remain (Crude Oil plus a small residual NGL), with the divested Canadian operations classified as discontinued.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and EBITDA.** Net income attributable to PAA was $1.435B in 2025, up 86% from $772M in 2024, though much of the jump is optical, as 2024 carried a $345M Line 901 incident charge. Adjusted EBITDA was $3.374B (vs $3.326B), and Adjusted EBITDA attributable to PAA was $2.833B (+2%). The underlying growth was roughly flat to modestly up. The Crude Oil segment generated $2.344B of Adjusted EBITDA (+3%) on higher Permian tariff volumes, the Cactus III acquisition, and tariff escalations, partially offset by Permian long-haul contract rates resetting to market and fewer merchant opportunities.

**Margins.** Net margin of ~4% and operating margin of ~3% are structural artifacts of the low-margin merchant pass-through revenue, not signs of distress. The fee-based infrastructure earns far higher unit economics; the headline ratios are diluted by the commodity flow-through.

**Cash flow tension.** Operating cash flow was $2.936B (up from $2.490B), and reported FCF was $2.29B. Adjusted Free Cash Flow was –$875M in 2025 (vs +$1.25B in 2024), and AFCF after distributions was –$2.17B, driven by ~$2.8B of acquisition capital. This was debt-funded by design. The Keyera proceeds (~$3.2B net) are the funding counterweight: PAA bought Permian crude assets and sold Canadian NGL to pay for them and delever.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt face value is ~$11.3B (~$10.8B long-term, ~$0.6B short-term), ~85% publicly-traded senior notes. PAA issued $3.0B of senior notes in 2025 and assumed/refinanced ~$1.1B of EPIC term loan. The partnership is investment grade across all three agencies, with a 3.25–3.75x target leverage band and >$2B liquidity. The current ratio of 0.96 (working-capital deficit of $198M) is normal for a merchant midstream funding self-liquidating inventory with short-term debt. A reported Altman Z-Score of 1.62 ("distress zone") is a structural false signal driven by the MLP capital structure (near-zero equity-to-assets) and the inflated low-margin revenue base; this is not a bankruptcy-risk entity.

**Capital allocation and distribution.** The distribution is $0.4175/quarter ($1.67 annualized), a ~7.7% yield with ~1.7x implied DCF coverage. PAA has $190M remaining on its equity repurchase authorization (minimal use) and repurchased 18% of Series A preferreds ($333M) in January 2025; EnCap redeemed all 12.7M of its Series A preferreds in January 2025. The deleveraging path from Keyera proceeds is the primary near-term balance-sheet catalyst.

## Growth Analysis

PAA is a low-growth, high-payout vehicle whose earnings momentum is currently positive but modest in magnitude. Estimate revisions support a constructive read: current-quarter consensus rose +21.5% over 90 days, next-quarter +22.5%, current-year +15.7%, and next-year +23.4%. The 30-day trend remains positive (next-year +10.4%, next-quarter +8.2%), indicating revisions are still accelerating rather than rolling over.

These revisions reflect a genuine fundamental improvement: management cited NGL timing/outperformance, captured crude opportunities, and a changed geopolitical backdrop in its guidance raise, and on June 15, 2026 hiked 2026 growth capex while holding maintenance capex at ~$185M. The capex increase is a constructive demand signal on Permian volumes.

The strong revision velocity (>20% in some lines) would, on a stock at a depressed trough multiple, support a more aggressive call. Here the signal is tempered by two facts. First, the magnitude of underlying growth is modest: Adjusted EBITDA attributable to PAA grew ~2% in 2025, and management's 2026 guidance implies low-single-digit growth. Second, the recent earnings record is mixed: PAA missed by 5.1% in Q1 2026 and 3.3% in Q4 2025 after beating by 9.7% and 22.1% in the prior two quarters, so consensus visibility is imperfect. The growth thesis is real but bounded; PAA is a yield-plus-deleveraging story, not a compounder.

The structural growth ceiling is the Permian recontracting dynamic. Production growth is slowing while takeaway capacity has expanded, and PAA's own filings repeatedly flag long-haul contract rates resetting to market. Volume growth and tariff escalations are currently offsetting this drag, but the balance could tip if Permian output decelerates further.

## Valuation Assessment

PAA trades at roughly 19.2x trailing earnings (forward and trailing P/E are identical at 19.2x), against an Energy peer median of 19.2x. On that basis the unit is fairly valued, not cheap. Relative to the peer set, PAA sits below DTM (31.1x) and PR (22.4x) but well above APA (8.6x) and DINO (10.6x); the dispersion reflects the heterogeneity of "Energy" as a category, mixing midstream, E&P, and refining. The cleaner midstream comparable, DTM at 31.1x, makes PAA look reasonable, but DTM is a faster-growing gas-gathering name, and the multiple gap is justified by growth differential, not mispricing.

The valuation anchor is the yield. At ~7.7% with ~1.7x coverage and investment-grade backing, the distribution provides a hard floor on the unit and a credible total-return base. On forward EPS of $1.79 (current year) and $1.98 (next year), the unit trades at ~12x and ~10.8x respectively, modest multiples that reflect both the low-growth profile and the structural margin headwinds. Applying the rising next-year estimate to a yield-supported framework, fair value clusters in the low-to-mid $20s.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $20, mean $23.61, median $24.00, high $27.00. Goldman's $24 target is representative. With the unit at $21.34, the median target implies ~12% upside plus the 7.7% yield, a reasonable but not asymmetric total return. We do not treat these targets as floors here: PAA is a mature, fairly-valued income vehicle where analyst targets reflect the same yield-plus-modest-growth math the price already embeds, not a low-multiple name with accelerating growth that consensus is chasing.

We score valuation 5.5 — neutral. There is no specific mispricing to exploit; the cheapness on forward earnings multiples is offset by structural tariff erosion and a low organic growth rate. A compressed multiple here reflects the genuine recontracting headwind rather than an unrecognized opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

PAA's differentiation is its integrated wellhead-to-water footprint (gathering → intra-basin → long-haul → Corpus Christi/Houston export docks), which generates pull-through volumes and merchant optionality that pure-pipe competitors lack. It competes against Enbridge, Energy Transfer, ONEOK, and Enterprise Products, and both partners with and competes against several of these through its JV network.

The competitive trajectory is the concern. The Permian midstream environment has shifted from capacity shortage, which supported premium tariffs, to capacity glut, which compresses them. PAA describes its operating areas as overbuilt, producing "heightened competition for uncommitted barrels and contract renewals." Legacy high-rate long-haul contracts are resetting to market, a recurring structural drag. E&P consolidation in the Permian cuts both ways: it stabilizes activity across price cycles (positive for volumes) but concentrates negotiating leverage among fewer, larger shippers (negative for tariff pricing).

The position is stable-to-modestly-eroding rather than improving. PAA retains scale, interconnectivity, and a defensible export-linked franchise, but its pricing power on incremental and renewing contracts is structurally weaker than it was previously. We score competitive position 5.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Permian recontracting (most important).** Long-haul tariff rates resetting to market in an overbuilt basin is a persistent margin headwind. It is partly known and priced, but the magnitude scales with how quickly Permian production growth decelerates, a real downside vector that is not fully reflected.

**Customer concentration.** ExxonMobil represented 31% of revenues in both 2025 and 2024 (27% in 2023). Most is crude merchant activity across multiple locations, which mitigates the loss-of-counterparty risk, but the concentration is high and any renegotiation of terms would be material.

**Keyera antitrust overhang.** The Canadian NGL sale closed despite a Section 92 application filed by Canada's Competition Bureau at the Competition Tribunal on May 4. Closing under an active challenge is unusual and leaves a post-closing tail: a tribunal could impose remedies or an unwind. The deleveraging thesis depends on the proceeds sticking.

**Acquisition integration and leverage.** The debt-funded 2025 M&A spree (EPIC/Cactus III with up to $350M of contingent earnouts) introduces integration and return-realization risk and elevated near-term leverage that the Keyera proceeds are intended to bring back into the target band.

**FERC index-rate litigation.** Pending D.C. Circuit petitions (LEPA v. FERC) over the 2021–2026 liquids pipeline index could adversely affect tariff escalation methodology and cash flows.

**MLP structural risks.** GP control (unitholders cannot remove the GP; AAP holds ~31% of voting equivalents), unlimited dilution capability, preferred units senior to common, and K-1 tax complexity are permanent features of the structure.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and does not flag near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 21.0% is roughly fair against 19.1% historical volatility (2.0pp spread), sits at the 9th percentile within its own chain, and the term structure is flat. The put/call skew is –4.5% (call-heavy), the put/call volume ratio is 0.33, and ATM IV collapsed from 41.0% to 21.0% since early June as the post-earnings and Keyera-close uncertainty resolved. Short interest is low at 2.3% of float (5.0 days to cover). The options market is pricing this as a low-volatility income name, consistent with the fundamental read: no informed bearish positioning is evident, and equally no unusual bullish accumulation ahead of a catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($25–27)
Crude macro continues improving, supporting Permian volumes and validating the growth-capex hike. Estimate revisions prove conservative, and FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA exceeds guidance. The Keyera proceeds delever the balance sheet into the lower half of the target band, freeing capacity for distribution growth or buybacks. The yield plus modest re-rating toward the high analyst target ($27) and continued distribution delivers a low-to-mid-teens total return. Goldman's move from Sell to Neutral marks the bottom of sentiment.

### Bear Case ($17–18)
Permian production growth decelerates, accelerating long-haul tariff erosion as legacy contracts reset faster than volume growth and escalations can offset. The Competition Tribunal imposes remedies on the Keyera transaction, impairing the deleveraging thesis and the funding counterweight to the acquisition spree. A weaker crude macro pressures merchant margins. The unit de-rates toward 9x next-year EPS even with the yield intact, implying ~$17–18, a ~17–20% decline from current levels.

### Base Case ($23–24)
PAA delivers ~$2.88B FY2026 EBITDA, deleverages on Keyera proceeds, and maintains the distribution at ~1.7x coverage. The recontracting drag is offset but not overcome by volume and tariff escalations. The unit holds in the low-to-mid $20s, delivering most of its return through the distribution plus modest appreciation toward the median target of $24.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income hold. The total-return expectation is dominated by the distribution rather than capital appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Permian tariff/contract data stabilizing or re-accelerating, evidence the recontracting drag has peaked, alongside FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA tracking above the midpoint.
- Keyera proceeds confirmed deployed to deleveraging with the Competition Tribunal challenge dismissed or settled without material remedy, and leverage moving into the lower half of the 3.25–3.75x band.
- A pullback to the high-$18s that pushes the yield above ~8.5% on an unchanged, well-covered distribution.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Distribution coverage falling below ~1.3x, or any signal of a distribution cut.
- A Competition Tribunal ruling forcing an unwind or material remedy on the Keyera transaction.
- Two consecutive quarters of declining next-year EPS revisions combined with evidence of accelerating Permian tariff erosion (segment EBITDA declining YoY).
- ExxonMobil contract renegotiation on materially worse terms.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and any Competition Tribunal development on the Keyera challenge. The FY2026 EBITDA delivery against guidance is the key full-year checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Plains All American is a sound, investment-grade midstream MLP executing a coherent strategy: divest non-core Canadian NGL assets, concentrate on Permian crude, and use the net Keyera proceeds to delever after a debt-funded acquisition year. The distribution is well-supported, estimate revisions are positive and still accelerating, and management's guidance raises and capex hike signal genuine fundamental improvement.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the balance of structural and valuation factors. The unit is fairly valued at ~19x trailing and the peer median, having already risen 16% over the trailing year to within ~12% of its 52-week high. The Permian recontracting headwind is persistent and partly underappreciated, ExxonMobil concentration is high, and the Keyera deal carries a live antitrust tail. The return here is the yield plus modest appreciation toward the mid-$20s, not a re-rating. We rate PAA a HOLD on income merits, with a constructive bias to add on a pullback to the high-$18s where the yield approaches 8.5%.
