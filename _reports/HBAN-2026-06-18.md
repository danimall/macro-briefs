**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Huntington Bancshares is a $34.5B-market-cap regional bank holding company that has, over the past 18 months, transformed itself from a Midwest-anchored franchise into a roughly $285 billion-asset institution with a major presence in Texas. Two acquisitions drove the change: Veritex Holdings ($12.6B assets, closed October 2025) and the larger Cadence Bancorporation ($53.5B assets, ~$7.4B price, closed February 2026). The investment case is unusual for a regional bank in that it is largely binary: management's path from $1.39 of 2025 EPS to $1.90–$1.93 by 2027 (and ROTCE from 15.7% to 18–19%) is roughly 70% synergy-driven, with $435M of cumulative cost saves targeted by end-2026 and $500M of revenue synergies by end-2028. The pivotal de-risking event is the Cadence systems conversion scheduled for late June 2026.

The underlying franchise is sound. The bank generates roughly 1.0% ROA and 15.7% ROTCE, operating cash flow exceeds net income, and capital and credit signals are benign so far, corroborated by peer commentary (Wells Fargo's CFO echoed the same "no change in borrower behavior" read at the June 9 Morgan Stanley conference). Early integration metrics are encouraging: 990 of 1,000 Cadence revenue-generating associates retained, and a 10x increase in digital account openings in the Cadence footprint since April 2026. The stock trades at ~13.1x trailing/forward EPS, modestly below the regional-bank peer median of 13.7x, and carries a ~3.6% dividend yield.

The reasons to wait rather than buy are specific and dated. The synergy-dependent EPS bridge has not yet survived the largest systems conversion, which is weeks away. The most recent quarter (Q1 FY26, reported April 23) missed by 23.5% ($0.25 vs. $0.33 estimate), the largest miss in the bank's recent record, reflecting deal-close noise but also signaling that the bridge is not clean. A director who joined via the Cadence merger sold $3.88M of stock on June 12. Texas commercial real estate and commercial lending exposure is being built faster than Huntington has underwriting history in those markets. And crossing into Category III regulatory territory (~$250B+ assets) raises capital, liquidity, and stress-test burdens. We rate HBAN a HOLD with a constructive bias, and would upgrade to BUY on confirmation of a clean Cadence conversion plus a Q2/Q3 quarter that re-establishes the EPS bridge.

## Company Overview

Huntington is the parent of The Huntington National Bank, headquartered in Columbus, Ohio. Historically its franchise was concentrated in Ohio, Michigan, and the upper Midwest, expanded by the 2021 TCF acquisition into Minnesota and Colorado. The defining strategic move of the current cycle is a deliberate pivot toward Sun Belt growth markets, executed primarily through M&A:

- **Texas entry (early 2024):** Hired a commercial banking team in Dallas.
- **Veritex Holdings (closed Oct 2025):** Dallas bank; ~$70M cost-save target already achieved.
- **Cadence Bancorporation (closed Feb 2026):** Bank dual-headquartered in Houston and Tupelo, MS, with 100+ Texas branches.
- **Organic expansion:** Branch-building in the Carolinas; expanded commercial presence in Atlanta, Miami, and Nashville.

The combined entity now sits in the same regional tier as Fifth Third, Citizens, and Regions. The business mix spans consumer & regional banking, commercial banking, and a sizable specialty lending and payments/treasury management franchise. Competitive advantages include a sticky, low-cost deposit base, a "fair play" consumer brand reputation, diversified fee businesses, and a disciplined credit culture, now extended into the fastest-growing demographic and commercial markets in the U.S.

## Financial Analysis

Standard industrial distress metrics are not meaningful for a bank; the Altman Z-Score of 0.23 is a mechanical artifact of treating deposits as ordinary liabilities and should be disregarded. On bank-appropriate measures, Huntington is healthy: positive net income, ROA near 1.0%, ROE ~9.1% (depressed by acquisition goodwill), and ROTCE of 15.7%. Operating cash flow exceeds net income, consistent with high-quality earnings.

**The earnings bridge is the central financial story:**
- **2025 actual:** EPS $1.39, ROTCE 15.7%.
- **2027 targets:** EPS $1.90–$1.93 (>30% cumulative), ROTCE 18–19%.
- **Synergy roadmap (CFO Wasserman, June 2026):** Veritex $70M cost saves achieved; Cadence $365M operating-expense reductions with "clear line of sight"; cumulative $435M cost synergies by end-2026; $500M revenue synergies by end-2028 ($50–75M in 2026).

The recent earnings pattern is mixed and warrants attention. Over the last four reported quarters, the bank posted one beat, one in-line, and two misses, with the Q1 FY26 print missing by 23.5%. This coincides with the Cadence close, so a portion reflects deal-related expense and purchase-accounting noise. It nonetheless underscores that the EPS bridge from $1.39 to $1.90+ is not yet running cleanly through reported results.

**Capital and regulatory:** Back-to-back deals created substantial goodwill (suppressing reported ROA/ROE versus tangible measures) and pushed Huntington toward Category III status (~$250B+ assets), bringing enhanced capital, LCR, and stress-testing requirements. CET1 trends and AOCI marks on the securities portfolio should be watched closely through and after integration. The dividend yield of ~3.6% sits modestly above the peer median of 3.0% and is well covered at current payout.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is genuine but conditional. Organic drivers — Carolinas branch expansion, commercial presence in Atlanta, Miami, Nashville, and a Texas footprint in one of the country's most attractive growth markets — are real but secondary. The dominant lever is M&A synergy realization.

On consensus estimates, the bridge looks like this: current-year EPS of $1.46, next-year $1.89. The 90-day revision trend is essentially flat (current year +0.2%, next year −0.8%), and 30-day revisions are flat to marginally positive. This matters for the scoring framework: the strongest forward signal — estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days alongside accelerating revenue — is not present here. Estimates are stable rather than inflecting upward. The thesis therefore rests on management delivering against targets the Street has already largely embedded, not on a consensus catching up to a fundamental surprise.

Early integration data points support the operational case: 99% Cadence associate retention and a 10x rise in digital account openings. These reduce the probability of revenue attrition derailing the synergy plan, but they are leading indicators, not confirmation. The genuine de-risking happens when the late-June systems conversion completes without customer attrition or operational disruption, and when one or two subsequent quarters show the cost-synergy run-rate flowing into reported EPS.

Huntington sits slightly below the median multiple with a slightly above-median yield. On a peer-relative basis it is fairly valued, not cheap. The bull argument is forward-looking: if EPS reaches $1.89 next year (consensus) and the market applies the current ~13.1x, the implied value is roughly $25, meaningfully above today's $16.85. The analyst price target range of $18.00–$22.50 (median $20.00) embeds a portion of that synergy realization.

The case against rewarding the cheapness is twofold. First, the discount to peers is modest and largely explained by integration uncertainty that the market is correctly pricing; management is publicly "frustrated" that shares are down YTD, which signals that investors are withholding credit until execution is proven. A low multiple alone is not a buy signal; it requires a specific reason the market is wrong, and here the market's skepticism is rational pending the conversion. Second, the forward EPS that drives the re-rating math is the synergy-dependent number that has not yet survived its largest operational test. The valuation is reasonable but the catalyst that would unlock the discount has not yet fired. We score valuation moderately: there is upside if the bridge delivers, but no asymmetric mispricing today.

## Competitive Landscape

Huntington's competitive trajectory is improving. By scaling to ~$285B in assets and planting a major flag in Texas, it differentiates itself from Midwest-only peers and positions against Sun Belt incumbents (Comerica, Cullen/Frost, Prosperity) and the money-center banks expanding in the state. The deposit franchise is sticky and the fee businesses (payments, wealth, capital markets) provide diversification beyond spread income. Securing one of the first Nasdaq "Texas-involved companies" listings is a cosmetic but real marker of the strategic reorientation.

The trajectory matters more than the level. Huntington is a challenger gaining scale and geographic reach in attractive markets, which is a stronger competitive setup than a static incumbent. The offsetting consideration is that the new Texas franchise carries an unproven competitive position: relationships and underwriting in Dallas and Houston are being built quickly, and the bank's pricing power and credit discipline in those markets are untested through a downturn.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Integration execution (the dominant risk).** Two acquisitions in rapid succession, with the large Cadence systems conversion in late June 2026. The entire EPS/ROTCE bridge hinges on delivering the targeted cost and revenue synergies on schedule. A conversion misstep, customer attrition, or talent loss would directly impair the thesis. Early signals (99% retention, 10x digital growth) are encouraging but not dispositive.

2. **Texas credit, unproven through a cycle.** Rapid Sun Belt commercial and CRE growth adds exposure to markets where Huntington's underwriting history is short. Management reports "no signs of major deterioration," corroborated by peers, but this has not been tested through a full credit cycle.

3. **Regulatory escalation.** Crossing into Category III raises compliance, capital, and liquidity burdens, increasing costs and constraining capital-return flexibility.

4. **Rate / NIM and AOCI sensitivity.** As a spread-driven regional bank, earnings are sensitive to the rate path and deposit competition. AOCI marks on the securities portfolio remain a sector-wide vulnerability, the issue that pressured regionals in 2023.

5. **Goodwill impairment.** The Cadence and Veritex deals created significant goodwill that could be impaired if synergies disappoint.

6. **Q1 FY26 miss.** The miss is partly deal noise, but it raises the bar for the next prints to demonstrate the bridge is intact.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($22–$25)
Cadence conversion completes cleanly in late June with minimal customer attrition. The cost-synergy run-rate flows into reported EPS through H2 2026, and revenue synergies build toward the 2028 target. EPS reaches $1.89–$1.93, ROTCE climbs toward 18–19%, and the market re-rates the stock toward peer-plus multiples on a proven Sun Belt growth franchise. At 13x on $1.90 the stock is ~$25; at the analyst high of $22.50 the implied multiple is conservative. Benign credit and a benign rate environment amplify the upside.

### Bear Case ($14–$15)
The conversion produces operational disruption or customer attrition; synergies arrive slower or smaller than guided. Texas CRE credit deteriorates as the bank's short underwriting history is tested, forcing higher provisions. Goodwill from back-to-back deals comes under impairment scrutiny. Category III costs erode the synergy benefit. EPS stalls near $1.50–$1.60, the multiple compresses to a discount on integration concern, and the stock revisits the 52-week low of $14.89, roughly 12% below the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold), constructive bias.**

This is a growth_hold: there is a credible 12-month appreciation thesis tied to a specific, dated catalyst (the late-June Cadence conversion and subsequent synergy realization), but the catalyst has not yet fired and the most recent quarter missed materially. The score sits below a BUY threshold because the upside is conditional on an unproven integration event and the valuation offers no asymmetric mispricing today: it is fairly valued versus peers, and the forward EPS that drives the re-rating math is the synergy number that just produced the Q1 miss in the quarter immediately preceding it.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 FY26 earnings report (expected late July 2026), which will be the first quarter reflecting a completed Cadence conversion.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Cadence systems conversion completes by early Q3 2026 with no material customer attrition and management reaffirms or raises the cost-synergy target.
- A Q2/Q3 FY26 quarter that re-establishes the EPS bridge (in-line-or-better print returning to the ~$0.40+ quarterly run-rate) with 90-day estimate revisions turning positive.
- CET1 holds at or above target while capital return resumes, confirming the Category III transition is non-disruptive.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A conversion misstep or visible customer/deposit attrition in the Texas franchise.
- A second consecutive double-digit EPS miss, or a downward guidance revision on the 2027 EPS/ROTCE targets.
- Texas CRE credit deterioration (rising NPAs or a step-up in provisioning) or a goodwill impairment charge.

## Conclusion

Huntington is a fundamentally healthy regional bank executing a credible, ambitious M&A-driven transformation into Texas. The path to >30% cumulative EPS growth and 18–19% ROTCE is supported by an already-achieved Veritex synergy target and encouraging early Cadence retention metrics. But the case is binary on integration execution, the pivotal conversion is still weeks away, the most recent quarter missed, estimate revisions are flat rather than inflecting, and a recently-joined director sold $3.88M of stock in June. At ~13x earnings the stock is fairly valued against peers, not cheap, and the discount the bulls want to capture is the integration risk the market is pricing rationally.

We rate HBAN a HOLD with a constructive bias and a clear upgrade path. The risk/reward improves materially once the late-June conversion de-risks and a clean quarter re-establishes the EPS bridge, at which point the stock should re-rate toward the $20–$22.50 analyst range. Until then, the prudent posture is to hold for the yield and the optionality on a successful integration, while watching the conversion and the next print as the decisive evidence.
