**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TSMC occupies the most defensible position in the AI hardware supply chain. It holds roughly 73% of the global pure-play foundry market and controls the two chokepoints that gate the pace of AI accelerator production worldwide: leading-edge logic fabrication (N3/N2, where Taiwan holds >90% of sub-7nm capacity) and CoWoS advanced packaging, which is 100% captive to TSMC with no merchant alternative. Nearly every advanced AI chip built in 2026 (Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, and the hyperscaler ASIC teams at Amazon, Google, and Microsoft) passes through TSMC's Taiwan operations.

The July 16 Q2 2026 report was a beat-and-raise that signals acceleration rather than plateau. Revenue reached $40.2B (+36% YoY), net profit rose 77% to a record, gross margin hit 67.7% (+9.1pp YoY), and diluted ADR EPS of $4.31 beat consensus by ~11%. Wafer shipments grew only 16.6% while revenue grew 36%, meaning the margin and revenue lift came from node mix (3nm/2nm) and pricing power, not volume, a more durable profit driver than a volume-led cycle. Management raised FY26 USD revenue growth guidance from ~30% to "slightly above 40%" and raised FY26 capex to $60–64B, the largest in company history, framing the AI capex cycle as multi-year and demand-backed.

The stock trades at approximately 19–20x next-year consensus earnings on rising estimates, roughly half the forward multiple of fabless AI names that capture the same demand. The reported headline trailing P/E of ~155x in the data feed is a data artifact (it reflects a single-quarter EPS distortion in the surprise table, not a real trailing figure); on the correct earnings base TSMC is not a premium-multiple stock. The gap between TSMC's valuation and its structural position exists almost entirely because of Taiwan geographic concentration, which is a genuine and unpriceable tail rather than a fundamental deficiency. We rate TSM a BUY with disciplined position sizing. The recent pullback to ~$416 from a June high near $479 improves the entry without any fundamental deterioration.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry. It manufactures chips designed by fabless customers and does not sell its own branded silicon, which removes it from competing with its own client base and reinforces its neutral-supplier position. Its customer roster spans essentially every major chip designer, and its revenue mix has shifted decisively toward AI: high-performance computing (AI accelerators and data-center silicon) reached 66% of Q2 2026 revenue, up 20% sequentially, while smartphone fell to 22% from 26% in Q1. By node, 7nm-and-below accounted for 77% of wafer revenue, with 5nm at 33%, 3nm at 30%, and 2nm at 3% in its first full quarter of volume production.

The competitive moat rests on two structural chokepoints. First, leading-edge logic fabrication depends on ASML EUV lithography and concentrates >90% of global sub-7nm capacity in Taiwan. Second, CoWoS 2.5D advanced packaging, which integrates logic dies with HBM memory, is proprietary to TSMC with no scalable merchant substitute. The packaging chokepoint is the more durable of the two: Samsung and Intel at least attempt to compete at leading-edge logic, but there is no alternative CoWoS supplier at scale, which makes TSMC's monthly revenue the industry's most-watched leading indicator for AI capex.

## Financial Analysis

Q2 2026 (reported July 16 via 6-K) was a record quarter on every relevant line:

- **Revenue: $40.2B** (NT$1.27T), +36.0% YoY, edging past the top of April guidance.
- **Net profit: NT$706.56B (~$22B), +77.4% YoY**, the highest ever and the ninth consecutive quarter of double-digit profit growth.
- **Diluted ADR EPS: $4.31**, ~11% above consensus.
- **Gross margin: 67.7%**, at the top of guidance and +9.1pp YoY.
- **Wafer shipments: 4,336k 12-inch equivalents, +16.6%.**

The divergence between volume growth and revenue growth is the core financial signal. The value creation is coming from node mix and pricing, both more sustainable than a volume-driven upturn. TSMC has notified customers of 5–10% price increases on 7nm-and-below nodes (~74% of wafer revenue), largely absorbable by AI customers whose end-product economics dwarf wafer cost. First-half 2026 revenue reached ~$75B (+35.6% YoY), and June 2026 broke a four-year streak of seasonal June declines (+6.2% MoM), evidence that AI demand has decoupled from the consumer electronics calendar.

The earnings surprise record shows four consecutive beats. The single anomalous line in the data feed (a June 2026 "actual" of $138.87 against a $24.57 estimate) is a currency/units mismatch in the source table, not a real result; the correct ADR figure was $4.31. On a clean basis, the beat cadence has been 4–8% ex the artifact, which supports confidence in the rising forward estimates.

Guidance was raised across the board. Q3 2026 revenue is guided to $44.6–45.8B (~37% YoY at midpoint), implying continued sequential acceleration. FY26 capex was raised to $60–64B from $52–56B, and management stated capex over the next three years will be "even more significantly higher" than the prior three. Q3 gross margin is guided down to 65–67% on the 2nm ramp, and the CFO flagged a 2–4pp near-term gross-margin drag from overseas (US, Japan) fab expansion, partially offset by productivity and advanced-tech demand.

We could not retrieve the audited balance sheet and cash flow this session because TSMC files a 20-F rather than a 10-K, and the FY2025 20-F body was not readable through available tools. The business historically generates very strong operating cash flow, and the capital-allocation story is the enormous capex escalation, which management frames as a demand-backed long-cycle commitment rather than speculative capacity. The financial-health score reflects the strong margin structure, cash generation, and node-mix-driven earnings quality, tempered by the fact that the record capex ramp will compress free cash flow and by the unretrievable balance-sheet detail.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is unusually durable for a company of this scale. FY26 guidance moved from ~30% to >40% USD revenue growth in a single mid-year raise, an atypically large revision that signals management sees the AI capex cycle accelerating. Wei's framing (the shift from generative to agentic AI implies more compute per inference) supports the view that demand intensity is rising, not saturating.

Estimate revisions confirm the momentum: current-quarter consensus rose +8.8% over 30 days and +10.0% over 90 days; next-quarter +12.6%/+14.0%; current-year +5.9%/+8.1%; and next-year +8.3%/+10.3%. Rising estimates across every window, with the acceleration concentrated in the near quarters, is a strong forward indicator. The largest margin-mix leg is still ahead: 2nm is only 3% of wafer revenue today, so the highest-value node has barely begun to contribute. As it ramps through 2027–28, it should lift both revenue mix and gross margin, even as it near-term pressures Q3 margins.

The demand signal is broad-based rather than single-customer. CoWoS demand is running at ~1M wafers in 2026 versus ~370k in 2024 (nearly 3x), and even with TSMC expanding CoWoS capacity ~80%/year the supply gap only narrows from ~20% to ~10% by year-end 2026 (TrendForce). This scarcity underpins the pricing power. Backing the broad AI infrastructure buildout (spanning hyperscaler capex and the associated infrastructure) the growth durability at the foundry layer is higher than any single-company story would imply. TSMC's growth is a levered, low-multiple expression of the same demand that supports Nvidia's and the hyperscalers' spending.

The Taiwan and Arizona expansion roadmaps (up to 25 new Taiwan factories over five years; a 6-fab Arizona plan with two packaging facilities enabling end-to-end US AI chip production by ~2028) provide multi-year capacity runway. The A14 process is on schedule for 2027 pre-production and 2028 volume.

## Valuation Assessment

On the correct forward earnings base, TSMC is inexpensive relative to its growth and quality. Consensus current-year EPS is $16.70 and next-year $21.29, both rising. At $416, that is ~24.9x current-year and ~19.5x next-year earnings. For a business growing revenue >40% this year with expanding gross margins and a near-monopoly on the AI supply chain's binding constraint, ~19–20x next-year with a PEG well below 1.0 is a discount to fair value, not a premium.

The reported ~155x P/E in the profile block is a data artifact tied to the distorted single-quarter EPS in the source feed; it does not reflect the real earnings base and should be disregarded. Morningstar's $534 fair value (Wide moat, Medium uncertainty, 3-star) sits ~28% above the current price. The sell-side range is $354 low to $700 high, with mean $527 and median $530; the current price is below every central estimate, and on a stock with rising estimates and accelerating growth those targets typically lag reality and get revised upward as consensus catches up.

The cleanest valuation argument is the peer differential. Press comparisons place TSMC at roughly half the forward multiple of Broadcom and well below Nvidia, despite capturing the same AI buildout as the indispensable "utility layer" of the hardware stack. The discount is driven by the Taiwan geographic concentration tail, which is real but unrelated to earnings power. Investors are being paid a persistent discount to own the demand at a lower multiple than the fabless names it enables.

We assign a valuation score of 7.0. A below-peer multiple is justified here by an identifiable mispricing (structural position vs. multiple) rather than a value trap. We temper it modestly because a portion of the discount is a genuine, permanent geopolitical risk premium that will not close absent a de-risking of Taiwan, which is not in our control. Cheapness alone is not the thesis; the catalyst is the accelerating estimate-revision cycle plus the untapped 2nm margin leg.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's competitive position is dominant and, on balance, still widening, though the trajectory has moderated from its peak. It holds ~73% of pure-play foundry share and is the only firm able to both fabricate leading-edge logic and package it via CoWoS at scale. The packaging chokepoint is the strongest element of the moat because there is no merchant alternative, which gives TSMC pricing power that flows through the entire AI supply chain.

The moderation in the competitive trajectory is real but second-order. Overseas fab expansion under US and tariff pressure carries a 2–4pp gross-margin drag, and the geographic diversification, while a demand tailwind funded by CHIPS Act and allied subsidies, dilutes the cost advantage that a fully Taiwan-concentrated footprint historically delivered. There is also a hidden dependency one layer up: TSMC's entire expansion math hinges on ASML delivering ~30%/year EUV capacity growth in 2027–28. TSMC controls its own execution but not its critical tool supplier's.

Against these, the offsets are strong. Node leadership is intact (2nm in volume, A14 on schedule); CoWoS scarcity persists through 2026; and pricing power is being exercised. The customer relationship is symbiotic: Nvidia and the hyperscalers cannot build accelerators anywhere else at the required volume. We score competitive position 8.7, consistent with the prior trajectory's gradual normalization from the near-perfect 9.5 as overseas margin drag and supply-chain dependency get properly weighted.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic concentration / geopolitical (the defining risk).** More than 90% of leading-edge fabrication and essentially all CoWoS packaging sit in Taiwan. The exposure runs beyond invasion to blockade or coercion scenarios that could disrupt energy and materials. Typhoon Bavi in July 2026, which shut Taiwan's markets and delayed a routine 6-K filing, was a small, live illustration of single-point-of-failure risk. This is the primary reason for the valuation discount and it is not fully hedgeable. The Arizona and Japan builds partially mitigate it over a multi-year horizon but do not resolve it near-term. This risk is low-probability, extreme-impact, and it is the reason we size the position with discipline rather than at maximum conviction.

**Customer concentration.** Nvidia alone holds ~60% of 2026 advanced-packaging capacity, and Nvidia + Broadcom + AMD together are estimated at 85%+ of CoWoS allocation. With HPC at 66% of revenue, a hyperscaler capex pause would hit TSMC directly. The counterweight is that the current demand signal is broad and accelerating, and TSMC's June and Q2 data confirm no foundry-level slowdown.

**Margin dilution from overseas expansion and tariffs.** US-made-chip pressure and Section 232 semiconductor tariff risk are active overhangs; overseas fabs carry a 2–4pp gross-margin drag. This is a known, partially priced headwind rather than a binary event.

**AI demand durability.** Roughly a quarter of revenue is directly AI-related and rising. Any deceleration in AI capex or an overbuild by financing-dependent "neoclouds" would pressure the raised guidance. Management's deliberately vague timing on the $100B Arizona add is read as retained flexibility in case demand cools, a prudent signal.

**2nm execution.** The near-term ramp pressures Q3 margins (guided to 65–67%) and yield/cost absorption is the next scrutiny point.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that the two largest risks (geopolitical, customer concentration) are severe and only partially priced, offset by the fact that most of the operational risks (tariffs, margin drag, 2nm ramp) are known and manageable. This is consistent with the recent trajectory of the risk score improving from 4.5 as the demand durability question resolved favorably.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.7% is rich relative to 39.6% one-year historical volatility (an 8.2pp spread), but sits at only the 11th percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is flat (near 47.7% ≈ far 46.4%). ATM IV collapsed from 62.3% to 47.7% over the prior three sessions as the earnings event resolved, so the elevated-vol window has largely passed. The 1-week put/call skew of 11.4% shows some near-term put demand, and unusual activity includes heavy volume in downside puts ($330 and $340 August strikes, deep out-of-the-money), consistent with tail-hedging against the Taiwan risk rather than a directional fundamental bet. The $428 July-31 call activity points to two-sided positioning. The options market is pricing the geopolitical tail as a persistent hedge cost, not signaling imminent fundamental deterioration. With IV having crushed post-earnings, the entry timing is reasonable rather than stretched.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($560).** The AI capex cycle continues to accelerate as management's >40% guidance implies. The 2nm mix leg ramps through 2027–28, lifting both revenue mix and gross margin back above 70% as overseas drag is absorbed by productivity and pricing. CoWoS scarcity persists, sustaining 5–10% price increases across leading-edge nodes. Next-year EPS of $21.29 proves conservative, and the stock re-rates toward ~26x on rising estimates, approaching the sell-side high cluster. Arizona de-risks the Taiwan concentration premium over time, narrowing the discount to fabless peers.

**Bear Case ($340–360).** A hyperscaler capex pause or neocloud overbuild breaks the demand narrative, and the >40% guidance proves the top of the cycle. Overseas margin drag plus Section 232 tariffs compress gross margin below the 65% guide floor. A Taiwan escalation (blockade, coercion, or worse) repriced even at low probability would drive a far larger drawdown than the bear target, which is why position sizing is the primary risk control rather than a stop-loss. The 2nm ramp encounters yield issues that pressure margins into 2027.

The base case rests between these: continued high-30s-to-40% revenue growth, gross margin holding in the mid-to-high 60s, and estimate revisions dragging the stale consensus targets upward over the next two-to-four quarters.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $475.** ~22.3x next-year consensus EPS of $21.29 = $475. This reflects a modest re-rating from ~19.5x next-year toward the low end of the sell-side central range as estimates continue to rise, without assuming the full Morningstar or bull-case multiple. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios.

**Bull case target: $560** (~26x next-year EPS on continued acceleration and margin recovery; approaches the sell-side high cluster).

**Bear case target: $340–360** (~-16% to -18% from current $416; break in the AI demand narrative plus margin compression, excluding the geopolitical tail which would be materially worse).

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, anchored to quarterly earnings (next report ~mid-October 2026) and the 2nm ramp trajectory through 2027.

**Upside/downside from current ($416):** +14% to base case, +35% to bull case; -14% to -18% to bear case (geopolitical tail excluded).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. FY26 revenue growth guidance cut back below ~30%, or a downward Q4 revenue guide signaling a demand break.
2. Gross margin falling below the 65% guide floor for two consecutive quarters on structural (not one-time overseas-ramp) causes.
3. A sustained reversal in estimate-revision velocity: consensus current-year and next-year EPS falling for two consecutive months.
4. Any material Taiwan Strait escalation (blockade, military action): immediate reassessment regardless of fundamentals.

## Conclusion

TSMC is the indispensable node in the AI hardware supply chain, controlling both leading-edge logic and the CoWoS packaging chokepoint that no competitor can replicate at scale. Q2 2026 confirmed an accelerating rather than plateauing cycle, with margin and revenue expansion driven by sustainable node-mix and pricing rather than volume, and the highest-value 2nm leg still almost entirely ahead. At ~19.5x next-year earnings with rising estimates and a PEG well below 1.0, the stock trades at roughly half the multiple of the fabless names it enables, and below every central sell-side target and Morningstar's $534 fair value.

The valuation discount is almost entirely a Taiwan geographic-concentration premium that is real, severe, and not hedgeable, the reason to size the position with discipline rather than at maximum conviction. Within the AI infrastructure theme, TSMC offers broad, backlog-confirmed demand, a dominant moat, and a reasonable multiple. The ~13% pullback from the June high on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals improves the entry. We rate TSM a **BUY**, deploying on weakness, with the Taiwan tail the defining constraint on sizing.
