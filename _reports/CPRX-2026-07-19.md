**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Catalyst Pharmaceuticals is a closed merger situation, not an investable common equity. On May 6, 2026, Catalyst signed a definitive agreement to be acquired by Angelini Pharma S.p.A. (backed by Italian state lender Cassa Depositi e Prestiti) for $31.50 per share in cash, valuing the company at roughly $4.1 billion. Stockholders approved the transaction on July 8, 2026, all regulatory approvals (including CFIUS review of the state-backed foreign buyer) cleared, and the deal closed on July 16, 2026. CPRX has been delisted from NASDAQ. The July 16 8-K carried the completion, delisting, change-of-control, and officer-departure items that formally end the public-equity life of the company.

The ~$31.48 price in the dataset is not a fundamentally-derived quote. It is the market pricing the stock within about two cents of the fixed $31.50 cash consideration, the standard residual spread in a merger arbitrage that is essentially complete. The traditional analytical question of whether the shares are cheap or expensive is moot: the payout is a fixed cash amount, the deal has closed, and there is no tradable forward equity remaining. Screening signals that would ordinarily be informative (the +11% current-year estimate revisions, the four-quarter beat streak, the 6.8% short interest) are noise here. The analyst consensus "targets" of $31.50 to $32.00 simply bracket the deal price rather than reflect independent valuation work.

We rate CPRX AVOID. The underlying orphan-drug franchise was genuinely attractive, debt-free, and cash-rich, and the sale at $31.50 was a defensible outcome for holders. The rating is a mechanical conclusion: there is no meaningful upside from the current price (roughly two cents to the deal terms), the shares are delisted, and any position taken while the stock traded was a merger-arb capture of the final residual spread, not a forward investment thesis. New capital cannot be deployed here.

## Company Overview

Catalyst was a Coral Gables, Florida–based commercial-stage rare-disease biopharmaceutical company operating in a single reportable segment. Its model was capital-light: it in-licensed and acquired approved orphan drugs and commercialized them through specialized rare-disease infrastructure rather than pursuing heavy internal discovery (R&D ran at roughly 3% of operating expenses). The portfolio comprised three marketed products:

- **FIRDAPSE (amifampridine)** — the core asset, approved in 2018 for Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS), an ultra-rare neuromuscular disorder. Roughly $99 million of Q1'26 revenue (+18% YoY), representing about two-thirds of total revenue. The August 2025 addition of LEMS/amifampridine to the NCCN Small Cell Lung Cancer guidelines provided a growth vector that could surface undiagnosed LEMS patients within oncology populations.
- **AGAMREE (vamorolone)** — a dissociative corticosteroid for Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy, licensed from Santhera (North American rights, 2023; launched March 2024). The fastest-growing asset at roughly $37 million in Q1'26 (+67% YoY), positioned to coexist with emerging DMD gene therapies.
- **FYCOMPA (perampanel)** — an epilepsy AMPA antagonist acquired from Eisai in 2023. In terminal decline following 2025 patent expiry; Q1'26 revenue collapsed to about $14 million (−61% YoY), and the company ceased marketing at the end of 2025.

For Angelini, the acquisition provided U.S. market entry and a bolt-on to its Brain Health and Rare Disease focus, adding entrenched U.S. commercial infrastructure and durable FIRDAPSE cash flows protected to approximately 2035.

## Financial Analysis

The business Angelini acquired was a high-quality specialty pharma: profitable, debt-free, and strongly cash-generative, an unusual profile in biotechnology.

- **Revenue (Q1'26):** $149.4 million total, up 5.6% YoY, with FIRDAPSE (+$15M) and AGAMREE (+$15M) growth more than offsetting the FYCOMPA generic cliff (−$22M). Full-year revenue scaled from roughly $214 million (2022) toward approximately $589 million (2025).
- **Profitability:** Q1'26 operating income of $73.2 million (49% operating margin), net income of $63.7 million (43% net margin), and diluted EPS of $0.50 versus $0.45 prior year. Full-year net income was approximately $214 million.
- **Balance sheet:** $755.9 million in cash (largely Treasuries and money-market), essentially no debt, $1.01 billion of stockholders' equity, and working capital of about $808 million. Leverage risk was effectively nil.
- **Cash flow:** Roughly $59.6 million of operating cash flow in Q1'26, dipping modestly YoY only because of a one-time $12.5 million AGAMREE sales-milestone payment to Santhera.
- **Capital allocation:** A $200 million buyback authorized in October 2025; $14.6 million repurchased in Q1'26 (~$23.67 average) and $39.9 million through May 7 (~$22.91 average), well below the eventual $31.50 takeout, value-accretive in hindsight.
- **Hidden margin tailwind:** In January 2026, the blended royalty on U.S. FIRDAPSE sales stepped down from a maximum of roughly 18.5% to approximately 6% as upstream royalty terms expired, a structural gross-margin improvement that landed as the sale process concluded.

These financials support a high Financial Health score. The score is now backward-looking: the balance sheet transferred to Angelini at close, and the cash pile earmarked for a "transformational" acquisition that never materialized effectively became part of the value Angelini captured.

## Growth Analysis

FIRDAPSE and AGAMREE both delivered strong organic growth, with the NCCN guideline addition and the DMD launch providing multi-year runway, partially masked by the FYCOMPA decline. Current-year EPS estimates rose +11% over 90 days and next-quarter estimates were up +12%, and the company beat consensus in all four of the last reported quarters (surprises ranging from 4.1% to 24.0%). Under ordinary circumstances these would be constructive forward signals.

They are not applicable here. Growth prospects as an equity attribute require a forward-owned claim on future earnings, and there is none: the company was acquired for a fixed cash amount and delisted. The revenue and earnings will accrue to Angelini, not to public shareholders. We score Growth Prospects low to reflect that there is no forward equity participation in whatever growth the franchise generates, regardless of how healthy the underlying trends were. The single-analyst basis for the near-term estimates also reduces their reliability even in the abstract.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation question is closed. The closing quote of roughly $31.48 sits about two cents below the deal price, the normal residual for a completed arb. Any framework (the 18.2x trailing/forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, DCF, or peer comparison against the biotech median P/E of 33.9x) is subordinated to the fact that the terms are fixed and paid.

The peer set (RCUS, CRSP, XENE, LGND, SRRK) is largely unprofitable and offers no useful read-through to a company being taken private at a defined multiple. The analyst targets are deal-anchored, not independent: the guidance to treat analyst targets as floors on inflection stocks does not apply when those targets simply mirror announced merger consideration. There is no mispricing to exploit: the two-cent residual is not asymmetric upside, and it carries the tail risk of any last-mile deal complication, which has now resolved by close. We score Valuation at the low end because there is effectively zero forward upside from the current price and no reason the market is wrong about the $31.50 clearing level.

## Competitive Landscape

The franchise's competitive standing was solid and de-risked before the sale. FIRDAPSE held effective monopoly status in LEMS through approximately 2035: the historical RUZURGI threat was neutralized via a 2022 settlement and non-compete, and all four Paragraph-IV ANDA filers (Teva, Lupin, Inventia, Hetero) were settled, with the final Hetero settlement on May 6, 2026 pushing generic entry to January/February 2035. Orphan-drug exclusivity lapsed on November 26, 2025, so the patent settlements, not exclusivity, were the durable moat.

AGAMREE competed against generic corticosteroids but was positioned as a foundational, better-tolerated steroid backbone able to coexist with DMD gene therapies, with ex-U.S. validation through Santhera's EU, UK, and Swiss approvals. FYCOMPA was a fully commoditized, declining asset post-genericization. The competitive position score sits at a neutral level: the moat was real and secured, but the trajectory as a standalone entity is no longer relevant. The assets now compete under Angelini's ownership, and the concentration profile (FIRDAPSE at roughly two-thirds of revenue, a single distributor at 90.1% of net product revenue in Q1'26) was a genuine single-point-of-failure vulnerability that a diversified-portfolio description obscured.

## Risk Assessment

The equity no longer exists, which overwhelms all conventional risk analysis. The merger-related risks that mattered — the $155.5 million termination fee, deal litigation, and CFIUS/regulatory uncertainty around a state-backed foreign acquirer — have all resolved with the July 16 close. The pre-close operating risks (extreme single-product concentration, 90% single-customer dependency, sole-source manufacturing reliance on Santhera and Eisai, and newly disclosed Section 232 tariff and Most-Favored-Nation pricing exposure, including the disclosure that Catalyst had not been invited to the MFN/tariff negotiations reached by 16 of 17 large pharma peers) are now Angelini's issues, not those of any CPRX holder.

The only residual risk to a holder at the current price is de minimis: the deal closed, so there is no remaining transaction risk, and the cash consideration is fixed. We assign a middling Risk Factors score to capture that the current price embeds essentially no idiosyncratic downside beyond the trivial arb residual, but also no protective asymmetry: the value is capped at $31.50 and has already been realized.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
There is no bull case as a forward equity. The most favorable historical framing is that Catalyst monetized a de-risked, cash-rich orphan-drug platform at $31.50, a ~47% gain over the trailing year and a clean exit relative to the risk of an expensive, uncertain "transformational" acquisition management had been hunting. For anyone who held into close, the outcome was fully realized in cash.

### Bear Case
For any new buyer at the current quote, the risk/reward is unattractive because of the absence of upside: roughly two cents to the deal terms, against the opportunity cost of deploying capital, in a security that is now delisted and cannot be accumulated. The stock offers no participation in the franchise's continued growth, which accrues entirely to Angelini.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: There is no short catalyst. The stock was acquired for cash and closed on July 16, 2026; it is delisted. A short would require the deal to break and the price to fall toward standalone fair value, but the deal has already completed.

**Timing window**: Not applicable — the transaction is closed.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest was 6.8% of float with 4.1 days to cover. In a completed cash merger, remaining short positions are covered at the deal price mechanically; there is no meaningful squeeze dynamic and no directional trade.

**Borrow and options signal**: The options-delta series shows only a +0.5% price move and no informative shift, with the options-sentiment field null. Volatility is collapsing toward the fixed cash payout, as expected. There is no bearish confirmation because there is no live equity to be bearish on.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: None. A short against $31.50 fixed cash consideration has no downside target and unbounded arb-completion risk (you are shorting a cash payment). Risk/reward is decisively unfavorable.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE** — the situation is a closed cash acquisition, not a company in distress. There is no catalyst, no timing trigger, and no tradable common equity. Avoid rather than short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: None as currently constituted. CPRX common stock has been delisted following the completed all-cash acquisition by Angelini Pharma. There is no public equity to re-enter. The only theoretical future consideration would be a newly listed successor security, which does not exist in this structure — Catalyst is now a private subsidiary of Angelini.

**Timeline**: The situation is resolved. Any residual shares held at the effective date convert to the right to receive $31.50 per share in cash. There is no ongoing thesis to monitor and no horizon over which a public-equity view would develop.

## Conclusion

We rate CPRX AVOID, reflecting that it is a closed merger situation rather than an investable equity. Catalyst was a well-run, debt-free, cash-generative orphan-drug franchise whose central overhang, the FIRDAPSE generic cliff, was contractually deferred to approximately 2035 through a series of patent settlements just as the company agreed to sell itself to Angelini Pharma. The shares are delisted.

The declining score trajectory (7.2 in April to 5.6 in June) tracked the market's progressive recognition that the equity was converging on a fixed cash outcome. This report completes that trajectory: with the deal closed, the valuation and growth dimensions score low not because the business deteriorated but because there is no forward equity claim, while financial health remains high on a backward-looking basis. The quote is a merger-arb residual worth roughly two cents against the $31.50 terms, offering no asymmetric upside and no reason to commit capital. Investors who held into close realized their value in cash; there is nothing further to do here.
