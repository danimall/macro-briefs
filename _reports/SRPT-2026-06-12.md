**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sarepta Therapeutics has experienced one of the more complete fundamental breakdowns in large-cap biotech over the past twelve months. The stock has fallen roughly 60% over the period and ~61% from its mid-2025 high near $38 to the current $15. The decline reflects genuine, escalating fundamental deterioration rather than market volatility: three patient deaths from acute liver failure tied to its AAV gene-therapy platform, a forced label restriction that removed the non-ambulatory Duchenne population entirely from ELEVIDYS, a boxed warning, FDA revocation of the AAVrh74 platform designation, and the failure of the ESSENCE confirmatory trial that now threatens the stable PMO exon-skipping franchise via potential accelerated-approval withdrawal.

The Q1 2026 reported net income of $331M (EPS $2.88) is an artifact, not a recovery. Of total revenue, $365M was one-time collaboration income — chiefly $325M recognized when Roche declined an option (a non-cash conversion of deferred revenue) plus a $40M Japan milestone. The underlying commercial business deteriorated sharply: ELEVIDYS U.S. product revenue fell 73% YoY to $102M. The market's forward P/E of ~5.5x is mechanically distorted by these non-recurring earnings and should not be read as a value signal. Strip out the collaboration income and the company is a shrinking-revenue, cash-burning entity facing a near-term liquidity management problem, with management explicitly citing the need to "meet 2027 financial obligations" as the rationale for a 36% workforce cut.

We rate SRPT AVOID. Both the growth pillar and the stability pillar are impaired at the same time, which is unusual and leaves no clean revenue base to underwrite a turnaround. The path forward runs through a set of binary, uncertain catalysts: the Cohort 8 sirolimus study (year-end 2026), FDA decisions on the failed ESSENCE/PMO labels, and early-stage siRNA readouts, against a backdrop of CEO departure, C-suite churn, active securities litigation, and ~$1B of debt. The risk distribution is heavily skewed to the downside.

## Company Overview

Sarepta is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company built around rare genetic disease, historically dominated by Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). It operates in a single reportable segment with two commercial pillars:

1. **PMO exon-skipping injectables** — EXONDYS 51, VYONDYS 53, and AMONDYS 45, all approved under the FDA's accelerated-approval pathway. This was the stable cash base, generating $228.5M in Q1 2026 product revenue, down only 3% YoY.

2. **ELEVIDYS (delandistrogene moxeparvovec)** — an AAVrh74-based gene therapy for DMD, formerly the growth engine and now the center of the company's crisis.

The company is attempting to pivot toward an siRNA pipeline via a large Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals collaboration (covering FSHD, DM1, ataxias, Huntington's, and IPF), supplemented by an LGMD gene-therapy program (SRP-9003). Ex-U.S. ELEVIDYS rights are partnered with Roche, with Sarepta retaining product ownership and manufacturing responsibility. Sarepta has no internal GMP manufacturing, relying on a limited set of contract manufacturers (Catalent for gene therapy, Aldevron for plasmid), a structural vulnerability that compounds the safety and supply risk.

The company employs ~835 people following a ~36% workforce reduction in July 2025, and carries a market capitalization of approximately $1.6B.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 collapse.** Sarepta swung from roughly $235M of net income in FY2024 to a net loss of approximately $713M in FY2025, with R&D nearly doubling to ~$1.52B. A large part of that R&D step-up was the $583.6M Arrowhead upfront expensed in Q1 2025. The Altman Z-Score sits in the distress zone at ~1.17.

**Q1 2026 — read past the headline.** Reported net income beat consensus, but the quality of that result is poor:
- Collaboration revenue was $365M, of which $325M came from Roche declining an option (a mechanical recognition of deferred revenue with no cash inflow) plus a $40M Japan milestone.
- ELEVIDYS U.S. product revenue collapsed from $375M, reflecting the safety crisis and the label restriction to ambulatory patients only.
- PMO products held flat.

The earnings beat was driven by financial mechanics, not operating performance. The forward P/E of ~5.5x looks cheap only because consensus current-year EPS ($4.83) is inflated by the same non-recurring collaboration income. The current-year estimate has risen +55.9% over 90 days for that reason, while the next-year estimate has fallen –18.4% and the current-quarter estimate has been cut –42.0% over 90 days; the operating trajectory the revisions describe is negative.

**Cash burn and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was –$202.7M in Q1 2026. Cash, equivalents, and investments fell to $748M from $954M at year-end 2025, a ~$206M single-quarter drawdown. During the quarter, Sarepta paid Arrowhead a $200M DM1 milestone plus a $50M annual license fee. Debt stands at roughly $838M carrying value of convertible notes ($158.6M of 1.25% 2027 notes and $893.4M principal of 4.875% 2030 notes), with quarterly interest expense up to $21.7M after the 2025 exchange that pushed out maturities at a higher coupon. A $600M secured revolver (currently undrawn after a $250M draw-and-repay in Q1) carries leverage and interest-coverage covenants. Inventory is bloated at ~$1.18B, largely ELEVIDYS work-in-progress, against collapsing demand, a material write-off risk flagged repeatedly in the filing.

Management asserts at least 12 months of liquidity and reiterated FY2026 net product revenue guidance of $1.2B–$1.4B. The combination of ~$200M/quarter operating burn, ongoing Arrowhead obligations, and the explicit reference to managing toward 2027 obligations frames cash runway as the central question, not a peripheral one.

## Growth Analysis

There is no credible near-term organic growth story. ELEVIDYS, the former growth engine, lost its non-ambulatory population from the label entirely and now carries a boxed warning for acute liver injury/failure following three patient deaths in 2025. Resumption of non-ambulatory dosing depends on the Cohort 8 sirolimus immunosuppression study, with data expected by year-end 2026, a binary outcome the company cannot control.

The PMO franchise, which would normally provide a stable base to fund any pivot, is now itself at risk. The ESSENCE confirmatory trial for VYONDYS 53 and AMONDYS 45 missed its primary endpoint in November 2025. Because these are accelerated-approval products, the FDA — armed with expanded FDORA withdrawal powers — could pull them from the market or force label changes. sNDAs were submitted in April 2026, but the outcome is unresolved. Having both the growth and the stability pillars impaired simultaneously is the defining feature of this situation.

The forward optionality rests on the Arrowhead siRNA pipeline (FSHD, DM1, ataxias, Huntington's, IPF) and the LGMD gene therapy SRP-9003. The siRNA early data shared in March 2026 was single-ascending-dose, small-sample, and preliminary; more is expected in H2 2026. The LGMD program is on FDA clinical hold following the SRP-9004 trial death, with the agency requiring sirolimus data before accepting a BLA. These are multi-year, contingent shots on goal, not near-term growth drivers, and the contractual cost of the Arrowhead relationship — up to ~$10.3B in milestones — is onerous relative to a $1.6B market cap.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiple comparison is unreliable here because the earnings denominators are distorted. The trailing P/E of ~42.9x and the forward P/E of ~5.5x bracket a value range that is meaningless: the forward figure is inflated by one-time collaboration income embedded in the consensus current-year EPS. Peer biotech medians (P/E ~13.0x across ADMA, HRMY, VCEL and others) are not directly comparable given the heterogeneity of the group and SRPT's binary event profile.

A more honest framing values Sarepta on its sustainable commercial base plus optionality, net of risk. The PMO franchise generates roughly $900M annualized but faces accelerated-approval withdrawal risk. Ambulatory-only ELEVIDYS is a fraction of its former run-rate. Against this, the company carries ~$1B of debt, ~$748M and falling cash, ~$200M/quarter burn, ~$1.18B of at-risk inventory, and up to ~$12.1B of aggregate contingent milestone/royalty obligations across all agreements. The analyst target range runs from a low of $5 to a high of $38 around a $20 median, a spread that reflects a genuinely bimodal outcome set rather than a tight intrinsic-value estimate.

We assign a valuation score of 4.5, below midpoint. The stock is not obviously expensive on sustainable cash flows, and there is embedded option value in the siRNA pipeline and a potential Cohort 8 success. But this is a compressed multiple reflecting structural impairment, not a clean mispricing with an identifiable near-term re-rating catalyst that breaks favorably. The ~61% drawdown from the high is consistent with sophisticated investors pricing real fundamental deterioration: the safety crisis, the label loss, and the ESSENCE failure are confirmed, not speculative. The burden of proof sits firmly on the bull case, and the catalysts that could vindicate it are themselves binary and unresolved.

## Competitive Landscape

DMD is intensely competitive and the AAV gene-therapy safety narrative is now an industry-wide overhang. Exon-skipping competition includes Nippon Shinyaku (Viltepso), Wave, Dyne, Entrada, and Novartis (via its Avidity acquisition) with antibody-oligonucleotide conjugates. The siRNA and target-overlap field includes Novartis/Avidity, Dyne, PepGen, Vertex, Arthex, uniQure, Roche/Ionis, Wave, and PTC. Other DMD entrants include Santhera (vamorolone), Italfarmaco (Givinostat), PTC (ataluren), and Edgewise.

Sarepta's competitive position is deteriorating on trajectory, not merely weak on level. The ELEVIDYS deaths, the FDA's revocation of the AAVrh74 platform technology designation, and the European Commission's refusal of marketing authorization in September 2025 collectively erode the franchise that defined the company's leadership in DMD. The patent picture also weakened: the Federal Circuit reversed in favor of Regenxbio/U-Penn on the '617 patent in February 2026. Reimbursement pressure (IRA dynamics, most-favored-nation executive orders, payors restricting ELEVIDYS coverage even for ambulatory patients) compounds the headwinds. The broader erosion of confidence in AAV gene therapy as a modality reduces the strategic value of Sarepta's historical core.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is severe and dominated by idiosyncratic, binary events:

1. **Safety crisis and boxed warning.** Three 2025 deaths from acute liver failure (two ELEVIDYS, one SRP-9004), shipment suspensions, an FDA-requested halt, a boxed warning, removal of the non-ambulatory population from the label, and revocation of the AAVrh74 platform designation. Non-ambulatory resumption hinges on Cohort 8 data due year-end 2026.

2. **PMO accelerated-approval withdrawal risk.** The ESSENCE confirmatory failure exposes VYONDYS 53 and AMONDYS 45, the most stable revenue stream, to potential market withdrawal or forced label change.

3. **LGMD clinical hold.** All investigational LGMD gene-therapy trials placed on FDA hold July 2025.

4. **EU rejection.** ELEVIDYS marketing authorization refused September 2025; ENVISION study paused.

5. **Litigation.** A securities class action names the CEO and other executives; consolidated derivative suits and active IP litigation add to the overhang.

6. **Contingent obligations.** Up to ~$10.3B Arrowhead milestones and ~$12.1B aggregate potential future payments against $748M cash.

7. **Concentration.** Two customers were 39% and 20% of Q1 product revenue; single-source ELEVIDYS drug substance manufacturing.

8. **Inventory write-off.** ~$1.18B inventory against collapsing demand.

The reported beta of 0.20 is misleading. It reflects idiosyncratic, FDA-driven volatility uncorrelated with the market, not stability: the filing discloses single-day moves of +35%/–42% over the trailing year. This is a high-risk, event-driven name carrying a low-beta label. The risk score of 2.5 reflects multiple unpriced-or-partially-priced binary catalysts with asymmetric downside.

### Options Market Signal

No options sentiment dataset was provided. The available positioning signal is short interest at 28.5% of float with 8.5 days to cover. This is very high and cuts in two directions: it confirms a widely held bearish thesis with structural deterioration as the driver, and it creates meaningful squeeze risk on any positive surprise (a favorable Cohort 8 readout or benign FDA decision on the PMO products). The crowded short is a reason not to actively short the stock, even while the fundamental case supports avoiding ownership.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The PMO products still generated ~$229M in Q1 2026 and held nearly flat. Ambulatory ELEVIDYS shipments have resumed, and the Roche partnership provides ex-U.S. optionality and milestone cash (the $40M Japan milestone is evidence). The Cohort 8 sirolimus study offers a defined path to restoring non-ambulatory dosing, which would re-open a large addressable population. The siRNA pipeline provides multiple shots on goal if any program reads out positively. With the stock down ~61% from its high and short interest at 28.5%, any positive resolution of a binary catalyst could drive a violent re-rating. In a favorable scenario, the stock could recover toward the analyst high of $38.

**Bear Case.** The Q1 profit was non-recurring; the operating business is shrinking, with ELEVIDYS U.S. sales down 73%. Cohort 8 fails or the FDA declines to expand the label, and ELEVIDYS remains a stub of its former run-rate. The ESSENCE failure leads the FDA to withdraw or severely restrict the PMO products, removing the company's only stable cash base. Cash burn of ~$200M/quarter against $748M and ongoing Arrowhead obligations forces a dilutive raise or restructuring before the 2030 notes come due. CEO departure and C-suite churn impair execution during the critical period. In this scenario the stock trades toward the analyst low of $5.

The asymmetry is unattractive at this stage. The bear case is supported by confirmed, dated events (the deaths, the label loss, the ESSENCE failure); the bull case depends on unresolved binary outcomes the company does not control.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest downside catalysts are the FDA's decision on the failed ESSENCE confirmatory trial / PMO labels (sNDAs submitted April 2026, decision timing uncertain) and the Cohort 8 sirolimus data due by year-end 2026. Each could drive the stock materially lower on a negative outcome, but neither is precisely dated.

**Timing window.** The most material binary (Cohort 8) resolves around year-end 2026, roughly six months out. The PMO/ESSENCE FDA path is open-ended. A six-month-plus window with no precise date weakens the case for an active short against carry costs.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 28.5% of float with 8.5 days to cover and ~25.1M shares short. This is above the 25% threshold that defines a crowded short. The bearish thesis is correct but heavily consensus, and any positive catalyst surprise would force a violent covering rally.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options sentiment data was provided. The 28.5% short interest implies elevated borrow cost and confirms crowding. There is no independent options confirmation available to support adding to the short.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A negative Cohort 8 or PMO outcome could push the stock toward the $5 analyst low (~67% downside). But the squeeze upside on a positive surprise is large given the crowding: a move back toward $25–38 is plausible. With a crowded book, an undated PMO catalyst, and a binary year-end event whose outcome is genuinely uncertain, the risk/reward does not clear the 2:1 bar for an active short.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The fundamental thesis (deteriorating franchise, masked earnings, liquidity pressure) is sound, but short interest at 28.5% and the violent squeeze potential on any favorable binary outcome make this a poor active short. Avoid ownership; do not press the short here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (conditions that would change the AVOID view):**
- A positive Cohort 8 sirolimus readout (year-end 2026) that re-opens non-ambulatory ELEVIDYS dosing, demonstrably reversing the revenue decline in subsequent quarters.
- A benign FDA resolution of the ESSENCE/PMO situation that preserves the exon-skipping franchise without material label restriction, restoring a stable cash base.
- Evidence of stabilized operating cash flow (quarterly burn narrowing toward breakeven) and a credible non-dilutive plan to address the 2030 notes, removing the liquidity overhang.
- Clustered open-market insider buying — absent throughout the drawdown — which would signal genuine internal conviction.

**Timeline.** The headwinds are likely to persist through at least year-end 2026, when Cohort 8 data and FDA decisions on the PMO products resolve. We would not revisit a more constructive stance until those binary events clear favorably and operating fundamentals show stabilization across at least two quarters. The next natural reassessment points are the Cohort 8 readout and each subsequent earnings release that reveals the underlying ELEVIDYS and PMO product-revenue trajectory net of one-time collaboration income.

## Conclusion

Sarepta's reported earnings mask a deteriorating commercial reality. The Q1 2026 profit was almost entirely non-recurring Roche collaboration income; the operating franchise is shrinking, and the PMO cash cow is now exposed to accelerated-approval withdrawal. Both the growth engine and the stability base are impaired at once, an unusually difficult configuration. The balance sheet — $748M and falling cash, ~$1B of debt, ~$200M/quarter burn, ~$1.18B of at-risk inventory, and billions in contingent milestone obligations — frames cash runway as the central question, and management's own language about managing to 2027 obligations underscores it. Leadership transition during the most fragile period of the company's history adds execution risk.

The compressed forward multiple is a function of distorted earnings, not value. The path to recovery runs through binary FDA and clinical catalysts the company cannot control, against active litigation and a 28.5% short interest that makes the stock dangerous to short as well as to own. We rate SRPT **AVOID**. We would revisit only on a favorable Cohort 8 readout, a benign PMO/ESSENCE resolution, and demonstrated stabilization of operating cash flow.
