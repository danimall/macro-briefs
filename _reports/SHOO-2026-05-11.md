## Executive Summary

Steven Madden is a mid-cap footwear and accessories company digesting its largest acquisition ever: the $403M purchase of Kurt Geiger in May 2025. Reported 2025 EPS of $0.63 understates normalized earnings by roughly 70% due to non-recurring acquisition charges, making the headline 38x trailing P/E misleading. On guided 2026 EPS of $2.00-$2.10, the stock trades at ~19x forward earnings, a moderate premium to footwear peer medians (14.6-14.9x) but justified by stronger growth (Q1 revenue +18% reported) and brand momentum.

The core risk is the macro overlay: 56.1% of purchases sourced from China, private label wholesale in "steep decline" due to tariff hesitancy, and consumer discretionary spending under pressure. The Q1 2026 beat ($0.45 vs. $0.39 estimate, +14.3% surprise) and raised guidance were encouraging, but organic revenue declined 4.8% — growth is entirely acquisition-driven. Until private label recovers and tariff visibility improves, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.

## Company Overview

Steven Madden designs, sources, and markets branded and private label footwear, accessories, and apparel across four segments: Wholesale Footwear (40.9% of 2025 revenue), Wholesale Accessories/Apparel (25.3%), Direct-to-Consumer (33.4%), and Licensing (0.5%). The company operates 399 stores, 133 international concessions, and 7 e-commerce sites. Key brands include Steve Madden, Kurt Geiger London, Dolce Vita, and Betsey Johnson. Manufacturing is outsourced entirely, with China (56.1%) and Cambodia (24.6%) as primary sourcing countries.

The Kurt Geiger acquisition (May 2025) roughly doubled international exposure and DTC penetration. Kurt Geiger operates primarily through concessions in UK department stores and full-price retail locations, adding accessible luxury positioning to SHOO's core mass/mid-tier portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $2.52B in FY2025 (+11% YoY), driven by Kurt Geiger. Q1 2026 revenue of $653M (+18% reported) masks a -4.8% organic decline — private label wholesale orders effectively paused due to tariff uncertainty.

**Profitability:** FY2025 reported operating income collapsed from $225M to $81M (margin from 9.9% to 3.2%). This is almost entirely non-recurring:
- $38.8M Kurt Geiger compensation charges
- $30.9M purchase accounting inventory adjustments
- $13.3M transaction costs
- $7.3M litigation
- $4.0M severance

Adjusted for these items, underlying profitability remained solid. Q1 2026 gross margin of 46.3% (+540bps YoY) demonstrates the positive mix shift from Kurt Geiger's higher ASPs and DTC penetration.

**Balance Sheet:** The company went from zero debt to $240M (term loan maturing May 2030) for the acquisition. Net debt is approximately $209M. Current ratio of 1.9x is adequate. The company made $60M in voluntary early debt repayments in 2025, and management has indicated potential buyback resumption in H2 2026 if tariff refunds materialize.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $162M, capex of $42.7M, yielding approximately $120M FCF — a 4.2% FCF yield on the current $2.84B market cap. Dividend of $0.84/share ($61M annually) is well covered by FCF at ~50% payout.

**Inventory:** Ex-Kurt Geiger inventory declined 2.5% YoY in Q1 2026, with turns at 5.0x (consolidated 3.8x). Disciplined management.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term drivers:**
- Kurt Geiger: guided to "low $600M" in 2026 revenue with mid-teens pro forma growth; entering Macy's (October 2026, 15 doors + Herald Square concession)
- Steve Madden brand: mid- to high-single-digit growth; DTC comps +17% in Q1; online searches +27%
- Dolce Vita: high-single-digit growth
- International expansion: Greater China JV (formed August 2025), India entry via Reliance Brands (Q4 2026), Australia JV

**Concerns:**
- Organic revenue declined 4.8% in Q1 2026 when stripping out acquisitions
- Private label "steep decline" with recovery deferred to 2027
- Consumer spending environment deteriorating
- Brand heat cycles in fashion are inherently temporary — 27% search growth is exceptional but unsustainable

**Guidance:** Revenue growth of 10-12% for FY2026, EPS of $2.00-$2.10. Backlog of $674M (+10.6% YoY) supports the revenue target.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.84 (current) on $2.05 midpoint FY2026 EPS guidance, SHOO trades at approximately 19x forward earnings. This compares to:
- Peer median: 14.6-14.9x (Wolverine and Deckers)
- SHOO 5-year average: ~18-22x
- FY2027 consensus EPS of $2.67 implies 14.6x on next-year earnings

The premium to peers is partially justified by stronger near-term growth (18% reported vs. peers declining/flat) and brand momentum. However, 56% China sourcing represents a structural margin risk that peers like Deckers have more successfully diversified away from. The stock has appreciated 70% from its 52-week low of $22.26 — much of the recovery from 2025's acquisition noise is already priced.

Analyst consensus target of $45.44 (median $49.00) implies 15-26% upside. The $31 low target reflects a scenario where tariff visibility doesn't improve and consumer spending weakens further.

On a DCF basis using $2.67 FY2027 EPS growing at 8-10% thereafter with a 17x terminal multiple discounted at 10%, fair value approximates $42-46 — roughly in line with current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

SHOO's core advantage is its "test-and-react" sourcing model — the ability to identify trends and deliver product to market faster than larger competitors. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, providing channel diversification. The Kurt Geiger acquisition adds an accessible luxury layer that competes directly with Coach, Kate Spade, and Michael Kors.

Competitive trajectory is positive: brand heat metrics (search growth, DTC comps, reduced promotions) suggest share gains in the current cycle. Both Steve Madden and Dolce Vita are outperforming at key retail partners. The concessions model (Kurt Geiger) is capital-light but carries real estate dependency on UK department store health.

Structural vulnerability: SHOO has no meaningful proprietary technology or IP beyond brand recognition. Fashion brands are cyclical by nature — current momentum will eventually normalize. The 56% China sourcing concentration is worse than most peers.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff exposure (HIGH):** Current assumption of 10% Section 122 tariffs through July 2026, rising to 15% thereafter. Cannot fully pass through costs — private label customers simply deferring orders. The Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling creates potential for $55M in refunds but also regulatory uncertainty.

**Consumer spending deterioration (HIGH):** As a consumer discretionary company selling footwear, SHOO is directly exposed to spending pullback.

**Kurt Geiger integration (MODERATE):** $403M acquisition with 74+ stores, 72 concessions, ~6,300 employees internationally. Internal controls excluded from SOX assessment. EBIT margins haven't recovered to pre-tariff levels. Concessions model dependent on UK department store foot traffic.

**Private label structural vulnerability (MODERATE):** No brand moat in private label — customers can simply pause orders. Recovery timing uncertain; management targeting 2027.

**Key person risk:** Steve Madden (the individual) remains critical to brand direction and identity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48-52):** Kurt Geiger achieves double-digit EBIT margins by mid-2027 as integration synergies materialize. Brand heat sustains through 2026-2027 holiday seasons. Tariff refunds ($55M) accelerate debt paydown and enable buybacks. Private label recovers in 2027 as tariff clarity emerges. FY2027 EPS reaches $2.80-$3.00. Stock re-rates to 17-18x = $48-54.

**Bear Case ($28-32):** Consumer recession hits in H2 2026 — mid-tier discretionary spending collapses. Brand heat cools as fashion cycle turns. Kurt Geiger UK concessions struggle. Tariffs escalate, compressing margins further. FY2026 EPS comes in at $1.70-$1.80 (below low end of guidance). Stock de-rates to 16-18x bear-case earnings = $27-32.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $32-34 range (15-16x FY2026 guided EPS) providing adequate margin of safety
2. Private label orders show quarter-over-quarter recovery in Q2/Q3 2026 filings
3. Tariff clarity emerges — either clear resolution or demonstrated margin resilience at current tariff levels through two consecutive quarters

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS guidance cut below $1.80 (would indicate tariff/consumer headwinds overwhelming brand momentum)
2. Kurt Geiger same-store sales turn negative for two consecutive quarters
3. US DTC comps decelerate below 5% (would signal brand heat cooling)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026) will provide visibility into: (a) private label trajectory, (b) Kurt Geiger margin progression, (c) consumer resilience through summer, and (d) tariff refund status.

## Conclusion

Steven Madden combines genuine operational improvement (brand momentum, margin expansion from Kurt Geiger mix, disciplined inventory) with significant external headwinds (56% China sourcing in a tariff-volatile environment, consumer spending deterioration, private label collapse). The Q1 beat was real — 46.3% gross margins represent structural improvement from the DTC/Kurt Geiger mix shift.

At ~19x guided FY2026 EPS, the stock is fairly valued given the growth-risk balance. The 70% appreciation from the 52-week low has already captured much of the transition from "noisy acquisition year" to "normalized earnings power." For the stock to work meaningfully from here, it needs either (a) tariff resolution that restores private label volume, (b) sustained brand heat through a difficult consumer environment, or (c) Kurt Geiger margin acceleration proving the acquisition thesis. The current macro environment makes all three less probable than they would be in a benign cycle.

HOLD with a reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings or on a pullback to the $32-34 range where risk/reward becomes more compelling.
