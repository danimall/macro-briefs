## Executive Summary

NCR Voyix is a post-spin-off entity attempting a complex transformation from legacy hardware/services provider to cloud-based SaaS platform for retail and restaurant point-of-sale. The transformation narrative has surface appeal: recurring revenue is growing to 62% of the mix, Adjusted EBITDA grew 22% in 2025, and the company's net leverage has declined to ~2.0x following the Digital Banking divestiture. The stock trades at $8.09, down 45% from its 52-week high, with analyst consensus suggesting 55%+ upside to the $12.50 median target.

However, the fundamental picture reveals a company still in distress-zone territory (Altman Z-Score 1.26) with negative GAAP free cash flow (-$375M in 2025), a $207M preferred stock redemption cliff arriving March 2027, declining EPS estimates across the near-term horizon, and a 30.9% short interest that reflects institutional skepticism about execution. Two CEOs in 27 months, $134M in environmental liabilities, ACH fraud losses, and a hardware transition creating single-source dependency on Ennoconn compound the risk profile. While sell-side analysts remain majority bullish, the balance of evidence suggests the downside risks outweigh the recovery optionality at current prices.

## Company Overview

NCR Voyix operates two segments serving retail (69% of revenue) and restaurant (30%) industries with point-of-sale software, self-checkout systems, payment processing, and field services. The company was created in October 2023 when NCR Corporation spun off its ATM/banking hardware business as NCR Atleos. In September 2024, VYX sold its Digital Banking segment to Veritas Capital for $2.45B, using proceeds to eliminate most of its term loan debt. Beginning January 2026, the company began outsourcing hardware manufacturing to Ennoconn Corporation, completing a transition toward a software-and-services business model.

The Voyix Commerce Platform is a cloud-based, microservices architecture offering SaaS solutions for enterprise and mid-market customers. Key competitive differentiators include the Aloha restaurant POS brand (deep enterprise penetration), integrated payments via Voyix Connect and Voyix Pay, and a large field services organization supporting complex multi-location deployments.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory: declining by design, but the pace raises questions.** Revenue fell from $3.17B (2023) to $2.82B (2024) to $2.69B (2025), with 2026 guidance of $2.19B-$2.30B implying another 17-19% decline. The company attributes this to hardware de-emphasis, the Ennoconn transition (shifting from gross to net revenue recognition), and the Japan banking divestiture. Recurring revenue reached $1.68B (62% of total) in 2025, growing 3% YoY — a modest pace that needs to accelerate to justify the platform valuation.

**Profitability is improving from a low base.** Adjusted EBITDA of $425M (+22% YoY) at 15.8% margins represents genuine progress. Operating income turned positive at $26M in 2025 after losses of $134M and $38M in the prior two years. However, Q1 2026 reported a net loss of $8M, and the company continues to incur $124M+ annually in transformation/restructuring costs that show no sign of ending.

**Cash flow remains the critical weakness.** GAAP operating cash flow was -$210M in 2025. GAAP free cash flow was -$375M. The "adjusted" FCF of $27M relies on adding back $284M in Digital Banking sale taxes (non-recurring, defensible), $37M in environmental remediation (recurring for years), $26M in pension contributions (recurring), and $21M in "acceleration projects" (subjective). The company is burning real cash while growing at low single digits.

**Balance sheet: improved but fragile.** Net debt of ~$870M against $425M Adjusted EBITDA yields ~2.0x leverage — within covenant requirements (4.25x). Cash of $231M plus $476M revolver availability provides liquidity. Debt maturities are manageable: $650M in 2028, $403M in 2029, $52M in 2030. However, the $207M Series A Preferred put right exercisable in March 2027 represents a near-term liquidity event not reflected in simple leverage ratios.

## Growth Analysis

The bull case rests on the platform conversion thesis: as customers migrate from perpetual licenses and hardware purchases to subscription software and embedded payments, margins expand and revenue becomes more predictable. Evidence for this trajectory exists — recurring revenue ARR is $1.12B in Retail (+4%) and $559M in Restaurants (-1%). The Voyix Commerce Platform is gaining traction, with Q1 2026 showing "early sales momentum."

Against this thesis: **EPS estimates are falling.** Current quarter estimates declined 26.9% over 90 days (from $0.24 to $0.18). Full-year 2026 estimates fell 13.2% over 90 days (from $1.04 to $0.91). This is the opposite of the estimate revision signal that characterizes successful turnarounds. When a transformation company's estimates are declining during the core execution period, it typically signals that the conversion is proceeding more slowly or at higher cost than expected.

Restaurant ARR declining 1% is particularly concerning given that restaurant tech is one of the fastest-growing segments in enterprise software. Competitors like Toast are growing 25%+ while VYX's restaurant business shrinks.

## Valuation Assessment

At $8.09 with ~139M diluted shares, VYX carries a market cap of ~$1.12B and an enterprise value of ~$2.0B. Against $425M 2025 Adjusted EBITDA, this implies an EV/EBITDA of ~4.7x — optically cheap relative to software peers. The trailing P/E of ~51x reflects minimal GAAP earnings.

The peer comparison is instructive: the median P/E among comparable IT services companies is 12.5x. At consensus 2026 EPS of $0.91, VYX would trade at ~8.9x forward earnings. At 2027 consensus of $1.02, it's ~7.9x. These are low multiples, but they reflect genuine uncertainty about earnings quality (heavy adjustments, one-time items) and the sustainability of margins during the hardware transition.

The EV/EBITDA of 4.7x would be compelling for a stable business, but VYX is mid-transformation with declining revenue, negative GAAP FCF, and elevated restructuring costs. The low multiple partially reflects the market discounting the "adjusted" EBITDA as overstated relative to real cash generation capacity. Given that GAAP FCF was -$375M while adjusted EBITDA was $425M, the market's skepticism has quantitative support.

Sell-side targets range from $8 to $18 with a median of $12.50, implying 55% upside. However, 7 of 8 analysts are Buy-rated despite the stock's 45% decline from highs, which suggests limited accountability in the coverage.

## Competitive Landscape

VYX competes in a rapidly evolving market where cloud-native competitors (Toast, Square/Block, PAR Technology) have structural advantages in product velocity and user experience for new customer acquisition. VYX's advantage lies in its installed base, enterprise relationships, and field services footprint — these create switching costs but are a depleting asset if net-new customer acquisition doesn't improve.

R&D spending of $155M (5.8% of revenue) is modest relative to competitors and declining in absolute terms. This raises questions about whether VYX can maintain feature parity with better-capitalized rivals. The company claims to have "reclaimed the top spot in new restaurant deployments" per industry reports, though the restaurant ARR decline (-1%) creates tension with that narrative at the financial level.

The Ennoconn dependency introduces competitive risk: if hardware quality or delivery degrades, customer satisfaction suffers regardless of software improvements.

## Risk Assessment

**Preferred Stock Redemption (March 2027):** ~200,000 shares with $207M redemption value, exercisable in less than 12 months. With $231M cash and barely positive adjusted FCF, this creates forced dilution (~8% of float if converted to equity) or a liquidity crunch. Neither outcome is favorable.

**Short Interest (30.9% / 10.1 days to cover):** This is among the highest short interest levels for any NYSE-listed stock. While squeeze dynamics exist, sustained 30%+ short interest with 10+ days to cover typically reflects institutional conviction in fundamental deterioration rather than momentum-based shorts. The short thesis likely centers on the FCF gap, preferred put right, and transformation execution risk.

**Control Weaknesses:** ACH fraud of $34M (only $16M recovered), ransomware attack costing $47M, and multiple out-of-period revenue adjustments signal fragile internal controls during a period of intense organizational change.

**CEO Transition:** Two CEOs in 27 months is destabilizing. James Kelly (new CEO, Feb 2025) comes from payments (EVO Payments) which is relevant, but he's still establishing strategy approximately 15 months into the role. No open-market insider purchases by any executive signals limited insider confidence at these prices.

**Environmental Liabilities:** $134M in reserves with $40M due in 2026 and $41M in 2027-2028 — real cash drains that persist regardless of business performance.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The March 2027 preferred stock put right is the most concrete catalyst. As the redemption date approaches without clear FCF improvement, the market will increasingly price either dilution or liquidity stress. Q2 2026 earnings (expected ~August 2026) will reveal whether the Ennoconn hardware transition is proceeding smoothly or causing customer friction.

**Timing window:** 6-10 months. The preferred redemption discussion will begin impacting the stock by Q4 2026 as holders evaluate their options and the company must communicate its plan.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 30.9% SI with 10.1 days to cover, squeeze risk is ELEVATED. Any positive catalyst (deal announcement, strategic alternative, significant earnings beat) could trigger a violent short squeeze. The stock already moved from $6.02 to $8.09 (+34%) in recent weeks, suggesting covering activity or speculative buying.

**Borrow and options signal:** High days-to-cover (10.1) suggests borrow may be constrained and expensive. The crowded nature of the short makes new positions less attractive on a risk-adjusted basis.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $4-5/share (preferred dilution + continued FCF burn + competitive erosion) vs. upside squeeze risk to $11-12. That's roughly 40-50% downside vs. 35-50% upside squeeze — approximately 1:1 risk/reward.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis has merit (FCF problems, preferred cliff, declining estimates, execution risk), but the 30.9% short interest and recent 34% bounce from lows make initiating new shorts unattractive. The remaining downside is limited relative to squeeze risk at this entry point. Avoid owning; do not actively short at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($12-14):** The Voyix Commerce Platform gains adoption velocity, recurring revenue accelerates to 8-10% growth, hardware transition completes smoothly by mid-2026, Adjusted EBITDA reaches $475-500M in 2026, and the preferred holders opt for conversion rather than cash redemption. The company achieves normalized FCF of $150-200M by 2027, supporting a re-rating to 6-7x EV/EBITDA. Share buybacks at depressed prices compound returns.

**Bear Case ($4-5):** Recurring revenue stagnates as enterprise customers evaluate alternatives (Toast, Oracle). Ennoconn transition creates service disruptions. Preferred holders demand $207M cash redemption in March 2027, forcing either a dilutive equity raise or asset sales at distressed valuations. Environmental liabilities and restructuring costs consume remaining cash. Estimates continue falling, and the stock approaches book value.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** (1) Two consecutive quarters of positive GAAP free cash flow (excluding one-time items), demonstrating the transformation is generating real cash; (2) Resolution of the preferred stock situation — either voluntary conversion or a refinancing plan that doesn't dilute existing shareholders; (3) Recurring revenue growth accelerating to 7%+ YoY with improving restaurant ARR; (4) Short interest declining below 20%, indicating institutional skepticism is moderating.

**Timeline:** The transformation thesis needs 18-24 months to prove out. The preferred put right resolves by March 2027. Until both FCF generation and the preferred overhang are addressed, the risk/reward remains unfavorable.

## Conclusion

NCR Voyix illustrates the gap between narrative and execution. The story — legacy hardware company transforming into recurring-revenue software platform — is compelling in theory. The reality is a company burning cash, facing a $207M preferred redemption cliff in 12 months, on its second CEO in 27 months, carrying the highest short interest in its peer group, and seeing estimates decline rather than rise during the critical execution window. At 4.7x EV/EBITDA on heavily adjusted figures, the stock appears cheap, but adjusted EBITDA of $425M coexists with GAAP FCF of -$375M — the market is rationally discounting the quality of reported earnings. The sell-side consensus of $12.50 relies on the transformation succeeding according to plan; the evidence suggests it is proceeding more slowly and at higher cost than assumed. Avoid until cash generation turns demonstrably positive and the preferred stock situation resolves.
