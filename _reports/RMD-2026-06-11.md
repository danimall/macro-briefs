**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ResMed presents the clearest fundamentals-versus-sentiment disconnect in the medical device space right now. The operating business has, if anything, accelerated over the past year: FY2025 revenue grew 10% to $5.15B, gross margin expanded from 56.7% to 59.4%, and the most recent quarter (Q3 FY2026, ended 3/31/2026) printed a record 62.2% gross margin (+290bps YoY), revenue +11%, and non-GAAP EPS +21% to $2.86. Over the same window, the stock fell from an August 2025 high of $293.81 to $192.50, a 34% drawdown. The driver is the market repricing the company's terminal value lower on the fear that GLP-1 obesity drugs will shrink the obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) patient pool.

The valuation case rests on a specific mispricing. At $192.50 against an annualized non-GAAP EPS run-rate of roughly $11.40 (Q3 $2.86 × 4) and consensus current-year EPS of $11.08, RMD trades at approximately 17x forward earnings. That is below the health-care equipment peer median P/E of 26.8x and below RMD's own multi-year range, for a business growing low-double-digits with 60%+ gross margins, a net cash balance sheet, and 32% FCF margins. The market is applying a structural-decline multiple to a company whose every reported metric points to acceleration.

The reason this is a BUY rather than a HOLD is that the central bear thesis is empirically weakening while the price still reflects it. A growing body of 2026 evidence — including data showing GLP-1 therapy is funneling more patients into sleep diagnosis and CPAP, and Zepbound's FDA clearance specifically for OSA raising the clinical profile of the disease — suggests obesity drugs are a net tailwind to a market where only ~20% of US sufferers are diagnosed. Management is accelerating buybacks into the weakness ($500M in nine months versus $300M in all of FY2025), and JPMorgan initiated at Overweight in April 2026. The GLP-1 and US reimbursement debate is unresolved and caps conviction, but the price already embeds the bear case, which is what makes the risk/reward asymmetric.

## Company Overview

ResMed is the global leader in cloud-connected medical devices and digital health software for sleep-disordered breathing, primarily OSA, plus COPD and other chronic respiratory diseases. Founded in 1989 around the commercialization of CPAP therapy, it operates in 140+ countries with ~10,600 employees and dual-listing on the NYSE and ASX. Fiscal year ends June 30; the company is currently in FY2026.

The business runs in two segments. Sleep and Breathing Health (~88% of revenue) covers CPAP/APAP/bilevel devices (AirSense 11, AirCurve 11, AirMini), ventilators, mask systems (AirFit/AirTouch), and diagnostics (NightOwl home sleep test). Devices are ~52% of total revenue and masks/accessories ~36%. Residential Care Software (~12%) is a SaaS portfolio (Brightree, MatrixCare, HEALTHCAREfirst, MEDIFOX DAN) serving home medical equipment, home health, hospice, and skilled nursing providers in the US and Germany.

The competitive moat has three pillars. First, a near-duopoly in PAP devices alongside Fisher & Paykel, materially reinforced by competitor Philips' multi-year recall-driven exit (Philips remains under a US consent decree). Second, a recurring-revenue razor/razor-blade model where an installed base of 30M+ cloud-connected devices (AirView) and 10M+ myAir users drives high-margin mask and accessory resupply. Third, a proprietary real-world clinical data asset. The May 2025 VirtuOx acquisition extends ResMed into the home/remote diagnostic testing funnel.

## Financial Analysis

ResMed's financials are among the strongest in the sector. The standout story is structural margin expansion. GAAP gross margin moved from 56.7% in FY2024 to 59.4% in FY2025 and accelerated to 62.2% in Q3 FY2026, a 290bps YoY gain driven by component cost deflation, manufacturing and logistics efficiencies, and the prior-year absence of field-safety-notice costs. These are durable drivers, not one-time items. Operating margin runs 32.7%, net margin 27.2%, ROE 23.5%, and ROA 17.1%.

Revenue has compounded at roughly 10%+ for four consecutive years: $3.58B (FY2022), $4.22B (FY2023), $4.69B (FY2024), $5.15B (FY2025). Q3 FY2026 revenue was $1.43B (+8% constant currency), with US masks growing 14%, confirming the resupply annuity is intact and growing faster than devices. Nine-month FY2026 revenue reached $4.19B (+10%). The relative soft spot is Residential Care Software, which has decelerated to ~4% constant-currency growth.

The balance sheet is exceptionally clean. Altman Z-Score is 4.32 (deep safe zone), current ratio 3.44, and debt/equity 0.37. The company holds a net cash position — $1.66B cash against ~$664M total debt as of 3/31/2026 — having deleveraged long-term debt from $1.43B in FY2023 to ~$658M, flipping from net interest expense to net interest income. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.75B and free cash flow $1.66B (32% FCF margin) against only $90M of capex, reflecting an asset-light model.

Two caveats on the optics. FY2025 GAAP net income growth of +37% (EPS $9.51 vs $6.92) was partly inflated by one-time tax items (IRS interest/penalty refunds, business-cessation benefits) that lowered the effective rate to 16.5% from 19.3%. The cleaner non-GAAP EPS growth was ~24% ($9.55 vs $7.72), still excellent. In Q3 FY2026 the effective tax rate rose to ~20.6%, compressing GAAP net income growth (+9%) relative to non-GAAP (+20%). Use the non-GAAP series as the cleaner read.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one small miss over the last four quarters (Q3 FY26 +1.3%, Q2 FY26 +2.1%, Q1 FY26 -1.2%). The beats are modest and consistent, which is appropriate for a mature compounder.

## Growth Analysis

The underlying TAM is large and underpenetrated: roughly 936M people with OSA globally and ~480M with COPD, against only ~20% diagnosis rates in the US and ~10% elsewhere. That supports a long growth runway independent of any single-year reimbursement or drug debate. The resupply annuity, where masks and accessories grew faster than new device sales, is the highest-quality recurring component of the model.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $11.08 (versus $11.03 ninety days ago, +0.5%) and next-year at $12.20 (essentially flat). This matters for how I weight the strongest bullish signal in the framework: rapid upward estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days. RMD does not have that. This is a steady ~10% grower whose multiple has compressed, not a story with consensus racing to catch up. I am therefore basing the BUY on the valuation re-rating case (multiple too low for the quality and growth) rather than on an estimate-momentum case. That distinction is the reason growth prospects score in the mid-range rather than higher.

The realistic forward growth profile is low-double-digit revenue with continued gross margin tailwind, supporting low-to-mid-teens EPS growth before buybacks. The next-year consensus EPS of $12.20 implies ~10% growth on the current-year base, which is consistent with the historical cadence. Residential Care Software at ~4% is a drag but small enough (~12% of revenue) not to alter the aggregate picture.

## Valuation Assessment

At $192.50, RMD trades at ~17x forward non-GAAP EPS and ~17.4x current-year consensus. The peer median P/E in health-care equipment is 26.8x (DXCM 31.2x, GEHC 15.5x, STE 26.8x, ZBH 22.6x, HOLX 31.3x). RMD is the highest-quality name in this group on margins, returns on capital, balance sheet, and FCF conversion, yet trades at a discount to the median and only a modest premium to GEHC, the lowest-multiple peer.

The mechanism of the mispricing is identifiable and falsifiable. The market is assigning a terminal-decline multiple to RMD because of the GLP-1 fear, pricing the stock as though the OSA patient pool will shrink. If that fear were correct, ~17x would be defensible or even generous. The 2026 real-world evidence is moving the other way: GLP-1 patients are being funneled into sleep testing and CPAP at higher rates as awareness rises, Zepbound's OSA clearance has raised the clinical profile of the disease, and even in a scenario where some mild-OSA patients drop therapy, the remaining mix skews toward higher-severity, more-adherent, higher-margin patients. If consensus shifts from "GLP-1 is a headwind" to "GLP-1 is neutral-to-positive," the multiple has room to re-rate toward the low-20s, consistent with a 10% grower of this quality.

On a sum of the parts, a 22x multiple on $11.40 forward EPS yields ~$251, roughly the August/September 2025 trading level and below the prior 52-week high of $293.81. Even a re-rating to 20x implies ~$228, ~18% upside. The analyst consensus mean target of $270.6 and median of $286.0 (range $180–$340) sit well above the current price. With the stock cheap relative to its growth and quality, I treat the consensus target as a reference point rather than a ceiling, though I anchor my base case below it given the unresolved narrative risk.

The dividend yield of ~1.2% (raised to $0.60/quarter from $0.53) plus buybacks provides absolute return support and a floor. Buyback acceleration into a 34% drawdown is management implicitly signaling the stock is undervalued at these levels.

## Competitive Landscape

ResMed's competitive position is strong and, on net, improving. The PAP market is a structural duopoly with Fisher & Paykel; Philips' recall-driven absence has been a multi-year share and margin tailwind that ResMed has used to entrench its installed base. Other competitors (React Health, BMC, Löwenstein, Apex, Yuyue) are smaller and regional. The 30M+ connected-device installed base and resupply annuity raise switching costs and generate recurring high-margin revenue that pure device makers cannot easily replicate.

The trajectory question centers on two items. Philips' eventual full re-entry could pressure pricing and share, though timing is uncertain and ResMed has had years to deepen its position. The VirtuOx acquisition and NightOwl home sleep test extend ResMed forward into the diagnosis funnel, which is strategically sound in an underpenetrated market where lowering the diagnosis barrier expands the treatable pool. The competitive position score was previously marked at 8.0–8.5 in prior reviews; I bring it to 7.5 to reflect the genuine (if unquantified) longer-term threat of Philips re-entry and the possibility that GLP-1 dynamics, even if net-neutral, alter the patient mix in ways that complicate share economics. The moat is intact; the trajectory carries more uncertainty than a year ago.

## Risk Assessment

**GLP-1 / obesity drug disruption** is the single most important swing factor. Because obesity drives OSA, GLP-1s (Zepbound/tirzepatide is now FDA-cleared specifically for OSA) could shrink the addressable pool. The 10-K flags this in risk factors and quarterly-fluctuation language. The bull rebuttal is that the funnel is vastly underpenetrated, that real-world data shows GLP-1 patients being routed into sleep testing and CPAP, and that the disease's rising clinical profile expands awareness. The debate is unresolved, which is why the stock is cheap and why conviction is capped.

**Reimbursement and regulatory headwinds** are US-centric and detailed: pending reinstatement of the DMEPOS Competitive Bidding Program (historically cut CPAP reimbursement), a 2.93% CY2025 Physician Fee Schedule cut (partially offset by a 2.5% 2026 bump under the OBBBA), $930B–$1T Medicaid funding cuts under the OBBBA that could reduce covered lives, and a proposed VA disability rating change that could reduce veteran CPAP demand. The 2022 AHRQ assessment questioning CPAP's long-term outcome evidence is a latent payor risk.

**Supply chain — rare-earth magnets.** China's export controls on rare-earth magnets, which are integral to device components, are a newly emphasized risk, alongside tariff exposure across Singapore, Australia, Malaysia, US, China, and France manufacturing.

**Regulatory expansion via VirtuOx.** The acquisition converts ResMed from a HIPAA business associate to a Medicare-enrolled covered entity, directly subject to the Anti-Kickback Statute, False Claims Act, Stark Law, and HIPAA covered-entity rules. VirtuOx carries its own Corporate Integrity Agreement through 2027 (from a 2022 $3.2M FCA settlement). ResMed completed its own 5-year CIA (from a 2019 $40.6M DOJ settlement) at the end of FY2025. Immaterial financially, but a new qualitative risk vector.

These risks are partly priced (the 34% drawdown is the market pricing GLP-1 and reimbursement fear), but they are real and binary in places (competitive bidding reinstatement timing, VA rating change). I score risk factors 5.5 — below the midpoint — because the GLP-1 and reimbursement overhang is a genuine, unresolved, potentially material swing factor rather than a generic sector truism. This is the specific concern that prevents a higher overall score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not flagging acute stress. Near-term ATM IV is 29.5% versus 24.2% one-year historical vol (+5.3pp), but IV percentile within the current chain is only 4%, and the term structure is in contango (29.5% near, 33.0% at six months), the normal/complacent shape. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±3.8%. Put/call skew is balanced near-term (1.9%) and turns mildly negative at three to six months (-3.3% to -3.6%), consistent with longer-dated hedging of the GLP-1/reimbursement debate rather than imminent fear.

The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.79 to 3.22 alongside an 8.2% price decline since early May. That reflects accumulated downside hedging, not informed bearish conviction ahead of a dated catalyst — there is no near-term binary event. With IV only modestly elevated and the term structure complacent, the options market is consistent with a stock that has been de-rated on narrative and is now trading on lowered expectations rather than pricing an imminent shock. The bad news is largely in the price.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The GLP-1 fear is wrong, or at minimum overstated, and 2026 real-world data continues to show obesity drugs expanding the diagnosis-and-treatment funnel. Gross margin continues its structural climb past 62%, EPS compounds low-to-mid-teens before buybacks, and the multiple re-rates from 17x toward the low-20s as consensus shifts from headwind to neutral-positive. The stock returns toward the high-$250s to $290, in line with the analyst mean ($270.6) and median ($286) targets and the prior 52-week high.

**Bear case.** GLP-1 adoption meaningfully reduces new CPAP starts over the next several years, US reimbursement cuts (competitive bidding reinstatement, Medicaid coverage reduction) bite, and Philips re-enters to pressure pricing. Estimates roll over, the multiple stays at or below 17x, and the stock drifts toward $160–$170, ~12–17% below current levels, where the net-cash balance sheet, FCF margin, and accelerating buyback provide support.

The asymmetry favors the long. The bear case is largely embedded in the drawdown and current multiple for a sector-best business, while the bull case requires only that the GLP-1 narrative stop deteriorating, which the 2026 evidence is already suggesting. Downside to the bear case is ~12–17%; upside to the base case is ~18–30%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $228 (~18% upside).** 20x forward non-GAAP EPS of $11.40, a modest re-rating from the current ~17x as the GLP-1 overhang stabilizes, anchored conservatively below the analyst mean given unresolved narrative risk.

**Bull case target: $274 (~42% upside).** 22x–24x on forward EPS approaching $11.75–$12.20 as gross margin compounds and consensus shifts to GLP-1-as-tailwind; consistent with the analyst mean target of $270.6.

**Bear case target: $165 (~14% downside).** ~14.5x forward EPS if GLP-1 erosion and reimbursement cuts materialize; the net-cash balance sheet and FCF margin limit further downside.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months.** Catalysts: quarterly prints (next ~early August 2026, FY26 Q4) that continue to show margin expansion and intact resupply growth; accumulating GLP-1 real-world data; competitive bidding reinstatement clarity; and sell-side re-rating following JPMorgan's Overweight initiation.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of US device revenue declining YoY, which would validate the GLP-1 erosion thesis on actual volumes rather than fear.
2. Gross margin reversing below ~58% (giving back the structural gains that underpin the EPS growth case).
3. Current-year EPS consensus revised down more than 8% from the current $11.08, signaling the resilient-estimate assumption is breaking.

## Conclusion

ResMed is a high-quality, net-cash, 60%+ gross margin medical device leader whose operating results have accelerated while its stock has fallen 34% on a GLP-1 disruption fear that 2026 evidence is increasingly contradicting. At ~17x forward non-GAAP earnings, a discount to a lower-quality peer set trading at a 26.8x median, the market is pricing a structural decline that the company's reported metrics (record 62.2% gross margin, +21% non-GAAP EPS, +14% US mask resupply) do not support. Management is buying back stock aggressively into the weakness, the dividend was raised, and JPMorgan has shifted the sell-side tone to Overweight.

The recommendation is **BUY**. The specific reason this clears the bar over a HOLD: the dominant bear thesis (GLP-1 shrinks the OSA pool) is the cause of the entire de-rating, and that thesis is empirically weakening while the price still fully embeds it, producing roughly 18–30% upside to base/bull against ~12–17% bear-case downside cushioned by a fortress balance sheet. The overhang is real and unresolved, which is why this is a BUY at the lower end of conviction rather than a high-conviction call, and why the risk and growth scores are deliberately tempered. A sector-best business at a sector-discount multiple, with the central bear narrative inverting, is an asymmetric entry.
