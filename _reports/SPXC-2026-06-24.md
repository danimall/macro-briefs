## Investment Research Report: SPX Technologies, Inc. (SPXC)

## Executive Summary

SPX Technologies has transformed itself over the past decade from a sprawling legacy conglomerate burdened with asbestos and power-generation liabilities into a focused two-segment industrial platform built on disciplined niche M&A. Revenue has grown from $1,461M (2022) to $2,265M (2025), a 16% three-year CAGR split roughly evenly between organic growth and acquisitions. Operating margins have expanded materially (segment HVAC margin 20.9%→24.5% from 2023, D&M 19.2%→23.6%), and free cash flow reached $241M in 2025 at a clean 10.6% FCF margin with operating cash flow consistently exceeding net income. This is a high-quality industrial business with a real, filing-corroborated tailwind: data-center and power-generation cooling demand, evidenced by a 34% HVAC backlog jump, customer down-payments on 2026-2027 projects, and a deliberate doubling of 2026 capex to $135-165M to add cooling/air-movement capacity.

The trajectory in this name has been positive: the composite score moved from 6.0 (April) to 6.9 (May), with management quality, financial health, and risk factors all improving as the data-center thesis hardened and the balance sheet was de-levered via the August 2025 equity raise. The current data confirms the operating story but introduces two offsetting facts that cap the case. First, near-term estimate revisions have turned modestly negative (current-quarter EPS -4.1% over 90 days, current-quarter -0.6% over 30 days), reflecting the new Section 232 metals-tariff overhang on a steel/aluminum/copper-intensive manufacturer. Second, the stock trades at ~30x forward EPS ($8.02 current-year consensus) near its 52-week high of $247.73, having appreciated 49% over the trailing year.

The result is a HOLD with a growth bias. The business deserves to compound and the demand backdrop is durable, but the entry price already reflects most of the visible upside. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$200s, where the forward multiple compresses into the mid-20s against still-rising out-year estimates (next-year consensus $9.06, revised +3.2% over 90 days).

## Company Overview

SPX Technologies (Charlotte, NC; ~5,300 employees across 16+ countries) operates two reportable segments serving infrastructure end markets:

- **HVAC (67% of 2025 revenue, $1,518.2M):** Package and process cooling, engineered air movement and handling for industrial markets (notably data centers and power generation), institutional and commercial markets, plus hydronic and electric heating/ventilation. Core brands include Marley, Recold, Cincinnati Fan, TAMCO, Ingénia, Weil-McLain, Qmark, and INDEECO. Segment margin 24.5%.
- **Detection & Measurement (33%, $746.9M):** Underground pipe/cable locators, pipeline inspection/rehabilitation, robotic systems, transportation fare systems, communication technologies (including defense), and aids to navigation. Brands include Radiodetection, Pearpoint, CUES, ULC Robotics, Genfare, and Sealite. Segment margin 23.6%.

The strategy is consistent: shed legacy liabilities (asbestos portfolio sold 2022, wind-downs of Heat Transfer and the South Africa DBT power business) and acquire sub-scale niche leaders in fragmented markets that can be lifted into a higher-margin platform. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and roughly 80% of revenue is U.S.-based. The M&A cadence is high: KTS (Jan 2025, $340M, defense/D&M), Sigma & Omega (Apr 2025, $143M, HVAC hydronics), Thermolec (Jan 2026, electric duct heating), and Crawford United (Feb 2026, ~$215M, engineered air handling, with non-core aerospace/defense/marine units carved out for sale).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability are both moving in the right direction.** Revenue rose 14.2% in 2025 (organic +6.3%, acquisitions +7.8%, FX +0.1%), a mix where roughly half the growth is organic, higher quality than a pure roll-up. Gross margin expanded to 40.5% (from 38.5% in 2023), operating income climbed to $350.4M (from $221.9M in 2023), and net income reached $244.0M at a 10.8% net margin. The margin expansion reflects operating leverage, favorable mix toward accretive cooling and data-center work, and continuous-improvement initiatives, not purely a cyclical updraft.

**Cash flow quality is strong.** Operating cash flow was $335.6M in 2025 against $244M net income, indicating clean earnings. FCF funded the M&A engine and balance-sheet de-leveraging (the company pays no dividend). The 2026 capex step-up (from $92.1M) is demand-driven capacity expansion for cooling and air movement, a forward signal rather than maintenance spend.

**The balance sheet is conservative for a serial acquirer.** Total debt was cut to $501.6M at YE2025 (from $614.7M), aided by the August 2025 equity offering (3.059M shares at $188, $551.1M net) used to repay revolver borrowings after the KTS acquisition. Cash stood at $364M, current ratio 2.48x, stockholders' equity $2,237.5M. The term loan and $1.5B revolver mature in September 2030 with ~$1,489.5M available. A subsequent $215M was borrowed in February 2026 to fund Crawford. Headline screening metrics misrepresent this profile: the Altman Z of ~2.1 ("grey zone") and an apparent operating-margin flag are artifacts of an intangible-heavy balance sheet (goodwill $1,043M + intangibles $868M = ~53% of assets) and mis-tagged operating income. Actual financial leverage is roughly 0.22x net debt/equity, indicating a financially healthy company.

**Two non-operating items flattered 2025 and should be normalized.** A $23.0M Filtran equity-stake gain (including $18.5M in Q4) following the Parker-Hannifin/Donaldson acquisitions of Filtran's underlying businesses, and a ~$15M cash-tax reduction from the July 2025 tax legislation. Run-rate earnings should be adjusted for these.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on a structural demand tailwind plus a proven acquisition machine. The data-center cooling narrative has hard evidence behind it: HVAC backlog rose from $436.8M to $584.5M (+34% YoY), D&M backlog from $220.9M to $350.3M, and management cited customer down-payments on large data-center projects scheduled to execute in 2026-2027 as a 2025 operating-cash-flow driver. The capex doubling is explicitly to serve this demand. This aligns with multi-source confirmation of data-center load growth visible across the power and electrical-equipment supply chain: hyperscaler capex, utility commercial KWh surges, and electrical-equipment orders all point in the same direction, which raises the durability of the demand thesis above a single-company story.

The earnings track record supports forward confidence: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, with Q1 2026 beating by 5.9% ($1.69 vs $1.60). The estimate-revision picture, however, is mixed. Out-year estimates are rising (next-quarter +2.9%, current-year +2.5%, next-year +3.2% over 90 days), confirming the structural story is being recognized. But near-term current-quarter estimates have fallen 4.1% over 90 days, reflecting the Section 232 tariff drag on a metals-intensive cost base. This is not the +30% estimate-revision velocity that signals a buy-the-inflection setup; it is a quality compounder with a modest near-term margin headwind. Growth prospects are good, supported by both the secular tailwind and the M&A optionality, but the revision data does not justify maximum conviction.

## Valuation Assessment

SPXC trades at ~30x current-year consensus EPS and ~26x next-year $9.06, at $236 near the 52-week high. On the surface this looks roughly in line with the peer median P/E of 29.4 (Pentair 18.2, Crane 37.4, Graco 24.2, Stanley Black & Decker 34.1, Valmont 29.4), but the peer set is heterogeneous and SPXC sits at the upper end relative to its growth rate. Mid-teens revenue growth and high-single-digit organic growth justify a premium to a low-growth industrial, but not a meaningful premium to the cohort median.

The case against a high valuation score is straightforward. The forward multiple already capitalizes the data-center tailwind that is well known and broadly reported (Zacks "data center cooling play" framing). The trailing-period earnings are flattered by ~$38M of non-recurring gains/tax benefits, so normalized run-rate earnings power is somewhat lower than headline. Near-term estimates are drifting down, not up. And the stock sits at the top of its 52-week range, which removes any absolute-yield or sentiment-extreme support for the bull case. Analyst targets (low $225, mean $266, high $310) bracket the current price with the low target essentially at spot; the consensus does not treat this as a clearly cheap stock.

We would not penalize SPXC for prior appreciation; the run has been earned by fundamental improvement. The question for the valuation score is price versus intrinsic value today, and at ~30x forward with decelerating near-term estimates and a live margin risk, there is no specific reason to believe the market is mispricing the stock to the upside. A genuinely attractive entry would be the low-$200s, where the multiple compresses into the mid-20s on rising out-year estimates. Valuation is the binding constraint on the rating.

## Competitive Landscape

SPX competes in fragmented niche markets where, per its own 10-K, reliable comparative figures are not available for most competitors; competition is local and product-specific (Marley/Recold in cooling towers, Radiodetection in locators, Genfare in transit fare collection). This fragmentation is the strategic opportunity: SPX consolidates sub-scale niche leaders into a higher-margin platform, and its margin trajectory (gross margin +200bps over two years, both segments expanding) indicates the consolidation is working.

The competitive position is improving on trajectory. HVAC organic growth of +6.1% in 2025 against rising backlog suggests share gains and pricing power in cooling, where data-center demand is concentrating. The defense-adjacent KTS acquisition deepens the D&M moat in digital interoperability. The principal risk to the competitive narrative is concentration of the bull thesis in data-center demand, which management explicitly names as a swing factor: a cyclical end market that cuts both ways. On balance, a company gaining share in fragmented niches with structural tailwinds and self-help margin expansion earns an above-average competitive score, tempered by the cyclicality of its fastest-growing end market.

## Risk Assessment

**Section 232 tariffs are the key near-term watch item.** The legal landscape flipped twice between the 10-K and the Q1 10-Q: the Supreme Court invalidated the 2025 IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026 (creating potential refund eligibility of uncertain amount and timing), and the government immediately pivoted to Section 232 metals tariffs on April 2, 2026. Section 232 is levied on the full customs value of any good containing covered metals (steel, aluminum, copper), a structurally harsher base for a metals-intensive manufacturer. SPX says it "substantially mitigated" prior tariffs via pricing but "may be unable to do so" under the new regime. This is a genuine, unresolved margin risk and the most likely driver of the soft near-term estimate revisions.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment is the structural risk of the roll-up model.** Intangibles total ~$1.9B (53% of assets). Management explicitly flags that KTS ($104.4M goodwill) and Sigma & Omega ($77.4M) approximate carrying value: a 10% fair-value decline would trigger ~$28.4M and ~$7.1M impairments respectively, with ASPEQ trademarks ($51.5M) similarly sensitive. These are recently acquired assets vulnerable if forecasts slip.

**Other risks:** percentage-of-completion fixed-price contracts in D&M (~$239M recognized over-time in 2025, exposed to cost overruns); U.S. government contracting exposure (elevated post-KTS, subject to appropriations and shutdowns); the Crawford aerospace/defense/marine divestiture, which must be sold within ~12 months or risks distraction; and legacy contingent liabilities (environmental reserves $43.7M, pension net liability $92.8M). None of these is a binary, near-term catalyst, but the impairment sensitivity and tariff uncertainty are specific enough to keep the risk score below average.

### Options Market Signal

The options chain is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 46.1% sits rich to 36.8% one-year historical volatility (a 9.3% spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 46.1% > long-term 41.6%), indicating the market expects elevated near-dated volatility, consistent with the tariff overhang and the next earnings print. The put/call volume ratio of 2.24 is elevated, though the put/call OI ratio of 0.38 is benign, suggesting recent hedging flow rather than a durable bearish open-interest stance. The implied ±9.1% move on the nearest expiry is meaningful for a sizing decision. Net read: the market is pricing a near-term catalyst with two-sided risk, which reinforces the case for patience on entry rather than chasing the high.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$300-310):** Data-center cooling demand proves durable and accelerates, HVAC backlog continues compounding, and the capex expansion converts to revenue at maintained or expanding margins. The M&A engine keeps adding accretive niches (Crawford, Thermolec) while the Crawford non-core units are divested cleanly. Section 232 tariff costs are recovered through pricing as they were under the prior regime, and the IEEPA refund provides a modest tailwind. Out-year EPS reaches the high-$9s to low-$10s, and the stock holds a ~30x premium multiple, supporting the $310 analyst high target.

**Bear case (~$185-195, roughly -18% to -22%):** Section 232 tariffs assessed on full customs value compress margins faster than pricing can recover, near-term estimates continue falling, and one or more recently acquired platforms (KTS most exposed) underperforms its acquisition model, triggering an impairment. Data-center order timing slips from 2026 into 2027 (a named risk), backlog conversion disappoints, and the premium multiple compresses toward the low-20s on $8 normalized EPS.

**Base case (~$255):** The data-center tailwind sustains mid-single-digit-plus organic HVAC growth, M&A continues at a disciplined pace, and margins hold near current levels. Tariff costs create a modest near-term drag that pricing largely offsets over 2-3 quarters. EPS reaches ~$9 next year, and the stock holds a ~28x multiple, roughly the analyst mean of $266, giving modest upside from current levels but not enough asymmetry to justify chasing at the high.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias).** The business is a quality compounder with a real tailwind, but the current price near the 52-week high at ~30x forward leaves insufficient asymmetry to upgrade.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$200s, compressing the forward multiple into the mid-20s on rising out-year estimates (next-year $9.06 and climbing).
- Evidence that Section 232 tariff costs are being recovered through pricing without margin erosion (HVAC segment margin holding ≥24% through 2026), combined with a resumption of positive current-quarter estimate revisions.
- A confirmed acceleration in data-center backlog conversion (HVAC backlog continuing to grow >20% YoY) with the 2026 capex translating to organic growth above the current ~6%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material impairment of KTS, Sigma & Omega, or ASPEQ intangibles, signaling the acquisition model is breaking down.
- HVAC segment margin compression below 22% driven by unrecovered tariff costs.
- Data-center order timing slips materially (down-payments not converting to 2026 revenue as guided) alongside continued downward estimate revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (late July, the implied-move catalyst the options market is pricing) is the natural reassessment point, with the Crawford divestiture timeline (~12 months) and the August 2026 D&M leadership handoff as secondary monitoring items.

## Conclusion

SPX Technologies is a well-executed industrial roll-up with expanding margins, clean cash flow, a conservative balance sheet, and a structural data-center cooling tailwind that is corroborated by hard backlog and capex evidence rather than narrative alone. Management has delivered strong shareholder alignment (5-year TSR indexed to 367 versus a peer 190), the August 2025 equity raise sensibly de-levered after KTS, and the orderly D&M succession signals bench depth rather than instability. The score trajectory has been rising for good fundamental reasons.

The constraint is price. At ~30x forward EPS near the 52-week high, with near-term estimates drifting down on a live Section 232 metals-tariff risk and the options market pricing imminent stress, the current entry already discounts most of the visible upside. The out-year estimate revisions remain positive and the demand story is durable, which keeps this a growth hold rather than a stock to fade. We rate SPXC a HOLD and would upgrade to BUY on a pullback toward the low-$200s or clear evidence that tariff costs are being absorbed without margin erosion.
