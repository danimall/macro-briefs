**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Jack Henry & Associates is a debt-free, high-margin core-banking and payments software provider to roughly 7,400 U.S. community and regional banks and credit unions. The business compounds revenue at ~7–8% organically with operating margins near 24%, free-cash-flow margins near 25%, and a 21% return on equity, all on a balance sheet that ended FY2025 with zero debt and an Altman Z-score in the safe zone. FY2026 year-to-date results have accelerated: nine-month revenue is up 8.0% to $1.90B, operating income up 20.6%, and EPS up 20.4% to $5.41, with remaining performance obligations of $8.0B providing forward visibility.

The story is a valuation-fundamentals disconnect. The stock has fallen ~30% over the past twelve months, from a ~$193 high to $123.26, and now trades at ~17.6x trailing and ~17.6x forward earnings, a discount to the ~19.7x peer median for transaction and payment processors despite Jack Henry's superior balance sheet, lower beta, and more consistent earnings stream. The proxy's pay-versus-performance disclosure quantifies the problem: five-year cumulative TSR of 104 versus a peer group at 216, meaning the stock has materially lagged its sector even as the underlying business compounded. Management is responding with a coordinated "the stock is cheap" posture: the CEO and CFO bought stock in the open market in May 2026 at $133–134, the board expanded buyback authorization by 5M shares in June 2026, and FY26 repurchases are running roughly 8x the prior year's pace.

We rate JKHY a BUY. At ~17.6x forward earnings, the market is pricing in growth deceleration and structural disruption that the current results contradict. The base case sees the stock re-rate toward its historical multiple as continued mid-to-high-single-digit organic growth and the up-market traction (the Woodforest win is the largest core signing in company history) reassure the market. The principal risks, renewal price compression and cloud-native/AI disruption of the core franchise, are real and worth monitoring, but neither is visible in trailing or forward data, and the de-rating now provides a margin of safety that did not exist at $185.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1976 and based in Monett, Missouri, Jack Henry provides core processing, payments, and complementary software to U.S. community and regional financial institutions. The client base spans roughly 1,670 core clients (over 950 banks on SilverLake, CIF 20/20, and Core Director platforms; ~715 credit unions on Symitar) plus ~5,710 non-core clients that buy standalone complementary and payment products.

The company reports four segments under its FY26 structure: Core (~30% of revenue), Payments (~37%), Complementary (~30%), and a small/negative-margin Corporate Services infrastructure bucket. Revenue splits into two streams: Services & Support (~57%, including multi-year private and public cloud hosting contracts) and Processing (~43%, card, remittance, and digital transaction fees). The business is built on six-year contract terms and high retention.

The strategic centerpiece is the Jack Henry Platform, a public-cloud-native, API-first, componentized "next-generation core" designed to let clients adopt modern services incrementally rather than through a single rip-and-replace migration. This is the company's primary competitive response to cloud-native challengers and the major incumbents (Fiserv, FIS, Finastra) racing to modernize their own cores. Jack Henry grew its net core footprints in both banks and credit unions in CY2024 despite secular industry consolidation, evidence it is taking share within a shrinking pool of institutions.

## Financial Analysis

Jack Henry's financials describe a consistent high-quality compounder. Revenue grew from $1,943M in FY2022 to $2,375M in FY2025, with operating income rising from $475M to $569M and net income from $363M to $456M over the same span. Diluted EPS reached $6.24 in FY2025, up 19% from $5.23, with part of that jump reflecting the absence of a one-time ~$16.4M voluntary-separation charge that burdened FY2024.

Margins are stable to expanding: operating margin ~24%, net margin ~19%, FCF margin ~25%. Operating cash flow was $642M in FY2025. The balance sheet is pristine: equity at 70% of assets, debt-to-equity of 0.43, zero debt at FY2025 year-end, ROE of 21.4%, and ROA of 15%. The Altman Z-score of 3.83 sits comfortably in the safe zone.

Q3 alone delivered revenue +8.7% and EPS of $1.71. The earnings beat record is strong and consistent: four consecutive beats, with surprises of 8.7%, 12.9%, 17.8%, and 14.9% over the last four quarters. Of the $8.0B backlog, ~23% is recognizable in the next twelve months.

One balance-sheet item warrants explanation rather than alarm. Cash fell to $20.6M from $102M and the company drew $90M on its revolver during FY26 YTD. This reflects discretionary capital allocation, $284M of buybacks (roughly 8x the prior year's pace) plus a $42M acquisition, not operational stress. Operating cash flow was strong at $459M, up 46% year-over-year, and the company secured a new $1.0B five-year revolver in March 2026 (replacing a $600M facility) for added flexibility. True reinvestment intensity runs higher than headline capex suggests: capitalized software of ~$172M per year exceeds the ~$53M of property capex, reflecting heavy investment in the modernization platform.

The OBBBA tax law (signed July 2025) restores immediate R&D expensing and 100% bonus depreciation, which management expects to meaningfully reduce near-term cash taxes, with limited P&L rate impact. An IRS audit of the FY2023 federal return opened in Q1 FY26 is routine, with $25.6M of gross unrecognized tax benefits reserved.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is almost entirely organic and broad-based. FY25 revenue grew ~7% reported (6.5% excluding deconversion noise), with Complementary the fastest-growing segment at +9.2%. The drivers are durable: cloud hosting migration (the largest single growth contributor cited), card and digital transaction volume, and faster-payments adoption. FY26 YTD growth sits at the upper end of the company's historical range.

Estimate revisions support the forward picture. Current-year consensus EPS has risen +2.1% over 90 days to $6.57, and next-year estimates are up +2.0% over 90 days to $7.10. The current-quarter estimate slipped -5.3% over 90 days to $1.44 (a near-term timing/seasonality artifact), but the full-year and out-year trajectories are positive. These are not the +30% revisions that signal a trough recovery; rather, they confirm steady, low-volatility compounding consistent with the business model. We weight growth as the most reliable forward dimension, and here it is solidly positive but not accelerating sharply, hence a 6.5 rather than a higher score.

The structural backdrop is manageable. The number of U.S. financial institutions is shrinking ~3%/year via M&A (FDIC bank count down ~13% from 2019–24, credit unions down ~15%), but aggregate assets are growing (banks +5% CAGR, CUs +8% CAGR). Jack Henry offsets the count decline through three levers: winning net new logos, moving up-market to larger institutions, and cross-selling more complementary and payments products per client. The Woodforest National Bank win (>$9B in assets, 740+ branches) is concrete evidence the up-market strategy is landing bigger institutions than its historical sub-$10B sweet spot. The September 2025 acquisition of Victor Technologies ($42M), a cloud-native embedded-payments provider, deepens the Payments-as-a-Service offering.

## Valuation Assessment

At $123.26, JKHY trades at ~17.6x trailing EPS of $6.24 and ~17.4x the $7.10 next-year consensus. Against the peer median P/E of 19.7x for the transaction and payment processing group, Jack Henry trades at a discount despite carrying zero debt, the lowest earnings volatility in the group, and a four-quarter beat streak. Peer dispersion is wide: EEFT at 9.7x and WEX at 15.3x on the low end, Shift4 at 44.0x and Sezzle at 30.8x on the high end, which reflects very different growth and risk profiles. Adjusting for quality and consistency, Jack Henry's discount to the median is difficult to justify on fundamentals alone.

This is the rare case where a compressed multiple does not appear to reflect structural impairment. The de-rating occurred while fundamentals improved. The drawdown magnitude requires a specific explanation of what sellers see, and the most credible candidates are: (a) sector-wide multiple compression in richly-valued payments names, and (b) fear that cloud-native and AI-driven disruption will erode the core franchise over the next 3–5 years. We take the disruption risk seriously but note it is not visible in any trailing or forward metric, and the modernization platform plus the Woodforest win argue Jack Henry is competing, not losing.

We treat cheapness with appropriate skepticism: a low multiple is a value-trap warning absent a catalyst. Here the catalysts are present and identifiable: continued mid-to-high-single-digit organic growth that contradicts the deceleration narrative, the aggressive buyback ramp, CEO and CFO open-market buying, and the up-market signing momentum. Analyst targets (low $161, mean $187, median $188, high $208) sit 31–69% above the current price. With the stock at ~17.6x forward earnings and growth durable, these targets behave as floors rather than ceilings, since consensus targets lag when growth holds. A re-rating to ~20x the $7.10 next-year estimate implies ~$142, and to ~22x implies ~$156.

## Competitive Landscape

Jack Henry operates in a consolidated oligopoly for U.S. bank and credit-union core processing, competing primarily against Fiserv, FIS, and Finastra in banking and against Corelation (an emerging modern challenger) in credit unions. Symitar is the most-used credit-union core for institutions above $25M in assets. The company's differentiation rests on service quality, a recurring theme management cites as its single most important moat, and its focus on the community/regional tier (institutions up to ~$55B in assets, systems tested to $150B).

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than eroding. Net core footprints grew in both banks and credit unions during industry consolidation, the Woodforest win demonstrates the ability to land institutions well above the historical sweet spot, and complementary/payments cross-sell continues to expand the highest-margin revenue path. Switching costs are extreme, since core conversions are multi-year, mission-critical projects, and six-year contracts plus high retention reinforce the moat.

The wildcard is cloud-native and AI disruption. Nimble entrants and the major incumbents are all racing to modernize cores. Jack Henry's bet on incremental, API-first componentization via the Jack Henry Platform rather than a forced re-platform is differentiated but unproven at scale. The company has committed real capital to it (a December 2025 addendum added $450M of cloud purchase obligations through 2033), and the FY25 10-K added a substantial new AI/ML/GenAI risk factor reflecting both the opportunity and the threat. The trajectory of this platform over the next 3–5 years is the key long-term swing factor, but the evidence to date, net share gains, up-market wins, and accelerating growth, points to a company holding or improving its position.

## Risk Assessment

**Contract renewal price compression** is the most company-specific revenue risk. Jack Henry explicitly flags that a meaningful number of six-year contracts renew annually and that certain renewals have produced price compression. Because client contracts run longer than vendor contracts, the company's ability to pass through inflation is structurally limited. Margins are currently expanding, so the risk is latent, but it is the single metric most worth monitoring.

**Client consolidation** is a persistent headwind: each M&A deal where a Jack Henry client is acquired by a non-client risks deconversion. The company has offset this through net footprint gains and rising assets-per-client, but the FI count continues to shrink ~3%/year.

**Technology disruption and modernization execution** carry both revenue and balance-sheet risk. The ~$172M/year of capitalized software carries amortization and impairment exposure if the Jack Henry Platform underdelivers. The newly elevated AI risk factor, covering regulatory uncertainty, model bias, explainability, and unauthorized employee use, reflects a genuinely heightened disclosure posture.

**Concentration and cybersecurity:** Jack Henry discloses heavy reliance on a single strategic card-processing vendor (agreement amended May 2025, extending to January 2038 with ~$1.0B remaining commitment), a concentrated off-balance-sheet obligation. As a critical processor for thousands of regulated institutions, a breach or settlement failure would be reputationally and financially severe. The company is also examined by federal banking regulators and the CFPB as a bank service provider despite not being chartered itself.

Most of these are well-understood, slow-moving structural risks rather than binary catalysts that the market is not pricing. The five-year TSR concern is a price phenomenon, not a fundamental one, and at this multiple it argues for mean reversion rather than continued underperformance. We score risk a 6.5: real structural risks, none acutely binary, with the recent de-rating having absorbed much of the disruption fear.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is constructive-to-neutral. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.8% sits modestly rich to 25.4% one-year historical vol but at only the 21st percentile of the current chain. The 1-month put/call skew is balanced at 2.2%, and put/call volume ratio is a benign 0.65. The term structure is in normal contango (near-term 31.8% < long-term 36.1%), indicating no near-term event panic. The put/call OI ratio fell from 1.74 to 1.52 as the price dropped 9.5% since late May, suggesting some bearish positioning has unwound rather than intensified. The implied ±6.5% near-term move is unremarkable for a name with an earnings catalyst on the horizon. No options-market signal contradicts the fundamental BUY thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Jack Henry is a 50-year-old, zero-debt, 25%-FCF-margin compounder growing ~8% organically with $8.0B of contracted backlog, trading at a discount to a peer median that includes lower-quality, more cyclical names. The de-rating has overshot. Continued mid-to-high-single-digit growth, accelerating up-market traction (Woodforest validates the large-client strategy), aggressive buybacks at the lows, and insider buying combine to re-rate the multiple toward its historical range. A move to 22x the $7.10 next-year estimate implies ~$156; analyst targets cluster at $187–188 mean/median, implying ~50% upside. Bull case: **$185** (~22x a normalized $8.40 forward EPS as growth and re-rating compound through FY27).

### Bear Case

The renewal price-compression dynamic intensifies, growth slips toward 5%, and cloud-native plus AI disruption begins to erode the core franchise; the multiple compresses further toward the low-teens as the market reprices a structurally lower terminal growth rate. The capitalized-software base faces impairment if the modernization platform underdelivers. Bear case: **$105** (~16x a flat ~$6.50 EPS with no re-rating), roughly -15% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $150** — ~21x the $7.10 next-year consensus EPS, a modest premium to today's multiple that reflects re-rating toward the peer median as durable ~8% growth reassures the market. This sits between the bear ($105) and bull ($185) scenarios. Upside to base case: ~+22%.

**Bull case target: $185** (~+50%).
**Bear case target: $105** (~-15%).

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months. Key catalysts are the FQ4 FY26 earnings report (August 2026, full-year delivery and FY27 guidance), continued buyback execution against the expanded authorization, and incremental large-client signings following Woodforest.

**Upside/downside from current ($123.26):** +22% to base, +50% to bull, -15% to bear, a favorable asymmetry of roughly 1.5:1 to base case and better to bull.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Organic revenue growth decelerates below 5% YoY for two consecutive quarters, confirming the deceleration the market fears.
2. Operating margin compresses materially (below ~22%) on renewal price compression, signaling the structural pricing risk has activated.
3. A capitalized-software impairment charge or a public acknowledgment that the Jack Henry Platform is losing competitive ground to cloud-native challengers.

## Conclusion

Jack Henry is a high-quality, debt-free software compounder whose stock has de-rated ~30% even as its fundamentals accelerated, and the company just landed the largest core win in its history. The market is pricing in growth deceleration and disruption that the current results contradict. Management's response, open-market insider buying and an 8x ramp in buybacks plus a fresh 5M-share authorization at the lows, is a coordinated signal of perceived undervaluation. The structural risks (renewal price compression, cloud-native/AI disruption) are genuine and warrant monitoring, but neither is visible in trailing or forward data, and the de-rating now provides the margin of safety that was absent at the highs.

We rate JKHY a **BUY** with a 12–18 month base-case target of $150 (~+22%), bull case $185, and bear case $105. The risk/reward is favorably asymmetric at this multiple for a business of this consistency. This continues a streak of BUY recommendations, but the case is stronger now than in prior reports: the same fundamentals at a lower price represent improving, not deteriorating, risk/reward, and the valuation discount to peers has widened while the business has not weakened.
