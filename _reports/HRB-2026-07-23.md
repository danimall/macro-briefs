**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

H&R Block is the largest branded tax-preparation company in the U.S., running a hybrid assisted-plus-DIY model across ~8,600 offices and generating roughly $600M of annual free cash flow on $3.76B of revenue. The business is asset-light and highly cash-generative (16% FCF margin, ~18.6% ROA, 26% EBITDA margin), and management returns essentially all of that cash to shareholders — $400M of buybacks plus ~$200M of dividends per year. At $39.55, the stock trades at ~7x trailing earnings and yields ~4.2%, roughly a 64% discount to the specialized-consumer-services peer median P/E of 19.5x.

The central problem is that this is a low-growth business whose entire top-line expansion is price-led. Assisted return volume was roughly flat through April 30, 2026, and DIY online paid volume declined 4.2% year-over-year — the company is losing units in the one channel (self-serve) that is structurally growing. Growth of 5.7% over the nine months of FY2026 came almost entirely from higher net average charges, and reported net income growth of 43% is flattered by an $84.1M one-time IRS-settlement tax benefit that pushed the effective rate to an unsustainable 8.1%. Normalized nine-month EPS growth is closer to 20%, and analyst estimates for the current and next quarter have drifted lower over the past 90 days.

There is a genuine offsetting positive that the market has partly discounted: the IRS terminated its free Direct File program for the 2026 season, removing the single largest structural threat that dominates H&R Block's risk factors — the prospect of the government becoming a free direct competitor. The stock has fallen ~30% from its 52-week high of $56.76 to below $40, so much of the bad news is priced. But with volumes soft, a largely new leadership team executing an unproven digital turnaround, and no near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple, this is a hold for the covered yield and capital-return floor rather than a call on appreciation. We rate HRB HOLD.

## Company Overview

H&R Block operates 6,802 company-owned and 1,814 franchise offices as of March 31, 2026, prepares roughly 11.3M U.S. assisted returns and 3.8M paid DIY online returns annually, and files 22.8M returns globally across the U.S., Canada, and Australia. The company reports as a single segment, with fiscal year ending June 30.

Revenue is concentrated in U.S. tax prep, which represents about 88% of the total. The FY2025 revenue stack: U.S. assisted tax prep $2.41B (64%), the core engine driven by pricing; U.S. DIY tax prep $384M, growing on price but shrinking on volume; royalties $193M, declining as HRB buys back franchises; financial services (Emerald Card, Spruce, Emerald Advance) $102M and declining; international $247M; and Wave small-business SaaS $109M, the fastest grower at +13%.

Competitively, H&R Block is #1 or #2 in assisted preparation against independents and CPAs, and a clear #2 in DIY behind Intuit's TurboTax. The moat is brand, physical footprint, and the human-plus-digital hybrid. New CEO Curtis Campbell, formerly CEO of TaxAct and a former Intuit product executive, was installed at the end of 2025 as a deliberate bet on improving digital/DIY competitiveness.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue grew 4.2% in FY2025 ($3.61B to $3.76B) and 5.7% over the nine months of FY2026 ($2.65B to $2.80B). FY2025 net income was $605.8M (16.1% net margin), EBITDA $976M (26% margin), with FCF of $599M. These are strong, durable margins for a services business, and cash conversion is high. Growth is overwhelmingly price-led, with assisted net average charge up 4.0% while total assisted volume was roughly flat (+0.6% for the nine months, flat through April 30).

**The tax-benefit distortion.** Nine-month FY2026 net income jumped 43% year-over-year, inflated by the $84.1M discrete tax benefit from settling an IRS exam of the 2020 return, which drove the effective rate down to 8.1%. On an adjusted basis, nine-month EPS rose roughly 22% and Q3 adjusted EPS about 11.9%. Any assessment of underlying earnings power must normalize out the tax benefit. Trailing reported EPS of $4.42 similarly benefits from this item; the FY2026 consensus of $5.16 and FY2027 of $5.82 are the more relevant anchors.

**The balance sheet "distress" signal is a red herring.** The Altman Z-Score of 1.19 and debt/equity of 35.7x are artifacts of years of aggressive buybacks that have driven GAAP equity to near-zero and negative (–$24.4M at March 31, 2026). Management openly warns it "is likely to continue to have a negative net worth under GAAP at various times." Roughly $1.49B of senior notes against ~$976M of EBITDA is about 1.5x gross leverage; the company is investment-grade (Moody's Baa3 / S&P BBB, both stable); the $1.5B revolver (extended to July 2030) had zero drawn at quarter-end; and the covenant of debt/EBITDA ≤ 3.5x sits comfortably against a 4.5x seasonal peak at the December quarter-end with ample cushion at year-end. Seasonality is extreme: substantially all revenue occurs February through April, so stockholders' equity swung from +$89M in July to –$823M in December to –$24M in March. GAAP equity metrics are structurally meaningless for this business.

**Capital allocation.** The entire equity story is capital return. FY2025 saw $400M of buybacks (average $61.10) plus $197M in dividends; the first nine months of FY2026 added another $400M in buybacks (average $50.90) plus a dividend raised 12% to $0.42/quarter. $700M remains on the $1.5B authorization. The buyback trajectory is a legitimate critique: management has repurchased at $61, then $51, and the stock is now ~$40 — value has been destroyed on those higher-priced repurchases even as the share count fell from 146M in FY2023 to ~127M. The dividend is well-covered (~$0.60B FCF against ~$0.20B in dividends), which anchors the income case.

## Growth Analysis

The growth outlook is the weakest part of the story. Low-single-digit top-line growth is entirely price-led, and the price lever is being pulled against a flat-to-declining volume base. The most important negative trend is DIY online paid volume falling 2.7% for the nine months and 4.2% through April 30 — losing units in the structurally growing self-serve channel, and doing so under a management team specifically hired to fix DIY. This matters because the long-run industry migration runs from assisted (higher price) toward DIY (lower price), and now generative-AI tax tools threaten to further commoditize DIY on both price and volume.

Estimate revisions confirm decelerating momentum: current-quarter consensus is down 4.1% and next-quarter down 10.2% over 90 days, with current-quarter flat over 30 days. The full-year figures are modestly positive (current year +3.2%, next year +4.4% over 90 days), but the near-term trend is negative. This does not meet the threshold for a positive earnings-inflection signal — revisions are broadly falling, not accelerating upward, and revenue growth is mid-single-digit, not the 30%+ pace that would flag a structural inflection.

The offsetting positives are real but modest. Wave small-business SaaS grew ~13% and international ~8%, diversifying away from the mature U.S. core, but off a small base. Franchise buy-ins ($55M in nine months) convert royalties into company-owned revenue, a deliberate consolidation that flatters reported growth. Assisted pricing power remains intact: 4% net average charge on flat volume implies durable brand value. But absent a stabilization in DIY units, this remains a business that grows revenue in the low single digits and earnings in the mid-to-high single digits through pricing and buybacks, not one with a compelling organic-growth path.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.55, HRB trades at ~7x trailing and forward earnings, a ~64% discount to the specialized-consumer-services peer median P/E of 19.5x (FTDR 19.2x, ADT 9.3x, MCW 21.5x, SCI 19.5x). On the FY2027 consensus of $5.82, the multiple is ~6.8x. The dividend yields ~4.2%, above the peer median of 2.0%.

The instinct on a screen this cheap is to call it a buy, but a low multiple on a business losing units in its growth channel is the classic value-trap configuration. Cheapness here reflects a market that is skeptical of the durability of price-led growth and worried about DIY erosion and AI disruption, not a mispricing to be arbitraged. The multiple has compressed for reasons, and there is no identified catalyst to re-rate it toward peers. Analyst price targets bracket the current price tightly (mean and median both $39.50, low $29, high $50), which tells us the sell side sees limited asymmetry; the targets are not lagging a rising-estimate inflection, so treating them as a floor would be misplaced.

What supports the valuation is absolute yield and cash-flow durability, not a growth re-rating. At ~7x earnings with ~$600M of FCF (roughly a 12% FCF yield on the ~$5.0B market cap) and a well-covered 4.2% dividend, downside is cushioned. The Direct File removal reduces the tail risk that would justify a permanently depressed multiple. The base case is that HRB stays cheap because it is a mature, low-growth, price-dependent business; the multiple is fair, not a bargain, until volume trends turn. We score valuation modestly above neutral: the yield and FCF support keep it from being a value trap, but there is no clear mispricing thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

The tax-prep industry is a stable duopoly-plus at the top (H&R Block and Intuit/TurboTax) fragmented among independents and CPAs below. Three secular forces are in tension. The negative: long-run migration from assisted to DIY at lower price points, and now AI tools (including H&R Block's own free "AI Tax Assist") that could commoditize DIY. HRB's declining DIY paid volume suggests it is losing this battle on units, which is the core competitive concern. The positive: the termination of the government Direct File program removes a structural threat, and assisted pricing power remains intact with net average charge up 4% on flat volume, evidence of durable brand and pricing.

The competitive trajectory is the relevant question, and it is deteriorating in the channel that matters most for the long term. HRB is defending its high-value assisted franchise successfully on price, but ceding self-serve share while installing a former TaxAct CEO to reverse it, an unproven turnaround. Level of market position is strong (clear #2 in DIY, #1/#2 in assisted), but trajectory in the growth channel is negative, which caps the competitive-position score below neutral.

## Risk Assessment

**Structural/competitive.** DIY volume erosion amid AI-driven commoditization is the primary idiosyncratic risk. If self-serve share losses accelerate and pricing power in assisted eventually caps out, the price-led growth model stalls.

**Government free-file — materially de-risked.** The Direct File termination neutralizes the 10-K's headline strategic risk. This reprieve is politically reversible after 2028 (restoration bills from Warren and King are pending), so it is a cycle-specific tailwind rather than a permanent structural change. The Free File industry consortium runs to October 2029.

**Vendor concentration.** Heavy single-vendor dependence on Pathward N.A. (all Emerald/Refund products; agreement extended only to June 30, 2027), Microsoft Azure (all cloud), and FIS/Galileo (card processing). Loss of any would be disruptive; the Pathward renewal in 2027 is a specific watch item.

**Regulatory/legal.** Ongoing government inquiries, class actions, and mass arbitrations related to Free File and the use of tracking "pixels" in DIY services (third-party data sharing). Legal fees rose 32.5% in FY2025.

**Seasonality and tax-law risk.** A single ~10-week revenue window means any IRS processing disruption (relevant given administration resource cuts), filing-deadline change, or delayed product launch has outsized impact.

**Leadership transition.** CEO, CFO, CAO, and CSOO have all turned over within ~18 months as a new growth strategy launches — broad execution risk.

**Options Market Signal.** Options positioning signals near-term stress. ATM IV of 51.1% is rich versus 40.6% historical volatility (a 10.5pp spread), sitting at the 82nd percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 51.1% > long-term 40.5%), pricing an imminent event consistent with the FY2026 year-end/Q4 print. The put/call volume ratio of 3.22 shows heavy near-term put activity, though the put/call OI ratio of 0.57 and roughly balanced skew (–2.2% near term) temper the read; the OI ratio fell from 0.85 to 0.57 since early June, suggesting some bearish OI unwound even as put volume spiked. IV rose 8.7pp alongside a 6.1% price gain. The implied move of ±11.2% into the nearest expiry frames the binary risk around the next earnings report. Rich IV argues against buying options and favors patience on any entry; it does not override the fundamental hold view.

**Short interest.** Short interest is elevated at 24.2% of float (19.1M shares, 8.4 days to cover). This creates two-sided risk: a squeeze on any positive volume surprise, but also confirmation that a substantial cohort of informed investors shares the bearish structural view. High SI combined with a compressed multiple and stalling volume is a caution flag, not a contrarian buy signal on its own.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** HRB is a cash machine trading at ~7x earnings with a 12% FCF yield and a 4.2% covered dividend, and the biggest structural overhang — government Direct File — is dead for this cycle. Assisted pricing power is intact (+4% net average charge), the balance sheet is investment-grade with clean maturities and zero revolver draw, and $700M of buyback capacity plus a rising dividend provide a hard floor on total return. If the new digital-first management team (led by a former TaxAct CEO) stabilizes DIY volume and reaccelerates the DIY/Wave/international adjacencies, the multiple could re-rate toward the low teens. On the FY2027 consensus of $5.82 at ~10x, that is ~$58 — near the prior high and consistent with the $50 analyst high target.

**Bear Case.** The growth model is exhausted: revenue growth is 100% price-led while DIY units shrink 4%+ in the structurally growing channel, and AI tools threaten to commoditize DIY further on both price and volume. Reported earnings are flattered by a one-time tax benefit; normalized growth is ~22% and decelerating (next-quarter estimates down 10% in 90 days). The buyback has been executed above current value, destroying near-term value. Management turnover is broad and the turnaround unproven. With 24% short interest and near-term options stress, any volume disappointment at the next print could drive the stock toward the $29 analyst low and the 52-week low of $28.16 — roughly 27% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on the well-covered ~4.2% dividend, ~12% FCF yield, and $700M of remaining buyback capacity providing a total-return floor, absent a credible catalyst to re-rate the multiple or reaccelerate volume growth.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- DIY online paid volume returns to positive year-over-year growth (currently –4.2% through April) for two consecutive tax seasons, evidencing a successful digital turnaround.
- Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive over a 30–90 day window (currently –4.1% and –10.2% over 90 days).
- The stock compresses toward the low-to-mid $30s (near the 52-week low of $28.16) while fundamentals hold, restoring clear asymmetric upside on the yield and FCF.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Assisted volume turns negative alongside continued DIY erosion, signaling the price-led model is breaking (pricing power capping out on shrinking units).
- Dividend coverage deteriorates or the buyback is suspended to preserve liquidity.
- IRS Direct File is restored (post-2028 political risk) or the Pathward agreement (expiring June 30, 2027) is not renewed on favorable terms.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoint is the FY2026 Q4/full-year earnings report (typically August), which will show the completed 2026 tax season volumes and normalize out the one-time tax benefit. The ±11.2% implied move around that print, combined with 24% short interest, makes it the key binary event.

## Conclusion

H&R Block is a durable, cash-generative franchise trading at a deep multiple discount to peers, with a de-risking tailwind from the termination of IRS Direct File and a strong capital-return floor. Those attributes justify holding the stock for its ~4.2% covered yield and ~12% FCF yield. But the growth model is stalling: revenue expansion is entirely price-led while the company loses units in the structurally growing DIY channel, estimate revisions are drifting lower, reported earnings are flattered by a one-time tax benefit, and a broad management turnover is executing an unproven turnaround. The ~30% decline from the 52-week high has priced much of this, and the cheapness plus yield limit downside, but there is no identifiable catalyst to re-rate the multiple until volume trends stabilize. The elevated 24% short interest and rich, backwardated options pricing corroborate that the market sees genuine near-term risk rather than an obvious bargain.

We rate HRB HOLD (income_hold), consistent with the prior two reads and reflecting a business that is worth owning for yield but not for appreciation until the DIY turnaround shows results. Overall score 5.15.
