## Executive Summary

Encore buys charged-off consumer receivables at deep discounts and collects them over 7-15 years. The business has just come through the sharpest earnings reversal in its recent history: GAAP net income went from −$206.5M in FY2023 and −$139.2M in FY2024 to +$256.8M in FY2025 ($10.91 diluted), with Q1 2026 net income of $86.2M ($3.86, +84% year over year). Cash collections rose 19.9% in FY2025 to $2,592.8M and a further 18.8% in Q1 2026 to $718.4M. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters, by 72.2%, 68.5%, 57.5% and 26.5% respectively. Guidance, as externally reported and arithmetically consistent with audited FY2025 figures, was raised between February and July 2026 from ~$12.00 EPS / ~$2.7B collections to $13.00 / $2.8B.

At $95.10 the stock trades at 7.3x the $13.00 2026 consensus and 6.7x the $14.11 2027 consensus, against a Consumer Finance peer median P/E of 16.6x (though that median is nearly uninformative here: the two closest comparables, PRA Group and Navient, both carry negative trailing earnings). Management repurchased 2.12M shares in FY2025 at an average of $42.26 and a further 345,548 in Q1 2026 at $57.88, cutting the share count ~9.5% in sixteen months, with $282.4M of authorization remaining — 13.8% of the current market capitalization. A $22.5M pre-tax refinancing opportunity ($0.81/share) exists in the 9.25% 2029 and 8.50% 2030 notes against $500M issued at 6.625% in October 2025. The October 2020 CFPB Stipulated Judgment expired in October 2025 and the company reports no material legal reserves.

This is a HOLD rather than a third consecutive BUY on entry arithmetic and three specific pieces of Q1 evidence, not a broken thesis. First, net portfolio investment — purchases less collections applied to principal — fell to $31-34M in Q1 2026 from $103.1M a year earlier as purchases went flat while principal collections rose 26.5%; the receivable book grew only 1.5% sequentially versus 15.8% for full-year 2025. Because portfolio revenue is mechanically the carrying basis multiplied by a fixed purchase effective interest rate, a plateauing basis caps the largest revenue line with a lag. Second, the estimate-revision (non-cash) share of "changes in recoveries" rose from 5% in FY2025 to 27% in Q1 2026, and that line was 56-62% of pre-tax income. Third, legal-channel cost-to-collect deteriorated ~260bp year over year to 35.7%. With a stock at 96.9% of its 52-week high going into an August 5 earnings release with ±10.8% implied on the nearest expiry, the risk/reward that justified buying at $70-83 no longer exists at $95.10.

## Company Overview

Encore operates one reportable segment through three units. **MCM** (Midland Credit Management, US) is the growth engine and describes itself as a market leader in US portfolio purchasing and recovery, primarily charged-off credit card debt: FY2025 purchases $1,174.0M (+17.5%) and collections $1,949.3M. **Cabot** (UK/Europe, including Wescot servicing/BPO) posted FY2025 purchases of $234.1M, down 33.7%, on collections of $640.9M. **LAAP** (Mexico, India) is immaterial at $2.5M of collections. Headcount is ~7,350, 79% international, with low-cost operations in Gurgaon and San José, Costa Rica; the latter operates under a 100% tax holiday through April 6, 2034.

The accounting mechanics matter for reading anything about this company. Purchased pools are carried as a negative allowance equal to the present value of expected recoveries. Revenue has two parts: **portfolio revenue**, accreted at a purchase effective interest rate that never changes over a pool's life, and **changes in recoveries**, which captures period over/under-performance versus forecast plus present-value revisions to future expectations. All forecast revisions land in the second line. Collections run through call center/digital (65% of US Q1 2026 collections, $361.5M), legal ($192.4M, 35%) and agencies ($2.6M); court costs in the legal channel are advanced upfront, before recoveries arrive.

Two standard screening outputs are actively misleading for this issuer and should be discarded. A widely propagated XBRL pull tags FY2025 revenue at ~$88M, which is exactly the servicing revenue line, versus actual total revenue of $1,768.8M — a 20x understatement that corrupts every revenue-derived ratio. And GAAP requires $1,137.0M of FY2025 collections applied to receivable portfolios to be classified in **investing** activities while deducting the non-cash changes-in-recoveries revenue from the operating reconciliation, so reported operating cash flow of $153.2M is a classification artifact, not a measure of cash generation. Any FCF figure built from reported OCF minus capex is economically meaningless here.

**Decomposing the FY2025 swing.** Of the $432.4M pre-tax improvement versus FY2024, roughly $119.1M is the simple absence of impairment (FY2024 carried $100.6M of Cabot goodwill and $18.5M of asset impairments; FY2023 carried $238.2M and $18.7M) and $298.5M is the swing in changes in recoveries from −$89.7M to +$208.8M, partly offset by $41.4M of higher interest expense. FY2025 operating margin was 35.4% and net margin 14.5%; Q1 2026 was 38.7% and 18.1%. FY2025 ROE on average equity was 29.5%, achieved on 3.9x debt/equity.

**Quality of the reversal.** The FY2025 changes-in-recoveries figure decomposed into $197.8M of actual cash collected above the model (95%) and only $11.0M of upward revision to future expectations (5%). That is a materially better quality of earnings than the headline volatility implies — the swing from loss to profit is grounded in real over-collection. But in Q1 2026 the split was $46.0M / $16.7M, so the non-cash share rose to 27%. Management states in the 10-Q that "sustained over-performance in recent quarters led to increases in forecasted future recoveries for recently acquired vintages." That is the correct accounting response to persistent outperformance and also the mechanism by which good news is pulled forward: FY2024 included −$167.9M of downward revisions to expected future recoveries, so the line demonstrably works in reverse. Changes in recoveries was 62% of FY2025 pre-tax income (~$336.2M) and 56% of Q1 2026 pre-tax income ($111.7M).

**Underlying cost leverage is roughly half what the reported line suggests.** Reported operating expenses fell 1.5% in FY2025, but excluding FY2024's impairments the base was $1,040.5M and FY2025 rose 9.8%. Collections grew 19.9% against underlying costs up ~10%: real operating leverage, and consistent with the 3.2pp improvement in cash efficiency margin to 57.8%, but not the step-change the expense line implies.

**Cash bridge.** For FY2025: collections $2,592.8M plus servicing/other $104.2M less cash operating expenses ex-D&A $1,113.4M ≈ $1,583.6M pre-interest, pre-tax; less $279.5M cash interest and $1,389.1M of cash portfolio purchases leaves roughly break-even discretionary cash flow, with $89.5M of buybacks and net portfolio investment funded largely by incremental borrowing (financing activities +$44.9M). The Q1 2026 bridge produces +$31.3M after purchases and capex, but two features make annualizing it wrong: cash interest paid was $37.3M against $73.1M of expense because the largest coupons pay in April/May and October/November, and cash taxes paid were $0.9M against a $25.5M provision.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/2026: assets $5,450.6M (receivables net $4,437.4M, goodwill $529.5M, cash $227.2M), liabilities $4,415.8M (borrowings $4,033.3M), equity $1,034.8M. Debt/equity 3.90x, improved from 4.10x. Net debt $3,806.1M. Interest coverage rose to 2.52x from 2.13x for FY2025. Only $21.0M of principal is due within a year; $791.3M of covenant-adjusted revolver capacity is undrawn; covenants (LTV ≤0.75, SSRCF LTV ≤0.275, fixed charge coverage ≥2.0) are reported in material compliance. The maturity concentration is 2028-2029: GBP 2028 notes $330.6M, EUR 2028 floating notes $479.6M, US Facility $450.0M (Oct 2028), revolver (Sept 2029), 2029 notes $500.0M at 9.25%, and $230.0M of 2029 converts.

Encore's disclosed leverage of 2.4x is a debt-buyer-specific metric: net debt divided by Adjusted EBITDA **plus** collections applied to principal ($676.958M + $953.476M = $1,630.4M at 12/31/25, giving 2.36x). That is economically defensible — principal recoveries genuinely service debt — but conventional net debt/Adjusted EBITDA reads 5.68x, roughly 2.4x more demanding. The trend improves on either basis (TTM rebuild to 3/31/26 gives 2.22x). Additional paid-in capital is $0, so further repurchases charge directly against retained equity, and management states buybacks may be funded from cash "and/or a drawing from our Global Senior Facility."

## Growth Analysis

**What is working.** US supply and pricing remain favorable: the MD&A cites lending and charge-off rates "near recent peak levels," issuers selling predominantly fresh paper, and Q1 pricing "at favorable levels as a result of elevated market supply." US purchase price multiples on recent vintages were *raised* during Q1 2026 (2024 US 2.4x→2.5x, 2025 US 2.3x→2.4x). ROIC recovered from 7.0% (2023) and 7.5% (2024) to 13.7% (2025). Collections equaled 109% of prior-year ERC. Estimated remaining collections stand at $9,806.5M on a 180-month basis and $8,414.6M on 84 months — these are undiscounted gross management forecasts before a ~41-43% cost to collect, interest and tax, and should not be treated as backlog.

**What is decelerating.** Purchases were flat in Q1 2026 ($362.8M, −1.4%) while collections applied to principal rose 26.5% to $328.4M, cutting net portfolio investment to $31-34M from $103.1M. The receivable book grew 1.5% sequentially (2.1% ex-FX) versus 15.8% for full-year 2025. Management's 2026 purchase guidance of $1.4-1.5B against $1.408B in 2025 implies 0-7% growth, while collections are guided to $2.7-2.8B. Because portfolio revenue equals basis × fixed EIR, a flat basis caps the largest revenue line, meaning 2027 growth would need to come from over-collection and cost efficiency rather than book growth. The counter-argument is real and should be weighed: US deployment is largely via forward flows "often committed early in the calendar year," minimum forward-flow commitments were $678.9M at 3/31/26 with actual purchases expected "significantly greater," and one quarter of lumpy deployment is not a trend.

**Europe is the structural drag.** Purchases fell 33.7% in FY2025, the 2025 European vintage mark was cut 2.3x→2.2x in Q1 2026, and Europe carries a 2.0% monthly EIR versus 3.4% in the US with far longer duration ($632.0M of European ERC sits beyond 2034 versus $238.6M for the US). Starving Europe of capital is rational given that yield-and-duration combination, but it means the growth engine is a single geography whose revenue share rose from 68.6% to 74.0% year over year — concentration increasing, not diversifying.

**Estimate revisions.** Current-year consensus is $13.00, up 7.7% over 90 days and flat over 30; next year is $14.11, up 9.8% over 90 days and 2.1% over 30. That is positive momentum but well short of the magnitude that flags systematic under-estimation of an acceleration. The single-analyst current-quarter estimate of $2.62 is down 3.0% over 90 days and sits 32% below Q1's $3.86 actual — a soft near-term bar given four consecutive beats, but a full-year $13.00 that requires meaningful second-half delivery. Coverage is thin (three analysts on the annual lines, one on the quarterly), which raises dispersion risk in both directions.

On headline multiples this is cheap: 7.3x forward against 12.0x for PROG Holdings and 16.6x for EZCORP. But the peer median is close to unusable — PRA Group (−2.2x) and Navient (−12.4x), the two most relevant comparables, both carry negative trailing earnings, and World Acceptance at 25.7x is a different business. Debt buyers have historically traded at single-digit forward multiples in favorable parts of the cycle precisely because earnings are model-influenced and leverage is high; 7.3x is within, not below, Encore's own good-cycle range.

The more binding constraint is the asset-side floor. Applying a 42% cost to collect to the 84-month ERC of $8,414.6M leaves ~$4,880M of net pre-interest, pre-tax cash; less $3,806.1M of net debt gives ~$1,074M. Adding a discounted-in-name-only tail from the 84-to-180-month ERC ($1,391.9M gross × 0.58 ≈ $807M, receivable in years 8-15) reaches roughly $1.9B undiscounted, against a $2.04B market cap. This exercise applies no discount rate, ignores servicing, ignores that fixed costs would fall in runoff, and ignores Encore's very low cash tax rate — it is directional only. The market is no longer paying an asset-backed discount. It is now capitalizing the ability to keep redeploying at 2.4-2.5x multiples and ~13.7% ROIC, which is exactly the variable Q1's net-investment collapse calls into question.

The disclosed Level 3 fair value of receivables ($4,979.6M) exceeds carrying value ($4,437.4M) by $542.2M, which supports the 1.30x adjusted book multiple. The 10-Q explicitly cautions that this total "does not represent, and should not be construed to represent, the underlying value of the Company," and the inputs are unobservable.

Two identified sources of value the multiple does not fully reflect: the $282.4M remaining authorization (though management declined to commit to a 2026 amount and executed monthly dollars fell from $13.2M in January to $2.2M in March as the price rose), and the refinancing spread available on the 9.25% and 8.50% notes. Call protection on those notes is unverified; typical high-yield secured structures carry 2-3 year non-call periods, which could push realization to 2027-2028.

Analyst targets bracket $105-$120 with a $113.33 mean — all above spot, implying 10-26% upside. On a stock at 7.3x forward with rising estimates, targets are more floor than ceiling, but with three analysts covering a $2B name they carry limited independent weight, and the $113 mean is close to my own base case.

**Score rationale (6.2):** the absolute multiple is low and there are two identifiable catalysts, but cheapness in this industry is the norm rather than the mispricing, the asset floor has been reached, and the earnings on which the multiple is calculated depend materially on a line item that reversed by $298M within twelve months across FY2024-FY2025.

## Competitive Landscape

The public debt-buying market is effectively three players — Encore, PRA Group and Jefferson Capital — above a long tail of subscale purchasers. Encore's own competitive read, echoed across the 10-K and 10-Q, is starkly bifurcated: in the US, scale is an advantage because "smaller competitors continue to face difficulties… because of the high cost to operate due to regulatory pressure and increasing cost of capital"; in Europe, "underlying default rates are generally low by historic levels, and consumer lending volumes have stagnated… portfolio pricing remains competitive," constraining deployment.

Against PRA Group in Q1 2026, Encore grew collections 18.8% to $718.4M versus PRA's +11% to $551.9M, but PRA reported a 61.8% cash efficiency ratio against Encore's approximately 60.4%. The definitions may differ materially between issuers and I could not reconcile them, so treat the efficiency comparison as indicative only. PRA's larger non-US footprint is sometimes framed as a diversification edge; Encore is moving the other way, with US revenue share rising to 74.0%.

Two competitive-position headwinds deserve weight. The 10-K flags carrier-level filtering and blocking of calls, texts and emails, a slow-burn erosion of contactability that pushes accounts toward the more expensive legal channel. And legal-channel economics are deteriorating: cost of legal collections rose 21.7% in FY2025 and 31.2% in Q1 2026 (court costs +36.0%, legal fees +21.8%) against ~21.6% growth in legal-channel collections, taking my derived legal cost-to-collect from 33.1% to 35.7%. That metric understates true cost because internal legal-channel employee expense sits in salaries. The benign explanation is timing — court costs are advanced upfront, so a ramp in placements mechanically depresses the ratio near term, and MCM legal collections at ~34.6% of MCM total collections are near a record low, consistent with digital carrying the growth. The structural explanation is falling productivity per placement or rising per-case fees. The disclosed data cannot distinguish them, and this is the cleanest live margin question in the filings.

Net trajectory: improving in the US, deteriorating in Europe, with a cost-side question mark. Stable overall, which supports a middling score rather than a high one.

## Risk Assessment

**Model dependence with earnings leverage.** Changes in recoveries was 62% of FY2025 pre-tax income and 56% of Q1 2026's, and depends on forecasts of collections up to fifteen years out. FY2024 demonstrated the downside with −$167.9M of downward revisions to expected future recoveries.

**Leverage and the 2028-2029 wall.** $4.03B of borrowings against $1.03B of equity, a maintenance fixed-charge coverage test at 2.0x, and two LTV tests. Near-term amortization is trivial ($21.0M within one year), but 2028-2029 concentrates the GBP 2028 notes, EUR 2028 FRNs, US Facility, revolver, 2029 notes and 2029 converts. This risk is macro-linked: the refinancing tailwind assumes high-yield markets stay open at or below 6.625%. A material widening in high-yield spreads would convert the refinancing tailwind into a headwind and make the wall harder.

**Forward flow lock-in.** Encore commits to volumes at set prices over 3-12 months, with $678.9M of minimum committed purchase price at 3/31/26. Most contracts contain repricing or termination provisions, but a sudden improvement in consumer credit — or a deterioration in collectability at fixed prices — hurts with a lag. The current cycle depends on the MD&A's statement that charge-off rates remain "near recent peak levels" continuing to hold. A Zacks industry note (June 23, 2026) reports the Federal Reserve has paused rate cuts and signaled a possible hike amid inflation signs; higher-for-longer rates sustain the consumer stress that generates charge-off supply, but a genuine consumer break would lengthen liquidation curves on paper already purchased.

**Cabot goodwill.** $529.5M remains after $338.8M of cumulative Cabot impairment across FY2023-FY2024. Europe is being starved of capital, its 2025 vintage mark was cut in Q1 2026, and its collections are FX-flattered ($12.2M tailwind in Q1). The annual goodwill testing date was moved effective FY2025 from the first day to the last day of Q4, stated reason alignment with budgeting — a defensible operational rationale, noted only because it changes the measurement date for a unit with an impairment history.

**Concentration and disclosure gaps.** The 10-K flags concentration among a small number of sellers and panel-access risk but does not quantify it, so I cannot say whether counterparty concentration improved. Item 5.02 contents of the March 20 and June 15/16 8-Ks and the Item 7.01/8.01 content of the July 22 filing are unread (SEC returned 403 on direct fetches); whether any executive change occurred is unresolved. Q2 2026 results are not in evidence.

**What has improved.** The October 2020 CFPB Stipulated Judgment, carrying a $15.0M penalty and following a 2015 Consent Order, expired in October 2025, and the Q1 10-Q reports no material developments, no new material proceedings and no material legal reserves. That removes the principal regulatory constraint on MCM for the first time in a decade, without removing underlying FDCPA/FCRA/TCPA/Regulation F exposure. Weighted-average rates fell on every floating facility year over year (revolver 6.56%→5.53%, US Facility 7.82%→7.18%, Cabot 7.78%→6.91%, EUR FRNs 7.10%→6.27%), with ~$1.5B of notional hedged and a new £255M SONIA cap effective January 2028.

**On Altman Z (0.79) and Piotroski.** Neither is diagnostic here. The Z-Score uses working capital (Encore has no classified balance sheet) and sales/assets (mis-mapped by the 20x revenue tagging error), and treats 3.9x leverage as distress when it is the business model, secured against a receivable book carried at $4.44B with a disclosed fair value of $4.98B. The Piotroski "OCF > net income" test fails on a GAAP classification artifact. Relevant solvency evidence is covenant compliance, $791.3M undrawn capacity, 2.52x coverage, $21.0M of near-term principal, and 84-month ERC of $8.41B against $3.81B net debt.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 60.9% against 34.8% one-year realized — a 26.1pp premium, with the term structure in backwardation to 45.5% (3m) and 41.3% (6m). The nearest expiry (August 21) prices a ±10.8% move, consistent with the August 5 earnings release. Put/call skew of 5.7% is flagged as elevated put demand, while open interest is heavily call-tilted (P/C OI 0.09) — recent hedging demand layered on a predominantly long-call book, which is what one would expect after a large move into a print. Short interest is modest at 7.0% of float and 3.4 days to cover, so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The prior snapshot's 439% ATM IV reading almost certainly reflects thin quoting in a small-cap chain rather than a real regime, and I do not read the decline to 60.9% as information.

The practical takeaway: options are expensive into a binary event, which argues against paying up for exposure here and in favor of either waiting for the print or expressing interest through premium sale rather than fresh long stock at 96.9% of the 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $130 (+36.7%)

US supply stays robust at 2.4-2.5x purchase multiples, and Q1's flat purchases prove to be forward-flow timing rather than a plateau: full-year deployment lands at the top of the $1.4-1.5B range and the receivable book resumes mid-single-digit sequential growth. Collections hit the $2.8B guide, cash efficiency clears 58%, and the legal cost ratio normalizes as advanced court costs convert to recoveries. The 9.25% 2029 and 8.50% 2030 notes are refinanced near 6.625%, adding ~$0.81 per share. Management resumes buybacks against the $282.4M authorization, compounding the ~9.5% share-count reduction already achieved. 2027 EPS reaches $14.11 plus the refinancing benefit; 8.7x on ~$14.90 gives $130. On this path the ROIC-linked compensation structure keeps capital from chasing low-multiple paper, and the multiple holds because earnings are visibly cash-backed.

### Bear Case — $68 (−28.5%)

Collections normalize toward the model and changes in recoveries flips negative, as it did in FY2024 when downward revisions to future recoveries hit −$167.9M. Simultaneously, the net-investment plateau bites: portfolio revenue growth stalls because the basis is flat, while interest expense stays near $293-300M and legal cost-to-collect grinds above 38% on structural rather than timing grounds. European default rates stay low, Cabot deployment stays suppressed and a further goodwill test pressures the remaining $529.5M. EPS reverts toward $9.50 and the multiple compresses to 7x on lower earnings quality, giving $66-68. Because APIC is exhausted, any debt-funded buyback in that environment reduces book equity one-for-one against tightening covenant headroom (FCCR floor 2.0x versus 2.52x coverage today).

### Base Case — $110 (+15.7%)

Guidance is substantially delivered: $13.00 EPS, collections near $2.8B, purchases $1.4-1.5B with net investment thin but positive. The legal cost ratio stabilizes rather than reverses. Refinancing happens but slips into 2027 on call protection. 8.5x on $13.00 = $110.50. This sits close to the $113.33 analyst mean and $105 low target, which is itself a reason not to expect much multiple expansion from here without a demonstrable reacceleration in deployment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month path to ~$110 is positive and catalyst-supported, but the 15.7% base-case upside against 28.5% bear-case downside does not justify new capital at $95.10 the day before an earnings release with 60.9% implied volatility.

**Addressing the trajectory.** Scores have moved 7.3 (May 8) → 6.5 (June 19) → 6.0 now, with two prior BUYs. The stock has risen 14.5% since the June report and ~36% since the March 31 balance sheet date (the 2029 convert's if-converted disclosure implies a 3/31/26 close of ~$70.12). No new hard financials have been published in that window, so most of the score decline is entry arithmetic rather than deterioration: a higher price on unchanged information is a weaker proposition, not a broken one. Where I do see genuine fundamental softening, it is specific and visible in the Q1 filing: net portfolio investment down 67-70% year over year, the non-cash share of recoveries up from 5% to 27%, legal cost-to-collect up ~260bp, and the European 2025 vintage mark cut. Those support the growth and risk score reductions independently of price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Deployment reaccelerates.** Q2 or Q3 purchases above ~$400M with the receivable portfolio growing ≥3-4% sequentially ex-FX, confirming Q1 was forward-flow timing.
2. **Earnings quality holds while guidance is raised again.** Changes in recoveries stays majority cash over-collection (estimate-revision share below ~25%) with 2026 EPS guidance reaffirmed at or above $13.00.
3. **Price.** A pullback to $78-82 (≈6.0-6.3x 2026 consensus) with fundamentals intact restores the asymmetry, as does confirmed refinancing of the 9.25% or 8.50% notes at ≤7%, which converts $0.81/share from a hypothesis into an earnings line.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Negative changes in expected future recoveries in any quarter** — the FY2024 pattern reasserting itself, which would signal marks reversing and would hit reported EPS hard given the line's 56-62% share of pre-tax income.
2. **Legal cost-to-collect above ~38%** with collections growth decelerating below ~8%, distinguishing structural cost inflation from placement timing.
3. **Balance sheet stress:** fixed charge coverage approaching 2.0x, LTV moving toward 0.75, a Cabot goodwill impairment, or evidence the 2028-2029 maturities are being addressed at materially higher spreads.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediately after the **August 5, 2026** Q2 release, with the specific items to check being purchases and net portfolio investment, the cash/non-cash split of changes in recoveries, legal cost of collections growth versus legal-channel collections growth, buyback activity and average price, and any update on 2026 guidance. Secondary checkpoint: the Q3 print, by which time two quarters of deployment data will settle the plateau question.

## Conclusion

Encore is a genuinely improved company at a materially worse entry price. The operating evidence is strong and mostly cash-backed: collections up 19.9% in FY2025 and 18.8% in Q1 2026, ROIC recovered to 13.7% from 7.5%, cash efficiency up 3.2pp, leverage falling on both the company's definition and a conventional one, interest coverage up to 2.52x, the CFPB Stipulated Judgment expired, and a capital allocator who repurchased roughly 9% of the company at an average $42.26 while the market was still pricing two loss years. At 7.3x forward with $282.4M of buyback authorization and an identifiable $0.81/share refinancing spread, the stock is not expensive.

What has changed is that the asset-side floor has been reached — a runoff exercise on 84-month ERC nets roughly $1.1B against a $2.04B market cap, so the market is now paying entirely for redeployment ability — precisely as Q1 showed net portfolio investment collapsing to $31-34M from $103.1M, the non-cash share of the earnings-swing line quadrupling, and legal costs growing faster than legal collections. Those three facts do not break the thesis; they cap it, and they arrive with the stock at 96.9% of its 52-week high, one day before an earnings release the options market prices at ±10.8%. Base case $110, bull $130, bear $68. Hold existing positions through the print; new capital should wait for either evidence that deployment has reaccelerated or a price closer to $80.
