## Executive Summary

Encore Capital Group buys portfolios of charged-off consumer receivables at deep discounts and collects them over multi-year horizons. The investment case over the past eighteen months has been an earnings recovery from two consecutive loss years (FY2023 net loss $206.5M, FY2024 $139.2M) to a record FY2025 net income of $256.8M, driven by two mechanically distinct forces: cash collections that persistently exceeded management's own model, and a structural decline in cost-to-collect from 51.0% of collections (FY2023) to 40.6% (Q1 2026). The shares have risen from $37.05 twelve months ago to $96.28, a 160% move that has taken the stock to near its 52-week high of $98.15.

The fundamental trajectory remains intact and is not in dispute. Cash collections grew 19.9% in FY2025 and 18.8% in Q1 2026 to $718.4M. Consensus current-year EPS has risen 7.7% over 90 days to $13.00 and next-year 9.8% to $14.11. Four consecutive quarters have beaten consensus by 26.5% to 72.2%. Debt/equity fell from 4.10x to 3.90x, the Cabot securitisation was extended a year and repriced 20bp tighter, and the company issued $500M of 6.625% notes in October 2025 against 9.250% paper placed earlier — direct evidence that lenders are marking Encore's credit better. Management called all $230.0M of its 2029 convertibles for cash settlement in September, extinguishing 3.73M shares (17.4% of the share count) of contingent dilution rather than issuing equity at $96.

What holds this back from a BUY at spot is the composition of the earnings and the position in the price. Changes in recoveries — the mark-to-model line — supplied 62.1% of FY2025 pretax income and 56.2% of Q1 2026 pretax income. Most of that is cash actually collected above forecast, which is high quality, but the recoveries-above-forecast rate has widened from 3.62% of collections in FY2024 to 7.63% in FY2025, and Q1 2026 saw the pure forward-estimate slice rise from 3.3% of pretax income to 14.9%. Cabot carries $529.5M of remaining goodwill after $338.8M of impairments in two consecutive years, and European deployment is being deliberately cut. At 7.4x current-year consensus with a base case of roughly $113, the upside to base is +17% against a bear case near $63 (-35%). That asymmetry, near the 52-week high, one day before a print priced for a ±10.9% move, argues for holding rather than adding.

## Company Overview

Encore operates a single reportable segment through three units. MCM (Midland Credit Management) is the U.S. business, focused predominantly on charged-off credit card debt and described in the 10-K as the market leader in U.S. portfolio purchasing and recovery. Cabot Credit Management is the European business, UK-centric with France and Spain as development markets, and includes a fee-based third-party servicing operation (Wescot). LAAP covers Mexico NPLs and an Indian asset reconstruction company and is immaterial.

Recovery runs through three channels. FY2025 U.S. collections split $1,272.1M call center/digital, $663.2M legal, $14.0M agencies; Europe split $263.4M, $229.4M, $148.2M. Cumulative purchase price across the company's history is $16.3B against $30.6B of historical collections.

The accounting matters for interpretation. Under CECL, Encore writes off the amortized cost of each purchased pool immediately and records a negative allowance equal to the present value of expected recoveries. The purchase-date effective interest rate is fixed and never revised (monthly EIR at 3/31/2026: U.S. 3.4%, Europe 2.0%). Revenue therefore comprises portfolio revenue (mechanical accretion at the fixed EIR) plus changes in recoveries, itself split between recoveries above/below forecast (cash variance in the period) and changes in expected future recoveries (a forward estimate revision). Reading Encore's earnings requires that split.

## Financial Analysis

**The three-year swing.** Total revenues grew 34.4% in FY2025 to $1,768.8M, but portfolio revenue — the accretion base — grew only 11.8% to $1,455.8M. The dominant driver was changes in recoveries at +$208.8M versus −$89.7M in FY2024, a $298.5M year-over-year delta larger than the entire $256.8M of FY2025 net income. Decomposed: recoveries above forecast $197.8M (FY2024: $78.2M) and changes in expected future recoveries +$11.0M (FY2024: −$167.9M).

That decomposition is the most important number in the filings. The $197.8M is cash collected in the period above model, not an accrual. FY2024's loss was the mirror image: a $167.9M downward revision to *future* expectations plus $119.1M of goodwill and asset impairments. The FY2025 recovery is predominantly cash-backed, which is a materially better earnings quality read than the headline "mark-to-model line = 62% of pretax income" implies.

The variance is nonetheless widening. Recoveries above forecast equalled 7.63% of collections in FY2025 ($197.8M ÷ $2,592.8M) versus 3.62% in FY2024. A model running 400bp more favorable year-over-year is either capturing a durable operational improvement it has not yet incorporated, or is being outrun by timing acceleration that reverses.

**Operating leverage is the durable part.** Total operating expenses *fell* in absolute dollars across three years — $1,206.1M → $1,159.0M → $1,142.2M — while collections rose 39% from $1,862.6M to $2,592.8M. Impairment-adjusted cost-to-collect: 51.0% (FY2023), 48.1% (FY2024), 44.1% (FY2025), 40.6% (Q1 2026, against ~43.6% in Q1 2025). Adjusted EBITDA doubled from $332.9M to $677.0M. This is fixed-cost leverage over a growing collections base, consistent with management's stated digital and technology drivers, and it is the mechanism that justifies a re-rating rather than a one-year earnings pop.

One tension inside it: cost of legal collections rose 21.7% in FY2025 to $315.5M and 31.2% in Q1 2026 to $89.2M, with court costs up 36.0%. The legal channel is the expensive channel, and the risk factors flag increasing carrier and consumer filtering of calls and emails, which pushes volume there. Digital gains and legal-channel cost inflation are running simultaneously; the net has been favorable so far.

**Balance sheet.** Borrowings of $4,033.3M against equity of $1,034.8M at 3/31/2026 gives debt/equity of 3.90x, down from 4.10x at year-end; equity/assets improved 18.3% → 19.0%. Interest coverage on income from operations was 2.13x in FY2025 and 2.52x in Q1 2026. Only $21.0M of $4,032.8M principal matures within one year; the maturity ladder is heavily weighted to 2028 and beyond (the earliest instruments — the Encore 2028 GBP notes and the 2028 EUR floating notes — mature in 2028). Revolver availability was $791.3M against $227.2M of cash. Derived blended cost of debt ≈ 7.0% (Q1 stated interest $70.5M annualized over ~$4,017M average borrowings). Covenants are LTV ≤ 0.75, super senior LTV ≤ 0.275, fixed charge coverage ≥ 2.0, with material compliance stated at 3/31/2026; the LTV definition is not disclosed in enough detail to replicate independently.

Liability management has been active and favorable: the Cabot securitisation was amended 3/18/2026 to extend from January 2030 to January 2031 with the margin cut from 3.20% to 3.00%; €200.0M of €415.0M floating-rate 2028 notes were redeemed at par on 5/28/2026; and the October 2025 issuance of $500M at 6.625% compares against 9.250% on the 2029 notes. Borrowings at 3/31/2026 do not yet reflect the €200M redemption.

**Cash flow requires a correction before interpretation.** Reported FY2025 operating cash flow was $153.2M against $256.8M of net income, which screens as poor earnings quality. It is largely a classification artifact: cash collections applied to the principal balance of receivable portfolios are investing inflows, $1,137.0M in FY2025. Economic cash generation was therefore approximately $1,290.2M against $1,389.1M of cash portfolio purchases, i.e. net deployment of ~$99M funded with debt. Q1 2026 OCF was $82.3M versus $45.3M.

Two cautions survive the correction. Reported net income embeds $208.8M (FY2025) and $62.7M (Q1 2026) of changes in recoveries. And cash taxes are running far below the provision — Q1 2026 cash taxes paid $860K against a $25.5M provision, plausibly NOL utilization from the loss years. That is cash-favorable now and finite.

Relatedly, the Altman Z-Score of 0.79 that appears on standard screens for ECPG traces to a revenue input mapped to servicing revenue only ($88.4M) rather than total revenues ($1,768.8M). Recomputed with correct inputs the score is roughly 1.29. Correcting it does not make the leverage benign, but it does make the screen non-diagnostic: a specialty finance company that funds asset purchases with debt by design will always score badly on a model calibrated to manufacturers. The covenant package, 2.1–2.5x interest coverage, $791.3M of undrawn revolver and near-absence of maturities before 2028 are the evidence that bears on solvency.

**Financial health score: 6.2.** Falling leverage, improving cost of new capital, a comfortable maturity ladder and a $542.2M disclosed excess of portfolio fair value over carrying value support the score. Against that: 3.90x leverage on Level 3 model-based assets, a 7.0% blended funding cost against roughly $1.47B of floating exposure, and a September cash settlement that raises interest expense.

## Growth Analysis

Cash collections are the cleanest top line: $1,862.6M (FY2023) → $2,162.5M (FY2024, +16.1%) → $2,592.8M (FY2025, +19.9%) → $718.4M in Q1 2026 (+18.8%). Portfolio purchases were $1,073.8M → $1,352.0M → $1,408.1M, with $362.8M deployed in Q1 2026.

Deployment has shifted decisively toward the U.S.: 75.9% of purchases in FY2023, 73.9% in FY2024, 83.4% in FY2025, 87.0% in Q1 2026. The MD&A gives the reason. U.S. charge-off rates are described as near recent peak levels with supply robust and pricing at favorable levels because of elevated market supply. In Europe, underlying default rates are low by historical standards, consumer lending volumes have stagnated, pricing remains competitive, and Encore is explicitly *constraining* the capital it elects to deploy. Q1 2026 revenue by geography: U.S. $351.7M (74.0%), UK $88.5M (18.6%), other Europe $33.4M (7.0%).

The underwriting scoreboard is the vintage purchase-price multiple. At 3/31/2026, U.S. vintages ran 2.7x pre-2022, 2.1x for 2022 (the trough), 2.4x 2023, 2.5x 2024, 2.4x 2025, 2.4x 2026; Europe 2.0–2.4x. Recent vintages sit below the legacy book, so returns on new capital are lower than the historical average. But between the 12/31/2025 and 3/31/2026 disclosures, the U.S. 2024 vintage was marked up from 2.4x to 2.5x and 2025 from 2.3x to 2.4x — corroboration for the +$16.7M forward revision recorded in Q1.

Forward flow agreements at 3/31/2026 carried an estimated minimum aggregate purchase price of $678.9M, with management expecting actual purchases to be significantly greater. These are typically 3–12 month contracts with renegotiation or termination provisions on quality deterioration, a partial protection against buying into a worsening vintage.

Consensus expects $13.00 current-year EPS (revised +7.7% over 90 days) and $14.11 next year (+9.8%), implying roughly 8.5% EPS growth off the FY2026 base. Current-quarter estimate is $2.62, trimmed 3.0% over 90 days on a single analyst. A widely circulated FY2026 guidance figure of $2.8B collections and $13.00 EPS could not be verified in any filing or company release; it reconciles precisely to FY2025 GAAP actuals grown 8% and 19% respectively, and it coincides exactly with the three-analyst consensus, which raises the possibility that consensus is anchored to it rather than independently derived. Treat any forward multiple built on $13.00 as resting on an unconfirmed figure of ambiguous basis.

**Growth score: 6.5.** Collections growth near 19%, four large consecutive beats, and a favorable U.S. supply environment support solid mid-teens-or-better revenue momentum. But the FY2025 EPS explosion came off a loss base and is not repeatable, forward consensus growth decelerates to high single digits, Europe is being shrunk by choice, and a meaningful slice of reported growth is currency (FX added ~$22.1M to FY2025 collections and ~$12.2M in Q1 2026).

## Valuation Assessment

At the $96.28 official close and 21,440,412 shares, market capitalization is $2,064M.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2025 diluted EPS $10.91 | 8.8x |
| P/E on LTM EPS ~$12.86 | 7.5x |
| P/E on current-year consensus $13.00 | 7.4x |
| P/E on next-year consensus $14.11 | 6.8x |
| Price/book (equity $1,034.8M at 3/31/26) | 2.00x |
| Consumer Finance peer median P/E | 16.6x |

The peer median is not a usable anchor. EZPW (16.6x) is pawn lending, PRG (12.0x) lease-to-own, WRLD (25.7x) installment lending — different balance-sheet structures and accounting. The two closest comparables by business model, PRAA at −2.2x and NAVI at −12.4x, are loss-making. Debt buying is a structurally low-multiple industry because assets are internally modeled Level 3 estimates funded with roughly 4x leverage, and reported earnings carry a large mark-to-model component. ECPG's own history has been roughly 6–10x forward in constructive periods. At 7.4x current-year, the stock sits mid-range of its own band rather than at a distressed discount.

Two independent cross-checks. First, the 10-Q discloses estimated fair value of receivable portfolios of $4,979.6M against $4,437.4M carrying value. A look-through — $4,979.6M less $4,033.3M borrowings plus $227.2M cash, plus $529.5M goodwill — gives ~$1,703M versus a $2,064M market cap, so the market pays about 1.2x a model-based net asset figure. That figure ignores taxes, corporate overhead and the value of the servicing business, so it should be read loosely, and the fair value is management's own Level 3 output. Second, 84-month ERC of $8,414.6M covers borrowings 2.09x, but ERC is undiscounted gross collections before cost to collect; at a 41–44% cost-to-collect only roughly 56–59% is available to service debt and equity, which reduces the effective cushion to roughly $4.7–5.0B against $4.03B of debt.

Building the scenarios:

- **Base $113** — 8.0x next-year consensus $14.11, roughly ECPG's mid-cycle forward multiple, consistent with continued collections growth near 15% and cost-to-collect holding in the low 40s. +17.4% from $96.28.
- **Bull $138** — 9.8x $14.11, requiring further vintage markups, cost-to-collect below 40%, and the recoveries-above-forecast rate holding above 6% of collections. Also implies the market accepts that the improvement is structural. +43.3%.
- **Bear $63** — recoveries above forecast normalizes from 7.63% toward the 3.62% FY2024 rate. On ~$2.8B of collections that removes roughly $112M pretax, ~$86M after tax at the recent effective rate, or about $4.00 per share, taking EPS toward $9.00; at 7x that is $63. A third Cabot goodwill impairment would compound it. −34.6%.

Analyst targets are $105 low, $115 median, $113 mean, $120 high — the entire published range sits above spot, and Citizens raised to $115 from $108 in mid-June. On an inflecting stock at a modest multiple, targets tend to lag; but ECPG has now closed to within 9% of the lowest published target after a 160% move, so the remaining gap is thin.

**Valuation score: 6.0.** The multiple is genuinely low in absolute terms against rising estimates, and there are identified catalysts. But the discount is structural to the industry rather than an anomaly, the multiple has already expanded materially from the $37 low, the stock is near its 52-week high, and the base case offers +17% against −35% downside. Cheapness alone is not a reason to buy here; the earnings composition has to prove out first.

## Competitive Landscape

Encore is one of three U.S.-listed debt buyers alongside PRA Group and Jefferson Capital (the latter per secondary reporting, not independently verified in a filing). On the available comparison, Encore's Q1 2026 collections of $718.4M grew 18.8% against PRA's reported $551.9M and +11% — larger in absolute terms and growing roughly eight points faster, which supports a share-gain read. PRA's reported "cash efficiency" of 61.8% is above the ~59.4% implied by Encore's 40.6% cost-to-collect, but these ratios are almost certainly not defined identically and should not be compared as an efficiency verdict.

The asserted moat has three legs: pricing and underwriting data accumulated across $16.3B of cumulative purchases and $30.6B of collections; an internal legal collections platform rather than full reliance on retained law firms; and a compliance posture the company markets as a Consumer Bill of Rights. The 10-K also argues that regulatory cost and cost of capital are squeezing smaller competitors out, favoring scale participants. That is management's characterization.

The trajectory is mixed. Improving: cost-to-collect down 700bp in three years, collections growing faster than the closest public comparable, vintage multiples recovering from the 2022 trough, and a demonstrably better cost of new debt (6.625% versus 9.250%). Deteriorating or concentrating: 87% of Q1 deployment in a single country's credit cycle, Europe shrinking by choice into competitive pricing, and channel erosion pushing collections into the expensive legal channel. The regulatory point cuts both ways — the 10-K risk factors state plainly that any sustained decrease in regulation or enforcement could lead to increased competition, meaning part of Encore's moat is created by the compliance burden it bears.

The U.S. tailwind is fundamentally a consumer-stress tailwind. Peak charge-offs create cheap supply, and the current environment sustains that supply. The same environment eventually pressures liquidation rates per dollar of face, and Encore's own risk factors note its statistical models depend on historical relationships that macro shifts can break.

**Competitive position score: 6.3.** Improving operationally, concentrating geographically.

## Risk Assessment

**Earnings composition.** The cash-variance portion of changes in recoveries is real; the pure forward-estimate slice rose from 3.3% of FY2025 pretax income to 14.9% in Q1 2026. The FY2025 10-K concluded the over-performance was current-period collections over-performance and did not represent a material shift in timing. The Q1 2026 10-Q repeats that conclusion and then adds that sustained over-performance led to increases in forecasted future recoveries for recent vintages. If the improvement is durable operational gain, further ERC markups are a mechanical earnings tailwind. If it is timing acceleration, the same cash is being recognized earlier and later periods underperform. The vintage markups favor the first reading; one quarter is not proof.

**Cabot goodwill.** $338.8M of accumulated impairment, entirely at Cabot ($238.2M in Q4 FY2023, $100.6M in Q4 FY2024), with $529.5M remaining at 3/31/2026. The annual testing date was moved from the first day of Q4 to the last day of Q4, stated as alignment with the budgeting process — an ordinary rationale whose practical effect is a measurement date three months later. The FY2025 test found no impairment and there were no interim triggering events in Q1. With European deployment deliberately curtailed, a third test failure is a live risk against $529.5M of carrying value, roughly 51% of book equity.

**Convertible settlement and hedge unwind.** All $230.0M of the 4.00% 2029 convertibles were called for cash redemption on September 24, 2026, with conversions after the call also settled in cash and the make-whole conversion rate increased to 16.2056 shares per $1,000. At $96.28 that is $1,548.28 per note of conversion value (16.2056 × $96.28), $356.1M in total, roughly $126M above principal. Capped calls struck to lift the effective conversion price from $65.89 to $82.69 have approximate intrinsic value of ~$58.6M to Encore (likely understated, since it ignores remaining time value), leaving a net unhedged cash premium of roughly $67.5M. Liquidity is not the constraint ($227.2M cash plus $791.3M revolver). But funding a 4.00% instrument with revolver borrowings at 5.53% weighted average raises cash interest cost, and the company's own release warns that counterparties unwinding hedges may sell shares in secondary market transactions with the effect of decreasing the market price. That is a technical share-supply overhang into late September independent of fundamentals.

**Rate sensitivity.** Roughly $1.47B of floating exposure (revolver $681.3M, Cabot securitisation $337.3M, U.S. Facility $450.0M), partially capped. A 100bp move is roughly $14.7M pretax, ~$0.53 per share, about 4% of consensus EPS. Nothing material matures before 2028, which limits the near-term refinancing exposure.

**Unresolved disclosures.** 8-Ks filed 2026-03-20 and 2026-06-15 each carried Item 5.02 (officer/director changes) and Item 5.03 (bylaw amendments) and were not readable. A CFO or business-unit president transition would be material to execution risk; routine director rotation would not. This is an open verification gap, not evidence of a problem.

**Other.** Seller concentration is flagged in the risk factors but unquantified — no named sellers or percentages — and a shift toward more U.S. deployment plausibly increases rather than reduces issuer-panel concentration. The October 2020 CFPB Stipulated Judgment expired in October 2025 with no material legal reserves at 3/31/2026, removing a real overhang. FX contributed materially to reported growth; AOCI stood at −$132.4M, predominantly translation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 62.9% against 34.8% one-year realized — a 28.2-point premium, 70th percentile within the chain — with term structure in backwardation (62.9% at one month, 47.0% at three, 41.9% at six). The August 21 tenor prices a ±10.9% move, which spans the August 5 Q2 print. That is a straightforward event premium rather than a regime signal: buying protection here is expensive, and for holders the structure favors writing covered calls into the print rather than paying up for hedges.

Two readings sit in tension. The put/call skew of 7.5% at the near tenor is characterized as elevated put demand and flips negative at the six-month tenor (−2.2%). Open interest, however, is heavily call-tilted at a 0.09 put/call ratio, and put/call volume is 0.00 — so the standing book is overwhelmingly long calls, consistent with momentum positioning rather than accumulated downside protection. Short interest is 7.0% of float at 3.4 days to cover: uncrowded, with no squeeze dynamic and no meaningful bearish institutional conviction expressed through the borrow. The composite read is a market braced for a large single-day move with no directional consensus, which is the correct posture given four consecutive beats of 26–72% against a stock already near its high.

**Risk factors score: 5.0.** Multiple specific, dated items — the August 5 print, the September convertible settlement and hedge unwind, the year-end Cabot goodwill test — sit alongside an earnings line that depends on a widening favorable model variance, near a 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($138, +43%).** Cost-to-collect continues below 40% as digital reach expands the payer book, and the recoveries-above-forecast rate holds above 6% of collections. Management converts sustained over-performance into further ERC markups on 2024–2026 vintages, as it began doing in Q1 with the 2.4x → 2.5x and 2.3x → 2.4x revisions, producing a mechanical earnings tailwind on top of collections growth. U.S. charge-off supply stays at peak levels through the consumer squeeze, keeping purchase pricing favorable while Europe stays capital-light. Consensus next-year EPS drifts from $14.11 toward $15.50 as the beat pattern continues, and the multiple re-rates toward 9–10x as the market accepts the improvement as structural. The remaining $282.4M of buyback authority retires stock, and the elimination of 3.73M convertible shares removes a prospective diluted-EPS drag.

**Bear case ($63, −35%).** The favorable variance to model — which widened roughly 400bp year-over-year to 7.63% of collections — proves to be collections acceleration rather than incremental total collections. Recoveries above forecast normalize toward 3.6% of collections, removing roughly $4.00 per share, and the Q1 forward markups reverse into downward revisions, repeating the FY2024 pattern, when a $167.9M negative revision plus impairments produced a loss year. Carrier and consumer call filtering pushes more volume into legal collections, where costs already grew 31.2% in Q1 and court costs 36.0%, arresting the cost-to-collect improvement. Cabot fails its year-end goodwill test for a third time against $529.5M of carrying value. Rising funding costs on $1.47B of floating debt compound. At 7x a normalized $9.00, the stock trades to the low $60s, roughly where it traded in February 2026.

The bear case is the reversal of a mechanism that produced exactly this outcome two years ago, and the leverage of 3.90x debt/equity on Level 3 assets means equity absorbs the full variance.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive with identified catalysts; the risk/reward at $96.28 does not justify new capital.

**Base case target: $113** — 8.0x next-year consensus EPS of $14.11, Encore's mid-cycle forward multiple, assuming collections growth of roughly 15%, cost-to-collect in the low 40s, and no goodwill impairment. **+17.4%** from $96.28.

**Bull case: $138** (9.8x $14.11), **+43.3%.** **Bear case: $63** (7x a normalized $9.00), **−34.6%.**

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with three discrete resolution points — the August 5, 2026 Q2 print, the September 24 convertible settlement and capped-call unwind, and the year-end Cabot goodwill test.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. A Q2 or Q3 print showing cost-to-collect at or below 40% *with* recoveries above forecast at or above 6% of collections and the forward-revision slice of pretax income below 10% — evidence the over-performance is operational rather than timing.
2. Price weakness to **$78–82** on the convertible hedge-unwind supply rather than fundamental news, which would put the stock at 5.5–5.8x next-year consensus with estimates still rising.
3. The year-end goodwill test passing with a disclosed Cabot headroom cushion, removing the impairment binary against 51% of book equity.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Recoveries above forecast falling below 4% of collections in any quarter, or a negative "changes in expected future recoveries" revision — the FY2024 pattern restarting.
2. A third Cabot goodwill impairment of any size.
3. Cost-to-collect rising above 45% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the legal-channel cost inflation has overwhelmed digital gains.
4. Fixed charge coverage approaching the 2.0x covenant, or LTV disclosure indicating reduced headroom.

**Reassessment timeline:** August 5, 2026 (Q2 results, ±10.9% implied), then the Q3 print, then the Q4/full-year report containing the December 31 goodwill test. The March/June 2026 Item 5.02 disclosures should be resolved from the next proxy or 10-Q; a CFO or business-unit president change would warrant an immediate re-read of execution risk.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory reads 7.3 (May) → 6.5 (June) → 6.0 (August) → 6.1 today, with valuation down 8.5 → 6.0 and growth 8.0 → 6.5. That decline is a function of price, not deterioration: the stock rose from roughly $84 in May to $96.28, compressing the valuation gap, while fundamentals improved — next-year consensus up 9.8% over 90 days, cost-to-collect down another 350bp, leverage down 20bp, the Cabot facility repriced tighter and extended, and $230M of convertibles retired for cash rather than shares. The marginal uptick to 6.1 reflects those improvements offset by a lower risk score near the 52-week high.

Encore has a genuine operating story: absolute operating expenses fell while collections rose 39% over three years, and the resulting 700bp cost-to-collect improvement is the mechanism that supports a durable earnings base rather than a one-year snap-back. Management's revealed capital allocation has been good — buybacks averaging $42.26 in FY2025 against $96 today, no repurchases during the loss years, decelerating purchases as the price rose, and a decision to settle the convertible in cash rather than issue 3.73M shares at a high price. The CEO retains 389,613 shares and has made no open-market sales; the June insider sales were pre-arranged 10b5-1 plans adopted at ~$68 and executed to their caps, which is low-information activity.

The constraint is that 56–62% of pretax income still runs through a line whose favorable variance to management's own model has more than doubled as a share of collections, in a business levered 3.90x against Level 3 assets, with a European reporting unit that has been written down twice and faces a third test. At 7.4x current-year and 6.8x next-year consensus, the market is pricing that risk about where it has historically priced debt buyers.
