**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SSR Mining is a mid-tier precious-metals producer (~$6.0B market cap, dual-listed Nasdaq/TSX) that has spent the past two years transforming itself from a Türkiye-anchored company into a North America-weighted gold producer with a clean balance sheet. The pivot follows the February 2024 Çöpler heap-leach pad failure that killed nine workers and permanently suspended that mine. The go-forward portfolio rests on four producing assets — Marigold (Nevada), Cripple Creek & Victor (Colorado), Seabee (Saskatchewan), and Puna (Argentina) — plus the Hod Maden development JV in Türkiye and a pending $1.5B sale of the Çöpler asset to Cengiz Holding, targeted to close before the end of Q3 2026.

The continuing business is highly profitable in the current gold environment. Q1 2026 continuing-operations revenue rose 83.7% YoY to $581.8M, generating $252.5M of net income ($1.23 basic EPS) and $210.8M of free cash flow. The headline GAAP result was a -$106.5M loss, but that is entirely a $338.2M non-cash write-down of the Çöpler disposal group booked in discontinued operations. A screen keying on GAAP net income would misclassify a very profitable quarter as a loss, which is the most genuine source of information edge in the name. The balance sheet is now exceptionally clean: $634M cash, no drawn debt after the 2019 convertible notes were fully converted/redeemed in Q1, and roughly $1.5B of additional cash inflow pending from the Çöpler sale.

The case against chasing the stock here is straightforward. Virtually all earnings growth is price-driven — gold-equivalent volumes rose only ~6% while realized gold climbed 62.5% to $4,770/oz and realized silver 182.7% to $91.79/oz. The company is a high-beta call option on precious metals, with extreme operating leverage in both directions. Layered on top are an unreserved Çöpler securities-litigation tail, deal-closing risk on the sale, and asset-level deterioration at Seabee (AISC of $6,053/oz on a grade collapse to 3.0 g/t). After a ~135% twelve-month run, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate SSRM a HOLD.

## Company Overview

SSR Mining (incorporated in British Columbia) is a precious-metals producer focused on the Americas. After the strategic reset of 2024-2026, the operating company consists of four mines:

| Asset | Location | Metal | Q1'26 Revenue | Q1'26 AISC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marigold | Nevada, USA | Gold (heap leach) | $187.6M | $2,365/oz |
| CC&V | Colorado, USA | Gold (heap leach) | $186.5M | $1,658/oz |
| Seabee | Saskatchewan, Canada | Gold (underground) | $28.4M | $6,053/oz |
| Puna | Jujuy, Argentina | Silver/lead/zinc | $179.2M | $1,458/oz GE |

CC&V was acquired from Newmont, and its first full quarter as an SSR asset drove the production and revenue step-up. The portfolio is geographically diversified across two stable jurisdictions (US, Canada) plus Argentina, with each mine operating independently. The company also retains the Hod Maden development project in Türkiye (a JV with Lidya Mines, a Calik Holding subsidiary) and is selling its 80% stake in Çöpler to Cengiz Holding.

Functionally, this is a "good-bank/bad-bank" recapitalization. SSR is jettisoning its problem asset for cash while eliminating convertible debt and buying back roughly 10% of its shares. The pro-forma post-close entity is debt-free, holds well over $2B in cash, and is fully levered to the gold cycle.

## Financial Analysis

**Continuing operations are strong; the GAAP headline is distorted by the Çöpler write-down.** FY2025 revenue was approximately $1.63B (+~64% YoY), and net income attributable swung to roughly +$396M from a 2024 net loss of ~$261M (driven by Çöpler reclamation, remediation, and impairment). Q1 2026 continuing-operations operating income was $300.4M. Adjusted EBITDA (continuing) was $340.6M, with free cash flow up from $71.9M a year earlier. Diluted EPS was $1.16 against $1.23 basic.

**The driver is price, not volume.** While gold-equivalent production rose only ~6%, this is the central financial fact of the investment case: earnings are a leveraged function of the metals complex, with little organic volume growth to provide ballast if prices reverse.

**Cost inflation is real.** Consolidated continuing AISC rose to $2,433/oz GE in Q1'26 (+39% YoY). Part of this is mechanical (royalties scale with the gold price), but the asset-level detail shows genuine pressure. Seabee's AISC reflects a mill-feed grade collapse to 3.0 g/t from 9.0 g/t — barely profitable at current prices and loss-making in a meaningful gold pullback. Whether this is a temporary mine-sequencing issue or structural grade decline is the key operational question. Puna's silver grade also fell to 112 g/t from 178 g/t with a higher planned strip ratio.

**The balance sheet is now exceptionally clean.** The $229.8M 2019 convertible notes were fully converted to 13.1M shares plus redeemed in Q1, leaving SSR essentially debt-free apart from a ~$66M related-party Hod Maden loan and held-for-sale finance leases. The Altman Z-Score grey-zone reading is a capital-intensity artifact of the mining model, not a distress signal. The ~$1.5B Çöpler sale proceeds would push net cash substantially higher.

**Capital allocation has turned aggressively shareholder-friendly.** The company has an approved NCIB buyback of up to 21.5M shares (~10% of float) and repurchased 9.2M shares for $300M at an average $32.52 immediately after quarter-end. That price sits above the current quote, which can be read two ways: management overpaid into a momentum spike, or the stock has since pulled back below where the company itself was a willing buyer. No dividend has been declared in 2024, 2025, or Q1 2026, so capital return is currently 100% buyback.

## Growth Analysis

SSR's earnings inflection is unambiguous in the trailing data — four consecutive beats, with Q1'26 EPS of $1.15 against a $0.87 estimate (+31.8%), and a prior streak of +61.6%, +18.0%, and +138.1%. But the source of that inflection is the gold and silver price, not expanding output. Gold-equivalent volumes grew only ~6%, and the asset base shows reserve-replacement and mine-life questions at both Seabee (grade decline) and Marigold (low stacked grade at 0.26 g/t).

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and warrants caution against extrapolating the beat streak. Current-year EPS consensus has risen meaningfully (+12.5% over 30 days, +15.3% over 90 days to $4.61), consistent with the rising gold price. But current-quarter estimates have been cut 14.1% over 30 days, next-quarter cut 11.4% over 90 days, and next-year cut 17.3% over 30 days. This is the signature of a commodity-driven model where forward estimates track spot price assumptions rather than a durable, self-funding growth engine. The net read is a price-sensitive earnings stream where consensus is whipsawing on commodity assumptions, not the clean +30% / +30% inflection signal that would warrant a high-conviction growth call.

The genuine forward optionality lies in capital deployment of the sale proceeds. Management could pursue further buybacks (accretive if shares trade at a discount to peers), initiate a dividend, execute debt-free M&A, or advance Hod Maden — the last of which would re-introduce the Türkiye jurisdiction risk the company is exiting. None of these is committed, so the growth thesis beyond the commodity cycle is currently optionality rather than a plan.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.81, SSRM trades at ~11.4x trailing earnings. On the current-year consensus of $4.61, the forward multiple is ~6.5x — optically cheap. The standard cycle-adjustment logic argues for using forward earnings on a stock with rising estimates, and on that basis the stock screens inexpensive. The complication is that the forward EPS is built on realized gold and silver at or near record levels. A "cheap" forward multiple on peak-cycle commodity earnings is the same trap that recurs across the energy and materials complex: the low multiple is inseparable from a commodity price that is the binding variable, and it compresses violently if the price mean-reverts.

Normalizing matters here. If gold pulls back toward more conservative assumptions, the consolidated AISC leaves materially thinner margins, and Seabee turns loss-making. On a normalized rather than peak realized price, the multiple is fair rather than deeply discounted.

Against peers, the research indicates SSR still trades at a discount, with 2025 TSR of 114% lagging the peer-group TSR of 261% — a persistent Çöpler/governance discount plus relatively high consolidated AISC versus lower-cost senior producers. That discount provides some downside support and re-rating optionality if the sale closes cleanly and litigation resolves. Analyst targets cluster well above spot (low $41, high $48, mean $43.8, median $44.0), implying ~38-61% upside. Those targets reflect continued strength in the gold complex; on a stock where the earnings stream is a leveraged bet on metals prices, treating consensus targets as a floor is not appropriate — they embed the same commodity-price extrapolation as the bull case. We view valuation as fair, not asymmetric: there is a real peer discount, but it is offset by peak-cycle earnings and an unreserved litigation tail.

## Competitive Landscape

SSR is a mid-tier (~$6B) precious-metals producer operating in a record gold-price environment driven by central-bank buying and macro uncertainty. The sector backdrop is the single largest tailwind, and it is exogenous to the company.

On the positive side, the post-Çöpler company is cleaner, lower-risk, and North-America-weighted, with a fortress balance sheet and pending cash inflow that provides rare flexibility for buybacks, M&A, or dividend initiation. On the negative side, SSR remains a price-taker leveraged to the cycle, with relatively high consolidated AISC versus lower-cost seniors and limited organic volume growth. Competitive position is best described as stable-to-improving on balance-sheet strength and jurisdiction mix, but not differentiated on cost structure — the high-cost asset (Seabee) is not a moat, and Marigold's low stacked grade raises mine-life questions. The 114% vs 261% TSR gap confirms the company has rebounded strongly while still underperforming peers, the residue of the Çöpler discount.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Çöpler securities litigation (the central tail risk).** The pad failure spawned consolidated U.S. securities class actions (Colorado) and Canadian class actions (BC/Ontario) alleging misrepresentation of safety controls. The U.S. case was dismissed in September 2025 but plaintiffs were granted leave to amend; a second amended complaint was filed in November 2025 and SSR moved to dismiss again in January 2026. No liability has been accrued because management deems it not probable or estimable, which means an adverse outcome is an unreserved, unquantified overhang invisible on the balance sheet.

2. **Sale-closing risk.** The Cengiz Holding deal requires Turkish regulatory approval and customary conditions. A delay or failure would leave SSR holding a suspended, written-down asset with ongoing care-and-maintenance burn (~$21.7M/quarter direct costs) and large reclamation liabilities (~$230M+).

3. **Commodity-price leverage.** With volumes roughly flat, nearly all earnings growth is price-driven. A gold/silver reversal would compress margins quickly, especially at high-cost Seabee.

4. **CC&V / Carlton Tunnel permitting.** SSR still owes Newmont up to $87.5M contingent on the Cresson permit amendment. Newmont (with CC&V as nominal plaintiff) sued Colorado regulators in March 2026 over whether Carlton Tunnel outflows require a discharge permit; the outcome could change remediation liabilities. Closure costs above $500M are 90% Newmont's responsibility, capping but not eliminating exposure.

5. **Argentina (Puna).** ARS currency volatility and high inflation cut both ways; Q1'26 benefited from ARS strengthening.

6. **Asset concentration and depletion.** Seabee grade decline and Marigold's low stacked grade raise reserve-replacement questions.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 62.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 64.9%, leaving implied vol roughly fair (spread -2.8%) and in the 17th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is flat (62.1% near vs 60.6% far), and put/call skew is balanced at 2.3% with a put/call OI ratio of just 0.17 — no elevated put demand. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.2%. The picture is a high-vol commodity equity with no acute directional bet embedded in options positioning; the market is pricing broad, evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a specific downside catalyst. This is consistent with the HOLD: there is no options-implied stress contradicting the fundamentals, and no cheap-vol entry signal either. Short interest is low at 3.2% of float (2.1 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded short to squeeze nor informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Çöpler sale closes cleanly, lifting pro-forma cash above $2B against a debt-free balance sheet. Gold and silver hold near current levels on continued central-bank buying and macro uncertainty, sustaining the continuing-operations earnings power (FY26 consensus $4.61). Management deploys the cash into accretive buybacks at a peer discount and/or initiates a dividend, the securities litigation is dismissed for good, and the Çöpler overhang lifts. The peer discount closes as the cleaner North-America story re-rates. Stock approaches the analyst range of $41-48.

### Bear Case
Gold reverses, compressing margins across a high-AISC portfolio; Seabee turns loss-making and the consolidated cost base squeezes earnings. The Çöpler sale slips or fails Turkish approval, leaving the company with a written-down asset, ongoing quarterly burn, and reclamation liabilities. The re-pleaded U.S. securities case survives the motion to dismiss, introducing a quantifiable liability. Forward estimates, already cut 11-17% across several periods, continue to fall. Stock retraces toward the low-$20s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the balance-sheet transformation and continuing profitability are genuinely strong, but the earnings stream is a leveraged commodity bet, the litigation tail is unreserved, and the stock has already run ~135% in twelve months, leaving balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Çöpler sale closes on terms with proceeds received, and management commits the proceeds to a defined, shareholder-friendly use (continued buybacks at a discount to peers or dividend initiation) rather than re-entering Türkiye via Hod Maden.
- Seabee demonstrates grade/cost stabilization over two consecutive quarters (AISC back toward the consolidated average), removing the marginal-asset concern.
- Final dismissal of the U.S. securities class action, removing the unreserved tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Çöpler sale failure or material delay beyond Q3 2026, leaving the written-down asset and ongoing burn on the books.
- A sustained gold reversal that pushes consolidated continuing AISC margins below comfortable coverage, with forward estimates continuing to fall.
- The re-pleaded securities case surviving the motion to dismiss with an estimable, accrued liability.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the targeted Q3 2026 sale close are the natural reassessment points. Monitor monthly gold/silver realized prices and Seabee cost trajectory.

## Conclusion

SSR Mining has executed a credible recapitalization: it is shedding its problem asset, has retired its convertible debt, sits on substantial cash with more coming, and is returning capital aggressively through buybacks. The continuing business generated $1.23 of EPS and $210.8M of free cash flow in Q1 2026, and the GAAP loss that headline screens will catch is a one-time non-cash Çöpler write-down. That distortion is the most genuine information edge in the name.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is that the earnings power is almost entirely a function of record gold and silver prices, with flat volumes, a high and rising cost base (Seabee is the clearest warning), an unreserved securities-litigation tail, and sale-closing risk — all after a ~135% twelve-month advance. The forward multiple looks cheap only because it capitalizes peak-cycle commodity earnings, and the analyst targets that imply substantial upside embed the same price assumption. Estimate revisions are mixed: current-year up, but several forward periods cut 11-17%. The risk/reward is balanced, the peer discount provides some support, and the catalysts (sale close, litigation resolution, capital deployment) are real but not yet de-risked. We would become buyers on a clean sale close with a defined cash-deployment plan, or on a pullback that re-establishes asymmetry. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
