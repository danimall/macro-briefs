**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Choice Hotels is a pure-play, asset-light hotel franchisor with roughly 7,575 open hotels and 656,825 rooms across 22 brands, generating about 28% operating margins on a fee-for-service model. The headline financials look stable (FY2025 revenue of $1,596.8M, +0.8%, and reported net income of $369.9M), but the quality of those earnings is poor. Strip out a one-time, non-taxable $100M gain on the remeasurement of the Choice Hotels Canada joint venture and underlying net income fell to roughly $270M from $299.7M in FY2024. Operating income declined 3.3% to $448.4M and operating cash flow fell to $270.4M from $319.4M. The franchise engine that drives the equity story is contracting.

The core problem is the domestic system. U.S. RevPAR fell 3.0% in 2025 (after -1.2% in 2024), and the U.S. room count shrank 2.9%, a net loss of 141 hotels, pulling U.S. royalty fees down $14.9M. The flagship Comfort and Quality Inn brands are losing units in the most commoditized, economically sensitive chain scales. Franchisee financial stress is showing through in the receivables: the allowance for credit losses jumped to $62.3M in Q1 2026 from $51.2M at year-end, FY25 bad-debt provisions ran about $35M, and $42.9M of franchisee notes receivable are more than 90 days past due or in non-accrual. Q1 2026 was a clean miss: adjusted EPS of $1.07 versus $1.33 consensus (-19.7%), with the stock falling about 14% on the print.

The decisive recent event is the leadership vacuum. On May 20, 2026, Patrick Pacious stepped down as CEO effective immediately after nine years in the role, with no permanent successor named. Interim CEO Dominic Dragisich was then a net seller of about $3.2M of stock on May 26. An abrupt, successorless departure immediately after the earnings miss, combined with negative "compensation actually paid" and 2025 TSR lagging the peer group, points to board dissatisfaction with the core-brand trajectory. At ~14.5x forward earnings the stock screens cheap against a peer median near 30x, but that discount is earned by shrinking units, falling estimates, and execution uncertainty. We rate CHH AVOID.

## Company Overview

Choice Hotels operates one of the largest hotel franchising platforms globally, with a portfolio spanning economy to upper-upscale: Comfort (upper-midscale), Quality Inn (midscale), extended-stay brands (WoodSpring, Everhome, MainStay, Suburban), upscale (Cambria, Ascend Collection), and the Radisson Americas brands acquired in 2022. Revenue is overwhelmingly U.S.-concentrated and driven by royalty fees (typically 5–6% of franchisees' gross room revenue), marketing and reservation fees, partnership and co-brand credit-card fees, and a small owned-and-managed hotel segment (17 owned, 13 managed).

The economic model is attractive in the abstract: incremental franchises carry low marginal cost, and the loyalty program (Choice Privileges, 74M+ members) plus brand recognition create switching costs for franchisees. The structural weakness is portfolio mix. Choice skews toward lower chain scales, the segments most exposed to value-conscious and lower-income travelers, who have been pulling back. The company competes for franchisees against better-capitalized Marriott, Hilton, Wyndham, and IHG, and the recent unit losses in Comfort and Quality suggest share erosion toward those brands and toward independents.

## Financial Analysis

The franchise core is flat-to-declining underneath a flattering top line. Core franchise and management fees were roughly flat at $673.2M (FY25) versus $669.6M (FY24). Ex-reimbursable core revenue sits near $980M. The 19.0% effective tax rate was flattered by the non-taxable Canada item versus 24.3% in FY24; the company bought the other half of the Canada JV in July 2025 for ~$114.5M.

Margins on a franchisor basis remain rich (~28% operating, ~23% net, ROA ~12.7%), which is why the business is not a value trap in the classic sense — it still throws off cash. The trajectory is negative.

The balance-sheet optics require explicit handling. Altman Z of 1.16 ("distress"), D/E of 15.1x, and a current ratio of 0.87 are artifacts of an asset-light franchisor that has repurchased so much stock that book equity is near zero ($181.2M at YE25, negative -$45.3M at YE24). This is financial engineering, not insolvency risk. Total leverage is a manageable 2.86x at YE25, rising to 3.04x in Q1 2026, against a 4.5x covenant, and the company is investment-grade and in compliance. Debt of about $2.0B is mostly fixed-rate unsecured notes (3.7% 2019/2020 notes, 5.85% 2024 notes) plus a ~$566M variable revolver at 4.93%. The genuine concern is the shrinking royalty base, not the capital structure.

Two earnings-quality watch items: cumulative treasury stock stands at $2.59B, so the equity figure understates the cash deployed into buybacks; and $642–667M is tied up in Cambria/Everhome owned hotels, JVs, loans and guaranties, which is real-estate and credit exposure a pure franchisor would not normally carry. Management guides that this capital intensity will decline as those brands reach scale, which would restore a cleaner asset-light profile and free cash flow if delivered.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is weak and concentrated in a few offsets that do not outweigh the core erosion. U.S. RevPAR declined on ADR -1.6% and occupancy -80bps; the U.S. room count fell to 496,979 rooms. Economy brands are shrinking fast (Econo Lodge -6.7%, Park Inn -44%). Q1 2026 was worse on the headline: GAAP EPS collapsed to $0.44 from $0.94 and operating income fell to $60.0M from $79.9M, though the prior-year quarter carried a roughly +410bp hurricane benefit that exaggerates the year-over-year decline.

The offsets: the average effective royalty rate is rising (5.06%→5.14% in FY25, 5.22% in Q1'26), international is growing (+130 hotels and +$11.5M royalty to $41.3M, helped by the Canada consolidation), partnership/co-brand credit-card fees rose $14.3M, and extended stay is expanding (WoodSpring +10.9%, Everhome scaling off a small base). These are real but insufficient to reverse a contracting domestic unit base.

Forward earnings momentum is negative, the more decisive indicator than any single quarter. Current-year EPS estimates have fallen to $7.10 from $7.26 ninety days ago and $7.20 thirty days ago; next-year estimates fell to $7.67 from $7.74 ninety days ago. There is no estimate-revision inflection here; consensus is grinding lower, the opposite of the rising-estimate signal that would justify forward-multiple generosity. The earnings track record reinforces low visibility: two misses and one in-line in the last four quarters, capped by the -19.7% Q1 2026 miss.

## Valuation Assessment

At $109.56, CHH trades at about 14.5x trailing and roughly 15.4x the FY26 consensus of $7.10. Against a peer median P/E near 30.8 (MTN 31.2, WH 30.8, HGV 27.8, TNL 20.6), the stock looks deeply discounted. The discount is earned, not a mispricing.

The forward-multiple generosity that applies to inflection stocks does not apply here, because there is no inflection. Estimate revisions are negative across every horizon, revenue growth is sub-1%, and the unit base is shrinking. The conditions for treating a low multiple as an opportunity — rising estimates, accelerating revenue, a specific catalyst — are absent. What we have instead is a compressed multiple reflecting a structurally challenged core, an open-ended CEO search, and falling earnings power once the Canada gain is stripped out.

The analyst price-target band ($86 low, $114 median, $128 high, $112.53 mean) brackets the current price tightly, consistent with a market that sees roughly fair value with two-sided risk, not asymmetric upside. The analyst recommendation distribution is bearish for a stable franchisor: of 16 ratings, 8 hold, 3 sell, and 2 strong sell, with only 1 strong buy and 2 buys. Five outright negative ratings is unusual and aligns with the weak fundamentals.

On a sum-of-parts basis the franchise engine deserves a premium to most lodging owners/operators because of its margin and capital profile, which is why we do not score valuation below 4: the cash flow is genuine and the 1.1% dividend (~$53M, well covered) plus $175–225M of guided buybacks provide some downside support. But the multiple is not cheap enough to compensate for a falling royalty base and the leadership vacuum. We score Valuation 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Choice's competitive position is deteriorating in its core. The U.S. room contraction and unit losses in Comfort and Quality indicate share loss to better-capitalized Marriott and Hilton brands and to independents. The franchisee value proposition (lower-cost flags for value-segment operators) is under pressure precisely when those operators are most financially stretched, which feeds back into unit attrition. Morningstar's framing that the next CEO will be "tasked with revitalizing existing brands" is a direct acknowledgment that the flagship brands need fixing.

The structural advantages remain (scale, loyalty program, asset-light operating leverage), and the rising royalty rate shows pricing power on the franchisees that remain. Extended-stay (WoodSpring) and international are genuine bright spots where Choice is gaining or holding. But the trajectory in the largest segment, domestic midscale and economy, is negative, and trajectory outweighs level. A franchisor losing rooms is, by definition, shrinking its royalty annuity. We score Competitive Position 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is the combination of a shrinking domestic system and a leaderless transition. The departure came against a backdrop of negative "compensation actually paid" and 2025 TSR (92.81) lagging the peer group (129.35), injecting strategic and execution uncertainty at the worst possible moment.

Franchisee credit deterioration is the concrete canary, and the receivables will worsen if RevPAR stays soft, accelerating unit attrition; Q1 2026 write-offs ran $29.6M. Secondary risks include OTA/AI booking disintermediation (newly emphasized in the risk factors), refinancing of the $400M 2029 notes and revolver at higher rates, and the $642–667M of Cambria/Everhome real-estate and loan exposure that carries credit risk a pure franchisor would not normally hold.

The Bainum family's concentrated ownership (Chairman Stewart Bainum Jr., age 79) is an explicit anti-takeover/control factor, amplified by ongoing buybacks. This limits the probability of a strategic-acquisition floor under the stock. The failed hostile pursuit of Wyndham (terminated March 2024, ~$38.5M+ in costs plus an $8.3M loss on shares bought for the bid) is a reminder of past capital-allocation missteps.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 48.6% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 36.0% (rich by 12.6 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (48.6% at one week versus 35.6% at three months), so the market expects a near-dated catalyst. The 1-week put/call skew of 19.9% indicates elevated put demand. This is consistent with a market hedging downside around the leadership transition and the next earnings update rather than pricing a recovery. The richness of near-term vol also makes options-based long entry expensive. The prior IV snapshot of 1036.6% was a data artifact; the current 48.6% is the meaningful read.

Short interest is the key two-sided risk: 35.4% of float is short with 10.5 days to cover. That is a structural-deterioration signal that many informed investors share the bearish view, but it also creates genuine squeeze risk on any positive surprise such as a credible permanent CEO appointment, a RevPAR stabilization, or a guidance reaffirmation. This squeeze potential is the primary reason we recommend avoiding rather than actively shorting.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($128):** The Q1 framing as "an inflection point toward rooms growth, RevPAR improvement and lower capital intensity" proves correct. Capital intensity falls as Cambria/Everhome scale, freeing cash flow for the guided $175–225M buyback against a 45M share count, which is meaningfully accretive at a depressed multiple. A credible permanent CEO is appointed and stabilizes Comfort/Quality. Rising royalty rates, international growth, and co-brand fees offset the U.S. softness, and adjusted EBITDA hits the high end of the $632–647M guide. The 35% short base covers into improving fundamentals, driving a re-rating toward the peer cohort. This is roughly $128, near the analyst high.

**Bear Case ($86):** U.S. RevPAR stays negative, the room count keeps shrinking, and franchisee bad-debt provisions accelerate, compounding the royalty erosion. The CEO search drags or produces an outsider who initiates a strategic reset (brand pruning, write-downs). Estimates continue falling and the stock de-rates toward the analyst low of $86, about 12x a falling earnings number. This is roughly -21% from current.

The asymmetry tilts negative because the bear case requires only a continuation of current trends, while the bull case requires a leadership resolution and an operating inflection that the data do not yet support.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The identifiable catalysts are the next earnings report (likely late July 2026), which will test whether U.S. RevPAR and the room count stabilize, and any 8-K announcing a permanent CEO. Both are genuine but two-sided: a confirmed turnaround narrative or a strong CEO appointment would be a short-squeeze trigger rather than a downside catalyst.

**Timing window:** The next earnings print is roughly 6–8 weeks out; the CEO search has no fixed resolution date. Neither is a clean dated downside event.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 35.4% of float short with 10.5 days to cover, the position is well above the 25% threshold at which squeeze risk dominates. The bearish thesis is already heavily crowded.

**Borrow and options signal:** The options market confirms near-term bearish positioning, but elevated borrow cost and the crowded short base raise the carry and the squeeze hazard. The high IV makes put-based expressions expensive.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Downside to the bear case of $86 is about -21%; upside on a squeeze or CEO/RevPAR surprise could exceed +15–20% given the 35% short float. That is below a 2:1 reward/risk, and the squeeze asymmetry is unfavorable.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The fundamental thesis (shrinking core, leadership vacuum, falling estimates) is sound, but at 35.4% short interest and 10.5 days to cover, the remaining downside is not worth the squeeze risk. Avoid owning it; do not press it short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the AVOID):**
- A credible permanent CEO appointment combined with a stabilization in U.S. RevPAR (a return to roughly flat or positive year-over-year) and a halt to net U.S. room losses, confirmed over two consecutive quarters.
- A genuine estimate-revision inflection — current-year consensus rising rather than falling — alongside evidence that franchisee bad-debt provisions and past-due notes are peaking (AR allowance declining from the $62.3M Q1'26 level).
- A multiple compression to roughly 11–12x forward earnings (~$86) on stabilized estimates would provide enough absolute valuation support to revisit, given the cash flow and buyback profile.

**Timeline:** The core operating headwinds (soft value-segment lodging demand, unit attrition) are likely to persist through at least the next two to three quarters, and the leadership uncertainty until a permanent CEO is named and lays out a credible plan. We do not expect a resolution before late 2026.

## Conclusion

Choice Hotels is a structurally high-margin franchisor whose core franchise engine is contracting. The reported FY2025 net income overstates the health of the business: strip out the Canada gain and underlying earnings fell, while RevPAR declined, the U.S. room count shrank, and franchisee credit metrics deteriorated. The abrupt, successorless CEO departure immediately after the earnings miss signals board dissatisfaction with the core-brand trajectory, and consensus estimates are grinding lower across every horizon. The ~14.5x forward multiple is a discount to peers near 30x, but it is earned by genuine fundamental erosion rather than a mispricing, and the analyst rating distribution (five negative ratings against three positive) reflects that.

The balance sheet is not the risk; the "distress" screen is a buyback artifact, and leverage is a manageable 3.0x against a 4.5x covenant. The risk is the slow erosion of the royalty annuity combined with execution uncertainty at the top. With 35% of the float already short and meaningful squeeze risk, the name is neither ownable nor a clean short. We rate CHH **AVOID** and would revisit only on a confirmed RevPAR/unit stabilization paired with a credible permanent CEO and a positive estimate-revision turn.
