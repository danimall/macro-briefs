## Executive Summary

Deckers presents two investment cases inside one ticker, and they point in opposite directions. The balance sheet and cash conversion are close to best-in-class for consumer discretionary: zero drawn funded debt, $1,602.6M of cash at June 30, 2026, an Altman Z-score of 4.64, FY2026 operating cash flow of $1,182.0M against net income of $1,024.1M (1.15x conversion), capex of only $84.6M, and free cash flow of $1,097.3M — a 20.1% FCF margin on 41.0% ROE. At $97.46 that is an 8.3% FCF yield on equity and 9.4% on an enterprise value of roughly $11.67B. The board added $3.5B to the repurchase authorization on May 20, 2026, leaving $4,679M available as of July 9, 2026, equal to 35.2% of current market capitalization, and the share count has already fallen 8.97% across five quarters (150.2M → 136.7M).

The operating trajectory is deteriorating on every filing-verified metric. Net sales growth has gone 18.2% (FY2024) → 16.3% (FY2025) → 9.8% (FY2026) → 5.7% (Q1 FY2027, 4.8% constant currency). Domestic net sales, 58.3% of the base, grew 0.2% in FY2026. Units sold fell 1.4% in the June quarter while revenue per unit rose 7.1%, so recent growth is price and mix rather than volume. HOKA segment operating income grew 0.8% on 7.7% sales growth and UGG segment operating income grew 0.1% on 4.9% sales growth; consolidated operating income fell 6.0% and net income fell 6.6%, with reported EPS up 1.1% only because of the buyback. FY2026 full-year segment margins compressed in both brands (HOKA −279bps, UGG −145bps), so this is a two-period pattern rather than a single quarter of noise.

We maintain HOLD, our fourth consecutive, with the overall score moving from 6.2 to 5.9. The score decline reflects confirmed operating deterioration in the July 30 10-Q, not the 4% price move since our last review. The recommendation is unchanged because the two forces roughly offset: at 13.1x guided FY2027 EPS and 11.6x FY2028 consensus, with a debt-free balance sheet and a buyback capable of retiring 6-7% of shares annually, downside asymmetry is not clearly unfavorable. Management's own guidance implies FY2027 net income declines, and a low multiple on possibly peak earnings is the textbook consumer-discretionary value trap. We want the October quarter, which the sell-side has identified as the HOKA back-to-school test, before changing the rating in either direction.

## Company Overview

Deckers designs and markets footwear, apparel and accessories through three reportable segments: HOKA, UGG, and Other brands (primarily Teva). The portfolio has been narrowed: Sanuk was sold in August 2024, and standalone operations for Koolaburra and AHNU were phased out during the third and fourth quarters of FY2026, driving Other brands net sales down 33.9% to $146.2M. FY2026 revenue of $5,472.3M was 50.0% UGG ($2,738.8M), 47.3% HOKA ($2,587.3M) and 2.7% residual.

The model is fully outsourced and asset-light. Independent third-party contractors manufacture all products, predominantly in Vietnam and Indonesia, with less than 5% from China or any other single country. PP&E is only $337.8M against $3,687.8M of total assets. Channel mix in FY2026 was 58.6% wholesale ($3,208.1M) and 41.4% DTC ($2,264.2M), with 212 company-owned stores at June 30, 2026 (144 UGG, 68 HOKA) versus 191 a year earlier; essentially all net store growth is HOKA. International was 41.7% of FY2026 sales, up from 36.1%.

No customer exceeded 10% of FY2026 net sales, but one customer represented 18.5% of net trade accounts receivable, which the 10-K notes are generally unsecured.

## Financial Analysis

**Gross margin is holding; operating margin is not.** Gross margin was 57.7% in FY2026, down just 20bps despite tariffs, and rose 60bps to 56.4% in Q1 FY2027 on favorable channel and product mix, full-price UGG selling, and FX, partially offset by incremental tariffs. That is real pricing power.

Everything below the gross line is moving the wrong way. SG&A grew 11.0% in FY2026 against 9.8% revenue growth, and 12.7% in Q1 FY2027 against 5.7% revenue growth — a 700bp gap. Q1 drivers were payroll +$12.5M, other SG&A (IT, commissions) +$11.8M, advertising and promotion +$10.3M, rent and occupancy +$8.4M on the HOKA store build, and FX remeasurement losses +$5.8M. Operating margin fell from 17.1% to 15.2%.

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | Q1 FY26 | Q1 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 55.6% | 57.9% | 57.7% | 55.8% | 56.4% |
| SG&A % sales | 34.0% | 34.3% | 34.6% | 38.7% | 41.2% |
| Operating margin | 21.6% | 23.6% | 23.1% | 17.1% | 15.2% |

Segment detail is the clearest bear evidence in the file. HOKA operating margin went 38.0% (FY2025) → 35.2% (FY2026) → 36.3% in Q1 FY2027 versus 38.8% prior-year; UGG went 39.6% → 38.2% full year, and 19.4% versus 20.4% in the seasonally depressed June quarter. Segment operating income growth of +0.8% (HOKA) and +0.1% (UGG) on 7.7% and 4.9% sales growth means operating leverage has inverted. Roughly $5.8M of the Q1 SG&A increase was non-cash FX remeasurement; excluding it, consolidated operating income would have declined roughly 2.5% rather than 6.0%. That softens but does not reverse the read.

**Cash flow quality is genuinely high, and the June quarter must not be annualized.** Q1 FY2027 operating cash flow of $47.9M reflects the seasonal UGG inventory build: inventory consumed $320.6M within the quarter and payables supplied $341.6M, with the balance sheet inventory line rising from $487.0M at March 31 to $807.6M at June 30. The year-ago comparable quarter generated $36.1M, so cash generation was 32.5% better year over year. The normalized reference is the FY2026 actual of $1,182.0M.

**Balance sheet.** Current ratio 3.54; $400M primary revolver undrawn with $399.4M available and only $0.6M of letters of credit; China facility undrawn; in compliance with all covenants at both March 31 and June 30. Of the $1,602.6M cash at June 30, $418.5M sits offshore, down from $653.9M after a $250M repatriation of previously-taxed earnings during the quarter, positioning cash onshore to fund domestic repurchases. Off-balance-sheet purchase obligations were $1,374.3M at March 31; undiscounted operating lease payments were $436.6M plus $22.7M signed but not commenced, with operating lease assets jumping from $335.1M to $432.5M in Q1 on $120.1M of additions (versus $45.3M prior year) tied to the Moreno Valley distribution center extension and the retail build.

Two screening artifacts warrant correcting because they materially misstate the company. First, a widely-circulated "debt/equity 1.48" figure equals total assets divided by equity ($3,687.8M / $2,499.6M = 1.475), an equity multiplier. True total-liabilities-to-equity is 0.475 and funded debt is zero. Second, some XBRL extracts label FY2026 pre-tax income ($1,326.4M) as net income; actual net income was $1,024.1M, corroborated by the proxy's pay-versus-performance table. A mechanical screen using the raw fields would overstate earnings by roughly 30% and call a debt-free company levered.

**Capital allocation.** No dividend; 100% of return is repurchase. FY2026 financing outflow was $1,084.0M versus $581.3M in FY2025. Q1 FY2027 repurchases were 3,258,352 shares at a weighted average $103.79 ($338.2M), plus 311,264 shares at $103.35 through July 9. Those average prices sit 6% above the current $97.46, so recent repurchases are modestly underwater — not a governance problem, but a reminder that management's price judgment has not been perfect. Cash deployed into buybacks destroys interest income, which a naive share-count model misses. FY2026 other income, net was $63.5M; Q1 FY2027 interest income already fell to $15.9M from $18.7M. A meaningful slice of the arithmetic EPS accretion is offset.

## Growth Analysis

The deceleration is monotonic and management is guiding to more of it. Initial FY2027 revenue guidance of $5.86–5.91B implies +7.1% to +8.0% off the FY2026 base, and Q1 delivered +5.7%. EPS guidance was raised in July to $7.35–7.50 from $7.30–7.45.

Run the guidance through the share count. FY2026 diluted EPS of $7.02 on $1,024.1M of net income implies ~145.9M average diluted shares. If average diluted shares fall to roughly 135M in FY2027 (consistent with 136.7M outstanding at June 30 and continued repurchase), the $7.425 guidance midpoint implies net income of about $1,002M, a 2% decline from FY2026. **Management's own guidance embeds a net income decline with EPS growth manufactured by the buyback.** Consensus of $7.53 for the current year sits just above the guided high end, and $8.40 for next year implies roughly 5% net income growth on a further reduced share count.

Three specific soft spots deserve weight:

1. **Domestic demand.** FY2026 domestic net sales grew 0.2% ($3,191.5M vs $3,186.7M) on 58.3% of revenue; Q1 improved to +3.2%. Part of the FY2026 flatness is the US-concentrated wind-down of Koolaburra, AHNU and Teva's value-wholesale exit, which the filings do not separate by brand, so underlying domestic HOKA/UGG growth was likely positive. Even granting that, the consolidated growth rate materially overstates the health of the majority of the business.

2. **Volume versus price.** FY2026 units grew 6.2% against 9.8% revenue (+3.3% per unit); Q1 units fell 1.4% against +5.7% revenue (+7.1% per unit). The MD&A credits "strategic price increases" for margin defense. The prior-year unit base includes phased-out low-price brands, which mechanically depresses reported unit growth, and the company does not disclose units excluding them, so the −1.4% cannot be cleanly interpreted. Directionally, Deckers is converting brand equity into price rather than units, which protects gross margin but is a finite lever into a softening discretionary environment.

3. **HOKA wholesale.** HOKA wholesale grew 2.9% ($446.8M vs $434.2M) and HOKA total grew 7.7%, down from 19.8% in the year-ago June quarter. Management attributes part of the international wholesale weakness to planned shipment timing from the prior-period European 3PL transition, which if accurate should compress the comparison in later quarters. HOKA DTC grew 17.3%, but company-owned HOKA stores rose from 48 to 68 (+41.7%), so DTC growth is partly purchased with rent.

The offsets are real. International grew 26.8% in FY2026 to 41.7% of sales, and the company has beaten consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters (surprises of +5.7%, +13.4%, +18.1%, +12.8%). Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current year +0.6% and next year +0.8% over 30 days. But the current-quarter estimate was cut 5.4% over 30 days to $1.79 while the full year rose, which shifts the earnings burden into the December holiday quarter (consensus $3.72). That raises execution risk rather than reducing it.

There is no +30%-in-90-days revision inflection here, and no revenue acceleration. The growth signal is a decelerating mid-single-digit top line with declining net income.

## Valuation Assessment

Derived from the frozen $97.46 basis and the June 30 balance sheet:

- Market capitalization **$13.27B**; cash $1,602.6M; funded debt zero → **EV ≈ $11.67B**
- **13.9x** trailing FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $7.02
- **13.1x** the FY2027 guidance midpoint of $7.425; **12.9x** consensus $7.53
- **11.6x** FY2028 consensus of $8.40
- **9.2x** EV/FY2026 operating income of $1,262.9M (9.6x capitalizing $472.3M of operating leases)
- **10.6x** EV/FY2026 FCF → **9.4% FCF yield on EV, 8.3% on market cap**
- Repurchase authorization remaining **35.2%** of market capitalization

Peer comparison is close to unusable as presented. The Footwear median P/E of 43.0 is meaningless: Crocs prints a negative multiple, and Steven Madden's 43x sits on a depressed earnings base. The only clean comparable is Wolverine World Wide at 14.8x, and DECK trades at a discount to it despite 41% ROE, a 20% FCF margin, zero funded debt, and $5.5B of revenue. That is the affirmative case for mispricing: the market is paying a lower-quality-peer multiple for a materially higher-quality balance sheet and franchise.

The counter-case is straightforward. A 13x multiple on a company where both core brands generated approximately zero operating profit growth in the most recent quarter, and where guidance embeds a net income decline, may be a correct multiple on peak earnings rather than a cheap multiple on trend earnings. The proxy's cumulative TSR index fell from 284.9 (FY2024) to 181.8 (FY2026), −36.2%, while net income rose from $759.6M to $1,024.1M. The market has repriced the growth rate, not the earnings. Whether that repricing is complete depends on whether FY2027-28 earnings hold, and the segment data does not yet establish that they will.

We treat the cheapness as necessary but not sufficient. Total-return arithmetic sets a floor: at an 8.3% equity FCF yield largely directed at repurchase, the share count can fall 6-7% annually, so even flat net income produces mid-to-high single-digit EPS growth. Hold the multiple and you earn roughly that. Compress the multiple to 11x on evidence of brand maturity and you lose 15-20% before earnings even fall.

Consensus targets: low $85, mean $122.81, median $120, high $184, with sentiment improving (5 strong buy / 8 buy / 11 hold / 2 sell, up from 4/7/13/2 three months ago) and a Jefferies upgrade to Buy on July 13. At 13x forward, DECK is not a premium-multiple stock, so the median $120 target (14.3x FY2028 consensus) is a reasonable reference, but it requires the FY2028 net income recovery that Q1 segment data does not support yet.

**Our base case is $108** — approximately 13.5x FY2028 consensus $8.40 discounted for one year, cross-checked as 14.3x FY2027 guidance midpoint. That is +10.8% from spot. Bull $140 (16.7x $8.40, +43.6%); bear $75 (11.5x a depressed $6.50, −23.0%). Probability-weighting roughly 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear yields an expected return near +9%, positive but not asymmetric enough for ACCUMULATE given the width of the distribution.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K is unusually candid for a company posting record results. It states that "barriers to entry have been reduced by access to offshore manufacturing and evolving technologies," that "the growth and visibility of our HOKA brand and UGG brand have attracted competitors specifically targeting the categories in which we operate," and that the company "has experienced, and expects to continue to experience, pricing and promotional pressure across our brands and channels." Morningstar assigns a narrow moat on brand intangible; the company's own risk language describes a contestable position.

Externally, HOKA and On Holding are the two share gainers against Nike in performance running. HOKA's growth came from an incumbent's share, and the source is now contested by a similarly-positioned insurgent pushing hard on innovation rather than by a slow-moving incumbent alone. Consumer-press anecdotes about HOKA loyalists trading down to Brooks, Asics and New Balance at $140-160 price points are low-quality evidence and we do not weight them, but they are thematically consistent with two things the filings do establish: the 10-K's warning that consumers may "trade down to lower-priced alternatives," and the Q1 combination of −1.4% units with +7.1% revenue per unit.

The intuitive risk ranking has partially inverted. UGG — the mature, more fashion-cyclical brand — delivered the better recent margin performance (Q1 segment gross margin +230bps to 54.9% on full-price selling), while HOKA showed the sharper growth deceleration (15.9% FY2026 → 7.7% Q1) and the larger segment margin decline. HOKA is now the asset requiring proof.

International remains the clearest structural runway at 41.7% of sales, but it decelerated to +8.4% in Q1, partly on the disclosed European 3PL timing distortion.

One discipline point: the shift from 36.1% to 41.7% international is geographic diversification, not reduced customer concentration. On the direct measure, one customer still represents 18.5% of unsecured net receivables.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs, two-sided.** The Q1 10-Q discloses that the Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, the Court of International Trade ordered CBP refunds in March, and Deckers began filing CAPE Phase 2 refund claims after June 30 against approximately $120M of previously-paid IEEPA tariffs. Under ASC 450 gain-contingency accounting, no receivable has been recognized. Sizing this as a sensitivity: $120M gross is 2.19% of FY2026 revenue; at the 22.8% FY2026 effective rate a full recovery would be roughly $92.6M, or about $0.67 per diluted share. The company itself lists three haircuts — cost-sharing accommodations owed to independent manufacturers, income taxes on refunds, and other factors — so net realization is plausibly far below gross and is not forecastable. Simultaneously the forward burden rose: the FY2027 tariff assumption was reportedly lifted to 12.5% (externally reported, not verified against the company's earnings release), and management states mitigation will not fully offset incremental tariffs this year.

**Two consumer class actions on tariff pricing.** The 10-Q discloses two purported consumer class actions "relating to alleged tariff-related pricing actions and potential governmental tariff reimbursements," with no damages estimate, jurisdiction, accrual or reserve disclosed. The economic structure is unusual: CBP pays Deckers, Deckers owes contractual accommodations to manufacturers, and plaintiff consumers assert a claim on the same pool. Magnitude is unknown, which is why it is not priced.

**Sheepskin concentration.** Sheepskin is used in a substantial portion of UGG products (50% of revenue), sourced primarily from Australia and "processed largely by two tanneries in China" meeting quality, volume and animal-welfare standards. No mitigation is disclosed. The 10-K separately flags opposition to animal-derived materials and potential legislation restricting sales.

**Manufacturing geography.** Less than 5% from China is a successful de-risking, but the result is concentration in Vietnam and Indonesia rather than diversification.

**Trademark.** Claims that "ugg" is generic succeeded in Australia and were rejected in the US, China, Türkiye and the Netherlands. An adverse precedent already exists in one jurisdiction for a brand producing half of revenue.

**Macro.** Deckers is premium-priced discretionary footwear holding price into a softening US consumer. Consumer sentiment was reported at a 12-month low around the July print. The company's current growth formula — price over volume — is the formula most exposed to a discretionary downturn.

**Operational.** One international 3PL transition is in flight, and a prior European 3PL transition already distorts HOKA wholesale comparisons.

**Not risks:** financial leverage (zero funded debt, covenants clean, $399.4M revolver available) and refinancing (nothing to refinance). Short interest of 6.1% of float with 2.6 days to cover indicates no crowded bearish positioning and no squeeze mechanics in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 32.2% sits well below 44.4% trailing one-year realized volatility (an 8th-percentile reading within the current chain), and ATM IV collapsed from 156.5% on July 22 to 32.2% following the July 23 print, a standard post-event vol crush. The term structure is in contango (32.2% at one week to 42.5% at six months), skew is call-heavy at the near tenors per the data provider's convention, and the put/call open-interest ratio is 0.92, essentially neutral and little changed from 0.88 three weeks ago. The nearest expiry prices a ±3.6% move; the three-month tenor ±10.2% and six-month ±19.2%.

Near-term positioning is not defensive and contains no signal of informed bearish accumulation, while longer-dated vol at 42.5% prices genuine uncertainty around the holiday quarter, consistent with our view that the decisive evidence arrives in the October and January prints. For a holder, cheap near-term vol makes covered-call overwriting unattractive and makes longer-dated protection relatively expensive; for a prospective buyer, low near-term IV is a mildly favorable entry condition rather than a thesis input. No divergence with our fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $140, +43.6%)

The Q1 deceleration is substantially artifact. HOKA international wholesale weakness reflects the prior-period European 3PL shipment timing that management identifies, which reverses in later quarters. The −1.4% unit decline is the mechanical residue of pruning low-price Koolaburra, AHNU and Teva value wholesale, and underlying HOKA/UGG units grew. Gross margin at 56.4% and rising proves pricing power intact; the SG&A surge is front-loaded investment in 20 new HOKA stores, the US loyalty program and IT that annualizes into leverage during FY2028. Back-to-school HOKA sell-through validates the product diversification thesis behind the July Jefferies upgrade.

On that path, FY2028 EPS reaches or exceeds consensus $8.40 while the share count falls another 6-7%, the tariff refund lands at some meaningful fraction of $92.6M after tax as unmodeled upside, and the multiple re-rates from 13x toward the mid-teens as the market re-underwrites durability. Sixteen-plus times $8.40 gets to $140. The balance sheet makes the wait cheap: zero funded debt, $1.6B cash, and a 35%-of-cap authorization that mechanically compounds per-share value at any price near current levels.

### Bear Case (target $75, −23.0%)

The deceleration is brand maturity plus competitive entry, and the price lever is nearly exhausted. HOKA's 15.9% → 7.7% path with a 279bp segment margin decline is consistent with a brand transitioning from land-grab to defense against On, and management's own risk factors concede that success attracted competitors and that pricing pressure is being experienced now. Domestic sales, 58% of the base, grew 0.2% in FY2026 and 3.2% in Q1 into a softening consumer with sentiment at a 12-month low. When price increases stop clearing, both volume and price compress at once, and gross margin follows.

SG&A that grew 12.7% on 5.7% revenue is not obviously reversible: rent on 68 HOKA stores is fixed, and the compensation plan pays on absolute revenue, operating income and pre-tax income with no ROIC or relative-TSR measure, which structurally rewards buying top-line growth with marketing and store spend. FY2027 net income declines per guidance; if FY2028 flattens rather than recovering, EPS lands near $6.50 and a decelerating discretionary name trades at 11-12x, or $72-78 — within reach of the $78.91 52-week low. The two tariff-pricing class actions could convert the $120M asset into a liability of unknown size. In that scenario the buyback is spending $1B+ per year to shrink a shrinking earnings stream, and destroying $60M+ of pre-tax interest income while doing it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (fourth consecutive). Existing holders should maintain exposure for the cash generation and the buyback; new capital has no urgent entry signal at 13.1x forward when the guided earnings direction is down.

**Base case $108 (+10.8%)** — 13.5x FY2028 consensus $8.40 discounted one year, cross-checked at 14.3x the $7.425 FY2027 guidance midpoint. **Bull $140 (+43.6%)** at 16.7x $8.40 with HOKA reacceleration and tariff-refund optionality. **Bear $75 (−23.0%)** at 11.5x a $6.50 depressed EPS. Timeframe 12 months.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. HOKA wholesale growth returning above 8% year over year in the October (Q2 FY2027) print, confirming the 3PL timing explanation.
2. Consolidated operating margin expanding year over year in any quarter, with SG&A growth below revenue growth — the single cleanest reversal of the current pattern.
3. Unit volume growth turning positive with revenue per unit still rising, evidencing that price and volume are not trading off.
4. A price near **$85-88**, where the FCF yield exceeds 9.5% on equity and the base case offers over 25% upside.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. FY2027 revenue guidance is cut below the $5.86B low end, or FY2028 consensus EPS is revised below $7.75 (a ~8% cut from $8.40).
2. Gross margin declines year over year for two consecutive quarters, indicating the pricing lever has broken.
3. HOKA segment operating income declines year over year in absolute dollars, or HOKA wholesale turns negative.
4. Repurchase pace falls below roughly $200M in a quarter without a stated strategic use of the cash — that would signal management sees the price as unattractive or liquidity as constrained.
5. Quantification of the two consumer class actions at a material level, or disclosure that tariff refunds will be substantially retained by manufacturers or plaintiffs.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2027 print in late October 2026 is the decisive checkpoint — it carries HOKA back-to-school sell-through, UGG pre-season wholesale shipments, and the first clean read on whether the European 3PL comparison reverses. Secondary checkpoints: any 8-K or 10-Q disclosure quantifying the tariff-refund receivable or the class actions, and the December-quarter print in late January 2027, which carries the $3.72 consensus that the recent 5.4% cut to the September quarter has made more load-bearing.

## Conclusion

Score trajectory has moved 7.5 → 7.4 → 7.1 → 7.0 → 6.0 → 6.2 → 5.9, with competitive position and growth prospects carrying the entire decline. The July 30 10-Q confirms rather than reverses that trend: revenue growth of 5.7% is the slowest in the disclosed series, units declined, and both core brands produced approximately zero operating profit growth while SG&A outran revenue by 700 basis points. We are lowering the composite on that evidence, not on the 4% price decline since our last review; the price move on modestly rising estimates and a raised EPS guide is, in isolation, a marginal improvement in risk/reward.

The recommendation stays HOLD because the two halves of this business offset. On one side, a debt-free balance sheet, 8.3% equity FCF yield, 41% ROE, and a repurchase authorization worth 35% of market capitalization that has already retired 9% of shares in five quarters. On the other, a decelerating premium-priced discretionary franchise, flat domestic demand, guided FY2027 net income decline, competitive entry the company itself names in its risk factors, and unquantified tariff-related legal exposure. Thirteen times forward earnings is cheap if $7.50 is trend earnings and expensive if it is peak. The October quarter is where that question gets answered, and the current holding already pays a mid-to-high single-digit per-share compounding rate from the buyback alone.

We would become buyers at $85-88 or on operating-margin expansion with SG&A growing slower than revenue. We would reduce on a revenue guidance cut, two consecutive quarters of gross margin decline, or HOKA operating income turning negative.
