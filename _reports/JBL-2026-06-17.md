**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Jabil has converted a low-margin, scale contract-manufacturing business into one of the cleaner public proxies for AI data-center buildout. The transformation is visible in the segment mix: Intelligent Infrastructure (cloud, data center, capital equipment, networking) grew from 32% of revenue in FY2023 to 41% in FY2025 and reached 49% of revenue in Q2 FY2026. Core (non-GAAP) EPS has accelerated from $8.49 in FY2024 to $9.75 in FY2025, with management raising FY2026 guidance to ~$35B revenue and ~$12.70 core EPS on a "meaningfully increased AI-related revenue outlook." The company beat consensus in each of the last four quarters, and EPS estimates have risen +6.5% (current year) to +9.3% (next year) over 90 days.

The difficulty is price. After a 108% advance over twelve months (from a $177 low to a recent $375, with a 52-week high of $399), JBL trades at roughly 30x guided FY2026 core EPS of $12.70 and ~50x trailing GAAP EPS. That multiple sits below the peer median (52.3x P/E across FLEX/FN/TTMI/SANM) but embeds continued double-digit AI-driven growth at a moment when the demand underwriting that growth is being actively stress-tested across the AI-capex complex. The same AI pivot that drove the re-rating has also concentrated revenue further: Customer A reached 16% of FY2025 revenue and top-5 concentration rose to 38% in H1 FY2026. The bull case and the principal risk are the same exposure viewed from opposite ends.

We rate JBL a HOLD. The execution is genuine and the earnings trajectory supports the stock at current levels, but the asymmetry is no longer attractive for new capital after the run, and the near-term options market priced an unusually large move (±9.3%, near-term IV at 159.9% vs. 42.3% realized) into the recent print. We would accumulate on a pullback toward the high-$200s to low-$300s, where the FY2026 multiple compresses toward the low-20s and the bear case is better contained.

## Company Overview

Jabil is among the largest electronics manufacturing services (EMS) providers globally, operating roughly 100 facilities across ~30 countries with approximately 135,000 employees. It offers design, manufacturing, and supply-chain management across the full product lifecycle. The business runs on a decentralized, single-customer "business unit" model with dedicated production lines, and it bears working-capital and component-procurement risk on a turnkey basis, passing through most material costs to customers. R&D is negligible at ~0.1% of revenue; this is a scale, automation, and integration business, not an IP business.

Following a September 2024 reorganization, the company reports three segments:

- **Intelligent Infrastructure** — the AI/cloud growth engine.
- **Regulated Industries** — automotive/transportation, healthcare/packaging, renewable energy. ~36-40% of revenue.
- **Connected Living & Digital Commerce** — warehouse automation, connected consumer, digital commerce. Shrinking from 30% (FY2023) to ~15-16% (FY2026), largely on the Mobility Business divestiture.

The portfolio reshaping is deliberate: exiting low-margin consumer mobility (Chengdu/Wuxi assets sold to BYD Electronic for ~$2.2B, closed December 2023, generating a $942M pre-tax gain) and redeploying capital into AI data-center infrastructure and regulated healthcare. Recent acquisitions — Hanley Energy ($748M, data-center power), Mikros Technologies ($63M, liquid cooling), Rebound Technologies ($133M, supply-chain), and Pharmaceutics International ($309M, pharma CDMO) — push Jabil up the value chain into higher-content AI rack components and diversify the regulated segment.

## Financial Analysis

GAAP earnings are misleading for this company and should be set aside in favor of core EPS. FY2024 net income of $1.39B was inflated by the $942M divestiture gain; FY2025 net income of $657M was depressed by the absence of that gain plus $181M of restructuring, a $97M Italy divestiture loss, and a $46M securities impairment. A naive reading shows net income falling 53% YoY in FY2025, the inverse of the underlying trajectory.

Core EPS tells the accurate story: $8.63 (FY23) → $8.49 (FY24) → $9.75 (FY25) → $5.55 in H1 FY2026 (versus $3.94 in the prior-year H1). Revenue, adjusting for the divestiture distortion, is accelerating: H1 FY2026 revenue rose 20.9% YoY, with Intelligent Infrastructure up ~53% (cloud/data center +45%) and Q2 FY2026 revenue of $8.28B (+23.1%). Gross margin has expanded from 8.3% (FY23) to ~9.0% (H1 FY26), and core operating margin is guided to 5.8% for FY2026, high for an EMS business and a function of favorable mix and operating leverage on the AI ramp.

Cash generation is consistent and high-quality at the operating level: operating cash flow of ~$1.6-1.7B annually, with adjusted FCF rising from $1.0B (FY23) to $1.32B (FY25) and guided above $1.4B for FY2026. Capex is low at ~1-2% of revenue, partly because customers co-invest in dedicated PP&E.

The balance sheet requires careful interpretation. Headline metrics look alarming — Altman Z-Score of 1.99 (grey zone), D/E of 11.25x, ROE of 43% — but these are artifacts of an aggressive buyback program, not distress. Jabil has repurchased $6.6B of stock since FY2016 ($2.5B in FY2024 alone), driving treasury stock to -$8.5B and shrinking stockholders' equity to ~$1.3-1.5B, while retained earnings remain healthy at $6.7B. Total debt is moderate at ~$3.9B of notes (post the January 2026 $1.0B issuance), against a $4.2B undrawn revolver and an investment-grade rating. Interest coverage is solid (operating income $1.18B vs. $147M interest), and the company is in compliance with covenants. Share count has fallen from 144M (FY2022) to ~107M (Feb 2026), a ~26% reduction that is itself a meaningful EPS tailwind.

Two items warrant flagging. First, Jabil sold ~$15.6B of trade receivables in FY2025 (across sale and securitization programs), with proceeds booked as operating cash flow. This is standard EMS practice but flatters reported OCF and compresses DSO; true cash conversion should be normalized for it. Second, the buyback-thinned equity base reduces balance-sheet cushion in a downturn or a credit-market freeze, a structural reality of the chosen capital structure rather than an accounting concern.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest part of the thesis. Jabil sits directly in the AI infrastructure buildout as a picks-and-shovels supplier: it builds servers, racks, networking, power, and cooling for hyperscaler and cloud customers without taking chip-design risk. The Mikros (liquid cooling) and Hanley (data-center power) acquisitions move the company into higher-margin, content-rich AI rack components.

The quantitative signal is firmly positive. The four-quarter earnings record shows beats of 1.6%, 6.2%, 4.5%, and 11.5%, indicating consistent conservatism in guidance. Estimate revisions are rising across every horizon: current quarter +7.1% (90d), next quarter +10.8%, current year +6.5%, next year +9.3% to $14.76. The combination of ~20%+ revenue growth and ~7-11% upward estimate revisions over 90 days is a constructive forward indicator, and the FY2026 guidance raise represents roughly 30% core EPS growth.

The durability of this growth depends on hyperscaler capex remaining strong, which is where the macro context matters. The broader AI-capex complex has shown a mix of confirming signals (multiple hyperscaler capex raises, continued data-center power and cooling demand) and emerging questions on the demand side (usage-plateau hypotheses at frontier labs, pricing pressure). Jabil's growth is real and multi-confirmed across its segment data, but it is downstream of a capex cycle that the market is now actively re-underwriting. We do not penalize the growth score for this — the demand is documented in the segment numbers — but it bears directly on how much of that growth one is willing to pay for.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$375, JBL trades at roughly 30x guided FY2026 core EPS of $12.70 and ~25x next-year consensus EPS of $14.76. On trailing GAAP EPS of $5.92, the multiple is ~50x, but trailing GAAP is distorted by the prior-year divestiture gain and is not the right anchor.

Against peers, the stock looks reasonable on a headline basis: the EMS peer median P/E is 52.3x (FLEX 65.2, FN 50.0, TTMI 101.8, SANM 52.3, TEL 21.2). Jabil's ~50x trailing / ~30x guided-core multiple is at or below the cohort. The PEG on next-year growth (~30% FY26 core EPS growth against ~30x guided) is near 1.0, which is supportive rather than stretched, and consistent with the cycle-adjusted approach appropriate for a company at an earnings inflection.

The reason we do not score valuation higher is the embedded assumption. At 30x guided core EPS, the price requires the AI-capex ramp and the associated mix benefit to persist, with no meaningful demand air-pocket and no loss or sharp reduction from the now-dominant Customer A. The stock's 108% twelve-month run rests on fundamental improvement, which is not itself a reason for a low valuation score, but a >100% advance that has carried the multiple to the top of its own range, on a business whose dominant growth driver is a single concentrated customer in a cyclical end-market, leaves limited margin of safety. Analyst targets (mean $374, median $384, high $430, low $287) bracket the current price almost exactly, suggesting consensus sees the stock as roughly fairly valued rather than as a clear bargain. The downside is the more instructive boundary: the $287 low target implies ~24% downside, and a de-rating to ~20x guided core EPS in a capex-slowdown scenario would put the stock near $255.

A constructive entry exists below current levels. At ~$300, the FY2026 multiple compresses to ~24x and the next-year multiple to ~20x, restoring asymmetry on a business growing core EPS ~30%. We would be buyers there.

## Competitive Landscape

Jabil competes with Foxconn, Flex, Celestica, Sanmina, and customers' in-house manufacturing. The EMS industry is intensely competitive and structurally low-margin (gross margins ~9%), with differentiation coming from scale, global footprint, automation, design-plus-supply-chain integration, and the stickiness of multi-year customer relationships. Pricing power is limited and players bear inventory and working-capital risk.

Jabil's competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. The deliberate exit from low-margin consumer mobility and redeployment into AI infrastructure and regulated healthcare has improved mix and lifted gross and operating margins. The Mikros and Hanley acquisitions move the company into higher-content, higher-margin segments of the AI rack, where differentiation and pricing are better than in commodity board assembly. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 611.9% versus a peer group at 182.3% reflects this repositioning. Against that, the moat remains a scale-and-execution moat, not an IP moat; the EMS model is fundamentally exposed to customers internalizing manufacturing or shifting volumes, and the deepening single-customer concentration is a competitive vulnerability as much as a growth driver.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant company-specific risk is customer concentration, and it is intensifying with the AI pivot. Customer A — almost certainly a hyperscaler in Intelligent Infrastructure — accounts for 16% of FY2025 revenue, within a top-5 that rose to 38% of revenue in H1 FY2026. Customers do not commit beyond roughly one quarter, and the Amazon warrant (up to 1,158,539 shares at $137.77, vesting against Amazon's purchases over seven years) signals that a major cloud customer's spend is tied to Jabil's equity. The upside (AI capex supercycle) and downside (single-hyperscaler dependence) are the same exposure.

Secondary risks: (1) AI/cloud demand cyclicality — management explicitly notes cloud customers carry different demand cycles, which complicates working capital; (2) thin-margin operating leverage — a ~9% gross margin means a demand drop erodes profitability quickly, the same mechanism that is currently delivering positive leverage on the way up; (3) tariff/trade-policy volatility — significant China and Mexico operations, with the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs creating both refund potential and continued uncertainty; (4) ~73% foreign-source revenue and material China exposure; (5) accelerating M&A with goodwill rising from $841M to $1.23B on Hanley, against $1.02B of historical accumulated impairments; and (6) the buyback-thinned equity base, which reduces flexibility in a credit-market freeze.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV stood at 159.9% versus 42.3% one-year realized volatility — a 117.6pp spread, in the 91st percentile of the current chain — with the term structure in steep backwardation (159.9% near-term vs. 61.0% at three months). The implied move on the nearest expiry was ±9.3%. This concentration is consistent with hedging around the Q3 FY2026 print rather than a directional bearish signal: the put/call skew is call-heavy (-16.0%) and the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.28 to 0.90 over the prior three weeks, indicating positioning skewed toward upside or de-hedging rather than accumulating downside protection. The market expected a large move around earnings and leaned toward the upside, which corroborates a long position, but it underscores that entries near these levels carry elevated event risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($430-460):** The AI data-center buildout sustains hyperscaler capex through FY2027, Intelligent Infrastructure continues compounding at 40%+, and the Mikros/Hanley content-up-the-stack strategy lifts margins beyond the 5.8% FY2026 guide. Core EPS reaches the high-$14s in FY2027 (consensus next-year $14.76), and the stock holds a ~30x multiple, supporting $430+ — consistent with the high analyst target of $430. Continued buybacks at ~$1B/year amplify per-share growth. The four-quarter beat streak suggests guidance remains conservative, leaving room for further estimate revisions higher.

**Bear Case ($255-287):** A hyperscaler capex air-pocket — whether from an AI-demand plateau, customer in-sourcing, or a single large customer reducing orders — hits the now-dominant Intelligent Infrastructure segment. Thin margins amplify the revenue decline into a sharper earnings drop, estimate revisions turn negative, and the multiple de-rates from ~30x to ~20x guided core EPS. The combination puts the stock near $255-287, roughly the low analyst target. The buyback-thinned balance sheet provides less cushion than the headline FCF suggests, and customer concentration at 38% of revenue means the bear case is a step-function rather than a gradual fade.

The base case sits closer to the bull boundary given documented order momentum and the beat track record, but from current levels the reward/risk is roughly symmetric — ~15% to the high target against ~24% to the low target — which is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY at ~$375.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q4 FY2026 / full-year report (fiscal year ends August 31; report expected late September 2026), which will confirm whether the ~$35B / $12.70 guidance was met and provide the FY2027 outlook — the decisive input on AI-capex durability.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the high-$200s/low-$300s, compressing the FY2026 multiple toward ~24x and restoring 2:1 reward/risk, with fundamentals intact.
- FY2027 guidance implying continued 20%+ revenue growth in Intelligent Infrastructure alongside an explicit reduction or diversification of top-5 customer concentration below ~33%.
- Sustained margin expansion above the 5.8% core operating guide, evidencing the content-up-the-stack strategy converting to durable mix benefit.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or a sequential decline in Intelligent Infrastructure revenue, signaling a hyperscaler capex air-pocket.
- Loss or material reduction of Customer A, or top-5 concentration rising above ~42% without offsetting diversification.
- Negative 90-day estimate revisions combined with a gross-margin contraction below ~8.5%.

## Conclusion

Jabil is a high-quality EMS leader executing a well-timed pivot into AI data-center infrastructure, delivering accelerating core EPS, robust FCF, and a share-count reduction that has compounded into 611.9% five-year TSR. The growth is real, multi-confirmed in the segment data, and supported by rising estimates and a four-quarter beat streak.

The constraint is price and concentration. At ~$375, the stock has re-rated to ~30x guided core EPS after a 108% twelve-month run, embedding continued AI-capex strength while customer concentration deepens into a single cyclical end-market. The risk/reward from current levels is roughly balanced rather than asymmetric, and the buyback-thinned balance sheet leaves less cushion than the headline cash generation implies.

We rate JBL a **HOLD** with a growth bias for existing holders. The thesis remains constructive and the earnings trajectory supports the stock, but we would wait for a pullback toward the high-$200s to low-$300s — or for FY2027 guidance that confirms capex durability and shows concentration diversifying — before committing new capital. The prior trajectory of declining scores (6.5 → 6.0 → 5.9) reflected valuation and risk dimensions tightening as the stock ran; the new guidance raise stabilizes the growth read and supports holding, but does not, at this price, justify an upgrade.
