**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carpenter Technology has delivered one of the cleaner industrial turnarounds of this cycle. Operating margin moved from negative in FY22 to 18.1% in FY25, gross margin from 8.2% to 26.7%, and diluted EPS from a loss to $7.42, achieved on lower volume through a deliberate shift toward higher-value alloys and disciplined pricing. The Specialty Alloys Operations (SAO) segment has now posted seventeen consecutive quarters of rising adjusted operating margin, reaching 35.6% ex-surcharge in the quarter ended March 31, 2026. The balance sheet is strong (net leverage 0.59x, interest coverage 19.6x), cash flow has inflected positive and is scaling, and recent contract wins carrying price increases above 30% confirm genuine pricing power in a tight-capacity oligopoly. The aerospace and defense up-cycle (62% of FY25 revenue) is intact, and a second secular leg is emerging in industrial gas turbines for data-center power generation, where Q3 FY26 Energy sales rose 49%.

The fundamental case is sound. The valuation case is not. The stock trades at roughly 60x trailing earnings and about 46x next-fiscal-year consensus EPS of $12.67, against a peer median P/E of 26.6x. At $575-592 it sits roughly 22% above the consensus analyst price target of ~$469 and at or above the highest published target ($580). The stock has appreciated 127% over the past year and 45% in the month since our prior review, while consensus EPS estimates have been essentially flat over the trailing 30 days (-0.0% current year, -0.4% next year). The earnings-inflection BUY signal that would justify looking past the headline multiple — rapidly rising estimates plus accelerating revenue — is not present here. Estimate momentum has stalled even as the price rose sharply.

We move to HOLD with a cautious lean and an "uncertain" classification. The business quality and intact cycle argue against shorting or avoiding a name with this operating momentum, but the price has run past every sell-side target on flat estimates and decelerating growth. We would own existing positions and resist adding here; the asymmetry that existed at $300-400 has closed. The overall score declines from 6.9 to 5.95, driven by the valuation and risk dimensions; the fundamentals confirmed and improved while the price ran ahead of them.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1889, Carpenter Technology produces and distributes premium specialty alloys — titanium, nickel, and cobalt alloys, stainless and tool steels, and metal powders — for critical, highly-engineered applications. The company melts, hot-forms, and cold-works raw inputs (nickel, cobalt, titanium, chromium, molybdenum, and scrap) into billet, bar, rod, wire, and strip, and operates its own global service and distribution network enabling just-in-time stocking, which differentiates it from many specialty-steel peers.

The business runs in two segments. Specialty Alloys Operations (SAO) is the core engine at roughly 89% of net sales and the overwhelming majority of profit, with integrated mills primarily in Reading and Latrobe, Pennsylvania, plus South Carolina and Athens, Alabama. Performance Engineered Products (PEP) houses Dynamet (titanium), Carpenter Additive (3D-printing powders and parts), and distribution operations, at materially lower margins (~9% ex-surcharge).

End-market exposure in FY25: Aerospace & Defense 62% ($1,768.6M), Medical 12%, Industrial/Consumer 12%, Energy 7%, Transportation 4%, Distribution 3%. International is roughly 41% of sales, and no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. The competitive moat rests on multi-year customer qualification cycles, proprietary process capability, 135+ years of metallurgical expertise, and fewer than ten serious competitors in high-value applications. Fiscal year ends June 30.

The financial trajectory is the strongest part of the story.

The recovery is volume-light and price/mix-heavy, which matters for its durability. FY25 revenue grew only 4% (8% ex-surcharge) on 6% lower pounds sold. The profit growth came from deliberately reallocating mill capacity toward complex, higher-value alloys, raising prices, and improving productivity, rather than from a simple demand rebound. SAO ex-surcharge operating margin reached 28.6% in FY25. The seventeen-quarter streak of rising SAO adjusted margins, achieved on flat-to-lower volume, indicates structural mix and pricing discipline rather than cyclical luck.

Q3 FY26 (quarter ended March 31, 2026) was another record: net sales $811.5M (+12%), operating income $186.5M (+35%), EPS $2.77. Nine-month FY26 EPS of $7.29 (adjusted $7.53) already approaches the full FY25 figure. The four-quarter earnings record shows four straight beats, in a 3-11% range, supporting confidence in near-term consensus.

Cash flow has inflected and is scaling: operating cash flow was $440M in FY25 and $365M in the first nine months of FY26 (versus $182M a year earlier), with adjusted FCF of $287.5M in FY25 (~10% FCF margin). The balance sheet is strong: net leverage 0.59x against a 3.50x covenant, interest coverage 19.6x, current ratio 3.65, and an Altman Z of 3.23. The November 2025 refinancing issued $700M of 5.625% notes due 2034 to redeem higher-coupon 2028 (6.375%) and 2030 (7.625%) notes, extending maturities and lowering the cost of debt (with a one-time $15.6M extinguishment loss). Capital return is modest: a $400M buyback authorization (~$236M used) and a flat $0.20/quarter dividend yielding a negligible 0.14%.

The one near-term financial swing factor is capex. Spending on the Athens, Alabama brownfield melt-capacity expansion is ramping from $154M in FY25 to roughly $260M guided for FY26, which will temporarily consume free cash flow. This is comfortably funded given the balance sheet, but it shifts the cash-generation profile from harvest to invest for the next 12-18 months.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth profile is real but decelerating from recovery rates. Consensus puts current-year (FY26) EPS at $10.57 — roughly +42% over FY25, a number that still carries cyclical-recovery acceleration — and next-year (FY27) EPS at $12.67, or about +20%. Twenty percent EPS growth from a high-quality specialty-alloy franchise is attractive on its own terms; the question is whether it justifies a 46x next-year multiple.

The demand picture is supportive. The aerospace and defense up-cycle is being driven by rising build rates, aging-fleet replacement, and structural sub-market orders; management characterizes the company as still early in the growth cycle. The June 2026 contract wins, with price increases above 30% tied to aerospace, defense, gas turbines, and space, confirm pricing power into tight industry-wide melt capacity. The narrative is also broadening: the 49% rise in Q3 FY26 Energy sales (+44% ex-surcharge) on industrial gas turbines for data-center power generation is a credible second secular leg that reduces reliance on the single 62% A&D concentration and links the company to the broad data-center power-demand build now visible across utilities, electrical equipment, and turbine suppliers.

The offsets are concrete. Medical, 12% of revenue and a growth story two years ago, fell 6% in FY25 and 23% in Q3 FY26 on supply-chain destocking. The estimate-revision picture has cooled: 90-day revisions are positive (+2.5% current year, +5.2% next year), but the trailing 30 days are flat to slightly negative across the board (-0.0% current year, -0.4% next year, -1.7% next quarter). The data feed characterizes recent earnings momentum as broadly flat-to-falling. This is not an inflection name with accelerating estimates; the most recent revision window has stalled. That distinction governs how much credit the valuation deserves.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the thesis turns cautious. At $575-592, Carpenter trades at:

- ~60x trailing EPS of $7.42
- ~56x current-year consensus EPS of $10.57
- ~46x next-year consensus EPS of $12.67
- PEG of roughly 2.3x on ~20% next-year growth

The peer set in industrial machinery and components trades at a median P/E of 26.6x (RBC 66.8x, ITT 32.9x, MLI 17.7x, LECO 26.6x, GGG 24.2x). Carpenter commands a deserved premium for its margin trajectory and aerospace exposure, but at roughly 1.7-2.0x the peer median forward multiple with growth decelerating toward 20% and recent estimates flat, the premium is full.

The most direct evidence that the price has outrun fundamentals is the analyst target distribution. Consensus mean and median targets sit at ~$469 and $468; the high target is $580. The stock at $575-592 trades roughly 22% above the consensus target and at or above the highest published target. The principle that analyst targets act as floors rather than ceilings applies to cheap, accelerating-estimate names; it does not apply to a stock at ~46-56x forward where targets and price already reflect the same growth extrapolation. When a name trades above its highest sell-side target on flat estimates, the targets are the market's considered view that the multiple is stretched, not stale lagging indicators of an underpriced stock.

The cycle-adjusted argument does not rescue valuation here. Carpenter's margins are near structural peaks (SAO ex-surcharge at 35.6%), not at a trough, so there is no depressed-earnings base to normalize upward. Pricing the stock as if the up-cycle is permanent embeds optimistic assumptions about both aerospace build rates and the durability of premium pricing. The valuation score is 3.0: there is no identifiable reason the market is underpricing this stock, and several reasons it may be overpricing it.

## Competitive Landscape

Carpenter occupies a structurally attractive position. The premium aero/defense/medical alloy market is an oligopoly where qualification barriers (multi-year customer approval cycles), capacity scarcity, and proprietary process capability confer real pricing power, now quantified by the >30% price increases on new contracts. Industry-wide melt capacity is tight, which is why Carpenter is investing in the Athens brownfield expansion to add high-value melt capacity rather than chase volume at lower margins.

The competitive trajectory is improving: the company is widening its margin advantage, exercising pricing power, and broadening into data-center gas turbines and semiconductor materials. The chief structural offset is concentration — 62% of revenue is A&D, and Boeing/Airbus build-rate stumbles would hit the highest-margin segment hardest — alongside the lumpiness of medical and industrial destocking, currently visible in the 23% medical decline. On balance the moat is widening and the competitive position scores 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Valuation and expectations risk** is the dominant near-term risk and is not adequately priced. At a multiple above all analyst targets, the stock is priced for continued flawless execution. Any deceleration in aerospace build rates, a sharper medical decline, or a surcharge/LIFO timing mismatch that dents a single quarter could trigger meaningful multiple compression from an elevated base. The asymmetry at this price favors disappointment over upside.

**Aerospace cyclicality and concentration.** The current strength is a recovery/build-rate cycle. A downturn or OEM production stumble (Boeing is named explicitly in the risk factors) hits the most profitable segment first.

**Medical destocking** is ongoing and masked by aerospace strength.

**Surcharge lag and raw-material/LIFO volatility.** Roughly 43% of sales are firm-price arrangements; nickel/cobalt/titanium and tariff swings create timing mismatches between surcharge revenue and LIFO cost of sales, producing quarter-to-quarter gross-margin noise.

**Capex execution.** The thesis hinges on filling the new Athens melt capacity with high-value work at sustained pricing while FCF is temporarily consumed. Execution risk rises as capex steps up.

**Asset concentration, labor, and pension.** Highly specialized equipment is concentrated in Reading, Latrobe, and Athens. The Washington, PA collective-bargaining agreement (179 employees) expired 8/31/25; Latrobe (461 employees) runs to 7/31/27. A $51.9M non-cash pension settlement hit FY24, with ~$115M of required contributions estimated over five years. The Q3 FY26 10-Q re-affirmed the 10-K risk set without material additions.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 49.1% against one-year historical volatility of 47.3% — fair, with a spread of just 1.8% and an IV percentile of only 15% within the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at -1.9%, and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.44 and 0.45), indicating no defensive positioning. The term structure is flat (1m 49.1% ≈ 6m 49.4%), so the market is not pricing a near-term binary event. The recorded jump in the options-delta snapshot to a 203% ATM IV reading is a thin-options data artifact and should be disregarded; the curve reads cleanly. The options market is complacent, with no elevated put demand confirming downside fears and no cheap volatility flagging an entry. This is consistent with a stock priced for continuation, and the absence of an embedded hedging premium is a mild caution given the stretched valuation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($650-720)

The aerospace up-cycle is multi-year and Carpenter is early in it. Pricing power on new contracts holds and broadens; the Athens expansion fills with high-value work; data-center gas turbines and semiconductor materials add a durable second growth leg that de-risks the A&D concentration. The SAO margin streak extends, FY27 EPS beats $12.67 and FY28 approaches $15, and the market continues to award a premium multiple to a structurally advantaged compounder. At ~48x a $15 forward number the stock reaches the low $700s. This case requires both continued operational execution and no multiple compression from an already-elevated base.

### Bear Case ($380-420, roughly -28% to -34% from current)

Aerospace build rates wobble or medical/industrial destocking deepens, growth decelerates toward 10%, and a stretched stock above all analyst targets re-rates toward a more defensible 30-35x forward multiple. On ~$12 of forward EPS at 32x, the stock falls to ~$385 — close to the low analyst target of $400. A cyclical peak in margins, a surcharge/LIFO-driven quarterly miss, or a broader risk-off de-rating of high-multiple cyclicals would catalyze it. The pervasive insider selling into the run is consistent with the price being full.

The base case sits between these: with growth real but decelerating, a fair multiple of ~38-40x on next-year EPS of $12.67 implies ~$480-510, broadly the consensus target zone and roughly 12-17% below the current price. That gap is the core of the cautious stance.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (uncertain).** The business merits ownership; the price does not merit adding. This is a position to hold for the structural quality while waiting for valuation to catch up to or correct toward fundamentals.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $400s (toward the consensus target of ~$469 or below), restoring forward P/E to the high-30s and PEG toward 1.5 — restoring asymmetric upside.
- A re-acceleration of estimate revisions (>+10% over 90 days on FY27 EPS) accompanying durable A&D and data-center turbine demand, which would justify the premium multiple at a higher absolute price.
- Open-market insider buying, which would signal management views the price as undervalued rather than full.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quarterly miss or guidance reset (e.g., FY27 EPS guided below $12) confirming the multiple is unsupported.
- A deepening of the medical decline beyond -23%, or aerospace build-rate cuts from Boeing/Airbus that threaten the 62% A&D base.
- A breakdown in price momentum below the ~$480-500 area on rising volume while estimates fall, signaling a multiple-compression unwind.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q4/FY26 earnings release (late July/early August 2026), which will set the FY27 framework and test whether pricing power and the data-center turbine leg are converting to guidance. The Washington, PA labor situation and Athens capex pace are secondary monitoring items.

## Conclusion

Carpenter Technology is a genuinely high-quality business — an oligopolist with widening margins, confirmed pricing power, a strong balance sheet, and exposure to two secular tailwinds in aerospace and data-center power. The fundamentals held since our prior review and improved. The problem is that the stock has risen 45% in a single month and 127% over the year while consensus estimates went flat, leaving it at ~60x trailing and ~46x next-year earnings, roughly 22% above the consensus analyst target and at or above the highest published target. The risk/reward that was compelling at $300-400 has inverted: base-case fair value sits roughly 12-17% below the current price, the bear case implies a 28-34% drawdown, and the bull case requires both flawless execution and no multiple compression from an elevated base.

We rate CRS a HOLD with a cautious lean. We would not initiate or add at $575-592, and existing holders should monitor the downgrade triggers closely. The combination of a premium valuation, decelerating estimate momentum, and uniform insider selling with zero open-market buying is the profile of a quality name that has outrun its fundamentals. The disciplined move is to wait for either a pullback toward consensus targets or a fresh re-acceleration in estimates before paying up.
