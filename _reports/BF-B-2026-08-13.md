## Executive Summary

Brown-Forman is a high-quality franchise producing low-quality shareholder outcomes, and the gap has persisted long enough that it should no longer be treated as temporary. Reported net sales have fallen three years running — $4,228M (FY2023), $4,178M (FY2024), $3,975M (FY2025), $3,928M (FY2026), a −2.43% compound annual rate — while net income declined from $1,024M in FY2024 (flattered by roughly $267M of divestiture gains) to $715M in FY2026. Five-year total shareholder return, per the company's own pay-versus-performance table, indexes to $37 against $100 invested at April 30, 2021, versus $152 for the S&P 500 Consumer Staples index: −18.0% annualized against +8.7%. The FY2024–2026 performance-share cycle measured three-year TSR at the **3rd percentile** of that index.

The valuation looks less attractive than the headline 16.7x forward multiple suggests. FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.53 absorbed $132M of non-cash brand impairments (roughly $0.29 per share pre-tax) plus $19M of restructuring, offset by an $18M non-recurring duty-drawback benefit and a $15M contingent-consideration gain. Normalizing, FY2026 earnings power was approximately $1.75–1.78. FY2027 consensus of $1.68 — revised down 1.1% over 90 days — therefore implies a further mid-single-digit decline in underlying EPS, and FY2028 at $1.70 implies no recovery. The stock is priced at roughly 16x flat-to-eroding earnings with a 3.3% dividend against a 4.63% 10-year Treasury.

The near-term price action argues for trimming rather than adding. Shares rose from $26.08 (July 24) to $29.52 (July 29) — 13% in three sessions on volume of 6.5M shares versus a 4.3M average — coinciding with press reports of an unsolicited all-cash approach at approximately $32 per share that the board and the controlling family reportedly rejected. Those reports are uncorroborated in any SEC filing available to us and should not be relied on as fact. What is structurally certain is that Class B shares carry **no vote**, Class A holds the entire franchise, and one family partnership (Wolf Pen Branch, LP) holds 60.3% of Class A with sole voting power. No bid can succeed without that entity's consent. Owning BF-B for takeover optionality means owning an option whose exercise decision belongs to a party that has, on the available reporting, already declined. We move to **REDUCE**.

## Company Overview

Founded in Louisville in 1870, Brown-Forman produces and sells more than 40 spirits and ready-to-drink brands in over 170 countries with approximately 4,900 employees. The portfolio is deliberately concentrated: the 10-K describes the Jack Daniel's family, led by Jack Daniel's Tennessee Whiskey, as "our most valuable asset – the engine of our overall financial performance." Surrounding it are fifteen years of line extensions (Tennessee Honey, Fire, Apple, Bonded, American Single Malt, Tennessee Blackberry) plus Gentleman Jack and a Jack Daniel's RTD family including the Coca-Cola partnership. Two American whiskey growth engines sit alongside: Woodford Reserve, described as the leading global super-premium American whiskey and exceeding 1.9M nine-litre cases in FY2026, and Old Forester. The diversification layer comprises Herradura and el Jimador tequila plus New Mix RTD, three Scotch single malts, Slane, Chambord, Fords Gin, and the FY2023 bolt-ons Gin Mare and Diplomático.

Two structural features matter more than the brand list. First, bourbon and Tennessee whiskey must be aged in new charred oak, typically at least three years, so production is a multi-year bet on demand; the filing warns that inventories "may be larger in relation to sales and total assets than in many other businesses." A demand slowdown arriving after a supply build-out becomes a working-capital and fixed-cost-absorption problem, not merely a revenue problem. Second, U.S. law bars direct-to-consumer sale by manufacturers, forcing reliance on distributors and, in 18 control states, state agencies.

Geographic mix: the U.S. fell from 45% of net sales in FY2024 to 42% in FY2026, with Mexico 8%, Germany 6%, Australia 5%, U.K. 4%. Because absolute net sales declined across the same period, the mix shift is equally consistent with faster U.S. decline as with international success and should not be read as evidence of diversification. Separately, the largest customer reached approximately 10% of consolidated net sales in FY2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Organic net sales — the basis used for incentive compensation — ran +17%, +10%, −1%, +1%, and +0.43% across FY2022–FY2026. The gap to reported figures is divestiture-driven (Finlandia, Sonoma-Cutrer, Alabama cooperage, and the Korbel agency termination effective June 30, 2025). Either series shows three years of stall after a two-year post-pandemic surge, consistent with demand pull-forward followed by distributor destocking and softening spirits consumption, compounded by American whiskey supply built during the boom now meeting slower demand.

**Earnings quality.** Operating income direction disclosed as percentage changes was +25% (FY24), −22% (FY25), −10% (FY26); chaining implies roughly $1.0bn in FY2026 on $3,928M of sales, an operating margin near 25.5% versus 27–28% previously. These levels are derived from disclosed percentage changes rather than read from the income statement and should be re-verified. Three FY2026 drags are disclosed: $132M of brand-name impairments ($45M Gin Mare, $87M Diplomático); SG&A up ~9%, partly on "costs associated with the contemplated business transaction discussions"; and unfavorable acquisition/divestiture comparisons. Offsets were $78M of annualized restructuring savings (against a $70M target), the $18M drawback benefit now fully exhausted, and a $15M favorable remeasurement of the Gin Mare earn-out — a gain that arises mechanically because the acquired brand is underperforming, and against a liability payable in cash on seller exercise no later than July 2027.

**The Q4 miss, read fairly.** The most recent reported quarter (ended April 30, 2026) delivered $0.58 against a $0.47 estimate, a 22.6% beat. The $0.12-against-$0.33 result in the surprise table is dated June 30, 2026 (Q1 FY2027) and post-dates the FY2026 10-K; the $132M FY2026 impairment sits within the FY2026 figures, not that quarter. The four fiscal 2026 quarters ($0.36, $0.47, $0.58 and the balance) reconcile to the $1.53 reported. The relevant conclusion is not that consumption collapsed in any single quarter but that the FY2023 acquisition vintage keeps consuming reported earnings — $179M written off two brands in two years ($47M Gin Mare FY2025 + $45M Gin Mare FY2026 + $87M Diplomático FY2026), following $148M of Finlandia impairments ($52M FY2022 + $96M FY2023) before that asset was sold.

**Capital allocation and credit.** FY2026 returned $427M in dividends and $400M in buybacks: $827M against $715M of net income, or 116%. Repurchases averaged approximately $28.48 per share against a current $28.03 — neutral execution, roughly 14.0M shares or 3.1% of the count. The dividend of approximately $0.92 per share is 60% of FY2026 GAAP EPS and 55% of FY2027 consensus, so the dividend itself is covered on an earnings basis. The credit signal is directionally negative: **Moody's downgraded the senior unsecured rating from A1 to A2 in November 2025**, in a year when the company distributed more than it earned while EBIT declined 10%.

FY2026 operating cash flow and capex are unverified in the available evidence, so free-cash-flow coverage of the $827M distribution cannot be confirmed. This matters in both directions — $132M of non-cash impairments means net income understates cash generation, while aged-inventory build can absorb cash that net income does not reveal. Do not annualize any single quarter to fill the gap; roughly 30% of net sales fall in the fourth calendar quarter and working capital is highly seasonal.

**Financial Health score: 5.5.** Solidly investment grade with high absolute margins and no disclosed refinancing cliff, offset by a rating downgrade, distributions exceeding earnings, an unverified FCF bridge, a near-term earn-out cash call, and inventory-intensity risk in a decelerating category.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus expects no growth. Current-year EPS is $1.68 (revised −1.1% over both 30 and 90 days, 17 analysts) and next year $1.70 (−2.1% over 90 days). Against impairment-adjusted FY2026 earnings power near $1.75–1.78, that is a further decline followed by stabilization. Current-quarter estimates are down 2.7% over 90 days. There is no positive estimate-revision signal here of the kind that would justify looking through a weak trailing period.

The growth levers management is pulling are real but margin-oriented rather than volume-oriented. The Operational Excellence program delivered $78M of annualized savings against a $70M target, following a 12% workforce reduction and the closure and sale of the Louisville cooperage. Route-to-market control is being taken in-house: 14 open states transitioned in FY2026, 11 control states realigned effective June 1, 2026, Italy moved to owned distribution in May 2025, and the Pabst partnership for Jack Daniel's Country Cocktails concluded with Brown-Forman assuming supply, sales, marketing and distribution effective July 7, 2026. Historically, insourcing distribution has improved Brown-Forman's margins and consumer proximity. It also distorts shipment timing versus depletions in both directions.

Against those levers sit category headwinds the filings acknowledge only obliquely. Tequila commentary is softer ("long-term growth prospects") than the Woodford and Old Forester language. RTD is being pushed hard, which is a defensive posture as much as an offensive one — RTD and tequila are the categories taking share from American whiskey. WHO and public-health scrutiny is called out explicitly. Generational moderation and GLP-1 penetration are not quantified anywhere in the disclosure but are the most plausible explanation for a premium spirits category that stopped growing while pricing held.

Tariff exposure is asymmetric and structural: 58% of net sales are outside the United States, and American whiskey is a designated retaliation target in trade disputes. This is a company-specific vulnerability that few staples peers share to the same degree.

**Growth Prospects score: 3.5.** Three years of contraction, negative revisions, consensus implying continued underlying erosion, and cost savings as the primary earnings lever.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.03 the shares trade at 18.3x FY2026 GAAP EPS of $1.53, 16.7x FY2027 consensus of $1.68, and approximately 15.9x impairment-adjusted FY2026 earnings power of ~$1.76. The apparent step-down from trailing to forward is an artifact of the impairment reversing out, not evidence of earnings acceleration.

Three anchors bear on whether that is cheap.

**Peer multiples, correctly de-rated.** The company's own Special Peer Group for its most consequential retention grant — Diageo (29%), Pernod Ricard (29%), Rémy Cointreau (14%), Campari (14%), LVMH (14%) — has itself de-rated substantially through the 2024–2026 premium-spirits slowdown, with the large diversified names trading in the low-to-mid teens on forward earnings. Brown-Forman at 16.7x on flat earnings is not at a discount to a peer set that carries broader category and geographic diversification. Anchoring on Brown-Forman's own historical 30–40x range is the error the last three years have punished.

**Absolute yield support.** The 3.3% dividend is covered at a 55% forward payout ratio, which is genuine support. It is also 133 basis points *below* the 4.63% 10-year Treasury [unverified — Treasury yield not in provided sources], in a regime where bond-proxy staples face multiple compression rather than expansion. A 0.34 beta has not protected against permanent capital loss: the stock has compounded at −18% annually for five years while behaving defensively against the index.

**Consensus provides no upside.** Mean target $27.79, median $28.00 — both at spot. The distribution is 1 strong buy, 1 buy, 13 hold, 2 sell, 2 strong sell, with sells rising from zero to two over three months. Analyst targets do not function as a stale floor here: that principle applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples, and here estimates are falling.

**The M&A premium.** The strongest bull argument is that a private buyer reportedly valued the equity at approximately $32 per share — 14% above spot — which would imply a floor. Two problems. First, the figure is press-sourced and uncorroborated in filings; the brief could locate no 8-K after July 23, 2026. Second, and decisively, Class B carries no vote and Wolf Pen Branch holds an absolute majority of the voting class with sole voting power, so a control premium is not realizable without family consent that has, on the available reporting, been withheld. A thin 14% premium for a category-leading asset is itself a plausible reason a board would call a proposal "not actionable." The July 27–29 move of 13% on 1.5–2x normal volume, with no earnings event, most likely embeds several dollars of speculation that decays if no filing materializes. An alternative reading — defensive rotation or short covering against 12.5% short interest — cannot be excluded, but a three-day 13% move in a 0.34-beta staple is not a sector effect.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** A market multiple on declining earnings with a sub-Treasury yield and a speculative premium embedded in the current print. Not a value trap in the solvency sense; not a mispricing either.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is fragmented — the ten largest global spirits companies control just over 20% of global volume (IWSR, CY2025) — and intensely competitive at premium-and-above price points against Bacardi, Becle, Campari, Diageo, LVMH, Pernod Ricard, Rémy Cointreau and Suntory. Brown-Forman concedes that "some competitors have substantially greater resources than we do."

The trajectory evidence is unflattering but nuanced. Three-year TSR ranked at the 3rd percentile of S&P 500 Consumer Staples, while transaction-adjusted operating income growth ranked at the **35th percentile**. Operating fundamentals have held up materially better than the share price — the multiple compressed faster than earnings. That gap is the crux of any bull case: either the market is discounting further fundamental decline that has not yet appeared, or the de-rating has overshot. The filings cannot settle it, and the falling estimate profile leans toward the former.

Company-specific competitive erosion is visible in two places. The FY2023 diversification vintage has been written down $179M in two years, so the attempt to broaden beyond American whiskey via acquisition has not yet worked. And the U.S. share of sales fell three consecutive years in a period of absolute revenue decline, which is consistent with domestic share loss to tequila, RTD and craft brands rather than with successful international expansion.

Offsetting: Jack Daniel's Tennessee Whiskey remains the world's number one American whiskey, Woodford Reserve has compounded volumes at a double-digit rate for 25 years and passed 1.9M cases, and the aged-inventory position plus provenance is not replicable by new entrants.

**Competitive Position score: 4.5** — leadership intact at the brand level, trajectory deteriorating at the portfolio and category level.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-brand-family concentration.** The 10-K names Jack Daniel's as the engine of overall financial performance with no offsetting segment disclosure. A durable shift in American whiskey demand hits Brown-Forman more directly than it hits Diageo or Pernod Ricard.

**Aged-inventory forecasting.** Whiskey produced today is sold in three to twelve years. Demand slowdown after a supply build pressures both gross margin (fixed-cost absorption) and cash flow (inventory carry). This is the most company-specific financial risk in the filing and is not visible in trailing margins yet.

**FY2027 will be unreadable.** By management's own description, the U.S. distribution overhaul is the most significant in over 60 years: 25 markets, 11 control states effective June 1, 2026. Add JDCC insourcing from July 7, 2026, a CFO who started March 31, 2026, an abandoned merger, and a possible CEO transition (an Item 5.02 8-K dated July 13, 2026 exists on EDGAR but was not read; press reports of a CEO change sit in tension with the June 18 proxy nominating Lawson Whiting as a director and a routine July 23 grant to him). Reported FY2027 net sales are likely to diverge from underlying depletions in both directions, and adjusted metrics will carry more adjustment lines than usual. Concentrating disruption into an already weak year is defensible sequencing; it also removes the ability to verify a thesis for several quarters.

**Governance that cannot be arbitraged.** Class B holds roughly 63% of the economics and 0% of the votes. Family entities hold 71.1% of Class A; Wolf Pen Branch alone holds 60.3% with sole voting power (though sole *investment* power over only 42.0M of its 101.6M shares). A dissident must state intent to solicit holders of ≥67% of voting power under Rule 14a-19. Say-on-pay occurs every three years, next in 2029, and the 2023 ">99% support" was cast by a base that is 71.1% family-controlled.

**Compensation calibration.** The FY2026 annual scorecard set target payout at **−2% organic net sales growth**; actual +0.43% produced a 149% payout. The adjusted organic operating income metric excluded contemplated-transaction costs, contributing +4 percentage points (−2% became +2%) and lifting that component's payout to 172% from roughly 100%. CEO total compensation rose 88% to $25,304,882 in a year when net income fell 18% and the TSR index fell from 49 to 37; the pay ratio was 439:1. Approximately $16.3M of one-time transaction compensation was awarded for a deal that did not close, deemed earned on the April 28, 2026 termination date. In fairness: the FY2024–2026 cycle paid only 55% of target, the one-time PBRSUs are gated on a three-year absolute ROIC hurdle (12.1%/13.6%/15.1%) and relative operating income against a demanding European peer set, the change-in-control plan is double-trigger with a 280G cutback rather than a gross-up, and the amounts are 0.13% of market capitalization. The issue is target calibration, which is a repeating annual mechanism, more than any single award.

**Trade policy.** 58% international mix with American whiskey a standing retaliation target. Precise tariff status in the FY2026 MD&A is unverified here and should be checked directly.

**Insider activity.** The Form 4 record examined showed scheduled July 23, 2026 annual grants (95,484 units to Whiting, 64,933 to Peters) and share withholding on May 27, 2026 performance-share issuance at $25.94 — no open-market purchases near a five-year low. The signal is muted because family members hold stakes through partnerships rather than personal accounts, but it is absent. The company has **no stock ownership guidelines for employees, including NEOs**.

**Short interest.** 12.5% of float, 4.5 days to cover, 35.3M shares short. Elevated for a staple, which cuts both ways: it corroborates institutional skepticism and creates squeeze risk on any confirmed transaction headline.

**Risk Factors score: 4.0.** Multiple live, dated, company-specific exposures with limited shareholder recourse.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $34 (+21%)

Underlying earnings power is $1.75–1.78, not the reported $1.53, and the market is capitalizing an impairment-depressed number. Operating income growth ranked at the 35th percentile of consumer staples while TSR ranked at the 3rd, implying the de-rating overshot fundamentals by a wide margin. The $78M cost program overdelivered, route-to-market insourcing across 25 markets plus JDCC structurally lifts margin from FY2028, the drag from Gin Mare and Diplomático is now largely written off, and the new CFO arrives from a transformation mandate at Whirlpool with a cost-and-capital brief. A confirmed transaction at or above the reported ~$32 level, or family willingness to engage a second approach, would reprice the equity immediately. At 20x $1.70, $34.

### Bear Case — $22.50 (−20%)

American whiskey demand is in a multi-year normalization that management cannot cost-cut around, and the 25-market distribution overhaul plus RTD insourcing produces shipment disruption that turns flat organic growth negative in FY2027. Consensus already implies underlying EPS below FY2026 adjusted; further cuts follow. The speculative premium from late July decays as no 8-K appears, returning the stock to its pre-news $25.70–26.41 range and then lower on a weak Q1 FY2027 print. Distributions at 116% of net income are trimmed or the buyback pauses; a second rating action follows the A2. Tariff retaliation on American whiskey compounds. At 13.4x $1.68 — roughly where diversified European peers trade — $22.50, essentially the 52-week low of $22.61.

### Base Case — $27.00 (−3.7% price, ≈ −0.4% total with dividend)

16.0x FY2027 consensus $1.68. Organic growth remains near zero, cost savings offset volume pressure, the dividend is maintained, the speculative premium half-decays, and the multiple holds near the peer band. Probability-weighting bull 20% / base 50% / bear 30% yields expected price return of roughly −3.6% and total return near −0.3% including the 3.3% dividend — approximately 5 percentage points below the risk-free rate. That shortfall, not any single fundamental flaw, is what drives the rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current strength rather than waiting for the Q1 FY2027 report. The shares sit 24% above the $22.61 52-week low and within 12% of the $31.92 high, with consensus mean and median targets at spot and the price containing a speculation premium tied to a bid the controlling shareholder reportedly declined. For holders unwilling to exit entirely, reduce to a half position and hold the remainder for the dividend, with the understanding that the yield is below the 10-year Treasury and the residual position is a bet on either a confirmed transaction or a category inflection, neither of which is currently evidenced.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended July 31, 2026, reportable late August/early September 2026) shows organic net sales growth of +2% or better with gross margin flat-to-up, demonstrating the control-state transition is not destroying shipments.
2. FY2027 consensus EPS revisions turn positive over a 90-day window (currently −1.1%), or FY2028 consensus moves above $1.85, indicating a genuine earnings inflection rather than cost-savings arithmetic.
3. Verified 8-K disclosure of a live strategic process with Wolf Pen Branch participation, or a price near $23–24 that restores a 4%+ yield and a low-teens multiple against a stabilized earnings base.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. FY2027 organic net sales guidance or delivery below −3%, indicating the distribution overhaul has compounded rather than masked demand weakness.
2. A dividend increase omitted or the buyback suspended, or a second Moody's/S&P action below A2, confirming that the 116%-of-earnings distribution was unsustainable.
3. Confirmed material tariff action on American whiskey exports, or a CEO transition disclosed alongside a strategic reset that resets the earnings base lower.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q1 FY2027 release is the next hard decision point and the first verified data covering the deal termination, the June 1 control-state realignment, and the July 7 JDCC insourcing. A confirmatory or contradicting 8-K on the reported approach could arrive sooner and should be treated as the binary event it is.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory for this name has declined from 5.8 in February to 4.7 in July, with competitive position down 2.5 points and management quality down 2.0. Current evidence confirms rather than reverses that direction: a third consecutive year of revenue decline, FY2027 consensus below impairment-adjusted FY2026 earnings power, estimates still drifting lower, a rating downgrade, distributions at 116% of net income, $179M written off two brands purchased three years ago, and a proxy disclosing 149% and 172% incentive payouts against targets set at negative organic growth in a year shareholders lost roughly a quarter of their remaining value. Overall score moves to 4.3.

The change that justifies moving off HOLD is price, not fundamental deterioration: the stock is 7% higher than in mid-July on an unverifiable takeover report, while estimates fell and no fundamental datapoint improved. Price appreciation on speculation, with the value-realizing mechanism controlled by a family partnership that has reportedly declined and that leaves Class B holders without a vote, is deteriorating risk/reward. Expected total return of roughly zero against a 4.63% risk-free rate [unverified — Treasury yield not in provided sources], with a −20% bear case anchored on the 52-week low and a bull case whose exercise decision belongs to a party outside the shareholder base, is a less-than-fair risk-adjusted proposition. **REDUCE.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Executive Summary and "Q4 miss" analysis conflated the June 30, 2026 (Q1 FY2027) −63.5% miss with the FY2026 fourth quarter, which was actually a +22.6% beat ($0.58 vs $0.47); the "bad trailing quarter" framing that partly supports the REDUCE narrative rests on a quarter that post-dates the fiscal year under analysis.
