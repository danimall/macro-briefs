## Executive Summary

Hilltop Holdings is three businesses inside a bank holding company: PlainsCapital Bank ($8.67B loans, $10.51B deposits), PrimeLending (national retail residential mortgage, wholly owned by the bank rather than the holdco), and HilltopSecurities (Texas-centric municipal underwriting, structured finance, wealth management, and correspondent clearing). The banking segment produced $98.3M of $100.5M of consolidated 1H 2026 pre-tax income. The other two units, in aggregate, produced $22.8M and the corporate segment lost $20.6M.

Two findings dominate the current setup. First, reported earnings have been misleading in opposite directions in consecutive years. FY2025's recovery to $166M of net income was substantially flattered by corporate items — merchant-banking investment income was $41.7M in 1H 2025 versus $0.7M in 1H 2026 — and 1H 2026's headline pre-tax decline of $6.6M masks a $15.6M (+14.7%) improvement across the three operating segments. Second, Q2 2026 diluted EPS rose 10.5% ($0.57 to $0.63) while income attributable to Hilltop rose 1.2% ($36.07M to $36.52M). The difference is an 8.9% reduction in diluted shares. The buyback is driving the per-share line while the operating improvement sits underneath it.

The stock has priced much of this. At $39.13 HTH trades at 14.5x TTM diluted EPS of $2.70, 1.054x book value of $37.12, and 1.209x tangible book of $32.36, against a TTM return on tangible common equity of 8.56%. It sits 3.2% below its 52-week high after a 29.4% twelve-month advance, and the four covering analysts are unanimously at Hold with a $42 target (+7.1%). Against that, three specific items argue for caution rather than accumulation: criticized loans rose roughly 49% in six months (to ~$186.7M from ~$124.9M) while the allowance for credit losses fell from 1.10% to 0.98% of loans; PlainsCapital's CET1 ratio fell 258bps in twelve months to 12.50% as capital was upstreamed to fund repurchases, making the current pace of share shrinkage finite; and the CFO guided net interest margin to moderate in 2H 2026 after five consecutive quarters of expansion. This is a HOLD close to the boundary with REDUCE, sustained above it by a ~7% annualized capital-return yield and rising book value per share, not by a margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Hilltop is Dallas-based, with ~3,600 employees across 304 locations in 47 states and $16.0B of total assets at 6/30/26. Segment structure and Q2 2026 economics:

| Segment | Q2 2026 pre-tax | Key metric |
|---|---|---|
| Banking (PlainsCapital) | $51.2M | 1.28% ROA, 55.0% efficiency, 3.42% NIM |
| Broker-Dealer (HilltopSecurities) | $12.4M | $124.0M net revenue, 10.0% pre-tax margin |
| Mortgage (PrimeLending) | $(2.0)M | $2.394B originations, 86.7% purchase |
| All Other / Corporate | $(11.6)M implied | Merchant banking, holdco costs |

The competitive asset is a decades-old Texas franchise in municipal public finance and middle-market commercial banking. The cost is concentration that the filings show is not improving: at 12/31/25, 76% of real estate loans were secured by Texas properties (32% Dallas-Fort Worth, 17% Austin), 76% of public-finance net revenue came from Texas issuers, and 88% of retail brokerage net revenue was generated through Texas and California locations. Revenue-mix diversity across banking, mortgage, and brokerage is not the same as macro-factor diversification, and a Texas downturn hits all three simultaneously.

The three-segment structure does hedge rate cycles to a degree, and Q2 illustrated it cleanly: falling short rates cut interest-bearing deposit costs 54bps and lifted bank NIM, while the recent rise in mortgage rates compressed PrimeLending's gain-on-sale margin from 248bps to 217bps. That is a genuine, if modest, offset.

## Financial Analysis

### Net interest margin: high-quality expansion, with a funding-mix caveat

Banking-segment NIM expanded five consecutive quarters: 3.16% → 3.23% → 3.29% → 3.38% → 3.42%. Consolidated NIM went 3.01% to 3.21%. The mechanism is visible in the average-balance data: cost of interest-bearing deposits fell from 2.91% to 2.37%, average interest-bearing deposits at other institutions fell from $1,211.0M to $469.1M (yielding 3.65%) while average loans held for investment rose from $8,073M to $8,493M (yielding 5.73%). Net interest spread widened from 2.01% to 2.42%. Purchase-accounting accretion contributed only 2-3bps, so this is mix and repricing rather than an accounting artifact or duration extension.

The offset matters. Total deposits fell $364M in 1H 2026 (from $10.878B to $10.514B) while short-term borrowings rose $566M (from $676.9M to $1,243.2M) and cash fell $481M. Notes payable and other borrowings cost 4.60% in Q2 versus 2.37% for interest-bearing deposits. Funding 7.6% year-over-year loan growth with wholesale borrowings and balance-sheet cash is arithmetically a NIM headwind, which is consistent with the CFO's guidance that NIM moderates in 2H. Noninterest-bearing deposits were $2.744B, 26.1% of total, down ~$88M in Q2 alone.

### Credit: improving headline, deteriorating internals

Headline metrics improved. Non-accrual loans fell to $54.8M (0.57% of loans) from $72.7M (0.80%); non-performing assets are 0.39% of total assets versus 0.53%; 1H 2026 net charge-offs of $7.47M annualize to 0.176% of average loans.

Beneath that, three things changed direction:

1. **Problem-loan mix rotated from C&I to CRE.** Non-accrual C&I fell from $40.99M (6/30/25) to $17.57M (6/30/26). Over the same window non-owner-occupied CRE non-accruals rose from $4.11M to $13.79M and owner-occupied CRE from $6.43M to $10.77M.
2. **Criticized assets rose while reserves fell.** Potential problem loans rose from roughly $124.9M to roughly $186.7M in six months, concentrated in substandard-accrual non-owner-occupied CRE (~$32M to ~$79M), with a special-mention bucket appearing that was nil at year-end. Over the same period the allowance fell from $91.5M to $84.9M — 1.10% to 0.98% of loans held for investment — and Q2 booked a $974K net provision *reversal*. Management's disclosure is explicit that the CECL macro model drove the release, "partially offset by a build in the allowance related to specific reserves."
3. **90+ days past due and still accruing rose to $40.2M** from $33.8M, though the company identifies these as primarily agency-guaranteed loans held for sale subject to PrimeLending repurchase mechanics, not core bank credit.

The alternative reading deserves weight: substandard-accrual loans are still performing and frequently cure, 66% of banking loans are real-estate secured, and the migration may reflect proactive downgrades of adequately collateralized credits. I do not infer earnings management; the disclosure is transparent in both directions. But the quantification is straightforward — moving from a 0.176% annualized charge-off rate to a mid-cycle 0.40% on the $8.67B book costs roughly $19M pre-tax, or about $0.25 of EPS. The provision line has been a tailwind in three of the last five quarters, and it is the single largest swing factor in the forward estimate.

### Capital, book value, and securities marks

Book value per share rose from $34.90 to $37.12 over four quarters even as total Hilltop equity fell from $2,198.6M to $2,126.2M — deliberate equity shrinkage against $161.8M of TTM earnings. Tangible common equity is $1,853.7M ($2,126.2M less $267.4M goodwill and $5.1M other intangibles), or $32.36 per share.

Securities marks are manageable. AOCI is -$81.0M, improved from -$94.7M. The held-to-maturity portfolio carries $745.2M amortized cost against $686.7M fair value, a $58.5M unrealized loss not in equity; recognized at a 21% tax rate that reduces TBVPS by roughly $0.81, to about $31.55. That is a modest adjustment, not a capital hole.

Capital ratios show a deliberate drawdown:

| | 6/30/25 | 6/30/26 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| PlainsCapital CET1/RWA | 15.08% | 12.50% | -258bps |
| PlainsCapital Tier 1/avg assets | 10.71% | 9.73% | -98bps |
| Hilltop CET1/RWA | 20.74% | 18.34% | -240bps |

Roughly $235M of subsidiary dividends were upstreamed in 1H 2026 to fund repurchases, while 7.6% loan growth consumed RWA. Consolidated CET1 of 18.34% remains among the highest in the regional-bank universe and holdco double leverage is minimal (notes payable $148.7M against $2,126.2M of equity, 7.0%). But bank-level buffer is being spent at roughly 250bps per year. At that pace PlainsCapital reaches a typical 10-11% operating CET1 target within 18-24 months, after which repurchases must be funded from earnings and the pace of share shrinkage — the primary driver of reported EPS growth — mechanically slows. That is a duration question about the EPS tailwind, not a solvency question.

### Earnings quality and the buyback trade-off

FY2026 1H repurchases were 2,488,216 shares at an average $37.99, or ~$94.5M, with the authorization raised in July 2026 to $200M (~$106M remaining through January 2027). The quarterly dividend went from $0.18 to $0.20 to $0.22 declared for August payment, a 10% increase; payout is 31.7%. Share count fell from 64,968k (12/31/24) to 57,284k (6/30/26), -11.8% in eighteen months.

Buying at $37.99 against TTM EPS of $2.70 is a 7.1% earnings yield versus roughly 3.0% after-tax on redeployed cash, so it is clearly EPS-accretive. It is simultaneously tangible-book-dilutive: paying a ~$5.9 premium to TBVPS on 2,488,216 shares costs ~$14.7M, or about $0.26 per remaining share per half-year. The reconciliation holds — 1H EPS of $1.27 less $0.40 of dividends is $0.87 retained, less $0.26 of buyback dilution is $0.61 against an observed TBVPS increase of $0.52, with the residual in stock compensation and AOCI. Management is trading tangible book growth for EPS growth. At 1.21x TBV that is a defensible judgment; at a materially higher multiple it stops being one.

### Earnings surprise record and estimate calibration

Four consecutive beats of 27.1%, 30.2%, 63.7%, and 49.5%. The magnitude says more about the calibration of a three-to-four-analyst consensus than about accelerating fundamentals — Q2's $0.63 beat a $0.42 Zacks consensus while GAAP income to Hilltop rose 1.2%. The practical implication is that the published FY2026 consensus of $2.41, which is *below* TTM actual of $2.70, is probably too low. My working FY2026 figure is ~$2.55 (1H actual $1.27 plus a seasonally stronger but NIM-pressured 2H), and FY2027 ~$2.65-2.75 on continued buyback plus modest operating growth, against consensus $2.49 (revised +3.8% over 30 days, +4.3% over 90 days). The revision table diverges internally: out-year estimates are drifting up while the next-quarter estimate was cut 8.5%, consistent with NIM moderation and mortgage margin compression near-term.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three identifiable levers, each reproducible from the segment data:

1. **Banking.** FY2026 average loan growth guidance was raised to 5-7%, explicitly excluding mortgage warehouse lending and PrimeLending-retained loans, so this is core commercial and CRE growth. Q2 delivered 1.28% ROA and a 55.0% efficiency ratio. This is the durable engine.
2. **Broker-dealer margin.** Pre-tax margin ran 5.8%, 18.3%, 18.4%, 12.7%, 10.0% over the last five quarters. Compensation-to-net-revenue improved from 67.0% to 62.0% year over year on 13% revenue growth. On ~$480M of annualized net revenue, reverting from the 11.3% 1H margin toward the 18%+ achieved in 2H 2025 would be worth roughly $29M pre-tax; half of that is ~$15M.
3. **Mortgage.** Moving PrimeLending from a $4.4M half-year pre-tax loss to breakeven is worth ~$8.8M annualized.

Levers 2 and 3 together are roughly $24M pre-tax, ~$18M after tax at a 24% effective rate, ~$0.32 per share, taking EPS toward $3.00 and ROTCE toward ~10.3%. Neither is guided, and neither is under management's full control — broker-dealer margin depends on municipal issuance volume and structured-finance opportunity, mortgage on housing turnover and rates.

Working against this: NIM guided to moderate; ~10% of loans sit at interest-rate floors that the 10-K says are expected to reprice to contractual rates at reset, eroding a yield support; 61% of loans are variable-rate, so the down-200bp NII sensitivity is roughly -4%; gain-on-sale margins compressed 31bps sequentially, worth roughly $6M of quarterly revenue on ~$2.0B of sales; and the provision line has more room to hurt than help from a 0.98% allowance. Growth is real but modest and share-count-assisted, which is why I score this dimension at 5.3.

## Valuation Assessment

There is a data conflict worth flagging: the quote feed reports "forward P/E" of 14.58 identical to trailing P/E, computed on FY2025 basic EPS of $2.64. That is not a forward multiple. I use TTM diluted EPS of $2.70 and consensus of $2.41 (FY2026) and $2.49 (FY2027).

At $39.125:

| Metric | HTH | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (TTM diluted $2.70) | 14.5x | 12.1x |
| P/E (FY2026 consensus $2.41) | 16.2x | — |
| P/E (FY2027 consensus $2.49) | 15.7x | — |
| P/E (my FY2026 estimate ~$2.55) | 15.3x | — |
| P/Book ($37.12) | 1.054x | — |
| P/Tangible book ($32.36) | 1.209x | — |
| Dividend yield ($0.88 run-rate) | 2.25% | 2.9% |
| TTM ROTCE | 8.56% | — |

HTH trades at a premium to the regional-bank peer median P/E (MBIN 12.1x, NWBI 15.6x, OFG 9.8x, FCF 12.9x, CUBI 9.8x) on both trailing and forward earnings, and at a below-median dividend yield, while generating a below-average return on tangible equity. That combination is not the profile of a mispriced security.

The bull-side reconciliation is excess capital. A single-stage relationship P/TBV = (ROTCE − g)/(COE − g) at 3% growth and 10% cost of equity implies the current 1.21x multiple requires ~11.5% sustainable ROTCE against 8.56% actual — a ~300bp gap. Much of that gap is explained by carrying capital the business does not need. Consolidated CET1 of 18.34% against a normalized 11% target on roughly $11.1B of risk-weighted assets implies something on the order of $800M of surplus, more than a third of market capitalization. Earnings measured against a normalized capital base would produce ROTCE in the 11% range, which reconciles to the multiple. The market is already crediting Hilltop for returning excess capital: the multiple is not cheap on reported returns and is roughly fair on normalized returns.

What that leaves is a capital-return yield of ~7% (2.25% dividend plus $106M of remaining authorization, 4.7% of market cap, through January 2027) and TBVPS compounding at roughly $1.00/year net of buyback dilution. Those support the price; they do not create asymmetry.

**Scenario values (12 months):**

- **Base $41 (+4.8%):** 15.5x normalized EPS of ~$2.65, cross-checked at 1.24x forward TBVPS of ~$33.0. Assumes bank NIM plateaus in the 3.35-3.42% range, loan growth at the 5-7% guide, broker-dealer margin in the low teens, mortgage near breakeven, provisions modestly positive expense, and continued buyback at a slower pace.
- **Bull $47 (+20.1%):** EPS ~$3.00 (broker-dealer margin back to 18%, mortgage breakeven, no provision build) at 15.5x, cross-checked at ~1.4x TBVPS of $33.5. Requires the two fee businesses to hit their own recent high-water marks simultaneously.
- **Bear $30 (-23.3%):** Texas CRE migration converts to charge-offs at ~0.40% (-$0.25 EPS), NIM compresses on wholesale funding, mortgage goodwill impairment (GAAP only), and the multiple resets to ~0.95x TBV. Roughly the 52-week low of $30.25.

Weighting base 58% / bull 20% / bear 22% gives a probability-weighted price return of ~+1.7%, or ~+4.0% including the dividend. That is approximately fair and not compelling. The consensus $42 target (+7.1%) sits between my base and bull cases, consistent with four Hold ratings and no revisions. The consensus target should not be read as a floor here: the stock is at 15.7x forward with mid-single-digit earnings growth, not at a compressed multiple against accelerating estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

PlainsCapital competes in Dallas-Fort Worth and Austin, among the fastest-growing and most contested U.S. banking markets, and the 10-K concedes competitors have "substantially greater resources and lending limits." The evidence it is competing effectively is concrete: 7.6% loan growth, five quarters of NIM expansion to 3.42%, 55.0% efficiency, 1.28% ROA. On a standalone basis this is a good regional bank.

The consolidated entity screens differently. Q2 noninterest expense of $266.7M against $315.8M of revenue is an 84.5% expense-to-revenue ratio, and employee compensation alone was 57.0% of revenue. A 55% efficiency bank is buried inside a cost structure inflated by two fee businesses that carry 60-67% compensation ratios by industry convention. That is the most plausible explanation for the persistent ~1.05x book multiple and for the proxy's disclosure that five-year TSR of 136 trailed the company's own selected peer index at 143, with outperformance in only one of five years.

Segment trajectories diverge. The bank is improving. The broker-dealer's Texas municipal advisory and underwriting franchise is the least commoditized asset in the group, and Q2 showed five points of compensation leverage on 13% revenue growth, but the pre-tax margin series is extremely volatile and the 10-K flags correspondent self-clearing attrition (large correspondents converting to self-clearing as they scale) as a specific risk. PrimeLending is the problem: ~$9-10B of annual volume is sub-scale against money-center banks and large independent mortgage banks, the segment has lost money in three of the last four half-year periods on the disclosed record, and PlainsCapital made a $5.0M capital infusion in Q2 2026 to maintain HUD/GNMA net worth compliance, following larger infusions in prior years. No restructuring or exit plan has been disclosed.

## Risk Assessment

**Texas CRE concentration with early deterioration.** CRE was ~41% of the banking loan book at 12/31/25 and 66% of banking loans are real-estate collateralized, with 76% Texas collateral and 32% DFW. Non-owner-occupied CRE non-accruals rose 3.6x in six months, substandard-accrual CRE roughly doubled, and the allowance fell to 0.98% of loans. This is the highest-conviction risk in the file and the primary reason the risk dimension scores below 5.

**Finite buyback runway.** Bank CET1 at 12.50% after a 258bp twelve-month drawdown implies 18-24 months before repurchases must be funded from earnings. Since substantially all reported EPS growth is share-count arithmetic, a slower buyback directly reduces reported growth even if operating earnings improve.

**Mortgage goodwill.** $267.4M of goodwill, with the October 1, 2025 test concluding only that it was "more likely than not" that fair value exceeded carrying value — language that signals a thin cushion, on a unit that "experienced lower-than-forecasted operating results during 2025." The 10-K states such a charge would have no impact on tangible or regulatory capital, so it would hit GAAP EPS and book value but not TBVPS, capital ratios, or buyback capacity.

**Funding and liquidity.** Uninsured deposits of $5.7B (55% of total), $4.8B (45%) excluding collateralized and internal accounts, against $750.5M of cash plus $1,450.6M of AFS securities, roughly 46% coverage before FHLB and Federal Reserve capacity. Top-15 depositor concentration is in the mid-teens percent of bank deposits. Cash fell $481M in 1H as it was deployed into loans, so the buffer is thinner than a year ago while the funding mix shifted toward wholesale.

**Legal and regulatory.** A federal criminal investigation of PrimeLending's mortgage "float-down" option, with grand jury subpoenas from the U.S. Attorney (W.D. Va.) dating to September 2020, has been open roughly six years without resolution. The 2023 MOVEit vendor breach — under which an unauthorized party "likely obtained information about substantially all of the Bank's customers, including social security numbers and account numbers" — leaves ongoing litigation and regulatory exposure. Item 1A also acknowledges past material weaknesses in internal control (none currently disclosed) and newly prominent language on AI, amended Regulation S-P, "debanking" scrutiny, and CFPB uncertainty.

**Rate positioning cuts both ways.** With 61% of loans variable and ~10% at floors scheduled to reprice, Fed cuts pressure NII (down-200bp sensitivity roughly -4%). The offsetting exposure is that falling long rates help mortgage volume but impair the newly rebuilt MSR asset, while rising long rates do the reverse — the Q2 result (bank NIM up, mortgage margins down on the recent increase in mortgage rates) illustrates these offsetting exposures. [unverified — no macro figures for the Fed policy path, Treasury yield levels, or dissent count appear in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**Governance.** Directors are removable only for cause and only by two-thirds vote; a 15%/one-year threshold applies to calling a special meeting; there is no cumulative voting; 10 million preferred shares are issuable at board discretion; and Item 1A discloses that Hilltop has no provision requiring an interested director to abstain and "does not expect to add" one. If the conglomerate-discount thesis is right, the mechanism for closing it — external pressure to separate the fee businesses — is structurally impeded. Value realization requires management to choose it, and the observed choice has been buybacks. Seven Item 5.02 filings between April 2025 and April 2026 indicate elevated officer/director churn; the underlying documents were not readable and the reasons are unresolved.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility fell from 93.1% (July 4) to 62.5% (August 7) while the price rose 1.0%, a standard post-earnings volatility crush around the July 23 print. The residual 62.5% is implausibly high for a $2.2B regional bank; the peer benchmark table reports a peer-group median beta of 0.73 (HTH's own beta is not provided). This points to thin, illiquid option series rather than genuine event pricing; I assign this low weight. Short interest of 6.5% of float at 7.0 days to cover is uncrowded and does not corroborate a bear thesis. There is no options evidence contradicting or confirming the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($47, +20%)

The three operating segments improved pre-tax income 14.7% year over year in 1H 2026, and that improvement is masked in headline EPS by the absence of prior-year merchant-banking gains. The bank is a legitimately good franchise: 1.28% ROA, 55% efficiency, five straight quarters of NIM expansion driven by mix and deposit repricing rather than duration risk, and 5-7% guided core loan growth in DFW and Austin. Two fee-business levers are identifiable and quantified: broker-dealer margin reverting to the 18% achieved in 2H 2025 (~$29M pre-tax) and PrimeLending reaching breakeven (~$8.8M). Together they take EPS toward $3.00 and ROTCE toward ~10.3%. The company is returning ~7% of market capitalization annually through a 2.25% dividend just raised 10% and $106M of remaining repurchase authorization, funded from a consolidated CET1 ratio of 18.34% that is among the highest in the industry, with holdco double leverage of only 7.0%. TBVPS is compounding at roughly $1.00 per year despite buybacks above tangible book. Consensus of $2.41 for FY2026 sits below TTM actual of $2.70 after four consecutive beats averaging 42.6%, so estimate risk is skewed upward.

### Bear Case ($30, -23%)

Substantially all reported EPS growth is share-count reduction, and the funding source for that reduction is depleting — bank CET1 has fallen 258bps in twelve months to 12.50% and reaches normal operating levels within roughly two years. Credit is deteriorating where it matters: criticized loans up ~49% in six months, concentrated in non-owner-occupied Texas CRE, while the allowance fell to 0.98% of loans and Q2 booked a net provision reversal. Normalizing charge-offs to 0.40% costs ~$0.25 of EPS. Deposits fell $364M in 1H while short-term borrowings rose $566M at 4.60%, a mechanical NIM headwind that management has already flagged for 2H. PrimeLending is chronically sub-scale, loss-making, and periodically recapitalized by the bank, with the most impairment-prone goodwill in the $267.4M balance. Five-year TSR trailed the company's own peer index, governance is structurally insulated against outside pressure, and the only insider activity in the recent window was selling — a director reduced his position ~56% (72,555 shares, ~$2.72M) into strength, with no open-market purchases by any officer or director. At 15.7x forward consensus versus a 12.1x peer median, a below-median dividend yield, and 8.56% ROTCE, the stock offers no cushion if either credit or NIM disappoints.

### Where I come out

The operating improvement is real and under-appreciated in headline numbers; the valuation already reflects the capital-return story and normalized-capital returns. Probability-weighted, base 58% / bull 20% / bear 22% produces roughly a 4% twelve-month total return. That is approximately fair, which is what HOLD means. I am not upgrading because there is no margin of safety 3.2% below the 52-week high with the allowance at a cycle-low 0.98% and criticized CRE building. I am not downgrading to REDUCE because the ~7% capital-return yield, 1.05x book multiple, rising BVPS, and industry-leading consolidated capital position make the downside path require an actual credit event rather than mere disappointment.

On trajectory: this is the fifth consecutive HOLD, with the score drifting from 6.0 (April) to 5.5 now. The decline is not price-driven — the stock is essentially flat against the July 4 reference of $38.82. It reflects two specific fundamental changes disclosed in the Q2 filings: the allowance falling to 0.98% while criticized loans rose ~49%, and the funding mix shifting $566M into wholesale borrowings as deposits declined $364M. Both are quantified, both are new since the prior review, and both justify lower risk and financial-health marks. Management quality steps down modestly from 6.0 to 5.8 on the one-directional insider record and continued absence of any strategic decision on a loss-making segment, offset by the dividend increase and disciplined compensation ($3.53M CEO total, down four consecutive years, 1.5x CEO/NEO ratio).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD. Hold rationale: uncertain** — the credit and NIM paths are genuinely two-sided, and the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction from $39.13.

**Twelve-month scenario framework (from $39.125):**

| Case | Value | Return | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull | $47 | +20.1% | ~$3.00 EPS at 15.5x; ~1.4x TBVPS $33.5 |
| Base | $41 | +4.8% | ~$2.65 EPS at 15.5x; ~1.24x TBVPS $33.0 |
| Bear | $30 | -23.3% | ~$2.35 EPS at ~12x; ~0.95x TBVPS |

Probability-weighted (58/20/22): ~+1.7% price, ~+4.0% total return including the $0.88 dividend.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 or Q4 2026 disclosure showing criticized/substandard loans flat or declining sequentially **and** the allowance stabilizing at or above 1.00% of loans held for investment. This is the single most important item to monitor.
2. Broker-dealer pre-tax margin printing 15%+ for two consecutive quarters, confirming the 2H 2025 level was structural rather than a one-off, worth ~$0.20+ of annualized EPS.
3. A price near $33-34 (roughly 1.02-1.05x tangible book, ~12.5x normalized EPS) without fundamental deterioration, which would restore a genuine margin of safety.
4. Disclosed strategic action on PrimeLending — sale, restructuring, or a credible path to sustained profitability — which would remove both the capital-infusion drag and the goodwill overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Quarterly net charge-offs annualizing above 0.35% of average loans, or non-accrual CRE exceeding $25M, which would convert the criticized-asset build into realized loss.
2. Banking-segment NIM contracting below 3.25% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the wholesale-funding mix shift is overwhelming deposit repricing benefit.
3. Deposits declining further while short-term borrowings exceed $1.5B, or noninterest-bearing deposits falling below 24% of total.

Hilltop is a good Texas commercial bank carrying a volatile municipal broker-dealer and a sub-scale, loss-making mortgage originator, with an unusually large capital surplus being returned through aggressive repurchases. Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 4% over twelve months, against a bear case 23% below the current price, is a fair-value outcome rather than an opportunity.

**HOLD.** Existing owners are compensated by a ~7% capital-return yield and roughly $1.00 per year of tangible book accretion while the credit question resolves.
