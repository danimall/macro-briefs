## Executive Summary

Adamas Trust (formerly New York Mortgage Trust) is a mid-cap internally-managed mortgage REIT that completed a significant strategic transformation in 2025, pivoting from a credit-heavy, multi-family portfolio toward Agency RMBS-dominated strategy. The results are visible: net income swung from a $104M loss in FY2024 to $101M profit in FY2025, EAD per share grew 141% to $0.89, and the investment portfolio expanded 42% to $10.5 billion.

The stock trades at $8.86, representing a 7.7% discount to GAAP book value ($9.60) and 16.7% discount to adjusted book value ($10.63), with a current annualized dividend yield of approximately 10.3%. Two consecutive quarterly earnings beats of 75% and 95% confirm the turnaround's momentum. However, recourse leverage increased from 3.0x to 5.0x over the year, 70% of recourse debt is subject to mark-to-market margin calls, and the company is issuing unsecured debt at 9.125-9.875% — a spread that signals material credit risk priced by fixed income investors.

The investment case reduces to a question of leverage tolerance: the income stream is attractive and improving, but the capital structure is fragile in precisely the macro environment currently developing (rising long-end yields, potential rate hikes, geopolitical oil supply disruption driving inflation higher).

## Company Overview

Adamas Trust operates two segments: an Investment Portfolio (~$10.7B) dominated by Agency RMBS (63%), residential loans including business purpose loans (30%), and legacy multi-family/other credit (7%); and Constructive, a wholly-owned business purpose loan originator operating in 48 states that originated approximately $1.8B in loans during FY2025. The company is internally managed with 221 employees (72% at Constructive).

The strategic pivot since 2022 has been dramatic. Agency RMBS went from essentially zero to $6.6B in three years, funded primarily by short-term repurchase agreements ($6.8B outstanding). The company securitized $945.5M of credit assets in FY2025, reducing repo reliance for the credit book. Constructive, acquired fully in July 2025, provides proprietary access to higher-yielding business purpose loans — a meaningful operational differentiator versus pure portfolio-based mREIT peers.

**Free Cash Flow:** $125.9M (operating CF $134M less $8.2M capex). Dividend payments to common shareholders totaled approximately $77.6M ($0.86/share × 90.2M shares), covered by both net income and operating cash flow.

**Dividend Coverage:** Current annual rate of $0.92/share against EAD of $0.89/share and EPS of $1.12. EAD coverage is marginally tight; GAAP earnings provide adequate support assuming no BV impairment.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth has been exceptional: 50% interest income CAGR driven by aggressive portfolio expansion from $7.4B to $10.5B. However, this growth was funded entirely by additional leverage (repo + unsecured notes), which fundamentally limits the quality of the growth.

Forward estimates show current year EPS at $1.06 (4 analysts) with next year at $1.07 — essentially flat growth from the FY2025 $1.12 base. This reflects: (1) the company approaching a natural leverage ceiling, (2) expectations that Agency RMBS spreads remain compressed, and (3) the Constructive segment needing time to reach profitability (lost $8.5M in first 5.5 months).

Estimate revisions tell a mixed story: current year estimates revised up 10.3% over 90 days (reflecting the Q1 2026 beat), but next year revised down 4.4% — analysts are upgrading near-term on momentum while tempering medium-term expectations, potentially reflecting rate environment concerns.

The Constructive platform represents genuine growth optionality. If it reaches breakeven (requires ~$2.5B+ in annual origination volume at current economics) and continues feeding proprietary assets into the portfolio, it provides a differentiated earnings stream. Current trajectory suggests this is 12-18 months away.

## Valuation Assessment

At $8.86, ADAM trades at:
- 7.6x trailing/forward P/E
- 0.92x GAAP book value ($9.60)
- 0.83x adjusted book value ($10.63)
- 10.3% dividend yield (annualized $0.92)

**Versus peers:**
- Peer median P/E: 14.8x (ADAM at 7.6x — 49% discount)
- Peer median yield: 11.8% (ADAM at 10.3% — below peer average)
- ABR trades at 14.8x with 11.8% yield; PMT at 9.1x with 15.2% yield

The discount to both book value and peer P/E multiples reflects the market's assessment of: (1) ADAM's 5-year TSR underperformance (91.34 vs. peer group 113.98), (2) the rapid leverage increase, (3) the recent loss history requiring more consistent earnings before re-rating, and (4) unsecured debt pricing (9.125-9.875%) confirming elevated credit risk.

The book value discount is reasonable for an mREIT in transition, but if ADAM delivers 2-3 more quarters of EAD near $0.20-0.25/share, the discount should narrow. At $9.60 GAAP BV growing at the company's stated 12.7% economic return rate, year-end 2026 BV would be ~$10.80, implying 18% upside from current price plus the ~10% yield — a total return of ~28% in a base case where execution continues.

The bull case in the analyst universe ($24 high target) appears to reflect a scenario where the stock re-rates to 1.5x BV at normalized earnings — plausible only if leverage stabilizes and the company sustains 12%+ ROE for multiple years.

## Competitive Landscape

ADAM competes against established agency mREITs (AGNC, NLY — both significantly larger and more liquid), hybrid/credit mREITs (MFA, TWO, ABR), and increasingly against private credit funds and banks. Its competitive positioning is middling:

**Advantages:**
- Internal management eliminates external fee drag (2-4% of equity annually at typical external managers)
- Constructive origination platform creates proprietary asset sourcing — most peers lack vertically integrated origination
- Active securitization program (4 transactions in 2025) demonstrates capital markets execution capability
- Diversified across Agency and credit — can shift allocation based on spread environment

**Disadvantages:**
- Scale disadvantage versus AGNC ($59B portfolio) and NLY ($79B) limits repo pricing power
- 5-year TSR of 91.34 vs. peer 113.98 demonstrates historical capital allocation weakness
- $1.41B accumulated deficit limits financial flexibility
- Senior unsecured pricing (9.1-9.9%) is higher than larger peers, reflecting market's credit risk assessment
- Constructive is pre-profit and adds operational complexity (48-state licensing, repurchase obligations)

Market share trajectory is improving through portfolio growth, but the competitive position relative to better-capitalized, lower-leverage peers remains disadvantaged.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Leverage/Margin Call Risk (HIGH):** The 5.0x recourse leverage ratio with $6.8B in short-term repo subject to mark-to-market represents the dominant risk. A 2-3% adverse move in Agency RMBS prices (achievable with a 30-50bps rate spike) would trigger margin calls potentially exceeding available liquidity. The company experienced exactly this scenario in March 2020 and was forced into distressed asset sales. With current leverage significantly higher than at the start of 2025, the fragility is quantifiably greater.

2. **Rate Environment (HIGH):** If rates rise further, Agency RMBS prices decline, NIM compresses (as repo rates reprice faster than asset yields), and book value erodes. At 5.0x leverage, a 1% decline in Agency RMBS prices would reduce book value by approximately $0.37/share (3.9%).

3. **Preferred Stock Floating Rate Conversion:** Series F preferred converts to floating rate in October 2026. At current SOFR levels, preferred dividend obligations will increase, directly reducing distributable earnings to common.

4. **Constructive Execution:** The origination platform is operating at a loss and adds regulatory/operational risk. If loan volumes decline due to a CRE slowdown or tighter credit, the segment becomes a cost center rather than a growth engine.

5. **Geopolitical/Inflation:** In an environment with elevated long-term Treasury yields, mREITs face adverse conditions. Further yield increases would compress Agency MBS prices and potentially force de-leveraging at unfavorable levels.

**Mitigating Factors:**
- $206.5M cash + $454M unencumbered securities provides margin call buffer
- Short interest only 3.4% (no crowded short pressure)
- Company has managed through several volatility episodes since the March 2020 forced sales
- Internal management structure means no external manager making leverage decisions contrary to shareholder interest

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($10.50-11.00, +18-24%):**
- EAD continues at $0.20-0.25/share quarterly (in line with recent results)
- Book value grows toward $10.80+ by year-end 2026 as spread income accumulates
- Constructive reaches breakeven and provides proprietary asset flow
- Stock re-rates toward 1.0x adjusted BV (from current 0.83x)
- Rate environment stabilizes — total return with dividends: 28-34% over 12 months
- Probability: 30-35%

**Bear Case ($6.50-7.00, -23-27%):**
- Rate hikes materialize; long-end yields sustain at elevated levels
- Agency RMBS prices decline 3-4%, triggering margin calls and forced de-leveraging
- Book value declines to $8.50-9.00 as mark-to-market losses accumulate
- Dividend cut required as EAD compresses below current $0.23/quarter rate
- Stock trades to 0.80-0.85x reduced BV — historical trough valuation for troubled mREITs
- Probability: 25-30%

**Base Case ($9.00-9.50, +2-7% price; ~12% total with dividends):**
- Rates remain volatile but stable in a range; no hike materializes
- EAD stabilizes at $0.20-0.22/share (slight compression from current levels)
- Book value grows modestly to $9.80-10.00
- Dividend maintained at current rate
- Stock trades sideways, supported by yield
- Total return: 10-17% over 12 months including dividends
- Probability: 35-40%

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD (Income):**

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Recourse leverage declines below 4.0x through portfolio rotation or equity issuance, reducing margin call risk
2. Rate environment shifts dovish — if rate hike probability falls substantially, the macro headwind dissipates
3. Constructive reaches quarterly profitability, confirming the vertical integration thesis

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Recourse leverage exceeds 6.0x or margin calls force asset sales at losses
2. Dividend cut announced (would signal EAD deterioration)
3. Book value declines more than 10% in a single quarter ($8.64 or below)
4. Long-term Treasury yields sustained at significantly elevated levels for 30+ days (creates acute leverage stress)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (likely late July 2026 for Q2). The rate environment trajectory over the next 60 days will determine whether the leverage burden becomes acute or remains manageable.

**Income Thesis Support:** At $8.86 with $0.92 annualized dividend, the 10.3% yield provides meaningful compensation while waiting for either the bull or bear scenario to emerge. The hold-for-income thesis remains valid as long as dividends are maintained and no forced de-leveraging occurs. Expected 12-month total return in base case: 10-17%.

## Conclusion

ADAM presents a genuine income opportunity at a 16.7% discount to adjusted book value with a 10.3% yield, supported by two consecutive quarters of substantial earnings beats and improving EAD. The strategic transformation from a chronic loss-maker to a profitable, internally-managed mREIT is substantive and confirmed by financial data.

However, the increase of recourse leverage to 5.0x, combined with 70% of debt subject to margin calls and a macro environment trending toward rate hikes, creates a fragility that prevents a higher-conviction recommendation. The unsecured debt market's pricing (9.1-9.9%) reflects what fixed income investors assess as the credit risk. The absence of insider open-market purchases at a material book value discount is a secondary concern.

This is an income hold: the yield compensates for waiting, the fundamentals are improving, but the capital structure creates a binary tail risk that is not adequately compensated at the current P/BV discount. Investors with explicit mREIT exposure mandates and tolerance for 25-30% drawdown scenarios in a rate shock can hold for the yield and potential BV appreciation; those without should remain on the sidelines until leverage stabilizes or the rate environment de-risks.
