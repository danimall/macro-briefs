**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ingersoll Rand is a global manufacturer of mission-critical flow-creation and industrial/life-science equipment (compressors, vacuum pumps, blowers, positive-displacement pumps, and fluid-handling systems) sold under a portfolio of premier brands. The business is high-quality: products are process-critical but represent a small fraction of total system cost, so customers prioritize reliability and service over price, generating recurring aftermarket revenue equal to 36.5% of total. Free cash flow is strong and consistent at $1,220M in 2025 (15.9% margin), the balance sheet is investment-grade at ~1.7x net leverage, and the faster-growing Precision & Science Technologies (PST) segment carries 30% EBITDA margins with secular exposure to life sciences, water, and renewable natural gas.

The problem is that all of the reported growth is inorganic. FY2025 revenue rose 5.7% to $7,651M, but the bridge was +$420M acquisitions and +$92M FX against −$96M organic. Q1 2026 repeated the pattern: +7.6% headline, −0.3% organic, with PST +4.4% offsetting ITS −1.6%. The profit engine (ITS, ~79% of revenue) is in an organic decline tracking soft industrial capacity utilization, and adjusted EBITDA margin compressed 50bps in 2025 and 140bps in Q1'26 on operating deleverage. Layered on top is a deal-execution blemish: the 2024 ILC Dover acquisition was substantially impaired ($229.7M goodwill + $36.1M tradename) within a year of close, and IR has filed a representations-and-warranties insurance claim on the transaction with uncertain recovery.

We rate IR a HOLD. The stock has already de-rated ~23% from its February 2026 high of $100.96 to $77.91, sits at the bottom of the analyst price-target range ($80 low), and trades at roughly 22x forward earnings — a premium that has compressed but does not yet offer asymmetric upside given negative organic volumes and a token dividend that provides no income support. Much of the cyclical bear case is in the price, which is why we do not rate it AVOID. But without organic momentum, without insider buying, and with an unresolved M&A-execution question, there is no catalyst to justify a BUY either. The setup is a quality franchise in a soft cycle.

## Company Overview

IR operates through two segments. **Industrial Technologies & Services (ITS)** is ~79% of revenue ($6,056M in 2025), comprising compression, vacuum, and blower equipment plus a high-attach aftermarket (40.6% mix), at a 28.9% segment EBITDA margin. **Precision & Science Technologies (PST)** is ~21% ($1,595M), covering specialized pumps, fluid management, single-use powder handling, and medical components, at a 30.0% margin and faster growth. The company runs 60+ manufacturing plants and 50+ service centers across six continents with 21,000+ employees, and operates an "IRX" execution playbook paired with serial bolt-on M&A.

The competitive moat is the installed base. Because the equipment is mission-critical but low-cost relative to the total process, customers value uptime and service responsiveness, producing the recurring aftermarket revenue that differentiates IR structurally from pure equipment makers. CEO Vicente Reynal architected the Gardner Denver–Ingersoll Rand merger and has built the M&A flywheel that defines the current model.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $5,916M (2022) → $6,876M (2023) → $7,235M (2024) → $7,651M (2025). The 2025 bridge is the central fact: +$420M acquisitions, +$92M FX, **−$96M organic**.

**Margins and earnings:** Gross margin held near 43.6% but is compressing on lower-margin product mix and one-for-one tariff pass-through (which dilutes the percentage). Adjusted EBITDA margin was 27.4% in 2025 and 25.4% in Q1'26, evidence of organic operating deleverage. GAAP net income fell to $581M from $839M, driven by $273M of impairments and a $127M equity-method investment loss; adjusted net income was roughly flat at ~$1,348M.

**Cash flow:** This is the strongest part of the story and the piece that anchors the HOLD rather than an AVOID. FCF was $1,220M in 2025 on $1,356M operating cash flow, with capex light at ~1.8% of revenue. On a $30.5B market cap that is a ~4% FCF yield. Q1'26 FCF dipped to $163M from $223M on working-capital build and higher cash taxes, a seasonal/timing item rather than a structural break. A franchise generating $1.2B of FCF with light capital intensity does not carry the cash-flow profile of an impaired business.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $4,785M is entirely unsecured senior notes (5.2%–5.7% coupons, laddered 2027–2054), supported by an undrawn $2.6B revolver and $2.6B CP program. Net leverage is ~1.7x against a 3.5x covenant; weighted-average rate ~5.0%. The Altman Z of 2.06 ("grey zone") is a false signal here, depressed by the impairment-driven negative EBIT/assets term and the goodwill-heavy asset base, not by liquidity stress. Current ratio is 2.06 and equity/assets is 0.55. The genuine balance-sheet risk is not solvency; it is that goodwill and intangibles total $12.7B, or 70% of assets, so any forecast deterioration triggers further non-cash charges.

**Capital allocation:** IR repurchased $1.0B of stock in 2025 (vs. $261M in 2024), reducing share count from ~403M to ~391M, with ~$903M of authorization remaining. The dividend is a token ~$32M/yr (0.1% yield), so there is no income support to the thesis.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth has been healthy, but it is manufactured from acquisitions, FX, and buybacks rather than end-market demand. ITS organic revenue was −1.8% in 2025 and −1.6% in Q1'26, with segment EBITDA margin down 130–210bps — the profit engine is contracting organically. PST is the bright spot at +4.4% organic in Q1'26 with secular exposure to life sciences, water/wastewater, and RNG compression, and is where IR is concentrating M&A (TMIC/Adicomp $192M, Scinomix $46.7M, Transvac, Lead Fluid).

The estimate-revision signal is frequently mischaracterized. The consensus data shows estimates that are essentially **stable, not degrading**: current-year EPS is $3.50 today versus $3.50 thirty days ago and $3.53 ninety days ago (−0.9%); next-year EPS is $3.84 versus $3.85/$3.90 (−1.4%). The largest move is −4.5% on the current quarter ($0.82 vs $0.86 ninety days ago), which reflects one soft quarter rather than a falling trajectory across the curve. The earnings track record is mixed — over the last four quarters there were two beats and two misses (+1.0% Q1'26 and +3.2% Q4'25, against −2.9% Q3'25 and −2.5% Q2'25). The growth concern is cyclical-organic stagnation, not a collapsing earnings outlook.

FY2026 guidance was reaffirmed at ~2.5%–4.5% total revenue growth, which again implies continued reliance on M&A and FX over organic volume. The durability of growth therefore depends on (a) the industrial-capex cycle turning, which would re-accelerate ITS aftermarket and equipment demand, and (b) IR continuing to find and integrate accretive bolt-ons without further ILC Dover–type misfires.

## Valuation Assessment

At $77.91 the stock trades at roughly 22x the $3.50 FY2026 consensus EPS (the 52x trailing/forward P/E in the screening data reflects the impairment-depressed GAAP base and is not the operative multiple). Against the peer set — DOV 27.3x, HUBB 27.7x, XYL 27.1x, OTIS 18.6x, SNA 19.9x, median 27.1x — IR sits at a meaningful discount to the median but above the cohort-low names (OTIS, SNA).

The fair-value bridge matters here, and I'll be explicit about why I do not place IR at the industrials floor. A franchise with 36.5% recurring aftermarket, an improving PST mix, 1.7x net leverage, and ~4% FCF yield merits a moderate discount to the 27x peer median, not the 18–19x cohort low; the cohort-low names lack IR's aftermarket recurrence and life-science growth optionality. A reasonable range is ~23–25x on $3.50, or roughly $80–$88, on which $77.91 is modestly cheap. The offset is that the discount is *earned*: negative organic volume, margin compression, goodwill-concentration risk, and the ILC Dover execution question all justify trading below higher-quality compounders that are growing organically. The valuation does not screen as a value trap, but it also does not offer the asymmetric, catalyst-backed upside that would warrant a high valuation score; there is no identified mispricing the market is missing, only a fair price for a business in a soft patch.

The analyst price-target structure ($80 low, $92 median, $115 high, $93.2 mean) places the current price below even the low target, with sentiment characterized as degrading. On a stock that was turning I would treat consensus targets as floors, but IR is not — organic is negative and revisions are flat — so I weight the targets as a wide, two-sided band rather than a directional signal.

## Competitive Landscape

IR competes in fragmented industrial flow/compression markets against divisions of larger players, most notably Atlas Copco. Its differentiation is brand breadth, global service/distribution density, and high aftermarket attach, which together support pricing and retention. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on mix: PST is taking IR up the value chain into higher-margin life-science and clean-energy end markets, which is where the secular growth lives. The qualifier is that this is also where execution risk has shown up — the ILC Dover impairments occurred in exactly the higher-multiple Biopharma and Aerospace & Defense units IR was trying to build. The moat is real and the mix shift is favorable, but the company has not yet proven it can execute M&A in the higher-growth verticals as cleanly as in its core compression business.

## Risk Assessment

The material company-specific risks:

1. **Acquisition-dependent growth.** The model masks flat-to-negative organic volume. If the M&A pipeline slows or multiples on targets rise, headline growth stalls.

2. **ILC Dover.** Both reporting units carved from the 2024 acquisition were substantially impaired within a year, and the representations-and-warranties insurance claim signals a diligence or undisclosed-liability dispute. The reconstituted "Life Sciences" unit now carries only a 30% fair-value cushion versus ≥85% elsewhere, flagging further impairment risk if forecasts slip.

3. **Goodwill concentration.** At 70% of assets, the balance sheet is sensitive to forecast deterioration or higher discount rates, both of which trigger non-cash charges.

4. **Organic ITS stagnation** tied to PMI/capacity utilization. This is cyclical, and it cuts both ways — a capex recovery is the upgrade path.

5. **FX and tariffs.** 54–57% of revenue is non-USD; a stronger dollar pressures translation. One-for-one tariff pass-through is margin-percentage dilutive and carries demand risk.

6. **A separate $127M equity-method investment loss** in 2025 (plus $24M in 2024) is a non-core capital-deployment misstep that dented GAAP earnings.

These risks are real but bounded and largely cyclical/deal-specific rather than terminal. The investment-grade balance sheet, $1.2B FCF, and false-alarm Altman Z confirm there is no solvency dimension to the risk profile.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The industrial-capex cycle turns within 2–4 quarters, re-accelerating ITS aftermarket and equipment demand and reversing the organic decline. PST continues to compound at mid-single-digit organic with 30% margins, and IR's M&A flywheel adds 3–5 points of growth annually with cleaner integration than ILC Dover. The remaining buyback authorization supports per-share metrics into the recovery. On a re-rating toward 25–26x recovering EPS (~$3.84 next year), the stock reaches ~$96–$100, roughly the prior high and the upper analyst target band.

**Bear case.** The capex cycle stays soft, ITS organic remains negative, and margin compression continues. The Life Sciences unit's thin cushion gives way to another impairment, and the R&W claim resolves unfavorably. M&A multiples rise or integration falters, removing the growth crutch. The stock de-rates toward the industrials floor at ~18–19x on stagnant EPS, or roughly $63–$67, ~14–18% below current.

The midpoint of these scenarios sits close to the current price, which is why the call is HOLD. The cyclical timing is genuinely two-sided, and the ILC Dover execution question is unresolved.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), with particular attention to ITS organic and the Life Sciences impairment cushion. The structural variable — the industrial-capex/PMI cycle — is a 2–4 quarter watch.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- ITS organic revenue turns positive (>0% YoY) for two consecutive quarters, confirming a cyclical turn rather than a one-quarter bounce.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin stabilizes or expands YoY, reversing the operating deleverage.
- Open-market insider buying emerges, or the R&W insurance claim resolves with a meaningful recovery, removing the M&A-execution overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second impairment in the Life Sciences/PST units, indicating the M&A flywheel is destroying capital rather than compounding it.
- Current-year EPS estimates begin falling materially (>5% over 90 days), shifting the revision trend from stable to deteriorating.
- Net leverage rises above ~2.5x to fund acquisitions while organic remains negative, indicating growth is being bought rather than earned.

## Conclusion

Ingersoll Rand is a well-run, investment-grade industrial franchise with a durable aftermarket-driven moat and strong free cash flow, currently working through a soft industrial-capex cycle that has left organic growth negative-to-flat and earnings dependent on M&A and buybacks. The market has already de-rated the stock to a level below the lowest analyst target, so the cyclical bear case is substantially priced in, which is why this is not a name to actively avoid or short. But with no organic momentum, no income support, an unresolved ILC Dover execution question, and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet, there is no catalyst to justify a BUY against a sector that has historically delivered negative selection alpha. The risk/reward is balanced around the current price. **HOLD**, with the next reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings and the industrial-capex cycle as the variable that will resolve the call in either direction.
