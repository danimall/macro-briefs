## Executive Summary

Agree Realty Corporation is a high-quality net lease REIT trading at $75.54, owning 2,756 single-tenant retail properties across all 50 states with 99.7% occupancy and 65.4% investment-grade tenancy. The company has delivered 16-19% annual revenue growth through aggressive acquisition activity, generating Core FFO growth of 8.7% YoY in Q1 2026 and reaffirming AFFO guidance of $4.54-$4.58/share. The dividend (4.2% yield, ~72% AFFO payout ratio) is well-covered and growing at 3-4% annually.

The investment case centers on whether the premium valuation (~16.5x forward AFFO vs. peer median ~14x) is justified by superior growth. Recent evidence is mixed: insider buying is exceptionally strong (CEO purchased $1M, a director added $1.5M in May 2026), but EPS estimate revisions are falling 4-5% over 90 days, the company missed estimates in 3 of 4 recent quarters, and the options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. The stock does not offer sufficient margin of safety for a BUY recommendation given the negative revision trajectory, premium multiple, and macro headwind from sustained high long-term rates.

The specific condition preventing a BUY despite reasonable fundamental quality: ADC trades at a 15-18% premium to net lease peer multiples while delivering negative estimate revisions (-4.8% on current quarter over 90 days, -5.0% on next year). This combination of premium multiple plus deteriorating earnings expectations creates asymmetric downside risk where any further growth deceleration or rate increase would compress the multiple toward peer levels (14x AFFO = $64-65 price, representing 14% downside).

## Company Overview

Agree Realty is a fully integrated net lease REIT founded in 1971, publicly traded since 1994. The company acquires, develops, and manages single-tenant retail properties under triple-net lease structures where tenants bear all operating costs. With 90 full-time employees managing 57.5 million square feet, the operating model is exceptionally capital-efficient.

The portfolio emphasizes e-commerce-resistant retail categories: grocery (10.3% of annualized base rent), home improvement (9.0%), convenience stores (7.7%), tire/auto service, auto parts, and dollar stores. No single tenant exceeds 10% of ABR, and only one lease out of 2,756 has collection concerns.

Growth is generated through three channels: (1) acquisitions ($1.44B deployed in 2025 at 7.2% cap rates), (2) ground-up development, and (3) a Developer Funding Platform providing capital to third-party developers. The company funds growth primarily through ATM equity issuance and investment-grade unsecured debt.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** Annual revenue grew from $430M (2022) to $718M (2025), a 67% increase over three years (approximately 18.6% CAGR). Q1 2026 rental income of $200.7M annualizes to $803M, suggesting continued 12-15% growth.

**FFO/AFFO Performance:** Core FFO/share grew 8.7% YoY in Q1 2026 to $1.13. AFFO/share grew 7.5% to $1.14. Full-year 2026 AFFO guidance of $4.54-$4.58/share implies 5-6% growth over FY2025's $4.33. The per-share growth rate significantly trails portfolio-level growth due to continuous dilution (shares outstanding grew 52% in 3.5 years, from 79M to 120M).

**Capital Structure:** Total debt of $3.72B against $6.24B equity (0.60x D/E), with a weighted average fixed interest rate of 4.04% and 5.9 years weighted average maturity. 87% of debt is fixed-rate with extensive swap hedging. Total liquidity of $2.28B provides ample runway for continued acquisitions.

**Spread Economics:** Q1 2026 acquisitions at 7.1% cap rates funded with debt at ~4.0-4.4% generate a 270-310 bps positive spread. This spread has narrowed from historical levels but remains accretive.

**Dividend Coverage:** The $3.144/share annual dividend represents a 72% AFFO payout ratio, providing comfortable coverage with room for continued dividend growth. The company has delivered 60 consecutive monthly dividends.

**Cash Flow Quality:** Operating cash flow of $504M in FY2025 (+17% YoY) exceeds net income, confirming high-quality earnings. Capex requirements are minimal (tenant responsibilities under net leases), with only $12M/year in capitalized building improvements.

## Growth Analysis

ADC's growth engine is acquisition-driven. The company deployed $1.57B in 2025 and $404M in Q1 2026 alone. With $2.28B in liquidity (including $1.37B in unsettled forward equity), the deployment pipeline is well-funded for 12-18 months.

However, several growth headwinds are emerging:

1. **Dilution offset:** Per-share AFFO growth of 5-6% in 2026 is roughly one-third of portfolio-level revenue growth, reflecting the dilutive cost of equity funding. As the base grows larger, maintaining per-share growth rates requires proportionally larger acquisition volumes.

2. **Negative estimate revisions:** Consensus EPS estimates are declining — current quarter down 4.8% over 90 days, next year down 5.0%. Only 3 analysts cover the stock for quarterly estimates, so individual revisions carry outsized weight, but the direction is clearly negative.

3. **Miss pattern:** The company missed EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters (Q2 2025 by -5.9%, Q3 2025 by -1.8%, Q4 2025 by -1.4%). Only Q1 2026 beat, by a modest 3.1%. This pattern suggests estimates may still be too high.

4. **Cap rate compression limits:** With acquisitions at 7.1% cap rates and debt costs at 4.0-5.6%, the spread is narrowing versus the 2020-2022 era when cap rates were similar but debt cost 2-3%.

The 7.8-year weighted average remaining lease term provides contractual rent escalation visibility, and same-store growth from built-in bumps contributes 1-2% annually independent of acquisitions.

This premium has historically been justified by ADC's superior growth rate (8% AFFO/share growth vs. NNN's ~3-4%). However, with estimate revisions turning negative and growth decelerating toward 5-6%, the premium is narrowing.

The analyst consensus target of $85 (median $84, range $80-$92) implies 12-14% upside, but these targets were set before the recent negative revision trend accelerated.

At elevated long-term Treasury rates, the REIT risk premium is compressed. ADC's 4.2% dividend yield offers a thin spread above risk-free long bonds — historically tight for a leveraged real estate vehicle. A reversion to a more normal 200 bps risk premium would imply meaningful downside, while an improved rate environment would support the current multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

ADC's competitive position within net lease REITs is strong and improving:

**Strengths:** (1) Industry-leading portfolio growth rate. (2) Differentiated development and DFP platforms provide proprietary deal flow. (3) Investment-grade balance sheet with tight credit spreads (SOFR + 72.5 bps). (4) 99.7% occupancy — among the highest in the sector. (5) Only one lease with collection concerns out of 2,756.

**Positioning vs. Realty Income (O):** ADC is roughly 1/5th the size of O, which allows it to grow faster on a percentage basis by acquiring properties that are too small for O's scale. This niche advantage is durable so long as the company maintains discipline.

**Sector resilience:** The e-commerce-resistant tenant focus (grocery, auto service, home improvement) is well-positioned for an environment where physical necessity-based retail outperforms discretionary. Grocery and convenience store tenants are among the most recession-resistant retail categories.

**Market share trajectory:** Growing from 2,370 to 2,756 properties in 15 months demonstrates continued ability to source and close deals at accretive economics.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate Sensitivity (Primary Risk):** In a sustained high long-term rate environment, net lease REIT valuations face compression. Each 50 bps increase in long-term rates compresses net lease multiples by approximately 0.5-1.0x AFFO. [unverified — specific rate levels and probabilities not supported by provided sources]

**Dilution Trajectory:** 8.7M shares sold via ATM in Q1 2026 alone (annualizing to 35M+ shares, or ~30% of current float). While acquisitions are accretive, the dilution pace is aggressive and limits per-share value creation. If deployment slows or cap rates compress further, the dilution becomes punitive.

**Tenant Credit (Tariff Exposure):** The Q1 2026 10-Q explicitly added tariff risk to its forward-looking disclosures. Home improvement (9.0% of ABR) and auto parts tenants have meaningful import exposure. In a tariff-driven cost environment, tenant margins could compress, increasing credit risk.

**CRE Broader Contagion:** While ADC's properties are single-tenant retail (distinct from distressed office/mall CRE), a broader CRE credit tightening could reduce liquidity for all real estate transactions and potentially compress acquisition cap rates or force the company to pay higher debt costs.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. ATM IV jumped from 14.7% to 21.6% (+6.9pp) over the past month while the stock was essentially flat (-0.4%). The term structure is in backwardation (21.6% near-term vs. 16.7% at 6 months), typically indicating the market expects resolution of an event in the near term, but the direction is uncertain.

The put/call open interest ratio surged from 0.44 to 4.94 — an 11x increase in relative put positioning. Put skew is elevated at 4.0%. This institutional positioning contradicts the bullish insider buying signal and suggests options market participants see downside risk from the rate environment or upcoming catalysts that the company's insiders may be discounting.

## Conclusion

Agree Realty is a well-managed, high-quality net lease REIT with exceptional occupancy, disciplined tenant selection, and a management team aligned through meaningful insider ownership. The 4.2% dividend yield is well-covered and growing. However, the stock's premium valuation (~16.5x forward AFFO vs. 14x peers) is difficult to sustain when: (1) estimate revisions are falling 4-5% over 90 days, (2) the company missed estimates in 3 of 4 recent quarters, (3) the rate environment creates headwinds for long-duration income assets, and (4) options markets are pricing elevated near-term risk despite minimal short interest.

The insider buying cluster (CEO + Director + CFO totaling ~$2.6M in recent months) is the strongest counterargument and prevents a more negative view. Management clearly believes the stock is undervalued. But insider confidence alone cannot override a negative revision trajectory combined with a premium multiple in a rising-rate environment. The appropriate stance is to hold for the income stream and reassess when either valuations compress to peer levels (creating a BUY opportunity at $68-70) or estimate revisions stabilize (suggesting the growth premium is deserved).
