**Research Date: August 7, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Interface reported a Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended July 5, 2026) that looks, on the headline, like a step-change: net sales $395.7M (+5.4%), GAAP gross margin 45.0% versus 39.4%, GAAP and adjusted diluted EPS both $0.88 versus $0.55/$0.60, adjusted EBITDA $87.7M (+35.2%), and full-year adjusted gross-margin guidance lifted from ~38.9% to 40.6%. The company disaggregated the margin itself: 131 basis points of operational improvement and **393 basis points from $15.6M of IEEPA tariff refunds**, recognized after the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating those duties. Stripping the refund at the quarter's 27.4% effective tax rate ($19.418M ÷ $70.825M) leaves ~$11.3M after tax, or ~$0.19 per diluted share on 58.3M shares. Ex-refund adjusted EPS was ~$0.69, +15% year over year rather than +46.7%, and ex-refund adjusted gross margin was ~41.1% versus 39.8%. Applying $15.6M against the $1.470B revenue midpoint accounts for 106bps of the ~170bps guidance raise, leaving underlying FY2026 gross-margin improvement at roughly 50bps versus FY2025's ~39.0% adjusted.

What remains after the adjustment is still a decent business improving at the edges. Q2 currency-neutral sales grew 3.8% on a clean 13-versus-13-week comparison, and currency-neutral orders grew 5.4% (AMS +4.8%, EAAA +6.4%), running ahead of shipments. The most durable finding is European: EAAA adjusted operating margin moved from 5.2% to 9.5% in the quarter and 5.0% to 8.0% for the half, on a segment that is ~39% of sales and cannot be flattered by U.S. customs refunds. The December 3, 2025 refinancing ($300M of 5.50% notes retired, replaced by a $170M term loan plus revolver maturing December 2030) cuts interest expense from $19.5M to roughly $10M and leaves net leverage at 0.5x. Those two items, plus the prospective absence of the ~$7.3M annual IEEPA drag disclosed in FY2025, are recurring.

The rating steps down from the prior BUY on price and earnings composition, not on business deterioration. **A price-basis conflict must be stated up front:** the authoritative frozen basis for this report is the $35.10 official close (the snapshot carries an `official_close_market_date_mismatch` error; the daily history's last bar is August 6 at $35.10), while the last extended-hours trade was $38.29 and the profile feed shows $38.58, approximately 9.1% above the basis, reflecting the August 7 earnings reaction. All arithmetic below runs on $35.10, with the executable-price implication flagged wherever it changes the conclusion. At $35.10 the shares are 14.8x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of ~$2.37, 16.1x the ~$2.18 ex-refund figure, 14.9x FY2027 consensus of $2.35, and 8.7x LTM adjusted EBITDA of $250.5M, with a probability-weighted return of roughly +14% against a $41 base case. At $38.3 the same base case is +7% and the ex-refund multiple is 17.6x for a ~4% organic grower. That is fair value.

## Company Overview

Interface designs and manufactures commercial floorcovering sold into specification-driven non-residential markets through architects, interior designers and dealers. The portfolio is four brands: Interface carpet tile (the core), Interface LVT, nora rubber flooring (acquired 2018, manufactured in Weinheim, Germany), and FLOR premium area rugs. Two disclosures matter structurally. LVT is **not manufactured** by Interface; the FY2025 10-K discloses reliance on a primary third-party supplier in South Korea, and nylon yarn, the largest carpet-tile input, is sourced from two major global suppliers. Interface therefore competes against vertically integrated players (Mohawk, Shaw/Berkshire, Tarkett, Milliken) while buying two of its most important inputs.

Reporting is two segments: AMS (Americas, ~61% of FY2025 sales, 16.3% FY2025 adjusted operating margin) and EAAA (Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia, ~39%, 6.7%). End-market mix is roughly balanced between corporate office and everything else, but the direction deserves care: the Q1 FY2026 10-Q put corporate office at **47% of net sales, up from 45%**, and in Q2 healthcare billings grew 19% globally with education and office each up 5%. Healthcare (largely nora-led, though the company did not attribute it) is the fastest-growing vertical; office is not shrinking as a share of revenue. The claim that Interface has structurally de-risked from the office cycle is not supported by its own mix data.

The differentiation is design and sustainability credentials, more than 30 years of published progress and a carbon-negative-by-2040 goal without offsets, which is commercially real in a market where embodied-carbon disclosure influences specification, and which shows up in a 41.1% ex-refund gross margin that a commodity flooring producer does not earn. It is also replicable by better-capitalized competitors, and the 10-K's own key-person risk factor names the company's **principal design consultant** alongside senior management, an unusual admission that a single outside design relationship is material to the moat.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 net sales were $1,386.9M, +5.4%, with a disclosed +$14.9M FX benefit (~1.1pp), implying ~4.3% organic. H1 FY2026 sales of $726.7M grew 8.0% reported but 5.1% currency-neutral, a $19.1M (2.9pp) euro tailwind, and Q1 carried a 14th week in a 53-week fiscal year. Q2, a clean 13-versus-13 comparison, grew **+3.8% currency-neutral**. Underlying demand is therefore running ~3.5-4%, essentially unchanged for two years.

**Margins and the refund.** The Q2 print illustrates why headline and underlying diverge here:

| | Q2 FY2026 | Q2 FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $395.7M | $375.5M |
| Adjusted gross margin | 45.0% | 39.8% |
| Adjusted gross margin ex-$15.6M refund (derived) | ~41.1% | 39.8% |
| Adjusted SG&A | $103.1M (26.1%) | $93.4M (24.9%) |
| Adjusted operating income | $74.9M | $55.9M |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $0.88 | $0.60 |
| Adjusted EPS ex-refund (derived) | ~$0.69 | $0.60 |

SG&A deleveraged 119bps to 26.1% of sales, which the release attributes to higher commissions and variable compensation on increased sales and profit. That creates a measurement caution on the segment data. AMS adjusted operating margin printed 24.6% ($61.0M on $247.7M) against a 16.3% FY2025 full-year base and 20.4% a year earlier. Because IEEPA duties were paid on German rubber and Korean LVT imported into the United States, the refund most plausibly landed predominantly in AMS; if all $15.6M sits there, AMS ex-refund operating income was ~$45.4M (18.3%), below last year's 20.4% on +3.4% sales. The company did not disclose the segment allocation, and the variable-comp accrual on refund-inflated profit means a mechanical deduction overstates any decline. **This is an unresolved question, not established AMS deterioration,** and it is the single most useful thing to look for in the Q3 report.

EAAA is not subject to that ambiguity. A segment going 5.2% → 9.5% (quarter) and 5.0% → 8.0% (half) with currency-neutral orders +6.4% is the highest-quality item in the dataset. Roughly 300bps on ~$540M of annualized EAAA revenue is ~$16M of operating income versus the FY2025 run-rate; convergence even halfway toward AMS's underlying high-teens margin would be worth multiples of that. Part of the reported improvement is FX-flattered on the revenue line (+8.8% reported versus +4.5% currency-neutral), which mechanically aids euro-denominated operating leverage, and two quarters do not establish a trend.

**A second non-recurring flatter sits in FY2025 earnings.** FY2025 pretax income was $136.9M against $116.1M net income, a 15.2% effective rate versus 23.4% in FY2024. H1 FY2025 taxed at 25.7% ($15.7M on $61.3M), implying an H2 FY2025 rate near 6.7% and roughly $14.6M (~$0.25/share) of discrete benefit. FY2026 guidance assumes a 26.0% adjusted rate. Anyone extrapolating the reported $1.96 FY2025 EPS is starting from an inflated base; at 26% it would have been ~$1.72.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $81.5M, total debt $204.4M, net debt $122.8M, equity $678.4M, disclosed net leverage 0.5x, nearest maturity December 2030. Total debt rose $22.8M in H1 to fund buybacks and working capital, a balance change rather than credit deterioration. Interest expense of $5.0M in H1 (versus $8.9M) tracks the ~$10M full-year guide. This is the strongest dimension of the story and removes refinancing risk from the thesis entirely.

**Cash flow is the weak link.** H1 net income rose $29.5M (+65%) while operating cash flow rose $10.0M to $51.9M and free cash flow rose $2.4M to $29.4M. Receivables consumed $36.5M (versus $14.7M) and inventories $19.3M (versus $12.1M). Conversion of net income fell to 69% from 92%. Receivables of $210.0M on Q2 sales of $395.7M imply ~48 days versus ~45 at year-end; inventory of $291.6M on Q2 COGS of $217.6M implies ~122 days. Capex rose to $22.5M with a $60M full-year guide (~4.1% of sales, above ~$41M D&A) for automation and manufacturing efficiency. The benign reading (building ahead of a 5.4% order book, with H2 the seasonally stronger conversion half and Q2 alone producing $38.4M of the $51.9M) is plausible; genuine collection slippage cannot be excluded from a single half. I have not annualized H1 free cash flow: the half spans 27 weeks in a 53-week year and excludes H2 cash-tax timing.

Note also that the profile's trailing/forward P/E field of 16.40 against $1.96 EPS implies a $32.14 price and reconciles to neither the frozen basis nor the market price; I have computed all multiples directly.

## Growth Analysis

Interface is a ~4% organic top-line grower whose earnings growth comes from four identifiable sources, three of which are finite:

1. **Interest expense**: $19.5M → ~$10M, roughly $9.5M pretax or ~$0.12/share after tax. Banked, recurring, and already in FY2026.
2. **Tariff relief**: the $15.6M catch-up is non-repeatable; the *prospective* absence of the $7.3M FY2025 IEEPA drag is recurring **if** it holds. Section 232 and Section 301 authorities remain available to reimpose duties on the same German rubber and Korean LVT.
3. **EAAA margin convergence**: the genuine multi-year opportunity, worth $16M+ of operating income at 300bps and more if the segment moves toward the low teens.
4. **Automation/mix**: ~50bps of underlying full-year gross-margin improvement, funded by capex up ~30%.

Forward evidence is mixed but net positive. Currency-neutral orders +5.4% exceed currency-neutral sales +3.8%. Backlog was $256.6M at April 27, 2026 versus $222.8M at February 2, 2026, but the like-for-like annual comparison in the 10-K is flat ($222.8M versus $223.4M a year earlier); FY2025 growth drew on the order book rather than adding to it. The FY guidance midpoint of $1.470B less H1 actual of $726.7M implies H2 of $743.3M, +4.1% against H2 FY2025's $713.9M, with the Q3 guide of $370-380M implying ~+2.9%. That deceleration is substantially the lapping of the FX tailwind and the 53rd week rather than demand deterioration.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current year $2.14 (+1.6% over 90 days), next year $2.35 (+3.8%), current quarter $0.62 (**-3.5% over 90 days; flat over 30 days**, the only negative line, consistent with a Q3 revenue guide characterized in secondary coverage as slightly below consensus). Four consecutive beats averaging ~25% support confidence in consensus, though the latest beat shrinks to roughly +6% ($0.69 versus the $0.65 estimate) once the refund is removed. Consensus current-year EPS of $2.14 sits ~10% below the $2.37 implied by guidance math, so a mechanical upward revision is likely within weeks. That revision is an accounting catch-up to a one-time item, and should not be treated as the same quality of signal as organic estimate momentum.

## Competitive Landscape

Interface's own 10-K describes a "highly competitive" market with "a large number of manufacturers," some with "greater financial resources," and concedes it "may face challenges competing on price." The evidence says it is not trying to: a 41.1% ex-refund gross margin is a premium-specification outcome. The structural vulnerability is input control: Mohawk and Shaw are vertically integrated into fiber, while Interface buys nylon from two suppliers and buys 100% of its LVT from one Korean supplier. In a price war or an input squeeze, Interface has thinner insulation, and the Q1 FY2026 10-Q explicitly flagged higher raw-material costs, higher energy costs and higher costs to procure LVT amid "increased global macro uncertainty." That flag sits awkwardly against the Q2 gross margin; the resolution is that the refund masked whatever input pressure was accumulating.

The nora rubber franchise in healthcare and transportation is the most defensible asset, a narrower and more technical category than carpet tile, and healthcare billings +19% is the best evidence of share capture in a vertical with secular rather than cyclical demand. In Europe, orders +6.4% currency-neutral alongside 430bps of margin expansion suggests Interface is winning specification at improving price against Tarkett and regional players, in the segment where it has historically been sub-scale. Trajectory is modestly improving; the level remains that of a ~$1.4B revenue niche competitor against far larger integrated rivals.

## Risk Assessment

**Earnings composition.** The largest risk is that investors capitalize FY2026 optics. FY2026 guided adjusted EPS includes ~$0.19 of refund; FY2025 reported EPS included ~$0.25 of discrete tax benefit and a 15.2% rate against a 26.0% FY2026 assumption. Sequentially, Q3 guidance implies ~40.8% adjusted gross margin and Q4 arithmetically ~37.8-38.0%, which resolves benignly (H1 ex-refund was 39.75%; Q4 volume of ~$368M versus $396M reduces fixed-cost absorption) but will read as deterioration to anyone comparing against reported Q2.

**Tariff reversal.** The exposure is two-sided and asymmetric: the $15.6M is banked and unrepeatable, while re-imposition of duties on German rubber and Korean LVT under Section 232 or 301 would remove the recurring ~$7M+ benefit as well.

**Supplier single points of failure.** One primary LVT supplier in South Korea and two nylon suppliers, both with dedicated risk factors. An interruption at either would be difficult to replace quickly, and an entire product category's cost and availability sit outside Interface's control.

**Office exposure.** Corporate office at 47% of Q1 FY2026 sales, up from 45%. Non-residential renovation cycles follow corporate capex and lease decisions with a lag; the specification-to-installation pipeline means a demand break would appear in orders one to two quarters before revenue. [unverified — no macro data (mortgage rates, payrolls, Fed policy) in provided sources]

**FX reversal.** The euro contributed +$14.9M to FY2025 sales and +$19.1M to H1 FY2026. Because H1 FY2025 comparisons preceded the sharpest euro strengthening, the tailwind compresses in H2 and reported growth converges toward the ~4% organic rate, including in EAAA's flattered operating leverage.

**Goodwill.** $158.5M of goodwill and intangibles (23% of equity), predominantly from the 2018 nora acquisition, exposed to a European downturn. The nora purchase-accounting amortization has fully run off ($0 in H1 FY2026 versus $2.6M), a modest GAAP tailwind that also removes a prior adjusting item and makes GAAP and adjusted results converge.

**Unresolved items.** The segment allocation of the refund is undisclosed; whether the refund is cash or a receivable at July 5, 2026 is undisclosed; and a June 4, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K could not be retrieved (annual-meeting timing makes a routine board matter most likely, and the CFO signed the August 7 release, so there is no CFO transition; this is unverified).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($50, +42% from $35.10; +31% from $38.29).** EAAA holds 9-10% adjusted operating margin and grinds toward the low teens, adding $30-40M of annualized operating income versus the FY2025 run-rate. Currency-neutral orders of +5.4% translate into 5%+ organic revenue in FY2027 as healthcare (+19%) and education compound while office holds flat. IEEPA relief proves permanent, removing a $7M+ annual drag; automation capex delivers another 50-75bps of gross margin. FY2027 EPS reaches ~$2.60 and the market pays 19-20x for a 0.5x-levered specialty manufacturer with expanding margins, matching the $50 street high.

**Bear case ($28, -20% from $35.10; -27% from $38.29).** Office demand rolls with the cycle; the flat year-over-year backlog at February 2026 proves the leading indicator and orders decelerate through H2. FX flips from a 2.9pp tailwind to a headwind, taking reported growth negative. Tariffs return under Section 232/301, reversing the recurring benefit; the Q1-flagged raw-material, energy and LVT cost inflation compresses gross margin back toward 39%. H1's 69% cash conversion proves to be collection slippage rather than order-book support, and $60M of capex on ~$1.47B of sales leaves free cash flow near $60-70M for the year. FY2027 EPS lands near $2.20 and the multiple compresses to ~12.7x, roughly $28, still above the 52-week low of $24.40.

**Base case ($41, +16.8% from $35.10; +7.1% from $38.29).** Organic growth holds 3.5-4.5%, EAAA sustains 8-9% margin, interest expense stays near $10M, IEEPA relief persists but is not repeated, and FY2027 EPS reaches consensus $2.35. At 17.5x, consistent with the normalized trailing multiple and with the 0.5x balance sheet, that is $41. Probability-weighted at 25/50/25, expected return from the $35.10 basis is approximately **+14%**; from $38.29 it is approximately **+4.5%**.

**On the score trajectory.** Prior reports ran 6.8 (BUY, May) → 6.0 (HOLD, June) → 6.6 (BUY, July 26, at $32.98). The step down to 5.94 is driven by valuation and revealed earnings composition, not by fundamental deterioration: the shares are ~16% above the July basis, the Q2 beat was ~two-thirds a customs refund, ~62% of the guidance raise is the same item, and H1 cash conversion fell to 69% from 92%. Two of the six dimensions moved: valuation down on price and normalized-multiple arithmetic (17.4x normalized trailing, 17.6x ex-refund at the market price), and management quality up marginally from the prior 5.8 as the December refinancing now shows through in a $3.9M H1 interest reduction and buybacks executed at $24.23 and $26.04 averages, well below the current price. Financial health and the European operating improvement genuinely strengthened. A third consecutive BUY would require either a lower price or evidence that AMS is expanding margin ex-refund; neither is available today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 results (early November 2026) is the decisive checkpoint. It is the first clean post-refund quarter, the first without the FX tailwind of comparable magnitude, and the report where the segment tables will reveal whether AMS margin expanded on an underlying basis.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price:** a decline into **$33-34** (14.0-14.5x FY2027 consensus $2.35; ~8.2x LTM EBITDA) restores a double-digit margin of safety to the $41 base case and would move the rating to ACCUMULATE without any change in fundamentals.
2. **EAAA durability plus AMS clarity:** EAAA adjusted operating margin at or above 9.0% in Q3 *and* AMS adjusted operating margin at or above 18.5% ex any residual tariff item, confirming the European gain is structural and that Americas margin did not go backwards in Q2.
3. **Cash conversion:** H2 operating cash flow converting above 90% of net income, taking full-year free cash flow above ~$110M (≈5.4% FCF yield at $35.10) and confirming the H1 working-capital build was order-book support.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Currency-neutral **orders growth falling below 2%** in any quarter, or backlog declining year over year, the leading indicator for the specification pipeline.
2. Re-imposition of tariffs on German rubber or Korean LVT under Section 232/301, or FY2027 adjusted gross-margin guidance below 39.0%, either of which removes the recurring relief.
3. Corporate office billings declining year over year while office remains ~47% of sales, or a Q3/Q4 guidance cut on the ~$1.470B midpoint.
4. Disclosure that Q2's AMS margin ex-refund was below 18% on +3.4% sales, indicating Americas operating deterioration behind the headline.

**Existing holders:** maintain. The 12-month path to $41 is intact, the balance sheet carries no refinancing risk before December 2030, and the European margin catalyst is unfinished. **Prospective buyers:** at the ~$38.3 executable price there is no entry signal; wait for $33-35 or for Q3 to resolve the AMS question. Do not chase the post-print gap into a name with 10.8% short interest where covering may account for part of the move.

## Conclusion

Interface is a well-run niche manufacturer that has done three genuinely valuable things in the last nine months: refinanced $300M of 5.50% notes into a facility maturing December 2030 and halved interest expense; taken a chronically sub-scale European segment from 5.0% to 8.0% adjusted operating margin on rising currency-neutral orders; and captured a $15.6M customs refund that management disclosed with unusual precision rather than burying in "other." The first two are recurring, the third is not, and the market has to separate them because the company's own guidance raise does not: roughly 62% of the 170bps gross-margin increase is the single refund, leaving ~50bps of underlying improvement on ~4% organic growth.

On the authoritative $35.10 basis the shares are 14.9x FY2027 consensus, 16.1x FY2026 adjusted EPS excluding the refund, and 8.7x LTM adjusted EBITDA, offering roughly +14% probability-weighted return against a $41 base, a $50 bull and a $28 bear. That is a reasonable holding but a thin initiation. At the post-print market price near $38.3 (9.1% above the frozen basis, 17.6x ex-refund earnings, above the $38.00 median analyst target) the base case offers ~7% and the bear case ~27% downside. **HOLD**, with a staged path back to ACCUMULATE at $33-34 or on a Q3 report that shows AMS margin expanding without a customs assist and EAAA holding 9%.
