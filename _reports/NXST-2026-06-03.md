**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nexstar is the largest local-television broadcaster in the U.S. and, as of March 19, 2026, the owner of TEGNA's 64 full-power stations after a $6.2B, debt-financed acquisition. On April 17, 2026, a federal judge in the Eastern District of California issued a preliminary injunction prohibiting integration of the two companies, ruling the merger "is presumed likely to violate antitrust laws based on the combined firm market share alone." Because the deal had already closed, Nexstar now carries TEGNA's assets and roughly $6B of incremental, deal-related debt while being legally barred from capturing a dollar of synergy. A court-ordered divestiture or unwind is qualitatively worse than a deal that simply never closed.

The headline financials look superficially strong: Q1 2026 revenue rose 13.1% to $1,396M and diluted EPS jumped to $5.09 from $3.37, while consensus current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 34% over 90 days. These numbers are misleading as a thesis input. The Q1 revenue lift is driven by the even-year political-ad cycle plus 12 days of consolidated TEGNA results, and the EPS estimate surge mechanically reflects pulling TEGNA's earnings into the consolidated entity — earnings the company cannot fully integrate and may be forced to disgorge. Underlying FY2025 revenue fell 8.5% to $4,949M in the non-election trough, advertising fell 18.9%, and net income attributable collapsed to $109M (depressed further by a $381M non-cash TV Food Network impairment).

We rate NXST AVOID. Total debt ballooned to ~$12.2B (≈85% of total capitalization) at quarter-end, ~$3.6B of which is floating with no interest-rate hedges. The equity is now a leveraged option on an antitrust and FCC outcome that is actively adverse, sitting on top of a structurally declining linear-TV business. The stock has fallen ~29% from its February high of $254 to $181. We do not view that drawdown as a mispricing to exploit; it reflects sophisticated investors repricing a binary legal risk that has not resolved and is currently trending against the company.

## Company Overview

Nexstar owns, operates, or services roughly 200 full-power stations across ~116 markets (pre-TEGNA), reaching approximately 39% of U.S. TV households at the federal ownership cap (achieved using the UHF discount). The business rests on two revenue pillars:

- **Distribution revenue** — retransmission consent fees paid by cable, satellite, and virtual MVPD operators. This was $2,924M in 2025, the larger and more stable leg.
- **Advertising revenue** — local and national spot, heavily cyclical on the two-year political cycle. This was $1,959M in 2025.

Nexstar also owns The CW Network (majority, a turnaround project), a 31.3% stake in TV Food Network (Food Network/Cooking Channel; partner Warner Bros. Discovery owns 68.7%), NewsNation, and digital assets. It uses a VIE structure, consolidating "sidecar" owners (Mission, White Knight, Vaughan) via local service agreements and guaranteeing Mission's ~$362M credit facility. That structure is itself a regulatory pressure point.

The TEGNA acquisition added 64 full-power TV and 2 radio stations across 51 markets, the Premion connected-TV ad platform, and multicast networks. On paper the combined entity reaches ~80% of U.S. households, well above the 39% cap. That gap is the entire substance of the legal fight.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (non-election trough):**
- Revenue down 8.5% YoY, driven by political advertising falling ~$446M and total advertising down 18.9%. Distribution roughly flat.
- Net income attributable down from $722M in 2024.
- Free cash flow ~$743M (OCF $891M less capex $148M). FCF substantially exceeds GAAP net income because of high D&A and non-cash charges, and this FCF is what services the debt.
- ~$351M returned to shareholders via buybacks and dividends; dividend $1.86/quarter ($7.44/year).

**Q1 2026 (election year + 12 days of TEGNA):**
- Political advertising swung positive (+$35M). TEGNA contributed only ~$106M (closed March 19).
- Net income attributable $164M.

**Balance sheet — the dominant feature:**
- Total debt rose to ~$12.2B at 3/31/26 from $6.3B at YE2025. Total assets ~$18.1B; goodwill ~$5.0B; FCC licenses ~$5.1B.
- ~$3.6B of floating-rate debt with no interest-rate hedges. A 100bp SOFR move shifts annual pre-tax interest by ~$57M.
- The stack includes 6.50% secured notes due 2033 ($3,390M), term loans due 2030/2033, assumed TEGNA notes (7.75%/7.25% '27, 5.00% '29), and $1,725M of new 7.25% notes due 2034 issued in April 2026 to redeem the 5.625% '27 notes.
- First-lien net leverage covenant ≤4.25x (steps to 4.75x post-material transaction); in compliance at 3/31/26.

The Altman Z-Score of ~0.95 sits in the distress zone, but this is structurally low for any highly leveraged broadcaster and does not signal imminent insolvency given robust FCF. The genuine financial-health concern is event-driven: ~$6B of incremental debt was raised against TEGNA assets and synergies, and those synergies are now legally frozen. If the deal is unwound, the company keeps the debt while losing the cash flows underwriting it.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data: current-year EPS revised +34% over 90 days, next-year +32%, next-quarter +50%. Under normal circumstances this magnitude of revision velocity combined with double-digit revenue growth would be a strong forward signal. Here, the signal does not carry its usual meaning. The upward revisions almost entirely reflect analysts folding TEGNA's earnings into the consolidated Nexstar model after the March 19 close, plus the even-year political-ad tailwind. They do not reflect organic improvement in the underlying business, and they are vulnerable to the legal event that defines the situation: if TEGNA must be held separate indefinitely or unwound, those consolidated earnings are not Nexstar's to keep.

Organic growth prospects are modest. Local broadcast TV is a mature-to-declining industry. Cord-cutting erodes the subscriber base that underpins retransmission fees. Advertising oscillates on a pronounced two-year political sawtooth. The entire structural growth thesis for the sector is consolidation — bigger groups negotiate better retrans rates and network terms. The TEGNA deal is the boldest expression of that thesis, and it is precisely what regulators and a federal court are now blocking.

The "hidden assets" are also eroding with hard deadlines. The TV Food Network partnership faces a December 31, 2026 dissolution trigger (management intends to renew with WBD), having contributed ~$137M of distributions and ~$102M of equity income in 2025 — meaningful but shrinking. The FCC approval requires divestiture of 6 stations within 2 years with no buyer identified.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: three beats and one miss over four quarters, including a -17.6% miss in Q3 2025. The 74.6% beat in Q4 2025 and 13.5% in Q1 2026 are flattered by the political cycle and deal timing rather than recurring outperformance.

## Valuation Assessment

At $181, NXST trades at ~38x trailing EPS of $3.0 and offers a 4.2% dividend yield. Peer broadcasters trade far lower: TGNA at 16.3x, FOX/FOXA at 15–17x. Peer median P/E is 16.3x with a 1.0% yield.

On a forward consolidated basis the optics invert. If one takes the revised current-year consensus of ~$34 EPS at face value, the stock trades at ~5x forward earnings — apparently extraordinarily cheap. That ~$34 figure assumes the consolidated entity stays consolidated and integrates, an assumption the court has explicitly enjoined. The honest forward number lies somewhere between standalone Nexstar's depressed trough earnings and the fully-synergized consolidated figure, with a wide distribution and a fat left tail (unwind, forced divestiture at distressed prices, continued hold-separate costs with no synergy offset).

The analyst price-target range — low $205, mean $252, high $290 — appears to anchor on the consolidated-and-integrated scenario and a discounting of the litigation tail. We do not treat these as floors. The targets reflect the bull scenario of a legal outcome that is currently going against the company, and they are vulnerable to downward revision on any adverse ruling. The 4.2% yield provides some support, but dividend coverage rests on the same FCF that the litigation threatens, and the company suspended buybacks in Q1 2026 to conserve capital for the deal.

We assign a 5.5 valuation score: the stock is genuinely cheap on consolidated forward earnings, but that cheapness is inseparable from a binary legal risk that could vaporize the earnings base. The distribution is two-sided and wide.

## Competitive Landscape

Nexstar's competitive position before the injunction was strong and improving through scale. Its local-TV footprint gives leverage in retransmission negotiations with MVPDs and in affiliation talks with the Big Four networks, plus the country's largest local-news production base. The principal consolidators — Nexstar, Sinclair, TEGNA, Gray — are all pursuing the same scale strategy.

The trajectory has now turned uncertain. The TEGNA litigation establishes the practical ceiling on consolidation: a court has ruled that combined market share alone is presumptively anticompetitive, and the lead private plaintiff is DIRECTV, an MVPD that pays Nexstar's retrans fees and fears its pricing power. The scale that is the moat is now the legal liability. If the deal unwinds, Nexstar reverts to its prior footprint having spent heavily and damaged a key customer relationship. The forced hold-separate has already triggered TEGNA leadership turnover and the appointment of a standalone TEGNA CEO effective June 1, 2026, undermining synergy capture regardless of the ultimate ruling. The outcome will set sector-wide precedent for consolidation runway, a headwind to the structural growth case.

## Risk Assessment

This is a binary, event-driven situation rather than a normal broadcaster, and the risks are concentrated and largely unpriced in the bull scenario:

1. **Deal unwind (the defining risk).** The April 17 preliminary injunction bars integration; an adverse trial outcome could force divestiture or unwind of the closed transaction. Nexstar has appealed to the 9th Circuit on an expedited basis.
2. **FCC approval not final.** Chair Brendan Carr has signaled the full Commission could vote. Cable associations, Newsmax, and public-interest groups are at the D.C. Circuit seeking to unwind FCC approval (emergency stay denied April 28; mandamus briefing continues). Approval carries binding conditions: expanded local-news investment, retrans-rate extensions to certain MVPDs through Nov 30, 2026, and the 6-station divestiture.
3. **Leverage and rate exposure.** ~$12.2B debt, ~$3.6B floating with no hedges, in a higher-for-longer environment.
4. **Secular linear-TV decline.** Cord-cutting pressures both retrans subscriber counts and advertising; the TVFN partnership faces its Dec 31, 2026 dissolution trigger.
5. **VIE/FCC ownership challenge.** A March 2024 FCC Notice of Apparent Liability re: WPIX/Mission alleges unauthorized transfer of control and exceeding the national cap; unresolved.
6. **Litigation overhang.** Local TV Advertising Antitrust MDL (trial set Nov 1, 2027) and the Cubs/Tribune tax dispute (potential ~$225M tax + ~$282M interest; 7th Circuit ruling pending).
7. **Customer concentration.** Two customers each ~14% of revenue; DIRECTV is both a top counterparty and the lead private merger plaintiff.
8. **Key-man and governance.** Founder/CEO Perry Sook is central (contract extended through March 2029). His 2025 total comp rose to $39.5M (comp-actually-paid $46.5M) in a year when net income collapsed and Adjusted EBITDA fell to $1,561M from $2,004M — a pay-for-performance concern, partly offset by 5-year TSR of 217 vs. peer 113.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 46.8% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 34.1% (a 12.7pp premium, 75th percentile within the chain), reflecting the binary legal catalyst the market is pricing. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.5%. The term structure is flat (near 46.8% ≈ far 45.5%), meaning uncertainty is distributed evenly across tenors rather than concentrated in a single dated event, consistent with multi-quarter litigation rather than one binary print. The near-term put/call skew is modestly call-heavy (-3.8%), but the put/call open-interest ratio jumped to 2.26 from 0.77 over the past month as the stock fell 9.7%, indicating accumulating downside hedging. IV fell from 60.0% to 46.8% over the same window, easing somewhat from peak but elevated. The signal supports caution: rich IV plus rising put OI on a declining stock does not argue for owning the equity outright.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$250–290):** The 9th Circuit narrows or stays the injunction, the FCC approval holds, and Nexstar integrates TEGNA, capturing the synergies that justify ~$34 consolidated forward EPS. At a re-rating toward the consolidated earnings base, the stock approaches analyst high targets. FCF of ~$743M+ (rising with integration) comfortably services the debt and funds the 4.2% dividend and resumed buybacks.

**Bear Case (~$120–140 or lower):** The injunction is upheld, a hold-separate persists indefinitely, or the court forces a divestiture/unwind at distressed prices. Nexstar retains ~$6B of incremental debt while losing the earnings underwriting it; floating-rate interest costs grind on FCF; the dividend coverage tightens. Standalone Nexstar reverts to trough economics (FY2025 net income attributable of $109M) on a structurally declining linear-TV base, while having damaged the DIRECTV relationship. At standalone trough earnings with a heavier debt load and no synergies, the equity is worth materially less than today.

The distribution is two-sided, but the left tail is fat and the catalyst is currently trending adverse. The insider record reinforces caution: executives sold into pre-injunction strength at $210–$220 in March and bought nothing on the dip to $178.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is a leveraged option on an adverse, ongoing legal process layered on a declining core business, not an asymmetric long.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- A favorable, durable legal resolution — the 9th Circuit vacating or substantially narrowing the injunction, AND final FCC approval surviving the D.C. Circuit challenge — that removes the unwind risk and de-risks the consolidated earnings base.
- Evidence that integration synergies can actually be captured (hold-separate lifted), validating the ~$34 forward EPS basis.
- A capital-structure de-risking step: meaningful debt paydown, interest-rate hedges placed on the ~$3.6B floating exposure, or net leverage trending toward the low end of the covenant.
- Insider open-market buying at depressed levels, signaling management conviction absent in the current Form 4 record.

**Timeline:** The headwinds persist until the litigation resolves. The 9th Circuit appeal is expedited but uncertain; the underlying antitrust trial and the parallel FCC/D.C. Circuit processes could extend through 2026 and into 2027. The TVFN dissolution trigger and the 6-station divestiture deadline add discrete pressure points within that window.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The binary catalyst — an adverse 9th Circuit ruling or a trial outcome forcing unwind/divestiture — is real and material, but it is not cleanly dated. The 9th Circuit appeal is expedited with no fixed decision date, and the antitrust trial timeline extends well into 2026–2027. The FCC and D.C. Circuit processes are similarly open-ended. There is no single dated event within a near-term window that reliably resolves lower.

**Timing window:** Diffuse across multiple quarters. The flat options term structure confirms the market sees uncertainty spread across tenors rather than concentrated in one print, which works against a tightly-timed short.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is 7.7% of float with 4.9 days to cover — uncrowded, leaving room, but also meaning informed investors have not piled in. A favorable legal headline (stay granted, FCC approval finalized) could squeeze the stock sharply given the heavily call-skewed near-term positioning and ±7.5% implied move on any single expiry.

**Borrow and options signal:** Rich near-term IV makes put-based expression expensive, and the near-term call-heavy skew indicates the options market is not uniformly bearish despite the legal overhang. Put OI has risen, but the headline pricing leans toward upside hedging. Carrying cost is elevated.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear target ~$120–140 (-23% to -34%) on an unwind; upside squeeze risk to $250+ (+38%) on a favorable ruling. With no dated catalyst, elevated IV/carry, and meaningful two-way legal optionality, the reward:risk does not clear the 2:1 bar for an active short. The downside is real but the timing is unfavorable and squeeze risk on any positive headline is severe.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish thesis is credible, but the catalyst lacks a firm timeline, the legal process is genuinely two-sided, and a single favorable ruling could produce a violent squeeze. This is a name to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Conclusion

Nexstar has converted itself into a leveraged bet on antitrust and FCC outcomes that are currently going against it. The company closed the acquisition, raised debt against it, and was then enjoined from integrating the assets — leaving it with a doubled debt load and synergies it cannot capture. The reported EPS strength and the estimate revisions are artifacts of the political-ad cycle and consolidating-but-frozen TEGNA earnings, not durable improvement. The drawdown from the February high reflects rational repricing of a binary legal risk that has not resolved.

The score trajectory improved from 4.6 (April) to 5.6 (May) on the optics of the consolidated earnings and rising estimates. The intervening evidence — the preliminary injunction's substance, the FCC "not final" signals, the forced TEGNA standalone CEO appointment, and the insider record of selling into strength and not buying the dip — reverses that improvement. We move back to AVOID at an overall score of 4.5. Until a durable, favorable legal resolution removes the unwind risk and validates the consolidated earnings base, the risk/reward is unattractive and skewed to the downside. Investors seeking broadcast exposure should wait for legal clarity; existing holders should recognize that the dividend yield does not compensate for the binary tail embedded in the capital structure.
