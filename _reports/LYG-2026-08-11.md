## Executive Summary

Lloyds' operating performance in H1 2026 was the best of the post-crisis era. Statutory pre-tax profit of £4.3bn rose 23% year on year against £4.12bn consensus; Q2 banking net interest margin reached 3.19%, up 15bps YoY and 5bps sequentially; cost:income improved to 49.0% from 52.2%; return on tangible equity reached 17.0%. The group generated 108bps of CET1 in six months and announced an incremental £1.0bn buyback on top of February's £1.75bn, alongside a 30% increase in the interim dividend to 1.58p. Management launched "Accelerate 2030," targeting ~20% RoTE and sub-45% cost:income by 2030 with ~£2bn of technology- and AI-enabled savings, and a flat pro forma CET1 target of ~13.0%, which means essentially all capital generated above growth needs is earmarked for distribution.

The difficulty is that the market has already paid for this. The ADR is up 37.5% over twelve months and sits 2.3% below its 52-week high of $6.34; the ordinary shares are up 44% and near a five-year high. On next-year consensus of $0.63 per ADR the stock trades at 9.8x, which is not demanding in isolation, but a rough triangulation of tangible net asset value puts price/TNAV near 1.8x — against a Gordon-growth justified level of roughly 1.75x on 17% RoTE, 3% growth and an 11% cost of equity. Today's price already capitalises delivery of the improvement rather than scepticism about it, and the four analysts covering the ADR carry it at Hold with a mean target of $6.455, 4.2% above the current price. The 30-day and 90-day consensus revisions for the current year are −3.8% and −6.2%, so earnings momentum in the estimate series is negative even as reported results beat.

Two unquantified binaries sit on top of a fully-valued price. The FCA's £9.1bn motor-finance redress scheme, against which Lloyds has provisioned £1.95bn cumulatively and explicitly declined to litigate, has been partially suspended pending challenges brought by other parties, with hearings expected December 2026 or February 2027. The outcome is genuinely two-sided (provision release if the scheme narrows, top-up risk if uptake exceeds FCA modelling) but unresolvable before 2027. Separately, an active UK debate over a bank windfall tax under the new Prime Minister falls disproportionately on Lloyds, which has essentially no international earnings to shift and, given the flat CET1 target, would absorb an incremental levy directly in buyback capacity. Our probability-weighted 12-month total return is roughly 5-6%, most of it dividend. That is a HOLD for existing owners on yield, not an entry point.

## Company Overview

Lloyds Banking Group is the largest UK-focused retail and commercial bank and the UK's largest mortgage lender, operating through Retail, Business & Commercial / Corporate & Institutional Banking, and Insurance, Pensions & Investments. Brands include Lloyds Bank, Halifax, Bank of Scotland, Black Horse (motor finance) and Scottish Widows. Lloyds Bank plc is the principal ring-fenced banking subsidiary; Lloyds Bank Corporate Markets plc is the non-ring-fenced entity, a structure mandated by UK ring-fencing legislation.

Almost all of the group's earnings, credit risk, funding and regulatory exposure sit inside one national economy. That concentration is the source of both the franchise quality — dominant retail deposit share, low funding cost, mortgage scale — and the risk profile: one rate cycle, one housing market, one regulator, one tax authority.

A methodological note materially affects what can and cannot be verified here: as a UK foreign private issuer, Lloyds files a Form 20-F rather than a 10-K, reports interims on Form 6-K rather than 10-Q, and is exempt from Section 16 insider reporting and US proxy rules. There are consequently **no Form 4 filings and no DEF 14A** for this issuer. The FY2025 20-F (filed 13 February 2026) was confirmed to exist via the EDGAR index but its text could not be retrieved. Segment revenue splits, market-share percentages, impairment charges, coverage ratios, risk-weighted assets, MREL and the UK leverage ratio are therefore not filing-verified in this report and are not asserted as facts. This is a real disclosure-quality consideration for ADR holders.

## Financial Analysis

### Reported trajectory

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statutory PBT | £1.99bn | £2.27bn | +14% |
| Net income | £4.52bn | £4.96bn | +9.7% |
| Net interest income | £3.36bn | £3.71bn | +10% |
| Banking NIM | ~3.04% (derived) | 3.19% | +15bps |
| Cost:income | 52.2% | 49.0% | −3.2pp |
| RoTE | 15.5% | 17.0% | +1.5pp |
| CET1 | 13.8% | 13.6% | −20bps |

H1 2026 statutory PBT was £4.3bn versus £3.5bn in H1 2025. FY2025 PBT was £6.7bn, up 12% and ahead of £6.4bn consensus, implying FY2024 of roughly £5.98bn (derived from the growth rate, not read from a filing).

### The composition of the beat matters

Q2 net income of £4.96bn came in essentially in line with £4.95bn consensus. The entire pre-tax beat (£2.27bn vs £2.10bn) came from costs and below-the-line items. Operating costs were described as broadly unchanged in H1 while income rose roughly 10%. Holding nominal costs flat through UK wage inflation is a genuine achievement and lends credibility to the £2bn Accelerate 2030 savings target. It is also finite in a way revenue growth is not: costs can be removed once, and "broadly unchanged" could reflect the timing of investment spend rather than a structural step-down, in which case a back-loaded 2026 cost base would reverse the flattery. The £2.10bn Q2 consensus was company-compiled, standard UK practice but not an independent benchmark.

### What drives NII, and what is unverified

Management attributes higher NII first to structural hedge income, then to franchise-led volume growth and average interest-earning asset growth. The mechanism is contractual: receive-fixed swap tranches written during the near-zero-rate era are maturing and being reinvested at materially higher rates, so the NII uplift accrues largely regardless of the next Bank of England move, provided deposit balances hold. The direction is an issuer statement. The **magnitude and remaining duration are not sourced** — hedge notional, maturing yield and reinvestment yield could not be obtained, and this is the single most important gap in assessing whether the ~20% 2030 RoTE target is mechanically supported or requires further management delivery.

An alternative explanation for part of the 15bps NIM gain is deposit repricing lag: asset yields resetting faster than savings rates are passed through. The company's own ordering argues against this being dominant, but it cannot be ruled out on the available evidence, and if it were a larger component the margin gain would be more competitively fragile, because deposit pricing is a choice competitors can force.

### A definitional item that overstates "other income"

H1 NII of £7.3bn plus other income of £3.3bn sums to £10.6bn gross, while two quarters at Q2's £4.96bn net income implies roughly £9.92bn — a gap of about £0.68bn. Both Reuters-derived and Alliance News commentary independently flag higher operating lease depreciation as an offset to profit growth, so the gap is most plausibly vehicle-leasing depreciation. This calculation assumes Q1 and Q2 net income were approximately equal and is therefore indicative rather than precise; if Q1 was weaker, the implied depreciation is smaller. The practical consequence is that headline "other income up 11%" overstates the P&L benefit, and the Accelerate 2030 target of high-single-digit *other operating income* CAGR may translate into materially slower net contribution growth.

### Capital and distributions

CET1 of 13.6% (13.1% pro forma for the 2025 buyback) against a ~13.0% pro forma 2030 target means the plan is to run at approximately today's level rather than build capital. With 108bps of H1 capital generation, distributions become the residual claimant on earnings. Announced 2026 buybacks total £2.75bn (£1.75bn in February, £1.0bn in July). At 113.35p and an assumed ~58-59bn ordinary shares, market capitalisation is roughly £66bn, so the announced buyback is about 4.2% of the equity. If the full-year dividend scales with the 30% interim increase, DPS of roughly 4.6-4.9p implies a 4.1-4.3% dividend yield on the ordinary shares, or about $0.25-0.26 per ADR — a forward yield near 4.0-4.2% on $6.196. The 2.66% dividend yield in the quote feed is a trailing figure that has not yet absorbed the increase. Combined shareholder yield is therefore around 8.0-8.5%, with the share-count assumption flagged as unverified.

### On the "negative free cash flow" flag

Automated screens will surface negative conventional free cash flow and high leverage for LYG. For a deposit-funded bank both are artifacts: operating cash flow swings with loan, deposit and trading balances, so a growing loan book mechanically produces negative "FCF," and corporate-style leverage is not the binding constraint. The relevant constraints are CET1 (13.6%), the UK leverage ratio, MREL and LCR — of which only CET1 was obtainable. This is a statement about metric applicability, not evidence of balance-sheet strength.

**Financial health score: 6.5.** Solid capital generation and best-in-class efficiency, offset by a £1.95bn open conduct provision with two-sided outcome risk, and by the absence of verified asset-quality data — impairments were noted qualitatively as rising in Q2 2026, and corporate defaults had already risen in H1 2025, but Stage 3 balances, coverage and the asset quality ratio are unobtained. That gap prevents a higher score for a lender at this point in the UK cycle.

Mid-single-digit revenue CAGR with cost:income falling ~4pp over four years implies high-single-digit earnings growth, augmented by 3-4% annual share-count reduction from buybacks — so mid-teens EPS growth is arithmetically plausible without any multiple expansion. Reuters reported analysts characterising the targets as conservative; a ~20% RoTE in 2030 versus 17.0% delivered today is only ~3pp over four years for a bank that has beaten consensus in each of the last two reported periods.

Against that, the estimate series is moving the wrong way. Current-year ADR EPS consensus has fallen from $0.56 to $0.52 over 90 days (−6.2%) and from $0.54 over 30 days (−3.8%); next year has slipped 3.1% over 90 days to $0.63. The coverage is thin — one analyst on the current year, two on next year — which lowers the signal quality in both directions, but there is no positive revision momentum to lean on here. The strong-inflection setup that would justify overriding a full valuation (rapidly rising estimates alongside accelerating revenue) is absent.

Two elements of the plan warrant scepticism. First, "focused international expansion" in corporate and institutional banking moves the group toward wholesale businesses where it has no scale advantage and where far larger US and European competitors operate — the same activities UK banks retreated from after 2008 because they consumed capital and produced volatile returns. Second, the mix shift toward fee income diversifies *revenue sources*, but Lloyds' fundamental concentration is geographic and macroeconomic. Growing UK insurance, pensions and investment fees does nothing to reduce UK credit-cycle exposure.

The domestic macro backdrop is a structural constraint on the credible pace of volume growth: for a bank whose earnings are a levered play on UK employment, housing turnover and corporate credit, single-country concentration caps how fast the loan book can grow even if margin holds. **Growth prospects score: 5.0.** A credible, well-specified but modest plan, with negative near-term estimate momentum and a domestic macro constraint.

## Valuation Assessment

The quoted trailing and forward P/E of 59.3x in the data feed is not usable: it derives from a $0.093 trailing ADR EPS field that appears to represent roughly a single quarter, and it is inconsistent with the consensus series. The reconcilable arithmetic is as follows.

Consensus ADR EPS is $0.52 for the current year and $0.63 for next year. At the $6.196 basis that is **11.9x current-year and 9.8x next-year**. Cross-checking from fundamentals: Q2 PBT of £2.27bn at an assumed ~28% effective UK bank rate (corporation tax plus surcharge) is roughly £1.63bn post-tax; less ~£0.1bn of quarterly AT1 distributions gives ~£1.53bn attributable, or ~£6.1bn annualised, which on ~58.5bn shares is ~10.4p per ordinary share, or ~41.6p per ADR ≈ $0.56 at prevailing sterling. That reconciles with the $0.52 current-year consensus within the error of the assumptions.

Working the same figures back through the reported 17.0% RoTE implies average tangible equity of roughly £36bn, or **TNAV per share around 61-62p**, putting price/TNAV near **1.8x** at 113.35p. Every step of that derivation carries assumption risk — tax rate, AT1 deduction, share count, single-quarter annualisation, and Lloyds' precise RoTE denominator convention — and a ±10% error in tangible equity moves the multiple to roughly 1.6-2.0x. It should be read as a range, not a fact. Confirming actual TNAV per share is the highest-priority follow-on task.

The range establishes a directional point: Lloyds no longer trades at the discount to tangible book that characterised UK banks for most of the post-crisis period. A Gordon-growth fair value of (RoTE − g)/(COE − g) gives 1.75x at 17% RoTE, 3% growth and 11% cost of equity; 1.56x if cost of equity is 12%; and 2.13x if the ~20% 2030 RoTE is fully capitalised. At ~1.8x, the market is paying today for delivery of most of the plan, with the 2030 ambition supplying the only clear headroom.

Peer context supports the same conclusion from a different angle. Lloyds' ~10x forward earnings is broadly in line with UK large-bank peers, but its price/tangible-book is the highest of the group because its RoTE is the highest. [unverified — no peer benchmark data was available in Phase 1 (peer_benchmarks: not available) and no peer NIM/RoTE/multiple comparisons appear in the research brief]

Consensus positioning corroborates: four analysts, all Hold, no Buys and no Sells over four consecutive monthly periods; targets range $5.95 to $7.02 with a mean of $6.455 and median $6.425. The mean implies 4.2% upside. Analyst targets can lag reality on inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples, but here estimates are falling and the stock trades near the top of the target range, so the targets should be read as a genuine constraint rather than a stale floor.

The support under the price is absolute yield, not multiple. Roughly 4.0-4.2% forward dividend plus ~4.2% of announced buyback gives a combined shareholder yield near 8%, and the flat ~13% CET1 target makes that durable so long as (a) no incremental UK bank tax is enacted, and (b) the motor-finance provision proves sufficient. Both are open questions.

**Valuation score: 4.8.** Not expensive on earnings, but full on tangible book, with negative revisions, consensus at Hold, and asymmetry tilted against the buyer at 2.3% below the 52-week high.

## Competitive Landscape

Lloyds' durable advantages are visible in the numbers rather than in strategy language: a very large, sticky, low-cost retail deposit base that makes the structural hedge valuable; scale economics evidenced by a 49.0% cost:income ratio that few retail banks achieve; and capital generation that funds distributions without shrinking the balance sheet. Lending and deposits both grew in H1 2026, which is the combination that sustains hedge income.

The trajectory question is harder. Reuters framed the result as continuing "a strong run of earnings from Britain's major banks, which have benefited in recent years from higher interest rates, resilient credit quality and robust capital generation." A material share of the 15bps NIM expansion and the 17% RoTE is a sector-wide rate-cycle tide lifting NatWest, Barclays' ring-fenced bank, Santander UK and Lloyds together. Peer NIM and RoTE comparisons that would separate franchise alpha from cycle beta were not obtainable, so the honest assessment is that Lloyds is a high-quality franchise whose *relative* trajectory versus domestic peers is unproven from the available evidence.

Two competitive negatives deserve weight. Black Horse's scale in motor finance, normally an advantage, has converted into roughly 21% of an industry-wide £9.1bn redress scheme (£1.95bn ÷ £9.1bn; an order-of-magnitude share, since Lloyds' provision includes administration costs and may be constructed on a different basis than the FCA's scheme-wide estimate). And FCA chief executive Nikhil Rathi's December 2025 remark to the Treasury Select Committee that aggrieved motor lenders are "very focused" on the negatives is a reminder that conduct cost in UK retail banking is recurring rather than one-off; the PPI precedent hangs over any judgment that £1.95bn is final.

**Competitive position score: 6.3.** A strong, stable franchise with genuine cost leadership; trajectory improving on efficiency, flat-to-unclear on share, and modestly negative where the international CIB push takes the group outside its scale advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Motor finance redress — the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** Lloyds added £800m in October 2025 for a cumulative £1.95bn, describing the FCA's proposed scheme as falling at "the adverse end of the range of previous expected outcomes." Q3 2025 profit fell 36% and full-year guidance was downgraded on that charge, a demonstration of how quickly a conduct item can overwhelm improving operating performance. The FCA published the £9.1bn scheme on 30 March 2026; Lloyds confirmed on 2 April that no change to the provision was required and, per an FT report relayed by Reuters in April, declined to mount a legal challenge (that specific item is two removes from primary and not company-confirmed here). On 2 July 2026 the FCA suspended parts of the scheme pending challenges brought by other parties. The scheme covers regulated agreements from 6 April 2007 to 1 November 2024 where the lender or a third party paid commission — a seventeen-year lookback. Outcomes are genuinely two-sided: a narrowed scheme could permit provision release; higher-than-modelled uptake could require a top-up. Lloyds' own disclosed sensitivity range around the £1.95bn and its eligible-agreement population were not obtainable, which is the most valuable missing datapoint on this risk. No motive should be inferred from the provisioning or no-litigation decisions; cost certainty and regulatory relations are a sufficient commercial explanation.

**UK bank taxation — the highest-impact unquantified risk.** With a new Prime Minister and an active public debate over a windfall tax or additional levy, HSBC's CEO has publicly warned against it while Charlie Nunn declined to take a position, saying the bank would "wait and see." Reuters reported that the government will continue a finance-friendly agenda "for now." Lloyds, with the least international earnings and the least ability to relocate activity, would bear a UK-specific levy most heavily in relative terms, and given the flat CET1 target, the pass-through would fall on buyback capacity rather than capital build. No measure has been proposed in legislative form or quantified, so this is a live policy debate rather than a modellable input.

**Concentration and credit cycle.** As the largest UK mortgage lender in a domestic economy to which its earnings are directly geared — UK house prices, transaction volumes and unemployment — Lloyds carries single-country cyclicality. Impairment charges rose in Q2 2026, and corporate defaults had already increased in H1 2025. Without the H1 2026 impairment charge, coverage ratios or Stage 3 balances, it is not possible to distinguish genuinely benign credit from early-cycle credit. This is the most consequential analytical gap in the file.

**Rate-cycle asymmetry.** The hedge tailwind currently supporting NIM was created by the prior low-rate era. A return to very low policy rates would eventually reverse it with a multi-year lag. Higher-for-longer supports margin but pressures credit and housing volumes; the two effects work against each other.

**Named issuer risk drivers.** The Lloyds Bank plc H1 2026 RNS names tariffs twice, including a parenthetical inserted into the general economic-conditions clause — indirect for a domestic bank, transmitting via UK corporate credit and GDP, and its prominence suggests the drafters see that channel as live. It also names "risks related to new and emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence" in the same period management announced the ~£2bn AI-enabled savings programme; both can be true, and the juxtaposition is worth holding in tension. Also named: defined-benefit pension scheme risk (gilt yields, longevity), a ring-fencing/structural-reform clause (relaxation would be a modest positive for capital and funding efficiency), standalone conduct risk, and the ability to derive cost savings. No claim is made that any factor was newly added, since prior-year comparison was not possible.

**Short interest and positioning.** Short interest is 0.0% of float with 0.1 days to cover and 3.27m shares short. There is no bearish positioning to fade and no squeeze mechanics in either direction; the ADR is a low-conviction, low-controversy holding for US investors.

**Governance disclosure.** There are no Form 4s and no proxy statement for this issuer, so there is **no insider-transaction signal in either direction** — this is stated as an absence, not as reassurance. LTIP linkage to the Accelerate 2030 RoTE and cost:income targets is unverified; a 2030 target without incentive linkage is a materially weaker commitment. Chair succession status is unknown. One positive disclosure behaviour: full-year reporting moved forward to 29 January 2026 from late February in prior years, generally a sign of confidence in close processes.

**Risk factors score: 4.5.** Two dated or live binaries with asymmetric downside (redress litigation hearings in Dec-2026/Feb-2027; UK levy debate), single-country concentration, and unverifiable asset quality — against a price 2.3% below its high that leaves little cushion.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $7.30 (+17.8%)

Structural hedge roll-on is contractual and multi-year, so NII compounds toward and beyond the >£14.9bn 2026 target with limited dependence on the Bank of England. Cost discipline that held nominal operating costs flat through UK wage inflation proves structural, delivering sub-50% cost:income in 2026 and putting the £2bn technology programme on a credible path to sub-45% by 2030. RoTE holds above 17% and analysts' judgment that the 2030 targets are conservative proves correct, prompting upgrades toward and beyond 18% by 2028. Legal challenges narrow the FCA scheme and Lloyds releases part of the £1.95bn provision, a clean, quantifiable earnings and capital catalyst. No incremental UK bank levy is enacted, so the ~£2.75bn annual distribution run-rate persists and 3-4% annual share-count reduction compounds mid-teens EPS growth. At 1.9-2.0x a rising TNAV, or ~11.5x $0.65 of upgraded 2027 EPS, the ADR reaches $7.30, plus ~4% dividend.

### Bear Case — $4.75 (−23.3%)

A UK recession converts an early-cycle credit picture into a real one: impairments rise from the Q2 2026 increase already flagged, mortgage volumes fall with housing turnover, and RoTE reverts toward 13-14%. An incremental UK bank levy is enacted, falling directly on distributions because the flat ~13% CET1 target leaves no capital buffer to absorb it, and the buyback is cut. The December-2026/February-2027 hearings uphold the scheme while participation exceeds FCA modelling, forcing a top-up beyond £1.95bn — a repeat of the October 2025 pattern in which a conduct charge cut quarterly profit 36% and triggered a guidance downgrade. Cost savings prove to have been investment-spend timing rather than structural, and the international CIB expansion consumes capital at sub-hurdle returns. On those inputs, ~8.5x a cut $0.55 EPS, or ~1.35x TNAV, gives $4.75.

### Base Case — $6.60 (+6.5% price, ~10.5% total return)

Accelerate 2030 delivers roughly as guided: 2026 RoTE above 16%, cost:income below 50%, mid-single-digit revenue CAGR thereafter. The redress provision proves broadly adequate with resolution pushed into 2027, and no bank levy is enacted in the next twelve months. The multiple holds at roughly 10.5x on $0.63 of 2027 consensus EPS, or approximately 1.8x a modestly growing TNAV, giving $6.60, with the ~4% dividend supplying the majority of a ~10.5% total return.

Probability-weighting base 55%, bull 20%, bear 25% gives roughly +1.7% expected price return plus ~4% dividend — a mid-single-digit expected total return with a 23% left tail. That is a fair-return profile, not an asymmetric one, and it does not clear the bar for new capital in a sector where positive raw returns have more often reflected the rate cycle than company selection.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income).** Horizon 12 months, with the natural reassessment points being the FY2026 results (expected late January 2027, given the shift to earlier prelims) and the motor-finance hearings scheduled for December 2026 or February 2027.

**Targets:** base $6.60 (+6.5%); bull $7.30 (+17.8%); bear $4.75 (−23.3%). Analyst range $5.95-$7.02, mean $6.455.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. The ADR trades toward **$5.40-5.60** (roughly 8.6-8.9x 2027 consensus EPS and ~1.55-1.60x estimated TNAV) with 2026 guidance intact — that entry restores a double-digit expected return with the same yield support.
2. The December-2026/February-2027 proceedings narrow the FCA scheme and Lloyds announces a **release from the £1.95bn provision**, converting the largest overhang into a capital and earnings catalyst.
3. The 2027 consensus revision trend turns positive for two consecutive months alongside confirmation of the structural hedge notional and remaining reinvestment tailwind, which would substantiate the ~18% 2028 RoTE step.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**
1. A UK bank windfall tax or incremental levy is **proposed in legislative form with a quantified rate**, given that the flat ~13.0% CET1 target routes any such cost straight into buyback capacity.
2. Lloyds **increases the motor-finance provision** above £1.95bn, or the H1/FY impairment charge and asset quality ratio deteriorate materially versus 2025 comparatives.
3. RoTE falls below the >16% 2026 target, or cost:income moves back above 50%, indicating the cost performance was timing rather than structural.

**Do not treat as an upgrade trigger:** further price appreciation toward the $7.02 high target on unchanged fundamentals. At ~1.8x tangible book with falling estimates, incremental multiple expansion would reduce, not improve, the forward return.

## Conclusion

Lloyds has executed well. A 17.0% return on tangible equity, a 49.0% cost:income ratio, 3.19% banking margin and 108bps of half-year capital generation are the best set of operating numbers this franchise has produced since the financial crisis, and the flat ~13.0% CET1 target converts that performance into a durable ~8% combined shareholder yield. The Accelerate 2030 plan is specific, measurable, and authored by the same executives who ran the prior programme, which makes them accountable for it.

The problem is price. A 37.5% twelve-month ADR advance to within 2.3% of the 52-week high has taken the stock from a discount to tangible book — the condition that defined UK bank investing for fifteen years — to roughly 1.8x estimated TNAV, which is approximately what 17% RoTE and an 11% cost of equity justify. Consensus is uniformly Hold with a mean target 4.2% above spot; current-year estimates have been cut 6.2% over 90 days; and the earnings beat that drove the July re-rating came from costs rather than revenue, which is the more finite of the two. Layered on top are a suspended £9.1bn redress scheme whose resolution Lloyds surrendered control of by choosing not to litigate, and a UK bank-tax debate that would fall on this bank harder than on any listed peer.

Existing holders are being paid roughly 4% in dividends plus a 4% buyback to wait for the December-2026/February-2027 hearings, and that is a reasonable proposition. New capital has no urgency: the return available at $6.196 is mid-single-digit expected total return against a 23% bear case, and the two catalysts that would materially improve the picture — a provision release or a lower entry price — are both identifiable and both worth waiting for. **HOLD.**
