**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Newell Brands reported a Q2 2026 that looked like an inflection: net sales $1,994M (+3.1%), gross margin 40.7% versus 35.4%, operating income $283M versus $171M, GAAP diluted EPS $0.25 versus $0.11, adjusted EPS $0.42 against a $0.19 consensus, and raised full-year guidance. The stock rose 39% intraday on July 31 and gave back most of it by midday. The reason for the round trip is visible in the 10-Q: approximately $126M of the $127M year-over-year gross profit increase came from the recovery of tariffs paid under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, invalidated by the Supreme Court on February 20, 2026. Excluding both refund components, Q2 gross profit was $686M against $685M a year earlier — flat on 3% higher sales, with underlying gross margin down roughly 100bps.

The half-year view, which nets out the intra-2026 timing distortion, is the fairer one and it shows genuine but modest progress: H1 gross margin ex-refund of 34.6% versus 33.9% (+70bps), and normalized operating income of $217M versus $192M (+13%) against the +65% headline. Segment normalization is where the analysis turns decisive. Excluding the 2025-related refund, H1 segment operating income was L&D $353M (from $300M, +18%), Home & Commercial Solutions $16M (from $22M), and Outdoor & Recreation −$10M (from +$3M). The entire improvement is in Learning & Development. The two segments that lost a combined $163M in FY2025 deteriorated further. And H1 reported sales growth of $42M (+1.2%) is fully accounted for by $62M of favorable FX plus a $25M change in estimate on customer programs, implying roughly −1.3% underlying.

The genuinely positive development is financing. On July 30 Newell closed a five-year, up-to-$800M asset-based revolver led by JPMorgan, drew $490M, and retired the $1.0B Credit Revolver whose Total Net Leverage covenant was scheduled to step down at the quarter ending September 30, 2026 — the clearest near-term solvency trigger in the annual report. The new facility's financial covenant is a springing fixed-charge coverage test. Separately, the senior notes traded above carrying value at 6/30/26 ($4,642M fair value versus $4,534M book) after trading below at year-end. Credit risk has been pushed out. Equity value has not been created: total debt rose from $4,673M to $5,006M over H1, interest expense runs at a 6.8% weighted average and climbing, FY2025 free cash flow was $17M against a ~$124M annual dividend, and tangible book value is approximately −$2.2B. We maintain AVOID at an overall score of 3.4, a modest improvement from 3.2 that reflects the covenant resolution rather than any change in earnings quality.

## Company Overview

Newell is a global manufacturer and marketer of consumer products, incorporated in Delaware, headquartered in Atlanta, selling in over 150 countries with operations in more than 45 and approximately 21,900 employees at year-end 2025, of whom roughly 61% work in manufacturing and supply chain. This is a capital- and labor-intensive manufacturer.

Three segments produced FY2025 net sales of $7,204M:

| Segment | FY25 Sales | FY25 Op Income | Margin | Key brands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home & Commercial Solutions | $3,772M (−7.3%) | −$138M | −3.7% | Rubbermaid, Yankee Candle, Calphalon, Crock-Pot, FoodSaver, Sistema |
| Learning & Development | $2,691M (−1.0%) | +$464M | 17.2% | Sharpie, Paper Mate, Parker, EXPO, Elmer's; Graco, NUK, Aprica |
| Outdoor & Recreation | $741M (−6.7%) | −$25M | −3.4% | Coleman, Contigo, Marmot, Campingaz |

The framing that matters: Learning & Development earned $464M of segment operating income while the other two segments lost $163M combined, and consolidated operating income was $39M on $7,204M of sales after $340M of impairments and $90M of restructuring. An investor is buying a good writing-and-baby business attached to a highly levered holding company and two structurally impaired segments.

Customer concentration is high and increasing. Amazon was 17% of FY2025 net sales (15% in 2024, 13% in 2023) and Walmart 13% (14%, 15%). Top two combined moved from 28% to 29% to 30%. The mix shifted from Walmart toward Amazon while total concentration rose — that is not diversification. The 10-K states there are generally no long-term supply contracts or minimum-purchase guarantees with the largest customers and that unshipped orders are not material. There is effectively no revenue visibility beyond sell-in forecasting.

### The multi-year base

Revenue fell 23.8% from FY2022 to FY2025, with three consecutive GAAP net losses totaling $889M. Gross margin recovered 490bps from the 2023 trough, which is real work by management, but it did not reach the operating line: FY2025 operating income of $39M was below FY2024's $67M, and interest expense, net was $321M — 8.2x operating income. Adding back the $340M of non-cash impairments gives $379M of adjusted EBIT against $321M of interest, or 1.18x coverage. The operating business, before impairments, barely out-earns its interest bill.

### Cash generation and the dividend

FY2025 operating cash flow was $264M (from $496M), capex $247M, free cash flow $17M — 0.2% of revenue. Management attributed the decline to lower receivable collections on lower sales, tariff cash costs, and higher incentive-compensation payments.

The dividend is $0.07 quarterly, $62M declared in H1 2026, roughly $124M annualized on ~426M shares. Against $17M of FY2025 free cash flow, the dividend was funded from the balance sheet while revolver borrowings rose. This is the single clearest capital-allocation error in the story: Newell issued $1.25B of 8.500% notes in May 2025 to retire 4.200% notes, and is simultaneously distributing ~$124M a year to shareholders. Retiring the 2027 notes with that cash would save roughly 6.4% pre-tax; paying it out costs the same and does nothing for the springing ABL maturity.

Two disclosed cash-flow-quality items deserve attention. Receivables sold under two factoring programs rose from $395M at 12/31/25 to $510M at 6/30/26 — a $115M increase that flows through operating cash inflows in the period of increase. This is GAAP-compliant sale accounting, but reported OCF should be read alongside the factoring balance rather than in isolation. A supplier finance program with ~119-day average terms carried only $7M at 6/30/26 and is immaterial.

Newell paid ~$120M of IEEPA tariffs in 2025 and ~$20M in 2026, concluded recovery was probable, and recorded a ~$100M pretax credit relating to 2025-expensed tariffs plus $38M relating to 2026 (of which $26M reverses a Q1 2026 expense), primarily in cost of products sold. Other receivables jumped from $102M to $285M.

Removing the prior-year component only, H1 gross profit was $1,225M versus $1,188M (+3.1%) and operating income $217M versus $192M (+13%). Removing both components from Q2, gross profit was $686M versus $685M. The improvement is real and it is small.

The revenue bridge is the more troubling item. H1 sales rose $42M; FX contributed $62M and a refinement of estimates on customer programs contributed $25M ($17M H&CS, $7M L&D Writing). The residual is approximately −$45M, or −1.3%. Management reports Q2 core sales of +2.3% on its own definition; that figure and this construction are not reconciled, and part of the gap is Q2-versus-H1 timing plus the disclosed pull-forward of retailer reset orders from Q1 into Q2. Product-line detail is consistent with the narrow read: Baby $516M (+8.4%), Writing $929M (+2.7%), Kitchen $827M (+1.5%), against Commercial $624M (−3.3%), Home Fragrance $232M (−4.9%), O&R flat. International sales were $1,208M versus $1,198M *including* the $62M FX tailwind, implying international volumes fell.

### Balance sheet at 6/30/26

Total debt $5,006M (from $4,673M), driven by revolver borrowings rising from $130M to $470M. Cash $209M, of which $144M sits at non-US subsidiaries. Net debt ~$4,797M. Weighted average interest rate 6.8% in Q2, up from 6.3%. Note stack includes $494M of 6.375% notes due 2027, $1,241M of 8.500% due 2028, and progressively longer maturities to 2046.

Against $2,451M of book equity sit $3,086M of goodwill and $1,578M of other intangibles — tangible book value of approximately −$2,213M. Deferred tax assets of $787M depend on future taxable income. Inventories rose from $1,281M to $1,491M in six months with finished goods up 20% to $1,149M; some of that is seasonal (Newell concentrates cash generation in H2), but a 20% finished-goods build against ~1% sales growth is worth watching against the company's own inventory-valuation risk factor.

Financial health screens in the distress zone: Altman Z 1.19, current ratio 1.07, debt/equity 3.48. The Z-score is dominated by near-zero EBIT/assets (0.004), so it is measuring the profitability collapse more than an imminent liquidity event. With market capitalization below book equity, substituting market for book value of equity would not rescue the reading.

**Financial Health: 3.0.** Roughly 5.7x net leverage on a constructed FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of ~$780M (using 12/31/25 net debt of $4,470M), 1.18x adjusted EBIT coverage, free cash flow of $17M, an uncovered dividend, negative tangible book, and rising absolute debt. The ABL refinancing and the notes trading above par are meaningful offsets to near-term default risk and are why this is 3.0 rather than lower.

## Growth Analysis

Management's own words are the most important growth input in the 10-K: "The Company expects that current market contraction is reflective of a *reset of demand levels*." That concedes the 2022 peak of $9.5B is not a baseline to model back toward, and it reframes $1.0B of impairments over three years as recognition of a permanently lower earnings base. Newell also attributes declines partly to "net distribution losses" — lost shelf space, which is harder to recover than soft demand.

Where growth exists, it is narrow and identifiable. Baby (+8.4% in H1) is the fastest-growing line, benefiting from safety regulation, brand trust and switching friction. Writing (+2.7%) benefits from Sharpie's habit-driven category economics. Together these sit in the segment earning 17%+ margins. Everything else is flat or shrinking.

Earnings growth over the next 12–18 months is more likely to come from cost than from volume. The December 2025 Productivity Plan targets over 900 role eliminations with $75–90M of charges, $56M recognized inception-to-date, and savings phasing through 2026 — so the H1 normalized run rate probably understates the exit rate. Gross margin expansion of 490bps since 2023 shows the supply-chain work is landing.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive rather than the negative momentum a summary label might suggest: current-year consensus $0.58 (+2.8% over 90 days), next quarter $0.23 (+11.7%), next year $0.67 (+1.7%), nine analysts. These are non-GAAP figures and nowhere near the magnitude that would flag an inflection. Consensus implies roughly 15% EPS growth from 2026 to 2027 — respectable, but on a base that does not convert to cash. The FY2025 gap between "normalized" earnings and $17M of free cash flow is the central tell: a 45.6% effective tax rate, $247M of capex, and $321M of interest can absorb the entire non-GAAP earnings line.

Cost headwinds are building. The 10-Q cites "increases in resin and transportation costs, driven largely by higher oil prices in light of the conflict in the Middle East." Resin (polyethylene, polypropylene, copolyester) is a core input, so crude is a direct margin variable, and the oil complex is not obviously mean-reverting from here. Tariff relief is also two-sided: since the IEEPA ruling the administration has imposed new Section 122 and Section 301 tariffs and proposed more, and the 10-Q describes a "highly uncertain trade environment."

**Growth Prospects: 3.5.** Underlying demand has not inflected at the consolidated level; growth is confined to two product lines; the credible earnings path is restructuring-driven cost reduction against a rising input-cost backdrop.

## Valuation Assessment

At $5.14 on 430.2M diluted shares, equity value is approximately $2.21B and enterprise value approximately $7.01B including $4.80B of net debt. Using a constructed FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of ~$780M (operating income $39M + D&A $311M + impairments $340M + restructuring $90M), EV/adjusted EBITDA is roughly 9.0x and net debt/adjusted EBITDA roughly 6.1x on the 6/30/26 balance sheet.

The optical case is straightforward. At $5.14 against management's post-Q1 normalized EPS guidance of $0.56–$0.60 (since raised, though we do not have the revised range), the multiple is 8.6–9.2x; against next-year consensus of $0.67, 7.7x. Peer median P/E in the consumer discretionary comparable set is 21.5x. The shares also trade below book equity of $2,451M, and the 52-week high of $7.13 is 39% above spot.

We do not find this persuasive, for three specific reasons.

First, the P/E is calculated on a number that does not become cash. FY2025 normalized metrics were respectable while free cash flow was $17M. The wedge — capex, cash interest at 6.8% and rising, and a 45.6% effective tax rate — is structural, not timing. An 8x multiple on non-cash-converting EPS is not a discount.

Second, the EV multiple is the honest one, and 9.0x EV/EBITDA for a business with declining underlying revenue, two of three segments losing money, and ~6x leverage is fair-to-full rather than cheap. The gap between the 8x P/E and the 9x EV/EBITDA *is* the leverage. That works in both directions: on $850M of EBITDA at 9.0x, equity is worth roughly $7.00; on $700M at 8.5x, roughly $2.40. A 1x change in the EV multiple moves equity value by about 35%. This is a levered call option on EBITDA, not a value stock.

Third, the multiple must absorb an unreserved contingency stack that is large relative to a $2.2B market cap: Section 245A regulatory exposure of $180–220M that management has not accrued; transfer-pricing assessment of $80M plus ~$34M of penalties; $360M of unrecognized tax benefits; certified Baby-business class actions with no reserve and an acknowledged possibility of material adverse outcome; two putative class actions seeking disgorgement of the IEEPA refund that drove the Q2 beat; and ~$131M of disclosed goodwill-impairment sensitivity in the Commercial reporting unit.

Positioning offers no independent support for a long. Consensus mean target is $5.59 and median $5.13 against a $5.14 price — the stock sits at the median. The distribution ($3.50 low, $9.00 high) is unusually wide, appropriately so for a levered equity, but there is no analyst-target floor to lean on here, and the guardrail against treating targets as floors applies: nine analysts split 2 strong buy / 1 buy / 5 hold / 1 sell is not a mispricing signal.

**Valuation: 4.0.** Up modestly from 3.5 because the removal of the September 2026 covenant trigger legitimately narrows the distress discount. Not higher, because the cheapness is measured on a metric that does not convert to cash and the EV multiple embeds no discount at all.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own competitive framing is defensive. It describes a channel "dominated by large omni-channel and e-commerce retailers" with "strong negotiating power," retailers who "import products directly from foreign sources and source and sell products under their own private label brands, typically at lower prices," and "a proliferation of digitally native brands" enabled because e-commerce reduces barriers to entry. With Amazon at 17% and Walmart at 13% of sales and no long-term contracts, pricing power sits with the buyer.

Newell's most credible structural advantage is its manufacturing footprint: 15 US plants plus two in Mexico producing goods representing over half of US revenues not subject to recently announced tariffs, supported by centralized procurement across 50+ countries. In a Section 301 world this is durable and differentiated versus import-dependent competitors. It also implies the other half of US revenue is import-exposed — FY2025 incremental cash tariff cost was ~$174M.

The competitive divide maps cleanly onto the segment P&L. Private label and digitally native entry pressure is most acute in commodity-adjacent kitchenware, storage and outdoor gear, which is exactly where Newell loses money. It is least acute in Writing (brand equity in a low-ticket habitual purchase) and Baby (safety certification, trust, switching friction), which is exactly where the 17% margins sit.

Trajectory is negative at the consolidated level. Revenue −23.8% since 2022 with explicit acknowledgment of distribution losses implies share loss on top of category contraction. Customer concentration rose. Two of three segments deteriorated in H1 2026 on a normalized basis.

One dependency deserves more attention than it receives. Both Item 1 and Item 1A state that the Baby business unit within L&D "has a single source of supply for products that comprise a majority of its sales and that owns the intellectual property for many of those products," with warnings that Newell may not be able to quickly replace the supplier and that retrieving tooling and molds may be difficult. Newell is the purchaser; the counterparty is the supplier and IP owner. Baby generated $516M of H1 sales and was the fastest-growing line inside the segment carrying the entire company. The filings name neither the counterparty nor the contract term, so renewal risk cannot be assessed. Sole-source arrangements in juvenile products are often stable given mutual tooling and certification lock-in, but this is a concentrated, disclosed dependency sitting precisely at the point of earnings power, and it constrains long-run margin capture in the one line that is growing.

**Competitive Position: 3.5.** A genuine tariff-footprint advantage and two defensible franchises inside L&D, against declining share, rising customer concentration, retailer power, and two segments in structural decline.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage against thin operating profit.** $5,006M of debt at 6/30/26 against FY2025 operating income of $39M and interest expense of $321M. Standard consequences apply: cash dedicated to debt service, reduced flexibility, cross-default and acceleration provisions.

**Ratings and the exhausted coupon ratchet.** Moody's downgraded senior unsecured debt to B2 in Q4 2025, and the 10-K notes this occurred "without any further impact to the interest rates," because the maximum step-up provision has been reached. Further downgrades no longer raise coupons on existing bonds but would raise the cost of new issuance. The May 2025 refinancing shows the cost already incurred: $1.25B of 8.500% notes due 2028 replacing 4.200% notes, roughly a 430bp coupon increase plus a $13M extinguishment loss.

**Covenant cliff resolved; refinancing deadline substituted.** The July 30 ABL removes the Total Net Leverage step-down at 9/30/26 and replaces it with a fixed-charge coverage test that springs only above specified utilization. Three caveats: committed capacity fell from $1.0B to $800M with the $500M accordion uncommitted; net availability at closing was ~$216M ($754M borrowing base − $490M drawn − $48M letters of credit); and the borrowing base flexes with receivables, inventory, equipment and IP, so liquidity contracts precisely in a downturn. The facility also matures on the earlier of July 30, 2031 and 91 days before the earliest scheduled maturity of $125M+ of material indebtedness — with $494M of 6.375% notes due 2027 outstanding, the effective maturity springs to roughly 91 days before those notes mature unless they are addressed. That is the next hard deadline, plausibly by mid-2027.

**Impairment risk with a circular trigger.** Impairments were $339M (2023), $345M (2024), $340M (2025). Cumulative goodwill written off is $5,180M against $8,266M gross — 62.7%; O&R goodwill is fully impaired, H&CS is 82% impaired with $747M net. The Commercial reporting unit's fair value is within 10% of carrying value, and a 10% EBITDA forecast cut implies ~$131M of impairment. Management's stated cause is worth understanding: fair values fell partly from "an increase in the reporting unit's discount rate, primarily due to the increased risk premium applied to enable reconciliation to the Company's total enterprise value given the decline in the Company's stock price." A lower share price mechanically produces impairments; a higher one reduces them. H&CS normalized operating income *fell* in H1, which is the wrong direction for the Commercial unit's forecast.

**Litigation directly against the Q2 earnings driver.** Two putative class actions (N.D. Ga., filed May 22 and July 6, 2026) allege Newell received windfall profits from price increases taken in response to the invalidated IEEPA tariffs and seek return of the refund to consumers. No reserve is disclosed. Collection of the $285M receivable also depends on a CBP process the 10-Q describes as still under development. Putative class actions are frequently dismissed at the pleading stage and the claims are legally novel — this is a risk to size, not a probable loss, but it sits on top of the item that produced the beat.

**Certified Baby class actions, unreserved.** Two certified class actions (ten states in N.D. Ill., three in N.D. Ga.) allege advertising misrepresentation seeking price-premium and statutory damages. Newell states loss is "reasonably possible," is unable to estimate a range, has recorded no reserve, and acknowledges an adverse outcome could be material. Certification materially raises settlement value, and the exposure attaches to the segment carrying the company.

**Tax contingency stack.** Section 245A/954(c)(6) regulations Newell has concluded were "not validly issued" and whose effects it has not recorded — an adverse outcome would require recognizing $180–220M of income tax expense plus possible interest and penalties. A Tax Court petition disputing $80M of additional taxes and ~$34M of penalties for 2011–2015. A new IRS examination of 2021–2024 federal returns, notified in Q2 2026. $360M of unrecognized tax benefits excluded from the contractual obligations table. Effective tax rates of 45.6% (Q2) and 45.5% (H1) versus a 21% statutory rate compound the leverage problem through cash-tax leakage.

**Environmental.** Newell and Berol are among 100+ PRPs at the Diamond Alkali Superfund Site, where EPA remedies for two operable units total ~$1.80B. Newell is in the lowest tier of relative responsibility and reserves $31M. The Consent Decree entry is on appeal, and 35 companies sued Occidental Chemical's Texas successor in February 2026 alleging valuable assets were moved away from the entity retaining CERCLA liabilities. If the liability-bearing entity is under-resourced, remaining PRP shares could rise.

**Controls history.** The risk factors state Newell "has experienced, and in the future may experience again, material weaknesses." A 2023 SEC settlement over sales practices and accounting matters cost ~$13M. Controls were assessed effective at 6/30/26. The history argues for scrutiny of estimate-driven line items, and the current period contains two large ones: the $25M customer-program true-up in revenue and the $285M tariff receivable.

**Dilution.** Shares issued rose from 447.1M to 457.3M in six months. H1 grants totaled 12.8M units (9.6M time-based, $44M; 3.2M performance-based, $15M) on ~426M shares — roughly 3.0% gross annual dilution. The mechanism matters: dollar-denominated awards settled in shares at a depressed price convert ~$59M of compensation expense into an outsized share count. There is no active repurchase authorization; the 198,503 shares acquired in Q2 at $3.72 were tax-withholding settlements.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 71.9% sits rich to 61.7% one-year historical volatility, in the 87th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (71.9% at one month versus 66.0% at six). That combination prices near-term event risk. ATM IV collapsed from 98.1% to 71.9% across the earnings print while the price rose 7.9%, the standard event-vol crush.

Directional positioning is unambiguously bullish, not bearish: put/call skew is −4.3% (call-heavy), put/call volume ratio 0.05, put/call open-interest ratio 0.27. Against 22.7% short interest and 4.2 days to cover, and a +39% intraday move on July 31 that Benzinga framed as a short squeeze, the cleanest read is speculative call buying and short-covering rather than fundamental accumulation. Two implications. Buying downside protection here is expensive at 87th-percentile IV. And the bullish options tilt is a warning to anyone considering the short side rather than confirmation of the long thesis — it reflects positioning mechanics, not cash-flow evidence.

**Risk Factors: 2.5.** The aggregate identified, largely unreserved exposure — $180–220M Section 245A, $114M transfer pricing (tax plus penalties), unquantified certified Baby classes, IEEPA disgorgement against a $285M receivable, $131M Commercial goodwill sensitivity — is large relative to a $2.2B market cap, sits alongside a 2027 refinancing deadline, and is not visible in trailing earnings.

## Management Quality

The forward-looking record is mixed and worth separating into tactical and strategic.

Tactically, management has executed. Gross margin recovered 490bps from the 2023 trough. The Productivity Plan is proceeding on schedule. Most importantly, the ABL was closed two months before the covenant step-down would have bound, with a five-year commitment from a JPMorgan-led syndicate and an Irish co-borrower structure — competent liability management, and the syndicate's willingness to commit is an independent credit vote. The notes repricing above book over H1 corroborates.

Strategically, capital allocation is poor. The dividend consumed ~$124M in a year that produced $17M of free cash flow, while the company issued 8.500% paper and drew $340M net on the revolver. Deleveraging is named a top operating priority in both the 10-K and 10-Q, yet none of the five DEF 14A performance measures — Adjusted Operating Cash Flow, Adjusted EPS, Adjusted Gross Margin, Core Sales, Free Cash Flow Productivity — is leverage-based, and all five are non-GAAP or company-defined. Debt rose $333M over H1 while those metrics could improve.

Alignment cuts both ways. Cumulative TSR of 22 against a peer group at 130 means holders lost roughly 78% while peers gained ~30%. The "compensation actually paid" column does impose real downside — −$21.8M for the CEO in 2025 and −$7.3M in 2022 — which is genuine alignment. But +$38.2M of CAP in 2024 against a −$216M net loss shows how a single share-price bounce generates large realized compensation without earnings recovery.

Insider activity is the clearest negative. Across Form 4 filings from February to July 2026 there were no open-market purchases by any officer or director. There were four discretionary sales in May 2026 at $3.60–$4.65, with the CHRO going to zero direct holdings and the L&D president going to zero, and the H&C–Home president to 867 shares. Sales clustered in the post-earnings open window after the annual vesting cycle, and diversification after RSU vesting is economically rational for executives concentrated in a $5 stock; we cannot exclude pre-existing 10b5-1 plans. The informative element is the absence of buying: no insider bought at $3.26–$4.65 while management publicly described a turnaround working across all three segments.

**Management Quality: 3.8.** Credit for the refinancing and margin execution; debits for an uncovered dividend funded with 8.5% money, incentive metrics that omit the stated top priority, ~3% annual dilution, and no insider buying at the lows.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to ~$9.00, +75%)

Restructuring savings phase in fully through 2026, lifting adjusted EBITDA toward $900M. L&D compounds at 15%+ operating income growth on Baby and Writing strength. H&CS stabilizes as retailer resets normalize and the US manufacturing footprint converts into share gains against import-exposed competitors under Section 301. The $285M tariff receivable collects, funding revolver paydown. The 2027 notes are refinanced at a coupon near 7% with the ABL springing maturity removed. On $900M of EBITDA at 10x, EV of $9.0B less ~$4.8B net debt gives roughly $9.75 per share. The 22.7% short interest amplifies any positive surprise, as the July 31 print demonstrated. This case is arithmetically reachable, which is why the analyst high target is $9.00.

### Bear Case (to ~$2.50, −51%)

Underlying revenue keeps declining 1–2%, consistent with the FX- and estimate-adjusted H1 figure. Resin and freight inflation from a $100+ crude regime compresses the gross margin gain. H&CS deteriorates further, triggering a Commercial goodwill impairment of ~$131M or more and deepening the retained deficit. Discretionary housewares demand softens further. The IEEPA refund is partially disgorged or fails to collect on schedule; the Section 245A position is not sustained, adding $180–220M of tax expense; one of the certified Baby classes settles materially. The dividend is cut, removing the retail-holder support. Adjusted EBITDA reverts to $700M; at 8.5x, EV of $5.95B less $4.9B of net debt leaves roughly $1.05B of equity, or about $2.44 per share. The analyst low of $3.50 sits between this and spot.

### Base Case (~$5.00–5.50, flat)

Adjusted EBITDA of $800–830M in FY2026 including a portion of restructuring savings and excluding the tariff windfall as a recurring item. Holding the current 9.0x EV multiple gives EV of $7.2–7.5B; net debt of $4.7B after partial refund collection leaves $2.5–2.8B of equity, or $5.80–6.50, against a probability-weighted drag from the contingency stack and the 2027 refinancing overhang. Fair value clusters around the current price and the $5.13 analyst median. There is no expected return to compensate for a distribution with a −51% left tail.

The asymmetry is the reason for the recommendation. A 1x move in the EV multiple swings equity value ~35%; a $100M swing in EBITDA swings it ~4.5%. That leverage sits on a business whose underlying sales are still contracting, whose earnings improvement is confined to one of three segments, and whose non-GAAP EPS converted to $17M of cash last year.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is the fourth consecutive AVOID and the score improves for the first time (3.2 → 3.4), driven entirely by the July 30 covenant resolution. The stock is essentially unchanged from the levels of both prior reviews (~$4.97 in mid-June, ~$5.08 in late July, $5.14 now), so this is not a case of price action having outrun the thesis. What has changed is that one tail risk was removed and two new ones were added (the July 6 IEEPA class action, the 2021–2024 IRS examination), while Q2 confirmed that underlying demand has not inflected.

**Re-entry criteria** — any two of the following would move this to HOLD; three would support a BUY:

1. **Dividend eliminated and redirected to debt reduction**, or total debt below $4.5B (from $5,006M) for two consecutive quarters. The current policy of distributing ~$124M annually while borrowing at 6.8–8.5% is the clearest self-inflicted drag on equity value.
2. **H&CS segment operating income positive and improving year over year on a tariff-refund-adjusted basis for two consecutive quarters**, together with positive core sales excluding FX and excluding one-off estimate refinements. H1 2026 normalized H&CS was $16M versus $22M; the turnaround thesis requires this to reverse.
3. **The $494M 6.375% notes due 2027 refinanced or retired at a coupon of 7% or below**, removing the ABL springing-maturity dependency. Success here would also validate improved capital-markets access.
4. **The certified Baby class actions resolved or reserved at a quantified, non-material amount**, and the Section 245A position either sustained or provided for.

**Price-based re-entry:** below approximately $3.50 (the analyst low) with criteria 2 and 3 trending favorably, the levered EBITDA math would create genuine asymmetry — at $3.50, a return to $800M EBITDA and a 9.5x multiple implies roughly 90% upside. We would want that cushion before owning this capital structure.

**Timeline and checkpoints:** 12–18 months. Q3 2026 earnings (late October) is the first clean test — the prior-period tariff refund does not repeat, and H&CS/O&R performance will be visible without it, against a Q3 sales guide management set above consensus. The Q4 annual impairment test (reported February 2027) resolves the Commercial reporting unit's $747M of goodwill. The 2027 note refinancing window opens in H1 2027.

**Downgrade triggers that would take this from AVOID to an active short candidate:** a dividend cut accompanied by a guidance reduction, ABL net availability falling below $150M, or an adverse ruling on the Section 245A position.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The strongest dated catalyst is Q3 2026 earnings in late October, where the ~$100M prior-period tariff credit does not recur and the year-over-year comparison strips out the H1 order pull-forward. A miss versus the raised guidance, or visible H&CS deterioration, is plausible. Secondary catalysts: the Q4 annual impairment test reported in February 2027 (Commercial unit, ~$131M sensitivity), and a dividend review. None is a hard-dated binary — the covenant deadline that would have been the sharp catalyst was removed on July 30.

**Timing window.** Three months to the first catalyst, six to seven months to the second. That is a long carry for a position where the primary thesis is gradual erosion rather than an event.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 22.7% of float short, 60.6M shares, 4.2 days to cover. The stock rose 39% intraday on July 31 before giving most of it back, which Benzinga attributed to a squeeze. Short interest above 20% with demonstrated squeeze behavior in the last week is disqualifying on its own.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning contradicts the bearish thesis. Skew is call-heavy at −4.3%, put/call volume ratio 0.05, put/call open interest 0.27. Near-term IV of 71.9% is rich to 61.7% historical volatility and sits at the 87th percentile of the chain, so expressing the view through puts costs 15.2% of spot in implied move for a one-month expiry. There is no options confirmation and the protection is expensive.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $2.50–3.50, or 32–51% below spot. Upside risk: a squeeze retest of the $7.13 52-week high (+39%) on any clean Q3, plus tail risk from a divestiture of H&CS or O&R that the 10-K's "review of non-core brands" language keeps live. Reward:risk approximates 1.2–1.3:1 on the bear midpoint against the squeeze scenario — well below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish fundamental case is sound but the mechanics are hostile: 22.7% short interest, a squeeze demonstrated within the last week, call-heavy options positioning, expensive protection at 87th-percentile IV, and no hard-dated catalyst now that the covenant cliff has been refinanced away. Avoid owning; do not short.

## Conclusion

Newell's July 31 report contained one genuine improvement and one accounting event. The improvement — 70bps of half-year gross margin expansion and 13% normalized operating income growth, concentrated entirely in Learning & Development — is real and reflects competent supply-chain and restructuring work. The event — a ~$138M pretax IEEPA tariff recovery, of which ~$100M relates to 2025 — accounts for roughly two-thirds of the headline gross profit gain and is itself the target of two putative disgorgement class actions with no reserve. Normalized for it, Home & Commercial Solutions and Outdoor & Recreation both moved backwards, and the segment holding $747M of at-risk goodwill is the one that deteriorated.

The July 30 ABL refinancing deserves credit. It removed the September 30, 2026 leverage-covenant step-down that was the sharpest solvency trigger in the annual report, replaced it with a springing coverage test, and secured a five-year commitment from a JPMorgan-led syndicate. Credit markets have re-rated the notes above carrying value. That reduces the probability of a disorderly outcome. It does not create equity value: committed capacity fell 20%, availability is now pro-cyclical with a borrowing base, and the covenant deadline has been swapped for a refinancing deadline tied to $494M of 2027 notes.

What remains unchanged is the arithmetic that governs this equity. Roughly 5.7x net leverage on a constructed $780M of adjusted EBITDA. Interest expense of $321M against FY2025 operating income of $39M. Free cash flow of $17M against a ~$124M dividend funded from the balance sheet while the company borrows at 6.8–8.5%. Negative tangible book value of $2.2B after writing off 62.7% of all goodwill ever recorded. A 45.6% effective tax rate. Underlying sales down roughly 1.3% in H1 once $62M of FX and a $25M estimate refinement are removed. And an unreserved contingency stack — $180–220M Section 245A, $114M transfer pricing (tax plus penalties), certified and unquantified Baby class actions, IEEPA disgorgement claims — that is material against a $2.2B market capitalization.

At 8x normalized EPS the shares look cheap; at 9x EV/EBITDA with declining revenue they do not, and the FY2025 gap between normalized earnings and $17M of cash is why the first number is not the relevant one. The distribution around the current price is roughly symmetric in magnitude and unfavorable in probability, with a −51% left tail that the leverage makes reachable on modest EBITDA disappointment. Management has bought time; it has not yet demonstrated that the business can grow, cover its interest bill from operations, and fund its distribution. We maintain **AVOID** with an overall score of **3.4**, and we would not short at 22.7% short interest with call-heavy options positioning and no hard-dated catalyst.
