**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Everus Construction Group is an 18-month-old standalone public company, spun out of MDU Resources on October 31, 2024, that has become one of the cleanest mid-cap proxies for the AI/hyperscaler data-center construction wave. Roughly 78-80% of revenue comes from its Electrical & Mechanical (E&M) segment, where commercial work (overwhelmingly data centers) drove a $876.8M FY2025 revenue increase. Reported numbers are strong and accelerating: FY2025 revenue rose 31.5% to $3.75B, net income rose 40.7% to $201.8M, diluted EPS reached $3.95, and the company has beaten consensus by 39-78% in each of its four reported quarters as a standalone. Q1 2026 continued the pattern (revenue +25.4%, net income +58.9%, EPS $1.14), and consensus EPS estimates have been revised up 11.5% on the current year and 10.9% on next year over the past 90 days.

The complication is that the same force powering the growth is the dominant risk. A single unnamed customer (presumably a hyperscaler or data-center developer) has grown from under 10% of revenue in 2024 to 17% in FY2025 to 19.1% of total revenue, 23.6% of E&M segment revenue, and roughly 19.7% of trade receivables in Q1 2026. That concentration has powered the growth and embeds binary dependence on one counterparty's capex program. Two secondary flags reinforce caution: total backlog has drifted lower ($3.06B at 3/31/25 to $2.93B at 3/31/26) even as revenue hits records, and roughly 42% of FY2025 EPS ($1.64 of $3.95) came from favorable percentage-of-completion estimate revisions, an industry-normal but elevated and reversible earnings input.

At $156, ECG trades at roughly 39x trailing and ~31-34x forward earnings, well above the construction-engineering peer median P/E of 22.8x. The data-center demand backdrop is real and broad-based (Quanta's record $48.5B backlog, IES's "particularly strong" data-center commentary, and Limbach's ~27% data-center bookings all corroborate it), but the multiple leaves little room for a capex pause or a problem with the key customer. We rate ECG a HOLD with a growth bias. The growth runway and execution track record justify owning the franchise; the concentration risk and premium valuation prevent a BUY at the current price.

## Company Overview

Everus is a U.S. specialty contractor headquartered in Bismarck, North Dakota, operating 16 wholly owned operating companies across two segments:

- **Electrical & Mechanical (E&M)** — ~78-80% of revenue. Electrical and communication wiring and infrastructure, fire suppression, renewables, and mechanical piping. This is the growth engine, driven by data center construction, hospitality, and high-tech work.
- **Transmission & Distribution (T&D)** — ~20-22% of revenue. Overhead/underground electrical, gas, and communications infrastructure, transportation lighting, plus the Wagner Smith Equipment unit that manufactures and distributes transmission-line tools.

The company is a contractor building facilities for data-center owners and hyperscalers. It does not own data centers or any AI technology; it is a picks-and-shovels participant whose fortunes track its customers' construction budgets rather than the underlying compute economics. The workforce is approximately 85% unionized, and the company differentiates on execution track record, safety, and national reach for large data-center customers. Peers span a wide size range: Quanta Services (~$35B revenue), MasTec, Primoris, MYR Group, IES Holdings, and Limbach.

The spinoff structure matters for interpretation. Pre-spin financials are carve-out basis and not fully representative of a standalone entity; the company has only ~18 months of independent public history, and system migration from MDU was only "substantially complete" as of the 10-K. Everus is a post-spinoff special situation positioned in front of a structural demand wave, not a mature standalone industrial with a long track record.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials show accelerating growth with modest margin expansion:

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2.85B | $3.75B (+31.5%) | $826.6M | $1,037M (+25.4%) |
| Gross margin | 11.9% | 12.1% | 11.2% | 12.6% |
| Operating margin | 6.7% | 7.1% | 6.2% | 7.5% |
| Net income | $143.4M | $201.8M (+40.7%) | $36.7M | $58.3M (+58.9%) |
| Diluted EPS | $2.81 | $3.95 | $0.72 | $1.14 |
| EBITDA | $232.2M | $319.8M | $61.8M | $88.9M |
| EBITDA margin | 8.1% | 8.5% | 7.5% | 8.6% |

E&M is the driver. FY2025 E&M revenue rose 43.8% to $2.92B with segment operating income up 59.3% and margin up to 7.5%. Q1 2026 commercial revenue rose $235.5M, again data-center-led, with E&M operating margin reaching 8.5% and T&D 9.9%.

The balance sheet is healthy and underlevered. Altman Z-Score is 3.92 (safe zone), the current ratio is 1.76, ROE is 32%, and ROA is 11.7%. Total debt is modest (a $281M term loan at 5.70% and an undrawn $225M revolver) with net leverage comfortably under the 3.0x covenant. Cash grew to $293M at Q1 2026, and the $158M SE&M acquisition (closed April 1, 2026) was funded entirely from cash on hand.

Two earnings-quality items deserve scrutiny. First, FY2025 free cash flow was only ~$90-100M (FCF margin ~2.4%), down from $128.8M in FY2024 despite net income rising 41%. The gap is working-capital intensity: contract assets and receivables consumed $79.9M as the business scaled. This is a growth-driven phenomenon rather than deterioration, but at roughly 2x revenue and ~39x earnings, GAAP profit is running ahead of cash. Q1 2026 operating cash flow surged to $143.7M (versus $7.1M in Q1'25) on favorable contract-liability timing, but quarterly construction cash flows are lumpy and should not be annualized.

Second, a large share of profit comes from favorable changes in estimates under the cost-to-cost (percentage-of-completion) method. In FY2025, favorable estimate changes added $113.7M to gross profit and $1.64 of diluted EPS, roughly 42% of the $3.95 total. In Q1 2026, estimate changes added $0.81 of the $1.14 EPS. This is normal for the industry, but it means reported earnings are estimate-dependent, and unfavorable revisions on large fixed-price jobs (driven by tariffs on copper, aluminum, steel, or labor inefficiency) would compress margins on locked-in prices.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is strong and confirmed across multiple indicators. The earnings beat track record is exceptional: four consecutive beats of 41.1%, 39.2%, 77.8%, and 72.8%. Consensus estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-year EPS has been revised up 11.5% over 90 days (to $4.62) and next-year up 10.9% (to $5.11). The current quarter and next quarter are also revised higher. This combination of accelerating revenue and rising estimates is among the more reliable forward signals for continued momentum.

The magnitude of the estimate revision (+11.5% on the year) is solidly positive but below the +30% threshold that typically marks a violent re-rating event, and the revenue growth, while strong at 25-31%, has decelerated from the 31.5% FY pace to 25.4% in Q1. The growth is real and durable, but moderating rather than re-accelerating.

The demand backdrop is broad-based, which raises the durability of the thesis above what a single-company story would warrant. Quanta hit a record $48.5B backlog and raised guidance; IES cited "particularly strong" data-center demand; Limbach reported data centers at ~27% of bookings. Three or more peers showing simultaneous data-center strength confirms a structural demand shift rather than an Everus-specific anecdote.

The SE&M acquisition adds a growth and diversification lever. SE&M is a North Carolina mechanical/electrical/plumbing contractor founded in 1923 serving pharma, complex industrial, and healthcare, generating ~$109M revenue in 2025 at high-teens EBITDA margins, accretive to ECG's ~8.5% EBITDA margin. It expands ECG into the Southeast, adds 200+ craft workers, and diversifies end markets toward recurring maintenance and retrofit revenue. It also demonstrates the company can execute cash-funded M&A.

The one growth caution is backlog. Total backlog has trended lower ($3.06B at 3/31/25 → $3.23B at 12/31/25 → $2.93B at 3/31/26), with E&M backlog down to $2.64B from $2.84B a year earlier. With most contracts running under 12 months, backlog is a near-term rather than multi-year indicator, and management cites award timing and a strong pipeline. But declining backlog amid record revenue means work is being burned faster than replaced, which sits at odds with the growth narrative and bears watching.

## Valuation Assessment

At $156, ECG carries a trailing P/E of ~39x. On the rising consensus, the forward multiple is roughly 34x on the current-year $4.62 estimate and ~31x on next year's $5.11. EV/EBITDA is approximately 25x trailing. Against the construction-engineering peer median P/E of 22.8x, ECG trades at a clear premium.

The peer set is heterogeneous, which matters. Tetra Tech sits at 16.6x, ACM at 14.5x, FLR at 22.8x, ACA at 28.4x, and MYR Group at 46.8x. The dispersion reflects different end-market exposures and growth rates. ECG's data-center-led growth (25-44% segment growth) and margin expansion justify a premium to the slower-growing infrastructure consultancies, but the question is whether ~31-34x forward is the right premium. On a growth-adjusted basis, applying next-year EPS of $5.11 against a ~15% forward earnings CAGR yields a PEG above 2.0 — not cheap, even crediting the growth.

This is where I diverge from treating analyst targets as floors. ECG sits at a premium multiple (~31x+ forward), so the principle that consensus targets lag inflection stocks applies with less force here: at premium valuations, targets and price tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation. The analyst range is $157 low / $185 high, with a $169.6 mean and $172 median. Oppenheimer raised its target to $180 from $120 post-Q1, and Guggenheim moved to Buy at $160. The upgrades are real, but the implied upside to the mean (~9%) is modest given the downside if the data-center capex cycle or the key customer disappoints.

The earnings-quality overlay also tempers the valuation case. With ~42% of FY2025 EPS from favorable estimate revisions, the "clean" run-rate earnings power is lower than the headline, and paying 31-34x forward on estimate-inflated earnings carries asymmetric downside in any quarter where revisions turn unfavorable. I score valuation at 5.0: neither a value trap nor a clear bargain, but a full price that prices in continued momentum with little cushion. The valuation has improved from the 3.5 of the prior trajectory as the forward multiple compressed on rising estimates, but it does not yet offer the asymmetric upside that would support a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

Everus is a credible mid-tier national player in a highly fragmented industry, but it is far smaller than Quanta (~$35B revenue versus ECG's $3.75B). Its competitive edge is being the electrical and mechanical contractor inside data centers, with differentiation on execution, safety record, a largely unionized workforce, and national reach for large data-center customers. The Q1 2026 print, which beat the same week Primoris missed and sold off, underscores relative execution strength.

The competitive trajectory is the relevant question, and it is mixed. ECG is winning share of a structurally growing pie and deepening a relationship with a major hyperscaler customer. At the same time, that customer concentration, higher and more acute than at diversified peers like Quanta, makes ECG more exposed to a single counterparty's capex cycle than its larger competitors. The declining backlog also suggests ECG is not replacing completed work at the pace required to sustain the current growth rate, which is a competitive caution against the broader sector tailwind. The prior trajectory marked competitive position down from 7.5 to 6.5; the rising concentration and softening backlog justify holding it slightly below that at 6.3. ECG is a strong operator gaining share within a favorable theme, but its narrower customer base and smaller scale cap the durability of its position relative to the diversified leaders.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is single-customer concentration, and it is accelerating. Everus added a dedicated risk factor flagging the uncertain "longevity" of data-center work. A pullback in hyperscaler capex or the loss of this single customer would hit revenue, margins, and receivable collectibility simultaneously. This is genuinely binary and is the swing factor in both directions.

Secondary risks compound the picture. Fixed-price contract and estimate risk means margins hinge on accurate cost-to-cost estimation amid tariff and labor inflation. Backlog softening sits at odds with the record-revenue narrative. Spinoff-related constraints include a tax-matters agreement restricting M&A and equity issuance until ~October 2026 (with uncapped indemnification to MDU if ECG triggers Section 355(e) tax), limited standalone history, and recently completed system migration. The company depends on ~$2.1B of surety bonds (max exposure ~$693M), where lost bonding capacity would impair its ability to bid. There is also an old contract dispute: a customer has withheld ~$31.3M since October 2023 on a time-and-materials project (revenue recognized, collection deemed probable, now litigated), plus growing claim positions ($38.3M at Q1'26, up from $25.8M).

The auditor change (8-K Item 4.01 in January 2026 plus an 8-K/A in March 2026) is most likely a routine post-spin transition off MDU's legacy auditor, with no evidence of a disagreement found, though the underlying 8-K text could not be fully retrieved and should be confirmed. The 10-Q reported no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K.

Given the acute and intensifying customer/receivables concentration that the market is only partially pricing, alongside the estimate-dependent earnings and backlog softening, I score risk factors at 4.5. These are specific, identifiable, and capable of moving the stock.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm to mildly complacent. Near-term ATM IV is 53.5% against 56.5% one-year historical vol, a "cheap" -3.0% spread, with the IV sitting in only the 7th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (0.4%), and the put/call volume ratio is 0.81. The term structure is in contango (53.5% near-term versus 61.2% long-term), a normal/complacent configuration. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV fell 9.6pp (63.0% → 53.5%) while price rose 11.5%, which reflects resolved post-earnings uncertainty rather than a buildup of hedging demand. Short interest is low at 4.5% of float with 2.5 days to cover, so the bearish view is uncrowded and there is no squeeze setup. The one notable feature is the 3-month tenor showing a -5.2% put skew and a 5.0 put/call volume ratio, suggesting some positioning for downside protection into the August window (around the next earnings print). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.9%, consistent with a stock that has historically gapped on earnings. The options market is not flagging imminent stress, which neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental caution; it means the concentration risk is not yet being priced as a near-term catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($185-200+).** The AI data-center buildout is a multi-year structural demand wave, corroborated by Quanta, IES, and Limbach. ECG is a clean picks-and-shovels beneficiary with an exceptional beat record (39-78% surprises for four straight quarters) and rising estimates. The single-customer relationship deepens as a hyperscaler scales capex, the SE&M acquisition diversifies geography and end markets while adding margin, and management proves it can compound through cash-funded M&A. On next-year EPS of $5.11 at a sustained ~36x multiple, the stock reaches ~$184; with further estimate upside and continued multiple support, $200 is achievable. Oppenheimer's $180 target sits in this zone.

**Bear Case ($95-110).** Hyperscaler capex pauses or the key customer reduces its program, exposing the binary concentration. Revenue decelerates, receivables (~20% concentrated in that one customer) come under collection pressure, and unfavorable percentage-of-completion estimate revisions reverse the ~42% of EPS that came from favorable changes. The declining backlog proves to be a leading indicator rather than award timing. The premium multiple compresses toward the peer median of ~22x. On normalized "clean" earnings power closer to $3.00-3.50 (stripping estimate-revision benefit) at ~30x, the stock falls toward $95-110, roughly 30-40% downside. The director's December open-market purchase at $92.18 indicates where fundamental value support was perceived not long ago.

The risk/reward at $156 is roughly symmetric: ~18-28% upside to the bull case versus ~30-40% downside to the bear. That balance, combined with a real but moderating growth thesis, supports holding the franchise without adding aggressively at current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale, owned for the data-center runway and execution quality, not for yield (there is no dividend).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$110s to low-$120s (~25x forward on $5.11), which would restore asymmetric upside while the growth thesis remains intact.
- Backlog stabilizing or re-accelerating (E&M backlog returning above $2.84B) alongside customer-base broadening that reduces the single-customer share below ~15%.
- An estimate-revision acceleration to +30%+ over 90 days combined with sustained 30%+ revenue growth, which would mark a re-inflection rather than the current moderation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The single customer's revenue share rising further (toward 25%+) without offsetting diversification, or any disclosure of program reduction by that customer.
- Unfavorable percentage-of-completion estimate revisions in any quarter, signaling reversal of the ~42% estimate-driven EPS.
- Backlog falling below ~$2.7B on continued revenue burn, confirming work is not being replaced.
- A guidance cut or earnings miss after the four-quarter beat streak, which would puncture the premium multiple.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early-to-mid August), the first reporting quarter to include a contribution from SE&M (acquisition closed April 1, 2026, i.e., the start of Q2), and which will test the backlog trajectory, customer concentration, and whether the estimate-revision tailwind continues. The expiry of the tax-matters M&A restriction (~October 2026) is a second checkpoint that could unlock larger stock-funded deals.

## Conclusion

Everus is a well-run mid-cap contractor with a genuine front-row seat to the data-center construction wave, an exceptional early beat record, healthy and underlevered finances, and rising consensus estimates. Those qualities justify owning the franchise. What prevents a BUY at $156 is the combination of an accelerating single-customer concentration that is now ~19% of revenue and ~20% of receivables, a backlog trend that contradicts the record-revenue narrative, earnings that are ~42% dependent on reversible percentage-of-completion estimates, and a ~31-34x forward multiple that prices in continued momentum with little margin for error. The growth is real but moderating, and the valuation no longer offers asymmetric upside. We rate ECG a HOLD (growth hold), with a clear preference to add on a pullback toward the mid-$110s to low-$120s or on evidence that the customer base is broadening. Overall score: 6.15/10.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: trailing P/E corrected from ~36x to ~39x (EPS $3.95 / $156 = 39.5x; the Phase 1 trailing P/E of 36.1x reflects the $157.71 feed price), making the stock modestly more expensive than the report stated, which, if anything, reinforces rather than undermines the HOLD.
