## Executive Summary

Gilead Sciences trades at $137.23, roughly 20.3x trailing and forward earnings, after pulling back 12% from its February 2026 high of $157.29. The stock is up 32% over the past year, driven by Yeztugo (lenacapavir) FDA approval for twice-yearly HIV PrEP, a Biktarvy patent settlement extending U.S. exclusivity to April 2036, and FY2025 results showing $9.5B in free cash flow on 34% operating margins. The underlying HIV franchise — 72% of product sales — is performing well, with Biktarvy growing 7% to $14.3B and Descovy surging 31%.

The pullback from February highs reflects accelerating gross-to-net pricing headwinds (41% of gross sales, up from 38%), Biktarvy's selection for Medicare price negotiation effective 2028, negative near-term estimate revisions (next quarter estimates down 12% over 90 days), and the overhang of $14.8B in pending acquisitions (Arcellx, Tubulis, Ouro Medicines) that require integration. Oncology pipeline setbacks — ASCENT-07 and EVOKE-01 failures, resulting in $8.6B in cumulative impairments — have forced a strategic reboot in that segment.

At the current valuation, Gilead offers a compelling risk-reward profile for a defensive healthcare compounder: a 2.4% dividend yield funded by consistent FCF, a dominant competitive moat in HIV that extends to at least 2036, and Yeztugo as a credible new growth vector with $800M guided for 2026 and analyst peak estimates of $6.4B. The key debate is whether pricing headwinds from IRA Medicare negotiation and MFN provisions offset volume growth and pipeline commercialization. We believe the market is pricing the negatives but not fully crediting the Biktarvy extension or Yeztugo's long-term trajectory.

## Company Overview

Gilead Sciences discovers, develops, and commercializes medicines primarily in HIV, viral hepatitis, oncology, and inflammation, operating in 35+ countries from its Foster City, California headquarters. The company holds approximately $170B in market capitalization.

**Revenue Mix (FY2025, $29.4B total):**
- HIV: $20.8B (72%) — Biktarvy $14.3B, Descovy $2.8B, Genvoya $1.5B, Odefsey $1.2B, Yeztugo $150M
- Liver Disease: $3.2B (11%) — sofosbuvir/velpatasvir, Vemlidy, Livdelzi
- Oncology: $3.2B (11%) — Yescarta/Tecartus (CAR-T), Trodelvy (ADC)
- Veklury (COVID-19): $911M (3%, declining rapidly)
- Other: $799M

Gilead completed its transformation from an HCV-centric business (peak Sovaldi/Harvoni era) to an HIV-anchored cash flow compounder. The current strategic pivot involves building oncology and inflammation platforms through M&A ($14.8B deployed in Q1 2026 across three deals) and launching Yeztugo as a differentiated HIV prevention franchise.

FY2024's depressed earnings ($0.38 EPS) stemmed from two non-recurring charges: a $3.8B CymaBay acquisition IPR&D write-off and a $4.2B Trodelvy NSCLC impairment. FY2025 reflects the company's true underlying profitability: 34% operating margin, 28.9% net margin, and 32.1% FCF margin. Revenue growth was modest at 2% YoY, but this understates the strength of the HIV franchise; excluding Veklury's 49% decline, core product sales grew mid-single digits.

FCF of $9.5B ($10.0B operating cash flow less ~$500M capex) comfortably covers capital returns of $5.9B ($4.0B dividends + $1.9B buybacks), R&D of $5.8B, and $1.8B in debt repayment.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt stands at $24.9B ($2.8B short-term, $22.1B long-term), producing debt-to-equity of 2.6x. This leverage is the primary drag on the Altman Z-Score (1.96, grey zone). However, net debt of approximately $14.3B against $9.5B FCF implies a net leverage ratio of ~1.5x, which is manageable for a large-cap biopharma. Cash and marketable securities total $10.6B, providing adequate liquidity with a current ratio of 1.55.

ROE of 37.5% and ROA of 14.4% reflect efficient capital deployment, though the ROE is amplified by the elevated leverage. The Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 on available signals indicates improving financial quality.

### Capital Allocation Priorities

Management allocates approximately $17B annually: $5.8B R&D, $5.9B shareholder returns, $1.8B debt service, and remaining flexibility for acquisitions. The $14.8B M&A commitment in Q1 2026 (Arcellx, Tubulis, Ouro) temporarily stretches the balance sheet, but the company's cash generation capacity can absorb this within 2-3 years.

### Gross-to-Net Pressure

Gross-to-net deductions reached 41% of gross product sales in FY2025, up from 38% in FY2024. This reflects the Medicare Part D redesign impact and is structurally likely to increase further as Biktarvy enters Medicare price negotiation (effective 2028), MFN Medicaid pricing takes hold, and 340B ceiling prices compress. Every 100 basis points of additional gross-to-net erosion translates to approximately $300M in lost net revenue, making this the single largest quantitative headwind to growth.

## Growth Analysis

### Yeztugo — The Primary Growth Driver

Yeztugo (lenacapavir), the first twice-yearly injectable HIV PrEP, generated $150M in partial-year 2025 sales following its June FDA approval. Management guided $800M for 2026, implying roughly 5x ramp. Payer access is expected to reach 90% by mid-2026 (up from 75% at year-end 2025). Analyst peak estimates (Needham) project $6.4B by 2035. The PEPFAR partnership targeting 3 million people in developing nations represents a large incremental volume opportunity on a longer timeline.

Yeztugo's growth trajectory depends on: (1) payer willingness to cover a premium-priced injectable vs. daily oral generics, (2) patient/provider adoption rates for a novel dosing paradigm, and (3) global regulatory approvals and access programs. The $800M 2026 guidance is credible given the launch curve but represents only 2.7% of expected total revenue — material at the margin but not yet transformative.

### BIC/LEN Combination

A Phase 3 readout showed positive results for daily oral bictegravir/lenacapavir as an HIV treatment combination, which could serve as both a Biktarvy successor and an extension of the lenacapavir platform. This de-risks the post-2036 patent cliff by potentially creating a follow-on franchise.

### Oncology: Strategic Reset

The three Q1 2026 acquisitions represent a deliberate rebuild after cumulative impairments of ~$8.6B:
- **Arcellx ($7.8B):** Anito-cel, a next-gen BCMA CAR-T for multiple myeloma, positioned against Legend Biotech/J&J's Carvykti
- **Tubulis ($3.15B):** Novel ADC platform with differentiated linker technology; lead asset TUB-040 in ovarian cancer
- **Ouro Medicines ($1.67B):** Bispecific T-cell engager for autoimmune diseases

These are early-to-mid-stage assets. Revenue contributions are 2028+ events at the earliest. Whether $14.8B produces better returns than the original Immunomedics/Trodelvy investment remains the central question for the oncology segment.

### Estimate Revisions — Near-Term Negative

Current quarter EPS estimates have fallen 6.2% over 90 days. Next quarter estimates are down 12.2% over 90 days and 8.2% over 30 days, indicating accelerating downward revisions. Full-year FY2026 estimates have drifted lower by 2.5% over 90 days to $8.55. However, FY2027 estimates have increased 3.2% to $9.62, suggesting analysts see the near-term headwinds as transitory and the growth trajectory recovering.

The divergence between near-term estimate declines and out-year estimate increases is consistent with a company absorbing pricing headwinds while building toward pipeline-driven growth. If the 12.2% decline in next quarter estimates accelerates further, it would signal more fundamental earnings deterioration rather than transitory pressure.

## Valuation Assessment

GILD trades at 20.3x trailing and forward earnings on $6.78 EPS, with consensus at $8.55 for FY2026 and $9.62 for FY2027. On FY2026 estimates, the stock trades at 16.1x; on FY2027, approximately 14.3x.

Analyst consensus targets a median of $160.50, with a range of $123 to $180. The mean target of $158.36 implies 15% upside from current levels. The distribution is skewed bullish: 22 of 30 analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, with zero Sells.

Relative to large-cap biopharma peers (AbbVie at ~15x, Amgen at ~16x, Merck at ~14x), Gilead's 16x forward multiple is in line on FY2026 estimates. The premium to some peers is justified by the higher growth potential from Yeztugo and the Biktarvy exclusivity extension. At 14.3x FY2027 estimates, the stock offers value if Yeztugo delivers on its guided trajectory and the HIV franchise sustains mid-single-digit growth.

The 2.4% dividend yield provides downside support. FCF yield on FY2025 numbers is approximately 5.6% ($9.5B / $170B market cap), attractive for a defensive compounder.

The stock would need to trade at approximately 19x FY2027 EPS of $9.62 to reach the analyst median target of $160.50 — a reasonable multiple for a franchise with this durability and growth profile. At 15x FY2027 (the bear case), the stock would be worth ~$144, roughly in line with current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

**HIV (72% of revenue):** Gilead's competitive moat is among the deepest in biopharma. Biktarvy controls the single-tablet HIV treatment market through demonstrated clinical superiority and physician preference. The patent settlement extending exclusivity to April 2036 removes the largest threat to the franchise for a decade. In prevention, Yeztugo's twice-yearly dosing creates meaningful differentiation versus ViiV Healthcare's monthly cabotegravir (Apretude). The combination of treatment dominance and prevention innovation creates a dual-moat position that is difficult for competitors to challenge simultaneously.

**Oncology (11% of revenue):** Competitive position is weaker. Yescarta faces headwinds from bispecific antibodies (J&J's Tecvayli, Pfizer's Elrexfio) that offer comparable efficacy without CAR-T manufacturing complexity. Trodelvy's addressable market has narrowed after trial failures. The Arcellx/Tubulis acquisitions represent a strategic attempt to rebuild, but these are unproven assets entering competitive markets.

**Liver Disease (11% of revenue):** Stable franchise with limited growth. Vemlidy and sofosbuvir/velpatasvir face generic erosion over time. Livdelzi (seladelpar) addresses PBC, a small market.

## Risk Assessment

### Regulatory and Pricing Risk (High)
Biktarvy's Medicare price negotiation (effective 2028) is the dominant near-term risk. Even a 15-20% Medicare price reduction on $14.3B in annual sales would translate to hundreds of millions in lost revenue, with cascade effects through 340B and Medicaid. The MFN deal and potential 100% tariffs on imported branded pharmaceuticals add further uncertainty.

### Franchise Concentration (Moderate-High)
72% of revenue from HIV — and 49% from Biktarvy alone — creates concentrated single-product risk. While the patent extension to 2036 addresses the timeline, any unexpected clinical or competitive development affecting Biktarvy would have outsized financial impact.

### Pipeline Execution Risk (Moderate-High)
The FDA clinical hold on GS-1720 and GS-4182 limits HIV pipeline diversity. The Q1 2026 acquisitions are early-stage and unproven. Integration of three simultaneous deals poses operational risk.

### Acquisition Integration (Moderate)
Arcellx, Tubulis, and Ouro closed or are closing in H1 2026. Management explicitly stated they will now "take some time to focus on integration." Integration distraction compounding with a challenging pricing environment is the primary operational risk.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV at 34.9% versus 28.1% historical volatility indicates the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty, with a 6.8pp rich premium. The term structure is in backwardation (34.9% near-term vs. 29.3% at 3-6 months), consistent with an imminent stress catalyst — likely the upcoming quarterly earnings or potential policy/pricing announcements. The put/call OI ratio has nearly doubled from 0.41 to 0.79 over the past week, while price declined only 1.3%, suggesting institutional participants are actively adding downside protection. The 1-month tenor shows a 6.0x put/call volume ratio, indicating concentrated hedging activity in the May expiration window. This divergence between moderate price decline and sharp options repositioning warrants caution on entry timing, though it does not override the fundamental thesis.

- Biktarvy continues 5-7% growth with exclusivity secured through 2036

## Conclusion

Gilead Sciences at $137.23 represents a defensive BUY in a healthcare franchise with a dominant HIV competitive position extended through 2036, $9.5B in annual free cash flow, and Yeztugo as a credible new growth vector. The declining score trajectory (from 7.1 to the current 6.62) reflects legitimate risks: accelerating gross-to-net headwinds, oncology pipeline rebuilding, and simultaneous integration of three acquisitions.

At 16.1x FY2026 and 14.3x FY2027 consensus estimates, with a 2.4% dividend yield and 5.6% FCF yield, the valuation provides a reasonable margin of safety for a franchise of this quality. The Biktarvy patent extension to 2036 — which adds an estimated $30-40B+ in cumulative revenue protection — is not fully reflected in a stock trading at peer-average multiples. The options market's near-term stress pricing (backwardation, elevated put activity) suggests additional short-term volatility is likely, which creates a better entry environment rather than invalidating the fundamental case.

The key monitoring variables are Yeztugo's commercial ramp relative to $800M guidance and the trajectory of gross-to-net deductions. If pricing headwinds prove more severe than modeled, the growth thesis weakens materially. If Yeztugo delivers and the franchise sustains mid-single-digit growth, the stock should re-rate toward $160.
