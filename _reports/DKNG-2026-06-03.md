**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

DraftKings has completed a genuine profitability turn. The company reached its first full-year GAAP profit in FY2025 after burning roughly $4.2B cumulatively across 2021–2024, grew revenue to $6.1B (a ~47% five-year CAGR), and in Q1 2026 posted net income of $21.1M against a year-ago loss, Adjusted EBITDA of $167.9M (+64% YoY), and a Sportsbook net revenue margin that expanded to 7.8% from 6.4%. Management maintained FY2026 guidance of $6.5–6.9B revenue and $700–900M Adjusted EBITDA, doubled its buyback authorization to $2.0B, and has repurchased $718M of stock since inception. On the operating story alone, this is a business that has crossed from cash incineration to cash generation.

Almost none of the recent profit improvement came from growing the customer base. Revenue advanced on monetization per user (ARPMUP +21%) while monthly unique payers fell 3.6% YoY to ~4.2M, missing consensus of ~4.63M. Sportsbook hold expansion is partly structural (parlay mix, product design) and partly the product of favorable sports outcomes that mean-revert. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every horizon — current quarter -22.9% over 90 days, current year -13.5%, next year -9.4% — the opposite of the rising-estimate signal that would support a constructive view. There is also an unresolved structural threat: CFTC-regulated prediction-market exchanges (Kalshi, Polymarket) that bypass state licensing and gaming taxes, with industry volume surging from under $5B/month in mid-2025 to ~$24B by April 2026.

The stock has fallen ~25% over the past year and is down ~48% from its summer-2025 high of $48.78. At ~$25.30 the question is whether the market is mispricing a durable margin-and-prediction-markets upside or correctly discounting decelerating user growth, falling estimates, a building litigation campaign, and an existential regulatory binary. Neither side of that debate is resolvable from current data, which is why this is a hold rather than a directional call. The thesis hinges on two unresolved swing factors, hold-rate durability and the prediction-market regulatory outcome, both of which are genuinely two-sided.

## Company Overview

DraftKings operates a single consolidated reportable segment spanning six product verticals: online and retail Sportsbook, online casino (iGaming), daily fantasy sports, digital lottery courier, the newly launched Prediction Markets, and other offerings. It is one of two dominant US online sports-betting operators alongside FanDuel (Flutter), forming an effective duopoly in regulated sports betting.

As of Q1 2026, Sportsbook is live in ~29 of 33 jurisdictions with legal online sports betting, and iGaming operates in six US states plus Ontario. The Q1 revenue mix was Sportsbook $1.095B (66%), iGaming $461M (28%), and Other $90M (6%). The company's competitive advantages rest on proprietary technology acquired through SBTech that enables low incremental cost to enter new states, a leading consumer brand, in-house risk management and trading, and a "Super App" strategy integrating its products into a single state-aware application. Morningstar assigns a narrow moat rooted in risk-management and product-innovation capabilities.

The structural lever for future growth is iGaming legalization in additional states, given iGaming's superior unit economics relative to sportsbook. DraftKings spent $26.4M on advocacy in Q1 tied to iGaming legislation and a sportsbook ballot measure, reflecting how much the growth case depends on legislative outcomes outside management's direct control.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature of DraftKings' financials is the profitability turn. Net income progressed from roughly -$1.5B (2021) and -$1.4B (2022) to -$0.8B (2023), -$0.5B (2024), and approximately breakeven in FY2025. Revenue scaled from $1.3B in 2021 to $6.1B in 2025. The trajectory is unambiguous.

Q1 2026 specifics:
- Revenue $1.646B, +16.8% YoY
- Net income $21.1M (vs. -$33.9M prior year); diluted EPS $0.03; Adjusted EPS $0.20 (vs. $0.12)
- Adjusted EBITDA $167.9M, +64% YoY
- Sportsbook net revenue margin (hold) 7.8%, up from 6.4%
- Cost of revenue 57.7% of revenue (from 59.9%), reflecting scale economics
- Gaming taxes $569.5M, ~35% of revenue — the single largest cost line

The earnings beat record is strong on adjusted figures: Q1 2026 EPS beat by 230%, Q4 2025 by 68%, and Q2 2025 by 91%, with one miss (Q3 2025, a seasonally weak quarter, by 22%). But the adjusted figures mask that Q1 2026 Adjusted EPS of $0.20 missed the ~$0.22 consensus, and the user-metric miss (MUPs ~4.2M vs. ~4.63M expected) is the more economically meaningful data point.

Balance sheet and liquidity:
- Cash $999.4M; total assets $4.31B; total stockholders' equity $605M; accumulated deficit $6.42B
- Debt: $1.26B zero-coupon convertible notes maturing March 2028 (conversion price ~$94.85, deeply out of the money at $25, so likely cash-settled or refinanced) plus a $594M Term B Loan (SOFR+1.75%, ~5.42%, maturing 2032); undrawn $500M revolver
- The 2028 maturity wall of ~$1.27B is the real balance-sheet item to monitor

The Altman Z-Score reads as distress (~1.6), but this is driven by the $6.42B accumulated deficit and thin equity base relative to assets, a backward-looking artifact of the cash-burn era. Current liquidity is comfortable ($999M cash, undrawn revolver) and full-year FCF is strongly positive (roughly $600M+ in 2025). The Q1 operating cash flow of -$48.4M is a seasonal working-capital swing (liabilities-to-users of -$123M from settlement timing), not a deterioration; full-year, not Q1, cash flow is the relevant figure.

Capital allocation has shifted decisively toward returning cash: a $2.0B buyback authorization (doubled November 2025), with 20.4M shares repurchased for $718M since inception including $98.6M in Q1. Buying back stock that has fallen ~48% from its high is sensible deployment, though the volatile share price makes timing difficult to judge.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated, and the split is the crux of the entire thesis. Revenue grew on monetization rather than users. ARPMUP rose 21% while MUPs fell 3.6% YoY (and 2.1% growth ex the Texas Lottery exit). For a company whose historical narrative was rapid user acquisition into newly legalized states, a declining payer count signals that the US market is maturing and that the top-of-funnel may be capping out.

Hold expansion drove nearly all of the Q1 profit improvement. The durability question is critical: parlay mix and product structure provide some structural lift, but hold percentage is partly a function of sports-outcome luck and mean-reverts over time. If hold normalizes lower while MUPs stagnate, the EBITDA ramp the guidance implies becomes harder to deliver.

The estimate-revision trend reinforces the caution. Consensus EPS has fallen across every period: current quarter to $0.30 from $0.39 (90 days ago), current year to $1.07 from $1.24, next year to $1.71 from $1.89. These falling estimates signal the Street is marking down forward earnings power as user softness and prediction-market uncertainty register. The one bright spot is the next-quarter revision (+23.9% over 30 days, though still -52.5% over 90 days), which is noise within a broadly negative pattern.

Prediction markets are the wild card for growth. If CFTC federal preemption holds, DraftKings (via its October 2025 Railbird acquisition, up to ~$285M) can offer a lower-tax, nationwide product, potentially expanding its addressable market into the ~17 states without legal sports betting. Management cites third-party data that ~70% of sports-prediction volume comes from states without legal online sports betting, framing the category as partly incremental rather than purely cannibalistic. April 2026 annualized prediction volume topped $1B with CAC down 80% via the flagship app. The opportunity is real, but it is contingent on a regulatory outcome that is actively contested.

## Valuation Assessment

DraftKings is structurally difficult to value on trailing earnings — trailing EPS is essentially zero, producing a meaningless ~276x P/E. The appropriate frame is forward earnings against the growth and quality of the business. On consensus FY2026 EPS of $1.07, the stock trades at ~24x; on FY2027 EPS of $1.71, ~15x. Those are not demanding multiples for a company guiding to 16%+ revenue growth and a 64% EBITDA inflection, which is the bull's strongest valuation argument.

The complication is that the forward estimates anchoring those multiples are falling, and the EBITDA guidance leans heavily on a hold rate that may not persist. A 24x forward multiple on an estimate that has been cut 13.5% in 90 days is less attractive than it appears, because the denominator is moving against the buyer. If estimates continue to fall and hold normalizes, the "cheap" forward multiple resets higher on a stationary price.

Analyst targets span an unusually wide range — low $20, high $74, mean $34.71, median $32 — reflecting the polarized view of the prediction-market binary. The spread is informative: this is a stock where the bull and bear cases produce a ~3.7x range in fair value. BNP Paribas initiated with the Street's only sell-equivalent rating in May 2026, Morgan Stanley lowered its target, and Truist and Morningstar remain bullish.

On balance the valuation is not stretched, but it does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify a high valuation score. The forward multiple looks reasonable only if estimates stop falling and hold persists, two assumptions the recent data does not support. A fair-value range of $28–32 (roughly 16–19x falling-but-stabilizing FY2027 EPS) sits modestly above the current price, with a wide error band driven by the prediction-market outcome. That is a balanced setup, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The core sportsbook and iGaming business is a maturing US duopoly with FanDuel, characterized by high marketing intensity and rising state gaming taxes. Within this structure DraftKings holds a defensible position on technology and brand, but the duopoly dynamic limits pricing power and the maturing market limits new-user growth — visible in the declining MUP count.

The disruptive vector is prediction markets. Kalshi and Polymarket pioneered the CFTC-regulated path that structurally undercuts the state-licensed model on both taxes (federal jurisdiction bypasses state gaming taxes) and geographic reach (nationwide vs. state-by-state). DraftKings' Railbird acquisition and "DraftKings Predictions" launch are a fast-follow to avoid disintermediation, and management's risk-management edge may translate into a competitive prediction-market business. But the company is responding to a threat it did not initiate, on a regulatory foundation it does not control, against well-funded competitors that have a head start.

The competitive trajectory is therefore ambiguous. In the core, DraftKings is stable-to-slightly-eroding (flat-to-down users, strong monetization). In the new category, it is a credible but late entrant in a structurally advantaged channel whose legal status is unsettled. New entrants like Fanatics add marketing pressure. The net read is a stable competitive position under genuine structural pressure, which warrants a middle score rather than a high one.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is heavily weighted toward unpriced or partially-priced binary outcomes, which is why this dimension scores low.

**Prediction-market regulation (the dominant swing factor).** The legal status is actively contested: states (e.g., Utah) argue these are illegal gambling; the CFTC under chair Mike Selig asserts exclusive jurisdiction; the White House is reviewing a CFTC rule; the NFL is lobbying to ban certain contracts; and legal commentators flag the major-questions doctrine as a potential vulnerability for CFTC authority. The outcome is binary: it could legitimize and expand DraftKings' new business or unwind it while leaving the core exposed to a tax-advantaged competitor.

**Addiction litigation wave (new and escalating).** The Q1 10-Q discloses a materially expanded slate of product-liability suits alleging the platform is defectively designed to encourage compulsive gambling, that DraftKings targets vulnerable users via VIP hosts and data-driven promotions, and that "risk-free" promos are deceptive. New 2026 matters include six product-liability suits (MA and PA, from March 2026), the Farley class action (April 2026), and a City of Baltimore enforcement action; several (Scanlon, Macek II, Youngs, Beyer/IL) survived motions to dismiss. The volume and consistency suggest a coordinated plaintiffs'-bar campaign reminiscent of opioid, social-media, and tobacco arcs. Individually immaterial today, collectively this could force changes to the VIP program, promotions, and notification engine that are core to monetization.

**Gaming-tax increases.** Already ~35% of revenue, with states raising rates (Illinois recently approved new taxes on prediction markets and DFS). Higher state taxes compress margins and ironically favor the federally-regulated prediction-market channel.

**Hold-rate normalization.** The single biggest profit driver (7.8% hold) is partly luck-driven and can mean-revert, which would undercut the EBITDA ramp.

**2028 maturity wall.** ~$1.27B of convertibles due, deeply out of the money at $25, requiring cash settlement or refinancing — manageable given the FCF trajectory but a real item.

**IRS audit** over fantasy-contest excise taxation (liability not estimable) and persistent IP litigation (Winview '543/'988 claims proceeding) round out the overhang.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in backwardation (near-term ATM IV 62.4% vs. long-term 52.6%), indicating the market is pricing imminent event-driven uncertainty, consistent with the unresolved prediction-market and litigation overhangs. Near-term IV is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 47.7% (a 14.7-point spread), at the 71st percentile of the current chain. The put/call skew is balanced (-0.0%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.12 — the market is pricing elevated overall uncertainty but not a directional bearish bet, which fits a two-sided situation rather than a one-way collapse. Short interest is moderate at 8.1% of float with 3.1 days to cover, neither a crowded short nor a clear signal of informed bearishness.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** DraftKings has crossed into sustained profitability and FCF generation. Hold-rate gains prove structural, iGaming legalization adds high-margin states, and the Railbird prediction-market entry — if CFTC preemption holds — opens a nationwide, tax-advantaged channel into the states without legal sports betting, expanding the TAM rather than cannibalizing the core. The buyback at depressed prices accretes value, and on FY2027 EPS the stock at ~15x is inexpensive for a duopoly leader. Bull target ~$45–50, roughly the prior high, on hold durability plus a favorable prediction-market outcome.

**Bear Case.** User growth has stalled (MUPs -3.6%), revenue gains rest on monetization that is approaching ceilings, and hold-rate-driven margin expansion mean-reverts. Falling estimates across every horizon confirm deteriorating forward earnings power. Prediction markets disintermediate the core via a structurally lower-tax channel that DraftKings cannot match within the state-licensed model, while the addiction-litigation wave forces costly changes to VIP and promotional engines. Regulatory hostility (major-questions doctrine, NFL pressure) unwinds the new business while the threat to the old one persists. Bear target ~$18–20, in line with the Street low and the 52-week low.

The two cases are roughly balanced in probability and produce a ~2.5x range in outcomes, a situation where directional conviction is unwarranted.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is an honest uncertain hold rather than a growth or income hold. There is no dividend, and while a positive 12-month appreciation case exists, it is contingent on two unresolved binary factors (hold durability and prediction-market regulation) of roughly equal weight to the downside case. The falling estimate trend specifically argues against a confident growth-hold classification — the data is moving against the bull, not for it.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus FY2026/FY2027 EPS estimates stop falling and turn positive over a 60–90 day window, confirming the turn is durable rather than estimate-driven momentum fading.
- MUPs return to YoY growth (ex one-time exits) for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the user-growth stall was temporary rather than structural.
- A favorable, durable regulatory resolution on prediction-market federal preemption that materially expands DraftKings' addressable market without a tax disadvantage to the core.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Sportsbook hold normalizes back toward 6.0–6.5% while MUPs continue declining, undermining the FY2026 EBITDA guidance — watch the next two quarterly prints.
- An adverse prediction-market regulatory ruling (CFTC authority struck on major-questions grounds, or hostile state actions) that exposes the core to a tax-advantaged competitor while impairing Railbird.
- Escalation of addiction litigation to a settlement or ruling that forces material changes to the VIP/promotional model, or a class certification in one of the surviving cases.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, approximately early August 2026) is the natural reassessment point — it will provide a second read on hold durability and MUP trajectory. The prediction-market regulatory situation should be monitored continuously given its binary nature.

## Conclusion

DraftKings is a fundamentally improved business — first GAAP profit, 64% EBITDA growth, positive FCF, a substantial buyback, and a not-demanding forward multiple. But the recent quarter's gains came overwhelmingly from a hold rate that may not persist and from monetization of a shrinking user base, consensus estimates are falling across every horizon, and the stock sits at the intersection of two binary risks: an unsettled prediction-market regulatory battle that could either expand or undermine the franchise, and a building addiction-litigation campaign that threatens the core monetization engine. At ~$25.30 the valuation is balanced rather than cheap, with a fair-value range of roughly $28–32 against a bear case near $18–20 and a bull case near $45–50. The wide analyst target spread ($20–$74) reflects a situation without a resolvable directional answer at present. The appropriate posture is to hold and monitor the specific, measurable triggers above rather than to take a side on the prediction-market binary before the regulatory and operating data clarify.
