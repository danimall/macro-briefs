**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Angi operates the leading U.S. online home-services marketplace (Angi, Angie's List, HomeAdvisor, Handy), matching consumers with home-improvement professionals across 500+ categories. The investment debate reduces to a single question: whether the deliberate destruction of roughly $270M of low-quality Network and Advertising revenue since 2022 leaves behind a smaller but structurally better, higher-trust, more profitable marketplace, or whether the contraction is a slower-motion version of the competitive erosion the market has feared for years. Revenue has fallen ~45% since 2022 (from $1,892M to $1,031M) while operating income swung from –$126M to +$65M, a textbook shrink-to-profitability. Q1'26 then reverted to a –$9.5M operating loss as sales and marketing spiked to 59% of revenue, showing the Proprietary-channel rebuild is currently margin-dilutive.

This report moves the recommendation to **HOLD** from the prior AVOID, and I want to be explicit about why. The prior AVOID calls were issued at higher prices; the stock has since fallen 63% to ~9x next-year consensus EPS ($0.65) with an ~18% trailing free-cash-flow yield ($45M FCF against a ~$249M market cap), $245M of cash, and a single cheap, long-dated tranche of debt (3.875%, due August 2028) that management is actively retiring below par. At those fundamentals, my honest estimate of fair value sits near the current price. A rating pinned to fair value ≈ spot is by definition a HOLD, not an AVOID; an AVOID would require either a downside target materially below the current price or an argument that the business is uninvestable at any reasonable price, and I can defend neither given positive FCF, manageable leverage, and a most-recent-quarter EPS beat of +19.1%.

The reason this is not a BUY is equally specific and is where I diverge from the constructive side of the Street consensus (2 buy / 5 hold / 0 sell, mean target $9.60). Consensus EPS estimates have been cut ~65% for the current year over 90 days, average monthly active Pros fell 22% YoY, and the company depends on Google for traffic in an environment where AI-driven search changes are an existential channel risk that management itself names as a top risk factor. The turnaround thesis is genuinely unresolved, the setup is two-sided, and HOLD (uncertain) is the honest classification.

## Company Overview

Angi connects consumers with home-services Pros through a lead-generation and advertising model. Because the company primarily generates and monetizes leads rather than performing services, gross margin runs ~95–96%. Following a 2025 reorganization, the business reports in two segments (U.S. and International). U.S. revenue splits across Lead revenue (~78% of U.S. revenue in Q1'26), Advertising (now deliberately wound to essentially zero), Services (pre-priced offerings), and Membership subscriptions.

The company completed its spin-off from IAC on March 31, 2025. IAC now holds zero ownership, all dual-class Class B super-voting shares converted to Class A, and a single class of stock trades, a clean governance structure. A 1-for-10 reverse split effective March 24, 2025 explains the sub-$10 price and ~40.4M share count.

Scale as of Q1'26: ~105,000 U.S. average monthly active Pros (down from ~134,000 a year earlier) and ~16 million projects over the trailing twelve months.

## Financial Analysis

**The profitability inflection is real, and it happened on shrinking revenue.**

| Operating income | –$126M | –$26M | +$22M | +$65M |
| Net income (to Angi) | –$128M | –$41M | +$36M | +$44M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | — | — | $145M | $140M |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $156M | $105M |
| Free cash flow | — | — | — | $45M |

By 2025, operating margin reached 6.3%, net margin 4.3%, and FCF margin 4.4% on ~95% gross margin. The provision for credit losses fell from $57M (2024) to $48.5M (2025), consistent with transacting with higher-quality Pros. On a partial single-filing basis the Piotroski F-score reads 5/5 (positive net income, ROA, and OCF; OCF exceeding net income; current ratio 1.65).

**The balance sheet is the source of the "grey zone" optics, but it is not a distress signal.** At YE2025, cash was $304M ($245M by Q1'26), against $500M of 3.875% senior notes due August 2028 (now ~$473M after repurchases), the only funded debt. D/E of 1.81 and an Altman Z of 1.95 look elevated, but that is a function of a modest equity base rather than liquidity stress: the notes are cheap, long-dated, and being retired below par. A new $175M undrawn secured revolver (net leverage covenant 4.0x) was added in November 2025. Net leverage is manageable against ~$140M of Adjusted EBITDA.

**Q1'26 is where the turnaround thesis gets tested.** Revenue was $238.2M (–3% YoY; U.S. –5%), and the company posted a GAAP operating loss of –$9.5M versus a +$20M profit a year earlier. The driver was a deliberate 18% jump in S&M to $140M (59% of revenue) to feed Proprietary channels, plus $15M of restructuring and higher depreciation. Adjusted EBITDA fell 17% to $22.9M. Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter, and the loss is partly front-loaded spend, but it demonstrates that replacing the walked-away Network revenue with Proprietary leads is currently expensive.

On the earnings-surprise record, the most recent quarter beat by +19.1% (–$0.22 actual vs –$0.27 estimate), and 3 of the last 4 quarters beat consensus. That track record matters for the balance of this report: the deterioration is showing up in guidance cuts and revenue trajectory, not in execution against near-term expectations.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth story here in the conventional sense; there is a mix-shift story with an unproven endpoint. The defining dynamic is "homeowner choice," implemented January 2025, which lets consumers pick their own Pros rather than being auto-matched. This intentionally collapsed Network revenue 72% in 2025 (from $373M to $103M) and Advertising 31%. In Q1'26, Advertising went to essentially zero (–$46K) and Network fell another 56%. Management's bet is that higher-quality Proprietary leads produce a more durable, higher-trust business. Proprietary revenue grew +17% in 2025 and +7% in Q1'26, but that growth has not yet outrun the legacy declines, and the +7% Q1 print is a deceleration from the full-year +17%.

The estimate-revision picture is negative and is the single most important reason this is not a BUY. Current-quarter consensus was cut 31.3% over 30 days and 72.7% over 90 days; current-year estimates were cut 65.4% over 90 days; next-year cut 47.5%. Falling estimates point to negative earnings momentum and cap any argument that the market is underestimating a recovery. I weight this heavily and against the bull case.

The demand-side long-term risk is that the digitization of home services, management's core runway thesis, may not accelerate, and AI-driven search self-service could reroute demand away from marketplaces entirely.

## Valuation Assessment

At $6.14, Angi trades at ~9x next-year consensus EPS of $0.65 and 13.6x trailing EPS of $0.94, against an ~18% trailing FCF yield and $245M of cash offset by ~$473M of long-dated notes. The audit is correct that this valuation already discounts a great deal of the contraction. A market pricing an FCF-positive, net-leverage-manageable business at ~9x forward with zero sell ratings is not pricing terminal decline; it is pricing a business it expects to shrink and stabilize.

Peer multiples are of limited use here given the heterogeneity of the Advertising cohort (DoubleVerify 30.8x, Ziff Davis 49.6x, Trade Desk 21.3x, Omnicom 285x on depressed earnings; median 49.6x). Angi's discount to that median is warranted by its declining revenue and channel risk, but the gap is wide enough that a stabilization would support meaningful re-rating.

I diverge from the mechanical read that a low multiple plus falling price equals a value opportunity. A compressed multiple on falling estimates is precisely the value-trap configuration to treat with skepticism; cheapness here is a warning about the estimate trajectory, not a standalone buy signal. But the opposite error, treating a 63% decline as confirmation of terminal impairment, is the mistake the prior reports drifted toward. My read is that fair value sits close to the current price. Below roughly $4.50–5.00 (near the 52-week low, ~4–6x next-year EPS with the FCF yield approaching mid-20s%), the asymmetry would tilt constructive and I would revisit a BUY. Above the high-$7s to $8s without evidence that Proprietary growth is outrunning legacy declines, the risk/reward tilts back toward AVOID. Between those, HOLD.

This directly reconciles the audit's central inconsistency: my fair-value estimate is not below the current price, so I cannot defend an AVOID; it is not clearly above it either, so I cannot defend a BUY. That is a HOLD.

**Reconciling against the Street.** Consensus is 2 buy / 5 hold / 0 sell, sentiment improving, with a recent buy upgrade and a mean target of $9.60 (median $11.00), materially above spot with no sell ratings. My HOLD is more cautious than the median target but no longer more bearish than every covering analyst; it aligns with the modal "hold" of the Street. Where I part from the constructive minority is on the estimate-revision velocity: the –65% current-year cut has not been reconciled with a $9.60 mean target that likely lags the reductions. My call is that consensus targets are stale in the same direction estimates have moved, and I would not underwrite the implied upside (mean target ~56% above spot) until Proprietary growth stabilizes.

## Competitive Landscape

Angi owns three of the best-known U.S. home-services brands but competes against Google Local Services, Thumbtack, Yelp, Nextdoor, and Houzz, plus the search and social giants that control its traffic funnel. Switching costs are low and consumer offerings are free, so Angi cannot lock in either side of the marketplace. Dependence on Google for both paid and organic traffic is the structural vulnerability, and AI-driven changes to search economics could compress or reroute that funnel.

Pro attrition is the metric to watch for a negative flywheel: average monthly active Pros fell 19% in 2025 and another 22% YoY in Q1'26. Some of this is deliberate (culling low-quality Pros), but a smaller network eventually degrades match quality and consumer experience if it overshoots. The proxy TSR data is stark: Angi's cumulative TSR indexed at 9.8 against a peer group at 263.67 over the measurement period, reflecting years of restructuring and prior over-monetization that damaged trust. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating on the metrics that matter (Pro count, revenue share of traffic), which anchors a low competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The homeowner-choice gamble is unproven.** Proprietary growth (+7% Q1'26) has not demonstrated it can outrun legacy Network/Advertising declines, and the rebuild is currently margin-dilutive.

**2. Google/AI channel risk.** The 10-K names AI as an existential risk: homeowners self-educating and search-economics disruption could both hit a lead-gen model directly. Management is leaning into AI internally (product development expense –61% YoY in Q1'26, layoffs justified by "AI-driven efficiency"), which helps margins now but does not address the demand-side risk.

**3. Pro attrition** (–22% YoY) risks a negative network flywheel.

**4. Refinancing.** ~$473M of notes due August 2028 must be refinanced or repaid; the revolver matures 91 days prior. This is manageable given the cheap coupon and 2+ years of runway, and the active discount repurchases reduce the wall, but it is a dated risk.

**5. Spin-off tax straitjacket.** Through ~March 2027, tax rules restrict M&A, large equity issuance, and certain repurchases without jeopardizing tax-free treatment, freezing any activist or strategic-transaction thesis.

**6. Worker-classification risk.** Reclassification of Pros to employees would create material tax/labor liabilities.

Most of these are known and at least partly priced into a ~9x forward multiple, which is why the risk score is a "priced but present" 4.0 rather than lower.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 240.9% to 113.7% (–127.2pp) over the last month while the stock rose 21.1% ($5.08 → $6.15), and the put/call OI ratio rose modestly (0.70 → 0.94). Falling IV alongside a rising price suggests the market is de-pricing near-term crash risk rather than positioning for a directional break in either direction. Short interest is 12.7% of float with 4.3 days to cover, elevated but not extreme, and the borrow/options complex is not confirming an aggressive bearish thesis. This non-confirming signal is one of the inputs that argues against AVOID and toward a balanced HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($9–11).** Q2'26 (seasonally strongest quarter, results due August 4) shows Proprietary growth accelerating and total revenue stabilizing, restructuring charges roll off, S&M normalizes below 55% of revenue, and margins re-inflect. If the market gains confidence that the shrink-to-quality endpoint is a durable ~$1B-revenue business generating $130–140M EBITDA and $45M+ FCF, a re-rating toward the Street mean ($9.60) and median ($11.00) is achievable. The deliberate nature of the revenue destruction, walking away from unprofitable Network/Advertising rather than losing it to competitors, supports this reading, and the bond buybacks at a discount are rational capital allocation.

**Bear case ($4–5).** Proprietary growth stalls below the legacy decline rate, Pro attrition continues, and estimates keep falling. The Q1'26 operating loss proves structural rather than seasonal, AI/Google traffic pressure intensifies, and the stock retests its $4.53 low as investors conclude the contraction is a managed decline rather than a turnaround. This is ~19–27% downside from spot.

The setup is two-sided, which is the core reason for HOLD. The deliberate revenue contraction distinguishes Angi from involuntary competitive displacement, and the FCF/leverage profile means this is not a solvency story, but the falling estimates and unproven Proprietary economics prevent a constructive call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2'26 results, due August 4, 2026, are the key near-term event, the seasonally strongest quarter and the cleanest test of whether the Proprietary rebuild is working. The August 2028 debt maturity is the longer-dated watch item.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Total revenue returns to flat-or-positive YoY with Proprietary growth clearly outrunning legacy declines, OR
- Current-year consensus EPS estimates stop falling and revise upward on two consecutive prints, OR
- The stock trades toward $4.50–5.00 (near the 52-week low), where the ~18%+ FCF yield and ~4–6x forward EPS create asymmetric upside even on a shrinking-but-stable business.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2'26 shows a second consecutive operating loss in the seasonally strong quarter with S&M failing to normalize, OR
- Average monthly active Pros fall another 15%+ sequentially (network-flywheel breakdown), OR
- Guidance or estimates are cut a further 20%+, signaling the contraction is uncontrolled rather than deliberate, OR
- The stock re-rates above the high-$7s/$8s without fundamental improvement, restoring an unfavorable risk/reward.

## Conclusion

I am moving Angi to **HOLD (uncertain)** from the prior AVOID. The change reflects both a 63% price decline that materially improved the risk/reward and a correction of an internal inconsistency in the prior framing: pegging fair value at the current price while rating AVOID is not defensible. At ~9x next-year consensus EPS, an ~18% trailing FCF yield, $245M cash, and a single cheap tranche of long-dated debt being retired below par, my fair-value estimate sits near the current price, which supports HOLD rather than AVOID. The most-recent-quarter beat (+19.1%), the non-confirming options/short signals, and the deliberate (not involuntary) nature of the revenue contraction all argue against a terminal-decline call.

This is not a BUY. Current-year estimates have been cut ~65% in 90 days, Pro attrition is running at –22% YoY, the Proprietary rebuild is currently margin-dilutive, and the Google/AI channel risk is real and unquantified. The turnaround is unresolved. I would turn constructive on evidence that Proprietary growth is outrunning legacy declines and estimates have stopped falling, or on a decline toward $4.50–5.00 where the FCF yield alone underwrites the downside. Until Q2'26 provides that evidence, the balanced call is to hold and reassess after the August 4 print.
