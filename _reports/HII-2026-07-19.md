**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Huntington Ingalls is America's largest military shipbuilder and the sole domestic builder of nuclear-powered aircraft carriers, one of two nuclear-submarine builders alongside General Dynamics' Electric Boat, and the sole builder of amphibious assault ships. The moat rests on sole-source and duopoly positions, 50-year ship lifecycles, nuclear expertise that cannot be replicated on any reasonable timeline, and a workforce whose skills take decades to build. Demand is not the problem. Backlog reached $54.0B in Q1 2026 (~4.3x annual revenue), the FY27 budget request proposes $65.8B for shipbuilding, and new franchise programs (the FF(X) frigate, the BBG(X) "battleship") add optionality beyond the modeled ~6% revenue CAGR.

The entire investment debate sits on the conversion of that demand into profitable cash flow. Legacy pre-COVID fixed-price contracts bid before the 2021-2023 inflation and labor-cost surge remain underwater, and the turnaround has not yet reached the income statement. Segment operating margin compressed from ~7.4% (FY23) to 5.0% (FY24) and recovered only to 5.7% (FY25). Q1 2026 margins moved the wrong way: segment margin fell to 5.6% from 6.3%, Newport News to 5.3% from 6.1%, while free cash flow ran -$461M and cash fell from $774M to $216M. Management guides FY26 FCF to $500-600M, below FY25 and below net income, signaling continued cash-conversion strain.

The stock reflects this tension. HII has fallen roughly 41% from its February 2026 high of $460 to $269, a drawdown large enough that "the market is wrong" requires a specific claim. The claim here is narrow: throughput improved 18% YoY in Q1 (versus ~14% in 2025), which is the operational precursor to margin recovery, but it has not yet flowed through the P&L, and until it does the market is correctly pricing execution risk rather than mispricing a working turnaround. We rate HII a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the demand backdrop and moat justify continued ownership, but the bar for an upgrade to BUY is evidence, in reported margins, that throughput gains are converting to profit.

## Company Overview

HII operates three segments:

- **Newport News Shipbuilding (~52% of revenue, $6.5B FY25):** The crown jewel. The only US builder capable of designing, constructing, refueling, and inactivating nuclear-powered aircraft carriers, and one of two capable of building nuclear submarines. Programs include Ford-class carriers (CVN 78 series), Virginia-class attack subs, Columbia-class ballistic-missile subs (as a subcontractor to Electric Boat), and carrier refueling/complex overhaul (RCOH).
- **Ingalls Shipbuilding (~25%, $3.1B FY25):** Non-nuclear ships in Pascagoula. Sole builder of amphibious assault ships (LHA/LPD) and one of two builders of Arleigh Burke-class destroyers (DDG 51), with 36 DDGs delivered.
- **Mission Technologies (~24%, $3.0B FY25):** C5ISR, cyber, AI/ML, unmanned systems, and live-virtual-constructive training. The higher-growth, asset-light services arm.

Approximately 81% of revenue comes from the US Navy and substantially all from the US Government. Barriers to entry are extraordinarily high; the constraint on the business is the ability to convert backlog into profitable throughput, not the availability of orders.

## Financial Analysis

**Top-line growth is genuinely accelerating.** FY25 revenue was $12.48B (+8.2% YoY), the strongest growth in years (FY24 +0.7%, FY23 +7.3%). Q1 2026 revenue was $3.10B (+13.4% YoY). Backlog grew from $48.7B a year earlier. The earnings-surprise record is clean — four consecutive beats (Q2 2025 +16.6%, Q3 +8.5%, Q4 +3.1%, Q1 2026 +0.9%) — though the magnitude of beats is shrinking.

**Margins are the problem, and the trajectory is unfavorable.** By segment, Ingalls fell from 13.2% (2023) to 7.6% (2025) and Newport News from 6.2% to 5.1%. Net cumulative catch-up adjustments — the cleanest marker of contract performance — were +$118M in FY23, -$126M in FY24, and -$28M in FY25. The Q1 2026 print was the discouraging data point, with shipbuilding margin at 5.7%, so the recovery the bull case needs is not yet visible.

**Cash flow is lumpy and currently weak.** FY25 FCF rebounded to $800M from $40M in FY24, but the MD&A attributes this to a favorable working-capital swing (timing of billings) rather than margin improvement. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was -$390M as a ~$640M trade working-capital build reversed the FY25 benefit. The FY26 guide sits below net income, which is the honest anchor for cash-generation capacity through the turnaround.

**Balance sheet is moderate, with a genuine pension tailwind.** Long-term debt is $2.7B, D/E 1.51x, appropriate for a capital-intensive contractor. The qualified pension plans are 126% funded (FAS), 2026 required cash contribution is only ~$2M, and the non-operating retirement item is a growing benefit (+$213M expected FY26). Altman Z of 2.14 sits in the grey zone, reflecting thin EBIT relative to a capital-heavy asset base rather than distress. Profitability metrics are thin for a moat business (ROE 11.9%, net margin 4.8%, ROA 4.7%).

**Capital allocation is conservative.** The dividend was raised to $1.38/quarter (11th-plus consecutive annual raise; $213M paid FY25), for a ~2.1% yield. There were no buybacks in FY25 or Q1 2026 despite a $1.35B remaining authorization — a prudent signal given the cash strain and rising capex (climbing to 4-5% of sales for capacity expansion), but it removes a near-term EPS-support lever.

## Growth Analysis

The demand environment is exceptional and broad-based. FY26 defense appropriations (signed 2/3/2026) fully fund carriers, Virginia/Columbia submarines, DDGs, and the Maritime Industrial Base. Two additional Block V Virginia subs were added in 2025, with advance procurement for all ten Block VI boats. New franchise optionality is confirmed against budget disclosures: Ingalls won the FF(X) frigate lead contract, and the FY27 request includes initial advance procurement for the lead ship of the BBG(X) "battleship." A $1.5B+ Maritime Industrial Base investment targets HII's supplier ecosystem and workforce directly.

Management's stated framing is a medium-term ~6% revenue CAGR to $16B+ by 2030, with margins returning toward historical norms as legacy fixed-price contracts roll off and new awards are booked with "balanced risk equations." Throughput doubled outsourced ("distributed shipbuilding") hours, and the Ingalls collective-bargaining agreements were renewed to March 2031, removing a near-term labor-disruption risk.

The growth caveat is the estimate-revision picture, which does not corroborate an inflection. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 11.2% over 90 days ($4.30 → $3.82); current-year is roughly flat ($17.35 vs $17.40); next-year is down slightly (-0.9%). This is not the +30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection; it is a company with rising revenue and stalled earnings visibility. Growth Prospects therefore earn a moderate score: revenue durability is high, but the earnings growth that drives the equity is unconfirmed.

## Valuation Assessment

At $269, HII trades at ~17.5x trailing EPS of $15.39 and ~15.5x the FY26 consensus of $17.35. The peer table (KTOS 342x, DRS 46x, HXL 65x, AVAV negative) is not informative — those are growth/turnaround names on distorted multiples; the relevant comparables are the large defense primes. Specific forward-multiple comparisons to GD and NOC are [unverified — actual: N/A] against the provided sources, but the peer benchmark data confirms HII trades at a meaningful discount to the aerospace/defense peer median (P/E 64.7x).

The temptation is to read the low multiple as cheap, but the discipline here is to treat a compressed multiple that reflects an unresolved margin question as a warning rather than a signal. HII is inexpensive precisely because the market doubts the margin recovery — FCF guidance below net income, Q1 margins declining, and an unresolved welding-quality overhang all argue that the discount is earned, not a mispricing. Cheapness in the absence of a confirmed catalyst is the classic value trap, and the estimate revisions (falling, not rising) do not supply the catalyst.

The multiple is not stretched, and the downside is bounded by the moat and the backlog. On a normalized basis, if segment margins recover toward the 6.5-7% range management targets, EPS approaches the $20 next-year consensus and a 17-18x multiple supports the mid-$300s, but that outcome is contingent on execution not yet in the numbers. The analyst mean target of $364 (median $360) sits well above the price, but with sentiment "degrading" (a StrongBuy dropped to zero, a StrongSell appeared) those targets are stale relative to the Q1 miss on margins. We score valuation as balanced: fairly priced for the execution risk, not an asymmetric bargain.

## Competitive Landscape

HII's competitive position is the strongest dimension of the story, though its trajectory has softened. It holds sole-source positions on carriers and amphibious ships and a duopoly with Electric Boat on submarines and destroyers. The 50-year ship lifecycle, nuclear expertise, and a workforce that includes 1,308 "Master Shipbuilders" with 40+ years of service make displacement effectively impossible on any relevant horizon.

The reason the competitive score is 7.5 rather than higher is trajectory, not level. Newport News booked unfavorable catch-up adjustments on Ford-class carriers and Virginia-class subs, and the self-disclosed welding-procedure noncompliance at Newport News is a quality event at a nuclear shipbuilder that erodes the execution premium the moat is supposed to command. The moat protects the franchise; it does not protect margins if throughput and quality problems persist. A market leader whose execution is slipping earns a lower score than one gaining ground, and the recent record justifies the modest markdown from prior reports.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Fixed-price cost overruns (the central risk).** ~46% of revenue is fixed-price incentive; legacy pre-COVID contracts remain underwater and recovery depends on both execution and repricing new awards to historical-norm margins. Failure to lift throughput/labor productivity produces more unfavorable EAC charges.
2. **Labor and throughput.** HII explicitly flagged personnel who "failed to achieve expected performance improvements... required rework." A large share of the skilled workforce is near retirement. The Q1 throughput gain is the operational linchpin; if it stalls, the thesis stalls.
3. **Newport News welding-quality noncompliance (under-appreciated overhang).** Self-disclosed to the government, unresolved as of Q1 2026, with no estimable outcome. On nuclear-relevant construction this can produce a surprise EAC charge, rework, and schedule/reputational cost.
4. **Single-customer / budget risk.** 81% Navy; a 43-day government shutdown occurred Oct-Nov 2025, and continuing resolutions limit new starts.
5. **Cash conversion.** FY26 FCF guidance below net income limits capital-return flexibility and keeps leverage from de-risking quickly.

There is also an unresolved antitrust "no-poach" class action (naval-engineer recruiting), which the brief indicates was settled in March 2026 for an amount HII deems immaterial.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV jumped from 34.9% to 46.3% over the past month while the price fell 5.7%, and the term structure is in backwardation (46.3% near-term vs 37.3% at six months), with an implied ±11.8% move on the nearest expiry. Skew is roughly balanced (-2.2%) and put/call OI collapsed from 1.51 to 0.64, so this is event-driven uncertainty rather than a directional bearish bet, consistent with a market bracing for the next margin print rather than positioning for a collapse. IV rich to HV (spread +10.9%) means the market is paying up for uncertainty; there is no cheap-vol entry signal here. Short interest is negligible at 2.6% of float (1.6 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($400-435).** Legacy fixed-price contracts roll off, the Q1 throughput gain sustains and converts to margin expansion toward the 6.5-7% segment range, FCF normalizes above net income, and the FF(X)/BBG(X) franchises add growth beyond the ~6% CAGR. Segment margin recovery lifts EPS toward the $20 next-year consensus, and a 17-18x multiple on a growing, moated, backlog-rich franchise supports the low-$400s. Demand is a near-certainty; only execution is in question.

**Bear case ($245-260).** Throughput gains fail to translate to margin, more unfavorable EAC charges accrue (welding-quality resolution being a specific trigger), FCF stays below net income, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the defense-prime range on a "structurally lower-margin contractor" reframe. A ~9-10% downside from current levels, bounded by the moat and dividend support.

**Base case ($305-315).** Modest, gradual margin recovery through 2026-2027 with continued top-line strength; the market pays ~17x a rising but not inflecting EPS. This straddles bear and bull: the business is sound and the demand is real, but the profit conversion is unproven.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early-mid August 2026 for Q2) is the critical checkpoint — it is the first look at whether the Q1 throughput gain is beginning to show in shipbuilding margins. The FY27 appropriations process is the secondary demand input.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of shipbuilding segment margin expansion (>6%, up YoY), confirming throughput-to-profit conversion.
- FY26 FCF guidance raised above net income, or actual FCF turning solidly positive in H2 2026 without a working-capital-timing asterisk.
- Resolution of the Newport News welding-quality matter without a material EAC charge, plus a resumption of buybacks under the $1.35B authorization at these prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material unfavorable EAC charge (welding-related or new fixed-price overrun) that cuts current-year EPS by more than ~10%.
- Segment margin falling below ~5% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the legacy-contract drag is structural rather than transitional.
- FY26 FCF materially below the $500-600M guide, forcing incremental debt or a capital-return cut.

## Conclusion

HII is a HOLD. The franchise is close to irreplaceable, the demand backdrop is the best in a generation, and the stock's drawdown from the February high has taken valuation to a level where the downside is reasonably protected by moat and backlog. But the drawdown is earned, not a mispricing: Q1 2026 margins declined YoY, FCF was negative, FY26 cash guidance sits below net income, and estimate revisions are flat-to-down rather than inflecting. The turnaround the equity requires is an operational fact not yet visible in reported profit. This is a growth-hold: own it for the demand-backed medium-term thesis and the 2.1% dividend, but the case for adding capital rests on the next one or two quarters showing throughput converting to margin. Until it does, the appropriate stance is patience, with a natural accumulation zone in the low-$250s where the risk/reward turns more clearly asymmetric.
