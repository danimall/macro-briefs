**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Under Armour is a turnaround-in-distress: a distant-fourth performance-apparel brand whose revenue has fallen from roughly $5.9B in FY2023 to approximately $4.97B in FY2026, with profitability swinging from positive to a net loss of ~$201M in FY2025 and wider losses in FY2026. The Q4 FY2026 print on May 12, 2026 (a $43M net loss, adjusted EPS of –$0.03 vs. –$0.01 consensus) and a weak FY2027 guide drove a 16.7% single-day decline, the worst in roughly four years. The core problem is structural rather than cyclical: a brand that has lost demand momentum, competing against Nike, adidas, Puma, and lululemon without scale, a proprietary technology moat, or marquee athlete equity, the last of which was just stripped further by Stephen Curry's departure to China's Li-Ning.

The most distinctive feature of this situation is the contrarian buyer. Fairfax Financial's Prem Watsa, a sophisticated deep-value investor and 10%+ owner, has been accumulating both Class A and Class C shares aggressively through January and again in May 2026, including open-market purchases immediately after the post-earnings crash at ~$4.97–$5.00. Post-transaction holdings reached roughly 44.2M Class A plus ~22M Class C shares. This is the clearest external vote of confidence available in the filings, and it deserves weight. But a single anchor investor's conviction does not convert a structurally declining, loss-making challenger brand into ownable equity for a 12-month horizon, particularly when FY27 profitability is partly contingent on tariff refunds that remain in active litigation.

We rate UAA AVOID with an overall score of 3.4, consistent with the prior trajectory (4.4 → 3.4 → 3.6 across May reports). New data — the enlarged $305M restructuring, the Curry/Li-Ning confirmation, and a FY27 guide leaning on contingent refunds — confirms rather than reverses the negative thesis. We do not see UAA as an active short candidate given 30.2% short interest, a 5-day cover, and Watsa's accumulation creating real squeeze risk.

## Company Overview

Under Armour designs and distributes branded performance apparel, footwear, and accessories. FY2026 revenue split roughly 57% wholesale, 41% direct-to-consumer (443 Brand House/Factory House stores plus e-commerce), and 2% licensing. By product, apparel is ~68% of revenue (HEATGEAR, COLDGEAR, UA RUSH, Iso-Chill), footwear ~22% (HOVR, UA Flow, Charged), accessories ~8%. Geographically, North America is ~58% of revenue (U.S. alone $2.6B), with EMEA/APAC/LatAm combined at ~43%; roughly 48% of revenue is generated outside the U.S.

Founder Kevin Plank returned as President and CEO in April 2024 and controls a majority of voting power through Class B shares (10 votes each). Class A carries one vote; Class C carries none. The company's fiscal year ends March 31.

The competitive position is candidly described in the company's own 10-K: its fabrics and technologies are "not unique," it has limited patent protection, and competitors hold greater financial, distribution, marketing, and digital resources, better brand recognition, and longer retailer relationships. This is a brand-rehabilitation story, not a growth story.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is one of sustained top-line erosion and margin collapse.

- **Revenue:** ~$5.9B (FY2023) → ~$5.7B (FY2024) → ~$5.2B (FY2025) → ~$4.97B (FY2026), a roughly 16% cumulative decline over four years. Q4 FY2026 revenue fell ~1% to $1.2B, with North America down 7% to $641M, partially offset by EMEA +7%, APAC +13%, and LatAm +22%.
- **Profitability:** Net income turned from positive in FY2023/FY2024 to a net loss of ~$201M in FY2025 and wider losses in FY2026. Q4 FY2026 posted a $34M operating loss and a $43M net loss (adjusted net loss $11M).
- **Margin drivers:** tariff-driven product-cost inflation, elevated promotional intensity (especially DTC), and restructuring charges. The restructuring plan has been extended to at least end of CY2026 and enlarged from $255M to $305M, indicating the reset is taking longer and costing more than initially projected.
- **Channel/category (Q4 FY2026):** wholesale –3% ($748M); DTC +5% ($406M), with owned stores +8% but e-commerce flat. Apparel flat ($778M), footwear flat ($282M), accessories +2% ($94M).
- **Liquidity and leverage:** cash of $309M as of March 31, 2026. UA issued new senior notes in June 2025 and terminated a material agreement in August 2025. The credit agreement carries leverage and interest-coverage covenants; UA has previously required covenant relief and been downgraded. With EBITDA declining against fixed covenants, this is the central financial risk to monitor.

A further governance overhang on the financials: UA disclosed material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting for both FY2024 and FY2025 (period-end reporting process and account classification), declared remediated only as of March 31, 2026. Combined with a CFO who arrived in February 2026, the controls track record warrants continued scrutiny even though the current status is "effective."

The earnings-surprise record is genuinely noisy: 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters, including a +537% surprise in the December 2025 quarter and a –69% miss in March 2026. This volatility reflects how thin and unpredictable earnings are at the trough, and it argues against placing high confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

There is no credible near-term growth thesis. FY2027 guidance (issued May 12, 2026) calls for revenue to decline slightly year over year — low-single-digit decline in North America partly offset by low-single-digit EMEA/APAC growth — including roughly 1 point of drag from the Curry Brand exit. Operating income is guided to $96M–$116M, adjusted operating income $140M–$160M, diluted EPS of breakeven to –$0.04, and adjusted EPS $0.08–$0.12.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and net-negative on the metrics that matter most. The current-quarter EPS estimate was revised up +35.9% over 30 days (to $0.02), but the next-quarter estimate was cut –50.4%, the current-year estimate fell –52.5% over 90 days (to $0.11), and the next-year estimate fell –36.1% over 90 days (to $0.24). The headline "stable" characterization masks a meaningful 90-day downgrade in the full-year and forward-year numbers, and the durable revisions point down.

The one bright spot is international, where APAC and LatAm are growing double digits. But at ~43% of revenue collectively, and with North America still ~58% and declining, the international base cannot offset domestic erosion. Management's strategy — refocusing on the 16–24-year-old core consumer, premium product (a $65 pima-cotton tee was the showcase example), a 25% SKU reduction over two years, cleaner inventory, and "marketplace discipline" with wholesale partners — is coherent but unproven, and deliberately shrinking wholesale mechanically pressures the top line further. Management projects North American "stabilization" in FY2027; sell-side reaction was skeptical, with GlobalData seeing no evidence of "sustainable recovery."

## Valuation Assessment

UAA is unprofitable, so trailing and forward P/E are both negative (–4.9x) and uninformative. The cleaner anchors are the analyst target distribution and peer comparisons.

Consensus targets span $4.00 (low) to $12.50 (high), with a mean of $6.28 and a median of $5.50 — bracketing the current $6.06 almost exactly. The wide dispersion reflects genuine disagreement about whether the turnaround succeeds, not a mispricing the market is overlooking.

Peer comps in apparel/luxury show a median P/E of 16.7x: GIII at 12.5x, CRI at 16.7x, CPRI at 32.1x, with OXM (–13.7x) and UA's own Class C (–5.0x) also lossmaking. UAA cannot be valued on earnings multiples because it has no earnings. On a price-to-sales basis, a ~$1.1B market cap against ~$4.97B revenue (~0.23x sales) looks optically cheap, but lossmaking subscale apparel brands routinely trade at depressed sales multiples precisely because the path to durable margin is unclear. Applying the company's own FY27 adjusted operating income midpoint of $150M, even a generous 12–14x EV/EBIT would not justify a dramatically higher equity value once net debt and the contingent nature of that profit (tariff-refund-dependent) are accounted for.

This is the textbook value-trap profile: a compressed multiple that reflects structural impairment rather than mispricing. Cheapness alone is not a buy signal here, and there is no clean independent catalyst to re-rate the stock. The lone genuine valuation argument is the asymmetry that a single large, sophisticated buyer (Fairfax) is willing to pay roughly $4.97–$6.30 in size, which establishes a soft floor but not an upside thesis. We score valuation 4.0: optically cheap, but without a falsifiable mispricing case strong enough to overcome the impairment.

## Competitive Landscape

This is the weakest dimension. UA is a distant challenger in a market dominated by Nike, adidas, Puma, and lululemon, plus growing private-label pressure from its own wholesale customers. The company's brand differentiation has eroded to the point that external observers describe consumers as "confused" about its positioning, and management's elevation of "modern marketing excellence" to its "highest priority" is itself an admission of lost demand momentum.

The Curry departure crystallizes the trajectory. After parting ways in November 2025, Stephen Curry reportedly signed a 10-year deal worth more than $400M with China's Li-Ning (press-reported via ESPN, not an SEC disclosure), taking the Curry Brand with him. UA frames this as ~1 point of FY27 revenue, but the strategic loss is larger: it removes UA's only credible basketball franchise and marquee NBA athlete in the exact footwear/culture category where the brand is weakest, and it hands that equity to a direct competitor. Footwear has never reached the scale or cultural cachet of Nike or adidas, and this exit removes optionality for a footwear-led revival.

The trajectory is deteriorating, not stabilizing. We score competitive position 2.5.

## Risk Assessment

Idiosyncratic risks not fully captured in the depressed price include:

1. **Tariff litigation contingency.** The FY27 profit outlook is partly predicated on anticipated tariff refunds, while the company simultaneously describes active, unresolved litigation: a Feb 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain IEEPA tariffs, replacement tariffs under alternative authority, then a May 7, 2026 Court of International Trade ruling against those (left in effect pending appeal). Roughly 65% of apparel/accessories are made in Jordan, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Cambodia; footwear is almost entirely Vietnam and Indonesia. An adverse outcome could erase the projected adjusted operating income improvement.
2. **Covenant/liquidity risk** against declining EBITDA, $309M cash, and recently issued senior notes.
3. **Restructuring execution** — extended and enlarged, with explicit warning of attrition beyond planned reductions.
4. **Founder voting control** concentrating strategic risk in one person and limiting external accountability.
5. **Recently remediated material weaknesses** plus a four-month-tenured CFO.
6. **Brand-relevance erosion**, the existential risk underlying all of the above.

Tariff cost inflation is squeezing the entire sector, and UA's core 16–24 demographic is discretionary and price-sensitive. [unverified — no macro "H2 2026 consumer cliff" projection appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

### Options Market Signal

The options data is ambiguous and partly corrupted. The reported jump in ATM IV from 52.0% to 2305.8% is almost certainly a thin-options/data artifact rather than a genuine signal and should be treated as non-informative. The more interpretable shift is the put/call open-interest ratio falling from 1.09 to 0.23 alongside a +19.8% price move, which suggests bearish positioning has lightened as the stock recovered off post-earnings lows. The dominant options-adjacent dynamic is squeeze risk, not confirmation of a bearish thesis. That cuts against shorting, not against avoiding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (upside toward the $12.50 street high).** The reset works: North America stabilizes in FY2027 as guided, SKU rationalization and marketplace discipline restore full-price selling and gross margin, the new marketing leadership (ex-adidas CMO Eric Liedtke) re-ignites brand heat with the core youth consumer, international double-digit growth scales, and tariff refunds materialize to support the profit guide. Fairfax's accumulation proves prescient and the stock re-rates toward the $12.50 street high as adjusted EPS inflects from the FY27 guided $0.08–$0.12 range toward the ~$0.24 that next-year estimates currently imply. This requires multiple independent positives to all break right.

**Bear case (~$4.00).** The brand continues to lose relevance, North America declines beyond "low single digit," promotional intensity persists, tariff refunds fail to materialize and the profit guide is missed, restructuring costs expand again, and covenant pressure builds against falling EBITDA. The stock retests the $4.13 52-week low and the analyst-floor $4.00 target. Given a four-year revenue decline, a just-departed marquee athlete, and a sector facing tariff-driven cost pressure, this path carries meaningful probability.

The asymmetry is not clearly favorable. The bull case requires execution on a turnaround that has repeatedly slipped; the bear case requires only continuation of the established trend. Fairfax's buying is the strongest counterweight, but anchoring on one investor's conviction is not a substitute for a base-rate-aware read of a structurally subscale brand.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The next dated catalyst is the FY2027 first-quarter print (expected around early August 2026), which could miss if tariff refunds slip or North American decline accelerates. The tariff-litigation appeal is also a potential negative catalyst but is undated. Neither is a clean, high-probability near-term trigger.

**Timing window.** The most actionable catalyst (Q1 FY27 earnings) is roughly six to eight weeks out from the data window; the tariff appeal is open-ended. Carry over a longer window is unattractive given borrow and squeeze risk.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 30.2% of float (48.4M shares) with a 5-day cover. This is firmly in crowded-short territory, well above the ~25% threshold where squeeze risk dominates. The presence of a large, motivated accumulator (Fairfax) buying into declines sharpens that risk materially — any positive surprise or further large purchase could force covering.

**Borrow and options signal.** The corrupted IV reading prevents a clean cost-to-borrow inference, but high SI typically implies elevated borrow cost. The falling put/call OI ratio into a rising price suggests bears have been covering, not pressing, which is a warning sign for new shorts.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Downside to the $4.00 floor is ~34% from $6.06; upside on a squeeze or tariff-refund confirmation could be 30–50%+ given the 30% short base. With reward roughly matching risk and a crowded book, the asymmetry does not favor an active short.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish fundamental thesis is largely correct, but the crowded short base and an aggressive deep-value buyer make the remaining downside insufficient relative to squeeze risk. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change our view to constructive):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or growing North American revenue (the segment turning from –7% toward flat/positive), confirming the turnaround is taking hold.
- A confirmed, recognized tariff-refund benefit removing the contingency from the FY27 profit guide, plus adjusted EPS delivery at or above guided range.
- Evidence of brand re-ignition in hard data — DTC e-commerce returning to growth (currently flat) and full-price sell-through improving gross margin.
- A 90-day estimate-revision trend that turns clearly positive on the full-year and forward-year numbers.

**Timeline.** We expect the structural headwinds — brand erosion, North American decline, tariff uncertainty, and an unfinished restructuring — to persist through at least FY2027. The first natural reassessment point is the Q1 FY27 print around early August 2026; the second is full-year FY27 results in spring 2027.

**Monitoring note.** Track Fairfax/Watsa Form 4 activity closely. Continued large open-market accumulation would not change our AVOID (the fundamentals govern the rating), but a reversal of that buying would remove the soft price floor and tilt risk decisively to the downside.

## Conclusion

Under Armour is a structurally subscale apparel challenger in a four-year revenue decline, now lossmaking, with eroding brand relevance, a just-departed marquee athlete handed to a Chinese competitor, an unfinished and enlarged restructuring, and a FY27 profit guide that leans on contingent tariff refunds. The estimate-revision trend points down on the metrics that matter, and the sector backdrop of broad tariff cost inflation is unfavorable. The lone differentiated positive is Fairfax/Prem Watsa's aggressive accumulation into the decline, which establishes a credible soft floor and warrants respect, but does not by itself constitute an ownable thesis on a 12-month horizon.

We rate UAA **AVOID** with an overall score of 3.4, in line with the prior trajectory. We do not view it as an active short: the crowded short base and a motivated deep-value buyer create real squeeze risk that makes the remaining downside insufficient relative to that risk. The appropriate posture is to stay out, monitor North American stabilization and tariff-refund recognition as the key re-entry signposts, and revisit after the Q1 FY27 print around early August 2026.
