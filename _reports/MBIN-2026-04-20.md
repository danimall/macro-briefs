## Executive Summary

Merchants Bancorp is a $19.4 billion asset bank holding company based in Carmel, Indiana, operating across three segments: multi-family mortgage banking, mortgage warehousing, and banking. FY2025 was a reset year: net income fell 32% to $219M ($3.78 diluted EPS), driven by $118M in credit loss provisions concentrated in five borrower relationships involving mortgage fraud, and a 34% surge in noninterest expense. The company entered into an FDIC Memorandum of Understanding in June 2025, adding a regulatory overhang.

The countervailing evidence is substantial. The deposit base has shifted from 42% brokered (FY2023) to 13% (FY2025). Warehouse lending volumes grew 46% to $66.3B, double the industry rate. Criticized loans declined 27%, NPLs declined 29%, and tangible book value per share grew 10% to $37.51. Q4 2025 earnings of $1.28 EPS beat estimates by 34%. The stock has rallied roughly 50% from its late-July 2025 lows, reflecting genuine fundamental improvement.

At $49.12, MBIN trades at 1.31x tangible book and approximately 13x trailing EPS that was depressed by abnormal credit costs. On normalized earnings power of $4.50–5.00 per share, the multiple compresses to 10–11x. The stock is near the top of its 52-week range and approaching the analyst consensus target of $48.67. The risk-reward from here is balanced: the recovery thesis has largely been priced, but MOU resolution and sustained credit normalization could support further upside into the mid-$50s over 12 months.

## Company Overview

Merchants Bancorp operates three complementary business segments that create structural synergies:

**Multi-family Mortgage Banking** ($526M assets): Originates and services multi-family housing and healthcare facility loans through FHA, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and Ginnie Mae programs. Ranks among the nation's top agency affordable lenders. Includes a LIHTC tax credit equity syndication platform that has raised $2.8 billion since 2020 and a registered investment advisor (MIP) deploying third-party institutional capital.

**Mortgage Warehousing** ($7.3B assets): One of the largest warehouse lenders nationally, providing short-term financing to non-depository mortgage bankers. Funded $66.3 billion in loan principal in 2025 (up 46% YoY versus a 22% industry increase). Capital-efficient with minimal credit risk since underlying loans are agency-eligible and held for days to weeks.

**Banking** ($11.3B assets): Portfolio lending for multi-family and healthcare bridge/construction loans, residential mortgage banking (retail and correspondent), SBA lending, agricultural lending, and community banking through seven Indiana branches.

The operational model is distinctly asset-sensitive: approximately 96% of loans reprice within three months. The company employs roughly 735 people, with an 8% turnover rate. Founding family ownership (CEO Michael Petrie and partners) amounts to approximately 57% of common shares.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability Deterioration and Recovery Path

FY2025 represented a meaningful step-down across all profitability metrics:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Net Income | $279M | $320M | $219M |
| Diluted EPS | $5.64 | $6.30 | $3.78 |
| ROA | 1.85% | 1.79% | 1.16% |
| ROE | 17.63% | 16.86% | 9.88% |
| Efficiency Ratio | 31.03% | 33.37% | 44.01% |
| NIM | 3.06% | 3.03% | 2.86% |

The three drivers: (1) Provisions surged from $24M to $118M, driven by $124M in charge-offs concentrated in five fraud-related borrower relationships. (2) Noninterest expense rose 34% to $300M, with $35.8M in added staffing, $14.7M in credit default swap premiums, $14M in collateral preservation costs on NPLs, and $5.6M in higher FDIC insurance. (3) NIM compressed 17 bps as asset yields fell 91 bps while liability costs fell only 81 bps.

Q4 2025 showed clear improvement: $1.28 EPS versus $0.96 estimate (34% beat), following a $0.97 Q3 print that beat by 21%. The two prior quarters missed meaningfully ($0.60 vs. $1.14 in Q2, $1.04 vs. $1.28 in Q1), coinciding with the fraud discovery and initial charge-off recognition.

### Balance Sheet Strength

Capital ratios improved through FY2025 despite the earnings hit:
- CET1: 9.9% (up from 9.3%)
- Tier 1 capital: 13.1% holdco / 12.8% bank
- Tangible common equity / tangible assets: 8.9% (five-year high)
- Tangible book value per share: $37.51 (up 10% YoY, compounding at ~20% CAGR since 2021)

The deposit transformation is the most important balance sheet development. Brokered deposits declined from 42% of total deposits in FY2023 to 13% in FY2025, while core deposits grew 39% to $11.3B. This de-risks the funding base and reduces sensitivity to wholesale funding market disruptions.

Total unused borrowing capacity stands at $5.3B, and when combined with held-for-sale loans, approximately 60% of assets are liquid.

Preferred stock outstanding totals approximately $551M across Series C, D, and E, creating a $41M annual preferred dividend burden. Series C is callable starting April 2026 at 6.0%—a likely redemption candidate given the rate environment.

### Cash Flow Consideration

Operating cash flow was negative $341M in FY2025 (negative $835M in FY2024). This is structural to the originate-to-sell model: loans originated for sale consume operating cash but generate investing cash flows upon disposition. The business model depends on functioning secondary markets, which introduces fragility in periods of severe market dislocation.

## Growth Analysis

Multiple growth vectors support the recovery thesis:

**Warehouse Lending (strongest driver):** $66.3B funded in 2025, growing at 2x the industry rate. Management attributes this to competitor exits. The MBA forecasts 7% growth in single-family originations for 2026, providing a base-rate volume tailwind. Warehouse lending is the most capital-efficient segment and generates custodial deposits that strengthen the funding base.

**LIHTC Syndication:** Raised $700.7M in equity in 2025, a 20% increase in syndication fees. The platform provides diversified fee income plus tax benefits that reduced the effective tax rate from 24.2% to 17.1%. This is a durable advantage as long as affordable housing tax credits persist.

**Agency Multi-family Expansion:** GSE multifamily purchase caps were expanded to $176B combined for 2026, the largest in recent years. A more permissive regulatory posture toward agency lending supports volume growth in the core origination business.

**Gain on Sale Income:** Grew 37% to $85.4M in FY2025, demonstrating the originate-to-sell model's strength even during a difficult credit year.

Normalized earnings power, assuming credit costs return to the $20–40M range and expense growth moderates, suggests $4.50–5.00 in diluted EPS is achievable by FY2026/FY2027. The forward P/E of approximately 12.9x on the current consensus of $3.80 likely underestimates the recovery trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $49.12, MBIN trades at:
- 1.31x tangible book value ($37.51/share)
- 13.0x trailing EPS ($3.78, depressed by abnormal credit costs)
- ~12.9x forward P/E on current consensus
- 10–11x on normalized earnings of $4.50–5.00
- 0.9% dividend yield ($0.40 annualized)

The analyst consensus target of $48.67 (range $46–$51, 3 analysts: 2 Strong Buy, 1 Hold) suggests limited near-term upside from current levels. The stock is 1% below the 52-week high of $49.51.

A bank generating 15–17% ROE (MBIN's pre-crisis range) on a growing tangible book value base would typically trade at 1.5–2.0x TBV among specialty bank peers. At 1.31x current TBV, there is valuation room if the earnings trajectory normalizes. However, the MOU overhang and elevated CRE concentration (324% of Tier 1 capital) justify a discount to historical multiples.

The 50% price appreciation since the July 2025 lows already reflects significant recovery expectations. The remaining upside requires demonstrated credit normalization and MOU resolution.

## Competitive Landscape

**Warehouse Lending:** MBIN's 46% volume growth (2x industry) and competitor exits suggest increasing market share in a structurally important niche. The capital efficiency of this business (short duration, agency-eligible collateral) and the custodial deposit generation create a flywheel that is difficult for competitors to replicate without comparable scale.

**Agency Multi-family Lending:** Top-ranked nationally. The end-to-end capability (origination, servicing, LIHTC syndication, MIP capital deployment) provides developers a single counterparty solution. This vertical integration is a genuine competitive moat.

**Lean Operations:** 735 employees managing $19.4B in assets produces extraordinary asset-per-employee productivity. The 8% turnover rate suggests strong organizational culture and institutional knowledge retention.

**Key vulnerabilities:** GSE reform or privatization could disrupt the agency lending model. Larger banks and fintech competitors apply pressure in warehouse lending. The 57% insider ownership, while aligning incentives, creates governance concentration risk.

## Risk Assessment

### Fraud Investigation (HIGH)

The 10-K references "ongoing investigation of borrowers involved in mortgage fraud or suspected fraud" multiple times. While management states losses have been "largely identified," the word "ongoing" creates residual uncertainty. Seventy percent of $124M in charge-offs were concentrated in five relationships. The company discontinued certain subordinated loan products as a result. Additional discoveries would directly impact earnings and could extend the MOU.

### FDIC Memorandum of Understanding (HIGH)

The June 2025 MOU requires maintenance of certain capital thresholds, management of asset concentrations, and implementation of risk mitigation plans. While the bank currently exceeds MOU capital minimums, the MOU may limit expansion and restrict dividends. Resolution timing is unknown. This overhang constrains the multiple.

### CRE Concentration (MODERATE-HIGH)

Multi-family loans represent 48% of loans receivable ($5.3B). CRE concentration at 324% of Tier 1 capital, while declining from 348%, remains elevated by regulatory standards. In a macro environment where multi-family property values face pressure from higher rates and slowing rent growth, this concentration is a material risk.

### GSE Reform (MODERATE)

MBIN is fundamentally dependent on Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Ginnie Mae, and FHA programs. The current administration's pro-banking posture is a tailwind, but any structural reform of the GSEs would reshape the competitive landscape. The $783M in Freddie Mac securitizations in FY2025 demonstrates the depth of this dependency.

### Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE)

With 96% of loans repricing within three months, NIM is directly sensitive to the short end. Further rate cuts would compress NIM (each 100 bps cut reduces net interest income meaningfully). Conversely, a rate hike cycle—a non-trivial probability given the current inflation trajectory—would benefit NIM but potentially stress already-weakened multi-family borrowers.

### Key Person Risk (MODERATE)

CEO Petrie and the founding family hold ~57% of common stock. The company is "highly dependent" on Petrie and COO Dunlap per the 10-K. Petrie does not have a non-compete agreement.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 51.6% significantly exceeds 1-year historical volatility of 37.5% (14.2pp spread). The term structure is in backwardation (51.6% near-term vs. 37.8% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event risk or elevated near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew at 4.1% shows modestly elevated put demand. The OI put/call ratio of 0.15 is extremely call-heavy, but with only 25 contracts across 3 expirations, the options market is too thin to draw meaningful institutional positioning conclusions. The rich IV relative to HV suggests the market assigns higher-than-historical probability to a sharp move; for investors considering entry, this is not a cheap-protection environment.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55–60, 12–18 months)

Credit costs normalize to $20–40M annually as the fraud-related charge-offs cycle through. NIM stabilizes or expands if the Fed holds rates steady. Warehouse volumes continue growing above industry rates. The MOU is resolved by mid-2026, removing the expansion constraint and valuation discount. Normalized EPS of $5.00+ on a growing tangible book (approaching $40+) warrants 1.5–1.7x TBV, or $60–68. The LIHTC platform continues reducing effective tax rate. Series C preferred redemption at 6.0% saves ~$15M in annual preferred dividends.

### Bear Case ($32–36, 12 months)

Additional fraud losses surface from the ongoing investigation. MOU requirements tighten, forcing capital raises at dilutive prices. Multi-family property values decline further, requiring additional provisions. NIM compresses on further rate cuts. The stock reverts toward tangible book value (~$37.51) or below if market confidence in earnings recovery falters. A broader credit event in CRE markets would disproportionately impact MBIN given its concentration levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
- Stock pulls back below $42 (1.12x TBV), providing a more favorable entry with the same recovery thesis intact
- MOU formally resolved, removing the regulatory overhang and expansion constraint
- Q1 2026 earnings demonstrate continued credit normalization (provisions under $15M, NPL ratio declining)
- Insider buying: open-market purchases by Petrie or other C-suite executives would signal confidence in the recovery path

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
- Disclosure of additional material fraud losses or expansion of the investigation scope
- MOU terms tightened or elevated to a formal enforcement action (Cease & Desist)
- CET1 ratio falls below 9.0% or Tier 1 capital ratio falls below 12.0%
- Two consecutive quarters of EPS below $0.90, suggesting the earnings recovery has stalled

### Reassessment Timeline
Next earnings report (likely late April/early May 2026 for Q1 2026 results) is the primary reassessment point. Credit quality trends, provision levels, and any MOU-related commentary will determine whether the recovery thesis is tracking.

## Conclusion

Merchants Bancorp is recovering from mortgage fraud losses, FDIC intervention, and a 32% earnings decline, and the trajectory is positive. The deposit transformation, warehouse market share gains, LIHTC platform expansion, and improving credit metrics all support a normalization path toward $4.50–5.00 in EPS.

The challenge for new capital is timing. The stock has already rallied 50% from its lows, trades near its 52-week high, and sits above the analyst consensus target. The MOU remains unresolved, the fraud investigation is described as "ongoing," and insider activity shows selling rather than buying. Near-term IV is rich, suggesting the market prices elevated uncertainty.

The correct posture is HOLD with a growth orientation: this is a company with a clear earnings recovery path and competitive advantages, but the stock price already reflects significant improvement. A pullback toward $42 or demonstrated MOU resolution would create a more compelling entry point.
