## Executive Summary

CNX Resources is a low-cost Appalachian natural gas producer trading at 5.1x forward earnings with a $5.2B market cap against $6.83B PV-10 reserves. The company generated $534M in free cash flow in FY2025, beat Q1 2026 EPS estimates by 27% ($1.20 vs. $0.94), and holds $2.4B in remaining buyback authorization — equivalent to 46% of its current market cap. The per-share value compounding story is intact: the share count is declining rapidly while production grows through operational efficiency and acquisitions.

The reason this stock does not warrant a BUY despite its apparent cheapness is specific and falsifiable: **near-term quarterly estimates are declining sharply (current quarter -19.4% over 30 days, next quarter -21.7%), the heavy 2026 hedge book ($2.74/Mcf on 73% of production) mechanically caps earnings upside in a rising gas price environment, and 19.3% short interest with 9.4 days to cover signals that institutional investors with informed views believe the stock is overvalued at current levels relative to its realized pricing power.** The consensus sell-side view is bearish (2 sells, 1 strong sell, 8 holds, 1 buy), and CNX's cumulative TSR has underperformed its peer group by ~190 percentage points over the measurement period. The structural demand thesis (data centers, LNG exports, coal retirements) is real but operates on a 3-7 year timeline — the market is unlikely to pay for it until basis differentials demonstrably tighten.

## Company Overview

CNX Resources operates as an integrated natural gas development, production, and midstream company concentrated in the Appalachian Basin (Marcellus and Utica Shale). The company holds 9.7 Tcfe of proved reserves across ~557,000 net Marcellus acres and ~612,000 net Utica acres, with ~2,600 miles of owned gathering pipelines. Production reached 629 Bcfe in FY2025, grown 91% over ten years. The operation is remarkably lean at just 390 employees.

CNX differentiates through vertical integration (owned midstream captures margin), ultra-low production costs ($1.53/Mcfe all-in including DD&A), and a unique environmental attributes business generating ~$78M annually from methane capture at abandoned coal mines. The company's stated primary financial objective is maximizing adjusted FCF per share through aggressive buybacks.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** FY2025 net income was $633M ($3.98/share) on production revenue of $1.73B (up 18% YoY). The improvement was driven by a 51% increase in realized natural gas prices ($2.99/Mcf vs. $1.98). Production margins expanded to $1.06/Mcfe from $0.94/Mcfe. However, hedge losses of $181M in cash settlements demonstrate the cap on price participation.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $1.03B against $495M in capex produced $534M in FCF — a 31% FCF margin on production revenue. At the current $5.2B market cap, this represents a ~10.3% FCF yield.

**Capital Allocation:** CNX deployed $528M in buybacks (16.9M shares at avg $31.00) plus $518M for the Apex acquisition in 2025. The $2.4B remaining buyback authorization provides years of runway at current pace.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $2.43B against $4.34B equity (1.1x D/E). The maturity profile is manageable post-refinancing: the 2029 notes were extended to 8-year 5.875% notes, and the $209M convertible notes matured May 1, 2026 (converted to ~12M net shares). The 0.44 current ratio is genuinely tight but addressed by revolver capacity and strong operating cash flow. No debt matures before 2029.

**Q1 2026:** EPS of $1.20 beat the $0.94 estimate by 27%. Revenue grew 53-67% YoY. This was the fourth consecutive earnings beat (average surprise +50% over four quarters).

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Growth:** Production has been flat-to-modest growth organically (~1.7 Bcfe/day maintained), with the Apex acquisition adding incremental volumes. Management prioritizes per-share growth via buybacks over absolute production growth — a deliberate strategy.

**Per-Share Growth:** With ~$500M+ in annual buybacks reducing the denominator by 8-10% per year, per-share metrics compound even with flat production. FY2025 diluted EPS of $3.98 compares to consensus of $3.02 for FY2026 (reflecting lower near-term gas prices in hedge-weighted results) and $4.28 for FY2027.

**Structural Demand Tailwinds:** U.S. power burn expected to hit 40.3 Bcf/d in summer 2026. Multiple 9-GW power plant proposals in Appalachian Basin. Data center growth requiring gas-fired baseload. Coal plant retirements removing capacity through 2030. LNG exports hit records (11.7M tons, March 2026). These are real drivers but operate on a 3-7 year timeline for Appalachian basis differential tightening.

**Utica Shale Optionality:** 612,000 net acres largely undrilled. Only 3 wells turned in-line Q1 2026. If well results (expected late 2026/early 2027) are strong, this provides decades of additional inventory beyond the Marcellus.

## Valuation Assessment

CNX trades at compelling absolute and relative multiples:
- Forward P/E: 5.1x (vs. peer median 17.3x)
- P/PV-10: 0.76x ($5.2B market cap vs. $6.83B PV-10)
- FCF Yield: ~10.3% (based on FY2025 FCF of $534M)
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 4-5x on trailing figures

The discount to peer median P/E (17.3x) appears extreme but reflects several structural factors: heavy hedging caps upside participation, Appalachian basis discounts compress realized prices, CNX pays no dividend (peers yield 2.6% median), and the company has persistently underperformed its peer group on TSR (340.8 vs. 531.8 cumulative).

The PV-10 discount is the strongest valuation signal — at $47/share implied PV-10 per share vs. a $37 stock price, the market is pricing in either (a) sustained gas price declines below the SEC pricing assumption ($3.387 Henry Hub), or (b) execution risk on reserve development. Given the current geopolitical environment supporting elevated energy prices, this appears overly pessimistic.

However, the negative near-term estimate revisions indicate the market expects a weaker near-term pricing environment than the trailing results suggest. The 2026 hedge book at $2.74/Mcf is below current spot and forward curves, creating a mathematical ceiling on near-term earnings surprises.

## Competitive Landscape

CNX's competitive position within Appalachian gas is strong on cost structure and asset quality:
- All-in production costs of $1.53/Mcfe (including DD&A) are among the lowest in the basin
- Owned midstream infrastructure captures incremental margin
- Gas blending capability reduces processing costs
- 99%+ of unproved acreage held-by-production — no lease expiration pressure

The company's competitive weakness is geographic concentration and the Appalachian basis discount. While peers (CRK, EQT) share this constraint, it depresses realized pricing relative to Henry Hub by $0.20-0.50/Mcf. The structural demand thesis would address this over time.

Environmental attributes ($78M revenue) represent a genuinely unique revenue stream among E&P peers, though declining from $95M (2024) and subject to regulatory uncertainty around 45Z tax credits.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**
1. **Appalachian basis widening** — If in-basin demand growth disappoints or pipeline egress increases, basis differentials could remain wide, compressing realized prices below hedge levels.
2. **Hedge ceiling on upside** — 73% of 2026 production hedged at $2.74/Mcf means the company cannot fully participate in gas price rallies. This is the specific mechanism by which the stock underperforms in a bullish energy environment.
3. **Short interest at 19.3%** with 9.4 days to cover — Institutional shorts likely express a view that the stock's valuation advantage is illusory given the hedge book constraints and that buybacks at $31/share (2025 average) look less accretive at $37+.
4. **CEO transition risk** — Alan Shepard took over January 2026. Execution continuity is unproven. DeIuliis remains as director with $87M in stock, providing alignment but also potential governance complexity.
5. **Current ratio of 0.44** — While manageable through revolver access, this is genuinely tight and creates vulnerability in a sustained downturn.
6. **Regulatory risk** — Cecil Township's 2,500-foot setback ordinance, if replicated statewide, would constrain drilling inventory.

## Conclusion

CNX Resources trades at 5.1x forward earnings with 10%+ FCF yield, backed by $6.83B in PV-10 reserves and a $2.4B buyback authorization that mechanically compounds per-share value. The company has beaten estimates four consecutive quarters by an average of 50%.

The specific constraint preventing a BUY recommendation is the **convergence of three falsifiable conditions**: (1) quarterly estimate revisions are declining at -20% pace, indicating near-term earnings momentum is negative; (2) the 2026 hedge book at $2.74/Mcf on 73% of production mathematically caps the company's ability to surprise to the upside this year; and (3) 19.3% short interest with 9.4 days to cover, combined with bearish sell-side consensus (2 sells, 1 strong sell vs. 1 buy), indicates informed institutional skepticism that I cannot dismiss without a specific catalyst to force covering.

The structural demand thesis (data centers, LNG, coal retirements) is genuine and represents the path to re-rating, but it operates on a 3-7 year timeline. The stock is a HOLD with a growth thesis: per-share value compounds at 8-10% annually through buybacks alone, the Utica represents upside optionality, and any of the three upgrade triggers above would shift the risk/reward decisively in favor of ownership.
