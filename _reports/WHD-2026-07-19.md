**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cactus is a Houston-based designer and manufacturer of pressure control equipment (wellheads, production trees, frac/flow-control) and spoolable steel-reinforced pipe (FlexSteel), serving onshore oil & gas producers. The company runs a debt-free balance sheet, generated ~$220M of free cash flow in 2025 on a ~20% FCF margin, and carries an Altman Z-Score of 4.12. On legacy financial-strength metrics it is one of the healthiest names in oilfield equipment. The complication is that its two organic engines both contracted in 2025 — total revenue fell 4.5% to $1,079M as the U.S. land rig count dropped ~6% and WTI averaged ~$65 — and management has responded by buying inorganic international growth through the January 1, 2026 acquisition of 65% of Baker Hughes' former surface pressure control business (now "Cactus International") for $344.5M in cash.

The transaction transformed the reported income statement and introduced the dominant question in the story. Q1 2026 revenue jumped 38.5% YoY to $388M, of which $126.9M (33% of consolidated) came from the newly consolidated JV, but that JV posted a $0.5M net loss on purchase-accounting drag, operating income fell to $49.5M from $68.6M a year earlier, and the headline GAAP figure was a $(0.70)/share loss. That loss is largely an accounting artifact: an $81.5M non-cash accretion of the redeemable Baker Hughes non-controlling interest (a put/call remeasurement treated as a deemed dividend). Operating net income attributable to Cactus was a positive $32.9M. A screen reading GAAP EPS would misclassify a cash-generative business as loss-making, and this accretion line will recur every quarter as the put is remeasured against JV EBITDA.

We rate WHD a HOLD. The bull case rests on a fortress balance sheet, a step-change to global scale, and a natural-gas / AI-datacenter power-demand tailwind. The bear case is concrete and dated: the acquired Middle East plants (UAE, Saudi Arabia) are being disrupted by the February 2026 Iran/US/Israel conflict, which impairs the very EBITDA that underpins a capped-but-mandatory ~$530-660M put obligation exercisable from January 1, 2028; the founders sold ~$20M of stock in early March and set up a 1.2M-share 10b5-1 plan with no offsetting open-market buying; and at ~51x trailing / ~15-19x forward earnings the stock is not priced for the integration and geopolitical risk it now carries. The July 29 print is the first clean quarter to gauge JV run-rate profitability and war impact, and the natural reassessment point.

## Company Overview

Cactus operates two segments:

- **Pressure Control** (~66% of 2025 revenue, $717M): wellheads, production trees, and frac/flow-control equipment sold and rented across the drilling, completion, and production phases under the Cactus Wellhead brand. One of the largest U.S. wellhead suppliers, with manufacturing in Bossier City (LA), Suzhou (China), and Hai Duong (Vietnam).
- **Spoolable Technologies** (~34%, $368M): FlexSteel spoolable steel-reinforced pipe and fittings for production/gathering/takeaway, manufactured in Baytown (TX), acquired February 2023 for ~$659M.

Revenue mix is roughly 76% product, 8% rental, 16% field service. Cactus serves 300+ customers, but a single customer represented 17% of 2025 revenue, up from 15% in 2024 and 10% in 2023 — a rising concentration.

The company is founder-controlled through an Up-C structure. Cactus Inc. owns 86.6% of Cactus Companies LLC; CC Unit Holders (largely the Bender family via Cactus WH Enterprises, ~12.1% voting) hold the balance through Class B shares redeemable 1-for-1 into Class A. A Tax Receivable Agreement obligates Cactus Inc. to pay 85% of realized tax savings to legacy owners, a ~$264.8M liability at 3/31/2026, with a change-of-control acceleration estimated near $251M that acts as a structural takeover deterrent.

## Financial Analysis

**Legacy strength is real.** At FY2025: current ratio 5.81, D/E 0.36, ROE 16.4%, ROA 10.8%, essentially debt-free ($17M of finance leases, no bank borrowings). Operating cash flow was $258M and FCF $220M on just $39M of capex. This is a capital-light manufacturer with genuine cash generation.

**2025 was a cyclical down year.** Operating income fell 13.5% to $250.5M; net income to Cactus Inc. fell 10.5% to $166M. Margins compressed on two identifiable pressures: escalated Section 232 steel tariffs on Vietnam/China imports plus higher domestic steel costs, and increased legal reserves tied to the Cameron/SLB SafeLink patent suit.

**Q1 2026 is transformed and hard to read.** The balance sheet ballooned — total assets $1.87B to $2.46B, inventories $277M to $404M, receivables $164M to $460M (including $170.8M unbilled), goodwill +$45M and intangibles +$216M from purchase accounting. Working-capital build of this scale is normal in a consolidation quarter, but it is a line to watch for how quickly receivables convert.

**Deal financing preserved the balance sheet.** The purchase (plus a $45.5M minimum-cash payment) was funded entirely from cash on hand, held in escrow at year-end as $371M restricted cash. In December 2025 Cactus amended its ABL to a $225M revolver extended to 2030 and added a $100M delayed-draw term loan, both currently undrawn. Cash fell from $494.6M (including restricted) to $291.6M after funding the deal, with $97.8M still earmarked for JV restructuring. Capital return continued in modest form — the dividend was raised to $0.14/quarter and $146.3M of repurchase authorization remains — though no open-market buybacks occurred in Q1 as cash was conserved.

**Earnings quality signal.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters (8.6% Q1 2026, 11.5% Q4 2025, 13.7% Q3 2025, then a -9.2% miss in Q2 2025) supports reasonable confidence in near-term consensus, with the caveat that the JV now injects a large new variable that management itself has limited visibility into. A June 2, 2026 8-K flagged a new material definitive agreement and direct financial obligation (Items 1.01/2.03) that was not read in full — a signal that JV-related financing activity may be starting, and the single most important near-term follow-up.

## Growth Analysis

The honest read is that Cactus has stalled organically and is buying growth. Ex-acquisitions, revenue declined two years running: Pressure Control fell in both 2024 and 2025, and Spoolable fell 9.5% in 2025. The Baker Hughes JV converts a mature, cyclically-soft U.S. core into a globally-scaled business with meaningful Middle East exposure, adding $228.8M of intangibles (15-year customer relationships, 10-year developed tech, 1-year backlog) and $537.5M of remaining performance obligations at 3/31/2026.

Estimate revisions are mixed. Current-year consensus has drifted down (-2.4% over 30 days, -1.6% over 90) to $2.91, but next-year has risen (+4.3% over 30 days, +5.5% over 90) to $3.59 — the market is modeling a JV run-rate uplift beyond a soft 2026. This is short of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine inflection; it is a modest, forward-weighted improvement layered onto a soft near-term base.

The structural tailwind is natural gas, up 61% in 2025 on AI-datacenter power-demand optimism, against weak oil. Q1 2026 saw a WTI spike toward ~$103 on the Iran conflict, but U.S. land drilling stayed flat on E&P capital discipline while international land rose ~1%, led by the Middle East — the exact region where the JV now operates and where war is disrupting activity. The growth thesis has a real engine, but its near-term realization is entangled with the same geopolitical risk that threatens it.

## Valuation Assessment

At $54.50, WHD trades at ~51x trailing EPS of ~$2.41 (depressed by the JV accounting artifact) and roughly 15-19x forward earnings on the $2.91 current-year / $3.59 next-year consensus. The trailing multiple is distorted by the non-cash NCI accretion and is not the right anchor; forward is the operative metric.

Peer medians in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services sit near 15.8x P/E (TDW 12.4x, OII 11.8x, WFRD 15.8x, LBRT 30.8x). On next-year EPS of $3.59, WHD at ~15x is roughly peer-median; on current-year $2.91 it is ~19x, a premium. The premium is defensible on balance-sheet quality and FCF margin, but it is not a margin-of-safety setup. The dividend yield is ~1.0%, thin relative to the risk being underwritten.

We do not treat the cheapness case as compelling here. A "cheap" screen in energy equipment has historically been a poor buy signal absent a specific catalyst, and the identifiable catalysts here are two-sided: a clean JV run-rate print on July 29 could re-rate the stock upward, but the 2028 put obligation and war disruption cut the other way. The stock has retraced from its $64.30 52-week high to $54.50 (a ~15% pullback), a moderate move consistent with digesting a messy quarter and rising war risk rather than a mispricing to lean into. We assign a below-average valuation score: the forward multiple is fair-to-full for the risk profile, and there is no identified independent catalyst offering asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

**Pressure Control** competes against Vault and divisions of SLB and TechnipFMC; the frac-stack rental market is fragmented with no player above 20%. The Baker Hughes transaction removes a surface-pressure-control competitor and converts it into a JV partner, consolidating the space and taking Cactus from a U.S.-onshore specialist to a global wellhead supplier.

**Spoolable Technologies** competes with Baker Hughes (now also a JV partner, an unusual dynamic), Mattr, and NOV in spoolable pipe, plus Tenaris/Vallourec in traditional steel line pipe. This segment shrank 9.5% in 2025.

The trajectory is mixed. Scale and international diversification improve the moat, but the U.S. core is losing organic volume, the 17% customer concentration is rising, and the newly acquired international revenue is itself concentrated in a few customers and now sits in a war zone. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving on scale, offset by organic softness and integration risk that has not yet proven accretive.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk here is elevated and not fully priced.

**Baker Hughes put/call (most material).** Baker Hughes retains 35% of Cactus International with a Put Right beginning January 1, 2028 at 6x TTM Adjusted EBITDA, capped at $660M (minimum $530M if Cactus elects to buy), plus deferred payments of $10M and $14.5M. The 10-Q explicitly states Cactus "may not have sufficient liquidity" if exercised and may need external financing on unfavorable terms. The $81.5M quarterly accretion is the accounting shadow of this obligation and will recur.

**Middle East war (new risk).** The February 2026 Iran/US/Israel conflict is directly disrupting the JV's UAE and Saudi plants, impeding the Strait of Hormuz supply chain, and causing "significant delays and decreased order activities." This threatens the JV EBITDA that sets the put price — a squeeze in which Cactus is contractually restricted from actions that reduce the Exit Price yet bears the operating disruption. Management "cannot estimate the potential impact." This is the specific, undated-but-live catalyst for downside.

**Integration and unknown liabilities.** Cactus had "no significant infrastructure in most of the countries where the JV operates" and relies on Baker Hughes transition services; R&W insurance is limited.

**Tariffs.** Section 232 50% steel tariffs are actively compressing margins; a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling deemed certain tariffs unlawful (Cactus filed refund claims) but the most material Section 232 exposure is unchanged.

**Cyclicality, concentration, and TRA.** 2026 U.S. drilling is flagged as likely lower; one customer is 17% of revenue; the ~$251M TRA acceleration is a change-of-control and liquidity overhang.

**Legal.** The Cameron/SLB SafeLink patent suit drove elevated 2025 reserves; trial delayed, outcome unpredictable.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.9% sits just below one-year historical volatility of 41.7% (a -2.8% spread, fairly priced), but the term structure is in backwardation — near-term 38.9% exceeds six-month 35.3%, and ATM IV rose 5.0pp over the past month while the price was flat. That combination indicates the market is pricing an imminent event, consistent with the July 29 earnings date. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.3%. Put/call skew is balanced (1.2%), so the market is pricing uncertainty rather than a directional collapse. This confirms the HOLD stance: the setup is binary into the print, not a clean directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The JV delivers a clean, profitable run-rate as purchase-accounting drag lapses, giving Cactus durable international scale and diversification away from a soft U.S. core. Natural-gas / AI-datacenter power demand sustains gas-directed drilling. The debt-free balance sheet and $220M+ FCF fund the 2028 put internally, converting the biggest overhang into a non-event. Next-year estimates ($3.59) prove conservative and the stock re-rates toward the $67 median / $73 high analyst target.

**Bear case.** War disruption in the UAE/Saudi plants suppresses JV EBITDA into 2027-28, forcing Cactus to fund a $530-660M put with external financing at unfavorable rates just as it exhausts its cash cushion, turning a fortress balance sheet into a levered one. The U.S. core stays soft, tariffs keep compressing margins, and the recurring accretion line keeps confusing the reported picture. Insider selling proves prescient. The stock retests the low-$40s toward the $50 analyst floor and below.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction ahead of the first clean JV quarter.

**Reassessment timeline:** July 29, 2026 Q2 earnings — the first quarter to assess JV run-rate profitability and quantify the war impact. Also monitor the June 2, 2026 financing 8-K for evidence of debt drawdown.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Q2 shows the JV operating profitably at scale (JV segment operating margin positive and EBITDA tracking toward the put-valuation assumptions), with consolidated operating income recovering toward prior-year levels.
- Management quantifies a bounded, manageable war impact rather than "cannot estimate," and demonstrates a funded path for the 2028 put (internal cash + undrawn $325M credit sufficient without dilution).
- Next-year estimate revisions accelerate above +10% alongside evidence of gas-directed U.S. drilling recovery.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- War disruption materially cuts JV order activity, with management guiding JV EBITDA down and signaling the put may require external financing on unfavorable terms.
- Cactus begins drawing debt for JV/put-related purposes at scale, eroding the balance-sheet mitigant that underwrites the whole thesis.
- Continued organic decline in both segments plus further insider selling under the 1.2M-share 10b5-1 plan with no offsetting activity, and a negative Cameron/SLB litigation development.

**Base-case framing:** at ~15-17x next-year EPS of $3.59, fair value clusters around $54-61 — roughly the current price, consistent with a hold. Upside to the $67 median target requires the bull-case JV run-rate to confirm; downside toward the $50 floor requires the war/put risk to bite.

## Conclusion

Cactus is a high-quality manufacturer that has taken a large, transformative bet at an awkward moment. The balance sheet is a genuine asset (debt-free, $220M+ FCF, $325M of undrawn capacity) and it is precisely what makes the Baker Hughes JV survivable. But the story now hinges on integrating an acquired Middle East business while war disrupts it, against a capped-but-mandatory 2028 put obligation whose price is set by the very EBITDA being impaired. Layer on two years of organic revenue decline, rising customer concentration, and founder selling with a sell plan and no open-market buying, and the risk profile is materially higher than the fortress-balance-sheet headline suggests.

The score trajectory has drifted from 6.3 in April to 5.7 in June, driven by a management-quality de-rating (insider selling around a leverage-adding, war-exposed deal) and a modest financial-health softening (the JV messiness), partly offset by improving growth prospects (inorganic scale). New data confirms that downward drift rather than reversing it. At ~15-19x forward, roughly peer-median for a name carrying above-peer idiosyncratic risk, the valuation offers no clear margin of safety. We maintain HOLD and would revisit on the July 29 print, upgrading if the JV proves profitable and the put path is demonstrably funded internally, downgrading if war disruption forces external financing.
