## Executive Summary

Bloomin' Brands reported Q2 FY2026 results on August 5, 2026 that beat consensus on both lines — revenue of $1.02B (+1.3%) against roughly $999M expected, adjusted diluted EPS of $0.39 against a $0.28 non-GAAP consensus — and raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $0.90-$1.00. The equity repriced roughly 41% in a single session, from an $8.92 close to $12.56, taking the stock to a 52-week high and above the highest published analyst target of $11.00.

The arithmetic underneath the raise is less encouraging than the price reaction. FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS from continuing operations was $1.14. The raised FY2026 range of $0.90-$1.00 therefore implies a 12%-21% year-over-year decline, the third consecutive down year against a FY2023 peak of $2.78. H1 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $1.06 ($0.67 + $0.39) already exceeds the top of the full-year range, so management is guiding to H2 adjusted EPS of roughly −$0.16 to −$0.06, consistent with the Q3 guide of −$0.22 to −$0.27. That Q3 range sits *below* the pre-print consensus of −$0.19, meaning the full-year raise was delivered alongside a softer-than-expected near quarter and an implicitly stronger Q4. Q4 is now the load-bearing quarter for the guide.

Two structural facts define the case. First, positive comparable sales are being produced by check and mix while guest counts fall: FY2025 U.S. comps +0.2% on traffic −1.4%; Q1 FY2026 U.S. comps +0.9% with Outback comps −0.3% on traffic −2.4%. The Wall Street Journal attributed the Q2 beat to a larger average check. Second, H1 GAAP earnings were substantially tax-driven — Q1 pretax income rose $0.6M (+1.4%) while the tax line swung ~$12.5M favorably to a $10.3M benefit at a −22.0% effective rate, explaining roughly 96% of the increase in continuing-operations net income. The mechanism (fixed FICA tip credits measured against a low forecast of full-year pretax income) means the tax tailwind cancels itself as pretax income recovers. Any model that assumes both operating recovery and a sustained negative tax rate double-counts.

We move from HOLD to HOLD, with the score edging down to 4.1 from 4.5. The business is marginally better than it was in June; the stock is substantially higher (roughly +55% from the June 18 close of $8.06).

Base case fair value sits at roughly spot, with a wide two-sided distribution around it.

## Company Overview

Bloomin' Brands operates and franchises four casual-dining concepts. As of December 28, 2025 it owned 967 restaurants and franchised 493, for 1,460 system-wide units across 46 states, Guam and 12 countries.

| Concept | Co-owned | Franchised | Avg. check | Off-premises |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outback Steakhouse (U.S.) | 548 | 118 | $29 | 25% |
| Carrabba's Italian Grill | 187 | 17 | $27 | 34% |
| Bonefish Grill | 156 | 2 | $37 | 17% |
| Fleming's Prime Steakhouse | 66 | — | $110 | 3% |

Roughly 96%+ of revenue is company-owned restaurant sales; franchising is a small, high-margin royalty stream (U.S. royalties 3.50%-5.75% of gross sales, international 2.75%-5.00%). Alcohol is 11% of U.S. restaurant sales. Beef, the majority of purchased protein, came primarily from four suppliers in FY2025.

The U.S. footprint is contracting. FY2025 saw 19 domestic openings against 40 closures (Outback: 15 open, 24 closed), a net decline of 21 units, and the FY2026 plan calls for only about six new Outbacks. Capital is being redirected into a program to remodel nearly all Outback restaurants by end-2028, which is why FY2026 capex is guided to $185M-$195M against $179.9M spent in FY2025 while unit count shrinks. This is defensive capital, and it ranks ahead of shareholder returns — the dividend was suspended in October 2025.

Management is almost entirely new. As of February 20, 2026, five of seven named executive officers had held their roles for 18 months or less: CEO Michael Spanos (September 2024, previously EVP/COO of Delta Air Lines and CEO of Six Flags, no prior restaurant-operating CEO experience), CFO Eric Christel (September 2025), Outback President Pat Hafner (January 2025), CHRO Jessica Mitory (August 2025), and a Chief Commercial Officer appointed February 2025 whose departure was announced in December 2025, roughly ten months later. Starboard Value reached an agreement with the company in January 2024 and Jonathan Sagal sits on the board.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and margin

FY2025 revenue was $3,956M, +0.1% versus $3,950M. Pre-2024 revenue figures are not comparable — Bloomin' sold 67% of its Brazil operations on December 30, 2024 and reclassified Brazil to discontinued operations, so the apparent decline from ~$4,671M in FY2023 is a deconsolidation, not an organic collapse.

The FY2025 margin break was severe:

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 FY2025 | Q1 FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant-level operating margin | 13.3% | 11.7% | 13.9% | 14.0% |
| Operating income | $139.8M | $37.2M | $57.2M | $59.1M |
| Operating margin | 3.5% | 0.9% | 5.6% | 5.6% |

Operating income fell 73% on flat revenue, which quantifies the operating leverage in a ~$3.5B largely fixed-and-semi-variable cost base. Drivers were commodity and labor inflation plus the value-oriented "Aussie 3-Course" platform at Outback absorbing pricing headroom, and the figure includes a $28.2M charge writing off the entire Bonefish Grill goodwill balance. The 2026 stabilization is real but narrow: restaurant-level margin +10bp and operating margin flat in Q1, against FY2026 commodity inflation guided at 4.5%-5.5%.

The longer series, from the company's own pay-versus-performance disclosure, is the material fact: adjusted diluted EPS of $2.45 (FY2021), $2.36, $2.78, $1.79, $1.13 (FY2025 total company; $1.14 on a continuing-operations basis). Five-year TSR indexed to $100 at FY2020-end finished at $42.47 against a peer index at $165.06 — a 123-point relative shortfall.

### Earnings quality: the tax line

Q1 FY2026 pretax income from continuing operations was $46.7M versus $46.0M. Continuing-operations net income rose $13.0M to $56.8M, and GAAP diluted EPS rose from $0.50 to $0.64. The income tax line moved from a ~$2.2M provision to a $10.3M benefit at a disclosed −22.0% effective rate — a ~$12.5M favorable swing accounting for approximately 96% of the net income increase.

The 10-Q attributes the negative rate to FICA tip credits, which are a largely fixed dollar amount, measured against a forecast of full-year pretax income that is lower for FY2026 than the forecast used in Q1 FY2025. The tax tailwind and the earnings weakness are the same fact. Two implications follow. First, applying a negative annual effective rate to a pretax *loss* quarter produces tax *expense*, which is the most plausible mechanical explanation for why the Q3 guide of −$0.22 to −$0.27 is materially worse than the prior year's Q3 adjusted result of −$0.03 despite flat-to-better operating performance. Second, if pretax income recovers, the fixed credit stops dominating and the rate normalizes toward the mid-20s, a headwind embedded inside the bull case. (Quarterly rates can also swing on discrete items — valuation allowances, stock-compensation true-ups — which we cannot rule out without the full tax footnote.)

### Cash flow and balance sheet

FY2025 operating cash flow was $275.9M against $179.9M of capex, giving $96.0M of free cash flow, a 2.4% FCF margin. Holding OCF flat and using the $190M FY2026 capex midpoint gives roughly $86M — a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast. At $12.555 that is an 8.9% FCF yield on FY2025 actuals and roughly 8.0% on the FY2026 sensitivity. On an enterprise value of approximately $1.83B (equity $1.075B + $755M funded debt, cash not netted and leases excluded), the FCF yield falls to ~5.3% — ordinary for a no-growth operator.

Funded debt at March 29, 2026 was $755M: $455M drawn revolver (down from $490M at year-end, consistent with deleveraging priority after the dividend suspension) plus $300M of 5.125% senior notes due April 2029. Debt to FY2025 operating cash flow is 2.7x ($755M / $275.9M), and $693.7M of revolver capacity was undrawn at FY2025-end. There is no near-term maturity wall.

The credit market's view is separable from the equity's. The 2029 notes were carried at fair value of $269.5M (89.8% of par) at December 28, 2025 and $264.5M (88.2%) at March 29, 2026. At 88.2 with ~3.05 years to maturity, the implied yield is approximately 9.5% — high-yield territory, and the mark *fell* between December and March. We have no post-rally bond mark; the Q2 10-Q was not available. Refinancing the notes at current perceived credit would roughly double their coupon cost.

Screening ratios read distressed for structural reasons. The current ratio of ~0.31 reflects a model with no receivables, minimal inventory, and large current liabilities including $377.9M of unearned gift-card revenue — customer prepayment that funds working capital at zero cost, and which is a deferred revenue liability rather than backlog or forward visibility. Total liabilities to equity of ~8.5x reflects an $917.6M accumulated deficit from historical buybacks plus capitalized leases against $333.6M of book equity; funded debt to equity is closer to 2.4x. The informative term is the direction of earnings: operating income at 0.9% of revenue.

Two items constrain confidence. The credit agreement's financial covenants were not reviewed, so headroom against $37.2M of FY2025 operating income is unverified and should not be assumed. And the lease stack is larger than the commitments table shows: $1,162M of undiscounted operating lease commitments plus a footnoted $945.6M of renewal options reasonably certain of exercise, roughly $2.1B in total against a $1.075B equity value.

## Growth Analysis

There is no revenue growth to analyze; there is a comp-quality question.

| Period | U.S. comps | Outback comps | Outback traffic |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2025 | +0.2% | −0.5% | ~−1.2% (system −1.4%) |
| Q1 FY2026 | +0.9% | −0.3% | −2.4% |
| Q2 FY2026 | n/d by brand | n/d | n/d |

Bonefish is the exception and the most encouraging datapoint in the file: Q1 FY2026 comps +6.1% on traffic +3.0% at a $37 average check. Positive *traffic*, rather than check, is evidence that the company's operating interventions can restore guest counts. The constraint is scale — Bonefish is 156 company-owned units whose goodwill was fully written off in FY2025, against Outback's 548 company-owned units.

Estimate revisions are negative on the near quarters and roughly flat further out. Pre-print consensus: current quarter −$0.19 (from −$0.13 ninety days ago, −51%), next quarter $0.10 (−42%), current year $0.87 (+3.6%), next year $0.95 (−1.3%). The +30% revision-velocity buy signal is absent here; the near-term direction is the opposite. Post-print, current-year consensus should move toward the $0.95 guidance midpoint. FY2027 consensus sits at $0.95 — flat against FY2026's already-declined base. Consensus models no recovery next year.

The earnings surprise record is genuinely good: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with beats of 75.7%, 16.0% and 32.4% on small absolute bases. That argues management is guiding conservatively and raises the probability that Q3 comes in better than −$0.22 to −$0.27. It does not change the full-year arithmetic.

Growth prospects score 4.5 — unchanged in substance from the prior 5.0, adjusted down marginally because the raised guide confirmed a loss-making H2 and a third consecutive year of adjusted EPS decline, offset by two quarters of margin stabilization and a demonstrated ability to beat.

The EBITDA estimate requires a caveat: depreciation and amortization was not captured in the reviewed data. Assuming D&A of roughly $175M-$185M against FY2025 operating income of $37.2M plus the $28.2M impairment gives adjusted EBITDA on the order of $245M-$270M. Adding capitalized leases would raise the EV/EBITDAR multiple materially.

The profile fields showing forward P/E and trailing P/E both at 36.57 alongside EPS of $0.10 are internally inconsistent (they reconcile to neither the price nor the disclosed EPS series) and are disregarded.

Peer comparison is close to useless in this group. Median restaurant P/E of 38.7x is distorted by trough earnings at Cracker Barrel (39.5x) and Papa John's (38.7x); Wendy's trades at 8.7x on a declining franchised model, BJ's at 26.0x, Dave & Buster's at negative earnings. Every comparable is at a cycle-distorted multiple. The more useful anchors are BLMN's own historical 10x-14x forward range and the multiples of the operators actually winning share — Texas Roadhouse and Darden — which trade at premiums that reflect positive traffic Bloomin' does not have.

The valuation question reduces to a single judgment: is $0.95 the trough, and does 13x a trough deserve a re-rating? Three considerations argue against paying up for that at $12.56.

First, the earnings base is low-quality. H1's improvement was 96% tax-driven at the pretax-to-net line, and the tax benefit reverses mechanically as pretax income recovers. A recovery to $1.30 of pretax-driven adjusted EPS delivers less after-tax than a naive model implies.

Second, the price now sits above every published analyst target. The convention that consensus targets lag reality on inflecting stocks applies when estimate revisions are positive and the multiple is undemanding. Here near-term revisions are negative, FY2027 consensus is flat, and the stock has already gapped through the top of the target range. Post-print targets will rise, plausibly into the $11-$14 band — which is where the stock already trades.

Third, prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, but the *reason* for the appreciation is. A 41% move on a check-driven beat and a guide that still embeds a 12%-21% EPS decline priced a narrative inflection ahead of an earnings inflection.

Scenario framework on FY2027 adjusted EPS:

- **Bear ($7.50, −40%):** Outback traffic stays negative through the remodel program, 4.5%-5.5% commodity inflation compresses restaurant margin back toward 12%, Out West restructures and Bloomin' absorbs support obligations, Outback goodwill impairs. FY2027 adjusted EPS $0.65-$0.75 at 10x.
- **Base ($12.00, −4%):** the remodel program plus simplification holds restaurant margin near 14% and comps at +1% to +2%, Outback traffic improves to roughly flat, the tax rate normalizes and offsets part of the operating gain. FY2027 adjusted EPS $1.05-$1.15 at 11x.
- **Bull ($18.00, +43%):** Outback traffic inflects positive on the remodels and value platform, delivering +3% comps. On $3.96B of revenue at 30%-40% incremental flow-through, a 3-point comp swing adds $35M-$45M of operating income against a $37.2M base. FY2027 adjusted EPS $1.50-$1.60 at 11x-12x, with credit re-rating narrowing the ~9.5% bond yield.

The distribution is wide in both directions and centered essentially at spot. That is a valuation score of 4.0 — neither a mispricing to exploit nor a clearly stretched multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

The category is recovering and Outback is not participating. Third-party reporting in February 2026 described Chili's delivering 8.6% same-store sales growth as households rotated back into casual dining after fast-food and fast-casual pricing eroded their cost advantage. Against that backdrop Outback delivered −0.5% comps in FY2025 and −0.3% with −2.4% traffic in Q1 FY2026. The problem is brand- and execution-specific, not macro. The caveat is that Chili's ~$17-18 check sits well below Outback's $29, so part of Chili's gain is trade-down that structurally bypasses Outback.

Within the chain-steakhouse set — Outback, LongHorn (Darden) and Texas Roadhouse — both competitors have been running positive traffic. Outback's $29 check occupies an awkward position: above Texas Roadhouse on price without the differentiation to command a premium, and it is leaning on a value platform (Aussie 3-Course) precisely when beef inflation is guided at 4.5%-5.5% from a supply base of primarily four suppliers.

The 10-K makes no moat claim: "all major casual dining restaurants in markets in which we operate would be considered competitors," plus supermarket prepared meals, improving QSR/fast-casual offerings and home delivery. What the company has are competitive assets rather than barriers: brand recognition, ~1,100 U.S. locations, the ~5,000 sq ft "Joey" prototype designed to lift new-unit ROI, and the Dine Rewards program. Off-premises mix of 25% at Outback and 34% at Carrabba's means a meaningful share of revenue arrives without the dine-in experience the remodel capex is meant to improve, and with third-party delivery economics attached.

Human capital is a constraint: ~64,000 team members, 81% hourly turnover and 20% restaurant-management turnover in FY2025, though ~90% of Managing Partner promotions were internal.

Competitive position scores 4.0 — trajectory marginally improving (Outback comps −0.5% → −0.3%, Bonefish traffic positive, revenue +1.3% in Q2) against continued share loss in a recovering category and a shrinking domestic footprint.

## Risk Assessment

**Outback impairment is closer to a base case than a tail.** At the Q4 FY2025 test, the Outback reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only ~3% and the Outback trade name by ~5%, with a disclosed sensitivity showing a 100bp discount-rate increase producing $36.1M of goodwill impairment. Bonefish's goodwill was already fully written off. The charge would be non-cash and irrelevant to adjusted-EBITDA covenants, but it would formally lower the long-run earnings base management assigns to 60% of the U.S. footprint. The offset: a thin cushion is arithmetically likely immediately after a depressed earnings year, and rising FY2026-27 forecasts widen it without any action.

**Out West / Cerca Trova is a dated contingency inside two quarters.** The franchisee operates 72-74 Outbacks in the western U.S. under a 2023 Resolution Agreement providing deferral, forbearance and reduced 2.25% advertising fees, which terminates December 27, 2026 or earlier on specified events. The Q1 FY2026 10-Q discloses that after March 29, 2026 Bloomin' received notice from the agent for Out West's senior lender that Out West is in default under its own credit agreement and forbearance agreement, with lenders not yet acting; Out West remained current to Bloomin'. The 10-K warns that Bloomin' may "need to provide additional support for the franchised locations." Annual fee revenue at risk is modest — roughly $9M-$11M on an assumed ~$3M average unit volume, our estimate rather than disclosure — but the contingent operating and occupancy exposure from absorbing 72 underperforming units is unquantified. Bloomin' has disclosed no guarantee or committed support obligation, and lenders have extended forbearance before; do not model a specific loss.

**Brand concentration in the losing brand.** Outback is ~60% of U.S. units and the clear majority of revenue, and there is no second engine large enough to offset it.

**Input costs.** FY2026 commodity inflation guided at 4.5%-5.5%, with beef the dominant protein from primarily four suppliers, into a brand whose traffic is already negative and whose pricing power is therefore constrained.

**Labor and tip-credit legislation.** 81% hourly turnover plus state minimum-wage exposure. Elimination of the tip credit would raise wage costs *and* remove the mechanism producing the current negative effective tax rate — a double hit.

**Covenant headroom unverified.** The most important unread item in the capital structure given $37.2M of FY2025 operating income. Compliance should not be assumed established.

**Executive turnover.** Five of seven NEOs at under 18 months tenure as of February 2026, and a July 1, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K whose contents we could not retrieve. Another senior departure would materially raise execution risk on the turnaround.

**Macro.** Casual dining at a $29 average check is exposed to consumer discretionary spending, and third-party reporting frames the current period as a traffic rotation back into casual dining from fast-food/fast-casual. Any reversal of that rotation would remove the category tailwind that the self-help thesis assumes as a backdrop. [Specific macro indicators — mortgage rates, application demand, housing wealth effect, sector relative performance — are not supported by the research brief and have been removed.]

**Q2 figures are press-sourced.** Revenue $1.02B, GAAP EPS $0.36, adjusted $0.39, net income $31.3M all come from coverage of the August 5 release rather than a reviewed filing. Brand-level comps, the Q2 debt balance, the post-rally bond mark, updated goodwill cushions and any Out West update are unverified. The GAAP/non-GAAP label on the Q3 guide is also unconfirmed, though the H2 reconciliation holds under either.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 23 contracts across four expirations — so conclusions should be held loosely. Near-term ATM IV is 69.8% against 75.4% one-year realized, so options screen slightly cheap to realized vol, and the term structure is flat (69.8% at one month and six months). The 15-day tenor prices a ±11.6% move, which is high in absolute terms and consistent with unresolved uncertainty after a 41% gap rather than with directional conviction.

The positioning signals conflict. The data source flags 27.4% put/call skew at the one-month tenor as elevated put demand, yet put/call volume at that tenor is 0.26 and put/call open interest is 0.06 — overwhelmingly call-weighted. At the three-month tenor the skew reads −11.6% with a put/call volume ratio of 1.75. Given the contract count, we do not draw a directional read from these figures. Short interest of 11.1% of float at 3.6 days to cover means a meaningful share of the August 5 move may have been mechanical covering, and it leaves less fuel for a further squeeze while preserving a bid under the stock if Q3 beats the −$0.22/−$0.27 guide.

Risk factors score 3.5 — specific, dated, asymmetric exposures (Out West expiry December 27 2026 with a live lender default, near-certain Outback impairment, unverified covenants, an unexplained officer-change filing) against a price that has removed the valuation cushion that existed at $6-9.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $18 (+43%)

Bloomin' is a self-help story with a working industry backdrop. Casual-dining traffic is rotating back into the category, and Chili's/Brinker has already demonstrated what happens to a similarly-priced casual operator when comps inflect. The operating leverage is extreme: on $3.96B of revenue against $37.2M of operating income, a 3-point comp improvement at 30%-40% flow-through roughly doubles operating income. Bonefish's Q1 result — +6.1% comps on +3.0% *traffic* — shows the new team's playbook can restore guest counts, and the Outback remodel program (nearly all units by end-2028) plus menu simplification is the transferred version of it. Management has beaten three of the last four quarters by 16%-76% and just raised full-year guidance; the H2 guide looks conservative. FCF is positive at ~$86M-$96M, the revolver is being paid down, there is no maturity before 2029, and $693.7M of revolver capacity was undrawn. Directors bought stock in the open market within weeks of the $5.26 low. If FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $1.50-$1.60, 11x-12x supports $18, with additional upside from the 2029 notes re-rating from a ~9.5% implied yield.

### Bear case — $7.50 (−40%)

The Q2 beat was check-driven while Outback traffic remains negative at 60% of the footprint, and 4.5%-5.5% commodity inflation into a value platform means the check lever is nearly exhausted. Guidance, even after the raise, implies adjusted EPS falls 12%-21% to $0.90-$1.00 — the third consecutive annual decline from $2.78 — with a loss-making H2 and a Q3 guide below consensus. H1's reported improvement was 96% tax-driven and that benefit reverses mechanically in any recovery. The Outback goodwill cushion of ~3% means a single forecast revision or discount-rate move produces a flagship impairment. The Out West agreement expires December 27, 2026 with the franchisee already in default to its own senior lender, and the 10-K contemplates Bloomin' providing support for the franchised locations. Book equity is $333.6M against ~$2.1B of potential lease obligations and unverified covenant headroom. On FY2027 adjusted EPS of $0.65-$0.75 at 10x, the stock is $7.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders have been paid for the risk they took at $5-9 and should not add at $12.56. New capital has no edge here — base case fair value is roughly at spot with a −40%/+43% distribution around it.

**Base case: $12.00 (−4% from $12.555).** 11x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $1.05-$1.15, assuming restaurant-level margin holds near 14%, comps run +1% to +2%, Outback traffic improves to roughly flat, and the effective tax rate normalizes toward the mid-20s as pretax income recovers, offsetting part of the operating gain. Cross-check: ~$86M of FY2026E FCF at a 9% required equity yield implies ~$955M of equity, or $11.15/share.

**Bull case: $18.00 (+43%).** Requires evidence of positive Outback traffic, not check-led comps.

**Bear case: $7.50 (−40%).**

**Timeframe:** 6-12 months, anchored on the Q3 FY2026 print (early November 2026) and the December 27, 2026 Out West agreement expiry.

### Upgrade triggers (to BUY)

1. **Outback traffic turns positive, or improves to better than −1.0%, for two consecutive quarters** with restaurant-level margin at or above 14%. This is the single fact the bull case requires and does not have. Check-led comps do not qualify.
2. **Out West resolves without Bloomin' assuming units or occupancy obligations** — a disclosed renewal, third-party sale, or orderly wind-down with quantified and immaterial impact.
3. **A price retracement toward $9.00-$9.50** (roughly 10x the FY2026 guidance midpoint) with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $0.95, which would restore a two-to-one payoff against the $18 bull case.

### Downgrade triggers (to AVOID)

1. **FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $0.90**, or a Q3 result worse than the −$0.27 low end, which would break the two-quarter stabilization pattern the rally is built on.
2. **Out West enters bankruptcy or liquidation, or Bloomin' discloses any support obligation, guarantee or receivable reserve** related to the 72-unit group.
3. **Restaurant-level operating margin falls back below 13%** in any quarter, indicating the 4.5%-5.5% commodity inflation is no longer being offset.
4. **A further senior departure** among the CEO, CFO or Outback President, which would restart the turnaround clock.

**Next reassessment point:** the Q2 FY2026 10-Q, which should resolve brand-level comps, the Q2 debt balance, the post-rally 2029-notes mark, updated goodwill cushions and any Out West development — followed by the Q3 print in early November.

## Conclusion

Bloomin' Brands delivered a genuine operational beat and a raised full-year outlook, and the market repriced the equity 41% in a session to a 52-week high above every published analyst target. The raise is real and the margin stabilization across two quarters is real. The problem is what the raised guidance actually says: adjusted EPS of $0.90-$1.00 against $1.14 last year, a loss-making second half, a Q3 guide below consensus, and FY2027 consensus flat at $0.95. H1's reported earnings improvement came almost entirely from a tax line whose favorability is a function of management's own low pretax forecast and which reverses as that forecast improves. Comps are being produced by check while Outback — 60% of the domestic footprint — loses guests at −2.4% in a category where a comparable operator posted 8.6% same-store growth.

At 13.2x guided earnings with a shrinking unit base, rising maintenance capex, no dividend, ~$2.1B of potential lease obligations, unsecured notes implying a ~9.5% yield, a ~3% goodwill cushion at the flagship brand, and a franchisee restructuring with a December 27, 2026 date on it, the stock is priced for the turnaround to work without yet having produced the traffic evidence that would confirm it.
