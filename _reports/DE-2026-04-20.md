## Executive Summary

Deere & Company is the world's preeminent agricultural equipment manufacturer, with $45.7B in FY2025 revenue, a $161B market cap, and credit ratings of A+/A1/A from all three major agencies. The company is navigating a severe agricultural downcycle: revenue has fallen 25% from the FY2023 peak of $61.3B, net income has declined 51% to $5.0B, and management guides US/Canada large ag unit sales down another 15-20% in FY2026. Despite this, Deere's stock has rallied 31% from its 52-week low of $433 to $590, pricing in a recovery that has not yet materialized in fundamentals.

The bull case centers on three catalysts: (1) the Supreme Court's February 2026 invalidation of IEEPA tariffs, which could recover a portion of the $961M+ in tariff costs absorbed over 15 months; (2) the Construction & Forestry segment, where backlog surged 73% to $3.8B and Q1 revenue grew 34%; and (3) the structural margin recovery embedded in FY2026 guidance (PPA margins guided to 11-13% vs. Q1's 4.4%). The bear case is that US large ag remains depressed through FY2027, SaaS/precision technology monetization lags management's timeline, and the right-to-repair legal overhang (FTC lawsuit, $99M class action settlement) structurally weakens the aftermarket moat. At 33x trailing earnings and 33x forward P/E on consensus FY2026 EPS of ~$17.89, the stock is priced at the upper bound of its historical cyclical range, leaving limited margin of safety if the recovery timeline extends.

## Company Overview

Deere operates through four segments. **Production & Precision Agriculture (PPA)**, representing 45% of equipment sales, manufactures large-scale row crop equipment — 4WD tractors, combines, planters, sprayers, and cotton pickers. This is the most cyclically exposed segment and the primary source of recent earnings pressure. **Small Agriculture & Turf (SAT)** at 26% covers utility/compact tractors, hay/forage equipment, riding mowers, and commercial turf care, sold through ~2,050 independent dealer locations and mass retailers including Home Depot and Lowe's. **Construction & Forestry (CF)** at 29% encompasses excavators, dozers, loaders, motor graders, plus the Wirtgen roadbuilding platform (milling machines, pavers, compactors). **Financial Services** provides retail and wholesale financing through John Deere Financial, with ~$49B in financing receivables and $7.5B in equipment on operating leases.

The company's strategic transformation — the "Smart Industrial Operating Model" — aims to layer SaaS subscriptions, precision technology, and autonomous capabilities onto the equipment platform. Deferred revenue of $2.1B (growing) reflects early traction, but the 10-K explicitly states that customer adoption of SaaS subscription services has been "slower than we expected."

Nine consecutive quarters of net income decline through Q1 FY2026. The Q1 earnings decline was amplified by $361M in pretax tariff costs ($272M after-tax), which alone accounted for more than 100% of the year-over-year net income decline.

Segment-level performance is highly divergent. PPA operating margin collapsed to 4.4% in Q1 (from 11.0% prior year) on tariffs, unfavorable mix, and warranty costs. SAT margins improved to 9.0% (from 7.1%) on 24% volume growth in dairy/livestock. CF margins expanded to 5.1% (from 3.3%) on 34% volume growth. The CF backlog of $3.8B (+73% YoY) is the strongest forward indicator in the portfolio.

### FY2026 Guidance

Management guides FY2026 net income of $4.5-5.0B (roughly flat to slightly below FY2025), implying full-year EPS of ~$16.60-$18.50. PPA sales are expected down 5-10% with margins recovering to 11-13%, which requires meaningful improvement from Q1's 4.4% — plausible given tariff relief post-IEEPA ruling and seasonal shipment patterns, but not without execution risk. Both SAT and CF are guided up ~15% with margins of 13.5-15% and 9-11% respectively.

The consensus FY2026 EPS estimate of $17.89 sits near the middle of guidance. FY2027 consensus of $23.04 implies a 29% earnings recovery, which requires both ag cycle inflection and margin normalization.

### Balance Sheet

Total assets of $106B are dominated by Financial Services (~$68B). Equipment operations carry manageable leverage given A+/A1/A credit ratings with stable outlooks. Cash of $6.8B (Q1-end, seasonal trough) plus $7.2B in unused credit lines provides ample liquidity. The Altman Z-score of 0.97 is misleading — it penalizes the structural asset/liability profile of the captive finance operation and should be disregarded for this type of business model.

### Cash Flow & Capital Returns

Even in a deep downcycle, Deere generated $7.5B in operating cash flow and $6.1B in free cash flow (13.4% FCF margin) in FY2025. Shares outstanding have declined from 306M to 270M since FY2022 — a 12% reduction — with $7.6B remaining under an $18B buyback authorization. The quarterly dividend of $1.62/share ($6.48 annually) provides a 1.1% yield. Total Q1 2026 cash returned to shareholders: $743M.

FY2026 operating cash flow is guided to $4.5-5.5B, which is lower due to working capital absorption during the downcycle but still supports continued buybacks.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term: Cyclical Recovery Dependent on External Factors

Consensus estimates reflect moderate earnings growth expectations: FY2026 EPS of $17.89 (roughly flat YoY) followed by FY2027 EPS of $23.04 (+29%). The 90-day revision trend is positive (+7.3% for current year, +4.2% for next year), though the 30-day trend has turned slightly negative (-0.6% current year, -0.5% next year), suggesting the initial post-IEEPA optimism is fading modestly as analysts incorporate broader macro uncertainty.

The earnings beat/miss record is constructive: 3 beats in 4 quarters, with large surprises in Q4 FY2025 (+15.5%) and Q1 FY2026 (+14.7%). The lone miss (Q1 FY2026 fiscal, or calendar Q4 2025) was minor at -0.9%. This pattern suggests management guidance tends conservative and execution has been solid relative to expectations.

### Medium-Term Growth Vectors

**Construction & Forestry surge:** The 73% backlog increase to $3.8B is the most compelling forward indicator. Infrastructure spending (IIJA funding ramp), reshoring activity, and roadbuilding demand support multi-quarter visibility. This segment could become a larger earnings contributor as ag remains weak.

**Technology monetization:** Deere's precision agriculture stack (See & Spray, AutoPath, John Deere Operations Center) represents genuine competitive differentiation. However, the 10-K's acknowledgment that SaaS adoption has been "slower than expected" tempers expectations. Deferred revenue of $2.1B is growing, but subscription revenue remains an immaterial percentage of the total. The payoff from this investment is a FY2028+ story at earliest.

**Tenna acquisition ($440M, February 2026):** Construction fleet management technology. Adds digital overlay to CF segment. Strategically coherent but small relative to total revenue.

**Share count reduction:** At the current buyback pace, Deere is reducing share count by 3-4% annually, providing a mechanical EPS tailwind of comparable magnitude independent of revenue growth.

### Longer-Term: Ag Cycle Recovery Timing Is the Key Variable

The USDA projects declining US net farm income. Input costs (fertilizer, energy, labor) remain elevated while commodity prices have corrected. Used equipment inventories are high, depressing trade-in values and extending replacement cycles. Deere states it "cannot reasonably foresee when these conditions will fully subside." Historical ag cycles have lasted 2-4 years from peak to trough; this cycle peaked in FY2023, placing a potential trough in FY2026-2027 under normal patterns.

## Competitive Landscape

Deere's competitive advantages are substantial and durable:

1. **Dealer network:** ~2,050 independent dealer locations in US/Canada provide geographic coverage and customer relationships that competitors cannot replicate in any reasonable timeframe. The dealer network serves as both a distribution and aftermarket revenue channel.

2. **Precision agriculture leadership:** Deere has invested $2.3B annually in R&D, building an integrated technology stack spanning guidance, autonomy, machine optimization, and data analytics. The Operations Center platform, See & Spray, and autonomous tractor capabilities are ahead of AGCO and CNH Industrial. This technology platform creates switching costs as farmers integrate their operations data.

3. **Captive finance:** John Deere Financial provides competitive financing that pulls equipment sales through the channel. The $49B receivable portfolio generates steady earnings and creates customer stickiness.

4. **Brand and installed base:** The John Deere brand carries pricing power in North American ag equipment. The installed base creates an aftermarket revenue stream (parts, service, software) that is higher margin than new equipment.

Key competitors: AGCO (AGCO, $10B market cap), CNH Industrial (CNHI, $16B), Caterpillar (CAT, construction only), Kubota (compact/utility tractors). Deere's scale advantage is significant — FY2025 revenue of $45.7B exceeds AGCO and CNH Industrial combined.

**Right-to-repair risk:** The FTC lawsuit and class action settlement could erode the aftermarket moat. If Deere is forced to open repair tools and software to independent shops, the Lifecycle Solutions strategy loses a structural advantage. Preliminary settlement talks are underway; outcome remains uncertain.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Agricultural Cycle Extension (Critical)
US/Canada large ag down 15-20% in FY2026 with no visibility on inflection. Farm income declining, input costs elevated, used equipment inventories high. If the trough extends to FY2027, consensus FY2027 EPS of $23.04 will be revised materially lower, potentially to $18-20 range.

### 2. Tariff Uncertainty (Critical — Near-Term)
The Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling provides potential relief, but trade policy remains fluid. Q1 FY2026 absorbed $361M in tariff costs. The company sources rare earth minerals from China, creating specific supply chain vulnerability. Retaliatory tariffs from export markets compound the exposure.

### 3. Right-to-Repair Legal Overhang (Significant)
FTC + 5 state AGs lawsuit (motion to dismiss denied, preliminary settlement talks). $99M class action settlement. Could structurally weaken aftermarket revenue model and Lifecycle Solutions strategy.

### 4. SaaS Adoption Lag (Strategic)
Management explicitly acknowledges slower-than-expected customer adoption of subscription services. The Leap Ambitions transformation timeline may slip, delaying the margin uplift embedded in long-term targets.

### 5. Financial Services Credit Quality
Non-performing receivables at $682M on $41.3B portfolio. Loan modifications doubled YoY ($64M vs $28M). If the ag downturn deepens, write-offs could increase meaningfully from the current $57M/quarter run rate.

### 6. Fertilizer Cost / Food Inflation Interaction
Doubled fertilizer costs (urea at ~$1,000/ton, 40% of global nitrogen transiting Hormuz) directly compress farmer margins, extending the equipment replacement cycle. Elevated input costs reduce farmer purchasing power and delay the recovery Deere's valuation is pricing.

- CF segment continues its 34% growth trajectory, sustained by infrastructure spending and $3.8B backlog

## Conclusion

Deere is a world-class industrial franchise with genuine competitive advantages: the strongest dealer network in agriculture, a leading precision technology stack, a captive finance operation, and consistent free cash flow generation even in a deep downcycle ($6.1B FCF in FY2025). Management has demonstrated disciplined capital allocation, returning $743M to shareholders in Q1 alone while maintaining A+ credit quality.

The specific obstacle to a BUY recommendation is valuation relative to cycle position: at 33x trailing earnings during the ninth consecutive quarter of net income decline, the stock requires a multi-condition recovery scenario to justify its current price. That recovery depends on: tariff resolution (IEEPA ruling helps but is incomplete), ag cycle inflection (management explicitly cannot forecast timing), PPA margin normalization (Q1's 4.4% must more than double to reach guidance), and continued CF momentum ($3.8B backlog supports this). Each condition has a reasonable probability of materializing, but the conjunction of all four within the market's implied timeline creates fragility in the thesis.

The 90-day positive revision trend (+7.3% current year) and strong beat/miss record (3 of 4 quarters) provide earnings quality comfort. The CF backlog surge and Supreme Court tariff ruling are genuine positive catalysts. The options market's elevated put demand and backwardated term structure confirm institutional uncertainty about near-term direction, and the 30-day negative revision trend (-0.6%) suggests the initial tariff-relief optimism is fading.

HOLD with a growth-hold classification. A pullback to the $510-530 range or upward revision in FY2027 estimates above $25 would create a more compelling entry point. Investors already positioned should maintain exposure for the recovery, but new capital should wait for better risk/reward.
