**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Enerpac Tool Group is a 115-year-old, Milwaukee-based maker of high-pressure hydraulic and mechanical tools for mission-critical industrial applications, operating a single Industrial Tools & Services (IT&S) segment plus a small biomedical-textiles business. The investment story through FY2025 was a successful margin transformation: the ASCEND program lifted operating margin from 5% (FY22) to 22% (FY25) and drove net income from $16M to $93M. The balance sheet is strong: Altman Z-score of 3.02, ROE near 31%, a 2.74 current ratio, modest leverage, and $111M of FY25 operating cash flow on $19M of capex.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because the company entered FY2026 with a clear negative turn. In Q2 FY26 (quarter ended February 28, 2026), gross margin compressed 410 bps to 46.4% and operating profit fell to $25M from $31M. Underneath flattering headline growth (+6% reported, ~2% organic), the service business — concentrated in Hydratight/North Sea energy maintenance — declined roughly 17-22% organically, while the product line grew ~5-6%. Tariffs are flowing into cost of goods sold and are not yet fully offset. Consensus estimates are falling: the current-quarter estimate was cut 7.5% over 30 days and the current-year estimate is drifting down.

The HOLD thesis is a recovery-dependent growth case: the service decline is largely cyclical (North Sea/EMEA maintenance turnarounds) and is being actively restructured; product growth and the heavy-lifting expansion provide a durable base; service comparisons ease in H2 FY26; and an aggressive buyback ($135M remaining) is shrinking the share count ~4% YoY. Consensus next-year EPS of $2.18 implies ~15% growth from current-year $1.90. Conviction is moderate, because near-term estimates are still negative and margins have only just rolled over. The next one-to-two earnings prints determine whether this re-rates toward the high-$30s/low-$40s or breaks toward the high-$20s.

## Company Overview

Enerpac designs and sells branded high-force tools — cylinders, pumps, hydraulic torque wrenches, bolt tensioners, and Heavy Lifting Technology — operating at 5,000–12,000 psi, under brands including ENERPAC, HYDRATIGHT, LARZEP, SIMPLEX, and DTA the Smart Move. The business splits into Product (~80% of sales, distributor and OEM channels) and Service & Rental (~20%, field technicians performing bolting, machining, and joint-integrity work, heavily weighted to refinery/petrochemical and North Sea/EMEA energy maintenance).

End markets span refinery/petrochemical, general industrial, MRO, machining and manufacturing, power generation, infrastructure, and mining. The FY25 geographic mix was US 37%, Europe 28%, Middle East 13%, Asia 11%, and other 11%. The model is asset-light (manufacturing is primarily light assembly of globally sourced components, with capex historically 2-3% of sales) and the moat rests on brand strength, a safety reputation in harsh environments, engineering/customization, and a broad global distribution footprint. The company employs roughly 2,100 people across 100+ countries.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory ($M, fiscal year ends August 31):**

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 571 | 598 | 590 | 617 |
| Gross margin | 46% | 49% | 51% | 51% |
| Operating margin | 5% | 14% | 21% | 22% |
| Net income | 16 | 47 | 86 | 93 |
| Operating cash flow | — | 78 | 81 | 111 |

The ASCEND transformation (March 2022–August 2024) delivered ~$54M of annualized operating-profit improvement at a $75M program cost (including $19M restructuring) and is the engine behind the margin expansion. The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story: a term loan of $187.5M, no revolver draw, $399.5M of revolver availability, covenant compliance (net leverage cap 3.75x; interest coverage minimum 3.0x), a 2.74 current ratio, ROE of 30.8%, and ROA of 16.1%. Free-cash-flow conversion is high, with capex in FY25 elevated by a now-completed Milwaukee HQ build-out.

The Q2 FY2026 print is the most important recent development. Revenue of $155M grew 6% reported but only ~2% organic after stripping out FX. The mechanism is a two-speed business: product organic sales rose ~5-6%, but service organic sales fell ~17% in the quarter (~22% in H1), concentrated in Hydratight/EMEA North Sea maintenance. IT&S segment margin fell to 21.2% in Q2 from 27.5%. Management explicitly cited "higher tariff-driven costs flowing through cost of goods sold," and inventory was pre-built to $93M from $79M, pushing primary working capital to 26% of sales from 21%. Diluted EPS for H1 FY26 fell to $0.67 from $0.78.

Capital allocation has pivoted to buybacks: $65.9M repurchased in H1 FY26 (versus $14.6M a year earlier) under a $200M authorization, dropping basic shares to ~51.9M from ~54.3M. This is a deliberate "buy while the stock is down" posture, but it drained cash from $152M to $99M. The dividend is token (~$0.04/year, ~0.1% yield), so this is not an income holding.

The earnings-surprise record is unimpressive recently: over the last four quarters, one beat, one in-line, and two misses (Q2 FY26 -2.0%, Q1 FY26 -4.6%). Visibility has deteriorated.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth flatters a weak underlying picture. FY25 reported 5% revenue growth was +3% DTA acquisition and +1% FX, leaving ~1% organic. Within the ~2% Q2 FY26 organic figure, the product line is genuinely growing (~5-6%, supported by infrastructure, energy-transition demand in wind and grid, and the DTA heavy-lifting expansion), while service is contracting sharply.

Estimate revisions are the clearest forward signal, and they are negative. The current-year estimate slipped 1.2% over 30 days to $1.90. There is no rising-estimate momentum to lean on. The single positive in the revision table is the next-quarter estimate (+3.2% over 30 days) and the next-year estimate holding at $2.18, which implies roughly 15% EPS growth off the depressed current-year base.

The growth thesis is therefore recovery-dependent rather than momentum-driven. It requires three things to materialize: (1) the Hydratight restructuring ($3.3M Q2 charge to rightsize the segment and shift toward higher-margin services) stabilizing the service drag; (2) North Sea/EMEA maintenance comparisons easing through H2 FY26 as the ~20% organic decline laps; and (3) product organic growth sustaining at +5-6%. If those hold, consensus next-year EPS is achievable and the buyback amplifies per-share growth. The counterpoint is that estimates are still being cut, the margin rollover is happening in real time, and there is no evidence yet that the service trough is in.

## Valuation Assessment

At a current price of ~$36.33–36.60, EPAC trades at ~22.9x trailing and ~19x current-year EPS, falling to ~16.7x next-year EPS. That is not stretched. Against the peer set in Industrial Machinery & Supplies & Components, the median P/E of 51.7 is distorted by names with negative or low earnings denominators (PRLB 73.5, AIN -34.6, NVRI -9.4). The cleanest comparable, Kennametal at 19.4x, sits roughly in line with EPAC's forward multiple, so EPAC is not obviously mispriced relative to a genuine peer.

Analyst price targets cluster tightly at $50–51 (mean $50.5), implying ~39% upside. These targets should be treated with caution: they predate or do not fully reflect the Q2 FY26 service collapse and the falling estimate trend. On a stock with negative earnings momentum, consensus targets are stale rather than a floor; the inflection logic that would make targets conservative (rising estimates, accelerating revenue) is absent here. The forward multiple is reasonable but not compelling: ~16.7x next-year EPS for a business whose organic growth is ~2% and whose margins have just rolled over is fair value, not a bargain. A re-rating requires the service stabilization to be demonstrated, not assumed.

The valuation score reflects this balance: not a value trap, not a clear opportunity. A low multiple accompanied by falling estimates warrants skepticism until the service trend turns.

## Competitive Landscape

Enerpac is a niche global leader in high-pressure hydraulic tools, a specialized and fragmented market where it competes on quality, availability, engineering, and safety rather than price. The product business benefits from broad industrial/infrastructure/MRO demand and secular controlled-force/heavy-lifting trends (energy-transition infrastructure, wind, grid), with DTA extending into automated horizontal heavy-load movement.

The trajectory is mixed. The product franchise is stable-to-gaining, growing 5-6% organically through a soft industrial backdrop, which is a credible competitive signal. The service business is the concern: a ~20%+ organic decline that the company is responding to with a Hydratight rightsizing implies the weakness is not purely end-market cyclicality but likely includes some scope or share loss in North Sea/EMEA maintenance. The decision to restructure rather than wait out the cycle is consistent with management seeing structural pressure on that segment's footprint. The DTA earn-out marked at just $2.5M against a $12M maximum suggests the recent acquisition is tracking below initial expectations, a modest negative on the inorganic-growth pillar. Net, this is a durable niche leader with a deteriorating service segment: a stable-to-slightly-eroding competitive picture rather than one of widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is the service/Hydratight cyclical exposure, which has moved from a 10-K risk factor to a materializing decline. The 10-K flags oil-and-gas demand and project delays; the Q2 10-Q confirms they have arrived. Tariff pass-through is the second active risk: components are globally sourced with significant China content, and Q2 shows tariffs are increasing COGS faster than pricing/productivity can offset. The durability of ASCEND gains is being tested in real time: a fresh restructuring less than 18 months after declaring the program complete directly challenges the company's own "may not fully maintain" risk language.

Goodwill and intangibles represent ~41% of total assets ($337M); a prolonged downturn in a reporting unit could trigger impairment (the FY25 test was qualitative only, versus a quantitative pass in FY24 with fair value >85% above carrying value). Other risks are lower-magnitude: distributor/agent channel concentration, foreign receivable collection, the open Dutch sanctions proceeding tied to pre-2019 Crimea-linked sales (reserved at the low end, viewed as immaterial), and Wisconsin anti-takeover entrenchment.

Several of these are partly priced: the stock is down ~12.7% over the trailing year and ~20% from its $45.54 52-week high, so the cyclical and tariff pressures are not a surprise to the market. The strong balance sheet (Z-score 3.0, no revolver draw, high FCF) caps the tail risk of a solvency-driven collapse. What is not resolved is whether earnings have troughed or whether further estimate cuts lie ahead.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility sits at 69.2%, up modestly from 67.9% a month earlier, elevated for an industrial of this size and consistent with uncertainty around the earnings trajectory. The put/call open-interest ratio jumped to 0.83 from 0.25 over the same window even as the price rose 7.6%, indicating increased downside hedging into the rally rather than clean bullish positioning. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Earnings are at a cyclical/margin trough. The Hydratight restructuring stabilizes the service drag, North Sea/EMEA maintenance comparisons ease in H2 FY26, and product organic growth of +5-6% continues on infrastructure and energy-transition demand. Gross margin recovers toward 49-51% as tariffs are priced through and service mix shifts higher. FY27 EPS reaches the high-$2.20s, and a buyback that shrinks the share count ~4% amplifies the recovery. Re-rating to ~20x → ~$46 (+27%).

**Bear case.** The service decline reflects structural scope/share loss, not just cyclicality, and continues past H2 FY26. Tariffs remain only partially offset, product growth decelerates into a softening industrial backdrop, and the post-ASCEND margin peak proves to be behind the company. FY26 EPS settles near $1.85, estimates keep falling, and a goodwill impairment surfaces. Derate to ~15x → ~$28 (-23%).

The balance of evidence is two-sided, which is why this is a moderate-conviction HOLD rather than a BUY or AVOID. The balance sheet and product franchise argue against AVOID; the falling estimates and rolled-over margins argue against BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next one-to-two earnings prints — Q3 FY26 (reported ~late June 2026) and Q4 FY26 (~October 2026) — are decisive. These will show whether the service organic decline narrows and whether gross margin recovers off the 46.4% Q2 trough.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Service organic decline narrows to better than -10% YoY, signaling the Hydratight trough is in.
- Gross margin recovers above 49% on at least one quarter, confirming tariff pass-through and margin durability.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year and next-year estimates rising rather than falling).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Service organic decline persists worse than -15% into H2 FY26, or product organic growth decelerates below 3%.
- Gross margin compresses further below 46% or operating margin falls below mid-teens.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or covenant headroom narrowing materially.

This is a growth-recovery hold, not an income hold; the dividend yield of ~0.1% provides no support. The thesis is appreciation contingent on the service trough resolving; absent that, the holding has no carry to justify patience.

## Conclusion

Enerpac is a high-quality, financially healthy niche leader whose well-documented margin transformation is now meeting a cyclical service decline, tariff cost pressure, and weak organic growth. The balance sheet (Z-score 3.0, ROE ~31%, strong FCF, low leverage) and the product franchise (+5-6% organic) are genuine strengths that keep this out of AVOID territory. The forward multiple (~16.7x next-year EPS) is fair but not compelling, and the analyst targets near $50 look stale against falling estimates. The recovery growth thesis — service stabilization via the Hydratight restructuring, easing comparisons in H2 FY26, product strength, and buyback-driven share-count reduction supporting ~15% implied next-year EPS growth — is specific and falsifiable, but conviction is moderate because near-term estimates are still being cut and margins have only just rolled over.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** The position is worth retaining for the recovery optionality, with the next two prints as the checkpoint. Upgrade to BUY on confirmed service stabilization and margin recovery; downgrade to AVOID if the service decline proves structural and margins compress further. New buyers should wait for evidence the service trough is in rather than anticipate it.
