**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The Timken Company is a top-tier global designer and manufacturer of engineered bearings and industrial motion products, with $4.58B of 2025 sales, an investment-grade balance sheet, and a 415-quarter dividend track record. The investment story is a mid-cyclical earnings recovery layered on a strategic repositioning. After three years of flat revenue and a ~24% adjusted-EPS decline (peak $7.05 in 2023 to $5.33 in 2025) compounded by ~$65M of incremental 2025 tariff costs, Q1 2026 showed a sharp, broad-based turn: revenue +8% YoY, operating margin recovery of 110bps to 13.7%, and an Industrial Motion segment EBITDA margin jump of 380bps to 21.5%. Management raised the full-year 2026 outlook to roughly +5% revenue growth and, at its May 2026 Investor Day, issued an aggressive 2028 plan targeting ~$8.50 adjusted EPS (a >55% increase from 2025) on segment margin expansion and portfolio pruning.

The fundamental trajectory is genuinely improving, and the data supports it. Four consecutive earnings beats, current-year EPS estimates revised +5.1% over 90 days and next-year estimates +9.1%, and a backlog that grew to $2.21B at year-end all confirm the trough was 2025. The problem is price. TKR has appreciated ~93% over the trailing twelve months to all-time highs near $144, and it trades at roughly 31x trailing GAAP EPS and ~19x next-year consensus EPS of $7.21. That multiple already embeds a substantial portion of the 2028 re-rating story, which leaves limited margin of safety against a still-cyclical, capital-intensive bearings business (two-thirds of sales) executing under a CEO with less than a year in the seat.

We rate TKR a HOLD (growth_hold). The fundamental turn is real and the franchise is high quality, which is why we are not negative. The combination of a full multiple, zero open-market insider buying into a record-high stock, three CEO transitions in twelve months, and a degrading sell-side sentiment trend argues against initiating fresh capital here. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$110s, or on hard evidence that the Industrial Motion margin walk to 25-27% is tracking ahead of plan.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1899 by Henry Timken (inventor of the tapered roller bearing), Timken operates two reportable segments across 116 manufacturing and service sites in 44 countries, employing roughly 19,000 people.

**Engineered Bearings** (~66% of 2025 sales, $3.0B; ~18.9% adjusted EBITDA margin) covers tapered, spherical, cylindrical, ball, plain and housed bearings under the Timken®, GGB® and Fafnir® brands, serving wind, agriculture, construction, auto/truck, aerospace, rail and metals/mining end markets. Timken remains the global authority on tapered roller bearings.

**Industrial Motion** (~34%, $1.56B; ~19% adjusted EBITDA margin, with higher structural margin potential) is a multi-brand roll-up of precision drives (Philadelphia Gear, Cone Drive, Spinea, CGI), automatic lubrication (Groeneveld, BEKA, the newly acquired Bijur Delimon), linear motion (Rollon, Nadella, Rosa Sistemi), couplings, seals, filtration and clutches/brakes. This segment carries the company's growth and re-rating thesis through exposure to automation, robotics, medical and aerospace/defense.

Timken sits within a consolidated global bearings oligopoly alongside SKF and Schaeffler, and it co-owns the CoLinx distribution JV with both. Its differentiation rests on application engineering, product performance and a strong aftermarket mix (roughly 40% distribution/end-user, 60% OEM) that captures recurring replacement-cycle revenue. No single customer exceeds 5% of sales, and backlog grew to $2.21B at year-end 2025 from $2.02B, a constructive demand signal.

## Financial Analysis

The financials show a three-year downcycle that turned in Q1 2026.

| Operating income ($M) | 607 | 657 | 611 | 541 | 169 (+17.1%) |

The 2023-2025 erosion was a function of soft demand across auto/truck, heavy industry and renewables, compounded by tariffs. The single largest 2025 swing was $65M of incremental tariff cost flowing through COGS; CEO transition charges added $20.8M, and a pension mark-to-market loss of roughly $11M hit Q4. Stripping those out, core operations held up materially better than the headline EPS decline from $5.47 to $4.11 would suggest. This matters for the recovery case: much of the margin decline was transitory or self-inflicted, not structural.

Q1 2026 confirmed the turn. Adjusted EPS came in at $1.67 versus a $1.50 estimate (an 11.1% beat). The Industrial Motion margin expansion of 380bps on volume and price/mix is the most important data point — it is the engine of the 2028 plan. Engineered Bearings margin slipped 120bps as $16M of segment tariff cost outpaced pricing, which is the one soft spot.

Cash generation is solid. 2025 FCF was a record $406M (8.9% margin), up from $306M in 2024, aided by working-capital release. Q1'26 FCF of just $0.5M reflects seasonal working-capital build to support growth, not deterioration. The balance sheet is sound but leveraged for the business: net debt/adjusted EBITDA of ~2.0-2.1x, net debt/capital of 32-34%, investment-grade ratings (Moody's Baa2 / S&P BBB-), with ample covenant headroom (net leverage 2.15x against a 3.5x max, interest coverage 8.2x against a 3.0x minimum). The headline D/E of 2.1x and Altman Z of 2.21 overstate risk: the equity base is depressed by $1.5B of goodwill and $1.0B of intangibles from the acquisition strategy, not by distress. Timken reduced total debt by $141M in 2025 and authorized a 10M-share buyback (~14% of shares) in February 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two layers: a cyclical recovery and a structural mix shift.

The cyclical layer is confirmed by the data. Four straight earnings beats, accelerating estimate revisions (current-year EPS +5.1% over 90 days, next-year +9.1%, with next-year accelerating to +5.5% over just the last 30 days), and the rising backlog all indicate end-market demand is recovering off the 2025 trough. Management's raised +5% revenue guide for 2026 sits above the +2-4% range in the 10-K.

The structural layer is the Investor Day plan. New CEO Lucian Boldea's "Elevate to Outperform" strategy reorients Timken around three verticals — aerospace & defense, power & electrification, and automation & industrial — and targets 2028 sales of $5.0-5.2B (mid-single-digit organic CAGR), Industrial Motion adjusted EBITDA margin of 25-27% (from ~19%), Engineered Bearings of 21-23% (from 18.9%), and adjusted EPS of roughly $8.50. Portfolio surgery is already underway: the $124.3M Bijur Delimon acquisition (higher-margin automated lubrication) and the agreed sale of the lower-margin, previously-impaired Belts business to Gates (closing Q3 2026, at a book loss) demonstrate willingness to take losses to upgrade mix.

J.P. Morgan's May 26 upgrade to Overweight explicitly ties Timken to the robotics and AI-infrastructure capex cycle through its motion-control exposure. The estimate-revision data is supportive but does not clear the most aggressive BUY threshold — current-year revisions of +5.1% over 90 days are positive momentum, not the +30% velocity that flags systematic underestimation. The growth is real and broad-based, but it is being recognized in real time rather than being missed by consensus.

The qualifier on the entire growth narrative: roughly two-thirds of revenue remains mature, cyclical, capital-intensive bearings subject to structural price competition. The re-rating rests heavily on Industrial Motion executing the margin walk, which is unproven beyond a single strong quarter.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the case for new capital weakens. At ~$138, TKR trades at ~26x the 2025 adjusted EPS base of $5.33; on the 2028 target of ~$8.50 it is ~16x, but that requires fully crediting the earnings increase that is currently one quarter into a multi-year plan.

Against peers in Industrial Machinery & Components, the picture is mixed. The peer median P/E is 29.4 (Donaldson 23.0, Flowserve 28.7, Valmont 29.4, Zurn 39.1). Timken's ~31x trailing is roughly in line with the median, and its ~19x next-year is a reasonable discount once the recovery earnings are credited. So the stock is not egregiously expensive relative to the cohort. But the dividend yield of ~1.0% is the lowest meaningful support in the group, and TKR's 52-week discount of essentially zero (trading at its high) contrasts with peers sitting 1-24% below their highs — Timken has already captured the re-rating others have not.

The analyst-target-as-floor principle applies only partially here. The mean target of $137.45 sits essentially at the current price, the median is $140, and the high is $158 (~15% upside in the bull case). The sentiment trend is flagged as **degrading**, and the recommendation mix has not improved (2 strong buy, 4 buy, 6 hold). When a stock at ~19x forward with positive but moderate revisions trades at consensus while sentiment degrades, the targets are not behaving as a floor that consensus chases upward. They are converging on the price.

The valuation dimension is the one we treat with the most skepticism in either direction. This is a fully-priced recovery, not a cheap one. The +93% twelve-month appreciation is not itself a reason to mark the stock down; it reflects real fundamental improvement. But measured against current and forward fundamentals today, the multiple prices in execution of a margin plan that has barely begun. We score valuation 4.5: the stock is fairly-to-fully valued with limited asymmetry, and the burden of proof sits on the bull to show why the 2028 plan deserves to be capitalized this far in advance.

## Competitive Landscape

Timken's competitive position is stable-to-improving and supports a mid-tier-to-good score. The bearings business is a consolidated oligopoly (SKF, Schaeffler) where Timken differentiates on application engineering, product performance and a high aftermarket mix that generates recurring replacement-cycle revenue. Customer diversification is excellent (no customer >5% of sales). The rising backlog signals share is at minimum holding through the recovery.

The deliberate strategic shift toward higher-growth motion-control, automation and aerospace/defense verticals is a constructive trajectory: Timken is repositioning toward markets with structural tailwinds (robotics, electrification) where its acquired brands (Cone Drive, Spinea, CGI) have credible positions. The Industrial Motion margin expansion in Q1 is early evidence the mix-upgrade strategy is working.

The offset to a higher score: the core franchise is mature and price-competitive, and the five-year cumulative TSR of 118 versus a peer 191 (a gap that widened every year from 2021 to 2025) is hard evidence the company has underperformed its competitive set over a full cycle. The trajectory is improving, but the level of demonstrated outperformance is not yet there. We score competitive position 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs — the dominant near-term swing factor, and a genuine two-way catalyst.** The legal status is in flux: the Supreme Court invalidated the broad IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, and a March 2026 Court of International Trade order (immediately suspended) directed CBP to process refunds. The government has signaled it will re-impose tariffs under alternative authority. This is asymmetric and underappreciated by standardized screens: refunds could surprise 2026 earnings to the upside, while a new tariff regime sustains the headwind. Either outcome is binary and outside management's control.

**Cyclicality.** Two-thirds of revenue is capital-intensive bearings exposed to auto/truck, heavy industry and renewables. The 2023-2025 downcycle triggered ~$137M of impairment/restructuring over five years. The current recovery is real but young; a macro stall (the world model's H2 consumer/industrial cliff scenario) would pressure the volume assumptions underpinning the 2028 plan.

**Input costs and FX.** SBQ steel is the key input, recovered only partially through surcharges. A large share of sales/costs are non-USD; FX swung equity +$216M in 2025 versus -$156M in 2024.

**Pension mark-to-market.** Timken recognizes actuarial gains/losses immediately in Q4; a 25bp discount-rate move swings the PBO by $8.3M, introducing fourth-quarter earnings volatility.

**Acquisition integration.** Industrial Motion is a multi-brand roll-up with $1.5B of goodwill; the Belts & Chain reporting unit was already impaired to zero in 2024. The margin-expansion plan depends on integrating bolt-ons cleanly.

**Management instability** (detailed below) is itself a risk to plan execution.

Net, the known risks are largely cyclical/sector truisms that are priced, with two genuinely idiosyncratic items: the tariff litigation (two-sided) and execution under a new CEO. We score risk factors 5.0: the tariff outcome and CEO-execution overhang are not fully resolved, but neither is a clear asymmetric downside catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-constructive. Near-term ATM IV of 41.2% is rich versus 33.1% historical vol (8.1pp spread), but the IV percentile within the chain is only 24%, and put/call skew is balanced (-1.3%). The put/call volume ratio of 0.19 and OI ratio of 0.32 indicate no meaningful put-demand or hedging pressure; the market is not pricing a left-tail event. The term structure is in contango (41.2% near-term vs. 45.5% six-month), a normal/complacent shape, though the rising six-month skew (-4.2%) hints at modest hedging into later catalysts (likely the tariff resolution and subsequent prints). IV rose 2.9pp alongside a 10.8% price gain since late May, consistent with momentum rather than fear. The implied ±8.2% move on the nearest expiry is modest. The options market is sanguine and does not challenge the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$158, ~+15%):** The Q1 turn proves durable, Industrial Motion margins track toward 25-27%, and the recovery cycle plus robotics/automation exposure carries adjusted EPS toward the 2028 ~$8.50 target faster than expected. Tariff refunds materialize, adding a one-time earnings tailwind. Consensus revises 2027-2028 estimates upward and the multiple holds at ~19-21x rising earnings. The stock re-rates with the motion-control theme.

**Bear Case (~$110-115, ~-17% to -20%):** Industrial demand stalls (consistent with a broad H2 industrial/consumer cliff), the Industrial Motion margin walk slows after the easy first-quarter comparison, and new tariffs replace the invalidated IEEPA regime, sustaining the COGS headwind. With the stock at all-time highs and ~19x forward already pricing the recovery, any guidance disappointment compresses the multiple back toward the low-$110s where it traded in April. The single-quarter margin proof point fails to generalize.

**Base Case (~$140-145, flat to ~+5%):** The recovery continues at a measured pace, 2026 delivers ~+5% revenue and adjusted EPS around the rising consensus ($6.15 current year, $7.21 next year), and the stock tracks earnings growth without further multiple expansion, since it is already at a peer-consistent multiple at its high. Even modest multiple expansion toward the high-end target requires the multiple to hold, but degrading sentiment and a full starting valuation argue for a more conservative mid-$140s base.

## Management Quality

This is the weakest dimension and a primary reason the score does not clear BUY. Timken churned through three CEOs in roughly twelve months: Richard Kyle (since 2014) handed to Tarak Mehta in September 2024, who departed "without cause" after ~6 months in March 2025 (with a $9.3M settlement), Kyle returned as interim, and Lucian Boldea was named permanent CEO on September 1, 2025. CEO transition charges hit both 2024 ($3.7M) and 2025 ($20.8M), and the near-monthly cadence of Item 5.02 8-K filings underscores broader leadership turnover continuing into 2026.

Capital allocation is otherwise disciplined: 415 consecutive quarterly dividends, 12 straight years of increases, a sensibly-sized 10M-share buyback, debt reduction of $141M in 2025, and mix-upgrading M&A (buying higher-margin Bijur Delimon, selling the impaired Belts unit). Compensation metrics are well-constructed and shareholder-aligned (adjusted EPS, EBITDA margin, FCF, ROIC, relative TSR). But the five-year TSR of 118 versus peer 191 quantifies the underperformance the new strategy must fix.

The most telling forward signal is insider activity: all recent Form 4 transactions are sells and routine grants, with **zero open-market buying** despite management having just issued a >55% EPS uplift target. Former/interim CEO and director Kyle sold ~54,000 shares in May-June 2026 at $117-127, and founding-family directors (including John M. Timken Jr.) also sold into the rally. These are largely disposals into strength and are low-signal individually, but the complete absence of conviction buying alongside a bullish 2028 reset stands out. Management quality scores 4.5: good capital allocation offset by severe leadership instability and no insider conviction behind the new plan.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), which will test whether the Industrial Motion margin trajectory and Engineered Bearings tariff recovery generalize beyond Q1. The Q3 close of the Belts divestiture and any resolution of the tariff litigation are the other near-term inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$110s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetry (forward P/E compressing toward ~15x next-year EPS).
- Q2/Q3 confirmation that Industrial Motion adjusted EBITDA margin is sustaining ≥21% (tracking the 25-27% target) with Engineered Bearings margin recovering as tariff pricing catches up.
- A favorable tariff-litigation outcome (confirmed refunds) that adds a quantified earnings tailwind, or open-market insider buying signaling conviction behind the 2028 plan.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Industrial Motion margin reverses below ~19% or full-year revenue guidance is cut below +3%, signaling the Q1 turn was a comparison artifact.
- New tariff regime replaces the invalidated IEEPA tariffs at a scale exceeding the 2025 $65M headwind, with no offsetting price recovery.
- Further C-suite departures (a fourth CEO transition or CFO exit) that materially raise execution risk on the 2028 plan.

## Conclusion

Timken is a high-quality, investment-grade industrial franchise in a confirmed mid-cyclical recovery, with a credible management plan to expand margins and shift mix toward secular-growth verticals. The Q1 2026 turn is real and broad-based, estimate revisions are positive, and the four-beat track record supports confidence in the recovery earnings. None of that is in dispute.

The recommendation is constrained by price and timing, not by the business. At ~31x trailing and ~19x next-year EPS at an all-time high after a +93% twelve-month run, the stock already capitalizes a meaningful share of a 2028 plan that is one quarter into execution under a CEO with under a year in the role. Sell-side sentiment is degrading, the consensus target sits at the current price, there is no insider conviction buying, and the franchise has underperformed its peer cohort over a full cycle. Industrials BUYs as a group carry a negative alpha bias against the market, which raises the bar for a fresh long here.

We rate TKR a **HOLD** with a growth_hold rationale: the 12-month thesis is positive and catalyst-rich, but the entry point does not offer adequate margin of safety. We would move to BUY on a pullback toward the low-$110s or on Q2/Q3 evidence that the Industrial Motion margin walk is tracking ahead of plan. Overall score: 5.6.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following correction may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the next-year forward P/E was overstated (~22x vs. actual ~19x on $7.21 consensus EPS), making the stock modestly cheaper on a forward basis than the report's valuation discussion implies; this strengthens, rather than weakens, the case against the HOLD.
