## Executive Summary

Selective's stock rose 11.8% over the past two quarters (from $85.75 on June 3 to $95.92 today, +22.5% over twelve months) while the underwriting business measurably deteriorated. Net premiums written fell 5% in Q2 2026 and 3% for the six months — the first contraction after years of growth. Direct new business premiums dropped 17% year over year to $206.1M. Renewal pure price decelerated to 6.5% from 9.9%. Standard Commercial retention fell from 85% to 82%, and E&S retention to 61% from 65%. Current-year loss ratios worsened in the lines where the company is already losing money: commercial auto's current-year loss ratio rose 9.1 points to 58.2%, general liability to 74.8% from 72.2%, and E&S to 45.4% from 40.3%. The Q2 operating EPS of $1.95 beat the $1.72 non-GAAP consensus, and the market took the shares down anyway. That reaction was the correct read.

The structural issue is that SIGI's reported 14.4% FY2025 ROE is overwhelmingly a bond portfolio. The 10-K's own ROE decomposition attributes 13.5 points to investments and 3.4 points to insurance operations, with a 2.5-point "other" drag. After-tax net investment income of $421.2M was 3.9x after-tax underwriting income of $107.4M, on invested assets of 3.32x common equity. That is normal construction for a P&C insurer, but it means the equity's earnings power depends far more on reinvestment yields and reserve adequacy than on premium growth — and reserve adequacy is where the record is weak. General liability and commercial auto produced $558.9M of cumulative adverse development over 2023-2025, offset by $209.5M of workers' compensation releases whose feedstock is shrinking (WC net reserves fell $55.7M year over year while total net reserves grew $780.0M, and WC premium is down 8% with renewal price at -4.0%).

At $95.92 the shares trade at 1.65x reported book value of $58.13, 1.58x adjusted book of $60.56, 12.8x trailing EPS of $7.49 and roughly 11.8x a 2026 operating EPS build of ~$8.16 that assumes zero prior-year casualty development and a 6-point catastrophe load. H1 2026 already absorbed 5.9 of those 6.0 points before peak hurricane season, against a Northeast/Mid-Atlantic footprint. Six insiders including the CEO and CFO bought in the open market at $75.72-$77.89; the company repurchased at an average of $78.28 in FY2025 and roughly $86.76 in H1 2026; the General Counsel sold 17,100 shares at $94.04 in July. Every one of those revealed-preference prices sits below today's. Probability-weighted expected return from here is modestly negative. We move to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Selective is a super-regional property & casualty insurer domiciled in New Jersey, marking its 100th year in 2026, underwriting through ten subsidiaries bound by an intercompany pooling agreement that lets all ten share a uniform AM Best A+ rating. FY2025 net premiums written of $4,866.5M split into Standard Commercial Lines $3,837.7M (79% of NPW, 98.3% combined ratio), Excess & Surplus $631.2M (13%, 87.8%), and Standard Personal Lines $397.7M (8%, 100.6%), for a total combined ratio of 97.2%.

The mix within Standard Commercial is the defining fact: general liability 33%, commercial automobile 31%, commercial property 21%, workers' compensation 8%. Roughly two-thirds of the largest segment sits in the two lines carrying the industry's most acute social-inflation problem, and only a fifth sits in the short-tail property line that has been carrying the earnings. Distribution runs through independent agents; growth levers are deeper penetration of existing agencies (net ~100 agency locations added in 2025 versus ~200 in 2024) and geographic expansion — fourteen states added since 2017, contributing $430M of 2025 premium (~9% of NPW) and roughly one point of marginal growth. Montana and Wyoming went live July 1, 2026.

The Community and Public Services unit — schools, religious institutions, child-care and social-service providers — is approximately 10% of Standard Commercial NPW. That is the abuse-and-molestation book, exposed to states that have reopened or extended statutes of limitations, producing claims decades after the alleged acts.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue compounded from $3,558.1M in 2022 to $5,336.9M in 2025, but the growth rate decayed each year (+18.9%, +14.9%, +9.8%) and has now turned negative on a written basis. Net income swung from $365.2M (2023) to $207.0M (2024, on the $316M general liability charge and a 103.0% combined ratio) to $466.4M (2025, 97.2%). Book value per share rose 18% in 2025 to $56.74, then only 2.4% in H1 2026 to $58.13.

**The 2025 result was flattered by catastrophe luck.** FY2025 net catastrophe losses were 3.5 points of the combined ratio, versus 6.5 points in 2024 and the 6.0 points management uses in its own 2026 plan. Normalizing the 2.5-point shortfall against FY2025 net premiums earned of $4,768.2M gives $119.2M pretax, $94.2M after tax, or roughly 3.0 points of ROE on average common equity of $3,164.6M. That puts cat-normalized 2025 operating ROE near 11.2% — below the company's 12% target and below its disclosed ten-year average of 12.1%, despite management describing the year as having exceeded target. The offsetting adjustment is real: 2025 also absorbed $90M of unfavorable prior-year casualty development, worth about 2.25 points of after-tax ROE, so a fully "planned-basis" figure (zero development, 6-point cats) lands nearer 13.4%. The honest conclusion is a range of roughly 11%-13.5% for underlying earning power, centered materially below the reported 14.4%.

**The combined-ratio bridge shows the underlying pick got worse, not better.** FY2025 versus FY2024: prior-year casualty development 1.9% (from 7.1%), current-year casualty loss costs **46.6% (from 43.1%)**, net catastrophe 3.5% (from 6.5%), non-cat property 14.3% (from 15.6%). The headline 5.8-point improvement in the combined ratio (97.2% from 103.0%) came from lapping the 2024 GL charge and from light cats. Current-year casualty loss costs rose 3.5 points on "elevated severity trend assumptions attributable to social inflation."

**Line-level economics are stark.** In FY2025, commercial property (80.3% CR, +$151.4M), workers' comp (75.6%, +$75.8M) and E&S (87.8%, +$73.9M) produced $301.1M of underwriting income. General liability (108.5%, -$104.5M) and commercial auto (110.6%, -$122.7M) destroyed $227.2M. Total all-lines underwriting income was $135.9M. Sixty-four percent of Standard Commercial premium sits in the two loss-making lines.

**Cash generation and capital are genuinely solid.** Operating cash flow of $1,233.0M in 2025 equalled 25% of NPW against capex of $38.7M. Total debt is $902M with debt-to-capital of 20.0% (19.7% at 6/30/26). NPW-to-statutory-surplus improved to 1.36x from 1.60x, and to ~1.30x at 6/30/26 — though a shrinking premium base mechanically improves that ratio, so it is not purely a strength signal. Ratings: AM Best A+ stable, Moody's A2, Fitch A+ (reaffirmed late April 2026), S&P A. Insurance subsidiaries upstreamed $140M to the parent in H1 2026 against zero in all of 2025, restoring parent liquidity to ~$391M with $466M of 2026 ordinary dividend capacity. The holding-company liquidity bind that appeared in 2025 has substantially relieved, though 2025 demonstrated the mechanism can bind.

Two second-order items. First, the February 2025 issuance of $400M of 5.90% senior notes carries an after-tax cost of about 4.66% against a 4.2% after-tax fixed-income portfolio yield — negative spread of roughly 46bp on a standalone basis. The economic case rests on the $200M contributed down to the subsidiaries in March 2025 supporting underwriting leverage and rebuilding surplus after the 2024 charge; NPW/surplus improving from 1.60x to 1.36x is consistent with that. Second, book value growth stalled in H1 2026 for balance-sheet rather than earnings reasons: the gap between adjusted and reported BVPS (after-tax unrealized investment losses per share) widened from $1.17 to $2.43, absorbing roughly $1.26/share of the period's earnings into AOCI on a 4.1-year-duration portfolio. Adjusted BVPS rose 4.6% to $60.56, which is the better read of economic progress.

Watch items in the portfolio: CLOs and ABS at roughly $2.615B (~26% of AFS fixed income), the AFS credit-loss allowance up from $31.3M to $39.1M, and the commercial mortgage loan allowance up from $0.213M to $3.464M on a ~$273M book (0.08% to ~1.27%). Individually immaterial to earnings; directionally worth monitoring.

**Earnings quality on the print record is inconsistent.** The last four quarters: +15.2%, -10.8%, +23.8%, -10.2% versus consensus. Two beats, two misses, with swings above 10% in every quarter. That is not a base to lean on for forward-estimate confidence.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has inverted. Q2 2026 NPW fell 5% (Standard Commercial -6%, GL -4%, commercial auto -7%, commercial property -4%, workers' comp -8%, Personal Lines -8%) and E&S declined 2% — its first decline after growth of +29% and +11% in the prior two years. New business premium fell 17%. Personal Lines new business fell 37% in 2025 on a 50% decline in new policy counts, then roughly another third in Q2 2026.

Management frames the contraction as deliberate remediation, and the segmented retention data supports the framing: per earnings-call commentary (secondary reporting, unconfirmed against a filing), retention in the weakest renewal cohorts was driven from 81% to 55% while the best cohorts hold near 89%. Pushing 13% rate on commercial auto and 11%+ on general liability while shedding the worst business is the correct response to three years of adverse development. But the arithmetic consequence is unavoidable: in an independent-agency model, sustained NPW contraction risks shelf space that is expensive to regain, and earned premium follows written premium with a one-to-two-quarter lag. H1 2026 net premiums earned were still up 3.7% to $2,432.7M; that tailwind reverses through 2027.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and should not be read as an inflection signal. Current-year consensus rose 3.6% in 30 days and 3.8% in 90 days to $8.15 — driven by the raise in after-tax net investment income guidance to $480M from $465M, not by underwriting. Current-quarter estimates fell 0.3%/1.6%, and next-year estimates fell 0.9%/0.8% to $8.78. Analyst distribution has degraded: 1 strong buy, 1 buy, 5 holds and 1 sell, versus 1/2/4/1 three months ago.

Geographic expansion is the only structural growth lever, and it is slow: $430M of premium from fourteen states, roughly one point of marginal growth. The stated shift in new-business mix away from contractors (43% of commercial premium in 2025 versus ~33% of H1 2026 new business) is a change in flow, not in the in-force book — segment mix (GL 33% / auto 31%) was flat year over year. This should not be described as achieved diversification.

## Competitive Landscape

The filings describe a bifurcated market, and SIGI is on the wrong side of both halves.

**Casualty is hardening because it must.** SIGI's GL renewal pure price accelerated from +5.4% (2023) to +8.6% (2024) to +11.3% (2025), and commercial auto is being pushed toward 13% in 2026. The drivers named in the 10-K are industry-wide: rising plaintiff-attorney involvement rates, third-party litigation funding, longer settlement times, broadening liability theories. Commercial auto frequency remains below pre-pandemic levels; the entire problem is severity. When a carrier raises both the current-year loss pick (+9.1 points in Q2) and the price (13%) simultaneously, it is signalling it does not yet believe it has caught the trend.

**Property and E&S are softening, and those are the lines paying the bills.** Commercial property renewal price decelerated from +10.0% (2024) to +7.9% (2025) to +3.6% (Q2 2026); E&S from +7.2% to +8.5% to +3.4%. The 10-K attributes E&S deceleration to "more capacity entering the E&S marketplace and the admitted markets' appetite for insureds previously written by E&S companies." The reinsurance market corroborates: 2026 ceded catastrophe premium is falling on risk-adjusted rate decreases, and softening reinsurance transmits to softening primary property pricing with a lag. Against a 2025 non-cat property loss ratio of 35.9%, low-single-digit rate leaves cushion, but the direction of the margin buffer is set.

**Workers' comp is in an exogenous rate downcycle.** Renewal price has been negative four periods running (-1.5%, -2.8%, -3.6%, then -4.0% in Q2 2026) because bureau loss costs keep falling. The line's 75.6% combined ratio in 2025 was flattered by 29.0 points of favorable development; the current-year loss cost ratio rose 8.0 points to 77.4%, meaning accident-year economics are near breakeven excluding releases.

The five-year total shareholder return in the proxy is the market's summary judgment: SIGI at $134.47 from a $100 base at 12/31/2020 versus the peer group at $181.29 — a 46.8-point cumulative shortfall, with the 2024 GL charge as the fulcrum. Competitive position is best described as a real but contestable regional franchise (agency relationships and Northeast/Mid-Atlantic underwriting depth) whose trajectory is currently negative on retention, new business, and mix.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Long-tail casualty reserve concentration — dominant.** At 12/31/25, net reserves of $6,347.6M were 79.4% concentrated in three long-tail buckets: GL $2,932.7M (46%), commercial auto $1,349.3M (21%), E&S casualty $755.1M (12%). Net reserves are 1.86x common equity with a 3.0-year duration. The disclosed range of reasonable estimates is $5,694M-$6,960M; recorded reserves sit at 51.6% of the range width, $20.6M above the midpoint. The high-end gap is $612.4M pretax, $484M after tax, **14.2% of common equity or roughly $8.05 per share**. The company's own sensitivity tables are more severe: a 15% adverse move in GL reported development alone is $500M pretax. Carrying at the midpoint is what best-estimate accounting requires and is neutral evidence in itself, but it means there is no visible margin absorbing a fourth consecutive year of casualty deterioration.

**2. No catastrophe cushion left in the 2026 guide.** Guidance is a 96.5%-97.5% combined ratio including 6 points of cats and zero prior-year casualty development. H1 2026 ran a 98.1% combined ratio and consumed **5.9 of the 6.0 catastrophe points** ahead of peak Atlantic hurricane season, against a Northeast/Mid-Atlantic-weighted book. Hitting the midpoint would require H2 near 95.9%. Management has reportedly steered toward the top of the range. Any meaningful H2 storm activity breaks the guide, and the sensitivity is roughly $0.80 of full-year operating EPS per additional 1.5 points of combined ratio (the scenario table implies ~$0.80 of operating EPS per point of combined ratio at this NPE level).

**3. The workers' comp release cushion is depleting.** 2025's $90.0M release equalled 12.1% of the ending $741.2M WC net reserve balance. The shock absorber that offset 37.5% of three years of GL/auto damage has finite and shrinking capacity. Counterpoint: releases derive from accident-year medical-severity re-estimates, not mechanically from balances, and could persist if medical inflation stays subdued.

**4. Catastrophe tail and a dated reinsurance event.** The 2026 program provides $1.4B in excess of a $100M retention, exhausting near a 1-in-211-year return period. Modeled net after-tax losses as a percentage of 12/31/25 equity: 1-in-100 = 3%, 1-in-250 = 5% ($184.2M), 1-in-500 = 20% ($711.1M ≈ $11.83/share). The **$325M High Point Re catastrophe bond expires December 31, 2026** and must be renewed or replaced; it excludes California, Florida, Texas and Louisiana. All NBCR losses are excluded from every property treaty.

**5. Social inflation and abuse/molestation reviver statutes.** With CAPS at ~10% of Standard Commercial NPW and states extending or reopening statutes of limitations to permit claims decades after alleged acts, this is a quantifiable exposure the filings treat explicitly rather than as boilerplate.

**6. Mitigants, fairly stated.** No adverse prior-year casualty development was recorded in Q2 or H1 2026 (with roughly $27M of favorable property development). Effective July 1, 2026, the casualty treaty's first-layer co-participation was cut from 20% to 8% — SIGI is now ceding materially more casualty severity, a deliberate de-risking step. The property per-risk limit rose to $115M xs $5M. Ceded catastrophe premium falls in 2026. Ratings are affirmed across all four agencies, leverage is 19.7%, and parent liquidity has been restored.

**7. Unresolved items.** Four Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed in fourteen months (2025-05-13, 2025-07-30, 2025-11-03, 2026-05-14), two of them bundled with Item 7.01 Regulation FD; filing contents could not be retrieved and no coverage was found. These should be treated as neither material nor immaterial. Revolver covenant thresholds (minimum consolidated net worth, maximum debt-to-capitalization) are confirmed to exist but not disclosed in the MD&A. Earnings-call detail cited above is secondary reporting, unconfirmed against a filing. The driver of the sixteenfold CML allowance increase was not verified.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 33.2% sits 8.5 points above one-year realized volatility of 24.6%, and the term structure is in backwardation (33.2% at 13 days versus 22.2% at 41 days and 24.5% at six months), with a ±5.3% implied move on the nearest expiry. That is the market pricing a discrete near-term event rather than a directional view: put/call skew is balanced at -2.7% and open-interest put/call is 0.06, i.e. overwhelmingly call-side. IV has also compressed from 36.1% on June 3 to 33.2% while the stock rose 11.8%, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 0.19 to 0.06. Two readings matter. First, the options market is not corroborating the bearish case — there is no crowded put position to fade or to lean on. Second, with only 41 contracts across three expirations, this chain is thin enough that inference should be light. The relevant conclusion for positioning is that near-term protection is expensive relative to realized volatility, so trimming exposure outright is cheaper than hedging it. Short interest of 3.8% of float and 2.7 days to cover confirms the name is uncrowded on the short side: there is no squeeze constraint, and no informed bearish consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $112 (+16.8%)

Casualty remediation works. Double-digit rate on GL and commercial auto earns through in 2027 against a loss trend that stabilizes, and the elevated current-year picks taken in Q2 2026 prove conservative rather than catch-up. Prior-year development stays at zero, as it has for two quarters. The normalized underwriting story is genuinely improving: FY2025's 97.2% less 1.9 points of development less 3.5 points of cats gives a 91.8% underlying ratio, while the 2026 guide of 96.5%-97.5% less 6.0 cat points and zero development implies 90.5%-91.5% — a 0.3 to 1.3 point underlying improvement. Higher-for-longer rates keep compounding the portfolio: after-tax NII guidance already rose to $480M, and reinvestment above the 4.2% book yield adds mechanically. Cat load comes in light again, as in 2025, and 2026 operating EPS reaches the $8.80 high end. Reduced ceded catastrophe premium and the casualty co-participation cut from 20% to 8% lower earnings volatility, and NPW/surplus at 1.30x supports both the dividend (raised 13% to $0.43 quarterly) and buybacks. On $9.00+ of 2027 earnings at 12x, or 1.65x a 2027 adjusted book near $68, the shares reach $110-112. The sell-side high target of $115 sits here.

### Bear Case — $74 (-22.8%)

The fourth consecutive year of adverse casualty development arrives. A $200-300M pretax GL/commercial auto strengthening — well inside the disclosed sensitivity of $500M for a 15% adverse GL reporting-pattern move, and roughly half the $612.4M gap to the high end of the reserve range — costs $2.60-$3.95 per share after tax and breaks the zero-development guidance premise. Simultaneously, H2 catastrophes exceed the 0.1 points of remaining budget, pushing the full-year combined ratio to 99%+ and operating EPS to $7.20 or below. The workers' comp release well runs dry as its reserve balance and premium base both shrink. Property and E&S rate at +3.6% and +3.4% fails to keep pace with trend, so the $301.1M of underwriting income from property, comp and E&S erodes just as GL and auto need it. NPW contraction persists into 2027, agency shelf space is lost, and earned premium follows written down. On $6.25 of earnings at 11x, or 1.25x book, the shares are $69-75. The sell-side low target of $80 is a milder version of this path.

### Base Case — $93 (-3.0%)

Management lands near the top of its guidance range at roughly 97.5%, delivering operating EPS near $8.16 and ROE near 14% on paper but 12%-ish normalized. Written premium keeps contracting mid-single-digits through 2026 and stabilizes in 2027 at a smaller, better-priced base. No large reserve charge, but no favorable development either. Investment income compounds. The multiple does not expand from 1.65x book, because the earnings quality behind it (3.9x investment-to-underwriting income, midpoint reserves, contracting premium) does not warrant a premium to the 8.7x peer median or to Hanover's 9.9x. Fair value of roughly 1.45x a year-end adjusted book near $64, or 11.4x base-case EPS, gives $93.

Probability-weighting base 50% / bull 25% / bear 25% gives an expected value of $93.0, or about -1% total return including the 1.79% dividend. Against a 9-10% cost of equity, that is a materially sub-fair expected return with a 23% downside tail against a 17% upside tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Scores have declined for four consecutive reviews (6.9 → 6.5 → 5.9 → 5.8 → 4.9) and the recommendation path has been BUY → BUY → HOLD → HOLD. What changed since the June 3 review is the combination: the stock rose 11.8% while written premium turned negative for the first time, price deceleration accelerated into the profitable lines, current-year loss ratios deteriorated in the loss-making ones, and E&S — the growth engine and the highest-margin segment — began contracting. That is fundamental deterioration coincident with multiple expansion, a genuine risk/reward deterioration rather than a price artifact.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength now rather than around the Q3 print. The near-term event risk is asymmetric and dated: hurricane season runs through October against essentially zero remaining catastrophe budget, and Q3 has historically been SIGI's reserve-review quarter (the shares fell 9% over October 22-23, 2025 on the Q3 print, and the 2024 GL charge drove a 103.0% full-year combined ratio). Near-term implied volatility at 33.2% versus 24.6% realized makes hedging expensive relative to simply reducing. Take exposure to roughly one-third of a full position. Retain that residual for the investment-income compounding and the 1.79% dividend, which is well covered at a 20-25% payout target (per secondary reporting on the July call; unconfirmed against a filing). Reduce to zero if either avoid trigger below fires.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
1. Price at or below **$82-85** (roughly 1.35x-1.40x adjusted book), restoring the multiple to the band where six insiders and the company itself were accumulating, with fundamentals otherwise unchanged.
2. Two consecutive quarters (Q3 and Q4 2026) with **zero or favorable prior-year casualty development** *and* a commercial auto current-year loss ratio back below 55%, evidencing that the Q2 pick increase was catch-up rather than the leading edge.
3. Written premium inflecting: NPW growth returning to flat-or-better with Standard Commercial retention recovering above 84%, confirming the remediation shed business without permanent agency-shelf loss.

**Avoid triggers.**
1. A prior-year casualty reserve strengthening of **$150M pretax or more** in any quarter, which would confirm the fourth consecutive year of adverse development and re-open the question of whether midpoint reserving is adequate.
2. Full-year 2026 combined ratio guidance withdrawn or revised above 99%, or catastrophe losses exceeding 8 points for the year.
3. Failure to renew or replace the **$325M High Point Re catastrophe bond** before its December 31, 2026 expiry on comparable economics, which would raise net catastrophe retention into a book where a 1-in-500 event already costs 20% of equity.
4. Disclosure that any of the four unexplained Item 5.02 filings involved an unannounced senior-officer transition in underwriting, actuarial or finance leadership.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 earnings in late October 2026 is the decisive checkpoint — it covers the hurricane window, the annual reserve review, and the first full quarter of the reduced casualty co-participation.
