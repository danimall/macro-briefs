**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

VIAVI Solutions has transformed from a stagnant test-and-measurement company trading near $9 into an $11.8B market cap AI-data-center and defense beneficiary that has appreciated roughly 427% from its 52-week low of $9.14. The re-rating is grounded in real fundamentals: Q3 FY26 revenue grew 42.8% YoY to $406.8M, non-GAAP operating margin expanded 430bps to 21.0%, and nine-month adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled to $233.7M. The company sits at the intersection of two durable secular themes: high-speed (400G/800G) optical and Ethernet test driven by AI data-center buildout, and resilient position/navigation/timing demand from aerospace and defense. The Spirent HSE/CE acquisition and the CyberFlood CF1000 launch position VIAVI as a picks-and-shovels supplier to the hyperscaler buildout the broader market is paying up for.

The complications are equally clear. Of the 42.8% Q3 revenue growth, $54.3M came from Spirent and an incremental $22.6M from Inertial Labs; organic growth is positive but more modest. Operating cash flow weakened to $47.2M for nine months (from $66M) on working-capital build and a $73.5M earn-out payment, and Q3 alone consumed $26.3M of operating cash. The stock trades at roughly 44x forward non-GAAP EPS, well above the level where estimate-revision momentum alone justifies a buy. The single loudest signal is insider behavior: the CEO sold over $31M across February–May 2026, taking one direct holding account to zero, alongside near-simultaneous open-market sales by multiple directors, the General Counsel, the CMO, and two segment heads, all clustered around a $45.00 equity offering.

We rate VIAVI a HOLD. The growth thesis is durable and the balance sheet is being actively de-risked, but at the current price the asymmetry that would justify a fresh buy is absent. The stock sits ~25% below the consensus analyst target range ($60–$70), but that gap is not enough to override stretched cash-flow-adjusted valuation, broad insider distribution into strength, and acquisition-integration risk. We would re-engage on a pullback toward the low-$40s (near the offering price) or on confirmation that organic growth and operating cash conversion are inflecting independently of the acquired revenue.

## Company Overview

VIAVI Solutions was spun out of the JDS Uniphase split in 2015, with Lumentum as the other half. It operates two reportable segments:

- **Network & Service Enablement (NSE)** — ~79% of revenue ($321.5M of $406.8M in Q3 FY26). NSE provides testing, monitoring, assurance, and security solutions for telecom and datacom networks (wireless, wireline, cloud, satellite, military, critical infrastructure), plus lab/production instruments and field-service tools. The growth engines within NSE are Lab & Production (data-center driven) and Aerospace & Defense / PNT. Segment operating margin reached 17.2%, up from 10.4% a year prior.

- **Optical Security & Performance Products (OSP)** — ~21% of revenue ($85.3M). OSP leverages proprietary optical coatings for anti-counterfeiting pigments (banknotes, sold through a long-standing alliance with SICPA), 3D sensing (consumer electronics), and government/automotive/industrial applications. OSP carries structurally higher margins (~35% operating) and is the company's profit anchor, though it depends heavily on a single partner relationship.

The company's competitive position rests on entrenched share in optical and fiber test, hard-to-replicate optical coating IP and volume manufacturing in OSP, deep carrier and equipment-maker relationships, and a strengthened high-speed Ethernet test position from the Spirent acquisition. VIAVI is a supplier of test and validation equipment to hyperscalers and network-equipment makers, not an AI model developer.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and growth.** Q3 FY26 revenue was $406.8M, +42.8% YoY; nine-month revenue was $1,075.2M, +35.4%. The bulk of the Q3 acceleration was acquisition-driven, with Inertial Labs contributing $22.6M (against $7.7M in the prior-year period). Stripping these contributions, organic growth is real but more modest, driven by data-center demand for lab/production and fiber/access products. Q4 FY26 guidance calls for revenue of $427–437M and non-GAAP EPS of $0.29–0.31, implying continued sequential strength.

**Profitability and the GAAP/non-GAAP gap.** The distinction between reported and adjusted profitability is unusually wide. Q3 GAAP operating margin was 6.1% versus non-GAAP operating margin of 21.0%. The gap reflects stock-based compensation (~$13.9M/quarter), acquisition intangible amortization (~$20.4M/quarter), restructuring ($17.3M in Q3), and contingent-consideration remeasurement. The nine-month GAAP net loss of $(63.1)M versus $26.8M profit a year prior is mechanically driven by a $46.2M loss on debt extinguishment and a $36.1M tax provision, not operational deterioration. Gross margin is healthy and stable at ~57% GAAP / ~62% non-GAAP.

**Cash flow — the watch item.** Operating cash flow was only $47.2M for nine months, pressured by working-capital build (accounts receivable +$62.5M, inventory +$37.3M) and a large contingent-consideration payment. Cash conversion has weakened precisely as the income statement has accelerated, which is the appropriate place for skepticism. Some of this is normal working-capital expansion to support 40%+ revenue growth, but the divergence between adjusted EBITDA and cash generation is a quality-of-earnings flag worth monitoring across the next two quarters.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$1.08B in net carrying value: $250M of 0.625% convertible notes (2031), $400M of 3.75% senior notes (2029), and a $450M Term Loan B (2032, at 6.17%). Cash is ~$499M. The $69.7B accumulated deficit is a legacy artifact of JDSU goodwill write-offs and depresses statistical distress scores misleadingly; equity is positive at $846.5M. Capital allocation is firmly oriented toward deleveraging — VIAVI prepaid $150M of the Term Loan B in H1 2026, and the $500M equity offering proceeds (May 2026) are earmarked to retire the entire remaining $450M Term Loan B. This trades higher interest expense for equity dilution at an elevated share price, a credit-positive but shareholder-dilutive move.

**Earnings surprise track record.** VIAVI has beaten consensus four straight quarters, by 10.7% to 15.6%. The consistency of the beats lends credibility to the forward estimate path.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions are strongly positive and are the most reliable forward indicator in the data. Current-quarter consensus has risen 25.7% over 90 days (to $0.30), next-quarter +23.4%, current-year +12.1% (to $0.94), and next-year +27.1% (to $1.28). The combination of +25%+ revision velocity on the near-term quarters and 35%+ revenue growth is the strongest signal in the dataset favoring a constructive growth view. We weight it accordingly in the Growth Prospects score.

The guardrail matters here. The estimate-revision buy signal is most powerful when the stock is cheap relative to the improving estimates (forward P/E below ~30x). VIAVI trades at roughly 44x the FY26 non-GAAP run-rate (~$1.10 annualized) and ~38x the $1.28 next-year estimate. At a premium multiple, beats are already partly expected and the multiple already discounts continued upward revisions, so the asymmetry that makes the signal a clean buy on inflection stocks is diluted. The growth is durable; the price already reflects much of it.

The durability case rests on two structural themes. AI data-center buildout is generating demand for high-speed optical and Ethernet test and now network-security validation (CyberFlood CF1000, a native 400G appliance launched May 2026 targeting hyperscalers validating AI infrastructure before deployment). The second theme is resilient PNT and aerospace/defense, where Inertial Labs and Jackson Labs position VIAVI in GPS-denied navigation, a growing defense priority. The counterweights: telecom carrier capex remains cyclical and consolidating (NSE Wireless is declining even as data-center and A&D grow), Asia-based competitors pressure pricing, and the network shift toward virtualization/software threatens legacy hardware test demand over time.

## Valuation Assessment

VIAVI trades at ~44x forward non-GAAP EPS and ~38x the $1.28 next-year consensus, with a GAAP P/E that is negative on the nine-month loss. Within its Communications Equipment peer set, the comparison is genuinely difficult because the group trades on distorted multiples: VSAT is loss-making (-287x), EXTR at 258x, DGII at 60x, NTCT at 32x, IDCC at 26x — a peer median P/E of 60.4 that reflects the sector's heterogeneity rather than a clean benchmark. On that basis VIAVI does not screen as expensive relative to peers, but the peer median is itself inflated by depressed or recovering earnings and is not a reliable anchor.

The more disciplined read uses cash-flow-adjusted forward earnings. The forward non-GAAP multiple is reasonable for a company growing 30%+, but the weakening operating cash conversion ($47.2M nine-month OCF against $233.7M adjusted EBITDA) argues that the non-GAAP earnings overstate near-term cash generation. A stock at 44x forward non-GAAP earnings whose cash conversion is running well below its adjusted profitability does not offer the margin of safety a cleaner converter would. The PEG on next-year estimates (~38x / ~36% next-year EPS growth) is near 1.0, which is fair rather than cheap.

The consensus analyst target range ($60 low, $70 high, $65 median) sits ~24% to ~45% above the current $48.31. On a clean inflection stock under 30x forward, we would treat that range as a floor. At a premium multiple with softening cash flow, we treat the targets as reflecting the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation. The recent $45.00 equity offering, which the company executed weeks before the current price, provides a useful reference point for where institutional buyers were willing to commit fresh capital. We assign a below-average valuation score: the price is defensible but not asymmetric, and the cash-flow gap is the specific reason we withhold a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

VIAVI's competitive trajectory is improving in its growth segments and stable-to-eroding in its legacy ones. In high-speed Ethernet and network-security test, the Spirent HSE/CE acquisition (bought from Keysight, which divested the unit to satisfy regulators in its own Spirent takeover) consolidates VIAVI's position against Keysight and others, and the CyberFlood CF1000 extends it into 400G AI-infrastructure validation. In optical/fiber test, VIAVI retains entrenched share. OSP's optical-coating and anti-counterfeiting franchise is a genuine high-margin moat with limited direct competition, offset by single-partner (SICPA) dependence and 3D-sensing mix pressure on margins.

The structural headwinds are real and underappreciated relative to the bull narrative. Telecom carrier consolidation gives customers pricing leverage; NSE Wireless is in decline; and the shift toward virtualized/software-defined networks lowers barriers to entry and threatens legacy hardware test demand. The net trajectory is favorable because the growing data-center and A&D exposures are outpacing the declining legacy lines, but the moat is widening in the new businesses while compressing in the old ones. We score competitive position above neutral, reflecting improving trajectory in the segments that matter for the forward growth story.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and convertible-note overhang.** Management explicitly flags increased principal obligations limiting flexibility. The 2031 convertible notes became convertible in the April–June 2026 window because the stock exceeded 130% of the $13.79 conversion price, forcing $244.5M to be reclassified to short-term debt and creating dilution potential. The equity offering retiring the Term Loan B is a credit-positive offset.

**Acquisition integration and earn-out volatility.** The Inertial Labs earn-out (up to $175M) has been a recurring P&L swing factor — its fair value oscillated from $116M to $139M to $68M across successive quarters, with a $73.5M cash payment already made; the Jackson Labs earn-out expired worthless. Integration of two material acquisitions must deliver synergies to justify the multiple.

**Customer and partner concentration.** A limited number of customers drive a large share of NSE revenue; OSP's anti-counterfeiting business depends on the single SICPA relationship. Greater China is ~16% of revenue, with Huawei Entity-List restrictions and shifting tariff exposure adding geopolitical risk.

**Cash conversion.** The widening gap between adjusted EBITDA and operating cash flow is the cleanest idiosyncratic risk not fully reflected at the current premium multiple. If working-capital intensity persists, the quality of the reported earnings inflection comes into question.

**Insider selling and management churn.** Covered in detail below; the breadth and timing of insider distribution into the rally is the single largest caution flag. Executive-departure 8-Ks in July 2025, August 2025, and May 2026 indicate ongoing leadership-team churn against the 10-K's own "management transitions" risk language.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is positioned constructively, not defensively. Near-term ATM IV is 92.4% against 61.3% one-year historical volatility — rich, signaling the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (the term structure is flat, with uncertainty evenly distributed across tenors). The put/call skew is heavily call-heavy (-134.4%), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.49 and OI ratio of 0.70 both lean bullish. There is no elevated put demand contradicting the upside case. The delta data shows ATM IV spiking sharply alongside a modest price decline, consistent with the market pricing a wide outcome distribution around upcoming catalysts (earnings, integration milestones) rather than directional bearishness. The rich IV argues against this being a clean low-volatility entry point.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

VIAVI is a legitimate fundamental turnaround riding two durable secular tailwinds. AI-data-center test demand (400G/800G optical, Ethernet, CyberFlood security validation) and defense PNT growth (Inertial Labs, Jackson Labs in GPS-denied navigation) provide multi-year revenue durability. Non-GAAP operating margin is expanding, adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled, estimate revisions are running +25%+ on near-term quarters, and the company has beaten consensus four straight quarters. The equity offering retires the 6.17% Term Loan B, materially de-risking the balance sheet. If organic growth sustains and cash conversion normalizes, the $1.28 next-year EPS proves conservative and the stock re-rates toward the $65 consensus and beyond. Bull case: ~$68 (consensus high $70), roughly +40% from current.

### Bear Case

The growth is acquisition-fueled, organic growth is modest, and the market has paid a full price for the inflection. Operating cash flow weakened even as the income statement accelerated, signaling a quality-of-earnings concern. Broad-based insider selling into the rally — including the CEO zeroing out a holding account — suggests those with the most information were reducing exposure near these levels. If integration falters, earn-out volatility resurfaces, telecom capex softens, or cash conversion fails to recover, the ~44x forward multiple compresses sharply. The $45 equity offering and convertible dilution cap per-share upside. Bear case: ~$36 (near the equity-offering reference and ~38x the current-year $0.94, a level the stock traded at in early April), roughly -26% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the growth thesis is genuine but the risk/reward at the current price is balanced rather than asymmetric, and the insider signal counsels patience over fresh commitment.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$40s (near the $45 equity-offering price), which would restore meaningful upside to the consensus range and improve the cash-flow-adjusted entry multiple.
- Two consecutive quarters of operating cash flow converging toward adjusted EBITDA (OCF/adjusted EBITDA recovering above ~60%), confirming the earnings inflection is cash-backed.
- Evidence that organic (ex-acquisition) revenue growth is accelerating into the mid-teens independent of Spirent/Inertial Labs contributions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Q4 FY26 or Q1 FY27 revenue or EPS miss against the $427–437M / $0.29–0.31 guide, breaking the four-quarter beat streak.
- Continued operating cash burn or a renewed working-capital deterioration despite revenue growth, signaling the reported earnings overstate cash generation.
- A reset of the Inertial Labs earn-out or a guidance cut tied to integration shortfalls, or further senior-executive departures.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q4 FY26 earnings release (expected late July/August 2026), which will test the guidance, cash conversion, and the durability of the data-center/A&D growth narrative.

## Conclusion

VIAVI Solutions has executed a real fundamental turnaround, repositioning toward AI-data-center test and defense PNT, expanding margins, and de-risking its balance sheet through a disciplined (if dilutive) equity-for-debt swap. The estimate-revision momentum and four-quarter beat streak support a constructive growth view. But the stock has appreciated ~427% from its low, trades at ~44x forward non-GAAP earnings against weakening operating cash conversion, and has seen broad, heavy insider selling into the rally, with the CEO and multiple officers and directors distributing stock right around the equity raise. Much growth is acquisition-fueled, and the premium multiple leaves little margin for integration missteps. The ~24% gap to the consensus target range is not sufficient asymmetry to override these concerns at a premium valuation with a quality-of-earnings flag. We rate VIAVI a HOLD and would prefer to re-engage on a pullback toward the offering-price area or on confirmation that organic growth and cash conversion are inflecting on their own.
