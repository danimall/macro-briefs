**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Medical Properties Trust is a hospital-focused net-lease REIT roughly eighteen months into a recovery from near-collapse. Its two largest tenants, Steward Health Care and Prospect Medical, both private-equity-backed operators, collapsed into bankruptcy in 2024–2025, cutting revenue nearly in half (from $1.54B in 2022 to $972M in 2025) and forcing ~$3.4B in cumulative net losses over three years, ~$2.8B of it non-cash impairment in 2024. The company survived by selling more than $2B of real estate, refinancing unsecured notes into secured notes, and slashing the dividend. As of early 2026 both problem tenants are essentially gone from the portfolio, portfolio EBITDARM coverage has improved to 2.6x, and management has pivoted from pure survival toward selective acquisitions and a $150M buyback.

The central analytical fact a standard data pull misses is that a large fraction of MPT's reported revenue is not yet being collected in cash. Several flagship tenants are on cash-basis accounting mid-ramp: HSA is paying roughly 50% of contractual rent, the ex-Steward re-tenanted group about 59%, and NOR (the ex-Prospect California hospitals) does not begin paying until June 2026. The entire bull case rests on these ramps reaching a $1B+ annualized cash-rent run-rate by Q4 2026. Management itself conceded HSA collections are "not where any of us would like to see them." Any slippage flows directly to dividend coverage and covenant headroom.

We rate MPT a HOLD (income_hold), with an overall score of 4.5, continuing the improving trajectory (3.1 → 4.3 → 4.4 → 4.5) but stopping short of a BUY. The 7.6% dividend yield is covered at roughly 62% of Normalized FFO, the balance-sheet crisis has been deferred rather than resolved, and the recovery is real. What keeps this from a BUY is the combination of a 0.72 Altman Z-score (deep distress), CCC+/B3 ratings, a $1.4B note due October 2027, and a bull thesis contingent on cash collections that are demonstrably behind schedule. Hold for the yield and the credible turnaround optionality; upgrade only when the cash-rent ramp shows up in reported collections.

## Company Overview

MPT is a self-advised, single-segment healthcare REIT (Maryland, REIT-electing since 2004) that owns hospital real estate leased to operators under long-term absolute-net leases, typically 15+ years with ~2% minimum escalators plus CPI bumps. It supplements rent with mortgage and working-capital loans and small non-controlling equity stakes in tenants. As of February 2026 the portfolio comprised 381 properties and ~39,000 licensed beds across 31 U.S. states, seven European countries, and Colombia: 165 general acute care hospitals, 68 behavioral health, 128 post-acute, and 20 freestanding ER/urgent care. Roughly half of assets are now outside the U.S., which distinguishes MPT from domestic-only healthcare-REIT peers and provides some insulation from U.S. Medicaid and political risk.

The moat is specialization: MPT underwrites a fragmented, hard-to-value, highly regulated asset class (customized, illiquid hospital buildings) and provides sale-leaseback capital that lets operators monetize real estate. Weighted-average remaining lease term is ~16.7 years with escalators on 99% of leases. The company runs lean, with 121 employees managing 380+ properties across nine countries. The top-five tenants by percentage of total assets are Circle Health (UK, 14.1%), Priory (UK behavioral, 8.7%), Healthcare Systems of America (US, 8.0%), Swiss Medical Network (5.8%), and Lifepoint Behavioral (5.4%). The former mega-tenants Steward and Prospect no longer appear.

The stock began trading under "MPT" in February 2026 (its 20th NYSE anniversary), having historically traded as "MPW."

## Financial Analysis

Total assets are $15.0B against $9.7B of debt and $4.6B of equity, a D/E of ~2.26x. The Altman Z-score of 0.72 sits in the deep distress zone, driven by negative EBIT/assets (-1.2%) on a leveraged, asset-heavy structure. Credit ratings are firmly sub-investment-grade: S&P CCC+ (negative outlook), Moody's B3 corporate (B2 on new secured notes).

Revenue collapsed from $1.54B (2022) to $872M (2023), recovered modestly to $996M (2024), and eased to $972M (2025) as the mega-tenant defaults and asset sales worked through. GAAP net losses ran three straight years: -$556M (2023), -$2.41B (2024), and -$277M (2025), producing a cumulative retained-earnings deficit of -$4.14B. The 2024 loss was dominated by ~$2.8B of impairments and negative fair-value marks; 2025 impairments were far smaller at ~$340M (mostly Prospect and Colombia), evidence the write-down cycle is largely behind the company.

On a REIT-relevant basis, Nareit FFO recovered to +$184M in 2025 from -$1.4B in 2024. Normalized FFO was $346M, or $0.58/share, down 28% from $0.80 as disposals reduced the rent base, Steward/Prospect cash rent was lost, and interest costs rose. The refinancing that solved the maturity problem carried a price: the weighted-average interest rate rose to 5.2% (2025) from 4.3% (2024) because MPT had to pledge collateral to access markets at a distressed rating, and interest expense jumped to $510M from $418M, now consuming roughly 52% of revenue. Operating cash flow was ~$231M (down from $245M), and cash rose to $541M from $332M.

The dividend was cut from $0.29/quarter (early 2023) to $0.15, then to $0.08, and has since been nudged back to $0.09/quarter ($0.36 annualized). Against Normalized FFO of $0.58, that is a ~62% payout, with the credit facility capping dividends at 95% of Normalized FFO. The earnings-surprise record is mixed but improving: three beats in the last four quarters (Q1 2026 EPS $0.05 vs $0.03 estimate, Q4 2025 $0.03 vs $0.01), with one large Q3 2025 miss (-$0.13 on impairment). These earnings ramps are aided by one-time catch-up payments (Vibra $18M, HSA $4M in Q4 2025), so headline FFO quality carries some noise.

Financial Health scores a 3.5. The distress-zone Z-score, sub-investment-grade ratings, interest consuming over half of revenue, and cash-basis revenue that is not fully collected are structural weaknesses. The offsets, cash cushion up YoY, permanent covenant relief negotiated in February 2025, and a manageable near-term maturity picture, keep it above the ≤3 range but well below neutral.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here means recovery, not expansion. The headline goal is a $1B+ annualized cash-rent run-rate by Q4 2026, up from a level currently suppressed by cash-basis tenants collecting well below contractual rent. Three ramps drive it: HSA (~50% of contractual rent, targeting 100% by Q4 2026), the ex-Steward re-tenanted group (~59% ex three North Dakota facilities, targeting 100% by Q4 2026), and NOR (ex-Prospect California, starting June 2026 and ramping to $45M/yr by December 2026). If these hit schedule, reported cash flow inflects materially even without new acquisitions, because the contractual rent already exists and is simply not being collected yet.

Underlying operating metrics support the direction. General acute operators added over $130M of EBITDARM YoY; post-acute added ~$50M for a second consecutive quarter (Ernest +15%, Vibra +28%, Median +8%). Median (Germany) posted its strongest quarter ever (EBITDARM +20% YoY, 90% occupancy) on improving reimbursement and orthopedics demand. The weak spots are behavioral: UK Priory is pressured by NHS budget and referral constraints (a funding issue, not a demand one), and U.S. behavioral is squeezed by nursing labor costs.

Estimate revisions are genuinely mixed rather than the clean +30% signal that would flag a stronger buy. Current-year EPS was revised +37.5% over 30 days (to $0.11) but is down 8.3% over 90 days; next-year was revised -11.1% over 30 days (to $0.15) but up 8.6% over 90 days; and the current-quarter estimate collapsed to zero. This is a low-analyst-coverage name (one to three estimators), so revision data is noisy and does not clear the bar for a decisive momentum signal. Growth Prospects scores 4.8: a credible, quantifiable recovery path with confirming operating metrics, discounted for the fact that the largest single input (cash-rent collection) is behind schedule and the growth is a recovery to a lower base rather than durable expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

MPT trades at $4.87 (from the most recent price series), roughly 25% below its $6.47 52-week high and ~23% above its $3.95 low, at a ~7.6% dividend yield and ~8.4x Normalized FFO. Trailing GAAP P/E is negative and not meaningful given impairment-driven losses; the relevant metrics are FFO multiple, NAV, and yield.

Against Health Care REIT peers (median P/E ~29.3, median yield ~6.1%), MPT's yield is a premium and its FFO multiple sits well below peers such as SBRA (29.3x GAAP) and CTRE (23.4x). That discount reflects a CCC+ balance sheet, a distress-zone Z-score, and cash-basis tenants, which is a setup where a cheap multiple can be a value trap absent a catalyst. The identifiable catalyst is the cash-rent ramp: if MPT reaches the $1B+ run-rate, Normalized FFO re-rates toward the low-$0.70s and the yield compresses, supporting a materially higher price. Management repurchasing 4.5M shares at ~$5.20 in 2025 signals it views NAV above the current price.

Analyst targets bracket the outcome: low $4.50, median $5.00, mean $5.71, high $8.00. We do not treat the median as a floor; this is a distressed-credit turnaround where targets appropriately reflect wide dispersion around a binary cash-collection outcome. Valuation scores 5.0: the discount to peers and to NAV is real and there is a specific catalyst, but the catalyst is unproven and the discount partly reflects genuine structural impairment. The score sits at neutral rather than higher because a low multiple on a distressed REIT warrants a catalyst before rewarding cheapness, and that catalyst is not yet in the reported numbers.

## Competitive Landscape

MPT is one of the largest owners of hospital real estate globally, and the specialization moat is intact: no single property exceeds ~2% of assets, leases run ~16.7 years with near-universal escalators, and ~50% international exposure diversifies away from U.S. reimbursement and political risk. Management frames the international book as "a cornerstone of portfolio stability," and Median's record quarter plus Swiss Medical's clinical tie-up with Mayo Clinic are reputational positives.

Competitive trajectory is improving from a low base. Re-tenanting 18 of 23 ex-Steward facilities to six operators and re-leasing the ex-Prospect California hospitals to NOR demonstrates MPT can find replacement operators for distressed assets, the core skill its model requires. The structural headwind is REIT-specific regulatory scrutiny of hospital ownership: Massachusetts and California have enacted laws restricting REIT sale-leasebacks of acute-care hospitals and new transaction-review regimes, a direct legacy of the Steward scandal that could constrain future deal flow in key states. Competitive Position scores 5.2, above neutral for the durable moat and improving coverage, but capped by the regulatory overhang and behavioral-segment softness. This dimension has historically been a weak predictor of returns; we weight it lightly.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the interaction of leverage and unproven cash collection. Debt is $9.7B, most of it non-amortizing balloon maturities. The near-term €500M unsecured note due October 2026 carries a 0.99% coupon, cheap enough that management will ride it to maturity and repay with cash plus revolver. The larger walls are the revolver plus $200M term loan (June 2027, after presumed extension) and $1.4B unsecured notes due October 2027, with 2027 contractual commitments totaling ~$2.1B against liquidity of only ~$1.0B (cash plus revolver) at February 2026. Refinancing $1.4B at CCC+/B3 rates would materially raise interest expense.

Tenant-credit risk is concentrated in the cash-basis accounts, and reported revenue overstates collected cash so long as those tenants ramp behind schedule. The Prospect bankruptcy tail leaves $61M of investment to recover in 2026 (not assured) plus up to $70M of potential additional DIP advances secured only by uncertain litigation claims. FX exposure (EUR/GBP/CHF/COP on ~50% of assets) affects revenue, equity, and covenant metrics. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025 Medicaid work-requirement reforms could pressure tenant reimbursement. Governance adds a flag: CEO comp rebounded to ~$15.9M in 2025 despite three years of GAAP losses and cumulative TSR of 37.95 versus a peer group at 163.81, though the pay-for-performance mechanism did claw "comp actually paid" negative in 2022 and to $1.2M in 2023.

Risk Factors scores 4.3, matching the prior read. Much of the downside (impairments, dividend cuts, mega-tenant defaults) is now behind the company and priced, which is why the score has climbed from 2.5, but the 2027 maturity wall and the cash-collection dependency remain specific, dateable risks that could move the stock materially in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 46.9% is rich versus 36.4% one-year historical vol (a 10.5pp spread), the term structure is in backwardation (46.9% near vs 40.0% long-dated), and ATM IV jumped from 41.0% to 58.0% over the past month even as the price rose 6.7%, indicating the market is bracing for an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew of 6.3% and a put/call volume ratio of 1.11 show elevated but not extreme put demand, with unusual activity in the August $4 put. This warrants caution on timing for anyone entering. Short interest is the defining feature: 30.9% of float short, 20.5 days to cover, 138M shares short. That is high enough to create meaningful squeeze potential on any positive cash-collection surprise, but it also reflects a well-informed bearish cohort that shares the balance-sheet concern. For a HOLD, the takeaway is symmetric: the same short interest that caps clean downside also caps the appeal of shorting.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Both problem mega-tenants are exited. The cash-rent ramps hit schedule, driving the $1B+ run-rate and lifting Normalized FFO toward the low-$0.70s. Portfolio coverage of 2.6x and improving operator EBITDARM confirm underlying asset health. Management's buyback at ~$5.20 and resumed acquisitions signal NAV well above the current price, and a $40M Q1 2026 UK REIT tax benefit helps. As collections normalize and the 2027 maturities are addressed at improving spreads, the yield compresses toward the ~6% peer median, implying a price around $6.00, with a squeeze on the 31%-short float adding upside toward the $8 high target.

**Bear case.** HSA and ex-Steward collections slip, the $1B run-rate is missed, and dividend coverage and covenant headroom tighten. Refinancing $1.4B in October 2027 at CCC+ rates raises interest expense further against liquidity of only ~$1.0B. The Prospect tail requires additional DIP funding with uncertain recovery, FX moves against the ~50% international book, or REIT-specific regulation constrains re-tenanting. In that scenario FFO stalls, the dividend is at risk again, and the stock retests the low-$4s to high-$3s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Reported cash collections from HSA and the ex-Steward group demonstrably ramp toward 80%+ of contractual rent, with the $1B+ run-rate on track in Q2/Q3 2026 disclosures.
- A concrete, favorably-priced refinancing or paydown of the October 2027 $1.4B note that visibly de-risks the maturity wall.
- Confirmed open-market insider buying (a data gap in current filings) alongside continued buyback execution below NAV.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cash-collection ramps stall or reverse (HSA or ex-Steward group flat or declining sequentially), threatening the >62% payout.
- Any indication the dividend is at renewed risk, or covenant headroom under the 95%-of-NAFFO cap tightens.
- Deterioration in the Prospect recovery beyond the disclosed $60M/$70M DIP exposure, or a ratings downgrade below CCC+.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 report (August), which should show early NOR rent and updated HSA/ex-Steward collection percentages, followed by the Q3 2026 print that will indicate whether the Q4 2026 run-rate goal is achievable.

## Conclusion

MPT is a genuine turnaround with a distressed balance sheet, and both facts must hold in the reader's mind at once. The company has exited its two catastrophic tenants, shrunk the impairment cycle dramatically (from ~$2.8B in 2024 to ~$340M in 2025), rebuilt Normalized FFO to $0.58, and re-covered a dividend at a ~62% payout yielding 7.6%. Operating metrics are improving, and management has enough conviction to buy back stock and resume acquisitions. Against that, the Z-score sits at 0.72, ratings are CCC+/B3, interest eats half of revenue, and the recovery's largest single input, cash rent collection, is running behind management's own expectations. The $1.4B October 2027 note would become expensive to resolve under a slower ramp.

We maintain HOLD (income_hold) with an overall score of 4.5, extending the improving trajectory without crossing into BUY. The 7.6% yield is the reason to own it today; the unproven cash-rent ramp and the 2027 maturity wall are the reasons not to own more. Upgrade only when the cash shows up in reported collections rather than in contractual rent that tenants are not yet paying in full. The path to a re-rating is dateable, and the burden is on management to demonstrate collections through the next two quarterly prints.
