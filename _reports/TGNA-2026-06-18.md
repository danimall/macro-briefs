**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TEGNA trades as a legal-arbitrage situation whose equity value is governed by the outcome of an antitrust fight over its acquisition by Nexstar Media Group. On August 18, 2025, TEGNA agreed to be acquired for $22.00/share in cash (~$6.2B). Shareholders approved on November 18, 2025; the FCC and DOJ cleared the transaction; and Nexstar declared the deal closed in March 2026. A federal court then issued a preliminary injunction freezing operational integration after DirecTV and a coalition of roughly 13 state attorneys general sued, arguing the combined entity reaches ~80% of U.S. TV households against a federal ownership cap of 39%. Nexstar has appealed to the Ninth Circuit and is seeking expedited review, with oral arguments targeted for approximately August 2026.

The agencies approved the deal but a court froze it, and that tension is the entire investment question. If the injunction is lifted and the deal stands, TEGNA shareholders are entitled to the $22.00 cash consideration, capping upside near that level. If the antitrust theory ultimately unwinds the transaction, TEGNA reverts to a standalone broadcaster whose fair value sits well below $22, because the recent price embeds a deal premium. The downside protection is thin: Nexstar owes a $125M reverse-termination fee under specified circumstances, a modest cushion against a $6.2B deal. The corporate layer is actively dissolving — the prior CEO, CFO, and several C-suite officers have departed, many collecting change-of-control payouts — which would complicate any reversion to standalone operation.

We rate TGNA AVOID. The expected-value math on a binary legal outcome with capped upside and meaningful downside does not favor new capital. The standalone business is a decent, free-cash-flow-generative but secularly challenged asset, not one we would own at a deal-inflated price. Investors already holding the position face a genuine merger-arb decision, but for new money the risk/reward is unattractive.

## Company Overview

TEGNA (founded 1906 as Gannett; spun into a pure-play broadcaster in 2015) is one of the largest U.S. local-TV station groups, operating 64 TV stations and 2 radio stations across 51 markets, reaching roughly 39% of U.S. TV households (38.7% without the UHF discount; 28.9% with it). It is the largest independent owner of Top-4 network affiliates (ABC/CBS/NBC/Fox) in the top-25 markets, with marquee properties including WFAA (Dallas), KHOU (Houston), WUSA (Washington DC), KING (Seattle), KXTV (Sacramento), and WXIA (Atlanta). The company produces over 1,700 hours of local news weekly, among the largest such output in the country, and won six National Murrow Awards in 2025.

TEGNA operates as a single reportable segment ("Media") with four revenue streams: Distribution (retransmission/carriage fees, ~54% of 2025 revenue), Advertising & Marketing Services (AMS, including the Premion connected-TV ad platform), Political (cyclical, concentrated in even years), and Other. The competitive advantage rests on local salesforces and trusted local-news brands, a "right to win" in hyper-local markets that is nonetheless structurally pressured by cord-cutting and the migration of ad dollars to digital platforms.

## Financial Analysis

TEGNA's revenue swings sharply on the even-year political-advertising cycle:

| Year | Revenue | Net Income | Op. Income | Notes |
|------|---------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| 2022 | $3.28B | $834M | $991M | Presidential year |
| 2023 | $2.91B | $607M | $734M | Off-year |
| 2024 | $3.10B | $773M | $785M | Presidential year |
| 2025 | $2.71B | $289M | $443M | Off-year |

2025 revenue fell 13% YoY, driven almost entirely by a 90% collapse in political revenue ($373M → $39M), which is the expected pattern in an odd year rather than a structural break. AMS declined 4% on a weak TV ad market, the absence of a Summer Olympics, and the loss of Gray Media as the exclusive Premion reseller. Distribution fell only 1% as rate increases nearly offset subscriber losses. Operating income fell 44% and Adjusted EBITDA fell 38% to $579M (a 21% margin), again a normal even/odd swing.

Cash generation remains a strength. 2025 operating cash flow was $326M and free cash flow $283M (a 10.4% FCF margin), with OCF exceeding net income, indicating clean earnings conversion. The balance sheet is manageable: $2.54B of fixed-rate debt (100% fixed), $291M cash, leverage of 2.78x against a 4.50x covenant. There are no 2026 maturities; the nearest is $200M in June 2027. TEGNA prepaid $550M of March-2026 notes ahead of the deal and has a largely undrawn revolver ($739M available). The Altman Z-score of 1.86 sits in the grey zone, typical for a leveraged, asset-light media company carrying $5.29B of goodwill and intangibles (77% of assets).

One balance-sheet line requires explicit treatment. Retained earnings fell from $8.55B (2024) to $3.17B (2025), a ~$5.4B decline far exceeding net income net of dividends. This reflects merger-related accounting and equity adjustments tied to the pending acquisition, not operating losses. A naive read of this line as catastrophic equity erosion would be wrong; we treat it as an accounting artifact. Pre-deal capital allocation was disciplined: buybacks of $653M (2023) and $275M (2024) shrank the share count from ~224M (2022) to ~162M (2025), now paused under merger restrictions, with the dividend held at $0.125/quarter, the maximum permitted under the merger agreement.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, TEGNA's growth profile is structurally constrained. Distribution revenue faces ongoing pressure from cord-cutting — pay-TV subscriptions fell roughly 5.9% in 2025 — that rate increases on renewals can only partially offset. Network affiliation renewals present a recurring repricing risk: ABC expires in 2026, NBC in early 2027, and CBS/Fox in 2028, with terms likely to worsen as networks push direct-to-consumer streaming (Peacock, Paramount+) that dilutes affiliate value. Digital ad-share loss is the other secular drag; BIA estimated digital at 54% of local ad spend in 2025, with TEGNA competing against Google, Meta, TikTok, and YouTube.

The near-term offset is the 2026 midterm cycle. Election ad spend is projected to reach a record, outpacing presidential years, on competitive California and Texas races. This is a genuine tailwind to standalone earnings power and one reason the frozen integration is costly to Nexstar, which cannot capture combined political inventory pricing. The Premion CTV platform, reaching all 210 markets, is the company's clearest digital growth asset, though hampered by Gray's exit as exclusive reseller.

The growth question is largely moot given the deal context. If the merger stands, growth accrues to Nexstar. If it fails, TEGNA's standalone trajectory is a low-single-digit, cyclically lumpy business in secular decline, supported in the near term by political revenue but without a structural reacceleration path.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation is the cash consideration, modified by deal-completion probability. With shares effectively pinned to the $22.00 cash price, the relevant exercise is a probability-weighted estimate, not a multiple analysis.

If the deal closes, shareholders receive the cash consideration, which is the ceiling. If the deal collapses, fair value reverts to standalone broadcaster levels below $22, because the current price embeds a deal premium. A standalone valuation on ~$579M Adjusted EBITDA at the 6-8x range typical for secularly challenged broadcasters, net of ~$2.25B net debt across ~162M shares, implies an equity value materially below the cash price, in the rough vicinity of the low-to-mid teens, with 2026 political revenue providing near-term support but not closing the gap. The $125M reverse-termination fee adds only about $0.77/share of protection.

The asymmetry is unfavorable for new capital. Upside is capped near $22 (less any remaining spread reflecting deal risk), while downside on a deal break is a step-change to standalone value. A cheap-looking standalone multiple here is not a buy signal; it would be the residual value of a business stripped of its deal premium with its corporate management dispersed. ## Competitive Landscape

The industry consolidation thesis is the strategic backdrop. Local broadcasters — Nexstar, TEGNA, Sinclair, Gray — argue that scale is existential to negotiate retransmission fees and compete with streaming and Big Tech for ad dollars. The Nexstar-TEGNA combination is the test case for whether courts will permit broadcasters past the 39% national cap via the UHF discount, after the Eighth Circuit's October 2025 *Zimmer Radio v. FCC* ruling eliminated the Top-Four Restriction and cleared the agency path.

TEGNA's standalone competitive position is stable-to-deteriorating. Its local-news brands and Top-4 affiliate concentration in large markets are real assets, but the moat is compressing on two fronts: networks increasingly bypass affiliates with direct streaming, and digital platforms continue to take local ad share. Premion is a differentiated CTV asset but lost scale when Gray exited as exclusive reseller. The competitive trajectory, independent of the deal, points to gradual erosion rather than share gains.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is binary and legal. The 10-K leads its risk factors with "Risks Related to the Merger," and these now subsume the standalone considerations.

1. **Deal-completion/antitrust risk (the central thesis).** Nexstar relies on the UHF discount for compliance with the 39% cap. If the deal unwinds, TGNA reverts to standalone with no cash consideration for shares.

2. **Unusual "closed but frozen" status.** Nexstar owns TEGNA as a subsidiary yet is barred from integrating, incurring lost synergies (Nexstar cites "tens of millions") while bearing acquisition debt. This pressures Nexstar more than TEGNA holders in the near term but keeps unwind risk live.

3. **Secular cord-cutting** pressuring 54% of revenue, and **affiliation-renewal risk** (ABC 2026, NBC early 2027, CBS/Fox 2028).

4. **Goodwill/FCC-license impairment risk.** Intangibles are 77% of assets; 2025 impairment testing relied partly on the merger-implied fair value. A deal failure raises impairment risk.

5. **Management attrition.** Prior CEO Mike Steib exited at the March 2026 closing, CFO Julie Heskett and other senior officers departed, and Patrick Paolini was named CEO in late May 2026 amid legal limbo. Many left to collect change-of-control payouts, which complicates any reversion to standalone operation.

6. **Thin downside protection.** The $125M reverse-termination fee is small relative to the $6.2B deal.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Ninth Circuit lifts the injunction, the antitrust theory fails against an already-approved FCC/DOJ transaction, and the deal stands. Shareholders receive $22.00 in cash, capturing whatever spread remains between the current price and the consideration. The agency path is cleared and the *Zimmer Radio* ruling removed a key obstacle; courts may defer to the regulatory determination on the UHF discount. Even if the deal experiences delay, the cash consideration anchors value. Standalone, 2026 record political ad spending supports near-term earnings if the deal slips. Bull target: ~$22.

### Bear Case
The court holds the injunction, the 80%-reach-vs-39%-cap theory prevails, and the deal unwinds. TEGNA reverts to a standalone broadcaster with a dispersed management team, secular revenue pressure, and a price that previously embedded a deal premium. Equity reverts toward standalone fair value in the low-to-mid teens, partially supported by 2026 political revenue. The $125M reverse-termination fee provides minimal cushion. Bear target: low-to-mid teens, roughly a 25-40% decline from a price near the cash consideration.

The expected value of a binary outcome with capped upside near $22 and a step-down to standalone value on a break does not favor new capital, particularly with the remaining spread thin and the carrying risk concentrated in a court timeline outside investors' control.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The specific, dated event is the Ninth Circuit's resolution of Nexstar's appeal of the preliminary injunction, with oral arguments targeted for approximately August 2026 and a ruling thereafter. An adverse ruling that sustains the injunction (or a subsequent decision that unwinds the deal) would drive TGNA toward standalone fair value, a meaningful decline from a price near the $22.00 consideration.

**Timing window**: The catalyst is concentrated around the ~August 2026 oral arguments and the subsequent decision. This is a reasonably proximate window, though appellate timelines can slip and an expedited trial adds uncertainty.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest, days-to-cover, and institutional ownership data were not retrievable. In merger-arb situations the float is typically dominated by arbitrage desks long the spread, which raises the risk of a sharp move higher on any positive deal-completion headline. The absence of data is itself a reason for caution.

**Borrow and options signal**: No options data was retrievable, so there is no confirmation of bearish positioning from skew or IV. Borrow on a near-cash-pinned arb name is typically expensive and the spread to $22 is narrow, meaning the carry-to-reward on a short of the residual spread is poor.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: A deal break takes the stock to the low-to-mid teens (roughly 25-40% downside). The upside risk to a short is severe and asymmetric: any positive Ninth Circuit signal snaps the price to the full $22.00 consideration, and the FCC/DOJ have already approved, raising completion odds. With capped reward (the spread plus the deal-break delta) against a binary squeeze to the cash price, the risk/reward does not clear a 2:1 short threshold.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** This is a binary legal outcome where the agencies have already approved and the remaining obstacle is a single court process. The setup carries severe asymmetric squeeze risk on any favorable headline, the spread is narrow, borrow is likely costly, and there is no options confirmation of a bearish thesis. The correct posture is to avoid, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: We would revisit TGNA only on one of two developments. First, if the Ninth Circuit clearly and credibly lifts the injunction and the path to closing the $22.00 cash deal becomes near-certain, a small residual spread could be worth capturing for arb-focused investors, though that is a spread trade rather than a fundamental investment. Second, if the deal unequivocally collapses and the stock resets to standalone value, we would reassess at a price reflecting ~6-8x standalone EBITDA, factoring in the 2026 political tailwind, secular distribution pressure, the affiliation-renewal schedule, and the cost of reconstituting a dispersed management team.

**Timeline**: The legal uncertainty should persist through the Ninth Circuit process, with the key resolution point around the ~August 2026 oral arguments and the subsequent ruling. We expect the binary to resolve within the next 6-12 months.

**What would change the view to BUY**: A definitive, low-risk path to the $22.00 cash consideration with a residual spread wide enough to compensate for remaining timing risk, or a standalone reset to a price meaningfully below standalone fair value with a clear operating plan under stable management.

## Conclusion

TEGNA's equity is a wager on a court outcome rather than a business. The merger that closed in March 2026 is operationally frozen by a preliminary injunction, and the antitrust theory is being litigated on appeal even after the FCC and DOJ approved the transaction. If the deal stands, upside is capped at the cash price. If it fails, the stock reverts to standalone broadcaster value materially below that, with only a $125M reverse-termination fee for protection and a corporate management team that has largely departed. The standalone business generates respectable free cash flow ($283M in 2025, a 10.4% margin) and carries manageable leverage (2.78x), but it is secularly challenged by cord-cutting, digital ad-share loss, and worsening affiliation renewals. For new capital, the binary outcome with capped upside and a meaningful downside does not justify ownership. We rate TGNA **AVOID**. Investors already in the position face a genuine arb decision around the Ninth Circuit timeline; new buyers should wait for the legal binary to resolve.
