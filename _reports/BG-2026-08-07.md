## Executive Summary

Bunge Global is eighteen months into the digestion of a transformational acquisition and one quarter into a visible cyclical recovery. The Viterra combination (closed 2 July 2025, $10,617M total consideration including ~65.6M shares) roughly doubled the revenue base and made Bunge one of the two or three largest oilseed processors and grain merchandisers globally. FY2025 adjusted EPS of $7.57 marked the trough of the post-2022 down-cycle; management now guides FY2026 to $9.25-$9.75, raised on 29 July from $9.00-$9.50. Consensus sits at $9.59 for the current year and $11.26 for next, with next-year estimates revised up 6.1% over 90 days. At $109.00 the stock trades at 11.5x guidance midpoint, 9.7x next-year consensus, and 1.31x book value of $83.05 per share — against peer medians in the mid-30s on depressed trailing earnings and a published analyst range of $116-$150 whose *low* end sits 6.4% above spot.

The reason this is ACCUMULATE and not BUY sits in the Q2 10-Q's own language. Management repeatedly attributes the strong results to "strong global demand due to the conflict with Iran," which lifted energy and vegetable-oil prices, alongside North American biofuel mandates and Black Sea sunflower tightness. Two of those three drivers are sector-wide and exogenous — ADM raised its 2026 forecast on the same conditions. FY2026 guidance implicitly requires H2 adjusted EPS of $5.42-$5.92, a 59-61% second-half weighting that, per the 10-K's own statement that Bunge "typically do[es] not experience material fluctuations in volume between the first and second half of the year," must come from margins and mix rather than harvest volume. The guidance is a bet that current crush spreads hold. Separately, the 10-K states that "certain of our competitors have added oilseed processing and refining capacity in response to growing demand" and describes how such additions "may then produce excess supply conditions."

The stock's 10% decline across 29-30 July on an essentially in-line adjusted print with raised guidance is, on unchanged-to-better fundamentals, an improvement in risk/reward — and a director bought 6,500 shares for ~$689K on 31 July and 4 August into that weakness. But three genuinely new disclosures in the Q2 filing cut the other way: Level 3 fair-value assets nearly doubled to $3,055M with $910M of unrealized gains still held on the balance sheet; Grain Merchandising & Milling was the one segment whose outlook management *lowered*; and purchase accounting finalized with $2,880M of Viterra goodwill against only $24M of identified intangibles, $828M of it sitting in that weakest segment. Net: positive expected return of roughly 12-14% over twelve months against a bear case near $84, which is a staged-entry profile, not a full-position one.

## Company Overview

Bunge is a Swiss-domiciled (Geneva registered office, operational headquarters in Chesterfield, Missouri), NYSE-listed agribusiness. The business model is spread capture across an owned physical network — crush plants, refineries, elevators, port terminals, barges and railcars — not branded consumer pricing. The 10-K is explicit that markets are "highly price competitive" and "sensitive to product substitution."

Four reportable segments, with H1 2026 adjusted EBIT and 30 June segment assets:

| Segment | H1 2026 Adj. EBIT | Segment assets | % of assets | % of segment EBIT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Soybean Processing & Refining | $822M | $19,744M | 44% | 56% |
| Softseed Processing & Refining | $450M | $7,595M | 17% | 31% |
| Tropical Oils & Specialty Ingredients | $74M | $4,164M | 9% | 5% |
| Grain Merchandising & Milling | $111M | $12,953M | 29% | 7.6% |
| Corporate & Other | $(231)M | $2,326M | — | — |

The asset-to-earnings mismatch in Grain Merchandising is the single most important structural fact about post-Viterra Bunge: 29% of reportable segment assets producing 7.6% of adjusted segment EBIT. Volume growth confirms the acquisition transformed throughput — Q2 Grain Merchandising volumes rose 185% year-over-year to 23,852 thousand metric tons, and softseeds merchandised went from 15kt to 1,296kt — but the earnings have not yet followed the assets.

Adjacent positions worth naming precisely: **Bunge Chevron Ag Renewables** is a consolidated variable interest entity in which Bunge is primary beneficiary and Chevron the joint-venture partner ($1,108M total assets, PP&E up from $714M to $900M in six months); Bunge markets the meal and oil it produces. **Ukraine** comprises four crush plants, two Mykolaiv export terminals and elevators, with Ukrainian subsidiaries each under 2% of consolidated assets and liabilities at year-end 2025.

## Financial Analysis

### The cycle, on a matched basis

Adjusted EPS, sourced from the proxy's pay-versus-performance table where it is the company-selected measure: $12.93 (2021), $13.91 (2022), $13.66 (2023), $9.19 (2024), $7.57 (2025), guided $9.25-$9.75 (2026). GAAP net income followed the same shape: $2,078M → $1,610M → $2,243M → $1,137M → $816M.

The per-share series understates the dollar recovery because the share count changed. FY2025 GAAP net income of $816M against $4.91 diluted EPS implies ~166.2M weighted diluted shares; Q2 2026 weighted diluted shares were 195.3M. So FY2025 adjusted net income was approximately $7.57 × 166.2M ≈ $1,258M, and FY2026E at the $9.50 midpoint is approximately $9.50 × ~195.5M ≈ $1,857M — **+48% in dollars versus +25% per share.** Roughly half the operating recovery is absorbed by the ~18% higher share count. These dollar figures are back-solved from per-share data, not company-disclosed.

### Q2 and H1 2026

| | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $24,041M | $12,769M | $45,902M | $24,412M |
| Gross profit | $1,681M | $738M | $2,447M | $1,335M |
| Gross margin | 6.99% | 5.78% | 5.33% | 5.47% |
| SG&A % of sales | 2.52% | 3.27% | 2.48% | 3.27% |
| Adjusted Total EBIT | $665M | $293M | $1,226M | $655M |
| Adj. EBIT margin | 2.77% | 2.29% | **2.67%** | **2.68%** |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | $3.47 | $2.61 | $3.81 | $4.10 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $2.00 | $1.31 | $3.83 | $3.12 |

Two facts a headline scan misses. First, **H1 adjusted EBIT margin on revenue was flat** (2.67% vs 2.68%) — the entire half-year earnings improvement came from volume and scale, not a wider spread per dollar of revenue. Q2's margin did widen (2.77% vs 2.29%), so the exit rate is better than the average, but the flat half-year figure should temper any thesis that Bunge has structurally repriced its spread. Second, **GAAP diluted EPS fell year-over-year for the half** ($3.81 vs $4.10) while adjusted rose 23% — an artifact of H1 2025's $155M pretax corn-milling divestiture gain (~$0.87/share on ~142M shares) against H1 2026's ~195M shares and $70M of Viterra integration costs.

Mark-to-market timing differences make GAAP quarterly EPS a weak signal here. Q2 removed **$437M** of MTM timing from Total EBIT ($1,060M GAAP → $665M adjusted) and $1.67/share from EPS. Adjusted came in *below* GAAP, the opposite of the usual non-GAAP-flattery pattern. Any beat/miss framing must be matched: adjusted $2.00 against the $2.01 consensus in the structured feed is a 0.3% miss — effectively in line. A third-party report characterized the same $2.00 as 2.9% above a differently-constructed non-GAAP consensus. Either reading makes it a non-event, which is why the print's own numbers do not explain a 10% two-day decline.

### Cash flow — the most commonly misread line

Reported H1 2026 operating cash flow was **negative $1,126M** (H1 2025: negative $1,357M) against $779M of capex. Annualizing this would be a serious error: the drivers are a $2,235M inventory build, $257M of secured supplier advances, $201M reduction in advances on sales and $155M of margin deposits — seasonal working-capital absorption against South American crop flows at higher commodity prices. The company's non-GAAP adjusted funds from operations for H1 was $1,291M (vs $693M prior year).

The full-year figure that does matter: **FY2025 operating cash flow of $844M against $1,723M of capex implies free cash flow of negative $879M**, while the company paid ~$502M of dividends and repurchased ~$551M of stock. Capital returns in FY2025 were funded from the balance sheet and Viterra-related financing, not from operating free cash flow. That is defensible in a trough year straddling a $10.6B acquisition close, but it is the reason financial health does not score above 5.5. Capex guidance of $1.5-$1.7B against ~$975M of D&A means Bunge continues investing at 1.5-1.7x depreciation (Argentina crush $370M and Tropical Oils $236M were the largest H1 lines).

### Balance sheet

Total debt rose from roughly $6.2B at end-2024 to **$15,214M** at 30 June 2026. Cash is $593M, down from $1,135M at year-end — and comparison to the $6,790M held at 30 June 2025 overstates deterioration, since that was a pre-closing acquisition war chest.

Leverage depends entirely on inventory convention, and both numbers are legitimate. Net debt of $14,621M against implied FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of ~$4,058M (≈$3,083M EBIT + $975M D&A) is **~3.6x**. Netting the $13,311M of readily marketable inventories — liquid, exchange-hedged commodity stocks — gives ~$1,310M and **~0.3x**. Three rating agencies use something closer to the second: **S&P A-/A-2, Moody's Baa1/P-2, Fitch BBB+/F-2, all stable.** Liquidity is ample: $9,665M of committed revolvers with $8,835M unused, a $3.0B CP program with $564M drawn, a single disclosed covenant (maximum debt-to-capitalization, in compliance), and no rating-downgrade acceleration triggers.

Interest cost is real regardless of convention. Cash interest paid rose to $365M in H1 from $203M; guided FY net interest is $620-$660M; a 100bp move in reference rates equals roughly $102M annually on variable-rate debt. Implied FY2026E adjusted EBIT of ~$3,083M against $640M of net interest is ~4.8x coverage (H1 actual ~4.2x): adequate, not comfortable, for a commodity processor.

One book-value nuance: accumulated other comprehensive loss is $(6,014)M, of which $(5,553)M is cumulative foreign-currency translation, largely Brazilian real and other EM currencies. Reported book value is materially suppressed by non-cash translation losses that would partly reverse on EM currency strength.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors, with different durability.

**Cycle recovery (high confidence, low durability).** FY2026 guidance implies +25% adjusted EPS and +48% adjusted net income off the FY2025 trough. The estimate revision pattern supports it: current-year consensus $9.59 (+1.6% in 30 days, +1.7% in 90), next-year $11.26 (+2.8% and +6.1%). Next-quarter estimates rose 6.2% in 30 days while current-quarter fell 3.8% — a shift in timing rather than level. The four-quarter surprise record is 3 beats and 1 marginal miss.

The H2 hurdle is the thing to underwrite. H1 delivered $3.83, so guidance implies H2 of $5.42-$5.92, or 59-61% of the year. Tested against history, the *shape* is normal — H1 2025's $3.12 against FY2025's $7.57 implies H2 2025 at 59% — so the skew is not a red flag. What it does require is **H2 2026 growth of +21.8% to +33.0% on a roughly flat share count**, driven by margin and mix rather than volume, since the 10-K states hemispheric diversification prevents material half-to-half volume swings. Consensus of $9.59 sits above the guidance midpoint and near the top of the range, leaving no cushion for a spread give-back. (Half-year adjusted EPS does not sum exactly to the annual figure because each period carries its own weighted share count; these are approximations.)

**Viterra scale (medium confidence, higher durability).** Throughput diversification is verified: Grain Merchandising volumes +185%, softseeds merchandised from 15kt to 1,296kt. The strategic case — origination across North America, South America, Australia, Canada and the Black Sea as a hedge against single-origin trade disruption — is the strongest argument for the deal in a world where the 10-K devotes substantial space to US-China disputes causing "significant volatility in commodity prices, disruptions in historical trade flows." What is *not* verified is any quantified synergy target or achievement. Secondary coverage characterized synergies as "running ahead of plan," but no company-stated dollar figure appears in any filing or issuer material reviewed. The H1 adjusted EBIT improvement ($1,226M vs $655M) is real; its split between synergy and cycle is unestablished.

**Per-share conversion (management-controlled).** The prior $2.7B authorization was completed in Q2 at a derived average of $95.13 across 28,383,187 shares — good execution. A new **$3.0B authorization approved 9 March 2026 with indefinite term was entirely unused at 30 June**, equal to ~27.7M shares or **~14.4% of outstanding** at $109.00. This is the mechanism that converts the +48% dollar recovery into per-share terms. It is constrained, though, by Swiss mechanics discussed below.

Portfolio actions are additive at the margin: the IFF/Solae lecithin and soy-protein business closed 1 March 2026 for $105M cash; European margarines and spreads are held for sale to Vandemoortele for ~$239M (expected to close in 2026, $202M assets / $62M liabilities held for sale); the ViOil Ukraine crush facility was added in Q4 2025.

Against the peer set — DAR at 41.4x, ADM at 35.3x, INGR at 9.7x, median 35.3x — BG screens cheap, but the comparison is nearly useless because peer trailing multiples reflect their own trough earnings. ADM at 35.3x trailing is not a valuation signal; it is a depressed-denominator signal. INGR at 9.7x is the more honest comparator, and BG at 11.4x trailing-adjusted-equivalent carries a modest premium to it for the Viterra scale step-up.

The more useful frame is cycle-adjusted earnings power. Legacy Bunge earned $1.6-$2.2B of GAAP net income at the 2021-2023 peak. The combined company at the FY2026 guidance midpoint earns ~$1,857M of adjusted net income on ~195M shares. On that arithmetic, 2026 is not a peak-earnings year for the enlarged entity — it is closer to the middle. If combined mid-cycle adjusted EPS is $10.50-$11.00, then 11.5x on 2026 is a fair-to-modestly-cheap entry. If the correct mid-cycle number is $8.50-$9.00 because the Iran-linked vegoil premium and record biofuel mandates are temporary, then 11.5x on 2026 is 12.8x on normalized earnings and the stock is fairly valued at best.

That ambiguity is why the valuation score is 6.5 rather than 7.5. Cheapness in cyclical processors is the classic value trap: the multiple does not expand, and the denominator moves against you. The specific arguments that the market is mispricing BG today are (a) the share-count masking of a 48% dollar earnings recovery, (b) a completely unused authorization equal to 14.4% of shares, (c) a 10% drawdown on an in-line print with raised guidance, and (d) an analyst range of $116-$150 (mean $141.22) whose lowest published target is above spot — which, given forward P/E below 12x and rising estimates, functions more as a floor than a ceiling. The specific counterargument is that the earnings base rests on a geopolitical energy premium the company itself names in its filings.

The mismatch between an $18-19 spread from spot to consensus mean and my own $126 base case is deliberate: the sell-side range appears to extrapolate current spreads into 2027 with less haircut than I am willing to apply.

## Competitive Landscape

Bunge's advantages are network position, origination scale and risk-management capability, not pricing power. The current cycle is being driven by three forces, only one of which is company-specific:

1. **Energy-linked vegetable-oil demand** — cited in the 10-Q as the Iran conflict lifting energy and vegoil prices, with soybean oil reaching multi-year highs. Sector-wide.
2. **Biofuel policy** — North American mandates at record blending volumes. Sector-wide; ADM's parallel guidance raise is direct evidence.
3. **Black Sea and European sunflower tightness** — where Viterra's origination assets give Bunge disproportionate participation. Closest to company-specific.

The bear case on structure is written into Bunge's own filings. Competitors have already added oilseed processing and refining capacity; Bunge now faces "additional competition for refining capacity from traditional petroleum companies, particularly in the United States"; and the supply/demand risk factor describes how high demand stimulates capacity additions whose "timing and extent... may then produce excess supply conditions." Bunge is itself expanding Argentine crush, the largest driver of Q2 soybean volume growth — rational individually, margin-eroding collectively. A slower structural headwind: increased on-farm crop storage in several regions lets producers hold grain longer and "increase price pressure on purchasers such as us," eroding the merchandiser's timing advantage.

On diversification, precision matters. Viterra clearly broadened Bunge's *throughput* mix geographically and by product. It does not establish customer diversification — no customer-concentration data appears in the materials reviewed, so no claim in either direction is supportable.

Trajectory assessment: competitive position improved in breadth and worsened in earnings efficiency. The prior series marked this dimension down from 7.5 to 6.0; the flat H1 adjusted EBIT margin and the Grain Merchandising asset/earnings gap justify holding near 5.8 rather than recovering.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The earnings driver is exogenous and can reverse.** Guidance embeds continuation of current crush and vegoil spreads. The 10-K warns that "lower prices for oil, gasoline or diesel fuel could result in decreased selling prices for ethanol, renewable diesel, biodiesel and their raw materials." This is the dominant risk and it is only partly discounted at 11.5x.

**2. Grain Merchandising goodwill.** Viterra goodwill finalized at $2,880M (Softseed $1,156M, Soybean $896M, Grain Merchandising **$828M**) against just $24M of identified other intangibles — a one-year-life trademark. None is tax-deductible; measurement-period adjustments *increased* goodwill by $574M. The segment holding $828M of it delivered $111M of H1 adjusted EBIT on $12,953M of assets and was the one segment whose outlook management lowered on 29 July. I have an unverified recollection that the FY2025 10-K critical-accounting-estimates discussion put this reporting unit's fair-value cushion in the single digits; **that figure is not established and should not be relied upon.** What is established is the combination of thin earnings, a lowered outlook and freshly recognized goodwill — which is where an impairment would surface at the Q4 test. An impairment would be non-cash, hitting goodwill, equity, book value and GAAP EPS, but not adjusted EPS, cash flow, or covenant compliance except through the capitalization denominator.

**3. Level 3 exposure roughly doubled.** Level 3 assets rose to $3,055M from $1,634M in six months, Level 3 readily marketable inventories to $2,851M from $1,407M. H1 recognized $925M of Level 3 gains in cost of goods sold, **of which $910M is unrealized on positions still held**. Disclosed unobservable inputs now include "lack of market corroborated information in Canada." Highest daily aggregated commodity position value rose to $2,645M in H1 from $1,307M for all of FY2025, with a disclosed 10% adverse move worth ~$265M. The mechanical explanation is acquiring Viterra's Canadian and Black Sea book; no inference about intent is warranted. The consequence stands: a larger share of reported gross profit rests on internal models in less-observable markets.

**4. Off-balance-sheet structures with a near-term renewal date.** Trade structured finance time deposits and letters of credit total $13,134M presented net, with $7,098M of H1 LC discounting proceeds in *operating* cash flow. Receivables sold and derecognized rose to $1,650M from $1,174M under a program upsized to $2.0B on 31 March 2026 — but **committed purchasers' commitments terminate 16 December 2026**, subject to 364-day extension, against a 2031 program end. A renewal failure would require ~$1.65B of on-balance-sheet funding. The program has been repeatedly renewed and most recently increased, which lowers but does not eliminate the risk. Divestiture indemnities carry $1.6B of maximum potential payments against $125M recognized, some without stated expiration, including on BP Bunge Bioenergia and the 2023 Russian operations sale.

**5. Brazilian tax contingencies.** Unprovisioned because loss is not deemed probable: ICMS claims of $163M and PIS/COFINS claims of $598M — $761M combined, with PIS/COFINS growing $108M in six months. Provisioned claims total $530M. Prepaid commodity contracts and advances to Brazilian farmers rose 75% to $835M, secured by crops and land mortgages that "may not be sufficient to fully protect us from loss."

**6. Governance and capital-return mechanics.** Glencore ~17%, CPP Investments ~14% and BCI ~3% held approximately 34% in aggregate at year-end 2025. Because Swiss law requires two-thirds of represented voting rights for statutory mergers and for issuances withdrawing preemptive rights, an aligned 34% bloc can arithmetically block such resolutions; Glencore and CPP each designate two directors at ≥10% ownership. Minority holders should not price takeover optionality. No lock-up or transfer restriction is disclosed, so a share-sale overhang can be neither confirmed nor excluded. Separately, Swiss withholding rules subject dividends not paid from qualifying capital-contribution reserves or par-value reduction to **35% withholding**, and treat buybacks for capital reduction as partial liquidation with the same exposure; Bunge describes a Swiss-institutional intermediation process to manage this and warns that failure to execute it could expose shareholders to the tax. This sits directly underneath the $3.0B authorization.

**7. EUDR.** The EU Deforestation Regulation becomes effective December 2026, requiring traceability for palm, soy and derived products into the EU. Bunge is a major palm and soy trader into Europe — a dated, near-term commercial and operational risk.

Internal controls were concluded effective at 30 June with no material ICFR changes in the quarter, though controls "have changed" with Viterra integration and further changes are anticipated. No Item 4.02 non-reliance filing, restatement or auditor change appears in the 8-K index. Two 8-Ks were identified from the index but not read: 1 April 2026 (Item 5.02, director/officer) and 2 April 2026 (Items 1.01/2.03, material agreement and direct financial obligation) — both open verification items.

### Options Market Signal

Options are not corroborating a stress case. Near-term ATM IV of 28.2% sits **below** trailing one-year realized volatility of 31.3%, at the 8th percentile of the current chain, having collapsed from 36.4% on 26 July — a standard post-earnings vol crush with no residual event premium. Skew is call-heavy at -9.1%, put/call volume is 0.44 and put/call open interest 0.72 (down from 0.91). The nearest expiry prices only a ±5.0% move. Term structure is in contango, with six-month IV at 35.2% and a put/call OI ratio of 1.30 at that tenor — the one place where protection is being accumulated, consistent with the spread-durability question sitting on a two-to-three-quarter clock rather than an immediate one. Short interest is unremarkable at 4.8% of float and 3.7 days to cover. Cheap near-term IV against elevated realized vol is a mildly favorable entry condition for staged purchases; the longer-dated put demand is the more informative signal and aligns with my own bear case timing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $150

Crush spreads hold through H2 as biofuel mandates ratchet and Black Sea sunflower tightness persists; H2 adjusted EPS lands at or above the $5.92 top of the implied range, and FY2027 approaches consensus $11.26 or better as Viterra synergies convert. Management deploys the $3.0B authorization aggressively at depressed prices, retiring 8-12% of shares and lifting FY2027 per-share earnings toward $12.00. Multiple re-rates modestly toward 12.5-13x as investors credit a structurally larger, geographically hedged origination network with a genuine trade-disruption hedge. **13x on $11.50 ≈ $150**, matching the top published target. EM currency strength partially reverses the $5.6B translation drag, adding book-value support.

### Bear Case — $84

The vegoil premium the 10-Q names as the demand driver unwinds. Simultaneously, competitor crush capacity — already flagged in Bunge's own risk factors, with ADM and others ramping — lands into softer demand and compresses spreads. H2 2026 misses the implied $5.42 floor; consensus at the top of the guided range provides no cushion. FY2027 adjusted EPS reverts toward $7.50-$8.00. The Q4 goodwill test produces an impairment in Grain Merchandising, denting book value and GAAP earnings and reviving deal-integration skepticism. Working capital reverses less favorably than modeled and FY2026 free cash flow again fails to cover dividends plus buybacks, curbing the authorization. **10.5x on $8.00 ≈ $84**, ~23% below spot and above the 52-week low of $76.01, where the balance sheet's investment-grade ratings and 1.0x book value provide support.

### What I judge most likely — $126

The biofuel-mandate leg of demand is policy-driven and durable through 2027; the energy-price leg is not. Assume partial spread give-back, FY2027 adjusted EPS of $10.50-$11.00 against consensus $11.26, and a multiple that stays near 11.5-12x because the market will not pay up for commodity-spread earnings. **11.7x on $10.75 ≈ $126**, +15.6% from $109.00, plus a 2.64% dividend. Probability-weighting base 50% / bull 25% / bear 25% gives roughly +11.5% price return and ~14% total return over twelve months. That is a positive expected value with a bear leg nearly one-and-a-half times the base upside — enough for staged accumulation, not enough for a full position.
