## Executive Summary

Lindsay Corporation has spent the last three quarters unwinding the mix that made fiscal 2025 look like a recovery year. FY2025 revenue rose 11% to $676.4M and diluted EPS rose 12.8% to $6.78, but the growth came almost entirely from one Middle East/North Africa irrigation project worth more than $100 million while North American irrigation fell 9%. That project completed in Q1 FY2026. Nine months into fiscal 2026, revenue is down 9% to $474.3M, gross margin has compressed 220bp to 29.6%, operating income is down 33% to $51.1M, and diluted EPS is $4.22 versus $5.79. Trailing-twelve-month EPS derives to approximately $5.21 against FY2025's $6.78.

The multiple has expanded as earnings contracted. At $114.29 the stock trades at roughly 21.9x trailing EPS, 21.7x the $5.26 current-fiscal-year consensus, and 18.9x the $6.05 FY2027 consensus, against an enterprise value of about $1.122B and 17.9x TTM operating income. Sixteen months ago the same price would have been 16.9x. An investor buying today is underwriting margin reversion in both segments, a third consecutive nine-figure MENA award, and a turn in the North American row-crop capital cycle. None of those is in evidence, and consensus is moving the other way: the current-quarter estimate has been cut 10.0% and the next-quarter estimate 16.9% over the past 30 days, with FY2027 cut 4.6%.

What supports the stock is real but insufficient at this price. The balance sheet carries $154.8M of cash against $115.0M of unsecured notes at a fixed 3.82% maturing February 2030, a $50M undrawn revolver extended to August 2030, and no near-term refinancing exposure. Management retired 667,000 shares (5.9% of the count) for $80.7M in nine months and has $100M of authorization remaining. Backlog inflected up 16% year over year to $136.1M. Road Zipper remains a near-monopoly product line at 41%-plus segment gross margin. Set against that: three-quarters of negative free cash flow (nine-month operating cash flow of $30.6M against $35.5M of capex), 69% of cash held offshore, an unquantified X-Lite product-liability and qui tam docket carrying no accrual, an IIJA program scheduled to expire in September 2026, and a pay-versus-performance disclosure showing CEO compensation at a five-year high in the same year ROIC hit a five-year low. Base case of roughly $120 against a bear case near $88 produces an expected return close to zero with 3:1 downside skew. This is the fifth consecutive review, and the fundamentals have deteriorated further since the last one; the rating moves to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Lindsay is a small-cap industrial with two businesses and an unusually global manufacturing footprint for its size: 1,275 employees at August 31, 2025, with plants in the U.S., Brazil, France, Türkiye, China, Italy and South Africa.

**Irrigation (84% of FY2025 revenue, $568.0M)** sells Zimmatic center-pivot and lateral-move systems, Perrot hose-reel travelers, replacement parts and large-diameter steel tubing through more than 200 independent U.S. dealers. A standard U.S. pivot spans roughly seven sections and irrigates 125–130 acres. The 10-K states that in a dry-land conversion only about half the total investment is the pivot itself; wells, pumps, underground pipe, electrical supply and the concrete pad make up the balance. The purchase is therefore a lumpy capital project whose timing depends on farm cash flow and credit availability, not an equipment swap. Layered on top is a subscription stack (FieldNET, exclusive to Zimmatic dealers; FieldWise; and the patented FieldNET Advisor scheduling engine) with weather and soil-moisture data ingested through a partnership with Pessl Instruments. The 10-K describes four manufacturers controlling a substantial majority of the U.S. pivot market and warns explicitly that price competition may prevent recovery of input-cost increases.

**Infrastructure (16% of FY2025 revenue, $108.4M)** is anchored by the Quickchange Moveable Barrier / Road Zipper System, in which interlocked barrier elements are repositioned laterally by a Barrier Transfer Machine, allowing lane counts to flex. Management states it has limited competition in this line. The segment also sells TAU/ABSORB crash cushions (competitive, and subject to the MASH-standards testing treadmill), preformed road-marking tapes, specialty barriers and railroad products, and operates an ISO 17025 crash-test lab. Road Zipper is both sold and leased: lease revenue was $3.876M of the segment's $27.731M in Q3 FY2026 (14%) and $12.057M of the nine-month $66.590M (18%), which is the only genuinely recurring revenue inside an otherwise project-driven segment.

## Financial Analysis

### The reported step-down

| Gross margin | 29.8% | 31.6% | (180bp) | 29.6% | 31.8% | (220bp) |
| Operating income ($M) | 18.5 | 23.8 | (22%) | 51.1 | 76.8 | (33%) |
| Operating margin | 11.5% | 14.0% | (250bp) | 10.8% | 14.7% | (390bp) |
| Diluted EPS | $1.53 | $1.78 | (14%) | $4.22 | $5.79 | (27%) |

Both segments compressed. Irrigation nine-month operating margin fell to 15.4% from 18.1% on "higher input costs and fixed cost deleverage on lower unit sales volumes." Infrastructure fell to 16.6% from 27.2% on a 21% revenue decline, because the prior year contained a $20 million Road Zipper project that did not repeat. One project's absence accounts for a 1,060bp segment margin swing, which is the cleanest available measure of how thin the recurring base is beneath the segment's headline profitability.

Geographically, nine-month North America irrigation fell 8% to $206.7M on "persistent weakness in commodity markets and tempered farmer sentiment," and international fell 7% as MENA project #1 wound down and Brazil volumes dropped on "limited available credit and elevated interest rates." Foreign exchange contributed a $9.5M tailwind to nine-month international revenue, so underlying constant-currency performance was worse than reported. The 27% EPS decline is smaller than the 30% net-income decline, so buybacks are doing measurable work cushioning per-share results.

### Trailing metrics and cash conversion

Deriving Q4 FY2025 by subtracting the nine-month FY2025 10-Q from the FY2025 10-K gives Q4 FY2025 revenue of $153.6M, operating income of $11.3M (a 7.4% margin; Q4 is structurally the weakest earnings quarter) and operating cash flow of $64.0M (the strongest cash quarter, as spring receivables convert). Adding that to nine-month FY2026 produces TTM revenue of approximately $627.9M (−7.2% versus FY2025), TTM operating income of about $62.5M for a 9.9% margin (versus 13.0%), and TTM diluted EPS of roughly $5.21. That EPS figure is a sum of quarterly prints across a shrinking share count and is approximate, not reported.

Nine-month operating cash flow of $30.6M against $35.5M of capex produced negative free cash flow of about $4.9M. That figure must not be annualized: Q4 FY2025 alone generated $64.0M of the year's $132.9M. But two things are genuinely worse irrespective of seasonality: nine-month net earnings are $18.9M lower, and working capital swung against the company (receivables −$21.7M, inventories −$6.1M, other current liabilities −$7.5M) versus a prior year in which inventories released $3.9M.

### Balance sheet and capital allocation

Cash fell from $250.6M at August 31, 2025 to $154.8M at May 31, 2026. The $95.8M decline is a capital-allocation choice, not operating distress: $80.7M of buybacks (667,000 shares, including the 1% excise tax, at an implied average of roughly $121.05, about 6% above the current price), $11.5M of dividends and $35.5M of capex. Share count fell from 10,804K to 10,169K, a 5.9% reduction in nine months, with only 42K of dilutive awards in the Q3 diluted count and no disclosed contingent-dilution overhang.

Debt is simple: $115.0M of unsecured Series A notes at a fixed 3.82%, entire principal due February 19, 2030, plus a de minimis Elecsys bond. The $50M Wells Fargo revolver (with a $50M accordion) runs to August 26, 2030 and was undrawn. Covenants are a funded-debt/EBITDA test and an interest-coverage test with compliance confirmed, though specific thresholds are not disclosed. With $115M of debt against roughly $85M of TTM EBITDA and $155M of cash, headroom is wide. Equity/assets slipped from 63.4% to 60.7% and working capital/assets from 46.3% to 38.5%. Bankruptcy risk is not the question here.

Two qualifications on financial health. First, $107.4M of the $154.8M cash sits at foreign subsidiaries, implying roughly $47M domestic, and the tax note cites an unfavorable withholding-tax impact from repatriation. Continuing buybacks at the recent ~$27M-per-quarter pace requires either repatriation with tax leakage or revolver draws. Second, capex is stepping up roughly 3x into a demand downturn: $18.8M (FY2023) → $29.0M (FY2024) → $42.5M (FY2025) → $50–55M guided for FY2026, with net PP&E rising from $99.7M to $166.4M. That is a defensible counter-cyclical investment in cost structure, and it plausibly explains part of the current fixed-cost deleverage, but it depresses near-term free cash flow and has coincided with three consecutive years of declining ROIC (14.8% → 13.4% → 11.0% → 10.9%).

One item deserves a flag on earnings quality. Nine-month net interest income was $7.618M versus $3.832M, a 99% increase, despite average cash falling and debt flat. Carry arithmetic on $115M at 3.82% (about $3.3M of cost over nine months) against roughly $200M average cash at ~4% (about $6.0M of income) nets to about $2.7M, leaving roughly $5M unexplained. Two plausible sources: amortization of the excluded component of a $200.0M / €183.2M cross-currency net-investment hedge program accounted for under the spot method, or high local deposit rates on the $107.4M held in Brazil, Türkiye and elsewhere. The filings do not disaggregate interest income, so this is unresolved. Either way, a non-trivial slice of pre-tax income is non-operating in character and should not be capitalized at an operating multiple. Separately, the Q3 10-Q confirms a tariff-refund recovery recorded after the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating IEEPA tariffs, but does not quantify it. Any FY2026 benefit is a one-timer, and margins still fell 220bp with it included, which suggests underlying cost pressure is worse than reported.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is negative with an unproven inflection.

**North America irrigation is in a multi-period downturn.** FY2025 −9%; nine-month FY2026 −8%; Q3 FY2026 −11%. The macro inputs disclosed in the 10-Q do not support a near-term turn: USDA's February 2026 forecast put 2026 net farm income at $153.4B, down 1%, with cash receipts down 3% and the gap filled by government transfers. Corn was down 2% and soybeans up 11% year over year at May 2026, with favorable weather producing higher production and inventories that maintain downward pressure. Management states explicitly that the $12 billion of one-time corn and soybean payments announced December 8, 2025 is "not expected to result in a meaningful increase in demand for irrigation equipment," and applied the same judgment to the prior year's projected farm-income jump. Refusing to treat transfer payments as demand is a credibility positive; it is also a management team telling investors not to expect a rebound from that source.

**Brazil is a financing problem, not a crop-price problem.** FY2025 international growth included higher Brazilian volumes; by Q3 FY2026 MD&A cites limited credit availability and elevated interest rates constraining farmer capital investment. That is outside management's control and, in the current global rate environment, unlikely to reverse quickly.

**The MENA cadence is the swing factor.** Project #1 (>$100M) completed in Q1 FY2026. A successor agreement announced December 2025 is valued above $80M with roughly $70M recognized in fiscal 2026, approximately 11% of likely FY2026 revenue in one project, after one irrigation customer represented 13% of consolidated FY2025 revenue (versus none above 10% in FY2024 or FY2023). Management landed a follow-on, which is evidence of a repeatable sovereign food-security capability. But project #2 is roughly 20% smaller, and the FY2025 irrigation gross margin decline from 30.3% to 29.2% was attributed specifically to the dilutive margin of project revenue. Growth bought at below-segment margins converts revenue expansion into limited earnings expansion.

A common misreading: international rose from 41% to 52% of irrigation segment revenue between FY2024 and FY2025, then fell to 49.3% in nine-month FY2026. That mix shift was driven by one customer. Geographic mix improved while customer concentration worsened; the two are not the same measure and here they point in opposite directions.

**Infrastructure faces a dated cliff.** Both filings state IIJA federal programs are scheduled to run through September 2026, and the language softened between them, from funding that "supports demand" in the 10-K to funding that "may support higher demand" in the 10-Q. Infrastructure backlog decreased in the most recent quarter even as total backlog rose. Reauthorization is a binary event roughly six weeks from now. [unverified — federal deficit and appropriations-process characterization not in the brief]

**Offsets.** Backlog of $136.1M at May 31, 2026 versus $117.1M a year earlier (+16%), with the sub-twelve-month component $116.0M versus $104.8M (+11%), against an FY2025 year-end trough of $110.7M. EPA finalized RFS volumes in March 2026 at roughly +16% over 2025, better than the 8–10% proposed, which is a genuine structural corn-demand improvement. OBBBA restored 100% bonus depreciation and adds roughly $65.6B of agricultural spending over a decade. The Farm Bill received a one-year extension in November 2025. Longer term, the majority of worldwide irrigation remains flood irrigation, and the pivot conversion opportunity in water-scarce regions is real. It converts slowly and in lumps.

Consensus reflects the mix: current-fiscal-year EPS of $5.26 (revised up 3.5%, which is largely Q4 arithmetic catching up after the Q3 beat) but current-quarter down 10.0%, next-quarter down 16.9%, and FY2027 down 4.6% to $6.05. The estimate-revision signal here is negative for the periods that matter.

Trailing multiples across the group are inflated by depressed denominators, so the median is not a usable anchor. Against AGCO's 14.1x, the closest comparable in terms of row-crop exposure and cycle position, Lindsay carries a 55% premium, which its Road Zipper franchise, net-cash balance sheet and subscription attach can partly justify but not fully.

The more relevant framing is what the current price requires. Normalized earnings power looks like FY2025's $74.1M of net income on a reduced share count: on 10.0M shares that is about $7.41, on the current 10.17M about $7.28. Applying 18x to a normalized $6.75–7.00 (haircutting for the dilutive margin of project revenue and the non-operating share of other income) gives $122–126. Applying 20x gives $135–140, roughly the analyst mean of $135.50. The bull case is not implausible: it requires margin reversion in both segments plus another MENA award plus a North American turn, and it delivers roughly the same multiple the stock already carries.

The problem is the downside. There is no valuation cushion at 21.9x trailing on earnings that fell 25%. If FY2027 estimates continue to drift down (they have already been cut 4.6%) the 18.9x forward becomes 21x on the revised number, and a 16x multiple on $5.30 of extended-trough EPS marks the stock at $85. Analyst targets bracket $121 low, $135.50 mean, $150 high, and the low target sits above spot. But with four covering analysts (one buy, three holds), and with the same analysts cutting the next two quarters by 10–17%, those targets are lagging rather than confirming. The "targets as floors" logic applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples; this is a stock with falling out-year estimates at 22x trailing.

Valuation scores 4.5. Not stretched to the point of impairment, and the balance sheet limits how far the multiple can compress, but there is no identified mispricing and no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Irrigation is a four-player U.S. oligopoly with commodity-adjacent economics. The 10-K's own competitive-factor list (price and seasonal programs, product quality, durability, controls, dealer retention and reputation, service, delivery time) describes a market differentiated by channel and service rather than product. The consequence is visible: pass-through language deteriorated from tariff costs having "been passed through to the market" in the FY2025 10-K to "a portion has been passed through" in the Q3 FY2026 10-Q, and nine-month irrigation gross margin ran 28.2%. The company's large-diameter tubing operation sits directly in the steel input stream, and the Item 1A warning that price competition may prevent cost recovery indicates the cost side is not improving on its own.

The durable competitive assets are the 200-plus dealer network with FieldNET tied exclusively to it (raising dealer switching costs), the installed pivot base generating parts and replacement demand, and the global manufacturing footprint that management cites as a tariff mitigant.

Infrastructure has better unit economics and worse visibility. Road Zipper's limited competition and 41.4% FY2025 segment gross margin are genuine, and once a corridor is engineered around the system, switching costs are high. But the real competitor is the do-nothing alternative of conventional concrete barrier, adoption requires a transportation agency to fund a specific corridor, and the 10-K states plainly that it is rare for a single customer to account for significant revenue in consecutive years. The crash-cushion line faces real competition plus the MASH-standards treadmill, under which products accepted under older NCHRP 350 criteria are generally no longer eligible for federal reimbursement.

Trajectory is what matters for scoring, and the trajectory is mildly negative: gross margin down 220bp, ROIC down for three consecutive years while capex tripled, and demonstrated inability to recoup input costs. Competitive position scores 5.0, a decent franchise on a deteriorating margin path.

## Risk Assessment

**Unquantified litigation with no accrual.** Note 8 of the Q3 10-Q discloses multiple product-liability suits arising from vehicle collisions with barriers incorporating the X-Lite end terminal; Tennessee and California qui tam actions filed under seal on November 27, 2023 by the same relator whose federal False Claims Act case was dismissed in October 2023 (Tennessee declined to intervene in March 2024 with service in June 2024; California declined in September 2024 with service in June 2025); and a Missouri Highways and Transportation Commission suit filed June 9, 2020 alleging defective design and seeking compensatory and punitive damages for removal and replacement of X-Lite terminals statewide. The company believes loss is not probable, has recorded no accrual, and is unable to estimate a range. The FY2025 10-K adds a second-order risk that a perceived product issue can lead to removal from government qualified-products lists, which would be a revenue event as well as a legal one.

**Environmental remediation in prolonged limbo.** Groundwater contamination at the Lindsay, Nebraska facility carries a $10.6M accrual ($0.5M current, $10.1M noncurrent), of which $8.0M is discounted at a 1.2% risk-free rate ($9.1M undiscounted). A revised remedial alternatives evaluation went to EPA and Nebraska DEE in August 2020; a thermal-remediation pilot completed in Q4 FY2023; a final report followed in November 2023; as of the July 2026 filing the plan is still not approved. Nearly six years without an approved plan means the accrual is an estimate against an undefined requirement. A discount-rate update would reduce the liability; scope expansion would increase it.

**MENA project exposure to an active conflict.** The Q3 10-Q adds language absent from the 10-K: deliveries in MENA have not yet been significantly delayed by the current Iran conflict, but expected project timing in the region could change as it evolves, alongside increased freight and input costs. That is a live risk. With roughly 11% of likely FY2026 revenue in one MENA project and the FY2027 pipeline dependent on landing a third, this is the single largest timing risk to the recovery case. [unverified — specific characterization of Hormuz transit levels, attacks on U.S. assets in Kuwait/Bahrain, Damietta terminal disruption, and freight-rate moves is not in the brief]

**IIJA expiry, September 2026.** A dated binary within weeks, with infrastructure backlog already decreasing and management's language softened.

**Concentration and cyclicality that the disclosure now names.** The FY2025 10-K carries a risk factor built specifically around the >$100M MENA agreement, warning that if a large project "is not subsequently followed by another similarly sized opportunity," financial performance could be negatively affected. That is the mechanism visible in the FY2026 numbers.

**Unresolved governance item.** Item 5.02 8-K filings appeared on July 23, 2025; September 15, 2025; October 14, 2025; October 24, 2025; November 7, 2025; January 12, 2026; and July 17, 2026. Confirmable: CFO Brian Ketcham's retirement effective December 31, 2025 and Samuel Hinrichsen's arrival (first Form 4 dated November 3, 2025 with zero prior holdings). Inferred from Form 4 title fields: an Irrigation president change from Gustavo Oberto to Brian Magnusson around December 2025, which would mean the segment representing roughly 85% of revenue changed leadership at the onset of its downturn. The July 17, 2026 filing, post-dating both the Q3 10-Q and the earnings call, is unidentified and unresolved. Management commentary and guidance from the July 2, 2026 call are not in evidence, which limits confidence in whether current earnings represent a trough.

**Management alignment.** CEO total compensation reached $5.096M in FY2025, up 39.6% and a five-year high, in the fiscal year ROIC hit a five-year low of 10.9%. Indexed cumulative TSR shows LNN at 144.4 versus a peer group at 244.64 over five fiscal years, with the gap widening every year since FY2022. Incentive measures (ROIC, revenue, operating margin, free cash flow) are all absolute rather than relative, so peer outperformance does not penalize payouts. No open-market insider purchases appear in the Form 4 window from August 2025 through January 2026; every disposition in the retrieved record was executed 18–31% above the current price. Offsetting this: the buyback pace demonstrates conviction, the debt structure is cheap and long-dated, and the dividend is comfortably covered at roughly 28% of TTM EPS. Management quality scores 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 35.9% sits 6.0 points above one-year realized volatility of 29.9%, and the term structure is in backwardation (35.9% at eight days, 34.0% at 36 days, 32.7% at 127 days), consistent with the market pricing near-term event risk, plausibly the Q4 FY2026 print in late October and the IIJA reauthorization window. Implied moves are ±4.5% for the nearest expiry and ±8.3% for September.

Direction is not confirmed. Put/call skew is essentially balanced at −1.5% near-term, and put/call open-interest ratio has collapsed from 1.36 on June 30 to 0.02, with ATM IV down 18.5 points over the same span while the price fell 7.7%. Open interest is 70 contracts across three expirations, so this chain is too thin to read as informed positioning either way. The one defensible takeaway: hedging demand that existed in late June has been removed even as the stock declined, meaning downside protection is neither crowded nor cheap-relative-to-realized. Short interest of 9.9% of float at 4.5 days to cover is moderate, enough to produce a squeeze on a strong Q4 print, not enough to constitute a crowded bearish position.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $148 (+29%)

North American irrigation troughs in FY2026 as bonus depreciation, the RFS volume increase and eventual commodity-price recovery restore farmer capital budgets. MENA project #2 delivers on schedule in Q4 FY2026 and FY2027, and a third award lands, establishing the sovereign food-security pipeline as recurring rather than episodic. Input-cost pressure eases and the IEEPA tariff refunds are collected. IIJA is reauthorized, restoring Road Zipper project visibility and returning infrastructure operating margin toward the 24% posted in FY2025. Margin reversion plus $100M of remaining buyback authorization on a $1.16B market cap takes share count toward 9.6M and FY2027 EPS to roughly $6.90; at 21.5x that is $148, coincident with the 52-week high. The $50–55M FY2026 capex program lowers the cost base before the upcycle, and the balance sheet (net cash, no maturity until 2030) funds the wait.

### Bear Case — $88 (−23%)

North American irrigation declines a fourth consecutive period as high production and inventories keep commodity prices suppressed and government transfers substitute for market income without generating equipment demand. Brazilian credit remains restrictive under a global higher-for-longer rate regime. MENA project #2 slips on regional conflict, freight or port disruption, and no third award materializes in FY2027, leaving the international comparison negative for a second year. IIJA lapses in September 2026 without reauthorization, and infrastructure operating margin stays near 16% rather than reverting to the mid-20s. Tariff pass-through remains partial, holding irrigation gross margin near 28%. FY2027 EPS settles near $5.30 and the multiple compresses to 16x on a business whose ROIC has declined three consecutive years, marking the stock at $85–88. The X-Lite docket produces an adverse Missouri ruling with punitive damages, or a qualified-products-list removal, adding an unmodeled charge. Buybacks slow because domestic cash is roughly $47M and repatriation carries withholding tax.

### Base Case — $120 (+5%)

Q4 FY2026 benefits from MENA #2 deliveries and peak road-construction season, delivering FY2026 EPS near the $5.26 consensus. FY2027 recovers partially to roughly $6.05 as North America stabilizes without inflecting, infrastructure margin recovers halfway toward FY2025 levels, and the buyback continues at a reduced pace. At 19.8x that is $120, or equivalently 18.5x a normalized $6.50. Total return including the 1.3% dividend is approximately 6% over twelve months.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 20% bull / 35% bear gives an expected return of approximately zero against an equity cost of capital of 8–9%, with 3:1 downside skew.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Twelve-month horizon.

**De-risking plan.** Trim exposure now rather than waiting for the late-October Q4 FY2026 print, which carries two-sided risk: MENA delivery timing and peak-season infrastructure revenue could produce a beat, while the IIJA expiry lands in September ahead of it. Existing holders should reduce to a residual position sized for the possibility that FY2026 proves the trough. Do not add. New capital has no reason to enter at 21.9x trailing on 25%-lower earnings with the out-year consensus being cut.

**Base case target: $120** (19.8x FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.05, equivalently 18.5x a normalized $6.50), +5% from $114.29.
**Bull case target: $148** (21.5x FY2027 EPS of ~$6.90 on a reduced share count), +29%.
**Bear case target: $88** (16.5x extended-trough FY2027 EPS of ~$5.30), −23%.

**Upgrade to HOLD requires** any two of: (a) Q4 FY2026 consolidated gross margin at or above 31%, demonstrating that input-cost pass-through has been restored rather than deferred; (b) IIJA reauthorization enacted or a successor federal surface-transportation program with comparable funding, plus infrastructure backlog rising year over year; (c) North American irrigation revenue flat or positive year over year for a single quarter, ending the four-period decline; (d) a price below roughly $100, which would put the stock near 16.5x FY2027 consensus and restore a margin of safety consistent with the bear case.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY requires** the above plus a third MENA award of comparable size at non-dilutive margin, or clustered open-market insider purchases by the CEO/CFO, neither of which has appeared in the Form 4 record through January 2026.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:** IIJA lapses without a successor program and infrastructure operating margin falls below 14% for two consecutive quarters; MENA project #2 revenue recognition is materially delayed or the agreement is amended downward on regional disruption; an adverse liability determination in the Missouri X-Lite action or a qualified-products-list removal that quantifies the currently unaccrued exposure; buybacks are suspended or the revolver is drawn to fund them, indicating domestic liquidity constraint; or FY2027 consensus falls below $5.25, which would put the stock above 21x on a fourth consecutive year of earnings decline.

**Reassessment timeline.** Two dated checkpoints: the September 2026 IIJA expiry/reauthorization outcome, and the Q4 FY2026 and full-year results in late October 2026, which will also provide the first full-year cash-flow figure and the FY2027 capex plan. ## Conclusion

Lindsay is a well-capitalized small-cap industrial with one genuinely differentiated product line, a defensible dealer moat in a four-player irrigation oligopoly, and a management team that has refused to talk its own book on farm-income headlines. Those attributes justify a premium to the cheapest names in agricultural machinery. They do not justify 21.9x trailing earnings that have fallen 25%, with nine-month operating income down 33%, gross margin down 220bp in both segments, negative nine-month free cash flow, and consensus cutting the next two quarters by 10–17% and FY2027 by 4.6%.

The multiple expanded while earnings contracted, which means the price already embeds the recovery. Delivering that recovery requires three things to go right at once: margin reversion in both segments, a third nine-figure MENA award, and a turn in the North American row-crop capital cycle, with the second exposed to an active Middle East conflict affecting delivery timing and freight, and the infrastructure leg facing a federal funding expiry within weeks. Against +5% to base case sits −23% to a bear case that requires nothing more than the current trends persisting for another four quarters. Management's own buybacks at an average $121.05 are now underwater, and no insider has bought in the open market through the decline.

The overall score moves to 4.9 from 5.4, reflecting the Q3 FY2026 margin evidence and the forward estimate cuts rather than the 7.7% price decline since the prior review. After four consecutive HOLDs on a deteriorating fundamental trend, the appropriate action is to reduce exposure and revisit below $100, where the bear case would be substantially priced and the balance sheet would provide a genuine floor.
