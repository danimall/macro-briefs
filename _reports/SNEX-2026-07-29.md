**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

StoneX Group operates a global financial-services network spanning execution, clearing, market-making, cross-border payments, and physical commodity trading across 180+ countries. The company is in the middle of an exceptional earnings run: operating revenue rose 64% year-over-year in Q2 FY26 (quarter ended 3/31/2026) to $1.57B, net income rose 143% to $174.3M ($2.07 diluted EPS versus $0.94), and return on equity reached 26.5%, up from 15.7%. These are outstanding absolute numbers. The central analytical question is how much of them recur.

Management's own MD&A attributes the surge to conditions that are inherently mean-reverting: gold/silver volatility and Comex/London price dislocations tied to potential U.S. metals tariffs, the U.S.-Iran conflict driving hedging demand, and elevated interest income on ~$14B of client float. Layered on top is the RJO acquisition (closed 7/31/2025), which contributed $213.5M of operating revenue (~14% of the quarter) and roughly doubled the futures-brokerage footprint. Strip out the volatility windfall and this is a good business enjoying a very good year, not a permanent step-change in earnings power.

The stock reflects this ambiguity. Shares trade near $72 after reaching the mid-$90s in mid-2026, a drawdown of roughly 20–25%, during which insiders monetized large option positions at $110–$135 (split-adjusted). Jefferies cut its rating to Hold in July 2026, arguing the stock already reflected its favorable operating position. At ~19.7x trailing earnings on what appears to be peak-cycle profit, with a rate-sensitive earnings stream facing a Fed that markets read as done hiking or possibly cutting, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric. We rate SNEX HOLD, with the durability of the current earnings base as the swing variable.

## Company Overview

StoneX connects commercial, institutional, and retail clients to global markets through four segments:

- **Commercial** — the largest operating-revenue segment ($537.6M in Q2 FY26, +115% YoY), housing global hedging (agriculture/energy), global metals (LME plus precious metals), physical supply and trading, and exchange-traded derivatives. This is where the physical-commodity gross revenue flows through.
- **Institutional** — equity and fixed-income trading, prime services, capital markets, correspondent clearing, and FX prime brokerage. The largest interest-income generator, running a very large repo/securities-lending book.
- **Self-Directed/Retail** — forex/CFDs (legacy GAIN Capital) and independent wealth management.
- **Payments** — cross-border FX payments in 140+ currencies, the highest-margin, lowest-capital-intensity segment and a genuine niche leader by country/currency coverage.

A critical caveat for any valuation screen: SNEX's headline revenue (~$132B annualized) is grossly inflated by physical precious-metals sales recognized on a gross basis. Total revenue in Q2 FY26 was $45.8B, but operating revenue (total revenue less cost of physical commodities) was only $1.57B. Roughly $41.4B of the $45.8B is physical precious-metals flow booked through the Middle East and Asia geography. Any EV/Sales or net-margin metric computed on gross revenue is meaningless. All analysis here uses operating revenue, net operating revenue, and net income.

The company effected a 3-for-2 stock split in March 2026; figures here are split-adjusted where relevant.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 FY26 (three months ended 3/31/2026):**
- Operating revenue $1,566.8M, +64% YoY
- Net operating revenue $829.1M, +70%
- Net income $174.3M, +143%; diluted EPS $2.07 vs. $0.94
- Adjusted EBITDA $296.9M, +115%
- ROE 26.5% (from 15.7%)

**Six months ended 3/31/2026:** operating revenue $3,005.0M (+58%); net income $313.3M (+100%); ROE 24.5%.

Three drivers explain the surge, and their durability varies:

1. **RJO acquisition** — contributed $213.5M operating revenue and $20.9M net income in the quarter, adding ~$6.4B of average client float and 37.8M listed-derivative contracts per quarter. This is a genuine, durable expansion of the earnings base, though purchase accounting was still preliminary at 3/31/26 and integration costs (severance $13.7M in 1H FY26, including UK collective-redundancy and US termination/retention) are ongoing.

2. **Metals/volatility windfall** — precious-metals operating revenue rose ~$116M in the quarter, attributed to gold/silver volatility, Comex/London dislocations tied to potential tariffs, and U.S.-Iran conflict hedging demand. This is the least durable component and the one that should be heavily discounted in normalized earnings.

3. **Interest on client float** — a rate-sensitive stream on client balances. A 25bp cut reduces annual net income by ~$10.8M. With markets pricing a peak in the hiking cycle and a plausible path toward cuts if energy inflation reverses, this line is a headwind, not a tailwind, over the next 12 months.

**Balance sheet:** total assets ballooned to $53.6B (from $31.3B a year earlier, +71%), driven mostly by the securities/repo book — repos payable $17.4B, reverse repos $15.2B, financial instruments owned $11.3B. This is a highly leveraged, low-net-margin flow/spread business by design; ~97% of assets are described as liquid, and regulatory net capital at all major subsidiaries comfortably exceeds minimums. Equity is $2.70B. The apparent leverage is a feature of the FCM/dealer model, not a distress signal.

**Debt:** two senior secured notes — $550M at 7.875% due 2031 and $625M at 6.875% due 2032, issued to fund the RJO cash consideration — plus $1.685B of committed facilities ($412M drawn). Manageable against cash flows and liquidity, though the 6.875–7.875% coupons are a meaningful fixed cost.

**Cash flow:** operating cash flow is highly volatile due to trading-book swings (+$1.54B for 1H FY26 vs. –$632M prior year). This is not a clean FCF signal for a flow business; interpret through operating revenue and net income.

**Capital allocation:** reinvestment via serial tuck-in M&A is the primary use of capital. A 3.375M-share buyback authorization (Oct 2025–Sep 2026) is essentially unused (only tax-withholding repurchases). No dividend. Share count is rising (79.0M at 3/31/26) from RJO stock consideration ($300M) and option exercises — a mild per-share dilution offset by earnings growth.

Financial health scores a 6.5: the model is resilient by design (liquid balance sheet, comfortable net capital, diversified earnings), but the leverage, low net margin, coupon burden, and rate sensitivity temper the score. Cash-hoarding is not the issue here; the risk is that the earnings supporting current ratios are cyclically elevated.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth is spectacular but must be decomposed. Consensus EPS estimates have risen sharply — current-year up 15.7% and next-year up 13.6% over 90 days (albeit from a thin single-analyst base) — reflecting the strong first half. The earnings-surprise record is mixed rather than consistent: two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with a 36.5% beat in Q2 FY26 but a –17.7% miss the prior quarter. This is characteristic of a business whose results are driven by market conditions rather than a smooth operating cadence, which lowers confidence in point estimates.

The durable growth engine is the serial acquisition machine. Post-RJO, StoneX has closed WCS International (UK wholesale banknotes, Payments), Intercam (LatAm brokerage/advisory, Retail), Plantureux (French ag brokerage, Commercial), and GEA Capital wealth assets, on top of prior-year deals (Benchmark Company, Octo Finances). This high-cadence tuck-in strategy has genuinely expanded the platform's reach and cross-sell surface, and the Payments segment is a structural growth vector less tied to the volatility cycle.

The cyclical growth engine is the metals/volatility/rate windfall, which is unlikely to repeat at the same intensity. The self-directed/retail segment already showed the flip side: FX/CFD revenue-per-million fell ~21% in 1H FY26, a reminder that spread capture is not guaranteed. The estimate-revision magnitude is real, but it sits close to the CRITICAL-signal threshold and is heavily contaminated by non-recurring volatility rather than a clean secular gain, so we do not treat it as a standalone strong-BUY trigger. Growth prospects score a 6.2 — solid, acquisition-supported, but with a large cyclical component that the market has correctly flagged as suspect.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$72 and ~19.7x trailing earnings (forward and trailing P/E are equal in the provided data), SNEX sits roughly in line with its brokerage peer set: Houlihan Lokey 21.8x, Piper Sandler 19.7x, Moelis 23.2x, Stifel 13.9x, BGC 30.4x — peer median 21.8x. On its face, that is a modest discount to the median. The problem is the earnings denominator. If FY26 net income is inflated by the volatility windfall and the rate tailwind, then the "19.7x" is understated relative to a normalized earnings base — the multiple on mid-cycle earnings could be meaningfully higher.

This is where our calibration on cheapness applies with force. A superficially reasonable multiple on peak-cycle earnings is precisely the value-trap pattern that sorts the wrong way. The estimate revisions are positive, but they reflect the same non-recurring drivers, so they do not independently vindicate the price. Jefferies' July downgrade — "operating fundamentals remain favorable" but the stock "already reflects its favorable operating position" — captures the setup: a good business at a fair price on flattered earnings.

The consensus price target of $112 (single data point, high/low/mean identical) implies ~56% upside, but this target sits uneasily against the July de-rating and the sharp reduction in analyst coverage enthusiasm (the buy/hold split moved from 2 buys to 1 buy, 1 hold). We treat the $112 as stale rather than a floor, because the conditions for treating consensus targets as floors — a cheap stock relative to durable, accelerating growth — are not cleanly met when the growth is cyclical.

We do not, however, penalize the stock simply for having declined from its highs; that alone is not a valuation input. A lower price on unchanged fundamentals improves risk/reward, but here the drawdown demands a specific thesis about what the sellers see. The most defensible interpretation is that the market is repricing the durability of the windfall and the rate tailwind, which is a legitimate fundamental concern rather than pure volatility. On that basis, valuation scores a 5.8: neither clearly cheap nor stretched, with the ambiguity centered on normalized earnings power.

## Competitive Landscape

StoneX's edge is breadth and integration rather than dominance in any single vertical. It competes as one of the largest independent FCMs (RJO being the oldest US futures brokerage), as a fixed-income dealer running a large repo/securities-lending book against banks, in retail FX/CFD (legacy GAIN against IG, CMC, Plus500), and in cross-border payments, where its 140+ currency / 180+ country coverage is a differentiated niche leader.

The competitive trajectory is improving. RJO expanded the futures franchise materially; the Payments segment is a genuine moat with high margins and low capital intensity; and the tuck-in acquisition cadence keeps widening the network's cross-sell surface. Segment revenue growth in Commercial (+115%) and the platform's ability to capture geopolitical hedging demand demonstrate scale advantages in volatile conditions. The prior report's competitive-position score had improved to 7.3; we mark it slightly lower at 7.0, reflecting that some of the apparent share gain is cyclical volume capture rather than durable structural displacement, and that RJO integration is not yet fully de-risked. The direction remains favorable.

## Risk Assessment

**Earnings-quality / cyclicality (the central risk).** The blowout leans on volatility (metals, energy) and the geopolitical events management explicitly names. A normalization of volatility would pressure Commercial and Institutional hard. This is peak-cycle earnings, and management effectively says so.

**Interest-rate sensitivity.** Client float generates rate-sensitive net interest, at ~$10.8M of annual net income per 25bp of cuts. With the hiking cycle likely at or near its peak and a plausible path toward cuts, this is a forward headwind.

**RJO integration.** RJO nearly doubled the FCM footprint; severance and preliminary purchase accounting mean integration risk and potential client attrition remain live.

**Counterparty/credit.** Bad-debt expense spiked to $12.4M in Q2 (from $0.1M), driven by an $8.0M precious-metals counterparty default and Supply & Trading receivables; the allowance rose to $63.9M. This line warrants monitoring as a tell on underwriting discipline in a volatile market.

**Legal/regulatory.** The BTIG trade-secrets matter resolved favorably in 2026 (net $1.9M, injunctive relief denied) and the SEC closed its inquiry with no action, but an open DOJ subpoena on the same conduct remains — a tail risk the company states cannot presently be determined.

**Governance / key-person.** Founder Sean O'Connor stepped back to Executive Vice-Chairman with Philip Smith now CEO. The transition is orderly on paper, but O'Connor architected the network; strategic continuity is a watch item.

Given the confluence of a peak-cycle earnings base, rate sensitivity into a likely-easing cycle, an open DOJ matter, and rising bad debt, risk factors score a 4.8 — the specific, not-fully-priced downside catalysts (earnings normalization, rate cuts, DOJ) are material. This is a step down from the prior 5.2 and reflects sharper visibility into how much of the earnings base is cyclical.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is call-heavy, not bearish: put/call skew is –3.2% to –3.5% across tenors, put/call volume ratio 0.53, and put/call OI ratio 0.40. Short interest is modest at 7.1% of float with 3.4 days to cover — an uncrowded position. Implied moves are elevated (11% at 1m, 22.3% at 6m) against 47.4% one-year historical volatility, consistent with a name whose earnings are event- and volatility-driven. The options market is not pricing an imminent collapse, which tempers the bear case, but the rich implied moves confirm the market views SNEX's earnings as high-variance. The absence of heavy put demand is a mild positive against our cautious risk score.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($95–112):** RJO and the tuck-in machine prove the earnings base has genuinely stepped up; volatility stays elevated on persistent geopolitical and tariff dislocations; the Payments segment compounds at high margins; and normalized EPS settles well above pre-2026 levels, making today's ~19.7x look cheap in hindsight. Positive estimate revisions continue, analysts re-rate toward the stale $112 target, and the stock recovers most of its drawdown. ROE sustains in the low-to-mid 20s.

**Bear case ($52–58):** volatility normalizes, metals dislocations resolve, and the Fed cuts, compressing both trading spreads and client-float interest simultaneously. FY27 EPS reverts toward a mid-cycle base, revealing that the current multiple was applied to peak earnings; the stock de-rates to ~14–15x (Stifel-like) on lower earnings. The DOJ matter or a further bad-debt event adds an idiosyncratic shock. Insider selling near the mid-2026 highs proves prescient.

**Base case ($70–80):** the durable pieces (RJO, Payments, expanded FCM scale) hold, but the cyclical windfall fades, leaving normalized EPS above the old base yet below the FY26 peak. The stock trades in a range around the current price as the market waits for evidence of which earnings base is real. This is the most likely 12-month outcome and underpins the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain — the swing variable is earnings durability, and current evidence does not support a confident call in either direction).

This marks the fourth consecutive HOLD. With the same call issued repeatedly, the bar for maintaining it is the persistence of a genuine two-sided setup, which is met here: strong fundamentals against a demonstrably cyclical earnings base at a fair-not-cheap multiple, with rate and DOJ tail risks unresolved.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters showing the post-RJO/Payments earnings base holds even as metals/volatility revenue normalizes — specifically, net income sustaining above ~$130M/quarter with metals contribution declining.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s that compresses the multiple below ~14x on normalized earnings, restoring asymmetric upside.
- DOJ subpoena resolved without material penalty, removing the tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY27 guidance or estimate revisions turning negative as volatility fades, confirming peak-earnings reversion.
- A material adverse DOJ development, or bad-debt expense continuing to escalate above the Q2 $12.4M run-rate, signaling deteriorating underwriting.
- Evidence of RJO client attrition or integration cost overruns eroding the acquired earnings.

**Reassessment timeline:** next earnings (Q3 FY26, fiscal year ends September 30) is the natural checkpoint — it will show whether the H1 FY26 windfall extends or fades, and whether the rate-sensitive float income is already rolling over.

## Conclusion

StoneX is a well-managed, genuinely diversified financial-services network that delivered exceptional results in the first half of FY26. The problem is not the quality of the business but the durability of the earnings — management's own disclosures tie the surge to volatility, tariff-driven metals dislocations, geopolitical hedging demand, and rate-sensitive client float, most of which are cyclical and some of which are actively reversing. RJO and the Payments franchise provide a real, durable expansion of the base, but not enough to underwrite the peak-cycle profit at today's multiple with confidence.

At ~19.7x trailing earnings that are likely inflated, with a rate tailwind turning into a headwind, an open DOJ matter, rising bad debt, and insiders having monetized large positions near the mid-2026 highs before a subsequent drawdown, the risk/reward is balanced. The options market's call-heavy, uncrowded positioning argues against an imminent break lower, but does not resolve the core question. We rate SNEX HOLD and will look for evidence in the next one to two quarters on whether the earnings base has genuinely stepped up or is simply enjoying a very good year.
