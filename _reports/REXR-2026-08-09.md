## Executive Summary

Rexford owns 409 industrial properties totaling ~49.9 million square feet, entirely in Southern California infill submarkets, leased to roughly 1,550 mostly small tenants with no single tenant above 2.4% of annualized base rent. The portfolio quality is not in dispute. What is in dispute is the forward earnings power of leases signed during the 2021-23 rent spike. Management's own filings state that infill Southern California market rents were ~24% below the mid-2023 peak at June 30, 2026 (worse than the ~22% disclosed at year-end 2025), that asking rates fell quarter over quarter in all five submarkets the company tracks, and that "releasing spreads on a portion of this expiring space may be negative" over the next several years. H1 2026 leasing bore that out: 261 leases across 6.2 million square feet at -7.7% net effective and -14.0% cash spreads. Even excluding the single 1.1 million square foot Tireco extension (-33.5% cash), H1 blended cash spreads were -6.1%.

The company's response is a genuine strategic reversal, documented inside the primary filings rather than only on a call. After acquiring 56 properties for $1.51B in cash in 2024, Rexford bought nothing in 2025, returned $183.1M of 1031 proceeds because no replacement met underwriting, cancelled six development projects, replaced both founder co-CEOs, and at the July 23 Q2 release quadrupled disposition guidance to $1.5-2.0B while authorizing $1.0B of repurchases through July 2028. Roughly $1B of proceeds is earmarked to retire 2027 maturities (weighted coupon ~4.08%) rather than refinance at current rates. Reproducing the arithmetic: selling $2.0B at a 5.75% cap forgoes ~$115M of NOI, retiring $1.0B saves ~$40.8M of interest, and $1.0B of buybacks at $36.74 retires ~27.2M shares (12.2% of the count), producing ~$2.39 of Core FFO per share against a $2.405 FY2026 midpoint — essentially neutral, with a ±100bp cap-rate band swinging the outcome to +2% or -2.4%.

That framing defines the investment case. At 15.3x Core FFO guidance, a 4.74% dividend covered at a 72% payout, and a ~5.8% implied cash cap rate against Southern California market cap rates plausibly in the 5.0-5.75% range, the stock is priced near asset value with a credible deleveraging plan and no near-term funding stress. It is not priced at a large discount, and the earnings stream backing that cap rate contains above-market rents scheduled to reset downward. Probability-weighting a $39.50 base case, $46 bull and $30 bear against the 4.74% yield produces roughly an 8% expected total return over twelve months — approximately a REIT cost of equity. HOLD, held primarily for the covered yield rather than for an appreciation thesis.

## Company Overview

Rexford is a self-managed UPREIT formed in Maryland in January 2013, electing REIT status from 2013, reporting a single segment. Annualized base rent of $791.3M at June 30, 2026 ($17.61/sf, or $16.69/sf excluding industrial outdoor storage, cell and solar) is distributed across Los Angeles (57.3% of ABR at year-end 2025), San Bernardino (18.7%), Orange County (10.9%), San Diego (6.8%) and Ventura (6.3%). Average tenant space is roughly 26,000-28,000 square feet. Approximately 12% of square footage sits inside the City of Los Angeles, which matters because Measure ULA imposes a 5.5% transfer fee on sales at or above $10M.

Tenant granularity is a real structural strength; industry concentration is less so. Three NAICS groupings — Manufacturing 22.5%, Wholesale Trade 21.7%, Transportation & Warehousing 21.6% — accounted for 65.8% of ABR at year-end 2025, and the 10-K states a substantial majority of tenants are non-investment-grade. The company is vertically integrated with 256 employees across five regional offices, and sourced ~77% of acquisitions by property count off-market or through lightly marketed processes since its IPO.

The FY2025 10-K reordered the stated growth levers, placing internal value-add management first, "Accretive Capital Recycling" second, and external acquisitions third, and added the sentence "We also sell assets programmatically as part of our capital allocation strategy" to the opening business description. Leadership turned over completely: Laura Clark, previously COO, became CEO April 1, 2026; John Nahas became COO; co-founders Howard Schwimmer and Michael Frankel left executive roles March 31, 2026 and the board at the May 19 annual meeting. Michael Fitzmaurice is CFO; Tyler Rose chairs the board.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported earnings are dominated by non-cash impairment and should not be used directly.** The quoted trailing and forward P/E of -21.06 reflects $631.6M of H1 2026 real estate impairment ($624.8M in Q2 alone) on top of $89.1M in FY2025 — $720.7M cumulative in roughly nine months. FY2026 GAAP guidance is a net loss of $(1.32)-$(1.27) per diluted share, revised from positive $1.22-$1.27 a quarter earlier, with $2.71/share of the swing attributed to company-share impairment. Impairment reduces net income, ROA and book equity but does not touch operating cash flow, NOI, Core FFO, or the EBITDA- and asset-value-based covenants. Any screen reading REXR as distressed on GAAP earnings or a low Altman Z is measuring REIT mechanics — mandatory distributions push cumulative dividends above cumulative GAAP earnings by construction, and capital-intensive real estate produces a sales/assets term near 0.08.

**Revenue and NOI have stopped growing and begun shrinking.**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rental income | 791.4 | 922.1 | 980.4 | 490.4 | 485.1 |
| Total revenues | — | 936.4 | 1,003.1 | 501.8 | 490.6 |

| Cash NOI | 540.4 | 645.8 | 698.6 | 352.4 | 338.6 |

FY2025's 6.3% rental income growth came almost entirely from the 2024 acquisition vintage: Same Property rental income grew 1.8% and Same Property NOI was essentially flat. In H1 2026 rental income declined 1.1% and Cash NOI declined 3.9% as dispositions took effect. Q2 2026 Same Property NOI was -0.5%; Same Property Cash NOI +1.5%.

**Per-share Core FFO growth has decelerated for four consecutive years:** $1.64 (2021), $1.96 (+19.5%), $2.19 (+11.7%), $2.34 (+6.8%), $2.40 (+2.6%), and FY2026 guidance of $2.38-$2.43 (-0.8% to +1.3%).

**Q2 2026's headline per-share improvement is low quality.** Company-share Core FFO per diluted share rose 6.8% to $0.63, decomposing as +1.2% dollar growth ($141.4M vs $139.7M) multiplied by a 5.2% reduction in weighted average diluted shares (223.8M vs 236.1M). Within the $1.7M of dollar growth: G&A fell $6.06M (share-based compensation expensed dropped from $10,091K to $778K, a $9.3M swing), other income rose $3.5M on a one-time dispute-resolution settlement with a former tenant, interest income fell $5.3M as cash dropped from $165.8M to $32.2M, interest expense rose $1.87M, and NOI contributed $0.51M. The company also recorded a $2.33M reversal of previously recognized transition-related share-based compensation because estimated Core FFO growth achievement was lowered — a benefit that arises because performance is tracking below target. Elimination of two co-CEO compensation packages is a permanent structural saving (FY2026 G&A guidance cut to ~$57M from ~$60M), but property-level economics did not improve.

**Balance sheet quality is the strongest part of the case.** At June 30, 2026: total debt principal $3,285.5M, 99.6% fixed including swaps, weighted average effective rate 3.725%, weighted average term 2.8 years, only $14.0M drawn on the revolver at quarter-end ($100.0M as of the filing date against $1.145B available). Cash $32.2M, total liquidity ~$1.3B. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDAre of 4.5x reproduces as $3,231.5M ÷ ($182.052M × 4) = 4.44x. Ratings are Baa2/BBB+/BBB+. Notes payable fair value of $3,207.1M against $3,263.7M carrying means the below-market coupon stack carries modest positive economic value, which would be surrendered on early retirement of fixed-rate notes rather than repayment at maturity.

The maturity ladder concentrates in 2027 at $1,019.1M: $575M of 4.375% exchangeable notes (March), a $300M term loan swapped to 3.577% (May), and $125M of 3.93% senior notes (July), weighted coupon ~4.08%. The exchangeables convert near $63.64/share, deeply out of the money and correctly excluded from the 223.8M diluted count. Covenants (total debt/asset value ≤60%, EBITDA/fixed charges ≥1.5x, unencumbered NOI/unsecured interest ≥1.75x) are in compliance.

**Cash flow and dividend.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $542.1M against $333.4M of capex ($13.3M recurring, $228.8M repositioning/development), leaving $208.7M of free cash flow. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $248.0M, down $32.8M year over year, with investing cash flow positive $143.9M as dispositions exceeded capex. The dividend was raised to $0.435 quarterly, annualizing to $1.74 — a 4.74% yield and 72.3% of the FY2026 Core FFO midpoint. On a seasonality-aware sensitivity rather than a forecast, doubling H1 operating cash flow gives ~$496M against ~$420M of distributions and ~$222M of annualized capex: cash flow covers the dividend but not the dividend plus development spend, with the gap funded by dispositions. H1 included lease-termination and settlement receipts and adverse working-capital timing (prepaid rents -$9.3M, payables/accruals -$21.7M), so the run rate should not be extrapolated.

**Two accrual signals deserve monitoring.** Straight-line rent recognition doubled to $25.1M in H1 2026 from $12.4M, with deferred rent receivable rising from $190.4M to $210.5M in six months, while cash releasing spreads were -14%. GAAP NOI is increasingly supported by non-cash accruals from newly signed leases carrying escalators and free-rent periods (the Tireco extension alone includes three months free in 2027). Separately, collectability reserves and write-offs reduced rental income by $1.4M in Q2 2026 versus $0.1M a year earlier, and $4.1M in H1 versus $2.4M; the press release names higher bad debt as a driver of the Same Property NOI decline.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question reduces to the lease roll. At June 30, 2026, expirations run 5.1% of square footage for the remainder of 2026 ($49.8M ABR), 13.0% in 2027 ($108.9M), 14.2% in 2028 ($135.8M), 14.1% in 2029, 14.2% in 2030 and 16.5% in 2031, with 4.9% vacant and 5.1% in repositioning. Applying observed cash spreads of -10% to -15% to roughly 14% of the roll implies a 1.4-2.1% annual drag on cash NOI, offset by contractual escalators around 2.5-3% on the standing portfolio plus development deliveries. That arithmetic yields flat-to-slightly-positive Same Property cash NOI at best, which is what guidance now says: Same Property NOI of -1.25% to -0.25% and Same Property Cash NOI of -0.75% to +0.25%.

Those guidance ranges were raised from -2.0%/-1.0% and -1.5%/-0.5% respectively, but part of the improvement is definitional. The 2026 Same Property Portfolio now excludes "properties included in the 2026 disposition guidance" — the same assets management has characterized as carrying rents materially above market. Removing the steepest mark-to-market resets from the same-store set mechanically raises reported growth. This is standard, disclosed presentation and arguably correct, since those properties will not be in the portfolio; it is not a like-for-like operational upgrade. Occupancy guidance also rose (95.3-95.7% from 95.1-95.6%), which the definitional change does not explain.

Occupancy is bifurcating. Total portfolio occupancy slipped from 90.2% to 90.0% over six months; the stabilized portfolio fell from 96.0% to 94.8%; Same Property ending occupancy from 96.5% to 95.1%. By county on a Q2 weighted-average basis versus the prior-year quarter: Los Angeles 96.5% (+330bp) and Ventura 94.6% (+320bp) improved, while Riverside/San Bernardino fell 370bp to 93.3% and Orange County fell 170bp to 95.9%. The Inland Empire is 18.7% of ABR. Management attributed the sequential Q2 decline to two Inland Empire move-outs slightly over 200,000 square feet each, one a bankruptcy, with the space already re-leased for September occupancy. Industrial outdoor storage occupancy on ~8.3M land square feet fell from 97.8% to 92.8% in six months — a 500bp decline in the asset class most often marketed as scarcity-protected.

The genuine growth engine remaining is development. Q2 2026 stabilized two projects (196,391 sf, $98.0M) at an 8.0% weighted average unlevered stabilized return on cost; year to date, four projects (341,280 sf, $146.6M) at 7.1%. Against a ~5.8% implied portfolio cash cap rate, that is 130-220bp of value creation per dollar deployed. The pipeline is being shrunk rather than grown: six projects (~850,000 sf) were cancelled in 2025 and all six sites sold in H1 2026, six of twelve H1 disposals were former near-term development sites, several completed buildings sat 0% leased at quarter-end (9615 Norwalk, 15010 Don Julian, 4416 Azusa Canyon, 12772 San Fernando, 1500 Raymond), and pipeline leasing year-to-date through July 23 totaled only 286,299 square feet. FY2026 starts guidance is unchanged at 1.2M sf / $160-170M.

**On consensus estimates:** the supplied EPS series ($0.98 current year, $0.99 next year, with +4.5% next-year revision on 1-2 analysts) does not reconcile to GAAP EPS (-$2.26 in Q2) or to Core FFO ($0.63 in Q2), and the "beat" history (3 of 4, including a 79% Q2 surprise) is measured against a basis that changes with impairment treatment. I treat that series and the surprise record as low-information and anchor on issuer Core FFO guidance, which was raised one penny at the Q2 release. The estimate-revision magnitude here does not approach the threshold at which revisions become a standalone signal, and coverage is too thin to weight.

## Valuation Assessment

**Multiple.** At $36.74 against the $2.405 FY2026 Core FFO midpoint, REXR trades at 15.3x. Peer P/E comparison is not usable — REXR's GAAP earnings are negative from impairment, and the peer set's reported multiples (STAG 29.5, TRNO 16.3, FR 24.3, CUBE 29.2, EGP 36.8; median 29.2) reflect widely varying depreciation and gain-on-sale mixes. The usable relative comparison is yield: REXR's 4.74% against a peer median of 3.2%, a ~150bp premium that prices the negative mark-to-market and single-market concentration.

**Asset value.** Building enterprise value from the frozen price: common 222,989,057 × $36.74 = $8,192.6M; OP/vested LTIP/vested performance units not owned by the company 8,716,332 × $36.74 = $320.2M; preferred liquidation preference $161.25M; net debt $3,231.5M. Enterprise value ≈ $11,905.6M, or ~$11,781.7M net of the $123.9M mortgage loan receivable (7.50% coupon, borrower current, matures 2028). Annualizing Q2 cash NOI ($173.010M × 4 = $692.0M) implies a 5.81% cash cap rate; on GAAP NOI ($186.782M × 4 = $747.1M), 6.28%. Annualizing a single quarter for a shrinking portfolio is a point-in-time sensitivity, not a forecast.

The important adjustment cuts against the bull case. Because in-place rents include blocks materially above market, a cap rate computed on current cash NOI overstates the yield on stabilized market rents. Reset the roll to market and the effective forward cap rate is closer to 5.3-5.5%. Against Southern California infill market cap rates plausibly in the 5.0-5.75% range, REXR is priced at approximately asset value, not at a wide discount. Book value per share of $33.82 (1.09x price/book) is a poor NAV proxy and is cited only to size the impairment-driven equity reduction from $8,460.6M to $7,541.6M in six months.

**The accretion math is the swing factor.** Starting from ~$543M of Core FFO (226M weighted shares × $2.405): sell $2.0B at 5.75% (-$115M NOI), repay $1.0B at 4.08% (+$40.8M interest), buy back $1.0B at $36.74 (-27.2M shares to ~195.8M) → $2.394/share, neutral. At 5.25% execution: $2.446 (+2%). At 6.25%: $2.343 (-2.4%). Management's "neutral to accretive through 2027 FFO per share" survives the arithmetic; the payoff is in leverage reduction and in removing negative mark-to-market from the roll, not in near-term earnings. The sensitivity ignores Measure ULA transfer fees on any City of Los Angeles component, prohibited-transaction tax characterization risk, and forfeited below-market debt value on early retirement.

**Scenarios.**
- **Base $39.50** — 16.3x an estimated $2.42 of 2027 Core FFO per share, assuming ~5.75% disposition execution, ~$1B of buybacks completed, and Same Property cash NOI flat. This coincides with the $39.50 analyst median. Upside +7.5% plus 4.74% dividend ≈ +12.2% total.
- **Bull $46** — 18x ~$2.55, requiring 5.25%-area execution, leverage toward 3.5-4.0x, market rents stabilizing in 2027 on multi-decade-low construction starts, and cash spreads returning to flat. Roughly the $45 analyst high. +25.2% price, ~+30% total.
- **Bear $30** — 13x ~$2.30 on 6.25%+ execution, incremental impairment as non-binding marks convert to executed prices, Inland Empire vacancy widening, and cash spreads worsening beyond -15%. Near the $32.14 fifty-two-week low. -18.3% price, ~-13.6% total.

Weighting base 50% / bull 20% / bear 30% gives roughly +8% expected total return — approximately a REIT cost of equity, with the dividend supplying more than half of it. Applying a higher long-end discount rate is warranted given elevated Treasury yields and heavy Treasury and corporate supply; that environment makes cap-rate compression an unlikely source of return and leaves the buyback and deleveraging as the operative levers. A "cheap" 15.3x multiple attached to declining cash rents is the classic value-trap configuration, and I score valuation only modestly above neutral for that reason.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural claim — Southern California infill is the nation's largest, most fragmented industrial market, with scarce developable land and dense consumption — remains intact, and fragmentation is what sustains the off-market sourcing channel (77% of acquisitions by count since IPO). Forward supply is a genuine 2027-28 positive; construction starts are near multi-decade lows.

Pricing has moved the other way. Company-disclosed market data at June 30, 2026: rents ~24% below the mid-2023 peak (versus ~22% at year-end 2025) and asking rates down quarter over quarter in every submarket — Los Angeles County (5.0% vacancy), Orange County (5.5%, rising), Inland Empire West (5.9%), San Diego (6.6%), Ventura (4.2%). Management's press-release framing emphasizes increasing tenant demand, positive net absorption and declining vacancy as early signs of strengthening conditions. Both readings fit: in industrial cycles volume typically recovers before pricing. But the same filing that reports improving absorption reports asking rents falling everywhere and the peak-to-current gap widening. Rents remain roughly 40% above pre-pandemic levels after an ~80% run in 2020-22, which is why the reset has years to run.

On the disposition side, Rexford is now competing with other sellers to place roughly 8 million square feet while reporting negative releasing spreads. Clark has said the focus is selling to owner-users, a buyer class that underwrites occupancy value rather than in-place NOI and is less cap-rate sensitive — a plausible route to pricing above institutional bids, though unverified in disposition economics. Six of twelve H1 disposals were former near-term development sites and seven sold at no gain or loss because they had already been impaired to the sales price, which is as consistent with pruning what could not be repositioned profitably as with opportunistic arbitrage.

Trajectory, not level, drives this score: portfolio square footage shrinking 51.2M → 49.9M, stabilized occupancy 96.0% → 94.8%, IOS occupancy -500bp, pipeline cancelled and shrinking, asking rents falling in all five submarkets. Offsetting: 7.1-8.0% development yields, best-in-class tenant granularity, and a supply picture that turns favorable in 2027-28.

## Risk Assessment

**Negative mark-to-market on the roll (primary, management-disclosed).** With 13-16% of square footage expiring annually through 2031 off 2021-23 rents and market rents 24% below peak, negative cash spreads are a multi-year condition. The Tireco extension (1.1M sf at 10545 Production Avenue, -31.0% net effective / -33.5% cash, converting from triple-net to gross) is the largest single instance; management states it is not indicative, and excluding it H1 blended spreads were +1.7% net effective / -6.1% cash. Even the ex-Tireco cash figure is negative.

**Impairment recurrence.** $1,225,984K of real estate carried at fair value at June 30, 2026 — $1,189.1M measured at Level 2 using non-binding offers and letters of intent, $36.9M at Level 3 using broker valuations — versus $150.0M at year-end 2025. Marks resting on non-binding indications can move if executed prices come in lower. FY2026 guidance embeds ~$2.71/share of company-share impairment, approximating the H1 charge, implying management does not currently expect material additional write-downs.

**Disposition execution.** The FY2025 10-K added expanded language on inability to complete planned dispositions, price reductions and extended timelines. Measure ULA imposes 5.5% on City of Los Angeles sales at or above $10M against ~12% of portfolio square footage; no LA City properties were sold in 2025, so this cost is untested at scale. Selling $1.5-2.0B — 13-17% of total assets — raises the salience of the 100% prohibited-transaction tax on property held primarily for sale, a factual determination outside statutory safe harbors. Tax Matters Agreements from UPREIT contributions both restrict taxable sales of certain properties during protected periods and require maintaining certain debt levels or offering guarantee opportunities, the latter in direct tension with deleveraging. Neither constraint is quantified in the filings.

**Concentration.** 100% Southern California, seismically active, with earthquake insurance subject to material deductibles and captive coverage of $25.0M only above a $350.0M attachment point, and no coverage for floods outside designated plains, riots, war or wildfires. Port of LA/Long Beach import exposure to tariffs, trade policy or labor disruption is a disclosed risk. Industry concentration of 65.8% of ABR in three groupings and a substantially non-investment-grade tenant base are consistent with the rising bad-debt trend already visible.

**Governance.** The FY2025 10-K added a "Stockholder activism" risk factor that did not previously exist, alongside $6.9M of "nonrecurring advisory service costs" and $4.4M of severance in FY2025 "Other expenses, net" (which jumped to $72.6M from $2.2M, including $60.2M of accelerated non-cash equity compensation for two executive officers). Secondary reporting attributes an August 2025 stake to Elliott Investment Management; no Schedule 13D or 13G was located, and Clark has said the position is unclear and Elliott "may not own shares at all anymore." Treat the stake as unverified. The practical consequence: the changes are in place and largely irreversible, but the external party that would police follow-through may be gone. Notably, the quadrupled disposition guidance and the $1.0B authorization both came after Elliott's reported step-back, which argues the strategy is now management-owned.

**Two-way capital-markets capacity.** A $1.25B ATM with $927.4M remaining sits alongside the $1.0B repurchase authorization and a $1.05B revolver accordion. Maintaining a shelf is standard housekeeping, but investors underwriting per-share accretion should not assume the share count only falls, particularly with Clark stating acquisitions "will be a part of our strategy."

**Contingent equity.** Unvested awards at June 30, 2026 were 628,208 restricted shares, 185,285 LTIP units and 2,011,382 performance units at maximum. Full maximum vesting of all 2.82M units equals ~1.27% of existing shares, or ~1.25% post-issuance ownership dilution — immaterial, and maximum achievement looks unlikely given roughly flat guided Core FFO growth and five-year TSR below the peer group. The company recorded a $2.33M compensation reversal in Q2 on lowered Core FFO achievement estimates.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 36.8% sits 10.5 points above 26.3% trailing realized volatility, at the 75th percentile of the chain, in backwardation (36.8% at 11 days versus 28.1% at 39 days and 25.5% at 130 days), with a 3.6% put/call skew the data source characterizes as elevated put demand and a ±7.8% implied move on the August 21 expiry. There is no scheduled earnings event in that window — Q2 was reported July 23 — so the near-dated premium is being paid for something else: plausibly disposition or transaction headlines, or the September FOMC. Confidence is low: the chain carries only 40 contracts across five expirations, the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.05 against a 0.95 volume ratio is internally inconsistent in a way that thin open interest explains, and the reported collapse from 3.70 to 0.05 since the June snapshot is more likely an expiration artifact than a positioning shift. The directional read I take from options is limited to this: near-dated protection is priced richly relative to realized volatility, which is consistent with the analyst distribution having shifted to three strong sells, and argues against paying up for near-term optionality. Short interest of 5.6% of float at 3.4 days to cover is uncrowded, with no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($46, +25% price / ~+30% total)

Rexford is selling the worst part of its own lease roll and shrinking the equity against it. If the disposition pool genuinely carries rents more than 20% above market (externally reported from the July call, not in a filing), then selling it converts a declining income stream into cash and mechanically removes the largest negative resets from the forward roll. Execution near a 5.25% cap makes the program ~2% accretive to Core FFO per share while cutting net debt/EBITDAre from 4.5x toward the high-3s. A $1.0B buyback at current prices retires 12.2% of the shares. Construction starts at multi-decade lows mean the 2027-28 supply picture supports rent stabilization, and the leasing volume, absorption and vacancy data management cites are consistent with a volume-before-pricing recovery. Development stabilizations at 7.1-8.0% unlevered returns against a ~5.8% implied cap rate demonstrate the value-add platform still creates value per dollar deployed. G&A is structurally lower with two co-CEO packages eliminated. The debt stack is 99.6% fixed at 3.725% with 2.8 years of term and no floating exposure to a higher-for-longer path. Three insiders bought in the open market on the same February day, and the new CEO has publicly committed to lower, more TSR-linked compensation. On these inputs, 18x $2.55 gets to $46 with a 4.74% dividend paid along the way.

### Bear Case ($30, -18% price / ~-14% total)

Every operating metric that matters moved the wrong way in H1 2026. Cash releasing spreads of -14.0% (-6.1% excluding Tireco) against a roll of 13-16% per year through 2031 means cash NOI per remaining square foot declines for several more years — management says so in the 10-Q. Stabilized occupancy fell 120bp in six months, IOS occupancy fell 500bp, the Inland Empire (18.7% of ABR) lost 370bp of occupancy year over year, bad-debt charges rose 14x in the quarter, and asking rents fell sequentially in all five submarkets. Q2's 6.8% per-share Core FFO growth was four-fifths share count, with the dollar growth driven by a non-cash compensation accrual reduction (itself caused by missing internal targets) and a one-time $3.5M legal settlement, against $0.5M of NOI contribution. Straight-line rent doubled while cash spreads went to -14%, meaning GAAP NOI is increasingly non-cash. $720.7M of impairment in nine months rests on non-binding offers, and $1.19B of assets are marked to indications that could fall. The Same Property guidance improvement is partly a definitional exclusion of the disposition pool. Capital allocation has a poor recent round trip: equity settled at $48.89 in Q1 2025, buybacks executed at $39.51 (FY2025) and $35.99 (H1 2026). The activist that catalyzed the reset may no longer own shares. At 5.3-5.5% on stabilized market rents the stock is already at asset value, so a disposition shortfall to 6.25% caps plus continued spread erosion supports 13x $2.30.

### What Would Change My Mind

The single most decisive datum is the executed cap rate on the disposition program, which management said should largely close before year-end. Cap rate determines whether the program is accretive or dilutive, whether current impairment marks hold, and whether the deleveraging-plus-buyback thesis is worth a multiple above 15x. The second is two consecutive quarters of cash releasing spreads improving toward flat, which would convert a multi-year headwind into a fading one.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory has been 5.1 → 5.1 → 5.6 → 5.4, and this review lands at 5.3. This is the fourth HOLD in five reviews, so the bar for repeating it should be explicit. It clears on arithmetic rather than inertia: probability-weighted total return of roughly 8% against a REIT cost of equity of a similar order, with the 4.74% dividend covered at 72% of Core FFO supplying more than half of it. The rating is an income hold. I am not underwriting appreciation, because the two mechanisms that could deliver it — cap-rate compression and same-store NOI growth — face a higher-for-longer rate environment with the long end under supply pressure, and a management-disclosed multi-year negative mark-to-market. Existing holders should keep the position and collect the dividend through the disposition program's execution window. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $36.74, roughly at asset value; the asymmetry improves materially in the low $30s, or on evidence that the disposition pool clears at 5.50% or better with cash releasing spreads narrowing toward flat.
