## Executive Summary

Valley National Bancorp is a $64.1 billion-asset regional bank with 230 branches concentrated in New Jersey, New York, and Florida. FY2025 was a recovery year: net income surged 57% to $598M, NIM expanded 20 bps to 3.05%, and the provision for credit losses fell 55% from $309M to $140M. Management is guiding for 11-13% NII growth in 2026, with NIM expected to reach 3.30% by year-end, a trajectory supported by ongoing deposit repricing (cost of deposits fell 49 bps in 2025) and a shift toward higher-yielding C&I loans.

The stock trades at $13.45, approximately 1.0x book value ($13.39/share) and 1.37x tangible book value ($9.85/share), with a forward P/E of 13.3x. This is a reasonable valuation for a bank in mid-recovery, but it is no longer cheap: VLY has already rallied 65% from its 52-week low of $8.04. The consensus analyst target range of $13.00-$16.00 (median $14.75) implies modest remaining upside.

The critical issue is the tension between VLY's improving earnings trajectory and its still-elevated CRE concentration (333% of capital, down from 474% two years ago but above the 300% regulatory guideline) alongside rising non-performing assets ($440M, up 18% YoY). Net charge-offs improved, but new inflows to non-performing status are outpacing resolutions. Until this trend reverses, credit quality improvement remains incomplete, and the stock's re-rating potential is capped.

## Company Overview

Valley National Bancorp operates through Valley National Bank, a relationship-focused commercial bank with roots in the NY/NJ metro area. The business breaks into two operating segments:

- **Commercial Banking (~80% of earning assets):** C&I loans ($11.0B), CRE loans ($26.8B), and construction loans ($2.5B). Generated $627M in pre-tax income in 2025.
- **Consumer Banking (~20%):** Residential mortgages ($5.8B), auto loans ($2.2B), plus wealth management, insurance, and private banking. Generated $135M pre-tax in 2025.

Management is actively reducing CRE concentration — the ratio has declined from 474% of capital in 2023 to 333% at year-end 2025 — while growing C&I lending (up 10.4% in 2025) and diversifying fee income through capital markets, treasury management, and niche verticals including cannabis banking, healthcare lending, and fund finance.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability Recovery

FY2025 marked a strong rebound from a depressed 2024:

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 | Change |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Net Interest Income | $1,764M | $1,629M | +8.3% |
| NIM (TE) | 3.05% | 2.85% | +20 bps |
| Non-Interest Income | $262M | $225M | +16.8% |
| Provision for Credit Losses | $140M | $309M | -54.8% |
| Net Income | $598M | $380M | +57.3% |
| Diluted EPS | $1.01 | $0.69 | +46.4% |
| ROAA | 0.96% | 0.61% | +35 bps |
| Efficiency Ratio | 54.4% | 58.0% | -354 bps |

The improvement was broad-based: NIM expansion drove higher NII, fee income grew across capital markets (+$14.8M) and service charges (+$13.0M), and the provision declined by $169M as net charge-offs fell from $202M to $117M.

Q4 2025 NIM reached 3.17%, up 12 bps sequentially — the strongest quarterly reading in two years. Cost of deposits fell from 2.94% (Q4 2024) to 2.45% (Q4 2025), reflecting a 49% cumulative deposit beta during the Fed's easing cycle.

### Balance Sheet

Total loans stand at $50.1B, up from $48.8B, with deposits at $52.2B (up from $50.1B). The loan-to-deposit ratio improved from 97.5% to 96.1%. Wholesale funding declined from 18.7% to 15.3% of total funding, a positive shift toward more stable, lower-cost core deposits.

Capital ratios are healthy: CET1 at 10.99%, total risk-based capital at 13.77%, and Tier 1 leverage at 9.63%. The bank repurchased 6.1M shares at an average price of $10.41 in 2025 ($63M total) and redeemed $215M in subordinated notes. Book value per share grew from $12.67 to $13.39, and tangible book value from $9.10 to $9.85 — an 8.2% increase.

Free cash flow was $326M (operating cash flow of $344M less $18M capex). Liquidity is adequate: $6.1B in liquid assets plus $22.5B in unused borrowing capacity.

### Credit Quality — The Unresolved Concern

The headline numbers improved: net charge-offs fell to 0.24% of average loans from 0.40%. But the underlying trend is less clean:

- Non-performing assets rose 18% to $440M (0.87% of total loans)
- Non-accrual loans increased to $434M from $360M, driven by a $79M increase in non-accrual CRE loans
- Past-due loans (30+ days) jumped from $99M to $141M
- The allowance coverage ratio of non-accruals declined from 155.5% to 134.4%
- Forgone interest on non-accrual loans grew from $32.5M to $42.6M

Management guides for NCOs of 0.15-0.20% in 2026. If achievable, this implies continued credit improvement. But NPA inflows exceeding resolutions is a trend that typically persists for 2-3 quarters before reversing. In the current macro environment — with a 50-55% recession probability, elevated CPI, and rates held at 3.64% — CRE borrowers face sustained pressure from high debt service costs and uncertain property valuations.

## Growth Analysis

### 2026 Guidance and Trajectory

Management's FY2026 guidance:
- NII growth: 11-13%
- NIM target: 3.30% by Q4 2026
- Loan growth: 4-6% (with C&I growth of ~10%)
- Deposit growth: 5-7%
- NCO ratio: 0.15-0.20%

The NIM expansion thesis rests on two pillars: (1) continued deposit repricing as term deposits reprice lower, and (2) mix shift from lower-yielding CRE to higher-yielding C&I loans. Both are credible. The bank's deposit beta of 55% in Q4 2025 suggests further room for cost-of-funds reduction, particularly if the Fed holds at 3.64% and term deposits continue to roll off at lower rates.

However, the guidance was set assuming additional rate cuts toward 3.00-3.25% by year-end 2026. If the Fed holds rates at current levels — which the macro environment strongly suggests — the deposit repricing tailwind weakens. Asset-side yields also stop expanding once the lending environment stabilizes. The bank's own sensitivity analysis shows a 100 bps rate decline would reduce NII by 1.46%, indicating the asset side is modestly more exposed to lower rates than the liability side. A prolonged hold scenario narrows the NIM expansion path to mix shift and deposit repricing of existing higher-rate term CDs.

C&I growth at 10% is achievable given the bank's investment in relationship bankers and niche verticals. Management noted targeted talent acquisitions in 2025, which partially explains the elevated officer turnover (9 officer-related 8-K filings in 18 months). If this is deliberate team-building for commercial banking capabilities, it supports the growth story.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

VLY beat estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters, with beats of 6.6%, 7.7%, and 2.9% offset by a 6.0% miss in Q1 2025. The improving beat trend suggests either management conservatism or accelerating fundamentals. Given the NIM trajectory, the Q1 2026 report (due April 23, 2026) should reflect continued margin expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | VLY | Context |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Forward P/E | 13.3x | Reasonable for a mid-cap bank in recovery |
| Price/Book | 1.00x | At book value — discount to well-capitalized peers |
| Price/TBV | 1.37x | Modest premium given $1.87B goodwill |
| Dividend Yield | 3.2% | Adequate; 56.4% retention ratio |
| ROATCE | 10.89% | Improving but below cost of equity |

At 1.0x book, VLY is priced for a bank generating a return on equity roughly equal to its cost of equity. The ROATCE of 10.89% is improving but still below the ~12% threshold where banks typically command above-book multiples. If NIM reaches 3.30% and credit costs normalize at 0.15-0.20%, ROATCE could approach 12-13% in 2026-2027.

Analyst consensus targets range from $13.00 to $16.00, with a median of $14.75, implying 10% upside from current levels. The $16.00 high-end target requires approximately 1.2x book, which would demand sustained ROATCE above 12%.

Much of the NIM expansion and credit improvement story is now reflected in the price. Remaining upside depends on execution of the CRE de-concentration strategy and continued credit normalization.

## Competitive Landscape

VLY operates in one of the most competitive banking markets in the country — the NY/NJ metro area plus Florida. It faces pressure from money-center banks (JPM, BAC, C) with broader product suites, from peer regionals (BKU, HTH), and from non-bank lenders in CRE.

VLY's five-year revenue CAGR of 9.2% outperformed peers BKU (4.3%) and HTH (-9.5%), reflecting successful deposit gathering and NIM management through the rate cycle. The bank's efficiency ratio of 54.4% is among the better readings for mid-cap regionals.

The niche verticals — cannabis banking, healthcare lending, fund finance — provide some differentiation, though these collectively represent a small fraction of total revenue. The central competitive question is whether VLY can successfully pivot from a CRE-heavy lender to a broader commercial bank without sacrificing growth or increasing risk. The C&I pipeline appears to be building, but the transformation is multi-year.

## Risk Assessment

### CRE Concentration (Primary Risk)

The CRE portfolio includes $3.0B in office loans (including $196M in Manhattan), $8.6B in multifamily (including $601M in >50% rent-regulated Manhattan buildings), and $2.5B in construction. A deterioration in NYC/NJ commercial real estate values would disproportionately affect VLY relative to peers with more diversified loan books.

Management's target of reaching 300% by December 2027 implies roughly 33 percentage points of reduction over two years. This requires sustained C&I growth combined with CRE paydowns or selective runoff. Achievable but not inevitable.

### Rising Non-Performing Assets

NPA inflows exceeding resolutions is a pre-recessionary pattern. In a recession scenario (50-55% base case probability), NCOs could revert toward 2024 levels (0.40%) rather than declining to the guided 0.15-0.20%. A 25 bps increase in NCOs from the guided level would add approximately $125M in provisions, reducing EPS by roughly $0.15-$0.18 (15-18% of guided earnings).

### Interest Rate Path Divergence from Guidance

VLY's 2026 NII guidance assumes further rate cuts. The current macro environment — with global inflation broadening (CPI 3.3%, UK 3.3%), an active energy crisis, and a Fed unanimously committed to 3.64% — makes additional rate cuts highly unlikely. The bank's sensitivity analysis shows modest asset sensitivity, meaning the NIM expansion path narrows if rates hold.

### Goodwill Risk

$1.87B in goodwill represents 24% of total equity. At current trading levels near 1.0x book, the stock price effectively assigns no value premium to the franchise above tangible assets. If goodwill were impaired — even modestly — the impact on GAAP book value and reported earnings would be material.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.3% is 9.6 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 28.7%, indicating the options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (38.3% near-term vs. 33.5% six-month), consistent with positioning ahead of the Q1 2026 earnings report on April 23. Put/call OI ratio of 1.09 is balanced. The implied ±8.4% move, combined with the recent beat streak, could resolve favorably, but rich IV means protection is expensive and the bar for a positive surprise reaction is higher.

Short interest at 7.3% of float with 3.7 days to cover is moderate — not crowded enough to squeeze, but enough to indicate a meaningful cohort of investors betting against the CRE concentration thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($15.00-$16.00, +12% to +19%)
- NIM reaches 3.30% by Q4 2026, driving NII growth above the 13% high-end of guidance
- C&I lending growth reduces CRE concentration below 320% by year-end 2026, earning a modest re-rating
- NPAs peak in Q1-Q2 2026 and begin declining, confirming credit normalization
- ROATCE reaches 12%+, justifying a price/book multiple of 1.1-1.2x
- Continued deposit repricing offsets the lack of further rate cuts

### Bear Case ($10.50-$11.00, -18% to -22%)
- CRE losses accelerate as NYC/NJ office and multifamily markets weaken further
- NCOs revert to 0.35-0.40%, requiring provisions of $200M+ (vs. $140M in 2025)
- NPA inflows do not abate, and management is forced to build reserves at the expense of earnings
- The Fed holds rates at 3.64% or higher, limiting NIM expansion to mix shift only
- Stock re-rates toward 0.85x tangible book (~$8.40), consistent with a bank generating sub-10% ROATCE with elevated credit risk

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **NPAs decline for two consecutive quarters** — specifically, non-accrual loans below $400M, demonstrating that the resolution pipeline is working.
2. **CRE concentration drops below 310% of capital** — within striking distance of the 300% regulatory threshold, signaling the strategic transformation is working.
3. **Stock pullback to $11.50-$12.00** (0.90-0.95x book) on a non-credit-related selloff, creating a more favorable entry point for the NIM expansion story.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **NPAs exceed 1.0% of total loans** — indicating the CRE credit cycle is worsening, not normalizing.
2. **NCOs exceed 0.30% for any quarter** — a reversal of the improving trend that would undermine the provision release story.
3. **Management fails to execute C&I growth** — if C&I loan growth comes in below 5% for FY2026, the CRE de-concentration strategy stalls.

### Reassessment Timeline
- **April 23, 2026:** Q1 2026 earnings. Focus on NIM progression, NPA trends, and updated 2026 guidance.
- **July 2026:** Q2 earnings — two-quarter NPA trend confirmation or denial.
- **December 2027:** CRE-to-capital target date — strategic thesis validation.

## Conclusion

Valley National Bancorp is a bank in mid-recovery trading at fair value. The NIM expansion trajectory is real and supported by deposit repricing mechanics. Management's strategic shift from CRE-heavy lending toward a diversified commercial bank is credible and already producing measurable results (CRE concentration down 141 percentage points in two years). The 3.2% dividend yield provides income while waiting.

However, the stock has already priced in substantial improvement, and the unresolved NPA trend introduces meaningful uncertainty about the pace of credit normalization. In the current macro environment — elevated inflation, rates likely to hold longer than VLY's guidance assumes, 50-55% recession probability — the margin of safety is insufficient for a BUY recommendation.

HOLD with a growth thesis. The April 23 earnings report will provide critical data on whether NPA inflows are stabilizing. Two consecutive quarters of declining NPAs, combined with continued NIM expansion, would warrant an upgrade.
