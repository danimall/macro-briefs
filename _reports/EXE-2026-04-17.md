## Executive Summary

Expand Energy is the largest independent natural gas producer in the United States, formed through Chesapeake Energy's October 2024 merger with Southwestern Energy. The company produces ~7.2 Bcfe/day across three major shale basins (Haynesville, NE Appalachia, SW Appalachia), was added to the S&P 500 in March 2025, and holds investment-grade ratings from all three agencies. At $97.36, the stock trades at ~10.7x the $9.05 FY2026E consensus — a meaningful discount to the E&P peer median of 14.3x — while generating $1.8B in free cash flow (2025) with 60%+ of projected 2026 gas volumes hedged.

The core investment thesis rests on two structural demand catalysts for U.S. natural gas: (1) LNG export capacity expansion along the Gulf Coast, where EXE's Haynesville assets and 35% interest in the NG3 pipeline provide direct access; and (2) rising domestic electricity consumption from data centers and AI infrastructure. Both are multi-year tailwinds with limited sensitivity to short-term commodity price swings. Proved reserves grew 24% organically in 2025 to 25,880 Bcfe (PV-10 of $19.4B), and management guides production flat-to-up in 2026 at $2.75-2.95B capex.

The primary risks are execution-oriented: the CEO and CFO have both been replaced within the past 12 months, the company carries $9.6B in undiscounted midstream commitments, and GP&T costs rose 21% per Mcfe year-over-year from legacy SWN contracts. Natural gas price sensitivity remains the existential variable — the 2024 loss at $2.03/Mcf pricing demonstrates downside vulnerability. However, at current strip pricing (~$3.50+ Henry Hub) and with the hedging floor in place, the risk/reward favors accumulation at these levels.

## Company Overview

Expand Energy Corporation was created on October 1, 2024 when Chesapeake Energy completed its all-stock merger with Southwestern Energy and renamed the combined entity. The company operates approximately 6,600 gross wells across 4.2 million gross acres (3.5 million net), with working interest production concentrated in:

- **Haynesville (LA/TX):** ~42% of production (~3,000 MMcf/day), dry gas, proximate to Gulf Coast LNG export terminals. 23% of proved reserves.
- **Northeast Appalachia (PA Marcellus):** ~37% of production (~2,624 MMcf/day), dry gas with premium access to eastern demand markets. 42% of proved reserves — the largest reserve base.
- **Southwest Appalachia (WV/OH Marcellus & Utica):** ~22% of production (~1,559 MMcfe/day), mixed gas/oil/NGL. 35% of proved reserves.

The company operates ~99% of its current daily production and maintains a vertically integrated oilfield services capability (drilling rigs, pressure pumping) inherited from SWN. The strategic NG3 pipeline (35% ownership interest, in service October 2025) gathers ~900 MMcf/day from Haynesville for delivery to Gulf Coast markets including LNG export facilities.

FCF of $1.8B represents a 15.8% FCF margin. Topline revenue includes ~$3.2B in pass-through marketing segment activity, which inflates the revenue denominator without contributing meaningful margin.

Per-unit cost discipline has been solid: production costs held at $0.24/Mcfe, G&A dropped from $0.14 to $0.07/Mcfe on merger scale leverage, and DD&A declined from $1.26 to $1.13/Mcfe (indicating favorable SWN acquisition economics). This is a structural drag that will take years to renegotiate.

The current ratio of 1.01 is tight on paper but immaterial given the revolver access. The Altman Z-Score of 2.47 sits in the "grey zone," depressed by the large asset base from purchase accounting and near-zero working capital; the Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 (on available signals) paints a healthier picture.

### Capital Allocation

In 2025, the company returned ~$865M ($765M dividends + $100M buybacks) and repaid $663M in debt. At current strip pricing, 2026 should generate similar or modestly higher FCF, supporting continued returns.

### Structural Demand Catalysts

**LNG exports:** The U.S. is on track to add 10-15 Bcf/day of LNG export capacity over the next 5 years. EXE's Haynesville position and NG3 pipeline provide low-cost delivery to Gulf Coast terminals. The current administration's reversal of the LNG export permitting pause (January 2025) accelerated this timeline. The Hormuz disruption and associated European energy security concerns have further increased urgency for non-Middle Eastern gas supply, a structural tailwind for U.S. LNG.

**Power generation demand:** Data center electricity consumption is adding measurable incremental gas demand. Management's MD&A cites "increasing structural demand gains from LNG, power generation, and industrials" and states the gas market balance has "tightened through 2027."

**Reserve growth:** Proved reserves grew 24% organically in 2025 to 25,880 Bcfe, with PUDs nearly doubling from 3,842 to 7,304 Bcfe. PV-10 of $19.4B compares to the current market cap of ~$23B — the stock trades at only 1.2x PV-10, leaving limited downside if reserves are realized.

### Production Guidance

2026 capex of $2.75-2.95B with 205-235 gross wells and 11-12 rigs. This is roughly flat to 2025's $2.85B and 272 wells, suggesting modestly lower activity but maintained production levels. The company has flexibility to accelerate if gas prices strengthen further.

Against peer medians of 14.3x P/E and 2.1% yield, EXE's valuation discount is ~25%. The closest pure-play gas comp is EQT Corporation at 17.6x — a 65% premium to EXE's forward multiple. Devon Energy trades at 11.0x and APA at 9.5x, but both have significantly different production mixes (higher oil weighting).

The analyst consensus target of $132.37 (median $133) implies ~36% upside. The target range of $95-$167 brackets the current price at the low end, suggesting analysts view the stock as near full downside pricing.

The valuation discount is partially justified by the management instability and integration risk discussed below, but a ~25% discount to peer median on the largest U.S. gas producer with investment-grade ratings and structural LNG demand catalysts is too wide. Applying a 12.5x multiple (still below peer median) to $9.05 FY2026E yields a $113 fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

EXE's competitive advantages as the largest independent U.S. gas producer:

1. **Scale in marketing and aggregation:** Higher daily volumes attract larger, more creditworthy counterparties and provide flexibility to optimize basin-level production allocation based on regional pricing.
2. **Vertical integration:** Owned drilling rigs and pressure pumping reduce well costs and improve operational control.
3. **Multi-basin diversification:** Three distinct operating areas with different regional pricing dynamics (Gulf Coast via Haynesville, eastern markets via Appalachia) reduce concentration risk relative to single-basin peers.
4. **LNG proximity:** The Haynesville position and NG3 pipeline provide a structural advantage in accessing LNG export pricing at lower transportation cost than Appalachian competitors.

The primary competitive risk is EQT Corporation ($36B market cap), which also holds substantial Appalachian acreage and trades at a premium multiple, suggesting the market assigns higher quality to EQT's operations or balance sheet. Coterra ($23B, 14.3x) is the most directly comparable by size, though its production includes meaningful liquids volumes.

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Sensitivity (Critical)
EXE derives ~96% of production volume from natural gas. The hedging program (60%+ of 2026 volumes hedged via collars) provides a floor but limits upside participation. A sustained decline to sub-$2.50 Henry Hub would pressure free cash flow toward zero and potentially force activity curtailment.

### Management Instability (Elevated)
CEO Dell'Osso was replaced in February 2026; CFO Singh was terminated without cause in mid-2025; a permanent CFO (Teunissen) was not appointed until April 6, 2026. Three C-suite changes within 12 months during a post-merger integration is a meaningful execution risk. The headquarters relocation from Oklahoma City to Spring, Texas adds cultural friction. Interim CEO Wichterich has relevant experience (led Chesapeake through post-bankruptcy restructuring) but the dual Chairman/CEO role and interim status create governance concerns.

Mitigating factor: Insider buying by Wichterich (2,000 shares at ~$107, March 2026), Director Gallagher (1,000 shares at ~$101, February 2026), and COO Viets (2,000 shares at ~$92, August 2025) provides conviction signals from the people with the most information.

### Midstream Commitment Overhang ($9.6B)
$9.6B in undiscounted gathering, processing, and transportation commitments over up to 20 years represents a massive fixed-cost base. If production volumes decline materially, minimum volume shortfall payments would erode cash flow. The GP&T cost trajectory demonstrates this drag is already materializing through legacy SWN contracts.

### Section 382 Tax Limitations
The SWN merger triggered ownership change limitations on NOL and interest deduction carryforward utilization. Further stock transactions could trigger additional, more restrictive limitations — including on tax depreciation and depletion for five years. This limits the tax shield value of the combined entity.

### Geopolitical / Macro Sensitivity
A durable Hormuz ceasefire would reduce global LNG import urgency and potentially weigh on U.S. gas export pricing. Conversely, if the ceasefire fails and supply disruption intensifies, European LNG demand accelerates and U.S. gas pricing could benefit materially. The company's LNG-proximate positioning makes it a direct beneficiary of global gas supply tightening.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 53.1% is sharply elevated relative to 31.9% historical volatility (21.2% spread), with IV at the 89th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (53.1% near-term → 28.6% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event risk — likely around the next earnings catalyst or a macro/commodity trigger — rather than sustained elevated uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 4.10 is high, suggesting institutional hedging activity, though the put/call volume ratio of 0.51 (more calls than puts trading) and balanced near-term put/call skew (0.0%) indicate that recent flow has been more neutral-to-bullish even as legacy put positions remain outstanding. Unusual call activity at the $95 strike (3.3x volume/OI) for May expiry suggests some positioning for upside from current levels. The options market is pricing elevated short-term risk but is not directionally bearish — the backwardation structure is consistent with a catalyst-driven resolution rather than sustained deterioration.

## Conclusion

Expand Energy offers a compelling entry point for natural gas exposure at a 25% discount to peer median valuation, supported by structural LNG demand growth, data center power consumption, and 24% organic reserve growth. The $9.05 FY2026 consensus with positive near-term revision momentum and 60%+ hedge protection provides earnings visibility. The 2.4% base dividend yield plus buybacks deliver income while waiting for multiple expansion.

The management instability is the most significant near-term overhang. The insider buying pattern (CEO, director, COO all purchasing open-market shares near recent lows) partially offsets this concern by demonstrating insider conviction at current prices.

At $97.36 and ~10.7x forward earnings, the stock prices in the management risk and commodity uncertainty while undervaluing the structural positioning. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $115.
