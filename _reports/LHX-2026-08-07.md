## Executive Summary

L3Harris reported a clean second quarter for fiscal 2026: revenue $5,881M (+8.4% on a like-for-like 13-week comparison), GAAP diluted EPS $3.13 versus $2.80–2.83 consensus, free cash flow $771M (+37%), orders $7.3B for a 1.2x book-to-bill, and record backlog of $42.0B. Management raised full-year GAAP EPS guidance to $11.80–12.00 from $11.40–11.60 and lifted revenue guidance to $23.2–23.7B. On the operating line, core segment income (removing $39M of newly-included segment investment income this year and $92M of product-line asset-sale gains last year) grew 17.1% with 114bp of margin expansion. Communications & Spectrum Dominance ran a 26.0% core margin, and management raised that segment's full-year margin guide from ~25% to mid-25%.

The shares nonetheless trade 23.7% below the March 2 high of $379.23, having touched $262.68 on July 30. Three things drove the de-rating: the FY2026 revenue guidance midpoint came in below sell-side estimates (a substantial part of which is a calendar artifact — H2 FY2026 has 26 weeks against 27 in H2 FY2025, so naive +4.6% H2 growth is +8.6% per week), the Missile Solutions IPO was postponed to mid-2027 with Kubasik stating that "market conditions do not reflect the value we're building," and European order sentiment soured sector-wide in late June. The first is a modeling problem, not a fundamental one. The second removes the nearest sum-of-the-parts catalyst and compresses the buffer against the December 31, 2027 automatic-conversion deadline on the Department of War preferred to roughly six months.

We remain at HOLD. The valuation is fair rather than compelling: 24.3x FY2026 GAAP guidance and 21.6x FY2027 consensus $13.42, but the earnings base carries roughly $0.90–1.00 per share of benefit from a 14.4% year-to-date effective tax rate that management's own disclosure describes as driven by volatile items (5.3% in FY2024, 16.9% in FY2025). Normalizing tax toward 21% puts the FY2027 multiple near 23.4x, in line with General Dynamics and above Northrop Grumman [unverified — specific peer forward multiples not in sources]. Add a shrinking recurring pension tailwind after the $1.4B annuitization, a mix-dilutive backlog (Space & Mission Systems +37.8% at a mid-10% margin; CSD, which supplies ~54% of segment profit, down 4.3%), and 81% of guided free cash flow still to be delivered in H2, and the risk/reward from $289.53 is approximately symmetric. Base case $310 (+7.1%), bull $390 (+34.7%), bear $240 (-17.1%).

## Company Overview

L3Harris is a mid-tier defense prime with ~45,000 employees including ~18,000 engineers and scientists, headquartered in Melbourne, Florida. U.S. Government revenue was 75% of the FY2025 total (76% in each of FY2023 and FY2024); international end-consumer revenue was $4.8B, or 22%, with no single foreign country above 5%. Fixed-price contracts represented 75% of FY2025 revenue — an unusually high share that shifts cost-overrun risk to the company. Company-funded R&D was $536M (2.5% of revenue).

Effective fiscal 2026 the company reorganized from four segments into three, which is the accounting scaffolding for the Missile Solutions separation:

| Segment | FY26 revenue guide | FY26 margin guide | Implied segment operating income |
|---|---|---|---|
| Space & Mission Systems (SMS) | ~$11,700M | mid-10% | ~$1,229M |
| Communications & Spectrum Dominance (CSD) | ~$8,000M | mid-25% | ~$2,040M |
| Missile Solutions (MSL) | ~$4,100M | low-12% | ~$492M |

That build totals ~$3,761M, or 16.0% of the $23.45B revenue midpoint, matching the "low 16%" consolidated segment margin guide. The distribution matters more than the revenue split: **CSD produces roughly 54% of segment operating income on 34% of revenue.** Tactical radios, spectrum and electronic warfare, night vision and targeting are the profit engine. Missile Solutions contributes about 13% of segment operating income at a 12% margin.

Two corporate actions dominate the near-term structure. First, on April 17, 2026 Aerojet Rocketdyne Holdings (the Missile Solutions segment) issued $1.0B face of 7% cumulative redeemable convertible preferred stock plus warrants to the Department of War for $973M net; AXYV Inc. is the newly formed entity that would list. Second, the Space Technology carve-out to AE Industrial Partners (AE takes ~60%, L3Harris retains ~40% at a $825M net enterprise value per the 10-Q; press reports cite $845M and describe the deal as closed in early August) removes certain space propulsion and avionics product lines while retaining RS-25 and hypersonics.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue rose from $17,062M (FY2022) to $21,865M (FY2025), an 8.6% CAGR, though the period includes Aerojet Rocketdyne and Viasat Tactical Data Links, which lifted goodwill from $17.3B to $20.0B. Operating margin expanded 240bp from 7.3% (FY2023) to 9.7% (FY2025) as the LHX NeXt cost program ran through. Net income reached $1,606M on diluted EPS of $8.53; operating cash flow was $3,106M against $424M of capex.

**Underlying growth is mid-to-high single digit, not low double digit.** The Q2 10-Q discloses 26 weeks year-to-date FY2026 versus 25 weeks in FY2025 (Q2 itself was 13 weeks in both). Normalizing per week — and excluding $146M of divested Commercial Aviation Solutions revenue from the prior-year Q1 — gives Q1 organic growth of ~6.3%, Q2 of +8.4%, and H1 of +7.35% ($447.1M/week versus $416.5M/week) against a reported ~+10%. The FY2026 revenue guidance midpoint of $23.45B against FY2025's $21.865B implies +7.25%, which independently corroborates the normalized rate. The same adjustment works in the company's favor for H2: 26 weeks this year versus 27 last year turns a naive +4.6% into +8.6% per week, i.e. accelerating.

**Earnings quality is the central analytical issue.** The +28% GAAP EPS headline overstates operating progress on three counts:

1. **Non-operating income.** "Non-service FAS pension income and other, net" rose $80M to $185M. Within that, recurring non-service pension income *fell* to $75M from $73M in the quarter and to $150M from $163M year-to-date (following a pension risk transfer that annuitized $1.4B of obligation), while net investment gains rose to $73M from $6M. At the 15.5% quarterly tax rate the $80M pretax swing is ~$0.36 per share on 187.3M diluted shares — roughly 52% of the $0.69 year-over-year EPS increase. The durable component is shrinking; the lumpy component is growing. Non-operating income was 26.1% of Q2 pretax income and 21.7% of FY2025 pretax income.
2. **EAC adjustments.** Net estimate-at-completion adjustments added $0.10 to Q2 EPS versus a $0.08 drag a year earlier (+$0.17 year-to-date versus -$0.16). Combined with the non-operating swing, roughly $0.54 of the $0.69 increase is not volume-and-price operating leverage. Within the net-favorable SMS figure sits $30M of *new unfavorable* adjustments on two programs.
3. **Tax.** The 14.4% year-to-date effective rate produced roughly $86M more net income on H1 pretax income of $1,299M than a 21% rate would — about $0.46 per share in the half, $0.90–1.00 annualized, or ~8% of the FY2026 guide. Unrecognized tax benefits are $754M, of which $635M would favorably affect future rates; the rate has swung from 5.3% to 16.9% to 14.4% in three periods.

Strip the presentation changes and the operating story is still good: core segment operating income +17.1% with +114bp of margin. CSD core was $506M on a 26.0% margin (+234bp); SMS core $267M on 9.0% (+130bp from a low base); MSL $130M at 12.3%, roughly flat.

**Cash flow and the H2 burden.** Year-to-date operating cash flow was $784M and free cash flow $584M against full-year guides of ~$3.6B and ~$3.0B. That leaves 81% of guided FCF for H2. This is seasonally normal — H1 FY2025 was 17% of the full-year total — but the per-week requirement is demanding: H2 FY2025 delivered $2,222M over 27 weeks ($82.3M/week); H2 FY2026 requires ~$2,416M over 26 weeks ($92.9M/week, +12.9%). Working capital was a year-to-date drag (receivables -$675M, payables -$369M, partly offset by contract liabilities +$656M). Capex steps up to ~$600M from $424M, partly for missile capacity.

**Balance sheet.** At July 3, 2026: cash $1,521M, total debt $10,999M (essentially all fixed-rate), net debt $9,478M. Net debt/EBITDA is ~3.0x on FY2025 EBITDA of $3,334M and ~2.53x on an H1 FY2026 run rate; interest coverage improved to 4.9x from 3.5x. Current ratio 1.18. The current portion of debt jumped to $1,815M ($550M of 3.85% notes due December 2026, $1,250M of 5.40% notes due January 2027), covered by cash plus expected H2 FCF plus $2.5B of revolver capacity — though the January note will likely be refinanced, roughly rate-neutral at best.

Two structural features deserve emphasis. Goodwill plus intangibles of $26,536M equal 62% of assets, leaving tangible book equity at approximately negative $6.65B; the Altman Z-Score of 1.95 reflects that intangibility rather than distress, given the coverage and cash-generation profile. Separately, **$968M of redeemable subsidiary preferred sits in mezzanine equity, outside both total liabilities ($22,088M) and total equity ($19,882M), with a disclosed highest redemption value of $1,422M.** Screens keyed to standard subtotals understate claims on the enterprise by $1.0–1.4B.

## Growth Analysis

The demand picture is genuinely constructive at the order level. Record backlog of $42.0B represents 1.79x FY2026 revenue guidance; book-to-bill was 1.2x in the quarter. Growth by product line in Q2 was broad: Propulsion Systems +$85M (+$210M year-to-date) on missile and munitions programs, Advanced Effects +$44M, Space Systems +$76M on classified work, ISR +$501M year-to-date (partly a material-procurement milestone), Mission Networks +$40M on higher FAA volume, and +$70M of international software-defined radio deliveries.

The identifiable multi-year drivers are: Golden Dome and munitions/shipbuilding funded within the July 2025 reconciliation package (~$155B national defense); the SDA Tracking Layer Tranche 3; FAA modernization (~$22B FAA appropriation plus $12.5B for air traffic control in reconciliation), a differentiated non-DoD vector with high switching costs; and NATO's 5%-of-GDP trajectory feeding a segment that sells equipment rather than long-cycle platforms.

Two constraints on the growth score. First, base-budget growth is thin — the GFY2026 appropriation provided $859B for the Department of War, only ~1% above the request, so the supercycle framing rests largely on multi-year reconciliation money rather than recurring topline growth. Second, and more consequential, **the backlog is growing fastest where margins are lowest.** SMS backlog rose 37.8% to $22,431M at a mid-10% margin guide, MSL 9.0% to $10,531M at low-12%, while CSD — 54% of segment profit — fell 4.3% to $9,031M with forward coverage of only 1.13x versus 1.92x (SMS) and 2.57x (MSL). Radios are structurally short-cycle and book-and-ship, and management raised CSD's margin guide, so the backlog decline is not by itself a demand signal. But consolidated margin expansion from here requires CSD to hold 25–27% on low-single-digit growth while the two lower-margin segments compound at 7–16%. That is an arithmetic headwind, and Golden Dome intensifies it.

Consensus reflects the tension. Current-year estimates rose 1.8% over 30 days and 1.9% over 90 days (consistent with the guidance raise), but next-year estimates fell 1.7% and 1.5% over the same windows to $13.42, and the current-quarter estimate is down 2.9%. Analyst sentiment is degrading at the recommendation level (strong buys 3 → 1 over two months; buys 13 → 11). The estimate-revision signal is mixed, nowhere near the magnitude that would argue for pressing a position.

## Competitive Landscape

L3Harris positions itself between the top-five primes and venture-backed entrants. The 10-K names BAE, Boeing, General Dynamics, Lockheed, Northrop, RTX and Thales as traditional competitors, and Anduril, Ursa Major and Silvus as non-traditional ones. Anduril also appears as a named strategic partner alongside Palantir, Shield Capital and Amazon Kuiper — an unresolved tension in radios and autonomy on one side and solid rocket motors on the other.

**Strengths.** CSD is a defensible franchise: 26% operating margins, pricing power, and international radio deliveries that scale with NATO spending rather than with U.S. platform cycles. MSL is one of very few Western alternatives to the incumbent solid-rocket-motor duopoly, and the Department of War's willingness to inject $1B of capacity capital is third-party validation of scarcity value. SMS margin improvement (9.0% core, +130bp) is real if from a weak base, with classified space and SDA work carrying the load.

**Weaknesses.** SMS must reach roughly 10.9% segment margin in H2 to hit the mid-10% guide against 10.1% in H1 (~9.7% excluding the $23M investment gain), in a market where the SDA increasingly favors commercial buses and Anduril is contesting integration work. MSL's 12.3% margin is thin for a business being prepared for a growth-multiple listing; the IPO case depends on margin expansion as DoW-funded capacity comes online, which is planned rather than demonstrated. And procurement reform — Other Transaction Authorities, portfolio acquisition executives — is acknowledged in the company's own risk factors as lowering barriers to entry, eroding a contracting-fluency advantage precisely where Ursa Major (motors) and Silvus (mesh radios) compete.

One framing discipline: the growth of international revenue to $4.8B/22% is not diversification away from Washington. U.S. Government revenue moved from 76% to 75%, Foreign Military Sales transact through the U.S. Government, and the DoW preferred has *increased* government entanglement by adding a shareholder relationship to a customer relationship.

Competitive position scores 6.0 — improving in CSD margins and MSL scarcity, contested in SMS, with the profit-concentrated segment showing a declining order book.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The Department of War preferred is expensive capital with a contingent cash claim.** Terms: 7% cumulative dividends compounded quarterly; automatic conversion into AXYV common on a qualified IPO by December 31, 2027 at a 20% discount; warrants over 3% of AXYV fully diluted at 100%/110%/120% strikes. If no qualified IPO occurs, redemption is at fair value in April 2031 at the greater of liquidation preference or FMV, with a 110% premium applied to the liquidation preference if contractual milestones are missed by end-2027. Disclosed highest current redemption value is $1,422M, which reconciles almost exactly to $1,000M compounded at 7% quarterly for five years ($1,415M), meaning the 110% premium is *not* embedded and a milestone shortfall could push the claim toward ~$1.56B. Instruments were issued at $1,386M of initial fair value against $1,000M of cash, with the $386M excess capitalized as a program investment intangible amortized as a *reduction to revenue* over 20 years. The bifurcated derivatives ($130M conversion feature, $186M warrants) mark to market through non-operating income, so an increase in AXYV's value would create a reported EPS charge. No fair-value change was recognized in Q2.

**2. IPO timing.** The postponement to mid-2027 leaves roughly six months of buffer against the December 31, 2027 conversion deadline. Slippage past that date converts a subsidiary-equity outcome into a 2031 cash obligation of $1.4–1.6B.

**3. Fixed-price concentration with a visible pattern.** 75% of revenue is fixed-price. FY2025 carried a $38M negative EAC on a classified Maritime program and a $25M Canadian Maritime Helicopter charge; Q1 FY2026 a $31M unfavorable EAC on a legacy naval sensor program; Q2 FY2026 $30M of new unfavorable adjustments inside a net-favorable SMS figure. Individually small, recurring in the same product areas.

**4. H2 cash conversion.** $2,416M of FCF required in 26 weeks, +12.9% per week versus last year's H2, against a year-to-date receivables build of $675M. This is the single largest near-term execution risk to guidance.

**5. Non-durable earnings supports.** ~$0.90–1.00 of the FY2026 EPS guide from a sub-15% tax rate; a recurring pension tailwind that has shrunk after $1.4B of annuitization (disclosed sensitivity: 25bp on the expected return assumption moves benefit income ~$17M); investment gains that were $77M year-to-date versus $4M.

**6. Goodwill.** $19,996M of goodwill plus $6,540M of intangibles equal 62% of assets. The October 2025 test cleared all reporting units by more than 30% (improved from 25%), but accumulated impairments already total $888M across the three segments, and FY2025 included an $85M impairment on the Space Technology disposal group.

**7. Other.** Energetic-materials ignition risk inherited from Aerojet; environmental liabilities of $660M gross against $485M of probable recoveries, leaving ~$175M net whose recoverability depends on continued government pricing relationships. The Q2 forward-looking-statement list added a risk absent from the 10-K enumeration: "unilateral contract action by the U.S. Government or unexpected issues related to the DoW's investment in our subsidiary."

Options positioning neither corroborates nor contradicts the fundamental view, and offers no timing edge.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $390 (+34.7%)

H2 delivers ~$2.4B of free cash flow, validating the ~$3.0B guide and the 5.6% FCF yield. CSD holds mid-25% margins on international radio demand while SMS reaches the mid-10% guide, so consolidated segment margin prints "low 16%" and FY2026 GAAP EPS lands at or above $12.00. Golden Dome and munitions awards from reconciliation money convert into orders, keeping book-to-bill above 1.0x and backlog above $42B. The AXYV IPO proceeds in mid-2027 at a valuation consistent with the DoW conversion mechanics (order of $13B), crystallizing a sum-of-the-parts gap on roughly 17.5% of revenue and removing the 2031 redemption overhang. Deleveraging continues toward 2.3–2.5x net debt/EBITDA, and the remaining $1.7B of buyback authorization is deployed at prices below the $338/$312/$309 averages paid earlier this year. At 29x FY2027 consensus $13.42 the shares reach $389, coinciding with the $393 high analyst target.

### Bear Case — $240 (-17.1%)

The receivables build persists and H2 free cash flow comes in at $2.0–2.2B, missing the guide and resetting the FCF-yield anchor. One or more fixed-price maritime or legacy naval sensor programs produces a charge large enough to swing segment margin, extending the pattern already visible in three consecutive quarters. The effective tax rate normalizes toward 21% as the domestic R&D expensing provision reduces credit benefit, removing ~$1.00 of EPS, while non-service pension income continues to shrink — together compressing FY2027 EPS toward $12.00–12.40. CSD backlog keeps eroding while mix shifts toward mid-10% and low-12% segments, capping consolidated margin. The AXYV listing slips past December 31, 2027, converting the preferred into a $1.4–1.6B contingent cash claim and eliminating the sum-of-the-parts catalyst. At 19–20x reset FY2027 earnings the shares trade to $240, modestly below the July 30 low of $262.68.

### Score Trajectory

Our overall score has drifted from 6.6 (May) to 6.2 (July) and now to 6.0, with management quality down 1.6 points and growth prospects down 1.1 over that span while valuation improved 0.3. The current quarter partly confirms and partly contradicts that drift. It confirms it on management quality and risk: no insider has bought in the open market through a 30% decline, the only open-market transaction was a small CHRO sale at $310.45, buybacks were executed at averages of $309–338 all above today's price, the proxy's own five-year TSR comparison shows $178.36 against a peer-group $237.57, and the off-cycle August 3 RSU grants to segment presidents at roughly double the CFO's award are most consistent with separation-related retention. It contradicts the drift on operations: the guidance raise, the 1.2x book-to-bill, record backlog and 114bp of core margin expansion are not the signature of a deteriorating business. We have therefore held growth and valuation roughly flat and let the lower management and risk marks carry the score. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the justification for repeating it is that the two dimensions we weight most heavily for winner selection — management quality (5.4) and financial health (6.1) — offer no positive discrimination here, while the identified re-rating catalyst has moved 11 months out.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive 12-month path with identifiable catalysts, but the expected return does not justify new capital at $289.53.

- **Base case target: $310 (+7.1%, +8.8% with the 1.73% dividend).** Math: 23x FY2027 consensus EPS of $13.42 = $309, cross-checked by a 5.6% FCF yield on ~$3.25B of FY2027 free cash flow = $58B equity ÷ 186.2M shares = $311.
- **Bull case target: $390 (+34.7%).** 29x FY2027 $13.42; aligns with the $393 high analyst target.
- **Bear case target: $240 (-17.1%).** ~19.4x tax-normalized FY2027 EPS of ~$12.40.
- **Probability-weighted return (55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear): approximately +6.6% price, +8.3% total return.** Reward-to-risk on base versus bear is 0.4:1; on bull versus bear, 2.0:1. Neither clears the bar for adding.
- **Timeframe: 12 months, anchored on the Q3 FY2026 report (late October 2026), the Q4/FY2026 report (late January 2027), and AXYV S-1 progress through H1 2027.**

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
1. Q3 FY2026 free cash flow of $1.1B or better, putting the full-year ~$3.0B guide within reach without a Q4 heroics assumption.
2. CSD backlog stabilizing or growing year over year with segment margin at or above 25.5%, removing the mix-dilution objection to consolidated margin expansion.
3. Price at or below $265 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $13.20 — roughly 20x normalized-tax earnings and a ~6.2% FCF yield.
4. An AXYV S-1 filing that discloses a valuation range consistent with $12B or more, converting the sum-of-the-parts thesis from inference to disclosure.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. FY2026 free cash flow guidance is cut below $2.8B, or Q3 FCF comes in under $900M.
2. A single-program EAC charge of $75M or more, or two consecutive quarters of net-unfavorable EAC.
3. FY2027 consensus EPS falls below $12.75 (a further ~5% cut) on guided margin compression rather than calendar effects.
4. Public indication that the AXYV listing will not occur before December 31, 2027, which reactivates the $1.4–1.6B redemption claim.

**Next reassessment: the Q3 FY2026 report in late October 2026,** which is the first clean test of the H2 cash-conversion requirement and of SMS's ability to close the ~180bp gap to its mid-10% margin guide.

## Conclusion

L3Harris is executing better than its share price implies. Four consecutive beats, a raised GAAP EPS guide, record $42.0B backlog at 1.79x coverage, 1.2x book-to-bill, 114bp of core segment margin expansion, and deleveraging to ~2.5x net debt/EBITDA describe a company whose operations are improving. The 23.7% drawdown from March reflects a revenue guide that looked light largely because of a 26-versus-27-week second half, an IPO postponement that management framed as value protection, and European order sentiment, not visible fundamental deterioration.

What keeps this at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE is the combination of a fair multiple and weak quality discriminators. At 24.3x FY2026 GAAP guidance and 23.4x tax-normalized FY2027 earnings, the shares sit in the upper half of the prime-group range, and roughly 8% of the FY2026 EPS guide rests on an effective tax rate management's own disclosure describes as volatile, alongside a pension tailwind that is structurally shrinking. The backlog is expanding fastest in the two segments earning 10–12% while the 26%-margin segment that generates 54% of profit saw orders decline 4.3%. On management, buybacks were executed 7–17% above the current price, no insider has bought through the decline, and the proxy discloses a 60-percentage-point five-year TSR shortfall against the company's own peer group.
