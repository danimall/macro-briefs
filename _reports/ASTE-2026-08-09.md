## Executive Summary

Astec Industries entered 2026 with a credible recovery story and has spent the year dismantling it. FY2025 was genuinely better: revenue $1,410.4M (+8.1%), gross margin up 140bps to 26.5%, operating income nearly tripling to $65.9M off a depressed FY2024 base. But $84.7M of that revenue came from TerraSource's post-acquisition stub, leaving organic FY2025 growth at roughly +1.6%. The 2026 pattern is worse in the place that matters: 1H2026 revenue rose 21.9% to $804.4M, of which $98.1M was acquired (organic ≈ +7.1%), while gross margin fell 180bps to 25.6%, operating income fell 29.8% to $29.4M, and net income fell 62% to $11.8M. Intangible amortization, acquisition interest and inventory step-up explain part of the gap mechanically; $24.8M of 1H manufacturing inefficiency including freight, duties and tariffs (versus $17.8M for all of FY2025) and quality costs that rose from $27.2M to $33.9M between FY2023 and FY2025 explain the rest, and that part is operational.

On August 5 management cut FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $160-175M from $170-190M, attributing the reduction to asphalt plant deliveries shifting into Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 amid uncertainty over federal highway funding. Adjusted EPS of $0.94 missed the $1.05 non-GAAP consensus, the second consecutive miss after Q1's $0.54 versus $0.85. The stock fell 13.8% on August 5 and now sits 32.6% below the April 30 high of $65.69. The arithmetic behind the remaining guidance is the central problem: 1H2026 guidance-basis adjusted EBITDA was $72.9M and 2H2025 was $72.0M, so the low end of the new guide requires 2H2026 of $87.1M (+21% year over year) and the high end $102.1M (+42%). Management guided the split as roughly one-third Q3 and two-thirds Q4, implying a Q4 of $58-68M against a Q2 that produced $42.6M on record revenue. Consensus is moving the other way: the current-quarter estimate has been cut 30.5% in 30 days, and FY2026 and FY2027 estimates are down 7.5% and 7.4%.

Two dated events land inside eight weeks. IIJA surface-transportation authorities expire September 30, 2026; the Senate's bipartisan continuing-resolution provision would extend authorities only to December 11 and, per Roads & Bridges reporting, would not continue the roughly $36.8B of annual USDOT advance appropriations. Infrastructure Solutions is 61% of revenue and 93% domestic, selling multi-hundred-thousand-dollar asphalt plants to contractors who need multi-year funding visibility before committing capital. Separately, the annual goodwill measurement date is October 1, applied to a Materials Solutions reporting unit whose goodwill was written off entirely in Q2 2024 on "declines in the Company's publicly quoted share price and increased interest rates, as well as lower than expected operating results," and which was re-loaded with $86.4M in Q3 2025. Every one of those 2024 triggers is present again. Against roughly +7% probability-weighted expected return and 43% realized volatility, that risk profile does not warrant new capital, and existing holders should reduce.

## Company Overview

Astec (incorporated in Tennessee, 1972) designs and manufactures equipment across the road-building value chain and adjacent aggregates, mining and materials-handling markets, employing 4,468 people at 12/31/25 with 117 U.S. and 160 foreign patents.

**Infrastructure Solutions** (FY2025 revenue $857.4M, segment operating adjusted EBITDA margin 15.7% versus 14.5% in FY2024) builds asphalt and concrete plants, pavers, milling machines, thermal systems, burners and controls under the Astec, Roadtec, Carlson, Heatec and Power Flame brands. Domestic revenue was $800.5M of $857.4M, or 93.4%.

**Materials Solutions** (FY2025 revenue $553.0M, margin 10.1% versus 8.0%) builds crushing, screening, conveying and bulk-handling equipment under KPI-JCI, Telsmith, Kolberg-Pioneer, Peterson, Osborn, Telestack and Breaker Technology, plus Pennsylvania Crusher, Gundlach, Jeffrey Rader and Elgin from the July 2025 TerraSource purchase. Foreign revenue was 40.4% of the segment.

Corporate and other expense of $49.2M sits below the segments. The aftermarket is the profit anchor: parts and component sales were $432.7M in FY2025, 30.7% of revenue, up from 29.7%. Management reported parts and service revenue of $135.5M in Q2 2026, +34.8% and 33.2% of sales (from the earnings call summary rather than the 10-Q, so treat the precise figure as unconfirmed).

Two acquisitions closed within six months: TerraSource for $252.6M cash on July 1, 2025 (into Materials Solutions, funded with a new $600M Wells Fargo facility) and CWMF for $67.5M cash on January 1, 2026 (into Infrastructure Solutions, funded partly on the revolver, which went from undrawn at 12/31/25 to $54.0M drawn at 6/30/26).

**Three EBITDA definitions, differing by ~35%.** FY2025 GAAP EBITDA was $104.6M (pretax $53.1M + net interest $15.2M + D&A $36.3M). The proxy's company-selected adjusted EBITDA was $124.0M. The guidance basis — segment operating adjusted EBITDA $189.9M less corporate $49.2M plus a $0.2M PP&E gain — was approximately $141.0M. FY2026 guidance of $160-175M is on the third and most permissive basis, which excludes the strategic transformation program ($19.6M in FY2025) and acquisition/integration costs ($16.9M). The reconciliation checks: Q2 2026's reported $42.6M equals segment $55.0M less corporate $12.4M, and Q2 2025's $33.8M equals $46.5M less $12.7M, producing the +26% growth management cited.

**Acquisitions have diluted consolidated margin so far.** 1H2026 guidance-basis adjusted EBITDA rose only 5.7% ($69.0M to $72.9M) on 21.9% revenue growth, with margin compressing 140bps from 10.46% to 9.06%. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA declined 14% year over year ($30.3M versus $35.2M) despite two acquisitions in the base. TerraSource contributed a $2.7M net loss in its first six months, though that figure is distorted by $7.4M of inventory step-up amortization that does not recur.

**Return on capital is the structural issue.** FY2025 NOPAT of $48.2M (operating income $65.9M at a 26.9% effective rate) against invested capital of $957.5M (debt $347.9M + equity $681.6M − cash $72.0M) gives ROIC of 5.0%. FY2024 was approximately 2.6% on a normalized tax rate; FY2023 approximately 5.3%. Astec has not covered its cost of capital on a GAAP basis in three years. On the adjusted basis and assuming the cut guidance midpoint holds — adjusted EBITDA $167.5M less ~$30M depreciation, taxed at 26.5%, over ~$1,017M of invested capital — ROIC computes to roughly 9.9%, which is approximately at a plausible WACC for a levered small-cap cyclical rather than above it. ROIC is one of two equally weighted PSU metrics, and no shares were earned for the performance period ending in 2025. The compensation scorecard and the GAAP arithmetic agree.

**Cash flow has not funded the dividend.** Cumulative FY2023-FY2025 free cash flow was $16.9M against $35.6M of dividends paid, so the $0.52 annual dividend has been balance-sheet funded for three years. 1H2026 FCF of $37.3M is a genuine improvement but should not be annualized: Astec discloses Q3 as its seasonally weakest quarter, FY2025 saw a $28.4M receivables build against an $11.8M inventory release, and cash taxes jumped from $8.2M to $19.7M. Inventories of $466.0M at 12/31/25 equal roughly 164 days of cost of sales and exceed annual gross profit by a third; working capital turnover is a PSU metric.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26, total debt was $393.4M against $76.8M of cash, so net debt $316.6M. Net leverage of 2.2x reproduces cleanly against LTM guidance-basis adjusted EBITDA of $144.9M (2.19x) versus a 3.50x covenant maximum and a 1.5-2.5x stated target; management guided to ~1.7x by year-end. Interest coverage is roughly 5x-plus versus a 2.50x minimum. Holding net debt flat, a covenant breach would require adjusted EBITDA to fall 37% to about $90.5M. Term loan amortization is modest until the $236.3M 2030 balloon, and the entire facility matures July 1, 2030. Solvency is not the near-term issue; the issue is that 2.2x leverage removes optionality in a downturn while the company earns 5% GAAP ROIC. A recalculated Altman Z of approximately 3.09 sits just above the 2.99 threshold and is carried by working capital and retained earnings, not profitability; the EBIT/assets term contributes only 0.16 of the total.

**Data note:** the provider snapshot lists forward P/E and trailing P/E as identical at 52.45. The trailing figure reconciles to LTM GAAP diluted EPS of approximately $0.85 ($1.68 − $1.34 + $0.51), so 52x is a trailing measure. Forward consensus of $3.33 (FY2026) and $3.90 (FY2027), which are adjusted-basis figures, imply 13.3x and 11.3x. The 52.45 forward field should be disregarded.

## Growth Analysis

**Backlog is the strongest datapoint and requires the most care.** Total backlog at 6/30/26 was $601.1M, +57.9% year over year: Infrastructure Solutions $288.6M (+12.7%) and Materials Solutions $312.5M (+150.6%). The 10-Q attributes part of the MS surge to large data center projects; management also cited electrification and critical-minerals mining. Implied Q2 orders of $460M (+49.1%) were reported on the call and pass an internal consistency check ($460M orders less $408.1M revenue implies a ~$52M backlog build), but were not verified in the 10-Q.

Three qualifications matter. First, the MS figure is not organic — TerraSource was acquired July 1, 2025, so its backlog is fully in the current period and fully absent from the base; the acquired portion at 6/30/26 is not disclosed. Second, Astec's backlog is unshipped customer orders, not a contractually defined remaining performance obligation, and there is no disclosure that it is non-cancellable. Third, IS backlog grew only 12.7%, and IS is the higher-margin segment.

**The mix shift is adverse.** Management flagged Q2 IS operating margin down roughly 130bps on a shift away from asphalt plants toward mobile paving and concrete equipment. Asphalt plants are the highest-margin line and the one where Astec holds genuine domestic share leadership against a small peer set. Mobile paving and concrete put Astec directly against Caterpillar, Wirtgen/Deere, Fayat and Ammann at lower margins. If federal reauthorization slips into 2027, the mix headwind persists even if consolidated revenue holds up through Materials Solutions.

**Estimate momentum is negative across every horizon.** Current quarter $0.53 versus $0.76 thirty days ago (-30.5%); current year $3.33 from $3.60 (-7.5%); next year $3.90 from $4.21 (-7.4%). Only the next-quarter line is up (+5.2%), which is consistent with deliveries being pushed rather than lost. Set against two consecutive adjusted misses (-36.7% in Q1, -11.6% in Q2) after two beats, this is deteriorating visibility rather than conservatism.

**The mechanical earnings tailwind is real but slow.** Expensed transformation costs fell from $33.5M (FY2024) to $19.7M (FY2025) to $8.4M (1H2026), with roughly $151M of a $180-200M program incurred through 2025, implying perhaps $7-14M annually across 2027-2029. The Note 8 intangible amortization schedule declines from $23.8M (2026) to $12.9M (2030), though CWMF adds roughly $4M annually not in that table. Combined, the GAAP-to-adjusted gap should narrow by perhaps $5-12M pretax per year without operational improvement. An $11.0M reversal of unrecognized tax benefits within twelve months (roughly $0.47/share) would be a further non-operating benefit. These are the most credible paths to GAAP EPS growth, and none of them requires demand to accelerate.

Which multiple you anchor on determines the conclusion, and both readings are partially valid. The specific reason I do not score valuation above neutral is that the cheap-looking multiple depends on guidance that was cut three days before the price basis and that still requires the largest quarter in company history. Applying a modest haircut to $155M for FY2026 and $165M for FY2027 puts EV/EBITDA at 8.6x and 8.1x — reasonable, not asymmetric, for a sub-scale manufacturer with a binary funding overhang and a live goodwill test.

Consensus targets are useful as floors when estimates are rising and the multiple is compressed relative to growth; here estimates are falling across every horizon, which makes these numbers stale rather than confirmatory. ## Competitive Landscape

Astec's Item 1 disclosure describes markets that are highly competitive and, in most lines, contested by companies substantially larger and better capitalized. That is an accurate self-assessment. In crushing and screening, Astec's $553M Materials Solutions segment competes with divisions of Metso, Sandvik, Terex and Weir. In paving, it competes with Caterpillar and Wirtgen/Deere. R&D of $26.9M is 1.9% of sales, thin for a machinery company.

The defensible positions are narrower and real: leadership in U.S. asphalt plants, a niche small enough that the diversified giants do not prioritize it; an installed base generating roughly 31% of revenue in aftermarket parts at higher margin and lower cyclicality than machines; and a dealer network, recently extended with two U.K. dealer additions. The TerraSource logic — buying an aftermarket-heavy processing business — is strategically coherent. Trajectory is mixed: aftermarket mix is improving (30.7% of FY2025 revenue, reportedly 33.2% in Q2 2026, growing 34.8%), while gross margin, segment margin in the higher-margin segment, and the product mix within IS are all moving the wrong way.

On whether the aggregates strength is share gain or sector participation, the available cross-check is partial. Terex's Q2 2026 Materials Processing commentary indicated rising U.S. crushing and recycling demand, which is directionally consistent with a sector-wide upcycle rather than Astec-specific share capture. The working interpretation is participation plus acquired scale, not share gain.

## Risk Assessment

**1. IIJA reauthorization (highest conviction, dated September 30, 2026).** Surface transportation authorities expire at the end of federal FY2026. The House T&I Committee has advanced its portion of the BUILD America 250 Act, but other House committees and the entire Senate have not produced text. Astec's own explanation for the guidance cut is asphalt plant deliveries slipping on funding-timing uncertainty. For a segment that is 61% of revenue and 93% domestic, this is the dominant swing factor for 2027 orders.

**2. Goodwill impairment, with a demonstrated precedent (October 1 measurement date).** Materials Solutions goodwill was fully written off in Q2 2024 ($20.2M) on share-price decline, higher rates and weaker operating results; accumulated MS impairment is $33.2M. Astec then added $86.4M of new goodwill to that same reporting unit in Q3 2025. Since then the share price is down roughly 29% from the February 2026 Form 4 reference of $62.34, guidance has been cut 7%, and 1H adjusted EBITDA margin compressed 140bps. A quantitative test faces a lower market-cap input and a lower earnings trajectory than the July 2025 purchase-price allocation embedded. The counter-argument is that MS fundamentals are improving — backlog up sharply, margin up from 8.0% to 10.1% — and that reporting-unit performance rather than consolidated performance drives the test. Any charge would be non-cash and would not affect the EBITDA-based covenant, but would hit $681.7M of book equity and reported EPS.

**3. Back-loaded guidance after two misses.** The low end of the new guide requires 2H adjusted EBITDA of $87.1M against $72.0M in 2H2025 and $72.9M in 1H2026, with roughly two-thirds in Q4 — implying $58-68M against a record Q2 of $42.6M. I cannot verify the Q4 2025 base (the quarterly 2H split requires the Q3 2025 10-Q), so the half-year comparison is the verified frame.

**4. Cost execution.** $24.8M of 1H manufacturing inefficiency including freight, duties and tariffs versus $17.8M for all of FY2025, plus $14.6M of inflation. Quality costs rose from $27.2M to $33.9M between FY2023 and FY2025 while revenue rose 5%, and management has not addressed this directly. Steel is the largest inventory input, with explicit disclosure that a price decline could force net-realizable-value writedowns against a $466M inventory book.

**5. Control and disclosure items.** Deloitte's opinion notes management excluded TerraSource — 23.8% of consolidated assets — from its ICFR assessment, permitted for a first-year acquisition but material in scope. The sole Critical Audit Matter is revenue timing on stored-equipment (bill-and-hold) arrangements, whose dollar amount is not quantified in the 10-K. Given that the guidance miss was attributed to "delivery shifts," the interaction between stored-equipment recognition and quarter-end timing warrants monitoring. The auditor tested it and issued an unqualified opinion; this is a disclosure gap, not evidence of misstatement.

**6. International losses and tax tail.** FY2025 pretax income was U.S. +$63.7M against foreign −$10.6M (FY2024: +$25.9M / −$12.0M). The deferred tax valuation allowance rose from $12.4M to $17.1M on Brazil and U.K. NOLs. U.S. examinations are open for 2013, 2014, 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2019 with $14.1M of gross unrecognized benefits.

**7. Priced-in offsets.** Litigation is substantially cleared: the 2019 shareholder class action settled for $13.7M funded entirely by insurance; Telsmith/37 Building Products settled at $6.3M against an $8.2M accrual for a $1.9M net benefit; GEFCO/VenVer settled at $8.4M. Short interest is 3.0% of float at 2.8 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short mechanic in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 70.8% against 43.5% one-year realized is unusually rich for a name that has already reported, and the term structure is in backwardation (70.8% at 11 days, 38.8% at 39 days, 48.3% at 130 days). The August 21 tenor prices a ±11.4% move. Small-cap options chains of this size (28 contracts across three expirations) can produce noisy ATM readings, so I would not treat the level as precise; the shape is the informative part, and it is consistent with the market pricing unresolved near-term stress rather than post-earnings normalization. The 39-day tenor's 34.7% put/call skew reading sits over the September 30 funding expiry, though the data source does not define the sign convention, so I decline to assign it a direction. This is consistent with the fundamental read: the market is paying up for short-dated protection into a window that contains the funding expiry and the goodwill measurement date. For holders, the richness of near-term vol makes covered-call overwriting or collar structures a more efficient de-risking route than outright sales for those with tax constraints.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($64, +44.6%)

Backlog and implied Q2 orders are demand signals that precede revenue, and the Materials Solutions drivers — data center construction, electrification, critical-minerals mining — are private-capital funded and independent of federal appropriations. Congress extends surface transportation authorities and restores appropriations in some form, asphalt plant deliveries deferred into Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 arrive rather than cancel, and the mix headwind reverses into the highest-margin line where Astec leads. The $24.8M of 1H tariff/freight/inefficiency drag and $3.6M of triennial ConExpo cost do not repeat at that scale, transformation expense falls from a ~$17M 2026 run rate toward $7-14M, and intangible amortization declines on schedule — together closing $5-12M of pretax GAAP-to-adjusted gap annually with no operational improvement. Aftermarket at 33% of sales growing 35% raises structural margin and reduces cyclicality. Deleveraging to ~1.7x restores optionality. On FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $185M at 9.5x EV/EBITDA, equity is worth roughly $64.

### Bear Case ($33, −25.4%)

IIJA authorities lapse or extend without advance appropriations, contractors defer plant purchases, and IS backlog converts slowly while new orders stall. FY2026 guidance is cut a second time — the required Q4 of $58-68M was not achievable from a $42.6M record base — and FY2027 adjusted EBITDA settles near $135M. The October 1 goodwill test produces a charge against the $86.4M re-loaded into Materials Solutions, hitting book equity and reported EPS and re-anchoring the market on GAAP metrics where the company earns 5% ROIC and trades at 12x EV/EBITDA. Manufacturing inefficiency and quality costs prove structural rather than transitory, gross margin holds near 25.5%, and the acquisitions remain margin-dilutive. At 7.5-8.0x on $135M with net debt near $310M, equity is worth $30-33.

### What the market is discounting, and whether it is wrong

A 32.6% drawdown from the April high demands a specific answer. The market is discounting three things: a 7% guidance cut, a 7-8% reduction in FY2026-FY2027 consensus, and a materially wider distribution of 2027 outcomes conditional on federal funding. On the first two, the market is right — those are confirmed. On the third, the drawdown looks proportionate rather than excessive: the multiple compressed from roughly 9.5-10x forward adjusted EBITDA in April to 8.0x now, which is a reasonable adjustment for a binary that resolves in seven weeks with 61% of revenue attached to it. I do not find a mispricing large enough to underwrite. The identifiable value is in the mechanical GAAP convergence and a possible $11.0M tax reversal, and both are second-order relative to the funding question.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Probability-weighted expected return of approximately +7% (45% base +8.5%, 25% bull +44.6%, 30% bear −25.4%) is below the required return for a levered small-cap cyclical with 43% realized volatility and a peer-median beta of 1.61, and the near-term asymmetry is negative with two dated adverse catalysts inside eight weeks.

- **Base case target: $48 (+8.5%).** 12.3x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.90; cross-checked as 8.5x FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $165M less net debt of ~$300M over 23.1M diluted shares.
- **Bull case target: $64 (+44.6%).** 9.5x FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $185M.
- **Bear case target: $33 (−25.4%).** 8.0x FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $135M.
- **Timeframe:** 6-9 months, tied to September 30 (IIJA expiry), October 1 (goodwill measurement date), the late-October Q3 print (which must evidence the Q4 ramp), and February 2027 (FY2027 initial guidance).

**De-risking plan.** Trim now rather than around a catalyst; both near-term catalysts skew negative and near-term implied volatility of 70.8% makes waiting expensive if it resolves against you. Reduce to a residual position of no more than one-third of a full weight, sized to participate if reauthorization passes cleanly. Holders with tax constraints can use the rich August-September vol to overwrite or collar instead of selling outright.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD.** (i) IIJA reauthorization enacted with advance appropriations continued at or near the ~$36.8B annual level; (ii) Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $30M or better *with* FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at $160-175M and asphalt plant orders confirmed as deferred rather than cancelled; (iii) the October 1 goodwill test passing without impairment.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE/BUY.** Consolidated gross margin recovering above 27% with adjusted EBITDA margin above 10.5%, evidencing that the $24.8M of manufacturing inefficiency and rising quality costs were transitory; plus net leverage at or below 1.7x and trailing twelve-month free cash flow covering the $11.9M dividend at least twice. A price in the low-to-mid $30s against unchanged FY2027 estimates would independently create the margin of safety that is absent at $44.25.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (i) A second FY2026 guidance cut, or Q3 adjusted EBITDA below $27M; (ii) a Materials Solutions goodwill impairment accompanied by any downward revision to segment backlog; (iii) net leverage rising toward 2.8x on working-capital build rather than falling toward 1.7x; (iv) IIJA lapsing with no reauthorization path into calendar 2027 and IS backlog declining sequentially.

**Reassessment timeline.** Formal review at the Q3 2026 release in late October, with an interim check on the September 30 funding outcome.

## Conclusion

Astec has spent 2026 buying revenue and losing margin. Sales grew 21.9% in the first half while operating income fell 29.8% and net income fell 62%, with roughly two-thirds of the revenue increase acquired. Management cut FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance 7% at the midpoint on August 5 and now needs a Q4 of $58-68M — larger than any quarter the company has produced — to reach even the low end, after missing adjusted consensus twice in a row and with the current-quarter estimate down 30.5% in thirty days.

The offsetting evidence is genuine. Backlog is up 57.9%, Materials Solutions is participating in a real aggregates upcycle driven by data centers and electrification, aftermarket revenue is 33% of sales and growing 35%, leverage at 2.2x sits inside a 3.50x covenant with no maturity until 2030, litigation is cleared, and roughly $5-12M of annual pretax GAAP-to-adjusted convergence arrives mechanically as the transformation program winds down. At 8.0x EV to guidance-basis adjusted EBITDA and 11-13x forward adjusted EPS, the stock is not demanding.

It is not cheap enough, though, to compensate for what sits in the next eight weeks.
