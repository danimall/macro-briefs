## Executive Summary

HCA is the largest investor-owned acute-care operator in the United States — 190 hospitals, 121 freestanding ambulatory surgery centers, 31 endoscopy centers, ~320,000 employees, 19 states plus England — and it is absorbing the largest payer-mix shock of the post-COVID period. The expiration of enhanced ACA premium tax credits at the end of 2025 has converted a substantial share of former exchange enrollees into uninsured patients arriving through emergency departments, where EMTALA obligates treatment. Same-facility uninsured admissions rose 15.5% in Q1 2026 and 23.4% in Q2 2026, against a five-quarter series that was flat to negative through Q3 2025. Uncompensated care cost was $2,697M in H1 2026 versus $2,171M, up 24.2%. On July 14, 2026 — ten days ahead of its scheduled release — HCA filed an off-cycle 8-K lowering 2026 net income guidance to $6.3–6.7B from $6.5–7.0B and widening its stated exchange headwind to $1.0–1.2B from $600–900M.

The Q2 print looked better than the business performed. Reported diluted EPS of $7.62 was up 11.6% and beat consensus by $0.08, but the quarter included $1,372M of incremental Florida state-directed-payment (SDP) revenue and $829M of related expense covering services from October 2024 through June 2026, of which roughly $980M of revenue and $557M of expense related to pre-2026 periods. Removing that $423M net catch-up leaves pretax income of ~$2,071M against $2,415M a year earlier, a decline of 14.3%. Management's own framing — approximately $400M of incremental net Medicaid supplemental benefit — produces essentially the same answer. Segment data corroborate independently: Atlantic Group (which contains Florida) EBITDA rose 35.1% while National plus American Group fell 10.4% combined, with American Group (Texas, Louisiana, Colorado) down 13.2%. Two-thirds of the hospital base contracted double digits.

At $407.04 the stock is 26.9% below its March 12 high of $556.52 and 15.0% above its July 14 low of $353.99. It trades at 14.4x FY2025 GAAP EPS of $28.33, 13.8x the $29.60 midpoint of lowered 2026 guidance, and 12.7x next-year consensus of $32.16 — where consensus itself has fallen 3.0% over 90 days and 2.8% over 30 days. Free cash flow of $7,692M in FY2025 against ~$88.5B of market value is an 8.7% yield and is the strongest support for the shares. Against that: guided 2026 net income declines 4.2% year over year, EPS growth of +4.5% comes entirely from retiring 8.3% of the share count, that repurchase is now partly debt-funded at an average H1 price of ~$460 versus $407 today, gross leverage has moved from 2.99x to 3.14x, and roughly 16% of Adjusted EBITDA derives from Medicaid supplemental programs subject to a legislated 10-percentage-point annual step-down beginning January 1, 2028. We hold. Fair value sits close to the current price, and the evidence does not yet support either a constructive or an actively negative call.

## Company Overview

HCA's strategy is market density rather than national breadth: build comprehensive local networks and support them with enterprise scale. The concentration is deliberate and disclosed as a principal risk — 102 of 190 hospitals sit in Florida and Texas, representing 51% of FY2025 consolidated revenue. Reporting runs through three geographic segments plus corporate (which houses seven English hospitals).

Q2 2026 segment detail: National (53 hospitals) $5,251M revenue and $1,184M adjusted segment EBITDA; Atlantic (64 hospitals) $7,573M and $1,827M; American (66 hospitals) $6,582M and $1,369M; corporate and other $353M.

The moat HCA claims is physician affiliation and local network completeness rather than technology or brand. The 10-K's competition discussion is candid about limits: not-for-profit and government competitors enjoy tax exemptions and endowment support unavailable to HCA, payer consolidation increases bargaining power over fee structures, and HCA faces competition for high-margin surgical share "from both our own and unaffiliated freestanding ASCs" — an explicit acknowledgment of internal cannibalization.

Florida and Texas both declined Medicaid expansion. That is usually cited as pure risk, and on the uninsured channel it is: non-expansion states have the largest exchange-dependent low-income populations, which is why HCA's uninsured admissions spiked hardest. But several 2025 Federal Budget Act provisions bite harder in expansion states — the provider-tax safe-harbor step-down from 6% to 3.5% between FY2028 and FY2032, work requirements, six-month redeterminations — and the SDP rate cap is 110% of Medicare in non-expansion states versus 100% in expansion states. The geographic concentration cuts both ways.

## Financial Analysis

**Long-run compounding, decomposed.** FY2022–FY2025 revenue grew from $60,233M to $75,600M, a 7.9% CAGR. Net income attributable to HCA grew from $5,643M to $6,784M, a 6.3% CAGR. Diluted shares fell from 294.666M to 239.495M, a 6.7% annual reduction. Diluted EPS compounded at 13.9%. Roughly half of three-year EPS growth came from share retirement. That is a long-standing, deliberate policy dating to HCA's LBO history, not a new gimmick, but it is the central fact for framing 2026, because the earnings side of the equation has now turned negative while the share-count side continues.

**FY2025 cash generation.** Operating cash flow $12,636M, capex $4,944M, free cash flow $7,692M (10.2% of revenue). Repurchases of 26.739M shares for ~$10.1B at an average of ~$378. Dividends $2.88/share, raised to $0.78/quarter in January 2026 ($3.12 annualized, 0.76% yield). Gross debt $46,492M against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $15,566M — 2.99x. Stockholders' deficit attributable to HCA of −$6,027M is the accumulated arithmetic of buying back more equity than cumulative retained earnings.

**H1 and Q2 2026.** Q2 revenue $20,230M (+8.7%), pretax income $2,494M (+3.2%), net income attributable to HCA $1,699M (+2.8%), diluted EPS $7.62 (+11.6%) on 222.828M diluted shares versus 241.911M. Adjusting Q2 revenue for the ~$980M prior-period SDP catch-up gives $19,250M, +3.5% rather than +8.7%. Adjusting pretax for the $423M net catch-up gives −14.3%.

**Payer mix is the mechanism.** Managed care and other insurers — HCA's highest-rate class — fell from 49.5% of revenue in FY2024 to 48.9% in FY2025 to 46.0% in H1 2026, and declined in absolute dollars: −1.2% in Q2 ($9,013M vs $9,124M) and −0.4% in H1. Medicaid fee-for-service rose from 6.6% to 7.8% to 10.8% over the same span. This is substitution of the commercial dollar with a lower-rate, administratively determined revenue stream that carries associated provider-tax expense and a legislated 2028 decay path. Describing it as diversification inverts the risk.

**Service mix is deteriorating alongside payer mix.** Outpatient surgeries fell from 1,044,415 (FY2023) to 1,022,812 (FY2025) and to 487,008 in H1 2026 from 504,985, −3.6%; Q2 alone was −4.4% (−3.4% same-facility). Inpatient surgeries were −1.3% in H1 and −2.3% in Q2. ER visits rose 1.5% in H1 and 3.5% in Q2. Freestanding ASC count fell from 125 at March 2025 to 118 at June 2026. Revenue growth is being carried by rate — revenue per equivalent admission +6.0% in Q2, +6.4% same-facility — and by government supplemental payments, not by high-acuity volume.

**Balance sheet and funding.** Total debt rose from $46,492M to $49,718M in six months. Commercial paper nearly doubled to $3,890M (38-day average life, 4.3%), and $1,010M was drawn on the senior unsecured facility. Cash $1,013M. Stockholders' deficit widened to −$6,642M. Days revenue in A/R rose from 51 to 55 as receivables built to $12,281M. Reconstructing HCA's own Adjusted EBITDA definition gives LTM EBITDA of ~$15,813M to June 30, so gross leverage is 3.14x (3.08x net), and ~3.23x if the SDP catch-up is stripped. Interest coverage of 6.6x in H1 remains comfortable. Credit facility availability was $3.086B at June 30 and $3.816B at July 24. Excluding commercial paper, working capital would be +$3.768B rather than −$122M, so the negative working capital is a funding-choice artifact.

**Capital returns are now debt-funded.** H1 free cash flow was $1,999M ($4,349M OCF less $2,350M capex) against $3,635M of buybacks and $354M of dividends. The ~$2.0B gap coincides with a $3,226M increase in the debt balance and ~$3,065M of net cash raised from debt on the cash flow statement. H1 repurchases averaged ~$460/share gross (disclosed average $447.53), with June executions at $379.12; those 7.909M shares are worth ~$3,227M today against $3,635M deployed.

Two mitigating points deserve weight. First, the 25.8% decline in H1 operating cash flow ($4,349M vs $5,861M) is substantially timing: a $1,417M A/R build tied to SDP receivables versus a $320M source a year earlier, and $725M of tax payments against $146M, the prior-year figure depressed by an IRS deferral for Tennessee taxpayers into Q4 2025. If the SDP receivable collects, cash flow recovers and leverage self-corrects. H1 free cash flow should not be annualized. Second, an Altman Z screen of 1.14 circulating on this name is an artifact — it carries EBIT/assets at zero when FY2025 EBIT was roughly $12.1B on $60.7B of assets, and it uses book equity that is negative by design. Recomputed with market equity, Z ≈ 2.6, a grey-zone reading consistent with 6.6x coverage and continuous investment-grade market access, not distress.

**Guidance, revised July 14, 2026** (secondary-sourced from reporting on the 8-K; not read directly): revenue $77.0–79.5B unchanged; net income $6.3–6.7B from $6.5–7.0B; Adjusted EBITDA $15.4–16.1B from $15.6–16.5B; diluted EPS $28.70–30.50 from $29.10–31.50. Against FY2025 actuals, midpoints imply revenue +3.5%, Adjusted EBITDA +1.2%, **net income −4.2%**, and EPS +4.5% — the last requiring an implied share count of ~219.6M, down 8.3%.

One detail in the revision resists explanation. The two named drivers moved net favorable at midpoints: Medicaid supplemental improved roughly $550M (from −$50/−$250M to +$300/+$500M versus prior year) while the exchange headwind worsened roughly $350M. Yet Adjusted EBITDA guidance fell $300M. That leaves roughly $500M of deterioration unattributed, and the most plausible candidate given the volume statistics is surgical mix. The caveats are real: midpoint arithmetic is crude when a range narrows asymmetrically (width fell from $900M to $700M), the two factor disclosures may not share a definitional basis, and management may simply have added conservatism ahead of a second half with accelerating uninsured trends. Treat the $500M as a directional signal of unattributed weakness rather than a computed number.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has three layers, and only one is favorable.

*Volume and acuity:* negative. Surgical cases — the highest-margin service line — have declined in absolute terms for two consecutive fiscal years and again in H1 2026, while low-acuity ER volume grows and the uninsured share of admissions rose from 7% to 8% of total in Q2. The regulatory direction reinforces this: the Medicare inpatient-only list is being phased out over three years beginning CY2026 with the ASC-payable list simultaneously expanded, site-neutral payment extends to drug administration in excepted off-campus departments from CY2026, the CY2026 Physician Fee Schedule carries a −2.5% efficiency adjustment aimed explicitly at surgical, imaging and orthopedic codes, the ASC update mechanism reverts to CPI in CY2027 absent CMS extension, and the WISeR model pilots AI-enforced prior authorization in six states including Texas with vendors paid a share of savings. HCA's stated response is outpatient build-out, but its freestanding ASC count has fallen seven centers in five quarters.

*Rate and mix:* mixed. Revenue per equivalent admission of +6.0% demonstrates pricing power intact, though part of that reflects SDP revenue and case mix rather than pure commercial rate. The commercial dollar base stopped growing, which is the more consequential fact.

*Share count:* the operative growth driver. $7.210B remained on the January 2026 $10B authorization at June 30. Continuing to retire ~8% of shares annually converts flat-to-declining net income into mid-single-digit EPS growth, but the funding is now incremental debt rather than surplus free cash flow.

Consensus is moving against the shares in every window: current quarter $6.78 (−6.8% over 30 days), next quarter $7.98 (−4.5%), current year $29.56 (−2.1%), next year $32.16 (−2.8% over 30 days, −3.0% over 90). The beat/miss record — three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the Q2 beat a thin 1.0% and Q1 a 0.8% miss — no longer supports high confidence in consensus. This is the inverse of an estimate-revision buy signal.

The thesis-critical unknown is whether the $1.0–1.2B exchange headwind annualizes into 2027 or is a one-time reset absorbed within 2026. No earnings-call transcript was obtainable, so management's framing is unverified. The 10-Q offers one technical nuance in the bulls' favor: the Q2 uninsured spike "includes attrition related to Exchange volumes from the first quarter that occurred during the second quarter," meaning part of the step-up is lagged catch-up and the Q2 rate of change may overstate the steady state.

## Valuation Assessment

On the frozen $407.04 basis: 14.4x FY2025 GAAP EPS of $28.33; 13.8x the $29.60 midpoint of lowered 2026 guidance; 13.8x current-year consensus $29.56; 12.7x next-year consensus $32.16. Dividend yield 0.76%. FY2025 free cash flow yield 8.7%; on guided 2026 capex of $5.0–5.5B and an operating cash flow recovery toward $11–12B, forward FCF yield is roughly 6.8–7.6%.

The peer comparison does not support a cheapness argument in the way the headline median suggests. Peer median P/E of 17.6 is pulled up by post-acute and skilled-nursing operators (EHC 17.6x, ENSG 24.0x, NHC 24.9x) whose businesses differ materially. HCA's closest comparables are acute-care operators: Tenet at 9.0x and Universal Health Services at 6.1x. HCA trades at a 60–135% premium to those two. Some premium is warranted — greater scale, superior density, better balance-sheet access, five-year TSR of 296 indexed versus 148 for the peer group — but the direction of the comparison is the opposite of a discount.

The valuation support that is real is absolute rather than relative: a business generating roughly $7B of annual free cash flow at ~$88.5B of market value, with pricing power demonstrated at +6% per equivalent admission, is not expensive. The problem is that the denominator of any multiple is under active revision. Guidance was cut once in July; estimates are falling in every window; and roughly 16% of Adjusted EBITDA sits in a legislated decay path from January 2028 that no consensus estimate can yet quantify. A 13–14x multiple on 2026 earnings is defensible if 2026 is the trough. It is not defensible if 2027 absorbs a full-year exchange headwind and 2028 begins a Medicaid step-down.

Anchoring fair value: 13x next-year consensus $32.16 is $418; 12x is $386; 14x is $450. If revisions continue at the recent 30-day pace, next-year consensus drifts toward $31, and 13x gives $403. Fair value therefore brackets the current price. Analyst consensus mean is $453.38 (median $450, low $380, high $579), implying ~11% upside, but consensus estimates are falling and the recommendation distribution has softened marginally (one strong buy from two three months ago, one sell maintained). On a name with negative revision momentum and a premium to its closest comps, analyst targets are not independent confirmation.

Valuation scores 5.3. The cheapness is genuine on cash flow but has an identifiable structural cause, which is the defining characteristic of a value trap rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The payer-mix shock is a sector event, not an HCA execution failure. HCA, Tenet, Community Health Systems and Universal Health Services all revised 2026 outlooks after Q2 for payer-mix shifts (peer specifics unverified). That removes share loss as the primary explanation and points to federal policy — EPTC expiration plus the 2025 Federal Budget Act — as the driver.

Historically, sector-wide distress has been how HCA gains share: distressed asset acquisition, physician recruitment from weakened competitors, and negotiating leverage in markets where alternatives fail. HCA enters this cycle with 6.6x interest coverage, $3.1–3.8B of revolver availability, and investment-grade market access demonstrated by the April 30, 2026 issuance of $3.0B of notes ($1.0B at 4.700% due 2031, $750M at 5.000% due 2033, $1.25B at 5.300% due 2036) and the May redemption of $2.5B of 2026 maturities. Nonprofits without state-government relationships or capital-market access will fare worse.

Set against that, the direction of travel in the payment system is against a hospital-heavy asset base, and HCA is not currently capturing the outpatient migration at the pace the hospital migration is occurring. Outpatient surgeries are down in absolute terms across two fiscal years and H1 2026, and the ASC portfolio has shrunk. Whether that reflects pruning of subscale centers, physician-owned competition capturing cases, or payer steerage, the outcome for HCA's mix is the same. Add payer consolidation, narrow and tiered networks, price-transparency publication of negotiated rates, and vertical integration between payers and providers, and the trajectory of competitive position is stable-to-eroding on mix even where market share holds. Competitive position scores 5.5 — a strong absolute position with a mildly negative trend.

## Risk Assessment

**Enhanced premium tax credit expiration (live, quantified).** Now sized by management at $1.0–1.2B for 2026. Roughly 88% of HCA's FY2025 uninsured admissions arrived through the emergency room, where EMTALA obligates screening and stabilization regardless of ability to pay. HCA cannot triage this volume away. Uncompensated care cost rose 24.2% in H1, charity care cost 37%.

**Medicaid supplemental dependence and the 2028 step-down (largest structural risk).** Medicaid supplemental and SDP revenue totaled ~$6.2B in 2025, $5.5B in 2024 and $4.4B in 2023 — 8.2% of FY2025 revenue, growing 41% in two years. The FBA caps SDP rates at 100% of Medicare in expansion states and 110% in non-expansion states, and grandfathered arrangements are reduced by 10 percentage points annually beginning with rating periods on or after January 1, 2028. Managed-Medicaid pass-through payments are generally prohibited by July 2027, and Medicaid DSH payments are cut by $8B in federal fiscal 2028. Applying the ~40% net contribution implied by the Florida program's Q2 economics ($1,372M revenue against $829M expense) to $6.2B suggests roughly $2.5B of EBITDA, or ~16% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, sits in a legislated decay path. That extrapolation from one state to a national portfolio is rough, and HCA states it cannot estimate the impact; certain states have applications pending that could *increase* grandfathered payments before the step-down begins. The direction, however, is set in statute, and consensus estimates do not extend to 2028.

**Provider-tax restrictions, actively crystallizing.** New or increased provider taxes are prohibited for state fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 2026, with the safe harbor stepping from 6% to 3.5% between FY2028 and FY2032 in expansion states. CMS issued a final rule in February 2026 and a proposed rule in July 2026. Florida and Texas rely on local rather than statewide provider taxes.

**Leverage, negative equity and funding mix.** $49.7B of debt, −$6.6B stockholders' deficit, current ratio 0.97, and $3,890M of 38-day commercial paper funding a long-lived asset base. Guided 2026 capex of $5.0–5.5B plus ~$8.6B of costs to complete projects already under construction over five years compete with the buyback for the same cash. A further 8-K on July 29, 2026 under Items 2.03/8.01 indicates an additional direct financial obligation created after quarter-end; its contents were not read, so the leverage figures above may understate current debt.

**Concurrent EHR migration and cost program.** Item 1A carries a discrete risk factor pairing the new enterprise EHR platform with "resiliency" initiatives and warning of "complex, staged deployments." An enterprise EHR conversion across 190 hospitals is among the most reliably disruptive projects in the sector, historically associated with temporary declines in throughput, coding accuracy and collections. Running it concurrently with a cost-offset program while absorbing a $1.0–1.2B payer-mix shock raises execution risk and lowers the credibility of near-term offset targets. No adverse impact has been reported and no vendor, timeline or capitalized cost has been disclosed.

**Other:** Florida and Texas at 51% of revenue; ~320,000 employees with unions at 35 hospitals and nurse-ratio mandates that could constrain admissions; regulatory unpredictability post-*Loper Bright*, already demonstrated by the nationwide stay of several provisions of CMS's June 2025 Exchange integrity rule in August 2025; Frist-affiliated holders at ~31% with board nomination rights.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 35.9% sits above one-year realized volatility of 30.3% and in the 72nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (35.9% at 14 days versus 31.8% at six months). The nearest expiry prices a ±5.7% move over 14 days, which is substantial for a hospital operator with no scheduled earnings until late October — consistent with hedging against policy headlines (EPTC legislation, CMS SDP and provider-tax rulemaking) rather than a company-specific event.

Positioning has shifted markedly since the July 19 snapshot: put/call open interest collapsed from 2.60 to 0.65 as the price rose 9.9%, and near-term put/call volume is 0.29 — protection lifted after the guidance reset and near-term flow now call-tilted. Longer-dated skew runs the other way, at −2.1% at three months and −4.4% at six months, the most negative in the chain. The combination reads as near-term complacency alongside retained demand for longer-dated downside — which maps onto the fundamental picture, where 2026 is largely disclosed and the unpriced risks (2027 annualization, 2028 SDP step-down) sit further out. Short interest of 4.1% of float and 3.4 days to cover means the bearish view is uncrowded and there is no squeeze dynamic supporting the price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $470–490 (+15% to +20%)

The 2026 shock is a discrete, policy-driven reset that HCA absorbs while its weaker competitors do worse. Uninsured admission growth decelerates in H2 as the Q1 attrition catch-up embedded in Q2 washes out. Congress restores some form of enhanced premium subsidy under midterm pressure, and coverage partially reverts. The SDP receivable collects, operating cash flow recovers toward the FY2025 pattern ($12.6B OCF, $7.7B FCF), leverage returns below 3.0x, and the buyback returns to being self-funded — retiring 8% of shares annually against a recovering earnings base. States' pending applications lift grandfathered Medicaid payments in 2026–2027 before the 2028 step-down, extending the runway. HCA uses its balance sheet to acquire distressed assets and recruit physicians from weakened nonprofits, and pricing power at +6% per equivalent admission continues. 15x $32 of 2027 EPS gives $480.

### Bear Case — $330–350 (−14% to −19%)

The exchange headwind annualizes into 2027 rather than lapping, because coverage losses are permanent absent legislation. Commercial revenue continues to decline in absolute dollars while uninsured admissions stay elevated, and the surgical mix keeps eroding as the inpatient-only list phase-out, expanded ASC list, site-neutral payments and the −2.5% Physician Fee Schedule adjustment migrate profitable cases out of hospitals — a shift HCA is not capturing, given its ASC count fell from 125 to 118. The unattributed ~$500M in the July guidance bridge proves to be structural mix rather than conservatism. The EHR migration disrupts throughput and collections in one or more markets. Leverage passes 3.5x as debt-funded buybacks continue against declining net income, forcing a repurchase slowdown that removes the only source of EPS growth. Consensus 2027 EPS resets toward $29–30 and the market applies 11–12x — Tenet's and UHS's territory — for $330–355.

### Base Case — $415–425

Fair value is approximately the current price. 13x a next-year figure that settles between $31 and $32.50 after further modest revision gives $403–423. The 8.7% trailing free-cash-flow yield and continued share retirement provide a floor; declining net income, falling estimates, rising leverage and the unquantified 2028 Medicaid cliff cap the multiple. Total return over twelve months is roughly the dividend plus low-single-digit price movement, with wide dispersion around it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the score has fallen from 7.4 in February to 5.4 in July to 4.96 now. The bar for maintaining an unchanged call should rise with repetition, so it is worth being explicit about what changed since the prior review. The stock rose 9.9% (from $371.18 to $407.04) while the Q2 10-Q disclosed that underlying pretax earnings fell ~14% once the Florida SDP catch-up is removed, that two of three segments contracted double digits, that leverage rose to 3.14x, and that $3.6B of largely debt-funded repurchases were executed at an average price above today's. Price up on weaker disclosed earnings quality means risk/reward deteriorated, which is why the score declines further even though the recommendation label does not change. We are not adding capital here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Management frames the $1.0–1.2B exchange headwind as non-recurring in 2027 guidance, or legislation restoring enhanced premium subsidies advances, and next-year consensus revisions turn positive for two consecutive months.
2. Managed care and other insurer revenue returns to year-over-year growth in Q3 or Q4 2026 (H1 2026 was −0.4%; Q2 was −1.2%), alongside stabilization or growth in outpatient surgeries on a same-facility basis.
3. Price toward $340–360 — roughly 11–11.5x next-year consensus and near the July 14 low — with operating cash flow recovering and gross leverage back below 3.0x. That combination would restore the asymmetry the current price does not offer.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Gross leverage exceeds 3.5x LTM Adjusted EBITDA, or the repurchase pace slows materially, removing the EPS bridge. Watch the $7.210B remaining authorization and the quarterly debt balance.
2. A second downward revision to 2026 guidance, or initial 2027 guidance implying another year of declining net income.
3. Evidence of EHR implementation disruption — a discrete drag disclosed in MD&A, or days revenue in A/R rising beyond 58–60 from the current 55 for reasons other than SDP receivables.
4. Q3 disclosure that commercial revenue declined again while uninsured admissions remained above 8% of total.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026 is the decisive checkpoint — it will show whether uninsured admission growth decelerated from the +23.4% Q2 pace once the Q1 attrition catch-up clears, whether the SDP receivable collected and cash flow recovered, and how leverage and repurchase pacing were reconciled. Interim monitoring points: CMS rulemaking on SDP and provider taxes following the July 2026 proposed rule; any congressional action on premium tax credits ahead of the November midterms; and the contents of the unread July 29, 2026 8-K, which may raise the reported debt balance.

## Conclusion

HCA remains the best-operated and best-capitalized company in its industry, and the shock it is absorbing is a federal policy change affecting every acute-care operator rather than a loss of competitive standing. The problem is that the standard bull framing — quality operator, low teens multiple, 8% free-cash-flow yield, 8% annual share retirement — is currently resting on earnings that are declining. Guided 2026 net income falls 4.2%; the reported Q2 beat was produced by a Medicaid true-up covering 21 months of prior service plus a smaller share count; two of three segments contracted double digits; the highest-rate payer class is shrinking in absolute dollars; the highest-margin service line has declined for two years running against a payment system actively migrating those cases out of hospitals; the repurchase that generates the EPS growth is now debt-funded above the current price; and roughly a sixth of EBITDA sits in a program with a statutory step-down beginning in January 2028 that no published estimate reaches.

At $407.04, close to our $415–425 base case, the market is neither ignoring these facts nor over-punishing them. The single question that determines whether this is 13x trough earnings or 13x the first year of a reset — whether the $1.0–1.2B exchange headwind annualizes — is not answerable from the filings and reporting available. Holding existing positions is reasonable given the cash generation and the possibility of legislative reversal. New capital should wait for either evidence that 2027 laps the headwind rather than absorbing it again, or a price in the $340–360 range where the 2028 Medicaid risk is compensated.

### Valuation and Price Discipline

- Current price: $407.04
- Fair value: $418.00
- Margin of safety: +2.7%
- Initiate/add below: $360.00
- Reduce/exit above: $470.00
- Rationale: 13x next-year consensus of $32.16 gives $418; continued revisions toward $31 give ~$403. Fair value brackets the current price, leaving effectively no margin of safety.

**Thesis:** HCA is the best-capitalized acute-care operator absorbing a federal payer-mix shock. An 8.7% trailing FCF yield and ~8% annual share retirement support the floor, but underlying Q2 pretax earnings fell ~14% ex the Florida SDP catch-up, two of three segments contracted double digits, leverage rose to 3.14x with buybacks now partly debt-funded, and ~16% of EBITDA faces a statutory step-down from January 2028. Value is fair, not asymmetric.

**Variant view:** Consensus treats 2026 as a trough at 13x. The under-appreciated point is quality of the multiple's denominator: the commercial dollar is shrinking in absolute terms, surgical volumes have fallen two straight years, and ~$500M of the July guidance cut is unattributed to either named driver.

### Catalysts

- **High — late October 2026:** Q3 2026 print shows whether uninsured admission growth decelerates from +23.4%, whether the SDP receivable collects, and how leverage and buyback pacing reconcile.
- **Medium — next 3-6 months:** Congressional action on enhanced ACA premium tax credits ahead of the November midterms would partially reverse coverage losses and the $1.0-1.2B headwind.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **October 2026 to January 2027:** A second downward 2026 guidance revision, or initial 2027 guidance implying another year of declining net income.
- **next 2-3 quarters:** Gross leverage exceeds 3.5x LTM Adjusted EBITDA or repurchase pace slows materially, removing the sole source of EPS growth.
- **Q3-Q4 2026:** Managed care revenue returns to year-over-year growth with same-facility outpatient surgeries stabilizing and next-year revisions turning positive two months running.
