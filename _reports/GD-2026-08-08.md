## Executive Summary

General Dynamics delivered a clean second quarter and raised full-year guidance for the second time in 2026. Four weeks after the balance-sheet date, the Navy awarded a combined ~$76.6 billion in submarine contract modifications to Electric Boat and HII's Newport News. GD's own release attributes $29.5B (five Columbia-class) plus $42.1B (nine Virginia-class) plus shipyard infrastructure support to Electric Boat. None of that sits in the reported $136.5B backlog, because fiscal Q2 closed July 5. Reported contracted position therefore understates the franchise, and the Q3 10-Q backlog table is the single most informative disclosure ahead.

The operating story has improved at the contract level. FY2025's 14bp of consolidated margin expansion came entirely from G&A leverage; gross margin actually fell 30bp to 15.13%. In H1 2026 that flipped: gross margin rose 48bp to 15.69% with G&A a 22bp headwind, netting 20bp of operating margin expansion sourced from contract performance at the shipyards and from Gulfstream's ultra-large-cabin mix. Balance sheet quality is being understated by common screens: gross debt/equity is 0.28x (not the 2.23x equity multiplier that circulates), net debt/EBITDA is roughly 0.5x, interest coverage is 17x, and management retired the June 2026 notes with cash rather than refinancing, cutting FY2026 net interest guidance from ~$340M to ~$270M. The Sherman Act no-poach class action was dismissed with prejudice on May 18, 2026, removing a treble-damages tail that had been live for thirty months.

What has not improved is entry price. At $392.05 GD trades at 23.3x the $16.84 FY2026 EPS I reconstruct from the 10-Q's own segment guidance, 23.1x the $16.96 consensus, and 21.1x the $18.54 FY2027 consensus — against roughly 9% forward EPS growth, a PEG near 2.5. The stock is 2.0% below its 52-week high of $400.00 and up 25.4% over twelve months. The dividend yields 1.62% and buybacks are explicitly limited to offsetting dilution (5.8M shares of authorization remaining, 2.2% of outstanding), so the equity return depends on earnings growth and multiple stability with no capital-return cushion. My base case of $426 offers 8.7% upside plus 1.6% of yield; the bear case of $325 is 17% down. That skew is approximately fair. HOLD, with a specific reassessment at the Q3 backlog disclosure.

## Company Overview

GD operates four segments through ten business units under a deliberately thin corporate center. FY2025 revenue was $52.55B.

**Marine Systems** (32% of FY2025 revenue; $16.72B) is the structural core. Electric Boat is prime contractor and lead design yard on *all* Navy nuclear-powered submarine programs and owns design and engineering. Its only peer, Newport News, is simultaneously a Virginia-class partner and a Columbia-class subcontractor. Columbia is a 12-boat program with a >$125B program of record and the highest Defense Priorities and Allocations System rating; first delivery is expected in 2028. Bath Iron Works is one of two DDG-51 builders with 11 destroyers in backlog through 2032; NASSCO holds seven T-AO-205 oilers.

**Technologies** (26%; $13.47B) comprises GDIT federal IT modernization and Mission Systems defense electronics — NSA Type-1 encryption, Space Development Agency ground segment for Tranches 1-2, submarine fire-control.

**Aerospace** (25%; $13.11B) is Gulfstream plus Jet Aviation. Gulfstream has just completed a roughly two-decade clean-sheet renewal (G500, G600, G700, G800 certified April 2025), with the G400 and super-midsize G300 still in development.

**Combat Systems** (17%; $9.25B) holds sole-source positions on the Abrams tank and Stryker, European Land Systems, and Ordnance and Tactical Systems (the world's largest Ball Powder propellant producer).

Customer mix in FY2025: U.S. government 68%, U.S. commercial 15%, non-U.S. government 8%, non-U.S. commercial 9%. Of $35.76B U.S. government revenue, the Department of War was $29.79B.

FY2022→FY2025 revenue CAGR was 10.1%; net income CAGR 7.5%. Diluted share count fell 1.8% in FY2025 (277.5M → 272.5M).

**Q2 FY2026:** revenue $14,094M (+8.1%), operating earnings $1,460M (+11.9%), margin 10.4% (+40bp), GAAP diluted EPS $4.24 versus $3.74. All four segments grew. Aerospace delivered 41 aircraft (versus 38) at a 14.5% margin, up 130bp. Marine grew 10.4% with margin up 40bp on improved performance at each shipyard. Combat Systems was flat on revenue with margin down 30bp on program mix. Against the $4.09 consensus, the $4.24 GAAP result was a 3.7% beat — the four-quarter record is three beats and one small miss.

**Margin composition is the most useful thing in the numbers.** FY2025 gross margin was 15.13% versus 15.43% in FY2024, a 30bp decline; G&A fell from 5.38% to 4.94% of revenue, netting +14bp at the operating line. Every basis point of FY2025 consolidated expansion came from fixed-cost leverage on rising volume, which reverses if growth stalls. In H1'26 gross margin improved 48bp to 15.69% while G&A rose 22bp, so the 20bp of operating expansion is now contract-level. Two qualifiers: $83M of H1'26 operating earnings (2.9%) came from favorable cumulative-catch-up contract estimate adjustments versus $62M a year earlier, and Aerospace mix toward G700/G800 lifts gross margin mechanically without implying better execution.

**Cash flow is strong but should not be annualized.** H1'26 FCF of $3,598M equalled 157% of net earnings versus 55% a year earlier, but roughly 80% of the $2,585M year-over-year operating-cash improvement came from working capital and deferred tax: customer advances +$1,062M swing, receivables +$620M, deferred tax +$463M, payables +$457M, inventories +$342M, partly offset by unbilled receivables −$646M. Net earnings contributed $277M. The 10-Q states capex will keep increasing in H2. Applying FY2025's 94% conversion and the three-year 94/85/115 range to reconstructed FY2026 net earnings of ~$4.62B implies FY2026 FCF of roughly $4.3-4.6B — a 4.1%-4.3% yield on $105.9B. GD did not guide FCF; this is a normalization.

**Balance sheet.** At July 5, 2026: cash $4,333M, total debt carrying value $7,516M, equity $26,826M, net debt $3,183M. Gross debt/equity 0.28x; net debt/EBITDA roughly 0.5x on a ~$6.28B FY2025 EBITDA proxy; interest coverage 17.1x. Debt principal fell from $8,074M to $7,572M in six months while cash rose $2.0B. Two watch items rather than problems: the committed bank facility was reduced from $5B to $4B and expires March 2027 ("may renew or replace"), and the 10-Q discloses that up to ~300,000 already-outstanding shares were sold to 401(k) participants without Securities Act registration, remediated by a July 1, 2026 Form S-8 and a planned rescission offer. GD received no proceeds; share counts are unaffected; management concluded disclosure controls were effective.

**Capital allocation.** Dividend raised to $1.59/quarter in March 2026, the 29th consecutive annual increase — $6.36 annualized, 1.61% yield, 37.7% of FY2026E EPS. Repurchases are described in the 10-K as opportunistic and "primarily to address dilution": $637M in FY2025 (down from $1.5B), $319M in H1'26 at an average Q2 price of $346.57. Capex rose 29% in FY2025 to $1,161M and another 29% in H1'26 to $437M, with Marine consuming $220M of that. Total FY2025 shareholder return was 56.5% of FCF, with the balance funding capacity and debt retirement. Company-defined ROIC has risen three consecutive years: 12.3% → 13.2% → 14.2%.

Financial Health scores 8.0. This is investment-grade quality with rising returns on capital and a management team retiring debt with cash into an elevated-rate environment. Screens showing an Altman Z of 2.28 and "debt/equity 2.23" are artifacts — the latter is the equity multiplier (assets/equity), and the Z reproduces only if book equity/assets is substituted for two different inputs; using retained earnings/assets (0.770) and market cap/liabilities (3.374) gives Z ≈ 4.49.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance reconstructs from the 10-Q's segment detail rather than requiring secondary sources: revenue 13.8 + 18.0 + 9.8 + 14.1 = $55.7B (+6.0%); segment operating earnings at the guided margins = $6,038M; less $180M corporate = $5,858M (10.52% margin); plus $15M other, less $270M net interest = $5,603M pretax; at the guided 17.5% tax rate = $4,623M; on ~274.5M diluted shares = **$16.84**. That reconciles to the reported $16.80-$16.90 range and to the $16.96 consensus. Three of four segments had revenue guidance raised intra-year; Combat Systems' margin guide was cut from ~14.1% to ~13.8%.

**Backlog and visibility.** Total backlog was $136.5B at July 5, 2026, from $130.8B at April 5 and $118.0B at December 31, 2025 (itself +30% from $90.6B). GD explicitly defines backlog as equal to ASC 606 remaining performance obligations, so the figure is comparable to RPO. Two scope limits apply: $32.4B (23.7%) is unfunded, with Marine carrying the heaviest share at $22.8B of $65.2B; and the separately disclosed $50.4B of "estimated potential contract value" (unexercised options, IDIQ funding expectations) is neither backlog nor RPO and should not be added. Backlog covers 2.45x FY2026E revenue; conversion is ~50% by YE2027 and 80% by YE2029. H1'26 book-to-bill was 1.3x in Aerospace and 1.8x across the defense segments.

**The July 29 award.** HII's release describes the same ~$76.6B as a *combined* total to Newport News and GD Electric Boat. Neither issuer disclosed its own incremental backlog addition, and some of these boats may already sit within GD's $22.8B of Marine unfunded backlog or $7.4B of Marine estimated potential contract value. The incremental RPO addition is therefore not established and could be materially below $71.6B. What is established: the demand side of the two-decade submarine ramp on which GD's 29% capex increases are predicated is now contracted, after what USNI describes as a nearly three-year delay. That is a genuine de-risking of the volume case.

**Segment growth quality is uneven.** Aerospace's ~$13.8B guide implies only ~5% growth after 16.5% in FY2025 — the delivery ramp is normalizing, and the G400/G300 introductions will bring new-model margin dilution at some future point. Technologies grew 4.2% in H1 with margin slipping 9.6% → 9.4%, though the mix improved: H1 growth was C5ISR-led (+$237M) rather than IT-services-led (+$50M), which is better-quality revenue even if it has not yet reached margin. Combat is mid-handoff: U.S. military vehicle revenue fell $203M in FY2025 and $217M in H1'26 on the M10 Booker termination and Army recapitalization, offset by $9.2B of 2025 international wheeled/tracked awards that drove segment backlog up 60.3% to $27.2B, and by munitions/energetics growth of $258M in FY2025 and $209M in H1'26.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest**: current year $16.96 (+1.7% in 30 days, +2.0% in 90 days), next year $18.54 (+1.6%, +2.3%). Next-quarter estimates edged down 1.1%. The high-conviction revision signal — +30% or more over 90 days paired with accelerating revenue — is not present here. This is a steady mid-to-high-single-digit compounder with unusually good forward visibility. Growth Prospects scores 7.0, unchanged from the prior assessment, with the submarine award offsetting Aerospace normalization and Technologies stagnation.

## Competitive Landscape

Marine Systems is arguably the strongest structural position in U.S. defense, and GD's own 10-K language is the best evidence: Electric Boat is prime and lead shipyard on *all* Navy nuclear submarine programs and owns design and engineering; its only peer partners with it on Virginia and subcontracts to it on Columbia. Barriers are physical (nuclear-capable graving docks), human (27,000+ Electric Boat employees against a target run-rate of one Columbia plus up to two Virginia boats annually), and regulatory. The binding constraint is throughput — supply chain across ~3,000 suppliers and skilled labor — which is why capex is up 29% two periods running and why the segment earns 7.3% rather than teens. The Marine investment case is volume and visibility, not margin. Mechanically, Marine's rise from 29% of revenue in 2023 to ~32.3% guided for 2026 dilutes consolidated margin.

Aerospace is at the mature, high-margin end of a genuine product cycle: margin moved 13.0% (FY24) → 13.3% (FY25) → 14.7% (H1'26 and guide), driven by ultra-large-cabin mix and R&D falling after G800 certification. Order momentum is real (1.5x Q2 book-to-bill) and domestically anchored (U.S. customers 68% of 2025 orders). Relevant competitors are Bombardier, Dassault, Textron Aviation and Embraer; GD's residual dependency on an Israel-based mid-cabin airframe supplier, which caused disclosed delays, persists into the G300 program.

Technologies is the weakest link: 26% of revenue, slowest growth, declining margin, 69% of consolidated goodwill with $1.8B of accumulated impairments already taken, and the greatest reliance on the softer estimated-potential-contract-value measure ($30.9B against only $18.0B of backlog, with ~85% of 2025 orders coming from IDIQ add-ons or option exercises). The segment was the one GD identified as hit by 2025 federal staff reductions, contract terminations, award delays and the shutdown.

On customer concentration, the consolidated U.S.-government share has drifted down (72% in 2023 → 68% in 2025), but that is not diversification. Marine, the fastest-growing segment, took 100% of its Q2 revenue from the U.S. government. The segments shrinking as a share of revenue are also government-heavy, so the consolidated ratio and the underlying single-customer dependency on the U.S. Navy are moving in opposite directions. Competitive Position scores 7.2: the submarine franchise is exceptional and just got confirmed, offset by Technologies' erosion, Combat's transition, and the margin-dilutive mix shift.

## Risk Assessment

**Marine margin thinness is the largest earnings lever.** 100bp on $18.0B of Marine revenue is $180M pretax, ~$149M after tax at 17.5%, or ~$0.54 of EPS — 3.2% of FY2026E. On a 12-boat, >$125B Columbia program at 7.3% margins with 51% of U.S. government revenue on fixed-price contracts, a reach-forward loss is the single most consequential unpriced event. GD has disclosed none.

**The most explicitly material contract risk sits in a footnote, not Item 1A.** Both the 10-K and 10-Q disclose a "large, long-term contract with an international customer for tracked vehicles" where variable consideration realized "could be less than our estimate, which could have a material unfavorable impact." Note E puts $1.3B of net unbilled receivables against it at both July 5, 2026 and December 31, 2025 — flat over six months, though GD says the balance should decline as deliveries continue. As a scale reference only, $1.3B is ~28% of FY2026E net earnings; the disclosed exposure is not the full balance, and "reasonably possible" is below the "probable" threshold at which a provision would be required. The program is not named.

**Technologies goodwill.** $14,515M of $20,927M consolidated goodwill, net of $1.8B already impaired. The last quantitative test (Q4 2022) showed fair value exceeding carrying value by only ~25% — the thinnest cushion disclosed — while Aerospace, Marine and Combat rely on 2018 quantitative tests. The Q4 2025 qualitative assessment found no indicators, and three subsequent years of growth plus a higher market cap have likely widened the cushion. An impairment would be non-cash, would not touch FCF, covenants, the dividend or GD-defined ROIC, but would hit reported net income and book equity.

**Item 1A is not a reliable risk map.** GD's risk-factor section is brief, boilerplate-heavy for a company this size, and still carries a COVID-19 factor. The GD-specific exposures live in the notes and MD&A.

**Governance and succession.** Phebe Novakovic has been Chairman and CEO since January 2013. On August 6, 2026, GD announced the election of President Danny Deep to the board — conventionally a precursor to a handoff, though no transition has been announced and the underlying 8-K text was not retrievable. A CEO change after thirteen years is a real, if benign-looking, transition risk in a company whose value rests on multi-decade program execution.

**Budget and appropriations.** 68% of revenue is U.S. government; FY2025 federal staff reductions, contract terminations and shutdown effects hit GD, "largely limited to our IT services business." The FY2026 appropriations status was not independently verified.

**Cleared risks.** The naval-architect no-poach putative class action was dismissed with prejudice on May 18, 2026. The Supreme Court held IEEPA tariffs unauthorized in February 2026, with refunds in progress; tariffs had cost Aerospace 30bp of margin in FY2025.

Risk Factors scores 5.5. The tails are specific and quantifiable but not priced into a 23.3x multiple with a 1.6% yield and no buyback cushion.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($465, +18.6%).** The Q3 10-Q quantifies a large incremental backlog addition from the $76.6B award, pushing total backlog well above $160B and confirming that GD has contracted demand certainty through the 2040s. Marine margin advances from 7.3% toward 8% as the shipyards work up their learning curves and the capex program relieves throughput constraints — worth ~$0.38 of EPS per 70bp. Aerospace holds 14.5%+ through 2027 on G700/G800 mix before G400 dilution begins. Munitions and European vehicle demand offset the U.S. Army recapitalization drag, and Technologies' C5ISR mix shift eventually shows up in margin. Cash-funded debt retirement continues, interest expense declines further, and FY2027 EPS reaches $19 against a 24-25x multiple justified by the visibility.

**Bear case ($325, −17.1%).** A Columbia-class or DDG-51 reach-forward loss reveals that Marine's 7.3% margin was optimistic at current supplier and labor cost inflation, taking $0.50-$1.00 out of EPS and resetting the multiple toward Northrop's 17x. The tracked-vehicle variable-consideration exposure crystallizes against the flat $1.3B unbilled balance. Technologies impairs part of $14.5B of goodwill as federal IT demand softens. Aerospace decelerates as the ~5% FY2026 revenue guide proves to be the new normal and G400/G300 introductions dilute margin. In this scenario, an investor holding a 23x multiple with a 1.6% yield and no buyback support has nothing to cushion the derating.

**What I am actually underwriting at $392.05.** The demand side of the submarine thesis is now contracted and no longer the question; the supply side (throughput, supplier cost, labor) is. GD is paying for that capacity out of free cash flow rather than borrowing — 43% of FY2025 FCF was retained — which is the right decision and also the reason there is no per-share shrink to lean on. Roughly $0.21 of the ~$1.40 total FY2026 guidance raise came from cash-funded debt retirement rather than operations, which is real but lower quality than segment margin gains. Fundamentals have confirmed the thesis for a third consecutive quarter, and the price has confirmed it too.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** The thesis is positive and catalyst-driven, but the expected return of roughly 7.6% (price plus dividend, probability-weighted) against 17% bear-case downside does not clear the ACCUMULATE bar at 2.0% below the 52-week high. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; I am holding the rating despite improved fundamentals because the improvement has been fully matched by price. The overall score rises to 6.5 from 6.3 on the guidance raise, the litigation dismissal, the interest-expense reduction, and the shift in margin composition from G&A leverage to gross margin.

## Conclusion

General Dynamics is executing well and its most important franchise just received the largest contracted demand confirmation in its history. Balance sheet quality is better than screens suggest — 0.28x gross debt/equity, 17x coverage, debt being retired with cash — and the composition of margin improvement has shifted from volume-dependent G&A leverage to contract-level gross margin. Estimates are rising, the beat record is 3-of-4, and a thirty-month treble-damages litigation tail was extinguished in May.

The rating is constrained by arithmetic rather than by doubts about the business. At $392.05, GD trades 23.3x FY2026E and 21.1x FY2027 consensus on ~9% EPS growth, with a 1.62% yield and buybacks deliberately capped at dilution offset. That leaves the entire return dependent on earnings growth and multiple stability, at a multiple that has already absorbed the submarine re-rating. Base case $426 gives 8.7% upside; the bear case at Northrop's current multiple is 17% down. Own the position, use cheap 20% implied volatility to hedge or write calls if that suits the mandate, and add on either a Q3 backlog step-up that is larger than the market expects or a pullback toward $340-350. Do not initiate a full position at the high.
