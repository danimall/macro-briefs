**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Popular, Inc. is the dominant bank in Puerto Rico, operating through Banco Popular de Puerto Rico with roughly $75.3B in assets and a low-cost, sticky deposit base of about $67.6B. The investment story in 2026 is a completed earnings recovery: net income rose 36% in 2025 to $833M, Q1 2026 delivered $245.7M ($3.78 EPS, an 11.1% beat), and adjusted ROTCE reached 15.46%, above management's ~14% medium-term target. With a CET1 ratio near 15.9% and loans essentially flat at $39.3B, the franchise generates high returns while carrying substantial excess capital, positioning it as a capital-return compounder rather than a growth bank.

The fundamentals confirm the improving trajectory. Consensus estimates have risen across every window (current-year EPS +5.5% over 90 days to $14.92, next-year +3.2% to $16.63), the company has beaten estimates four consecutive quarters (average surprise ~14%), and net interest margin is expanding. The complication is price: BPOP has appreciated roughly 50% over the trailing year and now trades at $172.25, below the analyst mean target of $186.2 and above the low target of $163, with the stock only ~1% below its 52-week high. Much of the re-rating that the recovery justified appears captured.

We rate BPOP a HOLD with a constructive, growth-oriented bias. The forward return is real but modest: buyback-driven EPS growth on top of a stable ~12-13x forward multiple supports high-single-digit to low-teens total return, not the 50% seen over the past year. The structural risk cluster (Puerto Rico economic concentration, the federal-funds/stimulus cliff underpinning recent deposit and credit strength, and the single-vendor Evertec dependency) keeps us from upgrading to BUY at a full valuation. We would become buyers on a pullback to the low-$150s where the risk/reward turns asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Popular is a diversified financial-services holding company built around Banco Popular de Puerto Rico, the largest bank on the island by assets and deposits. The franchise rests on entrenched #1 market share, unmatched branch and ATM density, deep Puerto Rico public-sector (government) deposit relationships, and a low-cost deposit franchise operating in a consolidated island banking oligopoly with limited new-entrant threat. A smaller mainland U.S. operation (Popular Bank, concentrated in New York, New Jersey, and Florida) provides modest geographic diversification.

The balance sheet is deposit-heavy and highly liquid. Total assets have grown steadily from $70.8B (2023) to $73.0B (2024) to $75.3B (2025). Deposits stood at ~$67.6B in Q1 2026 against loans held in portfolio of only ~$39.3B, with $33.6B in money-market and investment securities. This structure, a large, cheap deposit base funding a modest loan book plus a large securities portfolio, makes the bank both highly liquid and materially exposed to interest-rate movements through AOCI, which was a drag in 2022-23 and a tailwind more recently.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year net income path shows a clear trough and recovery: $935M (2021), $1,103M (2022, inflated by unusual items), $541M (2023 trough), $614M (2024), and $833M (2025, +36% YoY). Q1 2026 net income of $245.7M annualizes toward record territory, driven by net interest income of $670.2M and an expanding margin alongside lower operating expenses. Adjusted ROTCE followed the same arc: 18.5% (2021), down to 10.0-10.2% in the 2023-24 trough, back to 11.0% in 2025, and 15.46% in Q1 2026.

Capital is a defining strength. CET1 stood at 15.92% in Q1 2026, far above regulatory minimums, and stockholders' equity grew from $4.1B (2022) to $6.25B (2025), aided by AOCI recovery as rates stabilized. The bank returned $204M to shareholders in Q1 2026 alone through buybacks and dividends. With ROTCE above target and loans flat, there is no competing demand for that excess capital, so the buyback runway is substantial. At 64.5M shares outstanding, sustained repurchases can meaningfully compound EPS even without balance-sheet growth.

Earnings quality is solid. The four-quarter beat record (25.8%, 4.4%, 14.0%, 11.1%) reflects both conservative guidance and genuine margin momentum, which supports confidence in rising forward estimates. One caution surfaced in Q1 2026: modestly higher net charge-offs tied to a single commercial loan, with management guiding to "slightly higher expected charge-offs" ahead even as overall NPLs decline. This is a data point to monitor, not yet a trend. The reported XBRL "revenue" line (~$559M for 2025) is a bank income-statement classification artifact and should not be read as a top-line; net interest income (~$670M quarterly run-rate) plus net income are the true earnings measures.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is one of margin-driven earnings recovery and capital-return-driven EPS compounding rather than balance-sheet expansion. Growth is coming from net interest margin expansion (the rate-plateau tailwind lifting yields on the large securities book and loan portfolio faster than funding costs) and from share-count reduction. Consensus captures this, with next-quarter revisions accelerating (+2.9% over 30 days). Rising estimates across every window signal continued positive earnings momentum.

The estimate-revision magnitude here (+5.5% current-year over 90 days) is meaningful but below the +30% threshold that would flag a powerful under-appreciated inflection. This is a steady upgrade cycle, not a violent one, consistent with a bank whose recovery is well understood by the market. The durability question centers on Puerto Rico's economic condition. Much of the recent deposit surge, benign credit, and NIM strength traces to federal disaster-recovery and pandemic-era stimulus inflows. The eventual wind-down of those funds is the central threat to current earnings power: funding costs could rise as excess liquidity drains and credit could normalize. The +11% implied next-year EPS growth ($14.92 → $16.63) leans heavily on continued buybacks executed against a stable-to-improving macro; it does not assume the tailwinds reverse.

## Valuation Assessment

BPOP trades at $172.25 against trailing EPS of $12.30 (~14x trailing) and current-year consensus of $14.92 (~11.5x forward), moving to ~10.4x on next-year's $16.63. On absolute terms these are undemanding bank multiples, and roughly 0.7-0.8x tangible book would still leave room versus the ~15% ROTCE the franchise now earns. The dividend yields ~1.7%, with the total shareholder return dominated by buybacks.

The cheapness that justified the re-rating has largely been harvested. The stock sits below the analyst mean target of $186.2, a current-vs-target gap of only ~8%, and against a high target of $201. On this system's own evidence, a compressed bank multiple is not by itself a reason to expect outperformance: Financials as a group have delivered negative alpha versus the broad market, and cheap valuation scores have historically sorted the wrong way absent a specific, independent catalyst. Here the catalyst that drove the move (the ROTCE crossover) has already occurred and is priced. What remains is buyback-driven EPS accretion, which supports the multiple but does not obviously expand it.

We assign a mid-range valuation score. The forward P/E is genuinely reasonable for a high-ROTCE, over-capitalized bank, which caps downside. But the stock is fairly valued for what it is, with the easy re-rating captured, and the analyst-target-as-floor logic does not apply strongly when the primary catalyst is behind the company. Base-case fair value sits around $189 (~11.4x the next-year $16.63, consistent with the analyst median), roughly 10% above the current quote.

## Competitive Landscape

Popular's competitive position is strong and stable-to-improving. It holds leading share in a consolidated Puerto Rico banking market with a structural oligopoly, unmatched physical distribution, and entrenched government banking relationships that competitors cannot easily replicate. The low-cost deposit base is the core of the moat, and its persistence through the island's fiscal crisis, debt restructuring, and multiple hurricanes demonstrates durability. The ~15% ROTCE and ~16% CET1 combination stands out favorably against mid-cap U.S. bank peers, most of which cannot match both return and capital cushion simultaneously.

The trajectory is favorable at the margin: NIM expansion, declining NPLs, and record-pace earnings all point to a strengthening position within its market. The mainland U.S. operation is a smaller, more competitive business that diversifies away from island concentration but does not add moat. The competitive risk is the island's economic base, and therefore the size of the profit pool the oligopoly divides, being tied to federal reconstruction spending that will eventually taper.

## Risk Assessment

**Puerto Rico concentration** is the single most important risk. BPOP's fortunes are bound to a small island economy with a history of fiscal crisis, bankruptcy, debt restructuring, out-migration, and hurricane exposure. The mechanism to watch: federal disaster/stimulus inflows → elevated deposits and liquidity → benign credit and strong NIM → record earnings. As those inflows wind down, the chain partly reverses: funding costs rise, credit normalizes, and earnings power compresses. This is the structural bear case and the reason the forward multiple should not expand indefinitely.

**Evertec dependency** is the non-obvious tail risk. The 10-K flags heavy reliance on Evertec for core transaction and payment processing, rooted in a legacy ownership/spin-off relationship. Disruption or unfavorable contract renegotiation would directly impair operations, a single-vendor concentration a standard screen would miss.

**Public-deposit concentration** cuts both ways: the large and growing Puerto Rico government deposit base is a funding strength but concentrates counterparty and political risk, and these balances can move with the government's fiscal cycle. **Credit normalization** bears monitoring given the single-commercial-loan charge-off in Q1 2026 and management's guidance for slightly higher charge-offs. **Interest-rate sensitivity** on the $33.6B securities/money-market book remains a two-way AOCI exposure. Two undisclosed executive/director changes (Feb 27 and Apr 8, 2026 Item 5.02 8-Ks) are a flagged open item; the seniority and nature of those changes could not be confirmed.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($201-210):** The Puerto Rico recovery proves durable, NIM holds or expands further, and management deploys the ~16% CET1 into aggressive, sustained buybacks that compound EPS toward and beyond $17 next year while credit stays benign. The stock re-rates modestly toward 12.5-13x on higher earnings, reaching the high analyst target and beyond. Continued double-digit earnings beats extend the estimate-revision cycle.

**Base Case (~$189):** Buybacks and modest NIM support drive EPS to the ~$16.63 next-year consensus, the multiple holds near ~11-12x forward, and the stock delivers a high-single-digit to low-teens total return including the ~1.7% dividend. This tracks the analyst median of $189.

**Bear Case ($140-150):** Federal stimulus wind-down begins to pressure the Puerto Rico economy, deposit costs rise as excess liquidity drains, credit normalizes beyond the single-loan issue, and the market de-rates the franchise back toward tangible book. The stock retraces toward its 52-week trading range midpoint, a ~13-19% decline from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (the next print after the April 23 Q1 report), with continued attention to charge-off trends and NIM commentary.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$150s (near ~9-10x next-year EPS), restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Estimate-revision velocity re-accelerating toward +30% or a Q2 beat that materially lifts the full-year consensus above $15.50, indicating the earnings power is being systematically underestimated.
- Concrete evidence that the Puerto Rico deposit/credit strength is structural rather than stimulus-dependent (e.g., stable deposits and benign credit as federal funds taper).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Charge-offs accelerating beyond the single-loan issue into a broader commercial credit deterioration.
- Deposit costs rising materially as public-sector or federal-linked balances drain, compressing NIM and reversing the earnings recovery.
- An adverse Evertec contract renegotiation or operational disruption, or disclosure that the flagged executive changes involve senior leadership departures.

## Conclusion

Popular has completed a genuine earnings recovery: ROTCE above target, CET1 near 16%, four consecutive beats, rising estimates, and an emerging capital-return engine that can compound EPS with flat loan growth. This is a high-quality, well-capitalized franchise, and the fundamental trajectory is improving. The constraint is valuation and stage: the stock has already re-rated ~50% over the past year, sits below the analyst mean target and within ~1% of its 52-week high, and the primary catalyst (the ROTCE crossover) is now priced. The forward return is real but modest and depends on buyback-driven EPS growth against a Puerto Rico macro backdrop whose durability is the central open question. We rate BPOP a **HOLD** with a growth bias, and would move to a BUY on a pullback to the low-$150s where the risk/reward on this franchise turns clearly asymmetric.
