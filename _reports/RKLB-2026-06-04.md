**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rocket Lab is one of the few public companies executing on the space-and-defense growth thesis with real revenue, real backlog, and a tightening cost structure. FY25 revenue reached $601.8M (+38% YoY), Q1'26 grew 63% to $200.3M, gross margin expanded from 21% (FY23) to 38.2% (Q1'26 GAAP), and backlog climbed to $2.22B on the back of the $816M Space Development Agency Tracking Layer Tranche 3 award and a record launch contract signed in May. The business has re-centered from launch to satellites and defense components: Space Systems is now roughly two-thirds of revenue and US government revenue jumped to 47% of FY25 sales from 33% the prior year. There is a coherent, catalyst-driven growth story over the next 12 months.

The problem is price. At roughly $70B market capitalization on $602M of FY25 revenue, the stock trades near 115x trailing sales while burning more than $300M of free cash flow annually and carrying no path to GAAP profitability inside the estimate horizon. The forward P/E is negative (-349x on profile data), analyst consensus mean and median targets ($103.91 and $102.50) sit below the current price, and the rally has been substantially driven by the stock's status as the only investable public proxy for SpaceX ahead of that company's expected June IPO. That makes the valuation sentiment-dependent in a way fundamentals do not yet support.

The Neutron medium-lift rocket, the asset that justifies the multiple, is unproven and slipping. A Stage-1 tank ruptured during hydrostatic qualification testing in January 2026, pushing first launch to Q4 2026, and management explicitly flags further schedule risk. Combined with pervasive C-suite insider selling into the rally and a funding model built on continuous equity issuance, the setup is a high-quality growth story trading at a valuation that prices in success that has not happened yet. We rate RKLB HOLD on a growth basis: the trajectory and competitive position are strong enough to hold through the Neutron milestone, but the valuation forecloses adding new capital, and shorting a name with this much fundamental momentum and balance-sheet strength is not advisable.

## Company Overview

Rocket Lab operates two reportable segments. **Launch Services** is anchored by the Electron small-lift rocket (payloads to ~300 kg), the second most frequently launched orbital rocket globally in 2025, with 81 successful missions through Q1 2026 and only three failures in its history. Electron's moat is structural: 3D-printed electric-turbopump Rutherford engines, carbon-composite airframes, and a privately owned launch range at Mahia, New Zealand operating under a US–NZ technology-safeguards treaty that is difficult to replicate. HASTE is a suborbital hypersonic-test variant with defense applications. The medium-lift, partially reusable Neutron (13,000 kg to LEO) is in development and is the central growth catalyst.

**Space Systems** (spacecraft manufacturing, Photon buses, and merchant components: solar cells, reaction wheels, star trackers, radios, separation systems, optical and EO-IR sensors) is now the larger segment at roughly 67% of FY25 revenue. It has been built through a series of acquisitions (Sinclair, ASI, Planetary Systems, SolAero, GEOST), and the company is accelerating that roll-up to own supply-constrained satellite components for the proliferated-constellation era.

Founder, CEO and Chairman Sir Peter Beck holds super-voting Series A Preferred Stock (40.95M shares after a May 2026 partial conversion), giving him effective voting control tied to his continued tenure as CEO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong and the mix is shifting toward higher-value defense and satellite work.** Revenue moved from $244.6M (FY23) to $436.2M (FY24, +78%) to $601.8M (FY25). The FY25 split was approximately $402.8M Space Systems (+30%) and $199M Launch (+59%). Backlog rose to $2,219.8M at March 2026 from $1,847M in December 2025, with roughly 36% expected to convert within 12 months, providing real near-term revenue visibility.

**Margins are improving on operating leverage but the company remains deeply unprofitable.** Gross margin expanded from 21% (FY23) to 26.6% (FY24) to 34.4% (FY25) to 38.2% (Q1'26 GAAP). FY25 operating loss was -$228.8M (-38% margin) and net loss -$198.2M; Q1'26 net loss narrowed to -$45M (-22% margin). R&D is the swing variable at $270.7M in FY25 (+55%), driven by Neutron development. Electron unit economics are improving: cost per launch fell from $7.0M (FY23) to $4.8M (FY25), with revenue per launch around $9.3M in Q1'26.

**The balance sheet is strong, funded by serial equity issuance.** Liquidity was $1.48B in cash and marketable securities at March 2026 (cash alone $1.2B), supported by a $1.12B ATM program in FY25, $445M in Q1'26, and a new $1.0B ATM plus collared-forward program authorized in March 2026. Current ratio is 4.08, debt/equity 0.35, and Altman Z-score 3.22 (safe zone). The 4.25% convertible notes due 2029 have been almost entirely converted to equity (only $37.6M principal remains versus $355M issued), which removes a refinancing risk at the cost of dilution.

**Cash burn and dilution are the structural offsets.** FY25 free cash flow was -$321.8M (operating cash flow -$165.5M, capex $156.3M). Shares outstanding rose from ~496M (FY24) to ~576M (April 2026). The funding model is to issue equity opportunistically into strength to fund Neutron and the M&A roll-up. This is prudent for survival and well-executed, but it dilutes per-share value continuously. No dividend or buyback (also contractually restricted by a Department of Commerce direct-funding agreement).

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is real and broad-based, which is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID. Three pillars support it on a 12-month view:

1. **Backlog and defense demand.** The backlog is anchored by the SDA Tracking Layer Tranche 3 award (18 satellites, delivery 2029) and the May 2026 record launch contract. US government revenue at 47% of FY25 sales positions RKLB as an emerging defense-grade supplier into a sustained multi-vector defense spending cycle. The M&A roll-up (GEOST, defense optical/EO-IR sensors, $290M; Mynaric, laser inter-satellite comms, ~$155M; Motiv, space robotics, $40M + earnout) is building a vertically integrated, supply-constrained satellite component base that few competitors can match.

2. **Margin trajectory.** The gross-margin progression over three years reflects genuine operating leverage in Space Systems and improving Electron unit economics. As Space Systems scales and Neutron eventually contributes, the path to operating breakeven becomes credible, even if the timing keeps slipping.

3. **Neutron as a dated catalyst.** First launch is targeted for Q4 2026, within the investment horizon. A successful Neutron debut would validate the medium-lift/constellation thesis embedded in the valuation and open a far larger addressable market than Electron alone.

The counterweight: forward EPS estimates are falling, not rising. Current-year consensus moved to -$0.25 from -$0.22 (90 days ago), and next-year flipped from +$0.08 to -$0.04. The deterioration is driven by R&D investment and the Neutron slip rather than demand weakness, but it does push profitability further out and is a genuine negative signal on near-term earnings momentum. Revenue growth and backlog dominate the growth score; the deteriorating EPS path is captured in valuation and financial health.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is the binding constraint and the reason this is not a buy. On an ~$800M+ run-rate, RKLB trades near 115–120x trailing sales while still burning over $300M FCF annually. The forward P/E is negative; there is no positive-earnings denominator to anchor a multiple inside the estimate window.

Peer comparison underscores the dislocation. Aerospace & defense peers trade at a median P/E of 33.5 (TDG 38.9, NOC 16.8, LHX 33.5, GD 21.2, HWM 58.2); these are profitable, cash-generative primes. RKLB has no earnings to apply a P/E to and trades on a sales multiple roughly an order of magnitude above any of them. The cycle-adjustment argument that rescues genuinely cheap stocks does not apply here: there is no trough-earnings normalization that makes 115x sales with negative FCF look cheap.

Analyst targets reinforce caution rather than provide a floor. Mean target $103.91 and median $102.50 both sit below the current price of ~$114.70; the high is $150 and the low is $60. The principle that analyst targets lag reality on cheap, accelerating stocks does not hold at premium multiples: when a stock trades above its consensus targets at an extreme sales multiple, the targets and the price reflect the same optimism, and the asymmetry favors disappointment. A material portion of the year's 329% move appears tied to the SpaceX-IPO proxy trade and ETF/retail flows rather than fundamentals, and the stock is already ~24% off its $150.99 high with a sharp ~20% drawdown in the past week (from $143.48 on May 29 to $114.70 on June 3). Valuation scores 3.0: the multiple prices flawless execution, sits above consensus, and is highly sentiment-dependent.

## Competitive Landscape

RKLB's competitive position is strong and improving, which is the second reason this is a hold rather than a short. In dedicated small-lift launch, Electron's flight heritage and proprietary New Zealand launch range under a US–NZ treaty give it clear leadership and a hard-to-replicate operational moat. In space systems and components, the acquisition roll-up is assembling a vertically integrated, defense-grade supply chain (sensors via GEOST, optical comms via Mynaric, robotics via Motiv) at the moment proliferated-warfighter architectures (SDA) are funding multi-year demand.

Competition is formidable: SpaceX, ULA, Northrop, Firefly and Blue Origin in launch; Airbus, Lockheed, L3Harris, Maxar and Redwire in systems. Neutron targets the medium-lift/constellation market SpaceX dominates with Falcon 9, where RKLB has no flight heritage yet. On trajectory, RKLB is gaining share, widening its component moat, and is the only investable public pure-play in the space economy. Competitive position scores 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

- **Neutron execution (the lynchpin).** The January 2026 Stage-1 tank rupture and Q4 2026 target mean the catalyst justifying the multiple is unproven and slipping. Any further delay directly threatens the valuation. The securities class action dismissed with prejudice in April 2026 was specifically about Neutron-progress messaging, underscoring how sensitive the stock is to this milestone.
- **Concentration.** Top 5 customers were 49% of FY25 revenue; top 5 backlog customers 77% of backlog; US government 47% of revenue. The October 2025 federal shutdown already caused award and payment delays and Wallops workforce reductions. Contracts are largely fixed-price, carrying cost-overrun risk (the MDA Corp 17-bus contract is delayed with two-way liquidated-damages exposure).
- **Perpetual dilution.** Ten consecutive years of net losses funded by continuous equity issuance; the share count keeps rising. This is the structural offset to the balance sheet.
- **Key-man risk.** Heavy dependence on Beck, no key-man insurance, and his preferred-stock control reverts/converts on departure.
- **Valuation/sentiment.** The multiple is tied to the SpaceX-proxy narrative; a disappointing or volatile SpaceX debut, or a rotation out of speculative space names, could derate the stock independent of operations. The Motley Fool's caution after the SpaceX S-1 disclosed SpaceX's scale is an early signal of that risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 112.4% versus 90.3% trailing realized (rich by 22pp), and the term structure is in backwardation (112.4% near-term vs 98.7% six-month): the market expects the next few weeks to be more volatile than the long run, consistent with the SpaceX IPO window and ongoing Neutron headlines. Near-dated skew is call-heavy (-4.4% one-week, with heavy call volume at $116–$122 strikes), reflecting speculative upside positioning into the proxy trade rather than fundamental conviction. A $85 September put traded 6.7x open interest, indicating some informed downside hedging into the autumn (around the Neutron launch window). IV fell 8.2pp as the price dropped 17.7% over the past week, and the P/C OI ratio ticked up to 0.65, consistent with froth coming out of the recent spike. The signal is two-sided: near-term speculative call demand against a building autumn put hedge, fitting a binary-catalyst stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$150, the analyst high):** Neutron passes qualification and reaches first launch on or near schedule in Q4 2026, validating the medium-lift thesis. Space Systems continues compounding at 30%+ with margins moving through 40%, the SDA program and defense roll-up drive backlog past $3B, and a successful SpaceX IPO sustains the space-economy bid. At that point the multiple is supported by a credible path to operating breakeven and a defensible defense-prime profile. Upside ~31% from current price.

**Bear case (~$60, the analyst low):** Neutron slips again or fails its early flights, the securities-litigation appeal reopens Neutron-messaging scrutiny, government-funding disruptions continue to delay awards, and the SpaceX IPO either disappoints or simply removes the scarcity premium that has supported RKLB. With no earnings to anchor on, a sentiment unwind could compress the sales multiple toward 50–60x, implying roughly a halving of the stock. Downside ~48% from current price.

The base case sits between these: continued 30%+ revenue growth and margin expansion, Neutron progressing but with residual schedule risk, and the multiple holding in a wide, volatile range as the proxy trade and the Neutron milestone both resolve. Genuine growth and a strong competitive position against an extreme, sentiment-dependent valuation is why the call is HOLD rather than BUY or AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings report (revenue growth and margin trajectory), the SpaceX IPO window (mid-June 2026, a sentiment catalyst), and the Neutron first-launch attempt targeted for Q4 2026 (the fundamental catalyst).

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Neutron completes qualification and executes a successful first orbital launch, de-risking the medium-lift thesis, AND the stock trades at a sales multiple that prices that success rather than anticipates it (a meaningful pullback toward the $60–$80 range, or revenue catching up such that the multiple compresses below ~40x forward sales).
- Sustained positive forward EPS estimate revisions combined with a credible, dated path to operating breakeven, reversing the current deterioration (next-year consensus flipped from +$0.08 to -$0.04).
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, which would reverse the current uniform distribution pattern.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A second Neutron test failure or a Q4 2026 slip into 2027, which would invalidate the catalyst underpinning the valuation.
- A break below the 200-day moving average on accelerating volume alongside falling revenue growth (sub-30% YoY), signaling the proxy premium has unwound without fundamental support.
- Material widening of cash burn or a forced, dilutive equity raise at depressed prices, indicating the opportunistic funding model has broken.

This is a `growth_hold`: the specific 12-month thesis is continued 30–60% revenue growth, backlog conversion, gross margin expansion through 40%, the SDA Tranche 3 ramp, and the dated Neutron Q4 2026 catalyst. We hold through that milestone rather than shorting a name with this much fundamental momentum and a strong balance sheet, but the valuation precludes deploying new capital here.

## Conclusion

Rocket Lab is a high-quality, fast-growing space-and-defense business with a genuine moat, an expanding margin profile, a large backlog, and a dated near-term catalyst in Neutron. It is also among the most expensive equities in the public market on any sales-based measure, burning over $300M of cash a year, funded by continuous dilution, trading above its consensus analyst targets, and substantially propelled by a SpaceX-proxy trade whose durability is uncertain. The fundamental trajectory and competitive position are strong enough that shorting strength here would be a mistake; the burden of proof is on the bear, and the balance sheet removes the solvency case. But the valuation is too stretched, and the Neutron catalyst too unproven, to commit new capital.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Hold existing positions through the Neutron Q4 2026 milestone and the SpaceX IPO window; do not add at current levels. Re-rate toward BUY on a successful Neutron flight combined with multiple compression, or toward AVOID on a Neutron failure/slip or a sentiment unwind that breaks the 200-day moving average without fundamental support. Overall score 5.0.
