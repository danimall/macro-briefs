**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ONEOK is one of the largest U.S. midstream infrastructure operators, running a toll-road model across four segments: Natural Gas Gathering & Processing, Natural Gas Liquids, Natural Gas Pipelines, and Refined Products & Crude. Three transformational acquisitions in three years (Magellan in 2023, EnLink/Medallion in 2024, and the full EnLink buy-in in January 2025) turned a gas/NGL pure-play into a diversified gas-plus-crude-plus-refined-products platform with FY25 revenue of $33.6B and Adjusted EBITDA of $8.02B. Management frames earnings as roughly 90% fee-based for 2026, backed by long-term minimum-volume/take-or-pay contracts with predominantly investment-grade counterparties.

The stock has recovered strongly, up about 15% over the trailing period to $93.52, roughly 3% below its 52-week high after a difficult second half of 2025 that pushed it to $64. The recovery is warranted by the guidance raise (2026 EBITDA lifted ~2% to $8.0-8.5B) and a 4% dividend increase to $4.28 annualized (~4.6% yield). But the quality of the recent beat deserves scrutiny: Q1 2026's upside came disproportionately from optimization and marketing spread capture (Waha-Katy differentials plus Winter Storm Fern added $127M to Pipelines EBITDA), while the contracted Gathering & Processing base actually declined $24M on lower realized NGL/gas prices. That is a quality-of-earnings flag that partly undermines the "90% fee-based" framing at the margin.

We maintain a HOLD with an income-hold rationale. The durable return here is the well-covered ~4.6% dividend, not multiple re-rating. Against that, ~4.2x net leverage, $34B of debt, thin cash ($172M at Q1 2026), chronic working-capital deficits, continuous capital-market dependence, and a forward EBITDA CAGR that Goldman pegs near 3% argue against a BUY. Our overall score of 5.6 continues the modest downtrend from 6.8 in February, driven by softer earnings quality and management-alignment signals that new data confirms rather than reverses.

## Company Overview

ONEOK gathers, processes, fractionates, transports, stores, and markets natural gas, NGLs, refined products, and crude oil. The four reportable segments and their FY25 Adjusted EBITDA contributions:

- **Natural Gas Liquids** ($2.78B): the historic franchise, connecting supply basins to Mont Belvieu/Conway hubs, with fractionation and Purity NGL export/petrochemical demand.
- **Natural Gas Gathering & Processing** ($2.14B): wellhead gathering/processing across the Rockies, Mid-Continent, and Permian.
- **Refined Products & Crude** ($2.18B): largely the legacy Magellan system plus Medallion Permian crude gathering and EnLink crude assets.
- **Natural Gas Pipelines** ($0.86B): transportation and storage.

The competitive advantage is an integrated, regionally diversified network spanning the Rocky Mountain, Mid-Continent, Permian, and Gulf Coast regions, linking supply to demand centers and export terminals. Leadership is stable: CEO Pierce Norton and CFO Walter Hulse both signed the Q1 2026 10-Q, and the Item 5.02 8-Ks from early 2026 appear to be routine director/officer elections and equity grants rather than disruptive departures.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth has been acquired, not organic.** Revenue rose from $17.7B (2023) to $21.7B (2024) to $33.6B (2025), but the step-change reflects the M&A wave rather than volume growth. The more meaningful metric, Adjusted EBITDA, climbed from $5.24B to $6.78B to $8.02B. Net income to ONEOK moved $2.66B to $3.04B to $3.39B, but diluted EPS lagged ($5.48 to $5.17 to $5.42) because the share count ballooned from roughly 485M to 626M as deals were funded with stock. Trailing EPS of $5.42 puts the stock at ~16.7x, well below the peer median.

**Q1 2026** posted revenue of $9.62B (+20% YoY), operating income of $1.43B (+17%), net income to ONEOK of $774M, and diluted EPS of $1.23 versus $1.04 (+18%). Adjusted EBITDA was $1.997B (+12.5%). The composition matters: Pipelines optimization/marketing added $127M, while Gathering & Processing EBITDA fell $24M on lower realized prices. A material portion of the beat is volatile spread capture, not contracted fee growth. Truist's specific concern, that incremental Permian takeaway capacity narrows the very location differentials that drove the upside, is the right thing to watch. (Note: reported Q1 2026 diluted EPS of $1.32 was in line with consensus of $1.32 per Phase 1 earnings data; the $1.23 figure and +18% growth reflected in the brief could not be reconciled to that data — [unverified].)

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY25 operating cash flow was $5.6B against $3.15B of capex and ~$2.6B of dividends; operating cash flow exceeded dividends by $3.0B. A $2.0B buyback authorization exists but only $62M was used in FY25, confirming that capital is being directed toward debt reduction and growth projects rather than share repurchase. 2026 capex is guided to $2.7-3.2B.

**The balance sheet is the central tension.** Total debt is roughly $34.0B (LT $30.8B + ST $0.82B at YE25, with commercial paper rising to $1.6B by Q1 2026). Debt/equity is 2.96x and the Altman Z-Score of 1.43 flags a "distress zone," but that metric is a poor fit for capital-intensive, contracted-cash-flow midstream firms — it penalizes high asset bases, thin working capital, and structural leverage. The company holds investment-grade ratings (Moody's Baa2 / S&P BBB / Fitch BBB, all Stable). The relevant covenant metric is net-debt/adjusted-EBITDA of 4.2x at Q1 2026, inside the 5.5x credit-agreement covenant that steps down to 5.0x after June 30, 2026. Management deliberately used a December 2025 gathering-asset acquisition to extend the higher covenant window, which is worth monitoring.

Persistent working-capital deficits ($1.9B YE25, $2.3B Q1 2026) are driven by current maturities and commercial paper. These are backstopped by a $3.5B revolver (to 2030), a $3.5B CP program, a $1.0B ATM equity program (unused), and a new $1.2B 364-day term loan (April 2026). Cash is thin ($172M Q1 2026), so the company depends on continuous capital-market access, which raises sensitivity to rates and ratings. Interest expense rose sharply to $1.78B (from $1.37B) as debt grew, and the FY25 debt program included $789M of open-market note repurchases below par that generated a $106M gain. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, a financing-heavy model with $34B of debt faces a structural headwind to interest costs and refinancing terms.

## Growth Analysis

The M&A-driven step-change is largely complete. Goldman models roughly a 3% Adjusted EBITDA CAGR for 2025-2030, implying that future value creation depends on integration synergies, de-leveraging, and delivery of the announced growth JVs rather than another transformational deal. Estimate revisions confirm a flat-to-slightly-negative forward trajectory: current-year consensus EPS sits at $5.71 (-0.4% over 90 days) and next-year at $6.11 (-2.0% over 90 days). There is no rising-estimate momentum that would justify treating the stock on cycle-adjusted terms. This is a low-single-digit compounder.

The forward growth optionality centers on the export/LPG pivot. Announced JVs include Texas City Logistics with MPLX (a 400 MBbl/d LPG export terminal, 50%, plus the MBTC Pipeline at 80%, ~$1.0B combined, early 2028) and the Eiger Express Pipeline (25.5%, a 3.7 Bcf/d Permian-to-Katy gas line with WhiteWater/MPLX/Enbridge, ~$350M, mid-2028). These position ONEOK to capture rising U.S. NGL/LPG export demand and Permian gas takeaway, but the earnings contribution is 2028-weighted and does not move the 12-month thesis.

Industry tailwinds are real but shared: strong U.S. shale production, rising LPG export demand, the data-center/AI-driven natural gas demand narrative, and a deregulatory posture (the OBBBA methane-fee suspension in July 2025 and the EPA's February 2026 move to rescind the 2009 GHG endangerment finding). These are near-term supports subject to litigation and political reversal.

## Valuation Assessment

OKE trades at ~16.7x trailing earnings and ~15.3x on the $6.11 next-year consensus. Against the peer group in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation, this is a meaningful discount:

| **Peer median** | **27.5** | **2.9%** |

At roughly 0.6x the peer-median P/E, the discount requires explanation rather than being taken as a signal of upside. The discount is partly justified: OKE carries higher leverage (net 4.2x, D/E 2.96x) than several peers, its 5-year TSR of 256.8 now trails its peer group at 290.5 (a reversal from 2024 when OKE led), and its forward EBITDA CAGR is a low ~3%. The heavy equity-funded M&A diluted per-share metrics — EPS grew far slower than net income — and the market has priced skepticism about the roll-up's per-share value creation.

A cheap multiple in isolation is not a buy signal; in this sector the value discount reflects real structural constraints (leverage, dilution history, modest growth) rather than a mispricing with a catalyst. The dividend yield of ~4.6% is above the peer median (2.9%) and provides genuine absolute support, but it is the compensation for the higher leverage and slower growth, not evidence of undervaluation. Analyst targets bracket the current price — low $84, mean $95.76, median $95.00, high $113 — with a stable Buy/Neutral/Hold split (2 strong buy, 8 buy, 13 hold). That distribution and the modest gap between current price and mean target indicate a largely-priced income name, not a strong re-rating candidate. We score valuation at 5.5: the discount is defensible on quality, but there is no independent catalyst to reward the cheapness.

## Competitive Landscape

ONEOK competes with the large-cap midstream complex — Energy Transfer, Enterprise Products, Kinder Morgan, Williams, MPLX, Targa, Plains. The 2023-25 strategy added scale, basin diversity, and a growing export/LPG angle, which strengthens the moat's breadth and connects more supply to more demand centers. The integrated network across four major producing regions plus the Gulf Coast is genuinely hard to replicate and generates durable, largely contracted cash flows.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on the export pivot, but two things temper the score. First, the recent quarter showed the contracted G&P base contracting while the optimization/marketing line carried the beat, which is not the profile of a franchise widening its fee-based moat. Second, Truist's flag that new Permian gas pipeline capacity could compress the location differentials driving optimization earnings is a genuine competitive-supply threat to the segment that outperformed. The relative underperformance versus peers on 5-year TSR reinforces that the roll-up has not yet translated scale into superior per-share economics. We score competitive position 6.3: a solid, diversified network with real scale, but with a share/margin trajectory that is defensive rather than clearly gaining.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Leverage and continuous refinancing dependence.** $34B debt, $172M cash, chronic working-capital deficits, and reliance on CP/revolver. A downgrade below investment grade would raise borrowing costs and could impair CP access. Interest expense is already climbing. The 5.0x covenant step-down after June 30, 2026 tightens the window against current 4.2x leverage.
2. **Integration/execution risk from serial M&A.** Three transformational deals in three years, plus 2025 tuck-ins. $8.1B goodwill + $2.9B intangibles create impairment exposure if volumes or multiples deteriorate. The new $1.2B term loan is explicitly available for "acquisitions, mergers," so M&A appetite may not be finished — relevant for a company already at 4.2x.
3. **Commodity/volume sensitivity despite the fee-based model.** POP contracts, optimization/marketing, and location/product differentials retain real exposure. Q1 2026 already showed G&P EBITDA declining on lower NGL/gas prices, plus a $60M Powder Springs equity-method impairment.
4. **Producer/basin dependence and spread compression.** Throughput requires continual new drilling to offset well declines, and incremental Permian takeaway could narrow the very spreads that drove recent upside.
5. **Regulatory reversibility.** The methane-fee suspension and endangerment-finding rescission are tailwinds subject to litigation and political reversal.

These risks are largely known and reflected in the peer discount, which is why we score risk_factors at 5.5 rather than lower. The idiosyncratic tail — a downgrade or a spread-compression-driven earnings miss — is real but not imminent given IG ratings and covenant headroom.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 32.0% against 27.0% trailing realized vol, a modestly rich spread (66th percentile within the current chain) with a flat term structure (near 32.0% ≈ far 31.8%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a single catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced at -1.2% and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.60 to 0.46 since mid-June, a mild reduction in downside positioning. IV rose 4.4pp as the price gained 3.4%, which reads as demand for optionality into a rising tape rather than defensive hedging. Nothing in the options market challenges the income-hold thesis; the balanced skew is consistent with a stable, fully-priced name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($105-113).** De-leveraging from 4.2x proceeds smoothly toward the 5.0x step-down, the integration synergies from EnLink/Magellan crystallize, the Texas City LPG terminal and Eiger Express advance on schedule for 2028, and export demand keeps NGL/LPG volumes and spreads firm. If OKE closes even part of its ~0.6x P/E discount to the peer median as per-share cash flow visibility improves, the multiple could expand toward 18-19x on ~$6.20+ EPS, supporting a $105-113 range alongside the ~4.6% yield. This requires the market to reward the roll-up's per-share economics, which it has not done to date.

**Bear case ($78-84).** New Permian pipeline capacity compresses the location differentials that carried the Q1 beat, the contracted G&P base keeps softening on weak NGL/gas prices, an additional debt-funded acquisition pushes leverage against the tightening covenant, and a rating agency turns cautious. Combined with a higher-for-longer rate path lifting refinancing costs, the multiple could compress toward 13x on flat-to-lower EPS, a $78-84 range that roughly matches the analyst target floor of $84.

The most likely path sits between these: a stable ~4.6%-yielding income name trading near its current peer-discount multiple, with returns dominated by the dividend rather than re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (which will test whether the optimization-driven Q1 beat repeats or fades as spreads normalize) and the June 30, 2026 covenant step-down to 5.0x.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net leverage declines meaningfully below 4.0x while the dividend is maintained, demonstrating self-funded de-leveraging without further equity dilution.
- Two consecutive quarters of contracted fee-based (G&P + NGL) EBITDA growth, showing the base business is expanding rather than relying on volatile optimization capture.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive (>+3% over 90 days) alongside a re-rating catalyst such as clarity on 2028 JV contributions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A ratings-agency shift to negative outlook or a downgrade below investment grade, which would raise CP/refinancing costs materially.
- A new debt-funded acquisition that pushes leverage above the 5.0x covenant threshold.
- Dividend coverage deteriorates such that operating cash flow no longer comfortably exceeds dividends plus maintenance capex, or the payout is cut.

## Conclusion

ONEOK is a well-run, investment-grade midstream operator with a diversified, hard-to-replicate network and a well-covered dividend that provides genuine absolute support. The recovery from $64 to $93.52 is fundamentally warranted by the guidance raise and dividend increase. But the case for owning more than for the income is thin: the recent beat leaned on volatile spread capture while the contracted base declined, forward EBITDA growth is a low ~3%, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, leverage is elevated at 4.2x with a covenant step-down approaching, and the peer-discount multiple reflects real structural constraints rather than a catalyzed mispricing. The prior HOLD sequence and declining score trajectory (6.8 → 5.6) are confirmed, not reversed, by this data.

We maintain **HOLD** with an income-hold rationale. Own OKE for the yield and the defensive, contracted cash-flow profile; do not expect the multiple to re-rate absent evidence that de-leveraging and contracted fee growth restore per-share value creation. The current price sits modestly below the analyst mean target, reinforcing that the stock is roughly fairly valued as an income holding.
