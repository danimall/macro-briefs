**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Floor & Decor is a warehouse-format specialty retailer of hard-surface flooring (tile, laminate/vinyl, wood, natural stone) and adjacent installation categories, operating 270 large-format stores across 39 states with a long-term target of 500+ U.S. locations. The investment question is narrow and well-defined: revenue has grown to a record ~$4.68B in FY2025, yet operating income (~$270M) and net income (~$209M) sit roughly 30-32% below the FY2022 peak. This is operating de-leverage: the company has been opening stores faster than comparable-store sales can absorb the incremental SG&A, occupancy, and depreciation, against a backdrop of two consecutive years of negative comps driven by frozen existing-home turnover and elevated mortgage rates.

The stock has fallen ~38% from its 52-week high of $92.40 to roughly $46. The prior coverage trajectory moved from HOLD to AVOID to AVOID as the comp picture deteriorated through Q4 FY2025 and Q1 FY2026 (comps −4.8% then −3.7%, EPS down ~18% in Q4'25 and ~12% in Q1'26). That deterioration is real and the FY2026 guide (comps −2% to +1%) is being tested early. The case for continuing an AVOID, however, runs into a problem the data forces me to confront directly: the decline reflects multiple compression on a stock that traded above 25x at its highs, not fundamental impairment. Gross margin is expanding (43.6%, +30bps), funded debt is trivial ($198M term loan, $0 drawn on the $800M ABL, ~$910M liquidity), credit is BB/Ba3 stable, and roughly half the store base is immature. The damage is below the gross line, in deleverage that mechanically reverses if comps inflect.

A cluster of bottoming signals has emerged that a continued bearish call cannot simply set aside: CEO Paulsen and the new CFO made open-market purchases in early May 2026 at ~$48-50, roughly 30% below their own February grant strikes of $68.34; the board authorized a $400M (~7.6% of market cap) buyback funded without new debt and framed as an intrinsic-value signal; options stress de-escalated materially (P/C OI ratio collapsed from 80.27 to 14.03, ATM IV from 88.5% to 77.7% on a +6.5% price move); consensus saw a strongSell downgrade; and a contrarian institution initiated a ~$30M position. Against deteriorating comps, these argue the trough may be visible. The expected return is balanced with an asymmetric upside tail. That is a HOLD, not an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2000 and headquartered in Atlanta, Floor & Decor sells hard-surface flooring and installation materials through a warehouse format averaging ~76,000 sq ft, carrying ~4,200 SKUs and ~$2.7M of inventory at cost per store. The model rests on three pillars: the deepest in-stock job-lot assortment in the category (far exceeding the floor space big-box home-improvement allocates to hard surface), direct sourcing from 240+ manufacturers and quarries globally (largest single supplier ~10% of net sales) supporting an everyday-low-price strategy, and a roughly 50/50 mix of professional installer (Pro) and DIY/homeowner sales supported by a dedicated Pro sales force and free design services.

The structural growth thesis combines a target of at least 500 U.S. stores, a secular carpet-to-hard-surface conversion in residential flooring, and market-share gains in a fragmented industry. Roughly 50% of stores are under five years old, which in normal cycles is a tailwind as immature stores ramp toward mature productivity. The company also runs a commercial-surfaces subsidiary, Spartan Surfaces (acquired 2021). It pays no dividend.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year picture frames the entire thesis:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $4,264M | $4,414M | $4,456M | $4,684M |
| Operating income | $397M | $321M | $256M | $270M |
| Operating margin | 9.3% | 7.3% | 5.8% | 5.8% |
| Net income | $298M | $246M | $206M | $209M |
| Comparable store sales | — | — | (7.1)% | (1.8)% |
| Stores | ~191 | ~221 | 251 | 270 |

FY2025 revenue grew 5.1%, but the entire increase came from new stores (+$305.7M non-comp); comps were −1.8% (transactions −3.5%, partly offset by +1.8% average ticket). The earnings compression is the signature of a retailer building into a demand trough: new stores add occupancy, depreciation, and labor faster than comp sales can cover them, so operating margin has fallen from 9.3% to 5.8%.

The damage is not at the gross line. Gross margin rose 30bps to 43.6% in FY2025, helped by lower supply-chain/freight costs and direct sourcing. The compression is in SG&A deleverage (37.8% of sales) and rising depreciation from the store and distribution-center build-out. This distinction matters for the investment case: gross-margin erosion would signal pricing power loss or competitive displacement (structural); SG&A deleverage from over-building into a soft cycle is mechanical and reverses when comps recover and stores mature.

Cash flow is the genuine near-term watch item. Operating cash flow fell to $382M in FY2025 from $603M in FY2024, driven substantially by a $115M use of cash in trade payables (versus a $122M source in 2024), a working-capital swing that distorts the comparison. Capex remained heavy at $318M, leaving FCF of just $64M (1.4% margin). FY2026 capex is guided to $250-300M, moderating from the $447M FY2024 peak. With a $400M buyback and continued expansion against thin FCF, the company will draw down cash or tap the ABL unless operating cash flow recovers. The FY2026 OCF trajectory is the variable to monitor.

The balance sheet is not the risk most headlines imply. Funded debt is modest: $198M term loan, $0 drawn on the $800M ABL, ~$910M total liquidity, current ratio 1.33. The real leverage is operating leases: ~$1.79B of liabilities, growing fast with the build-out (lease liabilities rose ~$288M YoY). The Altman Z of 2.19 (grey zone) reflects depressed profitability ratios (EBIT/assets ~4.9%), not insolvency risk. S&P BB / Moody's Ba3, both stable, is consistent with a leveraged but solvent specialty retailer in a cyclical trough.

The earnings surprise record shows the inflection: 3 beats then a miss, with Q1 FY2026 EPS of $0.37 missing by 12.2%. Estimate revisions are broadly negative: current-year EPS revised to $1.90 from $2.07 ninety days ago (−8.2%), next-year to $2.17 from $2.44 (−11.0%). The 30-day revisions have flattened (roughly 0%), which suggests the bulk of the downward reset may have already occurred, but momentum is negative, not positive.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is contracting on a comp basis and flattered on a total-revenue basis by store openings. Comps went −7.1% (FY2024), −1.8% (FY2025), then re-accelerated downward to −4.8% (Q4'25) and −3.7% (Q1'26). The FY2026 guide of comps −2% to +1% with EPS of $1.98-$2.18 (boosted ~$0.08 by a 53rd week) was effectively trimmed after the soft Q1 print, with several sources citing a lowered ~$1.83-$2.08 range and full-year guidance ~1.9% below consensus.

The medium-term growth case is entirely conditional on housing. FND's demand is tied to existing-home turnover and discretionary renovation spend, both suppressed by elevated mortgage rates and weak existing-home sales. The 10-K newly and explicitly flags that post-2022 store vintages carry "lower first-year sales and initial returns" and "higher construction, occupancy, and operating costs," undercutting the unit-economics half of the 500-store narrative for as long as the cycle stays frozen. The company continues to open ~20 stores per year and is adding distribution capacity (Seattle DC opened, Baltimore-area planned).

The operating-leverage mechanism cuts both ways and is the crux of the bull case: with ~50% of stores immature and gross margin holding, a return to positive comps would flow through with disproportionate force as fixed SG&A and depreciation get covered. Normalized mid-cycle EPS of $3.00-$3.50 is plausible in a housing recovery, versus ~$1.90 today. I weight the growth dimension below midpoint because the timing of that recovery is genuinely uncertain and the near-term trajectory is still deteriorating. This is a candidate for an earnings inflection, not one in progress.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$46, FND trades at roughly 24x trailing EPS of $1.92 and ~24x the trimmed FY2026 consensus. On peer medians (P/E 21.2), it screens slightly rich, though the consumer-discretionary peer set is heterogeneous (Brunswick negative, Gentex 13.8x, Acushnet 31.8x) and offers weak read-across. The trailing multiple overstates richness because earnings are at a cyclical trough: on normalized mid-cycle EPS of $3.00-$3.50, the stock sits at ~13-15x, which is reasonable-to-cheap for a share-gaining specialty retailer with a long unit-growth runway.

The fair characterization of the ~38% drawdown is that it is rational repricing of a stock that carried a 25x+ multiple at its highs into a deteriorating comp environment, not the discounting of terminal impairment. The audit challenge is correct on this point: an AVOID requires a specific, falsifiable mispricing the market is missing, and I cannot identify one. The forward multiple is not compressed below the growth rate (it roughly matches a flat-to-down near-term EPS), so this is not a screaming-cheap inflection name. It is also not stretched against normalized earning power. The valuation is approximately fair with an asymmetric payoff: limited downside to ~$37-40 (analyst low $37, ~13-20% below current) versus a fat upside tail to $70+ on a normalized $3.00-$3.50 base at ~14-20x if housing thaws. That asymmetry, combined with no identifiable present mispricing, is the textbook profile of a HOLD.

Analyst targets bracket current price: low $37, mean $53.91, median $53.50, high $75. Consensus mean ~11% above current is consistent with a market that views the name as fairly valued with modest upside, not impaired.

## Competitive Landscape

FND's competitive position is stable-to-improving on trajectory, even as the cycle pressures absolute results. The company claims it is gaining share through the downturn, supported by a holding gross margin that signals it is not buying volume by sacrificing price, the opposite of what a displaced retailer would show. Its structural edge versus big-box (Home Depot, Lowe's allocate limited floor space to hard surface) and fragmented independents is breadth of in-stock job-lot assortment, direct sourcing, and Pro focus.

The honest counterweights: barriers to entry are "relatively low," larger and better-capitalized competitors are explicitly flagged as a risk, and suppliers could integrate forward. There is no evidence of secular share loss; the demand weakness is cyclical (frozen housing turnover), and the carpet-to-hard-surface conversion plus aging housing stock remain intact long-term tailwinds. I score competitive position at midpoint-plus: a genuine moat in assortment and Pro relationships, gaining share, but in a category with modest entry barriers and powerful adjacent competitors.

## Risk Assessment

**Housing-cycle demand (primary, partly priced).** Two years of negative comps trace directly to elevated mortgage rates and weak existing-home sales. With rates elevated, the trough could extend rather than resolve in 2026. Much of this is in the price after the decline, but a deeper or longer housing freeze would pressure both comps and new-store returns further.

**Deteriorating new-store economics (newly emphasized in 10-K).** Spending more to open stores that earn less is a self-inflicted (if strategic) drag that only reverses with the cycle. The market is rightly skeptical of the 500-store narrative's near-term unit economics.

**Tariffs (escalating 2025-specific wildcard).** As a majority-import retailer, FND faces both higher COGS and tariff-driven demand softening. Gross margin has held so far (mitigation via vendor negotiation, country diversification, selective price increases), making this the key swing variable for 2026 margins and a driver of the "renovation costs climbing" demand narrative. UFLPA enforcement and China-Taiwan exposure add tail risk.

**Leadership transition.** First CEO change since 2012 (Taylor to Executive Chairman, Brad Paulsen to CEO, Trevor Lang retired March 1, 2025) flagged as an execution risk in the 10-K. The transition is genuine, though insider buying by the incoming team partly offsets the concern.

**Thin FCF against a buyback.** FY2025 FCF against the buyback and capex will draw cash or tap the ABL absent OCF recovery. Watch FY2026 OCF; the FY2025 figure was depressed by a payables swing.

**ERP implementation** (phased go-live through 2027) and operating-lease obligations round out the idiosyncratic risk set.

### Options Market Signal

The options complex shows acute near-term stress that is de-escalating, which fits the bottoming read. Near-term ATM IV is 77.7% (90th percentile in-chain) versus 45.5% one-year HV, with the term structure in backwardation (77.7% near, 59.0% long-dated), implying a ±9.8% move on the nearest expiry and pricing imminent event risk. The directional skew is call-heavy (−6.0%), not put-heavy, which is unusual for a name in a downtrend and inconsistent with a market positioned for further collapse. Since mid-May the P/C OI ratio and ATM IV have collapsed while the stock rose, a coordinated de-stressing that argues the peak-fear point is behind. Short interest at 12.0% of float (4 days to cover) is elevated but not squeeze-territory, and the recent consensus strongSell downgrade (2→1) trims the bearish tail. The options picture confirms balanced-to-improving risk/reward, not a setup for an active short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The housing freeze is cyclical, not structural. As mortgage rates eventually ease and existing-home turnover thaws, comps inflect positive, ~50% immature stores ramp, and operating leverage reverses hard against a holding gross margin. Normalized mid-cycle EPS reaches $3.00-$3.50; at ~14-20x the stock reaches $50-$70+. Management's open-market buying ~30% below grant strikes plus a self-funded buyback signal insider conviction that the price is below intrinsic value. Share gains through the downturn position FND to compound when demand returns. Bull target ~$70.

**Bear case.** The housing freeze persists into 2027, comps stay negative, and FND keeps spending capex and SG&A for sub-par returns while tariffs compress the resilient gross margin. New-store economics stay impaired, FCF stays thin, and the buyback drains liquidity into a falling tape. The 24x trailing multiple compresses further toward depressed peers. Bear target ~$37-40 (−13% to −20%).

The expected-value balance is genuinely two-sided. The downside is contained (~13-20%) and largely already realized in the decline; the upside tail is unusually fat and supported by credible insider and corporate-action signals. I cannot articulate a specific present mispricing that justifies a bearish directional bet, and the bottoming signals are too coherent to dismiss. That balance is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026, ~late July/August 2026) and the trajectory of mortgage rates / existing-home sales data are the natural reassessment points. The thesis resolves on the timing of housing recovery, a 6-18 month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Comparable-store sales inflect to flat-or-positive for two consecutive quarters, confirming the operating-leverage reversal.
- A sustained downward move in mortgage rates accompanied by a recovery in existing-home sales/turnover data.
- Stabilizing-to-positive EPS estimate revisions (the 30-day series has already flattened to ~0%; a turn to positive would be decisive), ideally with the stock still near the low-$40s to low-$50s.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin breaks below ~42% on tariff pass-through failure or competitive pricing pressure; this would convert a cyclical deleverage story into a structural-margin story.
- Comps deteriorate beyond the −4% to −5% Q4'25/Q1'26 range, signaling demand erosion past a normal housing trough.
- FY2026 operating cash flow falls materially below the FY2025 level while the buyback and capex continue, forcing meaningful ABL draws and balance-sheet deterioration.

## Conclusion

Floor & Decor is a structurally advantaged specialty retailer enduring a cyclical earnings trough that the market has rationally repriced. The prior AVOID rating was anchored to a deteriorating comp trajectory that remains real, but the same data set now shows a contained downside (~13-20%), a fat upside tail on normalized earning power ($3.00-$3.50 EPS), a holding gross margin, modest funded debt, and a coherent cluster of bottoming signals: C-suite open-market buys ~30% below February grant strikes, a self-funded buyback, options de-stressing on a rising price, a consensus strongSell downgrade, and a contrarian institutional initiation. I cannot identify a specific, falsifiable mispricing the market is missing, and the thesis reduces to a bet on the timing of housing recovery rather than a present overvaluation. I rate FND **HOLD**, reassessing at Q2 FY2026 earnings and on the path of mortgage rates and home-turnover data.
