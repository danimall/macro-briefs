**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Amentum is one of the largest pure-play U.S. government engineering and technical-services contractors, created in September 2024 via a Reverse Morris Trust combining Jacobs' Critical Mission Solutions business with Legacy Amentum. The company generated $14.39B in FY2025 revenue and ~$516M of free cash flow, and it has spent its first full year as a public company deleveraging and integrating. The stock has fallen from roughly $36 in late January 2026 to ~$21 today, a ~40% decline that the trailing multiple alone obscures.

The central analytical tension is a quality of cash flows versus quality of growth debate. GAAP net income of $66M (0.4% margin) in FY2025 makes the business look distressed, but that figure is suppressed by $479M of purchase-accounting intangible amortization and elevated interest expense. Adjusted EBITDA of ~$1.1B, FCF of ~$516M, and reaffirmed FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.25+ tell a different story: at ~$21 and a ~$5.2B market cap, AMTM trades near 9x forward adjusted EPS with a ~10% FCF yield. GAAP EPS is inflecting upward (Q2 FY2026 net income to common $54M versus $4M a year prior) as CMS backlog intangibles amortize off and interest expense falls.

Against this improving cash profile sit real headwinds that justify caution rather than conviction. Revenue is flat-to-down (H1 FY2026 -2.8% YoY), consensus estimates are drifting lower rather than higher, only $6.9B of the $47.8B backlog is funded (14%), and the business carries ~3x net leverage plus concentrated private-equity ownership. Federal budget disruption (three separate government shutdowns in FY2026 and DOGE-driven efficiency pressure) is a genuine swing factor. The most identifiable catalyst, the expiration of the Reverse Morris Trust restrictions that block buybacks and M&A around September 2026, is still roughly two to three months out with an unproven payoff. We rate AMTM a HOLD. The risk/reward is favorable on price but lacks a dated trigger and shows no positive estimate-revision momentum to force the re-rating.

## Company Overview

Amentum operates through two segments serving primarily the U.S. federal government (~81% of FY2025 revenue as prime or subcontractor):

- **Global Engineering Solutions (GES)** — ~$8.85B FY2025 revenue. Large-scale environmental and nuclear remediation (DOE sites including Hanford, Savannah River, Oak Ridge, Idaho; UK Nuclear Decommissioning Authority/Sellafield), platform engineering, sustainment, and supply chain. Heavily international, with UK and Australia/AUKUS exposure. Segment adjusted EBITDA margin ~7.5%.
- **Digital Solutions (DS)** — ~$5.54B FY2025 revenue. Intelligence analytics, space systems (NASA COMET, JETS II, Artemis), cybersecurity, and next-generation IT for the Intelligence Community, DoD, and NASA. Segment adjusted EBITDA margin ~7.9%.

The business is asset-light (capex of only ~$27M against $14.4B of revenue) with ~50,000 employees across 70+ countries and a $47.8B total backlog as of April 3, 2026. Its defensible niches are DOE/UK nuclear remediation and NASA/space operations, where security clearances, site-specific incumbency, and high switching costs limit direct competition. The CMS merger roughly doubled the company's scale and was designed to let Amentum bid the largest full-lifecycle programs against Booz Allen, CACI, KBR, Leidos, Parsons, Peraton, SAIC, and V2X.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale distortion from the merger dominates the year-over-year picture.** FY2025 revenue of $14.39B was up 71.6% from $8.39B in FY2024, almost entirely from the CMS combination rather than organic growth. Underlying growth is flat-to-down: H1 FY2026 revenue fell 2.8% YoY, and Q2 FY2026 revenue of $3.48B was down 0.4%.

**GAAP profitability understates economics.** FY2025 operating income was $480M (3.3% margin) and net income to common just $66M (0.4% margin). The gap between GAAP and cash economics is the crux of the story:
- Intangible amortization of $479M in FY2025 is a non-cash purchase-accounting drag that is already declining (Q2 amortization $94M versus $120M a year prior as CMS backlog intangibles fully amortize).
- FY2025 FCF of $516M (3.6% margin) on $543M operating cash flow demonstrates cash conversion that GAAP net income masks.
- Q2 FY2026 net income to common jumped to $54M (from $4M) and H1 to $98M (from $16M), driven by lower amortization, reduced interest expense from deleveraging, and SG&A synergies (SG&A fell to 3.6% of revenue from 4.2%).

**Contract mix is shifting toward higher-margin fixed-price** (28-29% of revenue versus 22-24% a year ago) and away from cost-plus (57% versus 64%). This supports margins but adds cost-overrun exposure, which is amplified by the nuclear/environmental work in GES.

**Deleveraging is the core capital-allocation story.** Total debt has been reduced to $3.99B (April 2026) from ~$4.6B post-merger. Management made $722M of voluntary Term Loan prepayments in FY2025, funded partly by the $360M sale of Rapid Solutions to Lockheed Martin. Net leverage is roughly 3x EBITDA with D/E ~1.5x. The April 24, 2026 refinancing split the Term Loan into a $1.4B Term Loan A (SOFR + 1.25-2.00%) and a $1.591B Term Loan B (SOFR + 1.75%) and upsized the Revolver by $150M, extending maturities and lowering spreads. This de-risks the capital structure and removes the most pressing near-term financing concern.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.42 (grey zone) reflects low EBIT/assets (thin GAAP margins) and heavy goodwill ($5.7B, ~50% of assets), not liquidity stress; the current ratio is 1.32. There is no dividend and no buyback, both currently prohibited by the Reverse Morris Trust structure.

**FY2026 guidance (reaffirmed May 12, 2026):** Revenue $13.95-14.3B, Adjusted EBITDA $1.1-1.14B, adjusted diluted EPS $2.25+, and FCF $525-575M. The guidance implies flat-to-slightly-down revenue; the story is margin, FCF, and deleveraging, not growth.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the weakest leg of the thesis. Organic revenue is flat-to-declining, and FY2026 guidance codifies this: the midpoint implies no top-line growth. Part of the reported shrinkage is benign: contracts deliberately moved from consolidated to unconsolidated JVs shift revenue off the top line into the equity-earnings line (which rose to $45M in H1), and divestitures (Rapid Solutions) further reduce the base. Backlog grew to $47.8B, with funded backlog rising to $6.9B from $5.6B in the 10-K. But even adjusting for these, the underlying growth rate is not compelling.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration. Current-year consensus EPS of $2.42 has drifted from $2.43 thirty days ago; next-quarter estimates fell 1.3%. This is negative-to-flat earnings momentum, the opposite of the revision velocity that would signal a fundamental turn. The earnings-surprise record is a modest positive (four consecutive beats of 2-4%), but the beats are on decelerating estimates, which limits their forward-looking value.

The industry backdrop is bifurcated and favors Amentum's mix. Defense/intel/space demand is a tailwind (the FY2027 request implies +43% defense spending toward $1.5T), while DOGE-driven efficiency pressure and non-defense discretionary cuts (-21% in the FY2026 request) pressure civilian-agency work. Amentum is more defense/intel/DOE-weighted than pure civilian IT peers, which is favorable relative to the sector. But that favorable mix has not yet translated into positive organic growth or rising estimates, so the growth score reflects potential rather than delivery.

The clearest path to per-share value creation over the next 12-18 months is margin/FCF improvement plus deleveraging rather than revenue growth: a mechanical GAAP-EPS recovery as amortization rolls off and interest falls, not a demand-driven expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

On adjusted metrics, AMTM screens cheap. At ~$21 and ~$5.2B market cap, the stock trades near 9x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($2.25+) and roughly 8x EV/EBITDA on ~$1.1B Adjusted EBITDA, with a ~10% FCF yield. The trailing P/E of 34.5x is meaningless here; it capitalizes GAAP earnings depressed by non-cash amortization, and GAAP EPS is inflecting upward.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The Diversified Support Services peer median P/E is 21.9x, but the more relevant government-services comps are SAIC (12.9x) and KBR (10.4x). On an adjusted basis, AMTM's ~9x sits below both, which the market appears to justify on higher leverage (~3x net versus lower-levered peers), flat organic growth, and the PE-sponsor overhang. Analyst targets span $22 (low) to $40 (high) with a $30.5 median; the current price sits below the entire meaningful target range, at the analyst floor.

The discipline point from experience is that cheapness alone is anti-predictive; it flags value traps as often as opportunities, and it sorts the wrong way more often than not. AMTM's low multiple is not a buy signal by itself. The case for the multiple re-rating requires an independent catalyst: the September 2026 expiration of RMT restrictions unlocking capital return, or a turn in estimate-revision velocity, or funded-backlog stabilization proving the DOGE/shutdown drag is temporary. Absent one of those, the discount can persist. We assign a moderate valuation score: the FCF yield provides genuine downside support and the asymmetry favors upside, but there is no forcing mechanism to close the gap, and the estimate revisions are pointing the wrong direction.

Cycle-adjusted, the GAAP-EPS turn is real but is a mechanical accounting recovery, not a revenue-driven earnings acceleration. It does not clear the bar for treating analyst targets as stale floors, because the growth is not accelerating; it is flat with improving conversion.

## Competitive Landscape

Amentum occupies the consolidating mid-tier of government services. Its differentiated positions in DOE/UK nuclear environmental remediation and NASA/space operations face fewer direct competitors (BWXT, Fluor, Bechtel, AtkinsRealis on environmental; Leidos, KBR on space/IT) and carry high switching costs from clearances and site incumbency. Scale and breadth as a full-lifecycle prime are the strategic rationale for the CMS merger.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving. Backlog grew to $47.8B and net-favorable contract adjustments were consistent ($45M H1 FY2026 versus $28M prior year), suggesting disciplined estimating and execution as fixed-price exposure rises. But market share is not visibly expanding, organic revenue is flat, and the federal-services procurement environment is being reshaped by fixed-price mandates and budget-efficiency reviews that redistribute value away from civilian/DOE-adjacent work. Amentum's defense/intel/DOE weighting is favorable within that reshaping, which supports a competitive-position score at the midpoint. The 14%-funded backlog is a reminder that the headline backlog figure overstates the visibility of that competitive position; most of it is subject to appropriations, recompetes, and de-obligations.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are specific and material:

1. **Federal concentration and budget volatility.** 81% U.S. federal exposure into an unusually disrupted year: a government shutdown October 2 to November 12, 2025, a partial shutdown January 31 to February 3, 2026, and a DHS-specific shutdown February 14 to April 30, 2026. Management calls the impact "not material to date," but shutdowns directly pressured H1 revenue. DOGE efficiency drives and non-defense discretionary cuts are a live swing factor.

2. **Thin funded backlog.** Only $6.9B of $47.8B backlog (14%) is funded; remaining performance obligations are $10.1B. The backlog's realizability depends on appropriations and recompetes.

3. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** $5.7B goodwill plus $1.77B intangibles equal ~65% of total assets. A persistently depressed share price (market cap approaching book) could trigger impairment testing; a $186M goodwill impairment occurred pre-merger in FY2023.

4. **Reverse Morris Trust restrictions and Jacobs indemnity.** Section 355 restrictions block buybacks, M&A, and change-of-control actions until around September 2026 and create a potentially "substantial" indemnity obligation to Jacobs. This constrains strategic flexibility near-term but converts to a potential catalyst upon expiration.

5. **Controlling PE shareholder.** Concentrated ownership with board-nomination rights (up to 5 of 13 directors), corporate-opportunity waivers, and anti-takeover provisions. Secondary-share-sale overhang persists; the RMT lock-up runs to roughly September 2026.

6. **Leverage and rate exposure.** ~3x net leverage with a large floating-rate component; every 1% rate move is ~$15M of interest, though $1.5B of swaps hedges the Term Loan and the April refinancing lowered spreads.

7. **Nuclear/environmental liability.** Price-Anderson Act indemnity dependence and fixed-price cost-overrun risk, amplified by the rising fixed-price mix.

The Q2 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors. On balance, these risks are largely priced given the ~40% drawdown; the market is not ignoring shutdowns, DOGE, or leverage. The impairment and PE-overhang tails are the ones least reflected in a simple FCF-yield view.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 61.6% is rich versus 43.6% one-year historical vol (18.1-point spread) and sits in the 85th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (61.6% near versus 56.5% long-dated); the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the August 11 earnings date. Put/call skew is roughly balanced at -1.8%, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.35 is not bearish, though the put/call OI ratio of 1.15 leans mildly defensive. ATM IV has collapsed from 119.2% on June 17 to 61.6%, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 4.10 to 1.15, so the extreme bearish positioning that characterized mid-June has substantially unwound. The implied move into the next expiry is ±15.1%, framing a two-sided binary around the Q3 print. This is a market pricing event risk without a strong directional bias, consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$30-35, matching the median analyst target and prior highs).** GAAP EPS continues inflecting as amortization rolls off and interest falls; the multiple re-rates toward government-services peers as the market recognizes ~$2.25+ adjusted EPS and ~10% FCF yield. Funded backlog stabilizes, proving the shutdown/DOGE drag was transient. The September 2026 RMT expiration unlocks a buyback or dividend, providing a re-rating trigger and a floor. Defense/intel/DOE tailwinds convert to organic growth in FY2027. On $2.78 next-year adjusted EPS at 12-13x, this supports the low-to-mid $30s.

**Bear Case (~$15-17, roughly 20-27% downside).** DOGE efficiency drives and non-defense discretionary cuts prove structural rather than cyclical, pressuring GES/DS recompetes and de-obligating unfunded backlog. Flat revenue persists, estimate revisions keep drifting lower, and a depressed share price triggers goodwill impairment testing. The PE sponsor sells into weakness, adding an overhang. On stagnant $2.25 adjusted EPS at 7x with sentiment impaired, the stock retests the mid-teens.

The base case sits between these: a value-plus-time hold where equity value accrues mechanically as debt shrinks and amortization rolls off, but where the absence of a dated catalyst and negative estimate momentum caps near-term upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings on August 11, 2026, and the RMT restriction expiration around September 2026 are the two natural checkpoints. The GFY2027 appropriations process is a slower-burn input.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Funded backlog stabilizes or grows for two consecutive quarters and current-year estimate revisions turn positive (a reversal of the current -0.1% drift), confirming the DOGE/shutdown drag is temporary.
- Management announces a capital-return program (buyback or dividend) upon RMT expiration in Q4 CY2026, providing a re-rating catalyst and a floor.
- A pullback into the mid-to-high teens with FCF guidance intact, widening the FCF yield above ~12% and improving the asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge or explicit impairment-test disclosure, signaling the market-cap-below-book risk has crystallized.
- FY2026 FCF guidance cut below the $525M floor, breaking the deleveraging thesis.
- A large PE-sponsor secondary sale, or evidence that unfunded backlog is being de-obligated at an accelerating pace (recompete losses or program de-scopings disclosed in filings).

The below-floor valuation argues against actively avoiding the name. But the lack of a near-term forcing mechanism and the negative estimate-revision direction argue against a BUY. This is a hold-to-catalyst position.

## Conclusion

Amentum is a de-leveraging, cash-generative government-services business whose GAAP optics understate its economics. The deleveraging is real (debt down to $3.99B, $722M of voluntary prepayments, a completed refinancing at lower spreads), FCF of ~$516M supports a ~10% yield, and GAAP EPS is inflecting upward as merger amortization rolls off. At ~$21 after a ~40% decline against reaffirmed guidance, the price-based risk/reward has improved, and the stock trades below the entire meaningful analyst target range.

The offsetting reality is that this is a value-plus-time story without a dated catalyst. Organic revenue is flat-to-down, estimate revisions are drifting lower rather than accelerating, only 14% of backlog is funded, and the business carries ~3x leverage, heavy goodwill impairment risk if the price stays depressed, and a controlling PE overhang. The most identifiable trigger, the September 2026 RMT expiration unlocking capital return, is real but roughly two to three months out with an unproven payoff. The current data does not clear the higher bar required to change the HOLD call in either direction: the cash quality is improving but the growth quality is not, and the valuation discount lacks a forcing mechanism to close.

We maintain a **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale. Investors owning AMTM for the deleveraging and margin-inflection thesis have a reasonable floor from the FCF yield and analyst targets; new capital should wait for funded-backlog stabilization plus a positive estimate-revision turn, or for the capital-return unlock to materialize, before treating the discount as an actionable BUY.
