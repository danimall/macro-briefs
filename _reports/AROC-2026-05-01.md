## Executive Summary

Archrock is the largest outsourced natural gas compression services provider in the United States, operating 5,319 units totaling 4.8 million horsepower at 95-96% utilization. The business model generates recurring, fee-based revenue that is structurally less cyclical than upstream E&P — Archrock collects fixed monthly fees regardless of commodity prices, and its customers supply the fuel gas. Revenue has grown 76% over three years to $1.49B, while net income has expanded from $44M to $322M (7.3x increase), driven by extraordinary operating leverage as contract operations gross margins expanded 600 bps to 73% in 2025.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a dominant competitive position that continues to widen through acquisition-driven consolidation ($1.35B deployed in 18 months); (2) structural demand tailwinds from LNG export growth (9-10% annually), AI data center gas-fired power demand, and the secular outsourcing trend; and (3) a valuation at ~21x trailing earnings that is meaningfully below the peer median of 37x despite superior business quality metrics. The stock has rallied 65% over the past year to $38.75, now sitting at its 52-week high and slightly below the analyst consensus mean target of $39.67, which tempers near-term upside expectations.

The primary risk is leverage: $2.4B in debt against ~$901M Adjusted EBITDA (2.7x) in an environment where the capital-intensive acquisition strategy demands continued strong utilization and pricing. A CFO transition during a critical post-acquisition integration period adds execution risk. However, the debt is well-structured with no near-term maturities, covenant compliance is comfortable, and the January 2026 $800M note issuance at 6.0% demonstrates continued capital market access. At current levels, the risk/reward favors accumulation with tight sizing given the stock's proximity to its highs.

## Company Overview

Archrock operates through two segments: Contract Operations (85% of revenue), where the company owns, deploys, operates, and maintains compression equipment at customer sites under 12-60 month initial-term contracts; and Aftermarket Services (15% of revenue), providing parts, maintenance, and overhaul services to customers who own their equipment.

Approximately 75% of operating horsepower is deployed in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale. 60% of the fleet serves midstream natural gas gathering; 40% serves gas lift applications for oil production. The average unit stays at a customer location for approximately 6 years, creating significant switching costs despite theoretical 30-day contract termination provisions.

The company has undergone rapid consolidation: the TOPS Acquisition ($983M, closed August 2024) and NGCS Acquisition ($349M, closed May 2025) added over 1.1 million HP and cemented Archrock's position as the industry's clear scale leader. A smaller disposition of ~155 units (Flowco, August 2025) reflected portfolio optimization, pruning smaller/less-strategic assets.

Revenue growth accelerated to 29% in 2025, driven by acquisition contributions and organic rate increases. Net income grew 87% on 29% revenue growth, and contract operations adjusted gross margin expanded from 67% to 73% in a single year. This margin expansion reflects a fixed-cost business experiencing both volume and pricing tailwinds simultaneously.

Adjusted EBITDA of ~$901M represents a 51% increase over 2024. The implied EBITDA margin of ~60% on contract operations revenue is among the highest in energy services.

### Cash Flow & Capital Intensity

Operating cash flow of $622M in 2025 was strong, but capital expenditures of $502M ($348M growth + $111M maintenance + $43M other) consumed the majority. Free cash flow of $120M (8% FCF margin, 37% FCF/net income conversion) reflects the structural capital intensity of owning a compression fleet.

2026 guidance calls for $400-445M in capex ($250-275M growth, $125-135M maintenance), a ~$60-100M reduction from 2025. With operating cash flow likely growing given the full-year contribution of NGCS and continued rate increases, FCF should improve meaningfully in 2026 — potentially toward $200-250M.

### Balance Sheet & Leverage

Total debt stands at approximately $2.4B against ~$901M Adjusted EBITDA, yielding a leverage ratio of ~2.7x. The debt is well-structured:

- Credit Facility ($1.5B capacity) matures May 2028
- Senior Notes due 2028 and 2032
- $800M 2034 Notes issued January 2026 at 6.0%
- $300M 2027 Notes redeemed November 2025

No significant maturities until 2028. Covenant compliance is comfortable: Total Debt/EBITDA ≤ 5.25x (actual ~2.7x), EBITDA/Interest ≥ 2.5x (actual well above). The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 is a false distress signal — it penalizes the inherently low asset turnover (0.34x) of a $3.7B PP&E fleet generating $1.5B in revenue. The Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 confirms operational health.

Annual interest expense was $165M in 2025 and will likely approach $180-190M in 2026 given the full-year cost of the January 2026 issuance. Interest expense consumes roughly 20% of Adjusted EBITDA.

### Earnings Surprise Track Record

AROC beat estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters, with the most recent quarter (Q4 2025) delivering a 61.3% upside surprise ($0.67 actual vs. $0.42 estimate). The one miss (Q3 2025) was a marginal $0.01 shortfall. This pattern suggests analysts have been consistently underestimating the operating leverage inherent in the business, which supports confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Drivers

Three structural demand vectors support continued compression fleet expansion:

1. **LNG Export Growth:** EIA forecasts 9-10% annual LNG export growth in 2026-2027. Every incremental Bcf/d of LNG export demand requires upstream production, gathering, processing, and associated compression.

2. **AI Data Center Power Demand:** Natural gas-fired power generation for AI data centers is creating incremental demand for gas production and therefore compression. This is a multi-year demand driver that is largely unpriced in consensus estimates for mid-cap energy services companies.

3. **Secular Outsourcing Trend:** Producers continue shifting from owned to contracted compression, mirroring the broader oilfield services trend. This structural share gain occurs independent of commodity prices.

U.S. natural gas production hit a record 107.4 Bcf/d in 2025 (up from 103.0 in 2024), with the EIA forecasting continued modest ~1% growth. However, EIA projects a 3% decline in oil production in 2027, which could reduce gas lift compression demand (40% of Archrock's fleet).

### Acquisition-Driven Growth

The TOPS and NGCS acquisitions were executed at <7x EBITDA — disciplined pricing for compression fleet assets that immediately integrate at 95%+ utilization. With the credit facility expanded to $1.5B and covenant headroom remaining, further bolt-on acquisitions are possible, though management has indicated a shift toward organic fleet investment and deleveraging.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year (FY2026) EPS estimates stand at $2.04, up 8.6% over 90 days but down 1.5% over the last 30 days. Next-year (FY2027) estimates are $2.29, up 2.7% over 90 days but down 1.9% recently. The 90-day upward trend confirms fundamental improvement; the 30-day softening likely reflects the broader oil/gas sector's response to macro concerns. The positive 90-day trajectory is the more informative signal given the Q4 2025 blowout earnings beat.

The valuation discount exists because: (1) AROC carries higher leverage than KGS and other peers at comparable scale, (2) the stock has already rallied 65% from its 52-week low, and (3) it now trades near the analyst consensus mean target of $39.67.

The market is mispricing two elements. First, contract operations margin expansion (600 bps to 73% in 2025) has further to run given continued rate increases and full-year acquisition contributions in 2026. Second, the transition from $502M capex in 2025 to $400-445M guidance for 2026 should produce a step-change improvement in FCF that the current multiple does not reflect.

Base case fair value: 20x FY2027E EPS of $2.29 = $45.80, representing 18% upside. The 20x multiple is conservative relative to peers but accounts for AROC's leverage premium.

At $38.75, the stock sits at its 52-week high and near the analyst mean target ($39.67), limiting the margin of safety. The Q1 2026 earnings report on May 6 is the next catalyst — a beat would likely prompt consensus target revisions upward, while a miss after the Q4 blowout could trigger a 10-15% pullback.

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock's ~4.8M HP fleet makes it the clear #1 in outsourced U.S. natural gas compression. The company's scale advantages include:

- **Geographic density:** 75% of HP concentrated in Permian/Eagle Ford, enabling efficient service logistics and rapid redeployment
- **Large HP specialization:** 74% of fleet is >1,000 HP; these units have higher switching costs and longer deployment durations
- **OEM bargaining power:** Fleet scale provides procurement advantages on new equipment and parts
- **Customer relationships:** ~300 customers, 6-year average deployment duration, top 5 customers = 35% of revenue

The TOPS and NGCS acquisitions widened the gap vs. competitors like USA Compression Partners and Kodiak Gas Services. Industry consolidation favors the scale leader because smaller competitors face higher per-unit operating costs and lack the geographic density to offer rapid response.

Utilization at 95-96% is near physical capacity, providing pricing power on contract renewals and new deployments. The 10-K notes that the current tightness in compression availability supports rate increases — a dynamic reinforced by the structural supply constraint in large HP units (long lead times for new builds, limited OEM capacity).

## Risk Assessment

### Financial Leverage (High)
A 30% EBITDA decline (to ~$630M) would push leverage to ~3.8x — still within covenant limits (5.25x) but uncomfortable. The probability of a 30% compression is low given contract structures and 95%+ utilization, but it becomes meaningful if U.S. natural gas production declines or oil prices fall sharply (reducing gas lift demand).

### Acquisition Integration (Moderate)
The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that expected acquisition benefits may not materialize. Fleet integration, IT systems consolidation, and workforce management across these acquired operations carry execution risk.

### CFO Transition (Moderate)
Doug Aron's retirement and subsequent ~$6M in stock sales (~25% of his holdings) during a critical integration period introduces transition uncertainty. The CEO holds $90M+ in stock, which mitigates somewhat, but losing the CFO who structured these deals matters. The May 6 earnings call will provide color on the transition timeline and successor.

### Commodity Price Sensitivity (Moderate)
While Archrock's contracts are fee-based (not directly commodity-linked), customer activity levels are indirectly tied to oil and gas economics. The risk scenario is a sharp decline in oil prices reducing gas lift compression demand (40% of fleet). The base case for sustained production growth makes this a tail risk rather than a central case.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 45.6% is rich relative to 28.5% historical volatility — a 17pp premium reflecting uncertainty around the May 6 earnings report. The term structure is in backwardation (45.6% near-term vs. 37.2% at 6 months), confirming the market is pricing an imminent catalyst event rather than structural deterioration. Put/call OI ratio of 0.10 is extremely call-heavy, suggesting bullish positioning despite elevated IV. The IV collapse from 112.6% to 45.6% over the prior two weeks (alongside a 7.5% price increase) indicates that the major uncertainty event has partially resolved, with residual premium concentrated around the upcoming earnings date. This setup is consistent with a fundamentally healthy name approaching a known catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$48-50, +24-29%)
Archrock delivers on the operating leverage thesis: FY2026 EPS comes in at $2.20+ as full-year NGCS contributions, continued rate increases, and reduced growth capex drive both EBITDA growth and FCF expansion. The compression services sector re-rates upward as institutional investors recognize the AI power demand narrative. The stock trades toward 22x FY2027E EPS of $2.40+ = $53. A sharp decline in oil prices triggers reduced drilling activity and gas lift compression demand. U.S. natural gas production growth stalls. Acquisition integration difficulties surface (fleet redundancies, customer losses). Leverage becomes a constraint as EBITDA compresses while interest expense remains fixed. The stock de-rates to 13-14x trailing earnings.

## Conclusion

Archrock is a high-quality compounder in outsourced natural gas compression with a dominant market position, expanding margins, and multiple secular demand drivers. The 21x trailing earnings multiple represents a 43% discount to the peer median, with identifiable catalysts (FCF inflection from reduced capex, full-year acquisition contributions, AI power demand narrative) that should narrow this gap. The leverage profile demands respect but is well-managed with no near-term maturities and comfortable covenant headroom.

The primary concern at current levels is timing: the stock sits at its 52-week high. The May 6 earnings report is the near-term swing factor. A beat — supported by the Q4 2025 blowout and positive 90-day estimate revision trend — would likely drive consensus target upgrades. At 2-3% sizing with a 12-month horizon, the risk/reward favors accumulation. Investors with higher risk tolerance should consider adding on any pullback toward $36-37, which would provide an additional 2-3 turns of P/E compression for improved entry.
