## Executive Summary

J.B. Hunt has exited a four-year freight downcycle with a demonstrable earnings inflection. Q2 2026 revenue rose 19.4% to $3,495.3M, operating income rose 31.5% to $259.5M, and diluted EPS rose 45.8% to $1.91. Intermodal (JBI), half of revenue and roughly 52% of segment operating income, grew loads 10% year over year to a record 578,000-plus while ending the quarter with approximately 124,200 units of trailing capacity, down from 124,838 at December 31, 2025, on *negative* $12.1M of segment net capex for the half. The incremental operating margin on JBI's revenue growth was roughly 17.5% against an 8.6% segment average. Volume growth funded by asset velocity rather than capital is the highest-quality form of freight earnings recovery, and it is showing up in consensus: FY2026 EPS has been revised to $7.82 (+6.9% in 90 days) and FY2027 to $10.12 (+11.9% in 90 days), across 23 analysts, with four consecutive earnings beats behind it.

The problem is what the market is already paying. At $271.195, JBHT trades at 38.6x TTM diluted EPS of $7.035, 34.7x the FY2026 consensus and 26.8x FY2027. The FY2027 figure of $10.12 would exceed the FY2022 cycle-peak EPS of $9.21 on roughly 10% less revenue, which requires the operating ratio to reach or beat the 91.0% FY2022 peak. That is achievable — management cites $135M of structural cost removed over the past year, and the ex-fuel operating margin expanded up to 143bp in Q2 — but it is an expectation, not a contracted outcome. Meanwhile the two mechanical supports for the 2026 result are turning: container utilization near 90% leaves little asset-turn headroom, and FY2026 net capex is guided to $600–800M against $144.9M spent in the first half, implying a 3–4x sequential step-up that will consume free cash flow and lift depreciation into 2027.

Second, the cash statement diverged from the income statement in H1 2026. Operating cash flow fell 10.3% to $723.3M while net earnings rose 30.9% to $322.6M. Trade receivables consumed $300.9M of cash versus providing $42.0M a year earlier, a $342.9M swing, with receivables up 25.9% against 12.0% revenue growth. Cash taxes of $38.6M versus $121.0M flattered the reported figure, so the underlying working-capital drag is larger than the 10.3% decline shows. Bad debt expense declined in both Q2 and H1, which argues against a collections problem, but with $4.2M of balance-sheet cash the drag flows straight onto the revolver. Combined with a clustered insider selling pattern (seven sellers between April 21 and June 5 at $250–285, no open-market C-suite buying) and a two-thirds year-over-year reduction in buyback pace, the risk/reward at this price is roughly balanced. **HOLD**, with a 12-month base case of $290 (+6.9%), a bull case of $350 (+29.1%) and a bear case of $185 (−31.8%).

## Company Overview

J.B. Hunt is a North American surface transportation, delivery and logistics company incorporated in Arkansas in 1961, operating across the continental U.S., Canada and Mexico through five reported segments. FY2025 revenue was $11,999M with $865.1M of operating income.

Two segments produce essentially all the profit. In FY2025, JBI ($450M) plus Dedicated Contract Services ($377M) generated $827M of $865M consolidated operating income (95.6%). In Q2 2026, JBI $150.9M plus DCS $102.5M equalled $253.4M of $259.5M (97.6%). ICS (brokerage), Final Mile (FMS) and JBT (trailer-centric truckload) contributed $6.0M combined. Any view on JBHT is a view on intermodal and dedicated fleets.

The intermodal asset base differentiates the franchise. JBHT owns the boxes and performs most of the drayage rather than simply brokering rail capacity. At December 31, 2025 JBI operated 124,838 pieces of company-owned trailing equipment (primarily 53-foot high-cube containers), a chassis fleet of 104,474 units, 5,880 company tractors and 7,606 company drivers. The 10-K states the containers and chassis are "uniquely designed so that they may only be paired together for optimal productivity." That is management's characterization of the advantage; the verifiable fact is a captive, purpose-matched equipment pool of that scale, which means JBHT captures the drayage margin and controls equipment availability rather than depending on rail-owned or pooled chassis. The franchise dates to a 1989 agreement with BNSF's predecessor, the first linkage of a Class I railroad and a truckload carrier in joint service, and BNSF is not a party to the pending Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern transaction.

DCS provides contractual ballast: three-to-ten-year contracts (average roughly five), priced largely cost-plus with fixed costs recovered regardless of equipment utilization, 11,878 company trucks, and customer retention of approximately 94% in FY2025 and 96% in Q2 2026. None of JBHT's 31,750 employees are unionized, a structural cost and flexibility advantage relative to unionized LTL and rail labor.

Revenue fell 19.0% peak-to-trough and operating income 37.6% from FY2022 to FY2024. FY2025 stabilized (revenue −0.7%, operating income +4.1%). Q2 2026 is the step-change.

### Reported margins understate the improvement, with a caveat

Q2 2026 fuel surcharge revenue was $641.5M versus $351.9M, up 82.3%, inflating both revenue and cost on broadly pass-through economics. On the ex-fuel revenue line the company discloses: Q2 2026 operating margin of 9.09% ($259.5M / $2,853.8M) versus 7.66% ($197.3M / $2,576.3M), up 143bp, against only 70bp of improvement in the gross-revenue operating ratio. For H1, 8.48% versus 7.32%. The caveat matters: fuel surcharge programs carry lag, and the 10-K states the lag hurts operating income when fuel rises rapidly. Management quantified fuel as a roughly 100bp headwind to Dedicated margin in Q2. The 143bp figure should be treated as an upper bound.

A further quality qualifier applies to FY2025. Depreciation fell $46.4M (6.1%) to $714.8M, which the MD&A attributes to three causes including "an increase in the expected useful lives of our chassis and trailer fleets," alongside the absence of BNSF Logistics acquisition amortization and lower DCS truck counts, partly offset by higher container counts. The components are not separately quantified, so some portion of FY2025's $33.8M operating-income improvement reflects a non-cash estimate revision. Extending lives on chassis and 53-foot trailers with average ages of 10.5 and 7.1 years is a routine, auditable judgment; no inference about motive is warranted. It does mean cash flow and the ex-fuel margin deserve more weight than the reported operating-income delta.

ICS gross profit margin fell to 12.5% from 15.5%, which the 10-Q attributes to "increased purchased transportation expense as third party capacity has tightened across the industry." JBT swung to a $1.3M operating loss on 35% revenue growth for the same reason. Consolidated rents and purchased transportation rose 32.4% against 19.4% revenue growth, lifting the line from 43.3% to 48.0% of revenue. FMS is the segment without a cyclical excuse: revenue −10% in FY2025 and −6% in H1 2026, operating income more than halving from $60M to $27M, with stops down 11.2% to 3.83M. Management attributes part of that to deliberate revenue-quality actions, which is credible, but the stop count is a volume decline.

### Cash conversion

FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,678.3M against $730.7M of gross capex — $947.6M of free cash flow, 2.8x net income coverage. H1 2026 reversed the pattern: operating cash flow of $723.3M, down 10.3%, against net earnings up 30.9%. The reconciliation is unambiguous: receivables consumed $300.9M versus providing $42.0M, and receivables grew 25.9% (to $1,461.3M) against 12.0% revenue growth. The fuel-surcharge component of revenue is billed and collected like any other revenue, so an accelerating, fuel-inflated top line mechanically expands receivables faster than revenue when the acceleration is recent. Trade payables provided a $117.9M offset. Cash taxes fell $82.4M year over year, which flattered the reported OCF, so the true working-capital drag exceeds the reported decline. This is a monitoring item rather than a red flag, but it constrains the free-cash-flow case for FY2026 alongside the guided capex ramp.

### Balance sheet: better than the screens

Interest-bearing debt to equity was 0.41x at December 31, 2025 and 0.31x at June 30, 2026. Net debt of roughly $1,141M against TTM EBITDA of approximately $1,674M is about 0.68x. The widely quoted 1.22 "debt/equity" figure is total liabilities to equity. The FY2025 current ratio of 0.83 reflected $699.9M of 3.875% senior notes sitting in current liabilities; those matured and were repaid in full in March 2026, and the June 30, 2026 current ratio recalculates to 1.25. Debt composition is $744.1M of 4.90% notes due March 2030 (fixed) and $401.2M of variable facility borrowings at an average 4.61%; a one-percentage-point rate increase would reduce annual pretax earnings by $4.0M against $431.8M of H1 pretax income. Available liquidity at June 30, 2026 was roughly $1.3B (revolver capacity to November 2030 plus the unexercised term-loan remainder), before a $400M accordion.

The genuine constraint is that JBHT runs with $4.2M of balance-sheet cash and funds itself on the revolver. Liquidity is a facility-access question. Against 0.68x net leverage and full covenant compliance at both dates, this is a low-probability channel, but it is the reason the Altman grey-zone reading should not be dismissed outright.

ROIC on FY2025 figures is approximately 13.0% (NOPAT $651.4M on $5,014.6M invested capital). ROE of 16.8% is flattered by buyback-driven equity shrinkage — equity fell $449.4M in FY2025 despite $598.3M of net income.

## Growth Analysis

The 2026 upcycle is supply-driven. Multiple independent trade sources converge on carrier exits, English-language-proficiency and CDL verification enforcement tightening state by state, diesel up sharply, elevated spot rates and increased routing-guide failures. JBHT's own filings corroborate the mechanism from both sides: the 10-Q attributes intermodal demand to "the strong value proposition it presents for customers facing higher fuel prices and constrained driver and capacity availability in other transportation modes," and attributes ICS/JBT margin compression to the same tightening.

This configuration specifically favors intermodal. Rail linehaul is more fuel-efficient per ton-mile than trucking, so rising diesel widens intermodal's cost advantage, and scarce truck capacity forces routing-guide failures that push freight to rail. JBI monthly volume cadence accelerated through Q2 — up 9% in April, 9% in May, 12% in June — with Eastern volume up 16% year over year and 31% on a two-year stacked basis, versus Transcon up 5%. DCS reports a record pipeline measured in trucks, roughly 250 sold in Q2, a full-year target of 1,000–1,200 gross truck sales, and an anticipated return to fleet growth.

Two constraints bound the growth case. First, container utilization near 90% means the velocity lever that produced 10% volume growth on a shrinking asset base is largely spent; further volume requires capital at lower incremental returns, consistent with the $600–800M FY2026 net capex guidance against $144.9M in H1. Second, pricing — described by management as "the biggest remaining lever," with intermodal revenue per load ex-fuel up only about 1% — arrives through annual bid cycles, positioning the 2027 bid season as the decisive earnings driver. The asymmetry in timing is the core operational risk: purchased-transportation cost inflation hits ICS and JBT immediately, while the intermodal pricing offset is a 2027 event. If capacity re-enters first — Class 8 orders were surging in June 2026 on rate strength plus EPA 2027 pre-buy, and new capacity typically arrives 6–12 months later — the window narrows.

Estimate revisions confirm the direction without reaching the magnitude that would compel a BUY on momentum alone: current quarter $2.14 (+6.8% in 90 days), next quarter $2.30 (+8.0%), FY2026 $7.82 (+6.9%), FY2027 $10.12 (+11.9%). Four consecutive beats (7.9%, 1.4%, 2.7%, 17.8%) support confidence in near-term consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

JBHT's defensible position sits in intermodal and dedicated. The captive container/chassis pool (124,838 boxes, 104,474 chassis) plus self-performed drayage means JBHT controls equipment availability and captures the drayage margin, a structurally different position from asset-light intermodal marketing companies that lease rail-controlled or pooled equipment. DCS's cost-plus contracts with fixed-cost recovery regardless of utilization, three-to-ten-year terms and 94–96% retention are genuinely defensive. The non-union workforce is a cost and flexibility advantage against unionized LTL and rail labor.

Trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly improving, which is why competitive position stays at 6.5 rather than returning to the 7.5 assigned earlier this year. JBI is clearly gaining, with Eastern volume up 31% on a two-year stack. Against that, the J.B. Hunt 360 marketplace, positioned in the 10-K as a differentiator, saw revenue fall 11.8% in FY2025 to $349.1M while total ICS revenue fell only 2.8%, cutting marketplace share of the segment from 34.7% to 31.5%. The plausible benign reading is a deliberate shift toward contractual freight (64% of ICS volume in 2025 from 61%, roughly 65% in Q2 2026), which is inherently less spot-transacted; ICS's swing from a $56M loss to near-breakeven supports that. But the platform is not visibly taking share. The 2023 BNSF Logistics brokerage acquisition carried $26M of impairment or accelerated amortization in FY2024 and two years of segment losses before reaching a thin 0.4% operating margin in the most favorable brokerage pricing environment in three years.

The largest unresolved structural variable is the proposed $85B Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern combination. The STB unanimously accepted the revised application on May 28, 2026 while pausing to seek supplemental information; the parties responded on July 7, secured Canadian National's support on July 24 in exchange for operating rights across Midwest and South corridors, and added customer protections including expanded gateway pricing on July 28. JBHT is a customer of the Class I railroads — it purchases linehaul and sells an integrated door-to-door product. Two scenarios are live and neither is established: a single-line transcontinental railroad could market intermodal directly to shippers, reducing the value of multi-railroad coordination and increasing the bargaining power of JBHT's largest cost input; alternatively, the gateway-pricing and competitive-access commitments could preserve interline economics, and JBHT's incremental growth is currently concentrated in the Eastern network rather than Transcon, which is where single-line competition would bite hardest. No filing addresses the merger's effect on JBHT.

## Risk Assessment

**Rail dependency.** JBI is 50% of revenue and roughly 52% of segment operating income, dependent on linehaul rates and service levels JBHT does not control. Purchased transportation is more than half of total costs. The Q2 call flagged "some moderation in rail service" as volumes accelerated.

**Customer concentration — unquantified.** The FY2025 risk factors confirm JBHT "derive[s] a significant portion of our revenue from a few major customers," but the quantification was not retrievable. DCS retention of 94–96% measures contract renewal, not customer-base diversification, and segment mix measures composition. This is an open item, not a resolved one.

**Insurance and claims severity.** JBHT self-insures only $500,000 per occurrence, a low retention, but is subject to coverage-layer aggregate reimbursement limits, and the 10-Q states claims "from time to time exceed some of these existing coverage layer aggregate reimbursement limits." Claims accruals rose from $727.0M at December 31, 2025 to $809.7M at June 30, 2026, up 11.4% in six months. Insurance and claims expense rose 3.9% in H1 on "higher claim severity and increased insurance policy premiums expense, partially offset by lower claim volume." Frequency is improving (DOT preventable accidents per million miles down 11% YTD) while severity and premium cost rise. The accrual build may reflect exposure growth alongside 10% volume growth and slower payment timing rather than adverse development; the filings do not disaggregate.

**Purchased-capacity squeeze — live and quantified.** ICS gross margin down 300bp and JBT loss-making. Management characterizes these as manageable pending repricing, which is a forward expectation.

**Fuel.** JBHT holds no fuel derivatives. Surcharge lag hurts operating income when fuel rises rapidly; management quantified roughly 100bp of Dedicated margin headwind in Q2 despite pass-through mechanisms.

**Driver labor and independent-contractor classification.** Management described certain driver markets as "as tight as we've ever seen." JBT operates entirely on 2,003 independent contractors with zero company tractors; JBHT had 2,350 IC arrangements overall. Segment profit exposure is small ($21M FY2025); reclassification liability exposure is not proportional to segment profit.

**Macro demand.** Freight volumes remain exposed to a consumer slowdown. A supply-tight, demand-weak configuration is ambiguous for JBHT: it supports road-to-rail share conversion while capping absolute volume.

**Unresolved disclosure items.** A March 5, 2026 8-K carrying Items 7.01, 8.01 and 9.01 was not retrievable; the item combination most commonly indicates an investor presentation or non-earnings release, but the contents are unknown. An August 22, 2025 8-K (Item 5.02) was likewise unread.

**Governance.** The DEF 14A pay-versus-performance table shows five-year cumulative TSR of 148.91 against a company-selected peer group at 162.46 through December 31, 2025, with peers also ahead in FY2024. The 2026 rally falls outside that window, and peer-set composition was not reviewed.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($350, +29.1%)

The 2027 bid season delivers mid-single-digit intermodal contract price increases on top of continued 8–10% volume growth, and consensus FY2027 EPS proves conservative at roughly $11.00. The mechanism is specific: intermodal revenue per load ex-fuel is currently up only about 1% while truckload spot rates have been at multi-year highs, so the contract-versus-spot gap is unusually wide entering bids. Capacity does not return quickly because the exits are partly regulatory (ELP and CDL enforcement) rather than purely economic, and EPA 2027 equipment costs raise the marginal cost of new capacity. DCS returns to fleet growth on a record pipeline, adding 1,000–1,200 gross trucks with cost-plus economics. ICS and JBT reprice contractual books, removing the current drag. The $135M of structural cost removal proves permanent, driving the operating ratio below 91.0%. At 32x $11.00 the stock reaches $350, near the $370 street high. Optionality: a BNSF-led competitive response to UP–NS improves service on JBHT's core network.

### Bear Case ($185, −31.8%)

Class 8 orders surging in mid-2026 convert to delivered capacity in the first half of 2027, spot rates roll before JBHT locks in higher contract rates, and the pricing lever never gets pulled. FY2027 estimates compress toward $8.00 while the multiple derates to 22x on a cyclical whose earnings peaked, producing $176–185. Supporting evidence for this path already exists in the filings: ICS gross margin down 300bp and JBT loss-making show that JBHT is a large buyer of the capacity that is scarce, and management conceded contract rates "are still lagging current market conditions." Container utilization at 90% means incremental volume now costs capital at lower returns, with the FY2026 net capex lifting depreciation into 2027. H1 operating cash flow already fell 10.3% while earnings rose 30.9%; if receivables keep outrunning revenue on a near-zero cash balance, revolver borrowings rise into a higher-for-longer rate environment. A consumer slowdown removes the volume leg. Layer on UP–NS creating a transcontinental railroad with incentive to market intermodal directly, and the terminal value of the intermodal marketing role becomes a live question rather than a hypothetical.

### What tips the balance

The bull case requires a 2027 event; the bear case has partial confirmation in the current numbers. That is not a reason to avoid a well-capitalized franchise compounding at 13–15% ROIC. It is a reason not to pay 34.7x the current year for it.

## Conclusion

JBHT's earnings inflection is real, well-documented in the filings, and higher-quality than the headline operating ratio suggests: the ex-fuel operating margin expanded up to 143bp in Q2, and intermodal delivered 10% volume growth with negative segment net capex and roughly 17.5% incremental margins. Consensus is rising in response (FY2027 EPS +11.9% in 90 days), the balance sheet carries 0.68x net leverage with roughly $1.3B of facility capacity, and returns on invested capital sit at 13–15%. The franchise assets — a captive container/chassis pool at scale, self-performed drayage, cost-plus dedicated contracts with 94–96% retention, a non-union workforce — are genuine.

Against that, the stock at $271.195 already discounts most of the recovery. It trades at 34.7x FY2026 and 26.8x FY2027 consensus, and FY2027 EPS of $10.12 would exceed the prior cycle peak on roughly 10% less revenue. The two mechanical supports for 2026 are turning: container utilization near 90% caps the asset-turn lever, and FY2026 net capex guided to $600–800M against $144.9M spent in the first half will consume free cash flow and raise depreciation. H1 operating cash flow fell 10.3% while earnings rose 30.9%, flattered further by abnormally low cash taxes. Seven insiders sold between April 21 and June 5 at $250–285 with no offsetting open-market purchases, and the buyback ran at roughly one-third of the prior-year pace, leaving $791.4M of the $1B authorization unused.
