## Executive Summary

National Presto is no longer the cash-rich housewares company its name and dividend history suggest. Defense produced $405.9M of FY2025's $503.5M in consolidated sales (80.6%), up from 70.9% two years earlier, and the company now carries a Defense backlog of $1,835.6M — 4.52 years of FY2025 Defense revenue. Book-to-bill was 2.84x in FY2024 and 2.63x in FY2025. On the demand side, the thesis is intact and verifiable: AMTEC has been sole prime on the U.S. Army's 40mm Ammunition System since 2017, and roughly $1.0B of contract ceiling across the FY22-26 40mm award and the M918E2 IDIQ remains unawarded.

The problem is conversion. Defense gross margin has compressed for two consecutive years — 21.5% (FY2023) to 20.5% (FY2024) to 19.1% (FY2025), and 20.9% in Q1 2026 versus 21.8% a year earlier — while segment revenue grew 68%. Incremental gross margin on Defense growth was 14.6% in FY2024 and 16.0% in FY2025, both below the segment average. For a capital-intensive manufacturer that is the inverse of operating leverage: growth is dilutive to segment profitability. The mechanism is disclosed: substantially all DoD work is fixed-price, and escalation on the 2017 and 2022 contracts applies to only three materials (steel, aluminum, zinc). Net income fell 20% to $33.1M on a 29.7% revenue increase. Housewares gross margin collapsed from 24.8% to 7.9% on tariffs and mix, and posted an outright $2.4M gross loss in Q1 2026. The Safety segment lost roughly $4.9M at the gross-profit line on $2.0M of sales.

Cash generation is the weakest link. Cumulative FY2023–FY2025 operating cash flow was negative $17.2M against cumulative net income of $109.1M; FY2025 free cash flow was negative $36.2M. Inventory of $312.9M is 83.6% work-in-process, roughly 252 days, because Defense revenue recognizes on customer acceptance. Cash plus marketable securities fell from ~$92.8M at 12/31/2023 to $7.0M at 4/5/2026, the special dividend has been eliminated for two consecutive years (payout down 78% from $4.50 to $1.00 per share), and the $50M revolver expires September 30, 2026. At $139.12 — 30.0x FY2025 EPS of $4.63, 31.1x trailing-four-quarter EPS of $4.48, 24.6x EV/EBIT, 2.51x book, 0.72% yield — investors are paying a full defense-manufacturer multiple for earnings that are declining and cash flow that is negative. Base-case normalized earnings power supports roughly the current price; the bear case is ~32% lower. **REDUCE.**

## Company Overview

National Presto is a Wisconsin-incorporated, family-controlled holding company with three reported segments.

**Defense** (FY2025: $405.9M sales, $77.6M gross profit, 19.1% margin) operates under National Defense Corporation and comprises five plants assembled between 2001 and 2022: AMTEC (Janesville, WI — 40mm ammunition, fuzes, initiators; sole Army prime for the 40mm Ammunition System), Spectra Technologies (East Camden, AR — load, assemble and pack), Amron (Antigo, WI — 20mm–50mm cartridge cases), Tech Ord (Clear Lake, SD — detonators, lead azide, energetics), and Woodlawn Manufacturing (Marshall, TX — precision metal parts). The stated strategic logic is completeness: the company can supply the fuze, detonator, cartridge case, metal parts and final LAP for medium-caliber training and tactical rounds.

**Housewares/Small Appliance** (FY2025: $95.6M sales, $7.6M gross profit, 7.9% margin) sells pressure cookers, canners, skillets, fryers and vacuum sealers sourced from a handful of third-party Asian suppliers, primarily in China, on purchase-order terms with no long-term supply agreements. The sales force is six employees. Distribution moved from Canton to Nettleton, Mississippi during FY2025–Q1 2026.

**Safety** (FY2025: $2.0M sales) is Rely Innovations — smoke/CO alarms and commercial fire extinguishers. The 10-K describes it as "startup in nature" with "limited revenues," a characterization now in its seventh year.

Headcount was 1,200 at 12/31/2025 versus 1,126 a year earlier. Approximately 179 Amron employees are covered by a United Steel Workers contract running through February 28, 2030, which removes labor-disruption risk from the ramp period.

### Margin decomposition

The Housewares gross-profit decline of $17.889M reconciles as follows using only disclosed inputs: volume effect (revenue −$7.195M at FY2024's 24.78% margin) accounts for −$1.783M; disclosed Canton-to-Nettleton relocation costs account for −$1.261M; the residual **−$14.845M is rate compression**, which management attributes primarily to tariffs on goods shipped from Asia after January 31, 2025, expensed as period costs under LIFO. The company does not separately quantify the tariff dollar amount, and the residual also contains mix and pricing effects. Housewares then deteriorated further in Q1 2026 to a $2.363M gross loss and a $4.237M segment operating loss, including ~$1.909M of new-warehouse start-up costs.

Safety's drag is exact because segment gross profits sum to the consolidated figure with no eliminations. FY2025 Safety gross profit was $80.325M − $7.589M − $77.644M = **−$4.908M on $1.983M of sales**. Q1 2026 segment operating loss was $2.008M on $0.579M of revenue against $6.099M of segment assets. A roughly $8M annualized operating loss — presented as a run-rate sensitivity, not a forecast — equals about 20% of consolidated FY2025 operating income.

### Cash flow quality

Reported earnings and the underlying economics diverge here. Three-year cumulative operating cash flow was negative $17.174M (+$45.389M, −$53.426M, −$9.137M) against cumulative net income of $109.103M. FY2025 free cash flow was −$36.171M after $27.034M of capex.

The driver is the balance sheet. From 12/31/2023 to 12/31/2025, inventories rose $116.553M ($190.420M to $306.973M) and receivables rose $36.312M ($48.727M to $85.039M) — $152.865M of absorption against only $7.724M of payables growth.

Q1 2026 operating cash flow of +$31.347M should not be annualized. Of that, $36.657M came from a single receivables collection as Q4-2025 shipments were collected; inventories built a further $5.889M and payables/accruals fell $7.693M. Excluding the receivables swing, quarterly operating cash flow was approximately **−$5.310M**. Q1 is also seasonally favorable for Housewares collections. Inventory reached $312.862M at 4/5/2026, of which $261.463M (83.6%) is work-in-process — roughly 252 days on FY2025 average balances. Because Defense revenue is recognized primarily when the customer takes legal title and formally documents acceptance, National Presto finances the entire production cycle on its own balance sheet.

### Balance sheet

At 4/5/2026: total assets $469.312M, total liabilities $74.143M, stockholders' equity $395.169M, current ratio 5.9x, zero drawn debt (the $23.624M revolver balance at 12/31/2025 was repaid), cash $6.488M plus $0.501M of certificates of deposit. Recomputed Altman Z-score on FY2025 inputs is 8.74, comfortably in the safe zone, though driven overwhelmingly by market equity against very small liabilities. Solvency is not a concern.

Two facts qualify the fortress framing. First, cash plus marketable securities fell from ~$92.780M at 12/31/2023 to $6.989M at 4/5/2026, an $85.8M decline. Interest income fell $3.009M in FY2025 and interest expense rose $0.830M, cutting other income by 66%. The investment portfolio that historically cushioned earnings and funded special dividends has been redeployed into work-in-process inventory. Second, the $50.0M unsecured revolver (SOFR + 1.25%) **expires September 30, 2026**, roughly seven weeks out, and $50M is thin against $312.9M of inventory and ~$602.6M of open purchase orders. Renewal or upsizing is a near-term item with no disclosed resolution.

### Capital allocation

FY2025 uses were $27.034M of capex and ~$7.1M of dividends, funded by a $23.624M revolver draw, $14.411M of cash drawdown and $4.477M of net securities sales. The segment table shows **$22.275M of Housewares capex in Q1 2025 alone** — the great majority of FY2025 capex went to the Nettleton distribution center serving the consumer segment, while Defense capex was $0.708M in Q1 2025 and $0.534M in Q1 2026. The Canton facility was subsequently sold on 2/10/2026 for $4.800M (book gain $1.707M) and leased back short-term. Distribution centers are decade-life assets and the decision may have been substantially committed before the 2025 tariff shock changed the segment's economics, but the outcome is that roughly $22M of capital and ~$3.2M of expensed transition costs went to a segment that generated a gross loss in Q1 2026, while the segment carrying a $1.84B backlog received under $1.3M across the same five quarters.

The dividend is the clearest signal. Per-share dividends were $4.00 in FY2023 ($1.00 regular + $3.00 extra), $4.50 in FY2024 ($1.00 + $3.50), then **$1.00 in FY2025 with no extra**, and $1.00 regular paid March 20, 2026 with no extra. Cash returned is down 78% from 2024 and the current yield is 0.72% versus roughly 3.2% had the 2024 payout been maintained. MD&A does not explain the omission directly, but discusses increased interest expense and reduced interest income stemming from "increased investments in inventory required to support augmented Defense segment awards." Management has separately stated it will make further acquisitions, so retention for M&A is an equally consistent reading. Either way, the income constituency that historically held these shares has been removed.

### Backlog is real and large

| 12/31/2025 | 1,747.8 |

Because backlog is defined as customer orders less sales recognized against them, order intake is derivable: FY2024 orders $805.6M (book-to-bill 2.84x), FY2025 orders $1,068.1M (2.63x), Q1 2026 orders $187.4M (1.88x). Still expansionary, but decelerating. Backlog at 4/5/2026 equals 4.52 years of FY2025 Defense revenue.

Two qualifiers deserve equal weight. First, the disclosed delivery window lengthened from "18- to 42-month" in the 10-K (12/31/2025) to "18 to 48-month" in the 10-Q (4/5/2026), six months of additional tail in one quarter. That may simply be arithmetic on a larger backlog against unchanged capacity, but it argues against modeling rapid conversion. Second, Defense capex of roughly $0.5–0.7M per quarter means the segment is scaling through working capital and existing assets, which limits capital intensity but also caps throughput. Backlog coverage of 4.5 years is evidence that conversion is capacity-gated rather than demand-gated.

### Contract ceiling is option value, not backlog

Two vehicles carry substantial unawarded ceiling: the FY22-26 40mm Ammunition System award ($965M awarded of a $1,413M ceiling, one award year remaining, ~$448M unawarded) and the M918E2 High Velocity TP-DNT IDIQ ($256M awarded of $818.9M, ~$563M unawarded). Combined, roughly $1.0B. The 10-K is explicit that realization depends on military requirements, funding and procurement decisions controlled by the Army and DoD. The FY17-21 contract's deliveries complete in 2026, so a mix rotation toward newer, apparently thinner-margin awards is already underway.

### Q1 2026 and the calendar

Reported Q1 2026: net sales $118.649M (+14.5%), gross profit $17.478M (−3.5%), operating profit $8.032M including the $1.707M Canton gain, net earnings $6.626M (−12.9%), EPS $0.93 versus $1.07. Excluding the gain, adjusted EPS was approximately $0.74, a 31% decline. The quarter also included six additional days versus the prior-year period; per-day normalization implies roughly 7.4% underlying growth rather than 14.5%, though Defense revenue recognizes on acceptance rather than evenly, so that normalization is imperfect.

Segment detail: Defense sales $99.572M (+23%), gross profit $20.790M (20.9%), operating profit $14.277M; Housewares sales $18.498M (−16%), gross loss $2.363M, operating loss $4.237M; Safety sales $0.579M, gross loss $0.949M, operating loss $2.008M. The Housewares revenue decline was attributed to start-up issues at the new warehouse and an inability to ship during part of the quarter, with Safety affected by the same facility.

### The Q2 2026 gap

The most recent verifiable filing is the Q1 10-Q (filed 5/15/2026). A press report dated 2026-08-07 stated that Q2 earnings surged year-over-year on Defense growth, with shares up 13.3% since the report. Price action corroborates direction: the stock gapped from $121.42 (July 31) to a $134.91 close on August 3 on 224,113 shares against 84,528 average volume, then reached $139.57 by August 5. Direction is corroborated; magnitude, segment margins, cash flow and updated backlog are not. This is the single largest evidence gap, and it affects the earnings run-rate but not the structural conclusion: even generous assumptions about Q2 leave trailing EPS in the $5.00–5.50 range and the multiple at 25–28x, on a business consuming cash.

## Competitive Landscape

**Defense — a genuine, defensible niche, but without pricing power.** The award history is the strongest evidence: AMTEC won the Army's 40mm system prime contract in 2005 and 2010 as one of two primes holding the majority share, then in 2017 and 2022 as sole prime, and consolidated a competitor by acquiring DSE assets in November 2013 with $188M of undelivered 40mm work novated over. Qualification barriers — energetics handling, ATF and DoD 4145.26-M compliance, lot-acceptance testing, ITAR — are high and the number of qualified U.S. sources is small. Vertical completeness across fuze, detonator, case, metal parts and LAP is a structural advantage the primes largely vacated.

What the position has not delivered is margin. Two consecutive years of incremental gross margins below segment average, against 68% cumulative volume growth, indicate the company is capturing the replenishment cycle's volume but not its pricing. The disclosed mechanism is fixed-price contracting with escalation limited to steel, aluminum and zinc. Add a dependency the company does not control: the 10-K states that key components and services come from third-party subcontractors, "several of which the segment is required to work with by government edict… many of which it does not control." Press reporting attributes part of the Q1 2026 absorption shortfall to a two-week shutdown at the government-operated facility performing LAP for most 40mm rounds — unverified against any filing, but consistent with the disclosed structural dependency. Throughput on the largest program is gated by an asset the company neither owns nor funds.

Demand context is conflict-driven rather than baseline. The 10-K attributes recent sales augmentation to the Ukraine Security Assistance Initiative, having previously cited Iraq and Afghanistan supplementals, and warns that any significant decline or redirection of U.S. military expenditures would materially decrease sales and earnings. Funding is annually appropriated.

**Housewares — structurally disadvantaged and deteriorating.** The 10-K's own assessment notes industry consolidation, that larger companies enjoy assortment advantages, and that the company "may find it more difficult or lose the ability to place its products with its customers." Some customers maintain private label and buy brokered product directly from Asia — the same contract manufacturers National Presto uses. Six salespeople, no long-term customer contracts, no long-term supply agreements, a China-concentrated supply chain, a 7.9% FY2025 gross margin and a Q1 2026 gross loss. This reads as permanent economic compression rather than a cyclical trough.

**Safety — subscale against entrenched incumbents.** UL and ETL certification is expensive, time-consuming and requires fully tooled product; commercial extinguisher sales depend on state and local fire-safety official approvals, and the 10-K states in past tense that inability to obtain those approvals "has had" an adverse impact. The Rely FX dry-chemical launch is a credible move toward higher-spec commercial and military channels, but from a $0.6M quarterly revenue base against roughly $8M of annualized operating losses.

**Competitive position score: 6.0.** Defense trajectory is stable-to-consolidating with a real moat; Housewares is deteriorating and Safety is not gaining traction; and the Defense moat is not converting into margin.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is worsening, not diversifying.** Defense rose from 70.9% of consolidated sales in FY2023 to 73.2% in FY2024 to 80.6% in FY2025, essentially all with DoD and DoD primes. The 10-K's note that no customer reached 10% of consolidated sales in FY2025 (versus Amazon at 10% in FY2024 and 11% in FY2023) is a denominator effect: Amazon at 10% of FY2024's $388.2M is ~$38.8M, which held flat equals 7.7% of FY2025's $503.5M and ~40.6% of the Housewares segment. The 10-K still warns that losing Amazon would have a material adverse effect. A screen reading "no 10% customer" as reduced concentration reaches the wrong conclusion.

**Fixed-price exposure with minimal escalation.** Against $312.9M of inventory (83.6% WIP) and ~$602.6M of open purchase orders, the company absorbs input-cost and execution risk directly. The two-year incremental-margin record suggests this is already materializing.

**Lot-acceptance tail risk.** Products are accepted by test-firing samples from production lots that "typically constitute a sizable amount of product," and should a sample fail, "the cost to rework or scrap the entire lot could be substantial." With $261.5M of WIP and no disclosed reserve, this is a concentrated, unquantified exposure.

**Termination for convenience.** All U.S. government contracts are terminable at the government's convenience with recovery limited to costs incurred and profit on completed work. Given the WIP balance, an adverse termination would be a balance-sheet event.

**Revolver expiry, September 30, 2026.** Undrawn at 4/5/2026 but drawn to $23.6M at the prior year-end. $50M is thin against the working-capital profile, and failure to renew or upsize would constrain the ramp or extend the dividend suspension.

**Tariffs cut both ways.** The 10-K states plainly that increased tariffs on Chinese-imported product would have a material adverse effect. The IEEPA ruling created refund optionality; the Section 122 10% tariff was ruled illegal by the CIT on May 7, 2026 with appeal expected; and the administration has announced additional tariffs under alternative authorities. Management has also cited tariff-driven price increases reducing volumes, so the exposure hits both price/cost and demand.

**Governance and disclosure.** Maryjo Cohen holds Chair, President, CEO and Director simultaneously; control rests on a Voting Trust Agreement amended as recently as the FY2024 10-K, whose terms are not readable. Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (2025-11-25, 2026-03-04) and Item 5.03 charter/bylaw amendments (2026-05-21) are unretrievable. Cohen remains CEO per the 5/15/2026 10-Q signature and 4/14/2026 proxy, so neither 5.02 was a CEO departure, but her absence from the 1/2/2026 annual grant list is unexplained. An Item 1.05 material cybersecurity incident 8-K was filed 2025-03-06 for a March 1, 2025 system outage, with no quantified impact anywhere in MD&A. The Eau Claire CERCLA site has been remediated since 1998, with the standard caveat that existing accruals could prove inadequate.

**Positioning.** Short interest is 11.6% of float, 306,714 shares, 3.7 days to cover. On a 7.166M-share company with 84,528 average daily volume, that positioning cuts both ways: it corroborates institutional skepticism about the earnings-quality and margin story, and it creates squeeze risk on any strong print — the August 3 gap of 11% on 2.65x average volume demonstrates both. No listed options data was available for this analysis, so there is no implied-volatility or skew read to add.

**Risk factors score: 4.5.** The specific, unquantified exposures — lot acceptance on $261M of WIP, termination for convenience, September revolver expiry, bidirectional tariff outcomes, worsening sovereign-buyer concentration, and an opaque control structure — are not obviously discounted at 30x trailing earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case ($175, +25.8%)

The munitions replenishment cycle is a multi-year structural demand shift, not a spike, and National Presto sits in a niche with few qualified U.S. sources. A $1.84B backlog covering 4.5 years, roughly $1.0B of unawarded ceiling across two active vehicles, and 1.88x book-to-bill even in the slowest recent quarter give unusual revenue visibility for a $1B company. Margin compression is partly transitory: Q1 2026 Defense absorption was hurt by a government-facility shutdown and by start-up of a new distribution center, and if Q2 2026 shows Defense gross margin back above 21% the two-year incremental-margin concern weakens materially. Housewares is at a cyclical and policy-driven trough; the IEEPA refund claim is unquantified optionality worth perhaps $1.29–$1.61 per share after tax if it converts, and the Nettleton facility should lower long-run cost to serve once start-up ends. The balance sheet carries zero drawn debt, $395M of equity and a 5.9x current ratio, so the company can fund the ramp. Normalized FY2027 EPS of $7.50–7.60 at 23x supports $175, and CEO compensation of $754K against a $1B market cap means essentially no risk of value extraction.

### Bear case ($95, −31.7%)

Growth is dilutive to profitability and has been for two consecutive years. Defense gross margin fell 240 basis points across FY2023–FY2025 on 68% volume growth, incremental margins ran 14.6% and 16.0%, and Q1 2026 continued the trend. Fixed-price contracts with escalation on three materials mean the cycle transmits as revenue, not margin. Meanwhile the mix rotates toward the newer FY22-26 and M918E2 awards that appear to carry the thinner economics, and the disclosed conversion window already lengthened from 42 to 48 months in a single quarter. Cash economics are worse than earnings: three-year cumulative operating cash flow of −$17.2M against $109.1M of net income, FY2025 FCF of −$36.2M, 252 days of inventory that the company finances until government acceptance, and a liquidity buffer that fell from $92.8M to $7.0M. The $50M revolver expires in seven weeks. Housewares posts gross losses and faces customers who are also competitors sourcing from the same Chinese factories; Safety loses roughly $8M annually on $2M of sales after seven years as a "startup." The special dividend that anchored the shareholder base is gone for a second year, leaving a 0.72% yield. Capital allocation directed $22M to the loss-making consumer segment while the backlog-carrying Defense segment received under $1.3M. There is no insider open-market buying at 30x trailing earnings, and one director and the CFO sold in March 2026. If Defense margin drifts to 18% and Housewares stabilizes at only 5%, EPS is ~$5.10 and an 18x multiple puts the stock at $92 — near where it traded eight months ago.

Until that appears on file, and with the revolver renewal unresolved, the asymmetry runs the wrong way: roughly flat expected return against a 32% bear case. Trim, and revisit below $115.
