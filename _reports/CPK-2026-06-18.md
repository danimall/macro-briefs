**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chesapeake Utilities is a diversified gas-and-electric delivery company that has compiled one of the best per-share growth records in the regulated utility space: 19 consecutive years of EPS growth at a 9.1% CAGR, a 10-year dividend CAGR of 9.2%, and a reaffirmed target of $8.00 adjusted EPS by 2028 versus $6.01 in 2025. The 2023 acquisition of Florida City Gas for $922.8M roughly doubled the asset base and re-centered the company on Florida, where population-driven customer growth and a constructive Public Service Commission provide a long runway of approved pipeline expansions. Q1 2026 confirmed continued momentum: adjusted EPS +11%, adjusted net income +16%, revenue +18%, with residential customer growth of 2–3% across its territories.

The tension in the thesis is the financing model. CPK funds a $450–500M annual capital program against roughly $234M of operating cash flow, closing the gap with a mix of debt and continuous equity issuance through DRIP and ATM programs. That equity cost approximately $0.22 of EPS in 2025 and represents a structural headwind that rate-base growth must continually outrun. The model has worked, with adjusted EPS still growing 11.5% in 2025 net of dilution, but it leaves little cushion for execution slippage, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises both the cost of the debt half and the discount rate applied to the equity half.

At $120.03, CPK trades at ~19.2x trailing/forward earnings, a roughly 16% premium to the gas-utility peer median of 16.6x, while yielding only 2.4% against a peer median of 3.5%. The premium is defensible given the superior growth profile, but it largely prices that growth in. Near-term estimates are falling (current-quarter consensus down 7.6% over 90 days, next-quarter down 12.6%), a CFO with 36 years of tenure is retiring at the most capital-intensive moment in company history, and there is no insider buying to support a conviction entry. We rate CPK a HOLD for compounders willing to ride the rate-base growth, with a more attractive entry available in the low-$110s.

## Company Overview

Chesapeake Utilities operates two reportable segments. Regulated Energy (~74% of 2025 net income, $119.7M) comprises natural gas distribution (FPU and FCG in Florida, Delmarva in Delaware/Maryland), natural gas transmission (the FERC-regulated Eastern Shore interstate pipeline, Peninsula Pipeline in Florida, and Aspire Energy Express in Ohio), and FPU electric distribution in Florida. Unregulated Energy (~21% of net income, $21.3M) spans propane operations across seven states, Aspire Energy's ~2,800 miles of Ohio pipeline, the Eight Flags combined-heat-and-power plant, Marlin Gas Services' mobile CNG/RNG virtual pipeline, and sustainable/RNG investments.

The strategic identity is a "growth utility": a stable regulated base paired with non-regulated businesses targeting returns above traditional utility levels. The Florida City Gas acquisition was the defining transaction, at $922.8M cash for ~125,000 customers across eight Florida counties including Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach. This added $1.13B of assets and $460.9M of goodwill, and shifted the company's growth center decisively toward Florida.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory has been transformed by FCG. Revenue grew from $670.6M in 2023 to $930.0M in 2025, GAAP net income from $87.2M to $140.3M, and diluted EPS from $4.73 to $5.97 (adjusted $6.01). The 2025 result (GAAP net income +18.3%, adjusted EPS +11.5%) outran the post-acquisition dilution, which is the central test of this business model and one it passed.

Profitability is best measured by adjusted gross margin, since fuel costs are passed through. Adjusted gross margin reached $638.5M in 2025 (Regulated $494M, Unregulated $144.6M), largely insulated from commodity swings. ROE is strong and growing, and earnings quality is clean.

The balance sheet sits at roughly 50% equity / 50% debt within a target band of 50–60% equity, with long-term debt of ~$1.46B and short-term debt of ~$158M. The company is in compliance with all covenants, including the 65% funded-indebtedness cap. Recent financing included $200M of senior notes in August/September 2025 at an average 5.04%, $100M in November 2024 at 5.20%, and $123.2M of equity via DRIP/ATM in 2025. A new $100M ATM runs through November 2027 and $343.3M of shelf capacity remains. Cash of $1.8M at year-end is minimal but normal for a utility funding working capital through its $450M revolver.

The capital allocation story dominates the financial profile. Capex was $470.4M in 2025, guided to $450–500M for 2026, within a reaffirmed five-year (2024–2028) plan of $1.5–1.8B. Operating cash flow of $233.7M does not cover this, so the gap is closed by debt and continuous equity. The chain is direct: capex above cash flow drives continuous equity issuance, which drives per-share dilution (~$0.22/share in 2025), which means rate-base and organic growth must exceed that dilution to grow EPS. It has done so, but the margin is thin, and rising rates pressure both the debt cost and the equity cost of the model.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: one beat and three misses over the last four quarters, including a 12.1% miss in Q3 2025. Q1 2026's 3.6% beat is encouraging but does not establish a pattern of conservative guidance. Estimate revisions are the more important near-term signal, and they are negative: current quarter down 4.2% over 30 days and 7.6% over 90 days, next quarter down 4.7% and 12.6%. The next-year estimate is flat ($7.31), so the deterioration is front-loaded, partly the $(0.10) Worcester delay, but falling estimates are a headwind for both the growth and valuation scores.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest part of this story. Management reaffirmed an explicit 2028 adjusted EPS target of $8.00, implying roughly a 10% forward CAGR from the 2025 base of $6.01. For a regulated gas utility, a sustained ~10% per-share growth rate alongside 19 straight years of growth is genuinely top-decile.

The growth engine has three components. First, Florida organic customer growth, with residential adds of 2.0% at FCG, 2.2% at FPU, and 3.3% on Delmarva in Q1 2026, driven by population migration into Miami-Dade, Brevard, Palm Beach, and surrounding counties. Second, a long runway of PSC-approved pipeline expansions (St. Cloud, Wildlight, Boynton Beach, Miami Inner Loop, Central Florida reinforcement) supported by constructive Florida mechanisms (GUARD, SAFE surcharges, Storm Protection Plan) that reduce regulatory lag. Third, specific named projects building margin into 2027: the Worcester Resiliency Upgrade (ramping to ~$17.1M margin in 2027 after a delayed Q1 2027 in-service), and the Duncan Plains data-center pipeline in Ohio (an Aspire Energy Express agreement with American Electric Power to serve a fuel-cell facility powering a data center, in-service H1 2027, ~$1.5M margin). The Duncan Plains project connects CPK, at the margin, to the data-center demand theme driving the broader utility sector.

The constraint on the growth score is that this is a dilution-funded model with falling near-term estimates and a TSR that has lagged peers (company TSR index 127 vs. peer 183 in 2025) despite the EPS growth. The growth is real and durable, but per-share growth is structurally capped by equity issuance, and the market has not yet rewarded the gap between CPK's EPS growth and its peers' price performance.

## Valuation Assessment

At $120.03, CPK trades at ~19.2x trailing earnings (forward roughly the same on the $6.45 current-year estimate) and yields 2.4%. Against the gas-utility peer set — NWN 16.6x/3.9%, SR 15.6x/4.1%, OGS 17.0x/3.5%, NJR 16.1x/3.4%, SWX 26.7x/2.9% — CPK sits at a ~16% premium to the 16.6x median P/E and well below the 3.5% median yield.

The premium is defensible: CPK's ~10% targeted EPS CAGR is meaningfully above the mid-single-digit growth typical of the peer group, and on forward 2028 earnings of $8.00 the stock trades at ~15x, which is reasonable for that growth rate. But this is not a mispricing. The above-average growth is largely paid for at 19x, the yield offers little support relative to peers, and near-term estimates are falling rather than rising. The analyst range of $132–160 (mean $145.8) implies meaningful upside, but those targets predate some of the recent estimate cuts and the Worcester drag.

A "cheap" multiple is not present here, and even if it were, a compressed multiple in a higher-for-longer rate environment on a dilution-funded utility would more likely reflect the structural cost-of-capital headwind than a buying opportunity. The valuation is fair-to-slightly-full: no asymmetric upside at $120, with a better risk/reward available in the low-$110s where the forward yield approaches 2.6% and the premium to peers compresses. We score valuation at 5.5, neither stretched nor offering the identified mispricing that would warrant a high score.

## Competitive Landscape

CPK's regulated distribution franchises face no direct competition for residential and commercial customers within their territories; competition exists only at the industrial level via bypass risk and fuel switching, and natural gas retains a structural price advantage over oil and electricity for heating. This is a stable, defensible moat.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. The Florida growth engine — population-driven demand plus the PSC's accelerated, lower-lag recovery mechanisms — gives CPK a structurally better growth setup than peers concentrated in slower-growth or more adversarial regulatory jurisdictions. The data-center pipeline diversifies the Aspire Energy Express concentration away from its prior single anchor (Guernsey Power Station) and adds exposure to a high-demand theme. Against this, the segment carries single-customer concentration risk that is only now being addressed, and the company's per-share economics remain tethered to constructive rate-case outcomes across DE, MD, FL, OH, and FERC. The FCG rate case (filed April 2026, $47M revenue requirement, 11.25% requested ROE on a $780M rate base, decision late 2026/early 2027) is the single most important test of whether the competitive advantages convert to the $8.00 target. The Florida Office of Public Counsel's history of appealing FCG rate outcomes, with one prior case reaching the Florida Supreme Court, means the process may not be frictionless.

## Risk Assessment

**Florida concentration.** Post-FCG, the majority of growth and assets sit in Florida ($1.13B from FCG alone), concentrating exposure to hurricanes, severe weather, insurance availability, and a single regulator. The 10-K explicitly flags higher deductibles and insurance risk.

**Execution on a large capital program.** The five-year plan depends on regulatory approvals, rights-of-way, permits, materials, and labor, and the 10-K notes that the approval process has lengthened. Worcester is a live example: a $(0.10) FY2026 EPS drag from FERC permitting and weather delays.

**Capital-markets dependence.** The model requires capital in excess of cash flow, so a credit downgrade or market disruption would raise costs and could impair the plan. The first tranche of FCG-related debt matures in 2026 and must be refinanced; management expects lower rates, which is not guaranteed in a higher-for-longer environment.

**Goodwill.** $507.5M (mostly FCG) is ~13% of assets and ~32% of equity; deterioration in Florida assumptions could trigger a material non-cash charge, though none has occurred.

**Tariff/trade policy.** The FY2025 10-K materially expanded tariff language, warning that new tariffs could raise the cost of pipe, meters, transformers, and specialized equipment and delay projects, directly relevant because growth depends on buying that equipment.

**ERP and CFO transition.** The $75M+ SAP S/4HANA program (go-live Q2 2027) carries implementation risk, layered on top of the retirement of 36-year CFO Beth Cooper effective June 30, 2026, with COO Jeff Sylvester stepping in. The transition appears orderly, but continuity risk is elevated during the most capital-intensive phase in company history.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface shows near-term ATM IV at 59.7% versus 18.9% one-year historical volatility — rich, with a put/call skew of 20.9% indicating elevated put demand and a term structure in backwardation (59.7% near-term vs. 21.9% three-month). This points to the market pricing a specific imminent event into the front-week expiry. The single-day collapse from 78.0% to 59.7% IV and the thin 75-contract chain suggest this is a low-liquidity artifact around a near-dated expiration rather than a durable signal; CPK options are illiquid and the front-week reading should be treated with caution. The longer-dated 21.9% IV is the more representative number and is consistent with a low-beta utility. The elevated near-term put skew is worth flagging against any constructive stance but is not, on this data, a high-confidence bearish signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Florida's population-driven customer growth and the PSC's constructive, low-lag recovery mechanisms let CPK convert its capex plan into rate base faster than peers. The FCG rate case lands near the $47M/11.25% ROE request, Worcester and Duncan Plains margins ramp on schedule into 2027, and the $8.00 2028 EPS target is met. At a 19x multiple on $8.00, the stock reaches ~$152; a re-rating toward the growth profile pushes it to the high analyst target of $160. Dilution stays contained as organic growth outruns it, and the 19-year EPS streak extends.

**Bear case.** Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of both the debt and equity halves of the financing model, compressing the spread between allowed ROE and cost of capital. The FCG rate case is appealed or trimmed by the Florida OPC, tariffs inflate project costs, and Worcester-style delays recur. Falling near-term estimates prove to be the start of a pattern rather than a one-off, the CFO transition introduces execution friction, and the premium-to-peers multiple compresses toward the 16.6x median. At 16.6x on a reduced ~$6.30 near-term EPS, the stock falls toward $105, roughly 12% below current.

The base case sits between: continued ~10% EPS growth net of dilution, a constructive but not maximal rate-case outcome, and a multiple that holds near current levels. That supports a 12-month target of roughly $130 — about 8% upside plus a 2.4% yield — modest enough that the recommendation is a hold rather than a buy at $120.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, anchored to the FCG rate-case decision (late 2026/early 2027), the Worcester Q1 2027 in-service, and quarterly estimate-revision trends.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback to the low-$110s, compressing the peer premium and lifting the forward yield toward 2.6%, with fundamentals intact.
- FCG rate case approved at or near the $47M/11.25% ROE request, materially de-risking the $8.00 2028 target.
- Estimate revisions stabilize and turn positive over two consecutive quarters, reversing the current downtrend.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge or a credit downgrade that raises financing costs and impairs the capex plan.
- The FCG rate case is appealed and materially cut, breaking the path to $8.00.
- Adjusted EPS growth falls below mid-single digits as dilution overtakes rate-base growth, or the CFO transition produces a guidance reset.

## Conclusion

Chesapeake Utilities is a high-quality, above-average gas utility with a genuine growth engine in Florida and one of the best per-share track records in its peer group. The fundamentals support owning it as a compounder. The constraints on a stronger call are concrete: a dilution-funded capital model with thin margin for error, near-term estimates that are falling rather than rising, a CFO transition during peak capital intensity, no insider buying, and a valuation that already embeds a ~16% premium to peers while offering a below-peer yield. None of these breaks the thesis, but together they argue against an asymmetric entry at $120. We rate CPK a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, and would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$110s or a clean FCG rate-case outcome.
