## Executive Summary

Thor Industries, the world's largest RV manufacturer by revenue and units, is navigating the third year of a severe cyclical downturn. Revenue has declined 41% from the FY2022 peak of $16.3B to $9.6B in FY2025, with FY2026 guidance implying further contraction to $9.0-9.5B. At $76.94, the stock trades approximately 37% below its 52-week high of $122.83 and near its 52-week low of $73.29, pricing in substantial pessimism about recovery timing.

The fundamental picture is mixed. North American Motorized (+30% revenue growth) is a genuine bright spot, and FCF generation ($574M in FY2025) demonstrates the business's underlying cash quality even at trough. However, towable backlog collapsed 42%, Europe is generating losses, estimate revisions are negative across all periods, and the macro backdrop — elevated rates, compressed consumer savings, and tariff uncertainty — offers no clear catalyst for demand recovery in the next 6-12 months.

The stock's valuation on normalized mid-cycle earnings (6-10x on $8-12 EPS) is genuinely cheap, and board member insider buying above the current price provides some confidence in the floor. But "cheap on normalized earnings" requires a view on when normalization occurs, and the current consumer environment provides insufficient evidence to make that call with conviction.

## Company Overview

Thor Industries operates as a decentralized manufacturer of recreational vehicles through three segments: North American Towable (~39% NA market share), North American Motorized (~48% NA market share), and European RVs (~26% motorcaravan share via Erwin Hymer Group). The company sells through approximately 2,400 independent dealerships in North America and 1,100 in Europe. An "Other" segment (Airxcel, Postle) manufactures component parts. Key brands include Keystone, Jayco, Airstream, Thor Motor Coach, Tiffin, and Erwin Hymer Group brands.

The business model is highly cyclical, tied to consumer discretionary spending, interest rates, and dealer inventory cycles. RVs are among the most deferrable consumer purchases, making Thor acutely sensitive to macro conditions.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Trajectory:**
- FY2022 peak: $16.3B revenue, ~$20.60 EPS
- FY2025: $9.6B revenue, $4.84 EPS
- FY2026 guidance: $9.0-9.5B revenue, $3.75-4.25 EPS
- H1 FY2026 revenue: $4.51B (+8.5% YoY), pretax income $53.5M (vs. -$2.8M prior year)

The Q2 FY2026 EPS of $0.34 beat the $0.04 estimate by an enormous margin, and Q1 similarly beat ($0.41 vs. -$0.08). These beats are meaningful but partially reflect conservative estimates rather than fundamental outperformance — full-year guidance of $3.75-4.25 implies significant H2 contribution is needed.

**Balance Sheet:**

- Total debt: $890M ($365M term loan + $500M unsecured notes)
- ABL availability: ~$998M unused
- Debt-to-equity: 1.65x — moderate for a cyclical manufacturer
- Goodwill: $1.84B (26% of assets), with 75% showing fair value headroom under 25%

**Cash Flow:**
- FY2025 FCF: $574M (6.0% FCF margin vs. 2.7% net margin — strong cash conversion)
- H1 FY2026 operating cash flow: -$157M (seasonal inventory build of $339M)
- Dividend: $2.08/year (2.7% yield), well-covered by normalized cash flow
- $349M buyback authorization remaining through July 2027

**Segment Performance (H1 FY2026):**
- NA Towable: Sales -6.9%, units -18.3%, backlog collapsed 42% to $621M
- NA Motorized: Sales +30.1%, units +30.4%, backlog $1.04B (+29% YoY at FY25 end)
- Europe: Sales +10.1% (mostly FX), operating at -$38.9M pretax loss

The divergence between Motorized strength and Towable weakness is the defining characteristic of the current quarter. Motorized benefits from strong Class C demand and private-label partnerships; Towable suffers from the Heartland consolidation under Jayco and broad travel trailer weakness.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth visibility is limited. Estimate revisions are negative across all periods:
- Current quarter: -12.0% over 90 days
- Current year: -1.6% over 90 days
- Next year: -2.7% over 90 days

The industry's RVIA forecast for CY2026 is 349,000 units (+2% vs. CY2025), still far below the ~500K+ peak. The macro environment — depleted savings and elevated interest rates — directly impairs RV financing and consumer willingness to take on large recreational debt.

**Recovery catalysts that would change the picture:**
1. Fed rate cuts reducing consumer financing costs
2. Oil price normalization reducing towing costs
3. Consumer confidence recovery (no evidence in current data)
4. Dealer restocking cycle following aggressive destocking

The Motorized segment's 30% growth provides genuine structural proof that demand can recover when product and pricing align. The Keystone rebrand and Heartland consolidation represent H2 FY2026 catalysts that could improve Towable execution, but these are operational improvements at trough volume rather than macro demand drivers.

## Valuation Assessment

**Normalized earnings framework:**
- Mid-cycle revenue: $11-13B (halfway between trough and peak)
- Mid-cycle operating margin: 5-7% (vs. current ~0.5%, peak ~10%)
- Mid-cycle EPS: $8-12
- At $76.94, implied P/E on mid-cycle: 6.4-9.6x

This is genuinely cheap relative to the company's earning power through a normal cycle. However, the market is correctly flagging uncertainty about (a) when normalization occurs, (b) whether structural factors (dealer consolidation, used RV competition, European losses) permanently compress the earnings path, and (c) goodwill impairment risk on the $1.84B balance.

The stock trades at a discount to leisure peers on both P/E and FCF metrics. The discount is partially justified by the European drag, goodwill risk, and deeper cyclical trough than peers, but 7x trailing FCF provides genuine downside support.

## Competitive Landscape

Thor's competitive position is its primary strength. With ~39% NA towable and ~48% NA motorized market share, the company operates from a dominant position that provides procurement scale, dealer access, and product breadth advantages no competitor can match. Winnebago (WGO) is the nearest publicly-traded competitor; most of the remaining ~80 NA manufacturers are significantly smaller.

**Share dynamics:**
- Motorized share: stable at ~48% — executing well
- Towable share: declined from 40.3% to 38.2% (CY2024 H1 to CY2025 H1) — concerning
- European share: ~26% motorcaravan, ~17% caravan — restructuring mode

The towable share loss is a legitimate concern, though management attributes it to the Heartland transition (deliberately running down legacy product before loading refreshed lines). The Keystone rebrand and product refresh should provide evidence by H2 FY2026 of whether this thesis is correct.

The decentralized operating model allows Thor to maintain brand diversity and compete across every price point simultaneously. The entry-level dominance (single-axle trailers) also creates a future upgrade pipeline as entry buyers trade up over 3-5 years.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Consumer Demand / Cyclical Duration (HIGH)**
RVs are among the most discretionary purchases. With elevated financing costs and weak consumer sentiment, the recovery timeline is uncertain. Each quarter of delayed recovery compounds the operating leverage headwinds.

**2. Tariff Uncertainty (HIGH)**
The 10-Q explicitly references tariff risk. RVs contain significant imported components (chassis, appliances, materials). Thor cannot easily pass through tariff costs on a product already facing affordability challenges. The Supreme Court's recent IEEPA ruling creates additional uncertainty.

**3. European Segment Losses (MODERATE-HIGH)**
-$38.9M pretax in H1 FY2026 with ~$1B goodwill at risk. If restructuring fails to achieve profitability by FY2027, a material impairment charge becomes likely.

**4. Towable Backlog Collapse (MODERATE-HIGH)**
42% decline to $621M signals potential H2 revenue shortfall for the largest segment. Dealers ordering closer to need is structural, reducing future visibility.

**5. Customer Concentration (MODERATE)**
FreedomRoads at ~15% of sales represents unusual concentration for a manufacturer.

**6. Dealer Repurchase Obligations (MODERATE)**
$3.67B in standby commitments is a tail risk if dealer defaults accelerate.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-115 in 12-18 months):**
- Industry bottoms in H2 CY2026 with restocking cycle beginning as dealer inventories normalize
- Keystone/Heartland product refreshes drive towable share recovery to 40%+
- Motorized continues 20%+ growth trajectory
- Lower oil prices reduce consumer towing cost concerns
- Fed eventually cuts rates, improving consumer financing dynamics
- Mid-cycle EPS of $8-10 at 12-14x P/E implies $96-$140
- Dividend yield of 2.7% provides income while waiting

**Bear Case ($55-65 in 6-12 months):**
- Consumer recession materializes
- Rate relief remains delayed, extending affordability crisis
- European goodwill impairment ($500M-1B charge, non-cash but sentiment devastating)
- Towable share losses accelerate, Keystone/Heartland refresh fails to resonate
- Tariffs compound cost inflation, compressing margins further
- Dealer defaults trigger repurchase obligations, stressing liquidity
- Trough EPS of $2-3 at 15-20x P/E implies $30-60

## Conclusion

Thor Industries presents the classic cyclical value trap vs. generational buying opportunity tension. The dominant market position, strong FCF generation at trough, manageable debt, and mid-cycle earnings power of $8-12 EPS make the stock mathematically cheap on any recovery scenario. Board member Peter Busch Orthwein buying at $85-88 (above current price) provides informed insider confirmation.

However, the current macro environment — consumer stress, elevated rates, tariff uncertainty, and falling estimates — combined with operational challenges in Towable share and European profitability provides no clear catalyst for recovery within a 6-month window. The stock is 37% off its 52-week high for identifiable fundamental reasons, and the options market confirms near-term downside risk with heavy put positioning and elevated IV.

HOLD with a bias toward upgrading on further price weakness or macro improvement. The business survives this cycle; the unresolved variable is timing and entry price.
