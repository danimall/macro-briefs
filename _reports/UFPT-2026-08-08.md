## Executive Summary

UFP Technologies reported the quarter that resolves the central question hanging over this name. FY2025 revenue grew 19.5% but organic growth was 1.5% — the entire increase was purchased, with 2024–2025 acquisitions contributing $168.3M in 2025 versus $73.1M in 2024, a $95.2M step-up against $98.4M of total reported growth. Q2 2026 broke that pattern: revenue of $173.964M, +15.1%, with organic growth of 12.4% and only $4.065M of acquired-operation contribution. Gross margin recovered to 29.3% from 28.8% as the AJR labor disruption (a self-disclosed $6.3M, ~100bp drag on FY2025) wound down. Net income of $20.851M (+21.4%) and adjusted EPS of $2.92 were records, and the shares rose 18.5% on August 4.

The improvement is real and company-specific rather than sector beta. It also arrived in the price. At $314.01 the stock trades at 32.2x trailing adjusted EPS ($9.76 on a four-quarter roll), 29.7x the FY2026 consensus of $10.57 and 26.5x the FY2027 consensus of $11.84. Consensus implies ~12% adjusted EPS growth into FY2027, so the forward PEG is roughly 2.2. Peer median P/E in the health-care equipment set is 23.2x, and the single closest business comparable — Integer Holdings, also a medical-device contract manufacturer — sits at 23.2x. UFPT deserves a premium for 15%+ operating margins, ~14% ROIC and ~0.86x estimated net leverage; a 45% premium on 12% earnings growth is a full price rather than a mispricing.

Two things temper the quarter beyond valuation. First, operating leverage ran the wrong way on an adjusted basis: Q2 adjusted operating income grew 12.3% on 15.1% revenue growth, and first-half adjusted operating income grew only 6.4% on 9.6% revenue growth, as SG&A rose 21.8% in Q2 to 13.1% of sales. Gross-margin recovery is being consumed by back-office headcount, variable compensation and share-based comp (+41% year over year in Q2). Second, first-half operating cash flow fell to $18.816M from $39.141M despite higher net income, driven by a $30.5M receivable build that lifted quarter-end DSO to roughly 59 days from an estimated ~50 days at year-end. Management attributes this to a back-end-loaded quarter, and the near-zero movement in the credit-loss allowance argues against a collectibility problem. The cause is unresolved until the Q3 10-Q. We rate UFPT **HOLD**: a genuine growth thesis with no margin of safety at this entry.

## Company Overview

UFP Technologies is a contract development and manufacturing organization for single-use and single-patient medical devices, incorporated in Delaware in 1993 and headquartered in Newburyport, Massachusetts. The product set is deliberately unglamorous: protective drapes for robotic surgery, patient handling and comfort products, advanced wound care, infection prevention, surgical and endoscopic disposables, orthopedic implant packaging, cardiac implant components, catheter dispenser coils and biopharma manufacturing components. Processes span cutting, routing, compression and injection molding, vacuum-forming, micro-molding, thermoforming, laminating and RF/impulse welding. The company manufactures none of its own raw materials.

The company employed 4,846 full-time workers at January 24, 2026 (from 4,146 a year earlier) plus 503 temporary workers (from 189), across nineteen ISO 13485-certified sites of which thirteen are FDA-registered. Capacity has been added in Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic, Ireland, Costa Rica and Mexico. At June 30, 2026, 34.7% of long-lived assets sat outside the United States while only 16.8% of Q2 sales shipped internationally, giving a low-cost-country manufacturing footprint serving U.S.-domiciled demand.

The company reports one segment, disaggregating only Medical (93.2% of Q2 sales) and Non-medical (6.8%). Sub-market detail — robotic surgery, patient surfaces and support, interventional and surgical, infection control, cardiovascular, orthopedics and spine, wound care — comes from earnings commentary rather than audited disclosure. Management characterizes its competition as "smaller independent regional manufacturing companies," which is the strategic premise for the roll-up: consolidate sub-scale specialists and cross-sell them into existing blue-chip OEM relationships.

## Growth Analysis

The mechanism behind the Q2 acceleration is identifiable, which raises confidence relative to a generic beat. On the Q1 call management disclosed four simultaneous new program launches — three brand new, one a transfer, two in robotic surgery and one in infection prevention — with start-up costs incurred ahead of revenue and "three of those four customers have already asked us to double our capacity." The CFO framed the economics plainly: fixed costs are already in, revenue is not. Q2's 12.4% organic growth is consistent with that ramp beginning, and management guided qualitatively to "robust contributions" in the second half.

Growth composition in Q2 was broader than the top of the customer list: top-five customers grew 14.7% while the balance of MedTech grew 19.7% and non-medical was flat. Sub-market strength was in Surfaces and Support, Cardiovascular, Infection Control and Orthopedics. Capacity is being added in co-investment with customers — a third building in Santiago (patient surfaces and support) and a sixth in La Romana (robotic surgery), both taken in Q2 — with Santiago revenue up more than 200% in Q1, providing overhead leverage. Capex stayed at $2.906M in H1 because expansion is leased: the weighted-average remaining operating lease term extended from 3.78 to 5.52 years with $2.185M of new ROU assets, and $21.993M of undiscounted lease payments outstanding. Low capex with rising fixed lease obligations is a fair trade at this scale but converts flexibility into commitment.

Four constraints on the growth score:

1. **No forward visibility metric exists.** The company elects not to disclose unsatisfied performance obligations because contracts run one year or less; deferred revenue is $4.802M, 0.7% of TTM revenue, and unrelated to order book. There is no backlog, no RPO, and no quantitative guidance in either the Q1 or Q2 releases. For a stock at 26.5x forward earnings, forward modeling rests entirely on management's qualitative program commentary.
2. **Analysts trimmed the next two quarters even as they raised the year.** Current-quarter consensus fell 3.2% over 30 days to $2.65 and next-quarter fell 2.9% to $2.53, while FY2026 rose 1.8% to $10.57 and FY2027 rose 1.3% to $11.84. The full-year increase is largely the Q2 actual flowing through; the forward quarters were marked down. Q3 consensus of $2.65 implies a sequential decline from Q2's $2.92. This is not the >30% estimate-revision pattern that justifies paying a premium multiple.
3. **The acquisition engine is stalled at the margin.** Management stated that "over the next multiple years, acquisition growth will still be 50% of our overall growth," yet 2025 deals totaled roughly $14.8M of purchase consideration (AJR $2.8M, UNIPEC $7.5M, TPI $4.5M) against a ~$630M revenue base, and the company disclosed being "outbid on a couple of recent opportunities." If half of intended growth depends on M&A into a competitive pipeline, the organic base has to carry more weight than the historical CAGR implies.
4. **Non-medical is being deliberately shrunk** — 6.9% of H1 sales from 8.2%, with automotive exit described as "the new normal." A strategic choice, but roughly a one-point annual drag on total growth.

The Welch earn-out going to zero (against a $6.0M maximum, after missing EBITDA targets in 2024 and 2025 and judged unlikely to hit 2026) is evidence that not every deal in an M&A-dependent model works, and it partly explains weak 2025 organic growth as underperforming acquisitions entered the organic base.

Net: growth prospects improve from the prior read on confirmed organic reacceleration and identified program drivers, offset by the absence of any visibility metric and the M&A air pocket. Score 6.2, up from 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is program-level and real. Once a single-use component is designed into an FDA-cleared device and validated at a specific ISO 13485 plant, requalifying a supplier is slow and expensive. That protects installed programs, supports pricing on mature parts, and explains a decade of gross margin expansion from 25.5% (2022) to ~29% today alongside preferred-supplier status at the highest-quality end of MedTech. Intellectual property is not the defense: 21 active patents, with management stating that trade secrets and unpatented know-how are "generally as important as patent protection."

The ceiling is disclosed in the company's own risk factors. Item 1A names competition "in some cases from our own customers bringing programs in-house," and the Q2 2026 forward-looking statements make it concrete for the largest customer: the risk that it "decides to manufacture the products itself or through one of its affiliates or it obtains the products from other listed suppliers specified in our agreement." A supply agreement that enumerates alternative approved suppliers is a different competitive posture than sole-source.

Concentration trajectory is the clearest negative. Top-ten customers were 59.3% of sales in 2023, 68.1% in 2024 and 68.8% in 2025. Intuitive Surgical SARL was 24.3% and Stryker 21.5% of FY2025 sales (45.8% combined, versus 44.6% in FY2024). One customer was 32.1% of gross receivables at year-end. Management commentary points the other way — the CFO said on the Q1 call that "our business is becoming more and more diverse" — and Q2's 19.7% growth outside the top five supports that at the margin. But a single quarter of faster non-top-five growth does not establish declining concentration when the audited annual series is flat-to-worse.

Underneath, the FY2025 customer mix rotated: derived from disclosed percentages, Intuitive was approximately flat at $146.5M (from $147.3M), while Stryker rose roughly 67% to $129.6M. That rotation reduces single-customer dependence over time. It also means the fastest-growing revenue stream runs through the operations that produced the $6.3M AJR labor disruption and that are mid-transfer to the Dominican Republic, so the growth engine and the execution risk are the same asset. (The specific link between Stryker and the acquired safe-patient-handling business is inference from product category, not a filing fact.)

A material supply agreement was filed as an Item 1.01 8-K on February 24, 2026 (period February 19), and SEC full-text search confirms the body and EX-10.1 contain both "supply agreement" and "Intuitive Surgical." The documents themselves were not retrievable, so counterparty identification, term, exclusivity and the direction of the "rebates" referenced in the new risk language are unverified. Two readings are possible: rebates paid by UFPT (a price concession in exchange for expanded allocation) or receivable by UFPT (an economic positive), and we do not adopt either. What is verifiable is that new risk language appeared covering inability to renegotiate favorable terms, failure to achieve expected rebates, and in-sourcing or substitution by the largest customer.

Score 5.9 — modest improvement on broader-based Q2 growth, capped by worsening measured concentration and the disclosed substitution optionality on the largest account.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration (highest-weight risk).** 45.8% of FY2025 revenue in two customers, top-ten at 68.8% and rising, with 27.9% and 13.0% of gross receivables in two customers at June 30, 2026. Loss or in-sourcing of a single large program would be a step-function revenue event. The 20-year customer-list amortization life embeds an optimistic view of this durability.

**Cybersecurity — confirmed incident, unresolved scope.** A new risk factor was added in the FY2025 10-K and an Item 1.05 8-K filed February 24, 2026: detected on or about February 14, 2026, affecting "many but not all" IT systems including billing and label making; "certain files were exfiltrated"; the company was "still investigating the extent of any sensitive information," including whether personal information was taken, and "evaluating what legal and regulatory notifications and filings may be required." Direct costs to date are small (~$0.5M of nonrecurring legal in Q1 2026, combined with CEO transition legal costs). The residual exposure is notification, regulatory and litigation, and it remained open as of the August 6, 2026 10-Q.

**Cash conversion.** Discussed above; unresolved until Q3. A permanent working-capital step-up would reduce the FCF value of the revenue growth even if earnings are unaffected.

**Labor-eligibility compliance.** The AJR issue — a self-initiated E-Verify review of an acquired workforce — cost over $6.3M of incremental labor and ~100bp of FY2025 gross margin, with temporary workers nearly tripling. No enforcement action, fine or penalty is disclosed. For a company whose model is buying small U.S. labor-intensive converters, this is repeatable diligence risk.

**Execution on the Dominican Republic transition.** CEO Rock's Q2 language was candid: "the full realization of these benefits is taking longer than anticipated, [and] the investments are creating near-term mix and margin pressure within our U.S. operations." As of Q1, the third AJR program transfer had "not really started," with a PPAP/protocol process that "may take more than a year."

**Geographic.** The 10-K names civil unrest in Haiti as a risk to Dominican Republic facilities that "play a crucial role"; 34.7% of long-lived assets are offshore, and the DR is the designated growth-capacity location. Tariff exposure has been immaterial, with IEEPA refunds expected as vendor credits rather than a booked receivable, but new tariffs under different authorities have since been launched. Q2 forward-looking statements newly cite the ongoing conflict between the U.S. and Iran as a petroleum-based raw-material cost driver.

**No guidance, no backlog.** Each print is a two-sided event versus modeled expectations, with the September tenor pricing a 9.9% implied move.

**Short interest.** 22.4% of float, 1.243M shares, 5.5 days to cover on a 175K average daily volume. That is a large bearish position in a small float. It amplified the August 4 move upward and will amplify any disappointment; it also signals that a meaningful cohort of informed capital disputes the valuation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 33.6% against one-year realized volatility of 48.4% — implied vol is cheap to realized, at the 3rd percentile of the current chain, after collapsing from 50.6% on July 23 (a standard post-earnings crush). Skew is balanced at −1.8% and the term structure is in contango (33.6% at 12 days, 35.0% at 40 days, 36.5% at 159 days), pricing no near-term stress. The nearest expiry implies ±5.4%, the September tenor ±9.9% and the January 2027 tenor ±20.8%.

For holders sitting on a 52% twelve-month gain, downside protection is inexpensive relative to how much this stock moves: a 22.4% short position, a 7.75M-share float and no company guidance is a combination that has produced 18.5% single-session moves. We do not read direction into the skew values. The absence of elevated put demand neither corroborates nor refutes the concentration and cyber risks.

Score 4.4 — a dense stack of concentration, open-investigation and visibility risks that are only partially priced at 26.5x forward earnings.

## Management Quality

Governance and disclosure quality are better than average for a company this size. Management quantified its own labor problem ($6.3M, and the counterfactual 29.3% gross margin) rather than burying it in a mix comment; disclosed plainly that the Welch earn-out will pay nothing against a $6.0M maximum; and stated that the Dominican Republic benefits are running behind plan. Earn-outs were structured so sellers bore the downside. Debt has been reduced $72M in eighteen months. ROIC is one of three incentive metrics, which is the right counterweight for a serial acquirer.

The CEO succession was orderly and telegraphed: an Item 5.02 8-K and investor deck in December 2025, Mitchell Rock elevated from President in June 2026, Bailly moving to Executive Chairman for a stated one year, and CFO Lataille remaining for continuity. Succession risk is deferred rather than eliminated, since Bailly personally drove the acquisition strategy that management says will supply half of future growth.

Against that, three items argue for a below-average alignment score. First, FY2025 CEO summary compensation of $7,648,440 was up roughly 45% year over year and 2.9x the 2021 figure, in a year when organic growth was 1.5% and indexed TSR fell from 524.50 to 476.22. Two of the three incentive metrics — net sales and adjusted operating income — increase mechanically with acquisitions, so a year of purchased growth can clear targets without organic value creation; ROIC offsets this, but its weighting is not disclosed in the data reviewed. Second, we found no open-market insider purchases by any officer or director across roughly September 2025 through August 6, 2026, a window in which the stock traded from about $206 to $317. Every acquisition was a grant or option exercise. A VP sold 1,100 shares at $317.00 on August 6, three days after the beat; a director exercised 2,864 options at $22.02 and sold all of them four days later, leaving 855 shares. Most of this is routine diversification from deep-in-the-money low-strike options, and by itself insider selling is a weak signal, but the complete absence of buying across an 11-month re-rating is a mild negative. Third, six officer/director 8-Ks in eighteen months is an unusual density, partly explained by deliberate executive additions in business development, operations and legal, with one new officer's seniority unresolved from the retrievable documents.

The strongest offset is Bailly's 171,200 shares (~$53.8M at $314.01) and his statement that UFP "is still the largest investment I have by multiples of greater than 10 over the next largest stock." We weight that above the director sales. Score 5.7.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $400 (+27%)

Q2's 12.4% organic growth is the beginning of a multi-quarter ramp, not a peak. Four new programs launched simultaneously with start-up costs already expensed; three of four customers have asked for double the contracted capacity; two programs are in robotic surgery, the highest-growth MedTech sub-market. The DR campuses (six buildings in La Romana, three in Santiago) are co-funded with customers, and Santiago's >200% revenue growth is already delivering overhead absorption. Gross margin recovers past 30% as the AJR labor drag disappears entirely and the U.S.-to-DR mix shift completes; SG&A growth decelerates below revenue growth once the executive build-out and CEO-transition costs anniversary, turning operating leverage positive. Receivables unwind in Q3, restoring FCF conversion above 100% of net income. The renegotiated largest-customer agreement locks in expanded allocation across ~25% of revenue for a defined term. M&A resumes with the "one very large" opportunity management referenced. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $12.50–13.00 and the market holds a 31–32x multiple on visible mid-teens organic growth: $390–415. A 22.4% short position provides mechanical fuel on each confirming print.

### Bear Case — $225 (−28%)

The Q2 organic number flatters a comparison against a first half of 2025 depressed by the AJR disruption, wound-care destocking and the automotive exit, and organic growth reverts toward mid-single digits in 2027. The February 2026 supply agreement carried a rebate concession that structurally compresses gross margin on the largest account; alternatively, the customer exercises the substitution optionality its own agreement names, and a mature high-volume program moves in-house or to a listed alternative supplier. SG&A continues outrunning gross profit — the H1 pattern of 6.4% adjusted operating income growth on 9.6% revenue growth persists — so EPS growth undershoots the 12% consensus. Receivables prove to be a permanent step-up in working capital rather than timing, and FCF conversion settles near 60–70% of net income. The cyber investigation produces notification obligations and litigation. Consensus FY2027 EPS is cut toward $11.00 and the multiple compresses to the ~20x that the closest comp commands: $225. The stock traded at $236 three weeks ago and $173.86 in May, so this path requires no new information beyond disappointment.

### Base Case — $330 (+5%)

Organic growth moderates from 12.4% toward 7–9% as the new programs contribute but the comparison base normalizes; gross margin holds near 29–29.5%; SG&A growth decelerates modestly, delivering low-teens adjusted EPS growth in line with consensus $11.84 for FY2027. Receivables partially unwind, restoring FCF conversion to 85–95% of net income. Concentration stays roughly flat as non-top-five MedTech growth continues outpacing the top five without changing the annual measured percentages materially. The market applies 28x to FY2027 — a premium to the 23.2x comp, discounted from today's 26.5x-on-forward because visibility remains absent: $330.

Probability-weighted (base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25%): approximately +2%. That is a HOLD, not a REDUCE, because the base case sits at spot rather than below it and the operating improvement is genuine, but it is not an entry.

## Conclusion

Our prior two HOLD ratings on UFPT rested on a specific unmet condition: FY2025's revenue growth was purchased, organic growth was 1.5%, and the multiple required organic reacceleration to be justified. Q2 2026 supplied that evidence — 12.4% organic growth, gross margin back to 29.3%, records across sales, net income and EPS, with an identifiable mechanism (four simultaneous program launches, two in robotic surgery, three customers requesting double capacity) rather than an unexplained beat. Growth prospects and competitive position both improve from the July read, and overall score rises to 5.5 from 5.2.

The problem is that the stock rose 18.5% in one session and 32.9% over three weeks, moving from 20x forward earnings to 26.5x FY2027 consensus on 12% expected EPS growth. The base case is now roughly spot. Three specific items keep us from paying up: first-half adjusted operating income grew 6.4% on 9.6% revenue growth, so gross-margin recovery is being consumed by SG&A; first-half operating cash flow fell 52% on a $30.5M receivable build whose cause is unresolved; and the largest customer relationship — approximately a quarter of revenue — was renegotiated in February 2026 with new disclosed risk language covering rebate uncertainty and the customer's option to in-source or use alternative suppliers named in the agreement. The balance sheet is a genuine strength (~0.86x estimated net leverage, 12.4x coverage, no maturity until 2029), and management's disclosure quality is above average, but there has been no open-market insider buying across an 11-month re-rating and 22.4% of the float is short.

**HOLD.** Existing holders should stay long through the Q3 print, which tests the second-half ramp and the receivable unwind. New capital should wait for $250–265, or for evidence that operating leverage has turned positive at scale.
