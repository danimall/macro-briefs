## Executive Summary

General Mills closed FY2026 (53 weeks ended May 31, 2026) with net sales of $18,424.6M, down 5.4%, a GAAP net loss of $(87.6)M, and adjusted diluted EPS of $3.55 against $4.21 the prior year. The loss is an accounting artifact: $2,971M of restructuring, impairment and divestiture-valuation charges, of which the $1,500M North America Pet goodwill write-down is non-deductible and drove the 102.2% effective tax rate. The cash deterioration is substantive: operating cash flow fell 25.8% to $2,166.2M, with $670.7M of that swing coming from working capital reversing from a $192.4M source to a $478.3M use. Free cash flow of $1,626.3M covered 89.6% of the $1,815.6M of dividends and buybacks; the gap was funded by $1,830.2M of divestiture proceeds, principally the U.S. yogurt sale.

FY2027 guidance is for organic net sales of -1.5% to +0.5%, adjusted operating profit down 8-13% in constant currency, and adjusted EPS of $3.00-3.20. Management quantifies roughly nine points of the operating-profit decline as lapping the 53rd week, normalizing corporate incentive expense, and FY26 divestitures, so the underlying trajectory is closer to +1% to -4%. That mitigant is real and is the strongest element of the bull case. At the $3.10 midpoint, the FY27 dividend of $2.44 consumes 78.7% of EPS and roughly 83% of estimated free cash flow, leaving about $267M annually for buybacks and debt reduction combined, against net debt of approximately $13.15B that moves from 3.9x FY26 adjusted EBITDA to roughly 4.3x on FY27E EBITDA of ~$3,071M.

We move to REDUCE from HOLD. The downgrade is not driven by the FY26 loss, which was disclosed and largely non-cash. It is driven by the change in risk/reward since the last review: the shares have risen 4.7% from $36.91 on July 22 and 21.7% from the June 4 low of $31.75, while consensus current-year EPS has fallen 6.2% over 90 days and current-quarter consensus fell 10% in the last 30 days alone. At $38.645 the stock trades 2.9% above the $37.56 analyst mean and 4.4% above the $37.00 median. Price appreciation into falling estimates, on a business guided to a fourth consecutive year of organic contraction, with 12.0% of float sold short, is a deteriorating setup.

## Company Overview

General Mills manufactures and markets branded packaged food through four reportable segments. FY2026 results:

| Segment | Net sales | Reported YoY | Organic YoY | Segment op profit | YoY | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| North America Retail | $10,571.8M | −11% | −3% | $2,189.0M | −20% | 20.7% |
| International | $3,043.8M | +9% | +3% | $188.7M | +96% | 6.2% |
| North America Pet | $2,613.3M | +6% | −3% | $498.8M | flat | 19.1% |
| North America Foodservice | $2,169.5M | −6% | −1% | $333.0M | −6% | 15.3% |
| **Total** | **$18,398.4M** | | | **$3,209.5M** | | |

North America Retail is 57% of sales and 68% of segment operating profit, and it is the segment whose margin compressed most, roughly 230 basis points from an implied 23.0% in FY25. International, the only segment growing organically, earns a 6.2% margin. The mix shift toward the growing segment therefore dilutes consolidated margin rather than supporting it, which is the mechanism behind adjusted operating margin falling 190bps to 15.3% despite the divestiture of lower-margin yogurt.

Twenty percent of consolidated net sales were generated outside the United States. Walmart represented 22% of consolidated net sales and 31% of North America Retail net sales. Two 50%-owned joint ventures sit outside the segments, Cereal Partners Worldwide with Nestlé and Häagen-Dazs Japan, and swung from $58M of after-tax earnings in FY25 to a $(76)M after-tax loss in FY26, including an $85M pre-tax share of a CPW goodwill impairment tied to downward revisions in the Australian market.

Management's own FY26 scorecard is candid: the objective of returning North America Retail to volume growth was not achieved (organic pound volume −1%), and 65% of top-10 U.S. categories held or gained pound share, implying roughly 35% lost share.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Reported net sales: FY23 $20,094.2M, FY24 $19,857.2M, FY25 $19,486.6M, FY26 $18,424.6M, a cumulative 8.3% decline over three years. The organic series, which is also the company's selected compensation metric, reads +10.8% (FY23), −1.1%, −1.8%, −2.0%. FY27 guidance at a −0.5% midpoint implies a fourth consecutive contraction. The FY26 reported decline decomposes to organic −2 points (volume −1, price/mix −1), FX +1, net divestitures/acquisitions −6, and the 53rd week +2.

**Earnings quality.** The $2,971M charge breaks down as $1,500M non-cash Pet goodwill impairment, $303M of brand intangible impairments (Nudges, Uncle Toby's, True Chews), a $1,032M non-cash valuation loss on the pending Brazil divestiture, $95M of supply-chain restructuring, and $60M of prior restructuring, offset by a $1,049M net divestiture gain. Roughly 60% of the Brazil loss, $622.1M, is recycled accumulated foreign-currency translation adjustment that already sat in accumulated other comprehensive income, so the incremental equity impact is closer to $410M than $1,032M. Reading the headline figure as economic destruction of that size overstates the damage.

**Cash flow.** The deterioration is substantive. Operating cash flow fell from $2,918.2M to $2,166.2M. Capex of $539.9M leaves FCF of $1,626.3M. FCF conversion was 85% of adjusted after-tax earnings against a ≥95% long-term target. FY27 guidance assumes conversion returns to ~95%, which requires the working-capital drag to stop. The 10-K notes core working capital, a net liability, shrank from $303M to $165M, a use of cash. Supply-chain finance payables of $1,380.5M at Q3 (38% of accounts payable) declined year over year from $1,427.5M, so these programs were a modest drain rather than a source. They are not guaranteed by GIS and are not debt-equivalent, but a contraction in supplier participation would compress terms further.

**Leverage and solvency.** Total debt and noncontrolling interests were $13.6B at May 31, 2026, against cash of $453.8M (of which $446M is offshore). Net debt of ~$13.15B against FY26 adjusted EBITDA of ~$3,366.7M (adjusted operating profit $2,811.5M plus D&A $555.2M) is 3.9x, moving to roughly 4.3x on FY27E EBITDA of ~$3,071M absent debt reduction. Solvency is comfortable and distinct from leverage: interest expense of $538.6M implies a ~3.9% average cost, fixed-charge coverage runs roughly 5.2x on FY26 adjusted operating profit against a ≥2.5x covenant, the $2,700M revolver expiring October 2029 is undrawn, and only $1,054M of long-term debt matures within twelve months. The debt mix shifted to 83% fixed from 74%.

A screening Altman Z of 1.16 is mis-specified here: it substitutes book equity for market value of equity and uses GAAP EBIT depressed by the non-cash charges. Recomputed with market equity, Z ≈ 2.13; substituting adjusted operating profit, Z ≈ 2.34. The defensible range is the grey zone, not distress. The negative working capital that penalizes the score ($3,729.5M payables against $1,917.9M inventory) is structural for a scale food manufacturer. The correct framing is a capital-allocation constraint, not a bankruptcy question.

**Capital allocation.** FY26 dividends were $1,315.3M ($2.44/share) and buybacks $500.3M, executed as two accelerated share repurchase agreements in Q1 FY26 covering 10.0M shares at an average price of $49.92. Against the current $38.645, those repurchases are approximately 23% underwater. Total returns of $1,815.6M equalled 111.6% of FCF, effectively funded by the yogurt proceeds, which also financed roughly $2.0B of gross debt reduction.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three levers, and each has an identifiable offset.

**Price architecture and volume recovery.** Management lowered everyday shelf prices below key price cliffs. It worked on the chosen metric: base volume improved from a 10% decline in FY25 to +1% growth in Q4 FY26. The cost was FY26 price/mix of −1 point and adjusted operating margin down 190bps, and Harmening described consumers "buying more on promotion and less at everyday prices, driving a less profitable mix of volume through our P&L." Volume bought with price is only accretive if it is durable enough to generate scale and trade-spend efficiency, and one quarter of +1% base volume does not establish that.

**The $3B cost-savings program through FY2030.** Approximately $2B is Holistic Margin Management and ~$1B is the global transformation initiative and other actions, with a FY27 target of at least $750M. The HMM portion equals roughly 4% of COGS annually, or ~$489M on FY26 COGS of $12,229M. Guided FY27 input-cost inflation of 4-5% is $490-610M on the same base. HMM has been a recurring program for years and delivered 5% of COGS in FY26. The headline overstates the incremental benefit substantially: HMM roughly offsets inflation, and the genuinely new component is ~$250M per year on a straight-line assumption, against which the company is simultaneously reinvesting in lower shelf prices and higher advertising. That is the most economical explanation for how $750M+ of FY27 savings coexists with adjusted operating profit guided down 8-13%.

**North America Pet.** This was the designated growth engine and is now the impaired asset. FY26 reported Pet sales grew 6%, decomposing to organic −3% (volume −5 points, price/mix +2), acquisition +6, and the 53rd week +2. The reported growth is entirely the $1.4B Whitebridge acquisition plus the extra week. Underlying volume is contracting mid-single-digit while the unit carries roughly $5.65B of residual goodwill plus the Blue Buffalo intangible. Blue Buffalo entered the U.S. fresh pet-food sub-category in calendar 2025, described as a significant strategic investment, a competitive-response cost in a segment already missing plan.

The one genuine growth asset is International, up 3% organic, but at a 6.2% margin it cannot carry consolidated earnings. Estimate revisions confirm the trajectory: current-year consensus fell from $3.28 to $3.08 over 90 days (−6.2%), and current-quarter consensus fell 10% in 30 days.

Growth Prospects scored 2.8.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | Value at $38.645 |
|---|---|
| P/E, FY27 guided adj. EPS midpoint $3.10 | 12.5x |
| P/E, FY27 consensus $3.08 | 12.5x |
| P/E, FY28 consensus $3.21 | 12.0x |
| P/E, FY26 actual adj. EPS $3.55 | 10.9x |
| Enterprise value | ~$33.8B |
| EV / FY26 adj. EBITDA | 10.0x |
| EV / FY27E adj. EBITDA | 11.0x |
| FCF yield (FY26 actual) | 7.9% |
| FCF yield (FY27E ~$1,555M) | 7.5% |
| Dividend yield ($2.44) | 6.3% |
| Net debt / EV | 39% |

The single-digit-to-low-teens P/E is the most frequently cited bull argument and the most misleading number in the file. The gap between a 12.5x P/E and a 10-11x EV/EBITDA is leverage: 39% of enterprise value is net debt. An enterprise multiple of 11x on an EBITDA stream guided to decline roughly 9% is not distressed; the equity multiple is a levered claim on that stream, which amplifies both directions.

Peer context is unhelpful here because two of five comparables carry distorted multiples: SJM at −92.4x and MKC at 8.8x. Excluding those, HSY trades at 24.9x, HRL at 29.5x, TSN at 35.9x. GIS's 12.5x is a wide discount, but the peer group has a materially different earnings trajectory in each case, and peer median yield of 3.7% against GIS's 6.3% tells the same story from the other side: the market is pricing a higher probability of distribution risk at GIS. The sector-wide pattern, with Conagra cutting its dividend and guiding FY27 organic to −1% to −3% and Kraft Heinz taking a reported $7.4B impairment (single-source, unconfirmed), indicates the market is repricing branded packaged food as structurally challenged rather than cyclically weak.

What would make a high valuation score defensible is a specific reason the market is wrong. The candidates: (a) the underlying FY27 trajectory ex-artifacts is +1% to −4% rather than −8% to −13%; (b) 7.5% FCF yield at 12x on a business with 5.2x fixed-charge coverage; (c) options struck near multi-year lows creating management upside leverage. Against those: consensus estimates are falling, the price sits 2.9% above the analyst mean target of $37.56, the dividend consumes ~83% of FY27E FCF, and there is no dated catalyst that resolves the organic-growth question. This is a cheap multiple without a catalyst on a business in its fourth year of contraction, with a live impairment reflexivity loop attached.

Valuation scored 4.5 — the absolute yield and FCF support keep it from lower, but the price offers no margin of safety against the identified downside.

**Scenario math (all from the $38.645 basis):**

- **Bear $30 (−22.4%):** FY27 lands at the $3.00 low end, working-capital drag persists so FCF conversion misses 95%, and the board trims the dividend to fund deleveraging or a further impairment reduces book equity and widens measured leverage. 10x $3.00 = $30. The analyst low of $31 corroborates.
- **Base $39 (+0.9%):** 12.5x the $3.10 midpoint = $38.75. Total return of roughly 7% including the 6.3% dividend.
- **Bull $46 (+19.0%):** Organic returns to positive in H2 FY27, transformation savings exceed the ~$250M/year straight-line pace, inflation moderates below 4%, and the multiple re-rates to 14x on $3.30. The analyst high of $47 corroborates.

Weighting base 50%, bear 30%, bull 20% gives an expected price return of roughly −2%, or +4% including the dividend. That is below a fair required return for a 4.3x-levered business with negative organic growth, and the distribution is skewed toward the downside because the bear case has more identified triggers than the bull case.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names three specific pressures: branded competitors, private label, and retailers' own brands, with competition on innovation, quality, price, brand recognition and loyalty. The observable evidence is that GIS is losing ground on the metric it selected. Roughly 35% of top-10 U.S. categories lost pound share in FY26. NAR margin compressed 230bps. The pricing lever being pulled, closing gaps to competition and private label, is the same lever Conagra is pulling, which indicates category-wide margin compression rather than share gains accruing to anyone.

Customer concentration is the most direct competitive vulnerability. Walmart at 22% of consolidated and 31% of NAR sales sits alongside the 10-K's own language that large customers "may seek to use their position to improve their profitability through improved efficiency, lower pricing, increased reliance on their own brand name products, increased emphasis on generic and other economy brands, and increased promotional programs." One caution on interpretation: International rose from ~14.3% of net sales in FY25 to 16.5% in FY26, but that is predominantly a denominator effect from the yogurt divestiture. International's absolute sales grew $252M, of which FX contributed 4 points and the 53rd week 2 points against 3% organic. Changing revenue mix does not establish reduced customer concentration.

Relative positioning is mixed rather than uniformly weak. FY26 organic of −2% was worse than Conagra's −0.4%, but FY27 organic guidance of −1.5% to +0.5% is better than Conagra's −1% to −3%, and GIS has held its dividend where Conagra cut. Within GIS, Q4 U.S. Snacks and U.S. Meals & Baking both grew 5% organically, and NAR organic was flat in Q4 against −3% for the year. The Big G Cereal & Canada figure of −27% is largely divestiture mechanics: the division was formed by combining U.S. Morning Foods with Canada after the yogurt sale, and yogurt product-class sales fell from $1,082.8M to $102.0M over nine months, so reading it as a cereal demand collapse would be a material error.

Trajectory is deteriorating from a still-respectable level. Competitive Position scored 3.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Impairment reflexivity with a named watch list (highest-signal disclosure).** Goodwill plus indefinite-lived intangibles total $20.6B against total assets of $30.0B (68.6%) and book equity of $7,368.4M (2.80x). The FY26 mechanism matters more than the amount: the Q2 annual test produced only a $53M Uncle Toby's charge, then a Q4 triggering event arose from "sustained decline in market capitalization and stock price… heightened macroeconomic uncertainty and lower market multiples in our industry," which raised discount rates and produced $1,750M of charges. The 10-K states that Blue Buffalo and Progresso "had risk of decreasing coverage and we continue to monitor these businesses," with Blue Buffalo's exposure attributed specifically to the increase in discount rates. GIS must reconcile aggregate reporting-unit fair values to market capitalization plus a control premium. A lower share price raises the discount rate, triggers impairment, reduces book equity, and worsens reported leverage. The stock traded near $46 in early February and $31.75 on June 4; at $38.645 the conditions that produced the trigger have only partially reversed. The mitigant is that these charges do not touch cash, the dividend, or the earnings-based fixed-charge covenant. We did not verify the current carrying amount of the Blue Buffalo intangible, so the exposure cannot be sized.

**Dividend coverage.** At $2.44 on ~528M shares, the FY27 cost is ~$1,288M against FY27E FCF of ~$1,555M — 83% of FCF, 78.7% of guided EPS midpoint, and 81.3% at the low end. The board declared $0.61 quarterly on July 1 at "the prevailing rate," implying no annual increase was announced alongside Q4 results. That is prudent and preserves optionality; it also removes a support that income holders had priced. The forward rate and the 127-year streak come from secondary reporting; the FY26 paid rate of $2.44 versus FY25's $2.40 is a filing fact.

**Deleveraging capacity.** Returning to 3.5x on FY27E EBITDA would require roughly $2.3B of debt reduction against ~$267M of annual post-dividend FCF, about nine years. Brazil contributes only ~$140-150M gross (R$800M base price, closing expected by end of calendar 2026, subject to regulatory approval). Leverage is more likely to be fixed through the EBITDA denominator than the debt numerator, which makes the FY27 earnings outcome the dominant variable.

**Other named exposures.** Input inflation guided at 4-5% ($490-610M annually). Tariffs cited twice in the risk factors. Weight-loss drugs added to the demand-anticipation language. Ingredient scrutiny extended to color additives. Cumulative unrecognized pension actuarial losses of $2B, with U.S. plan five-year actual returns of −0.4% against a lowered 7.40%/7.10% ROA assumption; a 100bp lower ROA adds $57M of expense. A contested IRS notice of proposed adjustment on a fiscal 2019 capital loss.

**Unresolved governance items.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed within roughly four months (January 27, 2026 alongside a bylaws amendment; May 4; May 6). Their contents were not reviewed. Harmening remained Chairman and CEO as of the July 6, 2026 Form 4, and Dana McNabb appears as Chief Operating Officer receiving the second-largest equity grant. Whether that role is new, and whether these filings involve departures, is unverified. A span-of-control appointment during a multi-year turnaround is at least as plausible as succession positioning.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 28.4% against 26.8% one-year realized is fair, and the second-percentile IV reading within the current chain indicates near-term options are priced at the low end of their own recent range. IV fell from 31.8% to 28.4% between July 22 and August 13 while the price rose 4.7%, indicating declining hedging demand into a rising price. Put/call open interest of 0.73 and a near-term skew of −0.4% are balanced. The term structure carries the more useful information: skew widens progressively to −2.0% at the three-month tenor and −3.8% at six months, with the October 16 tenor showing the chain's highest IV at 32.2% and an 11.3% implied move. That pattern is consistent with positioning around the following earnings prints rather than around the next two weeks. Combined with 12.0% of float short and 4.5 days to cover, high for a large-cap staple, the options and short-interest picture corroborates the fundamental view: no near-term dislocation is priced, but institutional protection is concentrated in the three-to-six-month window where the organic-growth question resolves. Complacency in near-dated volatility alongside elevated short interest is not a favorable combination for a long position at a price above consensus target.

Risk Factors scored 4.0.

## Management Quality

The evidence cuts both ways and nets to below average.

Negatives: $500.3M of buybacks executed at an average of $49.92 in Q1 FY26, now roughly 23% underwater, at a moment when the balance sheet was carrying 3.9x leverage. Total shareholder returns of 111.6% of FCF funded by asset sales. No open-market purchases by any officer across the reviewed Form 4 record, including at $34.80-$37.77 in late June and early July after a ~25% decline from January levels; every transaction is either an annual grant or a Code F tax-withholding disposition. We weight the absence of buying as a mild negative rather than a strong signal, since large-cap staples executives commonly accumulate only through grants and Code F dispositions are non-discretionary. Zero organic growth was delivered against organic net sales growth being the company-selected performance measure for three consecutive years.

Positives: compensation is aligned to the downside. CEO "compensation actually paid" was −$2,738,708 in FY24 and −$751,091 in FY25 against reported totals of $16.06M and $12.49M, as mark-to-market on prior equity awards more than offset all cash and new grants. Five-year cumulative TSR of 99.81 against a $100 base trailed the peer group's 113.32. The July 6, 2026 grants (Harmening received 69,214 shares plus 346,069 options against 454,169 shares held, at a grant-date option fair value of $9.45 versus $13.26 a year earlier) are struck near multi-year lows and create substantial leverage to a genuine recovery. Anti-dilutive underwater awards excluded from diluted EPS rose to 12.4M from 5.3M. Holding the dividend flat rather than raising it, and directing yogurt proceeds to ~$2.0B of debt reduction, are both defensible allocations.

The counterpoint worth naming: FY27 guidance embeds normalization of corporate incentive expense, meaning the FY27 plan is calibrated to a payout at target, an easier bar than FY26 delivered against.

Management Quality scored 4.5.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($46, +19.0%).** The FY27 headline of −8% to −13% adjusted operating profit is ~9 points technical (lapping the 53rd week, incentive normalization, and divestitures), so underlying trajectory is +1% to −4%. Q4 base volume of +1% versus −10% in FY25 is a genuine, non-artifact improvement in the metric management prioritized, and Q4 organic was flat with U.S. Snacks and U.S. Meals & Baking both +5%. If inflation moderates below the guided 4-5%, HMM savings drop through to margin rather than being consumed. The impairment risk is entirely non-cash and reverses mechanically if industry multiples recover or the risk-free rate falls. At 12x with a 7.5% FCF yield, 5.2x fixed-charge coverage, an undrawn $2.7B revolver, and only $1,054M maturing within a year, the market is pricing structural impairment into a franchise that still earns 20.7% margins in its largest segment. Management options struck at $9.45 fair value give the team maximum incentive to deliver the recovery.

**Bear case ($30, −22.4%).** Organic contraction reaches a fourth year and the price-investment strategy proves margin-destructive without durable volume: FY26 price/mix −1, margin −190bps, and consumers shifting to promotion. FY27 lands at the $3.00 low end while working capital continues to consume cash, so FCF conversion misses 95% and the dividend exceeds free cash flow. Leverage passes 4.3x with no path to reduce it, forcing either a dividend trim or a suspension of buybacks and further balance-sheet stasis. Any renewed share-price weakness re-triggers the goodwill test, and the 10-K has already named Blue Buffalo (sitting in a unit with −5 point organic volume and ~$5.65B of residual goodwill) as at risk. Pet, the designated growth engine, keeps losing volume while funding a fresh-food entry. Walmart at 22% presses on price. GLP-1 adoption and ingredient scrutiny compound category-level volume pressure. Consensus keeps falling and the 12% short base is proven right.

**Which dominates.** The bull case requires an inflection that has not appeared in any organic data series, and it requires the market to re-rate a business with 39% of EV in debt and no deleveraging capacity. The bear case requires only continuation of the observed trend. At a price above the consensus mean target, with estimates falling and the dividend consuming 83% of forecast free cash flow, the asymmetry favors reducing exposure.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $39 (+0.9%), bull $46 (+19.0%), bear $30 (−22.4%). Probability-weighted expected total return approximately +4% including the 6.3% dividend, against materially larger downside dispersion.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the September Q1 FY27 print. The shares have recovered 21.7% from the June 4 low into falling estimates and now sit above the $37.56 consensus mean; the September and October option tenors carry the widest put skew in the chain, indicating institutional protection is concentrated in that window. Income-oriented holders who require the 6.3% yield can retain a partial position, but should size it recognising that ~83% FY27E FCF coverage leaves no cushion for an EBITDA miss and that the board's decision to hold the rate flat on July 1 already signals limited room. Residual exposure should be materially below benchmark weight.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Q1 or Q2 FY27 organic net sales at or above 0% with price/mix non-negative, confirming volume recovery is not purchased margin.
2. FY27 adjusted EPS guidance reaffirmed at or above the $3.10 midpoint with FCF conversion tracking at 95%+ and no repeat of the FY26 working-capital outflow.
3. A price below $33, restoring roughly 18% upside to base case and pushing the dividend yield above 7.4%.
4. Clustered open-market purchases by three or more officers, which would materially change the management read.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE:** all of the above, plus net debt / adjusted EBITDA declining below 3.9x on a reported basis, and a resolution of the Blue Buffalo impairment exposure via a disclosed carrying value with adequate coverage headroom.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. A reduction in the quarterly dividend below $0.61, or a stated suspension of buybacks to fund deleveraging.
2. A further goodwill or indefinite-lived intangible impairment naming Blue Buffalo or Progresso, particularly if it takes book equity below ~$6.5B and pushes reported D/E above 3.5x.
3. FY27 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $3.00, or two consecutive quarters of organic net sales below −2%.
4. Loss or material renegotiation of Walmart terms visible in NAR price/mix.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q1 FY27 results, expected mid-to-late September 2026, are the first hard test of whether Q4's flat organic and +1% base volume were the 53rd-week and trade-phasing artifacts management described or the start of a turn. The Brazil closing, expected by end of calendar 2026, and the FY26 proxy (not yet retrieved) are secondary checkpoints. The annual goodwill test at the start of Q2 FY27 is the next scheduled impairment event.

## Conclusion

General Mills is a solvent, cash-generative franchise with a constrained capital structure and a shrinking top line. Fixed-charge coverage of 5.2x against a 2.5x covenant, an undrawn $2.7B revolver, and $1,054M of near-term maturities rule out a solvency question. What remains is a business in its fourth guided year of organic contraction, generating $1,626M of free cash flow against a $1,288M dividend and $13.15B of net debt, with $20.6B of goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles sitting at 2.80x book equity and a management-named impairment watch list whose trigger is the share price itself.

The 12.5x multiple and 6.3% yield look like value, but they are the market's pricing of a levered claim on an EBITDA stream guided down 8-13%, where roughly nine points of that decline is technical and the rest is not. Since the last review the shares have risen 4.7% while current-year consensus fell 6.2% over 90 days and current-quarter consensus fell 10% in 30 days, leaving the price 2.9% above the $37.56 analyst mean with 12.0% of float short.
