## Executive Summary

ICU Medical is midway through a slow, expensive repair of a balance sheet and a product portfolio it bought in two large acquisitions: Hospira Infusion Systems (2017) and Smiths Medical (January 2022). The repair is working on the metrics that matter for cash: FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA rose 9.0% to $403.8M on revenue that fell 6.3% to $2,231.3M, lifting the Adjusted EBITDA margin 255bps to 18.10%. The May 2025 sale of 60% of IV Solutions to Otsuka removed a low-margin, capital-intensive business and delivered $211.2M of cash, $200.0M of which retired Term Loan A. The October 2025 refinancing pushed the TLA and revolver to 2030 and cut effective rates from 7.02% to 5.64% (TLA) and 7.56% to 6.56% (TLB), reducing net interest expense to $16.5M in Q1 2026 from $22.0M. The FDA closed the 2021 Oakdale Warning Letter on February 5, 2026.

What has not yet arrived is operating earnings power. Q1 2026 revenue fell 12.3% to $530.2M on the divestiture; the two franchises the company is actually trying to grow — Consumables and Infusion Systems — grew 5.9% reported but only about 3.5% in constant currency after backing out $10.4M of disclosed FX benefit. Gross margin expanded 414bps to 38.9%, yet gross profit dollars *fell* 1.8% to $206.2M, because the margin gain was mix arithmetic from exiting IV Solutions. SG&A worsened 315bps as a percentage of revenue to 29.2%, R&D fell 8.6% to $21.3M, restructuring and integration expense was flat at $16.8M in year five after Smiths, and operating income was essentially unchanged at $13.6M (2.6% margin). Q1's reported net income of $30.1M and diluted EPS of $1.20 sat on top of a **pre-tax loss of $4.0M**, converted by a $34.7M tax benefit of which $29.2M was a statute-of-limitations release. FY2025's $0.7M of GAAP net income depended on a $44.8M pre-tax divestiture gain.

The stock has moved roughly 40% since mid-May 2026 (insiders were selling at $124–132 in May), and the specific catalyst for that move is not identifiable in the filings available. At $171.96, enterprise value is $5,299.4M against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $403.8M — 13.1x, versus the 12x multiple the one external fair-value estimate uses to reach ~$200 on an EBITDA base 24% above FY2025's. On consensus non-GAAP EPS the stock is 21.1x current year ($8.16) and 18.4x next year ($9.34), a PEG of about 1.3. The consensus mean target of $176.63 is 2.7% above spot, and analyst sentiment has degraded (one strong-buy moved to hold in the last month). Near-term ATM implied volatility of 57.2% against 38.7% one-year realized, in backwardation with a ±9.7% implied move on the August 21 expiry, prices an imminent binary. The improving trajectory is real; the entry price is not attractive.

## Company Overview

ICU Medical manufactures intravenous therapy hardware and disposables, reporting one segment across three disclosed product lines. **Consumables** (Q1 2026: $278.3M, 52% of revenue) is the historical franchise — Clave/MicroClave/NanoClave needlefree connectors, Neutron, Tego, ClearGuard HD, SwabCap, oncology closed-system transfer devices (ChemoLock, ChemoClave), vascular access (Jelco, Port-A-Cath, PowerWand) and tracheostomy (Bivona, BLUselect). **Infusion Systems** ($179.6M, 34%) covers large-volume pumps and their dedicated disposables and software: the Plum Duo/Plum Solo platform cleared in April 2025, the legacy Plum 360 (Best in KLAS smart pump for eight consecutive years), PlumSets, CADD ambulatory pumps, Medfusion syringe pumps, and MedNet/PharmGuard software. **Vital Care** ($72.3M, 14%, down from ~28% a year earlier) holds hemodynamic monitoring, anesthesia/respiratory, temperature management, and the IV Solutions business now sold on behalf of the Otsuka joint venture.

The economics are razor/razor-blade with unusually long tails. A pump placed today generates dedicated set, software and service revenue across the multi-year life of a hospital fleet, and switching requires clinical retraining, drug-library rebuilds and EHR interoperability work. Hardware placements are therefore a leading indicator of consumable revenue. The Q1 2026 jump in deferred equipment revenue — $42.7M of equipment deferred pending implementation versus $19.9M a year earlier, plus $10.0M of software deferrals versus $1.2M, lifting contract liabilities to $67.5M from $41.2M — is the most useful forward datapoint in the filings. It is period activity, not a backlog figure, and the company does not define it as backlog.

Manufacturing is offshore-heavy: gross PP&E of $1,105.9M includes $169.8M in Costa Rica, $129.0M in Mexico and $73.2M elsewhere in Latin America. All Clave products come from a single Salt Lake City plant, an explicitly disclosed single point of failure. 39% of FY2025 revenue was non-US.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue quality.** FY2025 revenue of $2,231.3M declined 6.3%, with the IV Solutions divestiture removing roughly $213.9M. Consumables grew 6.8% and Infusion Systems 4.9%, a combined 6.0% on $1,793.4M. In Q1 2026 the same two lines grew 4.5% and 8.0% reported, but the company disclosed constant-currency growth of 2.0% and 5.8% — about 3.5% combined, with roughly 240bps of the reported figure supplied by currency. Vital Care excluding IV Solutions was still shrinking. On reported organic revenue, this is a low-single-digit grower today, and the case for acceleration rests on the deferred-equipment conversion, which is a timing hypothesis until it appears in revenue.

**Margin structure.** The 414bps of Q1 gross margin expansion came with a 1.8% decline in gross profit dollars. Inside COGS, quality remediation and recall costs fell $2.6M to $7.4M while tariffs and duties rose $7.8M to $13.1M. Excluding tariffs entirely, gross margin would have been 41.4% versus 35.6%. Below the gross line, the shrunken revenue base drove negative operating leverage: SG&A of $154.6M fell only 1.7% but rose to 29.2% of revenue from 26.0%. Restructuring, strategic transaction and integration expense of $16.8M was flat year over year — the clearest evidence that the "transitional costs roll off" argument has not yet been demonstrated.

**Earnings quality.** The five-year GAAP record is +$103.1M (2021), −$74.3M, −$29.7M, −$117.7M, +$0.7M (2025), against Adjusted EBITDA rising from $260.7M to $403.8M over the same period. The reconciling items are restructuring, integration, quality remediation and acquired-intangible amortization. FY2025's return to nominal GAAP profit required a $44.8M divestiture gain against roughly $83M of net interest on $42.8M of operating income. Q1 2026's $1.20 of diluted EPS required a 873% effective tax rate. Neither period demonstrates sustained operating profitability, which is why Adjusted EBITDA and cash conversion, not EPS, are the investable metrics here, and why the 21.1x current-year consensus P/E is a multiple on a number that excludes costs the company has been incurring for five consecutive years.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 free cash flow was $91.8M, a 4.1% margin and a 2.1% yield on the current market capitalization. Q1 2026 operating cash flow *fell* to $38.9M from $51.3M despite the higher reported net income, consistent with the earnings being non-cash tax benefits. Management reaffirmed 2026 capex of $85–100M plus $60–80M of "other future capital investments" covering restructuring, quality-system work and acquired field-action liabilities — $145–180M of committed outflow that caps 2026 free cash flow near or below the FY2025 level. Q1 also carried $38.8M of cash tax withholding on net share settlement of the March 2026 performance-share vesting (versus $8.4M a year earlier); a financing line, but real cash.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** At March 31, 2026: total assets $4,013.8M, cash $288.3M, inventories $605.6M, goodwill $1,485.6M, other intangibles $599.0M, Otsuka JV equity method investment $130.9M, total equity $2,111.9M. Tangible book equity is approximately $27.4M, or $1.10 per share — essentially all book equity is acquisition intangibles. No goodwill impairment has been recorded.

Term debt principal is $1,289.8M (TLA $745.3M, TLB $544.5M) against $288.3M of cash, so net debt is $1,001.5M and net leverage 2.48x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA. Covenants allow 4.50x secured net leverage, stepping to 4.00x from Q2 2027, with a 3.00x minimum interest coverage — Adjusted EBITDA could fall roughly 38% before testing the tighter limit, and interest coverage on annualized Q1 interest is about 5.4x. The $500M revolver is undrawn and the current ratio is 2.39. Ratings are Moody's B1, Fitch BB, S&P BB− (S&P outlook positive).

Two structural items deserve weight. First, **Term Loan B matures January 6, 2029, and if any TLB is outstanding 91 days before that date, the new TLA and the revolver accelerate** — $582.0M comes due in 2029 and $637.5M in 2030, and the springing clause means the 2029 tower cannot be deferred. Management states it is monitoring liquidity to address the potential earlier repayment obligation. With 2.5 years of runway, an undrawn revolver and 2.5x net leverage, a routine refinancing is the base case. Second, **the $750M swap paying 1.17% matured March 30, 2026** — Q1 2026 enjoyed its full benefit. Replacements are a $225M swap at 3.302%, an existing $300M at 3.88% to June 2028, and $173.7M at 1.32% maturing March 2027. Cash interest from Q2 2026 onward may not fall in line with the Q1 run-rate, since refinancing spread savings are partly offset by a step-up in hedged rates. Disclosed sensitivity is roughly $12.9M of annual interest per 100bps before swap offsets.

Capital returns are unavailable: a $100M authorization dating to August 2019 remains entirely unused, and the company states it is limited on share purchases under the credit agreement.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus non-GAAP EPS is $8.16 for the current year and $9.34 for next, implying 14.5% growth — driven by margin and interest, not volume. Estimate revisions are stable rather than inflecting: +0.9% for the current year and +1.6% for next year over 90 days, with the *next quarter* cut 3.0%. This is nowhere near the revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection, and it should not be treated as one.

The delivery record is better than the revisions imply: four consecutive beats, at +37.3%, +38.9%, +10.7% and +10.3%. The deceleration in surprise magnitude from ~38% to ~10% reflects the easy comparisons from quality-cost normalization having largely been harvested, with consensus catching up.

Three specific growth drivers are identifiable:

1. **Plum precision placement conversion.** The doubling of deferred equipment revenue and the eightfold jump in deferred software revenue are consistent with pumps sitting in hospitals awaiting go-live. If they convert, reported revenue steps up with a lag and pulls consumables behind it for years. This is the strongest element of the bull case and it is mechanically supported by the razor/blade model.
2. **Mix and cost normalization.** Adjusted EBITDA grew 9.0% on declining revenue in FY2025. Quality remediation costs are falling ($7.4M from $10.0M in the quarter). If restructuring and integration expense — flat at $16.8M per quarter — finally declines, GAAP converges upward toward cash earnings.
3. **Tariff refund optionality.** The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling against IEEPA tariff authority and the subsequent Court of International Trade ruling on refunds give ICU Medical a claim it describes as potentially "material," with $0 recognized. This is an unquantified, off-balance-sheet asset.

Against these: the CADD and Medfusion 510(k) applications were withdrawn in Q1 2026 at the FDA's request for additional testing, which stalls the refresh of two legacy Smiths platforms; R&D fell 8.6% in the same quarter; and the customer register is concentrating, with one distributor at 20% of consolidated net sales versus 17% a year earlier. The mix shift away from Vital Care changed the *product* mix without diversifying the *customer* base.

## Competitive Landscape

US infusion is effectively a three-player market — ICU Medical, Becton Dickinson and Baxter — with B. Braun and Fresenius Kabi in adjacent categories. ICU Medical is the smallest of the three. The most cited bull argument is that ICUI is taking pump share because of quality problems at BDX and underinvestment at BAX, and that the Plum precision platform's connectivity features will improve retention. That claim comes from a single fund letter and could not be corroborated against independent market-share data or competitor filings; treat it as unconfirmed opinion. Two filing-level facts are directionally consistent with it: eight consecutive Best-in-KLAS awards for Plum 360, and the doubling of deferred equipment revenue in Q1 2026.

The structural counterweight is disclosed in the 10-K: hospital purchasing is consolidating into GPOs and IDNs that favor full-line contracts, which disadvantages the smallest of three suppliers competing for shelf space against broader portfolios.

The uncomfortable symmetry: ICUI is reportedly winning share on a competitor's quality problems while itself operating under an April 2025 Warning Letter, having just withdrawn two 510(k) applications, and having issued four Class 1 recalls in 2024–2025 (tracheostomy tube kits, paraPAC plus ventilators, ProPort venous access, CADD Solis). A serious quality event at ICU Medical would cost revenue and hand back the share the thesis depends on. The FDA's QMSR final rule took effect February 2, 2026, adding an ISO 13485 transition on top of open remediation.

**Competitive position score: 5.5** — trajectory modestly improving on placements and the closure of the 2021 Warning Letter, capped by scale disadvantage in full-line contracting and unresolved regulatory execution.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory (highest-consequence, least visible).** The 2021 Smiths Warning Letter closed February 5, 2026 — a genuine positive, with $19.2M of remaining field-service corrective action still accrued out of $26.4M total. But a second Warning Letter dated April 4, 2025 remains open, citing changes to the MedFusion Model 4000 and CADD Solis VIP that require new 510(k) clearances. Those applications, described in the February 2026 10-K as "under review," were **withdrawn** in Q1 2026 after the FDA requested additional testing, with resubmission "as soon as possible." The 10-Q simultaneously states there were no material changes to risk factors, and no loss contingency is recorded. Withdraw-and-resubmit is frequently procedural and is not evidence of a safety finding; the near-simultaneous closure of the older letter cuts the other way. Two legacy Smiths platforms nonetheless cannot be refreshed on the original timeline, which pressures revenue and, over time, the intangibles carried from the 2022 acquisition against $27.4M of tangible equity.

**Tariffs — two-sided and datable.** Q1 2026 tariffs and duties were $13.1M versus $5.4M; 4x that is ~$52M annually, or ~13% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, though the regime changed mid-quarter so it is a sensitivity, not a run-rate. Costa Rica's reciprocal tariff rose from 10% to 15% on July 31, 2025, and Costa Rica is the second-largest manufacturing base. The September 2025 Commerce Section 232 investigation into medical consumables and equipment covers "the vast majority of our product portfolio," on a statutory clock of 270 days plus up to 90 plus 15 — a decision window landing roughly mid-to-late 2026. On the other side, the IEEPA refund claim is unquantified at $0 recognized. Both directions are material and neither is in the numbers.

**Customer concentration.** One distributor at 20% of consolidated net sales, up from 17%. Part of the increase is mechanical (divesting IV Solutions removed revenue that flowed through other channels), and the counterparty is most plausibly a large med-surg wholesaler rather than end demand. The metric is nonetheless moving the wrong way.

**Concentration of production and liability.** All Clave products from one Salt Lake City facility; single-source suppliers for certain resins; product liability insurance of $50M per occurrence against a portfolio of infusion pumps, tracheostomy tubes and vascular access devices with four recent Class 1 recalls.

**Capital structure.** The January 2029 TLB maturity with a 91-day springing acceleration on the TLA and revolver, hedge step-up from Q2 2026, and a refinancing that will be priced against prevailing rates at the time.

**Governance/disclosure.** Three items cluster without resolution: a February 20, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K amending the Executive Severance Plan, a Q1 2026 stock-comp increase attributed partly to "the adoption of a retirement policy" for equity awards, and Daniel Woolson's Form 4 title changing to "President" in May 2026 with no 8-K or press release located. This is consistent with succession planning but does not establish it. Jain remains Chairman and CEO.

**Score: 4.5.** Multiple unquantified binaries, none reserved, at a 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $219 (+27.4%)

Adjusted EBITDA reaches $470M in FY2027 as the $42.7M of deferred equipment revenue converts, consumable pull-through follows the pump placements, restructuring and integration expense finally declines from $16.8M per quarter, and quality remediation continues normalizing. Section 232 resolves benignly or with product exclusions, and a material IEEPA refund lands as unmodeled cash. At 13.5x $470M, EV of $6,345M less $1,001.5M of net debt plus the $130.9M JV stake is $5,474M of equity, or $219 per share. Optionality beyond that: ICUI is the smallest of three players in a consolidating market with a defined 2030 put/call on its 40% Otsuka stake, and the $100M authorization becomes executable once credit-agreement restrictions ease.

### Bear Case — $121 (−29.6%)

Adjusted EBITDA stalls near $390M. Tariffs run at $50M+ annually with Section 232 making the cost structural against a Costa Rica/Mexico manufacturing base; hedged interest steps up as the 1.17% swap benefit disappears; core organic growth stays at 3.5% constant currency while deferred equipment converts more slowly than the deferral flow implies; and CADD/Medfusion resubmission slips again, forcing an impairment test against $2,084.6M of goodwill and intangibles supporting $2,111.9M of book equity. At 10x $390M, EV of $3,900M yields $3,029M of equity, or $121 — approximately where the stock traded in April 2026. The bear case does not require distress; it requires only that the cash-earnings ramp priced into 13.1x fails to materialize.

### Base Case — $185 (+7.6%)

FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $440M (repeating FY2025's 9% growth, with the tariff drag offsetting further quality-cost relief) at 12.5x gives EV of $5,500M, equity of $4,629M, and $185 per share. Cross-check on earnings: 20x next-year consensus EPS of $9.34 = $187. The two methods agree within 1%.

Base-case upside of 7.6% against bear-case downside of 29.6% is roughly 0.26:1 — inadequate asymmetry for new capital, adequate for holding a position already established at a lower cost.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 2026 print, which lands at or immediately after this report's cutoff, followed by the Section 232 decision window (roughly mid-to-late 2026 on the statutory clock) and the Q3 print for evidence on deferred-equipment conversion.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**
1. **Constant-currency growth in Consumables + Infusion Systems above 6%** for two consecutive quarters, evidencing that the $42.7M of deferred equipment revenue is converting into recognized revenue and pulling consumables — the mechanical core of the bull case.
2. **Restructuring/integration expense below $12M per quarter** together with quality remediation below $6M, demonstrating that the add-backs are genuinely finite and that GAAP will converge upward on Adjusted EBITDA.
3. **Price below $145** (roughly 11x an FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $440M, restoring 25%+ upside to a $185 base case), or company guidance for FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA at or above $450M.
4. **Resolution of both tariff binaries favorably** — a Section 232 outcome that excludes medical consumables plus recognition of a quantified IEEPA refund.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. **A goodwill or intangible impairment charge**, or a CADD/Medfusion resubmission rejected or delayed beyond FY2026, either of which would confirm that the 2022 Smiths purchase price is not recoverable against $27.4M of tangible equity.
2. **Adjusted EBITDA guidance or trailing four quarters below $380M**, which would both break the improvement trajectory and reduce covenant cushion from 38% to roughly 34%.
3. **A new FDA Warning Letter, consent decree, or Class 1 recall on a Clave-family or Plum product** — the Salt Lake City single-site exposure and the share-gain-on-competitor-quality thesis both fail simultaneously in that event.
4. **Failure to term out Term Loan B by mid-2028**, which would activate the 91-day springing acceleration on $1.28B of debt.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on ICU Medical, and the trajectory of the underlying scores (4.4 in March, 5.3 in May, 5.5 in June, 5.8 in July) has been genuinely earned: the Warning Letter closure, the refinancing, the divestiture-funded deleveraging and 9% Adjusted EBITDA growth on declining revenue are real improvements. The overall score moves down to 5.3 rather than up because of price and event risk, not fundamental deterioration. The stock is 9.2% higher than at the last review with no new reported results in evidence, 2.4% below its 52-week high, 2.7% below a consensus mean that is drifting down in composition, and at 13.1x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA it already embeds a repeat of last year's cash-earnings improvement. The Q2 print sits directly on top of the report, priced at ±9.7%, with last year's equivalent event having produced a 13% single-day decline.

Three facts should govern how a holder thinks about this position. Organic growth in the franchises the company is trying to grow was 3.5% in constant currency, not the 5.9% the headline shows. GAAP profitability in the last five quarters has come from a divestiture gain and a discrete tax release, not operations. And the two largest swing factors for 2026 EBITDA — a Section 232 tariff decision covering the vast majority of the portfolio, and an unquantified IEEPA refund — are both unmodeled binaries whose resolution windows fall inside the next two quarters.

Hold what you own for the pump-placement conversion thesis, which has a credible mechanism and a leading indicator now visible in the contract-liability disclosure. Do not add at a 52-week high into an unresolved print. The re-entry level that restores an acceptable risk/reward against a $185 base case and a $121 bear case is the mid-$140s.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the earnings-surprise EPS actuals used in the report ($1.97/$1.91/$2.03/$2.10 per Phase 1) are non-GAAP and imply a trailing base far above the report's stated GAAP/diluted figures, and the peer-comparison claim that Merit Medical "trades near 15x forward earnings" was not supported by Phase 1 (only 28.8x trailing P/E is provided), which weakens the relative-valuation argument used to justify the entry-price caution.
