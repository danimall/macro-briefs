## Executive Summary

GEO is in the middle of the largest revenue expansion in its history and, simultaneously, its third consecutive year of declining operating income. Both statements are true from the audited filings, and reconciling them is the entire investment question. FY2025 revenue rose 8.6% to $2,631.5M while operating income fell from $310.0M to $257.5M and Adjusted EBITDA was flat at $464.4M versus $463.5M. Headline diluted EPS of $1.82 is roughly 70% attributable to the $232.4M pre-tax gain on the July 2025 sale of the Lawton Correctional Facility to Oklahoma for $312M; the company's own adjusted net income measure was $120.1M, or about $0.86 per diluted share — 30% below 2022 on 9% more revenue.

The forward case is stronger than the trailing one. FY2026 revenue is guided to $2.95-3.10B (+12% to +18%) on the 2025 award wave — Delaney Hall, North Lake, D. Ray James, Karnes, the lifting of the four-year Adelanto intake prohibition, USMS transportation, three Florida DOC managed-only contracts commencing July 1, 2026, and BI skip-tracing — plus two post-quarter ICE awards: Hudson, Colorado (reported ~$529M over five years, ~$106M annualized) and the 1,329-bed Hertford County, NC facility, the former Rivers Correctional Institution that sat idle at $36.0M net book value. GEO's release indicates Rivers should generate $80M of revenue in its first full year, roughly $60,200 per bed-year against the ~$45,000 per bed-year implied by management's blended idle-portfolio guidance. That is new, positive information: it removes the impairment overhang on the largest idle asset and suggests the activation pipeline is marked conservatively.

What stops this from being a BUY is price and unpriced risk. At $31.50 the stock sits 2.3% below its 52-week high after roughly doubling from the February 20 low of $12.51. Enterprise value of approximately $5.7B is 12.3x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, 10.7x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $535M, 25.8x consensus FY2026 EPS of $1.22, and 36.6x FY2025 adjusted EPS. Consensus FY2027 EPS was cut 6.5% over the past 90 days, and management is not guiding to full-year margin expansion. Against that, GEO carries 47.6% of revenue from ICE alone (up from 41.5%), an 8.61% weighted-average cost of debt, and three detainee-wage class actions — Adelanto, Colorado/Aurora, and Mesa Verde/Golden State — for which no loss has been accrued at all. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on the name and the overall score declines to 5.0 from 5.3, with the deterioration concentrated in valuation and risk rather than in the operating business.

## Company Overview

GEO owns, leases, and manages secure detention facilities, immigration processing centers, and community reentry centers, and provides electronic monitoring and supervision services. At December 31, 2025 the portfolio comprised roughly 75,000 beds across 95 facilities, including idle assets and development projects, plus electronic monitoring serving hundreds of thousands of individuals.

One housekeeping point that matters structurally: the "New Reit" label attached to the ticker is a legacy artifact. GEO revoked its REIT election effective January 1, 2022 and is a taxable C-corporation; the April 2024 Credit Agreement affirmatively prohibits GEO from re-electing REIT status. There is no common dividend (yield 0.0%), and there is no income thesis available here. All capital return runs through buybacks.

FY2025 segment structure:

| Segment | Revenue | Segment operating income | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. Secure Services | $1,827.0M | $328.6M | 18.0% |
| Electronic Monitoring & Supervision | $320.9M | $125.0M | 38.9% |
| Reentry Services | $286.5M | $61.0M | 21.3% |
| International (Australia, South Africa) | $197.1M | $16.5M | 8.4% |
| **Total** | **$2,631.5M** | **$531.0M** | **20.2%** |

Owned-and-leased secure services generated $1,388.3M and managed-only (customer-owned facility) $642.6M. Net PP&E of $1,884.2M against $3,843.6M of total assets is what separates GEO from a pure services contractor, and it is where most of the current optionality sits.

Customer concentration is the defining structural fact and it is worsening on every measure: federal agencies were 67% of FY2025 revenue (62% in 2024), ICE alone 47.6% (41.5% in 2024), USMS ~15.9%, BOP ~2.6%. Federal agencies represented 78% of accounts receivable at year-end 2025, up from 64%. Revenue *mix* is broadening — Florida DOC, USMS transportation, skip-tracing — but direct concentration data moved the wrong way, and mix shift should not be described as diversification when the underlying concentration metrics deteriorate.

### Balance sheet

Debt at December 31, 2025: revolver drawn $358.6M (commitment raised to $550M effective January 20, 2026), 8.625% senior secured notes due April 2029 of $650.0M, 10.250% senior unsecured notes due April 2031 of $625.0M, other debt and finance leases ~$36.9M — total $1,650.6M at an 8.61% weighted-average rate. By March 31, 2026 total debt was $1,590.3M with the revolver drawn $297.6M and cash of $80.2M. Net debt of $1,510.1M against guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $535M is 2.82x, inside management's sub-3.2x target. Interest coverage on ~$148M of cash interest is roughly 3.6x.

Near-term maturities are trivial (~$1.4M in 2026), with the wall at $651.5M in 2029 and $358.6M revolver in 2030. GEO bought four years of runway at a punitive cost of capital. This is where the rate environment bites: the working assumption that the 2029 and 2031 notes refinance materially cheaper is not safe. Every 100bp of refinancing cost on $1.275B of notes is ~$12.8M of pre-tax income, about 5% of FY2025 operating income.

Liquidity was tighter through 2025 than headline commitments suggested — $47.0M of incremental borrowing capacity at year-end after $358.6M drawn and ~$44.4M of letters of credit — improving to roughly $205-210M by March 2026. Restricted cash and investments of $179.4M are largely committed: Australian asset-replacement reserves, captive-insurer solvency, and deferred-compensation and executive retirement rabbi trusts.

**Financial health scores 5.2.** Leverage is manageable, the maturity profile is clean for four years, and the Q1 cash-flow rebound is genuine. Offsetting: an 8.61% blended debt cost, FY2025 free cash flow of negative $124.9M, operating cash flow far below net income, a receivable concentrated 78% in one payer with no reserve, restricted cash that is mostly unavailable, and unaccrued litigation exposure that would be a liquidity event against ~$205M of revolver availability. An Altman Z of approximately 2.18 at the current price sits in the grey zone, and would have read approximately 1.63, in the distress zone, at the ~$15 price where insiders transacted in March. The balance sheet did not change; the equity re-rated.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue growth is real, contracted, and better structured than in prior cycles.

FY2026 guidance, as reported from the May 2026 earnings call rather than a filing I can confirm: revenue $2.95-3.10B, Adjusted EBITDA $525-545M, GAAP net income $153-166M, EPS $1.10-1.25, with Q2 revenue $715-725M. Q1 2026 delivered revenue of $705.2M, up 16.6%, with Adjusted EBITDA of $131.4M (18.6% margin) and diluted EPS of $0.29 — a 213bp margin improvement year over year.

The award wave supporting it: Delaney Hall (1,000 beds, 15-year fixed-price ICE contract), North Lake (1,800 beds), D. Ray James (1,868 beds), Karnes (1,328 beds through August 2029), Adelanto (1,940 beds, with the four-year intake prohibition lifted in June 2025 under an approved settlement), a five-year USMS transportation contract across 26 judicial districts (~$60M/yr), three Florida DOC managed-only contracts effective July 1, 2026 plus a joint venture on the state-owned North Florida Detention Facility (~$100M/yr combined), and BI skip-tracing (up to ~$60M/yr, early in ramp). Post-quarter: Hudson, Colorado (~$529M over five years, ~$106M/yr) and Hertford County, NC (~$80M in the first full year).

Two structural features deserve emphasis. First, in the Delaney Hall, D. Ray James, Karnes and Folkston arrangements GEO retains ownership of the real estate while ICE contracts for support services plus exclusive use on a fixed-price basis. ICE pays a fixed price; GEO owns the building and provides operations. That shifts utilization risk from GEO to ICE, which matters when GEO's ICE census reportedly ran ~21,000 against ~26,000 contracted beds. Second, the Rivers/Hertford economics — ~$60,200 per bed-year of first-year revenue on a facility carried at $36.0M net book value, against the ~$45,000 per bed-year implied by management's >$300M blended idle-portfolio framing — suggest the remaining 6,646 idle beds ($192.5M net book value, $23.4M annual carrying cost rising to $26.7M by Q1 2026) are marked conservatively. First-year revenue at Rivers alone is 2.2x that asset's entire net book value.

The problem is conversion. Total segment operating income has been flat for three years. The FY2026 guidance midpoint implies a 17.69% Adjusted EBITDA margin, essentially flat versus 2025. Backing out guided H1 (roughly 18.5%) leaves implied H2 revenue near $1,600M on ~$271M of Adjusted EBITDA — a 16.95% margin, about 145bp below H1. Start-up and staffing costs on the Florida, Rivers, Hudson and Colorado activations are the most likely explanation, and management may simply be conservative. Either way, management is not guiding to full-year margin expansion, and that is the single most important qualifier on the growth story.

Estimate revisions corroborate the caution rather than the enthusiasm. Current-quarter consensus is $0.29 (+4.5% over 90 days) and current-year $1.22 (+2.1%), but next quarter was cut 10.3% to $0.30 and next year cut 6.5% to $1.66. This is not the pattern of a mispriced inflection where consensus chases the fundamentals upward; the out-years are being marked down while the stock rose. The earnings track record is respectable — three beats and one in-line over four quarters, including a 41% beat in Q1 2026 — but a 41% beat on a $0.20 estimate is a $0.08 absolute variance on a stock trading at $31.50.

**Growth prospects score 6.0.** Revenue growth of 12-18% with contracted backing and a genuinely undermarked idle-asset option, offset by three years of flat earnings power, guided-flat margins, and out-year consensus cuts.

What is present is a fully-priced recovery with an option attached.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is a duopoly of scale — GEO and CoreCivic — sitting downstream of a single dominant buyer whose budget and policy posture swing with elections. GEO's own total shareholder return index illustrates the amplitude: 325 at year-end 2024, 187 at year-end 2025 (a ~42% decline against a peer index of 114), then roughly a doubling in 2026 to date. The 2025 drawdown occurred *while* ICE detention capacity was expanding, because awards and per-bed economics arrived slower and thinner than the post-election narrative implied. That is the most useful historical calibration for underwriting the current re-rating.

GEO's durable advantages are physical and regulatory: an owned portfolio of secure-grade real estate that is extremely difficult to permit or replicate, four decades of ICE relationship history, and demonstrated willingness to litigate against state statutes designed to close its facilities. It won a permanent injunction against New Jersey's AB 5207 in August 2025 following a Third Circuit affirmance, and secured dismissal of California SB 1132's application to its ICE services in May 2025.

Three dynamics are eroding that position at the margin.

**The buyer is becoming a competitor.** The 10-K flags ICE developing its own facilities and non-traditional capacity, and adds a risk factor contemplating the sale of GEO-owned facilities to a governmental customer. The Q1 10-Q discloses that GEO "has also been in discussions with ICE regarding the potential sale of multiple facilities, subject to mutual agreement on price and our continued management of those facilities under long-term support services contracts," with no definitive agreement. If executed, that converts owned real estate to cash plus a management fee — asset-light, but it also compresses GEO's returns toward a services multiple and dissolves the ownership moat. This is distinct from the current fixed-price support-services contracts on GEO-owned buildings, which are a favorable middle ground, and the two should not be conflated.

**Alternatives-to-detention is being commoditized.** The ISAP repricing cost ~$88M of segment operating income in two years and the contract now runs one year at a time. That is a direct demonstration of monopsony pricing power applied to GEO's highest-margin business.

**State obstruction is a permanent, unpredictable cost center.** GEO is currently losing on Washington HB 1470 — the Ninth Circuit denied rehearing en banc on February 11, 2026 after vacating the district court's preliminary injunction — and the statute grants state agencies rule-making, inspection and testing powers over the Northwest ICE Processing Center plus a civil-penalty regime and a purported private right of action. Legal fees are expensed as incurred, not accrued in advance.

Contract duration compounds the fragility: the weighted-average period before next renewal or extension for acquired facility management contracts was approximately 1.3 years at year-end 2025, and roughly 18% of FY2025 revenue (~$469M across ~29 contracts) is subject to renewal or competitive re-bid during 2026.

**Competitive position scores 6.0**, down from 6.5. The real estate and relationship moats are real and the fixed-price structure is an improvement in contract quality, but pricing power in the highest-margin segment has demonstrably eroded and the principal customer is building competing capacity.

## Risk Assessment

**Detainee-wage litigation — the largest unquantified liability.** Four related class actions allege GEO must pay state minimum wage (and in some cases Trafficking Victims Protection Act damages) to detainees in the ICE-mandated Voluntary Work Program against the $1/day federal appropriation allowance. *Nwauzor* (W.D. Wash.) produced a $23.2M judgment plus $14.4M in fees and interest; GEO accrued $37.6M in Q3 2025 and filed a cert petition on January 9, 2026 after the Ninth Circuit denied rehearing en banc with six dissenting opinions. The Colorado/Aurora TVPA class was certified in 2017, had GEO's affirmative defenses struck on summary judgment in 2022, and on February 25, 2026 the Supreme Court affirmed the Tenth Circuit's holding that there is no immediate right to appellate review of a ruling on GEO's Yearsley government-contractor immunity defense. That decision is procedural rather than a merits rejection of immunity, but it removes the interlocutory off-ramp and pushes the case toward trial on claims permitting punitive damages. The Adelanto class (1,940 beds, class period from December 19, 2014 through final judgment, in a high-minimum-wage state) and the Mesa Verde/Golden State Annex class are stayed pending *Nwauzor*. **No accrual exists for Colorado, Adelanto, or Mesa Verde/Golden State.** If per-bed-year exposure resembles the Washington accrual, aggregate unaccrued exposure could be a multiple of $37.6M — significant but not existential against $1,504.1M of shareholders' equity, though a genuine liquidity event against ~$205M of revolver availability.

The offset cuts the other way: the Department of Justice has twice filed amicus briefs supporting GEO, arguing the federal $1/day appropriation preempts state minimum-wage law and that the Ninth Circuit's reasoning creates tension with other circuits. A circuit split with DOJ backing is a credible basis for cert and reversal, which would likely dispose of most of the portfolio at once. This distribution is genuinely bimodal and neither tail should be treated as remote.

**Concentration and appropriations.** ICE at 47.6% of revenue and rising, federal agencies at 78% of receivables, a credit-loss reserve of $857K, and a demonstrated 70% collapse in operating cash flow from a shutdown episode. Recurring appropriations brinkmanship makes this a live, recurring exposure rather than a one-off.

**Insurance gaps.** GEO "maintains a broad program of insurance coverage for these general types of claims, except for claims relating to employment matters, for which the Company carries no insurance." Self-insurance reserves for workers' compensation, general liability and auto rose to $67.3M from $56.9M, and self-insurance reserving is Grant Thornton's sole critical audit matter, with the auditor engaging its own actuarial specialist.

**Reputational and operational.** Colorado outlets reported in July 2026 that a GEO employee was arrested and charged in connection with the shooting of a protester outside the Aurora ICE Processing Center, and separately that a confirmed tuberculosis case triggered a dispute with county and state health authorities escalating to a formal Aurora City Council demand. These are externally reported and not addressed in any filing I reviewed. If substantiated, they map onto the 10-K's adverse-publicity risk factor, they concern the same facility at the center of the Colorado TVPA class, and a resulting negligent-supervision claim could fall within the uninsured employment-matters carve-out — an inference from the insurance disclosure, not a coverage determination. North Carolina's Governor publicly opposed the Hertford facility on July 24, 2026, though the state has separately enacted legislation mandating ICE cooperation.

**Idle-asset impairment.** 6,646 idle beds at $192.5M net book value with $23.4M of annual carrying cost (rising to $26.7M by Q1 2026), including Lea County (1,200 beds, $45.4M NBV) newly idled after New Mexico terminated its contract effective June 30, 2025. No impairment was taken in 2025 on the basis that undiscounted cash flows exceed carrying value — a judgment that depends on GEO continuing to win activations. Rivers materially de-risks $36.0M of that; the other $156M does not yet have contracts.

**Policy and tax.** The 10-K flags proposed California legislation imposing a gross-receipts tax on private detention operators effective January 1, 2027 if enacted. The rate I have seen described is high enough to be thesis-determinative for GEO's substantial California footprint (Adelanto, Mesa Verde, Golden State Annex, the newly purchased 770-bed Western Region facility, McFarland), and the specific bill and rate warrant direct confirmation before being used in a valuation case.

**Key-person and governance.** GEO replaced both CEO and CFO within six weeks while activating the largest contract wave in its history. Donahue gave retirement notice February 6, 2026 effective February 28; the founder, George Zoley, reassumed the CEO role effective March 1 under an agreement running to April 2, 2029 with roughly $7.2M of target annual compensation ($1.2M base, 200% target bonus, restricted stock of at least 300% of base). The Executive Retirement Agreement obligation grows from $16.3M at year-end 2025 to a disclosed $36.4M by year-end 2029, credited $1M annually plus 5% compounded quarterly, held in revocable rabbi trusts explicitly subject to creditor claims in insolvency; two additional unfunded executive defined-benefit plans carry a $29.0M projected benefit obligation with zero plan assets. CFO Suchinski's 139,667 restricted shares were disposed to the issuer on March 31, 2026, leaving zero — the pattern of forfeiture on departure. The separation terms (mutual release, non-disparagement, preserved equity vesting, a substantive two-year consulting mandate at $104,167 per month) are consistent with an amicable retirement; I do not infer motive from structure. What is objectively true is that succession failed once, key-person concentration in the founder is now near-total, and the compensation architecture is founder-centric and escalating.

**Risk factors score 3.3.** The unaccrued litigation tail, the rising single-agency concentration, the uninsured employment-claims carve-out, the California tax proposal, and near-total key-person dependence are not reflected in a 25.8x forward multiple at a 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $41 (+30%)

FY2026 guidance proves conservative because activation economics are better than management has modelled. Rivers at ~$60,200 per bed-year against a ~$45,000 blended assumption is the first hard datapoint, and the remaining ~5,300 idle beds activate over 2027 at similar terms, taking Adjusted EBITDA toward $620-650M by FY2027-28. ### Bear Case — $20 (-36%)

## Conclusion

GEO presents a rare combination: strong, contracted top-line growth alongside three consecutive years of flat earnings power, with the gap fully explained by an electronic-monitoring repricing that transferred ~$88M of segment operating income to the government and by labor costs rising to 52.6% of revenue. The FY2025 headline of $1.82 in diluted EPS is roughly 70% a real-estate gain; the underlying adjusted figure of ~$0.86 is below where the business earned in 2022 on materially less revenue.

The forward picture is better. Revenue is guided up 12-18%, the fixed-price support-services structure on GEO-owned real estate shifts utilization risk to ICE, capital allocation has been well executed — 8.50M shares repurchased at a $16.55 average against a $31.50 price, the Lawton sale funding $300M of floating-rate debt retirement, and the Western Region purchase structured as a like-kind exchange saving ~$9.3M of cash tax — and the Rivers award suggests $156M of remaining idle net book value is marked conservatively.

None of that is worth 25.8x current-year and 19.0x next-year consensus at a 52-week high, with next-year estimates cut 6.5% over 90 days, three unaccrued detainee-wage class actions including one now heading toward trial on punitive-damage claims, 47.6% of revenue from a single agency and rising, an 8.61% weighted-average cost of debt facing refinancing, and a founder-CEO who reclaimed the role after a six-week double executive turnover while an escalating retirement obligation sits in revocable trusts subject to creditor claims. Base case upside of 8% against 36% downside is the wrong shape.

**HOLD.** Existing holders should own the Q2 print rather than add into a ±11.7% implied move with implied volatility 15.5 points rich to realized; overwriting near-term calls is a reasonable way to monetize that premium. New capital should wait for either evidence of margin conversion in reported results or a price near $24-25.
