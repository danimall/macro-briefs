**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Alexander & Baldwin is Hawaii's largest owner of grocery-anchored neighborhood shopping centers, operating approximately 4 million sq ft of GLA across 22 retail centers, 14 industrial assets, and 4 office properties. The company traces its roots to 1870 and holds irreplaceable real estate in a supply-constrained market where urban entitlement takes 9-15 years.

The dominant analytical question for ALEX today is whether the $21.20/share take-private merger — agreed December 8, 2025 with a consortium of MW Group, Blackstone Real Estate, and DivcoWest — closes as expected. The deal values the enterprise at approximately $2.3 billion. If it closes, the total shareholder payout is $20.85/share net of the Q4 2025 dividend already paid. If it fails, the stock likely trades materially below the merger price given operating restrictions during the pendency period and the absence of a clear standalone growth catalyst.

The underlying CRE business is healthy: same-store NOI grew 3.6% in 2025, leased occupancy reached 95.6% (up 100 bps YoY), and comparable rent spreads averaged +6.8%. These metrics confirm the strategic rationale for the buyers: necessity-based retail in a geography with structural supply constraints. For public market investors, the analysis reduces to merger spread versus deal-break probability.

## Company Overview

Alexander & Baldwin operates two segments:

**Commercial Real Estate (CRE):** The core business generating $202.9M in revenue (FY2025), comprising grocery-anchored shopping centers, industrial assets, office properties, and ~145 acres of commercial ground leases across Hawaii. This segment delivered $132.5M in NOI (+4.0% YoY).

**Land Operations:** A legacy segment being wound down, generating $3.8M in FY2025 (down from $39.3M in FY2024). The June 2025 Mahi Pono termination agreement resolved the complex obligations from the 2018 agricultural land sale, creating a $45.3M refund liability payable over 4 years but eliminating a $62M deferred revenue overhang.

The company employs only 91 people — an extremely lean operation for managing $1.66B in assets, indicating heavy reliance on third-party property management and a focused corporate structure.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement (FY2025):**
- Total Revenue: $206.7M (down 12.6% from $236.6M due to Land Operations decline; CRE grew 2.8%)
- Operating Income: $80.0M (essentially flat YoY)
- Net Income: $64.7M ($0.89/share diluted), up from $60.5M ($0.83/share)
- FFO: $95.3M ($1.30/share), down 4.7% from $100.0M ($1.37/share)
- Adjusted FFO: $75.1M, down from $80.1M

The FFO decline reflects the absence of Land Operations contribution. Core CRE performance improved across all metrics.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total Assets: $1.66B
- Total Debt: $491.6M (carrying value)
- Shareholders' Equity: $987.2M
- Debt-to-Equity: 0.50x
- Cash: $11.3M + $442M revolver availability
- 89% of debt effectively fixed-rate at weighted average 4.69%

The November 2025 $200M term loan (maturity 2030, swapped to fixed) positioned the capital structure for the merger by extending duration and fixing rates. Net Debt ($480.3M) / NOI coverage is moderate for a REIT.

**Cash Flow:**
- Operating Cash Flow: $79.6M (down from $102.1M due to working capital/Land Ops timing)
- Capex: $52.2M ($12.7M maintenance, $36.0M discretionary)
- Free Cash Flow: $27.4M
- Dividends Paid: $75.4M ($1.025/share)

The company's dividend exceeded FCF in 2025, which is typical for REITs that fund dividends from operating cash flow before discretionary development capex. Maintenance FCF (after maintenance capex only) was approximately $66.9M, providing adequate coverage.

**Earnings Surprise Pattern:** Four consecutive quarters of beats — including a 329% beat in Q2 2025 (likely driven by the Mahi Pono resolution gain) and a 35% beat in Q3 2025. This pattern is somewhat misleading because REIT earnings are distorted by non-cash items and one-time land transactions.

## Growth Analysis

Growth analysis for ALEX is largely academic given the pending merger, but the underlying trajectory is relevant for understanding deal rationale:

- Same-Store NOI: +3.6% (FY2025), driven by occupancy gains and rent spreads
- Leased Occupancy: 95.6%, up 100 bps YoY; industrial at 97.6% (+240 bps)
- Rent Spreads: +6.8% on comparable leases (retail +7.6%, industrial +7.1%)
- Lease Activity: 200 leases signed (64 new, 136 renewals) covering 722,700 sq ft

The development pipeline includes two projects under construction ($26.6M committed) and the company had guided for $75-85M in 2026 capex — projects that will now be completed by the new private owners. Hawaii's structural supply constraints (only ~5% of land is urban-entitled) provide a natural growth floor.

As a standalone entity, ALEX would likely deliver 3-5% annual same-store NOI growth — consistent with well-positioned grocery-anchored REITs. The buyers likely see incremental value in: (1) development/redevelopment projects, (2) ground lease optimization, (3) land monetization without public market discount, and (4) the $107M in deferred tax assets (carrying full valuation allowance as a REIT but potentially valuable to a taxable structure).

## Valuation Assessment

The merger price of $21.20/share implies:
- ~16.3x FY2025 FFO ($1.30/share)
- ~23.8x EPS ($0.89)
- ~$575/sq ft of GLA
- ~17.4x trailing NOI ($132.5M)

These multiples represent a modest premium for Hawaii-focused grocery-anchored retail. Comparable mainland grocery-anchored REITs trade at 14-17x FFO. The Hawaii premium is justified by irreplaceable assets, structural supply constraints, and stable demand drivers (military, tourism, government employment).

For current shareholders, the valuation question is: what is the merger spread worth relative to deal-break risk? Without visible current price data (the financial data feed returned no price history), I cannot calculate the exact spread. The merger was announced December 2025 with expected Q1 2026 close. Given the timeline, a definitive proxy was filed January 2026 and the shareholder vote should have occurred or be imminent.

If the deal fails, standalone value is likely in the $16-18 range (12-14x FFO), representing 15-25% downside from the merger price.

## Competitive Landscape

ALEX holds a dominant market position in Hawaii commercial real estate:

**Structural Advantages:**
- Largest grocery-anchored shopping center owner in Hawaii
- Land entitlement process takes 9-15 years — massive barrier to new supply
- Only ~5% of Hawaii land is urban-entitled
- Portfolio anchored by necessity retailers (grocery) providing recession resistance

**Demand Drivers:**
- Military spending (significant and growing)
- Tourism (structural, though volatile)
- Government employment (stable)
- Population density in a geographically constrained market

**Competitive Vulnerabilities:**
- 100% geographic concentration — no diversification
- Tourism-dependent economy vulnerable to external shocks
- Hawaii legislature has considered eliminating REIT dividends paid deduction (would fundamentally alter economics)
- Climate risks (wildfires, hurricanes, tsunamis, volcanic activity)

The buyers' willingness to pay $2.3B validates the strategic value. MW Group's local presence combined with Blackstone's capital and DivcoWest's operational expertise suggests the consortium values both the irreplaceable asset base and the operational improvement opportunity.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Merger Completion Risk (PRIMARY)**
Binary risk for current holders. Requirements: shareholder majority vote, no Material Adverse Effect, no injunctive legal action. Merger-related lawsuits have been filed. The break fee asymmetry ($155.3M parent fee vs. $50.5M company fee) suggests buyers are committed and the board negotiated from a position of relative strength.

**2. Deal-Break Scenario**
If the merger fails, shareholders face: (a) a stock likely trading $16-18 based on standalone fundamentals, (b) accumulated operational restrictions during the pendency period, (c) potential management distraction, and (d) the $50.5M termination fee payable by ALEX in certain scenarios.

**3. Geographic Concentration**
100% Hawaii exposure creates correlated risk across natural disasters, tourism downturns, and state policy changes. The August 2023 Maui wildfires demonstrated the tail risk.

**4. Mahi Pono Cash Drain**
$45.3M payable over 4 years (~$12.65M annually for 2 years, then $10M for 2 years). Manageable but reduces FCF during the merger transition.

**5. Hawaii REIT Tax Legislation**
The state legislature has considered eliminating the dividends paid deduction for REITs — if enacted, this would require structural changes. As a private company post-merger, this risk transfers to the buyers.

**6. Operating Restrictions During Pendency**
ALEX cannot pay regular dividends (only REIT-minimum distributions), cannot acquire properties, and faces limitations on dispositions, debt incurrence, and capex. These restrictions constrain standalone value creation during the merger period.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (Merger Closes — Base Case):**
- Shareholders receive $20.85 net consideration (after Q4 dividend already paid)
- ~3:1 break fee asymmetry ($155.3M vs $50.5M) indicates buyer commitment
- Blackstone's involvement provides financing certainty
- No antitrust concerns for a real estate acquisition
- Underlying asset quality reduces Material Adverse Effect risk
- Expected return = merger spread (price-dependent)

**Bear Case (Deal Breaks):**
- Shareholder lawsuit secures injunction delaying/preventing close
- A Material Adverse Effect triggers (natural disaster, Hawaii economic shock)
- Shareholders vote against the deal (unlikely given premium to unaffected price)
- Stock trades to $16-18 standalone value, representing 15-25% downside from merger price
- Dividend restriction during pendency eliminates income while waiting
- Operating restrictions during failed merger period impair competitive positioning

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For current holders:**
- **Reassessment timeline:** Shareholder vote date (should be imminent or completed given Q1 2026 expected close)
- **Upgrade triggers to BUY:** Stock trading at >3% discount to net merger consideration ($20.85) with no new legal/regulatory obstacles to close
- **Downgrade triggers to AVOID:** (1) Injunction granted by court delaying close beyond Q2 2026, (2) Material Adverse Effect declared by either party, (3) evidence of financing contingency failure

**If merger closes:** Exit at $21.20 minus any additional dividends. No further action required.

**If merger fails:** Reassess standalone value at 13-14x FFO ($1.30 × 13-14 = $16.90-$18.20). At those levels, the underlying business quality supports a position, but the stock would need to settle before entering.

## Conclusion

ALEX is a merger arbitrage situation with limited analytical value as a traditional equity investment. The underlying CRE business is performing well — occupancy improving, rents growing, assets irreplaceable — which is why Blackstone/MW Group/DivcoWest agreed to pay $2.3B. The deal structure (~3:1 break fee asymmetry, straightforward regulatory path) suggests high probability of completion.

The recommendation is HOLD. Investors already positioned have a clear path to the $20.85 net consideration. New investors face a spread calculation that depends on current price (unavailable in our data), deal-break probability (estimated 5-10% based on clean structure and committed buyers), and time value of capital. Without knowing the current spread, I cannot recommend actively establishing new positions — this is purely a function of price versus the $20.85 payout, and is better evaluated through merger arbitrage frameworks than fundamental equity analysis.

For fundamental equity investors, there is limited reason to engage with a name where upside is capped at merger price and the operating restrictions during pendency prevent value-creating actions. The time to own ALEX for fundamental reasons was before the merger announcement; now the asset belongs to merger arbitrage desks.
