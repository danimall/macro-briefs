**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Alexander & Baldwin is a Hawaii-focused REIT owning the largest grocery-anchored shopping center portfolio in the state (~4 million sq ft GLA, 95.6% leased occupancy). The company has strong operating fundamentals: 3.6% same-store NOI growth, +10.2% new lease rent spreads, and moderate leverage (0.68x debt/equity). CRE revenue grew 2.8% YoY to $202.9M in FY2025 with net income increasing 6.8% to $64.7M.

The investment thesis is now entirely defined by the December 8, 2025 definitive merger agreement with a Blackstone/DivcoWest/MW Group joint venture at $21.20 per share in cash (~$2.3B enterprise value). The deal was expected to close Q1 2026, with a definitive proxy filed January 23, 2026. ALEX is therefore a merger arbitrage position where the key variables are deal completion probability, timeline, and the spread to the $21.20 offer price.

The underlying business quality is high — Hawaii's geographic barriers to entry (only 5% of land is urban-entitled, 9-15 year entitlement process) create a near-monopolistic local position that is essentially impossible to replicate. The buyers are paying 16.3x 2025 FFO, which appears reasonable but not generous for an irreplaceable portfolio. The asymmetric termination fee structure ($155.3M parent breakup fee vs. $50.5M company termination fee) provides meaningful downside protection for shareholders in the event of buyer failure.

## Company Overview

Alexander & Baldwin traces its origins to 1870, evolving from a Hawaiian sugar plantation into the state's premier commercial real estate company. The company converted to REIT status and has been simplifying its business model by monetizing legacy land holdings while growing its core CRE portfolio.

**Portfolio Composition (as of December 31, 2025):**
- 22 retail centers (grocery-anchored, neighborhood shopping centers)
- 14 industrial assets
- 4 office properties
- ~4 million square feet of gross leasable area
- 145 acres of commercial land under urban ground leases
- 91 employees managing the entire operation

**Two Operating Segments:**
1. **Commercial Real Estate** — $202.9M revenue (98% of total), the core income-generating business
2. **Land Operations** — $3.8M revenue (2025), legacy land sales/JVs being wound down

The company operates as a vertically integrated platform with in-house leasing, property management, construction, and development — unusual efficiency for a portfolio this size, evidenced by only 91 employees managing $1.5B+ in assets.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement
CRE revenue grew steadily from $194.0M (2023) to $197.4M (2024) to $202.9M (2025), representing consistent 2-3% organic growth. Total revenue declined 12.7% in 2025 due to Land Operations falling from $39.3M to $3.8M (only 130 acres sold vs. 430 acres in 2024). The core business continued growing regardless.

Operating income held flat at $80.0M despite the revenue decline, demonstrating cost discipline. Net income grew 6.8% to $64.7M ($0.89 EPS). FFO, the REIT-appropriate metric, declined 4.8% to $95.3M ($1.30/share) due to the absence of prior-year land sale cash flows and interest rate swap gains.

### Leasing Fundamentals
The operating metrics are strong across every dimension:
- Same-Store NOI growth: 3.6% (above grocery-anchored REIT averages)
- New lease rent spreads: +10.2% (28 comparable leases)
- Renewal rent spreads: +6.3% (115 comparable renewals)
- Industrial occupancy: 97.6%
- Retail occupancy: 95.4%
- Same-store leased occupancy: 96.8% (up 150 bps YoY)

### Balance Sheet
Total debt of $491.6M against shareholders' equity of $987.2M yields a 0.50x debt/equity ratio — conservative for a REIT. Debt maturities are well-laddered: $69M in 2026, $41M in 2027, $54M in 2028. The company executed a $200M syndicated 5-year term loan in November 2025, swapped to 4.69% fixed, likely in preparation for the merger.

Cash on hand is minimal ($11.3M) but $442M in revolving credit facility capacity provides ample liquidity. The weighted-average fixed interest rate of ~4.3-4.7% is manageable given the current rate environment.

### Cash Flow
Operating cash flow was $79.6M in 2025 (down from $102.1M in 2024, primarily due to lower land sale proceeds). After $52.2M in capex, free cash flow was approximately $27.4M. The dividend of $1.025/share exceeds FCF, though this is typical for REITs that fund growth capex from retained operations and external capital.

### Earnings Surprise Record
The company has beaten estimates in all 4 reported quarters, with particularly large beats in Q2 2025 (+328.9%) and Q3 2025 (+35.2%). This reflects both strong CRE operations and gains from the Mahi Pono settlement and other non-recurring items.

## Growth Analysis

In a standalone scenario (if the merger were to fail), ALEX's growth profile would be:
- **Organic growth**: 3-4% same-store NOI growth, driven by positive rent spreads and high occupancy
- **External growth**: Limited by Hawaii's small market — few acquisition targets exist at scale
- **Development pipeline**: $75-85M capex budgeted for 2026 on two projects
- **Land Operations**: Declining and being wound down

The structural growth constraint is geographic: Hawaii is a small, supply-constrained market. While this creates the competitive moat, it also caps the growth ceiling. The company's TSR of 101.19 vs. peers at 136.97 reflects this — investors applied a structural discount for single-state concentration and limited growth optionality.

With the merger pending, growth analysis is academic for current shareholders.

## Valuation Assessment

The merger offer of $21.20/share values ALEX at:
- 16.3x 2025 FFO ($1.30/share)
- 23.8x 2025 EPS ($0.89/share)
- ~$2.3B enterprise value
- ~$383/sq ft for the CRE portfolio

For a grocery-anchored retail REIT with 95.6% occupancy, 3.6% same-store NOI growth, and an irreplaceable geographic position, 16.3x FFO is within the range of comparable transactions but not at the high end. The buyers are acquiring below replacement cost — constructing this portfolio from scratch in Hawaii would cost materially more than $2.3B given entitlement timelines of 9-15 years.

The current investment question is purely about merger arbitrage: what is the spread between the current stock price and $21.20, and does it adequately compensate for deal risk and time value? Without current price data available, the exact spread cannot be calculated, but based on the expected Q1 2026 close timeline (which has likely passed or is imminent given the proxy was filed January 23, 2026), the spread should be minimal if the deal is proceeding normally.

If the deal breaks, the standalone value would likely be in the $16-18 range based on 12-14x FFO (reflecting the single-state discount and limited growth profile that existed pre-announcement).

## Competitive Landscape

ALEX occupies a unique competitive position that is nearly impossible to replicate:

1. **Supply constraints**: Only 5% of Hawaii's land is urban-entitled. The entitlement process takes 9-15 years. No new competitor can build a comparable portfolio in any reasonable timeframe.

2. **Dominant market share**: Largest grocery-anchored shopping center owner in Hawaii with 155 years of institutional knowledge and local relationships.

3. **Resilient demand drivers**: Hawaii's economy is anchored by federal government/military spending (stable) and diversified tourism (resilient). Population density and limited alternatives drive consistent retail demand.

4. **Vertical integration**: In-house leasing, property management, construction, and development with only 91 employees creates operating efficiency that new entrants cannot easily match.

The competitive position is the primary reason sophisticated buyers (Blackstone) are willing to pay $2.3B for a small, single-state REIT. The moat is geographic and structural, not operational — it cannot be competed away by a better-capitalized or better-managed competitor.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risk: Merger Completion
The deal requires majority shareholder approval and is subject to customary conditions. Key considerations:
- **Termination fee asymmetry favors shareholders**: Parent pays $155.3M if it walks; Company pays $50.5M if it walks (3:1 ratio)
- **Buyer consortium strength**: Blackstone Real Estate is among the largest and most experienced real estate acquirers globally; financing risk is minimal
- **Litigation risk**: Merger challenges are expected (standard for take-private transactions) but rarely succeed in blocking well-structured deals
- **No Material Adverse Effect triggers apparent**: The Hawaii economy is stable, the portfolio is performing well, and no obvious MAE conditions exist

### Secondary Risks (Relevant if Merger Fails)
- **100% geographic concentration** in Hawaii — natural disaster exposure (wildfires, hurricanes, volcanic activity, sea level rise)
- **Hawai'i REIT tax legislation** — recurring legislative attempts to eliminate the dividends-paid deduction would materially impair REIT economics
- **$45.3M Mahi Pono refund liability** — payments through 2029
- **Refinancing in elevated rate environment** — $69M maturing 2026
- **Limited growth optionality** constrains standalone TSR potential

### Deal Break Scenario
If the merger fails, the stock would likely decline 15-25% from the $21.20 level to a standalone valuation of $16-18. The $155.3M parent breakup fee ($2.13/share) would partially offset this decline. Key downside scenario: a material deterioration in Hawaii's economy or a specific property catastrophe triggers the MAE clause.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (Deal Closes — Base Case)
Shareholders receive $21.20/share in cash. The return is the spread between the current price and $21.20, earned over the remaining time to close. Given the proxy was filed January 2026 and the deal was expected to close Q1 2026, the timeline is short. The buyer consortium (Blackstone + DivcoWest + MW Group) has deep pockets and strong strategic rationale. Probability of close: 85-90%.

### Bear Case (Deal Fails)
If the merger breaks, standalone value is likely $16-18 (12-14x FFO), representing a 15-25% decline from offer price. The $155.3M parent termination fee ($2.13/share) provides partial protection. A material adverse event in Hawaii (natural disaster, severe tourism decline, legislative REIT tax action) is the most plausible path to deal failure. Probability: 10-15%.

### Upside Beyond Deal Price
Essentially zero. Competing bids are theoretically possible but unlikely given the go-shop period has passed and the consortium includes both a local partner (MW Group) and a global real estate leader (Blackstone). No other buyer can credibly replicate this partnership's advantages.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
- If the stock trades at a meaningful discount (>3%) to the $21.20 offer with no new negative deal signals — this would represent an attractive merger arb opportunity
- If a competing bid emerges above $21.20

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
- If the deal faces regulatory or shareholder vote obstacles not previously anticipated
- If a material adverse event occurs that could trigger the MAE clause
- If the buyer consortium signals financing difficulties (extremely unlikely given Blackstone's resources)

### Reassessment Timeline
- Shareholder vote date (likely Q1 2026 if not already occurred)
- Any litigation developments
- Hawaii economic data releases

## Conclusion

Alexander & Baldwin is a high-quality REIT with irreplaceable assets, strong operating metrics, and a conservative balance sheet. Under normal circumstances, the company's geographic moat and steady growth would make it a reasonable income investment, albeit one constrained by limited growth optionality and single-state concentration.

The pending $21.20/share take-private merger with Blackstone/DivcoWest/MW Group makes this a binary merger arbitrage position. Given the expected Q1 2026 close, the strong buyer consortium, the favorable termination fee structure, and the absence of obvious regulatory hurdles, deal completion probability is high (85-90%).

HOLD is appropriate because: (1) upside is capped at $21.20, (2) downside if the deal breaks is meaningful (15-25%), (3) without current trading price data, the exact risk/reward of the merger arb cannot be calculated, and (4) the time horizon is very short. Investors already holding should retain their position for the merger cash-out. New investors should evaluate only if the spread offers adequate compensation for the small probability of deal failure.
