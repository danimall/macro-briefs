**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

California Resources Corporation is the largest independent oil & gas producer in California, a pure-play in-state operator that has consolidated the basin through two all-stock mergers (Aera, July 2024; Berry, December 2025) and bolted on a first-mover carbon capture business via the Carbon TerraVault JV with Brookfield. The investment question is whether the market is correctly pricing a measurable earnings inflection that is already visible in consensus estimates. On the evidence, it is not.

The setup matches the strongest forward indicator available: violent, still-accelerating estimate revisions on a stock trading well below its peer multiple. Current-year consensus EPS has been revised from $2.66 to $6.50 over 90 days (+144.7%), with the 30-day move (+20.4%) confirming the trend is not decaying. Next-year consensus sits at $4.65 (+43.2% over 90 days), which is the cleaner post-hedge read on normalized earnings power. Against a current price of $52.12, that is roughly 8x current-year and 11x next-year earnings, versus a peer-group median P/E of 15.8x. There is a named, dated volume catalyst — SB 237 reopened Kern County permitting, CRC took 66 new-well permits in Q1, and management raised 2026 capex to fund a 7-rig H2 program — and two confirmed event catalysts in the last 30 days: CTV I's first CO2 injection in late May and Citi's upgrade to Buy on May 20. The lowest of 11 analyst targets ($72) implies ~38% upside; the mean ($82.45) implies ~58%.

The bear arguments are real but largely explain why CRC trades at 8–11x instead of 15.8x rather than negating the inflection: single-state regulatory concentration, a heavy hedge book that caps near-term cash participation in the oil rally, ~$1B of asset-retirement obligations, and recurring legacy liabilities. The headline Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $711M (-$8.02 EPS) is a non-cash mark-to-market on oil hedges driven by a ~40% Brent forward-curve spike, not operational distress: operating cash flow stayed positive at +$99M and commodity sales rose to $905M. We move the rating to BUY. This reverses the prior three HOLDs, which leaned on catalysts being unconfirmed (they are now confirmed) and on a stale internal score history that does not survive the corrected upside math.

## Company Overview

CRC operates two reportable segments. The cash engine is Oil & Natural Gas: ~154 MBoe/d in Q1 2026 (roughly 80% oil), drawn from conventional, low-decline reserves of 654 MMBoe (541 MMBoe proved developed at YE2025) across the San Joaquin, Los Angeles (Wilmington), Sacramento, and Ventura basins, plus a Utah/Uinta extension from Berry. The company also owns the Elk Hills power plant, which earns merchant power revenue and California "resource adequacy" capacity contracts while supplying its own field operations, a vertical integration no out-of-state E&P can replicate.

The second segment is Carbon Management (Carbon TerraVault), a CCS business storing CO2 in depleted reservoirs, run through a 51/49 JV with Brookfield. This is the strategic call-option on the energy transition, and it reached first CO2 injection at Elk Hills in late May 2026 (1.46M metric tons/year capacity, with stated ambitions toward 352M tons of permitted storage).

California's permitting and decommissioning-bonding regime makes new entry nearly impossible. That regime is simultaneously the company's moat — it deters competition and makes CRC the natural consolidator — and its largest existential risk if state politics turn against permitting again. Total assets nearly doubled from $4.0B (2023) to $7.4B (2025) through the Aera and Berry mergers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and production.** Oil/gas/NGL sales grew $2,155M (2023) → $2,537M (2024) → $2,910M (2025), with Q1 2026 annualizing higher at $905M/quarter post-Berry. Production rose 86 → 110 → 138 MBoe/d on a full-year basis, reaching 154 MBoe/d in Q1 2026. The growth is acquisition-led, but the SB 237 permit reopening now adds an organic drilling lever for the first time in several years.

**The Q1 2026 loss is a hedge artifact.** The driver of the net loss was a ~$792M non-cash mark-to-market loss on oil hedges, caused by the Brent forward-curve spike tied to the March 2026 Middle East conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption. The same hedge mechanic produced the 10-K's -7.2% operating margin and the distressed-looking 1.27 Altman Z-Score. For FY2025, net income was $363M, operating cash flow $865M, and FCF $543M (an 18.7% margin). Reading the GAAP loss as operational distress is the central source of mispricing here.

**The hedge book cuts both ways.** At March 31, 2026, CRC carried a $582M net derivative liability, and roughly 65% of remaining 2026 oil was hedged at a ~$64.99 floor. A further $10/bbl Brent rise would cost ~$195M in additional 2026 settlement payments. This caps near-term cash participation in the current oil rally. The hedge book rolls off into 2027, while the equity re-rating thesis runs off normalized forward earnings (the $4.65 next-year line already reflects the post-hedge picture).

**Balance sheet.** Long-term net debt was $1,310M in Q1 2026: $550M 8.25% notes due 2029, $750M 7.00% notes due 2034, plus a small revolver draw. The borrowing base was reaffirmed at $1.5B in April 2026, with ~$1.25B liquidity. D/E is ~2.0x; the current ratio of 0.89 is largely the $421M current derivative liability, not operational illiquidity. Management has been active on liability management: in March 2026 it issued $350M of add-on 2034 notes to redeem $350M of the higher-cost 8.25% 2029 notes (a $21M extinguishment loss for a lower run-rate), and the April 2026 revolver amendment cut pricing 25bps and removed a 10bps SOFR adjustment.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend has been raised steadily, $1.16 (2023) → $1.395 (2024) → $1.5675 (2025), with a current $0.405/quarter (~$1.62 annualized, ~3.1% yield, well covered by FCF). Buybacks totaled $377M in 2025 against a $1.78B program authorized through 2027 (~$600M remaining). Share count is modestly lower despite merger issuance (88.8M shares). The mid-year 2026 capex raise from $430–470M to $520–560M funds the 7-rig program; it is offensive capex enabled by the permit reopening, not a balance-sheet defense.

The financial health score is 6.0. Cash generation and liability management are solid for the business model, and E&P leverage of ~2.0x is reasonable. The score is held below 7 by the genuine ARO drag (~$1B), the recurring legacy liabilities, and the volatility the hedge book injects into reported results.

I am skeptical of "cheap" as a standalone signal, which historically sorts the wrong way as a value trap. The reason to reward cheapness here is the independent catalyst stack: SB 237 (named, dated), CTV I first injection (confirmed late May), and the Citi upgrade (confirmed May 20). Cheapness plus a confirmed catalyst is the configuration that clears the bar. Valuation scores 7.0.

## Competitive Landscape

CRC's competitive position is improving and structurally defensible. California is a high-cost, declining-production, heavily regulated basin, but it is also premium-priced and import-dependent (Brent-linked realizations of ~96–104%, with California premiums on NGLs and gas). The state's bonding and permitting regime deters new entrants and makes CRC the natural consolidator, which is exactly how Aera and Berry were acquired with limited competition.

The San Pablo Bay Pipeline shutdown and Valero's Benicia refinery closure (April 2026) reduce in-state demand outlets, but they also cut in-state supply, and CRC reports no realization hit yet from Benicia. The DPA-ordered Sable Offshore restart (~60 Mbbl/d) could compete for scarce pipeline capacity and pressure differentials, a watch item rather than a confirmed headwind.

The vertical integration (Elk Hills power, gas processing, resource-adequacy contracts) and the first-mover CCS position on owned pore space are differentiators no out-of-state E&P can match. CA resource-adequacy revenue is expected to fall $125–135M in 2026 as new capacity comes online statewide, a modest offset. Competitive position scores 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**California permitting/regulatory risk** is the central swing factor. Management explicitly warns permit issuance may not continue at the Q1 pace, and local jurisdictions retain authority to restrict or ban operations. This is a binary that could re-freeze the organic growth lever.

**Takeaway capacity contraction** is unique to a landlocked in-state seller: refinery closures and pipeline shutdowns reduce outlets, and the Sable restart could pressure differentials.

**Hedge-driven earnings volatility and capped upside** distort GAAP results and limit near-term cash participation in oil rallies. The cap is temporary (rolls into 2027), but it is real for anyone modeling near-term cash flow off spot.

**Carbon management policy dependence**: CTV economics hinge on 45Q and LCFS credits, EPA Class VI permits, and CO2 pipeline rules, with live activist litigation (the Kern County writ petition) and a federal administration cutting CCS grants.

**Legacy liabilities**: ~$1B ARO, the Signal Hill platform decommissioning (Oxy indemnity, ~$18M accrued and rising), and the CalGEM $25M Cat Canyon orphaned-well dispute (paid under protest, litigating to recover) are manageable but recurring cash drags.

**Integration risk** from back-to-back mergers (Berry purchase accounting remains preliminary) and **concentrated ownership** (four holders own ~41%) round out the idiosyncratic risk set. A March 16, 2026 officer/director departure 8-K is a minor governance flag.

Risk factors score 5.0. The single-state regulatory concentration and the permit-durability binary are genuine asymmetric risks that are partly — but not fully — priced into the discount.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 43.6% versus 36.2% trailing realized, so options are modestly rich, with the market pricing elevated uncertainty into the next month, consistent with the permit/Hormuz binary. The put/call skew of 9.7% shows elevated near-term put demand, and the put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.14 to 1.33 since early June as the price fell ~11%. The term structure is in contango (near 43.6% < long-term 45.7%), a normal/complacent shape that argues the defensiveness is event-hedging around the next print rather than a structural bearish bet. The 25% IV percentile within the current chain indicates the near-term vol is not extreme. Net read: the options market is hedging a binary, not signaling fundamental deterioration, consistent with our view that the discount reflects regulatory/hedge risk rather than business decline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** SB 237 permitting continues, the 7-rig H2 program drives organic production growth on top of Berry's full-period contribution, and the multiple re-rates toward the 15.8x peer median as the hedge book rolls off into 2027 and clean forward earnings become visible. CCS scales with policy support, adding a transition-equity premium. On $6.50 current-year EPS at a still-conservative ~13x (below peer median), the stock approaches the low end of the analyst range. Bull target: $85–91, in line with the consensus high.

**Bear case.** California re-freezes permitting or local jurisdictions restrict operations, killing the organic lever; the Sable restart pressures differentials; oil falls back toward the hedge floor, leaving the hedge book a net drag with limited upside offset; CCS policy support evaporates with federal grant cuts. The multiple stays at 8–11x and earnings compress toward the next-year $4.65 line. Bear target: ~$43, near the 52-week low, roughly 17% downside from spot.

**Base case.** Permitting continues at a moderated pace, the drilling program delivers, and the multiple partially closes the peer gap toward ~12–13x next-year earnings. Base target: ~$72, the lowest analyst target, ~38% upside.

The asymmetry favors the long: ~38% to base, ~63–75% to bull, against ~17% downside to bear. The audit's correction is material: the upside to mean consensus is ~58%, not the ~36% the prior HOLD framing implied, and 58% upside on a stock with accelerating estimates at a sub-peer multiple clears the bar.

On the prior trajectory: the score history (6.1 → 6.9 → 6.0 → 6.0 → 6.1) and three consecutive HOLDs leaned on catalysts being unconfirmed. Both the SB 237 permit volume and the CTV I first injection are now confirmed, and the Citi upgrade post-dates the HOLD framing. The price has fallen ~11% since early June on no fundamental deterioration — estimates rose over the same window — which improves rather than degrades the risk/reward. We do not treat the decline as a thesis break; we treat it as a better entry.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months**, tied to the cadence of permit issuance, the H2 2026 7-rig program ramp, and the 2027 hedge roll-off that lets clean cash flow surface.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Quarterly new-well permit issuance falls materially below the Q1 2026 pace (66 permits) or a local jurisdiction imposes an operating restriction, killing the organic growth lever.
2. Current-year consensus EPS revisions turn negative on two consecutive monthly reads, reversing the inflection signal that underpins the BUY.
3. The dividend is cut or the buyback is suspended, signaling FCF stress beneath the hedge optics.

## Conclusion

CRC is the inflection-BUY archetype: a stock trading at ~8x current-year and ~11x next-year consensus against a 15.8x peer median, with current-year estimates revised +144.7% over 90 days and still accelerating, a confirmed named volume catalyst (SB 237 permitting funding a 7-rig program), and a CCS business that just crossed from concept to operating reality. The headline Q1 GAAP loss is a non-cash hedge artifact, not distress, and the lowest of 11 analyst targets sits ~38% above spot with the mean ~58% above. The genuine bear arguments — single-state regulatory concentration, a near-term hedge cap, ARO drag — are the reason CRC trades cheap, not a reason the inflection is illusory. We rate CRC **BUY** with a base-case target of $72 (~38% upside) over 12–18 months, sizing for the permit-durability binary that the discount partly reflects.
