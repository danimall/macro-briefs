**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Digi International is a $2.5B market cap IoT connectivity company executing a transformation from one-time hardware sales to recurring subscription revenue. Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) reached $152M in FY2025 (+31% YoY), now representing approximately 35% of total revenue, with gross margins expanding 400bps to 62.9% and free cash flow margins reaching 24.5% on minimal capex ($2.6M annually). The business is asset-light and the subscription flywheel appears to be working.

The problem is valuation. At $66.88 (up 108% in 12 months), the stock trades at approximately 60x trailing EPS and ~27x FY2026 consensus EPS ($2.48). While the business quality merits a premium multiple, the recent complete C-suite transition (new CEO Bret Christensen, new CFO Robert Peterson) during a period of accelerating growth introduces execution risk that the market has not discounted. Revenue acceleration to 18-20% YoY growth in FY2026 is encouraging, but much of this is already reflected in the stock's near-doubling.

The stock is worth holding for investors already positioned, given the structural improvement and secular IoT/data center tailwinds. For new capital, the risk/reward at current levels is not asymmetric enough to justify a BUY recommendation — a 10-15% pullback would substantially improve the entry point.

## Company Overview

Digi International, founded in 1985 and headquartered in Hopkins, Minnesota, provides mission-critical IoT connectivity products, services, and solutions across two segments:

**IoT Products & Services (73.9% of FY2025 revenue / $318M):** Embedded and wireless modules, cellular routers, console servers (Opengear brand), RF products (XBee), and infrastructure management software (Digi Remote Manager). Products sold through major distributors (Arrow, Avnet, Digi-Key, Ingram Micro) accounting for ~60% of revenue.

**IoT Solutions (26.1% of FY2025 revenue / $112M):** Subscription-based services including SmartSense (temperature/condition monitoring for food service and healthcare), Jolt (cloud-based task/workforce management acquired August 2025 for $146M), and Ventus (Managed Network-as-a-Service for banking, gaming, retail).

The company employs 913 people globally with manufacturing fully outsourced to contract manufacturers in Thailand, Mexico, Taiwan, and Cambodia.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Acceleration:** After a channel inventory correction in FY2024 (-4.7% YoY), growth has meaningfully re-accelerated. Q1 FY2026 delivered $122M (+18% YoY) and Q2 FY2026 came in at $130.7M, beating the $126.1M consensus. Q3 FY2026 guidance of $130-134M implies an annualized run rate of $520-530M, representing sustained 20%+ growth from FY2025's $430M.

**Margin Expansion:**
- Gross margin: 55.7% (FY2022) → 62.9% (FY2025), a 720bps expansion driven by recurring revenue mix shift
- Operating margin: 11.3% (FY2024) → 13.1% (FY2025)
- FCF margin: 24.5% in FY2025 on $105M of free cash flow (only $2.6M capex)
- FCF conversion from net income: 258%, indicating high earnings quality

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt of approximately $137M ($159M long-term debt less $22M cash) against FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of approximately $108M (25.2% margin on $430M revenue) implies net leverage of ~1.3x — comfortable. The company deleveraged from $222M LTD in FY2022 and refinanced from a $350M term loan B to a $250M flexible revolver. Interest expense collapsed from $25M (FY2023) to $6.3M (FY2025).

**ARR Trajectory:** The critical metric. ARR grew from $116M (FY2024) to $152M (FY2025), +31% YoY. IoT Solutions ARR: $92M → $120M (+30%). IoT Products ARR: $24M → $32M (+33%). At current trajectory, ARR should approach $190-200M by end of FY2026, pushing recurring revenue above 35% of the total.

**Earnings Trajectory:** Trailing EPS of $1.08 understates normalized earnings power. Consensus estimates: FY2026E $2.48 (up 7.2% in last 90 days), FY2027E $2.77 (up 5.5%). The Q2 FY2026 beat ($0.62 vs. $0.58 estimate) and current quarter estimate revision to $0.66 (from $0.58, +15.1%) reflect accelerating momentum.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is driven by three reinforcing dynamics:

1. **Data center infrastructure spending:** The 10-K explicitly notes "significant demand from data center build outs" benefiting Opengear console servers. Hyperscaler spending directly translates to orders for network management hardware and services.

2. **Recurring revenue flywheel:** Each hardware deployment creates a recurring revenue stream through Digi Remote Manager, SmartSense subscriptions, or Ventus MNaaS contracts. As the installed base grows, the recurring revenue share of total revenue increases, improving margin mix and revenue visibility.

3. **Acquisition-driven expansion:** The Jolt acquisition ($146M, August 2025) extended the SmartSense platform into workforce management for food service — a logical adjacency that increases per-customer revenue potential. Management signals continued appetite for "acquisitions of scale."

**Revenue growth path:** FY2025 $430M → FY2026E ~$510-520M (20-21% growth) → FY2027E ~$575-600M (12-15% growth as Jolt contribution normalizes). EPS growth should outpace revenue growth given operating leverage and declining interest expense.

**Risks to growth:** Channel inventory cycles could recur. The Jolt integration is still early and unproven at scale. Legacy hardware product end-of-life creates a "treadmill" where recurring revenue growth must offset declining hardware lines.

## Valuation Assessment

At $66.88, DGII trades at:
- 60.5x trailing P/E ($1.08 EPS)
- ~27x FY2026E P/E ($2.48)
- ~24x FY2027E P/E ($2.77)
- ~25x EV/FCF (market cap $2.52B + net debt $137M = EV $2.66B / $105M FY2025 FCF)

Against peer medians (Communications Equipment): median P/E is 47.9x but the peer set is heterogeneous (HLIT at 200x, EXTR at 213x distort the median). The most relevant comparable, IDCC, trades at 24.9x with a 1.1% dividend yield. NTCT at 31.5x is larger and more mature.

The 27x forward P/E is defensible given 20%+ revenue growth, expanding margins, and the subscription model transition — PEG ratio on FY2026-2027 EPS growth of ~12% (from $2.48 to $2.77) yields a PEG of ~2.2x, which is elevated but not unreasonable for a recurring revenue business with 60%+ gross margins and minimal capital intensity.

The concern: the stock has appreciated 108% in 12 months and trades only 4% below its 52-week high of $69.81. Analyst consensus mean target of $68.50 offers negligible upside. The high target of $78 implies 17% upside, while the low target of $50 implies 25% downside. This asymmetry is unfavorable for new capital.

The valuation would become compelling at ~$55-58 (~22-24x FY2027E), which would align the PEG closer to 1.5x and provide a margin of safety appropriate for a company with a new management team.

## Competitive Landscape

DGII operates in a fragmented IoT connectivity market with no single competitor matching their product breadth. Individual product lines compete against well-resourced players:
- Console servers/data center: Vertiv, Lantronix
- Cellular routers: Cradlepoint (Ericsson), Sierra Wireless (Semtech)
- IoT monitoring: Various SaaS/sensor companies
- MNaaS: Multiple managed connectivity providers

Industry consolidation (Cradlepoint acquired by Ericsson, Sierra Wireless by Semtech) has reduced the number of independent competitors, potentially reducing pricing pressure. DGII's competitive moat rests on switching costs — once customers deploy Digi hardware integrated with Digi Remote Manager or SmartSense, the cost of rip-and-replace is high relative to the subscription fee. The mission-critical nature of deployments (food safety compliance, bank network uptime, data center management) creates stickiness.

However, this is a narrow moat. The company competes in multiple niche categories without dominant share in any single one. Larger technology companies (Cisco, HPE) could enter with bundled offerings at lower cost. The competitive advantage is primarily the integrated hardware + software + services stack rather than proprietary technology.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CEO/CFO Transition Risk (Primary):** Both CEO Ron Konezny and CFO James Loch departed in early 2026, replaced by Bret Christensen and Robert Peterson. A complete C-suite change during a critical transformation introduces strategy continuity risk, execution risk, and potential M&A philosophy changes. The market appears to be giving new management the benefit of the doubt given accelerating results.

**2. Acquisition Integration Risk:** Jolt ($146M) was acquired just months ago and is still early in integration. Goodwill and intangibles represent $744M of $923M total assets (81%). A slowdown in acquired business growth or failed integration could trigger impairment charges.

**3. China Supply Chain for Ventus:** The 10-K explicitly states Ventus relies "almost exclusively" on a Chinese manufacturer. In the current tariff environment, this creates direct cost and supply continuity risk for the MNaaS business.

**4. Customer Concentration (Emerging):** One distributor reached 13% of revenue in FY2025 — a new concentration threshold not previously breached. Loss or material reduction of this relationship would create a meaningful revenue gap.

**5. Valuation Risk:** At 60x trailing and 27x forward, any execution disappointment — a missed quarter, slower ARR growth, integration issues with Jolt — could trigger a disproportionate correction given the elevated expectations embedded in the stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($78-85 over 12 months):**
- ARR growth sustains 25-30%, reaching $190-200M by end of FY2026, driving margin expansion toward 65%+ gross margins
- Data center infrastructure cycle provides multi-year tailwind to Opengear, supporting 20%+ revenue growth
- New CEO successfully executes accretive acquisition strategy, expanding into adjacent verticals
- FY2027 EPS reaches $3.00+ as operating leverage compounds, justifying 28x = $84

**Bear Case ($45-50 over 12 months):**
- Jolt integration underperforms, ARR growth decelerates to 15-18%
- Data center capex cycle peaks or Opengear loses share to larger competitors
- New management changes strategic direction, creating execution uncertainty
- Channel inventory correction recurs, pushing revenue growth negative for a quarter
- Multiple compresses from 27x to 18-20x forward = $50-55

**Base Case ($65-72 over 12 months):**
- Company executes at consensus levels ($2.48 FY2026, $2.77 FY2027)
- ARR grows 20-25%, gradually expanding margin mix
- Stock trades at 24-26x FY2027E ($2.77) = $66-72
- Current price approximately reflects fair value with moderate execution

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $55-58 range (~22-24x FY2027E), providing margin of safety at current growth trajectory
- Q3 FY2026 results materially exceed guidance ($134M+ revenue, $0.70+ EPS), confirming new management is accelerating, not merely maintaining
- ARR update showing >35% YoY growth, indicating the Jolt integration is exceeding expectations

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
- Q3 FY2026 revenue or EPS miss guidance ($130M/$0.65 floor), breaking the beat-and-raise trajectory
- ARR growth decelerates below 20% YoY for any reported period
- New management announces strategic pivot away from recurring revenue model or pursues overleveraged acquisition (>3x revenue)
- Customer concentration reaches 20%+ for any single account

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected early August 2026 for Q3 FY2026). The company guided $130-134M revenue and $0.65-0.68 EPS for Q3. Any material deviation will inform whether the transformation trajectory is intact.

## Conclusion

Digi International's shift from hardware to recurring revenue is working: margins are expanding, FCF generation is excellent, and secular IoT/data center tailwinds provide demand visibility. The 31% ARR growth and 24.5% FCF margins are consistent with a high-quality small-cap compounder.

However, the stock's 108% appreciation in 12 months has pulled forward much of the value creation. At 27x forward and with a complete C-suite transition underway, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. Analyst consensus at $68.50 offers essentially no upside from current levels. The prudent course is to hold existing positions while awaiting either a pullback that creates a more favorable entry or Q3 FY2026 results that confirm the new management team can sustain the acceleration — at which point upgraded estimates would justify the premium multiple.
