## Executive Summary

Omnicom completed the acquisition of Interpublic Group on November 26, 2025, issuing 124.4M shares for $8.89B of consideration and creating the largest marketing services organization by revenue (LTM to June 30, 2026: approximately $22.4B). The first clean post-merger quarter delivered EBITA margin of 15.9%, up roughly 450bps year over year, and adjusted diluted EPS of $2.65 versus $2.61 consensus. Management raised FY2026 organic growth guidance to 4.5%–5.0% and says the $900M cost synergy target is on track. At $82.51 the stock trades at 7.8x current-year consensus adjusted EPS of $10.59 and 6.9x next-year consensus of $12.04, with a 3.88% dividend yield covered at roughly 31% of adjusted earnings and $2.3B of repurchase authorization remaining, about 10% of the share count.

The reason we are not buying it is a specific, unresolved measurement problem. Q2 revenue of $6,562.5M was essentially flat against combined prior-year revenue of $6,552.4M. The organic gain reconciles cleanly to $339.0M ($10.1M reported change plus $397.9M of dispositions less $69.0M of FX, on a $5,591.9M core base = 6.06%). But Omnicom calculates organic growth on gross revenue, which includes principal-based media trading pass-throughs, while Publicis and WPP report on a net basis. A consultancy reconstruction cited in trade press estimates roughly $500M of the $339M gain came from pass-throughs, implying comparable net organic revenue declined about 2%. We can verify the direction from filings: third-party service costs rose to 23.8% of FY2025 revenue from 21.3% in FY2024, and held at 23.2% of Q2 2026 revenue despite consolidating IPG Mediabrands, characterized on the call as generating little principal media. We cannot verify the magnitude; the cited $1.75B of Q2 pass-through costs does not tie to the 10-Q's $1,522.4M of third-party service costs. Omnicom does not disclose principal-media revenue.

That gap determines whether Omnicom is a share gainer that bought itself scale at 8x earnings or a share loser that bought itself cost synergies and is funding per-share growth with a debt-financed buyback. Three additional data points argue against paying up while the question is open: consensus EPS for the current quarter was cut 8.3% in the 30 days *after* the July 28 print (next quarter -7.5%), the stock sits 5.6% below its 52-week high after a 24% advance off the February low, and cumulative shareholder return has trailed the proxy peer group in each of the last three measurement years ($154.22 versus $224.01 at FY2025). We move to HOLD from the prior BUY. This is a downgrade on marginal evidence — post-print estimate cuts and no improvement in entry price — not a repudiation of the earnings power.

## Company Overview

Omnicom operates four networks — Omnicom Advertising (BBDO, TBWA, McCann), Omnicom Media (OMD, PHD, Hearts & Sciences, UM, Initiative, Mediahub, Acxiom), Omnicom Advertising & Marketing Services (Omnicom Precision Marketing, MRM, Omnicom Health incorporating IPG Health) and Omnicom Communications Network (FleishmanHillard, Ketchum, Golin, Weber Shandwick). These are aggregated into a **single reportable segment**, with the CEO, CFO and COO acting jointly as chief operating decision maker. Investors therefore cannot observe margin or growth by network, a material disclosure limitation when the central analytical question is where growth is coming from.

The 2026 revenue taxonomy is Integrated Media $3,259.4M (49.7% of Q2 revenue), Advertising $1,079.1M (16.4%), Public Relations $708.9M (10.8%), Health $586.0M (8.9%), and Experiential & Other $929.1M (14.2%). Roughly half the company is now media planning, buying and data. Geographically, North America is 60.8% of revenue (US alone ~$3.8B in Q2), Europe 24.2%, Asia-Pacific 8.9%.

Revenue recognition matters here. Omnicom reports net (agent) revenue in advertising, media planning, precision marketing, PR, health and branding, but gross (principal) revenue in experiential, most execution and support, and "certain media buying businesses" where it controls the buying process and takes pricing risk. Principal media sits in that last clause, inside Integrated Media, which grew 10%+ organically in Q2 on ~$3.1B of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was a charge year and its GAAP output is not usable as an earnings anchor.** Revenue rose 10.1% to $17,271.9M (one month of IPG), but one-time items totalled $2,141.4M pretax / $1,750.5M after tax / $8.50 per diluted share: $1,247.0M of severance, repositioning, real estate and contract cancellation; $547.1M of loss on assets held for sale and dispositions; $347.3M of acquisition costs in SG&A. The effective tax rate spiked to 87.1% from 26.3% because much of the charge base was non-deductible, producing a GAAP net loss of $(54.5)M and EPS of $(0.27). Adding back the charges gives adjusted EPS of roughly $8.23 versus $7.74 on the same basis in FY2024.

The data feed's 53.44x "forward" and "trailing" P/E is inconsistent with both the GAAP loss and forward consensus and should be disregarded. The usable anchors are FY2026 consensus of $10.59 (7.8x) and FY2027 consensus of $12.04 (6.9x). Cross-checking bottom-up: Q1 2026 actual $1.90 plus Q2 actual $2.65 plus Q3 consensus $2.56 plus Q4 consensus $3.22 = $10.33, slightly below the 12-analyst full-year figure, consistent with the five-analyst quarterly estimates having been revised down more recently.

**Q2 2026 run rate.** EBITA of $1,040.2M on revenue of $6,562.5M is a 15.9% margin, up from 11.4% a year earlier. Businesses being disposed of dragged EBITA margin 0.6 points in Q2 and 0.9 points in H1; excluding them, core margin was approximately 16.5%. H1 EBITA was $1,803.8M on revenue of $12,805.4M (14.1%).

**Cash flow requires care.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $2,938.2M against capex of $149.8M, producing free cash flow of $2,788.4M (16.1% of revenue). Capital intensity is under 1% of revenue, the structural attraction of the model. H1 2026 operating cash flow was *negative* $(932.4)M, driven by a $2,434.4M use of operating capital, chiefly accounts payable declining $2,278.2M. This is seasonal: H1 2025 was also negative at $(576.7)M, because holding companies collect from clients and pay media owners on a cycle that peaks in Q4. H1 cash flow cannot be annualized. Adjusting FY2026 for the roughly $341M of remaining severance and contract-termination cash and the guided $210M increase in net interest, a $2.0–2.5B FCF year implies a 9%–11% FCF yield on $22.6B of equity value.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt rose from $2,235.4M at December 31, 2025 to $6,665.8M at June 30, 2026: cash down $3,544.9M, debt up $885.5M. Uses were $2,988.2M of repurchases, $481.5M of dividends, $115.1M of capex and the $932.4M operating outflow, funded by $2,384.9M of new borrowings less $1,400.0M of repayments and $168.2M of disposal proceeds. Because June 30 is the seasonal working-capital trough and December 31 the peak, the increase is only partly structural. The credit-facility Leverage Ratio improved to 2.4x against a 3.5x maximum. Ratings are BBB+/Baa1. Commercial paper was used in H1 for the first time in years (average $147.3M, peak $632.6M, none outstanding at quarter-end), reflecting tighter intra-quarter liquidity management rather than distress.

March 2026 refinancing issued $400M 4.20% 2029s, $700M 5.00% 2033s, $600M 5.30% 2036s and €600M 3.85% 2034s, redeeming $1.4B of 3.60% 2026 notes at par; net incremental long-term debt of roughly $1.0B. Gross long-term debt of $10,183.4M carries a fair value of $9,624.7M, about 5.5% below par, consistent with coupons struck below current market yields.

Two balance-sheet items deserve explicit framing. Tangible book value is negative $13.9B ($9,661.8M equity less $18,740.7M goodwill less $4,834.5M intangibles), expected for an asset-light services business, but it means equity value rests entirely on cash-flow durability. Separately, a screening Altman Z of ~0.74 is structurally misleading for this model: gross media payables of $20.7B and receivables of $14.4B are pass-through client balances that dwarf net revenue, depressing working-capital-to-assets and asset turnover, while FY2025 EBIT/assets reflects the one-time charges. Covenant leverage of 2.4x against a 3.5x limit with no revolver drawings is the relevant read.

**Capital returns are doing measurable work.** The February 2026 $5.0B authorization included a ~$2.5B accelerated repurchase that delivered 24.7M shares initially and 7.5M on May settlement (32.2M total, implied average ~$77.6); Q2 open-market repurchases were 10.3M shares at $76.36 average. Share count went from ~198M pre-merger to ~322M at close to ~275M today — roughly a quarter of the IPG issuance already retired, at prices below the current market. The dividend was raised 14.3% to $0.80 quarterly ($3.20 annualized, 3.75% yield), consuming about 32% of adjusted EPS.

## Growth Analysis

The reported picture is strong and the underlying picture is unresolved.

Reported: Q2 core organic growth of 6.1%, H1 5.0%, FY2026 guidance raised to 4.5%–5.0% from 4.0%, adjusted EBITDA +20.4%, adjusted EPS +29%. Integrated Media grew over 10% organically. The US grew high single digits. Health and PR grew low-to-moderately. The Advertising discipline declined, with trade reports conflicting on magnitude (one says low single digits, another high single digits — unresolved).

Underlying: total revenue was flat against combined prior-year revenue. The growth is arithmetically concentrated in Integrated Media, which is where principal media sits, and third-party service costs grew faster than revenue in both FY2025 and Q2 2026. If a business generating little principal media (IPG Mediabrands) is blended into a business that does, the pass-through ratio should fall; it did not. Alternative explanations exist and cannot be excluded: Experiential & Other is a gross-revenue discipline that grew on World Cup activity, which raises pass-throughs independent of media trading; IPG cost classifications may still be mid-harmonization under preliminary purchase accounting.

The consequence for guidance comparability is direct. Omnicom's raised 4.5%–5.0% is on a gross basis. Publicis simultaneously raised FY2026 guidance to 4.5%–5.0% on a **net** basis, having delivered +4.8% net organic in Q2. WPP reported H1 adjusted (net) revenue down 4.7% like-for-like. On Omnicom's own definition it outgrows Publicis; on the net reconstruction it sits between Publicis and WPP, nearer the latter.

The disposition program also mechanically flatters growth and margin. The FY2025 10-K described ~$3.2B of revenue to be divested; the Q2 call raised that to $3.5–$3.6B annualized, with ~$2B completed in seven months and ~$525M more by year-end. Removing low-growth, low-margin revenue lifts both the organic rate and the margin of what remains. The benefit is real but arrives once, and the proceeds are negligible: H1 investing cash flow shows only $168.2M from assets held for sale, and at June 30 the remaining disposal group carried $808.1M of assets against $1,052.4M of liabilities — net negative $244.3M. Add the FY2025 $547.1M loss on held-for-sale assets and a further $34.3M H1 impairment, and the program reads as complexity reduction rather than a cash event. Wells Fargo pressed management on the call about whether 2026 growth and margin expansion are sustainable or a one-time effect; the CEO declined to answer, which is customary but leaves the durability question open.

**Estimate revisions are negative in every window**, and the near-term cuts came after the print: current quarter $2.56 versus $2.79 thirty days ago (-8.3%) and $2.84 ninety days ago (-9.8%); next quarter -7.5% / -6.5%; current year -2.1% / -3.0%; next year -1.3% / -2.1%. Part of this likely reflects the removal of divested earnings rather than deterioration in retained operations, but the direction is what it is, and it argues against treating consensus targets as a floor. The macro backdrop for the North American consumer categories that fund advertising (food and beverage 13% of revenue, automotive 10%, financial services 10%) is a source of risk the industry cannot hedge.

| Buyback authorization remaining | $2,300M ≈ 10% of shares |

The supplied peer table (TTD, ZD, DV, ANGI; median P/E 30.8x) is not a valid comparison set — those are ad-tech and marketplace businesses with different capital intensity, growth and margin structures. The relevant comparables are the holding companies: Publicis, WPP and Dentsu. Omnicom at 7.8x current-year sits between where the net-organic reconstruction would place its growth. The market is not obviously mispricing the stock; it is pricing the outcome the bears describe.

Two things do support the multiple. First, absolute yield: a 3.88% dividend covered at 32% of earnings and a high-single-digit-to-low-double-digit FCF yield give the equity a floor that does not depend on organic growth resuming. Second, share-count mechanics: completing the remaining $2.3B retires roughly 10% of shares, which on flat net revenue and delivered synergies is worth about 10 points of EPS growth on its own. Consensus mean target of $102.08 (median $100, range $77–$139) implies 24% upside; we treat that as stale relative to falling quarterly estimates rather than as independent confirmation, since the estimate direction and the target direction currently disagree.

What holds the valuation score at 6.2 rather than 7+: the multiple has been low for years while shareholders earned essentially nothing cumulatively since 2023, and the identified catalyst (synergy delivery plus buyback) is management's own arithmetic against an unverified revenue base. Cheapness that persists through a full merger cycle without re-rating is not a margin of safety by itself.

## Competitive Landscape

Omnicom is now the largest holding company by revenue and has assembled a credible data stack: Omni as the planning/activation/measurement layer, Acxiom identity and Interact from IPG, Flywheel Commerce Cloud from 2024. The CEO's framing — "now more of an operating company than a holding company" — is the coherent version of the bull case: centralized data and technology monetized across a larger media base, which is exactly the Publicis/Epsilon template.

The competitive read is mixed to negative on trajectory. Publicis is the structural share gainer, delivering +4.8% net organic in Q2 with Intelligent Creativity and Connected Media (87% of net revenue) up 6.5%. WPP is shrinking 4.7% like-for-like with continuing account losses, which makes it the largest available source of share for both Omnicom and Publicis. Omnicom's own Advertising discipline declined in Q2, and total combined revenue was flat.

Downstream, platforms continue to take share of total ad dollars (Amazon at roughly $20B of quarterly advertising revenue), demand softened at The Trade Desk on tightened brand budgets, and Warner Bros. Discovery cited weaker ad sales. Agencies increasingly intermediate spend they do not control, which is why principal media is attractive as a margin source and why it introduces balance-sheet risk: as principal, Omnicom forgoes sequential-liability protection and takes direct credit and pricing risk on media commitments the 10-K says "can substantially exceed the revenue from our services."

Client concentration improved on a percentage basis — largest client 2.0% of LTM revenue from 2.6%, top 10 at 15.3% from 19.0% — but that is substantially a denominator effect. On our reconstruction, absolute top-10 revenue rose roughly 13% (approximately $3,021M to $3,423M) on an LTM base that grew from ~$15.9B to ~$22.4B. Single-name sensitivity fell; absolute exposure to the largest relationships did not. Sector concentration worsened: Pharmaceuticals and Health Care went from under 15% of revenue to 19% of H1 2026 after combining Omnicom Health with IPG Health, adding exposure to US drug-pricing policy, DTC advertising rules and patent cliffs.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment cushion is narrowing on a measurable trend.** The May 1, 2025 test required a ~46% fair-value decline to fail at a 12.5%–12.8% WACC; the May 1, 2026 test shows ~37% at a 13.8%–14.2% WACC, with long-term growth held at 3.5%. WACC has risen roughly 300bps over two years. Both tests passed with disclosed sensitivities intact, but $18,740.7M of goodwill sits against $9,661.8M of equity and negative $13.9B of tangible book. An impairment would be non-cash and would not breach the 3.5x leverage covenant, but it would be a large, visible mark against a thesis that depends on cash-flow durability.

**No forward revenue visibility.** Substantially all client contracts are cancellable on approximately 90 days' notice without penalty, and most run one year or less. Unsatisfied performance obligations on contracts longer than one year were only $455.7M at June 30, 2026, explicitly scope-limited and not a backlog figure.

**AI cuts both ways and the 10-K says so.** One risk factor covers Omnicom's own use of generative and agentic AI; a separate one covers failure to adapt, naming the threat directly — "if our clients develop their own AI-related capabilities." Platform-native creative and buying tools compress the agency's value-add. Against that, AI is also the mechanism through which the $900M synergy target and further labor efficiency are delivered. Neither direction is resolvable from current disclosure.

**Governance.** CEO compensation of $69,865,846 in FY2025 against a $20M–$22M range in each of 2021–2024 is a roughly 3.2x step-up in a year when cumulative TSR was $154.22 versus a peer group at $224.01, with the gap widening every year since 2022. The most plausible explanation is a merger-related retention or long-dated award tied to closing and integrating IPG, which would align a large share of the outcome with post-merger performance; we did not verify the cause and infer no intent. Separately, the incentive plan's "Organic Growth" measure uses management's gross definition, so it rewards principal-media expansion identically to underlying fee growth. An 8-K filed January 29, 2026 under Item 5.02 (officer/director change) was not readable — a small open item.

**Insider record is neutral, not bearish.** Every Form 4 in the trailing twelve months is a grant (code A) or tax withholding (code F). There are no open-market purchases and no open-market sales by any officer or director. Screens flagging "insider selling" here are misreading mandatory tax settlements on vesting equity. The absence of buying while the stock traded at $70.83–$74.93 in May, below the company's own repurchase prices, is mildly relevant and not conclusive.

**Other filing-level items:** $442.5M of gross unrecognized tax benefits ($426.5M affecting the effective rate), Pillar Two exposure, $2,566.5M of lease liabilities, $655.4M of pension and $143.5M of postemployment obligations, $244.8M of contingent purchase obligations, $318.8M of redeemable non-controlling interests outside permanent equity, and roughly 47% of revenue in non-US currencies across 50+ currencies. FX guidance was cut from "over +2%" for FY2026 to flat in Q3 and +1.0% for the year, removing about a point of reported growth tailwind. Purchase accounting remains preliminary, so goodwill, intangibles, amortization and the impairment cushion are all subject to revision. Legal proceedings disclose nothing management expects to be material, and the Q2 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.4% sits below 35.3% one-year realized volatility (spread -3.9%), at the 40th percentile within the chain, with a flat term structure (31.4% near versus 32.2% at six months). The market is not pricing a near-term dislocation, and option protection is inexpensive relative to realized movement — favorable if you want to hedge an existing position rather than a signal to add exposure. Two details lean cautious: put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.66 to 1.01 between July 29 and August 7 while the price advanced 3.9%, and near-dated put skew is 3.5% (elevated put demand) against a flat -0.2% at three and six months. Short interest of 9.8% of float at 5.4 days to cover is meaningful but not crowded. Read together, positioning has accumulated near-term downside protection into a rising price without pricing sustained stress, consistent with our view that the next catalyst is a definitional/disclosure question rather than a solvency or demand shock.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $118 (+43%)

The merger produced a scale and data advantage that Publicis has already proven monetizable. Core EBITA margin of ~16.5% in Q2 is up ~450bps year over year with only partial synergy capture against a $900M target; completing the disposal program removes $3.5–$3.6B of low-margin revenue, further lifting mix. Capex under 1% of revenue converts margin to cash almost directly — FY2025 FCF was 16.1% of revenue. Completing the remaining $2.3B authorization retires roughly 10% of shares at prices near the company's $76–$78 average, on top of the 32.2M shares already taken via the accelerated program. If FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $12.40 and the multiple moves to 9.5x as WPP's account losses convert into Omnicom wins and net organic growth is demonstrated positive, the stock is worth $118, before $3.20 of annual dividends. The Street's high target is $139.

### Bear Case — $62 (-24.9%)

Reported organic growth is substantially a gross-up from extending principal-based media trading onto IPG's media base, and comparable net organic revenue is shrinking around 2%. On that reading Omnicom is closer to WPP (-4.7% like-for-like) than to Publicis (+4.8%), and the correct multiple is WPP-like. Margin expansion is a one-time function of disposals plus merger synergies that annualize away by 2027, which is why management declined to commit to its durability. The buyback that carries the per-share story is partly debt-funded — net debt rose $4.4B in six months and the company drew commercial paper for the first time in years — and the $2.3B remaining is a finite resource; the 10-K's own new risk factor concedes the merger "may not be accretive to adjusted earnings per share and may not generate significant cash to return to stockholders." The goodwill cushion narrowed 9 points in a year at a WACC 300bps higher than two years ago, tangible book is negative $13.9B, pharma is now 19% of revenue, and clients can leave on 90 days' notice. Apply 5.9x to $10.50 of FY2027 EPS and the stock is $62.

### Base Case — $92 (+11.5%)

Synergies land close to target and margin holds near 16%, but net revenue is roughly flat and the multiple does not re-rate. FY2027 adjusted EPS of $12.00 (marginally below the current $12.04 consensus, reflecting continued modest cuts and the loss of divested earnings) at 7.7x — the current multiple — gives $92, plus $3.20 of dividends for a ~15% total return. Probability-weighting bull 25% / base 45% / bear 30% gives about $89.50, or roughly 12% total return including income against 25% downside in the bear case. Positive expected value, but the left tail is fat because the central question is close to binary and unresolvable from disclosure.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders should keep exposure and collect the 3.88% yield while the buyback runs; new capital has no urgent entry signal 5.6% below the 52-week high with quarterly estimates falling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026), which is the first quarter in which nearly all divestitures have closed and the synergy run-rate should be visible without disposal noise. Secondary checkpoints: Publicis Q3 (mid-October) for a net-basis read on relative share, and WPP's H2 trajectory.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Disclosure or credible quantification of principal-media revenue, or an organic growth figure presented on a net basis, showing underlying growth of +2% or better. This is the single piece of evidence that would move the rating most.
2. Q3 EBITA margin at or above 16% *excluding* the disposal-group drag, with third-party service costs flat-to-down as a percentage of revenue — indicating margin gains are synergy-driven rather than mix- and gross-up-driven.
3. Current-year and next-year consensus EPS revisions turning positive for two consecutive months, reversing the current -2.1% / -1.3% thirty-day trend.
4. Price near $70 (approximately 5.8x FY2027 consensus, and below management's $76–$78 repurchase average), which would restore an adequate margin of safety against the bear case without requiring the growth question to be resolved first.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2026 organic guidance cut below 4.5%, or any restatement of the organic definition that reveals negative net organic growth.
2. Goodwill test cushion narrowing below ~25%, or a partial impairment charge — the trend from 46% to 37% at a WACC rising toward 14% is already one-directional.
3. Buyback pace slowing materially before the $2.3B authorization is exhausted, or covenant leverage rising above ~3.0x, either of which would remove the per-share support that the current multiple depends on.
4. Loss of a top-10 client relationship or evidence of pharma-category budget cuts, given 19% sector concentration and 90-day contract cancellability.

**Score trajectory context:** the sequence 6.9 (Feb) → 6.2 (Apr) → 6.5 (May) → 5.1 (Jun) → 6.0 (Jul) has been driven by declining competitive position (7.5 → 5.5) and management quality (7.0 → 5.0). Today's 5.4 continues that direction for specific reasons: consensus quarterly EPS was cut 7%–8% in the thirty days *after* the print that prompted the prior BUY, the price is unchanged at $82.51 official close, and WPP's August 6 report confirmed the sector's demand softness. The earnings power and capital-return arithmetic are intact; the entry has not improved while the evidence base has become marginally weaker.

## Conclusion

Omnicom is a cheap, cash-generative business with a credible self-help program — 7.8x current-year and 6.9x next-year adjusted earnings, a 3.88% dividend covered at 32% of earnings, capex under 1% of revenue, $900M of targeted synergies partly unrealized, and $2.3B of authorization equal to 10% of the share count. On the base case the stock is worth about $92 in twelve months, or roughly a 15% total return.

The reason that does not translate into a buy recommendation is that the most consequential number in the story cannot be verified. Reported organic growth of 6.1% is calculated on a gross basis that includes principal-media pass-throughs, and the filing-level evidence — third-party service costs at 23.8% of FY2025 revenue versus 21.3% in FY2024, and 23.2% of Q2 2026 revenue despite consolidating a low-principal-media business — supports the direction of the bear reconstruction without settling its magnitude. If net revenue is growing, the stock is worth well above $100. If it is shrinking 2%, the correct comparison is WPP, the multiple is right where it is, and the buyback is buying time rather than value. Total combined revenue was flat year over year, the Advertising discipline declined, and management declined to commit to the durability of the current margin and growth run rate when asked directly. Consensus estimates were cut after the print. Hold the position, collect the dividend, and require either disclosure that resolves the net-revenue question or a price near $70 before adding capital.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the dividend yield on the $3.20 annual payout at the $82.51 basis is 3.88% (payout ~32% of adjusted EPS, not 31%); the peer-comp sentence incorrectly assigned specific "low double digits / mid single digits" multiples to Publicis and WPP that are not in the sources; and the July payrolls/mortgage-rate macro claims were unverifiable in the brief and have been removed. None of these alter the earnings power or capital-return arithmetic underpinning the HOLD.
