## Executive Summary

Blackbaud is the dominant vertical SaaS provider for the social impact sector — nonprofits, educational institutions, and corporate CSR programs — with ~45 years of domain expertise and near-monopoly positioning in fundraising CRM and donor management. The company has completed a meaningful operational transformation: divesting the disastrous EVERFI acquisition, expanding margins to 30.5% non-GAAP operating (35.9% EBITDA), and aggressively repurchasing shares (reducing count ~14% since late 2023). At $37.81, the stock trades at ~7.3x 2026E non-GAAP EPS of $5.19, a deep discount to both its application software peers (median 28x) and its own historical range.

The stock has declined 48% from its 52-week high of $74.88, driven by two distinct concerns: (1) elevated uncertainty about government funding cuts impacting nonprofit customer budgets, and (2) the company's intentionally extreme leverage (27x debt/equity, 2.52x net leverage) which amplifies downside in any revenue shortfall scenario. The 2026 renewal cohort — representing ~40% of contracts with dollar values 40% higher than the 2025 cohort — is the near-term test of whether government funding pressure translates into elevated churn or merely delayed decision-making.

The long-term math is compelling: 5% organic growth + margin expansion to 40%+ EBITDA + 5-10% annual buybacks = 13%+ EPS CAGR through 2030, potentially reaching $8-9 in EPS. At even 12-15x, that implies significant upside. But the near-term path is genuinely uncertain — the government funding risk is new, unquantified, and potentially severe enough to break the retention model that underpins the entire thesis.

## Company Overview

Blackbaud operates as a vertical SaaS platform serving the social impact sector across 100+ countries. Revenue breaks down as:
- **Contractual Recurring (~64%):** Subscription SaaS including Raiser's Edge NXT (fundraising CRM), Financial Edge NXT, and education solutions
- **Transactional Recurring (~34%):** Payment processing on donations (55%), JustGiving consumer platform (20%), tuition management (20%), event-based usage (5%)
- **One-time Services (<2%):** Implementation and professional services

Total recurring revenue exceeds 98%, with three-year standard contracts incorporating mid-to-high single-digit annual price escalators. Gross dollar retention stands at ~92%.

The company employs a "land and expand" strategy leveraging its Blackbaud Verified Network, which connects nonprofits to corporate CSR platforms, creating network effects that reinforce switching costs. Recent launches include "Agents For Good" — an agentic AI product suite priced at tens of thousands annually, targeting fundraising staff budgets rather than IT budgets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:**
FY2025 reported revenue declined 2.3% to $1,128M, but organic growth (excluding EVERFI divestiture) was 5.5%. Q1 2026 organic growth was 4.2% ($281M), within the 4-6% long-term target range.

**Profitability:**
The margin expansion story is real and measurable:
- GAAP gross margin: 54.7% (2024) → 58.8% (2025), +410bps
- Non-GAAP operating margin: 27.6% (2024) → 30.5% (2025)
- Non-GAAP EBITDA margin: 35.9% (2025)
- Rule of 40: 41.4%
- Non-GAAP EPS: $4.06 (2024) → $4.45 (2025) → consensus $5.19 (2026E), representing 17% growth

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $266M in FY2025 with non-GAAP FCF of $204M (18.0% margin). Capital requirements are modest ($62M software development + $8M PP&E). Q1 2026 FCF was $37M, up ~$50M YoY.

**Balance Sheet — The Deliberate Leverage Problem:**
- Total debt: $1.11B (2024 Credit Facility, maturing April 2029)
- Stockholders' equity: $85M
- Debt/Equity: 27.1x
- Net leverage: 2.52x (covenant max 3.75x)
- Interest coverage: 6.27x (covenant min 2.50x)
- Available borrowing capacity: $414M
- Altman Z-Score: 0.68

The Z-Score is mechanically distorted by the intentional capital structure. Blackbaud generates $265M+ in annual operating cash flow against $68M in annual interest expense. Covenant compliance has comfortable headroom. The risk is binary: if the business performs to plan, this leverage is manageable and accretive. If revenue declines materially (e.g., 8-10%+ from nonprofit budget cuts), fixed debt service on a shrinking revenue base could force uncomfortable choices.

**Buyback Velocity:**
$214M repurchased in FY2025 (3.3M shares, ~7.9% of outstanding). Another 4.5% retired in Q1 2026 alone. $1.0B authorization outstanding. At current prices ($37.81), each dollar of buyback creates more per-share value than at prior price levels — management is buying at the lowest valuation in years.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Revenue Growth (4-6% target):** Supported by embedded contractual price escalators (mid-to-high single digits), new product cross-sells (Agents For Good), and transactional volume growth tied to charitable giving trends. Blackbaud's own research indicates "resilience in 2025 charitable giving." The 4.2% Q1 2026 print is at the low end of the range.

**EPS Growth (13%+ CAGR through 2030):** The compounding path is: 4-6% revenue growth + 100-200bps annual margin expansion + 5-10% annual buyback = 13%+ EPS growth. This is achievable without heroic assumptions. 2026 guided at 17% growth (consensus $5.19).

**AI Optionality (Unquantified Upside):** Agents For Good launched March 2026 with "oversubscribed" webinars. Priced at tens of thousands annually, targeted at thousands of existing customers. If 5,000 customers adopt at $30K/year = $150M incremental revenue opportunity over time. This is speculative but the data moat (30B+ ML predictions/year, proprietary philanthropic data) provides structural advantage vs. generic AI tools.

**India GCC (Structural Margin Improvement):** $6-8M transition costs in 2026, savings starting 2027. This is a multi-year margin tailwind.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.81 with 2026E non-GAAP EPS of $5.19:
- **Forward P/E: 7.3x** (non-GAAP) or 12.7x (GAAP trailing at $2.37 EPS)
- **Peer median P/E: 28.1x**
- **Discount to peers: 74%**

The disconnect between BLKB's 7.3x forward multiple and its peers (BlackLine at 70x, Agilysys at 64x, SPSC at 23x, ALRM at 18.5x) is extraordinary. Even adjusting for BLKB's lower growth (5% vs. peers potentially higher), a defensive SaaS business with 92% retention, 36% EBITDA margins, and 13%+ EPS growth deserves more than 7x forward earnings.

**Analyst targets:** $45 (low) to $65 (high), mean $51, median $50. All targets represent 19-72% upside from current price. The consensus target of $51 implies ~13x forward earnings — still below peer levels but 35% upside.

**What justifies the discount?**
1. Government funding uncertainty (new, unquantified)
2. Extreme leverage amplifying downside risk
3. EVERFI destruction of value ($500M+ lost) creating management trust deficit
4. The stock just declined 48% — momentum investors are selling

**Is the market wrong?** Partially. At 7.3x forward earnings, the stock prices in a scenario where either (a) growth declines to 0% or negative, or (b) the EPS estimates are significantly overstated. If estimates are roughly correct and retention holds at 88%+, the stock is materially undervalued. The uncertainty is whether the government funding risk is a 5% headwind (manageable) or a 15%+ headwind (thesis-breaking).

## Competitive Landscape

Blackbaud's competitive position is strong and arguably improving:
- **Monopolistic market share** in nonprofit fundraising CRM for organizations above ~$5M in revenue
- **Three-year contracts with escalators** create predictable revenue and high switching costs
- **Data moat deepens with scale** — 30B+ ML predictions, proprietary benchmarks, decades of philanthropic data
- **Regulatory compliance burden** (PCI DSS, HIPAA, state registration requirements) creates barrier for smaller entrants
- **Network effects** via Blackbaud Verified Network connecting nonprofits to corporate CSR budgets

**Competitive threats:**
- Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud targets larger organizations but lacks vertical depth
- Bloomerang targets SMB nonprofits — different segment
- Generic AI tools could theoretically reduce the value of Blackbaud's proprietary data over time

AI investments deepen the moat (harder for generic tools to replicate domain-specific agent capabilities), and the Agents For Good category expands addressable market beyond IT budgets.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Near-Term Risks:**

1. **Government Funding Cuts to Nonprofits (HIGH severity, HIGH uncertainty):** The administration's spending reduction efforts could directly impact Blackbaud's customer base. Nonprofits losing government grants may: cancel contracts, reduce seats, delay renewals, or shut down entirely. The 2026 renewal cohort concentrates this risk in the immediate term.

2. **Leverage Amplification (HIGH severity, MODERATE probability):** A 10% revenue decline would push leverage toward 3.0x and potentially constrain the buyback program — removing the primary EPS growth accelerator. A 15%+ decline could approach covenant limits.

3. **2026 Renewal Cohort Execution (MODERATE-HIGH):** Management explicitly warned that even stable retention rates will produce higher absolute churn dollars because the cohort is larger. Market expectations for this cohort are fragile.

4. **EVERFI Trust Deficit (MODERATE):** The $725M acquisition written off in three years raises legitimate questions about M&A discipline. Management now says they'll "simplify and rationalize" — but the prior track record warrants skepticism on future capital allocation beyond buybacks.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term ATM IV of 138.6% vs. 38.1% historical volatility represents an extreme 100pp spread, with the term structure in steep backwardation (138.6% 1-week vs. 47.8% 6-month). This indicates the market is pricing a significant near-term event or continued extreme uncertainty around the recent earnings report. Put/call skew at 21.9% confirms elevated demand for downside protection. The options market is pricing BLKB as a distressed situation despite the company's stable fundamentals — either the options market is mispricing vol (normalization opportunity) or institutional selling/credit stress has not yet fully manifested.

Short interest at 8.3% with 4.1 days to cover is elevated but not extreme — consistent with active but not crowded skepticism.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55-65, +45% to +72%):**
- Government funding concerns prove manageable (5% customer impact at most)
- 2026 renewal cohort retention comes in at 90%+ (in line with historical)
- Agents For Good achieves meaningful adoption in 2026-2027 ($30-50M incremental)
- Estimates prove conservative; 2026 EPS above $5.50
- Multiple re-rates from 7.3x toward 12-15x as execution proves out
- Aggressive buybacks at depressed prices accelerate per-share value creation
- By 2028-2030: $8-9 EPS at 15x = $120-135

**Bear Case ($25-30, -21% to -34%):**
- Government funding cuts cause 5-8% revenue decline as nonprofits churn
- 2026 renewal cohort retention drops to 85% or below
- Leverage approaches covenant limits, forcing buyback cessation
- Market treats BLKB as a value trap; multiple compresses to 5-6x
- Goodwill impairment write-down triggered
- Further executive departures signal internal acknowledgment of structural problems

**Base Case ($48-55, +27% to +45%):**
- Organic growth moderates to 3-4% (below target) as government funding creates headwinds
- Retention holds at 90-91% (slightly below historical)
- EPS achieves $5.00-5.20 in 2026
- Multiple recovers to 10-11x as near-term fears partially abate
- Buybacks continue at current pace, providing ongoing EPS support

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 or Q3 2026 results demonstrating renewal cohort retention at ≥90% — would confirm government funding fears are overblown
2. Stock price below $34 (6.5x forward EPS) with estimates stable — would create sufficient margin of safety to absorb bear scenario
3. Insider open-market purchases by CEO or CFO — would signal management conviction through skin-in-the-game

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Gross dollar retention drops below 88% in any reported quarter — would signal structural customer loss
2. Net leverage exceeds 3.0x (from current 2.52x) — would signal revenue decline and constrained capital return
3. Full-year 2026 EPS guidance reduced below $4.80 — would indicate growth thesis is broken

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July 2026) for Q2 results — this will provide the first full-quarter read on the 2026 renewal cohort and government funding impact. The July-August 2026 window is the key decision point.

## Conclusion

Blackbaud at $37.81 presents a genuinely interesting value case: a near-monopoly SaaS business trading at less than 8x forward earnings, with 92% retention, 36% EBITDA margins, and a management team buying back stock at an accelerating pace. The long-term math works — 13%+ EPS CAGR through buybacks and margin expansion is achievable under most scenarios where the customer base remains intact.

The reason for HOLD rather than BUY is the genuine uncertainty around two specific issues: (1) the government funding risk is new, unquantified, and potentially severe enough to meaningfully impair the retention rate that underpins the entire model, and (2) the 27x debt/equity leverage means any revenue miss is amplified through the capital structure in ways that could constrain the buyback program. The 48% decline from highs reflects something real about this uncertainty, and the options market (138.6% near-term IV in extreme backwardation) confirms institutional anxiety.

The stock is cheap enough that it doesn't need heroic assumptions to work. But it needs clarity on the 2026 renewal cohort. Patient investors willing to wait for Q2-Q3 data on retention and government funding impact will have materially better visibility.
