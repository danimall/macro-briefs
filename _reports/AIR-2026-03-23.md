## Executive Summary

AAR Corp is a mid-cap ($4.0B) aerospace services company focused on Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul (MRO) for commercial and government aviation customers. The stock has surged 66.3% over the past year, outperforming both the A&D sector and the S&P 500, driven by strong MRO demand, rising aircraft utilization, and defense contract wins including a recent $159.7M Air Force contract. Analyst consensus is overwhelmingly bullish with 10 of 12 analysts at Buy or Strong Buy.

The critical tension is between a genuinely favorable operating environment and extreme valuation combined with aggressive insider selling. The trailing P/E of 42.6x is rich for an MRO business, and the forward P/E of 321x implies consensus expects a severe earnings compression — likely from one-time charges, a CFO transition, or acquisition-related dilution. Meanwhile, from January 8 through February 4, the CEO exercised and sold approximately $7.1M worth of stock, the Interim CFO sold $1.2M, the CCO sold $2.3M, and the General Counsel sold $456K. This cluster of insider dispositions across the entire C-suite at prices between $95 and $109 represents a meaningful signal that management views the stock as fully valued or overvalued near current levels.

Given the valuation stretch, the gap between trailing and forward P/E suggesting upcoming earnings deterioration, and the breadth of insider selling, the stock warrants a HOLD despite favorable sector dynamics. The MRO secular story is real, but the price already reflects it and possibly more.

## Company Overview

AAR Corp provides aviation services including parts supply, component repair, airframe maintenance, and government services to commercial airlines, cargo operators, MROs, OEMs, and defense agencies. The company operates primarily in the US but serves a global customer base. AAR's fiscal year ends May 31, and the company files its 10-K in July.

The company sits squarely in the aerospace aftermarket, which benefits from aging fleet dynamics: as aircraft age, MRO spending per unit rises. Global air travel recovery post-COVID has increased aircraft utilization rates, extending maintenance demand. The defense side provides contract stability, as evidenced by the $159.7M Air Force contract secured in March 2026 through AAR Manufacturing LLC.

AAR recently appointed Dylan Wolin as CFO (announced February 11, 2026), replacing an interim CFO arrangement. This transition introduces execution uncertainty but also signals potential strategic repositioning.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive Drivers:**
1. **MRO Market Secular Growth:** Global MRO market is expanding at 4-5% CAGR, driven by aging fleets, rising aircraft utilization, and airline reluctance to retire older aircraft given OEM delivery delays (Boeing 737 MAX ramp challenges, Airbus A320neo backlog).
2. **Government Contracts:** The $159.7M Air Force contract adds revenue visibility. The current macro environment features $223B in identifiable US defense spending (per world model), directly benefiting A&D companies. AAR's government services segment should see contract flow acceleration.
3. **Stock Outperformance:** +66.3% over the past year versus 35.8% for the A&D equipment industry indicates the market is pricing in above-sector growth.

**Concerns:**
4. **Forward P/E Collapse:** The 321x forward multiple implies the market expects near-zero earnings ahead, which contradicts the growth narrative unless significant one-time items or structural changes are anticipated.
5. **CFO Transition:** Appointing a new CFO during a period of strong stock performance can precede strategic shifts (acquisitions, restructuring) that temporarily depress earnings.
6. **Oil at $119:** Higher fuel costs compress airline profitability → airlines defer discretionary MRO spending and pressure MRO pricing. This is a direct headwind to AAR's commercial business, though government contracts provide offset.

## Valuation Assessment

At 42.6x trailing earnings, AIR trades at a substantial premium to the broader industrials sector (typically 18-22x) and at the upper end of aerospace aftermarket peers. For context:
- TransDigm (TDG): ~30-35x (monopolistic parts supplier with pricing power)
- HEICO (HEI): ~55-60x (premium aftermarket franchise)
- TAT Technologies (TATT): competitor referenced in news, typically at lower multiples

AIR's trailing multiple is approaching HEICO territory without HEICO's pricing power or margin profile. The forward multiple of 321x, if accurate, means the stock is pricing in recovery beyond whatever earnings trough lies ahead — effectively requiring investors to look through a substantial near-term earnings decline.

**Beta of 1.31** means the stock will amplify market moves. With the S&P 500 below its 200-day MA and in its 4th consecutive weekly decline, high-beta names face additional drawdown risk in a risk-off environment.

## Competitive Landscape

AAR competes in a fragmented MRO market against ST Engineering, Lufthansa Technik, SIA Engineering, and numerous regional players. The company's competitive advantages include:

1. **Integrated service offering** spanning parts, repair, and airframe MRO
2. **Government services capability** providing access to defense contracts
3. **US-based operations** advantaged for ITAR-controlled defense work

However, MRO is fundamentally a labor-intensive, moderate-margin business without the pricing power of aerospace parts monopolists like TransDigm or HEICO. Barriers to entry are moderate — skilled labor and regulatory certifications provide some protection, but customers can and do multi-source MRO providers.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:**
AAR is positioned at the intersection of two durable demand drivers: aging commercial fleets requiring more MRO and rising US defense spending. The $159.7M Air Force contract demonstrates competitive positioning for government work. The new CFO appointment could signal strategic acceleration (M&A, new capabilities). If the forward P/E reflects temporary/one-time items rather than structural decline, the stock could re-rate to a normalized 25-30x on higher through-cycle earnings, implying 30-40% upside from a trough.

**Bear Case:**
At 42.6x trailing earnings, the stock has already priced in the MRO recovery and defense tailwinds. The 321x forward P/E signals a severe earnings decline that the market may not be adequately discounting at the current share price. C-suite selling of $11.5M in 28 days suggests insiders view the stock as fully valued. Oil at $119 will compress airline margins and could lead to MRO deferrals. With beta of 1.31, any broader market drawdown will hit AIR disproportionately. The stock has already risen 66% in a year; risk/reward skews negative at these levels.

## Conclusion

AAR Corp operates in a favorable sector with genuine secular and cyclical tailwinds. The defense spending environment is the strongest in years, and commercial MRO demand is structurally sound. The $159.7M Air Force contract validates the company's competitive position.

However, the stock's valuation at 42.6x trailing earnings is stretched for an MRO business, and the forward P/E of 321x signals a coming earnings decline that requires investigation before committing capital. The concentrated insider selling across five executives totaling $11.5M in under a month is the most concrete negative signal — these are the people with the best visibility into the business, and they are uniformly monetizing. Combined with a 1.31 beta in a deteriorating market environment (S&P below 200-day MA, oil at $119, rising rates), the risk/reward does not favor new positions. HOLD for existing shareholders; wait for clarity on the forward earnings trajectory and a more favorable entry point for new investors.
