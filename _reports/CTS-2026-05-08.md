## Executive Summary

CTS Corporation is a $1.8B market cap manufacturer of sensors, connectivity components, and actuators undergoing a multi-year transformation from a cyclical transportation-dependent business toward higher-margin diversified end markets (aerospace & defense, medical, industrial). This shift has produced 480bps of gross margin expansion since 2023 (34.7% → 39.5% in Q1 2026), and diversified markets now represent 57% of revenue versus 45% two years ago.

The Q1 2026 earnings report (April 29) confirmed the trajectory: revenue grew 11% YoY, adjusted EPS of $0.62 beat consensus estimates, book-to-bill was 1.1x, and management narrowed full-year guidance upward at the lower end to $2.35-$2.45 in adjusted EPS. The balance sheet is net cash positive with $236M of undrawn revolver capacity, providing M&A optionality to accelerate diversification.

The stock has appreciated significantly over the past year, reflecting the market's growing recognition of this transformation. At approximately 27.3x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.19) and ~24.9x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($2.40 midpoint), the valuation is fair-to-full in absolute terms but reasonable relative to the growth profile (estimated 11-19% forward EPS growth). The primary risks are insider selling (CEO's 10b5-1 plan for 130K shares, CFO's $1.3M sale), A&D book-to-bill of 0.75x in Q1, and tariff exposure on manufacturing in Mexico, China, and Taiwan.

## Company Overview

CTS Corporation, founded in 1896, manufactures highly engineered sensing and motion devices sold primarily through direct sales engineers (86% of revenue) to OEMs across four end markets: Transportation (43% of 2025 sales), Industrial (26%), Medical (16%), and Aerospace & Defense (15%). The company operates ~3,500 employees across facilities in North America (54%), Asia (31%), and Europe (15%), with 44% of revenue generated outside the United States.

The strategic pivot underway is straightforward: reduce dependence on cyclical transportation (which peaked at 55% of sales in 2023) by growing diversified markets organically and through acquisition. The July 2024 acquisition of SyQwest (final purchase price of approximately $128M, including contingent consideration) added U.S. Navy sonar capabilities and exemplifies the approach — bolt-on acquisitions in defense and medical that carry higher margins and longer revenue visibility than automotive platform business.

Key customers include Toyota (11.2% of 2025 sales) and Cummins (8.4%, down from 15% in 2023). The declining concentration reflects both successful diversification and weakness in Cummins-related commercial vehicle volumes.

## Growth Analysis

CTS's growth story has three distinct vectors:

**1. Diversified market organic growth (18% in Q1 2026).** Industrial book-to-bill of 1.29x and medical book-to-bill of 1.20x confirm demand momentum. Key wins include a large non-invasive aesthetics ultrasound award and underwater hull-penetrator contracts. Medical ultrasound applications are expanding into aesthetics and therapeutics, adjacent markets that increase CTS's addressable opportunity.

**2. M&A-driven growth in A&D and medical.** SyQwest contributed $22.3M in its first full year (FY2025). With $300M+ of acquisition firepower (cash + revolver), management has indicated M&A remains a strategic priority. The company's under-leveraged balance sheet is a deliberate choice to preserve optionality.

**3. Secular content growth in transportation.** EVs require more sensing content per vehicle than ICE vehicles. CTS's $1.1B total booked transportation business provides multi-year visibility. Growth here is modest but stable.

FY2026 EPS guidance of $2.35-$2.45 implies 7-12% growth versus FY2025 adjusted EPS of $2.19. Consensus for FY2027 is estimated at $2.89, representing approximately 19% growth, driven by continued mix shift, margin expansion, and buyback accretion.

Estimate revisions are positive: next-year EPS has been revised up approximately 11% over the past 90 days, and current-year up modestly. The direction is clearly favorable, though not at the +30% threshold that typically signals a strong inflection BUY.

## Valuation Assessment

As of the report date, at $59.85, CTS trades at approximately:
- 27.3x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.19)
- 24.9x FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint ($2.40)
- 20.7x FY2027 consensus EPS ($2.89)

The peer group median P/E of 89.5x includes several companies with cyclically depressed or abnormal earnings (e.g., BHE at 92x, IPGP at 150x), making a direct P/E comparison of limited analytical value. The more relevant framing: CTS trades at ~25x current-year earnings with 11-19% forward EPS growth, producing a PEG ratio of approximately 1.3-2.3x depending on the growth rate used. On a normalized EV/EBITDA or FCF-yield basis, CTS's valuation is more meaningful to assess versus specialized sensor and component peers.

The 16% FCF margin (on revenue) translates to approximately 4.9% FCF yield on the current market cap ($86M FCF / $1.76B). This is respectable for a company with this margin expansion runway.

The valuation is not cheap in absolute terms — 27x trailing earnings for a $1.8B industrial tech company that was declining in revenue two years ago. But it is reasonable given: (a) margin expansion has further to run as mix shift continues, (b) the balance sheet provides M&A-driven upside optionality, (c) forward growth rates of 11-19% support the multiple, and (d) the mix shift is reducing the cyclical discount the market historically applied.

The single analyst target of $58 (per available data) is already below the current price ($59.85), which suggests either stale coverage or limited sell-side attention. For a small-cap with limited covering analysts, this creates both opportunity (less efficient pricing) and risk (limited institutional sponsorship).

## Competitive Landscape

CTS operates in a fragmented market where no single competitor competes across all product lines. Larger players (TE Connectivity, Sensata, Amphenol) compete in specific segments but not across the full portfolio. CTS's competitive advantages are:

**Switching costs:** 86% direct-sale model with custom-engineered solutions creates high switching costs once designed into customer platforms. Automotive platform wins lock in 5-7 years of revenue.

**Diversification breadth:** Few mid-cap sensor/component companies span transportation, defense sonar, medical ultrasound, and industrial sensing. This diversification reduces cyclicality and enables cross-selling.

**Defense positioning:** SyQwest designs and manufactures sonar and underwater acoustic sensing solutions primarily for U.S. Navy applications (surface ships and submarines). These specialized capabilities strengthen CTS's position in defense programs where deep domain expertise and established relationships provide meaningful barriers to entry.

**Competitive trajectory:** Improving. The Cummins revenue decline (approximately $37M over 2 years, based on the decline from ~15% to ~8.4% of consolidated sales) represents customer-specific weakness, not competitive displacement — it has been more than offset by diversified market growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff and trade exposure (HIGH).** CTS manufactures in Mexico, China, and Taiwan. Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminum already impact costs. Rare earth materials (critical for sensor manufacturing) are primarily sourced from China. Escalation in US-China trade tensions could simultaneously impact supply costs and manufacturing operations.

**Transportation cyclicality (MODERATE).** Despite diversification progress, 43% of revenue remains in transportation. A sharp global auto production downturn would pressure results. China exposure specifically has been a headwind as customers lose market share there.

**A&D book-to-bill weakness (MODERATE).** Q1 2026 A&D book-to-bill of 0.75x is below 1.0. Management attributed this to program funding timing and expects improvement in later quarters. If defense budgets face cuts or fiscal consolidation occurs, this growth vector weakens.

**Environmental remediation liabilities (LOW-MODERATE).** $16.4M in reserves, plus the $6.7M Asheville EPA settlement. These are manageable given the balance sheet but represent ongoing legacy costs (~$5M/year in charges).

**Goodwill/intangible concentration (MODERATE).** $358M in goodwill + intangibles = 46% of total assets. Primarily from acquisitions. If SyQwest or other acquired businesses underperform, impairment risk exists.

**Insider selling (MODERATE).** CEO's 10b5-1 plan for 130K shares and CFO's $1.3M sale are concerning timing signals. At 63 years old with 13 years as CEO, O'Sullivan's plan may reflect succession planning rather than bearish conviction. Neither represents >50% of holdings nor constitutes coordinated cluster selling, so the signal is ambiguous but bears monitoring.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($72-78, +20-30%)

CTS's diversification story accelerates: A&D book-to-bill recovers above 1.0 in Q2-Q3 2026, medical ultrasound wins drive upside to revenue guidance, gross margins reach 41-42% by Q4 2026. FY2027 EPS reaches $3.00+ as mix shift and buybacks compound. Market assigns 25-27x forward multiple on growing, less-cyclical earnings stream, yielding $75-81 per share. Bolt-on M&A in late 2026 provides incremental growth catalyst. The stock re-rates from "cyclical industrial" to "diversified sensor technology," closing the valuation gap versus peers.

### Bear Case ($44-48, -20-25%)

Transportation demand weakens sharply (global auto production slowdown, EV transition disruption). A&D program funding is delayed or reduced. Tariff escalation impacts Mexico/China operations. Margins compress back toward 36-37% as mix shift stalls. FY2026 EPS comes in at the low end of guidance ($2.35) or below. Market reverts to "cyclical industrial" valuation of 18-20x, producing $42-47 per share. Insider selling proves prescient.

## Conclusion

CTS Corporation represents a structural transformation story at a fair-to-full valuation. The company is successfully executing a multi-year shift from cyclical transportation dependency toward higher-margin, more defensible markets in aerospace/defense, medical, and industrial sensing.

At approximately 27x trailing earnings and 25x current-year earnings with 11-19% forward growth, the stock offers acceptable value relative to the trajectory of improvement. The net cash balance sheet, 16% FCF margins, and active buyback program provide downside support and M&A optionality.

The BUY recommendation is driven by: (1) demonstrated and continuing margin expansion from mix shift, (2) forward estimate revisions trending positively, (3) balance sheet optionality for accretive M&A, and (4) reasonable valuation for the growth profile. Insider selling and the elevated options IV are monitoring items rather than thesis-breakers — the CEO is 63 with a long tenure and the CFO sold 10-20% of holdings during a period of record results. Position sizing should reflect the small-cap illiquidity risk (average volume ~200K shares/day) and the binary geopolitical risks to manufacturing operations.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The trailing P/E is ~27.3x (not 25.5x as originally calculated), the peer P/E comparison is distorted by abnormal earnings at several peers, and certain competitive-moat claims (sole-source, market-share stability) lack SEC-filing support — collectively these weaken the valuation discount argument underpinning the BUY case.
