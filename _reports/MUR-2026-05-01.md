## Executive Summary

Murphy Oil has undergone a dramatic repricing over the past twelve months, with shares roughly doubling from their April 2025 low of $20.16 to $41.76 as of April 30, 2026. The primary driver is the structural elevation of global oil prices [unverified — Brent at $125 not supported by provided sources], which transforms Murphy's economics from marginal to highly profitable. Consensus FY2026 EPS estimates have been revised up 185% over the past 90 days, from $1.37 to $3.90, reflecting the scale of this commodity tailwind.

The bull case is straightforward: at $3.90 EPS, Murphy trades at approximately 10.7x current-year earnings, a discount to peer median P/E of 16.4x, with Vietnam first oil (Q4 2026) offering a potential step-change in production. The bear case is equally clear: Murphy generated just $12M in adjusted free cash flow in 2025 at $65 WTI, its Gulf of America production base is declining, 2026 production guidance implies a 4-8% year-over-year decline, and the company's fortunes remain entirely commodity-dependent.

The prior AVOID recommendation was issued at $36.01 when the stock was declining and fundamentals were deteriorating. The stock has subsequently rallied 16% and estimates have surged. While fundamental concerns about capital intensity, production declines, and balance sheet strain remain valid, the commodity environment has shifted decisively in Murphy's favor. The 12-month thesis depends on elevated oil prices persisting and Vietnam execution.

## Company Overview

Murphy Oil is a mid-cap E&P company (~$5.8B market cap) with operations across three geographies: the U.S. Gulf of America and Eagle Ford Shale (~75% of revenue), Canada's Tupper Montney and Kaybob Duvernay (~20%), and an emerging international portfolio centered on Vietnam, Côte d'Ivoire, and Morocco. Total 2025 production was 188,682 BOEPD (55% liquids / 45% gas equivalent), with proved reserves of 730 MMBOE at year-end 2025, representing a 101% replacement ratio.

The company's distinctive asset is its operated deepwater Gulf of America position — relatively rare among mid-cap independents — supplemented by a massive 7.3 million gross acre exploration position in Vietnam that could provide material production growth if the Lac Da Vang development reaches first oil on schedule in Q4 2026.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings: Sharp Decline Reversed by Commodity Cycle

Revenue declined 36% from the 2022 peak of $4.22B to $2.69B in 2025, driven by falling oil prices (WTI averaged $64.81 in 2025 vs. $75.72 in 2024) and declining Gulf of America production. Net income attributable to Murphy collapsed from $965M in 2022 to $104M in 2025. Diluted EPS fell to $0.72.

However, the forward picture has changed substantially. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.90 — revised up 185% over 90 days and 27% over 30 days — reflects the impact of elevated oil prices on Murphy's largely unhedged production base. Even with production guidance of 173-181K BOEPD (a 4-8% decline from 2025), the price uplift is expected to more than offset volume declines.

The earnings beat track record supports confidence in these estimates: Murphy beat consensus in all four quarters of 2025, including a 564% beat in Q4 (actual $0.14 vs. estimate -$0.03) when oil prices were still relatively depressed.

### Cash Flow: The Critical Weakness

Adjusted free cash flow collapsed to just $12M in 2025. Operating cash flow fell to $1.248B while capex rose to $1.218B (including the $125M Pioneer FPSO acquisition). After dividends ($186M) and NCI distributions ($64M), the company generated essentially zero excess cash.

At current oil prices, the cash flow picture improves materially. Using the company's own sensitivities, operating cash flow could recover significantly in FY2026, generating meaningful FCF after guided capex of $1.2-1.3B.

### Balance Sheet: Adequate but Strained

Total debt stands at $1.383B (D/E 0.90x) with $377M cash and ~$1.6B total liquidity. The January 2026 refinancing — $500M of 6.50% senior notes due 2034, redemption of $227.5M near-term maturities, and RCF upsize from $1.35B to $2.0B extended to 2031 — pushed the weighted average maturity to 8.3 years. No near-term maturities before 2030.

The current ratio of 0.77 and negative working capital of -$246M are weak. The Altman Z-Score of 1.74 falls in the distress zone, though this metric is less predictive for capital-intensive E&P companies with significant reserves. The RCF upsize to $2.0B suggests management is building borrowing capacity for the Vietnam development cycle, implying they expect to draw on this facility.

### Production Trends: Volume Headwind

2025 production grew 2.4% to 188,682 BOEPD, but the composition shifted unfavorably. Gulf of America — the highest-margin asset — declined 9.4% (from 67.6K to 61.2K BOEPD) due to Samurai well issues, natural decline, and Khaleesi maintenance. Eagle Ford partially offset this with 25% growth to 32.1K BOEPD.

The 2026 guidance of 173-181K BOEPD represents a 4-8% decline from 2025 levels. DD&A rates in the offshore segment increased from $13.69 to $16.13/BOE, reflecting higher depletion costs as the reservoir matures. The company recorded $115M in impairments on the Dalmatian field in 2025 and $63M in impairments in 2024, suggesting ongoing portfolio pruning.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term: Commodity-Driven Earnings Recovery

The primary growth driver over the next 12 months is oil price. Murphy's largely unhedged production base benefits directly from elevated crude pricing. The next quarter consensus EPS estimate is $1.34, revised up 23% in 30 days and 411% over 90 days, reflecting the oil price tailwind.

### Medium-Term: Vietnam as Step-Change Catalyst

Vietnam represents Murphy's most significant growth option. The Lac Da Vang development targets first oil in Q4 2026, with the Hai Su Vang discovery (370+ feet net pay) offering additional upside. Murphy holds 7.3M gross acres across three PSCs at 40-65% working interest. If development succeeds, it could add meaningful production within 2-3 years.

The risk is proportionate to the reward: $320M exploration and appraisal capex is guided for 2026 including three Vietnam wells, significant acreage (4,267K net acres) is scheduled to expire in 2026, and complex PSC regulatory dynamics add uncertainty.

### Gulf of America Exploration

Recent discoveries at Cello #1 and Banjo #1 (Mississippi Canyon 385) are positive for reserve replacement and demonstrate continued exploration success. However, these are tieback opportunities that take 2-3 years to reach production. They maintain the reserve base but don't accelerate near-term growth.

### Growth Limitations

Production guidance of 173-181K BOEPD for 2026 implies the company cannot grow volumes without Vietnam. The Eagle Ford is performing well but insufficient to offset Gulf of America decline rates. The Tupper Montney provides gas optionality as Canadian LNG export capacity builds, but this is a 2027+ story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.76 and consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.90, Murphy trades at 10.7x current-year earnings versus a peer median of 16.4x. On FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.77, the multiple is 11.1x.

The discount to peers is partially justified: Murphy's production is declining while peers like Magnolia (MGY, 17.5x) have flatter production profiles, and the balance sheet is more levered. However, the magnitude of the discount — 35% below peer median — provides a margin of safety if oil prices remain elevated.

The analyst target range of $33.00-$63.00 with a mean of $41.93 suggests limited upside from current levels at consensus. The wide range reflects genuine uncertainty about oil prices and Vietnam execution. Piper Sandler's upgrade to Overweight (March 2026) contrasts with Morgan Stanley's Underweight, illustrating the bifurcation.

The forward P/E of 58x reported in the profile data appears to use trailing $0.72 EPS rather than forward estimates and is misleading. The operative multiple based on consensus forward earnings is 10.7x.

## Competitive Landscape

Murphy occupies an unusual niche: a mid-cap E&P with operated deepwater Gulf of America exposure, a conventional North American onshore position, and frontier international exploration acreage. This diversification provides optionality but spreads capital thin.

Relative to pure Permian players (like EOG, FANG, or DVN), Murphy's cost structure in the Gulf of America is higher and production decline rates are steeper without continuous investment. Relative to other deepwater operators (like Hess before the Chevron acquisition), Murphy lacks scale.

The Vietnam position is the differentiating asset. If Lac Da Vang reaches first oil on schedule and Hai Su Vang is confirmed as commercial, Murphy would possess a rare emerging-market deepwater growth asset among mid-cap peers. If Vietnam disappoints, the remaining portfolio is a declining Gulf of America position and moderate Eagle Ford/Montney production — a less compelling competitive profile.

Regulatory tailwinds from the Trump Administration (expanded Gulf leasing, expedited permitting, delayed methane regulations) benefit Murphy's U.S. operations broadly but are sector-wide, not company-specific.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Oil Price Dependency (HIGH):** Murphy is almost entirely commodity-price dependent with minimal hedging. At $65 WTI (the 2025 average), the company generated near-zero FCF. Any oil price decline would compress EPS and the stock price materially.

**2. Production Decline (MODERATE-HIGH):** Gulf of America production fell 9.4% in 2025 and 2026 guidance implies 4-8% total production decline. Without Vietnam, the portfolio erodes over time. Offshore DD&A increases ($16.13/BOE in 2025, up from $13.69) signal rising depletion costs.

**3. Vietnam Execution Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** $320M in exploration capex, Q4 2026 first oil target, complex regulatory framework, and expiring acreage create binary risk. Delays or dry holes would waste significant capital while the base portfolio declines.

**4. Balance Sheet Strain (MODERATE):** Near-zero adjusted FCF in 2025, negative working capital, Altman Z-Score of 1.74, and increased total debt from the January refinancing. The $2.0B RCF provides liquidity runway but drawing on it would increase leverage.

**5. Impairment Risk:** $178M in cumulative impairments over 2024-2025 across Dalmatian, Calliope, and Nearly Headless Nick fields. Further reserve write-downs are possible if oil prices revert.

**6. Trade Policy & Tariff Uncertainty:** The 10-K explicitly flags the February 2026 "global tariff" of 10% imposed by the Trump Administration, with potential increase to 15%, which may put upward pressure on the prices of goods and services across Murphy's operating jurisdictions.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55-63, +32% to +51%)

Oil prices remain elevated through 2026 due to structural supply constraints. Murphy generates strong EPS in FY2026, FCF recovers meaningfully, share buybacks resume at scale ($550M remaining authorization), and the stock re-rates toward peer-median multiples (14-16x). Vietnam first oil on schedule in Q4 2026 provides a production growth catalyst that further narrows the peer discount. Applied 16x to $3.90 EPS = $62.40.

### Bear Case ($25-33, -21% to -40%)

Oil prices decline due to diplomatic resolution or demand weakness, returning to lower levels. Murphy's EPS compresses to $1.50-2.00, FCF turns negative, Gulf of America declines accelerate, Vietnam delays or disappoints, and the stock trades at 14-16x depressed earnings. Applied 16x to $1.75 EPS = $28.00. A sustained downturn would also pressure the $1.40/share dividend ($200M annual cost).

## Conclusion

Murphy Oil is a commodity-leveraged mid-cap E&P that has benefited enormously from elevated oil prices. The 100%+ move from April 2025 lows prices in significant improvement, but at 10.7x FY2026 consensus EPS — a 35% discount to peer median — the stock retains upside if elevated prices persist.

The fundamental concerns are real: near-zero adjusted FCF in 2025, declining Gulf of America production, heavy capex commitments to Vietnam, weak balance sheet metrics, and insider selling. These risks justified the prior AVOID rating at lower prices. However, the commodity environment has shifted decisively, estimate revisions are strongly positive, and Vietnam first oil provides a specific near-term growth catalyst.

The HOLD rating reflects the balance between a specific 12-month growth thesis (commodity tailwinds + Vietnam first oil) and the genuine risks (price dependency, execution, balance sheet). The stock is not cheap enough to warrant a BUY given the binary commodity risk and declining production base, but earnings revision momentum and structural oil price support argue against avoiding the name at these levels. Either oil prices stay elevated and Vietnam executes, or they don't. The Q1 earnings report and Gulf of America production trends are the key near-term signals.
