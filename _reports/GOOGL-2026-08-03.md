## Executive Summary

Alphabet's second quarter of 2026 was the strongest operating quarter in the company's history and simultaneously the least readable. Revenue of $119,796M grew 24.2% year over year, operating income of $40,770M grew 30.4%, operating margin expanded to 34% from 32%, and Google Cloud revenue grew 81.8% to $24,768M at a 35.6% segment operating margin. Search & other — the line the generative-AI bear case says should be eroding — accelerated to +16.8% from 13.4% for FY2025. On the reported operating figures, this is a business gaining momentum at $480B of annualized revenue scale.

The complication sits below the operating line. Reported net income of $112,193M and diluted EPS of $9.11 include a $99,031M gain on equity securities, of which $77,544M is an unrealized markup on non-marketable holdings remeasured to fair value on observable third-party transactions. Strip the marks and apply the prior-year 16.9% effective rate to the residual, and quarterly core earnings power is roughly $33B, or about $2.68 per diluted share — approximately +16% year over year rather than +294%. The same accounting will run in reverse on any private-market repricing. Separately, free cash flow turned negative in the quarter (operating cash flow $39.1B against $44.9B of capital expenditure), the $45.4B FY2025 buyback has been reduced to zero for the first half of 2026 with $69.5B of authorization unused, and Alphabet raised $105.8B of gross external capital — $56.2B debt, $30.5B common equity, $19.1B mandatory convertible preferred — while establishing a $40.0B at-the-market equity program.

The stock has rallied 17.8% in eight sessions from the 23 July post-earnings low of $317.69 to $374.11. That rally, against fundamentals that are unchanged since the print, is the operative reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY. At $374.11 the shares carry 25.3x FY2027 consensus EPS of $14.77 and roughly 29x our estimate of core FY2027 earnings power, on a business whose free cash flow yield is compressing toward zero and whose 2027 earnings face a depreciation step-up from $122.8B of assets not yet in service. Base case $405 (+8.3%), bull $475 (+27.0%), bear $285 (−23.8%).

## Company Overview

Alphabet reports two operating segments plus Other Bets and a fourth, unallocated cost pool.

**Google Services** ($342.7B FY2025 revenue; $94.5B in Q2 2026) contains Search & other ($224.5B FY2025; $63,271M in Q2 2026, +16.8%), YouTube ads ($40.4B FY2025; $11,055M, +12.9%), Google Network ($29.8B FY2025; $7,303M, −0.7%), and subscriptions/platforms/devices ($12,911M, +15.2%). The FY2025 10-K states that more than 70% of total revenue came from online advertising. Search is the company.

**Google Cloud** ($58.7B FY2025; $24,768M in Q2 2026) now contains three distinct businesses: consumption-based GCP and Workspace subscriptions; Wiz, acquired for $29.5B and consolidated from 11 March 2026; and, new in Q2 2026, product sales of TPU systems comprising hardware, software, installation, support and extended warranty. Alphabet discloses neither Wiz's revenue contribution nor the TPU revenue amount, so the organic services growth rate inside the reported 81.8% cannot be derived.

**Other Bets** ($1.5B FY2025 revenue, −$7.5B operating loss) is principally Waymo, which announced a $16.0B investment round in February 2026 with the significant majority funded by Alphabet, plus GFiber, which is being deconsolidated into a joint venture in which Alphabet retains 49.99% — below the primary-beneficiary threshold. $7.1B of GFiber PP&E is held for sale.

**Alphabet-level activities** absorbs centralized frontier-AI R&D and is not allocated to segments. That cost ran −$5,789M in Q2 2026 against −$3,372M a year earlier, and −$16,760M for FY2025 against −$10,541M in FY2024.

A data-integrity note that materially affects any screened multiple: the supplied profile shows a $2.19T market capitalization, which reconciles to Class A shares alone (5,868M × $374.11 = $2.20T). Alphabet had 12,230M shares outstanding across Class A, B and C at 30 June 2026, implying total equity value of approximately **$4.58 trillion**. The supplied trailing and forward P/E of 17.87 divides that Class A figure into mark-inflated trailing EPS of $10.80 and understates the true multiple by roughly a factor of two.

## Financial Analysis

### Operating results are genuinely strong

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $282.8B | $307.4B | $350.0B | $402.8B |
| Operating income | $74.8B | $84.3B | $112.4B | $129.0B |
| Operating margin | 26.5% | 27.4% | 32.1% | 32.0% |
| Net income | $60.0B | $73.8B | $100.1B | $132.2B |

Q2 2026 extended the trend: cost of revenues fell from 41% to 38% of revenue. Segment operating income was Services $39,544M (41.8% margin, from 40.1%), Cloud $8,814M (35.6%, from 20.7%), Other Bets −$1,799M, Alphabet-level −$5,789M.

Within Search, the monetization mix inverted. FY2025 ran paid clicks +6% and cost-per-click +7%. Q2 2026 ran **paid clicks +13%, CPC +3%**. One reading is that AI-augmented result surfaces generate materially more monetizable engagements at lower average price per engagement, which would be constructive. An equally consistent reading is geographic and device mix — emerging-market and mobile query growth carries structurally lower CPCs. The 10-Q lists multiple drivers without quantifying any, so neither explanation is established. What is established is that Search revenue growth accelerated rather than decelerated.

Google Network is contracting: −0.7% in Q2, −2.3% for the six months, −1.9% in FY2025, with impressions −12% against cost-per-impression +13%. This is the business at the center of the DOJ ad tech remedy.

### Earnings quality is the central analytical problem

Q2 2026 pre-tax income of $138,753M included $99,031M of gains on equity securities: $77,544M unrealized on non-marketable holdings accounted for under the measurement alternative, $21,399M unrealized on marketable and other equity securities, and $278M realized. The balance-sheet counterpart is non-marketable securities rising from $68,687M to $131,461M, with measurement-alternative carrying value of $124,259M against $47,642M of initial cost and $85,732M of cumulative upward adjustments. Alphabet remeasured $87.9B of that pool in the quarter based on observable transactions, classified Level 2, and describes it only as "primarily consist of our investment in a private company." SpaceX is separately named at $94.1B — $80.0B in marketable securities under short-term sale restrictions plus $14.1B in other non-current assets restricted through Q3 2027.

Reconstructing core earnings: operating income $40,770M, plus other income excluding the equity gain of −$1,048M (interest income $1,430M, interest expense $1,278M, FX −$160M, and smaller items including $581M of PriceRunner interest and a −$457M equity-derivative mark), gives $39,722M pre-tax. At the prior-year 16.9% rate rather than the mark-inflated 19.1% actual, core net income is approximately **$33.0B and core diluted EPS approximately $2.68** on 12,309M diluted shares — about +16% on the $2.31 reported a year earlier. The tax rate is an assumption and the residual line carries mixed items, so treat this as a sensitivity rather than a company figure.

This matters for how the quarter is characterized. Reported consensus for the quarter was $2.87. GAAP EPS came in at $9.11. Sell-side models could not have forecast an $87.9B private-company remeasurement, so the widely repeated "triple the consensus" framing compares GAAP-including-marks against an expectation that effectively excluded them. On a core basis the quarter arguably came in slightly light. The direction of the comparison reverses with the basis chosen, and we could not verify whether the $2.87 was formally labeled GAAP.

The marks contaminate net income, net margin, EPS, ROE, ROA, the effective tax rate (19.1% vs 16.9%, with the deferred tax liability swinging from $919M to $22,819M), and any accrual-quality screen comparing operating cash flow to net income. They do not touch revenue, operating income, operating margin, operating cash flow, capex or free cash flow. Operating income is the clean lens, and it grew 30%.

### Cash flow has inflected the wrong way

| Operating cash flow | $63,897M | $84,859M |
| Purchases of property & equipment | $(39,643)M | $(80,598)M |

Q2 2026 standalone implied roughly **−$5.8B of free cash flow**, independently reported at −$5.85B. The mechanism is straightforward: capex doubled year over year while operating cash flow grew 33%.

Longer context matters more than the single quarter. FY2025 FCF was $73,266M ($164,713M OCF less $91,447M capex) against approximately $72.8B in FY2024 — essentially flat while net income grew 32%. Capex has been absorbing the entire incremental operating cash generation for a full year and has now begun absorbing more than all of it.

We are not annualizing the negative quarter. Q2 carried an unusual working-capital drag (six-month inventory build −$7,739M as TPU hardware stocks, receivables −$6,904M, other assets −$9,950M), and the six-month figure remains positive. A normalized 2026 outcome, with second-half collections improving seasonally and capex landing within the externally reported guidance range, plausibly lands between modestly negative and modestly positive full-year FCF. That is a range, not a forecast.

One adjustment cuts against the reported figure: property and equipment included in accrued liabilities and accounts payable was **$29,113M at 30 June 2026 versus $10,635M** a year earlier, with accrued P&E purchases inside current liabilities rising from $8,877M to $16,196M. Cash capex is lagging committed capex, so the reported spend understates the economic rate.

On 4 June 2026 Alphabet sold 29M Class A at $355.1982 and 29M Class C at $351.8018 in a public offering, and concurrently placed 14M Class A and 14M Class C privately with National Indemnity Company, a Berkshire Hathaway affiliate, for $10.0B net plus registration rights. Berkshire's affiliate is a supplier of capital here. On 5 June Alphabet issued 19M shares of 6.25% mandatory convertible preferred ($19.0B net, converting around 15 May 2029) and paid $1.0B for capped calls struck 50% above the offering prices. On 1 June it established a $40.0B ATM program, undrawn at quarter-end, with proceeds primarily earmarked for employee-equity tax obligations.

Dilution, stated properly: the June issuance added 86M shares to a 12,088M year-end base, a 0.71% increase in count leaving pre-existing holders with 99.29% of the company. The mandatory convertible converts into 42.99M–53.75M shares, 0.35%–0.44% on the 12,230M base. A fully drawn ATM at ~$374 would be roughly 108M shares, or 0.88%. Aggregate incremental contingent dilution beyond today's count is on the order of 1.3%. Separately, 294M unvested RSUs/PSUs carry $59.1B of unrecognized cost over 2.7 years — a compensation-expense runway and gross future issuance figure, not part of current fully diluted capitalization under the treasury-stock method.

The economic content is that a company generating $164B of annual operating cash flow concluded it needed $105.8B of external capital and the suspension of a $45B annual buyback to fund the 2026–2027 buildout at the desired pace. An equally consistent reading is opportunistic balance-sheet optimization — terming out 15-to-31-year debt and issuing equity near record highs. Both fit the disclosed facts; net cash of ~$60B means this is not distress. What is not ambiguous is that the per-share support from share retirement has stopped, which lands squarely on the broader market dynamic of US public share count ceasing to shrink.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, of which two are strong and one is unverifiable from disclosure.

**Search resilience.** The most important datum in the quarter is that Search & other accelerated to +16.8% against 13.4% for FY2025, at $63.3B of quarterly revenue. Two years into the generative-AI displacement thesis, the reported line shows no erosion. The volume-over-price mix shift (clicks +13%, CPC +3%) is the one caveat worth monitoring: if AI surfaces are trading price for volume, the durability of the revenue line depends on volume growth continuing to outrun price decay.

**Cloud scale.** Cloud is now 20.7% of total revenue against 14.1% a year ago and expanded operating margin to 35.6%. On the available comparisons this outpaces Azure (~40% in Q1 2026) and AWS (~28%) by a wide margin. Remaining performance obligations were $519.5B at 30 June, of which $513.9B is Cloud, with just over 50% expected to be recognized within 24 months — implying roughly $32B per quarter of Cloud revenue against $24.8B reported in Q2. Two caveats are material. First, RPO is Alphabet's own defined measure, excludes cancellable contracts, and is not "total backlog." Second, **in Q1 2026 Alphabet elected to include contracts with original expected terms of one year or less in reported backlog**, so growth against pre-2026 figures is not like-for-like and is overstated by an undisclosed amount. Deferred revenue, which is comparable, was only $10.1B versus $8.6B at year-end.

**TPU systems as a merchant business.** Alphabet has signed a limited number of agreements to supply TPU systems to customers requiring or providing on-premises infrastructure, began recognizing revenue in Q2 2026, and states the significant majority will be recognized in 2027. Inventory rose 4.1x to $9,991M. This is a genuine new revenue pool and a direct monetization of the silicon advantage, but it changes the character of the Cloud line: hardware carries different margins, working capital and timing, and arrangements "may include options which are accounted for as rights of return," introducing revenue-reversal risk.

**On the estimate-revision signal.** The revision table shows current-year consensus rising from $14.23 to $20.58 in 30 days, +44.7%. That would normally register as a powerful buy signal. It does not here: the increment is arithmetically the flow-through of the $99B non-cash equity mark into GAAP full-year consensus. The relevant series is next-year consensus, which rose +1.7% in 30 days and +4.9% in 90 days to $14.77 — constructive and orderly. We are explicitly declining to treat the +44.7% as a buy signal, because the underlying economics did not change by 44.7%.

The offsetting growth concern is competitive rather than financial. Flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro has been reported as months behind schedule with coding capability the bottleneck, and management acknowledged on the earnings call that Google needs to improve in coding and agentic AI, promising Gemini 4 with potentially near-monthly release cadence. Those are externally sourced characterizations; the filings discuss no model timelines. Alphabet is committing roughly $200B of annual capex and $707B of multi-year supply obligations on the premise of frontier leadership while conceding ground in the most commercially valuable AI workload.

## Competitive Landscape

Alphabet's disclosed moats are query-volume scale with the distribution agreements that feed it, vertically integrated custom silicon, an owned infrastructure base of $247.2B gross plus $122.8B not yet in service, and an equity portfolio that has become financially material in its own right. Two of those four are under simultaneous legal attack, which is the substance of the competitive-position downgrade.

The December 2025 DOJ Search final judgment imposes distribution restrictions, mandatory sharing of certain search data with competitors, and syndication offerings to rivals. Google appealed in January 2026 and moved to pause; the court denied the stay as premature, so remedies proceed during appeal. Separately, the Q2 2026 10-Q **modified** its DMA risk factor to state the Act requires in-scope companies to mandate sharing of search data with third-party search engines and to require "interoperable access to the Android operating system by third-party AI companies." That second clause is new and would let rival AI assistants reach Android users on terms comparable to Google's own. The proprietary-data advantage and the default-distribution advantage are both being legislated against on two continents at once.

Against that, the operating evidence points the other way. Cloud is taking share from AWS and Azure at a widening margin. Custom silicon has moved from internal cost advantage to external revenue line. The Intersect acquisition ($5.9B, closed 10 March 2026, consolidated as a VIE) is vertical integration into renewable power generation, addressing the binding constraint that management describes as compute and power rather than demand — corroborated in the filings by $85.2B of leases not yet commenced, $43,785M of credit-derivative notional backstopping third-party data-center payment obligations, and $24.1B of additional backstops under negotiation.

The competitive risk that is not in the filings is model leadership. Reported perception has shifted, in one analyst's characterization, "from leading edge to trailing edge," with Anthropic and OpenAI seen as ahead in coding. Whether that converts to enterprise Cloud share loss is unproven — Cloud grew 82% during the period the delay was reported — but it is the variable that would most directly invalidate the capex commitment.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The depreciation wall.** Assets not yet in service grew from $78.6B to $122.8B in six months and do not depreciate until placed in service. Six-month depreciation of P&E already rose 43%, from $9,485M to $13,586M. Assuming the disclosed ~60/40 server-versus-buildings split for technical infrastructure with six-year and 25-year lives, the idle pool alone implies roughly **$14.2B of incremental annual depreciation**, about 11% of FY2025 operating income, before the remaining 2026 capex enters service. The lives are our assumptions; the direction is robust and the magnitude is approximate. Compounding this, the Q2 10-Q **added** language warning that changes in historical asset performance, expected technology advancements and network deployment plans "could change the period over which we expect to benefit from the asset" — a warning that useful lives could shorten.

**2. Commitments and contingent exposures at unprecedented scale.** Purchase commitments and other contractual obligations went from **$149.1B at 12/31/25 to $811.0B at 6/30/26**, with $707.0B beyond one year, the significant majority long-term technical-infrastructure and component supply agreements generally fulfilled through 2030, plus energy agreements running 2–26 years with obligations through 2054 containing take-or-pay minimums and substantive termination fees. Add $85.2B of leases not yet commenced, $43,785M of credit-derivative notional (from $16,940M), $7.6B of financial guarantees, $24.1B of additional backstops subject to finalization, and $20.0B of contingent equity funding to a private company through 2030. These are heterogeneous and must not be summed into a liability. But roughly $1.0 trillion of commitments and contingent exposures against $922B of total assets is the defining feature of the current financial position, and the recognized fair value of the credit derivatives rose from a $69M liability to an **$815M** liability in six months — a 11.8x increase in market-implied counterparty stress, albeit from a low base.

**3. DOJ ad tech remedy, unquantified and overdue.** The April 2025 Eastern District of Virginia decision found Google's publisher tools unlawfully exclusionary while holding advertiser tools and the DoubleClick/AdMeld acquisitions were not anticompetitive. Remedies were tried in September 2025, closing arguments held November 2025, and as of the 23 July 2026 10-Q Alphabet is still "awaiting a final judgment" — roughly eight months. The filing states the DOJ proposal "includes structural remedies that could have a material adverse effect on our business" and that Alphabet "cannot estimate a possible loss." A Texas state-AG trial in the Eastern District of Texas follows. The earnings exposure is smaller than the revenue exposure: Google Network was $29.8B, 7.4% of FY2025 revenue, and carries by far the highest TAC intensity, so divestiture would be materially less dilutive to earnings than to the top line. The extended delay supports no inference about direction.

**4. Private follow-on damages as an open category.** The European Court of Justice denied Google's Android appeal in July 2026, finalizing the €4.1B decision; Alphabet paid $5.2B cash in July. The Stockholm Patent and Market Court awarded PriceRunner approximately $2.1B in a private action; Alphabet recognized $1.5B of principal in G&A plus $581M in OI&E and appealed. The 10-Q flags a whole category of "private claims stemming from regulatory proceedings in which Google's liability has been fully determined," with no estimable loss. Every finally adjudicated EC decision becomes a template. Accrued fines and settlements stood at $17,356M.

**5. Earnings-quality two-sidedness.** The $124.3B measurement-alternative book carries $85.7B of cumulative upward adjustments against $47.6B of cost. The same Level 2 remeasurement mechanism that produced $77.5B of gains produces losses on a private-market repricing, and the counterparty is unnamed in the filings. Press reporting attributes the markup to a named AI lab; Alphabet has not confirmed it, nor disclosed whether that entity is a material Cloud customer, nor stated that the $20.0B contingent funding is conditioned on purchases. If the attribution is correct, a circular flow exists — capital to a lab, lab consumes TPU capacity, Cloud revenue and RPO grow, subsequent round marks up the stake, gain runs through net income — but the filings do not support asserting it, and an ordinary strategic investment in a large customer is an equally consistent explanation.

**6. Advertising concentration.** More than 70% of FY2025 revenue was advertising and Search alone was $224.5B of $402.8B. Cloud growth lowers the percentage; it does not diversify demand. Alphabet discloses no customer-concentration data at all.

**7. New risk factors.** The Q2 10-Q added a standalone risk factor on ATM issuance, preferred conversion and dilution "depressing the stock price," and expanded the financing risk factor to name obligations arising under leases, backstops, guarantees and large commercial agreements as potentially harming financial condition and reducing flexibility. Management is telling readers, in the risk section, that the financing structure itself is now a risk.

### Options Market Signal

The headline readings look alarming and mostly are not. Near-term ATM IV of 46.6% against 31.7% one-year historical vol, a −34.6% put/call skew the data source labels call-heavy, and a term structure labeled backwardation all derive from the 1-day 3 August expiry, whose implied move is ±0.9%. The unusual-activity table is entirely same-day and next-session calls at $368–$390 with volume-to-open-interest ratios of 10x–53x — expiry-day gamma and momentum flow into the rally, not positioning ahead of a catalyst.

The informative part is the back end, and it is unremarkable: 33.8% at one month, 34.2% at three months, 33.3% at six months, against 31.7% realized, with skews of +3.7%, +0.4% and −3.5%. That is roughly fair vol and near-neutral directional structure. Put/call open interest of 0.60 and a 0.34 volume ratio show no hedging build. Short interest is 1.3% of float at 2.3 days to cover — no crowding on either side. The IV snapshot compressed from 47.8% to 46.6% while the price rose 1.3% in under an hour, consistent with mechanical expiry decay rather than a change in risk assessment.

The options market is pricing no imminent stress at the tenors that matter and offers neither confirmation nor challenge to the fundamental view. The three-month tenor's 8.6% implied move brackets the next earnings print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $475 (+27.0%)

Revenue growth of 24.2% at $480B annualized scale is being delivered by an accelerating Search franchise and a Cloud business growing 82% with margins that nearly doubled year over year. The generative-AI disruption thesis has now had two years to appear in the Search line and has not. Cloud RPO of $513.9B, even discounting the definitional expansion, implies roughly $32B of quarterly Cloud revenue over the next eight quarters against $24.8B reported. The TPU merchant business begins recognizing the significant majority of signed agreements in 2027, opening a hardware revenue pool that no other hyperscaler can replicate and that converts an internal cost advantage into external revenue. Alphabet is the only hyperscaler that designs its own accelerators, owns its own renewable generation developer, and funds the frontier labs consuming its capacity. At 25.3x FY2027 consensus against Meta at 20.7x on materially slower growth, the premium is modest for the growth and margin differential. If 2027 capex guidance stabilizes and free cash flow inflects positive, the $69.5B of unused repurchase authorization and a resumed buyback become the re-rating catalyst. Balance sheet remains net cash ~$60B with an Altman Z in the safe zone.

### Bear Case — $285 (−23.8%)

Two-thirds of the record quarter was a non-cash markup on an unnamed private holding; on a core basis earnings grew 16%, and our core estimate of $2.68 sits below the $2.87 consensus. Free cash flow was negative in the quarter and flat for the prior full year despite 32% net income growth, because capex has consumed all incremental operating cash. Capex guidance has stepped from $175–185B to $180–190B to $195–205B in roughly three months, with management signaling another increase for 2027, and cash capex understates commitments given $29.1B of P&E sitting in payables versus $10.6B a year earlier. Roughly $1.0 trillion of commitments and contingent exposures — including take-or-pay energy through 2054, $43.8B of data-center payment backstops whose recognized liability rose 11.8x, and $20B of contingent equity funding — is a structurally levered bet on AI demand materializing on schedule. Meanwhile the flagship model is months late, management concedes gaps in coding and agentic AI, the DOJ ad tech structural remedy has been pending eight months with no estimable loss, US and EU mandates are prising open both the data and distribution moats, and the first private follow-on damages award has landed at $2.1B with an explicitly open-ended category behind it. At $374.11, 8% above the post-earnings level and inside 12% of the all-time high, none of that is being discounted.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold). Horizon: 12 months.**

- **Base case target: $405 (+8.3%)** — 27.4x FY2027 consensus EPS of $14.77. Assumes Cloud growth decelerating from 82% toward the 50–60% range, Search holding mid-teens, 2027 capex guided within $220–250B without a step-function surprise, and an ad tech remedy that is behavioral or narrowly structural.

- **Risk/reward:** 8.3% to base against 23.8% to bear. That asymmetry is the arithmetic reason this is not a BUY at $374.11. At $330–340 — the analyst low target and roughly the 27–28 July trading range — base-case upside widens to 19–23% against comparable downside, which would support adding.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Positive free cash flow in Q3 or Q4 2026 with 2027 capex guidance held at or below roughly $230B, demonstrating that the buildout is self-funding.
2. Resumption of share repurchases from the $69.5B authorization, or explicit management commitment to a capital-return floor.
3. Ad tech final judgment imposing behavioral rather than structural relief, removing the largest unquantified overhang.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Search & other growth decelerating below 10% year over year for two consecutive quarters, or paid-click growth decelerating while CPC remains flat-to-negative — the specific signature of AI substitution eroding monetization.
2. 2027 capex guidance above $250B combined with a shortening of announced useful lives, which would compound the depreciation step-up.
3. Structural divestiture ordered in the ad tech remedy without a stay, or a DMA implementation decision granting third-party AI interoperable Android access on terms that materially degrade Gemini distribution.
4. A material downward remeasurement of the $124.3B measurement-alternative equity book, which would signal private-market repricing and reverse the mechanism that has been flattering reported earnings.
5. Cloud operating margin retreating below 25% while the Wiz and TPU contributions remain undisclosed, indicating the reported 35.6% was mix rather than operating leverage.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in late October is the primary checkpoint — it will show whether free cash flow recovered seasonally, whether the depreciation ramp is tracking the sensitivity above, and whether Cloud growth ex-inorganic contributions is disclosed with any greater granularity. The DOJ ad tech judgment could land at any time and is not calendar-dated.

## Conclusion

This is the third consecutive HOLD on Alphabet, and the score has drifted from 7.5 in April to 6.1 now. The trajectory deserves an explicit accounting, because it is not driven by deteriorating operations. Revenue growth accelerated from the mid-teens to 24.2%; operating margin expanded to 34%; Search accelerated; Cloud grew 82%. Every operating metric improved.

What deteriorated is the translation from operating results to shareholder cash. Financial health fell from 8.5 to 6.0 because free cash flow went from $73B and stable to roughly zero, long-term debt doubled in six months, and roughly $1.0 trillion of commitments and contingent exposures now sits against $922B of assets. Management quality fell from 7.5 to 5.5 because a $45B annual buyback was replaced by $105.8B of external capital raising and a $40B ATM, incentive design is anchored solely to relative TSR with no disclosed return-on-capital gate, and there has been no open-market insider buying — all C-suite Form 4 activity through 30 July 2026 is vesting and tax withholding. Competitive position fell from 9.0 to 7.0 because the flagship model is late in the workload where competitors are strongest, while US and EU remedies simultaneously target the query-data and default-distribution advantages.

Those are genuine changes in the investment case, and they were already largely reflected by the prior report. The incremental reason for a modestly lower score today is price: the stock has recovered 17.8% from $317.69 on 23 July to $374.11, absorbing the capex increase that initially drove the 4.7% post-earnings decline. At 25.3x FY2027 consensus and roughly 29x our estimate of core FY2027 earnings power, with a free cash flow yield near zero and a $14.2B annual depreciation step-up approaching, base-case upside of 8.3% does not compensate for bear-case downside of 23.8%.

We are also explicitly declining to treat the +44.7% thirty-day rise in current-year consensus as an earnings inflection. It is the mechanical flow-through of a non-cash equity markup into GAAP consensus. The comparable series — next-year consensus — rose 4.9% in 90 days, which is orderly and constructive but not evidence that the market is systematically underestimating this business.

Own Alphabet. It is the best-positioned large-cap in AI infrastructure on operating evidence, with a Search franchise that has refused to break, a Cloud business taking share, and merchant silicon optionality no competitor can match. But the entry price no longer justifies adding, the cash-generation profile has genuinely changed, and there is a specific, dated set of conditions — positive free cash flow, contained 2027 capex, resumed buyback, ad tech resolution — under which this becomes a BUY again. Wait for one of them, or wait for $330–340.
