## Executive Summary

TJX enters the second half of FY2027 with the strongest operating momentum in the off-price channel in three years and a valuation that already reflects it. FY2026 (ended January 31, 2026) produced revenue of $60,372M (+7.1%), pretax income of $7,299M, net income of $5,494M and diluted EPS of $4.87, with pretax margin of 12.09% — 281 basis points above FY2023's 9.28%. Q1 FY2027 extended the trend: net sales of $14,323M (+9.2%), a +6% consolidated comp against a +3% prior-year base, and EPS of $1.19 versus $0.92.

Two adjustments matter for how much of that operating step-up should be capitalized. First, FY2026 EPS included $0.14 from the credit-card interchange settlement in which TJX was plaintiff — a $419M net gain less $116M of incremental incentive compensation and $82M of discretionary bonus, or $221M pretax and $166M after tax. Clean FY2026 EPS is approximately $4.73 and clean pretax margin 11.72%, so genuine underlying margin expansion in FY2026 was roughly 22bp, not the 59bp reported. Second, Q1 FY2027 benefited from a $151M favorable year-over-year swing in derivative mark-to-market (+$54M versus −$97M), worth roughly $0.10 per share after tax, or about 39% of the $0.27 year-over-year EPS increase. TJX does not elect hedge accounting; these swings are symmetric and recur, but neither Q1 FY2026 nor Q1 FY2027 is a clean run rate.

The arithmetic of full-year guidance is the central issue. Externally reported FY2027 guidance of $63.2–63.7B in sales and $5.08–5.15 in EPS, at an estimated ~1,110M average diluted share count and a ~23.5% tax rate, implies pretax income near $7,422M and pretax margin of approximately 11.70% — essentially identical to FY2026's clean 11.72%, after Q1 already delivered +170bp. The guide therefore embeds margin compression across Q2–Q4 and residual H2 sales growth of roughly 3.6% against Q1's +9.2%. On comps, Q1's two-year stack was 9 points; the Q2 guide of +2–3% against a +4% prior year is a 6.5-point stack, roughly 250bp of two-year deceleration that a harder compare alone does not explain. TJX has a documented habit of conservative guidance — it beat and raised in Q2 FY2026, Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 — and at 30.5x forward earnings the market is pricing that habit as fact. We rate the stock HOLD with a base case of $168 (+7.9%), a bull case of $195 and a bear case of $124.

## Company Overview

TJX is the largest off-price apparel and home-fashions retailer globally, operating 5,214 stores and six e-commerce sites as of January 31, 2026, with merchandise priced generally 20–60% below full-price retailers' regular prices on comparable goods. FY2026 segment detail:

| Segment | Net Sales | Segment Profit | Margin | Stores | Long-term potential |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marmaxx (TJ Maxx, Marshalls, Sierra) | $36,585M | $5,528M | 15.1% | 2,748 | 3,000 Marmaxx + 325 Sierra |
| HomeGoods / Homesense US | $10,172M | $1,246M | 12.2% | 1,042 | 1,800 |
| TJX Canada | $5,629M | $757M | 13.4% | 589 | 650 |
| TJX International | $7,986M | $558M | 7.0% | 835 | 1,225 |
| **Total** | **$60,372M** | **$8,089M** | — | **5,214** | **7,000** |

Marmaxx is 60.6% of sales and 68.3% of aggregate segment profit. The U.S. is 78% of consolidated revenue, and the 10-K geographic table shows every regional share unchanged year over year (Northeast 21%, South 28%, Midwest 13%, West 16%, Canada 9%, Europe 12%, Australia 1%). Faster reported growth at TJX International (+11%) and Canada (+8%) did not shift the mix, because roughly 5 percentage points of International's growth was FX translation. Geographic concentration is stable, not improving.

The moat mechanism is specific and disclosed rather than generic. TJX buys less-than-full assortments in quantities from small to very large, disperses goods across a geographically diverse network, pays vendors promptly per terms, and declines to ask for markdown, advertising or promotional allowances, delivery concessions, or performance-based return privileges. Its vendor base is approximately 21,000 across 100+ countries, served by a buying organization of over 1,400 associates. The consequence is no customer concentration and no vendor concentration, and a model financed by TJX's own balance sheet — cash and credit rating function as operating assets because TJX absorbs merchandise risk in exchange for access.

The structural limitation is equally clear: e-commerce is approximately 2% of total sales in both FY2026 and FY2025, and management's own risk factor concedes consumer e-commerce spending may keep rising while the business remains primarily brick-and-mortar.

Both freight and shrink are cyclical normalizations rather than durable structural gains, which is why they matter to the H2 setup.

**Balance sheet (May 2, 2026).** Cash $5,580M; merchandise inventories $7,675M; total assets $36,158M; funded debt $2,870M ($999M 2.250% notes due 9/15/2026, $500M 1.150% 2028, $496M 3.875% 2030, $500M 1.600% 2031, $383M 4.500% 2050); shareholders' equity $10,403M. Goodwill is $97M, so there is effectively no acquisition-intangible impairment risk at the consolidated level.

The one substantive leverage item is $11,310M of operating lease liabilities ($1,714M current, $9,596M long-term) against $11,025M of right-of-use assets, plus approximately 180 signed-but-uncommenced leases excluded from that balance. Funded debt/equity is 0.28x; including capitalized leases, adjusted debt/equity is roughly 1.36x. Screening outputs that show a 3.51 "debt/equity" are reporting the equity multiplier (total assets/equity = $35,767/$10,190 = 3.510), and a grey-zone Altman Z built on zeroed EBIT is not applicable: with FY2026 operating income of $7,178M and market equity, the public-firm Z computes to roughly 6.8. The 9/15/2026 $999M maturity is covered 5.6x by cash on hand. Solvency is not a live question.

**Returns and cash conversion.** ROA of 15.4% and asset turnover of 1.69x are the cleaner efficiency reads; ROE of 53.9% on ending equity is inflated because TJX retires repurchased shares, charging the excess over par to APIC (now zero) and then retained earnings — the Q1 statement of equity shows $608M of repurchases charging $565M directly to retained earnings. FY2026 operating cash flow of $6,874M exceeded net income of $5,494M, and capex of $1,957M left free cash flow of $4,917M, an 8.1% FCF margin.

Note the timing split — the $419M gain hit Q4 FY2026 earnings, while the cash arrived in Q1 FY2027. Ex-settlement Q1 OCF was approximately $686M. No single TJX quarter should be annualized given seasonality (the company states H2 carries higher sales and income) and the Q1 accrued-expense drawdown of −$954M, largely prior-year incentive comp payout. As a sensitivity only: FY2026 OCF held flat less guided FY2027 capex of $2.2–2.3B gives roughly $4.6–4.7B, and adding the settlement collection widens that to roughly $4.6–5.1B.

**Capital allocation.** FY2026: capex $1,957M (renovations $921M, offices/DCs $851M, new stores $185M); 18.5M shares retired for $2.5B; dividends $1.8B; total returned $4.3B. FY2027 plan: capex $2.2–2.3B, dividend $1.92 annualized (+13%), buyback raised in the 10-Q to $2.75–3.0B from the 10-K's $2.5–2.75B, with $3.5B of authorization remaining. Planned FY2027 shareholder return of roughly $4.87–5.12B equals 2.83–2.97% of the current market capitalization.

Diluted shares have fallen from 1,178M (FY2023) to 1,120M (Q1 FY2027), about 1.2% per year. Q1 FY2027 repurchases retired 3.83M shares at an average of roughly $157.87, versus 5.1M shares in Q1 FY2026 for comparable dollars. Repurchase is a materially less efficient EPS lever at a 30x multiple than it was at 20x, and the growing dividend consumes an increasing share of the return.

**Earnings surprise track record.** Four consecutive beats: +7.5%, +3.7%, +1.9%, +15.9%. That record supports confidence that consensus FY2027 EPS of $5.21 — above the guidance midpoint of $5.115 — is achievable. It does not resolve whether the H2 margin path in guidance is conservatism or cost pressure.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has three legs, of decidedly different quality.

**Unit runway is the most durable.** 5,214 stores against 7,000 of stated potential in current geographies implies roughly 34% of remaining growth. The FY2027 plan is 146 net new stores (45 Marmaxx, 24 Sierra, 24 HomeGoods, 11 Homesense, 13 Canada, 19 Europe, 10 Australia), or about 2.8% unit growth. Spain entry began March 2026. HomeGoods carries the largest absolute runway at +758 stores.

**HomeGoods is the best-performing internal story.** Segment margin moved 10.9% (FY2025) → 12.2% (FY2026) → 12.9% (Q1 FY2027) on a +9% Q1 comp, closing to within roughly 180bp of Marmaxx from a ~320bp gap in FY2025. Part of that gain is lower freight, a cyclical tailwind, and Q1 commentary already cites higher markdowns partially offsetting markon gains at HomeGoods and International.

**TJX International remains the persistent shortfall.** At 7.0% FY2026 segment margin on $7,986M of sales (4.6% in seasonally weak Q1), International earns roughly half the U.S. rate. Closing the gap to 10% would add approximately $240M of pretax profit, or about $0.16 per share. This has not converted across multiple years, and TJX is now layering complexity: Spain, a 49% Mexico joint venture with Grupo Axo (MOS, carried at $226M), and a 35% non-controlling stake in Brands for Less in the UAE and Saudi Arabia (carried at $340M). Combined carrying value of $566M includes roughly $484M of premium over TJX's share of net assets — the impairment exposure if either underperforms. Both are reported on a one-quarter lag in SG&A and were immaterial to Q1 results.

**Estimate revisions are stable, not inflecting.** Current-year consensus moved from $5.11 to $5.21 over 90 days (+2.0%) and next year from $5.68 to $5.71 (+0.6%), with 30-day changes essentially zero. Next-quarter estimates were trimmed 1.9%. This is the profile of a steady compounder, not an inflection candidate — the elevated-revision signal that would justify paying a premium multiple is absent.

Consensus implies EPS growth of roughly +10.1% in FY2027 (versus clean FY2026 of $4.73) and +9.6% in FY2028. That is respectable and reasonably durable given the unit pipeline and the trade-down tailwind, but it is not accelerating.

## Competitive Landscape

TJX's structural advantage is intact and mechanically sound. The vendor-attraction terms — prompt payment, no markdown or advertising allowances, no delivery concessions, no return privileges, an excellent credit rating — are the reason 21,000 vendors including thousands of new ones in FY2026 sell to TJX, and they are hard for a subscale competitor to replicate because they require absorbing merchandise risk on one's own balance sheet.

The relative trajectory is less flattering. Burlington reported +6% comps in the comparable quarter with an EPS beat of $0.30 and raised FY guidance to $11.45–11.80. Ross Stores raised FY2026 same-store-sales guidance to 6–7% on top of a 5% FY2025 gain, plans approximately 110 new stores (about 5% unit growth, versus TJX's 2.8%), and reports a double-digit increase in comp-store customer count across demographics under CEO James Conroy, who joined in December 2024. (A secondary report of 17% Q1 comps at Ross is internally inconsistent with that same source's full-year guide and is not treated as established.)

On the credible reading, Ross's full-year comp guide sits at or above TJX's own plan, and Burlington matched TJX's Q1 comp. The off-price channel is broadly capturing trade-down, but TJX is not visibly out-comping its two closest peers in this cycle, and Ross is accelerating unit growth from a smaller base. That is a change from the prior decade's pattern. Offsetting: TJX's five-year TSR of 251.93 versus a peer group at 209.16 shows full-cycle outperformance, and its geographic and category diversification (HomeGoods +9% comp) should produce steadier if slower growth than a U.S.-only apparel-weighted competitor.

One structural nuance favors the channel. Tariff disruption at full-price retailers and brands generates the closeouts, cancellations and overruns TJX buys. The Q1 cost-of-sales improvement was driven partly by higher markon, which is consistent with buying unusually well — though it is equally consistent with retail price increases passed to consumers, which carries elasticity risk and which the 10-K itself flags as a threat to TJX's "reputation as a value retailer."

Competitive position score: 6.5 — a genuine and stable moat, with relative share momentum no better than peers and a 2% e-commerce position that management concedes is a competitive gap, now compounded by an explicit risk factor about being disadvantaged in AI adoption.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs in a reconstituted regime.** On February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs. TJX estimates it paid approximately $490M in IEEPA-related tariffs, filed phase-1 refund claims with CBP after quarter-end, and had received an immaterial amount as of May 29, 2026 with no receivable recorded pending ASC 450 realizability. Critically, an executive order thereafter imposed a new global tariff in addition to existing non-IEEPA tariffs. The ruling converted a past cost into a contingent claim without reducing forward exposure, and neither filing quantifies expected FY2027 tariff cost. Refunds may also flow to competitors and vendors, compressing any industry-wide pricing benefit TJX has been capturing through higher markon.

**Lapping freight and shrink.** FY2026 merchandise margin explicitly benefited from lower freight and lower shrink. The Q1 diesel-hedge asset rose to $50.0M from $6.2M at year-end, with a Q1 diesel gain of +$47M versus −$12M a year prior. A large positive diesel mark implies diesel rose against fixed strikes — meaning the hedge gain is the offset to higher cash freight surcharges that flow into cost of sales in later quarters. Coverage was approximately 40% of estimated notional requirements against a stated intention of ~50%. This is the most concrete channel through which H2 costs rise.

**Earnings quality.** Roughly 39% of the Q1 year-over-year EPS increase was derivative mark-to-market. The FY2026 effective tax rate of 24.7% and Q1's 22.6% both benefited from acquired transferable federal tax credits, a discretionary and potentially non-repeatable below-the-line lever. Neither item is improper, but both mean reported growth overstates the operating step-up.

**Inventory signal, ambiguous.** Per-store inventory (ex-e-commerce, ex-in-transit) was +10% at FY2026 year-end and +7% at Q1 FY2027. Countervailing: consolidated inventories grew 7.7% versus 9.2% sales growth, so turns modestly improved, and Sierra's newly included inclusion in the metric impairs comparability. The dedicated shrink risk factor specifically cites "increased inventory levels in our stores" as an aggravating factor, and Q1 already cites higher markdowns at HomeGoods and International.

**Succession concentration.** The six named executive officers average roughly 65 years of age and every one is a long-tenured internal promotee (CEO Ernie Herrman with TJX since 1989, CEO since January 2016; Executive Chairman Carol Meyrowitz with TJX since 1983). The insularity that produces the merchandising edge — the 10-K notes the off-price model requires significant internal training — is the same factor that concentrates succession risk. The risk factors name CEO transition explicitly. No Item 5.02 event has been filed since February 3, 2025, and no succession timetable has been disclosed.

**Consumer backdrop.** Rising fuel pass-through and a possibly weaker discretionary spending environment argue for caution into 2027. Off-price is the natural beneficiary of trade-down, and analysts covering the Q1 print reported no signs of consumer weakness. But a genuine consumer cliff compresses total apparel and home spending, and at 30x TJX has no multiple cushion for a comp deceleration to +1–2%.

**Not risks.** Leverage, covenants, refinancing, customer or vendor concentration, restatement history, auditor change, goodwill impairment at the consolidated level, and dilution overhang (14M of dilutive awards, 3M antidilutive excluded, no warrants or earn-outs).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 27.2% sits 7.6 points above 19.7% one-year realized volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (27.2% near versus 24.5% at six months), with the one-month tenor at 31.5% and a 5.8% implied move — consistent with pricing the late-August Q2 print as the dominant near-term event. ATM IV rose 3.5 points in a single session (23.7% → 27.2%) on a 0.9% price decline, so uncertainty is being repriced faster than direction.

Positioning is mixed rather than uniformly bearish. Put/call skew is −6.0% and described as call-heavy, and open-interest put/call is 0.66, both indicating that the standing book is not defensively positioned. Against that, the put/call volume ratio is 4.63 (7.12 at the one-month tenor) and there is one clean unusual print: 6,985 contracts of the August 28 $145 put against 141 open interest, 49.5x volume-to-OI, 7.0% out of the money at 30.2% IV. Someone is buying a specific, dated hedge sized to a post-earnings gap lower.

Short interest is negligible at 1.6% of float and 3.0 days to cover, so there is no crowded bearish position and no squeeze dynamic. The takeaway supports the fundamental read: the standing book is complacent, but fresh flow is paying up for downside protection into a print where the guidance arithmetic implies decelerating comps and flat clean margins. Options data does not change the recommendation; it corroborates that the Q2 report is a two-sided event on a fully valued stock.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $195 (+25%)

TJX guides conservatively and has beaten in four consecutive quarters, most recently by 15.9%. If Q2 comps land at +4–5% rather than the guided +2–3%, full-year EPS reaches $5.30–5.40 against guidance of $5.08–5.15, and the market extends the off-price cohort multiple of 33–35x to TJX. Trade-down accelerates as living costs squeeze mid-market consumers, precisely the demographic that shifts to TJ Maxx and Marshalls; the mid-market crack visible elsewhere in apparel retail is TJX's traffic source. Tariff disruption at full-price retailers expands closeout availability, sustaining the higher markon that drove Q1's 180bp cost-of-sales improvement. HomeGoods continues closing the margin gap to Marmaxx on its 758-store runway, International finally moves toward 10% segment margin (worth ~$0.16 per share), and $490M of tariff refunds convert to a recognized ~$0.34 per share. Net cash, $1.5B of undrawn revolvers and $3.5B of buyback authorization mean the company can lean into any share-price weakness. At 33x FY2028 consensus of $5.71 plus a partial refund benefit, $195 is reachable — close to the analyst high of $197.

### Bear Case — $124 (−20%)

The guidance implies compression because the compression is real. Freight and shrink, FY2026's two named margin tailwinds, lap out. The diesel hedge covers only 40% of notional against a 50% target, so rising fuel converts to cash freight surcharges in cost of sales while the mark-to-market gain that offset it in Q1 reverses. The post-IEEPA global tariff raises landed cost with no disclosed quantification, and higher retail prices meet elasticity — the 10-K's own warning about TJX's value-retailer reputation. Per-store inventory up 7–10% into a decelerating comp environment forces markdowns, which Q1 already flagged at HomeGoods and International. Ross accelerates to 6–7% comps and 5% unit growth while TJX comps toward +2%, and the relative-momentum premium reverses. A consumer cliff compresses total category spending rather than merely shifting it. On $5.10–5.15 of EPS at 24x — the low end of TJX's post-2021 range — the stock is $122–124, near the $125 low analyst target. Approximately 39% of the Q1 EPS gain being non-operating and roughly 1.2% annual share reduction at a 30x purchase price leave little to defend the multiple.

### Base Case — $168 (+7.9%)

TJX delivers FY2027 EPS of $5.20–5.25, modestly above the guidance midpoint, consistent with its beat pattern but well short of the +170bp margin trajectory Q1 implied in isolation. Comps run +3–4% for the balance of the year, freight and tariff costs offset most of the markon benefit, and clean pretax margin lands near 11.8%. FY2028 EPS reaches roughly $5.70. Applying 29.5x — the midpoint of TJX's own recent trading range and a continued modest discount to ROST at 33.4x — to FY2028 consensus of $5.71 gives $168. Add roughly 1.2% of annual share reduction and a 1.23% dividend, and total return over twelve months is roughly 9–10%. That is a fair outcome for a high-quality compounder and an inadequate one for new capital, given that the bear case is a 20% drawdown.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The twelve-month growth thesis is positive — mid-single-digit sales growth, high-single-digit EPS growth, 146 net new stores, and 34% of remaining unit runway — but the entry price at 29.9x consensus FY2027 EPS and 27.3x FY2028 does not offer asymmetry. Existing holders should stay; new capital should wait.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 FY2027 report, expected late August 2026. This is the decisive event, and the options market is pricing a 5.8% move around it.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Price toward $132–138 (23–24x FY2028 consensus $5.71) with fundamentals intact — comps at or above +3% and no guidance cut. A 15% decline on unchanged earnings power would put the FCF yield above 3.3% and the total shareholder return above 3.4%, restoring the asymmetry.
2. A Q2 print showing pretax margin expansion excluding derivative mark-to-market — specifically, cost-of-sales ratio improvement attributed to merchandise margin rather than hedge marks — combined with comps at or above +4% and a raised full-year EPS guide to $5.25 or better.
3. TJX International segment margin above 8% on a trailing-four-quarter basis, plus recognition of a material tariff-refund receivable. Together these are worth roughly $0.45–0.50 of incremental EPS not currently in estimates.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Consolidated comps below +1% in any quarter, or Q2 comps below the guided +2%.
2. Clean pretax margin compression (excluding derivative marks) in two consecutive quarters, with MD&A attributing it to freight, tariff or markdown pressure rather than mix.
3. Per-store inventory growth sustained at +10% or higher alongside an explicit markdown-driven merchandise margin decline — the combination that historically precedes an off-price gross-margin reset.
4. A full-year EPS guidance cut below $5.00, or a CEO transition announced without a named internal successor.

## Trajectory Note

Scores have declined from 7.0 (February) to 6.6 (July) to 6.3 now, and this is the fifth consecutive HOLD. The bar for repeating an unchanged call should be higher, so the reasoning deserves to be explicit. Financial health has genuinely improved across the sequence (7.5 → 8.5): net cash, corrected solvency math, OCF above net income, and full coverage of the September 2026 maturity. Management quality declined 8.0 → 6.0 on the June insider-selling cluster (five C-suite executives, roughly $27.5M in nine days, no offsetting open-market purchases) and on buyback efficiency deteriorating as the multiple expanded — Q1 retired 3.83M shares versus 5.1M a year earlier for comparable dollars. Risk factors declined 7.0 → 6.0 on the tariff-regime reconstitution and the lapping of freight and shrink tailwinds.

The incremental change this cycle is valuation, not fundamentals. Price is essentially flat since the prior review ($157.34 official close on July 31 versus $155.77 now, and roughly flat since the $171.7B market cap reported July 24), so this is not a case of marking scores to price action. What has changed is the decomposition: the Q1 beat is now understood to be roughly 39% derivative mark-to-market, FY2026's reported margin expansion is roughly 22bp rather than 59bp on a clean basis, and full-year guidance arithmetically implies flat clean margins for the balance of the year. Estimate revisions are stable at +2.0% for the current year rather than accelerating. A 30x multiple on that combination earns a lower valuation score than the same multiple would on genuinely accelerating operating margins.

The insider-selling cluster deserves calibration. It follows March 30 performance-award vesting (Herrman 212,072 shares delivered, Meyrowitz 94,136) with large same-day tax withholdings, which makes the June sales a recurring post-vest diversification pattern rather than an idiosyncratic signal. ## Conclusion

TJX is one of the better-run retailers in the market: 5,214 stores growing toward 7,000, roughly 21,000 vendors with no concentration on either side of the transaction, $2.71B of net cash, 15.4% ROA, $4.9B of annual free cash flow, and a business model that structurally benefits when full-price retail is disrupted. The Q1 comp of +6% and HomeGoods segment margin of 12.9% are real operating achievements, and the four-quarter beat record justifies confidence that FY2027 consensus of $5.21 will be met or exceeded.

The stock prices that. At 29.9x consensus FY2027 EPS, 27.3x FY2028, a PEG above 3, a 2.86% FCF yield and a 2.8–3.0% total shareholder return, TJX offers roughly 8% to a base case of $168 against a 20% drawdown to $124 if the H2 margin compression embedded in guidance turns out to be forecast rather than conservatism. Roughly 39% of the Q1 EPS increase was derivative mark-to-market, the two named FY2026 margin tailwinds (freight, shrink) are lapping, the diesel hedge covers 40% of notional against a 50% target, and a new global tariff replaced the invalidated IEEPA regime with no disclosed forward cost. Ross and Burlington are matching or exceeding TJX's comp momentum, so the relative-quality premium is not being extended by operating results.

HOLD. Own it for the compounding — mid-single-digit sales growth, high-single-digit EPS growth, a 13% dividend increase and a 2.8–3.0% capital return, all funded from a net-cash balance sheet. Add on a decline toward $132–138, or on a Q2 print that demonstrates margin expansion excluding hedge marks with comps at or above +4%. The late-August report is the decision point, and the options market is pricing a 5.8% move around it.
