**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dollar General is roughly 18 months into a self-inflicted turnaround. The margin collapse of FY2024 (operating margin fell from 6.32% to 4.22% on excess shrink, elevated damages, and bloated inventory) brought Todd Vasos back as CEO in 2023 to fix operational failures the company itself created. The repair is working. FY2025 gross margin recovered +107 bps to 30.7%, operating margin rebounded to 5.16%, and diluted EPS rose to $6.85 from $5.11. Q1 FY2026 continued the trend (gross margin +65 bps to 31.6%, EPS $2.00 up 12.4%, same-store sales +2.0%), and management raised full-year sales and profit guidance. The company has beaten consensus in four consecutive quarters, twice by double-digit percentages.

The recovery has stalled short of prior earnings power while new structural headwinds have emerged. Operating margin at 5.16% remains over a point below the FY2023 peak of 6.32%, and the drivers of the recovery (lower shrink and damages) are largely one-time normalization rather than durable expansion. Two headwinds are converging in FY2026-27: the Work Opportunity Tax Credit expired for employees hired after 12/31/2025, already lifting the Q1 effective tax rate to 24.9% from 23.4%, a structural EPS drag given DG's high-turnover entry-level workforce; and rising fuel costs are hitting both DG's own transportation and store expense and its low-income customer's trip frequency. These are why management's own raised guidance implies a decelerating second half.

At ~$120 the stock trades at ~17.4x trailing/forward earnings, a discount to the 20.9x peer median but consistent with an incomplete margin recovery, a live securities class action naming the outgoing CEO, a January 2027 leadership transition, and a multi-phase ERP migration going live mid-2026. The valuation is fair, not asymmetric. We rate DG HOLD. The consistent HOLD across five prior reports reflects a business that is genuinely improving operationally but faces offsetting structural constraints that keep risk/reward balanced.

## Company Overview

Dollar General is the largest U.S. discount retailer by store count: 21,055 stores across 48 states and Mexico as of May 1, 2026. The format is small-box (~7,500-8,500 sq ft), everyday-low-price (typically ≤$10), concentrated in rural and small-town markets across the South, Southwest, Midwest, and East. The core customer is value-conscious, low- and fixed-income, described in the 10-K as "first affected by, last to recover from" economic downturns.

The merchandise mix is heavily weighted to consumables (82% of FY2025 sales: food, paper, cleaning, health and beauty, pet), which carry lower margins. Higher-margin non-consumables (seasonal 10%, home 5%, apparel 3%) are the profit-mix lever, and non-consumables have outpaced consumables in comps for five straight quarters. Growth initiatives include Project Renovate (full remodels), the newer Project Elevate (partial remodels, ~2,250 planned in 2026), a nascent Mexico expansion, the pOpshelf non-consumable concept (180 stores, still a drag after a $214M FY2024 impairment), DG Media Network (retail-media advertising), and expanding delivery through myDG and third parties.

The competitive advantages are physical: unmatched footprint and convenience in underserved rural markets where competitors are retreating, low-cost operating discipline, private-brand penetration, and sourcing scale. The structural gap the 10-K acknowledges is e-commerce: DG "currently does not offer traditional online shopping to a significant degree," partially insulated by its rural base but exposed as customers adopt delivery.

## Financial Analysis

The financial arc is a margin collapse-and-recovery story that remains incomplete.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $37.8B | $40.6B | $42.7B (+5.2%) | $10.79B (+3.4%) |
| Operating profit | $3.33B | $1.71B | $2.20B | $638.5M |
| Operating margin | 6.32% | 4.22% | 5.16% | 5.92% |
| Net income | $2.42B | $1.13B | $1.51B | $444.1M |
| Diluted EPS | $7.55 | $5.11 | $6.85 | $2.00 |
| Same-store sales | — | +1.4% | +3.0% | +2.0% |

The recovery is real but bounded. FY2025 gross margin gains were driven by lower shrink, higher markups, and lower damages, precisely the problems that wrecked FY2024. But net margin is only ~3.5%. The +5.2% FY2025 sales growth produced a 30.7% gross margin versus 30.3% in FY2023 at meaningfully higher operating margin; SG&A pressure (labor, occupancy, D&A) is the persistent gap. Damages "remain elevated" per the 10-K, meaning the shrink/damages recovery is not fully realized.

Cash generation is a genuine strength. FY2025 operating cash flow was $3.63B (+$638M YoY) and FCF was $2.39B, a 5.6% margin. Inventory per store fell ~7% and inventory turns improved to 4.5x, aiding conversion. Deleveraging is underway: long-term debt fell from $7.0B (FY2023) to $4.57B (FY2025), with $1.65B of senior notes redeemed and $1.7B of long-term obligations repaid in 2025. No commercial paper or revolver was drawn as of May 2026; the $2.375B revolver is undrawn.

Leverage remains elevated relative to the company's own comfort level. Reported D/E is 2.64x and the Altman Z-Score is 2.26 (grey zone), though both are distorted by ~$11.2B of operating-lease liabilities inherent to a leased-store retailer: total liabilities of $22.9B against $8.8B of equity. Credit ratings are Baa3/BBB, both Stable, but Moody's downgraded from Baa2 to Baa3 in 2025, and management remains focused on protecting investment grade. Financial health scores solidly but not exceptionally: strong cash flow and active deleveraging offset by a downgraded rating and lease-heavy balance sheet.

On capital allocation, the dividend held flat at $0.59/quarter (~$520M/yr). Share buybacks have been suspended since 2022 with $1.38B of authorization unused, a deliberate choice to protect the credit rating. Resumption would signal management views deleveraging as complete, and its continued absence signals ongoing balance-sheet caution. Capex is guided to $1.4-1.5B for 2026, tilting toward remodels over new stores.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is steady but modest, and decelerating in the back half by management's own guidance. Q1 FY2026 traffic rose +1.4% and ticket +0.5%. Management raised the full-year sales growth guide to 3.7-4.2% and comps to 2.2-2.7%, but the guidance implies decelerating comps in H2.

The near-term demand tailwind is trade-down: high fuel prices and a stretched low-income consumer drive value-channel traffic. Five Below's reported +33% Q1 growth confirms the value channel is broadly strong, and DG's traffic gains show the effect is real. DG's core customer being squeezed drives incremental trips to DG.

Against this, the estimate revision picture is flat. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $7.37, up only +1.5% over 90 days, and next-year at $7.99, up +0.5%. Revisions are stable, not accelerating. This matters because the strongest forward buy signals come from estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days combined with accelerating revenue; DG shows neither. The growth engines (Project Elevate remodels, Mexico, DG Media Network, delivery) are credible but incremental, and pOpshelf remains a drag rather than a contributor.

The two structural headwinds cap the growth path. WOTC expiration is a permanent tax-rate increase, and rising fuel costs compress both margins and customer trip frequency. Together these are why the profit recovery, while real, does not extrapolate to a clean re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$120, DG trades at ~17.4x trailing and forward earnings (EPS $6.85; forward consensus $7.37). Against the Consumer Staples Merchandise Retail peer set, the picture is mixed:

DG trades at a ~17% discount to the peer median P/E and roughly in line with Dollar Tree (18.5x) and Target (17.5x). The discount is defensible rather than a mispricing: the operating margin recovery is incomplete, earnings face two structural headwinds (WOTC, fuel), DG carries a live securities class action, and it is mid-CEO-transition with a concurrent ERP migration. The 1.9% dividend yield provides modest support and exceeds the peer median.

This is not a cyclical-trough valuation requiring forward normalization. Estimate revisions are flat, not the >30% velocity that would signal an under-appreciated turn, and revenue growth is mid-single-digit, not the >40% acceleration that would mandate normalized-earnings analysis. The recovery is well understood by the market: DG has re-rated from a ~$95 low to ~$120 (and a $158 peak in February) as the turnaround played out. Applying peer-consistent judgment, the value dimension is best treated with skepticism given the equity lesson that "cheap" screens are more often value traps than opportunities absent an independent catalyst, and here the turnaround catalyst is largely priced.

Analyst consensus targets bracket the stock: low $90, mean $131, median $126, high $175. The stock sits below the mean, consistent with a fairly-valued view rather than a screaming discount. The mean target implies ~9% upside, roughly a fair-value band rather than asymmetric. We score valuation modestly above neutral to reflect the peer discount and yield support, tempered by the structural headwinds and the absence of a fresh catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

DG's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving in the current environment. The value channel is winning share as trade-down accelerates, and DG's rural footprint is genuinely differentiated: proximity and convenience in markets where mass merchants and grocers are absent or retreating. Traffic gains evidence share capture, and the favorable non-consumables comp mix supports the profit-mix lever.

The structural vulnerability is e-commerce. The 10-K's admission that DG does not offer traditional online shopping "to a significant degree" is a durable gap, only partially insulated by the rural base. Delivery expansion (myDG plus third parties) is a response but nascent. The company also competes against Walmart, Dollar Tree/Family Dollar, Five Below, grocery, drug, and warehouse clubs, a crowded field where DG's EDLP promise constrains its ability to pass through labor, tariff, and fuel cost inflation.

On balance, the trajectory is favorable in the near term (trade-down tailwind, share gains, mix improvement) but the moat is defensive rather than expanding, and the e-commerce gap is a slow structural drag. This warrants a score modestly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

Several specific, filing-grounded risks are not fully discounted:

**Securities class action (elevated).** The consolidated Washtenaw County litigation (M.D. Tenn.) alleges DG and current/former officers misrepresented the impact of store labor, inventory, and pricing practices (putative class May 2020-Aug 2024). The court dismissed the second amended complaint (6/23/2025) but granted leave for a third amended complaint (3/24/2026, the same day the CEO succession was announced); defendants moved to dismiss again (4/21/2026, briefing completed 5/29/2026). DG states an adverse outcome "could have a material adverse effect." Parallel derivative actions naming Vasos (including alleged misuse of material non-public information) are stayed pending this ruling.

**WOTC expiration (structural).** Management explicitly warns of a "significant negative impact to the effective tax rate" absent reauthorization. Given DG's high-turnover, entry-level workforce, this is a recurring EPS drag.

**Fuel and consumer stress (intensifying).** DG incurred "significantly higher fuel costs" in Q1, expected to persist, a margin and traffic double-edge, since high fuel prices also cause the low-income customer to minimize trip distances. Management flags customers as constrained, minimizing shopping-trip distances, with food-at-home inflation re-accelerating.

**SNAP/government-assistance cuts.** The 10-K flags 2025 SNAP work-requirement changes and, from January 2026, certain states excluding historically covered product categories, direct demand risk for the low-income core.

**ERP migration (new).** A multi-phase ERP replacement (finance, HR/payroll, procurement, merchandise payables) rolls out across 2026-2027, with the financial-reporting platform going live Q2 FY2026, an internal-controls and execution risk stacked on the CEO transition.

**Shrink/damages reversal.** The turnaround thesis rests on continued shrink and damage reduction; damages remain elevated, and any reversal hits gross margin directly.

The offset is that DG is defensive: a non-discretionary consumables mix, and a genuine trade-down beneficiary. But the concentration of a live litigation overhang, a structural tax headwind, execution risk under new leadership, and consumer-side fuel pressure keeps the idiosyncratic risk profile balanced rather than benign.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 42.4% is rich versus 35.9% one-year historical vol (6.5pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.4% near vs. 37.4% long-term), signaling the market expects an imminent event to move the stock. Put/call skew is elevated at 14.4% with a put/call OI ratio of 1.03, indicating meaningful downside hedging demand, a divergence worth flagging for anyone considering a long. IV has fallen from 53.7% to 42.4% since the June 3 snapshot as the stock rose +13.1%, so the acute post-earnings stress has partly resolved. Short interest is low at 4.2% of float (2.2 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The net read: the options market is not confirming a bullish thesis, and the elevated put skew tempers conviction on any near-term upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($150-175).** The margin recovery continues toward the FY2023 6.3% operating-margin peak; shrink and damages keep improving; trade-down traffic accelerates as consumer stress deepens; Project Elevate remodels and DG Media Network add margin mix; deleveraging completes and buybacks resume, signaling balance-sheet health and adding an EPS lever. Four straight beats and a raised guide support the case that consensus underestimates the trajectory. At the high analyst target of $175, this implies ~46% upside on a full margin normalization plus multiple re-rating toward the peer median.

**Bear Case ($90-100).** The WOTC tax-rate step-up and rising fuel costs cap earnings and drive the H2 deceleration the guidance implies; the shrink/damages recovery is largely spent, not extendable; the securities class action survives the motion to dismiss and moves toward a material settlement; the ERP migration disrupts controls; consumer stress eventually cuts even trade-down traffic as SNAP restrictions and fuel bite. At the low analyst target of $90, this implies ~25% downside on stalled earnings and multiple compression.

**Base Case (~$126-131).** The turnaround holds but does not accelerate; margins grind toward but do not reach the FY2023 peak; earnings compound in the mid-single digits net of the WOTC/fuel drag; the stock tracks flat-to-modestly-higher toward the analyst median/mean of $126-131, roughly +5-9% from ~$120, supported by the 1.9% yield. This is a fairly-valued defensive holding, not a re-rating candidate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain). The turnaround is real but the earnings path is capped by structural headwinds, valuation is fair rather than asymmetric, and material overhangs (litigation, transition, ERP) offset the operational improvement. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the bar for a change should be higher, and neither a fresh catalyst nor fresh deterioration is present to justify one.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating margin sustainably recovering above 6.0% (toward the FY2023 peak) across two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the recovery extends beyond shrink/damages normalization.
- Resumption of share buybacks, signaling management views deleveraging as complete and adding an EPS lever.
- Favorable resolution or dismissal of the Washtenaw County securities litigation, removing the material-adverse-effect overhang.
- Estimate revisions turning meaningfully positive (>5% CY over 90 days) alongside re-accelerating comps, versus the current flat trend.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store sales turning negative or comps decelerating below +1% as the consumer cliff bites the low-income core.
- Gross margin reversing (shrink/damages re-inflating), invalidating the turnaround.
- An adverse litigation development implying a material settlement.
- Credit-rating pressure returning (further downgrade toward high-yield), which would confirm balance-sheet strain.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (early September 2026) is the natural checkpoint: it will test the H2-deceleration hypothesis, reveal the full WOTC tax-rate impact, and show whether fuel costs are compressing traffic. The securities-litigation ruling on the renewed motion to dismiss is a separate, near-term catalyst to monitor.

## Conclusion

Dollar General has done the hard work of fixing self-inflicted operational damage; shrink, damages, and inventory discipline restored gross margin and drove four consecutive earnings beats and a raised FY2026 guide. The business is defensive, cash-generative, and a genuine trade-down beneficiary in the current consumer environment. That is the case for owning it.

The case against paying up is that the recovery has stalled over a point of operating margin below the FY2023 peak, the drivers were largely one-time normalization rather than durable expansion, and two structural headwinds (WOTC expiration and rising fuel costs) are converging to cap earnings and drive the H2 deceleration management's own guidance implies. Layered on are a live securities class action naming the outgoing CEO, a January 2027 leadership handoff, and a concurrent ERP migration. At ~17.4x forward earnings, a modest discount to the 20.9x peer median, the stock is priced fairly for an incomplete recovery with real overhangs. Flat estimate revisions confirm the market is not systematically underestimating the story. We rate DG HOLD, with a base-case fair value of ~$126-131, and would look to upgrade only on sustained margin expansion above 6%, a buyback resumption, or litigation resolution.
