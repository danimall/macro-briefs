**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Autoliv is the clear global leader in automotive passive safety systems (airbags, seatbelts, steering wheels, and inflators) with roughly 44% worldwide market share and a track record of growing content-per-vehicle faster than global light-vehicle production. The Q2 2026 print (reported July 17) exposed the central tension in the current story: GAAP operating margin fell to 6.8% from 9.1% on a ~$90M Türkiye restructuring charge, but adjusted operating income grew 7.3% and adjusted operating margin expanded to 9.6% from 9.3%. Operating cash flow hit a record $434M in Q2, leverage improved to 1.2x, and management repurchased $200M of stock in the quarter while reiterating full-year guidance of 10.5–11% adjusted operating margin. The underlying business is compounding while the headline is depressed by a self-funded footprint optimization that delivers ~$40M/year of savings from 2027.

The counterweight is a combination of near-term estimate pressure and idiosyncratic tail risk. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 18.6% over 30 days (to $2.18 from $2.67), and full-year 2026 estimates have drifted down 3.4%. Management has explicitly told investors that hitting the full-year adjusted margin requires a "significant improvement in Q4" driven by customer-compensation timing, with H1 running at 9.3%. That back-half dependency is the single largest execution variable for 2026. Layered on top are three large, un-accrued litigation contingencies: the ARC/Delphi inflator matter (NHTSA action covering ~52M inflators, no estimable range), the Stellantis side-curtain recall ($0–$209M, and possibly expanding to a second customer), and a BMW antitrust civil claim (€0–€95M).

At ~$119, ALV trades at roughly 12x current-year consensus EPS ($10.13) and ~10x next year ($11.83), a discount to the automotive-parts peer median of ~20.7x P/E, with a 3.0% dividend yield and a ~4%/year share-count reduction. The valuation is supportive rather than compelling: the discount is partly justified by cyclical LVP exposure and the open-ended litigation, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating before the Q4 margin step-up is confirmed. We rate the stock HOLD (growth_hold), reflecting a durable franchise and reasonable downside support but a lack of near-term asymmetry.

## Company Overview

Autoliv is a Delaware-incorporated holding company headquartered in Stockholm, operating through Autoliv AB and Autoliv ASP across 62 production facilities in 23 countries. It reports as a single operating segment. The product portfolio is passive safety: frontal, side, curtain, knee, and front-center airbags; seatbelts with pretensioners and load limiters; steering wheels; and inflators. FY2025 revenue was $10.8B, split roughly 68% airbags/steering wheels and 32% seatbelts.

Market position is dominant. Autoliv holds ~44% global share in passive safety (up from 27% in 1997), ~45% in seatbelts, and ~44% combined in airbags/steering wheels. Since 1997, its sales have compounded at ~5% versus a market growing ~2.8% and light-vehicle production ~1.9%, a structural outperformance driven by rising content per vehicle. CPV runs ~$350 in high-income markets versus ~$210 in medium/low-income markets, giving a multi-year runway as safety regulation (Euro NCAP, China NCAP, US NCAP) tightens and emerging markets add content.

The competitive set is consolidated: ZF AG and Joyson Safety Systems (Takata plus Key Safety) are the primary global rivals, alongside regional keiretsu suppliers (Tokai Rika and Toyoda Gosei for Toyota, Mobis for Hyundai/Kia) and BYD's vertically integrated FinDreams, to which Autoliv supplies inflators. Customer concentration is high: top-5 customers are ~44% of sales, top-10 ~70%. Contracts are platform-lifetime (5–7 years) but non-binding on volume, price, and exclusivity. By region, Asian OEMs are ~48% of sales (Japanese roughly two-thirds of that), European ~30%, US ~20%, and Chinese OEMs ~8%.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory.** H1 2026 net sales were $5,556M, up 5.0% reported (+0.9% organic, +4.1% FX). Q2 net sales were $2,803M, up 3.3% reported and +1.0% organic, outperforming global LVP (–0.3%) by ~1.3 percentage points. Underlying profitability is expanding while the headline is compressed by the one-time Türkiye restructuring.

**Guidance.** FY2026 guidance was reiterated: ~0% organic growth, 10.5–11% adjusted operating margin, ~$1.2B operating cash flow, capex under 5% of sales. The framework assumes LVP –2.5% and a +2.5% FX tailwind. Management guided Q3 margin roughly in line with H1 (~9.3%), with the required Q4 improvement driven by customer-compensation timing. The math is demanding: H1 at 9.3% adjusted implies the Q4 step-up must materialize to reach the range. This is the primary near-term execution risk.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** H1 FCF was $180M (from $147M) and Q2 cash conversion 338%. Net leverage improved to 1.2x against a ≤1.5x target; net debt is $1,695M. The balance sheet carries $8.5B assets, $2.5B equity, $2.04B total debt, and $377M cash, with PP&E of $2.34B reflecting the capital-intensive nature of Tier-1 supply. The grey-zone Altman Z reading (~1.87) is a function of high leverage and thin equity typical of the sector, not distress; leverage is comfortably within policy and declining. One item to watch is the supplier-finance program ($324M at Q2, down from $365M), embedded in accounts payable, which flatters reported working capital.

**Capital allocation.** Autoliv is running an aggressive, shareholder-friendly program: a $2.5B buyback authorization (July 2025–Dec 2029), $200M/1.65M shares repurchased in Q2 alone at an average $121.43, and ~$450M repurchased LTM. Diluted share count fell from 77.3M to 74.2M year-over-year (~4%). The quarterly dividend is $0.87, and the FY2026 buyback target is $300–500M with ~$2.05B still authorized. Against ~$1.2B guided operating cash flow, the ~$450M LTM capital return signals management's confidence in FCF durability, but it does consume the cushion that could otherwise absorb a large recall loss.

**Earnings track record.** The four-quarter record is three beats and one narrow miss: +11.4% (Sep '25), +8.7% (Dec '25), +6.6% (Mar '26), and –0.7% (Jun '26). The most recent quarter was essentially in line on adjusted EPS ($2.43 versus $2.45 consensus), with GAAP EPS of $1.35 down 38% purely on the restructuring charge. The pattern supports moderate confidence in forward estimates, though the recent narrow miss and falling near-term revisions temper it.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is modest by design in 2026, with management guiding to ~0% organic against a –2.5% LVP backdrop, meaning ~2.5 points of structural outperformance from content gains and share. The durable long-term driver is CPV expansion: passive-safety market value is projected to grow from ~$24B (2025) to ~$26B within three years even as LVP growth concentrates in lower-CPV emerging markets, because regulation and consumer expectations push more airbags, advanced seatbelts, and richer steering wheels into each vehicle.

The standout growth vector is China and India. Q2 sales to Chinese OEMs grew 44%, with domestic Chinese OEMs now 55% of Autoliv's China sales (up from ~40% a year ago). India grew 36% organic. Autoliv signed strategic cooperation framework agreements with Great Wall Motor (July 6) and XPENG (July 7), supply/partnership relationships via Autoliv Shanghai that position it with the winners in the domestic-OEM share shift. The offsetting dynamic is that sales to global OEMs in China fell ~24% in Q2, so the China story is one of continually re-winning content as share migrates from Western/Japanese brands to domestic ones. Success here is effectively existential for maintaining overall share.

Near-term earnings momentum is negative, the material near-term concern for the growth score. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 18.6% over 30 days (to $2.18 from $2.67) and current-year estimates down 3.4% (to $10.13). Next-quarter estimates are up modestly (+6.6% over 30 days), consistent with the back-half margin ramp, and next-year revisions are roughly flat (–0.9%). The revision profile does not present the +30% signal that would flag a high-conviction growth accelerant; it shows a company grinding out low-single-digit outperformance against a soft production cycle with a timing-dependent Q4.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$119, ALV trades at ~12x current-year consensus EPS ($10.13), ~10x next year ($11.83), and a trailing P/E of ~14x. The peer group in automotive parts shows wide dispersion: Lear 14.5x, Gentex 14.4x, Aptiv 40.3x, Dorman 20.7x, Dana negative, median ~20.7x. Autoliv trades at a meaningful discount to that median, closer to the value-oriented cluster (Lear, Gentex).

The discount is partly warranted and partly an opportunity. Warranted: direct exposure to a declining LVP cycle, high customer concentration, and the three large un-accrued litigation contingencies that the market rationally discounts. The opportunity: Autoliv is the highest-share, most cash-generative name in the group, running a ~4%/year buyback and a 3.0% yield, with adjusted margins expanding through the restructuring. On normalized 10.5–11% adjusted margins and the guided ~$1.2B operating cash flow, the equity looks reasonably valued rather than cheap.

The dividend yield of 3.0% (above the 1.4% peer median) provides absolute yield support, and the Q2 buyback at $121.43 indicates management views current levels as attractive. Analyst targets span $118 (low) to $148 (high), mean ~$134.70, median $134, implying ~13% upside to the mean from current levels. Given the stable analyst distribution (5 strong buy, 7 buy, 6 hold) and the moderate valuation, the mean target functions as a plausible base case rather than a stretch.

We are deliberately restrained on the valuation score. A low multiple in a cyclical consumer-discretionary supplier with open-ended recall liability can persist as a value proposition rather than converge to peer multiples. The re-rating case depends on the Q4 margin delivery and clarity on the ARC contingency, neither of which is dated. We therefore treat the valuation as supportive of downside protection but not as an independent buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Autoliv's ~44% share confers scale advantages in a consolidated top-three market (Autoliv, ZF, Joyson, plus regional keiretsu suppliers). The moat rests on decades of crash-test data, tooling scale, platform-lifetime relationships with OEMs, and technology leadership in pretensioners and active seatbelts. The tailwind from rising CPV even as LVP growth concentrates in emerging markets is real and broad-based.

The pivotal competitive question is China. As domestic Chinese OEMs (Chery, Nio, Geely, BYD, Great Wall, XPENG) take share from global OEMs, Autoliv is re-anchoring itself with the winners via +44% domestic-OEM growth and the new GWM/XPENG frameworks, while its legacy global-OEM China business shrinks ~24%. This is a competitive position that is being actively defended and, so far, held. Management also frames autonomous vehicles and roomier interiors as demand drivers for more advanced passive safety, a hedge against the ADAS/active-safety narrative that hurt the spun-off Veoneer business.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving on content and China domestic wins, offset by the perpetual re-win requirement of non-binding contracts and share migration. On balance the competitive position is a genuine strength that the market is not mispricing; it is a well-understood leadership franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**Litigation contingencies (the defining idiosyncratic risk).** Three large, largely un-accrued matters warrant close attention:
1. *ARC/Delphi inflator recall.* NHTSA's 2023 initial decision covers ~52M inflators, some supplied by Autoliv post-2009 Delphi acquisition. No final NHTSA decision yet; a consolidated MDL class action is pending (N.D. Georgia). Loss is "reasonably possible," no accrual, and no range can be estimated, leaving it open-ended.
2. *Stellantis side-curtain recall.* ~250K vehicles (2025), expanded +178K in March 2026; Stellantis sued an Autoliv subsidiary April 16, 2026. Estimated potential loss $0–$209M (substantially insured, no accrual). The 10-Q notes another customer has contacted the company about similar nonconformance, a live and escalating thread.
3. *BMW antitrust civil claim (Germany).* Stemming from the 2019 EC cartel settlement; claim ~€58M plus interest (~€95M total). Loss "reasonably possible," range €0–€95M, no accrual.

Much of the recall exposure is insured (replacement parts are not), but the ARC matter is uncapped and tied to the 52M-inflator action. The aggressive capital-return program reduces the balance-sheet cushion that could absorb a large loss.

**Cyclical and structural.** Sales track global LVP, guided down ~2.5% in 2026. Top-10 concentration (~70%) amplifies single-OEM resourcing risk. The China global-OEM erosion (~24% in Q2) requires continuous re-winning of content. A ~$110M gross raw-material headwind is expected for FY2026 (mostly mitigated), with ~20bps of tariff margin dilution and elevated call-off volatility pressuring efficiency.

**Governance.** C-suite churn is elevated: a new CFO (Monika Grama signed the 10-Q), the departure of Americas President Kevin Fox (announced June 26 for personal reasons), and prior director/officer changes. Former Chairman Jan Carlson sold ~$2.5M of stock in June 2026, and there is no open-market insider buying. Carlson's sale as a departing/former chairman is lower-signal, but the combination of turnover plus no buying is a neutral-to-mildly-negative read.

**Execution.** The back-half-loaded 2026 margin ramp is the near-term binary. H1 adjusted margin at 9.3% requires a material Q4 step-up to reach the guide; Q3 (roughly H1 level per management) will offer little relief and Q4 becomes the proof point.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute stress. Near-term ATM IV is 30.9% versus 27.6% historical volatility, modestly rich (3.2pp spread), in the 25th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (0.4% near-term), the P/C OI ratio is a benign 0.70, and the term structure is in contango (near 30.9%, six-month 33.8%), a normal shape. The implied one-month move is ±6.8%. Since mid-June, ATM IV rose 2.5pp as the price fell 7.2%, but the P/C OI ratio held steady, consistent with orderly repricing around the Q2 print rather than a directional bearish bet. The six-month –3.4% put skew shows some longer-dated hedging demand, consistent with the litigation tail, but nothing that contradicts a HOLD stance. Short interest at 6.1% of float (5.4 days to cover) is uncrowded.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$148).** The Türkiye restructuring proves margin-accretive on schedule, the Q4 step-up materializes and full-year adjusted margin lands at 11%, and the ~$40M/year savings compound from 2027. China domestic-OEM growth (+44%) and India (+36%) sustain 2–3 points of structural outperformance over LVP. The ARC and Stellantis matters resolve within insured bounds or are dismissed, removing the overhang. The ~4%/year buyback plus dividend compounds per-share value, and the market re-rates the multiple toward the peer median as cyclical concerns ease. At ~13x normalized ~$11.50 EPS, the stock reaches the high-target zone.

**Bear case (~$95–100, roughly –16 to –20% from current).** LVP deteriorates worse than the –2.5% guide, organic growth turns negative, and the Q4 margin ramp disappoints on customer-compensation timing, forcing a full-year miss below the 10.5% floor. The ARC inflator matter escalates to a final NHTSA recall decision with uninsured replacement-cost exposure, and the "another customer" thread on the Stellantis nonconformance materializes into a second recall. C-suite turnover proves disruptive during the restructuring. The stock retests its 52-week low (~$99) as the litigation tail is repriced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The franchise is durable and the valuation supports downside, but there is no dated near-term catalyst to force a re-rating, and the falling current-quarter estimates plus open-ended litigation cap the upside conviction.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October) is the key checkpoint; it confirms whether the required Q4 improvement is on track and whether the Stellantis "another customer" thread has expanded. Full-year margin delivery reads out at Q4/FY2026 (late January/February 2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3 confirms the back-half margin ramp with FY adjusted margin tracking to the guide, and current-year EPS revisions turn positive.
- The ARC inflator matter resolves within insured/estimable bounds, or the NHTSA action is narrowed/closed, removing the open-ended tail.
- A pullback toward the high-$90s to low-$100s (52-week low ~$99) that pushes forward P/E below ~9x on unchanged fundamentals, restoring clear asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q4 margin step-up fails and FY2026 adjusted margin lands below 10%, signaling structural rather than timing margin pressure.
- The ARC matter escalates to a final NHTSA recall with a material, uninsured cost estimate, or the Stellantis "another customer" contingency crystallizes into a second recall.
- Organic growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters, indicating loss of content/share outperformance versus LVP.

## Conclusion

Autoliv is a high-quality, cash-generative market leader whose Q2 GAAP miss masks an expanding adjusted margin and a self-funded, margin-accretive restructuring. The China domestic-OEM pivot (+44%) and India (+36%) are executing well, capital return is aggressive and disciplined, and the ~12x forward multiple with a 3.0% yield offers reasonable downside support. The trajectory of scores has been improving (management and financial health both stepped up over the past quarter), and the recent price decline reflects GAAP optics and a soft production cycle more than fundamental deterioration.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the absence of near-term asymmetry: current-quarter estimates have been cut 18.6% in 30 days, the full-year margin depends on an unconfirmed Q4 step-up, and three large un-accrued litigation contingencies, led by the open-ended ARC inflator matter, create a tail the market rationally discounts and that no dated catalyst will resolve soon. In this sector, cheapness relative to peers has historically been a persistent value characteristic rather than a convergence trade, and we treat it accordingly. We would move to BUY on confirmation of the Q4 margin ramp, resolution or bounding of the ARC exposure, or a pullback into the low-$100s. We would move to AVOID on a failed margin ramp or a material escalation of the recall liabilities.
