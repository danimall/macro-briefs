## Executive Summary

ExlService Holdings has declined 37% over the past year from $46 to $29, while delivering accelerating revenue growth (13.6% in FY2025), expanding margins (operating margin 15%, up 70bps), and beating earnings estimates in all four of the last quarters. The stock now trades at approximately 13x forward P/E on $2.24 FY2026 consensus EPS — a meaningful discount to its peer group median of 16.3x.

The selloff appears driven by two macro factors — the Indian rupee hitting record weakness near 97/USD (EXL has 43,200 employees in India) and general de-risking from India-exposed IT services names — rather than fundamental deterioration. Q1 2026 revenue of $570.4M beat consensus by 2.2%, adjusted EPS of $0.58 beat by approximately 9.4%, and management raised FY2026 guidance to $2.30-2.33B revenue and $2.18-2.23 adjusted EPS. Healthcare, the fastest-growing segment at approximately 24% growth, now represents 25.5% of revenue and carries the highest gross margins (43.6%).

The core investment thesis is that EXL is being priced as a traditional BPO company facing AI disruption, when it is the disruptor — embedding proprietary AI (including a domain-specific insurance LLM) into its operations to capture higher-value revenue while reducing labor intensity. At 13x forward earnings with 27.5% ROE, 14%+ FCF margins, and manageable leverage, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside.

## Company Overview

ExlService Holdings operates as a global data and AI company serving large enterprises in regulated, data-intensive industries. The company employs approximately 67,000 people across six continents, with major workforce concentrations in India (43,200), the Philippines (13,400), South Africa (4,100), and the US (2,900).

In early 2025, EXL restructured from two segments into four Industry Market Units (IMUs):
- **Insurance** (34% of revenue) — claims management, underwriting, subrogation via proprietary platforms
- **Healthcare & Life Sciences** (25.5%) — payment integrity, care management, revenue cycle
- **Banking, Capital Markets & Diversified Industries** (23.1%) — KYC/AML, fraud, analytics
- **International Growth Markets** (17.4%) — all verticals outside North America

Revenue is further categorized as "Data and AI-led" solutions versus "Digital Operations" services, with the strategic trajectory moving toward the former. The company serves approximately 590 clients, with 82.7% of revenue from North America.

## Growth Analysis

Three distinct growth vectors support the forward outlook:

**1. Healthcare AI payment integrity (~24% segment growth):** This segment grew 23.7% in FY2025, with 22.4% coming from existing client expansion and the remainder from new client wins including the ITI Data acquisition. EXL's proprietary data and algorithms identify claims overpayments and billing errors — a use case where AI delivers immediate, measurable ROI for healthcare payers. This segment carries 43.6% gross margins, the highest in the portfolio. The structural tailwind is increasing claims complexity, regulatory burden, and the shift to value-based care.

**2. Enterprise AI transformation across all verticals:** The segment restructuring signals that AI/analytics is now embedded as horizontal capability rather than a standalone upsell. Management cited strong momentum and execution against its data and AI strategy in Q1 2026. The company has developed a domain-specific insurance LLM leveraging proprietary data accumulated through decades of claims processing — a competitive asset that would be costly and time-consuming for competitors to replicate.

**3. International expansion:** With 82.7% of revenue from North America, geographic diversification represents significant runway. The hire of Bhupender Singh (former President and Co-CEO of Teleperformance SE) to lead International Growth Markets is a disproportionate leadership investment that signals management expects this segment to scale substantially.

Estimate revisions confirm the trajectory: current-year EPS estimates have risen 2.0% in the past 90 days and 2.5% in the past 30 days. The revision trend is positive but moderate — not yet at the velocity that indicates the market has fully recognized the growth acceleration.

The specific reason the market appears to be mispricing this stock: the selloff from $46 to $29 occurred while fundamentals improved (Q1 beat, guidance raised, margins expanding). The decline correlates with (a) the Indian rupee weakening to record lows near 97/USD, raising wage cost concerns, and (b) broad de-risking from India-exposed IT services names amid EM currency stress. However, EXL's revenue is approximately 83% North America-sourced while costs are heavily INR/PHP — rupee weakness is actually a tailwind to near-term margins (costs decline in USD terms), not a headwind. The market appears to be applying a generic "India risk" discount without distinguishing between companies that earn in rupees (genuinely impaired) versus those that spend in rupees and earn in dollars (beneficiaries of depreciation).

## Competitive Landscape

EXL competes across three dimensions:

**Against large IT services firms (Cognizant, Genpact, Infosys, TCS):** EXL differentiates through deeper domain specialization. Rather than operating as a generalist IT services provider, EXL focuses on specific verticals (insurance, healthcare) where its proprietary data assets and domain-specific AI create switching costs. The insurance LLM, developed using proprietary data from extensive claims processing experience, represents a meaningful competitive advantage that would be costly and time-consuming for a generalist to develop.

**Against pure-play AI vendors:** EXL's advantage is that it operates the business processes, giving it continuous access to real-world data that improves its AI models. A standalone AI vendor can sell software but cannot provide the integrated operations + analytics package that enterprise clients increasingly demand.

**Against clients' Global Capability Centers (GCCs):** This is the most differentiated risk. Large enterprises are building their own offshore operations with internal AI. EXL's counter: implementing domain-specific AI at scale requires specialized capabilities, proprietary training data, and experience managing the human-AI interface that most GCCs cannot replicate internally.

The trajectory is positive: the company won 16 new clients in Q1 2026 alone, existing client revenue expansion accounted for 10.6% growth in FY2025, and the Healthcare segment's 23.7% growth rate suggests share gains in a large addressable market.

## Risk Assessment

**AI cannibalization of legacy digital operations (HIGH, company-specific):** The 10-K acknowledges that AI "has reduced and replaced some of our historical services." EXL must outrun the decline in legacy FTE-based work with growth in AI-led revenue. If the transition stalls — if AI-led revenue growth doesn't offset digital operations erosion — margins could compress. The extended sales cycle for AI services (longer proof-of-concept, governance reviews) creates execution risk.

**Indian rupee volatility (MODERATE, macro-driven):** With 43,200 employees in India, a strengthening rupee would compress margins. The current INR level near 97/USD is favorable for EXL. The risk is a reversal — if EM stress resolves and INR strengthens, cost inflation resumes. EXL hedges substantially but cannot eliminate exposure. The stock decline may also reflect broader sector de-risking rather than a direct fundamental impact from FX.

**Client concentration (MODERATE):** Top 3 clients = 17.8%, top 10 = 34%. Loss of a major healthcare payer client could create a step-function decline in the fastest-growing segment.

**Tariff and anti-outsourcing policy risk (MODERATE):** The current US administration could impose tariffs or service taxes on offshore delivery. EXL's 10-K explicitly identifies this as a risk factor. Impact would be sector-wide but could force pricing adjustments.

**Short interest (MODERATE):** Short interest is estimated at approximately 8.8% of float, suggesting meaningful skepticism about either the AI transition thesis or macro exposure. However, any positive catalyst could create covering pressure.

**Indian Labor Codes (LOW-MODERATE):** New unified Labor Codes effective November 2025 increase benefit obligations. Full cost impact uncertain as rules are still being issued.

## Conclusion

EXLS presents an asymmetric risk/reward at $29.02. The stock is trading at 13x forward earnings — a 20% discount to peers — following a selloff driven by macro/currency factors rather than fundamental deterioration. The company beats estimates consistently (4-for-4 quarters), is growing revenue at 10-12% with expanding margins, generates strong free cash flow (6.7% yield), operates with manageable leverage, and has a specific high-growth catalyst in healthcare AI.

The primary risk is that AI cannibalization of legacy services outpaces new AI-led revenue growth, but the evidence to date shows the opposite: margins are expanding, not compressing. The macro risk (INR weakness) is paradoxically favorable to near-term margins. At a PEG of ~1.1x with 27.5% ROE and improving cash conversion, the stock offers value-with-growth characteristics that are rare in the current market.

The rating is BUY with a 12-month base case target of $38, representing 31% upside.
