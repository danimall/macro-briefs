**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

GE Aerospace is the cleanest large-cap expression of the commercial-engine aftermarket annuity. After shedding GE HealthCare (2023) and GE Vernova (2024), the legal continuation of General Electric now operates as a focused propulsion business with an installed base of roughly 50,000 commercial and 30,000 military engines, ~70% of revenue from high-margin recurring services, and a services backlog (remaining performance obligation) that grew to $211.3 billion in Q1 2026, up $20.7 billion in a single quarter. The Q1 print was exceptional: orders +87%, revenue +25% (adjusted +29%), LEAP deliveries +63%, internal shop-visit revenue +35% (versus +11% a year earlier), adjusted EPS +25% to $1.86, and FCF +14% to $1.66 billion. Credit agencies upgraded the company in early 2026 (Moody's to A2 February 2; S&P to positive outlook April 16). The franchise quality is not in dispute.

The dispute is price. At ~$332 the stock trades at roughly 44x consensus current-year EPS of $7.56 and ~46x the FY26 guided midpoint of $7.25, a premium to the aerospace/defense peer median P/E of 34.0. That multiple discounts years of uninterrupted aftermarket compounding. Management has already introduced a concrete crack in the volume leg of that thesis: in May 2026 it held FY26 EPS guidance while trimming its 2026 departures (flight-volume) outlook from mid-single-digit to flat-to-low-single-digit growth, with Q1 departures running +1.7% and the Middle East declining in March. The aftermarket annuity ultimately depends on flight hours, and the bet embedded in held EPS is that pricing and share-of-wallet gains offset softer volume.

We rate GE a HOLD. The recommendation reflects asymmetry, not skepticism about the business. Our base case ($350–355) sits about 5–7% above the current price; our bull case ($405) is +22%; our realistic bear case ($270) is roughly -19%. A reward:risk skewed close to 1:1 on a stock priced for perfection, with no dated near-term re-rating catalyst and a freshly disclosed volume-assumption cut, does not clear the bar for new capital here.

## Company Overview

GE Aerospace is the world's largest commercial aircraft engine franchise. Its defining economic feature is razor-and-blade: engines are sold at low or negative initial margin to seed decades of high-margin parts and shop-visit revenue under sole-source long-term service agreements (LTSAs), which underpin a services RPO with a 15+ year recognition tail.

Effective January 15, 2026, the company reorganized into two reportable segments. **Commercial Engines & Services (CES)**, ~73% of revenue, now owns the full commercial engine lifecycle; its flagship is the LEAP, produced through CFM International, a 50/50 joint venture with France's Safran, which powers the Boeing 737 MAX exclusively and competes with Pratt & Whitney on the A320neo. CES also includes GEnx (787) and GE9X (777X). Q1'26 CES margin was 26.4%. **Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT)**, ~23% of revenue, comprises military engines (F110, F404, T408) and additive/propulsion technology, at an 11.8% Q1 margin and tied to U.S. and allied defense budgets.

The moat is the installed base plus the contractual lock of LTSAs, which create extraordinarily high switching costs and multi-decade revenue visibility. The CFM/Safran structure shares R&D and risk but introduces partner dependency, and the airframer concentration (Boeing, Airbus) ties GE's near-term volumes to two customers' production rates.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (10-K, filed January 29, 2026):** Revenue $45.9 billion (+18.5% YoY), total segment profit $10.2 billion (~22% margin), adjusted EPS $6.37 (+38%), total RPO $190.6 billion (services $163 billion), LEAP deliveries 1,802 (up from 1,407). Total borrowings $20.5 billion with interest charges falling to $843 million from $986 million.

**Q1 2026 (10-Q, filed April 21, 2026):** GAAP continuing EPS was flat at $1.83, held back by a $309 million unrealized mark-to-market loss on the BETA Technologies eVTOL equity stake. Total commercial engines reached 640 (+50%). CES margin of 26.4% was down ~230 bps YoY, which management attributes to mix (more new-engine installs sold at low margin to seed aftermarket) and growth investment rather than deterioration; the $1.0 billion jump in sales discounts and allowances reflects this seeding. Operating cash flow was $1.87 billion.

A screening caution: GE's Altman Z-Score reads near-distress (~0.68). This is a false positive. The formula misfires because the balance sheet still carries a ~$35.7 billion run-off insurance liability book (backed by ~$38 billion of investments) and large deferred-service liabilities that distort working-capital and equity-to-assets ratios. The operating business is highly profitable and cash-generative, and the credit upgrades confirm it. Any model relying on the Z-Score in isolation would be wrong here.

**Capital allocation** is aggressive and shareholder-friendly. GE completed its $15 billion buyback (only $487 million remaining at quarter-end), the Board authorized a fresh $20 billion repurchase in December 2025, Q1'26 alone saw $2.2 billion (7.2 million shares) repurchased, and the dividend was raised ~31% to $0.47/quarter. The OBBBA tax benefit cut the Q1 effective rate to 11.5%. One reservation: repurchasing stock at ~44x forward earnings is a defensible use of excess cash but not obviously value-accretive at this multiple.

Financial Health score: 7.0. Strong cash generation, falling interest cost, fortress order book, and a positive credit trajectory, tempered by a sizable legacy tail-liability book that periodically drags cash and earnings.

## Growth Analysis

The forward indicators are strong and broad. Shop-visit revenue growth tripled to +35%, and the company announced a deep slate of commercial wins through Q1 (United 300+ GEnx, American LEAP-1A, Delta GEnx, Ryanair MoU to service ~2,000 CFM56/LEAP engines). The maturing LEAP fleet is entering its first heavy shop-visit wave, the most profitable phase of the engine lifecycle, and Pratt & Whitney's GTF powder-metal recall is pushing airlines toward LEAP and toward extending the life of older GE-powered aircraft — a direct aftermarket tailwind.

The qualifier is the volume assumption. Estimate revisions are stable rather than inflecting: current-year EPS up +1.7% over 90 days, next year +1.5%, current quarter +2.5%, but next quarter -3.4%. This is not the revision velocity that signals an underestimated earnings inflection; it is a high-quality compounder with consensus already in line. The trimmed departures outlook is more material, and elevated Middle East/Hormuz disruption risk raises the probability the volume leg softens further. Held EPS depends on aftermarket pricing and share gains offsetting volume — plausible given the GTF dynamic, but the cushion is thinner than the headline order growth implies.

Growth Prospects score: 7.3. Durable, multi-source, and confirmed in primary filings, marked down for the cut volume assumption and merely stable (not accelerating) estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$332 GE trades at ~41x trailing EPS of $8.05, ~44x consensus current-year $7.56, ~46x the FY26 guided midpoint of $7.25, and ~38x next-year $8.69. Against an aerospace/defense peer median P/E of 34.0 (RTX 34.0, BA 86.3, LMT 25.4, HWM 58.5, GD 21.8), GE sits at a clear premium, justified in part by superior aftermarket economics and growth but unsupported by a margin of safety.

On a growth-adjusted basis the picture is no cheaper. With consensus EPS growth around 15% (current year $7.56 to next year $8.69), the forward PEG is ~2.6 — expensive even for a high-quality franchise. The world model framing that ~44x current-year earnings "prices a flawless LEAP ramp" is the right characterization: the multiple leaves no room for the departures-driven volume disappointment that management has already partially flagged.

I deliberately do not penalize GE for prior appreciation; the run from ~$245 a year ago to $332 was earned by fundamental improvement. The valuation concern is forward-looking, and the asymmetry is unfavorable. A reversion to the peer median 34x on the next-year $8.69 estimate implies ~$295 (roughly in line with today); reversion to 34x on the FY26 guide midpoint of $7.25 implies ~$247, about -26%. A modest single-notch derating to the high-30s on one volume-driven guide cut lands the stock around $280–290, -13% to -16%. Against a Street high of $405 (+22%), the bull and bear cases are nearly symmetric, with the bear arguably more probable given the freshly cut traffic outlook.

This is a premium-multiple stock where analyst targets are not independent confirmation of upside; the mean target ($351) and median ($353) sit only modestly above the price and reflect the same aftermarket extrapolation embedded in the share price. The guardrail applies: at a 44x forward multiple, beats are expected (limited re-rating) while a miss is punished disproportionately.

Valuation score: 4.5. Quality and growth justify a premium, but the current price offers no asymmetric upside and embeds bear-case downside well beyond a comfortable threshold.

## Competitive Landscape

GE sits atop a consolidated, oligopolistic large-engine market alongside Pratt & Whitney (RTX) and Rolls-Royce. CFM's LEAP is the volume leader in the narrowbody segment that dominates the global fleet, and the installed base plus sole-source LTSAs make displacement nearly impossible within an engine's multi-decade service life. Competitive trajectory is improving: Pratt's GTF recall is driving share toward LEAP and extending the lives of GE-powered aircraft, the RPO provides revenue visibility no peer matches, and the RISE/Open Fan program (first airport testbed in Singapore with Airbus and CAAS, targeting ~20% better fuel efficiency around 2035) positions the franchise for the next architectural cycle. The CES margin "decline" is a feature of seeding new-install volume that converts into future aftermarket annuities. Competitive Position score: 8.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Valuation/expectations risk is dominant.** At ~44x forward, any execution stumble is amplified. **Air-traffic demand softening** is the freshest and most specific concern: management cut the departures outlook while holding EPS, and Hormuz disruption risk could pressure flight volumes further. **Supply chain** constraints still explicitly limit production despite quarter-over-quarter improvement. **Legacy stranded liabilities** retained at the parent are unusual and persistent: ~$35.7 billion run-off insurance (long-term-care morbidity and rate risk), Bank BPH Polish FX-mortgage litigation (~$2.09 billion estimated losses), environmental/exposure reserves (~$2.18 billion with exceedance risk), and an IRS audit (2016–2020 TCJA transition tax; the company flags the IRS may assert a material additional amount, unresolved within 12 months). **Single-program concentration** (777X/GE9X carries $2.4 billion of deferred nonrecurring engineering) and any engine safety/durability event carry outsized cost. **CFM/Safran dependency** and ~60% non-U.S. revenue add partner and geopolitical exposure. **Leadership reshuffle**: the February 2026 departure of Russell Stokes, who ran CES, accompanying the segment reorganization, warrants monitoring.

Risk Factors score: 5.5. The legacy book and franchise concentration are partly priced, but the premium multiple amplifies the downside from the specific, recently surfaced volume risk.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options surface shows an anomaly that should not be over-read. One-week ATM IV reads 77.3% against a one-year HV of 31.7%, yet the implied move on the nearest expiry is only ±1.6% and the one-month tenor sits at 34.6%, internally inconsistent and consistent with a data artifact around a single near-dated expiry. The more reliable structure is the longer-dated curve (one-month 34.6%, three-month 39.0%, six-month 34.5%), which is moderately elevated but not alarming. Near-term positioning is call-heavy (one-week put/call skew -5.2%, put/call volume 0.34), and short interest is negligible at 1.3% of float (2.5 days to cover), indicating no squeeze dynamic and no crowded bearish positioning. The one signal worth flagging is the six-month put/call volume ratio of 5.50 with a -3.9% skew, suggesting some longer-dated downside hedging accumulating into the back half of 2026, directionally consistent with the departures/aftermarket-volume concern rather than contradicting it. Net: options sentiment neither confirms nor challenges the HOLD; it leans mildly toward back-half caution.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($405, +22%):** The aftermarket flywheel carries earnings regardless of soft departures. Shop-visit revenue growth stays above 30%, LEAP's first heavy shop-visit wave plus GTF-driven share gains and pricing power offset flat volumes, RPO continues compounding past $211 billion, the buyback shrinks the share count, and the multiple holds in the mid-40s as the market re-rates aftermarket durability. Credit upgrades and the dividend hike validate balance-sheet strength.

**Bear case ($270, -19%):** The cut departures outlook proves optimistic. Hormuz disruption pressures global flight hours, shop-visit growth decelerates below ~25%, management is forced to trim FY26 EPS, and the multiple compresses toward the peer median (34x). A legacy-liability surprise (IRS assertion, Bank BPH, insurance reserve) adds an unrelated GAAP drag. At 35–37x a cut estimate, the stock revisits $270–290.

The base case sits between: high-quality compounding continues, but the premium multiple caps re-rating, leaving total return roughly in line with EPS growth less multiple drift, ~$350–355 over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The franchise warrants ownership; the current entry offers poor asymmetry.

**Concrete, falsifiable reason a BUY is not warranted now:** GE is an Industrials/aerospace name where new capital requires bear-case downside contained to roughly 15% plus a dated catalyst and a strong revision signal. None of the three holds. (1) The bear case is ~-19% to -26% — a derating to the 34x peer median on the FY26 guide midpoint of $7.25 implies ~$247. (2) There is no dated near-term re-rating catalyst; the next print (Q2, late July) risks confirming the departures slowdown rather than reversing it. (3) Estimate revisions are stable (+1.5% to +1.7% over 90 days, with next-quarter EPS down 3.4%), not the inflection that would justify overriding a 44x forward multiple. The May departures cut (mid-single-digit to flat-to-low-single-digit, Q1 actual +1.7%, Middle East declining) is the specific, measurable deterioration in the volume leg of the aftermarket thesis. This is falsifiable: if Q2/Q3 shop-visit revenue growth holds above ~25% and departures re-accelerate to mid-single-digit, the BUY-blocker is removed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$280s, which restores asymmetry (bear-case downside contracts below ~10%, bull-case upside expands toward +40%).
- Two consecutive quarters of shop-visit revenue growth above 25% with departures re-accelerating to mid-single-digit, confirming the pricing-offsets-volume bet.
- FY26 adjusted EPS guidance raised above the $7.10–$7.40 range, signaling the aftermarket is outrunning the volume cut.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A FY26 EPS guide cut, or shop-visit revenue growth decelerating below ~20%.
- A material legacy-liability event (adverse IRS assertion, Bank BPH escalation, or insurance reserve strengthening) impairing FCF.
- Multiple holding above 50x forward while estimates flatten — the asymmetric setup the SaaS derating playbook punishes.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), with the departures and shop-visit-revenue data points as the decisive read on the volume-versus-pricing debate.

## Conclusion

GE Aerospace is among the highest-quality industrial franchises in the market: a dominant installed base, an aftermarket flywheel that is visibly accelerating, a widening competitive lead reinforced by Pratt's GTF troubles, and a balance sheet that earned credit upgrades and funds the buyback and dividend increase. The score trajectory has drifted lower (7.5 in February to 6.4 now) not because the business deteriorated but because the stock rose from ~$286 in early May to $332 into improving fundamentals, compressing the risk/reward. The fresh departures-outlook cut against a 44x forward multiple leaves an unattractive ~1:1 reward:risk and no dated catalyst to force a re-rating. We hold the franchise, look to add toward the low-$280s, and would revisit the BUY case on confirmation that aftermarket pricing is carrying earnings through a softer volume environment.
