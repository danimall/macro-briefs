## Executive Summary

TechnipFMC is the world's largest pure-play subsea energy services company, operating with a $16.5B backlog that provides strong revenue coverage for 2026. The company has executed a textbook operational transformation: EBITDA margins have expanded from 8.8% (2021) to 18.5% (2025), with Subsea segment margins reaching 20% in Q1 2026 and full-year 2026 Subsea margin guidance of 21%–22%. Net income inflected from a loss in 2022 to ~$1B run-rate, the balance sheet has shifted to a $540M net cash position, and the company is returning >70% of FCF via buybacks that have reduced shares outstanding by 11% since 2022.

The stock has appreciated significantly over the trailing year, reflecting fundamental improvement rather than multiple expansion. At $72.46, shares trade at 24.5x FY2026 consensus EPS ($2.95) and 20.9x FY2027 ($3.47). These multiples are above peers but justified by: (1) structurally superior competitive positioning via proprietary iEPCI integration, (2) margin expansion trajectory as Subsea 2.0 orders flow through revenue, and (3) a capital return program that compounds EPS growth beyond organic. The specific non-consensus catalyst is that the street's FY27 estimate of $3.47 likely understates the margin expansion embedded in the backlog — management has stated that Subsea 2.0 represents approximately 80% of recent orders but only an estimated ~50% of 2027 revenue, implying further margin accretion into 2028.

The primary risk is the 27x trailing P/E on an energy services company where oil prices drive future sanctioning activity. A de-escalation of Hormuz tensions that brings Brent below $80 would slow new order growth, though the existing $16.5B backlog — with disclosed remaining performance obligations of $5.5B for the remainder of 2026, $4.8B for 2027, and $6.1B thereafter — provides substantial insulation.

## Company Overview

TechnipFMC operates two segments: Subsea (~87% of 2025 revenue, ~89% of Q1 2026 revenue) and Surface Technologies (~13% of 2025 revenue, ~11% of Q1 2026 revenue). The Subsea business designs, manufactures, and installs subsea production systems and provides integrated EPCI services through its proprietary iEPCI model, which bundles subsea production systems (trees, manifolds) with subsea umbilicals, risers, and flowlines (SURF) and installation vessels into a single contract. Surface Technologies provides wellhead systems and pressure control equipment, with 65% of that segment's revenue from international markets (primarily Middle East NOCs).

The company's competitive moat is structural: it is the only player that can offer fully integrated iEPCI contracts combining SPS manufacturing, SURF engineering, flexible pipe production, AND installation vessel fleet capacity under one umbrella. This integration eliminates interface risk for operators and reduces project timelines by 6-12 months. The Subsea 2.0 platform — a configure-to-order product architecture — provides up to 25% lower product cost and as much as 12 months shorter delivery time versus bespoke designs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability Trajectory:**
Revenue has grown from $6.70B (2022) → $7.82B (2023) → $9.08B (2024) → $9.93B (2025) → annualized ~$10.0B (Q1 2026: $2.49B, +11.6% YoY). Growth rates were +16.8% (2023), +16.1% (2024), and +9.4% (2025). The profitability inflection is more dramatic: gross margins expanded from 13.4% (2022) to 16.3% (2023) to 19.0% (2024) to 22.0% (2025) to 23.5% (Q1 2026). Net income went from -$107M (2022) to $964M (2025), with Q1 2026 alone delivering $261M.

Management is guiding 2026 Subsea revenue of $9.2B–$9.6B and Surface Technologies revenue of $1.15B–$1.30B, implying total revenue of approximately $10.35B–$10.90B. Subsea adjusted EBITDA margin guidance is 21%–22%, Surface adjusted EBITDA margin guidance is 16.5%–18%, and management expects total EBITDA to exceed $2.1B for 2026. Free cash flow guidance is $1.3B–$1.45B.

**Balance Sheet:**
Net cash of $540M (cash $961M less debt $420M). Debt has declined from $1.05B in 2022 to $420M currently. Investment-grade ratings (BBB-/Baa2) with a $1.25B undrawn revolver and $1.0B unused commercial paper program. This is an exceptionally clean balance sheet for an energy services company.

**Cash Flow & Capital Return:**
Q1 2026 FCF was $277M. Management targets ~65% EBITDA-to-FCF conversion and CapEx of ~3% of revenue. The company has committed to returning ≥70% of FCF to shareholders, primarily via buybacks. Since July 2022, $1.9B has been repurchased with $1.9B remaining authorized. Shares have declined from 450M to 401M (11% reduction). At a ~$1B/year buyback pace, this compounds EPS growth by 3-4% annually beyond organic.

**Earnings Quality:**
The Q1 2026 share-based compensation charge of $55.5M (vs. $14.1M prior year) was driven by a one-time Value Creation Plan vesting — largely non-recurring. Adjusting for this, underlying earnings power was even stronger than reported. The 4-quarter beat streak (average surprise +19%) suggests estimates consistently lag actual execution.

## Growth Analysis

**Backlog Visibility:** $16.5B total backlog provides approximately 1.5–1.6x annual revenue coverage at the midpoint of 2026 guidance. The disclosed remaining performance obligation (RUPO) schedule shows $5.5B for the remainder of 2026, $4.8B for 2027, and $6.1B thereafter. Adding Q1 actual revenue of $2.49B to remaining-2026 RUPO of $5.5B yields approximately $8.0B in visible 2026 revenue, representing roughly 74%–78% coverage of full-year 2026 revenue guidance — exceptionally strong visibility for an energy services company.

**Order Momentum:** Management reaffirmed a $10B Subsea order target for 2026. The opportunity pipeline stands at ~$30B over 24 months (7th consecutive quarterly increase, up 30%+ over 2 years). Average project size has grown to ~$800M, with projects >$1B more than doubling versus two years ago.

**Margin Expansion Runway:** Management has indicated that Subsea 2.0 represents approximately 80% of recent orders, but a smaller proportion of near-term revenue, given backlog conversion timing. As these higher-margin orders flow through revenue in 2027–2028, margins should continue expanding. The path from 20% Subsea EBITDA margin today toward the guided 21%–22% full-year range is well-supported by product mix shift alone, with potential for further upside as Subsea 2.0 penetration in revenue increases.

**EPS Growth:** Consensus estimates: FY26 $2.95 (+28% vs. FY25 $2.30), FY27 $3.47 (+18%). Combined with 3-4% annual share count reduction, EPS growth compounding at 20%+ is achievable through FY27.

Estimate revisions are positive across all periods: current year up 5.5% over 90 days, next year up 4.3%. The direction is consistently positive and the 4-quarter beat streak (avg +19%) suggests current consensus remains conservative.

No perfect pure-play public peer exists for TechnipFMC's integrated subsea model. Among broader oilfield service comparables, FTI commands a premium valuation. FTI's premium is justified by superior growth (11.6% revenue vs. flat), higher margins (20%+ vs. ~16%), and the structural competitive moat of iEPCI, though the peer set is imperfect given TechnipFMC's unique positioning.

**Valuation concern preventing a higher score:** At 24.5x current year earnings, FTI is priced for continued strong execution. The stock has appreciated substantially over the past 12 months. A miss on orders or margins would likely produce an outsized correction given the elevated multiple for an energy services name. There is limited margin of safety at current levels.

**What the market may be underpricing:** The FY27 consensus of $3.47 may prove conservative. If Subsea margins reach 22-23% on full-year 2027 revenue (vs. the ~20% being modeled), and the order pipeline converts at the expected pace, FY27 EPS could be $3.70-3.80. At $72.46, that would make the stock trade at ~19x FY27 — genuinely attractive for this growth and quality profile.

## Competitive Landscape

TechnipFMC's competitive position is exceptional and improving:

1. **iEPCI Integration Moat:** The only company capable of delivering SPS + SURF + flexible pipe + installation under one contract. Competitors (SLB/OneSubsea, Aker Solutions, Saipem) each cover portions but cannot replicate the full integration. Average project sizes growing to $800M+ creates natural barriers for smaller players.

2. **Subsea 2.0 Adoption:** Approximately 80% of new orders are on the Subsea 2.0 configure-to-order architecture, delivering up to 25% lower product cost and as much as 12-month schedule compression. These cost/time advantages are structural, not cyclical.

3. **Services Installed Base:** ~$2B/year in higher-margin recurring services revenue tied to the growing installed equipment base. This revenue is annuity-like and grows with each new project delivery.

4. **Client Diversification:** The company's 2025 10-K states its customer list exceeds 40 unique clients across major IOCs and NOCs (TotalEnergies, bp, Equinor, Petrobras, Shell, ExxonMobil, Eni). However, the company discloses meaningful customer concentration: two Subsea customers accounted for 15.5% and 14.0% of consolidated 2025 revenue, respectively. While diversified across many relationships, project deferrals or timing shifts from these major clients could affect near-term results.

5. **Geographic Reach into New Frontiers:** Suriname (GranMorgu with TotalEnergies), Namibia, Mozambique represent new basins where TechnipFMC is establishing first-mover positions.

The 5-year TSR of 649% vs. peer group TSR of 182% reflects a 3.6x outperformance driven by structural rather than cyclical factors.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Oil Price Sensitivity (Moderate):** While current oil prices support robust offshore sanctioning, the elevated price environment could moderate. Below $70-75 Brent, new project FIDs would slow. However, the backlog with disclosed RUPO extending well into 2028+ insulates revenue regardless of near-term price movements.

2. **Fixed-Price Contract Execution (Moderate):** Revenue comes from long-duration fixed-price contracts where cost overruns are absorbed by TechnipFMC. The improving margins suggest execution has been strong, but individual project problems could emerge. Warranty accruals of $81M are manageable.

3. **Valuation Risk at Cycle Premium (Elevated):** At 24.5x FY26 EPS, the stock is priced for strong execution. Any stumble on orders or margins would produce outsized downside given the energy services sector's typical valuation compression during sentiment shifts.

4. **Currency & FX Complexity:** A $4.3B FX derivative portfolio creates mark-to-market volatility. Operations span BRL, NOK, GBP, EUR — all actively hedged but introducing earnings noise.

5. **Customer Concentration:** While diversified across 40+ clients, two customers each accounted for more than 10% of 2025 revenue. The 10-K risk factors explicitly cite risk from the loss of major contracts or customer relationships.

6. **Geopolitical — Dual Exposure:** While Hormuz disruptions benefit FTI by redirecting capital to offshore basins and elevating oil prices, Middle East conflict could disrupt Surface Technologies revenue (currently ~11-13% of total).

7. **Insider Selling:** CEO sold $45.7M in shares (March 2026), effected pursuant to a pre-arranged trading plan adopted December 3, 2025. While he retains ~$174M in holdings (strong alignment), and the sale was pre-planned rather than discretionary, no insider purchases have been observed.

## Conclusion

TechnipFMC warrants a BUY recommendation at $72.46. The combination of: (1) a $16.5B backlog providing multi-year revenue visibility with ~75%+ coverage for 2026, (2) structurally expanding margins as Subsea 2.0 flows through revenue, (3) a net cash balance sheet with investment-grade ratings, (4) a proven capital return program compounding EPS via 3-4% annual share reductions, and (5) an unmatched competitive position in integrated subsea — creates a quality compounder within the energy services sector.

The specific non-consensus element is margin trajectory: the street models ~20% Subsea EBITDA for 2027, but with approximately 80% of recent orders on the Subsea 2.0 architecture (which structurally delivers higher margins through up to 25% lower product costs) and full-year 2026 Subsea margin guidance of 21%–22%, FY27 margins of 22-23% are plausible as the higher-quality backlog converts. This would push EPS $0.20-0.30 above current consensus, providing the re-rating catalyst.

The valuation at 24.5x current year and 21x next year is full but not stretched for a business growing EPS at 20%+ with this quality of competitive positioning and backlog visibility. The primary risk is a cyclical oil price correction that would compress multiples and slow future orders — partially mitigated by the existing backlog duration. Position sizing of 2-3% acknowledges the energy sector's inherent cyclicality while capturing the structural improvement.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Backlog revenue coverage is approximately 74%–78% (not 95% as originally stated), the Subsea 2.0 "80% of orders / 50% of 2027 revenue" claim is based on management commentary not verified in SEC filings, and no explicit Q2 2026 23% margin guidance was found in the SEC-furnished earnings release (full-year guidance is 21%–22%). These corrections reduce the certainty around the margin-catalyst timeline but do not invalidate the directional thesis.
