## Executive Summary

Titan International reported Q2 2026 revenue of $484.8M (+5.2%), gross margin of 15.5%, and diluted GAAP EPS of $0.09 — its first positive quarterly GAAP result since Q2 2025. The 10-Q filed the same day discloses that $6.0M of net IEEPA tariff refund recoveries were recognized as a reduction to cost of goods sold under the gain contingency model. Backing that out: operating income was $7.3M versus $10.2M in Q2 2025 (a ~28% decline, not the +31% increase reported), gross margin was 14.22% versus 15.03% (an ~81bp decline, not the +50bp expansion reported), and the $6.0M pre-tax benefit equals $0.093 per diluted share against reported net income attributable to Titan of $5.8M, or $0.09 per share. Because the U.S. valuation allowance drove the quarterly effective tax rate to (0.1)%, the refund flowed through essentially untaxed. The entire quarterly profit is accounted for by a contingent, industry-wide customs recovery that Deere also disclosed in its own results.

The market's response was to mark the stock down 10.7% from $8.24 (July 28) to $7.36 and to cut near-term estimates hard. Current-quarter consensus moved from +$0.01 to -$0.05 over 30 days; next-quarter from -$0.10 to -$0.18; next-year from $0.37 to $0.29 (-22.1% over 90 days). Those revisions are the cleanest read on what the print actually contained. The segment detail supports them: Agricultural revenue fell 5.0% (organically ~7.5% after +2.5% FX), gross margin fell 320bp to 11.4%, and segment operating income fell 72% to $3.2M on higher steel costs and OEM destocking. Agricultural revenue is down 24.4% from FY2023 ($980.5M → $740.9M); consolidated revenue looks flat only because the February 2024 Carlstar acquisition added $352M of Consumer revenue over the same period.

The capital structure converts a cyclical trough into a genuinely asymmetric downside. Total debt was $594.8M at 6/30/2026 against $179.8M of cash, of which $161.8M sits offshore — roughly $18M is domestically accessible against $554M of debt carried by the U.S. guarantor group, which itself lost $34.9M at the operating line in FY2025 and $14.6M in H1 2026. Net debt of $413.5M is 87% of market capitalization. Clean TTM EBITDA of ~$87-93M (stripping the tax allowance, $26.0M of Jackson, TN restructuring, and the $6.0M refund) implies 4.4x net leverage and an $888M enterprise value at 8-10x — not a trough-cycle bargain multiple for a business at ~1% operating margin generating negative free cash flow. We move to REDUCE from HOLD: the fundamental deterioration is specific and confirmed, not a price artifact.

## Company Overview

Titan manufactures off-highway wheels, tires, wheel/tire assemblies, and undercarriage systems, tracing to the Electric Wheel Company (1890) and built largely through acquisition — Goodyear's North American farm tire assets (2005), Continental's OTR tire assets (2006), Goodyear Latin America (2011), a 64.3% controlling stake in Voltyre-Prom in Volgograd, Russia, and The Carlstar Group (February 2024, now "Titan Specialty").

FY2025 segment structure:

| Segment | FY2025 Sales | FY2023 Sales | Change | FY2025 GM | FY2025 Op. Income |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agricultural | $740.9M | $980.5M | -24.4% | 12.5% | $27.5M |
| Earthmoving/Construction | $581.7M | $687.8M | -15.4% | 10.4% | $3.1M |
| Consumer | $505.8M | $153.5M | +229.5% | 19.8% | $19.1M |
| Corporate | — | — | — | — | ($28.9M) |
| **Total** | **$1,828.4M** | **$1,821.8M** | **+0.4%** | **13.9%** | **$20.8M** |

Two structural disclosures shape any forward view. First, the 10-K states plainly that backlog "is not considered material to, or a significant factor in, evaluating and understanding any of the Company's business segments" — there is no order book, no deferred revenue, and no RPO to underwrite an inflection. Second, the brand licenses central to the franchise have finite lives: Goodyear farm/ATV through end-2029, Goodyear truck through December 2028, Carlisle® through 2033, with FY2025 royalty expense of $11.1M. License renewal terms are not disclosed in the sections reviewed.

Genuine competitive assets exist: the largest agricultural wheel (54") made in North America and the largest earthmoving wheel (63") made globally, proprietary LSW low-sidewall technology, owned molds and dies that raise switching costs, and a vertically integrated undercarriage foundry in Spain. Titan also concedes in its own filing that competitors — Michelin, Bridgestone, BKT, Trelleborg, Berco, Caterpillar — are "larger and have greater financial and marketing resources," and that price-led competition recurs.

## Financial Analysis

### Multi-year margin compression

| ($M) | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 2,169.4 | 1,821.8 | 1,845.9 | 1,828.4 |
| Gross margin | 16.6% | 16.8% | 14.0% | 13.9% |
| SG&A | 132.8 | 134.9 | 191.8 | 203.3 |
| Operating income | 205.8 | 148.7 | 33.2 | 20.8 |
| Operating margin | 9.5% | 8.2% | 1.8% | 1.1% |
| Interest expense | (29.8) | (18.8) | (36.4) | (38.7) |
| Pretax income | 202.4 | 109.7 | 8.3 | (11.3) |
| Tax provision | 23.2 | 26.0 | 11.9 | **49.9** |
| Net income attrib. Titan | — | — | (3.6) | **(61.2)** |

Operating margin fell 840bp over three years. SG&A rose 51% from FY2023 to FY2025 on flat revenue, from 7.4% to 11.2% of sales, reflecting Carlstar's distribution-center cost base and acquisition-related amortization. The acquisition brought a higher gross margin (Consumer 19.8%) but not a higher operating margin: Consumer converted 19.8% gross into 3.8% operating in FY2025, versus Agricultural converting 12.5% gross into 3.7%.

The FY2025 tax provision of $49.9M on an $11.3M pretax loss — a (441.6)% effective rate — reflects valuation allowances established domestically and in Luxembourg. The mechanism is disclosed: management weights cumulative operating results over the most recent three-year period, and the U.S. and Luxembourg entities crossed into cumulative losses, which under ASC 740 constitutes objectively verifiable negative evidence. The charge was non-cash ($31.0M of deferred tax within operating cash flow) and is reversible if U.S. profitability returns. The practical consequences are that GAAP net income is now decoupled from pretax income and that the effective rate has collapsed toward zero — the Q2 2026 rate was (0.1)%, which is why the tariff refund passed through nearly untaxed.

The guarantor-only disclosure explains the substance. The guarantor group (parent plus Titan Tire Corporation, Titan Tire of Bryan, Titan Tire of Freeport, Titan Wheel Corporation of Illinois) posted FY2025 net sales of $468.5M, gross profit of $35.4M (7.6%), a $34.9M operating loss, and an $80.8M net loss, while carrying $554.0M of long-term debt. H1 2026: $249.0M of sales, 7.8% gross margin, $14.6M operating loss. Guarantor financials are affected by transfer pricing and cost allocation and the filing warns they may not indicate standalone results — but the direction is unambiguous. The debt sits on the operations that lose money.

### Cash flow

| ($M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 141.5 | 30.0 | (7.4) |
| Capex | (65.6) | (54.6) | (26.0) |
| Free cash flow | 75.9 | **(24.6)** | **(33.4)** |

FY2024's $141.5M of operating cash flow was substantially a one-time liquidation of working capital acquired with Carlstar plus destocking: accounts receivable contributed +$73.8M in FY2024 versus -$4.0M in FY2025, and inventory +$51.5M versus -$7.1M. It is not a run rate. FY2025 capex of $54.6M ran below D&A of $67.1M, with management stating the reduction "reflected Titan's efforts to optimize cash management in response to lower product demand." Under-investing relative to depreciation in a capital-intensive manufacturing business with larger, better-capitalized competitors is a slow erosion of position, not a cost saving.

Cash conversion improved to 103 days at 6/30/2026 from 111 days at year-end (DIO -12, DPO +3, DSO +7). Supplier-financing confirmed obligations within payables rose to $21.5M from $12.8M — small, but a payables-extension mechanism worth monitoring. The allowance for credit losses stands at $5.26M versus $2.00M a year earlier, with the step-up occurring in H2 2025 rather than H1 2026.

### Capital structure and the binding constraint

| Instrument | 6/30/2026 |
|---|---|
| 7.00% senior secured notes due April 2028 | $400.0M |
| ABL revolver ($225M commitment) | $150.0M |
| Titan Europe facilities | $23.5M |
| Other (Brazil working capital) | $21.3M |
| **Total debt** | **$594.8M** |

Maturities: H2 2026 $20.3M; 2027 $21.5M; **2028 $551.0M**; thereafter $1.9M. The ABL matures February 28, 2029 "or 91 days prior to the maturity of our 7.00% secured notes due in 2028" — a springing provision that pulls the effective revolver maturity to roughly January 2028. The notes must therefore be addressed during 2027, with negative free cash flow and no deleveraging to show refinancing counterparties.

Liquidity: cash $179.8M, of which $161.8M offshore (~$18M domestic); revolver borrowing base $200.3M less $150.0M drawn and $6.4M of letters of credit = $43.9M net availability. The springing fixed-charge coverage covenant (minimum 1.0:1.0, trailing four quarters) triggers when availability falls below the greater of $17.0M or 10% of the line cap ($20.03M) — a cushion of roughly $23.9M of incremental borrowing or borrowing-base shrinkage. The company states it was in compliance at 6/30/2026 and the covenant was not triggered; the credit agreement's FCCR definition was not available, so whether Titan would satisfy the test if sprung is unverified. What is verifiable is that a working-capital shock that simultaneously drew the revolver and compressed the AR/inventory borrowing base could consume that cushion quickly.

The notes traded near par ($399.6M fair value on $398.5M carrying value), so credit markets are not pricing distress. That is the single strongest counterpoint to the bear case and should be weighted accordingly. Both the 10-K and the 10-Q also state that "potential divestitures and unencumbered assets also could be a possible means to provide for future liquidity needs" — an unusual disclosure that contemplates asset sales.

## Growth Analysis

H1 2026 revenue rose 4.0% to $989.8M, of which FX contributed 3.1% — organic growth of roughly 0.9%. H1 produced a $0.5M operating loss and an $18.45M net loss attributable to Titan ($0.29/share), driven by $26.0M of Jackson, TN restructuring and impairment.

Q2 segment detail, which is where the forward signal sits:

- **Agricultural**: $183.6M (-5.0%; organic ~-7.5%), gross margin 11.4% versus 14.6% (-320bp), operating income $3.2M versus $11.5M (-72%). Drivers cited: lower farm income, higher financing costs, OEM inventory reduction, and higher steel costs.
- **Earthmoving/Construction**: $154.5M (+1.4%, entirely FX at +3.2% — organic ~-1.8%). Operating income $4.4M including $0.9M of refund, so ~$3.5M underlying versus $3.0M.
- **Consumer**: $146.6M (+27.2%) against a prior-year period hit by tariff uncertainty. Operating income $13.0M including $4.7M of refund, so ~$8.3M underlying versus $3.2M — a genuine and substantial improvement.

Consumer is the only segment with real operating momentum, and it is the segment most exposed to discretionary consumer spending and tariffs. It also carries the Jackson closure charges, which produced an H1 2026 Consumer operating loss of $3.0M despite $64.1M of gross profit.

Management maintained FY2026 guidance of $1.85-1.95B revenue and $105-115M adjusted EBITDA, with Q3 guided at $440-460M and $27-33M. Reconstructing from the 10-Q: Q1 2026 operating loss was -$13.8M with $17.1M of D&A (EBITDA $3.3M, ~$28.4M adding back $25.1M of restructuring), plus the company's reported Q2 figure of $34M gives H1 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $62M. Full-year guidance therefore implies H2 of $43-53M, and with Q3 guided at $27-33M, Q4 falls out at roughly $10-26M — in the seasonally weakest quarter, where derived H2 2025 unadjusted EBITDA was $33.4M for the full half. The company's adjusted EBITDA definition was not obtained (our Q2 bridge falls ~$2.6M short of the reported $34M), so treat this as a sensitivity rather than a forecast. Directionally, it identifies where a guidance cut would come from.

Industry context corroborates the cycle rather than a company-specific problem: AGCO cut its 2026 sales and earnings guidance on weak farm demand and tariffs the same day Titan reported; CNH's Q2 net income fell to $141M from $217M on 2% revenue growth; Deere raised its Construction & Forestry outlook and separately disclosed its own IEEPA refund recovery. The construction-versus-agriculture divergence and the one-off nature of tariff refund income are both industry-wide.

Earnings surprise history is noisy and low-information at these absolute magnitudes: +308% (Q2 2026, on a -$0.05 estimate), -100% (Q1 2026), in-line (Q4 2025), +363% (Q3 2025). Percentage surprises on near-zero estimates convey little. The estimate revisions convey a great deal, and they are negative across every near-term period.

## Valuation Assessment

Enterprise value, reproduced from the frozen price basis:

- Equity: $7.36 × 64,485,180 shares = **$474.6M**
- Plus net carrying debt $593.3M, less cash $179.8M → net debt **$413.5M**
- **EV ≈ $888.1M**

EBITDA on several bases:

| Basis | EBITDA | EV/EBITDA |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 unadjusted (EBIT $20.8M + D&A $67.1M) | $87.9M | 10.1x |
| FY2025 company adjusted (proxy) | $101.5M | 8.7x |
| TTM reported (Jul-25 to Jun-26) | $67.2M | 13.2x |
| TTM adding back restructuring, removing refund ("clean") | ~$87.2M | 10.2x |
| FY2026 guided adjusted, midpoint | $110M | 8.1x |

Net leverage on clean EBITDA is 4.4x; gross leverage 6.4x. The peer P/E median of 26.7 is not usable — TWI has negative trailing and current-year EPS. The relevant comparison is AGCO at 10.7x earnings, and AGCO just cut guidance. On the one forward earnings figure that exists, consensus next-year EPS of $0.29, TWI trades at 25.4x — and that $0.29 was cut from $0.37 in 30 days. The market is not paying a trough multiple on next-year earnings power; it is paying a full multiple on a rapidly declining estimate.

The bull argument is cycle-adjusted: company-reported adjusted EBITDA ran $253.0M (2022), $205.2M (2023), $128.1M (2024), $101.5M (2025) — a 60% peak-to-trough decline. If mid-cycle is $150-200M, then 8-9x implies an EV of $1.2-1.8B and equity of $800M-1.4B, or $12-22 per share. That is a real possibility and it is why three covering analysts carry Buy ratings with targets of $10-13.

Two objections carry more weight than the mid-cycle arithmetic. First, mix has changed permanently: the $253M peak was earned on an Agricultural segment generating $980M+ of revenue at materially higher margins; today Consumer contributes 28% of revenue at 3.8% operating margin, and Agricultural has structurally lower absolute volume. A recovery to $150M is plausible; a return to $200M+ requires both an ag capex upcycle and margin recapture in a segment where steel costs are currently rising. Second, the leverage cuts both ways with unusual force. Net debt at 87% of market capitalization means a $50M swing in mid-cycle EBITDA expectations moves the equity by roughly $400M of enterprise value against a $475M market cap — a 65-85% move. That optionality is symmetric, not a one-way call.

Applying the framework's own caution on cheap multiples: a compressed EV/EBITDA is not itself a buy signal, and here there is no identified near-term catalyst. The refinancing is an overhang, not a catalyst. Estimate revisions are falling, which historically pushes analyst targets down toward price rather than pulling price up to targets. Analyst targets function as floors only when a stock is cheap against *rising* estimates; TWI's estimates are being cut, so the $11.75 mean is stale in the unfavorable direction. Valuation scores 4.2 — genuine cyclical optionality, no margin of safety, no dated catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Titan is not obviously losing share. The Q2 pattern — agriculture weak, earthmoving/construction flat, consumer/aftermarket recovering — maps onto AGCO, CNH, and Deere's disclosures. Top-10 customer concentration improved from 40% (FY2023) to 33% (FY2025) and Deere from 13% to 10%, though that improvement is arithmetically driven partly by adding Carlstar's fragmented aftermarket revenue and partly by the shrinkage of Deere-linked ag revenue; it does not establish that Titan won new OEM business.

Position trajectory is mixed. Improving: aftermarket mix (Consumer Q2 gross margin 23.7% versus Agricultural 11.4%), a manufacturing footprint located in the markets served — a real advantage over import-dependent competitors like BKT under a tariff regime — and the Jackson consolidation taking the domestic footprint from seven plants to six with production transferred to remaining facilities. Deteriorating: capex below depreciation while Michelin, Bridgestone, and Caterpillar can sustain investment and absorb price competition through the cycle; the 10-K's own acknowledgment that "certain of the Company's competitors have reduced their prices in particular product categories, which has prompted Titan to reduce prices as well"; and the risk-factor disclosure that OEM customers "may develop in-house tire and wheel capabilities." Net: stable-to-slightly-deteriorating, scored 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing (highest materiality).** $551.0M matures in 2028, effectively a 2027 task given the ABL's springing maturity. Refinancing $400M of 7.00% notes will almost certainly come at a higher coupon; each 200bp adds ~$8M of annual interest against clean EBITDA of ~$88M and interest expense already at $38.7M. Mitigant: the notes trade at par, secured by the land and buildings of Titan Wheel Corporation of Illinois and the Titan Tire subsidiaries, with two years of lead time.

**Liquidity geography.** ~$18M of the $179.8M cash balance is domestically accessible against U.S.-domiciled debt of $554M. Repatriation may be available at modest cost, but the 10-K flags possible restrictions.

**Covenant cushion.** ~$23.9M of availability separates Titan from a springing 1.0x fixed-charge coverage test whose definition we could not verify.

**Tariff bidirectionality.** The Federal Circuit held in August 2025 that IEEPA did not authorize the challenged tariffs and the Supreme Court affirmed in February 2026; the administration then imposed a 10% global tariff under other authorities with stated intent to raise it to 15%. Titan is simultaneously a refund beneficiary and exposed to new duties. The $6.0M recognized is net of estimated customer obligations and the 10-K flags "potential disputes over entitlement to refundable duties" — the estimate itself is revisable. Total pending claims are not disclosed; do not model a refund stream.

**Russia.** Voltyre-Prom (64.3% owned) is ~7% of consolidated assets and 4-5% of sales. A consolidated Russian manufacturing asset is practically unsaleable at book value and a persistent complication for any refinancing or control transaction.

**Customer concentration.** Top 10 at 33% of FY2025 sales; Deere alone at 10%.

**Commodity pass-through.** No long-term commodity contracts and no derivative hedging on steel, natural and synthetic rubber, carbon black, bead wire, or fabric. Rising steel costs were explicitly cited in the Agricultural margin decline.

**Other:** hyperinflationary accounting in Argentina and Turkey ($1.1M net monetary loss in H1 2026); ABL at SOFR with a 5.66% weighted average rate and ~$2.3M of expense per 100bp if fully drawn; a collective bargaining agreement covering ~32% of U.S. employees expiring November 2028; a new "Geopolitical and Military Conflict Risks" factor added in Q2 2026; and an AIP registered block of 8,880,478 shares (~13.8% of shares outstanding) after AIPCT sold 3,041,288 shares at $8.95 in March 2026.

Equity-award dilution is immaterial — diluted shares (64.50M) essentially equal basic (64.38M), with awards antidilutive in H1 2026 and both 2025 periods and maximum contingent dilution on the order of 0.3-0.6%. There are no warrants, earn-outs, or convertibles disclosed. This is one clean element of the capital structure.

ATM implied volatility rose from 66.0% to 76.4% (+10.3pp) between July 19 and August 8 while the stock fell 3.9%. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($13.00, +77%)

The trough is real and the cycle turns. Agricultural equipment demand inflects during 2027 as farm income stabilizes and OEM destocking completes; Titan's adjusted EBITDA recovers toward $150M against a $101.5M FY2025 trough, aided by Jackson closure savings (six domestic plants, ~140 positions removed, with ~$4M of remaining costs), continued Consumer/aftermarket momentum at 23.7% gross margin, and a locally-positioned footprint that is genuinely advantaged versus import-dependent competitors under tariffs. At 8.5x on $150M with net debt at $390M, the equity is worth ~$13.70. Additional tariff refund claims are pending and could extend the cash benefit. Secured notes at par mean the 2027 refinancing is executed without equity dilution. Management compensation is well aligned — CEO compensation actually paid was **negative $501,805** in FY2024 and total comp has fallen 46% since 2021, with metrics keyed to adjusted EBITDA, cash flow, and working capital. Eventual completion of AIP's exit removes the technical overhang. Three covering analysts carry Buy ratings with a $11.75 mean target.

### Bear Case ($4.75, -35%)

The trough deepens before it turns. Agricultural gross margin fell 320bp year over year in Q2 with operating income down 72% and no backlog to signal an inflection; AGCO cut guidance the same day. Q4 2026 implied adjusted EBITDA of roughly $10-26M leaves ample room for a guidance cut. FY2026 EBITDA lands near $95M, FY2027 stays at $90-100M, and the 2027 refinancing prices at 9-10%, adding $8-12M of annual interest to a business already generating negative free cash flow with capex below depreciation. At 7.5x on $95M less $415M of net debt, the equity is worth ~$4.60. The U.S. guarantor group continues to lose money at the operating line, keeping the valuation allowance in place and the domestic cash balance near $18M. A working-capital shock compresses the $23.9M revolver cushion into a springing covenant test. The 13.8% AIP block continues to distribute into any strength.

The bear case does not require distress — only that mid-cycle EBITDA is $110M rather than $175M. Given 87% net-debt-to-market-cap, that difference alone accounts for most of the range between the two scenarios.

### Base Case ($7.75, +5%)

FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands within guidance near $110M, helped by an additional tranche of tariff refunds and Jackson savings, with FY2027 recovering modestly toward $120M as Consumer holds and Agricultural stabilizes at a lower level. At 8.0x on $115M less ~$420M of net debt, equity is worth ~$500M, or $7.75 per share. Free cash flow is roughly breakeven for the full year after a seasonal H2 working-capital release, leaving leverage flat and the 2027 refinancing to be executed on a business that has neither deleveraged nor demonstrated a margin inflection.

Probability-weighting bear 40% / base 42% / bull 18% produces an expected return of approximately +2% over twelve months, against a 40%-probability -35% downside. That is unfavorable asymmetry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Expected risk-adjusted return is negligible against a severe left tail, with no dated catalyst inside twelve months and a required 2027 refinancing.

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into strength ahead of the Q3 2026 print (late October/early November), which carries the highest information density of any near-term event: the $440-460M revenue / $27-33M adjusted EBITDA guide, the implied Q4 residual, and — most importantly — gross margin excluding any further tariff refunds. We would reduce exposure by roughly half at current levels and hold the remainder only as cyclical optionality sized to survive a -35% outcome. New capital should not be committed here.

**Base case target: $7.75** (8.0x $115M FY2027 adjusted EBITDA less ~$420M net debt ÷ 64.5M shares), +5.3% from $7.36.
**Bull case target: $13.00**, +76.6%.
**Bear case target: $4.75**, -35.5%.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with decision points at the Q3 2026 print and any 8-K addressing the 2028 notes.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Agricultural segment gross margin stabilizes at or above 13% for two consecutive quarters, with segment operating income no longer declining year over year.
2. A refinancing of the 7.00% notes announced at a coupon of 8.0% or below, removing the springing ABL maturity.
3. Consolidated gross margin, excluding any tariff refunds, expands year over year for two consecutive quarters.
4. Full-year 2026 free cash flow turns positive with net revolver availability above $60M.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE would additionally require** next-year consensus EPS revisions turning positive (currently -22.1% over 90 days) alongside a price below $6.00, which would put the equity near 6.5x mid-cycle EBITDA on a de-risked balance sheet.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one):**
1. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $100M, or Q3 adjusted EBITDA below the $27M guided floor.
2. Net revolver availability falling below $30M, or any disclosure that the springing fixed-charge coverage covenant has been triggered.
3. An announced divestiture of an operating business to meet liquidity needs, consistent with the "potential divestitures and unencumbered assets" language repeated in both the 10-K and 10-Q.
4. 2028 notes trading materially below par (below 92), indicating credit markets repricing refinancing risk.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October/early November 2026), with interim monitoring of AIP block sales, the notes' trading level, and any 8-K on the capital structure.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report (4.2 → 4.0 → 4.7 → 4.5) reflected improving assessments of competitive position and risk as Titan diversified into aftermarket and announced cost action. The Q2 2026 print reverses that direction on evidence rather than price. Reported improvement in operating income and gross margin inverts to declines once the $6.0M tariff refund disclosed in Note 1 is removed; the Agricultural segment, still the largest, saw operating income fall 72% on a 320bp gross-margin decline; consensus estimates were cut across every near-term period, including a 22.1% reduction to next-year EPS over 90 days; and H1 2026 free cash flow was negative $33.4M with capex running below depreciation. The stock's decline from its February high of $11.70 is explained by these developments, not by market error.

What keeps this from AVOID is that the balance sheet is stressed rather than distressed: secured notes trade at par, total liquidity is ~$224M, the covenant was not triggered at 6/30/2026, FY2026 guidance was maintained, equity-award dilution is immaterial, and management compensation is genuinely aligned on the downside. What keeps it from HOLD is that 8-10x EV/EBITDA on trough earnings offers no margin of safety when net debt is 87% of market capitalization, the U.S. operations that carry the debt lose money at the operating line, only ~$18M of cash is domestically accessible, and $551M must be refinanced by early 2028 with no order backlog to underwrite the recovery that the multiple already embeds. The three Buy ratings and $10-13 target range are directionally the bull case, but they were set against estimates that have since been cut and are more likely to converge downward toward price than to pull price upward.
