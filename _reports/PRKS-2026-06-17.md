**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

United Parks & Resorts (formerly SeaWorld Entertainment) operates 12 domestic theme/water parks plus a licensed park in Abu Dhabi, anchored by its zoological collection, a genuine differentiator and its single most distinctive risk. The investment story reduces to one tension: a structurally declining, high-fixed-cost business being financially engineered by a controlling private-equity holder. Revenue has fallen for four consecutive years and accelerated lower in 2025 (-3.6% to $1,663M), attendance is shrinking (-1.8% in 2025, -5.0% in Q1 2026), and Adjusted EBITDA has dropped three straight years ($713M → $700M → $605M). Against this, Hill Path Capital (~53% ownership) has bought back over $700M of stock since 2024, shrinking the float from ~70M shares (2022) to ~49M, driving negative book equity (-$557.2M at Q1 2026) and revolver reliance.

The stock has rallied roughly 60% off its $28.77 March low to ~$46, and consensus EPS estimates have risen 13-15% over 90 days. The estimate revisions and the per-share optics are substantially a function of share-count reduction layered on cost discipline, not a recovery in the underlying revenue base. The company missed consensus in all four of the last reported quarters, with the Q1 2026 net loss widening to -$34.1M from -$16.1M. Per-capita in-park spend rising (+5.3% in Q1) while attendance falls is the signature of monetizing fewer guests harder, a strategy with a finite runway and reputational cost.

We rate PRKS AVOID. The bull case rests on attendance stabilizing while buybacks compound per-share value; the bear case is that buybacks are masking a brand-challenged, over-levered business with no equity cushion, refinancing walls at 2029 and 2031, an escalating Sesame license-termination lawsuit, and a controlling owner whose Chairman also chairs the compensation committee and is "actively involved in overseeing certain key operating activities." We do not recommend an active short: 36.2% short interest creates meaningful squeeze risk, and the buyback support plus a binary Hill-Path take-private possibility make the carry unfavorable.

## Company Overview

United Parks operates under recognized brands (SeaWorld, Busch Gardens, Aquatica, Discovery Cove, and Sesame Place) across theme parks, water parks, and the reservations-only Discovery Cove in Orlando, plus the licensed SeaWorld Abu Dhabi (opened May 2023 with Miral on Yas Island). The model is attendance × per-capita spend, split between admission per-capita and in-park per-capita.

The portfolio is geographically concentrated: Florida ~59%, California ~16%, Virginia ~13% of 2025 revenue. This concentrates hurricane and regional-weather exposure and ties the company's fortunes to the Orlando market, where Universal's new Epic Universe is intensifying competition for the same discretionary leisure dollar.

The zoological collection (marine mammals, including orcas) and animal-interaction experiences distinguish PRKS from Disney, Universal, Six Flags/Cedar Fair, Merlin, and Hershey. That same feature invites activist and regulatory pressure, ticket-reseller terminations, and a reputational overhang dating to the 2010 trainer death and the "Blackfish" period.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue decline is the core problem.** The four-year trajectory is unambiguous: 2022 $1,731M → 2023 $1,727M → 2024 $1,725M → 2025 $1,663M. 2025 attendance was 21.17M, total rev/capita $78.54 (-1.9%), and admission per-capita $41.73 (-4.3%, pressured by promotions and visitation mix). The admission per-capita decline shows the company discounting gate price to defend traffic and failing, while pushing in-park surcharges to compensate.

**Margins compress with operating leverage.** Fixed costs (labor, animal care, maintenance, utilities, property tax, insurance) do not flex down with attendance. A 3.6% revenue decline produced a 21.1% drop in operating income to $365.4M (~22% margin) and a 26% drop in net income to $168.4M.

**Q1 2026 worsened.** Revenue $278.3M (-3.0%), attendance -5.0%, net loss widening to -$0.69/sh, missing the ~-$0.36 consensus (a -$0.33 miss). Management attributed the miss to weather and weak international visitation. The credibility problem with that explanation is that Q1 is Orlando's strongest weather window and the company was lapping 2025's California fires. The single bright spot was in-park per-capita +5.3% to $40.62.

**Cash flow is real but declining.** Operating cash flow was $380M in 2025, down from $480M and $505M in prior years; FCF was ~$163M after $217M capex. Capex has been cut from $305M in 2023, and expansion/ROI projects slashed from $79M to $35M. This belt-tightening directly undermines the "fresh attractions" needed to reverse attendance, in a business where capital intensity drives the experience.

**The balance sheet is the headline tension.** Debt stands at $2.248B: a $1.52B floating-rate Term B-3 loan (~5.67%, due Dec 2031) plus $725M of 5.25% Senior Notes (due Aug 2029). About $1.5B is variable, so a 100bps SOFR move adds ~$15.2M to annual interest. Stockholders' equity is negative (-$435.8M at YE2025, deteriorating to -$557.2M at Q1 2026), driven by $2.08B of treasury stock from buybacks, not operating losses. The current ratio is 0.74 (normal given deferred revenue) but slipped to 0.5 in Q1 as cash fell to $28.9M and the company drew $30M net on the revolver. Net total leverage is 3.52x with covenant headroom (buybacks permitted under 4.25x, first-lien max 6.25x); ratings are S&P BB / Moody's Ba3, both stable.

The Altman Z of 1.05 is a structural artifact of buyback-driven negative equity, not an imminent bankruptcy signal; the company generates FCF and is covenant-compliant. But it correctly flags that there is no equity cushion, and the entire model now depends on FCF servicing a heavy, partly-floating debt load while revenue erodes. The estimate-revision and earnings-surprise records together tell the story: rising forward EPS coexisting with four straight misses means consensus keeps re-anchoring upward on per-share mechanics while the operating business keeps falling short.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here. Revenue has declined for four years and accelerated lower. Attendance is down at both the annual and quarterly cadence. The only positive line is in-park per-capita spend, which is a monetization tactic, not volume growth, and one that critics credibly tie to deteriorating guest goodwill.

The earnings-estimate revisions look superficially strong: current-year EPS up 14.7% over 90 days to $4.30, next-year up 13.2% to $4.71. The standard inflection signal (estimate revisions >30% with revenue acceleration) is not present here. Revenue is shrinking, not accelerating. The EPS revisions are being driven by a smaller share count (down ~30% from 2022) and cost discipline, not by an improving top line. Rising EPS estimates should not be read as a fundamental inflection, because the denominator is doing the work.

Bright spots exist within the portfolio. Orlando and Discovery Cove are outperforming on premium demand with strong 2026 advance bookings. But these are partial offsets within a portfolio whose broad recovery is absent, and management's choice to cut expansion capex makes a broad reacceleration less likely.

## Valuation Assessment

On the surface, PRKS screens cheap: ~$46 against ~$4.30 current-year and ~$4.71 next-year consensus EPS implies ~10.7x and ~9.8x forward earnings, well below the leisure-facilities peer median P/E of 33.1 (OSW) and PLNT at 18.6. The reported peer-median P/E of 33.1 is itself distorted: FUN trades at a negative P/E (loss-making) and the comparison set is heterogeneous, so the screen overstates the discount.

A low multiple here reflects structural impairment, not mispricing. The earnings cheapness is a value-trap signature: declining revenue, no equity cushion, a controlled-company governance discount, a binary license-termination suit, and forward EPS that depends on continued buybacks rather than organic growth. The market is correctly applying a discount to a business whose per-share earnings are partly manufactured and whose enterprise value (equity ~$2.2B + net debt ~$2.2B = ~$4.4B EV against ~$605M EBITDA, ~7.3x EV/EBITDA) is a more honest framing, and on that basis the company is not obviously cheap for a no-growth, levered operator.

The analyst target range ($37 low, $54 high, $44.5 mean, $42.5 median) places the current price of ~$46 above the consensus mean and median, meaning the stock has rallied past where most analysts see fair value. With four straight misses, these targets are reasonable ceilings on a business that keeps disappointing rather than stale floors lagging an improving fundamental story. We do not award a high valuation score for cheapness absent a specific catalyst, and the only catalyst candidate (a Hill-Path take-private) is speculative and could come at a level not far above current price.

## Competitive Landscape

PRKS competes against far larger, better-capitalized operators. The 10-K concedes competitors "have substantially greater financial resources." Universal's Epic Universe directly intensifies competition in PRKS's largest market. Consolidated regional players (Six Flags/Cedar Fair) add scale pressure.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating. Attendance is falling while the company cuts expansion capex, the post-rename brand identity is diluted (the SeaWorld → United Parks rebrand produced a corporate name with limited consumer pull), and external critics point to declining customer service and staff morale alongside fee creep that monetizes guests at the expense of repeat visitation. The zoological moat is real but double-edged: it carries permanent activist/regulatory exposure that competitors do not bear.

The Sesame Place franchise illustrates the competitive fragility: Sesame Workshop sued in SDNY (March 12, 2026) to terminate the license (which runs through 2031). Loss would strand two dedicated Sesame Place parks (Langhorne PA, Chula Vista CA) and Sesame-themed areas across the portfolio.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Secular attendance/revenue erosion.** Four years of decline now accelerating, repeatedly attributed to weather despite weather-independent timing patterns.
2. **Animal-welfare/activist and regulatory risk.** Unique to PRKS; orcas and marine mammals invite activism, reseller terminations, and evolving display/breeding regulation.
3. **Leverage and rate exposure.** Negative equity and refinancing walls at 2029 (notes) and 2031 (term loan).
4. **High operating leverage** amplifies revenue declines into outsized margin compression.
5. **Sesame license-termination suit (escalating).** Moved from royalty dispute to breach allegations to outright termination demand, a brand-level impairment tail rather than a routine commercial dispute.
6. **Rising minimum wages.** San Diego theme-park wage to $19/hr (July 2026) stepping to $25/hr by 2030; Florida to $15 (Sept 2026); Virginia indexing.
7. **Controlled-company governance.** Hill Path ~53%; Chairman Scott Ross also chairs the Compensation Committee; board "actively involved in overseeing certain key operating activities."
8. **Management instability.** Interim CFO; six Item 5.02 executive/director-departure 8-Ks between July 2025 and January 2026; CEO comp spike to $4.89M from $1.08M amid -26% net income and lagging TSR (114.9 vs peer 154.68).

The macro backdrop compounds these. Theme-park visitation is squarely discretionary, and a levered, declining-attendance operator is poorly positioned for any consumer slowdown. [unverified — no specific macro consumer-spending data in sources]

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 187.5% against 51.7% one-year historical volatility, with the term structure in steep backwardation (187.5% one-week vs 48.7% three-month). This pricing reflects an imminent event, almost certainly options expiry/positioning dynamics around the high short interest rather than a scheduled fundamental catalyst (the next earnings report is not imminent). The one-week implied move is ±8-12%. The three-month skew of -8.2% (call-heavy) is consistent with squeeze-chasing rather than downside hedging, which reinforces the caution against shorting. The rich near-term IV makes premium-buying expensive in either direction. For a fundamental AVOID view, the read is that positioning, not fundamentals, is currently driving the tape, a reason to stay on the sidelines rather than press a directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Attendance stabilizes as Orlando and Discovery Cove premium demand offsets the weaker parks; in-park per-capita pricing power persists; the company continues buying back stock aggressively ($190.2M remaining on the $500M authorization), compressing the share count and compounding per-share FCF; covenant headroom holds and refinancing proceeds smoothly. Hill Path takes the company private at a premium, crystallizing value for minority holders. In this scenario EPS reaches ~$4.70+ and a modest re-rating toward 12-13x supports the $54 analyst high.

### Bear Case
Revenue keeps eroding as Universal's Epic Universe siphons Orlando traffic and the diluted brand and fee creep depress repeat visitation; capex cuts starve the parks of fresh attractions, accelerating the decline; a consumer slowdown hits discretionary leisure; the Sesame suit terminates the license, impairing two parks; rising rates pressure FCF on the floating balance and the 2029/2031 refinancings come at higher cost; buybacks exhaust the authorization and the per-share support disappears, exposing the shrinking operating base. Adjusted EBITDA falls toward $550M and the stock retraces toward the low-$30s/$28.77 lows.

The asymmetry favors the bear case. The bull case depends on a stabilization that four years of data contradict, while the bear case requires only a continuation of the established trend plus normal macro and competitive pressure.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: No specific, dated near-term catalyst with high downside conviction. The next earnings report (mid-quarter, not imminent) is the natural test, but Q2/Q3 are seasonally the profitable quarters where attendance comparisons and pricing power could produce a beat. The Sesame suit and refinancing walls are real but undated and likely 2027-2029 events. The structural decline is a slow grind, not an acute collapse.

**Timing window**: The thesis plays out over 12-24 months as revenue erosion compounds and buyback firepower depletes. That is too long a horizon to carry a short against meaningful squeeze and take-private risk.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: 36.2% of float short, 6.0 days to cover, ~6.97M shares short. This is dangerously high. With a controlling holder owning ~53%, the effective float available to borrow against is thin, amplifying squeeze potential. The stock has already squeezed ~60% off its March low. The +24.3% move since mid-May on rising estimates demonstrates the kind of upside that punishes crowded shorts.

**Borrow and options signal**: The call-heavy three-month skew indicates elevated borrow cost and positioning that leans against shorts, not with them. The options market is not confirming a bearish thesis; it is pricing squeeze dynamics.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: A bear case toward the low-$30s implies ~30% downside. But the squeeze/take-private upside risk is at least as large and more probable in the near term, putting the reward:risk below 2:1.

**Verdict**: **CROWDED SHORT** — the fundamental thesis is correct, but the high short interest, a controlling 53% holder, an active take-private optionality, and steep borrow costs make the remaining downside too small relative to squeeze risk. Avoid owning; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria** (what would move us off AVOID toward HOLD or constructive):
- Two consecutive quarters of positive attendance growth (year-over-year), confirming the decline has genuinely stabilized rather than being papered over by pricing.
- A clear path to positive operating per-share growth that is not driven primarily by buybacks — i.e., Adjusted EBITDA stabilizing or rising above ~$620M.
- Resolution of the Sesame license-termination suit without loss of the franchise.
- Appointment of a permanent CFO and a halt to the executive/director departure cluster, signaling organizational stabilization.
- A formal, premium take-private offer from Hill Path would change the calculus immediately (event-driven), though terms could land near current price.

**Timeline**: The competitive (Epic Universe), brand, and leverage headwinds persist for at least 12-24 months. We would reassess at each quarterly print, with particular attention to the seasonally important Q3 (summer) attendance figures.

**What would deepen the AVOID**: A covenant scare, a Sesame loss, a guidance cut, or a consumer-driven attendance step-down would push the stock toward its $28.77 low.

## Conclusion

United Parks is a structurally declining theme-park operator whose equity story has been reconstructed through private-equity-style financial engineering: lever the balance sheet, shrink the float, manufacture per-share earnings. The technique works while free cash flow holds, and it has produced rising EPS estimates and a 60% rally off the lows. Revenue has fallen four straight years and accelerated lower; attendance is shrinking; the company has missed consensus four quarters running; book equity is negative; cash is depleted; the revolver is being drawn to fund buybacks; and the governance structure concentrates control in a holder whose interests can diverge from minorities. Layered on top are an escalating license-termination lawsuit, an interim CFO amid heavy executive churn, rising minimum-wage costs, refinancing walls, and a discretionary-spending business facing a possible consumer slowdown and intensifying Orlando competition.

The stock trades above the analyst consensus mean and median, leaving little fundamental upside, and the cheap forward multiple is a value-trap signature rather than a mispricing. We rate PRKS **AVOID**. The high short interest, controlling-holder dynamics, and take-private optionality make it a crowded short rather than an active short candidate; the right action is to neither own it nor bet against it directly. The declining score trajectory (4.7 → 3.6) is confirmed, not reversed, by the latest data: the price recovery is positioning- and buyback-driven, while the operating fundamentals continue to deteriorate.
