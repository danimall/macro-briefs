## Executive Summary

Range Resources is a pure-play Appalachian natural gas and NGL producer with among the lowest operating costs in North America ($0.13/mcfe direct), 50+ years of remaining drilling inventory, and a balance sheet that has been steadily deleveraged from $2.7B net debt in 2021 to $1.2B today. The company beat Q1 2026 estimates by 19% on the back of elevated natural gas prices ($7.46/mcf NYMEX in February), and forward consensus reflects $4.29 in FY2026 EPS — putting the stock at roughly 10x forward earnings with a 5.3%+ FCF yield.

However, the investment case carries specific near-term risks that prevent a full-conviction BUY at $42.46. Natural gas prices have already rolled off their winter highs (current Henry Hub closer to $2.50-3.00/mcf for summer strip), creating a sequentially weaker earnings backdrop for Q2-Q3 relative to Q1's blowout. The 10.9% short interest (5.2 days to cover) represents meaningful institutional skepticism about the sustainability of current gas prices. Additionally, the stock sits just 12% below its 52-week high of $48.31, which was itself established during the February gas price spike — meaning the commodity tailwind is largely priced. The structural thesis (LNG demand + AI data centers + capital discipline) is real but requires sustained $3.50+ gas to translate into the earnings power consensus currently models.

## Company Overview

Range Resources operates exclusively in the Marcellus Shale of southwestern Pennsylvania, producing approximately 2.24 Bcfe/day (65% natural gas, 34% NGLs, 1% oil). The company holds ~769,000 net acres and operates 1,499 net producing wells with a 100% drilling success rate. RRC was one of the original developers of the Marcellus formation and has accumulated deep operational expertise in the basin over two decades.

The company's production profile includes a significant NGL component — approximately one-third of reserves — which provides diversification away from pure dry gas economics. RRC holds firm transportation capacity on multiple pipelines reaching the Gulf Coast, Midwest, Southeast, and Northeast markets, plus international LNG export exposure through contracted capacity.

## Growth Analysis

Production growth is deliberately modest — management targets low-single-digit annual growth (2-3%) while prioritizing free cash flow generation and capital returns. FY2026 capex guidance of $650-700M maintains this trajectory.

The growth story is primarily commodity-price-driven rather than volume-driven:
- **LNG exports:** New U.S. LNG facilities coming online in 2025-2027 structurally increase demand for U.S. natural gas over the next several years
- **AI data center power demand:** Eastern U.S. wholesale power prices up 76% due to data center load growth. Pennsylvania sits atop PJM Interconnection where this demand concentrates. New gas-fired generation is the marginal power source.
- **Capital discipline:** Industry-wide restraint in drilling keeps supply growth below demand growth, supporting prices

These tailwinds are real and multi-year in nature, but they are already partially reflected in the forward strip and analyst estimates. The key question is whether spot gas sustains above $3.50/mcf through summer 2026 or reverts toward $2.50-3.00 as winter demand fades and storage builds normalize.

Estimate revisions show positive momentum: FY2026 EPS consensus has risen 23.5% over 90 days (from $3.47 to $4.29). However, 30-day revisions for the current quarter turned negative (-8.7%), suggesting some analysts are already adjusting for summer gas price weakness.

## Competitive Landscape

RRC's competitive position within Appalachian gas is strong and improving:

1. **Cost structure:** $0.13/mcfe direct operating expense is among the lowest in the industry, providing substantial margin protection in low-price environments
2. **Inventory depth:** 27 million lateral feet of remaining drilling locations = 50+ years at current activity levels. This compares favorably to peers facing core inventory exhaustion
3. **Basin expertise:** 20+ years of Marcellus-specific operational knowledge, 100% success rate on wells drilled
4. **Market access:** Diversified pipeline portfolio reaching multiple demand centers — a material advantage in pipeline-constrained Appalachia
5. **NGL optionality:** 34% NGL reserves provide revenue diversification and higher-value liquids exposure

The main competitive risks are EQT (which is larger and potentially benefits from scale advantages in Appalachian processing/gathering) and the broader industry trend toward consolidation (Chesapeake/Southwestern merger creating Expand Energy). RRC remains a standalone operator in an increasingly consolidated industry, which creates both acquisition premium potential and strategic vulnerability.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Natural Gas Price Reversion (NEAR-TERM, HIGH PROBABILITY)**
Summer gas strip pricing suggests $2.50-3.00/mcf, which would produce materially weaker Q2-Q3 results. If summer prices stay below $3.00, the $4.29 full-year consensus may prove optimistic.

**2. Short Interest at 10.9% / 5.2 Days to Cover**
Nearly 20 million shares sold short reflects meaningful institutional skepticism. This level of short interest typically indicates either: (a) a specific bearish thesis on gas price sustainability, or (b) a sector hedge. Either way, it represents potential headwind to near-term price appreciation and confirms that sophisticated investors disagree with the bull case at current levels.

**3. $5.9B Fixed Transportation Obligations**
These ~$834M/year commitments are take-or-pay regardless of production volumes or prices. In a sustained low-price environment, they become a crushing fixed-cost burden. While they provide market access in a favorable environment, they severely limit RRC's ability to curtail activity in a downturn.

**4. Geographic/Regulatory Concentration**
100% exposure to Pennsylvania creates single-state regulatory risk. The Cecil Township expanded setback ordinance (2,500 feet from occupied structures, 5,000 feet from schools) and statewide citizen petition for similar measures (accepted by the Pennsylvania Environmental Quality Board in December 2025) represent tail risks that could materially constrain future drilling locations.

**5. Commodity Correlation / Macro**
RRC is fundamentally a leveraged bet on U.S. natural gas prices. In a recession scenario, industrial demand falls, power demand moderates, and gas prices likely retreat toward $2.00-2.50/mcf — which would compress earnings meaningfully and imply a lower stock price.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($52-59, +22-39%):**
- Natural gas prices sustain above $3.50/mcf through 2026-2027 as LNG demand structurally tightens the market
- AI data center power demand accelerates faster than expected, driving in-basin gas demand in PJM
- FY2026 EPS reaches $4.50-5.00+ on sustained pricing
- At 12x forward earnings (modest premium to current), stock reaches $54-60
- Buyback acceleration returns additional capital at attractive prices

**Bear Case ($33-37, -12-22%):**
- Summer gas prices retreat to $2.00-2.50/mcf as storage builds normalize and winter premium evaporates
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $3.00-3.50 (below current consensus of $4.29)
- Recession reduces industrial/power demand for gas
- $5.9B transportation commitments become increasingly burdensome
- Stock reverts to $33-37 (11-12x reduced earnings)

**Why not BUY at current levels:**
The specific concern preventing a BUY is the seasonality mismatch between the earnings beat that drove the stock higher and the go-forward pricing environment. Q1 2026's $1.52 EPS was produced at $5-7 average realized gas prices. Q2-Q3 will see $2.50-3.50 pricing. The 30-day revision downtick for current quarter (-8.7%) confirms analysts are already adjusting. At $42.46, the stock needs $4+ sustained gas to justify its multiple — and the probability of that through summer is low enough, combined with 10.9% short interest reflecting institutional skepticism, to create a legitimate risk that near-term earnings disappoint. This is a specific, testable concern about whether summer gas prices support the forward estimates embedded in the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $36-38 on summer gas price weakness — at that level, the risk/reward becomes asymmetric (15% downside to trough vs. 40%+ upside if gas recovers)
2. Henry Hub summer strip holds above $3.50/mcf for 30+ days, confirming the structural demand thesis is overwhelming seasonal weakness
3. Q2 2026 earnings beat estimates despite summer pricing, demonstrating NGL/hedge-related resilience

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Natural gas prices sustain below $2.50/mcf for 60+ days with storage builds materially above 5-year average
2. Pennsylvania enacts statewide setback legislation that restricts >20% of RRC's identified drilling locations
3. Management increases capex guidance above $750M without corresponding production growth, suggesting capital discipline deterioration

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely late July 2026). By then, summer gas pricing will be visible, storage trajectory will be established, and the sustainability of the forward estimate can be assessed with higher confidence.

## Conclusion

Range Resources is a well-run, low-cost Appalachian gas producer with genuine structural tailwinds (LNG + AI power demand) and an improving balance sheet. At ~10x forward earnings, the valuation is reasonable and the 5-7% FCF yield provides downside support.

However, the stock currently prices in a gas price environment ($3.50-4.00+ sustained) that faces a specific near-term headwind: summer seasonality. The Q1 blowout was driven by winter pricing that has already faded. A HOLD with growth orientation captures the thesis that 12-month returns will be positive (driven by the structural demand tailwinds eventually overwhelming seasonal weakness), while acknowledging that the next 2-3 months carry enough risk of a $36-38 pullback to make initiating at $42.46 premature. The right entry is either lower price or confirmed pricing sustainability.
