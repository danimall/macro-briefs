## Executive Summary

Avanos Medical is a specialty medical device company operating in enteral feeding systems and pain management solutions. The stock is trading at $24.80, effectively at the $25.00/share cash acquisition price offered by American Industrial Partners (AIP), announced April 14, 2026. The deal represents a 72% premium to the prior day's close and is expected to close in H2 2026 with no financing condition.

At current levels, this is a pure merger-arbitrage situation with approximately $0.20 (0.8%) of upside to deal completion against approximately $11 (44%) of downside to pre-announcement fundamental value if the deal breaks. The annualized return of 1.5-3% (depending on exact close date) does not adequately compensate for the binary tail risk. For investors not running dedicated merger-arbitrage strategies with portfolio-level diversification, the risk/reward is unfavorable.

The underlying business shows mixed fundamentals: segment revenue growth of 9% in Specialty Nutrition partially offset by GAAP losses, declining adjusted operating profit ($67M vs $87M prior year), $77M in goodwill impairments, and structural tariff exposure from Mexico-based manufacturing. These factors define both AIP's interest (addressable without quarterly earnings pressure) and the downside if the deal fails.

## Company Overview

Avanos Medical, headquartered in Alpharetta, Georgia, operates in two segments:

**Specialty Nutrition Systems (SNS)** — 62% of segment revenue ($433M in FY2025): MIC-KEY enteral feeding tubes, Corpak patient feeding solutions, and NeoMed neonatal feeding systems. This is a stable, recurring-revenue business (patients require ongoing tube replacements).

**Pain Management & Recovery (PM&R)** — 35% of segment revenue ($238M in FY2025): ON-Q surgical pain pumps, COOLIEF radiofrequency ablation for chronic pain, and Game Ready cold therapy. The RFA business benefits from the opioid reduction trend but faces Medicare reimbursement uncertainty.

The company employs 2,287 people, with 57.9% in Mexico-based manufacturing facilities. Distribution is heavily intermediated — Medline Industries accounts for ~12% of net sales.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Total FY2025 revenue was $701M, down from $820M in FY2022 due to the Respiratory Health divestiture ($110M sale in Oct 2023) and portfolio rationalization. Core segment revenue grew from $599M (2023) to $671M (2025), a 12% increase over two years.

**Profitability:** GAAP net loss of $72.9M in FY2025, driven by a $77M goodwill impairment on the PM&R unit. Adjusted operating profit declined from $87.3M to $67.1M, a 23% decline that partially reflects $32.4M in restructuring charges and tariff-related margin compression. Gross margin contracted from 55.4% to 50.5%.

**Balance Sheet:** Cash of $89.8M, long-term debt of $90.3M ($375M revolving facility largely undrawn), stockholders' equity of $778.2M. Debt/equity of 0.38x is conservative. Current ratio at 2.15x. Altman Z-Score of 3.46 indicates no near-term distress. Goodwill of $395M remains after $514M in cumulative impairments.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $74.7M (down from $101M in FY2024). Free cash flow approximately $43M after $31.6M in capex. FCF was deployed to the Nexus acquisition ($28M), debt repayment ($34M), and minority investments.

**2026 Guidance (now academic given the deal):** Revenue $700-720M, adjusted diluted EPS $0.90-$1.10.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are limited given the pending acquisition. For fundamental analysis purposes:

- SNS grew 9.2% YoY in FY2025, with enteral feeding up 8.6% and neonatal solutions up 10.8%
- RFA grew 10.1% — a strong pain management category benefiting from opioid alternative demand
- Surgical pain declined 8.5% due to deliberate product line exits
- The Nexus TKO acquisition ($27M) expands into adjacent vascular access market
- FY2026 guidance of $700-720M implies flat to modest growth, weighed down by the "China exit" strategy for tariff mitigation

Growth is structurally capped at mid-single digits for this portfolio absent M&A. The private equity owner will likely pursue operational efficiency over revenue acceleration.

The valuation score reflects the asymmetry problem: 0.8% upside vs. 44% downside to fundamental value. This is not an investment opportunity for equity investors without dedicated merger-arb infrastructure. ## Competitive Landscape

Avanos holds defensible positions in niche medical device categories:

- **Enteral feeding:** Leading market share with MIC-KEY and Corpak brands, strong clinical relationships, and recurring revenue profile
- **RFA pain management:** COOLIEF has built a clinical evidence base supporting radiofrequency ablation as an opioid alternative
- **NeoMed:** Growing franchise in neonatal/pediatric feeding with specialized products

Competitive threats come from Boston Scientific, Medtronic, Stryker, and Pacira in pain management. The key structural risk is Medicare Administrative Contractors (MACs) drafting policies questioning RFA reimbursement — a negative final determination could materially impair the PM&R segment.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal break risk (primary):** The transaction has no financing condition, unanimous Board approval, and is in a niche segment unlikely to attract antitrust scrutiny. HSR clearance, stockholder vote, and general regulatory approval remain outstanding. Historical medtech PE buyout completion rates exceed 90%, but the consequences of failure are severe (stock returns to ~$13-14).

**Timeline risk:** "H2 2026" is vague — if the close extends to Q4 2026, the annualized return on the $0.20 spread becomes negligible. Any regulatory delays compound this.

**Superior proposal risk:** The go-shop period has likely expired. Strategic acquirers (Medtronic, Boston Scientific) passed during the process, given the announcement language.

**Tariff impact on standalone value:** If the deal breaks, the company faces ongoing tariff headwinds from Mexico manufacturing concentration (57.9% of employees) and Chinese-sourced raw materials.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$25.00, +0.8%)
The deal closes on schedule in H2 2026. Shareholders receive $25.00 in cash. Returns depend entirely on timing: earlier close = better annualized return.

### Bear Case (~$13-14, -44% to -47%)
The deal breaks due to regulatory intervention, stockholder rejection, or material adverse change. The stock reverts to pre-announcement fundamental value, reflecting: declining adjusted operating margins, tariff headwinds, Medicare RFA reimbursement risk, and limited organic growth. At $13-14, the stock would trade at roughly 1x revenue and 7-8x adjusted EBITDA — consistent with a mid-quality medtech company with execution challenges.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Deal break — if HSR review identifies competitive concerns, stockholder vote fails, or MAC final determination on RFA reimbursement constitutes a material adverse change under the merger agreement. These are low-probability events but would be high-magnitude.

**Timing window:** H2 2026 close expected. HSR review typically 30-60 days from filing. No specific dated catalyst for a break.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 5.8% short interest with 1.2 days to cover. Uncrowded. Institutional ownership is likely concentrated in merger-arb funds that would sell rapidly on any deal-break signal, reducing squeeze risk.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options market is essentially dead (30 contracts). Put/call skew elevated at 172.7% on near-term suggests some protection demand. IV at 9.2% vs. 64.8% HV means puts are extremely cheap relative to the implied volatility of the standalone business — if the deal breaks, IV would explode and puts would multiply in value.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Downside to ~$13-14 on deal break (44% decline) vs. upside of $0.20 (0.8%). The ratio is extremely favorable for shorts IF you believe the deal will break. However, deal-break probability is likely <10%, making the expected value: 10% × $11 downside = $1.10 expected gain vs. 90% × $0.20 lost on cover = $0.18 expected loss. Expected value is positive but only if deal-break probability exceeds ~2%.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis requires a deal break that has <10% probability. While the risk/reward on a per-outcome basis is exceptional (55:1 on the event), the probability-weighted expected value requires conviction in a specific break mechanism. No identified catalyst with sufficient probability to warrant active shorting. The correct positioning for non-arb investors is to avoid owning the stock.

## Conclusion

Avanos Medical at $24.80 is a merger-arbitrage position with $0.20 of gross upside (0.8%) against $11 of downside (44%) if the deal breaks. The acquisition by American Industrial Partners at $25.00/share appears highly likely to close given no financing condition, unanimous Board approval, and limited antitrust overlap in niche medtech categories. The underlying business has defensible market positions in enteral feeding and RFA pain management but faces structural challenges (margin compression, tariff exposure, CEO transition, Medicare reimbursement uncertainty) that explain why AIP could acquire the company at a significant discount to its 2021-2022 trading range.

For equity investors, there is no risk-adjusted opportunity here. The stock is priced for deal completion. AVOID.
