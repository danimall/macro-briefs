**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Concentra is the largest provider of occupational health services in the United States, operating 632 stand-alone occupational health centers across 41 states and 411 onsite employer clinics across 45 states as of March 31, 2026. The business is a scaled B2B healthcare platform serving employers (not consumers), with revenue anchored in workers' compensation care governed by state fee schedules. Operationally the company is in good shape: Q1 2026 revenue grew 13.7% year-over-year to $569.6M, adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 21.2%, and net leverage fell to 3.4x against a 6.5x covenant.

The stock's price action diverges from the trailing headline metrics. Shares have recovered roughly 60% from a November 2025 low near $19 to $31.61, propelled partly by fundamentals (a Q1 beat, continuing margin expansion) and partly by a mechanical June 2026 Russell reclassification into the "Growth-Defensive" and "Defensive" indexes that triggered index-fund buying and a ~7% pop. At $31.61 the stock trades at ~23x trailing earnings and ~21x the $1.51 current-year consensus EPS, a full multiple for a mid-single-digit-to-low-teens grower whose near-term estimates have been drifting lower (current-quarter EPS revised down 4.5% over 90 days).

We move from BUY to a growth-oriented HOLD. The operating business is sound and the onsite-clinic segment (revenue +125% year-over-year) offers a genuine diversification and growth vector. But the valuation has re-rated ahead of the fundamentals, an unresolved cluster of physical-therapy False Claims Act matters sits without any disclosed reserve, and legacy Select Medical holders are selling steadily into the rally with no offsetting open-market insider buying. The risk/reward at current price is neutral. We would become buyers again on a pullback toward the mid-$20s or on resolution of the litigation overhang.

## Company Overview

Concentra was carved out of Select Medical (SEM) through a July 2024 IPO, remained a controlled subsidiary until Select's special distribution of ~104M shares in November 2024, and has operated as a fully independent company since. It still relies on Select under a transition services agreement, though TSA fees are winding down ($1.6M in Q1'26 vs. $3.7M in Q1'25) as it stands up its own back office.

The company runs three operating segments aggregated into one reportable unit:

- **Occupational health centers (91% of Q1'26 revenue):** workers' compensation care, employer services (drug/alcohol screens, physicals, clinical testing), and consumer/urgent care.
- **Onsite health clinics (7%, up from 3% a year ago):** clinics operated at employer worksites, the fastest-growing segment, driven by the June 2025 Pivot Onsite Innovations acquisition.
- **Other businesses (2%):** Concentra Telemed, Pharmacy, and Medical Compliance Administration.

Within the occupational health centers, workers' compensation is roughly 65% of visit revenue but only ~46% of visits, reflecting a higher revenue-per-visit ($213 vs. $97 for employer services). That mix matters: workers' comp reimbursement is set by state fee schedules, which gives Concentra rate visibility but also exposes it to regulatory rate-setting and to the secular decline in workplace injury frequency as workplaces get safer and more automated.

The competitive advantage is scale and national density. In a fragmented occupational health market, Concentra's footprint lets it serve large multi-state employers as a single vendor, and it consolidates smaller platforms (Nova, Pivot) alongside de novo center openings.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 was strong and showed accelerating operating momentum:**

- Revenue $569.6M, +13.7% YoY (from $500.8M), driven by organic volume (patient visits +6.7%, visits-per-day +6.7%), revenue-per-visit +3.1% to $151.47, and the Nova/Pivot acquisitions.
- Adjusted EBITDA $120.7M at a 21.2% margin (up from 20.5%), with cost of services falling to 70.1% of revenue from 71.3% on staffing efficiencies.
- Net income $52.3M; GAAP EPS $0.39, adjusted EPS $0.40. Effective tax rate ~24.9%.

**Balance sheet — the defining and most misunderstood feature.** Total debt is ~$1.60B: $650M of 6.875% senior notes due 2032, a $940.5M variable-rate SOFR-linked term loan, and minor other debt. This debt originated as a return of capital to Select at separation. Stockholders' equity is only $424.7M against $2.44B of liabilities, which is why standardized screens produce an alarming ~6x debt/equity, a ~44% ROE, and an Altman Z-Score in the "distress" zone (~1.1).

These screen values are artifacts of a leveraged spinoff, not signs of operational stress. The leveraged separation left a thin equity base, which mechanically inflates both leverage ratios and ROE. Operationally the company is comfortable: net leverage is 3.4x against a 6.5x covenant, interest expense of $26.0M/quarter is covered ~3.7x by ~$95.6M of quarterly operating income, and there is $434M available on the revolver plus $61.7M cash. Interest-rate exposure is partially hedged: swaps/collars fix ~$300M of the term loan through February 2028, leaving roughly $640M of the floating-rate loan unhedged (SOFR was 3.66% at quarter-end).

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Q1'26 operating cash flow was $21.0M (up from $11.7M), a seasonally working-capital-heavy quarter (AR grew $38.6M). Capex is modest (~$11M/quarter), consistent with an asset-light model relative to hospitals. Management is returning capital while delevering: a $100M buyback was authorized in November 2025, of which $15M was executed in Q1'26 (~0.66M shares at $22.68, leaving $65M), and the quarterly dividend of $0.0625/share (~0.8% yield) was maintained. FY2025 net income was $172.8M (roughly flat vs. $171.9M in 2024) on adjusted EBITDA of $431.9M (up from $376.9M), with EBITDA growth outpacing net income because of interest drag and separation costs.

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in near-term estimates: three beats and one small miss over the last four quarters, with the two most recent quarters beating by 17.0% and 22.1%. The beats have been against relatively low bars, and consensus revisions have been drifting lower on the near quarters.

## Growth Analysis

Concentra offers two distinct growth vectors. The core occupational health business is a mid-single-digit organic grower (visits +6.7%, revenue-per-visit +3.1%) supplemented by roll-up M&A and de novo openings (new centers announced near Kansas City International Airport and in Arizona in July 2026). This is durable but not explosive growth, and it depends on workers' comp fee schedules and injury frequency, both of which face secular headwinds.

The stronger engine is the onsite-clinic segment. Onsite revenue rose ~125% YoY to $37.2M in Q1'26 (from $16.6M), supercharged by the June 2025 Pivot acquisition ($55.2M, adding 240+ onsite clinics). If Concentra can scale direct-to-employer/onsite advanced primary care, it diversifies away from workers'-comp-fee-schedule dependence and taps a structurally growing employer trend. This is the segment we would weight most heavily in the growth thesis.

Estimate revisions temper the enthusiasm on the near-term picture. Current-quarter EPS has been revised down 4.5% over 90 days (to $0.42) and next-quarter down 5.5%, while full-year 2026 has edged up 2.1% (to $1.51) and 2027 up 1.7% (to $1.69). The picture is mixed: falling quarterly estimates against modestly rising annual estimates, which reads as near-term softness inside an intact multi-year growth story. This is not the >30% revision velocity that flags a fundamental inflection; the estimate momentum is neutral-to-slightly-negative on the near quarters, which argues against treating rising estimates as a BUY signal here.

Applying the durability lens: occupational health is a defensive niche tied to employment levels rather than discretionary spending, and the growth thesis rests on a single company (no broad sector confirmation), so we treat the growth as solid but company-specific, not thematically reinforced.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.61 the stock trades at ~23x trailing EPS ($1.28) and ~21x the $1.51 current-year consensus. Against a business growing revenue in the low-to-mid teens (partly acquisition-driven) with EPS growth to ~$1.69 in 2027 (implying ~19x on next-year), the multiple is full but not egregious. The PEG on the low-double-digit forward EPS growth path sits near or above 1.5, which does not offer a compelling asymmetry.

The peer set is heterogeneous and not directly comparable. Health Care Services peers by market cap (VCYT 43.9x, SHC 40.1x, CRVL 28.4x, PRVA 137.8x, RDNT negative) skew toward higher-growth diagnostics and physician-enablement names with a peer median P/E of 43.9x. On that basis Concentra looks cheap, but the comparison is misleading: those peers carry either faster growth or different capital structures, and CON's lower multiple appropriately reflects its slower core growth, its fee-schedule reimbursement exposure, and its leverage. We do not read the peer discount as a mispricing.

Our valuation score has fallen from 7.5 in May to 5.5 in June, and we cut it further to 4.5 now. That is not a reaction to the stock going down — it went up ~60%. The price appreciated while the near-term estimate picture softened, so the valuation cushion has eroded. In May the stock was a cheap re-rating candidate at ~$23; today at $31.61 much of that re-rating has happened, and part of it was mechanical (the Russell index reclassification and associated fund buying rather than fundamental catalysts).

The analyst target set — low $28, mean $33.375, median $34, high $37 — places the current price only ~5% below the mean and ~7% below the median. That is a narrow margin, and the stock is not the kind of cheap, high-revision-velocity name where targets act as floors that get chased upward. Here estimates are drifting lower on the near quarters and the multiple is already full, so we treat the consensus targets as a fair band rather than a floor. The low target of $28 implies ~11% downside, and the current price sits within striking distance of the 52-week high of $32.35.

We do not treat prior appreciation itself as a valuation input. The reason for the low score is that current price divided by current/forward fundamentals now leaves little asymmetric upside, and cheapness — where it exists relative to peers — lacks an independent near-term catalyst to unlock it. Cheap-looking multiples without a catalyst have historically been value-trap warnings, not buy signals; here the multiple is not even cheap on the company's own growth rate.

## Competitive Landscape

Concentra is the clear #1 in a fragmented occupational health market, competing against other occ-health centers, onsite clinic operators, urgent-care chains, and general providers. Its moat is national density: the ability to serve large multi-state employers as a single vendor is genuinely hard to replicate, and it provides a roll-up runway for consolidating small operators.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin. The onsite-clinic footprint is expanding rapidly and diversifying the revenue base, gross margins are expanding (cost of services down 120bp YoY), and de novo openings continue. The Russell reclassification to "Growth-Defensive" reflects the market's view of the niche as relatively non-cyclical, tied to employment levels rather than discretionary spending.

The offsetting secular concern is real: workplace injury frequency has been in long-term decline as workplaces get safer and more automated, which pressures workers' comp visit volume, Concentra's highest revenue-per-visit line. The pricing side is also constrained by state fee schedules that Concentra does not control. The onsite/direct-to-employer pivot is the strategic answer to both, and its early traction is the most encouraging competitive signal. On balance the position is stable-to-improving, which we score modestly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

**Physical-therapy False Claims Act cluster — the standout idiosyncratic risk.** Three overlapping matters center on PT billing integrity: a DOJ/HHS FCA investigation ongoing since 2021 (inherited from Select, with the related Kane qui tam unsealed in May 2024 after DOJ declined to intervene); a February 2024 California Department of Insurance subpoena regarding PT billing/coding; and a newly-unsealed November 2025 California whistleblower qui tam alleging that PT referral guidelines produced false claims and a retaliatory termination. The November 2025 matter is the freshest and most directly targets Concentra's own referral practices. No reserve or estimate has been disclosed. Outcomes are unpredictable and could produce recoupments, fines, or settlements. This is the primary tail risk and is not clearly priced into a full multiple.

**Leverage and floating-rate exposure.** The ~$640M unhedged floating-rate term loan means a higher-for-longer rate path or a covenant-tightening downturn would pressure free cash flow. Coverage is comfortable today (~3.7x), but the thin equity base leaves less room for error than at a lower-levered peer.

**Reimbursement and volume.** Revenue-per-visit depends on state workers' comp fee schedules; adverse changes hit the highest-margin line directly. Declining injury frequency is a slow-moving secular headwind to workers' comp volume.

**Labor cost inflation.** Personnel was $322M of $449M in segment expense in Q1'26. Clinician shortages and wage inflation could reverse the margin expansion that just occurred.

**Acquisition integration.** Growth leans on M&A; Pivot purchase accounting remains preliminary and could see measurement-period adjustments.

**Governance and ownership transition.** Board chairman Robert Ortenzio (Select co-founder) has sold 130,000 shares in each of May, June, and July 2026 into the rally (at $25.00, $25.00, and $30.50), on top of a 100,000-share gift in November 2025. These appear programmatic rather than a fundamental signal, but the steady one-way selling combined with recurring Item 5.02 officer/director departures (four since mid-2025, including July 10, 2026) points to an unsettled post-spinoff transition. There is no offsetting open-market insider buying.

**PJ&A data-breach litigation** — a consolidated class action (~4M patients notified) that the company believes is immaterial and covered by cyber insurance.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.0% sits rich to 26.8% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 39.0% vs. six-month 34.8%), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress consistent with the August 6, 2026 Q2 earnings date. The put/call skew of -23.0% at the front month is call-heavy, indicating positioning is not defensively bearish despite the run-up. IV has fallen from 45.9% to 39.0% since mid-June even as the stock rose 18.8%, reflecting some resolution of uncertainty into the rally. The net read: the options market is pricing a large move (±12.8% implied) around the next print but is not signaling directional pessimism, which neither confirms nor contradicts our neutral fundamental stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$37–40):** The onsite-clinic pivot compounds at high rates, diversifying revenue away from workers' comp fee schedules and re-rating the business toward a growth multiple. Margin expansion continues as staffing efficiencies and back-office insourcing offset labor inflation, driving EPS above the $1.69 2027 consensus. Continued delevering, buybacks, and the "Growth-Defensive" index positioning attract further institutional flow. The PT-billing matters resolve without material financial impact. In this scenario ~24x on ~$1.85+ of normalized EPS supports the high end of the analyst range around $37 and beyond.

**Bear Case (~$24–26):** The PT-billing FCA cluster produces a material settlement or recoupment, forcing a disclosed reserve and compressing the multiple. Workers' comp volume softens on declining injury frequency and any employment weakness in an H2 2026 consumer/labor slowdown, while labor inflation reverses the recent margin gains. Floating-rate interest expense stays elevated. Estimate revisions continue to drift lower, and the stock de-rates toward the low analyst target of $28 or below to the mid-$20s, roughly where legacy holders have been selling. This implies ~15–24% downside from current levels.

The base case sits between: mid-single-digit-to-low-teens revenue growth, EPS reaching ~$1.65–1.70 in 2027, and a multiple that holds near 20x, supporting a fair value in the low-to-mid $30s, close to where the stock trades today. That is the core of the HOLD: the price already reflects the reasonable base case, leaving upside contingent on the bull scenario and meaningful downside if the litigation or labor risks crystallize.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD with a 12-month reassessment horizon anchored to the August 6, 2026 Q2 print and the litigation calendar.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$20s (~$24–26) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring valuation asymmetry — a lower price on an intact operating story would be a stronger buy case, not a weaker one.
- Resolution of the PT-billing FCA cluster without a material financial impact, removing the primary tail risk.
- A demonstrated re-acceleration in the onsite segment (continued 50%+ growth) alongside positive estimate revisions across both quarterly and annual lines, converting neutral estimate momentum into a genuine inflection.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A disclosed material reserve or adverse settlement on any of the three PT-billing matters.
- Margin reversal — adjusted EBITDA margin falling back below ~20% on labor-cost inflation.
- A break of net leverage above ~4.0x, or evidence the floating-rate interest drag is materially impairing free cash flow.
- Accelerating negative estimate revisions (annual EPS turning down) combined with slowing organic visit growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release on August 6, 2026, followed by any developments on the California whistleblower qui tam.

## Conclusion

Concentra is a well-run market leader with a healthy operating profile, genuine margin momentum, and a promising onsite-clinic growth vector, wrapped in a leveraged-spinoff capital structure that screens far worse than the underlying business warrants. Correcting that misread is what justified the earlier BUY at ~$23. At $31.61, after a ~60% run partly driven by a mechanical index reclassification, the valuation has caught up to the fundamentals, near-term estimates are drifting lower, and the physical-therapy FCA cluster sits without a disclosed reserve. With legacy holders selling steadily and no open-market insider buying to offset them, the risk/reward is now balanced rather than favorable.

We rate CON a HOLD (growth_hold), overall score 5.65. We would re-engage on the buy side either on a pullback toward the mid-$20s or on resolution of the litigation overhang, and we would step to the sidelines entirely if the PT-billing matters produce a material reserve or if margins reverse.
