**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Millicom (operating brand TIGO) is the leading fixed-and-mobile telecom operator focused exclusively on Latin America, and it is in the middle of the most aggressive acquisition campaign in its history. Over the past 15 months the company has consolidated Tigo UNE/EPM, acquired Telefónica's controlling stake in Colombia's Coltel and then the Colombian government's remaining 32.5%, added Telefónica Chile (held as an associate under an NJJ-led turnaround), and entered Ecuador and Uruguay. Customers grew from ~52M at year-end 2025 to >69M by Q1 2026; homes passed rose from ~14M to >22M. Reported Q1 2026 revenue jumped 45.1% YoY to $1,985M. Organic revenue growth, however, was only +4.2%, and net profit attributable to owners fell 43.4% to $109M as integration, severance, higher interest, and minority dilution flowed through.

The stock reflects a market that has decided the consolidation story works. TIGO has appreciated roughly 138% from its June 2025 low near $36 to ~$89, now near the 52-week high of $89.49. At ~12x current-year EPS with a freshly approved $3.00/share dividend (~3.5% yield) and a buyback plan, the equity is no longer the deep-value situation it was a year ago. Estimate revisions are bifurcated: current-year and next-year EPS estimates are rising sharply (+38% and +51% over 90 days as analysts incorporate acquired earnings), while near-term quarterly estimates are being cut (-23% and -20% over 90 days as the cost of integration becomes clearer). The earnings record is volatile, with three misses in the last four quarters, including a 40% miss in Q1 2026.

This is a HOLD. The consolidator thesis has real structural backing (Telefónica's regional retreat creates a multi-year acquisition pipeline) and the NJJ/Xavier Niel ownership brings a disciplined operator playbook. But the equity has already re-rated to price much of that optimism, leverage sits at 2.76x with refinancing activity ongoing, EM/FX exposure is structural, and the near-term earnings trajectory is being revised down even as the full-year number rises on acquired scale. We see roughly balanced risk/reward at ~$89 and want confirmation that integration converts to the guided ≥$900M equity free cash flow and deleveraging toward ~2.5x before underwriting further upside.

## Company Overview

Millicom provides mobile, fiber/cable broadband, pay-TV (TIGO ONEtv), mobile financial services (TIGO Money), local content (TIGO Sports), and B2B services (Tigo Business) across Latin America. It is Luxembourg-domiciled with principal executive offices in Doral, Florida, and trades on Nasdaq. As a foreign private issuer it files a Form 20-F annual report and 6-K interim reports rather than 10-K/10-Q/Form 4 documents, which limits standardized insider-transaction and XBRL visibility.

Core markets span Central America (Guatemala, Honduras via JV, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama) and South America (Colombia, Bolivia, Paraguay, plus newly added Ecuador, Uruguay, and the Chile associate). The competitive model rests on incumbent or leading scale in several smaller LatAm markets, a fixed-mobile convergence (bundling) strategy designed to lift ARPU and reduce churn, and a vertically integrated digital ecosystem combining connectivity with payments, sports content, and TV.

The strategic posture is that of a consolidator buying assets that Telefónica and other multinationals are divesting as they retreat from Latin America. The company is acquiring scale rather than generating fast organic growth, and the investment question is whether management can integrate a transformational set of simultaneous deals and convert that scale into free cash flow and margin.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (record year):** Revenue ~$5.8B, net profit ~$1.3B, equity free cash flow (EFCF) $916M. That is a strong base year and the foundation for the bull case.

**Q1 2026 (the M&A surge):**
- Revenue $1,985M vs $1,368M, +45.1% reported but **+4.2% organic**.
- Service revenue $1,857M, +45.2% reported / +4.9% organic.
- Adjusted EBITDA $857M, +35.5% reported / +9.6% organic; margin 43.2%, with ~$119M of EBITDA from acquisitions and the margin absorbing Coltel integration/restructuring costs.
- Operating profit $416M, essentially flat YoY (-1.2%).
- Net profit to owners $109M vs $193M, **-43.4%**.
- Capex $193M, +46.0%.
- EFCF (ex-disposals) $225M, +66.5%.

The gap between reported and organic growth is the single most important framing point. Top-line and operational metrics look spectacular because the company bought them; the underlying demand profile is a modest ~4-5%. Net profit is moving the wrong way while revenue soars: integration costs, severance (~$26M Colombia voluntary retirement, ~$21M Chile severance in April 2026 alone), higher interest expense, and minority dilution are compressing the bottom line. Adjusted EBITDA margin of 43.2% remains healthy for a LatAm operator, and the +9.6% organic EBITDA growth outpacing +4.2% organic revenue indicates genuine operating leverage in the legacy business.

**Leverage and capital structure:** Net leverage rose to 2.76x post-acquisitions, with management guiding to ~2.5x by year-end 2026 and EFCF of at least $900M for 2026. The company is actively reshuffling debt: reopened 7.375% 2032 senior notes (+$87.5M), redeemed Paraguay 5.875% 2027 notes (~$139.7M), and arranged Coltel bridge facilities with Santander/HSBC/JPM. For a telecom consolidator, 2.76x is elevated but not alarming; the concern is the combination of rising leverage, a USD-denominated dividend, and revenue in volatile LatAm currencies.

**Capital return:** The May 20, 2026 AGM approved a $3.00/share dividend (paid in four quarterly installments Jul 2026–Apr 2027) plus a share repurchase plan, yielding ~3.4% at ~$89. Management is signaling that the guided EFCF comfortably funds returns. Approving a new dividend and buyback while carrying 2.76x leverage and absorbing integration costs is a deliberate balancing act, supportable if EFCF guidance holds and vulnerable if integration slips or FX deteriorates.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth metrics are dominated by acquisitions and will remain so through 2026 as the Colombia, Ecuador, Uruguay, and Chile assets fully consolidate. Current-year EPS estimates rose to $5.22 and next-year to $6.54 as analysts add acquired earnings; simultaneously, the near-term current-quarter estimate was cut to $0.62 and next-quarter to $0.80 as the in-year cost of integration becomes clearer. Rising full-year numbers built on falling quarterly numbers is what an acquisition-funded scale-up looks like, and it is why the headline full-year revision should not be read as a classic earnings-inflection BUY signal. The full-year revisions reflect M&A accretion arithmetic, not an organic demand acceleration, and the quarterly cuts show the underlying business is being revised down.

The durable growth drivers are: (1) prepaid-to-postpaid migration lifting ARPU; (2) convergence bundling reducing churn; (3) Colombia 5G expansion (management plans 4x coverage); and (4) the Chile turnaround under NJJ. The M&A pipeline remains active: CEO Benitez flagged Peru and Venezuela as potential acquisition targets, though with "no immediate plans." Telefónica's regional exit creates a steady supply of assets for a willing, disciplined buyer.

The risk is that organic growth of 4-5% is not, on its own, enough to justify the re-rated multiple if M&A pauses. The equity story depends on management continuing to find accretive deals and proving it can integrate them into rising EFCF rather than rising leverage and restructuring charges.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$89, TIGO trades at ~12x current-year EPS ($7.83 trailing EPS implies ~11.3x; consensus current-year $5.22 implies ~17x on a more conservative footing, though the reported trailing figure includes one-time items). The analyst price target distribution is wide: low $52.4, mean $83.2, median $85.0, high $100.0. The stock at ~$89 now sits above the mean and median targets and near the high: the consensus has not yet caught up to the current price, and the stock is no longer trading at a discount to where analysts think it belongs.

For an emerging-market telecom carrying 2.76x leverage and structural FX risk, a low-double-digit P/E with a ~3.4% yield is a reasonable but not compelling multiple. The stock was cheap near $36 a year ago and the rally has largely closed that gap. We do not penalize the move itself; the appreciation tracked genuine improvement in scale, EFCF, and the strategic position. The valuation question is price versus value today, and at ~$89 the equity prices in successful integration and continued deleveraging. The wide analyst dispersion (low target ~41% below current price) reflects the uncertainty we see: the bears flag overvaluation and integration risk, the bulls cite the cash-generation and consolidation optionality.

We do not treat consensus targets as a floor here because this is not a cheap-relative-to-rising-estimates setup; the rising full-year estimates are M&A arithmetic, and the near-term quarterly estimates are falling. On a sum-of-parts and forward-EFCF basis the stock is roughly fairly valued. A 5.5 valuation score reflects fair, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Millicom holds incumbent or leading positions in several smaller LatAm markets and is improving its competitive standing through consolidation. Acquiring Coltel gives it national scale in Colombia; the Chile associate adds a turnaround opportunity in a larger market. The convergence strategy (fixed + mobile + financial services + content) is a genuine moat-builder in markets where bundling raises switching costs. The organic EBITDA growth outpacing organic revenue indicates pricing and cost discipline in the legacy footprint.

The trajectory is improving on scale and share, which supports a 6.5 competitive score. The structural threats management itself names are real and worth pricing: satellite providers (Starlink-type low-earth-orbit broadband) directly target the rural and underserved connectivity segments where LatAm operators have historically enjoyed pricing power, and global digital platforms compress traditional telecom value. These are not unique to Millicom, but in markets with lower fixed-line penetration, satellite substitution is a more credible medium-term risk than in developed markets. The consolidator model is a defense against this, since scale and capital intensity are how a regional operator defends share, but it requires sustained investment and rising leverage.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration/execution risk.** TIGO is digesting an unusually large set of simultaneous acquisitions. The 43% YoY drop in net profit is the near-term, tangible cost. If integration of Coltel and the Chile turnaround slips, the EFCF guidance that funds the dividend and buyback comes under pressure.

**Leverage and refinancing.** Net leverage at 2.76x in a rising-rate, volatile-FX environment, with a USD-denominated dividend and ongoing debt reshuffling, is the central balance-sheet risk. The path to ~2.5x by year-end is achievable but depends on EFCF delivery and FX stability.

**Foreign-exchange / emerging-market.** Revenue across COP, CLP, PYG and other volatile currencies against USD debt and dividend is structural and material. A LatAm currency shock would hit reported earnings and the dividend's coverage simultaneously.

**Country/political and regulatory.** Buying out the Colombian government's 32.5% Coltel stake underscores direct sovereign-counterparty exposure. Operating across many smaller jurisdictions exposes the company to intervention and regulatory shifts.

**Earnings visibility.** Three misses in the last four quarters (Q1 2026 -40.2%, Q2 2025 -37.1%, Q3 2025 -6.8%) against one large beat (Q4 2025 +55.8%) indicate volatile, M&A-distorted quarterly earnings and reduce confidence in near-term consensus.

**Governance.** The NJJ/Xavier Niel anchor ownership is both the bull thesis (disciplined operator value creation, capital-return focus) and the governance risk (concentrated control, minority-interest and related-party dynamics). The "NJJ leading Chile" language confirms the controlling-shareholder playbook is now driving strategy.

These idiosyncratic risks (integration, leverage, FX, sovereign exposure) are partially but not fully priced at ~$89 near the 52-week high, which is why we assign a below-neutral 4.5 to risk factors.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 52.6% is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 35.7% (a 16.9-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (52.6% near-term vs 49.0% at six months), which typically signals an expected near-term catalyst or stress. The nearest expiry implies a ±8.9% move. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.3%) and put/call ratios are low (volume 0.52, OI 0.28), so the elevated IV reflects two-sided event uncertainty rather than a directional bearish bet. Short interest is modest at 4.0% of float (2.8 days to cover), so neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup is in play. The market expects volatility around the next data point but is not positioned directionally, consistent with our balanced HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Millicom is the primary beneficiary of Telefónica's structural retreat from Latin America, assembling national-scale assets at attractive prices under a disciplined, capital-return-focused NJJ playbook. As integration costs roll off and acquired assets stabilize, EFCF compounds toward and beyond the ≥$900M guide, leverage falls to ~2.5x, and the convergence/5G/Chile-turnaround levers lift ARPU and margin. The dividend grows, the buyback shrinks the share count, and consensus targets revise upward as the full-year revision velocity continues. Multiple re-rates toward the $100 high target as the consolidation thesis is validated. Bull case ~$100 (+12%).

**Bear case.** The growth is bought, not earned. Organic growth of 4-5% does not justify the re-rated multiple if the M&A pace pauses or integration disappoints. Net profit continues falling as restructuring and interest costs compound; the three-of-four-quarter miss streak persists; LatAm FX deteriorates, simultaneously hitting reported earnings and the USD dividend's coverage at 2.76x leverage. Satellite competition erodes pricing power in rural segments. The stock, near its 52-week high and above mean/median analyst targets, de-rates toward the low target of $52.4 (-41%) as the market reprices integration and EM risk.

The gap between the $100 high and $52.4 low target is a ~2:1 downside-to-upside skew from current levels, indicating the market has not converged on a view.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected August) is the key checkpoint: it will show whether organic growth is holding, whether integration costs are peaking, and whether EFCF and deleveraging are tracking guidance. The four quarterly dividend installments (Jul 2026–Apr 2027) provide ongoing coverage signals.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 prints confirming the ≥$900M EFCF run-rate and net leverage trending to ~2.5x, with net profit recovering as restructuring rolls off.
- Organic service-revenue growth sustaining ≥5% with ARPU expansion from convergence/postpaid migration, demonstrating the acquired scale is converting to durable cash flow rather than just consolidated revenue.
- A pullback toward the mid-$60s to low-$70s that restores a clear discount to forward EFCF.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net leverage rising above ~3.0x rather than falling, or a dividend/buyback reduction signaling EFCF stress.
- A material LatAm FX shock (COP/CLP/PYG) impairing reported earnings and dividend coverage.
- Continued quarterly misses with organic growth decelerating below ~3%, indicating the acquired assets are not stabilizing.

## Conclusion

Millicom is executing a coherent, structurally backed consolidation of Latin American telecom assets, and FY2025's record cash generation gives the strategy credibility. But the equity has already re-rated sharply off its low to near its 52-week high and above mean analyst targets, the headline growth is acquisition-driven while organic growth runs a modest 4-5%, net profit is contracting on integration costs, leverage sits at 2.76x with refinancing activity ongoing, and the earnings record is volatile. The rising full-year estimate revisions reflect M&A accretion arithmetic, not an organic inflection, and the near-term quarterly estimates are being cut. Risk/reward is roughly balanced at ~$89, with a wide and skewed analyst target range ($52.4–$100).

We rate TIGO a **HOLD**. The thesis is intact but largely priced; we want confirmation that integration converts to the guided EFCF and deleveraging before underwriting further upside, and we would become more constructive on a meaningful pullback or on a clean Q2/Q3 print that demonstrates organic durability and balance-sheet repair.
