## Executive Summary

Essent Group Ltd is a post-crisis private mortgage insurer trading at 8.6x trailing earnings and approximately 1.0x book value, generating 12% ROE with 67% free cash flow margins and returning capital through buybacks and dividends. The financial profile is exceptional for a company at this valuation: $849M FCF on a $5.5B market cap with minimal debt (0.29x D/E) and $1.7B in excess regulatory capital.

The specific obstacle preventing a BUY recommendation is a quantifiable, management-acknowledged deterioration in loss experience. The loss ratio has tripled from 3.7% (FY2023) to 16.8% (FY2025), with 93% of the $248B insurance-in-force book now entering peak claim years (3-6 post-origination). Claim severity simultaneously rose from 59% to 75%. Management explicitly states they expect losses to continue increasing. The options market is pricing imminent stress: near-term IV at 47.3% in steep backwardation versus 22.3% realized volatility, implying a ±17.8% move at nearest expiry. The market is correctly pricing rising credit deterioration that the company itself acknowledges.

The stock is a compelling income hold: 2.3% dividend yield + ~7% annual share count reduction = ~9-10% total capital return yield at current levels, supported by fortress-level capital adequacy (169% PMIERs sufficiency). But the timing for an upgrade requires either loss ratio stabilization or sufficient valuation compression to compensate for the known deterioration path.

## Company Overview

Essent Group Ltd, domiciled in Bermuda, provides private mortgage insurance (MI) through its subsidiary Essent Guaranty, Inc. (approved by Fannie Mae/Freddie Corp, licensed in all 50 states). The company also operates a Bermuda-based reinsurance arm (Essent Re) that participates in GSE credit risk transfer transactions and recently entered Lloyd's of London for P&C reinsurance (Q4 2025). A small title insurance operation was acquired in July 2023.

Founded in 2008 and IPO'd in 2013, Essent's entire book was written post-crisis (since May 2010), initially giving it a structurally cleaner risk profile than legacy competitors. With $248.4B insurance in force and 514 employees, the company generates approximately $1.3M net income per employee — among the highest in financial services.

The private MI industry is an oligopoly: six GSE-approved insurers compete for ~$300B+ annual new insurance written (NIW). Barriers to entry are near-prohibitive (GSE approval, state licensing across 50 jurisdictions, multi-billion-dollar capital requirements). Essent holds financial strength ratings of A2 (Moody's), A- (S&P), and A (AM Best), all stable.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is modest (+1.6% FY2024-FY2025) and constrained by the housing market environment:

- NIW running at ~$46B annually (vs. $120B+ during refi booms)
- Persistency at 85.7% supports IIF stability but means the book ages without refreshment
- Investment income contributes growth via rising yields on a growing portfolio
- Analyst consensus: FY2026E EPS $7.23, FY2027E $7.72 — implies 5-7% annual EPS growth

Growth catalysts are limited to: (1) mortgage rate declines stimulating originations (currently 6.5-7%), (2) continued share count reduction via buybacks, and (3) the nascent Lloyd's P&C reinsurance venture. Rate-driven origination recovery would boost NIW but simultaneously reduce persistency — a partial offset. The Lloyd's entry is too early-stage to size.

Estimate revisions are flat: current quarter estimates declined 3.2% over 90 days, current year essentially unchanged (+0.2%). No positive inflection signal present.

## Competitive Landscape

The private MI industry's oligopoly structure provides durable competitive positioning: six players, near-prohibitive barriers to entry, GSE-mandated demand. Essent's financial strength ratings (A2/A-/A) are at or above most competitors, and its post-crisis-only book was historically a differentiator.

Two concerning competitive trends:

1. **Customer concentration accelerating:** Top 10 customers generated 59.3% of NIW in 2025 (vs. 39.9% in 2023). This concentration reduces negotiating power and concentrates revenue risk. If a top-3 lender shifts MI share to a competitor, the impact is immediate.

2. **TSR underperformance:** Essent's cumulative TSR of 186% materially lags the peer group average of 325% per proxy disclosures. The market has consistently valued competitors more highly, suggesting Essent may face undisclosed competitive pressures or growth constraints.

The Lloyd's entry signals management sees limited organic growth in core MI and is diversifying — prudent strategically but introduces execution risk in an unfamiliar line of business.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Loss Ratio Normalization (HIGH — Quantifiable, In Progress)**
The tripling of the loss ratio reflects the expected seasoning pattern. The trajectory creates earnings headwinds that have not yet peaked. With 93% of IIF written since January 2020, the majority of the book enters peak claim years (3-6) through 2026-2027. Severity increases (59% → 75%) compound the impact. This is the primary reason the stock trades at 8.6x vs. what would otherwise warrant 10-12x.

**2. Basel III Endgame (MEDIUM — Binary, Uncertain Timing)**
If implemented as proposed, banks above $100B would receive no capital credit for MI on high-LTV loans — eliminating the capital incentive to require MI. The Trump Administration has created timing uncertainty, but the structural overhang persists.

**3. GSE Reform / FHA Competition (MEDIUM — Perennial)**
FHA's 11% MMI Fund ratio (vs. 2% minimum) provides room for premium cuts that would shift volume away from private MI. GSE privatization remains theoretically possible though has been dormant for 17+ years.

**4. Bermuda Tax Risk (LOW-MEDIUM — 5-Year Clock)**
The LIP exemption from Bermuda's 15% CIT expires after 5 years (2030) and qualification is uncertain annually. Essent Re houses $1.7B equity and receives 50% of NIW premiums — loss of exemption would meaningfully raise the effective tax rate.

**5. Customer Concentration (MEDIUM — Accelerating)**
59.3% of NIW from top 10 customers, up from 39.9% in just two years. Single-customer loss could remove 6-10% of new business flow.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress: ATM IV at 47.3% (vs. 22.3% realized) in steep backwardation (47.3% near-term vs. 27.6% at 6 months). The 25pp IV-over-HV spread and backwardation structure indicate informed participants pricing a specific near-term catalyst — potentially related to housing data releases, FOMC rate decisions, or loss reserve announcements. Put skew at 19.8% confirms elevated demand for downside protection. However, P/C OI ratio at 0.03 (extremely call-heavy in open interest) suggests the near-term stress pricing may be event-driven rather than structural repositioning.

IV compressed from 66.8% to 41.8% over the past three weeks (while price declined 1.5%), suggesting one stress event has partially resolved, but residual near-term pricing remains elevated.

## Investment Thesis

- Loss ratio stabilizes at 18-20% as the book seasons through peak years without a recession materializing
- Rate cuts in H2 2026 stimulate originations, boosting NIW 30-40%
- Continued 7% annual buyback supports 8-10% EPS growth even on flat net income
- Revaluation from 8.6x to 10x on FY27E $7.72 = $77

**Bear Case ($48-52, 12-19% downside):**

- Basel III Endgame implemented, structurally reducing MI demand
- ROE compresses to 8-9%, stock trades at 0.8x book = $47-48

**Base Case ($64-67, 8-13% upside):**

- NIW remains at ~$46B annually
- EPS: $7.23 (FY26), ~$7.50 (FY27) inclusive of buybacks
- Market assigns 9x on $7.23 = $65

## Conclusion

ESNT is a high-quality, capital-light financial business trading at a discounted valuation with fortress-level capital adequacy and exceptional cash generation. The 8.6x P/E on a 12% ROE, 67% FCF margin business would normally warrant a BUY recommendation. The specific, evidence-based barrier is the quantifiable and management-acknowledged loss ratio deterioration (3.7% → 16.8% with further increases expected) as 93% of the book enters peak claim years simultaneously. This is an in-progress fundamental transition confirmed by rising default counts, rising reserve-per-default, and rising claim severity.

The options market's steep backwardation and 25pp IV-over-HV spread independently confirm that informed capital is pricing near-term stress. The 2-for-4 quarterly beat/miss record (including a 9.5% Q4 miss) demonstrates that loss reserve adequacy is not yet stable enough for earnings predictability.

The stock is worth holding for its ~9% annual capital return yield, supported by $1.7B excess capital and $849M annual FCF. The upgrade path is clear: loss ratio stabilization proves peak claims are manageable within the existing earnings structure. Until that data arrives (likely Q2-Q3 2026 earnings), the prudent positioning is to collect the capital return and monitor.
