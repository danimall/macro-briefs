**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The New York Times has converted a structurally declining print newspaper into a digital subscription compounder, and the FY2025/Q1 2026 financials confirm the model is working at scale. Revenue grew 9.2% in 2025 to $2.82B and accelerated to +12% in Q1 2026; operating income rose 22.9% in 2025 and 54.5% in Q1; net income grew 17.1% and 77.4% respectively. The company ended Q1 2026 with 13.08 million total subscribers (12.52M digital-only), the most in its history, against a 15 million target for year-end 2027. The balance sheet is debt-free with roughly $1.1–1.2B in cash and securities, free cash flow reached $550M (flattered by one-time items), and the dividend was raised 28% in February 2026. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 6.5% for the current year and 5.5% for next year over 90 days, and the company has beaten earnings in four straight quarters, most recently by 27.5%.

The question is not whether the business is good but whether the current price compensates for the dominant risk the company itself names first: generative AI as a traffic-disintermediation threat. The 10-K states that AI has already reduced traffic as the web shifts from link-based referral economics toward "contained ecosystems" where users get NYT-derived answers without visiting NYT. Management is pursuing a deliberate "fight the bad actors, license to the good ones" posture, suing OpenAI/Microsoft and Perplexity for copyright infringement while signing AI content-licensing deals, and the early signs (growing licensing revenue, a >$250M News Corp/OpenAI comparable) validate the content as a monetizable asset. The outcome is binary and multi-year. NYT's three-year TSR of 142 trails its peer-group benchmark of 230, suggesting the market is already discounting the disintermediation overhang.

We rate NYT a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.45. At ~$73, the stock trades at roughly 31x trailing and ~25x forward EPS, a premium to the publishing peer median of 24.5x P/E that is defensible given superior growth and balance sheet quality, but not cheap enough to provide asymmetric upside ahead of an unresolved labor cycle and litigation that could move either way. We would upgrade on a forward multiple below ~20x or a favorable, dated catalyst in the AI-licensing/litigation arena.

## Company Overview

The New York Times Company consolidated to a single reportable segment in Q3 2025, reflecting CEO Meredith Kopit Levien's role as sole Chief Operating Decision Maker. Revenue flows from three streams:

- **Subscriptions (~69%)** — digital and print, centered on the digital "bundle" that combines core news with The Athletic, Cooking, Games (Wordle/Connections), Audio, and Wirecutter. The "bundle and multiproduct" cohort ended 2025 at 6.48M, up 24% YoY, and is the strategic engine: it monetizes and retains better than news-only subscribers, which fell 27% in 2025 as users are funneled into the bundle.
- **Advertising (~20%)** — increasingly digital (73% of ad revenue), with print in secular decline.
- **Affiliate, licensing & other (~11%)** — Wirecutter affiliate referrals, content licensing (including to AI/digital platforms), commercial printing, and HQ floor leasing.

The Times holds the largest daily and Sunday print circulation of any U.S. seven-day newspaper, operates in 234 countries with ~26% of digital subscribers international, and employs roughly 6,000 people including 3,000+ journalists. The Ochs-Sulzberger family trust holds ~95% of Class B shares and elects 70% of the board, which protects editorial independence and long-term strategy while precluding hostile takeovers and limiting Class A governance influence.

**Operating leverage is real.** Digital-only subscription revenue grew 14.3% in 2025 and 16.1% in Q1 2026. Digital advertising rebounded +20% in 2025 and +31.6% in Q1 2026 on display, podcast, and custom-campaign demand. Adjusted operating margin expanded to 19.5% (2025) from 17.6% (2024), and the GAAP operating margin moved 350bps higher year-over-year in Q1. This is the operating-leverage signature of a fixed-cost content base spread over a growing recurring revenue line.

**The balance sheet is pristine.** The company is debt-free with ~$1.1–1.2B in cash and marketable securities and an undrawn $400M facility extended to June 2030. The reported D/E of 1.47 reflects pension and deferred-revenue liabilities, not borrowings; the Altman Z of 2.92 sits in the grey zone only because the model penalizes asset-light media balance sheets. Leverage is genuinely minimal.

**Cash flow is high-quality but contains modeling caveats.** FCF margin was ~19.5% and OCF consistently exceeds net income. Two one-time items inflate the 2025/2026 headline: a ~$33M College Point land sale and a ~$65M cash-tax benefit from OBBBA R&D expensing, with ~$60M more expected in 2026. Management explicitly warns the tax benefit will not recur beyond 2026, so the 42% OCF growth should not be extrapolated; normalized FCF is somewhat below the headline $550M. Capital return is disciplined: the company targets returning ≥50% of FCF, raised the dividend 28% to $0.23/quarter, repurchased $165M in 2025 with ~$292M remaining on the authorization, and is slowly reducing share count (164.9M diluted in 2025 vs. 167M in 2022).

Financial Health scores 8.0. This is a downside-resilient capital structure with strong cash generation; we hold back from a 9 because cash hoarding without proportionate growth deployment caps capital efficiency, and the one-time items mean reported FCF overstates the run-rate.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is subscription compounding with margin expansion. ARPU is rising (digital ARPU $9.77 in Q1), and net adds were +310K QoQ in Q1. The bundle cohort is the highest-quality slice of the base.

Estimate revisions confirm positive earnings momentum: current-year EPS estimates moved to $2.87, and next-year estimates moved to $3.20. The 30-day current-quarter estimate ticked down 1.2%, but the medium-term direction is up. The four-quarter beat record (27.5%, 1.0%, 9.5%, 15.7%) supports confidence in the forward consensus.

Two caveats temper the growth score. First, this is positive but not inflection-magnitude revision velocity — the +6.5% 90-day move does not clear the +30% threshold that would force a cycle-adjusted re-rating, so the multiple should be judged on forward earnings at face value. Second, the subscriber funnel is the asset most directly exposed to AI disintermediation. The 15M-by-2027 target is achievable on current trajectory but depends on the bundle continuing to offset news-only attrition and on AI answer-engines not accelerating top-of-funnel erosion.

A transparency note: starting after Q4 2025, NYT will stop reporting subscribers/ARPU by category. This removes visibility into mix quality at exactly the moment news-only is shrinking, and is a disclosure step-down to monitor.

Growth Prospects score 7.0. Growth is durable, broad-based across subscriptions and digital advertising, and confirmed by rising estimates — the single most reliable winner-selection signal we weight. The AI funnel risk and the reduced disclosure keep it from an 8.

## Competitive Landscape

NYT's moat is a premium, trusted, Pulitzer-decorated brand monetized through a multi-product bundle that lowers churn and raises ARPU. Within traditional news (WSJ, Washington Post, FT, CNN, Guardian, ESPN) it is gaining share, with a record subscriber base and bundle-cohort growth widening engagement.

The competitive trajectory is two-sided, which is why this dimension scores below the company's standalone quality. The 10-K reframes the relevant competitive set as AI platforms and aggregators — Alphabet, OpenAI, Perplexity, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, ByteDance, X. The structural shift from referral-traffic economics to AI "answer engines" disintermediates publishers at the top of the funnel. Against this, NYT's defenses are (1) deepening direct, logged-in engagement via the bundle to reduce platform dependence, and (2) converting its content into a licensable AI training asset while litigating against unauthorized use. The licensing market becoming a "seller's market" (NYT's separately announced Amazon arrangement, ~$116M of remaining contracted licensing/advertising obligations) is the offsetting positive.

Net, the trajectory is improving within news but uncertain against the AI-platform layer that NYT now treats as its primary competitive frontier. Competitive Position scores 5.5: a strong incumbent with a widening direct-relationship moat, offset by a structural channel threat the company itself names as accelerating.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is unusually AI-centric, which is what differentiates NYT from a generic publisher.

1. **Generative AI disintermediation (dominant risk).** The 10-K frames the traffic decline as structural and accelerating. This threatens the subscriber funnel, advertising, and brand attribution simultaneously.
2. **Copyright litigation (binary, multi-year).** NYT leads landmark suits against OpenAI/Microsoft (filed Dec 2023, consolidated into an SDNY MDL April 2025) and Perplexity (filed Dec 2025). A March 2025 ruling dismissed unfair-competition and DMCA claims (DMCA with leave to replead) but allowed core copyright claims to proceed; the case is in high-stakes discovery. A favorable outcome is a cash award plus a licensing-rate floor; an adverse one undermines the IP-monetization thesis. NYT excludes ~$13.3M (2025) of AI litigation costs as a "special item" — watch for escalation.
3. **Labor (near-term wildcard).** ~43% of employees are unionized. The NewsGuild contract (~23% of FTEs) expired Feb 28, 2026, and Drivers/Machinists/Paperhandlers/Stereotypers contracts expired March 30, 2026, with negotiations ongoing. The Guild filed May 2026 grievances alleging AI surveillance of union workers. Employee costs are the largest, least-flexible expense base; a strike or rich settlement could pressure 2026 margins.
4. **Print secular decline.** Print subscribers fell to ~560K; print ad revenue down ~5–10%. Fixed print costs over fewer copies pressure unit economics. Acknowledged and irreversible.
5. **Pension overhang.** ~$226M total liabilities including ~$166M unfunded non-qualified and ~$60M multiemployer withdrawal; a 50bp discount-rate drop adds ~$55M. Well-managed but a volatility item.
6. **Dual-class control.** Family trust controls 70% of board seats — protects strategy, caps M&A optionality.

Risk Factors score 5.0. The AI disintermediation and litigation risks are partly priced (the TSR underperformance suggests so), but the labor cycle is a live, near-term, unresolved binary, and the litigation outcome is genuinely uncertain with material consequences either way. These are specific, dated, and not fully reflected in a ~25x forward multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 28.0% is essentially fair to 1-year HV of 28.2% (spread -0.3%), with IV in the 4th percentile of the current chain — the market is not pricing acute near-term stress. The put/call skew is elevated at 5.7% and the put/call volume ratio is 2.17, indicating active downside hedging or bearish positioning. The term structure is flat (28.0% near vs. 29.7% far), distributing uncertainty evenly across tenors, with the 3-month tenor (covering the next earnings and the labor-negotiation window) showing the highest IV at 35.1%. The P/C open-interest ratio fell sharply from 3.54 to 1.20 since late May as the price declined 3.8%, suggesting some unwinding of prior bearish positioning. The elevated put skew is a modest divergence against a constructive fundamental read, consistent with the binary labor/litigation overhang.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($95–100).** The bundle continues compounding toward the 15M-by-2027 target, digital advertising sustains 20%+ growth, margins expand toward 20%+ adjusted operating margin, and NYT wins or favorably settles its AI litigation — establishing both a cash award and an industry licensing-rate floor that converts the AI threat into a high-margin licensing annuity. In this scenario the market re-rates the durability of the recurring base, and forward EPS power moves toward $3.50+, supporting a high-$90s valuation at ~27x. This matches the $95 analyst high.

**Bear case ($55–60).** AI answer-engines accelerate top-of-funnel traffic erosion faster than the bundle can offset, news-only attrition spreads to the bundle, a costly labor settlement or strike compresses 2026 margins, the OBBBA tax tailwind rolls off (normalized FCF steps down), and an adverse litigation ruling undermines the licensing-rate thesis. The multiple compresses toward the lower end of the peer range (~18–20x) on decelerating growth, implying a ~$55–60 stock — roughly a 20% loss from current levels and near the 52-week low of $51.

**Base case ($80–85).** Subscription growth continues in the low-double-digits, margins expand modestly, estimates keep rising single-digits, and the litigation remains an open option without a definitive resolution. The stock re-rates toward the analyst mean of ~$85 as consensus targets are reaffirmed, implying ~10–17% upside. This is the most-likely outcome and sits between the bear and bull scenarios.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a constructive 12-month bias (growth_hold).

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) forward P/E compresses below ~20x on unchanged-or-rising estimates (a pullback toward $58–62 with intact fundamentals); (2) a favorable AI litigation ruling or a marquee AI-licensing deal that establishes a recurring, disclosed licensing-revenue line of material size; (3) a labor settlement reached without a strike and without a step-change in employee cost growth, removing the near-term margin overhang.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) net digital subscriber adds turn negative for two consecutive quarters, signaling the AI funnel threat is outrunning the bundle; (2) an adverse final ruling that materially weakens NYT's ability to protect/monetize content; (3) a prolonged strike or settlement that drives employee costs up enough to compress operating margins below ~13% on a sustained basis.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, plus the resolution of the NewsGuild/production-union negotiations and any material development in the OpenAI MDL discovery process. The 3-month options tenor (35.1% IV) marks the window the market views as event-dense.

## Conclusion

The New York Times is a high-quality subscription compounder with a debt-free balance sheet, accelerating digital revenue, expanding margins, rising estimates, and a four-quarter beat streak. The score trajectory has improved (6.1 → 5.9 → 6.9, financial health from 7.0 to 9.0, management from 5.5 to 7.0), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend. The recommendation remains HOLD for the third consecutive read, and the bar for continuing it should be high, but the change since the prior report is a ~16% price decline from the $87 high against fundamentals that are intact or improving. That decline improves the risk/reward at the margin without yet creating the asymmetric entry that would justify a BUY.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a full ~25x forward multiple (a premium to the peer median that is earned but not mispriced), a dominant and self-acknowledged AI-disintermediation risk already pressuring traffic, an unresolved labor cycle with three expired contracts, and a binary copyright litigation whose outcome cuts both ways. The licensing-vs-litigation strategy is the swing factor: if NYT converts its content into a durable, disclosed AI-licensing annuity while defending its funnel through the bundle, the stock re-rates to the high-$90s. If AI erodes the funnel faster than the bundle offsets, the multiple compresses meaningfully. At $72.83, the market is pricing a balanced outcome, and so are we. Overall score 6.45; HOLD with a positive 12-month bias.
