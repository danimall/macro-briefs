**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Exelixis is a profitable, single-molecule oncology company that the market is pricing as if its franchise expires in August 2026. The primary-source litigation record says otherwise. The composition-of-matter patent on cabozantinib ('473) does lapse August 14, 2026, but the courts have so far upheld three later patents ('439/'440/'015), pushing the lead generic challenger's earliest possible entry to January 15, 2030. One challenger (Sun) settled in December 2025, and the two 505(b)(2) filers are arguably non-substitutable. The defining near-term events are the Federal Circuit appeal on MSN II (oral argument scheduled June 4, 2026) and a consolidated trial scheduled November 2, 2026.

The fundamentals are strong and improving. Q1 2026 revenue grew 10% to $610.8M, net income rose 32% to $210.5M, diluted EPS hit $0.79, operating margin was ~41%, and gross margin ~96%. The balance sheet carries no debt and $1.4B of cash. The company has beaten consensus four straight quarters (by 7-14%), current-year EPS estimates have risen +5.2% over 90 days, and the stock trades at 13.2x forward earnings. That is a low-double-digit multiple on a debt-free business growing earnings 30%+, which warrants a constructive call rather than caution.

I am moving to BUY at $53.53. The bull case rests on the litigated 2030 protection holding and zanzalintinib (positive STELLAR-303 colorectal data, PDUFA December 3, 2026) successfully bridging Exelixis to a two-franchise platform before patent and IRA protections cluster-expire around 2028-2030. The risk is binary: an adverse Federal Circuit ruling or a zanzalintinib stumble would re-expose the franchise, which caps conviction and position size. At 13.2x forward with three positive-skew catalysts ahead and 16.1% short interest (10.4 days to cover), the asymmetry favors ownership.

## Company Overview

Exelixis is built almost entirely around cabozantinib, a multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor (MET, AXL, VEGF receptors, RET), sold as CABOMETYX tablets (~99.6% of net product revenue) and a tiny legacy product, COMETRIQ (~$2.2M/quarter). CABOMETYX is approved across advanced renal cell carcinoma (both monotherapy and in combination with Bristol-Myers Squibb's Opdivo), hepatocellular carcinoma, radioactive-iodine-refractory thyroid cancer, and, newly approved in March 2025, pancreatic and extra-pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. It is marketed in 68 countries outside the U.S.

The business model is asset-light and cash-rich. Exelixis retains U.S. commercial rights and out-licenses ex-U.S. rights to Ipsen (ex-U.S./ex-Japan) and Takeda (Japan), collecting royalties and milestones. It relies on contract manufacturers and runs as a single segment, funding an expanding internal R&D engine entirely from cabozantinib cash flows. The moat is the established clinical position of cabozantinib in RCC (the Opdivo combination drives long duration-of-therapy and recurring refills) plus a patent estate with a contested but litigated 2030 horizon.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is volume-led and durable. The RCC + Opdivo combination produces long duration-of-therapy and recurring refills, the NET launch (approved March 2025) is ramping, and management is expanding the salesforce and guiding net product revenue higher through 2026. The +32% net income on +10% revenue reflects operating leverage: costs grew far slower than the top line, and R&D actually declined 6% in the quarter.

Estimate momentum is net-positive and should be read in full rather than on a single print. Current-quarter EPS estimates are +5.6% over 30 days, current-year +5.2% over 90 days and +3.9% over 30 days, with the consensus explicitly characterized as "broadly rising — positive earnings momentum." The lone negative is next-year EPS at -3.6% over 90 days, which most plausibly reflects modeling of post-August-2026 generic-erosion scenarios and rising SG&A for the zanzalintinib launch buildout, not a deterioration in the underlying business, which is beating and raising.

The durable growth question is the successor asset. Zanzalintinib (next-gen TKI targeting TAM kinases/MET/VEGFR) is the designated bridge. STELLAR-303 (colorectal, + atezolizumab) posted positive top-line overall survival (HR 0.80, p=0.0045, published in *The Lancet*), the FDA accepted the NDA in February 2026, and the PDUFA target date is December 3, 2026. STELLAR-304 (non-clear-cell RCC, + nivolumab) reads out in 2H 2026. A broad STELLAR program (NET, meningioma, squamous NSCLC, CRC MRD+ with Natera) and a Merck collaboration on zanzalintinib + WELIREG in RCC extend the optionality, and Exelixis retains all global commercial rights to zanzalintinib. Growth prospects score 7.0: strong and confirmed near-term, with multi-year durability contingent on the pipeline transition.

## Competitive Landscape

In-market, RCC, HCC, thyroid, and NET are described as highly competitive, with cabozantinib facing checkpoint inhibitors and rival TKI combinations. CABOMETYX's position is currently underpinned by the durable RCC + Opdivo combination, which generates refills and long therapy duration, a stable rather than expanding moat.

The generic threat defines the competitive trajectory. The full ANDA/505(b)(2) cluster (MSN, Teva, Cipla, Biocon, Sun, Azurity, Handa) confirms that generics view cabozantinib as a high-value target. The favorable data points: the court upheld the '473 patent (MSN I), so MSN's ANDA cannot be approved before August 14, 2026; the MSN II ruling (Delaware District Court, October 2024) upheld '439/'440/'015, pushing earliest entry to January 15, 2030; Sun settled and dismissed in December 2025; and Exelixis argues Azurity/Handa's different salt forms are non-substitutable, having filed a citizen petition against Handa in April 2026. The unresolved threads: the Teva/Cipla/Biocon litigation status is not confirmable from available filings, and the Sun settlement's undisclosed entry date could matter.

Competitive position scores 6.5: the trajectory is stable-to-modestly-favorable given the litigated 2030 dates, but a single-molecule franchise facing a coordinated generic assault and an eventual cliff cannot rate higher until zanzalintinib is approved and ramping.

## Risk Assessment

The risks are concentrated and binary.

**Patent cliff (defining risk).** Litigation has thus far defended the franchise: near-term to August 2026 and, if the Federal Circuit affirms MSN II, to January 2030. The Federal Circuit appeal and the November consolidated trial are the genuine binary swing events. An adverse ruling would compress the protected horizon and re-expose the entire revenue base.

**Single-molecule concentration.** Essentially all revenue derives from cabozantinib. Any clinical, safety, competitive, or pricing shock is existential until zanzalintinib diversifies the base.

**Drug-pricing / IRA exposure.** The filings detail extensive exposure to Medicare price negotiation, Part D redesign, MFN executive orders, proposed CMS pilots, and a potential Section 232 tariff of up to 100% on imported pharmaceuticals. Exelixis qualifies for the IRA small-biotech exception through IPAY 2028 (applying to extend to 2030), a meaningful shield that expires roughly as patent protection does.

**340B and net-realization pressure.** Discounts/allowances rose 15% YoY in Q1'26, faster than gross revenue, with an active HRSA ADR proceeding.

**Customer concentration.** Distribution runs through Cencora (22%), McKesson (18%), CVS Health (16%), and Accredo (10%); Ipsen is 15% of receivables.

**Pipeline execution.** A zanzalintinib failure at the PDUFA or in STELLAR-304 would leave the franchise facing the eventual cliff with no successor.

The protections cluster-expire around 2028-2030, making that the critical horizon. Risk factors score 5.5: the magnitude of the binary patent and pipeline events is high and not fully resolved, though much of the cliff fear is already in the price.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Federal Circuit affirms MSN II, locking generic entry to January 2030. Zanzalintinib is approved at the PDUFA and STELLAR-304 reads out positively, validating the bridge from a one-drug company to a two-franchise oncology platform. CABOMETYX continues volume-led growth through 2026, the buyback shrinks share count further, and consensus re-rates the multiple toward the profitable-biotech peer group (NBIX at 25x). On $4.05 next-year EPS, even a modest re-rating to ~15-16x plus pipeline optionality supports $62 (the Street high) and beyond.

**Bear case.** The Federal Circuit reverses, collapsing the protected horizon toward 2026-2027 and triggering generic erosion modeling across all forward estimates. Or zanzalintinib stumbles at the PDUFA, leaving no successor as IRA and patent protections lapse around 2028-2030. The single-molecule concentration turns the franchise into a melting ice cube, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the target range ($40) or below.

The probability skew favors the bull case near-term: the franchise is defended to August 2026 regardless, the litigated record currently supports 2030, Sun has settled, and management's aggressive $750M+ buyback at ~$43 signals internal conviction that protection holds. The bear outcomes are severe and the events are genuinely binary, which is why this is a sized BUY rather than a high-conviction one.
