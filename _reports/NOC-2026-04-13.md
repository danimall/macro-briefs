## Executive Summary

Northrop Grumman is the fourth-largest U.S. defense prime by revenue (~$42B), with an irreplaceable franchise in strategic deterrence platforms: the B-21 Raider stealth bomber and the Sentinel ICBM. The company's $95.7B backlog (2.3x trailing revenue) provides multi-year earnings visibility that few industrial companies can match. The February 2026 B-21 production acceleration agreement, funded by $4.5B in reconciliation appropriations, represents a structural improvement in the program's risk/return profile: the worst of the fixed-price LRIP losses (~$2.0B cumulative) is likely behind the company, while upcoming NTE lots carry higher unit pricing and inflation-adjustment clauses.

The stock trades at ~23.2x trailing earnings ($29.08 EPS), a substantial discount to the 35.7x A&D peer median. This discount partially reflects the B-21's loss history and ongoing Sentinel Nunn-McCurdy restructuring, both of which are legitimate concerns. However, the pullback from $774 in early March to ~$674 (a 13% decline) has improved the risk/reward meaningfully. FCF grew 26% to $3.3B in 2025, the balance sheet carries $4.4B in cash, and the company has reduced its share count by 7.6% since 2022. Defense spending tailwinds — the $150B OBBBA allocation, the US naval blockade of Iranian ports driving further supplemental procurement, and Allied rearmament — provide a sustained demand backdrop.

The prior BUY recommendation issued in March at a higher price (-8.2% since) requires honest reassessment. What has changed materially: (1) the stock is 13% cheaper from the March high, improving the valuation entry; (2) the geopolitical backdrop has escalated substantially with the US naval blockade, increasing near-term defense spending probability; (3) estimate revisions have turned mildly negative (-3.1% current year over 90 days), reflecting normal conservatism ahead of Q1 2026 results. The core thesis — irreplaceable franchise, backlog visibility, B-21 production inflection — remains intact at a better price.

## Company Overview

Northrop Grumman operates across four segments serving predominantly the U.S. Department of Defense (84% of revenue):

- **Aeronautics Systems** ($13.0B, 31%): B-21 Raider, F-35 components, E-2D, autonomous systems. Lowest margin segment (6.3%) due to B-21 loss provisions.
- **Mission Systems** ($12.5B, 30%): Radar, EW, C4ISR, cyber, advanced microelectronics. Highest margin at 14.6% — the company's cash engine.
- **Space Systems** ($10.8B, 26%): Satellites, missile defense interceptors (NGI), GEM 63 boosters. Revenue declined 8% in 2025 on restricted program wind-downs.
- **Defense Systems** ($8.0B, 19%): Sentinel ICBM, precision strike, IBCS, ammunition. Growing 11% organically — the fastest segment.

The company's competitive moat centers on sole-source positions in programs with no viable substitutes: the B-21 is the only sixth-generation strategic bomber in production; Sentinel is the only ICBM modernization program. These positions are protected by decades of institutional knowledge, classified capabilities, and infrastructure investment exceeding $5B in digital engineering and manufacturing.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

Revenue grew 2.2% to $42.0B in 2025 (3% organic excluding the IMS divestiture). Growth has decelerated from 7.3% in 2023 and 4.4% in 2024. The deceleration is primarily attributable to the Space Systems wind-down (-8%) and the B-21 LRIP schedule — neither of which reflects underlying demand. Mission Systems (+10%) and Defense Systems (+8%) demonstrate that when programs are in production ramp, NOC delivers mid-to-high single-digit growth.

The $95.7B backlog, up 5% YoY, is the forward-looking metric that matters most. At 2.3x trailing revenue, this provides ~28 months of work assuming no new awards — but NOC books $40-50B in new awards annually. The backlog composition has shifted toward higher-margin programs (Mission Systems and Space Systems backlogs each grew 13%), which should support margin expansion as these contracts convert to revenue.

### Profitability

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|
| Operating Margin | 6.5% | 10.6% | 10.8% |
| Net Margin | 5.3% | 10.1% | 10.0% |
| ROE | 15.3% | 24.7% | 25.1% |
| FCF Margin | 5.3% | 6.4% | 7.9% |

The 2023 operating margin collapse to 6.5% was entirely driven by the $1.56B B-21 LRIP loss provision. The recovery to 10.8% in 2025 demonstrates the underlying business quality, though the additional $477M B-21 provision in Q1 2025 held Aeronautics margins to just 6.3%. Mission Systems at 14.6% is best-in-class for a defense segment of that scale.

### Cash Flow

FCF of $3.3B in 2025 represents 7.9% of revenue and grew 26% YoY. The improvement came from higher operating cash flow ($4.8B, +8%) and lower capex ($1.5B, -18%). The capex decline warrants monitoring: management flagged $2-3B in B-21 facility investment needed for accelerated production. If this spending materializes primarily in 2026-2027, FCF could compress temporarily before expanding as production ramps at improved unit economics.

FCF conversion relative to net income is approximately 79% ($3.3B FCF / $4.2B net income), slightly below the 100%+ threshold that signals highest-quality earnings but adequate for a capital-intensive defense manufacturer.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt of $15.2B against $4.4B cash yields net debt of ~$10.8B, or approximately 3.3x FCF. Debt/equity at 2.08x is elevated relative to industrials broadly but typical for defense primes with government-guaranteed revenue streams. The Altman Z-Score of 1.88 sits in the grey zone — a function of the Z-Score model's poor fit for defense contractors rather than genuine distress. With $95.7B in government-backed backlog and $3.3B in annual FCF, solvency risk is negligible.

Goodwill at $17.4B (34% of total assets) from the 2018 Orbital ATK acquisition is material. No impairment has been recorded, and the Space and Defense segments' performance supports carrying values, but this remains a risk factor if program execution deteriorates.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term (2026-2027)

Consensus estimates project $27.93 EPS for FY2026 and $30.17 for FY2027, representing 8% growth off the 2025 MTM-adjusted base of $26.34. These estimates have been revised down modestly (-3.1% and -4.0% over 90 days, respectively), reflecting normal analyst conservatism rather than fundamental deterioration. The 30-day revisions are essentially flat, suggesting the downward trend has stabilized.

The B-21 production acceleration is the primary catalyst. The Air Force plans to obligate $4.5B by October 2026 for bomber production, with at least 100 aircraft planned and advocacy for 150-200. The NTE lots (units 21-40) carry higher average unit prices and economic price adjustment clauses — a structural improvement over the fixed-price LRIP lots that generated the cumulative losses.

### Medium-Term (2027-2030)

The Sentinel ICBM production/deployment phases — not yet priced or contracted — represent $60-100B+ in potential future revenue. The program cleared its Nunn-McCurdy certification in July 2024, and Q2 2025 saw a $76M favorable EAC adjustment. The restructured baseline reduces near-term uncertainty while preserving the massive long-term revenue pipeline.

Defense Systems' 11% organic growth rate, driven by Sentinel ramp and ammunition demand, should sustain mid-to-high single-digit consolidated growth through the decade as Space Systems stabilizes and Aeronautics benefits from B-21 production ramp.

### Long-Term Demand Backdrop

The OBBBA reconciliation bill allocated ~$150B for defense through FY2034. The US naval blockade of Iranian ports, along with broader great-power competition (Western Pacific, Ukraine), reinforces the structural case for sustained defense investment above historical trendlines. The FY2027 Pentagon budget request of $65B for shipbuilding alone signals the scale of planned military modernization.

## Competitive Landscape

NOC occupies a position in the defense industrial base that is effectively irreplaceable:

1. **B-21 sole source**: No other company has the facilities, clearances, workforce, or institutional knowledge to produce a sixth-generation stealth bomber. The program has a minimum 100-unit production run with potential for 150-200. At estimated unit costs of $700M-$900M+, this is a $70-180B franchise.

2. **Sentinel sole source**: The only ICBM modernization contractor. Production/deployment phases represent $60-100B+ in future revenue spanning decades.

3. **Mission Systems technology moat**: Advanced radar (AESA), electronic warfare, and microelectronics capabilities that take decades to develop and are classified at levels that prevent new entrants.

4. **Vertical integration**: GEM 63 solid rocket boosters, missile warheads, and propulsion give NOC control over critical supply chain elements that other primes lack.

Competitive risks are primarily programmatic rather than market-share-based: execution on B-21 and Sentinel determines whether these franchises deliver adequate returns, not whether a competitor takes the work.

## Risk Assessment

### B-21 Fixed-Price Exposure (HIGH but DECLINING)

The risk profile is improving: LRIP lots (units 1-20) where losses are concentrated are largely priced; NTE lots (units 21-40) have higher unit prices and inflation adjustments; and the Q4 2025 EAC review made no significant changes. The worst case — another $500M+ provision on remaining LRIP deliveries — would reduce 2026 EPS by approximately $2.50 but would not alter the long-term production thesis.

### EO 14372 Capital Return Restrictions (MEDIUM-HIGH)

The executive order targeting defense contractor buybacks and dividends during underperformance could structurally reduce NOC's capital return capacity. The B-21 losses and Sentinel Nunn-McCurdy breach could invite scrutiny. NOC returned $2.9B to shareholders in 2025; any restriction would reduce this, lowering EPS growth from share count reduction (which has been 2.4% annually).

### Sentinel Program Risk (MEDIUM)

The Nunn-McCurdy breach and restructuring create uncertainty around the production/deployment contract pricing. The $76M favorable EAC adjustment in Q2 2025 is encouraging, but negotiation of the production phase contract will be a multi-year process with significant pricing risk.

### Government Budget / CR Risk (MEDIUM)

The current continuing resolution (through January 30, 2026) prevents new program starts. Extended CRs or another shutdown would delay contract awards and funding obligations, including the $4.5B B-21 production acceleration funds.

### Pension Sensitivity (LOW-MEDIUM)

A 25bp discount rate change swings pension obligations by ~$796M. The 2025 $527M MTM benefit could reverse, creating earnings volatility.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 31.2% sits 2.7pp above 1-year historical volatility of 28.5%, a roughly fair spread suggesting the market is not pricing exceptional near-term risk. The term structure is in backwardation (31.2% near-term vs. 27.2% at 6 months), indicating elevated short-term uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 0.50 and put skew of 6.8% reflect moderately elevated downside hedging but nothing extreme. IV at the 22nd percentile within the current chain means options are relatively cheap — favorable for establishing or adding to positions. Short interest at 1.3% of float is negligible.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$780-$815)

B-21 production acceleration executes on schedule; NTE lots achieve positive margins as inflation adjustments take effect. Sentinel production contract awarded at favorable terms. Defense budget expands further driven by the US naval blockade and Middle East escalation. Mission Systems sustains 14%+ margins while growing mid-teens. Share count reduction continues at ~2.5% annually. The stock re-rates toward 26-27x FY2027 EPS of $30+, reaching $780-$815.

### Bear Case (~$560-$590)

Another B-21 LRIP loss provision of $500M+. Sentinel production contract pricing disappoints, compressing long-term margin expectations. EO 14372 is aggressively implemented, restricting buybacks and reducing EPS growth by 1-2% annually. Government CRs persist through 2026, delaying the B-21 obligation. Multiple compresses to 19-20x on lower forward earnings of ~$28-29, yielding $530-580.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $754.** Derived from 25x FY2027 consensus EPS of $30.17. ~12% upside from ~$674.

**Bull case target: $815.** 27x FY2027 EPS of $30.17, reflecting re-rating toward peer median on B-21 production confirmation. ~21% upside.

**Bear case target: $570.** 20x FY2026 EPS of $28.50 (assumes $2+ EPS hit from additional B-21 provision). ~15% downside.

**Upside/downside ratio:** 1.4:1 to base case; 1.4:1 to bull case vs. bear case.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalyst dates: Q1 2026 earnings (April 2026), $4.5B B-21 obligation deadline (October 2026), Sentinel production contract progress updates (H2 2026).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Additional B-21 LRIP loss provision exceeding $750M in any single quarter — would signal cost overruns are worsening, not stabilizing.
2. EO 14372 implementation that formally restricts NOC buybacks by >50% — would structurally impair the EPS growth algorithm.
3. FY2026 consensus EPS revised below $25 — would indicate broad-based execution issues beyond a single program.

## Addressing the Prior Recommendation

The prior BUY issued March 13 at approximately $734 is down ~8.2%. What has materially changed to justify maintaining a BUY rather than downgrading:

1. **Price improvement.** The stock is 13% cheaper from the March high. The same business at $674 vs. $734 reduces the P/E from ~25.2x to ~23.2x, expanding the margin of safety.

2. **Geopolitical escalation.** The US naval blockade of Iranian ports (announced April 13) materially strengthens the near-term defense spending case. The OBBBA allocation is being spent into an environment of active military operations, increasing the probability and pace of contract obligations.

3. **B-21 production economics.** The February 2026 acceleration agreement with improved NTE lot contract terms remains the structural catalyst. Nothing in the subsequent 4 weeks has weakened this thesis.

4. **Estimate revisions stabilized.** The 90-day decline has flattened to essentially zero over 30 days. The revision trend has bottomed rather than continuing to deteriorate.

The core thesis remains intact at a better price.

## Conclusion

Northrop Grumman offers strategic irreplaceability at a valuation discount. The 35% discount to peer median P/E partially reflects legitimate B-21 execution risk, but the production acceleration agreement with improved contract economics signals the transition from loss-absorbing LRIP to margin-positive NTE production. The defense spending environment is the strongest in decades.

The primary risks — additional B-21 provisions, EO 14372 buyback restrictions, and Sentinel pricing uncertainty — are real but bounded. The B-21's remaining LRIP risk is narrowing as units are delivered; EO 14372's implementation timeline and scope remain unclear; and the favorable Q2 2025 EAC adjustment suggests the restructured Sentinel baseline is achievable.

At ~$674, the stock offers approximately 12% upside to a base case of $754 (25x FY2027E) with 21% upside to the bull case of $815. Downside to the bear case of ~$570 represents 15% risk. The risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside, supported by a defense spending backdrop that has strengthened since the prior recommendation. **BUY** with a 12-18 month horizon.
