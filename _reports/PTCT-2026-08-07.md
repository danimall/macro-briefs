## Executive Summary

PTC Therapeutics has replaced a collapsing revenue base with a scaling new product. Sephience (sepiapterin) for phenylketonuria generated $275.9M in H1 2026 from a zero base, grew 21.4% sequentially in Q2 to $151.3M, and crossed $250M of trailing-four-quarter net sales — a verifiable threshold, since crossing it triggered a $30.0M contractual milestone payment to former Censa holders that PTC accrued in Q2. Composition-of-matter protection runs to September 2038. Management pushed a September 2026 convertible maturity out to 2031 at a 0% coupon, leaving $2.23B of cash and securities against $591M of debt and only $3.0M of cash interest paid in H1.

The problem is what happens at the consolidated line. FY2026 product revenue guidance of $850–950M, against H1 actuals of $464.5M, implies H2 of $385.5–485.5M — a midpoint of $435.5M, or 6.2% *below* H1. Translarna fell 30.5% year over year after the EEA conditional marketing authorization was not renewed and the U.S. NDA was withdrawn in February 2026; Emflaza fell 45.2% with its final orphan exclusivity (ages 2 to under 5) expiring in June 2026 and no patent protection at all. Sephience is growing into that hole rather than on top of it. The Q2 EPS print of $0.92 versus a $0.43 estimate was not an operational beat: the quarter contained a $50M Phase 3-initiation milestone from Novartis, worth roughly $0.54 pre-tax on 92.0M diluted shares. Strip it and Q2 EPS was approximately in line. Consensus responded by cutting the current quarter 34.5% and the next quarter 46.0% over 30 days, and next-year EPS 10.2%, even as the current-year figure rose on the Q2 mechanics. The stock fell 9.9% on July 31, the session after the print, on 4.3M shares.

We are maintaining HOLD for a fifth consecutive cycle, with the overall score moving from 6.0 to 5.7. That decline is not a reaction to the drawdown from the July 9 high of $90.87; a lower price on unchanged fundamentals would improve risk/reward. It reflects three specific developments: the milestone-dependent quality of the Q2 beat, the 30-day cuts to forward-quarter and next-year estimates, and guidance arithmetic that implies H2 GAAP earnings near breakeven. Financial health improves on the refinancing; near-term earnings visibility deteriorates. At $70.85 we see roughly 16% to a base case of $82 against 27% downside to $52, with the single largest valuation input — whether the $2.32B liability for sale of future royalties is genuinely non-recourse — unverified.

## Company Overview

PTC operates as a single reportable segment from Warren, New Jersey, with 991 employees at year-end 2025. Its business has four distinct economic layers, and conflating them is the most common error in analyzing this company.

**Owned commercial products.** Sephience for PKU (approved by the EC in June 2025, FDA in July 2025 for ages 1 month and up, Japan in December 2025 across all ages and the full severity spectrum, and Brazil in February 2026); Translarna for nonsense-mutation DMD, sold almost entirely ex-U.S.; Emflaza for DMD in the U.S.; and Upstaza/Kebilidi, an AAV gene therapy for AADC deficiency. H1 2026 product revenue split: Sephience $275.9M, Translarna $101.2M, Emflaza $46.1M, Upstaza/Kebilidi $20.8M, other $20.5M.

**A monetized royalty.** Evrysdi (risdiplam) was discovered under PTC's SMA collaboration and is developed and commercialized by Roche. PTC has sold the entire royalty to Royalty Pharma across four tranches for $2.1B cumulative cash; following Amendment No. 2 in December 2025 the 10-K states plainly that Royalty Pharma owns 100% and PTC owns 0%. Because the sale is accounted for as a financing, PTC still books the royalty as revenue ($71.1M in Q2) and books offsetting non-cash imputed interest at an 11.0% effective rate.

**An out-licensed clinical asset.** Votoplam (PTC518) for Huntington's disease was licensed to Novartis in November 2024 and closed January 2025 for a $1.0B upfront, up to $1.9B in remaining milestones, a 40% share of U.S. profits *and losses*, and tiered double-digit ex-U.S. royalties. Novartis funds and runs the global Phase 3 INVEST-HD study (~770 patients), initiated April 2026, which triggered the $50M milestone recognized in Q2.

**A lean internal pipeline.** Vatiquinone for Friedreich's ataxia received a Complete Response Letter in August 2025 and now requires the open-label, natural-history-controlled PROVE-FA study initiating Q3 2026 with a 24-month primary endpoint — resubmission no earlier than roughly 2029. Earlier assets include PTC612 (oral NLRP3 inhibitor, Phase 1 entered Q2 2026, including an obesity/cardiovascular cohort), PTC844 (DHODH inhibitor, Phase 2a expected Q3 2026) and PTC303 (MSH3 splicing, clinic targeted 2027). Q2 R&D of $99.2M included only about $49M of direct program spend; payroll, stock compensation and facilities were roughly half the line.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 reported results describe a transaction, not a business.** Total revenue of $1,730.7M and net income of +$682.6M (from a −$363.3M loss) look like an extraordinary margin inflection. Management stated on the February 2026 call that product plus royalty revenue was $831M, and the 10-K puts net product revenue at $586.7M. That implies roughly $899.7M of collaboration and license revenue — essentially the Novartis upfront. Backing it out of the $866.9M of reported operating income leaves a core operating result of approximately −$33M before adding back Novartis-related cost of sales. Any model, screen or ratio anchored to FY2025 margins, ROE or ROA is anchored to a single payment.

**Q2 2026 is where the real run-rate appears, and it needs the same treatment.** Total revenue was $360.5M, operating income $149.0M, net income $83.5M, diluted EPS $0.92. Subtract the $50M Novartis milestone and revenue was $310.5M. Subtract the $71.1M of royalty revenue that never becomes PTC cash and cash-generating revenue was $289.4M. Against $211.5M of operating expenses (cost of sales $19.9M, amortization $11.8M, R&D $99.2M, SG&A $80.6M) and roughly $17.3M of net cash interest income, the quarter produced approximately $95M of pre-tax economic result including the milestone, or roughly $45M excluding it — call it $180M annualized as a sensitivity, not a forecast.

Three reasons not to annualize: ex-U.S. ordering is extremely lumpy, working capital absorbed cash in H1, and cash taxes are unrepresentative. On lumpiness, the geographic disclosure is stark. Russia contributed $25.6M in Q2 but $28.8M in H1, implying roughly $3.2M in Q1. Brazil contributed $8.2M in Q2 but $49.4M in H1, implying roughly $41.2M in Q1. Individual countries swing by an order of magnitude quarter to quarter because governments place centralized multi-month purchase orders.

**Cash conversion is the weakest link.** H1 operating cash flow was $70.1M against $80.7M of net income — sub-1.0x, in a period that *included* the $50M cash milestone. Depreciation and amortization (~$31.3M) and stock compensation ($46.7M) add back, but the royalty structure nets to only +$13.1M, and receivables ($239.0M) plus inventory ($91.4M, up from $79.6M) absorbed cash with the launch. Cash taxes were $6.1M with no federal payment, versus a $14.0M Q2 book provision.

**The royalty liability mechanics matter for screens, not solvency.** The liability rose from $2,308.4M to $2,321.4M over H1: $131.0M of non-cash imputed interest less $117.9M of royalty applied. Annualized, roughly $262M of accruing interest against roughly $236M of royalty — negative amortization. This is the mechanical reason a company with $2.23B of cash reports a −$165.4M stockholders' deficit and a −$2,883.5M accumulated deficit, and why the Altman Z-Score lands in the grey zone at approximately 2.1 with the retained-earnings term contributing about −1.24. Since PTC owns 0% of the royalty, none of this touches cash.

**Capital structure is now clean.** In June 2026 PTC issued $550M of 0% coupon 2031 convertible notes (conversion price approximately $107.48, roughly 52% above today's price) for $535.4M net, and repurchased $222.0M principal of the 1.50% 2026 notes (conversion price $52.52) for approximately $328.8M cash — a roughly $107M premium over par, being the cash cost of settling deep in-the-money conversion value. A further $10.0M converted; $55.5M remains. The September 2026 maturity wall is effectively gone and cash interest is negligible. Maximum contingent convert dilution is now 6,174,052 shares, 7.40% of shares outstanding or 6.89% post-issuance ownership dilution, and the 2031 notes only convert economically above roughly $107. Separately, options outstanding (6,626,163 at a $47.16 weighted-average strike) and unvested RSUs (3,522,449) constitute a distinct overhang; unrecognized stock compensation is $206.8M over 2.9 years, with FY2026 SBC guided at $95M (~1.6% of market cap). The ATM program has $93.0M of $125M capacity unused, with no shares sold in H1 2026 or H1 2025.

Financial health scores 6.4, up from 5.4 in the prior cycle. The upgrade is attributable to the refinancing and the liquidity position, tempered by thin operating cash conversion and a contingent-payment stack that converts commercial success into cash outflows.

## Growth Analysis

**Sephience is performing.** 1,647 patients on commercial therapy worldwide at June 30, 2026, against a 10-K estimate of 58,000 PKU patients globally — roughly 2.8% penetration. Sequential growth of 21.4% in Q2 ($124.6M to $151.3M), 84% of Q2 revenue from the U.S. ($127.55M), a Japanese label spanning all ages and the full severity spectrum, and patent protection to 2038. The $30.0M net-sales milestone trigger is filing-grade confirmation of a >$250M trailing run rate roughly four quarters after launch.

**Consolidated growth is another matter.** The guidance arithmetic is the central fact for the next two quarters. Total revenue guidance of $1.18–1.28B implies H2 of $546.9–646.9M against $633.1M in H1, midpoint −5.7%. Layer on unchanged GAAP R&D plus SG&A guidance of $775–815M against roughly $360M spent in H1, and H2 operating income at guidance midpoints computes to roughly $90–100M versus $193.9M in H1 — which, against roughly $131M of semi-annual non-cash interest offset by roughly $36M of interest income, puts H2 GAAP pre-tax near breakeven. That is why the current-quarter consensus fell 34.5% to $0.31 and the next quarter 46.0% to $0.25 over 30 days: those two figures sum to $0.56 for H2 against $0.90 delivered in H1.

Three explanations for the guide are plausible and we cannot distinguish among them: conservatism after two consecutive raises; the Q1 Brazil order (~$41.2M) and Q2 Russia order ($25.6M) were pull-forwards; or DMD erosion accelerates with Emflaza's final exclusivity lapsing exactly at the quarter boundary in June 2026. Management's characterization of "greater durability than expected" in DMD, read against filings showing −30.5% and −45.2%, means the declines were less severe than feared, not that the franchise is stable.

**Optionality is real but not near-term.** Votoplam is the largest single upside lever: roughly 135,000 Huntington's patients globally with no approved disease-modifying therapy, 40% U.S. profit share, up to $1.9B in remaining milestones, and development funded and executed by Novartis. The April 2026 PIVOT-HD 24-month long-term-extension interim showed 52% slowing of progression on cUHDRS at 10mg versus an external natural-history cohort. PTC plans to engage FDA in H2 2026 on accelerated approval, but Phase 3 remains the base case, and the profit share is symmetric — PTC funds 40% of early U.S. commercial losses if votoplam launches. Vatiquinone is pushed to roughly 2029.

Growth prospects score 6.0. Sephience justifies a higher number in isolation; the consolidated trajectory, the 30-day estimate cuts, and the 2029 vatiquinone timeline do not.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $70.85 basis: market capitalization $5,913M; enterprise value excluding the royalty liability $5,913 + $591 − $2,229 = **$4,275M**; including it, $6,596M.

Against FY2026 guided cash-generating revenue of $944M–$1,044M (total revenue guidance less roughly $236M of non-cash royalty revenue), the ex-liability multiple is **4.1–4.5x**, and the inclusive multiple is **6.3–7.0x**. We favor the ex-liability construction — PTC owns 0% of the royalty and the liability is expected to be settled from a stream already sold — but flag that we have not read the A&R Royalty Purchase Agreement for shortfall, true-up or guarantee provisions. This is the largest single swing factor in the valuation and it is unresolved.

On earnings: 44.3x the current-year consensus of $1.60 and 28.3x the next-year $2.50. Backing out net cash, the next-year multiple compresses to roughly 18.6x ($4,275M EV against roughly $230M of implied net income on 92.0M diluted shares). That is not demanding for a rare-disease franchise with a 2038 patent and Huntington's optionality. Two qualifications matter. First, the $2.50 estimate comes from three analysts and was cut 10.2% in 30 days; it also implies roughly 56% EPS growth from a current-year base that the company's own opex guidance makes look generous. Second, the internal inconsistency in the consensus set (H2 quarterly estimates of $0.31 and $0.25 summing to $0.56, against a full-year $1.60 with H1 at $0.90) signals that the estimate base is unstable rather than a stale floor waiting to be revised up.

The peer set (AMRX 44.0x, APLS 38.4x, ACAD 9.7x, INDV 19.6x, ALKS 49.4x; median 38.4x) is too heterogeneous to anchor on — the dispersion from 9.7x to 49.4x reflects wholly different asset mixes and patent positions. It does establish that 28x forward is not an outlier for mid-cap specialty pharma.

Analyst targets are mean $97.86, median $98.00, low $70.00, high $130.00, with 11 buy or strong-buy, 2 hold and 1 sell, stable over three months. We are not treating those targets as a floor: the condition for doing so is rising estimates at a reasonable multiple, and forward estimates here are being cut.

Our scenarios:

- **Base $82** (+15.7%). Implies EV of roughly $5,206M, about 14x a normalized $370M of EBIT achievable by 2028 on roughly $1.25B of cash-generating revenue as Sephience scales toward $1B while DMD drag diminishes. Roughly 33x next-year consensus EPS, a premium to today justified by the cash position and 2038 exclusivity.
- **Bull $112** (+58.1%). Implies EV of roughly $7,710M. Requires Sephience patient adds to continue at the current pace toward $1.5B peak with net price holding, DMD erosion flattening, and credible progress toward accelerated approval for votoplam.
- **Bear $52** (−26.6%). Sephience plateaus near $700M as U.S. payers push back on net price, Translarna and Emflaza approach zero faster than guided, and one of Otsuka's JNT-517, Maze's MZE-782 or Agios's AG-181 posts strong Phase 2 data. Implies roughly $2.5B EV plus net cash, and sits above the 52-week low of $43.18.

Probability-weighting 30/45/25 gives approximately +18% expected return over twelve months. The dispersion is wide and the near-term catalyst path is thin. Valuation scores 5.5 — fair with modest positive skew, not an identified mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

**PKU is small and about to get crowded.** Sephience must take share from BioMarin's Kuvan (sapropterin, now genericized, oral, effective only in the BH4-responsive subset) and Palynziq (pegvaliase, injectable, adults with poor Phe control). PTC's differentiation claim is mechanistic: a BH4 precursor *plus* an independent chaperone effect, giving it potential in the most severe non-BH4-responsive mutations that Kuvan cannot reach. Early evidence supports it — 1,647 patients in four quarters and the broadest label in Japan. The medium-term threat is that four named development-stage competitors (Otsuka JNT-517, Maze MZE-782, Agios AG-181, SOM-1311) are pursuing oral Phe reduction aimed at the same non-responsive population. Composition-of-matter protection to 2038 blocks generics; it does not block novel mechanisms. Mechanistic differentiation in a 58,000-patient market erodes when a second oral option arrives, likely 2028 or later.

**DMD is lost and PTC has stopped fighting.** The 10-K names Santhera, Sarepta (three approved products plus Elevidys), NS Pharma, Dyne, Wave, Daiichi Sankyo, Nippon Shinyaku, Solid Biosciences and REGENXBIO. Translarna's addressable niche is roughly 13% of DMD by mutation type, it lost EEA authorization in March 2025, and the U.S. NDA was withdrawn with no further U.S. development planned. Emflaza has no patents. There is no realistic path to re-growing either.

**Upstaza/Kebilidi is the cleanest monopoly and the smallest market** — no competing therapy addressing the underlying cause of AADC deficiency and no known late-stage candidates, against roughly 5,000 patients worldwide.

**Huntington's is the highest-optionality position, held at arm's length.** PTC participates through economics rather than operations, which removes execution risk and caps control.

Industry-level pricing pressure runs against the model. The 10-K catalogs IRA Medicare negotiation exposure for certain orphan-designated drugs, state Drug Affordability Review Boards setting upper payment limits, EU reference pricing and parallel distribution, and the December 2025 EU political agreement to replace regulatory data protection with variable-duration exclusivity. For a company whose economics rest on very high per-patient pricing in small populations, net price is the primary long-term variable, and every disclosed trend points one direction.

Competitive position scores 6.4 — essentially stable versus the prior 6.6, with Sephience execution offsetting the accelerating DMD erosion.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-asset concentration.** Sephience was 59% of H1 product revenue and is the only growing asset of scale. Revenue depends on two independent variables: patient additions into a finite pool, and net price durability against generic sapropterin.

**Two simultaneous structural declines with no floor.** Emflaza has no patent protection and lost its final orphan exclusivity in June 2026. Translarna's EEA path now depends on discretionary, country-by-country Article 117(3)/5(1) invocations outside PTC's control, and the 10-K warns the EC decision may affect reauthorizations in non-EEA geographies.

**Regulatory friction is a pattern.** Translarna: CRL 2017, appeal denied 2018, Study 045 missed its endpoint, resubmitted 2024, withdrawn 2026. Vatiquinone: MOVE-FA missed its primary endpoint in 2023, NDA filed on subscale/extension data, CRL in August 2025 stating substantial evidence of efficacy was not demonstrated. PROVE-FA again relies on a natural-history control, though this time with FDA meeting minutes indicating such a design could be acceptable. An alternative reading is that ultra-rare neurodegenerative disease simply makes randomized trials hard, and PTC's experience is not materially worse than peers.

**Contingent payments on the growth asset.** $152.5M of $217.5M development/regulatory milestones already paid; $109.0M per additional indication; net-sales milestones of $30.0M (triggered Q2 2026), $50.0M at >$500M and $80.0M at >$1.0B trailing; plus five roughly $90M Additional Milestone Payments tied to rolling three- to eleven-year aggregate net-sales thresholds from $3B to $11B under the August 2025 Rights Satisfaction Agreement, which retired approximately 90% of an 8%/10%/12% tiered royalty for $225.1M cash. A roughly 10% residual royalty and a low-single-digit Shiratori royalty persist, so Sephience's contribution margin is structurally below a wholly-owned asset. Because milestones are capitalized to intangibles (Sephience gross $315.7M of $481.6M; net intangibles $402.9M, 10.7-year weighted life), reported operating margin understates the asset's full cost.

**Channel and geographic concentration.** Three distributors each exceeded 10% of Q2 net product sales. Russia ($28.8M) and Brazil ($49.4M) were roughly 17% of H1 product revenue. The allowance for doubtful accounts is $15.6M. France's early-access pricing mechanism already produced a −$98.6M life-to-date sales-allowance adjustment on Translarna and remains a recurring structural exposure.

**Governance and management signals.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks in five months (March 25, June 9, July 30, 2026). A June 9 exhibit is a consulting agreement with Alethia Young, previously CFO, while Pierre Gravier signed the Q2 10-Q as CFO — a CFO transition is the natural inference but is not confirmed by a retrieved press release. A former Chief Human Resources Officer joined the board weeks after her June 2026 retirement, raising an independence question for that seat. Across roughly 25 Form 4s from March to August 2026 there were no open-market purchases by officers or directors, only sales and exercise-and-sells at $63–$90, including a director selling at $90.00 on July 9 and the CEO selling 10,292 shares at $68.57 on August 4. Much of this is consistent with pre-scheduled 10b5-1 programs (director Schmertzler's plan for up to 170,000 shares through February 2027 is explicitly disclosed) and mechanical exercise-and-sell patterns, and the CEO retains 383,868 shares (~$27.2M). We treat the absence of buying as mildly unsupportive rather than a signal, but it carries more weight than it otherwise would because forward estimates are being cut rather than raised. Management quality scores 5.0: sound liability management and a defensible PSU design (absolute price, relative TSR versus the Nasdaq Biotechnology Index, and regulatory milestones), against elevated executive churn, a board-independence question, and one-directional insider activity.

**Verification gaps that a buyer should price.** Recourse status of the $2.32B royalty liability (determines a 4.1–4.5x versus 6.3–7.0x EV multiple); the FY2025 revenue disaggregation; the CFO transition; the cause of the de-rating from $90.87 on July 9 to $70.85, which no 8-K in the window explains.

Risk factors score 4.5.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($112, +58%).** Sephience is a genuine multi-billion-dollar franchise still at 2.8% penetration of a 58,000-patient pool, with 21.4% sequential growth, the broadest label in Japan, and exclusivity to 2038. H2 guidance proves conservative, as the last two guides did, with the DMD decline flattening after the June exclusivity cliff and Sephience approaching a $1B run rate through 2027. The $2.23B cash pile, negligible cash interest and no maturity until 2031 mean the company funds growth without dilution. Votoplam supplies an unpriced option: 135,000 patients, no approved disease-modifying therapy, 40% U.S. profit share plus $1.9B of remaining milestones, with Novartis carrying the cost. Next-year EPS of $2.50 proves conservative and the stock re-rates toward the $98 analyst median.

**Bear case ($52, −27%).** The H2 guide is honest, not conservative: Q1's Brazil order and Q2's Russia order were pull-forwards, and Emflaza plus Translarna decline faster than Sephience can grow, producing two or three quarters of flat-to-down consolidated revenue and near-breakeven GAAP results. U.S. payers, which supplied 84% of Q2 Sephience revenue at the highest realized price, compress net price against genericized sapropterin. One of the four oral development-stage competitors posts convincing data in the non-BH4-responsive population, compressing the terminal value of a mechanistically differentiated asset. The contingent-payment stack turns success into cash outflows. Next-year consensus, already cut 10% in 30 days, falls toward $1.80, and 28x becomes 20x on a lower number — $50 or below, still above the $43.18 low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (fifth consecutive). Hold rationale: growth_hold.** We see a positive twelve-month expected return of roughly 18% probability-weighted, driven by an identified growth asset and named catalysts, but the near-term earnings path is thin and the dispersion is too wide for ACCUMULATE.

- **Base case target: $82** (+15.7% from $70.85). Roughly 14x a normalized $370M of 2028 EBIT on approximately $1.25B of cash-generating revenue, equivalent to about $5.2B enterprise value plus net cash.
- **Bull case target: $112** (+58.1%).
- **Bear case target: $52** (−26.6%).
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q3 2026 print in late October (which resolves whether the H2 guide was conservatism or deceleration), FDA engagement on votoplam accelerated approval in H2 2026, PROVE-FA initiation in Q3 2026, and PTC844 Phase 2a data.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:** (1) Q3 product revenue lands at or above $230M, which would put H2 on track for the upper half of the $385.5–485.5M implied range and establish that the guide was conservative; (2) Sephience patient count exceeds roughly 2,300 at September 30 with U.S. net price stable, evidencing continued additions rather than a channel-fill artifact; (3) FDA agrees to an accelerated-approval pathway for votoplam, or (4) the price falls toward $58–62 with the above metrics intact, which would restore a margin of safety against the bear case.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if:** (1) Q3 product revenue falls below roughly $190M, implying the H2 guide was optimistic; (2) Sephience sequential growth turns negative or U.S. gross-to-net deteriorates materially; (3) next-year consensus EPS is cut below $2.00, which at 28x would place fair value near $56; (4) an oral PKU competitor reports Phase 2 efficacy in non-BH4-responsive patients comparable to Sephience; or (5) disclosure emerges that the royalty liability carries recourse, shortfall or true-up obligations, which would move EV to roughly $6.6B and 6.3–7.0x cash-generating revenue.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026.

## Conclusion

PTC is a better company than it was twelve months ago on every balance-sheet and franchise-quality measure: a growing lead asset with exclusivity to 2038, $2.23B of liquidity, no maturity until 2031, negligible cash interest, and free optionality on Huntington's carried by a large-cap partner. It is also a company whose consolidated revenue line is being partly consumed by two structural declines, whose most recent EPS beat came entirely from a non-recurring milestone, and whose own guidance implies second-half product revenue roughly 6% below the first half with GAAP earnings near breakeven. Forward-quarter estimates were cut 34% and 46% in the last thirty days for that reason.

At $70.85 the shares offer roughly 16% to our base case, 58% to the bull case and 27% downside to the bear case — positive expected value with insufficient asymmetry. The rating stays HOLD; the score moves from 6.0 to 5.7 on deteriorating near-term earnings visibility, partly offset by an improved capital structure. Existing holders have no reason to sell into a launch that is working. New capital should wait for the Q3 print to resolve the guidance question, or for a price in the high $50s to low $60s that pays for the unverified royalty-recourse question and the four oral competitors coming in 2028.
