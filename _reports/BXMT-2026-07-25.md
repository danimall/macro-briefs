**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Blackstone Mortgage Trust is a commercial real estate finance REIT, externally managed by a Blackstone subsidiary, that originates senior floating-rate mortgage loans against institutional-quality commercial property. The investment case turns on a single question: how much further does the office-driven credit cycle have to run, and is the $20.75 book value close enough to right that the current ~0.80x price-to-book represents value rather than a warning. FY2025 marked a return to GAAP profitability ($109.6M net income, $0.64 EPS) after a $204M loss in 2024, but the recovery was driven almost entirely by a smaller CECL reserve build ($112.5M vs. $538.8M), not by operational improvement. Net interest income fell 23% YoY, from $479M to $367M, as the loan book shrank roughly $3.5B and impaired loans stopped accruing.

The dividend, reset from ~$0.62/quarter in 2024 to $0.47 currently ($1.88 annualized, ~11.3% yield at $16.69), is covered by core distributable earnings ($1.86/share prior to charge-offs) with essentially no margin of safety. Q1 2026 illustrated the fragility: distributable EPS of $0.21 came in well below the $0.47 dividend, and even the "prior to realized losses" figure of $0.49 only barely clears the payout. Consensus estimates have been cut steadily — current-year EPS down 13.2% over 90 days — and the company has missed EPS estimates in four consecutive quarters. This is a high-yield, show-me situation.

We rate BXMT a HOLD on an income basis. The stock offers a genuine ~11% yield, real structural defense (non-mark-to-market financing, $1.0B liquidity, no corporate maturities until 2027), and the sourcing advantage of the Blackstone platform. But the office tail is still open — six impaired loans plus 17 watchlist ("rated-4") loans totaling ~$2.9B in net book value are the swing factor — net interest income is structurally pressured, and there is no open-market insider conviction buying. The score has improved across four reads (3.9 → 4.7) as the credit trough passed, and that trajectory is warranted, but the setup does not yet support a BUY. Own it for the yield if you can underwrite the office marks; do not underwrite it for near-term appreciation.

## Company Overview

BXMT originates, acquires, and manages senior, floating-rate mortgage loans secured by institutional commercial real estate across North America, Europe, and Australia, operating as a single reporting segment. As of 12/31/2025 the core loan portfolio comprised 131 loans, $18.2B principal balance, weighted-average origination LTV of 64.9%, 97% floating rate (primarily SOFR-indexed), a 2.5-year weighted-average maturity, and an all-in yield of benchmark +3.39%. Including $1.3B of owned real estate (foreclosed collateral) and JV interests, the total investment portfolio is roughly $20.0B.

The company is externally managed by BXMT Advisors L.L.C., a Blackstone subsidiary, and has no employees. CEO and Chairman Tim Johnson is also Global Head of BREDS (Blackstone Real Estate Debt Strategies). The Manager earns base management, incentive, and expense-reimbursement fees (FY2025 management/incentive fees: $67.6M). The Blackstone affiliation is the substantive competitive advantage: access to the largest CRE owner in the world ($319B of real estate AUM, 787 professionals) provides proprietary market data, deal sourcing, and workout capability that a standalone REIT cannot replicate, which is genuinely valuable in a workout-heavy cycle.

BXMT maintains a set of joint ventures with other Blackstone-advised vehicles: a Bank Loan Portfolio JV (35% owned, buying performing loans from regional banks at discounts — $2.0B acquired in 2025), a Net Lease JV (75% owned, 178 triple-net properties), and a $75M commitment to a BREDS Core+ debt fund. These represent a deliberate pivot toward diversified, performing-loan income and away from pure transitional office/CRE lending, and BXMT pays no management fees or carry on its investments in these Blackstone-advised vehicles.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is a turnaround from a credit-loss trough, financed by deliberate balance-sheet shrinkage, with a dividend that has already been reset lower once.

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Total assets | ~$22B | ~$20B |
| Net income (loss) attrib. | $(204.1)M | +$109.6M |
| EPS (basic) | $(1.17) | $0.64 |
| Interest & related income | $1,769M | $1,356M |
| Net interest income | $479M | $367M |
| CECL reserve build (P&L) | $(538.8)M | $(112.5)M |
| Dividends declared/share | $2.18 | $1.88 |
| Book value/share | $21.87 | $20.75 |

The return to GAAP profitability should not be mistaken for operational recovery. On the distributable-earnings lens the picture is harsher: FY2025 distributable earnings were a loss of $245.3M ($(1.43)/share) after $562.9M of CECL charge-offs. Prior to charge-offs, distributable earnings were $317.6M ($1.86/share) — almost exactly the $1.88 dividend. That equality is the crux of the dividend-coverage story: covered, but with near-zero cushion.

Deleveraging is deliberate. In 2025 BXMT executed $6.1B of repayments and sales, including $2.3B of exited office loans, against $5.7B of new originations. Debt-to-equity rose to 3.9x (from 3.5x) and total leverage to 4.5x; the ratios climbed despite the shrinkage because equity fell (buybacks plus book-value erosion). Corporate debt is $2.9B; asset-level financing is $13.3B, much of it structured as non-mark-to-market (CLOs, asset-specific debt, term-matched secured facilities), which materially limits margin-call risk. The company lowered secured-debt spreads to +1.83% (from +1.92%) and issued a $1.0B CLO. Post-year-end it borrowed $770.8M under a new B-9 Term Loan (SOFR+2.50%, due 2030) to repay the higher-cost B-6, extending maturities.

On capital allocation, BXMT repurchased $109.4M of stock at an average $18.20 (accretive to book value by $0.13) and refreshed a $150M buyback authorization in October 2025, while maintaining an ATM program (~$481M capacity). The low Altman Z-Score (~0.4) flags "distress," but this is largely an artifact of applying a manufacturing-oriented bankruptcy model to a highly leveraged financial REIT; with $1.0B liquidity, non-MTM financing, and no corporate maturities until 2027, the relevant risk is credit quality and dividend coverage, not near-term insolvency. On balance, financial health earns a below-average 4.5: resilience against forced selling is real, but the earnings quality is weak, the dividend cushion is thin, and leverage is high with rising ratios.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the story on a near-term basis and the most interesting on a multi-year basis. Net interest income is under structural pressure from three directions: a shrinking loan book, lower-spread new originations (many 2025 European/industrial loans at +2.4%–3.0% replacing higher-yielding legacy paper), and a potential rate-cutting cycle. BXMT's floating-rate model benefits from rising rates and is hurt by cuts, though a weighted-average index floor of 1.31% provides partial protection. Consensus reflects this: current-quarter EPS estimates are down 8.4% over 90 days, next-quarter down 16.1%, and current-year down 13.2%. The four-quarter earnings track record is four consecutive misses, including a catastrophic Q4 2025 print ($-2.07 vs. $0.27 estimate) tied to charge-offs. Consensus cannot be treated as conservative here.

The counter-cyclical growth engine is the diversification pivot. The Bank Loan Portfolio JV is an underappreciated positive that leverages the Blackstone platform to source assets a standalone REIT could not, with fee-free economics on the Blackstone-vehicle investments. The 2025 origination mix skewed toward European/UK/Australian industrial and multifamily rather than U.S. office, and the Net Lease JV adds stable, long-duration income (15-year WAL, 3x rent coverage). The strategic question is whether these income streams can rebuild NII fast enough to offset the office runoff. Next-year consensus EPS of $1.95 (essentially flat over 90 days, unlike the falling near-term estimates) implies the market expects NII to inflect back up as the office overhang clears and the diversified book scales. That is plausible but unproven. Growth prospects score 4.5: a credible multi-year rebuild against a demonstrably deteriorating near-term earnings trend.

## Valuation Assessment

BXMT trades at $16.69, roughly 0.80x its $20.75 book value, with a ~11.3% dividend yield. Relative to mortgage-REIT peers, its ~11% yield sits below the peer median of 13.1%, and its price-to-book discount is narrower than the most distressed names (ARR -16%, PMT -27%, ABR -60% from 52-week highs) but wider than higher-quality ARI (-4%). The reported trailing P/E of ~27x is not a meaningful metric here — it reflects trough earnings depressed by charge-offs, and for a mortgage REIT, price-to-book and dividend coverage are the correct lenses.

The valuation debate reduces to book-value credibility. The bull case is that the $20.75 book value is close to right, office marks are conservative enough, and the stock re-rates toward 1x as impaired loans resolve, implying roughly 24% upside plus the yield. The bear case is that office marks are still too optimistic, further markdowns compress book value, and the discount persists or widens. The six impaired ("rated-5") loans plus 17 watchlist ("rated-4") loans totaling ~$2.9B in net book value are the swing factor. We are cautious about treating the discount to book as a clear signal: a cheap multiple against an office-heavy book that management itself warns "may continue" to require reserve increases is exactly the kind of setup where cheapness reflects genuine risk rather than mispricing. The absence of any identified near-term catalyst to close the gap, combined with falling estimates, keeps this a value-trap risk rather than a value opportunity.

Analyst targets span $19 (low) to $23 (high), with a $20.75 mean, implying 14% to 38% upside, but these targets have been drifting lower (KBW cut its target in late June 2026), so they function as soft anchors rather than firm floors, particularly given the negative estimate-revision trend. We score valuation a neutral 5.0: there is legitimate discount-to-book support and a plausible re-rating path, but no catalyst and a book value the market is right to question.

## Competitive Landscape

BXMT competes with commercial mortgage REITs (Starwood Property Trust, KKR Real Estate Finance, Apollo Commercial, Ladder Capital), banks, insurers, and private debt funds. The entire transitional-CRE-lending sector endured a severe 2022–2025 downturn driven by higher rates, the WFH-driven office demand collapse, and frozen CRE capital markets. BXMT appears past the peak of reserve-building, with 2024 the trough and 2025 the recovery to profitability.

The durable competitive edge is the Blackstone platform's information and workout advantages, which are most valuable during a distressed cycle. The non-mark-to-market financing structure (CLOs, asset-specific and term-matched secured debt) is a genuine differentiator versus more fragile peers who faced forced selling during the 2023 regional-bank stress. The strategic shift toward regional-bank loan portfolio purchases, geographic and property diversification, and net-lease income improves the competitive position at the margin. Offsetting this is the external-management conflict structure: the Manager and Blackstone affiliates compete for the same deals, JVs are struck with Blackstone-advised vehicles, and investment guidelines can be amended by the board without shareholder approval. Competitive position scores 5.5: a stable-to-modestly-improving trajectory anchored by a real platform advantage, tempered by conflicts and the ongoing office runoff.

## Risk Assessment

**Office CRE credit risk** is the dominant, company-specific risk. Of FY2025 charge-offs, $338M was concentrated in office. Six loans carry an $87.3M asset-specific CECL reserve, and essentially all six impaired loans are office or office-adjacent (Orange County, Austin, London, Chicago office; Boston life sciences/studio; a past-maturity San Francisco hospitality loan). Management explicitly warns office reserves "may continue" to rise and that future increases will be concentrated in the rated-"4" loans. This is the watchlist to monitor.

**Dividend coverage is thin.** The payout is just covered by core distributable earnings but was not covered by Q1 2026's distributable EPS. Continued realized losses could pressure the dividend again, and it has already been cut once.

**Office is migrating from the loan book onto the balance sheet.** BXMT now owns and operates $1.3B of foreclosed real estate across 12 assets, 8 of them office. Owned-RE revenue was $185M against $215.6M of expenses plus $70M of depreciation; the portfolio currently runs an operating loss, converting a credit problem into an equity real-estate problem and a drag on distributable earnings. The realization values on eventual sales versus carrying value are a key unknown.

**Refinancing wall** arrives in 2027: $335M Senior Secured Notes (Jan 2027) and $266M Convertible Notes (March 2027, conversion price $36.27, deeply out of the money). No corporate maturities before then reduces near-term forced-selling risk.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and does not corroborate an acute-downside thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 27.5% against 22.4% one-year historical volatility — modestly rich but unremarkable, with an IV percentile of just 33% within its own chain. Put/call skew is balanced (2.4%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.49, and the term structure is essentially flat (near 27.5% vs. far 28.5%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.0%. The put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 2.17 to 0.10 over the past month, indicating that prior bearish/hedging positioning has been unwound. Short interest is moderate at 7.5% of float with 5.1 days to cover, neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The options market is not pricing an imminent binary event, consistent with a slow-grind credit-resolution story.

Risk factors score 4.5: the office tail is material, specific, and not fully resolved, but the structural defenses (non-MTM financing, liquidity, maturity runway) and the fact that much of the credit stress is already reflected in the discount-to-book keep this from scoring lower.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($20–23, +20% to +38% plus ~11% yield):** The 2024 reserve build was the trough, office marks prove close to adequate, and impaired/watchlist loans resolve without materially further impairing the book. The diversification pivot (bank-loan JV, European/industrial/multifamily originations, net lease) rebuilds NII, distributable earnings comfortably re-cover the dividend, and the stock re-rates from 0.80x toward 1x book. In this scenario the investor collects an ~11% yield while the discount closes, delivering a mid-20s to high-30s total return over 12–18 months.

**Bear Case ($13–14, -16% to -22%):** Office continues to deteriorate — the rated-"4" loans migrate to impaired, owned real estate sells below carrying value, and book value grinds lower. Falling rates compress NII below the index floor's protection, distributable earnings fail to cover the dividend, and BXMT cuts the payout a second time. The stock de-rates further on a shrinking book and reduced yield, revisiting the low end of its 52-week range.

The realistic base case sits closer to the current price than to either extreme: a slow credit resolution, a held-but-uncovered-in-weak-quarters dividend, and a stock that oscillates in the $16–19 range while the office overhang clears and the diversified book scales.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July 2026), followed by each subsequent quarter. The key data points are distributable EPS prior to and after realized losses, the count and net book value of rated-"4" and rated-"5" loans, and any change in the dividend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Distributable EPS prior to realized losses stabilizes at or above $0.47 for two consecutive quarters, with realized losses declining, confirming dividend coverage.
- The rated-"4" watchlist bucket shrinks meaningfully without migrating to impaired, evidencing office stabilization.
- Net interest income inflects positively QoQ as diversified/performing-loan income scales, reversing the 23% decline.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second dividend cut, or distributable EPS prior to realized losses falling below $0.40.
- Book value declining below ~$19 on further office markdowns, indicating the current 0.80x discount is not conservative enough.
- Owned real estate sold materially below carrying value, or the owned-RE operating loss widening.

## Conclusion

BXMT is a credit-cycle recovery story trading at a ~20% discount to book with a high but only-just-covered ~11% dividend. The turnaround from the 2024 loss is real but fragile, powered by declining reserves rather than growing net interest income, with a still-material office tail on the loan book and a growing, loss-making owned-office portfolio. The Blackstone platform, non-mark-to-market financing, and diversification pivot are genuine strengths that justify the improving score trajectory (3.9 → 4.7 across four reads) and the upgrade from AVOID to HOLD. But thin dividend coverage, falling near-term estimates, four consecutive earnings misses, and the absence of any catalyst to close the book discount keep this short of a BUY.

We rate BXMT a **HOLD** with an income rationale. It is ownable for the ~11% yield by investors who can underwrite the office marks and tolerate the coverage risk. It is not a name to buy for near-term capital appreciation, and the case would strengthen materially only when distributable earnings demonstrate durable dividend coverage and the watchlist loan bucket shrinks. Current price $16.69; base-case fair value roughly $17–18 (book-value discount holding while credit resolves), with the yield doing the work rather than the multiple.
