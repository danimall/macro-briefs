**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rush Enterprises operates the largest network of commercial-vehicle dealerships in North America: 126 franchised Rush Truck Centers across 23 U.S. states and Canada, selling and servicing Class 4–8 trucks under a single Truck Segment. Its defining feature is the aftermarket ecosystem wrapped around truck sales. In 2025 the aftermarket (parts, service, collision, upfitting, leasing, F&I) generated 33.9% of revenue but 63.7% of gross profit, and its 130.7% absorption ratio means aftermarket gross profit more than covers all dealership fixed overhead. This is the structural reason net income rose 1.9% in Q1 2026 even as revenue fell 9%, a genuinely defensive characteristic that a "heavy equipment / cyclical" screen would misread.

The core debate is cyclical timing. New Class 8 unit sales fell 17.4% in 2025 after an 11.4% decline in 2024, the third straight year of a freight recession. The bull case is a trough-to-recovery play, supported by ACT Research's +5.7% U.S. Class 8 forecast for 2026, a backlog that rose to $1.46B in Q1 2026 (from $1.11B at year-end 2025), and a possible 2027 EPA Low NOx pre-buy as fleets accelerate purchases of cheaper pre-regulation diesels. The bear case is that near-term consensus estimates are falling (current-quarter EPS revised down 4.7% over 90 days), tariffs and macro uncertainty cloud the demand recovery, and the stock has already re-rated 41% over twelve months to a fresh 52-week high of $80.17.

Our recommendation is HOLD (growth_hold). The business quality and cyclical-recovery optionality are real, but the setup is no longer asymmetric. At $76.57 the stock trades at roughly 21x current-year consensus EPS ($3.67), a premium to the 18.5x peer median, while near-term estimate revisions are negative and analyst targets ($78 low / $90 high, $85 mean) put the current price only modestly below the low end of the range. The prior BUY-to-HOLD trajectory (6.7 in May to 5.8 in June) reflected this compression of risk/reward, and the current data confirms rather than reverses it. We would re-engage as a BUY on a pullback toward the mid-$60s or on confirmed backlog/pre-buy acceleration converting to positive estimate revisions.

## Company Overview

Rush Enterprises began as a single Peterbilt dealership in 1966 and has built the densest full-service commercial-vehicle retail network in North America. It sells new and used Class 4–8 trucks and buses and provides an integrated one-stop aftermarket ecosystem: parts, service, collision repair, mobile service units, upfitting, telematics, leasing/rental, and finance-and-insurance products. The company reports as a single Truck Segment.

Franchise concentration is central to both the moat and the risk. Peterbilt, a division of PACCAR, accounted for roughly 26.1% of total revenue in 2025; International (formerly Navistar) about 14.7%; and the remaining OEMs (Ford, Hino, Isuzu, Blue Bird, IC Bus, Battle Motors, Blue Arc) roughly 11.1%. The balance is aftermarket and used-vehicle revenue.

The economic engine is the absorption model. Management tracks how much of each dealership's fixed overhead is covered by aftermarket gross profit; a ratio above 100% means the recurring service-and-parts business alone keeps the dealership at breakeven before a single new truck is sold. RUSHA ran a 130.7% absorption ratio in 2025 and 126.9% in Q1 2026. Because aftermarket gross profit more than covers fixed costs, essentially all new and used truck gross profit (net of selling costs) flows through to operating profit. This keeps the company profitable through the trough of a truck cycle, and the mix is more defensive than the "construction machinery / heavy transportation" label implies.

## Financial Analysis

The cyclical downturn is unmistakable in the top line, but the P&L has held up because of mix.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 vs Q1'25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 6.78 | 7.57 | 7.45 | 7.06 | 1.68 vs 1.85 (–9.0%) |
| Operating income ($M) | 506 | 512 | 468 | 394 | 82 vs 92 |
| Net income ($M) | 391 | 347 | 304 | 264 | 61.5 vs 60.3 (+1.9%) |
| Operating margin | 7.5% | 6.8% | 6.3% | 5.6% | 4.9% vs 5.0% |
| Gross margin | 21.9% | 21.0% | 20.6% | 20.7% | 20.4% vs 19.3% |

Revenue has fallen three consecutive years off the 2023 peak, driven by the freight recession. New Class 8 unit sales dropped to 12,770 units in 2025 and Class 8 revenue fell 16.6%. Yet the mix shift is protecting the income statement: aftermarket revenue was roughly flat (+0.3% in 2025, +1.3% in Q1'26) at approximately $2.52B, and aftermarket gross margin ticked up to 36.9%. New Class 8 gross margin compressed to 8.6% in 2025 and 7.8% in Q1'26 (from 9.7%), reflecting competitive downturn pricing, but the higher-margin aftermarket carried the blend.

Q1 2026 net income rose year-over-year despite the revenue decline, aided by a roughly 50% drop in net interest expense (lower rates plus lower inventory financing) and a low 18.2% tax rate from stock-option-exercise benefits.

Cash generation is healthy. 2025 operating cash flow was $861.8M (inflated by inventory drawdown given floor-plan accounting) and free cash flow was approximately $462M, a 6.5% FCF margin. The balance sheet is sound: an Altman Z of 3.04 sits in the safe zone, current ratio is 1.4, and equity is roughly 50% of assets. The headline debt/equity of 2.01 overstates leverage, because most of the "debt" is floor-plan notes payable (~$918M), inventory financing collateralized by trucks that is normal and self-liquidating for a dealer. Core long-term debt is only about $275–278M. All debt is variable-rate, so a 100bp move translates to roughly $12M of annual interest.

On capital allocation, the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.19 (~$58M/year, a ~1.0% yield). A new $150M buyback was authorized on December 3, 2025, but nothing was repurchased in Q1 2026, after $194.9M of repurchases in 2025 under the prior program. Roughly $300–350M per year goes into the lease/rental fleet as recurring capex.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is cyclical, not secular, and it hinges on the timing of a freight recovery.

The near-term data cuts both ways. The bullish signal is the backlog: after collapsing 27% at year-end 2025, it rose to $1.46B at March 31, 2026 (from $1.40B a year earlier, +4.5% YoY). This diverges from the falling trend and is consistent with either early recovery ordering or a nascent 2027 emissions pre-buy. ACT Research forecasts U.S. Class 8 retail sales of 224,800 units in 2026, and management guides its share to 5.3–6.0%, or roughly 12,000–13,500 units, with aftermarket "flat to slightly up."

Estimate momentum is the bearish signal. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been revised down 3.3% over 30 days and 4.7% over 90 days; next-quarter down 2.4% over 90 days; and current-year down 0.6%. This is the opposite of the strong positive revision velocity that would signal a confirmed inflection. The one bright spot is next-year EPS, revised up 3.3% over 90 days to $4.53: analysts are penciling in a recovery but pushing it out. The earnings track record is respectable (three beats and one miss over four quarters, including a 14.0% beat in Q4'25 and a 7.9% beat in Q1'26), which supports confidence in near-term delivery even as the estimates drift lower.

The 2027 pre-buy is the primary upside optionality. The EPA Low NOx rule takes effect January 2027, and history shows fleets accelerate purchases of cheaper, simpler pre-regulation diesels ahead of such deadlines. If that dynamic materializes in the second half of 2026, unit volumes and backlog would surprise to the upside. The regulatory backdrop is genuinely uncertain (the EPA has repealed the GHG Endangerment Finding and Congress rescinded CARB's preemption waivers, all under active litigation), but the Low NOx rule itself is still expected to take effect, keeping the pre-buy thesis alive.

Growth prospects are directionally positive over a 12-month horizon but not yet confirmed by revision data, so we score this dimension modestly, weighting the aftermarket defensiveness and backlog inflection against the falling near-term estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $76.57 with trailing EPS of $3.27, RUSHA trades at roughly 23x trailing and about 21x current-year consensus EPS. Against the peer median P/E of 18.5x (GATX 18.5x, TRN 10.8x, ALG 18.5x, GBX 10.1x, TEX 43.3x), the stock carries a clear premium. On next-year consensus EPS of $4.53, the forward multiple compresses to roughly 16.9x, which is more reasonable but assumes the recovery lands on schedule.

We treat this as a valuation dimension where the "cheapness" case is weak. The stock is not cheap on current-year earnings, and while the forward multiple looks better on a recovery, the recovery is not yet visible in near-term estimate revisions, which are falling. The earnings-inflection framework rewards cheap stocks with positive revision velocity, and RUSHA has neither: it is at a premium to peers, not a cyclical trough multiple, and its 90-day revision trend is negative.

The analyst price targets frame the setup. The low target is $78, the mean $85, the median $86, and the high $90, putting the current price about 2% below the low end of the range and roughly 11% below the mean. On an inflection stock with rising estimates, analyst targets act as floors; here, with falling near-term estimates and the stock at a fresh 52-week high, the targets are more likely near ceilings, and the upside to the mean is a modest 11% against genuine downside if the recovery slips. The dividend yield of roughly 1.0% offers little valuation support.

The 41% twelve-month run is not itself a reason to mark the stock down. But the run has taken the multiple to a peer premium at a point where the fundamental case (falling near-term estimates, backlog inflection not yet in the P&L) does not obviously justify paying up. The valuation score reflects a fully-valued-to-slightly-rich setup with no identifiable near-term mispricing to exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

Rush competes against dealers of Freightliner (Daimler), Kenworth (PACCAR's other brand), Mack, and Volvo, plus independent service centers and parts wholesalers. Its differentiation is scale, geographic density, and service capability: the ability to absorb multi-unit fleet trade-ins, redistribute used inventory across a national network, and keep large fleets running through the densest one-stop service footprint in the industry.

The competitive trajectory is favorable at the margin. As trucks grow more complex and service requires heavy capital investment in diagnostics and EPA-compliant collision and paint capability, competition is shifting from price toward uptime and service capability. That structurally favors the largest, best-capitalized players, and Rush is the largest. The absorption model is evidence the moat is holding: aftermarket revenue was flat-to-up while truck volumes fell double digits, and aftermarket gross margin expanded.

The offsetting long-term threat is battery-electric trucks, which have fewer parts and require less service, a structural risk to the aftermarket engine. This is a multi-year, not near-term, concern, and the current regulatory rollback of ZEV mandates pushes it further out. Rush is also deepening its core Peterbilt footprint via the $35M Peterbilt of Louisiana acquisition signed in Q1 2026, following Leeds Transit ($25.6M) and Nebraska Peterbilt ($16.5M).

The competitive position is solid and improving in relative terms, but the score reflects the growing structural dependence on PACCAR (now Rush's largest OEM, a primary inventory lender via the new $800M PFC floor plan, and the holder of franchise-termination rights) and the long-dated EV threat to the aftermarket.

## Risk Assessment

**OEM concentration and the change-of-control provision.** This is the single most idiosyncratic risk. Peterbilt can terminate the dealership agreements if the Dealer Principals' aggregate voting power falls below 22% (currently ~40.8%), or if anyone other than Rusty Rush becomes Chairman plus CEO/President without PACCAR's written approval. The crown-jewel franchise is therefore contingent on continued Rush-family control, including estate-planning and estate-tax scenarios around Rusty Rush's super-voting Class B stock, which the company explicitly states it cannot control. The provision also deters acquirers and constrains buyback flexibility.

**Cyclicality.** The recovery thesis depends on the freight recession bottoming in 2026. Q1 2026 U.S. Class 8 retail sales fell 21% YoY and Class 4–7 fell 25.6%. If the recovery slips into 2027, near-term earnings and the backlog inflection reverse.

**Tariffs.** Newly enacted 25% commercial-vehicle tariffs on certain medium/heavy trucks and parts are an unquantified overhang on demand. Management expects price increases well below 25%, but the effect on backlog and unit demand is unclear.

**Deepening PACCAR dependence.** The 2024–25 financing reorganization routed Peterbilt inventory financing directly through PACCAR Financing (PFC, $800M) while cutting the BMO floor plan from $1.0B to $675M. Peterbilt is now the largest revenue source, a primary inventory lender, and the holder of franchise-termination rights, making PACCAR's health and goodwill existential to Rush.

**Management-change cluster.** A dense sequence of Item 5.02 (departure/election of officers/directors) 8-Ks filed on March 5, March 19, March 24, and June 30, 2026 is unresolved in the underlying data. Given the named-executive franchise provisions (Keller, Lowe, Pollard, Wilder, Goldstone, Eppes, McRoberts are designated Dealer Principals), any transitions involving these individuals warrant scrutiny for succession and control implications. Until the filings are resolved, this is an open risk rather than a confirmed problem.

**Long-term EV disruption.** BEV trucks threaten the aftermarket gross-profit engine over the multi-year horizon.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 45.3% against a one-year historical volatility of 30.2%, rich, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 45.3% vs. six-month 33.5%). The market is pricing imminent stress, likely around the upcoming earnings print, with an implied move of ±10.2% at the nearest expiry. Put/call skew at the front is balanced (1.6%) but turns modestly put-heavy at six months (-5.1%), consistent with hedging the recovery downside. Short interest is elevated at 12.8% of float with 8.3 days to cover: institutions carry meaningful bearish positioning, which creates modest squeeze potential on a positive surprise but also signals sophisticated skepticism about the recovery timing. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing the stock ahead of the print; better entry would follow an IV crush post-earnings.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$90–100).** The freight recession bottoms in H1 2026, ACT's Class 8 forecast proves conservative, and a 2027 EPA Low NOx pre-buy accelerates H2 orders. The backlog converts to unit sales, next-year EPS of $4.53 proves achievable or beatable, and the aftermarket keeps compounding margins. On a recovery, 20x on ~$4.75+ EPS supports ~$95, with the elevated short interest providing upside fuel on positive surprises. Reaches the $90 analyst high and beyond.

**Bear case (~$58–62).** The recovery slips into 2027, tariffs suppress demand, and near-term estimates keep falling. Current-year EPS lands closer to $3.50, the multiple compresses toward the peer median, and the stock gives back its cyclical premium. The management-change cluster surfaces a succession or Dealer-Principal issue that reintroduces the Peterbilt change-of-control tail. At 16–17x on flat-to-down earnings, the stock retraces to the low-$60s, roughly 20% below current levels.

**Base case (~$82).** The recovery is real but gradual, unit sales rise modestly in line with ACT, aftermarket stays flat-to-up, and the model keeps net income resilient. EPS lands around $3.70–3.90 for 2026 with next-year momentum building. At ~21x on improving forward earnings, the stock reaches the low-$80s, roughly in line with the analyst mean: about 7% upside from current levels, insufficient to justify a BUY at the top of the analyst range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold): a positive but not-yet-confirmed 12-month cyclical-recovery thesis where the current price offers inadequate margin of safety.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$60s, which would restore asymmetric risk/reward at roughly 17x current-year EPS.
- Positive current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimate revision turn (from the current negative trend to positive), confirming the recovery is landing on schedule.
- Backlog acceleration above ~$1.6B or explicit management confirmation of 2027 pre-buy ordering in H2 2026, converting the backlog inflection into visible unit-sale momentum.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmation that the Item 5.02 management-change 8-Ks involve a Dealer Principal or a succession event that jeopardizes the Peterbilt change-of-control provisions.
- Current-year EPS estimates falling below $3.40 alongside a backlog reversal, signaling the recovery has stalled.
- Aftermarket gross profit turning down year-over-year, which would break the defensive absorption story.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (late July 2026, Q2) is the immediate checkpoint: watch backlog, aftermarket trend, management pre-buy commentary, and the resumption or continued pause of the $150M buyback. The 2027 emissions deadline makes H2 2026 the key window for the pre-buy thesis to confirm or fail.

## Conclusion

Rush Enterprises is a higher-quality business than its "heavy transportation equipment" label suggests. The aftermarket/absorption model, growing margins through a three-year freight recession, is a genuine defensive characteristic, and the Q1 2026 net-income increase on a revenue decline demonstrates it. The backlog inflection, ACT's 2026 forecast, and the potential 2027 EPA pre-buy give the cyclical-recovery thesis substance.

The setup is no longer asymmetric. The stock has re-rated to a fresh 52-week high, trades above the peer median, and sits only ~2% below the low analyst target with ~7–11% upside to the mean and median. Near-term estimate revisions are falling, not rising, so the strongest inflection signal is absent. The Peterbilt change-of-control provision, the deepening PACCAR dependence, and an unresolved cluster of management-change filings add idiosyncratic tail risk that the premium multiple does not compensate. The declining score trajectory (BUY at 6.7 in May to HOLD at 5.8 in June) reflected this compression, and the current data confirms it.

We rate RUSHA a HOLD. The business is worth owning through the cycle, but the price already discounts the recovery. We would upgrade to BUY on a pullback to the mid-$60s or on confirmed positive estimate revisions and backlog acceleration.
