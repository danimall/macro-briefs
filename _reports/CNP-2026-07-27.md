**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CenterPoint Energy is a pure-play regulated multi-utility whose investment case has narrowed to a single variable: it is the wires operator for the fastest-growing large-load region in the United States. The Q1 2026 update pushed firmly committed load from ~7.5 GW to ~12.2 GW and pulled the "50% Houston load growth" milestone forward two years to year-end 2029, with roughly 8 GW of data-center load expected to be energized by 2029. That is a differentiated, above-consensus growth signal for a regulated utility, and it underwrites a ~$65.5B ten-year capital plan (with $10B+ of identified incremental opportunity on top) and a 7-9% long-term non-GAAP EPS CAGR target with 6% dividend growth.

The problem is price. At $44.56 the stock sits near its 52-week high after a ~17.8% run, trading at ~27x trailing and ~23x forward EPS against a multi-utility peer median around 20x, with a 2.2% dividend yield versus a 3.1% peer median. The load-growth optionality that differentiates CNP is the reason the stock re-rated above its peers. The growth is financed on a structurally negative free-cash-flow base (~-$2.4B FCF in FY25), ~$23B of consolidated debt, ~4.2x debt/equity, an FFO/Debt cushion (~12.5% vs. a 14-15% target) that leaves little margin, and a Moody's negative outlook on the parent and Houston Electric.

We rate CNP a HOLD (growth_hold). The load story is genuine and accelerating, which supports mid-to-high-end EPS delivery through 2028 and justifies owning the name through the cycle. But the stock is priced for that execution, the balance sheet is stretched into a higher-for-longer rate environment, and the Beryl/TEEEF regulatory scar remains the binding constraint on whether the $65.5B plan gets approved on favorable terms. The prior HOLD sequence (six-plus months) is confirmed by current data.

## Company Overview

CenterPoint operates three registrants: the parent (CNP), Houston Electric (CEHE, a transmission-and-distribution-only electric utility), and CenterPoint Energy Resources Corp. (CERC, natural gas LDCs), across two reportable segments:

- **Electric** (FY25: ~$4.87B revenue, $705M net income). Houston Electric is a wires-only TDU serving ~2.86M metered customers on the Houston/Galveston Gulf Coast through ~67 Retail Electric Providers, operating in ERCOT with no baseload generation of its own. Indiana Electric (SIGECO) is a small vertically integrated utility (~154K customers) in southwest Indiana with coal, gas, wind, and solar generation in MISO.
- **Natural Gas** (FY25: ~$4.49B revenue, $570M net income): ~4.0M LDC customers across Texas, Minnesota, Indiana, and Ohio (Ohio being divested).

The moat is the standard regulated-utility construction: exclusive service franchises, cost-of-service ratemaking, and a diversified multi-state footprint. What differentiates CNP from utility peers is location: Houston Electric sits at the center of ERCOT/Gulf Coast industrial, export, and data-center load growth. That location advantage is offset by Texas's affordability-focused, politically charged regulatory environment, most visibly demonstrated by the Hurricane Beryl fallout.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings (FY2025).** Revenue was $9.36B, up ~8.3% from $8.64B in FY24. GAAP net income was $1,052M ($1.60 diluted EPS); non-GAAP EPS was $1.76, with adjustments stripping out ZENS mark-to-market, divestiture effects, and TEEEF-removal impacts. The segment split is roughly balanced at ~57% electric / ~43% gas net income. Operating margin ran ~22.7%, net margin ~11.3%, and ROE ~9.4%, all typical for a regulated utility. Authorized ROEs cluster around 9.65-9.80% (Houston Electric 9.65% at a 43.25% equity ratio).

**The capital-intensity dynamic.** Operating cash flow was ~$2.49B against ~$4.87B of capex, producing free cash flow of roughly -$2.4B. This is structural and intentional, not distress: CNP is mid-build-out on the 2026-2035 capital plan (2026E capex ~$6.8B, ~71% electric / ~29% gas). Q1 2026 delivered $0.56 non-GAAP EPS (vs. $0.53 in Q1'25) and reaffirmed full-year non-GAAP guidance of $1.89-$1.91.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Consolidated debt is ~$23B (including ~$0.7B non-recourse securitization bonds) against ~$11.2B equity, or ~4.2x debt/equity. Cash is minimal (~$38M), normal for a utility running on credit facilities and commercial paper. Screening metrics like the Altman Z-Score (~0.81) and a low Piotroski score are false positives here: stable, rate-regulated cash flows support high leverage. The real leverage watch-item is credit quality: CNP parent is Baa2/BBB/BBB, Houston Electric A2/A/A, CERC A3/BBB+/A-, with a Moody's negative outlook on CNP and Houston Electric (S&P/Fitch stable). Management targets FFO/Debt of 14-15% with a 100-150 bps cushion, but reported only ~12.5% TTM (13.8% adjusted for one-time storm items). That cushion is thin, and in a higher-for-longer rate environment the negative-FCF financing model carries genuine downgrade risk.

**Funding strategy.** The plan is funded via internal cash, debt, ~$4B of equity through 2035 (largely pre-arranged forward sales: ~$1.1B already contracted, only ~$3B of new issuance 2028-35, which limits dilution), and capital recycling from asset sales. About 70% of 2026 financing needs were completed by Q1 2026. The Ohio gas LDC sale to National Fuel Gas (~$2.62B gross, ~$2.4B net, ~1.9x 2024 rate base / ~26x earnings; $1.42B cash plus a $1.2B seller's note at 6.5%) and the March 2025 Louisiana/Mississippi LDC sale (~$1.2B) recycle capital out of lower-growth gas jurisdictions into higher-growth Texas/Indiana electric. This is coherent, credit-friendly portfolio management.

**Earnings quality and surprise track record.** The four-quarter beat/miss record is unremarkable: one beat, two in-line, one miss, consistent with a utility that delivers to guidance rather than surprising. The Q3'25 12.6% beat was the outlier; the two recent in-line prints and one modest miss argue for treating consensus as reliable but not conservative.

## Growth Analysis

This is where CNP earns its premium. The load-growth story is accelerating, not merely holding, with the remainder of committed load beyond the ~8 GW data-center component diversified across energy/refining, exports, life sciences, advanced manufacturing, and logistics.

For a regulated utility, that translates directly into rate-base growth and a durable 7-9% EPS CAGR target through 2035, which management expects to run at the mid-to-high end in 2026-28. Estimate revisions corroborate a steady, if unspectacular, trajectory: next-quarter estimates are up 2.3% over 90 days and next-year estimates up 0.2%, with current-year guidance essentially unchanged at ~$1.91. These are the modest positive revisions characteristic of a utility with visible rate-base growth, not the +30%-in-90-days velocity that would flag a mispriced cyclical trough.

The world model's data-center power theme provides confirming context: hyperscaler capex, regulated-utility hard data (AEP Ohio commercial KWh +41%, I&M +71%), and electrical-equipment order books all point to real, broad-based load growth at the infrastructure layer. CNP is a legitimate beneficiary. Two caveats temper the growth score. First, the "12.2 GW firmly committed" and "8 GW data-center" figures are CenterPoint's own internal definitions and projections, not independently verified counterparties; large-load customers can delay, cancel, or prove transitory, and interconnection/supply-chain constraints are real. Second, and more fundamental, the per-share benefit of this growth is partially absorbed by external-capital dependence: equity issuance and a rising cost of debt on the negative-FCF model dilute the headline rate-base growth into a still-attractive but not extraordinary 7-9% EPS CAGR.

## Valuation Assessment

CNP trades at ~27x trailing EPS ($1.60 GAAP) and ~23x forward on the ~$1.91 2026 non-GAAP guide. Against the multi-utility peer set, that is a premium:

CNP sits above the peer median P/E while yielding 2.2% against a 3.1% peer median. The premium is not unjustified: CNP's 7-9% EPS CAGR target is top-decile among proxy peers, so a modest multiple premium for superior growth is defensible on a growth-adjusted basis. But the differentiating load-growth story is already in the multiple. The stock re-rated ~17.8% off its lows precisely because the market recognized the ERCOT/Gulf Coast load runway; buying at ~23x forward with a below-peer yield offers roughly fair value for a well-understood story rather than asymmetric upside.

Cheapness is not the case here. A below-median yield and above-median multiple do not support a high valuation score. The consensus target set (mean $46.25, median $47.00, range $40-50) implies ~4-5% base-case upside plus a 2.2% yield, or high-single-digit total return. That is a reasonable utility return but not a mispricing. This is a fully-covered name (18 analysts, ~56% Hold / 44% Buy) where the targets and the price reflect the same load-growth narrative. The valuation score reflects a fully-valued regulated utility with a stretched balance sheet, not a value opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

CNP is a regulated monopoly in each service territory; competition is limited to alternate energy sources for gas, immaterial distributed generation, and adverse regulatory or legislative change. The competitive trajectory is improving: CNP's location in ERCOT/Gulf Coast gives it an above-average, visible rate-base runway that most peers cannot match, and the two-year pull-forward of the Houston load milestone indicates the advantage is widening, not stable. Residential delivery-charge growth (~1.1% CAGR 2014-25 vs. ~3.3% for peers and ~2.8% inflation) is the affordability data management uses to argue that large-load investment is customer-accretive.

The offset is that this favorable position sits inside the least favorable regulatory environment of any large multi-utility. The 2024 Houston Electric rate case produced a ~$47M revenue decrease and a 9.65% ROE against a 10.4% request, a reminder that Texas regulators prioritize affordability over utility returns. The competitive-position score is above-average on trajectory but capped by the regulatory backdrop and by the reality that the load-growth edge is broadly recognized.

## Risk Assessment

**Hurricane Beryl / TEEEF regulatory scar (most material).** Beryl (July 2024) caused prolonged Houston outages, government investigations, litigation, and legislative scrutiny. CNP is recovering storm costs via non-recourse system restoration bonds. The related TEEEF ("mobile generator") saga saw CNP release 91% of its emergency-generation fleet to San Antonio/CPS Energy through March 2027 and stop charging Houston customers, after officials argued CNP should absorb ~$800M of TEEEF costs. A Texas Coalition complaint seeks to end cost recovery. The reputational and political capital consumed by Beryl is the single biggest wildcard for regulatory support of the capital plan, whose entire premise is that regulators will approve massive investment on the argument that it lowers residential bills.

**Execution and load-dependence.** The growth thesis hinges on data-center, refining/export, and manufacturing load materializing as contracted. Delays, cancellations, interconnection bottlenecks, transformer/supply-chain shortages, and tariff/steel costs all threaten the rate-base trajectory. Because the committed-load figures are CNP's own definitions, there is inherent optimism embedded.

**Financing and rate risk.** With a capex supercycle, chunky near-term maturities (2026 ~$1.8B, 2028 ~$3.0B including a $1B convertible), and a thin FFO/Debt cushion, CNP is highly sensitive to rates and capital-market access. The Moody's negative outlook is the concrete downgrade risk, and a higher-for-longer rate path directly raises the cost of the negative-FCF model.

**Regulatory lag / adverse outcomes.** ~85% of investment recovers via trackers/forward test years, which mitigates but does not eliminate lag; the negative Houston rate outcome shows Texas can produce adverse results.

**Other idiosyncratic risks.** REP counterparty concentration (Houston Electric receivables ~37% NRG affiliates, ~23% Vistra); Indiana coal transition (DOE 202(c) emergency order keeping F.B. Culley Unit 2 running through March 2026, ~$1B of cancelled renewables, sole coal supplier); ZENS overhang (~$828M principal maturing 2029, potential ~$897M deferred tax offset by carryforwards); and combined Chair/CEO role as a modest governance watch-item.

The risk score reflects genuine, near-term-relevant idiosyncratic risks—the Beryl/TEEEF regulatory overhang and the financing/downgrade risk—that are not fully priced into a stock at a peer-premium multiple. This is the dimension where CNP's trajectory has most deteriorated across our prior reports, and current data confirms rather than reverses that decline.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sanguine. Near-term ATM IV is 22.7% against 16.7% one-year historical vol—modestly rich, but the IV percentile within the current chain is a low 20%, and the term structure is flat (near 22.7% ≈ far 22.6%), indicating no near-term catalyst is being priced. Put/call skew is -10.7% (call-heavy) at the front, with a low 0.19 put/call OI ratio—positioning is neutral-to-bullish, not defensive. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.7%. The options market does not corroborate the balance-sheet and regulatory risks we flag: complacency here means any Beryl-related or downgrade surprise would not be well-hedged.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$50, ~12% upside plus yield).** Load growth continues to accelerate; the 12.2 GW committed pipeline converts to energized, rate-base-generating demand; regulators approve the $65.5B plan on constructive terms as the affordability narrative holds; CNP delivers at the high end of its 7-9% EPS CAGR; and the multiple sustains a peer premium on the strength of top-decile utility growth. The stock reaches the high end of the analyst range on ~$2.09+ forward EPS at a low-mid-20s multiple.

**Bear case (~$40, ~10% downside).** Beryl/TEEEF fallout hardens into adverse regulatory outcomes that trim the capital plan; large-load customers delay or fail to materialize at contracted scale; the thin FFO/Debt cushion breaks and Moody's downgrades, raising financing costs on the negative-FCF model; and the peer-premium multiple compresses toward the ~20x median as growth conviction fades. The stock de-rates to the low end of the analyst range near book-supported levels.

**Base case (~$47, ~5-6% upside plus 2.2% yield).** CNP delivers to its 2026 guide and mid-to-high-end EPS growth thereafter, the load story continues to develop constructively but with normal execution friction, the balance sheet stays intact without a downgrade, and the multiple holds roughly where it is. Total return of high-single digits, a respectable utility return consistent with a fully-valued name.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, with the Ohio LDC sale close (expected Q4 2026) and the ongoing Texas regulatory calendar as intermediate milestones.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$40s (near the $40 analyst floor), which would restore a peer-comparable yield and asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Concrete regulatory clearance of the Beryl/TEEEF overhang combined with constructive rate-case outcomes that de-risk the capital plan.
- FFO/Debt improving toward the 14-15% target with a Moody's outlook revision to stable, removing the downgrade risk from the financing model.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Moody's downgrade of the parent, or an FFO/Debt reading falling below ~12% without a credible path back.
- Material trimming of the capital plan or a downward revision to the 7-9% EPS CAGR target driven by cancelled/delayed large-load commitments.
- An adverse Texas regulatory ruling on TEEEF cost recovery or a punitive next rate-case outcome that signals structural regulatory hostility.

## Conclusion

CenterPoint has the best rate-base growth runway of any large regulated multi-utility, and the Q1 2026 acceleration in committed load is a real, differentiated signal. The PG&E-alumni management team is well-suited to financing a capital supercycle under regulatory stress, and the capital-recycling program funds growth with minimal dilution. Those are genuine positives that argue for owning the name through the cycle.

The stock is priced for that execution. At ~23x forward with a below-peer yield, the load-growth optionality is in the multiple, not available at a discount. The financing model is structurally negative-FCF against a thin FFO/Debt cushion and a Moody's negative outlook into a higher-for-longer rate environment, and the Beryl/TEEEF regulatory scar remains the binding constraint on whether the plan gets approved on favorable terms. The risk/reward is balanced.

We maintain HOLD (growth_hold). The declining score trajectory across our prior reports reflects a fully-valued utility with rising financing and regulatory risk, and current data confirms that read. We would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$40s or on concrete de-risking of the regulatory and credit overhangs; we would downgrade on a Moody's downgrade or a material trimming of the growth plan.
