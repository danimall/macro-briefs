**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zimmer Biomet is one of three or four scaled players in the global large-joint reconstruction oligopoly, generating $8.23B of FY2025 revenue across knees (40%), hips (25%), the faster-growing S.E.T. franchise (26%), and a technology/data segment anchored by the ROSA robot. The investment debate is a self-help/turnaround question: how much of the sharp 2025 earnings decline was structural versus one-time, and whether management can lift growth and margins while executing two disruptive go-to-market transitions in a price-pressured market where it has structurally lagged Stryker.

The evidence points to a balanced setup. The 2025 operating-margin compression to 13.3% (from 16.7%) was largely non-recurring: a ~$170M inventory/instrument write-down tied to a product-line discontinuation plan, ~$77M of tariffs now reversing after the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling, acquisition costs, and ~$55M of accelerated equity vesting. Q1 2026 margins snapped back to 17.9%, EPS beat by 11% ($2.09 vs $1.88 estimate), and consensus estimates are stable-to-slightly-up (current-year EPS +0.9% over 90 days, next-year flat at $9.00). This does not fit the falling-estimate, guidance-cut profile that would justify an outright avoid. Cash flow is genuinely strong: FY2025 FCF of $1.47B on a ~18% margin funds a dividend and an accelerating buyback (raised to up to $1B for 2026).

The offsets are equally real. Underlying pricing remains negative (-0.4%), and Q1's 9.3% headline growth was flattered by ~3.9pt of Paragon 28 acquisition contribution (which laps out after April 2026), ~2.5pt of FX, and a ~$30M tariff-refund tailwind, leaving low-single-digit organic growth consistent with management's modest +2.5-4.5% 2026 guide. The value-creation plan depends on executing a U.S. sales-force conversion and a China distributor overhaul (already generating litigation), while a ~5% goodwill cushion on the Paragon 28 unit, an unresolved CFO seat, and three-year TSR at roughly half the peer group weigh on credibility. With the stock at $91.12 (below both the $95 median and $98.60 mean analyst target) and a buyback bid underneath, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric. We rate ZBH HOLD.

## Company Overview

Zimmer Biomet designs, manufactures, and markets orthopedic and musculoskeletal devices across 100+ countries, with ~58% U.S. and ~42% international revenue (Europe and Japan the largest foreign exposures). FY2025 product-category revenue:

- **Knees** — $3,322M (40% of sales), +4.7% YoY
- **Hips** — $2,094M (25%), +4.7%
- **S.E.T.** (sports medicine, extremities, trauma, CMF/thoracic, foot & ankle) — $2,150M (26%), +15.2%, boosted ~10.5pt by the Paragon 28 acquisition
- **Technology & Data, Bone Cement and Surgical** — $666M (8%), including ROSA and the ZBEdge digital ecosystem

The moat rests on scale in reconstructive joints (co-led with Stryker, J&J DePuy Synthes, and Smith+Nephew), a large installed base of surgical instruments, surgeon relationships, and an expanding robotics/enabling-technology suite. Underlying demand is structurally supported by an aging, more active population and rising musculoskeletal procedure volumes, but the core knee/hip market is maturing (mid-single-digit growth) and increasingly price-pressured by GPOs, China volume-based procurement, and European reference pricing.

**The 2025 margin story was largely one-time.** Operating margin fell 340bps and net income dropped 22% despite 7.2% revenue growth. The MD&A bridge attributes the hit to a ~$170M inventory/instrument write-down tied to a December 2025 decision to discontinue certain product lines by 2032, Paragon 28/Monogram acquisition costs plus ~$55M of accelerated equity-vesting comp, ~$77M of paid tariffs, higher acquisition-debt interest, and inventory fair-value step-up. Gross margin fell to 61.6% from 63.8%. These items are non-recurring or reversing.

**Q1 2026 confirms the rebound but overstates the clean run-rate.** The rebound was flattered by a ~$30M favorable COGS adjustment recognizing a tariff-refund receivable after the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidated IEEPA tariffs. Normalizing for that refund, FX, and Paragon 28, the underlying organic trajectory is low-single-digit, which I address in Growth.

**Cash generation is the genuine strength.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,697M and FCF $1,473M (~18% margin) on modest ~$225M capex. This comfortably funds the $0.24/quarter dividend (~1.1% yield) and the buyback.

**Balance sheet: leveraged and goodwill-heavy, but not a stress point.** Total debt rose to $7.5B (LT debt $6.9B at YE2025) after debt-funded M&A; equity is $12.7B and D/E 0.59. Net debt/EBITDA runs ~2x, well inside the 4.5x covenant, and maturities ($576M Euro notes due December 2026, $600M notes due February 2027) are refinanceable against ~$1.5B annual FCF. The stated Altman Z of 2.15 (grey zone) is driven by the $14.7B goodwill + intangibles balance (64% of total assets), not by cash-flow leverage or liquidity (current ratio 1.98). I do not treat leverage as a material downside driver here.

**Earnings-surprise track record supports forward-estimate confidence.** Over four quarters ZBH posted three beats and one in-line print, including the +11% Q1 2026 beat. Combined with stable-to-slightly-up revisions, this argues consensus is not sitting on a downward cascade; forward EPS of ~$8.48 (CY) and $9.00 (NY) are credible anchors.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth flatters a modest organic reality. Of Q1 2026's +9.3%, roughly 3.9pt was Paragon 28 and ~2.5pt was FX, leaving low-single-digit organic growth. Paragon 28 laps out after April 2026, removing the largest inorganic contributor. Management's own 2026 guidance of +2.5-4.5% — explicitly dampened by the U.S./international go-to-market disruptions and negative pricing (-0.4%) — is the truer signal of the underlying trajectory.

Where growth can come from:
- **S.E.T./foot & ankle**, where Paragon 28 added scale in a faster-growing sub-segment (+15.2% category growth in 2025). Sustaining this after the lapping period depends on integration succeeding amid the sales-force transition.
- **Robotics/enabling technology**, where ROSA and the pending Monogram autonomous robot (clinical study underway, commercialization targeted 2027) are the strategic bets. This is where Stryker's Mako currently leads.
- **Restructuring savings** — the 2025 plan targets ~$175M annual gross savings vs the 2024 baseline (~$155M total charges through 2027), which supports margin recovery more than top-line growth.

I do not see the +30% estimate-revision signal that would elevate Growth Prospects; revisions are stable, and the growth engine is acquisition/FX-supplemented rather than organically accelerating. This is the primary reason the overall score sits below a BUY threshold despite the durable cash flows.

ZBH's trailing 24.3x sits at roughly half the peer median, and its forward multiple is well below any comparable. Per our discipline, a compressed multiple is not itself a buy signal — cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way and must be paired with a catalyst or a demonstrable mispricing. Here the discount is at least partly earned: negative pricing, execution-dependent growth, and a three-year TSR that halved the peer group (62.66 vs 126.87). The offsetting supports are concrete: ~18% FCF margin, an up-to-$1B buyback removing ~5-6% of the float at these prices, and a price below the analyst targets.

The analyst target range ($83 low, $130 high, $95 median, $98.60 mean) frames the risk/reward: roughly +4% to the median, +8% to the mean, +43% to the high in a full turnaround, versus ~-9% to the low. Because ZBH is not a premium-multiple stock, the median/mean targets can lag if margin recovery sustains, but the negative pricing and lapping Paragon 28 contribution keep me from treating those targets as clean floors. Fair value clusters in the low-to-mid $90s, close to spot, which is a HOLD structure. I score Valuation 5.5, modestly constructive on the FCF/buyback support and below-target price, discounted for the earned nature of the multiple compression.

## Competitive Landscape

Zimmer competes in a consolidated large-joint reconstruction oligopoly against Stryker, J&J DePuy Synthes, and Smith+Nephew. The competitive battleground has shifted toward robotics and enabling technology, where Stryker's Mako is the established leader in robotic joint replacement. Zimmer's response — ROSA plus the Monogram autonomous-robot acquisition — is credible but unproven at scale, and it is playing catch-up rather than setting the pace.

The trajectory is the concern. Core knee/hip growth of ~4.7% is mid-single-digit and price-pressured; the strategy to lift the mix (Paragon 28 foot & ankle, product pruning, sales-force reshaping) is precisely where execution can slip. Three years of TSR at roughly half the peer group suggests the market has been pricing skepticism that ZBH will out-execute Stryker, and nothing in the recent data reverses that. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than improving, which supports a below-midpoint score of 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter specifically for ZBH now, several newly emphasized in the 2025 10-K:

1. **U.S. sales-force transformation (execution risk).** Converting substantial portions of the U.S. sales force from independent distributors to employees, with added product specialization, is management's cited 2026 headwind. Loss of high-performing reps and their surgeon relationships to competitors during the transition is a self-inflicted, ZBH-specific risk.
2. **China go-to-market disruption and litigation.** Overhauling the China distributor network has triggered legal claims from displaced distributors; management warns of a "material adverse impact," compounded by VBP price cuts.
3. **Paragon 28 goodwill.** The Global Foot & Ankle reporting unit's fair value exceeds carrying value by only ~5%, a plausible impairment catalyst if the acquisition growth plan slips, especially now that integration is entangled with the sales-force overhaul. Other units carry >20% cushion.
4. **Product-line discontinuation.** The exit-by-2032 plan drove the 2025 charge and risks further write-downs and customer attrition.
5. **Governance/continuity.** An unresolved CFO transition (interim CFO Paul Stellato for multiple quarters), a Group President's open-market sale (~5,000 shares at ~$82.6 in May 2026), no insider buying, and a stark pay-vs-performance gap cluster into a credibility watch item.
6. **IRS disputes** for 2013-2015 and 2016-2019 with potential material cash payments; residual tariff uncertainty under alternative authorities; ongoing ERP integration risk.

Leverage is not a genuine downside driver at ~2x net debt/EBITDA inside a 4.5x covenant. The net of these is a moderate, largely priced risk profile with a few binary tails (Paragon 28 impairment, China litigation), a score of 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not confirming a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 37.5% is rich versus 33.1% historical vol and has jumped ~10pp over the past month, and the term structure is in backwardation (37.5% near vs 29.7% at six months), pricing imminent event stress consistent with the upcoming earnings print and the CFO/guidance overhang. But positioning leans mildly bullish: put/call skew is -5.0% (call-heavy), P/C OI ratio is a low 0.23, and the notable unusual activity was call buying at the $100 strike (4.8x volume/OI) into August. The ±9.5% implied move for the nearest expiry frames event risk in both directions. This positioning argues against treating ZBH as a short candidate and is consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$110-130).** The 2025 earnings decline was almost entirely one-time; margins have already recovered and tariff refunds add tailwind. Restructuring delivers ~$175M of savings, foot & ankle and robotics reaccelerate growth toward the high end of guidance, the sales-force transition completes without material rep attrition, and the buyback compounds per-share value against a depressed ~10.7x forward multiple. Re-rating toward the mid-teens forward multiple on ~$9 EPS drives the stock to the $110-130 analyst-high zone.

**Bear Case (~$83).** The two go-to-market transitions leak surgeon relationships and coverage, China litigation and VBP pressure results, negative pricing persists, Paragon 28's plan slips and triggers a goodwill impairment, and organic growth stalls below guidance as the Paragon 28 contribution laps out. EPS stagnates near $8.50 and the multiple stays compressed, taking the stock to the ~$83 low target (-9%).

**Base Case (~$95-98).** Margins hold in the high-teens as one-timers roll off, organic growth lands in the low-single-digits within guidance, and the buyback provides a floor. The stock drifts toward the $95 median / $98.60 mean target, a modest gain from spot with limited multiple re-rating until execution on the transitions is demonstrated.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (rationale: the FCF/buyback/valuation supports genuinely balance the low-organic-growth, execution-dependent reality, without a clear directional edge either way).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive underlying pricing or organic (ex-Paragon 28, ex-FX) growth above ~4%, evidencing the mix shift is working.
- A permanent CFO appointment plus explicit confirmation the U.S. sales-force conversion is complete with no material rep/surgeon attrition.
- Current-year or next-year EPS revisions turning up >+5% over 90 days, or a pullback toward the low-$80s that pushes forward P/E below ~9.5x while estimates hold, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Paragon 28 / Global Foot & Ankle goodwill impairment, or the ~5% cushion narrowing to zero.
- Guidance cut below the +2.5% low end, or a negative-revision cascade (CY/NY EPS down >5% over 90 days).
- Evidence the China distributor litigation or U.S. sales-force disruption is materially eroding volumes (segment revenue declines YoY).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the first print that laps out Paragon 28's contribution will be the cleanest read on organic growth), and any 8-K announcing a permanent CFO.

## Conclusion

Zimmer Biomet is a scaled, cash-generative orthopedic leader whose 2025 earnings trough was largely non-recurring and whose Q1 2026 results, stable forward estimates, and three-of-four beat record undercut a deterioration narrative. The stock trades below both analyst targets, generates ~$1.5B of FCF, and has an accelerating buyback providing a persistent bid, supports that cushion downside to roughly $83 (-9%) and make this a poor short candidate. Against those supports, growth is acquisition- and FX-dependent with negative underlying pricing, the value-creation plan rests on two simultaneous disruptive transitions in a market where ZBH has structurally lagged Stryker, and governance credibility is strained by an unresolved CFO seat and a TSR that halved the peer group. This is a balanced, execution-dependent setup with limited directional edge. We rate ZBH **HOLD** and would revisit toward BUY on demonstrated organic reacceleration or a low-$80s entry, and toward AVOID on a goodwill impairment or a guidance cut.
