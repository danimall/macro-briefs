**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EZCORP is the second-largest pawn operator in the United States and one of the largest in Latin America, with 1,506 stores across 16 countries after its January 2026 consolidation of Simple Management Group (SMG). The Class A shares have appreciated roughly 139% over the trailing twelve months to ~$31.72, driven by a genuine operating acceleration: FY2025 revenue rose 10% to $1,274M with net income up 32% to $109.6M, and Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended 3/31/2026) delivered revenue +46% to $446.9M and net income +93% to $49.1M. Same-store pawn loans outstanding rose 16% and consolidated PLO rose 33%.

The central analytical question is how much of this surge is durable. A disproportionate share of the upside comes from jewelry scrap, where gross profit rose 188% in FY2025 and scrap margins nearly doubled again in Q2 FY2026 (22% to 38%) on record gold prices. This tailwind reverses if gold corrects, simultaneously levering PSC income, collateral values, scrap margins, and inventory liquidation. The SMG acquisition and $300M of new 7.375% senior notes have amplified reported growth through M&A and leverage, while a ~21M-share convertible overhang compresses diluted EPS by roughly 25% versus basic. Stripping out the commodity and inorganic contributions, the core business is growing at a healthy but far more modest rate than the +93% headline implies.

We rate EZPW a HOLD. The business is well-run and structurally advantaged within a fragmented, consolidating industry, and the trailing 16.5x P/E is undemanding against rising estimates. But the cyclical quality of the earnings beat, a permanent governance discount (public holders own non-voting stock with one individual controlling all votes), persistent insider selling with zero open-market buying, and 23.8% short interest combine to make the current setup balanced rather than asymmetric. We would become constructive on a pullback that more fully discounts gold normalization, or on evidence that core same-store economics can sustain the current trajectory independent of scrap.

## Company Overview

EZCORP advances cash against tangible personal property (primarily jewelry, electronics, tools, and sporting goods) and monetizes that activity through three revenue lines: pawn service charges (PSC, ~37% of revenue and ~64% of gross profit), merchandise sales (resale of forfeited or purchased collateral at 35-37% gross margins), and jewelry scrap sales (melting gold, highly leveraged to the gold price).

The store base divides into two operating segments. **U.S. Pawn** (545 stores under EZPAWN and Value Pawn & Jewelry) generated roughly 74% of consolidated gross profit in FY2025 but is geographically concentrated, with Texas (45% of U.S. stores) and Florida (17%) together exceeding 62% of the domestic footprint. **Latin America Pawn** (815 stores under Empeño Fácil, Cash Apoyo Efectivo, GuatePrenda, and MaxiEfectivo) contributed about 26% of gross profit despite carrying 60% of the store count, reflecting smaller average store economics. EZCORP also holds a 43.7% equity stake in Cash Converters International (ASX: CCV) and, as of January 2026, consolidates SMG (105+ pawn stores across the U.S., Caribbean, and Central America).

The competitive position rests on scale (#2 in the U.S. behind FirstCash, #2 for-profit operator in Mexico, #1 in Guatemala), the EZ+ Rewards loyalty program with 6.9M members, a local store-as-supply-chain model that avoids warehousing, and access to capital for roll-up acquisitions in a fragmented industry. The shares are Class A non-voting; Phillip E. Cohen owns 100% of the Class B voting stock and serves as Executive Chairman.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory shows consistent top-line expansion with accelerating profitability:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $886M | $1,049M | $1,162M | $1,274M |
| Gross profit | $528M | $610M | $682M | $746M |
| Operating income | $75M | $92M | $113M | $149M |
| Net income | $50M | $38M | $83M | $110M |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $114M | $149M |
| Cash | $206M | $221M | $171M | $470M |

FY2025 was a record year. The single largest driver was jewelry scrap, where gross profit jumped 188% as scrap gross margin expanded from 15.0% to 26.6% on rising gold prices. PSC grew 9% on a 12% increase in pawn loans outstanding (PLO reached $307.5M), and merchandise margin held near 35%.

Q2 FY2026 accelerated this further. Diluted EPS was $0.58. Jewelry scrap sales surged 288% with scrap gross margin moving from 22% to 38%. Of the revenue gain, roughly $51M and 105 of the 117 acquired stores came from the newly consolidated SMG. Six-month FY2026 net income reached $93.4M versus $56.4M in the prior year. The earnings surprise record is strong: four consecutive beats, including +38.7% and +31.0% in the two most recent quarters.

The balance sheet is sound for an asset-heavy consumer finance model. Current ratio is 5.6x, debt/equity ~0.9x, ROE ~10.7%, ROA ~5.6%, and FCF ~$110M in FY2025. The Altman Z-Score of 2.62 sits in the grey zone, which is typical for a leveraged pawn operator and not a distress signal. The capital structure was reshaped in 2025: EZCORP issued $300M of 7.375% senior notes due 2032 in March 2025, converted ~$97M of 2025 convertibles into ~6.1M shares in April 2025, and retired the $6.4M remainder in cash. The $230M convertible notes due December 2029 remain, and total debt obligations are approximately $530M.

Two financial items warrant emphasis. First, dilution: diluted share count is ~83M versus ~58-62M basic, a 21M+ share overhang from convertibles that explains why diluted EPS lagged basic EPS materially in Q2 FY2026. Only ~8.5M unreserved Class A shares remain after November 2025 awards, constraining equity-funded M&A and compensation. Second, the cash decline from $505M (March 2025) to $354M (March 2026) reflects the $134M retirement of SMG's third-party debt plus acquisition outlays, capital deployed into the consolidation rather than lost.

## Growth Analysis

EZCORP has three growth vectors operating simultaneously, which is why the headline numbers look so strong, and why they must be decomposed.

**Core organic growth** is solid, reflecting genuine demand from cash- and credit-constrained consumers. This is the most durable component and aligns with a macro backdrop of elevated cost-of-living pressure on lower-income households. Pawn demand is counter-cyclical in this respect.

**Commodity leverage** is the largest swing factor and the least durable. This earnings stream is directly tied to the gold price, which has been at record levels. Gold normalization would compress scrap margins, soften collateral values, and reduce PLO simultaneously, with the same mechanism working in reverse. A meaningful portion of the multiple-expanding growth narrative is cyclical.

**Inorganic growth** through M&A is the third lever. Beyond SMG, EZCORP acquired 47 Mexico stores in FY2025 and 32 Guatemala stores in April 2026. The roll-up runway in a fragmented industry is real, but future M&A increasingly depends on debt-funded capital (the 7.375% notes) and is constrained by limited unreserved shares.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest in magnitude. Current-year EPS consensus has risen 8.3% over 90 days to $2.00, and next-year to $2.14 (+5.2%). These are healthy upward revisions but fall well short of the +30% velocity that would mark a fundamental change in core earnings power, consistent with the read that much of the reported beat is commodity-amplified rather than a structural step-change the Street is chasing.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$31.72 against trailing EPS of $1.42, EZPW trades at ~16.5x. On the rising current-year consensus of $2.00, the forward P/E is ~15.9x, falling to ~14.8x on next-year's $2.14. Against the Consumer Finance peer median P/E of 12.0x, EZPW carries a modest premium, which is defensible given its faster growth, cleaner balance sheet, and scaled multi-country footprint relative to debt-collection peers (ECPG at 6.3x, PRAA and NAVI at negative multiples) and consumer lenders (PRG at 12.0x, WRLD at 25.7x).

The valuation case is genuinely two-sided. On the supportive side: the absolute multiple is undemanding, estimate revisions are positive, the business is growing, and the analyst target range of $35-$44 (mean $39.6) sits well above the current price. On the cautionary side: the trailing EPS that anchors this multiple is itself elevated by record gold-driven scrap margins. If gold normalizes and scrap margins revert toward historical levels, normalized EPS would be lower than the current run-rate, which means the "cheap" forward multiple is partly an artifact of peak-cycle earnings. A low headline P/E built on cyclically inflated earnings is the profile that rewards caution.

The diluted share count compounds this. Using the ~83M fully diluted count rather than ~58M basic materially changes per-share math; the convertible overhang is a real claim on equity value that the simple market-cap-divided-by-basic-shares framing understates.

We assign a valuation score of 5.5. The stock is not expensive on reported numbers, but the asymmetry the bull case requires is undercut by the cyclical quality of the earnings base. We would treat the $35-$44 analyst range as a fair-weather scenario predicated on gold staying elevated, not as a conservative floor.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. pawn industry is large, mature, and highly fragmented: a handful of scaled operators (EZCORP #2, FirstCash #1) alongside thousands of 1-3 store independents. This structure is favorable for consolidation, and EZCORP has demonstrated the ability to execute roll-ups. The EZ+ Rewards program provides repeat-customer stickiness, and the store-as-supply-chain model avoids the warehousing costs that burden traditional retail.

Latin America is less fragmented but earlier in its expansion, where EZCORP competes against government-affiliated non-profit pawn operators in Mexico, a competitive and regulatory wrinkle that caps pricing power. The competitive position is improving on store count and scale, but the durability of the moat depends on capital access for acquisitions and on macro tailwinds (gold prices, consumer stress) that are external to the franchise rather than proprietary advantages. The trajectory is positive; the quality of the moat is more capital-and-scale-driven than structurally defensible, which supports a competitive position score of 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Gold price dependency** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. A sudden or significant decline in gold would hit PSC, PLO, scrap margins, and inventory liquidation at the same time. This is the single most underappreciated risk relative to the +93% net income headline.

**Governance** is a permanent structural discount. Phillip Cohen controls 100% of the voting Class B and holds effective board veto through unanimous-approval bylaws. Minority holders cannot elect directors or influence strategy. This is not a catalyst-driven risk but a persistent cap on the fair-value multiple.

**Geographic concentration** (62%+ of U.S. stores in Texas and Florida) exposes the company to single-state regulatory change and hurricane risk. **Regulatory and reputational risk** spans CFPB authority (held but not exercised), Latin American AML regimes (Mexican PROFECO and 2025 AML amendments), FCPA exposure, and firearms-business liability. **FX translation** through the peso, quetzal, lempira, and AUD is a meaningful swing factor in both directions: FY2025 constant-currency results were stronger than GAAP as the peso weakened, while Q2 FY2026 GAAP was flattered by a strengthening peso.

**Dilution and capital constraints** are live: the convertible-share overhang already compresses diluted EPS, and limited unreserved Class A shares constrain flexibility. **Goodwill** rose to $473.5M after SMG (a meaningful share of assets), carrying impairment risk if Latin America or SMG underperform. The legacy **AlphaCredit/Grupo Finmart tax indemnity** tail persists with $8M in escrow and a statute of limitations extending to February 2027, small in dollars but unresolved.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 49.2% sits rich relative to one-year historical volatility of 35.4% (a 13.8-point spread), indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty around upcoming catalysts. The put/call skew is -8.6% (call-heavy) with a near-zero put/call volume ratio, which leans bullish in positioning, with little appetite for downside protection at current levels. The term structure is flat (near 49.2% versus far 47.8%), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a single dated event. The reported jump in ATM IV to 458% in the options-delta snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. The call-heavy skew sits against the 23.8% short interest; bullish options positioning and a heavily-shorted float can coexist when the short thesis is structural (governance, gold reversion) rather than near-term catalyst-driven.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** EZCORP is the #2 U.S. pawn operator with a proven roll-up engine in a fragmented industry, riding genuine same-store demand from cash-constrained consumers. The trailing 16.5x and forward ~15x multiples are reasonable against rising estimates and a peer median of 12x, and analyst targets of $35-$44 imply 10-39% upside. If gold prices hold and the SMG integration delivers, the company compounds earnings through organic growth plus accretive M&A, and the high short interest (23.8%, 14.8 days to cover) creates squeeze potential on any positive catalyst. Bull case ~$42 (consensus high zone on sustained gold and continued M&A accretion).

**Bear Case.** A significant portion of the earnings surge is commodity-driven and mean-reverting. Gold normalization compresses scrap margins (38% back toward the historical mid-teens), softens collateral values, and reduces PLO simultaneously, exposing a far more modest core earnings base. The non-voting governance structure permanently caps the multiple; insiders are selling with no open-market buying; a value manager (First Wilshire) is trimming; and the convertible overhang dilutes per-share value. On normalized (post-gold-peak) earnings, fair value could be meaningfully below the current price. Bear case ~$24 (roughly -24% from current, reflecting gold reversion and multiple compression to peer levels).

**Base Case.** Gold stays elevated but does not climb further, core same-store growth continues in the low-double-digits, SMG integrates without surprises, and the stock trades roughly in line with the lower end of the analyst range as the market discounts both the M&A accretion and the cyclical risk. Base case ~$34, modest upside from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction over the next 12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that core same-store economics (PSC growth, merchandise margin, PLO) can sustain double-digit growth independent of jewelry scrap — specifically, two consecutive quarters where scrap-ex earnings growth exceeds 15%.
- A pullback to the low-$20s that more fully discounts gold normalization, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Initiation of open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, reversing the current net-selling pattern.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material gold price correction (>15%) that compresses scrap margins and PLO, with management confirming the earnings impact.
- Goodwill impairment on the SMG or Latin America segments, signaling acquisition overpayment.
- Deterioration in same-store PLO growth below 5% YoY, indicating the core franchise is stalling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q3 FY2026, expected early August 2026), which will show whether scrap margins held and whether SMG continues to contribute as modeled. The gold price path is the most important external variable to monitor continuously.

## Conclusion

EZCORP is a well-run, structurally advantaged operator in a consolidating industry, and the operating momentum is genuine. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the quality and durability of the earnings that anchor the valuation. The +93% net income quarter sounds like a fundamental step-change, but the decomposition reveals that record gold prices, the SMG acquisition, and leverage are doing much of the work, layered on a solid but more modest 16% same-store core. At ~16.5x trailing earnings the stock looks cheap, but that multiple rests on cyclically inflated profits that can mean-revert if gold normalizes.

Layered on top are a permanent non-voting governance discount, the convertible overhang, persistent insider selling with no offsetting open-market purchases, and a 23.8% short float that signals a meaningful body of investors holds a structural bearish view. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would turn constructive on a pullback that prices in gold normalization, or on demonstrated durability of the core franchise apart from the scrap tailwind. Until then, the appropriate posture is to hold and monitor the gold price and same-store metrics closely.
