**Research Date: August 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

U.S. Physical Therapy operates 780+ outpatient therapy locations across 44-45 states through a partnership structure in which local therapist-owners retain 25-35% of most clinics, plus an industrial injury-prevention segment contracted directly with employers. The model has produced real top-line growth — FY2025 revenue of $781.0M, up 16.3% — but that growth has been overwhelmingly acquired. Mature-clinic revenue was approximately flat in 2025 (+0.2% in Q2 2025, −0.5% in H1 2025 on the company's own disclosure), and while organic performance has improved to +3.5% in Q2 2026, the gap between 8.2% consolidated growth and 3.1% same-store growth is still filled with debt- and minority-interest-funded acquisitions.

The problem is the intersection of that model with the current price. Since the July reassessment the stock has risen 9.7% to $80.05, and it sits 37.6% above the May 8 low of $58.19. Over that same stretch the company delivered a second consecutive non-GAAP miss (Q2 Operating Results per share of $0.75 versus $0.86 consensus, following $0.46 versus $0.56 in Q1), reported physical therapy gross margin down 170bps year over year, and reaffirmed — did not raise — full-year Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $102.0-106.0M. Price improved; the operating evidence did not. That combination compresses margin of safety rather than expanding it.

The decisive arithmetic is the H2 bridge. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA was $95.010M against H1 2025 of $46.402M, implying H2 2025 of $48.608M. Reaffirmed FY2026 guidance therefore requires H2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $54.804-58.804M, or +12.8% to +21.0% year over year, against +1.7% delivered in H1. Some sequential step-up is structural — Q1 is seasonally weakest on deductible resets — but the year-over-year acceleration required is seven to twelve times what the first half produced, from a company that has missed four consecutive quarters. Meanwhile single-analyst near-term consensus implies an outsized H2 EPS acceleration that cannot be reconciled with an EBITDA guide of +13% to +21% while interest expense rises 28-33% and D&A grows. We rate USPH REDUCE at $80.05, with a base case of $76, a bull case of $99, and a bear case of $57.

## Company Overview

USPH reports two segments. **Physical Therapy Operations** comprises outpatient physical, occupational and speech therapy clinics, home-care therapy practices, and management-contract services including on-site athletic trainers. **Industrial Injury Prevention (IIP)**, consolidated primarily under Briotix Health (approximately 92% owned), delivers on-site injury prevention, post-offer employment testing, functional capacity evaluations and ergonomic assessment, contracted with and paid for directly by employers including Fortune 500 accounts.

The defining structural feature is the Clinic Partnership. USPH is general partner or managing member with equity interests generally 65-75% (overall range 30-99%), with the managing therapist holding the balance. Selling therapists sign employment agreements up to five years with non-competes up to two years post-employment, receive salary plus a revenue or profit bonus, and take earnings distributions on their ownership interest. In most acquired clinics the non-controlling partner holds a Put Right — the ability to force USPH to buy their interest at a predetermined multiple of earnings. Per the FY2025 10-K, these puts "do not expire, even upon an individual partner's death, and contain no mandatory redemption feature," and some are exercisable on specified anniversary dates rather than only upon termination of employment.

Payor mix on FY2025 net patient revenue of $650.4M: managed care/commercial 48.5%, Medicare/Medicaid 35.8%, workers' compensation 10.2%, other 5.5%. Against total net revenue of $781.0M, Medicare/Medicaid represents 29.8% — meaningful government exposure, but lower than the payor-mix table implies. Medicare's 40.1% share of visits versus 35.8% share of revenue confirms it pays below the blended average.

The 2026 strategic pivot is hospital affiliation. In February the company announced a 10-year alliance between its Metro subsidiary and NYU Langone covering 60 existing clinics, plus a 10-clinic gulf-coast alliance. Under these structures the hospital bills third-party payors and pays fees to the Clinic Partnership; USPH recognizes reimbursement of salaries, benefits and operating expenses as revenue plus variable per-encounter fees. First hospital affiliation revenue of $5.564M was recognized in Q2 2026, with 31 of 60 clinics integrated and 39 remaining for Q3.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2022 $553.4M → FY2023 $604.6M → FY2024 $671.4M → FY2025 $781.0M. H1 2026 $412.3M (+8.2%); Q2 2026 $214.1M (+8.5%). Organic drivers in Q2 2026 were constructive: visits +6.6% to 1,661,694, average daily visits per clinic 33.5 versus 32.7 (+2.4%), revenue per visit $107.59 versus $105.33 (+2.1%). Multiplying per-clinic volume and rate gives roughly +4.6%, broadly consistent with reported mature growth of +3.5% without requiring further explanation. That said, the mature-revenue definition changed this quarter — the cohort cut-off moved from January 1, 2024 to January 1, 2025, and hospital-affiliated clinics (which were operational before the cut-off and whose revenue recognition changed to include cost reimbursement) sit inside the mature cohort. The organic improvement is likely genuine; its precise magnitude is not cleanly separable from the definitional change.

**Margins are the core problem.** PT gross margin fell to 19.5% from 21.2% in Q2 and to 17.7% from 19.0% in H1. Management attributes roughly 100bps of the Q2 decline to health-benefit costs against a favorable prior-year comparison, leaving approximately 70bps unexplained. Salaries and related costs rose 130bps to 57.9% of PT revenue in Q2 and 90bps to 58.9% in H1 — labor cost per unit of revenue is rising faster than the 2.1% rate increase USPH is capturing. IIP is the offset, with H1 margin up 90bps to 20.4%. Corporate costs held at 8.9-9.0%.

The compression is not a one-quarter event. Against XBRL revenue, proxy-disclosed Adjusted EBITDA implies margins of 13.31% (2022), 12.85% (2023), 12.18% (2024) and 12.16% (2025). Revenue grew 41.1% from 2022 to 2025 while Adjusted EBITDA grew 29.0% — 115bps of erosion over three years. That is the signature of a roll-up buying revenue at lower incremental margin than its base, base-margin decay, or both.

**GAAP earnings are uninformative here, and the report must say so plainly.** H1 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.13 versus $1.38. Net income attributable to USPH fell $7.356M to $14.936M, but the revaluation of redeemable non-controlling interest charged directly to retained earnings grew from $1.903M to $17.663M. Because redemption value is a predetermined multiple of partnership earnings, better partnership performance mechanically depresses reported EPS. The $15.760M gross swing accounts for the overwhelming majority of the $1.25 EPS decline. The trailing P/E of 443.9x carried in market data is entirely an artifact of this mechanic (TTM GAAP EPS of roughly $0.18 implied by the profile EPS of $1.42) and should not be used. The charge is not fictional — it marks up the eventual buyout price — but it belongs on the balance sheet as a growing claim, not in a period earnings multiple.

**The underlying decline is real but far smaller than the headline.** H1 2026 operating income of $34.427M includes a $2.989M contingent earn-out revaluation loss; H1 2025's $44.580M included a $5.612M gain. Excluding earn-out revaluation, operating income was $37.416M versus $38.968M, down 4.0%, against reported −22.8%. The $8.601M swing is 84.7% of the reported decline. So the honest read is roughly −4% underlying operating income on +8.2% revenue: genuine negative operating leverage at a fraction of the headline magnitude. Non-GAAP measures corroborate — Operating Results per share $0.75 versus $0.81 in Q2, $1.21 versus $1.30 in H1; Adjusted EBITDA $26.952M versus $26.863M in Q2 (+0.3%) and $47.196M versus $46.402M in H1 (+1.7%).

**Cash flow is adequate at the consolidated level and thin at the attributable level.** H1 2026 operating cash flow $38.178M (+26.5%) less capex $10.737M gives $27.441M of consolidated FCF. Distributions to non-controlling interests consumed $12.326M — roughly 45% of that FCF — leaving $15.115M attributable to USPH against dividends paid of $13.871M, or 1.09x coverage (1.00x in H1 2025). H2 is structurally stronger for outpatient therapy, so H1 understates the annual run rate and should not be annualized; on the FY2025 operating cash flow figure of approximately $75.1M, H2 2025 generated roughly 60% of the year. Coverage on a full-year basis is therefore better than 1.09x, but it is not comfortable.

Discretionary H1 uses above the dividend totaled $69.462M: buybacks $19.233M, majority-interest acquisitions $21.133M, redeemable NCI purchases $6.531M, permanent NCI purchases $8.973M, and contingent consideration payments of $13.592M. This was funded with roughly $59.3M of net new debt plus a $10.683M cash drawdown. The earn-out line deserves attention: a $5.612M non-cash gain in H1 2025 that flattered reported operating income became a $13.592M cash outflow plus a $2.989M loss in H1 2026. This is the ordinary mechanical consequence of ASC 805 fair-value remeasurement and carries no inference about intent, but it does make year-over-year GAAP operating comparisons unreliable without normalization.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $24.887M (down from $35.570M), goodwill $716.535M plus intangibles $176.547M equal to 71.5% of $1,248.874M total assets, total debt $220.019M, net debt $195.132M. Tangible book value is approximately −$444M. For an asset-light services roll-up negative tangible book is expected, but with margins compressing, the 10-K's own forward-looking reference to "the possible write-down or write-off of goodwill and other intangible assets" is not boilerplate.

Net debt of $195.1M against TTM USPH-share Adjusted EBITDA of $95.8M is 2.04x, or 1.88x on 2026 guidance midpoint — comfortable on its face. The complication is $317.491M of redeemable non-controlling interest carried in temporary equity, up from $293.311M at year-end. This sits outside shareholders' equity of $448.656M and is a contractual obligation to purchase partner interests at a predetermined earnings multiple, settled in cash or notes at times largely outside the company's control, that grows as partnerships perform better. Adding it to net debt gives $512.6M of claims; that overstates true leverage because NCI holders also own their share of underlying earnings which the USPH-share EBITDA denominator excludes. The defensible framing is narrower: a $317.5M claim economically senior to common equity, equal to 26.6% of the $1.19B equity market capitalization at $80.05 and 33.9% at the $62.80 average price the company itself paid for stock in Q2. Roughly $88.7M sits in the bucket where the contractual holding period has lapsed but the holder remains employed, and a separate discrete put right of approximately $54.1M covering an 80% stake becomes first exercisable in January 2027.

**Refinancing risk is resolved.** The prior $325M facility maturing June 17, 2027 was replaced on April 14, 2026 with a $450M five-year facility ($175M term loan plus $275M revolver) maturing April 14, 2031, at a cost of $2.214M issuance costs, $0.288M amendment costs and a $0.124M extinguishment loss. This removes a genuine near-term wall and adds $125M of committed acquisition capacity. Borrowings rose from $161.8M to $221.0M in the process. One residual: interest expense rose 32.7% in Q2 and 27.7% in H1, and the $150M-notional swap at 2.815% fixed matures June 30, 2027, leaving USPH substantially floating on a larger balance from mid-2027 absent re-hedging.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors, in descending order of reliability.

**Hospital affiliation** is the largest and least proven. Sixty NYU Langone clinics plus ten gulf-coast clinics represent roughly 9% of the location base, with 39 clinics still to integrate in Q3. First-quarter contribution of $5.564M on a partial quarter suggests a meaningful revenue line, but the company has not quantified margin accretion, and the structure changes the economics rather than simply adding volume: the hospital bills payors, USPH receives cost reimbursement plus per-encounter fees. Management's stated expectation is "increasing physical therapy revenues and margins," and combined with front-desk virtualization and expanded cash-based programs, an accelerating H2 Adjusted EBITDA improvement "propelling us into 2027." The strategic logic is sound — hospital outpatient settings generally command different reimbursement than independent private-practice billing, and USPH sheds collection risk — but the accretion is unverified from any source.

**Acquisitions** continue at a measured pace: three deals in 2026 totalling $37.6M of purchase price for approximately $27.0M of annualized revenue (8-clinic practice at 50%, IIP business at 70%, 12-clinic practice at 67%). The blended 1.39x revenue on a 100%-of-revenue basis understates the effective multiple, since USPH purchased only partial interests. This channel is dependable but self-limiting: it consumes debt capacity, adds to the redeemable NCI claim, and has historically diluted margin.

**Organic** has genuinely improved, from approximately zero in 2025 to +3.1% in H1 2026, built on +2.4% per-clinic volume and +2.1% rate. Medicare turns from headwind to modest tailwind in 2026 (+1.75% after cuts of 3.5%, 0.75%, 2.0%, 1.8% and 1.5% in 2021-2025), which helps but does not recover cumulative erosion. Structural drags persist: PTA/OTA services at 85% of the Part B amount, the 2% sequester through FY2027, multiple-procedure payment reduction, and targeted medical review above $3,000 through 2027.

**On the estimate-revision signal.** Current-quarter consensus rose 11.7% in 30 days and 15.1% in 90 days; next-quarter rose 21.3% and 28.5%. On its face this resembles a strong momentum pattern. It does not qualify. Both figures rest on a single analyst. The revisions imply an H2 EPS well above the prior-year level, while the company's own reaffirmed EBITDA guidance implies H2 growth of +12.8% to +21.0% with interest expense up nearly 30% and D&A rising — the two cannot be cleanly reconciled. The six-analyst next-year figure of $3.41 is the more reliable anchor, and it is flat over 30 days and up only 3.4% over 90. The high-conviction revision signal is absent once analyst count and internal consistency are checked.

The earnings track record reinforces caution: four consecutive misses, with the two most recent at −17.3% and −12.8%. Management attributed Q1 primarily to severe winter weather; Q2 had no comparable explanation and produced a 130bps margin deterioration.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K describes both segments as "highly fragmented with no company having a significant market share nationally," with USPH "one of the largest national outpatient physical therapy services providers." Competition comes from hospital therapy departments, private and physician-owned clinics, and chiropractors, on quality of care, cost, outcomes, location convenience and referral relationships.

The partnership model is a genuine differentiator. Therapist-owners with equity in their local practice, operating under independent local brands while accessing national purchasing and negotiated payor contracts, addresses the principal management challenge in a business where the licensed clinician is the product. That advantage is paid for with the put obligation and the distribution leakage.

The competitive position trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Positive: mature revenue recovered from approximately zero to +3.5%, IIP margin expanded 90bps, and the hospital-affiliation pivot is a rational response to five years of Medicare therapy cuts. Negative: revenue per visit up only 2.1% against salaries rising 130bps as a share of revenue demonstrates weak pricing power against both concentrated payors and scarce licensed labor. And counterparty concentration has increased even as geography stayed diversified — 70 clinics, roughly 9% of locations, now depend on two hospital counterparties, and Q2 filings added a forward-looking factor covering "the impact of a termination of one or more of the Company's hospital affiliated arrangements." Changing revenue mix is not diversification when it concentrates counterparties.

Independent industry data on private-equity roll-up activity and direct comparables was unavailable for this review, which limits confidence in the competitive assessment beyond company disclosure.

## Risk Assessment

**Redeemable NCI put obligations.** $317.5M in temporary equity, up $24.2M in six months, priced at a predetermined earnings multiple, non-expiring, surviving a partner's death, with some tranches exercisable on anniversary dates. Approximately $88.7M is in the near-term exercisable bucket, up from roughly $79.8M at year-end. A separate discrete put right of approximately $54.1M over an 80% stake becomes first exercisable in January 2027 — a dated, specific liquidity event within the investment horizon. H1 2026 NCI purchases consumed $15.504M with no incremental revenue produced.

**Regulatory exposure pointed at the growth engine.** The FY2025 10-K states that certain hospital and physician management arrangements "may share characteristics identified by the OIG as potentially higher risk" and that "many of our arrangements do not fit squarely within existing regulatory safe harbors," with dedicated discussion of the 2003 OIG Special Advisory Bulletin on contractual joint ventures. New York is specifically named among states prohibiting fee-splitting and the corporate practice of medicine — and the flagship 2026 initiative is a 60-clinic New York affiliation. Item 3 discloses no specific material proceeding, and the company maintains a board Compliance Committee, CCO, hotline and periodic clinic audits, so this is a tail risk rather than a live matter. The expanded disclosure may simply reflect the New York transaction crossing a materiality threshold. But the strategy the company is leaning hardest on carries the least regulatory certainty of anything it does.

**Labor cost inflation outrunning reimbursement.** Structural, not cyclical, in a business with roughly 7,294 employees where the clinician is the product and part of the workforce co-owns the clinics.

**Guidance risk.** Reaffirmed FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA requires H2 growth of +12.8% to +21.0% against +1.7% in H1, following two consecutive misses. The Q3 print is the clean test.

**Control and reporting risk.** An interim CFO has signed filings for roughly five months while a financial and HR systems conversion proceeds in phases ($0.727M expensed in H1). Simultaneously, two metrics central to judging organic performance and labor efficiency changed definition in the same quarter the business model changed — salaries moved from cost-per-visit to percentage-of-revenue, and mature revenue's cohort cut-off shifted. Prior periods were restated to conform, which is correct treatment, but comparability is reduced exactly where scrutiny should be highest. Management concluded disclosure controls were effective with no material ICFR change for Q2.

**Governance.** A $100 investment became $65 over 2021-2025 while the peer group returned $134 — a 69-point gap — with CEO total compensation reaching a five-year high of $4.468M in 2025 and the company-selected performance measure, Adjusted EBITDA, up 28.8%. Both compensation metrics (Adjusted EBITDA and Operating Results) can be advanced by debt- and NCI-funded acquisition of partial interests that grow absolute EBITDA while diluting per-share economics. A charitable alternative reading — that sector multiple compression explains most of the TSR gap — is partly available, and the 2.1x CEO-to-average-NEO pay ratio does not suggest CEO-centric design. Separately, a director elected in May 2025 is simultaneously an operating executive of the largest recent acquisition, a landlord to the company (approximately $0.2M paid in H1, $2.4M future minimum), and a counterparty to earn-out and put-right obligations. Amounts are small and disclosed; the structure warrants monitoring.

**Insider activity.** Officers sold into strength across 2025 and March 2026 at $78.86-$86.57. The only open-market purchases were by directors: 4,000 shares at $69.00 in November 2025 (approximately $276,000) and 780 shares at $61.50-63.14 in June 2026 (under $50,000). No C-suite officer bought in the open market in the reviewed period. This selling is not accompanied by the countervailing pattern that would discount it — near-term estimate revisions rest on one analyst and the company has missed four straight quarters — so we treat the aggregate direction as a modest negative rather than noise.

**Goodwill.** $893.1M of goodwill and intangibles against negative tangible book of approximately $444M, with margins compressing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 42.4% against 40.5% one-year realized is fair value — no dislocation to exploit and no cheap hedge. The more informative datapoint is the change: ATM IV collapsed from 58.3% on July 23 to 42.4% as the price rose 9.7%, the classic post-event vol crush combined with relief that the Q2 miss did not break guidance. Term structure is flat (42.4% near versus 42.5% far), meaning the market distributes uncertainty evenly rather than pricing a specific near-dated catalyst; that is arguably complacent given a Q3 print that must demonstrate a 13-21% year-over-year EBITDA acceleration. Near-term skew is balanced at −1.4%, but the September tenor carries a +5.9% put skew with a 10.3% implied move — some positioning for downside around the next report. Short interest at 8.0% of float and 4.5 days to cover is moderate: neither a crowded bear position nor evidence of informed bullishness. Nothing in the options data contradicts the fundamental read; the flat term structure mildly reinforces that the Q3 risk is under-priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $99

The hospital-affiliation pivot delivers the margin uplift management implies but has not quantified. All 60 NYU Langone clinics plus 10 gulf-coast clinics complete integration in Q3, the affiliation revenue line scales from $5.564M in a partial quarter to a meaningful annualized contribution at better economics than independent billing, front-desk virtualization and cash-based programs add operating leverage, and FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA lands at or above the $106M high end. Mature revenue holds +3.5-4.5% on the 2026 Medicare tailwind of +1.75% and continued volume gains. FY2027 EBITDA reaches $115M, the market applies 15x, and the $450M facility maturing in 2031 funds continued tuck-ins at 1.4x revenue. Equity reaches roughly $102/share, consistent with the $100 analyst high. Supporting this: the mature-revenue recovery from zero to +3.5% is corroborated by disclosed volume and rate, IIP margin is expanding, refinancing risk is genuinely resolved, and the pipeline of hospital affiliations is described as expanding.

### Bear Case — $57

The H2 ramp does not materialize. Labor costs continue to outrun the 2.1% rate capture, the hospital-affiliation revenue proves lower-margin than implied or slips into 2027, and FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA lands near $100M with guidance trimmed at the Q3 print — a fifth consecutive disappointment. The January 2027 put right on the 80% stake plus continued NCI exercises consume $50-70M of cash that the new facility must fund, pushing net debt toward $260M against a $317M-and-growing redeemable claim. Dividend coverage on attributable FCF slips back toward 1.0x, and with the buyback authorization exhausted there is no corporate bid. The multiple compresses to 10x EV/EBITDA on $100M, giving $800M of equity or roughly $53/share; the 52-week low of $58.19 and the May trough define the zone. Downside of approximately 29%.

### Base Case — $76

Guidance is delivered near the low end at $102M as hospital affiliations contribute revenue with modest margin benefit. Mature revenue holds around 3%, PT margin stabilizes without recovering the 130bps lost, and FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $110M. The market pays 12.5x, netting to approximately $78, cross-checked at 22x $3.41 for $75. Call it $76, or 5% below spot, plus a 2.3% dividend.

Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 20%, bear 30% gives an expected price return of roughly −6%, or −4% including the dividend. The asymmetry is unfavorable: 24% to the bull case against 29% to the bear, with the base case slightly below spot.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** This is the fourth consecutive assessment and the first at REDUCE. The prior three HOLDs were issued at $65-73; the rating changes not because fundamentals deteriorated sharply but because the price rose 9.7% since July 23 and 37.6% off the May low while the operating evidence delivered a second consecutive double-digit non-GAAP miss, 130bps of margin compression, and reaffirmed rather than raised guidance. Score velocity of −0.25/month reflects genuine deterioration in the valuation and management-alignment dimensions, and the overall score of 4.5 no longer supports a fair-value HOLD from this basis.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than around the Q3 catalyst. The stock is 13.3% above its August 6 intraday low of $70.64 on an unexplained two-day rebound, near-term implied volatility has collapsed to fair value, and the corporate buyback that supported the shares at $62.80-63.99 is 99% exhausted. Holders wanting residual exposure to the hospital-affiliation option should reduce to a starter-sized position ahead of the Q3 report, which must demonstrate the year-over-year EBITDA acceleration from +1.7% to +13-21%. Income-oriented holders should note that dividend coverage on attributable free cash flow was 1.09x in H1 and that $1.84 annualized yields only 2.30% at this price — insufficient compensation for the risk profile.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
- Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $27.5M or better with PT gross margin flat or up year over year, evidencing the guided H2 inflection rather than a Q4-loaded promise;
- Full-year Adjusted EBITDA guidance raised above $106M, or a first quantification of hospital-affiliation margin accretion;
- Price back toward $65-68, restoring the base-case gap to double digits and approaching the company's own repurchase basis;
- A permanent CFO appointed with a disclosed open-market purchase, plus a new buyback authorization.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one):**
- FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $100M, or a Q3 non-GAAP miss exceeding 10% — a fifth consecutive miss;
- Exercise of the January 2027 put right on the 80% stake requiring cash or note settlement above roughly $55M without a corresponding earnings contribution disclosed, or aggregate NCI purchases exceeding $35M in FY2026;
- Any OIG, state regulatory or qui tam action naming the hospital-affiliation structure, or termination of either 2026 affiliation;
- Goodwill impairment charge, or an ICFR material weakness disclosed in connection with the systems conversion.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 report, expected early November, is the decisive checkpoint — it must show 39 additional hospital-affiliated clinics integrated, PT margin stabilization, and an EBITDA step-up consistent with the reaffirmed range. Secondary checkpoints: any 8-K disclosing a permanent CFO appointment or a new repurchase authorization, and the January 2027 put-right window.

## Conclusion

USPH runs a defensible business. The partnership model gives therapist-owners genuine equity in their local practice, geographic diversification across 44-45 states is real, the April 2026 refinancing pushed maturities to 2031 and added $125M of committed capacity, and the hospital-affiliation pivot is a coherent response to five consecutive years of Medicare therapy rate cuts. Organic revenue growth recovered from approximately zero in 2025 to +3.1% in H1 2026, corroborated by disclosed per-clinic volume and rate.

The problem is what an investor pays for that at $80.05. The multiple — 14.5x trailing and 13.4x guided EV/Adjusted EBITDA, 23.5x six-analyst next-year EPS — is roughly market-level for a business whose existing clinic base grows about 3%, whose gross margin fell 130bps, which has missed four consecutive quarters, and which leaks roughly 45% of consolidated free cash flow to minority partners before anything reaches shareholders. Full-year guidance requires an H2 acceleration seven to twelve times what the first half delivered. A $317.5M redeemable claim grows as the partnerships succeed and comes due at times the company does not control, with a specific $54M put window opening in January 2027. An interim CFO has signed filings for five months while the reporting system and two key operating metrics changed definition. And the two best-informed buyers of the stock — the company itself and a director — transacted at $62.80 and $61.50-63.14.

Base case $76, bull $99, bear $57, expected return approximately −4% including the dividend. That does not clear a HOLD bar from this basis. Reduce exposure and revisit either near $65-68 or on a Q3 print that converts the guided H2 inflection from a promise into a number.
