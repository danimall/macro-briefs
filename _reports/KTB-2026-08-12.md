## Executive Summary

Kontoor Brands reported Q2 FY2026 on August 12 and raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $5.25–5.35 from $5.15–5.25, with adjusted gross margin guidance lifted 150bp to 49.8–50.0%. Revenue guidance was left unchanged at $2.66–2.71B. Alongside the print, management announced it intends to deploy $400 million of expected Lee divestiture proceeds into an accelerated share repurchase and appointed Joseph Alkire President and CFO with expanded oversight of the Wrangler brand. The stock opened higher by roughly 9.6% against the prior close; at least one secondary account of the print described the shares as trading lower, which the price record contradicts. With 12.7% of float short and 7.4 days to cover, a portion of the move is likely mechanical.

The substance underneath the raise is narrower than the headline. Adjusted EPS from continuing operations was $1.06 against a $1.06 consensus — in line. The widely reported $1.50 versus $1.06 "42% beat" is an artifact of the Lee reclassification to discontinued operations: the $0.44 difference is Lee's adjusted contribution, which is leaving the company. On a like-for-like basis, organic revenue (excluding Helly Hansen and Musto) grew 1.5% to $470.4M, and Wrangler U.S. wholesale, the single largest channel in continuing operations, was flat. Reported GAAP gross margin of 56.2% includes a $15.4M gain on a manufacturing facility sale booked in cost of goods sold, worth 264bp of revenue, and Q1's GAAP gross margin carried a 350bp tariff-refund benefit of which $29.0M related to FY2025 duties. Adjusted gross margin of 53.8% is the right underwriting basis.

What Kontoor is offering an equity investor over the next twelve months is a per-share earnings story rather than a revenue story: 15.5x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, a 2.58% dividend, roughly 8.8% of shares retired via ASR, net leverage falling from ~2.3x toward management's sub-1.5x target, and $0.55/share of stranded Lee overhead available for recapture. That path can plausibly deliver FY2027 EPS of $6.30–6.45 and a $88–90 stock. It also depends on a transaction that has not closed, on cash taxes on a ~$500M book gain that have not been quantified, and on restructuring execution that has not yet been demonstrated. We remain at HOLD for a fifth consecutive review, with a reduction in the overall score to 5.5 from 5.7, and we would rather add in the low $70s — where management itself repurchased $75.4M during the first half at roughly $75/share — than chase a 10% gap.

## Company Overview

Kontoor was spun out of VF Corporation in 2019 and is now mid-way through its largest portfolio reshaping since. Continuing operations comprise two reportable segments, Wrangler and Helly Hansen, plus a small "Other" bucket (Musto, Chic, private label). Rock & Republic was sold in March 2026; Lee was agreed to Authentic Brands Group on May 21, 2026 for $750M initial consideration plus a $250M performance earnout, with a Q4 2026 close targeted.

First-half FY2026 economics (six months ended July 4, 2026, revenue $1,197.6M):

| Segment | Revenue | Mix | Segment profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wrangler | $904.9M | 76% | $260.7M | 28.8% |
| Helly Hansen | $272.3M | 23% | $21.5M | 7.9% |
| Other | $20.4M | 2% | $(2.5)M | n/m |

Channel mix: U.S. wholesale 63%, international wholesale 23%, DTC 14%. Geographic: U.S. 71%.

The operating differentiator is supply chain rather than pricing power. Roughly 330 contract facilities across 28 countries supply ~77% of units; the remaining ~23% comes from company-owned plants in Mexico, which remain USMCA-exempt from current tariff regimes. More than 80% of Bangladesh-sourced product uses U.S.-grown cotton, a potential exemption pathway under a U.S.–Bangladesh framework still under evaluation. Wrangler's shelf position in U.S. mass and Western/farm-and-ranch retail is a distribution moat that supports 23%+ segment margins; Helly Hansen contributes a European premium outdoor and technical-workwear franchise plus royalty income from a 50%-owned China joint venture.

## Financial Analysis

**The pre-acquisition growth record was flat.** Reported net revenue was $2,631.4M (FY2022), $2,607.5M (FY2023), $2,607.6M (FY2024), then $3,152.5M (FY2025). Of the 20.9% FY2025 increase, the company's own bridge attributes $475.5M to Helly Hansen, $61.3M to organic (which includes a 53rd week worth roughly two points), and $8.1M to FX. Approximately 18 of the 21 points were acquired.

**FY2025 GAAP profitability declined in the year revenue rose 21%.** Operating income fell to $336.8M (10.7% margin) from $342.3M (13.1%), absorbing $80.6M of Project Jeanius restructuring, $50.8M of acquisition costs, and Helly Hansen's 6.9% stub-period segment margin. Interest expense rose 52% to $62.2M; the effective tax rate rose 580bp to 24.3%; diluted EPS fell 7% to $4.05. Company-defined adjusted EPS was $5.59, reconciling to GAAP plus $1.54 of after-tax charges.

**Q2 FY2026 quality of earnings.** Revenue $584.3M (+19% reported, +18% cc). Adjusted operating income $93.6M (16.0% margin, +19%). Adjusted continuing-operations EPS $1.06 versus $0.94. Organic adjusted operating margin improved to 19.8% from 18.1%, roughly +170bp — the organic story is margin, not volume. Two items require flagging: the $15.4M facility-sale gain inside COGS, and a $0.30/share (~$16.6M) gap between GAAP discontinued-operations EPS of $0.14 and the $0.44 adjusted contribution, unitemized in the release, with no equivalent adjustment in the prior-year quarter. Helly Hansen's Q2 segment margin was 1.8% ($1.9M on $106.8M), the seasonal trough for a winter-weighted brand, which is a reminder of how back-half-dependent the acquired asset is.

**Cash flow is strong annually and seasonally weak in H1.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $455.8M against $21.0M of capex — a 13.8% FCF margin, though capex at 0.67% of sales was abnormally low. H1 FY2026 continuing-operations OCF was $39.6M versus $68.8M, with "other, including working capital" swinging to −$119.7M from −$36.6M on inventory build (to $526.1M from $435.9M at year-end, though down 3% year over year), the $53.7M tariff receivable, and accrued-liability drawdown. Full-year OCF guidance of ~$450M (including Lee in discontinued operations) implies ~$356M in H2. This is seasonal sequencing, not deterioration; annualizing H1 would be misleading. Capex guidance has been cut twice, from ~$45M to ~$30M.

One structural caveat: Kontoor operates receivable-sale programs with up to $437.5M of aggregate capacity, and sold balances have risen from $158.6M (March 2025) to $218.4M (December 2025) to $238.2M (March 2026). These are true sales, properly disclosed, and not an irregularity, but the rising balance flatters reported receivables, DSO, and operating cash flow relative to a non-factoring peer, and reversal would be a cash-flow headwind.

**Balance sheet.** At July 4, 2026: total debt $1,144.2M ($400M 4.125% notes due 2029; Term Loan A-1 $700M at 5.3%; A-2 $50M at 5.4%), cash $80.8M including discontinued operations, net debt ~$1,063.3M, revolver undrawn with $493M available. Total equity $618.5M against goodwill of $461.1M and intangibles of $447.7M — the Helly Hansen indefinite-lived trademark alone carries $400M. Equity is 21.9% of assets, a spin-off legacy that makes ROE of 40.3% flattering and the Altman Z-score of 2.35 (grey zone) mechanically low. Interest coverage on guided adjusted operating income is roughly $416M ÷ $56M ≈ 7.4x, and covenants (total leverage ≤4.50x, coverage ≥3.00x) are comfortable. The genuine issue the Z-score captures is impairment headroom, not solvency: there is almost no equity cushion against a trademark or goodwill write-down.

Derived leverage: guided adjusted operating income of $413–420M plus ~$45M of D&A gives continuing adjusted EBITDA of roughly $458–465M. Net debt of $1,063.3M implies ~2.3x. Management targets below 1.5x by year-end after Lee proceeds and $225M of voluntary term-loan repayment. No debt was repaid in H1, consistent with H2-weighted cash generation.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance (continuing operations, as raised August 12):

| Metric | Prior | Updated |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2.66–2.71B | $2.66–2.71B (+12–13%) |
| Adjusted gross margin | 48.3–48.5% | 49.8–50.0% |
| Adjusted operating income | — | $413–420M (+15–17%) |
| Adjusted EPS | $5.15–5.25 | $5.25–5.35 |
| Capex | ~$40M | ~$30M |
| Adjusted tax rate | — | ~20% |
| Interest expense | — | ~$56M |
| Avg shares | — | ~55.5M (excludes ASR) |

Three qualifications matter. First, the adjusted EPS guide absorbs approximately $0.55/share of unmitigated stranded overhead previously allocated to Lee (~$38M pretax) and $0.36/share (~$25M) of newly added brand-building investment. Second, H2 revenue is guided to mid-single-digit growth for both segments *excluding* the 53rd week, which added eight points to Wrangler's Q4 FY2025 growth rate. Third, the 12–13% headline revenue growth is almost entirely the Helly Hansen anniversary lap, not organic expansion.

On Helly Hansen, management characterized H1 pro-forma growth as double-digit. The company's own constant-currency table shows H1 segment revenue of $272.3M reported versus $255.9M constant currency — FX contributed $16.4M, or 6.0% of segment revenue. Working from the Q1 pro-forma disclosure and netting estimated Q1 FX gives roughly mid-single-digit constant-currency growth for the acquired asset. That derivation blends a segment-level FX adjustment with a Helly Hansen + Musto revenue base and is approximate; the Q2 10-Q pro-forma disclosure would settle it. The direction is clear enough to matter: the "high-growth acquired brand" pillar of the bull case is partly a NOK/EUR translation effect, and a currency reversal would expose it.

Estimate revisions are mildly negative: current year $5.22 (−2.5% over 90 days), next year $6.37 (−1.9%). The revision data largely predates the August 12 raise, and the 30-day readings are flat to marginally positive, so the trend should turn modestly upward. This is a slow-drift pattern consistent with a low-growth compounder rather than the revision velocity that flags an inflection.

The credible growth vector is per-share. Working from continuing net income of roughly $294M ($5.30 × 55.5M shares): add ~$15M after tax for partial stranded-cost recapture, ~$10M after tax from $225M of debt repayment at 5.3%, and ~$8M from low-single-digit organic operating income growth, then divide by a post-ASR share count of roughly 50.6M (retiring ~4.9M shares with $400M at ~$82). That yields approximately $6.40, bracketing the $6.37 consensus. Full stranded-cost elimination would push it toward $6.60. Half of that bridge is capital structure.

## Valuation Assessment

At $82.165 with 55.27M shares, market capitalization is $4.54B; adding net debt of $1,063.3M gives an enterprise value of $5.60B. Pro forma for Lee — assuming $750M gross proceeds less an estimated $100–125M of cash taxes on the ~$500M book gain, leaving ~$625–650M net — enterprise value falls to roughly $4.98B against continuing adjusted EBITDA of $458–465M, or **~10.8x**. On earnings:

- 15.5x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS ($5.30 midpoint)
- 12.9x next-year consensus of $6.37
- 20.3x trailing GAAP EPS of $4.05; 14.7x trailing adjusted EPS of $5.59
- 2.58% dividend yield ($2.12 annualized), roughly 40% of adjusted EPS
- ~6% free cash flow yield on a continuing-operations basis (adjusted operating income $416M, less $56M interest, taxed at 20%, plus $45M D&A, less $30M capex, less ongoing cash restructuring and integration spend running ~$25M per half)

The profile data field showing trailing and forward P/E both at 15.46 is internally inconsistent with the $4.05 GAAP EPS also shown; the arithmetic above is computed directly.

Peer medians in apparel and accessories sit at 21.8x trailing P/E (PVH 26.2, VFC 21.8, CPRI 19.9, COLM 14.8, with Warby Parker's 439x uninformative). Kontoor's 20.3x trailing GAAP is a slight discount to that median, and 15.5x on guided adjusted earnings is well below it, but peer trailing multiples are computed on depressed apparel earnings, so the comparison flatters Kontoor less than it appears. Columbia at 14.8x is the honest comparable: a similarly low-growth, balance-sheet-clean outdoor franchise.

The valuation is undemanding rather than mispriced. What the market is discounting is legible: 1.5% organic revenue growth, a flat core U.S. wholesale channel, 30% single-customer concentration where that customer is also a private-label competitor, and margin expansion that is partly self-help (Project Jeanius) and partly non-recurring (tariff refunds, asset-sale gains). A 15x multiple on those characteristics is not obviously wrong. We are wary of treating the discount to peers as the thesis: a compressed multiple on a stagnant top line is the standard configuration of a value trap, and the burden falls on the per-share earnings bridge rather than on relative cheapness.

Analyst targets span $50 to $131 with a $94.6 mean and $98 median, implying 15–19% upside from the mean/median. Ratings distribution is 2 strong buy / 5 buy / 2 hold / 1 strong sell, an unusually wide dispersion that fairly reflects a company whose FY2027 earnings base depends on an unclosed transaction. J.P. Morgan initiated Overweight with a $90 target in June, which is a single externally reported data point rather than consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

Wrangler's advantage is shelf position in U.S. mass and Western retail, reinforced by a low-cost, partly owned manufacturing base. Segment margin improved to 23.0% in FY2025 from 20.3%, and H1 FY2026 reached 28.8%, though that figure is inflated by tariff-refund credits that land disproportionately on the U.S.-import-heavy Wrangler business. The trajectory question is channel, not brand: Wrangler U.S. wholesale grew 1% in Q1 and was flat in Q2, while U.S. DTC grew 9% and international grew 10% reported (international DTC +31%). Both the 10-K and Q1 10-Q note retailers continuing to manage inventory conservatively.

The named competitive set includes Arc'teryx, Ariat, Calvin Klein, Carhartt, Columbia, Dickies, Levi's, Patagonia, The North Face, and Tommy Hilfiger, and, explicitly, expanding private-label programs from Kontoor's own largest retail partners. Kontoor's biggest customer competes with it on the same shelf, which is the structural constraint on the mass-channel model and the reason the distribution moat should not be confused with pricing power.

Exiting Lee improves portfolio quality: Lee revenue declined 5.1% in FY2025 with segment margin compressing to 9.2% from 11.3%. That Authentic Brands Group — an IP licensing platform, not an operator — is the buyer is itself informative: the acquirer values the trademark and royalty stream above the integrated operations, consistent with a motivated but not distressed seller obtaining roughly 1x sales and 11–14x segment profit for a shrinking asset.

The offsetting concern is that Helly Hansen enters a fiercely contested premium outdoor category against Arc'teryx (Amer Sports), Patagonia, Columbia, and The North Face — the last owned by Kontoor's former parent, several with stronger DTC franchises. Helly Hansen's marine/ski heritage and technical-workwear B2B channel are a defensible niche, but a narrower one than the "premium outdoor growth platform" framing implies.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, unimproved.** The FY2025 10-K discloses Walmart at 30% of net revenue in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025, with the top ten at 53%. Applied to reported revenue, a constant 30% share through a 20.9% revenue increase implies Walmart revenue growth of roughly 21% — hard to reconcile with 6% Wrangler U.S. wholesale growth and a declining Lee U.S. business. Either Walmart materially outgrew disclosed channel rates or the 30% figure is a rounded approximation carried forward. Under either reading, the operative conclusion holds: revenue *mix* diversified sharply in FY2025 (international wholesale +65%, DTC +49%) while disclosed single-counterparty concentration did not fall at all. Mix diversification is not counterparty diversification. The FY2026 10-K will restate this on a post-Lee, full-year-Helly-Hansen basis.

**Tariffs remain a two-sided open position.** IEEPA duties were ruled unauthorized in February 2026; Kontoor booked a $53.7M net receivable and had collected $23M as of the August release. Replacement Section 122 tariffs were ruled invalid in May and expired July 24, 2026 — Kontoor paid and expensed those and has recorded no receivable, leaving unrecognized potential upside. Working against that, new Section 301 tariffs of 10–12.5% took effect in July on the majority of Kontoor's sourcing countries, and FY2026 guidance assumes a 15% reciprocal rate in H2. Mexican owned production (USMCA-exempt) and >80% U.S. cotton content in Bangladesh-sourced goods are differentiated mitigants peers relying wholly on Asian contract manufacturing lack, but the Bangladesh exemption is described as something products "may qualify for," not an established benefit.

**Lee execution and stranded costs.** The forward-looking-statements list added language on completing the sale "if at all" and on the ability to mitigate stranded costs. Stranded overhead is $0.55/share unmitigated for FY2026 (~$38M pretax). Cash taxes on the ~$500M book gain are unquantified and could reduce net proceeds by $100M+; management's plan to deploy $400M into an ASR *and* repay $225M of term debt consumes essentially all of the net-of-tax proceeds, leaving no buffer for purchase-price adjustments. The $250M earnout depends on Lee's performance under an owner Kontoor does not control.

**Impairment headroom.** Continuing operations carry $461.1M of goodwill ($325.4M in Helly Hansen) and $416.4M of indefinite-lived trademarks against $618.5M of equity. The Helly Hansen purchase-price allocation was still preliminary as of Q1, with $43.0M of measurement-period goodwill additions already recorded. Lee's reporting-unit test in Q1 was qualitative only.

**Governance.** Scott Baxter remains combined CEO and Chairman. Six 8-Ks containing Item 5.02 (officer/director departures or appointments) were filed between July 2025 and July 2026; the specific causes are unverified but the frequency is high. The August 12 appointment of Alkire as President and CFO consolidates the finance function, global operations, and both operating brands under one executive, reducing the independence of finance from the businesses it reports on. Indexed TSR fell to 180.80 in FY2025 from 238.47, while the peer index rose to 223.27 — roughly a 30-point adverse swing in the acquisition year.

**Insider activity provides no positive confirmation.** Across the Form 4 record, transactions were grants and tax withholdings. No open-market purchases by any officer or director were observed. The one open-market sale was 4,000 shares by EVP Broyles at $81.02 on June 12, 2026. That single sale is low-signal in isolation; the complete absence of buying across a full year during which the stock traded from $88 to $56 is the more meaningful observation.

**Macro.** The Q1 10-Q added U.S.–Iran conflict as a named source of shipping-route, freight, fuel, and energy-cost disruption, and noted inflation accelerating late in Q1 on energy and tariffs. A U.S. mass-channel apparel supplier with 63% of continuing-operations revenue in U.S. wholesale sits directly in the path of any freight, fuel, and energy-cost disruption and tariff-driven inflation the company has flagged.

### Options Market Signal

The options snapshot straddles the earnings event and should be read accordingly. Near-term ATM IV of 62.7% against 47.6% one-year realized (a 15.1-point premium), backwardation into the December tenor at 45.3%, an implied move of ±8.7% on the August 21 expiry, and a jump in put/call open interest from 0.15 on July 24 to 2.03 all describe pre-event hedging rather than a structural bearish view. The realized move of +9.6% slightly exceeded what was priced, so near-term vol should compress from here, and buying options into this configuration is unattractive. Two observations carry past the event: the December tenor prints a *negative* put/call skew of −4.7%, meaning the market is not pricing a medium-term crash; and 12.7% short interest with 7.4 days to cover means the mechanical component of today's move is meaningful and may reverse. The 13.0% near-term put skew alongside a 10% upside gap is best read as expensive protection that has now paid out for hedgers and expired as a signal.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $108 (+31%)

Lee closes in Q4 at full value with modest cash-tax leakage; the $400M ASR retires ~9% of shares near current levels; stranded overhead is substantially eliminated through restructuring, restoring $0.50+ per share; Helly Hansen's H2 seasonal peak delivers on the mid-single-digit constant-currency base with segment margin above 12%, and the tax synergies embedded in the ~20% rate prove durable; Section 122 recovery litigation succeeds, adding unrecognized upside to the $53.7M IEEPA receivable already booked; Wrangler U.S. wholesale inflects positive on continued denim demand while international and DTC compound at high single digits. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $6.55–6.60 and the market pays 16.5x for a deleveraged, two-brand portfolio with mid-single-digit organic growth. Net leverage below 1.5x and a 2.5%+ dividend support the multiple.

### Bear Case — $61 (−26%)

Walmart resets Wrangler's assortment or expands private label into the price tiers Wrangler occupies; U.S. wholesale turns negative, taking 63% of continuing revenue with it. New Section 301 rates cannot be passed through in a mass channel where retailers are already managing inventory conservatively, and the 15% reciprocal assumption proves optimistic, compressing adjusted gross margin back below 49%. Stranded costs prove sticky, and a NOK/EUR reversal removes the 6% of Helly Hansen revenue currently supplied by translation, exposing mid-single-digit underlying growth as low-single-digit. Cash taxes on the Lee gain come in above expectation, forcing management to shrink the ASR or the debt repayment. With $618.5M of equity against $908.8M of goodwill and intangibles, any Helly Hansen impairment lands hard on book value. FY2027 EPS stalls near $5.30 and the multiple compresses to 11.5x.

### Base Case — $89 (+8.3%)

Lee closes in Q4 with $100–125M of cash taxes; the ASR executes near current prices, retiring ~4.9M shares; roughly half the stranded overhead is recaptured within twelve months; organic revenue grows 1–3% with margin doing the work; adjusted gross margin lands at the guided 49.8–50.0%. FY2027 adjusted EPS of approximately $6.30 at 14x — a modest discount to the apparel peer median, justified by 30% customer concentration and a flat core channel — gives $88, and the 2.58% dividend takes the total return to roughly 11%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold). Fifth consecutive HOLD; overall score 5.5, down from 5.7.**

The score reduction reflects specific new information rather than price action: Q2 confirmed organic growth of 1.5% with Wrangler U.S. wholesale flat; the headline "beat" resolved to an in-line continuing-operations result once the Lee reclassification is accounted for; new Section 301 tariffs of 10–12.5% arrived in July as an unrefundable cost; and the CFO appointment concentrates finance, operations and both brands under a single executive reporting to a combined CEO/Chairman. Those offset a welcome 150bp raise to adjusted gross margin guidance and clearer capital-return intent.

**Base case target: $89** — 14x FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$6.30 (continuing net income ~$294M, plus ~$15M after-tax stranded-cost recapture, ~$10M after-tax interest savings on $225M of repayment, ~$8M organic operating growth, on ~50.6M post-ASR shares), at a discount to the 21.8x apparel peer median warranted by concentration and channel stagnation.
**Bull case target: $108** (16.5x on $6.55).
**Bear case target: $61** (11.5x on $5.30).
**From $82.165: +8.3% to base, +31.4% to bull, −25.8% to bear.** Adding the 2.58% dividend, probability-weighted expected return is roughly 6% over twelve months, approximately fair, which is where HOLD belongs.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, keyed to the Lee close (Q4 FY2026), the Q4 print and ASR execution disclosure (roughly February 2027), and the FY2026 10-K (March 2027), which will restate customer concentration on a post-Lee basis.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of:**
1. Wrangler U.S. wholesale growth of ≥2% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the core channel is not structurally stalled.
2. Stranded-cost mitigation quantified in a filing at ≥$25M annualized, with the ASR executed at or below ~$85 average.
3. Price in the $70–73 range on unchanged guidance — below management's own H1 repurchase average of ~$75 and a level at which base-case upside exceeds 20%.
4. Helly Hansen H2 segment margin above 12% with company-published pro-forma constant-currency growth of ≥8%.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. The Lee agreement is terminated or repriced, or net-of-tax proceeds disclosed below ~$600M such that the $400M ASR plus $225M repayment cannot both be funded.
2. Adjusted gross margin guidance is cut below 49%, or FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is reduced.
3. Wrangler U.S. wholesale revenue declines year over year for two consecutive quarters, or the FY2026 10-K discloses Walmart above 32% of continuing-operations revenue.
4. A goodwill or trademark impairment is recorded, or net leverage fails to reach 2.0x by year-end FY2026.

**Next natural reassessment:** the Q3 FY2026 print (early November 2026), which should confirm the Lee closing timetable, the remaining IEEPA refund collection, initial Section 301 pass-through experience, and the H2 Helly Hansen seasonal ramp.

**For existing holders:** maintain. The dividend is covered at ~40% of adjusted EPS, the ASR provides mechanical per-share support, and deleveraging is on a credible arithmetic path. **For new capital:** wait. Entering after a 9.6% single-session gap in a business whose organic top line grew 1.5%, with 30% concentration in a customer that competes on the same shelf, does not offer the margin of safety a full position requires.

## Conclusion

Kontoor has done the harder part of the portfolio work well: it sold a shrinking brand at roughly 1x sales to a licensing platform that values the trademark more than the operations, bought a differentiated premium outdoor franchise, expanded Wrangler segment margin by nearly 300bp, and has committed the proceeds to retiring 9% of the share count and taking leverage below 1.5x. Guidance was raised on the August 12 print, and the market marked the shares up almost 10%.

What has not changed is the engine. Organic revenue grew 1.5% in the June quarter; the 63%-of-revenue U.S. wholesale channel was flat; Walmart remains 30% of revenue and is named in the company's own filings as an intensifying private-label competitor; a meaningful share of reported margin gain traces to tariff refunds and a facility-sale gain that will not repeat; and the FY2027 earnings bridge closes only if roughly half of $38M in stranded overhead is genuinely removed and a transaction that has not closed delivers net proceeds after unquantified cash taxes. At 15.5x guided adjusted EPS and ~10.8x pro-forma EV/EBITDA, the stock prices those tensions about right.

HOLD. Add in the low $70s, or on evidence that Wrangler's core U.S. channel has resumed growing and that the stranded costs are actually coming out.
