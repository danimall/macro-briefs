## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer and a direct beneficiary of the Strait of Hormuz closure, which has removed approximately 25% of global seaborne fertilizer trade for over 10 weeks. The stock has appreciated 40% over the past year and 53% YTD through early May, with consensus EPS estimates for the current quarter revised upward 174% over 90 days (from $2.16 to $5.91). At $123, the stock trades at approximately 7x current-year earnings estimates of $18.07, deeply discounted relative to Materials sector peers at 25.9x.

The fundamental case is strong: $1.8B in annual free cash flow, $2B in cash, no debt maturing until 2034, and an aggressive buyback program that has retired 20% of shares outstanding since 2022. The Donaldsonville CCS facility is operational and generating 45Q credits, while the Blue Point JV ($3.7B) positions CF for the emerging low-carbon ammonia market. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $983M confirms the earnings acceleration is real.

The primary risk is binary: if the Iran conflict resolves and Hormuz reopens, nitrogen prices could snap back rapidly, potentially compressing earnings by 50%+ within 2-3 quarters. The options market is pricing this concern aggressively, with near-term IV at 68% versus 41.5% historical volatility, elevated put skew at 19.0%, and a backwardated term structure indicating imminent event risk. The stock fell 3% on May 7 peace negotiation news alone.

## Company Overview

CF Industries Holdings operates the world's largest ammonia production network across six U.S. manufacturing facilities, two Canadian facilities, one UK facility, and a 50% JV in Trinidad. The Donaldsonville, Louisiana complex alone accounts for approximately 40% of total capacity. The company's core competitive advantage derives from North American natural gas pricing ($3-4/MMBtu) versus European producers ($10-15/MMBtu), creating a structural margin advantage that persists across commodity cycles.

The company's product mix includes anhydrous ammonia, granular urea, UAN (urea ammonium nitrate), and ammonium nitrate, sold into agricultural (fertilizer) and industrial (diesel exhaust fluid, nitric acid) end markets. CF's extensive Midwestern storage and distribution terminal network creates meaningful barriers to entry for imported nitrogen products in North America.

CF's earnings troughed in FY2024 ($6.74 EPS) and are now experiencing a structural uplift from the Hormuz closure that has compressed global nitrogen supply. The company is positioned on the right side of the marginal cost curve.

**Growth Vectors:**

## Valuation Assessment

At $123, CF trades at:
- 6.8x current-year consensus EPS ($18.07)
- 11.1x next-year consensus EPS ($11.06)
- 13.7x trailing EPS ($8.97)
- ~5.5x FY2026 estimated EBITDA (annualizing Q1 at $3.9B)

Peer Materials companies trade at a median 25.9x P/E. Even acknowledging CF's cyclical earnings profile and that current-year estimates reflect peak pricing conditions, the stock is trading at a severe discount. Normalizing to next-year estimates ($11.06, which still assumes elevated but not peak pricing), CF trades at 11.1x — still well below peers.

The key question is whether $11.06 is "normalized" or still elevated. If Hormuz reopens and pricing reverts toward 2024 levels ($313/ton), earnings could revert toward $6-7/share, implying a trailing-normalized P/E of ~18x. This is closer to fair value for a cyclical industrial, though still below peers given CF's superior FCF generation and capital return profile.

Bull case valuation: $150 (analyst high target) implies 8.3x current-year EPS — still cheap if Hormuz disruption persists through H2.
Bear case valuation: $86 (analyst low) implies earnings normalization to ~$7 EPS at 12x multiple.

The valuation is compelling on forward estimates, but the forward estimates themselves carry binary risk.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive position is strengthening on multiple dimensions:

1. **Cost curve position:** North American gas at $3-4/MMBtu versus European producers at $10-15+. This structural advantage widens during energy crises.
2. **Distribution moat:** Extensive Midwestern terminal network creates captive regional demand.
3. **First-mover in low-carbon ammonia:** Donaldsonville CCS already operational; Blue Point JV positions for Japanese co-firing demand.
4. **Scale advantage:** Largest global ammonia production network provides operating leverage and customer reliability.
5. **Geopolitical insulation:** 100% North American production base with no Hormuz exposure, unlike competitors dependent on Middle Eastern/Trinidadian/North African supply chains.

EU CBAM implementation (2026-2027) creates a potential premium market for CF's low-carbon ammonia that European competitors cannot easily access. Chinese export restrictions further support global pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geopolitical Binary (Severe):** The Hormuz closure is the dominant earnings driver. If the Iran conflict resolves, nitrogen prices could fall 30-40% within quarters. CF stock declined 3% on a single day of peace negotiation news (May 7).

**2. Natural Gas Cost Inflation:** Henry Hub averaged $6.32/MMBtu in early 2026, nearly double FY2025's $3.53. Each $1/MMBtu increase costs approximately $350M in annual margin. Record LNG exports suggest structurally higher domestic gas pricing.

**3. Yazoo City Production Loss:** All production idled since November 2025; restart not expected until Q4 2026. Reduces 2026 production by ~600K+ tons. While this supports pricing by constraining supply, it limits CF's ability to fully capitalize on elevated prices.

**4. Blue Point Execution Risk:** $3.7B project using ATR technology at unprecedented scale. Louisiana moratorium on Class VI well permits, tariff exposure on imported materials, and 2029 timeline create significant execution uncertainty. CF bears 40% of cost (~$1.5B).

**5. Overcapacity Cycle Risk (Medium-Term):** Multiple low-carbon ammonia projects announced globally. If supply growth outpaces new demand applications (maritime fuel, power generation), pricing could revert to 2017 lows.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is fundamentally well-positioned: lowest-cost nitrogen producer globally, generating exceptional free cash flow, aggressively returning capital, and benefiting from a structural supply shock that has driven a genuine earnings acceleration. The estimate revision velocity (+174% over 90 days) would normally constitute a strong BUY signal.

However, three factors argue for HOLD rather than adding capital at $123:

1. **Binary geopolitical catalyst:** The Hormuz disruption accounts for the majority of the earnings uplift. Unlike structural demand shifts, this catalyst could reverse in days. The downside on resolution is 23-30% while upside from sustained disruption is 14-22% — the asymmetry at current price favors waiting for a pullback.

2. **Options market divergence:** Near-term IV at 68% with elevated put skew and backwardation signals that informed participants are pricing meaningful near-term downside risk. When the options market prices imminent stress on a stock under consideration, the cost of patience is low relative to the cost of catching a geopolitical reversal.

3. **Valuation anchored to peak-cycle earnings:** At $123, the stock is cheap on current estimates but reasonably valued on normalized earnings ($6-7 EPS at 18x = $108-126). The market is partially correct to assign a discount for cycle peak — the question is whether this cycle sustains.

For existing holders, CF remains attractive. The $2B buyback authorization, 9.5% FCF yield on trailing numbers, first-mover low-carbon positioning, and 2029 Blue Point JV growth optionality support continued ownership. The stock is worth monitoring for pullbacks into the $105-110 range where risk/reward improves materially.
