**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Conagra closed fiscal 2026 (ended May 31, 2026) with net sales of $11,281.6M (−2.9%), a GAAP net loss of $1,916.2M, or $(4.00) per diluted share, and adjusted EPS of $1.72, down 25.2%. The loss is almost entirely non-cash: $2,382.4M of goodwill impairment and $547.2M of other intangible impairment, $2,731.4M after tax ($5.69/share). That charge reduces book equity, retained earnings (from $6,759.1M to $4,171.7M) and reported returns; it does not touch operating cash flow, free cash flow, cash interest or the leverage-based covenants. The trailing P/E of −3.76 shown in most data feeds is an artifact of the write-down and should be discarded.

The operating story underneath is the actual problem. Organic sales fell only 0.4% (volume −1.4%, price/mix +1.0%), yet adjusted operating profit fell 21.8% and adjusted operating margin fell 274 basis points to 11.3%. Refrigerated & Frozen — the largest segment at $4,641.8M — carried most of the damage, with margin down 351bp to 10.5% and profit down 25.5%. FY2027 guidance extends the compression: organic sales −1% to −3%, volume down mid-single digits against ~3% price/mix, adjusted operating margin 10.0-10.5%, adjusted EPS $1.40-1.50, capex up to ~$550M from $423.4M, free cash flow conversion above 90% versus 119% delivered, and net leverage rising to ~4.0x from 3.83x. Consensus of $1.45 for the current year sits exactly at the guidance midpoint, and the 14.6% cut to current-year estimates over 90 days is consensus catching up to the July 15 guide rather than fresh deterioration since.

Two things keep this from being an AVOID. First, the dividend cut to $0.175 quarterly ($0.70 annualized) converts an uncovered payout into a covered one: at ~479M shares the new rate costs ~$335M against derived FY27E free cash flow of roughly $627M, a 53% payout, versus ~107% at the old $1.40 rate. The remaining ~$292M is real deleveraging capacity. Second, the equity is priced for the reset — 10.4x guided FY27 adjusted EPS, 8.3x EV to derived FY27E EBITDA, an 8.7% forward FCF yield, and a 4.7% dividend that now looks fundable. New CEO John Brase bought 35,000 shares in the open market at $14.5895 on July 17, two days after announcing the cut. Against that, the covenant cushion is thin, October 2026 maturities are unresolved in the public record, and there is no strategic catalyst before an Investor Day in early calendar 2027. HOLD, for the cash return rather than for growth.

## Company Overview

Conagra is a North American branded packaged-food manufacturer reporting four segments. FY2026 net sales and adjusted operating margins: Refrigerated & Frozen $4,641.8M at 10.5% (from 14.0%); Grocery & Snacks $4,610.1M at 19.2% (from 20.8%); Foodservice $1,115.8M at 10.2% (from 12.0%); International $913.9M at 14.7% (from 15.1%). Brands include Birds Eye, Healthy Choice, Marie Callender's, Duncan Hines, Reddi-wip, Slim Jim and Angie's BOOMCHICKAPOP. Chef Boyardee was divested in Q1 FY26 for $607.0M of net proceeds.

An equity-method interest in Ardent Mills, the flour-milling joint venture, contributed $140.7M of equity earnings in FY26, down 22.8% from $182.4M on lower commodity trading revenue. That is roughly 8% of company-defined adjusted EBITDA from an asset Conagra does not control and cannot readily monetize without disturbing a supply relationship.

Two structural features frame the whole investment case. The ten largest customers were approximately 60% of FY26 net sales, with Walmart alone at approximately 29%; Conagra is about to push a ~3% price increase through a counterparty that is nearly three-tenths of its revenue. And the balance sheet is dominated by acquired intangibles: goodwill of $8,119.3M plus brands and other intangibles of $1,830.7M totals $9,950.0M against total stockholders' equity of $6,357.6M, so tangible book equity is negative roughly $3,592M. Most of that traces to the 2019 Pinnacle Foods deal, and recent indefinite-lived brand impairments have concentrated in Pinnacle-acquired brands.

## Financial Analysis

**The margin problem is not a revenue problem.** FY26 gross profit fell 10.2% to $2,698.4M and gross margin fell 194bp to 23.9% on organic sales that were essentially flat. Inflation running ahead of productivity, an unfavorable mix as frozen deleveraged, and fixed-cost absorption on declining volume explain the gap. Reported sales of −2.9% decompose into M&A/divestitures −4.6pp, the 53rd week +1.8pp ($204.3M), FX +0.3pp and organic −0.4pp. FY2027 faces the reverse 53rd-week headwind, so guided organic of −1% to −3% translates to reported sales of roughly $10.85B, which matters when comparing headline revenue lines year over year.

**Cash conversion is the genuine strength.** FY26 operating cash flow was $1,402.1M against capex of $423.4M, giving free cash flow of $978.7M and conversion of 118.9% on adjusted net income of $823.3M, the third consecutive year above 115%. Working capital contributed: inventories released $144.9M and receivables $35.0M, with inventory falling from $2,048.3M to $1,905.4M. Two caveats. The year-over-year OCF decline versus FY25's $1,691.9M partly reflects lapping an accelerated receivable collection in FY25, and litigation was a net ~$76.6M cash drag in FY26 ($156.8M of accruals net of payments against $80.2M of receivables net of recoveries). Working-capital harvesting is finite; the litigation drag should not recur at that level.

**Leverage is the binding constraint.** At May 31, 2026: notes payable $34.2M, current installments of long-term debt $778.2M and senior long-term debt $6,456.0M give total debt of $7,268.4M; less $218.0M of cash, net debt is $7,050.4M. The company reported net leverage of 3.83x, which implies leverage EBITDA of ~$1,840M. Building that up from disclosed components — adjusted operating profit $1,278.7M plus D&A $396.0M plus ~$146M of adjusted equity-method earnings plus ~$25M of pension non-service income = $1,845.7M — agrees within 0.3%, confirming that Conagra's covenant EBITDA includes JV earnings and pension income. Interest coverage on that basis is 4.8x ($1,846M ÷ $382.6M) against a 3.0x floor, so coverage is not the pressure point.

The leverage test is. Guided FY27 net leverage of ~4.0x against derived FY27E EBITDA of ~$1,707M (adjusted operating profit ~$1,112M + D&A ~$430M + equity earnings $140M + pension $25M) implies net debt of ~$6,828M. The 4.5x maintenance covenant at that debt level permits minimum EBITDA of $1,517M, leaving cushion of roughly $190M, or about 11% of guided EBITDA. For a company planning mid-single-digit volume declines while raising price ~3% into a value-conscious consumer, that is roughly one bad elasticity quarter of headroom. Two mitigants: the credit-agreement EBITDA definition almost certainly carries add-backs (restructuring, impairments) beyond what I reconstructed, which would widen the cushion, and the $2.0B revolver (expandable to $2.5B, maturing June 2030) was undrawn at year end. Treat 11% as an order of magnitude.

**Liquidity mechanics.** Current assets of $2,882.1M against current liabilities of $3,188.0M gives a current ratio of 0.90. That reads worse than it is: $778.2M of the current liabilities is the October 2026 note maturity ($500M at 5.3% and $262.5M at 7.125%, $762.5M total), backstopped by the undrawn revolver. But it does mean Conagra depends on capital-markets access. Management said on July 15 that options were still being evaluated, "whether that's commercial paper, whether that's term loans, whether that's public notes." An 8-K filed July 28 carrying Items 1.01, 2.03 and 8.01 is plausibly that financing; the instrument, size and pricing are not verified here. The existing note stack carries a ~5.0% weighted-average coupon, so refinancing at current investment-grade spreads should not be dramatically dilutive to interest expense, and guidance already assumes interest of ~$360M.

**The dividend cut, quantified.** The Board declared $0.175 per quarter on July 15 (payable September 2), annualizing to $0.70 from $1.40. At ~479M shares that saves ~$336M a year, reconciling with management's "roughly a billion dollars" over three years. Against derived FY27E FCF of ~$627M (>90% conversion on ~$697M of adjusted net income), the old rate would have consumed ~107% of free cash flow before any debt reduction, while capex simultaneously stepped up ~$127M. I am describing arithmetic rather than inferring motive; a new CEO may also have chosen to reset early to buy room he did not strictly need in FY27.

**Path to the 3.0x target.** At $1,707M of EBITDA, 3.0x implies net debt of $5,121M versus ~$6,828M expected at FY27 year-end, a $1,707M gap. At ~$292M a year of post-dividend free cash flow with flat EBITDA, that is roughly 5.8 years; if EBITDA recovers toward $1,900M by FY29, roughly 3.9 years. Divestiture proceeds are the realistic accelerant, and management confirmed no portfolio action is embedded in the FY27 guide. FY26 offers a warning on that route: the Chef Boyardee sale produced a $42.2M pretax book gain but carried $73.9M of associated income tax, a net $31.7M after-tax loss. Headline sale prices may overstate net deleveraging proceeds meaningfully.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth here on a one-to-two-year view, and the guidance says so. FY27 volume is guided down mid-single digits with price/mix ~+3%, inflation 5-6% against productivity above 4%, a $40M tariff headwind weighted to Q1 (tin-plate steel for cans is specifically named), advertising and promotion up $40M (+14%), gross margin roughly flat, and pricing effective only from mid-Q2 — a half-year benefit in FY27 with a favorable wrap in FY28. Q1 operating margin is guided to high single digits, which explains the 23.8% cut to current-quarter consensus ($0.37 → $0.28 over 30 days; $0.39 → $0.28 over 90 days) far more than any new deterioration.

The guidance build is internally consistent, which raises confidence in its achievability at the midpoint: FY26 sales of $11,281.6M × 0.98 less the $204.3M 53rd week ≈ $10,850M; at the 10.25% margin midpoint that is $1,112M of adjusted operating profit; less $360M interest, plus $25M pension and $140M equity earnings gives $917M pretax; at a 24% tax rate and 479.8M diluted shares that is $1.45 — the guidance midpoint exactly.

The forward case rests on FY28: a full-year wrap on the mid-FY27 pricing, savings from insourcing (roughly $100M of the $127M capex step-up is fried-chicken supply-chain insourcing), SKU rationalization out of a 5,500-SKU portfolio the CEO called "too large… too complex for too long," and reinvested A&P showing up in volume. Consensus for next year of $1.55 implies +6.9% off the $1.45 base. That is the recovery investors are being asked to underwrite, and it is contingent on elasticities behaving no worse than management assumed on a portfolio that, per a BNP Paribas analyst on the call and uncontradicted by management, is entering its sixth consecutive year of organic volume decline. Management did characterize FY27 assumptions as embedding "higher than historic elasticities," weighted toward frozen.

Estimate revisions are uniformly negative: current quarter −27.9%, current year −14.6%, next year −13.4% over 90 days. Growth Prospects scores 2.5.

## Valuation Assessment

Price feeds in the data conflict — the last close in the price series is $15.03 (July 30), while the profile and options snapshots show $14.51-$14.55. I use $15.03; the ~3% difference does not change any conclusion.

At $15.03 on 478.6M shares, market cap is $7,193M and enterprise value $14,243M against net debt of $7,050M:

Peer medians in packaged foods are P/E 11.2x and yield 1.8% (CPB 11.2x/6.8%, PPC 8.0x, POST 15.7x, LW 21.1x, CALM 5.5x). CAG at 10.4x forward is a modest discount to a heterogeneous and largely distressed peer set — CPB, LW and PPC are each 31-44% below their 52-week highs. There is no obvious mispricing on earnings multiples.

The support comes from cash. An 8.7% forward FCF yield with a 53% payout is a real floor argument, and a 4.7% dividend that no longer exceeds free cash flow removes the most obvious source of forced selling. Mean analyst target of $14.38 and median of $14.00 sit *below* the current price, with the distribution of ratings at 1 strong buy, 1 buy, 10 hold, 3 sell and 2 strong sell — the sell-side does not see upside from here. On stocks with falling estimates, I treat consensus targets as ceilings rather than floors; the "targets lag reality" argument applies to inflecting estimates, and these are going the other way.

The critical valuation mechanic is that equity is only 50.5% of enterprise value. A 10% change in either the EBITDA multiple or EBITDA itself moves the equity roughly 20%. That leverage cuts both ways and is the reason a stock this cheap on FCF is not a buy: the same balance sheet that makes the FCF yield look generous makes the equity a levered claim on a guidance range with an 11% covenant cushion. Valuation scores 5.0 — supportive, not asymmetric, and cheapness in this sector has repeatedly been a trap rather than a signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Conagra's stated advantages are scale in frozen and a portfolio spanning the value spectrum, and Q4 FY26 volume share gains were cited in frozen single-serve meals, frozen multi-serve meals, frozen vegetables, meat snacks, seeds and pudding. Set against that, the segment margin data show a deteriorating trajectory: total adjusted operating margin down 274bp, Refrigerated & Frozen down 351bp, Grocery & Snacks down 156bp, Foodservice down to 10.2% despite being the only organically growing segment (+1.4%). An analyst on the call framed operating margins as having fallen from ~16% a few years ago to guided 10-10.5%, and management did not dispute it.

Three factors make Conagra's position weaker than most peers running the same price-for-volume playbook. Leverage heading to 4.0x limits the ability to fund promotional defense with the same cash earmarked for deleveraging — the tension Barclays pressed on the call and which the CEO acknowledged by saying early investor feedback suggests "the reinvestment planned at this stage looks insufficient." Customer concentration means the FY27 price increase is negotiated with counterparties running their own price-investment agendas. And the acquired-intangible-heavy balance sheet means competitive underperformance surfaces as impairment rather than merely slower growth.

Category-level risk is also shifting. The FY26 10-K names pending litigation alleging certain products "should be considered 'ultra-processed'" — a specific litigation theory, not boilerplate — alongside the MAHA Commission, FDA synthetic-dye phase-out, extended producer responsibility packaging legislation and PFAS. Management positions the portfolio as a relative GLP-1 beneficiary (portion-controlled, high-protein, high-fiber), which is plausible but unquantified. Competitive Position scores 3.5: the frozen franchise is real, the trajectory is negative.

## Risk Assessment

**Covenant proximity.** ~11% of EBITDA of headroom to the 4.5x maintenance test at guided FY27 leverage. This is the mechanism by which an ordinary elasticity miss becomes a capital-structure conversation. Mitigants: broader covenant add-backs, an undrawn $2.0B revolver, amendment capacity, and management's repeated commitment to investment grade. Actual agency ratings and any post-cut actions are not verified here.

**October 2026 refinancing.** $762.5M due, plan not confirmed in the public record as analyzed. The July 28 Items 1.01/2.03 8-K plausibly addresses it. An analyst asked whether hybrid (junior subordinated) capital was contemplated within the 4.0x guide; management did not confirm. If hybrids were used, reported leverage would be flattered by partial equity treatment, worth checking when the filing is read.

**Reflexive impairment.** The Q4 charge was "primarily triggered by a sustained decline in the company's share price and market capitalization," the same trigger cited for the Q2 charge. The 10-K states Refrigerated & Frozen is the only reporting unit whose fair value exceeds carrying value by 10% or less (11.0% discount rate, 1.5% long-term growth), that a 50bp discount-rate increase reduces that unit's fair value by $120.0M, and that brands with under-10% cushion carry $902.1M with a 100bp royalty-rate reduction worth $215.8M. Aggregate carrying value of invested capital ($6,357.6M equity + $7,050.4M net debt = $13,408M) sits only ~6% below the current EV. Any further charge would be non-cash and would not affect covenant EBITDA — Conagra's covenants are leverage and coverage based, not net-worth based — but it would be a headline negative and would further reduce book equity. A customary 15-20% control premium in the reasonableness test makes this comfortable; this is a sensitivity, not a forecast.

**Litigation.** Cooking-spray claims consumed $141.1M of cash in FY26 through February plus $25M in FY25, with $44.3M more agreed, while the recorded insurance receivable fell from $81.8M to $1.6M and the accrual fell from $204.5M to $47.8M. A $25M compensatory verdict in *Esparza v. Conagra* on a diacetyl-exposure theory is under appeal. The smaller accrual reflects claims paid, not exposure eliminated, and new adverse outcomes look more likely to be self-funded — though a $1.6M recorded receivable is an accounting balance, not a statement of remaining policy limits. Beatrice legacy environmental exposure spans ~35 Superfund or state-equivalent sites with a $40.2M accrual.

**Fixed-cost absorption.** $2.72B of unconditional purchase obligations, $1.63B due in FY27, plus ~$826M of warehousing agreements with terms to 10 years. Mid-single-digit volume declines risk paying against unused capacity, which does not appear in a margin model.

**Technical/ownership.** June 29 volume of 158.9M shares against a 13.9M average is consistent with an index-related rebalance, corroborating secondary reports of S&P 500 deletion around late June. That would explain part of the March-June decline through forced selling and a shifted marginal holder base, and it also fed the market-capitalization-triggered impairment. Short interest is 13.3% of float at 2.0 days to cover — elevated positioning, but easily covered, so squeeze risk is moderate rather than acute.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 34.5% is rich against 29.9% one-year historical volatility, but sits at only the 15th percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in contango (34.5% at one month versus 37.1% at six months); the data characterize the near-term put/call skew of −1.4% as balanced. The market is not pricing an imminent event; the highest implied vol and the largest implied move (17.4%) sit at the December 2026 tenor, which brackets the October refinancing and the first two quarters of guidance execution rather than the next print. Put/call volume ratio of 1.86 against a call-heavy open-interest ratio of 0.45 suggests recent hedging activity layered onto a longer-standing call-oriented base; the single flagged trade is 1,028 August 21 $14 puts against 267 open interest, near-the-money protection into the Q1 report. The read is consistent with the fundamental view: no near-term crisis priced, meaningful uncertainty about the second half of FY27.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($18.50-19.00, +23% to +26%).** FY27 delivers at or above the $1.45 midpoint because Grocery & Snacks elasticities behave near 1:1 and the frozen price increase sticks with Walmart. The October maturity is refinanced at spreads near the existing ~5.0% coupon, removing the capital-markets overhang. Net debt falls ~$292M, leverage holds at 4.0x rather than drifting, and the FY28 wrap on mid-year pricing plus insourcing savings and SKU rationalization delivers the consensus $1.55. On 12x FY28E EPS of $1.55, or 9.0x EV/EBITDA of $1,750M less ~$6.8B net debt, the equity is worth $18.50-19.00, with a 4.7% dividend collected en route. Additional optionality: a divestiture at a reasonable after-tax multiple accelerates deleveraging, and the early-CY2027 Investor Day gives the new CEO a venue to reframe the portfolio.

**Bear case ($10.50-11.00, −27% to −30%).** Mid-single-digit volume declines prove optimistic once ~3% price lands mid-Q2 into a consumer already trading down, arguing for worse elasticity. EBITDA lands near $1,600M instead of $1,707M, leverage drifts toward 4.3x, the covenant conversation becomes live, and the equity re-rates to 7.5x EV/EBITDA: $12.0B EV less $7.0B net debt is $5.0B of equity, or ~$10.44. A further Refrigerated & Frozen impairment adds a headline negative and reduces book equity toward zero on a tangible basis. Take-or-pay obligations of $1.63B in FY27 bite on unused capacity, and an adverse cooking-spray verdict is now largely self-funded.

**Base case ($16.00, +6.5% price, ~+11% total return).** Guidance is achieved near the midpoint, the refinancing closes uneventfully, and the multiple holds at 8.5x EV to FY27E EBITDA of $1,707M: $14,510M EV less $6,828M net debt is $7,682M of equity, or $16.04 per share. The dividend adds 4.7%. The distribution around that base is negatively skewed because equity is only half of enterprise value.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The reason to own is the cash return — a 4.7% dividend now covered at ~53% of guided free cash flow plus an 8.7% forward FCF yield — not appreciation. Expected total return of roughly +11% to base case is not compensation for a −30% bear case on a business guiding its sixth-plus year of volume decline into a 4.5x covenant.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**
1. **Volume stabilization with pricing intact** — Q2 or Q3 FY27 organic volume decline of 3% or better against price/mix at or above +2.5%, confirming elasticities are inside management's assumption.
2. **Leverage moving the right way** — reported net leverage at or below 3.9x at any FY27 interim date, with the October 2026 maturities refinanced in straight senior notes or term debt (not hybrids) at a coupon at or below 6%.
3. **Price below $12.50** on unchanged guidance, which would put the stock near a 10.5%+ FY27E FCF yield and roughly 7.5x EV/EBITDA, restoring genuine asymmetry.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Guidance cut** — FY27 adjusted EPS guided below $1.35 or adjusted operating margin below 10.0%, which would push derived EBITDA toward the $1,550-1,600M range and cut covenant cushion to single digits.
2. **Financing stress** — the October maturity addressed with hybrids or secured debt, a drawn revolver at quarter-end, a credit downgrade to the bottom of investment grade with a negative outlook, or any covenant amendment request.
3. **Dividend or capital-allocation reversal** — a second cut, or a suspension of the residual buyback authorization framed as liquidity preservation.
4. **Concentration event** — a disclosed loss or material reset of terms with the ~29% customer.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q1 FY27 results (expected late September/early October 2026) are the first test, and management has pre-warned high-single-digit operating margin with the $40M tariff hit weighted there — a Q1 miss on margin alone is not thesis-breaking, but a volume decline worse than mid-single digits is. The October 2026 refinancing is the next hard event. The Investor Day in early calendar 2027 is where the SKU-rationalization and portfolio conclusions land; that is the earliest plausible re-rating catalyst and it is two-plus quarters away.

## Conclusion

The score moves from 4.0 to 3.9, and the recommendation stays HOLD. That reflects two offsetting developments since the last review. The FY27 guide is worse than the prior trajectory implied — adjusted operating margin to 10.0-10.5% from 14.1% two years ago, adjusted EPS down another 16% at the midpoint, capex up $127M, and leverage rising rather than falling — and consensus has cut current-year estimates to meet it. Growth Prospects at 2.5 and Financial Health at 4.0 carry that.

Working the other way, the dividend reset removed a payout that consumed ~107% of guided free cash flow and replaced it with one at ~53%, the new CEO put ~$511K of his own money in at essentially today's price, and the equity now trades at 10.4x guided earnings with an 8.7% forward FCF yield after a 26% decline from its 52-week high — part of which appears to be index-deletion mechanics rather than fundamentals. Management Quality at 5.5 holds on the insider purchase and the coherence of the capital-allocation reset; Valuation at 5.0 credits the cash yield without pretending cheapness is a catalyst.

What prevents a more constructive call is the arithmetic of a levered equity stub. Equity is 50.5% of enterprise value, so the ~11% EBITDA cushion to a 4.5x maintenance covenant translates directly into equity volatility, and the path to the stated 3.0x target is four to six years organically — longer if divestitures continue to carry the kind of tax leakage that turned a $42.2M book gain into a $31.7M after-tax loss. Own it for the dividend if you already do. New capital should wait for either volume stabilization with pricing intact, or a price near $12.50.
