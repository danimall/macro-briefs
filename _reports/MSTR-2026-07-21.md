**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Strategy Inc. (formerly MicroStrategy) is a leveraged bitcoin accumulation vehicle wrapped around a small, cash-flow-negative enterprise analytics software business. It holds roughly 843,775 BTC acquired for an aggregate ~$54.5B, funded through common stock, convertible notes, and perpetual preferred stock. The investment case rested entirely on a reflexive flywheel: as long as the stock traded above the value of its bitcoin holdings (mNAV > 1.0), the company could issue equity, buy more bitcoin, and grow bitcoin-per-share accretively. That mechanism has now inverted.

As of mid-2026, market capitalization (~$34B) sits below the value of the bitcoin holdings (~$52B), putting mNAV below 1.0. At a discount to net asset value, issuing common stock to buy bitcoin destroys bitcoin-per-share rather than building it. The company responded by doing what its 10-K explicitly said it did not anticipate doing: selling bitcoin. It disposed of 3,588 BTC (~$216M) in late June through early July 2026 to fund preferred dividends and rebuild a USD reserve, sold $467M of its own stock in mid-July, and paused bitcoin accumulation. JPMorgan flagged that the new sales policy introduces two-way risk to crypto markets. The perpetual preferred dividend obligation of ~$230M+ per quarter is the binding constraint, and CoinDesk estimated roughly 10 months of dividend runway at current cash levels.

The stock has fallen approximately 77-80% from its 52-week high of $454. Given the equity lessons that "cheap" is anti-predictive absent a specific catalyst, and that a >30% drawdown places the burden of proof on the bull case, the bearish thesis here is concrete and falsifiable: the accretion engine is broken, the funding channels are impaired (STRC preferred trades 25-26% below par despite an 11.25% coupon), and the company is now a forced seller into a weak bitcoin market. We rate MSTR AVOID.

## Company Overview

Strategy operates two mismatched businesses. The dominant one is a bitcoin treasury operation: raise capital, buy bitcoin, hold it, and repeat. Bitcoin holdings represent the overwhelming majority of the ~$61.6B balance sheet. The secondary business is legacy enterprise analytics (the Strategy One cloud platform and Mosaic data layer), generating only ~$477M of annual revenue — about 0.8% of total assets — and running an operating cash deficit. On-premise perpetual licenses reach end-of-support December 31, 2026, and the company is migrating customers to cloud subscriptions during a period of managed decline.

The company is not registered as an investment company and offers none of the protections of an ETF or mutual fund. Control rests with Executive Chairman Michael Saylor, who holds approximately 40.1% of total voting power through super-voting Class B shares (10 votes per share). President and CEO Phong Le and CFO Andrew Kang run day-to-day operations.

Functionally, this is a bitcoin holding company with a small software operation attached, and it must be analyzed as such. Traditional software valuation frameworks are irrelevant; the metrics that matter are bitcoin price, mNAV, preferred dividend coverage and runway, and continued access to capital markets.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet.** The adoption of fair-value accounting for bitcoin (ASU 2023-08, effective January 1, 2025) transformed the reported balance sheet. Total assets grew from $4.8B (2023) to $25.8B (2024) to $61.6B (2025). Stockholders' equity rose from $2.2B to $18.2B to $44.1B. Long-term debt stands at $8.16B; debt-to-equity is a superficially benign 0.24, current ratio 5.62, and Altman Z-Score 3.26. These ratios are artifacts of the massive marked-up bitcoin asset base, not evidence of operating strength. When bitcoin falls below cost basis, the equity cushion shrinks and the ratios deteriorate mechanically.

**Income statement.** 2025 revenue was $477.2M (+3% YoY) with gross profit of $327.8M (-1.8%). The operating loss of -$5.44B was driven almost entirely by a $5.40B unrealized bitcoin loss; net loss was -$3.85B, partially offset by a $1.68B tax benefit. Quarterly bitcoin marks are the entire story: Q1'25 -$5.91B, Q2'25 +$14.05B, Q3'25 +$3.89B, Q4'25 -$17.44B. Reported EPS and net income convey nothing about the underlying business. The earnings surprise history confirms this: a Q4'25 EPS "miss" of -1,518% and a Q2'25 "beat" of +34,929% are noise generated by bitcoin marks, not operational signal.

**Cash flow — the critical weakness.** Operating cash flow was -$67.2M in 2025; free cash flow -$75.5M. Unrealized bitcoin gains generate zero cash. The company funded its interest and preferred dividend obligations by issuing securities: $23.2B raised in 2025 alone (~$16.2B common, ~$7B preferred). Preferred dividends paid in 2025 were $381.4M, with the forward run-rate materially higher as the preferred stack has grown to ~$230M+ per quarter. The software business does not self-fund; the entire cash-servicing model depends on continuous access to capital markets.

**The runway problem.** With mNAV below 1.0, common equity issuance is dilutive to bitcoin-per-share. The company retained two levers to service dividends: sell more stock (now value-destructive) or sell bitcoin (now happening). The USD reserve was rebuilt to ~$3B, but CoinDesk estimated only ~10 months of dividend runway. This is the binding financial constraint and the reason the financial health score is 3.5.

## Growth Analysis

The relevant growth metric was never software revenue — it was bitcoin-per-share (BTC Yield), management's own preferred KPI. BTC Yield fell to 22.8% in 2025 from 74.3% in 2024. The accretion engine was decelerating sharply before the 2026 stress, and with mNAV below 1.0 it has now stalled entirely. With stock sales and skipped bitcoin purchases, accumulation has effectively paused.

Estimate revisions confirm negative momentum, though they are only meaningful in aggregate given the bitcoin-driven noise. Current-year consensus EPS has been slashed to $16.87 from $81.96 ninety days ago (-79.4%); next-year estimates fell to $8.77 from $19.08 (-54.0%). These revisions track the collapse in bitcoin marks and the impaired capital-markets access, not operating deterioration in software.

The software business itself offers no growth story: +3% revenue, declining gross profit, and a perpetual-license end-of-support cliff at year-end 2026. There is no organic growth thesis here that is independent of bitcoin price appreciation, and the leverage structure now amplifies downside rather than upside. Growth prospects score 3.0.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is genuinely difficult because the company itself warns that traditional methods do not apply and that its market cap has historically exceeded GAAP equity. The market has now flipped to the opposite extreme, valuing MSTR below the value of its bitcoin. This is the central valuation fact.

At ~$101 per share and ~$34B market cap against ~$52B of bitcoin, the market is assigning negative value to everything else: the ~$8.25B convertible debt, the ~$8.47B notional preferred stack with recurring dividends, corporate overhead, the cash-burning software business, and the forced-seller dynamics and reflexive downside risk. The bull argument (Insider Monkey and others) is that trading below NAV makes MSTR "undervalued." That argument fails to price the structural liabilities that sit ahead of common equity in the capital stack and the fact that the discount can persist or widen as long as the company remains a forced seller and its preferred funding channel is impaired.

The equity lessons here are directly applicable: a "cheap" valuation is anti-predictive absent a specific catalyst, and the discount-to-NAV thesis has no catalyst to close it. If anything, the catalysts point the wrong way: continued bitcoin sales to fund dividends, dilutive equity issuance, and potential index exclusion (MSCI's proposal to exclude digital-asset-treasury companies remains live). Analyst price targets (low $130, median $250, mean $304, high $570) are stale relics of the pre-inversion regime and reflect the flywheel-intact model that no longer holds. We do not treat them as floors; they are anchored to a mechanism that has broken.

There is a residual value case: if bitcoin recovers meaningfully and sentiment reverses, the discount to NAV could close and the flywheel could restart above mNAV 1.0. That optionality is why the valuation score is 4.5 rather than lower. But it is optionality on an external variable (bitcoin price) layered over a levered, cash-negative structure with a hard dividend obligation, not a mispricing the market is wrong about today.

## Competitive Landscape

MSTR pioneered the corporate bitcoin-treasury model, but its competitive moat has eroded on three fronts. First, spot bitcoin ETPs (approved January 2024, IBIT and peers) offer cheaper, cleaner bitcoin exposure without entity-level tax, leverage, preferred-dividend drag, or forced-seller dynamics. The "IBIT beats MSTR" argument gains force precisely when MSTR trades below NAV, because the premium that once justified the wrapper has disappeared. Second, copycat treasury companies across bitcoin and other digital assets have diluted MSTR's scarcity value. Third, bitcoin-linked equities with actual positive cash generation — Coinbase, cited for 13 straight positive-EBITDA quarters — look structurally superior to a company carrying fixed preferred dividends against negative operating cash flow.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating, not stable. The core innovation, a leveraged, publicly-traded bitcoin proxy, is being commoditized by cheaper alternatives at the exact moment its own leverage has turned into a liability. Competitive position scores 3.0.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are severe, specific, and several have already materialized, placing this well below the "already priced with recovery upside" bar.

1. **mNAV inversion (materialized).** The flywheel depends on the stock trading above bitcoin NAV so issuance is accretive. It is now below 1.0. At a discount, the model reverses: the company cannot grow bitcoin-per-share via equity and must lean on dilutive stock sales or bitcoin sales. A reflexive downward spiral is possible if sentiment does not recover.

2. **Dividend coverage / runway.** ~$8.25B convertible debt plus ~$8.47B notional preferred with recurring, partly compounding dividends against negative software cash flow. The ~10-month runway estimate is the sharpest near-term constraint.

3. **Forced bitcoin sales (now happening).** The bitcoin sales to fund preferred dividends directly contradict the stated ethos in the 10-K and introduce the two-way risk JPMorgan warned about.

4. **STRC preferred instability.** The board-set variable-rate STRC was hiked from 9.0% to 11.25% to defend $100 par, yet still traded 25-26% below par by mid-2026, evidence that the key funding channel is impaired precisely when it is most needed.

5. **Deferred tax reversal.** A $2.42B deferred tax liability on unrealized bitcoin gains reverses if bitcoin falls below cost basis (which occurred post-year-end), potentially forcing a valuation allowance against deferred tax assets and hitting net income.

6. **Index exclusion.** MSCI's October 2025 proposal to exclude digital-asset-treasury companies was deferred in January 2026 but remains a live risk that could force index-fund selling.

7. **Governance / key-man.** Saylor's 40.1% voting control limits external accountability; his departure is a named risk. A putative class action (Dodge) is pending over the STRK certificate amendment. An unspecified officer/director departure was disclosed in an 8-K on July 6, 2026 (Item 5.02), a red flag warranting monitoring.

Risk factors score 2.5, reflecting multiple binary and reflexive risks that are actively unfolding rather than dormant.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 90.4% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 73.8% (a 16.6-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 90.4% > long-term 83.9%), signaling the market expects imminent volatility. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-0.6%) at the near tenor but steepens to -4.0% at the six-month tenor, consistent with hedging against continued downside. Unusual activity shows concentrated near-dated put buying (the $96 July-24 put traded 6,015 contracts at 3.6x open interest). The implied ±6.7% move at the nearest expiry and ±39.2% at six months underscores that this remains a high-variance instrument. IV has come down from 102.8% in early June even as the stock fell 21%, suggesting some resolution of the acute panic, but the backwardation indicates the market is not treating the situation as stabilized. This confirms rather than challenges the cautious fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Bitcoin stages a sharp recovery, lifting the value of the holdings and restoring mNAV above 1.0. Sentiment reverses, the discount to NAV closes, and the flywheel restarts. Standard Chartered's framing that this is a "messaging, not solvency" problem proves correct, the company navigates the dividend obligations without a distressed capital raise, and the stock re-rates back toward NAV. Bull case ~$180-200, contingent almost entirely on a strong bitcoin rally and sentiment repair.

**Bear case.** Bitcoin stays weak or falls further. The company remains a forced seller of bitcoin to fund preferred dividends, accelerating holdings erosion. Impaired STRC access forces either deeper dividend hikes or dilutive common issuance below NAV, both value-destructive. The runway compresses; a distressed capital event or preferred restructuring becomes plausible. Index exclusion adds forced selling. The discount to NAV widens as the market prices the leverage, overhead, and forced-seller drag more harshly. Bear case ~$55-65, a 35-45% decline from current levels.

**Base case.** The stock oscillates with bitcoin in a wide band while the market continues to assign a discount to NAV, reflecting the structural liabilities and the broken accretion mechanism. Without a bitcoin rally, there is no organic catalyst to close the discount, and the dividend obligation is a persistent drain. Base case ~$85-100, roughly flat-to-modestly-lower, with high variance in both directions.

The asymmetry is unattractive: the upside requires a favorable move in an external variable (bitcoin) layered on top of a structure that now amplifies downside, while the downside includes several company-specific, actively-unfolding risks that do not require a bitcoin decline to hurt (dividend runway, funding-channel impairment, forced sales, potential index exclusion).

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest bearish catalysts are the ongoing forced bitcoin sales to fund preferred dividends, the runway compression, and any distressed capital raise or preferred dividend restructuring. Continued bitcoin weakness would accelerate all of these. However, none is a single dated event with a hard resolution date; the deterioration is gradual and reflexive rather than a binary trigger on a known calendar date. The next earnings report and quarterly dividend cycles are the natural checkpoints, but they do not force resolution.

**Timing window.** The runway problem plays out over roughly 10 months, but the actual price catalyst is bitcoin direction, which is unpredictable in timing. This is the core problem for a short: the downside thesis is credible but the timing is undated and heavily dependent on an external variable.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 12.7% of float with only 1.5 days to cover — moderately elevated but not extreme, and the low days-to-cover means limited squeeze fuel. Institutional short positioning is present. The bigger squeeze risk is bitcoin itself: this stock routinely moves ±7% in a day and ±39% implied over six months, and a sharp bitcoin rally would produce violent upside. The stock has already rebounded from $82 (June 26) to ~$101.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options confirm the bearish lean, but that also means downside protection is expensive and much of the thesis is already reflected in positioning. Rich IV raises the cost of expressing the short via puts.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$55-65 (35-45% downside) against a squeeze/bitcoin-rally upside that could easily be +40-80% in a strong bitcoin move. The reward:risk is roughly 1:1 or worse given the extreme two-way volatility and bitcoin dependence, well below the 2:1 threshold required to justify an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish thesis is correct on the fundamentals, but there is no dated catalyst that forces resolution, the timing depends entirely on an unpredictable external variable (bitcoin price), and the extreme two-way volatility makes the carry and squeeze risk unfavorable. This is a stock to avoid owning, not one to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit the thesis constructively if: (1) mNAV moves durably back above 1.0, restoring accretive issuance and repairing the core business model; (2) the STRC preferred trades back toward par, evidencing restored access to the primary funding channel and removing the forced-seller dynamic; and (3) the company demonstrates a self-funding or sustainable path for the ~$230M+/quarter preferred dividend obligation without relying on dilutive stock sales below NAV or continued bitcoin liquidation. A sustained bitcoin recovery is a necessary but not sufficient condition; the structural funding and governance issues must also resolve.

**Timeline.** The headwinds are likely to persist as long as mNAV remains below 1.0 and bitcoin trades below the levels needed to restore the premium. Given the ~10-month dividend runway and the reflexive nature of the discount, we expect the stress to remain live over the next 6-12 months absent a decisive bitcoin rally.

**Monitoring points.** Track bitcoin price relative to MSTR's implied average cost basis of roughly $64,500/BTC ($54.5B / ~843,775 BTC; note the aggregate cost and current holdings do not correspond to the same date, so treat this as approximate), the STRC discount-to-par, the pace and size of bitcoin sales in Reg-FD disclosures, cash reserve levels, and any further officer departures or capital-raise announcements. The next earnings report and quarterly dividend cycle are the natural reassessment points.

## Conclusion

Strategy is a leveraged bitcoin proxy whose defining mechanism has inverted. For years the model worked because the stock traded above the value of its bitcoin, making equity issuance accretive to bitcoin-per-share. With market cap now below bitcoin NAV, that engine runs in reverse: the company cannot grow bitcoin-per-share through equity, has begun selling bitcoin to fund the preferred dividend obligation, and faces its primary preferred funding channel trading below par. The software business burns cash and offers no independent value.

The stock is "cheap" only against a NAV framework that ignores the structural liabilities sitting ahead of common equity and the forced-seller dynamics now in play. There is no catalyst to close that discount other than a bitcoin recovery, and the downside risks — dividend runway, impaired funding access, ongoing bitcoin liquidation, potential index exclusion, governance concerns — are largely independent of any further bitcoin decline. The risk/reward is unattractive and the asymmetry favors caution. We rate MSTR **AVOID**. It is not an active short candidate given the absence of a dated catalyst and the extreme two-way volatility tied to bitcoin, but it is a structure we would not own until the mNAV inversion and funding-channel impairment resolve.
