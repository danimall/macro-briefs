**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Jones Lang LaSalle is one of four global-scale commercial real estate (CRE) services platforms, generating $26.1B of FY2025 revenue across leasing advisory, capital markets, real estate management, and investment management. The business is capital-light and fee-driven, but its headline top line materially overstates the economics: roughly two-thirds of revenue ($17.2B in REMS) is low/no-margin gross contract cost pass-through (reimbursed employee and vendor costs). The real profit engine is the ~$9B of fee revenue, heavily weighted toward the cyclical Leasing Advisory and Capital Markets segments, both of which are inflecting sharply off the 2023 rate-shock trough.

The earnings recovery is well underway and continuing. FY2025 net income rose 45% to $792M ($16.40 diluted EPS), Adjusted EBITDA rose 22% to $1,453M, and operating cash flow reached $1,194M. Q1 2026 extended the trend with revenue up 11% and EPS of $3.33 versus $1.14 a year prior, driven by Capital Markets Adjusted EBITDA up 59% and Leasing up 21%. Management guided FY2026 adjusted EPS to $21.80–$23.50 and stated it is trending toward the upper end. The company has beaten consensus four straight quarters (by 13.2% in Q1 2026 and 17.7% in Q4 2025), and consensus estimates are rising across every forward window — current-year up 3.7% and next-year up 5.3% over 90 days.

The stock trades at ~$325, roughly 14x consensus FY2026 EPS (~$23) and ~12x FY2027 (~$26.50), against a Real Estate Services peer group whose P/E multiples sit far higher. The market is pricing the cyclicality of the earnings engine and the artifact-driven optics of a 3.0% net margin, while under-rewarding the fee-revenue trajectory, share gains, and a buyback authorization that was expanded to ~$2.7B in February. This is a rate-sensitive cyclical recovery play, and the primary bear risk — a reversal in rates or credit availability that stalls transaction volumes — is the same risk the company flags first in its own 10-K. The risk/reward is skewed to the upside from here, and I rate JLL a BUY with disciplined sizing.

## Company Overview

JLL is a Fortune 500 CRE services and investment management firm with 113,000+ employees operating in 80+ countries. The modern platform was formed via the 1999 merger of the UK's Jones Lang Wootton with Chicago's LaSalle Partners. Effective January 1, 2026, the business reorganized into four reporting segments:

- **Real Estate Management Services (REMS)** — $20.0B FY2025 revenue (77% of total), spanning Workplace Management ($13.8B), Project Management ($3.8B), Property Management ($1.8B), and the folded-in Software & Technology Solutions. This is the annuity engine (~2.8B sq ft of workplace management and 2.9B sq ft of property management under contract), but $17.2B of the segment's $19.7B in expenses are pass-through gross contract costs, so net fee margins are far thinner than headline revenue implies.
- **Leasing Advisory** — $3.0B revenue, agency leasing plus tenant representation. High incremental margin, transaction-driven.
- **Capital Markets Services** — $2.4B revenue. Investment sales, debt/equity advisory, valuations, and loan servicing (a Fannie Mae DUS lender servicing ~$140B). The most cyclically sensitive segment.
- **Investment Management (LaSalle)** — $450M revenue, $86.4B AUM, advisory plus incentive fees, with $505.8M co-invested alongside clients.

The competitive moat rests on the integrated "One JLL" global platform, an investment-grade balance sheet (Baa1/BBB+) that repeatedly wins large multinational outsourcing mandates, and a leading technology/AI posture (JLL Falcon, JLL GPT, LeasingOS).

The 2023 trough (net income $225M) demonstrates how quickly the transaction businesses reverse when rates spike. That is the correct frame for the earnings power question: the current $16.40 trailing EPS is an early-recovery number rather than a mid-cycle one, and consensus expects ~$23 in 2026 and ~$26.50 in 2027 as the transaction cycle normalizes further.

FY2025 drivers were concentrated in the cyclical segments: Capital Markets Adjusted EBITDA rose 49% to $364M, Leasing rose 25% to $580M, and investment sales revenue outgrew the market (+21% vs +18%). REMS grew a steady 11% with modest EBITDA leverage. Investment Management (LaSalle) was the laggard, with revenue down 4% and EBITDA down 17% on lower incentive fees and a 3% AUM decline to $86.4B.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt is modest at ~$800M in senior/Euro notes, and the $3.3B credit facility had zero drawn at year-end 2025, with corporate liquidity of ~$3.9B. Reported D/E of 1.36 and an Altman Z of 2.76 (grey zone) are structurally depressed by the pass-through revenue model, which inflates asset turnover and compresses the headline net margin to 3.0%. These are artifacts of the accounting model, not distress signals. The average borrowing rate fell to 4.9% in 2025 from 5.9% in 2024, and 4.2% in Q1 2026.

A recurring seasonal nuance requires flagging so it is not misread: Q1 always shows a large cash use from operations (~$755M used in Q1 2026) because accrued incentive compensation is paid out early in the year, driving temporary CP and facility borrowing (Q1 2026: $615M CP + $340M facility). This is a normal working-capital pattern, not a leverage warning. Free cash flow was $978M in 2025.

**Earnings quality and momentum.** The four-quarter beat streak (2.1%, 5.2%, 17.7%, and 13.2%) raises confidence in the FY2026 guidance and current consensus. The estimate revision trend is uniformly positive: current-year EPS up 3.7% and next-year up 5.3% over 90 days, with next-year revisions accelerating (+1.4% in the last 30 days alone). This does not clear the >30% 90-day threshold that flags a violent inflection, but it is steady, broad-based positive momentum on top of a business already delivering ~30%+ EPS growth.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here has three layers. First, the cyclical recovery in the transaction segments — Capital Markets and Leasing — which is external and rate-dependent but currently running well above market volumes (Q1 2026 office leasing revenue +12% vs market volumes -1%). This is the earnings engine and it drives the FY2026 ~30%+ EPS growth.

Second, structural share gains. JLL is taking share within a consolidating industry: 2025 investment sales revenue outgrew the market, and Q1 2026 leasing revenue grew despite flat-to-declining market activity. The investment-grade balance sheet is a genuine competitive advantage in winning large corporate outsourcing mandates, where clients want financially durable long-term providers.

Third, the secular tailwinds cited in the 10-K: continued corporate real estate outsourcing (feeding REMS/Workplace Management), rising institutional allocations to real estate, sustainability/decarbonization services, and AI/data adoption. These support the more durable REMS annuity base.

The durability caveat is real. The transaction recovery is the growth, and it depends on rates and credit availability staying supportive. FY2026 guidance implies continued strong growth, but a macro reversal would hit the highest-margin segments first and fastest — the mechanism that took net income to $225M in 2023. I weight the growth thesis as real and confirmed by data (beats, rising estimates, share gains), but as macro-conditional rather than structurally guaranteed.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$325, JLL trades at ~14x consensus FY2026 EPS and ~12x FY2027. Trailing P/E of ~17x on $16.40 EPS overstates the multiple because 2025 was an early-recovery year, not mid-cycle earnings. The correct lens is forward: on rising estimates, the PEG is well below 1.0 (~14x forward against ~30% near-term EPS growth), which is inexpensive for a share-gaining market leader.

The peer comparison requires care. The reported Real Estate Services peer median P/E of 96.3 is distorted by names at trough or negative earnings — CoStar at 545x (a data business at a different point in its investment cycle), Kennedy-Wilson at 96x, Marcus & Millichap negative. The cleaner comparables are FirstService (39.9x) and Cushman & Wakefield (41.5x). On that basis JLL is priced at a substantial discount to the smaller services franchises. The analyst price target range reinforces the asymmetry: consensus low $306, mean $390.3, median $393, high $455. The current price sits essentially at the low end of the range. Under the guardrail that consensus targets act as floors on inflection stocks trading below 30x forward with rising estimates, JLL qualifies — it is at ~14x forward with a four-quarter beat streak and rising consensus, so the low target near current price is likely stale, and further upward estimate revisions should pull the target distribution higher.

I am mindful that "cheap" has been an anti-predictive signal in this environment, and that a compressed multiple can reflect structural impairment rather than mispricing. The falsifiable question is whether the discount reflects a genuine problem. My read is that the discount reflects the pass-through revenue model distorting standard screens (net margin, Z-score, revenue multiples) plus justified skepticism about the durability of a rate-driven cyclical recovery, not structural impairment. The fee-revenue base is growing, share is being gained, and the balance sheet is de-levering. That combination supports a valuation score in the mid-6s: there is a specific reason the market is under-pricing (screen distortion plus cyclicality discount), tempered by the honest acknowledgment that the earnings power itself is cycle-dependent.

## Competitive Landscape

The CRE services industry is consolidating around a handful of global platforms — JLL, CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield, and Newmark — where scale, balance-sheet strength, and technology are the moats. JLL's investment-grade rating is a repeatedly-cited advantage in winning large corporate outsourcing mandates.

The evidence points to improving position rather than a merely stable leader: JLL grew investment sales revenue faster than the market in 2025 and grew leasing revenue while market volumes fell in Q1 2026. Both are share gains during a period of industry stress, which is the higher-quality version of growth.

The competitive-position score has declined in prior reports (from 7.5 to 6.3), and I hold it near that level rather than reverting higher. The reason is the AI cost-plus margin threat, discussed in Risk Assessment: the 10-K's specific warning that AI-driven delivery efficiencies could force fee givebacks in JLL's largest REMS/Workplace contracts is a genuine, differentiated concern about the durability of the annuity moat, distinct from generic AI boilerplate. That caps competitive-position upside even as the transaction-segment share gains argue for a firm mid-6s score.

## Risk Assessment

**Transaction cyclicality (the dominant risk).** Capital Markets and Leasing are the highest-margin businesses and the entire current growth engine. They are directly geared to interest rates, credit availability, and investor confidence. The 2023 collapse to $225M net income shows how fast this reverses. A reversal in the transaction cycle is the single most material downside path and is only partly priced.

**AI cost-plus margin threat.** The 10-K specifically warns that AI-driven efficiencies could force fee givebacks in cost-plus REMS contracts, eroding the "resilient" annuity revenue the bull case relies on. It also cites legacy-data-architecture disadvantage versus new entrants and write-off risk on AI investments. This is a forward-looking, company-specific concern rather than a sector truism.

**Office-sector bifurcation.** Reduced transaction volumes and downward pressure on older/commodity office asset values threaten brokerage fees, REMS mandate scope, and LaSalle's office-heavy portfolio valuations.

**Safety-fatality disclosure.** The 10-K discloses a recent increase in serious safety incidents, including fatalities of both employees and contractors, prompting new safety controls. For a firm that markets itself on ethics and safety, this is a reputational and liability exposure worth monitoring for litigation.

**DUS loan loss-sharing.** As a Fannie Mae DUS lender, JLL retains roughly one-third of losses (capped at 20% of principal) on $26.6B of loans, an off-balance-sheet credit exposure with a $24.2M reserve and rep-and-warranty breach risk.

**Investment Management volatility and FX.** Co-investments ($505.8M) and proptech investments (~$358M) create earnings volatility via equity marks (proptech drove $38.8M of equity losses in 2025). 38% of revenue is non-USD, so reported results are sensitive to dollar strength.

Weighing these, the risk score sits at 5.6, modestly below neutral. The cyclicality risk is real but substantially known and priced; the AI-margin and safety disclosures are the genuine idiosyncratic tails the market may not fully reflect. This is consistent with the improving-risk trajectory noted in prior reports (from 4.5 to 5.5) as the balance sheet de-levered and liquidity strengthened.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV is 40.1% against 34.2% one-year historical volatility — rich, at the 75th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 40.1% > long-term 37.3%). The market is pricing imminent stress, most likely the next earnings print, with an implied move of ±8.7%. The put/call skew is roughly balanced (0.8% near-term), and the 6-month skew is only mildly negative (-3.5%), so the options market is pricing event uncertainty rather than directional bearishness. For an entry, this argues for patience: the rich near-term IV favors either scaling in or waiting for the post-earnings volatility crush rather than paying up ahead of the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($400–$455).** The transaction recovery continues, FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at the upper end of guidance, and FY2027 approaches $26.50 as capital markets normalize further. JLL keeps taking share, the ~$2.7B buyback authorization shrinks the share count meaningfully at a low-teens multiple, and consensus targets re-rate upward as beats compound. At ~15–17x $26.50 FY2027 EPS, the stock reaches $400–$450, converging on the analyst mean/high.

**Bear Case (~$240–$260).** Rates back up or credit availability tightens, stalling Capital Markets and Leasing volumes the way 2023 did. EPS growth flattens or reverses, the AI cost-plus margin threat begins to erode REMS fees, and the multiple compresses toward ~11–12x on a lower forward number. A drawdown to $240–$260 (roughly -20% to -25%) is plausible, consistent with the 52-week low of $258 and prior episodes of transaction-cycle stress.

**Base Case (~$400).** FY2026 EPS in the $23 range, FY2027 near $26.50, and a modest re-rating toward ~15x forward as the beat streak and rising estimates pull consensus targets higher. Buyback support and share gains do the rest.

The thesis rests on the transaction recovery holding and consensus catching up to a business already compounding EPS at ~30% off the trough. The asymmetry — roughly +23% to base versus -23% to bear — combined with rising estimates and a below-peer forward multiple, justifies a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Bull case target: $455** (17x FY2027 EPS; matches the analyst high). Upside of ~40%.
- **Bear case target: ~$250** (11.5x a stalled forward EPS near current trailing levels). Downside of ~23%.
- **Timeframe:** 12–18 months, tied to the FY2026 earnings cadence (next print with the ±8.7% implied move), continued capital-markets normalization, and consensus target revisions.
- **Upside/downside from current:** +23% to base, +40% to bull, -23% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Capital Markets or Leasing revenue growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters, signaling a transaction-cycle reversal rather than a temporary pause.
2. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cut below the $21.80 floor, or estimate revisions turn negative across the 30- and 90-day windows.
3. Evidence that AI-driven fee givebacks are materially compressing REMS margins, or a material safety-incident-related legal/reputational development.

## Conclusion

JLL is a top-tier global CRE services platform trading at ~14x forward earnings against a business compounding EPS at ~30% off the 2023 trough, with four consecutive beats, uniformly rising estimates, demonstrated share gains, and an accelerating buyback funded by recovering free cash flow. The market is anchoring on the low-margin pass-through revenue model that distorts standard screens and on the well-known cyclicality of the transaction segments, while under-rewarding the fee-revenue trajectory and the balance-sheet-backed competitive advantage. The primary risk — a rate/macro reversal that stalls Capital Markets and Leasing — is the same risk the company flags first, and it is only partly priced, which is why I size this with discipline rather than maximum conviction.

The recommendation history has oscillated between HOLD and BUY across recent reports; the current data (continued beats, rising estimates, price still well below the 52-week high and near the analyst low target) tilts the risk/reward back to the upside. The stock has declined modestly from prior highs on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, which improves the entry rather than degrading the thesis. **Recommendation: BUY**, with a preference for scaling in given rich near-term implied volatility, and a 12–18 month base-case target of ~$400.
