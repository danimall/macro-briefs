## Executive Summary

N-able sells a cloud platform — remote monitoring and management, backup, endpoint security, and MDR/XDR — to managed service providers who resell IT management to small and mid-market end-customers. On August 10, 2026 the company filed three consequential documents in one day: a Q2 2026 10-Q, an amended FY2025 10-K disclosing a material weakness in revenue-recognition controls, and an 8-K carrying both Q2 results and exit/disposal costs. FY2026 revenue guidance was cut from $554–559M to $539–542M, roughly 2.9% at the midpoint. The stock fell from $4.99 on August 7 to $3.21 on August 10, a 35.7% single-session decline, and now sits 64% below its 52-week high of $8.735.

At $3.145 the equity is $592.5M. Adding $398M of term debt and subtracting $115.8M of cash gives an enterprise value of $874.7M — 1.61x the $544.5M of ARR reported at June 30, and 5.40x the roughly $162M of FY2026 adjusted EBITDA implied by management's ~30% margin target. Including the $64.0M of Adlumin deferred consideration as a debt-like claim, those figures are 1.72x and 5.79x. Price-to-book is 0.74x. Those are low multiples for a subscription business with 106% trailing-twelve-month net revenue retention, no maintenance leverage covenant, and no debt maturity until November 2032. There is also a dated, contractual cash inflection: the November 20, 2026 payment is the last Adlumin instalment, after which roughly $70–78M of annual cash currently claimed by the acquisition schedule reverts to equity, worth about 12–13% of the current market capitalization.

The problem is that the low multiple is attached to a business whose forward numbers are moving down and whose near-term event calendar is negatively skewed. Guidance implies sequential revenue declines in both Q3 and Q4 (implied Q4 midpoint ~$134.9M versus $138.2M actual in Q2). Consensus EPS has been cut 7.0–11.6% across every horizon over 90 days, with the current quarter down 12.2% in 30 days alone. Management attributed the cut to a go-to-market leadership transition and to "shifting dynamics in the UEM and EDR markets" — UEM being the core franchise, where the company's own 10-K names NinjaOne, Kaseya and ConnectWise and concedes that "our customers have limited barriers to switching." Between now and February 2027 the identifiable catalysts are a possible goodwill impairment (the 10-Q discloses that a 17% decline from the June 30 stock price would have taken the single reporting unit's fair value to carrying value, and the stock is at or through that level), completion of an unfinished control remediation, the $64M November cash outflow, and the beginning of a $56M three-year minimum-spend commitment to an unnamed software vendor signed on July 31 — ten days before guidance came down. Probability-weighting a $2.20 bear, $3.60 base and $5.15 bull at 40/40/20 produces roughly $3.35, about 6.5% above the current price. That is inadequate compensation for a name with 159% at-the-money implied volatility and an unremediated weakness in the accounting for its principal revenue line.

## Company Overview

N-able was carved out of SolarWinds and remains a controlled company: Silver Lake and Thoma Bravo funds and co-investors held 111,564,512 shares, approximately 59.8% of voting power, at December 31, 2025. The board is classified with three-year staggered terms and two of eight directors are Silver Lake-affiliated. The charter contains a DGCL §203 analogue that expressly exempts the sponsor funds and their transferees from "interested stockholder" status, so the three-year business-combination moratorium does not constrain them.

The customer is the service provider, not the end business. Revenue therefore depends on a two-step chain: partners must retain and grow their own end-customer bases and must add devices and services on N-able's platform. ARR was $544.5M at June 30, 2026, up 6.0% year over year, with 2,706 customers above $50,000 of ARR (up 6.5%) accounting for 63% of total ARR. International revenue was 50.4% of FY2025 revenue and roughly 79% of employees sit outside the United States, including R&D and delivery in Poland, Romania, India, the Philippines, Portugal and Belarus.

The MDR/XDR capability came from the November 2024 acquisition of Adlumin. Management now describes N-able as "a global cybersecurity company delivering business resilience." Ten issued patents as of December 31, 2025 and the filing's own acknowledgement that the company "resell[s] third-party software and integrate[s] third-party software into our solutions that may be difficult to replace" define the limits of the intellectual-property moat.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 growth was purchased at a negative incremental cash margin.** On the revised 10-K/A basis, revenue rose from $465.6M to $508.8M (+$43.2M, +9.3%) while adjusted EBITDA fell from $169.3M to $151.0M (−$18.3M). Incremental adjusted EBITDA margin was therefore approximately −42%, and the margin itself fell 670 basis points to 29.7%. Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock compensation, acquired-intangible amortization, transaction costs and restructuring, so this cannot be attributed to Adlumin purchase accounting. The MD&A locates the build: cost of revenue rose 45.1% against 9.3% revenue growth, split roughly evenly between ~$13.4M of non-cash acquired-technology amortization and ~$14.4M of cash cloud-hosting and royalty costs; sales and marketing rose 20.3% (+$27.6M, of which ~$19.7M personnel); G&A rose 20.3% (+$15.5M) including ~$10.2M of transaction costs and a ~$3.0M increase in bad-debt expense.

The mechanism is straightforward. MDR/XDR carries security-operations-center labor and hyperscaler compute in cost of revenue, so scaling it structurally lowers the gross-margin ceiling relative to RMM. Layer on a ~$20M sales-personnel build and the negative incremental margin follows arithmetically. The alternative reading — that FY2025 was investment ahead of revenue that levers in 2026–27 — is not yet supported: Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin was 28.9% against 31.4% a year earlier, and H1 2026 was 27.8% against 29.2%.

**Q2 2026 itself was not a miss.** Revenue of $138.223M modestly exceeded the $137.954M consensus and non-GAAP EPS of $0.10 met the non-GAAP consensus. GAAP operating income was $16.5M (11.9%); non-GAAP operating income $33.2M (24.0%, down from 25.4%); adjusted EBITDA $39.9M (28.9%). GAAP net income was $1.8M on an effective tax rate of 77.2% in the quarter and 99.3% for the half. The price reaction was driven by the guidance revision, not the printed quarter.

**The guidance arithmetic is the most consequential number in the file.** Half-one actual revenue was $270.6M. Q3 is guided to $134.5–135.5M. Against the FY range of $539–542M, implied Q4 is $132.9–136.9M, midpoint ~$134.9M, versus $138.2M actual in Q2. Two consecutive sequentially declining quarters in a subscription business is a serious signal. The 10-K/A supplies a partially exculpatory mechanism: since 2024 N-able has converted month-to-month subscriptions to long-term committed contracts, which pulls forward point-in-time subscription revenue on the term-license component, so a conversion-heavy quarter is flattered and the following quarters are not. FX on 50.4% non-U.S. revenue is a second non-fundamental driver. Both caveats mean reported revenue is a noisier signal than usual in FY2026 and ARR is the metric to anchor on — but ARR growth of 6.0% corroborates the same zone as the 6.2% implied full-year revenue growth ($540.5M ÷ $508.8M).

**The margin target is heavily back-half loaded.** A ~30% FY2026 adjusted EBITDA margin on $540.5M is roughly $162M. Less H1 actual of $75.3M and the Q3 midpoint of $41.5M leaves approximately $45.2M in Q4, a 33.5% margin, against 27.8% in H1 — a step-up of roughly 570 basis points half-over-half. The July 20 workforce reduction of approximately 6%, with expected annual cash compensation savings of roughly $11–13M against one-time charges of roughly $4–6M, is the stated mechanism. Whether the savings land inside FY2026 at the required run-rate is the first thing to stress-test at the Q3 print.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Term loan $398M at a 6.42% effective rate maturing November 2032; $60M undrawn revolver; a new $75M delayed-draw facility whose availability expires December 16, 2026; cash $115.8M. Net debt of $282.2M against ~$162M of FY2026E adjusted EBITDA is 1.74x, or 2.14x including the $64.0M of deferred consideration as a debt-like claim. Cash interest of roughly $25.6M implies 6.3x interest coverage. The only financial covenant is a springing 7.50x first-lien net leverage test that is not live unless revolver borrowings exceed 40% of commitments. Solvency is not the issue here, and the Altman Z-Score of ~2.1 should be read as "not a fortress" rather than as distress — the model penalizes asset-light software and its EBIT/assets term is depressed by a GAAP operating income line that was roughly half non-cash amortization and transaction costs.

**Headline free cash flow overstates cash available to equity, for now.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $93.2M and capex plus capitalized software ~$18.1M, giving ~$75.1M of free cash flow (14.8% of revenue, a 12.7% yield on today's market capitalization). But FY2025 also carried approximately $50.9M of cash outflows for Adlumin deferred and contingent consideration. Net of that contractual, non-discretionary claim, FY2025 cash generation available to equity was closer to $24M, about 4.1% of the current market capitalization. Deferred consideration of $64.0M (fair value at June 30, net of $51.7M already paid) is classified as current with the second-anniversary payment due November 20, 2026 — and that is the last instalment. Holding operations flat and deducting restructuring cash costs, FY2027 free cash flow to equity should step up to roughly $70–78M, or 12–13% of the current market capitalization. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast: it assumes no replacement M&A (the company completed a July 2026 tuck-in with up to $6M+ of contingent payments over 36 months and retains an active acquisition strategy) and no operating deterioration.

**GAAP earnings are not usable.** Effective tax rates of 77.2% and 99.3% reflect a footprint in which profitable foreign jurisdictions are taxed while U.S. losses appear largely unbenefited. The trailing P/E of −89x in the data feed is noise. Value this on EV/ARR, EV/adjusted EBITDA and cash flow after acquisition consideration. Note also that the high cash tax rate is a permanent drag on FCF conversion that does not disappear as amortization rolls off, and that R&D tax incentives in Belarus (exemption through 2049), the U.K. and Romania are cited as material to the rate and could be withdrawn.

## Growth Analysis

The deceleration is monotonic: revenue +10.3% (FY2024), +9.3% (FY2025), ~+6.2% (FY2026 guided). ARR growth of 6.0% at Q2 confirms the zone rather than contradicting it. Subscription net revenue retention fell from 110% in FY2023 to 103% in both FY2024 and FY2025 — the 10-K/A concedes "our price increase in 2023 may have adversely affected our business" — and has since recovered to 106% on a trailing-twelve-month basis from 102% a year earlier.

That combination points to a specific diagnosis: the installed base is behaving better than a year ago while the outlook is being cut, which locates the weakness in new-partner acquisition rather than churn. That is consistent with a chief revenue officer changing on July 13, four weeks before the cut, and with management citing "the transition of go-to-market leadership" as a cause. New-logo weakness caused by sales-leadership disruption is more fixable than churn. Two caveats: TTM NRR lags by construction and can be flattered by the contract-conversion program, which raises committed spend per customer at signature; and management's second stated cause — UEM and EDR market dynamics — is not an execution explanation.

Estimate revisions are uniformly negative and are the cleanest forward read available. Current quarter consensus fell 12.2% in 30 days and 11.6% in 90; next quarter −10.9%/−10.1%; current year −7.2%/−7.0% to $0.39; next year −7.1% to $0.45. Five analysts cover next year. The analyst distribution also moved: one strong buy where there were two, three holds where there were two, and one strong sell retained throughout. Sentiment is degrading.

The demand backdrop management describes — CVE volumes more than tripling between Q2 2022 and Q2 2026, and a claimed base of roughly 500,000 protected end businesses reached through the partner channel — is real and supports a long-run patch-to-restore narrative. It has not yet converted into revenue growth above the mid-single digits, and the July 29 acquisition of a book of customer contracts for $3.0M cash plus contingent payments tied to converted ARR is a sensible but implicit acknowledgement that organic new-logo growth is currently hard.

| EV incl. deferred / FY2026E adj. EBITDA | 5.79x |

Peer context is of limited help because the comparables are heterogeneous and mostly higher-growth: median systems-software P/E of 31.9 across PRGS (20.7x), ATEN (46.3x), CVLT (87.2x), QLYS (31.9x) and PATH (24.9x). N-able has no usable P/E. The closest structural analogue is Progress Software — a levered, low-growth, high-margin software consolidator — which trades at 20.7x earnings; a direct EV/EBITDA comparison to N-able's 5.4x is not available in the provided data.

The valuation question is whether 5.4x is a discount or a correct rating. Three approaches:

*EV/EBITDA.* At 7.0x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $162M, EV is $1,134M; less $398M debt, plus $115.8M cash, less $64M deferred consideration gives $787.8M of equity, or $4.18 per share. At 6.5x, $3.75. At 6.0x, $3.32.

*Normalized FCF yield.* Post-November-2026 free cash flow to equity of ~$70M (after restructuring cash costs, assuming adjusted EBITDA holds near $160M) capitalized at 11% gives $636M of equity, $3.38 per share; at 10%, $3.72; at 13%, $2.86.

*EV/ARR.* Assuming ARR reaches ~$560M by mid-2027 and post-payment net debt of roughly $311M, 1.8x ARR gives $1,008M EV and $697M of equity, or $3.70.

The convergence is around $3.40–4.20, and I set the base case at $3.60, approximately 14% above the current price. Median sell-side target is $4.00 and RBC cut to $4.00 on August 11; the $10.00 high in the range is stale relative to the guidance cut and the $5.58 mean will likely converge toward the median as forecasts are reset.

The point that keeps this from being a compelling valuation call is that 5.4x EBITDA is only cheap if the EBITDA base is stable. Estimates are falling, revenue is guided down sequentially for two quarters, the $56M vendor minimum converts variable royalty cost into a largely fixed obligation just as volume visibility worsened, and the FY2026 margin target requires a 570-basis-point half-over-half step-up that has not been demonstrated. A cheap multiple on a base that is being revised down is not margin of safety. I score valuation 5.2 — genuinely low, with a dated and contractual catalyst in the November payment schedule, but with the asymmetry from here only mildly positive.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K/A names competitors unusually explicitly. Platform consolidators: Kaseya, ConnectWise, NinjaOne. Point-solution competitors: Acronis, Veeam, Sophos, TeamViewer, LogMeIn. The filing describes competitors "selling at zero or negative margins, offering concessions, solutions bundling or closed technology platforms," which reads as live pricing pressure rather than boilerplate, and concedes that "our customers have limited barriers to switching to a competitor's solution from our platform."

Two fronts matter. In UEM/RMM — the core franchise and the source of most ARR — the pressure is from a faster-innovating challenger set. Management's explicit reference to "shifting dynamics in the UEM market" as a cause of the guidance cut is a company-sourced statement about its own core product line, which is materially more informative than any external commentary. In EDR, N-able resells third-party engines, so the economics are a margin spread rather than a proprietary franchise, and endpoint security being bundled into broader platform licenses compresses that spread. I could not obtain independent third-party share data on NinjaOne, Kaseya or ConnectWise, so I cannot quantify share movement or distinguish share loss from execution disruption; that is a genuine evidence gap on a thesis-critical question, and it cuts against high confidence in either direction.

Adlumin is the strategic response — moving from reselling other people's security to owning an MDR/XDR outcome attachable across the MSP base. Strategically coherent; financially it is the direct cause of the FY2025 margin compression because MDR is delivered with SOC labor and compute rather than replicated software. The investment question reduces to whether N-able can attach Adlumin at sufficient volume to earn back the ~670 basis points of adjusted EBITDA margin it spent acquiring the capability. ARR growth of 6.0% and guided revenue growth of 6.2% do not yet demonstrate that it can.

One further structural note: the growing share of ARR from customers above $50,000 (63%, up from a smaller base) is increasing direct customer concentration, not diversifying it. Larger MSP partners carry more negotiating leverage, longer sales cycles and more procurement scrutiny, which sits awkwardly with a low-touch, high-velocity model. And the company absorbs the SMB cycle twice — through its partners' end-customer churn and through its partners' own solvency. The $3.0M FY2025 increase in bad-debt expense is a small number that reads directly on MSP credit quality.

Competitive trajectory is deteriorating on the company's own account. The prior report's upgrade of this dimension from 4.5 to 5.5 is reversed by the August 10 disclosure; I score it 4.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness in revenue-recognition controls, unremediated.** The 10-K/A states that controls were "not effective" at December 31, 2025 because the company "did not design and maintain effective controls over certain subscription arrangements for which revenue is recognized through manual processes outside our ERP system's automated revenue recognition module," and that the resulting errors were corrected by revision. Quantitatively the revision was small: FY2025 revenue down ~$2.6M (0.5%) to $508.8M, operating income down ~$2.9M to $33.9M, net loss widened ~$1.8M to $(18.8)M. No Item 4.02 non-reliance filing appeared in the 8-K record, consistent with revision rather than restatement treatment, and I draw no inference about motive from that determination. The forward-looking concern is qualitative and specific: the manual process sits precisely on the accounting judgment most affected by the company's strategic conversion of month-to-month subscriptions into long-term contracts with a point-in-time license component. Remediation is stated as in progress and incomplete.

**Goodwill impairment is live on the company's own sensitivity.** The 10-Q discloses that the interim June 30 test passed and that a decline of approximately 17% in the June 30 stock price would have reduced estimated fair value of the single reporting unit to its carrying value. Goodwill of ~$1,012M is 126% of $804.6M of total stockholders' equity. Anchoring fair value on market capitalization plus a control premium, and assuming carrying value approximates total equity, fair value at June 30 was roughly $804.6M ÷ 0.83 = $969M; at today's $592.5M market capitalization, fair value equals carrying value only if the applied premium is at least 36% ($804.6M ÷ $592.5M − 1). The current price sits at or through the disclosed trigger. Caveats matter: the actual June 30 closing price is not established here, carrying value shifts with Q3 earnings and currency translation, GAAP requires a sustained decline, and if fair value is a blend of income and market approaches the stock-price elasticity is lower than a market-cap-only model implies. Practically, an impairment would be non-cash, would not breach any covenant (the springing test is EBITDA-based and not live), and would not change intrinsic value. Its significance is as an audited confirmation that the forward view has deteriorated and as the removal of the 0.74x-book support.

**New fixed vendor commitment.** On July 31, 2026 N-able amended a software licensing agreement with an unnamed technology vendor, committing to a minimum spend of approximately $56M over three years (~$18.7M per year), with the arrangement also contemplating up to ~$5M of go-to-market investment funds whose direction the filing leaves ambiguous. This converts variable royalty cost into a largely fixed obligation ten days before guidance was cut. I cannot identify the counterparty and will not guess; my hypothesis that it relates to a security engine is inference, not fact. Ten days is not evidence of foreknowledge — vendor negotiations run on their own timelines — but the shortfall risk against declining volume is real.

**November 20, 2026 liquidity check.** Against the ~$64M accreting payment, N-able holds $115.8M of cash, generated ~$44M of operating cash flow in H1, and has $60M of revolver plus a $75M delayed-draw facility whose availability expires December 16, 2026 — weeks after the payment date. Coverage looks comfortable; the facility timing reads as ordinary treasury work ahead of a known obligation rather than distress.

**Governance and control.** Sponsors hold ~59% of votes and are expressly exempted from the charter's interested-stockholder provision, alongside a classified board, blank-check preferred authority and available controlled-company exemptions. The Delaware Court of Chancery has invalidated certain provisions of the sponsors' stockholders' agreement, the practical effect of which I have not verified. This cuts both ways: it makes a sponsor-led take-private or control-block sale structurally straightforward at a depressed price, and it makes open-market block selling equally straightforward. I found no evidence of any current process, approach or rumor.

**Other.** No FX hedging against 50.4% non-U.S. revenue; R&D facilities in Belarus and contractors in Ukraine; only ten issued patents; senior-management churn evidenced by Item 5.02 filings on 2025-03-28, 2025-12-17, 2026-05-11 and 2026-07-13 plus an 8-K/A on 2026-07-24.

### Options Market Signal

At-the-money implied volatility is 159.3%, against a realized 35.7% single-day move on August 10. The prior snapshot reading of 2,545.8% is implausible and I treat it as unreliable, so the reported delta carries no information; the level is what matters. Implied volatility near 160% after the news has already printed indicates the market is pricing continued large moves rather than a resolved situation — consistent with three unresolved items (ARR trajectory at the Q3 print, the impairment test, and control remediation). For a prospective buyer, near-term optionality is expensive; for the fundamental thesis, rich implied vol argues for waiting on evidence rather than paying for exposure to a wide distribution. Short interest of 5.7% of float with 1.8 days to cover is uncrowded, which means the decline was driven by long liquidation rather than short pressure and that no squeeze mechanic supports the downside case or cushions it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $5.15 (+64%)

The weakness is a go-to-market execution problem, not share loss. TTM net revenue retention improved to 106% from 102% and customers above $50,000 of ARR grew 6.5%, so the installed base is intact; the shortfall is in new-partner acquisition, which is exactly what a chief revenue officer transition disturbs and exactly what the new CRO's upmarket mandate is designed to fix. The 6% workforce reduction delivers $11–13M of annualized savings, restoring adjusted EBITDA margin toward 31–32% in 2027. The November 20 Adlumin payment is the last one, so FY2027 free cash flow to equity steps up to roughly $70–78M with no operating improvement required — 12–13% of today's market capitalization. On stabilized ARR of ~$560M and adjusted EBITDA near $160M, an 8x EV/EBITDA multiple against post-payment net debt of ~$311M gives $969M of equity, or $5.14 per share. Separately, sponsors holding 59% with no statutory moratorium, a 2032 maturity, moderate leverage and a 5.4x EBITDA multiple make a control transaction structurally straightforward; I assign no probability to it because there is no evidence of a process, but it is a genuine asymmetry.

### Bear Case — $2.20 (−30%)

Management's reference to UEM and EDR dynamics is share loss and spread compression, not execution. UEM is the core franchise; NinjaOne, Kaseya and ConnectWise are bundling and discounting; EDR is resold, so the margin spread is being competed away by platform licensing. ARR growth decelerates from 6.0% toward 3% or lower as new-logo weakness compounds, and the guided sequential revenue declines in Q3 and Q4 turn out to reflect that rather than conversion-timing noise. The $56M vendor minimum becomes a fixed cost against falling volume. Adjusted EBITDA settles near $150M rather than $162M, the FY2026 back-half margin step-up under-delivers, and a goodwill impairment removes the book-value support and confirms the derating. At 4.5–5.0x EV/adjusted EBITDA of $150M against post-payment net debt of ~$311M, equity is $364–439M, or $1.93–2.33 per share. The 52-week low of $2.92, set on June 18, 2026, is not the floor if ARR growth halves. The material weakness, if remediation slips or a second issue surfaces in the same manual process, adds a discount that does not reverse quickly.

**Weighting.** Bear 40%, base 40%, bull 20% gives an expected value of approximately $3.35, or +6.5% from $3.145. Against 159% implied volatility, an unremediated control weakness in the revenue line, and a near-term catalyst calendar in which every identifiable event between now and February 2027 is negative or neutral, that expected return is below fair compensation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** This is a downgrade from two prior HOLDs (5.1 in May, 5.0 in June). The trajectory instruction to hold a stale call to a higher bar is satisfied here by new, filing-grade information: a 2.9% FY2026 revenue guidance cut, guided sequential revenue declines, a material weakness in revenue-recognition controls disclosed six months after the original 10-K, a goodwill-impairment trigger the stock now sits at or through, and a new $56M fixed vendor commitment. Note the price context carefully: at $3.145 the stock is essentially where it was at the June 19 assessment ($3.06), and enterprise value is roughly unchanged, so the multiple has not compressed while forward estimates have been cut 7–12% and a control weakness has surfaced. The 36% single-day decline is not a case of price falling on unchanged fundamentals; the fundamentals moved.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength rather than into the current gap. Existing holders should reduce position size on any recovery toward $3.60–4.00 (the base case and the median sell-side target), and should not add ahead of the Q3 print. A residual position sized to tolerate a further 30% decline is defensible for holders who want exposure to the FY2027 free-cash-flow inflection; new capital has no reason to commit before the ARR trajectory is confirmed.

**Base case: $3.60 (+14%).** 6.5x EV/FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $162M = $1,053M EV, less $398M debt, plus $115.8M cash, less $64M deferred consideration = $707M equity ÷ 188.4M shares = $3.75; cross-checked against 1.8x forward ARR of ~$560M and an 11% capitalization of ~$70M normalized FCF, which give $3.70 and $3.38. Base set at $3.60.

**Bull case: $5.15 (+64%).** 8x EV/adjusted EBITDA of $160M against post-payment net debt of ~$311M.

**Bear case: $2.20 (−30%).** 4.5–5.0x EV/adjusted EBITDA of $150M against post-payment net debt of ~$311M.

**Timeframe.** Six to nine months, anchored on the Q3 2026 report (early November 2026, which will also carry the impairment determination and the control-remediation update), the November 20, 2026 deferred-consideration payment, and FY2027 guidance in February 2027.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD.** (1) Q3 ARR at or above $550M with year-over-year growth of 6% or better, confirming that the reported-revenue decline is conversion-timing rather than ARR deceleration. (2) Q3 adjusted EBITDA at or above the guided $41–42M with the FY ~30% margin target reaffirmed on current currency, evidencing that the restructuring savings are landing on schedule. (3) Completion of material-weakness remediation with a clean control assertion, or a price near $2.50 that widens the margin of safety on an unchanged operating base.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY.** Two consecutive quarters of ARR growth accelerating above 7% with net revenue retention holding at or above 106%, plus H2 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin above 31%. That combination would validate the execution-disruption reading over the share-loss reading and make 5.4x EV/EBITDA plainly wrong.

## Conclusion

N-able is cheap on every cash-based metric that matters — 1.61x EV/ARR, 5.40x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA, 0.74x book, and a normalized post-2026 free-cash-flow yield of 12–13% once the final Adlumin payment clears on November 20. Solvency is not in question: leverage of 1.74x net, no maintenance covenant in force, no maturity until 2032, $115.8M of cash and $135M of undrawn committed capacity. Retention is improving, with trailing net revenue retention at 106% against 102% a year earlier.

Set against that, growth has decelerated from 10.3% to 9.3% to a guided 6.2%; guidance implies sequential revenue declines in both Q3 and Q4; consensus EPS has been cut 7–12% across every horizon; management has named competitive dynamics in its own core UEM franchise and in resold EDR as causes of the cut; a material weakness sits unremediated on the exact revenue line most affected by the company's contract-conversion strategy; a goodwill impairment is live on the company's own disclosed sensitivity; and a $56M three-year fixed vendor commitment begins amortizing against declining volume visibility. Management quality reads poorly on the specifics that matter forward: CEO total compensation rose ~21% to $7,748,106 in a year when adjusted EBITDA fell in absolute dollars, GAAP net income turned negative, and the TSR index fell to 60 against a peer index of 170; there is no open-market insider buying at multi-year lows, though extended blackouts around the revision process make that weak evidence.

Probability-weighted expected value is roughly $3.35 against a $3.145 price — about 6.5% — with a −30% left tail, 159% implied volatility, and a catalyst calendar in which nothing constructive arrives before FY2027 guidance in February. The multiple is low enough that this is not a short and not an AVOID; the business generates cash, the balance sheet is not stressed, and the 2027 free-cash-flow inflection is contractual rather than hoped for. But at an unchanged enterprise value on estimates that are 7–12% lower than at the last review, with a control weakness added, the correct action is to trim and wait for the Q3 ARR print. **REDUCE.**
