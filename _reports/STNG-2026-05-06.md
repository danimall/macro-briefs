## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is the world's largest publicly listed pure-play product tanker company, operating 87 vessels with an additional 10 newbuildings under construction. The company has engineered a balance sheet transformation from $2.9B net debt in 2021 to a net cash position of approximately $385-513M as of March 2026, an achievement without precedent among tanker companies of this scale. This financial position, combined with an industry-lowest $11,000/day cash breakeven, creates asymmetric exposure to the current rate environment where LR2 pool/spot rates have been reported at approximately $101,000/day and MR pool/spot rates at approximately $36,500/day in early Q2 2026 bookings.

The investment thesis rests on three legs: (1) near-term earnings power at current spot rates implies the stock trades at an attractive multiple of forward earnings; (2) structural supply constraints (approximately 21% of the global fleet >20 years old, minimal effective net fleet growth, sanctioned tonnage) support multi-year rate elevation independent of any single geopolitical catalyst; and (3) the balance sheet provides extraordinary optionality — management can simultaneously fund newbuilding commitments, execute share buybacks, and maintain liquidity to acquire distressed assets in a downturn.

The primary risk is rate mean-reversion if the Iran/Strait of Hormuz conflict resolves and ton-mile demand normalizes. However, even at rates 50% below current levels, the company generates substantial free cash flow above its breakeven. The stock has appreciated 113% over the trailing year, but this reflects a fundamental repricing that is still incomplete relative to forward earnings power.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers was founded in 2009 by Emanuele Lauro and operates primarily in the marine transportation of refined petroleum products — gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, and naphtha. The fleet of 87 owned vessels (32 LR2, 41 MR, 14 Handymax) is commercially managed through three pooling arrangements operated by Scorpio Commercial Management (SCM), a related party. The company has also reached agreements to sell six MR and three LR2 vessels, expected to close in Q2 2026, which would further reduce the operating fleet.

The fleet has an average age of 10.2 years, placing it well below the global fleet average in a market where aging is accelerating. Management is actively reshaping the fleet composition: selling older 2014-2015 vintage tonnage at prices exceeding original purchase prices while ordering modern, scrubber-fitted newbuildings with 2027-2029 delivery dates.

The company is incorporated in the Marshall Islands and headquartered in Monaco, operating under a foreign private issuer framework that provides operational flexibility but limits certain shareholder transparency protections available to U.S.-incorporated companies.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings**

For the full year 2025, STNG reported vessel revenue of $938.2M, net income of $344.3M, and diluted EPS of $7.03. Q4 2025 delivered vessel revenue of $252.7M, net income of $128.1M, and diluted EPS of $2.59.

As of the March 25, 2026 operational update, the company disclosed preliminary Q1 2026 TCE rates by vessel class: LR2 pool/spot rates of approximately $51,000/day, MR pool/spot rates of approximately $32,000/day, and Handymax pool/spot rates of approximately $34,000/day. These rates were explicitly noted as subject to change as pool results and voyages were finalized. Time-charter-out rates were lower across all classes. Full Q1 2026 financial results have not yet been filed as of the date of this report.

For Q2 2026, the company disclosed early bookings as of March 25: LR2 pool/spot rates of $101,000/day (16% of days covered), MR pool/spot rates of $36,500/day (16% of days covered), and Handymax pool/spot rates of $32,000/day (10% of days covered). Time-charter-out rates were significantly lower at $30,200/day for LR2s, $26,800/day for MRs, and $23,000/day for Handymax. The majority of Q2 days remained unbooked at the time of this disclosure, meaning the fleetwide blended rate will depend heavily on how the remainder of the quarter is booked.

**Balance Sheet Strength**

The balance sheet is among the strongest in the tanker sector:
- Cash and equivalents: $974.0M (March 20, 2026)
- Total debt: $589.1M (March 20, 2026)
- Net cash: $384.9M actual; $512.8M pro forma after then-announced vessel sales (as of March 20, 2026)
- Pro forma liquidity (including $747.5M revolver availability): approximately $1.85B as of March 20, 2026

Following the April 2026 issuance of $375M in convertible notes (adding approximately $363.3M in net proceeds but also $375M in gross debt) and the concurrent $100M share repurchase, the net cash and liquidity figures require further reconciliation. Additional vessel-sale proceeds will increase cash upon closing. These transactions move in offsetting directions — the convertible adds both cash and debt simultaneously.

The $375M convertible notes issued in April 2026 at 1.75% coupon are low-cost capital — the coupon is below current cash deposit yields — though the notes create senior unsecured debt obligations due April 15, 2031, potential dilution above the $100.39 conversion price, and refinancing complexity. This is attractively priced financing, not free capital.

**Cash Flow Generation**

Management's sensitivity table illustrates the operating leverage from the $11,000/day breakeven:
- At $20,000/day: ~$260M annual cash flow
- At $30,000/day: ~$548M annual cash flow
- At $40,000/day: ~$836M annual cash flow
- At $50,000/day: ~$1.1B annual cash flow

Based on the preliminary Q1 2026 disclosed rates (which averaged in the $30,000-$50,000/day range across vessel classes and employment types), annual cash flow generation is substantial. At elevated Q2 LR2 spot rates, near-term cash generation could be extraordinary — though coverage of Q2 days remained limited as of March 25 and fleetwide earnings will depend on the vessel-class mix and the unbooked remainder of the quarter.

**Capital Returns**

The dividend has been progressively raised from $0.40 to $0.45/quarter ($1.80 annualized, 2.2% yield). Management repurchased 1.35M shares at $74.36/share (~$100M) in April 2026, concurrent with the convertible offering. As of February 10, 2026, $173.4M remained available under the company's 2023 Securities Repurchase Program.

## Growth Analysis

**Earnings Momentum**

Estimate revisions have been strong, reflecting the elevated rate environment in early 2026. The company has a consistent earnings beat record. The critical consideration is whether current consensus adequately captures the Q2 2026 rate spike — given limited Q2 coverage as of March 25, there is upside risk to near-term estimates if LR2 spot rates remain elevated for a significant portion of the quarter.

**Fleet Growth and Renewal**

The 10-vessel newbuilding program (4 MR, 4 LR2, 2 VLCC) adds capacity through 2029 while older vessels are being sold. The net effect is a younger, more efficient fleet. Multi-year time charters at $29,000-$33,000/day for select LR2 vessels lock in base earnings that cover the $11,000/day breakeven by nearly 3x. These charters cover a subset of the fleet, not the entire operating base.

**Industry Supply Dynamics**

According to industry data cited by management, the product tanker order book stands at approximately 18% of the existing fleet, but effective net growth is estimated at only ~3%/year because:
- Approximately 21% of the global fleet exceeds 20 years of age (rising to ~30% by 2028)
- A significant portion of tonnage is sanctioned and aging
- A substantial share of the LR2 fleet trades crude (crossover capacity)
- New ordering activity has been limited

Fleet replacement appears to be structurally lagging scrapping and retirement, creating a tightening supply curve that management believes supports rates independent of demand shocks. These figures are based on industry estimates and should be viewed as directional rather than precise.

The convertible notes create potential dilution of approximately 3.74M shares if the stock exceeds $100.39. The concurrent buyback of 1.35M shares offsets roughly 36% of this potential dilution, not all of it. Diluted EPS calculations should incorporate this possibility above the conversion price.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG is one of the largest listed pure-play product tanker owners/operators, with scale benefits through pool participation. Key competitive advantages include:

1. **Scale**: Largest pure-play listed product tanker company — pool operations create commercial optimization advantages (cargo matching, repositioning efficiency, customer relationships) that smaller operators find difficult to replicate.

2. **Balance sheet**: The net cash position is unmatched among peers. Hafnia, Torm, and Ardmore all carry meaningful leverage. This creates optionality during downturns (acquisitions at distressed prices) and stability during cycles.

3. **Breakeven advantage**: At $11,000/day, STNG remains profitable in rate environments that force leveraged peers into cash preservation or asset sales. This is the lowest breakeven in company history and management characterizes it as among the lowest in the sector.

4. **Fleet quality**: Modern, scrubber-fitted fleet with active renewal program positions STNG for increasingly stringent emissions regulations (CII ratings, EU ETS).

5. **Commercial management**: SCM-managed pools provide commercial optimization, though the related-party structure introduces governance concerns. Independent benchmarking of outperformance versus Baltic indices is not publicly available.

Competitive position is improving: management is selling at peak values, ordering at competitive newbuilding prices, locking in multi-year charters at attractive rates, and accumulating the financial resources to be a consolidator if the cycle turns.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate Reversion (High Impact, Moderate Probability)**

The Iran/Strait of Hormuz conflict is the primary driver of current elevated rates. A diplomatic resolution would send spot rates significantly lower. However, management argues restocking demand would sustain rates above historical averages for quarters after resolution. The company's disclosed Q2 booking data as of March 25 showed high LR2 spot rates but only 16% of days covered — fleetwide earnings sensitivity depends heavily on the unbooked remainder of the quarter and the vessel-class mix.

**Geopolitical Binary Outcome**

The stock is exposed to a binary geopolitical event: deal confirmation → rates normalize toward $30,000-$40,000/day over months; deal failure → rates could exceed current levels. This creates genuine near-term uncertainty.

**Fleet Shrinkage**

Active vessel sales have reduced the fleet from 99 to 87 vessels (with 9 more pending sale, expected to close Q2 2026). If the rate environment persists, the company has reduced its revenue-generating base during the boom. However, newbuildings (10 vessels) will partially offset, and the capital recycled into buybacks/cash provides value regardless. The post-sale fleet will be further reduced, lowering operating leverage.

**VLCC Expansion**

Entry into VLCCs introduces crude tanker market risk that STNG has historically avoided. Two vessels at ~$256M total are manageable within the overall fleet but add complexity. Management has explicitly backed away from further VLCC orders, limiting this risk.

**Related Party Risk**

All commercial operations flow through SCM, a related party. While operationally sensible (pooling economics), this creates potential conflicts of interest around fee structures and preferential treatment that cannot be independently verified given the company's foreign private issuer status.

**G&A Cost Inflation**

G&A expenses rose 49% YoY in Q4 2025 to $35.7M, driven primarily by compensation-related costs. In a boom environment, management enrichment is expected but the magnitude deserves monitoring.

**Convertible Dilution**

The $375M convertible notes carry a conversion price of $100.39/share. If fully converted, approximately 3.74M new shares would be created (~7.4% of current outstanding). The concurrent buyback of 1.35M shares partially offsets this but does not neutralize the full potential dilution.

- Iran deal fails or extends in ambiguity; Q2 2026 LR2 spot rates remain elevated for a substantial portion of the quarter
- Full-year 2026 EPS significantly exceeds FY2025's $7.03

- Multi-year time charters locked at attractive rates provide earnings visibility

**Thesis invalidation triggers**:
1. Spot rates fall below $25,000/day blended average for two consecutive months without geopolitical disruption — would signal structural demand deterioration rather than temporary normalization

## Conclusion

STNG combines structural advantages (scale, lowest breakeven, modern fleet) with a very strong balance sheet that provides protection across scenarios. The stock has doubled over the trailing year, but FY2025 earnings were solid at $7.03/share and the disclosed Q1/Q2 rate environment suggests meaningful improvement in 2026.

A rapid peace resolution would send the stock 15-25% lower. But structural supply dynamics (fleet aging faster than replacement, sanctioned tonnage, crossover trading) support above-historical rates for years regardless of any single conflict resolution, according to management and industry commentary. At trailing multiples with a net cash position representing meaningful per-share value, and with rates significantly above FY2025 averages, the risk/reward favors ownership even acknowledging the binary.

Given the strong rate environment, balance sheet quality, disciplined capital allocation, and estimate revision momentum, we maintain **BUY** with a 12-month base case target of $95-100.
