## Executive Summary

Stryker Corporation has declined approximately 14–15% from its July 2025 high of $404.87 to approximately $346, driven primarily by a Q1 2026 earnings miss caused by a cyberattack that disrupted operations for approximately four weeks. Revenue missed by approximately $300M (based on analyst estimates) and adjusted EPS came in at $2.60 vs. approximately $3.01 expected. The stock currently trades at approximately 23x the company's maintained full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $14.90-$15.10.

The fundamental business remains intact: 10.3% organic constant-currency revenue growth in 2025 at $25B scale, gross margins stable at 64%, and free cash flow of $4.3B (approximately 3.2% yield on current market cap). Management maintained full-year guidance, suggesting the impact from delayed procedures is expected to be recovered in Q2-Q4. At approximately 23x forward earnings, Stryker trades roughly in line with peers like Boston Scientific, though it delivers among the highest organic growth rates in large-cap medtech.

The primary risk is execution: if the cyberattack caused permanent customer losses, if the ERP migration was compromised, or if tariff headwinds prove larger than management's guidance absorbs. These are quantifiable risks with identifiable resolution dates (Q2 earnings), making the current pricing attractive with defined catalysts.

## Company Overview

Stryker is a global medtech leader by market capitalization (approximately $133B), operating across two segments: MedSurg & Neurotechnology (62% of $25.1B 2025 revenue) and Orthopaedics (38%). The company's competitive advantages center on the Mako robotic-arm assisted surgery platform, broad product portfolio spanning surgical instruments, endoscopic systems, neurovascular devices, and joint replacement implants, and deep surgeon training relationships that create switching costs.

The company sells products in approximately 61 countries with 76% US / 24% international revenue mix. Stryker's stated capital allocation priority hierarchy is: (1) acquisitions, (2) dividends, (3) share repurchases. They completed ~$5.0B in acquisitions in 2025, anchored by the Inari Medical acquisition establishing a Peripheral Vascular platform.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** Stryker has compounded revenue at ~11% annually over 2022-2025, with organic constant-currency growth of 10.3% in 2025. This is exceptional for a $25B revenue medtech company; peers Medtronic and Abbott grow organically at 5-6%. The growth is volume-driven (pricing contributed only 0.4% in Orthopaedics), which is structurally healthier though it means margin expansion must come from operating leverage rather than price increases.

**Profitability:**
- Gross margin: 64.0% (2025), stable and expanding gradually
- Adjusted operating margin: ~26.3% (2025)
- Adjusted EPS trajectory: $10.60 (2023) → $12.19 (2024) → $13.63 (2025) → $14.90-$15.10 guided (2026)
- GAAP margins depressed by $732M intangible amortization and acquisition-related charges

**Cash Flow:**
- Operating cash flow grew from $3.7B (2023) to $5.0B (2025)
- Free cash flow: $4.3B in 2025, representing 17.1% FCF margin
- FCF yield on current market cap: approximately 3.2% (reasonable for a 10% grower)
- Dividends ($1.3B) well-covered at ~30% payout ratio

**Balance Sheet:**
- Net debt: ~$11.9B ($15.9B gross debt - $4.0B cash)
- Net debt/EBITDA: approximately 1.9x (investment-grade appropriate)
- Interest expense rose 48% YoY to $607M due to Inari acquisition funding
- Current ratio: 1.89x with $7.0B working capital surplus
- $3.0B undrawn revolver provides liquidity buffer
- Goodwill: $19.3B (40% of $47.8B total assets) reflects acquisition-heavy strategy

**Q1 2026 Miss — Context:**
- Revenue: $6.02B actual vs. approximately $6.35B expected (miss of ~$300M based on consensus estimates)
- Adjusted EPS: $2.60 actual vs. approximately $3.01 expected (-13.7% miss)
- Cause: A cyberattack identified March 11, 2026 disrupted Stryker's infrastructure for ~4 weeks. SEC filings describe the perpetrator as a "threat actor"; some reports have linked the attack to an Iran-associated group, though the company has not confirmed specific attribution in its filings.
- The company's April 2026 8-K/A stated it was fully operational across global manufacturing, commercial, ordering, and distribution systems, though the investigation remained ongoing with potential risks including system/data integrity, customer impacts, litigation, and regulatory scrutiny.

## Growth Analysis

Stryker's growth profile is among the strongest in large-cap medtech:

**Organic growth drivers:**
1. **Mako robotic platform** — A leader in robotic-arm assisted joint replacement with more than 2 million robotic procedures performed across Total Knee, Total Hip, and Partial Knee, available in more than 45 countries. New organizational structure ("Ortho Tech") aligns robotics with orthopedic customers more directly.
2. **Neurovascular** — 16% growth in Neuro Cranial sub-segment, addressing stroke and brain treatment markets.
3. **Peripheral Vascular** — 50.6% growth in 2025 (Inari platform). The Amplitude IVL technology acquisition further expands the peripheral vascular portfolio.
4. **International expansion** — 24% of revenue with faster growth rates in emerging markets.

**2026 Guidance:** 8.0-9.5% organic sales growth; $14.90-$15.10 adjusted EPS (9.3-10.8% growth). Consensus FY2026 EPS of $14.98 sits within guidance range. FY2027 consensus of $16.73 implies 11.7% EPS growth.

The 10.3% organic growth rate at this scale, sustained over 3+ years, suggests genuine competitive share gains rather than market tailwinds alone. Procedure volumes in orthopedics and neurosurgery benefit from aging demographics with a 15-20 year secular runway.

## Competitive Landscape

Stryker occupies a strong competitive position that is stable-to-improving:

**Market share dynamics:** 10.3% organic growth vs. medtech industry growth of ~5-6% suggests consistent share gains across multiple product categories. The breadth of outperformance (MedSurg, Neuro, Ortho all growing above market) suggests systemic competitive advantages rather than single-product luck.

**Mako competitive position:** The robotic surgery platform has a large installed base that creates meaningful switching costs. Competing platforms from J&J (VELYS), Zimmer Biomet (ROSA), and Smith+Nephew (CORI) have smaller installed bases with less clinical evidence accumulated to date, though the competitive gap may narrow as these platforms mature.

**Weakening dynamics:**
- China volume-based procurement explicitly designed to compress device pricing
- Hospital consolidation increasing buyer power
- AI/ML in surgical navigation is an emerging competitive vector where Stryker faces risk from faster-moving competitors in emerging technologies

**Strengthening dynamics:**
- Inari acquisition establishes new growth vertical (peripheral vascular) with first-mover advantages in specific thrombectomy procedures
- IVL technology acquisition (Amplitude) expands vascular portfolio
- Organizational restructuring (Ortho Tech) improves robotics go-to-market alignment

## Risk Assessment

**1. Cyberattack Recovery (HIGH — but time-bounded):**
Key unknowns: Were any procedures permanently lost to competitors? Was the ERP migration compromised? Is the company now spending significantly on security remediation? The investigation remains ongoing with potential risks including system/data integrity, customer impacts, litigation, regulatory scrutiny, and reputational risk. Resolution: Q2 results (expected late July) will demonstrate the degree of recovery.

**2. Tariff Exposure (MODERATE):**
Stryker manufactures in China, Mexico, and EU — all subject to new tariffs. At 64% gross margins, the company has more pricing power buffer than most manufacturers, but orthopedic pricing is essentially flat. A scenario involving 100-200bps gross margin headwind from tariffs could reduce EPS by an estimated $0.30-$0.60, though the actual impact will depend on management's ability to mitigate through supply chain adjustments and selective pricing.

**3. Inari Goodwill Impairment Risk (MODERATE):**
The Peripheral Vascular reporting unit's fair value exceeds carrying by only 12%. A 100bps WACC increase would trigger $198M impairment. In the current rate environment, this is a real risk. Additional acquisitions (Amplitude) increase the goodwill base further.

**4. German Tax Assessment ($754M):**
If unsuccessful in appeal, this represents a material one-time cash outflow. Foreign tax credits may not be available on a timely basis or at all. Not an ongoing earnings risk but a balance sheet event.

**5. Consumer/Hospital Spending Environment:**
In a stagflationary macro environment with potential recession, elective procedures could be deferred. Stryker's mix includes both discretionary (knee/hip replacements) and non-discretionary (trauma, stroke) procedures. Historically, orthopedic volumes decline 3-5% in recessions.

## Investment Thesis

Management's maintained guidance proves accurate. Q2 shows strong revenue recovery as delayed procedures are rescheduled. Tariff impacts are manageable within existing guidance range. Stock re-rates to 25-27x forward earnings as the cyberattack is confirmed as a one-quarter event. Inari integration progresses well with continued 30%+ vascular growth. At 25x $15 EPS = $375; at 25x $16.73 (FY27) = $418.

**Bear Case ($280-$300, -13% to -19%):**
Cyberattack caused permanent customer switching. Tariffs compress margins beyond guidance. Recession drives 3-5% procedure volume decline. Inari faces impairment. German tax assessment resolved unfavorably. Stock de-rates to 18-20x reduced estimates of $14-$15 EPS = $252-$300.

**Base Case ($375-$400, +8% to +16%):**
Partial Q2 recovery (most but not all lost revenue returns). Tariffs create modest headwind absorbed within guidance range. Organic growth moderates to 8% from 10%. Stock re-rates to 25-27x $15 EPS = $375-$405 as market gains confidence in recovery trajectory.

## Conclusion

Stryker at approximately $346 represents a high-quality compounder moderately discounted by a one-quarter operational disruption. The ~15% drawdown from highs prices in meaningful near-term uncertainty, though management's maintained guidance suggests confidence in recovery. At approximately 23x forward earnings for a company growing EPS at 10%+ organically, with 64% gross margins and $4.3B in free cash flow, the risk/reward appears favorable for investors with a 6-9 month horizon.

The specific reason the market may be mispricing the stock: it is treating a 4-week operational disruption (with explicitly maintained full-year guidance) as warranting a sustained discount to historical multiples. Orthopedic procedures are generally not discretionary on a multi-quarter basis — a patient needing a hip replacement in March is likely to get it in April or May. However, the company's own filings note that the investigation remains ongoing and risks remain regarding customer impacts, litigation, and reputational effects.

The risk is non-trivial: tariffs, recession-driven deferrals, ongoing cyber risk, and execution uncertainty on Inari integration are all present. But these risks are common to the sector, and Stryker's organic growth profile and operational quality provide a margin of safety. BUY with 2-3% position sizing, adding on further weakness below $320, and targeting Q2 earnings as the primary validation event.

The recommendation is contingent on Q2 confirmation of cyber recovery and re-rating toward historical multiples.
