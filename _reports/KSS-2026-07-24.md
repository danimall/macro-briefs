**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kohl's is a US-only omnichannel department-store retailer in year three of a top-line decline, whose equity now trades far more on short-squeeze positioning than on operating fundamentals. Revenue has fallen from $18.1B (FY ended Jan 2023) to $15.5B (FY ended Jan 2026), roughly 14% cumulatively, with comparable sales negative every year. The stock is up 48% over the trailing twelve months, but that move reflects a 31%-of-float short base being squeezed on modest sequential improvement (the May 28 Q1 print produced a ~19% one-day spike), not a demonstrated return to growth.

The genuine positive here is balance-sheet repair, not sales. FY2026 operating cash flow more than doubled to $1.38B and free cash flow reached roughly $1.0B, driven by aggressive inventory reduction and a one-time interchange-litigation settlement. Management drew the revolver to $0 (from $290M a year earlier), repaid $353M of maturing notes, repurchased $137M of notes on the open market, and cut the dividend more than 75% to $0.50 annualized. Moody's revised its outlook to positive in Q1 FY2026, the first ratings improvement after a string of downgrades. This materially lowers near-term bankruptcy risk (Altman Z of 2.03, grey zone rather than distress).

The offsetting realities cap the fundamental case. The headline net income jump to $272M was flattered by a $129M pre-tax settlement gain; adjusted operating income was essentially flat year over year ($510M vs $509M). Management's own FY2026 guidance is comps of -2% to flat, meaning the base case is managed decline, not turnaround. Debt is junk-rated (Moody's B2, S&P B+ negative, Fitch BB- negative), with new 2030 notes carrying a 10.0% coupon secured by 11 distribution centers, signaling expensive and constrained capital. Governance is unstable: a CEO fired "for cause" after four months, a revolving C-suite, and trailing TSR indexed at 54 versus peers near 164. We move the recommendation to HOLD with an uncertain rationale, acknowledging the trajectory improvement but recognizing that the equity is dominated by positioning dynamics rather than a durable earnings thesis.

## Company Overview

Kohl's operates 1,151 stores (as of May 2, 2026) plus Kohls.com, selling moderately priced apparel, footwear, accessories, beauty, and home goods under a mix of national and proprietary brands. It reports as a single segment and has zero geographic diversification, leaving it fully exposed to the pressured US moderate-income consumer.

Two structural features define the model. First, the Sephora partnership (launched 2020) now spans more than 1,100 stores. Kohl's owns the inventory and funds the capex, and splits operating profit 50/50 with Sephora. Accessories, the category that includes Sephora, was the only growing category in FY2025 (+2.0%) and is the clearest traffic driver in the business. Second, the credit-card program, whose accounts are owned by Capital One with Kohl's sharing net risk-adjusted revenue, has historically been a meaningful high-margin contributor through "Other revenue." That stream is in structural decline (down $84M in FY2025 and again in Q1 FY2026) as card usage falls and buy-now-pay-later substitutes.

The competitive position is squeezed on three sides: off-price (TJX, Ross), mass merchants (Walmart, Target), and e-commerce (Amazon). Management explicitly concedes difficulty differentiating on national brands where consumers comparison-shop on price.

Net sales fell 4.0% in FY2025 with comps down 3.1%, declining across every category except Accessories. The reported net income carries a quality-of-earnings flag from the credit-card interchange litigation settlement. Stripping that out, adjusted EPS was $1.62 versus $1.50. The GAAP profit trend should not be extrapolated.

The durable positive is cash generation and deleveraging, driven by inventory down 7% and receipts down 8% (plus the settlement). The revolver was drawn to $0 from $290M at prior year-end and $545M a year before that. Cash rose to $674M at Jan 2026 (and $429M at May 2026 after seasonal build). The company repaid the maturing notes and repurchased $137M of notes on the open market ($87M plus $50M). Long-term debt sits near $1.4B; the current ratio improved to 1.46 from 1.08. On the ~$2.1B market cap, that FCF profile screens attractively, but a large portion of FY2026 free cash flow came from a one-time working-capital release (inventory cannot be perpetually squeezed) plus the settlement, so a normalized run-rate FCF is materially lower.

Margin discipline is a genuine bright spot. Gross margin improved to 37.5% in FY2025 and held at 39.9% in Q1 FY2026 despite falling sales, reflecting inventory discipline, higher proprietary-brand penetration, and moderating shrink. This demonstrates management can defend margin even as revenue shrinks, which is what keeps the balance-sheet repair intact.

Q1 FY2026 (ended May 2, 2026) showed sequential improvement: net sales -1.7%, comps -1.1% (best quarterly trend since 2021), a seasonally normal net loss of $(0.13) per share, and digital sales +4.0% at 26% penetration. The earnings-surprise record is a clean four-for-four of beats over the last four quarters (most recent by 32%, prior quarter by 24%, and two larger beats before that of 150% and 90%). That track record supports confidence that current consensus estimates are conservative, though these are beats against low bars in an environment where the top line still declines.

Capital allocation priorities are explicitly reinvest, dividend, debt reduction, then buybacks "over the long-term." The dividend was cut from $2.00/share (FY2024) to $0.50 annualized ($0.125/quarter), a defensible move given the leverage and cash-flow priorities, and capex was trimmed to $350-400M.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here in the ordinary sense. Management guides FY2026 comps of -2% to flat, which is decline-to-stabilization, not expansion. Transaction volume fell roughly 4% in both FY2025 and Q1 FY2026. The one category showing growth is Accessories/Sephora, and while that partnership is a real traffic driver, the 50/50 profit split limits the flow-through, and it is not large enough to offset erosion in apparel and home.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not the kind of signal that changes a rating. Current-year EPS consensus rose to $1.42 from $1.36 ninety days ago (+5.0%), and current-quarter estimates rose +3.2% over 90 days. These are small, single-digit revisions rather than the +30%-plus velocity that would indicate a systematic underestimation of a fundamental turn. Next-quarter estimates were actually cut 9.2% over 90 days. The revision pattern is consistent with a stabilizing, low-visibility business, not an accelerating one.

The most credible near-term earnings tailwind is contingent and non-operational: roughly $140M in potential tariff refunds filed after the Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling struck down the IEEPA tariffs Kohl's paid (~$190M between Feb 2025 and Feb 2026). This is treated as a gain contingency, not yet recognized, and could reduce COGS if realized. New Section 122 tariffs partially replace the old regime and are themselves under legal challenge, so the net effect is uncertain. This is optionality, not a growth engine.

## Valuation Assessment

KSS trades at approximately 7.7x trailing earnings against a Broadline Retail peer median of 25.3x, and the dividend yields 2.6%. On the surface this looks deeply cheap. Our discipline is to treat a low multiple as a value-trap flag until an independent catalyst justifies the discount, and cheapness alone has historically sorted the wrong way.

The trailing multiple is distorted by the settlement gain that inflated GAAP EPS. On the forward consensus of $1.42, the stock trades near 13x, still below peers but on flat-to-declining earnings against a structurally shrinking revenue base. The peer set is not a clean comparison: Macy's, the closest structural analog, trades at 10.3x and is also a declining department store, which suggests the market applies a persistent discount to this subsector, not a temporary mispricing. Amazon, eBay, Etsy, and Ollie's trade at 20-29x because they are growing; Kohl's is not, so applying their multiples is inappropriate.

The valuation case rests on three legs, none of which is decisive. First, absolute cheapness: the ~$2.1B market cap against ~$1.0B FY2026 FCF is a high headline FCF yield, but that FCF is inflated by a one-time inventory release and the settlement, so normalized FCF yield is meaningfully lower. Second, hidden real-estate value: net PP&E is roughly $6.8B and the company owns significant real estate, an intermittent activist thesis, but no fresh activist or sale-leaseback catalyst is active, and the newly pledged distribution centers reduce unencumbered asset value. Third, the tariff-refund optionality. The analyst price-target dispersion is unusually wide (low $9, median $16, mean $17.85, high $41), which signals that this is a binary/positioning situation rather than a stock with a consensus intrinsic value.

We score valuation modestly below neutral. The stock is cheap on trailing metrics, but the discount is structurally justified by secular decline, junk credit, and governance risk, and the identified catalysts (real estate, tariffs) are unconfirmed or contingent. This is a value name that requires a specific trigger to re-rate, and no clear trigger is currently in hand.

## Competitive Landscape

Kohl's occupies the least defensible position in retail: mid-tier department store between off-price winners taking apparel share, mass merchants winning on price and convenience, and e-commerce winning on assortment and speed. The company's own filings acknowledge that on national brands, consumers comparison-shop on price, which erodes any differentiation. The trajectory is deteriorating, not stable: comps have been negative for three consecutive years, and cumulative TSR indexed to 54 against a peer group near 164 quantifies the share and value loss.

The genuine competitive assets are the Sephora shop-in-shops (a defensible beauty traffic driver) and the loyalty/credit ecosystem (which is eroding). Proprietary-brand penetration is rising and supports margin, but proprietary brands also carry the risk of alienating the value-seeking customer if pricing or quality slips. The strategic playbook (curated assortment, value leadership, frictionless omnichannel) is defensive stabilization, appropriate for the situation but not a moat-widening program. We score competitive position low on trajectory grounds.

## Risk Assessment

**Secular top-line erosion** is the dominant risk and is only partly priced. Any acceleration of decline (a weakening moderate-income consumer) would pressure the balance-sheet-repair narrative.

**Credit and cost-of-capital risk** is acute. Ratings are junk across all three agencies. A coupon step-up has already raised the 2031 notes 175 bps, and the 2030 notes' 10.0% secured coupon signals scarce, expensive capital. The Moody's outlook improvement to positive is a real offset but does not change the rating category.

**Credit-card program decay** removes a high-margin revenue stream at an uncertain pace and is sensitive to consumer credit deterioration.

**Governance instability** is a first-order risk. CEO Ashley Buchanan was terminated "for cause" in May 2025 after roughly four months for undisclosed conflicts of interest. Board chair Michael Bender stepped into the CEO role and was made permanent. The 8-K stream shows repeated Item 5.02 executive changes across 2024-2026, a revolving C-suite following Michelle Gass's 2022 departure. The June 2026 hire of Elliott Rodgers (ex-Foot Locker COO, ex-Ulta supply chain) as COO effective September 2026 is a credible operating addition, but it does not resolve the board-credibility question. A July 1, 2026 8-K discloses a new material definitive agreement whose terms were not visible in this review, which warrants monitoring.

**Lease-heavy balance sheet**: roughly $4.7B of lease liabilities plus $453M of financing obligations, with an 18-year average lease term, limits flexibility to shrink the fleet.

The reason the risk score is not lower is that most of these risks are already reflected in the valuation and credit spreads, and the balance-sheet de-risking (revolver at $0, current ratio 1.46, Z-score in the grey zone) meaningfully reduces the tail. The idiosyncratic swing factor not fully priced is the ~$140M tariff refund, which is asymmetric to the upside.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress and confirms this is a positioning-driven equity. Near-term ATM IV is 137.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 75.2%, an IV/HV spread of nearly 62 points and a 92nd-percentile IV reading, with the term structure in steep backwardation (137% near-term versus 65% at six months). ATM IV jumped 64.8 points since the June 22 snapshot on only a 3.4% price move, indicating the market anticipates an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is roughly balanced near-dated (1.9%) but flips negative further out (-9.6% at 3 months, -5.7% at 6 months), showing hedging demand for downside on a longer horizon. The 31% short interest with 5.3 days to cover creates genuine squeeze risk; that same dynamic makes the stock capable of outsized moves in either direction and argues against sizing any position aggressively. Rich IV also makes this an expensive stock to hedge or express directionally through options.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The balance sheet has been repaired faster than the market credits: revolver at $0, ~$1B FCF, cash of $674M at year-end, note buybacks, and a Moody's outlook improvement to positive remove the bankruptcy tail that had compressed the multiple. Margins are holding near 40% gross despite falling sales, proving cost discipline. Q1 comps of -1.1% are the best since 2021 and suggest the decline is decelerating toward stabilization. The ~$140M tariff refund is an unrecognized gain that could show up as a COGS benefit. With 31% short interest, any string of stabilizing prints forces continued covering, and the stock re-rates toward the mid-$20s (roughly where it traded in late 2025). Hidden real-estate value provides a floor and a latent activist catalyst. Bull target: $24-25.

### Bear Case
The turnaround is cosmetic. Adjusted operating income was flat, the FCF surge was a one-time inventory release plus a settlement, and normalized run-rate FCF is far lower. Secular decline resumes as the moderate-income consumer weakens, comps fall back toward -3% to -4%, and the credit-card revenue decay accelerates. Junk-rated 10% secured debt keeps the cost of capital punishing, and governance instability produces another strategic misstep. Short covering exhausts, and the stock reverts toward the low end of the analyst range near $9-11. Bear target: $10-11, roughly -40% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. We do not have sufficient evidence for a confident directional call: the balance-sheet improvement is real and lowers downside, but the equity is dominated by short-squeeze mechanics and the fundamental thesis is managed decline, not growth.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive or flat comps (not just decelerating declines), confirming stabilization has turned to growth.
- Recognition of the ~$140M tariff refund as a realized COGS benefit, or an additional ratings upgrade to a higher category.
- A concrete real-estate monetization or credible activist involvement that surfaces the ~$6.8B PP&E value.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +15% over 90 days on the current year, indicating a genuine earnings inflection rather than low-single-digit drift.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comps deteriorating back below -3%, or FY2026 guidance cut, signaling the stabilization has failed.
- Renewed revolver draws or a ratings downgrade, reversing the deleveraging narrative.
- Further "for cause" executive departures or evidence the July 2026 material agreement is dilutive or distressed.
- Gross margin compression below ~37%, indicating pricing power is eroding.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release (the fiscal Q2 print, expected late August 2026), with the tariff-refund resolution and any detail on the July 1 material agreement as interim monitoring points.

## Conclusion

Kohl's has done the hard, unglamorous work of balance-sheet repair, and that lowers the probability of a distressed outcome enough to justify moving off an outright AVOID. The revolver at zero, the current ratio, and the Moody's outlook improvement are concrete and reduce the tail risk that had dominated the prior thesis. The score trajectory (3.6 to 4.2 over four reports) reflects this genuine improvement, and today's data confirms rather than reverses it.

The improvement is defensive. The top line is still shrinking, guidance is for continued decline, the headline profit recovery was largely a one-time settlement, and the equity trades primarily on short-interest positioning rather than on demonstrated operating momentum. Governance remains a first-order concern after a CEO fired for cause and a revolving C-suite. The 7.7x trailing multiple is cheap, but the discount is structurally justified and the re-rating catalysts (real estate, tariff refunds) are contingent or unconfirmed. The wide analyst target dispersion captures the reality that this is a binary special situation, not a stock with a settled intrinsic value.

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Own it only if you are explicitly playing the balance-sheet de-risking plus squeeze optionality with modest size and tight discipline. We would upgrade on two quarters of flat-to-positive comps or a realized tariff refund, and downgrade on any resumption of comp deterioration or reversal of the deleveraging. The rich near-term implied volatility and severe backwardation argue for patience on entry and against expressing this through options.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the bull-case "mid-to-high $20s / $28-30" re-rating target exceeded the 52-week high of $25.22 and the late-2025 trading range (which peaked near $24.91), and has been corrected to the mid-$20s ($24-25); the bull case's upside premise is modestly less generous than originally stated.
