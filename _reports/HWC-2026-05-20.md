## Executive Summary

Hancock Whitney is a $35.5 billion-asset Gulf South regional bank delivering 1.40% ROA, 14.5% ROTCE, and 3.55% NIM following a strategic securities portfolio restructure in January 2026. The stock trades at $66.42, representing ~10.4x forward 2026 consensus EPS of $6.41 and 1.58x tangible book value ($42.16). Analyst consensus targets range from $72 to $86 (mean $78.75), implying 8-30% upside.

Management is executing on a multi-pronged capital deployment strategy (dividends raised 25% over two years, $249M buyback completed in 2025, $377.6M OFB acquisition expanding into Orlando), NIM is expanding, and earnings estimates are rising. However, institutional skepticism is pronounced — 11.6% short interest with 8.4 days to cover, options in backwardation (35.8% near-term IV vs 30.6% 3-month and 26.8% HV), and the stock sits 12% below its February 2026 high of $75.43.

The specific risk preventing an upgrade to BUY: HWC's 18% CRE loan exposure ($4.3B) intersects with the current environment where $875B in US CRE maturities face refinancing at yields above 5%, and the bank's own management has increased its recession scenario weighting to 60%. Modified loans nearly doubled year-over-year to $163M. These are measurable credit quality signals that must stabilize before the valuation discount can close.

## Company Overview

Hancock Whitney Corporation operates 181 banking locations across the Gulf South (Mississippi, Alabama, Louisiana, Florida, Texas, Tennessee, Georgia). The business model is traditional relationship banking: $24B in loans, $29.3B in deposits (95% core), and $39.7B in assets under administration. Revenue is primarily net interest income with supplemental fee streams from trust/wealth management, service charges, and bank card fees.

The strategic direction centers on geographic diversification toward Florida and Texas growth markets while maintaining disciplined credit standards and returning excess capital. Two Florida acquisitions in 12 months (Sabal Trust for wealth management, OFB Bancshares for commercial banking in Orlando) represent a deliberate shift from the hurricane-prone, energy-dependent Gulf South core.

## Competitive Landscape

Hancock Whitney occupies a defensible mid-size regional bank niche: large enough to offer commercial banking, wealth management, and treasury services ($35.5B assets) while small enough to maintain relationship-based service. The 95% core deposit base provides stable, low-cost funding that pure digital competitors cannot easily replicate.

The Florida expansion strategy is well-timed. Orlando (OFB acquisition) is among the fastest-growing US metros. Combined with the Panhandle, Tampa Bay, and Dallas presence, HWC is systematically building exposure to population-growth corridors. The Sabal Trust acquisition adds sticky wealth management relationships that generate recurring fees.

Competitive risks include: fintech encroachment on payments and deposits (management added stablecoin/GENIUS Act risk language to the 10-K), larger regionals competing for the same Florida/Texas growth, and potential margin compression if the rate environment deteriorates sharply.

HWC's competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: NIM expanding, efficiency ratio declining, new markets being added, and revenue-generating talent being hired. The TSR outperformance (213.75% vs. 152.71% peer group) reflects this execution over the measurement period.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CRE Concentration in a Stressed Refinancing Environment (PRIMARY RISK):**
CRE-income producing loans grew 13% in 2025 to $4.3B (18% of total loans). While office CRE is modest at 3% of loans, the broader CRE portfolio faces headwinds from: (a) elevated long-term yields making refinancing at par uneconomic for many borrowers; (b) significant US CRE maturities creating industry-wide stress; (c) healthcare and multifamily sub-segments growing within the book. The mechanism (deals failing to close at current rates) is already operating across the market.

**2. Recession Probability:**
Management's own economic scenario weights assign 60% probability to a slower-growth/mild recession path (up from 50% in Q4 2025). Unemployment peaking at 7.2% in that scenario would stress consumer and small business portfolios.

**3. Elevated Short Interest:**
Nearly 7 million shares are short against a float traded at ~816K/day average. This creates: (a) overhang from potential forced selling on any negative catalyst; (b) informational content — professional short sellers have identified a thesis; and (c) potential squeeze dynamics on positive catalysts.

**4. Interest Rate Sensitivity:**
HWC is asset-sensitive. A -300bp scenario reduces Year 1 NII by 5.2% and Year 2 by 13.9%. The $1.8B in cash flow hedges provides some protection in a falling rate environment.

**5. Hurricane/Geographic Concentration:**
181 of 181 locations are in hurricane-prone coastal or near-coastal states. A major Gulf Coast hurricane during the current fiscal year would create both direct credit losses and operational disruption.

**6. Acquisition Integration:**
The OFB deal ($377.6M all-cash) deploys significant capital and limits remaining buyback capacity for 2026. Integration of a $2.1B-asset bank into a $35.5B platform carries execution risk, though HWC's historical M&A track record is solid.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated near-term uncertainty: 35.8% near-term IV vs. 26.8% HV (9pp rich), with term structure in backwardation (near 35.8% > 3-month 30.6%). Put/call skew of 4.9% indicates elevated put demand. The implied move of ±8.2% over the nearest month suggests the market expects a potential catalyst or event.

This backwardation and elevated put demand is consistent with the short interest signal — institutional positioning expects downside within 1-3 months. The divergence between this options pricing and the positive fundamental trajectory (rising estimates, expanding NIM, analyst upgrades) cannot be ignored.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Short interest declines below 8% of float, indicating institutional skepticism is fading
2. Two consecutive quarters of declining modified loans AND stable pass-watch credits
3. OFB acquisition closes successfully (expected Q3 2026) without material integration cost overruns or unexpected credit issues in the acquired portfolio

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Modified loans exceed $250M or NCOs breach 0.35% of average loans
2. NIM contracts below 3.40%
3. Management suspends or reduces buyback activity citing capital conservation needs
4. CET1 drops below 11.5%

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July), which will show the first full quarter of securities restructure benefit and provide updated credit quality metrics post-OFB announcement.

## Conclusion

Hancock Whitney is a fundamentally sound regional bank executing well on a clear strategic plan, trading at a meaningful discount to peers. The valuation (10.4x forward, 1.58x TBV) combined with 13% earnings growth and a 3% dividend yield creates an attractive risk/reward profile on paper.

The specific obstacle preventing a BUY recommendation is the combination of three quantifiable, unresolved signals: (1) 11.6% short interest with 8.4 days to cover, representing meaningful informed bearish positioning; (2) options in backwardation with 9pp IV premium and elevated put skew, pricing near-term stress not visible in trailing fundamentals; and (3) the CRE refinancing environment creating sector-level tail risk directly relevant to HWC's $4.3B CRE book while management itself has increased recession probability weighting to 60%.

These are falsifiable conditions. If short interest declines, credit metrics stabilize through Q2-Q3, and OFB integrates successfully, the path to $78-86 (analyst consensus range) is clear. The stock is worth holding for that optionality while monitoring the specific triggers above.
