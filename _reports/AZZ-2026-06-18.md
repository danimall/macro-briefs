**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AZZ is North America's largest independent provider of metal-coating services, split between high-margin hot-dip galvanizing (AZZ Metal Coatings, 46% of FY26 sales, ~27% operating margin) and coil coating (AZZ Precoat Metals, 54% of sales, ~15% operating margin). Over the past four years the company added the coil business via the 2022 Precoat acquisition, then deconsolidated and monetized its cyclical electrical-infrastructure business through the AVAIL joint venture. The proceeds funded a deleveraging from roughly 5x net leverage post-deal to 1.4x at FY26, alongside the redemption of expensive 6% preferred stock and repeated Term Loan B repricings that cut the weighted-average interest rate to 5.94% from 7.54%.

The single most important analytical point is that FY26 headline numbers overstate the underlying business. GAAP net income jumped to $317M and diluted EPS to $10.50, but roughly $204.5M of pretax equity earnings were one-time gains from the AVAIL JV selling its two largest units (Electrical Products Group to nVent for $975M; Welding Services to Pelican Energy), net of a $45.9M JV impairment and a $9.6M Brazil accounting adjustment. On a normalized basis, adjusted EPS was $6.19 (FY26) versus $5.20 (FY25), and adjusted EBITDA was $367.6M versus $347.9M — mid-single-digit underlying growth, not the ~498% the GAAP line implies. The trailing P/E of ~14.5x is therefore a statistical artifact; the relevant metric is ~22x forward adjusted EPS.

The growth case rests on a genuine demand shift: nine-month utilities sales rose ~29% YoY ($114M vs $89M), and management explicitly cites data-center and transmission-and-distribution (T&D) demand as growth drivers. Galvanized steel is used heavily in transmission towers, substations, and data-center structural steel — the highest-margin part of the mix. At ~$152 the stock has run 71% over twelve months and sits near the low end of the $138–200 analyst target range, so the open question is timing and price rather than business quality. We rate AZZ a HOLD with a growth bias.

## Company Overview

AZZ operates two reportable revenue segments plus a minority JV stake:

- **AZZ Metal Coatings ($759M FY26, 46% of sales):** Hot-dip galvanizing plus surface technologies (spin galvanizing, powder coating, anodizing, plating) for corrosion protection of fabricated steel. 42 galvanizing plants and 4 surface-tech plants. Operating income of $204M (~27% margin); segment EBITDA $235M. Galvanizing is a local business — plants serve customers within a limited transport radius, which creates regional pricing moats. Key inputs are zinc and natural gas.

- **AZZ Precoat Metals ($891M FY26, 54% of sales):** Coil coating — cleaning, treating, painting, and curing steel/aluminum coil before fabrication, across 14 US plants serving construction, appliance, HVAC, container, and transportation. Lower margin (~15% operating; EBITDA $176M). Paint is a pass-through cost; key inputs are natural gas, steel, and aluminum scrap.

- **AZZ Infrastructure Solutions:** No revenue — a 40% non-controlling equity stake in the AVAIL JV (60% owned by Fernweh), the former electrical-infrastructure business deconsolidated in 2022. After AVAIL's divestitures, the residual stake is worth only ~$20M (down from $107M) and consists mainly of industrial lighting and remaining welding.

The company is North America–centric (US ~97% of sales, Canada ~3%), has no customer above 10% of sales, and is construction-weighted (~56% of sales), making it seasonal with a weak fiscal Q4 (winter). Fiscal year ends February 28.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $1.324B (FY23) → $1.538B (FY24) → $1.578B (FY25) → $1.650B (FY26). FY26 growth of +4.6% came almost entirely from Metal Coatings (+14.1% on higher steel volumes in construction, electrical, and industrial), while Precoat declined -2.3% on lower coil volume partly offset by pass-through pricing.

**Earnings — read the adjusted line, not GAAP.** Equity in JV earnings was $209.7M in FY26 versus $16.2M in FY25. Any analysis treating $10.50 as run-rate earnings will badly misprice the stock.

**Deleveraging is the genuine value creation.** Long-term debt fell from $1.06B (FY23) to $478M (FY26); net leverage dropped to 1.4x from 2.5x a year earlier. AVAIL JV distributions ($286.8M in FY26, of which $273.2M was classified as operating cash) retired debt. Management repriced Term Loan B to SOFR+1.75% from SOFR+3.75% in early FY24 and added a $150M receivables securitization facility at SOFR+0.95%. Interest expense fell from $107M (FY24) to $56M (FY26). Covenant max net leverage is 4.5x — ample headroom.

**Cash flow quality is strong but flattered.** FY26 operating cash flow was $525M and FCF $444.7M (~27% FCF margin), though ~$273M of that was the one-time JV distribution. Excluding it, underlying FCF generation remains healthy, and capex is moderating now that the ~$122M Washington, MO greenfield is operational.

**Balance sheet:** Equity $1.337B; goodwill $715M plus intangibles $410M (~51% of total assets, a material impairment-exposure point). Current ratio 1.7. Altman Z of 2.99 sits in the grey zone, dragged by the goodwill-heavy post-acquisition asset base rather than any liquidity stress; improving coverage and falling leverage argue the credit is strengthening. ROE was 23.7% (GAAP-inflated) and ROA 14.3%.

We score Financial Health 7.0 — strong cash generation, a deleveraged and repriced capital structure, and ample covenant headroom, tempered by the goodwill-heavy asset base and the still-largely-variable-rate debt with only half swap-hedged.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying growth is mid-single-digit and steady, not explosive. FY27 guidance (reiterated February 2026) is sales of $1.725–1.775B (+4.5% to +7.6%), adjusted EBITDA of $360–400M, and adjusted EPS of $6.50–7.00 (+5% to +13% on the $6.19 base). That is a reasonable range with a track record of mostly meeting it — three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 8.9%.

The composition of demand is shifting. Galvanized steel goes into transmission towers, substations, and data-center structural framing, the highest-margin part of the Metal Coatings mix. If grid electrification and data-center buildout sustain, AZZ's demand backdrop is structurally better than its construction-cyclical history suggests.

Estimate revisions are constructive but not the kind of velocity that signals re-rating. Current-year consensus is up +0.9% over 30 days and +0.5% over 90 days; next year is up +1.8% / +3.3%. The current-quarter estimate was cut -7.1% over 30 days, reflecting near-term seasonality. These are not the +30%/90-day revisions that mark a re-rating candidate; they confirm steady, modestly improving momentum.

We score Growth Prospects 6.0 — credible secular tailwind (electrification/data-center) layered on a mid-single-digit organic base, with the AVAIL JV earnings contribution now largely non-recurring and immaterial going forward.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$152, AZZ trades at ~22x forward adjusted EPS of ~$6.84 consensus (or ~21–23x against the $6.50–7.00 guidance range). The trailing P/E of 14.5x is meaningless — it divides price by the one-time-gain-inflated $10.50 GAAP EPS. The forward P/E of 19.9x reported by data services is closer but still benefits from the way consensus blends GAAP and adjusted figures; the cleanest read is ~22x on normalized adjusted earnings.

Against peers, the Industrials comparable set shows a median P/E of 30.7x, but that set is heterogeneous and includes negative-earnings names (REZI, RXO). AZZ at ~22x forward sits below FELE (30.7x) and CSW (41.0x) but above KBR (10.4x). The discount to higher-multiple peers is partly earned — AZZ is more cyclical and construction-exposed than a pure data-center play, and its earnings include commodity (zinc, natural gas) sensitivity that the market rightly discounts.

The key valuation judgment: ~22x forward on a business growing adjusted EPS mid-single-digits to low-double-digits implies a PEG well above 1.5x. This is not a cheap stock relative to its growth. The electrification tailwind is real, but it is already in the multiple — the stock re-rated from ~$89 a year ago to ~$152, driven by both the deleveraging story and growing recognition of the grid/data-center demand. A low-P/E "value" read here would be a trap created by the GAAP distortion; on normalized earnings the stock is fairly-to-fully valued.

Analyst targets ($138 low, $165 median, $200 high, $161.67 mean) place the current price near the bottom of the range, which on a premium-multiple stock is more a sign of limited margin of safety than of a floor that will be revised upward. We score Valuation 5.0 — neither cheap nor egregiously expensive, with the asymmetry roughly balanced after the run.

## Competitive Landscape

AZZ positions itself as the #1 independent post-fabrication galvanizer and coil coater in North America. Competition comes from other independent galvanizers, captive (in-house) galvanizing at manufacturers, and substitute corrosion protection (stainless/aluminum substitution, paint, weathering steel). In coil coating, competitors are other toll coaters and integrated steel/aluminum mills.

The durable competitive edge is plant density. Galvanizing is a local business constrained by transport economics, so a network of 46 plants creates proximity advantages that a new entrant cannot easily replicate market-by-market. The breadth of coating capabilities (galvanizing, powder coating, anodizing, plating) deepens customer relationships. The competitive trajectory is improving in the Metal Coatings segment, where volumes grew 14.1% and the highest-margin utilities/grid mix is expanding. Precoat is more commoditized and saw volume declines; pass-through pricing protects margins but limits pricing power. The Canton Galvanizing acquisition ($30.1M, July 2025) and Washington, MO greenfield add modest capacity in line with demand.

We score Competitive Position 6.5 — a structurally advantaged, scale-leading galvanizing franchise with an improving high-margin mix, partly offset by the more commoditized coil business.

## Risk Assessment

- **Earnings normalization / JV wind-down:** With AVAIL's two largest businesses sold, the JV's earnings contribution is non-recurring and the residual ~$20M stake could face further impairment. Investors who anchor to $10.50 GAAP EPS will be disappointed when the recurring run-rate (~$6.50–7.00) becomes the reported figure.
- **Cyclicality / construction dependence:** ~56% of sales tied to construction. A North American construction or industrial downturn would pressure volumes and pricing in both segments.
- **Commodity exposure:** Zinc and natural gas (Metal Coatings); steel/aluminum/gas (Precoat). Partially hedged (~$97M zinc + $7.3M gas forwards) and partially pass-through, but margin risk remains if input costs rise faster than recovery pricing. Zinc cost rose meaningfully in FY26.
- **Goodwill/intangible impairment:** ~$1.1B combined (~51% of assets), tied to construction-market assumptions and the company's own stock price as an indicator.
- **Interest-rate risk:** Effectively all ~$515M of debt is variable-rate; only ~half is swap-hedged at 5.509% through June 2027. The swap roll-off and unhedged portion create rate exposure.
- **Governance / succession:** CEO Thomas Ferguson is 69 and has led since 2013. The April 2025 Executive Retiree LTI Program signals retirement planning is underway. CFO Crawford is new (2024). An orderly Metal Coatings leadership transition is in process (Stovall retiring June 2026, Bella promoted).
- **Litigation:** STI $5.5M judgment (under appeal, accrued); TECO $5.2M adverse verdict (FY25); environmental reserve $18.1M.

These are mostly priced risks rather than hidden binary events; the most underappreciated is the GAAP-to-normalized earnings step-down. We score Risk Factors 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility jumped sharply to 88.2% from 35.0% between early May and mid-June, while the price rose only +3.8%. That magnitude of IV expansion against a modest price move points to event-hedging or thin options liquidity around the recent grind to new highs near $157, rather than a clear directional bet (the put/call OI ratio reads flat). Short interest is low at 3.0% of float (4 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short or squeeze dynamic. The elevated IV indicates the market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty, consistent with a stock that has run hard and sits near the low end of its analyst target range. For a holder, this favors patience over chasing the move.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($185–200):** Grid electrification and data-center buildout sustain double-digit growth in the high-margin utilities/T&D mix. Metal Coatings continues compounding at low-double-digits, Precoat stabilizes, and adjusted EPS reaches the high end of guidance ($7.00) and beyond into FY28 ($8.00+). The deleveraged balance sheet funds bolt-on galvanizing acquisitions and buybacks. The market awards a ~26–28x multiple as AZZ is increasingly viewed as an electrification-infrastructure play rather than a construction cyclical. $200 implies ~28x on $7+ EPS.

**Bear Case ($110–120):** A North American construction/industrial slowdown pressures volumes in both segments. Zinc or natural gas cost spikes compress Metal Coatings margins faster than pass-through recovery. The market re-rates the stock toward a mid-teens cyclical multiple on normalized earnings as the AVAIL gains roll off and reported EPS steps down. $115 implies ~17x on ~$6.50 EPS, a -24% decline from current levels.

**Base Case ($150–165):** Adjusted EPS lands in the $6.50–7.00 guidance range; utilities/T&D demand holds; the multiple stays around 22–24x. The stock tracks earnings growth with limited multiple expansion from here, given it has already re-rated.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale. The business is good and the secular demand story is credible, but at ~$152 the entry lacks asymmetric upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$120s (~18x forward adjusted EPS) without fundamental deterioration, restoring a margin of safety against the bear case.
- Two consecutive quarters of utilities/T&D sales growth above 20% YoY, confirming the electrification mix shift is durable and accelerating, paired with positive estimate revisions.
- Evidence of FY28 adjusted EPS trending above $7.50, which would compress the forward multiple meaningfully below the current ~22x.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Metal Coatings volume growth turning negative, or utilities sales decelerating to flat, signaling the electrification thesis is failing.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or a further AVAIL JV impairment beyond the residual ~$20M.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin compression of more than ~200bps from commodity costs not recovered through pricing.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q1 FY27 earnings (reported ~July 2026) is the natural checkpoint — it will show whether utilities/T&D momentum sustains into the new fiscal year and whether management holds the $6.50–7.00 adjusted EPS guide.

## Conclusion

AZZ has earned its re-rating: a clean balance-sheet repair (net leverage 1.4x from 2.5x), a strategic simplification into a focused coatings pure-play, repriced low-cost debt, and a genuine pivot in demand composition toward grid electrification and data-center steel. The franchise is high-quality, the cash generation is strong, and the growth backdrop is structurally improved.

The issue is price. At ~$152 the stock trades at ~22x normalized forward earnings — a premium that already embeds the electrification tailwind — and sits near the low end of the analyst target range after a 71% twelve-month run. On the earnings that actually recur, the stock is fairly-to-fully valued, not cheap. With the bull case requiring continued multiple expansion and the bear case offering ~24% downside in a construction slowdown, the risk/reward here is roughly balanced. We rate AZZ a HOLD and would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$120s or on confirmation that the utilities/T&D demand surge is accelerating rather than normalizing.
