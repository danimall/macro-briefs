**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tapestry is a two-brand accessible-luxury company built around Coach, which now drives roughly 80% of sales and essentially all of the profit after the FY2025 divestiture of Stuart Weitzman and the abandoned Capri acquisition. The headline FY2025 GAAP figures are misleading: net income fell 77.6% and EPS printed $0.82, but the entire decline is one-time noise from an $854.8M non-cash Kate Spade impairment plus ~$268M of terminated-Capri costs. Underlying non-GAAP EPS grew to $5.10 from $4.29, and the business has accelerated sharply since. Through nine months of FY2026, revenue rose 15.9%, operating margin reached 24.0%, and diluted EPS of $5.58 already exceeds full-year FY2025 non-GAAP earnings. Coach grew 31.5% in Q3 with Greater China sales nearly doubling.

The stock trades at ~$144.96 (~$145.87 most recent), up ~76% over the trailing year. On forward earnings, this is ~20.8x current-year ($6.97 consensus) and ~18.7x next-year ($7.74), below the 26x apparel/luxury peer median. Estimates have risen +7.4% over 90 days, the company has beaten four straight quarters (by 26%, 20%, 8%, 0.5%), generates ~$1.1B+ in free cash flow, and is retiring shares aggressively (count down from ~264M in FY2022 to ~202M). Analyst mean and median targets of $164 and $169 imply 13–17% upside.

I am moving to BUY. The combination of a reasonable forward multiple and measurable, broad-based earnings acceleration is the most reliable setup for forward outperformance, and the primary downside risks (a near-zero-contribution Kate Spade segment and tariff drag) are either non-cash or already absorbed in the reported and still-expanding margins. The discipline I apply: Consumer Discretionary is a structurally difficult sector for stock selection, and demand is discretionary and China-sensitive, so this warrants measured position sizing rather than maximum conviction. The evidence supports ownership.

## Company Overview

Following the August 4, 2025 sale of Stuart Weitzman to Caleres for $109.1M, Tapestry operates two reportable segments:

- **Coach** — the growth engine. FY2025 sales of $5.60B (80% of total), operating margin of 33.5%, $1.88B operating income. Coach's nine-month FY2026 operating margin reached 36.6%, among the highest in branded consumer goods. Momentum is strong with Gen Z and in Greater China, where Q3 FY26 sales nearly doubled ($423M vs $258M).
- **Kate Spade** — the chronic laggard. FY2025 sales of $1.20B (down ~10%), down a further ~11% in FY26 YTD, with operating margin compressed to roughly 0.1%. Subject of an $854.8M impairment in Q4 FY2025.

The model is predominantly direct-to-consumer (wholesale ~13%, licensing ~1%), with ~40% of sales international. Management's "Amplify" 2028 strategy, unveiled at the September 2025 investor day, prioritizes Gen Z acquisition, handbag/leather-goods leadership, and international expansion, with roughly 70% of future growth expected from international markets, concentrated in China and Europe.

## Financial Analysis

The optics-versus-reality gap is the central feature of the financials. FY2025 GAAP net income of $183.2M was depressed entirely by one-time items. Non-GAAP operating margin expanded ~130bps to 20.0%, and gross margin expanded 210bps to 75.4%.

FY2026 has accelerated materially:
- **9 months (ended 3/28/26):** Operating margin 24.0%, net income $1.18B.
- **Q3 FY26:** Revenue +21.2%, Coach +31.5% (constant-currency +29%), diluted EPS $1.65 (vs $0.95).

The trailing P/E of ~46.9x is meaningless here because the trailing-twelve-month base still carries the FY2025 impairment trough.

Cash generation is strong. FY2025 FCF was ~$1.09B (OCF $1.22B less $123M capex); nine-month FY26 operating cash flow was $1.46B. The balance sheet is cleaned up post-Capri: ~$2.40B long-term notes (2027/2030/2032/2035 maturities), ~$1.05B cash, an undrawn $2B revolver, and a new $2B commercial paper program. Net leverage is ~1x against >$2B EBITDA, well inside the 4.0x covenant.

Any "distress" signal from an Altman Z-Score near 1.79 or a reported 6.67x debt/equity is an accounting artifact, not a financial warning. Under Maryland law, repurchased shares are retired rather than held as treasury, so the aggressive buyback drove retained earnings to a -$3.17B accumulated deficit and crushed book equity to $857.8M. Layered on top is the non-cash Kate Spade impairment. Positive net income, ROA, and operating cash flow (with OCF > NI), a 1.87 current ratio, ~21% ROE, and a 15.6% FCF margin all describe a healthy cash machine. The leverage ratios reflect equity shrinkage from buybacks, not weakness. I score Financial Health 6.5 — strong cash flow and modest real leverage, held below 7 because the two-brand concentration and discretionary cyclicality cap the resilience grade.

## Growth Analysis

This is a textbook inflection setup, and I weight it heavily:

- **Revenue growth +15.9% (9-mo), accelerating to +21.2% in Q3.** Coach +31.5% with Greater China nearly doubling.
- **Earnings inflection:** 9-mo EPS $5.58 vs $3.12, already above full-year FY25 non-GAAP.
- **Four consecutive beats** of 26.0%, 19.8%, 8.0%, and 0.5% — the cadence is improving, not fading.
- **Estimate revisions +7.4% over 90 days** to $6.97 current-year and $7.74 next-year, with continued upward drift over the last 30 days. Twenty to twenty-one analysts contribute, so the revision is broad.

The combination of revenue growth above 15% (and Coach above 30%) with rising consensus estimates is among the strongest forward indicators available. The growth is durable rather than a one-quarter pop: it is driven by structural Coach momentum among younger consumers and a China recovery that is bucking the broader luxury slowdown. Kate Spade is a drag, but at ~0.1% operating margin it contributes essentially nothing, so the consolidated trajectory is governed by Coach. Growth Prospects: 7.5.

| Forward P/E (next-yr, $7.74) | ~18.7x | — |
| Dividend yield | ~1.1% | 1.1% |

Peers are heterogeneous: Ralph Lauren at 26.0x, Nike at 30.3x (-45% off its high, struggling), Lululemon at 9.8x (-54% off its high, decelerating). TPR sits below the RL/NKE multiples while delivering faster, accelerating growth than either. The PEG on current-year EPS growth is comfortably below 1.5x given mid-teens-to-30%+ growth depending on the line.

The trailing P/E reflects an impairment-distorted base. The relevant question is price versus forward and normalized earnings, and on that basis the multiple does not capture the inflection. Analyst targets of $164 mean / $169 median imply 13–17% upside; for a name with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, those targets function as a floor that lags reality rather than a ceiling. I score Valuation 7.0. I deliberately do not push higher: a 76% trailing-year run means sentiment is no longer washed out, and the absolute multiple, while reasonable, is not deeply cheap.

## Competitive Landscape

Tapestry occupies the accessible-luxury tier — below European hard luxury (LVMH, Kering, Hermès), above mass market. The striking dynamic is that Coach is gaining share among younger consumers and in China precisely when many European luxury houses report China softness and Nike/Lululemon are derating. The FTC's block of the Capri merger — on the theory that combining Coach with Michael Kors would reduce competition in "accessible luxury handbags" — confirms that the regulator views Coach as the category leader against a now-struggling Capri.

The moat rests on Coach brand equity, ~76% gross margins reflecting pricing power, vertically controlled DTC distribution, sophisticated clienteling/consumer data, and scale sourcing across Vietnam, Cambodia, India, the Philippines, and China. The cumulative five-year TSR of $780 per $100 invested versus a peer group at $101 quantifies the execution differential. The trajectory is improving (share gains, margin expansion, China acceleration), which matters more than the absolute level. Competitive Position: 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Kate Spade further-impairment risk ($382.7M).** Management warns that continued FY2026 profitability declines could trigger impairment of remaining goodwill and brand intangible. This is non-cash, on a segment already contributing near-zero profit. Such a write-down would barely affect intrinsic value, which is overwhelmingly Coach-driven. I no longer treat this as a primary thesis risk — it is an accounting event, not a cash-flow event. The real Kate Spade question is the opportunity cost of an unproven turnaround, which is modest.

**Tariffs.** Most U.S.-sold product is imported from tariff-affected countries; management guided to ~230bps of FY2026 operating-margin headwind, with ~180bps hitting Q3 gross margin. This drag is already embedded in the reported margins. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were invalid creates unbooked upside: Tapestry has remitted ~$115M and may be eligible for refunds (no receivable booked). A new 10% Section 122 global tariff took effect February 24, 2026, so the headwind persists, but the refund optionality is a positive tail not in estimates.

**Concentration and cyclicality.** With Stuart Weitzman gone, Coach drives ~85%+ of sales and nearly all profit. Demand is discretionary and sensitive to recession, Chinese consumer sentiment, and tourist flows. In a stagflationary macro backdrop with a possible H2 consumer cliff, this is the most genuine risk to the thesis.

**Tax.** Pillar Two/QDMTT lifted the effective rate to ~17.5% in FY26 (from 13.6%) and should keep pressuring it — a knowable, incremental drag already in forward estimates.

**Litigation.** Two Capri-related securities class actions were dismissed without prejudice March 31, 2026; plaintiffs filed an amended complaint April 30, 2026. Not expected to be material.

**Governance note.** An April 6, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02) indicates an officer departure/election whose details could not be confirmed. Flagged for follow-up; the core executive team (Crevoiserat, Kahn, Roe) appears intact.

Net, Risk Factors at 5.5: the cyclical/China exposure is real and macro-correlated, but the most-cited overhang (Kate Spade impairment) is largely a non-event for intrinsic value, and tariff costs are already absorbed.

### Options Market Signal

The headline 136.7% ATM IV is on the 1-day-to-expiry (June 18) contract, where IV is mechanically inflated by pin and gamma effects and carries no information about fundamental risk. The informative tenors are benign: 1-month 36.3%, 3-month 35.5%, 6-month 41.0% — all near or modestly above the 41.2% one-year historical vol, with next earnings ~August 2026. The term structure does not price event stress into the earnings window. The only mildly bearish signals are a 5.7% near-term and -4.3% six-month put skew, consistent with routine discretionary-cyclical hedging rather than a directional alarm. Put/call volume (0.48) and OI (0.43) ratios are call-skewed. Short interest at 9.0% of float (5.9 days to cover) is moderate. Nothing in the options or positioning data contradicts the fundamental case.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$205, +41%):** Coach continues compounding at 20–30% with China and Gen Z driving share gains; consolidated operating margin pushes toward the high-20s%; Kate Spade stabilizes or is divested, removing the drag; tariff refunds (~$115M) materialize as an earnings/cash tailwind; the buyback shrinks share count further. EPS reaches ~$8.50+ and the multiple holds at ~24x, matching the high analyst target.

**Bear Case (~$88, -39%):** A consumer recession and China relapse stall Coach growth; tariff drag widens; Kate Spade requires further impairment and continued cash investment; the forward multiple compresses toward the low-teens as cyclical discretionary names derate. This is the low analyst target and reflects the structural fragility of a two-brand discretionary franchise in a stagflation regime.

**Base Case (~$167, +15%):** Coach grows mid-to-high teens, consolidated EPS reaches ~$7.75 (next-year consensus), and the multiple holds near ~21–22x as estimates continue rising. This aligns with the $164 mean / $169 median analyst targets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: ~$167** — ~21.5x next-year consensus EPS of $7.74. **+15% from current.**
- **Bull case target: ~$205** — ~24x ~$8.50 EPS on continued Coach acceleration plus tariff refunds. **+41%.**
- **Bear case target: ~$88** — ~13x compressed multiple on stalled growth and consumer recession. **-39%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two earnings reports (August 2026 Q4 FY26 and the November 2026 Q1 FY27 print) and continued estimate-revision trajectory.
- **Upside/downside:** +15% to base, +41% to bull, -39% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Coach constant-currency revenue growth decelerates below ~10% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling the inflection has faded.
2. 90-day forward EPS estimate revisions turn negative, reversing the current +7.4% trend.
3. Consolidated operating margin contracts YoY (beyond the known ~230bps tariff drag), indicating the pricing power/operating-leverage story is breaking.

## Conclusion

The evidence describes a structurally inflecting franchise priced at a reasonable forward multiple. Revenue is accelerating, earnings have already surpassed the prior full year through nine months, the company has beaten four straight quarters, and consensus estimates are rising — all below the peer median forward multiple, with strong free cash flow and a shrinking share count. The two most-cited concerns dissolve under scrutiny: the Kate Spade impairment risk is non-cash on a near-zero-profit segment, and the tariff drag is already embedded in expanding reported margins.

I rate TPR a **BUY** with a 12-month base-case target of ~$167 (+15%). The discipline I impose is on sizing rather than direction: Consumer Discretionary is a structurally challenging sector for stock selection, demand is China- and recession-sensitive, and the stock has already run ~76% over the past year, so this is a measured-conviction position rather than a maximum-weight one. The combination of a reasonable multiple and broad-based, accelerating fundamentals is the most reliable forward setup, and it points to ownership.
