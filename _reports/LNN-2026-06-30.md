**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lindsay Corporation is a financially pristine, well-run oligopolist in center-pivot irrigation (Zimmatic) and moveable-barrier infrastructure (Road Zipper) with a net-cash balance sheet, roughly $90M of annual free cash flow, and durable long-term demand drivers tied to water scarcity, food security, and infrastructure funding. The business is currently in a cyclical downturn. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended Feb 28, 2026) revenue fell 16% YoY to $157.7M, operating income fell 59%, and EPS dropped to $1.15 from $2.44 as North American irrigation weakened on low commodity prices and cautious farmer sentiment, and infrastructure fell 58% on the non-repeat of a prior-year ~$20M Road Zipper project.

The central analytical issue is the quality of Lindsay's recent growth. The headline FY2025 recovery (+11% revenue to $676M) was driven almost entirely by a single MENA irrigation project (international irrigation +39%) while North American irrigation declined 9%. International project revenue is margin-dilutive, lumpy, and geopolitically exposed. As the first MENA project rolled off in FY2026, the underlying domestic softness became visible. A new >$80M MENA supply agreement (~$70M recognized in FY2026) backfills the concentration but repeats the same low-margin, single-geography dependence. Consolidated gross margin compressed to 26.9% in Q2 from 33.4% a year earlier, driven simultaneously by the loss of high-margin Road Zipper revenue and the higher weight of low-margin international projects.

We rate LNN a HOLD. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen 21% for the current year and 37% for the current quarter over the past 90 days, and the stock trades at roughly 18x trailing earnings on what is likely trough-and-still-falling profit. The balance sheet and countercyclical buyback bound the downside, and the long-term structural thesis is intact, but there is no identifiable near-term catalyst and the risk of a prolonged value trap is real if North American demand stays soft. We lack sufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction until the ag cycle turns or estimate revisions stabilize.

## Company Overview

Lindsay operates two segments. **Irrigation** (~84% of FY2025 revenue, $568M) makes center-pivot, lateral-move, and hose-reel systems under the Zimmatic and Perrot brands, sold through 200+ independent U.S. dealers plus an international network. Lindsay is one of four manufacturers controlling the substantial majority of the U.S. center-pivot market, competing primarily with Valmont/Valley. A growing recurring-revenue technology layer (FieldNET remote monitoring, FieldNET Advisor ML-based irrigation recommendations, Elecsys IIoT) differentiates the offering. International was 52% of irrigation revenue in FY2025, up from 41% in FY2024, reflecting a mix shift toward large project sales.

**Infrastructure** (~16% of FY2025 revenue, $108M) centers on the Road Zipper/Quickchange Moveable Barrier system, a high-margin product with limited direct competition, plus crash cushions (TAU line), specialty barriers, road-marking tape, and railroad signals. Infrastructure carried a 24.3% segment operating margin in FY2025 versus 17.1% for irrigation, but is lumpy and driven by government funding (IIJA, running through Sept 2026) and one-off project timing.

The company manufactures across the U.S., Brazil, France, Türkiye, China, South Africa, and Italy, giving it tariff and currency flexibility. CEO Randy Wood has led since January 2021, having joined in 2008 from CNH/Case IH.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet is a genuine strength. Altman Z-Score of 3.63 sits in the safe zone, current ratio is 3.71, and debt/equity is 0.58. Against ~$186M of cash at Feb 28, 2026 (down from $250M at fiscal year-end after buybacks), the only debt is $115M of fixed-rate 3.82% Senior Notes due 2030, leaving the company effectively net cash. ROE runs ~13.9%, ROA ~8.8%. FY2025 free cash flow was $90.4M, a 13.4% FCF margin.

FY2025 (ended Aug 31, 2025) was a recovery year on the headline: net income $74.1M ($6.78 diluted EPS, +12%), operating income $88.1M (13.0% margin). The composition matters more than the number: the entire gain came from international irrigation (+39% to $294M) on one MENA project, while North America irrigation declined. One MENA customer represented 13% of consolidated revenue.

FY2026 has reversed sharply. First-half revenue declined 11%, operating income 38%, net income 35%. North American irrigation is soft on persistent commodity-market weakness and tempered farmer sentiment; Brazil is constrained by high interest rates and tight credit. Backlog rebounded to $151.8M at Feb 28 (from $110.7M at fiscal year-end) largely on the new MENA project, but that revenue is lower-margin.

The earnings surprise record confirms deteriorating visibility: after a +25.4% beat in the June 2025 quarter, Lindsay missed by 10.9% in the September quarter, beat by 3.4% in the December quarter, then missed the March quarter by 32.4% ($1.15 actual vs. $1.70 estimated). Two beats and two misses over four quarters, with the most recent miss the largest, signals reduced forecasting reliability into a soft cycle.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth prospects are weak and the leading indicators are pointed down. Consensus EPS for the current quarter has been cut 37.4% over 90 days (to $1.21 from $1.93), current-year estimates cut 20.9% (to $5.08 from $6.42), and next-year estimates cut 10.0% (to $6.34). Revisions have stabilized at zero over the trailing 30 days, which stops the bleeding but does not yet signal a turn.

The demand backdrop is unfavorable. USDA projects 2026 net farm income down ~1%, with the increase concentrated in government payments rather than crop receipts; management is explicit that transfer payments do not meaningfully lift irrigation demand. Corn was down ~10% YoY as of February. Favorable weather (abundant rain, high crop inventories) is a demand negative for irrigation. Brazil, a key growth market, remains constrained by high rates.

Growth that does exist is coming from lumpy, lower-margin international projects. The new MENA agreement sustains headline revenue but does not restore the higher-margin North American replacement/aftermarket business or the high-margin Road Zipper project cadence. The structural long-term drivers (population growth, water scarcity, food security, and the recurring FieldNET technology transition) are intact and durable, but they are multi-year themes, not FY2026 catalysts. Given that growth prospects are the single most reliable dimension for sorting outcomes and Lindsay's near-term trajectory is falling estimates plus a margin-dilutive revenue mix, this dimension scores below average.

## Valuation Assessment

At $124.23, LNN trades at ~18x trailing EPS of $6.78 and roughly the same on forward (consensus current-year $5.08 implies a ~24x forward multiple, next-year $6.34 implies ~20x). Against peers, the median agricultural/farm-machinery P/E is 32.2 (Deere 32.2, CNH 33.4, Toro 26.7), with AGCO the outlier at 10.7. On a headline basis Lindsay looks cheaper than the large-cap peer set, but the comparison is misleading: Lindsay's earnings are trough-and-falling while the peer multiples reflect their own cyclical positioning, and Lindsay's superior balance sheet and technology mix have historically warranted a premium that is now compressing.

The forward multiple sits on earnings that are still being revised down, so the "low" multiple is a function of a numerator that may keep falling. A stock scored cheap without an independent catalyst has repeatedly proven to be a value trap in this environment, and Lindsay fits that pattern: no dated catalyst, falling estimates, and a margin mix moving the wrong way. The cycle-adjusted case cuts against a high score too; this is not a trough where earnings are inflecting upward (estimates are still declining), so applying normalized mid-cycle earnings power requires believing in a North American demand recovery that has no visible trigger.

What supports the price is downside protection rather than upside asymmetry: a net-cash balance sheet, ~$90M FCF, a $125M remaining buyback authorization being executed aggressively into weakness, and a modest 1.2% dividend. The analyst consensus mean and median both sit at $124, essentially at the current price, with a $113 low and $135 high, a tight band reflecting genuine uncertainty rather than conviction. We assign a below-average valuation score: the multiple offers no clear mispricing to exploit, and the compressed forward multiple reflects real earnings risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Lindsay's competitive position is the stronger part of the story. The U.S. pivot market is a rational four-player oligopoly where competition runs on technology, dealer relationships, service, and seasonal pricing programs rather than destructive price wars. The Zimmatic brand is well-established, and the FieldNET recurring-technology layer builds switching costs and a subscription revenue stream that should grow structurally regardless of the equipment cycle. In infrastructure, Road Zipper is a differentiated, low-competition franchise that management explicitly describes as having limited competition, the key margin driver, albeit project-lumpy.

The trajectory, however, is mixed rather than clearly improving. North American irrigation share appears stable, but the segment is shrinking with the cycle. The international mix shift toward large MENA projects is dilutive to margins and concentrated in a single geography with geopolitical exposure. The global manufacturing footprint provides real tariff/currency optionality that peers with more concentrated footprints lack. On balance the moat is stable-to-modestly-favorable (durable oligopoly plus a genuine near-monopoly product) but not visibly widening at present, which places this dimension slightly above neutral. Competitive position has been a softer signal recently, so we confirm it against the growth and financial-health picture rather than leaning on it heavily.

## Risk Assessment

**Agricultural cyclicality and North American demand** is the dominant near-term risk, and it is only partly priced. Demand hinges on commodity prices and net farm income, both currently unfavorable, and the timing of a recovery is unknown.

**Customer and project concentration** is material: the 13% MENA customer concentration means the growth engine now depends on a single geography with lumpy, margin-dilutive projects. Management flags the Middle East conflict as a potential disruptor to MENA delivery timing. If a large project is not followed by another, revenue can drop sharply, as the Q2 collapse demonstrated.

**Infrastructure product liability (X-Lite end terminal)** is a persistent tail risk. After the DOJ dismissed a federal False Claims Act suit in 2023 as meritless, the same relator re-filed near-identical state-level claims in Tennessee and California (served June 2024 and June 2025). A separate Missouri DOT suit seeks punitive damages. Lindsay has taken no accrual (loss not deemed probable) but acknowledges a reasonably possible, unestimable loss. Insurance is in place, but the reputational and qualified-products-list implications are hard to quantify.

**Government funding dependence** (IIJA through Sept 2026, reauthorization uncertain) and **environmental remediation** at the Lindsay, NE facility ($10.6M accrued, EPA plan not finalized, could exceed accrual) are secondary but real.

On the positive side, the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA tariff ruling creates uncertain refund optionality on previously paid tariffs. Because several of these risks (concentration reversing, litigation tail, funding cliff) are specific catalysts for downside not fully reflected in the multiple, we score risk factors below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. One-month ATM implied volatility is 54.4% against 29.0% one-year historical volatility (a rich 25.4-point spread, 82nd percentile within the chain), with the term structure in backwardation (54.4% near-term versus 33.0% at six months), consistent with the market expecting an imminent event, most plausibly the next earnings report. Put/call skew of 4.0% shows elevated put demand, and the put/call open-interest ratio jumped to 1.36 from near zero in the latest snapshot. The implied one-month move is ±9.2%. For a stock we rate HOLD, this confirms the market's caution and argues against adding size ahead of the print; it also means options are expensive to buy as protection. Short interest is moderate at 8.9% of float (3.4 days to cover), not a crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$150).** North American commodity prices recover and farmer sentiment improves, restoring high-margin replacement and aftermarket irrigation demand. Bonus depreciation (restored under OBBBA) and eventual IIJA reauthorization support equipment purchases and infrastructure demand. Road Zipper project cadence normalizes, and the MENA project stream continues to backfill international. FieldNET recurring revenue compounds, gradually improving margin quality. The net-cash balance sheet and aggressive buyback shrink the share count meaningfully at depressed prices, amplifying the earnings recovery. On a normalized ~$7.00 EPS at a peer-justified ~21x, the stock approaches $150.

**Bear Case (~$95).** The North American ag downturn persists into FY2027, MENA projects fail to renew after the current $80M agreement, and the international mix keeps margins compressed. Estimates continue to fall, the X-Lite litigation produces an adverse ruling, and the stock re-rates to a trough multiple on trough earnings. At ~$4.50 depressed EPS on ~21x, or a balance-sheet floor near tangible support, the stock revisits the 52-week low around $97, roughly 22% below current.

**Base Case (~$127).** The cycle stays soft through calendar 2026 with revisions stabilizing rather than recovering. Buybacks and the dividend support the price, the balance sheet caps downside, and the stock trades in a range around current levels until a demand catalyst emerges. EPS recovers modestly toward the $6.34 next-year consensus, supporting ~20x, roughly $127.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q3 FY2026 earnings report (expected early July 2026), followed by fiscal year-end results in fall 2026. Given the options-market pricing of an imminent event, the near-term print is decisive for range-bound-versus-breakdown.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive EPS estimate-revision velocity for two consecutive prints (reversing the current -21% current-year trend), particularly evidence that North American irrigation orders have inflected.
- Confirmation that MENA project revenue is being replaced by higher-margin domestic replacement/aftermarket demand, restoring consolidated gross margin toward the low-30s.
- A durable improvement in commodity prices or farmer sentiment that USDA net-farm-income data corroborates.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Continued estimate cuts through fiscal Q3/Q4, indicating the trough is deeper than priced.
- An adverse X-Lite litigation development requiring an accrual, or a material environmental cost above the $10.6M reserve.
- Evidence the MENA project stream is not renewing, removing the only source of headline growth while North America stays weak.

## Conclusion

Lindsay is a high-quality business at an awkward point in its cycle. The balance sheet is strong, the competitive position is genuinely defensible, and the long-term water-scarcity thesis is real. But the near-term earnings picture is deteriorating, the growth that exists is lower-margin and concentration-heavy, and consensus estimates have been cut sharply over the past 90 days. At ~18x trailing on trough-and-falling earnings, the valuation offers downside protection from the balance sheet and buyback but no clear upside asymmetry, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating.

The score trajectory, from a BUY at 5.7 in March down to a stable 5.5 HOLD, reflects a business whose financial health has strengthened (buybacks, net cash) even as growth and risk have deteriorated (falling estimates, concentration, litigation). New data confirms rather than reverses that trend: the Q2 miss and the 90-day estimate cuts validate the cautious stance. We reaffirm HOLD; the evidence does not support a confident call in either direction until the ag cycle turns or revisions turn positive. Long-term investors can hold for the structural thesis and buyback support; new capital has no reason to rush in ahead of a soft print with the options market pricing a ±9% move.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the trailing P/E was overstated as ~23x when $124.23 / $6.78 EPS is ~18x, making the stock somewhat cheaper on trailing earnings than the report characterized (though the forward multiple on falling estimates remains the more relevant, and less flattering, figure).
