## Executive Summary

Tripadvisor enters the second half of 2026 as two businesses moving in opposite directions with a large pile of cash arriving between them. Continuing-operations revenue fell 7% in both Q2 and H1 2026, but adjusted EBITDA fell 21% and 35% respectively, because the profit mix is inverted: Hotels & Other, whose revenue is declining 20-23% year over year, generates roughly 68% of trailing continuing segment profit, while Experiences (Viator), at 63% of continuing revenue, generates about 32% at an H1 segment margin of 2.5%. Every dollar of revenue that shifts from the declining segment to the growing one currently reduces group profit. That is the central fact in the equity.

The offsetting fact is the balance sheet. The $345M of 0.25% convertible notes were repaid in full with cash on April 1, 2026, leaving a single $836M floating-rate term loan at 6.39% maturing in 2031 with no financial covenants and an undrawn $500M revolver. On August 1, 2026 Tripadvisor exercised its put and on August 2 signed a definitive equity purchase agreement to sell TheFork to American Express Travel for $700M cash, closing expected by year-end. Against $843M of continuing cash, pro forma net cash is roughly $690M before adjusting for seasonal merchant float and undisclosed cash taxes on an estimated ~$440M book gain; a conservative adjustment for both puts it nearer $430-520M, or 34-41% of the market capitalization. Enterprise value on that basis is roughly $740-830M against TTM continuing adjusted EBITDA of approximately $248M, about 3.0-3.4x.

We are not treating that multiple as a buy signal. It is low because consensus expects the denominator to shrink, and the H1 evidence supports them: Hotels metasearch revenue is down 23% with cost-per-click *rising*, which is the signature of lost distribution rather than lost pricing; Booking and Expedia together are ~21% of consolidated revenue concentrated almost entirely in that segment, and both have their own incentive to reduce metasearch dependence; and Q3 guidance of 5-7% Experiences bookings growth is below H1's 7% pace. Three of the last four quarters missed consensus. Our probability-weighted 12-month value is roughly $11.80 (+9%) against a bear case of $8.25 (-23%): positive but not enough, at this confidence level, to justify new capital. **HOLD**, with genuine interest below $9.

## Company Overview

Following the reclassification of TheFork as a discontinued operation on June 14, 2026, Tripadvisor reports two continuing segments:

**Experiences** — Viator, described in filings as a pure-play experiences online travel agency, plus Tripadvisor-branded experiences points of sale. Both run on Viator's centralized supply platform (operator onboarding, inventory, bookings, payments, fraud prevention, support). Tripadvisor also syndicates its experiences supply to third-party endemic and non-endemic demand partners, a wholesale channel distinct from its owned consumer funnel.

**Hotels and Other** — the legacy Tripadvisor guidance platform, including hotel metasearch (click-based referrals to OTAs and hotels), media/display advertising, and other lines (cruise, dining affiliate commissions, B2B restaurant tools).

The company is deliberately narrowing. As of the start of 2026 it no longer offers vacation rentals, flights or rental cars. Stated strategy is to grow and scale the Experiences marketplace while "optimizing legacy offerings within the Hotels and Other segment for profitability." Continuing-operations headcount is approximately 1,605 (2,465 total less ~860 at TheFork), with 70% of total employees based in Europe.

Governance changed materially in April 2025 when the Liberty TripAdvisor merger eliminated the dual-class structure, retired ~53.1M treasury shares and cancelled Class B stock. There is no controlling stockholder. Separately, filings disclose $3.3M of shareholder-activism costs expensed in Q1 2026, an amendment to the adjusted EBITDA definition specifically to add back "significant shareholder activism" costs, a March 23, 2026 8-K carrying a material definitive agreement, a director change and a bylaw amendment simultaneously, and two directors (Carl Sparks, Laura Bisesto) who held zero shares before the June 29, 2026 annual grant. The occurrence of activism and a probable negotiated board settlement is well supported; the identity, size and terms of any stake are not verified in any source we reviewed, and we do not build the thesis on them.

The mechanism behind the 35% H1 EBITDA decline on a 7% revenue decline is marketing intensity in the growth segment. Experiences segment marketing was $170.7M on $278.6M of revenue in Q2 (61.3%) versus 56.2% a year earlier, and 64.8% versus 60.6% for H1. Bookings grew 5% and gross booking value 3% in Q2 while marketing spend grew 12%. Take rate is roughly stable — Experiences revenue over GBV was approximately 19.9% in Q2 and 17.2% in H1 versus ~17.5% prior year (GBV is disclosed rounded to $0.1B, so precision here is about ±1pp) — which locates the problem in customer acquisition cost, not commission compression. That is a more fixable problem in principle, and management explicitly frames the spend as buying share. So far it is a losing exchange rate.

On the other side, the Hotels & Other decline is volume-driven. MD&A states hotel metasearch revenue fell on lower click volumes from free and paid channels, which "more than offset an increase in pricing as measured in cost-per-click rates." Rising CPCs on falling volumes means the remaining traffic is higher-intent but the pool is shrinking.

**Reconstructing the profit split.** FY2025 total adjusted EBITDA was ~$318.7M, of which TheFork contributed ~$20.4M, implying ~$298.3M continuing. Less recast H1 2025 continuing EBITDA of $143.5M gives ~$154.8M for H2 2025; adding H1 2026's $92.8M yields TTM continuing adjusted EBITDA of approximately **$248M**. The same method by segment gives Hotels & Other ≈ $168M (FY25 ~$207M − H1 25 $120.1M + H1 26 $81.6M) and Experiences ≈ $81M, summing to $249M, within 0.6% of the top-down figure. These are derived estimates, not disclosed figures; the company has not published a full-year recast continuing-operations segment table and stranded-cost reallocation could shift the split modestly.

**Cost actions are real.** H1 personnel expense including stock compensation fell $43.3M (−17%), with SBC down from $51.5M to $35.3M (−31%) on lower 2026 grant values. Hotels & Other operating expenses fell $42.5M. The Q4 2025 restructuring accrual ran from $18.5M to $5.3M with $20.1M of payments; actions should be substantially complete by Q4 2026.

**Cash flow requires a float adjustment.** H1 continuing operating cash flow was $252.4M against $22.5M of capex, but $183.0M of that came from an increase in deferred merchant payables — traveler cash not yet remitted to operators. The 10-Q states the pattern reverses in H2, particularly Q3. Stripping working capital entirely, H1 cash generation is roughly adjusted EBITDA $92.8M less cash interest $28.6M less cash taxes $4.4M less capex $22.5M ≈ **$37M**. Anyone annualizing the headline figure is overstating capacity by an order of magnitude. Two further notes: H1 cash taxes of $4.4M were unusually low versus $15.5M prior year, and growing adoption of "Reserve Now, Pay Later" would reduce the float benefit over time.

**Balance sheet (June 30, 2026):** cash $843.2M continuing plus $51.9M inside held-for-sale assets; Term Loan B $836.0M principal at SOFR+2.75% (6.39%), 1% annual amortization, no financial covenants, 2031 maturity, carried at $824.4M and marked at $801.5M (~96, not distressed pricing); $500M revolver undrawn with $496.4M available to 2028 under a maximum net leverage covenant, in compliance; finance leases $38.8M; goodwill $724.1M continuing; equity $662.5M. Short-term debt fell from $353.2M to $8.5M with the April 1 convertible repayment, and the associated capped calls expired unexercised.

A mechanical Altman Z-Score built off the December 2025 balance sheet and today's market cap lands near 1.51, nominally distressed. That reading is not credible here: goodwill and deferred tax assets inflate total assets, merchant float inflates current liabilities and suppresses the working-capital term, and a depressed equity value suppresses the market-value term. The company holds nearly as much cash as debt, has no covenants on the term loan, no maturity before 2028, and just retired $345M with cash. The screen is measuring balance-sheet composition and cheapness, not default probability.

**Data conflict to note:** the quote feed reports both trailing and forward P/E at 66.18 against EPS of $0.31, which implies a $20.72 price, near the 52-week high. At the authoritative $10.78 close, trailing P/E on $0.31 is approximately 35x. We treat the 66x figure as stale and use the current price against consensus estimates throughout.

## Growth Analysis

There is no group growth to model at present; there is a decline to size.

**Hotels & Other.** The 10-Q is explicit that SEO traffic acquisition "has been negatively impacted by search engines changing their search result placement and underlying algorithms to increase the prominence of their own products," and that the segment "will continue to be impacted… including AI overviews displacing top ranked links, reduced click-through rates and a shift towards platform based non-traditional search." Hotels revenue fell 23% in Q2 and H1; media/advertising fell 12%. The decision-critical question the filings do not answer is how much of the 20-23% segment decline is external (search/AI) versus self-inflicted: the company cut Hotels & Other marketing 15-19% to optimize for profitability and exited vacation rentals, flights and rental cars at the start of 2026, each of which mechanically reduces revenue. Until that split is disclosed or becomes visible through a stabilization quarter, the terminal value of two-thirds of the profit pool is unresolved.

**Experiences.** The market is genuinely attractive — large, fragmented, mostly still booked offline — and management says so while adding the qualifier that these trends present opportunity "as well as to many competitors." Bookings grew 5% in Q2 with GBV up 3%; Q3 guidance is 5-7% bookings growth, below H1's 7% pace. Growth came "primarily driven through growth on Viator and third-party points of sale, partially offset by SEO-related headwinds in the Tripadvisor point of sale," which means the supply platform and wholesale channel are working while the owned demand funnel weakens. That is a defensible business but structurally lower-margin than a demand-owning marketplace, and it substitutes dependence on demand partners for dependence on search.

**Estimate revisions are negative across every window.** Next-quarter consensus fell 5.6% in 30 days and 2.6% in 90; next-year fell 8.1% and 7.9% to $1.46; the current-year figure fell 41.5% and 43.8% to $0.77, though with only two contributing analysts that series is unreliable and inconsistent with the quarterly stack. We anchor on the 13-analyst next-year $1.46. The earnings record does not inspire confidence in consensus either: 1 beat and 3 misses in four quarters, including −71.4% in Q4 2025 and −67.2% in Q1 2026.

**Our working forecast.** H2 2025 continuing adjusted EBITDA was ~$154.8M. With Hotels & Other still declining, Experiences marketing intensity elevated, and management expecting continued macro impact in H2, a 20-25% H2 decline gives ~$116-124M, putting FY2026 continuing adjusted EBITDA at roughly **$209-217M** versus ~$298M in FY2025. For FY2027 we assume Hotels & Other revenue decline decelerating to 12-15% at ~25% margins (segment EBITDA $130-145M) and Experiences revenue up 5-8% with margin recovering to 8-10% as marketing normalizes ($75-95M), approximately **$205-240M**, roughly flat. Adjusted EBITDA excludes SBC, which annualizes near $71M continuing, or ~29% of trailing EBITDA.

## Valuation Assessment

**Pro forma capital position.** Cash $843.2M plus $700M gross less $17.5M mid-point transaction costs = $1,525.7M against $836.0M of term loan principal, giving headline net cash of ~$690M. Two haircuts are required. First, $484.2M of the June 30 cash is matched by deferred merchant payables owed to operators, and June 30 sits near the seasonal peak of that float (December 31, 2025: $308.1M); reducing net cash by the $176.1M seasonal differential gives ~$514M. Second, cash taxes on an estimated ~$440M book gain (700 − 223.1 net assets − 19.0 AOCI − 17.5 costs) are not disclosed; a $50-150M range is plausible, taking float-adjusted net cash to roughly **$430-490M**.

At $10.78, market capitalization is $1,262M, so enterprise value is approximately **$772-832M**, about **3.1-3.4x** TTM continuing adjusted EBITDA of ~$248M, or **3.6-3.9x** our FY2026E of ~$213M. On earnings, $10.78 against next-year consensus of $1.46 is **7.4x**, versus a peer median P/E of 10.9x.

The peer set is the most useful context: Yelp 10.4x, QuinStreet 10.9x, ZoomInfo 6.7x, Cars.com 22.0x, Shutterstock loss-making with a 10.5% yield, and drawdowns from 52-week highs of −14% to −73%. The entire small-cap interactive-media cohort trades at single-digit multiples because the market is pricing search and AI disintermediation of ad- and referral-driven models. Tripadvisor's discount to that cohort is therefore not evidence of a discrete mispricing; it is a within-cohort ranking of terminal-value risk, and TRIP's two-counterparty concentration in the profit segment justifies sitting at the low end.

**Scenario framework (12 months):**

- **Bear — $8.25 (−23%).** Hotels & Other decline persists at 18-20% or accelerates as AI answer surfaces mature and Booking/Expedia trim metasearch spend; Experiences margin does not inflect. FY2027 continuing EBITDA ~$165M at 3.5x = $578M EV, plus $400M net cash = $978M equity = $8.35/share. The 52-week low is $9.01 and the lowest analyst target is $9.00.
- **Base — $12.25 (+13.6%).** Hotels decline decelerates toward the low teens as the marketing-cut and vertical-exit comparisons anniversary; Experiences margin recovers partially. FY2027 EBITDA ~$210M at 4.75x = $998M EV, plus $430M net cash = $1,428M = $12.20/share. Modest support from a buyback: the remaining $110M authorization at $10.78 retires ~10.2M shares, 8.7% of the count.
- **Bull — $17.00 (+58%).** Experiences marketing normalizes to FY2025 intensity and segment margin returns toward 10-12%, Hotels stabilizes in single-digit decline, and the board authorizes a materially larger repurchase against the $700M. FY2027 EBITDA ~$250M at 6x = $1,500M EV plus $480M net cash = $1,980M ≈ $16.90/share.

Probability-weighting 35% bear / 45% base / 20% bull gives $11.80, a **+9.5%** expected return. Analyst consensus mean is $14.43 and median $15.00 with a $9-$21 range, but the sentiment trend is degrading and estimates are falling in every window, so we do not treat the consensus target as a floor here; that treatment applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates, which this is not.

**Dilution.** Reported diluted shares were 117.884M in Q2. Unvested RSUs of 10.464M (weighted-average grant price $15.14) plus target PSUs of 1.762M equal 12.226M, or 10.4% of shares outstanding and 9.5% post-issuance ownership dilution if fully issued. These are contingent — PSUs require Compensation Committee target achievement on a zero-to-200% range, and all 34,000 market-based RSUs were cancelled in H1 2026 for non-attainment. The 1.825M options at a $29.33 average strike are far out of the money and irrelevant to realistic scenarios. Grant value is falling while grants are struck at depressed prices, so share-count dilution per compensation dollar is rising: H1 2026 grants of 4.459M RSUs alone equal 3.8% of shares outstanding.

## Competitive Landscape

Tripadvisor's legacy franchise sits between a search gatekeeper upstream and two large OTA buyers downstream, and both ends are compressing. Google's shift toward self-preferencing and AI overviews reduces the free traffic that Hotels & Other converts into paid referrals; Booking and Expedia, at approximately 21% of consolidated revenue concentrated almost entirely in that segment, are simultaneously customers and competitors with structural incentive to reduce metasearch dependence. Deriving from disclosed figures: if FY2025 continuing revenue was ~$1,670M and Hotels & Other was ~$751M, then roughly $397M of Booking/Expedia revenue equals about half of the segment. Both figures are estimates, not disclosures, but the direction is clear, and mix shift toward Experiences does not reduce it. The disclosed concentration percentage has not improved, and because Hotels & Other's share of profit (68%) far exceeds its share of revenue (37%), the profit pool's dependence on those two counterparties is more acute than the headline suggests.

In Experiences, Viator's genuine asset is supply-side infrastructure plus a wholesale syndication channel. Its problem is that it competes for the same paid acquisition channels as GetYourGuide, Klook, Airbnb Experiences and the OTAs, and the H1 numbers show CAC rising faster than bookings. Rising reliance on third-party points of sale for growth is evidence the supply platform is winning share of supply while the owned brand funnel weakens.

The proxy's pay-versus-performance table quantifies the outcome: cumulative indexed TSR of $50.59 against a peer group at $138.27 over five years. Whatever is wrong is company- and sub-segment-specific, not sector-wide.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Search/AI disintermediation of the profit engine.** Highest-consequence risk and partially realized. Determines whether Hotels & Other's ~$168M of TTM segment profit is worth 4x or 2x. The unresolved external-versus-self-inflicted split is what makes this un-underwritable at present.

**2. Counterparty concentration.** Booking plus Expedia ≈21% of consolidated revenue, nearly all in Hotels & Other. A contract or bidding change at either would hit the profit pool directly.

**3. Experiences margins may not scale.** H1 segment margin of 2.5% with marketing at ~65% of segment revenue. If Viator's CAC is structurally elevated by competition for the same channels, the company is trading a high-margin declining asset for a low-margin growing one.

**4. Macro and event sensitivity.** The 10-Q attributes H1 weakness to Middle East conflict, Hawaii flooding, civil unrest in Mexico, and European/US weather in May-June, with effects including higher cancellation rates across all points of sale and continued impact expected in H2. Because Experiences revenue is recognized largely at delivery, destination-level disruption flows through with little offset.

**5. Tax contingencies.** A January 2021 HMRC closure notice for 2012-2016 intercompany financing deductions could add an estimated $20-30M of income tax expense excluding interest; HMRC is also auditing 2017-2023 and the company warns of potentially significant additional liabilities; accrued interest on unrecognized tax benefits is $25.9M; the IRS is examining 2018. Separately, cash taxes on the ~$440M TheFork gain are undisclosed and material to pro forma net cash.

**6. Transaction completion.** Closing conditions include regulatory and antitrust clearance, plus system-segregation costs. Deal risk is materially lower than a month ago — the put has been exercised, the French works council consultation completed July 30, and a definitive equity purchase agreement was signed August 2 — but it is not zero, and Tripadvisor is the transition-services *provider*, which carries execution cost.

**7. Q3 optics.** Q3 is the quarter in which merchant-payable float reverses. Headline operating cash flow will look materially worse than H1's $252.4M regardless of operating performance, and investors who annualized H1 may react to it.

**8. Short interest.** 33.3% of float short, 8.1 days to cover. This cuts both ways: it reflects deep structural skepticism, and it makes any positive catalyst — a large new repurchase authorization, a stabilization quarter in Hotels — mechanically violent to the upside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 47.8% against 59.1% one-year realized, a −11.4-point spread at the 12th percentile of the current chain, with the nearest expiry pricing only a ±5.6% move. That is post-event vol crush: the ~24% single-day decline on August 6 has already happened and the market is not pricing another near-term shock. Term structure is in contango, 47.8% near versus 59.2% at six months, with the December tenor pricing a ±27.4% move: uncertainty is located in the outer months where the TheFork close, capital-allocation decision and two more prints land, not in the next two weeks.

Put/call open interest of 3.13 is high, and the nearest tenor carries a put/call skew the data source characterizes as elevated put demand. With 33% of float short, some portion of that put open interest is likely hedging or synthetic structure rather than outright directional bearishness, so we do not read it as independent confirmation of the bear case. Longer-dated skew values are negative, but without an explicit sign convention we draw no directional inference from them. The actionable takeaway: cheap near-dated volatility makes options a more efficient way to express either side of the December catalyst than the common stock is at this price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$17.00, +58%)

The market is capitalizing a trough. Adjusted EBITDA of ~$248M TTM is being valued at roughly 3x enterprise value while $430-490M of float-adjusted net cash — 34-39% of market capitalization — sits on the balance sheet with $700M of it arriving by year-end. Two of the three drags are self-inflicted and reversible: Hotels & Other revenue is down partly because the company deliberately cut its marketing 15-19% and exited three verticals, and Experiences margin is down because management chose to spend 65% of segment revenue on acquisition "in order to drive revenue growth and increase market share." Dial marketing intensity back to FY2025 levels and Experiences margin returns toward double digits on a revenue base 5% larger. Take rate is stable at ~17-20%, so there is no pricing erosion to fix.

The capital structure has been simplified to a single covenant-free term loan due 2031, the convertible overhang is retired, there is no controlling stockholder, activism has produced board additions, and management has stated the proceeds go to debt paydown and/or repurchase. Retiring $400M of term loan saves ~$25.6M of annual pre-tax interest against H1 operating income of $15.0M. The remaining $110M authorization alone retires 8.7% of the shares at this price; a larger authorization against $700M could retire far more, into a 33% short position.

### Bear Case (~$8.25, −23%)

Two-thirds of the profit comes from a business losing distribution to the platform that supplied it, and the two customers who monetize that distribution are competitors. Cost-per-click is rising while volume falls, which is what a shrinking, higher-intent residual looks like on the way down. AI answer surfaces are early in deployment, so the traffic decline has further to run, and the company's counter of "AI-enabled" first-party data is an unquantified assertion with no engagement, retention or repeat-rate metric in the filings to test it.

The replacement business does not yet earn its cost of capital: 2.5% H1 segment margin, marketing up 12% for 5% bookings growth, Q3 guided below the H1 pace, and growth increasingly sourced from third-party demand partners rather than owned brand. Adjusted EBITDA excludes ~$71M of annualized SBC, which is 29% of the measure; after SBC, capex and interest, normalized free cash generation is a fraction of the headline. Consensus has missed three of four quarters and is being cut in every window. Five-year indexed TSR of $50.59 against a peer group at $138.27, with 2025 compensation actually paid at its five-year high in the year TSR bottomed, is not the profile of a management team the market will pay a re-rating multiple to. And nothing forces capital return: zero shares were repurchased in H1 2026 with $110M authorized and the stock as low as $9.60.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders have a covenant-free balance sheet, a definitive $700M cash inflow, and a 3x enterprise multiple supporting the position. Prospective investors have no urgency: the expected return of roughly +9% does not compensate for an unresolved terminal-value question on two-thirds of the profit pool, and the entry improves materially at $9.

- **Base case: $12.25 (+13.6%)** — 4.75x FY2027E continuing adjusted EBITDA of $210M = $998M EV, plus $430M float- and tax-adjusted net cash, ÷ 117.1M shares.
- **Bull case: $17.00 (+58%)** — 6x $250M EBITDA plus $480M net cash, on Experiences margin inflection and a materially larger repurchase authorization.
- **Bear case: $8.25 (−23%)** — 3.5x $165M EBITDA plus $400M net cash, on continued 18-20% Hotels decline and no Experiences margin recovery.
- **Timeframe:** 9-12 months, anchored on three dated events — Q3 2026 results (early November, the first quarter showing float reversal and a clean read on Hotels trajectory), the TheFork close by year-end 2026, and the capital-allocation decision that follows it.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Experiences segment adjusted EBITDA margin above 11% for a full year, or segment marketing falling below 60% of segment revenue for two consecutive quarters, with bookings growth maintained at 5%+.
2. Hotels & Other revenue decline decelerating to single digits year over year, with management disaggregating how much of the prior decline was the deliberate marketing cut and vertical exits.
3. A new repurchase authorization of $300M or more announced alongside the TheFork close, with execution disclosed at prices near current levels.
4. Price at or below $9.00 with the above operating trajectory unchanged — at that level the float-adjusted net cash approaches 45% of market capitalization and the bear case is largely in the price.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Hotels & Other revenue decline accelerating past 25% year over year, or any disclosure of reduced spend or changed terms from Booking or Expedia.
2. Experiences segment margin failing to exceed the prior year in the seasonally strong Q3, or Q4 bookings guidance below 5%.
3. TheFork proceeds deployed neither to debt reduction nor repurchase — for example, into an acquisition — or disclosed cash taxes on the gain exceeding ~$150M.
4. HMRC extending its 2012-2016 position across 2017-2023 with a quantified assessment materially above the disclosed $20-30M range.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings (expected early November 2026), or immediately on an 8-K disclosing the TheFork close, a new repurchase authorization, or a Schedule 13D.

## Conclusion

The score moves from 5.4 to 4.8, reversing the improving trajectory of the prior two reviews, and the reversal is grounded in the income statement rather than the tape. Since the June review, adjusted EBITDA declined 21% in Q2 and 35% in H1, the growth segment's margin compressed ~290bp with marketing rising to ~65% of its revenue, Q3 bookings guidance came in below the H1 pace, and next-year consensus was cut 8%. The drawdown from the $20.16 high, roughly 46% to the $10.78 close, is not a case of price detaching from fundamentals; the fundamentals moved first.

What improved is certainty on the balance sheet: the put is exercised, the definitive purchase agreement is signed, and $700M of cash is now a contractual expectation rather than a process. That takes float- and tax-adjusted net cash to roughly $430-490M against a $1,262M market capitalization and leaves enterprise value near 3.1-3.4x trailing continuing adjusted EBITDA, with no covenants and no maturity before 2028.

Those two facts do not resolve into a recommendation stronger than HOLD, because the thing that would resolve them — evidence on whether Hotels & Other's 20% revenue decline is mostly a deliberate optimization or mostly lost distribution — is precisely what the filings decline to disaggregate. A 3x multiple on a shrinking denominator is not automatically cheap, and the entire small-cap interactive-media cohort trades at single-digit earnings multiples for the same reason. New capital should wait for a stabilization quarter in Hotels, an Experiences margin inflection, or a price near $9. Existing holders retain real optionality from the balance sheet and a possible large repurchase into a 33% short position.
