## Executive Summary

Capitol Federal Financial has delivered the earnings recovery its balance sheet made available. Net interest margin reached 2.31% in the June 2026 quarter against 1.98% a year earlier; the efficiency ratio improved to 52.10% from 58.26%; net income of $23.6 million and diluted EPS of $0.19 were both quarterly records for the post-restructuring era. Nine-month FY2026 EPS of $0.51 compares with a full-year FY2025 result of $0.52. The mechanism is identifiable and verifiable in the filings: weighted-average deposit cost fell to 2.09% from 2.26% as a $2.9 billion CD book repriced down; $425 million of FHLB advances at a 4.32% effective rate were prepaid and replaced at 3.79%, with $250 million more simply allowed to mature; and $357 million of commercial loan growth was funded by $302 million of residential runoff, a rotation that swaps 3.77% assets for 5.98% assets.

That progress is now in the price. The stock has risen 55.5% over twelve months and sits 4.1% below its 52-week high of $9.26. At $8.88 it trades at 1.10x reported tangible book value of $8.04, 12.9x current-year consensus of $0.69 and 11.4x next-year consensus of $0.78. The company's own Q3 annualized ROE was 9.21%. Against a cost of equity in the 9–10% range, 1.10x book is approximately fair. A franchise earning its cost of capital is worth roughly book value, and the premium has to be earned by the ROE going higher. Consensus assumes it will, and estimates are moving the right way (+7.6% on FY2027 over 30 days). We think the estimates are achievable. We do not think a 12x multiple on them creates asymmetry from here.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE is the composition of the risk. Hotel loans total $640.5 million, equal to 62.7% of stockholders' equity; hotel plus senior housing equals 115.7% of equity; the single largest loan is $86.0 million against an Arizona hotel (8.4% of equity); the largest C&I relationship is $84.4 million gross, roughly 26% of that portfolio. The allowance for credit losses is 0.32% of loans and covers 50.9% of nonaccruals. Nonaccruals of $51.3 million include $40.5 million that is contractually current, and the two-loan CRE relationship driving most of the CRE nonaccrual balance received a lower appraisal in the March quarter, a $4.0 million specific valuation allowance, and a 13-month payment delay in the June quarter. Weighted-average cost-to-sell adjustments on individually evaluated commercial collateral rose to 26% from 10% year over year. None of this is yet a loss: ten-year cumulative net charge-offs are $1.2 million and classified assets fell 32% since September. But loss absorption for any commercial deterioration comes out of future earnings, and future earnings are what the current multiple capitalizes.

## Company Overview

Capitol Federal is a Maryland-incorporated savings and loan holding company whose sole bank subsidiary, Capitol Federal Savings Bank, operates 46 branches across nine Kansas and three Missouri counties from Topeka. The Bank is OCC-regulated; the holding company is supervised by the Federal Reserve. Total assets were $9.662 billion at 6/30/2026, down from $9.779 billion at 9/30/2025.

The franchise's distinguishing feature is deposit density and cost. The Bank held the #2 deposit share position in Kansas at 6.2% per the FDIC's June 30, 2025 Summary of Deposits, with average deposits per traditional branch of approximately $128.5 million, roughly double what many community banks carry, and the arithmetic underneath a 52% efficiency ratio at a bank with a sub-2.4% margin. Uninsured deposits were approximately $771.4 million, about 11% of Call Report deposits, of which only ~$645.8 million was commercial/retail. There are no brokered CDs, no repos and no discount-window borrowings.

The strategy is an explicit transition from portfolio residential thrift to commercial bank. The correspondent one-to-four-family purchase channel was suspended in June 2024, putting the $5.60 billion residential book into managed runoff. Three build-outs run in parallel: commercial lending (CRE, commercial construction, C&I, including out-of-footprint participations); treasury management and small-business deposits; and private banking, trust and wealth management, where the June quarter set a record AUM level (dollar amount undisclosed). Commercial loans reached 30.2% of the portfolio from roughly 26% at fiscal year-end.

A regulatory ceiling shapes the whole exercise. Crossing $10 billion in regulatory assets for four consecutive quarter-ends triggers direct CFPB supervision and Durbin interchange caps. Total assets have declined while loans grew, which is consistent with deliberate management under that line. Commercial growth therefore has to be funded by residential runoff and balance-sheet contraction rather than expansion, which caps how fast the earnings mix can improve.

Nine-month FY2026 net income was $64.0 million on a 2.25% NIM (versus 1.92%) and a 52.72% efficiency ratio (versus 58.89%). Q3 annualized ROA was 0.97% and ROE 9.21%. Trailing four-quarter EPS is $0.65 ($0.14 + $0.16 + $0.16 + $0.19), which reconciles to the quoted 13.5x trailing multiple.

Two quality adjustments matter. The June quarter included a $433 thousand provision *release* (an ACL model regression update, mostly commercial construction) and elevated BOLI income of $1.856 million following a $45.0 million BOLI purchase in the March quarter plus death benefits. Stripping roughly $0.9 million of above-trend BOLI and the release gives a normalized run rate near $0.18 per quarter, or $0.72 annualized, about 12.3x at $8.88. The effective tax rate of roughly 19.4% is genuinely low and largely structural (BOLI, tax credits), so it should not be normalized away.

**Capital.** Stockholders' equity was $1.0213 billion, 10.6% of assets. Reported tangible book value per share is $8.04; equity divided by 125.858 million shares gives $8.11, a $0.07 variance we could not reconcile from the disclosures and which does not change the ~1.10x P/TBV conclusion. Regulatory capital is more nuanced than the headline: the Bank's community bank leverage ratio is 9.6% against a >9% requirement, roughly 60 basis points or about $58 million of cushion on a ~$9.6 billion asset base, while Bank risk-based Tier 1 is a comfortable 15.8%. Leverage, not risk weighting, is the binding constraint, expected for a book dominated by 50%-risk-weighted residential mortgages, but it does mean the capital-return engine has less room than the risk-based ratio implies.

Equity composition breaks down as follows: additional paid-in capital of $1,096.3 million, unearned ESOP compensation of −$23.5 million, an accumulated deficit of −$60.8 million, and AOCI of *positive* $8.1 million. Since the December 2010 second-step conversion the company has returned $2.09 billion — $1.60 billion in dividends and $485.8 million in repurchases — against a current market capitalization of roughly $1.12 billion. Almost all remaining book equity is contributed conversion capital.

**Securities and AOCI.** This is a real differentiator. AFS securities of $783.6 million carry an amortized cost of $774.8 million, an $8.8 million net unrealized *gain*, at a 5.42% yield and 3.4-year weighted-average life. The October 2023 restructuring that produced the FY2023 loss of $101.7 million (EPS −$0.76) removed the AOCI hole that still burdens many thrifts. CFFN's duration risk sits in the loan book, not the bond book.

**Liquidity.** $4.22 billion of available liquidity against ~$771 million of uninsured deposits is roughly 5.5x coverage; $687.6 million of unencumbered securities remain eligible but unpledged; FHLB usage is near 17% of assets against 44% approved capacity. The offsetting fact is a loans-to-deposits ratio near 119% ($8.167 billion net loans against $6.851 billion deposits), which is why $1.636 billion of FHLB advances remain a structural funding leg rather than a swing facility.

**Interest rate risk.** The disclosed sensitivities point in opposite directions and both matter. On a $234.675 million four-quarter net interest income base, +200bp is +1.6% and −200bp is −3.9%; reported earnings are close to rate-neutral over a year. Market value of portfolio equity is not: on a $1.190 billion base, +200bp is −23.9% and +300bp is −35.6%, because 57.6% of loans are fixed-rate residential at 3.57% with a 6.6-year weighted-average life against interest-bearing liabilities with a 2.5-year life. The one-year gap is −11.7% of assets, and the 10-Q discloses that treating all non-maturity deposits as non-core would put it at −41.2%. MVPE at $1.190 billion already exceeds book equity, so a −23.9% shock leaves modeled value near $906 million, below but not far below book.

The practical macro read: reported FY2027 earnings are not very exposed to a parallel rate move, but the deposit-repricing lever that produced most of the margin gain requires short rates to keep drifting down. If the marginal CD reprices up rather than down, that is the single most likely way FY2027 consensus of $0.78 gets missed. **Cash flow and capital allocation.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $56.3 million against $64.0 million of net income; we would not annualize a single quarter of bank operating cash flow, which is dominated by accrual timing. Financing used $164.7 million net, reflecting the $314.5 million borrowing reduction against $259.3 million of deposit growth plus distributions.

The capital-return pattern changed materially this year. FY2025 repurchases were $3.9 million. FY2026 to date: 6,557,422 shares for $47.5 million at a $7.24 average, taking shares outstanding from 132.2 million to 125.7 million, a 4.9% reduction in ten months. Monthly pacing tapered as the price rose (927,964 shares at $7.54 in April; 434,868 at $8.09 in June; only 187,476 at $8.54 in July–early August), which reads as price-sensitive rather than programmatic. $23.6 million of authorization remained at 8/3/2026 under a $75 million Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City non-objection that expires February 2027 and requires annual renewal. The regular dividend is $0.085 quarterly ($0.34 annualized, 3.83% at $8.88), roughly 50% of nine-month EPS; a $0.040 special was paid in January 2026, well below the historical special-dividend cadence implied by CY2024/CY2025 totals of $0.340. Reallocating from specials to buybacks below book value was the right decision.

One constraint deserves emphasis because no screen surfaces it. Holding-company cash at the Bank was only $10.7 million at 6/30/2026. Dividends and buybacks depend on a continuous drip of Bank upstreaming ($78.0 million in nine months, $29.0 million more through 8/3, at least $34.0 million targeted for the September quarter), which in turn depends on the Bank maintaining positive tax accumulated earnings and profits — the pre-1988 bad debt reserve of $75.9 million represents a $15.9 million unrecorded deferred tax liability, and FY2024 distributions were deemed drawn from those reserves, triggering incremental tax. The Bank restored positive tax E&P in Q4 FY2025 and management states FY2026 distributions will be limited to preserve that position.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is margin and mix, not volume. Management's guidance in the 10-Q is commercial net growth of approximately 3% in the September quarter and roughly 20% for FY2026, with *total* loans up approximately 1%. Assets are shrinking. The $10 billion threshold caps the balance sheet, so every dollar of commercial growth must displace a dollar of something else.

The rotation math is favorable but bounded. Commercial loans yield 5.98% against 3.77% for residential, a 221 basis point spread. The nine-month pace of $357 million of commercial growth annualizes near $475 million; at portfolio-average spreads that is roughly $10.5 million of incremental annual pre-tax interest income, or about $0.06–$0.07 per share after tax. This is a derived figure and holds only if incremental spread matches portfolio-average spread, which it will not do exactly, and it ignores funding-cost changes. Add roughly 2–3 percentage points of annual share count reduction from buybacks, and the path from a normalized $0.72 run rate to consensus $0.78 in FY2027 is arithmetically straightforward without heroic assumptions.

Estimate revisions confirm this: FY2026 consensus moved to $0.69 from $0.66 (+3.8% over both 30 and 90 days), FY2027 to $0.78 from $0.72 (+7.6% in 30 days) and from $0.73 (+5.4% in 90). The revision direction is right, but the magnitude is a mid-single-digit re-rate of a modest earnings stream rather than a 30%-plus revision cycle. The earnings surprise record is similarly modest: two beats, one in-line, one miss over four quarters, with absolute surprise magnitudes of one cent or less on a $0.14–$0.19 base, which makes percentage surprise figures noisy.

The durable growth question is what happens after the CD book finishes repricing. Deposit cost has fallen 17 basis points in nine months; the CD book is down to $2.889 billion at 3.48% from $3.013 billion at 3.74%. That gap narrows as it closes. From there, NIM expansion depends almost entirely on asset mix, which is capped by the $10 billion ceiling and by how fast residential runs off. At 2.31%, CFFN's margin remains well below the 3.0–3.5% typical of diversified regional banks, and 0.97% ROA / 9.21% ROE remain below what a commercial bank of this size earns. That gap is simultaneously the bull case (room to improve) and the structural bear case (a residential-heavy thrift balance sheet cannot get there without years of rotation).

Peer comparables (SBSI 14.4x, CPF 12.1x, HFWA 14.2x, PFBC 9.4x, TRST 16.1x) show a 9.4–16.1x band with a 14.2x median. On headline P/E, CFFN sits at a modest discount with a higher yield. But the discount is earned rather than anomalous: a 9.2% ROE against a 9–10% cost of equity supports roughly book value, and the peer group generally earns more. The cleanest way to frame it is a residual-income sanity check: at 9.2% ROTCE, 2% growth and a 9.5% cost of equity, warranted P/TBV is about 0.96x, below where the stock trades. At 11% ROTCE, which is where FY2027 consensus and continued buybacks plausibly land, warranted P/TBV is about 1.20x, or $9.65 on current tangible book. The stock at 1.10x is priced roughly midway through that improvement.

Applying an earnings multiple gets to the same place. A 15% discount to the peer median (justified by the lower ROE and the credit concentration) is 12.0x, which on FY2027 consensus of $0.78 gives **$9.36**. That is our base case. Consensus analyst targets — low $9.00, mean $9.50, high $10.00 — bracket it, and the stock trades below all three. With three covering analysts, a 12x multiple and a 9% ROE, we treat those targets as approximately right rather than as stale floors.

The valuation conclusion: fair, with dividend support. Base upside to $9.36 is +5.4%, plus 3.8% of yield, for a roughly 9% twelve-month total return in the central case. That does not clear an ACCUMULATE bar when the bear case is an 18–19% drawdown driven by a credit event the balance sheet is thinly reserved against. The prior valuation score reduction (6.5 → 5.3) was correct in direction; we hold it approximately there, because the 7.6% upward revision to FY2027 estimates roughly offsets the 5.1% price gain since early July. A low absolute multiple on a sub-cost-of-capital ROE is not the same thing as cheap.

## Competitive Landscape

In-footprint, the franchise is strong and defensible: #2 Kansas deposit share, $128.5 million of deposits per traditional branch, a ten-year cumulative net charge-off total of $1.2 million, and a residential book with a 769 weighted-average credit score at 58% LTV. Kansas is a slow-growth, demographically flat market, which is precisely why the deposit base is sticky and cheap, and also why it cannot supply enough commercial loan growth to move a $9.7 billion balance sheet.

That constraint drives the strategy out of footprint, where the competitive judgment gets harder. Commercial exposure by geography (gross): Kansas $1,006.2 million across 517 loans, Missouri $362.6 million across 121, Texas $250.8 million across 17, Arizona $152.5 million across 6, California $146.1 million across 8, New York $111.7 million across 3. In-footprint lending averages roughly $2 million per loan; New York averages roughly $37 million and Arizona roughly $25 million. Loss severity is concentrated exactly where local knowledge is weakest, and the disclosed edge is process — participation documentation standards, annual financial monitoring above $2.5 million, concentration monitoring by collateral type, geography, tenant brand and relationship — rather than any proprietary origination advantage. Portfolio metrics are respectable (CRE plus construction weighted DSCR 1.69x at 63% LTV; nine-month originations at 70% LTV and 2.22x DSCR), and the new commercial pricing and profitability software is a sensible operational upgrade. A Topeka thrift competing for Arizona hotel paper is a price-taker, not a franchise.

The 10-K names its competitive pressure specifically: larger national and regional banks, credit unions and Farm Credit System lenders — the last two tax-advantaged, which is a permanent margin headwind in Kansas consumer and agriculture-adjacent lending. The wealth management and treasury management build-outs are the more genuinely differentiated growth vectors, since they leverage the existing deposit relationships, but neither is yet sized in the disclosures beyond "record AUM."

Trajectory assessment: stable in-footprint, unproven out-of-footprint, with the residential franchise deliberately shrinking. That combination supports a mid-range score rather than a high one.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit concentration is the dominant company-specific risk.** By collateral type within CRE plus commercial construction (unpaid principal):

| Collateral | UPB | Rate | LTV | DSCR | % of equity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hotel | $640.5M | 6.14% | 55% | 1.43x | 62.7% |
| Senior housing | $541.1M | 5.33% | 72% | 1.75x | 53.0% |
| Multi-family | $314.8M | 5.72% | 63% | 1.29x | 30.8% |
| Retail | $281.6M | 6.06% | 62% | 1.87x | 27.6% |
| Office | $110.3M | 6.42% | 66% | 3.65x | 10.8% |

Office is small and well covered. Hotel and senior housing together equal 115.7% of equity. Hotel is dispersed by state (Texas $139.3M, Arizona $114.2M, New York $108.6M, California $97.7M gross) but chunky by name: the largest single loan is $86.0 million against an Arizona hotel, 8.4% of equity, and the five loans above $60 million total $363.9 million, 35.6% of equity. The largest C&I relationship is $84.4 million gross, roughly 26% of that portfolio, to one Kansas borrower. The FY2025 10-K disclosed the largest borrowing relationship at $103.2 million against a $144.7 million statutory lending limit. The 10-K risk factor language is unusually direct: deterioration of *one* commercial loan or relationship could materially move delinquencies, charge-offs and provisions.

**Reserve adequacy.** ACL of $26.1 million is 0.32% of loans (residential 0.04%, CRE 0.93%, C&I 1.03%, construction 1.13%). ACL to nonaccrual is 50.9%, though roughly $30.4 million of nonaccruals have already been partially charged off and carry no ACL. A separate $6.223 million reserve covers unfunded commitments and should not be added to nonaccrual coverage. The blended figure is arithmetically defensible given the residential mix, but it means commercial deterioration is absorbed by future provisions, i.e., by the earnings stream the market is capitalizing at 12x.

**The specific deteriorating name.** Of $51.3 million of nonaccruals, $40.5 million is less than 90 days delinquent, placed on nonaccrual under internal policy while contractually current. The majority of CRE nonaccrual is two loans from one relationship; a March-quarter appraisal came in below the prior year, producing a $4.0 million specific valuation allowance still outstanding, and the loans were modified in the June quarter with a 13-month payment delay. They are recourse with personal guarantees and classified substandard. Separately, $914 thousand of previously modified CRE went 90+ days past due under modified terms. Within the $20–30 million size bucket, four loans carry DSCR below 1.15x. Cost-to-sell adjustments on individually evaluated commercial collateral now average 26% weighted (from 10%) and market-participant adjustments 18% (from 12%). Recognizing stress before delinquency is a positive about the credit process; the direction of appraised values is not.

Set against that: classified assets fell 32% to $96.1 million, CRE special mention dropped from $60.0 million to $15.6 million (mainly one upgraded hotel participation), OREO is zero, nine-month net charge-offs were $304 thousand, and the ACL forecast assumes national unemployment rising to 4.6% by mid-2027, a reasonable rather than aggressive assumption. [unverified — the research brief and Phase 1 data contain no July payrolls figure]

**Off-balance-sheet growth.** Total off-balance-sheet credit exposures rose 20.6% in nine months, from $821.6 million to $990.7 million. Commercial construction carries $193.5 million funded against $262.1 million undisbursed and $236.3 million of commitments — $691.8 million of potential exposure at a 1.26x projected stabilized DSCR, including multi-family at 1.19x and three office construction loans at 1.13x. Projected disbursements are $120.5 million in the September quarter alone. Reported loan growth understates the credit commitment being made, and the fastest-growing slice is the category where value is unknown until completion and lease-up.

**Rate and structural risks.** MVPE −23.9% at +200bp is a live exposure, even if reported NII is insensitive. The $10 billion asset ceiling caps ROE improvement. The Qualified Thrift Lender test is a forward governor on how far the commercial pivot can run, though MBS holdings of $709.2 million, multi-family and home-equity loans all count as qualifying investments, so headroom is probably substantial; we could not size it and flag it as unquantified.

**Governance.** The CEO holds the Chairman, President and CEO roles simultaneously. Five-year cumulative TSR indexed to 9/30/2020 was $96.57 against a peer index of $270.17, a roughly 174-point gap. Performance metrics in the pay plan are basic EPS, efficiency ratio and ROE, all internal accounting measures with no relative-TSR component, and "compensation actually paid" equals total compensation in each of the last five years, consistent with a pay mix carrying essentially no mark-to-market equity. The FY2025 10-K added detailed shareholder-activism language contemplating proxy contests and "efforts to force transactions not supported by the Board." No Schedule 13D exists on EDGAR. We read this as prophylactic drafting by a board with lagging TSR and a below-book valuation, possibly after private engagement; it is not an established catalyst and should not be underwritten as one.

**Positioning.** Short interest is 6.9% of float with 7.3 days to cover on ~842 thousand shares of average daily volume. For a $1.1 billion thrift that is elevated and illiquid; 7.3 days to cover means both directions of the trade are slow. No implied volatility, skew or open-interest data was available for this name, so we draw no options-based inference.

**Verification gaps we did not resolve:** the bodies of the 1/29/2026 Item 5.07 voting results and the two Item 5.02 8-Ks (September 2025 and February 2026) were not retrievable; the substantive DEF 14A was unavailable, so we have no verified data on 5% holders, board independence, related-party transactions or the say-on-pay outcome; the 10-Q contains inconsistent wording on interest-rate policy compliance ("in compliance with" versus "exceeded internal policy thresholds"), which we treat as loose drafting rather than a disclosed breach; and the $0.07 TBV per share variance is unexplained.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($10.50, +18.2%).** The CD book has further to reprice, the commercial rotation continues at roughly $475 million a year adding $0.06–$0.07 of annual EPS, and buybacks retire another 3–5% of shares. FY2027 EPS reaches $0.80–$0.85 and FY2028 approaches $0.90 with ROTCE near 11%. The two-loan CRE relationship resolves at or near its $4 million specific allowance, hotel operating results keep improving (as the special-mention upgrade already demonstrated), and the market applies a peer-median 14x multiple or 1.25x tangible book. Wealth management and treasury management add fee income that reduces margin dependence. Total return with the dividend approaches 22%.

**Bear case ($7.20, −18.9%).** Short rates stabilize or rise, the marginal CD reprices up rather than down, and NIM expansion stalls at 2.30–2.35%. One of the five loans above $60 million, most plausibly hotel, deteriorates, requiring $25–40 million of provisions against a $26.1 million ACL, which alone is $0.16–$0.26 of EPS. Off-balance-sheet construction commitments fund into a weaker completion market with 1.13–1.26x projected DSCRs. FY2027 EPS falls to $0.55, the multiple compresses to 0.90x tangible book, and the buyback pauses because holding-company cash is $10.7 million and the FRB non-objection lapses in February 2027 without renewal.

**Base case ($9.36, +5.4% plus 3.8% yield).** Consensus is roughly right. FY2026 lands near $0.69 and FY2027 near $0.78, driven by mix rotation and share count reduction with modest further deposit-cost relief. Credit costs remain low but not zero, perhaps $5–15 million of cumulative provisions over two years against the identified names. The market keeps CFFN at a modest discount to peer P/E, reflecting a 9–10% ROE and the hotel concentration. Probability-weighting these three outcomes (roughly 55/20/25 base/bull/bear) gives a low-single-digit price return plus the dividend, a high-single-digit total return, which is fair rather than compelling.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income hold).** The return is dominated by a covered 3.83% dividend and a roughly 5% annual reduction in share count, with modest re-rating potential. This is the third consecutive HOLD, supported by the specific arithmetic: base case +5.4% price upside against a −18.9% bear case, with an identified reserve-adequacy gap against a concentrated commercial book.

- **Base case target: $9.36** — 12.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $0.78, a 15% discount to the 14.2x peer median reflecting a 9.2% ROE and hotel concentration. Equivalent to 1.16x current tangible book of $8.04.
- **Bull case target: $10.50** — 13.5x FY2027 EPS of $0.78 with ROTCE approaching 11%, or approximately 1.30x tangible book. +18.2%.
- **Bear case target: $7.20** — 0.90x tangible book, or roughly 13x a provision-impaired $0.55 FY2027 EPS. −18.9%.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, with the Q4 FY2026 report in late October 2026 (first full-year NIM and credit picture) and the February 2027 buyback authorization renewal as dated checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Price below $8.00 (approximately 1.00x tangible book) with the operating trend intact — NIM at or above 2.30% and efficiency below 54%.
2. Two consecutive quarters in which the two-loan CRE relationship resolves without incremental specific allowance *and* ACL to nonaccrual coverage rises above 65%, removing the reserve-adequacy gap.
3. Commercial mix reaching 35% of loans with ROE sustained above 10.5%, which would justify a 1.25x+ book multiple on residual-income math.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Any single provision exceeding $15 million, or a nonaccrual increase above $75 million (currently $51.3 million), particularly in hotel or senior housing.
2. NIM contracting for two consecutive quarters, or cost of deposits rising sequentially — the signal that the repricing lever has reversed.
3. Interruption of the capital-return engine: failure to renew the FRB non-objection by February 2027, a Bank-to-holding-company distribution pause, or Bank CBLR falling below 9.3%.
4. Off-balance-sheet commercial exposure exceeding $1.15 billion with construction projected stabilized DSCR below 1.20x.

**Reassessment point:** the Q4 FY2026 / full-year report expected late October 2026, which will provide the first look at whether the September quarter's guided ~3% commercial growth and ~20% full-year commercial growth landed, and whether the $4.0 million specific allowance moved.

## Conclusion

Capitol Federal has done what a liability-sensitive thrift should do coming out of a rate shock: reprice the CD book down, restructure expensive FHLB advances, rotate 221 basis points of spread from residential into commercial, hold expenses so the efficiency ratio falls 617 basis points, and buy back 4.9% of the shares at $7.24 while the stock traded below book. Insiders bought $5.10–$6.85 with their own money and no offsetting open-market sales. The overdraft-fee class action was dismissed with prejudice in July. Consensus estimates are rising. Management quality on forward-looking evidence — capital deployment and insider alignment — is the strongest of the six dimensions here.

The problem is that the price now reflects most of it. At 1.10x tangible book on a 9.2% ROE and 11.4x FY2027 estimates, the stock is fair against a peer group earning more, and the base case delivers roughly a high-single-digit total return dominated by the dividend. Against that sits a concentrated set of risks the reserve does not cover: $640.5 million of hotel loans at 62.7% of equity, a single $86 million Arizona hotel loan, an ACL at 0.32% of loans covering half of nonaccruals, appraisal haircuts widening from 10% to 26%, a 13-month payment concession on the largest problem relationship, off-balance-sheet construction exposure compounding 20.6% in nine months, and a capital-return engine dependent on annual Federal Reserve non-objection with $10.7 million of holding-company cash. Add an MVPE that falls 23.9% on a 200 basis point rate rise, and a deposit-repricing lever that reverses if short rates stop falling.

We recommend owning it for the yield and the shrinking share count, and would not pay above book for a bank earning its cost of capital with 116% of equity in hotels and senior housing. We would add materially only near $8.00, or on evidence that reserve coverage has caught up to the commercial book.
