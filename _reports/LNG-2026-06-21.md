**Research Date: June 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cheniere is the largest U.S. LNG exporter and the second-largest LNG operator globally, with >60 mtpa of expected liquefaction capacity across Sabine Pass and Corpus Christi. The investment case rests on a rare combination for the energy sector: multi-decade contracted cash flows ($290.6B SPA backlog, ~90% of anticipated production contracted through the mid-2030s) layered onto a concrete, dated volume ramp (Corpus Christi Stage 3, 5 of 7 trains substantially complete) and a disciplined capital-return program ($10B buyback authorization, ~21% of market cap). At $227, the stock trades ~24% below its March 2026 high of $300.89 and ~12% below the lowest published analyst target of $259.

The headline numbers are misleading and must be set aside. The Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $(3.50)B / $(16.65) per share was driven almost entirely by $4.8B of non-cash, unfavorable fair-value swings on IPM (Integrated Production Marketing) derivatives as global-vs-Henry Hub spreads widened amid Middle East volatility. Underlying operations improved: operating cash flow was +$1.08B, export volumes rose 66 TBtu year-over-year, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance. A screen on trailing EPS or P/E badly misreads this company; the correct lenses are operating cash flow, Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, and Distributable Cash Flow, all of which were stable-to-improving.

Reconciling the valuation: consensus operating EPS estimates are $17.91 (current year) and $19.51 (next year), up +20.4% and +31.5% respectively over the trailing 90 days. At $227 that is ~12.7x and ~11.6x forward, against a midstream peer median P/E of 27.5x. Rising estimates of this magnitude paired with a sub-30x forward multiple and a concrete operating catalyst (Stage 3 ramp, raised guidance) is the configuration that has historically preceded continued re-rating, and it is supported here by three agency upgrades validating the de-leveraging plan. We are upgrading to a measured BUY. The primary offsets are genuine but bounded structural cost headwinds and the negative beta history of energy-sector positions, which do not justify forgoing a name at this discount with a live operating ramp.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates two export terminals. Sabine Pass (Louisiana, >30 mtpa) is held through publicly traded subsidiary Cheniere Energy Partners (CQP), in which Cheniere owns a 48.6% LP interest, 100% of the GP interest, and 100% of the incentive distribution rights. Corpus Christi (Texas, >30 mtpa expected) is 100% owned, with the Stage 3 midscale expansion (7 trains) ramping and Midscale Trains 8 & 9 under construction. Since the first cargo in February 2016, Cheniere has shipped >325 million tonnes across >4,760 cargoes to 40+ countries.

The business model is built on take-or-pay long-term contracts. SPAs (Sale & Purchase Agreements) carry a fixed fee per MMBtu plus a variable fee (generally ~115% of Henry Hub, covering feedgas, transport, and fuel), insulating Cheniere from U.S. gas price risk; customers pay the fixed fee even if they cancel cargoes. IPM agreements have Cheniere buy feedgas at a global-LNG-index price less a fixed liquefaction fee, producing a take-or-pay-style fixed margin. The backlog totals $290.6B ($107.7B fixed fees + $182.9B variable), with $111.4B of unsatisfied transaction price at a 9-year weighted-average recognition timing. This contracted visibility is unusual for any energy asset and is the reason this name behaves more like contracted infrastructure than a commodity producer.

FY2025 net income to Cheniere rose to $5.33B (+$2.1B YoY), helped by $2.3B of favorable IPM derivative marks but also by an $876M genuine margin increase from higher volumes as Corpus Christi Stage 3 ramped. Operating margin was 45.6%, net margin 34%. The Q1 2026 loss is a derivative artifact: a 10% move in commodity prices swings the Liquefaction Supply Derivative fair value by roughly $3.1B (Item 3, 10-Q), and the marks are non-cash, long-dated (up to 15 years), and based on Level 3 inputs (international-LNG-vs-Henry-Hub spread inputs ranging 60%–537%, weighted average 183%). Reported GAAP net income is a poor proxy for economic performance quarter-to-quarter.

The balance sheet is investment-grade project finance. Total debt was $23.9B at Q1'26 with a 4.65% weighted-average coupon and maturities laddered to 2056. Liquidity is ~$8.3B ($1.3B cash + $6.6B undrawn). Debt sits in four largely non-recourse structures (SPL, CQP, CCH, parent). The frequently cited Altman Z-Score of 1.41 ("distress zone") is a false signal here: the model penalizes the 4.4x D/E and negative working capital appropriate to a high-fixed-cost producer, but ignores the take-or-pay contracts, ample coverage, and the recent agency upgrades. It should be disregarded for a contracted-infrastructure entity.

Capital allocation is the core equity mechanism. The plan is to de-lever to ~4x, repurchase stock, grow the dividend, and fund accretive growth. FY2025 saw $2.7B repurchased (12.1M shares at $221.55 average), $0.7B of debt reduction, and a >10% dividend raise to $2.22/year. In February 2026 the Board lifted the buyback authorization to ~$10B through 2030, with $9.7B remaining at quarter-end. Q1'26 repurchases averaged $202.58, below today's price. Share count has fallen from 251M (2022) to ~210M, mechanically accreting per-share metrics independent of commodity prices.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is driven by physical capacity additions and structural demand, not commodity-price extrapolation. Stage 3 Train 5 was substantially complete in March 2026, with the full seven-train ramp adding contracted volume through 2026. Positive FID and full notice to proceed were issued on CCL Midscale Trains 8 & 9 (~$2.9B Bechtel EPC, to 2H2028). The CCL Expansion (24 mtpa) FERC application was filed in February 2026, and the SPL Expansion (~20 mtpa, two-phased) is approaching FID, with a Bechtel EPC contract for the first phase reportedly signed in late May 2026 (dollar figure unconfirmed; FID targeted 2026/27). Approximately 8 mtpa is under construction.

Commercial momentum is intact: new long-term SPAs with JERA (~1 mtpa, 2029–2050), CPC Corporation Taiwan (up to ~1.2 mtpa, 2026–2050), and an IPM with Canadian Natural Resources (140,000 MMBtu/day, ~15 years from 2030). The structural demand backdrop supports the expansion pipeline: Wood Mackenzie forecasts global LNG demand +64% to 671 mtpa by 2040 with a structural supply gap of ~104 mtpa by 2040, and Europe's LNG demand hit a record ~125 mtpa in 2025 (+27% YoY) as it replaces Russian gas ahead of a planned 2027 ban on residual Russian LNG.

The estimate-revision signal is the cleanest forward indicator. Consensus next-year operating EPS rose +31.5% over 90 days and current-year +20.4%, with the most recent 30-day moves still positive for the forward periods. These are operating estimates reflecting the Stage 3 volume ramp and the raised 2026 guidance, not GAAP-with-marks figures. Estimate revisions of this magnitude combined with a sub-30x forward multiple is among the more reliable setups for continued outperformance, and we weight it accordingly in the Growth and Valuation scores. The one caveat is the thin analyst panel (3–4 names producing the forward estimates), which warrants confirmation at the next print rather than over-extrapolation.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP EPS of $24.13 the stock screens at 33.7x, but that figure is dominated by FY2025 derivative gains and is not the right metric. On forward operating consensus, $227 is ~12.7x current-year and ~11.6x next-year EPS. The midstream/energy peer set (FANG, DVN, OXY, OKE, TRGP) carries a median P/E of 27.5x. Cheniere trades at less than half that, despite a higher-quality, longer-duration contracted cash-flow profile than most peers and a per-share accretion engine in the 21%-of-cap buyback.

We are deliberately disciplined about rewarding "cheap": a low multiple alone is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal. What distinguishes Cheniere is the independent catalyst stack that explains why the discount should narrow: (1) rising operating estimates on a concrete volume ramp; (2) three agency upgrades through early 2026 (S&P to BBB+, Fitch to BBB, Moody's to Baa2 / CCH Baa1) validating de-leveraging and supporting a lower cost of capital; (3) a buyback retiring stock below today's price; and (4) the FDDEI export-income tax regime lowering the go-forward effective rate from 2026. This is cheapness paired with catalysts, the configuration that historically rewards a higher valuation score.

The street is overwhelmingly constructive: 22 buy/strong-buy against 1 hold and 0 sells, with the lowest published target ($259) already ~14% above spot, the mean ($303) implying +34%, and the high at $340. On names trading below 30x forward with rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag and get revised upward as estimates catch up; the lowest target sitting above the current price is confirmation, not a ceiling. We anchor a base case below the street mean to account for derivative-driven optical volatility and the near-term supply-glut overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's moat is durable. It is the dominant U.S. LNG exporter with lowest-cost-quartile brownfield expansions on existing land, proximity to abundant U.S. shale gas, Henry Hub indexing that decouples economics from oil, and a scale/reliability reputation built across 4,760+ cargoes. The contracted backlog and four-structure capital architecture insulate the base business from the commodity tape: roughly 90% of anticipated production is take-or-pay, so a glut compresses only the ~10% marketed at spot.

The vertical-integration move via the January 2026 buyout of the remaining redeemable NCI in the Gregory Power Plant (taking 100% of the gas-fired plant adjacent to Corpus Christi) is an underappreciated de-risking step, securing power for the expanding complex against rising grid/electricity costs. The principal long-term competitive risk is price competition when re-contracting SPAs that expire in the mid-2030s, against a wave of new U.S., Canadian, and Qatari capacity. That risk is real but distant relative to the current contracted window. Competitive position is stable-to-strong; we hold the score at 7.0, reversing the trajectory's recent erosion, because the data (record volumes, advancing FIDs, IG upgrades, new SPAs) confirm the position is intact rather than deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant interpretive risk is GAAP earnings volatility from IPM/Level 3 derivatives. $3.6B of derivative gains in FY2025 flipped to multi-billion losses in Q1'26. These are non-cash and economically irrelevant to the contracted cash flows, but they will continue to generate optically alarming headlines that move the stock short-term.

Two genuinely company-specific structural cost headwinds deserve attention and would be missed by a standard screen:
- **Section 301 U.S.-built-vessel mandate.** USTR's April 2025 ruling requires 1% of U.S. LNG exports on U.S.-built vessels from April 2029, scaling to 15% by 2047. The U.S. effectively builds no LNG carriers today, making this a unique compliance/cost risk. The more severe original threat (suspension of export licenses) was removed, and China-linked vessel fees were deferred a year as of November 2025, but the build-vessel timeline stands.
- **Ad valorem property-tax incentive roll-off.** Corpus Christi incentives begin expiring in 2026 and Sabine Pass in 2027. FY2025 property tax was only ~$89M, but the magnitude of the step-up is not yet quantified.

Neither is sized in current filings, and both are 2026/2029+ phenomena. Against $107.7B of contracted fixed fees and a >60 mtpa platform, they are real but bounded margin pressures rather than thesis-breakers.

The supply-glut transition is the most-debated near-term risk. Management itself flags ~20 mtpa of new global supply in 2025 and a "more ample supply landscape." We size this precisely: it bears only on the ~10% of volume Cheniere Marketing sells at spot, while ~90% is take-or-pay and protected. Marketing margin is a modest contributor to total margin relative to the fixed-fee base, so even a sharp spot-margin compression is a single-digit-percent hit to Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, not enough to offset a 14–34% gap to street targets. The competing tightening case (Wood Mackenzie's June 2026 Iran-conflict scenarios modeling up to ~20% of global supply disrupted) shows the spot exposure cuts both ways; Cheniere is among the most insulated LNG names to that binary.

Other risks: construction/EPC execution on four expansion projects (Bechtel fixed-price turnkey, but change-order risk); customer concentration (one customer at 18% of Q1'26 receivables, none >10% of FY2025 revenue); and SPA termination rights for delivery failures or force majeure.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and does not contradict a constructive thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 30.7% versus 27.1% one-year historical vol, only modestly rich (3.6pp), in the 7th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (1.2%), the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.45, and the term structure is flat. ATM IV collapsed from 43.7% to 30.7% over the four days into June 21 and the P/C OI ratio fell from 0.92 to 0.60, indicating resolved post-earnings uncertainty and a lean toward call interest, not defensive put accumulation. Short interest is just 1.9% of float (1.6 days to cover). The options and short-interest data are consistent with the overwhelmingly bullish analyst panel and show no informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300–$340).** Stage 3 fully ramps on schedule, SPL/CCL Expansion FIDs convert the pipeline into bankable backlog, the supply-glut narrative proves transient (or the Iran-conflict tightening case dominates), agency upgrades compress the cost of capital, and the buyback meaningfully shrinks the share count into a recovering multiple. Forward estimates continue to revise up, and the stock re-rates toward the street mean ($303) and high ($340). This recovers the March high and extends it on FID-driven backlog growth.

**Bear case ($185–$200).** A sustained global LNG glut compresses spot marketing margins for several years, the Section 301 vessel mandate and ad valorem roll-off prove materially more costly than current disclosures imply, EPC change orders or financing strain delay the expansion FIDs, and continued derivative-driven GAAP losses keep sentiment depressed. A verified, held Hormuz reopening would also pressure near-term spot LNG. This takes the stock back toward its 52-week low ($186.20), roughly -13% to -18% from current.

The asymmetry favors the long: the bounded, largely non-cash and 10%-of-volume nature of the downside drivers sits against a contracted backlog, a per-share accretion engine, and an operating ramp that the entire street and the company's own raised guidance corroborate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $275** (~+21% from $227). Mechanism: ~14x forward on $19.51 next-year operating EPS, a multiple still well below the 27.5x peer median and justified by the contracted backlog, de-leveraging, and share-count reduction, but discounted from the street mean to reflect derivative-driven optical volatility and the unresolved near-term glut overhang. This sits between the bear and bull cases.

**Bull case target: $320** (~+41%), toward the street mean-to-high as FIDs advance and estimates continue revising up.

**Bear case target: $195** (~-14%), back toward the 52-week low on a sustained glut and crystallized structural cost headwinds.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to: the Stage 3 completion cadence through 2026, the SPL/CCL Expansion FID decisions (2026/27), and the next two earnings prints (which will confirm whether the +20–31% estimate revisions hold on a still-thin analyst panel).

**Upside/downside from $227:** +21% to base, +41% to bull, -14% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Consensus next-year operating EPS revisions turn negative for two consecutive quarters (the estimate-revision pillar breaks).
2. Management cuts or withdraws the raised 2026 volume/EBITDA guidance, or a Stage 3 train slips materially.
3. The Section 301 vessel mandate or ad valorem roll-off is quantified at a level that materially impairs go-forward unit margins, or an SPL/CCL Expansion FID is shelved.

## Conclusion

We upgrade Cheniere to **BUY** with disciplined sizing. The prior cautious stance rested on grounds that do not bear weight: a backward-looking observation that the buyback executed at $202–$221 (below today's price) is not a reason to wait when the Board authorized $10B going forward; the cluster of C-suite sales at $290–$300 were, by the filings' own characterization, planned sales near a local peak rather than distress signals (low short interest and balanced options confirm no informed bearish view); and the supply-glut risk bears on only ~10% of volume, a single-digit-percent EBITDA exposure that cannot offset a 14–34% discount to street targets.

What remains is a high-quality, investment-grade contracted-infrastructure asset at ~12x forward operating earnings versus a 27.5x peer median, with estimates revising up +20–31% over 90 days on a concrete volume ramp, $290B of take-or-pay backlog, three fresh agency upgrades, a 21%-of-cap buyback retiring shares below the current price, and a price ~24% off its high and below even the most bearish analyst target. We size this as a measured rather than maximum-conviction position: the energy sector's historical beta drag and two genuine-but-unquantified structural cost headwinds (Section 301, ad valorem) keep us from a full-conviction rating, and the thin forward-analyst panel warrants confirmation at the next print. On the weight of the evidence, the configuration is a buy, and we would add on any further weakness driven by GAAP-loss optics or a verified Hormuz reopening.
