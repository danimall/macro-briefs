## Executive Summary

Charles River has spent three years shrinking. Revenue peaked at $4,129.4M in FY2023 and fell in FY2024 (-1.9%) and FY2025 (-0.9%), with H1 FY2026 down another 0.8% to $1,999.9M. GAAP operating income fell from $617.3M to $25.2M over that span, though roughly $524M of the FY2025 gap is goodwill impairment ($165.0M), long-lived asset impairment ($259.1M) and restructuring ($99.8M); adding those back gives a 13.7% underlying operating margin versus 14.8% in FY2024, a ~110bp compression rather than a collapse. Operating cash flow was essentially flat across those two years at $734.6M and $737.6M.

The Q2 FY2026 print (August 5) is the first forward turn in the file. Reported revenue of $1,004.1M was down 2.7% but reflected the first positive *organic* growth since Q3 2023; DSA net book-to-bill reached 1.19x, the highest in nearly four years; DSA backlog recovered from $1.9B at year-end to $2.0B; non-GAAP EPS of $3.02 beat the $2.74 FactSet consensus by 9.4%, the fourth consecutive beat and the largest of the four; and FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance was raised to $11.15-$11.45 from $10.80-$11.30. JPMorgan upgraded to Overweight with a $310 target, up from $180. The stock rose 11% on the print and 17.7% over two weeks.

The problem is price. At $267.49 the stock trades at 23.7x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $11.30 and 21.2x the FY2027 consensus of $12.63, sits near its 52-week high, and is within 0.1% of the consensus mean target of $267.71. Free cash flow guidance was raised to $400-420M, yet that midpoint is 19-23% *below* FY2025 actual FCF of $518.4M — a 3.2% FCF yield and roughly 37x EV/FCF for a company guiding flat-to-+1% organic revenue. Two weeks of a 17.7% move against consensus revisions of +1.4% (current year) and +2.6% (next year) is multiple expansion, not earnings capture. Set against an unresolved structural question, regulatory substitution away from animal testing in the ~60%-of-revenue DSA franchise, plus a revived securities class action, an effective tax rate of 167% in H1, and $1,120.3M of now-unhedged floating-rate revolver debt, the asymmetry from here is unfavorable. HOLD, no new capital, trim bias on further strength.

## Company Overview

Charles River sells the regulated inputs and testing work biopharma needs before human trials. Three segments (FY2025):

| Segment | Revenue | YoY | Segment op. margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research Models & Services (RMS) | $846.1M | +2.0% | 5.3% (incl. $102.0M impairment) |
| Discovery & Safety Assessment (DSA) | $2,402.9M | -2.0% | 17.7% |
| Manufacturing Solutions | $766.4M | -0.4% | -24.0% (incl. $274.0M impairments) |
| **Total** | **$4,015.4M** | **-0.9%** | 0.6% consolidated GAAP |

DSA, comprising GLP toxicology packages submitted to the FDA plus discovery services, is the economic core at roughly 60% of revenue. RMS breeds and supplies research models including non-human primates. Manufacturing contains Microbial Solutions (Endosafe, Accugenix, Celsis), Biologics Testing, and, until May 2026, a CDMO business.

The moat as the filings describe it: scale across the full non-clinical continuum; regulatory entrenchment, since switching toxicology vendors mid-program invalidates comparability of a validated data package; and, newly, vertical integration of NHP supply, which management frames as delivering schedule certainty competitors lack. H1 FY2026 geographic mix was US 54.2%, Europe 27.0%, Canada 11.9%, Asia-Pacific 5.8%, relevant because the China-CRO bear case is often overstated relative to CRL's actual Asia exposure.

The portfolio was substantially rebuilt in nine months: the $507.3M K.F. (Cambodia) NHP asset acquisition closed January 14, 2026; PathoQuest was taken to 100% for $67.6M in April; the CDMO and Cell Solutions businesses went to GI Partners on May 6; certain European Discovery Services went to IQVIA for $125.2M on May 22. All of it followed the May 6, 2025 Cooperation Agreement with Elliott Investment Management.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings quality is the central analytical problem.** GAAP is unusable: FY2025 net loss of $(144.3)M, H1 FY2026 net loss attributable to common of $(16.3)M and diluted EPS of $(0.34), Q2 EPS of $(0.03) against $1.06 a year earlier. The trailing P/E field of -54.4x is arithmetic noise. But non-GAAP overstates the case in the other direction: restructuring has run $107.0M (FY2024), $99.8M (FY2025) and $54.6M in H1 FY2026 alone. Three consecutive years of "one-time" charges is a run rate, not an exception.

**The H1 margin improvement is roughly half as large as reported.** H1 operating income of $239.8M (12.0% margin) versus $174.8M (8.7%) reads as +330bps. It includes a $38.5M pre-tax gain from the March 2026 Wilmington, MA sale-leaseback ($60.1M net proceeds against $21.6M book value), booked in SG&A. Excluding it: $201.3M / $1,999.9M = 10.07%, or +140bps. Most of the remainder came from lower intangible amortization ($14.6M in Q2 versus $65.4M a year earlier, which had included $35.5M of accelerated CDMO amortization). The sale-leaseback also creates recurring rent expense going forward.

The same gain conceals RMS deterioration. RMS H1 operating income of $86.1M on $417.8M of revenue (20.6%) drops to $62.9M / 15.05% excluding the $23.2M allocated gain, against a derived prior-year margin near 18.6%, roughly 355bps of underlying decline. Management attributes this to flat NIH budgets and slower grant processing; the August 2025 Supreme Court ruling that lower courts lacked jurisdiction to reinstate grant funding allowed nearly $800M of federal grants to be terminated. Manufacturing's Q2 margin jump from 6.0% to 34.9% is subtraction of a loss-maker from both numerator and denominator, not operating leverage.

**Cash conversion is the weak link and the one guidance line that goes down.** H1 FY2026 FCF was $133.8M ($220.8M OCF less $87.0M capex) versus $281.7M, a 52% decline, and that was *with* a $56.2M cash-tax tailwind (taxes paid $17.8M versus $74.0M). The 10-Q cites higher variable compensation payments (the FY2025 bonus paid at 139% of target and settled in Q1) and acquisition/divestiture cash costs. FY2026 FCF guidance of $400-420M against H1 actual implies H2 FCF near $276M versus roughly $237M in H2 FY2025, up ~17%. That is plausible given the Cambodia NHP cost benefit, non-recurrence of the bonus outflow, and potential conversion of the NHP inventory build ($254.1M finished products versus $169.4M), but it is a back-half-loaded target from a company whose Q1 is structurally cash-negative.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt at June 27, 2026 was $2,445.4M ($2,637.4M total debt and finance leases less $192.0M cash), up from roughly $1,903M at year-end. On GAAP FY2025 EBITDA of ~$428.5M that is 5.7x; adding back impairments and restructuring gives ~$952.4M and 2.57x. The adjusted figure is the more useful one for solvency, and 2.57x with no maturity before the 2028 notes and ~$880M undrawn on a $2.0B revolver maturing December 2029 is manageable. Three $500M fixed tranches (4.25% 2028, 3.75% 2029, 4.00% 2031); weighted-average rate 4.08%.

The exposure that deserves flagging: the $500M-notional 4.65% interest rate swap matured in FY2024 and was not replaced, and the revolver draw nearly doubled from $616.5M to $1,120.3M in six months to fund the Cambodia closing, PathoQuest, and $300M of buybacks. That is $1.12B of unhedged floating debt; each 100bp move is roughly $11.2M pre-tax, about $0.17 of after-tax EPS. Adding unhedged floating leverage to repurchase stock is the least defensible financing decision in the file.

Contingent items beyond funded debt: $105.0M of remaining Cambodia deferred consideration, $45.0M of fully-accrued contingent consideration, up to $45.0M of committed funding for the divested CDMO/Cell Solutions businesses' future EBITDA losses and capex (which the filing states is "expected to be fully used by GI"), and a redeemable non-controlling interest carried at $42.5M puttable in December 2030 at a fixed $54.0M. The long-term tax liability line rose from $15.3M to $211.1M in six months, largely from uncertain tax positions assumed in the Cambodia carve-out; unrecognized tax benefits total $158.2M plus $32.7M interest and $34.2M penalties.

Screening outputs (Altman Z of 1.73, Piotroski 3/5) are driven mechanically by the impairment-suppressed EBIT and net income terms. Corrected for market rather than book equity and actual retained earnings, Z is approximately 2.85 — grey zone, not distress, and sensitive to the equity input: at the May 2026 price near $160 the corrected figure falls to roughly 2.05.

## Growth Analysis

Three forward indicators are positive and are the reason this is not a REDUCE.

**Bookings.** DSA net book-to-bill of 1.19x, and backlog rebuilt from $1.9B to $2.0B. In a business where revenue converts from backlog over multiple quarters, book-to-bill above 1.1x is the leading edge of a revenue re-acceleration. Management noted capture rates have ticked up and that any returning pricing power will not reach revenue until 2027 because of backlog timing, which argues FY2027 is the year the mix improves, not FY2026.

**End-market funding.** Global biotech funding was $11.9B across 88 deals in H1 2026 against $8.0B across 64 deals in H1 2025. This flows to DSA bookings with a lag and is the cleanest exogenous demand input available.

**Cost and portfolio self-help.** The ~$300M cumulative annualized savings program is targeted for completion by end-2026, with more than $175M already benefiting FY2025. Divesting the CDMO removed a structurally loss-making business, and the Cambodia NHP cost benefit begins in Q3 with full effect in Q4 as animals clear quarantine. Management guided to at least 500bps of H2 margin improvement.

Against that: reported revenue is still declining, organic guidance is flat-to-+1%, and the divestitures shrink the revenue base by roughly 7% of FY2025. RMS is deteriorating on NIH funding with no visible catalyst. The EPS growth from $10.28 (FY2025) to $11.30 (FY2026 guidance midpoint) is sourced substantially from divesting loss-makers, lower amortization, and a 3.0% reduction in share count over six months, rather than from volume. Non-GAAP EPS has been flat for five years: $10.32, $11.12, $10.67, $10.32, $10.28.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest and mixed in composition: current year $11.26 (+1.4% over both 30 and 90 days), next year $12.63 (+2.6% / +2.1%), next quarter +2.0%, but the *current* quarter was cut 3.2% over 30 days from $3.05 to $2.95. This is a low-single-digit upward drift with an intra-year timing shift, not the kind of estimate velocity that reliably precedes further multiple expansion.

Peer medians are not usable at face value: the Life Sciences Tools cohort shows P/E of 77.8x (TECH), 46.9x (BIO), 29.9x (MEDP) and two negatives (AVTR, AZTA), a median of 46.9x that reflects impairment-distorted denominators rather than a coherent multiple. MEDP at 29.9x trailing is the closest structural comp, a services business with actual growth. CRL at 21-24x forward carries a defensible discount to that: MEDP grows, CRL does not yet.

The bull valuation argument is normalized earnings power. If H2's guided 500bps of margin improvement annualizes and DSA bookings convert to mid-single-digit organic growth in FY2027, EPS of $13.50-$14.00 is reachable, and 23x that is $310-$322. That is the JPMorgan case and it is not unreasonable. But it requires the margin ramp to be delivered and durable, and it assumes the market continues paying a low-20s multiple for a business whose terminal demand method is under active regulatory review.

The bear valuation argument is cash. A 3.2% FCF yield with FCF guided down 19-23% year over year, on flat revenue, with $2.45B of net debt of which $1.12B floats unhedged, is a full price. Normalizing FCF back to the FY2025 level of $518.4M gives $10.79 per share and a 4.0% yield, still not cheap for a low-growth business.

The market-based check is the most telling. The consensus mean target is $267.71 against a price of $267.49 — the stock has arrived exactly at where the sell side collectively values it. The median of $295.50 implies 10.5% upside and the high is $310; the low is $135. That $135-$310 dispersion is the widest signal in the file: analysts do not agree on whether this is a 21x recovering franchise or a business facing method obsolescence, and the price now sits at the midpoint of that disagreement rather than at a discount to it.

Valuation score of 4.5, down from 5.5 in the prior review, reflects the 17.7% price move against a 1.4% earnings revision. Nothing about the business is worse; the entry is.

## Competitive Landscape

CRL remains the scale incumbent in regulated non-clinical work, and two dynamics have shifted favorably. Management stated on the Q2 call (per a third-party summary; unverified against a transcript) that regulated work has not migrated to China in a major way, that clients are taking a wait-and-see stance, and that pricing for Western-facing Chinese CROs is converging toward parity, removing the historical cost advantage. CRL's Asia-Pacific revenue is only 5.8% of H1 revenue, so the China narrative was always disproportionate to its P&L relevance. The FY2025 10-K still lists low-cost-jurisdiction competition as a risk.

Vertical integration of NHP supply is the new competitive claim. Owning Cambodian and Mauritian breeding operations — $391.6M of bearer biological assets at June 27, 2026, up from $135.6M — is intended to deliver schedule certainty in a market where NHP availability has been the binding constraint on toxicology study starts. If that holds, it is a real differentiator in the highest-margin part of DSA. It also concentrates jurisdictional, tariff, and tax risk in two small countries, one of which was the subject of a DOJ criminal investigation into a supplier as recently as 2022.

The counterweight is that revenue declined for three years while the sector recovered, which is evidence of share loss or, at minimum, of a franchise unable to defend price. The CDMO and Cell Solutions exit — where CRL paid the buyer $12.4M net and committed up to $45M of further funding, against roughly $770M of cumulative recognized impairment and divestiture losses — is an admission that the 2020-2022 expansion into cell and gene therapy manufacturing failed. That was a sector-wide mispricing, not a uniquely CRL error, but the capital is gone.

Competitive position 5.3 — modestly improving from a low base on bookings and NHP integration, still structurally challenged in RMS.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory substitution away from animal testing (the terminal-value risk).** The FY2025 10-K documents a worsening trajectory: FDA Modernization Act 2.0 removed the statutory animal-testing requirement in 2022; the FDA announced in April 2025 its intent to expand New Approach Methodologies (organ-on-chip, computational models, advanced in vitro assays) in preclinical safety; further FDA statements followed in October 2025; the UK announced a phase-out roadmap in November 2025. The filing states plainly that eliminating animals in research "may have material adverse effects." The board dissolved two committees and created a NAMs and Science Committee in May 2025, which met once in 2025. Management's counter — that AI-driven molecule design generates more programs requiring validation, with material impact 1-2 years out — is a hypothesis about volume offsetting method substitution, not established. This risk applies to roughly 60% of revenue and is the reason the stock traded near $145 nine months ago. At $267.49 it is materially less discounted than it was.

**Revived securities litigation.** The May 2023 class action (D. Mass., against the company, Foster and former CFO Smith; class period May 2020 - February 2023) was dismissed in July 2024, but the First Circuit reversed in part on August 15, 2025, returning it to district court. Lead plaintiff substitution was still in motion as of October 2025. Two derivative suits, one including insider-trading claims against directors, remain stayed. The company states it cannot estimate exposure and discloses no reserve. Every *regulatory* NHP overhang cleared in 2025 — USFWS in July, DOJ closing both grand jury and civil investigations in August, the SEC declining enforcement in November, CITES clearance in November. Only the private litigation went the other way.

**Tax.** Effective tax rates: 72.8% (FY2024), -42.9% (FY2025), 101.4% (Q2 FY2026), 167.0% (H1 FY2026). Valuation allowance $323.3M. FY2026 guidance reportedly assumes a $0.20 EPS headwind from proposed Mauritius legislation — where the Noveprim NHP operation sits — offset by a $0.19 deferred compensation gain (per an unverified third-party call summary). The Cambodia purchase accounting is preliminary and included $233.3M of assumed liabilities incorporating the seller's uncertain tax positions, with no disclosure of indemnities in the sections reviewed.

**Interest rate.** $1,120.3M of unhedged floating revolver debt. Every 100bp is ~$0.17 of EPS.

**Governance.** FY2025 bonuses paid at 139% of target — the proxy says revenue and operating income goals were exceeded — in a year with revenue down 0.9%, GAAP operating margin of 0.6%, and GAAP diluted loss per share of $(2.91). CEO total compensation rose to $15.44M from $13.47M. Five-year indexed TSR: CRL 81 versus peer group 148, a 67-point shortfall. The anchoring incentive metric, non-GAAP EPS, has been flat for five years. Mitigants: the 2023 PSU tranche paid at 87.3% because relative TSR was below target, and say-on-pay carried at ~96%.

**Management and insider signal.** Insider ownership is thin: all executive officers and directors held 660,726 shares, 1.3% of shares outstanding at March 16, 2026. In the roughly May-August 2026 Form 4 window, no open-market purchases were observed. Foster sold 75,000 shares at $225 on June 29 (~$16.9M), which carries limited informational content from a 75-year-old retiring CEO diversifying, and Parisotto sold 5,046 at $263 on August 5, earnings day. Girshick adopted a 10b5-1 plan on May 18, thirteen days into her tenure; that is the compliant mechanism and covers a modest fraction of holdings. No insider committed new capital during a 78% twelve-month advance. Against that, the buyback execution has been good: $350M at ~$166.67 average in FY2025 and $300M at ~$176.47 in H1 FY2026, both far below the current price, with $700M remaining on the $1.0B authorization.

**Elliott.** The activist is the operative catalyst framework: four board seats via the May 2025 Cooperation Agreement, and three of five seats (Graves as chair, Barg, Enyedy) on the Strategic Planning and Capital Allocation Committee that oversees divestitures, capital structure, and repurchase policy. The divestiture program, the $1.0B authorization, and the CEO succession all followed. But Elliott does not appear in the 5% beneficial owner table (Vanguard 12.1%, BlackRock 7.1%, Invesco 5.5%), so its economic stake and direction of travel are unknown. Board influence and economic alignment are different things, and a shareholder cannot assess Elliott's holding period from the available record.

**Verification gaps that materially affect the thesis.** The Q2 call content — organic inflection, 1.19x book-to-bill, ≥500bps H2 margin guidance, the Mauritius tax headwind, China pricing parity, and the Eli Lilly TuneLab collaboration (in which TuneLab is Lilly's platform and CRL is a wet-lab service provider, not the owner) — comes from a third-party summary, not a company transcript or 8-K. Full-year revenue-dollar guidance was not obtained. Customer concentration was not established, and revenue mix shifting through divestitures is not evidence of customer diversification.

### Options Market Signal

The most informative options datapoint is the two-week change: put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.18 to 2.44 while the price rose 17.7% and ATM IV fell from 63.5% to 46.6%. Positioning flipped decisively toward downside protection into the rally. Near-term IV of 46.6% against 45.4% one-year realized is fair, and IV percentile within the chain is 21%, so protection is being accumulated at reasonable cost rather than in panic. Near-term put/call skew of 3.8% indicates elevated put demand; the six-month skew reads -3.2%, and the term structure is in contango (46.6% near, 52.9% at six months), which places the market's perceived risk further out rather than immediately. Put/call volume ratio 1.77. Implied move on the August 21 expiry is ±7.3%. Short interest is 8.0% of float at 3.6 days to cover, meaningful but not crowded.

The read: the options market is not pricing an imminent dislocation, but the holders who own the stock after a 78% advance have bought insurance, and they have bought it in longer tenors. That is consistent with the fundamental picture, a near-term catalyst path (Q3 margin ramp) sitting on top of an unresolved multi-year question.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($330, +23.4%).** DSA book-to-bill converts to mid-single-digit organic growth in FY2027 as backlog rolls forward and pricing power returns. The guided ≥500bps H2 margin improvement is delivered and annualizes: Cambodia NHP costs drop with full effect in Q4, Manufacturing margins approach 40% as the CDMO exit annualizes, and the ~$300M savings program completes. Biotech funding continues recovering. FY2027 EPS reaches $13.50-$14.00 rather than the $12.63 consensus, and the market pays 23-24x. The Elliott-controlled capital allocation committee deploys the remaining $700M authorization plus further divestiture proceeds. NAMs adoption proves additive — more AI-designed molecules requiring validation — rather than substitutive.

**Bear case ($185, -30.8%).** Bookings prove a single-quarter artifact and book-to-bill falls back below 1.0x; RMS continues eroding on flat NIH budgets with no offset. The H2 margin ramp under-delivers because the Wilmington sale-leaseback rent, restructuring, and the elevated 10.6%-of-revenue unallocated corporate cost persist. FCF stays near the $400M low end, keeping the FCF yield at 3.1% with net debt at $2.45B and $1.12B floating. An FDA or UK NAMs milestone re-focuses the market on DSA terminal value, and the multiple compresses to 17x on $11 of EPS. The stock traded at $150-160 in May 2026 on these same facts; $185 requires only a partial reversal of sentiment.

**Base case ($285, +6.5%).** Organic growth turns modestly positive, H2 margin improves but by less than 500bps net of recurring costs, FY2027 EPS lands near consensus at $12.63-$13.00, and the stock holds a 22-23x multiple. $285 is 22.6x FY2027 consensus and implies roughly a 4.0% yield on normalized FCF near $545M.

Probability-weighting 30% bull / 45% base / 25% bear gives an expected return of approximately +2%, below a fair return on equity of this volatility (45% realized). The asymmetry is the problem: 30.8% of downside against 6.5% of base-case upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing owners maintain exposure through the Q3 catalyst; prospective investors have no entry signal at $267.49. Holders sitting on gains from the $150-180 range can reasonably trim 20-30% of the position on further strength above $290 without waiting for a thesis change.

- **Base case target: $285** (+6.5%) — 22.6x FY2027 consensus EPS of $12.63, ~4.0% yield on normalized FCF of ~$545M.
- **Bull case target: $330** (+23.4%) — 23.5x FY2027 EPS of $14.00 on delivered margin expansion.
- **Bear case target: $185** (-30.8%) — 17x FY2027 EPS of $11.00 on a bookings reversal or NAMs escalation.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 FY2026 print (late October / early November 2026), which is the first quarter to contain the Cambodia NHP cost benefit and the initial installment of the guided H2 margin improvement.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 FY2026 shows the Cambodia cost benefit landing with segment margins expanding on an ex-gain basis, DSA book-to-bill sustained at or above 1.15x, and reported organic revenue growth of +2% or better.
2. FY2026 FCF guidance raised toward or above the FY2025 actual of $518.4M, closing the EPS/cash-conversion divergence, with H2 FCF tracking to at least $276M.
3. A price retracement into the $215-230 range on unchanged bookings — roughly 18-19x FY2027 consensus with an FCF yield near 4% — which would restore a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. DSA book-to-bill falls below 1.0x, or backlog declines from $2.0B, in either of the next two quarters.
2. A dated FDA or EMA action materially narrowing GLP animal-study requirements for a defined study class, or a US phase-out roadmap analogous to the UK's November 2025 announcement.
3. FY2026 FCF guidance cut below $400M, or the revolver draw rising above $1.3B without a hedge being put in place, or a district-court ruling in the reinstated class action establishing a quantified exposure range.
4. Any quarter in which reported ex-gain operating margin fails to improve year over year while restructuring charges continue at the ~$50M-per-half rate.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 FY2026 earnings release, with an interim check on Form 4 activity — the first open-market insider purchase above $250 would be a genuinely new datapoint given the current one-way flow.

## Conclusion

Prior reviews of this name (4.9 → 5.2 over six months, four consecutive HOLDs) tracked a business getting incrementally less bad at a price that made the risk acceptable. Q2 FY2026 confirms the operational turn: first organic growth in eleven quarters, book-to-bill at a four-year high, backlog rebuilt, a fourth consecutive beat with the largest margin of the four, and a second guidance raise. That is real, and it validates the earlier decision to hold rather than exit.

What has changed is the price. A 17.7% two-week advance against +1.4% to +2.6% of consensus revision is multiple expansion, and the stock now sits near its 52-week high, within 0.1% of the consensus mean target, at 23.7x guided non-GAAP EPS and 31x guided free cash flow per share, with free cash flow guided 19-23% *below* the prior year even after the raise. Beneath the headline, roughly half of the H1 margin gain came from a real estate gain booked in SG&A, RMS margins declined ~355bps ex-gain, restructuring has been a $100M annual run rate for three years, and $1.12B of revolver debt floats unhedged.

The overall score declines to 4.96 from 5.2 entirely through the valuation dimension (5.5 → 4.5) and a low risk score, offset by improving growth and competitive readings. Hold what you own into the Q3 margin catalyst; commit no new capital at $267.49; revisit constructively at $215-230 or on evidence that the guided H2 margin improvement is converting to cash rather than to adjusted EPS.
