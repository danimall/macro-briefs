## Executive Summary

Hilltop Holdings is a Dallas-based diversified financial holding company operating a Texas-focused commercial bank ($15.8B assets), a leading municipal advisory/broker-dealer, and a national residential mortgage origination business. The company reported a strong FY2025 ($2.64 EPS, +52% YoY) driven by NIM expansion, broker-dealer momentum, and mortgage loss narrowing, with Q1 2026 continuing the trend ($0.64 EPS vs. $0.49 consensus).

The investment case centers on two dynamics: (1) aggressive capital return — the company retired ~19% of shares outstanding since 2022 and currently yields ~7.5% on a total capital return basis (buybacks + dividends annualized), and (2) a potential mortgage segment move from a ($62.7M) annual loss toward breakeven if rates decline, which would add $0.30-$0.50/share to EPS. Against these positives, the stock trades at 14.1x trailing earnings — a 24% premium to peer median P/E of 11.4x — with NIM likely at peak (management guided flat-to-down), meaningful CRE concentration (41% of loan book), and the founder/chairman selling $9.9M in shares near the current price.

The risk/reward profile supports holding for income and buyback-driven compounding, but does not justify a new position at current levels given the peer valuation premium, limited organic growth visibility, and concentrated geographic/credit risks.

## Company Overview

Hilltop Holdings operates through three segments:

**PlainsCapital Bank:** $15.8B-asset Texas commercial bank with $10.9B deposits and $8.3B held-for-investment loan portfolio. NIM expanded to 3.38% in Q1 2026, with CRE representing 41% of loans. Geographic concentration: 76% of real estate loans in Texas (32% DFW, 17% Austin).

**HilltopSecurities:** Full-service broker-dealer specializing in municipal advisory (76% Texas-derived), structured finance, fixed income trading, retail brokerage, and clearing services. Pretax income reached $67.6M in FY2025, with Q1 2026 up 60% YoY.

**PrimeLending:** National residential mortgage originator that has operated at a loss since 2022 due to elevated rates. Losses narrowed from ($62.7M) in FY2023 to ($17.5M) in FY2025 and approximately ($8M) annualized in Q1 2026. Management targets $9B-$10B production in 2026.

FY2025 represented a genuine earnings step-up: banking NIM expanded to 3.17% (full-year) with Q4 reaching 3.28%, the broker-dealer grew pretax 29%, and mortgage losses halved. The merchant banking gain ($27.8M from Moser Holdings) was a one-time item representing approximately $0.35/share of the $2.64.

Adjusting for the merchant banking gain, normalized FY2025 EPS was approximately $2.29. Q1 2026 EPS of $0.64 (annualized $2.56) suggests the earnings trajectory is sustainable without one-time items, though NIM at 3.38% may represent near-peak.

### Balance Sheet & Capital Position

- Book value per share: $36.42
- Tangible book value per share: $31.97
- CET1 ratio: 19.1% (approximately double the "well-capitalized" minimum)
- Total deposits: $10.9B (54% uninsured)
- Total equity: $2.17B
- Goodwill: $267.4M (PrimeLending impairment risk flagged by management)

At 19.1% CET1, Hilltop could absorb significant credit losses while maintaining regulatory buffers. Management is deploying excess capital through buybacks ($184M in FY2025, $47.5M in Q1 2026) and dividends ($45.4M in FY2025).

### Capital Return Profile

At the Q1 2026 pace ($59.3M/quarter = $237M annualized), total capital return represents approximately 10.7% of current market cap ($2.2B). The $125M buyback authorization through January 2027 implies continued share count reduction of 5-6% annually. Share count compression from 70.4M (2022) to ~58.5M (current) has been a primary EPS growth driver.

### Credit Quality

Net charge-offs: 0.22% (rising from 0.15%), concentrated in auto note finance ($3.6M in Q1 2026 alone). Non-performing assets remain low, but one CRE credit migrated to non-performing in Q1. The auto note portfolio has been flagged for several quarters without resolution.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth prospects are modest:

- **Loan growth:** 4-6% guided (reasonable for current environment)
- **NIM:** Guided flat-to-modestly down from 3.38% peak
- **Broker-dealer:** Momentum strong (+60% pretax YoY in Q1) but cyclical and tied to municipal issuance volumes
- **Mortgage:** Structural headwinds persist (rate lock-in effect, affordability), though losses are narrowing

The primary growth mechanism is financial engineering through buybacks. If net income stays flat at ~$160M and shares decline from 58.5M to 55M over the next 12-18 months, EPS rises mechanically from $2.73 to $2.91 — roughly 7% growth without any business improvement.

The bull case for organic growth requires either: (a) rate cuts reviving mortgage volumes (management expects 2 cuts in 2026), or (b) sustained broker-dealer strength from elevated market volatility and municipal issuance. Neither is high-confidence.

Current-year consensus of $2.38 appears conservative given Q1's $0.64 run-rate and positive revision momentum (+13.9% over 30 days). Full-year 2026 EPS of $2.50-$2.70 seems achievable, which at $37.80 implies 14-15x forward P/E.

The P/E premium is partially justified by: (1) higher capital return velocity, (2) diversified revenue streams (broker-dealer provides non-credit earnings), and (3) the option value of mortgage recovery. However, the premium also reflects: (1) a lower dividend yield than peers, (2) ROE of 7.1% which is below the peer group, and (3) geographic concentration risk.

Analyst consensus targets cluster tightly: $39.00-$40.00, with the median at $40.00. This implies 5.8% upside from the current $37.80. All four covering analysts rate HTH as HOLD.

At tangible book of $31.97, the stock would need to trade at 1.33x TBV to reach $42.50 (my estimated fair value if mortgage reaches breakeven and buybacks continue). This represents ~12.5% upside in a constructive scenario.

## Competitive Landscape

Hilltop occupies a differentiated position among regional banks due to its three-segment structure, but this diversification creates complexity more than synergy. The banking franchise is mid-tier in a competitive Texas market. The broker-dealer has genuine competitive advantages in Texas municipal advisory. The mortgage business is subscale relative to national competitors and lacks structural differentiation.

The broker-dealer's public finance franchise (76% Texas municipal revenues) provides stable, relationship-driven income with meaningful barriers to entry. However, it faces concentration risk — tax-reform changes to municipal bond treatment would directly threaten this business, and the 10-K explicitly identifies this as a risk factor.

Competitive position is stable but not improving in any measurable way. Loan growth at 4-6% is market-rate, not share-gaining. The mortgage business is losing share by design (prioritizing profitability over volume).

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (41% of loans):** In the current elevated-rate environment with CRE refinancing challenges accelerating nationally, Hilltop's $2.1B non-owner occupied CRE portfolio carries above-average risk. Total office exposure of $875M is particularly vulnerable.

**Geographic Concentration (Texas):** All three segments are heavily Texas-weighted. Texas economic deceleration — from energy price volatility, natural disaster, or cyclical downturn — would impact the entire company simultaneously.

**Uninsured Deposits (54%):** The 2023 bank failures demonstrated that concentrated uninsured deposits create runnable funding structures. At 54% uninsured ($5.9B), Hilltop has above-average vulnerability to confidence-driven deposit flight.

**Interest Rate Path:** If the Fed holds or hikes, NII benefits but mortgage recovery delays further. If cuts materialize, mortgage improves but NIM compresses. The company is not cleanly positioned for either scenario.

**Insider Selling:** Chairman Gerald Ford sold $9.9M in shares at ~$38 in March 2026. While the sale represents a small fraction of his total holdings, it occurred near the stock's 52-week high and coincides with multiple executive departures (four Item 5.02 8-K filings in the past 12 months). This warrants monitoring.

## Conclusion

Hilltop Holdings is a well-run, overcapitalized Texas financial holding company generating attractive total capital returns (~7.5% yield from buybacks and dividends) with a potential earnings uplift from mortgage recovery. At 14.1x trailing EPS — a 24% premium to peer median — the stock prices in much of the good news. Organic growth is limited to low-single-digits, NIM appears at peak, and the CRE/geographic concentration creates above-average downside risk in a stressed scenario. The founder's $9.9M share sale near the stock's high and recurring executive departures add qualitative caution.

The appropriate posture is HOLD for existing shareholders collecting the 2.1% dividend yield and benefiting from ongoing buyback-driven EPS accretion. New positions should wait for either a valuation pullback to ~11-12x earnings ($30-33) or fundamental confirmation that the mortgage segment has reached profitability. The analyst consensus (4 HOLDs, $39-40 targets) appropriately captures the limited upside from current levels.
