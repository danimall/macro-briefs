## Executive Summary

Valero Energy is experiencing an earnings inflection of historic magnitude. Quarterly EPS estimates for the current quarter have been revised upward 126% in 90 days (from $4.07 to $9.22), driven by the Strait of Hormuz disruption which has elevated US Gulf Coast refining margins to levels comparable to the 2022 post-pandemic super-cycle. Q1 2026 delivered a 32% earnings beat ($4.22 vs. $3.19 consensus), the fourth consecutive beat, with net income swinging from a $595M loss in Q1 2025 to $1.3B profit.

The stock has appreciated significantly from its 52-week low of $115.65. At approximately 9.2x current-year consensus earnings, VLO trades at a discount to peers when measured against forward earnings power in the current margin environment. The critical variable is the US-Iran peace deal framework — if the Strait reopens, refining margins compress rapidly and VLO's earnings power reverts toward the trough levels of 2024-early 2025.

Our maintained BUY recommendation reflects the asymmetry: at an estimated elevated probability of continued elevated margins through H2 2026, the expected value of holding VLO remains positive. However, the duration of the geopolitical disruption is inherently uncertain and investors should size positions accordingly.

## Company Overview

Valero is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner, operating 14 active refineries (after Benicia's April 2026 idling) with approximately 3.0 million barrels per day of throughput capacity. The business operates in three segments: Refining (dominant, ~95% of adjusted operating income in strong environments), Renewable Diesel (50% JV with Darling Ingredients), and Ethanol (12 plants, 1.7B gallons/year capacity).

The company has no upstream crude oil production. Valero benefits from crude supply disruptions that widen differentials between its feedstock costs (primarily Western Hemisphere crudes at discounts) and product prices (set by Brent-linked global markets). The Strait of Hormuz closure limits crude supply to Asian and European refiners while US Gulf Coast operators access discounted Canadian and domestic barrels.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

Revenue peaked at $176.4B in 2022 and fell to $122.7B in 2025, but this revenue decline masks improving unit economics. Adjusted operating income rose 16% YoY to $4.4B in 2025 as throughput volumes increased by 76K BPD and operational discipline improved. The GAAP decline to $2.3B net income was driven by the $1.1B California impairment (non-cash, non-recurring).

Q1 2026 represents a step-change: refining segment operating income of $1.8B in a single quarter compares to the full-year 2025 adjusted figure of $5.3B. Q1 2026 average Brent crude was $77.92/bbl according to Valero's earnings release — the $114-$118/bbl Brent levels driven by the Hormuz crisis emerged primarily in late April and May 2026. The Q1 strength was driven by improving crack spreads and operational recovery from the prior year's turnaround-heavy quarter, with the full Hormuz-driven super-cycle expected to manifest more fully in Q2 2026 results.

**Balance Sheet:**

- Total liquidity: approximately $10.8B as of Q1 2026 ($5.9B cash + credit facilities)
- Long-term debt: $9.2B total debt as of March 31, 2026
- Net debt-to-capitalization: 18% as of Q1 2026
- Investment-grade ratings at all three agencies (stable outlook)
- Altman Z-Score: 3.3 (safe zone)
- Current ratio: 1.65

The balance sheet is positioned to weather a margin downturn. Valero maintained IG ratings through the 2024 trough when net income fell to $2.8B, and the substantial liquidity buffer provides meaningful coverage during stress periods.

**Capital Return:**

In 2025, Valero returned $4.0B to shareholders ($2.6B buybacks + $1.4B dividends), representing 69% of operating cash flow. Share count has declined 22% in three years (395M → 309M). A new $2.5B buyback authorization in February 2026, combined with $1.7B remaining from prior programs, provides $4.2B of total capacity (~6% of current market cap). Valero has no obligation to complete these purchases, and actual repurchase activity will depend on market conditions and cash generation, but the authorization signals management confidence.

**Free Cash Flow:**

2025 operating cash flow was $5.826B with capital investments attributable to Valero of approximately $1.8B (including $1.6B of sustaining capital investments), yielding free cash flow of approximately $4.0B. 2026 capex is guided at $1.7B (attributable to Valero). If the elevated margin environment persists through H2 2026, FCF could improve significantly from the 2025 level, though the magnitude depends heavily on realized crack spreads in subsequent quarters.

## Growth Analysis

Valero's "growth" is almost entirely a function of refining margin cycles, not organic volume expansion. Throughput capacity is relatively stable. The growth case rests on:

1. **Cyclical tailwind (dominant factor):** Current-quarter EPS estimates revised up 126% in 90 days. This is a cyclical mean-reversion toward peak margins driven by the Hormuz disruption. The duration depends on geopolitics.

2. **Structural capacity optimization:** Exiting California (Benicia idled, Wilmington under strategic review) concentrates capital on higher-return Gulf Coast and Mid-Continent assets. The remaining refinery portfolio has superior complexity, waterborne access, and crude slate flexibility.

3. **Share count reduction:** The $4.2B buyback authorization, if fully executed at current prices, would represent approximately 6% of outstanding shares. From 309M shares today, sustained buybacks support EPS growth even on flat earnings.

4. **Ethanol segment stability:** $374M operating income in 2025 (+30% YoY) provides a baseline earnings stream independent of refining cycles.

5. **Renewable diesel (mixed):** This segment swung to a $156M operating loss in 2025 from $507M profit in 2024. However, Q1 2026 showed improvement with $139M operating income (vs. a $141M loss in Q1 2025). Tariffs on foreign feedstocks, loss of the blender's tax credit, and OBBB legislation (enacted July 2025, restricting credits to fuels from US/Mexico/Canada feedstocks for fuel produced on or after January 1, 2026) create structural headwinds, though higher RVOs for 2026-2027 may support demand. The 50/50 JV structure with Darling limits Valero's ability to restructure or exit.

The estimate revision velocity (+126.9% on current year in 90 days) represents the strongest BUY signal in our framework. However, the revisions reflect an exogenous geopolitical event, not an operational improvement that compounds. If Hormuz reopens, estimates reverse with similar velocity.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current multiples:**
- Trailing P/E: approximately 30-33x (on depressed $7.57 TTM EPS that includes the Q1 2025 loss quarter)
- Forward P/E (current year): approximately 9.2x based on $27.58 consensus FY26 EPS
- Dividend yield: approximately 1.9%

The elevated trailing P/E reflects the cyclical trough in TTM earnings rather than overvaluation — characteristic of cyclical companies exiting a downturn. The forward multiple on current-year consensus is more relevant for assessing value.

**Peer comparison:**
Among independent refiners, PSX and MPC trade at higher trailing multiples. VLO's forward multiple on current-year consensus earnings reflects the market's partial discounting of unsustainable peak margins.

**Valuation framework:**

The key question is what "normalized" EPS looks like. Three scenarios:
- **Bull case (margins persist):** FY26 EPS of $27-30 at 10x = $270-300. FY27 at $22-25 at 11x = $242-275.
- **Base case (partial normalization):** FY26 at $25-28, FY27 at $16-20. At 12x FY27 mid-cycle = $192-240.
- **Bear case (Hormuz reopens rapidly):** FY26 at $15-18 (H2 reverts to trough), FY27 at $10-12. At 14x mid-cycle = $140-168.

The consensus price target range of $181-$300 captures this distribution. Mean target of $258 implies modest upside from current levels in the base case.

## Competitive Landscape

Valero's competitive position is strengthening within the current cycle:

**Advantages:**
- Gulf Coast refinery concentration provides access to discounted Western Hemisphere crudes (WCS, Eagle Ford, Permian)
- Complex refinery configurations process heavy sour crude at wider discounts to Brent — these discounts expand during supply disruptions
- No upstream exposure means no earnings drag from crude price declines (unlike Exxon/Chevron)
- Export infrastructure allows capturing international product premiums
- Benicia exit removes a structurally disadvantaged asset

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Port Arthur fire (435K BPD, the largest refinery) limits capture of current margins. The refinery has resumed operations at reduced capacity, but the full extent of damage, repair timeline, and capital costs remain uncertain per SEC filings
- Pure refining exposure means no diversification cushion during margin troughs
- Renewable diesel JV with Darling faces structural policy headwinds

**Market position trajectory:** Improving. The California exit concentrates Valero on its highest-return assets. U.S. refinery capacity has been declining (LyondellBasell Houston closure, multiple east coast closures in prior years), tightening domestic supply/demand balances. Valero's remaining Gulf Coast/Mid-Continent portfolio is among the most cost-competitive globally.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geopolitical Binary (HIGH — immediate):** US-Iran peace deal negotiations create a binary outcome. The EIA's April 2026 forecast assumed the conflict does not persist past April and projected Brent declining below $90/bbl by Q4 2026 and averaging $76/bbl in 2027. If a deal materializes, oil could fall significantly and refining margins would compress rapidly. This is the single largest risk to the investment thesis.

**2. Port Arthur rebuild uncertainty (MODERATE — multi-quarter):** A fire damaged a distillate hydrotreater unit on March 23, 2026, prompting a full shutdown. The refinery has resumed operations at reduced capacity, but per Valero's 10-Q, the cause, full extent of damages, repair/replacement plan, timing, capital costs, and insurance proceeds remain uncertain and not reasonably estimable. This limits earnings capture during the current elevated margin environment at Valero's largest and most advantaged refinery.

**3. Renewable diesel uncertainty (LOW-MODERATE — ongoing):** While Q1 2026 showed improvement ($139M operating income), the segment faces structural headwinds from tariffs, OBBB feedstock restrictions, and RFS Set II regulatory proposals. The 50/50 JV structure limits Valero's operational flexibility.

**4. Cyclicality reversion (MODERATE — 6-18 months):** Even without a peace deal, refining margins eventually normalize as demand destruction at high prices reduces product demand, and supply-side responses (turnaround completions, capacity restarts) increase product availability.

## Conclusion

Valero merits a maintained BUY rating on the strength of estimate revision velocity (+127% in 90 days), consecutive earnings beats, a forward P/E of approximately 9.2x on current-year consensus, and the expectation that the geopolitical premium sustaining refining margins persists through H2 2026. The stock's appreciation from its low reflects genuine fundamental improvement — EPS estimates have moved faster than the stock.

Position sizing should reflect the binary geopolitical risk. VLO belongs in a portfolio at 2-3% weight (reduced 25% from full position per the geopolitical risk management framework), not as a concentrated position. The risk/reward remains positive on a probability-weighted basis given the magnitude of earnings capture in the persistence scenario, but the margin of safety has narrowed as the stock has appreciated.

The near-term peace deal window and the next 6 weeks (Port Arthur repair timeline disclosure, margin trajectory) will determine whether this cycle extends or compresses.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Multiple material corrections — including overstated 2025 FCF ($4.0B actual vs. $5.1B reported), unsupported geopolitical probability estimates, incorrect trailing P/E, and unverified Port Arthur damage specifics — reduce confidence in the valuation framework supporting the price target. The BUY recommendation is contingent on sustained elevated forward earnings that remain unvalidated by Q1 2026 realized results (Q1 Brent averaged $77.92, not $118).
