**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Churchill Downs Incorporated owns the Kentucky Derby, the longest continuously held annual sporting event in the U.S., and has built a high-margin Historical Racing Machine (HRM) network across Kentucky, Virginia, and New Hampshire that functions as a quasi-casino business in states without legalized casino gaming. FY2025 produced record net revenue of $2.93B (+7.0%) and record Adjusted EBITDA of $1.21B (+4.0%), with operating cash flow of $769.8M comfortably exceeding GAAP net income of $383M. Q1 2026 extended the trend: revenue $663M (+3%), net income $83M (+8%), Adjusted EBITDA $257M (+5%), led by the Live and Historical Racing segment.

The stock has fallen roughly 24% from its December 2025 high near $114 to ~$87, leaving it at approximately 12.7x the current-year consensus EPS of $6.87 versus a peer median P/E near 20x. The drawdown is driven by three identifiable concerns rather than a fundamentals collapse: regional gaming softness (Gaming segment EBITDA fell $23.9M in 2025), frustration over the absence of a long-rumored casino divestiture, and balance-sheet leverage (~$5B total debt against ~$1B GAAP equity, with $1.3B of notes maturing in 2027–2028). The Altman Z-Score of 0.93 reads as distress, but this is a formulaic artifact of buyback-driven thin equity and a large intangible base; actual covenant leverage of 1.4x secured net (versus a 4.0x limit) and interest coverage of 3.9x are comfortable.

We rate CHDN a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The franchise quality and forward valuation are attractive, and the April 2026 acquisition of the Preakness Stakes IP for $85M extends the moat with an asset-light royalty stream. The central re-rating catalyst is a regional-casino divestiture that would address both the growth-mix and leverage concerns simultaneously, but it remains undated; the HRM legal precedent set in Louisiana introduces a genuine tail risk; and the company sits in a consumer-discretionary segment facing a softening second-half spending backdrop. The setup is balanced at the current price.

## Company Overview

Churchill Downs is a 150-year-old racing, gaming, and online wagering operator headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky, organized into three reportable segments:

**Live and Historical Racing (~49% of revenue, $1.44B; ~$637M Adjusted EBITDA).** The fastest-growing and most strategically central segment. It includes Churchill Downs Racetrack and the Kentucky Derby franchise, plus an expanding fleet of approximately 10,190 Historical Racing Machines across Kentucky, Virginia, and New Hampshire. HRMs are slot-like devices whose outcomes are tied to previously run races, a regulatory structure that allows casino-style gaming under racing licenses in states without legalized casinos.

**Wagering Services and Solutions (~18%, $526M; ~$177M Adjusted EBITDA).** TwinSpires online horse-race wagering, the Exacta HRM technology platform (expanding internationally), and capital-light sports-betting market-access arrangements. CHDN serves as a B2B technology vendor and market-access provider to operators such as FanDuel and DraftKings rather than operating a national consumer sportsbook, which avoids the customer-acquisition cash burn that has pressured pure-play operators.

**Gaming (~36%, $1.05B; ~$483M Adjusted EBITDA).** Ten wholly owned regional casinos plus equity stakes in Rivers Des Plaines (61.3%) and Miami Valley Gaming (50%). This is the slower-growth, competition-exposed segment management is reportedly evaluating for divestiture. The equity-method properties are a meaningful and often-overlooked cash contributor — $36M distributed to CHDN in Q1 2026 alone.

The competitive structure splits cleanly: the Derby franchise and the license-protected HRM build-out are differentiated, high-margin, hard-to-replicate assets, while the regional casino portfolio is commoditized and structurally low-growth.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability trajectory.** Revenue grew from $1.81B (2022) to $2.46B (2023), $2.73B (2024), and $2.93B (2025), with the most recent year up on new HRM venue openings (The Rose, Owensboro, Roseshire, Casino Salem).

GAAP net income fell 10.3% to $383M from $426.8M, but the decline is almost entirely a non-cash $85.1M impairment of Chasers gaming rights (partially offset by a $40M settlement gain) plus higher interest expense. Adjusting for one-time items, underlying earnings were roughly flat. Operating margin compressed from 25.9% to 23.4%, driven by the impairment and higher depreciation from newly opened venues.

**Cash flow quality is high.** FY2025 operating cash flow was roughly double GAAP net income, and Q1 2026 generated $295M of operating cash flow. Capex is moderating toward $180–220M of maintenance-level spending, though major projects (the $280–300M First Turn building at Churchill Downs through 2026–2028, the $180–200M Rockingham Grand Casino redevelopment) keep near-term gross capex elevated.

**The balance sheet is the central debate.** Total debt was $5.16B at year-end 2025, reduced to $4.95B at Q1 2026 via revolver paydown. Against stockholders' equity near $1.0B, GAAP debt/equity is roughly 6.4x, and the current ratio of 0.6 plus the Altman Z-Score place the company in formulaic "distress" territory.

This reading is misleading for this business model. The thin equity base is a deliberate consequence of aggressive buybacks ($425M in 2025) and a $3.4B goodwill/intangible base, not operational stress. The metrics that govern actual financial flexibility are healthy: consolidated secured net leverage of 1.4x against a covenant limit of <4.0x, and interest coverage of 3.9x against a >2.5x requirement. The Z-Score's working-capital and equity ratios systematically penalize an asset-heavy, buyback-heavy operator. The caveat is real nonetheless: at ~$5B of debt with meaningful variable-rate exposure (a 1pp SOFR move costs roughly $14M), flexibility is constrained and the refinancing cliff matters.

**Refinancing wall.** $600M of 2027 notes (5.50%) and $700M of 2028 notes (4.75%) come due. Management states it is exploring refinancing via cash, revolver, and capital markets. The 2031 notes carry 6.75%, so refinancing at current rates would raise interest expense and pressure net income, and a springing maturity clause on the revolver/Term Loan A adds urgency. This is the channel through which the macro rate environment most directly touches the equity story.

**Capital returns.** Fifteen consecutive years of dividend increases (latest +7% to $0.438/share, a ~0.5% yield) and roughly $430M of remaining buyback authority. Buybacks cut the share count from ~74M (2024) to ~70M (Q1 2026).

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is the HRM network, not the casinos. The HRM build-out in Virginia and Kentucky effectively constructs a regional casino footprint in states that have not legalized traditional casinos — differentiated, license-protected, and high-margin. New venue openings (Owensboro Feb 2025, Roseshire Sept 2025, Marshall Yards Feb 2026, the Rosie's Richmond expansion) drove the FY2025 revenue record, and the announced Rockingham Grand Casino in New Hampshire (825 HRMs / 32 tables, opening mid-2027) extends the pipeline.

The Live and Historical Racing segment led Q1 2026 with +$11M EBITDA. NBC will air the Kentucky Oaks in prime time for the first time in 2026, and the planned First Turn building and Finish Line Suites/Mansion renovations support continued premiumization of the Derby franchise.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising: current-year consensus EPS was revised +2.9% over 90 days and flat over 30 days; next-year EPS of $7.27 was revised down 2.1% over 90 days. This does not meet the threshold of an accelerating earnings-inflection signal. The Q1 2026 beat (EPS $1.21 vs. $1.02, +18.2%) was encouraging, but the four-quarter record shows two beats and two misses, including a -8.0% Q4 2025 miss, which argues for confidence in consensus rather than a setup for large upward revisions.

The structural headwind worth naming: U.S. thoroughbred races fell 4.7% in 2025 to roughly 29,000, so the underlying racing industry is shrinking. CHDN's response — monetize the premium end (the Derby, now Preakness IP) while harvesting casino-style economics through HRMs — is the correct strategic answer, but it ties the growth story to the legal durability of the HRM structure.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$87, CHDN trades at approximately 12.7x current-year consensus EPS and 12.0x next-year EPS. The peer median in Casinos & Gaming is a P/E of 19.9x (RRR 19.9x, MCRI 21.6x; BYD at 3.8x and the negative readings at CZR/PENN reflect idiosyncratic items rather than a clean comparable set). On a forward basis, CHDN sits at roughly a 35–40% discount to the credible peers, despite owning the highest-quality single asset in the group.

Some of that discount is earned: higher leverage than most peers, the refinancing cliff, regional gaming exposure, and HRM legal risk all justify a multiple haircut. But the gap appears wider than the fundamentals warrant for a business generating $769.8M of operating cash flow and growing revenue at mid-single digits with a moat asset at its core. Analyst price targets — low $110, mean $137.25, median $138.50, high $155 — imply 27% to 78% upside, and the 12-analyst coverage is unanimously positive (3 strong buy, 9 buy, 0 hold/sell). We treat those targets cautiously given the absence of a near-term catalyst, but the directional signal is consistent with a meaningful discount.

The cleanest re-rating mechanism is a regional-casino divestiture. Such a transaction would simultaneously improve the growth mix (removing the drag from a low-growth, competition-exposed segment) and reduce leverage (using proceeds to pay down debt ahead of the 2027/2028 wall), addressing the two principal bear arguments at once. The problem is timing: the catalyst is undated, and the stock is being penalized for the delay.

We assign a valuation score of 6.8. The forward multiple is genuinely cheap relative to franchise quality and peers, but the discount is partly justified by leverage and legal risk, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rate. This is a reasonable price, not an obvious asymmetric entry.

## Competitive Landscape

The Kentucky Derby is a genuinely un-replicable asset with luxury sponsorship appeal, pricing power, and 150 years of brand equity. The April 2026 acquisition of the Preakness Stakes and Black-Eyed Susan Stakes IP — licensed back to the State of Maryland for an annual fee — gives CHDN control of two of the three Triple Crown jewels and creates an annuity-like, asset-light revenue stream. It was disclosed only as a subsequent event in the Q1 10-Q and is easily missed by standardized data pulls, a quiet but strategically powerful reinforcement of the moat.

The HRM network is a first-mover, license-protected position in attractive non-casino states, and the Exacta technology platform extends that advantage internationally. The capital-light sports-betting strategy (market-access fees from operators such as Bet365, Golden Nugget, FanDuel, DraftKings) avoids the cash burn that has impaired pure-play sportsbook operators.

Against these strengths, the FY2025 10-K added emphasis on two emerging threats. Prediction markets — CFTC-regulated event contracts on sports outcomes, currently subject to litigation — could divert wagering activity. iGaming, now live in seven states plus Maine, could cannibalize brick-and-mortar gaming. Neither is acute today, but both warrant monitoring. The score trajectory shows competitive position drifting from 7.5 to 7.0 over recent reports; we hold at 7.0, reflecting the franchise strength offset by the structural shrinkage of the racing industry and the new competitive vectors.

## Risk Assessment

**HRM regulatory/legal risk is the defining idiosyncratic exposure.** The Louisiana Supreme Court ruled the 2021 HHR Act unconstitutional (final May 8, 2025), forcing the shutdown of roughly 500 HRMs across 13 Louisiana OTBs (~$18.9M revenue / ~$8.1M EBITDA hit). This is a live precedent. With HRMs now driving the majority of growth and the machines heavily concentrated in Virginia and Kentucky, a successful constitutional or referendum challenge in a larger state would be materially more damaging than the Louisiana loss, an asymmetric tail risk that is difficult for the market to price.

**Leverage and refinancing.** Covenants are comfortable today, but the debt load with $1.3B maturing in 2027–2028, variable-rate exposure, and a thin equity cushion limit flexibility. Refinancing at current rates raises interest expense.

**Regional gaming weakness.** Gaming EBITDA fell on the Louisiana HRM cessation, increased competition at Rivers Des Plaines, and operational disruptions in Mississippi. The Q1 10-Q flags Presque Isle (PA) for potential future impairment.

**Kentucky Derby concentration.** A single weekend in early May drives a disproportionate share of high-margin revenue. Weather, an equine safety incident, a sponsorship loss, or a security event would be acutely damaging.

**Consumer cyclicality.** As a discretionary-spending business, CHDN is exposed to a softening consumer backdrop, particularly at the regional casino properties where the customer base skews more price-sensitive than the Derby franchise.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-constructive. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 35.7% against 31.6% one-year historical volatility — only modestly rich, and at the 7th percentile within the current chain, indicating the market is not pricing acute near-term event risk. The put/call skew is -5.8% (call-heavy), the put/call volume ratio is just 0.03, and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±4.1%. The elevated put/call open-interest ratio of 4.22 reflects standing hedges rather than fresh directional positioning, and that ratio has declined from 5.17 over the past month. The flat term structure (35.7% near vs. 36.4% far) shows uncertainty evenly distributed across tenors. The call-heavy near-term skew and falling put OI lean mildly positive, consistent with the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$130–140).** A regional-casino divestiture is announced, simultaneously de-risking the balance sheet (proceeds applied to the 2027/2028 wall) and re-rating the remaining business toward the higher-quality racing/HRM mix. The forward multiple closes most of the gap to the ~20x peer median, and the Derby/Preakness franchise plus the HRM pipeline (Rockingham mid-2027) drive continued mid-single-digit revenue growth. At ~20x forward EPS of $7.27, this implies ~$145. The bull target captures most but not all of that re-rating given execution and timing uncertainty. Analyst high target $155.

**Bear Case (~$72–75).** No divestiture materializes, an HRM legal challenge surfaces in a larger state (the Louisiana precedent generalizing), regional gaming weakness deepens into a consumer downturn, and refinancing the 2027/2028 notes at 6.75%+ rates pressures net income. The multiple stays compressed at ~10x forward earnings and the leverage overhang persists. This implies roughly 15% downside to the low-$70s.

**Base Case (~$100).** Solid franchise execution continues, HRM venues ramp, the Preakness IP adds incremental high-margin revenue, but the divestiture catalyst remains undated and leverage caps the multiple. A modest re-rate toward ~14x forward EPS of $7.27 implies ~$100, roughly 15% upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is constructive — a cheap forward multiple, a moat asset, an HRM pipeline, and a credible (if undated) de-leveraging catalyst — but the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, the catalyst is not on a clock, and the HRM legal tail plus consumer cyclicality cap conviction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Announcement of a regional-casino divestiture with proceeds earmarked for debt reduction, or a refinancing of the 2027/2028 notes at terms that do not materially raise interest expense.
- Positive estimate revisions exceeding +5% over 90 days alongside continued HRM venue ramp, signaling the market is catching up to franchise earnings power.
- Open-market insider buying at current depressed levels, which would convert the current neutral management signal into a conviction signal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A successful HRM constitutional/referendum challenge in a state larger than Louisiana (Virginia or Kentucky would be materially damaging).
- Covenant secured net leverage rising toward 3.0x, or a refinancing executed at rates that meaningfully compress net income.
- A guidance cut or Gaming-segment EBITDA decline accelerating beyond the FY2025 pace.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (the Derby quarter — historically the largest and highest-margin period, with the 152nd Derby benefiting from the prime-time Oaks broadcast), and any 8-K disclosure on divestiture or refinancing.

## Score Trajectory Context

The overall score has drifted from 6.4 (March) to 6.1 (May), with declines in Growth Prospects (6.5→6.0), Competitive Position (7.5→7.0), and Management Quality (6.5→6.0), against three consecutive HOLDs. The current data largely confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it: estimates are stable rather than rising, the divestiture catalyst remains absent, and management has not bought stock into the decline. We hold the overall at 6.1. The price has fallen further (from the high $90s toward $87), and on unchanged fundamentals that improves the entry math at the margin, but it does not by itself justify an upgrade; the absent catalyst and the HRM legal tail are the binding constraints, not the lower price. Management Quality stays at the low end of neutral (5.8): capital allocation is disciplined (15 years of dividend growth, sensible buyback cadence, the smart Preakness IP deal), but the lack of open-market buying at a 24% drawdown is a missed signal of conviction, and the buyback-thinned equity base, while a defensible choice, removes balance-sheet cushion.

## Conclusion

Churchill Downs is a high-quality franchise trading at a justified-but-overstated discount. The Kentucky Derby and now Preakness IP are irreplaceable, the HRM network is a differentiated growth engine, and the cash flow is high-quality, with operating cash flow nearly doubling GAAP net income. At ~12.7x forward earnings versus a ~20x peer median, the stock is cheap relative to franchise quality, and a casino divestiture would address both the growth-mix and leverage concerns in one move.

The reasons to wait rather than buy are concrete: the re-rating catalyst is undated, the HRM structure carries a genuine constitutional tail risk demonstrated in Louisiana, the 2027/2028 refinancing cliff sits in a higher-rate environment, and the business is exposed to a softening consumer. The setup is balanced. We rate CHDN a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale, and would upgrade on a confirmed divestiture, a clean refinancing, accelerating estimate revisions, or insider conviction buying.
