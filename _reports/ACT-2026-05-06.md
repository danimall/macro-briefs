## Executive Summary

Enact Holdings is a private mortgage insurer generating $670-690M in annual net income with 419 employees, producing ROE of approximately 13% and net margins above 54%. The stock trades at 9.4x forward earnings ($4.81 FY2026E), roughly in line with peer ESNT (9.3x) and at a slight premium to NMIH (8.4x), but with superior capital position (162% PMIERs sufficiency), an accelerating buyback program (up to $500M authorized), and recent rating upgrades from Fitch and Moody's with positive outlooks from S&P and A.M. Best.

The primary investment thesis rests on a compounding capital return story: with share count declining from 163M (2022) to ~141M currently and up to $500M in additional buyback authorization at current prices (~8% of market cap), EPS growth of 5-8% is achievable even with flat net income. The discount to fair value stems from two quantifiable overhangs — Genworth's 80% ownership creating governance complexity and suppressed float, and credit cycle normalization risk as delinquencies trend upward from historically depressed levels. Both are real but neither represents a binary risk: Genworth is selling proportionally (not dumping), and loss ratios remain at 11% against what management has indicated is a normalizing but still favorable environment.

Options pricing suggests significant near-term uncertainty, likely reflecting elevated short interest combined with thin liquidity rather than fundamental deterioration. This disconnect between elevated options pricing and improving fundamentals (rating upgrades, accelerating buybacks) supports the thesis that the stock is undervalued.

## Company Overview

Enact Holdings provides private mortgage insurance to U.S. mortgage lenders, covering losses on low-down-payment loans (>80% LTV) that are sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. The company has operated since 1981, went public in September 2021 as a minority IPO from parent Genworth Financial, and maintains approximately 15-18% market share among six active private MI competitors.

Key operating metrics as of year-end 2025:
- Insurance-in-Force (IIF): $273B primary
- Risk-in-Force (RIF): $71.4B primary
- New Insurance Written (NIW): $51.5B (2025)
- Customer count: ~1,600
- Weighted average FICO of IIF: 746
- Persistency: 82%

The business model is characterized by minimal variable costs once policies are in-force. Monthly premiums stream in against a fixed cost base, with investment income from a $6.1B portfolio providing supplemental earnings.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** Net income has been remarkably stable at $665-704M annually over the past four years despite varying origination environments. Net margins exceed 54%. Full-year 2025 ROE was 13.0% (adjusted operating ROE of 13.3%). Operating cash flow grew from $632M (2023) to $725M (2025), driven by investment income growth as portfolio yield expanded from 3.6% to 4.4%.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt-to-equity of approximately 0.14x ($744M long-term debt vs. $5.4B equity). PMIERs sufficiency at 162% ($1.9B excess). Risk-to-capital ratio of 10.1:1, well under the 25:1 regulatory maximum. $435M undrawn revolving credit facility. The balance sheet is conservatively managed relative to the regulatory minimum.

**Book Value Compounding:** GAAP BV/share was $37.66 at Dec. 31, 2025 (ex-AOCI BV/share of $37.87). At the current price of ~$43, the stock trades at approximately 1.14x GAAP book value, reasonable for a ~13% ROE business with strong capital return execution.

**Capital Returns:** $503M returned in 2025 ($382M buybacks + $121M dividends). New authorization of up to $500M in share repurchases approved in February 2026, with no specified expiration date. The 14% dividend increase (to $0.24/quarter, or $0.96 annualized) provides a 2.2% yield at current prices. Full execution of the buyback authorization at current prices would represent approximately 8% of market cap.

**Earnings Quality Concern:** Reserve releases contributed meaningfully to profitability: $200M in 2025, $252M in 2024. Current-period primary delinquencies contributed approximately $299M of loss expense in 2025, while GAAP losses incurred were $109.5M after favorable prior-year reserve development. As delinquencies normalize (2.62% and rising from 2.10% in 2023), reserve releases will diminish, creating an earnings headwind of perhaps $50-100M annually over the next 2-3 years.

## Growth Analysis

Enact is not a high-growth business. Revenue grew from $1.10B (2022) to $1.24B (2025) — approximately 4% CAGR. Premium income is essentially flat ($957M → $981M, 2023-2025) as higher persistency offsets lower origination volumes. Growth in total revenue has been driven primarily by investment income ($207M → $266M over the same period).

EPS growth is more compelling due to buyback-driven share count reduction: from 163M (2022) to ~141M currently. If the up-to-$500M buyback authorization is substantially executed at ~$43/share, shares outstanding could decline approximately 8% over the execution period, converting flat net income into 5-8% EPS growth. Consensus estimates reflect this: $4.81 FY2026E and $5.06 FY2027E (5.2% growth).

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current year up 3.4% over 90 days, next quarter up 2.8%. This is not a high-velocity revision story, but the direction is constructive.

Upside growth scenarios: (1) rate cuts drive origination volumes higher, boosting NIW and eventually IIF/premium; (2) continued investment income growth as the portfolio rolls at higher yields; (3) GSE reform that increases private MI penetration. Downside: recession compresses NIW, reserve releases reverse, and loss ratio normalizes to 20%+.

## Valuation Assessment

At $43.06, ACT trades at:
- 9.4x trailing and forward P/E (on $4.52 trailing, $4.81 FY2026E)
- ~1.14x GAAP book value ($37.66 GAAP BV/share)
- ~6.0x pre-tax operating income

**Peer comparison:** ESNT at 9.3x P/E with 2.2% yield; NMIH at 8.4x with no dividend; WD at 31.1x (different business model — commercial mortgage origination). ACT's 9.4x multiple with 2.2% yield and 162% PMIERs sufficiency is broadly in line with peers but doesn't reflect the rating upgrade trajectory or buyback acceleration.

**Discount to intrinsic value:** A ~13% ROE business growing BV at high-single-digit rates annually, with consistent earnings and aggressive buybacks, should command 1.2-1.5x book in a normal environment. That implies $45-57 fair value range. The Genworth overhang (governance discount, suppressed float) plausibly explains a 0.8-1.0x discount to standalone value. As Genworth gradually reduces ownership and governance complexity diminishes, this discount should narrow.

**Specific mispricing thesis:** The market prices ACT as if reserve releases will collapse imminently and loss ratios will spike to historical stress levels. In reality, the existing portfolio has strong credit characteristics (746 FICO average, embedded HPA of 30-50% on 2020-2022 vintages), and loss ratios likely normalize to 12-18% rather than 25%+. At a 15% steady-state loss ratio with flat premiums, net income moderates to $580-620M — still supporting $4.30-4.60/share in EPS with the reduced share count, or 10x at current price. Buyback accretion then compounds this to $5+ within 18 months.

## Competitive Landscape

The private MI industry is a stable oligopoly with six participants. Barriers to entry are high: PMIERs capital requirements, multi-year data needed for risk models, GSE approval process, and rating agency requirements. No new entrant has emerged in over a decade.

Enact's competitive position is stable rather than gaining or losing share materially. The company maintains diverse customer relationships (~1,600 customers), though its largest customer accounted for 22% of NIW and 12% of total revenues in 2025. Rating upgrades in 2025 (Moody's to A2, Fitch to A) along with positive outlooks from S&P and A.M. Best provide competitive advantage as some institutional counterparties require minimum ratings for CRT transactions.

Risk-based pricing transparency is declining as technology tools increasingly commoditize MI selection on price alone. This creates modest long-term margin pressure across the industry but hasn't yet materially affected profitability for any participant.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit Cycle Normalization (Moderate):** Delinquencies rising from 2.10% → 2.62%, loss ratio from 4% → 11%. This is normalization, not deterioration, and embedded HPA provides meaningful loss mitigation. However, 2020-2022 vintages reaching peak default years (3-5 years post-origination) with some higher-DTI concentrations (24% with DTI >45%) creates upward loss pressure through 2027.

**GSE Reform (Low probability, High impact):** PSPA amendment establishing conservatorship exit process creates theoretical tail risk. Privatization could either expand or contract the MI role. We estimate the probability of material GSE reform within 18 months is low, based on political dynamics and institutional inertia, though this remains inherently uncertain.

**Genworth Overhang (Structural, Diminishing):** Suppresses valuation but Genworth is not a forced seller. Proportional selling maintains ~80% ownership for tax purposes. This overhang resolves slowly (years, not quarters) but represents quantifiable discount rather than fundamental impairment.

**Recession Scenario:** Under a severe economic downturn, claims would increase materially. However, 162% PMIERs sufficiency plus $1.9B in CRT protection provides substantial buffer. The 2008-2012 experience (when MI losses were catastrophic) occurred with far weaker credit quality, no CRT programs, and minimal embedded HPA.

**Customer Concentration:** Enact's largest customer accounted for 22% of NIW and 12% of total revenues in 2025 (up from 19% of NIW in 2023). While the company serves approximately 1,600 customers overall, loss of its largest customer would materially impact volumes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-55, +16-28%):** Buyback accretion drives EPS to $5.30+ by FY2027. Loss ratio normalizes at 12-15% due to embedded HPA and strong portfolio credit quality. Rating upgrades continue (S&P and AM Best both on Positive outlook). Genworth gradually reduces stake, improving float and governance. Market re-rates to 10-11x forward earnings as overhang diminishes. P/B expands to 1.2-1.3x growing book value.

**Bear Case ($34-36, -16-21%):** Recession materializes, loss ratio spikes to 25%+, reserve releases fully reverse (net $100-150M headwind). Buyback suspended to preserve capital. Genworth sells shares to fund parent obligations at inopportune time, creating technical pressure on thin float. Stock returns to 7-8x trough earnings of $3.50-4.00.

**Base Case ($48-50, +12-16%):** Modest EPS growth of 5-7% through buyback accretion. Loss ratio normalizes to 14-16%. Rating trajectory continues to improve. Stock trades toward 10x $4.81 FY2026E earnings as Genworth overhang gradually fades.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $48-50 over 12-18 months. Math: 10x forward P/E on $4.81-5.06 FY2026-27E EPS, reflecting modest multiple expansion from current 9.4x as rating upgrades and buyback execution continue.

**Bull case target:** $55 (18 months). Requires: multiple expansion to 11x + EPS growth to $5.00+ from buyback accretion + favorable loss development.

**Bear case target:** $35 (-17% from current). Requires: recession + loss ratio >20% + buyback suspension.

**Upside/downside:** +16-28% upside to base/bull; -17% downside to bear. Risk/reward of approximately 2:1.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with quarterly earnings as natural checkpoints. Next catalyst: Q2 2026 earnings (likely August 2026) to confirm loss ratio trajectory and capital return execution.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Loss ratio exceeds 18% for two consecutive quarters (would signal credit cycle turning faster than embedded HPA can absorb)
2. PMIERs sufficiency falls below 140% (would constrain buyback capacity)
3. Genworth announces accelerated sell-down below 80% ownership threshold (would create technical pressure and tax implications)

## Conclusion

Enact Holdings is a high-quality, capital-light financial compounder trading at a modest premium to GAAP book value due to quantifiable structural overhangs (Genworth ownership, credit cycle normalization fears) that are gradually resolving. The 9.4x forward P/E provides adequate margin of safety against moderate credit deterioration. The up-to-$500M buyback authorization at current prices can mathematically drive 5-8% EPS accretion even with flat net income, while the 2.2% dividend yield provides income support. The Genworth-driven thin float (~19% public) creates technical dynamics that may amplify short-term volatility but do not reflect fundamental impairment.

BUY with a 12-18 month horizon and $48-50 base case target.
