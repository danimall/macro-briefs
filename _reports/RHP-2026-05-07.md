## Executive Summary

Ryman Hospitality Properties operates five Gaylord Hotels mega-convention resorts and two JW Marriott properties totaling 11,869 rooms, plus an entertainment portfolio centered on the Grand Ole Opry. The company owns 5 of the top 6 largest non-gaming convention hotels in the U.S. by meeting space, with 400,000+ square feet per property creating effective barriers to new competition. Revenue grew 10.2% to $2.58B in 2025, with Q1 2026 posting record results ($664.6M revenue, FFO $2.32/share beating consensus by 14%) and a 27% surge in net definite group room night bookings.

The core tension is between this genuinely differentiated competitive position and a capital structure carrying $4.0B in debt against $750M in equity, with net income declining 28% over the past two years due to rising interest expense and depreciation from acquisition-funded growth. At $110.42, the stock trades at 29x trailing earnings and sits within 2% of its 52-week high. Analyst consensus is $117.20 (6% upside to mean), and all 15 covering analysts rate it Buy or Strong Buy — but that unanimity itself limits incremental positive positioning. The booking recovery and record Q1 are positive, though the elevated rate environment creates meaningful refinancing risk on the $700M of 4.75% notes maturing October 2027.

## Company Overview

Ryman Hospitality Properties is a self-advised REIT (converted from C-corp in 2013) that owns and manages two complementary business segments:

**Hospitality (83% of revenue):** Five Gaylord Hotels properties (Nashville, Orlando, Dallas, Washington DC, Denver) plus two JW Marriott properties (San Antonio, acquired June 2023; Phoenix/Desert Ridge, acquired June 2025). Total capacity: 11,869 rooms with extensive meeting and convention space. All properties are operated by Marriott International under long-term management agreements extending to 2027-2060.

**Entertainment (~17% of revenue, ~70% owned through OEG):** Grand Ole Opry, Ryman Auditorium, Ole Red venues (6 locations), Category 10 venues (Luke Combs-themed), Block 21 in Austin, Southern Entertainment (music festivals), and amphitheater management. A ~30% minority interest is held by Atairos/NBCUniversal.

The Gaylord Hotels concept is self-contained convention resorts where guests stay, meet, dine, and recreate without leaving the property. This drives Total RevPAR of $491/night — well above industry averages — through high food & beverage capture rates. Approximately 73% of rooms are sold to group business, contracted years in advance, providing multi-year revenue visibility.

Organic growth (ADR increases of 3-5% annually, occupancy recovery) is supplemented by inorganic additions: JW Marriott Hill Country (2023) and Desert Ridge (2025) contributed incremental revenue but also $625M in new debt.

**Profitability — Compression Under Weight of Capital Structure:**

Operating income was effectively flat at $487M in 2025 despite 10.2% top-line growth. The math: operating expense growth of 13.1% outpaced revenue growth, reflecting construction disruption at Opryland and Texan plus higher depreciation ($278M, up from $236M). Net income fell to $247M (-11.7% YoY, -28% from 2023's $342M peak), driven almost entirely by interest expense rising to $241M in 2025 from $211M in 2023.

**REIT-Relevant Metrics:**
- Adjusted EBITDAre: $794.7M (+4.9%)
- Adjusted FFO: $539.6M (+2.2%)
- FFO/share: ~$8.65 (implying 12.8x P/FFO at current price)
- Dividend: ~$4.50/share annualized (4.1% yield), payout ratio vs. FFO approximately 52%

**Balance Sheet — Highly Leveraged:**
- Total debt: $4.0B
- Debt/equity: 6.62x
- Weighted average interest rate: 6.5% (88% fixed after swaps)
- Interest coverage: OCF $590M / interest $241M = 2.4x
- Available liquidity: $471M cash + $780M revolver = $1.25B
- No maturities until October 2027 ($700M at 4.75%)

The capital structure is aggressive but manageable given the strong OCF generation and no near-term maturities. The revolver was upsized to $850M in January 2026, extending maturity to 2030. However, refinancing the $700M of 4.75% notes in 2027 at current market rates (6.5%+) would add approximately $12-15M in annual interest expense.

**Free Cash Flow vs. Dividends:**
2025 FCF of $232M was exceeded by $291M in dividends — a ~$59M shortfall funded through the REIT's debt/equity issuance model. This is structural to the REIT format but constrains financial flexibility during periods of heavy capital investment ($350-450M annual capex through 2027).

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers:**
1. **Booking recovery:** Q1 2026 net definite group room nights booked surged 27% — the strongest first quarter since 2018. Group room nights on books for all future years are 1.2% higher with 5.3% higher estimated ADR. This suggests the -10.5% same-store booking decline in 2025 (attributed to "economic policy uncertainty") was temporary.

2. **Construction completion:** Gaylord Opryland's $225M expansion and Gaylord Texan's room renovation both depressed same-store performance in 2025. As these complete, underlying occupancy and RevPAR should recover.

3. **Desert Ridge integration:** Operating at only 57.7% occupancy in its first partial year (seasonal Phoenix market), JW Marriott Desert Ridge posted a $779K operating loss. Full-year contribution in 2026-2027 should be meaningfully positive as the property reaches stabilized occupancy.

4. **Entertainment expansion:** Category 10 Las Vegas (late 2026), Category 10 at Universal Orlando CityWalk (late 2027), Ole Red Indianapolis (late 2027), plus amphitheater growth.

**Estimate revisions:**
Current year EPS estimate: $3.98 (up 6.7% over 90 days). Next year: $4.83 (up 3.1% over 90 days). These are modest positive revisions — not the 30%+ velocity that signals strong BUY on inflection. The Q1 beat ($1.03 vs. $0.81 estimate, +26.5%) was strong, but the prior two quarters were mixed (miss in Q4 2025, beat in Q3 2025).

**Growth constraints:**
- Consumer/corporate spending sensitivity in a stagflationary environment
- Construction disruption continues through 2027
- Desert Ridge operating loss during ramp
- Southern Entertainment weather vulnerability
- The group convention business, while contracted in advance, faces cancellation risk if economic conditions deteriorate sharply (2025 showed +27K cancelled room nights)

## Valuation Assessment

At $110.42, RHP trades at:
- 29x trailing P/E
- ~28x forward P/E on $3.98 CY2026 EPS estimate
- ~23x forward P/E on $4.83 CY2027 EPS estimate
- ~12.8x P/FFO (more relevant for REIT)
- 4.1% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 22.6x, with APLE at 19.3x and DRH at 22.6x. RHP's 29x premium is partially justified by its differentiated competitive position, higher growth rate, and entertainment diversification. However, the premium is substantial (28% above peer median P/E). The peer median yield is 3.6% vs. RHP's 4.1% — relatively in line.

**P/FFO context:** At 12.8x P/FFO, RHP trades at a meaningful premium to traditional hotel REITs (typically 8-11x), but the convention/entertainment hybrid model and above-average organic growth warrant some premium.

**Analyst targets:** Mean $117.20 (6.1% upside), median $115 (4.1% upside), range $105-$133. The stock is currently above the low target and within 6% of consensus mean. With all 15 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy, there is limited room for incremental upgrades to drive re-rating.

The stock has appreciated 18% over the past year and sits 2% below the 52-week high of $112.50. At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced: meaningful upside requires either (a) a material acceleration in bookings/RevPAR beyond current guidance, or (b) a decline in long-term interest rates that reduces refinancing risk and improves REIT valuations broadly.

## Competitive Landscape

RHP's competitive position is genuinely exceptional. The five Gaylord Hotels properties collectively hold 5 of the top 6 positions among non-gaming U.S. convention hotels by meeting space. Each property has 400,000+ sq ft of contiguous meeting space, creating a self-contained ecosystem that smaller hotels cannot replicate. The capital requirements to build comparable facilities (typically $500M+ per property) and multi-year development timelines create structural barriers.

**Key competitive dynamics:**
- **Pricing power:** Total RevPAR of $491/night reflects the capture of food, beverage, and ancillary revenue that drives margins above pure room-rate hotels.
- **Multi-year visibility:** 73% group mix with bookings placed years in advance provides revenue line-of-sight unavailable to transient-focused peers.
- **Entertainment synergy:** Nashville assets (Opry, Ryman Auditorium, Ole Red) drive incremental leisure demand to Gaylord Opryland, the largest property.

**Primary competitive threat — Gaylord Pacific:** Marriott opened a 1,600-room, 360,000 sq ft meeting space property in Chula Vista, CA in May 2025 under the Gaylord brand — but NOT owned by RHP. Marriott is deploying the brand that RHP developed and owns against RHP's interests. The 10-K specifically flags this as a risk, though management has not publicly addressed the strategic implications. If Marriott continues expanding the Gaylord brand without RHP ownership, it erodes the exclusivity premium.

**Marriott concentration:** All hospitality operations are managed by Marriott under contracts extending to 2027-2060. RHP cannot change operators or operational approaches without enormous cost and disruption. This creates structural alignment (Marriott incentivized to maximize RevPAR for fee income) but also dependence.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Interest Rate / Refinancing Risk (HIGH):**
The $700M of 4.75% senior notes maturing October 2027 will almost certainly be refinanced at higher rates — recent RHP issuances priced at 6.50%. In a persistently elevated rate environment, the cost of capital for levered REITs remains punishing. Each 100bps increase on $700M adds $7M in annual interest.

**2. Economic Sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH):**
Corporate group bookings are inherently tied to corporate profitability and confidence. The 2025 episode (-10.5% same-store bookings, +27K cancellations) demonstrated vulnerability to policy uncertainty. If consumer/corporate conditions deteriorate further, the booking recovery could stall. However, the multi-year forward booking structure provides a buffer.

**3. Leverage in a Potential Credit Stress Environment (MODERATE):**
At 6.62x debt/equity with HY spreads potentially widening, RHP's debt costs could increase at refinancing. The company carries high-yield debt, meaning any credit market dislocation directly impacts its borrowing costs. Current interest coverage of 2.4x provides margin but is below investment-grade comfort levels.

**4. Gaylord Pacific Brand Dilution (MODERATE):**
Marriott's decision to open a competing Gaylord property without RHP ownership introduces a dynamic that could dilute brand exclusivity over time. If additional third-party Gaylord properties are developed, RHP's pricing premium narrows.

**5. Construction Execution (MODERATE):**
$350-450M annual capex through 2027 creates ongoing disruption risk at individual properties. Opryland's 2025 revenue declined 2.3% during construction, and Texan's RevPAR fell 4.1% during renovation.

- Construction disruption ends at Opryland/Texan, releasing pent-up occupancy
- Desert Ridge reaches 70%+ occupancy within 12 months
- Interest rates moderate, compressing REIT cap rates and reducing refinancing risk
- Entertainment pipeline (Category 10 LV, Universal) opens on time, contributing incremental FFO

- Gaylord Pacific brand dilution intensifies (additional third-party properties announced)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**

- Interest coverage ratio falls below 2.0x
- Additional Gaylord-branded properties announced outside RHP ownership

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (likely late July 2026 for Q2 results). Monitor monthly group booking pace disclosures and any management commentary on the 2027 refinancing strategy.

## Conclusion

Ryman Hospitality Properties occupies a genuinely differentiated competitive position in the group convention hotel market, with structural barriers to new supply and multi-year revenue visibility. The Q1 2026 results and 27% booking recovery are encouraging after 2025's temporary slowdown. However, at $110.42 (29x P/E, 12.8x P/FFO, 2% below 52-week high), the stock prices in most of the known positives. The 4.1% dividend yield provides income support, and FFO growth of 3-7% annually should drive mid-single-digit returns. Balanced against this are $4B in debt with refinancing approaching in 2027, sensitivity to economic conditions, and the unresolved Gaylord Pacific brand dilution question. With analyst consensus offering only 6% upside and the options market pricing elevated near-term uncertainty, the appropriate posture is to hold for the income and growth trajectory while awaiting a more attractive entry point or stronger fundamental acceleration.
