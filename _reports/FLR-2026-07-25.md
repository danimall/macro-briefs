**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fluor is a self-help story dressed in misleading GAAP financials. The reported 2025 operating loss of -$378M and negative operating cash flow of -$387M are almost entirely artifacts of a single one-time item: the $643M revenue reversal and $649M cash payment tied to the Santos judgment in Australia. Strip that out and the underlying business is a thin-margin engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) services firm generating roughly $500M of adjusted EBITDA on ~$15.5B of revenue. The 2024 "profit" of $2.1B is equally uninformative, inflated by tax benefits and NuScale mark-to-market gains against pretax income of only $613M. Any analysis that treats trailing GAAP as representative of the franchise will reach the wrong conclusion in both directions.

The genuine event of the last twelve months is the balance-sheet transformation. Fluor monetized its entire NuScale stake for $2.43B since September 2025 and is deploying the proceeds into a ~$1.4B 2026 buyback that has already retired roughly 16% of shares year-over-year (169M to 142M). With minimal net debt ($1.07B gross against $3.2B cash and securities) and the single largest source of earnings volatility now removed, management can produce meaningful EPS accretion even on flat operating income. Guidance for 2026 is adjusted EBITDA of $525-560M and adjusted EPS of $2.60-2.80, putting the stock at roughly 18-20x adjusted earnings.

The offset is that the operating business is not growing and the legacy legal tail remains live and unpredictable. New awards fell to $2.7B in Q1 2026 from $5.8B a year earlier, backlog contracted to $25.7B from $28.5B, segment profit collapsed to $8M (0.2% margin) on a $96M DOD False Claims charge and a Middle East mining cost overrun, and near-term consensus EPS is drifting lower. A newly filed securities class action challenges the central turnaround thesis by alleging management overstated the effectiveness of its risk mitigation. At a peer-comparable multiple with balanced options positioning and no open-market insider signal, this is a HOLD. We assign an overall score of 5.05, consistent with the recent trajectory.

## Company Overview

Fluor is one of the world's largest publicly traded EPC firms, headquartered in Irving, Texas. It operates through three reportable segments:

- **Urban Solutions** (~$2.4B Q1 2026 revenue; $19.0B backlog): the largest and growth-oriented segment, spanning mining & metals, life sciences, advanced technology/data centers, infrastructure, and the professional staffing business.
- **Energy Solutions** (~$0.7B Q1 2026 revenue; $4.3B backlog): refining, chemicals, LNG, and energy transition. The highest-margin segment in Q1 2026 at 10.5%.
- **Mission Solutions** (~$0.5B Q1 2026 revenue; $2.5B backlog): U.S. government work including DOE nuclear site management, DOD support, and disaster response, with ~$1.1B of unfunded government backlog.

Total backlog was $25.7B at March 31, 2026, down from $28.5B a year earlier. The most important structural feature is that 82% of backlog is now reimbursable (cost-plus) rather than lump-sum, a deliberate shift away from the fixed-price contracting that produced years of losses and a 2019-2020 accounting restatement. This lowers the risk of catastrophic project write-downs but caps margin upside. Roughly 58% of Q1 2026 revenue is at-cost pass-through, so headline revenue substantially overstates the true earning base ("adjusted net revenue"), and reported margins understate the underlying services economics.

Fluor competes with EMCOR, KBR, Jacobs, AECOM, Worley, and privately held Bechtel in a fragmented and intensely competitive global market. Its scale, integrated self-perform capability, and DOE/government franchise are the principal differentiators.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** was $15.5B in 2025, down ~5% from $16.3B in 2024 and roughly flat versus $15.5B in 2023. Growth is muted, and backlog conversion is the swing factor.

**The reported 2025 loss is a one-time distortion.** GAAP shows an operating loss of -$378M and negative gross profit, driven almost entirely by the Santos revenue reduction (a Queensland court upheld a referee finding that the ~AUD 236M contractual liability cap did not apply). Fluor paid $649M cash to Santos in December 2025. Adjusting for this, management's 2025 adjusted EBITDA was ~$499M, below its internal target and confirming a weak operating year, but a very different picture from the headline loss.

**Segment economics are thin and lumpy.** Q1 2026 total segment profit was just $8M (0.2% margin) versus $131M (3.3%) a year earlier, dragged by a $96M DOD legal charge in Mission Solutions and $37M of cost growth on a Middle East mining JV in Urban Solutions. The business runs at low-single-digit segment margins, and even small project or legal items swing quarterly results materially.

**The balance sheet has been transformed.** The completed NuScale divestiture generated cash via variable-price forward sale agreements ($1.35B in February 2026 for 71M shares; $473M in April 2026 for the final 40M shares). Cash and marketable securities stood at $3.2B at March 31, 2026, up from $2.2B at year-end. Debt is modest ($506M of 4.25% 2028 notes, $575M of 1.125% convertible 2029 notes), with no revolver borrowings and $903M of available facility capacity after letters of credit. This is an appropriately low leverage profile for a project-based EPC firm and supports a Financial Health score above the midpoint despite the reported loss.

**Cash flow was negative in 2025 but recovered.** FY2025 operating cash flow was -$387M (FCF -$437M), driven by the Santos payment and working-capital swings. Q1 2026 operating cash flow rebounded to +$110M from -$286M in Q1 2025. The FCF conversion issue is Santos-specific, not a structural accrual problem, but the market cannot yet see a clean cash-generation quarter through the noise.

**Capital allocation is aggressive on buybacks.** Fluor is redeploying NuScale proceeds into repurchases. It bought back 11M shares for $516M in Q1 2026 alone, and the board expanded the authorization by 30M shares. The share-count reduction is the primary lever driving EPS accretion on otherwise flat operating earnings.

The earnings-surprise record is a caution: over the last four quarters, Fluor posted one beat and three misses, capped by a -77.5% miss in Q1 2026 ($0.14 actual versus $0.62 estimate). Visibility into quarterly EPS is poor given the legal-charge cadence.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the story and has deteriorated across recent reports. New awards were 55% international, and the prior-year quarter benefited from a large life-sciences award. Remaining performance obligations of $24.5B provide revenue visibility but not acceleration.

Estimate revisions are mixed but tilting negative on the near-term picture. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose +7.7% over 90 days to $0.70, and next-quarter estimates rose +16.5% to $0.85, but both were flat-to-down over the trailing 30 days. Current-year EPS was cut -7.0% over 90 days to $2.57, and next-year estimates are essentially flat at $3.27. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings acceleration; forward estimates are broadly stable to falling. The management guidance narrowing (from $2.60-3.00 to $2.60-2.80 adjusted EPS, citing Middle East disruption) reinforces the cautious read.

The sector tailwinds are real: data-center/AI infrastructure, reshoring, life sciences capacity, mining for energy-transition metals, and LNG all support front-end engineering activity, which management describes as robust. The July 2026 Saudi Aramco Program Management Consultancy Long-Term Agreement is a meaningful reimbursable franchise win that lifted the stock. But Fluor has deliberately traded growth for risk reduction with its reimbursable backlog, which structurally caps the margin and growth upside it can extract from those tailwinds. The infrastructure-demand theme is confirmed broadly across the data-center supply chain, but Fluor participates as a lower-margin services provider rather than a high-operating-leverage beneficiary.

We score Growth Prospects at 4.8, consistent with the declining trajectory from 6.0. The bottom-line growth over the next 12-18 months comes from buybacks, not operating expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.19, Fluor trades at roughly 19x the midpoint of guided 2026 adjusted EPS ($2.70) and ~16x the $3.27 next-year consensus. Against the Construction & Engineering peer set, this is not a discount: the peer median P/E is 32.9x, but that median is heavily skewed by growth names (MYRG 46.8x, GVA 37.5x, ECG 32.9x, ACA 28.4x). The more relevant comparable is TTEK at 16.6x, and Fluor's forward multiple sits close to that. Fluor lacks a dividend (0% yield versus a peer median of 0.2%), removing any absolute-yield support.

A "cheap"-looking multiple is not itself a buy signal; the question is whether there is a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock. The bull answer is EPS accretion: a ~16% share-count reduction converts a flat operating business into meaningful per-share earnings growth, and if buybacks continue in 2027, the $3.27 next-year estimate could prove conservative. The bear answer is that the multiple already reflects this. 16-19x forward on a low-margin, no-growth services firm with a live legal tail is a fair-to-full multiple, not a screaming discount, and the buyback windfall is one-time (the NuScale cash is now largely deployed).

The stock is roughly at analyst consensus (mean $52.44, median $52.50), with a target range of $40-64. The "analyst targets are floors" principle does not apply here because Fluor is not an inflection stock; estimates are falling on the current year rather than accelerating, so consensus targets are not stale in the bullish direction. We score Valuation at 4.8: the multiple is reasonable but not asymmetric, and the compressed-versus-peers optics reflect genuine structural constraints (thin margins, litigation, no growth) rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Fluor's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding. Its scale, self-perform capability, and DOE/government franchise remain genuine moats, and the reimbursable-backlog shift materially de-risks the model versus the fixed-price disasters of the late 2010s. The Aramco PMC win and robust front-end engineering activity confirm the franchise still competes for large international programs.

The trajectory is the concern. 2025 total shareholder return of 248 lagged the peer group at 388, the first year of clear relative underperformance after several years of outperformance. EMCOR and other peers have delivered far stronger returns. Backlog is shrinking, new awards are down sharply, and the strategic decision to prioritize contract-structure safety over aggressive bidding means Fluor is structurally ceding the higher-margin lump-sum work to competitors willing to take that risk. This is a defensible choice given Fluor's history, but the company is not gaining share or widening its moat; it is stabilizing a franchise that had been impaired. We score Competitive Position at 5.0, in line with the declining trend from 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk here is above sector norms and not fully priced, which drives a below-midpoint Risk Factors score of 4.5.

**Legacy litigation tail (the defining risk).** The Santos judgment (appeal scheduled July 2026, insurance recovery unresolved with five of 30+ insurers initially disputing coverage) shows the magnitude of legacy exposure. Legacy loss projects still in backlog carry ~$181M of unfunded losses, and two substantially complete Kuwait lump-sum projects have ~$343M in outstanding letters of credit awaiting change-order resolution.

**New securities and derivative litigation.** A securities class action filed September 2025 (N.D. Texas) alleges misleading statements from February to July 2025 about market conditions, rising costs on three infrastructure projects, and the effectiveness of risk mitigation, a direct legal challenge to the turnaround narrative. Four derivative actions and a books-and-records demand followed. A separate NuScale-related class action (February 2026, D. Oregon) names Fluor as NuScale's controlling shareholder.

**Recurring adverse rulings.** The Q1 2026 $96M DOD False Claims charge (a 2013 Afghanistan matter, jury found for Fluor on 3 of 4 claims but awarded $15M subject to trebling, under post-trial challenge) followed a $28M reserve in the prior-year quarter, a recurring pattern of lumpy, hard-to-model legal charges.

**Credit-rating collateral trigger.** A one-notch downgrade to BB/Ba2 would force Fluor to collateralize its credit facility with liens on U.S. assets. Given repeated GAAP losses, this is an underappreciated tail risk, though the $3.2B cash position mitigates near-term concern.

**Geopolitical concentration.** Management already trimmed 2026 guidance for Middle East disruption, and its stated positioning for future project work in Venezuela is a high-risk frontier bet.

**Governance transition.** A near-complete C-suite and chairman turnover (Breuer as new CEO effective July 2025, Constable exiting via a consulting agreement, ex-CFO Brennan and Reynolds also on consulting arrangements) during active litigation and strategic repositioning adds execution uncertainty.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is flashing near-term stress but not a directional bear thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 63.7% is rich versus 54.7% one-year historical vol and sits at the 84th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (63.7% near-term versus 50.8% at six months); the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print. The skew is call-heavy (-3.8%) with a put/call volume ratio of just 0.13 and put/call OI ratio of 0.04, so the elevated IV is not put-driven fear; it reflects two-sided event risk. IV jumped 16.5 points (47.2% to 63.7%) since late June while the price fell only 3.2%, consistent with rising uncertainty into a binary event. The rich IV argues against chasing entry immediately ahead of the print; a post-earnings vol crush would offer a cleaner setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Fluor is a balance-sheet cleanup that the market is undervaluing. The NuScale windfall removes the single largest source of earnings volatility and funds a buyback that retires ~16% of shares, driving double-digit EPS growth on flat operating earnings. At ~16x next-year consensus with $3.2B of cash and minimal debt, the stock is cheap for a de-risked, reimbursable-backlog franchise with genuine exposure to data-center, LNG, mining, and government infrastructure demand. Continued buybacks in 2027 plus the Aramco program win and front-end engineering conversion push EPS toward $3.50+, supporting a re-rating toward the high end of the $40-64 analyst range. Bull case ~$64 (+23%).

### Bear Case
The buyback is a one-time windfall on a structurally challenged business. New awards have more than halved, backlog is shrinking, segment margins are 0.2%, and the legal tail keeps producing $50-100M charges that cannot be modeled. The securities class action validates the market's doubt about whether risk mitigation actually works. If the Santos appeal fails and insurance recovery disappoints, or a rating downgrade triggers the collateral requirement, sentiment reverses. Once the NuScale cash is deployed, the stock is a no-growth EPC firm at ~16-19x forward with negative estimate momentum. Bear case ~$40 (-23%).

### Base Case
Buybacks deliver EPS accretion that roughly offsets flat-to-soft operating earnings, keeping the stock range-bound near consensus. The multiple holds at ~18-19x guided adjusted EPS as the balance-sheet-transformation benefit is offset by weak awards and litigation overhang. Base case ~$52-55, roughly flat to modestly higher over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the balance-sheet story and the operating/litigation drag are close to offsetting, and there is no clear catalyst to break the four-report HOLD streak.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- New awards recover to $4B+ in a quarter with backlog stabilizing or growing, confirming the front-end engineering pipeline is converting.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive over consecutive quarters.
- A favorable Santos appeal outcome or a material insurance recovery that reduces the legacy legal overhang, combined with a clean segment-margin quarter (>3%).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A rating downgrade to BB/Ba2 triggering the collateral-lien requirement.
- An adverse securities-class-action development or a further large, unexpected legacy legal charge (>$100M).
- Segment margins remaining sub-1% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the reimbursable-backlog shift is not translating into stable services profitability.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (the options market is pricing a ~14% move around it) and the July 2026 Santos appeal hearing are the near-term catalysts. Reassess after both resolve.

## Conclusion

Fluor has meaningfully improved its financial position: the NuScale monetization and aggressive buyback are real and shareholder-friendly, which is why Financial Health has risen from 4.5 to the high-5s over the past several reports. But the operating engine is not growing, competitive positioning has softened (2025 TSR lagged peers for the first time in years), and the legacy legal tail continues to generate unpredictable charges while a new securities class action challenges the turnaround narrative directly. The valuation is fair rather than compelling: ~16-19x forward is close to the most relevant peer (TTEK) with no dividend support and negative near-term estimate momentum.

The recent score history (5.3 → 5.4 → 5.2 → 5.2) captures a business in stasis: balance-sheet strength rising, growth and competitive position declining, netting to a stable mid-5 profile. New data confirms rather than reverses that pattern. We maintain **HOLD** with an overall score of **5.05**. The bar to move off this call is a genuine improvement in new awards and estimate revisions on the upside, or a rating/litigation shock on the downside, neither of which is evident today.
