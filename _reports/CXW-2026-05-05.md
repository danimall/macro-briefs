## Executive Summary

CoreCivic is experiencing a structural revenue step-change driven by the Trump administration's immigration enforcement expansion, with an estimated $540M+ in annualized revenue from five reactivated facilities and the Farmville acquisition ramping through 2026, though one facility (Midwest Regional Reception Center) remains blocked by court injunction and is excluded from company guidance. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, provides $75B in mandatory ICE funding through September 2029, creating multi-year demand visibility that is unusual for government contractors — though this funds ICE broadly, not CoreCivic specifically, and contracts remain terminable without cause. At 12.2x–13.0x forward P/E ($1.49–$1.59 FY2026E guided EPS) against a selected-sector peer median of approximately 21.7x, the stock is pricing in significant political risk that the funded legislative mandate partially mitigates.

The stock has declined approximately 17.5% from its May 2025 high of $23.54 to $19.42, creating an entry point where the risk/reward skews favorably on a 12-18 month basis. Operating cash flow, which declined in 2025 due to $130M in working capital consumption from rapid ramp, should recover materially in 2026 as facilities stabilize and accounts receivable collections normalize. Separately, activation CapEx of $75M pressured free cash flow in 2025 and drops to $35-40M in 2026. The remaining $300.5M buyback authorization at current prices would retire approximately 15% of float.

The critical risk is political: 35% of revenue comes from ICE, and a future administration could restrict private detention contracts. This risk is partially mitigated by the multi-year funded legislative mandate, 97% historical contract renewal rates, and growing state demand from overcrowding. Analyst consensus targets range from $28-32.

## Company Overview

CoreCivic owns and operates 44 correctional and detention facilities (~68,000 beds), 20 residential reentry centers (~4,100 beds), and 5 leased properties (~8,000 beds) across the United States. It owns approximately 57% of all privately owned prison beds in the U.S. and manages 41% of all privately managed beds. The company revoked its REIT election in 2021 to gain capital allocation flexibility.

**Revenue composition (FY2025):** Federal agencies 54% (ICE 35%, USMS 18%), state agencies 37%, other approximately 9%. The federal concentration intensified in 2025 as ICE revenue surged 36% from $564.8M to $770.7M.

**Operating model:** Per-diem pricing ($108.86/compensated man-day in 2025, +7.2% YoY) with fixed-cost leverage on occupancy. Facilities have high operating margins at full utilization given the fixed-cost nature of staffing and real estate.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term drivers (12-18 months):**

1. **Facility stabilization:** Five facilities activated in 2025 are ramping toward full occupancy. Combined stabilized revenue potential: ~$500M annually. California City expected to stabilize in Q1 2026; West Tennessee by end of Q1 2026; Diamondback in Q2 2026. The Midwest Regional Reception Center (~$60M annual) remains blocked by court injunction and is explicitly excluded from 2026 guidance — representing both a risk and a potential upside catalyst if resolved.

2. **Margin recovery:** Startup costs (signing bonuses, travel reimbursements, registry nursing, overtime) dissipate as facilities reach staffing equilibrium. Operating margin per compensated man-day was 23.6% in 2025 vs. 24.0% in 2024. Management expects improvement as activation costs roll off.

3. **ICE population growth:** The OBBBA provides $45B specifically for detention capacity through 2029. ICE daily detained population continues expanding under current enforcement posture.

4. **State demand:** Montana and Georgia growing populations creating state opportunities. States face overcrowding and staffing challenges that create outsourcing tailwinds.

**Medium-term (2-4 years):**

FY2027E consensus is $2.06 EPS (2 analysts), representing 28% growth from FY2026E. If the company achieves its ~$450M EBITDA stabilized run-rate and continues buybacks, earnings power could exceed $2.50 by FY2028.

**Estimate revision trends:** Current quarter estimates have declined 3.8% over 30 days, and next-year estimates dropped 11.8% over 30 days. This negative revision momentum is a caution signal, though the current-year estimate is essentially flat (-0.1% over 30 days, +2.1% over 90 days) and the next-year decline from $2.34 to $2.06 likely reflects a single analyst adjusting a placeholder estimate rather than fundamental deterioration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $19.42, CXW trades at:
- 12.2x–13.0x FY2026E forward P/E ($1.49–$1.59 guided EPS)
- 18.0x trailing P/E ($1.08 FY2025 EPS)
- ~9.4x FY2027E P/E ($2.06)
- ~6.9x–7.0x EV/EBITDA on $437–445M guided 2026 EBITDA (assuming $1.23B debt + $1.92B market cap - $98M cash = $3.05B EV)

Against a selected-sector peer median P/E of approximately 21.7x, CXW's ~12.5x forward multiple represents a significant discount. CoreCivic's peer universe is limited — GEO Group is the only close public comparable — so the peer median reflects a broader sector comparison rather than a direct-peer analysis. This discount is partly justified by political binary risk and government contract dependence, but the magnitude appears excessive given: (1) multi-year legislated ICE funding through 2029, (2) 97% contract renewal rate over the last 5 years, (3) 28% earnings growth expected in FY2027, and (4) $300.5M remaining buyback capacity at a price below intrinsic value.

**Fair value calculation:** At a conservative 12x FY2027E of $2.06 = $24.72 (27% upside). At 14x (still materially below peers, reflecting political risk discount) = $28.84 (49% upside). The analyst consensus of $29.80 implies approximately 14.5x FY2027E, which appears reasonable for a company with multi-year revenue visibility, improving FCF, and aggressive buybacks.

The market is pricing CXW as if the political risk will materialize within 2-3 years. The OBBBA funding provides demand visibility through 2029, though ICE retains discretion over how and where it houses detainees, and CoreCivic's contracts remain terminable without cause.

## Competitive Landscape

CoreCivic's competitive position is strengthening:

- **Market share:** 57% of privately owned prison beds, 41% of managed beds. No erosion.
- **Asset base advantage:** Owning 40+ facilities creates switching costs and geographic coverage that no competitor can replicate within the timeframe of this analysis.
- **Barriers to entry:** Zoning/permitting challenges, $100M+ capital per facility, multi-year government relationship requirements. New entrants cannot compete within a 5-year horizon.
- **Pricing power:** Revenue per compensated man-day grew 7.2% YoY to $108.86. Government partners have limited alternatives given capacity constraints.
- **Contract renewal:** 97% rate over 5 years demonstrates stickiness.

**Threats:** ICE exploring non-traditional detention alternatives (military bases, warehouse conversions, soft-sided facilities, BOP/state/local facilities, and direct real estate acquisition). This could absorb some marginal demand growth without displacing existing CXW contracts, but the 10-K explicitly identifies this as a competitive risk. The larger threat is if ICE begins directly acquiring real estate rather than leasing from private operators — this is noted in the 10-K risk factors and represents a meaningful consideration for long-term capacity utilization.

Anti-privatization legislation exists in multiple states (CA, CO, NJ, OR, WA, IL, MD), but this has been a persistent background risk for decades without materially impacting the federal business. California AB 1633 (50% gross receipts tax) is the most specific near-term legislative threat, potentially affecting $130M+ in California revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Political Binary (Severity: HIGH):** The single most important risk. A post-2028 administration could decline to use appropriated funds for private detention or issue executive orders restricting private detention contracts. ICE also has expanding non-traditional alternatives including military bases, BOP facilities, soft-sided facilities, and direct real estate ownership. This risk is partially priced (significant peer discount) but remains the dominant factor in any sizing decision.

**Legal Challenges to Activations (Severity: MODERATE-HIGH):** Leavenworth injunction blocks $60M/year facility and is explicitly excluded from 2026 guidance. California City faces NGO lawsuit. Torrance County challenge pending. Pattern of sophisticated legal opposition is expanding. Each facility blocked represents $40-130M in unrealized annual revenue. Company states it "cannot predict whether or when it will be able to accept populations" at Midwest.

**California AB 1633 (Severity: MODERATE):** 50% gross receipts tax proposed for Jan 1, 2027. Would devastate California operations (~$130M revenue). Not yet passed, but signals legislative hostility in the state.

**Customer Concentration (Severity: MODERATE):** ICE alone at 35% creates single-customer risk. 31% of total revenue from contracts expiring by December 31, 2026. However, the 97% historical renewal rate and funded OBBBA mandate mitigate renewal risk.

**Rate Environment (Severity: LOW-MODERATE):** 7.4% average cost of debt with no maturities until October 2027. Refinancing into a 5%+ 30Y environment would be challenging but is not imminent. The company's low beta (0.677) and essential-service nature provide some insulation from broader macro stress.

**Negative Estimate Revisions (Severity: LOW-MODERATE):** FY2027 consensus declined 11.8% over 30 days ($2.34 to $2.06). With only 2 analysts covering that year, this may reflect one analyst adjustment rather than fundamental deterioration. Current-year estimates remain stable.

## Conclusion

CoreCivic represents an asymmetric opportunity at ~12.5x forward earnings with multi-year revenue visibility supported by legislated ICE funding. The binary political risk is real but temporally distant (2029 OBBBA expiration), while the FCF recovery, margin normalization, and aggressive buyback program provide near-term catalysts within a 12-18 month investment horizon. The analyst consensus Buy with a $28-32 target range reflects conviction that current pricing excessively discounts a business with 28% earnings growth, 97% contract renewal rates, and $300M+ in remaining buyback capacity. Position sizing should reflect the political binary (2-3% max, not a core position) and the stock's illiquidity (~$20M daily value traded).

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** EV/EBITDA corrected from ~4.4x to ~6.9x–7.0x, forward P/E corrected from 9.4x to 12.2x–13.0x, and Midwest facility excluded from 2026 ramp assumptions — collectively these reduce the magnitude of apparent undervaluation and the certainty of near-term facility stabilization, though the directional thesis remains intact at a ~7x EV/EBITDA with 28% earnings growth.
