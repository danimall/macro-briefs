**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pilgrim's Pride is a vertically integrated chicken producer with FY2025 revenue of $18.5B, 82.2% owned by JBS S.A. The investment question is entirely about where the company sits in the poultry commodity cycle. FY2024 and FY2025 were peak-margin years driven by cheap feed (corn/soybean meal) and tight supply; net income roughly tripled from the 2023 trough ($322M, $1.36 EPS) to the 2024 peak ($1.09B, $4.57 EPS). Q1 FY2026 marked the cyclical turn: diluted EPS fell to $0.43 from $1.24, U.S. operating income dropped 72.7% to $87M, and the consolidated operating margin compressed to 3.6%. The driver is industry oversupply: USDA data showed Q1'26 ready-to-cook production up 3.4% on record egg sets and placements, pushing breast prices below the five-year average even as demand held firm.

This is a normal mean-reverting commodity cycle, not a structural impairment. Feed costs and supply historically rationalize within two to four quarters, and the balance sheet (Altman Z-Score 3.29, ROE 29.4%, ~$1.1B liquidity, no refinancing cliff) gives the company ample staying power through the down-leg. The key question for the equity is whether the market has already discounted the rollover. The evidence suggests it largely has: the stock is down 34% year-over-year and 41% from its August 2025 high of $50.56, trades near the low of the analyst target range ($30.00 low vs. $30.06 current), and sits at its own mid-cycle fair value of roughly $2.95 normalized EPS at a 10x cyclical multiple.

We move to HOLD from a prior AVOID. The downgrade history (HOLD → AVOID → AVOID, scores declining 5.6 → 5.0) correctly captured the deterioration in fundamentals, but an AVOID near the analyst floor, on a known cyclical at the early down-leg with a sound balance sheet and a durable moat, overstates the residual downside. The asymmetry (roughly -15% to -20% to a deeper-trough bear case against +30% on normalization) is modestly positive, but negative estimate momentum (current-year EPS revised down 19.9% over 90 days), uncertain trough timing, and an entrenched JBS-control governance overhang keep us from a constructive call. This is a two-sided setup at fair value.

## Company Overview

Pilgrim's Pride is one of the world's largest protein companies, operating across three reportable segments:

- **United States (~59% of sales, $11.0B):** Primarily fresh chicken (80.9% of U.S. sales), serving foodservice (Chick-fil-A) and retail (Kroger, Costco, Publix, H-E-B). Highest-margin segment at 10.7% FY2025 operating margin. Two large customers together represent ~16.8% of consolidated sales; no single customer exceeds 10%.
- **Europe (~29%, $5.4B):** U.K./Ireland/continental operations under Moy Park, Richmond, and Fridge Raiders, weighted to prepared/value-added foods (58.6% of Europe sales). Lower margin (5.1%) but improving on a multi-year restructuring.
- **Mexico (~11%, $2.1B):** Fresh chicken serving affordable-protein demand; 7.9% FY2025 margin.

Vertical integration is the structural moat: ~4,500 contract growers, 36 feed mills, 50 hatcheries, and 39 processing plants. In the U.K. fresh market, vertical integration is effectively a prerequisite. Brands include Pilgrim's, Just Bare, and Gold Kist. JBS S.A., via subsidiaries, beneficially owns 82.2% of the company and controls up to eight of ten board seats, which dominates the governance and capital-allocation story.

## Financial Analysis

The financial health screen is strong on an absolute basis but reflects peak-cycle earnings:

| Metric | 2023 (trough) | 2024 | 2025 (peak) | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $17.4B | $17.9B | $18.5B | $4.53B (+1.6% YoY) |
| Gross profit | $1.12B | $2.31B | $2.36B | $345M (–37.7% YoY) |
| Operating margin | 3.0% | 8.4% | 8.7% | 3.6% |
| Net income | $322M | $1.09B | $1.08B | $101M |
| Diluted EPS | $1.36 | $4.57 | $4.54 | $0.43 (vs $1.24) |

The Q1'26 turn is unambiguous. U.S. gross profit fell 49.3% and U.S. operating income fell 72.7% as the industry over-produced. Mexico operating income fell 70.2% on commodity chicken price declines. Europe was the offset, up roughly 32% on restructuring benefits and FX.

The balance sheet is sound. Altman Z-Score of 3.29 sits firmly in the safe zone; ROE was 29.4% and ROA 10.5% at peak (both will compress as the cycle turns). Debt/equity of 1.81 looks elevated but is manageable against ~$2.3B Adjusted EBITDA and ~$1.1B available liquidity. Total debt is ~$3.1B, entirely unsecured senior notes (3.50% 2032, 4.25% 2031, 6.25% 2033, 6.875% 2034) with no near-term maturity wall, and management is actively deleveraging via open-market note repurchases plus a $250M tender of the 6.25% 2033 notes in April 2026 at $1,056.90 (a ~$14M premium).

The central capital-allocation development is the ~$2.0B in special dividends paid in 2025 ($6.30/share in April plus $2.10/share in September), the first dividends in years, funded from the cash hoard. This drew cash down from $2.04B at YE2024 to $640M at YE2025 to $542M at Q1'26. Roughly 82% of those distributions flowed to JBS. Capex is simultaneously ramping into the downturn: $711M in 2025, guided to $900–950M for 2026 (Ellijay, GA expansion and commodity-to-retail plant conversions). Free cash flow compresses on two fronts, falling margins and rising capex, just as the cash cushion has been deliberately reduced. The company is not financially stressed, but its peak-cycle balance-sheet flexibility was harvested by its controlling owner ahead of a decline.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative and the estimate trend confirms it. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut to $3.48 from $4.34 ninety days ago (-19.9%), with the current-quarter estimate down 30.9% to $0.94 and next-quarter down 6.7% to $1.11. The earnings surprise record turned: after beats of +7.7% and +6.7% in mid-2025, PPC missed by 11.4% in Q4'25 and 27.6% in Q1'26. This is the signature of a cyclical that has rolled past peak and is still searching for a trough.

The decline is supply-driven, not demand-driven, which matters for the recovery path. Chicken benefits from being the cheapest protein amid high beef prices and stretched consumers, so volumes held (Q1 revenue +1.6% YoY). The pressure came from record egg sets, placements, higher liveweights, and a 3.4% rise in ready-to-cook production. Poultry supply historically rationalizes within two to four quarters once margins fall, as producers cut placements and the cycle bottoms. The timing of that response is the open variable. We have one quarter of the down-leg; the cycle could deepen before reversing.

Europe is the one structural improvement: operating income rose 60.5% in FY2025 and 32% in Q1'26 on restructuring, a genuine self-help offset to U.S. and Mexico commodity weakness that the market underappreciates relative to the U.S. headline. It is not large enough to reverse the consolidated trajectory but provides a stabilizer.

## Valuation Assessment

At $30.06, PPC trades at 8.6x the current-year EPS estimate ($3.48) and 8.0x next year ($3.78). On a cycle-adjusted basis, averaging the 2023 trough ($1.36) and 2025 peak ($4.54) implies roughly $2.95 mid-cycle EPS; at 10x that supports ~$30, and at 12x ~$35. The stock therefore sits at, not below, its own mid-cycle fair value.

Peer context cuts both ways. The packaged-foods median P/E is 15.7x (POST 15.7x, Marzetti 17.1x, Tootsie Roll 29.2x), but those are less cyclical, more branded businesses that deserve higher multiples. The closest cyclical comparable, Cal-Maine (eggs), trades at 5.5x. Protein cyclicals characteristically carry low multiples near the top of the cycle (earnings about to fall) and high multiples near the bottom (earnings about to recover); PPC at 8x on declining estimates is mid-cycle, neither distressed-cheap nor expensive.

The asymmetry is the key point the prior AVOID underweighted. The stock is down 41% from its high to near the analyst low target ($30.00 low, $37.50 median, $39.25 mean, $56.00 high). A deeper-trough bear case (FY2026 EPS settling toward $2.50–3.00 against the current $3.48 estimate, holding an 8–9x multiple) points to roughly $24–26, or -15% to -20%. Normalization back toward mid-cycle at 12x or a modest recovery toward consensus implies $35–39, roughly +20% to +30%. That skew is modestly positive, which argues against AVOID. It does not argue for BUY, because the multiple is already mid-cycle (not trough-cheap), estimates are still being cut, and the controlling-shareholder governance discount is structural. The market is fairly pricing a two-sided cycle.

## Competitive Landscape

PPC competes with Tyson, Wayne-Sanderson, and Perdue in U.S. chicken, plus regional players in Europe and Mexico. The industry is commodity-driven with limited durable pricing power; external analysis notes average ROIC barely exceeds cost of capital across the full cycle. PPC's competitive edge is full vertical integration and scale, which lowers unit cost and is a near-requirement in the U.K. fresh market. The value-added and branded mix push (Just Bare, Pilgrim's, Europe prepared foods) is a deliberate effort to dampen commodity volatility, and the European restructuring is producing measurable margin gains. Competitive position is stable to slightly improving on the value-added mix, but the underlying business remains a price-taker in a cyclical commodity market; the moat protects cost position and share, not through-cycle returns.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity cyclicality** is the dominant risk and is largely priced. Earnings swing 3x+ across the cycle on feed costs (corn 45.9%, soybean meal 33.7% of feed cost) and chicken prices. The down-leg is underway; the risk to the equity is that it deepens or extends beyond the typical two-to-four-quarter window.

**JBS controlled-company governance** is the most idiosyncratic and least-priced risk, and its direction is ambiguous. JBS controls the board and capital decisions, and interests can diverge from minority holders. The 2025 special dividends extracted peak-cycle cash, 82% to JBS, ahead of a downturn. The *City of Miami Beach* derivative suit (filed July 2025, tentatively settled April 10, 2026) alleges directors and JBS breached fiduciary duties via a 2024 charter amendment benefiting JBS. The related-party cost-sharing agreement explicitly contemplates a scenario "where JBS USA becomes 100% owner of PPC," and JBS's own recent U.S. listing keeps a take-private possibility live, which could be a premium event or a value-extraction overhang depending on terms.

**Litigation and tax** are recurring, structural costs rather than one-offs. Cumulative Broiler Antitrust settlements total $706M ($22.9M in Q1'26), running ~$160M/year, with Phase 2 discovery proceeding. The Mexican SAT assessment on the 2015 Tyson de México acquisition (~$269.5M under appeal, $88.2M accrued) was reaffirmed by the Tax Court in March 2026 and is the largest single open item. A December 2025 executive order directing DOJ/FTC food-sector antitrust investigations adds fresh tail risk. The U.K. HMRC Onix review heads to a hearing window in early 2027.

**Other risks** include highly pathogenic avian influenza (flagged as "increasingly common," with H5 detected across U.S. states, capable of triggering culls and export bans), the EU Deforestation Regulation effective December 30, 2026 (new diligence costs), and FX translation (a 10% peso move is roughly a $64M swing).

### Options Market Signal

The options structure is calm and does not corroborate an acute-downside thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 34.7% versus 28.3% one-year historical volatility, rich by 6.4pp but in only the 2nd percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in contango (34.7% near, 37.4% at three months), a normal configuration rather than event stress. The put/call skew of roughly 3.0% reflects mild cyclical caution, and the implied one-week move is ±3.7%. IV ticked up only 0.9pp as the stock rose 6.9% off its lows. The market is pricing this as a fairly valued cyclical, not a name pricing an imminent break lower, consistent with the view that the bear case is largely discounted.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$35–39, +20% to +30%):** The poultry supply response arrives on schedule. Producers cut placements over the next two to four quarters, breast prices recover toward the five-year average, and feed costs stay benign. FY2027 EPS reverts toward mid-cycle ($3.50–4.00) and the multiple re-rates to 11–12x. Europe restructuring continues to add margin. JBS either sustains the deleveraging path or moves to take PPC private at a premium to the current price. The stock, sitting near the analyst low target with a sound balance sheet, has positive skew if the cycle bottoms within the typical window.

**Bear Case (~$24–26, -15% to -20%):** Industry oversupply persists longer than the typical cycle. Egg sets and placements remain elevated, breast prices stay below average through 2026, and FY2026 EPS troughs toward $2.50–3.00, below the current $3.48 estimate, implying further negative revisions. Capex ramps into falling margins, compressing free cash flow, while JBS continues special distributions, pressuring the balance sheet. Litigation and the Tyson de México tax assessment crystallize as cash drains. At 8–9x deeper-trough earnings, the stock retests $24–26.

The base case is range-bound around $28–32 as the market waits for evidence of a supply-side response, with estimates still drifting lower in the interim.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026, expected late July) is the key checkpoint for whether U.S. margins are stabilizing or deteriorating further, and for any read on USDA egg-set/placement trends signaling a supply response.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- USDA egg sets/placements turn flat-to-negative for two consecutive months and breast prices recover toward the five-year average, signaling the supply rationalization that historically bottoms the cycle.
- Current-year EPS estimates stabilize and revisions turn positive for two consecutive months.
- A credible JBS take-private offer at a premium to the current price, or a clear shift toward returning capital to minority holders rather than peak-cycle extraction.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY2026 EPS estimates breach $2.50 with continued negative revisions, indicating the down-leg is extending well beyond the typical window.
- JBS announces further large special dividends into the downturn that materially erode the liquidity cushion or push leverage above ~3x net debt/EBITDA.
- An adverse resolution of the Tyson de México tax case (beyond the $88.2M accrued) or a material escalation of the December 2025 antitrust executive order.

We expect the cyclical headwinds to persist for at least two to four quarters before a supply response becomes visible.

## Conclusion

Pilgrim's Pride sits in the early down-leg of a well-understood poultry cycle, and the cyclical deterioration is real. Those facts justified moving off the optimistic stance of earlier in the year. But the stock has already fallen 41% from its high toward the analyst low target and to its own mid-cycle fair value of ~$30, the balance sheet is sound, the vertical-integration moat is intact, and the supply-driven downturn historically reverses within two to four quarters. This is mean reversion in progress, not structural impairment, and the equity is not a short candidate: short interest is only 2.2% of float, there is no debt or solvency catalyst, and the balance sheet provides a floor.

The asymmetry from here is modestly positive (-15% to -20% downside against +20% to +30% on normalization), but negative estimate momentum, uncertain trough timing, capex ramping into the downturn, and the structural JBS-control governance discount keep us from a constructive call. The risk/reward is two-sided at fair value. **HOLD.** Existing holders are compensated by a sound balance sheet and positive cycle skew; new capital lacks a clear edge until the supply response is visible in the data.
