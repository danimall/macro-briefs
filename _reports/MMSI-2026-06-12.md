**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Merit Medical is a leading manufacturer of predominantly single-use ("disposable") interventional and diagnostic medical devices spanning cardiology, radiology, oncology, critical care, and endoscopy. The investment case rests on a razor-blade consumables model layered with higher-growth therapeutic platforms, and on a durable margin expansion story: non-GAAP operating margin climbed from 14.4% (2021) to 20.3% (2025), GAAP gross margin rose to 48.7% (2025), and operating cash flow reached $297M with free cash flow of $215.7M (14.2% FCF margin). The company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters and beat again in Q1 2026, with management raising FY2026 guidance.

The stock has fallen ~29% from its 52-week high of $96.74 to $66.89, and a cluster of open-market insider purchases by five C-suite executives in early May 2026 at $60-63 — including a brand-new CEO — is among the stronger conviction signals in the file. Those facts support a constructive medium-term view. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: the current-quarter consensus EPS estimate was cut 8.9% over 30 days and 10.1% over 90 days (to $0.96 from $1.07), even after a clean Q1 beat. That downward revision on the most proximate quarter, set against a founder-to-professional CEO transition completed only in January 2026 and a stock that still trades at ~16x forward FY26 EPS, removes the asymmetry required for a BUY. Options markets reinforce caution, with near-term implied volatility in backwardation (38.1% vs 33.9% six-month) signaling the market expects near-term stress around the next print.

The bar for a Health Care BUY is high in the current environment: a score of 8.0+ with a confirmed catalyst inside 60 days and contained downside. Merit clears the quality screen but not the catalyst-and-asymmetry screen. We rate it HOLD (growth_hold) with upgrade triggers tied to the current-quarter revision trend reversing and the leadership transition demonstrating clean execution over one or two more prints.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1987 in South Jordan, Utah, Merit Medical employs roughly 7,500 people across 43 countries and holds approximately 2,200 patents and applications. The business is a broad-line consumables franchise (kits, trays, sheaths, guidewires, catheters, embolics) augmented by faster-growing therapeutic platforms. Key franchises include SCOUT radar tumor localization, the Wrapsody cell-impermeable endoprosthesis for dialysis outflow (which management explicitly flags as disproportionately important to results), Embosphere/HepaSphere embolotherapy, Prelude access sheaths, EsophyX (GERD), the newly acquired C2 CryoBalloon (Barrett's esophagus), and the HeRO graft.

Effective Q1 2026, under new CEO Martha Aronson as chief operating decision-maker, Merit collapsed its prior two-segment structure (Cardiovascular plus Endoscopy) into a single operating segment with two product categories: "Foundational" (access, procedural solutions, OEM, vascular intervention — Q1 revenue $255.5M) and "Therapeutic" (cardiac therapies, oncology, renal, endoscopy — Q1 revenue $126.4M, growing faster at +9.9%). This reframing signals a strategic tilt toward higher-margin, higher-growth therapeutic devices. Geographically, the mix is roughly 60% US and 40% international, with EMEA the growth driver and China the largest single international market but under sustained pricing pressure. About 34% of sales are denominated in foreign currencies, with CNY and EUR the largest exposures.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily: $1,151M (2022) → $1,257M (2023) → $1,357M (2024) → $1,516M (2025, +11.8%). Q1 2026 revenue was $381.9M (+7.5%). Growth blends mid-single-digit organic expansion with acquisition contribution (Cook, Biolife, EsophyX, C2 CryoBalloon).

The margin expansion is corroborated across multiple measures rather than resting on a single non-GAAP adjustment. GAAP gross margin moved 46.4% → 47.4% → 48.7% (2023-2025) on price and mix. GAAP operating margin rose 9.9% → 11.5% → 12.2%. Net income progressed $94.4M → $120.4M → $128.5M, with FY2025 EPS of $2.13. Q1 2026 EPS of $0.68 (vs $0.49) was aided by a ~$12.5M pre-tax gain on the DualCap divestiture, so the underlying improvement is somewhat smaller than the headline.

Cash flow quality is high. Operating cash flow rose $145M → $221M → $297M (2023-2025), consistently exceeding net income, a sign of clean earnings. FCF represents a 14.2% FCF margin and roughly a 5.4% FCF yield at the current ~$4.0B market cap. Capex is rising ($35M → $82M) on a new Utah distribution facility and automation, with a 2026 guide of $80-100M; this is investment for capacity and efficiency, not maintenance drag.

The balance sheet is sound. Cash stood at $446M (FY25) and $488M (Q1 26). Debt is essentially a single instrument: $747.5M of 3.00% Convertible Senior Notes due February 2029, with a conversion price of $86.83 and a capped call lifting the effective dilution threshold to ~$114.68. The $700M revolver is undrawn (~$697M available). Equity is $1.6B, the current ratio is 4.34, and D/E is 0.7. The Altman Z of 2.84 sits in the "grey zone" but is driven by the convert leverage and asset base rather than any distress signal; liquidity and coverage are robust.

Two watch items deserve emphasis. First, the effective tax rate is climbing — 15.8% (2023) → 19.8% (2024) → 24.8% (2025) → 23.4% (Q1 26) — on fewer discrete benefits, the OBBBA, and Pillar Two. This is a structural multi-point EPS headwind that partially offsets operating leverage. Second, goodwill ($507M) plus intangibles ($609M) reflect the acquisitive strategy and carry impairment risk if deals underperform; combined, these exceed roughly two-thirds of equity.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is mid-single-digit to high-single-digit organic, supplemented by tuck-in M&A and demographic tailwinds as interventional procedure volumes rise with aging populations. The Therapeutic category is the faster-growing leg, and the reorganization toward it should support both growth rate and mix-driven margin over time. The four-quarter beat record (surprises of 16.4%, 8.4%, 6.9%, and 10.7%) gives reasonable confidence that consensus is set conservatively at the quarterly level.

The signal that tempers the growth view is the estimate revision pattern. Full-year estimates are stable to slightly positive (current year $4.07, +1.6% over 30 days; next year $4.40, +0.2%), but the current-quarter estimate was cut to $0.96 from $1.06 thirty days ago and $1.07 ninety days ago. A company that just beat and raised seeing its most proximate quarter marked down meaningfully points to near-term softening (China VBP pricing pressure, tax-rate drag, FX, or conservatism on the View Point integration) that the stable full-year number masks by relying on a back-half recovery. This argues against treating the next print as a clean upside catalyst. The estimate revision threshold that would constitute a strong BUY signal (+30% over 90 days) is far from present; the actual trend is mildly negative on the near term.

Acquisitions continue to feed the pipeline: View Point Medical (~$140M, closed April 2026, adding OneMark detection imaging and tissue markers adjacent to SCOUT), C2 CryoBalloon (Nov 2025), and the DualCap divestiture (Feb 2026, evidence of portfolio pruning under new leadership). The strategy is competent and disciplined but is incremental rather than transformational, which caps the growth rate the market will underwrite.

## Valuation Assessment

At $66.89, Merit trades at ~28.8x trailing FY2025 EPS of $2.13 and ~16.4x forward FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.07. The forward multiple is reasonable in absolute terms for a high-quality consumables medtech with expanding margins and strong FCF, but it is not cheap relative to mid-single-digit organic growth. The PEG, using ~7-10% blended EPS growth, sits near 1.6-2.3x — adequate, not compelling.

The peer table requires care. The Health Care Supplies median P/E of 56.6 is distorted by loss-makers (XRAY at -3.2, NEOG at -3.2) and by very high multiples on others (ICUI 73.8, NVST 56.6, HAE 34.8). Anchoring to that median would manufacture a false discount. On a quality-adjusted basis, Merit's forward ~16x is below most profitable peers, which reflects its lower growth rate and lower-margin Foundational mix rather than a clear mispricing. I do not treat the peer median as a meaningful undervaluation signal here.

The drawdown does not by itself make the stock cheap, and given its magnitude the burden is on identifying what sellers are missing. The constructive elements are the insider buying cluster and the unbooked tariff-refund optionality from the February 2026 SCOTUS IEEPA ruling (Merit was importer of record on affected goods). Neither is quantified or time-bound enough to underwrite a re-rating. Analyst targets (low $74, mean ~$89.5, high $110) imply meaningful upside, but with the current quarter being revised down, those targets are not yet acting as floors. The valuation is fair: enough downside support from FCF and the insider signal to avoid an AVOID, not enough identified mispricing to justify a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Merit competes against substantially larger players — BD, Teleflex, Medtronic, Boston Scientific, Cook Medical, Abbott, Terumo, and Edwards in cardiac/peripheral; Hologic and Argon in oncology; Olympus and Getinge in endoscopy. Its competitive edge is breadth of ancillary consumable lines, manufacturing integration, customer focus, and a steady cadence of product launches and tuck-in acquisitions, rather than blockbuster innovation. The position is stable-to-modestly-improving: the Therapeutic category is gaining mix, gross margin is trending up (a sign of pricing/mix power), and SCOUT/Wrapsody hold differentiated niches.

Offsetting this, China VBP is explicitly expected to keep pressuring pricing into 2026 in the company's largest international market, EU MDR will force discontinuation of some products rather than re-certification, and competitors' AI adoption is flagged as a forward risk. The Wrapsody concentration management calls out is a single-platform dependency. The net trajectory is constructive but not a clear share-gaining story against the scale players, supporting a middle competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is execution through the leadership transition. Founder/CEO/Chairman Fred Lampropoulos resigned as CEO in October 2025 and as director/Chair in January 2026, ending ~38 years of founder leadership; Martha Aronson now leads. The board granted retention RSUs to the CFO/CLO/CHRO in February 2026 to stabilize the team. This carries upside (professionalization, the single-segment refocus, divesting underperformers) and downside (loss of founder vision, no key-man insurance on Aronson). A new CEO's first full year is inherently higher-variance, and the current-quarter estimate cut overlapping with the transition raises the stakes on the next two prints.

Other company-specific risks: China VBP pricing pressure (>500 distributors); tariff/trade-cost exposure on imports from Mexico, Ireland, and China; integration and impairment risk on $1.1B of goodwill plus intangibles amid an active M&A pipeline; the EU MDR transition (deadlines end-2027/2028); EtO sterilization and PFAS regulatory tightening; a new Q1 2026 risk factor on the US/Israel/Iran conflict (energy and resin/petroleum input-cost exposure); and the rising effective tax rate as a structural EPS headwind. The convert matures February 2029 with conversion at $86.83 — well above the current price — so refinancing rather than dilution is the more likely 2028-29 event to monitor.

One overhang has cleared: the DOJ-related Corporate Integrity Agreement from the 2020 settlement expired in October 2025 and was formally closed January 23, 2026, a genuine de-risking event.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 38.1% versus 31.0% one-year historical volatility — rich by 7.1 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (38.1% near-term vs 33.9% six-month). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the next earnings print and with the current-quarter estimate cut. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.9% near-term, -6.2% at six months), and put/call OI is low at 0.11, so positioning is not aggressively bearish, but the rich, backwardated near-term vol confirms event risk into the next report. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.4%. This reinforces the decision not to pre-position ahead of the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90-110):** Margin expansion continues as the Therapeutic mix grows, the new leadership team executes the single-segment refocus cleanly, full-year estimates prove conservative as the beat streak extends, and unbooked tariff refunds materialize. The insider buying cluster proves prescient. Re-rating toward 20-22x forward EPS plus estimate upside drives the stock back toward prior highs and the $110 analyst high target.

**Bear Case ($52-58):** China VBP and the rising tax rate compress the margin story's translation into EPS, the current-quarter cut proves the start of a broader near-term miss, the founder transition introduces execution stumbles or an underperforming acquisition triggers goodwill impairment, and the multiple de-rates to ~13-14x forward on lowered growth confidence. This implies roughly 13-22% downside from current levels.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced rather than favorable, which is the core reason for the HOLD. The quality and FCF support limit downside, but the near-term estimate cut and absence of a dated catalyst cap the probability-weighted upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July / early August 2026), which will reveal whether the current-quarter estimate cut was a one-off or the start of a near-term deceleration, and provide a first clean read on execution under the new CEO.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates stabilize and turn positive (reverse the -8.9% 30-day current-quarter revision) alongside a Q2 beat-and-raise.
2. Two consecutive prints demonstrating clean execution through the leadership transition with Therapeutic category growth sustained at high-single-digits or better and gross margin holding ≥48.5%.
3. A pullback toward $58-60 on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric upside to the analyst mean of ~$89.5, OR concrete recognition of the SCOTUS tariff-refund optionality.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A Q2 2026 miss on revenue or EPS combined with a guidance cut, confirming the current-quarter revision was a leading signal.
2. Goodwill or intangible impairment on the View Point, C2, or Biolife acquisitions, or gross margin falling below ~46%.
3. Departure of a second senior executive (CFO/CLO/CHRO) beyond the founder transition, signaling instability the retention awards failed to contain.

## Conclusion

Merit Medical is a high-quality consumables medtech with a credible, multi-year margin expansion record, strong and high-conversion free cash flow, a clean four-quarter beat history, a sound balance sheet, and an unusually constructive insider buying cluster. Those attributes, plus the drawdown, keep the stock well clear of an AVOID. The reason it is not a BUY is concrete and monitorable: the current-quarter consensus EPS estimate was cut even after a Q1 beat, the leadership transition from a 38-year founder is only months old and raises near-term execution variance, near-term options vol is rich and in backwardation pricing event stress into the next print, and there is no dated catalyst inside 60 days to drive a re-rating. At ~16x forward FY26 EPS for mid-single-digit organic growth, the valuation is fair rather than mispriced, and the drawdown places the burden on the bull case, which the current revision trend undercuts. We rate MMSI HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, and would move to BUY on a stabilizing/rising current-quarter estimate trend confirmed by a Q2 beat-and-raise, or on a pullback toward $58-60 that restores asymmetry.
