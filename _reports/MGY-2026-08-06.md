## Executive Summary

Magnolia has spent eight years building a reputation on a narrow set of promises: spend roughly half of cash flow, carry almost no debt, grow production moderately, and return the residual. The FY2025 numbers validate that model — 67.4% EBITDA margin, $11.72/boe of cash operating costs, $409M of free cash flow on $1.31bn of revenue, net debt of $133M against $884.7M of EBITDAX (0.15x), and 48.4 million shares retired for $945.4M since 2019. On July 20, 2026 the company agreed to acquire WildFire Energy for approximately $4.06bn including assumed debt, a transaction larger than its own pre-deal equity value. Funding is now executed: 46.32 million shares priced at $23.75 (~$1.10bn gross), $500M of new senior notes closing August 5, $600M of assumed notes due 2029, plus an inferred revolver draw of roughly $700-950M against a newly committed $1.75bn elected facility.

The transformation is real in both directions. Production goes from 106.1 Mboe/d (Q2 2026) to roughly 159 Mboe/d; Giddings acreage exceeds 1.25 million net acres; Magnolia acquires a sand mine covering ~80% of its own proppant needs and 500+ miles of gas gathering. It also takes net debt/EBITDAX from 0.15x to an estimated 1.3-1.5x at $70 WTI, dilutes existing holders by roughly 30% of ownership, and does so with no commodity hedges on the balance sheet at either 12/31/25 or 3/31/26. The deal was won in a competitive auction, the implied ~6.25x unsynergized EV/EBITDAX on WildFire sits above Magnolia's own 5.28x standalone multiple, and the $100M synergy target is what closes that gap. There is no multiple arbitrage here.

Timing is the uncomfortable part. Q2 2026 revenue of $478.81M (+50.1% YoY) and EPS of $0.99 versus $0.90 consensus were driven almost entirely by price — revenue per boe rose 27.7% sequentially to roughly $49.59 while production rose only 3.5% — and that price came from Strait of Hormuz disruption that Magnolia itself flagged in its Q1 10-Q. Crude has since fallen below $80, and Q3 consensus has been cut 26.9% in thirty days to $0.56. Magnolia announced a leveraged, equity-funded acquisition into a price spike that was already fading, and it carries that leverage unhedged. At $23.72 the pro forma equity is priced at roughly $67 mid-cycle WTI, which is defensible rather than euphoric — but the distribution around it is wide and macro-driven. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Magnolia is a single-segment independent E&P operating exclusively in South Texas across two areas: Karnes (60,187 gross / 39,935 net acres), the mature, oil-rich legacy core inherited from the 2018 EnerVest/TPG Pace formation; and Giddings (741,586 gross / 561,950 net acres), a large Austin Chalk/Eagle Ford position developed on what management repeatedly calls a "gradual and measured" basis. At 3/31/26 the company held interests in ~2,890 gross (1,960 net) wells, produced 102,564 boe/d, ran two rigs, and employed 262 people. That headcount against ~100 Mboe/d is among the leanest in US onshore.

Two structural items completed in Q1 2026 matter for governance. The dual-class structure is gone: EnerVest affiliates redeemed 4.8 million LLC Units into Class A and sold them to the public, and Magnolia LLC repurchased and cancelled the final 0.7 million units for $19.8M (an implied $26.39/share). Class B shares outstanding are zero, noncontrolling interest is eliminated, and Magnolia owns 100.0% of Magnolia LLC. For an eight-year-old SPAC-formed company, complete sponsor exit and collapse to a single share class removes a real overhang.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025: flat revenue, volume growth offsetting price deterioration.** Revenue was $1,311.8M versus $1,315.9M; production grew 11.2% to 36.424 MMboe (99.8 Mboe/d) while realized oil fell 15% to $63.18/bbl. Gas realizations rose 79% to $2.76/Mcf, lifting gas to ~15% of revenue. Operating income fell to $439.2M from $512.0M; net income to $337.3M from $397.3M; diluted EPS $1.73.

Management's own disclosed operating margin series is the cleanest read on the trend: 56% (2021) → 63% (2022) → 45% (2023) → 39% (2024) → 33% (2025). GAAP reconciles ($439.181M ÷ $1,311.845M = 33.5%). Three consecutive years of compression, price-driven rather than cost-driven.

**Unit economics remain top-quartile.** Revenue per boe of $36.02 against cash operating costs of $11.72/boe (LOE $5.12, GTP $1.84, other taxes $2.10, G&A $2.66) produces a cash margin near $24.30/boe. EBITDA of $883.7M (operating income + $437.8M DD&A + $6.8M ARO accretion) equals $24.26/boe, a 67.4% margin at $63 realized oil. The driver is geographic — realizations track LLS/Houston rather than inland benchmarks — combined with genuinely low absolute costs.

**Cash generation and allocation were tightly balanced.** FY2025 operating cash flow $878.6M, oil and gas property additions $469.5M, free cash flow $409.2M (31.2% of revenue). Reinvestment rate 53.4%. Shareholder returns of $322.1M (buybacks $205.5M, dividends $113.1M, NCI distributions $3.5M) equalled 78.7% of pre-acquisition FCF; 8.9 million shares, or 4.7% of the opening count, were retired. Cash rose only $6.7M on the year.

**Pre-deal balance sheet was fortress-grade, and a widely circulated screen misstates it.** Total assets $2,903.1M, equity $1,999.2M, total liabilities $903.9M, debt $400M principal of 6.875% notes due December 2032, revolver undrawn, cash $266.8M. Actual debt/equity is 0.20x; total-liabilities/equity 0.45x. A screening output reporting "D/E of 1.45" is reporting the equity multiplier (assets/equity = 1.4521), and the resulting Altman Z of 2.39 substitutes equity/assets for both the retained-earnings and market-equity terms. Recomputed properly (retained earnings/assets 0.333; market equity/liabilities ~5.0), Z is approximately 4.5 — comfortably safe zone. Any pre-deal distress framing on MGY is an artifact.

**Pro forma, the leverage step-up is genuine.** Estimated total debt at close: $400M (2032 notes) + $600M (assumed 2029 notes) + $500M (new notes) + ~$870M revolver ≈ $2.37bn, with net debt near $2.3-2.4bn. Pro forma EBITDAX at ~$70 WTI: Magnolia standalone Q1 2026 EBITDA annualizes to ~$979M; WildFire at 53,000 boe/d, 70% oil, ~$51.5/boe revenue and ~$16/boe cash costs supports ~$650-690M. Combined ~$1.63bn unsynergized, ~$1.73bn with the $100M target. That is roughly 1.36x net debt/EBITDAX synergized at $70 WTI, widening toward 1.6x at $60 and compressing toward 1.11x at $85. The RBL covenant is 3.50x leverage and 1.00x current ratio; leverage headroom is wide in all cases. The current ratio is thinner — Q1 2026 was 1.10x and the deal consumes most of the cash balance, with acquired receivables the offset. Monitor, do not assume comfort.

**The tax tailwind is self-liquidating.** FY2025 cash taxes were $7.8M on $417.4M of pretax income (1.9%), attributable to restored 100% bonus depreciation under the July 2025 legislation. Net deferred taxes have already flipped to a $20.7M liability at 12/31/25. Post-close capex on WildFire assets will generate fresh IDC and depreciation deductions, so convergence toward the ~19% book rate will be gradual, but the direction is one way.

**Earnings track record supports confidence in near-term consensus, with a caveat.** Four quarters: two beats (Q2 2026 +9.8%, Q1 2026 +2.1%), two in line. No misses. The beats are price-driven, which means the delivery record tells you more about realizations than about operational control.

## Growth Analysis

Three distinct growth vectors, with different qualities of evidence.

**Acquired scale (confirmed).** Production rises roughly 50% to ~159 Mboe/d at close. WildFire brings ~810,000 net acres in Giddings at 70% oil weighting and a 29% base oil decline rate — a low decline that should require less maintenance capital per barrel than Magnolia's existing base. Pro forma capital spending is capped at 55% of adjusted EBITDAX. Note that FY2025 actual reinvestment was 53.1%, so the disclosed 55% ceiling is slightly *above* the recent actual, not below it. A ceiling is not a target, and the low-decline acquired base plausibly lowers the blended rate, but the 55% figure does not by itself evidence improvement.

**Organic (confirmed, modest).** Standalone 2026 production growth guidance was raised to 6% from 5% alongside the Q2 release. Q2 production of 106.1 Mboe/d was +3.5% sequentially. FY2025 reserve replacement was 151% (142% organic) with organic F&D of $9.28/boe against DD&A of $12.02/boe — recent capital is being deployed below the historical book basis, which is the single most relevant operational argument for underwriting 810,000 new acres. The recycle ratio of 2.3-2.6x is healthy.

**Inventory duration (asserted, unproven).** Reported reserve life of 5.77 years (4.57 years proved developed) understates asset life because PUDs are only 20.7% of proved reserves — Magnolia books near-term locations rather than using the full five-year SEC window. The standardized measure of $2,518.5M against a standalone EV of $4.67bn (1.85x) implies roughly 35-45% of pre-deal equity value sat in resource outside proved reserves. That is the unbooked Giddings inventory, and extending it is the stated strategic logic of the acquisition. It is also the part that cannot be verified from filings.

**Estimate revisions are mixed and require careful reading.** Next-year consensus rose 26.4% over 90 days to $3.31 (13 analysts), but that revision coincides with the deal announcement and reflects an asset and share addition rather than organic acceleration. Working through the pro forma arithmetic — ~$1.73bn EBITDAX, ~$810M DD&A on a stepped-up basis, ~$158M cash interest, 19% tax — implies roughly $2.30/share at $70 WTI and ~$3.45 at $85. The $3.31 consensus therefore embeds something in the low-$80s WTI. Meanwhile next-quarter consensus was cut 26.9% in thirty days to $0.56, a direct read on the post-spike price fade. Current-year consensus of $2.56 carries one analyst and should be ignored as a consensus signal, though it reconciles to Q1 $0.54 + Q2 $0.99 + roughly $1.03 in H2.

The honest summary: the growth is real, largely purchased, per-share diluted by ~30% of ownership, and its earnings translation is set by a commodity price the company does not hedge.

## Valuation Assessment

**Pro forma capitalization.** 236.97M shares outstanding post-offering (including an apparently exercised greenshoe) plus 32.2M consideration shares equals ~269.2M. At $23.72 that is a $6.39bn market capitalization and, with ~$2.35bn net debt, an ~$8.7bn enterprise value.

|---|---|---|---|
| EBITDAX | ~$1.47bn | ~$1.73bn | ~$2.12bn |
| EV/EBITDAX at $23.72 | 5.9x | 5.0x | 4.1x |
| Net debt/EBITDAX | ~1.6x | ~1.36x | ~1.11x |

**What the price implies.** At $8.7bn EV and Magnolia's own historical 5.28x standalone multiple, the market is capitalizing roughly $1.65bn of EBITDAX, which the sensitivity grid places near $67 WTI. The strip sits just below $80 after falling from above $80. The market is discounting the spike as temporary, which is a reasonable instinct; the question is whether it is discounting too much. Modest upside exists if the current strip holds.

**Peer context.** The reported peer median P/E of 32.5x is distorted (MUR 65.8x, CRC negative, CNX 4.4x). The cleanest comparable is MTDR at 13.8x with a 2.7% yield, 30% below its 52-week high. MGY at ~13.8x trailing (FY2025 EPS $1.73) with a 2.9% yield, 27.6% below its high, is priced roughly in line. There is no peer-relative mispricing to exploit, and a low headline multiple in a commodity business at the top of a price spike is the classic value trap configuration — cheapness here needs a catalyst, not just a number.

**Deal valuation.** $4.06bn ÷ 53,000 boe/d = ~$76,600 per flowing barrel. Implied EV/EBITDAX on WildFire alone is ~6.25x unsynergized and ~5.41x with synergies, versus Magnolia's standalone 5.28x. Pro forma EV/EBITDAX of ~5.0x synergized is essentially the same multiple the market applied to the smaller, unlevered company. The equity case therefore rests entirely on synergy delivery (the $700M NPV equals ~11% of pro forma market cap), acquired inventory quality, and a lower blended reinvestment rate — not on buying assets cheaply. A competitive auction process, per Bloomberg's June 26 report that Magnolia had emerged as front-runner, usually implies a full price.

**Analyst targets.** Low $26 (+9.6%), mean $32.76 (+38.1%), median $33, high $41. Even the low target is above spot, and the buy/hold split improved from 9/9 to 11/7 over three months. These are useful corroboration but not independent evidence: the target cluster near $33 requires roughly $80-85 WTI on my arithmetic, the same assumption embedded in the $3.31 next-year consensus. I weight them as a sanity check on the bull case, not as a floor.

Valuation scores 5.5. There is a defensible discount to the strip, but the discount is not large, the multiple offers no arbitrage, and the asymmetry is contingent on an unhedged macro variable.

## Competitive Landscape

Magnolia's cost advantage is geographic rather than technological. Gulf Coast proximity delivers LLS/Houston-linked realizations ($63.18/bbl in FY2025, $70.29 in Q1 2026) rather than the wider inland differentials Permian and Bakken operators absorb, and combined with $11.72/boe cash costs it produces the 67% EBITDA margin. That advantage is durable because it is locational.

Post-close, Magnolia becomes the largest acreage holder in the South Texas Eagle Ford/Austin Chalk trend with >1.25 million net Giddings acres. Two vertical-integration assets come with the deal: a sand mine covering ~80% of Magnolia's proppant consumption plus 100% of WildFire's requirements with third-party sales, and 500+ miles of Giddings gas gathering pipeline. Magnolia becomes the owner-operator of both, insourcing two cost centers most E&Ps rent, and this is plausibly where a meaningful share of the >$100M synergy target originates alongside longer laterals from contiguous acreage and eliminated corporate G&A.

Two competitive-position offsets deserve weight. First, Karnes crude egress remains contracted to a third party under a Plains Eagle Ford Energy gathering agreement expiring June 30, 2027 — a renegotiation inside twelve months of closing, with the counterparty aware of Magnolia's increased scale. Second, purchaser concentration moved the wrong way: FY2025 disclosed two purchasers at 41% and 20% of revenue, versus FY2024's three at 31%, 24% and 12%. The aggregate top-three share fell from 67% to 61%, but the largest single purchaser's share rose ten points. Commodity mix diversification (gas to ~15% of revenue) addresses price correlation and says nothing about counterparty credit or offtake continuity; those are different exposures and the direct measure worsened.

Trajectory is improving on scale, integration and acreage position; stable-to-slightly-worse on offtake dependency and margin, the latter price-driven. Score 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Unhedged commodity exposure against newly added leverage.** No commodity derivative assets or liabilities appear on the 12/31/25 or 3/31/26 balance sheets and no hedging note exists in either filing. Unhedged at 0.15x leverage is a defensible policy choice. Unhedged at ~1.36-1.6x with a ~$870M revolver draw is a materially different proposition, and it is the single largest risk in this equity. Company-disclosed sensitivity: $1.00/bbl moves standalone revenue ~$14.6M annualized, roughly doubling pro forma to ~$28M. Whether the amended $2.0bn borrowing-base facility imposes hedging requirements is not verifiable from available filings.

**2. The oil path is a live two-sided binary.** Crude topped $80 before falling below $80, Gulf exports remain reported constrained through the Strait of Hormuz, and OPEC+ decisions remain a factor. Verified sustained transit normalization plus restored OPEC+ supply is the bear path; renewed attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure is the bull path. Neither is forecastable, and Magnolia has chosen to take the full exposure.

**3. Deleveraging is price-contingent.** Management describes the debt increase as "temporary," enabled by free cash flow above the shareholder-return program, while simultaneously raising the dividend 9% to $0.18/quarter and committing to buybacks of "at least 1% of outstanding shares per quarter." At $70 WTI, $560M of estimated FCF against $189M of dividends and ~$255M of buybacks leaves roughly $116M/year for debt reduction — a multi-year path, not a swift one. At $85 WTI the picture transforms (~$500M/year of paydown). At $60 WTI the returns program approximately consumes all free cash flow. The likely resolution at mid-cycle prices is that buybacks flex down, which is a stated intention rather than an obligation.

**4. Integration and subsurface risk on 810,000 net acres.** The acquired upside spans Austin Chalk, Eagle Ford and Woodbine. Magnolia's own Giddings de-risking has taken eight deliberately gradual years. The company is now underwriting a large acreage block on its subsurface interpretation with a substantially larger balance sheet at risk. The FY2025 F&D record ($9.28/boe organic, below $12.02 DD&A) is the best available evidence that it can, and it is indirect evidence.

**5. Litigation.** Both the FY2025 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q disclose a suit in which plaintiffs claim entitlement to a minority working interest in certain Karnes County assets. Exposure is "not reasonably estimable," and the Co-Defendants (certain former LLC Unit Holders and an EnerVest fund) "retain all such liability." Those parties fully exited their Magnolia equity in February 2026, so the indemnity now rests on contract alone. That is an observation about alignment, not evidence of impaired recoverability; disclosure language is unchanged.

**6. Pro forma balance-sheet screens will deteriorate.** Recomputing Altman on estimated pro forma figures (liabilities ~$3.35bn, assets ~$7.2bn, retained earnings unchanged at ~$1.04bn) gives roughly 2.15, moving from safe to grey zone, driven by market equity relative to liabilities and retained-earnings dilution against a doubled asset base. For a 1.36x-levered E&P with 67% EBITDA margins and covenant headroom to 3.50x this is not a distress signal, and Altman was calibrated on manufacturers. It will nonetheless show up in quantitative screens and may affect flow.

**7. Reported reserve life leaves no PDP annuity.** A 4.57-year proved developed R/P means continuous drilling is mandatory. Benign explanation, real operational consequence.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (28 contracts across three expirations), so read it as directional color rather than positioning evidence. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 71.7% against 32.6% one-year realized, a 39pp premium at the 79th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (71.7% at 14 days versus 37.3% at 133 days). Implied volatility jumped from 39.6% to 71.7% between July 21 and August 6 while the price fell 4.0%. The August 21 tenor prices an ±11.1% move. The data source labels the near-term 4.8% put/call skew as elevated put demand; the three-month tenor reads -13.4%, so the near-dated caution does not extend down the curve, and open interest remains call-tilted at 0.38.

The practical takeaways: the market is pricing an imminent, discrete event — deal close (expected late Q3), the associated 8-K disclosures, and oil headlines — with high near-term uncertainty and much less six months out. Near-term options are expensive; holders can monetize that through covered calls, and buyers of protection are paying up. Elevated near-dated volatility with a rich premium to realized argues against establishing new long exposure into the event rather than for it.

Separately, short interest is 15.6% of float at 6.5 days to cover. Some portion is mechanical (equity-offering hedging and index flows around a 46.3M-share issuance), but at that level it creates genuine squeeze risk on a bullish oil surprise, cutting both ways against the bear case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $38 (+60%)

Hormuz disruption persists or re-escalates, WTI holds $85+, and pro forma EBITDAX reaches ~$2.12bn. At 6.0x — a modest premium for scale, vertical integration and a low-decline acquired base — enterprise value is $12.9bn, equity ~$10.6bn, or ~$39/share; I mark the bull at $38 for conservatism against the $41 high analyst target. Free cash flow of ~$950M funds $189M of dividends, ~$255M of buybacks and ~$500M/year of debt reduction, returning leverage toward 0.5x within three years and validating the "temporary" framing. Synergies exceed the $100M target (the $700M NPV suggests management sees durability), Woodbine and Austin Chalk results outside the proven fairway confirm the inventory thesis, and 15.6% short interest accelerates the move. The buyback resumes at prices below the $23.75 issuance, making the round trip accretive rather than dilutive.

### Bear Case — $17 (-28%)

Sustained verified Hormuz transit resumes, restored OPEC+ supply lands into a weaker growth economy, and WTI settles in the mid-$50s to low-$60s. Pro forma EBITDAX falls to ~$1.35-1.47bn, net debt/EBITDAX rises toward 1.6-1.7x, and the shareholder-return program consumes all free cash flow. Buybacks are suspended, which removes the per-share growth engine that has defined the equity story; the dividend survives but the raise looks premature. Multiple compresses to 4.75x on a levered unhedged producer, giving ~$16-17/share. Compounding risks: acquired inventory disappoints outside the fairway, the June 2027 Plains gathering renegotiation lands unfavorably, and the 41%-of-revenue purchaser relationship requires re-pricing. The prior 52-week low of $21.07 was set on an unlevered balance sheet; the pro forma entity has no such floor.

### Base Case — $28 (+18%)

WTI averages $70-75, the deal closes late Q3, and synergies are partially delivered. EBITDAX ~$1.80bn at 5.5x gives $9.9bn EV, $7.55bn equity, $28.05/share. Rounded to $28. Free cash flow near $560-620M funds the $0.72 annualized dividend (3.0% yield at $23.72), buybacks at a reduced pace, and $100-200M/year of debt reduction. The stock re-rates modestly as the market accepts that the pro forma entity generates high-single-digit free cash flow yields at mid-cycle prices, which is roughly where the sell-side low target of $26 sits.

**Reward-to-risk from base case: 18% up versus 28% down, or 0.64:1.** That ratio, not the absolute upside, is why this is a hold rather than a purchase.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Two dated checkpoints. First, deal closing, expected late Q3 2026, with the accompanying 8-K and the Q2 2026 10-Q that will carry WildFire-related risk factors and the first disclosed pro forma capitalization (including the actual revolver draw and new-note coupons, neither currently verifiable). Second, the Q3 2026 earnings release (late October/early November), which is the first quarter of combined operations and the first test of the $0.56 consensus against realized post-spike prices.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Hedging disclosure.** If the amended facility or management action results in 40%+ of pro forma 2027 oil production hedged at $70 or better, the bear case narrows materially and the leverage becomes ordinary rather than distinctive. This single change would be worth roughly a full point on risk factors.
2. **Price near $20.** At $20 with pro forma net debt of $2.35bn, EV falls to ~$7.75bn and EV/EBITDAX to 4.5x at $70 WTI, with a ~12% free cash flow yield. That level compensates for unhedged exposure at ~1.36x leverage.
3. **Synergy confirmation with realized deleveraging.** Two consecutive quarters showing pro forma cash operating costs at or below $14/boe, annualized synergy capture tracking at or above $100M, and net debt declining sequentially. That would convert the "swift and steady" claim from assertion to evidence.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Buyback suspension without a corresponding debt-reduction step-up**, which would confirm the $60-WTI arithmetic in which the returns program consumes all free cash flow.
2. **Pro forma net debt/EBITDAX above 2.0x** on a trailing four-quarter basis, or a current ratio below 1.05x, either of which brings covenant management into the equity story.
3. **Two consecutive quarters of Giddings well results below the acquired base's implied productivity**, or a downward reserve revision on the acquired acreage — the direct test of the inventory thesis on which the $4.06bn price rests.
4. **Verified sustained Hormuz normalization with WTI below $60** while the company remains unhedged.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** hold through the close. The near-dated options premium (71.7% IV versus 32.6% realized) is unusually rich, making covered call writing against a portion of the position an efficient way to harvest the event premium. Do not add at $23.72.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory on this name has run 6.6 → 5.3 over three months, with management quality (-1.7) and financial health (-1.6) carrying the decline. That markdown was correct in substance: the company traded a 0.15x-leverage balance sheet for a 1.3-1.5x one, diluted ownership by ~30%, and bought assets at a full price in a competitive auction with no multiple arbitrage, having repurchased stock at $30.38 in March before issuing at $23.75 in July.

This review marks a modest improvement to 5.65, and the reasons are specific rather than sentimental. The financing is executed rather than prospective — 46.32M shares placed, $500M of notes closed August 5, a $1.75bn committed facility in place — which removes funding risk. Q2 delivered a 9.8% EPS beat and standalone production guidance was raised to 6% growth from 5%. Next-year consensus is up 26.4% over ninety days and the buy/hold split improved from 9/9 to 11/7, with even the low sell-side target 9.6% above spot. The price is 4-5% lower than at the prior review on unchanged-to-better company-specific data, which improves risk/reward rather than degrading the thesis.

What prevents an upgrade is the composition of the remaining risk. Magnolia has taken a roughly nine-fold increase in leverage into an oil tape that has fallen from above $80 to below $80, and it carries that leverage with no hedges. At $23.72 the market is capitalizing roughly $67 mid-cycle WTI, which is a reasonable rather than a distressed price, and which leaves 18% to my base case against 28% to my bear case. The controllable parts of this story — cost structure at $11.72/boe, F&D at $9.28/boe below book DD&A, vertical integration, executive stability, a governance structure now free of its sponsor — are good. The variable that will determine the return is not one management controls, and it has chosen not to insure against it.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $28 (+18%), bull $38 (+60%), bear $17 (-28%). Hold existing positions through the close and the first combined quarter; new capital should wait for hedging disclosure, evidence of synergy capture with sequential debt reduction, or a price near $20.
