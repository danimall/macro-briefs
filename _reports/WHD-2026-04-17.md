## Executive Summary

Cactus Inc. is a leading U.S. manufacturer of pressure control equipment and spoolable pipe technologies for the onshore oil and gas industry. On January 1, 2026, the company closed a $344.5M acquisition of a 65% controlling interest in Baker Hughes' international Surface Pressure Control (SPC) business, creating a new entity, Cactus International, that nearly doubles the Pressure Control segment's revenue and gives the company its first meaningful international footprint, primarily in the Middle East.

The financial profile remains strong: near-zero financial debt ($17M), $220M in FY2025 free cash flow, 23% operating margins, and an Altman Z-score of 4.12. But the domestic cycle is deteriorating. U.S. land rigs have fallen from 667 (2023) to 545 (2025), FY2025 revenue declined 4.5%, adjusted EBITDA peaked in 2023 at $398M and fell to $353M, and analyst estimates are being cut across every forward period. At 22x forward earnings, a premium to the 18.2x peer median, the stock embeds meaningful execution premium on Baker Hughes integration before that deal has reported a single full quarter. Coordinated insider selling by both co-founders (~$20M combined in March 2026) adds a caution signal at precisely the moment when integration risk is highest.

The stock has rallied 48% from its October 2025 lows on Middle East expansion enthusiasm and geopolitical-driven oil price volatility. At $53.36, the market is pricing the bull case on integration success. The risk/reward from here requires confident execution on an international business the company has never operated, against a domestic backdrop of falling rig counts and tariff headwinds on its Vietnam and China manufacturing base.

## Company Overview

Cactus Inc. operates through two segments. **Pressure Control** (~66% of FY2025 revenue, $717M) designs, manufactures, sells, and rents wellhead systems, production trees, and frac equipment under the Cactus Wellhead brand. **Spoolable Technologies** (~34%, $368M) manufactures FlexSteel-branded spoolable pipe used for production and gathering applications, acquired via the FlexSteel/HighRidge deal in February 2023 for ~$659M. The company's revenue mix is approximately 76% product sales, 8% rentals, and 16% field services.

Founded in 2011 by Scott and Joel Bender, who have been in the wellhead manufacturing business since the late 1970s, Cactus operates manufacturing facilities in Bossier City, LA; Suzhou, China; Hai Duong, Vietnam; and Baytown, TX, with service centers across major U.S. basins and in Australia and the Middle East. The Baker Hughes JV, effective January 2026, adds substantial international operations primarily in the Middle East.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $1.079B, down 4.5% from $1.130B in FY2024, driven by a 6% decline in average U.S. land rig counts (545 vs. 580) and a 15% drop in average WTI ($65.39 vs. $76.63). Pressure Control held up relatively well (-0.9%), while Spoolable Technologies declined 9.5%, reflecting its higher sensitivity to completion volumes. Adjusted EBITDA has been in decline since peaking at $398M in FY2023, falling to $392M in FY2024 and $353M in FY2025.

Operating income was $250.5M (23.2% margin), down from $289.6M (25.6% margin) in FY2024. Margin compression stemmed from three sources: Section 232 steel tariffs (50%) and China/Vietnam import tariffs inflating input costs; increased litigation reserves in the Pressure Control segment; and $12M in incremental corporate expenses related to the Baker Hughes transaction.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet.** Operating cash flow was $258M, with FCF of $220M after just $39M in capex, a 20.4% FCF margin that is exceptional for OFS companies. Financial debt is essentially zero ($17M total). The current ratio of 5.81 is inflated by restricted cash for the Baker Hughes closing. Post-transaction, the cash position has been meaningfully reduced by the $344.5M acquisition outflow.

Key balance sheet items to monitor: $203M in goodwill and $148M in intangibles from FlexSteel; and a Tax Receivable Agreement (TRA) liability estimated at $251M (discounted), requiring Cactus to remit 85% of realized tax savings to TRA holders. This is a persistent cash drain that structurally reduces shareholder-available cash flow.

**Earnings Trajectory.** EPS was $2.41 for FY2025. Current consensus estimates: FY2026E $2.96, FY2027E $3.41. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative — current quarter estimates have been cut 5.0% over the past 90 days, next quarter cut 5.3%, FY2026 cut 3.0%, and FY2027 cut 3.9%. This negative revision momentum is the dominant quantitative signal and directly conflicts with the stock's 48% rally from October lows.

**Earnings Surprise History.** Three beats in the last four quarters, with the sole miss (Q2 2025, -9.2%) occurring during the domestic activity trough. Q4 2025 beat of 11.5% was encouraging but has not reversed the estimate revision trend.

## Growth Analysis

**Baker Hughes JV — The Bull Case Catalyst.** Q1 2026 Pressure Control revenue guidance of $295–305M implies the acquired international business contributes approximately $115–125M per quarter, or $460–500M annualized. This nearly doubles the Pressure Control segment's revenue base and shifts the company's geographic exposure meaningfully toward the Middle East, where upstream spending is increasing independently of U.S. onshore cycles.

If the JV executes well, the growth profile transforms. Middle East drilling activity has been growing while U.S. land declines. The JV provides access to national oil companies and international markets that Cactus could not have entered organically. The 10-K noted the company previously had "no significant infrastructure in most of the countries where the Joint Venture is doing business." Baker Hughes is providing transitional support, but the learning curve is steep.

**Domestic Headwinds.** U.S. land rig counts have declined three consecutive years. A sustained decline below 500 rigs would compress domestic margins further, and the domestic business still generates the majority of segment-level cash flow.

**Spoolable Technologies.** The FlexSteel business declined 9.5% in FY2025. This segment ties directly to wells being connected to production infrastructure. With fewer wells being drilled and completed, organic growth requires market share gains, which are not evident in the data.

## Valuation Assessment

At $53.36, WHD trades at 22.3x trailing earnings ($2.41 EPS) and 18.0x FY2026E ($2.96). Forward P/E of ~18x sits at the peer median of 18.2x, which would be fair if earnings estimates were stable. They are not. The more relevant comparison: OII trades at 6.9x, TDW at 7.6x, and INVX at 18.2x. KGS at 42x is a gas-infrastructure premium name and not directly comparable.

The stock's 48% rally from October lows has already priced in considerable Baker Hughes JV upside. Consensus price target median of $59 (mean $58.22) implies only 10.6% upside from current levels, with the low target of $50 sitting 6.3% below and the high at $69 representing 29% upside. With 3 strong buys, 3 buys, and 3 holds among covering analysts, sentiment is supportive but not unanimous.

On an EV/EBITDA basis, using FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $353M and an enterprise value of approximately $3.7B (minimal net debt post-deal cash outflow, adjusted for TRA), the stock trades at roughly 10.5x trailing EBITDA. This is fair for a diversified OFS company with above-average margins, but rich for one facing negative estimate revisions and domestic cyclical pressure.

## Competitive Landscape

Cactus is one of the largest U.S. wellhead suppliers, competing against Vault, SLB divisions, and TechnipFMC. Its key advantages are: proprietary designs with shorter delivery times, a vertically integrated manufacturing model, and strong customer relationships built over decades. FY2025 operating margins of 23.2% and FCF margins of 20.4% significantly exceed most OFS peers, indicating genuine cost advantages and/or pricing power.

The Baker Hughes JV adds international scale but requires different capabilities than the U.S. onshore market, including NOC relationship management, local content requirements, and longer sales cycles.

Customer concentration is rising: one customer accounted for 17% of FY2025 revenue, up from 10% in FY2023. This trajectory warrants monitoring.

## Risk Assessment

**Domestic Cyclical Risk (HIGH).** A sustained decline below 500 rigs would compress domestic margins materially, and management's own guidance expects further weakness in activity levels.

**Baker Hughes Integration Risk (HIGH).** The company is integrating an international business across countries where it has no prior infrastructure, relying on Baker Hughes transition services. Baker Hughes retains a 35% stake with a put right exercisable from January 2028 at up to $660M EV. If triggered, this could require $200M+ in external financing, meaningfully altering the balance sheet. The $530M minimum floor on a Cactus-initiated call also represents a substantial potential obligation.

**Tariff Exposure (ELEVATED).** Manufacturing in Vietnam and China creates direct exposure to Section 232 steel tariffs (50%), synthetic opioid tariffs on Chinese imports, and Vietnam-specific tariffs. Inventory carrying costs increased $27M in FY2025 due to tariff-inflated values. Further escalation targeting Vietnam would undermine the company's supply chain diversification strategy.

**Insider Selling (NOTABLE).** Both co-founders sold approximately $10M each (~$20M combined) in early March 2026, converting CC Units to Class A shares and immediately selling. This was coordinated, sizable, and occurred shortly after closing the Baker Hughes deal and reporting Q4 earnings. No open-market purchases by any insider were recorded.

**Estimate Revisions (NEGATIVE).** Negative estimate momentum at a stock trading above peer median multiples creates valuation vulnerability.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call OI ratio of 0.66 (call-heavy) and balanced put/call skew (1.7%) suggest the options market is not pricing significant near-term downside risk. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($65–69, 22–29% upside).** Baker Hughes JV ramps successfully, contributing $450–500M in annualized revenue at margins comparable to or above domestic Pressure Control (26–30%). International growth offsets domestic declines, and Cactus trades to 20x FY2027E EPS of $3.40+. Middle East upstream spending cycle extends through 2027+. Tariff headwinds stabilize or partially reverse.

**Bear Case ($38–42, 21–29% downside).** U.S. rig counts decline below 500, compressing domestic margins. Baker Hughes JV integration proves difficult — transition services end before Cactus can operate independently, creating revenue disruption. Baker Hughes exercises its put right in 2028, requiring Cactus to finance $200M+ at unfavorable terms. Tariff escalation on Vietnam undermines manufacturing economics. Stock re-rates to 12–14x forward earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q1 2026 earnings (due late April/early May 2026) demonstrate JV segment margins above 20% with evidence of standalone operational capability.
2. Stock pulls back to $45–47 (15.2–17.0x FY2026E), removing the execution premium and creating adequate margin of safety.
3. Estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive over a 30-day period.

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q1 2026 JV revenue below $100M per quarter or segment margins below 15%, indicating integration difficulties.
2. Additional coordinated insider selling by the Bender family exceeding $10M.
3. U.S. land rig count declines below 480, combined with oil sustained below $60/bbl.

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q1 2026 earnings (expected late April/early May 2026), the first quarter fully consolidating the Baker Hughes JV, is the single most important near-term data point.

## Conclusion

Cactus Inc. has an excellent balance sheet, above-peer profitability, and a potentially transformational international expansion via the Baker Hughes JV. But at 22x trailing / ~18x forward earnings, at or above the peer median, the stock already prices in successful execution of an international business the company has never operated, against a backdrop of declining domestic activity, uniformly negative estimate revisions, and coordinated insider selling by both founders. The specific risk preventing a BUY is the combination of: (1) negative earnings momentum (FY2026E cut 3%, FY2027E cut 3.9% over 90 days, accelerating); (2) the unproven Baker Hughes JV, which has not yet reported a single quarter of consolidated results; and (3) insider selling at scale during the period when management should have the highest conviction in the JV's prospects. Until Q1 2026 earnings validate the JV revenue contribution and margin profile, the risk/reward does not justify adding new positions at current levels. HOLD.
