**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Reinsurance Group of America is one of the world's leading pure-play life and health reinsurers, with roughly $4.3 trillion of life reinsurance in force and $164 billion of total assets. The franchise has become one of the better-executing capital compounders in the Financials complex: net income reached a record $1.18 billion in 2025 (from $617 million in 2021), adjusted operating ROE ex-AOCI improved to 15.7% (from 8.4% in 2021), and Q1 2026 delivered $6.49 billion of revenue (+23% YoY) and adjusted operating income before tax of $611 million (+26%). Net investment income rose 38% YoY on higher reinvestment yields and a rebound in variable investment income, and operating cash flow was $2.87 billion in the quarter. The Financial Solutions engine (longevity, pension risk transfer, and asset-intensive reinsurance) is scaling rapidly, aided by the July 2025 Equitable Holdings block reinsurance transaction that nearly doubled U.S. asset-intensive assets over two years.

The complication is price. The stock has run 22% since mid-2026 to $241.79, sitting near its 52-week high of $244 and just above the analyst low target of $212. That rally is the direct reason our recommendation moves back to HOLD after two consecutive BUY calls at lower prices. The fundamentals remain intact-to-improving, but the asymmetry has compressed: current-quarter and next-quarter estimates have edged lower over 30 days, current-year consensus rose only +3.4% over 90 days, and traditional-book loss ratios ticked up in both the U.S. and Asia Pacific in Q1'26. The entry no longer offers the margin of safety that justified prior BUYs at $195–210.

We rate RGA a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.3. The 12-month case still points modestly higher — mid-teens operating ROE, rising net investment income, an accelerating capital-return program ($500M buyback plus a dividend hike), and analyst targets clustered above the current price — but the risk/reward at $242 is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would re-engage on a pullback toward book value (~$203/share) or on confirmation that the traditional loss-ratio drift is noise rather than trend.

## Company Overview

RGA operates through nine geographic and business-line segments spanning the U.S. & Latin America, Canada, EMEA, and Asia Pacific. The business splits into two economic engines:

- **Traditional Reinsurance**: individual and group life, health, disability, critical illness, and long-term care. This is long-duration mortality/morbidity risk, with premiums earned over 10–30+ years and profitability driven by claims experience relative to pricing. It is the core underwriting franchise and, per management, the "most significant risk" line.
- **Financial Solutions**: longevity reinsurance, asset-intensive reinsurance (primarily annuities), pension risk transfer, capital/financial reinsurance, and stable-value products. Profitability here is driven by investment spreads and fees. This is the faster-growing, more capital-markets-sensitive part of the company.

The competitive moat rests on mortality/morbidity analytics, fast individual-underwriting quote turnaround, deep ceding-insurer relationships, global scale, and strong financial-strength ratings. The global life reinsurance market is oligopolistic and relationship-driven; RGA sits in the top tier alongside Munich Re, Swiss Re, SCOR, and Hannover Re, and competes in asset-intensive and PRT markets against PE-backed platforms such as Apollo/Athene and KKR/Global Atlantic.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Net income progressed $617M (2021) → $623M (2022) → $902M (2023) → $717M (2024) → $1,182M (2025, record). Adjusted operating ROE ex-AOCI climbed to 15.7% in 2025, a structural improvement rather than a one-year spike.

**Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025.** Total revenue $6,494M (+23%); net premiums $4,595M (+14%); net investment income $1,701M (+38%). Net income $331M vs $288M; diluted EPS $4.98 vs $4.27 (+17%). Adjusted operating income before tax $611M (+26%). Investment yield ex-spread reached 4.93% (+29bps), and variable investment income swung to +$26M from –$6M as LP/JV distributions recovered. Operating cash flow was $2,873M.

**The earnings-vs-comprehensive-income divergence is the report's most important nuance.** Q1 2026 posted a total comprehensive loss of $(38)M despite $330M of net income, because rising rates produced $(1,659)M of unrealized investment losses, partly offset by a $1,308M discount-rate benefit on future policy benefits. This is largely an LDTI (ASU 2018-12) and mark-to-market artifact. Economically, higher rates are a tailwind: reinvestment yields are rising and net investment income is up 38%. Gross unrealized bond losses grew to $8.5B (from $7.0B), and management states it can and intends to hold to recovery. An analyst reading comprehensive income or unadjusted book value naively would misinterpret an improving franchise; the adjusted operating metrics are the right lens, and they are strong.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total equity $13.4B; book value ~$203/share on 65.5M shares. The fixed maturity portfolio is $107.3B, 94.3% investment grade, 68% corporate and well-diversified. Long-term debt is $6.1B at a 5.40% average rate, comfortably inside the 35% debt/cap covenant. The screening Altman Z-Score flags distress, but that model is built for industrials and misreads a reinsurer's reserve-heavy, high-leverage balance sheet; the actual ratios (record earnings, 15.7% ROE, $2.87B operating cash flow) describe a healthy franchise.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters: +14.2% (Q1'26), +33.5% (Q4'25), +9.0% (Q3'25), and –16.1% (Q2'25). The Q2'25 miss illustrates that reinsurance earnings are lumpy quarter-to-quarter on claims experience, which reduces confidence that any single beat is a durable trend. On balance the record supports consensus but not aggressive upward extrapolation.

**Capital return.** A fresh $500M buyback was authorized Jan 29, 2026 ($450M remaining after Q1 repurchases of 225,638 shares near $221.59), and the dividend was raised to $0.93/quarter. The effective tax rate is elevated at 24.9% on foreign-jurisdiction mix and a foreign-tax-credit valuation allowance, a structural drag versus domestic-heavy peers.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three legs, all currently working:

1. **Rising reinvestment yields.** Higher-for-longer rates are a durable tailwind to spread income as the portfolio rolls into higher coupons.
2. **Financial Solutions scaling.** The Equitable transaction added a 75% quota share of Equitable's in-force individual life liabilities (~$12B total, including $16.1B separate-account liabilities) and contributed +$39M pre-tax in Q1'26. U.S. asset-intensive assets reached $37.2B (from $25.8B YoY). Management is treating large block reinsurance as a repeatable growth template, not a one-off.
3. **New-business production.** $150.6B of face amount added in Q1'26 (vs $131.7B prior year), with Asia Pacific Traditional production surging to $40.0B (from $14.3B).

The offsetting signal is estimate momentum. Consensus current-year EPS is $26.97, up +3.4% over 90 days but essentially flat over 30 days (–0.4%); current-quarter and next-quarter estimates have edged down over 30 days. Next-year consensus of $29.25 has risen modestly (+1.3% / 90d). This is a stable, mid-single-digit compounder rather than one showing the >30% revision velocity that would flag acceleration. Growth Prospects score 6.5, reflecting a durable secular tailwind (PRT, longevity, asset-intensive block demand) tempered by decelerating revision momentum. This continues the modest downward drift in this dimension seen across prior reports (7.5 → 6.8 → 6.5); the deceleration is real but gradual, not a break.

## Valuation Assessment

At $241.79, RGA trades at ~13.1x trailing EPS of $17.69 (per profile), roughly 9x consensus 2026 EPS, and about 1.2x book value. On an absolute basis these multiples look inexpensive, and the dividend yields ~1.6%.

Against reinsurance peers, the picture is more nuanced. The peer set — Everest Group (6.9x), RenaissanceRe (4.9x), MGIC (8.1x), SiriusPoint (6.0x) — carries a median P/E of 6.9x. But those are predominantly property-cat and mortgage-insurance names whose earnings are inflated by a hard P&C pricing cycle now rolling over; their low multiples partly reflect the market pricing in cyclical earnings decline. RGA's life & longevity book has more stable, longer-duration earnings and deserves a premium to that cohort, so the ~9x forward multiple is not obviously cheap relative to appropriately-adjusted peers.

The key valuation fact is the re-rating. The stock has moved from ~$195–210 in the periods that supported prior BUY calls to $241.79 today, capturing most of the upside to the analyst mean of $257. The median target of $269 implies ~11% further upside, and the high target is $288. Cheapness alone is not a buy signal, and here a superficially low multiple does not translate into asymmetric upside because the price has already absorbed much of the improving-fundamentals story. We do not penalize the stock for having appreciated — the run was driven by genuine earnings gains — but we assess price against forward fundamentals today, and at ~1.2x book with flat-to-slightly-negative near-term revisions, the risk/reward is balanced. Valuation scores 5.5. The move to BUY would require either a pullback toward book value or a re-acceleration in estimate revisions.

## Competitive Landscape

RGA's position is stable-to-improving. It is a scaled, highly-rated incumbent in an oligopolistic industry where relationships, underwriting reputation, and financial strength are the barriers to entry. The secular tailwinds are real and broad: pension plans continue de-risking (PRT), primary insurers continue offloading capital-intensive annuity back-books (asset-intensive), and RGA is a preferred counterparty given its ratings and discipline.

The competitive pressure is from PE-backed and Bermuda platforms (Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic) that have aggressively entered asset-intensive and PRT markets with lower cost of capital and looser reserve constructs. RGA competes partly by sponsoring its own sidecar vehicles (Ruby Re, $4.1B ceded recoverable) to recycle capital. The caution flag: Q1'26 traditional loss ratios drifted higher — U.S. Traditional 91.7% (from 91.0%) and Asia Pacific Traditional 85.5% (from 84.0%). These are small moves, but the core mortality book is where a negative surprise would hurt most. Competitive Position scores 6.8, reflecting a widening opportunity set offset by margin pressure at the low-cost-of-capital end of the market and the marginal loss-ratio deterioration.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Assumption/experience risk (the core risk).** Mortality, morbidity, longevity, and lapse deviations from pricing. Quarter-to-quarter volatility is inherent (see the –16.1% Q2'25 miss), and the Q1'26 traditional loss-ratio drift warrants monitoring.
2. **Rating-downgrade triggers.** Most derivative agreements carry termination events if senior debt falls below BBB+/Baa1 or financial strength below A-/A3. A downgrade would be more damaging for RGA than for a typical financial, potentially forcing collateral posting and permitting treaty recapture. This is a real tail risk, though not currently signaled.
3. **Acquisition/integration risk.** Management explicitly names the Equitable transaction as a risk factor. The block-deal growth strategy raises both upside (fee/spread income) and risk (integration, counterparty performance, assumed liabilities greater than expected).
4. **Investment/credit and rate risk.** A large, spread-sensitive portfolio; $8.4B of funds-withheld arrangements where RGA bears investment performance without controlling the assets; and a growing, illiquid alternatives/LP book.
5. **Reserve financing (Reg XXX/A-XXX/PBR).** Growing collateral needs reliant on captives, LOCs, and sidecar vehicles.
6. **Tax and regulatory.** Elevated 24.9% ETR; exposure to OECD Pillar II, Bermuda corporate income tax, and emerging AI-underwriting/data-privacy scrutiny.

The Q1'26 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors from the 2025 10-K. Risk Factors score 5.8: no single binary event is imminent, but the ratings-trigger sensitivity, block-integration exposure, and the fact that these risks live in the core book keep this below a clean pass.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 26.4% versus 1-year historical vol of 23.9%, a fair 2.5-point spread, with IV at the 39th percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew is a modestly elevated 4.4%, and the term structure is in backwardation (26.4% near-term > 23.4% long-term), which typically signals the market pricing near-term stress or an event. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±6.8%. Short interest is negligible at 2.0% of float (3 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic and no crowded bearish positioning. The options market is pricing a fair amount of near-term uncertainty (backwardation plus put demand) without conviction of a large move, consistent with a stock that has run into a fully-valued zone and faces a next earnings print. This aligns with our balanced fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($288)
Adjusted operating ROE holds mid-teens, net investment income continues compounding on higher reinvestment yields, the Financial Solutions engine executes additional Equitable-style blocks, and the $500M buyback plus dividend growth accelerate per-share compounding. Consensus targets re-rate as 2027 EPS approaches $29+, and the stock reaches the high analyst target of $288 (~19% upside). Traditional loss ratios normalize and the Q1 drift proves to be noise.

### Bear Case (~$185)
Traditional loss ratios continue drifting higher (the "most significant risk" per management), a claims-experience surprise hits the core book, or an integration/counterparty issue emerges on assumed blocks. A ratings scare triggers collateral/recapture concerns. In that scenario the stock derates toward ~$185 (~23% downside), a level consistent with prior stress lows and roughly book value.

### Base Case ($257)
Mid-single-digit operating growth, stable-to-modestly-rising net investment income, and continued capital return support ~9.5x forward EPS on ~$27.5–28 blended forward EPS, landing near the analyst mean around $257 (~6% upside). This is the most likely path and it is a modest-return, balanced-risk outcome rather than an asymmetric one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth case is intact — record operating earnings, rising NII, an accelerating buyback — but the 22% re-rating has captured most of the upside that justified prior BUYs, leaving a balanced setup.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward book value (~$203/share) or into the low-$210s, restoring a margin of safety with unchanged fundamentals.
- A resumption of positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year consensus rising >3% over 30 days) alongside a confirmed re-acceleration in adjusted operating income.
- Confirmation at Q2/Q3 that traditional loss ratios (U.S. and Asia Pacific) have stabilized or reverted, removing the core-book concern.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse claims-experience event, or two consecutive quarters of deteriorating traditional loss ratios.
- Any ratings-agency negative action or outlook change touching the BBB+/A- derivative-termination thresholds.
- A block-integration writedown or counterparty problem on assumed liabilities, or a dividend/buyback pullback signaling capital strain.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release (the ~$6.79 next-quarter consensus print) is the natural checkpoint, with the annual longevity/mortality assumption review (typically Q3/Q4) a key subsequent input. The elevated cadence of executive/director departures (a dense series of Item 5.02 filings across 2025–2026, plus the CEO transition to Tony Cheng) also warrants monitoring for organizational stability, and is the primary reason Management Quality sits at 5.8: capital-allocation and comp alignment (embedded-value performance metrics, a large CEO SAR grant, pay-actually-paid falling with TSR) are constructive, but there are no open-market insider purchases and the turnover is unusual.

## Conclusion

RGA is a well-run, structurally improving global life & health reinsurer with genuine secular tailwinds in longevity, pension risk transfer, and asset-intensive block reinsurance, a mid-teens operating ROE, and an accelerating capital-return program. The business quality supports ownership. The issue is entry price: at $241.79, up 22% since mid-2026 and near its 52-week high, the stock has re-rated past the point where the risk/reward is asymmetric. Estimate revisions have flattened, traditional loss ratios have drifted marginally, and the price now sits above the analyst low and near the mean target. We move to HOLD (growth_hold), overall score 6.3, consistent with the modestly declining trajectory of recent reports. We would become buyers again on a pullback toward book value or on confirmation that near-term earnings momentum is re-accelerating. Existing holders should stay long for the compounding and the yield; new capital should wait for a better entry.
