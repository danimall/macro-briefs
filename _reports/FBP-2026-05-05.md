## Executive Summary

First BanCorp is one of the largest banks based in Puerto Rico (~$19.1B in assets), operating a dominant consumer and commercial franchise on the island with a growing Florida commercial lending platform. The bank is generating strong returns — Q1 2026 ROAA of 1.89% and ROAE of 17.92% — driven by a structurally advantaged NIM (4.75% in Q1 2026) and a low-cost, sticky deposit base with below-peer deposit betas. The stock trades at approximately 10.8x forward earnings, which is inexpensive for a bank producing these returns with a clean balance sheet (CET1 16.93%, NPAs 0.57% of assets).

The primary investment thesis centers on a structural NIM tailwind: a large portfolio of MBS purchased during 2020-2022 at below-market yields is gradually rolling off and being reinvested into higher-yielding assets. While the yield gap has narrowed as the portfolio seasons (average MBS yield rose to approximately 2.98% in Q1 2026), a meaningful reinvestment spread remains that supports continued NIM expansion over the next 2-3 years. Combined with a 92% capital return payout and rising consensus estimates, the risk/reward is compelling at current levels. The principal risk is geographic concentration in Puerto Rico and the recent disruption of PROMESA oversight board governance.

## Company Overview

First BanCorp operates as the holding company for FirstBank Puerto Rico, offering commercial and consumer banking services across Puerto Rico (~77% of loans), the U.S. and British Virgin Islands (~4%), and Florida (~19%). The company consolidated its position through the 2020 acquisition of Banco Santander Puerto Rico, significantly expanding its deposit franchise to $16.7B in total deposits (as of December 31, 2025). Popular, Inc. remains the largest financial institution based in Puerto Rico by assets.

The business generates revenue primarily through net interest income ($221.0M in Q1 2026) supplemented by non-interest income from mortgage banking, insurance, and service fees. Six reportable segments include Mortgage Banking, Consumer (Retail) Banking, Commercial and Corporate Banking, Treasury and Investments, United States Operations, and Virgin Islands Operations.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability metrics are peer-leading:**
- Q1 2026 net income: $88.8M ($0.57 EPS), up 21% YoY
- Q3 2025 net income: $100.5M ($0.63 EPS), up 36% YoY (includes one-time $16.6M tax benefit)
- Adjusted Q3 2025 net income: $81.6M, up 11% YoY
- NIM: 4.75% in Q1 2026 (up from 4.57% in Q3 2025), driven by lower funding costs and asset yield improvement
- Efficiency ratio: 49.14% in Q1 2026 (improved from 50.2% in Q3 2025)
- 9-month 2025 OCF: $341.3M; annual 2025 operating cash flow: $448.6M (FCF approximately $437.5M after capex)

**Balance sheet is fortress-quality:**
- CET1: 16.93% (Q1 2026; well-capitalized threshold: 6.5%)
- Total loans: $13.1B (first time above $13B since 2010)
- NPAs/Total Assets: 0.57% (Q1 2026)
- Net charge-offs (annualized): 0.65% in Q1 2026; below prior-year levels but slightly higher sequentially (0.62% in Q3 2025, 0.63% in Q4 2025)
- ACL/Total Loans: 1.87% (adequate reserve coverage)
- Tangible book value/share: $12.45 as of Q1 2026 (up from $9.91 at year-end 2024)

**Capital return is aggressive and well-supported:**
- $200M buyback program approved October 2025
- Dividend raised 11% to $0.20/quarter starting Q1 2026 (approximately 3.3% yield at current price)
- Q1 2026 net payout ratio: 92% of earnings
- TruPS fully redeemed ($61.7M), eliminating high-cost subordinated funding
- For full-year 2025, the company repurchased $153.7M of common stock and paid $115.5M in dividends to common shareholders

The bank's average rate on total interest-bearing liabilities was 2.09% in Q1 2026, declining from 2.23% in Q1 2025, indicating an exceptionally sticky deposit franchise with low beta characteristics. Non-interest-bearing deposits at approximately $5.4B (approximately 32% of total deposits) provide a structural funding advantage.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is moderate but structurally supported:

1. **NIM expansion tailwind (2-3 year duration):** The MBS portfolio (approximately $3.6B in average balance in Q1 2026) carries a yield of approximately 2.98%, still meaningfully below current reinvestment opportunities in the 5%+ range. As these securities prepay and mature, the estimated 200+ bps reinvestment spread provides a multi-quarter earnings tailwind, though the magnitude has moderated as the portfolio's average yield has risen from earlier lows. An additional $1.4B in non-Treasury securities maturing within 12 months at approximately 1.66% yield amplifies the near-term opportunity.

2. **Loan growth:** Guidance revised to 3-4% for FY2025 (from mid-single-digit). Florida commercial platform growing deliberately ($204M in 9 months). Consumer portfolio mix shifting toward lower-risk secured lending.

3. **Earnings trajectory:** Q1 2026 EPS of $0.57 reportedly beat analyst consensus estimates. FY2026E consensus is estimated at $2.24 with FY2027E at approximately $2.39, per market data sources.

4. **Puerto Rico macro recovery:** $37.2B in FEMA disaster recovery obligations with billions remaining to be disbursed. First balanced budget certified in 2025. These flows support deposit growth and credit quality.

Growth is constrained by the relatively mature Puerto Rico market and deliberate shrinkage of unsecured consumer lending. Florida provides optionality but is unlikely to move the needle meaningfully in the near term at ~19% of the loan book.

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.19, FBP trades at:
- 10.8x forward P/E (FY2026E $2.24)
- 10.1x FY2027E ($2.39)
- ~1.94x tangible book value ($12.45 as of Q1 2026)
- ~3.3% dividend yield (growing)

Among mainland regional banks, a ~10-11x multiple on an 18%+ ROAE with rising NIM, minimal credit stress, and aggressive buybacks is below fair value. Banks producing comparable ROAE with structural NIM expansion typically trade at 11-13x forward earnings.

Analyst consensus target is reportedly $26.00 (range $25-$27). According to market sources, Raymond James upgraded to Strong Buy in April 2026 with a $27 target. The valuation discount likely reflects Puerto Rico geographic concentration risk, which I view as adequately compensated at current levels given the bank's capital strength and credit metrics.

**Fair value estimate:** 11.5x FY2026E EPS of $2.24 = $25.76. At 12x FY2027E EPS of $2.39 = $28.68 (bull case).

## Competitive Landscape

First BanCorp's competitive position is strong and improving:

1. **Strong market position in Puerto Rico:** Post-Santander acquisition, FBP is one of the largest banks on a consolidated island with limited new entrants. This supports pricing power on both loans and deposits.

2. **Deposit franchise quality:** Approximately 32% non-interest-bearing mix with low cost on interest-bearing deposits is a structural moat. Deposit betas have remained remarkably low through the rate cycle.

3. **Diversification strategy:** Florida expansion reduces single-market concentration without compromising credit quality (U.S. segment NPAs only $11.3M on approximately $2.5B in loans).

4. **NIM superiority:** 4.75% NIM in Q1 2026 is well above levels typically reported by mainland regional bank peers, reflecting both asset yield advantage and deposit cost advantage.

5. **Operating leverage:** Efficiency ratio at 49.1% and improving, indicating the bank can grow earnings without proportional cost growth.

The primary competitive risk is digital disruption, though Puerto Rico's banking market favors physical presence and relationship banking for the foreseeable future.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic concentration (HIGH):** 77% of loans in Puerto Rico. Economic recovery depends on continued federal disaster relief flows and fiscal stability. The Trump administration's removal of 5 of 7 PROMESA oversight board members creates genuine governance uncertainty.

**Interest rate sensitivity (MODERATE):** Asset-sensitive profile means a -200 bps shock reduces NII by ~8.4%. However, the securities portfolio rollover partially offsets rate declines by replacing low-yield assets, though Q1 2026 NII did decline modestly sequentially due in part to variable-rate loan repricing in a falling rate environment.

**CRE exposure (MODERATE):** $2.5B in commercial mortgages (approximately 19.5% of portfolio). CRE repricing risk is elevated nationally. Puerto Rico office market is smaller and more concentrated than mainland markets. $109.5M in office maturities within 12 months requires monitoring.

**Consumer credit normalization (LOW-MODERATE):** Auto loan originations declined 11.5% YoY in Q3 2025. Credit card portfolio shrinking. Net charge-offs remain below prior-year levels but have ticked slightly higher sequentially (0.62% to 0.65% from Q3 2025 to Q1 2026), suggesting stabilization rather than continued improvement.

**PROMESA governance disruption (MODERATE):** Five oversight board members terminated with no replacements announced. If fiscal discipline erodes, government deposit stability and the broader economic environment could deteriorate.

**Rising rate environment / stagflation (MODERATE):** In the current macro regime of elevated inflation and potential stagflation, bank earnings are supported by asset sensitivity in the near term, but credit quality could deteriorate if recession materializes. FBP's low beta (0.84) and fortress capital position provide substantial cushion.

**Short interest:** Reported at approximately 8.0% of float — elevated but not extreme. This likely reflects residual Puerto Rico risk perception rather than fundamental deterioration.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($27-29, +12% to +20%):**
- NIM expansion continues through 2026-2027 as securities roll off below reinvestment rates
- Loan growth reaccelerates to 5%+ as Florida platform scales
- Buyback reduces share count by 5-7% annually at current pace
- Puerto Rico economic recovery strengthens deposit growth
- Multiple re-rates to 12x forward earnings as Street recognizes structural earnings power
- Target: 12x $2.39 FY2027E = $28.68

**Bear Case ($18-20, -17% to -25%):**
- Puerto Rico recession driven by federal funding disruption after PROMESA board collapse
- CRE credit losses spike, particularly in Florida where rate sensitivity is highest
- Consumer credit deterioration accelerates beyond current provision levels
- Rate cuts compress NIM faster than securities reinvestment can offset
- Multiple compresses to 8x on perceived Puerto Rico risk
- Target: 8x $2.24 = $17.92

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $26.00 (11.6x FY2026E EPS of $2.24). This represents the reported consensus target and reflects modest multiple expansion from 10.8x to 11.6x, justified by rising estimates and consistent earnings delivery.

**Bull case target:** $28.68 (12x FY2027E EPS of $2.39). Achievable within 12-18 months if NIM expansion continues and buyback-driven EPS growth delivers.

**Bear case target:** $18.00 (-26% from current). Requires material credit deterioration or Puerto Rico-specific macro shock.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Primary catalysts: Q2 2026 earnings (July 2026), continued NIM expansion trajectory, buyback execution pace, Puerto Rico fiscal governance developments.

**Upside/downside from current ($24.19):** Base case +7.5%, bull case +18.6%, bear case -25.6%. Risk/reward is approximately 2.5:1 (bull/bear probability-weighted).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. NIM declines below 4.25% for two consecutive quarters (indicating rate sensitivity is dominating securities rollover benefit)
2. Net charge-offs exceed 1.0% annualized (indicating credit cycle turn)
3. PROMESA governance vacuum leads to measurable government deposit outflows (>$500M over 2 quarters)

## Conclusion

First BanCorp offers a rare combination among regional banks: peer-leading profitability (18%+ ROAE), structural earnings growth from securities reinvestment, fortress capitalization, and aggressive capital return — all at approximately 10.8x forward P/E. Geographic concentration in Puerto Rico is the primary risk but appears adequately compensated at current valuation. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $26.00 and bull case of $28.68.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The NIM reinvestment tailwind is smaller than originally framed (current MBS yield ~2.98% vs. 2.16% previously cited), reducing the estimated incremental NII uplift and narrowing upside to the base-case target.
