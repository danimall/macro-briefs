**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

McDonald's is the world's largest quick-service restaurant franchisor, running 45,699 restaurants across 100+ countries, roughly 95% of them franchised. The economics are those of a royalty-and-rent collection engine layered on a global real estate portfolio: franchised revenue ($16.5B in FY2025, ~62% of the total) carries margins near 90% of restaurant-level profit, and the company owns ~56% of the land and ~80% of the buildings at its consolidated-market restaurants, generating an inflation-protected, sales-linked cash stream. Operating margin was 46.1% in FY2025, operating cash flow exceeded $10.6B, and free cash flow was $7.2B. The business quality is not in question.

The debate is about growth quality and price. U.S. comparable sales in FY2025 rose 2.1% "benefiting from average check growth" — pricing, not traffic. Management has since pivoted hard to value (revamped Extra Value Menu, $3 items), which are margin-dilutive traffic-defense moves that flatter the top line only if they convert to guest counts. The Q1 2026 rebound (U.S. comps +3.9%, global +3.8%) flatters against a weak -3.6% year-ago base, and management flagged that April 2026 U.S. sales "turned slightly negative" as high gas prices pressured low-income diners. Consensus estimates are drifting lower across every window (current year -2.1% over 90 days, next year -1.6%), and the risk factors now explicitly cite weight-loss medications as a factor that could alter consumer demand for calorie-dense QSR — a genuinely new secular threat.

At $264.75 the stock trades at ~21.8x trailing EPS ($11.95) and ~20.5x the $12.92 current-year consensus, a discount to the restaurant peer median of ~26.8x but on mid-single-digit earnings growth with negative revision momentum. This is a defensive, income-anchored holding rather than a source of asymmetric upside. We rate MCD a HOLD on an income basis, supported by the ~2.8% dividend yield and 50 consecutive years of increases, with the recognition that the score trajectory has been declining (7.0 in February to 5.8 in June) as the growth and competitive-position theses have softened. Our analysis confirms that softening rather than reversing it.

## Company Overview

McDonald's operates through three segments. The U.S. (13,706 units) is the largest single-country profit center at $5.8B operating income. International Operated Markets (IOM — Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain, UK) has overtaken the U.S. as the largest segment at $6.4B operating income. International Developmental Licensed Markets & Corporate (IDL — 75+ countries, 99% franchised) includes equity-method stakes in China's Grand Foods Holding (48%) and McDonald's Japan (35%), which generate royalty and equity earnings without consolidation.

Roughly 68% of operating income is now earned outside the U.S., which diversifies away from U.S. consumer softness but raises FX and geopolitical exposure. FX was a meaningful ~$0.16 EPS tailwind in FY2025 that can reverse.

The strategic framework, "Accelerating the Arches," targets 50,000 restaurants by end-2027 (the fastest unit-growth period in company history), 250M 90-day active loyalty users, and $45B loyalty systemwide sales by 2027. The structural advantages are real: ~29,000 drive-thrus (>95% of U.S. units), delivery from ~90% of the system, and a loyalty program across 70 markets. Chicken (McCrispy) and beverages are the identified growth categories, and the "Best Burger" rollout is nearly complete across markets by end-2026.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is exceptional and stable. FY2025 operating margin was 46.1% (up from 45.2%), net income was $8.56B (+4%), and diluted EPS was $11.95 (+5%). Cash from operations rose 12% to $10.6B, free cash flow was $7.2B, and FCF conversion ran ~84% of net income, healthy for a capital-intensive-reinvestment year. Revenue grew 4% but only 2% in constant currency, confirming FX did much of the reported work.

The balance sheet requires interpretation. Standardized screens flag an Altman Z-Score of 0.21 ("distress zone") and negative stockholders' equity of -$1.79B (FY2025; -$1.29B at Q1 2026). This is a mechanical artifact of decades of buybacks: treasury stock of -$79.7B against $70.9B of retained earnings drives book equity negative. It is not financial distress. A business generating >$10B of operating cash flow at 46% operating margins does not carry solvency risk. The leverage figure that matters is $40.1B of long-term debt (total debt ~105% of capitalization), at a 4.0% weighted-average rate, 97% fixed. Interest expense of $1.58B rose 5% in 2025 and is guided up 4-6% in 2026 on higher refinancing rates. Operating cash flow equals ~26% of total debt, so servicing is comfortable, but the leverage is structural and rate-sensitive, a persistent earnings headwind rather than a threat. Credit ratings are BBB+ / Baa1, solid mid-tier investment grade.

Capital allocation is shifting. FY2025 returned $7.1B to shareholders ($5.1B dividends, $2.0B buybacks). Buybacks were dialed back roughly 35% from 2023 ($3.1B / 11.1M shares) to 2025 ($2.0B / 6.7M shares) while capex rose 21% to $3.4B, guided to $3.7-3.9B in 2026 to fund the unit-growth push. This is a deliberate bet on new restaurants over financial engineering. It is defensible while ROIC holds near 20%, but ROIC has drifted from 25.2% in 2023 to 21.8% in 2024 to 20.3% in 2025, worth monitoring as new units mature and the incremental capital dollar earns less than the base.

The dividend is a genuine anchor: 50 consecutive years of increases, a Q4 2025 raise of 5% to $1.86/quarter ($7.44 annualized), covered comfortably by $7.2B of FCF against ~$5.1B of dividends. At the current price the yield is ~2.8%, above the peer median of 2.0%.

## Growth Analysis

The core issue is that growth is real but modest and pricing-led. FY2025 global comps were +3.1%; U.S. +2.1% driven by check growth, not traffic. That is a pricing lever running out of room after the 2021-2024 price increases that ceded MCD's value-leader perception. Management's response — the revamped value menu and $3 items — is the correct competitive move but is margin-dilutive traffic defense, not accretive growth.

Q1 2026 showed a sharp U.S. rebound to +3.9% comps (versus -3.6% in Q1 2025), with IOM the standout at +3.9% comps and systemwide sales +16% reported / +6% constant currency. Two facts temper this. First, the year-ago base was weak, so a chunk of the "rebound" is arithmetic. Second, the CFO disclosed that April 2026 U.S. sales turned slightly negative amid ~$4.55/gal gas prices and low-income consumer anxiety, and the CEO said consumer spending "could be getting a little bit worse." This aligns with the broader mid-market consumer softening.

The estimate revision trend confirms negative earnings momentum: current-quarter EPS revised -0.6% (30d) / -2.8% (90d), next-quarter -0.7% / -3.9%, current-year -0.5% / -2.1%, next-year -0.7% / -1.6%. Every window is falling, and the 30-day figures are not decelerating meaningfully; this is a steady drift lower, not a bottoming. Under a disciplined reading, falling estimates on a stock trading near 21x earnings is a warning against paying up, not a buy signal.

The structural offsets are the 50,000-unit target, IOM strength, and loyalty scaling. These support mid-single-digit systemwide sales and EPS growth over time. What they do not support is an acceleration that would justify multiple expansion. The GLP-1 dynamic is the swing risk to the medium-term demand curve: the company itself now flags weight-loss medications as a factor that could alter consumer behavior in the informal-eating-out segment. The magnitude is unknowable but the direction is unfavorable for calorie-dense QSR, and it is not the kind of risk a standardized screen would capture.

## Valuation Assessment

At $264.75, MCD trades versus a restaurant peer median of 26.8x. On its face this looks like a discount. But the discount is largely earned: peers like CMG (28.6x) and TXRH (26.8x) carry meaningfully higher unit and comp growth, while MCD delivers mid-single-digit EPS growth with negative revision momentum. SBUX's 78x is a depressed-earnings artifact and not a clean comparable. On a growth-adjusted basis, ~20.5x forward for ~5-7% EPS growth implies a PEG well above 2.0, not cheap for the growth on offer.

The cycle-adjusted framework does not rescue the valuation here. This is not a trough-earnings inflection stock: there are no +30% estimate revisions, no revenue acceleration, no margin inflection. Earnings are near-peak, margins are already at 46%, and revisions are falling. Under those conditions trailing/forward P/E is the correct metric, and analyst targets (mean $324.71, median $320.00) reflect the pre-softening consensus that has yet to fully catch down to the deteriorating estimate trend. Treating those targets as floors would be a mistake; the target-lag principle applies to cheap, positively-revising inflection stocks, not to a mature name with falling estimates.

The valuation support is the ~2.8% dividend yield with a 50-year growth record and ~84% FCF coverage. That yield sets an approximate floor and is why the downside is bounded rather than severe. But absolute yield support is a defensive anchor, not a mispricing catalyst. We assign a mid valuation score: the stock is neither obviously cheap (the discount to peers is justified by lower growth) nor a value trap (the franchise quality and yield support prevent that), and there is no identified catalyst for the market to re-rate it higher. The equity-lessons caution against rewarding apparent cheapness without an independent catalyst applies directly.

## Competitive Landscape

McDonald's competes in the broad informal-eating-out segment against QSR peers (Burger King, Wendy's), fast-casual, convenience stores, grocery, and coffee. The current battleground is value, and here the evidence is favorable: multiple 2026 industry sources credit MCD with out-executing Wendy's and Burger King on value after deliberately rebuilding its value proposition, and QSR data cited MCD holding or gaining U.S. share in Q1 2026 while much of the industry struggled. The largest global drive-thru network, delivery from ~90% of the system, and a 70-market loyalty program are durable structural advantages.

The competitive-position trajectory has weakened from the earlier reads (this dimension fell from 9.0 to 6.5 across prior reports). The moat is intact — real estate ownership, franchise scale, brand — but the trajectory is now stable-to-slightly-improving on value execution rather than clearly widening. Two counterweights matter: the company's own 5-year cumulative TSR of 160 trails its peer group (173) and the S&P 500 (196), confirming MCD has been a relative laggard despite fundamental stability; and the GLP-1 secular threat is an industry-wide demand headwind that no operator can moat against. We settle on a moderate competitive-position score: a leader that is defending share well in the near term but without a clear structural share-gain story that the market is underpricing.

## Risk Assessment

The specific, non-generic risks that matter now:

1. **Low-income consumer weakness and exhausted pricing.** U.S. comps have been carried by check, not traffic. If the value push does not convert to guest counts, the pricing lever is largely spent and comps flatten. This is the central near-term risk.

2. **GLP-1 / weight-loss drug demand.** Newly emphasized in the risk factors as a factor that could alter IEO-segment demand. Structural, slow-moving, and unfavorable for calorie-dense QSR; magnitude unknown.

3. **Company-operated U.S. margin compression.** U.S. company-operated margins fell 14% in FY2025 and 25% in Q1 2026 on labor/commodity inflation and soft sales. Small in profit terms (franchised is ~90%) but a leading indicator of the cost pressure franchisees face, which ultimately drives the royalty base.

4. **Structural leverage and rate sensitivity.** ~$40B debt at 4.0%, refinancing into a higher-for-longer rate environment; interest expense guided +4-6% in 2026. A persistent earnings drag, not a solvency issue.

5. **Geopolitical / FX.** ~68% of operating income is international. Anti-American sentiment, boycotts, and FX reversal are live variables.

Governance is neutral-to-mildly-cautious. Insider activity is routine option-exercise-and-sell plus 10b5-1 plans with no open-market purchases: no bullish signal but nothing alarming. Pay-for-performance alignment is functioning (comp-actually-paid fell in the weak FY2024 and rose in the recovering FY2025). Early-2026 Item 5.02 filings appear to be routine succession/comp housekeeping rather than disruptive exits.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is signaling calm, not stress. Near-term ATM IV is 22.6% versus 18.0% historical vol, modestly rich but at the 2nd percentile within its own chain, indicating low absolute uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.3%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.53, and the term structure is flat (near 22.6% vs far 22.9%). IV has collapsed from 38.7% to 22.6% since mid-June as the post-earnings and macro-event uncertainty resolved. Short interest is negligible at 1.8% of float (2.3 days to cover). There is no options-market divergence from the fundamental view: the market prices MCD as a low-volatility defensive holding, consistent with the income-hold thesis. The 6-month skew of -3.5% is the only mild note of downside hedging, reasonable given the consumer-cliff overhang.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($320-340).** The value pivot converts to sustained traffic gains, U.S. comps re-accelerate durably above the pricing-only base, IOM strength continues, and the 50,000-unit target compounds systemwide sales. FX turns neutral-to-positive again, estimate revisions inflect upward, and the market re-rates the defensive quality back toward the peer median of ~26x, closer to the analyst mean target of $325. The 2.8% growing dividend adds to total return. This case requires the low-income consumer to stabilize and the pricing-lever concern to prove overstated.

**Bear Case ($225-240).** The low-income consumer weakens further, the value menu dilutes margin without lifting traffic, comps flatten or turn negative, and the GLP-1 demand drag begins to show in the numbers. Estimates continue drifting lower, ROIC keeps declining as new units mature, and the multiple compresses toward the high-teens on a mature-with-decelerating-growth profile. The dividend and franchise quality bound the downside to roughly -9% to -15% from current.

**Base Case ($278).** Modest mid-single-digit EPS growth continues, comps run in the low-single digits on a mix of some traffic recovery and residual pricing, and the multiple holds near ~21x. This implies ~$14.12 next-year EPS at ~20x = ~$282, tempered by the negative revision drift toward the low-$270s. Total return is roughly the ~2.8% dividend plus low-single-digit price appreciation, a defensive ~5-8% total-return holding, not an alpha source.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold), 12-month horizon.**

This is an income-and-quality hold, not an appreciation call. The thesis rests on the ~2.8% growing dividend, ~84% FCF coverage, 50-year raise record, and the durability of the franchise cash stream: defensive characteristics that justify ownership but do not offer asymmetric upside at ~21x earnings with falling estimates.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive U.S. traffic (not just check) growth, confirming the value pivot is converting rather than diluting.
- Estimate revisions turning positive across current-year and next-year windows.
- A pullback toward the high-$230s / low-$240s (near the analyst low target of $250 and toward a ~3.1%+ yield), which would restore a meaningful yield-plus-growth total-return case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- U.S. comps turning sustainably negative alongside continued company-operated margin compression, signaling the pricing lever is exhausted with no traffic offset.
- Clear evidence of GLP-1-driven demand erosion in reported IEO/segment traffic.
- A dividend growth pause or ROIC decline below ~18%, indicating the reinvestment bet is not earning its cost of capital.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the summer U.S. traffic/comp data, with particular attention to whether the April 2026 softening extended through the quarter. The consumer-spending trajectory into H2 2026 is the macro variable that will most affect the near-term call.

## Conclusion

McDonald's is one of the most durable cash-generating franchises in the market, and the balance-sheet "distress" flags from standardized screens are artifacts of buyback-driven negative equity, not solvency risk. The real issue is a maturing, pricing-led growth profile meeting a softening low-income consumer, an exhausted pricing lever, a new GLP-1 secular demand overhang, and a consensus that is quietly revising estimates lower across every window. At ~21x forward earnings for mid-single-digit growth, the valuation is fair-to-full, a justified discount to faster-growing peers rather than a mispricing, and there is no identified catalyst for re-rating.

The declining score trajectory (7.0 → 5.8 across prior reports) reflects the erosion of the growth and competitive-position theses, and our analysis confirms that erosion rather than reversing it: the Q1 2026 rebound flattered against a weak base and April already softened. Own MCD for defensive income and cash-flow durability, with the ~2.8% growing dividend as the anchor of a modest ~5-8% total-return expectation. We rate it **HOLD**, and would look to add only on a pullback toward the high-$230s / low-$240s or on confirmed U.S. traffic recovery.
