**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Figure Technology Solutions is a blockchain-native capital-markets fintech that IPO'd in September 2025. Originally a retail HELOC lender, the company has repositioned as a B2B marketplace for originating, funding, selling, and trading tokenized assets on the Provenance Blockchain. The business is profitable: Q1 2026 total net revenue was $167.0M (+97.6% YoY), operating income was $41.9M (25.1% margin), and adjusted EBITDA was $82.7M (~49.6% margin, +190% YoY). The "net income exceeds operating income" gap traces to a $6.9M discrete tax benefit from option exercises rather than crypto mark-to-market gains, so the operating profitability is real and cleanly disclosed.

The operating business works, with demonstrable unit-cost advantages (~$1,000 cost-to-originate versus an industry ~$11,500) translating into expanding margins. The question is how much credit the current ~$5.6B market cap (roughly mid-teens EV/revenue run-rate, ~55x trailing P/E) embeds for unproven digital-asset optionality: YLDS (an SEC-registered interest-bearing stablecoin), OPEN (on-chain equity issuance), and the broader tokenized-credit thesis. These are novel with early proof points (an OCC-chartered bank holding YLDS for treasury; a second OPEN issuer filing), but they live in an unsettled regulatory regime where reclassification could impair the highest-multiple part of the story.

The case against owning here rests on three concrete items beyond valuation. First, uniform insider selling across the entire C-suite and multiple directors with zero open-market buys, in a recently-IPO'd name trading below analyst targets. Second, concentration that contradicts the diversified-marketplace narrative: one counterparty (Toledo Bend) is ~13% of revenue, top-5 buyers are 52% of UPB sold, and four customers are 82% of trade receivables. Third, short-dated warehouse funding with several facilities maturing May–July 2026. Estimates are rising (+16% current-year over 90 days), a genuine positive, but the stock has already round-tripped from a high near $78 to a low near $25 and currently trades near $32, and quarterly results have been lumpy (a Q4 2025 miss drove a 26% one-day drop). The balance of evidence supports HOLD.

## Company Overview

Figure operates a single reportable segment organized around three verticals on the Provenance Blockchain:

1. **Debt & structured finance** — the core engine. A loan origination system plus Figure Connect, a whole-loan marketplace where 380+ third-party partners originate (mostly HELOCs, increasingly first-lien, DSCR, SMB, and residential transition loans) and a broad pool of buyers purchase. Figure earns ecosystem/technology fees, origination fees, gain-on-sale, and servicing fees, and services a $14.6B HELOC portfolio (191,545 loans).
2. **Equity & non-debt digital assets** — Figure Exchange (an ATS) and OPEN (On-Chain Public Equity Network), which lets companies issue equity natively on-chain. Figure issued its own Blockchain Common Stock (FGRS) as the foundational OPEN listing.
3. **Capital & financing markets** — YLDS (an SEC-registered, interest-bearing face-amount certificate) and Democratized Prime, a DeFi lending marketplace.

The competitive claim is structural: blockchain-based perfection, standardization, and automation drive a cost-to-originate near $1,000 versus an industry average management cites at ~$11,500. Figure reports being the leading U.S. HELOC originator and a dominant share of tokenized private credit. Substantially all revenue and assets are U.S.-based. Control sits with co-founder/Executive Chairman Mike Cagney via Class B shares (10 votes each). CEO is Michael Tannenbaum; CFO is Macrina Kgil.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 income statement (vs. Q1 2025):**
- Total net revenue: $167.0M, +97.6% YoY (from $84.5M)
- Operating income: $41.9M (25.1% margin) vs. $8.1M
- GAAP net income: $45.0M ($0.21 basic / $0.18 diluted EPS), aided by a $6.9M discrete tax benefit
- Adjusted EBITDA: $82.7M (+190% YoY), ~49.6% margin (up from ~33%)
- Net take rate: 3.8% (within the 3.5–4.0% guidance range), stable as mix shifts toward larger, lower-take-rate first-lien loans

**Revenue mix (Q1 2026):** Ecosystem & tech fees $47.3M (+203%, driven by Figure Connect volume +237%), gain on sale of loans $49.4M (+66%), origination fees $23.1M (+85%), interest income $19.4M, gain on servicing asset $12.9M, servicing fees $9.8M. The fastest-growing line (ecosystem/tech fees) is also the most capital-light and highest-quality.

**Volume:** Ecosystem volume $3.7B (+136%); consumer loan marketplace volume $2.9B (+113%); Figure Connect represents 56% of volume. Q2 2026 guidance of $3.8–4.1B consumer loan marketplace volume implies continued sequential growth, and management called April a record month.

**Balance sheet:** Total assets $2.73B; cash $1.46B; total equity $1.29B. Leverage is largely non-recourse warehouse/funding debt secured by loans held for sale; recourse debt is only ~$53M of $319M cost-basis debt. There is $1.59B of undrawn committed funding capacity, and the company is in compliance with all debt covenants. The $142M accumulated deficit (legacy losses) is shrinking.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow was –$37.8M in Q1, which is mechanical for an originate-to-sell model (loans held for sale flow through operating cash; $1.2B originations + $1.0B purchases against $2.0B sales). This is not a red flag in isolation, but it does mean reported earnings are not converting to cash on a quarterly basis, and funding capacity is a real constraint on growth.

**Operating leverage is showing up.** Operations and processing cost fell to 74bps of volume from 93bps while volume doubled; engineering output rose 25% on flat headcount; support-chat containment hit 70%. The unit-cost advantage is translating into margin, which is the crux of the bull case.

**Earnings track record is mixed.** Over three reported quarters: one beat (+103% in Q3 2025), two misses (–58.6% in Q4 2025, –4.5% in Q1 2026). The Q4 miss drove a 26% single-day decline. Visibility into the bottom line is lower than the headline growth implies, partly because results carry crypto fair-value swings (–$3.8M in "Other expense, net" in Q1, largely offset by matching collateral liabilities) and discrete tax items.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is strong and broad-based. Revenue nearly doubled YoY, the fastest-growing line is capital-light, partner count hit a record (80 added in Q1, including Flagstar Bank and the 7th-largest U.S. mortgage lender), and adjacent products are scaling fast: YLDS reached $598M and Democratized Prime matched offers $368M, both up ~80% QoQ. The Credibly partnership (May 2026) extends Democratized Prime into SMB working-capital lending, keeping the company on track to exceed its 2026 goal of 8–10 third-party originators.

Estimate revisions support the momentum: current-quarter EPS +11.7% over 30 days and +19.2% over 90 days; current-year +8.7%/+16.1%; next-year +6.1%/+12.1%. Revisions are accelerating (30-day moves are a meaningful fraction of 90-day moves), a genuine positive forward signal.

Two qualifications temper how much weight this carries. First, the revision magnitude (+16% current-year) is solid but not the +30% velocity that signals a clear trough-to-inflection re-rating, and the stock already trades at a premium multiple rather than a depressed one, so rising estimates alone do not establish asymmetric upside. Second, management frames much of the growth as market creation (the sub-$300K first-lien segment as "greenfield"), which is plausible given the cost advantage but is unproven at scale through a full credit cycle. The HELOC and first-lien engine is geographically concentrated (CA 24.7%, FL 11.9%) and rate-sensitive, so a housing or rate shock would compress origination volume and gain-on-sale economics simultaneously.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$32, FIGR carries a trailing P/E of ~55x and an EV/revenue run-rate in the mid-teens. On forward estimates the multiple compresses: current-year consensus EPS of $1.19 implies ~27x, and next-year $1.39 implies ~23x. On a PEG basis, ~23-27x forward against revenue growth near 100% and EBITDA growth near 190% looks reasonable in isolation, if the growth durability and margin profile hold.

The complication is that the multiple is not a clean bet on the lending business. A substantial portion of the value embeds optionality on YLDS, OPEN, and tokenized credit: products with real early traction but unsettled regulatory standing. Stripping those out and valuing the core debt/structured-finance engine as a high-growth specialty fintech marketplace would support a lower multiple than the market currently assigns. The premium is the digital-asset call option, and that option is binary on regulatory outcomes.

Peer benchmarking is difficult, as there is no clean comparable for a blockchain-native originate-to-sell marketplace with embedded tokenized-asset platforms. Against high-growth fintech marketplaces broadly, ~23-27x forward earnings for a business compounding revenue near 100% is defensible. Against the reality that two of the last three quarters missed and the stock has traveled from a high near $78 to a low near $25 to ~$32, the market is signaling that it does not yet trust the earnings trajectory or the optionality premium.

The analyst range is wide: low $33 (Mizuho trimmed), high $75, mean ~$54, median $55, with roughly 70% of coverage bullish. The mean implies meaningful upside, but the recent downward revision and the wide dispersion reflect genuine disagreement rather than a consensus floor. I treat the consensus as a wide band of uncertainty, not as an actionable target, because the swing factor (digital-asset regulatory characterization) is not something analysts can model with confidence. Net, valuation is fair-to-full, not asymmetric — a 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat claim is the unit-cost advantage sourced from blockchain-based loan perfection, standardization, and automation. If accurate and durable, this lets Figure serve segments (sub-$300K first-lien) that competitors skip on poor economics, which is market creation rather than pure share-taking. The 380+ partner network and the record 80 partner adds in Q1 (with marquee names like Flagstar) suggest a widening marketplace moat with network effects: more originators attract more buyers, which attracts more originators.

The trajectory is improving: share gains in HELOC, a dominant claimed position in tokenized private credit, and operating leverage that confirms the cost advantage is real. The OPEN platform attracting a second issuer (Open World Ltd.) is early external validation that the on-chain equity thesis is not purely self-referential.

The constraint on the competitive score is concentration. The marketplace is scaling faster than it is diversifying: Toledo Bend is ~13% of Q1 revenue, top-5 buyers are 51.6% of UPB sold, four customers are 81.6% of trade receivables, and top-5 originators supply ~78% of purchased loan UPB. A diversified network with genuine network effects should show declining concentration over time; the current readings indicate the moat is real but the network is still thin on both sides. The score reflects an improving but not-yet-fortress position.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration and counterparty risk.** The buy-side and sell-side concentration means the loss of one or two large counterparties would materially dent revenue. This is not yet priced as a tail risk because the marketplace narrative emphasizes breadth.

**Funding dependence.** Warehouse facilities are short-dated and covenant-laden. Interest expense rose 54% YoY. An event of default could accelerate. The company added Facility 7 ($250M) post-quarter and has $1.59B undrawn capacity, which mitigates near-term refinancing risk, but the model is structurally dependent on continuous access to funding.

**Regulatory characterization.** YLDS, Blockchain Common Stock, OPEN, and Democratized Prime sit in an evolving regulatory framework. Reclassification of these instruments or products could be materially adverse and would impair the highest-multiple part of the valuation. This is the principal swing factor and it is genuinely two-sided: favorable regulatory clarity would re-rate the stock upward.

**Loan repurchase obligations.** Contingent obligations to repurchase up to $2.0B UPB; $16.8M recorded obligation plus a $5.9M reserve. In a housing downturn with rising early-payment defaults, this contingency grows.

**Governance and structure.** Dual/triple-class equity, controlling shareholder Cagney, an extensive related-party web (Provenance Foundation/HASH, FTX-sourced Solana, Signum/Hastra, Reflow), and consolidated/deconsolidated VIEs make the financials harder to underwrite than a plain-vanilla lender. The HASH token valuation is internally-influenced and Level-3-like, though it is carried at only ~$11.9M, so the direct balance-sheet exposure is small. Internal control was deemed effective and filings are current.

**Insider selling.** Every named C-suite officer and multiple directors sold in March–May 2026 with zero open-market purchases: CEO option-exercise-and-sells plus a 132,861-share tax-withholding event, CFO recurring sales, Chief Capital Officer ~$3M+ in May, and directors converting Class B to Class A and liquidating the converted blocks. Much of this is explainable as post-IPO/post-lockup diversification and programmatic exercise-and-sell. Calibration caution applies: insider selling against rising estimates is often low-signal. But the complete absence of any buying, broad participation across the team, and the timing in a young, richly-valued name warrant weight. On May 14, 2026, the board added change-in-control severance/acceleration for key executives, which signals retention focus and possible M&A-readiness.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 109.5% against 103.7% one-year historical vol — rich, in the 87th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (109.5% near-term versus 81.1% long-term). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with an event-driven setup. Put/call skew is balanced (0.3%), put/call volume ratio is 0.65, and the nearest-expiry implied move is ±6.4%. There is no pronounced directional put-buying that would flag a specific downside catalyst, but the elevated near-term vol and backwardation indicate the market expects a large move soon and is uncertain about direction. For a name with a 26% single-day move in its history, this is a warning to size cautiously, not a directional tell. Short interest is moderate at 5.8% of float, 2.4 days to cover — uncrowded, so neither a squeeze setup nor evidence of a strong informed bear consensus.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$54-75).** Revenue continues compounding near 100%, the cost-to-originate advantage holds and widens the marketplace moat, partner count and volume keep growing, and the digital-asset stack (YLDS, OPEN, tokenized credit) gains regulatory clarity and institutional adoption. Operating leverage drives the adjusted EBITDA margin above 50%. Estimates continue rising and consensus targets prove to be a floor. On strong execution plus a regulatory tailwind, the stock re-rates toward the Bernstein bull case (~$56) and beyond (toward $75) as the tokenized-credit TAM (~$4T cited) is taken seriously.

**Bear case (~$20-22).** A Q2 or Q3 miss resets expectations, as the Q4 2025 print did. A housing or rate shock compresses HELOC/first-lien origination and gain-on-sale economics simultaneously, while early-payment-default repurchase obligations climb. An adverse regulatory characterization of YLDS or OPEN impairs the optionality premium embedded in the multiple. Concentration bites if a large buyer or originator pulls back. The stock de-rates toward a lender-only multiple, roughly 12-15x forward earnings on flat-to-down estimates, in the low-$20s.

The two cases are roughly symmetric around the current price, which is why this is a HOLD. The growth and operating leverage are real, but the premium multiple already prices a good outcome, and the binary regulatory swing factor plus genuine execution volatility cut both ways.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an `uncertain` HOLD; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. Reassessment is event-driven around quarterly earnings (next print expected ~August 2026) and any material regulatory development affecting digital-asset products.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive clean earnings beats with maintained or raised volume guidance, demonstrating the Q4 2025 miss was idiosyncratic and that the estimate revisions are converting to actual results.
- A favorable regulatory clarification for YLDS, OPEN, or tokenized credit that de-risks the optionality premium, combined with declining counterparty concentration (top-5 buyers below ~40% of UPB).
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $20s on unchanged fundamentals, which would shift forward valuation to ~17-18x and restore asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An earnings miss accompanied by a guidance cut, or net take rate falling below the 3.5% guidance floor, signaling competitive or mix pressure.
- A warehouse-facility non-renewal, covenant breach, or material rise in the loan-repurchase obligation reserve, indicating funding or credit stress.
- An adverse regulatory ruling reclassifying a core digital-asset product, or a step-up in insider selling that includes Class B liquidation by the controlling party beyond programmatic levels.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (~August 2026) is the primary checkpoint, with the short-dated warehouse maturities as an interim funding-access test.

## Conclusion

Figure is a profitable, fast-growing fintech with a demonstrable unit-cost advantage that is translating into expanding margins. The operating business is not a crypto mark-to-market illusion: the profitability is clean, the growth is broad-based, and estimates are rising. That is a better foundation than most recently-IPO'd names offer.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are specific. The ~$32 price embeds a premium that depends materially on unproven digital-asset optionality subject to binary regulatory outcomes; the company shows heavy buy-side, sell-side, and geographic concentration that contradicts the diversified-marketplace framing; funding is short-dated and rate-sensitive; two of the last three quarters missed, with one producing a 26% single-day drop; and every insider is selling with none buying. Against a wide analyst range with recently lowered targets, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. I rate FIGR a HOLD and would become more constructive on either a pullback toward the low-$20s with intact fundamentals or two consecutive clean beats that prove the earnings trajectory.
