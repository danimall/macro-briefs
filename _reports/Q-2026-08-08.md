## Executive Summary

Qnity Electronics is the former DuPont electronics materials business, separated on November 1, 2025 and public for nine months. The operating story is straightforward: more than 90% of FY2025 revenue came from consumable, unit-driven products, revenue grew 22% in Q2 FY2026 on 23% volume growth, and the acceleration is concentrated in Interconnect Solutions (+30%), where AI accelerator packaging drives demand for plating chemistries, dry-film resists, packaging dielectrics, polyimide film and thermal interface materials. Current-year consensus EPS is up 20.4% and next-year EPS up 22.1% over 90 days, with a further 10-11% added in the last 30 days. The company has beaten in all four quarters as a public entity by an average of approximately 15% (surprises of 9.2%, 27.7%, 14.8% and 8.7%), and raised FY2026 revenue guidance twice — from $5.225-5.375B in May to $5.55-5.65B in August, against a consensus of roughly $5.38B.

The stock has fallen from $168.98 on June 18 to $138.00, and sits 22.2% below its June 22 high of $177.28. The peer group de-rated harder over the same window (MKSI −31%, ENTG −33%, ONTO −29%, AEIS −31%, FORM −35% from respective 52-week highs), so this is predominantly sector multiple compression rather than a company-specific verdict. Falling price against rising estimates improves risk/reward, and it is the main reason our valuation score moves up from 4.5 to 5.3 after four consecutive HOLDs.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE is the gap between the growth headline and the cash and margin evidence. Consolidated Adjusted Operating EBITDA margin was 30.2% in Q2 FY2026 versus 30.3% a year earlier, and 30.7% in H1 versus 31.0% — six quarters with no consolidated margin expansion despite 20%+ growth, because Semiconductor Technologies margin is compressing (34.0% in Q2 from 35.1%) and standalone corporate cost has risen to −$40M in H1 from −$15M. Free cash flow fell roughly 50% to $164M in H1 as receivables consumed $152M and capex rose 39%, leaving a 1.1% FCF yield annualized and about 2.2% on a working-capital-normalized basis. The raised guidance implies H2 revenue of $2,806-2,906M — only +2% to +6% sequentially and +13.8% to +17.8% year over year, decelerating from Q2's +22% — with implied H2 adjusted EPS of $2.18-2.38 against roughly $2.22 in H1. At 30.7x the adjusted guidance midpoint and 18.7x FY2026E EV/EBITDA, the multiple assumes an acceleration the guidance does not contain.

## Company Overview

Qnity operates two reportable segments. **Semiconductor Technologies** generated $2,642M of FY2025 sales (55.6%) at a 35.8% Adjusted Operating EBITDA margin: CMP pads and slurries, post-CMP and advanced cleans chemistries, lithographic materials including EUV photoresists, Kalrez perfluoroelastomer seals and OLED display materials. **Interconnect Solutions** generated $2,112M (44.4%) at 25.5%: advanced circuit and packaging chemistries (copper pillar plating, redistribution layers, solder bump, packaging dielectrics), Laird thermal interface and EMI shielding, and Advanced Flex Technologies (Kapton polyimide film, Pyralux flexible copper-clad laminate).

Three structural features define the model. First, the consumables mix: revenue tracks wafer starts, layer counts and panel area rather than capital equipment order cycles, which makes the revenue profile structurally less cyclical than wafer fab equipment. Second, the content mechanism: management discloses that advanced nodes consume 3-5x more processing material per wafer than legacy nodes, which is the only disclosed offset to persistently negative pricing. Third, the moat rests on qualification lock-in rather than contracts: the 10-K states customer contracts contain no long-term purchase commitments, only non-binding forecasts, while average relationship length among the top 10 customers exceeds 30 years. Roughly 40 manufacturing sites, >10,000 employees (60% Asia Pacific), ~80% of products made and sold in the same region, ~7,000 patents and applications.

Customer concentration in FY2025: Samsung 11%, TSMC 8%, top 10 combined 34% (versus 34% in 2024 and 32% in 2023). China was approximately 33% of FY2025 sales; in Q2 FY2026, China was $478M of $1,429M (33.4%), of which ICS China was $283M — 41.3% of the segment that carries the growth thesis.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY2023 $4,035M, FY2024 $4,335M (+7%), FY2025 $4,754M (+10%), H1 FY2026 $2,744M (+20%), Q2 FY2026 $1,429M (+22%). Price/mix has been negative in every disclosed period: −2% in FY2024, −1% in FY2025, roughly −1% in Q2 FY2026. All growth, including the current AI-driven acceleration, is volume and content. This is normal for a qualified-consumables supplier absorbing annual customer price-downs, and it means operating leverage rather than pricing must carry margin.

**Segment divergence.** In Q2 FY2026, Semi revenue was $744M (+16%, volume +18%) at a 34.0% margin versus 35.1% a year earlier; ICS revenue was $685M (+30%, volume +28%) at 28.8% versus 26.0%. ICS margin has expanded roughly 990bps over three years (18.7% FY2023 → 28.6% H1 FY2026) while Semi has compressed from 36.1% in FY2024 to 34.0% in the latest quarter. The gap between segments has narrowed from ~1,740bps to ~520bps. Semi H1 capex more than doubled to $125M from $57M, which is consistent with capacity being added ahead of the revenue that will absorb it — the benign reading of the compression, though not yet visible in results.

**Consolidated margin is flat.** Q2 Adjusted Operating EBITDA $431M on $1,429M = 30.2% versus 30.3%; H1 $842M on $2,744M = 30.7% versus 31.0%. Segment sums reconcile ($450M less $19M corporate for Q2; $882M less $40M for H1). FY2025 reconciles independently to $1,441M, matching the company-selected performance measure in the April 2026 proxy. The adjusted margin has been ~30-31% for six quarters, with ICS expansion absorbed by Semi compression and a corporate line that went from −$15M to −$40M in H1 as DuPont allocations were replaced by standalone cost.

**GAAP earnings and the adjusted bridge.** H1 diluted EPS fell to $1.31 from $1.82; Q2 to $0.59 from $0.90, on 20% and 22% revenue growth. The bridge is almost entirely below the EBITDA line: interest expense $122M in H1 versus $0 in the carve-out predecessor; transformation, integration and other charges $70M versus $19M; indirect legacy costs −$45M versus zero; FX losses $30M on a −$1,189M notional book not designated as hedges; and effective tax rate 28.5% versus 20.8%. Two of these are permanent: roughly $236M of annual cash interest and a structural tax step-up that includes a disclosed limitation on interest deductibility.

The reported Q2 Adjusted Earnings of $250M ($1.19 per diluted share) sits $126M above GAAP net income available to common of $124M. Reconstructing from 10-Q line items — transformation $42M, indirect legacy costs $42M, intangible amortization $50M, FX $5M, less a $5M asset-sale gain — accounts for the gap and implies the adjusted definition adds back intangible amortization. That amortization is scheduled at $170M (2027), $144M (2028), $110M (2029) and $89M annually thereafter. It is a legitimate add-back convention, but it and the ~$90M annualized legacy PFAS cost are the substance of the difference between 30.7x adjusted and 42-53x GAAP. This reconstruction is derived from filed line items rather than read off the company's reconciliation table; treat the composition as an estimate.

**Cash flow is the weakest evidence.** H1 FY2026 operating cash flow $376M (−22%) against capex $212M (+39%) gives derived FCF of $164M, versus $327M a year earlier — a 6.0% FCF margin against 14.3%. Receivables consumed $152M (versus $28M) and inventories $77M (versus $41M), partly offset by $134M from payables. Annualizing H1 FCF gives ~$328M and a 1.1% yield on the market cap; adding back roughly $160M of incremental working-capital build versus the prior year gives ~$650M and a 2.2% yield. Both figures are sensitivities, not forecasts; the 10-K discloses that ICS peaks mid-year and moderates in Q1/Q4, so H2 working capital may release. On either basis this is a low-FCF-yield equity.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $961M ($662M held offshore), debt $4,020M ($1,000M secured notes at 5.750% due 2032, $750M unsecured at 6.250% due 2033, $2,350M term loan B at 5.698% due 2032), undrawn $1.25B revolver to October 2030, only $23M of principal due within twelve months against $236M of interest. Gross leverage 2.79x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA; net leverage 2.12x, falling to ~1.82x on the H1 annualized run rate of $1,684M; interest coverage roughly 6.1x. Long-term debt fair value of $4,107M against ~$4.1B face means the credit trades near par, and on July 1, 2026 lenders repriced the term loan from SOFR+200 to SOFR+175, cutting cash interest by about $6M annually. Screens flagging distress on this name (an Altman Z near 1.79) mis-handle two inputs — spin-off fresh-start retained earnings and book rather than market equity — and are not informative here.

The two balance-sheet items that do matter: goodwill of $7,518M plus $1,015M of other intangibles equals 59.5% of $14,331M total assets, with goodwill alone at 103.5% of $7,261M shareholders' equity and derived tangible book value of negative $1,272M; and retained earnings of only $260M reflect the fresh start. Impairment risk is remote while EBITDA compounds near 20%, but the equity has no tangible asset support.

## Growth Analysis

The demand mechanism is legible in the segment data. AI and HPC accelerators require larger packages, more redistribution layers, higher-layer-count and lower-loss PCBs, organic interposers and more aggressive thermal management. Each maps directly to ICS product lines, which is why ICS grew 30% with 280bps of margin expansion while Semi grew 16% with compression. Third-party validation exists on the customer side: ASE Technology Holding, a major OSAT, named Qnity its 2025 Best Supplier in Advanced Packaging Materials in May 2026, and the June 2026 organic-interposer product launch (including Intervia 8540HSP) is on-thesis product cadence.

One relationship is frequently mischaracterized and should carry no revenue in a model. The March 18, 2026 collaboration announcement has Qnity *using* NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Nano, ALCHEMI BMD NIM, LAMMPS Kokkos and CUDA-X-accelerated Abaqus for internal materials modeling and simulation. Qnity is the adopter of NVIDIA tooling for its own R&D productivity; no supply agreement, revenue commitment or NVIDIA purchase of Qnity products is disclosed.

**The estimate revision signal is present but below the strongest threshold.** Current-year consensus moved from $3.81 to $4.59 (+20.4% in 90 days, +10.9% in 30 days); next year from $4.39 to $5.36 (+22.1% and +10.1%). The 30-day pace relative to the 90-day trend, alongside revenue growth of 20%+, is favorable. It falls short of the >30%/90-day signal we treat as a strong standalone buy trigger, and forward P/E of 25.7x on next-year consensus against ~17% consensus earnings growth (next-year $5.36 versus current-year $4.59) gives a PEG near 1.5 — above the level at which rising estimates alone justify paying up.

**Guidance embeds deceleration.** FY2026 revenue guidance of $5.55-5.65B against FY2025's $4,754M is +16.7% to +18.8%. With H1 actual at $2,744M, implied H2 is $2,806-2,906M, +2% to +6% sequentially and +13.8% to +17.8% against derived H2 FY2025 of $2,466M. On earnings, reconstructing Q1 adjusted EPS at roughly $1.03 plus reported Q2 of $1.19 gives H1 near $2.22, so the $4.40-4.60 full-year range implies H2 of $2.18-2.38 — flat at the midpoint. Two readings compete: the company has beaten and raised twice, so H2 may be conservative; alternatively, H2 FY2025 already contained the early AI ramp and ICS seasonality is genuinely weaker in Q4 (FY2025 Q4 revenue of $1,190M declined sequentially from Q3's $1,276M). Either way, the near-term catalyst path does not offer sequential earnings growth.

The transformation plan launched in February 2026 targets roughly $100M of run-rate Adjusted Operating EBITDA benefit by end-2028 — about 6% of the current run rate. H1 charges of $70M (IT independence $48M, transformation initiatives $10M, integration $6M, severance $2M, asset-related $4M) mean the cash cost is front-loaded against an unproven benefit.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors in the 10-K are Entegris, Merck KGaA, Resonac, Element Solutions and MKS Instruments. Few of them span both front-end fab consumables and back-end interconnect, packaging and flex materials, and the two-segment structure supports management's breadth claim.

The trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly improving. ICS is clearly gaining: 990bps of margin expansion over three years, 30% growth, a customer-side supplier award from a top OSAT, and product launches aimed at organic interposers used in large AI accelerator packages. Semi is the harder read — volume up 18% but margin down 110bps year over year and 120bps sequentially, with capex doubling. The benign explanation is start-up and under-absorption cost ahead of capacity fill; the less benign one is competitive pricing in CMP and cleans against Entegris and Merck. Filings do not decompose price from mix, so we cannot distinguish them. Leadership of that segment also appears new: Katherine Dei Cas filed a first Form 4 as President, Semiconductor in August 2026 holding 2,937 shares.

Two structural qualifications matter. Customer concentration has been flat at 34% for two years; the revenue mix shift toward ICS (44.4% of FY2025 to 47.9% of Q2 FY2026) changes segment composition and says nothing about reduced dependence on individual buyers. And under the IP Cross-License Agreement, DuPont retains sole right to file, prosecute, maintain, defend and enforce patents and know-how licensed to Qnity; if DuPont declines to enforce, Qnity states it may be unable to prevent competitors from selling competitive products. For a company whose stated asset base is ~7,000 patents, enforcement discretion resting with the former parent weakens the legal component of the moat, leaving qualification switching costs as the operative barrier.

## Risk Assessment

**44% of DuPont's legacy PFAS liabilities, funded without control.** The Applicable Qnity Percentage of 44%, fixed December 2, 2025, applies to DuPont's Legacy Liabilities, MOU funding obligations, legacy PFAS liabilities and liabilities of divested DuPont businesses, and the 10-K describes the amounts as uncapped. Accrued at June 30, 2026: MOU $88M, New Jersey $66M plus $4M accretion, North Carolina $43M, other $52M — $253M total, up from $190M at December 31, 2025. Reasonably possible additional exposure is approximately $100M above accrual, with the company stating that further costs may be material and cannot be reasonably estimated. Qnity has irrevocably granted DuPont sole and exclusive authority to litigate and settle these matters, and DuPont may direct Qnity to remit to third parties. A single share of Series A Preferred held by a noncharitable purpose trust, whose unanimous consent is required to amend the relevant charter provisions and which is prohibited from voting in favor of such amendment, makes the arrangement effectively permanent. The structure most plausibly reflects tax and legal certainty for the spin architecture rather than any intent to disadvantage Qnity holders, and $353M of accrued-plus-reasonably-possible exposure is about 1.2% of market cap against a 25-year New Jersey payment schedule and $323M of offsetting indemnification assets due from DuPont. The unquantifiable tail, and the second-order tax effect already visible (the non-deductible North Carolina accrual helped push the Q2 effective rate to 31.7% from 22.4%), are the live issues.

**China.** 33.4% of Q2 revenue, and 41.3% of ICS — concentration is worst in the segment carrying the growth thesis. Local-for-local manufacturing addresses tariffs but not licensing prohibitions. An extension of export controls into advanced packaging or high-layer-count PCB materials would land disproportionately on ICS.

**No contractual demand backing.** No backlog or remaining performance obligation is disclosed; deferred revenue is $3M current and $44M noncurrent and relates to a specific capacity project expected to begin commercial production in 2027, not to demand. The AI ramp exists only in shipments.

**Control environment.** The FY2026 10-K will carry Qnity's first SOX 404 management assessment and auditor attestation, executed while the company exits Transition Services Agreements and spends toward ~$180M of standalone IT costs ($48M in H1 alone), with Michael G. Goss signing as Interim CFO in the combined roles of Principal Financial Officer and Principal Accounting Officer — a configuration in place since at least February 2026. Management concluded disclosure controls were effective as of June 30, 2026 with no ICFR changes in the quarter. The January 16, 2026 8-K carrying Item 5.02 alongside earnings is the presumed CFO change, but the filing text was not retrievable; the departing officer's identity, the reason and the status of any search are unconfirmed, and no adverse inference about finance-function quality is supported.

**Structural constraints.** Tax Matters Agreement restrictions on acquisitions, mergers, asset sales and equity issuances run to approximately November 1, 2027 under Section 355(e), with potential indemnification of DuPont for corporate-level tax even absent Qnity participation. The Corteva Letter Agreement imposes a $1.1B Minimum EBITDA condition and liability-assignment requirements on transfers. There is no takeover option value in this equity before late 2027, and a value-unlocking separation of Semi from China-heavy ICS is effectively precluded.

**Other.** Negative tangible book value of $1,272M against goodwill at 103.5% of equity; an escalating effective tax rate (15.7% → 19.6% → 24.2% → 28.5%); $410M of nonconsolidated affiliate investments generating $47M of FY2025 equity earnings (~5% of pre-tax income) that Qnity does not control, including a $79M related-party note payable to HD MicroSystems; and $285M of noncontrolling interests contributing $23M of H1 net income (7.7% of consolidated).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $185 (+34.1%)

Content-per-wafer growth at advanced nodes (3-5x more processing material) plus rising package complexity sustains 15-20% revenue growth into FY2027, taking revenue to roughly $6.3-6.5B. Semi margin compression proves to be start-up cost on capacity that doubled in H1 capex, and reverts toward 36% as volume absorbs it; ICS holds near 29%; the $100M transformation benefit begins landing in 2027. Consolidated adjusted margin expands to roughly 32%, producing FY2027 EBITDA near $2.0B and adjusted EPS near $6.00 as interest expense falls on the repriced term loan and the Pillar Two "side-by-side" framework relieves part of the tax step-up. At 31x that is $186; on 20x EV/EBITDA, $171 to $185 depending on net debt paydown. Working capital normalizes and FCF conversion recovers toward 60-70% of adjusted earnings, giving a 3%+ yield. Estimate revisions running 10% per month continue, and the covering analysts — whose low target is already $150 — move targets higher.

### Bear Case — $98 (−29.0%)

H2 confirms the deceleration embedded in guidance rather than the conservatism bulls assume: revenue growth slows to the low teens, Q3 adjusted EPS lands flat sequentially, and the market re-rates a 30x multiple toward 20x on a trimmed FY2027 estimate near $4.90, giving $98. Semi margin compression proves to be pricing rather than absorption, and the persistent −1% to −2% price/mix becomes −3% as regional subsidized competitors qualify in. Chinese export-control expansion touches advanced packaging materials, hitting 41% of ICS revenue. FCF stays near $300-400M, leaving a sub-1.5% yield with no valuation floor and negative tangible book. A first-year SOX 404 attestation under an interim CFO produces a control deficiency, or the legacy indemnity accrual steps up again as it did by 33% in six months. The sector context is unhelpful: peers are already 29-35% off highs and semiconductor positioning remains crowded after a large year-to-date run.

### Base Case — $155 (+12.3%)

FY2027 revenue of approximately $6.3B at a 30.5-31% adjusted margin gives EBITDA near $1.94B; at 18x EV/EBITDA that is $34.9B enterprise value, less $3.06B net debt, or $152 per share. Cross-checked on earnings: FY2027 adjusted EPS of $5.75-5.85 (consensus $5.36 revised modestly higher on the current pace) at 26-27x gives $150-158. We use $155, equivalent to 28.9x current next-year consensus. Probability-weighting 50% base, 25% bull and 25% bear gives roughly $149, or +8% from $138 — positive but thin against 57.7% realized volatility and a 29% bear case.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive 12-month growth thesis with identifiable catalysts, and we expect modest appreciation. Existing holders should stay. New capital does not have adequate margin of safety at 18.7x forward EV/EBITDA with a 1-2% FCF yield and six quarters of flat consolidated margin.

**Base case target:** $155 (+12.3%) — 18x FY2027E EBITDA of ~$1.94B less $3.06B net debt, cross-checked at 26-27x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $5.75-5.85.

## Conclusion

Qnity has delivered on the operating metrics that matter for a consumables supplier levered to AI packaging: 22% revenue growth on 23% volume, four consecutive beats averaging approximately 15%, two guidance raises in two quarters, and consensus EPS up 20-22% in 90 days. The balance sheet is sound at roughly 1.8x net leverage with 6.1x coverage, an undrawn $1.25B revolver, $23M of near-term maturities, and lenders who tightened the term loan spread nine months after issuance.

The valuation does not yet reward new capital. Six quarters of flat consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin at approximately 30% undercut the operating-leverage premise behind an 18.7x forward EV/EBITDA multiple; free cash flow halved to $164M in H1 on a 1.1-2.2% yield; and the raised FY2026 guidance implies H2 revenue growth decelerating to +14-18% with flat sequential adjusted EPS. Layered on that is a 44% share of uncapped DuPont legacy PFAS liabilities whose litigation and settlement Qnity has irrevocably surrendered to DuPont, a structure a purpose trust exists specifically to make permanent, 33% China revenue concentrated at 41% within the fastest-growing segment, and a first SOX 404 attestation to be executed under an interim CFO holding both the PFO and PAO roles.

The 18% decline since mid-June against 10-11% higher estimates is a real improvement in entry arithmetic, and it moves the valuation score up after four flat HOLDs. It compresses the multiple from roughly 41x to 30x current-year earnings without making the stock cheap. Probability-weighted expected return of approximately +8% against a −29% bear case, with the next print offering no sequential earnings growth, supports maintaining exposure rather than adding to it. We would become buyers at $118-125, or sooner on evidence that consolidated margin is finally expanding.
