## Executive Summary

Sarepta is a single-indication genetic medicine company whose equity value now hinges almost entirely on one dated regulatory event. On February 28, 2027 the FDA has a target action date on supplemental applications to convert AMONDYS 45 (casimersen) and VYONDYS 53 (golodirsen) from accelerated to traditional approval. The company's own June 30, 2026 press release states that in the Phase 3 ESSENCE confirmatory study the primary endpoint "was not met," and the filings rest on post-hoc analyses adjusting for disease-progression heterogeneity and COVID-19, a week-96 non-primary ascend measure, and largely conference-presented real-world evidence. Sarepta's own risk language contemplates FDA "directives to remove these products from the market or alter labels." The phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomer (PMO) franchise generated $230.6M in Q2 2026, roughly $922M annualized, which is approximately 70% of guided 2026 product revenue and — at a derived 72-76% product gross margin — effectively all of the company's gross profit. Sarepta does not break out revenue among EXONDYS 51 (not covered by this review), AMONDYS 45, and VYONDYS 53, so the precise amount at risk on that date cannot be determined from disclosure.

The second leg of the business has deteriorated and then stopped moving. ELEVIDYS fell from $281.9M in Q2 2025 to $102.0M in Q1 2026 and $98.1M in Q2 2026 — a 3.8% sequential decline two quarters into an intensive commercial re-launch conducted with a boxed warning for acute liver failure and with the non-ambulatory population removed from the indication. Management narrowed FY2026 product revenue guidance to $1.2-1.3B from $1.2-1.4B and said second-half revenue would be modestly below the first half, implying H2 of $541-641M against H1 of $659.2M. The one visible ELEVIDYS re-expansion catalyst, Cohort 8 of ENDEAVOR testing sirolimus-enhanced immunosuppression, slipped from year-end 2026 to Q1 2027 because investigators are dosing sequentially rather than in parallel. That delay also pushes the SRP-9003 BLA further right, since the FDA stated in December 2025 that it requires sirolimus data before accepting that filing.

We move to REDUCE from HOLD. The change is not driven by price — the stock is essentially unchanged since our late-July review — but by three specific developments in the Q2 disclosure: guidance narrowed to the lower half of the prior range, the ELEVIDYS catalyst slipped two quarters, and the $75M collaboration revenue raise is attributable to Roche contract manufacturing that carried a negative gross margin in H1 2026 ($85.5M of revenue against $97.9M of Roche product cost of sales). Our probability-weighted expected value is $15.50-16.50 against a $16.78 price, with a bear case of roughly $6 (-64%) and a bull case of $27 (+61%). The distribution is bimodal, the modal favorable branch is seven months away, and the near-term catalyst set (H2 revenue step-down, $266.3M of H2 purchase obligations, inventory realizability, a pending securities-class-action dismissal ruling, and Dyne's competing exon-51 filing) skews negative. A 32.4% short interest with 7.1 days to cover makes this a stock to be underweight rather than short.

## Company Overview

Sarepta reports as a single segment and sells two product families addressing Duchenne muscular dystrophy.

The PMO exon-skipping franchise comprises EXONDYS 51 (2016), VYONDYS 53 (2019), and AMONDYS 45 (2021). All three are weekly IV infusions built on proprietary morpholino chemistry, and all three remain on accelerated approval, meaning continued marketing is contingent on verifying clinical benefit. More than 1,800 patients have been treated across the portfolio over more than a decade.

ELEVIDYS (delandistrogene moxeparvovec-rokl) is a one-time AAVrh74 gene therapy that received accelerated approval in June 2023, traditional approval for ambulatory patients aged four and above in June 2024, and accelerated approval for non-ambulatory patients in June 2024 — the last of which was withdrawn from the Indication and Usage section in November 2025 alongside a boxed warning. Roche commercializes ex-US and purchases supply from Sarepta.

The third leg is an early-stage siRNA platform licensed from Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals effective February 2025, covering DUX4/FSHD (SRP-1001), DM1 (SRP-1003), Huntington's (SRP-1005), and additional targets. Sarepta has no internal GMP manufacturing, depending on Catalent for ELEVIDYS and SRP-9003 and Aldevron for plasmid.

New CEO Michael Severino, M.D., took office July 28, 2026, five business days before leading the Q2 call. Douglas Ingram retired as CEO and left the board the same day.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue has inflected down for the first time

Net product revenue by fiscal year, from the pay-versus-performance table in the April 2026 proxy: $612.4M (2021), $843.8M (2022), $1,144.9M (2023), $1,788.0M (2024), $1,864.3M (2025). Guidance of $1.2-1.3B for 2026 implies a 30-36% decline — the first contraction in the company's commercial history.

The composition matters more than the headline:

| Product revenue | Q2 2025 | Q1 2026 (derived) | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| PMO | $231.3M | $228.5M | $230.6M |
| ELEVIDYS | $281.9M | $102.0M | $98.1M |
| **Total** | **$513.1M** | **$330.5M** | **$328.7M** |

PMO revenue is flat year over year (-0.3%), splitting US $198.6M / rest-of-world $32.0M in Q2. ELEVIDYS is down 65% year over year and, more importantly, down sequentially. The commercial re-launch has not produced a revenue inflection through two quarters.

### H1 GAAP profitability is an accounting artifact

H1 2026 reported total revenue of $1,132.1M, operating income of $371.7M, net income of $326.1M, and diluted EPS of $2.99. Of the $365.0M in H1 collaboration revenue, $325.0M was recognized when Roche declined an option in February 2026, accelerating recognition of previously deferred revenue with no cash inflow in the period. The cash flow statement mirrors this: deferred revenue was a $307.0M use of operating cash, and the balance fell from $527.3M to $220.3M.

Excluding that item, H1 revenue was $807.1M and operating income $46.7M (5.8% margin); adding back the $39.0M litigation contingency charge gives $85.7M (10.6%). Q2 is the cleaner quarter because collaboration revenue was zero: revenue $401.3M, operating income $13.3M, and a GAAP net loss of $(4.9)M or $(0.05) per share. Excluding the litigation charge, Q2 operating income was $52.3M (13.0%), which annualizes to roughly $209M before $45.5M of annual cash interest.

Two consequences follow. First, the reported Q1 non-GAAP EPS of $3.16 against a $0.90 consensus and the current-year consensus EPS of $4.34 are dominated by this non-cash event and should not be capitalized. The supplied data field showing forward P/E equal to trailing P/E at -12.75 is internally inconsistent with a positive $4.34 current-year consensus and we do not use either figure. Second, the four-quarter surprise record (three beats, one miss, including a 184.7% beat in Q1) overstates forward visibility because the beats compare non-GAAP results to non-GAAP estimates on a base inflated by the Roche recognition.

### Cash flow was near breakeven, funded by a non-repeatable working-capital release

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $(5.6)M versus $(322.1)M a year earlier, with capex of $2.6M against $75.4M, giving free cash flow of roughly $(8.2)M. Total cash, investments, and restricted cash ended H1 at $945.0M versus $953.8M at year-end 2025 — down only $8.8M despite absorbing approximately $250M of Arrowhead payments (the $200.0M DM1 milestone accrued at year-end plus the $50.0M annual installment).

That is a genuine execution result, and the restructuring is visible: Q2 R&D of $91.3M versus $204.4M (-55%), SG&A $107.6M versus $137.9M (-22%), R&D manufacturing expense $1.8M versus $80.7M. But roughly $158.5M of the funding came from liquidating working capital — manufacturing deposits +$69.3M, other assets +$67.8M, receivables +$21.4M. Current manufacturing deposits are down to $48.5M from $113.5M, so that lever is substantially drawn. Remaining Roche deferred revenue of $220.3M against $527.3M means the capacity to book further non-cash collaboration revenue has shrunk by roughly 58%.

### Capital structure: manageable near term, thinner than it looks

At June 30, 2026: 1.25% converts due September 15, 2027 with $158.6M principal (fair value $149.9M, ~94.5% of par); 4.875% converts due September 1, 2030 with $893.4M principal, $689.8M carrying value, $760.3M fair value (~85% of par). Total principal $1,052.0M against $945.0M of liquidity gives net debt of $107.0M. The $600M secured revolver was drawn and repaid $425.0M during H1 and ended at zero, in compliance with a 3.5:1.0 maximum secured net leverage and 2.5:1.0 minimum interest coverage tested quarterly.

Two mechanical points. The $203.6M unamortized discount on the 2030 Notes will accrete as roughly $40-50M per year of non-cash interest through 2030 (H1 2026: $19.0M), structurally depressing GAAP EPS relative to cash economics. And the 85%-of-par quote on the 2030 Notes reflects both credit concern and the collapse in conversion value after an 87% equity drawdown; it is not a clean credit spread read, but it is not encouraging either.

### Inventory is the largest unresolved item

Total inventory was $1,130.6M at June 30 (raw materials $127.8M, work-in-process $946.7M, finished goods $56.2M), up $31.3M during H1 despite ELEVIDYS revenue falling 70% year over year. H1 write-downs were $11.6M against $165.3M of reserves and write-offs in FY2025. Own-product cost of sales, excluding the $97.9M of Roche product cost, annualizes to approximately $283.8M, implying roughly four years of coverage. Non-cancelable manufacturing commitments total $428.0M, with $266.3M falling in H2 2026. Inventory per share of $10.71 exceeds cash and investments per share of $8.95.

The offsetting reading deserves weight: the 10-Q discloses operating cycles of two years for PMO and roughly 3.5 years for ELEVIDYS, so long-dated inventory is structurally normal here, the commitments explicitly include ELEVIDYS supply for Roche ex-US launches, and some inventory converts into contract manufacturing revenue. We cannot distinguish deliberate supply security from demand misjudgment from the disclosure. What is not ambiguous is the cash requirement: $266.3M of contractual purchases in the next six months against $945.0M of liquidity and roughly breakeven operating cash flow.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.17 computed off FY2025 is a poor solvency instrument here — it is calibrated on manufacturers and the implementation substitutes book equity for retained earnings, which is more generous than the classical formulation given a $(4,877.0)M accumulated deficit. Treat it as confirming that the balance sheet has little cushion for another shock, not as a solvency verdict. Near-term solvency is not the question; debt service in 2028-2030 against a shrinking revenue base is.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term growth. The 2026 guide implies a 30-36% revenue decline; H2 is guided below H1; ELEVIDYS has flattened at roughly a $392M annualized run-rate; PMO is flat; and the collaboration revenue raise of $75M carries the negative-margin Roche contract manufacturing profile.

Estimate revisions are consistent with that. Current-year consensus EPS has been cut 10.5% over 30 days and 10.1% over 90 days to $4.34; next-year sits at $2.87, below the current year. The +30.8% thirty-day revision to next-quarter EPS ($0.45 to $0.58) is on a small base and is not the inflection pattern that would justify treating revision velocity as a positive signal — the annual figures, which carry the information, are being cut.

The forward optionality is real but unresolved and mostly beyond twelve months:

- **ELEVIDYS recovery.** Management attributes the flat revenue to a roughly six-month lag from patient enrollment form to infusion and points to increased enrollment-form activity from returning and new sites, expecting benefit in 2027. That is mechanically plausible for a one-time therapy requiring site scheduling and payer authorization. It is also unfalsifiable until Q1-Q2 2027.
- **Cohort 8 of ENDEAVOR**, now Q1 2027, gates any path to restoring the non-ambulatory indication and to FDA acceptance of the SRP-9003 BLA.
- **siRNA readouts in H2 2026** for SRP-1001 (FSHD) and SRP-1003 (DM1) were explicitly pre-framed by management as validating the biological chain from muscle exposure to DUX4 knockdown, not functional benefit, given slow disease progression and six-month follow-up. Sarepta has committed or paid roughly $1,175M in cash to Arrowhead since February 2025 — comparable to two-thirds of the current market capitalization — with $200.0M of installments still owed across 2027-2030. The first data cannot resolve whether that capital was well spent.

Severino explicitly rejected separating the DMD and siRNA businesses, arguing DMD revenue funds the siRNA programs without external financing. That preserves optionality at the cost of continuing to route declining PMO cash flow into preclinical and Phase 1 assets.

Peer medians in the biotech comparable set show a P/E of 14.2, but three of five peers carry negative earnings and the set is heterogeneous; the multiple is not a usable anchor. On price to guided sales and price to book, Sarepta is inexpensive against any commercial-stage rare-disease franchise with an intact regulatory base. The question is whether the base is intact.

Cheapness here is a description of the risk, not an argument against it. At $1.88B enterprise value the market is paying roughly 2x annualized revenue for the PMO franchise alone and ascribing little residual value to ELEVIDYS, the Roche relationship, the $1,175M already committed to the siRNA platform, or the approximately $116M Nippon Shinyaku judgment (awarded December 2024, judgment entered January 2025, post-trial motions pending) that does not appear to be recognized as an asset. That is either a mispricing or an accurate discount for a business in secular revenue decline with $1,052.0M of debt principal, thin operating margins, injunction risk on its gene therapy, and a failed confirmatory trial supporting 70% of revenue. Both readings fit the same price.

We resolve it by scenario:

- **Favorable February 2027 outcome (probability ~50%).** Traditional approval for one or both products converts PMO from contingent to durable. Consolidated 2027 revenue of roughly $1.1-1.2B, adjusted operating income $200-250M, cash interest $45M, minimal cash taxes given NOLs, free cash flow $130-170M. At 12-14x FCF, equity of $1.9-2.4B, or $18-23 per share, with ELEVIDYS and siRNA optionality on top. Wolfe's $27 target (July 9, 2026) sits at the upper end of that branch and is our bull case.
- **Adverse February 2027 outcome (probability ~35%).** Withdrawal or severe label restriction on two of three PMO products removes most of the gross profit. Withdrawal procedures are slow, so revenue would erode over 12-24 months, but the equity would price a terminal value against $1,052.0M of principal, $428.0M of commitments, and likely inventory write-downs. $5-8 per share; the low analyst target is $5.
- **Deferral, mixed outcome, or CRL requiring additional data without withdrawal (probability ~15%).** Roughly $15-18.

Probability-weighted: 0.50($22) + 0.35($6) + 0.15($16) = $15.50. Shifting the favorable branch to 55% gives $16.30; at 60% it gives $17.10. Across the plausible range, expected value brackets the current price with a negative tilt. The distribution is bimodal, so the "base case" is a weighted expectation rather than a modal outcome — the modal outcome is the favorable branch, but the adverse branch costs more than the favorable branch pays.

Note that the analyst dispersion itself ($5 low, $38 high, $22.09 mean, $20.00 median) is the widest in our coverage and is the cleanest available measure of how unresolvable the central question is from outside the company. Recommendation distribution is 7 buy / 11 hold / 3 sell / 2 strong sell.

## Competitive Landscape

The exon-51 franchise is under direct attack. On July 20, 2026 the FDA accepted Dyne Therapeutics' NDA for DYNE-251 (z-rostudirsen), targeting the same exon-51 skipping mechanism and the same patient population as EXONDYS 51. Citi noted on July 21 that z-rostudirsen could differentiate on higher efficacy. The asymmetry is what matters: Dyne is filing on functional data while Sarepta defends accelerated approval on post-hoc analyses of a failed trial. Sarepta's own R&D head acknowledged on the Q2 call that a competitor's traditional approval "could change the landscape for accelerated approval."

Importantly, EXONDYS 51 is not part of the February 2027 sNDA package, so the Dyne threat and the PDUFA risk are partly complementary rather than overlapping. A favorable February 2027 decision on AMONDYS 45 and VYONDYS 53 would not insulate EXONDYS 51 from a differentiated exon-51 entrant.

Management's counter is commercial rather than molecular: a decade of real-world evidence, roughly 90% adherence, established home-infusion networks, and optimized reimbursement and patient-assistance infrastructure, which they expect to delay competitive impact to late 2027. In an ultra-rare indication where prescribing concentrates in a handful of centers, that argument carries weight. It is a delay argument rather than a durability argument.

Elsewhere the competitive position has weakened on multiple axes since mid-2025: the AAVrh74 platform technology designation granted June 2, 2025 was revoked; all four investigational LGMD gene therapy programs were placed on clinical hold July 21, 2025 following a death in the Phase 1 SRP-9004 trial, still in place as of December 2025; ELEVIDYS carries a boxed warning and lost its non-ambulatory indication; and Roche declined an option in February 2026 shortly after the boxed warning. The Roche supply agreement remains unfinalized, operating purchase-order-by-purchase-order, which is the contractual basis for the $75M collaboration revenue raise.

## Risk Assessment

**The February 28, 2027 PDUFA.** Issuer-confirmed. The evidentiary basis is a failed primary endpoint supported by post-hoc analyses, a non-primary week-96 measure, and largely conference-presented real-world evidence. Sarepta's own June 30 release names the risk of FDA reliance limits on real-world evidence and possible market removal. The same release lists the impact of a federal government shutdown on the FDA as a risk factor, introducing schedule risk to the date itself. The FDA has repeatedly exercised flexibility for Sarepta specifically, patient advocacy is publicly supportive, and the agency accepted the filings rather than refusing them — these are real offsets, but the base rate for converting a failed confirmatory trial to traditional approval is not high.

**Patent litigation with injunction exposure.** The REGENXBIO/University of Pennsylvania '617 patent case was revived when the Federal Circuit reversed Sarepta's §101 summary judgment on February 20, 2026; en banc rehearing was denied April 22 and the mandate issued April 29. Plaintiffs seek injunctive relief and treble damages. Sarepta recorded a $39.0M contingency charge in Q2 for "potential resolution of certain patent litigations" with an agreement in principle but no executed documents; the filing does not name the counterparty. The Genzyme (Sanofi) case is set for trial June 14, 2027, and the PTAB director's November 2025 denial of institution on the '542 and '721 patents leaves them intact for that trial.

**Governance and securities litigation.** Oral argument on the motion to dismiss the securities class action was held August 4, 2026 — one day before the Q2 release — with a decision pending. The class period was extended to June 22, 2023 – November 3, 2025 and sitting President & COO Ian Estepan was added as a defendant in January 2026. Five derivative suits name eight sitting or former directors and specifically challenge the 2025 proxy's compensation disclosures, the November 2024 repurchase authorization, and certain director stock sales. The Q2 2025 repurchase of 651K shares at approximately $38.80 has lost roughly $14.4M of value at the current price.

**Customer concentration is worsening.** H1 2026 product revenue was 37% Customer 1 and 21% Customer 2, against 22% and 13% in H1 2025. Receivables at June 30 were 89% concentrated in four counterparties (34%, 24%, 21%, 10%). These are specialty distributors rather than end payers, so this is channel and credit leverage rather than demand concentration, but the direction is unfavorable and the mix shift toward PMO is not diversification.

**Dilution.** Convert shares of 16.007M on an if-converted basis equal 15.2% of shares outstanding, or 13.2% post-issuance ownership dilution; capped calls partially offset the 2027 tranche. Separately, 20.8M of potential award-related shares (12.2M outstanding awards plus 8.6M plan capacity) represent maximum contingent issuance of 19.7% of existing shares, not current fully diluted capitalization.

### Options Market Signal

The current chain prices near-term ATM implied volatility at 64.9% against 82.2% one-year realized — a 17.3-point discount, with IV in the 27th percentile of the chain and a contango term structure (64.9% at one month rising to 70.5% at six months). The put/call skew is 7.1% overall, concentrated in the September tenor (+4.7%, put/call volume ratio 0.80), consistent with hedging around the next print rather than acute stress. Put/call open interest of 0.57 has risen from 0.10 in the prior snapshot, indicating accumulating protection. The reported jump in ATM IV from 84.3% to 692.4% between snapshots is not credible for a liquid $1.8B name and we treat it as a data artifact rather than a signal.

Read together, the options market is not pricing near-term dislocation but is pricing meaningfully elevated uncertainty at the six-month tenor, which brackets the February 2027 decision. The cheap near-term vol is more usefully read as an inexpensive way to own the February optionality than as evidence of complacency about it.

**Short interest.** 32.4% of float short with 7.1 days to cover. This is a crowded short. It constrains any bearish expression to underweighting rather than shorting, and it means that a favorable Cohort 8 readout, a dismissal of the securities class action, positive siRNA interim data, or any constructive FDA signal could produce a violent upward move disconnected from fundamentals. That squeeze risk is the strongest argument against a more negative rating.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $27 (+61%)

The PMO franchise is genuinely durable: $230.6M in Q2 versus $231.3M a year earlier, roughly 90% adherence, more than 1,800 patients treated over a decade, and a 72-76% product gross margin. The FDA converts at least one of AMONDYS 45 and VYONDYS 53 to traditional approval in February 2027, removing the existential overhang and validating the real-world evidence approach. Cost structure is permanently lower — R&D down 55%, capex down 97% — so roughly $920M of PMO revenue plus a stabilizing ELEVIDYS supports $200-250M of adjusted operating income against $45.5M of cash interest. Liquidity is adequate: $945.0M against $107.0M of net debt on a principal basis and no maturity until September 2027. ELEVIDYS inflects in 2027 as the enrollment-form pipeline converts, Cohort 8 restores a non-ambulatory path, and the SRP-9003 BLA is accepted. The approximately $116M Nippon Shinyaku judgment converts to cash. The new CEO's compensation is two-thirds premium-priced options, aligning him with a re-rating. With 32.4% of the float short, resolution of any one binary forces covering.

### Bear Case — $6 (-64%)

The FDA declines to convert on post-hoc analyses of a failed trial, and 70% of revenue and effectively all gross profit enter a wind-down. Even absent that, Dyne secures traditional approval on functional data for exon 51 and takes share from EXONDYS 51 — a threat the February PDUFA does not address. ELEVIDYS never inflects; the six-month enrollment lag was a narrative and the product settles near $400M with a boxed warning and no non-ambulatory indication. Inventory of $1,130.6M — four years of own-product cost of sales, growing while demand fell 70% — requires write-downs, while $266.3M of non-cancelable H2 purchase obligations consumes cash the company cannot generate. The working-capital levers that funded H1 are drawn: manufacturing deposits down to $48.5M from $113.5M, remaining Roche deferred revenue down 58% to $220.3M. The REGENXBIO/UPenn injunction lands. $1,052.0M of principal against a revenue base heading below $1B forces a distressed refinancing of the 2030 Notes, which already trade at 85% of par. The $1,175M paid to Arrowhead cannot be recovered, and management continues routing declining cash flow into Phase 1 assets rather than shoring up the balance sheet.

### What the price is telling us

At 1.50x EV to guided product revenue and 1.16x book, the market is already assigning substantial probability to the bear branch. That is why this is a REDUCE and not an AVOID: the cheapness is real, the liquidity position is genuinely adequate through 2027, and the short interest creates asymmetric upside on any resolution. But cheapness against a bimodal distribution where the adverse branch costs 64% and the favorable branch pays 61% does not produce positive expected value at a 50/35/15 probability split, and the near-term catalysts fall on the wrong side.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $16.50 (probability-weighted, -1.7% from $16.78). Bull case $27 (+61%). Bear case $6 (-64%).

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into any strength above $18 rather than at market — the stock has traded between $14.68 and $20.52 over the trailing three months and mean-reverts violently on headline flow. Reduce to no more than one-third of a normal position size and hold the residual as a call option on the February 2027 decision. Do not average down ahead of the Q3 print (expected early November 2026), which is the first test of the H2 guidance step-down and the first opportunity to observe whether ELEVIDYS enrollment-form activity is converting. Given 32.4% short interest and 7.1 days to cover, do not express this view via short sale or short-dated puts; if hedging a residual long, the six-month tenor at 70.5% implied volatility spans the PDUFA and is the appropriate instrument.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
1. Q3 2026 product revenue at or above $320M (implying H2 tracking toward the upper half of the $541-641M implied range) with ELEVIDYS returning to sequential growth.
2. Inventory declining sequentially from $1,130.6M with write-downs of less than $25M in the quarter and disclosure that the $266.3M of H2 manufacturing commitments has been renegotiated or absorbed without drawing the revolver at quarter-end.
3. Any FDA communication — advisory committee scheduling, Type A/B meeting minutes disclosure, or an 8-K — indicating alignment on the casimersen/golodirsen conversion path. This would move our favorable-branch probability above 60% and expected value above the current price.
4. Dismissal of the securities class action on the pending motion, which removes a discrete overhang and unlocks any insider buying.
5. Clustered open-market purchases by Severino, Estepan, or two or more directors. There have been none observed in the roughly 25 most recent Form 4s reviewed; director Stephen Mayo sold 7,239 shares at $17.02 on March 12, 2026, retaining 1,765.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. A complete response letter, refuse-to-file action, advisory committee vote against conversion, or any FDA communication indicating the February 28, 2027 decision will be adverse.
2. Inventory write-downs exceeding $100M in any quarter, or the revolver outstanding at a quarter-end date, either of which would indicate the liquidity bridge is under strain.
3. Q3 or Q4 2026 product revenue below $270M, or FY2026 guidance cut below $1.2B.
4. An injunction granted in the REGENXBIO/UPenn '617 matter, or failure to execute the settlement documents underlying the $39.0M Q2 accrual.
5. Announcement of an equity raise or a secured financing at terms implying the 2030 Notes cannot be refinanced at par, or Cohort 8 slipping beyond Q1 2027.

**Reassessment timeline.** Three sequenced decision points: the securities-class-action dismissal ruling (pending, argued August 4, 2026); the Q3 print in early November 2026 with the H2 revenue and inventory checks plus the SRP-1001/SRP-1003 interim readouts; and Cohort 8 in Q1 2027 immediately ahead of the February 28, 2027 PDUFA. We expect the central question to remain unresolved until Q1 2027, which is why we are unwilling to carry full exposure through it.

## Conclusion
