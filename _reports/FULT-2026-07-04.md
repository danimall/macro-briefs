**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fulton Financial is a $32.2B-asset Mid-Atlantic regional bank executing its fundamentals well in a difficult rate environment. The standout metric is net interest margin, which expanded 15 basis points year-over-year to 3.58% in Q1 2026 even as the Fed cut rates, a function of disciplined deposit repricing (deposit costs down 25bps to 1.78%) and the paydown of expensive FHLB advances (average balance down 69% YoY). Credit quality is improving: non-performing assets fell to 0.55% of assets, criticized and classified loans dropped 5.7%, and ACL coverage of non-performing loans strengthened to 209%. Capital is robust (CET1 11.9%), the dividend was raised to $0.19/quarter, and the April 2026 close of the all-stock Blue Foundry acquisition deepens the New Jersey footprint. This is a competently run consolidator delivering a 14.8% operating ROTCE.

The problem is price. The stock has appreciated 27.7% over the trailing twelve months to $24.11, near its 52-week high of $24.71. It now trades essentially at the analyst mean target of $24 and above the median of $23. The asymmetry that existed when management was repurchasing 1.21M shares at an average $20.21 in Q1 2026 has largely closed; those buybacks are now roughly 19% in the money, which is good for shareholders but tells you the smart internal buyer is no longer being offered the price it wanted. Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising (current-year EPS consensus at $2.19, up only 3.0% over 90 days and flat over 30 days), so there is no earnings inflection to pull the multiple higher.

We maintain a HOLD (growth_hold). The business deserves credit for improving fundamentals and the score trajectory (5.8 in March, 6.5 in May, 6.15 now) reflects a genuinely better company. But the risk/reward at $24 is balanced. We would become buyers again on a pullback toward the low-$20 to low-$21 range where management itself was accumulating, or on evidence that Blue Foundry accretion and continued NIM defense are pushing consensus estimates meaningfully higher.

## Company Overview

Fulton Financial Corporation is a Pennsylvania-based financial holding company operating through a single bank subsidiary, Fulton Bank, N.A., across a five-state Mid-Atlantic footprint (Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland, New Jersey, and Virginia). It reports as one segment.

Scale as of March 31, 2026:
- Total assets: $32.2B
- Net loans: $24.3B
- Total deposits: $26.8B
- Shareholders' equity: $3.5B
- Market cap: ~$4.6B

Roughly 79% of revenue is net interest income; the remaining 21% is fee income diversified across wealth management (~$24.5M/quarter, AUM-driven), commercial banking (merchant/card, cash management, capital markets), consumer banking, and mortgage banking (servicing a $4.1B third-party portfolio with an MSR fair value of $52.9M).

The loan book is commercial-tilted: commercial mortgage $10.0B (41%), residential mortgage $6.7B (28%), commercial & industrial $4.5B (18%), home equity $1.3B, construction $0.9B, consumer $0.6B. Commercial mortgage plus construction represent ~45% of loans, the primary credit-risk concentration to monitor.

The "FultonFirst" program is an operating-model simplification aimed at reducing non-interest expense and sharpening relationship-banking focus; implementation costs ran $1.6M in Q1 2026. The M&A cadence (Prudential Bancorp in 2022, Blue Foundry in 2026) signals a scale-through-consolidation strategy consistent with a bank that lacks the technology budget of money-center peers.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings.** Net income grew from ~$284M (2023) to ~$289M (2024) into a strong 2025. Q1 2026 net income was $94.8M ($92.2M to common), diluted EPS of $0.51 (up from $0.49 a year earlier); operating EPS was $0.55. Trailing twelve-month EPS is $2.08.

**Net interest margin.** The most impressive line. The mechanism is what matters: interest income fell $9.7M YoY as asset yields declined with rates, but interest expense fell more (down $20.5M) as deposit costs repriced down and the bank shed high-cost FHLB advances (average balance down $488M, or 69%). Expanding NIM in a rate-cutting cycle demonstrates competent asset/liability management.

**Asset sensitivity.** The balance sheet is modestly asset-sensitive: a +100bp shock adds ~$27.4M to annual NII (+2.4%); a -100bp shock costs ~$15.8M (-1.4%). Further Fed cuts are a mild headwind, but the demonstrated ability to defend NIM via deposit repricing softens the impact.

**Profitability.** ROA 1.20% (operating 1.30%), ROE 11.2%, operating ROTCE 14.8%, efficiency ratio 56.7% — solid-to-strong for a regional bank of this size.

**Capital.** CET1 up to 11.9% from 11.8%, Tier 1 risk-based 12.7%, total risk-based 15.2%, Tier 1 leverage 9.9%. All comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds, providing capacity for the Blue Foundry deal, buybacks, and dividends.

**Capital allocation.** Dividend raised to $0.19/quarter from $0.18. A new $150M buyback authorization for 2026 (up to $25M usable for preferred/sub-debt); 1.21M shares repurchased in Q1 2026 at an average $20.21. Management also issued $300M of 5.95% fixed-to-floating subordinated notes due 2036 in May 2026, refinancing $195M of 2030 notes, an opportunistic termout.

**AOCI marks.** Accumulated other comprehensive loss widened to -$221.9M (from -$198.7M at YE2025) as rates backed up in Q1. AFS securities carry ~$266M of gross unrealized losses; HTM carries ~$166M of unrecognized losses (fair value $1.43B vs. $1.59B amortized cost). These are rate-driven marks on GSE-guaranteed/high-grade paper, not credit losses, but they suppress tangible book value and remain sensitive to rate moves.

FCF-type metrics do not apply cleanly to a bank; the financial health case rests on strong pre-provision profitability, robust capital, improving credit, and disciplined liability management. We score Financial Health 7.2, consistent with the prior report and reflecting genuine strength across the most decision-relevant bank metrics.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is steady compounding rather than inflection. Consensus current-year EPS is $2.19; next-year is $2.36 (up 1.8% over 90 days). This is stable-to-modestly-positive revision momentum, not the 30%+ velocity that signals a fundamental turn. The company has beaten estimates in each of the last four quarters (by 3.0%, 3.3%, 4.6%, and 15.6%), which lends confidence to consensus but the beats are narrowing back toward low-single-digit magnitudes.

Three growth levers are in play. First, Blue Foundry: an all-stock deal (0.650 FULT per Blue Foundry share, 12.44M shares issued) that closed April 1, 2026, deepening New Jersey. Q1 2026 results are pre-close, so purchase-accounting accretion, CDI amortization, and the 12.4M share count increase reshape Q2 2026 onward, a swing factor not yet in the numbers. Second, the $207.7M in-market loan portfolio purchase in Q1 (mostly commercial mortgages) is an inorganic way to grow earning assets amid tepid organic loan demand, which is itself a tell that organic demand is soft. Third, continued NIM defense as deposits reprice.

The honest read: this is a mid-single-digit earnings compounder with M&A-driven scale gains. The estimate-revision signal does not meet the threshold that would argue for a re-rating catalyst. We score Growth Prospects 6.0, a modest step down from the prior 6.5 not because fundamentals deteriorated but because the growth is durable-but-unremarkable and the revision velocity has flattened.

## Valuation Assessment

FULT trades at ~11.5x trailing and forward earnings, a 3.1% dividend yield, and roughly 1.3x tangible book. Against the regional-bank peer set, this is reasonable but not compellingly cheap:

|--------|-----|-----------|
| IBOC | 11.3 | 1.9% |
| FFIN | 18.1 | 2.6% |
| ASB | 10.1 | 3.4% |
| TCBI | 13.5 | 0.8% |
| SFBS | 15.0 | 1.9% |
| **Peer median** | **13.5** | **1.9%** |
| **FULT** | **11.5** | **3.1%** |

FULT sits below the peer-median P/E of 13.5x and offers an above-median dividend yield. That is a modest discount, but it is not the kind of mispricing that warrants a high valuation score. The discount is partly justified: FULT delivers mid-single-digit earnings growth, carries meaningful CRE concentration, and faces integration risk from Blue Foundry. The lower-P/E peers (ASB at 10.1x, IBOC at 11.3x) also trade cheap for reasons.

The more decision-relevant point is where the price sits relative to signals. The stock trades essentially at consensus, near its 52-week high and its all-time buyback reference point. On an inflection stock with rising estimates, we would treat consensus targets as stale floors. This is not that stock: forward P/E is reasonable but estimate revisions are flat, so there is no engine to pull the multiple higher near-term. Management, the most informed buyer, was accumulating at ~$20.21 and has not been buying open-market at $24. That is the cleanest single valuation signal available.

Cheapness alone is a weak buy signal, and the peer discount here is modest and partly earned. We score Valuation 5.2 — the stock is fairly priced with limited near-term asymmetry, not a value trap but not a bargain.

## Competitive Landscape

Fulton competes as a mid-sized (~$32B) regional against national banks, other regionals, community banks, and fintechs across the Mid-Atlantic. It explicitly flags its lack of scale/technology budget relative to money-center banks. Its competitive response is density in local markets, relationship banking, and consolidation.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The NIM expansion in a rate-cutting cycle is a genuine relative bright spot versus peers facing compression, and improving credit metrics suggest disciplined underwriting. The Blue Foundry deal adds New Jersey density. Against that, organic loan demand is soft (the bank bought a loan portfolio rather than originating it), and the company operates in a structurally challenged industry where fintech and scale pressures grind on smaller regionals. FultonFirst is a rational cost-efficiency response but is not a moat-widener.

We score Competitive Position 6.0. The bank is holding its own and executing better than the peer average, but there is no evidence of durable share gains or moat widening that the market has failed to price. Consistent with the calibration lesson that competitive-position signals have decayed, we do not lean on this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

The most decision-relevant company-specific risks:

**CRE concentration, particularly office.** CRE is ~41% of loans, which screens as a red flag on an automated model. The granular disclosure paints a more contained picture: non-owner-occupied office is only $730M (~3.0% of loans) at a 63% weighted-average origination LTV, concentrated in Philadelphia ($351M). Multi-family non-owner-occupied is larger at $1.52B (LTV 58%). This is a well-underwritten, modest office exposure relative to the sector's reputation.

**Deposit/liquidity.** ~36% of deposits are uninsured (~$9.6B); state and municipal deposits are ~13% of total and rate-sensitive. Backup liquidity is ample ($7.4B FHLB availability, $2.6B fed funds lines, $3.7B FRB discount window). Given the 2023 regional-bank deposit-flight episode, this remains a watch item but is well-covered.

**Merger integration.** Blue Foundry systems conversion is planned for Q3 2026; the usual synergy, retention, and conversion-execution risks apply. This is the risk category most expanded in recent filings.

**Interest-rate/AOCI.** Rate volatility hits both NIM (mild asset sensitivity) and tangible book via AFS/HTM marks. The -$221.9M AOCI loss widened in Q1.

**Credit normalization.** Net charge-offs rose to 0.25% annualized (from 0.21%), driven by C&I charge-offs (~$9.8M in Q1, versus a small net recovery a year earlier). This is the one soft spot worth tracking — whether idiosyncratic or the start of a trend. It is offset by improving leading indicators: criticized/classified loans down $83.5M (-5.7%), special-mention down $121M, non-accruals down 7.7%.

On balance, the risks are largely priced and several (CRE, credit) are trending favorably. There are no undisclosed binary events. We score Risk Factors 6.0. Per calibration, we do not overweight this dimension in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 55.1% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 26.7% (a 28.4-point spread), and IV jumped from 26.6% to 55.1% since late May. The near-term skew is call-heavy (-6.4%), and the 3-month tenor shows a sharply positive skew (37.4%). The elevated 1-month IV with an implied move of ±5.7% is consistent with positioning ahead of the next earnings release. The call-heavy near-term skew is mildly constructive, but the rich IV suggests the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty rather than directional conviction; this argues against chasing the stock at the 52-week high and favors patience. Short interest is modest at 5.8% of float (5.7 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($27):** Blue Foundry accretion materializes cleanly in Q2–H2 2026, NIM continues to defend as deposits reprice, credit metrics keep improving (criticized loans falling, C&I charge-offs prove idiosyncratic), and the FultonFirst efficiency program drops the efficiency ratio below 56%. Consensus revises next-year EPS above $2.40, the multiple holds at ~11.5x forward, and the 3.1% dividend plus buyback support a total return in the mid-teens. At $27 the stock would trade ~11.4x the high end of a rising estimate range, matching the analyst high target.

**Bear Case ($20):** C&I charge-offs prove to be the leading edge of a broader credit-normalization cycle, CRE (particularly Philadelphia office) softens, Blue Foundry integration hiccups on the Q3 systems conversion, and further Fed cuts pressure the modestly asset-sensitive balance sheet. Rate volatility widens AOCI marks and pressures tangible book. The stock re-rates back toward the ~$20 buyback level (down ~17% from current), where management was a willing buyer.

**Base Case ($24):** Steady mid-single-digit earnings compounding, NIM holding near 3.5%, credit stable-to-improving, dividend and buyback continuing. The stock stays range-bound near consensus as the earnings grind offsets a flat multiple, delivering a total return roughly equal to the 3.1% dividend plus low-single-digit price appreciation over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The business is compounding and the fundamentals are improving, but the current price offers balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback toward the low-$20 to low-$21 range where management was repurchasing stock, restoring asymmetry
- Consensus next-year EPS revised above $2.40 (currently $2.36), signaling Blue Foundry accretion and NIM defense are exceeding expectations
- Two consecutive quarters confirming C&I charge-offs were idiosyncratic (NCOs stabilizing back toward 0.21% or below) alongside continued declines in criticized/classified loans

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NCOs rising above 0.40% annualized or a reversal in criticized/classified loan trends (a return to rising special-mention balances)
- Blue Foundry integration problems surfacing at the Q3 2026 systems conversion (deposit runoff, one-time charges materially above guidance)
- Meaningful deposit outflow or a spike in uninsured-deposit sensitivity

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July), which will show the first Blue Foundry-inclusive results and clarify the credit trajectory. The Q3 2026 systems conversion is the second natural checkpoint.

**From current $24.11:** base case +~0% (plus 3.1% dividend), bull case +12%, bear case -17%.

## Conclusion

Fulton Financial has earned its improving score. The NIM expansion in a rate-cutting cycle, the improving credit metrics that run counter to the CRE-fear narrative, the robust capital position, and the disciplined liability management all mark this as a well-run regional bank. The score trajectory reflects a genuinely better company, and this report's modest step-down is a valuation judgment, not a fundamental one.

The constraint is price. At $24.11 the stock trades near its 52-week high, essentially at the analyst mean target, and roughly 19% above where management itself was buying back shares in Q1. Estimate revisions are flat, so there is no near-term catalyst to pull the multiple higher. The modest peer-P/E discount is partly justified by mid-single-digit growth and integration risk. For a Financials name, the calibration record cautions that raw returns here tend to be beta rather than selection skill, which reinforces the need for a company-specific catalyst before paying up.

We rate FULT a HOLD for the compounding earnings and 3.1% dividend, and would look to add on a pullback toward the low-$20s or on evidence that Blue Foundry accretion and continued NIM strength are lifting consensus estimates.
