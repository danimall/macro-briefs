## Executive Summary

Liberty Media is now a pure-play global motorsport commercial-rights holder: Formula 1 (wholly owned) and MotoGP (~84% owned since July 2025), with essentially nothing else after the Atlanta Braves, Sirius XM and Liberty Live split-offs. The December 15, 2025 Liberty Live separation also ended the tracking-stock structure, so holders now own the assets directly. The two franchises are legally exclusive and long-dated: the FIA regulatory-services arrangement runs to 2110, the FIM Grand Prix rights to December 31, 2060, and the contracts monetizing them specify advance payment and annual fee escalators. That produces high forward revenue visibility (~$19.3B of remaining performance obligations, including ~$3.6B already contracted for 2027 against FY2025 total revenue of $4,482M), structurally negative working capital, and a revenue base that grows without incremental volume.

The Q2 2026 print looked catastrophic and mostly wasn't. Consolidated revenue fell $407M to $934M, but the decomposition reconciles as −$462M at F1, −$139M from the disappearance of QuintEvents following the split-off, +$170M from newly consolidated MotoGP and +$24M of lower eliminations. F1's decline is dominated by pro-rata recognition mechanics — 5/22nds of season-based revenue recognized versus 9/24ths a year earlier — with team payments falling in lockstep from $513M to $316M. Two spring races (Bahrain, Saudi Arabia) did not take place because of Middle East conflict; Bahrain has been recovered through a relocation to Sepang, Malaysia on October 2–4, retaining the Bahrain name, so the assumed 2026 calendar is 23 races against 24 in 2025. Because H2 2026 recognizes 15/23 of season revenue versus H2 2025's 13/24, the next two prints should show sharp positive year-over-year F1 growth. The market understood this: the shares rose 2–4% on a large GAAP miss.

What holds us at HOLD is price against unresolved binaries. At roughly $25–26B enterprise value the equity is at ~25x trailing-twelve-month Adjusted OIBDA of $1,013M and ~19–21x our normalized 2027 estimate of $1.25–1.35B, with a 3.6% FCF yield on FY2025 free cash flow of $789M. F1 said it would wait until September to decide on the remaining Middle East races, with Qatar and Abu Dhabi (the traditional finale) in question. F1's covenant leverage rose from 2.3x to 2.7x in a single quarter while its debt fell $10M, entirely a trailing-EBITDA denominator effect. This is how a calendar problem becomes a capital-allocation problem: $4,358M of the $4,855M of debt sits behind subsidiary restricted-payment tests, MotoGP at 4.5x has never upstreamed cash, and no shares were repurchased from May 1 through July 31 despite $1.1B of authorization. Base case $100 (+6%) against a $72 bear case (−23%) is not adequate compensation.

## Company Overview

**Formula 1.** Holds the exclusive commercial rights to the FIA Formula One World Championship, monetized through three primary streams (race promotion fees, media rights fees, sponsorship) plus "other" revenue from Paddock Club hospitality, team freight and logistics, F2/F3 and F1 Academy, licensing, and the Las Vegas Grand Prix, where F1 is itself the promoter and therefore takes ticketing revenue and event cost risk directly. FY2025 F1 revenue was $3,873M (+13.5%), split $3,086M primary and $787M other.

**MotoGP.** Formerly Dorna Sports, renamed MotoGP Sports Entertainment Group. Exclusive commercial rights to the FIM Grand Prix World Championship to end-2060 plus World Superbike. Same three-stream model plus VIP Village hospitality. Acquired July 3, 2025 for a preliminary ~$3,659M for ~84% of equity, net cash outlay $3,267M.

**Corporate and Other.** Overhead, Grand Prix Plaza rental income (~$6M/quarter) and residual investments. This line collapsed from $145M of quarterly revenue to $6M after QuintEvents left with the Liberty Live split-off, a third of the headline Q2 revenue decline.

A data-integrity note the reader should hold onto: the $2.247B "market cap" in the supplied quote covers the Series A class only (~23.99M shares). Liberty has three classes — Series A (one vote), Series B (ten votes), Series C (non-voting, and by far the largest). The company carries $15,879M of assets and $4,855M of face debt. The supplied trailing/forward P/E of 110.93 and trailing EPS of −$0.13 are internally inconsistent (the four disclosed quarterly EPS actuals sum to $0.70), so we do not use per-share GAAP earnings as a valuation anchor.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (the cleanest normalized anchor available).** Revenue $4,482M, +22.7%, with F1 up 13.5% organically and MotoGP contributing $325M from July 3. Operating income doubled to $577M. Adjusted OIBDA rose 38% to $1,068M. Operating cash flow $908M against capex $119M gives $789M of free cash flow — 85% OIBDA-to-OCF conversion, 74% of Adjusted OIBDA after capex, and a 17.6% FCF margin. That figure absorbs $50M of one-time Concorde signing incentives and $27M of acquisition costs, and includes only half a year of MotoGP.

**Margin trajectory is improving at the operating level.** F1's Adjusted OIBDA margin went from 23.2% (791/3,411) to 24.4% (946/3,873). Consolidated margin rose from 21.2% to 23.8%, helped by MotoGP's higher 35.1% margin (201/573 pro forma) and the exit of low-margin QuintEvents.

**Why MotoGP keeps more of each dollar.** F1 pays teams $1,400M — 36.1% of F1 revenue in FY2025, down from 37.1% — and the variable prize-fund elements are calculated by formula with reference to F1 revenue and costs. MotoGP's equivalent IRTA payments sit inside a total cost of motorsport revenue of $292M on $573M of revenue. The formulaic nature of F1 team payments caps upside on revenue growth but automatically cushions downside when races are lost, which is what happened in Q2.

**Q2/H1 2026.** F1 revenue $764M (−38%), MotoGP $170M, consolidated $934M. F1 Adjusted OIBDA fell 61% to $139M and its margin compressed from 29.4% to 18.2%, because SG&A does not flex with race count — it rose 14% to $89M on personnel and IT despite four fewer events. That within-quarter deleverage is real economics, distinct from the timing effect. H1 consolidated revenue $1,645M versus $1,788M; H1 Adjusted OIBDA $387M versus $442M.

**Cash flow requires care.** H1 2026 OCF of $673M included +$673M from "payables and other liabilities" and −$268M from current and other assets, a net +$405M working-capital inflow, roughly 60% of reported OCF. Deferred revenue rose from $263M to $1,117M as promoters, broadcasters and sponsors prepaid for the season; that balance unwinds through H2 as events are delivered. Pre-working-capital operating cash in H1 was approximately $268M. Annualizing H1 OCF would overstate cash generation by several hundred million dollars.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 pre-tax income from continuing operations of $733M included $288M (39%) of realized and unrealized gains on financial instruments, driven by +$335M on FX forwards; FY2024 swung the other way at −$139M. In H1 2026 that line fell to $56M from $235M, which accounts for most of the drop in net earnings attributable to Liberty from $209M to $62M. These forwards hedge real euro- and sterling-denominated exposures at a UK-domiciled F1 and euro-functional MotoGP, so the marks are volatile rather than fictitious. GAAP net income, ROE (7.7%) and net margin (13.3%) are therefore poor period-to-period comparators; Adjusted OIBDA and free cash flow are the operative measures, and they are also management's compensation metrics.

**Balance sheet and covenants.** At 6/30/26: face debt $4,855M ($475M 2.25% converts due 2027, $3,330M F1 senior loans, $1,028M MotoGP facilities, $22M other), cash $1,465M. Covenant net leverage: F1 2.7x (from 2.3x at 3/31/26), MotoGP 4.5x (from 4.7x), consolidated 3.4x. Counting converts at conversion value (~5.72M shares at ~$82.98 conversion price × ~$95 ≈ $544M) gives debt of ~$4,924M and net debt of ~$3,459M; against TTM Adjusted OIBDA of $1,013M that reproduces the disclosed 3.42x, which validates the underlying inputs. Interest expense runs ~$136M per half, so coverage is ~3.7x. Only ~$497M of debt sits at the corporate level; the rest is behind F1 and MotoGP restricted-payment tests. F1 distributed $2.5B to Liberty in FY2025 to help fund MotoGP; MotoGP has never distributed. In June 2026 MotoGP repriced its facilities and repaid $114M. Liberty has no corporate debt rating.

Book equity is largely intangible: $6,877M of goodwill and $4,843M of amortizing intangibles against $15,879M of assets. An Altman Z-Score of 1.82 flags this, but the score is calibrated on manufacturers and penalizes the 0.29 asset turnover and thin book returns that are artifacts of purchase accounting for an asset-light IP business. The operative solvency facts are 3.4x covenant leverage, 3.7x coverage, $1,465M of cash, full compliance, and $19.3B of contracted performance obligations. The Z-Score's legitimate signal is narrower: the route by which equity would erode is a goodwill write-down, and the MotoGP purchase-price allocation was still preliminary at 12/31/25.

Financial Health scores 5.9: strong contracted cash conversion and negative working capital, offset by structurally trapped subsidiary cash, a covenant ratio sensitive to event count, a $759M redeemable non-controlling-interest put growing with MotoGP's fair value, and near-term convertible settlement.

## Growth Analysis

**The 2026 shape is a timing problem with a one-race economic hole.** Assuming a 23-race calendar, H2 2026 recognizes 15/23 (65.2%) of season-based revenue versus H2 2025's 13/24 (54.2%). Full-year F1 revenue should land roughly flat to modestly up: escalators of perhaps 5–7% offset by one fewer race (~4% of event-linked revenue) and the absence of FY2025's one-time F1-movie media revenue. A plausible FY2026 F1 Adjusted OIBDA range is $900M–$980M against $946M in FY2025. That is a sensitivity, not guidance — the company provides none — and it assumes Qatar and Abu Dhabi are held.

**2027 is where the growth case sits.** A full calendar, contractual escalators, reported new races (Turkey has been cited by sell-side), and the commercial levers management named: sponsorship, licensing and premium hospitality. Bernstein sizes sponsorship at ~$1.1B by FY2027 and ~$1.2B by FY2028 against a ~$268M base when Liberty took control in 2017, and licensing at $80–150M today with a path to $175–310M by 2028. These are third-party estimates relayed through secondary reporting, not filings, and should be treated as directional.

**MotoGP is the incremental growth asset and the evidence is mixed.** FY2025 pro forma revenue grew 8.6% in constant currency with Adjusted OIBDA up 14.9% ($175M → $201M). Q2 2026 was −5% revenue and −2% Adjusted OIBDA in constant currency on event mix and softer contractual media rights; H1 was better at +1% and +5%. The structural moves are the right ones: five-year agreements with all manufacturers and teams through 2031 (closing a genuine cliff — the 10-K disclosed IRTA participation only to December 31, 2026), CAA appointed global sponsorship agency, promoter renewals with Malaysia to 2031 and Silverstone to 2028, broadcast extensions with DAZN and Sky DACH. Currency matters: FY2025 MotoGP revenue grew 13.9% in USD but 8.6% constant currency, and the direction reversed in Q2.

**Explicit ceiling on one axis.** Domenicali has ruled out a fourth US race. With three already, US growth must come from monetization intensity rather than event count, which is consistent with management naming sponsorship, licensing and hospitality as the levers.

**Estimate revisions are mixed and thinly covered.** Current quarter +13.3% and next quarter +24.1% over 30 days reflect the H2 recognition shift; but current-year consensus fell 27.6% over 30 days to $1.34 and next year fell 6.9%/10.0% to $2.29, on one to two contributing analysts. The near-quarter revisions do not constitute the kind of broad signal that would justify paying up; the full-year direction is negative and the coverage base is too thin to be robust.

Growth Prospects scores 6.2: a durable escalator-plus-monetization path in the high single to low double digits on Adjusted OIBDA, capped by team payments at ~36% of F1 revenue, no additional US events, and undisclosed Concorde revenue-share terms.

## Valuation Assessment

We derive enterprise value rather than rely on the class-level market cap. Using ~231M total shares across Series A/B/C (a mid-point sensitivity — the filings reviewed did not yield a confirmed total count) and a blended ~$94–95 price: equity ~$21.7–21.9B, plus net debt $3,459M (converts already counted at conversion value, so the ~5.7M conversion shares must not be added again), plus redeemable NCI $759M, gives EV of roughly $25.9–26.2B.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / TTM Adjusted OIBDA ($1,013M) | ~25.6x |
| EV / FY2025 Adjusted OIBDA ($1,068M) | ~24.3x |
| EV / 2027E Adjusted OIBDA ($1.25–1.35B) | ~19–21x |
| FCF yield (FY2025 FCF $789M on ~$21.7B equity) | ~3.6% |
| P/E on next-year consensus $2.29 | ~41x |

The supplied peer table contains only sibling Liberty tracking classes (LLYVA, LLYVK, FWONK) whose P/Es of −20.6, −21.5 and 117.7 carry no information. The relevant cohort is scarce sports-rights assets, and the read there is relative rather than absolute: over the past twelve months TKO Group is up 16% and Madison Square Garden Sports up 93% while FWONK is down ~1.6% and −0.5% year-to-date. That underperformance is explicable: Liberty absorbed a real 2026 revenue disruption plus the optics of two split-offs and a large acquisition.

The valuation question is whether ~25x trailing OIBDA and a 3.6% FCF yield is asymmetric on a business we expect to compound Adjusted OIBDA at 8–12% normalized, with an unresolved calendar and an undisclosed change to its largest cost line. We think not. The multiple already capitalizes the escalators, the sponsorship ramp and MotoGP's margin advantage. Liberty paid roughly 26–27x for MotoGP itself ($4,356M implied 100% equity plus ~$1.0–1.15B of facilities against $201M of pro forma Adjusted OIBDA), so the company's own most recent capital-allocation decision confirms the asset class is priced richly rather than cheaply.

Sell-side targets cluster at $100–116 (mean $108, median $108.5) with 6 buys and no holds or sells, and Morgan Stanley raised to $125 on July 29. At a ~25x multiple these targets are not independent confirmation of value — they embed the same 2027 recovery extrapolation the price does. The mean of $108 implies ~15% upside from the current price, which is close to our bull-case path rather than a base case.

One FWONA-specific consideration: FWONA at $93.66 (August 11 quote) against FWONK at $98.00 (secondary source, August 6) implies a ~4.4% discount for the class that carries a vote over the class that does not. The prices come from different sources on different dates and should be re-verified on same-date quotes before being treated as a persistent spread. If it is persistent, the trade-off is a modest entry discount plus a largely symbolic vote — Malone holds ~49.5% of voting power — against materially worse liquidity (average volume ~141k shares, 6.7 days to cover on short interest) and no index sponsorship.

Valuation scores 4.5: full price, ~6% base-case upside, no absolute yield support, and no identified mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is contractual and close to unassailable at the rights level. There is one Formula 1 and one MotoGP; Liberty holds exclusive commercial rights to both, with the FIA regulatory arrangement running to 2110 and the FIM rights to 2060. A large majority of promotion, media and sponsorship contracts specify advance payment and annual fee escalators, which is why $19.3B of remaining performance obligations exists and why the market looked through a 38% quarterly revenue decline.

Trajectory is stable-to-improving with one open question. Constructive: Las Vegas Grand Prix extended ten years to 2037, Pirelli through 2028, attendance and digital impressions up season-to-date, and at MotoGP the entire grid locked through 2031 with CAA appointed to professionalize sponsorship, the precondition for selling long-dated media and sponsorship inventory. Also constructive on demand: the 2019 Netflix partnership is widely credited with the US demand shift, and the sport remains under-penetrated in the US and Asia relative to its global position.

The open question is US reach. F1's US rights moved from ESPN's linear channels to Apple TV under a five-year deal. Liberty disclosed total hours watched up 13% year-over-year and described viewership as up, but declined to disclose an average-minute audience. Hours watched on a subscription streamer is not methodologically comparable to linear AMA. The commercial risk is second-order but real: sponsorship pricing is ultimately underwritten by reach, so a materially smaller paywalled US audience would eventually pressure the sponsorship growth thesis that carries most of the 2027–2028 upside. Six league-level sponsorship deals were reportedly signed before Apple's first US race aired, which argues the near-term pipeline is intact.

Competitive Position scores 7.2: high franchise quality with an improving MotoGP structure, marked down from higher for the unquantified US audience transition and MotoGP's negative constant-currency Q2.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical calendar risk, already realized and not yet resolved.** Saudi Arabia is gone from the season. The issuer states plainly that "additional calendar changes may be necessary," and the remaining Middle East races await the September decision. Each lost race removes roughly 1/23rd of season-based revenue and its associated team payment, and mechanically tightens the trailing-EBITDA covenant. This sits on top of an unresolved regional conflict.

**Covenant-to-capital-allocation transmission.** The entire covenant-leverage move is denominator. Covenant EBITDA is a credit-agreement construct that may differ from Adjusted OIBDA, and headroom to the actual restricted-payment threshold is undisclosed — the point is sensitivity, not proximity to breach. The consequence is visible: no repurchases from May 1 through July 31, 2026, with $1.1B of authorization idle, and sell-side framing capital return as something that comes into view only as leverage falls. Consolidated cash rose $133M over the quarter.

**Team-payment and Concorde economics.** The 2026 Concorde Commercial Agreement is signed and cost $50M of one-time incentives, but the revised revenue-share formula was not located in the filings reviewed. This is the single largest lever on F1 margin and its direction is unverified. The economic terms of MotoGP's new five-year team and manufacturer agreements are also undisclosed; a higher cost share would compress the 35% margin that is the principal argument for MotoGP's superior unit economics.

**Impairment exposure.** Goodwill splits F1 $3,956M and MotoGP $3,069M at 12/31/25, with the MotoGP allocation preliminary. QuintEvents took a $73M goodwill impairment in FY2024, so the company does write down underperforming units.

**Governance.** Dual-class structure with Series B at ten votes and non-voting Series C. Malone owns a small minority of economics and has resigned from the board — his Form 4 reporter title changed from "Chairman of the Board" to "10% Owner" between December 2025 and March 2026. Voting control is now exercised from outside the boardroom. A May 12, 2026 reincorporation (read as a move to Nevada, and flagged as requiring confirmation) would broaden officer and director exculpation relative to Delaware, reducing minority-shareholder litigation leverage.

**Convertible maturity.** $475M face, 2.25%, due 2027, conversion price ~$82.98 on Series C and in the money. Share settlement would issue ~5.72M shares — about 2.4% post-issuance ownership dilution on a 231M base — but settlement method appears elective. Because the notes are carried at fair value, FWONK appreciation creates non-cash losses in the financial-instruments line.

**FX.** MotoGP is euro-functional and should be underwritten on constant currency, where FY2025 revenue grew 8.6% versus 13.9% in USD and Q2 2026 reversed to −5%.

Risk Factors scores 4.0: multiple dated or near-dated binaries (September race decision, undisclosed Concorde terms, MotoGP put rights beginning around the third anniversary of the July 2025 close with up to half payable in unregistered shares, 2027 converts) that the market's post-print reaction suggests are only partially priced.

### Options Market Signal

The FWONA chain is thin (56 contracts across four expirations) and the readings partly conflict, so treat them as weak corroboration. Near-term ATM IV of 33.5% against 24.2% one-year realized is rich, with a backwardated term structure (33.5% at nine days versus 24.2% at six months) and an implied move of ±6.2–7.2% on the nearest expiry — unusual given earnings passed on August 6, and plausibly consistent with the September Middle East calendar decision drawing near-dated hedging or speculation. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call skew of −4.4% and put/call open interest of 0.07, among the most call-tilted readings we see anywhere. A separate longitudinal snapshot shows ATM IV falling from 37.7% to 18.8% between June 4 and August 11 as the price rose 17.6%, which does not reconcile with the 33.5% near-term print; we do not draw a directional conclusion from either in isolation. Short interest is a modest 5.7% of float at 6.7 days to cover. Net read: no options-market confirmation of a bearish case, and no cheap-vol entry signal either — near-dated protection costs more than realized vol has warranted.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $122 (+30%)

The calendar normalizes at 23 races for 2026 with Qatar and Abu Dhabi held, H2 prints show sharply positive year-over-year F1 growth on 15/23 versus 13/24 recognition, and FY2026 F1 Adjusted OIBDA lands at the upper end of $900–980M. 2027 delivers a full 24-race calendar plus new venues, escalators, and the sponsorship and licensing ramp: consolidated Adjusted OIBDA reaches ~$1.35B (F1 ~$1.10B, MotoGP ~$230M, less ~$25M corporate). MotoGP's teams-locked-to-2031 structure plus CAA converts into media-rights repricing at F1-style economics. Consolidated leverage falls through 3x, the restricted-payment tests loosen, and the idle $1.1B authorization is deployed. At 24x EV/2027E Adjusted OIBDA: EV $32.4B, less $3.46B net debt and $0.76B NCI, equity $28.2B, ~$122 per share on 231M shares.

### Bear Case — $72 (−23%)

F1 loses Qatar and/or Abu Dhabi in the September decision, taking 2026 to 21–22 races; Bahrain's promoter fee is reduced for a Sepang-hosted event and F1 absorbs part of the hosting cost, so the FY2026 hole exceeds one race. The 2026 Concorde revenue share moves against Liberty, lifting team payments above 36% of F1 revenue. MotoGP's constant-currency softness persists and its new team agreements carry a higher cost share, compressing the 35% margin that justified a 26–27x purchase multiple. F1 covenant leverage moves toward 3x, restricted-payment headroom tightens, buybacks stay frozen, and the redeemable NCI put approaches with cash constrained. 2027 Adjusted OIBDA of $1.15B at 18x: EV $20.7B less $4.2B, equity ~$16.5B, ~$71–72 per share — essentially the 52-week low of $73.70.

### Base Case — $100 (+6%)

23 races held in 2026; FY2026 F1 Adjusted OIBDA ~$940M and consolidated ~$1.06B; 2027 consolidated Adjusted OIBDA ~$1.30B. At 21x: EV $27.3B, less $3.46B net debt and $0.76B NCI, equity ~$23.1B, ~$100 per share. Probability-weighting 55% base, 20% bull and 25% bear gives an expected return of roughly +3–4% — approximately fair, with the distribution's left tail wider than its right in the near term because the September decision resolves before most of the 2027 recovery is evidenced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders should stay; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $94.005 with ~6% base-case upside against 23% bear-case downside and a dated binary inside the next six weeks. The hold is classified uncertain rather than growth: the single largest quantitative unknown for FY2026 — whether Bahrain's promoter fee survives a Sepang-hosted race, who bears Sepang's costs, and whether Saudi fees were forgiven, deferred or insured — is unresolved, as are the Concorde revenue-share terms governing 36% of F1 revenue.

**Reassessment timeline.** Two hard dates: the stated September 2026 decision point on the remaining Middle East races, and the Q3 2026 report (expected early November), which is the first print that should show the H2 recognition inversion (15/23 versus 13/24) in reported numbers.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE, and to BUY on two or more):**
1. Confirmation that Qatar and Abu Dhabi are held, taking the 2026 calendar to 23 and removing the denominator risk to F1's covenant ratio.
2. Q3/Q4 prints showing F1 Adjusted OIBDA margin recovering toward the FY2025 24.4% level (not merely the mechanical revenue rebound), confirming that the Q2 compression to 18.2% was fixed-cost deleverage rather than a cost-base step-up.
3. Resumption of share repurchases against the $1.1B authorization, or consolidated covenant leverage printing below 3.0x — either would evidence that restricted-payment headroom has been restored.
4. A price in the low-to-mid $80s on unchanged fundamentals, which would take EV/2027E to roughly 17–18x and put base-case upside above 20%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE, or AVOID on two or more):**
1. Loss of Qatar or Abu Dhabi from the 2026 calendar, or any disclosure that Bahrain's fee is reduced or Sepang costs fall on F1.
2. F1 covenant leverage above 3.0x, or any disclosure of restricted-payment test failure limiting distributions to Liberty.
3. Disclosure that team payments exceed 38% of F1 revenue under the 2026 Concorde agreement, or MotoGP Adjusted OIBDA margin falling below 30% on the new team agreements.
4. Goodwill impairment at either segment, or finalization of the MotoGP purchase-price allocation that materially reduces the intangible base.
5. Disclosure of a US average-minute audience materially below the ESPN-era baseline, or sponsorship renewals at flat-to-down pricing.

## Conclusion

The business is high quality and the Q2 headline overstated the damage: a 38% F1 revenue decline was roughly two-thirds pro-rata recognition timing and the QuintEvents divestiture, with the permanent 2026 hole amounting to about one net race of event economics plus within-quarter fixed-cost deleverage. The rights are exclusive to 2110 and 2060, contracted revenue visibility is exceptional at $19.3B of remaining performance obligations, and FY2025 free cash flow converted 74% of Adjusted OIBDA. MotoGP's grid locked through 2031 within six months of the 10-K flagging a one-year participation cliff is concrete evidence that Liberty's ownership adds value there.

The price does not leave room for what remains unresolved. At ~25x trailing and ~19–21x 2027E Adjusted OIBDA with a 3.6% FCF yield, the equity already capitalizes the escalators, the sponsorship ramp and MotoGP's margin advantage, while a September decision on Qatar and Abu Dhabi sits directly ahead, the Concorde revenue-share formula on the largest cost line is undisclosed, and $4,358M of the debt sits behind subsidiary tests that have already frozen a $1.1B buyback authorization. Management paid 26–27x for MotoGP by levering F1 and drawing consolidated cash from $2,956M to $1,055M, pricing this asset class as expensive, and insider activity in the window reviewed was consistent net selling with no open-market purchases alongside a CEO compensation step-up to $39.3M. HOLD, with the low-$80s as the level at which the asymmetry becomes interesting again.
