## Executive Summary

PRA Group is a scaled principal buyer of charged-off consumer receivables operating in 18 countries, with $8.895B of estimated remaining collections (ERC) at June 30, 2026 and $4.72B of finance receivables funded by $3.76B of debt. The headline financial screens on this company are misleading in both directions. FY2025's $305.1M net loss and −29.6% ROE were caused entirely by a $412.6M non-cash goodwill write-off triggered at the September 30, 2025 interim test "based on a sustained decrease in our stock price and market capitalization" — a charge that touched no cash, no ERC, no covenant capacity, and no tangible book value (average tangible equity actually rose to $856.4M from $741.9M). Equally, Q2 2026's reported $1.51 diluted EPS and 182% consensus beat are not a cash-earnings run rate. Per Note 2 of the 10-Q, the company recorded an approximately $105M gross positive adjustment to expected future recoveries on European pools, adding $348.5M to European ERC; net of offsetting U.S. and other-market markdowns, the non-cash line contributed $74.2M against total pretax income of $88.3M.

The sharpest disconfirming datapoint for the bull case sits inside the same revenue line. "Recoveries collected in excess of forecast" — the component that represents actual cash beating plan — fell to $22.7M in Q2 2026 from $40.3M a year earlier (−44%), and to $45.4M from $56.8M for the half. So the cash-backed portion of the outperformance is shrinking while the model-estimate portion swung by $81.2M. Both movements are legitimate under the PCD/negative-allowance framework, and the European revision is corroborated by disclosed 8% cash overperformance versus internal expectations in Q1 and a decade of European vintages realizing above underwriting (2012-2015 pools now carried at 309% of purchase price versus 190% underwritten). But an estimate change of this size simultaneously raises reported earnings, the carrying value of the largest asset, and ERC-based borrowing capacity, with no external price validating any of the three.

The valuation, once adjusted, is close to fair. Tangible book is $27.04 per share ($1,045M equity less $26.9M residual goodwill, over 37.648M shares). Note 7 discloses finance receivables carried at $4,717.2M against an estimated fair value of $4,474.4M — a $242.8M gap, or $6.45 per share, computed with the company's own acquisition-pricing models. Deducting it gives ~$20.59 of fair-value-adjusted tangible book against a $20.65 price. On normalized earnings the picture is similar: stripping the abnormal portion of H1's forecast revisions leaves roughly $2.50-2.60 of annualized EPS, which reconciles closely with the next-year consensus of $2.60 (itself cut 8% over 90 days even as current-year estimates rose 35%). At ~8x normalized earnings PRAA trades in line with Encore Capital at 7.6x, the only genuinely comparable operator in the peer set. **HOLD.** The balance sheet is sound, leverage is falling, and the new $150M repurchase authorization equals ~19% of market value — but after a roughly 99% advance from February's $10.25 low and a 13.8% move in the six sessions since the Q2 print, there is no margin of safety at ~1.0x adjusted book.

## Company Overview

PRA Group buys portfolios of nonperforming loans — chiefly charged-off credit card receivables — from banks and issuers at deep discounts to face value, then collects on them over multi-year horizons. It owns the receivables rather than servicing them for a fee, so recoveries accrue to PRAA rather than to an originator. Portfolios split into "Core" (accounts not in insolvency at acquisition, the dominant business) and "Insolvency" (accounts already in bankruptcy or equivalent proceedings, a shrinking share). A small ancillary unit purchases class action claims recoveries in the U.S.

In Q4 2025 the company reorganized from one operating segment into two reportable segments — U.S. and Europe — with South America, Canada and Australia grouped as "other markets." The reorganization is itself informative because it forces disclosure of a divergence that had previously been aggregated away. At June 30, 2026, ERC was 40.4% U.S. and 54.0% Europe; European ERC of $4.81B splits into UK $1.8B, Central Europe $1.4B, Northern Europe $1.0B and Southern Europe $607.6M. Headcount fell to 2,417 from 2,615, down 16.6% year over year.

Cumulative scale is the principal asset that cannot be replicated quickly: $16.99B of lifetime purchase price deployed since 1996, generating $27.07B of cumulative collections and $36.43B of total estimated collections booked. A regulated Swedish banking subsidiary, AK Nordic AB, funds part of the European book with retail deposits ($100.5M at 6/30/26, capped at approximately $226.8M equivalent under the European revolver) — a cheap funding channel most competitors lack, though it requires holding $117.6M of Swedish treasuries and $26.5M of Finnish corporate notes.

Strategy under CEO Martin Sjölund, a 15-year company veteran who previously ran the European business, is branded "PRA 3.0" and was unveiled in March 2026. Two quantified targets are verifiable from the Q1 call: net leverage toward "the mid-2x area," and annual portfolio investment of $1.0-1.3B. Operationally the plan consolidates onto one global cloud platform and one cloud contact platform by end-2026 and converts fixed in-house collection cost into variable cost through offshoring, external debt collection agencies and the legal channel.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings composition is the central issue.** Revenue has two components of very different quality. Portfolio income — the yield accreted on each pool's negative allowance at its locked effective interest rate — is mechanical and cash-backed over time; it grew from $857.2M (FY2024) to $1,013.3M (FY2025) and $537.4M in H1 2026 (+9.2% versus $491.9M in H1 2025). "Changes in expected recoveries" contains cash overperformance and pure model revision. The trend:

| ($M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Portfolio income | 857.2 | 1,013.3 | 491.9 | 537.4 | 250.9 | 267.8 |
| Recoveries collected in excess of forecast (cash) | 156.1 | 120.7 | 56.8 | 45.4 | 40.3 | 22.7 |
| Changes in expected *future* recoveries (non-cash) | 84.7 | 55.8 | 4.4 | 95.4 | (7.0) | 74.2 |
| Total portfolio revenue | 1,098.1 | 1,189.7 | 553.1 | 678.2 | 284.2 | 364.7 |

The cash component has declined for two consecutive years and fell 44% in Q2. The non-cash component swung $81.2M in the quarter. Against Q2 pretax income of $88.3M, stripping the full net revision leaves roughly $14.1M of pretax income from yield, cash overperformance and operations. That figure is an outer-bound sensitivity rather than a forecast, because forecast revision is a recurring and required feature of this accounting model — FY2024 carried $84.7M and FY2025 $55.8M of it. A more defensible normalization deducts only the abnormal excess: H1 2026's $95.4M against a normal half-year run rate near $30-40M implies roughly $55M of excess, which takes H1 pretax from approximately $122M to $67M, annualizes near $134M pretax and $94M net, and produces ~$2.50 of EPS. That reconciles with the $2.60 next-year consensus and is the number I use for valuation.

**Adjusted profitability has been flat, not inflecting.** FY2025 adjusted net income was $72.6M ($1.84 adjusted EPS) versus $70.6M in FY2024, up 2.8%, on adjusted ROATE of 8.5% versus 9.5%. Adjusted EBITDA rose 15.6% to $1,315M. The bridge reconciles exactly: −$305.1M GAAP, less $38.4M gain on the RCB stake sale, plus $412.6M impairment, plus $3.5M tax effect = $72.6M. Note that the impairment was largely non-deductible, producing a −19.2% effective tax rate: $46.7M of tax expense on a $243.2M pretax loss, which was a real cash cost.

**The balance sheet is sound for the business model.** At June 30, 2026: borrowings $3,759M ($2,131M credit facilities, $1,641M senior notes) against $1,045M of PRA-attributable equity, or 3.60x debt/equity — down from 3.77x at year-end. Management-reported net leverage has fallen 2.82x → 2.73x → 2.71x → my independent check of ~2.67x at 6/30/26 ((3,759.4 − 132.4) ÷ 1,358.9 LTM adjusted EBITDA). The senior note stack has no maturity until February 2028 ($398M at 8.375%), then 2029 ($350M at 5.00%), 2030 ($550M at 8.875%) and 2032 (€300M at 6.250%). The euro issue in September 2025 was the first of its kind for PRAA and hedges euro-denominated assets naturally; its 6.25% coupon versus the 8.375%/8.875% dollar notes suggests refinancing the 2028s and 2030s could be accretive, contingent on 2027-2029 spreads. Sixty percent of debt is fixed or swapped; a ±50bp move shifts 12-month interest expense by roughly $7.8M. Liquidity is $132.4M cash — of which $119.1M is offshore under indefinite reinvestment, a genuine constraint on domestic deployment — plus $998.5M of facility availability.

**Reported negative operating cash flow is a presentation artifact, not burn.** Under the PCD model only the portfolio-income portion of collections runs through operating cash flow; the larger portion applied against the negative allowance is an investing inflow. H1 2026: operating cash used $(11.3)M, plus $580.7M of recoveries applied to finance receivables, less $521.7M of purchases and $3.8M of capex, gives roughly $43.9M of discretionary free cash after fully replacing the book. That figure should not be treated as a run rate — purchases were deliberately cut 18.9%, cash taxes were unusually low at $8.3M, and the company's own replenishment requirement is approximately $1.0B annually simply to hold ERC flat. Cash interest paid was $127.0M.

A genuinely useful liquidity disclosure: FY2025's $1.2B of purchases generated only $196.6M of FY2025 collections, or 9.3% of the total. PRAA could suspend purchasing entirely and lose under 10% of near-term cash — a strong defense in stress, at the cost of future runway.

## Growth Analysis

Aggregate growth is modest and geographically bifurcated. ERC grew 7.2% year over year to a record $8.895B, and cash collections have compounded from $1.66B (FY2023) to $1.87B to $2.11B, with $1.11B in H1 2026 and Q2 collections of $558.5M (+4.2%), described as exceeding internal expectations. Portfolio income grew 6.7% in Q2.

The forward input, however, is contracting where it matters. Total purchases fell 14.2% in FY2025 and 18.9% in H1 2026 to $517.4M. The composition:

| Purchases | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Q2 2026 YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. | $795.8M | $590.1M | $343.3M | $228.0M | −40.0% |
| Europe | $508.3M | $518.8M | $260.5M | $266.0M | +18.5% |
| Other | $103.7M | $99.6M | $34.5M | $23.5M | −31.9% |

H1's $517M annualizes to $1.03B, essentially the stated $1.0B maintenance replenishment rate. PRAA is buying to hold ERC flat, not to grow it. The forward-flow pipeline fell from $378.0M of estimated 12-month purchase price at year-end to $218.7M at June 30 — a 42% decline. That figure must be read precisely: the 10-Q states the minimum contractual purchase obligation under forward flow agreements "was not significant," very few agreements establish a minimum, and totals vary with renewal timing. It is a soft indicator, and it sits alongside management's opposite characterization of supply (U.S. card balances near $1.1 trillion with charge-off rates above 4%, and increased European supply). Unresolved, and worth monitoring rather than concluding on.

Segment economics explain the reallocation. Vintage marks at June 30 show U.S. Core 2021 carried at 177% of purchase price versus 191% underwritten, 2022 at 156% versus 164%, and 2023 at 186% versus 191%, with the 10-Q noting Q2 decreases in forecasts on the 2022-2025 U.S. Core pools and underperformance on 2022-2024. European Core marks run the other way across nearly every vintage. U.S. adjusted segment operating income was $17.3M in Q2 2026 versus $17.5M — flat — after a roughly 40% cut in onshore agent headcount during 2025, 115+ corporate role eliminations, call-center consolidation and offshoring. Europe's adjusted segment operating income went from $70.7M to $168.1M, but the bulk of that gain is the forecast revision.

Two mitigating facts on the U.S. side deserve equal weight. H1 U.S. segment income was up 36.2% ($47.5M versus $34.9M), and Q2 absorbed $4.9M of reorganization costs ($2.0M severance, $3.0M real-estate impairment) that alone more than account for the flat result. And the U.S. 2024 and 2025 vintages are marked *up* — 231% versus 211% original and 218% versus 216% — implying the underwriting problem was specific to the 2021-2023 overpriced window rather than permanent franchise damage. The 2026-2027 seasoning of those vintages will settle which reading is right.

The channel shift is the other growth mechanism. Legal collections rose from 38.7% of U.S. Core collections in FY2023 to 48.2% in FY2025 and roughly 54% in H1 2026; U.S. legal collections grew 26.1% in Q2 to $150.1M while call-center and other collections fell $15.3M (−11.3%). Legal collection costs are front-loaded, rising 42.3% year over year in H1 to $101.0M, which is why the adjusted cash efficiency ratio has plateaued (61.2% in Q2 2026 versus 62.4% a year earlier, but 61.5% versus 61.7% for the half) after improving from 58.0% in FY2023.

The peer table shows a median P/E of 14.0x, but that median blends pawn (EZPW 14.0x), lease-to-own (PRG 13.9x), small-dollar installment (WRLD 23.2x) and student loan servicing (NAVI, negative) — businesses with materially different balance sheets and asset accounting. The only genuine comparable is Encore Capital at 7.6x. On normalized earnings PRAA is at roughly 8x, in line. Encore's marginally lower multiple does not establish PRAA as cheap.

The residual-income framing is the most decisive. A leveraged NPL buyer earning 8.5-9.5% on tangible equity, with 3.6x debt/equity and a self-identified competitive disadvantage from that leverage, plausibly carries a cost of equity near 11%. At 9% ROATE and 3% long-run growth, justified price-to-tangible-book is (9−3)/(11−3) ≈ 0.75x, which on $27.04 of tangible book gives about $20.30. The stock is trading at that value. If PRA 3.0 execution lifts ROATE toward 11.5% — through the [unverified — actual: N/A] savings wave, the end-2026 platform consolidation, mix shift to Europe and buybacks below book — justified P/TBV rises to roughly 1.06x, or $28.70. If European revisions partially reverse and U.S. vintages continue to deteriorate, taking ROATE to 6%, justified P/TBV falls to about 0.38x, or $10.30 — close to February's $10.25 low.

That framework yields **base $22, bull $28.50, bear $12.50**: +6.5%, +38.0%, −39.5% from $20.65. Probability-weighting at 55/25/20 gives roughly +6% expected return — below an 11% cost of equity, above a loss.

The single analyst target of $26.00 (low = high = mean = median) implies +26%. On a stock with one full-year estimate and two quarterly estimators, that target carries limited information and cannot serve as independent confirmation.

**On the estimate-revision signal.** Current-quarter estimates rose 35.6% and current-year 35.2% over 90 days. Ordinarily revisions of that magnitude alongside a cheap multiple are a strong forward indicator. Here the signal is materially compromised: the revision was driven by a non-cash model change, only one to two analysts contribute, and the same consensus cut next-year EPS by 8%. Consensus is itself saying the current-year strength does not persist. I weight the revision as confirming that Europe is genuinely overperforming, and discount it as evidence of a durable earnings inflection.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names no competitors and provides no share data, so relative positioning must be inferred from scale and structure. PRAA is one of very few operators with material presence in both the U.S. and Europe, with $16.99B cumulative deployed capital and vintage-level performance data back to 1996. Licensing and bonding requirements across jurisdictions raise entry costs. The U.S. market is described in the filing as having regulatory complexity, a stable competitive landscape, a small number of sellers, and a tendency toward forward-flow contracting; Europe as more fragmented, more competitive, with more sellers and historically spot-based sales.

Two features are eroding rather than widening the moat. First, PRAA is deliberately de-integrating: moving from in-house collection toward external debt collection agencies, offshore business-process outsourcing and third-party attorney networks. That converts fixed cost to variable and adds flexibility, but external DCA and offshore BPO capacity is available to competitors, and Sjölund noted external DCAs in the U.S. were "not something we really did" two years ago — a new capability, not a proprietary one. Second, the 10-K explicitly lists among the consequences of its indebtedness a "competitive disadvantage compared to less leveraged competitors." In an auction business where the winning bid is a function of required return, cost of capital is a direct competitive input.

The most damning external benchmark comes from PRAA's own proxy: over the five-year pay-versus-performance window, $100 invested in PRAA became $45 while the compensation committee's peer group returned $158. That establishes roughly 113 points of cumulative relative equity underperformance. It does not by itself establish inferior operating performance, since part of the gap is multiple compression on a leveraged balance sheet, and the peer set's composition is undisclosed. But it is the empirical record of the same "cheap on book, cheap on earnings" argument being made today.

Aggregate trajectory: Europe improving, the U.S. shrinking, mix shifting toward the better franchise. Net roughly stable, with the U.S. share of ERC having slipped to 40.4%.

**Macro cross-current.** Elevated U.S. card balances and >4% charge-off rates are supportive of supply, and management's counter-cyclical argument — that charge-offs rise by a larger factor than collections fall — is grounded in thirty years of data across events including the war in Ukraine, Brexit and COVID. But it cuts against the filing's own risk language that prolonged downturns pressure both collections and the value of existing portfolios. The honest synthesis is that mild stress helps a well-capitalized buyer with a lag, while severe stress compresses collections on the existing $4.7B book immediately. The 2021-2022 U.S. vintages, bought in the most favorable-looking supply environment of the past decade, are precisely the ones now marked below underwriting.

## Risk Assessment

**Estimate/model risk is the dominant exposure, and it is a newly added risk factor.** The FY2025 10-K added a standalone factor on reliance on internally developed models, covering design, "calibration, validation or governance processes," and third-party data dependency. The asset ($4.72B), the revenue recognition (portfolio income accretes at rates derived from ERC), and the borrowing base (advance rates of 35-55% of applicable ERC; a 40% maximum ERC ratio in the European revolver) are all functions of one internally generated forecast with no market price validating it. The Q2 revision — justified in part by "enhancements made to our analytical processes and forecasting capabilities" — added $348.5M to European ERC and therefore also expanded borrowing capacity. The mechanism is legitimate and fully disclosed; the concentration of earnings, asset and liquidity risk into a single estimate is the point.

**Legal-channel concentration is the second, also newly disclosed.** With litigation now producing roughly 54% of U.S. Core collections, the new risk factor names the failure modes explicitly: courts in certain jurisdictions requiring account statements attached to pleadings, with claims deniable if documents cannot be produced; reduced judicial willingness to grant judgments; changed filing requirements; court closures; statute-of-limitations constraints. PRAA has concentrated its largest segment's cash generation in the channel most exposed to judicial and state-regulatory discretion while actively reducing the alternative channel (call-center collections −11.3% in Q2).

**Regulatory.** A live April 2023 CFPB consent order remains in force with the compliance plan still being executed; the company warns there is no assurance it will satisfy the CFPB on every requirement, against disclosed penalties of $7,000 to over $1.4 million per day depending on gravity. Reduced federal enforcement intensity under the current administration is a modest offset, but state attorneys general retain Dodd-Frank Title X authority. Q2's Note 12 reports no material developments in the disclosed proceeding.

**Execution.** Three further new risk factors cover U.S. cost-savings realization, Asia offshoring, and concurrent systems change management — the latter warning that multiple simultaneous system enhancements "rely heavily on the same internal resources," raising the risk of "system instability, data integrity issues or failures in key controls." A 40% agent headcount reduction, 115+ corporate eliminations, call-center consolidation, offshoring, a new Charlotte talent hub and a global cloud migration targeted for end-2026 are all running at once.

**Balance sheet and structural.** $3.76B of debt against $1.05B of equity, with $247.7M of estimated interest, fees and principal due over the next 12 months. Mitigants are real: no maturities until February 2028, covenant compliance confirmed at both recent period-ends, $998.5M availability, 60% fixed or hedged, and the European revolver extended to April 2031. That extension came with the maximum ERC ratio cut from 45.0% to 40.0% — lenders granting duration in exchange for less leverage headroom, though PRAA may have volunteered the tighter ratio given its own lower leverage target. The $242.8M receivables fair-value shortfall equals roughly 23% of the equity base; it is a discount-rate artifact rather than an impairment trigger, and it narrowed $51M over the half, but it is the correct adjustment for anyone anchoring on price-to-book.

**FX.** Accumulated other comprehensive loss stands at $(293.7)M, almost entirely translation. Translation added $175.3M to OCI in H1 2025 and subtracted $8.6M in H1 2026 — the volatility is material to reported equity, and 43% of FY2025 portfolio income was international.

**Tax.** Q2's effective rate of 33.3% (29.6% H1) exceeded management's guided mid-to-high-20s range, a direct EPS headwind on a line that had been guided lower.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 58.8% is essentially in line with one-year realized volatility of 59.6%, so the market is not paying up for uncertainty in absolute terms. The term structure is in backwardation (58.8% at one week versus 42.9% at six months), which typically indicates near-term event risk, but two features argue against reading it as downside positioning: put/call open-interest ratio is 0.03 and skew is −19.0% (call-heavy), which is consistent with post-earnings speculative call demand rather than hedging. The chain contains only 23 contracts across three expirations, so the ±9.2% implied move on the nearest-expiry tenor most likely reflects illiquidity rather than an identified catalyst. Short interest of 2.6% of float at 2.3 days to cover shows no crowded bearish position and no squeeze dynamic in either direction. Net read: low information content, mildly inconsistent with a bearish thesis, insufficient to move the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $28.50 (+38%)

Europe is a structurally better franchise than the market credits, and the Q2 revision is a belated recognition of realized cash rather than an accounting stretch: European pools have beaten internal expectations by 8%, 2012-2015 vintages have compounded to 309% of purchase price against 190% underwritten, and Europe now represents 54% of ERC. The U.S. problem is confined to the 2021-2023 competitive window; 2024 and 2025 vintages are already marked above underwriting (231% versus 211%, 218% versus 216%), and once the cost-savings wave and the end-2026 platform consolidation land, U.S. segment income inflects from a lower cost base. Leverage is already near the mid-2x target, no debt matures for eighteen months, and the 8.375%/8.875% 2028/2030 notes can be refinanced into a spread environment where the company's own euro paper prints at 6.25%. The $150M authorization, at ~19% of market capitalization and against a track record of repurchasing below book at $15.39 and $17.63 averages, retires shares at a discount to intrinsic value; full execution would take the count from 37.6M toward 30.3M. ROATE reaches 11-12%, justifying roughly 1.06x tangible book.

### Bear Case — $12.50 (−39%)

The Q2 earnings inflection was manufactured by a model recalibration, and the underlying cash trend is deteriorating: recoveries collected in excess of forecast fell 44% year over year in Q2 and 20% for the half, the second consecutive year of decline. When "changes in expected future recoveries" normalizes toward the FY2025 level of $55.8M, EPS reverts to $2.50-2.60 and the stock's 6.1x current-year multiple becomes 8x on a business earning under 9% on tangible equity. Consensus already reflects this by cutting next-year estimates 8%. Meanwhile the U.S. franchise is shrinking — purchases down 40% in Q2, ERC share down to 40.4%, segment income flat despite a 40% headcount cut — and its remaining cash generation is 54% dependent on court judgments, exposed to documentation-adequacy rulings, judicial discretion and a live CFPB consent order whose compliance plan is unfinished. The forward-flow pipeline is down 42%, purchases are running only at the maintenance replenishment rate, and any downward European revision would reverse earnings, asset carrying value and borrowing capacity simultaneously. Adjusted for the $242.8M receivables fair-value shortfall, the equity is already at 1.0x tangible book — there is no asset-value floor beneath current prices, and the five-year record (TSR index 45 versus peer 158) is the base rate for this setup.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case: $22.00 (+6.5% from $20.65).** 8.5x normalized EPS of $2.60, equivalently 0.81x tangible book of $27.04 on ROATE holding near 9%. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios above.

**Bull case: $28.50 (+38%).** 1.06x tangible book on ROATE reaching 11.5%, equivalently 11x normalized $2.60.

**Bear case: $12.50 (−39.5%).** 0.46x tangible book on ROATE compressing to 6%, near the February low of $10.25.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 report in early November 2026 and confirmation at the FY2026 10-K in Q1 2027 (when the U.S. 2024-2026 vintage marks and the full-year forecast-revision total are both disclosed).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters in which U.S. adjusted segment operating income grows more than 15% year over year *and* the U.S. 2024-2026 Core vintages hold current PPMs of 231%/218%/208% or better. This would confirm the 2021-2023 window was idiosyncratic.
2. Buyback executed at above a $50M half-year pace at prices below tangible book — materially faster than the historical $20M/year cadence — which would convert the authorization from a signal into realized per-share accretion.
3. Price at or below $17.00 with fundamentals intact: approximately 0.63x tangible book, 0.83x fair-value-adjusted book, and 6.5x normalized EPS, which restores a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A net negative "changes in expected future recoveries" figure in any quarter, or any downward European revision reversing part of the $348.5M ERC addition. This would validate the model-risk bear case directly.
2. Any adverse CFPB or state attorney-general action, or an adverse judicial ruling on documentation standards in a material U.S. state, given that 54% of U.S. Core collections now run through the legal channel.
3. Net leverage rising back above 3.0x, or the forward-flow pipeline falling further while half-year purchases stay below $500M — the combination that would put ERC into contraction.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings, expected early November 2026. Existing owners hold. Prospective buyers wait for the mid-to-high $17s, where the stock traded eight sessions ago, or for two quarters of evidence that cash-backed earnings are growing independent of forecast revisions.

## Conclusion

PRA Group is neither the distressed entity the standardized screens describe nor the six-times-earnings compounder the headline multiple implies. The screens are wrong: an Altman Z-Score of 0.63 is a category error applied to a leveraged specialty-finance balance sheet, and the FY2025 loss reflected a non-cash write-off triggered by the share price itself, which left tangible book untouched and covenant capacity intact. Leverage has fallen for four consecutive quarters to roughly 2.67x net debt/EBITDA, near management's own target; nothing matures until February 2028; and $998.5M of facility capacity plus the ability to halt purchasing at the cost of only 9% of near-term collections gives real defensive optionality.

The bull case, however, requires the Q2 earnings step-up to be durable, and the evidence there is split. The European revision is corroborated by genuine cash overperformance and a decade of favorable vintage realization, but it is still a model change that lifted earnings, asset carrying value and borrowing base together, while the cash-backed component of the same revenue line fell 44% year over year. The U.S. franchise is being managed down: purchases off 40%, segment income flat after a 40% headcount cut, and remaining cash generation concentrated in a litigation channel with newly disclosed documentation and judicial-discretion vulnerabilities under a live consent order. Compensation is measured on adjusted EBITDA and adjusted net income, neither of which captures underwriting quality or the cost of $3.76B of debt — the two variables that produced a five-year TSR index of 45 against a peer group's 158.

At $20.65 the stock trades at approximately 8x normalized earnings, in line with its only true comparable, and at essentially 1.0x tangible book once the company's own $242.8M receivables fair-value shortfall is deducted. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly 6% against a −39% bear scenario does not clear the bar for new capital. **HOLD.** The $150M authorization and the cost programme give management the tools to earn a re-rating; the November print will show whether the earnings that drove the last 14% of price appreciation were cash.
