**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Bancorp is the holding company for First Bank, a ~$12.7B-asset Carolina community bank operating 113 branches across some of the fastest-growing MSAs in the Southeast (Charlotte, Raleigh-Durham, Charleston, Greensboro, Wilmington). The investment story over the past 18 months is a net-interest-margin recovery: NIM bottomed at 2.89% in 2024, climbed through 2025 to a 3.40% full-year figure, and reached 3.67% in Q1 2026. Q1 2026 net income of $46.7M ($1.13 diluted EPS) was up 28% year-over-year and ran at roughly a 1.4% ROA, versus a depressed 0.63% ROA in 2024 and 0.89% for full-year 2025. The franchise has a genuinely superior funding base — 32% noninterest-bearing deposits, time deposits only 8% of the book, total deposit cost down to 1.32% — and pristine credit (NPAs 0.32% of assets, net charge-offs 0.06-0.10%).

The recovery is real but mostly engineered and mostly realized. Management deliberately sold roughly $1B+ of 1.6-1.8% bonds in 2024-2025, crystallizing $109.6M of pre-tax securities losses, and reinvested into 4.1-4.4% paper. That trade is the primary driver of the +78bp NIM expansion. It worked: underlying pre-provision earnings power is climbing, and the franchise is normalizing toward its historical ~1.3% ROA potential. The corollary is that the easy gains are behind, the balance sheet is now only modestly asset-sensitive into a Fed that may cut once more in 2026, and the bank still carries roughly $268M of unrealized losses (AFS + HTM) it has not earned back, concentrated in long-dated, ~2.1%-yielding municipals.

At $60.29 the stock trades at 20.6x trailing EPS, a ~72% premium to the regional-bank peer median of 12.0x, and sits below the entire analyst target range of $64-68. Near-term estimate revisions have turned modestly negative (current-quarter -0.9% / 30d, next-year -1.9% / 30d), multiple directors have sold stock into the recovery with zero open-market buying, and an unexplained auditor change plus a cluster of board/management departures warrant caution. The fundamental trajectory is positive enough to hold for the continued ROA normalization and M&A optionality, but the valuation has run ahead of the visible earnings power, and the risk/reward at current price does not support adding. HOLD.

## Company Overview

First Bancorp, headquartered in Southern Pines, NC, operates as a single-segment community and commercial bank through First Bank. As of year-end 2025 the bank held $12.7B in total assets, $8.7B in loans, and $10.7B in deposits across 113 branches in North Carolina and South Carolina, with ~1,353 FTEs. Its markets are concentrated in Carolina metros: Charlotte, the Research Triangle, the Piedmont Triad, Wilmington, Asheville, and Greenville-Spartanburg/Charleston.

The business model combines relationship-based commercial and consumer banking with mortgage lending, a national SBA 7(a) franchise, accounts-receivable financing, and investment advisory services. The loan book is real-estate-centric (~88% secured by real estate), with non-owner-occupied CRE the largest category at 33%, followed by residential 1-4 family at 20% and owner-occupied CRE at 15%.

First Bancorp has historically been a serial acquirer — GrandSouth Bancorporation closed in January 2023, preceded by a string of earlier deals — leaving $478.8M of goodwill (~29% of equity) on the balance sheet. No acquisitions have closed since GrandSouth; 2024-2025 was an organic-growth and integration phase, with management controlling headcount and expenses while deposit and loan growth came entirely from existing markets.

## Financial Analysis

**The margin inflection is the core financial fact.** Net interest income rose from $332.3M (2024) to $398.2M (2025), and Q1 2026 NII of $107.1M was up 15.4% year-over-year. Two forces drove the NIM progression. First, Fed rate cuts allowed the deposit base to reprice down (money-market cost fell ~38bps year-over-year), and the granular, low-time-deposit mix meant funding costs fell faster than asset yields. Second, the securities loss-earnback transactions swapped ~1.6% bonds into ~4.1-4.4% paper.

**Earnings recovery, with the caveat that GAAP is distorted.** FY2025 net income was $111.0M ($2.68 diluted EPS), up from $76.2M ($1.84) in 2024. Both years were distorted by the securities restructurings — FY2025 noninterest income was actually negative $7.9M because of the $71.6M realized loss. Stripping that out, pre-provision earnings power has climbed steadily. The Q1 2026 ~1.4% ROA run-rate is the cleanest read on normalized earnings power, and it materially exceeds the FY2025 quarterly cadence.

**Profitability still below the historical peak.** ROE of 7.16% (FY2025) remains well short of the 1.39% ROA / 13.4% ROE the bank posted in 2022. The gap reflects the goodwill-heavy balance sheet and the securities drag. The Q1 2026 run-rate suggests normalization toward ~1.3% ROA is underway, the central plank of the constructive case.

**Capital and credit are strong.** CET1 of 14.13%, total risk-based 16.12%, leverage 11.46% (Q1'26) — all more than 200bps above well-capitalized thresholds. TCE/TA improved sharply to 9.63% from 8.22% a year earlier as AOCI losses receded. ACL/loans is 1.42% (340% coverage of nonaccruals). The one soft spot is classified loans up 12.7% QoQ to $66.0M, but that is off a tiny base.

**Capital allocation is conservative and shareholder-friendly.** The dividend was raised to $0.24/quarter in Q1'26 from $0.22; the bank bought back 93k shares in Q1'26 with $34.9M remaining on a $40M authorization, and redeemed $18M of sub debt in 2025. The earnings beat track record is consistent — four straight beats (Q1'26 +2.1%, Q4'25 +12.2%, Q3'25 +7.7%, Q2'25 +4.6%) — supporting reasonable confidence in near-term estimates.

The structured XBRL feed returned stale/mislabeled figures (e.g., $237.9M "total assets" for 2023); the 10-K and 10-Q primary filings are authoritative and used throughout. Actual 2025 total assets are $12.7B.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is ROA-and-NIM normalization plus deposit/loan compounding in strong markets, with M&A as embedded optionality. Loans reached $8.8B and deposits $11.0B in Q1'26 (+2.5% QoQ, all organic), and the Carolina footprint provides above-average underlying market growth.

The most powerful leg of the growth — the NIM expansion — is largely realized. The securities loss-earnback trades have been executed (twice, $38M in 2024 + $71.6M in 2025), and the balance sheet is now only modestly asset-sensitive (+200bp = +5.5% NII; -200bp = -5.2% NII). With the FOMC signaling at most one cut in 2026, the deposit-repricing tailwind is mostly captured. From here, NIM is more likely stable than sharply rising, which means forward growth depends on (a) further ROA normalization as the securities drag rolls off the comparison, (b) organic balance-sheet growth, and (c) a return to M&A given the excess capital.

Estimate revisions support the inflection thesis only partially. Over 90 days, current-year EPS estimates rose +1.4% (to $4.66) and current-quarter +1.0%, but the most recent 30 days show broad softening: current quarter -0.9%, next quarter -0.8%, current year -0.7%, next year -1.9%. The Zacks upgrade to Buy in June 2026 reflected the 90-day positive revision momentum, but the 30-day deceleration is a flag — this does not meet the threshold of a strong, accelerating estimate-revision signal that would mark a genuine earnings inflection. It is a low-single-digit grind. Next-year consensus of $4.98 implies ~7% EPS growth off the $4.66 current-year base, consistent with a normalizing community bank rather than a high-growth story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $60.29, FBNC trades at 20.6x trailing EPS of $2.68, but trailing EPS is depressed by the FY2025 securities loss, so the cleaner read is the forward multiple. On current-year consensus of $4.66, the stock trades at ~12.9x; on next-year $4.98, ~12.1x. Those forward multiples are reasonable for a normalizing bank, and the cycle-adjusted view is more flattering than the trailing 20.6x suggests.

The problem is relative valuation. The regional-bank peer median P/E is 12.0x (TRMK 12.0, CUBI 9.8, NBTB 13.5, TBBK 11.0, BBT 22.1). On trailing earnings FBNC is at a ~72% premium; on forward earnings it sits roughly in line with peers at ~12-13x. The forward multiple is not stretched, but it is also not cheap — the market is already paying a fair price for the normalization. The 1.6% dividend yield is below the peer median of 2.2%, removing yield support as a valuation cushion.

Analyst targets bracket $64-68 (mean $66.35, median $66.00), placing the stock ~6-13% below the consensus range. On a low-momentum bank with decelerating 30-day revisions and a forward P/E already in line with peers, those targets are not floors — the inflection-stock guidance that treats consensus targets as lagging applies to cheap names with accelerating estimates, which FBNC is not. The targets sit modestly above the current price, implying a fair amount of the normalization is already priced.

A reasonable base case applies ~13x to next-year EPS of $4.98, yielding ~$65, in line with consensus and ~8% above the current price. That is a fair-value zone, not a mispricing. The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside; the upside to base case roughly matches the downside if normalization stalls or the embedded securities losses force a defensive posture.

## Competitive Landscape

The franchise's structural advantage is its deposit base. With 32% noninterest-bearing checking, time deposits at only 8% of deposits, brokered deposits negligible ($4.9M), and total deposit cost of 1.32%, First Bancorp funds itself more cheaply and stickily than most deposit-hungry peers. This is the genuine moat, and it is why the bank captured the NIM recovery so strongly when the Fed began cutting.

The bank competes in strong demographic markets against money-center banks, super-regionals (Truist), credit unions, and fintechs. Its edge is relationship banking and the core deposit franchise rather than scale or technology. The NIM compression of 2023-2024 was sector-wide; the recovery is also sector-wide as banks reprice deposits — FBNC is participating strongly and now sits above the typical 3.3-3.5% community-bank NIM, which argues its execution has been better than average.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the funding side, offset by a real-estate-heavy loan mix that limits differentiation on the asset side. A new risk-factor disclosure around stablecoins and deposit disintermediation was added in the Q1 2026 10-Q — forward-looking and not yet material, but a vector worth monitoring for any deposit-rich community bank.

## Risk Assessment

**Embedded securities losses (the largest concrete risk).** Despite the loss-earnback restructurings, FBNC carries ~$197.7M of unrealized AFS losses (in AOCI) and ~$70.6M of unrecognized HTM losses (fair value $440.9M vs. $511.4M book) as of Q1'26. The HTM book is long-dated municipals (8.5-year weighted maturity) yielding only ~2.1%. These are rate-driven, not credit-driven, and crystallize only on forced sale, but ~$268M of combined unrealized losses against $1.2B of tangible common equity is a real constraint on flexibility and a tail risk if rates back up.

**CRE concentration.** ~88% real-estate-secured, 33% non-owner-occupied CRE. Management notes office is only ~6.5% of loans (largest single office loan $33M, mostly non-metro) and stresses no geographic/industry concentration, but the sector backdrop — high CRE prices relative to fundamentals and elevated delinquencies — keeps this a monitored exposure.

**Hurricane Helene.** Western NC/SC markets took a $13.0M provision in 2024, $11.1M of which was reversed in 2025 as impacts proved manageable. Largely de-risked, with a residual $1.9M ACL on ~$258M of consumer loans in the impacted path.

**Uninsured deposits.** ~$4.5B (≈41%) of deposits are uninsured; including collateralized deposits, ~66% are insured/collateralized. The granular, relationship-based base mitigates flight risk, and liquidity is robust (34% total liquidity ratio including $2.5B of lines plus $1.9B brokered capacity), but this remains a post-2023 structural vulnerability.

**Goodwill.** $478.8M (~29% of equity); annual test passed, no impairment to date. A sustained stock decline or performance deterioration could trigger a charge.

**Governance.** An auditor change (March 2025) plus four separate director/officer (5.02) 8-Ks in roughly six months is more turnover than typical for a community bank. EDGAR full text for these specific filings was inaccessible, so the reasons — routine retirement vs. disagreement — are unconfirmed. The combination warrants follow-up and is the basis for a cautious management and risk read until resolved.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options data is internally inconsistent and should not be over-read. The 1-week ATM IV of 76.6% against 28.0% one-year historical vol implies a ±8.2% move, and the term structure shows steep backwardation (76.6% near vs. 26.7% at three months). However, the ATM IV collapsed from 163.2% to 76.6% in a single overnight reading, the put/call OI ratio is very low at 0.08 (call-heavy), and the -184.7% put/call skew points to call-side positioning rather than downside hedging. The cleaner one-month read is a benign 21.7% IV. These thin-options artifacts are non-informative as a directional signal; the call-heavy skew, if anything, is mildly inconsistent with a stress event. Short interest is moderate at 5.5% of float with 9.6 days to cover — no squeeze setup and no crowded-bear positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$70-73).** ROA normalizes to ~1.3% as the securities drag rolls off the year-over-year comparison, NIM holds in the high-3.60s, organic deposit and loan growth compounds in strong Carolina markets, and the bank redeploys excess capital into an accretive acquisition. Apply ~14-15x to a normalized EPS approaching $5.20-5.40 and the stock reaches the low-$70s. The clean credit book, sticky low-cost deposits, and proven earnings-beat cadence support the upper end.

**Bear Case (~$48-50, ~-18-20%).** Rates back up, marking the embedded securities losses larger and pressuring tangible book; the modestly asset-sensitive balance sheet means a deeper-than-expected Fed easing cycle compresses the NIM gains; CRE credit normalizes off the current pristine base; and the multiple compresses toward the 12.0x peer median on next-year EPS, which on a stalled ~$4.70 base implies ~$48-50. Net insider selling and the unexplained governance turnover would prove to have been the informed signal.

**Base Case (~$65, ~+8%).** ROA normalization continues but at a measured pace, NIM stabilizes rather than re-expands, EPS grows mid-single-digits to ~$4.98 next year, and the stock holds a ~13x forward multiple, reaching roughly consensus. The most likely path leaves modest upside that does not clear the bar for adding capital, given the offsetting risks.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is the continued ROA normalization from ~0.89% toward ~1.3% as the securities-loss drag rolls off, plus optionality from a return to M&A on excess capital. This is a specific, measurable trajectory — Q1'26's ~1.4% ROA run-rate already demonstrates it — but the valuation has caught up to the visible earnings power, capping the asymmetry.

- **Base case target:** ~$65 (13x next-year EPS of $4.98), ~8% above current.
- **Bull case target:** ~$70-73 (14-15x normalized EPS approaching $5.20-5.40), ~16-21% above current.
- **Bear case target:** ~$48-50 (12x stalled ~$4.70 EPS), ~18-20% below current.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with quarterly earnings (next ~July 2026) the natural checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. ROA confirmed sustainably above 1.3% for two consecutive quarters with NIM holding ≥3.65%, demonstrating the normalization is durable rather than a peak.
2. A pullback to the low-$50s (forward P/E below ~11x next-year EPS), restoring asymmetric upside.
3. Clustered open-market insider buying, or resolution of the auditor/board-turnover questions in a benign direction, reversing the management caution.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Net charge-offs or NPAs rising materially (NPAs above ~0.75% of assets) or classified loans continuing to climb at double-digit QoQ rates, signaling CRE credit deterioration.
2. A goodwill impairment charge, or a forced realization of HTM securities losses.
3. Adverse disclosure on the auditor change or board departures (disagreement rather than routine), or accelerating estimate cuts (current-year revisions turning negative beyond ~3-4%).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (~July 2026), and any 8-K clarifying the governance items.

## Conclusion

First Bancorp is a well-run, well-capitalized Carolina community bank with a superior low-cost deposit franchise and pristine credit, executing a real NIM-driven earnings recovery that is normalizing ROA toward its historical ~1.3% potential. The fundamental trajectory is positive, and the Q1'26 ROA run-rate plus four consecutive earnings beats give reasonable confidence in the normalization path.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because the easy part of the recovery — the engineered NIM expansion from the securities loss-earnback trades — is largely behind, the balance sheet is no longer strongly positioned to benefit from further rate moves, and the stock already trades at a forward multiple in line with peers, below the analyst target range, with the dividend yield offering no valuation cushion. Layer on the embedded unrealized securities losses, net insider selling into the rally with zero open-market buying, decelerating 30-day estimate revisions, and an unexplained auditor change alongside elevated board/management turnover, and the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. Hold for the continued normalization and M&A optionality; revisit as a BUY on a pullback into the low-$50s or on confirmation that sustained 1.3%+ ROA is durable.
