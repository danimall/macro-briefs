## Executive Summary

Ares Capital is the largest publicly traded business development company, with $29.35B of investments at fair value against $13.89B of equity at June 30, 2026. The investment case has always rested on two pillars: a scale-driven funding advantage on the liability side, and a granular, directly originated middle-market loan book on the asset side. The first pillar is intact and arguably strengthening. The second is showing measurable wear.

Three facts define the current setup. First, net asset value per share has declined in three consecutive quarters, from a $20.01 peak in Q3 2025 to $19.35 at June 30, 2026, a $0.66 (3.3%) erosion with share count flat at 718M. The portfolio's fair value moved from $235M *above* amortized cost at December 31, 2025 to $326M *below* cost at June 30, 2026 — a $561M adverse swing in six months, of which roughly 82% sits in the non-affiliate loan book rather than in one or two identifiable blowups. Second, first-half dividend coverage depends on an accounting artifact: reported six-month NII of $757M ($1.054/share) includes an $82M credit from reversal of the capital gains incentive fee accrual, itself mechanically *caused* by the unrealized depreciation. Excluding it, core NII was approximately $675M, or $0.94/share, against $0.96 declared. Third, the stock has rallied 7.7% from the $18.61 close ARCC disclosed for July 23, 2026 to the current $20.055, converting a 3.8% discount to NAV into a 3.6% premium. The entry price today is materially worse than it was three weeks ago on unchanged fundamentals.

Against that, the offsets are substantive rather than cosmetic. Non-accruals remain 1% of investments at fair value. Realized results for the half were *positive* (+$101M net realized gains). Funding is exceptional: $6.7B of undrawn committed capacity across a $5,481M revolver extended to 2031 at a 1.53% weighted spread, three bank funding facilities, twelve unsecured note series, three CLOs and a new $1.0B commercial paper program, with a 4.8% weighted average rate and 4.1-year weighted maturity. The July 2026 refinancing of the ADL CLO 1 securitization achieved roughly a 40bp spread improvement (blended SOFR+1.86% into SOFR+1.46%/1.70%/1.90% tranches) — verifiable evidence that securitization markets remain open to ARCC collateral on improving terms. And insiders bought: the CEO, CFO, COO and a director all made open-market purchases in February 2026 at $19.13–$19.29, below the then-prevailing $19.94 NAV, with no open-market officer or director sales in the record since December 2021.

The result is a stock priced approximately at fair value for its risk profile. A 9.6% distribution against roughly break-even core coverage, a premium to a declining book, and a credit cycle whose direction in ARCC's two largest exposure buckets is currently adverse do not add up to an attractive entry. They also do not add up to an exit for holders collecting the yield.

## Company Overview

ARCC is an externally managed closed-end fund electing BDC status under the 1940 Act and RIC treatment for tax. It has no employees; Ares Capital Management LLC advises it for a base management fee, an income based fee on net investment income above a hurdle, and a capital gains incentive fee on cumulative realized and unrealized gains. Ares Operations LLC provides administration.

The portfolio is private, directly originated credit to U.S. middle-market borrowers, with an accumulated equity and PIK-preferred tail. Expressed as a share of the $29.35B portfolio, the largest industry exposures are Software & Services at approximately 22.0%, Financial Services 13.4%, Health Care Equipment & Services 9.9%, and Commercial & Professional Services 9.6%. (The 10-Q presents these as percentages of *net* assets — 46.45%, 28.34%, 20.84% and 20.25% respectively — which sum above 100% because the company runs 1.15x debt/equity. The two conventions must not be conflated.)

Two positions distinguish ARCC from a plain loan book. Ivy Hill Asset Management, wholly owned, is carried at $2,842.8M: a $946.0M subordinated revolver at a 10.23% coupon plus a member interest marked at $1,896.8M against $1,700.5M cost. The Senior Direct Lending Program joint venture (87.5% membership) carries $1,153.7M of subordinated certificates at an 11.73% coupon. Combined, that is $3,996.5M, or 28.8% of the $13,891M equity base. Both are valued primarily by discounted cash flow rather than the yield and enterprise-value analyses applied to the loan book, and in each ARCC is simultaneously the equity owner and the junior-most creditor. The most recent annual filing on record is a 10-K/A containing only Item 15, filed to furnish separate audited IHAM financial statements under Rule 3-09 — a mechanical confirmation that IHAM has crossed the significant-subsidiary threshold.

Borrower-level granularity is genuinely good: the largest non-affiliate positions (Denali Intermediate $377.6M principal, Reddy Ice $343.6M cost, Apex Service Partners ~$199M) each sit under roughly 2.7% of net assets.

Top-line growth is thin. Total investment income rose 3.1% year over year in Q2 and 3.7% for the half. Interest expense rose 13.8% and 14.2% respectively. NII grew 5.0% in Q2; GAAP net income fell 53%. The gap is entirely portfolio marks.

**Earnings quality.** Reported Q2 NII per share was $0.500 ($359M ÷ 718M shares), covering the $0.48 dividend at 104%. Strip the $21M capital gains incentive fee credit and it is $0.47 — below the distribution. For the half: $0.94 ex-credit against $0.96 declared. This reconciles to the balance sheet, where the capital gains incentive fee payable went from $82M to zero. The mechanism is not discretionary — the GAAP accrual moves with cumulative realized and unrealized gains, so depreciation reverses it and flatters NII in the same period NAV falls. It also works in reverse: a NAV recovery would re-accrue the fee and depress reported NII. ARCC's own non-GAAP core EPS convention typically excludes this item, and the July 29 press release was not available, so the company-labeled figure is unverified. The analytically useful conclusion is that half-year core earnings power of roughly $0.94–$0.97 against $0.96 of distributions is break-even, not comfortably covered.

**PIK.** Aggregating the counterparty categories, Q2 PIK interest was $58M and PIK dividends $64M, totaling $122M, or 15.9% of total investment income — down from 17.6% in Q2 2025. The absolute level remains high: one dollar in six of investment income is accrued rather than received in cash, concentrated in double-digit PIK preferred equity at Symplr (11%), Cornerstone OnDemand (10.5%), Digicert (10.5%), athenaHealth (10.75%), Kaseya (14.59%), CentralSquare (15%) and Banyan Software (14%). Because PIK income is taxable to a RIC and must be distributed, this is a structural cash-flow tension against a $0.48 quarterly cash distribution. The modest year-over-year improvement argues against the strongest version of the bear case here.

**Fee load.** Base plus income based fees of $389M for the half annualize to roughly 5.5% of average net assets (~$14.1B) and consumed 25.4% of total investment income. Interest expense took a further 27.9%. Note the framing carefully: the contractual base fee is levied on total assets excluding cash, so against a ~$30B asset base the contractual *rate* is far lower. The 5.5% figure measures the burden borne by equity holders, not the fee schedule. Both framings are legitimate; a court examining the Section 36(b) claim would look at the contractual rate against comparables.

Leverage rose while absolute debt *fell*. The ratio moved because equity fell faster — a mechanical consequence of NAV erosion, and the channel through which further marks tighten the balance sheet without any new borrowing. At 1.15x, ARCC sits well inside the 2:1 regulatory ceiling; the 10-Q reports compliance in all material respects with every facility and indenture. Liquidity is $383M cash plus $241M restricted against roughly $6.7B undrawn.

**Refinancing crosscurrent.** ARCC retired $1,150M of 3.875% January 2026 Notes and $1,000M of 2.150% July 2026 Notes, replacing capacity with $750M of April 2031 Notes at 5.343% effective and $800M of January 2030 Notes at 5.324% effective — a 200–320bp step-up on roughly $2.15B, worth on the order of $50–65M of annual interest. Yet the reported weighted average rate *fell* from 4.9% to 4.8%. The reconciliation is disclosed: eight of twelve unsecured series ($6,700M notional) are swapped fixed-to-floating, and effective rates include swap impact. So the improvement in reported funding cost is a base-rate effect, not a credit-spread achievement on unsecured paper. On *secured* debt the spread win is real — the ADL CLO 1 refinancing genuinely cut roughly 40bp.

**Rate sensitivity (company table, annualized, ex income based fee effects).** −100bp costs $93M of net income (~$0.13/share); −200bp costs $180M (~$0.25/share, or 13% of the $1.92 distribution). +100bp adds $94M. Portfolio mix: 71% variable, 12% fixed, 9% non-income-producing, 1% non-accrual, 7% the IHAM equity dividend. 96% of variable assets ex-SDLP carry rate floors; the credit facilities and all three CLOs float with no floors. Having deliberately swapped most unsecured debt to floating, ARCC has removed the natural hedge fixed-rate liabilities would provide in a cutting cycle.

**Credit.** Non-accruals were 1% of investments at fair value. The schedule nonetheless shows a legible cohort of impairment. Deeply marked positions include Absolute Dental ($88.4M cost → $43.0M), ADG/GEDC ($99.4M → $31.5M), Pluralsight ($57.9M → $21.8M), Kellermeyer Bergensons ($69.3M → $48.5M) and Laboratories Bidco ($69.8M → $51.8M). The two largest single markdowns are software junior capital: Symplr ($421.9M → $276.8M, −34%) and Cornerstone OnDemand ($364.6M → $232.3M, −36%), together $278M of unrealized loss. Health-care services stress appears in PetVet ($137.6M → $115.5M) and BVI Medical ($155.3M → $128.0M). The pattern is 2020–2022 vintage software LBOs where ARCC holds second lien and PIK preferred, plus private-equity-rolled veterinary, dental and behavioral platforms.

Offsetting equity winners are material and should not be ignored: FEH Group ($187.1M cost → $276.2M), Imaging Business Machines ($14.8M → $84.5M), SageSure ($70.0M → $129.1M), Arxis ($9.9M → $57.5M), Mavis Tire ($24.6M → $52.2M).

A genuine analytical gap: without Note 8's fair-value hierarchy detail, spread-driven marks cannot be separated from credit-driven marks. The yield methodology mechanically writes down performing loans when comparable market spreads widen. That distinction matters enormously for whether the $561M swing reverses.

## Growth Analysis

There is very little growth here, and that is the honest characterization.

The portfolio shrank modestly ($29,485M → $29,349M). Share count was flat at 718M through the half. The ATM equity program refreshed on April 28, 2026 across five banks is dormant optionality — a BDC generally cannot issue below NAV absent specific stockholder approval, and the stock traded at or below NAV for the entirety of Q2 (range $17.73–$19.35 against a $19.35 NAV, meaning the quarterly high exactly equalled book). So the primary BDC growth engine, accretive equity issuance above NAV funding a levered portfolio, is switched off.

Consensus reflects this. Current-year EPS of $1.91 has been revised down 1.1% over 30 days and 0.9% over 90; next year is $1.93, up 0.3% and 0.4%. That is a flat two-year earnings profile with a mild negative drift, from 13–14 covering analysts. ARCC has missed consensus in each of the last four quarters, by 1.6% to 4.1% — small misses, but four in a row, and the pattern argues against assuming upside to $1.91.

The one growth channel that is working is the origination pipeline: $1.5B backlog at July 23, 2026, and July 1–23 commitments of $244M at a 10.2% weighted average yield against $132M of exits at 8.3%. A 190bp reinvestment spread would ordinarily be a strong signal. The mix undercuts it. New commitments were 47% first lien, 17% second lien, 19% senior subordinated, 11% the IHAM subordinated loan and 5% SDLP certificates; exits were 100% first lien senior secured. ARCC is being repaid on senior paper and redeploying further down the capital structure and into its own affiliated vehicles. The yield uplift is compensation for subordination, not improved pricing on comparable first-lien risk. The countervailing point is sample size: $244M over 23 days is 0.8% of the portfolio and cannot be extrapolated to a full quarter.

New-vintage first-lien pricing in the schedule corroborates the compression: 2026 vintages including VRS Buyer (SOFR+3.50%), Spaceship Purchaser (SOFR+3.75%), ISQ Hawkeye (SOFR+4.00%) and Livewire (SOFR+4.25%) price meaningfully tighter than the SOFR+4.50–5.00% modal first lien and far tighter than 2021–2022 second liens at SOFR+6.50–10.00%.

Net: expect flat NII per share, a portfolio roughly stable in size, and NAV as the swing factor. Growth prospects score 4.3.

## Valuation Assessment

Three anchors, and they need to be kept separate.

**Price to NAV.** At $20.055 against the $19.35 June 30 NAV, ARCC trades at 1.036x book — a 3.6% premium. ARCC's long-run pattern is a premium to NAV as the sector's quality name, and 1.04x is not extreme by that history. But the denominator is falling roughly 1.2% per quarter. If NAV drifts to approximately $19.10–$19.30 by mid-2027 and the multiple holds at 1.03–1.06x, price lands at $19.70–$20.45. Most of the total return then comes from the distribution.

**Earnings multiple.** The supplied P/E fields require reconciliation. Trailing and forward P/E are both reported at 14.93 while the EPS field shows $1.858 — those are inconsistent ($20.055 ÷ 14.93 = $1.34). The 14.93x reconciles to trailing *GAAP* EPS of approximately $1.34–$1.36, which is what the marks have done to reported earnings (6M 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.36 against $0.88 a year prior). The economically relevant multiple is against core NII: 10.5x the $1.91 current-year consensus, or 10.7x the $1.88 annualized ex-fee-credit run rate. For a large-cap BDC that is a middle-of-the-band multiple.

**Yield.** $1.92 annualized is 9.57% at the current price and 9.92% on the $19.35 NAV. Absolute yield support is the strongest leg of the valuation case, and it has been paid flat for ten consecutive quarters (Q1 2024 through Q2 2026). The qualifier is coverage: at ~$0.94 of half-year core earnings against $0.96 declared, the distribution is being funded at the margin by spillover flexibility rather than out of current core earnings power.

**Peer and target context.** Consensus targets run $19.00 low, $20.50 median, $20.62 mean, $23.00 high. The stock sits 2.8% below the mean and 2.2% below the median — effectively at target. Analyst sentiment is characterized as degrading, with one recommendation shifting from buy to hold over two months (currently 4 strong buy / 7 buy / 3 hold, no sells). Analyst targets function as floors on inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples; that is not this situation. Estimates are drifting down, the multiple is mid-band, and the stock has already closed the gap to consensus.

The temptation with a 9.6% yielder at 10.5x is to score valuation highly on the yield alone. That would be a mistake. There is no identified mispricing: the market re-rated the stock 7.7% in three weeks following the Q2 print, moving it from a 3.8% discount to a 3.6% premium, and it now discounts a stable NAV and a maintained dividend — precisely the two things the last three quarters of data put in question. A high valuation score requires showing why the market is wrong; here the market looks approximately right. **Valuation: 4.8.**

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory diverges sharply between the two sides of the balance sheet.

**Liability side: widening moat, verifiable.** In mid-2026 ARCC simultaneously refinanced a CLO at roughly 40bp tighter spreads, extended its revolver to a 2031 maturity at a 1.53% weighted spread with an accordion to ~$8.2B, amended the BNP facility, and established a $1.0B commercial paper program backed by the revolver. It maintains three bank funding facilities, twelve unsecured note series and three CLOs, carrying $22,666M of total committed capacity against $15,924M drawn. ARCC's cost of funds is being set by its size, rating and collateral quality rather than by sector sentiment. Smaller BDCs cannot assemble this liability stack. With $6.7B undrawn against a $1.5B backlog, ARCC can price large club deals rather than take them.

**Asset side: compressing.** New-vintage first-lien spreads are demonstrably tighter than legacy paper, the July mix shows redeployment into second lien, senior subordinated and affiliated vehicles, and the sector tilt — residential/commercial HVAC, fire and life safety, waste, utility infrastructure services, insurance brokerage — is sensible and defensive but also the most heavily banked corner of direct lending, which is exactly why spreads there are tight. The defensive posture is prudent; it is not differentiated.

**Diversification requires care.** Borrower-level concentration is low and appears stable, with no non-affiliate position above roughly 2.7% of net assets. Affiliate-level concentration is high and rising: controlled-affiliate investment income grew 15% to $141M, with IHAM/SDLP interest income (ex-PIK) rising to $59M from $40M. A revenue mix shifting toward two affiliated vehicles that already represent 28.8% of net assets is not diversification. These are different measures answering different questions and neither substitutes for the other.

**Competitive position: 5.5** — a strengthening funding franchise offset by asset-side spread compression and a mix shift down the capital structure.

## Risk Assessment

**1. NAV erosion compounding through leverage.** Three consecutive quarters of decline, a $561M six-month swing from unrealized gain to unrealized loss, with 82% of the six-month unrealized loss in the non-affiliate loan book — broad-based, not idiosyncratic. Each dollar of markdown mechanically raises debt/equity (1.12x → 1.15x with debt *falling*). No covenant breach is disclosed, but headroom narrows.

**2. Valuation opacity concentrated in affiliates.** 28.8% of net assets in IHAM and SDLP, marked primarily by DCF on levered, affiliated vehicles, with ARCC as both equity owner and junior-most creditor in each. IHAM's member interest is carried $196.3M above cost. Mitigating: from Q1 2025, substantially all investments are reviewed quarterly by an independent valuation provider issuing a positive assurance opinion, and the Rule 3-09 trigger produces *more* disclosure via standalone audited IHAM financials. Those financials were not read here.

**3. Section 36(b) derivative action.** Filed May 26, 2026 in S.D.N.Y. by Martin Siegel, derivatively on ARCC's behalf, alleging the *adviser* received excessive fees; remedies sought include disgorgement and rescission of the advisory agreement under Section 47(b). Read the counterparties correctly: disgorgement would flow *to* ARCC, so a plaintiff win is not straightforwardly negative for shareholders, though rescission would be genuinely disruptive to an entity with no employees. The *Jones v. Harris*/*Gartenberg* standard is demanding and most such suits fail. Probability of large adverse outcome is low; the overhang is real, arriving alongside three quarters of NAV decline and a fee load that a plaintiff will foreground. This characterization derives entirely from ARCC's own one-paragraph summary; the complaint was not read.

**4. Sector concentration in the stressed cohorts.** Software & Services is the largest exposure and hosts the largest markdowns. Health Care Equipment & Services contains the vet/dental/behavioral roll-ups with the highest incidence of non-accrual footnotes. Financial Services, beyond IHAM, contains a large and correlated book of RIA/wealth-platform loans (Lido, Merit, Waverly, Wealth Enhancement, Beacon Pointe, Clearstead, Perigon, Pathstone, Steward Partners and others) — a single sponsor-driven consolidation theme financed repeatedly.

**5. PIK dependence.** 15.9% of investment income, concentrated in double-digit PIK preferred in software borrowers, taxable to the RIC and distributable in cash. Improving modestly, but structurally tensioned against a cash distribution.

**6. Rate direction.** A structural short position in cuts (−$0.13/share per 100bp).

**7. Disclosure gaps to flag rather than resolve.** No FY2025 10-K Item 1A was retrievable (the most recent annual filing is a 10-K/A limited to Item 15), so changes to risk factors versus FY2024 cannot be identified. No earnings press release, guidance or transcript was available, so there is no management commentary, no company-labeled core EPS, and no non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP consensus comparison — the coverage arithmetic above is derived, not compared to management's figure. Non-accruals at *cost* (typically higher than at fair value) are not confirmed. Note 8's fair-value hierarchy was not retrieved, so spread-driven and credit-driven marks cannot be separated. These are genuine limits on confidence and are part of why the risk score is low.

**Risk factors: 4.3.**

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 13.3% against 20.2% one-year realized — cheap, in the 3rd percentile of the current chain, with a balanced 0.2% put/call skew and a ±1.9% implied move on the nine-day tenor. The term structure is in contango (13.3% at one week, 18.9% at one month, 19.7% at six months), which is the normal, complacent shape. Put/call open interest fell from 2.27 on June 3 to 0.73 today while ATM IV compressed 11.2 points and the price rose 7.3%. The straightforward reading: hedging demand that was in place ahead of the Q2 print has been unwound, and the options market prices no near-term dislocation. That is a divergence worth naming — a portfolio with three quarters of deteriorating marks and 28.8% of book in DCF-valued affiliates is not obviously a 13% vol asset, and the six-month tenor at 19.7% is the more honest number. For holders, cheap near-term vol makes protective puts an inexpensive way to carry the yield through the next print. Short interest of 6.3% of float with 8.5 days to cover is uncrowded and offers no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Management Quality

The insider record is the clearest positive in the file, and it is the highest-signal category: open-market purchases by sitting C-suite officers. A February 2026 cluster followed the FY2025 results — CEO Kort Schnabel 12,500 shares at $19.13, COO Jana Markowicz 15,000 at $19.20, CFO Scott Lem 5,186 at $19.29, Director Mary Beth Henson 4,000 at $19.14 — all below the then-prevailing $19.94 NAV. Schnabel, promoted internally from Co-President, had also bought 13,000 shares at $20.39 in October 2025, roughly tripling his holding to 40,500. There are no open-market officer or director sales in the retrieved record since December 2021, and the only dispositions are non-economic (a 300,172-share gift by Arougheti, who retains 2,016,785 shares, and Ares Management's redemption of preferred). Tempering: dollar amounts are modest ($76K–$288K), BDC executives commonly face ownership guidelines, and the timing has not been good — Co-President Miller's 40,000-share purchase at $23.32 in March 2025 is roughly 14% underwater.

Capital allocation is more debatable. The $1.0B buyback authorization, extended in February 2026 through February 2027, went entirely unused in Q2 2026 while the stock traded at or below NAV for the full quarter and at a 3.82% discount on July 23. The only open-market share activity was 1,192,850 shares purchased at a $17.80 average in April 2026 to satisfy the DRIP. Meanwhile fresh ATM equity machinery was installed across five banks on April 28. The defensible reading: a buyback consumes liquidity and *raises* the already-elevated 1.15x leverage ratio, and deploying into a $1.5B backlog at ~10.2% yields is a better use of capital than retiring stock at a 4% discount; the ATM is dormant optionality for a future premium. The structural fact that the base fee is levied on total assets, and therefore that shrinking equity reduces adviser revenue, is worth naming — but the liquidity and leverage rationale independently explains the decision and no filing discloses the board's reasoning, so motive should not be inferred.

Conventional pay-for-performance analysis is not possible: the DEF 14A structured fields returned null across CEO compensation, compensation actually paid, TSR and performance measures, which is consistent with ARCC having no employees and executives being compensated by Ares Management. That is a definitional consequence of external management, not a disclosure failure, and should not be scored as one. Alignment runs through personal share ownership and the adviser's fee economics, of which only the capital gains incentive fee penalizes NAV destruction — and it did so this half, producing the $82M credit.

No Item 5.02 officer or director departures appear across the eighteen most recent 8-Ks (July 2025–July 2026), a quiet stretch that suggests the CEO handover was completed before the window. **Management quality: 6.2.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $22.30 (+11.2% price, ~21% total return with distributions)

Unrealized marks are exactly that. Realized results for the half were positive (+$101M), non-accruals remain 1% of fair value, and a meaningful share of the $561M swing may reflect wider comparable market spreads mechanically marking down performing credits rather than borrower deterioration. If spreads stabilize, the marks reverse, NAV recovers toward $19.75–$20.00, and the capital gains incentive fee re-accrues against restored book value. Funding costs keep improving on the secured book (the CLO refinancing precedent, plus the untapped $1.0B CP program). ARCC re-rates to 1.15x a $19.40 NAV — within its historical premium range for a name compounding book — at $22.30, still 3% below the $23.00 high consensus target.

### Bear Case — $16.25 (−19.0% price)

Marks are the leading edge of realized credit. The 2020–2022 software LBO cohort where ARCC holds second lien and 10–15% PIK preferred (Symplr, Cornerstone OnDemand, Digicert, Kaseya, CentralSquare) converts from markdown to non-accrual and restructuring; the vet/dental/behavioral roll-ups follow. Non-accruals rise from 1% toward 3–4% of fair value, PIK stops accruing, and NII per share falls below $0.45. The distribution is cut to $0.42 to align with core earnings. NAV erodes to $19.10 and the multiple compresses to 0.85x on a cut dividend — $16.25, above the $17.40 52-week low but implying that a re-test would be exceeded. A resumption of rate cuts (−200bp costs $0.25/share) accelerates the arithmetic, and a broader private-credit repricing removes the liability-side advantage that currently distinguishes ARCC.

### Base Case — $20.50 (+2.2% price, ~11.8% total return)

NAV stabilizes in the $19.20–$19.40 range as software junior-capital marks bottom while equity winners (FEH Group, SageSure, Imaging Business Machines) offset. Core NII holds at $0.47–$0.48 per quarter, the distribution is maintained at $0.48 with roughly break-even coverage supported by spillover, and the multiple stays at 1.05–1.06x book. $20.50 equals 1.06x a $19.35 NAV and 10.7x the $1.91 current-year consensus. Nearly all of the return is the 9.6% distribution.

Probability-weighting base 50% / bull 20% / bear 30% gives roughly −2.4% expected price return plus approximately 9% of collected distributions — call it a mid-single-digit expected total return. That is at or slightly below fair for the risk taken, which is the definition of a hold.

## Conclusion

ARCC is the highest-quality vehicle in a sector where quality currently matters. Its funding structure — $6.7B undrawn, a revolver to 2031 at a 1.53% spread, a CLO refinanced 40bp tighter in July 2026, a new $1.0B commercial paper program, 4.1-year weighted maturity at 4.8% — is a genuine and widening advantage. The stock has rallied 7.7% since July 23 and now trades at a 3.6% premium to a book value that is declining roughly 1.2% per quarter, within 3% of consensus targets, with no valuation cushion.

The distribution is the return. Nine point six percent, paid flat for ten quarters, from a balance sheet with ample liquidity and no covenant issue, is worth owning if you already own it. Paying a premium to a falling book for approximately break-even coverage, ahead of a print that will determine whether the marks were spreads or credit, is not worth initiating. Wait for a discount to NAV near $18.30, or for two quarters of stabilized book value.
