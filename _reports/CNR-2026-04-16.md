## Executive Summary

Core Natural Resources, Inc. is the product of a January 2025 merger of equals between CONSOL Energy and Arch Resources, creating the largest U.S. coal producer with 11 mines across six states, two marine export terminals, and a virtually debt-free balance sheet. The combined entity generated $4.16B in revenue in FY2025 but posted a net loss of -$153M, driven by $380M+ in purchase accounting depreciation step-ups, $112M in Leer South fire-related costs, and weak metallurgical coal pricing. Adjusted EBITDA of ~$512M provides a more accurate picture of underlying cash generation.

Three factors support the view that FY2025 represents a trough: (1) Leer South longwall resumed operations in December 2025, removing the largest operational headwind; (2) the IRC Section 45X production tax credit for met coal takes effect in 2026; and (3) geopolitical supply disruptions — particularly Strait of Hormuz tensions and simultaneous non-renewal of Iranian and Russian oil waivers — are tightening global energy supply. Against this, thermal coal margins are compressing (from $28/ton to $20/ton in High CV Thermal), the met coal market remains oversupplied, the company missed EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters by wide margins, and current-quarter estimates are being slashed (-52% over 90 days). The stock has rallied 20.8% over the past year and sits at $89.31, within the range of its 52-week band of $63-$115. At ~18x the FY2026E consensus of ~$4.92, the stock is not cheap relative to energy peers, though next-year estimates ($8.96) would imply a more reasonable ~10x multiple if achieved.

## Company Overview

Core Natural Resources operates four segments: **High CV Thermal** (PAMC complex in Pennsylvania/West Virginia plus West Elk in Colorado), **Metallurgical** (five mines in West Virginia producing high-vol and low-vol met coal), **Powder River Basin** (Black Thunder and Coal Creek surface mines in Wyoming), and the **Baltimore Marine Terminal** (coal export terminal plus 35% interest in Dominion Terminal Associates). The company produced approximately 65.8M tons in the first nine months of 2025. Export revenue constituted 52% of total coal revenue in Q3 2025 ($515M of $994M), making Core highly exposed to international coal pricing benchmarks (Newcastle, API2, and met coal indices).

The merger created a diversified portfolio spanning the three major U.S. coal basins with integrated export logistics. The PRB segment is explicitly a sunset business managed for current demand with a $125M reclamation fund in place.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $4.16B roughly doubled from $2.24B in FY2024, reflecting the full-year contribution of Arch's operations. However, the revenue increase came with a profitability collapse: net income went from +$286M (FY2024) to -$153M (FY2025).

The loss is attributable to identifiable, largely non-recurring items:
- **Purchase accounting DD&A:** $621M total (vs. $224M pre-merger), including ~$258M in incremental amortization of Arch's fair value step-ups on $2.6B of acquired PP&E
- **Leer South fire costs:** $112M total ($76M through 9 months, $36M in Q4), with only $43M in insurance recoveries
- **Elevated G&A:** $215M (vs. $115M), inflated by merger transaction costs, severance, and stock-based compensation modifications

Segment profitability through nine months of 2025: High CV Thermal generated $460M in Adjusted EBITDA (83% of total), PRB contributed $56M, the Marine Terminal added $41M, and Metallurgical ran at -$5M. Cash margins per ton: High CV Thermal at $20.27/ton (down from $28/ton), Met at $7.65/ton, and PRB at $1.55/ton.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $306M for FY2025. Capital expenditures totaled $285M, producing free cash flow of only $21M — essentially zero for a $4.6B market cap company. The thin FCF reflects the simultaneous headwinds of merger integration costs, fire remediation spending, and depressed met coal pricing. Q4 alone generated $27M in FCF, suggesting some sequential improvement.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is the strongest aspect of the business. Cash of $432M against $425M in total debt (primarily tax-exempt bonds and equipment financing) produces a net leverage ratio of approximately zero. Total liquidity reaches ~$995M including revolver capacity. Covenant metrics are comfortable: first lien gross leverage 0.18x (limit 1.50x), interest coverage 50.13x (minimum 3.00x). The current ratio stands at 1.60x.

However, long-term obligations are substantial: $492M in asset retirement obligations, $250M in pneumoconiosis benefits, $209M in OPEB liabilities, $74M in workers' compensation, and potential UMWA litigation exposure of $65-90M. A new OWCP rule requiring 100% security for self-insured black lung liabilities could create additional cash collateral requirements of uncertain magnitude.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are mixed and heavily dependent on commodity price recovery and operational normalization.

**Positive catalysts:**
1. **Leer South restart:** Longwall operations resumed December 18, 2025 after nearly 12 months idle. Full normalization should add 2-3M tons of high-vol met coal production and eliminate $100M+ in annual fire/idle costs.
2. **IRC Section 45X credit:** Met coal designated as a "critical material" qualifying for a 2.5% monetizable production tax credit on production costs, effective 2026 through 2029.
3. **Merger synergies:** Integration of Arch's logistics and operations with CONSOL's export terminal infrastructure should yield procurement and transportation efficiencies, though management has not quantified specific targets.

**Headwinds:**
1. **Thermal coal margin compression:** High CV Thermal realized pricing fell from $66.39 to $61.11/ton while cash costs rose from $38.43 to $40.84/ton.
2. **Met coal oversupply:** Global met coal markets remain challenged by surplus production and weak Chinese steel demand. Realized met coal pricing collapsed from ~$156/ton to $101/ton.
3. **PRB secular decline:** Domestic coal-fired generation continues shrinking, limiting PRB volume potential.

Estimate revisions present a conflicting picture. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 52% over 90 days (from $0.56 to $0.27), and next-quarter estimates fell 85% (from $2.38 to $0.36). However, FY2026 full-year estimates rose 13% over 30 days to $4.92, and FY2027 estimates rose 17% to $8.96. This suggests analysts expect a back-half-loaded recovery driven by Leer South normalization and potential commodity price recovery, but near-term visibility is poor.

The earnings surprise record — 1 beat in 4 quarters, including misses of -334%, -207%, and -111% — indicates the Street has consistently overestimated the company's earnings power since the merger closed.

At $89.31 and a market cap of $4.6B, CNR trades at:
- ~18.2x FY2026E EPS ($4.92)
- ~10.0x FY2027E EPS ($8.96)
- Trailing P/E: negative (loss year)
- Dividend yield: ~0.45%
- Price/Book: ~1.25x ($3.68B equity)

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 20.7x (LBRT) with a median yield of 3.4%. CNR's yield of 0.45% is substantially below peers, reflecting the post-merger dividend cut from $0.25 to $0.10/quarter. At 18.2x forward earnings, CNR is priced in line with the peer median for a company with below-peer returns and greater operational uncertainty. If FY2027 estimates of $8.96 are achievable, the stock looks inexpensive at 10x — but the path to that number requires met coal recovery, full Leer South normalization, and stable thermal margins, none of which is guaranteed given the recent miss track record.

The analyst consensus target is $112.25 (range $105-$119), implying 25-33% upside. All four covering analysts rate the stock Buy with no Holds or Sells — a unanimously bullish consensus that creates asymmetric downside risk if operational normalization disappoints.

**Adjusted EBITDA basis:** FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$512M ($409M through 9 months + $103M in Q4) implies an EV/EBITDA of approximately 8.5x. If Leer South normalization and reduced fire costs push EBITDA toward $700M in FY2026, the multiple compresses to ~6x, which would be attractive for the sector.

## Competitive Landscape

Core is the largest U.S. coal producer with structural advantages:

1. **Export terminal ownership:** The Baltimore Marine Terminal and 35% DTA interest provide integrated logistics access to Atlantic and Asian seaborne markets that competitors must lease or contract for.
2. **Low-cost positioning:** The PAMC complex and Leer mine are among the lowest-cost operations in their respective coal types in North America.
3. **Balance sheet superiority:** Near-zero net debt is exceptionally rare in the coal industry, where most operators carry meaningful leverage.
4. **Diversification:** Spanning thermal, met, and PRB coal across six states reduces single-asset and single-commodity risk.

The primary competitive vulnerability is exposure to global commodity pricing with limited ability to influence benchmarks. In a market with oversupplied met coal and softening thermal demand, even the lowest-cost producer faces margin compression. Warrior Met Coal (HCC) and Alpha Metallurgical Resources (AMR) compete directly in met coal; Peabody Energy (BTU) competes across thermal and PRB.

## Risk Assessment

### Operational Risk
While Leer South longwall operations resumed in December 2025, the mine has yet to demonstrate sustained normal-rate production. West Elk also experienced fire/idle costs in Q4. Any recurrence of production interruptions would be severely damaging to the met segment recovery timeline.

### Commodity Price Risk
The current geopolitical environment (Hormuz blockade, Russian supply restrictions) supports energy prices broadly, but coal-specific benchmarks have not responded proportionally. Coal competes with natural gas for power generation — if gas prices spike on geopolitical disruption, that could support domestic thermal coal demand, but the relationship is indirect and variable.

### Legacy Liability Risk
The combined $492M in AROs, $250M in black lung benefits, $209M in OPEB, and potential UMWA litigation of $65-90M reduce the effective net cash position. The new OWCP 100% security requirement for self-insured black lung liabilities is a meaningful regulatory risk with undefined capital requirements.

### Integration Risk
Arch's internal controls have not yet been assessed under Core's SOX 404 framework (excluded in the first year per SEC guidance). Arch constituted 51% of revenue and 55% of assets. Integration of two major coal companies with distinct operational cultures, IT systems, and safety protocols presents execution risk through 2026.

### Cybersecurity Risk
An 8-K filed January 12, 2026 disclosed a cybersecurity incident. Limited details are available. Mining operations increasingly depend on digital monitoring and safety systems, making cyber risk operationally relevant.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 67.1% is 16.5 percentage points above historical volatility of 50.7%. The term structure is in sharp backwardation (67.1% near-term vs. 44.9% at 6 months), indicating imminent-stress expectations likely tied to earnings/operational uncertainty. Put/call skew of 51.1% shows elevated put demand, consistent with institutional hedging of downside risk. The volume-weighted put/call ratio of 1.10 and elevated put skew suggest informed participants are buying protection. For a stock with unanimous analyst Buy ratings, this divergence between options positioning and sell-side consensus warrants attention.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$110-$119)
Leer South achieves full production normalization through H1 2026, adding 2-3M tons of high-vol met coal at improved margins. Met coal prices recover toward $130-$140/ton as global steel production stabilizes. The Section 45X credit adds $25-$40M annually to met segment economics. Purchase accounting DD&A begins declining, improving GAAP optics. Thermal pricing stabilizes near current levels as geopolitical supply disruptions support global energy markets. Management executes on the remaining $797M buyback authorization, reducing share count by 10-15% over two years. FY2027 EPS approaches $9-$10, and at 12x earnings, the stock reaches $110-$120.

### Bear Case (~$65-$75)
Met coal prices remain below $100/ton as Chinese steel demand weakens further. Leer South encounters additional operational issues (ventilation, ground conditions, regulatory delays). Thermal coal margins continue compressing as Newcastle/API2 benchmarks decline. The new OWCP black lung security rule requires $100M+ in cash collateral. PRB volumes contract faster than expected. The company generates sub-$100M in annual FCF, insufficient to fund meaningful buybacks while maintaining the dividend and meeting capital needs. At 8x a reduced FY2027 EPS of $6-$7, the stock revisits $50-$55.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Leer South demonstrates two consecutive quarters of normalized production rates** (>1.5M tons/quarter of met coal at cash margins >$20/ton), confirming fire risk is resolved
2. **FY2026 current-quarter EPS estimates stabilize or rise**, signaling analyst confidence in operational recovery
3. **Stock pulls back to $75-$80** (~15x FY2026E), providing a more adequate margin of safety given operational uncertainties

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Another production interruption** at Leer South or a major mine (fire, roof collapse, regulatory shutdown) that would further delay met segment normalization
2. **Met coal realized pricing falls below $90/ton** for two consecutive quarters with no recovery in sight
3. **Free cash flow remains below $100M in FY2026** despite Leer South restart, indicating structural cash generation problems

### Reassessment Timeline
- **Next earnings (likely May 2026):** Q1 2026 results will be the first to reflect a full quarter of Leer South longwall operations — the single most important data point for the thesis
- **Mid-year 2026:** Re-evaluate after Q2 results confirm or deny operational normalization trajectory

## Conclusion

Core Natural Resources occupies a strong competitive position as the largest, most diversified, and least-levered U.S. coal producer. The near-zero net debt balance sheet and $1B liquidity provide substantial financial flexibility. However, FY2025 was an objectively poor year: a net loss, near-zero free cash flow, a 3-of-4 earnings miss record, and compressing margins across both thermal and met coal segments. The stock has already rallied 20.8% over the past year, pricing in substantial recovery expectations.

The path to FY2027 EPS of $8.96 requires met coal price recovery, sustained Leer South production, and stable thermal pricing — conditions that remain speculative given the recent operational track record. At 18x current-year estimates with a 0.45% dividend yield and options pricing reflecting elevated uncertainty (67% IV vs. 51% HV in backwardation), the fundamental picture matches the market's skepticism. The unanimous analyst Buy consensus and $112 mean target offer upside potential, but the consistent pattern of earnings misses and the options-market hedging activity suggest the consensus may again be too optimistic.

The appropriate stance is to wait for evidence of operational normalization at Leer South before increasing conviction. Q1 2026 results will be the critical test.
