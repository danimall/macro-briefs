**Research Date: June 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cohen & Steers is a $3.9B-market-cap boutique asset manager specialized in real assets and alternative income, primarily REITs (~64% of AUM) and preferred securities (~20%). The franchise is operationally healthy: AUM reached a record $93.1B at Q1 2026 (up from $85.8B a year earlier), revenue grew 7.5% in FY2025 to $556.1M and a further 8.3% YoY in Q1 2026, and the business runs at a GAAP operating margin in the low-30s with ROE around 28%. The balance sheet is clean: the only debt is an undrawn $100M revolver, and net liquid assets stood at $370.5M at Q1. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up steadily (+4.4% current-year, +5.0% next-year over 90 days), signaling positive earnings momentum off a recovering revenue base.

The central tension is that operational health has not translated into share-price performance. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows CNS's indexed 5-year TSR at 99.77 against a peer group at 196.16; the stock has roughly halved relative to peers since 2021 despite record AUM and steady earnings. The market has de-rated the franchise on its rate sensitivity, its real-estate concentration, and secular fee pressure from passive products. The growth pivot the firm is executing, into private real estate (CNSREIT) and a late-entry active-ETF suite, is genuine but margin-dilutive today, requiring seed capital and upfront fixed costs that management explicitly warns may not be recovered.

We rate CNS a HOLD on an income basis. The ~3.5% dividend yield is well-covered and the cash-generative advisory engine is durable, but at ~25x trailing and ~22x forward earnings the stock is fairly valued for a structurally challenged, rate-sensitive specialist with no near-term re-rating catalyst. The reported negative free cash flow is an accounting artifact of seed-fund consolidation and should not be read as financial distress. This is a yield-and-quality holding rather than an appreciation vehicle, and we would require evidence of organic-flow acceleration or fee-accretive product conversion before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1986 and headquartered in New York with offices in London, Dublin, Hong Kong, Tokyo, and Singapore, Cohen & Steers is a pure-play global investment manager focused on real assets and alternative income. More than 99% of revenue is asset-based fees earned across three vehicle types:

- **Open-end funds** (~$44.8B, ~48% of AUM, ~67 bps fee rate): mutual funds, the non-traded CNSREIT, and the new active-ETF suite.
- **Institutional accounts** (~$36.0B, ~38 bps): advisory and subadvisory mandates; the lowest-fee segment.
- **Closed-end funds** (~$12.3B, ~89 bps): the highest-fee vehicle, employing ~$3.6B of bank leverage.

Strategy concentration is high: ~63.8% of AUM is in real estate securities (with ~25.9% in just three flagship U.S. real estate funds), ~20% in preferred securities, and the balance across global listed infrastructure (the fastest-growing strategy), global/international real estate, natural resources, and multi-strategy. The firm is a recognized specialist in REITs and preferreds, asset classes where active management still commands premium fees relative to single-digit-bps passive products.

The franchise is capital-light and high-margin. Its competitive defense rests on genuine specialist expertise and a strong active track record. Its structural challenge is the secular flow toward passive and ongoing fee compression, the same headwinds that have de-rated active equity managers broadly.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $556.1M, up 7.5% YoY on higher average AUM ($88.6B average, +5.7%), with open-end fund advisory fees the fastest grower (+12%). Q1 2026 revenue was $145.6M, +8.3% YoY. FY2022 revenue ($566.9M) still exceeds FY2025; revenue troughed in 2023 ($489.6M) during the rate-driven REIT/preferred drawdown and has been recovering since. This is a recovery, not uninterrupted secular growth.

Net income to common was $153.2M in FY2025 (roughly flat versus $151.3M), with diluted EPS of $2.97 (flat YoY) and adjusted EPS of $3.09 (+5.5%). GAAP operating margin compressed to 32.0% from 33.4% in 2024 and 33.6% in 2023, driven by ~$11.5M of one-time fund-launch and UTF rights-offering costs plus higher G&A. Q1 2026 margin recovered to 34.4% GAAP / 35.1% adjusted, suggesting the 2025 compression was partly transitory. ROE around 28% and ROA around 18% are excellent for the sector.

**Balance sheet.** The balance sheet is clean. Altman Z-Score of 3.59 sits in the safe zone. The only debt is a $100M undrawn revolver (extended in August 2025, maturing 2029); the reported D/E of 0.43 is inflated by consolidated-fund/noncontrolling-interest accounting rather than real leverage. Net liquid assets were $370.5M at Q1 2026.

**Cash flow — the key correction.** Headline FY2025 operating cash flow was –$120.4M and FCF –$126.4M, which would flag the company as cash-burning on a standardized screen. This is an accounting artifact. GAAP consolidation of seed-invested funds routed $319.1M of fund securities purchases through operating cash flow, while financing inflows from noncontrolling interests (+$228.7M) offset it. The underlying advisory business is highly cash-generative; net liquid assets grew over the period. The weak Piotroski/FCF reading should be discounted for this reason; this is the single most important analytical correction in the file, and it supports a financial-health score well above what the screening data implies.

**Capital allocation.** The firm pays a $0.67/quarter dividend ($126.9M paid in 2025, held flat into 2026), plus ~$28M/year of buybacks that only offset RSU tax withholding, so share count is actually rising (~51.2M basic). Capital is increasingly redirected toward seed investments ($175M committed, $69.1M still unfunded at Q1 2026) to fund the private-RE and ETF buildout. The dividend is well-covered (payout against $2.97 EPS implies room), and the yield support is the cleanest part of the investment case.

**Earnings track record.** The recent surprise pattern is mixed: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters, with the misses small (–1% to –4.3%). The narrow miss magnitude argues against deteriorating visibility, but it does temper confidence in upside surprises against rising estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two layers. The cyclical layer is the ongoing recovery in rate-sensitive AUM: record AUM, positive Q1 net inflows (+$497M), and steadily rising consensus estimates. Estimate revisions are constructively positive: current-year EPS revised from $3.34 to $3.48 over 90 days and next-year from $3.70 to $3.88, with 30-day moves accelerating. This is genuine positive earnings momentum, though the magnitude is well short of the +30% threshold that would force a forward-multiple-driven re-rating thesis. Revenue growth in the high-single digits does not clear the bar for treating this as a cyclical-trough name.

The structural layer is the franchise pivot. Three initiatives matter:

1. **Global listed infrastructure**: AUM grew ~30% in FY2025 with strong inflows, benefiting from data-center/AI power-demand and energy-transition themes. This is the standout organic grower and aligns CNS with a durable, broad-based demand theme rather than a single-company story.
2. **Active ETFs**: CNS entered the active-ETF market in 2025 (U.S. real estate, preferreds, natural resources) and added listed infrastructure and short-duration preferreds in December 2025. This is a strategically necessary but late entry into a fast-growing vehicle where CNS lacks the multi-year track records that drive ETF flows, an initial competitive disadvantage.
3. **Private real estate (CNSREIT)**: The non-traded REIT is actively acquiring grocery-anchored shopping centers through programmatic JVs (Charlotte, Oro Valley, Austin in spring 2026), with CNS holding a 32.3% seed stake. Higher and stickier fees are the prize, but the buildout is capital-intensive and execution-heavy, with back-end-loaded and uncertain payoff.

The growth thesis is real but margin-dilutive near-term, and the durability of organic growth depends on rate and REIT-cycle dynamics. We score growth prospects at 5.5: positive momentum and a credible diversification path, weighed against modest absolute growth and a recovery (rather than secular-expansion) base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $74.99, CNS trades at ~24.8x trailing EPS and ~21.5x forward EPS. For a high-margin, capital-light asset manager with ~28% ROE and a covered ~3.5% yield, this is a fair-to-full multiple, not a distressed one. The PEG is unattractive: mid-single-digit forward growth against a low-20s multiple gives a PEG well above 2, which does not support a cheap-on-growth argument.

The analyst price-target distribution is wide and informative: mean $70.33, median $77.00, low $52.00, high $82.00, with a polarized recommendation split (1 buy, 1 hold, 1 strong sell). The current price sits above the mean but below the median, and the strong-sell anchor reflects the structural-fee-pressure bear case. With the stock not cheap relative to growth and the consensus split, we do not treat analyst targets as a floor here; this configuration reflects genuine disagreement rather than lagging optimism.

The relevant valuation tension is the relative-TSR gap. CNS's 5-year indexed TSR trails its peer group by roughly half, meaning the equity has materially de-rated even as AUM hit records. A bull would read this as a mispriced quality franchise; a bear would read it as the market correctly pricing a rate-sensitive, structurally challenged active manager mid-pivot. We see no specific catalyst that closes that gap in the next 12 months. The cheapness-as-opportunity argument is weak: across the broad market, low-multiple names without an independent catalyst have tended to stay cheap, and CNS's discount is explained by identifiable structural concerns (real-estate concentration, fee compression, near-term margin drag) rather than a clear mispricing. We score valuation at 5.0: fairly valued, with a balanced risk/reward and no asymmetric upside at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

CNS occupies a structurally pressured corner of asset management: active, specialty equity/income strategies facing secular passive flows and fee compression. Its defense is real: genuine specialist depth in REITs and preferreds, asset classes where active management still earns 67–89 bps versus low-single-digit bps for passive, plus a strong relative-performance record and a recognized brand (P&I "Best Place to Work" six straight years).

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Positives: listed infrastructure is gaining share within a growing category, and the firm is defending against vehicle disruption by launching active ETFs. Negatives: it enters the ETF market late and without track records, the open-end mutual-fund core faces the same passive substitution pressure as the broader industry, and its highest-fee closed-end and open-end vehicles are the most exposed to fee compression over time. A constructive read on the 2025 margin compression is that it was partly self-inflicted growth investment rather than competitive fee collapse, a more favorable interpretation than sector-wide erosion.

On balance, the moat is stable-to-modestly-pressured rather than widening. We score competitive position at 5.5, reflecting genuine specialist expertise offset by structural headwinds and an unproven late entry into the fastest-growing vehicle.

## Risk Assessment

**Real-estate and rate concentration.** ~64% of AUM (25.9% in three funds) is in real estate securities, and both core franchises (REITs and preferreds) are rate-sensitive. A REIT/rate shock hits revenue directly and quickly, as the 2023 drawdown demonstrated. The 10-K flags ongoing commercial-real-estate distress (office vacancies, elevated refinancing rates). With the higher-for-longer rate path a live macro theme, this concentration is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.

**Preferred-securities tail risk.** Preferreds compete with risk-free yields and carry tail risk; the 10-K cites the 2023 Credit Suisse AT1 write-down as a reminder.

**Client and distribution concentration.** Daiwa Asset Management is ~4.5% of revenue and ~8.7% of AUM, concentrated in Japan-distributed U.S. real estate subadvisory, exposed to yen moves and Japanese distribution dynamics. Subadvisory and gatekeeper-platform decisions can move AUM with little notice.

**Strategy-execution risk.** The private-RE and active-ETF buildout requires upfront fixed costs and seed capital ($69.1M still unfunded) that may not be recovered, and is margin-dilutive today.

**Founder ownership overhang.** Executive Chairman Robert Steers (~23.4%) and Board Chairman Martin Cohen (~17.7%) and families control ~41% combined, with an active S-3 shelf covering ~21M resale shares: a latent supply overhang if they monetize, and a constraint on minority-shareholder influence.

**Leadership transition.** A multi-seat C-suite transition coincides with the strategic pivot: Amit Muni was appointed permanent EVP & CFO effective June 8, 2026 after an interim period under Michael Donohue, alongside the long-tenured CEO/CIO leadership. A CFO change during the firm's most ambitious product expansion elevates near-term execution risk.

These risks are partly but not fully priced: the relative-TSR discount captures the structural concerns, but the concentration and execution risks remain meaningfully two-sided. We score risk factors at 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV (36.1%) is rich versus 1-year historical volatility (27.1%), a 9-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (36.1% near-term versus 30.8% at six months), pricing an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is elevated at 7.3% with an implied move of ±7.4% on the nearest expiry, indicating some demand for downside protection. IV has eased modestly since the prior snapshot (37.7% → 36.1%) even as the stock rose 5%, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 0.13 to 0.05, so positioning is not heavily bearish in aggregate. The signal is consistent with our HOLD: elevated near-term uncertainty, but no extreme one-directional bet. Short interest is low at 3.7% of float (3.9 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** CNS is a high-quality, capital-light specialist trading at a steep relative-TSR discount that the market will eventually close. Record AUM, positive net flows, rising estimates, and a margin recovery in Q1 2026 suggest the 2025 compression was transitory. Listed infrastructure compounds at ~30% on durable AI-power and energy-transition demand, and the private-RE/ETF pivot converts to higher, stickier fees over time, re-rating the franchise toward its asset-manager peer cohort. In this scenario the stock works toward the high-$80s ($85–90), roughly 13–20% upside, helped by multiple recovery as flows accelerate.

**Bear case.** A rate or REIT shock hits the ~64% real-estate-concentrated AUM directly, reversing the recovery much as 2023 did. The ETF entry fails to gain traction against entrenched, track-record-rich competitors; the private-RE buildout absorbs capital without commensurate returns; founders begin monetizing the S-3 shelf; and the CFO transition disrupts execution. Estimates roll over and the structural-fee-pressure bear thesis (the strong-sell analyst) is vindicated. In this scenario the stock works toward the low-$50s ($52–55), the analyst low target, roughly 27–31% downside.

**Base case.** Steady mid-single-digit AUM and fee growth, a well-covered ~3.5% dividend, and a multiple that holds in the low-20s forward. The stock tracks earnings growth with no re-rating, ending roughly flat-to-modestly-higher around $77–80 over 12 months. The dividend provides the bulk of the total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The thesis rests on yield support and a durable, cash-generative franchise rather than expected appreciation. The risk/reward is balanced: ~13–20% upside to the bull case against ~27–31% downside to the bear, with a base case near the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic-growth acceleration: sustained net inflows above ~2% of beginning AUM for two consecutive quarters, particularly if active ETFs cross a credible AUM threshold (e.g., the suite reaching $2B+ with positive flows).
- Evidence the private-RE/ETF pivot is fee-rate-accretive: blended fee rate stabilizing or rising rather than mixing down, with the seed-capital drag rolling off.
- A multiple compression toward ~17x forward (high-$50s/low-$60s on unchanged fundamentals) that restores an asymmetric entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A rate/REIT shock driving net outflows and a decline in average AUM, with current-year estimates revised down >5%.
- Founders activating the S-3 shelf for a material secondary, creating a supply overhang.
- Margin re-compression below ~30% GAAP that signals the growth investment is structurally rather than transitorily dilutive.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the options market is pricing a catalyst there), with the CFO transition and ETF/CNSREIT flow data the key items to monitor through year-end 2026.

**On trajectory:** Scores have oscillated (5.6 → 5.0 → 5.4) with the recommendation flipping HOLD → AVOID → HOLD, reflecting a genuinely balanced setup rather than a clear directional thesis. The current data confirms the balanced read: financial health is the strongest and most stable dimension (the FCF artifact correction supports a 7.0), while valuation and competitive position remain the constraints. The stock is ~$3.60 (5%) higher than at the prior report on essentially unchanged fundamentals and modestly improved estimates, which marginally tightens the upside but does not change the income-hold conclusion. We hold the overall at 5.4.

## Conclusion

Cohen & Steers is an operationally sound, high-margin specialist asset manager at record AUM, with a clean balance sheet, a well-covered ~3.5% dividend, and constructive estimate-revision momentum. The reported negative free cash flow is an accounting artifact of seed-fund consolidation, not distress. The case against owning it for appreciation is equally clear: a ~64% real-estate-concentrated, rate-sensitive book; secular fee pressure on the active-management core; a margin-dilutive, execution-heavy pivot into late-entry ETFs and capital-intensive private real estate; a founder ownership overhang; and a CFO transition mid-pivot. At ~21.5x forward earnings with a PEG above 2 and a polarized analyst panel, the stock is fairly valued with no identifiable 12-month re-rating catalyst.

We rate CNS a HOLD on an income basis. Own it for the yield and the durable advisory cash engine; do not expect it to close its relative-TSR gap absent organic-flow acceleration or evidence the product pivot is converting to fee-accretive AUM.
