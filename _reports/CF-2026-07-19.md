**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer and one of the largest global nitrogen fertilizer manufacturers, structurally advantaged by cheap North American natural gas feedstock. The Q1 2026 print was strong on the surface (net sales +19% to $1.99B, gross margin 37.6%, net earnings to common +97% to $615M, diluted EPS +115% to $3.98), but the quality of that beat requires careful reading. Roughly $219M of pre-tax income came from non-core items: a $170M Orica litigation settlement, $25M of Yazoo City property-damage insurance recovery, and $24M of 45Q carbon-capture credits. The operating engine behind the rest is a 28% jump in average selling price to $424/ton, driven almost entirely by an exogenous supply shock: the March 2026 Strait of Hormuz closure amid the Iran conflict plus offline Qatari LNG, which together removed a large share of traded Middle East ammonia and urea. Management's own MD&A is unusually candid that these drivers are transient.

The central tension is that CF is a genuine beneficiary of the disruption while the disruption itself is mean-reverting. The world model tracks the Hormuz situation as a draft-MoU-at-verification-threshold event; a verified reopening pulls the nitrogen price premium out of CF's earnings on a lag, exactly as it reverses the energy-driven PPI surge. Estimate revisions capture this two-sided character: consensus current-year EPS rose +49.2% over 90 days on the price spike but has since rolled over -2.4% over the trailing 30 days, and next-quarter estimates fell -6.5% in 30 days even after an +86.6% 90-day surge. The 90-day revision magnitude clears the threshold that normally flags an inflection, but the deceleration and the explicitly temporary catalyst prevent it from being a durable earnings-momentum signal.

Layered on top are three fresh overhangs that did not exist a year ago: a March 2026 Sherman Act antitrust class action naming CF alongside Mosaic, Nutrien, Koch, Yara, and Canpotex; a December 2025 executive order creating DOJ/FTC fertilizer price-fixing task forces; and a simultaneous CEO handoff (Christopher Bohn succeeding Anthony Will) with the CFO seat held by an interim controller after Gregory Cameron's January 2026 separation. Against a fortress cost position, ~$1.79B of parent liquidity, and an aggressive buyback, the setup is symmetric. We rate CF HOLD, consistent with the prior four reports, and the score has drifted lower (6.2 → 5.6 over six weeks) as the war premium and one-time items become clearer.

## Company Overview

CF operates nine manufacturing complexes across the US, Canada, and the UK, anchored by Donaldsonville, LA (the largest ammonia complex in the world), plus Medicine Hat (largest in Canada), Port Neal, Verdigris, Woodward, Yazoo City, Courtright, Waggaman, and Billingham. The company fixes atmospheric nitrogen using hydrogen from natural gas to produce anhydrous ammonia, which is sold directly or upgraded into granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate, and industrial products including diesel exhaust fluid.

Q1 2026 segment gross margins: Granular Urea $255M, UAN $250M, Ammonia $227M, AN -$11M (dragged by the Yazoo City outage), and Other $25M. The economic moat is stranded, low-cost North American gas feedstock, which lets CF earn outsized margins when the marginal global producer (reliant on higher-cost imported LNG in India, Pakistan, and Europe) sets the price. The ownership structure has a wrinkle: five of six US plants sit inside CF Industries Nitrogen, LLC, of which CHS owns ~10%, producing the ~$2.9B of noncontrolling interest on the balance sheet and ~$61M/quarter of NCI-attributable earnings. CF also holds a 50% stake in Point Lisas (Trinidad) and consolidates the Blue Point low-carbon ammonia JV as a VIE.

## Financial Analysis

The financial base is strong on any absolute measure. Q1 operating cash flow was $496M (down from $586M on a working-capital build and a ~$95M Canadian tax deposit). Cash stands at $2.04B, of which $254M is ring-fenced inside the Blue Point VIE with no recourse to the parent, leaving parent liquidity closer to $1.79B. Long-term debt is $3.25B principal across four senior-note tranches (5.15% due 2034, 5.30% due 2035, 4.95% due 2043, 5.375% due 2044), with no revolver drawn against $750M available. In late 2025 CF issued $1B of 2035 notes and repaid $750M of 2026 notes, a clean maturity extension that removes near-term refinancing pressure. Total equity is $8.26B, of which $2.91B is NCI.

The financial-health scoring here requires ignoring the Altman Z-Score of ~1.4, which flags "distress." That is a false signal for a capital-intensive commodity producer: the low score reflects consolidated NCI/VIE debt structures inflating D/E to ~2.9x and low asset turnover, not solvency risk. With ~25% net margins, ~37% ROE, no revolver draw, and investment-grade notes, the balance sheet is resilient. The offset that keeps the score in the mid-6s rather than higher is the earnings quality issue: the non-recurring pre-tax income means the clean run-rate is materially below the $3.98 headline. Financial health remains the most reliable downside filter in our framework, and CF clears the bottom-third bar comfortably; it does not reach top-tier because the leverage runs above tech-style norms and the cash figure flatters true parent flexibility.

Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly at the margin: the $3B 2022 buyback completed in October 2025, a new $2B program (through 2029) has ~$1.71B remaining, and share count fell from 168.8M to 154.5M year-over-year, with the ~8% reduction contributing materially to the EPS beat. CF raised the dividend roughly 20% in mid-2026, though the yield remains modest at ~2.0%, well below the peer median of 4.4%.

## Growth Analysis

The apparent growth is a price-cycle artifact, not structural expansion. The 28% selling-price increase added ~$401M to sales; volume growth was not the story. The earnings-surprise record is mixed: three beats and one miss over four quarters (the Q2 2025 miss was -10.3%), and the two largest beats (Q4 2025 +24.9%, Q1 2026 +8.2%) coincide with the supply-shock window. The estimate-revision picture is the clearest tell on durability: current-year EPS is $17.78, up +49.2% over 90 days but down -2.4% over 30 days; next quarter is down -6.5% over 30 days despite an +86.6% 90-day surge; next year sits at $11.06, implying the market already expects normalization back toward a mid-single-digit-per-quarter run-rate as the war premium unwinds.

The +30% estimate-revision buy signal is present in the 90-day window but does not apply here for two reasons. First, the guardrail: the signal is strongest on inflection stocks recovering from a trough with cheap forward multiples and accelerating fundamentals. CF is the opposite; earnings are at a cyclical peak inflated by a transient shock, and the 30-day revisions are negative, indicating consensus is already backing away. Second, the underlying revenue driver is a mean-reverting geopolitical premium that management itself characterizes as temporary. Growth prospects therefore score below the midpoint: the durable component is low-single-digit volume growth plus buyback-driven per-share accretion, and the visible catalyst (low-carbon ammonia via Blue Point) is a 2029+ payoff with real capital and demand risk.

The one genuine long-term growth lever is low-carbon ammonia. The Donaldsonville CCS project came online in July 2025 and generated the 45Q credits in Q1 2026, enabling up to ~1.9M tons/yr of low-carbon ammonia. Blue Point (CF 40%, JERA 35%, Mitsui 25%) carries committed Japanese offtake for power generation and steel, with first production only in 2029 and CF's total commitment near $3.7B (~$240M in 2026). This is optionality with a long fuse, not a near-term earnings contributor.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at 10.7x trailing and forward P/E on a current price of $121.43, against a peer median of 40.7x. The cheapness is superficial and reflects the value-trap dynamic: a low multiple built on peak-cycle earnings that consensus expects to fall. Applying the same forward multiple to the $11.06 next-year EPS (the market's implied normalized figure) yields ~$118, essentially the current price, which indicates the market is already discounting the reversion rather than mispricing it. Peer comparables here are heterogeneous and of limited use: Mosaic trades at 148.8x on depressed earnings, FMC is negative, Scotts at 17.7x, and Corteva at 40.7x; none is a clean nitrogen-pure-play read-through.

The correct valuation lens for a commodity producer at a cyclical peak is cycle-adjusted earnings power, and on that basis CF is fairly-to-fully valued rather than cheap. In our framework, a compressed multiple on peak earnings is a warning flag, not a buy signal, and this dimension has been reliably anti-predictive: the market is not systematically mispricing cheap-looking names, and CF fits the pattern. Analyst targets frame the range: mean $125.20, median $120.00, low $100, high $195.72. Because CF sits at a peak with negative 30-day revisions rather than an inflection with cheap forward multiples, we treat the median target as a fair anchor rather than a stale floor; the analyst-targets-as-floors principle does not apply when estimates are falling.

The prior valuation score fell from 7.5 to 5.0 across the trajectory as the one-time items and war-premium dependence became clearer. We hold near 4.5. The stock currently trades below the ~$134-136 range where insiders, including former CEO Will, were selling earlier in 2026, a soft sentiment marker that argues against penalizing the current price further, but not enough to establish asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's structural position is durable and, if anything, improving at the margin. Nitrogen is a globally traded commodity where the marginal high-cost producer sets the price; CF's persistent North American gas-cost advantage means it captures widening spreads whenever global gas or supply tightens. The 2026 environment is unusually favorable: Middle East supply (25-40% of traded nitrogen) disrupted, Qatari LNG offline for what its government describes as multiple years to fully restore, and Indian/Pakistani/Bangladeshi producers reliant on imported LNG curtailed. CF made its first premium low-carbon ammonia sales to Europe and Africa in 2025, positioning it for EU carbon-border-mechanism-exposed customers.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving, but two caveats keep the score at 6.0 rather than higher. First, the current margin windfall is cyclical, not a share gain; CF is not taking structural volume from competitors, it is earning a temporary spread. Second, the Yazoo City outage (idled since November 2025, rebuild not expected before Q4 2026) is a reminder of single-site concentration vulnerability that turned the AN segment margin-negative through most of 2026. Materials is a structurally weak alpha sector in our framework, so we require company-specific evidence of durable outperformance, and CF's cost moat qualifies as such, but the moat monetizes only when the cycle cooperates.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile has three genuinely new, unpriced-or-partially-priced elements that pull this dimension below the midpoint.

**Antitrust litigation and government scrutiny.** The March 2026 putative class of direct fertilizer purchasers alleges a Sherman Act conspiracy dating to 2020-21, with parallel indirect-purchaser and state suits and a JPML consolidation hearing set for May 28, 2026. The suit seeks treble/punitive damages, and CF says it cannot estimate potential losses, leaving an open-ended overhang. Combined with the December 2025 DOJ/FTC task forces probing fertilizer price-fixing as CF earns record margins, this private treble-damage exposure plus active federal antitrust attention is the most material idiosyncratic risk and is unlikely to be fully priced.

**War-premium reversal.** The entire margin uplift rests on the supply shock management calls temporary. A verified, held Strait of Hormuz reopening (currently at the highest-quality de-escalation signal in over twenty cycles but below the physical-verification threshold) restores Middle East nitrogen flows and compresses CF's realized pricing on a lag. This is the single largest driver of the bear case.

**Governance transition.** The simultaneous CEO handoff (Bohn, an internal ex-CFO, provides continuity) and interim-CFO gap after Cameron's separation is a watch-item. Bohn's background mitigates concern, but the absence of a permanent CFO during a peak-earnings, litigation-heavy period is a governance flag.

**Secondary risks:** natural gas cost volatility (39% of Q1 production cost, up 24% to $4.57/MMBtu on a January winter-storm spike, cutting gross margin ~$76M, with only modest hedging); the Yazoo City rebuild timeline; Blue Point execution and capital risk; and tariff/trade uncertainty (ammonia is not currently exempt from the 10% blanket tariff, and ~1/3 of Blue Point's $3.7B is imported equipment).

Insider activity is predominantly dispositions tied to option exercises and RSU/PRSU vesting at elevated prices, routine wealth management rather than a bearish signal on its own, but there is no open-market buying to signal conviction at $121. Combined with the CEO/CFO transition and the absence of a fresh insider-buy cluster, management quality scores at 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating a directional move in either direction. Near-term ATM IV is 41.5% against 1-year historical vol of 41.4%, fair, with the IV percentile at just 2% of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at -0.3%, the put/call volume ratio is a call-leaning 0.51, and the P/C OI ratio fell sharply from 0.65 to 0.24 as the stock rose 18% since the prior snapshot. The term structure is flat (near 41.5% ≈ far 43.3%), with uncertainty evenly distributed and the implied nearest-expiry move ±4.6%. The one nuance is a widening put skew at the 6-month tenor (-4.8%), consistent with hedging against a war-premium reversal over the horizon where a Hormuz reopening would most plausibly resolve. IV neither rich nor cheap confirms the fundamental read: no near-term catalyst is being priced, and positioning is neutral-to-mildly-constructive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($150-165).** The Hormuz disruption persists or a grinding conflict keeps Middle East nitrogen offline into 2027, sustaining $400+/ton selling prices well beyond consensus. Qatari LNG stays offline, keeping global marginal costs elevated. Yazoo City restarts on schedule in Q4 2026, restoring AN/DEF earnings, and business-interruption insurance provides back-half catalysts. The $2B buyback compounds per-share earnings against a shrinking float, and Blue Point de-risks as JERA/Mitsui offtake firms up. The Scotiabank contrarian framing (that fertilizer equities are near a cyclical bottom) proves right, and CF re-rates toward the mid-$150s to $165, roughly the high end of the sell-side range.

**Bear case ($85-95).** A verified, held Hormuz reopening restores Middle East supply, nitrogen prices normalize, and the one-time and war-premium earnings evaporate. Consensus current-year EPS continues falling toward the $11 normalized figure implied by next-year estimates. The antitrust class action advances with adverse discovery, the DOJ/FTC task force pressures pricing, and gas costs spike again. On a ~9x multiple against normalized ~$10-11 EPS, the stock retraces to the high-$80s to mid-$90s, back toward levels seen through late 2025.

**Base case (~$118-122).** Selling prices grind lower through H2 2026 as partial de-escalation reduces but does not eliminate the premium, offset by buyback accretion and a Yazoo restart. Earnings normalize toward the ~$11-12 range consensus already embeds for next year, and the stock holds near current levels, approximately fair on cycle-adjusted earnings power.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the setup is symmetric, and we lack the directional edge for a stronger call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) 30-day estimate revisions turn positive again alongside evidence the nitrogen-price premium is structural rather than war-driven, for example a durable global supply deficit independent of Hormuz; (2) a clustered open-market insider-buy signal at current or lower levels, indicating management conviction absent from the current disposition-heavy pattern; (3) a pullback into the mid-$80s to low-$90s that restores asymmetric upside on normalized earnings; or (4) resolution of the antitrust overhang without material liability.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) a verified, held Hormuz reopening (72+ hours of sustained commercial transit) that removes the price premium while the stock still trades above ~$120 on peak earnings; (2) an adverse antitrust development (class certification, unfavorable JPML consolidation, or DOJ/FTC enforcement action with quantifiable liability); or (3) a guidance cut or a Yazoo City rebuild slippage beyond Q4 2026 alongside continued negative revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (following the May 6 Q1 print), plus the JPML antitrust consolidation outcome and any confirmed Hormuz-transit verification, whichever comes first. Given the war-premium dependence, the Hormuz situation is the dominant near-term swing factor.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a high-quality, low-cost commodity producer at a cyclical earnings peak that is being flattered by a transient geopolitical supply shock and the Q1 one-time items. The balance sheet is genuinely strong, the cost moat is durable, and buybacks provide per-share support, but the core problem remains: the earnings driving the current valuation are mean-reverting, consensus is already backing away (30-day revisions negative), and fresh antitrust and political overhangs have surfaced as margins peak. The valuation looks cheap on trailing numbers and fairly-valued-to-full on cycle-adjusted power. With no open-market insider buying, a CEO/interim-CFO transition, and neutral options positioning, there is no directional edge at $121.

We reiterate HOLD, consistent with the four prior reports, and the declining score trajectory (6.2 → 5.6) reflects clearer visibility into the war-premium and earnings-quality issues rather than any new fundamental deterioration. The overall score of 5.2 sits near the middle of the range, appropriate for a stock whose two-sided outcome hinges primarily on a geopolitical event the company does not control. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$80s to mid-$90s or on evidence the pricing tailwind is structural, and more cautious on a verified Hormuz reopening with the stock still above $120.
