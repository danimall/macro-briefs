**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cummins is two businesses sharing one ticker. Power Systems and Distribution have become a high-growth, high-margin data-center power franchise: Power Systems grew EBITDA 48% YoY in Q1 2026 to $577M at a 29.5% margin, with power-generation sales up 28% on AI infrastructure demand. The Engine and Components segments are in a cyclical downturn: Q1 2026 Engine EBITDA fell 39% with North American heavy-duty shipments down 16% and medium-duty down 25%. Consolidated revenue looks placid (+3% in Q1, -1% for FY2025), masking the internal divergence. The investment question is whether the power-gen growth engine plus a second-half 2026 truck recovery can drive the next up-cycle. The Street, led by UBS's June upgrade to $850, is betting it can.

The fundamental story is improving, and the trajectory data confirms it: financial health and growth prospects have both stepped up across the last two reports while valuation has compressed. The problem is price. CMI has appreciated 128% over the trailing twelve months and trades at 37x trailing earnings and roughly 25x the $28.20 current-year consensus. The stock sits at $714.99, below the $744 consensus mean and the $755 median, while analyst sentiment is degrading (hold ratings rising, the buy-rating count thinning at the margin). The re-rating that the bull case requires has substantially already happened. That is the reason this is a HOLD and not a BUY: there is no margin of safety to absorb a continued Engine drawdown, a deregulation-driven non-cash credit writeoff, or a delayed truck recovery, and the options market is pricing imminent stress with near-term IV at 97.5% in steep backwardation.

I rate CMI a growth HOLD. The power-gen pivot is real and durable, deserving credit in growth and financial-health scores. But the entry point offers asymmetric downside, and I would wait for a pullback toward the high-$500s/low-$600s — closer to the analyst low target of $520 and the level where forward multiples compress below 22x — before adding.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1919 in Columbus, Indiana, Cummins is a global power systems company operating through five segments:

- **Engine (~26% of sales):** Diesel, natural gas, and gasoline engines up to 15L for heavy- and medium-duty trucks, light-duty automotive (Stellantis), and off-highway applications. The most cyclical segment and the one currently in the deepest downturn.
- **Components (~25%):** Aftertreatment, turbochargers, fuel systems, drivetrain, and automated transmissions (Eaton Cummins JV). Shrank materially after the March 2024 Atmus filtration spin-off.
- **Distribution (~30%, now the largest):** Parts, service, and power generation through roughly 640 locations, a recurring aftermarket annuity stream now augmented by data-center power demand.
- **Power Systems (~18%, the growth engine):** Standby and prime generators, high-horsepower engines, and alternators (Stamford/AVK). The primary beneficiary of the AI/data-center power buildout.
- **Accelera (~1%):** Zero-emissions technologies (battery, electric powertrains, electrolyzers), now in active retreat.

Roughly 50% of equity income comes from three Chinese JVs (Chongqing, Dongfeng, Beijing Foton). PACCAR is the only customer above 10% of sales (13%, down from 16%), and the top four OEMs account for 31%. Both ratings agencies hold Cummins at the A tier with stable outlooks (S&P A, Moody's A2).

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 headline:** Revenue $33.67B (-1% YoY); net income attributable $2.84B (-28%); diluted EPS $20.50. The 28% net-income decline is largely optical: FY2024's $3.95B was inflated by a one-time $1.3B non-taxable Atmus divestiture gain. Underlying gross margin improved to 25.3% from 24.7%, and FY2025 operating income was a healthy ~$4.0B (~12% margin), up 7% YoY. ROE was 23.9%, ROA 8.7%. Any screen showing a negative operating margin or a "grey zone" Altman Z-score of 2.07 is picking up a computation artifact and a moderate-leverage industrial profile, not distress: net leverage is 0.22x and the credit ratings are solidly investment-grade.

**The segment divergence is the entire story.** FY2025 EBITDA by segment, YoY:
- Power Systems +44% to $1,694M; margin to 22.7% from 18.4%
- Distribution +31% to $1,808M; margin 14.6%
- Engine -16% to $1,382M (NA heavy-duty shipments -23%, total units -12%)
- Components -12%; Accelera -$896M (including $458M of charges)

**Q1 2026 confirmed the divergence is accelerating.** Revenue $8.40B (+3%); EPS $4.71 vs $5.96 (-21%, distorted by a $199M Accelera charge tied to the low-pressure fuel-cell business sale and customer-obligation settlement). Distribution EBITDA rose 18%; Engine margin collapsed to 10.4% from 16.5%. Off-highway grew 25% on China construction, a bright spot inside an otherwise weak Engine segment.

**Cash and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow rebounded to $3.6B (from $1.5B, which had absorbed the $1.9B EPA settlement payment), with FCF of $2.4B. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $309M (vs -$3M prior year). Capex is steady at ~$1.2B, guided to $1.35–1.45B for 2026 with 65%+ directed to North American power-gen capacity. The dividend was raised 10% to $2.00/quarter in mid-2025, and buybacks resumed in Q1 2026 ($243M at ~$537/share) with ~$2.0B of authorization remaining. The balance sheet carries $3.2B cash, debt/capital of 36.5%, and pension plans funded above 110%.

This is a financially resilient, cash-generative industrial with improving earnings quality as the one-timers (2023 EPA charge, 2024 Atmus gain, 2025 Accelera charges, Q1 2026 fuel-cell charge) wash out. Financial health earns a 7.8.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the durability of the data-center power tailwind, and the evidence is broad rather than single-source. Distribution power-gen grew 17% in Q1 2026. Management cited a data-center backlog extending six to eight quarters and, at the May 21, 2026 Analyst Day, raised long-term targets across the board. The natural-gas microgrid deals (Circe Energy, West Texas HPC) show Cummins moving up the value chain from backup gensets toward primary behind-the-meter generation, a higher-value and stickier position.

Estimate revisions support the momentum: current-year consensus rose 7.8% over 90 days to $28.20, next-year +4.6% to $32.35. The earnings-surprise record is strong (three beats in four quarters, the lone miss being the Q4 2025 print distorted by charges). The Q1 2026 beat of 9.0% reinforces that consensus has been catching up to the power-gen reality.

The constraint on the growth score is the Engine cycle. The bull case for the next earnings up-leg explicitly requires a North American truck recovery in H2 2026. Management's tone turned slightly more constructive (NA truck to "begin improving over the remainder of 2026," versus the 10-K's "weak in first half"), but this remains forward-looking optimism in a segment where shipments are still down double digits. Growth prospects earn a 7.3 — above neutral on the power-gen secular story and rising estimates, but capped because roughly half the consolidated business is cyclically contracting and the recovery is not yet in the data.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the BUY case breaks. CMI trades at 37.3x trailing EPS of $20.50 and roughly 25.4x the $28.20 current-year consensus, ~22.1x the $32.35 next-year estimate. Against peers, the picture is mixed: Caterpillar trades at 44.7x and Wabtec at 37.1x (peer median 37.1x), while Paccar trades at 25.0x. CMI is at the peer median on trailing P/E, so it is not a screaming outlier, but it is not cheap, and the peer group itself has been re-rated by the same power and infrastructure themes.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the stock here. The estimate-revision velocity (+7.8% on the current year over 90 days) is real and positive, but it falls well short of the +30% threshold that would justify treating trailing multiples as meaningless. This is a quality industrial with solid mid-single-digit-to-low-teens earnings momentum, not a trough-earnings stock tripling its EPS. PEG on the ~15% forward EPS growth between this year and next ($28.20 → $32.35) against a ~25x forward multiple sits above 1.5, not the sub-1.5 that would flag mispricing.

The clearest evidence the re-rating is complete: at $714.99, CMI trades just below the consensus mean target (4.1% upside) and the median (5.6% upside), with the analyst low at $520 (-27%) and high at $874 (+22%). The analyst-targets-are-floors logic does not apply; that principle is reserved for stocks that look cheap on forward metrics with sharply rising estimates. Here the stock is near consensus, estimate-revision velocity is moderate, and sentiment is degrading (hold ratings rose from 9 to 11, a 22% increase, while the buy count thinned). When sentiment is degrading and the stock sits near the mean target, consensus is more likely to be a ceiling than a floor.

The 128% trailing-twelve-month appreciation is not itself a reason to mark the stock down; the absence of a margin of safety into a still-deteriorating Engine cycle is. A reasonable base case applies ~24x to next year's $32.35, yielding ~$775 over a 12-month horizon, roughly 8% from here, which does not clear the bar for a quality industrial carrying real cyclical and regulatory downside. Valuation earns a 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Cummins, Caterpillar, and Rehlko (formerly Kohler Energy) are the primary beneficiaries of data-center backup and prime-power demand, a structural multi-year tailwind re-rating the entire power-gen complex. Cummins' edge is its integrated portfolio: engines, alternators, controls, and a 640-location distribution and service network providing recurring aftermarket revenue. The move toward natural-gas microgrids and behind-the-meter prime power moves the company up the value chain into stickier, higher-value positions. Eighty-one years of PACCAR partnership and deep OEM relationships (Traton, Daimler, Stellantis) anchor the engine franchise.

The competitive trajectory is positive in power and neutral-to-eroding in engines. The February 2026 EPA rescission of on-highway GHG standards removes part of the emissions-compliance technology moat that historically differentiated Cummins from lower-cost competitors and won OEM business. Vertical-integration risk is explicit: the major OEM customers also build their own engines. On balance, the competitive position is improving where it matters most (power) but facing a newly weakened moat in the legacy engine business. Competitive position earns a 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

Several risks are not fully reflected in a stock trading near the consensus target:

1. **North American truck cyclicality is materializing, not hypothetical.** The H2 2026 recovery is a management forecast, not a data point. A delayed recovery removes the second leg of the bull thesis.

2. **The deregulation paradox.** Less environmental regulation is a net negative here. The February 2026 EPA rescission strands clean-tech investment (Accelera, Amplify) and erodes the compliance moat, while putting $99M of emission compliance credits (down from $127M at year-end 2025) at risk of a non-cash writeoff.

3. **Accelera is being wound down, not scaled.** Within ~18 months: electrolyzer exit, fuel-cell business sold, and the Amplify battery JV (30%-owned with Daimler and PACCAR) delayed indefinitely, with production start now uncertain versus a prior 2028 target. Cumulative Accelera losses and charges exceed $1.0B across 2024–Q1 2026. The $412M Amplify carrying value (contributed) plus $418M future commitment is a candidate for further impairment.

4. **Customer and China concentration.** PACCAR at 13% of sales; top four OEMs at 31%. Roughly 50% of equity income flows from three uncontrolled Chinese JVs exposed to China demand, US-China export controls, and repatriation friction.

5. **Tariffs.** Net impact has been immaterial through Q1 2026 on mitigation and pass-through, but the February 2026 Supreme Court strike-down of IEEPA tariffs and reimposition under other statutes leaves forward uncertainty.

These are bounded but real, and at current valuation there is little cushion. Risk factors earn a 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing a near-term stress signal. Near-term ATM IV is 97.5% against one-year historical volatility of 33.8%, a 63.7-point spread, with IV in the 85th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (97.5% near-term vs 44.4% at three months), which the market uses to price imminent, event-driven uncertainty rather than a sustained regime. ATM IV jumped 99.6 points since the early-May snapshot on a flat price, indicating the market is positioning for a catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.6%) and the put/call volume ratio is 0.82, so this is elevated uncertainty around an upcoming event rather than a directional bearish bet. For a BUY at this price, the near-term implied move of ±4.7% and the rich IV argue against chasing; for a HOLD, it reinforces patience. The low short interest (1.7% of float, 2.1 days to cover) confirms there is no crowded bearish positioning to fade.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to ~$874, +22%)
The data-center power tailwind proves multi-year and durable. Power Systems sustains 30%+ EBITDA margins and high-double-digit growth, Distribution power-gen compounds, and the NA truck cycle turns positive in H2 2026, re-accelerating Engine and Components. Consolidated EBITDA growth re-accelerates into 2027 as one-timers fully clear. Next-year EPS beats the $32.35 consensus, and the market applies a ~27x multiple on the strength of the re-rated power business. UBS's $850 target and the raised Analyst Day long-term targets anchor this scenario.

### Bear Case (to ~$520, -27%)
The NA truck recovery slips into 2027, leaving Engine and Components in a prolonged downturn. The data-center backlog growth decelerates, exposing the ~25x forward multiple as priced for perfection. The $99M emission-credit writeoff lands, further Amplify impairment follows, and the Chinese JV equity income softens on China demand weakness. Multiple compresses to ~18–20x forward as a cyclical industrial, pulling the stock to the analyst low.

The base case sits between these, roughly $775 on ~24x next year's $32.35, about 8% from current levels over twelve months. The skew from $714.99 is asymmetric to the downside: ~22% to the bull case versus ~27% to the bear, with the most-likely outcome a modest gain that does not justify chasing near the consensus target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale: the underlying franchise is improving, but the entry point is unattractive.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback to the high-$500s/low-$600s (forward P/E below ~22x on the $32.35 next-year estimate), restoring a margin of safety. The high-$500s sits near the analyst low and would create a 2:1 reward/risk to the base case.
- Confirmed NA truck-shipment recovery in reported data: heavy-duty or medium-duty shipments turning positive YoY in a quarterly print, not just management commentary.
- Power Systems backlog re-confirmed extending beyond eight quarters with margins sustained above 28%, alongside current-year estimate revisions accelerating above +20% over 90 days.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NA truck recovery pushed to 2027 or later in management guidance, with Engine EBITDA margin remaining below 11%.
- A material Amplify impairment combined with the $99M emission-credit writeoff and a guidance cut.
- Data-center backlog growth decelerating, with Power Systems revenue growth dropping below 10% YoY.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), which will provide the first hard read on the H2 truck-recovery thesis and the durability of power-gen backlog. The elevated near-term options volatility points to a catalyst being priced; monitor for the resolution.

## Conclusion

Cummins has executed a credible pivot from a cyclical engine maker toward a data-center power supplier. Financial health is strong, earnings quality is improving as one-timers clear, and estimate revisions are positive. The trajectory across the last two reports — financial health and growth stepping up, valuation compressing — captures a business getting better and a stock getting more expensive relative to that improvement.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY, despite a constructive 6.2 composite, is specific and falsifiable: at $714.99 the stock trades near the $744 consensus mean target with analyst sentiment degrading, at ~25x forward earnings on ~15% forward EPS growth (PEG above 1.5), with no margin of safety to absorb a still-deteriorating Engine cycle (-39% segment EBITDA), a $99M deregulation-driven credit writeoff risk, potential further Amplify impairment, and a near-term options market pricing imminent stress (97.5% IV in backwardation). The base case offers ~8% upside against ~27% downside to the bear case. I would buy this franchise on a pullback toward the high-$500s/low-$600s, where forward multiples compress below 22x and the reward/risk turns favorable.
