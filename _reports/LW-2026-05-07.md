## Executive Summary

Lamb Weston is the dominant North American frozen potato producer navigating a severe cyclical trough driven by industry overcapacity, restaurant traffic weakness, and the aftereffects of an aggressive acquisition-funded expansion. The stock has declined 53% from its 52-week high of $67.07 to $43.23, reflecting earnings that have halved from $4.98 (FY24) to $2.50 (FY25) with further deterioration in FY26.

The investment case rests on three specific catalysts within a 12-month window: (1) JANA Partners purchased $16M+ of stock at $40-43 in April 2026 while already on the board — the single strongest bullish insider signal possible from an activist with direct operational visibility; (2) QSR traffic turned positive in Q3 FY26 for the first time since late FY24, with burger traffic growing in February 2026; and (3) cost savings of $250M+ annualized by FY28 ($200M by FY27) are being implemented under new leadership with AB InBev operational turnaround credentials. However, the 4.25x D/E ratio, 1.73 Altman Z-Score, and potential for prolonged industry overcapacity create meaningful downside risk that prevents a BUY recommendation at current levels.

The critical question is whether earnings stabilize before covenants are pressured. Operating cash flow of $868M (FY25) and normalizing capex (~$500M in FY26 vs $638M in FY25) provide a path to FCF of $300-400M, supporting debt service. The company is not in imminent distress but has minimal margin for error.

## Company Overview

Lamb Weston is the #1 supplier of value-added frozen potato products in North America (~25%+ market share) and a leading global supplier, operating manufacturing facilities across 8 countries and serving customers in 100+ markets. French fries constitute the majority of revenue.

**Revenue split:** North America (~66%) and International (~34%). The North America segment serves QSR chains (McDonald's is the single-largest customer), foodservice distributors, and retail channels. The International segment operates in Europe, Asia-Pacific, and Latin America.

The company was spun off from ConAgra in 2016 and expanded significantly through the LW EMEA acquisition (completed FY24, ~$700M implied value), which added European manufacturing capacity but coincided with the onset of industry overcapacity.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory — severe decline:**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY26 (9mo) |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|-------------|
| Revenue | $5.35B | $6.47B | $6.45B | $4.84B |
| Gross Margin | 26.8% | 27.3% | 21.7% | ~20% |
| Operating Margin | 16.5% | 16.5% | 10.3% | ~7.5% |
| Diluted EPS | $6.95 | $4.98 | $2.50 | ~$1.30 |

The margin compression is driven by: (1) aggressive price/mix reductions (-7% in most recent quarter) to recover volume lost during the ERP transition and to compete in an oversupplied market, (2) higher input costs (potatoes, edible oils, energy), and (3) incremental depreciation from new capacity coming online.

**Balance sheet — the binding constraint:**

- Total debt: ~$4.0B (as of Feb 2026)
- Debt/Equity: 4.25x
- Interest expense run rate: ~$180M/year
- Cash on hand: $57-71M
- Revolver availability: ~$1.2B
- Altman Z-Score: 1.73 (distress zone)
- Current ratio: adequate for near-term obligations

The leverage is extreme for a consumer staples company. Peer median leverage is closer to 2.0-2.5x. The debt was accumulated primarily through the EMEA acquisition and capacity expansion. Management is covenant-compliant as of February 2026, but sustained EBITDA declines below ~$1.0B would begin to pressure maintenance covenants.

**Cash flow — the saving grace:**

Operating cash flow was $868M in FY25 and $596M through 9 months of FY26 (vs $485M prior year). The improvement comes from working capital management (inventory reduction). With capex normalizing from $638M (FY25) toward $400-500M (FY26) and potentially ~$257M run rate thereafter, free cash flow should improve to $350-500M annually even at current depressed earnings. This cash flow generation provides the runway to service debt while the turnaround takes hold.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth is negative.** FY26 full-year EPS consensus is ~$2.81, which would represent a modest recovery from the depressed FY25 base when adjusting for restructuring charges. FY27 consensus is $3.00, implying low-single-digit growth.

**Identifiable catalysts for recovery:**

1. **QSR traffic inflection:** U.S. QSR traffic turned positive (+1%) in Q3 FY26 for the first time since late FY24. Burger traffic specifically returned to growth in February 2026. Each 1% improvement in restaurant traffic translates to approximately 1% volume growth for LW.

2. **Cost savings program:** $250M+ annualized savings by FY28, with $200M by FY27. Through Q3 FY26, the company has already delivered $90M in run-rate savings. This directly flows to EBITDA — $200M on a ~$1.1B base would represent an 18% uplift.

3. **Capacity rationalization:** LW has closed/curtailed multiple facilities (Munro, Argentina; Netherlands production line). If competitors follow with rationalization, pricing power should recover over 12-24 months.

4. **International potential divestiture:** Starboard Value is pressing for a sale/review of international operations. The segment generates ~$1.65B revenue at ~6% EBITDA margins. A sale at even 6-8x EBITDA (~$700-900M) could be used to reduce debt by 20%+, significantly improving the financial profile of the remaining North America-focused business.

**Growth constraints:** Industry overcapacity is structural and may take 18-24 months to resolve. Price/mix declines are expected to continue through FY26. GLP-1 drug adoption represents a longer-term secular headwind to french fry consumption, though magnitude is uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

At $43.23, Lamb Weston trades at:
- ~5.0x FY25 Adj. EBITDA ($1.22B)
- ~20x trailing P/E on depressed $2.50 EPS
- ~15.4x forward FY26 EPS ($2.81)
- ~14.4x forward FY27 EPS ($3.00)
- 3.6% dividend yield ($1.54/share)
- ~36% below 52-week high

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 11.4x, but this comparison is misleading — CPB at 11.4x is a declining business, CAG is generating losses, and MKC at 7.9x has its own issues. LW's forward P/E of ~15.4x on trough earnings is not unreasonable for a market leader in recovery mode.

**Trough valuation framework:** If the cost savings program delivers $200M by FY27 and volume continues to recover, normalized EBITDA could reach $1.3-1.4B. At 7-8x EV/EBITDA (below the historical 9-10x range given elevated leverage), enterprise value would be $9.1-11.2B. Subtracting $4.0B debt implies equity value of $5.1-7.2B, or $37-52/share. The current price of $43 sits in the lower-middle of this range, suggesting limited downside if the base case plays out but meaningful upside if execution exceeds expectations.

JANA's $40-43 buying provides a valuation signal from an informed insider. An activist with board seats, direct access to internal plans and data, buying $16M of stock is a concrete data point about their assessment of intrinsic value relative to market price. They are not buying for a 5% return.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market position:** LW remains the dominant North American frozen potato producer. Q3 FY26 volume growth of +12% in North America demonstrates the ability to win and retain contracts. The ERP-related customer losses from FY24 appear to have been largely recovered.

**Industry structure:** The frozen potato market is a consolidated oligopoly — LW, McCain, and Cavendish Farms (Canada) control the majority of North American production. Barriers to entry are substantial (capital-intensive processing, grower relationships, cold-chain logistics). This structural moat suggests that once overcapacity resolves, pricing power should return.

**International competitive position is weaker:** In Europe, LW faces well-established competitors (McCain, Aviko/Cosun) on their home turf. The LW EMEA acquisition has not yet delivered expected returns — International segment EBITDA declined 80% in Q3 FY26. This is the weakest part of the business and the most likely candidate for divestiture.

**Key risk to competitive position:** The intense price competition (7% price/mix decline) demonstrates that even market leaders cannot maintain pricing in an oversupplied environment. However, LW's scale advantages in procurement and manufacturing should allow it to outlast smaller competitors in a war of attrition.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical risks:**

1. **Leverage/covenant risk (HIGH):** At 4.25x D/E and 1.73 Z-Score, the company has minimal cushion. If EBITDA declines below ~$1.0B annualized, covenant compliance becomes a concern. The probability of this scenario is 15-20% given that EBITDA through 9 months of FY26 is $860M (annualizes to ~$1.15B) and cost savings are being realized.

2. **Prolonged industry overcapacity (MODERATE-HIGH):** If competitors do not rationalize capacity and restaurant traffic remains flat, the trough could extend 12-18 months beyond current expectations. This would pressure the turnaround timeline and potentially force dividend cuts.

3. **Antitrust litigation (MODERATE):** The class action alleging price coordination since 2021 covers a period when industry pricing was aggressive (LW revenue grew from $4.1B to $6.5B largely through pricing). If successful, treble damages could be material — potentially hundreds of millions. Discovery stage creates ongoing uncertainty.

4. **McDonald's concentration (MODERATE):** While not quantified in filings, loss of McDonald's would be devastating. The recent focus on customer partnerships as a strategic pillar acknowledges this dependency.

5. **Tariff exposure (MODERATE):** International segment faces potential tariff risk on both imports (Canadian potatoes) and exports. The April 2025 tariff actions cited in risk factors add uncertainty.

6. **Securities fraud litigation (LOW-MODERATE):** Class action related to ERP implementation and pricing disclosures (2023-2024 class period). Motion to dismiss pending.

## Conclusion

Lamb Weston presents a classic turnaround situation: a market-leading business with structural competitive advantages temporarily impaired by leverage and industry cyclicality. The HOLD recommendation reflects the tension between identifiable catalysts (activist-driven operational improvements, traffic recovery, potential divestiture) and the binding constraint of 4.25x leverage on a declining earnings trajectory.

The thesis that justifies holding rather than avoiding is specific: JANA Partners purchased $16M of stock at current prices while sitting on the board, QSR traffic has inflected positive, and $200M+ in cost savings are being implemented by new leadership with a proven operational track record. These are measurable developments that can be tracked quarterly. The 12-month expected value is positive given the asymmetry between a $50 base case (+16%) and the $30-35 bear case (-19-31%), weighted by the probability that earnings stabilize given demonstrated cash flow generation ($868M OCF on $4.0B debt = 22% coverage ratio before capex).

The stock is not a BUY because the leverage creates a 15-20% probability of a scenario where covenants are pressured and equity value is severely impaired. Until debt is meaningfully reduced — most likely through an international divestiture or 2-3 quarters of deleveraging via FCF — the risk/reward does not favor aggressive positioning.
