## Executive Summary

DT Midstream is a pure-play natural gas midstream company with a genuinely high-quality contract book on the pipeline side (~92% firm service, ~99% firm at the joint ventures), an investment-grade balance sheet with no maturities before 2029, and 2.3x dividend coverage on distributable cash flow. The Q2 2026 print reaffirmed both 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance ($1,155-1,225M) and the 2027 early outlook ($1,225-1,295M), added roughly $300M of new FIDs (LEAP Phase 5, first-phase Viking modernization), filed the FERC Section 7(c) application for Guardian G3, and brought the commercialized project backlog to $2B. The single most important operational development is that the Gathering segment, which posted three consecutive years of declining operating income ($229M in 2023 → $210M → $199M in 2025), inflected: H1 2026 segment Adjusted EBITDA rose from $166M to $199M (+20%), with the February 1, 2026 Stonewall-Mountain Valley Pipeline interconnect the identifiable driver (+$16M from Appalachia).

The problem is price, not business quality. At $132.92 the enterprise value is roughly $16.9B ($13.56B equity + $3.35B debt − $0.17B cash + $0.14B NCI), which is 14.2x guidance-midpoint 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $1,190M and 13.4x the 2027 midpoint of $1,260M — closer to 15.0x on 2026 if the roughly $0.9-1.1B of estimated proportional joint-venture debt is included. Against that, 2026 guidance implies only +1.5% to +7.6% EBITDA growth over the FY2025 actual $1,138M, and the 2025→2027 midpoint CAGR is 5.2%, roughly half the 10.3% four-year historical CAGR from 2021's $768M. Return on invested capital of about 7.0% ($571M after-tax operating profit including equity earnings on ~$8.17B of invested capital) sits essentially on top of the 6.8% Pipeline and 7.5% Gathering WACCs DTM itself used in its October 2025 goodwill test. The premium multiple is a pure call on brownfield conversion economics.

Two things have changed since our last review. First, the stock is down 13.1% from the May 26 high of $152.88 and about 8% from mid-July levels, on reaffirmed guidance — a modest improvement in risk/reward that we credit rather than penalize. Second, Williams agreed to acquire Momentum Midstream for up to $5.5B, adding more than 4,000 miles of Haynesville gathering and transmission, ~6 Bcf/d of system capacity, and take-or-pay lines capable of 4.05 Bcf/d, alongside the Shelby Trough Connector (750 MMcf/d, up to 1.5 Bcf/d, 2Q 2028) and $1.5B Delta Access (2.25 Bcf/d, 1Q 2029). DTM's LEAP is 2.1 Bcf/d targeting 2.3 Bcf/d by 2H 2028. Already-contracted LEAP expansions are insulated; what is at risk is the option value on uncontracted expansions beyond 2028, which is precisely what a 14x multiple on 5% growth requires. We maintain **HOLD** with a base case of ~$141, bull $165 and bear $105.

## Company Overview

DTM was spun out of DTE Energy in July 2021 and operates 588 employees across two segments. **Pipeline** was 55% of FY2025 consolidated revenue ($687M of $1,243M) and includes LEAP (221 miles, 2.1 Bcf/d, Haynesville-to-Gulf Coast takeaway), Stonewall (85%-owned), Bluestone, the Michigan System, the Washington 10 Storage Complex (94 Bcf working capacity, 91%-owned), Birdsboro, and the DTM Interstate Transportation group acquired at year-end 2024 — Guardian (1.3 Bcf/d into Wisconsin), Midwestern (1.5 Bcf/d, bi-directional) and Viking (1.0 Bcf/d, MN/ND/WI). The segment also holds three non-operated equity-method JVs: Millennium (52.5%), NEXUS (50%) and Vector (40%), carried at $1,243M at June 30, 2026.

**Gathering** was 45% of FY2025 revenue ($556M), anchored by Blue Union (443 miles, 2.6 Bcf/d, Haynesville) plus Appalachia Gathering, Susquehanna, Tioga, Ohio Utica and the small Clean Fuels coal-mine-methane system.

The contract asymmetry between segments is the operating story. Pipeline revenue is ~92% firm reservation charges; Gathering is only ~57% firm with ~36% "flowing gas" tied to connected proved-developed-producing reserves. The defining structural feature is customer concentration: Expand Energy was approximately 45% of consolidated operating revenues in FY2025 and carries its own risk-factor heading in the 10-K. That measure excludes JV revenue, whose customer base skews toward utilities and LDCs, so look-through concentration is somewhat lower — but DTM does not disclose that figure, and revenue mix shift is not the same as measured diversification.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Operating revenue went $920M (FY22) → $922M → $981M → $1,243M (FY25). Operating income $478M → $471M → $489M → $614M. Net income attributable $370M → $384M → $354M → $441M, with FY2025 diluted EPS of $4.30. Company-reported Adjusted EBITDA compounded from $768M in 2021 to $1,138M in 2025, a 10.3% CAGR.

The FY2025 headline overstates underlying momentum. Revenue rose $262M (+26.7%), but the MD&A attributes $212M of the $244M Pipeline increase to the Midwest Pipeline Acquisition that closed December 31, 2024. Ex-acquisition, organic revenue growth was approximately 5.1%.

**Q2 2026.** Net income $112M and diluted EPS of $1.09, with Operating Earnings identical at $112M / $1.09 — zero non-GAAP adjustments. Consensus was $1.17, so the reported quarter registers as a 7.5% miss on the surprise data. The effective tax rate was 32% versus 24% a year earlier because of a non-recurring deferred-tax-liability adjustment; at a 24% rate, tax would have been roughly $40.6M rather than $53M, implying about $0.12/share of drag. Management chose not to adjust that out. Underlying earnings power was therefore roughly in line, and the reporting discipline is a credit to non-GAAP integrity — most issuers would have excluded it. Adjusted EBITDA of $305M brought H1 to $613M versus $557M, +10.1%.

**Segment detail.** Pipeline Adjusted EBITDA went $391M (H1 2025) → $414M (H1 2026), +6%, with Q2 at $200M versus Q1's $214M on lower short-term contract revenue and lower recovery of operational flow order fees on LEAP. Gathering went $166M → $199M, +20%. The revenue drivers were Blue Union volumes +$27M, Appalachia +$16M from MVP, Tioga +$7M, Ohio Utica +$5M, Susquehanna −$4M. Susquehanna's continued decline (−$22M in 2025) is unexplained in the filings; it could be contract step-downs, natural PDP decline, or capital reallocation by the customer, and we would not assert which.

**Balance sheet.** Debt is $3,350M principal: 4.125% 2029s ($970M), 4.375% 2031s ($980M), 4.300% 2032s ($600M), 5.800% 2034s ($650M), and a $150M Guardian term loan at SOFR + 1.95% maturing 2033. Cash $172M, so net debt $3,178M — **2.67x** the 2026 guidance midpoint against a 5.0x covenant. The revolver is $1.0B undrawn (expires December 2029) with $17M of LCs, of which $16M is issued on behalf of Millennium; total liquidity roughly $1.2B. No maturities until 2029, and only $150M floats. Investment grade at all three agencies, with the May 16, 2025 Investment Grade Event releasing collateral and loosening negative covenants.

The reported leverage understates the economics. DTM's Adjusted EBITDA includes its proportional share of JV EBITDA ($144M of $613M in H1 2026, 23.5% of the total) while the associated JV debt sits off balance sheet. Proportional JV interest expense was $27M in H1, implying roughly $54M annualized; at 5.0-6.0% average rates that solves to $900M-$1,080M of proportional debt, cross-checked against DTM's $787M share of the two disclosed JV note issuances ($416M Millennium, $371M NEXUS). Taking ~$950M, look-through net debt is ~$4.1B and look-through leverage roughly **3.4-3.5x**. Still investment-grade-appropriate, and the agencies rate DTM with full knowledge of the structure, but not the fortress the 2.67x headline implies. JV debt balances and rates are not disclosed; this is our estimate.

Relatedly, the 2023-2025 decline in equity-method earnings ($177M → $162M → $138M) is a financing artifact rather than asset decay. The JVs' combined 100% net income rose from $156M (H1 2025) to $174M (H1 2026) on revenue of $410M → $418M. Millennium and NEXUS issued notes and distributed the proceeds up to DTM ($416M and $371M respectively), which lowered reported equity earnings while delivering cash.

**Cash flow and the tax shield.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $502M versus $432M. Book tax expense of roughly $88M compares to **$5M of cash taxes paid**, reconciled by an $80M deferred-tax add-back; the deferred tax liability rose from $1,270M to $1,350M. Drivers are accelerated depreciation on a $7,148M gross PP&E base and the Midwest Pipeline Acquisition's treatment as a taxable deemed asset acquisition with stepped-up basis. This is a real cash benefit and the primary reason DCF far exceeds net income, but it is a timing benefit: convergence toward the 24-26% book rate would be roughly $75-85M/year, about $0.75/share or ~9% of DCF. Sustained heavy growth capex can defer the crossover for years, which is the historical pattern for growing midstream companies, so we treat it as a medium-term sensitivity rather than a dated risk.

**DCF and dividend.** DTM's H1 2026 bridge reconciles exactly: $613M Adjusted EBITDA − $144M JV EBITDA − $77M cash interest − $5M cash taxes − $35M maintenance capital + $96M JV distributions = $448M (versus $407M in H1 2025). Q1 DCF of $274M versus Q2's $174M is almost entirely semi-annual interest timing, so neither quarter annualizes. Our full-year sensitivity — $1,190M − ~$290M JV EBITDA − ~$155M cash interest − ~$13M cash taxes − ~$85M maintenance capital + ~$185M JV distributions — is roughly **$832M, or $8.16/share**, against an annualized dividend of $3.52 × 102.0M = $359M. That is ~2.3x coverage and a 43% payout. Retained DCF of ~$473M approximately funds the $405-485M growth capital program, consistent with no equity issuance since November 2024 and share-count dilution of only ~0.4%/year from stock compensation. The current quarterly dividend of $0.88 is +7.3% year over year; the yield at $132.92 is **2.65%**.

**Returns on capital.** FY2025 NOPAT on operating income was $466M ($614M × 0.759 at the 24.1% effective rate), plus $105M of after-tax equity earnings = $571M. Invested capital was roughly $8,171M (debt $3,350M + equity ~$4,875M − cash $54M), giving ROIC of about **7.0%**, or 7.7% excluding $781M of goodwill. DTM's own goodwill-test discount rates were 6.8% (Pipeline) and 7.5% (Gathering). Book capital is inflated by acquisition accounting ($781M goodwill plus $1,832M intangibles equals 26% of assets), so incremental brownfield returns are almost certainly higher than the blended book figure — that is the bull case, and DTM does not disclose a return-on-new-growth-capital metric that would let us test it.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is mid-single-digit and the 2028 backlog is where the value sits. 2026 guidance of $1,155-1,225M implies only +1.5% to +7.6% over FY2025's $1,138M. The reaffirmed 2027 outlook of $1,225-1,295M puts the 2025→2027 midpoint CAGR at 5.2%, roughly half the 10.3% delivered 2021-2025.

The identified project set is credible but back-end loaded:

- **LEAP Phase 5**, +200 MMcf/d, FID in Q2 2026, explicitly supported by new long-term contracts. Management targets 2.3 Bcf/d LEAP capacity by 2H 2028 (Phase 4 took it to 2.1 Bcf/d in September 2025).
- **Guardian G3**, +537 MMcf/d (roughly +40% of Guardian's capacity), FERC Section 7(c) application filed late June 2026, underpinned by 20-year negotiated-rate precedent agreements with investment-grade utilities, fully in service Q4 2028.
- **DTM Interstate Transportation modernization phase 1** (in service 2H 2027) and **Viking modernization phase 1** (FID Q2 2026).
- A Midwestern lateral to a power plant expected in 1H 2026.

Total commercialized backlog is $2B of capital projects per the Q2 release. Note the definitional discipline: this is capex to be spent, not revenue to be received. A third-party "$3.4B backlog" figure circulating in published commentary is inconsistent with company disclosure and we do not use it. Separately, remaining performance obligations of $1,396M in the 10-Q are not backlog — the scope excludes short-term contracts, variable/volume consideration and index-linked pricing, which removes most gathering revenue.

The demand backdrop maps to two live secular themes: Gulf Coast LNG export takeaway (LEAP, Blue Union) and Midwest power/industrial load (Guardian G3's utility contracts, the Midwestern power-plant lateral). We found no named data-center contracts in DTM's filings or Q2 release, so DTM's AI-load exposure is indirect — utility and power-generation load, one step removed from the hyperscaler capex cycle. Its largest customer looks steady rather than accelerating: Expand Energy reaffirmed full-year production and guided to lower second-half deal spending.

Consensus is drifting the wrong way, modestly. Current-year EPS estimates fell 1.4% over 30 days to $4.72 and next-year fell 1.1% to $4.94; the current quarter is down 1.1% over 90 days. Only the next quarter is up (+1.0%). This is not the >30% positive revision velocity that signals an inflection; it is a mid-single-digit grower with slightly softening near-term estimates, which caps how much benefit of the doubt the multiple deserves.

Against the supplied peer set (median P/E 19.3x, median yield 6.1%), DTM screens expensive on both. That comparison is only partly informative — the set mixes a distressed-earnings mineral vehicle (VNOM, negative P/E), two tanker owners (INSW, LPG) at 7-10x on cyclically peak rates, and two gathering-and-processing names (AM 24.9x, KNTK 19.3x). The relevant read is that DTM trades at a wide premium to G&P peers on earnings while yielding less than half the group, which is consistent with its higher firm-contract mix and lower leverage, but leaves nothing for execution error.

The cycle-adjustment question matters here and the answer is unfavorable to the bulls. There is no trough to normalize from: FY2025 was a record, H1 2026 EBITDA is +10.1%, and the company's own forward guidance decelerates to ~5%. Trailing earnings are not depressed; they are near-peak-of-trend. So the 14.2x multiple has to be judged against 5% growth and 7% book ROIC, not against a recovery. We also note that a 2.65%-yielding, long-duration, capital-intensive asset is directly exposed to the long end of the curve. Higher-for-longer compresses the multiple at which the market will capitalize a mid-single-digit-growth infrastructure cash flow, independent of anything DTM does.

Analyst consensus is $155 mean / $154.5 median, low $130, high $176 — implying +16.6% to the mean. We treat that as informative but not confirmatory: the rating distribution is 2 strong buy, 5 buy, 7 hold, 1 sell, the lowest target sits at spot, and the stock is not in the category (cheap versus rising estimates) where targets function as floors. Here estimates are falling slightly while the multiple is premium, which is the configuration where targets lag on the way down rather than up.

Our own framing: applying 14.0x to 2027E $1,260M gives EV of $17,640M; less $3,178M net debt and $142M NCI leaves $14,320M of equity, or **$140.4/share** — call it $141. That is +6.1% in price and +8.8% total return with the dividend. Bull case of 15.5x on the top of the 2027 range ($1,295M) gives ~$165. Bear case of 12.0x on the bottom of 2026 guidance ($1,155M) gives ~$103; we round to $105, near the $100.48 52-week low. Probability-weighting roughly 55/20/25 base/bull/bear produces a mid-single-digit expected total return, which is fair-to-slightly-thin. That is a HOLD, not a REDUCE — the balance sheet, coverage and reaffirmed guidance limit the path to the bear case — and not an ACCUMULATE, because a 14x entry on 5% guided growth offers no margin of safety.

Valuation score of 5.0. The 13% derating from May is a genuine improvement in the entry (the multiple was ~15.2x at $145 in mid-July), and we credit it. But we cannot identify a specific mispricing: no compressed multiple against rising estimates, no yield support relative to peers, no cycle-trough earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is roughly stable with one new headwind and one new structural threat.

**The pipeline franchise is strengthening.** Guardian G3's 20-year negotiated-rate agreements with investment-grade utilities are exactly the contract structure a midstream investor should want, and LEAP expansions are contracted before FID. The MVP interconnect converted a declining Appalachian gathering position into a growth one.

**Regulated rate compression at the acquired pipelines is a real, unquantified headwind.** Guardian's most recent approved rate proceeding cut the maximum tariff rate by approximately 13% effective April 1, 2025, with an additional 5% effective April 1, 2026. Guardian was part of the $1.2B package bought at the end of 2024. Max-rate reductions bite only on contracts priced at maximum tariff, and negotiated-rate contracts including G3's are insulated, so the magnitude may be modest — but DTM does not quantify it and we cannot. The apparent acquisition logic is to buy under-earning regulated assets and grow rate base through modernization and expansion, which the FID pattern supports; the tariff cuts are the near-term cost of that strategy.

**Williams is buying scale in DTM's core basin.** The agreement to acquire Momentum Midstream for up to $5.5B (~$3.5B cash and debt plus ~$2B equity) adds more than 4,000 miles of Haynesville gathering and transmission, more than 1 million dedicated acres, ~6 Bcf/d of system capacity, processing and treating plants, and three take-or-pay pipelines capable of 4.05 Bcf/d serving 140+ customers including 10 LNG plants. Williams also disclosed the Shelby Trough Connector (750 MMcf/d initial, up to 1.5 Bcf/d, 2Q 2028) and Delta Access ($1.5B, 2.25 Bcf/d, 1Q 2029). Set against DTM's LEAP at 2.1 Bcf/d targeting 2.3 Bcf/d by 2H 2028, that is potentially 3.75+ Bcf/d of competing Haynesville-linked takeaway on the same timeline, backed by Transco's downstream franchise and a materially larger balance sheet.

The near-term risk is limited because DTM's expansions are contracted first. The risk is to option value beyond what is committed: incremental Haynesville takeaway rates in 2029+ and the competition for uncontracted producer volumes. There is a legitimate counterargument — Haynesville growth to serve Gulf Coast LNG could absorb both, and Williams' assets concentrate in East Texas / western Haynesville around different hubs (Bethel, Carthage, Silsbee, Gillis) than DTM's LEAP corridor, making them partly complementary. This is unresolved and it materially affects the terminal growth assumption embedded in a 14x multiple. It is the single largest reason we are not upgrading on the price decline.

**Structural limitations remain:** three basins/regions (Haynesville, Appalachia, Upper Midwest), no Permian, no NGLs, no processing scale, and a 10-K acknowledgment that Northeast permitting "has become increasingly challenging," which constrains the Appalachian option set relative to the Gulf Coast.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration.** Expand Energy at ~45% of consolidated operating revenues, with its own risk-factor heading. Expand is investment grade and is DTM's growth engine in both the Haynesville and Northeast Appalachia, and it has reaffirmed production guidance. But a change in its capital allocation, a downgrade, or a contract renegotiation would be material and slow to replace.

**Gathering volume exposure.** Only ~57% firm and ~36% flowing-gas. This is the segment with the thinner goodwill cushion (44% at the October 1, 2025 test, versus 73% for Pipeline, using 2.5% terminal growth).

**Non-control and off-balance-sheet leverage.** 23.5% of H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA came from Millennium, NEXUS and Vector, none of which DTM operates. DTM extends credit support to the JVs rather than the reverse: $16M of LCs on Millennium's behalf and a CAD $70M revolving facility to Vector (max USD $49M) required by Canadian catastrophic-event regulations, with funding deemed remote.

**Cash-tax convergence.** $5M of cash taxes against roughly $88M of book expense in H1. Convergence toward the book rate is ~$75-85M/year, or roughly 9% of DCF. Timing is not disclosed.

**Other disclosed items.** Portions of DTM Interstate Transportation, the Northern Michigan assets and the storage assets are more than 50 years old, which is why modernization capital keeps appearing in FIDs. Gathering systems are currently non-jurisdictional; FERC reclassification would impose rate regulation. Vector and NEXUS carry cross-border tariff exposure. Up to $34M of Clean Fuels contingent consideration remains with no milestones achieved since the $10M paid in 2024 — immaterial in dollars, but a small data point on acquisition underwriting. Working capital is thin (current ratio 1.07), which is why the undrawn $1.0B revolver matters. The Antero v. Stonewall matter resolved favorably: after a January 2026 trial, the court ordered on April 2, 2026 that no damages be awarded, though appeals remain possible.

We note that an Altman Z-Score of 1.53 on this business is a false distress signal — it is depressed almost entirely by the 0.12 asset-turnover term, which is structural for long-lived pipelines, against zero revolver borrowings, $1.2B liquidity, no maturities to 2029 and IG ratings from all three agencies. The correct conclusion is "wrong tool," not "no leverage risk"; the thin working-capital term does reflect something real.

Risk score of 5.0. None of these are hidden, but the combination of 45% single-customer concentration, an unquantified regulated rate cut at recently acquired assets, a quarter of EBITDA from non-operated JVs with undisclosed debt, and a ~$0.75/share latent DCF headwind is substantial for a stock priced at 14x EBITDA with no cushion.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (91 contracts across four expirations) so confidence is low, but the readings are internally inconsistent in a way worth flagging. Near-term ATM IV of 26.7% is rich to 20.7% one-year realized, and the term structure is in backwardation (26.7% at one month versus 22.1% at six months), which usually signals a near-dated event. ATM IV also rose from 25.8% to 32.5% between July 30 and August 7 while the price fell 1.3%. Yet the skew is call-heavy (-4.4% at the three-month tenor) and put/call volume is 0.14, with put/call open interest at 0.00. So the market is paying up for near-term movement without expressing directional downside — the opposite of the configuration that would corroborate a bear case. Q2 has already reported, so we cannot identify the catalyst the backwardation is pricing; a plausible mechanical explanation is illiquidity in a small chain rather than informed positioning. Short interest of 5.3% of float with 5.7 days to cover is unremarkable and uncrowded. Net read: no options-market confirmation of either the bull or bear case, and the elevated near-term vol argues against paying up for immediate entry.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$165, +24%)

The $2B commercialized backlog converts at brownfield incremental returns well above the 7% blended book figure. LEAP reaches 2.3 Bcf/d by 2H 2028 on contracted volumes; Guardian G3 adds 537 MMcf/d under 20-year investment-grade utility contracts from Q4 2028; the modernization phases grow regulated rate base. Gathering's +20% H1 inflection extends as MVP-linked Appalachian volumes ramp and Blue Union grows with Expand's LNG-oriented development. EBITDA re-accelerates from the 5.2% 2025-2027 guided pace back toward the historical 10% range in 2028-2029, and the market applies 15.5x to a higher base. Meanwhile the cash tax shield persists because growth capex stays heavy, DCF coverage stays above 2x, retained cash keeps funding growth without equity, and the dividend compounds at 7%. Haynesville production growth to serve Gulf Coast LNG proves large enough to absorb both DTM's and Williams' expansions, and the two footprints prove complementary rather than substitutable. The equity re-rates back through the May high.

### Bear Case (~$105, -21%)

Growth stays at the guided 5% and the market stops paying 14x for it. Guardian's 13%-plus-5% max-rate reductions prove more consequential than the silence in disclosure implies. Williams' 3.75+ Bcf/d of competing Haynesville takeaway on a 2028-2029 timeline compresses incremental takeaway economics, so the option value beyond the contracted backlog is written down and the terminal growth assumption falls. Expand Energy reallocates capital — Susquehanna's continued decline (−$22M in 2025, −$4M in H1 2026) is the leading indicator — and Gathering's volume-exposed 43% reverts, pressuring the 44% goodwill cushion. Cash taxes begin converging, taking ~$0.75/share out of DCF. The long end stays elevated, and a 2.65%-yielding infrastructure asset derates toward the 12x EBITDA the sector paid before the LNG/power narrative took hold. Estimates continue drifting down from $4.72/$4.94. At 12x on the low end of 2026 guidance the equity is $103.

### What resolves it

The decisive evidence is whether 2027 guidance firms toward or above the $1,295M high end when the company formalizes it, and whether Guardian G3 receives its FERC certificate on schedule. Secondarily, whether Gathering's H1 inflection holds through H2 without Susquehanna's decline accelerating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners should keep the position; the balance sheet, 2.3x DCF coverage and reaffirmed guidance make the bear case a multiple event rather than a solvency or dividend event. New capital has no urgency at $132.92.

**Base case: $141 (+6.1% price, +8.8% total return with the $3.52 dividend), 12 months.** Math: 14.0x on 2027E Adjusted EBITDA of $1,260M (guidance midpoint) = $17,640M EV; less $3,178M net debt and $142M NCI = $14,320M equity ÷ 102.0M shares = $140.4. Cross-check: $8.60/share of 2027 DCF at a 6.0% DCF yield = $143.

**Bull case: $165 (+24%).** 15.5x on the top of the 2027 range ($1,295M), requiring visible re-acceleration beyond 2027 as the backlog converts.

**Bear case: $105 (-21%).** 12.0x on the bottom of 2026 guidance ($1,155M), near the $100.48 52-week low.

**Timeframe and catalysts:** Q3 2026 report (late October) for confirmation that Gathering's H1 inflection held and that maintenance capital is tracking within the $490-570M full-year plan; formal 2027 guidance (Q4 report, February 2027); the Guardian G3 FERC certificate decision following the late-June 2026 Section 7(c) filing; LEAP Phase 5 construction progress toward 2H 2028.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
1. Price at or below **$118-122**, which puts EV/2027E EBITDA near 12.5x and the DCF yield above 6.7% on unchanged guidance.
2. Formal 2027 guidance at or above $1,295M (the current high end), or a 2028 outlook implying EBITDA growth above 8%.
3. Guardian G3 certificated on schedule *and* one additional Gulf Coast expansion FID with contracted volumes, confirming that Williams' entry is not compressing DTM's commercialization pace.

**Upgrade to BUY** would additionally require disclosure of returns on newly deployed growth capital in the low teens or better, or a reversal in EPS estimate revisions to positive with Gathering Adjusted EBITDA growth sustained above 15% year over year.

## Conclusion

DTM has done what it said it would do. Guidance for 2026 and 2027 was reaffirmed, H1 Adjusted EBITDA grew 10.1%, the Gathering segment inflected after three years of decline, $300M of new FIDs were added with LEAP Phase 5 contracted before commitment, Guardian G3 was filed with FERC on 20-year investment-grade utility agreements, the balance sheet carries no maturity before 2029 with $1.2B of liquidity, and the growth program is funded from retained cash flow with essentially no dilution. Management's decision not to adjust away a self-described non-recurring tax item that cost roughly $0.12/share of reported EPS is a small but real signal about reporting quality.

The stock nonetheless requires a re-acceleration it has not yet earned. Guidance implies 5.2% EBITDA growth through 2027 against a 10.3% four-year history, book ROIC of roughly 7% sits on the company's own 6.8-7.5% WACC assumptions, and 14.2x EV/2026E EBITDA (about 15.0x including estimated proportional JV debt) with a 2.65% yield leaves no cushion for the unquantified Guardian rate reductions, the latent cash-tax convergence, 45% customer concentration, or Williams' arrival with 3.75+ Bcf/d of competing Haynesville takeaway on the same 2028-2029 timeline. Consensus estimates are drifting down about 1% over 30 days, which is the configuration in which premium multiples are vulnerable rather than the one in which analyst targets act as floors.

The 13% pullback from May improves the entry meaningfully — the multiple has come in roughly a full turn since mid-July on unchanged guidance — but base-case upside of about 6% in price against 21% downside is not asymmetry. We stay at **HOLD** and would become buyers in the $118-122 range or on evidence that the $2B backlog is converting at incremental returns well above the installed-base figure.
