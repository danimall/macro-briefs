## Executive Summary

AMETEK is executing well and the shares are priced for it. The August 4 second-quarter release was a clean beat on every line that matters for a short-cycle instrumentation business: revenue $2,044.4M (+15%), adjusted operating margin 26.6% (+60bps), adjusted diluted EPS $2.09 (+17%), operating cash flow $483.7M (+35%), and free cash flow $451.7M converting at 111% of net income. More important than the print, total orders grew 28% after 23.3% growth in Q1 (of which 22 points were organic). For a company that recognized 82.5% of Q1 revenue at a point in time and has only about 39% of estimated FY2026 revenue covered by twelve-month backlog, two consecutive quarters of 20%-plus order growth is the highest-quality forward signal available. Management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $8.20-8.30 from $7.94-8.14.

The problem is the price against that improvement. At $248.49 the stock is 30.1x the $8.25 guidance midpoint, 34.3x the $7.24 GAAP midpoint, roughly 22.9x estimated FY2026 EBITDA, and 28.2x a next-year consensus of $8.81 that has moved only 1.0% in ninety days. Against guided adjusted EPS growth of 10-12%, that is a PEG near 2.7. The dividend yields 0.55%. Working forward to a defensible FY2027 adjusted EPS of roughly $9.05 (consensus $8.81 plus the modest Indicor contribution and early margin expansion) at 29x produces a base-case target of $265, about 6.6% above spot and effectively in line with the $263.39 analyst mean. Bear-case multiple compression to 23x on $8.60 lands near $200, a 19.5% decline. That asymmetry does not support a third consecutive BUY.

The swing factor is the $5.0B all-cash Indicor Instrumentation acquisition, announced May 6 and expected to close in the second half of 2026. It adds approximately $1.1B of sales (14.9% of FY2025 revenue) at 4.5x revenue and, on a reasonable EBITDA estimate, roughly 14.7x EBITDA — funded with a $1.0B revolver carve-out plus a new $4.0B term loan, with no equity issued. Year-one incremental interest of roughly $250M against acquired operating income of roughly $290-310M leaves annualized accretion of about $0.14-0.23, or 1.7-2.8%, and an unlevered after-tax return on the $5.0B near 4.9% — at or below cost of capital. Every dollar of value depends on AMETEK expanding acquired margins 200-500bps over two to three years. The Paragon precedent (a 130bps EMG margin drag in 2024 becoming +200bps in 2025 and +290bps in Q2 2026) says the playbook works; Indicor is roughly three times Paragon's revenue scale, and no synergy target has been published. We rate AME HOLD with a positive twelve-month growth thesis and an upgrade trigger tied to price and to disclosure at the Indicor close.

## Company Overview

AMETEK manufactures electronic instruments and electromechanical devices from Berwyn, Pennsylvania, with approximately 22,500 employees at year-end 2025 and FY2025 revenue of $7,401.1M. Two reportable segments:

**Electronic Instruments Group (EIG)** — $4,919.1M, 66.5% of sales. Seventy percent is process and analytical instrumentation (process analyzers, emission monitors, spectrometers, metrology, materials and force testing) serving power generation, pharma, medical, water, semiconductor and oil and gas. Thirty percent is aerospace and power instrumentation (power quality monitoring, UPS, programmable power, EMC test, turbine sensors, cockpit displays, embedded computing, commercial aerospace spares and MRO).

**Electromechanical Group (EMG)** — $2,482.0M, 33.5%. Seventy percent is automation and engineered solutions (precision motion control, brushless motors, blowers, heat exchangers, single-use surgical instruments, implantable components, harsh-environment connectors, specialty metal powders). Thirty percent is aerospace thermal management plus a third-party MRO network.

The structurally differentiating feature is customer dispersion. EIG's five largest customers were approximately 4% of EIG sales with no customer above 2% of net sales; EMG's top five were approximately 15% of EMG sales, which translates to roughly 5% of consolidated revenue. For an industrial with material aerospace and semiconductor exposure, that is unusually low single-customer risk.

The business model is a serial acquirer built on an asset-light base: capex was 1.8% of sales in both 2024 and 2025, net PP&E was $855.2M against $16.07B of total assets, and RD&E was $382.8M (5.2% of sales) with approximately 27% of 2025 sales from products introduced in the prior three years. From the start of 2021 through December 31, 2025, AMETEK completed 15 acquisitions adding approximately $1.8B of annualized sales. The LKC purchase accounting is the archetype: of a $217.8M price, $117.3M went to goodwill and $115.5M to identifiable intangibles against $0.5M of PP&E. AMETEK buys intellectual property, brands and customer relationships, which is why the cash flow is strong and why goodwill plus intangibles is 70% of the balance sheet.

The three-year revenue CAGR of 6.4% sits at the low end of the "high single digit" growth objective. FY2025's +6.6% decomposed to +4% acquisitions, +2% organic and +1% FX, and beneath that, the larger, higher-margin segment shrank organically. EIG grew 5.6% on +6% acquisitions and +1% FX against **−1% organic**, with operating margin falling 130bps to 29.4% (management reconciles this as 100bps of acquisition dilution and 50bps of integration cost against +20bps of core expansion). All consolidated organic growth came from EMG (+8% organic, margin up from 20.0% to 23.3%).

That mix has reversed in 2026. Q1 revenue was $1,928.4M (+11.3%: +5% organic, +4% acquisition, +2% FX) with record operating income of $514.9M (26.7%) and EIG back to +2% organic. Q2 EIG sales were +14% and core margin up 40bps to 30.1%, and EMG sales +17% with core margin up 290bps to 26.2%.

**Orders are the decisive data.** FY2025 orders of $7,579.4M grew 11.3% but only 4% organically, with book-to-bill of 1.02 and year-end backlog of $3,581.5M. Q1 2026 orders were $2,217.6M, up 23.3% with organic up 22%, for a book-to-bill of 1.15 and backlog of $3,870.7M. Management attributes the strength to semiconductor and commercial aerospace in EIG and to medtech, defense and automation in EMG.

Two caveats on backlog. First, remaining performance obligations expected beyond one year were only $684.7M at March 31, 2026, so approximately $3,186M of the $3,870.7M backlog converts within twelve months. Second, 82.5% of Q1 revenue was recognized at a point in time. Orders convert quickly, which cuts both ways: the surge should show in revenue promptly, and there is little protection if orders roll over.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 free cash flow was $1,671.6M on $130.2M of capex, a 22.6% FCF margin, with operating cash flow at 1.22x net income. The one blemish was FY2025 operating cash flow declining 1.5% while net income rose 7.6%, attributed to working capital investment. Conversion recovered in 2026: H1 operating cash flow of $935.2M was up 20.4% on $776.6M. AMETEK's cash generation is second-half weighted (H1 2025 was 43.1% of the FY2025 total), so a naive doubling of H1 understates the year. Applying the same seasonal split as a sensitivity implies roughly $2.17B of FY2026 operating cash flow; that is an illustration, not a forecast, and Indicor closing costs and working capital for the order surge could move it.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026, total debt of $2,036.2M less cash of $495.4M gives net debt of $1,540.7M, or 0.67x FY2025 EBITDA of $2,296.9M. FY2025 interest coverage was 23.5x. Two structural features: a $2.3B commercial paper program (established January 2025, $740.0M outstanding at year-end 2025) funds working capital, which is why the current ratio screens at only 1.06; and roughly $959M of GBP- and EUR-denominated debt is designated as a net investment hedge against a company with 48.2% international sales, disclosed as 100% effective.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.96 shown on some screens is a specification artifact — it substitutes book equity for retained earnings and for market equity. Using the correct inputs (retained earnings $12,252.5M/assets $16,067.5M; market equity against total liabilities of $5,438.8M), the score is above 8, deep in the safe zone, consistent with 23.5x coverage and 0.67x leverage. Similarly, a "3 of 9" Piotroski reading reflects only three computable signals, all of which passed.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth case rests on three legs, and each is verifiable.

**Cycle recovery in EIG.** EIG organic revenue went from −1% in FY2025 to +2% in Q1 2026 to total growth of +14% in Q2 with core margin above 30%. Order strength is concentrated in semiconductor and commercial aerospace. The three-quarter progression in EIG is more consistent with an instrumentation cycle turning up than with share shifts, which the internal data cannot distinguish. [unverified — the brief provides no independent industry data confirming a sector-wide semiconductor capex upcycle or hyperscaler/data-center spending comparisons.]

**EMG operating leverage.** EMG delivered +11% organic in Q1 with margin up 380bps to 25.7%, then +17% total in Q2 with core margin up 290bps to 26.2%. This is the Paragon integration maturing, and it is the single best piece of evidence that the Operational Excellence playbook converts acquired margin gaps into earnings.

**Acquisition contribution.** LKC ($209.6M, January 2026), First Aviation Services (~$80M of annual sales, April 2026) and, pending close, Indicor (~$1.1B of sales) supply inorganic revenue. Management's own FY2026 guidance implies 10-12% adjusted EPS growth off $7.43 — back at the Growth Model's double-digit objective after 7.1% in 2024 and 8.8% in 2025.

Three qualifications temper the growth score. First, guidance forensics imply Q4 deceleration: H1 adjusted EPS of roughly $4.07 plus Q3 guidance of $2.08-2.10 leaves approximately $2.09 implied for Q4 versus roughly $2.01 in Q4 2025 — about +4% growth (+1% to +7% across the band) versus +17% in Q2. The most coherent explanation is a full quarter of interest on $5.0B landing against a partial quarter of Indicor earnings, or Indicor being excluded from guidance entirely; the company has not stated which, and no Q2 call transcript was available to resolve it. With Q2 orders up 28%, this should not be read as demand weakness.

Second, consensus revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current year $8.15 (+1.1% over 90 days), next year $8.81 (+1.0%). The high-conviction estimate-revision buy signal — 30%-plus ninety-day revisions alongside accelerating revenue — is absent here. There is a mechanical catch-up coming, since consensus of $8.15 sits below the raised $8.20-8.30 guidance range, but that is a 1-2% adjustment, not a re-rating catalyst.

Third, RD&E grew 2.9% in 2025 against 6.6% sales growth, so research intensity slipped even as the growth narrative strengthened. The 27% vitality index is the metric to watch for whether that matters.

**Earnings delivery record** supports confidence in the guide: four consecutive beats (+6.4%, +2.3%, +2.5%, +3.8%). This is a management team that guides conservatively and clears the bar by low-to-mid single digits.

The supplied peer set — ROK 47.5x, EMR 32.9x, WWD 46.5x, NVT 55.9x, NXT 31.2x, median 46.5x — puts AME's 38.8x trailing GAAP multiple at a discount to the median. That is the single most supportive valuation data point, and it is a weak one: the set is heterogeneous, several members carry cyclically depressed trailing earnings, and none is a clean comp for a diversified instrumentation compounder. Against the more relevant cohort of instrumentation and diversified-industrial compounders, AMETEK's 30x forward adjusted multiple is at or modestly above the middle of the range, not below it.

The GAAP-to-adjusted gap deserves explicit treatment. FY2026 guidance reconciles $1.01/share of difference ($8.25 adjusted midpoint less $7.24 GAAP midpoint) as $1.21 of pre-tax acquisition-intangible amortization (roughly $278M) plus $0.12 of acquisition costs. Amortization has risen from $215.1M (2023) to $247.7M (2024) to $277.3M (2025) and will rise again with Indicor. An investor paying 30x adjusted is paying 34x GAAP, and the difference represents cash previously deployed. This is a framing dispute rather than an accounting problem, but it is where much of the multiple debate sits, and it is a structurally growing wedge for a company whose model is buying intangibles.

Building the base case: FY2027 consensus of $8.81 plus roughly $0.20 of Indicor annualized accretion plus perhaps $0.04 from early margin expansion gives approximately $9.05. At 29x — a small discount to the current forward multiple to reflect three-times-higher leverage — that is $262; we round the base case to **$265**, or +6.6% from spot, which coincides with the $263.39 analyst mean and $263.50 median.

The bull case requires order conversion to run hotter than guided and Indicor margins to move early: FY2027 adjusted EPS near $9.50 at 32-33x gives $305-315. We use **$305**, +22.7%, consistent with the $303 analyst high.

The bear case is multiple compression rather than collapse. If the order surge proves partly pull-forward, EIG's cycle stalls again, and Indicor dilutes segment margins the way FARO did (100-140bps), FY2027 adjusted EPS holds near $8.60 and the multiple compresses toward 23x: **$200**, −19.5%. The $217 analyst low sits between base and bear.

Roughly 7% base-case upside plus 0.55% of yield against 20% downside is not an asymmetric setup. There is no identified mispricing here: the market is paying a full compounder multiple for a company that is delivering compounder results and is about to lever up to buy $1.1B of revenue at 4.5x sales. Valuation scores 4.8.

## Competitive Landscape

AMETEK's 10-K is candid that its markets are "highly competitive" and that it competes with vertically integrated manufacturers with greater resources. It disclaims dependence on any single patent and notes royalties are immaterial. The moat is therefore application engineering, installed base, qualification barriers (particularly aerospace, where products are built to customer specifications and stringent reliability requirements), and switching costs — reinforced by extreme customer dispersion and a 27% three-year new-product vitality index.

The relevant comparison set is Roper, Danaher, Fortive, Teledyne, Keysight, Mettler-Toledo, Emerson, Halma and Hexagon. Indicor, LLC is the entity created from Roper's divested industrial and process businesses, so AMETEK is buying assets a peer chose to exit. We could not verify from primary sources which specific businesses constitute "Indicor Instrumentation," which determines whether AMETEK is acquiring genuinely differentiated niche instrumentation with consumables and aftermarket recurring revenue (AMETEK's characterization, which supports the 4.5x sales price) or more commoditized industrial product lines. That is an open verification gap on a $5.0B commitment.

The metrology platform build is a real, disclosed strategy rather than opportunistic dealmaking: Virtek (October 2024), Kern (January 2025) and FARO (July 2025, roughly $830M) consolidated with Creaform into one EIG platform competing with Hexagon, Nikon Metrology, Zeiss, Renishaw and Mitutoyo. FARO was a long-time subscale-margin public company, which is consistent with the 100bps of EIG acquisition margin dilution in 2025 and 140bps in Q1 2026 — the opportunity and, if the gap proves structural rather than operational, the risk.

Trajectory is improving: EIG organic went −1% → +2% → total +14% with core margin back above 30%, and EMG core margins are expanding at 290bps. Management's framing of exposure to "the global infrastructure build-out," including smart grid, renewables and data centers, is directionally credible given the power-quality, programmable-power and thermal-management portfolios — but AMETEK discloses no data-center revenue anywhere, so this should be treated as qualitative rather than a sized business line. Competitive position scores 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill and intangibles at 70% of assets, rising to roughly 75%.** At March 31, 2026, goodwill plus net other intangibles was $11,431.5M against $16,309.5M of assets. If roughly $4.7B of the Indicor price lands in goodwill and intangibles — reasonable at 4.5x sales — the ratio rises to approximately 75% of a roughly $21.6B balance sheet. The FY2025 impairment tests found no impairment, and management elected the quantitative relief-from-royalty test rather than the qualitative screen for indefinite-lived intangibles, a conservative choice that strengthens the conclusion. This is where a demand disappointment would show up as a non-cash but sentiment-relevant charge.

**Integration and leverage on the largest deal in company history.** Indicor's $1.1B of sales equals roughly 61% of the combined annualized sales added by all 15 acquisitions from 2021 through 2025, and the $5.0B price is 5.4x total FY2025 acquisition spending. Financing is essentially 100% debt: the revolver was upsized from $2.3B to $3.5B maturing June 9, 2031 with up to $1.0B available for the acquisition, plus a new senior unsecured term loan of up to $4.0B in three tranches maturing three, four and five years from draw — a structure corroborated by the June 12, 2026 8-K carrying Items 1.01 and 2.03, though the filing text itself was not retrievable. The issuer's Q2 release corroborates the narrative by disclosing $10.0M of one-time Indicor bridge financing fees inside GAAP interest expense. Pro forma net debt of roughly $6.5B (less interim free cash flow) against roughly $2.89B of pro forma EBITDA implies 2.0-2.3x leverage and roughly 7x coverage — a threefold leverage increase that remains comfortably investment grade.

**Rate and refinancing risk.** Term loan pricing is linked to credit rating or leverage, so a downgrade or leverage drift mechanically raises interest cost. Three tranches maturing three, four and five years from draw will be refinanced at whatever the curve offers, and the revolver matures June 9, 2031. [The brief provides no macro rate data — Fed meeting outcomes, hike probabilities, or the level of the 30-year Treasury — so the prior-draft characterization of the rate environment has been removed as unverified.]

**Short-cycle visibility.** With only about 39% of revenue covered by twelve-month backlog and 82.5% of revenue recognized at a point in time, quarterly results depend on orders received within the quarter, and the 10-K states plainly that visibility into distribution-sold markets is limited. A tariff- or capacity-driven pull-forward inside the 22-28% order growth cannot be ruled out from disclosure.

**Tax.** The FY2025 effective rate was 17.7%, well below the 21% statutory rate, implying substantial low-taxed foreign income. Gross uncertain tax benefits were $239.5M at March 31, 2026 (up from $229.3M), of which $190.8M would hit the effective rate if recognized — roughly 13% of FY2025 net income. Pillar Two relief under the proposed "Side-by-Side" framework is contingent on individual jurisdictions legislating it, and AMETEK is already recording top-up tax where local rates fall below 15%.

**Risk-factor staleness.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors were drafted before the Indicor agreement and contain no discussion of the leverage step-up, term-loan schedule or Indicor-specific integration. A revised set should be expected in the next 10-Q or 10-K. The Q1 10-Q did add language on Middle East geopolitical developments while stating limited regional exposure.

Contained items include environmental reserves of $40.4M (PRP at 13 non-owned sites, de minimis at most) and asbestos suits with no judgments rendered to date and seller indemnification on certain claims.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.6% against 22.4% one-year realized is modestly rich, but the more informative reading is the collapse from 35.9% on July 29 to 26.6% on August 4: the earnings event vol is spent, and IV now sits in the 11th percentile within its own chain. The term structure is flat (26.6% near versus 26.5% six-month), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated around any dated catalyst; there is no options-implied pricing of the Indicor close. Put/call open interest of 0.33 is call-tilted and near-term skew is described as balanced at 2.2%. Short interest of 1.6% of float and 2.8 days to cover confirms no bearish positioning. The options market neither corroborates nor challenges the fundamental view; it prices AME as a low-drama compounder, which is also the source of our valuation concern.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $305 (+22.7%)

Two quarters of 20%-plus organic order growth in a book-and-ship business convert to double-digit organic revenue within two to three quarters. EIG rides a genuine semiconductor and commercial aerospace upcycle with core margin pushing past 30.5%; EMG holds its 290bps core expansion as Paragon fully matures. Indicor closes in the second half of 2026, and the Operational Excellence playbook lifts acquired margins 300bps-plus over 24 months, adding $0.50-0.70 of run-rate EPS by 2028 while $1.6-2.0B of annual free cash flow delevers back toward 1x by 2028 and restores the buyback. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $9.50 and the market pays 32-33x for a re-accelerated compounder with restored double-digit EPS growth. Four consecutive beats and a raised guide make this the higher-probability tail than a typical bull case.

### Bear Case — $200 (−19.5%)

The order surge includes pull-forward and long-lead awards that convert in 2027 rather than revenue this year, and book-to-bill falls back below 1.0 by Q4. Indicor dilutes segment margins the way FARO did — 100-140bps — and its 4.5x sales price proves to reflect a mix closer to commoditized industrial product than differentiated instrumentation, with margin expansion arriving in year three rather than year one. Meanwhile interest expense of roughly $250M lands immediately, goodwill and intangibles reach 75% of assets, buybacks stay suspended so EPS growth must be fully operational, and a rating-linked cost of debt rises. FY2027 adjusted EPS stalls near $8.60 and the multiple compresses to 23x.

### Base Case — $265 (+6.6%)

Orders convert to high-single-digit organic growth, decelerating from the 22-28% booking rate as comparisons harden. Indicor closes in the second half of 2026, contributes roughly $0.20 of annualized accretion net of interest, and begins margin work in 2027. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches approximately $9.05, and the market pays 29x — a modest discount to today's forward multiple for three-times-higher leverage. Add 0.55% of dividend for a total return near 7%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is positive — orders, margins and cash conversion are all inflecting up, and guidance was raised — but the entry price does not compensate for the Indicor leverage step-up and the thin year-one economics.

**Addressing the trajectory.** Scores have moved 6.9 → 7.0 → 6.7, with two consecutive BUY recommendations. This report downgrades to HOLD at an overall 6.4. The downgrade is not driven by price appreciation from $237 (June 18) to $248.49; it is driven by three items that have changed since the prior review. First, the June 9 financing structure converted the Indicor announcement into a committed roughly 100%-debt-funded transaction that triples net leverage, priced off rating or leverage. Second, the August 4 print, while strong, put arithmetic on the table that narrows the gap between price and defensible value: 30.1x guided adjusted EPS against 10-12% guided growth leaves roughly 7% base-case upside. Third, management quality evidence has weakened at the margin — buybacks fell from $443M in FY2025 to effectively nil, there is no open-market insider buying, and the proxy discloses five-year relative TSR of 175 versus a peer index of 190 alongside a 44% increase in compensation actually paid (largely a mark-to-market artifact, but the relative underperformance is real). Financial health remains high; the leverage step-up moves it from 8.0 to 7.5 rather than materially lower.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price at $215-225, which puts the stock near 24-25x FY2027 adjusted EPS of roughly $9.05 and restores at least 2:1 upside-to-downside. The $217 analyst low is in this zone.
2. At or shortly after the Indicor close, disclosure of a quantified synergy or margin-expansion target, a stated deleveraging path and confirmed pro forma leverage at or below 2.3x, combined with book-to-bill holding above 1.05.
3. Two further quarters of 20%-plus organic orders converting to double-digit organic revenue growth with EIG core margin above 30.5% and consensus FY2027 EPS revised above $9.20.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Book-to-bill below 1.00 for two consecutive quarters, confirming the 2026 order surge was pull-forward.
2. Indicor integration diluting segment margins by more than 150bps beyond guidance, or net leverage above 3.0x, or a credit-rating downgrade that raises term-loan pricing.
3. Any goodwill or indefinite-lived intangible impairment, or an adverse resolution of a material portion of the $190.8M of uncertain tax benefits that would hit the effective rate.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in late October, which should carry the first revised risk-factor set addressing Indicor and, if the deal has closed, the first quantified pro forma leverage and integration commentary. The Indicor close itself, guided to the second half of 2026, is the more important date.

## Conclusion

AMETEK is a high-quality asset-light compounder in the middle of a genuine order upcycle, with extreme customer dispersion, 23.5x interest coverage before the Indicor draw, 22.6% free cash flow margins and a four-quarter record of beating consensus. Nothing in the August 4 release argues against owning it.

What argues against buying more at $248.49 is arithmetic. Thirty times guided adjusted earnings and 34x guided GAAP earnings, on 10-12% adjusted EPS growth and estimate revisions of roughly 1% over ninety days, leaves a base-case twelve-month total return near 7% against a bear case near −20%. The $5.0B Indicor acquisition — 4.5x sales, roughly 14.7x estimated EBITDA, entirely debt-funded — generates a year-one unlevered after-tax return near 5% and $0.14-0.23 of annualized accretion while tripling net leverage. Its value is real but sits in 2027 and 2028, contingent on 200-500bps of margin expansion that has precedent (Paragon) but no published target and three times the scale.

Hold existing positions through the Indicor close and the Q3 print. New capital should wait for $215-225, or for management to quantify the Indicor margin plan and deleveraging path while order momentum holds. Base case $265, bull $305, bear $200.
