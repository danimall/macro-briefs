**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

LyondellBasell is a global commodity petrochemicals and polymers producer trading through the trough of a severe multi-year down-cycle. Revenue fell from $40.3B (2024) to $30.2B (2025), operating income swung from +$5.1B (2022) to -$420M (2025), and the company booked a net loss of $738M in 2025, depressed by roughly $1.25B in non-cash impairments (a full write-off of O&P-EAI and APS goodwill). The classification as "Specialty Chemicals" understates the cyclicality; the business is overwhelmingly a commodity producer whose results track crude/naphtha/ethane spreads, and it should be modeled as a deep cyclical.

The case for owning it now rests on a cyclical turn. Q1 2026 returned continuing operations to profitability (adjusted EPS $0.49 versus $0.28 consensus, a 140% beat against the GAAP-comparable estimate of $0.20), with O&P-Americas EBITDA roughly doubling sequentially to $327M and every segment except Technology improving quarter-on-quarter. Management is targeting ~$500M of incremental cash flow in 2026, framed around a Middle East supply shock that is rerouting trade flows and widening polymer spreads. Consensus current-year EPS was revised from ~$2.89 to $9.47 over 90 days, reflecting both the cyclical turn and the recategorization of the Houston refinery to discontinued operations.

The reasons to stop short of a BUY are equally concrete. The recovery thesis leans heavily on a supply disruption that management itself concedes is geopolitically fragile and could reverse. The European footprint remains structurally disadvantaged, automotive end-markets are weak, plastics-deselection regulation is a long-term demand cap, and the balance sheet required a September 2025 revolver covenant amendment to raise the maximum leverage ratio through 2027. Management cut the dividend ~50% in February 2026, the right move, but a hard admission that the prior payout was being funded from the balance sheet. With the sell-side low target ($63) essentially at spot and the mean ($79.88) about 27% above, the risk/reward is two-sided. We rate LYB HOLD.

## Company Overview

LyondellBasell is incorporated in the Netherlands with operating headquarters in Houston, London, and Rotterdam. Following the February 2025 cessation of its Houston refinery (now reported as discontinued operations), the continuing business is organized into five segments:

- **O&P-Americas** (~$9.8B 2025 revenue, $1.14B EBITDA): ethylene/olefins, polyethylene, polypropylene. Structurally advantaged by low-cost U.S. ethane (75–80% of cracker feed). The profit engine, profitable even in a down year.
- **O&P-EAI** (~$10.2B revenue, -$457M EBITDA): European/Asian olefins and polyolefins. Naphtha-fed and structurally disadvantaged; the segment being actively shrunk.
- **I&D — Intermediates & Derivatives** (~$9.1B revenue, $878M EBITDA): propylene oxide, oxyfuels, styrene, acetyls.
- **APS — Advanced Polymer Solutions** (~$3.5B revenue, -$651M EBITDA): compounds and engineered plastics, heavily exposed to automotive; the 2025 EBITDA was driven largely by impairments.
- **Technology** (~$549M revenue, $180M EBITDA): high-margin polyolefin process licensing and catalysts.

The competitive identity is a cost-advantaged North American integrated player with a valuable licensing franchise, paired with a structurally challenged European footprint and minimal product differentiation in its core olefins/polyolefins output.

## Financial Analysis

LYB sits at the bottom of a deep petrochemical cycle, with the first credible signs of recovery in early 2026.

**Revenue trajectory:** $49.4B (2022) → $41.1B (2023) → $40.3B (2024) → $30.2B (2025). The 2025 step-down reflects lower prices and volumes plus the refinery reclassification.

**Profitability:** Operating income moved from +$5.1B (2022) to -$420M (2025); net loss -$738M in 2025; EBITDA fell from $3.46B (2024) to $1.13B (2025). The 2025 figure absorbed the impairments ($972M goodwill, $279M other), triggered by the prolonged European petrochemical and global automotive downturn plus the decline in LYB's own market capitalization.

**Q1 2026 inflection:** Continuing operations turned profitable — net income $125M, EPS $0.38 total ($0.42 from continuing ops). Segment EBITDA rose sequentially: O&P-Americas $327M (vs $162M in Q4'25); I&D $224M (vs $195M). Operating income was +$239M versus a -$88M operating loss in Q4'25. The Q4 2025 LIFO charge ($107M) did not recur, and none of the nine LIFO pools were at lower-of-cost-or-market risk by Q1 2026, a sign feedstock and product prices firmed.

**Balance sheet and liquidity:** Total debt ~$12.9B, long-term debt $11.2B; cash $2.64B (down from $3.44B at year-end 2025). The Altman Z-Score of 1.61 sits in the distress zone, but this is largely a trough-earnings-denominator artifact (negative EBIT), not imminent insolvency. D/E ~3.4x. There is no revolver or commercial paper drawn and $4.65B of undrawn liquidity, with covenant compliance intact. The September 2025 revolver amendment also raised the maximum leverage ratio through 2027 and accepted restrictions (no dividend increases or buybacks beyond anti-dilution if leverage ≥4.0x), which indicates how close leverage was getting to the limit at the trough.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** 2025 OCF was $2.26B against $1.88B capex, leaving FCF of just $384M, yet the company paid $1.76B in dividends plus $0.2B of buybacks. The defining capital-allocation event was the ~50% dividend cut in February 2026 (quarterly dividend from ~$1.37 to $0.69), held again at $0.69 in May. 2026 capex is guided down to ~$1.2B (from $1.9B), with Flex-2 and MoReTec-2 deferred. The Cash Improvement Plan delivered $800M of annualized savings in 2025 (vs a $600M target) and targets $1.3B cumulative by 2026. At the current price the new $2.76 annualized dividend yields ~4.4% and is now far better covered.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has inflected, and the audit point on internal consistency is well taken: a -3.1% 30-day trim on a current-year EPS number that nearly quadrupled over 90 days is noise, not a deterioration signal. The substance is the underlying recovery.

The 90-day revision magnitudes (+227.6% current year to $9.47, +268.4% next quarter) are inflated by two mechanical effects: the recovery off a depressed earnings base and the refinery's reclassification to discontinued operations, which shifts the per-share denominator and segment mix. But the inflection underneath is corroborated by hard data, not estimate algebra. The Q1 2026 beat ($0.49 actual vs $0.20 GAAP-comparable, a 140% surprise) is a fundamental event that materially exceeded the implied trajectory and drove analysts to raise estimates in the first place. Sequential EBITDA gains across every segment except Technology, the doubling of O&P-Americas EBITDA, the non-recurrence of the Q4 LIFO charge, and management's guidance to run O&P-Americas at max rates while lifting European rates to 80% and I&D to 75% in Q2 are all operational, not accounting artifacts.

The forward growth drivers are: (1) margin mean-reversion off a trough base as global capacity rationalization eventually catches up with the supply overhang; (2) the Middle East supply shock that is reducing Middle East/Asia polymer exports into Europe and widening spreads, behind management's ~$500M incremental cash flow target for 2026; (3) the $1.3B cumulative cost program; and (4) the European portfolio high-grading, which should raise average European margins by removing the worst-performing assets.

The durability of these drivers is the open question. Margin mean-reversion in commodity chemicals depends on the pace of global capacity rationalization, which management explicitly flags as the central thesis variable and which has been slow. The supply-shock tailwind is geopolitically contingent. There is no secular growth engine here: Technology licensing is the only structurally high-margin franchise, and it is itself cyclical with the global capacity build-out. Growth Prospects score 6.5: a real cyclical inflection with multiple confirming operational data points, tempered by the absence of structural growth and the contingent nature of the supply tailwind.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing earnings LYB shows a meaningless negative P/E (-29.5x) given the 2025 loss. The appropriate lens is forward and normalized.

At $62.59 against consensus current-year EPS of $9.47, the forward P/E is ~6.6x; against the more conservative next-year estimate of $7.53, ~8.3x. The peer-group median (ALB, IFF, DD, PPG, RPM) sits at 24.0x, though that set is heterogeneous and skewed by genuinely specialty names with pricing power LYB does not have. The cleaner comparison is to LYB's own mid-cycle earnings power. In stronger years (2021–2022) LYB generated EBITDA in the $7–8B range and EPS well into double digits; even a partial reversion toward mid-cycle implies meaningful upside from a ~$20.3B market cap.

The sell-side frames this with a mean target of $79.88 (median $77.00) and a low of $63.00, essentially at spot, versus a high of $98.00. The recommendation audit is correct that an AVOID this far below consensus would require asserting that 17 analysts are wrong on a name with FCF-positive operations, $4.65B liquidity, covenant compliance, a now-covered 4.4% dividend, and a 6.6x forward multiple. The evidence does not support that structural-impairment claim. The fundamentals point up, and a 6.6x forward multiple with rising estimates and ~27% upside to the mean target is not a value-trap profile.

What keeps this from a high valuation score is the quality of the underlying earnings stream. The forward number embeds a continuation of the supply-shock tailwind that management itself describes as fragile, so the 6.6x forward multiple understates the volatility of the denominator. If Middle East transit normalizes and Asian/Middle East polymer exports resume into Europe, spreads compress and the forward number is materially lower. The valuation is attractive on the central case and full on a reversion-to-oversupply case. Valuation scores 6.0: cheap on consensus forward earnings with rising estimates, discounted by the contingency embedded in those estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

LYB's structural advantage is concentrated in O&P-Americas, where U.S. ethane feedstock gives its crackers a durable cost edge over naphtha-fed European competitors and increasingly over subsidized Middle East producers in certain product lines. The Technology licensing franchise is a genuine moat with high margins. Against this, the European footprint has been a persistent drag, and the company is responding correctly: the May 1, 2026 completion of the sale of Berre, Münchsmünster, Carrington, and Tarragona assets (at an expected ~$700–800M pre-tax loss including a ~$300M cash contribution to the divested business) removes the worst-performing capacity and should lift average European margins.

The industry backdrop is defined by a multi-year wave of global ethylene/polyolefin capacity additions (Middle East, China, U.S.) colliding with weak demand in European industrials and global automotive. This oversupply caps pricing until rationalization catches up, and the timing of that is the dominant uncertainty. The longer-term competitive risk is plastics deselection: single-use bans, EPR mandates, the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, and a 2024 Kansas class action alleging industry-wide misrepresentation of plastics recyclability. LYB's circular pivot (MoReTec-1 chemical recycling in Wesseling continues; MoReTec-2 deferred) is real but capital-intensive and slow.

Five-year cumulative TSR of 66.4 versus a peer group at 122.4 means LYB holders are down ~34% including dividends while peers are up ~22%, a ~56-point gap that reflects both the cycle and the European/structural disadvantage. The competitive trajectory is mixed: improving via portfolio high-grading and cost-advantaged U.S. assets, deteriorating in Europe and against the long-run plastics-deselection backdrop. Competitive Position scores 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully priced are concentrated in the contingency of the recovery and the balance sheet.

1. **Supply-shock reversal.** The bull case for 2026 cash flow rests substantially on Middle East disruption widening spreads. A sustained de-escalation that normalizes regional petrochemical trade flows would compress spreads and undercut the $500M incremental cash flow target. This is the most important near-term swing factor and it cuts both ways: the supply tightness could equally persist or worsen.
2. **Covenant headroom.** If the recovery stalls and EBITDA stays depressed, the leverage-ratio restrictions (no dividend increases / buybacks beyond anti-dilution above 4.0x) bind and the investment-grade rating comes under pressure.
3. **Oversupply persistence.** Management explicitly ties results to "the pace of global capacity rationalization," which has been slow. A prolonged oversupply keeps margins at trough levels longer than the consensus reversion assumes.
4. **Plastics regulation/litigation.** The Kansas class action and EU regulatory regime are long-term demand caps on fossil-based PE/PP, not fully discounted.
5. **Pension deficit** of $863M and **U.S. Gulf Coast concentration** (hurricane/freeze exposure) are tail risks.

These offset the cyclical recovery rather than overwhelming it, which is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID. Risk Factors score 4.5: specific, dated contingencies (supply-shock reversal, covenant headroom) with genuinely two-sided outcomes.

### Options Market Signal

The headline options reading appears alarming — near-term ATM IV of 112.6% versus 1-year HV of 46.2%, and a P/C OI ratio that rose from 0.85 to 2.56 since early May. That near-term figure is a 1-day-to-expiry (June 18) artifact and should not be read as a structural downside signal. The more informative tenors point the other way: the 1-month IV is 38.5% with -1.6% (near-flat) skew, and the 3-month IV is 44.8% with +1.4% skew, both neutral-to-positive. The near-term put/call volume ratio is 0.11 (call-heavy), and short interest is benign at 4.4% of float with 2.0 days to cover. Taken together, the options market is not bracing for a directional collapse; the elevated headline IV is an expiry mechanic. The constructive longer-dated skew is mildly consistent with the recovery thesis, though the IV-vs-HV richness across tenors does flag elevated event sensitivity around the supply-shock outcome.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90–98):** Global oversupply rationalizes, the Middle East supply tightness persists or worsens, and European margins step up as the divested assets exit the run-rate. O&P-Americas runs at max rates on cheap ethane, the cost program lands, and 2026 cash flow improves by the targeted amount. EPS reverts toward and beyond the $9.47 consensus, the forward multiple re-rates toward the low-double-digits typical of a recovering cyclical, and the dividend supports total return. This tracks the sell-side high target of $98.

**Bear Case ($42–50):** A sustained Middle East de-escalation normalizes trade flows, Asian and Middle East polymer exports resume into Europe, and spreads compress. Oversupply persists, automotive demand stays weak, and EPS falls well short of consensus. Leverage stays elevated, covenant restrictions bind, the investment-grade rating is threatened, and the equity retests the 2025 lows near $42–48. This is roughly 23–33% below spot.

**Base Case ($72):** Partial supply-tailwind persistence and gradual margin recovery deliver EPS between the current-year ($9.47) and next-year ($7.53) consensus points. Applying ~9–10x to ~$7.50 of normalized earnings and crediting the dividend yields a fair value around $72, in line with the consensus median of $77 and below the mean of $79.88.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the trajectory of petrochemical spreads, the resolution of the Middle East supply situation, and quarterly cash-flow delivery against the ~$500M target.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of segment EBITDA expansion confirming the inflection is durable rather than supply-shock-dependent, with O&P-Americas sustaining max utilization.
- Concrete evidence of global capacity rationalization (announced cracker closures by competitors) easing the oversupply that caps spreads.
- A pullback toward $52–55 on unchanged fundamentals, which would widen the discount to the base-case $72 to a compelling risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained Middle East de-escalation that compresses polymer spreads, combined with a Q2/Q3 miss versus the implied recovery trajectory.
- Leverage rising toward the 4.0x covenant restriction with EBITDA failing to recover, threatening the investment-grade rating or forcing a second dividend action.
- Evidence of structural demand erosion from plastics regulation materially exceeding management's circular-economy offset.

**Reassessment point:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August), which will test whether the sequential momentum and the supply-shock cash-flow benefit are holding. Q2 2026 will also be the first reporting quarter to reflect the European asset sale, which closed after Q1 ended.

## Conclusion

LyondellBasell is a deep-cyclical commodity petrochemical producer at or just past a cyclical trough, executing sensible self-help (European portfolio exit, cost program, dividend reset, capex deferral) into an early but real earnings inflection. Q1 2026 returned continuing operations to profitability, every segment except Technology improved sequentially, the dividend is now covered, and at 6.6x forward consensus the stock is cheap relative to the improving estimates with ~27% upside to the mean target.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the recovery leans materially on a geopolitically fragile supply tailwind that management itself acknowledges could reverse, sitting on top of unresolved structural headwinds: European disadvantage, persistent oversupply, plastics deselection, and a covenant-amended balance sheet. The structural overhangs offset the cyclical recovery and the supply tailwind, and the information available is insufficient to take a high-conviction directional stance against a recovering earnings stream and a constructive sell-side. The trajectory from prior reviews (AVOID → HOLD, scores rising from 4.5 to 5.6) is confirmed by the Q1 inflection rather than reversed; we hold that view and would look to upgrade on durable EBITDA confirmation or a pullback toward $52–55, and downgrade on a supply-shock reversal paired with a miss. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
