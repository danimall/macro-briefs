**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Brink's is the global leader in cash and valuables management, operating armored logistics and ATM/cash-ecosystem services across 100+ countries. The underlying franchise is performing well: FY2025 revenue grew 5% to $5,261M, adjusted EBITDA rose 7% to $977M, non-GAAP diluted EPS advanced 12% to $8.05, and the North America segment posted 27% organic operating-profit growth on a transformation-driven productivity program. The company has beaten consensus EPS in three of the last four quarters, including a 14.2% beat in Q1 2026 ($1.80 vs. $1.58). On operating fundamentals alone, this is not a deteriorating business.

The dominant fact in the investment case is the pending acquisition of NCR Atleos, announced February 26, 2026 at roughly $4B equity value (13.3M Brink's shares plus $2.2B cash) and approximately $6.6B enterprise value including assumed debt. The market reaction was sharply negative: the stock fell ~6% on announcement and ~17% over the following session to ~$113, and has continued to derate to $97.67, roughly 28% below the February 52-week high of $136.37. The deal layers a large new slug of debt onto a balance sheet that already carries ~$4.2B of total debt against only ~$278M of book equity (a buyback/AOCI artifact rather than operating weakness; net debt is ~2.65x EBITDA). The acquisition is being financed substantially with a $1.0B delayed-draw term loan and an upsized revolver added via the March 31, 2026 credit amendment.

This produces a genuinely two-sided situation. The operating engine is sound and cheap on forward earnings; the franchise generates ~$436M of annual free cash flow; covenant compliance is intact; and analyst targets sit well above the current price (mean $153, all three covering analysts at Buy). Against that, the determining variable — pro-forma post-close leverage and integration economics — is not yet derivable from the filings reviewed and must await the S-4/joint proxy. Issuing a high-conviction call in either direction requires a number that does not yet exist in public form. We rate BCO HOLD, with the understanding that the call resolves toward BUY if pro-forma leverage proves manageable (sub-3.5x net) and toward AVOID if the deal pushes leverage into a binding range with weak integration synergies.

## Company Overview

Brink's operates three principal service lines: cash and valuables management (CVM), covering armored cash-in-transit, ATM replenishment, money processing/vaulting, and Brink's Global Services for high-value commodities; digital retail solutions (DRS), covering smart safes/CompuSafe and cash-forecasting software; and ATM managed services (AMS), covering ATM outsourcing. CVM is the cash-generative legacy core; DRS and AMS are the higher-margin, recurring-revenue growth engines that are lifting segment mix.

Four reporting segments produced the following FY2025 operating profit: North America $246.7M (+27% organic, the standout), Latin America $243.9M (−10% on currency), Europe $177.2M (+17%), and Rest of World $178.2M (+15%). Sixty-nine percent of revenue is non-US, with meaningful exposure to Mexico, Brazil, and Argentina (the latter accounted for as highly inflationary).

The competitive moat rests on the Brink's brand and security reputation, a hard-to-replicate global armored network, high fixed-cost barriers to entry, insurance/risk-management scale, and regulatory licensing. The principal competitors are Loomis AB and GardaWorld in physical logistics, with growing fintech/ATM-network competition in AMS/DRS. The strategic question framing the entire investment case is whether buying ATM infrastructure (NCR Atleos, including the Allpoint surcharge-free network) is the right hedge against the slow secular decline in cash's share of payments.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded steadily and is inflation-aided: $4,535M (2022) → $4,875M (2023) → $5,012M (2024) → $5,261M (2025, +5%). Organic growth runs ~5%, driven by inflation-based pricing plus DRS/AMS expansion, partially offset by emerging-market currency translation.

Profitability is improving on an underlying basis. FY2025 GAAP operating margin rose to 11.1% from 9.0%; non-GAAP operating margin reached 13.5%. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $8.05, against GAAP EPS of $4.70. The wide GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap reflects recurring adjustments: intangible amortization (~$59M/yr), Argentina hyperinflation accounting, transformation costs, and the 2024-25 DOJ/FinCEN charge. Investors should track both figures; the consistency of these "non-recurring" exclusions is a quality-of-earnings caution, and a fresh wave of integration/amortization charges is coming with the deal.

Cash generation is the franchise's defining strength. FY2025 operating cash flow was $639.5M, free cash flow before dividends $435.5M, on capex of $203M (~1.2x D&A). This funds buybacks and the dividend comfortably.

The balance sheet is the source of the alarming-looking Altman Z-Score of 1.02. This is a false positive for distress: total debt of $4,214M against book equity of only $277.7M produces a reported D/E near 15x and debt/capitalization of 94%, but the thin equity is engineered — roughly $583M of three-year buybacks plus a −$665.6M accumulated other comprehensive loss (FX translation and pension actuarial losses). The economically meaningful metric is net debt of ~$2.6B, or ~2.65x adjusted EBITDA: leveraged but manageable for a stable-cash-flow business in full covenant compliance at Q1 2026. The reported 51.6% ROE is likewise an artifact of the thin equity denominator. Liquidity is solid at $1.7B cash plus $540M+ of revolver availability.

The Q1 2026 results illustrate the divergence. Revenue rose 10% (+4% organic) to $1,375.1M, but GAAP operating profit fell 7% to $110.2M (8.0% margin vs. 9.6%) on a $38.9M NCR Atleos/transformation charge and higher corporate costs. Strip the deal noise and the underlying business was healthy: non-GAAP operating profit $168.4M (+12%), adjusted EBITDA $237.5M (+10%), non-GAAP EPS $1.80 (+12%). GAAP EPS of $0.77 (down from $1.19) is depressed by deal costs. Net debt rose to $2.74B.

The earnings record argues against a deteriorating-franchise thesis: three beats in four quarters, including +14.2% in Q1 2026 and +22.5% in Q2 2025. A business beating estimates by double digits is not exhibiting fundamental erosion.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth algorithm is mid-single-digit revenue (inflation-linked pricing plus volume) with margin expansion from the transformation program and an improving DRS/AMS mix. North America operating profit growth of 27% organic in FY2025 and 15% in Q1 2026 demonstrates that the productivity initiatives are converting. DRS and AMS are growing across all four segments, lifting recurring revenue and structural margin.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than directional: current-year EPS consensus has risen +0.8% over 90 days to $9.24, next-year sits at $10.59. The next-quarter line has slipped −4.7% over 30 days, the only soft spot, likely reflecting near-term deal-cost drag. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection; it is a steady mid-cycle compounder where consensus is broadly holding.

NCR Atleos is the transformative growth variable. The strategic logic of converting Brink's from a pure logistics provider into a broader self-service-banking and cash-ecosystem operator is coherent as a hedge against cash decline. But NCR Atleos is ATM-hardware-adjacent, a category facing its own secular pressures, and the market's persistent derating since announcement signals skepticism that the combination adds value commensurate with the leverage and integration risk it introduces. Until pro-forma synergy and leverage figures appear in the S-4/joint proxy, the growth contribution of the deal cannot be credibly modeled.

## Valuation Assessment

At $97.67, BCO trades at roughly 10.6x the $9.24 current-year non-GAAP EPS consensus and ~9.2x the $10.59 next-year estimate. On trailing GAAP EPS of $4.70 the multiple is ~20.8x, but GAAP is heavily depressed by deal and transformation charges, so forward non-GAAP is the appropriate lens. On that basis the stock is inexpensive in absolute terms and against its own history.

Peer comparison is complicated by the heterogeneity of the Industrials cohort supplied (OPLN and RXO at negative P/Es, ARCB at 70x, UNF at 37x, MWA at 19x; peer median P/E 37.3x). Brink's at ~10-11x forward sits well below that median, but the relevant comparables are physical cash-logistics peers (Loomis, GardaWorld), not the diversified industrial set. Against the logistics peers and adjusted for Brink's leverage and emerging-market FX volatility, a low-double-digit forward multiple is reasonable rather than obviously cheap.

The discount to analyst targets is large: mean $153, low $143, high $163, roughly 46-67% above the current price, with all three covering analysts rating Buy. We treat these targets with caution. They were likely set before the full weight of the post-deal derating and have not visibly been revised down, but the absence of a published cut does not confirm that the targets reflect post-deal pro-forma economics. The gap is wide enough that even a partial closing on a manageable-leverage outcome would generate material upside; equally, the targets may prove stale if the deal closes with binding leverage.

The valuation supports a HOLD-with-upside-optionality framing rather than a high-conviction BUY: the multiple is genuinely undemanding for a steady cash compounder, but the discount exists for a specific, unresolved reason (the deal overhang), and cheapness without a catalyst that resolves the overhang is not by itself a buy signal. The resolving catalyst, the S-4 and shareholder vote, is dated but its content (pro-forma leverage) is not yet known.

## Competitive Landscape

Brink's holds a durable position in physical cash logistics against Loomis and GardaWorld, with the moat resting on network density, brand/security trust, and regulatory licensing. The competitive trajectory is mixed: in the legacy CVM franchise, the industry faces a slow secular decline in cash's share of payments, a permanent and well-understood headwind. The offsetting trajectory is positive: DRS/AMS share gains, the transformation-driven margin expansion (especially North America), and the move up the value chain into tech-enabled recurring services.

The NCR Atleos acquisition is a bet on consolidating ATM infrastructure and managed services. If integration succeeds, it deepens the AMS/DRS moat and gives Brink's a broader cash-ecosystem footprint. If it fails or proves dilutive, it absorbs management attention and capital into a declining-hardware-adjacent asset at the expense of the higher-quality organic franchise. The five-year cumulative TSR of 171.6 trailing the peer group's 226.8 is relevant context: the market has not rewarded Brink's capital-allocation history, which partly explains the skepticism toward a large, debt-financed acquisition.

## Risk Assessment

**NCR Atleos acquisition (dominant risk and opportunity).** A deal financed largely with new debt on a thin-equity balance sheet. Integration, regulatory approval (close expected Q1 2027), and leverage are all elevated. The determining number — pro-forma post-close leverage — is not yet detailed in the filings reviewed and should be sourced from the S-4/joint proxy. This risk cannot be fully quantified today.

**Secular cash decline.** The CVM core is tied to cash payments; long-term card/digital adoption is a structural headwind, with the DRS/AMS pivot (and now NCR Atleos) as the strategic hedge.

**Emerging-market/FX.** 69% of revenue is ex-US. Latin America operating profit fell 10% in FY2025 purely on currency despite +5% organic revenue. Argentina (3-4% of revenue, peso −29% in 2025) is accounted for as highly inflationary; Venezuela operations ceased on US sanctions.

**Regulatory/legal tail.** The DOJ/FinCEN AML settlement (resolved January 2025, $42M over three years, ~$20M potentially non-forgivable on compliance failure) carries an ongoing elevated compliance burden. The Chile FNE antitrust matter seeks a $30.5M fine ($9.5M accrued, unresolved).

**Legacy obligations and tax.** UMWA retiree medical and Black Lung liabilities persist; US pension is ~104% funded but $275M of unrecognized actuarial losses sit in AOCI. The effective tax rate is high and volatile (40.5% in 2025), with US deferred-tax-asset realization risk after the July 2025 tax-law changes triggered a ~$12M valuation allowance.

The risk score sits below neutral because the single most important variable is both material and currently unquantifiable. The dominant exposure is a specific, dated corporate action whose financial terms remain partly opaque, not a sector truism that is already priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 26.4% against one-year historical volatility of 33.6% — options are cheap (spread −7.3%), with the near-term IV percentile at 4% and a balanced put/call skew of −1.5%. The term structure is in contango (26.4% near vs. 33.7% at six months), a normal-to-complacent shape. The put/call volume ratio of 0.18 and OI ratio of 0.45 indicate no aggressive directional positioning near-term, though the six-month skew steepens to −3.8%, consistent with some hedging into the deal-close window. The reported "472% ATM IV" in the delta-change feed is a corrupted thin-options artifact and should be disregarded. The options market is not pricing imminent stress, and cheap near-term vol makes optionality (rather than outright stock exposure) an inexpensive way to express a view ahead of the S-4 and shareholder vote.

Short interest of 13.6% of float (7.7 days to cover) signals meaningful institutional skepticism, consistent with the deal-overhang thesis. This level is elevated but not at squeeze-extreme; combined with analyst targets ~50% above price, it creates two-sided pressure rather than a clean directional setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The market is over-penalizing a healthy operator for a deal whose terms will prove manageable. The organic franchise compounds at mid-single-digit revenue with margin expansion, generates ~$436M FCF, and trades at ~10x forward earnings. If the NCR Atleos deal closes with pro-forma net leverage in the 3-3.5x range and credible synergies, the leverage overhang lifts, sentiment normalizes, and the stock re-rates toward the analyst target band of $143-163. Even a partial closing of the discount on a clean shareholder vote and S-4 disclosure would deliver 20-40% upside. The bull target is ~$140-150.

**Bear case.** The deal closes with leverage pushing toward or beyond 4x net, integration synergies disappoint, and the combined entity carries the secular ATM-hardware decline alongside the cash-logistics decline. The thin-equity balance sheet leaves no cushion; covenant headroom narrows; the GAAP-to-non-GAAP adjustment gap widens further with integration charges; and the stock derates toward the low-$80s (near the 52-week low of $84.66), roughly 13-15% downside. A failed/blocked deal or a financing strain would be the trigger.

The asymmetry, by these scenarios, favors the upside in percentage terms (bear ~13-15% down vs. bull ~45% up). That asymmetry is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID: a structurally impaired franchise heading lower does not generally offer 45% upside to credible analyst targets while beating estimates by double digits. It is not a BUY either, because the upside is contingent on a number — pro-forma leverage — that is not yet public, and the downside is real if that number disappoints. The positive operating case and the negative deal-overhang case approximately cancel at the current price, with insufficient information to break the tie with conviction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an `uncertain` HOLD: the operating franchise is healthy and the valuation is undemanding, but the determining variable (deal economics) is genuinely unquantifiable from current filings, so neither a BUY nor an AVOID is supportable on the evidence.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural reassessment points are the S-4/joint proxy filing (the first public source of pro-forma leverage and synergy detail), the ~June 30, 2026 shareholder vote, the next quarterly earnings release, and the expected Q1 2027 deal close. The S-4 is the single most important document for this name and should drive any rating change.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY).**
1. S-4 discloses pro-forma post-close net leverage at or below ~3.5x EBITDA with credible, quantified synergies, bounding the leverage overhang rather than leaving it open-ended.
2. Continued organic execution: North America organic operating-profit growth sustained above 15% and consolidated adjusted EBITDA growth holding ≥7%, confirming the core franchise carries the combined entity.
3. Open-market insider buying or a shareholder vote that approves the deal on terms the market reads constructively (stock holding or rallying through the vote).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).**
1. S-4 reveals pro-forma net leverage approaching or exceeding ~4x with thin synergy support and narrowing covenant headroom.
2. A guidance cut, a covenant amendment under duress, or evidence of integration-cost overruns beyond the disclosed deal charges.
3. Material erosion in the core franchise — organic revenue growth turning negative or North America margin reversal — that would signal the operating engine can no longer offset the leverage.

## Conclusion

Brink's is a healthy, cash-generative global cash-logistics leader that the market has derated ~28% from its February high on a specific, dated corporate action, the NCR Atleos acquisition. The operating evidence (revenue +5%, adjusted EBITDA +7%, non-GAAP EPS +12%, North America +27% organic, three of four quarters beating consensus, ~$436M FCF, full covenant compliance) does not support a structural-impairment thesis; the decline is deal-overhang derating, not earnings collapse. That argues firmly against an AVOID.

At the same time, the case for a BUY rests on a number that is not yet public: pro-forma post-close leverage. Layering a large debt-financed deal onto a balance sheet with thin book equity and ~2.65x net leverage removes the cushion, and whether the result is a manageable 3.5x or a binding 4x+ will be the difference between re-rating and further derating. Until the S-4/joint proxy makes that number derivable, the positive operating case and the negative deal-uncertainty case approximately offset.

We rate BCO **HOLD (uncertain)**. The stock is cheap on forward earnings with credible upside to the $143-163 analyst band if the deal closes cleanly, and limited downside (~13-15%) to the low-$80s if it does not — an asymmetry that favors patience over either chasing the upside or shorting the overhang. The catalyst that resolves the uncertainty (the S-4 and shareholder vote) is dated and near; revisit this name on that disclosure rather than committing with conviction today.
