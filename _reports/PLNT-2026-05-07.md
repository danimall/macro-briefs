## Executive Summary

Planet Fitness shares have declined 59% from their July 2025 peak of $114.47 to $44.01 (as of the latest available data showing $63.96 in early May 2026, with analyst data referencing $44.01 post-Q1 earnings). The decline accelerated following the May 7, 2026 Q1 earnings report, which revealed a 36% year-over-year decline in net new member additions, a full-year guidance cut from 9% to 7% revenue growth, and the withdrawal of the company's 3-year growth algorithm presented just months earlier at its 2025 Investor Day.

The fundamental cause is identifiable and self-inflicted: management repositioned marketing toward "premium fitness" messaging that alienated the core beginner/casual member demographic that made Planet Fitness the largest gym chain in the U.S. The CEO acknowledged this error explicitly on the earnings call. The central analytical question is whether this is a 2-3 quarter fixable execution stumble on a structurally sound franchise model, or whether it signals permanent competitive erosion as rival low-cost chains close the gap. With $255M+ annual free cash flow, 2,896 locations, 20.8M+ members, and a predictable recurring revenue base, the business has significant intrinsic value. At ~17-25x forward earnings (depending on which estimate you use), the stock is discounted relative to its history but appropriately so given the uncertainty around growth recovery.

The absence of insider buying at these levels, the negative estimate revision trend (-7.4% over 90 days for FY2026), and extreme options-implied volatility (84% near-term vs. 32% historical) all suggest the market assigns meaningful probability to further deterioration before stabilization.

## Company Overview

Planet Fitness operates the largest fitness center franchise system in the U.S. by member count, with approximately 20.8 million members across 2,896 clubs (2,604 franchisee-owned, 292 corporate-owned) in 50 states plus international markets including Canada, Mexico, Australia, Spain, and Panama. The business generates revenue through three segments: Franchise (35.4% of revenue — royalties, ad fund, placement fees), Corporate-owned clubs (41.2% — dues, enrollment, annual fees), and Equipment (23.4% — mandatory sales to franchisees for openings and replacements).

The franchise model is capital-light by design. Planet Fitness earns royalties at ~6.7% of franchisee revenue (creeping toward 7%) while franchisees bear the real estate and operating costs. Membership dues are collected via electronic fund transfer (92% of corporate members), creating a highly predictable, recurring revenue stream with minimal working capital intensity. The company's contractual pipeline includes commitments for approximately 750 additional new clubs.

The competitive positioning centers on the "Judgement Free Zone" brand targeting first-time and casual gym-goers at a $15/month entry point (raised from $10 in 2024). PF Black Card penetration reached 66.5% in 2025, up from 63.9% the prior year, demonstrating upsell traction at higher price points.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** Revenue grew from $937M (2022) to $1.324B (2025), representing a 41.3% increase over that three-year period (12.2% CAGR). The 2025 acceleration was driven by a 21.1% surge in equipment revenue (replacement cycle) and 6.7% system-wide same-club sales growth. Operating margins expanded from 27.4% to 29.8%. Adjusted EBITDA reached $552M (41.7% margin), growing 13.1% YoY. Adjusted EPS was $3.07, up 18.5% YoY. GAAP EPS was $2.62.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $418M (2025), up 21.7% YoY. Free cash flow of $255M (19.2% margin) after $164M in capex. FCF/net income conversion of 116% indicates high-quality earnings. The business requires relatively modest maintenance capex given the franchise-heavy model.

**Balance Sheet:** This area requires careful interpretation. Negative stockholders' equity of -$483M is entirely driven by $1.1B in accumulated buyback deficits. Long-term debt stands at $2.46B in fixed-rate securitized notes (5.27%-6.24% coupons). Cash and marketable securities total ~$541M. Interest coverage is adequate at 3.6x on operating income and 5.1x on EBITDA. The current ratio of 2.11 is healthy. The Altman Z-Score of 0.98 (distress zone) is a structural artifact of the capital-light franchise model plus aggressive buybacks — comparable to McDonald's and Domino's, which carry similar balance sheet profiles.

**Capital Allocation:** The company returned $500M in buybacks during 2025, including a $350M accelerated share repurchase at an average price of $108.76 — shares now trading at $44, representing significant value destruction from timing alone. A new $500M buyback program was authorized in December 2025.

**2026 Guidance (revised):** ~7% revenue growth (down from 9%), ~4% adjusted EPS growth (down from ~9%), ~1% same-club sales (down from 6.7% in 2025). Management withdrew the 3-year growth algorithm of 10-15% revenue / 15-20% EPS annual growth.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has materially deteriorated over the past quarter. The headline issue is a 36% decline in net new member additions in Q1 2026, which management attributed to a marketing repositioning toward "premium fitness" messaging that confused and alienated the core casual/beginner demographic.

Several growth vectors remain intact:
- **Unit growth:** 104 new clubs opened in Q4 2025 alone; pipeline of ~750 contractual commitments persists
- **Equipment replacement cycle:** The 21% equipment revenue surge reflects 2017-2020 vintage clubs entering mandatory 5-9 year replacement windows, providing multi-year tailwind
- **International expansion:** Australia, Spain, Mexico offer long-term runway, though Spain is currently loss-generating (-$3.5M adjusted EBITDA in 2025)
- **Gen Z penetration:** Fastest-growing demographic for the brand
- **GLP-1 net tailwind:** Evidence suggests weight loss medications are driving more, not less, gym engagement

However, the near-term trajectory is concerning. Estimate revisions tell the story: current-year EPS has been cut 7.4% over 90 days (from $3.55 to $3.29), and next-year estimates have fallen 5.4% (from $4.26 to $4.03). Current-quarter estimate has fallen 9.2% over 90 days.

Management acknowledged the error, suggesting awareness, but withdrew its growth framework rather than offering revised long-term targets. This signals internal uncertainty about how quickly member acquisition can be restored to prior levels.

- 10.9x forward FY2027E EPS ($4.03)

This compares to the only available peer with a positive P/E — OneSpaWorld at 33.8x. PLNT's historical trading range has been 25-40x earnings during growth phases. The current multiple range is the lowest valuation since pandemic lows.

On FCF: at $44/share and ~79M shares outstanding, the market cap is ~$3.5B. With $255M in annual FCF, that's a 7.3% FCF yield. Net of $2.5B debt less $541M cash, EV is approximately $5.4B, implying EV/FCF of ~21x.

The analyst consensus target sits at $103 (median) with a range of $85-$175. These targets likely reflect pre-crash valuations and will be revised lower, but even at a meaningful haircut, there appears to be upside if the business stabilizes.

At 13.4x forward earnings on a business generating $250M+ in annual FCF with 40%+ EBITDA margins, the market is pricing in meaningful probability of sustained growth impairment. If growth stabilizes at even 5-7% rather than the prior 10-15%, the stock is undervalued at these levels.

However, if member attrition accelerates and same-club sales turn negative, the equity cushion above the $2.5B debt load compresses rapidly. The stock deserves a discount to historical multiples given the strategic uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

Planet Fitness holds a dominant market position as the largest gym chain in the U.S. by membership count. However, the competitive position is under unusual pressure:

1. **Price gap compression:** The $10→$15 Classic Card increase narrowed PLNT's historical pricing advantage vs. competitors. Rival low-cost chains now compete at similar price points.
2. **Brand positioning confusion:** The marketing misstep actively undermined the brand's differentiation as the "non-intimidating" beginner gym. Competitors are filling the gap.
3. **Digital alternatives:** While fitness apps haven't replaced in-person gyms, they represent an alternative for the most casual segment of Planet Fitness's target demographic.
4. **Franchise model advantages persist:** 86 franchise groups operating 2,604 clubs create a distributed growth engine with minimal corporate capital requirements. The contractual 750-club pipeline demonstrates franchisee commitment despite current headwinds.

The competitive position is stable rather than deteriorating structurally. The brand misstep is correctable — Planet Fitness spent decades building recognition as the beginner's gym, and a few quarters of confused messaging haven't permanently erased that positioning. But the window for competitors to gain share is open while PLNT course-corrects.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**
1. **Membership growth recovery uncertainty (HIGH):** No visibility on when net new adds return to positive YoY growth. If the Classic Card price hike permanently impaired acquisition rates (elasticity exceeded expectations), the growth algorithm doesn't recover to prior levels.
2. **Leverage with declining growth (MODERATE-HIGH):** $2.46B in securitized debt with covenants tied to system-wide sales and debt service coverage. A sustained membership decline (not yet occurring at system level, but net adds declining 36% is a leading indicator) could eventually stress covenants.
3. **Capital allocation destruction (MODERATE):** The $500M remaining authorization at current prices would be accretive, but management's prior track record of ill-timed buybacks doesn't inspire confidence.
4. **Competition intensification (MODERATE):** Rivals expanding at similar price points while PLNT's differentiation is temporarily blurred.
5. **Tariff exposure on equipment (LOW-MODERATE):** Equipment sourced from Canada, China, Mexico, Germany. Could slow new club builds if costs rise.
6. **International expansion losses (LOW):** Spain generating -$3.5M annual losses. Containable but dilutive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75-85, 70-93% upside from $44):**
- Marketing error is fixable within 2-3 quarters; net member adds stabilize by Q3 2026
- Equipment replacement cycle provides multi-year revenue tailwind independent of member growth
- New $500M buyback at $44 retires ~14% of float (vs. ~5% retired at $108)

- Competitor expansion is a decade-old dynamic that hasn't materially dented PLNT's dominant share

**Bear Case ($30-35, 20-30% further downside):**

- Competitors capture share during brand confusion window
- Same-club sales turn negative, triggering franchisee dissatisfaction and slower unit growth
- Covenant stress emerges if system-wide sales decline 15%+ from current levels
- Management credibility gap widens after algorithm withdrawal and buyback timing failure

## Conclusion

Planet Fitness at $44 represents a structurally sound franchise business (40%+ EBITDA margins, $255M+ FCF, dominant market position) trading at a discounted valuation (13-17x forward earnings) due to a self-inflicted marketing error and subsequent guidance cut. The bull case — that the marketing misstep is correctable within 2-3 quarters and the stock re-rates toward 20x+ — is plausible and would yield 70%+ returns. The bear case — that the $10→$15 price hike permanently impaired the value proposition and competitors capture lasting share — would send the stock to $30-35.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY: (1) estimates are falling, not rising, which is a negative momentum signal; (2) management withdrew guidance rather than offering revised targets, indicating they lack confidence in timing; (3) no insider buying despite a 59% decline; (4) the options market is pricing 84% implied volatility, indicating sophisticated participants see material near-term event risk that hasn't resolved.

The correct action is to wait for evidence of membership stabilization in Q2 2026 before committing capital. The stock is cheap enough to recover substantially even from a higher entry point if the thesis proves correct.

The valuation framing of "~13-17x forward earnings" may understate the vendor-reported multiple; if the vendor figure is authoritative, the "cheap relative to history" characterization and margin-of-safety argument may warrant revisiting.
