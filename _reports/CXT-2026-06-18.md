**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Crane NXT is a security, detection, and authentication company carved out of the 2023 Crane Holdings split. It retained the Payment & Merchandising Technologies business and has spent the two years since reshaping itself through debt-funded M&A. The strategic logic is coherent: the legacy Crane Payment Innovations (CPI) vending/payment-acceptance business throws off roughly 26% operating margins and strong cash but is in secular decline, so management is redeploying capital into anti-counterfeiting, brand protection, and pharma/food serialization (track-and-trace), markets with regulatory tailwinds and faster organic growth. Three acquisitions in two years (OpSec 2024, De La Rue Authentication May 2025, Antares Vision closed March 2026) execute that pivot.

The cost of the pivot is visible in the financials. Total debt rose from roughly $751M at end-2024 to about $1.5B by March 2026, the corporate credit rating is sub-investment-grade (BB+/Ba1), and total operating margin compressed from 20.6% (2023) to 14.9% (2025) as lower-margin acquisitions, purchase-accounting amortization, and integration restructuring diluted the mix. Net income fell to $145M in 2025, the lowest in the standalone era. Core organic growth was only +0.7% in 2025; the entire reported revenue gain was acquisitions and FX, with the Currency business masking continued vending declines. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.11 (down 70%) overstates the deterioration because roughly $20M of the operating-profit decline was one-time transaction costs and a non-cash Antares Class B stock-comp mark; non-GAAP EPS of $0.60 beat consensus.

The stock has fallen about 32% from its October 2025 high near $69 to the mid-$40s, and the post-deal de-rating leaves CXT at roughly 11x forward consensus EPS of $4.23, a discount that prices in real risk. The most encouraging signal is that the CEO and CFO both made open-market purchases with their own cash on June 12 below the current price. Against that, estimate revisions are negative across the current quarter and year, a goodwill-impairment watch item on the Authentication unit is explicitly flagged, and integration of three deals is unproven. The risk/reward is genuinely two-sided, which supports a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Company Overview

Crane NXT was created on April 3, 2023, when Crane Holdings split into Crane NXT (CXT) and Crane Company (CR). CXT retained the payment and merchandising technologies franchise. For accounting purposes Crane Company was the spinnor/successor, so CXT's pre-2023 figures are carve-out combined statements with no continuity to the standalone ~$1.4–1.7B revenue base. Year-over-year analysis must begin in 2023.

As of Q1 2026 the company reports two segments:

- **Security & Authentication Technologies (SAT)** — Crane Currency (banknote design and production, micro-optics anti-counterfeiting) plus Crane Authentication (OpSec acquired 2024, De La Rue Authentication acquired May 2025). Customers are central banks, governments, and brand owners. Q1'26 sales $192.8M.
- **Detection & Traceability Technologies (DTT)** — the legacy CPI vending/payment-acceptance business plus the newly acquired Antares Vision (inspection and track-and-trace for life sciences and food & beverage). Q1'26 sales $194.9M.

The company describes itself as a principal competitor in most of its markets, with defensible positions in micro-optics banknote security and government authentication contracts, both high-barrier, trust-based franchises. The DTT segment is new as of Q1 2026; the prior structure carried this business as standalone CPI.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue (standalone, GAAP):** 2023 $1,391M → 2024 $1,487M (+6.9%) → 2025 $1,657M (+11.4%). The 2025 gain decomposes into +8.9% acquisitions, +1.8% FX, and only +0.7% core. Organic growth is barely positive and entirely carried by Currency while CPI/vending shrinks (core sales −3.6% in 2025; Payment Acceptance products −4.8% core).

**Margins are the central concern.** Total operating margin fell from 20.6% (2023) to 18.1% (2024) to 14.9% (2025). SAT operating margin fell from 23.0% to 18.1% to 12.0% as lower-margin OpSec and De La Rue acquisitions, purchase-accounting amortization and step-up, and integration restructuring diluted the segment. CPI/DTT held a strong ~26% margin in 2025 but is contending with vending volume declines. Net income fell from $188M (2023) to $184M (2024) to $145M (2025); diluted GAAP EPS moved from roughly $3.27 to $3.18 to about $2.50.

**Q1 2026:** Sales rose 17.4% to $387.7M (De La Rue +8.0%, organic +5.6%, FX +3.8%), but operating margin fell to 5.7% from 11.3% and net income fell 70% to $6.4M ($0.11 GAAP EPS). The collapse was largely non-recurring/non-cash: roughly $10.7M of Antares liability-classified stock comp (Class B shares tied to 2028 EBIT targets) plus $9.3M of higher transaction costs. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.60, about 4% above consensus ($0.58 estimate per Phase 1 data). DTT organic sales fell 5.1% on lower hardware/vending volumes; SAT organic rose 22.4% on strong Currency demand.

**Balance sheet and leverage are the key risk.** Total debt rose from $751M (2024) to $1,140M (Dec 2025) to roughly $1,509M (Mar 2026) after funding De La Rue (£300M) and Antares (~$418M total). Net debt/equity climbed to 72.5% at year-end and higher post-Antares. Corporate ratings are BB+/Ba1, sub-investment-grade. A €430M Term Loan B (2032 maturity) was added and the revolver expanded to $800M. Goodwill and intangibles are roughly $2.19B at Q1'26, more than 55% of total assets, with goodwill alone at $1,398M. The Altman Z-Score of 1.87 sits just above the 1.81 distress line, reflecting leverage from the transformation rather than operational distress.

**Cash flow quality is softening.** Operating cash flow ran $276M (2023) → $214M (2024) → $241M (2025). Capex is light (~$43M in 2025, guided $75–80M in 2026), so FCF remains positive, but interest expense (−$60M in 2025, up 26%; tracking ~$17.8M/quarter in 2026) is increasingly eating into it. The dividend is modest at roughly $39M/year ($0.18/quarter), about 1.7% yield, and is covered by FCF but competes with deleveraging for capital. No buybacks occurred in Q1 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests entirely on acquisitions converting into organic acceleration, because the existing portfolio is barely growing. Reported revenue growth has been respectable, but stripping out M&A and FX leaves +0.7% core in 2025. The Currency business is currently a tailwind, but banknote orders are lumpy and depend on central-bank and U.S.-government award timing rather than recurring as a steady annuity. The CPI/vending base faces a secular decline in cash usage and increased banknote durability, both named in the 10-K, plus near-term tariff-driven demand softness.

The strategic destination is more attractive than the current trajectory. Antares pushes CXT into pharma serialization and food/beverage traceability, markets driven by regulatory mandates (anti-money-laundering, track-and-trace requirements) and brand protection. If management integrates the three deals, restores Authentication margins toward Currency-like levels, and extracts organic growth from Antares' end markets, the long-term profile improves materially. None of that is yet evident in the numbers.

Estimate momentum is negative. Current-quarter consensus has been revised down 1.7% (30d) and 1.2% (90d); next-quarter down 2.0%/3.0%; current-year down 0.8%/0.9%. Next-year is marginally positive (+0.7%/+0.4%), the only constructive line. This is the opposite of the revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings acceleration. The earnings beat record is thin support: two beats and two in-line over four quarters, with surprises of 0–4%, indicating consensus is reasonably well-calibrated rather than systematically conservative. On a 12-month horizon there is no identifiable catalyst that reliably re-rates the stock, which is why the growth thesis here is uncertain rather than convicted.

## Valuation Assessment

CXT trades at roughly 11x forward consensus EPS of $4.23 (and ~10x the $4.73 next-year figure), against a peer median P/E of 29.5 in Electronic Equipment & Instruments. On a headline basis that is a steep discount. The discount is partly justified: peers like BMI (29.5x) and OSIS (26.5x) carry cleaner balance sheets and better organic growth, while the cheapest peer, ITRI at 12.9x, sits near CXT's multiple and is itself a leverage-and-execution story. The peer set is heterogeneous (MIR at 168x, PI negative), so the median overstates the relevant comparison.

The key valuation distinction is GAAP versus normalized earnings. Trailing GAAP EPS of ~$2.50 (~19x at the current $46.93) is depressed by acquisition noise; the Antares Class B liability ($36.7M as of Q1'26, marked against 2028 targets) will keep injecting earnings volatility through 2028, so trailing multiples will remain distorted. On the ~$4.23 forward consensus figure the stock is genuinely cheap at 11x, but cheapness alone is not a thesis here. The discount reflects real risks: flat core growth, sub-IG leverage, a named goodwill-impairment watch item, and negative estimate revisions. A low multiple on a no-organic-growth business with a declining core and integration risk is a value setup that requires the acquisitions to deliver before it re-rates. Analyst targets (low $50, mean $66.83, high $85) sit well above the current $46.93, but the sentiment trend is degrading and the targets predate the most recent estimate cuts.

A reasonable base case applies ~13x to ~$4.40 normalized FY26-27 EPS as integration noise fades, supporting a value in the high-$50s, meaningful upside if management executes. The bear case applies the current trough multiple to a year of margin pressure and a possible Authentication goodwill charge, holding the stock in the high-$30s. The spread is wide enough that the stock is fairly valued for its risk rather than mispriced.

## Competitive Landscape

CXT holds defensible positions in fragmented, specialized niches. Micro-optics banknote security and government authentication contracts are high-barrier, trust-based franchises where switching is rare and qualification is slow. The CPI payment-acceptance business retains ~26% margins and strong cash generation, evidence of durable pricing in its installed base even as volumes decline.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The legacy moat (cash/vending) is structurally eroding, so the company is deliberately reallocating toward authentication and traceability, where it is buying rather than building share. Those acquired positions (OpSec, De La Rue, Antares) are real assets in growing markets, but their margins are depressed today and integration is unproven. SAT's 22.4% organic surge in Q1'26 shows the Currency franchise remains strong; the question is whether the acquired Authentication and traceability assets can hold and expand share against established serialization and brand-protection competitors. Cumulative TSR underperformed the peer group for the first time in 2025 (191.0 vs. 219.8), after outperforming in 2023 (225.6 vs. 158.2), a signal that the market has marked down the competitive transformation pending proof. The position is stable-to-improving on a multi-year view but is not yet demonstrating the share gains that would justify a higher score.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Acquisition integration and leverage.** Three deals in two years loaded the balance sheet to ~$1.5B of debt at a BB+/Ba1 rating, diluted margins, and created ~$25M of Crane Authentication restructuring. Deleveraging capacity and dividend safety depend on integration delivering against ~$240M annual operating cash flow plus rising interest expense and $123.5M of assumed Antares debt to settle in Q2'26.

2. **Goodwill impairment, specifically named.** Management disclosed that a hypothetical 10% decline in fair value would leave every reporting unit comfortably covered except the recently acquired Authentication unit, which would fall within 10%. It holds 27.9% of total goodwill. With De La Rue/OpSec margins still depressed, an impairment is a credible 2026–27 GAAP catalyst (non-cash, but sentiment-negative).

3. **Secular decline in cash usage and vending weakness.** The 10-K warns that global cash trends and banknote durability could reduce demand; vending volumes are already falling, compounded by tariff-driven softness.

4. **Lumpy SAT revenue.** Banknote orders depend on central-bank and U.S.-government award timing.

5. **FX and geopolitical exposure.** Half of sales are outside the U.S. (JPY, GBP, EUR, SEK); Mexican Maquiladora manufacturing is tariff-sensitive; central-bank business in high-corruption-index countries carries FCPA exposure.

6. **Separation tax risk.** The 2023 spin relied on an IRS ruling; a challenge could create a material federal tax liability.

7. **Antares Class B compensation overhang.** The Reinvestment shares convert on 2028 revenue/EBIT targets at a 0–3x multiplier, creating a volatile mark-to-market stock-comp liability ($36.7M at Q1'26) that will swing reported earnings through 2028.

Several of these are partially priced into the depressed multiple, but the goodwill watch item and the leverage trajectory are specific, dated risks that could move the stock and are not fully reflected in trailing data.

### Options Market Signal

The options market shows balanced positioning rather than a directional bearish bet. Near-term ATM IV is 40.6% versus 38.5% one-year historical vol, fairly priced (2.1% spread), 36th percentile within the chain. Put/call skew is flat (-0.0%) and the put/call OI ratio has collapsed from 4.71 a month ago to 0.13, a large unwinding of bearish/protective open interest that coincided with the stock's 23.6% rebound from $37.98 to the mid-$40s. Term structure is in mild backwardation (40.6% near vs. 37.6% at three months), signaling some near-term event sensitivity but not acute stress. The signal does not contradict a HOLD; it confirms the market sees a two-sided, not collapsing, situation. Short interest is elevated at 19.2% of float with 9.3 days to cover, meaningful bearish institutional positioning that creates squeeze potential on any positive integration surprise, but also reflects credible skepticism about the leverage-and-execution story.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The acquisition-driven margin and GAAP-EPS depression is transitory. As transaction costs roll off and the Antares Class B mark stabilizes, normalized EPS power of ~$4.23–4.73 becomes visible, and at 11x forward the stock is materially mispriced. Authentication margins recover toward Currency levels, Antares delivers organic growth in regulated traceability markets, deleveraging proceeds from ~$240M annual OCF, and the CEO/CFO open-market purchases on June 12 (at $42.13 and $41.96, below the current price) mark the bottom of the de-rating. A re-rating to ~13x normalized EPS supports the high-$50s, with the analyst high of $85 reflecting full execution. Elevated short interest adds squeeze fuel on any beat.

**Bear case.** Core organic growth stays near zero as vending declines outpace Authentication recovery; integration of three deals strains a sub-IG balance sheet; rising interest expense erodes FCF and pressures the dividend; and the Authentication reporting unit (sub-10% fair-value cushion, 28% of goodwill) takes an impairment that confirms the deals were overpaid. Negative estimate revisions continue, and the multiple stays at trough. The stock holds the high-$30s, roughly its May 2026 low, a 15–20% downside.

The two scenarios are roughly balanced in probability, which is the core reason this is a HOLD. The bull case requires execution that is not yet in the numbers; the bear case requires only that current trends persist.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an uncertain HOLD, not a growth or income hold. The income (1.7% yield) is too modest to anchor the thesis, and the growth thesis depends on unproven integration. I lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive core organic growth (>2%) with DTT/vending stabilizing, confirming the acquired assets are growing rather than just adding scale.
- Consolidated operating margin recovering toward 16%+ on a clean (ex-transaction-cost) basis, demonstrating Authentication margin restoration.
- Estimate revisions turning positive (current-year consensus rising) alongside visible deleveraging (net debt/EBITDA trending down), which would validate the normalized-earnings re-rating case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment on the Authentication unit, or a downgrade of the corporate rating below BB+, signaling the leverage/integration risk is crystallizing.
- Core organic growth turning negative for two quarters with continued margin compression, indicating the vending decline is outrunning the pivot.
- A dividend cut or covenant pressure, signaling FCF is no longer covering capital commitments.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY26, expected early August 2026), with the Q2 settlement of assumed Antares debt and the first full quarter of the DTT segment providing the clearest read on integration progress and leverage trajectory.

## Conclusion

Crane NXT is a transformation story priced at a transformation discount. The 11x forward multiple is genuinely cheap relative to peers, and the CEO and CFO buying stock with their own cash below the current price is the highest-conviction insider signal in the file. But cheapness on a business with flat core organic growth, a structurally declining cash/vending core, sub-investment-grade leverage from three back-to-back deals, negative estimate revisions, and a named goodwill-impairment watch item is a value setup that requires proof before it pays. The ~32% drawdown from the October high reflects sophisticated sellers pricing real execution and balance-sheet risk, not just volatility.

The risk/reward is balanced: meaningful upside to the high-$50s if integration delivers, 15–20% downside to the high-$30s if current trends persist or an impairment lands. That symmetry, combined with the absence of a near-term catalyst that reliably re-rates the stock, supports HOLD. I would turn constructive on two quarters of positive core organic growth and a clean margin recovery, and turn negative on an Authentication impairment or a rating downgrade. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
