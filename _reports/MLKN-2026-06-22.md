**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MillerKnoll is the furniture collective formed by Herman Miller's July 2021 acquisition of Knoll. After a post-merger revenue peak of $4.09B in FY23, sales fell 11% in FY24 and stabilized at $3.67B in FY25 as the office-furniture cycle softened. The company is now showing a genuine operational recovery: Q3 FY26 net sales rose 5.8% (3.8% organic), total orders rose 9.2%, North America Contract orders rose 13.1%, and backlog grew to $711.6M. The Knoll integration is complete after $144.4M of cumulative spend, removing a multi-year drag on reported earnings. With adjusted EPS of roughly $1.95 in FY25 and a $16.50 share price, the stock looks cheap at ~8.5x adjusted earnings.

The problem is that the operational recovery is not translating into rising forward earnings expectations. Consensus next-year EPS has been cut 11.8% over the past 90 days (from $2.27 to $2.00), current-year estimates are down 6.2%, and next-quarter estimates are down 14.7%. The most recent print (Q3 FY26, reported March 25) missed by 6.3% and triggered a 22% single-day decline from ~$19.36 to ~$15.03. That price action, combined with a steadily falling estimate trajectory, indicates the order momentum is being offset by margin pressure (tariffs, mix) and a still-fragile demand backdrop. A rising order book that does not lift earnings estimates is not a bankable recovery.

Beneath the income statement, the balance sheet carries the real structural risk. Debt-to-equity sits near 2.0x, the Altman Z-score is 1.84 (grey zone), and roughly 40% of total assets ($1.15B goodwill plus $0.43B indefinite-lived intangibles) sit on a base that management explicitly flags as having thin impairment cushions: the Coverings reporting unit is only 10% above carrying value, and Global Retail and Holly Hunt are flagged as at-risk for further write-downs. With no insider open-market buying, a CEO 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 142,657 shares, recurring executive departures, and five-year TSR at less than half the peer group, the qualitative signals do not support stepping in front of a falling estimate trend. We rate MLKN **AVOID**.

## Company Overview

MillerKnoll designs, manufactures, and distributes interior furnishings for office, healthcare, education, and residential settings across more than 100 countries. The brand portfolio includes Herman Miller, Knoll, Muuto, HAY, Design Within Reach (DWR), Holly Hunt, Geiger, Maharam, Edelman, NaughtOne, Spinneybeck|FilzFelt, DatesWeiser, and KnollTextiles.

The business is organized into three reportable segments (reorganized effective March 1, 2025):

- **North America Contract** (~54% of sales; $1,965M FY25): office, healthcare, and education furniture sold primarily through independent dealers, plus textile brands.
- **International Contract** (~18%; $660M): the same model across EMEA, APAC, and Latin America.
- **Global Retail** (~28%; $1,045M): DWR studios, eCommerce, wholesale, and the Holly Hunt luxury brand. Higher gross margin (~46%) but directly exposed to consumer discretionary spending, interest rates, and housing.

Distribution is omni-channel: independent dealers carry the bulk of contract sales, supplemented by direct sales (including ~4% to the U.S. federal government), eCommerce, wholesale, and 75 retail studios. The competitive moat rests on design heritage spanning more than a century, brand equity, and a global dealer network. Manufacturing is assembly-based and lean, giving the cost structure meaningful variability through a downturn.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has been stagnant-to-declining since the FY23 peak.** FY22 $3,946M → FY23 $4,087M → FY24 $3,628M (−11%) → FY25 $3,670M (+1.1%). The office-furniture cycle softened materially over this window, and the company has not yet demonstrated durable top-line growth above the FY23 high.

**FY2025 reported a GAAP loss driven by a one-time, non-cash charge.** Operating income fell to $50.5M from $167.2M, almost entirely because of a $130M non-cash impairment (Global Retail goodwill $30.1M, Holly Hunt goodwill $62.2M, Knoll & Muuto trade names $37.7M). GAAP net loss was −$36.9M (diluted EPS −$0.54) versus +$82.3M (+$1.11) prior year. Adjusted EPS was $1.95 versus $2.08, so underlying earnings power was only modestly down and the headline loss is largely an accounting artifact. Gross margin was 38.8% (−30 bps), pressured by tariffs (−30 bps) and mix. Operating cash flow was $209.3M (down from $352M on a working-capital build), free cash flow roughly $102M, capex $107.6M. The dividend was held at $0.75/share, yielding ~4.5% at the current price.

**FY2026 shows operational improvement through three quarters.** Q3 (ended Feb 28, 2026) operating earnings were $44.9M (against a −$82.2M loss a year ago that included the impairment), EPS $0.34, adjusted EPS $0.43. Nine-month net sales were $2,837.5M (+4.8%) and nine-month net income was $67.9M versus $20.2M. Global Retail returned to profitability.

The qualifier is that Q3 missed consensus: $0.43 adjusted versus $0.46 expected, a 6.3% miss, the first miss after a streak of beats (the prior three quarters beat by +3.7%, +28.5%, and +34.7%). The market read this as deceleration in earnings visibility rather than confirmation of a recovery, sending the stock down 22% on the print.

**The balance sheet is the central concern.** Total debt is ~$1.31–1.34B against equity of ~$1.28–1.34B. The current ratio of 1.58 is healthy and near-term liquidity is ~$594M. Management has cleared the maturity wall by extending Term Loan A to April 2030 and Term Loan B to August 2032 (incurring an $8M extinguishment loss) and adding a $90M accounts-receivable securitization facility in September 2025. Buybacks were cut sharply, $12.3M YTD versus $84.8M the prior year, consistent with prioritizing deleveraging over per-share return.

## Growth Analysis

The bull narrative rests on cyclical recovery: order growth and backlog growth as the office-furniture cycle bottoms and corporates resume capital spending. That order momentum is real and is the strongest forward signal in the data.

The problem is that orders are not translating into rising earnings estimates, which is the variable that actually drives the stock. Consensus revisions have moved decisively in the wrong direction:

- Current quarter: $0.52, flat over 30 days but down 15.6% over 90 days
- Next quarter: $0.37, down 9.8% over 30 days and 14.7% over 90 days
- Current year: $1.82, flat over 30 days but down 6.2% over 90 days
- Next year: $2.00, flat over 30 days but down 11.8% over 90 days

The pattern is a broad downward reset across every horizon over the past 90 days. The reason is the gap between order growth and margin: tariffs (steel input costs, explicitly described as "material") are compressing gross margin, only partially offset by pricing. So volume recovers while per-unit profitability erodes, leaving forward EPS lower than it was three months ago even as the top line improves.

This is the crux of the growth case. Strong order books on a furniture maker matter only if they convert to expanding earnings. Estimate revisions of −11.8% next year and −14.7% next quarter are the opposite of the rising-estimate trend that would justify a constructive call. The most reliable forward indicator here, consensus revision velocity, is negative. We weight that signal heavily, and it does not support a growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

On a trailing GAAP basis the stock shows 111.7x P/E, distorted by the FY25 impairment loss and not meaningful. On adjusted FY25 EPS of $1.95, the stock trades at ~8.5x; on the current consensus FY26 figure of $1.82, ~9.1x; on FY27 consensus of $2.00, ~8.3x. The dividend yield provides some absolute support.

Against peers in Office Services & Supplies the picture is mixed. The peer median P/E is 21.5x and median yield 1.4%, so MLKN screens as both cheaper and higher-yielding. But the peer set is heterogeneous and several names carry their own distortions (HNI at 1854x, WSC negative). On a like-for-like basis MLKN's single-digit forward multiple reflects a genuine discount that the market is applying for specific reasons: ~2.0x leverage, a grey-zone Z-score, a 40%-of-assets intangible base with thin impairment cushions, and an estimate trajectory that is falling rather than rising.

The analyst price target of $32 (a single contributor, mean = median = high = low) implies ~94% upside and is so far above the current price and so thinly sourced that it carries little informational weight. With only two analysts covering the name (one buy, one hold) and the lone target stale relative to the post-earnings reset, this is not independent confirmation of value.

The key judgment is that a cheap multiple here is a value-trap signature rather than a mispricing. The conditions that would make the low multiple an opportunity (rising estimates, a catalyst, evidence the market is wrong about earnings power) are absent. Estimates are falling, there is no near-term catalyst beyond the upcoming Q4 print, and the discount is explained by real structural risk. A low multiple paired with falling forward earnings and impairment-prone assets has historically continued to underperform rather than re-rate. We score valuation at 5.5, not punitively low given the absolute yield and adjusted-earnings discount, but well short of the level that would reward cheapness on its own.

## Competitive Landscape

MillerKnoll competes in a fragmented, highly competitive contract-furniture market against Steelcase, HNI/Kimball, and Haworth, and in a crowded retail-furnishings market against national and online home retailers. Its differentiation is design leadership and the breadth of its brand portfolio, supported by a global dealer network.

The competitive trajectory is concerning at the relative-value level. Five-year indexed total shareholder return is 83.91 versus a peer group at 186.95, so MillerKnoll has roughly halved while peers more than doubled. That is the market's verdict that the Knoll integration, leverage, and earnings volatility destroyed relative value despite the strategic logic of the combination. The order-growth recovery is real and broadly consistent with an industry-wide office cycle bottoming, but it is not evidence of share gains; peers are recovering on the same cycle. The thin goodwill cushions in Coverings, Global Retail, and Holly Hunt suggest some sub-businesses are underperforming their acquired carrying values, which is itself a competitive-position tell. The trajectory is stable-at-best rather than improving, supporting a middling score.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and covenants.** Debt from the Knoll deal carries a leverage-ratio maintenance covenant and restricts dividends, buybacks, and M&A. With the Z-score in the grey zone, this is the dominant structural risk. Refinancing has eased timing pressure but not the underlying leverage.

**Recurring impairment risk.** A weaker macro environment or higher discount rates could trigger additional non-cash charges, as already occurred for $130M in FY25.

**Tariffs and trade policy.** Elevated to a top forward-looking risk; steel is a key input and tariff impacts have been "material," partially mitigated by price increases. This is the proximate cause of the falling margin estimates.

**Cyclical office demand and hybrid work.** Contract revenue tracks corporate profitability, office construction, and vacancy. The recovery is underway but remains macro-dependent, and would hit the discretionary-exposed Global Retail segment in a consumer downturn.

**Federal exposure (~4% of sales).** Budget-efficiency and procurement pressure are cited headwinds.

**New 10-Q risk factor.** Middle East conflict: supply chain, energy/petroleum input costs, and regional demand.

**FX.** GBP, EUR, and DKK exposure on international operations.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The office-furniture cycle has bottomed and MillerKnoll is recovering: orders +9.2% total, North America Contract orders +13.1%, backlog +3.7%, Global Retail back to profit. The FY25 GAAP loss was a non-cash artifact; underlying adjusted EPS of $1.95 means the stock trades at ~8.5x earnings power. The Knoll integration is complete after $144.4M of spend, removing a multi-year GAAP drag. Maturities are extended to 2030/2032, and free cash flow is being directed to deleveraging. If margins stabilize as tariffs are passed through and orders convert, adjusted EPS could recover, supporting a re-rate toward the prior $23 high. The 4.5% dividend pays you to wait.

### Bear Case
Order growth is not converting to rising earnings estimates: every forward consensus line is down over 90 days (next year −11.8%, next quarter −14.7%), and Q3 missed by 6.3%, the first miss after a beat streak. Tariffs are compressing margin faster than pricing can offset. Leverage near 2.0x with a grey-zone Z-score and ~40% of assets in impairment-prone goodwill/intangibles (Coverings only 10% above carrying value) keeps further write-downs and covenant sensitivity live. A consumer-spending downturn would hit the higher-margin Global Retail segment. With five-year TSR at less than half the peer group, no insider open-market buying, a CEO 10b5-1 sell plan, and recurring executive departures, there is no signal that insiders see value here. Downside toward the $13.77 52-week low and below on a further estimate reset or a renewed impairment.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The Q4 FY26 / full-year results call (announced May 27, 2026) is the next dated event. Given the 90-day estimate cuts and the Q3 miss, there is real risk of soft FY27 guidance and/or commentary on margin pressure. A renewed impairment to Global Retail, Holly Hunt, or Coverings is a plausible non-cash negative. None of these is a high-confidence, asymmetric catalyst, however; orders are growing and the company could just as easily reaffirm the recovery narrative.

**Timing window.** The next print resolves within weeks; FY27 guidance and tariff developments extend the relevant window 3–6 months.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest of 6.2% of float with 4.3 days to cover is moderate and uncrowded, with limited squeeze risk. The low SI also means informed investors are not heavily positioned short, a mild warning against the bearish view.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning is balanced (put/call skew −2.1%, OI 0.48), not confirming an aggressive bearish setup. Rich near-term IV makes put protection expensive and raises the carrying cost of expressing the view through options. Borrow on a $1.1B-cap NASDAQ name is generally available but the rich IV signals the market already prices a large move.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target toward the $13.77 52-week low (~17% downside) against a plausible squeeze/beat to $20+ (~21% upside) on any reaffirmation of the order recovery. That is roughly 1:1 or worse, below the 2:1 threshold required to short. The order-growth and dividend support cap the downside, and the balanced options positioning does not confirm the thesis.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is "leveraged, cyclically-challenged business with falling estimates," not "actively collapsing company with a timed catalyst." Order growth, the dividend, cleared maturities, and balanced options positioning make the risk/reward too symmetric to short. This is a name to avoid owning, not to bet against.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. **Estimate trajectory turns positive.** A reversal from the current −11.8% next-year revision to a stabilizing or rising trend over two consecutive months would signal that order growth is finally converting to earnings. This is the single most important condition.
2. **Margin stabilization confirmed.** Q4 FY26 / FY27 guidance showing gross margin holding or expanding despite tariffs (rather than the −30 bps FY25 trend) would address the core reason estimates are falling.
3. **Deleveraging progress with no further impairment.** D/E moving below ~1.7x and a clean quarter with no new write-down to Coverings, Global Retail, or Holly Hunt would reduce the structural overhang.
4. **Insider conviction.** Clustered open-market purchases by C-suite executives (currently absent) would be a meaningful change in signal, especially given the existing CEO 10b5-1 sell plan.

**Valuation re-entry.** A move toward the $13.77 52-week low would provide more margin of safety to reconsider even absent the above, but only paired with stabilizing estimates.

**Timeline.** The headwinds (tariff-driven margin pressure, a soft office-demand recovery, and leverage/impairment risk) are likely to persist through at least FY27 (calendar 2026 into 2027). Reassess at the Q4 FY26 print and again at the first FY27 quarterly result. Given the score trajectory (three of the last four reads at HOLD/AVOID with scores in the 4.4–5.2 range), the bar for upgrading is the appearance of rising estimates, which is not yet present.

## Conclusion

MillerKnoll presents a genuine operational recovery (order growth, backlog growth, a return to GAAP profitability after a non-cash impairment year) wrapped around a balance sheet and an estimate trend that do not yet justify ownership. The cheap headline multiple (~8.5x adjusted earnings, 4.5% yield) reflects real risk rather than mispricing: leverage near 2.0x, a grey-zone Z-score, ~40% of assets in impairment-prone goodwill and intangibles with thin disclosed cushions, and forward earnings estimates that have been cut across every horizon over the past 90 days while the stock dropped 22% on the most recent miss.

The order recovery is the bull's strongest card, but orders that do not lift earnings estimates do not move the stock, and the controlling variable here (consensus revision velocity) is negative. Until estimates stabilize or turn up, margins prove they can absorb tariffs, and there is evidence of insider conviction, the risk/reward favors staying out. We rate MLKN **AVOID** and would revisit on a positive estimate turn or a move toward the $13.77 52-week low with confirming fundamental stabilization.
