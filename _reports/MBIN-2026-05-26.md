## Executive Summary

Merchants Bancorp is a $2.1B market cap diversified bank holding company with $19.4B in assets, operating across multi-family mortgage banking, warehouse lending, and traditional banking. The company experienced a severe earnings reset in 2025 (EPS $3.78 vs. $6.30 in 2024) driven by $117.8M in credit provisions concentrated in approximately 5 borrower relationships involving mortgage fraud. The stock has rallied 45% from its 52-week low as Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 earnings demonstrated normalization, with EPS beats of 34% and 5% respectively.

At $46.78, MBIN trades at approximately 11.2x trailing earnings, 9.1x consensus FY2026 EPS of $5.12, and 1.25x tangible book value of $37.51. These metrics are attractive relative to the peer median P/E of 10.9x, particularly given MBIN's superior efficiency ratio (44% vs. industry 55-60%) and demonstrated share gains in warehouse lending. However, three specific near-term risks prevent a full-conviction recommendation: (1) an active FDIC/IDFI Memorandum of Understanding constraining growth, (2) multiple shareholder fraud investigations by Schall Law Firm and Pomerantz LLP, and (3) the options market pricing imminent stress via backwardated term structure with put/call OI ratio at 1.80 (up from 0.15 in April).

The stock's risk/reward becomes asymmetric to the upside only after the MOU is lifted and litigation outcomes clarify, likely in H2 2026 or early 2027. Until then, the path is 12-month appreciation toward $51-55 on continued earnings normalization, with material downside risk ($38-40) if litigation produces adverse findings or credit losses re-accelerate.

## Company Overview

Merchants Bancorp, headquartered in Carmel, Indiana, operates through three segments that create meaningful synergies:

**Multi-family Mortgage Banking:** Originates and services multi-family housing and healthcare facility loans through GSE programs (FHA, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac). Consistently ranks among the largest agency affordable lenders nationally. Added a LIHTC syndication platform that has raised $2.8B in equity since inception in 2020 ($700.7M in 2025 alone).

**Mortgage Warehousing:** One of the largest warehouse lenders nationally, providing short-term financing to mortgage bankers. Funded $66.3B in loan principal in 2025, up 46% YoY — double the industry growth rate of 22%. This segment took share as competitors exited the space.

**Banking:** Portfolio lending for multi-family and healthcare bridge/construction loans, residential mortgage banking, SBA lending, and traditional community banking through seven Indiana branches.

The operational architecture is lean: 735 employees managing $19.4B in assets ($26.4M assets per employee), with 96% of loans repricing within 3 months providing natural interest rate risk management.

## Risk Assessment

**1. FDIC/IDFI Memorandum of Understanding (HIGH):** Entered June 30, 2025. While management states it's "informal" and capital exceeds agreed levels, the MOU constrains growth, may restrict dividends below certain capital thresholds, and creates regulatory overhang. Its confidential nature means investors cannot independently assess the requirements. The MOU must be lifted before the market will fully re-rate the stock.

**2. Shareholder Litigation (MODERATE-HIGH):** Multiple law firms investigating securities fraud claims likely related to the timing and adequacy of disclosures about fraud-related loan losses. Class action filings typically take 12-18 months to resolve. Even if ultimately dismissed, the litigation creates headline risk and potentially constrains management's communication flexibility.

**3. Residual Credit Risk (MODERATE):** While NPLs are declining, $128M in multi-family nonaccruals and $60M in REO remain. Healthcare facility NPLs of $59.6M are a separate credit concern. One additional large borrower default could re-accelerate provisioning. The 324% CRE concentration (though declining) remains above regulatory comfort levels.

**4. CEO Selling (LOW-MODERATE):** Michael Petrie sold ~$2.1M in common stock in February-May 2026 at $44-47. He retains ~$86M in holdings, and the family's 57% ownership provides structural alignment. The sales are small relative to total holdings (~2.4%) but the absence of any insider buying against the backdrop of the stock's recent rally warrants attention.

**5. Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE):** NIM compressed 17bps in 2025 as the Fed cut rates to 3.50-3.75% by year-end 2025 (from 5.25-5.50% peak). If further rate cuts occur, MBIN's variable-rate book could see continued asset yield compression, though the 96% repricing characteristic means the balance sheet adjusts quickly in either direction.
