## Executive Summary

Zions Bancorporation is a $9.2 billion market cap regional bank operating seven affiliate brands across the Western US. The bank delivered 21% EPS growth in 2025, expanded NIM by 21 bps to 3.21%, and has a structural tailwind from "latent NII" — assets repricing to higher yields while funding costs have already reset — that management estimates at +7% NII growth for 2026 even absent rate changes. Capital has rebuilt substantially (CET1 11.5%, up from 10.9% in 2023), the technology platform modernization is complete, and estimate revisions are uniformly positive (+4.8% current year over 90 days).

The valuation at 9.7x forward earnings is modestly below the peer median of 11.7x, with a 2.9% dividend yield. The discount reflects real risks: a concentrated $13.4B CRE portfolio with significant near-term maturities (47% of multifamily alone maturing within 12 months), elevated uninsured deposits at 45% of total, and meaningful interest rate sensitivity in a macro environment where the probability of rate hikes has risen to 45-50% by year-end. The current rate regime with 30-year Treasuries above 5.1% creates direct pressure on CRE refinancing capacity. While the stock has appreciated 32% over the past year on earnings recovery, the forward path depends heavily on whether elevated rates trigger meaningful credit losses in the CRE book, which current 1.19% reserves may not fully anticipate.

## Company Overview

Zions operates through seven separately managed affiliate banks: Zions Bank (Utah/Idaho), California Bank & Trust, Amegy Bank (Texas), National Bank of Arizona, Nevada State Bank, Vectra Bank Colorado, and The Commerce Bank of Washington/Oregon. The bank holds $89 billion in assets with $61.2B in loans and $75B in deposits.

The company's unique structure as a standalone national bank (no holding company) creates regulatory simplicity but constrains M&A optionality. Geographic concentration is primarily in Utah/Idaho, Texas, and California (77% of commercial lending). Zions completed a multi-year core banking system replacement (TCS BaNCS) in July 2024, positioning it ahead of peers technologically.

**Net Interest Income Dynamics:**
NII grew to $2,627M in 2025 (+8.1% YoY) driven by NIM expansion to 3.21% from 3.00%. The key driver was a 55 bps decline in interest-bearing liability costs while asset yields declined less. Management's "latent sensitivity" model projects further NII tailwind of +7% in 2026 from contractual asset repricing, independent of rate moves. This is the single most compelling fundamental argument: $30.5B in commercial/CRE loans reprice within 6 months, locking in higher spreads as the portfolio seasons.

**Credit Quality:**

- ACL/loans: 1.19% (down from 1.25%)
- NPAs: $320M (0.52% of loans)

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $1,073M less $121M capex = $952M free cash flow. FCF yield on market cap: 10.3%.

## Growth Analysis

Earnings growth is decelerating from the exceptional 21% pace of 2025 but remains healthy. Consensus expects $6.50 in FY2026 (+8.2%) and $6.75 in FY2027 (+3.8%). The deceleration reflects the narrowing of the NIM expansion opportunity as repricing becomes more fully reflected.

Growth vectors:
1. **NII latent repricing** (+7% structural tailwind through 2026): High-confidence, contractually embedded
2. **Loan growth**: 3% in 2025 with C&I (+5.2%) leading. Consensus expects similar pace
3. **Capital markets fees**: +14% ex-CVA in 2025, benefiting from rates volatility and swap demand
4. **Efficiency gains from technology**: Core modernization complete; headcount down 11% from 2019; AI/automation early stage
5. **FirstBank branch acquisition**: Adds ~$630M deposits in California

Growth constraints: Western US economic sensitivity, CRE construction slowing, limited M&A capacity due to national bank structure, and the potential for rate hikes compressing loan demand.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive (+4.8% for current year over 90 days, +2.8% for next year). This is supportive but well below the +30% threshold that would signal a powerful upward re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $62.85, ZION trades at:
- 9.7x forward P/E (FY2026E $6.50)
- 9.3x next-year P/E (FY2027E $6.75)
- 1.54x tangible book value ($40.79)
- 2.9% dividend yield
- 10.3% FCF yield

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 11.7x. ZION's 9.7x represents a 17% discount. Western Alliance (WAL) trades at 9.1x with higher growth but higher CRE risk; SouthState (SSB) at 10.1x; Cullen/Frost (CFR) at 13.5x with a premium franchise.

The discount to peers is partially justified: ZION carries higher CRE concentration, larger unrealized securities losses ($1.9B AOCI), and higher uninsured deposit ratios than most peers. However, the discount has narrowed considerably from the 40%+ gap during the 2023 crisis as capital has rebuilt and NIM expanded.

**Where the market may be mispricing ZION modestly:** The 9.7x multiple doesn't fully reflect the embedded NII growth from latent repricing or the technology advantage from completed core modernization. On $6.75 FY2027 earnings at 11x (peer median), the stock would trade at $74. Whether the CRE maturity risk justifies the 2x turn discount is the central valuation question.

The analyst consensus target of $68.75 (mean) / $67.50 (median) implies 8-10% upside, with the high at $80. The floor target of $63 is essentially current price — limited downside protection from the sell-side.

- Completed core banking modernization (TCS BaNCS) ahead of most regional peers
- Affiliate model provides local brand loyalty with centralized operations
- Strong deposit franchise (81% loan-to-deposit ratio)

**Weaknesses:**
- No holding company structure limits M&A flexibility
- Efficiency ratio (62.6%) is above-average; several peers operate below 58%
- Geographic concentration in Western US creates correlated risk
- Market share in each state is relatively modest (#2-#15 positions)

**Technology Differentiation:**
The completed core system replacement is a genuine differentiator that most peers still have ahead of them. This enables stablecoin experimentation (TCS Quartz), AI deployment (Google Gemini), real-time payments, and modern digital banking. The execution timeline for converting this platform advantage into measurable efficiency gains or revenue growth remains uncertain.

The departure of Jennifer Smith (CTOO since 2015, architect of the modernization) in early 2026 introduces execution risk during the crucial phase of extracting value from the new platform.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CRE Maturity Wall (PRIMARY RISK):**
$13.4B CRE portfolio with 47% of the $4B multifamily segment maturing within 12 months. With 30Y Treasury yields above 5% and Fed rate hike probability at 45-50%, refinancing capacity is directly threatened. Weighted average LTV of 59% provides cushion, and zero multifamily nonaccruals/charge-offs to date is reassuring. But the combination of elevated rates + potential hikes + near-term maturities creates a non-linear risk that warrants caution. Office CRE ($1.7B) already shows 4% nonaccruals and 9.3% classified — a leading indicator of what could spread.

**2. Rate Environment Asymmetry:**
ZION is asset-sensitive (+100 bps = +4.0% NII). In the current macro regime where hikes are possible, this is a tailwind for NII. However, rate hikes simultaneously compress CRE valuations and increase refinancing costs for borrowers — creating a tension where NII benefits are offset by credit deterioration. The net effect is uncertain.

**3. Uninsured Deposits (45%):**
This was the proximate cause of the 2023 regional bank crisis for peers. ZION maintains 130% liquidity coverage of uninsured deposits, but a confidence shock (perhaps triggered by CRE losses) could reignite deposit flight risk.

**4. NDFI Underwriting Failure:**
The $50M fraud loss from two related mortgage finance borrowers represents a specific control breakdown. NDFI exposure has declined from $2.4B to $2.0B, but the segment retains risk. Recovery via legal action is uncertain.

**5. AOCI Overhang:**
$1.9B in unrealized losses (improved from $2.4B) continues to suppress tangible book value. Basel III Endgame proposals could require inclusion in regulatory capital, potentially constraining capital return. Amortization timeline: 5-7 years.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($72-80, +14-27%):**
- Latent NII tailwind delivers 7%+ growth in 2026-2027, with loan growth accelerating
- CRE maturities refinance successfully at manageable rates as the multifamily market stabilizes
- Technology advantage translates to measurable efficiency gains (sub-60% efficiency ratio by 2027)
- P/E re-rates toward peer median of 11.7x as 2023 crisis overhang fully fades
- Basel III Endgame softens, enabling capital return acceleration (buyback yield of 3-5%)
- Math: 11x × $6.75 FY27E = $74.25; 12x × $6.75 = $81

**Bear Case ($45-50, -20-28%):**
- Elevated rates trigger material CRE losses beyond current reserves; classified multifamily loans escalate from 17.5% toward 30%+
- Additional $200-400M in provisions required, compressing EPS to $4.50-5.00
- Deposit confidence shock as CRE headlines emerge, compressing funding stability
- Broader regional bank selloff as CRE stress becomes systemic
- Math: 9x × $5.00 = $45; recovery to 10x × $5.50 = $55

**Base Case ($67-70, +7-11%):**
- NII growth of 5-6% in 2026 as repricing tailwind is partially offset by competitive deposit pricing
- CRE losses elevated but manageable — incremental provisions of $50-100M vs. consensus
- EPS of $6.30-6.50 achievable with modest credit headwinds
- Multiple stable at 10-10.5x as peers also face CRE concerns
- Math: 10.5x × $6.40 = $67.20

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $55-57 (9x FY26E or ~1.35x TBV), providing sufficient margin of safety against CRE losses
2. Two consecutive quarters demonstrating CRE maturity resolution without material charge-offs (Q2+Q3 2026)
3. Rate hike probability declining below 25% (Hormuz resolution or disinflation data), removing the CRE refinancing headwind

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Classified CRE loans exceeding 6% of total CRE (currently 3.91% overall; office already at 9.3%)
2. Provision expense exceeding $150M in any single quarter (vs. $72M current run rate)
3. Uninsured deposit outflows exceeding 5% of total in any quarter

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected July 2026) will provide the first data point on multifamily maturity resolution and any incremental CRE stress. The June 16-17 FOMC meeting (Warsh's inaugural) will clarify rate path, directly affecting the CRE refinancing thesis.

## Conclusion

Zions Bancorporation offers a credible earnings recovery story at a reasonable valuation (9.7x forward P/E, 17% below peer median). The latent NII tailwind, completed technology modernization, and improving capital position support continued earnings growth of 5-8% through 2026-2027. Estimate revisions are modestly positive and the Q1 2026 beat (+7.3%) confirms operational execution.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY because of CRE maturity concentration: 47% of the $4B multifamily segment maturing within 12 months while 30-year yields exceed 5% and rate hike probability is 45-50%. This creates a quantifiable downside scenario — if 10-15% of maturing CRE loans experience distress requiring incremental provisions of $200-400M — that current reserves (1.19% ACL/loans) do not appear to cover. The weighted average LTV of 59% provides meaningful cushion, and zero multifamily charge-offs to date is reassuring, but the refinancing environment has deteriorated materially since these loans were originated. Until 1-2 quarters of maturity resolution data confirm that refinancing is proceeding without material credit events, the risk-adjusted return does not justify a BUY at current prices.

If CRE maturities resolve cleanly and rates stabilize or decline, the path to $70-75 is plausible. If CRE losses materialize at scale, the downside to $45-50 is proportionally larger than the upside to $74-80, making the asymmetry insufficient at $62.85.
