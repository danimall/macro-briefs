**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

GE Vernova is the highest-quality direct equipment beneficiary of the AI/data-center electricity buildout. As one of three OEMs globally able to supply large-frame gas turbines at scale, plus a top transformer maker (now controlling Prolec GE outright) and a leading grid-equipment supplier, GEV sits on the physical bottleneck that constrains every hyperscaler's power expansion. The numbers behind the thesis are unusually concrete for an industrial: remaining performance obligations (backlog) of $163.3B at Q1'26, up 32% year-over-year, guided toward $200B by 2027; gas-turbine manufacturing slots reportedly sold out through ~2030; and Q1'26 data-center-driven Electrification orders that exceeded all of 2025 combined. Power segment EBITDA margin expanded from 11.6% to 16.3% and Electrification from 11.1% to 17.8% in a single year. This is a multi-year, customer-prepaid, margin-accretive demand setup with rare visibility.

The complication is valuation and timing. The stock has roughly doubled over twelve months, hit an all-time high of $1,181.95 on April 23, 2026, and has since fallen about 27% to ~$867. The headline current-year consensus EPS of $31.00 (revised +110.7% over 90 days) looks like a textbook earnings-inflection BUY signal, but on inspection ~90% of Q1'26 reported EPS came from one-time gains (a $3.99B Prolec remeasurement gain plus a $0.33B Proficy sale gain). The clean forward number is next-year consensus EPS of $24.31, which has revised only +8.1% over 90 days. On that basis GEV trades at roughly 36-37x forward earnings, which is full, above the threshold where rising-estimate momentum alone justifies paying up, and in a macro window that is actively de-rating high-duration multiples.

We rate GEV a **HOLD (growth_hold)**. The 12-month thesis is intact and the consensus mean target of $1,216 implies meaningful upside, but the entry point is not asymmetric. We would accumulate on a pullback toward the low-$700s (where the forward multiple compresses to ~30x) or on confirmation that Wind losses are narrowing and order momentum has survived the June FOMC. Buying a premium-multiple industrial into a documented AI-complex de-grossing, a known event cluster (SpaceX IPO June 12, FOMC June 16-17), and options markets pricing imminent stress (near-term IV at the 92nd percentile, term structure in backwardation) is the wrong moment to add.

## Company Overview

GE Vernova was spun off from General Electric on April 2, 2024, and its installed base generates roughly 25% of the world's electricity. The company reports three segments:

- **Power (~53% of Q1'26 revenue):** Gas Power (heavy-duty and aeroderivative turbines, the crown jewel), Nuclear Power (including small modular reactors), and Hydro. The economics are a razor/razor-blade model: equipment sales seed decades of high-margin contractual service revenue.
- **Electrification (~32%):** Transmission, grid systems integration, power conversion and storage, and grid automation/software. The fastest-growing segment, now amplified by the Prolec GE transformer acquisition.
- **Wind (~15%):** Onshore and Offshore turbines and blades. The chronic loss-maker.

GEV is the *supplier* into the AI-power theme, not an AI company. It sells the turbines, transformers, and grid gear that utilities, independent power producers, and hyperscalers need to bring firm, dispatchable power online. That distinction matters: the demand for its equipment is physical and contracted, but the stock's valuation has become tethered to AI sentiment, which the company explicitly flags as a risk factor in its 10-K.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and segment performance.** FY2025 revenue was $38.07B, up 9.0% from $34.94B. Q1'26 revenue was $9.34B, up 16% year-over-year (+7% organic): Electrification +61% (Prolec GE plus organic +29%), Power +12%, and Wind −23%. The growth is concentrated in the two profitable segments while the loss-making segment shrinks, which is the right composition.

**Profitability — read the adjusted numbers, not GAAP.** FY2025 net income of $4.88B was flattered by a $2.05B income-tax benefit from releasing deferred-tax-asset valuation allowances. Q1'26 net income of $4.75B ($17.44 diluted EPS) was almost entirely non-operating. The durable signal is Adjusted EBITDA of $0.9B (9.6% margin) in Q1'26, up from $0.46B (5.7%) a year earlier. Segment EBITDA margins tell the real story: Power 16.3% (from 11.6%), Electrification 17.8% (from 11.1%), Wind −26.7% (worsened from −7.9%). Any model anchored on the trailing GAAP EPS of $17.69 or the resulting "25x P/E" headline misreads the company in both directions: the trailing number is inflated by gains, and the durable operating earnings power is what should be capitalized.

**Backlog.** Total RPO reached $163.3B at 3/31/26, up 9% sequentially. Composition: Equipment $75.9B, Services $87.4B, with the services book providing multi-decade visibility (16% within one year, 91% within fifteen years). Power RPO $99.7B (+30%); Electrification RPO $42.4B (+70%). This is the single most important fundamental metric, and it is accelerating.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Q1'26 operating cash flow was $5.2B and FCF $4.8B, but heavily inflated by a $5.6B increase in contract liabilities (customer down-payments and slot-reservation deposits). This is the structural feature of a long-cycle prepaid-order business: customers fund GEV's working capital. Cash was $10.2B against total debt of only ~$2.9B; the company remained net-cash positive even after a $2.6B senior-notes issuance to part-fund Prolec. Ratings are investment grade (S&P BBB / Fitch BBB+, both Positive outlook).

A note on screening artifacts: GEV's Altman Z-Score of ~1.08 flags "distress," but this is a false signal. The model penalizes negative working capital (current liabilities $48.1B exceed current assets $43.0B), but $31.8B of that deficit is contract liabilities — customer prepayments that fund the business, not a solvency hole. With investment-grade ratings, $10.2B cash, minimal debt, and quarterly FCF in the billions, the balance sheet is robust.

**Capital allocation.** The buyback authorization was raised to $10B in December 2025 (from $6B); $4.6B repurchased life-to-date and $1.3B in Q1'26 alone, with a $0.50 quarterly dividend. Share count is declining (basic shares ~269M from ~275M).

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on a structural shift in electricity demand that has multiple independent confirmations rather than a single-company story. Hyperscaler capex guidance, government policy support for grid and nuclear, and order data across the power-equipment complex all point the same direction. Within GEV specifically:

- **Order momentum:** Q1'26 orders up ~71% year-over-year, with data-center-driven Electrification orders exceeding all of 2025 combined.
- **Capacity scarcity as pricing power:** Gas-turbine slots are reportedly sold out through ~2030, giving GEV visibility and pricing leverage unusual for an industrial. The 10-K notes customers are paying slot-reservation deposits to secure delivery.
- **Transformer scarcity:** The Prolec GE buy-in (full ownership for $5.25B cash, closed February 2026) adds ~10,000 employees and seven plants (five U.S.) into a globally short transformer market, deepening the Electrification moat.
- **Margin trajectory:** Power and Electrification EBITDA margins both expanded ~450-700bps year-over-year, evidence of operating leverage as higher-priced backlog converts to revenue.

The estimate-revision data requires care. The headline current-year revision is contaminated by the one-time Prolec and Proficy gains booked in Q1'26; it will revert toward the operating run-rate as those gains roll off. The clean read is next-year consensus EPS revised +8.1% over 90 days, and next-quarter +12.8% — positive and supportive, but not the +30%+ operating inflection the headline implies. The underlying earnings power is genuinely inflecting (Adjusted EBITDA margins), but the magnitude is overstated by the accounting.

The principal drag is Wind: −$382M EBITDA in Q1'26 (−26.7% margin), with Offshore continuing to book higher contract losses. The Vineyard Wind project saw a December 2025 federal leasing pause (lifted January 2026), turbines are installed, and GEV is resolving outstanding claims and counterclaims. The bull case implicitly assumes Wind losses shrink as Offshore backlog burns off; an escalation in Vineyard counterclaims could surprise negatively.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the entire debate. On trailing GAAP EPS of $17.69 the stock looks like ~25x, but that EPS is inflated by gains and meaningless as a forward anchor. On clean next-year consensus EPS of $24.31, GEV trades at roughly **36x at $867 / 37x at $902.70**, a premium multiple.

Against peers (Industrials, by market-cap proximity), peer median P/E is 34.0: RTX 34.0, ETN 38.7, DE 33.1, UNP 22.4, BA 86.3 (the outlier). The cleanest comparable is Eaton (ETN), the other electrical-equipment, data-center-exposed name, at 38.7. GEV at ~36x forward is roughly in line with ETN and a premium to RTX/DE/UNP. The premium is defensible on growth (RPO +32%, Electrification +61% revenue, expanding margins) but it is not cheap.

The estimate-revision BUY framework is strongest when a stock is inexpensive relative to rising estimates: forward P/E below ~30x or PEG below ~1.5. GEV fails both: ~36x forward against durable EPS growth in the high-single to low-double digits implies a PEG well above 1.5. At premium multiples the asymmetry reverses: beats are expected and produce little re-rating, while any miss derates the stock hard. The 2025-2026 software derating demonstrated that a correct sector theme does not protect a premium-multiple stock from 40-50% drawdowns when multiple compression arrives.

There is genuine 12-month upside to consensus. Analyst targets range from $836 (low) to $1,424 (high), mean $1,216, median $1,250 — roughly 35-40% above the current price. These targets embed continued multiple support and backlog conversion toward $200B. They are credible if the macro cooperates, but they are not independent confirmation of mispricing at a 36x multiple; they reflect the same growth extrapolation the price already carries.

Net: the ~27% pullback from the April high has improved the entry, but not enough. The multiple remains full and rate-sensitive in an environment where the discount-rate ceiling binds high-duration names. This is a 5.5/10 valuation — fair-to-rich, with upside that depends more on multiple maintenance than on a cheap starting point.

## Competitive Landscape

GEV's competitive position is strong and improving. In large-frame gas turbines it operates in an effective oligopoly with Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi: capital intensity, long qualification cycles, and installed-base service economics make new entry impractical, and the result is multi-year sold-out capacity with pricing power. The "power-to-rack" integrated scope (generation plus conversion plus grid equipment) is a differentiator hyperscalers value when they need turnkey delivery against a deadline. Prolec deepens an already scarce transformer position, and the 91%-services RPO structure locks in decades of recurring revenue per equipment placement.

The trajectory is positive: share-of-wallet gains in grid equipment, expanding margins versus prior-year, and a widening moat from capacity scarcity. The main competitive watch item is government-backed Chinese competition in grid and conversion equipment over the longer term, plus sole-sourced critical-material exposure (rare earths, specialty metals, semiconductors). Neither is a near-term threat to the order book.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully reflected in the multiple:

1. **AI-sentiment beta (acute now).** GEV's own 10-K adds a risk factor stating its stock is "frequently linked to AI investment trends and sector sentiment," producing significant volatility. The current AI-complex de-grossing is hitting GEV's multiple regardless of order momentum. The drawdown from the April high tracks this de-rating, not a fundamental deterioration. [unverified — specific SoftBank/Super Micro/Broadcom market-stress signals are not in the provided sources]

2. **Wind/Offshore fixed-price overruns.** EBITDA negative and worsening in Q1'26. Vineyard Wind counterclaims are a live, unquantified margin risk on fixed-price contracts.

3. **GE IRS audit / Tax Matters Agreement indemnity.** The IRS is auditing GE's 2016-2020 consolidated returns (which included GEV businesses). An unfavorable outcome could trigger "material" indemnification obligations onto GEV. This is explicitly not reasonably estimable and no liability is accrued — an open-ended contingent liability that appears in no screen.

4. **Capacity-expansion mismatch.** GEV is investing to expand turbine capacity against slot-reservation deposits. The 10-K warns that if demand softens, "slot reservation agreements may not result in orders," exposing idle capacity and write-down risk.

5. **Tariffs:** estimated $250-350M cost impact in 2026 after mitigation.

6. **TMA strategic constraints:** the agreement restricts M&A, large stock issuance, and disposing of Gas Power for a multi-year period to preserve tax-free spin treatment.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 68.5% versus 48.2% one-year historical vol (rich by 20.3 points), at the 92nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (68.5% near versus 51.2% at six months). The put/call OI ratio jumped from 1.09 to 2.05 as the price fell 13.1% over the two weeks into June 11, and the one-week skew is +11.0% (put-heavy near-term). Some of this is event-hedging into the June 12 SpaceX IPO and the June 16-17 FOMC, which historically overstates durable stress around scheduled catalysts, but the doubling of put/call OI and the backwardation are consistent with a high-duration AI-linked name being actively de-grossed. The market is paying up for downside protection precisely in the window where a hawkish FOMC guide could compress the multiple further. This argues against adding at ~36x ahead of the cluster.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$1,250, consensus median; ~+44%)
The power supercycle is durable and under-modeled. Backlog reaches $200B by 2027 ahead of schedule, gas-turbine slots stay sold out through 2030 with pricing power intact, Wind losses narrow as Offshore backlog burns off, Power/Electrification margins continue expanding, and the $10B buyback shrinks the share count into rising earnings. The multiple holds at ~40x+ on the strength of visibility and scarcity, and the AI-rotation pressure proves transient. Analyst targets re-rate higher as backlog converts.

### Bear Case (~$700; ~−19%)
The macro de-rates high-duration multiples. A hawkish FOMC guide gaps yields higher, the AI de-grossing pulls the multiple from ~36x toward ~29x, Vineyard counterclaims escalate Wind losses, and any single order-timing wobble (lumpy hyperscaler orders) is punished asymmetrically at a premium multiple. The IRS/TMA contingency surfaces a material number. The stock revisits the low-$700s where the forward multiple is more defensible.

The base case sits around $960-1,000 over twelve months — roughly holding the current multiple as backlog builds, with the order book and margin expansion offsetting macro drag. That is ~11-15% upside from $867, attractive but not asymmetric given the ~19% bear-case downside and the near-term event risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the June 16-17 FOMC (multiple-setting macro event for high-duration names) and the Q2'26 earnings release (~late July), when order/RPO momentum and Wind loss trajectory will be tested.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-$700s, compressing the forward multiple to ~30x on $24.31 next-year EPS, where the estimate-revision support and PEG framework would favor accumulation.
- Confirmation over two quarters that Wind EBITDA losses are narrowing (Q2 Wind margin better than Q1's −26.7%) alongside RPO growth holding above +25% — evidence the bifurcated business is converging toward the profitable segments.
- A hawkish-FOMC pass that leaves the multiple intact while order momentum continues, removing the near-term macro overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Backlog/RPO growth decelerating below +15% year-over-year, signaling the order surge is cresting (slot saturation).
- Materialization of the GE IRS/TMA indemnity at a quantified, material figure.
- Vineyard counterclaims producing a discrete, sizable Offshore charge that pushes Wind losses wider rather than narrower.
- A guidance cut or a clear miss on Adjusted EBITDA margin trajectory at the Power or Electrification level.

## Conclusion

GE Vernova is a genuinely advantaged business — an oligopoly supplier at the physical bottleneck of the electricity buildout, with a deposit-funded backlog, expanding margins in its two core segments, a net-cash investment-grade balance sheet, and shareholder-friendly capital allocation. The growth durability and the 12-month upside to consensus targets justify owning it. What prevents a BUY at ~$867-903 is the combination of a full clean-forward multiple (~36-37x on $24.31, above the threshold where rising estimates alone warrant paying up), a headline EPS-revision signal that is largely an accounting artifact rather than operating inflection, and an acutely hostile near-term window: a known event cluster (SpaceX IPO June 12, FOMC June 16-17), an AI-complex de-grossing the company is explicitly exposed to, and options markets pricing imminent stress at the 92nd IV percentile in backwardation.

We rate GEV **HOLD (growth_hold)**. This is a high-quality compounder we want to own at a better entry. Accumulate toward the low-$700s, or upgrade on confirmation that Wind is converging and order momentum has cleared the June macro events.
