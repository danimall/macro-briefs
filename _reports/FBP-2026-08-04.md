## Executive Summary

First BanCorp is delivering the best operating results in its post-crisis history, and the operating mechanism is unusually transparent. Q2 2026 produced $96.15M of net income, $0.62 diluted EPS (+24% year over year), a 4.87% net interest margin, a 48.07% efficiency ratio, 2.02% return on average assets and 19.49% return on equity. The engine is balance-sheet remix rather than balance-sheet growth: average interest-earning assets actually declined $114M year over year, while average loans rose $334M and average securities and interest-bearing cash fell $449M. The FY2025 10-K quantifies the remaining runway at approximately $1.1B of 2026 investment cash flows carrying a 2.41% average yield, redeployable into loans yielding above 6%. That spread pickup — roughly $34M of annualized pre-tax net interest income, or about 18bp of margin on $18.87B of average earning assets — reconciles independently to management's guided 3-5bp of quarterly margin expansion, which was raised from the 2-3bp indicated in the February 10-K.

The problem is price. At $29.26 the stock is 0.5% below its $29.40 52-week high and has appreciated 41.8% over twelve months, of which roughly 16% has come since the last time we reviewed the name in mid-June. Current-year consensus of $2.40 puts the multiple at 12.2x, next-year consensus of $2.52 at 11.6x, against a regional-bank peer median of 12.3x. Tangible book multiple is 2.31x on $12.68 per share. Analyst targets bracket $28 to $33 with a $31 mean, 6% above spot. The valuation gap that made this compelling at $24 in May and $25 in June has largely closed. Multiple expansion, not estimate revision, did most of the work: consensus current-year EPS rose 6.3% over 90 days while the stock rose materially more.

Two fundamental issues also argue for patience. First, Q2's 12bp sequential margin gain included roughly 7bp from acceleration of unamortized purchase discount and net deferred fees on Puerto Rico refinancings — real cash income that pulls forward future accretion, so underlying expansion was closer to 5bp, at the top of the new guide rather than above it. Second, credit is improving on the lagging metric and deteriorating on the leading one: annualized net charge-offs fell to 0.49% from 0.65%, but loans 30-89 days past due rose $32.9M to $143.4M with $20.7M of that increase in consumer loans and finance leases, mainly auto, while allowance coverage declined to 1.85% from 1.87% in Q1 and 1.90% at year-end. Management labels the delinquency trend seasonal. It may be, but this is the portfolio most exposed to a household-spending slowdown, and the reserve cushion is thinning at the same time.

We move to HOLD with a $32.50 base case (+11%), a $35 bull case (+20%) and a $24 bear case (-18%).

## Company Overview

First BanCorp is the holding company for FirstBank Puerto Rico, a full-service commercial bank with 57 branches in Puerto Rico, 8 in the U.S. and British Virgin Islands, and 8 in Florida, staffed by 3,218 employees. At December 31, 2025 the balance sheet carried $19.1B of total assets, $13.1B of loans held for investment, $16.7B of deposits and $2.0B of stockholders' equity. Loans reached $13.3B by June 30, 2026.

Earning assets are roughly 69% loans and 31% securities and short-term investments. Within loans, commercial mortgage exposure is $2.6B (about 19% of the book), split $1.7B in Puerto Rico and $0.8B in Florida, alongside meaningful consumer, auto and finance lease, and residential mortgage portfolios. Fee income is modest and granular: Q2 2026's $35.7M comprised card and processing income $12.5M, deposit service charges $9.9M, mortgage banking $3.7M, insurance commissions $3.1M and other $6.5M. There is no capital-markets business. This is a spread lender whose earnings are determined by deposit cost, asset yield and credit cost.

The distinguishing characteristic is funding. Excluding government deposits, the average cost of interest-bearing checking and savings accounts was 0.66% in both Q1 and Q2 2026, unchanged in the fourth year of a high-rate environment. FirstBank funded a 6.11% earning-asset yield with a 2.07% cost of interest-bearing liabilities in Q2, producing a 4.04% net interest spread. Mainland regionals of comparable size have spent three years defending deposit betas; FBP's core book did not reprice.

## Financial Analysis

### The five-year record reframes the story

The proxy's pay-versus-performance disclosure provides a cleaner series than a two-year data pull:

| Fiscal year | Net income | Pre-tax pre-provision income |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $281.0M | $391.5M |
| 2022 | $305.1M | $475.3M |
| 2023 | $302.9M | $459.5M |
| 2024 | $298.7M | $451.1M |
| 2025 | $344.9M | $499.2M |

Net income declined modestly for three consecutive years before rising 15.5% in 2025; pre-tax pre-provision income fell in both 2023 and 2024 before reaching a record. FBP is a cyclical spread lender in a small economy, not a serial compounder. Liability costs repriced faster than the securities book on the way up; the reverse is happening now. Anyone paying 2.31x tangible book is paying a premium multiple at a favorable point in a cycle this company has not escaped. Per-share results held up better than the aggregate during the plateau — EPS was $1.81 in 2024 on $298.7M of net income — because of persistent buyback accretion.

### FY2025 and the earnings-base adjustment

FY2025: net interest income $868.9M (+7.6%), margin 4.58% versus 4.25%, non-interest income $131.9M (flat), non-interest expense $498.1M (+2.3%), efficiency 49.77%, provision $86.0M (+43.6%), tax expense $71.9M (down $20.6M), net income $344.9M, diluted EPS $2.15 (+18.8%), ROA 1.81%, ROE 18.74%.

The tax line fell while pre-tax income rose, driven partly by a $16.6M reversal of a deferred tax asset valuation allowance. Management's own adjusted net income was $325.3M, excluding that reversal, a $2.3M employee retention credit and a $1.1M FDIC special assessment benefit — approximately $2.03 of adjusted EPS on the ~160.4M diluted share count implied by $344.9M ÷ $2.15. GAAP effective tax rate was 17.25%; excluding discrete items the 10-K discloses 21.6%. Q2 2026 ran at 20.0%.

This cuts both ways for the investment case. Trailing valuation on clean earnings is 14.4x rather than 13.6x. But the *growth* is better than the headline suggests: normalized 2026 earnings against a clean $2.03 base implies roughly 19% growth, not the ~13% one gets by comparing to the inflated $2.15. The valuation allowance is a non-cash tax item — it affects net income, EPS, effective rate and equity, not net interest income, pre-provision earnings or cash flow. $75.0M of allowance remains against a $149.0M net deferred tax asset, so further reversals are possible and would again inflate GAAP results.

Normalizing: stripping the ~$3.4M purchase-discount and deferred-fee acceleration (≈$2.7M after tax, ≈$0.018 per share) puts normalized Q2 EPS near $0.60. ### Capital, liquidity and returns

At June 30, 2026: CET1 16.96%, total capital 18.21%, leverage 11.72%, non-GAAP tangible common equity 10.08%. Cash and equivalents $561.3M; total available liquidity 19.60% of assets (down from 20.14% in Q1), including $2.1B of free high-quality liquid securities and $1.1B of FHLB capacity. The FY2025 10-K put total available liquidity at 132% of uninsured deposits excluding collateralized government balances. Allowance coverage 1.85%; non-performing loans $94.6M, or 0.71% of loans.

Capital return is the cleanest element. FY2025 saw $150M of repurchases, $115.7M of common dividends and a $61.7M trust preferred redemption — approximately 95% of net income — with shares outstanding falling from 163.9M to 156.6M. Q2 2026 returned $50.0M of buybacks and $31.0M of dividends, 84% of the quarter's earnings. The quarterly dividend was raised 11.1% to $0.20 in Q1 2026 and re-declared in July; annualized $0.80 on $29.26 is a 2.73% yield versus a 2.6% peer median. Remaining authorization was $187.2M as of February 20, 2026. At the Q2 pace, annualized capital return of roughly $324M equals about 7.3% of market capitalization — a run-rate sensitivity, since buyback pace is discretionary.

One reconciliation note: the $31.0M Q2 dividend at $0.20 implies approximately 155.0M shares, while the supplied share count is 151.6M. Continued repurchases between the record date and the quote date is the plausible explanation. Per-share metrics are unaffected; aggregate yield figures carry roughly 2-3% imprecision. Separately, the supplied profile lists trailing and forward P/E identically at 12.22, which corresponds to $2.40 consensus rather than $2.15 trailing GAAP EPS (13.6x). We use consensus explicitly throughout.

Standard bankruptcy-risk screens should be disregarded here: the Altman Z-Score has no coherent interpretation for a deliberately leveraged depository. The relevant solvency tests are regulatory capital (16.96% CET1), tangible common equity (10.08%) and reserve coverage (1.85%), all comfortable.

## Growth Analysis

Three drivers, with different durability.

**Securities redeployment (highest confidence, finite).** Roughly $1.1B of 2026 investment cash flows at a 2.41% average yield redeployed above 5.5% adds approximately $34M of annualized pre-tax net interest income — about 18bp of margin, or roughly $0.175 per share after tax at a 20% rate. That figure reconciles to management's 3-5bp quarterly guide. Realized expansion was 7bp in Q1 and 12bp in Q2, both above the original 2-3bp guide, though Q2 was flattered by non-recurring accretion. The runway is two to three years at the current pace, after which this driver exhausts.

**Loan growth (moderate confidence).** Q2 originations were $1.7B, up 21% year over year and $469.5M sequentially, concentrated in commercial and construction lending in Puerto Rico. Net loan growth was $168.8M — the gap between origination and net growth reflects refinancing volume and runoff. Full-year guidance is 3-5%. The important unresolved question is whether Puerto Rico commercial and construction demand reflects a durable expansion or a federal disaster-recovery spending pulse with an end date. We could not verify current Puerto Rico employment, GDP, population trend or remaining undisbursed federal obligations, and we flag this as the most material gap in the analysis rather than assuming either answer.

**Buyback accretion (high confidence, self-limiting).** EPS grew 18.8% in FY2025 against 15.5% net income growth; the gap is share shrinkage. At an ~84% payout ratio and 19.5% ROTCE, organic retained growth is roughly 3%, with buybacks adding perhaps 3-4% to per-share results. That supports mid-to-high-single-digit EPS growth once remix ends. Repurchasing at 2.31x tangible book is materially less accretive to book value than repurchasing at the 1.7x of a year ago, which is a real consideration for capital-allocation efficiency going forward.

Estimate revisions corroborate momentum without signaling an inflection: current-year consensus rose 6.9% over 30 days and 6.3% over 90 days to $2.40; next year rose 5.2%/6.4% to $2.52. The earnings surprise record is four consecutive beats averaging roughly 14.7%, which raises confidence that $2.40 is achievable and possibly conservative. This is steady upward drift at a multiple that has already expanded, not the +30% revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection at a compressed multiple.

Growth score: 6.2. Real, quantified, near-term visible, but geographically capped and with the largest driver on a defined timer.

Peers: TCBI 13.5x, FULT 11.0x, UCB 12.6x, CATY 12.3x, IBOC 11.3x.

The bull argument is straightforward: FBP trades at the peer median earnings multiple while generating roughly double peer return on assets, a 48% efficiency ratio and top-quartile capital. On quality-adjusted grounds it should command a premium P/E, not parity. The two multiples are internally consistent — parity on P/E with double the ROE arithmetically requires roughly double the P/TBV — so the 2.31x tangible book is not an independent objection so much as the same observation restated.

The bear argument is that current earnings are cyclically elevated and geographically concentrated, so the market's refusal to award a premium multiple is rational. Net charge-offs of 0.49% annualized are near a cyclical floor; allowance coverage is being drawn down; margin sits at a record 4.87% with 7bp of the last 12bp from non-recurring accretion. Assume through-cycle net charge-offs of 0.80-1.00% (adding roughly $40-65M of provision, or $0.20-0.33 per share after tax) and 30bp of margin mean reversion, and normalized earnings power is closer to $1.90-2.10. On $2.10 the stock is 13.9x — full for a single-jurisdiction bank with a BB+ holding-company rating at Fitch. The 2022-2024 plateau at approximately $300M of net income is the empirical support for that normalization.

Both readings are defensible, which is why we land at fair value rather than mispricing. Analyst targets bracket $28 to $33 with a $31 mean and median, 6.0% above spot, and the low target is below the current price. Consensus has been moving up (one buy upgrade in the last month, 5 buys / 2 holds now), and on a stock with rising estimates and a sub-13x multiple we would treat the mean target as stale rather than binding. But the distribution is tight and the stock is inside it, a meaningfully different setup from the May and June reviews, when the shares traded at roughly 10.3-11.2x current-year estimates and 1.9-2.0x tangible book.

Our base case applies 13.0x to next-year consensus $2.52 for $32.50, an 11.1% gain. Getting to that multiple requires the market to award a modest quality premium it has withheld for two years. Valuation score: 5.8.

## Competitive Landscape

The 4.87% margin and 0.66% core interest-bearing checking and savings cost are the evidence of structural advantage. Puerto Rico's banking sector consolidated substantially over the past fifteen years through failures and acquisitions, leaving a small number of scaled deposit franchises; FirstBank itself absorbed Banco Santander Puerto Rico in 2020, and the unamortized purchase discount still flowing through net interest income is a residue of that transaction. A concentrated deposit market with limited new entry is one plausible explanation for persistently cheap, sticky core deposits. A demand-side explanation deserves equal weight: island depositors may simply be less rate-sensitive given lower average balances and thinner digital-brokerage penetration. Either way, the durability of the 0.66% core deposit cost is the single most important variable in this equity, and we could not verify deposit market-share data.

OFG Bancorp also reported strong Q2 2026 results, which suggests the favorable margin environment is island-wide rather than FBP-specific. That cuts both ways: market structure persists longer than execution advantages, making the returns more durable, but it also means FBP cannot claim a proprietary franchise.

Where FBP does appear to differentiate is asset quality and capital discipline — 0.71% non-performing loans, 1.85% coverage, 16.96% CET1 and a five-year total shareholder return of 266 indexed versus 213 for the proxy peer group. The structural headwinds are demographic and fiscal rather than competitive: multi-decade population decline, a completed sovereign debt restructuring under continuing federal oversight, and post-2017 economic activity substantially financed by finite federal appropriations. Competitive position score: 6.5 — the margin trajectory is improving, the underlying economy is not.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-jurisdiction concentration.** 65 of 73 branches are in Puerto Rico or the USVI/BVI. Direct Puerto Rico government exposure is $297.8M, with $138.7M to USVI public corporations. A fiscal deterioration, hurricane, or resumption of population outflow would simultaneously hit loan demand, credit costs, collateral values and government deposit balances. The correlations are tighter than for a mainland bank of similar size.

**Government deposit concentration.** Fully collateralized government deposits were $3.0B at June 30, 2026, up $167.7M in the quarter — roughly 18% of deposits. These balances fell approximately $600M during 2025, which is why total deposits declined $201.2M despite $193.3M of core deposit growth. They are also the marginal source of funding-cost pressure: blended interest-bearing checking and savings cost rose 5bp to 1.26% on higher government deposit rates while the ex-government measure was flat at 0.66%. Because these balances are fully collateralized, outflows are met by releasing pledged securities rather than asset sales; this is an earnings-volatility risk more than a liquidity risk. Uninsured deposits were $7.5B including government balances, $4.8B excluding collateralized government deposits (29.79% of total).

**Consumer credit at the front of the pipeline.** The most important item in an otherwise clean quarter: 30-89 day past dues rose $32.9M to $143.4M with $20.7M of the increase in consumer loans and finance leases, mainly auto, while coverage fell to 1.85% and provision commentary noted "a lower benefit from macroeconomic factors than in the previous quarter." Early-stage delinquency leads charge-offs by two to four quarters. Management calls the trend seasonal, and one quarter of migration is weak evidence of a cycle turn; the 5bp coverage decline is also arithmetically consistent with loan growth rather than deliberate release. But this is the portfolio most exposed to a household spending slowdown. The disclosed sensitivity bounds the exposure: a more adverse macroeconomic scenario would add approximately $43M to the allowance, roughly $0.22 per share after tax.

**Commercial real estate.** $2.6B of commercial mortgage exposure, with the $1.7B Puerto Rico book 40% retail, 26% office and 19% hotel. $563.0M matures within twelve months at a 5.64% weighted average rate, of which $136.3M is office CRE at 5.53% — existing coupons are close enough to market that refinancing shock should be contained. The derived ~$440M of Puerto Rico office exposure against $2.0B of equity is a genuine concentration.

**Florida generates risk out of proportion to its footprint.** Eight branches, but $796.9M of the $1.1B shared national credit book, $594.8M of brokered CDs, and the $14.8M commercial and industrial nonaccrual migration that drove Q2's non-performing loan increase. Shared national credits are participations where FirstBank neither leads underwriting nor controls workout. Management appears to be shrinking this book (SNC exposure fell from $1.3B to $1.1B in FY2025), which is the right direction. We would not describe Florida as delivering diversification: deposits, branches and revenue remain overwhelmingly Puerto Rico, and a changing loan mix does not by itself reduce franchise concentration.

**Rate path.** The FY2025 10-K assumed two Fed cuts in H2 2026. For the remix trade, a higher-for-longer path would be modestly favorable — reinvestment yields stay elevated — while a faster-than-expected easing cycle would compress the asset-yield side of the trade and shrink the reinvestment tailwind. The remix benefit is a spread pickup rather than an outright rate bet, which partially insulates it, but the absolute reinvestment yield matters.

**Holding-company credit standing.** BB+ from Fitch at both the holdco and bank, BBB/BBB+ from KBRA. Below investment grade at Fitch constrains wholesale funding flexibility.

Risk score: 5.2. The concentration risks are known and partly reflected in the peer-parity multiple; the auto delinquency trend against declining coverage, and the hurricane/fiscal tails, are less clearly priced.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 102.9% on May 6 to 40.1% on August 4, with the shares up 20.6% over the same span. Most of that is post-earnings vol crush plus a likely stale, illiquid May reading; put/call open interest of 0.01 indicates effectively no put open interest at all. We read this as a thin options book rather than directional information, and we assign it low weight. The absence of any hedging demand is at least consistent with the fundamental view that near-term downside risk is idiosyncratic (credit, government deposits) rather than event-driven, but the book is too illiquid to support a directional inference.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $35 (+19.6%)

The securities remix continues delivering 3-5bp of quarterly margin expansion through 2027, taking margin toward 5.10-5.20%. Loan growth lands at the top of the 3-5% guide as Puerto Rico commercial and construction demand proves durable rather than a spending pulse. Auto delinquency proves seasonal and reverses in Q3, net charge-offs hold near 0.50%, and 2027 EPS reaches $2.65-2.75 against the $2.52 currently modeled. Continued repurchase of roughly 3-4% of shares annually plus a growing dividend compounds per-share results. The market finally awards a quality premium — 13.5x on $2.60 — recognizing that a 2.02% ROA, 48% efficiency, 17% CET1 bank should not trade at the multiple of a 1.1% ROA mainland peer. $35.

### Bear case — $24 (-18%)

The auto delinquency increase is the leading edge of a consumer credit cycle rather than seasonality. Net charge-offs migrate toward 0.85-1.00%, provision rises $40-65M, and the adverse-scenario reserve build of approximately $43M lands. Purchase-discount accretion normalizes, removing several basis points from margin. The government deposit block repeats its 2025 $600M outflow, forcing replacement with brokered or FHLB funding at market rates and compressing spread. Federal reconstruction disbursements wind down, loan demand slows, and the remix runway exhausts by 2028 with no successor driver. Earnings settle near $2.20-2.30 and the multiple compresses to 10.5x, roughly 1.8x tangible book. $24.

### Base case — $32.50 (+11.1%)

13.0x next-year consensus $2.52. Margin expansion continues at the lower end of guidance as accretion normalizes; loan growth of 3-4%; net charge-offs drift modestly higher toward 0.60-0.70% without a credit event; capital return of roughly $320M annually. A modest quality premium to the 12.3x peer median is awarded as the third and fourth consecutive quarters of 2%+ ROA accumulate. Total return of roughly 14% including the 2.73% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — base case +11.1% plus a 2.73% dividend — but the risk/reward has compressed from the earlier reviews and the sector default in regional banks is negative alpha absent a distinct, unpriced catalyst. Existing holders should stay; new capital should wait for either evidence or a better price.

**Score trajectory.** Three prior BUYs at 7.2, 7.1 and 6.4 have worked: the shares are up roughly 20% from the early-May level and 16% from mid-June. Today's 6.3 continues the decline, and the reason is specific rather than reflexive. Fundamentals improved — Q2 beat by 14.8%, guidance was raised, consensus rose 6-7% — but the multiple expanded further, from roughly 10.3-11.2x current-year and 1.9-2.0x tangible book to 12.2x and 2.31x. That is a genuine change in the price-versus-value relationship, not price action mistaken for deterioration. The dimension declines relative to prior reviews are concentrated in valuation (asymmetry consumed) and risk factors (auto delinquency rising into declining coverage). Financial health, competitive position and management quality are unchanged from the June assessment.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 shows 30-89 day past dues flat or declining from $143.4M with allowance coverage held at 1.85% or above, confirming the seasonality explanation, alongside underlying margin expansion of 3bp or more excluding purchase-discount accretion.
2. A pullback to $25.00-26.00 — approximately 10.4-10.8x current-year consensus and 1.95-2.05x tangible book — on unchanged fundamentals.
3. Verified evidence that Puerto Rico commercial and construction loan demand is independent of federal disaster-recovery disbursements, which would extend the growth runway beyond the securities remix window.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Annualized net charge-offs above 0.85%, or 30-89 day past dues above roughly $175M, in either of the next two quarters.
2. Allowance coverage falling below 1.75% while early-stage delinquency continues rising — reserve release into a deteriorating pipeline.
3. Government deposits declining more than $400M in a single quarter with replacement funding sourced from brokered CDs or FHLB advances, compressing the spread.
4. A material Puerto Rico fiscal event or a significant hurricane landfall.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 earnings release, expected late October 2026. That print is the first clean test of whether the auto delinquency increase was seasonal and whether margin expansion sustains once purchase-discount acceleration normalizes.

## Conclusion

First BanCorp is a well-run, exceptionally well-capitalized bank executing a transparent and quantifiable margin trade in a concentrated deposit market that lets it fund a 6.11% asset yield at 2.07%. Q2 2026's 2.02% ROA, 19.49% ROE and 48.07% efficiency ratio place it in the top decile of U.S. banks, and the disclosed $1.1B of 2.41%-yielding securities cash flows provide roughly $0.17-0.18 per share of visible after-tax earnings uplift. Management raised margin guidance within five months of the 10-K, has beaten consensus four quarters running, uses well-chosen incentive metrics that cannot be gamed through reserve releases, and returns 84-95% of earnings to shareholders.

What has changed is the entry price. At $29.26 the shares sit 0.5% below the 52-week high, at 12.2x current-year and 11.6x next-year consensus versus a 12.3x peer median, 2.31x tangible book, and inside a $28-$33 analyst target range. On through-cycle earnings of $1.90-2.10 — supported by the 2022-2024 plateau at roughly $300M of net income — the multiple is closer to 14x. The remaining fundamental questions are unresolved in ways that matter: whether rising auto delinquency into declining reserve coverage is seasonal, whether Puerto Rico loan demand outlives federal reconstruction spending, and whether the 0.66% core deposit cost persists. Three consecutive buy calls have been rewarded; the fourth would require paying a quality premium the market has withheld for two years, on the same evidence that is already in consensus estimates.

HOLD. Base case $32.50, bull $35, bear $24. Add on a pullback to $25-26 or on Q3 evidence that consumer credit is stabilizing.
