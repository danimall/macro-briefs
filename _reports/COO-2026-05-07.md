## Executive Summary

The Cooper Companies has declined 24% over the past year to $61.21, reaching new 52-week lows despite measurably improving fundamentals. Q1 FY2026 showed 6.2% reported revenue growth (8% reported at CooperVision, 3% organic), gross margin recovery to 68%, operating margin expansion to 21%, and 37% operating cash flow growth. Five insiders — including the CEO — collectively purchased over $1.3M in stock at $80-84 in December 2025, all now 25-30% underwater. The Browning West activist cooperation agreement and board appointment of STERIS veteran Walter Rosebrough Jr. provide a governance catalyst for sustained operational improvement.

The stock trades at approximately 14x annualized Q1 FY2026 operating income and ~32.7x trailing (distorted by FY2025 restructuring charges). The consensus analyst mean target of $91.07 implies 49% upside, with the lowest target ($73) still approximately 19% above the current price. The primary risks are tariff exposure given manufacturing in Costa Rica, Hungary, and Puerto Rico — which already pressured Q1 gross margins by approximately 60bps — plus a £72M UK tax contingent liability. These are real but appear more than priced at current levels.

## Company Overview

Cooper Companies operates two segments: CooperVision (~67% of revenue), the third-largest global contact lens manufacturer, and CooperSurgical (~33%), a diversified fertility and women's health platform. The company has 15,000+ employees across 130+ countries.

CooperVision's competitive position centers on: (1) the broadest silicone hydrogel lens parameter range in the industry, (2) MiSight 1 day — the only FDA-approved contact lens for slowing myopia progression in children aged 8-12 — with recent Japanese MHLW approval expanding its addressable market, and (3) the MyDay premium daily disposable franchise gaining share against J&J and Alcon.

CooperSurgical operates in fertility consumables/services (IVF, donor gametes, genomic testing), Paragard (the only non-hormonal IUD in the US), and surgical devices. The segment underwent significant restructuring in FY2025 but is recovering.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2022 $3.31B → FY2023 $3.59B → FY2024 $3.90B → FY2025 $4.09B → Q1 FY2026 $1.02B (+6.2% reported YoY). The FY2025 deceleration to 5.1% reflected FX headwinds and Asia-Pacific softness. Q1 FY2026 total company organic growth was approximately 3%, below the company's full-year FY2026 organic guidance range of 4.5%-5.5%, though management expects improvement through the year.

**Margin recovery in progress:** FY2025 operating margin compressed to 16.7% (from 18.1%) due to CooperSurgical restructuring write-offs and severance. Q1 FY2026 jumped to 20.8% (vs. 18.9% prior year Q1), with CooperVision reaching 30% segment margins. Annualizing Q1's $213M operating income yields $850M+ versus the $684M reported for all of FY2025. Q1 non-GAAP gross margin of 68% was down 60bps year-over-year, with management attributing the decline to tariff impacts; excluding tariffs, gross margin would have been flat.

**Cash flow generation:** Operating cash flow of $796M in FY2025 (+12% YoY), accelerating to $261M in Q1 FY2026 alone (+37% YoY). FCF of $434M in FY2025 represents a 10.6% FCF margin, with Q1 run-rate suggesting improvement toward $500M+ annualized.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt ~$2.5B (D/E 0.50). The February 2026 refinancing extended $950M of term loan maturity to 2031, with $550M remaining due December 2026 — manageable against $1.37B revolver availability. Altman Z-Score of 2.73 is optically "grey zone" but distorted by low asset turnover from $5.4B goodwill/intangible load rather than balance sheet stress.

**Capital allocation:** $290M buybacks in FY2025 at $69.30 average, plus $92.5M in Q1 FY2026 at $82.04. $874M remains authorized. At $61, incremental repurchases are highly accretive relative to prior execution prices.

Revisions are modestly positive over 90 days (+2.5% current year) but flat over 30 days — stability rather than deterioration despite the stock decline. ## Valuation Assessment

At $61.21, Cooper trades at:
- ~32.7x trailing EPS ($1.87 on a GAAP basis distorted by FY2025 charges)
- ~13.2x forward FY2026 guidance midpoint EPS ($4.62)
- ~12.2x FY2027 estimated EPS ($5.02)
- ~14x annualized Q1 FY2026 operating income on a market-cap basis ($850M run-rate); higher on an enterprise value basis given ~$2.5B total debt
- ~27x trailing FCF ($434M FY2025)

The trailing P/E is misleading. FY2025 was a restructuring year with CooperSurgical operating income collapsing 63% on write-offs. The forward multiple of 13.2x is substantially below the peer median (29.2x for Align Technology) and below the company's historical 20-25x range. Even applying a conservative 18x multiple to $4.62 guidance midpoint EPS yields $83 — 36% above current levels.

The consensus analyst mean target is $91.07 (49% upside), with the lowest target at approximately $73 (19% upside). All 17 covering analysts have targets above the current price. With 11 of 17 at Buy/Strong Buy, this is a consensus bullish view with significant execution against price.

The 32% discount to 52-week highs requires explanation. The stock fell ~13% on August 28, 2025 following Q3 FY2025 results where organic revenue growth of 2% missed expectations, and has drifted lower since March 2026. The post-March decline appears driven by macro/tariff fears (manufacturing in Costa Rica, Hungary, Puerto Rico explicitly flagged in 10-K risk factors) and the UK tax ruling. Tariffs already compressed Q1 gross margins by approximately 60bps, and further escalation remains a legitimate concern.

## Competitive Landscape

**Contact lenses:** Cooper is #3 globally behind J&J Vision Care and Alcon, but management's FY2026 guidance implies organic growth at or modestly above the ~5% market rate. MiSight's position as the only FDA-approved contact lens for pediatric myopia progression management is a durable regulatory advantage — competitors would need multi-year clinical trials to replicate FDA approval — though other myopia-management modalities (orthokeratology, scleral lenses, atropine drops) provide alternative treatment pathways.

**Fertility:** CooperSurgical holds a leading position in fertility consumables with a uniquely integrated platform (gamete donation, genomic testing, consumables, cryostorage). Vitrolife competes in devices/media but lacks the services breadth.

**Share positioning:** CooperVision's Q1 FY2026 organic growth of 3% was below the estimated ~5% market rate, though this reflected particular softness in Asia Pacific (down 4% organically). Americas organic growth of 6% suggests continued share gains in the company's largest region. The toric/multifocal segment (~6% organic growth) is structurally advantaged as the aging population requires more complex lens prescriptions.

**Competitive risks:** J&J's resources dwarf Cooper's, and Bausch + Lomb is reportedly evaluating strategic alternatives that could result in a more aggressive combined entity. The Miudella IUD (FDA-approved but not yet marketed) will compete directly with Paragard when launched.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff exposure (HIGH):** Manufacturing facilities in Costa Rica, Hungary, Puerto Rico, UK, and Belgium create direct exposure to current and potential trade barriers. The 10-K explicitly escalated tariff language in risk factors. Further tariff escalation could compound the existing gross margin headwind. In the current environment with broader protectionist sentiment, this is the primary overhang.

**UK tax litigation (MODERATE):** The March 2026 FTT ruling against Cooper on Sauflon acquisition payroll withholding creates £0-71.7M exposure plus interest (~$90M potential). Company is appealing but the outcome is uncertain.

**ERP implementation (MODERATE):** Multi-year system transition across both segments. The material weakness has been remediated, but ERP implementations remain operationally disruptive through completion.

**Customer consolidation (MODERATE):** Optical chains and fertility clinic networks are consolidating, increasing buyer power and creating pricing pressure.

**CooperSurgical execution (MODERATE):** The restructuring is showing early results (8% operating margin, +33% YoY growth), but sustained margin recovery to mid-teens operating margin needs multiple quarters of confirmation. The segment also faces ongoing competitive, regulatory, customer-consolidation, and Paragard/Miudella risks.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85-95, +39-55%):** CooperSurgical margins normalize to 12-14% operating margin over FY2026-27 while CooperVision organic growth accelerates toward the 4.5%-5.5% guided range and potentially beyond. Browning West-driven operational improvement yields further margin expansion and disciplined capital allocation. Tariff fears prove manageable (manufacturing mix shifts or carve-outs apply to medical devices). Multiple re-rates from 13x to 18-20x forward earnings. MiSight Japan launch contributes meaningful incremental revenue by FY2027.

**Bear Case ($50-55, -15-18%):** Tariffs materially impact gross margins (200-300bps compression) without ability to pass through given competitive pressure from J&J/Alcon. CooperSurgical restructuring stalls. UK tax ruling upheld with full £72M payment. Multiple quarters of below-market organic growth suggest market share loss.

## Conclusion

Cooper Companies presents a compelling risk/reward at $61.21. The stock trades at its cheapest forward multiple in years on tariff fears and post-restructuring noise, while underlying operations show early signs of recovery: margins expanding, cash flow growing, and activist-driven governance improvements underway. Five insiders bought stock at $80-84 six months ago. The 2.3:1 upside/downside ratio with specific catalysts over a 12-18 month horizon meets the bar for a BUY recommendation.

The prior declining score trajectory (6.2 → 5.6) reflected legitimate concerns about CooperSurgical's FY2025 collapse and the governance vacuum. Both have been partially addressed: Q1 FY2026 shows CooperSurgical recovery, and Browning West provides external accountability. The price decline from $84 to $61 since the prior report, on stable-to-rising estimates, represents improving risk/reward rather than thesis deterioration.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Q1 FY2026 CooperVision organic growth was 3% (not 8%), weakening the share-gain acceleration component of the thesis; the investment case rests more heavily on margin recovery, FCF generation, buybacks, and the activist governance catalyst than on confirmed above-market organic growth.
