**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Applied Digital is a Dallas-based data-center developer transitioning from crypto-mining hosting into a build-to-suit AI/HPC "power landlord" model. The story is straightforward to state and difficult to underwrite: the company has assembled a contracted lease portfolio it values at roughly $36B (minimum lease payments of ~$15.5B on the balance sheet), anchored by CoreWeave at its Ellendale campus and an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler at Harwood, and it is racing to build the physical capacity that converts those commitments into cash flow. Revenue rose 139% YoY to $126.6M in Q3 FY26, and management targets more than $1B of NOI within five years.

The problem is the capital structure sitting between that backlog and the common shareholder. In roughly nine months APLD raised ~$3.6B, stacking 2030 Notes at 9.25%, 2031 Notes at 6.75% (held in escrow pending a power agreement), $450M convertibles, and a ~$5B Macquarie preferred structure whose NCI carries a 12.75% PIK dividend escalating to 16.75%, a 1.80x minimum multiple-of-invested-capital liquidation preference, and redemption rights beginning April 2028. Common holders are structurally subordinated to expensive project debt and to this preferred. The business burns cash (9M FY26 operating cash flow −$42.9M; investing −$1.58B), carries 100% revenue concentration in three customers (56% CoreWeave in Q3), and dilutes steadily (share count rose from ~225M to ~285M in nine months).

At ~$30 versus a 52-week range of $9.79–$50.73 and analyst targets clustered well above spot ($48 low, $80.5 median), APLD is priced as a call option on flawless execution. The bull case is real: the CoreWeave A3 SPV restructuring is a genuine credit enhancement, and the 2031 Notes pricing 250bps below the 2030 Notes shows the cost-of-capital improvement the bull thesis needs. But the setup is binary, offers no margin of safety, and lacks the single most reliable bullish insider signal (open-market purchases). We rate the shares HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the optionality is legitimate, but the risk/reward does not clear the bar for a BUY given the subordination, cash burn, and execution dependency.

## Company Overview

Applied Digital (founded 2021, fiscal year ending May 31) designs, builds, and operates high-performance data centers. It runs three segments:

- **HPC Hosting** — the strategic core. Purpose-built, direct-to-chip liquid-cooled ("waterless cooling") facilities leased to hyperscalers and neoclouds under long-term (15–30 year) triple-net operating leases (ASC 842). Flagship assets: Polaris Forge 1 (Ellendale, ND, 400 MW, CoreWeave tenant), Polaris Forge 2 (Harwood, ND, 200 MW, unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler), and Delta Forge 1 (southern U.S., up to 300 MW critical IT load, recently broke ground, initial ops targeted mid-2027).
- **Data Center Hosting** — the cash cow. 286 MW of crypto/Bitcoin mining hosting (Jamestown 106 MW, Ellendale 180 MW) running at full capacity. Highest ROA in the company (~$13.9M segment operating profit in Q3 FY26).
- **Cloud Services** — being divested via a reverse merger of the GPU-cloud business into Ekso Bionics (renaming to ChronoScale/CHRN), with APLD retaining ~97% initially; expected to close Q4 FY26.

The differentiation is early-mover build-to-suit AI infrastructure sited in low-cost power regions (the Dakotas), with a 100 MW liquid-cooled facility already online. The demand backdrop is favorable: management cites hyperscaler capex accelerating from ~$400B toward ~$700B annually, which is consistent with the broad, multi-source confirmation of data-center load growth visible across the utility, power-equipment, and hyperscaler-capex complex. APLD's role is that of the physical landlord; CoreWeave is a tenant, not a subsidiary, and Macquarie is a preferred-equity provider, not an operator.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is inflecting sharply.** HPC contributed $71.0M of Q3 total revenue ($44.1M base rent, $18.9M tenant fit-out, $8.1M power pass-through). Nine-month FY26 revenue reached $352.6M, up 101% YoY. Segment operating profit for 9M FY26: Data Center Hosting +$35.9M, HPC +$12.9M, Cloud −$24.9M.

**GAAP losses remain large, and the adjusted framing requires skepticism.** Q3 FY26 net loss to common was −$100.9M (−$0.36/share), driven by a $59.7M non-cash Cloud write-down (recognized when the business was reclassified out of held-for-sale) and $48.9M of stock-based compensation. Management's non-GAAP framing excludes Cloud entirely and reports Adjusted EBITDA of $44.1M (41% adjusted margin) and Adjusted net income of $33.2M ($0.09/share). The gap between GAAP and adjusted is enormous, dominated by heavy SBC and one-timers; the adjusted profitability narrative rests on HPC plus crypto hosting only, and the decision to exclude Cloud is itself a signal that management views it as low-quality.

**The balance sheet was transformed by a financing blitz.** Cash and equivalents jumped to $1.73B from $44M at May 2025 (total cash including restricted ~$2.1B). Total assets reached $6.25B from $1.87B in ~9 months. Property & equipment net was $3.0B, with $1.5B in construction-in-progress. Total long-term debt was $2.59B, plus $923M of redeemable noncontrolling interest (Macquarie) in temporary equity. Stockholders' equity was $1.58B against a $583M accumulated deficit.

**Cash flow is a capital-consumption profile, as expected for a mid-buildout developer.** 9M FY26 operating cash flow was −$42.9M (improved from −$122M), investing was −$1.58B (capex, versus −$466M prior year), and financing was +$3.62B ($2.5B debt, $900M Macquarie NCI, ~$740M preferred, ~$196M ATM equity). The Altman Z-Score screens in the distress zone, but that metric is not the right lens for a pre-stabilization infrastructure developer with contracted long-dated leases. The relevant question is whether the ~$15.5B of contracted minimum lease payments converts to stabilized, refinanceable, cash-flowing assets before financing costs and dilution erode equity value.

The earnings track record reinforces limited visibility: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters, with the most recent reported quarter missing consensus by roughly 210% (−$0.40 actual vs −$0.13 estimate). This is a company whose reported earnings are difficult to forecast because they are dominated by non-cash and timing items.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line growth is among the fastest in the sector: +139% YoY in Q3, +101% for the nine months, with the HPC segment ramping from a standing start. The backlog is the real growth asset — the ~$36B total contracted lease value grew with a June 2026 15-year, 210 MW hyperscaler lease (~$5.2B), and management claims ~1 GW of additional grid power in negotiation. The five-year target of >$1B NOI, if achieved, would represent a step-change in cash generation relative to today's cash-burning profile.

The thematic tailwind has multiple independent confirmations. Hyperscaler capex is accelerating; regulated-utility data on data-center load is corroborating the demand at the grid level; power-equipment and structural-materials suppliers are all reporting data-center-driven order growth. This is a broad, structural demand shift rather than a single-company story, which raises the durability of the top-line thesis relative to a narrower narrative.

The caution on growth is that the estimate-revision trend is negative. Next-quarter EPS was revised −34.5% over 30 days and −44.1% over 90 days; next-year EPS moved to −$1.23 from −$0.90 (−37.4% over 90 days). This is the opposite of the +30% positive-revision inflection signal that would strengthen a BUY case. The revenue is growing fast, but consensus earnings are deteriorating as the market prices in escalating financing costs and dilution. Growth in revenue is not translating to growth in per-share value on the current trajectory, which is why the growth score, while high, is tempered.

## Valuation Assessment

APLD has no meaningful earnings multiple (trailing and forward P/E are negative), so valuation must be framed on the backlog-to-NOI conversion and on peer/asset-based logic. Market cap is ~$8.6B at ~$30. Enterprise value is materially higher once ~$2.7B of debt and the ~$923M Macquarie NCI are added, and the effective claim ahead of common is larger still given the 1.8x MOIC preference on the Macquarie structure.

The bull anchor is the >$1B NOI five-year target: at a data-center/infrastructure NOI multiple, $1B of stabilized NOI could support an enterprise value several multiples of today's, which is the basis for the analyst target cluster of $48 low / $80.5 median / $106 high — all well above spot. Under the standing principle that analyst targets lag reality on genuine inflection stocks, these could be read as floors. But that principle applies most cleanly to cheap-looking stocks with positive estimate revisions. Here the revisions are falling, the earnings are negative, and the targets embed flawless multi-year execution rather than an observable near-term mispricing. The targets are more accurately read as scenario values contingent on the buildout completing on schedule and the debt refinancing lower.

The offsetting reality for equity holders is subordination. Even if the assets stabilize and NOI hits target, the Macquarie preferred (1.8x MOIC preference, redemption clock from April 2028) and the project debt absorb the first and most senior slices of value creation. The common is a residual claim on a levered asset base, which means the equity is worth far less than a naive NOI-multiple-times-EV calculation suggests until the capital stack is simplified and refinanced cheaper.

We do not treat the "cheapness" of a large backlog relative to market cap as a buy signal; that framing has historically been a value trap when it ignores the seniority of claims and execution risk sitting between the asset and the shareholder. We score valuation 4.5: not stretched in the sense of an overpriced earnings multiple, but offering no margin of safety given subordination, dilution, and the binary nature of the outcome. There is genuine upside optionality embedded in the price, and also a plausible path to substantial impairment if the buildout slips or capital markets tighten.

## Competitive Landscape

APLD's competitive position is improving on trajectory. It was an early mover in operational liquid-cooled AI capacity, it has secured low-cost power in the Dakotas, and it has demonstrated it can win large, long-dated hyperscaler leases (the June 2026 $5.2B/210 MW deal and the Harwood investment-grade tenant). The CoreWeave A3 SPV restructuring materially improved the credit quality of its anchor cash flows and supports the 2030 Notes.

The moat is real but not deep. APLD competes against neocloud and crypto-miner-turned-AI-host peers (IREN, TeraWulf, Core Scientific, Cipher), and the binding constraint across the sector is power and construction execution, not demand. APLD's advantage is speed-to-market and site selection, but hyperscalers hold negotiating leverage and can multi-source. The 100% revenue concentration in three customers — and 56% in CoreWeave, itself a BB-rated entity ultimately dependent on its own hyperscaler and AI-lab customer demand — means the competitive "position" is really a bet on a handful of counterparties honoring long leases. The improving cost of capital (2031 Notes 250bps inside the 2030 Notes) is the clearest evidence the market is beginning to underwrite APLD as a lower-risk landlord, and that repricing is the mechanism by which competitive standing translates into shareholder value.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific, quantified, and largely not yet resolved; this is a low risk-factor score, not a sector-truism penalty.

1. **Customer concentration.** Customer A (CoreWeave) = 56% of Q3 revenue; A+B+C = 100%. The A3 SPV restructuring with parent springing guarantees and a $50M letter of credit is a genuine mitigant, but ultimate demand still traces to CoreWeave's own customer base.

2. **Senior, expensive capital ahead of common.** 2030 Notes at 9.25%, Macquarie preferred at 12.75%→16.75% PIK with a 1.8x MOIC liquidation preference and redemption from April 2028. Common is subordinated to all of it.

3. **Escrow/power risk.** The 2031 Notes' escrow release hinges on executing an electric-service agreement, with special mandatory redemption if unsatisfied by 6/30/2026. Power procurement is the binding constraint on the whole model.

4. **Completion guarantees and multi-campus execution.** APLD must fund shortfalls to hit lease commencement dates across Polaris Forge 1, 2, and Delta Forge 1 simultaneously. Delays cascade into financing and lease consequences.

5. **Governance flags.** The B&W/Base Electron guarantee (up to $50–100M termination fee on a 1.2 GW MISO power project) is an off-balance-sheet contingency tied to a related party partly owned by insiders. A securities class action (McConnell v. APLD) alleges overstated Data Center Hosting profitability and cloud-transition prospects; motion to dismiss pending.

6. **Dilution.** Share count rose from ~225M to ~285M in nine months via ATM, ~49M Series G preferred conversions, and large grants; 28M warrants and ~46M convertible-linked shares remain. The Series G commitment ballooned to $1.59B.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 110.8% versus 103.9% one-year historical vol (rich by ~6.9pp), with the term structure in backwardation (110.8% near-term vs 99.4% long-term), consistent with the market pricing an imminent catalyst or continued turbulence. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.4% (elevated put demand), and the six-month tenor shows a −9.9% put skew, consistent with hedging against downside. IV compressed from 117.6% to 110.8% since early June even as the price fell 32.6%, and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.51 to 0.72. The signal reinforces the fundamental read: this is a high-uncertainty, event-driven equity where the market is paying up for downside protection. For a BUY thesis this would be a meaningful divergence to flag; for our HOLD it corroborates the caution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
APLD is an early-mover AI power landlord with improving tenant credit (CoreWeave A3 SPV). Revenue is growing >100% YoY, the cost of debt is falling (2031 Notes 250bps inside the 2030 Notes), and the demand backdrop is structural and broadly confirmed. As the three campuses reach stabilization and the 9.25% 2030 Notes are refinanced cheaper on the strength of investment-grade tenant cash flows, per-share value re-rates sharply toward the $1B NOI target. Analyst targets of $80.5 median / $106 high reflect this path. Bull target: **$70–80** (roughly 130–165% upside), contingent on on-time completion and successful refinancing.

### Bear Case
Construction slips, the electric-service agreement or escrow condition fails, or capital markets tighten, forcing further dilution and preferred accretion. The Macquarie preferred's 1.8x MOIC and PIK escalation compound ahead of common, and the securities litigation and related-party power guarantee crystallize. CoreWeave's own demand softens. In this scenario the equity, sitting behind ~$2.7B of debt and expensive senior preferred, is impaired toward the low end of its 52-week range. Bear target: **$12–15** (roughly 50–60% downside).

### Base Case
The buildout proceeds broadly on plan with the usual timing frictions, revenue continues to ramp, and the cost of capital gradually improves, but dilution and financing costs keep per-share value creation slower than the backlog headline implies. The stock trades on execution milestones and hyperscaler-capex sentiment. Base target: **$32–36**, modestly above current levels, reflecting balanced but wide-tailed outcomes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the optionality is real but the risk/reward does not clear the BUY bar given subordination, cash burn, and negative estimate revisions.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Completion and lease commencement of Polaris Forge 1/2 on schedule with no completion-guarantee draws, confirming the backlog-to-cash-flow conversion.
- A refinancing of the 9.25% 2030 Notes at materially lower cost, evidencing the market underwriting APLD as a stabilized landlord.
- A turn in EPS estimate revisions from negative to positive, alongside a simplification of the capital stack (reduced reliance on the Macquarie preferred).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Failure to satisfy the 2031 Notes escrow/power condition, triggering special mandatory redemption.
- Any construction delay that draws on completion guarantees or delays lease commencement, or a material adverse development in the CoreWeave relationship.
- A large, punitive equity raise (further diluting common ahead of the Macquarie preferred) or an adverse ruling on the securities class action.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly earnings release (the FY26 fourth-quarter/year-end report, expected in the second half of 2026) and the ChronoScale/Ekso spin close are the near-term checkpoints, with the power-agreement/escrow deadline (6/30/2026) and construction milestones the key event risks over the next two to three quarters.

## Conclusion

Applied Digital is a legitimate participant in a structural, broadly confirmed demand shift, with a large contracted backlog and an anchor tenant whose credit has genuinely improved. It is also a cash-burning, heavily leveraged developer whose common shareholders sit behind ~$2.7B of high-cost debt and a Macquarie preferred structure engineered to extract 1.8x its capital before common participates. The equity is a call option on flawless multi-campus execution and a step-down in cost of capital.

Three factors keep this from being a BUY: the absence of a margin of safety, the deteriorating EPS estimate trend that contradicts the standard inflection buy signal, and the lack of any open-market insider purchases — executives are monetizing vested equity into strength while the CEO's incentive is a $122M market-priced PSU grant tied to share price rather than capital discipline. Three factors keep this from being an AVOID: the ~$36B backlog with improving tenant credit, the falling cost of debt evidencing a real value-creation mechanism, and the durable, multi-source demand backdrop. The honest characterization is uncertain: a wide distribution of outcomes with legitimate optionality on both tails. HOLD.
