## Executive Summary

TTM Technologies has transformed from a commoditized PCB manufacturer to a high-growth AI infrastructure and defense technology company. Revenue grew 30.4% YoY in Q1 2026 to $846M, driven by explosive data center/networking demand (now 36% of sales, up from 14% three years ago) and sustained aerospace & defense spending. The company is investing $300-320M capex in FY2026 to build ultra-HDI manufacturing capacity in Syracuse, NY and expand in Malaysia and Wisconsin.

The fundamental story is compelling: structural competitive advantages from security-cleared domestic manufacturing, rising margins (21.4% gross), and a CEO targeting a doubling of earnings over two years. However, the stock has appreciated 555% over the past year to $164.64, pricing in substantial future growth at ~90x trailing P/E. Free cash flow is negative as capex consumes all operating cash flow, customer concentration is intensifying rapidly (top 2 at 26% of Q1 revenue), and every named executive has adopted 10b5-1 selling plans. At ~46x consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.56, the valuation demands multi-year hypergrowth execution without interruption.

The stock warrants holding for existing positions given the genuine AI infrastructure tailwind and defense moat, but new capital faces unfavorable asymmetry at current levels. A pullback to $120-130 (30-35x FY2026E) would offer a more attractive entry.

## Company Overview

TTM Technologies operates 24 specialized manufacturing facilities across North America and Asia, employing approximately 18,200 workers to produce advanced printed circuit boards (PCBs), IC substrates, RF components, and complete mission systems. The company serves ~1,300 customers across two primary segments:

**Aerospace & Defense (A&D):** 13 domestic facilities producing PCBs, assemblies, and mission systems including the U.S. Navy's AN/APS-153 multi-mode radar. Q1 2026 revenue: $352M (40% of total). The company operates under a Special Board Resolution (SBR) with DCSA, requiring all board members to hold U.S. citizenship, creating meaningful barriers to entry.

**Commercial:** 9 fabrication plants (3 domestic, 4 China, 1 Malaysia, 1 Canada) serving data center/networking (AI), automotive, and medical/industrial markets. Q1 2026 revenue: $495M (60% of total).

The company joined the S&P MidCap 400 in January 2026 and underwent a CEO transition in September 2025, with Edwin Roks replacing the retired Thomas Edman.

- Gross margin improved sequentially: 18.5% (FY23) → 19.5% (FY24) → 20.7% (FY25) → 21.4% (Q1 2026)

**Cash Flow Problem:**
- FY2025 operating cash flow: $292M. Capex: $293M. FCF: ~$0.
- Q1 2026 operating cash flow: $22M. Capex: $107M. FCF: **negative $85M.**
- FY2026 capex guidance raised to $300-320M.

The company is consuming all cash generation to fund capacity expansion. With $410M cash and $190M undrawn revolver (~$600M total liquidity), this is manageable for 12-18 months if revenue growth continues, but leaves no margin for error.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $923M (4.00% Senior Notes $500M due 2029; Term Loan $341M due 2030 at ~5.9% variable; Asia ABL $80M)

- Current ratio: 1.88

The Z-Score in the grey zone reflects elevated leverage for a cyclical manufacturer, though debt is manageable at current growth rates. The 4.00% senior notes provide stability; the variable-rate term loan ($250M hedged at 3.49% through April 2027) presents modest refinancing risk.

## Growth Analysis

TTM's growth is driven by three converging tailwinds:

**1. AI Data Center Infrastructure (Primary Driver):** Data center/networking grew from 14% of revenue to 36% in three years. All major hyperscalers require advanced HDI/ultra-HDI PCBs for AI server racks, networking switches, and optical interconnects. Q2 2026 guidance of $930-970M (midpoint 14.9% above consensus) confirms acceleration continues.

**2. Syracuse Ultra-HDI Facility (H2 2026 Catalyst):** Volume production commencing H2 2026 positions TTM as potentially the sole domestic ultra-HDI manufacturer for classified defense applications. Construction is complete, equipment is arriving.

**3. Defense Modernization:** A&D revenue of $352M in Q1 2026 provides stable high-margin baseload. Navy platform sustainment (AN/APS-153 sole source) and NATO spending commitments provide multi-year visibility independent of AI cycles.

**Estimate Revisions:**
- Current year EPS: +19.1% over 90 days ($3.02 → $3.56)
- Next year EPS: +20.4% over 90 days ($4.18 → $5.03)
- Revisions are accelerating (30-day moves of +16.3% and +12.1% respectively)

CEO Roks has guided for 15-20% revenue CAGR over three years and targeted doubling EPS to ~$5 within two years. Current consensus for FY2027 ($5.03) essentially validates this target.

At $164.64, TTM trades at:
- Trailing P/E: ~90x (on $1.68 TTM EPS per Phase 1 data)
- Forward P/E (FY2026): ~46x (on $3.56 consensus)
- Forward P/E (FY2027): ~33x (on $5.03 consensus)
- EV/Revenue (FY2025): ~6.0x
- FCF Yield: Negative (no free cash flow currently)

**Peer Comparison:**
- SANM: 44.2x P/E
- FN (Fabrinet): 61.7x P/E
- PLXS: 39.0x P/E
- Peer median: 61.7x

At 46x FY2026E, TTM trades at a premium to EMS peers but is growing materially faster. The premium is partially justified by 30%+ revenue growth vs. peer median growth of 10-15%. However, the absolute level demands sustained execution.

**PEG Assessment:** FY2026-to-FY2027 EPS growth of 41% ($3.56 → $5.03) yields a forward PEG of ~1.1x on FY2026E P/E — reasonable if growth materializes. The key question is whether $5 EPS represents a waypoint to higher earnings or a temporary peak before AI capex cyclicality reasserts.

**Valuation Risk:** The stock is priced for a growth trajectory where AI capex continues uninterrupted, Syracuse ramps on schedule, and margins continue expanding. Any interruption — a single quarter of hyperscaler capex slowdown, a Syracuse delay, or tariff escalation on China PCB operations — could trigger derating from 46x to 30-35x, representing 25-35% downside.

Analyst consensus target of $170 (mean) provides only 3.3% upside from current levels, and the low target of $160 is below the current price. The stock has essentially reached its consensus ceiling.

## Competitive Landscape

TTM's competitive position is strong and improving:

**Structural Moats:**
1. Security-cleared domestic manufacturing at scale — extremely rare in PCBs
2. Ultra-HDI technology leadership (Syracuse facility positions for next-gen defense/AI)
3. Sole-source positions on Navy platforms (AN/APS-153)
4. Breadth from simple PCBs through complete mission systems
5. Multi-decade relationships with top 10 customers

**Market Position:** TTM is one of the largest North American PCB manufacturers in a highly fragmented global market dominated by Asian commodity producers. The technology intensity of AI and defense applications creates natural barriers that commodity PCB makers cannot easily overcome.

**Risks to Position:**
- Chinese/Taiwanese manufacturers (Unimicron, Kinwong) are moving upmarket
- AT&S (Austria) is building advanced substrate capacity
- Customer concentration implies dependency, not just partnership strength

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks (Not Fully Priced):**

1. **Customer Concentration Spike:** Top 2 customers at 26% of Q1 2026 revenue (from 23% in FY2025). If one major hyperscaler shifts to alternative suppliers or delays orders, the impact is disproportionate. At 90x trailing earnings, even a 5% revenue miss would produce a severe derating.

2. **Free Cash Flow Hole During Expansion:** Capex of $300-320M against $292M FY2025 OCF means no shareholder returns and thin liquidity cushion. If revenue growth stalls, the company would face simultaneous margin pressure and cash consumption.

3. **AI Capex Cyclicality:** Data center/networking concentration at 36% of revenue creates vulnerability to any moderation in hyperscaler spending. Historical precedent: TTM took $32.6M goodwill impairment in FY2024 and $44.1M in FY2023 during prior downturns.

4. **China Operations (50% of Revenue, 54% of Employees):** Four fabrication plants in China exposed to tariff escalation, FOCI restrictions, and potential supply chain decoupling. $156M cash held in foreign subsidiaries.

5. **Execution Risk on Multiple Simultaneous Expansions:** Syracuse, Eau Claire, Penang, and potential Europe — all happening concurrently under a CEO with <1 year tenure.

## Conclusion

TTM Technologies presents a genuine structural growth story underpinned by AI infrastructure demand and an irreplaceable domestic defense manufacturing position. The financial trajectory — 30% revenue growth, expanding margins, and a clear path to $5+ EPS — is among the strongest in the EMS sector.

The problem is price. At $164.64 and ~46x FY2026 consensus, the stock has already captured much of the visible growth narrative. Free cash flow is negative, customer concentration is rising to uncomfortable levels, every executive is setting up selling plans, and the options market is pricing 86% implied volatility — indicating sophisticated participants see material two-way risk. Analyst targets cluster at $170, providing minimal upside from current levels.

This is a high-quality business priced for continued perfection. Existing holders should maintain positions given the structural tailwinds, but new capital faces unfavorable risk/reward. The stock needs either a meaningful pullback or a significant guidance raise beyond current consensus to justify entry at these levels.
