## Executive Summary

Cable One is a rural/suburban broadband provider whose investment thesis has fundamentally broken over the past two years. The company is losing residential data subscribers at 5.8% annually, revenue has declined for four consecutive years (from $1.71B in 2022 to $1.50B in 2025), and the balance sheet carries ~$2.6B in debt against a $580M market cap. An Altman Z-Score of 0.62 places CABO deep in the distress zone.

The near-term financial picture is dominated by two unavoidable obligations: a $575M convertible note maturity (March 2026) that must be settled in cash given the conversion price of $2,275 vs. the ~$102 stock price, and a mandatory MBI acquisition (~$480M in cash plus ~$870M in assumed debt) closing around October 2026. Post-MBI, pro forma debt could exceed $4 billion against combined EBITDA of roughly $950M, implying 4x+ leverage on a declining revenue base.

The stock has fallen 63% from its 52-week high and 94%+ from its peak. While a ~4.0x EV/EBITDA multiple appears optically cheap for a broadband business, the numerator (enterprise value) will likely increase as debt grows from the MBI acquisition, while the denominator (EBITDA) is declining. This is a value trap.

## Company Overview

Cable One operates under the Sparklight brand, providing broadband internet, video, and voice services to approximately 1.0 million residential and business customers across 2.9 million passings in 24 states. The company concentrates on secondary and tertiary markets, with 75% of customers in Arizona, Idaho, Mississippi, Missouri, Oklahoma, South Carolina, and Texas.

Revenue composition reflects a broadband-centric strategy: residential data (60.1%), business data (15.3%), residential video (12.5%), and other services (12.1%). The company has deliberately de-emphasized video, letting that segment decline naturally while focusing on higher-margin data services. This strategy worked when the company was the dominant or sole broadband provider in its markets. It is failing now because approximately 60% of CABO's footprint has been overbuilt by wired competitors offering 100+ Mbps speeds, and 5G fixed wireless access (FWA) from T-Mobile and Verizon is taking share at price points of $25-50/month.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory: Four Consecutive Years of Decline

| Year | Revenue | YoY Change |
|------|---------|------------|
| 2022 | $1,706M | — |
| 2023 | $1,678M | -1.6% |
| 2024 | $1,580M | -5.8% |
| 2025 | $1,501M | -5.0% |

Revenue declined 12.0% cumulatively over three years. The decline accelerated from 2022 to 2023 (-1.6%) to 2023-2024 (-5.8%), and while it modestly improved in 2025 (-5.0%), the underlying subscriber trends suggest further deterioration.

### Subscriber Losses Are Accelerating

Residential data PSUs declined 5.8% YoY (from 955K to 900K), a loss of 55,300 subscribers in 2025. Total PSUs declined 7.3%. Business data was the only segment showing resilience (-0.8%). Residential data ARPU was flat at $80.84 (+0.6%), meaning the company cannot offset volume losses through pricing.

### Profitability Collapse

Operating income swung from positive $442M in 2024 to negative $207M in 2025, driven by $586M in non-cash impairment charges ($497M franchise agreements, $89M goodwill). Even excluding impairments, operating income declined. Net income was -$356M (EPS -$63.21).

Adjusted EBITDA remains positive at $802M (53.5% margin), down from $854M in 2024 and $917M in 2023. EBITDA is declining at roughly 6-7% annually, tracking subscriber losses.

### Cash Flow: The Sole Remaining Positive

Operating cash flow was $563M. Free cash flow was $278M (18.5% FCF margin) after $285M in capex. The dividend was suspended in Q2 2025, saving ~$67M annually. This cash generation is what keeps the company solvent, but it is insufficient to service the upcoming debt obligations without drawing heavily on the revolver.

### Balance Sheet: Distress-Level Metrics

- **Total debt: ~$2.6B** (down from $3.57B in 2024 due to partial repayments)
- **Cash: $153M**
- **Current ratio: 0.4** ($307M current assets vs. $759M current liabilities)
- **Working capital deficit: -$452M**
- **Goodwill + intangibles: $2.82B** (50.4% of total assets, post-impairment)
- **Debt-to-equity: 2.9x**
- **Altman Z-Score: 0.62** (deep distress zone; safe threshold is 2.99)

The current liabilities spike reflects reclassification of the $575M convertible notes maturing March 2026. These 0% coupon notes have a conversion price of $2,275.83 — with the stock at $102, they will be settled entirely in cash. A March 16, 2026 8-K filing under Item 2.03 (creation of a direct financial obligation) likely relates to the revolver draw needed to fund this redemption.

The stacking of the convertible note redemption in March 2026, MBI acquisition in October 2026, and Term Loan B-4 maturity in 2028 creates a three-year wall that requires either refinancing at higher rates, asset sales, or equity issuance — all of which are dilutive or unavailable at current credit metrics.

### The MBI Acquisition: Forced Leverage Increase

Following the January 2, 2026 exercise of a put option, Cable One must acquire the remaining MBI equity for ~$480M plus assume ~$845-895M in MBI term loans (due November 2027). MBI itself is losing data subscribers at 6.6% annually and saw revenue decline 3.3% in 2025.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story. Every major revenue line except business data is declining. Management's strategic initiatives include:

1. **DOCSIS 4.0 deployment** — maintains speed parity with fiber but does not create a competitive advantage, since fiber overbuilders are already offering comparable or superior speeds.
2. **Mobile MVNO pilot** — launched Q4 2025 in several markets, following the playbook of Charter and Comcast. This could reduce churn modestly by bundling mobile with broadband, but CABO is years behind larger operators and lacks the scale for favorable MVNO economics.
3. **BEAD/RDOF participation** — CABO can bid for government broadband subsidies, but these same programs are also funding competitors to build in CABO's existing territory.

Estimate revisions show a single analyst projecting $41.46 in FY2026 EPS (up +19.3% from ninety days ago), but this is a single data point and must be viewed skeptically given CABO's 1-for-4 quarterly beat record over the past year, with three misses averaging -86% surprise magnitude.

The Q4 2025 earnings miss (-$1.35 actual vs. $6.78 estimate, -119.9% surprise) was the most recent quarter and the worst miss in the set. While impairment charges inflated the miss, even adjusted results disappointed.

## Valuation Assessment

At $102 per share and 5.67M shares outstanding, CABO's equity market cap is $580M. Adding ~$2.6B in net debt, enterprise value is approximately $3.2B.

| Metric | CABO | Cable/Broadband Peer Range |
|--------|------|---------------------------|
| EV/EBITDA | ~4.0x | 6-8x (healthy operators) |
| P/E (trailing) | Negative (net loss) | 12-20x |
| Forward P/E | N/M (single analyst) | 12-18x |
| FCF yield (equity) | ~48% ($278M / $580M) | 5-10% |

The 4.0x EV/EBITDA multiple is cheap relative to healthy broadband peers, and the 48% FCF yield to equity looks extraordinary. But these metrics are misleading for three reasons:

1. **The denominator is shrinking.** EBITDA has declined from $917M to $802M in two years (~6-7% annual decline). At this trajectory, 2027 EBITDA could be ~$700M, which at 4x would imply $2.8B EV — meaning the equity could be worth less than the current $580M if debt increases via MBI.

2. **FCF does not belong to equity holders.** With $2.6B in debt (growing to $4B+ post-MBI), virtually all cash flow is dedicated to debt service and mandatory repayments. Equity holders are last in line.

3. **The MBI acquisition mechanically increases EV without proportionally increasing EBITDA.** Adding ~$1.3B in obligations for a business declining at 6.6% subscriber rates dilutes value per share.

The consensus analyst target range of $80-$142 (median $112.50) reflects deep uncertainty. Zero analysts rate CABO as Buy or Strong Buy; four are Hold, one Sell, one Strong Sell. This distribution is among the most negative for a mid-cap company.

## Competitive Landscape

Cable One's competitive position is deteriorating on multiple fronts:

**Fiber overbuilding:** 60% of CABO's footprint is now overbuilt by competitors offering 100+ Mbps. Municipal networks and cooperatives continue expanding, often subsidized by federal and state programs. CABO's DOCSIS 4.0 upgrade maintains speed parity but does not create differentiation.

**Fixed wireless access:** T-Mobile and Verizon FWA offerings at $25-50/month are taking share in precisely the lower-density markets CABO serves. T-Mobile has added 6M+ FWA subscribers nationally since 2022, and rural markets are a primary growth vector for carriers looking to monetize mid-band spectrum.

**Government-subsidized competition:** The BEAD program is directing billions to broadband deployment in areas where CABO is the incumbent. While CABO can bid for BEAD grants, many awards will go to fiber-based competitors, creating new entrants in what were previously quasi-monopoly markets.

**Scale disadvantage:** Larger cable operators (Charter at ~32M passings, Comcast at ~62M passings) can amortize technology investments, programming costs, and MVNO agreements over a vastly larger subscriber base. CABO's 2.9M passings provide minimal negotiating leverage.

Average data usage of 835 GB/month and sub-20% peak network utilization demonstrate the physical network's quality. But network capacity is necessary, not sufficient, for subscriber retention when competitors offer comparable speeds at lower prices.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Near-Term Risks

1. **Convertible note maturity (March 2026):** Likely funded via revolver draw, consuming nearly half the $1.25B facility. The March 16 8-K filing suggests this is already done.

2. **MBI acquisition close (October 2026):** Post-close, revolver availability could be minimal, leaving the company with limited financial flexibility.

3. **Refinancing risk (2027-2028):** MBI term loans mature November 2027. CABO Term Loan B-4 and 2028 convertible notes total $1.08B. Refinancing this volume with declining EBITDA and 4x+ leverage will be expensive. A spread widening of 200-300bps on $2B+ in debt would consume $40-60M in additional annual interest expense.

### Structural Risks

4. **Secular subscriber decline:** 5.8% annual residential data losses may accelerate as fiber overbuilding continues and FWA improves. There is no historical precedent for a cable operator reversing this trajectory once it begins.

5. **Further impairment charges:** After $586M in 2025 impairments, $2.82B in goodwill and intangibles remain. Continued subscriber losses and elevated discount rates make additional write-downs likely.

6. **Material weakness history:** A material weakness in internal controls was identified as of December 31, 2023, and a September 2024 8-K (Item 4.02) indicated non-reliance on previously issued financial statements. While management reports remediation, the history introduces uncertainty around financial reporting quality.

7. **Equity method investment losses:** $138M in 2025, $205M in 2024. Clearwave Fiber and other equity method investments represent additional off-balance-sheet exposure to the same competitive dynamics.

### Mitigants

- FCF remains positive at $278M annually
- $1.25B revolver provides liquidity bridge (though capacity is being consumed)
- New CEO may bring strategic clarity
- Mobile MVNO pilot could reduce churn if executed effectively
- Insider buying by value-oriented directors (Wallace Weitz's $928K purchase) suggests informed parties see value

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($130-145)
The new CEO stabilizes subscriber trends through improved bundling (mobile MVNO), DOCSIS 4.0 deployment, and selective BEAD participation. EBITDA stabilizes at $780-800M. MBI integration proceeds smoothly. Debt markets remain open for refinancing at manageable spreads. The stock re-rates to 5x EV/EBITDA as the market prices in stabilization. Probability: 15-20%.

### Bear Case ($30-55)
Subscriber losses accelerate to 8-10% as fiber overbuilding reaches 70%+ of the footprint and FWA penetration increases. EBITDA declines to $650-700M by 2027. Refinancing the 2027-2028 maturities requires punitive terms or covenant violations. Additional impairments wipe out remaining goodwill. The equity becomes a residual claim on a declining, overleveraged business. Probability: 35-40%.

### Base Case ($75-95)
Subscriber losses continue at 5-7% annually. EBITDA declines to $720-760M by 2027. MBI acquisition closes and adds scale but not growth. Refinancing proceeds at elevated spreads, compressing FCF available to equity. The stock trades at 3.5-4.0x EV/EBITDA on the lower EBITDA base. Probability: 40-50%.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Multiple identifiable catalysts with specific timelines: (1) MBI acquisition closing ~October 2026 will crystallize the pro forma leverage increase and force the market to price in $4B+ in debt; (2) 2027-2028 refinancing cycle begins in earnest, likely with credit spread widening; (3) continued quarterly subscriber losses through 2026 earnings reports. The most immediate catalyst is the May 2026 Q1 earnings report, which will be the first under the new CEO and may include MBI integration cost guidance.

**Timing window:** 6-12 months. The MBI close and subsequent refinancing discussions (late 2026 into 2027) are the primary timing anchors.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest at 14.7% of float with 6.3 days to cover is elevated but below the threshold where squeeze risk dominates. The low float (5.67M shares) and moderate daily volume (~168K shares) mean position sizing must be conservative to avoid liquidity traps. Institutional ownership composition should be monitored.

**Borrow and options signal:** The stock's extreme decline (-60% over 12 months) means borrow costs are likely elevated. The negative trailing P/E and absence of any Buy ratings suggest the bearish thesis is well-known but not fully crowded. No options data was provided, so skew and IV signals cannot be assessed.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $30-55 represents 47-71% downside from $102. Bull case of $130-145 represents 27-42% upside. At current levels, the risk/reward for a short is approximately 1.5:1 to 2.0:1, depending on stop-loss placement. A stop at $135 (32% risk) versus a target of $55 (46% reward) yields approximately 1.4:1 — marginal.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** at current levels. The stock has already declined 63% from its 52-week high and 60% over 12 months. While the fundamental deterioration supports continued downside, the magnitude of the decline already prices in significant distress. The risk of a squeeze (14.7% SI, low float), a positive surprise from the new CEO, or an acquirer approaching at a modest premium to current levels makes the risk/reward for a new short position unfavorable. The residual downside exists but is better captured by simply avoiding the stock. If the stock rallies to $130-140 on any positive news (e.g., better-than-expected Q1, MBI synergy guidance), the short setup would improve materially.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** All three conditions would need to be met before considering CABO:
1. Residential data subscriber losses stabilize at <3% YoY for two consecutive quarters
2. Pro forma net debt / EBITDA declines below 3.5x following MBI integration
3. Mobile MVNO achieves >50K subscribers, demonstrating churn reduction efficacy

**Timeline:** These headwinds are structural, not cyclical. Expect 12-24 months minimum before any reassessment is warranted. The 2027-2028 refinancing cycle will either validate recovery or trigger further distress.

## Conclusion

Cable One is a declining broadband provider facing structural competitive headwinds, unsustainable leverage, and mandatory financial obligations that will consume available liquidity. The 4.0x EV/EBITDA multiple reflects a market that understands the equity is a residual claim subordinated to $4B+ in debt on a shrinking business. Zero analyst Buy ratings, 14.7% short interest, and a 1-for-4 quarterly earnings beat record all confirm the negative consensus. Insider purchases by value-oriented directors are noted but insufficient to override the fundamental picture. AVOID.
