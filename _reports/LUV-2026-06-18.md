**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Southwest Airlines is in the middle of the largest strategic overhaul in its 50-year history, dismantling the three pillars of its original model (point-to-point low-cost flying, open seating, and "Bags Fly Free") in favor of a segmented, ancillary-rich product that more closely resembles legacy network carriers. Under pressure from Elliott Investment Management, which obtained substantial board representation and reshaped governance through 2025-2026, the company introduced bag fees (May 2025), assigned and extra-legroom seating (January 2026), fare tiers, premium cabins, and hub-and-spoke connectivity with seven new partner carriers. The early operating evidence is positive: Q1 2026 was a record first quarter, with revenue up 12.8% to $7.25B, ancillary revenue sold separately tripling to $715M, and operating income of $330M reversing a $(223)M loss in the prior-year quarter.

The problem is what sits underneath the revenue inflection. Southwest's operating margin remains structurally thin (roughly 1.5% in 2025 even as revenue hit a record $28.1B) and the company terminated its entire fuel-hedging program in 2025, the practice that defined the airline for two decades, and is now 100% exposed to spot fuel. Management has disclosed that the forward curve implied Q2 2026 fuel of $4.10-$4.15/gallon versus $2.73 in Q1. At a 1¢/gallon = $5.6M quarterly sensitivity and thin margins, a ~50% sequential fuel jump can erase much of the transformation's earnings gain in a single quarter. Layered on top is an aggressive capital-return program ($1.25B repurchased in Q1 2026 alone against negative free cash flow) that has drawn cash down from $9.3B (2023) to $3.2B (2025).

At ~$47.86 (recent trade $46.66), LUV has rallied 48% off its 52-week low and trades at roughly 18x current-year consensus EPS of $2.70, a clear premium to network peers United (10x) and SkyWest (8.6x). The market is paying up for an unproven model conversion at a carrier with thin margins, newly unhedged fuel exposure, and a cash balance being deliberately depleted. With the most likely near-term catalyst (Q2 fuel shock) pointing down and the stock priced for execution success, we rate LUV **AVOID**.

## Company Overview

Southwest operates a single reportable segment as a major U.S. passenger airline serving domestic and near-international markets. It flies an all-Boeing 737 fleet of 800 aircraft as of Q1 2026 (294× 737-700, 196× 737-800, 310× 737-8 MAX; 705 owned, 95 leased; average age 11 years). The transformation under way is substantive rather than cosmetic:

- **Bag fees** for most fare products began May 28, 2025, ending "Bags Fly Free."
- **Assigned and extra-legroom seating** began January 27, 2026, requiring retrofitting of 780 aircraft and ending 50+ years of open seating.
- **Flight credit expiration** was reintroduced (credits now expire within one year).
- **New fare tiers, premium seating, and a RECARO cabin refresh** (84 aircraft in service as of April 2026).
- **Network shift toward hub-and-spoke** with codeshare/connectivity partnerships (seven carriers since February 2025, including All Nippon Airways via Honolulu/SFO/SEA/LAX gateways).
- **Onboard upgrades**, including in-seat power and at least 300 Starlink-equipped aircraft by end-2026 (as a connectivity customer, not a technology owner).

CEO Robert Jordan retains the top job and now carries the title President, CEO & Vice Chairman of the Board. Founder-era Chairman Gary Kelly has fully exited; the board has been downsized to 11 directors after two resignations effective February 23, 2026. Tom Doxey is EVP & CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Revenue has grown steadily post-COVID — $23.8B (2022) → $26.1B (2023) → $27.5B (2024) → $28.1B (2025) — but profitability has deteriorated even as the top line rose. Operating income fell from $1,017M (2022) to $224M (2023), $321M (2024), and $428M (2025), leaving a 2025 operating margin of roughly 1.5%. Net income has been stuck near $440-540M for four years. The legacy low-cost model stopped generating adequate returns, which is why management is converting the airline toward a legacy-carrier ancillary structure.

**The transformation is working on the top line.** The lever is ancillary revenue, which tripled to $715M from $245M a year earlier, direct evidence the bag-fee and seating monetization is flowing through. RASM rose 11.2%, yield up 11.5%, average fare up 16.6% to $225.93.

**Cost pressure is building.** CASM rose 2.6% in Q1 2026; ex-fuel and profit-sharing it rose ~2.3%, with contractual wage step-ups the main driver (labor +6.3%). The larger near-term cost risk is fuel. Management flagged that Middle East escalation drove Q1 fuel ~$164M above forecast. The mechanism is direct: higher spot crude → higher jet fuel → unhedged 100% pass-through to a ~1.5%-margin cost base → potential swing back toward break-even or losses. At $5.6M quarterly impact per 1¢/gallon, a $1.40/gallon sequential move is roughly $784M of quarterly fuel cost increase against Q1 operating income of $330M.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** 2025 operating cash flow was $1.84B but capex was $2.67B, producing negative free cash flow of ~$(831)M, driven by aircraft pre-delivery payments. Q1 2026 OCF improved to $1.4B on a seasonal air-traffic-liability build of $1.1B. Cash fell from $9.3B (2023) to $7.5B (2024) to $3.2B (2025), and was $3.3B at Q1 2026. The drawdown is largely self-inflicted via capital return and debt prepayment rather than operating distress: the company repaid $1.6B convertible notes at maturity (Q2 2025), redeemed all three Treasury PSP loans early (~$2.1B, final $426M prepaid April 2026), took a new $500M secured term loan (March 2026), and retains a $1.5B undrawn revolver. Long-term debt is down to $4.6B, the company holds investment-grade ratings from all three agencies, and ~$16.5B of unencumbered assets provide liquidity backstop. The working-capital deficit of ~$6.5B is normal for airlines (air-traffic liability is a non-cash performance obligation).

The capital-allocation philosophy shift raises financial fragility. Southwest repurchased $1.25B of stock in Q1 2026 alone (28.4M shares via ASRs and open market) plus an $89M quarterly dividend — more than its quarterly operating cash flow net of capex — while drawing down cash and adding secured debt. Treasury stock ballooned to $14.8B and share count fell from a 584M weighted-average in Q1'25 to ~498M. Returning capital aggressively while free cash flow is negative reduces the buffer available if fuel or demand sours.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is real but narrow and early. The ancillary-revenue inflection is the most credible piece, confirming that bag fees and seat monetization are converting at scale. This is a structural revenue lever that Southwest had never pulled, and there is meaningful runway as premium seating and fare-tier adoption expand across the 780-aircraft retrofit.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive in the very near term but were sharply cut over 90 days. Current-quarter consensus is $0.47 (up 9.1% over 30 days but down 61.2% over 90 days); current-year consensus is $2.70 (up 2.1% over 30 days, down 32.8% over 90 days). The pattern of small recent upticks against very large 90-day downward revisions reflects analysts marking down the year on fuel and then nudging the next quarter up at the margin. This is not the +30% upward-revision velocity that signals a clean earnings inflection; it is a stabilization off a sharply lowered base.

The earnings track record is mixed: over the last four quarters, one beat, one in-line, and two misses, including a 5.8% miss in Q1 2026 and a 16.1% miss in Q2 2025. The Q3 2025 "432% surprise" is an artifact of a near-zero estimate base. Forward visibility is therefore moderate, and the largest near-term variable (fuel) is outside management's control and no longer hedged.

Capacity growth is constrained by Boeing. The order book is 473 firm MAX plus 144 options through 2031 with $14.8B in capital commitments; 81 of 2024-25 contracted aircraft slipped into 2026, and the 737-7 (Southwest is launch customer) remains uncertified, with timing controlled by the FAA. Net capex guidance is $3.0-3.5B for 2026 against ~66 MAX-8 deliveries and ~60 retirements.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$47.86, LUV trades at roughly 18x current-year consensus EPS of $2.70 and ~10.6x next-year consensus of $4.53. The forward multiple on the current year sits at a clear premium to network-carrier peers: United at 10.0x, SkyWest at 8.6x, Air Lease at 7.0x. American (47.9x) and Alaska (75.6x) trade on depressed trailing earnings and are not clean comparables. The peer-median P/E is 10.0x.

The bull framing is that on next-year consensus of $4.53, LUV trades at ~10.6x, in line with the network-carrier group, and that if the transformation compounds ancillary revenue the multiple is reasonable. The problem is that the $4.53 next-year figure embeds continued execution success and a normalized fuel environment, and it was itself revised down 8.1% over 90 days. On the current year — the period over which the fuel shock actually hits — the stock is expensive relative to a low-margin, capital-intensive, cyclical business that does not control its largest variable cost.

A moderate-looking forward multiple should not be read as cheap here. The earnings base is fragile (1.5% operating margin), the model conversion is unproven at scale, fuel is unhedged into a rising curve, and the cash buffer is being deliberately depleted. Analyst targets bracket the current price (mean $45.95, median $45.00, low $30, high $60), which means the stock is already trading slightly above consensus mean with a wide dispersion that reflects genuine disagreement about whether the turnaround sticks. We do not treat the high target as a floor here: this is a thin-margin cyclical with a binary near-term fuel input and no cleanly rising estimates. The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Southwest is converging on the legacy/network playbook — segmentation, ancillary fees, premium seats, hub connectivity, and global partnerships — because its differentiated low-cost model stopped generating adequate returns (sub-2% operating margins for three straight years). The strategic question is whether it can capture legacy-style ancillary and premium revenue without losing the cost advantage and brand loyalty that defined it.

The competitive trajectory is ambiguous. On the positive side, J.D. Power 2026 customer satisfaction still led the industry, suggesting the brand has not yet broken. On the negative side, the company is dismantling the operational efficiency mechanism (open seating) that historically gave it a structural turn-time and cost edge, and it is now running assigned-seat, hub-and-spoke flying at a scale it has never operated. News coverage through mid-2026 is mixed, with customer-facing friction around assigned-seat fees, upsell text alerts, standby changes, and bait-and-switch accusations. The all-Boeing fleet remains both a cost simplification and a single-supplier dependency that has repeatedly forced capacity re-plans. Network rationalization — suspending O'Hare and Dulles, cutting staffing at Atlanta, Fort Lauderdale, and Philadelphia (effective June 4, 2026), following the first major layoff in company history in February 2025 — is rational but signals that the prior network footprint was not earning its keep. The competitive position is in transition with the cost-advantage moat narrowing as the model converges with legacy peers.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel — newly unhedged.** This is the most material change versus prior years and the dominant near-term risk. Southwest closed its entire hedge book right before a fuel spike. With ~1.5% operating margins, the implied ~50% sequential jump to ~$4.10+/gallon for Q2 2026 could swing the company back toward losses. This is a deliberate strategy change, not a one-off, and it raises earnings variability structurally.

**Cash drawdown funding buybacks.** The cash drawdown to $3.2B against negative FCF, combined with a single-quarter $1.25B repurchase and a new $500M secured term loan, increases financial fragility if the turnaround or fuel environment deteriorates.

**Transformation/brand execution.** Dismantling "Bags Fly Free" and open seating attacks the company's identity. Early evidence is encouraging on revenue, but customer friction and loyalty risk are real and the operational risk of assigned-seat, hub-and-spoke flying at Southwest's scale is unproven.

**Boeing dependence.** 100% Boeing fleet, $14.8B in capital commitments, uncertified 737-7, and repeated delivery slippage constrain capacity planning.

**Litigation — turning favorable.** The consolidated 10b-5 securities class action (S.D. Tex.) over the December 2022 IT meltdown was dismissed without prejudice on March 31, 2026, with leave to amend by April 24, 2026. The activist-related derivative suit was dismissed with prejudice March 17, 2026 under Texas SB 29's 3% ownership threshold; the plaintiff appealed to the 5th Circuit. Both are net positives but not fully resolved.

**Governance.** Effective control has shifted to Elliott's reshaping — board cut to 11, founder-era leadership gone, Jordan elevated to Vice Chairman. The bag-fee decision itself is the subject of litigation alleging the board acceded to Elliott pressure, and the SB 29 3%-threshold bylaw is a defensive governance move.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 182% against 1-year historical volatility of 44.6% — a 137-point spread that places IV at the 88th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (182% near-term versus 47.1% at three months), and ATM IV jumped from 63.2% to 203.3% over the past month even as the price rose 23.5%. Put/call skew is elevated at 4.2%, and the one-month tenor shows a 19.5% put skew. This rising-IV-into-rising-price pattern indicates the market is hedging a specific upcoming catalyst, consistent with the Q2 fuel-cost disclosure and the next earnings release, rather than expressing complacency. The signal reinforces the bearish near-term read. Short interest is moderate at 7.9% of float (4.4 days to cover), so the name is not a crowded short, and there is no open-market insider buying to counter the cautious positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The transformation is genuinely converting: ancillary revenue tripled YoY, Q1 2026 was a record first quarter with a swing to $227M net income, and J.D. Power satisfaction still leads. If premium seating and fare-tier adoption compound across the 780-aircraft retrofit while fuel normalizes, next-year EPS of $4.53 is achievable and the stock re-rates toward network-carrier multiples on a higher earnings base. Investment-grade ratings, $16.5B of unencumbered assets, and a deleveraged $4.6B long-term debt load provide a cushion. Bull case ~$60 (consensus high), roughly +25% from current.

**Bear case.** Unhedged fuel at $4.10+/gallon erases the Q2 margin gain and pushes the company back toward break-even, exposing the fragility of a ~1.5% operating margin and a deliberately depleted $3.2B cash balance. Brand erosion from fee-and-seating friction caps the ancillary lever, and the stock de-rates as the market questions whether the legacy-model conversion was worth surrendering the cost advantage. Bear case ~$30 (consensus low), roughly -37% from current.

The asymmetry favors the bear case over the next two to three quarters because the most probable near-term catalyst (Q2 fuel) is dated, quantified by management, and points down, while the bull case requires multiple quarters of clean execution and a fuel reversal the company can no longer hedge against.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is a mediocre risk/reward at the current price, not necessarily a business in collapse; the verdict is to stay out of the equity, not to short it (see below).

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Forward P/E compresses toward the network-peer median (~10x current-year, implying a price near $27 on $2.70 EPS, or a higher EPS base at the current price) so that valuation no longer prices in flawless execution.
- Two consecutive quarters demonstrating that the ancillary-revenue lever holds margins through a normal-to-elevated fuel environment — i.e., operating margin sustained above ~4-5% with fuel at or above current spot.
- Evidence that the cash drawdown has stabilized: free cash flow turns positive or the buyback pace is moderated to within operating cash flow net of capex.
- Resumption of even partial fuel hedging, which would materially reduce the earnings variability that currently caps our risk score.

**Timeline:** The dominant headwind (Q2 2026 fuel) resolves at the late-July earnings release. We expect the unhedged-fuel and cash-fragility risks to persist through at least the next two to three quarters until the transformation demonstrates margin durability across a full fuel cycle.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The Q2 2026 earnings release (expected late July 2026) is the specific dated event. The disclosed forward curve implying a ~50% sequential fuel increase against a ~1.5% operating margin could compress or eliminate operating income and produce a miss versus the $0.56 next-quarter consensus.

**Timing window.** The catalyst resolves at the late-July print, roughly six weeks out — a reasonable horizon that does not require extended carry.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 7.9% of float with 4.4 days to cover — moderate, uncrowded, and below the >20% threshold that creates meaningful squeeze risk. There is room to express the view, but the modest SI also means informed investors are not heavily aligned with the bearish thesis.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market is confirming near-term caution. However, this richness makes outright put-buying expensive — the market is already pricing a ±3.1% nearest-expiry move and a 13.3% three-month implied move, so much of the downside catalyst is pre-priced. The very high near-term IV is the principal obstacle to an attractive short structure.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear-case target ~$30 (-37%); upside risk on a fuel reversal or a clean beat is the ~$60 high target (+25%). On paper the reward:risk is roughly 1.5:1, below the 2:1 bar. The fuel input is genuinely two-sided (a Hormuz de-escalation could send crude lower and reverse the thesis), and IV is too rich to short cleanly via options.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The catalyst is dated and the fundamental concern is real, but the risk/reward falls short of 2:1, the fuel input is two-sided and outside the company's control in both directions, and near-term implied volatility is too elevated to structure the trade economically. This is a name to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Conclusion

Southwest's transformation is doing what it was designed to do on the revenue line: ancillary revenue tripled, Q1 2026 was a record quarter, and the company swung from loss to profit. But the equity is being asked to pay ~18x current-year earnings for a model conversion that is early, brand-disruptive, and unproven at scale, layered on a ~1.5% operating margin made structurally more volatile by the 2025 decision to terminate all fuel hedging. With the forward curve implying a ~50% sequential fuel cost increase into Q2, a depleted cash balance, and Elliott-driven buybacks running ahead of free cash flow, the most probable near-term outcome skews to a margin disappointment rather than a re-rate. The stock has already rallied 48% off its lows into a near-term implied-vol spike that signals the market is bracing for stress. We maintain **AVOID**, consistent with the prior four assessments, and would revisit only on multiple compression toward peer multiples or two quarters of demonstrated margin durability through an elevated-fuel environment.
