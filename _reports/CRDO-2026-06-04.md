**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Credo designs high-speed connectivity silicon and cables for AI and data-center networks. The company moved through a fundamental earnings inflection in FY2026 that is now visible in audited results, not forecasts: revenue grew more than 200% year over year to roughly $1.35B, GAAP operating margin expanded from -19% in FY2024 to +37% in the quarter ended January 31, 2026, and net income reached $303.2M over the first nine months of FY2026 against $266.8M of revenue in the comparable prior period. The Q4 report on June 1, 2026 delivered a single quarter whose revenue exceeded all of FY2025, beating consensus near $432M. The setup is a business emerging from a low base with accelerating revenue and operating leverage simultaneously.

The forward earnings path supports a BUY rather than a HOLD. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up +28.6% for the current year and +48.9% for next year over the trailing 90 days, with the next-year number now at $8.62. At a current price of ~$214.60, that is roughly 25x next-year EPS on a company compounding revenue at triple-digit rates — a PEG well below 1. The forward multiple sits inside the reasonableness gate (<30x forward, PEG <1.5) where rising estimates lead price. The catalyst is mechanical: consensus is still catching up to a business that has pre-announced above its own guidance and beaten four quarters running.

Conviction is deliberately bounded rather than maximum. The risks are specific: finished-goods inventory tripled to $169M with no long-term customer purchase commitments, TSMC is the sole foundry against a live Taiwan-tail backdrop, the absolute multiple (~85x trailing) prices perfection and has produced beat-and-dip reactions, and insiders have sold persistently through the run. These cap position sizing rather than neutralize the inflection. The Q4 gross-margin step-down to a guided 64-66% from ~68.5% is already embedded in the upward EPS revisions, so it does not break the forward bridge. The measurable catalyst the data documents — consensus chasing a confirmed inflection at a reasonable forward multiple — supports a sized BUY.

## Company Overview

Credo is a fabless semiconductor company founded in 2008, incorporated in the Cayman Islands and headquartered in San Jose. Its technology base is proprietary SerDes (serializer/deserializer) and DSP intellectual property, which it deploys across a product portfolio led by Active Electrical Cables (AECs) — the "HiWire"/ZeroFlap copper cables that have driven more than 95% of recent product-sales growth. Around the AEC franchise sit optical DSPs (Seagull, Dove, and the newer Lark family for linear-receive optics), PCIe Gen6 retimers (Toucan), line-card PHYs, SerDes chiplets, and a SerDes IP licensing business with 50-plus engagements.

The company operates as a single reportable segment serving hyperscale and data-center customers. Its competitive differentiation rests on an "n-1" node strategy — achieving competitive performance and power on cheaper, mature TSMC process nodes to lower cost — and on having pioneered the AEC product category, including a dual-ToR switch architecture developed with Microsoft. Principal competitors are Broadcom, Marvell, and the more directly comparable Astera Labs. FY2026 saw the company widen its addressable market with ZeroFlap optics, Active Line Cards, OmniConnect memory solutions, and the PILOT diagnostic/telemetry software platform.

The demand backdrop is the AI back-end and scale-up networking build-out. Rack-scale GPU clusters expanding from 8 to 72-plus GPUs require dramatically denser interconnect, favoring Credo's low-power AECs over optical active cables and passive copper, while driving retimer and DSP attach. Ethernet becoming the standard for AI back-end fabrics and rising PCIe Gen6/CXL adoption reinforce the structural demand. Because the demand spans broad-based hyperscaler interconnect, the durability of the growth thesis is higher than a single-company story.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue moved from $184M (FY2023) to $193M (FY2024) to $436.8M (FY2025, +126%) to roughly $1.35B in FY2026 (>200%). The nine months ended January 31, 2026 produced $898.1M of revenue against $266.8M in the prior-year period. Q3 FY2026 alone was $407.0M, up 201% year over year, and Q4 exceeded all of FY2025's revenue in a single quarter.

Operating leverage is genuine. Gross margin rose from 57.7% (FY2023) to 64.8% (FY2025) to 68.5% in Q3 FY2026. GAAP operating margin swung from -19% in FY2024 to +37% in Q3 FY2026 as R&D fell from ~49.5% to ~21% of revenue and SG&A from ~31% to ~15%. Net income was $157.1M in Q3 FY2026 and $303.2M over nine months, versus a $28.4M loss in FY2024. Operating cash flow was $282M over nine months FY2026 against $7M a year earlier, and free cash flow is positive.

As of January 31, 2026 the company held $1.22B in cash plus $81M in short-term investments against total equity of $1.85B, debt-to-equity of ~0.19, and a current ratio near 10x. Altman Z of 5.97 sits firmly in the safe zone. During FY2026 the company raised $736.3M net through an ATM program with Goldman Sachs (4.8M shares) at elevated prices, requiring no debt.

Three quality-of-earnings items warrant monitoring. First, stock-based compensation is heavy at $132.9M over nine months (~15% of revenue), inflating the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap and contributing to share-count growth from ~155M to ~184M over roughly two years via ATM issuance, warrant exercise, and equity comp. Second, inventory ballooned to $207.9M from ~$90M, with finished goods tripling to $169M; management attributes this to backlog and new-product ramps, but $11.8M of obsolescence was already taken in nine months, and the combination of an inventory build with no long-term customer purchase commitments is the cleanest bear data point. Third, accounts receivable grew to $243M alongside the revenue scale.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the strongest of the six dimensions. Three vectors compound: AEC volume scaling with hyperscaler AI cluster build-out; an optics inflection (ZeroFlap, Lark DSPs, the Hyperlume microLED interconnect technology acquired for $92M in September 2025); and platform TAM expansion into memory/OmniConnect, Active Line Cards, and the PILOT software layer.

The forward indicators are unambiguous. Estimate revisions show current-year EPS rising +9.8% over 30 days and +28.6% over 90 days, and next-year EPS rising +13.4% over 30 days and +48.9% over 90 days to $8.62. The earnings beat track record reinforces confidence in consensus: four consecutive beats of 10.5%, 9.8%, 33.5%, and 40.7%, with management pre-announcing Q3 at $404-408M versus a $335-345M guide — evidence either of conservative guidance or genuinely accelerating demand visibility, both constructive for forward estimates.

The estimate-revision magnitude is large, and it is paired with revenue growth above 200% — the combination that signals the market is still underestimating the inflection. Because the next-year forward multiple is ~25x (below the 30x reasonableness gate), this signal is operative rather than already-priced; at a premium multiple the same revisions would carry less weight, but at ~25x on triple-digit growth the multiple has not yet absorbed the estimate path.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the dimension that bounds conviction without breaking the thesis. On a trailing basis the stock screens at ~85x, which is meaningless at the front end of an earnings inflection, because trailing earnings reflect a period that bears no relation to the current run-rate. The relevant metric is forward EPS against forward growth. At ~$214.60 and next-year EPS of $8.62, the stock trades at ~25x next-year earnings. For a company growing revenue above 200% with expanding operating margins, that produces a PEG well below 1, which is the quantitative core of the BUY.

The peer cohort matters here. Astera Labs and the broader AI-connectivity group trade at premium multiples that reflect a sector re-rating on structural demand, not historical norms. Anchoring CRDO to pre-AI semiconductor multiples would mis-frame the regime. Within the re-rating cohort, ~25x forward on triple-digit growth is not stretched.

The gross-margin objection needs to be resolved quantitatively. Management guided Q4 to 64-66%, a step-down of roughly 250-450bps tied to the lower-initial-margin optics ramp and product mix. This guidance was issued during the same 90-day window in which analysts revised estimates up. The analysts setting $8.62 for next year have the margin mix in front of them and the EPS path still inflects, because revenue is growing far faster than the margin is compressing. The margin step-down is embedded in the forward bridge that supports ~25x; it does not impair it.

On the inflection-stock framework, analyst targets function as lagging floors rather than ceilings when a stock is cheap relative to rising estimates. The consensus mean target is $250.91 (~17% above current), the median $256, and the high $300, with sell-side stacking at 4 strong buy / 13 buy / 1 hold and no sells. With estimates still being revised up at double-digit velocity, those targets are likely to be marked higher as consensus catches up. The guardrail — that targets are not independent confirmation at >50x forward — does not bind here because the forward multiple is ~25x.

## Competitive Landscape

Credo's competitive position is improving on trajectory, which the scoring weighs more than absolute scale. It pioneered the AEC category and holds a differentiated low-power, low-cost position via the n-1 node strategy, and it has a flagship hyperscaler design win with Microsoft on the dual-ToR architecture. Against Broadcom and Marvell it is a fraction of the size, and Astera Labs is the closest comparable; the company's claim to be the only player offering a complete suite of high-performance connectivity solutions is a marketing position, but the revenue and margin data corroborate genuine share capture in AECs.

The most important competitive datapoint is customer diversification. In FY2025 a single customer was 67% of revenue and the top ten were ~90%. By Q3 FY2026 the end-customer mix had spread to three large accounts in the 17-39% range (Customer B 39%, Customer D 32%, Customer E 17%). Amazon's full exercise of its customer warrant by January 2026 removed a contra-revenue drag and signals a maturing strategic relationship. Concentration remains severe in absolute terms, but the trajectory is de-risking, which strengthens the competitive read.

The optics and platform expansion (Lark DSPs, ZeroFlap, Hyperlume microLED, OmniConnect, PILOT) broadens the moat beyond a single product category, and new long-dated San Jose leases through ~2036 signal management's own confidence in sustained scale.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the second dimension that caps conviction, and these are specific, not sector truisms.

Customer concentration, even after diversification, is the dominant risk. With no long-term purchase commitments and a purchase-order sales model, a single large hyperscaler reducing or delaying orders could hit revenue sharply. The 2023 episode — when the then-largest customer cut its forecast and hammered FY2023/FY2024 revenue — demonstrates that the downside is tangible and has occurred before.

The inventory build amplifies the concentration risk. Finished goods against no firm commitments create write-down exposure if hyperscaler order timing slips, and obsolescence has already been recognized. This is the metric to watch most closely into the next print.

Supply-chain and geopolitical concentration is structural. TSMC is the sole foundry, and assembly/test is in Asia. A Taiwan disruption is a low-probability, high-impact supply risk that is not fully diversifiable. Hong Kong-booked revenue (56% of FY2025) and assets (43%) reflect contract-manufacturer contracting locations rather than end-demand, but expose the company to HK National Security Law and U.S. sanctions regimes. Cayman incorporation brings weaker shareholder protections and PFIC/CFC tax considerations for U.S. holders, and U.S. outbound-investment rules constrain the PRC subsidiaries.

Valuation/expectations risk is independent of fundamentals: at a premium multiple after a parabolic run, even clean beats have produced selloffs, and the June 1 Q4 beat was met with a dip. The June 1, 2026 8-K carried an Item 5.02 officer departure/election concurrent with earnings whose details were not disclosed in available reporting; this is an unresolved thread that merits follow-up but is not yet a quantified negative.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 120.6% versus 1-year historical vol of 84.4% — rich, in the 87th percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in backwardation (120.6% near-term versus 97.5% at six months), the signature of an imminent event being priced. Skew is balanced (2.7% near-term, mildly put-tilted at longer tenors of -4.8% to -6.1%), and put/call ratios are below 1 (volume 0.68, OI 0.72), so the positioning is not aggressively bearish. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.4%. The market expects volatility, not a directional collapse. For a fundamentally constructive thesis, the rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry sizing and against chasing into the event-vol spike, consistent with treating the print as a variance event rather than a directional signal. Short interest is modest at 6.8% of float with 1.7 days to cover, so there is neither a squeeze tailwind nor a crowded-short warning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Revenue compounds off the AI interconnect super-cycle as AECs scale and the optics/platform layers (Lark, ZeroFlap, Hyperlume, OmniConnect, PILOT) broaden TAM. Customer diversification continues, reducing the single-account vulnerability that historically defined the risk. Operating leverage holds even as gross margin normalizes to the mid-60s, because revenue grows faster than margin compresses. Consensus continues revising EPS upward and the multiple holds, taking the stock toward the $300 high target and beyond as next-year EPS proves conservative. Bull target ~$300 (+40%).

**Bear case.** A large hyperscaler reduces or delays orders, the $169M finished-goods inventory turns into write-downs, and the no-commitment order model amplifies a single-quarter air pocket into a multi-quarter revenue reset, echoing the 2023 episode. The premium multiple compresses hard on any growth deceleration, and a Taiwan/TSMC disruption is the tail that breaks the supply base entirely. Bear target ~$150 (-30%), reflecting a de-rate to ~18-19x a haircut next-year EPS.

**Base case.** The inflection continues, estimates keep rising, and consensus targets get marked up. The stock re-rates toward and through the mean target as the next print confirms the trajectory, with the gross-margin step-down already absorbed in the EPS path. Base target ~$256 (+19%), in line with median consensus and ~30x next-year EPS of $8.62 with upward revision optionality.

The audit challenge is correct on the substance: the quantitative inflection-BUY gates are largely met (>200% revenue growth, large +28.6%/+48.9% 90-day estimate revisions, ~25x forward with PEG <1, four consecutive double-digit beats, de-risking concentration, fortress balance sheet). The three reasons that might argue for HOLD do not survive scrutiny: the margin step-down is embedded in the upward revisions, the concentration trend is improving not deteriorating, and the insider selling is largely 10b5-1 with replenishing grants rather than a discretionary bear signal. The recommendation is therefore BUY, sized below maximum conviction because of the genuine inventory, geopolitical, and absolute-multiple risks that cap rather than negate the thesis.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** ~$256 (+19% from $214.60). Math: ~30x next-year EPS of $8.62, in line with median consensus and supported by ongoing upward estimate revisions; this sits between the bear and bull cases.

**Bull case target:** ~$300 (+40%). Continued estimate revisions and the optics/platform ramp push next-year EPS above $8.62 and the multiple holds at the high end of the re-rating cohort.

**Bear case target:** ~$150 (-30%). A hyperscaler order air pocket plus inventory write-downs trigger a de-rate to ~18-19x a reduced next-year EPS.

**Upside/downside from current:** +19% to base, +40% to bull, -30% to bear.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two earnings prints (Q1 FY2027 and Q2 FY2027) and continued hyperscaler AI capex confirmation.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Year-over-year revenue growth decelerating below ~50% on any quarterly print, signaling the inflection has rolled over rather than broadened.
2. A material finished-goods inventory write-down (>$25M in a quarter) or sequential revenue decline, confirming the no-commitment order-timing risk.
3. Forward EPS estimates rolling over (90-day revisions turning negative), which would remove the consensus-catch-up catalyst that underwrites the ~25x forward case.

Position sizing should be moderate given the rich near-term IV, the premium absolute multiple, and the sole-source TSMC/Taiwan tail; entry on volatility-driven pullbacks rather than into the event-vol spike improves the risk/reward.

## Conclusion

Credo presents a genuine AI-driven earnings inflection that has graduated from forecast to audited results: revenue up more than 200% in FY2026, operating margin swinging from -19% to +37%, and consensus EPS revised up +28.6% (current year) and +48.9% (next year) over 90 days. At ~25x next-year EPS of $8.62 on triple-digit revenue growth, the stock clears the quantitative gates of the inflection-BUY profile: a PEG below 1, four consecutive double-digit beats, de-risking customer concentration, and a fortress balance sheet with $1.22B of cash and net positive equity. The documented catalyst is mechanical: consensus is still chasing a business that has pre-announced above its own guidance.

The case for restraint is bounded: ballooning finished-goods inventory with no long-term commitments, sole-source TSMC exposure against a live Taiwan tail, a premium absolute multiple that prices perfection, and persistent insider selling through the run. These cap position sizing rather than neutralize the thesis. The gross-margin step-down to 64-66% is already embedded in the upward EPS revisions, the concentration trend is improving, and the insider selling is largely 10b5-1. **Recommendation: BUY**, sized moderately, with entry favored on volatility-driven pullbacks rather than into the near-term event-vol spike.
