## Executive Summary

Expedia Group delivered strong Q4 2025 results — revenue of $3.55B (+11.4% YoY, beating by 4.2%), adjusted EPS of $3.78 (+58% YoY), and full-year operating margin expansion of 310bps to 12.7%. The B2B segment grew 18% in 2025 and now represents 33% of revenue, providing a higher-growth, less cyclical earnings stream. The multi-year platform migration is largely complete, and the resulting cost leverage drove adjusted EBITDA to $3.5B (23.8% margin). Free cash flow of $3.1B (21.1% margin) funds aggressive buybacks — the share count is down 20% over three years.

The problem is the macro regime into which these results are being delivered. Consumer sentiment just hit a record low of 47.6 (University of Michigan), oil has breached $102 on the US naval blockade of Iranian ports, and recession probability sits at 45-50%. Travel is among the most discretionary consumer expenditures. Expedia's own 10-K notes "weaker than expected travel demand in the United States in the first half of 2025" — and that was before the current geopolitical escalation. Management's 6-9% FY2026 revenue guidance was issued on February 12, before the blockade, before record-low sentiment readings, and before the potential for 4%+ CPI to further erode real incomes. The forward-looking guidance has not been stress-tested against the current environment.

At ~12x forward EPS ($19.35 consensus), the stock is cheap relative to its 5-year history and to Hilton (47x) or Airbnb (34x), but trades at a modest premium to cruise operators (CCL at 13x, RCL at 18x) that face similar macro sensitivity. The valuation already embeds skepticism about travel durability — which is appropriate. With insufficient visibility into the next two quarters of consumer behavior, the position warrants holding rather than adding.

## Company Overview

Expedia Group operates a global online travel marketplace connecting travelers with ~3.6 million lodging properties, 500+ airlines, and ancillary services (car rentals, cruises, activities, insurance). Revenue is 80% lodging-derived and organized into three segments:

- **B2C ($9.5B, 64% of revenue):** Consumer-facing brands including Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, Travelocity, and others. Grew 2% in 2025.
- **B2B ($4.8B, 33% of revenue):** Technology and inventory supply to airlines, offline agents, online retailers, corporate travel, and financial institutions via API and white-label solutions. Grew 18% in 2025.
- **trivago ($417M, 3% of revenue):** Majority-owned hotel metasearch. Grew 33% in 2025 after a multi-year rebuild.

The business model is 70% merchant (collect from traveler, remit to supplier), 22% agency (commission-based), and 8% advertising/media. The merchant model produces structural negative working capital — cash is collected at booking, suppliers are paid post-stay — which generates significant working capital benefits as the business grows.

Barry Diller controls ~32% of voting power through dual-class shares.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

Revenue grew from $11.7B (2022) to $14.7B (2025), an 8.1% CAGR. Growth accelerated modestly from 6.6% in 2024 to 7.6% in 2025. Gross bookings reached $119.6B, up from $110.9B.

The growth mix is critical: B2B contributed 18% growth on a $4.8B base, while B2C grew just 2% on a $9.5B base. Advertising and media revenue ($1.175B combined) grew ~22%. If B2B growth decelerates — plausible as partners saturate — and B2C remains at 2%, consolidated growth would compress to 5-6%, roughly the lower end of the 6-9% FY2026 guidance.

Q4 2025 showed acceleration: 11.4% revenue growth with B2B bookings up 24%. Q1 2026 guidance of 11-13% revenue growth includes ~4 points of FX tailwind, implying organic growth of 7-9%.

### Profitability

Operating margin expanded from 9.6% (2024) to 12.7% (2025), driven by efficiency gains across three lines:
- Cost of revenue: 12.3% → 9.9% of revenue (payment and customer service optimization)
- Technology: 10.6% → 8.7% (cloud optimization, completed platform migration)
- G&A: 6.0% → 5.2% (headcount reductions)

Adjusted EBITDA reached $3.5B (23.8% margin), up 19% YoY. This margin expansion is the product of multi-year investment in platform unification — Hotels.com and Vrbo now share a common front-end stack and the "One Key" loyalty program. The restructuring program added $107M in charges in 2025 with ~$60M expected in Q1 2026, suggesting further cost harvesting ahead.

Net income was $1.29B, modestly affected by $167M in minority investment losses and a $178M Italian tax settlement.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow of $3.11B (21.1% margin) represents high-quality earnings. Operating cash flow of $3.88B was boosted by working capital inflows from growing deferred merchant bookings — a structural feature of the model, not an accounting artifact. CapEx is modest at $770M (~5% of revenue), primarily capitalized website development.

### Balance Sheet

Cash and equivalents of $5.4B against $6.3B face-value debt (including $1B convertible notes that were due February 2026 and $750M in senior notes maturing within 12 months). The company refinanced $1B of 6.25% notes into 5.40% notes due 2035 in February 2025, and the March 2026 8-K filing likely represents additional refinancing activity.

The debt-to-equity ratio of 19.0x and Altman Z-Score of 0.74 appear alarming but require context. Stockholders' equity of $1.3B is depleted by $5.3B in buybacks over three years, not by operating losses — retained earnings improved from -$1.4B (2022) to +$1.7B (2025). The negative working capital ($16.7B current liabilities includes deferred merchant bookings) is a cash-generative feature of the merchant model. Investment-grade credit ratings (BBB/Baa2 stable) confirm the market does not view this as distressed.

Still, the thin equity cushion and near-term maturities create refinancing sensitivity. In a credit market disruption — which current conditions make plausible — borrowing costs would rise.

### Capital Allocation

Buybacks have been aggressive and well-timed: 40.2M shares repurchased over three years, reducing diluted shares from 156.7M to 125.4M (20% reduction). This amplifies per-share growth beyond operating growth. Dividends were reinstated at $0.48/quarter ($200M annually). $1.6B remains authorized for repurchases.

## Growth Analysis

Four growth vectors warrant assessment:

**1. B2B (HIGH conviction).** The addressable market for travel technology infrastructure — powering airline, hotel chain, and financial institution booking systems — expands as travel digitization penetrates emerging markets. B2B EBITDA grew 22%. This segment is less consumer-cyclical because it is powered by long-term contracts and API integrations.

**2. Advertising and Media (MODERATE conviction).** $1.175B growing ~22%. As the platform accumulates more first-party booking data, advertising relevance and pricing power increase. This is a high-margin revenue stream with structural tailwinds.

**3. Vrbo/Hotels.com Turnaround (MODERATE conviction).** Management stated both brands "returned to growth" in Q4 2025 after the platform migration. If the One Key loyalty program drives cross-brand engagement, it could reaccelerate B2C growth from the anemic 2% level.

**4. AI and Automation (SPECULATIVE).** Management is positioning AI as a tool to reduce customer acquisition costs and improve personalization. Jefferies argues AI agents will favor scaled OTAs who can afford performance marketing. This is plausible but unproven, and the downside scenario — where Google or independent AI agents disintermediate OTAs — is equally plausible.

Consensus estimates project $19.35 EPS in FY2026 (+97% vs. $9.81 trailing) and $22.96 in FY2027. The massive jump from trailing to forward EPS reflects both the Q4 2025 earnings inflection and the 20% smaller share base. Current-year estimates have risen 7.1% over 90 days, with minimal movement in the last 30 days. The stability of recent revisions suggests analysts are not yet incorporating the macro deterioration that accelerated in late March and April.

**DCF consideration:** $3.1B FCF on a $28.2B market cap + $0.9B net debt = ~10.2% unlevered FCF yield. This is attractive for a company growing FCF in the high teens. However, FCF sustainability depends on travel demand holding, which is the core uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

Expedia is the #2 global OTA behind Booking Holdings (BKNG, ~$150B gross bookings vs. EXPE's $120B). BKNG has higher direct traffic, better margins, and stronger international positioning, trading at a deserved premium.

The competitive dynamics are unfavorable on three fronts:

**1. Google.** Expedia spent ~$7.35B on direct marketing in 2025, with a significant share flowing to Google for search traffic. Google's expansion of Google Travel, Google Flights, and Hotel Ads directly threatens the traffic funnel. The 10-K explicitly flags Google as a potential disintermediator. Selling and marketing at ~50% of revenue is the company's largest expense category and its most structurally vulnerable.

**2. Hotel direct channels.** Major chains (Marriott, Hilton, IHG) increasingly push "book direct" with better pricing, loyalty perks, and exclusive inventory. This pressures both B2C volume and take rates. ADR growth has been muted even in strong travel periods.

**3. Alternative accommodations.** Vrbo is a distant #2 to Airbnb in mindshare. While the platform migration and One Key integration should help, Airbnb's first-mover advantage and network effects in the alternative space are substantial.

The B2B segment provides genuine differentiation — no other OTA has a comparable infrastructure business at this scale and growth rate. This is the moat, though it is infrastructure- rather than brand-based.

## Risk Assessment

### Macro Sensitivity (HIGH)
Travel is among the first discretionary expenditures consumers cut. The current combination of record-low consumer sentiment, rising oil and gasoline prices, and 45-50% recession probability creates direct headwinds to booking volumes and ADRs. The 10-K acknowledged "weaker than expected U.S. travel demand in H1 2025" under milder conditions. Management's 6-9% FY2026 revenue guidance has not been updated for the current environment.

### Google/AI Disintermediation (HIGH)
The $7.35B annual marketing spend to Google creates existential dependency. AI-powered travel agents could either benefit scaled OTAs (the Jefferies thesis) or disintermediate them (the bear case). Neither outcome has been validated.

### Leverage and Refinancing (MODERATE)
$6.3B face-value debt with near-term maturities. The March 2026 8-K likely addresses refinancing, but thin equity ($1.3B) and sub-1.0 current ratio (0.73) create sensitivity to credit market disruptions. Investment-grade ratings provide protection, but spreads are widening in the current environment.

### Tax and Legal Exposure (MODERATE)
IRS transfer pricing disputes for 2011-2016 could result in ~$675M federal tax liability if lost. Italian withholding tax settlements have cost $285M over two years. Occupancy tax litigation is ongoing in multiple jurisdictions.

### Dual-Class Governance (LOW-MODERATE)
Barry Diller's 32% voting control creates succession risk and limits minority shareholder influence on strategic decisions.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 51.1% is rich to historical vol of 45.2% (5.9% spread), indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 1.31 and 4.4% put skew confirm mild bearish positioning, though not extreme. The 1-month put/call volume ratio of 5.65 is high, suggesting institutional hedging activity ahead of a potential macro-driven repricing of travel names. The flat term structure (near 51.1% ≈ far 49.1%) indicates uncertainty reflects persistent macro concern rather than a specific catalyst. Short interest at 6.6% of float with 1.9 days to cover is modest and not a positioning concern in either direction. The options market is consistent with a cautious fundamental view — elevated but not panic-level hedging.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($290-310)
- B2B growth sustains at 15%+ and begins to command a premium multiple as the market recognizes it as a distinct business line
- One Key loyalty program drives B2C reacceleration to 5-7% growth
- Margin expansion continues toward 25%+ EBITDA margin through cost leverage
- Consumer environment stabilizes (recession avoided, oil retreats on negotiated de-escalation)
- Buybacks at depressed levels amplify EPS growth to 20%+ through FY2027
- AI tools reduce CAC rather than disintermediate OTAs, benefiting scaled players

### Bear Case ($155-175)
- Recession materializes, compressing travel demand 10-15%
- B2C growth turns negative as consumers cut discretionary travel
- Hotel direct channels accelerate share gains, eroding take rates
- Google or AI agents disintermediate a meaningful portion of search-driven bookings
- Credit market stress forces refinancing at significantly higher rates
- IRS transfer pricing dispute resolves unfavorably (~$675M exposure)
- Stock re-rates to 8-9x forward EPS on reduced earnings and risk premium

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Consumer sentiment stabilizes above 55 and/or oil retreats below $90**, reducing recession probability below 35%. This would validate the FY2026 guidance range and restore confidence in travel demand.
2. **Q1 2026 results (expected May 2026) confirm 11-13% revenue growth guidance** with stable or improving B2C trends. B2B bookings sustaining 20%+ growth would further support.
3. **Stock declines to $190-200 (~10x FY2026E EPS)**, providing a margin of safety that compensates for macro risk.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Q1 2026 revenue misses guidance by >300bps** or management withdraws/reduces FY2026 guidance, signaling demand deterioration.
2. **B2B growth decelerates below 10%** for two consecutive quarters, undermining the structural growth thesis.
3. **Credit downgrade from investment grade** or refinancing at spreads >300bps above current rates.

### Reassessment Timeline
Q1 2026 earnings (expected May 2026). This is the first reporting period that will capture the current macro regime. It will show whether the blockade-era consumer is still booking travel, or whether the sentiment collapse has translated to booking cancellations.

## Conclusion

Expedia's operational transformation is validated by the numbers: B2B growth, margin expansion, and FCF generation are all strong. At ~12x forward earnings, the stock is inexpensive relative to its own history and to less-cyclical travel peers. The 4-for-4 quarterly beat streak and 7.1% upward estimate revision over 90 days provide fundamental support.

However, the macro environment has deteriorated materially since the February earnings report. Record-low consumer sentiment, rising oil and gasoline prices, and elevated recession probability all directly threaten the demand assumptions embedded in FY2026 guidance. The B2C segment (64% of revenue) grew only 2% even in a functioning economy.

The correct posture is to hold existing positions and wait for Q1 2026 results to reveal whether the operational improvements can offset the macro headwinds. Adding exposure here means underwriting a travel demand forecast that the market has not yet stress-tested against the current geopolitical and consumer environment.
