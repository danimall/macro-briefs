## Executive Summary

EchoStar Corporation at $124.64 represents one of the most unusual — and risky — equity situations in public markets today. The company's investment case rests almost entirely on the value of a ~2%+ SpaceX equity stake (estimated at $31-40B+ depending on SpaceX's IPO valuation) and $8.5B+ in cash from a $40B spectrum sale approved by the FCC in May 2026. Against this sits $22B in debt, $12.36B in current liabilities, structurally declining operating businesses losing subscribers every quarter, and a $2.4B FCC-mandated escrow plus $3.5B in Crown Castle litigation.

The stock has appreciated ~493% from its 52-week low of $14.90 to its current $124.64, driven primarily by the SpaceX proxy narrative. With SpaceX's direct IPO expected mid-June 2026, this proxy rationale faces an explicit expiration date. Every senior executive who could sell did sell in March 2026, totaling $15.5M in dispositions with zero open-market purchases. The company skipped its Q1 2026 earnings conference call. The current ratio of 0.42 indicates near-term solvency depends on deal closings proceeding without delay.

At a market cap of ~$19.8B and trailing losses of -$50/share (driven by $17.6B in impairments acknowledging the failed 5G buildout), this is a binary outcome stock where the bull case requires everything to work — SpaceX IPOs at $2T+, escrow/litigation resolves favorably, debt gets refinanced — while the bear case has multiple independent failure paths.

## Company Overview

EchoStar is a telecommunications conglomerate formed through the 2023 merger of EchoStar Communications and DISH Network. The combined entity operates across three segments:

**Pay-TV (DISH TV & Sling TV):** 7.0 million total subscribers (5.02M satellite, 1.98M streaming), declining by ~800K annually. Revenue ~$11B but shrinking as cord-cutting accelerates.

**Wireless/Broadband:** 7.51 million wireless subscribers (essentially flat) and 739K broadband subscribers (declining). The greenfield 5G network buildout has been effectively abandoned, with the spectrum sold to SpaceX and AT&T.

**Satellite Services/Spectrum:** The company's crown jewel — extensive AWS-3 and AWS-4 spectrum holdings that were sold to SpaceX and AT&T for $40B total. EchoStar receives ~$8.5B in cash, $11.1B+ in SpaceX equity (~2%+ ownership at $212/share), and SpaceX assumes $2B in interest payments through late 2027.

Chairman/CEO Charlie Ergen controls the company through super-voting Class B shares, making this effectively a controlled entity.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story at EchoStar. Every operating segment is in decline:

- Pay-TV subscribers: -803K in 2025
- Wireless subscribers: -9K in Q4 2025 (vs. +90K year-ago)
- Broadband subscribers: declining
- Revenue: declining ~5% annually

The "growth" thesis is purely an asset monetization story. If the SpaceX stake appreciates from an IPO at $1.75T to $2.5T+, the value of EchoStar's ~2%+ stake grows proportionally. But this is not operating growth — it's financial engineering and asset liquidation.

Estimate revisions show massive positive momentum: current year EPS revised from -$3.61 (90 days ago) to +$1.71 today. This reflects the spectrum deal closing and debt reduction flowing through to EPS, not operating improvement. The volatility of estimates (next quarter revised from $8.10 to $0.78 over 90 days) reflects the lumpy, non-recurring nature of these gains.

**Bull Case SOTP:**

- Cash from spectrum: $8.5B = ~$54/share
- AT&T spectrum proceeds: additional billions

**Bear Case SOTP:**

**vs. Peers:** T-Mobile trades at 20.3x P/E with positive growth trajectories. GOGO at 43x. No peer comparison is meaningful because no peer has this asset structure.

## Competitive Landscape

EchoStar's competitive position in its operating businesses is weak and deteriorating:

**Pay-TV:** DISH is a distant #2 behind DirecTV in satellite, and Sling is a minor player in streaming. ESPN Unlimited and Fox One launched in August 2025, further fragmenting the sports content that was DISH's retention moat. The business is in terminal decline.

**Wireless:** The company effectively conceded it cannot compete against T-Mobile, AT&T, and Verizon by selling its spectrum rather than building a network. The 7.51M subscriber base has no clear path to growth without spectrum or network investment.

**Spectrum Value:** Here, EchoStar's position was strong — the AWS-3/AWS-4 holdings proved worth $40B. But this is a one-time monetization event, not a sustainable competitive advantage.

The company's future competitive positioning depends entirely on what emerges post-spectrum-sale: a smaller, more focused entity harvesting declining pay-TV and wireless for cash flow, or potentially pivoting into something new with the proceeds. Management has provided minimal guidance on the go-forward strategy.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **SpaceX IPO Proxy Dissolution (HIGH PROBABILITY, NEAR-TERM):** The SpaceX IPO is expected mid-June 2026. Once investors can own SpaceX directly, EchoStar loses its primary bid driver. The stock's ~493% appreciation from its 52-week low is substantially attributable to this proxy dynamic. When investors can buy SpaceX directly, why own it through a heavily indebted, declining-business wrapper? Selling pressure could be acute.

2. **Solvency Risk:** Current ratio of 0.42. If spectrum deal proceeds are delayed by litigation (Crown Castle's $3.5B claim, $2.4B escrow requirement), the company faces a genuine liquidity crisis within 6-12 months.

3. **Litigation/Escrow Overhang:** $2.4B FCC-mandated escrow + Crown Castle seeking $3.5B + potential other 5G contractor claims. Total contingent liabilities could consume $6-8B of proceeds, dramatically reducing equity value.

4. **SpaceX Stake Lockup/Illiquidity:** The ~$11.1B in SpaceX equity may face lockup restrictions post-IPO (typically 180 days). EchoStar cannot monetize this stake quickly, while debt service continues.

5. **Short Interest at 36.2%:** While this creates squeeze potential (explaining recent volatility), it also reflects well-informed investors betting against the equity. Days to cover of 6.7 means any momentum reversal is amplified.

6. **Universal Insider Selling:** $15.5M sold in March 2026 by three C-suite executives. Zero purchases. Ergen transferring Class B shares to GRATs for estate planning. The people with the most information are reducing exposure.

### Options Market Signal

The unusual activity in $125 calls (4.9x Vol/OI) suggests near-term directional interest, but the flat term structure indicates uncertainty extends well beyond the next few weeks. The market is pricing ±33% over 6 months — an enormous range consistent with a binary outcome stock.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** SpaceX IPO (expected mid-June 2026). Once SpaceX trades directly, EchoStar loses its sole rationale as a proxy vehicle. Secondary catalyst: any litigation ruling favoring Crown Castle ($3.5B) or FCC complications with the $2.4B escrow.

**Timing window:** 2-6 weeks. The SpaceX IPO is the nearest concrete catalyst. Q2 earnings (likely August) could provide second wave of selling if subscriber losses accelerate and no conference call is held.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 36.2% with 6.7 days to cover is EXTREMELY high. This is the dominant risk for shorts. The crowded nature of the trade means any positive catalyst (SpaceX IPO priced above expectations, litigation resolution, short squeeze dynamics) could generate violent upside moves. Recent price action ($147 high → $124 current) suggests some squeeze potential has already dissipated.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options are relatively cheap (IV < HV). P/C OI ratio of 0.33 indicates bullish options positioning. The options market is NOT confirming the bearish thesis — call buyers dominate. This divergence is a warning signal for shorts.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Base case downside to $80-110 (12-36% lower). Upside risk to $150+ on squeeze (20%+ higher). At current price, risk/reward is approximately 1.5:1 to 2:1 on the downside, but squeeze risk makes the distribution fat-tailed to the upside. Effective risk/reward accounting for squeeze probability is closer to 1:1.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT** — The thesis is directionally correct (proxy premium dissolves post-SpaceX IPO, insiders selling, businesses declining), but 36.2% short interest with 6.7 days to cover creates unacceptable squeeze risk. Additionally, the options market's bullish positioning suggests momentum traders could sustain the bid through the SpaceX IPO event. The trade was correct at $140-147; at $124, much of the easy money has been made on the short side. Better to simply avoid owning than to actively short at this level.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** Consider re-evaluating if (a) stock trades below $60/share, where sum-of-parts floor might provide asymmetric upside, (b) SpaceX IPO occurs and stock stabilizes post-rotation at a level where SpaceX stake value clearly exceeds enterprise value plus all contingent liabilities, or (c) Crown Castle litigation resolves for <$1.5B and escrow is released without material reduction.

**Timeline:** Expect headwinds to persist for 3-6 months post-SpaceX IPO as proxy holders rotate out and the market reprices EchoStar on its own fundamentals (declining businesses, massive debt, uncertain litigation outcomes).

## Conclusion

EchoStar at $124.64 is a stock where the market has already priced in a favorable resolution of multiple binary uncertainties. The SpaceX stake is real and potentially worth multiples of the current stock price — but so is the $22B in debt, $6-8B in potential litigation/escrow claims, and the structural decline of every operating segment. Insiders selling universally at $107-114 in March (below current price) is the clearest signal available about management's own assessment of fair value.

The imminent SpaceX IPO creates a specific, dated catalyst for selling pressure as proxy holders rotate. At 36.2% short interest, the stock could move violently in either direction. For investors seeking SpaceX exposure, waiting to buy SpaceX directly eliminates the debt, litigation, and declining-business risks that EchoStar bundles in.

The appropriate posture is to avoid this equity entirely until the SpaceX IPO resolves the proxy dynamic and the market establishes a new clearing price based on fundamentals rather than narrative. The risk/reward at $124 is unfavorable given the number of independent failure modes and the limited additional upside relative to what's already priced in.
