## Executive Summary

HSBC's ADRs have risen 64.8% over the past twelve months, from a $62.73 low to $103.35, and sit 4.2% below the $107.92 fifty-two-week high. The operating improvement behind that move is real but narrower than the headlines suggest. H1 2026 profit before tax of $19.5bn was up 23% on $15.8bn, revenue rose 11% to $37.7bn, net interest margin improved to 1.61%, customer lending grew $34bn, and total wealth assets crossed $1trn with net new money up 32%. Management raised 2026 banking net interest income guidance to ≥$46bn and reaffirmed ≥17% return on tangible equity, excluding notable items, in each of 2026, 2027 and 2028.

Three things temper this. First, decomposing the reported aggregates puts Q1 2026 PBT at roughly $9.4bn against roughly $9.5bn in Q1 2025 — approximately flat. The entire first-half improvement came in Q2, against a Q2 2025 quarter that itself missed consensus at $6.3bn. Two independent secondary summaries of the same H1 release cite growth of ~6% and ~13% rather than +23%, most plausibly on constant-currency and/or ex-notable-items bases; the NIM improvement was explicitly described as helped by foreign exchange translation. The organic growth rate is likely closer to half the headline. Second, capital return contracted sharply: buybacks of up to $3bn were announced at both Q1 and Q2 2025 versus up to $1bn at H1 2026, equal to 0.28% of the $354bn market capitalisation. CET1 fell 0.8pp to 14.1% following the $13.6bn purchase of Hang Seng Bank's 37% minority, which completed in January 2026. Third, valuation. On our derived tangible-equity estimate of roughly $158.6bn (from FY2025 reported RoTE of 13.3% and roughly $21.1bn attributable), the shares trade near 2.2x tangible book. A Gordon-growth framework at 17% RoTE, 3% growth and a 10% cost of equity justifies 2.00x; at an 11% cost of equity, 1.75x. Sustaining 2.2x requires RoTE near 18.4%, above the guided floor.

The result is a business we are happy to own and unwilling to add to. On forward earnings the stock is not expensive — consensus of $8.60 per ADR for 2026 and $9.47 for 2027 puts it at 12.0x and 10.9x — but consensus is thin (two contributors) and has been cut 2.3% and 4.2% respectively over thirty days. Sell-side positioning is unambiguous: four rating or target actions in the week of the H1 print with no Buy, Citi downgrading to Neutral, BNP Paribas to Underperform, and JPMorgan's target roughly 8% below the London price. The ADR consensus median target of $102 sits below spot. We rate HSBC HOLD on income grounds: a ~3.2-3.6% distribution plus tangible book compounding at roughly 8-9% (17% RoTE at a ~50% payout) supports existing exposure, while a decade-high multiple, a live UK bank-levy debate and increased 100% loss absorption on Hong Kong commercial property leave no cushion for new capital.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1865, HSBC Holdings plc is Europe's largest bank by assets, the London Stock Exchange's most valuable listed company at approximately £274bn, and employs 211,479 people. Following the reorganisation implemented from October 2024, it reports four operating segments: Hong Kong (including Hang Seng Bank); UK (retail and wealth including *first direct* and *M&S Bank*, UK Commercial Banking, HSBC Innovation Bank); Corporate and Institutional Banking; and International Wealth and Premier Banking.

The strategic direction is deliberate narrowing — exits from Canada, France, Argentina, New Zealand and South Africa, with disposal gains still flowing through reported earnings as recently as Q4 2025. The remaining franchise is a hard-currency wholesale and trade-finance business layered over a Hong Kong and UK deposit base, with a cross-border wealth engine as the designated growth vehicle. That combination is not diversifying in the way a geographic map implies: the dollar-funded trade franchise and the Hong Kong retail and property book are two expressions of the same Asian trade-and-capital-flows bet.

A structural note on evidence quality. HSBC is a UK-domiciled foreign private issuer. It files a Form 20-F annually (FY2025, filed 26 February 2026) and 6-Ks for interim results, not 10-Ks, 10-Qs, 8-Ks, proxies or Form 4s. Consequently there is no XBRL data, no Altman or Piotroski screen (both structurally inapplicable to a deposit-funded bank in any case), and no SEC-filed insider transaction record — HSBC directors file UK PDMR notifications. The financial figures below derive from secondary reporting of HSBC's own results releases, cross-checked across independent outlets. Directions and magnitudes are well supported; precise decimals await the 20-F and the 4 August 6-K. Governance — compensation, board composition, related-party transactions, insider activity — is substantially unexamined, and we have scored management accordingly rather than assuming the best.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was a low-quality year.** Profit before tax fell 7.4% to $29.91bn (against $28.86bn of company-compiled consensus) on revenue up 4% to $68.27bn. Reported RoTE was 13.3%. Q4 2025 PBT of $6.8bn was up $4.5bn year on year, implying roughly $2.3bn in Q4 2024, "largely due to favorable one-off items linked to business disposals," with Q4 revenue up 42% to $16.4bn and Q4 operating expenses up 8% to $9.3bn. Backing out the quarters: 9M 2025 PBT was $23.11bn and Q3 2025 roughly $7.3bn. A soft first three quarters were rescued at the headline by disposals.

One definitional caution matters for judging the gap to target: the 13.3% is *reported* RoTE, while the ≥17% guidance is *excluding notable items*. Part of the apparent 370bp gap is definitional rather than operational. We were unable to establish FY2025 RoTE ex-notables, which is the correct bridge, and we flag this as a genuine gap in the analysis rather than a rounding issue.

**H1 2026 improved materially, with the improvement concentrated in one quarter.** Q2 PBT was $10.1bn (+60%) on Q2 revenue of $19.1bn (+16%). The derived effective tax rate is 21.5% ($4.2bn of tax on the half's PBT). Expected credit losses rose 20% to $2.4bn, including provisions tied to Hong Kong commercial real estate, additional allowances for the Middle East conflict, and a $400m fraud-related securitisation exposure in the UK. Subtracting Q2 from the half gives Q1 2026 PBT of roughly $9.4bn against roughly $9.5bn in Q1 2025 — flat to marginally down, corroborated by contemporaneous commentary referencing a Q1 profit miss. The +60% Q2 growth rate is substantially a base effect against a Q2 2025 that missed on both profit and revenue with opex up 10%. That does not make $10.1bn of quarterly PBT a poor result — AJ Bell's Russ Mould called it the best HSBC quarter he can find — but it is not a run-rate.

**Capital.** CET1 of 14.1% at 30 June 2026 is down 0.8pp from December, the dominant identified driver being the January Hang Seng settlement. The decline also reflects risk-weighted asset growth from lending and FX; we cannot attribute the 0.8pp precisely. Fourteen-point-one percent is a comfortable absolute level for a G-SIB, but we believe it may sit toward the low end of management's operating range. We could not confirm this (management's target range was not established in the sources), which limits inference about distribution headroom. The bank launched a $5bn debt offering to fund a bond buyback, confirming unimpaired wholesale access.

**Capital return is the clearest signal in the print.** The up-to-$1bn buyback, to complete before Q3 results, is roughly a one-sixth run-rate versus the $3bn announced at each of Q1 and Q2 2025. The second interim dividend was maintained at $0.10 per ordinary share. The $13.6bn Hang Seng cash outlay pre-committed roughly four quarters of buyback capacity; capital rather than earnings is the 2026 constraint on distributions. The alternative reading — that management is deliberately rebuilding buffer ahead of RWA growth and UK fiscal uncertainty, with a larger programme resuming at Q3 or full year — is equally consistent with the evidence and is the single most tractable near-term catalyst either way.

**Guidance:** RoTE ≥17% ex-notables in 2026, 2027 and 2028; banking NII ≥$46bn in 2026 (raised, attributed to a more favourable rate environment); year-on-year revenue growth through 2028; ~50% dividend payout; ECL ~45bp of average customer loans; operating expense growth ~1% in 2026. Q2 opex grew 2%, running slightly ahead of the full-year guide, possibly on timing, and worth watching.

**Reported data conflicts we resolve explicitly.** The supplied profile shows trailing and forward P/E of 84.44 against EPS of $1.20. That is an ADR-versus-ordinary-share mismatch: $1.20 is per ordinary share, and one ADR represents five ordinary shares. On ADR EPS of approximately $6.16 the trailing multiple is 16.8x. Similarly, the 1.93% dividend yield in the profile does not reconcile with a ~50% payout on $6.16 of ADR earnings; the plausible range on declared interims is roughly 3.0-3.6%, and a $3.73 trailing figure would imply a 60.6% payout against the stated ~50% target and does not reconcile with plausible declared interims. We treat the forward yield as approximately 3.2-3.6% and unconfirmed at the decimal.

**Score: 6.3/10.** Strong absolute capital, a conservative funding profile, 21.5% tax rate, and demonstrated market access. Offset by CET1 possibly near the low end of its range, buyback capacity consumed by an acquisition, ECL rising 20% with three distinct new provision drivers, a $400m single-name fraud loss indicating a structured-lending control gap, and two consecutive years in which headline earnings were flattered by non-operating items (disposals in 2025, FX in 2026).

## Growth Analysis

The credible engine is wealth. The $1trn wealth-asset milestone and +32% net new money represent a fee-based, capital-light stream layered onto an existing Hong Kong and UK deposit franchise, targeted at cross-border Chinese wealth against UBS and Standard Chartered. If this proves durable, it justifies a structurally higher multiple than HSBC's history, because fee income at low marginal capital intensity mechanically lifts RoTE. We have no comparative net-new-money data for UBS or Standard Chartered and therefore cannot verify share gain, only growth.

Against that, the balance of evidence points to mid-single-digit organic growth. FY2025 revenue growth was 3-4% on either revenue definition (HSBC's $68.27bn or an aggregator's $63.22bn — the levels differ by definition, the growth rates agree). Q1 2026 was flat year on year. Two secondary summaries of the H1 release cite ~6% and ~13% growth versus the headline figures, and the NIM improvement was explicitly FX-aided. Our hypothesis is that the lower figures are constant-currency and/or ex-notable-items; we cannot verify the bridge, and we therefore decline to use +23% as an organic input.

Consensus agrees the pace moderates. Next-quarter consensus of $2.05 per ADR is below the $2.27 current quarter, and full-year 2026 of $8.60 against H1 actuals of roughly $4.40 implies a weaker second half. Thirty-day revisions are negative on both years (-2.3% and -4.2%), even though ninety-day revisions remain modestly positive (+1.1%, +1.8%). The earnings surprise record is unremarkable: two in-line quarters in 2026 after 11.0% and 4.8% beats in the back half of 2025. Visibility is adequate; the cushion has gone.

Much of the 2026 move is sector, not company. European bank indices sit near record highs, UK banks had a strong week on high interest rates and market turbulence, and Asian peers reported the same wealth tailwind. HSBC is being treated as a read-through for the European bank complex. Sector-driven multiple expansion is the component most likely to reverse if policy rates fall, which is precisely the exposure the ≥$46bn banking NII guide embeds. The reported dependence of both the NII guide and the recent NIM improvement on a favourable rate and FX environment makes this the most tractable macro risk to the growth case, though the sources do not establish a specific rate-path or timing.

**Score: 5.5/10.** A genuine, differentiated wealth growth engine and raised NII guidance, set against low-single-digit underlying revenue growth, a flat Q1, FX-flattered reported figures, negative thirty-day estimate revisions, and consensus modelling second-half deceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

Two lenses give different answers, and the divergence is the crux.

**On earnings, the stock is not expensive.** At $103.35 against consensus of $8.60 (2026) and $9.47 (2027) per ADR, the multiples are 12.0x and 10.9x, with a ~3.2-3.6% yield. Trailing is 16.8x on $6.16. For a bank guiding to 17%+ RoTE that is defensible in absolute terms. (We have no verified peer-multiple benchmark in the supplied data, so we do not assert a specific comparison to US large-cap or UK domestic banks.) A 2026 estimate of $8.60 per ADR implies roughly $29.5bn attributable, or approximately 18.6% RoTE on our derived tangible-equity base — above the guided floor. Either the estimate is optimistic, our tangible-equity figure is too low, or the ≥17% guide is conservative. Each is possible; the ambiguity is itself a reason not to lean hard on the forward multiple.

**On book value, it is priced for full delivery.** Reconstructing from the RoTE identity — FY2025 reported RoTE of 13.3% on roughly $21.1bn attributable — implies average tangible equity of approximately $158.6bn, sanity-checked against FY2024 (roughly $22.9bn attributable at ~14.6% RoTE implies ~$157bn). Against a $354.19bn market capitalisation that is roughly 2.2x tangible book, or 2.2-2.4x on a $150-160bn band. This is a derived rather than a disclosed figure and the whole conclusion is sensitive to it; if actual tangible equity is materially higher, the multiple is lower and the case softens. Independent corroboration of the direction exists: a February 2026 third-party note flagged a decade-high 1.7x price-to-*book* (a structurally lower multiple than price-to-tangible-book) and rated the shares Hold, before a further 30%-plus advance.

Reverse-engineering with the standard bank framework, justified P/TBV = (RoTE − g) ÷ (CoE − g):

| RoTE | g | CoE | Justified P/TBV |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17% | 3% | 10% | **2.00x** |
| 17% | 3% | 11% | **1.75x** |
| 18.4% | 3% | 10% | **2.20x** |

The market is paying for RoTE near 18.4% at a benign 10% cost of equity. Framed on earnings, 17% RoTE on $158.6bn tangible equity is roughly $26.9bn attributable, or 13.2x the current market capitalisation — a 7.6% earnings yield with a 50% payout implying a 3.8% dividend at target. Reasonable, not compelling, for a bank carrying Hong Kong property, UK fiscal and Middle East tails.

**External positioning corroborates.** Four sell-side actions in the week of the print with no Buy: Citi downgraded to Neutral (target cut to 1,570p) citing the 40% year-to-date move; BNP Paribas downgraded to Underperform; Morgan Stanley raised to 1,674p at Equal Weight; JPMorgan raised to 1,450p at Neutral, roughly 8% *below* the market. The ADR consensus is Hold with a $106.08 mean and a $102.00 *median* — below spot — on only three analysts, and the recommendation mix moved this month to 1 buy / 1 hold / 1 strong sell. We disregard a third-party quant model showing 91% overvaluation; mechanical historical-multiple models penalise any structural re-rating by construction, and this one does not incorporate the RoTE step-up. Its direction is nonetheless consistent with the P/TBV work.

We do not treat proximity to analyst targets as independent proof, but when the median target sits below the market price and two houses have downgraded in the same week the print was released, that is a positioning fact worth naming.

**Score: 4.5/10.** Forward earnings look reasonable; tangible book is at a decade-high multiple that already capitalises full guidance delivery. Margin of safety is minimal and the asymmetry runs to the downside on any RoTE shortfall, rate reversal or UK levy. This is a fully-valued rather than a cheap stock, and it should not be scored as one.

## Competitive Landscape

HSBC's differentiator is scale in cross-border wealth transfer and payments — a franchise built on Hong Kong deposit primacy, a UK retail and commercial base, and a dollar-clearing wholesale network. The January 2026 full acquisition of Hang Seng consolidates the Hong Kong position and, per a single-source Reuters report, is being followed by cross-bank leadership over seven shared corporate functions, which is where synergies would have to originate. Morningstar's read is that revenue and cost synergies come "gradually in the medium term," which is a measured way of saying the $13.6bn cash cost was immediate and the benefit is not.

Trajectory by segment is mixed. Wealth is improving on the evidence available. CIB competes against US investment banks with superior scale in markets and advisory, and HSBC is tightening pay in exactly that division. UK retail faces domestic competition and now a fiscal overhang. The market-exit programme improves returns on capital while shrinking the network effect that underpins the cross-border proposition. Net: modest improvement concentrated in one division, stable elsewhere, with no verified share-gain data.

**Score: 6.0/10.**

## Risk Assessment

**UK windfall tax or bank levy — the most concrete unpriced threat.** Press reporting in the week of the print describes growing calls for a levy on bank profits under the new Prime Minister, and CEO Georges Elhedery publicly warning that an extra levy would damage growth. No legislation exists; this is policy speculation. But a CEO lobbying in public implies a non-trivial perceived probability, and at the current valuation with earnings at cyclical highs, a levy would compress both the earnings numerator and the multiple. HSBC has absorbed UK levies and surcharges before without existential damage, and the UK is one of four segments; this is an earnings and sentiment risk, not a solvency one.

**Hong Kong commercial real estate, with loss absorption now at 100%.** Before January 2026, roughly 37% of Hang Seng's credit losses were economically borne by minority shareholders below the attributable line; HSBC now absorbs all of it. Because Hang Seng was already fully consolidated for accounting purposes, this increase in economic exposure is invisible in consolidated financial statements and would be missed by a standard screen. Reporting at the time of the announcement placed it "amid worsening property loans in Hong Kong." We make no inference about management motive from that timing — the correlation is documented, causation is not established, and buying a minority at a cyclical low could prove well-timed. We have not been able to size Hong Kong CRE balances or coverage ratios.

**Middle East.** HSBC runs a substantial MENA franchise and a large trade-finance book. A Strait of Hormuz closure would hit trade volumes, regional credit quality and oil-linked corporate exposures simultaneously, and the surrounding news environment (Hormuz shipping and oil-price headlines in the same week) remains unsettled. We have not sized HSBC's specific MENA exposure.

**Rate and FX reversal.** The ≥$46bn banking NII guide is explicitly predicated on a favourable rate environment, and the NIM improvement was FX-aided. A stronger dollar mechanically reverses part of reported revenue growth without any operational deterioration, and shrinks dollar-reported wealth AUM. Both drivers are exogenous, and Invezz's stated key risk is a sharp drop in interest rates or credit losses forcing profit and buyback expectations down.

**Operational control.** The $400m fraud-related securitisation exposure in the UK is roughly 2% of half-year PBT, but it is a single-name control failure rather than a credit-cycle loss, and it raises questions about structured-lending underwriting. Reported by a single secondary outlet; counterparty and recovery prospects unknown.

**Execution and retention.** Management targets an ~8% payroll cost reduction and a net 15% cut in managing director positions, with reporting that minimal or zero bonuses would be used to push out underperformers specifically in investment banking and wealth management — the two divisions that must deliver the revenue underpinning ≥17% RoTE. The Group COO role has changed hands.

**Disclosure asymmetry.** As a foreign private issuer HSBC reports semi-annually in depth, files no US proxy, and provides no SEC-filed insider data. US investors receive less granular standardised disclosure than for a domestic peer, and standard screening tools fail outright. This is a real if unglamorous risk for ADR holders, and it is the reason several of our inputs are derived rather than disclosed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.2% against 27.7% one-year realised is fair value (spread -1.5%) at the 31st percentile of the current chain, with a flat term structure (26.2% near versus 27.5% far); the market prices no concentrated near-term event and no regime change. Positioning is more informative than the level: put/call open interest of 1.78 with a 12.6% near-term put skew indicates elevated put demand held in size, while the put/call *volume* ratio of 0.19 shows little fresh urgency. The three-month tenor is the exception, with a 3.06 volume ratio and a modestly negative skew reading. Short interest of 5.48m shares at 4.9 days to cover is unremarkable. Read together with the sell-side downgrades, this is protection accumulated against a fully-valued position rather than an expression of imminent distress, consistent with our HOLD and a reason not to characterise the setup as an actionable short either.

**Score: 4.3/10.** Multiple specific, live, currently unquantified downside catalysts (UK levy, Hong Kong CRE with elevated loss absorption, Hormuz), thin capital headroom, and no valuation cushion to absorb any of them.

## Management Quality

Elhedery is executing a coherent simplification agenda with visible pace: market exits, a cost programme, a Tokyo headquarters sale, and functional integration of Hang Seng. The compensation reset toward a performance-driven model improves pay-for-performance alignment in principle.

The capital allocation record is the open question. HSBC paid $13.6bn — a 33% premium to Hang Seng's 30-day average — for a 37% minority in a subsidiary it already controlled, on the stated rationale that this was "a better use of capital than buybacks." That claim is testable but unproven, and the immediate, measurable consequences were a CET1 ratio 0.8pp lower, a buyback cut to roughly one-sixth of the prior run-rate, and a negative market reaction to the $1bn announcement. Synergies are expected only gradually over the medium term. HSBC now absorbs 100% of the credit losses in the asset class that was deteriorating at the time of the deal. The dividend was maintained; operating expenses are running slightly ahead of the ~1% guide.

We have no insider transaction data, no CEO compensation figures, no pay-versus-performance or board composition data, and no related-party disclosure — HSBC files no US proxy and its insiders file UK PDMR notices. Governance is unexamined rather than clean, and we score it as unexamined.

**Score: 5.3/10.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $120 per ADR (+16.1%)

Management delivers ≥17% RoTE ex-notables in 2026 and reaffirms for 2027-28. The wealth franchise compounds from $1trn with net new money above 20%, and the market accepts it as a structurally higher-quality, lower-beta earnings stream deserving a permanently higher multiple — a view the 0.58 beta suggests is already forming. Policy rates stay higher for longer, delivering the ≥$46bn banking NII guide with upside. Hang Seng synergies emerge faster than Morningstar's medium-term framing, CET1 rebuilds toward the middle of its range on retained earnings, and a materially larger buyback resumes at Q3 or full-year results. No UK levy is enacted. On roughly $170bn of tangible equity by end-2026, 2.4x P/TBV supports $408bn of market capitalisation, or approximately $119 per ADR — equivalently 12.7x 2027 consensus EPS of $9.47.

### Bear Case — $80 per ADR (-22.6%)

A UK sector levy is enacted, taking a visible bite from UK segment earnings and resetting the multiple the market will pay for a bank with cyclical-peak returns. Hong Kong commercial property provisions escalate against 100% loss absorption, pushing ECL above the ~45bp guide. Rate cuts or dollar strength reverse the FX and NII tailwinds, exposing an organic growth rate nearer 6% than the headline. RoTE settles at 15-16% ex-notables rather than ≥17%, and the multiple compresses toward 1.7x tangible book — the decade-high on price-to-book that a third party flagged six months and 30% ago. On $165bn of tangible equity, 1.7x gives $280bn, or roughly $82 per ADR. We round to $80.

### Base Case — $105 per ADR (+1.6%)

RoTE lands at or slightly above 17% ex-notables. Organic revenue growth runs 4-6% with FX contributing the remainder of the reported figure. ECL comes in near the 45bp guide with Hong Kong CRE a persistent drag rather than an accident. The buyback resumes at a larger but not 2025-scale cadence as CET1 rebuilds. No UK levy in the next twelve months, but the debate persists and caps the multiple. Tangible equity compounds to roughly $165bn (17% RoTE, ~50% retention, less the Hang Seng premium effect); at 2.15x that is $355bn, or approximately $103-106 per ADR. We set $105, equivalent to 11.1x 2027 consensus of $9.47.

**Probability-weighted:** 55% base (+1.6%), 25% bull (+16.1%), 20% bear (-22.6%) = +0.4% price return, plus a ~3.3% distribution = approximately +3.7% total return over twelve months. That is below a reasonable 9-10% cost of equity for the risk taken and is the arithmetic reason this is a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE. It is above zero, and the distribution plus book compounding gives existing holders a defensible reason to stay, which is why it is not a REDUCE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the first decision point at Q3 2026 results.

**Targets from $103.35:**
- **Base: $105** (+1.6%) — 2.15x P/TBV on ~$165bn tangible equity; 11.1x 2027 consensus $9.47
- **Bull: $120** (+16.1%) — 2.4x P/TBV; 12.7x 2027 consensus
- **Bear: $80** (-22.6%) — 1.7x P/TBV on ~$165bn tangible equity

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A Q3 or full-year buyback authorisation of $2.5bn or more alongside CET1 rebuilding toward 14.5%, demonstrating that the Hang Seng outlay was a timing constraint rather than a structural one.
2. Disclosed RoTE excluding notable items at or above 17% for two consecutive reporting periods, together with a disclosed TNAV per share confirming tangible equity materially above our derived $158.6bn (which would mechanically lower the 2.2x multiple).
3. A price near $85-88 — roughly 1.8x our derived tangible book — which would restore a double-digit expected return under the base case.
4. Definitive resolution of the UK levy question with no material charge, removing the multiple cap.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Enactment or formal proposal of a UK bank windfall levy with a quantified group cost above roughly $1.5bn annually.
2. ECL guidance raised above 55bp, or Hong Kong CRE provisions accelerating sequentially for two quarters.
3. Banking NII guidance for 2027 set below $46bn, or an explicit withdrawal of the ≥17% RoTE commitment for any of 2026-28.
4. Consensus 2027 EPS cut below $8.75 per ADR (a further ~7.6% from the current $9.47), which would break the forward-multiple support entirely.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results — the buyback size and the CET1 trajectory are the two disclosures that most efficiently resolve the capital-constraint question. We would also revisit immediately on the FY2025 20-F becoming readable, since a disclosed TNAV per share and RoTE ex-notables would replace two of our derived inputs and could move the valuation score in either direction.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** HSBC is a better bank than it was two years ago: narrower, more profitable, with a genuinely differentiated cross-border wealth franchise. H1 2026 PBT and the raised banking NII guide are real, and the reported dependence of that guide on a favourable rate environment is worth watching.

The problem is the price. Twelve months of 64.8% appreciation have taken the shares to roughly 2.2x our derived tangible book — a level that requires RoTE near 18.4% at a 10% cost of equity, above the guided floor — while the underlying growth rate is probably nearer 6% than the 23% headline, Q1 2026 was flat year on year, capital return has fallen to 0.28% of market capitalisation, thirty-day estimate revisions are negative on both forecast years, the median ADR target sits below spot, and two houses downgraded the stock in the week the results were released. Set against a live UK windfall-tax debate that the CEO is publicly lobbying against, and a Hang Seng transaction that raised loss absorption on Hong Kong commercial property from 63% to 100% in a way consolidated accounts do not reveal, the risk is asymmetric to the downside.

Existing holders are being paid roughly 3.2-3.6% while tangible book compounds at mid-to-high single digits, and that combination justifies maintaining exposure. New capital has no margin of safety at $103.35. We would add toward $85-88, or on a Q3 buyback and CET1 trajectory that demonstrates the distribution constraint has lifted.

Several inputs here are derived rather than disclosed — most importantly tangible book value per share, the organic growth rate, and the forward dividend — and we have flagged each where it bears on the conclusion. The recommendation does not require any of those estimates to be precisely right, only that the shares are somewhere near fair value rather than materially below it.
