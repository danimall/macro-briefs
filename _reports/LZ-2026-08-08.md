## Executive Summary

LegalZoom sells business formation transactions and then converts those buyers into annual subscriptions — registered agent, compliance filings, attorney advice, virtual mail. Subscription is now 65% of revenue, cash is collected up front (deferred revenue $221.4M), the balance sheet carries zero funded debt against $167.2M of cash, and reported free cash flow was $147.9M in FY2025 on $756.0M of revenue. At $823M of enterprise value that is 4.8x adjusted EBITDA and 5.6x reported free cash flow. On those numbers the stock screens as one of the cheapest cash-generative internet businesses in the small-cap universe.

The Q2 2026 filing undermines the numbers that produce that screen. Transaction revenue fell 1% year over year. Business formations fell 5% to 125K. Subscription units fell 3% to 1,892K, and 1% sequentially. Annual small business retention slipped to ~57% from ~58%, which management attributes to its own bundling strategy lowering the quality of the subscription base. Revenue still grew 6.6%, but the growth came from a 5% ARPU increase driven by registered agent price increases on a shrinking unit base. The deferred-revenue build, the closest available proxy for new and renewed bookings, was $17.5M in H1 2026 versus $29.6M in H1 2025, 41% smaller. Guidance reported on August 5 of $795-805M for FY2026 implies second-half revenue growth of +0.7% to +3.3% against +9.7% in the first half. The mechanism is disclosed in the company's own risk language: Google's prioritization of AI-generated summaries has reduced organic traffic and pushed spend into paid search, with the filing adding that because AI overviews cite multiple sources, "any individual publisher's impact may be diluted, diminishing brand recognition." That shows up in the P&L as customer acquisition marketing dollars per formation rising from $357 to $421, up 18%, while formations fell.

The stock fell roughly 30% over August 5-6 on a quarter where non-GAAP EPS of $0.16 beat a $0.15 consensus. The market repriced terminal value, not the current quarter, and on the evidence available that repricing is defensible rather than an error. Offsetting it: stock compensation is falling fast (10.9% of revenue in H1 2026 versus 16.1%), a 13% workforce reduction was committed in August, $80.4M of buyback authorization remains (8.1% of market cap), net cash is 16.9% of the equity value, and the CEO bought 125,000 shares on the open market at $6.15 in May. Probability-weighted value of approximately $5.90 against a $5.77 price is fair. HOLD, classified as uncertain. The decisive variable is whether the acquisition funnel stabilizes, and neither the filings nor the guidance answers that yet.

## Company Overview

LegalZoom operates in all 50 states and 3,000+ counties, with direct filing integrations to secretaries of state, the IRS, and the USPTO. One reporting segment; the CEO is the chief operating decision maker evaluating consolidated net income.

Two revenue lines. **Transaction** ($71.9M in Q2 2026, 35% of revenue) covers formation, annual report and compliance filings, and IP/trademark work, recognized on fulfillment and inclusive of pass-through government fees. **Subscription** ($133.4M, 65%) covers registered agent, compliance packages, attorney advice plans, legal forms, tax and accounting, and virtual mail, recognized ratably on terms of 30 days to one year.

Three assets in the filings constitute genuine differentiation. First, LZ Legal Services holds an Arizona alternative business structure license (September 2021) permitting non-lawyer ownership of a legal-services operation, allowing LegalZoom to employ and contract attorneys to deliver limited-scope advice, which the risk factors state it generally cannot do elsewhere. Second, filing automation at scale: H1 2026 saw a 61% increase in annual report filing revenue "as a result of filing automation." Third, brand, though the filings themselves identify the mechanism by which AI search erodes it.

Two clarifications on relationships. LegalZoom is a *customer* of AWS for hosting, a *paying advertiser* on Google's platforms, and a *beneficiary* of Google's organic index, with no contractual protection in either channel. Google is a supplier of traffic, not a partner or customer. The company's disclosed AI-channel efforts are described only as "strategic partnerships and collaborations, including with AI platforms," with no counterparty named, no economics disclosed, and no basis to model contribution.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2024 $681.9M → FY2025 $756.0M (+10.9%). The FY2025 MD&A attributes ~$33.2M of transaction and ~$18.2M of subscription revenue to the Formation Nation acquisition (closed February 10, 2025). Removing the $51.4M gives ~$704.6M of comparable revenue against $681.9M, or approximately **+3.3% organic growth** versus the +10.9% headline. This calculation assumes no revenue interaction between the two brands, which is unlikely to be exactly true, but the direction is unambiguous.

H1 2026 revenue $412.1M (+9.7%); Q2 2026 $205.3M (+6.6%), with transaction -1% and subscription +11%.

**Operating results.** FY2025 operating income of $25.0M includes a $14.3M gain on the March 31, 2025 headquarters sale. Excluding it, operating income was $10.6M versus $35.6M in FY2024, a 70% decline on 10.9% revenue growth, driven by sales and marketing expense growth. Q2 2026 operating income $10.7M versus $(3.1)M; H1 $13.4M versus $5.9M. H1 gross margin improved to 66.1% from 64.3%.

**The stock compensation question is the entire valuation debate.** FY2025 SBC of $113.7M equalled 15.0% of revenue and exceeded FY2025 net income of $15.4M by more than 7x. Adjusted EBITDA, the company-selected compensation metric, excludes it by definition. The constructive development is the trajectory: H1 2026 SBC of $44.9M is 10.9% of revenue, down from 16.1%, with G&A SBC alone falling $13.0M. On an SBC-inclusive basis:

| | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adjusted EBITDA | $172.2M | $76.0M | $82.4M |
| Less SBC | $(113.7)M | $(60.4)M | $(44.9)M |
| **SBC-inclusive** | **$58.5M** | **$15.6M** | **$37.5M** |

First-half SBC-inclusive earnings power roughly doubled. It also establishes that the "22% adjusted EBITDA margin" corresponds to something near a 9% SBC-inclusive operating margin. Part of the SBC decline is mechanical rather than managerial, reflecting lower expense on 2024 market-condition PSUs whose fair value collapsed with the share price. H1 2026 granted 10.7M new RSUs ($70.9M grant-date fair value) plus 5.3M PSUs, refilling the expense pipeline at low prices, which means more shares per dollar of expense.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow $86.8M, capex $12.2M, free cash flow $74.7M versus $72.9M. This should not be doubled: the 10-Q states media spend is historically highest in Q1 to align with formation seasonality, and H1 benefited from a $17.5M deferred-revenue build and $8.7M accounts-payable increase against a $15.6M accrued-expense decline. A normalized approach: FY2025's 19.6% FCF margin applied to a ~$800M FY2026 gives ~$157M, less the ~$6M Q3 restructuring cash cost. Reported FCF less SBC was $34.2M in FY2025 and $29.8M in H1 2026; both framings are legitimate and a report citing only the first is incomplete.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $466.4M; cash $167.2M; goodwill $140.7M; total liabilities $339.2M; zero funded debt; equity $127.2M; accumulated deficit $1,217.9M. The $100M revolver (JPMorgan agent, matures July 14, 2030) is undrawn and its 4.50:1.00 net first lien leverage covenant is tested only above 35% utilization; it is not live. Current ratio 0.67x and working capital of -$105.9M look alarming until you note that current deferred revenue of $221.2M is 69.5% of current liabilities and is extinguished by delivering registered agent and compliance services, not by paying cash. Excluding it, coverage is 2.19x.

The accumulated deficit deserves specific treatment because it distorts mechanical screens. Between 12/31/25 and 6/30/26 it worsened $83.5M despite $6.3M of net income: shares are purchased for immediate retirement, so the excess of repurchase price over par is charged directly to accumulated deficit rather than APIC. Roughly $170M of cumulative repurchases sits inside the $1.22B figure. A classic Altman Z computed on Q2 2026 inputs lands near -0.25, with the retained-earnings term alone contributing -3.66. For a company with zero funded debt, $167M of cash, a dormant covenant, and ~$75M of half-year free cash flow, that score describes buyback accounting rather than solvency risk. The operative risk here is terminal-value erosion, not insolvency.

**Tax is a structural drag.** Effective rate 57% in Q2 and 61% in H1 2026 (51%/52% prior year) against a 21% statutory rate, driven by non-deductible SBC and reduced excess tax benefits. The mechanism is circular: a depressed share price means awards vest below grant-date value, shrinking the excess benefit and raising the cash tax rate. This persists while the stock stays below historical grant levels.

**Earnings delivery has been weak.** Four quarters: one beat, three misses (-11.7%, -6.7%, -12.1%, then +3.6%). That record argues for discounting rather than extending consensus.

## Growth Analysis

Every volume metric is contracting.

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business formations | 131K | 125K | -5% |
| Subscription units | 1,955K | 1,892K | -3% |
| ARPU | $256 | $270 | +5% |
| Retention (annual SMB) | — | ~57% | from ~58% |
| Marketing $ per formation | $357 | $421 | +18% |

Units down, price up, revenue up. That profile can persist several quarters but is arithmetically self-limiting: registered agent price increases cannot be repeated indefinitely against a shrinking base without accelerating churn, and the company has already disclosed that its bundling, which inflates reported subscription units, is itself lowering retention.

Context on share: FY2025 formations grew ~4% (500K versus 482K) while the MD&A cited ~9% growth in total U.S. formations, and much of even that 4% came from Formation Nation rather than the legacy funnel. Q2 2026 then fell 5% outright. LegalZoom is losing share in the market that constitutes its entire customer acquisition event.

**Guidance implies the price lever is nearly exhausted.** Using reported guidance of $795-805M against H1 actual of $412.07M, implied H2 revenue is $382.9-392.9M versus H2 2025 of $380.4M, or **+0.7% to +3.3%**. Backing out the ~$194M Q3 guide leaves Q4 of roughly $189-199M. These guidance figures come from secondary reporting on August 5-6 and could not be verified against the company's press release; the implied growth arithmetic inherits that uncertainty. It is corroborated by the filed metrics, which point the same way.

Consensus estimate revisions are consistent: current quarter $0.20 (-8.8% over 30 days), current year $0.72 (-2.9% over 90 days), next year $0.80 (-2.2%). Modest in magnitude, uniformly negative in direction. There is no inflection signal here.

**What is defensible.** The revenue mix is migrating toward the categories generative AI cannot easily substitute. Registered agent requires a physical in-state address and statutory service-of-process capacity. Compliance and annual report filings require state-by-state system integration, the source of the 61% H1 growth in that line. Virtual mail requires physical infrastructure. Attorney-delivered advice via the Arizona ABS requires a license. Document preparation, forms, templates, and DIY guidance are the exposed categories, and they sit disproportionately in the shrinking transaction line. The mix shift is genuine progress. The problem is that unit counts are falling *inside* the defensible core too.

**Cost actions are real.** The August 2026 plan cuts headcount ~13% at ~$6.0M cost, substantially all cash and recognized in Q3, complete by year end. Reductions are already visible: technology and development payroll fell $6.7M in H1 on lower headcount. If the company retains most of the savings, FY2027 SBC-inclusive earnings power improves materially even on flat revenue. That is the strongest identifiable path to a higher stock price.

## Valuation Assessment

The peer table supplied (EXPO, FCN, BAH, TRU, EFX; median P/E 18.4x) is a poor comparison set; those are consulting and credit-bureau businesses, and LegalZoom is a consumer/SMB internet subscription business with a channel-disintermediation problem. The more useful reference class is cash-generative internet marketplaces whose demand originates in search, which trade at high single-digit to low-teens multiples of SBC-inclusive owner earnings when growth is flat and lower when it turns negative.

At $5.77 and ~$990M market cap with $167.2M net cash:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV | ~$823M |
| EV / FY2025 adjusted EBITDA ($172.2M) | 4.8x |
| EV / FY2025 reported FCF ($147.9M) | 5.6x |
| EV / (FY2025 adj. EBITDA − SBC) = $58.5M | 14.1x |
| EV / LTM revenue ($792.5M) | 1.04x |
| P/E on consensus FY2026 non-GAAP EPS $0.72 | 8.0x |
| GAAP trailing P/E (EPS $0.08) | ~63x |
| Net cash / market cap | 16.9% |
| Remaining buyback authorization / market cap | 8.1% |

The 4.8x and the 63x describe the same company. Which one you use is the whole argument, and the honest answer sits between them: SBC is non-cash to the income statement but is borne by shareholders through issuance. The evidence for that is in the share count. H1 buybacks of 12.6M shares for $89.0M took the count from 177.6M (12/31/25) to 167.5M (6/30/26), but the 10-Q cover reports 171,264,812 shares at July 31, a ~3.81M increase in one month on RSU/PSU settlement. Net seven-month reduction is ~3.6%, not 5.7%. Roughly a third of the repurchase spend offsets issuance rather than shrinking the float.

**Owner-earnings base case.** FY2026 revenue $800M at FY2025's 19.6% FCF margin gives ~$157M reported FCF, less ~$6M restructuring cash = ~$151M, less ~$88M of SBC (H1 annualized) = ~$63M of SBC-inclusive free cash flow. Assume partial retention of restructuring savings lifts that toward $70M in FY2027. Capitalized at a 7.5% EV yield, appropriate for a business with roughly flat revenue and an unresolved channel threat, EV is $933M; plus $167M net cash gives $1,100M, or **$6.41 per share on 171.6M shares**. That is +11% from $5.77.

**Bull.** Restructuring savings largely retained, SBC declining toward 8% of revenue, revenue reaccelerating to ~+3% as partnership and AI-platform channels scale: owner earnings ~$95M by FY2027, capitalized at 7% with continued buyback shrinking the count → **$8.75**, +52%. This sits between the analyst mean of $8.10 and the high of $10.00.

**Bear.** Formation decline accelerates into 2027, the ARPU lever exhausts as retention falls below 55%, revenue declines 3-5%, adjusted EBITDA margin compresses to ~17%, owner earnings fall to ~$35-40M, and the multiple compresses to a 10% yield → **$3.75**, -35%.

Probability weighting 20% bull / 45% base / 35% bear gives $5.94, approximately 3% above spot. That is fair value, which is what defines a HOLD.

**On analyst targets.** Low $7.00, mean $8.10, high $10.00, median $7.50, all above spot. These are not independent confirmation. Sentiment is degrading (strong buy 1→0, buy 3→2, hold 4→6 over one month), one broker moved to neutral on August 7, and the target set has not been reset for the August 5 guidance change. Treating consensus targets as a floor is appropriate for inflection stocks with rising estimates; here estimates are falling and the targets are stale.

The valuation score of 5.5 reflects that the stock is genuinely cheap on the metrics management reports and roughly fair on the metrics shareholders actually own. A low multiple on a non-GAAP measure that excludes 11-15% of revenue, attached to a business with declining units, is not a demonstrated mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive set per the risk factors: law firms, solo attorneys, online legal document services, legal plans, secretaries of state, and other service providers. Law firms retain an advantage LegalZoom cannot replicate outside Arizona — direct legal advice, which LegalZoom "generally cannot offer due to laws and regulations regarding the unauthorized practice of law."

Two forces are reshaping the economics, both visible in the filings.

**The acquisition channel is being restructured by a supplier LegalZoom does not control.** The Q2 10-Q elevates Google's changes to the first item under "Key Factors Affecting Our Performance," stating that algorithm changes, prioritization of AI-generated summaries, and Google's enforcement of its policy on advertising federal filings "are reshaping how potential customers find and engage with LegalZoom. We have seen these changes result in a reduction in organic traffic and a higher emphasis on paid search." For a business whose funnel begins with a query like "how to form an LLC," an AI answer resolving the query in-page changes unit economics directly. The second-order effect the filing identifies is more consequential: because AI overviews cite multiple sources, individual publisher impact is diluted, "diminishing brand recognition." That attacks the brand moat, not just the traffic.

The P&L quantification: marketing per formation up 18% in Q2 and 16% in H1 while formations fell 5% in the quarter. This ratio is not a true CAC — the denominator excludes consumer/estate planning, IP, and standalone subscription sales that the same spend supports, and part of the increase is deliberate brand investment with longer payback. As a same-basis year-over-year trend it is reliable, and it is deteriorating at roughly triple the rate of revenue growth.

**Generative AI is a partial product substitute.** The risk factors state directly that "potential customers may view these tools as replacements to some of the services we provide." Combined with state governments expanding free or low-cost online formation portals, and AI generating step-by-step instructions on how to use them, the DIY path gets materially easier, attacking the transaction that funds the entire subscription flywheel.

The company is responding: subscription mix shift, ARPU expansion, filing automation, channel diversification into partnerships and AI platforms, and a 13% headcount cut framed as an AI-native reorganization. The adjusted EBITDA series ($47.7M in FY2021 to $172.2M in FY2025, per the pay-versus-performance table) shows the cost side has been managed competently. The trajectory of the competitive position, which matters more than the level, is deteriorating: share loss against a growing formation market, falling retention, and rising acquisition cost.

## Risk Assessment

**AI-search disintermediation (dominant).** Discussed above. It has no identifiable floor and it is the reason the multiple compressed. The market is pricing it; whether it is pricing it correctly is unknowable from the current disclosure.

**Retention and the bundling feedback loop.** Retention at ~57% implies roughly 43% annual churn on the measured cohort, and management attributes the decline to its own bundling of low-retaining subscriptions into formation offerings. The metric also excludes Formation Nation, enterprise customers, former UK operations, and the consumer legal advisory plan, so it does not describe the whole book.

**No backlog.** The 10-Q explicitly omits remaining-performance-obligation disclosure because contracts longer than one year are immaterial. The $221.4M deferred revenue balance is prepaid cash for services deliverable within roughly twelve months. Revenue visibility beyond four quarters is minimal and each renewal cycle is fully re-exposed to the 57% retention rate.

**Regulatory.** The Arizona ABS structure is "generally untested in U.S. courts." Separately, ACJA §7-209 restricts holding 10% or more of the parent of a licensed ABS, and the Arizona Supreme Court can force divestiture, knowing or unknowing, and can suspend or revoke the license. That is an unusual constraint on the shareholder register and an impediment to activist accumulation or a strategic acquirer. FinCEN's March 21, 2025 interim final rule eliminating beneficial-ownership reporting removed an entire filing product roughly two and a half years after it was created, a live demonstration of regulatory revenue reversibility.

**Concentration.** No customer exceeded 10% of revenue, but one partner held 10%+ of accounts receivable at both 6/30/26 and 12/31/25, and H1 2026 recorded $1.15M of credit-loss allowance for uncollectible partner receivables versus $0.195M a year earlier. Small in absolute terms, this is a near-six-fold increase at the same moment management is leaning harder on partnership-driven acquisition. Mix shift toward subscription has improved revenue composition but has not improved customer or partner concentration.

**Overhang and structure.** Holders of ~55M shares (~32% of the July 31 count) have registration rights. Francisco Partners entities retain board-nomination rights. The charter waives the corporate-opportunity doctrine for a named list that includes Bryant Stibel Growth, LLC and Bryant-Stibel Fund I LLC — entities affiliated with the sitting CEO — which "have no duty to communicate or present corporate opportunities to us" and are not prohibited from investing in competing businesses. This is standard sponsor-era drafting, not evidence of wrongdoing, but more consequential when a waived party is affiliated with the chief executive.

**Unresolved.** The adjusted EBITDA reconciliation discloses $1.543M of H1 2026 expense for "the evaluation and pursuit of strategic transactions," a change in character from 2025 when the same line related to the completed Formation Nation deal. Acquisition, divestiture, and whole-company scenarios are all consistent with the disclosure. No conclusion should be drawn; it is two-sided optionality of unknown magnitude.

**Contingent dilution.** Reported diluted shares in Q2 2026 were 171.6M. Excluded anti-dilutive awards total 38.4M (5.0M options, 33.3M RSUs/PSUs, 0.15M ESPP), with 2024 PSUs reflected at 200% and 275% of target. The 2026 PSU program granted 5.3M units at target, 3.1% of the 171.3M shares outstanding, or 3.0% ownership dilution post-issuance. At theoretical maximum (revenue component 300%, TSR component 400%) the program reaches roughly 18M shares, or ~9.7% ownership dilution, an outcome requiring large revenue over-achievement across three annual periods alongside top-percentile relative TSR, which the declining formation trend and ~1-3% implied H2 growth make unlikely for the revenue component in 2026. The maximum figure is not current fully diluted capitalization.

### Options Market Signal

The options market on LZ is thin — 24 contracts across four expirations — and therefore low-information. Near-term ATM IV of 56.6% sits below 68.0% trailing realized volatility (a normal post-event vol crush) at the 17th percentile of the current chain, with a contango term structure rising to 67.1% at six months and a 31.6% implied move at that tenor. That shape prices near-term calm and material longer-dated uncertainty, which matches the fundamental picture: the next quarter is unlikely to resolve the funnel question. The 19.4% one-month put/call skew indicates elevated put demand, but put/call volume of 0.06 and open interest of 0.02 mean almost no puts are actually held; bearish positioning is expressed in the stock (11.9% of float short, 13.9M shares, 4.7 days to cover), not in options. The six-month IV corroborates that the market views terminal value as genuinely open.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($8.75, +52%)

LegalZoom is a debt-free business with $167.2M of cash generating ~$150M of reported free cash flow against an $823M enterprise value. Sixty-five percent of revenue is subscription, and the largest components — registered agent and compliance filings — require physical statutory presence and state-by-state system integration that a language model cannot replicate. The company has already demonstrated it can automate into that moat: annual report filing revenue grew 61% in H1. Three things then compound. SBC falls from 15% of revenue toward 8-9%, closing most of the gap between the 4.8x headline multiple and the 14x SBC-inclusive multiple; H1 already narrowed it by more than $20M year over year. The 13% headcount reduction drops to the bottom line in FY2027 on a flat revenue base. And $80.4M of remaining authorization, equal to 8.1% of market cap, retires shares at $5.77 rather than the $7.05 H1 average. On flat revenue and $95M of FY2027 owner earnings capitalized at a 7% EV yield, the equity is worth roughly $8.75. The unexplained strategic-transaction spend and a roughly 74% cumulative TSR shortfall against peers make a whole-company transaction a plausible if unmodellable path to the same value.

### Bear Case ($3.75, -35%)

The formation transaction is the entire customer acquisition event, and it is being disintermediated by a supplier LegalZoom pays but does not control. Formations fell 5% while acquisition spend per formation rose 18%; the company grew formations at less than half the market rate in FY2025 even with an acquisition contributing. Subscription growth is price on a shrinking unit base, retention is falling because of the bundling that inflates the unit count, and the deferred-revenue build, the cleanest bookings proxy, decelerated 41%. Guidance implies second-half growth of under 3.3%. In 2027 the ARPU lever exhausts, retention falls through 55%, revenue turns negative, adjusted EBITDA margin compresses toward 17%, and the market applies a declining-business multiple to SBC-inclusive owner earnings of $35-40M. The 61% effective tax rate persists precisely because the share price is depressed, and a third of buyback spend leaks into offsetting issuance. Equity value of roughly $3.75, with net cash of $0.97 per share providing the only hard floor.

### What Would Settle It

One number, reported quarterly: business formations. If formations return to flat-to-positive year over year with marketing per formation stable, the bull case is live and the current multiple is wrong. If formations decline again in Q3 while acquisition spend per formation rises, the subscription base has an arithmetic ceiling and the bear case is the base case.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** Probability-weighted value of ~$5.94 against a $5.77 basis is approximately fair. Existing holders should not sell into a 30% two-day gap that followed a non-GAAP EPS beat and roughly flat revenue guidance; new capital has no reason to commit before the Q3 print.

**Base case: $6.41 (+11%).** FY2026 revenue $800M at a 19.6% FCF margin = $157M, less $6M restructuring cash and ~$88M SBC = ~$63M of SBC-inclusive free cash flow, rising toward $70M in FY2027 on partial retention of restructuring savings; capitalized at a 7.5% EV yield = $933M EV, plus $167M net cash = $1,100M ÷ 171.6M shares.

**Bull case: $8.75 (+52%).** As described above.

**Bear case: $3.75 (-35%).**

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored on the Q3 2026 report (early November) and the Q4/FY2026 report (February 2027), which together determine whether the $194M Q3 guide held and whether restructuring savings reached the margin line.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Business formations flat or positive year over year in Q3 or Q4 2026, with marketing dollars per formation flat or improving sequentially.
2. Subscription units flat or growing sequentially, with annual small business retention at or above 57% for two consecutive quarters.
3. A price near $4.75-5.00 with the above metrics merely stabilizing rather than improving; at that level the base case offers a ~30% margin of safety and the net cash position covers 20% of the equity value.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID on any of:**
1. Subscription units declining more than 3% year over year for two more consecutive quarters, or retention falling below 55%.
2. FY2027 revenue guided down year over year, or Q1 2027 revenue below $195M (versus the ~$206M H1 2026 quarterly average).
3. Marketing dollars per formation rising another 15%+ year over year in Q3 or Q4 while formations decline; this would confirm the funnel economics are breaking rather than adjusting.
4. Buyback spend continuing to exceed free cash flow with cash falling below ~$130M, which would convert the balance-sheet cushion into a consumed asset.

**Reassessment date: the Q3 2026 earnings release (early November 2026).** That print carries the first full quarter of AI-search impact management flagged as accelerating, the initial restructuring charge, and the first evidence on whether $194M was conservative or optimistic.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report ran 4.9 (AVOID) → 5.9 (HOLD) → 5.6 (HOLD) with improving management quality, financial health, and valuation marks. The August 5 disclosure reverses part of that. The improvement in financial health remains largely intact — zero funded debt, $167.2M cash, a dormant covenant, and SBC-inclusive earnings power that doubled year over year in the first half. Management quality steps down from 6.5 to 5.5: the CEO's $769K open-market purchase at $6.15 and nineteen months of leadership stability after a period of near-continuous officer churn are genuine positives, but $89.0M of H1 repurchases at an average $7.05 (now marked 18% below cost), funded partly by drawing cash from $203.1M to $167.2M, is execution that bears on capital-allocation judgment, and the primary compensation metric remains indifferent to the equity issuance that runs at 11-15% of revenue. Growth prospects and competitive position step down materially: the guidance reset is real fundamental deterioration, not a sentiment event, and it is corroborated by formations -5%, subscription units -3%, retention -1 point, and a 41% smaller deferred-revenue build.

The price decline is therefore justified rather than a mispricing to exploit. What remains is a fairly-valued, debt-free, cash-generative business with a genuine defensible core in registered agent and compliance filings, a cost program in flight, 8.1% of market cap in remaining buyback authorization, and a structural threat to its acquisition funnel that the filings describe candidly and that no available evidence shows to be stabilizing.
