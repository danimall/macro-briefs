## Executive Summary

MGM's equity is now governed by a takeover process rather than by operations. On June 1, 2026, People Incorporated (formerly IAC), which holds 66,822,350 shares — 26.6% of the outstanding count — submitted a non-binding all-cash proposal at $48.30 per share. A special committee of independent directors, advised by outside counsel and bankers, has been evaluating it for more than ten weeks. The stock traded briefly above the offer on June 1-2 and has since drifted to $43.98, a 9.8% gross discount. That shift from pricing a probable bump to pricing a meaningful discount is the single most informative data point in the file, and it has two credible drivers that cannot be fully separated: fading expectation of a topping bid, and genuine deterioration in the standalone value that would be revealed if the proposal lapses.

The July 29 Q2 report supplied the deterioration. Consolidated revenue rose 1.0% to $4.451B while Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA fell 5.7% to $610.4M; first-half EBITDA is down 7.3%. The headline "Las Vegas strength" does not survive inspection of the gaming statistics in the 10-Q: Strip table drop fell 2.0% and slot handle rose 0.5%, while table win percentage jumped from 22.9% to 29.6%. Holding drop constant and applying prior-year hold produces roughly $102M less table win, which would have turned reported Strip revenue of $2,170.0M into a 2.2% year-over-year decline instead of a 2.6% increase, and EBITDAR into a 9-10% decline instead of +3.5%. Normalizing to a mid-cycle Strip hold nearer 26% rather than to last year's depressed figure halves that adjustment, but the direction is the same in every version: room rate, table volume and slot volume were flat to down. Consensus has responded — FY2027 EPS estimates have been cut 9.2% in 30 days and 10.0% in 90 days.

The arithmetic that follows is unfavorable rather than alarming. A signed deal at $48.30 closing mid-2027 is worth roughly $46-47 discounted to a twelve-month horizon (+5-7%). A negotiated bump to $50-51, which Stifel has argued for, is worth +14-16% but faces a structural obstacle: People Inc. stated in writing that it will not sell its stake or vote in favor of any transaction resulting in a change of control to another party, which makes a competing bidder's path nearly impossible and removes the special committee's principal source of leverage. A withdrawal or rejection sends the stock back toward standalone value on cut estimates and a de-rated casino group — roughly $36, or -18%. Weighting those outcomes 55/15/30 gives an expected value of approximately $44.03, essentially unchanged from spot, against a 4.20% two-year Treasury. That is a less-than-fair risk-adjusted return with binary variance, which supports a REDUCE rather than a HOLD.

## Company Overview

MGM operates four reportable segments. Las Vegas Strip Resorts comprises nine properties — Bellagio, Aria/Vdara, The Cosmopolitan, MGM Grand, Mandalay Bay, Luxor, New York-New York, Excalibur and Park MGM — generating $8.44B of FY2025 revenue and the largest profit pool. Regional Operations covers MGM Grand Detroit, Beau Rivage, Borgata, National Harbor, Springfield and Empire City; Northfield Park was sold in April 2026. MGM China, approximately 56%-owned and separately listed in Hong Kong, holds MGM Macau and MGM Cotai and one of six Macau concessions, contributing $4.46B of FY2025 revenue. MGM Digital consolidates LeoVegas and related subsidiaries at $654M of FY2025 revenue and remains EBITDAR-negative. BetMGM, the 50% North American joint venture, is equity-accounted.

The structural fact that governs risk analysis is that MGM owns almost none of the real estate it operates. The June 30, 2026 10-Q carries $23.878B of operating lease liabilities against $21.66B of right-of-use assets, with a 23-year weighted-average remaining term at a 7% discount rate. Landlords include VICI Properties under an eleven-property master lease, the Bellagio REIT Venture in which MGM retains 5%, and separate ventures owning Mandalay Bay/MGM Grand and The Cosmopolitan. Contractual cash rent over the next twelve months is $1.8B, escalating at a 2% minimum on the VICI lease and CPI-linked with a 2% floor and 3% cap thereafter. Total undiscounted future minimum operating lease payments are $52.37B.

## Financial Analysis

**Three years of flat EBITDA.** Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was $2.336B in FY2023, $2.411B in FY2024 and $2.426B in FY2025 on revenue that rose from $17.24B to $17.54B. GAAP operating income fell 33% in FY2025 to $1.00B and net income attributable to MGM fell from $747M to $205.9M on impairments and non-operating items.

**FY2025 attributable earnings were largely a tax item.** Pretax income was $281M against an income tax *benefit* of $240M, a −85.5% effective rate, driven by roughly $283.7M of valuation-allowance release on foreign tax credit carryforwards. Excluding that discrete item, attributable net income would have been approximately break-even to modestly negative. The geographic split shows a $237M domestic pretax loss against $518M of foreign pretax income (summing to the reported $281M). The domestic loss is heavily burdened by the $256M Empire City goodwill impairment plus roughly $93M of related write-downs, all corporate expense and the full ~$2.26B of triple-net rent, so adding back the Empire City charges alone leaves domestic pretax near break-even. It is also partly an artifact of transfer pricing. The verifiable conclusion is that FY2025 GAAP EPS is not a usable earnings-power proxy, and the residual FTC deferred tax asset (~$334M gross against a ~$265M allowance, with $134M expiring in 2026 and $200M in 2027) remains a disclosed source of two-way earnings volatility. The 1H26 effective rate normalized to 19.2%, confirming the FY2025 benefit does not repeat.

**Cash flow is real but smaller than the headline.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $2.529B less $1.069B of capex gives $1.460B, or $5.80 per share and a 13.2% yield at $43.98. Two adjustments are required. First, FY2025 benefited from approximately $35M of net tax refunds versus $267M of taxes paid in FY2024 — a roughly $300M favorable swing tied to completion of the IRS 2015-2019 examination. Second, MGM Osaka equity funding of roughly $238M is a hard contractual commitment classified in investing. Normalizing both leaves roughly $0.9-1.2B of discretionary cash flow, an 8.3%-10.8% yield. A portion of consolidated cash sits at MGM China, roughly 44% of whose distributions accrue to minority holders.

First-half 2026 operating cash flow of $1.127B less $396M capex equals $731M, but this should not be annualized: it includes a $230.8M favorable working-capital swing as the income tax receivable fell from $220.2M to $1.6M, plus $69M of net refunds received, and the first-half convention calendar is seasonally supportive. Stripping the tax timing benefit and applying management's guided remaining-2026 capex of $575-675M (implying FY2026 capex near $0.97-1.07B) points to roughly $1.0-1.2B for the full year before Osaka funding. A naive 2× of first-half FCF would give $1.46B; that figure is a sensitivity, not a forecast.

**Two-tier leverage.** Debt principal at June 30, 2026 was $6.103B, of which approximately $2.34B sits at MGM China. Cash was $2.547B. Net debt of $3.556B against trailing Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.40B is roughly 1.5x — conservative. Adding $23.878B of operating leases and $0.213B of finance leases gives $27.65B against run-rate EBITDAR of approximately $4.62B (twice the sum of first-half EBITDA of $1.191B and triple-net rent of $1.117B), or **~6.0x lease-adjusted net leverage**. Standardized screens reflect the second view: total liabilities of $36.47B against total equity of $3.37B is 10.8x, and the Altman Z-Score sits in the distress zone. That model was calibrated on pre-ASC-842 manufacturers and overstates bankruptcy proximity for a company with $2.5B of cash, $5.0B of undrawn revolvers ($2.3B corporate, fully undrawn; HK$23.4B at MGM China with $344M drawn), compliance with all covenants, and only $400M of 2026 and $1.425B of 2027 maturities. It nonetheless points at a real feature: fixed charges are enormous and largely non-cancellable.

**Fixed-charge coverage is the metric that matters.** Run-rate EBITDAR of $4.62B against $1.8B of cash rent plus $345-365M of consolidated cash interest is roughly 2.1x. Because rent escalates at 2%+ regardless of results, a 20% EBITDAR decline compresses coverage to ~1.7x and a 40% decline to ~1.3x, before capex, taxes or the ~$2.1B remaining Osaka commitment. Cash rent already absorbs approximately 38% of pre-rent property cash flow.

**Capital allocation has narrowed to one lever.** The dividend is suspended and the 10-Q states it may not resume "in the foreseeable future or at all." Share count fell 14.6% in eighteen months, from 294.374M to 251.586M, but repurchase spend collapsed from ~$1.2B in FY2025 to $253M in 1H26 versus $711M in 1H25. Per the Item 2 table, every Q2 repurchase occurred in a single month — 4,300,599 shares in May at an average $37.64, with zero in April and June. $1.36B remains authorized. Capital in excess of par is zero, so repurchases now charge directly to retained earnings.

The Northfield Park disposal was the year's best-executed transaction: $546M gross proceeds, a $255M gain, derecognition of $228M of intangibles, and a VICI master lease amendment cutting annual cash rent by $53M (from $775M to $722M, escalating to $736M on May 1). The partial lease termination reduced ROU assets by $1.1B and lease liabilities by $1.2B with an additional $51M gain. Selling low-multiple regional assets while extracting proportionate rent relief is the highest-return lever available, and it de-risks the lease-adjusted balance sheet in a way buybacks cannot. The lever is finite: a handful of candidate assets remain, VICI holds a right of first offer on further gaming development adjacent to Empire City, and each disposal shrinks the EBITDA base against a rent obligation that declines only partially.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth to underwrite. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA has been flat for three years and is down 7.3% in the first half of 2026. The segment detail:

- **Las Vegas Strip:** FY2025 revenue −4% to $8.44B, EBITDAR −8% to $2.858B, margin 33.9% from 35.2%; RevPAR $229 from $245. Q2 2026 EBITDAR of $735.1M was up 3.5%, but on the hold normalization above the underlying figure was down 9-10%. First-half EBITDAR fell 2.5% to $1,484M with margin at 34.1% from 35.5%, despite the Q2 hold benefit, because 1H25 included $42M of business-interruption insurance proceeds versus $6M in 1H26 and 1H26 carried $40M of higher self-insurance reserves.
- **Regional:** Q2 EBITDAR −9.2% to $280.2M; same-store first-half EBITDAR −4.1% to $495.2M.
- **MGM China:** Q2 revenue −1%, main-floor table drop −6.6% to $3,815M, EBITDAR −14.8% with margin at 23.3% from 27.1%. Roughly half the margin decline is a new intercompany branding license fee ($21M in the quarter, $44M in the half); adding it back leaves EBITDAR down ~7.8% and margin down ~190bp, so the "Macau collapse" headline overstates operating deterioration. The royalty also shifts economics away from MGM China's ~44% minority — noncontrolling-interest income fell 56% in the quarter to $30.4M — which raises MGM's attributable share of Macau while reducing the subsidiary's reported profit.
- **MGM Digital:** first-half revenue +29.9%, but marketing costs rose 31% and gaming taxes 46%, leaving the EBITDAR loss at ~$56M with the Q2 loss widening to −$30.9M from −$25.7M. A $111M goodwill impairment was recorded at a Digital reporting unit in Q2, six months after the FY2025 10-K flagged one Digital unit's fair value exceeding carrying value by only ~7% on ~$341M of allocated goodwill. Goodwill fell from $4,902.0M to $4,768.7M in the half.
- **BetMGM** is the one clear positive: MGM's share of operating income was $30.5M in 1H26 versus $6.6M in 1H25, and cumulative losses in excess of investment shrank from $160M to $131M. Composition matters — the Q1 swing (from −$15.2M to +$7.4M) did the work; Q2 improved only 6%.

The industry backdrop is worse than cyclical softness. Las Vegas received approximately 38.5 million visitors in 2025, down 7.5% — the largest annual decline since 1970 excluding the pandemic — with Canadian visitation down 17.4% and air service down 8% domestically and over 20% internationally in fiscal 2026, largely attributed to Spirit Airlines' bankruptcy. First-half 2026 visitation was roughly flat against that reduced base: stabilization at a lower level. MGM's own filings corroborate the direction through RevPAR and ADR. Management described the quarter as strong in April and May and "more challenged" in June, with luxury healthy and Luxor and Excalibur weak.

Forward optionality exists but is distant and diluted. MGM Osaka opens toward 2030; MGM's ownership fell from 50% to ~39% in April 2026 as other shareholders funded, recovering to ~40% in July with ~43.5% expected at completion, while MGM's cash commitment of JPY428B is unchanged and roughly JPY335.9B (~$2.1B) remains to be funded quarterly through 2028. MGM provides an uncapped guarantee to fund construction completion and full opening, and its ~$544M carrying interest is pledged as collateral for the project credit agreement.

Consensus reflects this. FY2026 EPS of $1.91 is up 3.9% in 30 days but down 3.9% in 90 days. The four-quarter surprise record is one beat, one in-line and two misses, including a 40.2% miss in Q3 2025.

## Valuation Assessment

There is a data inconsistency in the source feed: the quoted trailing and forward P/E of 26.27 cannot be reconciled to the quoted EPS of $0.76 at a $43.98 price. I use consensus estimates and reproducible enterprise-value arithmetic instead.

| Metric | At $43.98 | At $48.30 offer |
|---|---|---|
| Equity value (251.586M sh) | $11.07B | $12.15B |
| + Net debt ($6.103B − $2.547B) | $3.56B | $3.56B |
| + NCI (book) | $0.85B | $0.85B |
| **EV excluding leases** | **$15.48B** | **$16.56B** |
| ÷ FY2025 Adj. EBITDA $2.426B | **6.4x** | **6.8x** |
| + Leases ($23.878B + $0.213B) | $39.57B | $40.65B |
| ÷ run-rate EBITDAR $4.62B | **8.6x** | **8.8x** |
| Normalized FCF yield ($0.9-1.2B) | **8.3%-10.8%** | 7.6%-9.9% |
| P/E on FY2026 $1.91 / FY2027 $2.08 | 23.0x / 21.1x | 25.3x / 23.2x |

The valuation reads differently depending on which frame you accept. On normalized free cash flow the equity is statistically cheap at an 8-11% yield, which is precisely the characteristic that attracted a financial acquirer. On lease-adjusted EBITDAR, 8.6x is a full multiple for a business with flat three-year EBITDA, structurally lower Las Vegas visitation and a Macau concession expiring December 31, 2032. On earnings, 21.1x next-year sits essentially at the 21.8x peer median, so there is no relative discount, and the peer median is itself distorted (Boyd at 3.6x, Caesars negative). The casino group has already de-rated: Wynn is 24% below its 52-week high, Churchill Downs 27%.

Stifel's $50-55 range implies roughly 9.2-9.8x lease-adjusted EBITDAR and requires either a Las Vegas recovery or explicit credit for Osaka and BetMGM option value not visible in trailing figures. That is a single-source view and should be weighted accordingly. Widely reported "$18 billion" and "nearly $19 billion" transaction values do not reconcile to any construction reproducible from the 10-Q balance sheet; the $12.4B figure cited elsewhere is consistent with equity value at the offer.

Higher-for-longer rates work against this structure in two places. The lease liability is discounted at 7% over a 23-year weighted-average term with escalators of at least 2%; a rising long end raises the required return on rent-like fixed claims and compresses the residual equity. Separately, the proposal requires roughly $8.92B of cash to acquire the 184.764M shares People Inc. does not own, funded from People's cash, MGM's own balance-sheet cash, and additional debt and equity commitments. People Inc. expects no financing condition, but the proposal is non-binding and the credit environment is tightening at the margin.

## Competitive Landscape

MGM's asset base is not replicable: nine Strip properties spanning luxury to value, the largest convention and group footprint on the Strip (approximately 20% of Q2 room mix, with record Q2 convention ADR and catering revenue), a scaled loyalty database feeding physical and digital businesses, and one of six Macau concessions. Barry Diller's June 1 letter framed the thesis around "real world assets that AI cannot easily replicate or disintermediate," which is a fair description of the moat.

The trajectory is what concerns me. Las Vegas has bifurcated: luxury is holding while Luxor and Excalibur required an all-inclusive package promotion to stabilize occupancy (more than 30,000 room nights since launch). Regional gaming faces a durable structural headwind that MGM has conceded: it withdrew its New York downstate license application, triggering a $256M Empire City goodwill impairment plus ~$93M of related write-downs including a $52M gaming license intangible, while the New York Gaming Commission licensed three downstate integrated resorts with live table games in 2025. Empire City and Borgata will face new licensed competition without MGM holding a downstate license. Boyd Gaming's flat Q2 confirms the regional softness is industry-wide rather than MGM-specific, which limits the share-loss reading but does not improve the earnings outlook.

In Macau, the concession carries a MOP19.7B (~$2.5B) ten-year investment commitment from January 2023, MOP1B of bank guarantees, government redemption rights from 2030 with one year's notice against "reasonable and fair damages," and no guarantee of extension past 2032. Digital remains sub-scale and loss-making against far larger competitors, and the FY2025 risk factors newly name prediction markets as a competitive threat — a category that competes for sports-betting handle under a different regulatory framework than licensed sportsbooks.

## Risk Assessment

**The deal is binary and unsigned.** People Inc.'s proposal is expressly non-binding, subject to confirmatory diligence and negotiation of a definitive agreement, and reserves the right to withdraw or modify at any time. The latest located evidence (August 3) confirms only that the special committee continues to review. Management declined to take questions on the offer at the July 29 call.

**The bidder has pre-emptively constrained the process.** People Inc. stated it has no intention to sell its stake or to vote in favor of any transaction resulting in a change of control to another party. With 26.6% of the shares and two board seats, that removes the auction as a source of price improvement. Barry Diller (bidder chairman) and Joseph Levin (bidder CEO) both sit on MGM's board; Diller has committed to recuse. The special committee is empowered with outside advisers, which is consistent with a structure aimed at the *MFW* conditions, though whether a majority-of-the-minority vote will be required is not disclosed. Whether the April 3, 2026 Voting Agreement among MGM, IAC and Diller contains a standstill, voting cap or support obligation is unknown; I could not retrieve the exhibit, and it materially affects deal mechanics.

**Off-balance-sheet guarantees.** MGM guarantees the shortfall on the Bellagio REIT Venture's $3.01B of indebtedness maturing 2029, and the FY2025 10-K discloses a comparable ~$3.0B arrangement for the Mandalay Bay/MGM Grand landlords. MGM is the tenant, does not own the real estate, and separately guarantees the landlord's mortgage debt after lenders exhaust remedies against the collateral. These are carried at a fair value described as immaterial and appear nowhere in the $6.103B debt figure. In a severe Strip downturn, rent obligations and guarantee exposure on the same assets could arise together.

**Other named exposures.** The uncapped Osaka completion guarantee with the equity interest pledged as collateral; the FTC valuation allowance, which the 10-Q's forward-looking risk list explicitly names; further Digital goodwill impairment given a segment that remains EBITDAR-negative with a widening loss; pending state regulator investigations from the September 2023 cyber incident, with business-interruption insurance proceeds declining from $56M in 1H25 to $8M in 1H26 and mechanically hurting comparisons.

**Governance and alignment.** CEO total compensation rose 60% to $25.33M in FY2025, a year when Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was flat and attributable net income fell from $747M to $206M. The disclosed long-term performance measures are total shareholder return, relative TSR versus the S&P 1500 Hotels/Restaurants/Leisure index, and stock price. Management is therefore not directly compensated on EBITDA, margin, return on capital or rent coverage — the metrics that determine whether the post-sale-leaseback model works. A change of control at $48.30 is favorable to relative-TSR-linked awards, a mild alignment concern during a buyout negotiation. Officer insider activity contains no open-market purchases; the only meaningful open-market buying is the bidder's own accumulation of ~2.1M shares for ~$77.1M at $36.30-$37.30 between December 2025 and March 2026, which establishes that People Inc. was a willing buyer at $36-37 before offering $48.30. The May repurchase at $37.64 executed four weeks before the proposal became public, followed by zero buybacks in June, has two plausible explanations: deal-related constraints once material non-public information existed, or ordinary discipline as the price approached assessed value. I do not treat it as an established finding.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning does not corroborate acute stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.1% sits 6.8 points *below* one-year realized volatility of 37.9%, and the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.30 is call-tilted. ATM IV has fallen from 44.4% on July 22 to 31.1%, consistent with post-earnings decay rather than rising deal anxiety. Skew is balanced at 1.7%. The term structure is mildly backwardated (31.1% near versus 27.5% at six months), and the six-month tenor prices a ±14.2% move with a −3.2% skew, a reasonable market-implied width around a deal-versus-break binary and close to my own scenario spread. One flagged trade is directionally informative: 1,884 October $39 puts against 160 open interest, 11.8x volume-to-OI, struck 11.3% below spot — break-case protection near my bear target. Short interest of 16.7% of float at 7.8 days to cover cuts both ways: it caps the enthusiasm for shorting into an unsigned deal, and it creates squeeze risk on any definitive-agreement announcement. That squeeze risk is the main reason this is a trim rather than a short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($51, +16%).** The special committee extracts a bump to $50-51 and signs a definitive agreement in Q4 2026, arguing that $48.30 represents only 8.8x lease-adjusted EBITDAR and a 12.0% FY2025 free cash flow yield for control of irreplaceable Strip real estate operations, one of six Macau concessions, half of BetMGM, and Osaka optionality. Gaming approvals across Nevada, Michigan, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York and Mississippi proceed without incident, and the 16.7% short base is forced to cover. Supporting evidence: People Inc. bought at $36-37 and has committed capital and reputation; Stifel values the equity at $50-55; the analyst mean target is $50.57 with a high of $60.

**Bear case ($36, −18%).** The committee rejects $48.30 as inadequate, People Inc. declines to raise given a standalone case that has weakened since June, and the proposal lapses. The stock reverts to standalone value on cut estimates: at 6.0x EV/EBITDA excluding leases on ~$2.30B of FY2026 EBITDA, enterprise value of $13.8B less $3.56B net debt and $0.85B NCI implies roughly $37 per share; at 5.5x, roughly $33. Las Vegas visitation is stabilizing 7.5% below 2024, air service is down, table hold normalizes against the Q2 benefit, the Digital loss widens with further goodwill impairment risk against ~$341M of allocated goodwill, Macau operating drop continues falling into the 2032 concession clock, and the dividend remains suspended with buybacks curtailed. Fixed-charge coverage of 2.1x becomes the governing metric as $1.8B of rent escalates against a shrinking EBITDAR base.

**Base case ($46.50, +5.7%).** A definitive agreement is signed at or very near $48.30 and closes mid-to-late 2027 after gaming and competition approvals; discounted to a twelve-month horizon that is worth roughly $46.50. Probability-weighting 55% base, 15% bull and 30% bear gives $44.03 — 0.1% above spot before any carrying cost.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure now rather than into event headlines. The position pays approximately 10% gross to accept an unsigned, non-binding controlling-shareholder proposal with 18% downside on withdrawal, on a business whose EBITDAR is declining and whose out-year consensus has been cut 10% in 90 days. Reduce to a residual position sized so that the bear case is tolerable; for accounts unwilling to hold binaries at all, exit fully. Do not short: 16.7% short interest at 7.8 days to cover against a live proposal creates asymmetric squeeze risk on any 8-K announcing an agreement.

**Base case target: $46.50** (+5.7%). Derivation: $48.30 offer discounted for a mid-to-late 2027 close at a mid-single-digit required return over a twelve-month horizon.
**Bull case target: $51** (+16%) — negotiated bump, implying ~9.0x lease-adjusted EBITDAR.
**Bear case target: $36** (−18%) — proposal lapses; 6.0x EV/EBITDA excluding leases on ~$2.30B FY2026 EBITDA less $3.56B net debt and $0.85B NCI.
**Timeframe:** the binary should resolve within three to nine months; a signed agreement would then require roughly nine to fifteen additional months for gaming approvals.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better:**
1. An 8-K disclosing a definitive merger agreement at $48.30 or above, with a majority-of-the-minority condition and no financing condition. At that point the spread becomes an arbitrage question rather than a fundamental one, and the appropriate rating depends on the residual spread and expected close date.
2. Two consecutive quarters of Las Vegas Strip EBITDAR growth on *volume* — table drop and slot handle up year over year with RevPAR flat or better — rather than on hold percentage.
3. A price near or below $36-37 without deal withdrawal, which would restore an 11-13% normalized free cash flow yield and an adequate margin of safety on the standalone case.

**Downgrade triggers toward AVOID:**
1. Public withdrawal or rejection of the proposal without a replacement transaction, combined with FY2027 consensus falling below $1.85.
2. Fixed-charge coverage (run-rate EBITDAR ÷ cash rent plus cash interest) falling below 1.8x, which on current rent and interest implies run-rate EBITDAR below roughly $3.9B, or Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA below approximately $2.1B.
3. A further Digital goodwill impairment materially exceeding $111M, or any impairment charge at a Las Vegas or Regional reporting unit.
4. Any disclosure indicating landlord shortfall guarantees are being called, or a Bellagio REIT Venture refinancing at terms that raise MGM's contingent exposure ahead of the 2029 maturity.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediately on any 8-K addressing the People Inc. proposal; otherwise at the Q3 2026 report in late October, which will show whether the Q2 table-hold benefit reversed and whether Regional and Macau volumes stabilized.

## Conclusion

MGM presents a business with genuine, irreplaceable assets, a real free cash flow stream of roughly $0.9-1.2B normalized, and adequate near-term liquidity, wrapped in a capital structure where $23.9B of non-cancellable escalating lease liabilities and roughly $6B of contingent landlord shortfall guarantees sit above the equity. Three years of flat EBITDA turned into a 7.3% first-half decline, and the reported Las Vegas improvement in Q2 was produced by a 670bp swing in table win percentage rather than by volume. Consensus is cutting the out-year by 10%.

Against that, the equity's near-term path is set by an unsigned $48.30 proposal from a 26.6% holder that has explicitly foreclosed a competing transaction. The market has moved from pricing a bump above the offer in June to pricing a 9.8% discount today, and the probability-weighted expected value from $43.98 is essentially flat over twelve months against a 4.20% risk-free alternative, with 16% upside and 18% downside. Negligible expected excess return, binary variance, deteriorating standalone fundamentals, and a governance setup that limits the committee's negotiating leverage together argue for reducing exposure rather than holding it. The score declines to 4.3 from 5.0 on specific new evidence from the July 29 filing and the subsequent estimate cuts, not on the 3.3% price move. Investors who want this asset should wait for either a signed agreement, which converts the position into a defined arbitrage, or a break down toward $36, which would restore a margin of safety on the cash flow.
