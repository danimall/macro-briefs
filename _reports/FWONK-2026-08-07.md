## Executive Summary

Liberty Media has finished rebuilding itself into a pure-play global motorsport rights company. Following the July 2025 acquisition of ~84% of MotoGP for approximately $3,659M, the December 2025 split-off of Liberty Live Holdings, and the May 2026 reincorporation in Nevada, FWONK is direct common equity in a company whose only significant subsidiaries are Formula 1 and the MotoGP Group. Any multi-year comparison drawn from a standardized data source is unusable: FY2022-2023 "revenue" for this filer included consolidated SiriusXM. The starting point for analysis is FY2025 and the Q2 2026 10-Q filed August 6.

On that basis, this is a high-quality franchise at a full price with a near-dated binary attached. F1 grew FY2025 revenue 13.5% and Adjusted OIBDA 19.6%, reflecting operating leverage as the rights model implies, since team payments are contractually formulaic while incremental media and sponsorship dollars carry high margin. Remaining performance obligations of roughly $19.3bn, including $3,605M contracted for 2027, give unusual multi-year visibility. Q2 2026 revenue fell 38% at F1 on five races versus nine, which is a season-fee recognition artifact rather than a demand event (team payments fell in lockstep, $316M from $513M). What is not an artifact: the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix is permanently lost from the 2026 calendar, the Bahrain round was relocated to Sepang on October 4, and F1 has deferred to September a decision on the Qatar and Abu Dhabi rounds. Four of the originally scheduled 24 events sit in the Gulf, in a region where the ongoing conflict in the Middle East caused the April cancellations. F1 generally does not carry event-cancellation insurance.

We are downgrading from BUY (issued July 26) to HOLD. The reason is the content of the August 6 disclosure, not the share price, which is roughly 3% higher than at the prior call. Three things changed: the MotoGP repricing thesis, which is the entire justification for a ~29x trailing acquisition multiple, showed H1 constant-currency revenue growth of just +1% and Q2 of -5%, decelerating from +8.6% pro forma in FY2025; consolidated net covenant leverage rose from 3.0x to 3.4x on a shrinking trailing OIBDA denominator even as debt fell $134M; and the parent repurchased zero shares between May 1 and July 31 with $1.1bn of authorization outstanding while the stock traded in the mid-$80s, against $299M of corporate cash and $475M of convertible notes due August 2027. At $99.69, base case fair value is roughly $110 (+10%), with a $132 bull and a $78 bear. That is fair, not compelling.

## Company Overview

**Two rights franchises.** F1 is the exclusive commercial rights holder to the FIA Formula One World Championship under 100-Year Agreements effective January 1, 2011 through December 31, 2110. MotoGP holds commercial rights to the FIM MotoGP and World Superbike championships under an FIM Agreement running to December 31, 2060. Both monetize identically through three primary streams: race promotion fees (3-7 year promoter contracts), media rights fees (3-5 years), and sponsorship (3-5 years), plus hospitality, freight, licensing, feeder series, and rental income from Grand Prix Plaza in Las Vegas ($6M in Q2, $12M in H1).

The largest cost is team payments, a contractual share of profits distributed to the eleven F1 teams under the 2026 Concorde Commercial Agreement, which commits all eleven through December 31, 2030. MotoGP has now matched that: in June 2026 it signed new five-year agreements with all manufacturers and all eleven teams through 2031, resolving the IRTA-expiry risk the FY2025 10-K had flagged as unsettled.

**What the model is and is not.** Calling this an asset-light royalty is imprecise. The 10-K discloses $2,878M of motorsport agreement obligations — payments due to the FIA, FIM, and IRTA. The sanctioning bodies are paid counterparties, not passive licensors, and they hold termination rights for material breach, unpermitted change of control, or insolvency. Liberty owns neither the teams nor, with the exception of Las Vegas, the events.

**Capital structure.** Shares at July 31, 2026: Series A 23,991,058, Series B 2,381,188, Series C 224,336,239 — 250,708,485 total. At $99.69, all-class equity value is $24,993M; the $22.8bn market cap in the data feed captures only Series C. There is a data inconsistency: the profile reports trailing EPS of -$0.13 alongside a trailing P/E of 40.75, which cannot both be true. We rely on the filings and the consensus estimate table.

Debt fell $134M and cash rose $133M, yet consolidated leverage rose 0.4x. That is arithmetically only possible through a smaller trailing OIBDA denominator, which is what happened: TTM Adjusted OIBDA = FY2025 $1,068M + H1 2026 $387M − H1 2025 $442M = **$1,013M**. The more consequential balance-sheet issue is structural subordination. Corporate-level debt is ~$497M against $299M of corporate cash. The remaining $1,166M of cash sits inside the F1 ($1,024M) and MotoGP ($142M) credit groups, which are non-recourse to Liberty and governed by restricted-payment tests. **Neither group made distributions to the parent in H1 2026.** The August 2027 convertible therefore requires refinancing, a distribution from F1 (which has capacity at 2.3-2.7x and upstreamed cash in 2025), or share settlement. The notes were in the money at $99.69 — conversion price ~$82.98, conversion value ~$570M against $475M par, carried at fair value of $586M — so full share settlement would issue 5,724,000 shares, 2.28% of outstanding shares and 2.23% post-issuance ownership dilution. Manageable, but a real claim on the same liquidity that must also address the MotoGP put beginning July 2028.

Facility terms are reasonable. F1: TLA to September 2029, TLB to September 2031, $500M revolver undrawn, 5.42% weighted-average rate, $2.2bn of swaps to September 2031 (with counterparty early-termination rights in September 2029). MotoGP repriced in June 2026 into a €720M TLB due 2032 and a $209M TLA due 2030 with margins stepped down and $114M repaid, at a 4.94% weighted-average rate. Both groups were covenant-compliant at June 30; thresholds and headroom were not disclosed, so headroom cannot be quantified.

## Growth Analysis

**F1 is repricing well and is contractually capped on volume.** FY2025 revenue +13.5% with OIBDA +19.6% demonstrates the engine: renew media rights and sponsorship above expiring rates, add higher-value promoter contracts, and let the formulaic team-payment share absorb only its contracted portion. Recent commercial evidence supports continuation: Las Vegas extended ten years through 2037, Pirelli through 2028, ServusTV renewed. What F1 cannot easily do is add inventory: expansion beyond 24 events requires 70% team consent, above 25 requires FIA approval, and from 2031 anything above 17 events requires a new agreement. Domenicali has publicly ruled out a second US race. Growth must come from price, not volume.

**The 2026 calendar is a down year with an unresolved tail.** The assumed calendar is 23 races versus 24 in 2025, one fewer event due to not holding the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix, after Bahrain was relocated to Sepang on October 4. The contractual floor matters: broadcast fees reduce only below approximately 14-16 F1 events for non-force-majeure reasons, so at 21-23 events the bulk of season-based media fees is preserved. The economic damage concentrates in specific promoter fees plus associated hospitality, freight, and Paddock Club revenue, partly offset by lower pro-rata team payments. Sepang's economics are undisclosed and a Malaysian promoter fee is unlikely to match a Gulf rate. Expect a modest negative true-up in Q3 as H1's 8/22nds recognition becomes 8/23rds.

**MotoGP is the growth thesis and it has not begun.** Q2 2026 MotoGP revenue was -2% USD and -5% constant currency versus pro forma Q2 2025, with the company citing declining contractual media rights revenue and lower title sponsorship. H1 was +6% USD but only +1% constant currency; Adjusted OIBDA +10% USD, +5% constant currency. FY2025 pro forma had been +8.6% cc revenue and +14.9% OIBDA, so growth decelerated in the first half of the first full year of Liberty ownership. Three explanations coexist and the evidence does not yet discriminate: the 2027-2031 team and manufacturer agreements signed in June were the precondition for repricing and the commercial push (CAA appointed as global sponsorship agency, DAZN and Sky DACH renewals, Malaysia to 2031, Silverstone to 2028) begins with the 2027 cycle; legacy media contracts are locked low and must roll; or MotoGP's media value is structurally below what the ~29x trailing acquisition multiple assumed. The net-basis hospitality change and event mix mechanically suppress reported growth, obscuring the underlying trend. The 2027 season is the test.

**What consensus is actually saying.** Estimate revisions look superficially strong — next quarter +26.9% over 30 days and +38.4% over 90 days, current year +6.3%/+10.5%. But next-year estimates are essentially flat (-0.1% / +0.6%). The intra-year revisions are consistent with calendar reallocation (more races recognized in H2, plus the Sepang restoration) rather than an upgrade to earnings power; analysts have not raised next year despite the MotoGP agreements. We therefore do not treat the revision velocity as a signal of accelerating earnings power; at ~47x next-year GAAP EPS the stock is not in the valuation zone where revision momentum drives re-rating.

**Contracted visibility is genuine but bounded.** RPO at June 30: $2,192M for the remainder of 2026, $3,605M in 2027, $3,219M in 2028, $8,734M for 2029-2033, $1,580M thereafter — roughly $19.3bn. This excludes contracts with original expected duration of one year or less, which for MotoGP explicitly includes some media and sponsorship deals. It is a floor on multi-year contracted revenue, not a total order book, and should not be compared to differently defined backlogs.

## Valuation Assessment

**Enterprise value at $99.69.** All-class equity $24,993M + net debt ($4,855M − $1,465M = $3,390M) + redeemable NCI at $759M carrying value = **$29,142M** ($28,383M excluding the NCI).

- EV / TTM Adjusted OIBDA ($1,013M) = **28.8x**
- EV / normalized full-calendar Adjusted OIBDA (~$1,120M: F1 $946M + MotoGP $201M − ~$27M corporate) = **26.0x**
- Normalized FCF yield on equity = low single digits (~2.2-2.4% on a $550-600M normalized FCF sensitivity)
- GAAP P/E on consensus: 52.5x current year ($1.90), 46.8x next year ($2.13)

**Peer comparison is uninformative.** The supplied Entertainment set spans Roku at 89.7x, SIRI at 11.7x, and two Liberty Live classes at negative multiples; the "median P/E 34.7" is FWONA, the same asset in a different share class trading at an 18% discount to its 52-week high. There is no clean comparable for a sanctioned-sports rights holder with a 2110 concession, which is why the multiple is set by rate levels and growth conviction rather than by a peer band.

**Is the market wrong?** The bull argument is that 26x normalized OIBDA understates a business with 34-84 year exclusive rights, mid-teens F1 OIBDA growth, $19.3bn of contracted revenue, and an unexercised MotoGP repricing option. The counter is arithmetic: at a low-single-digit normalized FCF yield, the equity is priced as a long-duration asset whose value is sensitive to the discount-rate regime. The rights concession lengthens duration risk rather than shortening it. A higher-for-longer rate environment is a direct headwind to this multiple, and the September calendar decision is a live near-term threat to the denominator.

**Cycle adjustment cuts both ways.** 2026 is a trough calendar year, so normalized OIBDA of $1,120M is roughly 10% above TTM — a real but modest adjustment, not the kind of trough distortion that makes a headline multiple meaningless. Building 2027: F1 at $1,030-1,080M (24 races, continued media and sponsorship repricing), MotoGP at $225-240M (new agreements, CAA-driven sponsorship), corporate at -$30M → **roughly $1,260M**. At $99.69 the stock trades at 23.1x that 2027 estimate. The consensus mean target of $119.25 implies roughly 27x 2027E — the analyst community is assuming both a full calendar and MotoGP delivery.

**Scenario framework** (net debt ~$3,100-3,400M, NCI $780-820M, ~251.5M shares):

## Competitive Landscape

F1's competitive position is close to unassailable at the level of the property and constrained at the level of scale. There is no substitute at the top of open-wheel motorsport, and the commercial concession runs to 2110. The relevant competition is other premium live sports content bidding for media budgets and attention, and on that axis F1 has been a share gainer for six years.

The key uncertainty is the US media transition. Apple is F1's licensee for exclusive US rights under a five-year agreement covering every session from the 2026 season, replacing ESPN. The economics are not disclosed in any filing; any circulated annual figure is unverified. Moving from linear distribution to subscription streaming plausibly raises the fee while narrowing addressable reach — an inference, not a fact. Management reports viewership up year-over-year with total hours watched +13%, but that is company-sourced and Apple has not published audience data. US fan growth underpinned the 2019-2024 F1 re-rating and sets the ladder for global sponsorship and promoter fees, so the absence of independent verification is a material information gap over the next two renewal cycles.

MotoGP's competitive logic is the reason to own the stock at all: apply F1's centralized commercial playbook to a property previously under-monetized under private ownership. The implied purchase price — 84% of equity at $3,659M implies 100% equity of ~$4,356M; adding $1,140M acquired debt less $375M acquired cash gives EV of roughly $5,121M, or ~25.5x FY2025 pro forma OIBDA and ~29x the implied FY2024 figure — was full. The $759M redeemable NCI carrying value now implies 100% MotoGP EV of roughly $5,630M, about 10% above the reconstructed acquisition EV, but that is a contractual Level 2 model output, not third-party validation.

We do not credit the revenue-mix shift (roughly 84% F1 / 16% MotoGP in H1) as risk diversification. Both franchises depend on holding an exclusive concession from a single governing body, both monetize through the same three fee streams, both face government-linked promoter counterparties, and both were disrupted by the same 2026 geopolitical calendar problem. No customer or counterparty concentration data was disclosed, so no claim about concentration improving or worsening is supportable in either direction.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Gulf calendar risk, now realized and still open.** Two April 2026 rounds were cancelled due to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East; one was relocated; Saudi Arabia is permanently lost for 2026; and the Qatar and Abu Dhabi decisions are deferred to September. Four of the originally scheduled 24 rounds are in the Gulf — roughly 17% of the calendar and, given that Gulf promoter fees sit at the high end of the range, a larger share of promotion revenue. This is the highest-impact near-term variable. If both remaining Gulf rounds fall out, TTM Adjusted OIBDA compresses further and covenant leverage rises mechanically again — most acutely at MotoGP, already at 4.5x, even though it is the F1 calendar that shrinks.

**2. Sanctioning-body termination.** The FIA and FIM agreements can be terminated for material breach, unpermitted change of control, interference with the championship, or insolvency; the 10-K states termination could force F1 to discontinue operations. Very low probability, total severity. This is the genuine tail in the equity.

**3. Parent liquidity and structural subordination.** $299M corporate cash, $475M converts due August 2027, no H1 2026 distributions from either non-recourse credit group, and restricted-payment tests whose formulas are not disclosed.

**4. Redeemable NCI — a dated, quantified claim.** Rollover sellers hold ~16% of MotoGP with put rights over one-third in July 2028, two-thirds in July 2030, and the remainder in July 2031, priced at contractual fair market value, settleable up to 50% in unregistered FWONK shares. Carrying value rose from $693M to $759M with an $88M fair-value adjustment charged directly to retained earnings in Q2. At today's mark, full redemption implies roughly $380M cash plus ~3.7M shares (1.47% post-issuance dilution) — modest now, growing with MotoGP's model valuation, and competing with the 2027 convertible for the same parent liquidity.

**5. MotoGP leverage and unproven integration.** 4.5x net covenant leverage, euro-functional (a 10% EUR/USD move shifts MotoGP revenue by ~$17M per quarter), and +1% constant-currency H1 growth against a 26-29x consolidated multiple that assumes acceleration.

**6. Governance thinned in a nine-month window.** Malone held 49.49% of aggregate voting power at June 30; the 10-Q discloses that his board resignation ended further exchanges under the July 2021 agreement and that his voting power could exceed 50%. The company reincorporated from Delaware to Nevada effective May 12, 2026. There is no CFO title among principal officers — Wendling serves as principal financial officer in a Chief Accounting Officer capacity — for a company that just closed a $3.7bn cross-border acquisition. Series C is non-voting. These are disclosed facts; we do not infer intent from the reincorporation's legal effect, and Malone's March 2026 written puts are inconsistent with a holder seeking to disadvantage the common.

**7. Tax exposure.** The SiriusXM and Liberty Live split-offs were structured as tax-free under Section 355 without private letter rulings. Q2's 76% effective tax rate and the disclosed possibility of net cash payments to settle items under discussion with tax authorities are live items.

**8. Consumer-side exposure at Las Vegas.** Las Vegas is F1's only self-promoted race, meaning Liberty bears ticketing and hospitality economics rather than collecting a fee. That converts a fixed-fee stream into discretionary consumer exposure. The ten-year extension to 2037 raises the duration of that exposure; its fee structure and capital commitments are undisclosed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 38.3% against 25.0% realized (a 13.2-point premium, 70th percentile within the chain) in a backwardated term structure — 38.3% at one month versus 36.0% at six — prices near-term event risk. That is consistent with the September Qatar/Abu Dhabi decision rather than with an already-reported earnings event. The reported 10.7% one-month put/call skew would ordinarily corroborate downside positioning, but the chain carries 92 total contracts with a put/call volume ratio of 0.01 and a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.00, and skew flips to -8.1% and -7.0% at three and six months. With essentially no put open interest, the skew figure is not a reliable positioning signal and we assign it low weight. The usable takeaway is the volatility level and shape: the market pays a meaningful premium for the next month of optionality, which argues against buying the stock ahead of the September decision and in favor of waiting. Short interest of 3.7% of float with 3.6 days to cover shows no crowded bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $132 (+32%)

Qatar and Abu Dhabi are held in September, and the 2027 calendar returns to 24 events. F1 delivers another year of low-teens revenue growth with OIBDA growing faster, taking F1 OIBDA toward $1.08bn in 2027 as the Apple relationship, Las Vegas through 2037, and the Pirelli/ServusTV/broadcast renewals feed the sponsorship and promoter fee ladder. MotoGP's 2027-2031 team and manufacturer framework plus the CAA appointment enable the repricing that has been absent, lifting MotoGP OIBDA toward $250M. Consolidated 2027 Adjusted OIBDA of ~$1,350M at 27x EV/OIBDA — a multiple this asset has sustained before — supports $132, with optionality from a resumed $1.1bn buyback if F1 upstreams cash at 2.7x leverage. Independent corroboration: Morgan Stanley and Guggenheim both carry Buy ratings (July 30), the consensus mean target is $119.25 with a high of $135, and hedge fund ownership rose to 78 holders from 71 in Q1, with at least one manager publicly adding on the Middle East cancellations.

### Bear Case — $78 (-22%)

The September decision removes Qatar and Abu Dhabi, cutting 2026 to 21 races and pushing MotoGP's covenant leverage above 4.5x on a mechanically smaller trailing base while F1's optics deteriorate. MotoGP's constant-currency revenue decline proves structural rather than transitional — legacy media contracts renew flat, and the ~29x trailing acquisition multiple is revealed as overpayment, raising impairment risk against $3,006M of MotoGP goodwill. The Apple transition trades reach for fee, US audience growth stalls, and the sponsorship ladder that drove the 2019-2024 re-rating flattens. With a low-single-digit FCF yield and the long end elevated, the multiple compresses to 20x on $1,150M of OIBDA. Parent liquidity tightens into the August 2027 convertible with no subsidiary distributions, forcing either share settlement or a refinancing at a higher coupon, while the July 2028 MotoGP put approaches. Governance offers no protection: Series C is non-voting, voting control drifts above 50% to a non-director, and the company sits in Nevada.

### Our view

The franchise quality is real and the rights duration is genuinely rare. What is missing at $99.69 is compensation for two specific unresolved items: a dated, uninsured calendar binary in September, and a MotoGP repricing thesis that has produced +1% constant-currency growth in its first full half under new ownership. Both are knowable within six months. The stock is 9% below its 52-week high, so this does not require a contrarian thesis; it is an asset priced for execution that has not yet occurred, at a low-single-digit cash yield. We would own it lower.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (downgraded from BUY). Rationale: growth_hold** — there is a specific, catalyst-linked twelve-month path to appreciation, but the margin of safety at $99.69 is inadequate for new capital.

**Base case: $110 (+10.3%)** — 25x EV/2027E Adjusted OIBDA of $1,260M (F1 $1,030-1,080M on a restored 24-race calendar with continued media/sponsorship repricing; MotoGP $225-240M; corporate -$30M), less ~$3,100M net debt and ~$800M redeemable NCI, over ~251.5M shares.

**Bull case: $132 (+32.4%)** — 27x on $1,350M, per the bull case above. Consistent with the $135 high analyst target.

**Bear case: $78 (-21.8%)** — 20x on $1,150M with both remaining Gulf rounds lost and MotoGP repricing failing.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to three dated checkpoints: the September 2026 Qatar/Abu Dhabi calendar decision; the Q3 2026 print (which will show the 8/22nds-to-8/23rds true-up and the first read on Sepang economics); and the FY2026 10-K in February 2027, which should disclose the 2027 calendar and a full year of MotoGP under Liberty ownership.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY on any of:**
1. Qatar and Abu Dhabi confirmed to proceed **and** a 24-event 2027 calendar published, with consolidated covenant leverage returning to 3.0x or below.
2. MotoGP constant-currency revenue growth of +5% or better for two consecutive quarters, or disclosed media-rights renewals at rates management characterizes as above expiring terms.
3. Price at $88-92 (roughly 22-23x EV/2027E OIBDA), which restores a double-digit margin of safety to the $110 base case. This is where we would add.
4. Resumption of repurchases under the $1.1bn authorization funded by an F1 distribution, which would simultaneously resolve the parent-liquidity question and the 2027 convertible.

## Conclusion

FWONK owns two legally exclusive world championships under concessions running to 2110 and 2060, with roughly $19.3bn of multi-year contracted revenue and a demonstrated ability to reprice F1 inventory faster than its contractual cost share grows (FY2025 revenue +13.5%, Adjusted OIBDA +19.6%). The Q2 headline collapse — revenue -38% at F1, Adjusted OIBDA -61% — is overwhelmingly a season-fee recognition artifact of five races versus nine, with team payments falling in lockstep.

What keeps this at HOLD rather than BUY is the price against two unresolved items. At $99.69 the enterprise is valued at $29.1bn, 26x normalized Adjusted OIBDA of ~$1.12bn and a low-single-digit normalized free-cash-flow yield. Against that full multiple, F1 faces an uninsured September decision on two Gulf rounds in a region where the April rounds were cancelled due to the ongoing Middle East conflict, and MotoGP — the asset that justified a ~29x trailing acquisition multiple — delivered +1% constant-currency revenue growth in H1 and -5% in Q2. The parent has $299M of cash against $475M of converts due August 2027 with no H1 distributions from either non-recourse credit group, and bought back no stock between May and July while the shares traded in the mid-$80s. Governance protections thinned materially: Series C is non-voting, voting power is drifting above 50% toward a non-director, and the company redomiciled to Nevada.

Base case $110 (+10%), bull $132 (+32%), bear $78 (-22%) — roughly +7.5% probability-weighted. Existing holders should stay long; the franchise is not impaired and the 2027 setup is favorable if the calendar restores and MotoGP begins to reprice. New capital should wait for either $88-92 or the first hard evidence on those two questions. Both should be answerable by the Q3 print.
