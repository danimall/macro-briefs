## Executive Summary

Performance Food Group closed fiscal 2026 (year ended June 27, 2026) with record net sales of $67.84B (+7.2%) and record Adjusted EBITDA of $1,929.4M (+9.2%). Beneath those headlines sits the central fact of this investment case: GAAP operating profit of $887.5M produced only $359.3M of net income, because interest expense reached $413.7M — up 78% in two years and equal to 46.6% of operating profit. GAAP diluted EPS of $2.29 is 17.9% below FY2024's $2.79 despite $9.56B more revenue. The Cheney Brothers acquisition (October 2024, ~$2.0B, funded entirely on the ABL) and a finance-lease-funded fleet build have shifted a large share of the operating improvement to lenders and to purchase-accounting amortization.

The operating business is genuinely improving. Gross margin expanded from 11.28% (FY2024) to 11.93% (FY2026); organic independent case growth was +5.9% with independents reaching 42.1% of Foodservice sales, the highest-gross-profit cohort; Convenience grew Adjusted EBITDA 16.7% on 5.9% sales growth with margin expanding from 1.66% to 1.83%. Adjusted free cash flow, after deducting the $241.6M of finance-lease principal that a peer buying trucks outright would show as capex, was roughly $788M — a 4.8% yield on the equity. That is a defensible mid-cycle cash profile.

What we cannot get to is asymmetry. At $104.48 the stock is 45.6x FY2026 GAAP EPS, ~29x amortization-adjusted EPS of approximately $3.57, 22.9x FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.55 (sum of reported quarters), 18.3x FY2027 consensus of $5.71, and ~12.0x EV/Adjusted EBITDA ($23.1B EV including $6.8B of debt and finance leases). Consensus has cut FY2027 by 3.0% over 90 days, the company has missed adjusted EPS in three of the last four quarters (Q4 by 2.7%), and the shares fell 8.3% across August 12–13 as the Q4 release and 10-K landed — a move larger than a 2.7% miss explains, implying forward commentary we cannot verify from the filings. Base case $117 (+12%), bull $137 (+31%), bear $86 (-18%). Probability-weighted expected return is mid-single-digit on a no-dividend staple. That is a HOLD, and we classify it as an uncertain hold rather than a growth hold: the FY2027 EPS ramp consensus requires is plausible but unproven, and the strategic optionality is real but unquantifiable.

## Company Overview

PFG is one of three national broadline food-away-from-home distributors alongside Sysco and US Foods, distributing over 300,000 products from approximately 154 facilities to over 350,000 customer locations. Three segments (FY2026 net sales / Segment Adjusted EBITDA):

| Segment | Net Sales | YoY | Seg. Adj. EBITDA | YoY | Margin (FY26 / FY25) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foodservice | $36,560.3M | +8.7% | $1,293.0M | +5.8% | 3.54% / 3.63% |
| Convenience | $25,965.2M | +5.9% | $475.4M | +16.7% | 1.83% / 1.66% |
| Specialty | $5,056.6M | +3.1% | $360.4M | +3.5% | 7.13% / 7.10% |
| Total | $67,839.5M | +7.2% | $1,929.4M | +9.2% | 2.84% / 2.79% |

The revenue mix is Convenience-heavy (38% of segment sales) but the profit mix is Foodservice-weighted: Foodservice generated 60.7% of segment Adjusted EBITDA on 54.1% of segment sales. Specialty carries the best margin (7.13%) and the worst volume trend — cases declined 0.6% in FY2026, so its 3.1% sales growth is price/mix rather than units. Convenience is a ~1.8%-margin business with structural cigarette decline offset by alternative nicotine mix shift.

The KPI management steers on is independent-restaurant case growth, because independents pay for service. FY2026: total Foodservice cases +6.6%, total independent cases +10.2%, organic independent cases +5.9%. Company-wide total cases +5.1% against organic +2.8% — the 230bp gap is acquisition contribution, and it matters for judging how much of the growth is repeatable without further capital deployment.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement, three years ($M):**

| | FY2026 | FY2025 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 67,839.5 | 63,298.9 | 58,281.2 |
| Gross profit | 8,090.6 | 7,416.6 | 6,577.1 |
| Operating profit | 887.5 | 816.3 | 826.4 |
| Interest expense | 413.7 | 358.4 | 232.2 |
| Net income (GAAP) | 359.3 | 340.2 | 435.9 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 1,929.4 | 1,766.9 | 1,506.1 |
| Diluted EPS | $2.29 | $2.18 | $2.79 |

Gross margin rose 65bp over two years to 11.93%. Adjusted EBITDA margin rose 26bp to 2.84%. GAAP operating margin *fell*, from 1.42% to 1.31%. The mechanism is straightforward: operating expenses grew $602.8M in FY2026, of which personnel was +$227.3M, acquisition-related opex from Cheney included +$156.4M, D&A +$57.1M (driven by transportation equipment and facilities under finance leases), fuel +$47.6M, and $20.2M of legal and professional fees tied to shareholder activism and the US Foods clean team agreement. Below the line, interest expense added another $55.3M. Interest now consumes 21.4% of Adjusted EBITDA, up from 15.4% in FY2024.

Combined FY2026 D&A was $813.9M (depreciation $541.9M, intangible amortization $272.0M) against cash capex of only $384.1M. Part of that wedge is purchase-accounting step-up that runs off over disclosed 10–12 year intangible lives, which is a legitimate reason to look past GAAP. Part of it is finance leases, where the economic cash cost appears as $241.6M of financing-section principal payments rather than as capex. Both readings deserve weight: the amortization is genuinely non-cash, and the lease principal is genuinely cash.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $1,413.7M (FY2025: $1,210.1M). Conventional FCF (OCF less capex) was $1,029.6M; after finance-lease principal, adjusted FCF was approximately $788M, or 4.8% of market capitalization and $5.02 per share. Two quality caveats are material. First, the FY2026 OCF improvement was substantially tax timing: the company received $52.3M of refunds, and cash taxes paid in the first nine months were $2.3M versus $111.1M a year earlier, attributable to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's 100% bonus depreciation and modified interest limitation — with management explicitly stating there is no material effect on the effective tax rate (26.0% in FY2026). This is deferral, visible in the deferred tax liability rising to $925.9M. Bonus depreciation is durable for a distributor that reinvests continuously, so this is not purely one-time, but the FY2026 step-change should not be extrapolated. Second, the LIFO reserve add-back to Adjusted EBITDA grew to $101.1M (FY2025 $88.1M, FY2024 $62.3M), of which $96.7M was Convenience — a real economic cost of replacing inventory at 4.5% product inflation that the non-GAAP measure excludes and that grows with inflation.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt including finance leases was $6.8B at year-end with $2.9B of ABL availability. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA is 3.47x; adding ~$1.0B of operating leases pushes lease-adjusted gross leverage toward 4.0x. 73% of debt is fixed; roughly $1.8B floats, so each 100bp costs ~$18M pre-tax (~$0.085/share, ~3.7% of GAAP EPS). The February 19, 2026 refinancing — $1,060M of 5.625% Notes due 2034 replacing the 5.500% Notes due 2027 — cleared the near maturity eight years out at a 12.5bp coupon increase, competent liability management. Nearest maturities are now the 2029 notes and the September 2029 ABL, a maturity concentration worth watching but three years away. The ABL's fixed-charge covenant is a springing test triggered only if availability falls below the greater of $375M or 10% of the borrowing base; with $2.9B available, liquidity risk is low.

Goodwill plus intangibles of $5,181.1M exceeded book equity of $4,721.7M at the Q3 balance sheet date, so tangible book is negative. For an acquisitive distributor that is a consequence of strategy rather than distress, but it removes the balance sheet as downside support and makes goodwill impairment a live tail — PFG has used only the qualitative "step zero" test in each of FY2024–FY2026.

**Financial health score: 5.0.** Strong liquidity, refinanced maturities, real cash generation, and covenant headroom argue for more. Interest at 46.6% of operating profit, 3.5x net leverage in a higher-for-longer regime, negative tangible book, a tax-flattered cash-flow year, and share count drifting up ~0.7% annually argue for less.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth engine is independent-restaurant penetration and Performance Brands. Organic independent cases +5.9% in FY2026, independents at 42.1% of Foodservice sales, and 65bp of two-year gross-margin expansion is evidence of share gain in the cohort that pays the most for service. Convenience added 8.8% case growth in Q3 from new chain wins, and earned income from manufacturers for distribution services — a structural, inflation-independent revenue line.

Three offsets temper the growth score. First, organic case volume company-wide was +2.8%; the difference between that and reported 5.1% is bought, and buying it costs interest and amortization. Second, Foodservice's Adjusted EBITDA margin declined from 3.63% to 3.54% despite the favorable independent mix, because segment opex grew 11.7% against gross profit growth of 10.2% — mix benefit was more than consumed by personnel, fuel, insurance and acquisition opex. Third, part of Convenience's 16.7% EBITDA growth is inflation-dependent: inventory holding gains flow to reported gross profit while the offsetting LIFO replacement cost is added back to Adjusted EBITDA. In a disinflating environment both reverse.

Consensus assumes the interest and amortization drag begins to unwind: FY2027 adjusted EPS of $5.71 against approximately $4.55 delivered in FY2026 implies +25% growth. The mechanics are plausible — Cheney anniversaries, deleveraging (net ABL repayments were $384.0M in FY2026), and organic case growth on a fixed cost base. But the delivery record is poor: three misses in four quarters, and FY2027 consensus has been cut 3.0% over 90 days with the current-year figure down 0.8%. There is no company FY2027 guidance available to us, and the 8.3% two-day decline coincident with the release suggests the market received a forward number below the $5.71 consensus.

Demand-side risk deserves naming rather than boilerplate. Food-away-from-home is where consumer retrenchment shows first. The 10-K also names GLP-1 weight-loss drugs explicitly under changes in consumer eating habits, which for a distributor whose profit pool is restaurant traffic plus candy and snack volume is a long-duration demand question rather than a filler risk. Independent restaurants — PFG's highest-margin cohort — are precisely the customers with the least contractual stickiness and the highest failure rate.

**Growth prospects score: 5.5.**

## Valuation Assessment

Multiple views, all at $104.48:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 GAAP EPS $2.29 | 45.6x |
| P/E on amortization-adjusted EPS ~$3.57 | 29.3x |
| P/E on FY2026 adjusted EPS ~$4.55 | 22.9x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $5.71 | 18.3x |
| EV/Adjusted EBITDA (EV ~$23.1B) | 12.0x |
| EV/Adj. EBITDA incl. operating leases | 12.5x |
| Adjusted FCF yield ($788M) | 4.8% |
| Dividend yield | 0.0% |

The amortization-adjusted EPS bridge: $2.29 plus $272.0M of intangible amortization at a ~74% after-tax rate over 157.0M shares, or roughly $1.28, giving ~$3.57. The gap between 45.6x GAAP and 12.0x EV/EBITDA is the single most important valuation fact here — the bull case requires accepting non-GAAP earnings, and the reconciling items growing fastest (interest +78%, depreciation +52.6%, LIFO add-back +62%) are the ones representing real cash or real economic cost.

Peer context: median Food Distributors P/E is 33.5x (USFD 33.5x, SYY 23.0x, CHEF 52.5x, ANDE 12.7x). PFGC's 45.6x GAAP sits above the median, but the comparison is contaminated by PFG's much larger purchase-accounting amortization load. On EV/EBITDA, ~12.0x is broadly in line with where large broadliners trade, and PFG grows faster than Sysco while carrying more leverage — those roughly offset. Sysco offers a 2.6% yield; PFG offers none.

Cross-check on base value two ways. Applying 12.0x to FY2027E Adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.07B (+7.2%) gives EV of $24.8B; deducting ~$6.4B net debt after modest paydown leaves $18.4B of equity, or $117 per share on 157.5M shares. Separately, 20.5x FY2027 consensus $5.71 is also $117. Bull case: 13.0x on $2.10B EBITDA, or 23x on $5.90 of EPS, gives $133–$136; a control-premium outcome could support the $143 high analyst target. Bear case: flat EBITDA of $1.93B at 10.5x, or 16x on $5.30, gives $85–$86.

That is +12% to base, +31% to bull, -18% to bear. The consensus mean target of $123.31 and median $130 imply more, and 12 of 14 analysts are positive — but with estimates falling 3% over 90 days and one sell rating added recently, those targets are stale relative to the direction of revisions rather than a floor.

We do not treat the stock as cheap. A compressed forward multiple on a 1.3% operating-margin business at 3.5x leverage with negative tangible book and a three-of-four miss record is the market pricing risk, not overlooking it. **Valuation score: 5.0.**

## Competitive Landscape

PFG competes in a fragmented industry topped by a three-firm national oligopoly. Its stated advantage is scale funding Performance Brands, procurement economics and route density, and the filings support the claim where it counts: independent-case share gains and gross-margin expansion in the highest-value cohort.

The Cheney Brothers deal is the clearest available read on consolidation economics, and it is sobering. PFG paid ~$2.0B, allocating $726.1M to PP&E (37% of price) — as much a Southeast infrastructure purchase as a customer book. Cheney contributed $3.7B of FY2026 net sales and added $142.8M of gross profit to Foodservice against $123.5M of incremental opex, implying roughly $19M of incremental Adjusted-EBITDA-level year-over-year contribution — before interest on the ~$2.0B of ABL debt used to buy it. The Q3 filing disclosed Cheney generated a $19.1M net loss on $1,756.2M of sales in its first nine months under PFG ownership, driven by purchase-accounting D&A. Two years in, the deal has added scale and Southeast density without yet adding per-share earnings.

Named competitive threats worth pricing: group purchasing organizations recruiting PFG's independent customers would transfer negotiating leverage in exactly the highest-margin channel; the absence of long-term contracts with certain customers means volume is re-competed continuously; and in a 1.31% operating-margin business, a competitor with structurally lower cost-to-serve can take share indefinitely on price. PFG also capitalizes upfront cash incentives paid to customers ($75.4M contract asset, up from $67.0M), which is where accelerating independent churn would first appear.

Trajectory is the right lens, and it is mixed-to-positive: gaining share in independents, expanding gross margin, growing Convenience profitability — while Specialty cases shrink and Foodservice segment margin slips. **Competitive position score: 6.0.**

## Risk Assessment

**Operating leverage on a 1.31% margin.** A 25bp gross-margin slip on FY2026 revenue is ~$170M, roughly 47% of GAAP net income. This is the business model, not news, but it sets the amplitude of every other risk.

**Diesel, largely unhedged.** Collars covered only ~32% of Q4 FY2026 gallons and ~13% of the following twelve months. A 10% rise in diesel adds ~$31.3M annually (~$0.15/share after tax), with surcharge recovery on a one-month lag. The 10-K cites Middle East hostilities as having contributed to significantly higher fuel prices in fiscal 2026, so this is a live and quantified exposure rather than a hypothetical.

**Rates.** ~$1.8B floats; each 100bp is ~$18M pre-tax. The ABL averaged 5.30% in Q3 FY2026, down from 5.86% at the FY2025 year-end. Consensus FY2027 EPS growth assumes interest drag stabilizes or falls, which is not guaranteed.

**Tobacco and nicotine decline** in a segment that is 38% of revenue at 1.83% margin, plus e-vapor regulation, excise-tax changes, and excise-tax float exposure.

**Consumer demand.** Restaurant traffic is the first line item households cut. GLP-1 adoption is a named, long-duration overhang on both restaurant volume and candy/snack distribution.

**Goodwill.** $3.57B goodwill plus $1.61B intangibles against $4.72B equity, tested only qualitatively for three consecutive years. Any independent-customer churn or Cheney underperformance flows to an impairment headline before it flows to cash.

**Strategic binary, direction unknown.** The 10-K quantified $20.2M of fees for shareholder activism and a clean team agreement with US Foods, and added "the potential impacts of shareholder activists or potential bidders" to its risk-factor list. A clean team agreement is the specific instrument competitors use to exchange sensitive information under counsel supervision in antitrust-sensitive diligence, so substantive exploration of a combination occurred. But no merger agreement, letter of intent, termination fee, deal-specific risk factor, or subsequent-event disclosure appears anywhere in the filings, and the fee is reported in the past tense as a fiscal-2026 expense. Direction (acquirer or target), scope (whole-company or partial), and status (live, dormant, dead) are all unestablished. An activist-linked director was seated in November 2025 following a September 2025 filing carrying the item-code signature of a cooperation agreement, but no Schedule 13D was retrievable, so stake size and standstill terms are unverified. This is optionality we can name but not underwrite — and part of the 45.6x GAAP multiple may reflect control-premium hope that could deflate.

**Information gap.** Shares fell 8.3% across August 12–13 on the Q4 release and 10-K. A 2.7% adjusted EPS miss does not explain that move; forward guidance or call commentary we do not have almost certainly does. Buying into that gap is not warranted.

**Unquantified potential positive.** The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court invalidation of IEEPA tariffs may produce refunds; the company states it cannot estimate the effect. It must not be modeled.

**Risk factors score: 4.8** — several specific, quantified, non-priced catalysts for downside (diesel, rates, goodwill, the strategic binary resolving negatively), partially offset by low liquidity risk and cleared litigation (the JUUL MDL exposure sits with subsidiaries fully indemnified and defense-funded by JUUL, and the parallel Illinois case was dismissed in October 2025).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 30.3% against 29.1% one-year realized is fair, and the collapse from 52.7% two days earlier confirms the earnings event has passed rather than that risk was resolved favorably. Term structure is in mild backwardation (30.3% at one week versus 26.6% at six months), consistent with residual near-term event sensitivity. Put/call open interest of 1.42 shows accumulated protection, while the near-term skew reads call-heavy — positioning is two-sided rather than directional. Short interest is 3.6% of float at 2.2 days to cover: no crowding, no squeeze mechanics, and no evidence that informed money is pressing a bear case. The options market corroborates the fundamental read of a fairly-priced situation with a live but unpriced strategic tail; it neither confirms nor challenges the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($137, +31%).** Independent case growth continues at 5–6% organically, Performance Brands penetration extends the 65bp of gross-margin gain, and the Cheney/finance-lease amortization and interest drag begins to anniversary. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $5.90–$6.00; the market pays 23x, or 13x EV/EBITDA on ~$2.10B, as deleveraging carries net leverage toward 3.0x. Bonus depreciation keeps cash taxes low, funding both paydown and the 99.7%-unused $498.5M repurchase authorization. Separately, the US Foods engagement resolves into a value-creating transaction — the highest-value route-density trade available in the sector — supporting the $143 high target or above.

**Bear case ($86, -18%).** Restaurant traffic softens as the consumer retrenches; independent-customer churn accelerates and the $75.4M capitalized incentive asset requires impairment testing. Diesel stays elevated with only 13% hedged and a one-month surcharge lag. Product inflation decelerates, removing Convenience's inventory holding gains and shrinking the LIFO add-back, so the 16.7% segment EBITDA growth reverses toward low single digits. Rates stay higher for longer on the floating $1.8B. Adjusted EBITDA is flat, the market pays 10.5x, and the strategic option expires worthless with the $20.2M a sunk cost. Goodwill testing moves from step zero to a quantitative test.

**Base case ($117, +12%).** Revenue grows 5–6%, Adjusted EBITDA grows ~7% to ~$2.07B, adjusted EPS lands near $5.50–$5.70 (slightly below current consensus, consistent with the three-of-four miss record and the -3.0% ninety-day revision), and the multiple holds near 12x EV/EBITDA. Modest deleveraging accrues to equity. The strategic option neither resolves nor disappears.

At 45/20/35 weights on base/bull/bear, expected return is roughly +5%. For a zero-dividend staple, that is approximately fair — the definition of HOLD.

**On management quality specifically**, the evidence is weak rather than alarming and we score it 4.6. Two of three incentive metrics (Adjusted EBITDA, Net Sales) are mechanically increased by debt- and lease-funded acquisitions because both acquisition interest and purchase-accounting D&A sit below the Adjusted EBITDA line; FY2025 PEO compensation actually paid hit a five-year high of $22.4M in a year GAAP net income fell 22%. Relative TSR is the third metric and the five-year record (313.74 indexed versus a 138.91 peer index) genuinely earned payouts, so we do not infer that the Cheney deal was chosen to move a metric — capability expansion in the Southeast is a sufficient rationale. The $500M authorization raised in May 2025 went 99.7% unused through FY2026 at prices between $83 and $115, most plausibly because clean-team and activist constraints prevented transacting; we decline to infer motive. Nine insiders sold and none bought across 20 months, but nearly all selling matched Rule 10b5-1 plans adopted in a tight February 2026 window, and the Executive Chair retained 1,621,552 shares (~1.0% of the company) while exercising options that were expiring with a $26.57 strike. The absence of any purchase is a mildly negative signal; it is not evidence of a bearish internal view. The unresolved CEO identity — the proxy names Holm as FY2025 PEO while the Q3 filing lists him as Executive Chair, with the intervening officer-change filings unread — is a governance transparency gap we cannot close.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 FY2027 results, expected early November 2026. That print delivers the first clean read on FY2027 EPS trajectory against the $5.71 consensus and the first post-10-K view of independent case growth without acquisition contribution.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price toward $88–$93 with fundamentals intact (organic independent case growth ≥5%, gross margin flat-to-up). At $90, base $117 is +30% and the FY2027 multiple compresses to ~15.8x — that is the margin of safety this capital structure requires.
2. Two consecutive quarters of adjusted EPS in line or better with FY2027 consensus stabilizing at or above $5.70, combined with net debt/Adjusted EBITDA moving below 3.2x.
3. Material repurchase activity against the $498.5M authorization — dollar volume above $75M in a quarter would signal both that transaction constraints have lifted and that the board views the equity as undervalued.
4. A disclosed, value-creating resolution of the US Foods engagement with terms in a filing.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2027 consensus cut below $5.30 (a further -7%), or a second consecutive quarter of Foodservice segment Adjusted EBITDA margin decline.
2. Organic independent case growth decelerating below 3%, or any impairment charge against the $75.4M customer-incentive contract asset — the earliest visible signal of independent churn.
3. Convenience Adjusted EBITDA growth falling below 4% as product inflation decelerates, confirming that inventory holding gains and rebate estimates were carrying that segment.
4. Any move from step-zero to a quantitative goodwill test, or an impairment against the $3.57B goodwill balance.
5. Net leverage rising above 4.0x on a further debt-funded acquisition without disclosed per-share accretion.

## Conclusion

**HOLD at $104.48.** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on PFGC, and the bar for repeating it should be high, so state the case plainly: the FY2026 10-K confirms rather than resolves the tension that has held this rating in place. The distribution business is winning where it matters — 5.9% organic independent case growth, 65bp of gross-margin expansion, 16.7% Convenience EBITDA growth — while the capital structure built to buy that position routes the gains to interest expense (46.6% of operating profit) and purchase-accounting amortization ($272.0M). GAAP EPS of $2.29 remains 18% below FY2024 on 16% more revenue.

The score of 5.2 continues the drift from 6.2 in May, and the composition matters: competitive position holds at 6.0 on genuine share gain, while financial health (5.0), management quality (4.6) and risk factors (4.8) drag. The 8.3% two-day decline improves entry arithmetic and we do not mark down the thesis for it — but the decline coincided with a Q4 miss, an unavailable FY2027 guide, and a 3.0% ninety-day consensus cut, so the price move is not purely sentiment.

At 18.3x FY2027 consensus and 12.0x EV/EBITDA, with +12% to base and -18% to bear, expected return is approximately fair. The strategic optionality from the US Foods clean team agreement and the activist board seat is real and potentially large, but no merger agreement exists in any filing and the direction is unknown; it is not something to pay for. Existing owners should hold and let the November print resolve the FY2027 trajectory. New capital should wait for $88–$93 or for two quarters of delivered adjusted EPS with leverage moving through 3.2x.
