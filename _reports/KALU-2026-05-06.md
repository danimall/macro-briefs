## Executive Summary

Kaiser Aluminum has undergone a fundamental earnings transformation over the past 12 months, driven by 50% Section 232 tariffs on aluminum imports, the completion of multi-year capacity investments at Trentwood and Warrick, and a conversion revenue pricing model that captures rising fabrication premiums. Q1 2026 delivered record Adjusted EBITDA of $129M (annualized $516M vs. $310M in FY2025), FCF of $69M after negative FCF in FY2025, and the company raised full-year guidance to 20-30% EBITDA growth. The business model is structurally sound: metal-price-neutral conversion economics, high qualification barriers in aerospace and packaging, and declining capex against growing earnings.

The problem is price. KALU has risen 167% from its May 2025 low of $65.69 to $175.92, now trading above the analyst consensus target of $165 and within 4% of its 52-week high of $181.47. At ~17x forward earnings on $10.23 current-year consensus, the stock is pricing in sustained tariff protection, full aerospace recovery, and continued metal price lag benefits — all of which carry reversal risk. Coordinated insider selling ($15M+ across CEO, CFO, EVP, and directors in late April 2026) after the stock doubled signals that management itself sees fair value at or below current levels. The correct posture is to hold existing positions for the growth runway while acknowledging that entry at $176 offers limited margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Kaiser Aluminum manufactures semi-fabricated specialty aluminum products — flat-rolled plate and sheet, extruded rod and bar, and drawn tubes — serving four primary end markets: Packaging (51% of shipments), General Engineering (22%), Aero/HS Products (18%), and Automotive Extrusions (9%). The company operates 14 manufacturing facilities, primarily in the U.S., with approximately 3,840 employees (~65% unionized under USW contracts).

The critical business model feature is metal price neutrality. Kaiser passes aluminum commodity price fluctuations through to customers, earning profit on the "conversion" — the value-added fabrication work. Reported revenue of $3.37B in FY2025 includes ~$1.92B of aluminum pass-through; true conversion revenue was $1.45B. This distinction matters enormously for valuation — the company's economic engine operates on $1.31/lb conversion revenue, not the $3.04/lb total sales price.

Key facilities include Trentwood (Spokane, WA) for aerospace and general engineering plate, and Warrick (Evansville, IN) for packaging coated products. Both are effectively irreplaceable production assets with limited global alternatives.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability:**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 (Ann.) |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Net Sales | $3.09B | $3.02B | $3.37B | ~$4.1B |
| Operating Income | $95.8M | $87.7M | $188.8M | ~$252M |
| Adj. EBITDA | ~$210M | $241.0M | $310.2M | ~$516M |
| Net Income | $47.2M | $46.8M | $112.5M | ~$252M |
| Conversion Rev/lb | ~$1.24 | ~$1.24 | $1.31 | ~$1.45+ |

Operating income more than doubled in FY2025 (+115% YoY) on better pricing/mix, lower manufacturing costs from completed capacity investments, and $93M of favorable metal price lag (vs. $45M in FY2024). Q1 2026 extended the acceleration with $129M EBITDA on 31.8% margin (+1,200bps YoY).

**Cash Flow Inflection:**

FY2025 OCF was $111M but FCF was negative $25.5M due to $137M capex and $207M working capital build from rising aluminum prices. Q1 2026 generated $69M FCF in a single quarter. With capex guided at $120-130M for FY2026 (declining from peak) and EBITDA growing 20-30%, the FCF profile is structurally improving. At the guided midpoint, FY2026 FCF could approach $200-250M.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt of ~$1.05B in fixed-rate senior notes (4.50% due 2031, 5.875% due 2034). D/E of 2.1x is elevated but manageable for a conversion-model manufacturer with predictable cash flows. Net debt leverage improved to 2.8x EBITDA at Q1 2026 vs. 3.4x at year-end. Liquidity of $596M (primarily revolver availability) provides substantial cushion. No maturities until 2031.

**Key Accounting Consideration:**

The switch from LIFO to Weighted Average Cost inventory valuation, effective January 1, 2025, coincided with rising aluminum prices. Under rising prices, WAC produces lower COGS relative to LIFO. While the company restated prior periods for comparability, this change likely contributed to reported margin improvement. Additionally, $93M of metal price lag in FY2025 — nearly 30% of Adjusted EBITDA — represents timing benefits rather than operational improvement and will reverse if aluminum prices decline.

## Growth Analysis

**Estimate Revisions — Strongly Positive:**

Current-year EPS consensus has risen 35.7% in 30 days (from $7.54 to $10.23), driven by the Q1 2026 beat of $3.74 vs. $1.90 estimate (97% surprise). Next-year estimates at $10.81 have risen 18.6% over 30 days. This degree of upward revision momentum is exceptional and reflects genuine fundamental inflection.

**Growth Drivers:**

1. **Tariff protection (50% Section 232):** Creates a structural pricing umbrella. Foreign competitors "cannot reduce prices quick enough" per CEO Harvey. This is the single largest driver of current earnings power.

2. **Capacity utilization:** Warrick's fourth coating line targeting 80% utilization in 2026 (commissioned in 2025). Trentwood Phase VII expansion completed. These investments depressed 2024-2025 margins but are now contributing.

3. **Aerospace recovery:** Aero/HS shipments fell 16% in FY2025 due to Boeing production issues and plate destocking. Management describes this as "seventh inning" — implying 1-2 more quarters before recovery volumes flow through. This represents a genuine additional growth layer not yet in the numbers.

4. **Defense demand:** Some programs seeing "quadrupled expected demand" per CEO commentary. General Engineering segment benefits from military armor, ordnance, and semiconductor manufacturing applications.

5. **Packaging secular shift:** Aluminum's infinite recyclability drives secular shift from plastic. Kaiser's coated can products (~75% of packaging shipments) carry higher margins.

**Risks to Growth:**

- Tariff reversal or reduction would immediately compress pricing power
- Metal price lag reversal if aluminum falls
- Automotive weakness (-8% in Q1 2026) tied to macro uncertainty
- Single-facility concentration risk at Trentwood and Warrick

## Valuation Assessment

At $175.92, KALU trades at:
- 19.2x trailing P/E (on $9.16 TTM EPS)
- ~17.2x forward P/E (on $10.23 current-year consensus)
- ~5.5x EV/EBITDA on annualized Q1 run rate (~$516M)
- ~8.6x EV/EBITDA on FY2026 guided midpoint (~$370-400M)

The stock is trading above the analyst consensus target of $165.25 (median $168). The mean target implies 6% downside from current levels. One strong sell recommendation exists alongside one buy and two holds.

The valuation is reasonable if current earnings power is sustainable — 17x on $10+ EPS for a manufacturer with strong competitive position and declining capex is not expensive in absolute terms. The key uncertainty is whether $10+ EPS represents normalized earnings or a cyclical peak amplified by tariff protection and metal price lag tailwinds.

Bear case valuation: If tariffs are reduced or metal lag reverses, normalized EPS could be $6-7, implying 25-29x forward — clearly overvalued. Bull case: If aerospace fully recovers and Warrick reaches full utilization, EPS could reach $12-13, implying 13.5-14.7x — undervalued.

The market is pricing in the optimistic scenario. At $176, the stock requires continued execution plus sustained tariff protection plus aerospace recovery to justify current multiples. This is a priced-for-perfection situation rather than a mispricing opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

Kaiser occupies a strong but narrow competitive position:

1. **Qualification barriers:** Multi-year customer qualification processes in aerospace (Boeing, Airbus supply chains) and packaging (FDA compliance) create switching costs that are genuinely high.

2. **Domestic protection:** As one of few remaining U.S.-based specialty aluminum fabricators, Kaiser benefits both from tariffs and from defense/government mandates requiring domestic sourcing.

3. **KaiserSelect® differentiation:** Proprietary products that reduce waste for customers create sticky relationships beyond commodity pricing.

4. **Limited alternatives:** There are very few qualified aerospace plate producers globally; Trentwood is one of the largest.

Competitive risks include Constellium (European producer increasingly serving North American aerospace) and Novelis (packaging specialist with scale advantages). UBS recently initiated coverage preferring Constellium over Kaiser on scrap spread dynamics.

The competitive position is improving in the near term due to tariff protection and capacity investments. Whether it's improving structurally depends on whether tariffs persist — without them, the import competition that existed pre-2018 would reassert.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Dependency (Critical):** The single largest risk factor. Management explicitly credits tariffs for "better demand" and pricing power. The 50% Section 232 rate creates an enormous pricing umbrella that could be reduced under different political leadership or trade policy shifts. This is partially priced (the stock was $66 pre-tariff enhancement in May 2025) but any indication of reversal would cause significant derating.

**Metal Price Lag Reversal:** $93M of favorable timing benefits in FY2025 represents a significant earnings quality concern. If aluminum prices decline from current elevated levels, this tailwind becomes a headwind in future quarters.

**Concentration Risk:** Two facilities (Trentwood, Warrick) produce the vast majority of high-value products. An operational disruption at either would be severe. The explicit Alcoa dependency at Warrick for water and operational resources adds an unusual counterparty risk.

**Labor Relations:** Four USW contracts expire within one year. At ~65% unionized with USW holding 40% board nomination rights through 2030, labor disruptions are a recurring risk that constrains management flexibility.

**Customer Concentration:** Top 5 customers = 56% of sales, with the largest at 16%. Loss of any major relationship would be immediately material.

**Insider Selling:** CEO sold $8.7M, CFO sold $1.5M, EVP sold $2.8M, plus director sales of $2.6M — all in late April 2026 after the stock doubled. While partially tied to equity grant vesting, the coordinated nature and timing signals that management sees limited near-term upside at these levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($195-210, 11-19% upside):**
- Aerospace volumes recover fully in H2 2026 (+15-20% from trough)
- Warrick coating line reaches 85%+ utilization
- Tariffs remain intact through 2027
- Metal price lag remains favorable
- FCF exceeds $250M, enabling accelerated deleveraging and potential buyback restart
- Forward P/E re-rates to 18-20x on $10.50+ sustained EPS

**Bear Case ($110-130, 26-37% downside):**
- Tariff reduction or elimination under new trade policy direction
- Metal price lag reversal as aluminum declines from elevated levels
- Aerospace recovery delayed by Boeing production issues
- Consumer weakness impacts packaging demand
- Normalized EPS reverts to $6-7 range
- Stock re-rates to 16-18x normalized earnings

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $140-150 range (14-15x forward EPS), providing 15%+ upside to fair value with margin of safety
- Aerospace shipment volumes show consecutive quarterly growth, confirming recovery thesis
- Tariff policy receives bipartisan legislative support (reducing reversal risk)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any indication of Section 232 tariff reduction or elimination
- Q2 or Q3 2026 EBITDA below $100M (suggesting Q1 was peak rather than baseline)
- Insider selling accelerates further from current elevated levels
- Metal price lag turns negative (aluminum prices decline >15% from current levels)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) will be the critical data point — it will confirm whether Q1 represents sustainable run-rate or a one-time combination of favorable timing effects.

## Conclusion

Kaiser Aluminum is a fundamentally improved business. The completion of capacity investments, 50% tariff protection, aerospace recovery potential, and conversion pricing model create a genuine multi-year earnings growth story. The Q1 2026 results and raised guidance confirm the inflection is real.

However, the stock at $176 has priced in the optimistic scenario. Trading above analyst consensus, with coordinated insider selling at these levels, and dependent on sustained tariff protection for current earnings power, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. A 167% move in 12 months has compressed future returns. The correct approach is to hold for the continued growth trajectory while waiting for either a meaningful pullback or additional evidence that the earnings run-rate is sustainable and not amplified by reversible tailwinds.

HOLD — with positive 12-month growth thesis contingent on tariff persistence and aerospace recovery confirmation.
