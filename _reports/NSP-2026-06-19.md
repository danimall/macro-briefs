**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Insperity is a premium, founder-led Professional Employer Organization (PEO) that experienced a severe earnings collapse in 2025, with operating income falling from +$117M in 2024 to -$10M and GAAP EPS swinging to -$0.19. The cause was a single-variable shock: health benefits costs rose 9.2% per covered employee against contractual pricing that resets only annually at roughly 3%, compressing gross profit per worksite employee (WSEE) by 15% ($285 to $242/month). This was a margin event against a structurally lagged pricing model; revenue still grew 4% to $6.81B and the WSEE base held near 310,000.

The investable question is whether 2025 was a cyclical/operational trough or a structural break. Q1 2026 data points to the former. Health cost inflation decelerated to 5.2%, the gross-profit-per-WSEE decline narrowed from -15% to -2%, and adjusted EBITDA returned to growth (+1% YoY at $103M). Management engaged three concrete margin levers: annual repricing at renewals, a new UnitedHealthcare contract effective 1/1/2026 that cut per-claimant stop-loss retention to $500K from $1M (a structural reduction in tail risk), and plan-design changes plus an ~8% non-sales headcount cut. The 2026 guide of $170-230M adjusted EBITDA implies 30-76% growth.

The strongest forward signal is insider activity. Founder-CEO Paul Sarvadi made repeated open-market purchases through 2026: roughly $4.7M in March at $22-24, $2.9M in May at $28-30, and $7.9M (233,000 shares) at $34.05 on June 3, roughly doubling his stake from ~457K to ~1.1M shares. The CFO, COO, GC, and a director also bought in the open market. Clustered C-suite buying that accelerated as the stock recovered is a high-conviction bet that the trough is transitory. We rate NSP a HOLD with a positive 12-month bias; the recovery is credible and underway, but the pace is uncertain, the dividend exceeds GAAP earnings, and the Chubb workers'-comp renewal in September 2026 is an unhedged second-shock risk that keeps this short of a BUY.

## Company Overview

Insperity pioneered the PEO category in 1986 and remains one of the largest U.S. operators. It provides outsourced HR to small and medium-sized businesses (roughly 10-5,000 employees) under a co-employment model. Through a Client Service Agreement, Insperity becomes co-employer of the client's WSEEs, assuming payroll, payroll-tax administration, benefits, workers' compensation, and compliance, while the client retains operational control.

The revenue model drives everything about the financials. Insperity bills clients "gross billings" of ~$45.6B in 2025 but recognizes revenue only as the markup over WSEE payroll cost (~$6.8B in 2025). It collects payroll funding concurrently with payroll, carrying minimal client credit risk. This produces extremely high asset turnover (~3.1x) on razor-thin equity (~$46M on $2.2B of assets), because the balance sheet is dominated by pass-through payroll and tax liabilities. Standard balance-sheet ratios (debt/equity of ~48x, Piotroski 1/5) are artifacts of this structure plus the loss year, not solvency signals; the Altman Z of 3.17 sits in the "safe" zone, driven by asset turnover.

Service tiers are HR360 (flagship full-service), HR360 Select Edition (lower-cost, ~16% of WSEEs), and HRScale, a new middle-market offering built on Workday's HCM platform, with first clients live in Q1 2026. The company averaged ~310,000 WSEEs/month in 2025 across ~4,200 corporate employees and 73 offices in 44 markets. It differentiates on a high-touch model with a staff-support ratio ~55% above industry average, historically translating to gross profit per WSEE ~113% above industry. Largest competitors are the PEO units of ADP and Paychex, plus TriNet, Vensure, and tech-driven entrant Rippling.

## Financial Analysis

The 2022-2025 trajectory shows a clear three-year margin erosion rather than a one-off shock:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $5.94B | $6.49B | $6.58B | $6.81B (+4%) |
| Gross profit | $1,011M | $1,037M | $1,052M | $900M (-14%) |
| Operating income | $250M | $219M | $117M | -$10M |
| Net income | $179M | $171M | $91M | -$7M |
| Diluted EPS | — | $4.47 | $2.42 | -$0.19 |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | $354M | $270M | $131M (-51%) |

Net income fell from $179M (2022) to $91M (2024) to -$7M (2025) as margins compressed even while revenue grew each year. The proximate trigger was the medical-cost-trend miss, plus a ~$40M adverse swing in prior-period claims run-off (a +$11M cost headwind in 2025 versus a -$29M tailwind in 2024). Operating income per WSEE went from +$32 to -$3/month.

Reported operating cash flow of -$278M and FCF of -$309M in 2025 are misleading. PEO operating cash flow swings with payroll timing and the day the period ends, and the 2024 comparison was inflated by $440M of client Employee Retention Credit refunds that flowed out in early 2025. The more meaningful liquidity measure — "adjusted cash" excluding payroll/tax pass-throughs and client prepayments — was ~$57M at year-end 2025 and ~$36M at Q1 2026, supplemented by a $750M revolver with ~$380M undrawn. True liquidity is thin but adequate.

Q1 2026 marks the turn. Revenue rose 2% to $1,895M; gross profit fell 3% to $302M, but the gross-profit-per-WSEE decline narrowed to -2% from a -21% drop in Q4 2025. The earnings-surprise record reflects the volatility: Q1 2026 beat by 5.1% ($1.31 vs $1.25 estimate) after three consecutive misses, the worst being Q3 2025 at -188.9%. The beat after a streak of misses is consistent with a turning point, though one quarter is not a trend.

Capital allocation is the central tension. Insperity paid ~$90M in dividends (~$2.40/year, ~6.6% yield) and repurchased ~$19M of stock in 2025 despite the loss; the dividend currently exceeds GAAP net income. Management amended its credit facility in late 2025 to exclude dividends from the interest-coverage covenant and raise the maximum leverage ratio, an explicit move to protect the payout. On a cash-earnings basis, if the 2026 adjusted EBITDA guide materializes, the dividend is covered; if the recovery stalls, the dividend and the bull case are simultaneously at risk.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a margin-recovery story, not a volume story. Management is deliberately pursuing price over volume, walking away from underpriced business, with WSEEs guided down 1-2.3% for 2026. WSEE count and top-line revenue will be poor proxies for the turnaround in 2026; gross profit per WSEE and adjusted EBITDA are the metrics that matter.

The 2026 guide is the anchor: adjusted EBITDA $170-230M (+30% to +76% over 2025's $131M), adjusted EPS $1.60-2.60 (vs $1.03), GAAP diluted EPS $0.48-1.48. The width of the range signals genuinely low visibility on the pace of margin restoration. The recovery levers are concrete and partly structural: annual repricing flowing through 2026 renewals, the new United contract terms, plan-design changes, and a ~8% headcount reduction in non-sales and BPA roles. Within the mix, middle-market WSEEs grew 9% and now represent 29% of the base, and HRScale went live in Q1.

Estimate revisions remain a near-term headwind and temper enthusiasm. Current-quarter consensus EPS was cut 21.3% over 90 days, next-quarter cut 36.5%, current-year cut 4.1%, and next-year cut 9.6%. Falling estimates against an improving operational picture reflect the gap between sell-side caution after three misses and management's guide; it is the single most important reason this is not a higher-conviction call. The bull thesis requires the Q1 turn to compound; the estimate trend says the Street is not yet convinced. We weight the operational data above the lagging revisions, but the divergence keeps Growth Prospects mid-range rather than high.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here given the loss year (-56x, an artifact of negative EPS). The appropriate frame is forward and normalized. At $36.22, on the 2026 adjusted EPS guide midpoint of ~$2.10, NSP trades at ~17x; on next-year consensus of $2.80, ~13x. Against the peer median P/E of 23.2 (RHI 24.4, PCTY 23.2, KFY 14.5), NSP trades at a discount on forward numbers, but the discount is justified rather than a clear mispricing: NSP carries demonstrated margin volatility and dividend-coverage risk that the higher-multiple peers (PCTY, RHI) do not.

The valuation does not offer obvious asymmetry today. On the bull side, if 2026 adjusted EBITDA reaches the upper guide ($230M) and the multiple re-rates toward peers on a restored EPS [unverified — 2027 EPS not in sources], the stock has meaningful upside toward the high analyst target of $54. On the bear side, if margin recovery stalls and the dividend is cut, fair value compresses toward the low-$20s where the stock traded as recently as February-March 2026. The mean analyst target of $39.50 sits ~9% above the current price; the median of $35.50 sits below it. With estimates falling, not rising, the "targets lag reality" logic does not apply. A cheap forward multiple on a business with realized margin instability is the kind of optical discount that has historically required an independent catalyst before it pays off; the dividend yield provides some support but the payout itself is the contested variable. We score valuation neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

The PEO industry is large, fragmented, and under-penetrated — Insperity estimates only ~10% of the SMB market is a strong fit, and most "competition" is companies keeping HR in-house. Insperity competes at the premium, full-service end against ADP, Paychex, TriNet, and fast-growing, price-undercutting tech entrants Rippling and Vensure.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-pressured. Retention improved to 83% in 2025 from 81% in 2024, and the high-touch model continues to command premium gross profit per WSEE. But the 2025 margin shock exposed the structural vulnerability of the partially self-funded benefits model concentrated on a single carrier (United, ~85% of coverage), and the longer-term strategic risk is that lower-cost, AI-enabled competitors erode the premium-service value proposition. HRScale is the defensive/offensive response in the middle market, but it is unproven, launched only in Q1 2026, and increases per-client concentration risk as it targets larger clients (>1,000 WSEEs). The company is holding its position rather than gaining share, which supports a neutral score on trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Health benefits cost-trend mismatch (the primary risk, now realized).** With ~85% of coverage through United on a partially self-funded basis, Insperity bears claims-cost risk but reprices annually. A cost spike instantly compresses margins, as 2025 demonstrated. The new $500K stop-loss retention reduces tail exposure structurally, but the model remains the dominant swing factor.

**Chubb workers' comp renewal (9/30/2026).** This is the most underappreciated near-term risk. An unfavorable renewal could deliver a second cost shock into the recovery year. There is no public read on terms yet; it warrants close monitoring.

**Dividend coverage.** The dividend exceeds GAAP net income. Covenant amendments protecting it confirm management's intent but also flag that coverage is the binding constraint. A cut would be the clearest signal the turnaround has failed.

**HRScale/Workday execution.** Significant resources committed to an unproven product targeting larger, more concentration-prone clients.

**Concentration and contract cancellation.** Geographic concentration (TX 17%, CA 15%, NY 10%); standard CSAs cancel on 30 days' notice; attrition ~17%/year, concentrated in Q1. ERC claims filed on behalf of clients create a contingent liability if the IRS disallows claims.

**Short interest** of 13.6% of float (4.2M shares, 5.4 days to cover) reflects elevated institutional bearishness, consistent with a contested turnaround.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 70.3% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 69.7% (spread 0.5%), so options are fairly priced rather than signaling an imminent surprise; the IV percentile within the current chain is low (15%). Put/call skew is balanced (2.9%), not flagging directional fear. The term structure is in mild contango (70.3% near-term vs 74.5% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.8%. The reported P/C volume and OI ratios of 0.00 and the anomalous "ATM IV 699.8%" delta reading are thin-options data artifacts and are treated as non-informative. Net read: the options market is not pricing an acute near-term catalyst, consistent with the slow-grind recovery thesis rather than a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-54).** The Q1 2026 turn compounds. Health inflation continues normalizing, repricing flows through 2026 renewals, the $500K stop-loss caps large-claim volatility, and adjusted EBITDA lands at the upper end of guidance ($230M). Adjusted EPS recovers toward $2.60+ at the top of the 2026 guide. The multiple re-rates toward the peer median (~20-23x) on restored earnings, and the founder-CEO's ~$15M of open-market buying proves prescient. Upside to the high analyst target of $54 (+49%).

**Bear Case ($20-24).** Margin recovery stalls — the Chubb renewal delivers a second cost shock, or health inflation re-accelerates, or competitive price pressure from Rippling/Vensure forces concessions. Adjusted EBITDA misses toward the low end (~$170M or below), the dividend is cut to preserve liquidity, and the stock retraces to the low-$20s (-34% to -45%).

**Base Case (~$42).** The recovery proceeds at a measured pace, with 2026 adjusted EBITDA near the guide midpoint (~$200M) and adjusted EPS ~$2.10. The dividend holds on cash-earnings coverage. The market awards ~18-19x forward earnings on partially-restored confidence, supporting a price in the low-$40s (+16%).

The balance of evidence favors the recovery: the margin problem is mechanical and being fixed, the United contract is structurally de-risked, and management is betting substantial personal capital at current levels. What holds this short of a BUY is the combination of falling consensus estimates, the unhedged Chubb renewal, the dividend-coverage tension, and the absence of demonstrated multi-quarter margin restoration. One inflection quarter against three prior misses is encouraging but not yet conclusive.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive 12-month bias (growth hold). The thesis is that 2025 was a cyclical/operational trough being actively repaired, supported by the Q1 2026 turn and founder-CEO insider buying.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August), followed by the Chubb workers'-comp renewal outcome around 9/30/2026. These two events should resolve much of the recovery's visibility.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 confirms the margin recovery: gross profit per WSEE flat-to-positive YoY and adjusted EBITDA growth accelerating beyond the +1% Q1 print, with management reaffirming or raising the guide.
- Estimate revisions turn positive — consensus current-year and next-year EPS revised upward over a 30-90 day window, reversing the current downtrend.
- Favorable Chubb renewal terms confirmed without a material cost step-up.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut, or covenant pressure forcing a liquidity-preserving action.
- Health cost inflation re-accelerating above ~7-8% per covered employee, or the Chubb renewal delivering a quantified second cost shock.
- Adjusted EBITDA tracking below the low end of guidance ($170M) through mid-year, signaling the turnaround has stalled.

## Conclusion

Insperity's 2025 collapse was margin-driven, mechanical, and in principle fixable — a health-cost-trend miss against an annually-repriced contract structure, not a deterioration in demand or competitive standing. Q1 2026 provides credible, accelerating evidence the fix is working, and adjusted EBITDA returned to growth. The 2026 guide implies 30-76% adjusted EBITDA recovery, and the founder-CEO has put ~$15M of personal capital behind the trough thesis, buying in size as the stock rose from ~$19 to ~$34.

The recovery's pace is genuinely uncertain — consensus estimates are still falling, the dividend exceeds GAAP earnings and depends on the recovery materializing, and the September 2026 Chubb renewal is an unhedged second-shock risk. The forward valuation (~13-17x recovery earnings) is reasonable but not compellingly cheap relative to the realized margin volatility, and offers no obvious asymmetry until the Street's estimate trend turns. We rate NSP a HOLD with a positive bias, with the path to a BUY upgrade running directly through a Q2 confirmation of the margin recovery and a clean Chubb renewal.
