**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

StandardAero is the world's largest independent, pure-play provider of aerospace engine aftermarket (MRO) services, spun into the public market via a Carlyle-backed October 2024 IPO at $24. The business sits at the intersection of two durable tailwinds: an aging global engine installed base that supply-chain constraints are keeping in service longer (raising shop-visit volumes), and a wave of 2010s-vintage engines entering their prime maintenance windows through the decade. FY2025 revenue grew 15.8% to $6,062.5M with broad-based strength, and the small but high-margin Component Repair Services (CRS) segment grew 19.6% at roughly 28-30% EBITDA margins, the clearest source of future margin expansion inside an otherwise ~13% consolidated EBITDA-margin story.

The reported financials require careful reading. Net income leapt from $11.0M to $277.4M, but roughly $108M of that swing came from lower interest expense following post-IPO deleveraging, not operating outperformance. The cleaner gauges are +36.7% operating income and a flat 13.3% adjusted EBITDA margin, held back by LEAP/CFM56 new-program ramp costs. Deleveraging is real and material: net debt/EBITDA fell from 5.3x to 2.9x in a year, and the competitive moat (OEM authorizations, first independent LEAP-1A/1B MRO in the Americas, ~80% of revenue under long-term agreements) is genuine.

Three items keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. First, management disclosed three unremediated material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025, uncomfortable in a company whose revenue depends on judgmental "estimated margin by engine platform" inputs where a 1% change moves annual revenue by $17.5M. Second, Carlyle still owns ~31% after three secondaries in twelve months, an ongoing supply overhang. Third, the stock trades at roughly 24x forward EPS against estimates that are broadly flat-to-falling and a 3-of-4 recent earnings-miss record. The secular compounding case is intact; the entry math and controls picture argue for patience.

## Company Overview

StandardAero operates two reporting segments:

- **Engine Services (~88% of revenue; ~13.2% adjusted EBITDA margin):** Full-suite MRO of gas turbine engines and APUs, on-wing and field service, asset management, and engineering across commercial, military/helicopter, and business aviation end markets.
- **Component Repair Services / CRS (~12% of revenue; ~28-30% adjusted EBITDA margin):** Engine piece-part and accessory repairs. CRS is the high-value growth engine, with margins roughly double the Engine Services segment and growth of ~20% per year.

The company has 100+ years of operating history, ~8,000 employees, and facilities across 11 countries. Its competitive position is gated by OEM authorizations and licenses (Rolls-Royce, Honeywell, Safran), most strategically its position as the first independent LEAP-1A/1B MRO provider in the Americas, the successor platform to the enormous CFM56 installed base it already services. Roughly 80% of revenue is under long-term agreements. Military/helicopter is ~17.8% of revenue ($1,081M).

The structural tension in the business is that OEMs are simultaneously StandardAero's licensors, its largest suppliers (four largest OEM suppliers account for a majority of parts purchases), significant customers (top four OEM customers ~36% of revenue, down from 41%), and its largest competitors (GE Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce, Safran service divisions). Independents MTU, ST Engineering, and SR Technics compete in engine MRO; HEICO is the key benchmark in high-margin component repair.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong and broad-based.** By segment, Engine Services grew +15.3% and CRS +19.6%; by end market, commercial aerospace +17.6%, business aviation +12.1%, military +9.4%. Q3 2025 accelerated to +20.4% and Q4 2025 grew ~13.5%, with Q4 EPS of $0.24 versus $0.12 a year prior.

**The headline earnings jump is a capital-structure event.** Interest expense fell from $282.5M to $174.2M following the IPO deleveraging and refinancing, and one-time IPO/debt-extinguishment charges were absent. The operating read is more modest: operating income +36.7% to $551M and adjusted EBITDA +17% to $808M, but adjusted EBITDA margin was flat at 13.3% because LEAP and CFM56 new programs are still coming down the learning curve, carrying near-term cost drag ahead of future volume.

**Cash flow is real but light.** Operating cash flow was $316.7M and free cash flow $234.3M, an FCF margin of ~3.9% suppressed by ~$90M of growth-platform capex and a $162.5M working-capital use (contract assets and inventory build to service ramping volumes). Working-capital intensity is a structural feature of scaling MRO throughput and should be monitored, but it is an investment-for-growth signature rather than a distress signal.

**The balance sheet is improving from a leveraged base.** Total debt is $2,247M (a single term loan maturing October 2031 at a ~6.8% weighted-average variable rate) against $290M cash. D/E is 1.46, current ratio 2.2, with $735.9M of revolver availability. The Altman Z-Score of 2.47 sits in the grey zone, typical for a de-levering, capital-intensive industrial. Deeply negative retained earnings (−$1.29B) are a scar from the prior LBO capital structure, not current operating losses. Each 1 percentage point rate move is roughly $30M of annual interest, a live consideration in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Earnings surprise track record is a caution flag.** Over the last four quarters the company has missed three times (Q3 2025 −5.1%, Q4 2025 −4.0%, Q1 2026 −15.5%) and beaten once (Q2 2025 +6.6%). The magnitude of the most recent miss, combined with the ICFR material weaknesses, argues for discounting the precision of near-term consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is favorable and multi-sourced. The engine-MRO aftermarket benefits from an aging installed base kept flying by new-aircraft supply constraints, a wave of 2010s-delivered engines entering peak maintenance windows, elevated defense spending, and a growing business-jet fleet. These are visible across the aftermarket complex, giving the top-line trajectory higher durability than a company-specific narrative would carry.

Within that, the sharper edge is mix. CRS grows ~20% per year at ~2x the segment margin of Engine Services. Every point of CRS mix shift lifts consolidated margin, and the segment posted a record 30.7% margin in Q3 2025. The LEAP authorization is strategically central: it positions StandardAero to capture the successor aftermarket to the CFM56 base it already dominates as those engines age into their maintenance cycle.

The near-term estimate picture is where the growth thesis loses momentum. Consensus current-year EPS is $1.18, revised down 2.1% over 90 days; next-year is $1.48, essentially flat (+0.4% over 90 days on the next-quarter line; current-year revised −2.1%, next-year −0.4%). Current-quarter estimates are down 2.6% over 90 days. This is not the +30% estimate-revision velocity that would flag a genuine inflection; it is a solid grower whose consensus is drifting modestly lower as the LEAP ramp drag and recent misses reset expectations. The revenue growth is real (mid-teens), but it is not accelerating from a depressed base, so the growth score reflects durable expansion rather than an inflection signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.70, SARO trades at ~24x the $1.18 current-year consensus and ~19.4x the $1.48 next-year estimate. The trailing 32x reflects reported earnings that are understated by the capital-structure transition, so forward multiples are the relevant lens.

Peer comparison is genuinely difficult here because the aerospace and defense set is heterogeneous: KTOS at 342x, HXL at 65x, DRS at 46x, HII at 20x, AVAV loss-making, with a peer-median P/E of 64.7. Against that dispersion, SARO's ~24x forward looks reasonable-to-cheap on a headline basis. But the honest read is that the peer median is distorted by a few high-multiple names, and the cleanest comparable, HEICO in high-margin component repair, is a widely recognized aftermarket compounder trading at a premium justified by superior consolidated margins that StandardAero does not yet match at the group level. StandardAero's ~13% consolidated EBITDA margin is well below best-in-class aftermarket peers, so a discount to the aftermarket compounders is warranted until CRS mix lifts the blended margin.

Analyst targets span $30.00 low to $41.50 high, mean $35.46, median $35.00, meaningful upside on paper. On an inflection stock with rising estimates I would treat these as floors, but here estimates are flat-to-falling, so the "targets are floors" logic does not apply; consensus targets and consensus estimates are drifting in opposite directions, which typically resolves toward the estimate trend. The forward multiple is defensible for a mid-teens grower with a widening high-margin segment, but it is not cheap enough to offer asymmetric upside given the controls and governance overhangs. A compressed or "cheap-looking" multiple is not itself a buy signal, and this multiple is full rather than cheap, so the valuation is fairly priced with limited margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

StandardAero's position is improving on the vectors that matter. It is the largest independent multi-platform MRO provider, and its independence lets it service many platforms across many customers, unlike captive OEM shops. The LEAP-1A/1B authorization extends its franchise into the next-generation narrowbody aftermarket, a structural share-vector as the CFM56 base ages out and LEAP volumes scale. CRS is gaining share in the highest-margin niche of the value chain, and ~80% long-term-agreement revenue provides visibility and switching costs.

The central risk to competitive position is the OEM triple-dependency. OEMs dominate aftermarket share on the newest platforms early in their lifecycle and hold the levers to in-source work, revise work-scope and pricing, or revoke authorizations. This caps the moat's absolute width even as the trajectory improves. The military segment is also now subject to CMMC cybersecurity certification requirements effective November 2025 that could gate contract eligibility. On balance the trajectory is positive, share-gaining in CRS and LEAP-positioned in engine services, which supports an above-average competitive score, tempered by the structural OEM leverage over the business.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weaknesses in ICFR (the single most important diligence item).** As of December 31, 2025, management disclosed three material weaknesses: an inadequate control environment/insufficient accounting personnel, deficient period-end reporting and review of complex estimates, and weak IT general controls (change management, user access, computer operations). Remediation is underway but incomplete. This is especially acute because revenue relies on judgmental margin-by-engine-platform inputs. Reported precision should be discounted until an unqualified ICFR opinion is achieved, and the Q1 2026 EPS miss sits uncomfortably against this backdrop.

**Private-equity supply overhang.** Carlyle's stake fell from 45.6% to ~31.4% and GIC's from 10.3% to ~7.1% through three secondaries in twelve months (March 2025 at $28, May 2025 at $28, January 2026 at $31). Carlyle retains the right to designate a board majority until it owns <25%, alongside a classified board, anti-takeover provisions, and a corporate-opportunity waiver favoring the sponsor. Continued sell-downs are a structural overhang, though each round has cleared at a rising price ($24 → $31), signaling healthy demand absorption.

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** The deleveraging trajectory is favorable but the balance sheet remains leveraged at 2.9x, with the variable-rate term loan carrying ~$30M of annual interest sensitivity per point.

**Other risks:** OEM triple-dependency (existential lever over work-scope and authorizations); LEAP/CFM56 learning-curve execution and supply-chain delays; tariff uncertainty (February 2026 SCOTUS invalidated IEEPA tariffs, but alternatives remain possible); underfunded UK pension; and a Section 163(j) disallowed-interest carryforward ($786M / $185.6M tax-effected) with a $120.2M valuation allowance and potential Section 382 limitation risk on ownership changes.

An unresolved thread: an 8-K (Item 2.01) dated November 10, 2025 indicates a completed transaction late in 2025, yet the FY2025 10-K MD&A states there were no acquisitions during the year, suggesting a very-late-close or immaterial deal. This should be confirmed before relying on any acquisition claim.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 55.5% versus 35.0% one-year historical, an elevated 20.5-point spread and the 87th percentile within the current chain, with term structure in backwardation (55.5% near-term vs 47.5% long-term). This indicates the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with the leadership transition, the ICFR overhang, and the next earnings print. The put/call skew is call-heavy (−6.5%) and the near-term implied move is ±13.4%, so positioning is not one-sidedly bearish despite the rich vol. IV rose from 42.8% to 55.5% over the past month against a +4.1% price move, reinforcing that the market expects a catalyst-driven swing. For a prospective buyer, the rich near-term IV argues against chasing and favors waiting for a post-event volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38-42).** The aftermarket tailwind is durable and multi-sourced (aging installed base, shop-visit backlog, defense spending, business-jet growth). CRS continues compounding ~20% at ~30% margins, lifting the blended margin toward the high-single-digit-percent EBITDA expansion the current multiple already partly anticipates. LEAP volumes scale past the learning curve, converting today's cost drag into tomorrow's high-visibility, long-term-agreement revenue. Deleveraging continues below 2.5x, interest expense keeps falling, and FCF conversion improves as growth capex normalizes. Management remediates the material weaknesses, removing the primary quality discount. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the analyst high of $41.50 as blended margins expand.

**Bear Case ($20-23).** The material weaknesses prove more than administrative: a restatement or negative surprise in the judgmental margin estimates undermines confidence in reported growth. LEAP ramp costs persist longer than guided, keeping consolidated margins flat and disappointing the margin-expansion narrative embedded in the multiple. Carlyle accelerates its sell-down into softer demand, and the ~24x forward multiple compresses toward the mid-teens as consensus estimates continue to drift lower. Recent misses continue. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low of $23.83 and below on a de-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month case rests on durable mid-teens revenue growth, CRS mix-driven margin expansion, and continued deleveraging (genuine catalysts), but the entry multiple is full, near-term estimates are drifting lower, and the ICFR and PE-overhang risks are unresolved. This is worth holding for the secular MRO compounding, not chasing at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Remediation of the three ICFR material weaknesses confirmed with an unqualified opinion, removing the quality discount.
- Consensus EPS estimate-revision velocity turns positive (current-year and current-quarter both revised up) alongside a return to earnings beats.
- A pullback toward the mid-$24s (near the 52-week low), where forward P/E compresses below ~20x and the margin of safety re-opens.
- CRS mix shift demonstrably lifting consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin above ~14% for two consecutive quarters.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any restatement, ICFR opinion adverse to management's remediation timeline, or a material adverse revision to the "estimated margin by engine platform" methodology.
- Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin declining rather than expanding, indicating LEAP ramp costs are structural rather than transitional.
- An accelerated Carlyle sell-down clearing at a declining price (breaking the $24 → $31 rising-clearing-price pattern).
- Net debt/EBITDA rising rather than continuing to decline.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the associated ICFR remediation update; the October 1, 2026 CEO transition to Paul McElhinney is a natural governance checkpoint.

**Base case reference:** ~22x the $1.48 next-year EPS ≈ $32-33, roughly 12-15% above the current $28.70 over 12 months, sitting between the bear ($20-23) and bull ($38-42) cases. Upside to base ~12-15%, to bull ~32-46%; downside to bear ~20-30%.

## Conclusion

StandardAero is a legitimate beneficiary of a real, broad-based engine-aftermarket upcycle, with a defensible independent moat, a genuinely valuable high-margin CRS growth engine, and a rapidly de-levering balance sheet. The score trajectory (5.7 → 6.5 → 6.0) reflects improving management alignment and deleveraging, and the current data confirms the operating improvement while flagging that the near-term estimate momentum has stalled and the ICFR issues remain open. The price decline from the January high to the high-$20s has not been accompanied by fundamental deterioration in the top line or backlog, but it has been accompanied by flat-to-falling estimates and three of four earnings misses, so this is not a case where the market is simply wrong about an intact franchise.

The combination of a full ~24x forward multiple, unremediated material weaknesses in a company that relies on judgmental margin estimates, a persistent Carlyle supply overhang, and rich near-term implied volatility pricing imminent stress keeps the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. Own it for the secular compounding and CRS mix shift; wait for either remediation and an estimate-revision turn or a pullback into the mid-$20s before upgrading. **HOLD.**
