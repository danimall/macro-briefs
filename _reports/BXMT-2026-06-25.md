**Research Date: June 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Blackstone Mortgage Trust is a commercial real estate finance REIT that originates and holds floating-rate senior mortgages, externally managed by a Blackstone subsidiary. The investment debate is unusually two-sided and resolves around a single question: whether commercial real estate credit losses, concentrated in legacy office, have peaked or have further to run. The stock at $17.47 trades at roughly 0.84x book value of $20.75 and under 9x next-year consensus EPS of $1.96. That valuation prices in continued NAV erosion and a possible second dividend cut. The genuine ambiguity is whether the discount is a mispricing or a fair reflection of credit that hasn't yet stabilized.

The bear evidence is real: four consecutive earnings misses, a Q1 2026 distributable EPS of $0.21 against a $0.47 dividend, realized CECL charge-offs draining roughly $0.28/quarter, current-year estimates cut 14% over 90 days, and a fresh $343M Blackstone-related Chicago office loan default in June 2026. The dividend is covered only on a pre-realized-loss basis ($0.49 vs. $0.47), making the ~10.4% yield a "show-me" yield rather than a safe one. No insider has bought open-market stock below book.

The countervailing evidence is what shifts this from an AVOID to a HOLD. The decline reflects valuation compression on credit problems that are already disclosed, not a fresh negative surprise; the Chicago default is one known data point in a managed run-off. The stock now sits at a hard book-value floor: every covering analyst's target is above spot, with the lowest at $19.50 (+12%) and the consensus mean at $20.94 (+20%), against a 7-buy / 2-hold / 0-sell consensus. The dividend was reaffirmed at $0.47 for Q2 2026, financings have been termed out, and next-year EPS estimates fell only 3.5% (vs. 14% for the current year), meaning the recovery year is holding. With downside structurally capped by the discount to book and no dated forward negative catalyst, the defensible posture is to wait for distributable-earnings coverage to confirm in either direction.

## Company Overview

BXMT runs a spread-lending model: it borrows through repo facilities, securitizations, and term loans, lends at higher floating rates against institutional-quality commercial real estate across North America, Europe, and Australia, and distributes the net interest margin. The loan book is predominantly floating-rate senior mortgages, which benefited as policy rates rose but now face the other side of that trade: high rates pressure borrower debt-service coverage and property values.

The defining structural feature is external management by BXMT Advisors L.L.C., a Blackstone subsidiary. Blackstone (~$1.3T AUM) provides privileged deal flow, sponsor relationships, and CRE market intelligence, a genuine origination advantage. It also embeds a conflict: management fees accrue to Blackstone regardless of shareholder outcomes, and BXMT sometimes lends against assets where Blackstone is the equity sponsor. The June 2026 Chicago office default involved a Blackstone-related loan, illustrating the entanglement.

Management is rotating out of office exposure and into industrial, multifamily, and diversified credit strategies. The bull case for a 2026 rebound rests on this pivot succeeding: redeploying a shrinking book into new loans at wide enough spreads to rebuild earnings power before the dividend coverage gap forces a cut.

## Financial Analysis

**The balance sheet is in managed run-off.** Total assets fell from ~$25.4B (2022) to ~$20.0B (2025) as repayments outpaced new originations during the credit cleanup. Shareholders' equity eroded from ~$4.5B to ~$3.5B, with negative retained earnings of ~$(1.9B) reflecting cumulative CECL charge-offs. Book value per share declined from ~$21.87 to $20.75 over the year.

**The GAAP-vs-cash earnings disconnect is the central analytical issue.** FY2025 GAAP net income swung to +$110M from a $(204)M loss in 2024, but this overstates stability. Distributable Earnings were a loss of ~$(245)M, or ~$(1.43)/share, driven by ~$563M of realized CECL charge-offs. The GAAP "profit" largely reflects CECL reserve mechanics, not cash earnings power. BXMT paid $1.88/share in dividends in 2025 against negative distributable earnings, meaning the dividend was funded partly from capital.

**Q1 2026 is the clearest single read on dividend sustainability:**
- GAAP EPS: $(0.04) (net loss of $6.3M)
- Distributable EPS: $0.21
- Distributable EPS prior to realized losses: $0.49
- Dividend: $0.47/share

The dividend is covered before realized losses ($0.49 vs. $0.47) but not after ($0.21). Realized credit losses are draining roughly $0.28/quarter. The dividend was already cut once (from ~$2.18 to $1.88/yr); a second cut is a live risk if charge-offs persist, though management reaffirmed $0.47 for Q2 2026.

**Financing.** As a mortgage REIT, BXMT runs high balance-sheet leverage by design; the relevant question is the quality and term of its repo/financing lines, not absolute debt levels. Management completed $2B+ of corporate and securitized debt financings in Q1 2026, extending maturities and reducing mark-to-market repo risk. This materially de-risks the near-term liability side and is a constructive data point the bear narrative tends to omit.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story here in the near term; the book is shrinking. The "growth" thesis is really an earnings-power *recovery* thesis: can management redeploy capital into industrial/multifamily and diversified credit fast enough and at wide enough spreads to rebuild distributable earnings?

The estimate revision pattern is informative and split. Current-year EPS estimates fell 14% over 90 days to $1.47, and current-quarter estimates fell 12.9%, clearly negative near-term momentum, consistent with four straight misses. But next-year EPS estimates of $1.96 fell only 3.5% over the same window. The Street is marking down the trough but largely holding the recovery year. That divergence suggests analysts view the current weakness as cyclical credit drag rather than permanent earnings impairment. The recovery is unconfirmed, since distributable EPS has to climb back above the dividend before the thesis is validated, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

The valuation creates a genuine floor that I underweighted in prior reports. Three points support it: (1) the 0.84x book discount already prices meaningful further NAV erosion; (2) under 9x normalized earnings on the Street's recovery year, with that estimate down only 3.5% over 90 days, is not a multiple that demands continued deterioration; (3) every covering analyst target sits above spot — low $19.50 (+12%), mean $20.94 (+20%), median $20.75 — with zero sells. An AVOID against a zero-sell Street whose lowest target is 12% above the current price requires the bear case to contain a fresh, dated negative catalyst that the market hasn't seen, and it doesn't.

The discount to book is simultaneously the bull and bear case. If office defaults continue, book value keeps falling and 0.84x will prove fair, not cheap. The valuation floor is real but not a catalyst; it caps downside without forcing a re-rating. That balance, with no clean directional edge, is what a 5.5 valuation score reflects. I do not treat the cheapness as a standalone buy signal; the market's discount on financials with credit problems has historically sorted the wrong way without an independent recovery catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

BXMT competes with Starwood Property Trust (STWD), Arbor Realty (ABR), Ladder Capital (LADR), and Apollo Commercial (ARI). The entire commercial mortgage REIT sector has been pressured by the post-2022 CRE downturn, particularly office, where valuations remain 30-50% below pre-pandemic peaks in many markets.

BXMT's competitive advantage is the Blackstone affiliation — deal sourcing, sponsor relationships, and market data — a durable origination moat that does not disappear in a downturn. Its disadvantage versus more diversified peers like STWD has been higher office concentration, which is exactly what the pivot is unwinding. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving: the origination platform is intact and the office rotation, if executed, narrows the structural gap to peers. The trajectory depends on redeployment execution, which is unproven, so I score this at the lower end of "stable" rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Office credit deterioration** — the #1 risk. Legacy office drives the bulk of CECL reserves and charge-offs; the Chicago default confirms the cycle isn't fully resolved.
2. **Dividend sustainability** — covered only pre-realized-loss. Continued charge-offs could force a second cut, which would pressure the stock despite the book-value floor.
3. **Book value erosion** — negative retained earnings and ongoing reserve build mean further NAV declines are possible.
4. **Interest-rate two-sidedness** — high rates support income but pressure borrower coverage and property values; rate cuts would compress net interest income.
5. **External management conflict** — fees to Blackstone regardless of performance; lender/sponsor entanglement.
6. **Redeployment execution** — rebuilding earnings power on a shrinking book is unproven.

Much of this is already in the price at 0.84x book. The risk that is *not* priced is a second dividend cut, which would be a sentiment shock even if fundamentally telegraphed. I score risk at 4.5: the magnitude of idiosyncratic credit risk is meaningful but substantially reflected in the discount, with asymmetric recovery upside if charge-offs peak.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is balanced-to-cautious, not bearish. Near-term ATM IV of 28.9% is rich versus 22.5% one-year HV (6.4pp spread), and IV rose 6.9pp over the past month alongside a 3.9% price decline; the market is pricing elevated uncertainty into the next earnings cycle, consistent with the coverage-confirmation question. The put/call OI ratio of 2.17 (up from 1.99) leans defensive, but the put/call skew is essentially flat at 0.8%, indicating no aggressive demand for downside protection. The term structure is flat (28.9% near ≈ 27.6% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific near-term event. This is consistent with a wait-and-see stance rather than a directional collapse thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($20.94–23.00):** Charge-offs peak in 2026, the office pivot redeploys capital into industrial/multifamily at attractive spreads, distributable EPS recovers toward the next-year consensus, the dividend holds, and the discount to book narrows toward parity. Re-rating from 0.84x toward 1.0x book plus the yield delivers a 20%+ total return. The Blackstone origination moat and termed-out financing support the recovery path.

**Bear Case (~$15.00):** Office defaults continue beyond Chicago, charge-offs persist above $0.28/quarter, book value erodes toward ~$18, the dividend is cut a second time, and the stock de-rates further on the cut even as it stays near 0.84x of a lower book. Roughly 14% downside from spot.

**Base Case (~$18.50):** Charge-offs grind down but distributable coverage remains marginal through 2026; the dividend holds at $0.47; the stock oscillates between 0.85x and 0.95x book as the market waits for confirmation. Modest upside plus the yield.

The evidence is genuinely balanced. The coverage gap and credit overhang are real; the book-value floor and held dividend are equally real. Neither side carries a dated forward catalyst that resolves the question in the next quarter.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) is the key data point — specifically the distributable EPS print and the charge-off run-rate. The Q3 print after that confirms or breaks the trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Distributable EPS (after realized losses) recovers to cover the $0.47 dividend for two consecutive quarters
- Realized CECL charge-offs decline materially below the ~$0.28/quarter run-rate, signaling credit has peaked
- Open-market insider buying emerges below book value, signaling management conviction

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second dividend cut, or distributable EPS before realized losses falling below the dividend
- Book value erosion accelerating (new large office defaults beyond Chicago)
- Next-year EPS estimates beginning to fall in line with the current-year cuts (the recovery-year assumption breaking)

## Conclusion

I am moving BXMT from AVOID to HOLD (uncertain rationale). The prior negative rating rested on a sub-coverage dividend and ongoing charge-offs, but those facts are disclosed and the stock has compressed to a hard valuation floor: 0.84x book, under 9x normalized earnings, every analyst target above spot, a reaffirmed dividend, and termed-out financing. There is no dated forward catalyst to drive the stock materially lower from here, and the discount to book caps downside — conditions under which a conviction-negative rating has historically been the wrong call on depressed names that subsequently rally.

This is not a buy. The dividend is uncovered on actual distributable earnings, four straight misses and falling near-term estimates leave the recovery unconfirmed, and the cheapness on financials with unresolved credit problems is not a standalone signal. It is a balanced "wait for coverage to confirm" situation. The next two earnings prints — distributable coverage and the charge-off trajectory — will resolve the call in one direction or the other. Until then, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric and the appropriate posture is to hold and monitor.
