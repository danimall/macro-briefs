**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Archrock is the largest U.S. provider of outsourced natural gas compression services by total fleet horsepower. Its business model produces unusually stable cash flow for an energy-services name: fee-based contracts where the customer supplies the fuel gas, ~95% fleet utilization, packages that stay on-site roughly six years, and a diversified base of ~300 customers (top five = 35% of revenue, none above 10%). The secular demand backdrop is real and broad: record U.S. gas production, LNG export growth (EIA forecasting +9–10%/yr through 2027), pipeline exports to Mexico, rising gas-to-oil ratios in aging fields, and incremental power demand from data centers. The Contract Operations segment (85% of revenue) carried a 73% adjusted gross margin in 2025.

The investment question is whether the current price compensates for what is now a maturing growth story. Revenue grew 28.6% in 2025, but that was M&A-driven (TOPS in August 2024, NGCS in May 2025), and management has guided 2026 growth capex down (to $250–275M from $348M), signaling a pivot from build-out toward free-cash-flow harvest and capital return. The 2025 record net income of $322M was flattered by ~$47M of asset-sale gains and a ~$35M one-time sales/use-tax audit benefit; run-rate earnings power is meaningfully lower. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised down to $2.00 from $2.07 ninety days ago, and the stock fell ~12.5% after Q1 despite a YoY revenue beat.

The stock has run 60% off its 52-week low to ~$39, now trading at ~21x trailing and ~19.5x forward earnings. That re-rating reflects genuine quality recognition, but it leaves the stock within $1 of the lowest published analyst target ($40) and removes the asymmetry that supported a BUY earlier this year. We rate AROC a HOLD on a growth basis: the multi-year volume thesis remains intact, but a CFO departure paired with heavy insider selling, decelerating growth, and falling near-term estimates argue for waiting rather than adding at the high end of the range.

## Company Overview

Archrock operates two segments. **Contract Operations** (~85% of revenue) owns a fleet of approximately 5,319 compressor units (~4.79 million horsepower) and provides turnkey compression under fixed monthly fees. As of year-end 2025, ~74% of operating horsepower is large units (>1,000 HP), average fleet age is 10 years, and spot utilization is ~95%. Roughly 60% of the fleet serves midstream gathering and processing; ~40% serves gas-lift oil production enhancement. **Aftermarket Services** (~15%) supplies parts and maintenance for customer-owned compression.

The structural appeal is that compression is a must-run service essential to gas production and transport. Contracts run 12–60 month initial terms then convert to month-to-month with 30-day cancellation. Because customers supply the fuel gas, Archrock has minimal direct commodity-price exposure, and its cash flow profile resembles infrastructure more than an oilfield services beta play. The company has been deliberately concentrating its fleet toward large horsepower, selling ~325,000 HP in 2025 (~85% small-HP units, including the August 2025 Flowco disposition) while acquiring Permian-focused large-HP capacity through TOPS and NGCS.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings trajectory.** Revenue grew from $990M (2023) to $1,158M (2024) to $1,490M (2025), driven by the TOPS (~$869M cash) and NGCS ($349.4M) acquisitions. Net income rose from $105M to $172M to $322M. The headline 2025 net margin of ~21.6% is real but should be normalized for the asset-sale gains and tax-audit benefit. Underlying run-rate earnings are closer to the ~$2.00 current-year consensus EPS than the $1.83 trailing print suggests after adjustment.

**Margins.** Contract Operations adjusted gross margin reached 73% in 2025 (up from 67%), aided by scale, higher rates, and the tax benefit. Q1 2026 contract operations margin was 72%, broadly stable. The soft spot was Aftermarket Services, down 8% YoY on reduced major-maintenance demand, a potential early signal of customer maintenance-budget tightening.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $622M in 2025; capex of $502M (growth $348M + maintenance $111M) left ~$120M of FCF. With 2026 capex guided to $400–445M, FCF should improve materially this year as the growth phase moderates. The quarterly dividend was raised 16% to $0.22 (current yield ~2.4%), and ~$117M of buyback authorization remains (prior repurchases averaged $20.91/share, well below today's price).

**Balance sheet.** This is the most misread part of the story. The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 flags "distress," but that is an artifact of an asset-heavy, levered infrastructure model with a large accumulated deficit (–$2.26B, a legacy of the 2015 Exterran spin and historical impairments) that depresses book equity. Debt/equity is ~1.9x and long-term debt is $2.4B at 6.0–6.6% coupons, appropriate for utility-like cash flows at 95% utilization. The Piotroski F-score (5/5 on the tested components) confirms genuine operating strength: positive net income, ROA, OCF, OCF>NI, and a 1.54 current ratio. Management has actively termed out maturities: $800M 6.0% notes due 2034 issued in January 2026 (repaying the revolver), $800M 6.25% 2028 notes redeemed in April 2026, $300M 2027 notes redeemed in November 2025, and the revolver upsized to $1.5B (only ~$97M drawn at 3/31/26) with a December 2025 amendment cutting borrowing margins 0.25%. There is no near-term maturity wall, and lender terms are improving, a clear differentiator from the broader credit-stress backdrop affecting more fragile energy issuers.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue $373.8M (+7.7% YoY); net income $73.8M (+4.2%); EPS $0.41, which missed the $0.47 estimate by 13.4%. The miss followed a strong Q4 2025 ($0.67 actual vs. $0.42 estimate, +61.3%), and the four-quarter record is 2 beats / 2 misses, a mixed visibility pattern. Remaining contract performance obligations (backlog) stand at $864.6M through 2032.

## Growth Analysis

The secular demand case is the strongest part of the thesis and is corroborated by multiple independent signals: EIA forecasts of record U.S. gas production into 2026–2027, LNG export capacity growth, Mexico pipeline exports, rising gas-to-oil ratios requiring more compression in maturing fields, and incremental power demand tied to data-center buildout. These are durable, multi-year volume drivers, not a single-customer story.

The near-term growth picture is more measured. The 2025 revenue growth was acquisition-fueled and will not repeat organically; the NGCS deal laps in 2026. Management's cut to 2026 growth capex is a deliberate signal that the build-out phase is moderating in favor of FCF and capital returns. Estimate revisions reflect this: current-year EPS has fallen 3.3% over 90 days, and the current quarter was trimmed 1.4% over 30 days. The one bright spot is next-year EPS, revised up 2.5% to $2.40 over 30 days, implying ~20% earnings growth in 2027 if delivered, partly the mechanical effect of falling growth capex flowing into FCF and partly continued rate and utilization strength.

The strong estimate-revision BUY signal (>30% over 90 days alongside >30% revenue growth) is not present here. Revisions are mixed-to-falling on the near term and only modestly positive on the out-year. This is a steady infrastructure compounder entering a more normal growth cadence, not a stock where consensus is systematically too low. Growth Prospects score 5.8 — solid secular demand, but decelerating momentum and falling current-year estimates cap the dimension that historically discriminates winners best.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$39, AROC trades at ~21x trailing EPS ($1.83) and ~19.5x current-year consensus, declining to ~16x next-year. The dividend yields ~2.4%.

Against peers in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services, the screen shows a peer median P/E of 55.0, which makes AROC look cheap on the surface. That comparison is misleading: the peer set (KGS at 88.7x, NOV at 85.3x, WHD at 55.0x) is distorted by trough or transitional earnings at several names. The most relevant direct comparable is Kodiak Gas Services (KGS), the other large-HP compression pure-play, whose elevated P/E reflects its own M&A accounting rather than a sustainable run-rate. AROC's yield is well above the peer median 1.3%, providing genuine absolute support.

The harder question is whether the multiple is justified on fundamentals today. After normalizing 2025 for the one-time items, AROC is paying ~20x for a business now growing earnings in the high single to low double digits with falling current-year estimates. That is a fair price for a quality infrastructure compounder, but it is not asymmetric upside. The history of this name's own scoring reflects this: valuation has declined from 6.0 to 5.5 across recent reports as the price re-rated from the low-$30s. Prior price appreciation is not itself a reason for a low score; the issue is current price versus current/forward fundamentals, not where the stock was. At ~19.5x forward against a decelerating growth profile, with the stock $1 above the lowest analyst target and below the mean ($42.33) and median ($43), the upside to consensus is ~9% to the median and ~17% to the high target ($46), with a thin cushion below. We score valuation 5.0: not a value trap, but no longer offering the mispricing that warranted a stronger call, and our calibration treats apparent cheapness on a distorted peer screen with appropriate skepticism.

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock is the scale leader in U.S. large-horsepower compression. The market is described in filings as "highly competitive with low barriers to entry," which tempers the moat narrative, but Archrock's advantages are durable in practice: the largest large-HP fleet, geographic density (Permian + Eagle Ford = ~75% of HP), a standardized fleet that lowers operating and maintenance capex per unit, and a long operating history. The shift toward large horsepower and away from small-HP units improves fleet economics and concentrates the business where the secular demand is strongest.

Industry consolidation is the operative theme: Archrock's own TOPS/NGCS deals and the Kodiak/CSI combination point to a market reducing fragmentation, which over time should support pricing discipline. Main publicly traded competitors are USA Compression Partners (USAC) and Kodiak Gas Services (KGS). Archrock's competitive trajectory is improving on fleet quality and scale, which is why we score competitive position 6.8, the highest of the six dimensions. The strategic minority investments (ECOTEC, Ionada, FGC Holdco/ColdStream, Shoreline AI) position the company for the emissions/electrification transition without large balance-sheet commitments.

## Risk Assessment

**Leadership transition (live risk).** The Q1 10-Q exhibit list contains a Transition and Separation Agreement dated March 25, 2026 for CFO Douglas Aron (8-K, Item 5.02). This coincides with heavy insider selling: Aron sold ~295,000 shares (~$10.6M) across March 27–May 18 at $34.76–$38.30, and an SVP (Ingersoll) sold 33,000 shares in May. Q1 SG&A also included a $3.7M acceleration of LTI expense tied to an executive retention agreement, and a second Item 5.02 8-K (February 24, 2026) signals an additional officer/director change. The clustering of CFO departure with sizable sales is a governance item worth monitoring for continuity of the capital-allocation and refinancing playbook.

**Acquisition integration.** Two large deals in under 18 months. The 10-Q flags risk of failing to achieve NGCS benefits and integration difficulty, and NGCS purchase accounting ($73.6M goodwill, deferred-tax items) remains preliminary and subject to adjustment.

**Long-term demand transition.** Electrification of compression and decarbonization could erode demand for gas-powered units over time; customers increasingly want electric drives. The current regulatory backdrop is a near-term tailwind (methane-rule rollbacks reduce compliance cost) but raises longer-term ambiguity.

**Concentration and contract structure.** Two customers account for ~30% of trade receivables. After short initial terms, contracts go month-to-month with 30-day cancellation, a renewal/churn risk in a downturn. A board member is affiliated with customer Hilcorp ($9.0M Q1 revenue), disclosed and modest.

**Interest-rate sensitivity.** The higher-for-longer rate path raises refinancing cost on a financing-heavy model, though no near-term maturities and improving lender terms mitigate this currently.

We score risk factors 5.5: the CFO transition is a specific, recently surfaced governance item not fully priced, and the aftermarket softness plus mixed earnings visibility are genuine near-term overhangs, balanced against a well-managed balance sheet and a demand backdrop with several confirming signals.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$46).** Secular gas-compression demand sustains high-single-digit organic volume growth; large-HP rate strength and ~95% utilization hold; 2026 growth-capex moderation drives a step-up in FCF that funds dividend growth and buybacks below intrinsic value; next-year EPS of $2.40 proves achievable and analysts revise targets toward $46. At ~19x on $2.40, that supports the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear case (~$30, ~-23%).** Aftermarket softness proves an early indicator of broader customer maintenance-budget tightening; an energy/macro downturn triggers month-to-month contract churn; NGCS integration disappoints or purchase accounting adjusts unfavorably; rate-driven refinancing pressure compresses the multiple on a levered model; the CFO transition disrupts capital-allocation discipline. A de-rating to ~14–15x normalized $2.00 EPS returns the stock toward the low $30s.

**Base case (~$43).** Organic growth in the high single digits, stable-to-improving margins, rising FCF, and continued capital return support the analyst median target. At ~18x on $2.40 next-year EPS, ~$43 is the most-likely 12-month outcome — roughly 10% upside, with the bear case ~23% below current.

The asymmetry that supported a BUY in the low-$30s earlier this year has largely been realized in the 60% run to ~$39. The thesis is intact; the entry is no longer cheap.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive on FCF inflection and secular demand, but the risk/reward at ~$39 is balanced, not asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-to-mid $30s (≥18x forward compression below $35), restoring the asymmetry that existed earlier in the year.
- A clean CFO succession with reaffirmed capital-allocation discipline plus a return to positive current-year estimate revisions (current-year EPS revised back above $2.05).
- Aftermarket Services revenue stabilizing or returning to growth, removing the customer-maintenance-tightening signal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Sustained aftermarket and/or contract-operations volume declines signaling customer budget cuts (two consecutive quarters of organic revenue contraction).
- A guidance cut to 2026 EBITDA or Cash Available for Dividend, or a dividend coverage shortfall.
- NGCS integration writedown or material unfavorable purchase-accounting adjustment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) for evidence on aftermarket trends, the new CFO transition, and FCF delivery against the lower-capex guide.

**Upside/downside from current (~$39):** ~+10% to base case ($43), ~+17% to bull case ($46), ~-23% to bear case ($30).

## Conclusion

Archrock is a high-quality, cash-generative infrastructure compounder with utility-like fee-based revenue, the leading large-HP compression fleet, an actively managed balance sheet with no near-term maturity wall, and a genuine multi-year demand backdrop. The "distress" Z-Score is a screening false alarm. None of that is in dispute.

The recommendation rests on price and momentum, not business quality. The stock has re-rated 60% off its low to ~21x trailing / ~19.5x forward against falling current-year estimates, a moderating growth phase, normalized 2025 earnings that were flattered by ~$82M of one-time items, and a CFO departure coinciding with insider selling. At ~$39, within $1 of the lowest analyst target and ~9% below the median, the asymmetry that justified a BUY in the low-$30s has been earned out. We rate AROC a HOLD with a growth bias, and would become buyers again on a pullback toward the mid-$30s or on confirmation that the FCF-harvest phase and leadership transition are proceeding cleanly. Within the broader energy posture of holding rather than adding, AROC's idiosyncratic compression demand and minimal direct commodity exposure make it one of the more durable holds in the group, but not a name to chase at the top of its range.
