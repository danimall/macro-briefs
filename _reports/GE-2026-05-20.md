## Executive Summary

GE Aerospace is the world's largest commercial aircraft engine manufacturer by installed base, operating a razor-and-blade model where each of its ~50,000 commercial engines generates 30+ years of high-margin aftermarket revenue. The company posted Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.86, beating consensus estimates by approximately 16%, with adjusted revenue of $11.614B, up 29% YoY. At approximately $306, the stock trades roughly 13% below analyst consensus targets and 12% below its February 2026 peak of $348.48, following a selloff triggered by management cutting its air travel growth forecast from mid-single-digits to flat-to-low single digits.

The specific mispricing thesis: the market is treating a temporary air travel growth deceleration as a structural headwind to a business where approximately 70% of revenue comes from services on a $211.3B backlog (as of March 2026). Shop visit demand is primarily driven by cumulative flight hours accumulated over years, though GE acknowledges that demand tends to follow commercial air travel and freight demand over time. The LEAP fleet is entering its first major maintenance cycle as early-delivery engines accumulate sufficient cycles for initial overhauls, providing a structural tailwind to services revenue. Meanwhile, defense grew 19% YoY in Q1 with a $5B USAF contract secured.

The bear case centers on tariff impacts on long-term service agreement margins (flagged in the 10-K, though Q1 2026 disclosures show a reversal of most prior tariff-related charges), supply chain constraints limiting the production ramp, and Boeing dependency. These are real but bounded risks on a business generating $10.2B in segment profit with 26.6% CES margins still expanding.

## Company Overview

GE Aerospace operates through two segments: Commercial Engines & Services (CES, 73% of revenue) and Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT, 23%). GE/CFM narrowbody engine programs, including CFM56 and LEAP, power approximately 75% of narrowbody takeoffs globally through CFM International (50/50 JV with Safran), serving the majority of Boeing 737 and Airbus A320 family aircraft. Key platforms include LEAP (current generation single-aisle), GEnx (widebody), GE9X (Boeing 777X), and the legacy CFM56 fleet generating shop visits.

The installed base model creates a self-reinforcing competitive dynamic: airlines standardize on engine types to reduce parts inventory and training costs, which locks in decades of service revenue. Total Remaining Performance Obligations stand at $211.3B as of March 2026 (up from $190.6B at December 2025), providing visibility into revenue extending beyond 2040.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $306, GE trades at approximately:
- 41x FY2026E EPS (~$7.46 consensus)
- 33x FY2027E EPS ($9.20)
- A premium to the ~30-32x peer median P/E (RTX at approximately 30x is the closest comp)

The premium reflects GE's superior growth profile (20%+ revenue growth vs. RTX's mid-single-digits, expanding margins vs. RTX's GTF remediation costs). However, 41x current-year earnings is not cheap in absolute terms. The stock requires continued double-digit earnings growth to sustain this multiple.

**Why the market may be overreacting (specific thesis):** The 12% decline from the February peak ($348.48) may overstate the impact of flat-to-low-single-digit air travel growth on a services-backlog business. Shop visits are primarily driven by cumulative flight hours already logged on the installed base; an engine that has accumulated sufficient cycles needs its overhaul regardless of whether near-term traffic grows 1% or 5%. However, GE explicitly acknowledges that demand for equipment and services tends to follow commercial air travel and freight demand, meaning prolonged traffic weakness could defer some maintenance spending. The stock may be pricing the headline "air travel growth cut" more severely than the underlying services demand mechanics warrant.

**Upside math:** Analyst consensus mean target of approximately $350–$353 implies roughly 15% upside from $306. At 35x FY2027E of $9.20 = $322 (5% upside to conservative target). At 38x FY2027E = $350 (14% upside).

**Valuation limitation:** The multiple has no margin of safety for execution disappointment. If LEAP ramp slows or margins compress, a 5-turn de-rating to 33x FY2026E = $246 (20% downside). This valuation tension is what distinguishes this from a high-conviction position.

## Competitive Landscape

GE Aerospace's competitive position is among the strongest in industrial markets:

1. **~75% of narrowbody takeoffs** powered by GE/CFM programs — the LEAP engine has demonstrated superior time-on-wing performance vs. Pratt & Whitney's GTF, which faces well-publicized powder metal inspection issues requiring fleet groundings. P&W's GTF reliability challenges may be shifting some airline preferences toward LEAP for new orders.

2. **Sole-source on key Boeing platforms** — GE9X is the exclusive engine for the Boeing 777X. On other platforms such as the 787, airlines may choose between GE (GEnx) and Rolls-Royce (Trent 1000), though GE holds a significant share.

3. **Switching costs are extreme.** Airlines cannot change engine types on existing aircraft. Parts, tooling, training, and MRO infrastructure are all engine-specific. Once an airline commits to CFM/GE, they typically remain for the life of the fleet (20-30 years).

4. **Scale advantages in services.** GE's 50,000 commercial engine installed base generates data, parts commonality, and MRO network density that smaller competitors cannot replicate.

5. **RISE program** positions GE for the 2030s replacement cycle with a 20%+ fuel efficiency improvement over LEAP — critical as airlines face carbon mandates.

Competitive dynamics appear favorable: P&W's GTF issues have created an opportunity for CFM/LEAP, and the RISE program has received positive early reception from airlines.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff impact on LTSA margins (MODERATE — evolving favorably)**
Management disclosed in the 2025 10-K that tariffs were reducing estimated profitability of long-term service agreements. However, Q1 2026 disclosures reveal that GE reversed a majority of the tariff-related charge recorded in Q1 2025, reflecting late-2025 aerospace tariff agreements with the EU, UK, Japan, and Korea that eliminated tariffs on aerospace equipment. GE also reports utilizing pricing and cost actions to mitigate remaining impacts. The residual risk exists for supply chains exposed to regions not covered by these agreements, but the worst-case tariff scenario appears to be diminishing.

**2. Boeing production dependency (MODERATE)**
Boeing's 737 MAX production rate directly determines LEAP-1B delivery volume. Boeing's ongoing production challenges (quality issues, regulatory scrutiny) constrain GE's near-term equipment revenue. Mitigation: Airbus A320neo uses LEAP-1A, providing offsetting volume; services revenue is independent of new production.

**3. Air travel demand uncertainty (MODERATE — may be partially over-discounted)**
Management's guidance cut from mid-single-digits to flat-to-low single-digit traffic growth reflects geopolitical tensions and oil price risks. GE explicitly states that demand for equipment and services tends to follow air travel and freight demand, so extended weakness could delay some shop visits and spare parts demand. Mitigation: the $211.3B RPO backlog and cumulative utilization on the existing fleet provide substantial insulation; deferred visits driven by accumulated cycles eventually materialize, though timing may shift.

**4. Legacy liabilities (LOW but persistent)**
Bank BPH (Poland): approximately $2.1B estimated exposure as of March 2026. Environmental remediation: $2.1B in reserves (~$250M annual spending). Insurance run-off: potential statutory capital contributions. These are known, reserved, and manageable relative to $10.2B annual segment profit, but they create a persistent drag.

## Conclusion

GE Aerospace at approximately $306 presents a BUY opportunity based on the thesis that air travel growth concerns are being partially overpriced against a services-backlog model where shop visit demand benefits from cumulative flight hours on a growing installed base. Strong Q1 2026 results (EPS beat of ~16%), 26.6% CES margins still expanding, $211.3B backlog, and dominant narrowbody positioning provide fundamental support. The roughly 13% discount to consensus analyst targets creates an entry point with modestly favorable risk/reward.

The primary constraint preventing higher conviction is the approximately 41x FY2026E multiple, which requires continued 15%+ EPS growth to sustain. While GE's Q1 2026 disclosures show a reversal of most prior tariff-related charges following aerospace trade agreements, residual tariff or trade policy risk remains. The combination of LEAP shop visit acceleration, defense budget tailwinds, and competitive advantages from P&W's GTF issues provides multiple growth vectors.

Position sizing should be 2-3% given the industrial sector and elevated multiple. The stock is suitable for investors with a 12-month horizon who believe aerospace aftermarket services are partially mispriced during periods of temporary traffic softness.
