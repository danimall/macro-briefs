**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Astrana Health is a physician-centric, risk-bearing value-based-care manager that coordinates total cost of care for roughly 1.6 million patients across a network of more than 20,000 contracted physicians, predominantly in California. The investment case is a turnaround-in-progress. FY2025 was a transformational-acquisition year: revenue grew 56% to $3.18B, driven mostly by the $675M Prospect acquisition that closed July 1, 2025, but GAAP net income to Astrana fell 48% to $22.5M as transaction costs, higher medical utilization, a $13.0M legal charge, and interest expense rising to $49.9M compressed profitability. The market punished the stock into the high-teens/low-$20s by February 2026. Q1 2026 then showed the thesis working: net income up 116% to $14.4M, adjusted EBITDA up 82% to $66.3M, operating cash flow up 309% to $68.1M, and net leverage tracking toward the ≤2x target ahead of plan. The stock has roughly doubled off its low to ~$45.

The central analytical question is whether Q1 2026's "well-controlled medical cost trend" is a durable operating improvement or a favorable-seasonality head-fake in a business where capitation risk is uncapped. Astrana bears up to 100% of cost overruns under global capitation and ACO REACH Global track, and FY25's margin compression demonstrated that this risk is live rather than theoretical. The full-risk transition, now ~80% of Care Partners capitation revenue and ~40% of membership, increases both revenue per member and the concentration of medical-cost-inflation downside onto Astrana's balance sheet.

We rate ASTH a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. After a ~82% run over twelve months and a doubling from the February trough, the stock trades at ~74x trailing GAAP EPS and ~15x the FY26 consensus of $2.92. Estimate revisions are mildly positive (+8.6% for the current year over 90 days) and cash-flow quality has clearly improved, which supports the turnaround narrative. But a newly disclosed material weakness in business-combination accounting at a serial acquirer, the Prospect seller's Chapter 11 filing six days after close with recourse waived, and the structural fragility of the VIE consolidation model create idiosyncratic downside that is not fully reflected at the current multiple. We would need two to three quarters confirming durable cost control and clean remediation before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Astrana (formerly Apollo Medical Holdings) is headquartered in Alhambra, California and operates a physician-centric, technology-enabled model built around assuming financial risk for total cost of care under capitation, then managing that care efficiently to capture the spread. Three reportable segments:

- **Care Partners (~95% of revenue, $3.02B FY25):** the core risk-bearing business, a network of IPAs, ACOs, and California Restricted Knox-Keene licensed health plans that take professional and, increasingly, institutional (full) risk across Medicare Advantage, Medicare FFS (ACO REACH/MSSP), Medicaid (Medi-Cal), Commercial, and Exchange lines.
- **Care Delivery ($251M):** ~60+ owned/affiliated clinics plus, new in 2025, an acute care hospital (Foothill Regional Medical Center, Tustin CA).
- **Care Enablement ($247M):** proprietary MSO/population-health technology platform sold internally and to third parties.

Because California and other states prohibit the corporate practice of medicine, Astrana does not own its physician groups. It consolidates them as Variable Interest Entities via long-term Management Services Agreements and nominee-shareholder arrangements (notably Allied Pacific of California). This structure underpins essentially all consolidated revenue and is a common but legally fragile arrangement in the sector. Competitors include Optum (UnitedHealth), Heritage Provider Network, Privia Health, and Aledade.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is explosive but acquisition-driven.** Revenue moved from $1.14B (2022) to $1.39B (2023) to $2.03B (2024) to $3.18B (2025, +56%). Q1 2026 revenue was $965M, up 56% YoY. Of the $1.15B FY25 revenue increase, roughly $616M came from the Prospect acquisition and ~$539M from prior Care Partners deals plus members transitioning to full risk. Organic growth exists, but the headline number is inseparable from M&A.

**GAAP profitability compressed sharply in FY25, the central tension in the story.** Operating income declined to $78.5M from $89.4M (–12%) despite the revenue increase. Net income to Astrana fell to $22.5M from $43.1M (–48%). The drivers: higher medical utilization, $25.9M in Prospect transaction/integration costs, a $13.0M legal loss contingency, $5M of expensed debt-issuance costs, and higher interest expense. Adjusted EBITDA rose 21% to $205.4M, but adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 6% from 8%; Astrana is adding scale at structurally lower incremental margins as it moves into full-risk and hospital operations.

**Q1 2026 marks a clear improvement.** Diluted EPS was $0.29 (+107%); adjusted EBITDA margin recovered to 7%; FCF rose 372% to $64.1M. Management attributes this to well-controlled medical cost trend and full-risk contracts tracking to underwriting. The improvement is real, but a single quarter in a business with seasonal utilization patterns and IBNR reserve judgment is insufficient to declare the trend durable.

**Balance sheet: leveraged but deleveraging.** Total debt was $1.05B at YE25 (vs. $428M YE24) after drawing $707.3M to fund Prospect. D/E is ~2.13; Altman Z is 2.46 (grey zone). Net leverage was ~2.3x pro forma TTM in Q1 2026, with management targeting ≤2x. Term loans and revolver carry ~5.72% rates, with a $200M swap fixed at 3.179%. A $777M bullet maturity comes due in 2030, distant enough not to be a near-term refinancing concern. Cash rose from $429M (YE25) to $478M (Q1 2026); the current ratio is ~1.4. Approximately $447M of cash sits above FDIC-insured limits, a disclosed risk. Goodwill and intangibles total $1.14B, ~51% of total assets, creating impairment exposure if integration underperforms.

**Cash-flow quality improved materially.** OCF was $114.6M FY25 (from $52.2M); FCF was $104.5M. One balance-sheet subtlety: medical liabilities jumped from $335.7M (YE25) to $439.3M (Q1 2026), and receivables rose sharply, reflecting the timing dynamics of IBNR reserves and capitation. IBNR estimation is a key judgment area, and reserve adequacy is a direct swing factor for reported margins. FY26 guidance is revenue $3.8–4.1B, adjusted EBITDA $250–280M, and FCF $105–132.5M.

The earnings surprise record is uneven: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters, including a –97.8% miss in Q3 2025 and a –48.7% miss in Q2 2025. Q1 2026 itself was a slight miss ($0.29 actual vs. $0.30 estimate). This track record argues for caution on forward-estimate confidence despite the improving cash-flow trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

The structural tailwind is genuine. The U.S. shift from fee-for-service to value-based care is a multi-year secular trend, with CMS projecting national health spending to reach $8.6T by 2033. Astrana's differentiation claim is offering clinical delivery, MSO administration, and proprietary technology together at scale, which few competitors integrate.

The forward growth thesis rests on the full-risk membership ramp, which increases revenue per member and captures more of the medical-cost spread. The Q1 2026 launch of a delegated full-risk model with a Texas payer covering more than 14,000 MA members is meaningful evidence of geographic diversification beyond the California base. Estimate revisions are supportive: current-year consensus rose +8.6% over 90 days to $2.92, and next-year rose +14.4% to $3.42, though both stalled over the trailing 30 days.

The growth quality debate is the same one that governs the whole thesis. Revenue growth of 56% with expanding full-risk mix meets the mechanical thresholds that normally favor a constructive growth score, but the full-risk transition concentrates uncapped downside onto the balance sheet rather than reducing risk. The revenue growth is not accompanied by margin expansion at the GAAP level: FY25 margins compressed, and the Q1 recovery is one quarter. We score growth prospects moderately positive (6.8) rather than high, reflecting durable secular demand and rising estimates offset by acquisition-dependence and margin uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$44.70, ASTH trades at ~74x trailing GAAP EPS of ~$0.46 and ~15.3x forward consensus. Peer medians in Health Care Facilities sit around 24.9x (SEM 15.5, OPCH 16.2, NHC 24.9, USPH 130.3, ACHC negative, a heterogeneous set). On forward GAAP earnings, Astrana is not cheap relative to slower-growth peers like SEM or OPCH, and the trailing multiple looks extreme because FY25 represents a trough burdened by one-time integration costs.

The cycle-adjusted argument favors using forward figures: FY25 GAAP EPS was depressed by transaction, legal, and financing charges that management characterizes as largely non-recurring, so trailing 74x overstates the multiple. On $2.92 FY26 and $3.42 FY27, the stock trades at ~15x and ~13x. That is not a demanding multiple for a company guiding to ~20% revenue growth and expanding adjusted EBITDA. The PEG on next-year growth is comfortably below 1.5.

The counterweight is the wide non-GAAP-versus-GAAP gap. Adjusted EPS of $2.20 (FY25) sits against GAAP diluted EPS of ~$0.46. The bull case rests almost entirely on adjusted EBITDA and FCF, and the durability of the add-backs is the crux: if integration and transaction costs keep recurring (plausible for a serial acquirer with an unremediated business-combination-accounting material weakness), the "clean" forward earnings will not materialize as consensus expects. We treat the apparent forward cheapness as conditional on a turnaround that is one quarter old rather than as an established mispricing.

We assign a valuation score of 4.5. The forward multiple is reasonable if the turnaround holds, but the earnings quality is unresolved, the misses in three of the last four quarters undercut forward-estimate confidence, and the stock has already re-rated substantially off its lows. This is a fairly valued turnaround where the cheapness is conditional on confirmation rather than further multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

Astrana's competitive position is improving on trajectory but faces well-capitalized incumbents. The integrated three-part model (delivery + MSO + technology) is a genuine differentiator relative to single-function competitors, and the full-risk transition plus Texas expansion indicate the company is gaining share and geographic breadth in value-based care. Care Enablement's third-party technology sales add a scalable, higher-margin adjacency.

The structural risk is that it competes against Optum/UnitedHealth, a far larger and better-capitalized player with its own physician networks, alongside Heritage, Privia, and Aledade. Healthcare consolidation could allow payer partners to bypass Astrana or squeeze management fees. Payer concentration remains high: four payers accounted for 59.8% of revenue (down from 66.2%, a modest improvement), and the business is still predominantly California-based and Medicaid/Medicare-dependent. On balance, the trajectory is positive through share gains, membership growth, and diversification, but the moat against larger scaled competitors is narrower than the integrated-model narrative suggests. We score competitive position 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

Astrana carries a concentration of idiosyncratic risks that are not fully priced, which drives our low risk-factors score of 4.0.

1. **Material weakness in internal controls (newly disclosed).** Astrana identified a material weakness related to accounting for business combinations, driven by rapid acquisition-fueled growth. For a serial acquirer, this is a direct threat to financial-statement reliability. Remediation is ongoing and explicitly unremediated.

2. **Prospect integration and seller bankruptcy.** The Prospect seller entities filed Chapter 11 on July 7, 2025, six days after close. A July 1 letter agreement eliminated the escrow and most recourse against the sellers, so Astrana may absorb costs of any seller breach or non-compliance (unpaid creditor claims, risk-based capital shortfalls) with limited recourse. Astrana also has no prior experience operating a hospital.

3. **Uncapped capitation/utilization risk.** Under global capitation and ACO REACH Global track, Astrana bears up to 100% of cost overruns. FY25 margin compression from higher utilization demonstrates the risk is live.

4. **VIE consolidation fragility.** All consolidated revenue depends on MSAs and nominee-shareholder arrangements surviving corporate-practice-of-medicine challenges and accounting-rule stability. An adverse determination would be existential to the reported financials.

5. **Regulatory/policy headwinds.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (7/4/2025) tightens Medicaid work requirements and provider taxes from 2026; the 2027 Medicare Advantage Advance Notice changes risk-adjustment; and a re-established HHS-DOJ False Claims Act Working Group targets Medicare Advantage. Management argues impact is manageable and smaller than for the broader MA market, but these are unquantified.

6. **Concentration and cash risk.** Four-payer concentration at 59.8%, geographic concentration in California, and ~$447M of cash above FDIC-insured limits.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 73.6%, roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 71.9% (a fair 1.6% spread), but the term structure is in backwardation (73.6% near-term vs. 63.4% at six months), which the market uses to price imminent stress rather than persistent elevated vol. The put/call skew of 7.8% signals elevated put demand, and IV has risen 16.4 points since early May even as the stock climbed 13.8%; the market is paying up for downside protection into the next earnings print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±17.1%, consistent with a binary reaction to whether Q1's cost control persists. Short interest at 10.3% of float (3.8 days to cover) reflects meaningful institutional skepticism without extreme squeeze risk. This positioning aligns with our two-sided fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The FY25 GAAP compression was a digesting-a-transformational-acquisition year, and Q1 2026 shows the underlying model working: margins recovering, FCF surging, deleveraging ahead of plan, and geographic diversification via Texas. On $2.92 FY26 and $3.42 FY27 consensus, the stock at ~15x forward is reasonable for ~20% revenue growth and expanding adjusted EBITDA. If cost control proves durable and the full-risk ramp captures the medical-cost spread as underwritten, forward earnings power supports a materially higher price. Bull case ~$60–65, in line with the high analyst target of $65, reflecting continued estimate revisions and full-risk margin capture.

### Bear Case

The Q1 improvement is favorable seasonality, not a durable operating change, and utilization normalizes back toward the FY25 pattern that halved earnings. The material weakness in business-combination accounting surfaces restatement or reserve-adequacy issues; integration costs keep recurring, invalidating the adjusted-EBITDA add-back thesis; and the Prospect seller bankruptcy generates absorbed liabilities with no recourse. Goodwill and intangibles at 51% of assets face impairment if integration underperforms. Bear case ~$28–32, roughly a 30–37% decline from current, approximating the early-2026 trough and the low analyst target of $41 as a nearer floor if only sentiment (not fundamentals) deteriorates.

### Base Case

The turnaround largely holds but with lumpy quarters, continued (smaller) integration costs, and margins settling between the FY25 trough and the Q1 recovery. FY26 lands near the middle of guidance. The stock trades sideways-to-modestly-higher as the market waits for multi-quarter confirmation. Base case ~$46–50, near the current price and the analyst mean of $49.33.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; we lack sufficient multi-quarter evidence to make a confident directional call after the stock's doubling.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters (Q2 and Q3 2026) confirming adjusted EBITDA margin holding at or above 7% with well-controlled medical cost trend, demonstrating the Q1 improvement is durable rather than seasonal.
- Remediation of the business-combination-accounting material weakness disclosed as complete in a subsequent 10-Q with no restatement.
- Net leverage reaching ≤2x with FCF tracking toward the upper half of the $105–132.5M guide.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quarter reverting to FY25-style margin compression (adjusted EBITDA margin back toward 6% on rising utilization) or a fourth GAAP miss.
- Any goodwill impairment, restatement, or material Prospect-seller liability absorption.
- Adverse regulatory determination on VIE consolidation or a quantified negative from OBBBA/MA rate changes.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings report (expected early August 2026), which the options market is pricing as a ±17% event. The full-risk cost-trend durability question resolves over the Q2–Q3 2026 window.

## Conclusion

Astrana Health is a leveraged value-based-care roll-up in the middle of a turnaround that is one quarter old. The bull narrative (margins recovering, cash flow surging, deleveraging ahead of plan, and a reasonable ~15x forward multiple) is coherent and supported by mildly positive estimate revisions. The bear counterweight is equally concrete: uncapped capitation risk that demonstrably halved GAAP earnings in FY25, a newly disclosed material weakness in the accounting function that matters most for a serial acquirer, the Prospect seller's post-close bankruptcy with recourse waived, and a structural VIE consolidation model that carries existential tail risk. The wide GAAP-versus-adjusted gap means the entire bull case rests on the durability of add-backs that a serial acquirer has structural reasons to keep incurring.

After a substantial run off the February lows, the risk/reward is roughly balanced. The forward valuation is not demanding if the turnaround holds, but the earnings-quality and internal-controls questions prevent us from treating apparent cheapness as an established mispricing, and the three-of-four-quarter miss record undercuts forward-estimate confidence. We rate ASTH a HOLD and would revisit toward BUY on two to three quarters of confirmed margin durability plus clean material-weakness remediation, or toward AVOID on any margin reversion, impairment, or restatement. Overall score: 5.35.
