## Executive Summary

ESCO Technologies is delivering the best operating year in its recent history. Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended 6/30/2026) produced sales of $339.0M (+14%, organic +8%), adjusted EPS of $2.20 (+38%), and a third upward revision to FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance — from the initial $7.50-7.80 in November, to $8.00-8.25 in May, to $8.30-8.40 now, a 9.2% raise at the midpoint against an original guide set nine months ago. Backlog reached a record $1,540.5M, up 35.9% from FY2025 year-end, on nine-month orders of $1,344.9M (1.43x book-to-bill, this time without acquired backlog). Nine-month adjusted EBIT margin expanded 250bp to 21.1%, and the Aerospace & Defense segment reached a 30.0% adjusted EBIT margin in the quarter. Management deliberately de-levered into the pending Megger transaction: total debt fell from $186M at 9/30/2025 to $85M at 6/30/2026, leaving $11.8M of net debt against roughly $225M of nine-month adjusted EBITDA.

The valuation has not followed the operating record down. At $303.82 the stock trades at 36.4x the midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, 33.1x the $9.19 FY2027 consensus, and 56.4x trailing GAAP continuing-operations EPS of roughly $5.39. Enterprise value of approximately $7.88bn is about 25x our estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $310-315M and 6.0x guided sales. FY2025 free cash flow of $164.1M against a $7.87bn market capitalization is a 2.1% yield. That is a full multiple for a company generating 8% organic revenue growth, where the reported revenue and EPS acceleration is substantially acquisition-driven and where the gap between adjusted and GAAP earnings widened from $0.70 to $1.57 per share of acquisition amortization in nine months.

The dominant unresolved variable is the agreed $2.35bn acquisition of Megger Group Limited — roughly 30% of ESCO's market capitalization, funded with $0.9bn of cash and approximately $1.4bn of newly issued stock, which on our arithmetic is about 4.67M shares, an 18.0% increase in share count and 15.3% ownership dilution for existing holders. ESCO's own FY2025 10-K names Megger as one of only four significant competitors in its Utility Solutions segment; the company states all regulatory filings are underway with closing expected in fiscal Q1 2027 (October-December 2026). The headline 14x EBITDA multiple is a synergized forward figure; backing out the $60M of guided three-year cost synergies implies roughly $108M of standalone 2026E EBITDA and a 21.8x ex-synergy entry price. We rate ESE **HOLD**: base case $335 (+10%), bull $400 (+32%), bear $220 (-28%), with the antitrust decision, disclosed pro forma covenants, and initial FY2027 guidance as the events that will resolve the call in either direction.

## Company Overview

ESCO is a deliberately decentralized holding company owning niche engineered-products businesses across three segments (FY2025 continuing revenue $1,095.4M, +19.2%):

- **Aerospace & Defense — 44% of FY2025 revenue** ($478.2M, +40.4%; 26.2% segment EBIT margin). PTI Technologies and Crissair (hydraulic filtration, fluid control, miniature electro-explosive devices for ejection seats), Mayday Manufacturing (precision machined landing-gear and rotor components), Globe/Westland (elastomeric signature-reduction and submarine hull treatments), and ESCO Maritime Solutions, the former Ultra Electronics Signature Management & Power business acquired April 2025 for ~$472M net of cash, supplying magnetic and electric field countermeasures and ultra-quiet propulsion motors for U.S. and UK naval vessels.
- **Utility Solutions Group — 35%** ($380.0M, +3.0%; 24.9% margin). Doble Engineering (high-voltage diagnostics, consulting, and a proprietary apparatus-performance data library licensed through a portal), Morgan Schaffer, Altanova/I.S.A., and NRG Systems (wind/solar measurement).
- **RF Test & Measurement — 21%** ($237.2M, +13.2%; 14.4% margin). ETS-Lindgren (RF/EMC chambers, shielded rooms, antennas) and MPE Limited (feedthrough capacitors, HEMP filters).

Two FY2025 portfolio actions reshaped the mix: the July 2025 sale of VACCO to RBC Bearings for ~$270M net proceeds and a $172.6M after-tax gain (exiting the Space business after a FY2024 pretax loss of $1.1M on $107.6M of sales), and the Maritime acquisition, which brought $364.2M of acquired backlog and $95.2M of FY2025 revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin mechanics.** FY2025 consolidated EBIT margin fell 30bp to 15.6% even though all three segments expanded margins, because Corporate costs rose $29.4M — $21.4M of incremental acquisition amortization and $5.5M of acquisition costs, principally Maritime, plus higher share-based compensation. That pattern is the structural signature of an amortization-heavy roll-up: segment economics improve while consolidated GAAP margins do not.

FY2026 has broken that pattern at the adjusted line. Nine-month adjusted EBIT of $198.0M on $938.0M of sales (21.1%) compares with $138.0M on $742.7M (18.6%) — 250bp of expansion, roughly half from the higher-margin Maritime mix and half from organic operating leverage. GAAP EBIT for the nine months was $134.3M. Nine-month GAAP continuing EPS was $3.66 versus adjusted $5.75; of the $2.09 gap, $1.57 is acquisition amortization, up from $0.70 in the prior-year period.

**Cash conversion.** FY2025 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $200.4M against $116.3M of continuing net earnings, a 1.72x conversion. Capex was $36.3M plus $15.8M of capitalized software; free cash flow measured against capex alone was $164.1M (15.0% margin), or roughly $148M including capitalized software. Two qualifications matter. First, the FY2025 working-capital tailwind was driven principally by a $135.7M increase in contract liabilities attributed to Maritime and to organic builds at Globe and Doble; the portion arriving through purchase accounting is not operating cash flow, and customer advances are a balance that must eventually be worked off as revenue is delivered. Second, nine-month FY2026 continuing OCF of $193.4M (versus $88.3M) sits against *total* OCF of only $134.0M, because discontinued operations consumed $59.3M — principally the ~$59M of cash taxes paid in Q2 on the VACCO gain, which drew current income tax payable from $62.0M to $5.8M. That was a liability extinguished by cash, not an earnings event.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/2026: cash $73.2M, total debt $85M, net debt $11.8M, equity $1,617.3M, total assets $2,420.0M. Debt fell $101M over nine months ($130M drawn, $231M repaid). The facility structure per the Q2 10-Q is a $500M revolver plus a $375M delayed-draw incremental term loan, both maturing 30 August 2028, seven-bank syndicate led by JPMorgan, weighted-average rates of 5.06% and 5.27% in Q2, with $440M available at 3/31/2026 and stated covenant compliance. Recomputing Altman Z from filed FY2025 inputs and current market equity yields approximately 7.0, comfortably in the safe zone; a screening figure of 2.69 that circulates on this name reflects a mis-mapped operating margin and should be disregarded. The Z-score is dominated by the market-equity-to-liabilities term and would compress in a drawdown, and it is a pre-deal figure.

Goodwill plus net intangibles of $1,424.8M equals 88% of book equity. No impairments were recorded at 9/30/2025 or in H1 FY2026.

One item that would mislead a mechanical screen: Q3 GAAP interest expense of $8.7M against adjusted interest of $1.85M, with $0.20 per share of adjustment attributed to Megger-related debt financing. ESCO is paying commitment and ticking fees on financing it has not drawn while borrowings *fell* $101M. The ~$6.9M quarterly delta is a reasonable proxy for the carrying cost of the financing commitment and will persist until the deal closes or terminates.

**Earnings quality and delivery.** Four consecutive beats: +7.6%, +23.0%, +2.8%, +2.7%. The magnitude of the beats has narrowed as the year progressed, which is consistent with guidance catching up to operations rather than with deteriorating delivery. Roughly 48% of A&D revenue (21% of consolidated) is recognized over time on cost-to-cost; FY2025 cumulative catch-up adjustments *increased* after-tax earnings by $2.6M ($0.10 per diluted share). That is modest and disclosed, but it establishes that reported earnings contain estimate revisions capable of reversing.

## Growth Analysis

**Order and backlog evidence is the strongest part of the file.** Backlog of $1,540.5M is up 35.9% from $1,133.6M at FY2025 year-end, on nine-month organic orders of $1,344.9M against $938.0M of shipments. Segment detail: Test posted record backlog of $248.6M on orders up 42%, which management attributes to industrial shielding projects and EMI filters for U.S. data centers — the clearest link in the portfolio to the AI/data-center electrical buildout, and in the lowest-margin segment (15.3% nine-month adjusted EBIT) with the most headroom. Doble orders rose 30% to $113.3M in the quarter on grid-maintenance and expansion spending. A&D order lumpiness is program-driven: $67M of Virginia Class Block V.2/VI plus $15M of Columbia Class in a single prior-year quarter.

Two qualifications on backlog. Remaining performance obligations of $1,470.0M at 3/31/2026 were only ~55% expected to convert within twelve months, down from a ~64% characterization at 9/30/2025 — visibility lengthened, near-term conversion velocity slowed. And RPO excludes unexercised options and IDIQ orders, so it is neither a total-opportunity figure nor a guaranteed one; the 10-K's own language notes government contracts may be terminated for convenience.

**Organic growth is 8%, not 19%.** FY2026 revenue guidance of $1.30-1.33bn implies 18.7-21.4% growth against FY2025's $1,095.4M, but Q3's decomposition was organic +$20M (+8%) and Maritime +$23M. Within USG, reported segment growth of 8% in Q3 masks Doble +17% offset by NRG −29% (orders −27% to $13.5M) on the expiration of U.S. renewables tax credits — a policy-driven decline unlikely to reverse on its own.

**Estimate revisions are positive but not an inflection signal.** Current-year consensus moved +0.5% over 30 days and +0.9% over 90 days to $8.24; next year +0.2% over 90 days to $9.19. That is directional confirmation from a thin three-to-four analyst panel, not the +30% revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings trough recovery. The more informative datapoint is management's own guidance path: $7.65 midpoint → $8.35 midpoint in nine months, with Q4 guided to $2.55-2.65 against a $2.52 consensus.

**Megger is the growth case beyond FY2027.** Megger would add ~$590M of 2026E revenue to USG, taking that segment to roughly $971M — about 51% of a ~$1.9bn pro forma revenue base and displacing A&D as the largest segment, inverting the FY2025 mix. Our accretion sensitivity: Megger standalone EBITDA of ~$108M less incremental interest of ~$48M on $900M at ~5.3% leaves ~$60M pre-tax, or roughly $1.51 per share after tax on 30.58M shares; against that, dilution alone takes a standalone $8.35 to $7.07, a $1.28 drag. Year-one adjusted EPS accretion is therefore roughly neutral before synergies and clearly positive as the $60M phases in over three years. On a GAAP basis the same transaction will be materially dilutive, because purchase amortization on $2.35bn will be large. Every input in that sketch is ours; the company has not published accretion guidance we could locate, and Megger's financials are unaudited company-supplied figures.

## Competitive Landscape

**Utility diagnostics.** The FY2025 10-K names four significant USG competitors: OMICRON Electronics, Megger Group, Vaisala, and Qualitrol (Ralliant). Doble's defensibility rests on a bundle of instruments, expert consulting, testing services, and an extensive apparatus-performance data library delivered to registered users through a portal — a genuine data asset that competitors cannot replicate quickly. Megger's disclosed product set (battery, cable, circuit breaker, relay, motor test equipment; online monitoring; grid analytics) is largely adjacent to Doble's transformer-centric portfolio, which is both the strategic logic and probably the antitrust defense. Demand is supported by global grid maintenance and expansion spending, consistent with the utility load-growth theme visible across the regulated-utility complex.

**Naval defense.** Competitors include Pall, Moog, Safran/Sofrance, TransDigm, Parker Hannifin, Collins Aerospace, L3Harris and Leonardo DRS. ESCO's position is narrow and qualification-based: submarine signature reduction and hull treatments, plus Maritime's magnetic/electric countermeasures and ultra-quiet propulsion. Maritime's IP is described as retained in design, engineering and manufacturing processes rather than primarily patented — trade-secret and specification protection rather than patent protection. A 30.0% adjusted EBIT margin implies sole-source or near-sole-source positions on specified programs, which is durable while programs run and fragile if a program is restructured. Multi-front defense demand is a supportive backdrop, but the exposure is program-specific rather than broadly cyclical.

**RF test and shielding.** Competitors include Rohde & Schwarz, MVG, TDK RF Solutions and Schaffner. ESCO describes itself as a global leader in EM shielding with vertical integration in critical RF components, and purchased an IP portfolio in FY2025 covering a tunable antenna capable of meeting a proposed DoD testing standard — a regulatory-driven demand hook. The data-center EMI filter orders are the segment's most interesting new vector.

Management's own framing is worth carrying: "Although we are a leading supplier in several of the markets we serve, we maintain a relatively small share of the business in many of our other markets," and competitive position "cannot be precisely stated." Trajectory is improving (margin expansion in all three segments, consolidation of a named USG competitor, exits from sub-scale lines), with NRG as the visible deterioration. **Competitive position 6.5.**

## Risk Assessment

**1. Megger antitrust review is a genuine binary, not a formality.** ESCO is acquiring a company it names as one of four significant competitors in the same segment, describing the rationale as "a more comprehensive set of solutions for our regulated electric utility customers" — the language of a horizontal combination. Filings are underway in multiple jurisdictions given Megger's UK, European, North American and Asian hubs. A remedy requiring divestiture of overlapping lines would impair the synergy case that supports the 14x headline multiple; an outright block removes the growth case beyond FY2027 but also removes 15% dilution and ~$7M per quarter of ticking fees, so the equity outcome of a break is genuinely ambiguous rather than clearly negative.

**2. Price paid and dilution.** The 14x multiple is synergized. Ex-synergy it is 21.8x on ~$108M of standalone 2026E EBITDA (18.3% margin on $590M of revenue), and those Megger figures come solely from ESCO's own April 2026 announcement — no audited Megger statements appear in any ESCO filing. Existing holders give up 15.3% of ownership, and TBG AG becomes one of the largest holders with nomination rights for one board seat, in a company that already has a classified board, no shareholder right to call special meetings, blank-check preferred authorized, and a Missouri two-thirds merger vote.

**3. Unverified financing terms.** A 3 June 2026 8-K carried Items 1.01, 1.02 and 2.03 — new material agreement, terminated agreement, and creation of a direct financial obligation — almost certainly the replacement facility funding the cash consideration. Facility size, tranching, maturities, pricing, and post-close covenant leverage limits are undisclosed to us. Our ~2.2x pro forma net leverage estimate (net debt ~$912M against ~$418M pro forma adjusted EBITDA) therefore has no covenant context.

**4. Rising government and program concentration.** U.S. Government direct and indirect sales moved from 17% (FY2023) to 20% to 23% of continuing revenue, and the H1 FY2026 customer-type table shows government revenue of $220.0M of $599.0M (36.7%) versus $103.7M of $446.4M (23.2%) a year earlier. Segment diversification and customer concentration are moving in opposite directions. The 10-K warns that a reduction or delay on Navy submarine programs "could extend for more than a single year," and separately flags UK naval funding decisions as a direct Maritime exposure. Megger, if it closes, would mechanically dilute the government share.

**5. Operational single points of failure.** Doble relies on six contract manufacturers, one of which produces ~23% of Doble's products from a single U.S. location; Globe has a single supplier of critical materials for a significant military production program; aerospace titanium and helium are periodically short; several A&D operations sit near major California fault lines.

**6. Control-environment scope.** Management's disclosure-controls conclusion at 3/31/2026 excludes Maritime's internal control over financial reporting pending integration — permitted practice, but the largest new revenue contributor sat outside the assessment perimeter. Megger will reopen the same window post-close. ESCO's own risk factors name decentralization as a control risk: "we may be slower or less able to identify or react to problems affecting a key business," including compliance problems.

**7. Multiple risk.** At 36x guided adjusted EPS with a 2.1% FCF yield, there is no valuation cushion for a quarter that misses. One secondary account of the Q3 print noted revenue below consensus alongside the EPS beat, and the stock fell roughly 7% in the session following the release. We cannot attribute that move to a single cause from the supplied data — candidate drivers include the revenue line and deal-related positioning — but the sensitivity itself is the point.

**8. Environmental.** ESCO is a responsible party in several ongoing investigations and remediations of contaminated third-party properties, with costs "difficult to quantify but could have a significant effect."

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 39.1% against 33.0% one-year realized — rich by 6.1 points — with a flat term structure (39.1% near, 37.7% at six months) and a ±6.4% implied move on the 21 August expiry, which is large for a name that has already reported. Implied volatility compressed from 44.7% on 20 July to 39.1%, consistent with earnings uncertainty resolving, while put/call open interest rose from 0.16 to 0.32 and put volume ran at 13x call volume on the observation date. The reported near-term skew is balanced (0.0%); we do not infer direction from the six-month value. The combination we read is fresh protection being bought into a stock that just fell 7%, against standing open interest that remains call-tilted, with residual event premium priced through the Megger decision window. Short interest is low at 3.7% of float and 2.3 days to cover, so positioning is not crowded in either direction. This neither confirms nor contradicts our fundamental view; it is consistent with a market that expects a discrete resolution rather than a trend.

**Risk factors score 4.7** — a dated regulatory binary on 30% of market capitalization, undisclosed post-close covenants, and rising government concentration, against a multiple that prices continued execution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $400 (+31.7%)

Megger clears without remedies in fiscal Q1 2027. Pro forma leverage lands near 2.2x with adequate covenant headroom, and management begins delivering against the $60M synergy target on schedule. Doble's 30% order growth and Megger's adjacent product lines compound as global grid capex accelerates; Test converts its record $248.6M backlog and 42% order growth into margin expansion from 15.3% toward the high teens on data-center shielding and EMI filter demand; A&D holds 28-30% margins as Virginia Class Block V.2/VI and Columbia Class volumes run. FY2028 adjusted EPS reaches $10.50-11.00 on ~30.6M shares as synergies phase in, and the market pays 37-38x — the multiple it paid in June 2026 — for a business with 51% of revenue in regulated utility markets and 85% in utility plus defense. NRG is divested or annualizes away. $400 is 41x FY2027 consensus or roughly 37x an FY2028 pro forma figure.

### Bear Case → $220 (-27.6%)

Two credible paths. First, regulatory: reviewers require divestiture of overlapping USG product lines, cutting the synergy pool and leaving ESCO with 15% dilution against a diminished asset, or the deal is blocked after ESCO has absorbed four or more quarters of ~$7M ticking fees. Second, multiple: with 8% organic growth, a 2.1% FCF yield, and 56x trailing GAAP earnings, a compression to 26x on a standalone $8.35-8.50 puts the stock at $217-221 without requiring any operating disappointment. Contributing pressures: government revenue at 37% collides with appropriations timing or a submarine program restructuring; the fixed-price and over-time revenue base produces a negative cumulative catch-up after a $2.6M positive in FY2025; contract liabilities that added $135.7M of FY2025 working capital reverse as advances are delivered; NRG's decline persists and Test's data-center orders prove to be a pull-forward. The balance sheet makes a solvency outcome implausible — the risk is de-rating, not distress.

### Base Case → $335 (+10.3%)

Megger closes late in calendar 2026 with modest conditions. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $9.20-9.50 on a partial Megger year, with dilution largely offsetting acquired earnings before synergies. Organic growth persists at 6-9%, adjusted EBIT margin holds near 21%, and the market pays 35-36x. That is $335. It also approximately equals 40x a standalone FY2027 $8.50 should the deal break and the multiple hold on a cleaner, unlevered, undiluted company; the two paths converge closer than the headlines suggest, which is why we do not treat the deal outcome as directionally decisive for a 12-month holder.

**Probability-weighted:** 50% base (+10.3%), 25% bull (+31.7%), 25% bear (-27.6%) = approximately **+6.2%** expected 12-month return, plus a 0.1% dividend. Positive but below the threshold that would justify adding capital at this price.

### Score Trajectory

Four consecutive HOLDs with the overall score drifting 6.5 → 6.4 → 6.1 → 6.0 (velocity −0.20/month). The current data both confirms and partially reverses that drift. Reversing it: guidance has been raised twice since the May report, backlog set a record on organic orders, nine-month adjusted EBIT margin expanded 250bp, net debt fell to $11.8M, and the entry price is 16% below the June high — all of which argues the operating trend has strengthened, not weakened. Sustaining it: management quality (previously 6.5 → 5.6) stays at 5.5 because the largest capital-allocation decision in company history is being made at 21.8x ex-synergy on unaudited seller figures, with 15.3% ownership dilution, no offsetting buyback, no open-market insider buying in the retrieved February-July 2026 Form 4 window (director RSU activity only), and adjusted EPS as the company-selected compensation metric — the measure most likely to reward amortization-excluded M&A. ROIC's presence among the five plan metrics is a genuine offset, as is the VACCO exit, which monetized a loss-making unit at a $172.6M after-tax gain and funded the deleveraging. Financial health rises modestly from the prior 6.3 to 6.8 on the $101M nine-month debt reduction and a recomputed Altman Z near 7.0, tempered by ~$912M of pro forma net debt against unverified covenants. Growth prospects hold at 7.0 rather than recovering toward 8.0, because the 8% organic rate and slowing backlog conversion (55% within twelve months, from ~64%) are the honest read behind a 19-21% reported top line. We are repeating the rating, but on a different balance of evidence: better operations, better entry price, unchanged absence of margin of safety, and one large unresolved binary.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing holders should maintain exposure through the Megger decision. New capital should wait.

- **Base case target $335** — 36x an FY2027 pro forma adjusted EPS of approximately $9.30, in line with the 33-38x forward adjusted multiple the stock has carried through FY2026. **+10.3% from $303.82.**
- **Bull case $400** — +31.7%. Requires clean regulatory clearance, disclosed covenant headroom, and evidence of synergy capture, with Test margins inflecting on data-center backlog.
- **Bear case $220** — −27.6%. Multiple compression to 26x standalone earnings, or a remedy-impaired deal.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to three dated events — the Q4/FY2026 print and initial FY2027 guidance (November 2026), Megger regulatory resolution and closing (guided fiscal Q1 2027, i.e. October-December 2026), and the FY2027 10-K, which will be the first filing to carry Megger-specific risk factors and post-close covenant terms.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Megger closes without material remedies **and** the company discloses pro forma net leverage at or below 2.5x with covenant headroom of at least one turn, with FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance at or above $9.50 on the post-issuance share count.
2. Test segment adjusted EBIT margin exceeds 17.5% for two consecutive quarters on conversion of the $248.6M backlog, evidencing that data-center shielding demand carries structurally better economics than the segment's 15.3% nine-month rate.
3. The price falls to $250-260 (roughly 30x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, 27x FY2027 consensus) on no fundamental deterioration, which would convert base-case upside from 10% to 30%.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if:**
1. Regulators require divestiture of USG product lines, or the deal is abandoned after material fee absorption, **and** management signals a replacement acquisition at comparable multiples.
2. Organic revenue growth decelerates below 4% for two consecutive quarters, or nine-month adjusted EBIT margin gives back more than 100bp from 21.1%.
3. Post-close covenant leverage headroom proves to be less than half a turn, or goodwill and intangibles (currently $1,424.8M, 88% of book equity) sustain an impairment charge.
4. Cumulative catch-up adjustments on over-time contracts turn negative by more than $0.10 per share in any quarter, indicating the FY2025 $2.6M benefit is reversing.

**Next reassessment: the FY2026 fourth-quarter release and initial FY2027 guidance in November 2026**, or immediately on any 8-K announcing regulatory clearance, a second request, or termination.

## Conclusion

ESCO has produced a genuinely strong operating year: 19-21% guided revenue growth, 38-39% adjusted EPS growth, 250bp of nine-month adjusted EBIT margin expansion, four consecutive earnings beats, three upward guidance revisions, record backlog on a 1.43x organic book-to-bill, and a balance sheet carrying $11.8M of net debt after $101M of deliberate nine-month deleveraging. The niches are defensible — 30% A&D margins imply sole-source program positions, and Doble's data library is a real asset — and the demand backdrop across grid capex, naval programs and data-center electrical infrastructure is corroborated by order growth in each.
