**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

APA Corporation trades at $36.68 after a 94.8% run off its June 2025 low of $17.74, recovering roughly to the midpoint of its 52-week range. The move has been driven by deleveraging execution (debt down ~$1.6B in 2025), cost cuts ($350M annualized achieved, $450M targeted by end-2026), a string of earnings beats, and a sharp upward revision to consensus EPS as oil-price assumptions reset higher on the Iran/Hormuz risk premium. Current-year consensus EPS has been revised up 188.5% over 90 days to $6.47, which puts the trailing and forward P/E near 8.5x. The headline multiple is cheap; the question is what earnings power it is being applied to.

The earnings figure driving that multiple is a commodity-premium artifact. Consensus implies an oil and US gas environment materially above what APA realized in Q1 2026, when US gas was sold at negative prices (-$0.32 to -$0.35/Mcf), the company curtailed ~88 MMcf/d of US gas, US production fell 11% YoY, and operating cash flow dropped 49% YoY to $554M. The Q1 EPS beat (non-GAAP $1.38 vs $1.13 estimate, +21.9%) was met with a negative stock reaction because the cash-flow and volume trajectory underneath the beat was weak, and a $244M "net gain on oil and gas purchases and sales" (a marketing/arbitrage gain off wide Permian-to-Gulf gas differentials) flattered reported income without representing repeatable production economics. On normalized $70-75 WTI, the implied earnings power is well below the $6.47 the multiple is currently being struck against.

The offsetting case is specific: Suriname's GranMorgu development is de-risked post-FID, with TotalEnergies as operator carrying 87.5% of the first $10B of gross capex, a $75M first-oil payment to APA, and first oil targeted for 2028 at 220,000 b/d FPSO capacity. That is asymmetric optionality a pure-play Permian peer does not have. Weighed against it: a ~$1B Gulf of America legacy decommissioning contingency on assets sold over a decade ago, Egypt counterparty/FX/sovereign risk (~15% of revenue through EGPC), insider selling into the rally with no open-market buys, and a five-year TSR (196.81) that has lagged the peer group (295.11) by ~100 points. The balance lands on HOLD. The score trajectory has improved from 5.5 to 6.0 over four prior HOLD reports, and current data confirms incremental improvement in financial health while reaffirming that the valuation is more contingent on the oil tape than the headline multiple suggests.

## Company Overview

APA is an independent oil and gas exploration and production company (holding company over operating subsidiary Apache Corporation), headquartered in Houston with ~1,791 employees and a $13.0B market capitalization. Production comes from three producing regions plus one development asset:

- **United States / Permian Basin** — The foundational asset: ~62% of 2025 production, 53% of oil and gas revenue, and 74% of proved reserves. Midland Basin (~288K net acres) and Delaware Basin (~166K net acres), with legacy Gulf of America offshore acreage. 100% drilling success rate in 2025.
- **Egypt (Western Desert)** — Largest acreage holder in the region (~7.5M gross acres), 31% of 2025 production (including Sinopec's one-third noncontrolling interest), 17% of reserves. Operated via a merged concession agreement with EGPC under production-sharing contracts. A new gas sales agreement effective January 2025 (floor $2.65/MMBtu with upside on incremental volumes) has improved gas-drilling economics.
- **UK North Sea** — In managed decline: 7% of production, 2% of reserves. APA expects to cease North Sea production before 2030, citing the 38% UK Energy Profits Levy and aging-infrastructure obligations.
- **Suriname (Block 58, GranMorgu)** — 50% working interest, TotalEnergies operator, FID reached October 2024, ~$10.5B gross investment, first oil targeted 2028 at 220,000 b/d.

The differentiator versus pure-play Permian peers is diversification (US shale plus international conventional plus offshore exploration optionality), which provides capital-allocation flexibility but adds international political/fiscal risk and aging-asset decommissioning liabilities that pure-play peers do not carry.

## Financial Analysis

**Full-year 2025:** Revenue of $17.84B, down from $19.47B in 2024 on a 14% drop in realized oil prices ($66.92/bbl) and asset sales. Net income was $1.434B ($3.99/diluted share) versus $804M ($2.27) in 2024, but the year-over-year jump is largely an impairment artifact — 2024 carried $1.1B of impairments ($796M North Sea, $315M US). The underlying improvement came from cost reduction: LOE fell to $8.86/boe (-13%). Operating cash flow rose 26% to $4.5B, aided by collection of past-due Egypt receivables, a one-time benefit that does not recur.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation:** Total debt was reduced ~$1.6B in 2025 to ~$4.5B; ~$3.6B has been repaid since YE2021, and ~$555M more was redeemed post-Q1 2026. Weighted-average interest rate is 5.62%, all fixed. Equity is $7.0B, cash $516M at YE2025, D/E 0.42, Altman Z-Score 9.39 (safe zone), current ratio 0.82 (below 1, but typical for E&P). The capital-return framework returns 60% of free cash flow: $0.25/quarter dividend (~$360M/yr, 2.63% yield) plus buybacks — 12.9M shares for $280M in 2025 at an average of $21.73, with 21.9M shares remaining on authorization. Buying back at $21.73 in 2025 was sensible capital allocation; the relevant point for a buyer today is that the company itself was acquiring stock at roughly 41% below the current price.

**Q1 2026:** Net income $446M ($1.26/sh) versus $347M ($0.96) in Q1 2025; revenue $2.33B. The market reaction was negative despite the beat, and the reason is in the cash statement. The prior-year quarter was flattered by a one-time Egypt receivable catch-up, so the year-over-year decline overstates underlying deterioration, but the core signals are still weak — US production down on natural decline, non-core sales, and curtailment of ~88 MMcf/d of US gas and 6,800 b/d of NGLs because Waha hub prices were negative. The $244M "net gain on oil and gas purchases and sales" should not be extrapolated as recurring production economics. G&A of $115M included ~$25M of higher-than-expected stock comp from mark-to-market on the rising share price.

The earnings-surprise record is strong on paper — four consecutive beats (79.5%, 16.7%, 40.4%, 21.9%) — but the magnitude of recent beats has compressed and the beats have been against estimates that were chasing a rising oil tape, not against a stable demand-driven baseline.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth story is overstated by the commodity cycle. Estimate revisions are extraordinary on the surface: current-year EPS revised +188.5% over 90 days to $6.47, current quarter +290.5% to $2.10, next year +68.1% to $4.32. These figures meet the mechanical threshold that often flags an earnings inflection. The cause here is not a fundamental volume or margin inflection — it is consensus marking up oil-price assumptions on the Hormuz risk premium. The same revisions reverse if oil mean-reverts toward $70-75. Applying the inflection-stock framework to a number that is a function of the oil futures curve would be a category error; the appropriate treatment is to normalize.

The genuine, non-commodity growth driver is Suriname, de-risked relative to typical frontier exploration because it is post-FID with a major operator funding the bulk of near-term spend. It is also long-dated — value accrues in 2028, not in the next twelve months — so it supports a patient holding case more than a near-term re-rating.

Egypt offers near-term volume growth (+8% net in Q1 on the gas program, ~half of a 12-rig program now gas-focused under the improved gas sales agreement, plus 2M net new exploration acres awarded in Q3 2025). Against that, the US base is in decline and being actively curtailed on negative gas pricing, North Sea is winding down to zero before 2030, and 2026 capex of ~$2.1B is being held flat. The net trajectory is modest organic decline in the core US asset offset by Egypt growth and a long-dated Suriname catalyst. That supports a growth-prospects score in the mid-range, not higher.

## Valuation Assessment

At $36.68, APA trades at ~8.6x trailing and ~8.5x forward earnings, a discount to the peer median P/E of 19.3x. But the peer set is heterogeneous (DINO refiner 10.8x, AR gas 11.5x, PR 22.3x, OVV 19.3x, DTM midstream 31.6x), and APA's discount is partly structural — it carries the GOA decommissioning contingency, Egypt counterparty risk, and a five-year TSR that has lagged peers by ~100 points. The cheap headline multiple is being applied to an oil-premium-inflated $6.47 consensus EPS.

The disciplined approach is to value on normalized earnings. The Q1 quarter, with negative US gas realizations and an arbitrage-flattered headline, illustrates how much of consensus depends on commodity prices staying elevated. On a normalized $70-75 WTI environment and stripping the non-repeatable marketing gain, implied earnings power is meaningfully below $6.47 — call it roughly $3.50-4.50 — which puts the stock at 8-10x normalized, closer to fair than cheap for a declining-US-base independent with international and decommissioning overhangs. Seeking Alpha's ~6.7x forward P/E / 3.6x EV/EBITDA and $46.72 target rest on the elevated consensus number; on normalized earnings the discount narrows substantially.

The valuation's asymmetric upside is the Suriname option, which the trailing multiple does not capture. A first-oil event in 2028 at 220,000 b/d gross with TotalEnergies funding the bulk of capex is real embedded value. That optionality, plus the 2.63% yield and disciplined 60%-of-FCF return framework, supports a valuation score above neutral but below the level that would justify a BUY on a commodity-contingent name where the energy complex has a poor realized track record.

Analyst targets span $34 (low) to $62 (high), mean $43.27, median $42.50. Sentiment is degrading at the margin (one strongBuy downgraded to buy; 15 holds and 4 sells dominate the 27-analyst panel). Treat these targets as oil-price-contingent rather than as independent confirmation of upside.

## Competitive Landscape

APA is a mid-cap diversified independent competing against larger, lower-cost pure-play Permian operators. Its competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating. The diversification that distinguishes it (international conventional plus offshore optionality) provides capital-allocation flexibility and the unique Suriname catalyst, but it also dilutes the per-barrel cost advantage that the best Permian pure-plays enjoy and saddles APA with decommissioning liabilities peers do not carry.

On trajectory: the US core is in managed decline and being curtailed on negative gas pricing, which is a structural takeaway disadvantage in the Permian gas market rather than a company-specific failing, but it pressures volumes and realizations. Egypt is gaining (acreage awards, gas economics), and the Callon integration delivered synergies and Delaware/Midland inventory. The 100% 2025 drilling success rate and $8.86/boe LOE indicate competent operational execution. Net, the company is holding its position rather than gaining share, with the clearest competitive differentiation being Suriname optionality that won't pay off until 2028.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is heavier than the cheap multiple implies.

1. **Gulf of America legacy decommissioning.** APA's most distinctive balance-sheet risk: $878M recorded contingent liability (up to $0.9-1.2B potential) plus an agreement to lend GOM Shelf up to $400M, on assets sold to a now-twice-bankrupt buyer over a decade ago. Total ARO is $2.9B. This is an off-strategy, slow-bleed cash drain that screens miss and pure-play peers don't carry.

2. **Egypt / EGPC counterparty and sovereign risk.** ~15% of worldwide revenue, a history of FX shortages and delayed payments (largely cleared in Q3 2025 but can recur), expropriation/renegotiation/repatriation risk, and Sinopec's one-third stake ($430M distributions in 2025).

3. **Negative Waha gas basis.** Recurring negative realized US gas prices forcing curtailments — a structural Permian takeaway problem partly mitigated by basis swaps on ~1/3 of 2026 firm transport.

4. **North Sea regulatory escalation.** 38% Energy Profits Levy through March 2030; the OPRED/OSPAR "clear seabed" consultation could raise removal costs above current estimates.

5. **Iran/Middle East conflict** — the only material risk-factor change added in the Q1 2026 10-Q, cutting both ways: it inflates the oil-price assumptions currently lifting APA's consensus EPS, but a Hormuz disruption that hits Egypt's FX/payments is a transmission channel into APA's largest international counterparty.

6. **Frontier execution.** Suriname (2028 first oil), Alaska appraisal, and Uruguay (litigation seeking to enjoin seismic/drilling) carry deferral/impairment risk.

7. **Tax/holding-company structure.** $2.3B deferred tax asset subject to Section 382 ownership-change limitation; reliance on subsidiary distributions.

The commodity exposure cuts in APA's favor under the prevailing Hormuz risk premium but against it if the conflict de-escalates and oil mean-reverts. Because the entire estimate-revision picture is a function of oil, the single biggest swing factor for the stock is the energy tape, which is binary and not in APA's control.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-cautiously constructive and does not flag acute stress. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.27 to 0.39 over the late-May to early-June window as the price slipped from $38.80 to $37.24, indicating modestly increased hedging into a softer tape but well short of a put-skew warning. Short interest is elevated at 10.3% of float with 4.2 days to cover — a meaningful but not extreme bearish institutional stance, and not high enough to constitute squeeze fuel on its own. The combination is consistent with a stock that the market views as fairly valued and commodity-dependent rather than as a high-conviction long or a crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Oil stays elevated on a sustained Hormuz/Middle East premium; consensus EPS of ~$6.47 proves achievable; the 60%-of-FCF return framework drives continued buybacks (21.9M shares authorized) and the dividend; deleveraging continues; Egypt gas growth offsets US decline; and Suriname GranMorgu progresses on schedule toward 2028 first oil, re-rating the stock for an option the current multiple ignores. At 9-10x a sustained ~$6 EPS the stock reaches the high-$40s to low-$50s, toward the analyst mean of $43.27 and beyond.

**Bear case.** The Iran conflict de-escalates, oil mean-reverts toward $70-75, and the upward estimate revisions reverse. US production keeps declining ~11% with negative Waha gas forcing curtailments; the $244M marketing gain does not repeat; operating cash flow stays under pressure; the GOA decommissioning liability bleeds cash; and Egypt payment delays recur. On normalized earnings the stock is worth low-to-mid $30s, near the analyst low of $34, roughly 7-10% below current.

The honest read is that the bull case is largely a bet on the oil price and the bear case is largely a bet against it, with Suriname as the one company-specific upside lever and GOA/Egypt as the company-specific downside levers. That symmetry, against a sector where commodity-premium-inflated E&P longs have a poor realized track record, supports holding rather than buying.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August) for US production stabilization and operating cash flow trajectory; ongoing monitoring of the Hormuz situation as the dominant oil-price swing factor; Suriname development milestones through 2028.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A durable energy regime — sustained verified Hormuz disruption or a structural oil floor — combined with evidence that core US volumes have stabilized (US production decline narrowing below ~5% YoY) and operating cash flow recovering above ~$700M/quarter, OR
- A pullback toward the low-$30s that restores asymmetric upside to the normalized-earnings fair range and the Suriname option, OR
- Concrete Suriname de-risking (cost/schedule confirmation, first-oil pull-forward) that the market can begin to capitalize.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A decisive, verified Hormuz de-escalation driving oil toward $70 with consensus EPS revisions turning sharply negative,
- An escalation of the GOA decommissioning liability above the current $1.2B upper estimate or a GOM Shelf default forcing the $400M loan, or
- Recurrence of material Egypt payment delays / FX repatriation problems.

## Conclusion

APA is a competently run, deleveraging mid-cap E&P trading at a cheap headline multiple, with a differentiated and de-risked Suriname catalyst maturing into 2028. The case against pressing it is that the cheap multiple is being applied to an oil-premium-inflated consensus EPS, the core US asset is in decline and selling gas at negative prices, operating cash flow fell 49% YoY in Q1, the Q1 beat was partly an arbitrage artifact that drew a negative market reaction, and the company carries a ~$1B off-strategy decommissioning overhang plus Egypt counterparty risk. Insiders sold into the 2026 rally with no offsetting open-market purchases, and the five-year TSR lags peers by ~100 points. The stock is up 94.8% off its low; that appreciation reflects real deleveraging and cost progress, but the forward case now hinges on whether the oil price holds, which is a macro bet rather than a company-specific edge.

The score improves incrementally to 5.8, consistent with the upward trajectory, with valuation marked down from its prior level because the discount narrows materially on normalized rather than oil-premium earnings. Recommendation: **HOLD**. The most attractive action is to wait for either a pullback toward the low-$30s that restores asymmetry or confirmation that US volumes and cash flow have stabilized, rather than chasing a commodity-contingent name near the middle of its range.
