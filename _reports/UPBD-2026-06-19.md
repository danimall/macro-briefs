**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Upbound Group is a holding company that lends to and leases goods for cash- and credit-constrained consumers through four segments: Acima virtual lease-to-own (~54% of FY25 revenue), the legacy Rent-A-Center store base (~40%), the Brigit fintech business acquired in January 2025 (~5%), and Mexico (~2%). At $18.32 the stock sits ~35% below its 52-week high of $28.03, down ~24% over the trailing year, and trades at 12.1x forward earnings with an 8.3% dividend yield. The central question is whether that drawdown reflects terminal deterioration or a leveraged, cash-generative business being repriced for a regulatory overhang that is already substantially reserved.

The evidence points to the latter. The FY25 GAAP earnings collapse (net income -40.7%) was largely non-operational: gross profit rose +9.2%, and the headline decline came from ~$60.7M of legal accruals plus ~$60M of Brigit deal costs, amortization, and stock comp running through "other gains and charges." Q1'26 confirmed the underlying business is healthier than FY25 net income suggested: revenue +3.7%, operating margin up 100bps to 6.3%, net income +44.4%, diluted EPS $0.61 versus $0.42. The company generated $238.7M of FCF in FY25 (covering the ~$88M dividend roughly 2.7x), repaid $516M of debt, and extended its term loan maturity to 2032. Estimate revisions are rising (Current Quarter +1.5%/30d, Next Year +1.2%/90d), Zacks upgraded the name to Strong Buy on 5/21/26, and the sell-side consensus is unanimously non-bearish (1 strong buy, 5 buy, 1 hold, 0 sells) with a low target of $20, above spot.

Against this sit genuine risks that justify caution rather than a buy: 3.71x debt-to-equity with ~$1.1B of unhedged floating-rate debt (a 1% rate move = ~$10M pretax), an unsigned 43-state Multistate AG settlement that would impose injunctive changes on Acima even if the cash component is reserved, Acima GMV turning -5.9% YoY in Q1 despite +8.6% for full-year FY25, Brigit net advance losses drifting to 3.5% from 2.4%, and Rent-A-Center's persistent secular decline. We rate UPBD a HOLD with a constructive lean, owning it for the covered yield and a plausible settlement-relief re-rating while respecting the leverage and the regulatory tail.

## Company Overview

Upbound serves underserved consumers who do not qualify for traditional credit, monetizing them across lease-to-own and fintech products.

**Acima** ($2.51B FY25 revenue, ~54%): virtual and staffed lease-to-own offered through thousands of third-party retailers via in-store kiosks, online "waterfall" integrations, and a growing direct-to-consumer channel. Proprietary algorithmic decisioning approves customers banks decline. FY25 GMV was $2.01B (+8.6%) at an 11.7% segment operating margin. This is the growth engine and the highest-risk segment on both regulatory and merchandise-loss dimensions.

**Rent-A-Center** ($1.90B, ~40%): legacy company-owned stores (1,720 corporate + 327 franchise at Q1'26) plus rentacenter.com, now ~28% of segment LTO revenue. Gross margin is high (~66%) but the segment is in secular decline: FY25 revenue -4.2%, same-store sales -2.2%, shrinking store count via closures and refranchising.

**Brigit** ($206M partial-year FY25, ~5%): a fintech acquired 1/31/25 offering earned wage access ("Instant Cash"), Credit Builder, subscriptions, and a deals marketplace. Very high gross margin (~88%) and ~27% segment operating margin in Q1'26. This is the strategic pivot toward "financial health technology."

**Mexico** ($79M, ~2%): 141 LTO stores, roughly breakeven.

Upbound competes with Progressive Leasing/Prog Holdings and Katapult in virtual LTO, BNPL providers (Affirm, Klarna, Afterpay) encroaching on its customer base, payday/installment lenders, and EWA peers (DailyPay, EarnIn, MoneyLion) for Brigit. Its differentiation is omnichannel reach plus proprietary decisioning data, but Acima itself flags low barriers to entry in virtual LTO as a structural risk.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY25 revenue was $4.70B (+8.7% YoY), but the growth is acquisition- and Acima-driven: Brigit added ~$206M and Acima ~$251M, masking an $83M (-4.2%) decline at Rent-A-Center. Organic core LTO is roughly flat-to-down.

**Profitability.** FY25 operating profit fell 23.4% to $223M (4.8% margin versus 6.7% in FY24); net income fell 40.7% to $73.2M. The mechanism matters: gross profit rose +9.2% to $2.27B, while "other gains and charges" jumped $108M to $212M, comprising ~$60.7M of legal accruals, ~$60.1M of Brigit acquisition costs/intangible amortization/stock comp, and impairments. Q1'26 reversed the optics: revenue +3.7% to $1.22B, operating margin 6.3% (versus 5.3%), net income +44.4% to $35.8M, diluted EPS $0.61. Lower legal accruals (-$6.6M) and G&A discipline drove the improvement. The Q1 earnings surprise record is consistent: three beats in the last four quarters, with the most recent print at $1.08 versus $1.05 estimate.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $305.6M and FCF $238.7M (5.1% margin); Q1'26 OCF was $170.7M. Total debt is ~$1.6B (down to ~$1.5B at Q1'26): $450M of 6.375% notes due Feb 2029, an $870.6M term loan at 6.42% extended to Aug 2032, and a $135M ABL revolver at 5.76% due Jun 2029. Approximately $1.1B is variable rate with no interest rate swaps; a 1% rate move is roughly $10M of annual pretax impact. This is the most acute financial vulnerability given the higher-for-longer rate path. Debt-to-equity is 3.71x, Altman Z is 2.12 (grey zone), equity-to-assets is 21%, and the entity is non-investment-grade. Goodwill plus intangibles ($838M) exceed total equity ($696M), so an Acima or Brigit underperformance carries impairment risk.

**Capital allocation.** The $1.56/year dividend yields ~8.3% and cost ~$88M in FY25, covered ~2.7x by FCF. The $235M buyback authorization was untouched in FY25 and Q1'26, with capital prioritized to debt paydown ($516M repaid in FY25) and the Brigit deal. Active deleveraging against a covered dividend is the strongest part of the financial profile and undercuts a distress narrative.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely mixed, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a clear call in either direction.

On the constructive side, the consolidated business is showing earnings momentum the GAAP FY25 figures obscure. Q1'26 EPS rose 45% with margin expansion, and consensus estimates are drifting up rather than down, with the Zacks Strong Buy upgrade reflecting that revision momentum. Brigit is the high-margin optionality and the strategic bet that a fintech subscription model can supplement declining store LTO.

On the cautionary side, the headline FY25 Acima GMV of +8.6% turned to -5.9% YoY in Q1'26. That divergence is the key thing to monitor: if it reflects deliberate underwriting tightening, it protects merchandise losses; if it reflects demand softening, the growth engine is decelerating into a weakening subprime consumer. The broader consumer backdrop is unfavorable for this customer base, as subprime borrowers are the most exposed to inflation, tax-refund timing, and the loss of stimulus, and payment behavior directly drives merchandise losses. Rent-A-Center's structural decline is a persistent drag, and the e-commerce shift within that segment has uncertain net profitability.

The estimate revisions here are modestly positive: Current Year is flat at $4.20 and Next Year is +1.2%/90d, indicating improving sentiment rather than a step-change. We weight this as supportive of the floor rather than a catalyst for a re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $18.32 against $4.20 current-year and $4.76 next-year EPS estimates, UPBD trades at 12.1x forward and ~10.7x next-year earnings. The 8.3% dividend yield provides absolute support: even with no multiple re-rating, an investor earns a covered high-single-digit yield while the company deleverages.

Peer comparison is awkward because the sector screen pulls heterogeneous Consumer Discretionary names: Cracker Barrel at 39.5x, Gentherm at 49.2x, BJ's Restaurants at 26x, and two Under Armour share classes with negative earnings. The peer median P/E of 39.5x is not a meaningful benchmark for a leveraged subprime-finance platform. More relevant: virtual-LTO and consumer-finance comps generally trade at low-double-digit multiples reflecting regulatory and credit risk, so UPBD's 12.1x is roughly fair-to-cheap within its true cohort rather than against the screened restaurant peers.

The analyst targets are informative here. The low target is $20, above spot, with a median of $28 and a high of $41, and a unanimously non-bearish consensus. On a cheap stock with rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag rather than lead, which argues the downside is reasonably bounded. We do not, however, treat the median $28 as a near-term base case; the regulatory binary and GMV deceleration are real reasons the market discounts those targets.

The honest read is that 12.1x forward on a deleveraging, FCF-generative platform with a covered 8.3% yield is consistent with priced-in risk. There is asymmetric upside if the Multistate AG matter settles cleanly (the cash is reserved, so a signed deal removes uncertainty rather than capital), but the leverage and Acima deceleration cap how aggressively one can underwrite that. We score valuation moderately constructive (6.0): cheap with identifiable catalyst optionality, but not a screaming dislocation given the genuine tail risks.

## Competitive Landscape

Upbound is a scaled leader in both virtual LTO (Acima) and traditional LTO (Rent-A-Center), but the competitive trajectory is mixed rather than improving. Acima's moat is its retailer integrations and proprietary decisioning data, yet the company itself flags low barriers to entry in virtual LTO, and BNPL providers are steadily encroaching on the underbanked customer base that LTO historically served. Rent-A-Center competes against big-box and e-commerce retailers and is in clear secular decline. Brigit enters a crowded EWA field against DailyPay and EarnIn, where the differentiation is the high-margin subscription bundle.

The dominant competitive variable is regulation. The LTO and EWA industries face coordinated, rising scrutiny: AGs pushing to recharacterize LTO as "credit" (triggering APR caps and disclosures) and EWA as "loans." The December 2025 CFPB pivot (withdrawing its proposed credit-treatment rule and issuing an advisory opinion that compliant EWA is not credit under TILA) is a meaningful tailwind for Brigit, but the opinion lacks force of law and states diverge (CT and MD treat EWA as loans; CA requires registration). Net, the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating, which supports a mid-range score.

## Risk Assessment

**Acima regulatory overhang — the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** A 43-state Multistate Attorneys' General investigation running since November 2021 into Acima's LTO practices is "nearing a non-binding agreement in principle" on monetary and injunctive terms. The FY25 $72M / Q1'26 $74.2M legal accrual incorporates the expected cash payment, so the capital impact is largely reserved, but a binding settlement is not assured, and any settlement would impose injunctive changes to Acima's business practices. A separate NY AG lawsuit (filed August 2024) remains in active litigation with a motion to dismiss pending. The DC AG matter settled in Q1'26; the McBurnie California class action settled for $14M (paid April 2026). Because the cash is reserved, a clean signed settlement is more likely to trigger relief than capital impairment, while a breakdown into 43 separate state lawsuits would be a negative surprise.

**Leverage and rates.** 3.71x D/E, ~$1.1B unhedged floating debt, non-IG rating, and a cross-default provision that a material unpaid judgment could trigger across the ABL, term loan, and notes. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the unhedged exposure is a recurring earnings headwind.

**Brigit-specific regulatory surface.** A permanent FTC injunction (November 2023 ROSCA settlement, $18M paid, up to 15 years of compliance obligations) and reliance on Coastal Community Bank for Credit Builder expose Brigit to "true lender"/Madden recharacterization risk. Near-term cash claims include a $60M earnout tied to 2026 Brigit performance and $75M of deferred consideration ($37.5M due ~February 2026).

**Operating signals to monitor.** Acima GMV -5.9% in Q1, Brigit net advance losses rising to 3.5% from 2.4%, and Rent-A-Center secular decline.

**Management transition.** Heavy C-suite turnover: CEO Fadel retired 6/1/25 (CFO Karam promoted to CEO), new CFO Khouri (Nov 2025), new CGO (Sept 2025), new CTO (March 2026), plus additional director/officer changes in May/June 2026. A largely new team is executing the fintech pivot, which is both opportunity and execution risk. The insider signal is modestly positive: Director Jeffrey Brown made an open-market purchase of 2,329 shares at $19.41 (~$45K) on 4/28/26, with no insider selling beyond routine tax withholding.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.2% is fair against 47.7% one-year historical vol (spread -0.5%), with IV in the 26th percentile of its own chain; the options market is not pricing acute near-term stress. Put/call skew is balanced (0.4%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.33 and OI ratio 0.46, and the term structure is in contango (near 47.2% < long 68.0%), a normal/complacent structure. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.1%. The reported 564.3% long-dated ATM IV in the options-delta snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($28, the analyst median)

The Multistate AG matter settles cleanly with the cash already reserved, removing the dominant overhang and triggering a relief re-rating. Q1'26's earnings inflection proves durable as Brigit's high-margin subscription model scales and legal/G&A costs normalize lower. Acima's Q1 GMV softness proves to be deliberate underwriting discipline rather than demand collapse, and continued debt paydown shrinks interest expense. On $4.76 next-year EPS, a re-rating toward 13-15x against a de-risked regulatory profile supports $28, with the 8.3% yield paid while waiting. A clean settlement plus an Acima GMV re-acceleration are the two unlocks.

### Bear Case ($14, ~24% downside)

Acima GMV deceleration proves structural as subprime consumers weaken under inflation and tariff pass-through, merchandise losses climb, and the growth engine stalls. The Multistate settlement breaks down into 43 state lawsuits or imposes injunctive practice changes that compress Acima's economics. Unhedged floating-rate debt keeps interest expense elevated, and Rent-A-Center's secular decline accelerates. A multiple compression to ~9-10x on flat-to-down earnings and dividend-coverage concern (even at 2.7x today, a cut becomes a discussion if FCF falls) takes the stock toward $14.

The bear case is substantially priced: the stock is already 35% off its high, the cash component of the dominant binary is reserved, and the business is deleveraging with positive earnings momentum. That balance makes this a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, late July/early August) is the key checkpoint, with the Multistate AG settlement an ongoing binary that could resolve at any time.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A signed, clean Multistate AG settlement that removes the regulatory overhang without material injunctive changes to Acima's economics; the cash is reserved, so resolution is largely a de-risking event.
- Acima GMV returning to positive YoY growth in Q2/Q3'26, confirming the Q1 decline was underwriting discipline rather than demand softening.
- Continued deleveraging that takes D/E below ~3.0x while maintaining the covered dividend, ideally alongside any hedging of the floating-rate exposure.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A breakdown of the Multistate negotiation into separate state litigation, or a settlement requiring injunctive practice changes that materially compress Acima margins.
- Acima GMV declines deepening beyond Q1's level alongside rising merchandise losses, signaling structural demand erosion.
- Brigit net advance losses climbing meaningfully above 3.5% or a regulatory recharacterization of EWA/bank-partner products.
- Any dividend coverage deterioration (FCF falling toward ~1.5x coverage) given the 8.3% yield is central to the thesis.

**Constructive entry view:** The stock is already attractively priced for the income case at $18.32 (8.3% covered yield, 12.1x forward). We would add with more conviction toward the high-$15s/low-$16s (near the 52-week low of $15.82), where the yield exceeds 9% and the regulatory tail is even more fully discounted. We would not chase above ~$22-23 ahead of settlement clarity.

## Conclusion

Upbound is a deleveraging, free-cash-flow-generative subprime-consumer-finance platform mid-pivot toward fintech. The FY25 earnings collapse that drove much of the drawdown was largely non-operational, with gross profit rising while legal accruals and Brigit deal charges hit GAAP net income, and Q1'26 confirmed the underlying business is healthier than the trailing figures suggest. Estimate revisions are rising, the sell-side is unanimously non-bearish with a low target above spot, and the dominant regulatory binary has its cash component reserved.

These facts undercut a "structural impairment" thesis. The genuine risks (3.71x leverage with ~$1.1B unhedged floating debt, an unsigned Multistate settlement that could impose injunctive changes, Acima GMV decline, and Rent-A-Center's secular decline) are sufficient to keep us off the buy button but not sufficient to steer investors away at any price when the bear case is so substantially discounted. We rate UPBD a HOLD, owned for the covered yield and a plausible settlement-relief re-rating, with the Q1 GMV trajectory and the Multistate outcome as the two variables that will decide whether the next move is to BUY or AVOID.
