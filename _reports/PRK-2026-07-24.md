**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Park National Corporation is a Newark, Ohio-headquartered bank holding company that closed the all-stock acquisition of First Citizens Bancshares on February 1, 2026, adding a Tennessee region and pushing consolidated assets from ~$9.8B to ~$13.0B, crossing the $10B regulatory threshold. The underlying franchise is high quality: a record FY2025 (net income $180.1M, ROAA 1.78%), a premium net interest margin that expanded to 4.80% in Q1'26, and a low-cost deposit base where the cost of interest-bearing deposits fell to 1.62% while absorbing $2.2B of acquired deposits. On an adjusted basis, Q1'26 diluted EPS rose ~19% to $3.06, adjusted ROTE was 16.2%, and the efficiency ratio was 55.6%.

The tension is between franchise quality and current price. PRK trades at ~$185, near its 52-week high of $192.77, at roughly 17x trailing and ~15x forward EPS. Full-year 2026 consensus has been revised up ~4.7% over 90 days to $12.40, but near-term momentum is deteriorating: the company missed EPS estimates in each of the last two quarters (-5.9% and -9.0%), current-quarter and next-year estimates have edged lower over the trailing 30 days, and nonperforming assets rose to $107.6M (1.11% of loans) from $70.0M (0.87%) at year-end. Analyst coverage is uniformly Hold with a mean target of ~$184, essentially at spot.

We rate PRK a HOLD. This is a well-run bank executing a sensibly-priced acquisition, but the appreciation case is capped by a full valuation against a stock already at highs, and the downside case is anchored by real, monitorable credit deterioration and the Durbin interchange headwind. The score trajectory (5.8 → 6.7 → 6.0) and three prior consecutive HOLDs argue for restraint: nothing in the current data warrants a stronger call in either direction. The next natural reassessment is the Q3 2026 core-systems conversion and the first clean, merger-cost-free quarter that follows.

## Company Overview

Park operates through its principal subsidiary, The Park National Bank, on a relationship-banking model organized around local market regions. The historical footprint is central and southern Ohio; the strategy over the past several years has been deliberate expansion into higher-growth, demographically attractive markets: the Carolinas (Carolina Alliance, NewDominion) and now Tennessee (First Citizens). The bank reports as a single segment.

Revenue rests on three streams. Net interest income dominates, driven by a diversified loan book weighted toward commercial and CRE (~61% of loans), residential real estate and mortgage, home equity, and a sizable consumer/installment book that includes indirect auto and a nonbank consumer-finance lending vertical. Fiduciary and wealth management generated $12.3M in Q1'26 (up ~7% on AUM growth), a stable, high-quality fee source. Deposit service charges, debit-card interchange, and mortgage banking round out noninterest income.

The competitive edge is the deposit franchise: 27.8% noninterest-bearing after the deal (32%+ pre-deal), supporting a net interest margin well above typical community-bank peers. The First Citizens acquisition ($324.1M, all-stock, 1.99M shares) added $2.6B of assets, $1.6B of loans, and $2.2B of deposits, and shapes the next 12-18 months of the story.

## Financial Analysis

A data caveat matters here. Standardized XBRL pulls returned parent-company-only figures (~$241M total assets), which are meaningless for a $13B bank holding company; Altman Z and Piotroski F scores do not apply to banks. The analysis below uses consolidated statements from the 10-Q and 10-K.

**Full-year trajectory.** Consolidated net income progressed $148.4M (2022) → $126.7M (2023) → $151.4M (2024) → $180.1M (2025, a record). ROAA improved to 1.78% in FY2025 from 1.53% and 1.27% in the prior two years, top-decile for a community bank. FY2025 net interest income reached $437.3M (from $398.0M), and provision fell to $11.5M from $14.5M.

**Q1 2026 — first quarter with First Citizens.** GAAP net income was $41.7M, down 1.1% YoY, depressed by $15.5M of merger-related expenses. Adjusted net income was $53.5M, adjusted diluted EPS $3.06 (vs. $2.57), adjusted ROAA 1.83%, adjusted ROTE 16.2%, adjusted efficiency ratio 55.6%. NIM expanded to 4.80% (4.75% ex-accretion) from 4.62%, and NII rose 20.5% to $125.8M. The cost of interest-bearing deposits fell to 1.62% from 1.76%, evidence of deposit-pricing discipline. Pro-forma for a full quarter of First Citizens, diluted EPS was roughly $3.09.

**Balance sheet at 3/31/26.** Total assets $12.98B (from $9.81B at YE'25), loans $9.67B, deposits $11.0B, loan/deposit ratio ~88%. Capital remains robust: total risk-based 14.71%, CET1 13.48%, leverage 12.36%, all well above "well-capitalized," despite the deal shaving CET1 from 13.99%. Tangible equity is ~$1.40B; goodwill/intangibles rose to $302.6M (goodwill $263.4M) post-deal.

**Capital allocation.** Park is a consistent dividend grower ($1.10/quarter, up from $1.07), yielding ~2.4%. The all-stock deal preserved cash, and Park redeemed $190M of subordinated debt in September 2025 ($175M 4.50% notes + $15M trust preferreds) using cash on hand, deleveraging into the acquisition and cutting borrowing costs (average borrowings fell 55% YoY). No buybacks in Q1'26; authorizations remain open. AFS securities carry a manageable ~$42M unrealized loss (down from ~$63M a year prior), and Park excludes AOCI from regulatory capital.

The earnings quality is genuine: spread income backed by a strong deposit franchise, not one-off gains. The credit picture is where the caution lies (see Risk Assessment).

## Growth Analysis

The 12-month growth thesis rests on three legs. First, the First Citizens deal is immediately accretive on an adjusted basis, with cost synergies still to come after the Q3 2026 systems conversion — the Tennessee region currently runs two core platforms simultaneously, inflating data-processing costs. When that duplication ends, the efficiency ratio should improve and synergies should flow through, providing a mechanical earnings tailwind into late 2026 and 2027.

Second, the geographic mix-shift toward faster-growing Carolinas and Tennessee markets should support above-Ohio organic loan and deposit growth over time. Third, wealth management (~7% AUM-driven fee growth) and a premium NIM give Park earnings quality that many community peers lack.

The counterweight is the near-term estimate trajectory. The full-year revision is positive, but the more recent 30-day trend is fractionally negative across every window (current quarter -0.8%, next quarter -0.1%, current year -0.2%, next year -0.6%). More telling, GAAP EPS missed consensus in each of the last two quarters, reversing a prior beat streak. Much of the recent miss is merger-cost noise, but the pattern signals that reported visibility has degraded during integration. The +4.7% 90-day full-year revision is well short of the +30% threshold that would flag a systematic re-rating, and it is not paired with accelerating organic revenue growth. This is a steady mid-single-digit compounder augmented by an accretive deal.

The Durbin interchange cap, triggered by crossing $10B, will pressure debit-card fee income (~$7.0M/quarter) in coming quarters and partially offset the synergy tailwind.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$185, PRK trades at ~17.0x trailing EPS ($11.11) and roughly 14.9x current-year consensus. For a well-capitalized bank generating ~1.8% adjusted ROAA and 16%+ adjusted ROTE, that multiple is fair-to-slightly-full rather than cheap. Price-to-tangible-book is elevated — tangible equity is ~$1.40B against a ~$3.4B market cap, roughly 2.4x TBV — a premium that reflects the quality of the franchise and NIM but leaves little margin of safety.

Analyst targets bracket the stock tightly: low $180, high $189, mean ~$184, median $184, with the current price essentially at the mean. On a genuinely cheap, high-momentum name where consensus targets lag reality, targets function as floors. That does not apply here: PRK is not at a compressed multiple relative to growth (90-day revisions are +4.7%, not +30%+), and the near-term revision trend is flat-to-negative. When a fairly-valued bank trades at the analyst mean with decelerating quarterly EPS, the target cluster reflects a genuine ceiling, not a stale lag.

The valuation dimension is scored low (4.8) because the current price already discounts the franchise quality and the deal accretion, offering roughly symmetric risk/reward. There is no identifiable catalyst that would force a re-rating higher within 12 months; the synergy realization is already partly in consensus. A trailing low-P/E screen would flag PRK as "cheap" against the broad market, but for a bank at this quality and this point in the cycle, the multiple is close to full value. A more attractive entry would require a pullback toward the low-to-mid $160s (roughly book-plus-premium normalized), where the deal accretion and NIM premium would offer clearer upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Park's competitive position is stable-to-improving. It competes against larger Midwest regionals (Huntington, Fifth Third) at the top end and community peers (Peoples Bancorp, First Financial, WesBanco) in its core, and now against Tennessee community banks. Its structural differentiator is the deposit franchise and the resulting premium NIM (~4.8%), which sits well above typical community-bank levels and reflects disciplined pricing and sticky core relationships. Lowering interest-bearing deposit costs while integrating the acquired book is direct evidence the franchise is not deteriorating under scale.

The First Citizens deal extends the multi-year strategy of migrating from slower-growth central Ohio into demographically attractive Southeastern markets. Executed at a conservative price ($324M for $220M of net identifiable assets, generating $104M of goodwill), it improves the medium-term growth mix. The bank has also engineered itself to near-interest-rate-neutrality (±2.7%/-2.9% net income sensitivity to ±200bps), reducing rate-cycle earnings volatility relative to more asset-sensitive peers.

The competitive-position score of 6.5 reflects a durable, high-quality franchise with an improving geographic footprint, tempered by the reality that community banking is a share battle in commoditized markets and that the acquired Tennessee credit administration is unfamiliar territory. The trajectory improvement noted in prior reports (6.0 → 6.8) is confirmed by the deal's clean close and the deposit-cost discipline, though we mark it slightly below the prior 6.8 given rising NPAs.

## Risk Assessment

The financial data (Financials sector) carries a negative-alpha caution by default, and Park's specific risks are concentrated and monitorable rather than sector-generic:

1. **Rising nonperforming assets.** NPAs jumped to $107.6M (1.11% of loans) from $70.0M (0.87%) at YE'25. OREO rose $23.7M (mostly acquired First Citizens residential), but nonaccruals also rose to $80.5M with legacy commercial and mortgage deterioration cited by management. This is the single most important thread to watch.

2. **Acquired-credit uncertainty.** Park recorded a $15.6M Day-1 ACL plus a $3.2M additional qualitative overlay because First Citizens' loans reserved materially lower than Park's legacy book — an implicit acknowledgment that the acquired portfolio may be riskier than headline due diligence suggested, in markets where Park's credit administration is unfamiliar.

3. **Integration and $10B threshold.** Core-systems conversion is scheduled for Q3 2026; until then, dual-platform costs inflate expenses. Crossing $10B triggers the Durbin interchange cap and heavier CFPB/stress-testing obligations, a step-change in compliance cost.

4. **Idiosyncratic credit pockets.** $268.8M lent to nonbank consumer-finance companies (a sector under stress; being wound down, $20.4M watch-list, $1.4M nonaccrual) and $238M of special-purpose high-LTV (≥90%) mortgage programs with elevated delinquency.

5. **CRE concentration** ($3.0B) amid management-flagged sector stress, and uninsured deposits of ~$2.4B (21.6% of total, partly collateralized at $738M).

Management's own ACL scenario weighting assigns 50% to a moderate recession and forecasts Ohio unemployment near 5%, a cautious posture that partly pre-provisions for the macro tail.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that these risks are real and specific but largely surfaced and partly reserved, with a fortress capital base (CET1 13.48%) providing a substantial buffer. The prior improvement in this dimension (4.5 → 5.3) is essentially held, with a slight mark-down for the NPA increase.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 27.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 25.0% — fair pricing, with a modest 2.9-point premium. The put/call skew is balanced (-2.4%), the put/call OI ratio is low (0.12), and IV has fallen sharply since early July (35.2% → 27.8%), suggesting event uncertainty has resolved without leaving elevated hedging demand. The term structure is in mild backwardation (27.8% 1m vs. 22.5% 6m), pointing to some near-term event pricing, but the implied 1-month move of ±6.4% is unremarkable. Short interest is low at 4.5% of float (5.7 days to cover). Nothing in the options or short-interest data contradicts the fundamental HOLD; positioning is broadly neutral.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($205-215).** The Q3 2026 systems conversion removes duplicate-platform costs, cost synergies flow through, and the adjusted efficiency ratio compresses below 55%. NIM holds near 4.8% as deposit-cost discipline persists, NPAs prove to be a one-time acquired-OREO bump rather than a trend, and the Tennessee/Carolinas mix delivers above-Ohio organic growth. Full-year 2026 EPS beats consensus toward $12.75+, and the market re-rates a de-risked, post-integration bank toward 17x forward, implying the high-$200s to low-$200s. Roughly +11% to +16% from current.

**Bear Case ($150-160).** Acquired First Citizens credit deteriorates faster than the Day-1 reserves anticipated, NPAs climb past 1.5% of loans, provisions rise, and the qualitative overlay proves insufficient. CRE stress and the nonbank consumer-finance book add pressure. The Durbin interchange cap and dual-platform costs compress the efficiency ratio, integration proves messier than guided, and quarterly misses continue. The multiple compresses toward 13x forward on ~$12.00 realized EPS. Roughly -14% to -19% from current, near the 52-week low.

**Base Case ($188-195).** Integration proceeds on schedule, synergies partially offset the Durbin drag, NIM stays premium, and NPAs stabilize around 1.0-1.2% without a provision spike. EPS lands near consensus. The stock grinds modestly higher on dividend growth plus mid-single-digit EPS growth, but the full starting valuation caps re-rating. Roughly +2% to +5% from current plus a ~2.4% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The franchise quality supports ownership, but the full valuation at a 52-week high, decelerating quarterly EPS, and rising NPAs leave insufficient evidence for a confident directional call in either direction over the next 12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $160s (roughly 13x forward), which would restore an asymmetric entry against the deal accretion and NIM premium.
- Q3 2026 systems conversion completes on schedule and the first clean post-merger quarter shows adjusted efficiency ratio below 55% with NPAs stabilizing/declining.
- A clear positive turn in 30-day estimate revisions (returning to positive across current-quarter and next-year windows) combined with a return to EPS beats.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NPAs rise above ~1.5% of loans, or nonaccruals accelerate, forcing a provision spike above the Day-1 reserve run-rate.
- Evidence that acquired First Citizens credit is materially worse than reserved (further qualitative overlays or measurement-period adjustments through Feb 2027 that increase goodwill/ACL).
- Efficiency ratio deteriorates post-conversion rather than improving, signaling synergies are not materializing.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release, and specifically the first post-Q3-2026 conversion quarter (Q4'26 report in early 2027), which will be the first clean read on synergies, credit trend, and the Durbin interchange step-down.

## Conclusion

Park National is a well-managed community bank with a premium net interest margin, a differentiated low-cost deposit franchise, fortress capital (CET1 13.48%), and a conservatively-priced, adjusted-accretive acquisition that extends its footprint into faster-growing Southeastern markets. The management transition (Miller/Trautman) looks orderly, compensation is aligned to the right banking metrics, and the 5-year TSR (170.9) beat the peer group (156.0).

The problem is price and timing. At ~$185, near a 52-week high, at ~15x forward on flat-to-slightly-negative recent revisions and two consecutive quarterly EPS misses, the stock is fairly valued with roughly symmetric risk/reward. Rising NPAs (1.11%), acquired-credit uncertainty, and the Durbin interchange headwind provide concrete downside anchors that offset the synergy upside. Analyst coverage is uniformly Hold at a mean target essentially equal to spot. With three prior consecutive HOLDs and a fair-to-full valuation, the bar for a stronger call is not met. We maintain HOLD, own the ~2.4% dividend and modest compounding for existing holders, and would look to accumulate on a pullback toward the low-$160s or on confirmation that post-conversion synergies and stabilizing credit justify a re-rating.
