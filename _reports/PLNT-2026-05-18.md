## Executive Summary

Planet Fitness has experienced a 53% decline from its 52-week high of $114.47 to the current price of $53.76, with most of the damage concentrated in two distinct episodes: a gradual decline from October 2025 through April 2026 driven by macro headwinds and GLP-1 concerns, followed by a single-day 31% crash on May 7, 2026 when Q1 earnings revealed disappointing January enrollment, full-year guidance was cut to $3.19 EPS (vs. $3.37 consensus), and Black Card price increases were cancelled.

The fundamental question is whether this is a cyclical trough in membership growth driven by macro softness (negative real wages, gas >$4.50, consumer confidence collapse) or a structural impairment of the addressable market from GLP-1 weight-loss drugs and intensifying low-cost competition. At 16.9x guided FY2026 earnings, the valuation discount to historical ranges (25-40x) is substantial, and insider buying from the CEO and a board member post-crash signals internal confidence. However, estimates are falling rapidly (-15% on next-year EPS over 90 days), the company carries $2.5B in securitized debt with rising interest costs, and the cancelled price increase suggests management itself acknowledges demand elasticity is higher than previously assumed.

The risk/reward is roughly balanced at current levels. The stock could re-rate significantly if membership growth stabilizes and estimates trough, but could also face further downside if Q2 enrollment trends confirm structural demand impairment rather than seasonal weakness.

## Company Overview

Planet Fitness operates 2,909 fitness centers globally (2,617 franchisee-owned, 292 corporate-owned) serving approximately 21.5 million members. The business model combines capital-light franchise economics (6.7% average royalty on franchisee billings) with corporate-owned club operations and equipment sales to franchisees. Revenue breaks down as: Franchise (40%), Corporate-owned clubs (42%), Equipment (18%).

The core value proposition is accessibility: $15/month Classic Card membership targeting the 80% of Americans without gym memberships, branded as a "Judgement Free Zone." The Black Card tier ($24.99/month with premium perks) has reached 66.5% penetration, up from 63.9% in FY2024. The company has contractual commitments for approximately 750 new club openings in its pipeline.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory** has been strong but is decelerating:
- FY2023: $1,071M (+14.3% YoY)
- FY2024: $1,182M (+10.3%)
- FY2025: $1,324M (+12.1%)
- Q1 2026: $337M (+21.9% YoY, heavily inflated by equipment timing — $62M vs. $28M prior year)

**Same-club sales growth decelerated from 6.7% in FY2025 to 3.5% in Q1 2026.** This is the critical metric and the primary driver of the crash. Management attributed weakness to softer-than-expected January enrollment — historically the strongest period for new signups.

**Profitability remains excellent:**
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 41.5% (Q1 2026), 41.7% (FY2025)
- Operating margin: 29.3% (Q1 2026), 29.8% (FY2025)
- FCF: $255M (FY2025), representing a 19.2% margin
- Operating cash flow: $148M in Q1 2026 alone

**Balance sheet carries significant leverage:**
- Total debt: ~$2.51B in securitized senior notes
- Stockholders' equity: negative $483M (buyback-driven)
- Cash + marketable securities: $572M
- Net debt/EBITDA: ~3.5x ($1,938M net debt / $552M EBITDA)
- Interest expense trending from $100M (FY2024) to $108M (FY2025) to a $132M annualized run rate in Q1 2026
- Debt maturities: $923M due 2029, $397M due 2030, $1.1B thereafter

The Z-Score of 0.98 is mechanically driven by negative equity from buybacks rather than operational distress. With $400M+ annual operating cash flow and $572M in liquidity, near-term solvency is not a concern. However, the rising interest burden (rates range 3.86% to 6.24%) is a meaningful margin headwind, and refinancing $923M in 2029 at current rates (30Y Treasury >5.1%) would further compress earnings.

**Capital allocation has been aggressively shareholder-oriented but poorly timed:**
- $500M repurchased in FY2025, including $350M ASR at $108.76 VWAP
- $50M repurchased in Q1 2026 at ~$81/share average
- Share count: 85.8M → 83.7M → 79.8M (Q1 2026)
- The December/January ASR represents a ~$175M paper loss at current prices

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects have deteriorated meaningfully since mid-2025:

**Positive factors:**
- 750-club pipeline provides a multi-year runway for unit growth
- Black Card penetration (66.5%) still has upside — each upgrade adds ~$10/month per member
- International expansion into Spain, Mexico, Australia (though all currently loss-making)
- Equipment replacement cycle every 5-9 years creates recurring revenue with existing base

**Negative factors:**
- Same-store sales growth decelerated from 6.7% to 3.5% in one quarter
- January 2026 enrollment disappointed during the single most important month for gym signups
- Black Card price increase cancelled, removing a straightforward growth lever
- Estimate revisions are uniformly negative: current-year EPS estimates fell 9.7% over 90 days; next-year EPS fell 15.2%
- Macro headwinds (negative real wages, elevated gas prices) directly compress discretionary consumer spending on gym memberships

**GLP-1 structural risk:** The company itself disclosed this risk in its 10-K. Oral GLP-1s (first FDA-approved in 2026) lower adoption barriers relative to injections. If even 5-10% of potential members perceive medication as substituting for gym-based weight management, that reduces the addressable market by millions of potential signups. This is not quantifiable yet but represents genuine uncertainty about the long-term growth ceiling.

## Valuation Assessment

At $53.76 with guided FY2026 EPS of $3.19, PLNT trades at 16.9x forward earnings. Against next-year estimates of $3.60, it trades at 14.9x. For context:

- Historical PLNT trading range: 25-40x forward earnings
- Current peer (OSW): 31.3x
- PRKS: 12.5x (more distressed)
- FUN: negative earnings

The compression from historical multiples to 17x reflects two things: (1) lower growth expectations (mid-single digits vs. prior double-digit EBITDA growth), and (2) a structural risk premium for GLP-1 demand destruction. If the slowdown is purely cyclical and membership growth recovers to 5%+ same-store sales, re-rating toward 22-25x ($70-80 on current-year estimates) is plausible. If growth continues decelerating toward 0-2% same-store and the GLP-1 thesis gains evidence, 12-14x ($38-45) is the downside.

The median analyst target of $61.00 implies 13% upside. The mean target of $69.50 implies 29% upside. However, these targets are stale — most were set pre-crash or in immediate reaction to it, and further estimate cuts are likely.

**FCF yield** at current market cap ($4.25B) is approximately 6.0% based on FY2025's $255M FCF. If FY2026 FCF compresses proportionally with earnings guidance ($3.19 vs. $3.07 achieved in FY2025 — actually up 4%), FCF yield remains in the 5.5-6.5% range. This provides meaningful downside support for a business with 40%+ EBITDA margins.

## Competitive Landscape

Planet Fitness holds a dominant position in U.S. low-cost fitness: 20.8M+ members across 2,900+ locations, far exceeding any single competitor. The brand recognition ("Judgement Free Zone"), standardized club format, and franchise economics create meaningful barriers to displacement.

However, competitive intensity is increasing:
- **Crunch Fitness**, EOS Fitness, and YouFit are expanding aggressively in the same price tier
- The NAF contribution increase to 3% (from 2%) signals management's recognition that more advertising spend is needed to maintain competitive positioning
- Digital fitness has matured but coexists with physical gyms rather than displacing them

The cancelled price increase is the most concerning competitive signal. Planet Fitness historically demonstrated pricing power — the ability to raise Black Card prices with minimal churn. Pulling this lever suggests management perceives that competitive alternatives or member price sensitivity has increased. Whether this reflects temporary macro headwinds (consumers more price-sensitive with negative real wages) or permanent competitive dynamics is the central analytical question.

**Franchisee health** shows emerging stress: $26.6M in promissory notes to franchisees is unusual for a healthy system and suggests some operators are experiencing cash flow challenges. If franchisee profitability deteriorates, new club openings could slow below contracted commitments.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Structural demand impairment from GLP-1 drugs (HIGH — unquantifiable).** Oral GLP-1s launched in 2026 remove the injection barrier. If weight-loss motivation accounts for 20-30% of gym signups and GLP-1 adoption captures even a portion of those, the long-term membership growth ceiling is permanently lower. No data yet confirms or denies this — it is pure uncertainty premium.

**2. Continued estimate deterioration (HIGH — active).** Estimates are falling 10-15% over 90 days across all periods. The May 7 guidance cut already incorporated January weakness, but if Q2 enrollment remains soft, further cuts are likely. Management's credibility is damaged by the December $350M buyback at $108 followed by a guidance cut four months later.

**3. Leverage in a rising-rate environment (MODERATE).** 3.5x net debt/EBITDA is manageable at current cash flow levels but creates refinancing risk. The $923M due in 2029 will need to be addressed in 2028 at potentially much higher rates. Securitization covenants tied to system-wide sales levels introduce acceleration risk if membership growth deteriorates further.

**4. Executive turnover (MODERATE).** CFO departure during Q1 2026 following CEO transition in 2024 suggests organizational instability. Two C-suite changes within 18 months during a growth deceleration period is concerning.

**5. Consumer spending deterioration (HIGH — macro-driven).** With real wages negative, gas >$4.50, and consumer confidence collapsing, discretionary gym memberships are vulnerable. Planet Fitness's $15/month price point provides relative insulation, but the cancelled price increase suggests even this low price point is not immune to consumer stress.

**6. Tariff exposure on equipment segment (MODERATE).** Equipment sourced from Canada, China, Mexico, and Germany faces tariff risk. Equipment generated $310M in FY2025 revenue with margins that could compress on cost pass-through challenges.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($72-85, 34-58% upside)
- The membership growth slowdown is primarily cyclical, driven by macro headwinds (negative real wages, elevated gas prices, consumer uncertainty) that suppress discretionary spending
- January 2026 enrollment weakness was an anomaly, and Black Friday / New Year 2027 campaigns restore normal signup patterns
- GLP-1 adoption has minimal net effect on gym memberships (users may exercise more, not less, to maintain weight loss; many GLP-1 users are not the same demographic as gym joiners)
- 750-club pipeline executes on schedule, driving 5-7% unit growth per year
- Stock re-rates from ~17x to 22-25x forward as growth visibility returns
- Insider buying (CEO at $49.54) proves prescient; management accelerates buybacks at current levels with $450M remaining authorization

### Bear Case ($37-45, 16-31% downside)
- GLP-1 adoption accelerates, permanently reducing the addressable market for fitness centers by 10-15%
- Same-store sales growth decelerates further to 0-2% as macro recession takes hold
- Franchisee stress intensifies — promissory notes are early signals of broader system weakness
- Refinancing $923M in 2029 at rates 200-400bp above current cost permanently compresses EPS
- Competition from Crunch, EOS, and others erodes pricing power further
- Stock de-rates to 12-14x forward (peer PRKS level) reflecting low-growth, leveraged, secularly challenged positioning

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 same-store sales growth rebounds to ≥5% (demonstrating Q1 was anomalous rather than structural)
2. Net member additions in April-June 2026 exceed year-ago levels (verifiable from Q2 reporting)
3. Estimate revisions stabilize or reverse (next-year EPS stops falling, requiring analyst estimates to hold above $3.50 for 30+ days)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 same-store sales growth <2% (confirming structural deceleration)
2. Any franchisee defaults on promissory notes or securitization covenant breach
3. Further guidance cut below $3.00 FY2026 EPS

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026, covering Q2 which captures the critical spring enrollment period). This is the decisive data point for determining whether the membership growth impairment is cyclical or structural.

## Conclusion

Planet Fitness is a strong franchise business trading at a historically cheap multiple (17x guided earnings) following a legitimate growth scare. The 53% drawdown has created a meaningful valuation gap if the slowdown proves cyclical, supported by 6% FCF yield, insider buying, and a dominant competitive position. However, GLP-1 demand destruction is a real structural risk without available data to quantify or dismiss. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative. The macro environment (negative real wages, PPI 6%, elevated gas prices) directly pressures the low-income consumer who represents Planet Fitness's core member.

Valuation support is real, but growth uncertainty prevents conviction. The Q2 earnings report covering spring enrollment will be dispositive. Until that data point, HOLD is the appropriate posture. The stock is cheap enough that aggressive downside from here requires further fundamental deterioration, but not cheap enough relative to the structural uncertainty to justify a BUY with high confidence.
