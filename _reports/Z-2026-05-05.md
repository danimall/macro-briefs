## Executive Summary

Zillow Group has declined 53% from its September 2025 high of $93.88 to $43.68, driven primarily by the FTC antitrust lawsuit filed in September 2025, deteriorating housing market sentiment, and a Q4 2025 earnings miss. The stock now trades at approximately 13x trailing adjusted EBITDA ($622M) on a market cap of ~$7.9B — a reasonable multiple for a platform business growing 16% YoY, but one that still embeds considerable uncertainty.

Zillow's integrated transaction platform strategy is producing results (For Sale revenue per TTV +8%, Enhanced Markets >40% of connections, Rentals +39%, Mortgages +37%), but the FTC lawsuit directly targets the rentals syndication deal that powered 45% of Q4 Rentals growth. If the deal is unwound, Zillow loses both its fastest growth vector and the $87M/year cost structure built around it. Combined with the current housing affordability crisis, this creates a balanced but deeply uncertain setup.

The analyst consensus (median target $73.50, range $55-$85) implies 26-95% upside, but 5 of 7 analysts rate the stock HOLD — suggesting the Street sees value but lacks conviction on timing. Given the binary litigation risk, the fragile path to sustained profitability, and the macro headwinds facing residential real estate from elevated rates, the appropriate posture is patience.

## Company Overview

Zillow Group operates the most-visited real estate platform in the United States with ~235 million average monthly unique users and 9.6 billion visits annually. The company has evolved from a pure advertising/lead-generation platform into an integrated transaction ecosystem spanning search, touring, financing, and closing.

**Revenue Segments (FY2025, $2.58B total):**
- Residential: $1.7B (66%) — Premier Agent advertising, SaaS tools, Zillow Showcase
- Rentals: $630M (24%) — Multifamily advertising, rental applications, syndication
- Mortgages: $199M (8%) — Zillow Home Loans originations, mortgage advertising
- Other: $50M (2%) — Display advertising

The company's strategic moat rests on three pillars: (1) a living database of ~173 million U.S. homes with proprietary Zestimate valuations (1.8% median error), (2) brand dominance ("Zillow" is searched more than "real estate" on Google), and (3) network effects where 80% of U.S. transactions involve agents using at least one Zillow product. Total addressable market is estimated at $202 billion.

## Growth Analysis

Zillow is growing meaningfully faster than the underlying housing market (16% vs. 3% market TTV growth), indicating genuine share gains and improved monetization.

**Segment Growth (FY2025):**
- Rentals: +39% ($630M) — driven by multifamily listings +44%, now 2.4M monthly active listings
- Mortgages: +37% ($199M) — origination volume +52% to $4.8B
- Residential: +7% ($1.7B) — For Sale revenue per TTV improved from 7.9 to 8.5 bps

The "Enhanced Markets" strategy is the growth engine: over 40% of connections now flow through integrated experiences where Zillow coordinates the agent, tour, and mortgage in a single workflow. Zillow Home Loans achieves "double-digit customer adoption rates" in Enhanced Markets — if this scales, unit economics improve substantially as the company captures both the advertising fee and the mortgage margin.

**Growth Headwinds:**
1. Housing transaction volumes remain depressed. With elevated mortgage rates and the "lock-in effect" suppressing existing home inventory, total market activity is unlikely to accelerate in H2 2026.
2. Estimate revisions are negative over 90 days: current quarter EPS revised -10.4%, next quarter -7.2%. While 30-day revisions have stabilized (+0.3-0.4%), the direction has been down.
3. Earnings track record is weak: 1 beat, 1 in-line, 2 misses over the last 4 quarters. This reduces confidence in forward estimates.

**Current Year EPS Estimate:** $2.22 (7 analysts). Next year: $2.98. At $43.68, this implies ~20x CY EPS or ~15x NY EPS on consensus.

The valuation is neither cheap enough to compensate for the litigation binary risk nor expensive enough to warrant avoidance. The stock needs a specific positive catalyst (FTC resolution, housing recovery, margin expansion proof) to re-rate.

## Competitive Landscape

Zillow's competitive position is improving in absolute terms but faces intensifying competition:

**Strengths:**
- Brand and traffic leadership are durable: 235M monthly uniques, 9.6B annual visits
- The company outgrew the housing market by 13 percentage points in 2025
- 80% agent engagement rate creates switching costs
- Rentals market has no MLS equivalent — winner-take-most dynamics favor the scale leader

**Competitive Threats:**
- CoStar/Homes.com is investing ~$1B/year in marketing to build a competing platform. CoStar also filed a copyright suit (amended March 2026) against Zillow.
- AI-native search tools could disintermediate traditional real estate portals over time (Zillow's own 10-K acknowledges this risk)
- Compass resolved its antitrust suit but remains the largest residential brokerage competitor

**Regulatory Landscape:**
- NAR Settlement (March 2024) changed commission structures and could compress agent marketing budgets over time
- DOJ continues investigating NAR practices
- FTC actively challenging Zillow's rental market position

The competitive trajectory is net positive — Zillow is gaining share across all segments — but the risk of regulatory-forced divestitures in Rentals is the key concern. If the FTC prevails, Zillow's fastest growth vector gets disrupted at a formative stage.

## Risk Assessment

**1. FTC Antitrust Lawsuit (Binary, High Impact):**
Filed September 2025 by the FTC and 5 state AGs, alleging the $100M Redfin deal was anticompetitive in multifamily rentals advertising. Discovery is underway; motion to dismiss was filed January 2026. If Zillow prevails, the stock re-rates immediately. If it loses, remedies could include: unwinding the Redfin deal (loss of ~$87M in costs but also the traffic driving 39% Rentals growth), potential fines, and behavioral restrictions on future partnerships. This single risk justifies the entire stock discount.

**2. Housing Market Macro:**
Elevated mortgage rates represent a sustained headwind. With the "lock-in effect" suppressing existing home inventory, there is limited visibility on when transaction volumes will meaningfully recover. Existing home sales remain near multi-decade lows.

**3. Profitability Fragility:**
$23M GAAP net income on $2.58B revenue is a 0.9% margin. A single bad quarter in mortgages or a legal settlement could push the company back to losses. SBC of $390M dwarfs net income and produces ongoing dilution despite aggressive buybacks.

**4. Founder Control / No Voting Rights:**
Class C shares (ticker Z) carry zero voting rights. Barton and Frink control ~57.7% of votes. Investors have no governance recourse if management makes poor decisions (as demonstrated by the iBuying debacle).

**5. AI Disruption:**
The 10-K explicitly flags AI-based search and recommendation tools as a potential traffic disintermediation risk. While Zillow is investing in AI internally, its value proposition could be compressed if consumers shift to AI assistants for home search.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($70-85, +60-95%)
- FTC lawsuit dismissed or settled with minimal behavioral remedies (no deal unwind)
- Housing market stabilizes as rates normalize below 7% in 2027
- Enhanced Markets strategy scales to >60% of connections, driving mortgage attach rate improvement
- Adjusted EBITDA margins expand toward 28-30% as operating leverage compounds
- $1.8B NOL shield begins generating meaningful tax savings
- Buybacks more effective as SBC moderates (management guided lower SBC in 2026)

### Bear Case ($25-35, -20-43%)
- FTC prevails; Redfin deal unwound, Rentals growth collapses from 39% to single digits
- Housing recession as mortgage rates persist above 7% and inflation prevents Fed cuts
- CoStar's $1B/year marketing spend gains traction, eroding Zillow's traffic advantage
- AI-native search tools reduce organic traffic to traditional real estate portals
- SBC dilution accelerates; GAAP profitability proves unsustainable

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. FTC motion to dismiss granted, or settlement terms announced that preserve the Redfin deal structure
2. Stock reaches $35-38 (implying <9x adjusted EBITDA), where downside protection from the NOL shield and cash balance creates a margin of safety
3. Two consecutive quarters of EPS beats demonstrating operational execution through the macro headwind

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FTC ruling forces unwinding of Redfin deal or imposes behavioral remedies that structurally limit Rentals monetization
2. Housing transaction volumes decline >10% YoY for two consecutive quarters
3. Adjusted EBITDA margin contracts below 20% as competitive spending ramps

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (likely early May 2026 for Q1, or early August for Q2), FTC procedural rulings, and housing data through the spring selling season.

## Conclusion

Zillow's transformation from advertising platform to integrated transaction ecosystem is producing tangible results: 16% revenue growth in a flat market, improving monetization per transaction, and first-time GAAP profitability. The valuation at ~13x adjusted EBITDA is below typical platform multiples, reflecting legitimate concerns about the FTC lawsuit, housing macro headwinds, and dilution from stock-based compensation.

The investment case is genuinely binary around the FTC outcome. If the suit resolves favorably, the stock has 60-95% upside to analyst targets. If it doesn't, the fastest growth segment gets disrupted at a critical scaling stage, and the stock could decline to $25-35. With housing transactions depressed and the litigation timeline unclear, there is insufficient visibility to take a directional position. HOLD with a lean toward accumulation if FTC risk resolves or the stock reaches the $35-38 range.
