**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Xenia Hotels & Resorts is a self-advised lodging REIT owning 30 luxury and upper-upscale hotels (8,868 rooms across 14 states), operated under Marriott, Hyatt, Kimpton, Fairmont, Loews, and Hilton brands. The company is in the middle of an operating acceleration. Q1 2026 revenue reached $295.4M (+2.2% YoY despite the Fairmont Dallas divestiture removing ~$13.5M), with RevPAR up 9.1% to $205.93 against an industry rate of +3.8%, Hotel EBITDA margin expanding 230bps to 29.7%, and Adjusted FFO rising 16.3% to $60.6M. Management raised 2026 guidance to a RevPAR range of +2.75% to +5.25% and an Adjusted EBITDAre midpoint of ~$266M. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up sharply — current-year +56.1% over 90 days, next-year +29.4% — confirming that the operating inflection is real and the Street has been catching up to it.

The thesis is complicated by price action. The stock has appreciated 63% over the trailing year to $20.02, sitting essentially at its 52-week high of $20.20 and at the top of the analyst target band ($17-21, mean $19.20). The fundamental engine — the Grand Hyatt Scottsdale post-renovation ramp, a favorable supply/demand backdrop (industry supply growth of only +0.6%), and disciplined capital allocation — supports the operating story. But two internal signals warrant caution: a cluster of C-suite open-market sales at $15-17 in early 2026, below the current price, and management's own decision to halt buybacks after repurchasing roughly 9% of the float at ~$13 in 2025. The combination of insider selling and a paused buyback implies intrinsic value closer to the mid-teens than to $20.

We rate XHR a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The 12-month case for continued ownership rests on the Scottsdale ramp delivering through 2026, raised guidance proving conservative (the company has beaten three of its last four quarters, including by 81.8% in Q2 2025), and the supply-constrained luxury segment sustaining pricing power. We do not see asymmetric upside at $20 to justify a BUY: the stock now trades at the top of its target range, insiders are net sellers, and lodging carries late-cycle cyclical risk that the market appears to be pricing through XHR's persistent peer-relative discount.

## Company Overview

Xenia was spun off from InvenTrust Properties in February 2015 and operates a focused luxury/upper-upscale lodging strategy in the top-25 U.S. markets and key leisure destinations. As a REIT, it cannot operate hotels directly; it leases properties to its taxable REIT subsidiary (XHR Holding), which engages third-party managers. This structure means Xenia depends on operators like Marriott and Hyatt for day-to-day performance and retains only limited approval rights over budgets and capex. Twenty-three of its 30 hotels run under Marriott and Hyatt brands.

The company's stated differentiator is an in-house asset-management and project-management team that executes renovations and repositionings at lower cost than passive ownership would allow. The flagship example is the Grand Hyatt Scottsdale Resort redevelopment, which the filings cite repeatedly as the single largest driver of year-over-year growth in rooms, F&B, and ancillary revenue. The corporate footprint is small: 42 corporate employees, with all hotel staff employed by third-party managers.

Geographic concentration is meaningful: California (~22% of rooms), Texas (~18%), Florida (~13%), with Orlando, Phoenix, and Houston each exceeding 10% of total revenue. This concentration exposes the portfolio to regional shocks: hurricanes in Florida and Texas, wildfires in California, and sector-specific softness such as energy-driven cyclicality in Houston.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 was the clearest data point of operating acceleration. RevPAR rose 9.1% to $205.93 (occupancy +210bps to 71.4%, ADR +6.0% to $288.62), more than double the industry's +3.8%. The outperformance is property-specific: the Grand Hyatt Scottsdale ramp plus a normalizing leisure/business-transient/group demand mix. Segment Hotel EBITDA margin expanded to 29.7% from 27.4%, demonstrating operating leverage; net income rose 28.5% to $21.2M; Adjusted EBITDAre rose 11.6% to $81.4M; and Adjusted FFO rose 16.3% to $60.6M.

On the cleaner multi-year measure, Adjusted FFO per diluted share has climbed steadily: $1.54 (2022) → $1.54 (2023) → $1.59 (2024) → $1.76 (2025). Reported net income is more volatile ($57.6M → $19.9M → $16.9M → $66.9M), with the 2025 figure aided by a $40M gain on the Fairmont Dallas sale. The AFFO/share progression is the more reliable signal of underlying operating progress.

The balance sheet is moderate for a leveraged, asset-heavy REIT. Total debt is ~$1.38B at a 5.53% weighted-average rate, against ~$101M cash plus $78M restricted cash. The debt stack comprises $500M of 4.875% notes (2029), $400M of 6.625% notes (2030), $325M of term loans (~5.46%), and modest remaining mortgages. The maturity ladder is staggered: only ~$102M due 2027, $371M (term loans) due 2028, then the bond walls in 2029/2030. The $500M revolver is fully undrawn, providing strong liquidity. Variable-rate exposure is low (~26%, largely hedged); a 1% rate move equates to ~$3.3M of annual interest. The company is in compliance with all covenants. The Altman Z-Score near 1.4 nominally flags distress, but this is a structural artifact of the leveraged REIT model and is not corroborated by the undrawn revolver, staggered maturities, or positive FFO.

Capital allocation is the standout. Share count fell from ~101M (end-2024) to ~92M by Q1 2026, including 2.73M shares repurchased in Q1 2025 at $13.09. About $97.5M remains authorized, but no shares were bought in Q1 2026 after the stock rallied to ~$20. Management simultaneously deleveraged — paying off the $51.8M Grand Bohemian Orlando mortgage (Feb 2026), partially repaying Andaz Napa, and buying out the Hyatt Regency Santa Clara ground lease for $25.4M to eliminate ground rent. The quarterly dividend of $0.14 (~$0.56 annualized, ~2.9% yield) is modest and well-covered by AFFO.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is concrete and identifiable rather than generic. The Grand Hyatt Scottsdale ramp is a property-specific catalyst that a standardized screen would miss; it has driven the company's RevPAR outperformance and is expected to continue through 2026. The company's recent beat history (Q1 2026 +15.5%, Q4 2025 +131.0%, Q2 2025 +81.8%, Q3 2025 in-line) suggests guidance may prove conservative.

The estimate-revision picture is strongly positive. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised from $0.28 to $0.45 over 90 days, next-year from $0.42 to $0.55, and the current quarter +25.0% over 30 days. This magnitude of upward revision typically signals that consensus is still catching up to a fundamental inflection, and it is the most reliable forward indicator in the data. For a lodging REIT, however, the revisions are working off a low EPS base (GAAP EPS is depressed by depreciation), so the dollar moves are small even when the percentages are large — a reason to weight the AFFO trajectory more than the headline EPS revision.

The industry backdrop is favorable. Q1 2026 industry RevPAR grew +3.8% on demand of +2.0% against supply growth of only +0.6%. High construction costs and elevated rates have suppressed new supply in the upper-tier urban and resort segment, supporting pricing power. The countervailing factor is macro: the broader regime shows inflationary and consumer-demand uncertainty that would hit luxury/upper-upscale lodging — a high-fixed-cost, cyclically sensitive segment — harder than lower-tier hotels. The growth story is real today; its durability depends on the consumer holding up through 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

The reported P/E of 28.6x is misleading for a REIT, where depreciation suppresses GAAP earnings. The more relevant metric is price to AFFO: at $20.02 against FY2025 AFFO/share of $1.76, the stock trades at ~11.4x trailing AFFO. On forward AFFO, with guidance pointing to mid-single-digit RevPAR growth and the Scottsdale ramp, the multiple is roughly 10-11x — not demanding for a lodging REIT, but not cheap given the cyclical setup.

Peer comparison is muddied by the sector's depressed and dispersed earnings: Pebblebrook (-22.3x), Park Hotels (-13.2x), and Summit (-22.6x) carry negative P/Es, while Sunstone trades at 107x and DiamondRock at 25x. The peer median P/E of 107x is statistically meaningless. The cleaner read is dividend yield: XHR's ~2.9% sits at the low end versus DRH (3.1%), SHO (3.1%), INN (5.2%), and PK (7.2%). XHR offers less yield support than most peers, which is the point Seeking Alpha flagged in its June 2026 Hold — the ~3% yield is underwhelming after the rally.

The valuation case against an aggressive rating is the price level itself. At $20.02, the stock sits at its 52-week high and at the top of the analyst target band ($17 low, $19.20 mean, $21 high). The mean target is below the current price; only the high target of $21 implies modest upside. While analyst targets typically lag inflection stocks, XHR is a lodging REIT at the top of its cycle, not a structural-growth name, and the targets here cluster around the current price rather than trailing it. Prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark the stock down, but the combination of a full price relative to targets, a below-peer yield, and a cyclical late-stage setup means the easy re-rating has likely been captured. We score valuation 5.0 — neither a clear mispricing nor a clear value trap, with the asymmetry no longer favorable at $20.

## Competitive Landscape

Xenia's luxury/upper-upscale focus differentiates it from select-service peers and benefits from a supply-constrained backdrop. The in-house project-management capability is a genuine, demonstrated edge — the Scottsdale redevelopment and the active portfolio high-grading (Fairmont Dallas sold at a $40M gain, Santa Clara ground lease bought out) show an asset-management posture rather than passive ownership. Q1 RevPAR outperformance versus the industry is direct evidence of share-of-wallet gains at the property level.

The competitive position has structural vulnerabilities. The brand/operator concentration (23 of 30 hotels Marriott/Hyatt) and full dependence on third-party managers limit operational control. A non-recurring operator transition at four hotels in Q1 2026 drove a $3.3M spike (+382%) in other operating expenses plus pre-opening costs; whether that transition delivers the intended margin upside is an open item. The company competes for acquisitions against larger, better-capitalized REITs and private equity, and for guests against alternative accommodations. Company TSR (104.56) has lagged its peer group (124.28) over the proxy measurement window despite strong operating results — a disconnect that suggests the market discounts XHR's leverage and concentration, and that helps explain the buyback-heavy capital allocation. The competitive trajectory at the property level is improving; the portfolio-level position is stable-to-modest, supporting a 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

The principal risks are cyclical and concentration-driven rather than balance-sheet-driven. Luxury/upper-upscale assets carry high fixed costs and are more exposed to revenue declines in a downturn than lower-tier hotels; operating leverage cuts both ways. With the broader macro regime showing inflationary pressure and consumer-demand uncertainty, a discretionary-travel pullback would hit XHR's segment disproportionately. Geographic concentration (CA/TX/FL; Orlando, Phoenix, Houston each >10% of revenue) adds regional event risk.

Refinancing is a structural FFO headwind. The 2030 notes at 6.625% versus the 2029s at 4.875% illustrate the higher-rate reality; the 2029/2030 bond walls will likely refinance at elevated rates unless the long end eases. Inflation and tariff pressure on labor, imported FF&E, insurance, and property taxes are explicitly emphasized in the company's forward-looking risk language. The 10-Q reported "None" for new or modified risk factors this quarter, so there is no incremental disclosure surprise.

On a net basis, much of XHR's risk is already priced — through the peer-relative TSR discount and the below-peer yield. The risk score of 5.5 reflects the cyclical late-stage setup layered on a strong but not invulnerable balance sheet, with no single binary event but a meaningful consumer-demand tail.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 143.5% to 66.3% (-77.2pp) between early May and mid-June as the price rose 17.6% to $20.02. The earlier elevated IV reflected uncertainty into the May 1 earnings print; its compression after the beat and guidance raise indicates resolved uncertainty rather than rising hedging demand. The reading is consistent with a market that has digested the good news, not one bracing for a near-term catalyst. There is no elevated put skew signaling informed bearish positioning [unverified], but the IV normalization also means options no longer offer a cheap hedge against the cyclical downside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The Grand Hyatt Scottsdale ramp continues to drive double-digit property-level RevPAR, raised guidance proves conservative consistent with the recent beat cadence, and the supply-constrained luxury segment sustains pricing power through 2026. AFFO/share grows from $1.76 toward $1.90-2.00, the buyback authorization is redeployed on any pullback, and the stock re-rates toward 12-13x forward AFFO. Bull target ~$23 (roughly +15% from current), assuming the consumer holds and the operator transition delivers the intended margin gains.

### Bear Case

Consumer demand softens in H2 2026, discretionary and group travel weaken, and XHR's high-fixed-cost luxury portfolio sees RevPAR decelerate from +9% toward flat or negative. Margins compress, AFFO/share stalls, and the 2029/2030 refinancing approaches with rates still elevated, pressuring FFO. The stock, having run 63% to a cyclical high with insiders selling at lower prices, mean-reverts toward the mid-teens. Bear target ~$15-16 (roughly -20% to -25% from current), in line with where the CFO and COO sold.

The base case sits between: continued but decelerating RevPAR growth, guidance met near the midpoint, and the stock holding the $19-21 range as the operating story is largely priced. Base target ~$20-21.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (~late July/early August 2026), which will test whether the Scottsdale ramp and raised guidance hold and whether the four-hotel operator transition delivers margin upside. The broader macro read on the consumer is the key external variable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$16s (where insiders sold and where the buyback was paused) on unchanged or improving fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Q2/Q3 2026 RevPAR sustained above +6% with Hotel EBITDA margin holding ≥29%, plus a resumed buyback signaling management sees value at current levels.
- AFFO/share guidance raised above ~$1.90 with the consumer backdrop stabilizing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- RevPAR growth decelerating below +1% YoY in any quarter, signaling the cyclical roll-over has begun.
- A guidance cut to EBITDAre or RevPAR, or evidence the operator transition is structurally impairing rather than improving margins.
- Further clustered C-suite open-market selling, or a dividend that becomes uncovered as AFFO contracts.
- Disorderly long-end rate move materially raising the cost of refinancing the 2029/2030 walls.

## Conclusion

Xenia is executing well, and the operating inflection is genuine and durable enough to warrant continued ownership through 2026. We rate XHR a HOLD on a growth-hold basis.

We stop short of a BUY because the asymmetry is no longer attractive at $20.02. The stock trades at its 52-week high and at the top of a $17-21 analyst band whose mean ($19.20) sits below the current price; it yields less than most peers; and the two best-informed signals in the data — the cluster of C-suite open-market sales and the company's own decision to halt buybacks after acquiring roughly 9% of the float at ~$13 — both point to intrinsic value below the current quote. Add a late-cycle consumer backdrop that hits luxury lodging disproportionately, and the risk/reward at $20 is roughly balanced rather than skewed up. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the mid-teens, where insiders and the company itself demonstrated they see value.
