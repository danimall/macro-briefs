## Executive Summary

NBT Bancorp is a $16 billion-asset community banking franchise operating across seven Northeastern states, with unusually diversified fee income (approximately 27-28% of revenue) from retirement plan administration, wealth management, and insurance services. The bank completed a transformative acquisition of Evans Bancorp in May 2025, expanding into the Buffalo/Rochester metros and pushing total assets up ~16%. Q1 2026 results showed the combined entity generating 1.29% operating ROA and 15.50% ROTCE — well above community bank averages.

The stock trades at approximately 10.8x forward earnings (FY2026E) and 1.70x tangible book value, a discount to the 14.5x peer median P/E, despite higher fee income diversification and improving operating profitability. Analyst consensus targets approximately $48.92, implying 8-9% upside before accounting for the 3.3% dividend yield. A $977K open-market purchase by Director Delaney in January 2026 at $42.97 is the largest insider buy in recent history and signals board-level conviction that the stock is undervalued.

The primary risk is a 134% YoY increase in potential problem loans to $271.8M, partially driven by the Evans acquisition ($60.5M) but also reflecting organic credit migration in commercial portfolios amid macroeconomic pressure. While net charge-offs remain controlled at 17 bps (Q1 2026 annualized), this early-stage credit migration warrants monitoring through the next two quarters. The negative estimate revision trend (-1.1% over 90 days for current year estimates) reflects this caution but is modest in magnitude and does not offset the fundamental value proposition.

## Company Overview

NBT Bancorp operates NBT Bank, a community bank with 47-county presence in upstate New York, northeastern Pennsylvania, Vermont, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Maine, and Connecticut. The franchise serves 613,000+ deposit accounts with a granular average balance of $22,014, providing a naturally sticky funding base.

The bank generates revenue through three primary channels: (1) net interest income from a $11.6B loan portfolio (56% commercial, 22% residential, 22% consumer); (2) fee income from EPIC Advisors, a national retirement plan administration platform ($61.6M in 2025 fees); and (3) wealth management and insurance services ($62.8M combined 2025 fees). This fee diversification — representing $195M annualized — is unusual for a bank of this size and diversifies revenue away from spread income, though parts of it remain market-sensitive and seasonal.

The Evans Bancorp acquisition (closed May 2025) added $2.2B in assets, 18 branches, and a foothold in the Buffalo/Rochester metros. Core system conversion was completed shortly after the merger close, and the operations were fully integrated by mid-2025. Earnings synergies, balance-sheet optimization, and post-merger operating leverage continue to flow through results.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Growth:** Net income grew from $118.8M (2023) → $140.6M (2024) → $169.2M (2025), with EPS progression of $2.65 → $2.97 → $3.33. On an operating basis (excluding acquisition costs), 2025 EPS was $3.82. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.98 annualizes to approximately $3.92, consistent with full-year consensus of $4.17.

**Net Interest Margin:** FTE NIM expanded 36 bps to 3.59% in 2025, driven by asset repricing (loan yields 5.73%), lower deposit costs (2.09%, down from 2.36%), and reduced wholesale funding reliance. The interest rate spread widened to 2.90% from 2.41%.

**Fee Income:** Retirement plan administration fees grew 30% over two years ($47.2M → $61.6M). Wealth management grew 29% ($34.8M → $44.8M). Combined, these recurring fee streams grew at rates far exceeding the banking industry average.

**Asset Quality:** Net charge-offs were 17 bps annualized in Q1 2026, up slightly from 16 bps in Q4 2025. NPLs/total loans were 0.53% at March 31, 2026, up from 0.45% at year-end 2025. The allowance for loan losses stood at $138.6M, or 1.20% of total loans, providing approximately 226% coverage of NPLs. Potential problem loans surged to $271.8M from $116.1M at year-end 2024 — $60.5M from acquired Evans loans and the remainder from organic commercial migration. The CECL model shows a 24% ACL increase under a full stagflation scenario.

**Capital:** CET1 of 12.07% and total RBC of 14.24% provide substantial buffers above well-capitalized minimums. Tangible equity ratio of 8.95% is adequate. The 2M-share repurchase authorization through 2027 provides capital deployment flexibility.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $235.2M and free cash flow of $218.9M in 2025 comfortably cover the dividend payout and share repurchases.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is driven by three vectors:

1. **Evans Synergies (2026-2027):** With core system conversion already complete, the focus is on cost synergies, customer retention, and balance-sheet optimization. Management guided 3-4% expense growth for 2026, implying synergy offsets to nominal inflation. The sold AFS portfolio ($255.5M) and redeemed subordinated debt ($118M) demonstrate active balance-sheet management.

2. **Fee Income Platform Expansion:** EPIC Advisors is a national platform that can grow independently of the branch network. Retirement plan administration fee growth of 15%+ annually is plausible given market appreciation and new plan acquisitions. Wealth management benefits from a similar dynamic at $44.8M with upside from AUM growth.

3. **Loan Growth (Low-to-Mid Single Digits):** Management guided low-to-mid single-digit loan growth for 2026. The residential solar portfolio ($737M) is in run-off, creating a headwind that must be offset by commercial and consumer origination. Net of solar run-off, core loan growth is likely 5-6%.

Consensus estimates of $4.17 (2026) and $4.53 (2027) imply 12% and 9% EPS growth respectively. The negative revision trend is modest and reflects the Q1 2026 marginal miss (operating EPS of $0.97 vs. approximately $1.00 consensus estimate), not fundamental deterioration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.20, NBTB trades at:
- 10.8x forward P/E (FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.17)
- 13.6x trailing GAAP P/E (2025 reported EPS of $3.33)
- 1.70x tangible book value ($26.54 TBV/share)
- 1.24x book value ($36.32 BV/share)

Against peer medians of 14.5x P/E, the stock trades at a meaningful discount despite: (a) higher fee income diversification (27-28% vs. ~20%), (b) superior Q1 2026 ROTCE (15.5%), and (c) a proven acquisition integration track record. A re-rating to the peer P/E of 14.5x on $4.17 2026E EPS implies $60.50, though a more conservative partial re-rating to 12.5-13.5x (acknowledging CRE concentration and geographic headwinds) implies $52-56.

The 3.3% dividend yield provides current income while awaiting the re-rating. The dividend payout ratio is approximately 35-44% of earnings (depending on whether forward or trailing EPS is used), which is sustainable with room for continued modest annual increases.

Analyst targets cluster between approximately $46.50 and $52.00, with a mean near $48.92. At the current price, analyst targets generally represent upside, consistent with the consensus rating mix that leans modestly positive.

## Competitive Landscape

NBT occupies a strong position as an established independent community bank across rural/suburban Northeast markets. Its competitive advantages include:

**Granular Deposit Base:** 613,000 accounts with $22K average balances are extremely difficult to replicate and provide funding stability in smaller markets where larger institutions (M&T, KeyBank) have less entrenched retail presences.

**Fee Income Platform:** EPIC Advisors is a national-scale retirement plan administrator embedded in a community bank — a rare combination that gives NBT earnings diversity most peers lack. This platform grows independently of branch count.

**Agricultural/Rural Expertise:** Deep roots in upstate NY agricultural and small business lending create relationship-driven moats in markets where larger banks lack local expertise.

**Serial Acquisition Competence:** Successful integration of Salisbury and Evans demonstrates repeatable M&A execution — a valuable skill in the consolidating community bank sector.

The primary competitive challenge is geographic: the bank's core markets in upstate NY and rural New England generally exhibit below-national-average economic and population growth. The Evans acquisition partially mitigates this by adding exposure to the relatively more dynamic Buffalo/Rochester economies.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CRE Concentration & Potential Problem Loan Migration (PRIMARY RISK):** CRE at 41% of loans ($4.8B) is the bank's largest exposure, with 78% non-owner occupied. Office represents ~$487M (13% of non-owner occupied). The 134% increase in potential problem loans, while partially Evans-driven, signals broader commercial stress. Under a full stagflation scenario, the ACL would need to increase 24%. This is the specific, falsifiable risk that constrains the conviction level: if potential problem loans continue migrating toward $350M+ in Q2-Q3 2026, it would signal credit deterioration beyond normal cycling and threaten the earnings trajectory.

**2. Estimate Revision Momentum:** Estimates are declining modestly (-1.1% to -1.8% across periods). While the magnitude is small, the direction is negative and could reflect analyst concern about credit costs. If Q2 2026 shows NCOs rising above 25 bps, the credit thesis would materially weaken.

**3. Integration Execution:** While core system conversion for Evans was completed shortly after the May 2025 merger close, achieving full run-rate cost synergies and retaining acquired customer relationships remain ongoing execution challenges through 2026.

**4. Macro/Rate Sensitivity:** With the yield curve flattening potential under a stagflation scenario, NIM compression is possible if short rates remain elevated while long rates moderate. The bank's asset sensitivity (positive to parallel rate rises) could become a headwind in an inverted/flat curve environment.

**5. Geographic/Economic Exposure:** Northeast regional economy with below-average growth potential limits organic expansion.

## Conclusion

NBTB represents a well-managed community bank with superior fee income diversification, trading at a discount to peers despite improving operating profitability. The Evans acquisition provides a specific catalyst for earnings growth through continued synergy realization and balance-sheet optimization in 2026-2027, and the $977K director purchase at $42.97 provides internal conviction. The 3.3% dividend yield provides downside support.

The recommendation is BUY with the following rationale: (a) the forward P/E discount to peers is quantifiable and unjustified given NBT's superior fee diversification; (b) Evans post-merger earnings synergies and operating leverage in 2H 2026 should drive earnings higher; (c) insider buying of this magnitude ($977K) at prices below current has a strong historical correlation with 12-month outperformance; and (d) the risk/reward is asymmetric (11-24% upside vs. 14% downside to bear case, before the 3.3% dividend).

The constraint on full-conviction sizing is the potential problem loan trajectory. If Q2 2026 data shows stabilization, the conviction level increases. If migration accelerates, the thesis should be revisited. Position sizing at 2% given this uncertainty is appropriate.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Q1 2026 credit metrics (NPLs rising to 0.53%, allowance coverage declining to ~226%) are less favorable than year-end figures cited in the original analysis, and the Evans system-conversion catalyst has already occurred, narrowing the forward catalyst argument to earnings synergies and balance-sheet optimization rather than a discrete future event.
