## Executive Summary

Bristow Group is a contracted vertical-flight aviation operator misclassified in oil and gas equipment and services. Two-thirds of revenue comes from transporting personnel to offshore installations on flight-hour and standing-charge contracts; roughly a quarter comes from ten-year-plus search-and-rescue contracts with the UK and Irish governments. The investment question is narrow and testable: the company has affirmed 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $295–325M four consecutive times, H1 2026 delivered $139.1M, and the implied second half of $156–186M requires a 12% to 34% sequential step-up concentrated almost entirely in a Government Services segment whose adjusted operating income *declined* sequentially through H1 ($9.5M in Q1 to $7.2M in Q2, with a GAAP operating loss of $2.1M in Q2).

The bull mechanism is identifiable and largely mechanical. FY2025 carried $28.2M of incremental pass-through subcontractor cost on the UKSAR2G and Irish Coast Guard fixed-wing elements, explicitly guided to subside as transitions conclude; the final UKSAR2G base transitions in late 2026; two new seasonal bases operate through the northern-hemisphere summer that sits in Q3; $160.6M of construction-in-progress (11% of long-lived assets) has not yet begun earning; and Government Services holds $657.9M of segment long-lived assets — 52.8% of the company total — while producing an H1 operating loss of $1.2M. If those costs roll off on schedule, returns on the largest capital pool in the company go from approximately zero to 8–10%, and the 2027 consensus of $5.31 in EPS (8.6x at $45.85) becomes reachable.

The bear mechanism is the delivery record. Three of the last four quarters missed consensus, by -19.9%, -56.5% and -31.7%; current-year consensus has been cut 17.3% in 90 days to $3.53 and next-quarter estimates 10.8% in 30 days; H1 conventional free cash flow was -$75.9M against a company-defined "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" of $117.2M that deducts only $25–30M of maintenance capex rather than the $108.7M actually spent in six months; the drags management called transitory are still being cited (customer availability penalties of $6.6M in H1, up $1.9M year over year, driven by OEM parts delays Bristow does not control); and insiders sold in a Feb–Mar 2026 cluster spanning CEO, CFO, COO and three directors at $44.90–$49.04 while leaving $121M of buyback authorization untouched with the stock at $41.92 in June. We rate the shares HOLD. The multiple is undemanding but the delivery evidence is not, and the decisive data point — the Q3 2026 Government Services step-up — is unverified.

## Company Overview

Three reportable segments. **Offshore Energy Services (OES)**, 66% of FY2025 revenue ($990.5M of $1,490.5M) and 65% of Q2 2026, organized across Europe ($411.3M FY2025), the Americas ($387.5M) and Africa ($191.7M). **Government Services**, 26% of FY2025 revenue ($379.4M) and 27% of Q2 2026, dominated by two contracts: the second-generation UK SAR award from the Maritime and Coastguard Agency / Department for Transport (ten years, twelve bases including two new seasonal sites, rotary-wing plus fixed-wing plus UAS, transition begun late 2024 and completing late 2026) and the Irish Coast Guard contract (ten years, approximately €670M, six AW189s plus two fixed-wing, final base at Waterford live February 1, 2026). **Other Services**, 8%, principally fixed-wing regular passenger transport in Australia plus dry-leasing and parts.

Fleet at June 30, 2026: 218 aircraft (167 owned, 51 leased) — 90 heavy (60 S92 at average age 16 years, 30 AW189 at 8), 72 medium, 15 light-twin, 25 light-single, 13 fixed-wing, 3 UAS, with four AW189s under construction. Twelve air operator certificates across 20 countries; 83% of FY2025 revenue earned outside the United States. Headcount 3,601, roughly 60% under collective bargaining agreements.

Two recent corporate actions matter. Berry Aviation closed July 13, 2026 for $105.0M all cash from balance-sheet cash — 22 fixed-wing turboprops, ~300 employees, FAA and EASA Part 145 repair station, end customers including the US Army, Air Force, SOCOM and TRANSCOM, with issuer-disclosed 2025 revenue above $100M and 2025 EBITDA of ~$12M against ~$18M expected in 2026 (8.75x trailing, 5.83x forward). Separately, on June 23, 2026 Bristow announced intent to sell its Norway OES business; at June 30 it met neither held-for-sale nor discontinued-operations criteria, and no buyer, price or timing has been disclosed.

The predecessor entity emerged from Chapter 11 in October 2019 and merged with Era Group in June 2020. Retained earnings therefore reflect only post-2020 accumulation — relevant when reading balance-sheet screens.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory ($000s):** revenue $1,185,204 (FY2022) → $1,297,429 → $1,415,491 → $1,490,512 (FY2025), a 7.9% three-year CAGR. Pretax income went from a $4.4M loss to $151.2M; net income attributable from -$15.8M to $129.1M; equity from $835.8M to $1,059.7M.

**FY2025 quality of earnings.** Consolidated operating income rose $26.2M to $158.8M. The segment decomposition reconciles exactly: OES +$33.4M (margin 13.7% → 16.7%), Government Services -$16.0M (margin 6.4% → 1.3%), Other -$3.9M, Corporate +$12.7M. Two components deserve weight. OES repairs and maintenance fell $34.0M year over year "primarily due to higher vendor credits" — almost precisely the size of the entire OES operating income improvement — with magnitude, counterparties and contractual basis undisclosed. And the Corporate improvement was substantially asset-disposal gains: $11.8M of net gains in FY2025 against $1.0M of net losses in FY2024, a $12.8M swing against a $12.7M Corporate improvement. Neither treatment is improper, and Adjusted EBITDA excludes the disposal gains ($13.9M subtracted in the LTM reconciliation). But the FY2025 inflection was driven materially by OEM credits and fleet-sale gains rather than by operating leverage on the base business.

**H1 2026.** Revenue $800.5M, +10.1%. Operating income $74.3M, -2.5%. Pretax $37.9M, -57.8%. Net income attributable $34.3M, -42.0%. Diluted EPS $1.14 versus $1.98.

The $51.9M pretax decline decomposes cleanly: non-operating "Other, net" swing of -$43.2M (H1 2026 FX losses of $12.2M and pension costs of $2.8M against prior-year FX gains), interest expense -$6.5M, debt extinguishment -$2.8M, interest income +$2.6M, operating income -$1.9M. Roughly 83% of the earnings decline is a currency translation swing, not operating deterioration. The company held no outstanding FX hedges at June 30.

Within operating income, D&A rose 56% to $53.3M on a disclosed change in accounting estimate: management shortened S76D useful lives after finalizing plans for earlier retirement, adding $13.6M of depreciation in H1 with approximately $13.3M more expected in H2 2026 and $4.4M in 2027, completing by early 2027. No impairment was triggered. Adding that charge back gives adjusted H1 operating income of $87.9M versus $76.2M, +15.3%.

**Pricing versus volume — the strongest operating signal.** OES flight hours fell 1.3% (47,290 versus 47,900) while OES revenue rose 4.7%. Revenue per OES flight hour was $10,911 versus $10,284, +6.1%. That figure includes fuel pass-through (fuel expense rose 36% to $53.5M) and favorable GBP/EUR translation, so the pure rate effect is smaller — but the direction confirms management's disclosure that approximately half of OES contracts reset in 2026 at leading-edge rates, only a portion repriced so far.

**Cost pressure.** Personnel costs rose to 25.6% of revenue from 24.2%, +136 basis points, despite headcount falling from 3,660 to 3,601. Attribution: mix (Ireland, Brazil, Africa additions offsetting UK and US reductions), unfavorable FX, overtime, and a $0.5M UK labor-agreement adjustment. Eight collective bargaining agreements were expired at December 31, 2025 — including UNITE covering 492 UK engineers and non-management staff — and are being renegotiated in 2026.

**Cash flow — the weakest element.** Operating cash flow of $32.8M versus $98.4M, a $65.6M decline, of which $71.5M is attributed to working capital: receivables up $27.5M, increases in other assets for capitalized start-up costs on new Government Services contracts incurred before revenue commences, and an $18.4M payables reduction on tax and OEM vendor payment timing. Capex $108.7M (of which $90.5M in OES for AW189 renewal, versus $28.3M in H1 2025); asset sale proceeds $30.0M. Conventional free cash flow was **-$75.9M**, or -$45.8M including disposals, funded by the January note issuance. Cash taxes of $14.9M exceeded book tax expense of $3.6M by $11.3M, with full-year cash taxes guided at $25–30M.

We would not annualize H1 cash flow: H1 is the seasonally weaker half on daylight hours, absorbed the peak of start-up cost capitalization, and included a year-end payables reversal. Equally, investors should not treat the company's "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" of $117.2M LTM as distributable — it deducts only maintenance capex guided at $25–30M against roughly $110M+ of annual growth capex currently committed to contracted AW189 deliveries. Conventional FY2025 FCF was $56.4M, a 3.8% margin and $1.90 per share (4.1% yield at $45.85).

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $2,382.6M, liabilities $1,293.4M, equity $1,089.2M. Current ratio 2.26x, up from 1.90x. Cash $312.3M plus $59.3M ABL availability equals $371.6M liquidity. Debt of $745.5M carrying comprises $489.0M of 6.750% senior secured notes due 2033 (issued January 2026 at par, refinancing the 6.875% 2028 notes with a $2.8M extinguishment loss — approximately $100M of incremental principal, a 12.5bp coupon reduction and a five-year extension), $151.0M of UKSAR debt due 2036 and $105.4M of IRCG debt due 2031. Fair value $764.0M, a modest premium to carrying. The ABL was extended to 2031 with a 25bp margin cut, but total commitments were reduced from $85M to $70M; no borrowings outstanding, $9.9M of letters of credit.

Net debt on a principal basis is $453M; LTM Adjusted EBITDA of $266.3M gives **1.70x**. Pro forma for Berry, net debt rises to roughly $558M and leverage to approximately 1.96x including Berry's $18M. Guided 2026 cash interest of ~$45M implies roughly 5.9x coverage on guided EBITDA. No maturities before 2031 beyond $27.4M of annual amortization.

The Altman Z-Score screening at 2.19 substitutes total equity for retained earnings; the textbook formulation using retained earnings of $441.7M against $2,311.3M of assets gives approximately 1.82, or 1.96 using market equity in the X4 term. That reading penalizes a post-Chapter-11 equity base with only six years of retained earnings accumulation and an asset-heavy secured-debt structure. Direct solvency evidence — 1.70x leverage, 5.9x coverage, $371.6M liquidity, undrawn revolver, notes marked above par — is materially more informative and reasonably comfortable, contingent on Adjusted EBITDA converting to cash. H1 2026 says it has not yet.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is real and multi-sourced. The 2026 guide of $1,640–1,720M is 10–15% above FY2025. H1 delivered $800.5M, so H2 needs $839.5–919.5M, or +4.9% to +14.9% sequentially. Berry contributed nothing in H1 and at above $100M annualized should add roughly $46M over 5.5 months of ownership, leaving organic H2 revenue needing flat to +9% against a seasonally weaker H1. That is achievable, and the Q2 release was reported as beating on revenue.

The earnings guide is the stretch. Implied H2 requirements against H1 actuals: consolidated adjusted operating income $155.3–185.3M versus $124.7M (+24.5% to +48.5%); Adjusted EBITDA $155.9–185.9M versus $139.1M (+12.1% to +33.7%). Segment concentration is stark — Government Services must produce $38.3–48.3M against $16.7M in H1, a 2.29x to 2.89x step. Berry sits inside that segment and contributes perhaps $8–9M of EBITDA in the stub period, less at the operating-income line after acquired-intangible amortization. Legacy Government Services therefore needs roughly $29–40M against $16.7M, having gone sequentially *backward* through H1.

A useful independent cross-check: 2026 consensus EPS of $3.53 against H1 actual of $1.14 implies H2 EPS of $2.39, more than double the first half. Consensus and company guidance embed the same step-function, and both have been revised down — current-year estimates by 17.3% over 90 days and 9.6% over 30 days.

Structural growth beyond 2026 is better supported than the near-term ramp. Approximately half of OES contracts reset in 2026 with only a portion repriced, so rate benefit remains ahead. Government Services contracts run ten years-plus with extension options and are described as offering attractive cash-flow yields into the middle of the next decade. Berry opens a US Department of Defense channel Bristow previously lacked, with the certifications (Part 135, Part 145 FAA and EASA, AS9100D) required to bid, into a NATO spending environment the 10-K explicitly cites as an opportunity. The accelerated S76D depreciation drops from ~$26.9M in 2026 to $4.4M in 2027, a mechanical GAAP tailwind of ~$22.5M pretax, or roughly $0.61 per share after tax.

Advanced air mobility is optionality with capped downside: $58.8M of unfunded commitments including deposits on five Electra EL9 aircraft for 2029–2030 delivery subject to certification, terminable without further liability if certification deadlines or performance minimums are missed, plus operator roles in Vertical Aerospace and BETA Technologies programs. Bristow is the prospective operator in each case, not a manufacturer. We assign this no base-case value.

## Competitive Landscape

The supply side of this industry is genuinely favorable and the 10-K quantifies it: effective utilization of offshore-configured medium, super-medium and heavy helicopters is near 100%; recent OEM deliveries have gone largely to closed markets in China and the Middle East that do not add contestable Western capacity; militaries are now competing for the same production slots; lead times run up to 24 months; and fewer OEMs build relevant certified models than a decade ago. The competitor set — CHC, NHV, Omni Helicopters, PHI — is consolidated and capital-constrained, with CHC and Bristow's own predecessor both restructuring in 2019. The moat is the certified airframe base plus twelve AOCs plus safety and mission credentials, not technology.

Evidence that scarcity is converting into economics: OES flight hours -1.3% with OES revenue +4.7% and revenue per flight hour +6.1%, with Europe explicitly attributing sequential growth to higher rates partially offset by lower utilization. New offshore contracts also carry more operator-favorable cancellation provisions, a durable commercial improvement independent of the rate cycle.

Two offsets keep this from being a high competitive-position score. First, reliability is deteriorating in a way that costs money and reputation: the S92 is 29% of the fleet at an average 16 years, the 10-K discloses that parts delays have grounded multiple aircraft and forgone business, and this has crystallized as $6.6M of H1 customer availability penalties in Government Services, up $1.9M year over year. Bristow pays penalties to government customers while separately receiving credits from OEM vendors — distinct counterparty relationships that should not be netted. Availability performance is exactly what gets scored on SAR rebids. Second, customer concentration is flat-to-worsening at approximately 63% for the top ten and 36% for the top three. Berry and the Norway exit shift the revenue *mix* toward government work, but Berry's end customers are US defense entities and prime contractors, which may concentrate rather than diversify counterparty exposure. Mix shift is not concentration reduction.

Demand is the neutral leg: a mid-cycle activity plateau that the 10-K says may persist through much of 2026, with deepwater growth in Nigeria and Brazil offsetting mature-basin softness in the North Sea and US Gulf. Helicopter operators contract later and longer than other offshore services, which cushions downturns and delays recoveries.

## Risk Assessment

**Guidance delivery (highest weight).** Government Services must deliver $38.3–48.3M of H2 adjusted operating income against $16.7M in H1, with the H1 trend negative and drags management called transitory still being cited in Q2: $3.6M of quarterly availability penalties, $1.5M of fuel expense exceeding fuel revenue on UKSAR2G rebilling lags, and unspecified transition costs "persisted beyond the commencement of operations at select bases." The availability penalties are tied to OEM parts flow Bristow does not control.

**UKSAR concentration and termination asymmetry.** The UK SAR contract was approximately 19% of FY2025 revenue. The Department for Transport may cancel at will subject to a fee, and the 10-K discloses Bristow cannot compel transfer of its ground facilities to a replacement contractor — a termination would leave fixed costs on non-revenue assets. Government Services holds $657.9M of segment long-lived assets, 52.8% of the company total, currently near break-even.

**Unhedged FX.** 83% of revenue is non-US, principally GBP and EUR, with no outstanding hedge contracts at June 30. FX swung pretax income by $43.2M between H1 2025 and H1 2026. Exchange controls constrain repatriation in Brazil and Nigeria (13% of FY2025 revenue); cash held outside the US fell to 43% from 65%.

**Labor.** Eight expired collective bargaining agreements at year-end 2025, including 492 UK UNITE members and 163 Nigerian pilots and engineers, all being renegotiated in 2026 into a personnel line already up 136 basis points as a share of revenue.

**Fuel and the Iran conflict.** The only material risk-factor change in the Q2 10-Q was new language stating the company is experiencing increased fuel prices due to the ongoing conflict with Iran, with expanded discussion of rebilling lags and markets — specifically Australian regular passenger transport — where fuel cannot be fully passed through. H1 fuel cost rose 36% to $53.5M. Note the asymmetry: sustained high crude is a near-term cost to Bristow and only a lagged, contracted benefit to offshore activity.

**Earnings-quality items.** The durability of OEM vendor credits (magnitude, counterparties and contractual basis undisclosed) determines whether FY2025 OES margins are sustainable. The effective tax rate was 0.5% in Q2, 9.6% in H1, 14.4% in FY2025 and above 100% in FY2023, with LTM income tax a net benefit of $5.2M — net-income-based ratios are noisy, though EBITDA-based leverage metrics computed above the tax line are unaffected. Unquantified contingent tax exposure is disclosed as reasonably possible but not probable.

**Structural.** Non-US persons may not collectively control more than 25% of voting power, with automatic voting-right reduction and aircraft-deregistration risk on breach. This caps the foreign shareholder base and effectively precludes a foreign acquirer.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 71.2% against 31.9% one-year realized, at the 95th percentile within the chain, with the term structure in steep backwardation (71.2% at 13 days versus 40.6% at 41 days and 33.9% at 132 days) and the reported put/call skew of 41.3% characterized as elevated put demand. Put/call open interest rose from 0.44 to 0.78 since July 22 while ATM IV rose 18.9 points and the price moved +1.2%. The nearest expiry prices a ±11.0% move.

Two readings, and we cannot separate them. Elevated near-term volatility *after* an earnings release is unusual and would normally suggest positioning ahead of an unannounced catalyst — plausibly Norway disposal terms, a contract award, or a Government Services update. Against that, the chain contains only 37 contracts across five expirations; in a name with 204,000 shares of average daily volume, implied volatility from a chain that thin is as likely to reflect wide bid-ask spreads as informed flow. We treat the signal as a caution against writing short-dated premium and as a reason not to size aggressively, not as evidence for either direction. Short interest of 4.4% of float and 3.8 days to cover confirms no crowded positioning either way.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($65, +41.8%)

The second-half ramp is contract mechanics, not optimism. FY2025 carried $28.2M of incremental pass-through subcontractor cost explicitly guided to subside as transitions conclude; the last UKSAR2G base transitions in late 2026; two seasonal bases run through the Q3 summer; $160.6M of construction-in-progress starts earning; annual contractual escalations apply. Government Services reaches $55–65M of adjusted operating income, taking returns on $657.9M of assets from approximately zero to 8–10%. Meanwhile roughly half of OES contracts reset in 2026 at leading-edge rates into a fleet at near-100% effective utilization with 24-month OEM lead times and militaries bidding for the same slots, and H1 already showed +6.1% revenue per flight hour on -1.3% hours. Accelerated S76D depreciation falls from ~$26.9M to $4.4M in 2027, adding roughly $0.61 per share. Berry brings a US DoD channel at 5.83x forward EBITDA against Bristow's own ~6.2x. Leverage stays near 2x with no maturities to 2031, and a Norway disposal plus $121M of unused buyback authorization (8.9% of market capitalization) provides per-share optionality. On 2027 consensus of $5.31 the stock is 8.6x; a re-rating toward 12x on a contracted-cash-flow profile gets to $65.

### Bear Case ($35, -23.7%)

Guidance set in November 2025 and affirmed four times has not survived contact with execution: three consecutive misses averaging -36%, current-year consensus down 17.3% in 90 days, and the segment carrying the entire second-half ramp moving backward within H1 with a GAAP operating loss in Q2. The drags are not fully within management's control — availability penalties trace to OEM parts delays, and the S92 fleet averages 16 years. Conventional free cash flow was -$75.9M in H1 while the company promotes an "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" figure of $117.2M that ignores $110M+ of annual growth capex; the FY2025 earnings inflection was roughly the size of an undisclosed OEM vendor credit plus a $12.8M swing in disposal gains. Eight expired labor agreements are being renegotiated into a rising personnel cost line, FX is unhedged against 83% foreign revenue, and management declined to buy stock at $41.92 with $121M authorized while insiders sold in a six-person cluster at $44.90–$49.04. Five straight years of relative TSR underperformance suggests the discount is a considered market judgment, not an oversight. A 2026 EBITDA miss to $270M at 5.8x takes the equity to $35.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD | hold_rationale: uncertain | Horizon: 12–18 months**

- **Base case target: $52 (+13.4%).** 2027E EPS $4.60 at 11.3x, cross-checked at $325M 2027E Adjusted EBITDA × 6.3x less ~$510M net debt.
- **Bull case target: $65 (+41.8%).** 2027E EPS $5.60 at 11.6x, or $360M EBITDA × 6.8x.
- **Bear case target: $35 (-23.7%).** 2027E EPS $3.40 at 10.3x, or $280M EBITDA × 5.8x; approximately the 52-week low of $35.03.
- **Probability-weighted return:** approximately +12% including the 1.09% dividend, at low confidence given a three-analyst estimate base and an unverified H2.

**This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score has declined from 6.6 to 5.4.** That decline tracks identifiable fundamental deterioration rather than price action: the price is essentially unchanged since the last review ($45.42 to $45.85) while three consecutive quarters missed, current-year consensus was cut 17.3%, Government Services adjusted operating income fell sequentially, and H1 conventional free cash flow printed -$75.9M. Flat price on worse fundamentals is worse risk/reward. What stops this from being a REDUCE is that the drags are specifically identified and mechanically transitory on the company's own disclosure, leverage is 1.70x with no maturity wall to 2031, revenue is growing 10%, and 6.2–6.8x EV/EBITDA does not embed the ramp.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 Government Services adjusted operating income of $18M or better in the single quarter (versus $16.7M for all of H1), with availability penalties below $2M in the quarter, confirming the transition-cost roll-off.
2. Conventional operating cash flow less total capex turning positive on a trailing-two-quarter basis, or full-year 2026 conventional FCF guided above $40M.
3. Resumption of open-market repurchases under the $121M authorization below $45, or clustered open-market insider buying — either would resolve the current signal that management does not see the shares as undervalued at these levels.
4. Norway disposal announced with disclosed proceeds and a credible multiple, plus 2027 OES revenue guidance that isolates the retained business.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 2026 Government Services adjusted operating income below $12M, or a downward revision to the $295–325M Adjusted EBITDA range. Either would make a fourth consecutive miss and a 2027 consensus reset the base case, and the bear target becomes the reference.

## Conclusion

Bristow owns genuinely scarce assets — offshore- and SAR-configured heavy and medium helicopters at near-100% effective industry utilization, twelve air operator certificates, and ten-year government contracts — and it is converting that scarcity into price, with revenue per offshore flight hour up 6.1% on volumes down 1.3%. At 6.2–6.8x EV/EBITDA and 8.6x the 2027 consensus, the market is not paying for the contracted-cash-flow profile that the Government Services build-out is supposed to produce.

The reason is delivery. Three of four quarters missed, current-year consensus is down 17.3% in 90 days, H1 conventional free cash flow was -$75.9M against a promoted "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" figure that excludes committed growth capex, and the affirmed 2026 guide rests entirely on a Government Services second half that must double-to-triple sequentially after moving backward through H1. Management chose a $105M acquisition over repurchasing stock at $41.92 with $121M authorized, and six insiders sold into the $44.90–$49.04 band that brackets today's price. Five consecutive years of relative TSR underperformance suggests this discount has been earned rather than overlooked.

The expected return of roughly +12% with a 65-point spread between bull and bear does not clear an accumulation bar for a small-cap with unhedged FX, eight expired labor agreements, and a single binary print in front of it. Existing holders should stay, collect the 1.09% dividend, and treat the November Q3 report as the decision point. New capital should wait for either that print to confirm the ramp or a price near $40 that pays for the risk that it does not.
