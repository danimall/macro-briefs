## Executive Summary

LCI Industries is the dominant supplier of engineered components to the North American RV industry, with increasing diversification into transportation, marine, and housing markets. The company's content-per-unit growth strategy has allowed it to outperform underlying RV unit volumes — revenue to RV OEMs declined only 4% in Q1 2026 despite a 15% drop in wholesale towable shipments. Operating margins are expanding (8.7% in Q1 2026 vs. 3.3% at the 2023 trough), and the balance sheet is well-managed at 1.9x net debt/EBITDA.

However, the investment case faces a specific, quantifiable problem: retail RV demand declined 17% YoY in Q1 2026, management cut its 2026 wholesale forecast by 20K units (to 315K-330K), and the macro environment (elevated rates, consumer stress, tariff uncertainty) argues against a near-term demand recovery. Coordinated insider selling by the CEO, both Group Presidents, and a departing executive in February 2026 at $142-148/share — prices 25-30% above today — provides a clear signal that the people with the most information took liquidity when it was available. The stock's 4 consecutive earnings beats are encouraging, but the forward picture is clouded by accelerating retail weakness and a fluid tariff regime that has already cost $98.5M in segment profit impact in 2025.

At ~13x forward earnings with a 4.1% dividend yield, the valuation is reasonable but does not offer sufficient margin of safety given the deteriorating demand trajectory and elevated short interest (13.5% of float). The risk/reward improves materially below $100, where the stock would trade at ~11x earnings and provide meaningful downside protection through cycle-trough valuation support.

## Company Overview

LCI Industries operates as a vertically integrated manufacturer and supplier of components across multiple end markets, with primary exposure to recreational vehicles. The company operates through two segments:

**OEM Segment (77% of revenue, 66% of operating profit):** Supplies over 75,000 SKUs to RV, transportation, marine, and housing OEMs. The RV subsegment (61% of OEM sales) provides chassis, axles, furniture, windows, doors, appliances, leveling systems, and frames. Transportation (24% of OEM) serves bus, trailer, and specialty vehicle markets. Marine and housing round out the portfolio.

**Aftermarket Segment (23% of revenue, 34% of operating profit):** CURT Manufacturing (towing/hitching products, ~50% of segment) plus RV and marine aftermarket through dealer networks and direct-to-consumer channels. Higher margins and more stable demand characteristics than OEM.

The company operates 100+ manufacturing facilities, primarily in North America, with growing European operations acquired through the Polyplastic/Lewmar acquisitions. Two customers — Berkshire Hathaway/Forest River (18%) and Thor Industries (15%) — represent 33% of consolidated sales.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Recovery:**
- 2023 trough: $3.78B revenue, $83M net income, $3.26 EPS
- 2024: $3.74B revenue, $189M net income, $5.60 EPS
- 2025: $4.12B revenue, $188.3M net income (per 10-K), $7.57 EPS
- 2026E: $4.2-4.3B revenue, $8.75-9.25 adjusted EPS (management guidance)

Revenue grew 10% in 2025 on only 2% wholesale unit growth, demonstrating the content-per-unit thesis in action. Content per towable unit reached $5,670 (up 11% YoY). This structural driver provides organic revenue growth independent of industry volumes.

**Margin Trajectory:**
- Q1 2026 operating margin: 8.7% (vs. 7.8% Q1 2025)
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 11.5% (vs. 10.3% Q1 2025)
- Sourcing savings: $44.5M in 2025 through supply chain diversification
- Facility consolidation: 8-10 additional closures planned for H2 2026

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:**
- 2025 FCF: $278M (6.8% FCF margin)
- Q1 2026 FCF: -$43M (seasonal working capital build, consistent with prior Q1 patterns)
- Net debt/EBITDA: 1.9x
- Available liquidity: $737M ($142M cash + $595M revolver)
- No significant maturities until 2030 convertible notes ($464M, 3.0% coupon)

The company returned $242.6M to shareholders in 2025 ($128.6M buybacks + $114M dividends). At the current $4.60/share dividend (~$112M annual), the 4.1% yield is well-covered by trailing FCF at 2.4x coverage.

**Key Financial Concern:** Aftermarket operating margins declined from 12.6% (2024) to 10.3% (2025), driven by tariffs, product mix shift, and capacity investments. CURT hitch unit sales declined 14% (907K vs. 1.06M). The aftermarket is supposed to be the stable segment — its deterioration warrants close monitoring.

**Estimate Revision Trend:** Current year estimates rose modestly (+1.6% over 30 days, +6.0% over 90 days), but next quarter estimates are declining (-3.9% over 30 days). The 90-day current year revision of +6.0% reflects management's guidance raise (low end from $8.25 to $8.75), but the 30-day deceleration suggests the Q1 beat has been absorbed and retail weakness is now weighing on near-term expectations.

## Valuation Assessment

At $114.16, LCII trades at:
- 15.1x trailing EPS ($7.57)
- 12.8x current year consensus ($8.92)
- 11.3x next year consensus ($10.06)
- ~7.5x EV/EBITDA (estimated on ~$530M 2026E EBITDA)
- 4.1% dividend yield

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E of 19.8x suggests LCII is cheap relative to auto parts peers. However, the comparison requires context: Patrick Industries (24.6x) has higher growth visibility, Dorman Products (19.8x) has more stable aftermarket exposure, and Adient/Dana are negative earners. LCII's discount reflects its cyclical RV exposure and current demand deterioration.

**Cycle-Adjusted View:** At mid-cycle EPS of $9-10 (management's 2026 guidance range), the stock trades at 12-13x — reasonable for a cyclical manufacturer but not deeply discounted given the demand trajectory is worsening. Peak-cycle EPS was $20+ (2022). A return to 2022 peak is unlikely in this rate environment. Normalized through-cycle EPS is probably $10-12, implying the stock is approximately fairly valued at 10-12x through-cycle earnings.

**Why the stock is not a clear BUY despite reasonable valuation:** The multiple is cheap for good reason — retail demand is declining, guidance was cut, and the macro setup argues against a near-term recovery catalyst. At $100-105 (~11x forward), the valuation would provide sufficient margin of safety to absorb further demand deterioration. At $114, the margin of safety is thin relative to identifiable risks.

## Competitive Landscape

LCII's competitive position is its strongest attribute. The company has built a near-monopoly in RV components through decades of acquisitions, manufacturing scale, and geographic proximity to Elkhart-area OEMs. Key competitive advantages:

1. **Scale economics:** 100+ facilities provide cost advantages and proximity to customers that smaller competitors cannot match
2. **Breadth of portfolio:** 75,000+ SKUs create switching costs — OEMs would need to qualify multiple new suppliers to replace LCII
3. **Content growth capability:** R&D pipeline (OneControl, leveling systems, connected products) allows continuous share-of-bill expansion
4. **Customer relationships:** Decades-long relationships with Forest River and Thor, who have limited alternatives at LCII's scale

The primary competitive risk is customer vertical integration — both Thor and Forest River have captive manufacturing capabilities. However, LCII's breadth and cost position have thus far prevented meaningful backward integration. The 10-K notes barriers to entry are "generally low" for individual product categories, but LCII's advantage is in the aggregate portfolio and service level, not in any single component.

## Risk Assessment

**Quantified Risks:**
1. **Tariff impact:** $98.5M in combined segment profit impact in 2025 ($75.8M OEM + $22.7M aftermarket). The 150-day worldwide tariff and 36% imported materials exposure create ongoing uncertainty. Each 5% tariff escalation on imported materials = ~$10-15M additional annual cost pressure.
2. **Retail demand collapse:** Q1 2026 retail towable units at 52,200 (down 17% YoY) is the worst since the COVID shutdowns. If retail doesn't stabilize, dealer inventories cannot be rebuilt, and wholesale shipments face further downside risk below the 315K guidance floor.
3. **Customer concentration:** 33% of sales from two customers with no long-term contracts. Loss of either relationship would be material (Forest River alone = $740M+ in annual revenue).
4. **Convertible dilution:** $464M in 2030 convertible notes at 3.0%. While capped call/warrant structures partially offset dilution, full conversion at lower prices could add 3-4M shares.

**Options Market Signal:** Near-term IV at 43.5% versus 33.9% historical volatility (9.5pp premium) in a backwardated term structure signals the market is pricing imminent stress or event risk. The recent earnings release (May 5) likely accounts for some of this, but the persistence of elevated near-term vol post-earnings suggests ongoing uncertainty. Short interest at 13.5% of float with 6.3 days to cover confirms meaningful institutional bearish positioning.

**Insider Activity Context:** The coordinated February 2026 selling by CEO Lippert (50,000 shares), Ryan Smith (20,000 shares), and Jamie Schnur (20,000 shares) at $142-148 is the most actionable negative signal. While the CEO retains ~$40M in holdings, the timing — within days of the earnings release — suggests these executives perceived the stock as fully valued or foresaw the demand softness that subsequently materialized. The stock has declined 20-25% since their sales.

2. Two consecutive months of positive retail RV registration data (reversing the -17% trend)
3. Management raises 2026 wholesale forecast back above 330K units OR raises EPS guidance above $9.25

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**

2. Management cuts 2026 EPS guidance below $8.50
3. Aftermarket segment shows >15% operating margin compression (below 9%)
4. Net debt/EBITDA exceeds 2.5x

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August 2026) will provide the next major data point on retail demand trends, tariff cost absorption, and margin sustainability. RVIA monthly data provides interim monitoring.

## Conclusion

LCII is a high-quality cyclical business with the best competitive position in its industry, trading at a reasonable but not compelling valuation. The specific reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY: the retail RV demand trajectory is deteriorating (down 17% YoY in Q1 2026), management has already cut guidance once, and the underlying consumer environment argues against near-term stabilization. Coordinated insider selling at $142-148 in February 2026 by C-suite executives with direct visibility into order patterns reinforces that the people closest to the business saw the current softness coming and chose to reduce exposure.

The stock is not overvalued — at 12.8x current year earnings with a 4.1% dividend yield, it provides reasonable return potential if the cycle turns. But buying a cyclical manufacturer facing accelerating demand weakness without a specific recovery catalyst requires a deeper discount to compensate for the probability of further estimate cuts. The appropriate entry point is $95-100, where the valuation would provide meaningful downside protection (10-11x earnings on potentially reduced estimates) and position an investor to benefit from the eventual cyclical recovery.
