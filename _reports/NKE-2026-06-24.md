**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nike is roughly two years into a deliberate marketplace reset that began under prior leadership's over-distribution of classic franchises (Air Force 1, Dunk, Jordan) and over-indexing on direct/digital channels. The reset drove FY2025 revenue down 10% to $46.3B and net income down 44% to $3.22B (diluted EPS $2.16). The stock has fallen roughly 30% since the prior research report and sits at $42.38, about 47% below its 52-week high of $80.17 and near multi-year lows. The central question for the next 12 months is whether profitability follows the top line, which has inflected from -10% in FY25 to roughly flat in Q3 FY26, and on what timeline.

The composition of the margin story has changed in a way that matters. Through the first three quarters of FY26, gross margin fell 250bps, but in Q3 the dominant driver was +270bps of higher product costs from U.S. tariffs, concentrated in North America. The promotional and inventory-obsolescence drag that defined FY25 is fading; average selling price was a positive contributor (+80bps) in Q3 and obsolescence reserves were lower. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized, against which Nike has paid roughly $1.0B, sets up a tariff-refund benefit that the June 23 8-K confirmed will appear in the Q4 FY26 results due June 30, a benefit explicitly excluded from prior guidance.

The most distinctive forward signal is the insider-buying cluster. CEO Elliott Hill bought $1M of stock twice (December 2025 at $61.10, April 2026 at $42.27), and directors Tim Cook, Robert Swan, and John Rogers made open-market purchases at $42-61 during the trough. This is genuine conviction near lows rather than routine grants or tax-withholding. Against that, Greater China revenue continues to decline (-12% constant-currency in 9M FY26), Converse swung to an EBIT loss and is in a "strategic reset," buybacks are paused, and out-year EPS estimates have been cut 22% over 90 days. We rate NKE a HOLD with a constructive bias, classified as a growth hold: the recovery is real but unproven on margins, and the asymmetry is not yet wide enough for a BUY.

## Company Overview

Nike is the world's largest designer, marketer, and seller of athletic footwear, apparel, and equipment. It sells through two channels: NIKE Direct (owned stores plus digital, roughly 42% of brand revenue) and wholesale (roughly 58%). The NIKE Brand is over 90% of total revenue; the remainder is Converse, a wholly-owned subsidiary. Reportable segments are North America (~42% of brand revenue), EMEA, Greater China, and Asia Pacific & Latin America, plus the cost-center Global Brand Divisions and Corporate. The Jordan sub-brand generated $7.3B in FY25.

The competitive moat rests on brand strength, scale, an athlete and league endorsement portfolio carrying a $16.2B contractual obligation, and innovation capability. Manufacturing is fully outsourced to a concentrated base of contract manufacturers located almost entirely outside the United States, which is the structural source of the tariff exposure now dominating the margin discussion. CEO Elliott Hill, a Nike veteran, returned in late 2024 to run the "Sport Offense" / "Win Now" strategy, with Mark Parker as Executive Chairman and Phil Knight as Chairman Emeritus. The Knight family vehicle Swoosh, LLC controls roughly 78% of Class A stock, which elects about 75% of the board, concentrated control that effectively eliminates activist or takeover pressure.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was the trough of the reset. Revenue was $46.3B (-10% reported, -9% constant-currency); net income $3.22B (-44%); gross margin 42.7% (-190bps); EBIT margin collapsed to 8.2% from 12.7%; ROIC fell from 34.9% to 20.2%. The cause was deliberate: flooding the channel with markdowns to clear tired franchises and repositioning Nike Digital to full price.

The FY26 trajectory shows top-line stabilization with lagging profitability. For the nine months ended February 28, 2026, revenue was $35.4B (+1% reported, -1% constant-currency). Q3 FY26 revenue was flat year-over-year (-3% constant-currency), with North America wholesale +11% in the quarter and +15% over nine months, the clearest evidence the wholesale re-engagement is working. Profitability has not yet followed: 9M net income fell to $2.04B (-32%), 9M gross margin dropped to 41.0%, and Q3 net margin was 4.6% with EBIT margin 5.6%.

The driver of the Q3 130bps gross-margin decline matters for the forward case. The dominant contributor was higher product costs from U.S. tariffs, concentrated in North America (where segment gross margin fell 350bps). Average selling price was a positive contributor and obsolescence reserves were lower, meaning the promotional drag that defined the prior fiscal year is reversing while a more policy-dependent tariff headwind has replaced it. That distinguishes a transiently depressed margin from a structurally impaired one.

Cash flow softened. Nine-month operating cash flow was $1.23B versus $3.24B a year earlier, driven by a working-capital build (receivables rising on wholesale shipments, payables down). Capex rose to $546M from $330M. The balance sheet remains investment-grade and solid: total assets $37.1B, equity $14.1B, long-term debt $7.0B plus $1.0B current, and cash plus short-term investments of $8.1B. A $1.0B bond was repaid in FY25. Buybacks were paused in Q1 FY26 ($122M repurchased over nine months versus $2.8B prior year), while the dividend (~$1.8B/year, $0.41/quarter, current yield ~3.6%) was maintained. Inventory held flat at roughly $7.5B, which is healthy and indicates the channel cleanup is largely complete. The retained-earnings line is a small deficit (-$610M) because cumulative buybacks have exceeded retained earnings; this is an artifact of capital return, not a solvency concern. S&P cut Nike to A+ from AA- (July 2025) and Moody's to A2 from A1 (November 2025), still solidly investment grade, but a recognition of eroded earnings power.

Earnings delivery has been strong relative to a low bar: four consecutive beats, including +22.9% in the most recent quarter and +78.1% two quarters prior. This reflects estimates that were cut aggressively into the reset rather than genuine reacceleration, but it does support some confidence that consensus is no longer ahead of reality.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line inflection is genuine, led by North America wholesale +11-15%. The growth thesis from here rests on three pillars. First, continued wholesale re-engagement as Nike rebuilds shelf space with partners it previously deprioritized; North America is the proof-of-concept, EMEA is mid-progress. Second, an innovation cycle in running, basketball, and football aimed at recovering share lost to On, Hoka, New Balance, and a resurgent Adidas. Third, the 2026 World Cup as a concentrated demand-creation window, into which management is leaning with new football boots, federation kits, and retail displays.

Two segments cut hard against the growth case. Greater China revenue fell 13% in FY25 and 12% constant-currency over nine months of FY26, with gross margin down 420bps in FY25, and management explicitly expects negative China impacts to continue throughout FY2027, a function of local competition (Anta, Li-Ning), weak traffic, and elevated channel inventory. Converse is in freefall, down 19% in FY25 and 37% constant-currency in Q3, swinging to a -$40M EBIT loss; it is in a "strategic reset" and is a plausible divestiture or deeper-restructuring candidate, particularly with a new capital-allocation-focused CFO arriving. Nike Brand Digital, once a prized channel, fell 20% in FY25 and another 9-12% in FY26 as management deliberately deprioritized promotional digital volume.

The estimate-revision picture argues for restraint. While near-term quarterly estimates are stable (current quarter +1.4% over 30 days), the medium-term picture has deteriorated: current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 46.4% over 90 days, next-quarter -27.3%, and next-year -22.1% to $1.84. This is the opposite of the upward-revision velocity that flags a high-conviction inflection. Top-line stabilization is real, but the earnings power the market will eventually capitalize is still being marked down. We weight growth heavily as the most reliable selection dimension, and here the signal is mixed: revenue inflecting, earnings estimates still falling. That nets to a middling score.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of ~27.9x on FY25 EPS of $2.16 is not the right anchor for a company at a cyclical earnings trough, but neither is this a classic deep-value inflection where a 150x trailing multiple masks tripling earnings. On the current-year consensus of $1.50 and next-year $1.84, the stock trades at roughly 28x current-year and 23x next-year EPS. Those are not cheap multiples for a company whose out-year estimates are still being revised down. The valuation case depends on normalized earnings power well above the depressed FY26 base: if Nike recovers toward a 12-13% EBIT margin (versus 5.6% in Q3 FY26) on a flat-to-growing revenue base, EPS in the $3.00-3.50 range is plausible in a 2-3 year horizon, which would put the stock at roughly 12-14x normalized earnings, clearly attractive if the recovery materializes.

The analyst target distribution is unusually wide: low $23, high $120, mean $58.72, median $51.50. That dispersion captures the binary nature of the turnaround thesis rather than offering a clean signal. The mean implies roughly 41% upside and the median roughly 24% (against the $41.59 current price referenced in the data), both above the current price, and sentiment has been improving (strong buys 2→5, holds 19→13 over three months). We treat these targets cautiously rather than as confirmation; on a stock where out-year estimates are falling, consensus targets are not a reliable floor.

The relevant lesson from our own track record is that a low or compressed multiple is anti-predictive absent an independent catalyst; cheapness alone marks value traps. Here there are two concrete catalysts that prevent the "cheap and stuck" verdict: the Q4 FY26 tariff-refund benefit and the structural improvement in margin composition (discounting fading, tariffs transient and now legally contested). That keeps the valuation score at the midpoint rather than low. The stock is not asymmetrically cheap on near-term earnings, but it is reasonably priced against a credible normalized-earnings recovery, with identified near-term catalysts.

## Competitive Landscape

The reset is fundamentally about repairing a self-inflicted wound. Under prior leadership Nike over-distributed classic franchises and pushed too hard into direct/digital, eroding marketplace health and brand heat while nimbler competitors took share, particularly in running. The strategy now is to re-engage wholesale, cut supply of tired franchises, accelerate innovation, and reposition digital to full price. The core bull case is that the brand's still-dominant consumer affinity (an Evercore-cited survey claims 95% of consumers say "Nike is a brand for me" versus ≤60% for the nearest rival) gives the company the raw material to re-monetize.

The trajectory is the relevant question, not the level. Nike remains the largest brand by far, but it has been losing share in running to On and Hoka and faces a resurgent Adidas. North America's wholesale rebound is the clearest evidence of stabilizing competitive position; EMEA is improving; China and Converse are deteriorating. On balance, the competitive position has stopped deteriorating in the most important market but has not yet demonstrated durable share recovery. The prior research sequence cut the competitive-position score from 7.5 to 4.5 as the reset deepened; the North America wholesale data and stabilizing brand-heat signals warrant a modest improvement to 5.0, better than the trough read, but not yet a clear improvement story given China and running-category share.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs** are the dominant near-term margin risk and the dominant near-term catalyst simultaneously. Management warned of a "material gross incremental increase to Cost of sales" and a negative FY26 gross-margin impact. The Supreme Court ruling against the ~$1.0B Nike has paid converts part of this from a permanent headwind into a contingent refund. The 10-Q conservatively called recovery "not probable," but the 8-K disclosed that Q4 FY26 results will include a tariff-refund benefit not in prior guidance. The amount is not yet quantified, which makes the June 30 print a genuine swing factor.

**Greater China** is a structural, multi-year drag with management explicitly guiding negative impacts through FY27. **Converse** is EBIT-negative and in strategic limbo. **Digital** weakness, while partly deliberate, reflects the loss of a once-premium channel. The **March 2026 restructuring** booked $304M of pre-tax severance over nine months ($230M in Q3) with more cost actions signaled. **Legal exposures** include a Belgian Customs claim (potential material duties since FY2018), an EU State Aid investigation into Netherlands tax rulings, an ongoing IRS audit (FY2017-2023), and securities class-action and derivative suits tied to the prior decline. A new EMEA ERP rollout in Q3 introduces operational transition risk. The **dual-class control** structure (Swoosh, LLC ~78% of Class A) removes activist pressure as a catalyst, which cuts both ways: it protects the long-horizon reset but limits external accountability.

Against these, the macro backdrop adds a consumer-discretionary caution: a slowing discretionary consumer would pressure athletic-footwear demand precisely as Nike tries to rebuild full-price sell-through. Consumer Discretionary is among the weakest sectors for stock selection in our experience, which raises the bar for any constructive call here.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly constructive and does not contradict a hold-with-upside view. Near-term ATM IV is 42.0% versus 38.8% one-year historical volatility (slightly rich), but IV percentile within the current chain is only 4%, meaning vol is near the low end of its own recent range rather than pricing acute stress. The put/call skew is -3.6% (call-heavy), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.41 leans bullish, though the put/call OI ratio of 1.27 reflects some standing hedge inventory. The term structure is in contango (near 42.0% rising to 47.2% at six months), with the one-month tenor jumping to 61.9% IV and a ±10.6% implied move, pricing meaningful uncertainty around the June 30 earnings print and the tariff-refund quantification. The setup is consistent with a binary catalyst rather than a directional bearish bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($60-75).** The top-line inflection extends as North America wholesale momentum broadens to EMEA. Tariffs prove transient: the Q4 FY26 print delivers a material tariff-refund benefit, and the legal reversal removes a chunk of the gross-margin drag over FY27. Discounting continues to fade (ASP already a positive contributor), obsolescence stays low, and EBIT margin recovers toward 11-13% on a flat-to-growing revenue base, producing $3.00-3.50 of normalized EPS. The World Cup catalyzes football demand, buybacks resume as cash flow normalizes, and the insider-buying cluster proves prescient. At 18-22x normalized EPS the stock re-rates to $60-75.

**Bear case ($30-35).** China deterioration accelerates and bleeds into FY28, Converse's losses widen or its divestiture is value-destructive, and the running-category share loss to On/Hoka proves structural rather than cyclical. A weakening discretionary consumer hits full-price sell-through just as Nike rebuilds wholesale shelf space, forcing renewed promotion and re-collapsing the margin recovery. The tariff refund is smaller than hoped and new tariffs replace the old. Out-year estimates continue falling, and the stock de-rates to 18-20x a $1.70-1.85 EPS base, or roughly $30-35.

**Base case ($50-54).** Revenue grows low-single-digits in FY27, gross margin stabilizes as the tariff drag partially reverses but China remains a drag, and EBIT margin recovers to 8-9%, producing EPS around $2.20-2.50. The Q4 tariff refund provides a near-term boost. At roughly 22x that recovering earnings base, the stock trades to $50-54, consistent with the analyst median of $51.50.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q4 FY26 earnings report on June 30, 2026 is the immediate catalyst and the first natural reassessment point; it will quantify the tariff-refund benefit and provide updated FY27 framing. The fuller recovery thesis plays out over 12-18 months as margins either recover or stall.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin inflects positive year-over-year for two consecutive quarters, confirming the tariff drag is reversing and discounting is not returning.
- Out-year EPS estimates stop falling and turn positive (current-year and next-year revisions flat-to-up over 90 days, reversing the next-year cut).
- A quantified, material Q4 tariff refund (meaningful fraction of the ~$1.0B paid) plus evidence of EMEA wholesale acceleration matching North America's pace.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- North America wholesale momentum stalls (growth decelerating below +5%) or reverses, removing the proof-of-concept for the reset.
- China revenue declines accelerate beyond the current pace or China gross margin compresses further, signaling structural rather than cyclical loss.
- Renewed promotional activity reappears in the margin bridge (ASP turning negative, obsolescence reserves rising), indicating the channel cleanup failed to hold.
- Insider behavior reverses to net open-market selling by the C-suite or board.

## Conclusion

Nike sits at a genuine top-line inflection with a still-unproven margin recovery. The revenue trajectory has improved, the margin headwind has shifted from structural discounting to a transient, legally contested tariff drag, and the insider-buying cluster is a meaningful conviction signal at the trough. A near-term tariff-refund catalyst lands with the June 30 print. Against that, China and Converse remain structural drags, out-year EPS estimates are still being cut, buybacks are paused, and the consumer-discretionary macro backdrop adds caution.

This is a HOLD with a constructive bias, classified as a growth hold. The recovery is real enough and the insider signal strong enough to avoid an AVOID; the falling out-year estimates and unproven margin recovery are decisive enough to withhold a BUY. The risk/reward is roughly balanced around the current $42.38: modest upside to a $50-54 base case, real upside to a $60-75 bull case if margins recover and tariffs reverse, against a $30-35 bear case if China and the consumer worsen. We would become buyers on confirmation of two consecutive quarters of positive gross-margin inflection, or on a pullback toward the high-$30s that widens the asymmetry while the fundamentals hold.
