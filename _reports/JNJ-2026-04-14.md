## Executive Summary

Johnson & Johnson delivered $94.2B in FY2025 revenue (+6.0% YoY), driven by 8.4% volume growth that more than offset a -3.1% price headwind from Medicare Part D redesign and biosimilar erosion. The company reported a Q1 2026 beat on both revenue and EPS, raised full-year 2026 guidance, and is benefiting from a $7.0B talc reserve reversal that signals diminishing litigation overhang. The oncology franchise — anchored by DARZALEX ($14.4B, +23%) and supplemented by CARVYKTI ($1.9B, +96%) — is the primary growth engine, while TREMFYA ($5.2B, +40%) is executing a credible replacement for the declining STELARA franchise.

The balance sheet carries $47.9B in total debt ($27.8B net), elevated by the $14.5B Intra-Cellular Therapies acquisition that closed in April 2025. At $19.7B in free cash flow (20.9% FCF margin), JNJ comfortably services this debt and has raised its dividend for 63 consecutive years. The stock trades at approximately 15x forward earnings, a discount to large-cap pharma peers, with the talc litigation resolution, TREMFYA's IBD expansion, and the orthopaedics separation providing distinct catalysts over the next 12-18 months.

In the current macro environment — with recession probability at 45-50%, rising energy costs flowing through CPI, and record-low consumer sentiment — JNJ's defensive characteristics (healthcare demand inelasticity, strong FCF, diversified revenue base) provide portfolio ballast. The STELARA cliff and pending LOE risks on OPSUMIT ($2.3B) and SIMPONI ($2.7B) are real headwinds, but the pipeline depth and acquisition strategy are positioned to absorb them.

## Company Overview

Johnson & Johnson operates two business segments following the 2023 Kenvue spin-off:

**Innovative Medicine (~64% of FY2025 sales, $60.4B):** Prescription pharmaceuticals spanning oncology, immunology, neuroscience, pulmonary hypertension, and cardiovascular/metabolism. The segment contains JNJ's highest-growth and highest-margin assets.

**MedTech (~36% of FY2025 sales, $33.8B):** Surgery ($10.1B), Orthopaedics ($9.3B), Cardiovascular ($8.9B), and Vision ($5.5B). The cardiovascular subsegment benefits from Shockwave and Abiomed integrations. Orthopaedics, the weakest performer at 1.1% growth, is being separated as a standalone entity (announced October 2025, targeting 18-24 month completion).

Key recent M&A includes: Intra-Cellular Therapies ($14.5B, closed April 2025) adding CAPLYTA for depression/bipolar disorder ($700M in ~9 months of ownership), and Shockwave Medical (closed May 2024) fully integrated into cardiovascular.

The company employs approximately 138,200 people globally and has a 5-year revenue CAGR of 6.7%.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $94.2B grew 6.0% YoY ($88.8B in FY2024), with volume contributing 8.4 percentage points offset by -3.1 points of price erosion and +0.7 points from currency. This volume-driven growth profile is more sustainable than price-driven growth in a healthcare environment facing increasing pricing pressure from IRA Medicare negotiations and international reference pricing.

Gross margin of 67.9% declined from 69.1% in FY2024, reflecting mix shift toward lower-margin MedTech revenue, STELARA erosion (high-margin product losing share to biosimilars), and early tariff impacts on MedTech COGS. R&D spending declined 15% to $14.7B, partly due to IPR&D charge comparisons, but R&D as a percentage of revenue (~15.6%) remains appropriate for a diversified pharma/medtech company.

Operating cash flow of $24.5B and free cash flow of $19.7B (20.9% FCF margin) are strong. FCF comfortably covers the $12.4B dividend and $6.0B buyback program with $1.3B to spare — before accounting for the $17.5B in acquisition spending funded primarily by new debt issuance.

### The STELARA Math

STELARA sales declined 41.3% in FY2025, from $10.4B to $6.1B, removing 6.2 percentage points from worldwide operational growth. The replacement portfolio added approximately $6.7B in incremental revenue:

- DARZALEX: +$2.7B (to $14.4B)
- TREMFYA: +$1.5B (to $5.2B)
- CARVYKTI: +$0.9B (to $1.9B)
- SPRAVATO: +$0.6B (to $1.7B)
- CAPLYTA: +$0.7B (partial year acquisition)
- ERLEADA: +$0.6B (to $3.6B)

The $6.7B of gains vs. $4.3B of STELARA losses demonstrates that the replacement pipeline is outpacing the erosion. However, STELARA will likely lose another $2-3B in FY2026, and OPSUMIT ($2.3B) faces generic competition beginning in 2026, adding a second material LOE.

### Balance Sheet

Net debt increased from $12.1B to $27.8B, driven by the $9.2B debt issuance for the Intra-Cellular acquisition. Total debt of $47.9B against $19.7B cash and equivalents yields a net debt/EBITDA of roughly 1.2x — manageable for a company of JNJ's cash flow generation. The Altman Z-Score of 1.59 is misleading: it penalizes JNJ's $48.8B goodwill and $50.4B intangible assets from acquisitions, depressing asset-efficiency ratios. A company generating $24.5B in OCF with 63 consecutive dividend increases is not in financial distress.

Goodwill and intangibles total $99.2B, approximately 50% of total assets. This is the acquired intellectual property base of the company and represents real economic value (DARZALEX alone generates $14.4B annually), but any significant impairment would impact book value materially.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Growth Drivers (FY2026-2027)

1. **DARZALEX franchise expansion:** $14.4B and growing 23%, with frontline myeloma (CEPHEUS) and smoldering myeloma (AQUILA) label expansions. Analyst estimates project $17B+ by 2029. Each new indication adds patient populations without meaningful incremental COGS.

2. **TREMFYA IBD expansion:** The UC and Crohn's Disease approvals transform TREMFYA from a dermatology drug into a multi-franchise immunology platform competing with AbbVie's Skyrizi/Rinvoq. Management's IBD commercial rollout is the critical execution variable for offsetting continued STELARA decline.

3. **CARVYKTI scaling:** $1.9B and growing 96%, constrained by manufacturing capacity. As capacity expands and earlier-line myeloma data matures (MajesTEC-3), this CAR-T therapy has a path to $4-5B.

4. **CAPLYTA:** $700M in ~9 months of JNJ ownership. The MDD adjunctive treatment approval expands the addressable market substantially. Integration execution will determine whether this acquisition justifies its $14.5B price tag.

5. **SPRAVATO monotherapy filing:** If approved for treatment-resistant depression as monotherapy (not adjunctive to oral antidepressant), the commercial potential expands meaningfully from $1.7B.

### Longer-Term Pipeline

ICOTYDE (icotrokinra) for psoriasis offers a differentiated mechanism. IMAAVY (nipocalimab) for generalized myasthenia gravis addresses a growing rare disease market. RYBREVANT subcutaneous formulation improves patient convenience. The pipeline breadth across oncology, immunology, and neuroscience reduces single-asset dependency.

### Growth Headwinds

- STELARA erosion will continue through FY2026-2027 (likely losing another $3-4B)
- OPSUMIT ($2.3B) faces generic competition starting 2026 — management explicitly flagged "significant reduction in future sales"
- SIMPONI ($2.7B) has biosimilar applications pending
- IMBRUVICA ($2.8B) declining 7.1% under competitive pressure
- Medicare Part D redesign and IRA price negotiation create structural pricing pressure
- Orthopaedics separation will consume management bandwidth and generate "significant expenses"

The cumulative LOE exposure over 2025-2028 across STELARA, OPSUMIT, SIMPONI, and IMBRUVICA is approximately $14-15B in at-risk revenue, partially offset by a growth portfolio that added $6.7B in FY2025 alone.

## Valuation Assessment

JNJ trades at approximately 15x forward earnings. HSBC recently raised its price target to $280, implying roughly 16% upside from the ~$241 level referenced in the research. The stock has appreciated ~48% over the trailing twelve months, recovering from the talc litigation overhang.

At 15x forward earnings, JNJ trades at a discount to large-cap pharma peers (Eli Lilly at 40x+, AbbVie at ~18x, Merck at ~14x — though Merck carries its own LOE risks with Keytruda). The discount partially reflects: (1) the STELARA cliff, (2) residual talc litigation risk, (3) the complexity of a simultaneous acquisition integration and business separation.

The $19.7B FCF represents a ~5.1% FCF yield at the current enterprise value — reasonable but not cheap by healthcare standards. The 63rd consecutive dividend increase ($5.14/share, ~2.1% yield) provides income support.

**Fair value framework:** If JNJ achieves $10.50-$11.00 in adjusted EPS for FY2026 (consistent with the guidance raise) and deserves a 16-17x multiple (reflecting improving talc outlook and growth portfolio momentum), that implies a $168-$187 fair value range. At 18x — which would require clear evidence that the LOE headwinds are fully managed and TREMFYA's IBD expansion is succeeding — the stock reaches $189-$198. The HSBC $280 target at roughly 25x forward earnings requires aggressive assumptions about pipeline value realization.

Given the quality of the franchise (DARZALEX alone covers >25% of Innovative Medicine revenue and is growing 23%), the current 15x multiple undervalues the growth assets while appropriately discounting the LOE risks and remaining litigation tail.

## Competitive Landscape

### Oncology

JNJ's multiple myeloma franchise ($17.4B across DARZALEX, CARVYKTI, TECVAYLI, TALVEY) is the largest in the world. DARZALEX has no near-term LOE risk and is still expanding into earlier disease settings. CARVYKTI competes with BMS's Abecma in CAR-T for myeloma; CARVYKTI's CARTITUDE-4 data in earlier lines gives it a differentiation advantage.

### Immunology

The STELARA-to-TREMFYA transition is the critical competitive battle. TREMFYA (IL-23 specific) competes against AbbVie's Skyrizi (also IL-23) and Rinvoq (JAK inhibitor). In IBD specifically, AbbVie has a significant head start with Skyrizi already approved in Crohn's and UC. TREMFYA's differentiation will depend on clinical data quality and the speed of its IBD commercial rollout.

### MedTech

Cardiovascular is the strongest subsegment (+15.8%), but faces competitive pressure in Pulsed Field Ablation from Boston Scientific and Medtronic. Orthopaedics at 1.1% growth is being appropriately separated. China volume-based procurement remains a recurring headwind across all MedTech categories. The Surgery segment ($10.1B) is undergoing restructuring with $0.9-1.0B in expected costs to exit non-strategic product lines.

### Wholesaler Concentration

Three wholesalers (McKesson, AmerisourceBergen, Cardinal Health) account for 48.4% of gross revenues (21.8%, 15.5%, 11.1%). This is standard for US pharmaceutical distribution but creates channel dependency and limits pricing flexibility.

## Risk Assessment

### Talc Litigation (Diminishing but Not Resolved)

The $7.0B reserve reversal and the Beasley Allen disqualification represent significant positive developments. The remaining $3.4B accrual covers 67,000+ pending lawsuits. The risk has shifted from existential threat to manageable tail risk, but resolution is uncertain and any adverse court ruling could reverse the trend. The ~$1.1B remaining opioid settlement is separately accrued.

### LOE Concentration (2025-2028)

The combined $14-15B LOE exposure across STELARA, OPSUMIT, SIMPONI, and IMBRUVICA is the primary fundamental risk. Management's M&A strategy and organic pipeline are designed to offset this, but execution risk is material. If TREMFYA's IBD launch disappoints or CARVYKTI manufacturing scaling stalls, the revenue gap widens.

### Tariff and Pricing Policy Risk

The 100% tariff on patented pharmaceutical imports announced in April 2026 creates uncertainty despite JNJ reportedly having a deal with the administration. MedTech COGS are already being impacted by tariffs per the 10-K. The IRA Medicare Drug Price Negotiation program, which JNJ is actively litigating (seeking Supreme Court review), creates additional structural pricing headwinds. In a stagflationary macro environment where the administration is simultaneously raising tariffs and pressuring drug prices, pharmaceutical companies face a dual squeeze on revenue and costs.

### Orthopaedics Separation Execution

Separating a $9.3B business is operationally complex. Management time and capital will be consumed over 18-24 months. Stranded costs, IT system separation, talent retention, and stand-up costs for the new entity are all potential drags. The strategic logic is sound (focus on higher-growth assets), but execution matters.

### Balance Sheet Leverage Post-Acquisition

Net debt at ~1.2x net debt/EBITDA is manageable, but the elevated leverage reduces flexibility for further large acquisitions at a time when LOE replacements may require additional pipeline purchases.

### Options Market Signal

No current options data was available for JNJ. Given the recent Q1 2026 beat and guidance raise, the stock likely sits at a post-earnings IV trough. Healthcare as a defensive sector typically carries lower implied volatility than the broader market. The absence of elevated put activity would be consistent with the improving fundamental picture.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($190-200, 17-18x forward earnings)

TREMFYA's IBD expansion succeeds, reaching $8-10B in revenue by 2028. DARZALEX continues growing through label expansions to $17B+. CARVYKTI scales past manufacturing constraints to $4-5B. CAPLYTA integration succeeds with $2B+ annual run rate. Talc litigation resolves favorably with remaining reserves proving adequate. Orthopaedics separation unlocks a valuation re-rating as the remaining JNJ portfolio demonstrates higher growth and margins. The stock re-rates from 15x to 17-18x as investors price in the improved growth-mix profile.

### Bear Case ($140-150, 13-14x forward earnings)

TREMFYA loses the IBD battle to AbbVie's Skyrizi, limiting revenue to $6-7B. OPSUMIT and SIMPONI LOEs hit simultaneously in 2026-2027, creating a second revenue cliff before the pipeline can compensate. CARVYKTI manufacturing issues persist, capping revenue at $2-3B. Tariff implementation raises MedTech COGS materially. Talc litigation takes an adverse turn (new court ruling, settlement requirement above reserves). The orthopaedics separation is delayed or costly. In this scenario, JNJ's organic growth stalls at 1-2%, and the multiple compresses to reflect a mature, ex-growth pharma conglomerate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $180-185, based on 16.5x forward earnings on $11.00 FY2026 adjusted EPS. This reflects improving growth portfolio momentum, diminishing talc overhang, and a gradual multiple re-rating from the current 15x. **Upside from current (~$165): 9-12%.**

**Bull case target:** $200, based on 18x forward earnings. Requires TREMFYA IBD success, DARZALEX expansion, and talc resolution. **Upside: ~21%.**

**Bear case target:** $140, based on 13x forward earnings. LOE headwinds materialize faster than pipeline can offset. **Downside: ~15%.**

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts include: (1) TREMFYA IBD launch trajectory (commercial data through H2 2026), (2) OPSUMIT generic entry timing and erosion rate, (3) talc litigation milestones, (4) orthopaedics separation filing (expected mid-2026 to late 2026).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. TREMFYA quarterly sales growth decelerates below 15% YoY for two consecutive quarters, suggesting IBD launch failure
2. Management reverses the talc reserve improvement (re-accrues >$3B in new talc charges)
3. DARZALEX growth decelerates below 10% YoY, suggesting the oncology franchise is maturing faster than expected

## Conclusion

JNJ at 15x forward earnings represents a high-quality healthcare franchise trading at a discount during a transition period. The STELARA cliff is real and ongoing, but the replacement portfolio added $6.7B in FY2025, outpacing the $4.3B in STELARA losses. The talc litigation overhang is materially improving. The Q1 2026 beat and guidance raise confirm operational momentum.

In a macro environment characterized by rising inflation (April CPI likely >4.0%), recession risk (45-50%), and geopolitical uncertainty, JNJ's defensive characteristics provide portfolio value. The stock offers asymmetric risk/reward: 9-12% upside to base case, 21% to bull case, with 15% downside in the bear scenario. The multiple is low enough that growth portfolio expansion and talc resolution provide identifiable catalysts for re-rating. BUY with a 12-18 month horizon.
