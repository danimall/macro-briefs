## Executive Summary

Ross Stores is the second-largest off-price retailer in the US, operating 2,267 stores with a highly efficient business model generating $2.2B in free cash flow on $22.75B in revenue. The company benefits from a secular shift in consumer spending toward value retail — off-price now captures 63% of combined visits vs. department stores, up from below 50% pre-COVID. Q4 FY2025 (ended January 2026) delivered exceptional 9% comp growth and EPS of $2.00 vs. $1.89 consensus.

The investment challenge is valuation relative to growth. ROST trades at 34.4x trailing EPS and approximately 31x FY2026E earnings ($7.37), representing a 48% premium to apparel retail peer median (23.3x). Revenue is compounding at ~7% annually with FY2026 guidance of 3-4% comps and 110 new stores. This is solid but not exceptional growth that would justify a premium of this magnitude. The stock has rallied 63% from its June 2025 low of $124.49, pricing in much of the structural thesis.

The specific risk preventing an upgrade is tariff concentration: over half of all merchandise sold originates from China, and further tariff escalation from the current environment could compress operating margins — which already declined 35bps in FY2025 despite 5% comp growth — at the same time that $1.1B in capex creates distribution center startup cost headwinds.

## Company Overview

Ross Stores operates two off-price concepts: **Ross Dress for Less** (1,904 stores) targeting middle-income households with 20-60% discounts off department store prices, and **dd's DISCOUNTS** (363 stores) targeting lower-to-moderate income consumers with 20-70% discounts. The company operates exclusively brick-and-mortar with no e-commerce presence — a deliberate choice that supports the treasure-hunt shopping experience and low-cost operating model.

The competitive advantage rests on 800+ merchants executing opportunistic buying of manufacturer overruns, canceled orders, and closeouts. The "packaway" system (37% of inventory at fiscal year-end) allows Ross to warehouse bargains and release them at optimal seasonal timing. Management identifies a long-term runway to 3,600 stores (59% growth from 2,267 today).

**Balance Sheet Strength:**

- Long-term debt: $1.02B (declining rapidly; $700M repaid in April 2025, $500M due April 2026)
- Post-April 2026, remaining debt is approximately $525M — effectively zero net debt
- Altman Z-Score: 3.05 (safe zone)
- Current ratio: 1.58

**Capital Allocation:**
- Share repurchases: $1.05B in FY2025 at average $147.61 (well below current $227)
- Dividends: $528M (raised 10% to $0.445/quarter)

- New $2.55B repurchase authorization through January 2028
- FY2026 capex: $1.1B (record, driven by 9th distribution center buildout)

The company generates over $2B in annual FCF against minimal debt obligations, allowing simultaneous capacity investment and aggressive shareholder returns. The 34.7% ROE on a capital-light model with declining leverage is a premier retail financial profile.

## Growth Analysis

**Quantitative Growth Trajectory:**
- 3-year revenue CAGR: ~6.8%
- FY2025 EPS growth: ~19% (partially inflated by buybacks and one-time items; adjusted ~10%)
- FY2026E EPS consensus: $7.37 (+11.5%)
- FY2027E EPS consensus: $8.14 (+10.4%)
- Same-store sales guidance: 3-4% for FY2026

**Growth Drivers:**
1. Store count expansion: 110 new stores planned for FY2026 (5% unit growth); runway to 3,600 total
2. dd's DISCOUNTS acceleration: 25 new stores in FY2026 (vs. 10 in FY2025) — well-timed given consumer trade-down trends
3. Self-checkout pilot expansion: could improve labor cost leverage over time
4. Off-price secular share gains from department store decline

**Growth Constraints:**
- Revenue growth is structural but mid-single-digit, not high-growth
- Operating margin expansion limited near-term by DC startups and tariff headwinds
- Packaway inventory declined from 41% to 37% — recent comp outperformance partially consumed stored inventory, which isn't infinitely replenishable at the same quality
- No e-commerce optionality

Estimate revisions are modestly positive (+4.1% over 90 days for the current quarter, +3.1% for current year, +3.5% for next year) but do not meet the threshold for a high-conviction signal. This is steady-state growth, not acceleration.

**The Bull Case on Valuation:** High ROE (34.7%), near-zero net debt, recession-resistant model, and 3,600-store runway justify a quality premium. If the consumer environment deteriorates, Ross benefits from trade-down as a defensive compounder.

**The Bear Case on Valuation:** 10% EPS growth at 34x is a PEG of 3.4x — expensive for a mature retailer growing in the mid-single digits. The stock has already rallied 63% from June 2025 lows ($124.49). At $227, much of the quality premium is embedded. Upside to consensus mean target ($234) is only 3%.

**My Assessment:** The valuation prices in continued execution and a stable consumer environment. It does not offer meaningful margin of safety for negative scenarios (tariff escalation, consumer weakness, margin compression). This is a high-quality business priced as a high-quality business.

## Competitive Landscape

Ross holds clear structural advantages:
- **Scale:** 2,267 stores provide buying leverage across 800+ merchants sourcing from thousands of vendors
- **No vendor dependency:** Unlike department stores, Ross requires no promotional allowances or co-op advertising
- **Operating cost structure:** No e-commerce overhead, self-service format, minimal store buildout
- **Traffic momentum:** Placer.ai data shows Ross foot traffic +12% YoY in Q4, outpacing TJ Maxx (+2.8%), Marshalls (+3.3%), and Burlington (+9.4%)

The secular tailwind is real and measurable. Off-price has gained share from department stores for 15+ years, and the current environment of tariff uncertainty, inflation pressure, and consumer value-seeking accelerates this trend. Ross's explicit lack of e-commerce may become a differentiator as competitors bear fulfillment costs.

Competitive risks: TJX remains larger with international scale; Burlington is growing faster from a smaller base. Ross's 5-year TSR of 179% lagged its peer group at 209%, suggesting the market perceives slightly weaker competitive positioning or execution relative to direct competitors.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff/China Exposure (HIGH):** Over 50% of merchandise sold originates from China. FY2025 already absorbed ~$0.16/share in tariff costs. The tariff environment represents a structural cost headwind that could intensify. While Ross's buying model provides flexibility (they buy excess inventory from brands absorbing tariffs), this advantage is partially offset by direct cost increases on their own imported goods.

**2. Margin Compression Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** FY2026 faces continued DC startup costs (9th center under construction) plus elevated capex at $1.1B. Management guides "slightly higher" margins, but the combination of tariff headwinds and infrastructure investment limits expansion potential.

**3. CEO Transition Risk (MODERATE):** James Conroy (from Boot Barn) represents the first external CEO in Ross's modern era. His public comments about potentially raising average unit retail prices represent a potential strategic divergence from the deep-value proposition that drives Ross's traffic. If price increases erode the value perception, traffic growth could decelerate.

**4. Geographic Concentration (MODERATE):** ~50% of stores in California, Texas, and Florida. Corporate HQ and half of distribution capacity in California. Natural disaster vulnerability is concentrated.

**5. Consumer Sensitivity (LOW-MODERATE):** While off-price is "recession-resistant," it is not recession-proof. In severe downturns, even value shoppers reduce discretionary apparel purchases.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($265-290):** Off-price secular share gains accelerate as tariff-driven excess inventory floods the market with buying opportunities. Q4's 9% comp proves Ross is the primary beneficiary of consumer trade-down. FY2027E EPS reaches $8.50+ on margin expansion from self-checkout rollout and operating leverage against DC costs. 35x forward P/E on $8.50 = $298. New CEO brings fresh strategic vision that reinvigorates growth without sacrificing the value model.

**Bear Case ($165-185):** Tariff escalation compresses operating margins 100-150bps as China-sourced merchandise costs rise faster than selling prices. Consumer spending weakens meaningfully, reducing traffic even to off-price. FY2026E EPS comes in at $7.00 or below. Multiple contracts to 25x (still a premium to peers) as growth decelerates to 5-7% EPS. 25x × $7.00 = $175.

**Base Case ($230-250):** Ross executes on 3-4% comps and 110 new stores. Margins stabilize but don't expand meaningfully. EPS delivers $7.30-$7.50. Multiple holds at 32-33x on quality premium and defensive positioning. Limited upside from current levels unless growth accelerates or multiples re-rate higher.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $195-205 range (26-28x FY2027E EPS), providing 15%+ upside to base case on unchanged fundamentals
2. FY2026 quarterly results demonstrate sustained comp growth >5% with stable-to-expanding margins, suggesting tariff headwinds are being absorbed
3. Estimate revisions accelerate above +5% over 30 days (currently +0.1-0.5%), indicating consensus is meaningfully behind

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Same-store sales decelerate below +1% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the trade-down thesis is weakening
2. Operating margins compress below 11.0%, suggesting tariff/cost headwinds are overwhelming the model
3. CEO Conroy implements meaningful AUR price increases that result in negative traffic comps

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected late May/early June 2026 for Q1 FY2026). The Q1 report will provide the first clean read on whether FY2026 guidance of 3-4% comps and margin expansion is tracking.

## Conclusion

Ross Stores is a high-quality defensive compounder with an outstanding financial profile (34.7% ROE, 9.7% FCF margin, near-zero net debt) positioned on the right side of a structural industry shift toward off-price retail. The business model works in both strong and weak economies, and the 3,600-store runway provides multi-year visibility.

The specific reason this is not a BUY at $227 is quantifiable: at 34.4x trailing earnings and 31x forward, the stock prices in continued 10% EPS growth with no margin for error. The consensus mean target of $234 offers only 3% upside. Downside to the bear case ($175 on tariff-driven margin compression) represents 23% risk. With tariffs on >50% China-sourced merchandise already compressing margins and the consumer environment facing structural headwinds, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside at this multiple. A pullback to $195-205 (26-28x FY2027E) would create a more compelling entry point where the quality premium offers adequate margin of safety against the identifiable headwinds.
