## Executive Summary

Otis is the world's largest elevator and escalator company, and the economics of the business rest almost entirely on one asset: a 2.5-million-unit maintenance portfolio that generated 65% of FY2025 net sales and 91% of segment operating profit at a 25.1% margin. That margin has now compressed for two consecutive quarters — 23.0% in Q1 2026 and 23.2% in Q2 — breaking a three-year expansion streak. Reported 1H26 Service operating profit rose $40M, but $34M of that was disclosed foreign-exchange benefit, leaving roughly $6M of constant-currency profit growth on approximately $315M of organic Service revenue growth. An incremental constant-currency margin near 2% against a 23.1% segment average is the reason the stock has fallen 21.8% from its February high of $94.57, and it is a more consequential development than the China new-equipment decline that dominates most discussion of the name.

The diagnostic is unusually clean. On July 22 management cut FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $4.01–$4.05 from $4.20–$4.24 while leaving net sales at $15.1–15.3B and organic growth at low-to-mid single digits, with CEO Judy Marks stating that the sales outlook and market expectations remain unchanged. Demand is not the problem: Service organic growth of 9% in Q2 was the strongest since the 2020 separation, modernization organic revenue accelerated to +24%, modernization backlog was up roughly 30% at constant currency, and remaining performance obligations stood at $19.7B with about 75% expected to convert within 24 months. The problem is field labor cost, mechanic productivity ramp, materials, tariffs and internally generated "organizational initiative" costs. Cost problems are generally more fixable than demand problems, but the fix depends entirely on execution, and the guidance record now argues against granting management the benefit of the doubt. The $4.01–$4.05 guide implies -1% to 0% adjusted EPS growth against a $3.01 (2021) → $4.05 (2025) progression that compounded at 7.7%.

At $73.99 the shares trade at 18.4x the FY2026 guide midpoint of $4.03, 16.1x next-year consensus of $4.61, roughly 15.3x EV/EBITDA, and a 5.7–5.9% adjusted free-cash-flow yield on the $1.60–1.65B guide, against a peer-median P/E of 27.7x. That discount is real but it is not self-executing: consensus is falling (current year -3.2% over 30 days, next year -2.6%), three of the last four quarters missed on EPS, the UpLift program is complete and no longer available as a margin lever, roughly $3.5B of sub-3% debt reprices between 2027 and 2031, and the $2.0B buyback authorization has only $500M remaining with zero shares repurchased in June. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on the name and the score moves down modestly, to 5.4 from 5.7, because the Q2 print converted a one-quarter margin question into a two-quarter one while estimates continued to fall on a roughly flat share price. The valuation is supportive enough to keep existing holders in the stock and the demand book is at records; neither is sufficient to commit new capital before the FY2027 guide.

## Company Overview

Otis was separated from United Technologies on April 3, 2020 and operates two reported segments under a centralized global model.

**New Equipment** (35% of FY2025 sales, 9% of segment profit) designs, manufactures and installs elevators, escalators and moving walkways for developers, general contractors and government infrastructure customers. Platforms include Gen2 (over one million units sold since 2000), Gen3, Gen360 and SkyRise.

**Service** (65% of sales, 91% of segment profit) performs maintenance, repair and modernization on both Otis and third-party equipment. The strategic logic is a conversion funnel: new installations become service contracts, which generate decades of maintenance revenue and eventually modernization revenue.

Scale metrics from the FY2025 10-K: a ~2.5 million-unit maintenance portfolio described by the company as the industry's largest; ~1.1 million connected units under Otis ONE; ~37,000 service mechanics within ~45,000 field professionals and ~72,000 total employees; presence in more than 200 countries with direct operations in more than 70 and over 1,400 branches; 16 factories, 11 R&D centers, ~4,600 issued patents. Approximately 71% of FY2025 sales were international, with employees split 45% Asia, 34% EMEA, 21% Americas. No single customer or service contract is material. Sixty-four percent of the U.S. workforce is covered by collective bargaining, with the principal U.S. agreement expiring in July 2027.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue mix has shifted decisively toward Service.**

| ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 14,209 | 14,261 | 14,431 |
| New Equipment | 5,812 | 5,367 | 4,989 |
| Service | 8,397 | 8,894 | 9,442 |
| Operating profit | 2,186 | 2,008 | 2,133 |
| Operating margin | 15.4% | 14.1% | 14.8% |
| Diluted shares (M) | 414.6 | 404.4 | 394.9 |

Consolidated revenue compounded at roughly 1.8% annually from FY2022's $13,685M to FY2025's $14,431M. Underneath, New Equipment contracted 14% cumulatively over three years while Service grew every year. FY2025 New Equipment organic sales fell 7%, driven by a greater-than-20% organic decline in China for the second consecutive year.

**Segment margins are the decisive datapoint.**

| Segment operating margin | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New Equipment | 6.6% | 6.1% | 4.8% | 3.3% | 3.1% |
| Service | 24.0% | 24.6% | 25.1% | 23.0% | 23.2% |

New Equipment at 3.1% is the weakest post-spin reading available, attributed to lower volume, unfavorable price, regional and product mix, and higher organizational-initiative costs. The Service reversal matters more. The mechanism management describes is front-loaded: hire and retain mechanics to service a record modernization backlog, absorb the cost now, harvest the revenue later. The competing reading is that Otis is under-pricing structural field-labor inflation and that new mechanics reach productivity more slowly than the plan assumes. Both are consistent with the disclosed facts. In Q1 specifically, Service operating profit was down $10M at constant currency on +5% organic sales, which is the datapoint most difficult to reconcile with a pure timing explanation.

**Guidance.** April 22: sales $15.1–15.3B, adjusted operating profit ~$2.5B, adjusted EPS $4.20–$4.24, adjusted FCF $1.60–1.65B. July 22: sales range unchanged, adjusted EPS $4.01–$4.05. The EPS midpoint fell $0.19, or 4.5%, with no change to the top line. Note a verification limitation: the characterization of July as the *second* consecutive cut rests on secondary reporting; the original January 2026 adjusted EPS guide was not independently confirmed. What is confirmed is one cut of 4.5% between April and July, and that the resulting guide implies the first stall in adjusted EPS since the separation.

**Cash flow improved, with two adjustments required before extrapolating.** 1H26 operating cash flow was $680M against $405M, and capex of $77M produced $603M of reported free cash flow versus $335M. Roughly $126M of that was German tax litigation refunds received in Q1, which management itself excludes from adjusted FCF (Q1 adjusted FCF $272M versus reported $380M), and 1H26 carried a $284M inflow from current contract assets and liabilities. Stripping the refunds gives approximately $477M of cleaner 1H FCF against the $1.60–1.65B full-year guide, implying $1.1–1.2B in H2. That is demanding but consistent with the historical seasonal pattern — FY2025 delivered $1,444M of FCF on $1,596M of OCF — and it is the guidance line most worth stress-testing. Cash quality is supported by supplier-finance obligations *declining* from $831M at year-end to $716M at June 30, so the cash improvement was not manufactured by leaning harder on payables, though $98M of that sits under 240-day payment terms.

**Balance sheet: leveraged, solvent, and structurally misread by screens.** Reported shareholders' equity was -$5,748M at June 30, decomposing into treasury stock of -$5,005M, accumulated other comprehensive loss of -$979M, an accumulated deficit of only -$117M and paid-in capital of $353M. The negative equity originates in the 2020 spin capital structure, where debt proceeds went to the former parent, and has since been deepened by buybacks. The accumulated deficit has shrunk from -$2,865M at YE2022. The 0.83 current ratio is similarly a feature: current liabilities of $8,225M include $3,023M of customer contract liabilities — cash prepaid for maintenance not yet performed — and excluding those, current assets of $6,839M cover the remaining $5,202M at about 1.31x.

What does matter: total debt of $8,436M and net debt of $7,623M, up from $6,860M at year-end, with the ~$763M increase funding $807M of buybacks and $193M of acquisitions. Net debt/EBITDA of roughly 3.2x on ~$2,363M of EBITDA; interest coverage near 8.9x; weighted average maturity 6.0 years; weighted average long-term rate up to 3.2% from 3.0%. Liquidity is adequate — $813M cash, an undrawn $1.5B revolver to August 2030, an undrawn $1.5B commercial paper program, stated covenant compliance, investment-grade ratings. Interest expense is repricing mechanically upward: the May 7 issuance of $700M at 4.488% due 2029 replaces the €600M 0.318% notes maturing December 15, 2026, adding roughly $29M of pre-tax annual interest or about $0.06 per share after tax. With over $3.5B of sub-3% paper maturing 2027–2031, this is a multi-year headwind independent of operations. Net interest expense already rose to $85M in 1H26 from $71M.

**Earnings quality and delivery.** Three misses in the last four quarters (-1.6%, -1.9%, -2.5%, +3.0%). The misses are small in magnitude but consistent in direction, and they coincide with falling consensus. Q2 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.12 against $0.99 was flattered by a 17.9% effective tax rate and the absence of prior-year restructuring charges; the adjusted comparison is the relevant one, and the Q2 adjusted EPS actual was not obtainable from the sources available. One accounting item to monitor without over-reading: an out-of-period adjustment in Q1 2026 recognizing $114M within equity to correct an immaterial error relating to prior step acquisitions, with disclosure controls concluded effective and no impact on results of operations or cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

The order book is the strongest element of the case and it is genuinely strong. Modernization orders grew 43% year over year in Q4 CY2025 and 11% at constant currency in Q1 2026; modernization backlog was up roughly 30% at constant currency at March 31; modernization organic revenue accelerated to +24% in Q2 and +16% in 1H. Service organic growth of 9% in Q2 was the fastest since the spin. RPO of $19.7B with ~75% converting inside 24 months implies roughly $7.4B per year of RPO-sourced revenue, about half the sales guide. Note that Otis does not define RPO as total backlog and discloses backlog only as growth percentages, so the 30% modernization figure cannot be dollar-sized.

New Equipment is the drag and the regional detail is what matters. Q1 New Equipment orders were +1% at constant currency, with more than 20% growth in the Americas offset by a low-teens decline in China and a greater-than-20% decline in Asia Pacific. China deserves precision. The 10-K states China represents approximately one fifth of global New Equipment sales but more than half of global New Equipment unit volume. That implies roughly $1.0B of China New Equipment revenue — about 7% of consolidated sales — at revenue per unit near one quarter of the non-China average. The revenue drag is therefore smaller than headline China exposure suggests, but the unit exposure is the strategic asset, since each installed unit is the entry point to a multi-decade service annuity. Otis disclosed lower conversion and retention rates in China, which is the more concerning signal than the volume decline itself. The offsetting development is that China is becoming partly a modernization-stimulus market, which is lower-capital and higher service intensity, and Otis bought out the Otis Electric minority partner for roughly $215M to control that pivot.

The growth arithmetic that determines the equity return is straightforward. Revenue grows low-to-mid single digits. Below the line, interest expense rises mechanically, the buyback tailwind of 2–3% is at risk with $500M of authorization remaining, and the UpLift program that delivered ~$200M of run-rate savings for ~$300M of cost is complete, with the 10-Q stating no similar costs are expected going forward. That removes both a headwind and a lever. Consequently every dollar of future EPS growth must come from Service pricing and mechanic productivity — precisely the variable that has moved the wrong way for two quarters. Next-year consensus of $4.61 implies 14% growth off the 2026 midpoint, which requires a Service margin recovery of roughly 100–150bp. That is achievable but it is not yet evidenced, and consensus has been cutting rather than raising.

Growth prospects score 5.0: strong volume and order momentum, no earnings growth, and negative revision momentum across every measured period.

## Valuation Assessment

At $73.99 (380.7M shares, ~$28.2B market cap, EV approximately $36.1B including $7.62B net debt and $294M of noncontrolling interests):

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 guide midpoint ($4.03) | 18.4x |
| P/E on next-year consensus ($4.61) | 16.1x |
| P/E on FY2025 GAAP EPS ($3.505) | 21.1x |
| EV/FY2025 EBITDA (~$2,363M) | ~15.3x |
| EV/FY2025 EBIT | ~16.9x |
| Adjusted FCF yield (guide $1.60–1.65B / mkt cap) | 5.7–5.9% |
| Dividend yield ($1.76 annualized) | 2.38% |
| Peer median P/E | 27.7x |

The headline gap to peers — DOV 27.3x, XYL 27.1x, HUBB 27.7x, IR 49.6x, CRS 59.2x — is 34%. Some of that discount is earned. Otis is delivering 0% adjusted EPS growth this year against peers with mid-single-digit-or-better earnings trajectories, it carries 3.2x net leverage against a negative book equity base, and roughly 7% of sales sits in a Chinese new-equipment market contracting more than 20% annually. Applying peer multiples to Otis without adjusting for that would be careless. A cheap multiple on a decelerating earnings base is the classic setup where cheapness fails to convert.

What supports the shares is the composition of the discount. The de-rating from roughly 22x forward at the February high to 18.4x now, against a 4.5% EPS guidance cut, means the market has taken about 16 points of multiple out for a 4.5% earnings reduction. That is the market pricing a durability question about Service margin, not just an earnings reduction. If Service margin returns to the 24.5–25% range on the record modernization backlog, FY2027 EPS of $4.60–$4.80 at 19–20x supports $88–96. If it does not, $4.10–$4.20 at 15–16x supports $62–67. The gap between those outcomes is the whole investment question, and 1H data cannot adjudicate it.

Two further considerations. First, the absolute yield support is genuine: a 5.7–5.9% adjusted FCF yield with a 44% dividend payout and investment-grade ratings limits how far the multiple can compress absent a demand break, and the sales guide was reaffirmed. Second, the analyst distribution is unusually one-sided — the low target of $78 sits *above* the current price, mean $90.5, median $89, high $107, with 2 strong buys, 5 buys, 7 holds and no sells, and sentiment described as improving. On a stock at 18.4x forward with a PEG below 1.5 on consensus, targets clustered above spot are more credible than they would be at a premium multiple. But consensus targets are not independent confirmation when consensus EPS is simultaneously being cut; the same analysts have reduced their next-year numbers 2.6% in 30 days without moving targets commensurately, which suggests target staleness in both directions.

Valuation scores 5.5. The discount to peers and the absolute cash yield are real, but there is no dated catalyst forcing a re-rating and the earnings base underneath the multiple is still being revised down. Cheapness here is a reason to keep owning, not a reason to buy.

**Base case: $82** — 18.5x an FY2027 adjusted EPS estimate of $4.45, which haircuts consensus $4.61 by about 3.5% to reflect the guidance track record and the incremental interest step-up. That is +10.8% from $73.99, plus a 2.38% dividend, for roughly 13% total return over 12 months. **Bull: $96** (20x on $4.80 with Service margin restored above 24.5%), +29.7%. **Bear: $64** (15.5x on $4.15 as compression proves structural and the buyback tailwind lapses), -13.5%. Probability-weighting 25/45/30 gives approximately +8% price return plus dividend, roughly a fair-to-slightly-positive risk-adjusted outcome.

## Competitive Landscape

Otis competes with KONE, Schindler and TK Elevator, but the disclosure that reframes the moat is the 10-K statement that independent service providers hold approximately 50% of service units in the aggregate industry portfolio, though a smaller share by value. The Service segment, where all the profit sits, is a fragmented, locally contested market rather than a four-firm oligopoly at the unit level. The OEMs' advantages are parts logistics scale, technical data on their own installed base, brand with building owners, and increasingly digital capability — 1.1 million of 2.5 million portfolio units are connected (44%), and Otis ONE subscription revenue grew 35% in 2025 per management commentary.

This context explains two capital-allocation patterns. The routine bolt-on acquisitions of local service providers ($193M in 1H26 versus $82M in 1H25, mostly Service) are consolidation against a fragmented base. The WeMaintain purchase — majority ownership of a French provider described by management as digitally native and AI-enabled, for $170M with $142M of goodwill, $64M of intangibles and $33M of redeemable noncontrolling interest — is Otis buying a disruptor's capability rather than only adding units. R&D at $152M in FY2025 is about 1.0% of sales, so Otis is deploying digital capability incrementally rather than out-spending competitors toward it.

The single most valuable unresolved item in this file is whether peers are experiencing comparable service-margin pressure. KONE, Schindler and TK Elevator 1H2026 disclosures were not obtainable. If peers are also compressing, the cause is field-labor inflation flowing through the sector with a pricing lag and 18.4x forward on a temporarily depressed base is likely a mispricing. If peers are expanding service margins while Otis contracts, the problem is execution and the multiple deserves to stay lower. The circumstantial evidence cuts both ways: Otis's stated causes are generic and cost-side (wages, materials, tariffs, mix), it held its sales outlook, and orders are at records — all consistent with sector-wide pressure. Against that, Otis repeatedly cites "higher costs resulting from organizational initiatives," which points to internally generated cost, and it does so immediately after declaring a $300M transformation program complete and after CFO commentary that the service model would continue to support margin expansion. Guidance reduced on internal cost right after a transformation was declared finished is a credibility issue independent of what peers report.

Competitive position scores 6.0, down from prior assessments. The portfolio scale and connected-unit lead are intact and the modernization order book confirms Otis is winning work. The trajectory is what moves: New Equipment unit share in the largest global market is eroding with lower conversion and retention, and Service margin is compressing against an unverified peer baseline.

## Risk Assessment

**Service margin durability.** The primary risk is that the 200bp compression reflects structural mispricing of field labor rather than front-loaded investment. This intersects with the July 2027 expiry of the principal U.S. collective bargaining agreement covering a 64%-unionized U.S. workforce, which is a dateable negotiation over exactly the cost line currently causing the problem.

**China unit erosion.** Roughly 7% of consolidated sales, but more than half of global New Equipment unit volume, declining more than 20% organically for three consecutive periods with orders still falling and disclosed weakness in conversion and retention. The revenue effect is contained; the long-run service-funnel effect is not, and it will not appear in reported results for years.

**Uncapped RTX indemnity.** The separation agreements' indemnification obligations to RTX and Carrier are explicitly not subject to any cap. Otis and RTX openly disagree about both the scope of the indemnity payable and Otis's liability for interest accruing on amounts already paid, in the context of a German tax matter where Otis expects roughly €313M net of tax in refunds. The indemnity payable held at $55M through Q2 and separation-related adjustment expense is declining ($177M FY2024, $70M FY2025, $5M 1H26), but the dispute is unresolved and the obligation is unbounded.

**Unsized European cartel claims.** Otis discloses accrued overcharge claims in certain European countries with no amount or range, stating historical settlement experience has not been material. This is an open tail with no sizing, and negative book equity means there is no accounting buffer for an adverse outcome.

**Tariffs and shipment disruption.** FY2025 tariff impact was approximately $20M with similar expected in 2026, but the Q2 10-Q escalated the language to note that new 2025 and 2026 import tariffs could have a material impact on 2026 and future years. The Q1 release separately cited Middle East geopolitical shipment delays in New Equipment and modernization — a live exposure given continued disruption in the region.

**Interest repricing and buyback runway.** Roughly $3.5B of sub-3% debt matures 2027–2031, with the first replacement already costing about $0.06 per share annually. The buyback that reduced diluted shares 2.9% has $500M of authorization remaining after $807M was spent in 1H26 at an average near $83, roughly 12% above the current price, and no shares were repurchased in June. A June pause with $500M remaining is fully explicable by ordinary board re-approval timing and should not be read as a management valuation signal, but absent a new authorization the 2–3% EPS tailwind narrows.

Risk factors score 5.0. The China decline and the margin compression are priced — that is what the 21.8% drawdown represents. The uncapped indemnity, the unsized cartel claims, the 2027 labor negotiation and the multi-year interest step-up are less visible in the current multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.2% against 21.3% one-year realized is rich by 4.9 points, but that sits at only the 33rd percentile within the current chain and the term structure is in contango (26.2% at 12 days versus 22.7% at 40 days and 29.1% at 131 days). Contango with modest near-term richness is a complacent structure, not a stress signal. The nearest expiry prices a ±4.3% move with no earnings event inside it. Put/call skew of -11.7% is characterized as call-heavy, and open-interest positioning has shifted meaningfully in the same direction: the put/call open-interest ratio fell from 1.12 on May 27 to 0.57 now while the price rose 2.7%. That is the options market reducing downside protection into the guidance cut rather than accumulating it — a mild divergence from the falling-estimate picture, and a point of corroboration for the view that the market has largely finished repricing the margin problem. The December tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 8.67, but across only 85 contracts in the whole chain that reading is too thin to interpret. Short interest of 4.3% of float and 3.8 days to cover indicates no crowded bearish position.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($96, +30%)

Demand is confirmed and management has already told the market it will not cut the top line: sales guidance of $15.1–15.3B and low-to-mid-single-digit organic growth survived both the April and July revisions. Service organic growth of 9% is the strongest since the spin, modernization organic revenue is accelerating (+24% in Q2), modernization backlog is up roughly 30% at constant currency, and RPO of $19.7B converts about 75% within two years. The margin compression is the cost of hiring and retaining mechanics ahead of that backlog; as those mechanics reach productivity, incremental Service margin normalizes toward the 23–25% band and reported margin recovers 100–150bp. FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.80 at 20x, a still-material discount to a 27.7x peer median, supports $96. Cash generation underwrites the wait: a 5.7–5.9% adjusted FCF yield, a dividend raised to $0.44 quarterly at a 44% payout, investment-grade ratings, $3B of undrawn committed liquidity, and supplier-finance balances falling rather than rising. Every analyst target sits above the current price, short interest is 4.3%, and options positioning has shifted away from downside protection.

### Bear Case ($64, -13.5%)

The Service margin reversal is structural, not transitional. Constant-currency Service profit growth of roughly $6M on approximately $315M of organic revenue growth — and an outright $10M constant-currency decline in Q1 on +5% organic sales — is difficult to reconcile with a pure hiring-timing explanation. If field labor inflation is running ahead of contractual price escalators, the fix requires multi-year repricing across a 2.5-million-unit portfolio contested by independents holding roughly half of industry service units, and it arrives into a July 2027 U.S. labor negotiation. Meanwhile every offsetting lever is closing: UpLift is complete with no further savings expected, interest expense is repricing upward on $3.5B of maturing cheap debt, and the buyback has $500M of authorization left after being executed at roughly $83 average. Guidance credibility is impaired — EPS cut 4.5% on internal cost immediately after declaring a $300M transformation finished, three EPS misses in four quarters, consensus down 3.2% in 30 days for the current year. China continues to shrink more than half of global unit volume with disclosed conversion and retention deterioration, degrading the future service funnel invisibly. On $4.15 of FY2027 EPS at 15.5x, the shares are worth $64, roughly 7% below the 52-week low of $69.16.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD. Hold rationale: uncertain.** The decisive variable — whether Service margin compression is transitory or structural — cannot be resolved from the data available, and the peer margin comparison that would resolve it was not obtainable. Expected return is approximately fair: probability-weighted upside of roughly 8% on price plus a 2.38% dividend, against a bear case 13.5% below spot.

- **Base case: $82** — 18.5x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.45 (consensus $4.61 haircut ~3.5% for guidance record and the ~$0.06 interest step-up). +10.8% from $73.99, plus dividend ≈ 13% total return.
- **Bull case: $96** — 20x on $4.80 with Service margin above 24.5%. +29.7%.
- **Bear case: $64** — 15.5x on $4.15 with compression persisting and the buyback lapsing. -13.5%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the two decision points being the Q3 2026 print in late October (first evidence of H2 margin trajectory and the H2 free-cash-flow ramp) and the FY2027 initial guide in late January 2027.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Service segment operating margin recovering to 24.0% or better in a reported quarter, with constant-currency Service operating profit growth of at least 10% year over year — the specific metric that has failed for two quarters.
2. FY2027 initial adjusted EPS guidance of $4.55 or higher, which would restore the pre-2026 growth trajectory and validate the transitory reading.
3. Evidence that KONE and Schindler are reporting comparable 1H26 service-margin compression, establishing the pressure as sector-wide labor inflation with a pricing lag rather than Otis execution.
4. A new buyback authorization of $1.5B or more announced alongside resumed repurchases at prices below $80, which would restore the 2–3% share-count tailwind and signal board conviction at current levels.
5. Price below $67 with fundamentals unchanged, which on the base case would put the discount to $82 above 20% and improve the risk-adjusted return enough to justify staging in.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. A third reduction to FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance, or an FY2027 guide below $4.30, which would make the earnings stall a two-year event rather than a one-year one.
2. Service segment margin falling below 23.0% in Q3 or Q4 2026, indicating compression is deepening rather than stabilizing.
3. FY2026 adjusted free cash flow tracking below $1.50B, which would break the 5.7–5.9% yield that currently anchors valuation, or evidence that the H2 ramp is being met through payables extension (supplier-finance obligations rising back above $831M).
4. A quantified adverse development on the RTX indemnity or European cartel claims exceeding $300M, given there is no book-equity buffer.
5. New Equipment orders declining at constant currency in consecutive quarters, which would remove the "cost problem, not demand problem" framing that underpins the base case.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release in late October 2026.

## Conclusion

Otis presents a specific, testable question rather than a general one. The company's entire equity story rests on a Service franchise that produces 91% of segment profit at a mid-20s margin, and that margin has compressed roughly 200bp over two quarters while generating approximately $6M of constant-currency profit on $315M of organic revenue growth. Management attributes this to deliberate front-loaded investment in mechanic capacity ahead of a modernization backlog up roughly 30% at constant currency, and the demand evidence supporting that framing is strong: sales guidance untouched through two revisions, Service organic growth at a post-spin high of 9%, RPO of $19.7B. The competing explanation — that field labor inflation is outrunning contractual pricing across a portfolio contested by independents holding half of industry service units — is equally consistent with the disclosed data and cannot be ruled out from 1H results.

Valuation reflects that uncertainty rather than resolving it. At 18.4x the FY2026 guide, 16.1x next-year consensus, 15.3x EV/EBITDA and a 5.7–5.9% adjusted free-cash-flow yield against a 27.7x peer median, the shares are priced for the margin problem to persist for at least another year.
