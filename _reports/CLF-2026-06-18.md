**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cleveland-Cliffs is the largest flat-rolled and automotive-grade steel producer in North America, a vertically integrated, blast-furnace-based operator that self-supplies the majority of its iron ore. After a deeply negative 2025 — a $1.43B net loss, Adjusted EBITDA of just $37M, and roughly $1.0B of negative free cash flow — the company posted a genuine first-quarter 2026 improvement: revenue of $4.92B (+6% YoY), Adjusted EBITDA swinging to +$95M, and the net loss narrowing to $(229)M. The swing is almost entirely a hot-rolled-coil (HRC) price story. HRC averaged $980/ton in Q1 2026, up 24% YoY, and the expiry of a chronically loss-making ArcelorMittal slab supply contract removed roughly 10% of unprofitable volume. The business is a levered call option on two variables management does not control: U.S. HRC prices and the durability of the administration's 50% Section 232 steel tariffs.

The improvement has not repaired the balance sheet. Long-term debt rose again in Q1 to $7.76B, the ABL was re-drawn to $959M from $452M, cash fell to $45M, and operating cash flow was still negative $(325)M even with HRC up 24%. The $951M equity raise in October 2025 (75M new shares, a ~15% increase in share count) repaid the ABL only for it to be re-drawn within two quarters. Deleveraging is happening through dilution rather than cash generation. The Altman Z-score sits at 1.81, inside the distress-zone boundary, and the 10-K raises the going-concern hypothetical conditioned on cash generation and refinancing. The favorable maturity profile (no maturities until 2029, ~$3.1B liquidity) is the reason this does not imply imminent solvency risk, but it does not change the trajectory of cash burn.

Two further signals push this to an AVOID rather than a hold. First, the stock has appreciated 65% off its 52-week low ($6.72) to $12.68, running directly into the strongest part of the tariff-and-price tailwind, which compresses forward asymmetry. Second, management's behavior is a clear caution: CEO Lourenco Goncalves sold his entire 3,000,000-share direct stake in February 2026 (~$37.3M), with the CFO and COO also selling, against a backdrop of $19.0M of CEO comp in a year of a $1.43B loss and a cumulative TSR (91.2 indexed) running roughly 3.5x worse than the steel peer group (324.8). The contingent catalysts — a non-binding POSCO MOU and a J.P. Morgan-led asset sale process, both aimed at debt reduction — are real but unsigned, and the equity already prices their optionality plus continued HRC strength.

## Company Overview

Cleveland-Cliffs operates one reportable segment, Steelmaking (~97% of revenue), with "Other Businesses" comprising Tubular, Tooling & Stamping, and European operations. It is uniquely integrated for a U.S. producer: iron ore mining and pelletizing in Michigan and Minnesota, a direct-reduced-iron (HBI) plant in Toledo, ferrous scrap processing, primary blast-furnace/BOF steelmaking, and downstream finishing, stamping, tooling and tubing. The company employs ~25,000 people, with more than 90% of its ~20,000 hourly workers unionized.

The franchise was assembled through aggressive M&A: AK Steel (2020), ArcelorMittal USA (2020), and Stelco (November 2024). The Stelco deal nearly doubled long-term debt to fund roughly $2.5B of cash consideration. Through 2025 the footprint was substantially reshaped: Steelton, Conshohocken and Riverdale were closed, Dearborn's blast furnace/BOF was idled, and Minorca plus partial Hibbing iron ore operations were idled. A historically unprofitable ~1.5M-ton/yr ArcelorMittal slab supply contract expired in December 2025 and was not renewed, a structural margin positive from 2026 onward.

The competitive differentiator worth taking seriously is captive iron ore. Cliffs sources the majority of its iron feedstock internally at relatively stable cost, while EAF competitors (Nucor, Steel Dynamics) depend on volatile, increasingly scarce prime scrap and pig iron. Busheling scrap averaged roughly $424–434/lt recently, above the ~$385–400 historical average, which improves Cliffs' relative cost position in a tight-scrap regime. The automotive franchise (roughly $5B of direct auto revenue, with 40–45% of flat-rolled shipments on fixed-price contracts) dampens spot volatility and carries higher through-cycle margins.

FY2025 was a trough: Steelmaking segment EBITDA was negative $16M, negative operating cash flow of $462M, and negative $1.0B free cash flow. The drivers were subdued demand (autos in "recession-like conditions" per management), product-mix-driven lower realized prices, $300M of higher D&A, $239M of idled-facility charges, a full year of Stelco costs, and higher interest. Interest expense rose to ~$594M in 2025 from ~$289M in 2023 as debt doubled.

Q1 2026 was a real improvement on the income statement: Steelmaking segment EBITDA was +$80M, and EPS was $(0.42) versus $(1.01) in Q1 2025 (Adjusted EBITDA of −$179M in the prior-year quarter). Gross margin improved $310M YoY on higher HRC pricing and the favorable post-slab-contract mix.

The cash story did not follow. The negative Q1 operating cash flow was driven by a $441M accounts-receivable build as prices rose. The retained deficit widened to $(766)M. The October 2025 equity raise repaid the ABL, but within two quarters the ABL was back near its drawn level and total debt rose. Deleveraging is occurring through share issuance, not cash flow.

Financial-health screens corroborate the stress: Altman Z = 1.81 (distress-zone boundary), partial Piotroski F = 2/5, ROE −23%, ROA −7%, D/E 2.24. The current ratio of 1.95 is the bright spot. The one genuine mitigant is the maturity profile: no maturities until 2029 ($1.27B), the ABL maturing in 2028, roughly $3.1B of liquidity, and ~$3.2B of secured-note capacity. This long runway is why a Z-score near 1.8 does not translate to near-term solvency risk. It does not change the fact that the company burned cash even in a recovering quarter.

## Growth Analysis

Forward earnings momentum is positive. EPS estimate revisions are broadly rising: current-year consensus improved to $(0.36) from $(0.45) thirty days ago (+20.2% revision), next-quarter consensus moved to +$0.18 from $(0.01) ninety days ago, and next-year consensus sits at +$0.50. The Q1 improvement and HRC strength are pulling estimates up, and the slab-contract roll-off plus continued footprint rationalization (Q2 2026 idling of the Gary plate finishing line and consolidation of Burns Harbor plate to one mill) support sequentially better margins.

The substance of this "growth," however, is cyclical recovery off a trough rather than secular expansion. Revenue has declined every year since 2022 ($22.99B → $18.61B), and the 2026 improvement is a price-driven rebound, not volume growth or new-market penetration. The genuine secular pocket is electrical steel (GOES/NOES), where Cliffs is one of few U.S. producers, leveraged to grid-transformer shortages and AI/data-center electricity demand, consistent with the broad data-center power buildout. That is a minority of the portfolio inside an otherwise cyclical, automotive-and-construction-driven business.

The forward consensus turning positive ($0.50 next year) is contingent on HRC holding near current levels and tariffs remaining intact. The earnings record undercuts confidence in those estimates: three misses in the last four quarters, including a 21.4% miss in Q1 2026 and a 29.7% miss in Q2 2025. Estimate revisions are rising, but on a name with weak delivery visibility, the revision magnitude should be discounted, and the +30%-type revision velocity that appears to signal a clean inflection here is partly an artifact of estimates crossing through zero (small-denominator effects produce the +1,553.8% and −695.1% prints), not a reliable indicator of durable acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

CLF trades at $12.68 with negative trailing and forward P/E (EPS −$2.90 trailing; consensus −$0.36 current year), so earnings multiples are not meaningful at this point in the cycle. The relevant frame is normalized earnings power and EV. Market cap is ~$7.0B; against $45M of cash, enterprise value is roughly $14.7B. On the consensus next-year EPS of $0.50, the stock trades at ~25x a still-depressed earnings figure that itself assumes HRC strength persists.

Against steel peers, the picture is mixed. The peer median P/E is 18.1x (CMC 17.4x, WS 18.1x), but those names are profitable; CLF is not, which is why the equity is the high-beta laggard. The peer-relative signal that matters is the cumulative TSR comparison disclosed in the proxy (CLF indexed to 91.2 versus a peer group at 324.8), reflecting the market correctly pricing CLF as the leveraged, lower-margin blast-furnace operator in a peer set with healthier balance sheets.

The analyst target range of $8.00 (low) to $15.00 (high), with an $11.70 mean and $11.50 median, places the current price above the consensus mean and median. On an inflection name with a compressed multiple, targets can lag reality, but that principle applies to cheap stocks with clean balance sheets, not to a name carrying $7.8B of debt where the mean target sits below the current price. The consensus is not a stale floor; it reflects a fair read of contingent upside already partially captured in the rally off the low.

The temptation is to score cheapness highly because the stock looks like a coiled cyclical recovery. That is the value-trap profile to be skeptical of: a low or absent multiple paired with structural impairment (negative cash generation, dilution-based deleveraging) and no de-risked catalyst. The catalysts that would justify a higher valuation — a signed POSCO deal, completed asset sales, sustained HRC above $980 — are real but unconfirmed, and the equity already prices them as base case. Valuation scores a 4.0 because the asymmetry has compressed after the run and the cheapness is contingent on outcomes outside management's control, not because the multiple is high.

## Competitive Landscape

Cliffs' competitive position is the strongest dimension of the story and is improving at the margin. The structural setup favors integrated U.S. producers: 50% Section 232 tariffs, 25% auto tariffs, below-historical import levels, and tight scrap markets. Management's framing — that the U.S. is the only major steel-producing country that consumes more than it produces — is directionally accurate. In a high-scrap-price regime, rising scrap costs help Cliffs' relative position because it self-supplies iron ore, while EAF peers face margin pressure. That counter-positioning is underappreciated.

The automotive franchise, built over decades and protected by fixed-price annual/multi-year contracts on 40–45% of flat-rolled volume, is a durable moat. Electrical steel adds a secular growth pocket tied to grid and data-center electrification. The footprint rationalization through 2025 and the slab-contract roll-off improve the cost structure going forward.

The qualifier is that competitive strength does not equate to financial strength. Cliffs is the leveraged, high-beta operator in its peer set, and the relative TSR underperformance reflects that. Auto competition is intensifying at contract-renewal time, North American light-vehicle production (15.3M units) remains well below the pre-COVID ~17M, and the Canadian Stelco operations weakened 2025 results. Competitive position scores a 6.0: improving trajectory and a real moat, capped by the financial fragility that makes the company unable to convert that position into cash through the cycle.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by leverage, commodity sensitivity, and policy contingency, and several risks are not fully reflected at the current price after the rally.

**Leverage and negative cash generation.** $7.8B of debt against a business that burned $1.0B of FCF in 2025 and another $325M of operating cash in a recovering Q1 2026. The recovery thesis depends entirely on sustained higher HRC; the 10-K's financial-risk section raises the going-concern hypothetical conditioned on cash generation and refinancing. The 2029 maturity wall and liquidity push the risk out, but the runway does not solve the burn.

**Commodity and policy concentration.** The entire 2025→2026 swing is an HRC story ($851 avg 2025 → $980 Q1 2026). The thesis leans heavily on the 50% Section 232 tariffs. The USMCA mandatory joint review begins in 2026, a concrete, dated risk event. If tariffs are weakened, exempted, or removed, imports rise and U.S./Canadian prices fall, a direct downside catalyst on a business with both operating and financial leverage. Few large-cap industrials are this explicitly dependent on a specific administration's trade posture.

**Governance and alignment.** CEO Goncalves sold his entire 3,000,000-share direct stake in February 2026 (~$37.3M), reducing direct holdings to zero, alongside COO (200,000 shares) and CFO (214,308 shares) sales. This is clustered C-suite selling, not isolated diversification, and the CEO selling 100% of direct holdings is a materially bearish signal even setting aside the routine-sale caveat. The only open-market buy was a small ~$200K director purchase. CEO comp of $19.0M in a $1.4B-loss year, against TSR 3.5x worse than peers, compounds the alignment concern. The CFO is the CEO's son, a related-family governance consideration. Two Item 5.02 officer/director-departure 8-Ks (Nov 2025, Feb 2026) warrant monitoring.

**Accounting item.** A Q3 2025-identified "immaterial error" understated COGS across interim 2025 and FY2022–2024. Management deemed it immaterial, did not restate, and concluded disclosure controls effective. Small, but the kind of item to watch given the acquisition-heavy history.

**Offsetting optionality.** The non-binding POSCO MOU explicitly contemplates "substantial proceeds that could be used to reduce indebtedness," and the J.P. Morgan-led asset-sale process targets debt paydown. Both are unsigned. The MinnTac 25% purchase option ($500M, exercisable to Jan 2027) and early-stage rare-earths exploration add speculative optionality.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is flashing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 199.5% against 68.8% one-year historical vol — richly priced, in the 94th percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in steep backwardation (199.5% near vs. 71.5% long-dated), which the market uses to price an imminent event. ATM IV jumped 159.8pp since the prior snapshot even as price rose 18.6%, indicating rising uncertainty rather than complacency. The put/call skew is roughly balanced (−1.6%) and the put/call OI ratio is low (0.27), so positioning is not one-sidedly bearish, but the 14.5% short interest (81.98M shares, 5.0 days to cover) confirms meaningful institutional skepticism. For a long thesis the backwardation and elevated near-term IV argue against chasing into the next catalyst; for the AVOID thesis they corroborate that the market expects volatility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** HRC holds at or above current levels and tariffs remain intact through the USMCA review, sustaining the Q1 margin recovery. The slab-contract roll-off, footprint rationalization, and tight-scrap cost advantage compound into positive free cash flow by late 2026. The POSCO partnership signs and/or asset sales close, delivering proceeds that materially cut the debt load and re-rate the equity off its distressed multiple. Electrical-steel demand from grid and data-center electrification provides a secular kicker. In this scenario the leverage works powerfully on the upside and the stock approaches the $15 analyst high.

**Bear case.** Tariffs soften or carve-outs expand under the 2026 USMCA review, imports rise, and HRC retreats toward 2025 levels. Operating cash flow stays negative, the ABL stays drawn, and the company must dilute further or refinance high-coupon notes (7.0–7.625%) at unfavorable rates as the 2029 wall approaches. The POSCO MOU lapses unsigned. North American auto production stays below 16M units and contract renewals come in weaker. In this scenario operating-plus-financial leverage drives the equity back toward the $8 analyst low, a roughly 37% decline from current levels.

The base case sits between: HRC drifts but does not collapse, deleveraging proceeds slowly through asset sales and dilution rather than cash, and the stock trades in a wide band as it has all year. After the 65% run off the low, the risk/reward from $12.68 is unfavorable: the upside requires multiple contingent positives to land, while the downside requires only one policy or price reversal.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The most concrete dated risk is the USMCA mandatory joint review beginning in 2026, which could weaken or carve out the Section 232 tariff structure the thesis depends on. The Q2 2026 earnings report is the nearer-term catalyst: with operating cash flow still negative in a strong-HRC Q1 and a $441M AR build, a sequential HRC softening or another working-capital drag could disappoint. Neither has a precise date that makes the timing clean.

**Timing window.** The earnings catalyst is roughly one quarter out; the USMCA review is a multi-month 2026 process with no single resolution date. This is a diffuse timeline rather than a sharp event, which raises carry cost relative to payoff.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 14.5% of float — elevated but below the >20–25% threshold where squeeze risk dominates. The crowdedness is meaningful, and the stock's demonstrated capacity for sharp rallies (65% off the low, repeated double-digit single-day moves) creates real squeeze risk on any positive HRC, tariff, or POSCO headline.

**Borrow and options signal.** The steep IV backwardation makes options expression expensive and reflects the market already pricing imminent volatility. Skew is balanced rather than confirming a directional bearish lean, which is a warning sign for new shorts: the options market is not paying you to be bearish.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A tariff/HRC reversal points toward the $8 analyst low, roughly 37% downside. Upside risk from a POSCO signing, asset-sale proceeds, or an HRC squeeze is comparable in magnitude given the leverage and the rally momentum. That produces a reward:risk closer to 1:1 than the 2:1 required, with elevated squeeze risk and expensive options.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental case is weak and the AVOID is well-supported, but the catalysts are diffuse, short interest is already elevated, the stock has proven its ability to rally violently on policy and price headlines, and options are richly priced. This is a name to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID. The current price embeds the favorable HRC-and-tariff environment plus optionality on unsigned strategic catalysts, leaving unattractive forward asymmetry after the rally.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- **Signed deleveraging event.** A definitive POSCO transaction or completed asset sale that durably reduces net debt below ~$6.0B from cash proceeds (not dilution) would materially de-risk the balance sheet and warrant a fresh look.
- **Cash-flow inflection.** Two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow with the ABL reduced below $500M, demonstrating the recovery is converting to cash rather than treading water.
- **Valuation reset with intact fundamentals.** A pullback toward the $8–9 area (near the analyst low) with HRC and tariffs intact would restore the cyclical-call asymmetry that is absent at $12.68.
- **Tariff clarity.** A constructive resolution of the 2026 USMCA review that locks in the Section 232 framework would remove the most important policy tail.

**Timeline.** The leverage and cash-generation headwinds persist until either HRC sustains well above $980 for multiple quarters or a cash-funded deleveraging event closes. The USMCA review and the strategic-process outcomes are 2026 events; the contingency will dominate the equity for at least the next 12 months.

## Conclusion

The trajectory has improved — prior scores moved from 3.8 in March to 4.0 in May, and the Q1 2026 results confirm the cyclical bottom was in 2025. The current data validates the operational improvement (HRC +24%, Adjusted EBITDA positive, slab contract gone) but does not support upgrading the recommendation. The balance sheet got worse, not better, in Q1: debt rose to $7.76B, the ABL was re-drawn, cash fell to $45M, and operating cash flow stayed negative even with prices up sharply. Deleveraging via dilution is not deleveraging in any durable sense, and the CEO selling his entire direct stake into the rally is a signal that deserves weight.

This is a high-beta, levered call on HRC prices and tariff policy, with real competitive differentiation (captive ore, automotive franchise, electrical steel) undercut by a stressed balance sheet (Z = 1.81), three earnings misses in four quarters, and contingent catalysts that are unsigned. After a 65% move off the low into the strongest part of the tailwind, the price already reflects the good news; the asymmetry favors the downside if either tariffs soften under the 2026 USMCA review or HRC rolls over. The recommendation is **AVOID**. Investors seeking cyclical steel exposure have cleaner balance sheets available in the peer set; investors already long should use strength to exit rather than add.
