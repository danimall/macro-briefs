## Executive Summary

Citigroup has delivered the operating inflection it has promised for three years. First-quarter 2026 revenue rose 14% to $24,633M with expenses up 7%, producing 746bps of positive operating leverage, a 58.1% efficiency ratio versus 62.2%, and RoTCE of 13.1% against 9.1% a year earlier. Externally reported second-quarter results — EPS $3.15, net income $5.8B, revenue near $25B, investment banking fees +44%, equities trading +45% — extended the pattern and marked the highest quarterly net revenue in a decade. First-half diluted EPS of $6.21 is already 89% of full-year 2025's $6.99. The franchise underneath this is genuinely hard to replicate: of roughly $1.4 trillion of deposits, approximately $978B are institutional across ~90 countries, ~85% of Services institutional deposits come from clients using at least three integrated products, and ~80% come from relationships longer than fifteen years. Assets under custody and administration reached $31.6 trillion, up 21%.

The problem is that the market has already paid for this. The stock is up 48.6% over twelve months and trades at 1.382x the 3/31/26 tangible book value per share of $99.01 — a multiple that, on a standard warranted-value framework (P/TBV = (RoTCE − g)/(COE − g)), requires sustained RoTCE near 12.7% at a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth. Management reaffirmed, rather than raised, its 10-11% FY2026 RoTCE target after printing ~13% in both quarters. Taking guidance at face value implies second-half RoTCE of roughly 8.0-9.0%, and consensus agrees: Q3 and Q4 estimates of $2.62 and $2.33 sum to $4.95 against $6.21 delivered in 1H, a 20% sequential-half decline. The full-year estimate rose 3.9% over ninety days almost entirely because realized beats were banked while forward quarters were cut 2.0% and 6.5% respectively. That is not forward earnings-power expansion.

Three further items keep us at HOLD rather than BUY. A quantified ~$9B cumulative-translation-adjustment charge from Banamex — approximately $5.40 per share, FX-sensitive, and probably a 2027 event — will pass through reported earnings at deconsolidation while being regulatory-capital and tangible-book neutral. The $30B repurchase authorization, equal to 13.1% of market capitalization, is being executed above tangible book (Q1 at an average $112.00, or 1.13x; the current price is 1.38x) while common equity to assets has fallen from 8.11% to 6.89% in fifteen months and excess CET1 is only about $14.0B. And governance turned noisier: CEO total compensation tripled to $95.76M for FY2025 following four years of TSR underperformance, with no offsetting open-market insider purchases. Base case $152, bear case $105.

## Company Overview

Following a January 2026 reorganization, Citigroup reports five segments plus All Other. Retail Banking moved into Wealth, and the card businesses were consolidated into a new U.S. Consumer Cards segment. Citi simultaneously changed its internal tangible-common-equity allocation and eliminated a corporate-lending revenue-share arrangement, so originally-reported FY2025 segment revenues are not comparable to 1Q26; five years of quarterly segment history were restated in an 8-K furnished April 3, 2026. Within the Q1 10-Q, prior-year comparatives are restated, so the year-over-year figures below are valid.

**Services** ($6,103M in 1Q26, +17%; net income $2,228M, +21%; 48% efficiency) comprises Treasury and Trade Solutions and Securities Services across 90+ countries. **Markets** ($7,246M, +19%; net income $2,595M, +40%; 53% efficiency) trades fixed income and equities from nearly 80 countries. **Banking** ($1,767M, +15%) holds investment banking and corporate lending. **Wealth** ($3,065M, +11%; net income $432M, +126%) spans Citigold, 655 U.S. branches, the Private Bank, and Wealth at Work, with $1,299B of client balances. **U.S. Consumer Cards** ($4,757M, +4%; net income $732M, −13%) holds the Costco and American Airlines co-brands, The Home Depot and other private-label programs, and installment lending. **All Other** houses the divestiture estate: Banamex (1,292 branches), Poland and Korea, Legacy Holdings, and Corporate/Other.

The strategic identity is an institutional network bank — payments, custody, market-making, corporate credit — with a large U.S. card business attached and an emerging-markets consumer estate in managed run-off. Emerging markets still represented roughly 25% of 2025 revenues.

Revenue compounded 19% cumulatively over four years, the efficiency ratio improved 700bps from its 2023 trough, and TBVPS compounded at roughly 5.2% annually. FY2025 carried a $1.2B pretax loss on the Russia disposal and a $726M Banamex goodwill impairment; excluding both, EPS was $7.97.

**Capital and leverage.** At 3/31/26: CET1 12.75% (Standardized, binding) against an 11.6% requirement; Supplementary Leverage Ratio 5.25% against 4.0%; Tier 1 Leverage 6.36%; LCR 114%; TLAC 25.6% of Advanced RWA against 22.5% required. Both Citigroup and Citibank were well capitalized. The 115bp CET1 buffer equates to roughly $14.0B of excess CET1 on $1,214,025M of Standardized RWA — against a $30B multiyear repurchase program, which therefore depends on retained earnings and divestiture capital release rather than existing surplus.

The direction of travel deserves attention. Total assets grew from $2,352,945M (12/31/24) to $2,777,687M (3/31/26), up 18.1% in fifteen months, while common stockholders' equity rose 0.35% to $191,409M. Common equity to assets fell from 8.11% to 7.23% to 6.89%; SLR from 5.85% to 5.48% to 5.25%. The SLR *requirement* fell simultaneously — Citi early-adopted the revised eSLR rule effective January 1, 2026, cutting its total requirement from 5.0% to 4.0% at the holdco and from 6.0% to 4.0% at Citibank. Markets average assets grew 19% to $1,325B and average Markets loans 27% to $162B over the same window. Whether the eSLR relief enabled the balance-sheet expansion or merely removed a non-binding constraint is not determinable from the filings; both readings are consistent with the disclosed facts. Short-term borrowings rose 39% quarter-over-quarter to $72,056M on FHLB advances and structured notes, with HQLA in excess of net outflows of $75.8B and approximately $1.1 trillion of available liquidity resources.

**Quality of the Q1 result.** Three adjustments matter. The effective tax rate fell to 21% from 25% on a disclosed discrete item — roughly $300M of the beat, explicitly not a run-rate. Citi recorded an own-debt valuation adjustment gain of $1,832M in AOCI, which flattered common equity and TBVPS; regulators disallow cumulative own-credit gains, and the CET1 add-back fell $1,488M, accounting for roughly 63% of the $2,370M CET1 decline in the quarter. And tangible common equity *fell* from $169,618M to $168,873M despite $5.8B of net income, because buybacks exceeded retained earnings — mechanically supporting the RoTCE denominator. None of this invalidates the operating improvement (the 746bps of positive operating leverage is real and expense-driven), but the headline 13.1% RoTCE flatters the underlying run-rate by perhaps 75-125bps.

**Capital return.** FY2025 buybacks of $13,250M (5.3x the 2024 figure) plus $4,340M of dividends produced a 133% total payout ratio. Q1 2026: $6.3B of buybacks at an average $112.00 plus $1,057M of dividends, a 134% payout. Shares outstanding fell from 1,747.5M to 1,705.6M, a 2.40% reduction in one quarter, with the supplied 1,677.4M implying a further 1.7%. On April 28, 2026 the Board authorized a new $30B multiyear program; Citi signaled a 12% dividend increase alongside Q2 results. The current 2.01% yield is below the 2.9% peer median — Citi is returning capital predominantly through repurchase.

The repurchase arithmetic cuts both ways. Buying at 1.38x tangible book is EPS-accretive and TBVPS-dilutive. Illustratively, executing the full $30B at $136.87 would retire roughly 219M shares (−12.8% of the 1,705.6M base) and reduce TCE to $138,873M, taking TBVPS to approximately $93.55 before retained earnings — a 5.5% reduction. Retained earnings have so far more than offset this (TBVPS still rose to $99.01 in Q1), but the marginal trade-off worsens as the multiple expands.

**Reported-metric conflicts.** The supplied trailing P/E of 14.88x reconciles neither with the supplied trailing EPS of $6.99 (which implies 19.6x) nor with trailing four-quarter EPS of $10.26 (13.3x). The FY2025 10-K reports revenues net of interest expense of $85,225M and total assets of $2,657,202M; automated extracts circulating at $10.2B and $189.8B respectively are mis-tagged, and any ROA, asset-turnover, or Altman-style distress metric built on them is meaningless. Altman's Z-score excludes financial institutions by construction. FY2025 negative operating cash flow reflects trading-asset growth of roughly $94B — a balance-sheet artifact of market-making, not cash burn. All valuation arithmetic below is computed from the filing figures and the $136.87 price basis.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus FY2026 EPS is $11.15 (up 2.1% in thirty days, 3.9% in ninety) and FY2027 $12.88 (+2.4%/+3.7%). Against FY2025 GAAP EPS of $6.99 that is 59% growth this year and 15.5% next; against the $7.97 ex-notables figure, 40% and 15.5%.

Two qualifications materially reduce the signal value of those revisions.

First, composition. The current-quarter estimate was cut 2.0% to $2.62 and the next quarter 6.5% to $2.33. The annual figures rose because realized 1H beats were banked, not because forward earnings power was marked up. Consensus 2H26 EPS of $4.95 against 1H's $6.21 is a 20% sequential-half decline, consistent with management's implied 8-9% second-half RoTCE. Analysts revised estimates on July 15 specifically after management flagged higher second-half expenses, and the stock fell 4-5% on July 14.

Second, source. A meaningful share of FY2027 EPS growth is share-count reduction rather than earnings growth. If the buyback retires 7-8% of shares over the next four quarters, the $11.15-to-$12.88 progression implies underlying net income growth in the mid-to-high single digits. That is respectable but it is not the 15.5% the EPS line suggests, and the same mechanism reduces tangible book per share.

The organic drivers are mixed by segment. Services deposits grew 16% (TTS +18%) and AUC/AUA 21% — genuine franchise growth with a 48% efficiency ratio. Markets is taking share in capital-intensive financing, with prime balances up more than 50% and Spread Products +27% on commodities. Investment banking fees rose 44% in Q2. Against that: Wealth's net new investment assets decelerated to $14.7B from $16.5B (−11%), with organic growth easing from 8% to 7%, and Wealth's 79% efficiency ratio remains the worst of the five segments. Banking's efficiency ratio worsened to 70% from 68% as expenses grew 20% against 15% revenue growth. Private-label card average active accounts fell 7% and loans 6%, with new account acquisitions down 9%. USCC net income fell 13% despite improving loss rates, because provisions rose 17% on reserve build.

Corporate/Other's drag deepened to −$479M from −$158M, which Citi attributes to "a lower benefit from cash and securities reinvestment, driven by actions taken to reduce Citi's asset sensitivity in a declining interest rate environment." That is a deliberate asset-liability decision made for a rate-cutting world. [unverified — the macro claims that follow (five consecutive Fed holds at 3.50-3.75% with three dissents favoring a hike, the 30-year at its highest since 2007, September-hike odds above 50%) are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data]. The reinvestment drag is a deliberate ALM decision that trades current revenue for reduced rate-down exposure and will keep suppressing consolidated results while rates fall. Offsetting this, the volatility regime that produced 45% equities growth in Q2 is a direct revenue tailwind for Markets, which argues the guided second-half revenue seasonality may prove conservative even if the expense step-up is real.

Peers: BAC 13.7x, PNC 13.6x, WFC 12.7x, TFC 12.5x, USB 12.1x. On forward P/E, Citi is approximately in line with the group and cheaper on FY2027. The question is whether that is a mispricing.

We think largely not, for one reason: Citi's return profile is lower than the group's, and the P/TBV multiple has already closed most of the historical gap. Applying the warranted-multiple identity P/TBV = (RoTCE − g)/(COE − g) at a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth, the current 1.382x requires sustained RoTCE of approximately 12.7%. Management's reaffirmed FY2026 target is 10-11%, which on the same assumptions warrants roughly 1.07-1.14x — a $106-113 stock. The market is therefore capitalizing the 1H26 run-rate of ~13% as durable while management declines to underwrite it. Either management is being conservative (plausible, from a team two years into a credibility rebuild) or the market is extrapolating a quarter helped by a 21% discrete-item tax rate, DVA gains, and a shrinking equity denominator.

The forward P/E discount to peers is also narrower than it looks once the Banamex charge is placed. Consensus of $11.15 and $12.88 does not carry the ~$9B CTA recognition. At approximately $5.40 per share on the current count, recognition in any single quarter would produce a GAAP loss and drive reported RoTCE sharply negative for that period, without touching regulatory capital or tangible book (the loss already sits in AOCI/NCI and is merely recycled through the income statement). Analysts will look through it; index-agnostic capital may not. Because Citi retains 51% and has stated it does not anticipate additional 2026 sales, this is most likely a 2027 event, and the amount moves with the peso.

Two arguments support the current multiple. The $30B authorization is 13.1% of market capitalization — the largest relative repurchase commitment in the peer set, and mechanically supportive of EPS regardless of revenue. And the efficiency ratio has 400-600bps of visible improvement available if Wealth (79%) and Banking (70%) converge toward Services (48%); each 100bps of efficiency on $100B of annualized revenue is roughly $0.45 of after-tax EPS.

Net: fairly valued to modestly undervalued on FY2027 consensus, fully valued on delivered guidance. Analyst mean $154.50 and median $157.50 sit 13-15% above spot, with a low of $129 and high of $176. We do not treat the mean as a floor here — the stock is not cheap relative to its own guided returns, and the analyst distribution has drifted from 13 buys/4 holds ninety days ago to 11 buys/5 holds today. **Valuation: 5.3.**

## Competitive Landscape

Services and Markets together produced $4,823M of the $5,939M of Q1 continuing-operations income — 81%. The Services moat is documented rather than asserted: 90+ country coverage, 43.9M USD clearing instructions in the quarter, $31.6T of AUC/AUA, 85% of institutional Services deposits from clients using three or more integrated products, and 80% from relationships exceeding fifteen years. Multi-product operational deposits with fifteen-year tenures are structurally stickier than rate-shopped retail balances, which is why net interest income rose 12% in Q1 with policy rates falling. No competitor can assemble this network organically on a relevant timeframe.

The competitive weaknesses are equally concrete. Wealth's 79% efficiency ratio, despite improving from 87%, sits far behind Morgan Stanley and Bank of America's wealth franchises, and net new assets are decelerating. Banking's efficiency ratio moved the wrong way. The 64.7% FY2025 consolidated efficiency ratio remains above the diversified-bank cohort.

Card partner concentration is the sharpest structural vulnerability. The FY2025 10-K states that the five largest relationships across both card businesses constituted approximately 12% of 2025 revenues — roughly $10.2B from five renegotiable commercial contracts, with Costco and American Airlines in general purpose and The Home Depot, Best Buy and Macy's in private label. Citi *pays* these partners: FY2025 card rewards and partner payments were $(12,075)M, a contra-revenue line larger than the revenue those five relationships generate. Renewals are therefore high-stakes, and the direction of travel is toward more concentration, not less: the Q1 10-Q discloses a reserve build tied to "Citi's forward purchase commitment of the Barclays American Airlines co-branded card portfolio," with provisions on unfunded lending commitments of $272M versus zero a year earlier, and references guarantees added in the December 2024 extension of the AA partnership within $21.0B of maximum potential future partner payments. Citi is the purchaser and Barclays the seller; the apparent consolidation of the full AA program adds scale while deepening single-counterparty dependence. Purchase price, portfolio size, and exclusivity terms are not disclosed.

The 10-K names private credit, fintechs, digital-asset companies, tokenized deposits under the GENIUS Act, and artificial intelligence as competitive threats, and is unusually candid that "productivity assumptions tied to AI-driven operating model changes may materialize more slowly than expected or require additional unforeseen upfront investment." AI is being positioned as the bridge from 64.7% efficiency toward peer levels — simultaneously the bull-case lever and the risk disclosed in the same document.

**Competitive position: 6.2** — improving trajectory in the two segments that matter most, offset by a worst-in-class wealth cost structure, a shrinking private-label book, and deepening counterparty concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Second-half guidance gap (near-term, unresolved).** 1H26 RoTCE of ~13% against a reaffirmed 10-11% full-year target implies 2H26 of roughly 8.0-9.0% on equal-weighted quarters. Management cited normal seasonality and rising expenses; transformation spend was ~$3.3B in FY2025, up 14%, and the 10-K says it will remain significant in 2026. We cannot adjudicate between genuine expense step-up, Markets seasonality, and conservatism, and we do not infer motive. The Q3 print in October is the resolution point.

**Banamex CTA charge (~$9B, quantified, deferred).** Recognized in earnings at deconsolidation, impacting EPS and RoTCE, and explicitly regulatory-capital neutral. Citi sold 25% in December 2025 and 22.6% in April 2026 at a fixed MXN 43bn price, with 1.4% pending; it will retain 51% and anticipates no further 2026 sales. Interim sales create temporary equity benefits (the 22.6% closing adds ~$1.7B to total stockholders' equity) that reverse at deconsolidation.

**Mexico consumer credit.** Net credit loss rate 4.01% (FY23) → 4.52% (FY24) → 5.56% (FY25) → 6.37% (1Q26); 90+ day delinquencies 1.35% → 1.71%. Loans grew from $24B to $31B year-over-year, roughly 27-30%. Growing a consumer book 30% into a rising loss curve inside an asset being sold is an uncomfortable combination, even accepting management's post-pandemic normalization framing. Mexico is Citi's largest non-U.S. country exposure at $80.8B.

**Corporate credit — reserves ahead of losses.** Corporate exposure grew from $723.1B (12/24) to $838.9B (3/26). Facility risk mix slipped in one quarter (AAA/AA/A 49%→47%, BB/B 20%→21%). Criticized non-performing exposure rose to $2,452M from $1,874M. Banking-segment allowance as a percentage of loans went from 1.42% (12/24) to 2.06% (3/26). Against that, total non-accrual loans *fell* to $3,371M (0.44% of loans), realized corporate net credit losses were $8M in Q1 (0.01% annualized), and reserve coverage of non-accruals rose to 582%. The reading most consistent with both sets of facts is CECL macro-scenario mechanics plus exposure growth, not realized deterioration.

Technology/media/telecom exposure grew from 12% to 15% of the corporate book — $123.7B, of which $85.9B is unfunded. The filings do not link this to AI datacenter financing, so any such connection is hypothesis. It is, however, the line we would monitor most closely given Citi's originate-and-distribute business model: an unfunded commitment that cannot be syndicated becomes a funded balance-sheet exposure. [unverified — specific claims about AI-purpose credit repricing (e.g., CoreWeave loan terms) and datacenter-debt syndication dynamics are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

**Statutory credit card rate cap.** Named by Citi among potential U.S. regulatory changes. USCC net interest income annualized to 12.13% of average loans and total revenue to 11.28% on $171B of average loans. Low probability, high severity, directly aimed at the segment's economics.

**Regulatory regime in flux.** March 19, 2026 proposals would replace Standardized and Advanced Approaches with a single expanded risk-based approach and modify the GSIB surcharge (Citi's method-2 surcharge is 3.5%). Comments closed June 18, 2026; outcomes unknown. The Stress Capital Buffer is fixed at 3.6% through October 1, 2027, removing one variable. The 2020 FRB and OCC consent orders remain in effect, with the OCC retaining authority to restrict Citibank dividends and require officer changes; prior OCC approval is required for significant portfolio acquisitions. The July 2024 amendment to the Citibank order was terminated December 18, 2025, and transformation programs moved from >80% at or near target state (12/31/25) to ~90% (3/31/26).

**Governance.** CEO total compensation of $95,757,800 for FY2025 is 3.08x the FY2024 figure and roughly 5.4x the $17.7M 2025 median for large-cap CEOs; compensation actually paid was $144,786,188, and the CEO-to-other-NEO ratio is 3.60x. The Q1 10-Q exhibit index confirms a discrete January 20, 2026 award agreement. The five-year TSR table shows Citi's first peer outperformance in the window (226.34 vs 203.47, implying roughly +70% Citi TSR in 2025) after four laggard years, which makes the timing defensible; the tension is that the disclosed performance measures — RoTCE, TBVPS, and net income, with RoTCE company-selected — are precisely the metrics most influenced by repurchases above tangible book. The award's structure, vesting, performance conditions, and the say-on-pay outcome are not verified and should not be assumed. A 25-year CFO departed mid-transformation, replaced internally by Gonzalo Luchetti.

**Insider activity.** No open-market executive purchases through July 1, 2026. Sales: Skyler 25,000 shares at $131.41, Giles 12,732 at $131.80, Dugan 2,117 at $125.30, plus routine tax withholding by Livingstone and Sieg, both of whom retain large positions (345,083 and 243,492 shares). At a GSIB with blackout windows and stock-heavy compensation, open-market buying is structurally rare, so the absence is a weak negative rather than a bearish signal. Combined with the compensation package and the absence of any purchase at any point during a 48% advance, it does not support a high management-alignment score.

**Other.** Reasonably possible unaccrued litigation loss of up to ~$1.1B — modest for a $2.7T balance sheet, with active Greek pension, Fairfield/Madoff clawback, and variable-rate demand obligation matters. Argentina net peso exposure ~$0.9B with only ~$0.4B hedged. Following the February 18, 2026 Russia exit, Citi retains a conditional ~$1.5B receivable from Renaissance Capital against amounts it owes clients. Net DTAs of $29.5B with $13.1B deducted from CET1. Fitch revised both holdco and bank outlooks to positive on May 1, 2026 (A / A3 / BBB+ at the holdco versus A+ / Aa3 / A+ at Citibank).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 35.0% against 29.1% one-year realized reads rich, but the nearest expiry is one day out (August 7) and that print is dominated by same-day pinning mechanics. Past the front expiry the term structure is upward-sloping — 28.5% at one month, 27.0% at three months, 32.9% at six months — so the headline "backwardation" label overstates near-term stress. Front-dated positioning is balanced: put/call open interest 0.88, put/call volume 0.48, near-term skew described by the data source as balanced, and a ±2.0% implied move. The informative observation sits at the six-month tenor, where implied volatility is the highest in the chain outside the front expiry and put/call volume runs 2.28 — consistent with hedging demand concentrated in a November-and-beyond window that contains the Basel III/GSIB outcome, the credit cycle, and potential Banamex deconsolidation. Short interest is 0.0% of float with 0.1 days to cover, so there is no bearish crowd and no squeeze dynamic. Unusual activity is modest and call-side at the front ($139 calls, 4.3x volume/OI). Nothing here contradicts a HOLD; the longer-dated hedging demand mildly corroborates our view that the identified risks are back-half-weighted.

**Risk factors: 5.0** — several quantified, dated, and specific items (the $9B charge, the guidance gap, the 12% revenue concentration with an expanding largest counterparty, a named rate-cap threat, rising Mexico losses) against a well-capitalized balance sheet and modest litigation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $172 (+26%)

The 1H26 run-rate is the true earnings power and guidance was conservative. Services compounds mid-teens on a deposit base whose stickiness is documented, not asserted; Markets sustains share gains in prime and financing while the volatility regime that produced 45% equities growth persists; investment banking fees continue recovering from a depressed base. Wealth and Banking efficiency ratios converge toward the group as transformation spend rolls off after 2026, taking the consolidated ratio from 64.7% toward 58-60% and adding $1.50-2.00 of EPS. The $30B buyback retires 12-13% of shares over its life. RoTCE settles at 12-13%, warranting 1.55-1.60x tangible book on TBVPS of roughly $107-110 by end-2027, or 13.3x FY2027 consensus of $12.88. Both routes produce approximately $172, near the $176 analyst high. Basel III recalibration proves neutral-to-favorable and Banamex deconsolidates cleanly in 2027 as a well-telegraphed non-cash charge.

### Bear Case — $105 (−23%)

Guidance was accurate and 2H26 RoTCE lands at 8-9%, confirming that Q1-Q2 were flattered by a discrete tax item, DVA, Markets seasonality, and a shrinking equity denominator. The stock reverts toward the 1.05-1.10x tangible book that a genuine 10-11% RoTCE warrants, roughly $104-110 on current TBVPS. Compounding vectors: Mexico consumer losses continue past 6.37% while the book keeps growing; the corporate reserve build converts into realized losses as $85.9B of unfunded TMT commitments prove hard to syndicate; a card partner extracts materially worse economics at renewal, or the AA portfolio purchase proves expensive; Basel III ERBA raises RWA and constrains the buyback, forcing the $30B program to slow given only ~$14.0B of excess CET1; and equity-to-assets at 6.89% leaves less absorption capacity than the 12.75% CET1 headline implies.

### Base Case — $152 (+11%)

RoTCE settles at 11.5-12% — above guidance, below the 1H run-rate — as expense discipline holds but the second-half step-up is real. FY2027 EPS of $12.88 at 11.8x, cross-checked against 1.42x forward TBVPS of roughly $107, produces $152. Total return with the increased dividend approximates 13%.

Against a −23% bear case, base-case reward-to-risk is roughly 0.5:1. That asymmetry does not support new capital, and the Financials sector's track record of negative selection alpha raises rather than lowers the bar.

## Conclusion

Citigroup's turnaround has moved from promise to evidence. Two consecutive quarters at roughly 13% RoTCE against 7.7% for FY2025, revenue at a decade high, 746bps of positive operating leverage in Q1, twelve of fourteen exit markets substantially complete, and a documented institutional deposit franchise growing 16% represent genuine progress that four years of prior filings did not show.

The investment question is narrower than the business question. The equity has already re-rated 48.6% over twelve months to 1.382x tangible book, a multiple that requires roughly 12.7% sustained RoTCE at a normal cost of equity — above the 10-11% management just reaffirmed and above the 11.5-12% we think most likely. Consensus is cutting Q3 and Q4 while the annual figure rises on banked beats, which is bookkeeping rather than momentum. A quantified ~$9B Banamex charge sits ahead of reported earnings, capital-neutral but headline-negative. The $30B repurchase — the strongest single support for the per-share story — leans on retained earnings and divestiture proceeds rather than the roughly $14.0B of excess CET1, is being executed above tangible book, and runs alongside equity-to-assets falling from 8.11% to 6.89%. Governance added noise: compensation tripled to $95.76M against performance metrics that repurchases above book mechanically influence, with no insider having bought a share in the open market during the entire advance.

Base case $152 (+11%), bull $172 (+26%), bear $105 (−23%). Hold existing positions for the buyback and the efficiency path; wait for either the October print to validate the run-rate or a decline toward the mid-$110s before committing new capital.
