**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RBC Bearings is a precision-components manufacturer that has re-mixed itself from a cyclical industrial into a backlog-visible aerospace and defense play. Fiscal 2026 (year ended late March 2026) revenue grew 14.3% to $1,870.9M, net income rose 23% to $287.6M, and diluted EPS reached $9.09 versus $7.70 the prior year. The defining data point is backlog: it jumped 2.5x year-over-year to $2.3B from $0.9B, driven by $1.1B of marine defense work (submarine/Newport News programs) and the $0.6B contributed by the July 2025 VACCO acquisition. This gives the company multi-year revenue visibility that its historical valuation framework, built around cyclical bearings demand, never contemplated.

The near-term earnings picture is favorable. Three tailwinds converge in FY27: the ~$13.2M VACCO purchase-accounting margin drag ceases after Q1; interest expense continues to fall as the term loan is retired (down to ~$154M post-quarter from a ~$1.8B Dodge-era peak); and operating leverage flows through as commercial-aero volumes scale. Management guides FY27 Q1 sales to $500–510M (+14.7–17.0%) with adjusted gross margin expanding to 45.25–45.5% from FY26's 44.4%. Estimate revisions are positive across all windows (current-year EPS +3.1% over 90 days to $14.44), and the company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters.

The constraint is price. At $595 the stock trades at roughly 41x FY27 consensus EPS ($14.44) and ~36x FY28 ($16.39), a meaningful premium to the industrial-machinery peer median of ~32x, and RBC pays no dividend versus a peer-median 1.2% yield. The A&D super-cycle narrative is real but well understood and largely embedded in the multiple. The Industrial segment, still 58% of revenue, grew only 3.8% in FY26 and is genuinely cyclical. From $595 the risk/reward is roughly balanced: a business worth owning for its compounding earnings power, but not one offering asymmetric upside at the current entry point. We rate RBC a HOLD with a constructive bias, and would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$500s or confirmation that the margin/earnings inflection is delivering ahead of consensus.

## Company Overview

RBC Bearings manufactures highly engineered precision bearings, gearing, and engineered components across two segments:

- **Industrial (58% of FY26 sales; $1,082.9M):** Bearings, gearing (Dodge brands: Quantis, Tigear, MagnaGear), mounted bearings, and motion-control components for construction, mining, aggregate & cement, food & beverage, grain, warehousing, semiconductor, power generation, and rail. Key customers include Caterpillar, Komatsu, Halliburton, and large distributors. This segment is anchored by the ~$2.9B, debt-funded Dodge Industrial acquisition (FY2022).

- **Aerospace & Defense (42%; $788.0M):** Precision bearings and engineered systems for commercial aircraft, defense aerospace, defense marine, ground vehicles, missiles/guided munitions, and space. Customers include Boeing, Airbus, Newport News Shipbuilding, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, Raytheon, Blue Origin, SpaceX, and the DOD.

The competitive moat rests on product qualifications. FAA PMAs and DOD/OEM certifications take 6 months to 6 years to obtain, and because components are designed-in during original platform development, RBC is frequently the sole or primary supplier for the life of an aircraft or defense platform. This produces a long tail of high-margin aftermarket revenue and pricing durability that larger commodity-bearing competitors (SKF, Timken, Regal Rexnord) cannot easily contest on niche, low-volume, high-specification products. The company operates 65 facilities across 11 countries and has completed 30 acquisitions since 1992, with a repeatable margin-improvement playbook via methods and systems integration.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth and profitability.** FY26 revenue rose 14.3% (9.2% organic ex-VACCO). Net income was up 23%, outpacing revenue on operating leverage and falling interest expense. Gross margin held flat at 44.4% (A&D 40.7%, Industrial 47.0%) despite absorbing the VACCO purchase-accounting drag and restructuring, a respectable outcome that understates underlying margin momentum. Operating margin was ~22.5%; net margin 15.4%.

Adjusted EBITDA has compounded steadily: $266.5M (FY22) → $433.9M → $482.1M → $519.8M → $605.3M (FY26). That trajectory reflects the integration of Dodge and the shift toward higher-value A&D content.

**Cash generation and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $415.7M (up from $293.6M), against $73.1M of capex, yielding free cash flow of ~$342.6M (18.3% margin). OCF exceeds net income, signaling high-quality earnings and disciplined working capital. Capital allocation has been directed entirely at reinvestment, debt paydown, and M&A. There is no dividend, and a $100M buyback authorized in 2019 has never been used; the share "repurchases" in the filings are tax-withholding retirements, not open-market buybacks.

**Balance sheet and deleveraging.** Total debt fell to ~$875.5M at March 2026 from the Dodge-era peak, and the term loan was reduced further after quarter-end. The remaining structure is manageable: a Term Loan maturing November 2026 (small balance), $500M of 4.375% Senior Notes due October 2029, and a $500M revolver extended to October 2030 with covenants loosened (the October 2025 amendment removed the minimum interest-coverage covenant). Interest expense fell to $49.8M from $59.8M, a continuing earnings tailwind. Current ratio is 2.18; D/E is 0.52.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.85 ("grey zone") is a false alarm here. It reflects low asset turnover (0.37) and a goodwill/intangible-heavy balance sheet from acquisitions, not solvency stress. Real credit metrics — falling leverage, $342M FCF, loosened covenants, ample revolver availability ($296.3M) — point to a strengthening balance sheet. The genuine balance-sheet risk is goodwill impairment, not distress: goodwill ($2.0B) plus intangibles ($1.4B) equal ~66% of total assets. Management reports reporting-unit fair values exceed carrying value by a minimum 38.8% cushion, but an Industrial downturn could pressure that headroom.

The financial-health picture is strong on the dimensions that most reliably discriminate winners: high-quality FCF conversion, deleveraging into a rate-sensitive environment, and covenant relief. We score it 7.5, a touch below the prior report's 8.0 given the goodwill concentration and the unhedged floating-rate exposure created when the interest-rate swap expired December 30, 2025 without replacement.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is bifurcated by segment.

**Aerospace & Defense is the engine.** A&D grew 32.9% in FY26 (19.1% ex-VACCO). Commercial aero rose 17.8% on Boeing/Airbus build-rate recovery; defense grew 64.5% (22.9% ex-VACCO), led by marine and missiles/guided munitions. In Q3 FY26, A&D accelerated to +41.5% (defense +86.2%). The segment has re-mixed from 33% of sales in FY24 to 42% in FY26, shifting the company toward higher-visibility, backlog-driven revenue.

The backlog is the crux. RBC now has revenue visibility unusual for a company historically viewed as cyclical, underpinning management's confidence in continued double-digit A&D growth. It also concentrates the growth in a handful of long-cycle, cancellable government programs subject to funding, timing, and inspection delays, the source of quarterly lumpiness the company explicitly flags.

**Industrial is the ballast, not the accelerant.** The segment grew 3.8% in FY26 (distribution/aftermarket +4.8%, OEM +1.5%), with strength in aggregate & cement, warehousing, and food & beverage offset by softness in mining/metals, power generation, oil & gas, and semiconductors. This is a mid-single-digit grower tied to cyclical end markets, and at 58% of revenue it caps the blended growth rate. The consolidated 9.2% organic growth in FY26 is a fair characterization of the sustainable base once VACCO annualizes.

FY27 Q1 guidance (+8.3–10.6% organic) with adjusted gross margin of 45.25–45.5% confirms the inflection: margin expansion as VACCO purchase accounting rolls off and commercial-aero volume flows through fixed-cost plants. Consensus reflects this, with next-year EPS at $16.39, implying ~13% EPS growth. The revisions are positive but modest in magnitude, well below the +30% velocity that would signal a systematic under-estimation of a trough inflection. This is a solid, durable growth story, not an explosive one. We score Growth Prospects 7.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At $595, RBC trades at ~65x trailing EPS ($9.09) and ~41x forward consensus, declining to ~36x FY28. Because RBC is not at a cyclical earnings trough — earnings have grown steadily for four years and estimates are rising only in the low-single-digits — the forward multiple is the correct lens, and it is genuinely elevated.

Against the industrial-machinery peer set, the premium is clear:

RBC's forward P/E is roughly 28% above the peer median, and the company pays no dividend versus the median. The premium is partly justified: RBC's TSR has run 268.5 versus 172.5 for its peer group, its A&D backlog visibility exceeds most industrial peers, and its ~13% forward EPS growth is above-median. But a PEG of ~3.1 (41x / 13% growth) is expensive for a business whose largest segment grows mid-single-digits. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $490, mean $620, median $640, high $680), placing the stock only ~4–8% below the median target; at a >40x multiple these targets are not independent confirmation of undervaluation, as they largely extrapolate the same growth already embedded in the price.

The valuation case for higher scores would require identifying a specific reason the market is underpricing this stock. We do not see one that is not already reflected. The backlog is disclosed and widely followed; the margin inflection is guided; the deleveraging is visible. A cheap valuation score here would be a value-trap error in reverse, paying a full price for a well-understood quality story. We score Valuation 4.5: the multiple is stretched relative to the sustainable growth base, and the entry point is unattractive rather than distressed.

## Competitive Landscape

RBC's competitive position is stable-to-improving and among the strongest dimensions in this analysis. The moat is qualification-driven: sole/primary supplier status for the life of a platform, earned through multi-year certification processes that competitors cannot short-circuit. The A&D re-mix (33%→42% of sales in two years) is shifting revenue toward the highest-moat, highest-visibility end of the portfolio, and the marine defense backlog locks in share on programs with essentially no competitive re-bid risk once designed-in.

On the Industrial side, the Dodge franchise gives RBC scale in mounted bearings and power transmission, though this segment competes more directly with larger players and grew only ~4% in FY26. Top-10 customer concentration improved to 35% of FY26 sales from 44% in FY25, a favorable trajectory that reduces single-customer dependency even as backlog concentrates in marine defense programs.

The company wins on niche engineering, product breadth, and irreplaceable qualifications rather than scale. Segment revenue growth (A&D +19% organic) is running well ahead of the broad bearings industry, evidence of share gains and structural tailwind capture. We score Competitive Position 8.0, consistent with the improving trajectory in prior reports, though slightly below the prior 8.5 to reflect that the A&D re-rating is now well recognized rather than an unpriced edge.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** ~40% of assets are goodwill plus indefinite-lived intangibles from Dodge and VACCO. An Industrial downturn could erode the reporting-unit cushion and trigger a large non-cash charge. This is the most material idiosyncratic balance-sheet risk.

**Marine defense concentration.** $1.1B of the $2.3B backlog is in a small number of long-cycle, cancellable government programs. This is both the growth driver and a concentration risk: quarterly results can be lumpy on government inspection/approval timing, and appropriations shifts could slow conversion.

**Government-spending dependence.** ~8% of sales tie directly/indirectly to the U.S. government (~1% direct, ~7% indirect), materially more through prime-contractor exposure. Budget-process uncertainty is a live variable.

**Tariffs and steel costs.** Steel is the primary raw material and subject to Section 232 tariffs. Many contracts are fixed-price, limiting cost pass-through and creating a margin lag. Management reports no material impact "to date" but flags the situation as fluid.

**Key-man and succession.** CEO Dr. Michael Hartnett is central to strategy and serves as chairman/CEO/CODM. A June 24, 2026 8-K carried both a material-agreement item and an officer/director-change item; the primary document was not retrievable. Given Hartnett's centrality, this filing warrants direct review; it may relate to succession or executive arrangements, a governance uncertainty we cannot fully resolve.

**Interest-rate exposure.** The interest-rate swap expired December 30, 2025 and has not been replaced, leaving some floating-rate exposure on the remaining term loan. With the term loan now small (~$154M) this is modest in dollar terms.

We score Risk Factors 5.5. The impairment and marine-concentration risks are real and specific but partly offset by strong FCF, low absolute leverage, and improving customer diversification; several risks are asymmetric to the downside but not imminent binaries.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 36.2% versus 26.6% trailing realized, a rich ~9.6-point premium sitting in the 68th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (36.2% near-term versus 30.6% at six months), which signals the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with an approaching earnings print. The near-term put/call volume ratio of 3.00 looks heavily put-skewed, but the actual put/call skew is roughly balanced (0.8% near-term, drifting to -3.3% at six months), so the volume figure likely reflects hedging of gains rather than a directional bearish bet. The implied ±7.8% move on the nearest expiry is wide. For a prospective buyer, the rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors waiting for the post-earnings vol crush to establish a position at a better entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$720–760).** The A&D super-cycle proves durable: commercial-aero build rates keep climbing, marine defense converts the backlog on schedule, and munitions demand stays elevated. Margins inflect to ~46%+ as VACCO purchase accounting rolls off and operating leverage compounds. FY28 EPS beats consensus toward $17.50+, and the market awards a sustained ~44x on visible double-digit A&D growth. The unused buyback and dividend optionality become an incremental catalyst once M&A appetite is satisfied. This maps to ~$720–760, roughly 21–28% above current.

**Bear case (~$450–490).** Industrial cyclicality bites: mining, energy, power gen, and semiconductor weakness deepens and drags the 58%-of-revenue segment negative, compressing the blend to mid-single-digit growth. A defense appropriations slip or program timing delay causes backlog conversion to disappoint in a lumpy quarter. The market re-rates a premium multiple toward the peer median (~32x), and any goodwill-impairment scare on the Industrial reporting unit amplifies the derating. This maps to ~$450–490, roughly 18–24% below current, and aligns with the low analyst target.

The base case sits between: ~13% EPS growth delivered, a modestly compressing but still-premium multiple, and mid-teens total return over 18 months as earnings power grows into the valuation rather than the multiple expanding further.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD. The bar for continuing unchanged is appropriately higher, and the incremental data (backlog surge, VACCO margin rolloff, deleveraging) genuinely strengthens the fundamental case, but it does so alongside a re-rating that leaves the valuation full. The right posture is to own the quality and wait for a better entry, not to chase at $595 into an event-heavy period with rich near-term IV.

- **Base case target: $645.** ~40x FY28 consensus EPS of $16.39 minus a small premium haircut, or equivalently ~44x FY27 $14.44. Roughly 8% above current. This assumes consensus delivery and a stable-to-slightly-compressing multiple as earnings grow into the valuation.
- **Bull case target: $740.** ~45x on a beat-driven ~$16.50 FY27 print. ~24% above current.
- **Bear case target: $470.** ~29x on $16.39 FY28 EPS as the multiple reverts toward the peer median on Industrial cyclicality or a defense-timing disappointment. ~21% below current.
- **Timeframe:** 12–18 months, keyed to FY27 Q1, successive marine-defense backlog conversion, and commercial-aero build-rate data through 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Pullback toward the low-$500s (~$510–530), which would compress the forward multiple toward ~35x and restore asymmetry.
2. Adjusted gross margin confirmed above 45.5% in FY27 Q1/Q2 with organic A&D growth sustained above 15%, evidence the inflection is running ahead of consensus.
3. Initiation of the long-authorized buyback or a dividend, signaling capital-return capacity beyond M&A.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Industrial organic revenue turning negative for two consecutive quarters, dragging blended growth to low-single-digits.
2. A material goodwill-impairment charge or a disclosed narrowing of the reporting-unit fair-value cushion below ~15%.
3. A backlog conversion miss or defense program delay that cuts forward EPS estimates by more than 5%, or an adverse resolution of the unresolved June 2026 8-K executive/succession item.

**Next reassessment:** FY27 Q1 earnings.

## Conclusion

RBC Bearings is a genuinely high-quality franchise executing well against two real, multi-year tailwinds: commercial-aero recovery and a naval/munitions defense super-cycle. The backlog surge, the coming margin inflection as VACCO purchase accounting rolls off, and the deleveraging that steadily reduces interest expense all support the improving score trajectory of the last two reports. Financial health is strong, the competitive moat is durable and widening at the high-value A&D end, and estimate revisions are pointed in the right direction.

The obstacle is entirely price. At ~41x forward FY27 EPS and a PEG above 3, with no dividend, a mid-single-digit Industrial base still representing 58% of revenue, and the A&D acceleration widely understood, the stock offers little asymmetry from $595. The valuation is not distressed and is not a value trap; it is simply full for the growth on offer. The near-term options market is pricing event stress into the upcoming print, another reason not to chase.

We rate RBC a HOLD with a constructive bias. This is a stock to own for its compounding earnings power over multiple years, but the disciplined entry is on weakness toward the low-$500s or on confirmation that the FY27 margin inflection is delivering ahead of consensus. Absent those, the risk/reward from here is balanced.
