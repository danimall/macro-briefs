## Executive Summary

Avis Budget Group is a fundamentally impaired business trading at an elevated price relative to its intrinsic value following an extraordinary short squeeze in April 2026 that took the stock from ~$100 to $848 intraday before collapsing to current levels near $158. The company carries $25.3 billion in total debt (mostly vehicle-backed ABS) against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of just $748 million, with annual interest expense of ~$1.34 billion exceeding operating profit. Stockholders' equity is negative $3.4 billion, and the Altman Z-Score sits at 0.05 — deep in the distress zone.

The investment case requires assessing whether the fleet rotation pain (which drove $3B+ in impairments over 2024-2025) is now behind the company, and whether normalizing vehicle economics can restore EBITDA to a level that services the debt. Q1 2026 showed encouraging signs: revenue per day +3%, small gains on vehicle disposals (vs. $334M loss prior year), and normalized depreciation. However, Adjusted EBITDA of -$113M in Q1 2026 (seasonally weak but still deteriorating YoY) and three earnings misses in the last four quarters undermine confidence in a near-term recovery. With 62.8% short interest still outstanding and the stock trading above the analyst consensus target of $127, the risk/reward is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Avis Budget Group operates the Avis, Budget, and Zipcar brands across approximately 180 countries, with an average fleet of ~620,000 vehicles (down from 684,000 in 2024 as management deliberately right-sizes). The Americas segment contributes ~76% of revenue, with International at ~24%. Approximately 84% of the fleet consists of "risk vehicles" without manufacturer buyback guarantees, creating substantial residual value exposure. Revenue in FY2025 was $11.7 billion, essentially flat for four consecutive years, while profitability has swung from $2.76 billion net income in FY2022 to a $890 million net loss in FY2025.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement Deterioration

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| Revenue | $12.0B | $12.0B | $11.8B | $11.7B | $2.53B |
| Net Income | $2.76B | $1.63B | -$1.82B | -$0.89B | -$0.28B |
| Adj. EBITDA | $4.13B | $2.49B | $0.63B | $0.75B | -$0.11B |

Revenue has been remarkably stable, which means the profit collapse is entirely driven by cost structure — specifically fleet depreciation ($3.0B in FY2025 vs. $830M in FY2022), impairments ($518M EV fleet writedown in FY2025, $2.47B in FY2024), and interest expense ($1.34B annually). The company's Adjusted EBITDA of $748M cannot cover its $1.34B interest bill, meaning it is structurally cash-flow negative on a fully-loaded basis.

### Balance Sheet: Negative Equity, Extreme Leverage

- Total debt: $25.3B (Dec 2025), declining to ~$24.4B by March 2026
- Stockholders' equity: -$3.4 billion
- Cash + revolver availability: $915M
- Altman Z-Score: 0.05 (extreme distress)
- Current ratio: 0.72
- Vehicle program debt maturities: $4.4B within 1 year, $5.0B in 1-2 years

The negative equity is a product of aggressive buybacks ($8.1B cumulative since 2013, reducing shares from ~120M to ~35M) combined with recent losses. The pivot from buybacks to filing a 5-million-share ATM offering in March 2026 — representing 14% dilution — signals that financial engineering has reached its endpoint.

### Cash Flow: Structurally Consumed by Fleet Investment

Operating cash flow of $3.3B in FY2025 appears strong but is misleading in isolation. Investing activities (primarily fleet purchases) consumed $5.2B. The vehicle-backed debt is self-amortizing through vehicle disposals, creating a continuous treadmill where operating cash flow funds fleet rotation rather than debt reduction or shareholder returns.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is essentially zero. FY2025 revenue of $11.7B is within 3% of FY2022 levels. The Q1 2026 +4% YoY improvement reflects modest revenue per day improvements (+3%) offset by 1-4% volume declines. The company is deliberately shrinking its fleet (684K → 620K) to improve utilization rather than pursuing growth.

Estimate revisions are severely negative:
- Current quarter EPS: -39.0% revision over 90 days ($2.97 → $1.81)
- Current year EPS: -64.6% over 90 days ($9.66 → $3.42)
- Next year EPS: -30.5% over 90 days ($12.08 → $8.40)

The bull case relies on profitability normalization — specifically that fleet depreciation and disposition losses have peaked and will revert toward historical norms as the post-pandemic used car market stabilizes. Q1 2026 showed early signs of this (a $10M gain on vehicle sales vs. a $334M loss in Q1 2025), but one quarter does not establish a trend, particularly against the 3-out-of-4 earnings miss track record.

## Valuation Assessment

At $157.71 with trailing EPS of -$25.26, the stock has no meaningful trailing P/E. On forward consensus of $3.42 for FY2026, the stock trades at ~46x forward earnings — a premium multiple for a company with negative equity, extreme leverage, and declining estimates.

The analyst consensus target is $127 (mean), with a low of $85 and a high of $160. The current price of $158 sits at the top end of analyst targets. The distribution of analyst recommendations — 0 buys, 5 holds, 1 sell, 2 strong sells — reflects professional skepticism.

Compared to industrial peers trading at ~26.6x P/E (median), CAR's forward multiple is premium on both absolute terms and relative to its fundamentally impaired capital structure. A stock with $25B in debt, negative equity, and earnings that don't cover interest should trade at a discount.

The only scenario justifying the current valuation is rapid EBITDA recovery to $2B+ (which requires used car price stabilization + interest rate cuts + tariff clarity), combined with successful debt reduction. The probability of this scenario materializing within 12 months is low given the macro backdrop.

## Competitive Landscape

Avis Budget competes against Enterprise (private, largest fleet), Hertz (publicly traded, facing similar EV/depreciation challenges), and emerging mobility services (ride-hailing, car-sharing, autonomous vehicles). The industry is mature, highly price-competitive, and cyclical.

The company's competitive position is stable but undifferentiated. Revenue per day improvements in Q1 2026 suggest some pricing power is returning, and the deliberate fleet reduction should improve utilization metrics. The Avis and Budget brands maintain broad recognition and distribution. However, there is no structural moat — switching costs for consumers are minimal, and fleet assets depreciate continuously.

Hertz's parallel distress (with similar EV fleet losses) means the industry structure isn't deteriorating through competitive displacement — both major public players are struggling with the same fleet economics. This offers some comfort that the problem is cyclical rather than structural, but it also means there's no competitive differentiation driving a recovery catalyst.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Debt sustainability (existential):** Any ABS market disruption, used car value decline, or recession could trigger covenant issues. The company must continuously refinance $4.4B in debt maturing within 12 months.

2. **Used vehicle residual value:** With 84% risk fleet, a recession-driven used car market decline would require additional impairments and could reduce ABS collateral values below triggers that accelerate repayment.

3. **Rising rates + tariffs:** New vehicle acquisition costs are rising (tariffs), while financing costs remain elevated. Both compress margins from opposite directions.

4. **Dilution risk:** The ATM offering for 5M shares hangs over the stock. If shares were sold during the April squeeze (which would be financially rational), significant dilution may already be occurring.

5. **SRS concentration / change-of-control:** SRS potentially owning >50% of shares could trigger 101% put provisions on $5B+ in senior notes, creating a liquidity crisis.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing extreme uncertainty. Near-term IV of 157% (vs. 112% realized) indicates the market expects continued violent moves. The severe backwardation in term structure (157% near vs. 65% far) reflects short-squeeze aftershocks rather than fundamental catalyst pricing. Put/call OI ratio of 1.93 indicates elevated put demand at longer tenors (3-month P/C volume ratio of 4.17 is heavily put-dominated), consistent with institutional positioning for further downside. The 62.8% short interest creates persistent squeeze risk that makes the stock functionally untradeable on fundamental merit — price discovery is dominated by positioning mechanics rather than business fundamentals.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely late July). The company needs to demonstrate EBITDA recovery in the seasonally strongest quarter. Consensus expects $10.29 EPS in Q2, but the track record is 3 misses in 4 quarters, with dramatic miss magnitudes (-665.9% surprise in Q4 2025). Additionally, any disclosure that ATM shares were sold during the squeeze would confirm dilution.

**Timing window:** 8-10 weeks to Q2 earnings. Shorter-term, continued unwinding of squeeze mechanics as IV normalizes.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 62.8% of float is short with only 1.1 days to cover. This is extremely high and creates permanent squeeze risk. However, the borrow has presumably stabilized post-squeeze (IV declining from 232% to 157% over the past month), and the concentrated ownership (SRS + Pentwater controlling 71% of shares) means the actual tradeable float is minimal. The stock moved from $848 to $158 without a meaningful short squeeze in reverse, suggesting the squeeze mechanics have largely exhausted themselves.

**Borrow and options signal:** IV remains rich at 157% vs. 112% HV, indicating elevated borrow costs implicitly. The call skew at near-term (-88% put skew = extreme call skew) reflects squeeze-related call buying, not fundamental optimism.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Fair value based on fundamentals sits at $85-110 (consistent with analyst range). From $158, downside to $95 = -40%. Upside risk on another squeeze = potentially $250+, representing +60%. Risk/reward is approximately 0.67:1 on a fundamental basis, but the squeeze risk makes this unfavorable.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — Despite correct fundamental thesis (the company is distressed and overvalued), the 62.8% short interest and concentrated ownership create unacceptable squeeze risk. The correct expression is to avoid owning the stock, not to short it. The post-squeeze normalization is largely complete (ATM IV down 75pp in one month), meaning the easy money on the short side has been made by those who sold at $400-700.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** Would reconsider if: (1) Stock declines to $85-95 range (analyst consensus low/mean) AND short interest normalizes below 30% AND Q2 EBITDA demonstrates clear recovery trajectory toward $1.5B+ annualized; (2) Used car market stabilizes with 3+ months of flat/rising wholesale prices; (3) Company successfully refinances 2026 maturities at rates below 6%.

**Timeline:** Headwinds persist for 12-18 months minimum. The current rate environment works against the interest cost relief needed for EBITDA coverage. The fleet rotation gains need 2-3 more quarters of confirmation. The short squeeze distortion needs 6+ months to fully normalize in positioning.

## Conclusion

Avis Budget Group is a fundamentally distressed company trading above fair value due to residual short squeeze mechanics and 62.8% short interest creating artificial price support. The capital structure is unsustainable without either dramatic EBITDA recovery or material balance sheet restructuring. Earnings estimates are collapsing (-65% current year revision over 90 days), the company missed 3 of its last 4 quarters, and management's pivot from buybacks to equity issuance confirms the end of financial engineering.

The single positive signal — Q1 2026 vehicle disposition gains returning — is insufficient to overcome the structural impairment. At $158, the stock trades at the analyst consensus high target of $160, with 0 buy recommendations and 3 sell/strong sell ratings. The macro environment (elevated rates, tariff uncertainty, potential recession) works against every pathway to fundamental recovery. Maintain AVOID.
