## Executive Summary

M/I Homes is a mid-sized national homebuilder trading at approximately 1.0x book value ($123/share book vs. $121 market price) with an industry-leading 18% debt-to-capital ratio and $689M in cash. FY2025 EPS declined 25% to $14.74, Q1 2026 showed further deterioration (EPS down 36% YoY), and consensus estimates are falling at -13.5% over 90 days for the current year.

The margin collapse is concentrated in the Southern segment (57% of revenue), where gross margins fell 680 bps to 19.8% on a combination of higher lot costs, impairment charges, warranty issues, and escalating incentives. The company is spending approximately $200M annually on rate buydowns to maintain sales pace. With mortgage rates at 6.5-7.0% and the macro environment deteriorating, there is no near-term catalyst for margin recovery.

At 8.2x trailing earnings and 9.6x current year estimates with the stock near book value, the valuation provides a floor. But the earnings trajectory is negative, the backlog is shrinking, and the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty (41.2% IV vs. 34.1% HV, backwardation). This is a high-quality balance sheet waiting for a better operating environment.

## Company Overview

M/I Homes designs, constructs, and sells single-family homes and attached townhomes across 17 markets in 10 states, operating through Northern (Columbus, Cincinnati, Indianapolis, Chicago, Minneapolis, Detroit) and Southern (4 Florida, 4 Texas, Charlotte, Raleigh, Nashville) segments. The company serves first-time through luxury buyers with an average sales price of $459K (Q1 2026).

Key business characteristics:
- **232 active communities** (up from 220 in 2024), with 5% growth targeted for 2026
- **Smart Series** (affordable product line) represents ~52% of homes sold — a deliberate affordability pivot
- **M/I Financial** captures 93% of company mortgages, providing vertical integration and rate buydown capability
- **Self-develops ~80% of lots**, giving cost control but requiring significant working capital ($1.17B spent on land in FY2025)
- **~50,000 lots under control** representing a 5.6-year supply

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement Deterioration

FY2025 revenue of $4.42B was essentially flat (-2% YoY), but net income collapsed 29% to $403M. The driver was gross margin compression: overall margins fell 360 bps to 23.0%, with the Southern segment experiencing a 680 bps decline.

The margin erosion stems from four sources:
1. **Incentive escalation**: $200M in FY2025 vs. $131M in FY2024 (+$69M), primarily mortgage rate buydowns to 4.875-5.0%
2. **Lot cost inflation**: $65M increase from higher development costs flowing through
3. **Impairments/write-offs**: $47.7M in charges ($35.9M inventory + $11.8M deposits)
4. **Warranty**: $11.2M for Florida attic ventilation issues

Q1 2026 continued the trend: EPS $2.55 (down 36% YoY), gross margin 22.0% (down 390 bps), ASP $459K (down 4%). The company is selling cheaper homes at lower margins with higher incentives.

### Balance Sheet Strength

The balance sheet remains exceptional:
- Shareholders' equity: $3.17B (~$123/share book value)
- Homebuilding debt-to-capital: 18%
- Long-term debt: $700M ($400M due 2028 at 4.95%, $300M due 2030 at 3.95%)
- Cash: $689M
- Revolving credit: $807M available of $900M facility (extended to 2030)
- Altman Z-Score: 3.03 (safe zone)
- Leverage ratio: 0.02 vs. 0.60 covenant maximum

This company could endure a severe downturn without existential risk.

### Cash Flow & Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow was $137M in FY2025, constrained by $1.17B in land purchases and development spending. Free cash flow was only $128M — well below net income — because the company is investing heavily in its land pipeline.

Share repurchases totaled $202M in FY2025 (1.6M shares at ~$126 average). An additional $220M remains authorized. At the current price, this could retire another 1.8M shares (7% of outstanding) — meaningful per-share value creation if earnings stabilize.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are negative in the near term:

- **Backlog**: 1,809 homes at year-end 2025 (down 29% YoY); further declined in Q1 2026
- **New contracts**: Up 3% in Q1 2026, but March orders weakened materially after strong January/February
- **ASP declining**: $479K → $459K as product mix shifts toward affordable
- **Consensus estimates falling**: Current year EPS at $12.65, down 13.5% over 90 days; next year at $15.46, down 5.2%
- **Community count growth**: 5% planned for 2026, which helps volume but further pressures margins given the incentive environment

The structural long-term thesis (decade of underbuilding, supply deficit) remains intact but is overwhelmed by cyclical headwinds: mortgage rates at 6.5-7.0%, consumer confidence deterioration, and geopolitical uncertainty adding further pressure on disposable income.

For earnings to inflect positively, one or more of these conditions must change: (a) mortgage rates decline below 5.5%, (b) Southern market impairments abate, (c) lot cost inflation moderates, or (d) demand strengthens enough to reduce incentive spending. None appears imminent.

## Valuation Assessment

MHO at $121 trades at:
- **9.6x current year EPS** ($12.65 consensus)
- **7.8x next year EPS** ($15.46 consensus)
- **~1.0x book value** ($123/share)
- **Trailing P/E of ~8.2x** ($14.74 EPS)
- **Forward P/E of 9.1x** (per vendor data)

The discount to peers (peer median P/E of 18.4x) reflects both the earnings miss track record (3 of last 4 quarters missed) and the margin compression trajectory. However, the book value floor provides asymmetric protection — at 1.0x book for a business generating 12.7% ROE even in a bad year.

The analyst consensus target of $160 (range $155-$165) implies 32% upside and roughly 12.6x current year earnings — achievable if margins stabilize but requiring a macro environment that doesn't exist today.

On normalized earnings, if FY2027 achieves $15.46 EPS (current consensus) and the stock re-rates to 11x, the target is $170. The negative revision trend (-13.5% over 90 days) suggests estimates may continue declining.

## Competitive Landscape

M/I Homes occupies a defensible mid-market position but faces competitive pressure from larger builders with superior scale:

**Advantages:**
- Vertically integrated mortgage (93% capture) enables efficient rate buydown execution
- Conservative balance sheet allows continued investment while leveraged competitors retrench
- Smart Series product line well-positioned for affordability constraints
- Self-lot development provides cost control (when markets cooperate)

**Disadvantages:**
- Scale disadvantage vs. Lennar ($36B) and D.R. Horton ($37B) in purchasing and land bank efficiency
- Geographic concentration: heavy Florida/Texas exposure (4 markets each) at the worst time
- Spec-heavy strategy (~50% of deliveries) increases quarter-to-quarter volatility
- No dividend — unlike KBH (2.1% yield), offering no income cushion during drawdowns

The shift from Northern to Southern geographic mix (57% of revenue from South) has been value-destructive in the current cycle. Management acknowledges this, noting resource reallocation toward stronger Midwestern markets — but this is a multi-year process.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Continued margin compression** — Each 100 bps of gross margin = approximately $45M pre-tax, or $1.30/share after tax. If margins decline another 100-200 bps from here, current year estimates are still too high.

2. **Backlog erosion as forward revenue indicator** — The 23-29% backlog declines signal weaker forward closings unless spec sales compensate at adequate margins. Spec homes carry more pricing risk.

3. **Mortgage rate sensitivity** — Each 25 bps of rate buydown costs approximately $3,500/home at the average $459K price point. If market rates rise further, buydown costs escalate further.

4. **Tariff risk on building materials** — Lumber, steel, and aluminum tariffs not yet impacting costs but represent a delayed headwind. Management flagged this specifically.

5. **Consumer weakness** — With gas prices elevated and consumer confidence declining, the demand backdrop for $459K homes is deteriorating.

6. **Land bank risk** — $1.6B in purchase commitments and already $48M in charges taken. If markets deteriorate further, additional impairments are probable.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty: 41.2% IV vs. 34.1% HV (7.1pp spread), with term structure in backwardation. Put skew at 6.5% indicates elevated downside hedging demand. However, the very low put/call volume ratio (0.08) and OI ratio (0.12) suggest limited institutional positioning for a major decline. The P/C OI ratio rising from 0.00 to 0.12 over the past 10 days is still minimal.

Net assessment: the options market sees near-term event risk (backwardation) but not a crowded directional trade. This is consistent with the "uncertain" classification — neither a clean buy setup nor imminent breakdown.

### Short Interest

At 6.0% of float with 5 days to cover, short interest is moderate. Not crowded enough to create squeeze risk, but enough to confirm some institutional capital is positioned for further weakness.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$155-165, +25-35%)
- Mortgage rates decline 75-100 bps as the economy weakens, reducing incentive costs and stimulating demand
- Southern segment impairments were largely one-time; underlying margins stabilize at 21-22%
- Share repurchases ($220M remaining) shrink outstanding shares by 7% at current prices, supporting EPS
- At 1.0x book with 12%+ ROE capacity, permanent capital loss is unlikely
- Long-term housing supply deficit eventually reasserts itself

### Bear Case (~$100-105, -15-18%)
- Mortgage rates remain at 6.5-7.0% or rise further
- Consumer spending weakens further
- Additional inventory impairments in Florida/Texas markets ($30-50M)
- Estimate revisions continue declining → stock re-rates to 7-8x trough earnings
- Geopolitical-driven inflation further erodes affordability

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Mortgage rates decline below 6.0% (creating affordable entry point for buyers and reducing incentive costs)
2. Q2 2026 gross margin stabilizes or improves sequentially (22.0%+ homebuilding gross margin)
3. Estimate revisions flatten or turn positive

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Q2 2026 gross margin falls below 20.5% (indicating further structural deterioration)
2. Net new orders turn negative YoY for two consecutive quarters
3. Stock breaks below $100 on fundamental deterioration (not just macro)
4. Additional impairment charges >$30M in a single quarter

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026). The spring selling season results will be the critical test of whether demand is holding in a worsening macro backdrop.

## Conclusion

M/I Homes presents a high-quality balance sheet (8.5/10 financial health) in a deteriorating operating environment. The stock trades at book value with conservative leverage, providing exceptional downside protection. However, three consecutive earnings misses, declining estimates, backlog erosion, and Southern segment margin collapse argue against aggressive positioning today.

The appropriate stance is HOLD with an "uncertain" classification. The balance sheet provides durability — this business will survive any realistic macro scenario. But earnings momentum is firmly negative, and the macro backdrop (rising rates, consumer weakness) is hostile to homebuilders. The correct entry point is either: (a) a rate environment that supports organic demand recovery, or (b) a price decline to $100-105 that creates sufficient margin of safety on trough earnings. Neither condition exists at $121.
