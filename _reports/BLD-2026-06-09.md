**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

On April 18, 2026, TopBuild signed a definitive merger agreement to be acquired by QXO, Inc. (NYSE: QXO) in a transaction valued near $17 billion. Each BLD share converts into either $505.00 cash or 20.200 QXO shares at holder election, subject to proration so the aggregate cash/stock mix is capped. The deal is unanimously board-approved, backed by a voting agreement with QXO's controlling holder Jacobs Private Equity II, fully financed (QXO launched a ~$3 billion loan sale in June 2026), and targeted to close by the end of Q3 2026, with a January 17, 2027 outside date and a $600 million termination fee. The current price of $404.83 must be evaluated against these terms, not against TopBuild's operating multiples.

The defining number is the spread. At announcement on April 20, BLD closed near $489.83 against the $505 headline, roughly a 3% gross spread. Today the gross spread to the cash value has widened to about 24.7%. The driver is the decline in QXO's stock (which fell ~14% the week of announcement and traded as low as ~$22.85), which drags down the value of the 20.2-share stock-election alternative that prorated holders are forced to partly accept, rather than deal-break risk. Because of proration, most holders receive a blended consideration, so BLD effectively trades as a proxy on QXO's share price and on deal certainty rather than a clean $505 cash arbitrage.

The recommendation is HOLD with a defined catalyst: deal completion in the Q3 2026 window at a blended value above the current price. This is a dated, falsifiable thesis. The reason it is not a BUY is the two-sidedness introduced by the QXO stock leg and proration mechanic: the realized consideration floats with a volatile acquirer equity, and a deal break reverts BLD to a re-levered cyclical that has lost its takeout premium and is seeing organic volumes fall 5-8% with compressing margins.

## Company Overview

TopBuild is the leading U.S. and Canadian installer of insulation and commercial roofing, and a specialty distributor of insulation and building products, spun out from Masco in 2015. It operates two reportable segments:

- **Installation Services** (~59% of gross sales; $3.18B 2025 revenue): 200+ branches installing fiberglass, spray-foam, and cellulose insulation, plus commercial roofing (re-roofing, recurring maintenance, new construction) following the Progressive Roofing acquisition, and ancillary products (windows, gutters, garage doors, shelving).
- **Specialty Distribution** (~$2.52B 2025 revenue, +7.8% YoY): 250+ distribution centers selling building and mechanical insulation and accessories to thousands of contractors and dealers.

The stated competitive logic is combined buying power across both segments, a dual installer-plus-distributor model that reaches a broader builder base, and a countercyclical distribution business (contractors return to distributors during downturns). Management has been shifting toward non-cyclical revenue — commercial re-roofing and maintenance, industrial mechanical insulation — through an aggressive acquisition program. That program is what attracted QXO, Brad Jacobs's building-products roll-up vehicle that previously acquired Beacon Roofing (~$11B, 2025) and Kodiak Building Partners (~$2.25B, 2026) on the way to a stated $50 billion revenue platform target.

## Financial Analysis

Underneath the deal, TopBuild is a high-cash-flow operator whose near-term earnings are being pressured by housing cyclicality and acquisition-related amortization.

**Revenue and margins:** 2025 revenue was $5.41B (+1.5%), but the growth was entirely acquisition-driven — organic volume fell ~8.1%, with pricing +0.8% and acquisitions +8.8%. Profitability is deteriorating: net income fell 16.2% to $522M from $623M in 2024. Operating margin compressed to 14.6% from 16.6%; gross margin to 29.0% from 30.5%. Both segments eroded: Installation to 18.5% (from 19.7%), Specialty Distribution to 12.8% (from 15.1%).

**Q1 2026 confirms the trend:** Revenue rose 17.2% (again entirely acquisitions; volume −5.5%, price −1.6%), but operating margin fell to 12.1% from 14.4% and net income declined to $104.8M from $123.4M. The margin pressure reflects lower volume, residential price competition, ~$14.5M of one-time branch consolidation and severance, ~$11.4M of inventory step-up amortization, and a jump in acquisition intangible amortization to $35.9M from $18.2M.

**Cash flow remains strong and high-quality:** 2025 operating cash flow was $756M, FCF $697M (~12.9% FCF margin) on minimal capex of $59M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $161M. FCF conversion above 100% of net income indicates clean earnings quality.

**The balance sheet has been materially re-levered:** Long-term debt doubled to $2.78B from $1.33B to fund the acquisition spree, lifting D/E to ~1.85x. The Altman Z-score of 2.17 sits in the grey zone, driven by leverage rather than distress. The variable-rate term loan ($1.2B at 4.92%) carries $11.8M of interest sensitivity per 100bp. Liquidity is ~$1.2B (cash $269M plus $934M revolver availability), and the company is in compliance with its 3.75x net-leverage covenant. The historical equity-shrink lever is now frozen: $434M of buybacks in 2025 and $966M in 2024 cut the share count from 32.4M to ~28.1M, but repurchases are halted under the merger agreement.

## Growth Analysis

For a standalone company the growth picture would be uninspiring: revenue growth is acquisition-funded, organic volume is contracting, and margins are compressing across both segments. Estimate revisions confirm the soft backdrop — current-year EPS consensus of $18.20 is down 1.9% over 90 days, and next-year $20.93 is down 2.6%. Next-quarter estimates fell 3.4% over 90 days. There is no positive earnings inflection here; the trend is a controlled deceleration as residential new construction weakens under affordability and rate pressure.

Growth is not the operative variable. The relevant 12-month return driver is the merger consideration, which converts a softening cyclical's standalone growth question into a binary on close probability, timing, and the realized blended value after proration. The growth_hold rationale rests entirely on that catalyst. The Growth Prospects score of 5.0 reflects the absence of an operating growth thesis offset by the deal optionality.

## Valuation Assessment

The only valuation framework that matters is the deal arithmetic, where the blended realized value depends on QXO's share price at close.

Standalone reference points: BLD's trailing P/E of 22.4x sits well above the homebuilding peer median of 12.4x (TOL 10.4x, TMHC 10.6x, NVR 15.0x, MTH 12.4x), with IBP — the closest installer comp — at 21.1x. Stripped of the takeout premium, BLD's standalone equity in a softening cycle carrying $2.8B of acquisition debt would more reasonably trade toward the installer/distributor range. The Feb 2026 grant price of $545.06 and pre-deal trading in the $400s-$500s suggest the standalone market valued the business in that band before the cycle deteriorated and the premium evaporated.

The analyst target band ($433 low, $505 high, $490.5 median) reflects deal-completion scenarios rather than fundamental DCF work: the median sits near the deal value, and the low near the bottom of plausible blended outcomes.

The asymmetry: at $404.83, completion at the $505 cash value is a ~25% gain. A blended outcome (a mix landing near $450-480 depending on QXO's price at close) is a 10-19% gain. A deal break reverts the stock toward a standalone value plausibly in the $330-400 range given cyclical headwinds and lost premium, a downside of roughly 0-18%. The skew favors holders, which supports a 6.5 valuation score, but the QXO equity leg caps the conviction because the upside is not a fixed cash number.

## Competitive Landscape

TopBuild is a market leader with genuine scale advantages: industry-leading ~18% EBITDA margins, a capital-light installer model, and growing exposure to non-cyclical commercial and industrial end markets (data centers, energy efficiency, infrastructure). These are the attributes that made it a target. Standalone competitors include Builders FirstSource, Home Depot, Lowe's, and fragmented regional installers and distributors. The ~$800B North American building-products distribution market is consolidating rapidly, and QXO's bid is itself evidence that scale players see strategic value in TopBuild's position.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-positive on a fundamental basis — share is being defended and the non-cyclical mix is improving — but homebuilder consolidation pressures installer pricing, and skilled-labor and immigration-enforcement exposure remain structural constraints. Competitive Position scores 6.0: a strong franchise whose near-term pricing power is being tested by the cycle.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is now dominated by deal mechanics rather than operating fundamentals.

**Deal-specific risks (the binding constraints):**
- **QXO stock-price risk.** The 20.2-share stock leg floats with QXO equity, which has been volatile and declining. This is the primary reason the gross spread widened: it is a consideration-value problem more than a deal-break problem, but it directly caps holder returns under proration.
- **Closing risk.** Requires both TopBuild and QXO shareholder approvals plus HSR and other regulatory clearances. The Q3 2026 target leaves room to drift toward the outside date.
- **Break risk.** A failed deal reverts BLD to a softening, re-levered standalone trading below current levels and far below $505. The $600M termination fee and non-solicit provisions discourage competing bids, reducing the odds of a higher topping offer.

**Standalone/business risks:** cyclicality and rate sensitivity (residential demand actively suppressed; multi-family slow into 2026), integration risk across eight 2025 acquisitions plus three 2026 deals (goodwill $3.07B + intangibles $1.33B = ~66% of assets, raising impairment exposure), elevated variable-rate leverage, fiberglass supplier concentration, and labor availability.

Read together, the options market is hedging deal/QXO-stock risk rather than signaling imminent collapse.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (deal completes at or near cash value): ~$490-505
The deal closes in Q3 2026 with proration leaving the blended consideration close to the $505 cash value, or QXO's stock recovers enough that the stock leg approaches parity. Holders capture the 21-25% spread to current price. This is the base expectation for a board-approved, fully-financed strategic transaction with a controlling-holder voting agreement.

### Bear Case (deal breaks): ~$330-400
Regulatory friction, a shareholder vote failure, or QXO financing strain breaks the deal. BLD reverts to standalone value — a softening cyclical (organic volume −5 to −8%, margins compressing) carrying $2.8B of acquisition debt and no longer eligible for the takeout premium. At a 12-15x multiple on pressured ~$18 EPS the standalone equity sits in the $330-400 range, a 0-18% decline from current levels.

### Base Case: ~$450-480
The deal closes but proration plus QXO's depressed stock leaves the blended realized value below the $505 cash headline, landing in the $450-480 range, a 11-19% gain over 6-9 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 6-9 months, tied directly to the Q3 2026 close target, with the January 17, 2027 outside date as the backstop.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- HSR antitrust clearance obtained and both shareholder votes scheduled or passed, compressing deal-break risk to negligible and converting the spread to near-pure arb return.
- QXO's stock recovers materially (toward $25+), lifting the value of the 20.2-share leg so the blended consideration approaches the $505 cash value with a still-meaningful spread to current price.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Credible regulatory or antitrust resistance emerges (HSR second request with substantive concerns), or either shareholder base signals rejection.
- QXO's financing falters (the ~$3B loan sale fails to clear) or QXO stock declines further such that a prorated blend falls below the current $404.83, eliminating the positive skew.
- Public reporting that either board is reconsidering, or a material adverse change at TopBuild that triggers a closing condition.

**Reassessment timeline:** The early-June stockholder election deadline and any HSR clearance news are the next discrete checkpoints; the Q3 2026 close is the primary resolution event.

## Conclusion

TopBuild is a merger-arbitrage position trading at a wide spread, and the recommendation is HOLD on a defined catalyst: deal completion in the Q3 2026 window at a blended value above the current $404.83. The thesis is concrete and falsifiable: a board-approved, fully-financed acquisition with a controlling-holder voting agreement, a break fee deterring competing bids, and a gross spread to the cash headline driven mostly by QXO's stock decline rather than deal-break risk.

The reason this stops short of BUY is the proration mechanic. Because most holders end up partly owning QXO, the realized consideration floats with a volatile, declining acquirer equity rather than locking to a fixed cash number, and a deal break reverts BLD to a softening, re-levered cyclical that has lost its takeout premium. The return skew favors holders, but the upside is capped by the QXO stock leg and the downside is real if the deal slips toward the outside date or breaks. For existing holders the position is worth keeping through resolution; new capital should wait for HSR clearance and a scheduled shareholder vote to compress break risk before treating the spread as a clean arbitrage. The score trajectory has drifted lower (6.0 → 5.9 → 5.6) as organic fundamentals softened, which the deal catalyst now overrides as the dominant driver but does not reverse on operating merit.
