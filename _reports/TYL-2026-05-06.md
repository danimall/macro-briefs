## Executive Summary

Tyler Technologies is the largest pure-play U.S. public sector software company, serving government entities across approximately 15,000 locations with 87% recurring revenue and approximately 2% annual client attrition. The stock has declined approximately 49% from its August 2025 high of $621 to $320, driven by broader market weakness and government spending concerns, despite fundamentals that have strengthened materially: Q1 2026 EPS beat consensus, FCF more than doubled YoY, guidance was raised, and subscriptions grew 18% YoY.

At $320, the stock trades at approximately 21.7x trailing FCF (~$621M) — a reasonable multiple for a capital-light business growing 9-10% topline with ~27% FCF margins and accelerating earnings. The P/E of ~45x on trailing earnings overstates the valuation picture because Tyler is in the midst of a SaaS migration that depresses near-term GAAP earnings while building long-term recurring revenue value. On FY2026 consensus EPS of $12.65, the forward P/E is approximately 25x, declining to 22x on FY2027 estimates of $14.36.

Two open-market insider purchases in February 2026 (Director Teed at $310 for $496K; CAO Diaz-Pedrosa at $325 for $198K), combined with Q1 2026 FCF performance and a raised revenue guide, suggest the fundamental floor is near current levels. The primary risk is a prolonged government IT spending freeze, though Tyler's ~2% attrition rate and multi-year contract structure provide substantial insulation.

## Company Overview

Tyler Technologies provides integrated software solutions exclusively to U.S. state, local, and federal government agencies. The company operates two segments: Enterprise Software (back-office systems for courts, public safety, education, tax/property) and Platform Technologies (payments processing, digital services, data platforms, low-code development tools).

The business model centers on extremely high switching costs. Government software implementations are multi-year projects involving deep data migration, workflow customization, and employee training. Once installed, Tyler's solutions become embedded in the operational infrastructure of agencies. This produces the company's ~2% annual client attrition rate — one of the lowest in enterprise software.

Tyler's strategic direction is clear: migrate its installed base from on-premises to cloud/SaaS while cross-selling additional products (average customer uses 3 of Tyler's modules; target is 10-12). New contracts are already 89% subscription-based. The "Tyler 2030" plan targets 80%+ of on-premises customers migrated to cloud by 2030, with peak migration volume expected 2027-2029.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Composition and Growth:**

Total revenue reached $2.33B in FY2025 (+9.1% YoY), accelerating from 5.5% growth in 2023. The critical metric is subscription revenue, which grew 18.1% to $1.59B (68% of total, up from 63%). Within subscriptions, SaaS fees grew 21% to $778M and transaction-based fees grew 16% to $808M. Q1 2026 continued the trend at $613.5M (+8.6% YoY).

Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) reached $2.06B, growing 11% YoY — providing strong forward visibility.

**Margins — Expanding on Mix Shift:**

Gross margin expanded 270bps to 46.5% in FY2025, driven by higher-margin SaaS revenue displacing lower-margin on-premises implementations. Operating margin improved to 15.3% (from 14.0%). The 73% R&D increase ($118M to $205M) is largely attributable to redeployment of engineering resources from cost-of-revenue to R&D as on-prem support teams shifted to cloud development, though Tyler also cited increased investment in product initiatives including AI. Total headcount was roughly flat.

**Cash Flow — Exceptional Quality:**

FY2025 operating cash flow: $653.5M. Company-defined free cash flow: ~$620.8M (26.6% FCF margin), after subtracting both $16.0M in property/equipment capex and $16.8M in capitalized software development. FCF conversion is approximately 197% of GAAP net income ($315.6M), reflecting the capital-light nature of the business. Q1 2026 FCF of $102.8M more than doubled vs. prior year Q1.

**Balance Sheet:**

The $600M convertible note due March 2026 has been repaid (net-share settlement for any premium, but stock was below conversion price, so minimal dilution). At December 31, 2025, Tyler had $1.015B in cash and $142.5M in investments. However, following the $600M note repayment in March 2026 and $250M in Q1 share repurchases, cash and investments declined to approximately $398M as of March 31, 2026 — before the April FTR acquisition payment and additional April buybacks. Tyler retains a $700M undrawn revolving credit facility, providing ample liquidity. Post-repayment, Tyler has effectively zero funded debt, though total liabilities of $1.24B at March 31, 2026 include deferred revenue, operating leases, and deferred tax liabilities. The balance sheet carries $2.59B goodwill and $780M intangibles from acquisition history — typical for serial acquirers in software.

**Capital Allocation:**

$1.0B buyback authorized February 2026. Through April 29, 2026, Tyler had repurchased $250M (799,856 shares) during Q1 2026 plus approximately $100M (298,144 shares) after quarter-end, leaving approximately $653M remaining under the authorization. Four tuck-in acquisitions in FY2025 ($92M total). "For The Record" acquisition (~$223M cash at closing) completed April 14, 2026 — Tyler's third-largest deal, expanding judicial recording/transcription TAM.

## Growth Analysis

Tyler's growth algorithm operates on three vectors:

**1. New Customer Acquisition:** 612 new SaaS clients added in FY2025 against a total addressable market of ~90,000+ government entities in the U.S. Tyler's 10-K discloses more than 45,000 successful installations across approximately 15,000 locations, suggesting significant remaining market opportunity in entities and agencies not yet served.

**2. Cloud Migration of Existing Base:** 488 on-premises clients converted to SaaS in FY2025. "Tyler 2030" targets 80%+ migration. Each conversion increases per-customer revenue, as SaaS pricing is typically higher than maintenance-only revenue according to management commentary. Public safety has reached "nearly 100% cloud" for new engagements; the broader portfolio is following.

**3. Cross-Sell Expansion:** Average customer uses 3 Tyler products; the long-term target is 10-12. The FTR acquisition adds a new module (digital court recording) that can be sold into Tyler's existing 3,000+ courts & justice clients. Significant cross-sell potential exists, though the revenue impact depends on product mix, pricing, and implementation timelines.

**Forward Estimates:** Consensus projects $12.65 EPS in FY2026 and $14.36 in FY2027, representing 13.5% earnings growth. Revenue estimates have been revised upward following the Q1 beat and FTR acquisition contribution (~$30M added to 2026 guidance). Some analysts project $3B revenue and $500M earnings by 2029 under the Tyler 2030 framework.

**AI Optionality:** Tyler is deploying AI internally (shortening data conversion from "months to weeks") and evaluating AI-enhanced product features that may be offered exclusively in the cloud — creating additional migration incentive. Management discussed potential per-use AI pricing models on the Q1 call.

## Valuation Assessment

At $320, Tyler's valuation metrics:
- Market cap: ~$13.5B
- P/FCF (trailing): ~21.7x on ~$621M company-defined FCF
- Forward P/E: ~25x FY2026E ($12.65), ~22x FY2027E ($14.36)
- Trailing P/E: ~45x (distorted by SaaS transition depressing near-term GAAP earnings)
- EV/Revenue: ~5.8x on $2.33B

**Peer Comparison:** Tyler's forward non-GAAP P/E of ~25x should be compared to peers on the same basis. Comparable application software companies such as Okta (~21x), Dynatrace (~19x), Guidewire (~32x), and PTC (~20-21x) trade in a range that places Tyler at roughly in line with or at a modest premium to the peer group median. Tyler's premium is supported by its superior retention metrics (~2% attrition vs. 5-10% for most SaaS peers), exceptional FCF conversion, and end-market stability from government contracts.

PTC at ~20-21x forward P/E is the closest comparable on valuation and business model maturity.

**FCF Yield:** At ~21.7x FCF, Tyler offers a ~4.6% FCF yield — attractive for a business with this quality of cash conversion, growth profile, and competitive moat.

**Why the Market May Be Wrong:** The stock's ~49% decline from peak appears driven by: (a) broader market rotation away from high-multiple software, (b) government spending freeze fears that have not materialized in Tyler's results, and (c) convertible note overhang now resolved. Fundamentals have strengthened while the stock declined, creating the opportunity.

Analyst median target of $440 implies 38% upside; consensus range is $335-$650. Simply Wall St estimates fair value at $439.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market Structure Advantage:** Tyler is the only pure-play public sector software company operating at this scale. Competitors either serve multiple verticals (Oracle, SAP, Workday — government is a sideline) or focus on narrow niches (Axon in body cameras/RMS, Motorola in communications). This enables Tyler to build the deepest domain expertise and broadest product suite in the market.

**Switching Costs as Moat:** ~2% annual attrition over decades of operating history. Government software implementations involve 2-5 year deployments, deep regulatory customization, and massive data migration. Switching vendors means re-training entire workforces. This creates de facto lock-in without the negative connotation — agencies genuinely benefit from continuity.

**Increasing Returns to Scale:** As Tyler adds modules and integrations, the value of the integrated platform increases for existing customers. Cross-selling from 3 to 5+ products deepens entrenchment. The FTR acquisition extends this logic into judicial data/recording.

**Structural Tailwinds:** Government digital transformation is accelerating (COVID pushed many agencies online). Aging on-premises infrastructure is reaching end-of-life. Cybersecurity requirements exceed what individual agencies can manage on-premises. These are multi-year secular forces independent of budget cycles.

## Risk Assessment

**Government Budget Sensitivity (Moderate):** Tyler derives 100% of revenue from government entities. While government IT budgets are funded by property taxes, fees, and stable revenue sources, a severe recession or fiscal crisis could defer spending. Mitigant: Tyler's multi-year contracts provide insulation; no evidence of slowdown in Q1 2026 results ("heightened activity" cited by management).

**AI Disruption (Moderate, Long-term):** Tyler faces the dual challenge of incorporating AI into products while defending against potential AI-native competitors. The 10-K contains extensive new AI risk language. However, Tyler's proprietary government data (court records, criminal justice, property) is a significant competitive asset — generic AI tools cannot easily replicate domain-specific government workflows without access to Tyler's data and regulatory expertise.

**Contract Disputes (~$20M Loss Reserves):** Tyler recorded approximately $10.7M in loss reserves related to two state contracts in the PT segment, plus a separate ~$9.7M non-cash loss reserve for a previously disclosed contract dispute. Combined impact of ~$20M is notable given Tyler's typically clean client relationships. Ongoing litigation risk exists but appears contained and manageable relative to $2.3B revenue.

**AWS Concentration:** Tyler relies heavily on AWS for cloud hosting infrastructure. Pricing increases or service disruptions would be materially impactful. No diversification strategy disclosed.

**Macro Selloff in High-Multiple Software:** Tyler trades at premium multiples. A broader de-rating of software multiples — particularly if rate expectations remain elevated — could compress valuation further regardless of fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($440-500, 35-55% upside)

Tyler executes the Tyler 2030 migration plan as outlined. SaaS mix reaches 75%+ by FY2027, driving operating margins toward 20%+. Cross-sell acceleration from 3 to 5+ products per customer compounds revenue growth to 12-14% annually. FCF reaches $800M-$900M by FY2028. FTR acquisition synergies and AI-enhanced products create upsell opportunities. The stock re-rates to 30x forward earnings on improved growth visibility, producing a $430-500 price target. Government IT spending remains stable-to-accelerating.

### Bear Case ($250-280, 15-22% downside)

Government budget austerity deepens. State and local entities defer IT spending for 12-18 months. Tyler's revenue growth decelerates to 4-5% while the market applies a 20x forward P/E given perceived growth deceleration. AI disruption from generalized platforms (e.g., Microsoft/Google offering government workflow tools) erodes Tyler's moat over time. Additional contract disputes surface, raising questions about implementation quality. Stock trades to $250-280 range.

## Conclusion

Tyler Technologies presents an attractive entry point at $320 after an approximately 49% drawdown from peak that is disproportionate to fundamental deterioration. The business is generating strong FCF, growing subscriptions 18% YoY, and operating in a structurally favorable market with minimal client attrition. Insider buying at current levels, a clean balance sheet post-convertible resolution (though liquidity has been materially deployed for buybacks and the FTR acquisition), and upward estimate revisions all support the thesis that the stock is mispriced relative to its forward earning power.

The ~21.7x FCF multiple for a business of this quality, with this much growth runway (90,000+ addressable entities, cross-sell from 3 to 10-12 products), and this level of competitive insulation, represents an opportunity created by the broader software de-rating. The forward P/E of ~25x is modestly above some application software peers on a forward basis, but Tyler's superior retention, FCF conversion, and non-cyclical government end-market justify a premium.

Position sizing of 2-3% is appropriate given the options market's elevated near-term uncertainty and the broader macro environment's pressure on government-exposed names. The risk/reward at 2:1 base case skew with clear fundamental support favors ownership.
