## Executive Summary

Phillips Edison delivered a clean second quarter and raised full-year guidance on July 23: Core FFO of $0.69 per share (+7.8% year over year), Same-Center NOI +3.8%, 2026 Core FFO guidance lifted to $2.73–$2.79 from prior levels, and gross acquisitions raised to $500–600M from $400–500M. The same day, Moody's affirmed the Baa2 senior unsecured rating and moved the outlook to positive, citing occupancy sustained above 97% since 2024 and Q1 leasing spreads of 36.2% (new) and 21.2% (renewal). February's $350M 4.750% 2033 notes issuance retired $323M of bank term debt maturing January 2027, cutting variable-rate exposure from $376.75M to $103.75M and extending weighted-average maturity to 5.5 years. On operating and credit execution, this is a well-run REIT.

The investment question is what that execution is worth at $40.045. At the raised guidance midpoint the stock trades at 14.5x 2026 Core FFO, a 3.25% distribution yield covered at a 47.1% payout, and an implied portfolio cap rate of roughly 6.2%–6.4% on annualized Q2 NOI. Core FFO per share grows about 6.2% in 2026 (from $2.60 in 2025), of which roughly 80bp of dollar growth is absorbed by share-count creep. Guidance implies H2 Core FFO of $1.38 per share, identical to H1, despite $500–600M of gross acquisitions — the arithmetic signature of equity-funded acquisition growth at a thin spread. PECO's Core FFO yield is 6.89% and new unsecured debt priced at 4.750% in February; the marginal blended cost of capital sits in the high-5s against grocery-anchored acquisition cap rates that management does not disclose. The internal alternative — development and redevelopment at 9%–12% underwritten incremental unlevered yields — carries only $82.0M in process against a half-billion-dollar acquisition program.

Two operating details deserve more attention than the headline print gives them. Anchor leased occupancy has declined in consecutive comparison periods (99.1% at 12/31/24 → 98.7% at 12/31/25; 98.9% at 6/30/25 → 98.4% at 6/30/26) while inline occupancy set records at 95.5%, and portfolio retention fell to 88.7% in H1 from 92.4%. The most likely explanation is deliberate anchor-box recapture and redevelopment, supported by the disclosed Publix teardown-and-rebuild program and a $4.4M Q2 decline in D&A. The same pattern would also appear if anchors were exiting involuntarily and being backfilled with lower-credit small-shop space; the share of ABR from grocery-anchored centers slipped from 95.1% to 94.1% year over year, and the filings do not disclose a signed-not-open pipeline or an anchor watchlist to resolve it. Meanwhile the cost of executing new leases rose about 30% per square foot in H1 ($36.00 vs. $27.64), converting some of the 34.5% new-lease spread into capital rather than cash flow. We maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

PECO is an internally managed UPREIT owning 302 wholly-owned grocery-anchored neighborhood and community centers across 31 states (33.9M sq ft), plus 28 centers held in three unconsolidated joint ventures, for 330 centers and roughly 37.4M sq ft in aggregate. Annualized base rent on the wholly-owned portfolio was $560.4M at June 30, 2026, up from $531.5M a year earlier. Average remaining lease term is 4.5 years excluding options. One reportable segment; Jeffrey S. Edison is Chairman, CEO, and the designated chief operating decision maker.

Rent is unusually granular for the subsector. No tenant exceeds 4.9% of ABR (Kroger 4.9%, Publix 4.9%, Albertsons 3.3%, Ahold Delhaize 3.0%) and the top 20 tenants are 29.0% of ABR, because the economics are concentrated in small-shop inline space rather than the anchors. Approximately 74% of ABR (including pro rata JV share) comes from tenants management classifies as providing necessity-based goods and services — a company-defined term, not a standardized measure.

A second, capital-light line advises three JVs and one private fund, earning asset and property management fees, leasing commissions, construction management fees, and captive-insurance premiums. Fees and management income was $7.5M in H1 2026, +23.0% year over year, but only about 2.0% of total revenue. PECO holds 14%–31% economic stakes in the JVs while acting as paid manager, and guarantees $308.2M of JV mortgage debt in limited non-recourse-carveout/environmental capacity plus a $27.5M full-recourse guaranty on an NRV mortgage due January 2029. None of these guaranties is recorded as a liability.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025 revenue was $726.6M (+9.9%), Core FFO $360.7M or $2.60 per diluted share, and Same-Center NOI $454.7M (+3.8%). H1 2026 revenue was $380.4M (+6.8%), Core FFO $191.9M or $1.38 per share (+7.0% from $1.29), and Same-Center NOI $242.1M (+3.7%). Q2 revenue was $189.6M (+6.7%) with Core FFO of $0.69 versus $0.64.

GAAP net income requires care. Q2 net income attributable to stockholders was $41.1M, diluted GAAP EPS $0.33 versus $0.10, but gain on disposal of property was $19.4M in Q2 2026 against a $0.066M loss a year earlier, and $26.2M in H1 versus $5.5M. The 3.3x GAAP EPS increase reflects disposition timing, not operating improvement. This has direct consequences for the market-data signals attached to this name. The four-quarter "beat" record (surprises of 45%, 144%, 40%, 77%) is measured against GAAP EPS estimates and is therefore largely gain-timing noise rather than evidence of forecast conservatism on the operating line; the honest FFO comparison is $0.69 versus $0.64. Similarly, the consensus revision table showing next-year EPS falling 6.4% over 90 days is a one-to-two-analyst GAAP series, and a lower assumed level of future disposition gains would produce exactly that. Management raised Core FFO guidance in the same window. We do not read negative earnings momentum here; we read a metric mismatch.

**Same-center detail.** H1 same-center revenue +3.3% against operating expenses +2.2% produced NOI +3.7% — positive operating leverage with a thin spread (about 110bp in H1, 70bp in Q2). Real estate taxes rose 5.8% in Q2 and 5.2% in H1 consolidated on higher assessed values plus acquisitions, partly recovered through CAM. The $8.0M H1 same-center rental increase was attributed to a $0.54 increase in average minimum rent per square foot and only a 0.3% occupancy improvement, confirming that growth is now rent-driven.

**Leasing.** Q2 new-lease comparable spread was 33.7% (vs. 34.6%) and renewal 21.2% (vs. 19.1%); H1 was 34.5% and 21.2%. The headline jump in new-lease ABR per square foot from $21.84 to $29.06 is substantially mix: inline rose from 66% to 78% of new-lease square footage and inline rents run roughly 2–3x anchor rents. Like-for-like new-lease pricing was flat to slightly lower; renewal pricing genuinely improved, and renewals were the larger bucket (891 of 1,224 thousand sq ft in Q2). Against that, H1 new-leasing capital was roughly 584,000 sq ft × $36.00 ≈ $21.0M versus 631,000 sq ft × $27.64 ≈ $17.4M — about $3.6M more capital on 47,000 fewer square feet. At ~1.9% of H1 Core FFO this is not thesis-breaking, but it widens the gap between FFO and distributable cash.

**Cash flow.** H1 operating cash flow was $173.6M (+10.8%), flattered by a tenant prepayment on a development project (deferred income rose to $35.6M from $23.0M). Doubling H1 gives ~$347M; normalizing away the ~$7.7M year-over-year working-capital benefit gives ~$332M. Given seasonality in snow removal, CAM true-ups, and tax timing, $330–350M is the defensible range with the lower end preferred. Non-cash revenue is moderate: straight-line rent added $6.1M to H1 income (~3.2% of Core FFO) and net above/below-market lease amortization $5.1M; net deferred rent receivable is $84.8M, about 15% of ABR.

**The capital-structure fact that governs the equity story.** H1 operating cash flow of $173.6M less $105.8M of distributions paid leaves $67.8M; less $50.5M of capital expenditures leaves roughly $17.3M of internally generated capital. Against that, PECO acquired $268.4M of property, funded with $83.4M of disposition proceeds, $83.1M net of ATM equity (2.0M shares at a $42.06 gross average), and $72.3M of net borrowings. The distribution is comfortably covered — declared distributions of $90.5M against $191.9M of Core FFO is a 47.2% payout, rising to 73.0% including all capital spending — but essentially the entire external growth program is externally funded.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $5,444.9M; liabilities $2,776.8M; stockholders' equity $2,374.3M; noncontrolling interests $293.9M; no preferred outstanding. Gross consolidated debt $2,478.1M at a 4.4% weighted-average rate and 5.5-year weighted-average term, 95.8% fixed including hedges. Revolver drawn $142.0M of $1.0B with $827.3M available to January 2029. Company-reported net debt of $2,528.9M (JV pro rata inclusive) against TTM annualized Adjusted EBITDAre of $492.3M is 5.14x, improved from 5.2x at year-end. The $1,390.0M accumulated deficit reflects cumulative distributions in excess of GAAP income and is structurally normal for a REIT. Goodwill of $29.1M is 0.5% of assets; no impairments in either period.

Two near-dated mechanics: the $161.75M term loan matures January 2027 (coverable from revolver availability but requiring action), and PECO's single $200M notional swap at 3.4% fixed SOFR matures September 1, 2026. If unreplaced, unhedged variable debt rises mechanically from $103.75M (4.2% of gross debt) to $303.75M (12.3%). Further out, the 2.625% 2031 notes carry a $311.7M fair value against $345.5M carrying; refinancing $350M from 2.625% to roughly 4.95% would add about $8.1M of annual interest, or ~$0.058 per share.

We flag one methodological point carried from the underlying research: standardized bankruptcy and quality screens computed on an XBRL revenue tag capturing only ~$12.8M against actual FY2025 revenue of $726.6M are unreliable for this issuer, and Altman Z is structurally inapposite for a deliberately levered, heavily depreciated, low-turnover equity REIT. Leverage, liquidity, and cash-flow components are unaffected, and they read as investment grade.

**Financial Health: 6.8.** Baa2 with a positive outlook, 5.1x leverage, 95.8% fixed-rate debt, a 47% payout, no preferred, no impairments, and $827M of revolver capacity. The deduction is capital efficiency rather than resilience: only ~$17M of H1 free capital after distributions and capex means growth is structurally dependent on external issuance, and the ATM is close to exhausted (approximately $91M of the $250M February 2024 program remained at June 30, reduced by ~$6.5M gross issued subsequently).

## Growth Analysis

The 2026 guidance raise sets Core FFO at $2.73–$2.79 per share (midpoint $2.76), Nareit FFO $2.67–$2.72, Same-Center NOI growth 3.40%–4.00%, and gross acquisitions $500–600M. From $2.60 in 2025, the midpoint implies +6.15% per-share growth. Decomposed: dollar Core FFO growth of roughly 7.0% (from $360.7M to approximately $386M) less about 80bp absorbed by share count (138.9M FY2025 weighted-average diluted shares to roughly 139.5–140M).

Three constraints define the growth ceiling.

First, occupancy has no headroom. Total leased occupancy of 97.3% and anchor occupancy of 98.4% mean incremental NOI must come from mark-to-market on expirations, not fill. PECO's 4.5-year average remaining lease term is genuinely helpful here — shorter duration captures the mark-to-market faster — at the cost of more frequent re-leasing capital, which is precisely what the 30% increase in new-lease cost per foot reflects.

Second, the acquisition machine is running at a thin spread. At $40.045 the Core FFO yield is 6.89%; February unsecured issuance priced at 4.750%. A blended marginal cost in the high-5s leaves modest room against grocery-anchored cap rates, which management does not disclose. The only stated return hurdle is the 9%–12% underwritten incremental unlevered yield on development and redevelopment, with $82.0M in process. Guidance implying H2 per-share Core FFO of $1.38, identical to H1, despite $500–600M of gross acquisitions is consistent with equity funding offsetting in-year accretion. We read that as non-heroic guidance construction, but also as confirmation that scale growth is not translating into per-share acceleration.

Third, the fee business is real but small. Fees and management income of $7.5M grew 23.0% in H1 and represents about 2.0% of revenue; growing NGCF (three properties, 31% PECO) is a sensible way to control assets and earn fees without full balance-sheet consumption, but it cannot move the per-share needle in the medium term. The December 2025 dissolution of Necessity Retail Partners removed the realized performance income add-back from Adjusted EBITDAre.

Contractual fixed lease payments total $2,702.7M ($266.9M for the remainder of 2026, $528.1M in 2027, $457.3M in 2028). This is not backlog: it excludes variable and recovery income (23% of Q2 rental income), excludes all renewal options, and is reduced for abatements and cash-basis tenants.

**Growth Prospects: 5.5.** Durable mid-single-digit per-share compounding with high visibility and low volatility, but no identified path to acceleration, a fully priced occupancy base, and per-share accretion partially neutralized by the equity funding required to buy the growth.

## Competitive Landscape

The sector tailwind is supply, not demand. Neighborhood retail construction has been minimal for over a decade, which is why 97%+ occupancy is broad-based across the grocery-anchored group and why 21%–34% releasing spreads are achievable. Management states the mechanism plainly: rental rate growth depends on negotiating leverage, "which typically occurs when occupancy at our centers is high." The corollary is that the tailwind is fully expressed in occupancy, so forward NOI growth across the group is now a function of mark-to-market alone.

PECO's differentiation rests on three evidenced items. Rent granularity: no tenant above 4.9% of ABR and top-20 concentration of 29.0%, materially more diversified than grocery-anchored peers whose anchors carry a larger share. Anchor credit quality: Kroger, Publix, Albertsons, Ahold Delhaize, Sprouts, Giant Eagle, Raley's, UNFI. Vertical integration monetized twice — leasing, property management, construction, and captive insurance charged on owned assets and earned as fees on managed assets, a channel few similarly sized shopping-center REITs operate.

The trajectory is where the reservation lies. Anchor leased occupancy has declined on both year-over-year and year-end comparisons; portfolio retention fell 370bp to 88.7% with inline retention at 81.0%; the share of ABR from grocery-anchored centers slipped from 95.1% to 94.1%. The benign reading — deliberate anchor recapture and redevelopment, supported by the disclosed Publix teardown program, the $4.4M Q2 decline in D&A, and 9%–12% underwritten redevelopment yields — is the more likely one. But PECO discloses no signed-not-open pipeline, no anchor watchlist percentage, and no leased-to-physical occupancy bridge, so the alternative reading (anchors exiting through rationalization, backfilled by smaller-credit inline tenants at higher gross rents and higher capital cost) cannot be excluded from the filings. A big-box involuntary vacancy typically carries a 12–24 month gap between vacate and rent commencement plus heavy capital.

Relative to Kimco and Regency, PECO carries less scale and a higher cost of capital; InvenTrust is the closer size comparison. Same-Center NOI guidance of 3.40%–4.00% is mid-pack for the group in this cycle.

**Competitive Position: 6.2.** A genuinely durable asset base with a differentiated fee platform, offset by an unresolved anchor-occupancy pattern, deteriorating retention, and rising re-leasing capital intensity. Stable-to-marginally-eroding trajectory rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Occupancy has no headroom and anchor space is the pressure point.** Addressed above. The falsifiable test is whether anchor occupancy stabilizes or recovers by year-end while retention returns above 91%.

**Catastrophe concentration with retained risk.** Florida 11.8%, California 11.3%, and Texas 10.2% of wholly-owned ABR total 33.3% in three states, and the 10-Q explicitly warns the portfolio is "particularly susceptible to adverse natural or economic events" there. PECO's captive insurer, Silver Rock, writes general liability, wind, reinsurance, and other coverage for PECO and all three JVs, backed by $30.7M of letters of credit that also cover development completion guaranties. A severe Gulf or Southeast wind event would hit through two channels: property damage and downtime, plus captive claims. The alternative reading is that a well-capitalized captive is a standard sector cost-efficiency tool, and PECO's is subject to regulatory capitalization and third-party actuarial review. Either way, retention is not zero.

**External capital dependence.** With approximately $17M of H1 internally generated capital after distributions and capex, the $500–600M acquisition program requires debt, dispositions, and equity in combination — PECO used all three in H1. The ATM has roughly $84–85M of capacity, or about 2.1M shares at $40, a further ~1.6% of shares and ~1.6% ownership dilution. A new or upsized program is a plausible near-term event, and issuance below the $42.06 H1 average would be less accretive.

**Financing mechanics.** The September 1, 2026 swap maturity ($200M notional at 3.4% fixed SOFR) would triple unhedged variable-rate debt to $303.75M if unreplaced. The $161.75M term loan matures January 2027, coverable from $827.3M of revolver availability. The 2031 notes step-up is roughly $0.058 per share and distant.

**JV asymmetry.** PECO holds 14%–31% economic interests while guaranteeing $308.2M of JV mortgage debt in limited capacity plus $27.5M on a full-recourse basis, none recorded as a liability. Apportionment agreements limit but do not eliminate the asymmetry.

**Governance-linked disposition constraint.** Tax protection agreements (2017 TPA expiring October 4, 2027; 2021 TPA running to 2031) obligate the Operating Partnership to indemnify contributing partners — including Mr. Edison, other officers, and certain directors — for personal tax liabilities triggered by disposition of protected properties, with a make-whole amount of approximately $109.3M at June 30, 2026 and no recorded liability. This creates a personal financial interest in not selling specific assets. The countervailing evidence is that the constraint appears non-binding in practice: 13 properties were disposed between 12/31/24 and 6/30/26, including four in H1 2026 for $86.9M at a $26.2M gain and three more for $39.7M post-quarter. TPAs are also standard UPREIT contribution consideration. We note the quantified constraint without inferring motive.

**Tenant credit and consumer bifurcation.** Management flags retailer rationalization and pressure on lower-income shoppers, while stating tariff-related sourcing uncertainty has produced "minimal disruption to leasing activity or rent collection trends." Two indicators diverge and should be kept distinct: the P&L collectibility adjustment improved to $(1.313)M in Q2 from $(1.446)M and to $(2.464)M in H1 from $(2.673)M, while the general reserve balance rose to $3.7M from $2.6M against gross AR growth to ~$63.5M from ~$54.6M (4.8% → 5.8% of gross AR). Current collection performance is good; the reserve build is small and plausibly mechanical. Broader consumer context is a caution rather than a live problem here: weak discretionary spending pressures small-shop tenants at the margin, which is exactly why 74% necessity-based ABR and 89.6% Q2 retention matter.

**Disclosure notes.** The Q2 10-Q states there have been no material changes to risk factors from the FY2025 10-K, so nothing was added in either quarter of 2026. An August 10, 2026 8-K (Items 8.01 and 9.01) could not be retrieved and should not be treated as material in either direction pending review. A ~$38M Chaska Commons acquisition reported July 15 by a regional trade publication is consistent with the disclosed program but not individually confirmed in a filing.

### Options Market Signal

The options data on PECO carries low information content and we do not treat it as a signal. Only 21 contracts exist across four expirations. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 108.9% against 15.6% one-year realized volatility, and a prior snapshot at 428.7%, are almost certainly artifacts of wide quotes on near-zero open interest rather than a market pricing imminent stress in a 3.25%-yielding investment-grade REIT that has traded in a $33–$44 band for a year. The put/call open interest ratio of 0.07 rests on a negligible base. The one tenor with plausible information is the January 2027 expiry at 22.2% implied against 15.6% realized, a modest premium consistent with rate-driven uncertainty. Skew is unreported, so no directional inference is available. Short interest of 4.2% of float and 4.5 days to cover indicate no crowded bearish position.

**Risk Factors: 6.2.** Few binary idiosyncratic events, investment-grade credit, 95.8% fixed-rate debt, a 47% payout, and diversified tenancy against genuine, quantified exposures: catastrophe concentration with captive retention, unresolved anchor occupancy, external-funding dependence, and $335.7M of unrecorded contingent guaranty exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($47.50, +18.6%)

PECO compounds Core FFO per share at 6%–7% through 2027 on a 3.25% covered distribution, and the multiple re-rates from 14.5x toward 16x on two developments. First, Moody's converts the positive outlook to a Baa1 upgrade, lowering unsecured spreads and widening the acquisition spread that currently limits per-share accretion; the February 4.750% issuance becomes the high-water cost mark rather than the floor. Second, the anchor-occupancy decline resolves as confirmed redevelopment: anchor occupancy recovers toward 99%, redevelopment deliveries at 9%–12% unlevered yields lift Same-Center NOI above the 4.00% guidance ceiling, and retention returns above 91% as recapture concludes. Cap-rate compression against a portfolio already carried at 1.16x undepreciated cost would support the multiple. The unused $250M repurchase authorization provides optionality if the stock de-rates instead. Sell-side targets at $46–$47 are consistent with this path.

### Bear Case ($35.50, -11.4%)

A 3.25% yielding REIT with an implied 6.2% cap rate de-rates to 12–12.5x forward Core FFO, and the acquisition spread turns negative: management either slows the $500–600M program (removing the dollar growth that per-share guidance already treats as neutral) or continues issuing equity below $40, converting acquisitions into dilution. Concurrently, the anchor pattern resolves unfavorably — one or two grocer format exits or bankruptcies create involuntary big-box vacancy with 12–24 months of downtime and heavy capital, while retention stays below 89% and new-lease cost per foot compounds above $40. Same-Center NOI decelerates below 3.4%, the September swap roll and January 2027 term loan reprice at higher rates, and a Gulf or Southeast wind event hits both the portfolio and Silver Rock. In that stack, $35.50 is reachable without any distribution risk — the 47% payout stays intact, so the damage is multiple and per-share growth rather than income.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold). Horizon: 12 months, with reassessment at the Q3 print in late October 2026.**

This is the third consecutive HOLD, so the bar for repeating it warrants explicit treatment. The score has risen from 5.8 to 5.96 on evidence, not sentiment: guidance was raised on July 23, Moody's moved the outlook to positive the same day, leverage fell to 5.1x, and the February refinancing removed the January 2027 bank maturities from the near-term picture — all while the price is below the level at the prior review. That combination is an improvement in risk/reward. It is not sufficient for ACCUMULATE. At 14.5x forward Core FFO with 6% per-share growth, a peer-median yield, an implied cap rate that is not a discount to private-market pricing, and a marginal cost of capital converging on acquisition yields, expected total return of roughly 8% is approximately fair compensation. Nor is there impairment justifying REDUCE: the distribution is covered at 47%, credit is improving, and 95.8% of debt is fixed.

**Base case $43** — 15.0x 2027E Core FFO of ~$2.91 (5.5% per-share growth from the 2026 midpoint), consistent with a flat-to-marginally-higher multiple. +7.4% price, ~10.6% total return.
**Bull case $47.50** — 16.3x 2027E. +18.6%.
**Bear case $35.50** — 12.2x 2027E. -11.4%.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Price in the low $36s or below with 2026 guidance intact — approximately 13.1x forward Core FFO and a 3.6% yield, which would restore the margin of safety the current price lacks.
2. Anchor leased occupancy stabilizing at or above 98.4% with portfolio retention back above 91% in the Q3 or Q4 report, resolving the recapture-versus-attrition ambiguity in favor of the benign reading.
3. A Moody's upgrade to Baa1, or disclosed acquisition cap rates demonstrating a spread above 100bp over the blended marginal cost of capital — either would validate that the $500–600M program is per-share accretive rather than scale-additive.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Any equity issuance materially below $38, or an upsized ATM used at prices below the H1 $42.06 average, without a disclosed accretive use of proceeds.

## Conclusion

PECO does what it says it does. Occupancy has been above 97% since 2024, renewal spreads improved to 21.2%, Same-Center NOI is running at 3.7%–3.8%, the distribution consumes 47% of Core FFO, leverage fell to 5.1x, and February's refinancing converted $323M of short-dated bank debt into 7-year fixed paper. Moody's positive outlook is a fair verdict on that execution.

The equity, at 14.5x the raised 2026 Core FFO guidance midpoint, already reflects it. Per-share growth of about 6% requires a $500–600M acquisition program funded almost entirely externally, at a spread over a high-5s marginal cost of capital that management does not quantify. Guidance implying H2 per-share Core FFO identical to H1 is the clearest evidence that scale growth and per-share growth are not the same thing here. Two operating details — anchor occupancy declining across consecutive comparisons and retention falling 370bp while new-lease capital cost per foot rose 30% — most likely reflect deliberate redevelopment, but the filings do not contain the disclosures needed to confirm that, and the Q3 report is the natural place to resolve it.

Existing holders should keep the position for the covered distribution and mid-single-digit compounding. New capital should wait for either the low $36s, confirmation that the anchor pattern is redevelopment rather than attrition, or evidence that the acquisition spread has widened enough to make the growth program accretive per share. **HOLD.**
