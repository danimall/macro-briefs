**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Reinsurance Group of America is one of the world's largest dedicated life and health reinsurers, with roughly $4.3 trillion of life reinsurance in force and $164 billion in total assets. The investment case turns on three things working at once: a durable, knowledge-based underwriting moat, a structurally growing Financial Solutions franchise (asset-intensive annuity reinsurance, longevity, and pension risk transfer), and a valuation that compensates for the genuine assumption and credit risks embedded in the model. At ~$211, the stock trades at approximately 7.8x current-year adjusted operating EPS consensus ($27.07) and ~11.4x trailing GAAP EPS, against a 15-16% adjusted operating ROE. Analyst targets cluster well above spot (mean $252, median $261), and current-year estimates have risen +3.6% to +3.8% over the past 90 and 30 days respectively.

The headline GAAP optics understate the business. GAAP ROE of 9.7% is depressed by an ~$8.5 billion unrealized loss position on available-for-sale bonds, a rate-driven mark management intends to hold to recovery, not a credit event. The operative number is adjusted operating ROE of 15.2% (16.2% ex-notables), and operating cash flow of $2.87 billion in Q1'26 alone — roughly twice net income — confirms the underlying earnings stream is real. Naive screening tools (an Altman Z-score of 0.33, D/E of 10.6) flag RGA as distressed; these are artifacts of treating ~$150 billion of policyholder reserves as debt and are meaningless for a life reinsurer.

We rate RGA a BUY with a base-case target of $250 over 12 months, roughly 19% above the current price. The thesis is supported by rising estimates against a falling-stock setup (the stock is ~8% below its February high while consensus has moved up), a discounted multiple, and disciplined capital return. We hold the score at 6.6 — flat versus the prior read — and acknowledge the recent downward drift in our growth, competitive-position, and management-quality scores. The downward revisions reflect genuine considerations (a CFO transition, the cyclical roll-over in the broader reinsurance hard market, the absence of open-market insider buying) rather than fundamental deterioration, and the discounted valuation now more than compensates for them.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1973 and headquartered in Chesterfield, Missouri, RGA reinsures mortality, morbidity, longevity, and lapse risk — risks that are reasonably predictable over multi-year horizons, in contrast to the catastrophe-driven volatility of property-casualty reinsurers. The business runs along two product lines across four geographic segments (US & Latin America, Canada, EMEA, Asia Pacific) plus Corporate:

- **Traditional reinsurance** — individual and group life, health, disability, critical illness, and long-term care. This is the legacy core, where RGA competes on mortality data depth, underwriting expertise, and speed of quoting.
- **Financial Solutions** — the growth engine: asset-intensive annuity reinsurance, longevity reinsurance, pension risk transfer, stable value products, capital/financial reinsurance, and a funding agreement-backed note program. This line is increasingly driven by investment spreads rather than mortality experience.

Client concentration is moderate — the five largest clients account for ~21% of gross premiums, with no single client above 10%. Financial strength ratings are strong: A.M. Best A+ (second highest, stable), S&P AA- (stable), Moody's A1 (negative outlook). The negative Moody's outlook is the most concrete external risk flag because certain treaties allow client termination or require collateral posting on a downgrade.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded meaningfully: ~$18.6B (FY23) → ~$22.1B (FY24) → ~$23.7B (FY25), driven by Financial Solutions expansion and the Equitable transaction that closed July 31, 2025.

Q1 2026 (period ended March 31, 2026) was strong across the board:
- Net income of $330M ($4.98 diluted EPS) versus $286M ($4.27) a year earlier.
- Adjusted operating EPS of $6.97 versus $5.66 prior year, beating ~$6.19 consensus by ~13%.
- Total revenues of ~$6.49B (10-Q), up ~24% YoY.
- Net premiums of $4,595M (+14%) and net investment income of $1,701M (+38%), the latter reflecting both the Equitable asset base and higher reinvestment yields.
- Adjusted operating income before tax of $611M versus $485M (+26%).

The earnings-quality picture is favorable. Operating cash flow of $2,873M in Q1'26 ran roughly twice net income, a stronger liquidity signal than the distorted balance-sheet ratios. The gap between the 9.7% TTM GAAP ROE and the 15.2-16.2% adjusted operating ROE is almost entirely AOCI noise from the ~$8.5B unrealized bond loss (up from ~$7.0B), against only ~$1.1B in unrealized gains. This is a held-to-recovery mark on a 94.3% investment-grade fixed-maturity portfolio (corporates ~68%, ex-spread average yield 4.93%, +29bps YoY), not a credit deterioration.

The balance sheet is sound for the business model. Total equity is ~$13.4B, total investments ~$137B, total debt ~$6.1B with an undrawn $850M syndicated revolver. The minimum consolidated net worth covenant of $5.8B sits against $13.4B of equity — ample cushion. The effective tax rate of ~24.9% is elevated versus the US statutory rate due to foreign jurisdictions and the global minimum tax (Pillar II).

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and disciplined: the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.93 (from $0.89), a $500M buyback was authorized January 29, 2026 ($450M remaining at quarter-end), and a financing rotation issued $400M of 6.375% subordinated debentures to redeem $400M of 5.75% notes in June 2026.

The earnings track record is solid but lumpy: across the last four reported quarters, three beats (+14.2%, +33.5%, +9.0%) and one miss (-16.1% in Q2'25). The single miss is a reminder that assumption charges can produce sharp quarterly swings even at a well-diversified reinsurer.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two distinct legs. The first is the structural shift of the industry toward Financial Solutions — insurers increasingly cede annuity and pension blocks to optimize capital, and RGA is leaning hard into this. The Equitable transaction (a 75% quota share of ~$12B of in-force individual life reserves plus ~$16.1B of separate-account liabilities via modco) is the single largest driver of recent revenue and NII growth, contributing ~$39M of pre-tax income in Q1'26 and a meaningful portion of the NII jump. RGA's use of Ruby Re — a sponsored, third-party-capitalized, non-consolidated sidecar holding ~$4.1B of ceded recoverable for US asset-intensive business — provides a capital-efficient funding mechanism to keep scaling this line.

The second leg is the traditional mortality book, where Q1'26 commentary cited a favorable claims environment and broad-based regional growth. This is the more cyclical and assumption-sensitive component.

Estimate revisions support the growth call. Current-year consensus has risen to $27.07 from $26.14 ninety days ago (+3.6%) and from $26.08 thirty days ago (+3.8%) — modest in absolute terms but moving in the right direction while the stock has declined ~8% from its February high. Next-year consensus of $29.02 implies ~7% EPS growth. These are not the +30% estimate-revision velocities that flag a sharp acceleration, so we do not treat this as the highest-conviction signal in our framework; it is a steady, confirming positive rather than a step-change.

The growth prospects score has drifted down from 7.5 to our current 6.8 across the trajectory. The decline reflects the maturation and increasing investment-spread dependence of the model rather than a demand problem — future earnings depend progressively more on credit discipline and reinvestment yields than on mortality alone, which raises both the upside and the variance.

## Valuation Assessment

RGA is genuinely cheap relative to its quality and growth, and the discount is the core of the BUY case. On trailing GAAP EPS of $17.69 the multiple is 11.4x. Book value per share is artificially suppressed by the AOCI mark, so we anchor on operating earnings power and ROE.

The peer table requires care. The same-sector "Reinsurance" comparables (Everest Group at 6.9x, RenaissanceRe at 4.9x, MGIC at 8.1x, SiriusPoint at 6.0x) are predominantly property-casualty and mortgage insurers operating at the peak of a hard pricing cycle now rolling over — their depressed multiples reflect cyclical earnings the market expects to normalize down. RGA's life/health and longevity book has a structurally different, more recurring earnings profile and deserves a premium to that P&C cohort, not parity. Against life-insurance and diversified financial peers, a high-single-digit operating multiple on a 15-16% ROE franchise is a discount.

The clearest external anchor is the analyst target set: high $288, low $212 — with the low target essentially at the current price and the mean ~20% above. Evercore ISI raised its target to $267 (Outperform) on May 18, 2026. On a stock that is cheap relative to rising estimates (forward P/E under 8x), these targets function as floors rather than ceilings; consensus has historically lagged RGA's operating performance.

The valuation dimension carries a well-documented tendency to flag value traps when cheapness lacks a catalyst. Here the catalysts are identifiable: rising consensus estimates, the Equitable book ramping, ongoing buyback execution shrinking the share count, and the potential for a Moody's outlook stabilization. We score valuation 7.0 — high enough to credit the asymmetry, restrained because the multiple has been persistently low for a reason (assumption-charge variance and the AOCI overhang depress reported book and GAAP ROE).

## Competitive Landscape

Life and health reinsurance is consolidated among a handful of global players, and RGA is firmly top-tier. Its moat is knowledge-based — mortality data depth, underwriting expertise, transaction execution speed, and entrenched client relationships — rather than scale of capital. The 10-K candidly notes that several competitors have greater financial resources, which keeps the moat anchored in intangibles that are hard to replicate but also hard to defend against capital-rich entrants in commoditized lines.

The structural tailwind is the Financial Solutions shift, where RGA's deal-execution capability and capital-partnership structures (Ruby Re) position it well to win annuity and pension cessions. The competitive-position score has eased from 7.5 to 6.8, reflecting two things: the broader reinsurance hard market rolling over (a cyclical headwind to pricing across the industry), and the model's growing dependence on investment spreads, where the moat is narrower than in core mortality underwriting. The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-softening rather than deteriorating, which keeps the score solidly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

The most company-specific risks, drawn from the 10-K (which the Q1'26 10-Q confirms had no material changes):

1. **Assumption risk (mortality/morbidity/longevity)** — the central risk. Adverse experience versus pricing flows through future-policy-benefit remeasurement and can produce lumpy charges. The prior UK/EMEA mortality assumption update that drove a notable loss is the cautionary precedent; Q1'26 by contrast cited a favorable environment. This is the key quarter-to-quarter variable.

2. **Reserve financing and collateral/captive risk** — Regulation XXX/A-XXX and PBR require large statutory reserves financed through captives and Bermuda/Barbados vehicles. The cost and availability of collateral directly constrains how much business RGA can write, and regulatory change here is a structural overhang.

3. **Rating downgrade risk** — Moody's negative outlook is live. A downgrade could trigger treaty termination or collateral posting, with strategic consequences disproportionate to the modest direct financial impact.

4. **Investment/credit and rate risk** — the large fixed-income portfolio, the unrealized loss position, and the funds-withheld arrangements that expose RGA to performance on assets it does not control. The shift toward asset-intensive business raises the stakes on credit discipline.

5. **Counterparty/retrocession risk** — RGA cedes risk and relies on retrocessionaires, with ~3 major reinsurers representing roughly a third of ceded receivables (ex-Ruby Re).

6. **Integration risk** — the Equitable transaction is large and recent; due-diligence accuracy and liability/data integration matter.

We score risk factors 6.0. The risks are real and specific, but most are inherent to the business model and broadly understood by the market. The binary tail — a Moody's downgrade — is identifiable and monitorable rather than hidden, and the discounted multiple already embeds a degree of assumption-charge variance.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm and broadly consistent with the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 22.5% sits essentially in line with 23.4% one-year historical vol (spread -1.0%, "fair"), and IV is in the 5th percentile of its current chain — uncertainty is low and the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst. The put/call OI ratio of 0.21 is light, and short interest is negligible (1.8% of float, 2.7 days to cover), so there is little bearish positioning to lean on. The one detail of interest is a 5.5% near-term put/call skew (elevated put demand at the front), which fades to ~0.1% by the six-month tenor. This points to some near-term downside hedging, consistent with the quarterly assumption-charge risk, but no structural bearish stance. The flat term structure and cheap IV mark this as a reasonable entry point for a fundamental long.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($288)

The Financial Solutions franchise continues compounding as insurers cede annuity and pension blocks; NII growth from the Equitable book and higher reinvestment yields sustains a 15-16% operating ROE. Mortality experience stays favorable, estimate revisions continue rising, and the multiple re-rates toward 9-10x forward operating EPS as the market credits the recurring, spread-driven earnings stream. Buybacks shrink the share count meaningfully, and a Moody's outlook stabilization removes the rating overhang. This is roughly the high analyst target of $288 (~37% upside).

### Bear Case ($175)

A lumpy assumption charge — UK longevity, US mortality, or a GMLB hedging gap — produces a quarterly miss reminiscent of Q2'25, reminding the market that reported earnings are volatile. Credit spreads widen or defaults rise in the corporate-heavy portfolio, pressuring the spread business. A Moody's downgrade triggers collateral posting or treaty friction. The multiple compresses toward the P&C-reinsurer cohort. Downside to ~$175 is roughly 17% below current.

### Base Case ($250)

Operating EPS lands near the $27 current-year / $29 next-year consensus, ROE holds in the mid-teens, capital return continues, and the multiple drifts modestly higher toward ~8.5x forward as estimates rise. This produces a ~$250 target, about 19% above the current price, consistent with the mean analyst estimate and below the median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $250.** ~8.6x the $29.02 next-year operating EPS estimate, in line with the mean analyst target and a modest re-rating from the current ~7.8x current-year multiple. ~19% upside from $211.

**Bull case target: $288.** ~10x forward operating EPS on multiple expansion plus sustained estimate revisions; ~37% upside.

**Bear case target: $175.** A multiple compression toward the P&C-reinsurer cohort following an assumption charge or rating downgrade; ~17% downside.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two to three earnings reports (the next around August 2026) and any Moody's rating action.

**Upside/downside from current:** +19% to base, +37% to bull, -17% to bear — a roughly 1.5:1 reward-to-risk skew favoring the long.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- A Moody's downgrade from A1, or a deterioration in the S&P/A.M. Best outlooks to negative.
- Adjusted operating ROE falling below 12% for two consecutive quarters, or a single assumption charge exceeding ~$200M pre-tax.
- Current-year operating EPS estimates turning negative on revision (a sustained downtrend rather than the current +3.8% 30-day / +3.6% 90-day rise).

## Conclusion

RGA offers a quality life/health reinsurance franchise at a discounted multiple, with rising estimates meeting a stock that has pulled back ~8% from its February high — an improving rather than deteriorating risk/reward. The recent downward drift in our scores (management quality from 7.0 to 5.8 on the CFO transition and absence of open-market buying; growth and competitive position each easing on the model's growing spread-dependence and the cyclical reinsurance roll-over) reflects genuine, monitorable considerations, but none amount to fundamental impairment. Operating cash flow at twice net income, a 15-16% adjusted operating ROE, disciplined capital return, and analyst targets clustering ~20% above spot support a constructive view.

The management-quality score is the principal drag: the CFO transition (Axel André departing July 17, 2026, succeeded effective June 22, 2026 by 25-year internal veteran Laura Cockrill) reads as orderly succession by a capital/collateral specialist well-suited to the role, but it coincides with the Financial Solutions book scaling, and the absence of open-market insider buying — alongside one EVP's ~$1.47M sale — keeps the signal neutral-to-soft. We weight this appropriately but note it is the dimension our framework treats as least predictive in the current regime.

We recommend BUY with a 12-month base-case target of $250 (~19% upside). The principal risks to monitor are quarterly assumption charges, credit performance in the corporate-heavy portfolio, and the Moody's negative outlook. Given the discounted multiple, the favorable reward-to-risk skew, and the steady positive estimate revisions, the asymmetry favors ownership.
