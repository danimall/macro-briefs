**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Ormat Technologies is the world's only vertically integrated geothermal energy company, operating 1,340 MW of generating capacity across 35 power plants globally. The company has undergone a substantial re-rating over the past 12 months (+93% from its 52-week low) driven by hyperscaler data center demand for 24/7 baseload carbon-free power — geothermal is the only renewable technology that delivers 84% capacity factors. The Google 150MW PPA signed in February 2026 and the Switch 13MW PPA validated this demand-pull dynamic.

However, the stock now trades at 67x trailing earnings and roughly 62x forward consensus for 2026, making it the most expensive utility by a factor of 3x relative to peers (median P/E of 21.5x). Near-term EPS estimates are falling sharply (-35% over 90 days for the current quarter), the core Electricity segment revenue declined in 2025 despite 12.5% total company growth, and CFO/EVP insider selling to near-zero positions at $130-135 raises questions about near-term conviction from those with the deepest visibility.

## Company Overview

Ormat operates three segments: Electricity (70% of 2025 revenue), Products (22%), and Energy Storage (8%). The company explores geothermal resources, manufactures proprietary Ormat Energy Converter units, builds power plants, and operates them under long-term PPAs averaging 14 years remaining. Operations span the U.S. (Nevada, California, Hawaii, New Jersey), Kenya, Guatemala, Indonesia, Honduras, and Guadeloupe.

The February 2026 Google PPA (up to 150MW via NV Energy's Clean Transition Tariff, deliveries 2028-2030, 15-year term post-COD) positions Ormat at the intersection of two structural demand themes: AI infrastructure buildout and carbon-free baseload generation.

The company's energy storage segment (415MW/1,038MWh across 19 BESS facilities) is scaling rapidly with a 2.7GW/10GWh development pipeline, though it faces intensifying competition and China tariff risks on battery imports.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $734M (2022) → $829M (2023) → $880M (2024) → $990M (2025) → $1,110-$1,160M guided for 2026. Q1 2026 delivered $404M (+75.8% YoY), though this was heavily inflated by a $105M one-time Topp 2 product sale.

**Profitability concerns:** Despite 35% cumulative revenue growth since 2022, net income has been essentially flat at ~$124M for three consecutive years (2023-2025). Diluted EPS of $2.02 barely exceeds the 2022 level. The Electricity segment's gross margin compressed from 36.6% (2023) to 28.5% (2025) due to higher depreciation, property tax increases, and resource curtailments. Generation revenue actually declined 1.2% in 2025.

**Cash flow and leverage:** Operating cash flow of $335M (2025) against $620M capex produces -$285M free cash flow. The company raised $1B in convertible notes (March 2026) to fund growth capex of $675M guided for 2026 and repay 2027 maturities. Total debt stands at ~$2.66B with net debt/EBITDA of 4.36x. Interest expense consumed 14.3% of 2025 revenue ($142M) and will increase with the new convertibles. The balance sheet is stretched but within covenant limits (6.0x max).

**Tax benefit dependency:** Income from sale of tax benefits contributed $66.7M in 2025 — more than half of pretax income of $105.7M. This is recurring under IRA/OBBBA but represents legislative concentration risk.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers:**
- Energy Storage segment: Revenue grew from negligible to $79M in 2025, with Q1 2026 at $45M (+153% YoY) and margins expanding to 59.1%. The 2.7GW pipeline suggests this segment could become 20%+ of revenue by 2028.
- Product segment: Backlog of $352M (68% concentrated in New Zealand). Revenue is lumpy — the $105M Topp 2 delivery inflated Q1 but won't recur quarterly.
- New PPAs at $100+/MWh vs. $60-$80 historically — structural pricing uplift that flows through over the next decade as legacy contracts expire.

**Medium-term catalysts (2028 targets):**
- Electricity: 1.65-1.75 GW (from 1,340 MW, +23-31%)
- Storage: 950-1,050 MW (from 415 MW, +130-153%)
- Google PPA deliveries begin 2028-2030

**Long-term optionality:**
- Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS) partnership with SLB and $25M Sage Geosystems investment could dramatically expand the addressable geothermal market. However, EGS remains pre-commercial with uncertain timing.

**Estimate revision headwinds:** Current quarter EPS estimates have fallen 35% over 90 days (from $0.39 to $0.25). Next quarter estimates fell 44% ($0.42 to $0.23). Full-year 2026 estimates declined more modestly (-6.2% over 90 days to $2.22). This divergence between quarterly cuts and relatively stable annual forecasts suggests analysts expect lumpy quarterly revenue recognition (product segment timing) rather than structural deterioration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $137.90 (near the data's last price of $139.69), Ormat trades at:
- **67x trailing P/E** (on $2.02 EPS)
- **~62x forward P/E** (on $2.22 consensus 2026)
- **~55x next year** (on $2.49 consensus 2027)
- **Dividend yield:** 0.35% (vs. peer median 2.8%)
- **Market cap:** $8.5B

Peer utilities trade at a median P/E of 21.5x. Ormat's premium of 3x the sector median requires either (a) growth dramatically exceeding peers, or (b) a structural re-rating that places the company outside the utility peer set entirely.

The growth rate supports some premium: 12-17% revenue growth guided for 2026 vs. utility peers growing 3-8%. But current EPS growth is negligible — the company is in a capital-intensive investment phase where incremental EBITDA is consumed by depreciation and interest. The market is pricing 2028+ earnings power today.

Analyst consensus target of $135.45 (median $135) is below the current price, with a range of $122-$152. The stock has already traded through the mean target. Post-Q1 upgrades (UBS $152, Oppenheimer $144, Piper $142, Baird $143) reflect the beat, but the stock at $138-140 is at or above the bulk of these targets.

At a more generous 45x multiple on 2027 consensus of $2.49, fair value would be ~$112. To justify the current price on 2027 earnings requires a 56x multiple — achievable only if the company meaningfully beats elevated expectations. On 2028 earnings (assuming 15% growth to ~$2.86), 45x produces ~$129. The current price of ~$138-140 effectively prices in 2028-2029 execution with no stumbles.

## Competitive Landscape

Ormat's competitive position is genuinely differentiated:
- **Vertical integration:** No other public company spans exploration → manufacturing → construction → operation in geothermal. This creates a sustainable cost advantage and proprietary knowledge base accumulated over 60+ years.
- **182 patents** on proprietary ORC (Organic Rankine Cycle) technology.
- **84% capacity factor** vs. 20-30% for solar/wind makes geothermal uniquely suited for baseload data center demand.
- **Long-term PPA portfolio** with weighted average remaining life of ~14 years provides revenue visibility.

However, competitive threats are emerging:
- EGS technology (if proven at scale) could allow oil & gas companies with horizontal drilling expertise to enter the geothermal market without Ormat's legacy geological advantages.
- Binary power plant competitors (Turboden/Mitsubishi, Kaishan, Baker Hughes) challenge the Product segment.
- Energy storage is intensely competitive with lower barriers to entry — Ormat's differentiation here is operational scale rather than technology moat.

The competitive position is strengthening in geothermal (data center demand validates the technology's value proposition) but the storage segment faces margin pressure from competition and tariff uncertainty.

## Risk Assessment

**Resource depletion:** McGinness Hills (141MW, largest U.S. plant) experiencing 5°F/year temperature decline. Don A. Campbell declining 3-4°F/year. Platanares (Honduras) declining 4-5°F/year. Brawley written off entirely ($7.2M impairment). This is an ongoing geological reality requiring continuous well drilling expenditure that depresses free cash flow indefinitely.

**Curtailments intensifying:** U.S. grid curtailments reduced generation by 277,923 MWh in 2025 vs. 121,299 MWh in 2024 — a 129% increase. This erodes revenue from productive assets and is driven by grid congestion outside the company's control.

**Customer concentration and collection:** SCPPA (17.8%), NV Energy (13.8%), KPLC-Kenya (11.9% with $29.5M overdue). Honduras' ENEE has $20.3M overdue due to sovereign fiscal stress.

**China tariff/FEOC risk:** Battery imports for energy storage face escalating tariff and regulatory headwinds. Company accelerated imports to "safe harbor" before the Dec 2025 deadline, but new projects face higher costs or supply constraints.

**Dilution risk:** $1.17B in convertible notes outstanding post-Q1 2026 creates meaningful dilution risk if shares trade above conversion prices at maturity (2027 and 2031 tranches).

**Legislative risk:** Tax benefit income ($66.7M, over half of pretax income) depends on IRA/OBBBA framework continuity through 2033. Any modification to energy tax credits would materially impact earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- Data center demand for baseload geothermal is structural and accelerating. Google PPA is the first of many hyperscaler contracts.
- New PPAs at $100+/MWh represent 30-60% pricing uplift vs. legacy contracts, creating a decade-long earnings ramp as old PPAs expire.
- EGS technology, if proven, could expand addressable market by 10-100x.
- Energy Storage segment at 2.7GW pipeline and 59% gross margins demonstrates successful diversification.
- 2028 targets of 1.65-1.75 GW electricity and 950-1,050 MW storage, if achieved, would support $3.50+ EPS.
- At $3.50 EPS and a 45x growth-utility multiple, fair value would be ~$158 (15% upside).

### Bear Case
- Core Electricity revenue declined in 2025 and margin compressed 800bp over two years.
- Resource depletion at key plants (McGinness Hills, Don Campbell, Platanares) is accelerating, requiring ever-increasing well drilling capex to maintain production.
- Near-term estimates are falling sharply (-35% for current quarter). The company must spend $675M+ annually in capex for years before the growth shows up in EPS.
- At 67x P/E, any execution miss (Google PPA delayed, EGS fails to scale, curtailments worsen, storage tariffs bite) triggers a re-rating toward 35-40x.
- At 35x on $2.49 2027 EPS, downside target is ~$87 (-37% from current levels).
- Insider selling (CFO to 1,970 shares, EVP to zero) at $130-135 suggests those with the most visibility see limited near-term upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Stock pulls back to $110-115 (providing 35-40x forward P/E cushion on 2027 estimates)
2. Electricity segment revenue returns to positive growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming new PPA pricing is offsetting curtailments and resource issues
3. Insider purchases by C-suite executives (reversal of current selling pattern)

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Google PPA receives NV PUC denial or material modification reducing scope below 100MW
2. EPS estimates for 2027 fall below $2.00 (indicating the growth investment thesis is not converting to earnings)
3. Net debt/EBITDA exceeds 5.5x (approaching covenant limits in a rising rate environment)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (likely August 2026 for Q2 results) will reveal whether the guided $0.25 quarterly EPS represents a trough or a structural quarterly run-rate problem. NV PUC Google PPA approval expected H2 2026 is the key catalyst to monitor.

## Conclusion

Ormat Technologies owns a genuinely differentiated asset in the renewable energy landscape — 24/7 baseload geothermal power uniquely suited for the data center demand surge. The Google PPA validates the strategic thesis. The Energy Storage business is scaling impressively. Long-term optionality from EGS is meaningful if unquantifiable.

The problem is the price. At 67x trailing and 62x forward earnings, with EPS essentially flat for three years, the stock prices in flawless execution of ambitious 2028 targets. Core Electricity revenue declined last year. Margins compressed 800bp. Resource depletion at key plants accelerates spending requirements. The CFO and EVP of storage/BD sold to near-zero at $130-135.

This is a HOLD. The thesis is sound; the entry point is poor. The asymmetry at this valuation favors patience: a pullback to $110-115 (on a quarterly miss, PPA delay, or broader market weakness) would create a materially more attractive risk/reward for a business with genuine long-term structural tailwinds.
