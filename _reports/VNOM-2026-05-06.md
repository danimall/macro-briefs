## Executive Summary

Viper Energy is a pure-play mineral and royalty interest company owning approximately 86,639 net royalty acres (as of March 31, 2026) in the Permian Basin, collecting royalties on production from over 43,000 wells without bearing any drilling, completion, or operating costs. Following transformational acquisitions in 2025 (the $4B Sitio acquisition and $3.6B Diamondback Drop Down), VNOM's production has nearly tripled to ~134,000 BOE/d in Q4 2025, making it one of the largest publicly-traded pure-play mineral and royalty companies in the most productive U.S. oil basin.

The company reported Q1 2026 total operating income of $511M (+109% YoY), diluted EPS of $0.53, and raised oil production guidance approximately 2.3% at midpoint. With leverage targets essentially met following the February 2026 non-Permian divestiture ($617M), management has signaled intent to return "upwards of 100% of future cash available for distribution" via dividends and buybacks ($1.2B remaining authorization). The stock trades with an approximately 3.0% base dividend yield and potential total yield above 5% including the variable component.

The primary investment risk is VNOM's near-complete commodity price exposure with minimal hedging. At current prices, VNOM offers attractive risk/reward for investors willing to accept oil price volatility in exchange for a high-quality, zero-capex cash flow stream with organic growth visibility.

## Company Overview

Viper Energy owns mineral and royalty interests — the perpetual right to receive a percentage of production revenue from wells drilled on its acreage, with zero obligation to fund any drilling, completion, or lease operating costs. This structure creates an extraordinarily capital-light business: VNOM's total cash operating expense is $3.52/BOE (production taxes + G&A only), producing cash operating margins of approximately 91%.

The company is managed by Diamondback Energy (FANG), which owns ~42.1% voting power and operates approximately 35% of VNOM's net royalty acreage. VNOM has no employees — all personnel are Diamondback employees provided via a Services and Secondment Agreement. ExxonMobil is the second-largest operator at 14% of royalty income.

**Post-2025 transformation profile (as of March 31, 2026):**
- 86,639 net royalty acres (after February 2026 non-Permian divestiture)
- Production: 120-132 MBOE/d (2026 guidance, raised with Q1 results)
- 88 gross rigs currently operating on acreage
- 1,370 wells in active development + 1,351 line-of-sight wells
- 406,035 MBOE proved reserves (78% proved developed producing)
- Investment-grade credit (BBB-/BBB-/Ba1)

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Cash Flow**

FY2025 royalty income reached $1.346B (+58% YoY from $854M), with operating cash flow of $1.053B (+70% from $620M). The partial-year integration of Sitio and the Drop Down assets means 2025 understates run-rate economics. Q1 2026 annualizes to roughly $2B+ in royalty income on the fully integrated asset base.

The reported FY2025 net loss of $206M is entirely attributable to a $768M non-cash ceiling test impairment — an accounting artifact from recording Diamondback's Drop Down properties at historical carrying value that exceeded SEC-priced fair value when WTI averaged only $64.73. Operating cash flow was never impaired. Q1 2026 confirmed the return to profitability with consolidated net income of $215M ($97M net income attributable to Viper Energy, or $0.53 diluted EPS).

**Balance Sheet — De-Leveraged**

Post-February 2026 divestiture, total debt stands at approximately $1.62B ($1.6B in Guaranteed Senior Notes plus $20M of revolver borrowings), with the $500M Term Loan fully repaid. Net debt is approximately $1.59B, approaching management's $1.5B target. Liquidity stands at $1.4B in revolving credit availability. The current ratio of 3.72x and debt/equity of 0.52x indicate comfortable financial health.

**Capital Return — Accelerating**

The annual base dividend was raised to $1.52/share ($0.38/quarter) in February 2026, implying a base yield of approximately 3.0% at recent prices. With the variable component, Q1 2026 declared a total dividend of $0.68/share (implying approximately 5.5% annualized total yield). Management's signal of returning "upwards of 100% of future cash available for distribution" combined with $1.2B remaining buyback authorization represents a meaningful catalyst for per-share value creation.

**Operating Leverage**

Cash operating costs of $3.52/BOE means VNOM remains highly profitable even at depressed commodity prices. Oil at $60/bbl would still generate substantial positive cash flow given zero capital spending requirements. Interest expense at $2.76/BOE (declining after debt reduction) further supports cash distribution capacity.

## Growth Analysis

VNOM's growth comes from two channels: organic production growth on existing acreage and accretive acquisitions.

**Organic Growth:** With 88 gross rigs active on VNOM's acreage, 1,370 wells in active development (6-8 month production timeline), and 1,351 line-of-sight wells (15-18 months), production growth is visible without additional M&A. Average lateral lengths extending beyond 11,600 feet improve per-well economics. The Q1 2026 oil production guidance raise (~2.3% at midpoint, from 64 to 65.5 Mbo/d) confirmed the organic trajectory.

**Acquisitive Growth:** The Riverbend acquisition announced in May 2026 — comprising $337M in cash plus approximately 3.7M Class A shares (total consideration of approximately $522M at current share prices) — demonstrates continued appetite for bolt-on deals. VNOM's scale and Diamondback sponsorship provide proprietary deal flow in the fragmented Permian mineral market. Investment-grade credit expands the available debt-funded acquisition capacity.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen 47.4% over 90 days (from $1.45 to $2.14), reflecting both the full-year production contribution from acquisitions and modestly improving oil price assumptions. Next-year estimates are up 37.7% over the same period. The 30-day revision shows a pullback (-7.0% current year, -17.6% current quarter) likely driven by the recent oil price decline.

**Production Growth Visibility:** FY2026 guidance of 120-132 MBOE/d represents 26-39% growth over FY2025's average of 95,126 BOE/d. This is predominantly from full-year contribution of 2025 acquisitions rather than requiring new drilling decisions.

## Competitive Landscape

VNOM is one of the largest publicly-traded pure-play mineral and royalty companies in the Permian Basin, a position secured through the 2025 acquisitions. Texas Pacific Land (TPL), with approximately 224,000 net royalty acres principally in the Permian, is larger but operates a different model encompassing surface rights and water services. Key competitive advantages for VNOM:

1. **Zero-capex model:** Unlike non-operating working interest companies (NOG) or E&P companies, VNOM never bears drilling costs. This eliminates capital allocation risk entirely on the operational side.

2. **Operator quality:** Diamondback (35% of acreage) and ExxonMobil (14%) are among the most efficient Permian operators. Production decisions are made by operators with strong track records.

3. **Scale advantage:** In a fragmented mineral rights market, VNOM's scale provides deal flow advantages, lower per-unit G&A ($1.01/BOE), and investment-grade credit access that smaller competitors lack.

4. **Geographic concentration:** 100% Permian Basin (post-divestiture) focuses on the most productive, lowest-cost basin in the U.S., where drilling activity is most resilient through commodity cycles.

5. **Organic growth engine:** 88 active rigs on acreage provide production growth without VNOM spending capital or making decisions. The mineral interest is perpetual — production continues as long as economic wells remain to be drilled.

The Sitio acquisition eliminated the primary public pure-play competitor, and scale advantages widen with each bolt-on deal.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Exposure (HIGH):** VNOM has minimal hedging — only 40K bbl/d of puts at $51.75 in Q1 2026, declining to 20K bbl/d in Q2 at $48.13. Based on current oil production of approximately 65,000 bbl/d, a $10/bbl move in WTI translates to approximately $230-240M in annualized oil revenue impact before considering differential changes, hedges, and production growth.

**Operator Concentration:** Diamondback contributes 55% of royalty income. A material reduction in FANG's drilling activity would directly impair VNOM's organic growth. This risk is mitigated by FANG's own capital discipline and shareholder interests being aligned with maintaining activity.

**Diamondback Related-Party Risk:** The 2025 Drop Down resulted in immediate impairment, raising questions about transaction pricing fairness. While approved by independent directors, future drop-downs should be scrutinized. Additionally, Diamondback's periodic secondary sales (~$590M in March 2026) create supply pressure on the stock.

**Dilution:** Share count nearly doubled through 2025 acquisitions (total economic units now ~359M). While per-share metrics benefited on a cash flow basis, any future all-equity deals — including the equity component of the Riverbend acquisition (~3.7M new Class A shares) — would further dilute existing shareholders. The $1.2B buyback authorization partially offsets this.

**Gas Basis Differential:** VNOM received only $1.08/Mcf vs. Henry Hub $3.62 in 2025 due to Permian gas takeaway constraints. ~25% of production by volume (gas) faces this structural discount until new pipeline capacity comes online.

**Impairment Risk:** The 2025 10-K warned of "reasonably likely" additional material impairment in Q1 2026; however, VNOM's Q1 2026 release reported $215M consolidated net income without an impairment charge, indicating this risk did not materialize. Future impairments remain possible if oil prices decline materially below SEC pricing assumptions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55-65):** Oil prices remain elevated and VNOM's production of 125-135 MBOE/d at strong WTI pricing generates robust annual operating cash flow. Dividends rise as management distributes 100%+ of cash flow. Buybacks at $1.2B authorization further compress share count. Stock reaches $55-65 as market re-rates the distribution growth.

**Bear Case ($35-40):** Oil normalizes to lower mid-cycle levels. VNOM's revenue moderates and cash flow compression reduces distributable cash, with variable dividends declining. If oil falls to sustained weakness amid OPEC unwinding cuts, the stock revisits its November 2025 lows around $35-36. At these levels, however, the yield floor provides support given the ultra-low cost structure.

**Base Case ($52-57):** Oil oscillates in the mid-range through H2 2026 amid geopolitical ambiguity. Production executes at/above guidance midpoint. Dividends reach $2.00-2.30/share annually (base + variable). Stock gravitates toward analyst consensus of $57 as full-year 2026 cash flow visibility improves with Q2/Q3 earnings.

## Conclusion

Viper Energy offers a differentiated way to invest in Permian Basin production growth through a zero-capex royalty model that converts ~91% of royalty income to pre-interest cash flow. The 2025 acquisitions have been integrated, leverage targets achieved, and the company is entering an accelerated capital return phase. At ~$49.90, the stock offers a 3% base yield with meaningful upside from both organic production growth and potential commodity price tailwinds.

The primary risk — unhedged commodity exposure — is real and amplified by current commodity price uncertainty. However, constructive analyst sentiment, rising production estimates, zero-capex cash conversion, and management's commitment to 100%+ distribution of available cash create a favorable setup for income-oriented energy investors with a 9-12 month horizon.

The Q1 2026 beat, guidance raise, and achieved leverage targets stabilize the thesis. The recommendation remains BUY with the recognition that position sizing should account for the oil price binary outcome.
