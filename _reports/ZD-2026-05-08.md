## Executive Summary

Ziff Davis just sold its highest-quality segment (Connectivity/Ookla) to Accenture for $1.2B in cash, leaving behind a portfolio of digital media and SaaS businesses that generated approximately $250M in adjusted EBITDA in FY2025 but face accelerating secular headwinds from AI and search algorithm changes. The stock trades at $42.48 ($1.6B market cap), implying the market values continuing operations at approximately $750M or less after accounting for the ~$850M in net cash post-deal — roughly 2.7-3x EV/EBITDA on trailing figures.

This valuation appears optically cheap, but may reflect appropriate discounting for a business in structural decline. Q1 2026 continuing operations declined 1.9% YoY with Technology & Shopping (the largest remaining segment) down 12.9%. Three consecutive quarterly earnings misses, estimate revisions of -24 to -33% over 90 days, and no open-market insider purchases all signal that fundamentals are deteriorating faster than the market expected. The 16.2% short interest confirms substantial institutional bearish conviction.

The investment case hinges on whether management can redeploy $1.7B+ in post-deal liquidity to offset secular decline in the core digital media business. At current prices, the cash floor provides meaningful downside protection, but the absence of visibility into stabilization or a clear capital allocation plan (guidance deferred, strategic review ongoing) makes conviction difficult in either direction.

## Company Overview

Ziff Davis operates a portfolio of digital media brands and subscription SaaS businesses across four continuing segments:

- **Technology & Shopping** (24.5% of FY2025 revenue): CNET, PCMag, Mashable, Lifehacker, RetailMeNot. Primarily advertising-driven. Declining under AI/search pressure.
- **Health & Wellness** (27.7%): Everyday Health Group, BabyCenter, MedPage Today. Mix of advertising and professional services. The most defensible remaining segment (+11% FY2025, flat Q1 2026).
- **Cybersecurity & Martech** (19.2%): VIPRE Security, IPVanish VPN, MOZ SEO tools. Subscription-based but declining (-1.9% FY2025).
- **Gaming & Entertainment** (12.6%): IGN (440M+ monthly users), Humble Bundle. Community-oriented, more resilient to AI displacement (+7.2% Q1 2026).

The company has historically operated as an acquisitive roll-up, deploying $3.3B across ~100 acquisitions since 2012. Post-Connectivity divestiture, approximately 55% of revenue is advertising-dependent, 23% is subscription SaaS, and 22% is Health & Wellness (mixed model).

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation presents a genuine tension between the cash-floor thesis and the declining-business thesis:

**Sum-of-Parts:**
| Component | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Net cash (post-Connectivity) | ~$850M |
| Continuing ops implied value | ~$750M |
| Implied continuing ops EV/EBITDA | ~2.7-3.0x |

At face value, 2.7x EBITDA for the continuing business appears cheap — the peer median P/E is 37.2x and the advertising sector generally trades at higher multiples. However, the market is clearly pricing structural impairment into these businesses.

**What Makes This Not Obviously Cheap:**
1. EBITDA includes ~$229M D&A vs. $119M capex — the "excess" D&A is amortization of acquired intangibles that represent real past capital deployment, not free earnings
2. If Tech & Shopping continues declining at 12-13% annually, the EBITDA base erodes quickly
3. The Cybersecurity & Martech goodwill at zero excess value makes further impairments likely
4. The ~$250M adjusted EBITDA figure may not be sustainable; Q1 run-rate is $254M annualized but margins are compressing

**What Limits Downside:**
1. ~$850M net cash vs. $1.6B market cap provides a hard floor
2. Even in a severe decline scenario (revenues -15% and margins compressed), the cash alone supports $22-24/share
3. Buybacks at current prices ($42.48 vs. historical $56 avg) are more accretive
4. Strategic review could yield additional asset sales

**Forward P/E Context:**
At $42.48 and consensus $5.23 FY2026 EPS, the stock trades at ~8.1x forward adjusted earnings. This is cheap relative to peers (37.2x median) but the adjusted EPS figure adds back substantial real costs and the estimate trajectory is sharply negative.

**At what price does this become investable?** Below ~$35, the market would be valuing continuing operations at approximately 1.5-2x EV/EBITDA — pricing in near-complete deterioration of the advertising business. At that level, any stabilization in Health & Wellness or Gaming alone would justify the valuation, and the buyback at those prices would be highly accretive. Alternatively, confirmation of H2 2026 revenue stabilization (rather than just management projections) would justify the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural Headwinds:**
The core competitive threat is existential: Google's AI Overviews and generative AI models (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) can synthesize product reviews, health information, and tech comparisons without directing users to publisher sites. ZD's own reporting notes significant declines in Google search traffic to news sites.

**Competitive Position by Segment:**
- **Tech & Shopping:** Direct competition from AI for informational queries. CNET and PCMag brand value is real but declining as traffic source shifts. Future PLC and Fandom compete for similar audiences. The moat is narrowing measurably.
- **Health & Wellness:** Relatively defensible. Medical content requires expert credentialing, regulatory compliance, and professional trust that AI cannot easily replicate. BabyCenter and professional education have community elements.
- **Gaming & Entertainment:** IGN's 440M+ monthly users represent genuine community engagement. Gaming audiences seek entertainment, discussion, and shared experience — less susceptible to AI summarization. Competes with GameSpot (owned by Fandom), Kotaku, and YouTube/Twitch.
- **Cybersecurity & Martech:** Fragmented markets with no defensible moat. MOZ competes against SEMrush, Ahrefs; VPN competes against NordVPN, ExpressVPN. Declining customer counts confirm competitive pressure.

**Lawsuits as Optionality:**
The Google antitrust suit (filed Feb 2026) aligns with DOJ momentum and could yield damages if successful. The OpenAI copyright case (filed April 2025) is more novel but establishes a claim on AI training data usage. These are potential call options but highly uncertain and multi-year in nature.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **AI-driven revenue decline acceleration (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** If Tech & Shopping declines 15-20% annually and margin compression continues, the remaining business generates progressively less EBITDA against which the "cheap" valuation was calculated.

2. **Capital deployment risk:** Management has demonstrated poor capital allocation historically. The $1.7B+ in post-deal cash creates deployment risk — a bad acquisition at this juncture would destroy the cash-floor thesis.

3. **Goodwill impairment risk:** The Cybersecurity & Martech unit at zero excess fair value will likely require write-down if the segment doesn't stabilize.

4. **Convertible note cash demand:** $149M due within 12 months (well-covered by cash) plus additional notes through 2028. Not a solvency risk given the Connectivity proceeds, but constrains buyback/M&A firepower.

5. **Absence of guidance:** Deferred FY2026 guidance means limited forward visibility. Investors lack clarity on management's expectations for stabilization.

**Mitigating Factors:**
- Net cash position provides hard floor
- Active strategic review may surface additional value (divestitures, take-private interest)
- Lawsuits against Google/OpenAI represent asymmetric upside options
- Gaming & Health segments showing resilience

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55-60, +30-42% upside):**
- Connectivity sale closes cleanly; management deploys $400-500M in buybacks at $40-45 (retiring 20-25% of float)
- H2 2026 revenue stabilization materializes as AI search traffic impact plateaus
- Strategic review identifies additional value-creating transactions (partial divestitures of Health & Wellness at premium valuations)
- Google or OpenAI lawsuits reach favorable preliminary rulings
- At $55, stock would still trade at only 4-5x EV/EBITDA on normalized continuing operations

**Bear Case ($30-35, -19-31% downside):**
- Tech & Shopping decline accelerates to -15-20% as AI Overviews expand coverage categories
- Management deploys cash on another value-destructive acquisition (as demonstrated by $58M video game publishing loss)
- Cybersecurity & Martech goodwill impairment materializes
- Estimates continue declining as revenue contraction proves faster than cost cutting
- At $30, stock would trade at approximately 1.5x EV/EBITDA — pricing near-terminal decline but protected by cash

**Base Case ($42-48):**
- Business muddles through with low-single-digit revenue decline for continuing operations
- Buybacks offset earnings decline, keeping EPS roughly flat at $5.00-5.50 adjusted
- No major capital allocation missteps or breakthroughs
- Stock remains range-bound until either stabilization or further deterioration becomes clear in the data

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of organic revenue stabilization (flat or better) in continuing operations, specifically Tech & Shopping returning to flat/positive
2. Management provides clear FY2026-27 guidance demonstrating path to low-single-digit growth
3. Stock declining to $33-35 range where the cash floor provides more than 2:1 asymmetry

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Management announces a large acquisition (>$300M) rather than returning cash to shareholders
2. Q2 2026 continuing operations revenue decline accelerates beyond -5% organic
3. Goodwill impairment in Cybersecurity & Martech unit confirms permanent capital loss
4. Additional executive departures or board turnover without strategic clarity

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely August 2026) will provide the first clean quarter of post-Connectivity standalone results and the first test of management's "return to growth in H2 2026" claim. The Connectivity sale closing (expected Q2-Q3 2026) will crystallize the capital deployment decision.

## Conclusion

Ziff Davis occupies an uncomfortable middle ground. The ~$850M net cash position post-Connectivity creates a meaningful downside floor (~$22-24/share in a severe scenario), and the 2.7x EV/EBITDA on continuing operations is cheap by any historical standard. However, three consecutive earnings misses, accelerating AI-driven traffic declines in the core segment, historical capital allocation failures, and deferred guidance create insufficient basis for a constructive call.

The 16.2% short interest reflects real institutional conviction that the digital media model is in secular decline, and the Q1 2026 data (-12.9% in Tech & Shopping) validates this thesis directionally. The key variable is pace — if the decline rate stabilizes at mid-single digits, the valuation is compelling; if it accelerates, the EBITDA that underpins the "cheap" thesis erodes quickly.

At $42.48, the stock is not cheap enough to offer compelling risk/reward against the structural headwinds (estimated 1.3-1.5x upside/downside from the bear case), nor is there sufficient evidence of stabilization to justify conviction in recovery. The appropriate posture is to wait for either (a) price to decline to $33-35 where the cash floor creates genuine asymmetry, or (b) evidence that revenue stabilization is materializing in the data rather than management projections.
