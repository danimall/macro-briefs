**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Baxter International is a $10.5B-market-cap medical-technology company in the final, most difficult stage of a multi-year breakup. After divesting BioPharma Solutions (2023, $3.96B) and Kidney Care/Vantive (January 2025, $3.8B to Carlyle), the "RemainCo" is a smaller, more levered, lower-growth business than the franchise investors bought a decade ago. FY2025 produced a GAAP operating loss of –$308M and a net loss of –$957M (–$1.87/share); Q1 2026 swung to a continuing-operations loss; core gross margin compressed roughly 4.2 points in FY25 and another ~5.0 points in Q1 2026; and organic sales turned negative (–1% group, –4% U.S.) at the start of 2026. The dividend was cut ~94% to a token $0.01/quarter, removing the historical income-investor support without yet delivering the deleveraging payoff.

The investment debate reduces to a single question: can new CEO Andrew Hider execute an operational-excellence turnaround fast enough to outrun a balance sheet that both S&P and Moody's now view with a Negative outlook at the lowest investment-grade rung (BBB-/Baa3)? The equity is a levered call on margin recovery, which was moving the wrong way in Q1 2026. The flagship Novum IQ Large Volume Pump remains under a Class I recall and U.S./Canada ship hold with "uncertain" timing, and the company carries a securities class action alleging it misled investors about that product. The covenant on its revolver was loosened in November 2025 to accommodate Q4'25–Q3'26.

We rate BAX **AVOID**. This is a deleveraging turnaround with deteriorating fundamentals, distress-zone leverage metrics, recurring goodwill impairments, an incomplete leadership team (new outside CEO plus an interim CFO during covenant stress), and a binary product-recall overhang. None of the elements of the bull case — clean Novum resolution, tariff relief, margin inflection, successful deleveraging — were visible in the most recent results. The stock has fallen ~35% over the past twelve months, so much of the bad news is priced; that is why this is an AVOID rather than an active short.

## Company Overview

Following its breakup, Baxter operates three reporting segments:

- **Medical Products & Therapies (~47% of sales; $5.3B FY25):** sterile IV solutions, infusion pumps/systems, administration sets, parenteral nutrition, and Advanced Surgery (hemostats/sealants). Baxter is a dominant U.S. supplier of IV fluids.
- **Healthcare Systems & Technologies (~27%; $3.07B):** the legacy Hillrom franchise — smart beds, patient monitoring, the Welch Allyn diagnostics brand (Front Line Care), and care-communication/OR integration.
- **Pharmaceuticals (~22%; $2.49B):** specialty injectables, inhaled anesthesia, and an international drug-compounding business.

The competitive position is entrenched but defensive. Baxter benefits from a large installed base, long-dated hospital/GPO/IDN contracts ($8.87B in remaining performance obligations), and mission-critical product status (IV fluids, infusion). The same long-term contracts that lock in volume also prevent timely pass-through of tariffs and input-cost inflation, which is precisely where margins are now breaking. The reported FY25 revenue figure is partly an artifact: the +6% headline includes roughly 3 points from the post-divestiture Manufacturing & Supply Agreement under which Baxter sells products back to Vantive (the divested Kidney Care unit, now a Carlyle portfolio company), plus FX. Strip those out and the underlying business is flat-to-declining.

## Financial Analysis

The financials describe a company not currently earning its cost of capital.

**Revenue.** Continuing-operations sales were $11.24B in FY25 (+6% reported, +3% operational) versus $10.64B in FY24. The optically large 2023→2025 decline from $14.8B reflects Kidney Care/BPS moving to discontinued operations, not organic shrinkage. The concern is the inflection: Q1 2026 organic sales fell ~1%, with U.S. down ~4%, driven in part by structurally lower IV-solutions demand after post-Hurricane-Helene fluid-conservation practices took hold.

**Profitability.** FY25 results reflected ~$2.09B of special items. Q1 2026 produced a continuing-operations loss of –$17M versus a ~+$64M profit a year earlier. Trailing EPS of –$1.75 makes trailing and forward P/E meaningless (both shown as ~–10.7x). The relevant earnings power is the consensus adjusted figure: ~$1.92 FY26 / ~$2.02 FY27.

**Margins.** This is the core problem. The gross-margin compression noted above was driven by tariffs, manufacturing/supply cost inflation, and adverse mix. Segment operating margins compressed across all three units in Q1 2026: MP&T 19.3%→14.5%, HST 13.2%→9.4%, Pharma 10.8%→7.4%. Margin compression of this breadth and magnitude is the opposite of what a turnaround thesis requires at its outset.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow from continuing operations was $951M in FY25; against $513M of capex, free cash flow was roughly $440M (~4% FCF yield on the current cap). Q1 2026 OCF improved to $213M from –$99M, but partly on working-capital timing (AR collections, extended vendor terms), which is not a sustainable margin signal.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** This is where the equity risk concentrates. Total debt is ~$9.46B against $2.0B cash and $6.0B equity (D/E ~2.3x). The Altman Z-Score of 1.43 sits in the distress zone, ROA is –4.8% and ROE –15.6%. Goodwill ($4.9B) plus intangibles ($4.2B) total ~$9.1B, roughly 150% of equity — a large impairment-exposed asset base on a company that has impaired Front Line Care goodwill two years running ($425M FY24, $485M FY25) and Welch Allyn's trade name $290M in FY25. Both agencies revised their outlook to Negative in Q1 2026 after briefly moving to Stable in Q4 2025. A downgrade to high-yield would raise financing costs across ~$9.5B of debt.

**Capital returns.** The $0.01/quarter dividend (down from $0.17) is the correct priority given the balance sheet, but it eliminates the income support that historically underpinned the shares. The $1.3B buyback authorization is unused.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story here today. The reported revenue line is flattered by the Vantive supply agreement and FX; underlying volume turned negative in Q1 2026. The structural headwinds are concrete and quantified:

- U.S. IV-solutions demand has been structurally reduced by post-Helene fluid-conservation practices in hospitals — a behavioral shift in Baxter's largest U.S. franchise.
- Injectables face generic pricing competition and supply constraints.
- The flagship growth driver, the Novum IQ Large Volume Pump, is under a Class I recall and a U.S./Canada ship hold; there are effectively no meaningful Novum LVP sales while the hold persists, and management calls the release timing "uncertain." This was the central growth bet for Infusion Therapies; Spectrum IQ is the fallback.

Estimate revisions confirm the absence of an inflection. Current-quarter consensus was cut 16.1% over 30 days (to $0.37 from $0.44), while full-year FY26 (~$1.92) and FY27 (~$2.02) estimates are essentially flat to slightly negative. This is well short of the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection. The growth thesis is entirely a margin-recovery thesis — getting back the ~9 points of cumulative gross-margin erosion through the new "Baxter Growth and Performance system" continuous-improvement playbook — not a top-line one. That recovery is unproven and was not visible in Q1.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward GAAP P/E are negative and uninformative. The honest valuation anchors are forward adjusted EPS and free cash flow:

- On consensus adjusted EPS of ~$1.92 FY26, BAX trades at ~10.6x forward; on ~$2.02 FY27, ~10.1x.
- FCF yield is ~4% on ~$440M of FY25 FCF.

Against the Health Care Equipment peer set, the multiple is optically cheap: peer median P/E is 44.3x (PODD 35.7x, RVTY 47.1x, MASI 44.3x, GMED 18.7x, PEN 73.9x). But the comparison is not clean — those peers are GAAP-profitable growth names, while BAX is a low-growth, GAAP-loss-making, distress-zone-leverage turnaround. The appropriate read is that BAX deserves a discount, and ~10x forward adjusted earnings is the market correctly pricing (a) negative organic growth, (b) ~9 points of unrecovered margin, (c) Negative credit outlook with a loosened covenant, and (d) the Novum recall and litigation overhang.

The valuation is best characterized as a value trap pending a recovery catalyst. A low multiple on depressed, declining earnings is not asymmetric upside unless the margin recovery materializes, and we have no evidence it has begun. Analyst targets bracket the uncertainty: low $17, median $19.5, mean $21.5, high $40. The stock at $20.39 sits just above the median and well within the range, with Citigroup having downgraded to Sell at a $17 target in late May 2026. The roughly 2.4x spread between the $17 and $40 targets reflects how binary the outcome is. We assign a modestly below-neutral valuation score: the multiple is low, but for defensible reasons, and there is no identified near-term catalyst to close the gap to fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

Baxter competes in mature, regulated, capital-intensive med-tech niches against larger and better-capitalized peers: ICU Medical and BD in infusion; Stryker-adjacent players and Linet in hospital beds; Fresenius and B. Braun in IV/pharma; and multiple generics manufacturers in injectables. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating rather than stable:

- The Novum LVP ship hold cedes infusion-pump share to competitors during the very window when Baxter intended to take it.
- Hillrom, the ~$10B+ acquisition that was the strategic rationale for entering connected care, continues to underperform — evidenced by back-to-back Front Line Care goodwill impairments and the Welch Allyn trade-name write-down. The company is also defending antitrust suits from Linet and Reading Hospital in the hospital-bed segment.
- Long-term GPO/IDN contracts protect volume but neutralize pricing power precisely when tariffs and input costs are rising, structurally compressing margins relative to peers with more pricing flexibility.

The moat in IV fluids and infusion is real and durable, which prevents this from being a structurally collapsing business. But the direction of travel — share loss in infusion, recurring impairments in connected care, and margin compression across all three segments — supports a below-neutral competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk stack is unusually deep and, in several cases, not fully priced:

1. **Novum IQ LVP Class I recalls and ship hold** — the single largest company-specific overhang, with ~$105M of charges already booked, no meaningful sales while holds persist, and "uncertain" release timing.
2. **Securities and derivative litigation** — *Electrical Workers Pension Fund Local 103 v. Baxter* (N.D. Ill., Oct 2025; class period Feb 2022–Jul/Oct 2025) and consolidated derivative suits allege misleading statements about Novum, naming current/former officers including Almeida.
3. **Credit/leverage** — a downgrade to junk would raise financing costs on ~$9.5B of debt.
4. **Tariffs and sole-source supply** — management expects results to "continue to be adversely affected"; long-term contracts prevent timely pass-through, and no tariff-refund asset has been booked despite claims filed.
5. **Recurring impairments** — Front Line Care goodwill ($1.51B carrying) has only ~$160M cushion to a further 5% fair-value decline; additional write-downs are plausible.
6. **Tax** — IRS transfer-pricing exam (Costa Rica/Puerto Rico) with a NOPA received Q1 2026; ~$280M reserved ex-penalties/interest, plus a U.S. valuation allowance against deferred tax assets given cumulative U.S. losses.
7. **Governance/leadership** — a new outside CEO (Hider) plus an interim CFO (Zielinski) during covenant stress is a high-risk configuration. A December 2025 DOJ Civil Investigative Demand on IV flexible containers (False Claims Act) and an ongoing Hillrom DOJ CID add legal tail risk.

Offsetting positives are modest: North Cove (Marion, NC) is fully operational again, and the India (Claris) FDA Warning Letter is on a path to closure after an October 2025 VAI reclassification.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 162.1% against 48.6% one-year historical volatility (a ~113.5-point spread), the term structure is in steep backwardation (near-term 162.1% vs. long-term 37.6%), and ATM IV rose 26.8 points within a single 38-minute window on June 12. Put/call skew of 12.0% indicates elevated put demand, consistent with the 13.0% short interest (5.3 days to cover) and institutional bearish positioning. The backwardation and rich front-end IV typically reflect hedging into a known near-term event or earnings/regulatory catalyst rather than a directional crash forecast; the implied move on the nearest expiry is a relatively contained ±4.3%. The signal reinforces caution but does not, by itself, identify a dated downside catalyst large enough to justify an active short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The bull case is entirely a turnaround call. Hider, the former ATS Corporation CEO known for continuous-improvement operating discipline, executes the "Baxter Growth and Performance system" and recovers a meaningful portion of the ~9 points of lost gross margin over 18–24 months. The Novum LVP recall is cleanly resolved by the FDA, the ship hold lifts, and the pump relaunches as a tangible catalyst and a source of infusion share gains. Tariff refunds are realized and pass-through improves as contracts roll. Deleveraging proceeds, the Negative credit outlook reverts to Stable, and the equity re-rates from ~10x to a mid-teens multiple on a recovered earnings base [unverified — recovered EPS scenario not in sources], supporting a higher share price. The $40 high analyst target embeds this full recovery.

### Bear Case
Margin compression continues into 2026 as tariffs and input costs outpace cost-out efforts; organic sales stay negative as IV demand remains structurally lower. A further goodwill impairment in Front Line Care (only ~$160M of cushion) hits reported results. A credit downgrade to high-yield raises financing costs on ~$9.5B of debt and forces a further covenant amendment. The securities litigation and DOJ matters create incremental charges and management distraction during an already-incomplete C-suite transition. Earnings settle below consensus and the multiple stays at ~9x or lower, taking the stock toward the $16–17 area (Citi's $17 target and the recent technical bottom).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** Headwinds are expected to persist through at least the next 2–4 quarters, given that margin recovery is unproven, Novum timing is "uncertain," and the credit outlook is Negative.

**Re-entry criteria (any two would change our view):**
- **Margin inflection confirmed** — segment operating margins stabilize and gross margin recovers at least 200–300 bps for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the operating-system playbook is working.
- **Novum LVP resolution** — FDA clears the Class I recall and the U.S./Canada ship hold lifts with a defined relaunch timeline.
- **Credit stabilization** — both agencies revert the outlook to Stable, with leverage trending down and no further covenant amendments required.
- **Permanent CFO appointed** with a credible deleveraging and margin-recovery plan, and any open-market insider buying by the new management team (currently absent despite a near-multi-year-low stock).

**Timeline:** Reassess at each quarterly print. The next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (margin trajectory and any Novum update) and the next ratings-agency review. We would also reassess on any resolution of the securities litigation.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There is no single, dated, high-conviction downside catalyst. The most plausible negative catalysts — a credit downgrade to high-yield, a further Front Line Care impairment, or another quarter of margin compression — are real but not calendar-bound. The next earnings print (Q2 2026) is the closest scheduled event, but Q1 misses are already partly reflected in the ~35% trailing-twelve-month decline and the dividend cut.

**Timing window.** Diffuse. The leverage and margin concerns play out over multiple quarters, which means carrying cost (borrow plus a ~13% short-interest crowd) erodes the trade without a near-term trigger.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 13.0% of float (42.4M shares) with 5.3 days to cover — elevated, meaning the bearish thesis is already substantially expressed. This crowds the trade and raises squeeze risk on any positive surprise (clean Novum resolution, credit-outlook upgrade, or a takeover/strategic-review headline given the depressed equity and breakup history).

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market confirms bearish positioning (12.0% put skew, near-term IV 162.1% vs. 48.6% HV, steep backwardation), but rich front-end IV makes put-based expressions expensive and signals the consensus is already short-leaning. Buying protection here means paying up for a view the market already holds.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A bear-case target of ~$16–17 implies ~16–22% downside from $20.39. The upside risk on a turnaround surprise, recall clearance, or strategic action is plausibly 30–50%. That is a sub-1:1 reward:risk, below the 2:1 bar required for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is a deteriorating, over-levered turnaround, not an actively collapsing business with a dated trigger. The stock has already fallen ~35%, sits near the low end of analyst targets, and carries elevated short interest. The remaining downside is limited relative to squeeze and takeout risk. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Conclusion

Baxter is a deleveraging turnaround whose fundamentals were moving in the wrong direction at the start of 2026: organic sales turned negative, gross margin compressed, all three segments saw operating-margin erosion, and the GAAP loss persisted. The balance sheet is the binding constraint — distress-zone Altman Z of 1.43, D/E ~2.3x, BBB-/Baa3 ratings with a Negative outlook, and a loosened covenant. The flagship Novum pump is under a Class I recall with uncertain timing and an attendant securities class action, the connected-care franchise keeps impairing goodwill, and the company is being run by a new outside CEO alongside an interim CFO. The dividend has been cut to a penny.

The equity is a levered call on a margin recovery that is plausible under Hider's operating-excellence playbook but entirely unproven and absent from the latest results. At ~10x forward adjusted earnings the stock is not expensive, but it is cheap for defensible reasons, and there is no identified near-term catalyst to close the gap. We rate BAX **AVOID** and would revisit only on confirmed margin inflection, a clean Novum resolution, and credit stabilization. We do not recommend shorting: the ~35% decline, elevated short interest, and proximity to the low end of the target range cap the remaining downside relative to squeeze and strategic-action risk.
