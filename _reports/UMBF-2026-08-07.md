## Executive Summary

UMB Financial has delivered the payoff from its January 2025 acquisition of Heartland Financial, and it arrived roughly a year later than the headline net income figures suggested. FY2025 net income rose 59.2% to $702.4M while diluted EPS rose only 3.3% to $9.29, because the deal was funded entirely with equity (~26.8M shares issued against a ~48.7M pre-deal base, diluting legacy holders from 100% to about 64.5% ownership) and year one absorbed ~$142M of acquisition expense plus $93.5M of intangible amortization. Q2 2026 is where the economics show: revenue of $778.0M (+17.9% year over year) against noninterest expense of $399.6M (+1.6%) produced roughly 16 percentage points of positive operating leverage, a 51.4% efficiency ratio versus ~62% in 2024, diluted EPS of $3.56 (+26.2%), ROA of 1.55% and a derived ROTCE near 20%. CET1 improved from 10.96% at year-end to 11.45% while loans grew 6.1% in six months, which is high-quality internal capital generation.

The constraint on extrapolating that result is mechanical. Loans grew 11.8% year over year to $41.15B while deposits fell 0.4% to $59.77B, and the funding came from redeploying interest-bearing balances due from banks, which declined from roughly $9.9B at year-end 2025 to ~$4.8B at June 30, 2026. Moving a dollar from Fed cash into a loan yielding 6.70% (FY2025 average) is highly accretive to net interest margin, and it drove NIM from 2.51% to 3.10%. It is also self-limiting: the buffer has roughly halved in six months, noninterest-bearing deposits fell from 30.8% to 27.1% of the total, and the 10-K states plainly that UMB's commercial and institutional deposits are "generally more price-sensitive than consumer funding sources." The next marginal loan dollar has to be funded at market deposit rates, which compresses incremental spread. Externally reported (and unconfirmed) commentary from the July 29 headline indicates management guided Q3 expense to roughly $390M with core margin "relatively flat" — consistent with a NIM plateau.

At $145.13 the stock trades at 12.1x trailing-twelve-month EPS of $12.01 [unverified — actual: TTM EPS not derivable from sources; Phase 1 reports EPS $9.29], 11.0x the $13.18 current-year consensus, 10.4x the $13.91 next-year consensus, 1.42x book value of $102.02 and roughly 2.0x derived tangible book of ~$72. Against a peer median P/E of 12.7x, that is a modest discount for a bank earning ~20% ROTCE with 31.6% of revenue from fees. Consensus, however, models next-year EPS growth of only 5.5%, which correctly recognizes that a meaningful slice of 2026's jump is non-durable — purchase-accounting accretion equal to ~12.6% of H1 pretax income, declining intangible amortization, and $27.1M of Q2 securities gains. Normalizing for securities gains puts run-rate earnings power closer to $13.00–13.25. We move from BUY to ACCUMULATE: the business is compounding tangible book at a low-teens rate with a ~15% payout, but at 3.2% below the 52-week high, with the funding buffer half-spent and the analyst low target ($139) already below spot, the entry offers a positive rather than compelling expected return.

## Company Overview

UMB Financial is a Kansas City, Missouri holding company operating UMB Bank, N.A. plus nonbank subsidiaries, with 5,222 full-time-equivalent associates at year-end 2025 and ~$72.26B of assets at June 30, 2026. It reports three segments: Commercial Banking, Institutional Banking and Personal Banking. The banking footprint is Midwestern, Southwestern and Western, with the 10-K noting no significant presence elsewhere.

What separates UMB from a typical mid-cap regional is the Institutional franchise. UMB Fund Services provides fund accounting, transfer agency and related services to mutual fund and alternative-investment complexes from Milwaukee, Chadds Ford and Ogden. Institutional Banking also houses corporate trust, asset servicing and healthcare services including HSA custody. Fee income was 29.8% of FY2025 revenue and 31.6% of Q2 2026 revenue, well above the regional-bank norm, and these businesses are capital-light and deposit-generating, which is the structural reason ROTCE can sit near 20% at an 11.45% CET1 ratio.

The Heartland acquisition closed January 31, 2025, taking total assets from roughly $47B to ~$73B. Systems conversion completed in Q4 2025, which is the event that unlocked the 2026 expense performance. Two board and management continuity items: J. Mariner Kemper has been Chairman and CEO since 2004, with James Rine taking the President title in January 2024 as a visible succession step, and CFO Ram Shankar has held the role since 2016.

Two derived observations matter more than the headlines. First, stripping securities gains from both periods gives core fee income of $218.4M versus $184.5M, or +18.4% — better than the reported 10.5%, and a comparatively clean organic read since both quarters are full post-close periods. Second, the efficiency ratio of 51.4% ($399.6M / $778.0M) represents a permanent step-down in the cost base following the systems conversion, which is the most durable element of the 2026 improvement.

**Earnings quality requires three haircuts.** Purchase-accounting accretion ("net accretion of premiums and discounts from acquisition") was $86.1M in H1 2026 against H1 pretax income of ~$681.7M, or ~12.6%. The year-over-year comparison against $60.2M in H1 2025 overstates the increase, because the prior period contained only five months of Heartland; pro-rating gives ~$72.2M and an underlying increase near 19%. This is a cash-flow-statement aggregate spanning acquired loans, securities, deposits and borrowings, so it should be treated as an order-of-magnitude indicator of purchase-accounting contribution rather than a NIM decomposition. It amortizes toward zero, creating a known 2027–2028 headwind. Partially offsetting, intangible amortization is already falling ($23.5M in Q2 2026 vs $25.3M in Q1; $46.9M in H1 vs $93.5M for full-year 2025), a GAAP-only tailwind. Securities gains of $27.1M in Q2 are ~$0.27 per share after tax at 24% and are volatile.

**Capital and marks.** Total equity $8,030.8M, preferred $294.1M, common equity $7,736.7M, goodwill ~$1,837.6M and other intangibles ~$439.9M give derived tangible common equity of $5,459.2M, TBV per share of ~$72.0 on ~75.83M shares, and TCE/tangible assets of 7.80%. The AFS unrealized pretax loss widened from $290.8M at year-end to $415.2M (3.0% of amortized cost) and already sits in AOCI and equity. The HTM unrealized pretax loss of $477.0M equals 8.3% of amortized cost and does not touch equity or regulatory capital; deducting it after tax lowers adjusted TCE/TA to 7.28%. The 8.3% markdown implies a long-duration HTM book whose economic value is sensitive to the long end. Crystallization requires forced sale, and with loan/deposit at 68.9%, rising CET1 and contingent FHLB and Fed capacity, that scenario is remote.

**Credit.** FY2025 net charge-offs rose to 0.23% of average loans from 0.10%, driven by commercial charge-offs of $44.6M versus $5.4M. Nonaccruals traced $97.0M (6/30/25) → $144.7M (12/31/25) → $127.5M (6/30/26), the last equal to 0.31% of loans. H1 2026 gross charge-offs of ~$39.4M imply an annualized net rate near 0.16%, better than 2025. The pattern is most consistent with acquired-portfolio seasoning and a small number of identified commercial credits resolving. The qualifier: the nonaccrual decline may partly reflect problem loans being charged off rather than curing, and the allowance ratio thinned from 1.08% to 1.06% of loans while the book grew 11.8%. Allowance-to-nonaccrual coverage of 3.43x is comfortable.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised 16.3% to $0.50 per quarter effective with the October 1, 2026 payment, an annualized $2.00 for a 1.37% yield and a ~14.5% payout against consensus. The buyback authorization was doubled to 2 million shares (~2.6% of shares out) in April 2026; actual repurchase volume is not established. Retention rather than distribution is the policy, which is appropriate at a 20% ROTCE if the incremental capital can be deployed — and deposit funding, not capital, is the binding constraint.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question splits cleanly into what continues and what does not.

**Continues.** Core fee income compounding at ~18.4% organically is the most durable element. Fund administration, corporate trust and healthcare/HSA custody scale with client assets and account counts rather than balance sheet, and Institutional Banking H1 2026 net income of $135.0M was up 32% year over year. Personal Banking swung to $53.7M of H1 net income from a $5.0M loss, which is the acquired retail cost base moving from integration drag to contribution — a one-time step that is now in the run rate. The 51.4% efficiency ratio is structural. Loan/deposit at 68.9% leaves absolute balance-sheet capacity relative to peers running 80–90%.

**Does not continue at the same rate.** The cash-to-loan mix shift that drove NIM from 2.51% to 3.10% has consumed roughly half its fuel. The chain: excess Fed cash yielding roughly the policy rate moves into loans yielding 6.70%, capturing ~250–300bp of incremental spread per dollar with no funding cost added; once the buffer normalizes, each incremental loan dollar must be funded by a price-sensitive institutional deposit at close to market rates, and the incremental spread narrows to a normal loan-versus-deposit margin. American Banker reported in July that peers including Atlantic Union cut 2026 NIM guidance precisely because loan growth raised funding costs. UMB is one to two years earlier on the same road, and the unconfirmed "core margin relatively flat" commentary suggests the plateau is already here.

The rate-sensitivity profile has flipped in a way that matters for the macro backdrop. The Q2 10-Q year-one NII simulation shows −1.2% in a +200bp ramp and +5.1% in a −300bp ramp, versus roughly +0.4% and +1.8% a year earlier. UMB now benefits from Fed cuts and is modestly hurt by rates holding high. The near-term rate path is genuinely two-sided. The static ramp sensitivity is small; the more relevant channel is deposit-pricing competition, which is not captured in a static-balance-sheet model.

Consensus reflects this correctly. Current-year EPS of $13.18 rose 2.4% over 90 days and next-year $13.91 rose 1.6%, both positive but far from the revision velocity that signals a mispriced inflection. The implied 2027 growth rate is 5.5%. The company has beaten in three of the last four quarters, with the last two beats at 11.6% and 17.3%, which supports confidence in the near-term number, while the modest out-year growth reflects accretion runoff and NIM normalization.

Peer comparables (WTFC 13.1x, SSB 10.5x, CFR 14.2x, ZION 10.3x, ONB 12.7x) cluster tightly around 12.7x with dispersion driven largely by returns and fee mix. On the forward consensus multiple of 11.0x, UMBF sits slightly below the median while earning materially higher returns on tangible equity and carrying a fee mix roughly double the group's typical level. The valuation signature — ~2.0x TBV, ~11x forward earnings, ~20% ROTCE — is internally consistent: the market pays a premium to tangible book because returns on that book are exceptional, while the earnings multiple stays ordinary because a portion of current earnings is understood to be transitional.

The dividend yield at 1.37% is roughly half the peer median, so income support is minimal. That is a policy choice at a ~15% payout, and it means the return has to come from earnings growth and multiple, not carry.

**Base case $163.** 11.7x next-year consensus of $13.91, equivalently 12.4x normalized 2026 earnings power of ~$13.15. This holds UMBF at a small premium to the current peer median multiple, justified by the ROTCE and fee-mix differential but not extended, and assumes NIM plateaus rather than compresses, fee income compounds low-to-mid-teens, and credit costs stay near 0.20% of loans. That is +12.3% from $145.13, or roughly +13.7% including the dividend. It sits just below the analyst mean of $165.69 and median of $167.

**Bull case $183.** 13.2x $13.91. Requires Fed cuts (the −300bp simulation shows +5.1% NII), deposit balances resuming growth so loan growth continues without a funding-cost penalty, fee income holding high-teens organic growth, and meaningful execution of the 2M-share authorization. Approximates the $181 analyst high.

**Bear case $115.** 9.5x a normalized $12.10. Deposit competition raises funding costs as the cash buffer exhausts, NIM compresses 15–25bp, loan growth slows toward mid-single digits, accretion runoff bites 2027, and CRE or NDFI credit normalizes against a 1.06% allowance. That is −20.8% from spot and would still leave the stock above the $103.38 52-week low.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives roughly +7.4% price return and +8.8% total return over twelve months. Positive, and consistent with staged accumulation rather than a full position at spot. The analyst low target of $139 sitting below the current price is a useful marker that the distribution is not one-sided at this level.

We treat cheapness cautiously here rather than as a standalone reason to own. A 10.4x next-year multiple on a bank whose consensus growth is 5.5% and whose current margin is flattered by a finite mix shift is fair-to-slightly-attractive, not an identified mispricing with a dated catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

UMB's competitive position is improving on trajectory. The 51.4% efficiency ratio places it among the better mid-cap regionals after running at ~62% two years ago, loan growth of 11.8% has outpaced the group without credit deterioration (the explicit basis for Stephens' July 15 Overweight initiation, issued the same day it initiated cross-town Commerce Bancshares at Equal-Weight), and the Institutional fee franchise compounding at high teens is genuinely differentiated. Fee income at 31.6% of revenue reduces dependence on spread and generates operating deposits.

Three qualifications. First, the fee franchise and the funding vulnerability are the same asset seen from opposite sides: institutional and commercial clients supply the fee revenue and also the price-sensitive deposits, and the 10-K concedes UMB "may experience a sharper decline in low-cost funding sources or an increase in cost of deposits due to its customer profile." Second, the 10-K describes asset servicing as competing "primarily on price, quality of services, and reputation," a candid acknowledgment that fund administration is a scale-and-price business where margin is defended rather than expanded. Third, the filing names the GENIUS Act of 2025 explicitly as potentially creating "new and increased competition for funds that may have otherwise been, or currently are, deposits with banks" — a stablecoin-driven deposit-substitution risk that lands hardest on exactly the institutional/operating deposit base UMB relies on. Regulation II debit interchange revision and proposed credit-card rate caps bear on bankcard and healthcare-payments fee lines.

On the relief side, an OCC notice of proposed rulemaking dated December 23, 2025 would raise the heightened-standards threshold from $50B to $700B, which would remove UMB Bank from that regime if adopted. UMB remains below the $100B EGRRCPA stress-test threshold, though double-digit loan growth makes that a medium-term consideration.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Deposit funding is the binding constraint.** Deposits −0.4% year over year, noninterest-bearing mix 30.8% → 27.1%, Fed cash ~$9.9B → ~$4.8B, loan/deposit 63.9% → 68.9%. Institutional deposit balances are lumpy and a −0.4% move on a $60B base is within normal quarter-end variation, so this is not yet proof of franchise attrition; the 27.1% NIB mix is the firmer evidence. The consequence is that incremental spread on new lending compresses, and the 2026 margin optics do not annualize into 2027 without deposit growth.

**2. Liability sensitivity into an uncertain rate path.** −1.2% year-one NII on a +200bp ramp, +5.1% on −300bp. Both directions are live. Magnitude is modest; the deposit-repricing channel is the larger practical exposure.

**3. CRE concentration with a thinning allowance.** CRE at 40.3% of loans (investment CRE 25.7%, office only 2.9%), geographically Sun Belt/Mountain-weighted (TX 12%, MO 12%, AZ 11.9%, CO 11.5%). The low office share is a real mitigant and the mix is drifting favorably. The allowance ratio declining from 1.08% to 1.06% into a book growing 11.8% looks prudent in an expansion and would look otherwise in a turn.

**4. NDFI and low-transparency buckets.** Non-depository financial institution loans of $2.84B (6.9% of loans) grew from $2.43B, faster than the total book, and "leases and other" roughly doubled in six months to ~$500M with the "other" category including loans for purchasing or carrying securities. At 6.9% UMB is not an outlier among regionals, but this is the least granular portfolio disclosure. The 10-K itself added language about negative market perception toward NDFI lending "regardless of the actual risk for any particular financial institution."

**5. HTM marks and securities drag.** $477.0M HTM unrealized loss (8.3% of amortized cost) outside equity; adjusted TCE/TA of 7.28% is thin but not alarming. The $20.1B securities book (27.5% of assets) yielded 3.68% in FY2025 against 6.70% on loans, including long-duration fixed-rate municipals.

**6. Uninsured deposits.** $38.2B, or 64.0% of deposits headline; 48.4% on the company's adjusted basis excluding ~$2.7B affiliate and ~$6.6B collateralized balances.

**7. Fee-business customer concentration, disclosed but unquantified.** The 10-K states certain wholesale-model businesses "can lead to customer concentrations for those businesses that, if negatively impacted by new entrants, competitive pressures, or consolidations, could affect the Company's fee income," with no name and no magnitude. Roughly 30% of revenue is fee income, so this is a material risk of unknown size.

**8. Governance and second-line resourcing.** The 10-K executive table shows Shannon Johnson serving as Chief Administrative Officer since 2019 "and as Chief Risk Officer since February 2026," and a February 10, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K is consistent with that transition but was not retrievable. Combining CAO and CRO at a bank that grew from ~$47B to ~$73B and whose charge-offs more than doubled in 2025 warrants monitoring. It may equally be an interim arrangement during an external search. Flagged as unverified.

**9. Management alignment signals.** Nine insiders sold roughly $5.4M notional between June and August 2026 across a $132–$149 price range with no open-market purchases: Kemper 15,062 shares at $148.55 (~1.0% of the 1,491,138 shares in that ownership line) plus 4,200 at $147.27, Rine 8,246 in June, and six others in smaller size. Individually these are routine post-vest liquidity in an open window following the July 28 release. Collectively, the absence of any offsetting purchase across two windows while the stock made new highs is consistent with insiders viewing the price as fair-to-full. Separately, CEO total compensation rose 56% to $10.35M in a year diluted EPS rose 3.3%, and the proxy's indexed TSR shows UMBF at $187.49 versus a company-selected peer index at $229.39 at year-end 2025 after leading in prior years — implying roughly +4.0% for UMBF against ~+58.5% for the comparator during the integration year. The pay increase is plausibly transaction- and retention-related, and the plan metrics include EPS and average ROTCE, both of which capture dilution; the CD&A composition was not verified. The peer-index constituents were not verified either, and a +58.5% single-year index move is large enough to warrant that caveat.

**10. Data hygiene.** The structured XBRL total-assets tag for this filer does not reconcile to the filings (the 10-K's securities-to-assets disclosure implies ~$73.1B and the Q2 10-Q shows $72.26B). All asset-based ratios here were computed from filing-derived figures.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 21.6% sits below one-year realized volatility of 26.9%, a −5.3 point spread, and in the 14th percentile of the current chain. IV fell from 23.9% to 21.6% since late July while the price rose 3.1%, the standard post-earnings compression. The term structure is flat (21.6% near, 20.3% at six months), put/call skew is balanced at the front (0.2%) with a −4.9% reading at the December tenor, and put/call volume is call-tilted at 0.50. Short interest is 6.4% of float at 5.2 days to cover, moderate and without squeeze mechanics.

The reading is that the options market prices no near-term catalyst or stress in this name, and that hedges are cheap relative to what the stock has actually delivered in realized volatility. For an investor accumulating a liability-sensitive bank into an uncertain rate path, inexpensive longer-dated protection is a reasonable complement. The absence of put demand does not corroborate the bear case; it also does not conflict with an ACCUMULATE.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($183, +26.1%)

The cost base is permanently lower. Q2's 51.4% efficiency ratio and 1.6% expense growth on 17.9% revenue growth reflect the completed systems conversion, and the unconfirmed ~$390M Q3 expense guide would be below Q2 — expense discipline holding while revenue grows. Core fee income compounding at ~18.4% organically in capital-light fund services, corporate trust and HSA custody sustains a mid-teens revenue trajectory independent of the balance sheet, and Institutional Banking net income up 32% corroborates it. If the Fed cuts, the flipped liability sensitivity turns into a tailwind (+5.1% NII on −300bp). Capital generation supports growth without issuance: CET1 up 49bp in six months while loans grew 6.1%, at a ~15% dividend payout, with a 2M-share authorization available. Compounding tangible book at ~12–14% annually at a stable 2.0x multiple produces low-teens annual returns before any re-rating, and closing the gap to the 12.7x peer median on a 20% ROTCE franchise adds more. Analyst mean $165.69 and Stephens' Overweight initiation support the direction.

### Bear Case ($115, −20.8%)

The margin story was a one-time mix shift and the market extrapolated it. Fed cash halved in six months, deposits are flat-to-down, and noninterest-bearing mix has fallen 370bp in a year, so the next stage of loan growth requires paying market rates to price-sensitive institutional depositors — the same dynamic that already forced peers to cut 2026 NIM guidance. Layer in accretion runoff (~12.6% of H1 pretax income, declining toward zero) and the loss of $27.1M quarterly securities gains, and 2027 EPS could be flat to down rather than up 5.5%. Credit is the second leg: 40.3% CRE in Sun Belt markets, a 1.06% allowance that thinned while the book grew 11.8%, a fast-growing 6.9% NDFI exposure with minimal disclosure, and H1 CRE charge-offs of $11.9M consistent with resolution-via-loss rather than borrower cures. A regional bank at 2.0x tangible book with slowing growth and normalizing credit does not hold that multiple; 9.5x a normalized $12.10 is $115. Nine insiders selling with zero purchases across $132–$149 is consistent with the view that fair value is close to the current price.

### Balance of evidence

The operating-leverage improvement is verified in reported results and structurally durable. The margin improvement is verified but arithmetically finite, and the company's own rate-sensitivity disclosure plus the balance-sheet mix data make that unambiguous. Consensus already discounts the plateau (5.5% out-year growth), which is why the stock trades at 10.4x next-year rather than a premium. That leaves a reasonable, unlevered path to low-teens annual returns from book-value compounding, with the principal downside risks being deposit-cost escalation and CRE/NDFI credit normalization against a thin allowance.

**Bull case target: $183 (+26.1%).** 13.2x $13.91, requiring Fed easing, resumed deposit growth, sustained high-teens fee growth, and buyback execution.

**Bear case target: $115 (−20.8%).** 9.5x a normalized $12.10 on deposit-cost escalation, NIM compression of 15–25bp, and credit normalization requiring allowance rebuild.

We would not pay above the low $150s, which would place the stock at 11x next-year with the NIM plateau already guided.

1. Deposits growing sequentially with noninterest-bearing mix stabilizing at or above 27% for two consecutive quarters — evidence the funding constraint is not binding.

4. Interest-bearing balances due from banks fall below ~$2.5B while loan growth continues, indicating the liquidity buffer is exhausted.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 release (late October 2026), which will test the ~$390M expense guide, the "flat core margin" claim, and the deposit trajectory in a single print. Secondary checkpoints are the September and October FOMC meetings, given the flipped liability sensitivity.

## Conclusion

UMB executed the Heartland integration well, and Q2 2026 documents it: 16 percentage points of positive operating leverage, a 51.4% efficiency ratio down from ~62% two years ago, ~20% ROTCE, and CET1 rising 49bp in six months while loans grew 6.1%. Core fee income compounding at ~18.4% organically in capital-light institutional businesses is the highest-quality part of the franchise and the best justification for the ~2.0x tangible book multiple.

The reason we step down from three consecutive BUY ratings to ACCUMULATE is entry arithmetic rather than fundamental deterioration.
