**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

JLL is one of the two or three largest commercial real estate (CRE) services and investment management firms globally, with $26.1B in FY2025 revenue, 113,000+ employees, and operations in 80+ countries. The investment case is a cyclical-recovery story: after the 2022–2023 rate shock crushed transactional volumes, leasing and capital markets are rebounding hard. FY2025 revenue grew 11%, operating income 26%, net income to common 45%, and adjusted EBITDA 22% to $1,453M. Q1 2026 extended the momentum: revenue +11%, adjusted EPS $3.43 (+48.5%), with Capital Markets adjusted EBITDA +59% and Leasing +21%. The company has beaten consensus in four straight quarters, twice by double digits.

The stock trades at roughly 16x trailing EPS and ~13x current-year consensus ($22.99), a wide discount to its Real Estate Services peer set (median P/E ~96x, distorted by trough earnings at peers but still indicative of relative cheapness). The headline P/E understates the discount further because $17.2B of the $26.1B reported revenue is pass-through gross contract cost reimbursed by clients; the economic business is the fee-revenue base, where adjusted EBITDA margins are healthier and expanding through platform leverage. Management has pivoted to buybacks: a $2.2B authorization added in February 2026 (~$2.7B total available, ~19% of market cap), an ASR executed near $289, and M&A spend cut to a trickle, signaling it views the equity as undervalued.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because of the combination of an AI-commoditization overhang and cyclical earnings dependence. The most-expanded risk factor in the FY25 10-K is artificial intelligence, framed as both opportunity and existential threat, and the stock fell ~4% on a clean Q1 beat as the market priced AI-disruption fears over fundamentals. Estimate revisions are positive but modest (+4.9% current year over 90 days, well short of the inflection threshold), and the cyclical engine driving the recovery would reverse sharply in a rate shock or recession. The franchise quality and balance sheet are genuine; the asymmetric mispricing the bulls require is not clearly present at $304.

## Company Overview

JLL traces its roots to 1783 (Jones Lang Wootton) and took modern form via the 1999 merger with Chicago-based LaSalle Partners. Roughly 62% of revenue is U.S.-dollar denominated; 38% is international, with GBP, EUR, AUD, and INR the largest non-USD exposures. Effective January 1, 2026 the company recast to four reportable segments:

- **Real Estate Management Services (REMS)** — $20.0B revenue (77% of total), the annuity engine, comprising Workplace Management ($13.8B), Project Management ($3.8B), and Property Management ($1.8B), with ~2.8–2.9B sq ft under management. This is recurring "Resilient" revenue but structurally low-margin, generating only ~$437M adjusted EBITDA on $20B because much of the revenue is pass-through gross contract cost reimbursed by clients.
- **Leasing Advisory** — $3.0B revenue, $580M adjusted EBITDA. High-margin transactional brokerage benefiting from an office-led recovery.
- **Capital Markets Services** — $2.4B revenue, $364M adjusted EBITDA. Investment sales, debt/equity advisory, loan servicing ($140B serviced; Fannie Mae DUS lender). The most cyclical and fastest-growing segment.
- **Investment Management (LaSalle)** — $450M revenue, $86.4B AUM, earning fees plus incentive fees and co-investment income.

The Software & Technology Solutions unit ($232M) was folded into REMS effective January 1, 2026, and proptech investments (JLL Spark, ~$340–358M fair value) moved to "All Other." Competitive moats include the integrated "One JLL" global platform, scale, brand (18 consecutive years on the "World's Most Ethical Companies" list), an investment-grade balance sheet (Baa1/BBB+), and a growing AI stack (JLL Falcon, JLL GPT, JLL Azara).

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory is a cyclical recovery off a depressed base:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $20.9B | $23.4B | $26.1B (+11%) | $6.39B (+11%) |
| Operating income | $868M | $868M | $1,098M (+26%) | $205M (+71%) |
| Net income (to common) | $654M | $547M | $792M (+45%) | $159M (+188%) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | — | $1,186M | $1,453M (+22%) | $274M (+22%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | $11.30 | $16.40 | $3.33 |

The recovery is concentrated in the cyclical units. FY25 Capital Markets adjusted EBITDA rose 49% and Leasing 25%, versus REMS at +10%. In Q1'26 the pattern intensified: Capital Markets +59%, Leasing +21%, REMS only +7%, with investment sales +27%, debt advisory +30%, and equity advisory ~+80%. Investment sales revenue grew 21% globally in 2025, outpacing the broader market's 18%, evidence of share capture.

Reported margins look thin (FY25 net margin ~3.0%, operating margin ~4.2%), but this is an accounting artifact: $17.2B of the $26.1B revenue (66%) is pass-through gross contract cost reimbursed by clients. The economically relevant base is fee revenue, where adjusted EBITDA margins are materially healthier and expanding through platform leverage. A standardized screen reading the headline net margin will systematically misjudge this business.

Cash generation is solid. FY25 operating cash flow was $1.19B (up from $785M), with FCF of roughly $979M. The balance sheet is investment-grade: at year-end 2025 the $3.3B facility was essentially undrawn, no commercial paper was outstanding, cash was $599M, and long-term debt was ~$806M (€350M Euro notes plus $400M 6.875% notes due 2028). Reported D/E of 1.36x overstates leverage because of large reimbursable and warehouse balances; the company manages to investment-grade leverage and coverage covenants and remained in compliance, with ~$3.9B of corporate liquidity. The Altman Z of 2.76 (grey zone) is likewise an artifact of an asset-heavy, low-margin pass-through model, not a distress signal. The four-quarter beat record (+13.2%, +17.7%, +5.2%, +2.1%) supports confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth engine is cyclical: the 2024–2025 recovery in capital markets and leasing off the rate-shock trough. FY2026 guidance is adjusted EPS of $21.80–$23.50, with management noting it trends toward the upper end amid "macro fluidity." Consensus sits at $22.99 for the current year (revised +4.9% over 90 days) and $26.11 for next year (+5.1% over 90 days), implying continued double-digit earnings growth as the cycle extends.

The estimate revision picture is positive but not an inflection signal. Current-quarter estimates are essentially flat over 30 days (-0.5%), and the 90-day current-year revision of +4.9% is well below the +30% threshold that would mark a true earnings inflection. This is a steady, confirmed recovery rather than an explosive re-rating setup. Revenue growth of 11% is healthy but does not clear the >40% bar that would justify treating trailing multiples as irrelevant.

Secular tailwinds are credible and durable: growth in corporate real estate outsourcing (which feeds the resilient REMS annuity), rising institutional allocations to real estate, urbanization, sustainability/decarbonization services, and AI/proptech. Office leasing outperformed market volumes (U.S. revenue +14% versus market volume +7% in Q1'26), signaling share gains and a flight-to-quality benefit. The "Resilient" REMS base, ~77% of revenue but only ~30% of segment EBITDA, cushions downside while the transactional segments drive upside in a recovery.

The principal growth risk is that the most powerful contributors, Capital Markets and Leasing, are the most macro-sensitive. A renewed rate shock or recession would hit these segments hard and quickly reverse the earnings momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

At $304, JLL trades at ~16x trailing EPS and ~13x current-year consensus, with a PEG comfortably below 1.0 on the implied double-digit growth. Against the Real Estate Services peer set, the discount is wide: the peer median P/E is 96x, though that figure is distorted by trough earnings (CoStar 545x, Cushman & Wakefield 41.5x, FirstService 39.9x) and is not a clean comparable. The more relevant point is that JLL trades below the cleaner cyclical comparables and below its own global peer CBRE on forward earnings.

Cheapness alone is anti-predictive: a low multiple is a warning of a value trap unless an independent catalyst is present. The valuation case here rests on three legs. First, the fee-revenue margin expansion is real and not yet fully reflected in a headline-margin screen, so on the economically correct base the multiple is lower than it appears. Second, the buyback authorization, with an ASR already executed near $289, provides a self-reinforcing per-share tailwind and a management signal that the equity is undervalued. Third, analyst targets (mean $387, median $389, high $447 against a low of $294) imply ~27% upside to the mean. On an inflection stock with PEG <1.0, consensus targets typically lag; here the wide gap between price and target floor argues the discount is at least partly a sentiment overhang rather than a fundamental judgment.

The counterweight is that the discount is not a free lunch. It reflects a genuine AI-commoditization fear and cyclical earnings that the market is reluctant to capitalize at a full multiple. JLL has company-specific outperformance evidence (share gains, buybacks, fee-margin leverage), but the cyclical dependence and AI overhang keep the asymmetry constructive rather than compelling. A valuation score of 6.5 reflects a clear relative discount with catalysts present, tempered by the structural reasons the market assigns it.

## Competitive Landscape

JLL operates in a consolidating but still-fragmented global CRE services market. Its primary global peer is CBRE; it also competes with regional and local brokers, investment banks, accounting/consulting firms, and increasingly with tech/SaaS entrants and self-performing in-house corporate teams. The industry is consolidating toward large integrated platforms, which structurally favors JLL.

The competitive trajectory is positive on current evidence. Office leasing and investment sales share gains (covered above) both indicate JLL is capturing volume from the market. The investment-grade balance sheet is a genuine differentiator in winning large REMS outsourcing mandates, where clients require financially strong long-term partners. The "One JLL" integrated platform and proprietary research/data create switching costs in the annuity REMS business.

The central competitive debate is AI. The bear thesis is that generative AI could commoditize brokerage and advisory work, and that JLL's cost-plus contract structure would force it to pass AI-driven cost savings to clients, compressing margins. The bull thesis, which management is actively pushing, is that AI (JLL Falcon, JLL GPT used by tens of thousands of employees daily) is a productivity and share-gain lever for the scaled incumbent with proprietary data. The honest read is that this tension is genuinely unresolved: a 113,000-person workforce and legacy data architecture could slow adoption relative to nimbler entrants, while scale and data could entrench the leader. The market is currently pricing the bear interpretation, which both creates the valuation discount and caps the near-term re-rating.

## Risk Assessment

The most company-specific and recently expanded risks from the FY25 10-K:

1. **AI as a double-edged sword** — the single most-expanded risk factor. The cost-plus contract structure is the specific mechanism that could compress margins if AI cost reductions must be passed to clients. This maps directly to the market overhang and is the central risk in the stock.
2. **Transactional revenue volatility** — Leasing, Capital Markets, and incentive fees are lumpy and macro-sensitive. The current strength is cyclical and would reverse with a rate shock or recession.
3. **Office-sector bifurcation** — evolving workplace strategies pressure older/lower-quality office assets, affecting brokerage volumes, REMS footprints, and LaSalle's office-heavy portfolios.
4. **Safety incidents (newly emphasized)** — the 10-K explicitly discloses a recent increase in serious safety incidents including fatalities of employees and contractors, an unusual operational disclosure and a reputational/liability watch item.
5. **Fannie Mae DUS loss-sharing** — ~$26.6B unpaid principal subject to loss-sharing (roughly one-third of losses, capped at 20% of origination balance). Off-balance-sheet credit exposure; current reserves ~$24M, no losses in Q1'26. A benign-but-real credit tail that would matter in a CRE credit downturn.
6. **Cyber/fiduciary/client-fund handling** — JLL handles substantial client funds; phishing/fraud risk is elevated.
7. **FX translation** — a 10% GBP move equals roughly $12M operating income.

The Q1'26 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors. The risk score of 5.5 reflects that the AI overhang and cyclical dependence are partially but not fully priced (the AI debate is unresolved and binary in direction), while the DUS exposure and safety disclosures are idiosyncratic tails the market has not focused on.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 33.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 33.7%, effectively fair (spread -0.4%), with IV in the 18th percentile of the current chain, so options are not pricing elevated near-term stress. The put/call skew is modestly elevated at 3.1% at the 1-month tenor, indicating some put demand consistent with the AI/cyclical overhang, but the 6-month skew flips to -3.6% (call-leaning), suggesting longer-dated positioning is more constructive. The put/call OI ratio fell from 1.24 to 1.04 over the latest snapshot as the price rose 1.2%, a mild bullish de-hedging. The implied 1-month move is ±7.0%. The term structure is flat, indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. The options market is neutral-to-mildly-constructive and does not flash a warning that would override the fundamental call.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The cyclical recovery in capital markets and leasing has multiple years to run off a depressed base, with confirmed share gains. Fee-revenue margins expand through platform leverage as transactional volume returns, and the market is systematically misjudging the business by reading the pass-through-inflated headline margin. The buyback, executed at prices below current, compounds per-share earnings. AI proves a share-gain and productivity lever for the scaled, data-rich incumbent rather than a commoditizer. Re-rating toward the analyst mean of $387 (~27% upside) as the AI fear fades and consensus targets prove to be floors. Bull case: $400–447.

### Bear Case

A rate shock or recession reverses the Capital Markets and Leasing recovery, exposing the lumpy, low-quality nature of the earnings. Generative AI commoditizes advisory work, and the cost-plus contract structure forces margin give-backs to clients. The CRE credit cycle turns, activating the DUS loss-sharing tail. The thin ~3% net margin offers little cushion in a downturn, and the cheap multiple proves a value trap rather than a discount. Bear case: $238–260 (revisiting the 52-week low).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month growth hold. The fundamentals are improving and the franchise is high-quality, but the absence of a true earnings inflection and an unresolved AI overhang keep the risk/reward constructive rather than asymmetric enough for a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions accelerate beyond +10% over 90 days, or two consecutive quarters of >10% beats accompanied by upward fee-margin guidance, confirming the recovery is being underestimated.
- Tangible evidence the AI overhang is resolving in JLL's favor — measurable margin or productivity gains attributed to the Falcon/GPT stack, or contract restructuring that mitigates the cost-plus pass-through risk.
- A pullback toward $270 or below on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore clear asymmetry to the analyst-target floor.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut on the FY26 range, or a quarterly miss in Capital Markets/Leasing revenue signaling the cyclical engine is stalling.
- A material rate shock (10Y materially higher) that re-freezes transactional volumes.
- Activation of the DUS loss-sharing reserves beyond the current ~$24M, signaling a CRE credit downturn.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July/early August) for the read on the upper-end-of-guidance trajectory and Capital Markets momentum.

## Conclusion

JLL is a market-leading, investment-grade CRE services franchise in a confirmed cyclical upswing, with record FY2025 earnings, four straight beats, share gains in its highest-margin segments, and a buyback authorization equal to ~19% of market cap signaling management's view that the equity is undervalued. At ~13x current-year fee earnings with a PEG below 1.0 and analyst targets ~27% above the current price, the valuation is genuinely discounted relative to its peer set and its own franchise quality.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY for specific and material reasons: the powerful earnings engine is cyclical and macro-sensitive; the AI-commoditization overhang is real, unresolved, and directly tied to the cost-plus contract structure; and the estimate-revision velocity (+4.9% over 90 days) falls short of a true inflection that would justify capitalizing the earnings at a full multiple. The franchise data continues to confirm the positive trend, but at $304, after the stock recovered from its February drawdown, the asymmetric mispricing the bull case requires is not clearly present. A deeper pullback, accelerating revisions, or AI-overhang resolution would convert this into a BUY.
