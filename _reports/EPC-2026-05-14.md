## Executive Summary

Edgewell Personal Care is a $841M market cap consumer products company operating primarily in wet shaving (Schick, Billie, Wilkinson Sword) and sun/skin care (Banana Boat, Hawaiian Tropic, Cremo). The stock has declined 37% from its 52-week high of $28.92 to $18.22, reflecting fundamental deterioration: three consecutive earnings misses, organic revenue contraction (-2.4% in Q2 FY26), margin compression from tariffs (420 bps headwind in Q2), and a multi-year supply chain restructuring that is running over budget.

The company is divesting declining Feminine Care ($340M to Essity, completed February 2026), consolidating manufacturing in Mexico, and investing in brand building. This plan is rational but carries extreme execution risk given: (1) leverage at 3.3-3.5x net debt/EBITDA, (2) only 7% goodwill headroom on the Wet Shave unit ($1.57B carrying value), and (3) $750M in notes maturing 2028 requiring refinancing. Any further operational stumble could trigger impairment charges exceeding $200M and materially worsen refinancing terms.

The Cremo brand (+38% growth) and improving U.S. market share (+50 bps) provide reasons for long-term optimism, but these are insufficient to offset the near-term structural risks. At 8.7-10.7x adjusted FY26 EPS, the valuation appears cheap for Consumer Staples, but the discount correctly reflects the probability of further impairment, refinancing pressure, and continued category decline.

## Company Overview

Edgewell Personal Care was spun out of Energizer Holdings in 2015. Following the February 2026 divestiture of its Feminine Care segment, the company operates two segments:

- **Wet Shave (~60% of revenue):** Schick, Wilkinson Sword, Billie, Edge, Skintimate. The #2 global player behind P&G's Gillette, with strong positions in Japan and Europe. North America is in structural decline (-1.3% category consumption in Q2 FY26).
- **Sun and Skin Care (~40% of revenue):** Banana Boat, Hawaiian Tropic, Wet Ones, Cremo, Jack Black, Bulldog. Seasonal and weather-dependent, with Cremo emerging as the high-growth engine.

Walmart represents 17.4% of sales with no long-term supply agreements. International operations contribute 46% of revenue. The company employs approximately 5,700 people globally.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Essentially flat over four years at $2.17B-$2.25B. FY25 organic growth was -1.3%. Q2 FY26 organic sales declined 2.4% despite U.S. share gains, indicating category headwinds are overwhelming share capture.

**Profitability collapse:** GAAP operating income fell from $227M (FY23) to $96.6M (FY25), driven by a $51.1M goodwill impairment and $53.1M in restructuring charges. Even adjusted operating margins compressed from 11.9% (FY24) to 9.9% (FY25). Tariffs are a structural headwind: 150+ bps in FY25, accelerating to 420 bps in Q2 FY26.

**Cash flow deterioration:** Operating cash flow fell from $231M (FY24) to $118M (FY25). FY26 adjusted FCF is guided to $80M-$110M — a 9.5-13% FCF yield at current market cap, but this is during "peak capital intensity" per management and includes elevated restructuring spend.

**Balance sheet fragility:**
- Total debt: $1.42B (90% fixed-rate, 4.125%-5.50%)
- Net debt/EBITDA: targeting 3.3-3.5x at FY26 end
- Goodwill + intangibles: $2.21B (59% of total assets)
- Wet Shave goodwill headroom: 7% above carrying value
- Banana Boat trade name headroom: 4% above carrying value
- Z-Score: 1.82 (grey zone — elevated bankruptcy probability)

The balance sheet is the central risk. At 7% and 4% headroom on the two largest intangible assets, any revenue shortfall, margin compression, or increase in discount rates (from rising interest rates or peer multiple compression) triggers impairment. A single 1x reduction in market comparables would breach the Wet Shave carrying value, potentially generating $200M+ in charges.

**Debt maturity profile:** $750M due 2028, $500M due 2029. If the business hasn't demonstrated clear recovery trajectory before 2027-2028 refinancing negotiations, spreads will widen materially. Current rates of 4.125%-5.50% were secured in a more favorable environment; refinancing at current market rates with 3.3x leverage and declining earnings would likely cost 150-250 bps more.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic growth: negative.** Revenue has been flat-to-declining for four years. FY26 guidance is -1% to +2% organic — at the midpoint, essentially zero growth. The company's largest category (Wet Shave, North America) is in structural decline as consumer habits shift toward less frequent shaving, lower-priced alternatives, and DTC brands.

**Bright spots exist but are small-scale:**
- Cremo: +38% growth in Q2 FY26, expanding into body wash and deodorant. Credible brand momentum, but likely represents <10% of total revenue.
- International Wet Shave: +3.5% growth in FY25. Japan and Europe remain stable markets.
- U.S. Sun Care consumption: +17% in Q2 FY26 with EPC gaining 180 bps of share. Positive but highly seasonal and weather-dependent.

**The "peak pain" narrative:** Management positions FY26 as the trough — elevated restructuring costs from the Mexico plant consolidation, duplicate site operations, and strategic investments. If the consolidation delivers the promised ~200 bps of gross margin expansion in FY27-28, adjusted EPS could recover to $2.50-$3.00. This is the bull case, but plant transitions frequently run over budget and behind schedule. Phase 1 is already proving "more complex than planned" per management commentary, with overtime, airfreight, and extended duplicate-site costs.

**Estimate revision trend: decisively negative.** Current year EPS estimate has fallen 18.1% over 90 days (from $2.33 to $1.91). Next year estimate down 15.3% (from $2.50 to $2.12). Current quarter slashed 46% in 90 days. Analysts are systematically cutting estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $18.22 with FY26 adjusted EPS guidance of $1.70-$2.10, EPC trades at 8.7-10.7x adjusted earnings. The FY27 consensus of $2.12 implies 8.6x forward P/E. Against peer median P/E of 13.9x, this represents a 38% discount.

The discount is partially justified:
- Peers (Central Garden, WD-40, Interparfums) have stable-to-growing revenue. EPC has declining organic sales.
- Peers have manageable leverage. EPC is at 3.3-3.5x with a 2028 maturity wall.
- Peers do not face impairment risk on 59% of their asset base. EPC does.
- The closest comparable, Energizer (ENR), trades at 6.3x P/E with a 6.8% yield, suggesting the category may warrant an even lower multiple.

The 3.3% dividend yield provides modest income support, but dividend coverage is thin at ~$28M annual payout against $80-110M FCF guidance. Any further cash flow deterioration puts the dividend at risk.

**Book value context:** Market cap ($841M) is well below shareholders' equity ($1.55B pre-Fem Care), implying the market prices significant probability of further impairment charges reducing book value toward market value. This is a rational market conclusion, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Edgewell is structurally disadvantaged relative to its primary competitors:

- **P&G (Gillette):** ~$80B annual revenue company with >10x Edgewell's marketing budget and unmatched retail leverage.
- **Unilever:** Similar scale advantage with Dollar Shave Club providing DTC capabilities.
- **Kenvue (J&J's consumer spinoff):** $15B revenue with Sun Care brands (Neutrogena, Aveeno) that overlap with Banana Boat/Hawaiian Tropic.

Edgewell's competitive position is deteriorating in its core category. North America Wet Shave volume has been declining for years as consumers shave less frequently and substitute cheaper alternatives. Private-label penetration continues to rise. The Billie brand (acquired 2021) brought DTC capabilities but has not arrested overall category trends.

Cremo represents the best competitive asset — growing 38% with expansion into adjacent categories. But it remains small relative to the portfolio and faces competition from well-funded P&G and Unilever brands in grooming.

**Trajectory: declining.** Share gains in Sun Care (+180 bps) are positive but insufficient to offset Wet Shave structural headwinds. The company is gaining share in a shrinking category while losing absolute volume.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Goodwill impairment (HIGH PROBABILITY, HIGH SEVERITY):** A 100 bps increase in the discount rate or a 1x decline in market multiples breaches the threshold. Given rising rates and persistent stock price weakness, this appears likely within 12 months, potentially generating $200-400M in charges.

2. **Refinancing risk (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Management must demonstrate meaningful earnings recovery before refinancing negotiations in 2027. Current trajectory does not support this.

3. **Tariff escalation:** The company is consolidating into Mexico during a period of extreme trade policy uncertainty. Additional tariffs on Mexican imports would directly impair the cost rationale for the plant consolidation.

4. **Execution failure on transformation:** Running duplicate manufacturing sites, launching multiple brand campaigns simultaneously, integrating a new leadership team (new President of North America), and managing a divestiture. Each adds complexity; together they create compounding execution risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($23-25, +26-37% upside)
- Mexico plant consolidation delivers on schedule, generating 200 bps gross margin expansion in FY28
- Cremo scales to $200M+ brand, driving positive organic growth by FY27
- FY27 EPS recovers to $2.50+ as restructuring charges roll off and margins normalize
- Refinancing executes smoothly at modest spread premium
- No impairment charges triggered
- Stock re-rates toward 10-11x forward earnings ($25-27.50)

### Bear Case ($10-13, -29-45% downside)
- Wet Shave impairment triggered: $300-500M charge devastates book value and signals to credit markets
- Mexico consolidation runs 12+ months over budget; duplicate costs persist into FY28
- North America organic growth remains negative as consumer weakens further
- Tariff escalation on Mexican imports negates cost savings from consolidation
- Refinancing in 2027-2028 at 250+ bps wider spreads; potential forced asset sale
- Dividend cut required as FCF proves insufficient for debt service + capex + dividend
- Stock trades toward 5-6x impaired forward EPS ($10-13)

### Base Case ($16-19, approximately flat)
The most likely 12-month outcome is continued volatile trading near current levels as the market waits for evidence that the transformation is working. FY27 guidance (likely provided in November 2026) becomes the key signpost. The stock remains range-bound until either (a) clear earnings recovery is demonstrated or (b) impairment/refinancing concerns crystallize.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Two specific catalysts exist. First, Q3 FY26 earnings (expected August 2026) — the seasonally critical Sun Care quarter where weather-dependent results and further tariff impacts could drive another miss. The company has missed 3 of the last 4 quarters. Second, the annual goodwill impairment test (September 30 fiscal year-end) could trigger formal write-downs if the stock remains below $20 and comparable multiples compress further.

**Timing window:** Q3 earnings in August 2026 (3 months) and impairment testing around November 2026 (6 months). Both are within reasonable short holding periods.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 12.8% of float with 10.6 days to cover is elevated but not extreme. The long days-to-cover means any forced covering would be multi-day, but the lack of any obvious positive catalyst (no M&A speculation, no activist involvement) limits squeeze probability.

**Borrow and options signal:** The 76.9% IV (rich vs. 42.2% HV) means put protection is expensive. The 2.01 put/call OI ratio and backwardation structure confirm institutional bearish positioning is already present, increasing cost-to-carry for new shorts via options.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $10-13 (-29-45%) vs. upside risk to $23-25 (+26-37%). Raw risk/reward of approximately 1:1 is unfavorable for an active short. The transformation could work, and a single positive earnings surprise would trigger short covering on 10.6 days-to-cover.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis (declining category, execution risk, impairment probability) is correct, but the risk/reward is approximately symmetric. Elevated short interest and long days-to-cover create meaningful squeeze risk relative to the remaining downside. The appropriate action is to avoid owning the stock, not actively short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the view to HOLD/BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive organic revenue growth (demonstrating category stabilization)
2. Net leverage below 2.5x with clear path to refinancing at manageable spreads
3. Goodwill headroom expanding to >20% on both Wet Shave and Banana Boat (requires sustained earnings growth and/or higher stock price)
4. Insider open-market purchases by CEO or CFO (demonstrating personal conviction in near-term recovery)

**Timeline for headwind persistence:** 12-18 months minimum. The Mexico plant consolidation won't deliver steady-state margins until FY28. The refinancing window opens in 2027. The transformation is a 2-3 year process that is less than halfway complete with limited evidence of success to date.

**Reassessment point:** Q3 FY26 earnings (August 2026) and FY27 guidance (November 2026). If Q3 demonstrates positive organic growth and management raises FY27 EPS expectations above $2.50, the thesis warrants reconsideration.

## Conclusion

Edgewell Personal Care faces a difficult combination of structural category decline, elevated leverage, thin impairment headroom, negative estimate revisions, and execution risk from a multi-year transformation. While the valuation (8.7-10.7x adjusted EPS) appears optically cheap for Consumer Staples, the discount correctly reflects the probability of further impairment charges, refinancing pressure, and continued operational deterioration.

The absence of insider buying despite a 37% stock decline, three consecutive earnings misses, and 12.8% short interest all confirm that informed participants see limited near-term recovery probability. Cremo's growth and the eventual margin benefits of plant consolidation could create long-term value, but the 12-month outlook offers unfavorable risk/reward. The downside scenarios (impairment, refinancing pressure, continued misses) are more probable and more severe than the upside scenarios, which require multiple quarters of flawless execution that the company has not demonstrated.
