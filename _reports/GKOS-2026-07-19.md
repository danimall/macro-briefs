**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Glaukos has moved from a controlled-launch story to a company delivering broad-based commercial growth. Q1 2026 revenue of $150.6M grew 41% year over year, U.S. glaucoma revenue rose 58%, and iDose TR — the intracameral travoprost implant approved in late 2023 — reached roughly $54M in a single quarter, annualizing to a run-rate above $200M. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $620-635M, implying about 24% full-year growth, and non-GAAP gross margin expanded to approximately 84%. The reported GAAP gross margin decline from 75% to 56% in 2025 is entirely a $112.9M non-cash impairment of the Photrexa developed-technology intangible, triggered by the October 2025 approval of the superseding Epioxa product. A standardized screen would misread this as margin deterioration; the underlying franchise economics are high-margin and improving.

The investment case turns on reimbursement mechanics rather than macro variables. iDose TR physician fees are set MAC-by-MAC across seven regional contractors, and adoption is gated by how quickly the remaining contractors publish favorable professional fees. Progress is accelerating: Palmetto published code 0660T in late March 2026, Epioxa now has access pathways covering more than 100M commercial lives, and CMS assigned Epioxa a product-specific J-code (J2789) effective July 1, 2026. The January 2026 approval of unlimited iDose re-administration materially expands the recurring-revenue opportunity per patient. These are the events that underpin the guidance raise and the roughly 46% stock rally since late May.

I rate GKOS a BUY with an overall score of 6.6. The combination of accelerating revenue, a category-creating lead product with expanding margins, a de-risked balance sheet, and analyst consensus targets ($165 mean, $190 high) above the current $156.83 supports the call. The score is held back from higher conviction by persistent GAAP losses, negative near-term estimate revisions, reimbursement execution risk, and the absence of open-market insider buying.

## Company Overview

Glaukos is an ophthalmic pharmaceutical and medical technology company headquartered in Aliso Viejo, California, with approximately 1,094 employees. It operates in one reportable segment across five technology platforms addressing four therapeutic areas. The company pioneered Micro-Invasive Glaucoma Surgery (MIGS), launching the first iStent in 2012.

The product portfolio spans four buckets. The **iStent family** (iStent, iStent inject W, iStent infinite) comprises micro-scale trabecular bypass stents for glaucoma and forms the historical revenue base. **iDose TR** is a first-of-its-kind intracameral travoprost implant that delivers glaucoma medication continuously for up to about three years; approved in December 2023 with a controlled launch in February 2024, it is the current growth engine. **iLink / corneal health** covers bio-activated corneal cross-linking for keratoconus, where the legacy epi-off Photrexa product is being superseded by the incision-free epi-on Epioxa (approved October 2025, launched March 19, 2026). **iLution** (transdermal) and **Retina XR** (sustained-release intravitreal, including GLK-401 for wet AMD) are earlier-stage pipeline assets.

Revenue mix in 2025 was roughly 83% iStent, iDose, and glaucoma accessories, and about 17% iLink/corneal. Geographic split was 74% U.S. and 26% international, with no customer exceeding 10% of sales. Competitive positioning rests on clinical differentiation and category leadership: iDose TR is category-creating in procedural glaucoma pharmacotherapy, and Epioxa is the only FDA-approved epi-on cross-linking therapy.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue trajectory shows a clear acceleration. Sales grew from $282.9M in 2022 to $314.7M in 2023, $383.5M in 2024, and $507.4M in 2025 (+32%). Q1 2026 revenue was up 39% constant-currency. Within the quarter, U.S. glaucoma revenue rose to $93.5M with iDose TR contributing its ~$54M, international glaucoma grew 23%, and corneal grew 15%. The FY2026 guidance raise implies continued strong growth against tougher comps.

The gross margin picture requires care. The entire GAAP decline reflects the one-time Photrexa impairment, recorded in cost of goods sold after Epioxa's approval rendered Photrexa non-recoverable. Underlying non-GAAP gross margin was approximately 84% in Q1 2026, up from 82% in Q1 2025. This is a high-margin franchise, and the reported decline is an accounting artifact.

On the operating line, GAAP net loss widened to $187.7M in 2025 from $146.4M in 2024, but non-GAAP operating loss improved to $(10.5)M in Q1 2026 from $(15.2)M a year earlier. The company is approaching operating breakeven as iDose revenue scales against a largely fixed commercial and R&D base. SG&A rose 27% and R&D rose 10% in 2025, reflecting deliberate launch and pipeline investment rather than distress.

The balance sheet is healthy. Altman Z-score of 3.02 sits in the safe zone; the current ratio is 4.69; debt-to-equity is 0.36; equity-to-assets is 0.73. Cash and short-term investments total roughly $277-280M. The 2.75% convertible notes due 2027 were fully retired in 2024, reducing long-term debt from $283M in 2023 to about $68M, now primarily a finance lease on the Aliso facility. Interest expense is minimal.

Cash flow improved materially: operating cash use narrowed to $(14.8)M in 2025 from $(61.3)M in 2024, with FCF of $(22.5)M. The item to watch is accounts receivable, which jumped from $61M to $109M as Glaukos offered extended payment terms to support the iDose launch. This is a strategic choice that flatters reported revenue relative to cash collection and warrants monitoring for collectibility as the base grows. The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in estimates — three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the two most recent beats at 35% and 39% relative to consensus loss estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is one of the cleaner ones available in medtech because it is broad-based across products and geographies rather than a single-line story. iDose TR is the primary driver, and its trajectory is strong against a controlled launch just two years earlier. The unlimited re-administration approval converts iDose from a fixed-duration product into a recurring-revenue franchise per patient, materially expanding lifetime value.

The second leg is Epioxa, the only FDA-approved epi-on cross-linking therapy for keratoconus. Its site-of-care network already covers about 65% of the U.S. population (targeting ~95%), and payer access pathways include four of the five largest payers. This positions corneal health for reacceleration after transient Photrexa-to-Epioxa transition headwinds.

International adds a third leg: EU MDR and UK certification for the iStent family in June 2025 opens European launches under the new regulatory framework. The pipeline is deep: iDose TREX Phase 2a showed 8.6-10.8 mmHg IOP reduction at three months, iDose TRIO Phase 3b enrollment is complete, the iStent infinite mild-to-moderate PMA pivotal completed enrollment in Q4 2025, and PRESERFLO MicroShunt is advancing in the U.S.

Estimate revisions are the one soft spot in the growth data. Consensus current-year EPS rose 3.2% over 90 days, but next-quarter estimates fell 47.2% over the same period and next-year moved down 4.4%. This reflects launch-investment timing and the loss of the Ivantis/Hydrus royalty tailwind (contractually expired April 26, 2025) more than demand weakness — revenue guidance was raised in the same window. I weight the raised revenue guidance and the top-line acceleration more heavily than the loss-line estimate softness, but the revision direction keeps me from scoring growth at the highest tier.

## Valuation Assessment

At roughly $9.2B market cap against FY2026 revenue guidance, GKOS trades at approximately 14-15x forward sales while still GAAP-unprofitable. Trailing and forward P/E are meaningless at a loss-making stage, so the appropriate lens is EV/sales adjusted for growth and gross margin, plus the path to profitability.

The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited direct use: Masimo at 127x P/E, Insulet at 35x, Globus at 19x, with two peers (Bruker, Baxter) posting negative earnings. The median P/E of 34.8x reflects the mix of profitable and unprofitable names and offers little precise anchor. On a revenue-multiple basis, a ~14-15x forward-sales multiple on a company growing above 24% with 84% non-GAAP gross margins and a category-creating lead product is a growth/story multiple, not an obviously cheap one, but not indefensible for the quality and growth rate on offer.

Analyst targets provide the clearest asymmetry signal: mean $165, median $166.5, low $138, high $190, against the current $156.83. All thirteen covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy with zero holds or sells. On a stock with rising revenue guidance and reasonable multiples relative to growth, these targets tend to lag and get revised upward after beat-and-raise quarters, so I treat the $165 mean as a near-term floor rather than a ceiling and the $190 high as achievable if reimbursement clarity continues.

I score valuation at 5.0 — neutral. The multiple is not cheap, but the "cheapness" trap does not apply here; the risk is the opposite, that a rich revenue multiple prices in successful execution. The valuation is fair for the growth and quality, with modest upside to consensus targets, which supports a BUY on the strength of the other dimensions rather than on the valuation itself.

## Competitive Landscape

Glaukos holds genuine category leadership in two areas. In procedural glaucoma pharmacotherapy, iDose TR's closest competitor is AbbVie's Durysta (bimatoprost implant), but iDose's ~3-year duration versus months, now paired with unlimited re-administration, is a meaningful differentiation. Broader MIGS competition includes Alcon (Hydrus), Sight Sciences, AbbVie, New World Medical, and Iantrek.

In corneal health, Glaukos has the only FDA-approved bio-activated cross-linking therapy, and Epioxa's epi-on, incision-free approach is a standard-of-care advance over the painful epi-off procedure. The threats to monitor are Epion Therapeutics, developing a competitive cross-linking therapy, and compounding pharmacies that gained an opening after Photrexa's orphan exclusivity expired in 2023.

The trajectory is improving rather than merely stable: iDose is taking category share it is largely creating, Epioxa extends a monopoly-adjacent position in corneal disease, and the European launches open new geography. Pay-versus-performance data corroborate the competitive strength, with cumulative TSR of 150 versus a peer group at 78. The main structural competitive variable is reimbursement, a company-affecting fundamental rather than a generic sector risk. I score competitive position at 8.0, consistent with the prior report's read.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is reimbursement. iDose TR professional fees are set MAC-by-MAC; as of the 10-K, only four of seven contractors had published fees. Slow or inconsistent publication directly gates adoption. Separately, 2026 CMS Final Rules cut physician fees for cataract/surgical MIGS Category I codes, and LCDs finalized by five of seven MACs in November 2024 confirmed non-coverage for MIGS-on-MIGS combinations, pressuring legacy iStent-with-cataract volumes.

Concentration risk is real: the growth thesis rests almost entirely on two launches (iDose ramp, Epioxa uptake), and nearly all iStent/iDose production runs through a single San Clemente facility in an earthquake- and wildfire-exposed region. The Ivantis/Hydrus royalty income expired in April 2025, creating a comparison headwind. Drug-pricing exposure through Medicaid Drug Rebate, 340B, and IRA inflation-rebate rules reduces net pricing. Newly expanded 10-K risk language covers AI regulation, the Q4 2025 government shutdown potentially delaying FDA reviews, tariff uncertainty (limited given U.S.-centric sourcing), and Loper Bright regulatory uncertainty. The Q1 non-GAAP reconciliation surfaced a small (~$0.7M) trade-secrets litigation add-back that is not prominent in the risk factors and warrants monitoring in the next 10-Q. The accumulated deficit of $933M underscores that profitability timing remains uncertain.

Much of this is at least partially priced given the loss-making status and the reimbursement dependency being well understood by the market. The accelerating reimbursement progress reduces the binary character of the central risk. I score risk factors at 5.5 — the reimbursement swing factor and launch concentration are material and not fully de-risked, but they are resolving in a favorable direction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 61.5% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 51.7% (a 9.8-point spread) and in the 82nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (61.5% near vs. 53.2% long-dated), consistent with an expected catalyst-driven move soon, likely the next earnings print and continued reimbursement news flow. The implied ±15.4% move on the nearest expiry is large. The put/call skew is negative (-3.6%, call-heavy), and the put/call OI ratio shifted from 0.46 to 1.24 as the stock ran 22.9% higher, indicating positioning rotation rather than accumulating downside bets. The rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry timing or scaled accumulation rather than a single-day full position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** iDose TR continues its ramp as remaining MACs publish favorable professional fees, unlimited re-administration lifts per-patient revenue, and Epioxa scales on the new J-code and payer coverage. Non-GAAP gross margin holds near 84%, operating leverage carries the company to breakeven, and European launches add a fresh growth vector. Revenue reaches or exceeds the $635M top of guidance in 2026 with a durable 20%+ growth trajectory beyond. The stock re-rates toward the $190 analyst high and beyond as the loss narrows to profitability. Bull target: $190.

**Bear Case.** Reimbursement stalls: remaining MACs delay or set unfavorable iDose fees, MIGS-on-MIGS non-coverage and cataract MIGS fee cuts pressure legacy volumes, and Epioxa payer build-out lags. The AR build proves to reflect aggressive extended terms that compress into a collections problem, and continued GAAP losses plus the Huntsville and Aliso capex step-up extend the path to profitability. The ~15x forward-sales multiple compresses on any guidance disappointment. Bear target: ~$115 (roughly -27% from current), near the pre-Q1-beat trading range and the $138 analyst low as a nearer floor.

**Base Case.** iDose and Epioxa scale roughly in line with the raised guidance, reimbursement clarity continues incrementally, and the company approaches operating breakeven through 2026. The multiple holds and consensus targets drift upward after beat-and-raise quarters. Base target: $178.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $178.** This reflects the current ~14-15x forward-sales multiple applied to modestly upgraded 2027 revenue expectations as consensus revises upward following continued execution, roughly consistent with the gap between current price and the analyst high with the mean drifting higher. Approximately +14% from $156.83.

**Bull case target: $190** (+21% from current) — the current analyst high, achievable if reimbursement clarity accelerates and 2026 revenue prints at or above the $635M top of guidance.

**Bear case target: ~$115** (-27% from current) — reimbursement stall, a guidance miss, or a receivables-quality problem drives multiple compression back toward pre-Q1 levels.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to the next two earnings prints (the next report following the April 29, 2026 quarter) and reimbursement milestones through 2026, including the July 1 Epioxa J-code activation and remaining MAC fee publications.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. iDose TR quarterly revenue growth decelerates materially (sequential decline or U.S. glaucoma growth falling below ~20% YoY) in the next two prints.
2. Management cuts or fails to reaffirm the $620-635M FY2026 revenue guidance.
3. Accounts receivable continues to build faster than revenue with any disclosed collectibility reserve or write-down, signaling the extended-terms strategy has become a quality-of-revenue problem.

## Conclusion

Glaukos presents a genuine commercial inflection, with iDose TR at run-rate scale, accelerating revenue, raised FY2026 guidance, non-GAAP gross margins near 84%, and a de-risked balance sheet after the 2024 convert retirement. The trajectory of the scores (5.2 → 6.5 → 6.5) is confirmed by new data, and I nudge the overall to 6.6 as the Q1 beat-and-raise and reimbursement progress reinforce the growth and competitive dimensions. The heavily weighted growth and competitive-position dimensions are strong (8.0 each), financial health is solid for a company approaching breakeven (6.5), and the balance sheet supports the runway to fund the Huntsville and Aliso buildout and pipeline without financing pressure.

The offsets are real and keep this from being a maximum-conviction call: valuation is fair rather than cheap (5.0), management quality is neutral given routine insider selling into the rally and no open-market buying (5.5), and idiosyncratic risk around reimbursement and launch concentration remains material though resolving favorably (5.5). Negative near-term loss estimate revisions and the AR build are the two data points to watch. On balance, with analyst targets above the current price, a broad-based growth engine, and reimbursement catalysts accelerating, the risk/reward favors ownership. **BUY.**
