## Executive Summary

HCI Group delivered another exceptionally profitable quarter. Q2 2026 gross premiums earned rose 6.0% to $320.8M, net premiums earned 9.4% to $219.0M, pretax income 17.6% to $111.0M, and diluted EPS 8.1% to $5.60 against a consensus somewhere between $5.00 and $5.13 depending on the aggregator. Book value per share reached $86.60, up 47.9% year over year. The net combined ratio was 61.5%, the gross loss ratio 22.2%, and catastrophe losses were zero — as they were in Q2 2025, 1H 2025 and 1H 2026. Annualized first-half return on equity was 27.8%. On trailing after-NCI earnings of $310.4M the stock trades at 7.3x; on additive diluted EPS of $23.20 it trades at 7.8x; against a peer median P/E of 14.6x, that is a wide discount.

The discount exists for identifiable reasons, and two of them got worse this quarter. First, the growth is not accruing to HCI shareholders. Consolidated GWP grew 7.2% in Q2, but Homeowners Choice grew 0.5% and TypTap grew 0.1% — combined +0.35%. The entire increment came from CORE and Tailrow, the two consolidated reciprocal exchanges owned by their policyholder-subscribers. Noncontrolling interests absorbed $20.7M of first-half net income against $8.7M a year earlier and $21.4M for all of FY2025. Second, policies in force fell from ~313,400 at 12/31/2025 to ~290,100 at 6/30/2026, a 7.4% sequential decline, with zero policies assumed from Citizens in the half against 13,917 in 1H 2025. Management states average premium per policy is roughly unchanged, so this is unit attrition, not mix. Florida competitors are filing rate decreases (Security First cut 5.6%), Citizens' policy count hit a record low in June, and the takeout pipeline that drove 60,820 assumed policies in 2025 has gone quiet.

The score trajectory (6.9 → 6.9 → 7.3 → 6.8 → 6.3) has been declining for three cycles, and this report continues that direction to 5.8 while moving off the four-consecutive-BUY sequence. The reason is not the price — at $180.89 the stock is roughly where it stood at the prior review — but the content of the Q2 filing: the per-carrier premium decomposition and the sequential policy count are new, quantified evidence that the growth engine has stalled, and the company now sits at the front of peak Atlantic season with the 2026-27 reinsurance retentions and first-event limits undisclosed. Base case $203 (+12.2%), bull $255 (+41.0%), bear $138 (-23.7%). That distribution supports holding, not adding.

## Company Overview

HCI is a Tampa-based insurance holding company operating five reported segments, of which one matters economically:

**Insurance Operations.** Two wholly owned carriers — Homeowners Choice Property & Casualty (Q2 GWP $228.2M), the legacy Florida homeowners book, and TypTap Insurance Company (Q2 GWP $110.5M), the technology-underwritten homeowners and flood carrier.

**Exzeo.** The insurance technology stack, carved out via an NYSE IPO (XZO) on November 6, 2025 at $21.00 per share. HCI retained 83.1% (75,000,000 of 90,200,252 shares) at 6/30/2026. Exzeo reported Q2 segment revenue of $60.75M, of which $6.5M came from non-affiliates. For the half, $118.8M total and $11.3M non-affiliate. Roughly 90% of the segment's revenue is billed to HCI's own insurance subsidiaries and eliminates on consolidation.

**Reciprocal Exchange Operations.** CORE (condo, writing since 2024) and Tailrow (writing since 2025), each capitalized with a $25M 9% subordinated surplus note and managed through an attorney-in-fact subsidiary. These are consolidated variable interest entities owned by their policyholder-subscribers; their underwriting profit is attributed to noncontrolling interests. Stated maximum exposure to loss is $25M each.

**Real Estate** ($102.7M of investments, $0.5M of 1H pretax income) and **Corporate & Other** (−$7.4M) are immaterial.

Also disclosed: two captive reinsurers (Claddaugh in Bermuda; Fortex Reinsurance SPC Ltd., licensed as a Cayman Class B insurer in March 2026), and perRisk Insurance Company, an Arizona surplus-lines carrier that has not commenced business.

The "13 states" and "diversified holding company" framing does not survive contact with the geographic disclosure: Q2 GPE was $291.9M Florida (91.0%) versus $28.9M non-Florida (9.0%), with non-Florida GPE down about 4.3% year over year, and the 10-Q states that substantially all assets are located in Florida. Concentration is increasing.

### Margins are flat, and FY2025 was the anomaly

Derived net combined ratios: Q2 2026 61.5% (32.5% loss + 29.0% expense) versus Q2 2025 61.9%; 1H 2026 59.2% versus 1H 2025 59.0%. FY2025 printed 56.3%, better than the current run rate, because it absorbed a $61.2M derecognition of ceded losses tied to a favorable revision of the Hurricane Milton estimate. The clean cat-free underlying run rate is 59-62%, not 56%. The gross loss ratio ticked up from 21.3% to 22.2%.

The margin tailwind in the quarter came from the June 1 reinsurance renewal, not underwriting. The cession rate fell from 33.88% to 31.74% of GPE ($102.5M/$302.6M versus $101.8M/$320.8M), worth roughly $6.9M in the quarter — with only one of three months under the new program. Management is reported to have quantified the run-rate saving at more than $10M per quarter, but that figure comes from a single secondary source and is not confirmed in any filing or issuer release. Taken at face value it would be about $40M pretax, roughly $30M after tax at the ~25.3% effective rate, or about $2.35 per diluted share — approximately 10% of cat-free EPS. It should be carried as unverified.

There is a second, less comfortable reading of the lower cession. HCI licensed Fortex in Cayman in March 2026, and the 10-Q states it "will allow us additional flexibility to selectively retain risk and reduce the cost of third-party reinsurance." Risk retained inside a consolidated captive does not leave the group; it moves from a third-party balance sheet to HCI's. If a meaningful part of the cession-rate decline reflects layers now sitting in Claddaugh or Fortex, reported margin improvement overstates the improvement in risk-adjusted earnings and the landfall-year downside is worse than the prior structure implied. Global property-cat pricing genuinely softened through 2025-26 after two benign seasons, so a price win at unchanged retentions is entirely plausible — but the 2026-27 retentions, first-event limit, aggregate limit and reinstatement terms are not disclosed in the Q2 10-Q or the release. Two Item 1.01 8-Ks (June 1 and June 15) likely contain the placement. Until those terms are known, the catastrophe downside cannot be quantified with confidence.

### Balance sheet: strong, with a new exposure

Debt is $67.5M against $1,079.9M of HCI stockholders' equity, a 6.3% debt/equity ratio. Interest expense fell from $3.7M to $1.1M year over year after the $172.5M 4.75% convertible notes converted into 2,187,063 shares in Q2 2025. The revolver has $150M of capacity with $36.0M drawn; a $75M ATM facility is entirely unused. Insurance subsidiaries dividended $77.5M to the holding company in the half, so holdco liquidity does not depend on the credit facility. Operating cash flow was $273.9M in 1H 2026 versus $307.0M in 1H 2025, with cash taxes paid rising to $52.7M from $38.0M.

The composition changed materially. AFS fixed maturities went from $597.3M to $1,091.0M while cash fell from $1,210.1M to $872.3M — a deliberate duration extension into a 4.6-year portfolio (76% AA-band, 17% BBB). The portfolio already carries a $12.7M net unrealized loss and AOCI swung from +$1.5M to −$9.4M. HCI's own disclosed sensitivity is a $137.0M fair-value reduction on a +300bp shock, equal to 12.7% of stockholders' equity. This is a live exposure to book value that did not exist twelve months ago. It is the price paid for net investment income of $18.9M, up 15% year over year.

Reserves fell from $576.5M to $559.0M: $54.4M of reduction on 2025-and-prior non-cat years, $46.5M on catastrophe reserves (Ian, Helene, Milton), offset by $83.4M established for the 2026 year. These are balance movements running off predominantly through payment; the development exhibit shows no prior-period development recognized in incurred losses in either half-year. It would be wrong to describe this as $100.9M of favorable releases. What does warrant attention is composition: of the $559.0M, $506.9M (90.7%) is IBNR and only $52.1M is case reserves, of which $42.6M relates to prior accident years. That leaves roughly $9.5M of current-year case reserves against $647M of half-year gross premium. The benign reading is a fast-paying, low-severity, largely unlitigated post-reform book. The less benign reading is that a reserve balance which is 91% actuarial estimate is nearly untestable from outside, and small assumption changes move earnings materially. Reinsurance recoverable on unpaid losses is $229.1M, with 75.5% of total recoverables concentrated in six reinsurers including the Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund.

### Earnings surprise pattern is mostly a weather artifact

Four consecutive beats: +10.1% (Q2 2026), +4.8% (Q1 2026), +70.0% (Q4 2025), +114.2% (Q3 2025). The two enormous beats were third- and fourth-quarter prints where analysts modeled a catastrophe load that did not materialize. That is a weather outcome, not evidence of conservative guidance or superior execution, and it should not be extrapolated. Note that the current Q3 2026 consensus of $2.59 against Q2 actual of $5.60 again embeds a cat provision — which is why full-year consensus of $18.45 sits well below the cat-free run rate. Financial Health scores 7.0: minimal leverage, high cash generation, holdco self-sufficiency and genuine buyback capacity, discounted for the new rate exposure to book, the IBNR-heavy reserve base and the recoverable concentration.

This is where the reported numbers and the shareholder-attributable numbers diverge.

| **Reciprocals subtotal** | **$43.7M** | **$19.0M** | **+129%** |

The income statement corroborates the decomposition: NCI took $20.7M of first-half net income versus $8.7M a year earlier and $21.4M for all of FY2025. In six months, minority interests absorbed as much profit as in the entire prior year.

This is not necessarily bad structure. Growing through reciprocal exchanges lets HCI collect attorney-in-fact management fees, Exzeo platform billings and 9% interest on $50M of surplus notes without HCI shareholders bearing the underlying catastrophe risk — capital-efficient, and arguably the right response to a softening market. But the segment disclosure does not isolate the management-fee stream, so the shareholder-attributable economics of exchange growth cannot be sized. What can be said is that consolidated top-line growth of 7.2% materially overstates growth in earnings attributable to HCI shareholders.

The policy count is the harder datapoint. Sequentially: ~313,400 (12/31/2025) → ~290,100 (6/30/2026), −7.4%. Zero Citizens assumptions in the half against 13,917 in 1H 2025 and 60,820 across all of 2025. Underlying attrition was therefore roughly 23,300 policies with no takeout offset, against roughly 15,100 the prior year with a large offset. GPE is an earned, lagging measure; the +6.0% GPE growth will decelerate mechanically over the next two to three quarters unless new takeouts or a new distribution channel arrive. CORE's 54% GPE decline suggests part of the reduction is deliberate condo-exposure shedding, and Citizens assumptions are lumpy enough that a single large second-half tranche could reverse the trend — Citizens officials themselves have said an upturn in their count is "bound to occur." Both readings fit the data.

The offsets to flat premium: net investment income compounding on a $1.27B portfolio; the reinsurance saving; a 4.02% reduction in share count in six months; and Exzeo's non-affiliate revenue growing from $2.9M to $11.3M year over year, nearly 4x, with management attributing it to new insurance-carrier customers on the platform. Exzeo also launched "Exzeo Ventures" for AI-native products. That third-party ramp is the one genuine new growth vector, and at $11.3M of half-year revenue it is not yet material.

Estimate revisions are stable: current-year consensus $18.45 (+1.0% over 30 days), next-year $18.79 (+1.7%), with near-quarter estimates down 1.5%. Next-year growth over current-year is 1.8%. There is no inflection signal here of the kind that would justify overriding valuation discipline. Growth Prospects scores 5.5 — per-share growth is real via buyback, investment income and reinsurance cost, but organic unit growth in the businesses shareholders own has stopped.

Peer medians in the small-cap P&C set are 14.6x P/E and 1.7% yield (STC 14.6x, PRA 19.6x, UFCS 9.6x, PLMR 16.1x, AGO 8.7x). HCI's 7.3-7.8x trailing multiple is the cheapest in that cohort. The question is whether the discount is a mispricing.

Two adjustments matter. First, trailing earnings reflect two consecutive seasons with zero catastrophe losses. The five-year net income series from the proxy — 2021 $7.2M, 2022 −$54.6M, 2023 $89.3M, 2024 $127.6M, 2025 $320.4M — is violently cyclical, with a five-year mean of $98.0M. That sample is too small to be a base rate, and Florida's 2022-23 litigation reforms (SB 2-A, HB 837) genuinely lowered attritional loss costs mid-sample, so pre-2023 years understate current normalized earning power. Still, a 2024-analog multi-landfall season took after-NCI diluted EPS to $8.89, which would put the stock at roughly 20x.

Second, the consensus number is already cat-loaded. Q3 2026 consensus of $2.59 against a Q2 actual of $5.60 reflects an expected storm provision; full-year $18.45 versus a $22.10 cat-free annualization of the first half is the same adjustment. Consensus of $18.45-18.79 is therefore close to a defensible normalized figure, and it converges with an independently constructed normalization — blending a cat-free year near $22-23 with a 2024-analog near $9-10 at roughly 65/35 gives about $17.80, and adding a partial year of the claimed reinsurance saving lifts it toward $19-20. Call normalized power $18.50, with the acknowledgement that the weighting is judgmental and the 2024 analog understates today's larger premium base while overstating today's reinsurance cost.

At $180.89 the stock is 9.8x normalized $18.50. For a business with 28% cat-free ROE, 6.3% debt/equity, a 4%-per-half share shrink and demonstrated expense advantage, 11x normalized is defensible: **$203 base case, +12.2%**. Cross-check on book: 2.09x current book, or roughly 2.15x a mid-2027 book near $95 if 2026 is cat-light after dividends and buybacks. Florida-concentrated specialists have historically traded 1.0-1.5x book; HCI's 2.09x already prices most of the ROE premium.

The cheap multiple is doing less work than it appears. Reward for cheapness requires a specific reason the market is wrong, and here the two candidate catalysts — the >$10M-per-quarter reinsurance saving and the reported GEICO product launch — are both unverified from a single secondary source, with the GEICO arrangement's structure (who underwrites, who distributes, who bears risk, which states, whether the subject is insurance capacity or Exzeo technology) entirely unestablished. A genuine national distribution channel would be thesis-changing given flat owned-carrier premium; a narrow fronting or technology arrangement would not.

One valuation trap to avoid: the Exzeo stake cannot be added to a separately valued insurance business. Exzeo bills HCI's own carriers for roughly 90% of its revenue, and those fees eliminate on consolidation. At a stale, low-tier June quote of $13.43, XZO's market cap is about $1.21B and HCI's 83.1% stake about $1.01B against HCI's own $2.26B — which establishes only that the market pays roughly 13x for the fee stream and roughly 7-8x for the consolidated whole. The correct economic figure is consolidated after-NCI earnings.

Analyst targets range $225-$255 (mean $238.33), 24-41% above spot, with one broker raising to $265 [unverified — Phase 1 target high is $255; the $265 figure is a single secondary-aggregator datapoint]. The coverage universe is three to five analysts and the mean rests on cat-free extrapolation. On an inflection stock at a reasonable multiple those targets would be a floor; here estimate revisions are flat and the multiple discount is structurally warranted, so they are directional support rather than confirmation. Valuation scores 6.0: a real discount, largely justified, with the entry point poor relative to the seasonal risk window.

## Competitive Landscape

HCI's advantages are measurable. Q2 gross loss ratio of 22.2% and gross expense load of 19.8% of GPE ($63.5M of acquisition, G&A and other operating expense) produce a gross combined ratio near 42% — outlier-good for personal property. The technology stack allows granular Florida risk selection, and the company has a decade-plus record of assuming Citizens policies.

The trajectory is the concern. Florida is in the second phase of its reform cycle: SB 2-A eliminated one-way attorney fee shifting and AOB abuse; HB 837 tightened comparative negligence. Loss costs fell, incumbents earned outsized returns for two seasons, and new capital entered. The consequences now visible: dozens of rate-decrease filings, Security First cutting 5.6% on average as part of a two-year series, multiple independent reports that competition has returned, and Citizens' policy count at a record low in June 2026. HCPCI and TypTap premium is flat, non-Florida premium is shrinking 4.3%, and units fell 7.4% in six months. That is what a softening cycle looks like at the individual-carrier level.

HCI's response has been rational: push growth into vehicles where third-party subscriber capital bears the risk, harvest cheaper reinsurance, and shrink the share count. None of those widen the moat. The one genuine moat-extension is Exzeo's third-party carrier adoption, which is growing fast off $11.3M of half-year external revenue. Competitive Position scores 5.2 — a strong absolute cost position with a stable-to-deteriorating trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-state catastrophe concentration.** 91.0% of Q2 GPE from Florida; substantially all assets in Florida. This report is published at the front of the peak Atlantic window (August-October). One landfall in a populated corridor dominates any single year's result. The 2022 comparison (−$54.6M net loss) is instructive but not directly transferable: equity is roughly five times the 2022 level, so an Ian-scale event is a book-value event rather than a solvency event.

**Undisclosed 2026-27 reinsurance terms.** Retentions, first-event limit, aggregate limit, reinstatement provisions and FHCF participation are not in the Q2 filing. Combined with the Fortex licensing language about selectively retaining risk, the possibility that part of the ceded-premium reduction is retained rather than repriced cannot be excluded. This is the single largest analytical gap in the file and directly limits confidence in downside scenarios.

**Reserve estimation.** 90.7% IBNR, with roughly $9.5M of current-year case reserves against $647M of half-year gross premium. Small IBNR assumption changes move earnings materially and are not externally testable.

**Reinsurance collectibility.** 75.5% of recoverables in six counterparties, one of them the FHCF; $229.1M recoverable on unpaid losses.

**Rate regulation and pricing power.** Florida OIR must approve filings. A market where peers cut 5-6% is a market where HCI's pricing power erodes.

**Growth dependence on Citizens depopulation, now stalled.** Zero assumptions in 1H 2026.

**Interest-rate exposure to book value.** −$137.0M of AFS fair value on a +300bp shock, 12.7% of equity. Newly material after the 1H repositioning.

**Governance-structural.** The CEO holds executive roles at both HCI and its 83.1%-owned public subsidiary (employment agreement with Exzeo Group dated December 9, 2025), while roughly 90% of Exzeo's revenue is billed to HCI's carriers — an inherent transfer-pricing exposure that determines how consolidated profit splits between HCI shareholders and Exzeo's 16.9% minority. This is conventional for a founder-led carve-out and implies nothing improper; the structural exposure is worth naming. Consolidated gross premiums earned is one of four compensation performance measures, which rewards reciprocal-exchange premium that does not accrue to HCI shareholders. 200,000 unvested restricted shares carry a $200-for-30-consecutive-days price gate with no operating or relative condition — about 11% above the official close. Related-party flows with Oxbridge Re (director Jay Madhu is its chairman/CEO) are immaterial in size: $930K of ceded premium in the 2025-26 treaty year, replaced by $408K of transaction-service fees in 2026-27.

**Unverified catalysts.** The GEICO product and the >$10M-per-quarter reinsurance saving both rest on a single secondary headline.

Risk Factors scores 4.5. A substantial portion of the catastrophe risk is priced — that is why the stock trades at 7-8x cat-free earnings — but the retained-risk question, the undisclosed program terms, the IBNR-heavy reserve base and the newly created rate sensitivity to book are not obviously in the price, and the timing window is immediate.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing the seasonal risk. Near-term ATM IV is 28.6% against 32.5% one-year realized (a −3.9% spread, 2nd percentile within the current chain), having collapsed from 41.5% on July 25 after the print. The put/call volume ratio is 0.09 and open-interest ratio 0.06 — effectively no put demand — with a −13.0% call-heavy skew and a contango term structure (28.6% at 13 days, 30.8% at 41 days, 33.9% at 132 days). The December tenor prices a ±15.1% move, only modestly above the near-dated ±4.4%.

For a Florida-concentrated property writer entering peak hurricane season, 28.6% near-dated implied volatility is inexpensive optionality. The practical implication for existing holders is that September-October downside protection is cheap relative to realized volatility, and the term structure does not require paying up for the window that matters. Short interest is 4.6% of float with 2.5 days to cover — uncrowded, with no meaningful squeeze dynamic in either direction. The absence of put demand is a complacency signal rather than a confirmation of the bull case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $255 (+41.0%)

A third consecutive cat-free Atlantic season lets 2026 land near $23 of after-NCI diluted EPS, with the June 1 reinsurance program annualizing at the reported $10M-plus per quarter and the 2026-27 retentions confirmed unchanged. The GEICO arrangement is verified as a genuine distribution channel for TypTap or HCPCI capacity, restoring owned-carrier premium growth and resolving the policy-attrition problem. Citizens' count turns higher and the takeout pipeline reopens in 2H 2026 or 2027. Exzeo's non-affiliate revenue continues compounding from $11.3M, making the technology segment a genuinely independent asset. Book value per share reaches the high $90s to low $100s, and 11x $23 gives $253. Consensus targets ($225-$255, mean $238) sit in this band, and the $80M buyback executed at an average $158.63 — 12.3% below the current close — demonstrates that management will support the stock at lower prices.

### Bear Case — $138 (−23.7%)

A Helene/Milton-analog season takes after-NCI EPS to roughly $9-10 and book value per share to the high $70s to low $80s after cat losses and any further AOCI deterioration on the $1.09B bond portfolio. If part of the cession-rate reduction reflects risk retained in Fortex or Claddaugh rather than a price win, the loss is larger than the historical structure implies. The market then applies roughly 1.7x a depressed book, giving $135-140. An alternative bear path requires no storm at all: unit attrition continues at the 1H pace, Citizens takeouts stay at zero, competitors keep filing decreases, HCI-owned premium declines outright, and the multiple compresses from 7.8x to 6.5x trailing on a shrinking base — arithmetically similar territory. Secondary risks: a +200-300bp long-end move driving a $90-137M AFS mark against equity, and any Demotech or AM Best downgrade, which would impair mortgagee acceptance of HCI policies.

### Base Case — $203 (+12.2%)

Normalized after-NCI EPS of $18.50 — consistent with both a 65/35 weather blend and current consensus of $18.45-18.79 — at 11x. Book value compounds toward the mid-$90s; the multiple stays at a structurally justified discount to the 14.6x peer median because 91% of premium remains in one state and unit growth is absent. Probability-weighting 45% base, 25% bull, 30% bear yields roughly +8.4% price return plus 0.9% of dividend, about +9% total. That is a fair, not compelling, expected return, and it is earned across a distribution whose left tail resolves within 90 days on a variable no analyst can forecast.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (hold rationale: uncertain).** Existing holders are adequately compensated at 9.8x normalized earnings with a 28% cat-free ROE and management repurchasing stock below the current price. New capital has no urgent entry signal: roughly 9% probability-weighted return against a 24% bear case, with the decisive variable (Atlantic season) resolving between now and November and three thesis planks (GEICO structure, 2026-27 retentions, whether policy attrition reverses) unverified.

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the substantive reassessment at the Q3 2026 print in early November, which will report both storm experience and whether any Citizens tranche was assumed in the second half.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Verified distribution channel.** An issuer document or 8-K establishing the GEICO arrangement's structure — specifically that HCI or TypTap underwrites policies distributed at scale — accompanied by owned-carrier GWP growth above 5% year over year. This would resolve the central growth problem.
2. **Policy count stabilizes or reverses.** Policies in force at 9/30/2026 at or above ~290,100, ideally with a disclosed Citizens assumption. Combined with unchanged average premium per policy, this would remove the attrition thesis.
3. **Price.** A decline to $150-158, which is the level at which management itself bought $80M of stock, would take the base case to roughly +28-35% and restore the margin of safety. A post-season entry near $155 with reinsurance terms disclosed would clear the BUY bar.

## Conclusion

HCI is a well-run, minimally levered insurer earning a 28% return on equity in a Florida market that its own state's litigation reforms made temporarily very profitable, trading at 7.3-7.8x trailing earnings and 2.09x book. The Q2 print was good on every reported line. It was also the print that documented the problem: the two carriers HCI shareholders own grew premium 0.35%, policies in force fell 7.4% in six months with zero Citizens takeouts, noncontrolling interests took $20.7M of first-half profit against $8.7M a year earlier, and the entire consolidated growth rate came from exchanges whose underwriting profit belongs to policyholder-subscribers. Capital allocation has been genuinely good — 504,330 shares repurchased at an average $158.63, a 4.02% share-count reduction, debt/equity at 6.3% — and that is the most credible signal in the file about management's own view of value.

The score moves to 5.8 from 6.3, continuing a three-cycle decline, and the recommendation moves off four consecutive BUYs to HOLD. That reflects new filing content rather than price action: the stock is roughly flat versus the prior review, so the change is driven by the stalled owned-carrier growth, the sequential policy decline, the newly created interest-rate exposure to book, and the fact that the company now sits at the front of peak Atlantic season with its reinsurance retentions undisclosed. Base $203, bull $255, bear $138. The upside is real and the balance sheet can absorb a bad season; the entry point does not compensate for a 24% left tail that resolves on weather within 90 days. Own it if you own it. Add at $150-158, or after the season with the program terms and the distribution arrangement verified.
