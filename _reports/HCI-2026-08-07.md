## Executive Summary

HCI delivered its fourth consecutive earnings beat in Q2 2026: diluted EPS $5.60 against roughly $5.09 consensus (+10.1%), revenue $246.7M (+11.1%), pre-tax income $111.0M (+17.6%), and book value per share compounded 47.9% year over year to $86.60. The balance sheet is the strongest in the company's history: total debt of $67.5M against $1,079.9M of equity (6.3% debt/equity), $2.15B of cash and investments, and net premium-to-surplus near 0.8x. On trailing earnings of roughly $23.30 per diluted share the stock is 7.8x; on consensus 2026 EPS of $18.45 it is 9.8x, against a property-casualty peer median of 14.6x.

The problem is what sits underneath those figures. The six-month net combined ratio was 59.2% versus 59.0% a year earlier: net loss ratio 31.0% versus 30.9%, net expense ratio 28.2% versus 28.1%. Underwriting margin was flat. The 8.4% growth in net income after noncontrolling interests came from four non-underwriting sources: net investment income (+$6.0M), Exzeo third-party revenue (+$5.1M), lower interest expense (+$5.1M following retirement of the convertible notes), and a 4.0% reduction in share count. Three of those four are one-time step changes, not compounding improvements. The owned insurance franchise has stopped growing. Homeowners Choice and TypTap together wrote $338.7M of gross premium in Q2 versus $337.5M, or 0.35% growth. All of the reported +7.2% consolidated premium growth came from Condo Owners Reciprocal Exchange and Tailrow, reciprocal exchanges in which HCI holds no equity and whose underwriting profit is attributed to noncontrolling interests. NCI's share of consolidated net income rose from 6.0% to 12.3% of the total in the half.

Two developments during the quarter changed the risk profile in ways the disclosure does not yet let an investor size. The cede ratio fell 220bp to 31.7% of gross premiums earned, which the company attributes to lower 2026-27 catastrophe program costs, and in the same period a new Cayman-domiciled captive, Fortex Reinsurance SPC, was licensed with the stated purpose of selectively retaining risk to reduce third-party reinsurance cost. The 2026-27 first-event retention and total program limit are not disclosed in the filings we reviewed. Entering the mid-August-to-mid-October peak of the Atlantic season, that is the single most consequential unquantified input, and it determines whether the margin improvement is a price concession from a softening reinsurance market (free) or a transfer of tail risk onto HCI's own balance sheet (levered). We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

HCI is a Tampa-based holding company operating in 13 states around a Florida homeowners insurance core. Four entities write premium: Homeowners Choice ($228.2M Q2 GWP, +0.5%), TypTap Insurance Company ($110.5M, +0.1%), Condo Owners Reciprocal Exchange ($10.8M, −21.6%) and Tailrow Reciprocal Exchange ($32.8M, +529%). Two captive reinsurers sit alongside, plus a claims manager, a commercial real estate arm, and Exzeo Group (NYSE: XZO), the insurance technology and MGA subsidiary that IPO'd 8.0M primary shares at $21.00 in November 2025 for $168.0M gross ($156.2M net into Exzeo). HCI owned 83.15% of Exzeo (75.0M of 90.2M shares) at the most recent disclosure after Exzeo repurchased 834,250 of its own shares for $12.0M at roughly $14.38 average through 7/8/26.

The structure requires care in reading. Exzeo's FY2025 revenue of $217.0M at a 48.8% operating margin is almost entirely intercompany; its own 10-K states that revenue from its two non-HCI-affiliated customers "was immaterial in 2025." Those fees are a cost to HCI's carriers and are eliminated in consolidation. A sum-of-the-parts that adds HCI's insurance book value to the market value of the Exzeo stake double-counts. What the IPO genuinely created is $156M of net cash (83% attributable to HCI), a public reference price, and acquisition currency, against permanent cession of 16.85% of the after-tax profit on fees HCI's own carriers pay, roughly $14-15M annually, for $156M of proceeds (about 10x).

The reciprocal exchanges present the mirror image. HCI consolidates CORE and Tailrow as variable interest entities because its subsidiaries hold the attorney-in-fact agreements, but it holds no equity; its participation is through management and platform fees. It gets none of their underwriting upside. Whether it bears downside beyond the $93.2M NCI carrying value, through surplus notes, capital-maintenance commitments, or the practical necessity of recapitalizing an exchange it manages, is not established in the filings we reviewed.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025.** Gross premiums earned $320.8M versus $302.6M (+6.0%); premiums ceded $101.8M versus $102.5M, cutting the cede ratio to 31.7% from 33.9%; net premiums earned $219.0M versus $200.1M (+9.4%). Net investment income $18.9M versus $16.4M. "Other" revenue, which survives intercompany elimination and therefore represents genuine third-party Exzeo business, was $5.1M versus $2.6M. Losses and LAE $71.1M versus $64.5M, lifting the gross loss ratio slightly to 22.2% from 21.3%. Interest expense $1.08M versus $3.74M. Pre-tax income $111.0M versus $94.4M; net income after NCI $73.8M versus $66.2M (+11.5%); diluted EPS $5.60 versus $5.18.

**The margin did not improve.** Aggregating the six-month expense lines (losses and LAE $136.7M, policy acquisition $64.1M, G&A personnel $46.3M, other operating $14.1M) against $441.2M of net premiums earned gives a 59.2% net combined ratio, versus 59.0% on the comparable 2025 basis. Both halves carry zero catastrophe load; the Atlantic season runs June through November. A 59% first-half combined ratio is not a full-year proxy in either direction.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $2,648.5M at 6/30/26 versus $2,528.9M at year-end. Fixed-maturity available-for-sale securities rose to $1,091.0M from $597.3M while cash fell to $872.3M from $1,210.1M, a deliberate redeployment into duration that carries a $12.7M gross unrealized loss (1.15% of $1,103.8M amortized cost) and swung AOCI from +$1.5M to −$9.4M. That is small and unalarming for an AFS portfolio held against long-dated reserves, but it is a new sensitivity. Six-month net investment income of $36.2M on average cash-plus-investments near $2,074M implies an annualized yield of roughly 3.5%, low against available short rates, consistent with a portfolio still heavily weighted to liquidity for catastrophe funding, and a plausible continuing tailwind as redeployment proceeds.

Leverage is minimal: $36.0M revolver plus $31.5M long-term debt against $1,079.9M of equity. The quarterly interest expense delta annualizes to roughly $10.6M of pre-tax relief, though that is a run-rate sensitivity rather than a forecast since the revolver balance can change. Loss and LAE reserves fell to $559.0M from $576.5M, with reinsurance recoverable on unpaid losses down to $229.1M from $262.0M.

**Capital allocation.** The $80M authorization announced 3/3/26 was completed 7/17/26: 504,330 shares at roughly $158.6 average, below the current price, and share count fell 4.0% in the half. No successor authorization has been disclosed. The dividend is $0.40 quarterly ($1.60 annualized, 0.88% yield at $180.89), a low payout appropriate for a catastrophe-exposed writer. Additional paid-in capital declined $87.3M against a $136.9M rise in retained earnings, consistent with buybacks plus the charge for repurchasing Exzeo noncontrolling interests, less dividends.

**Trailing earnings and the denominator problem.** FY2025 net income after NCI derives to approximately $299M, cross-checked two ways (retained-earnings roll-forward, and the proxy's $320.4M consolidated figure less implied NCI). Trailing twelve-month net income after NCI is therefore roughly $310.4M, or about $23.30 per diluted share after the two-class allocation to participating unvested shares, which is 7.8x at $180.89. But the proxy's five-year net income series makes the denominator's character plain: 2021 $7.2M, 2022 −$54.6M (Ian), 2023 $89.3M, 2024 $127.6M (Debby, Helene, Milton), 2025 $320.4M (no landfall). FY2024 after NCI was roughly $120M, or approximately $9 per share on the then-larger share count, about 20x the current price. HCI trades at 7.8x a no-storm year, roughly 20x a three-storm year, and at a loss multiple in a major-landfall year.

Consensus already reflects this. FY2026 consensus of $18.45 against first-half actual diluted EPS of $11.05 implies second-half earnings near $7.40, with Q3 at $2.59; analysts are embedding a meaningful catastrophe load. That matters for how much of the "cheapness" is real.

## Growth Analysis

The reported figures and the economics attributable to HCI shareholders diverge materially.

On gross written premium, consolidated growth of +7.2% decomposes into Homeowners Choice plus TypTap at +0.35% and the reciprocal exchanges at +130% ($43.7M versus $19.0M, rising from 5.3% to 11.4% of total GWP). On gross premiums *earned*, TypTap actually contracted 0.9% and CORE fell 53.7%. Reciprocal underwriting profit is attributed to noncontrolling interests. HCI participates through fees.

Corroborating evidence from Exzeo's own 10-K is harder to explain away as mix: gross dollar retention fell from 95.1% in 2024 to 87.2% in 2025, a 790bp deterioration in policyholder retention on the platform, and premium per managed policy fell 14.3% from $5,159 to $4,423. Exzeo attributes lower commission rates on its single commission-based carrier to Florida "competitive market dynamics." Geographic concentration worsened, not improved: Exzeo-managed premium in Florida rose from 84.8% to 91.3%. The entity-mix shift is a change in the character of growth without any accompanying diversification. The company's release states average premium per policy for its own carriers "remained relatively consistent," which sits in tension with the platform-wide metric and suggests mix is doing real work; we cannot adjudicate between the two from the available disclosure.

The Citizens depopulation channel that drove much of the 2024-25 policy growth appears to have gone quiet, with no takeout policies assumed in the first half of 2026 against roughly 13,900 a year earlier. We flag this as requiring re-verification, but if accurate it removes the mechanical policy-count growth that flattered prior years.

Against that, three genuine growth vectors exist, all with incomplete disclosure. First, reinsurance cost savings from the June 1 program: if the externally reported ">$10M per quarter" figure is accurate and sustained, $40M annual pre-tax at the observed 25.3% effective rate is $29.9M after tax, roughly $2.36 per diluted share, about 10% of no-storm EPS. That figure comes from a news headline whose body we could not access and is not verified against a company transcript or filing. Second, a possible GEICO distribution arrangement. Correctly characterized, this would make GEICO a distribution agent earning commission on a product underwritten by an HCI carrier, not a customer, underwriter or risk-bearer. The underwriting entity, states, commission economics, exclusivity and volume are all unknown. If real and scaled, it addresses exactly the problem the segment data exposes. Third, Exzeo third-party revenue: consolidated "Other" revenue of $8.2M for the half versus $3.0M is genuine 2.7x growth, from a base under 4% of Exzeo's FY2025 revenue.

Consensus captures the muted picture: FY2027 at $18.79 is 1.8% above FY2026's $18.45. Thirty-day revisions are +1.0% and +1.7% for the two years, with quarterly estimates trimmed 1.5%. **There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here.** The aggressive ninety-day revision pattern that would argue for aggressive positioning is absent (ninety-day revision history is not available in the data), and the earnings-beat magnitudes have collapsed from 114.2% and 70.0% in the second half of 2025 to 4.8% and 10.1% in 2026, consistent with the benign-weather tailwind now being fully embedded in estimates.

The discount is not an anomaly to be arbitraged; it prices earnings volatility that the peer set does not carry to the same degree.

The more useful exercise is normalized earnings power. Annualizing first-half net premiums earned gives roughly $882M for 2026. Holding the net expense ratio at 28.2% and the non-catastrophe net loss ratio at 31%, a 12-point annual net catastrophe load produces a 71% combined ratio and $256M of underwriting profit; adding roughly $75-80M of net investment income, deducting interest and 25% tax, and allocating roughly 12% to noncontrolling interests yields approximately $16.70 per diluted share, or 10.8x at the current price. That is genuinely below the peer median and supports the case that HCI is not expensive.

The sensitivity that governs the conclusion is the catastrophe load itself. At an 18-20 point load, which is where the number goes if the 2026-27 program materially raised first-event retention or added captive quota-share participation, normalized EPS falls to roughly $12-13 and the multiple becomes 14x, in line with peers and offering no margin of safety. The retention is undisclosed. A valuation whose fair-value estimate spans 10.8x to 14x on an input the company has not published does not support a high conviction score, however attractive the headline multiple looks.

On book value, 2.09x is a full multiple for a P&C insurer, justified only by return on equity. FY2025 delivered roughly 28% ROE against average equity near $1.0B; a normalized 16-18% ROE against an 8-9% cost of equity supports something in the 1.9-2.2x range. The stock is therefore approximately fairly valued on book and modestly cheap on normalized earnings.

Analyst coverage is thin (five ratings: one strong buy, three buy, one hold) but uniformly above the market: low target $225, high $255, mean $238.33, median $235, with a secondary source reporting Citizens raising to $265. That is 24% to 47% above the current price. On a stock at under 10x forward earnings with stable-to-rising estimates, we would ordinarily treat consensus targets as a lagging floor rather than a ceiling. Here we discount them somewhat for the same reason we discount the multiple: they are being computed off a no-storm run rate, and none of the published notes we could access address the retention question.

## Competitive Landscape

The Florida homeowners market that HCI dominates has completed a full cycle: mass insolvencies and withdrawals through 2022, Hurricane Ian, the 2022-23 tort reforms that curtailed one-way fee shifting and assignment-of-benefits abuse, then capital re-entry and Citizens depopulation. The filings now show the next phase: competition.

The evidence is specific rather than atmospheric. Platform gross dollar retention fell 790bp. Premium per managed policy fell 14.3%. Exzeo attributes lower commission rates to Florida competitive dynamics. Owned-carrier gross written premium grew 0.35%. Reinsurance costs fell, which the company attributes to the new program, a market-wide dynamic that accrues to every Florida writer and will be partially competed away through rate. The Citizens takeout channel appears dormant. The trajectory of HCI's competitive position, which is what matters for scoring, is deteriorating, and this is the fourth consecutive reporting period in which that has been the direction.

What genuinely differentiates the company: a vertically integrated technology and MGA stack that lets it stand up new premium vehicles (Tailrow went from $5.2M to $32.8M of quarterly GWP in a year) quickly and with policyholder-member capital rather than its own; an exceptionally liquid, low-leverage balance sheet that permits writing through a market others exit; and low gross loss ratios in benign years (21-22%). Those are real advantages. They are also advantages in capacity deployment, which is worth less when the constraint on growth shifts from capacity to price.

On the technology side, Exzeo's structural handicap is acknowledged in its own filing: it must persuade rival carriers to route premium and data through a platform majority-owned by a Florida homeowners underwriter, against established core-systems vendors, MGUs and internal build teams, many with "greater financial and other resources, greater name recognition, and longer operating histories." Its stated beachhead, de novo carriers and mid-sized homeowners writers avoiding upfront technology cost, is sensible and narrow.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Catastrophe retention, undisclosed, entering peak season.** The 2026-27 first-event retention and total program limit are not established in the filings we reviewed. The cede ratio fell in the same period a Cayman captive was licensed for the express purpose of retaining risk to reduce third-party reinsurance cost, and the company now describes itself as operating "two captive reinsurers." The two explanations, market-price softening versus retained tail, have opposite implications and cannot be separated from public disclosure. This exposure is measurable only in a landfall year, and the seasonal window opens now.

**2. Reserves are approximately 90% IBNR with no development signal.** Loss and LAE reserves of $559.0M appear to comprise roughly $507M of IBNR against only ~$52M of case reserves, and the development table showed zero incurred loss attributable to prior accident periods in both six-month columns, with prior-period reserves declining roughly $101M purely through settlement. Our reconciliation confirms internal consistency (net paid on prior periods ~$67.9M plus the $32.9M decline in reinsurance recoverable ≈ $100.8M). The most likely readings are presentational: favorable and adverse movements netting to approximately zero, or a practice of releasing redundancy only as claims close. None implies impropriety. It does mean the table that exists to signal reserve adequacy provides none, and that the reserve is substantially a model output. Weight the actual loss history, which includes a −$54.6M year, above the disclosed development record. Both the IBNR share and the zero-development figures require re-verification.

**3. Reciprocal exchange contingent obligations.** HCI receives none of CORE's or Tailrow's underwriting profit. Whether it bears their downside beyond the $93.2M NCI carrying value, through surplus notes, capital-maintenance commitments, or practical necessity, is unresolved. Tailrow's premium is growing 5x. Until the support structure is disclosed, that growth should be read as a fee stream with an undetermined contingent liability attached.

**4. Governance complexity.** The CEO chairs and runs both HCI and Exzeo, sitting on both sides of the transfer-pricing negotiation between carriers that pay fees and an entity 16.85% owned by outside shareholders. Gross Premiums Earned is a disclosed performance measure, and it can be advanced by growing reciprocal premium whose underwriting profit belongs entirely to noncontrolling interests, a partial misalignment against the actual growth strategy, though Net Income, ROE and Relative TSR are also measures and the GPE weighting is unknown. Related-party dealings with Oxbridge Re include roughly $0.4M of 2026 fees for a treaty year in which it apparently did not participate as a reinsurer; immaterial in size, but the kind of item warranting plain-English explanation. Key-person concentration is material: NEO average total compensation of $935K against $8.2M for the CEO.

**5. Deposit-method reinsurance accounting.** The Q2 10-Q discloses a deposit-method policy for contracts that do not transfer insurance risk, with roughly $11.3M of related payments in financing activities. Deposit accounting is required GAAP treatment when a contract fails risk-transfer testing and is not itself a red flag, but a contract failing risk transfer provides no economic protection in a large loss. Which treaties are involved, and whether they relate to the captives, requires verification.

**6. Equity overhang.** Approximately 200,000 unvested HCI restricted shares carry a $200-for-30-consecutive-trading-days condition, within roughly 11% of the current close. Income allocated to participating securities of $6.186M (4.20% of after-NCI income) implies roughly 540K participating unvested shares, bringing a fully-loaded count near 13.2M, about 4.3% above reported diluted. Separately, 6,350,000 fully vested Exzeo options at a $23.00 weighted-average strike equal 7.04% of Exzeo's shares; full exercise would cut HCI's ownership from 83.15% to 77.68%, a 5.47 percentage-point reduction. Both conditions are deeply out of the money at Exzeo's ~$14 reference price, and 6.0M of the options require HCI board approval to exercise, a conditional scenario, not current dilution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 28.7% sits below 32.5% trailing realized (a −3.8% spread) at the 2nd percentile of the current chain, with a call-heavy skew of −13.0%, a put/call volume ratio of 0.09, and a contango term structure rising to 33.9% at six months. The nearest expiry prices a ±4.4% move; the December tenor prices ±15.1%.

Read plainly: the options market is pricing essentially no near-term tail risk in a Florida homeowners writer during the second week of August, with positioning tilted toward calls. The longer-dated tenor captures more of the season. Two implications. First, this is complacency at the wrong seasonal moment, and it corroborates the view that the retention question is not priced, supporting a low risk-factors score. Second, for holders who want to keep exposure through the season, protection is cheap in absolute and relative terms; buying September or December puts against a position costs less than realized volatility has warranted. Short interest of 4.6% of float at 2.5 days to cover confirms the bearish case is uncrowded, which cuts both ways: more room to fall, but also no informed short consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $252 (+39%)

A quiet 2026 season delivers a second consecutive no-landfall year. The June 1 reinsurance program's cost reduction, worth roughly $2.36 per share after tax if the reported $10M-per-quarter figure holds, flows through the second half at full weight rather than the one month it contributed in Q2, so second-half combined ratios improve mechanically. Investment income keeps building as the remaining $872M of cash redeploys from a 3.5% blended yield toward market rates. The GEICO distribution arrangement proves real and material, restoring organic policy growth at the owned carriers and answering the one criticism the segment data cannot deflect. Exzeo third-party revenue continues compounding from $8.2M in the half toward a base where the platform's software-like margins are earned from unaffiliated carriers rather than transfer pricing. A successor buyback authorization retires another 4% of shares below intrinsic value. FY2027 EPS reaches $22 and the multiple expands to 11.5x as the market accepts a lower-volatility, fee-weighted earnings mix. Analyst targets ($225-$265) converge toward realization.

### Bear Case — $128 (−29%)

A major Florida landfall arrives in the 2026 season, and the reinsurance savings prove to have been purchased with retention rather than granted by price. Losses breach a higher first-event retention, the captives absorb a quota-share layer, and a reciprocal exchange's surplus is impaired, obliging HCI either to absorb GAAP losses beyond the $93.2M NCI carrying value or to recapitalize an exchange it manages for reputational and regulatory reasons. The 90% IBNR reserve, with no independent development signal, requires strengthening as claims emerge. Book value per share falls from $86.60 toward $80, the multiple compresses toward 1.6x, and the stock trades below its $136.37 fifty-two-week low. Separately and more slowly, the softening Florida market continues: retention keeps eroding from 87.2%, price competition compresses the 21-22% gross loss ratio advantage, and the fee-based reciprocal model proves to be premium growth without earnings growth for common shareholders.

### Probability-weighted arithmetic

At 25% bear / 50% base / 25% bull: 0.25(−29.2%) + 0.50(+9.5%) + 0.25(+39.3%) = **approximately +7.4% expected return**. That is a positive but unexceptional risk-adjusted return, concentrated in a base case that requires benign weather, and it is being offered at the start of the seasonal window that determines the bear case. The trailing multiple is low; the margin of safety, once the retention uncertainty is priced honestly, is not wide.

1. Evidence that first-event retention rose materially in the 2026-27 program, or that captive quota-share participation increased, without an offsetting increase in disclosed program limit.
2. Owned-carrier gross written premium turning negative year over year, or Exzeo platform gross dollar retention falling below 85%.
3. Adverse prior-period reserve development appearing in the development table after four periods of zero, or a reserve strengthening charge.
4. Disclosure of surplus notes, guarantees or capital-maintenance commitments to CORE or Tailrow that materially exceed the $93.2M noncontrolling-interest carrying value.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report, or immediately upon a named storm making Florida landfall.

For existing holders, the cheapest available action is to keep the position and buy September or December puts while near-term implied volatility sits at the 2nd percentile of its own chain and below realized volatility. For new capital, wait.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Our score declines from 5.8 to 5.5, extending a trajectory that has run from 7.3 in May to here. That decline is not a reaction to price; the stock is up 29% over twelve months and rose 4.3% on the Q2 print. It reflects specific, verifiable deterioration in the two dimensions that matter most for the forward case: owned-carrier gross written premium growth of 0.35%, platform retention down 790bp to 87.2%, premium per managed policy down 14.3%, Florida concentration rising to 91.3%, and a six-month combined ratio flat at 59.2% while reported earnings grew on investment income, retired debt, third-party fees and share count. Offsetting that, financial health remains genuinely strong (6.3% debt/equity, $2.15B of liquidity, 0.8x premium-to-surplus, book value compounding 47.9%), and management executed an $80M buyback at $158.6 average, below the current price.

The valuation is the closest thing to a positive case, and it is conditional. At 9.8x FY2026 consensus, 2.09x book, and roughly 10.8x our normalized estimate against a 14.6x peer median, HCI is cheap if the normalized catastrophe load is 12 points and approximately fairly valued if it is 18-20. The company has not disclosed the input that decides which. Buying a Florida homeowners writer at the start of peak hurricane season, on a full book multiple, into an options market pricing no tail risk, while the disclosure gap on retention remains open, produces roughly +7% probability-weighted expected return with a −29% left tail. That is a fair, not a compelling, proposition. We would rather own it at $150-155, or after the Q3 filing establishes what HCI actually kept.

### Valuation and Price Discipline

- Current price: $180.89
- Fair value: $198.00
- Margin of safety: +9.5%
- Initiate/add below: $155.00
- Reduce/exit above: $230.00
- Rationale: Fair value $198 from two converging methods: 10.5x FY2027 consensus $18.79 and 2.1x estimated year-end book near $94. Both hold the current multiple; upside narrows to zero if the normalized cat load is 18-20 points.

**Thesis:** HCI earns high returns in no-storm years on a Florida homeowners book with a fortress balance sheet (6.3% debt/equity, $2.15B liquidity, 0.8x premium-to-surplus). But H1 earnings growth came from investment income, retired debt, third-party fees and buybacks, not underwriting: combined ratio flat at 59.2% and owned-carrier GWP +0.35%. Cheapness is conditional on a 12-point normalized catastrophe load the company has not disclosed.

**Variant view:** Consensus targets of $225-265 and a 7.8x trailing multiple are computed off a zero-catastrophe denominator. Our variant: reported +7.2% premium growth belongs largely to reciprocal exchanges whose underwriting profit accrues to noncontrolling interests, so headline growth overstates economics attributable to common holders.
