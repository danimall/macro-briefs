**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vornado is a New York City–concentrated office and street-retail REIT trading at $37.65, up roughly 52% from its March 2026 low of $24.57 and back within range of its 52-week high of $43.37. The recovery has been driven by three identifiable developments: the company cleared its 2026–27 refinancing wall by pushing most maturities to 2031, PENN 2 reached 90% leased after full-floor signings by Veeva and Altana AI, and a Ken Griffin/Citadel option on 350 Park Avenue created a binary positive catalyst (Vornado can either co-develop a marquee tower or put the site to KG, with $900M of a $1.2B put price accruing to Vornado, decision due July 2026). Easing rates would mechanically help high-leverage office REITs [unverified — no macro rate context in sources].

The fundamental picture underneath the rally is more sober. Reported 2025 net income of $905M is almost entirely a non-cash $803M gain on the NYU 770 Broadway sales-type lease; adjusted FFO/share was flat at $2.42 versus $2.37. The number that matters most and is easiest to miss is cash same-store NOI, which fell 5.5% in FY2025 (NY office cash NOI −6.6%) and 2.9% in Q1 2026, dragged by a 51.2% cash-NOI collapse at 555 California Street in San Francisco. Two Vornado loans are in default, including the mortgage on its own headquarters at 888 Seventh Avenue. GAAP leasing spreads remain positive (NY office +10.4% FY25, +11.7% Q1'26), which indicates demand for the best space is real and the cash drag is free-rent timing rather than pricing collapse; the gap between accrual and cash earnings is the central tension in this name.

We move to HOLD (uncertain) from the prior AVOID. The bear thesis of flat real earnings, declining cash NOI, concentration, and defaults has not changed. What has changed is price and the catalyst calendar. After a 52% move, the stock no longer offers the asymmetric short setup the prior reports implicitly leaned on, and the July 350 Park decision plus a materially de-risked balance sheet create genuine two-sided optionality. Short interest is effectively zero (0.0% of float), and the options market is call-heavy with IV at the 6th percentile of its own chain, so there is no crowd or borrow edge to a bearish trade. This is a name to monitor through the July catalyst rather than to short or to chase.

## Company Overview

Vornado operates through Vornado Realty L.P. (owns ~90.9%) and is one of the largest owners of Manhattan commercial real estate. Roughly 88–89% of NOI comes from the New York metro area and ~78% from office. Core holdings: 51 Manhattan properties (19.2M sf office, 2.3M sf street retail concentrated on Fifth/Madison Avenue and Times Square, 1,640 residential units); a 32.4% stake in Alexander's (ALX, anchored by Bloomberg at 731 Lexington); THE MART in Chicago (3.7M sf); and 70% of 555 California Street in San Francisco (1.8M sf). The strategic centerpiece is the PENN District redevelopment around Penn Station. No single tenant exceeds 10% of revenue. Chairman/CEO Steven Roth has run the company for decades and controls it via Interstate Properties (7.1% of VNO, 26% of ALX).

The competitive frame is the bifurcated office market. Trophy, transit-adjacent Class-A space is leasing while commodity space is impaired. Vornado is positioned on the favorable side, with its best assets exactly where flight-to-quality demand concentrates, but the rest of the portfolio is still absorbing the post-COVID and high-rate shock, and the company carries extreme geographic and asset-class concentration in a structurally challenged sector.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is flat.** Approximately $1.81B in 2025, essentially unchanged versus $1.79B in 2024 and $1.81B in 2023. This is a no-growth top line.

**The headline net income is an accounting illusion.** Net income jumped to $905M (2025) from $70M (2024), but $803M of that is the one-time, non-cash GAAP gain on the NYU 770 Broadway sales-type lease (NYU prepaid $935M on a 70-year master lease; VNO used proceeds to repay the $700M mortgage). The trailing P/E of 10.2x is computed off this distorted EPS of $4.20 and is not a meaningful valuation metric.

**FFO — the real cash-economics proxy — is flat to declining.** Adjusted FFO/share was $2.42 (2025) versus $2.37 (2024). Q1 2026 FFO/share fell to $0.49 from $0.67 a year earlier (the prior-year quarter contained a $76M JV gain), and Q1 2026 posted a GAAP net loss of −$0.12/share.

**Cash NOI is the most important and most overlooked number.** FY2025 same-store NOI at share was +5.4% on a GAAP basis but −5.5% on a cash basis (NY office cash NOI −6.6%), reflecting heavy free-rent concessions on newly signed leases. In Q1 2026, total same-store cash NOI was −2.9%, with 555 California Street cash NOI down 51.2% on lease rolldowns and free rent in a weak SF market. The GAAP-versus-cash divergence is the single most important signal in the filings.

**Leasing spreads are positive, which limits the bear case.** FY2025 NY office GAAP releasing spread +10.4%, cash +7.8%; Q1 2026 NY office GAAP +11.7%. The cash drag is free rent burning off on new leases, not a pricing problem, which is an important distinction for the recovery thesis.

**Leverage is heavy but typical for the model, and the near-term wall is cleared.** Consolidated debt was ~$7.2B at FY25, rising to ~$7.76B at Q1 2026; pro-rata share including JVs ~$9.6–10B. Weighted-average rate ~4.7–4.8% and climbing as low-coupon debt refinances into the 5.5–6.9% range. D/E ~1.46. The accumulated deficit of ~$3.5B reflects years of impairments. The 2026 refinancing wave was largely cleared in early 2026: both revolvers were refinanced/upsized (SOFR+1.05% and SOFR+1.16%), the $850M term loan was refinanced (SOFR+1.20%), $500M of 5.75% senior notes due 2033 were issued to repay $400M of June-2026 notes, and One Park Avenue ($525M) and 7 West 34th ($250M) were refinanced. Most near-term maturities are pushed to 2031.

**Liquidity is adequate.** $2.6B at Q1 2026 ($1.2B cash + $1.4B revolver availability).

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is a token $0.74/year, paid once annually in Q4 — effectively a deep cut maintained since 2023 (current yield ~2.0%, far below the 4.4% peer median). This is a NAV-recovery story, not an income story. Buybacks are the real signal: ~$80M in Q1 2026 at $29.08/share, plus a new $300M authorization in April 2026, which suggests management believes shares trade below NAV.

## Growth Analysis

There is no top-line growth here, and FFO is flat. The growth case rests on FFO recovery rather than expansion, and the evidence is mixed.

The positive thread is leasing momentum and embedded mark-to-market. PENN 2 reaching 90% leased validates the ~$750M redevelopment and supports the PENN District thesis. Positive GAAP leasing spreads (+10–12%) indicate that as free rent burns off, cash NOI on these leases will inflect higher over the next 12–24 months. The Park Avenue Plaza acquisition (49% of a 1.2M sf Class-A building at a $1.1B valuation, assuming a share of the $575M loan at 2.99%) adds trophy Park Avenue exposure with cheap legacy financing.

Estimate revisions appear sharply positive in percentage terms (current-year EPS revised +71.4% over 90 days, next quarter +100%), but these are off near-zero or negative bases (current-year EPS estimate of −$0.02, next quarter $0.02) tracked by a single analyst, and they reflect REIT net income (which is distorted by depreciation and one-time gains), not FFO. They do not constitute the inflection signal that the +30% revision rule is designed to flag, and the next-year estimate was actually revised down 13.8% over 90 days. We do not treat the revision data as a reliable forward indicator for this name.

The offsetting drag is real: 555 California Street's cash NOI fell more than half year-over-year, THE MART occupancy is 80%, and retail occupancy is 78.3%. Cash NOI is contracting in aggregate even as the best NY assets re-lease. Net, growth prospects are weak-to-stabilizing rather than accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing 10.2x P/E is meaningless, computed off GAAP EPS inflated by the one-time gain. On the correct metric, the stock trades at roughly 15.6x FY2025 adjusted FFO of $2.42, which is not cheap for a no-growth, high-leverage office REIT with declining cash NOI.

Peer comparison is muddy because office-REIT P/Es are distorted across the group (ARE −8.4x, CUZ −907x, BXP 33.3x — none informative). The cleaner read is yield and discount-to-NAV. Vornado's ~2.0% yield is less than half the 4.4–5.6% peer median, reflecting its deliberately starved payout; this is not a yield-support story. On NAV, management's buybacks at $29–35 and the new $300M authorization suggest insiders see the stock below private-market value, and the $900M put proceeds on the 350 Park site are a concrete data point on trophy-asset marks.

We assign a middling valuation score of 4.5 deliberately. Our own track record shows that a "cheap" headline multiple in this sector has been an anti-predictive value-trap signal, and the inputs here are mixed: the stock is not cheap on FFO, the cash-NOI trend is negative, and any NAV discount is the standard office-REIT discount rather than a unique mispricing. There is genuine optionality (the Citadel put, PENN re-leasing mark-to-market) that could close part of a NAV gap, but it is not the asymmetric, identifiable mispricing that would justify a high valuation score. After a 52% rally, the easy part of the re-rating has already happened.

## Competitive Landscape

Vornado owns trophy assets in the strongest Manhattan submarkets, where bifurcated office demand is concentrating. PENN 2 at 90% leased and double-digit GAAP leasing spreads are concrete evidence that its newly amenitized product near Penn Station is winning tenants. This is a genuine and improving competitive advantage in NY office.

The trajectory is two-sided rather than uniformly improving. The NY core is gaining ground (positive spreads, PENN momentum, the Park Avenue Plaza add), but the secondary markets are losing it: 555 California (SF) at 86.7% occupancy with cash NOI down 51%, and THE MART (Chicago) at 80%. The portfolio is bifurcating internally along the same lines as the broader market. Net competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving in the assets that matter most, dragged by the non-core tail.

## Risk Assessment

**Distressed assets and defaults — the most company-specific risk.** Several loans are in or near default: 888 Seventh Avenue (Vornado's own HQ) — $244.5M mortgage matured December 2025 unpaid, event of default, forbearance to March 2027 with interest deferred; 606 Broadway — $74.5M in maturity default accruing +3% default interest; 650 Madison Avenue (22.2% JV) — already written to zero in 2022, received a default notice October 2025 (cured November 2025), $800M loan. These are non-recourse, so direct downside is capped at equity (often already zero), but they signal real asset-level stress and are partly already priced.

**Cash NOI erosion** — the free-rent overhang means cash same-store operations are declining even as GAAP NOI rises.

**Refinancing/rate risk** — substantially mitigated for 2026–27 after the refinancing wave, but the cost of capital is migrating up (refis into the 5.5–6.9% range), which compresses FFO over time. A higher-for-longer rate path is a structural headwind to a financing-heavy REIT.

**Concentration** — ~88% NY metro, ~78% office. Direct exposure to NYC fiscal/political conditions (rent-the-rich rhetoric, Local Law 97 carbon penalties, CLCPA mandates), which the company flags prominently.

**PENN 1 ground-rent litigation** — arbitration set rent at $15M or $20.2M/year; a NY court vacated the arbitration (October 2025), VNO appealing. An adverse ruling claws back the $17.24M accrual reversal that boosted 2025 NOI and raises ground rent to $20.2M/year retroactive to 2023.

**Key-man and related-party risk** — heavy dependence on Steven Roth; overlapping Interstate Properties/Alexander's interests and management fees create structural conflicts.

**AI demand risk** — the 10-K newly emphasizes AI potentially reducing or reconfiguring tenant space needs.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 38.0% versus 33.7% one-year HV — modestly rich in absolute terms, but at the 6th percentile of its own chain, indicating low relative uncertainty after the rally. The put/call skew is −3.4% (call-heavy), the put/call volume ratio is just 0.11, and IV fell from 43.9% to 38.0% as the stock rose 17% since late May. The term structure is flat. This is the profile of resolved near-term uncertainty and bullish-to-neutral positioning, not a market bracing for downside. Combined with short interest at effectively zero (19,370 shares), there is no informed bearish crowd here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$45, in line with the high analyst target).** The Citadel/350 Park put delivers $900M of cash to Vornado (or a marquee co-development), validating trophy-asset marks. PENN 2 re-leasing mark-to-market and the burn-off of free rent inflect cash NOI positive over the next 12–24 months. The de-risked balance sheet plus an easing long end re-rates the whole office complex. The buybacks at $29–35 prove accretive, and the NAV discount closes.

**Bear case (~$28, in line with the low analyst target, ~26% downside).** Cash NOI keeps declining as the SF/Chicago drag and free-rent overhang outweigh PENN gains. The PENN 1 appeal goes against the company, clawing back the accrual and raising ground rent. Rates stay higher-for-longer, compressing FFO as more low-coupon debt refinances up. The 52% rally proves to be a sentiment swing rather than a fundamental turn, and the stock retraces toward the office-REIT NAV-discount range. Additional non-core loans slip into default.

**Base case (~$37, roughly flat from current).** Cash NOI stabilizes near the bottom as PENN momentum offsets the secondary-market drag; the Citadel put closes positively; FFO holds around $2.40; the stock consolidates its gains while the market waits for the cash-NOI inflection to show up in the numbers.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the bear and bull cases are close to balanced after the rally, and the binary July catalyst makes a high-conviction call premature.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The 350 Park option resolves favorably (either a $900M cash put to Vornado or a confirmed Citadel-anchored co-development) AND management redeploys proceeds accretively or accelerates buybacks below NAV.
- Same-store cash NOI turns positive for two consecutive quarters, confirming the free-rent burn-off has flipped to cash growth.
- A clear NAV-discount widening (stock falling materially while leasing momentum and asset marks hold) that restores asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The PENN 1 appeal is lost, clawing back the $17.24M accrual and adding $20.2M/year retroactive ground rent.
- Additional non-core loans default and begin to threaten recourse or cross-default provisions.
- Cash same-store NOI accelerates its decline (worse than −5% at-share) on broadening NY office weakness, not just SF/Chicago.
- The stock re-approaches the 52-week high (>$42) on momentum without a corresponding cash-NOI inflection, restoring an unfavorable risk/reward.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 2026 350 Park decision is the next hard catalyst; the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August) will show whether the Park Avenue Plaza close and continued PENN leasing move cash NOI. Reassess at both points.

## Conclusion

We upgrade Vornado to HOLD from AVOID, but the upgrade reflects a changed price and catalyst calendar, not a changed fundamental verdict. The bear thesis from prior reports remains intact: flat FFO masked by a one-time GAAP gain, declining cash same-store NOI, extreme NYC office concentration, and distressed loans including the mortgage on the company's own headquarters. None of that has improved.

The setup has improved. The 52% rally has removed the asymmetric short opportunity; the 2026–27 maturity wall is largely cleared; PENN 2 validates the redevelopment thesis; and the Citadel/350 Park option delivers a concrete, dated, positive catalyst either way in July. With short interest at zero and options positioning call-heavy and complacent, there is neither a mechanical edge to a bearish trade nor evidence the market is positioned for a fall. The assessment is two-sided: cash NOI must inflect for the rally to be justified, and that has not yet shown up in the numbers. Investors should hold through the July catalyst and the August Q2 print, watching cash same-store NOI as the decisive metric, and reassess from there.
