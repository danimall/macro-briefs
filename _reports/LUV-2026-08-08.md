## Executive Summary

We are upgrading Southwest Airlines from AVOID to HOLD, ending a four-report streak of negative ratings, on the basis of one specific piece of evidence: the company absorbed the fuel shock that underpinned the bear case and did not break. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $8,432M (+16.4%), adjusted operating income of $585M at a 6.7% margin (up 3.3 points year-over-year), and adjusted diluted EPS of $0.94 against a $0.52 consensus — an 82.5% beat — while fuel cost per gallon rose 69% to $3.92 and imposed a disclosed $1.17 headwind to adjusted EPS in that single quarter. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised from $2.63 to $3.42 over 90 days (+29.9%), with the current quarter up 32.8%. That combination of revenue acceleration, margin expansion and sharp upward revisions at a forward multiple below 14x is the profile we would normally treat as a strong buy signal, and we weight it as the decisive argument against continuing to recommend AVOID.

We stop at HOLD rather than moving further because the revision momentum is thinner than it looks and the structural position is unchanged. Three checks matter. First, the revision is front-loaded: current-year estimates rose 29.9% but next-year estimates rose only 9.3%, and consensus of $3.42 still sits in the lower half of the company's own $3.25-$4.25 guide — the street was too low, rather than the company clearing a fair bar. Second, earnings quality is mixed: $105M of aircraft disposal gains sit inside Q2 adjusted operating income (18% of the total) and are not excluded as a special item, while part of the near-tripling of "ancillary sold separately" revenue is a reallocation of Chase co-brand consideration rather than incremental customer cash. Third, and most important, Southwest cut the floor of its full-year guide 19% (from "at least $4.00") in the same three-week window that Delta reaffirmed and United raised its adjusted EPS outlook to $9-$11 while disclosing roughly $6B of incremental fuel cost. The likeliest explanation is structural: with no premium cabin, no long-haul international, no meaningful cargo and no refinery, Southwest has the fewest offsets to a fuel shock and entered it with roughly a 1.9% adjusted operating margin in FY2025.

At $47.05 the stock trades at 13.8x current-year consensus and 9.5x next-year consensus, against a passenger-airline peer median P/E of 10.0 that includes United at 10.0x with a raised guide. Our base case of $51 (11x a normalized $4.60 of adjusted EPS) is 8.4% above spot; the bull case of $65 requires jet fuel back near $2.75-$3.00; the bear case of $33 follows from a renewed fuel spike or a domestic leisure demand break. Probability-weighted at 50/25/25 that is roughly a 6% price return plus a 1.5% dividend — below what we require for a cyclical with 43.5% realized volatility and a beta of ~1.25.

## Company Overview

Southwest reports as a single segment: scheduled US and near-international passenger air transportation. As of June 30, 2026 the fleet was 803 aircraft (708 owned, 95 leased), average age 11 years, all Boeing 737 — 288 -700s (137 seats, 20-year average age), 192 -800s and 323 MAX 8s. Average stage length in Q2 was 784 miles and average passenger haul 1,088 miles.

The company is roughly 18 months into a commercial-model rebuild executed under a cooperation agreement with Elliott Investment Management dated October 23, 2024 (amended February 19, 2025). The sequence is dated in the Q2 10-Q: checked-bag fees introduced May 28, 2025; assigned and extra-legroom seating and a four-tier fare structure launched January 27, 2026; nine international interline partners added since February 2025 (most recently Singapore Airlines and Air Premia, connecting at LAX/SEA/SFO/HNL); first Starlink-equipped aircraft in service June 22, 2026; and network retrenchment effective June 4, 2026 with operations suspended at Chicago O'Hare and Washington Dulles and staffing reduced at Atlanta, Fort Lauderdale and Philadelphia. Management stated in the July 22 release that the initiatives are "substantially completed."

Two things about this history bear on the current call. The central 2024-25 bear argument — that monetizing bags and seats would destroy the brand — has not been borne out: Southwest was named #1 in customer satisfaction among economy passengers in the J.D. Power 2026 North America study for the fifth consecutive year, Rapid Rewards reached roughly 100 million members with enrollments up 35%, and Chase co-brand card acquisitions grew 28% with double-digit growth in every month of Q2. Simultaneously, the model is retreating from the expensive hubs it entered in 2022-2024, and capacity growth for FY2026 has been cut to about 1.5%.

Revenue compounded 5.6% annually from 2022 to 2025 while operating income fell 58%. FY2025 net margin was 1.57% and return on average equity 4.8%. On an adjusted basis FY2025 operating income was approximately $539M, a 1.9% margin. That is the margin buffer with which Southwest entered a fuel shock.

### Q2 and H1 2026

Q2 revenue $8,432M (+16.4%, all-time record); GAAP operating income $285M (3.4%); adjusted operating income $585M (6.7%); GAAP diluted EPS $0.47; adjusted $0.94 versus $0.43. H1 revenue $15,682M (+14.7%), GAAP operating income $615M versus $2M, adjusted $915M versus $117M.

Unit economics tell the story precisely:

| Load factor | 79.3% | 78.5% | +0.8 pt |
| Average passenger fare | $225.61 | $186.65 | **+20.9%** |

| FTEs | 73,456 | 72,242 | +1.7% |

This is entirely yield-led. Capacity was flat, passengers carried fell 3.3%, average haul lengthened 4.7%, and fares rose 21%. Fuel now represents 27% of CASM (4.70¢ of 17.30¢) versus 19% a year earlier. Salaries are 43% of CASM and rose 7.2% per ASM on step and pay-rate increases against flat capacity, so labor productivity per ASM is deteriorating, not improving. Maintenance fell 11.4% per ASM on fewer -700 engine shop visits — a genuine fleet-renewal dividend.

Two items reduce the quality of the reported margin expansion. Gains on asset disposition were $105M in Q2 and $187M in H1, booked as a credit inside "Other operating expenses" and **not** excluded from adjusted results; against $585M of Q2 adjusted operating income that is 18% of the total, and it is the primary reason "Other operating expenses" fell 2.8%. Separately, "Passenger ancillary sold separately" rose from $309M to $875M, but the filing states the line "includes the co-brand impact" of the 2025 Chase amendments, under which more co-brand consideration is now classified in passenger revenue; loyalty air-transportation revenue fell $104M in the same quarter and the loyalty air traffic liability declined from $4,849M (Dec-2024) to $4,174M (Jun-2026). The gross +$566M ancillary step-up is an upper bound on new customer economics, and the disclosure does not permit sizing the split.

Southwest also reversed $285M of breakage revenue recognized in 2022-2025, cutting net income $185M ($0.37/share), after lowering the assumed non-redemption rate on roughly $7.9B of non-expiring flight credits from ~23% to ~20%. Roughly $1.8B of those credits remain outstanding; a further three-point revision on the same population would be approximately $237M. The revenue is deferred into air traffic liability rather than lost, but past reported revenue was flattered and the estimate is now explicitly acknowledged as less predictable because the transformation altered redemption behavior.

### Balance sheet and cash generation

At June 30, 2026: cash $3,791M, total liquidity $5.3B including an undrawn $1.5B revolver (expires August 2028); total debt $5,946M ($2,156M current, $3,790M long-term); net debt $2,155M, up from $1,670M at year-end; equity $7,082M; unencumbered aircraft and related assets roughly $15.7B net book value; investment grade at all three agencies; reported gross leverage 2.1x, which reconciles against approximately $3.25B of TTM EBITDAR.

The weak link is cash conversion. FY2025 free cash flow was negative $831M (operating cash flow $1,842M less capex $2,673M), and cash fell $4,278M over the year while roughly $2,549M of buybacks and about $3.24B of debt retirement were funded from the pandemic-era cash balance. H1 2026 operating cash flow of $1,947M included $1,021M of seasonal air traffic liability build; Q2 alone produced $530M of operating cash with ATL down $65M. Against full-year net capital spending guided to "the low end of, or below, $3.0-$3.5 billion," full-year free cash flow is plausibly near breakeven. Current maturities of $2,156M inside twelve months are covered by cash and the revolver but represent a real call on liquidity in a year of thin free cash flow.

Two structural changes deserve emphasis. Southwest drew a $500M senior secured term loan on March 11, 2026 and upsized it $1.0B on May 19, secured by aircraft with a minimum collateral coverage requirement — the first meaningful departure from an unsecured balance sheet, though $15.7B of unencumbered assets means it is a first step rather than a constraint. And the buyback lever is largely spent: 28.4M shares were repurchased in Q1 2026 for roughly $1,250M (~$44.0/share, 5.8% of shares outstanding), none in Q2, and only $450M of authorization remains — about 1.9% of shares at the current price. Diluted weighted shares fell 8.9% year-over-year, and there is no meaningful contingent dilution: the $1.6B convertible notes were repaid at maturity in Q2 2025 and the dilutive RSU effect is roughly 0.8%.

Non-GAAP after-tax ROIC improved from 3.2% (TTM June 2025) to 9.0% (TTM June 2026), but average invested capital fell from $17.4B to $13.3B over the same span. Roughly half the improvement came from shrinking the denominator, which is finite and now largely used.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 14.7% in H1 is real and record-setting, and the components are identifiable: fares +20.9%, managed (corporate) business revenue at an all-time quarterly record and +30% year-over-year, ancillary monetization from bag fees and paid seating, and mix from longer hauls and connecting itineraries via the partner network.

The question is durability, and here the evidence is two-sided. The corporate revenue gain is the most credible structural improvement in the entire program — assigned seating, extra legroom, GDS distribution and tiered fares removed the specific frictions that excluded Southwest from corporate travel programs, and corporate contracts are sticky once won. Against that, growth is arriving with volume attrition (revenue passengers −3.3%, trips flown −0.1%, seats flown −0.4%) and Q3 guidance calls for ASMs down 1% to flat. Fare-led growth against declining passenger counts eventually meets an elasticity limit, and management is not planning to grow into it. The H2 comparison also gets harder mechanically: Q3 RASM guidance of +17.5% to +19.5% explicitly incorporates the headwind of lapping the 2025 bag-fee implementation.

The estimate revision pattern is instructive. Current quarter +32.8% over 90 days, current year +29.9%, next quarter +21.1% — but next year only +9.3%. A durable multi-year inflection normally lifts out-year estimates comparably. The concentration in near-term periods is consistent with two things that are largely exogenous or one-time: a better forward fuel curve than assumed 90 days ago, and the Q2 beat. We treat the revision signal as genuine and material — it is why we are no longer recommending AVOID — but not as evidence of a durable earnings step-change.

The full-year guide embeds a profit shape worth flagging. H1 adjusted EPS was $1.39 and Q3 is guided to $0.50-$0.75, so the $3.25-$4.25 full-year range implies Q4 adjusted EPS of $1.36 to $2.11, midpoint approximately $1.74 — about 2.8x the Q3 midpoint and well above the record Q2 quarter. Domestic airlines do not normally earn their peak in Q4. Three mechanisms could produce it: a backwardated fuel curve as of July 17 (a $0.70/gal decline from Q3 levels on roughly 500M gallons is about $350M pre-tax, or $0.55/share); a weak Q4 2025 base; and the first holiday peak with assigned and extra-legroom seating fully monetized plus a full quarter of O'Hare/Dulles savings. The disclosure does not allow us to distinguish among them, and the low end of the range looks like the realistic planning assumption. Consensus of $3.42 sits nearer that low end than the midpoint, which we read as the street reaching the same conclusion.

Fleet growth is constrained by Boeing. Eighty-one aircraft contractually owed for 2024-2025 were never delivered and were folded into 2026 commitments, making the stated 2026 contractual total 167 against expected actual deliveries of 64 MAX 8s. The -7 remains uncertified with Southwest as launch customer and 264 units still firm; in H1 2026 the company converted 19 of its 2026 -7 deliveries into -8s and exercised eight -7 options for 2027. Substituting 175-seat -8s for 137-seat -7s adds more seats per departure than the fleet plan assumed, which raises the volume required to fill them — in tension with a yield-led strategy currently shedding low-fare traffic. The alternative reading is simply that -8s are available and -7s are not, and the order book permits redesignation by design.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.05 with 489.2M diluted shares, market capitalization is approximately $23.0B and enterprise value approximately $26.0B including $2,155M net debt and $857M of capitalized aircraft leases.

|---|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $3.42 | **13.8x** | Peer median 10.0x; UAL 10.0x |
| P/E on FY2026 guidance mid $3.75 | 12.6x | — |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $4.95 | **9.5x** | — |
| EV/EBITDAR (TTM ~$3.25B) | ~8.0x | — |
| EV/EBITDAR (FY2026E $3.9-4.3B) | 6.1-6.7x | — |
| Price/book ($14.48/share) | 3.25x | — |
| Dividend yield ($0.72) | 1.53% | Peer median 0.0% |

The peer set is heterogeneous (AAL 47.9x, ALK 75.6x, UAL 10.0x, SKYW 8.6x, AL 7.0x), so the median is of limited use. The comparison that matters is United at 10.0x with a **raised** 2026 guide of $9-$11 adjusted EPS versus Southwest at 13.8x with a **cut** floor. Southwest carries a better balance sheet — investment grade at all three agencies, 2.1x gross leverage, $15.7B unencumbered assets — and a stronger loyalty and co-brand franchise. It carries a materially worse revenue mix for a fuel shock and a thinner margin. On current-year earnings, the market is paying a 38% premium to the better-positioned large peer, and we do not think the balance-sheet quality justifies that spread given that free cash flow is near zero and the buyback authorization is nearly exhausted.

The 9.5x on FY2027 consensus looks cheap, and it is the strongest quantitative bull argument. But it is largely a fuel bet rather than an operating-leverage bet. Working the arithmetic: if 2027 fuel averages $3.20/gal against roughly $3.80 in 2026, that is $0.60 on approximately 2,118M annual gallons, or $1,271M pre-tax, roughly $2.00/share gross and about $1.65 net of profit sharing. Add that to a $3.50 2026 base and consensus $4.95 is undemanding. Hold fuel at $3.80 and 2027 is more like $3.75-$4.25 on initiative maturation and network savings alone. The plausible FY2027 range is therefore roughly $3.75 to $5.50, midpoint about $4.60.

We anchor the base case at 11x $4.60 = **$51**, 8.4% above spot, which is close to the $52 analyst median and consistent with a modest premium to United's multiple for the balance sheet, offset by the mix disadvantage. Applying the estimate-revision principle that consensus targets lag on inflection stocks requires a genuine multi-year inflection; here the out-year revision is only +9.3% and the company itself lowered the floor of its guide, so we do not treat $52 as a stale floor.

We do not score valuation highly. A sub-10x out-year multiple on a cyclical with unhedged input costs, near-zero free cash flow, roughly 18% of adjusted operating income from asset sales and a revenue base being repriced rather than expanded is the configuration in which a low headline multiple most often proves to be a fair price rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The July guidance divergence is the cleanest available read on relative position. Delta reaffirmed full-year guidance on July 10 with fare measures up 11-12% and management stating fares would stay elevated even if fuel moderates. United raised its outlook on July 15 while disclosing roughly $6B of additional fuel cost. Lufthansa warned on August 4 after Q2 operating profit more than halved. Southwest cut its floor on July 22. Same shock, opposite guidance direction from the two US network carriers. The mechanism is structural: Southwest has no premium cabin to reprice, no long-haul international network, no material cargo business and no refinery hedge, so the revenue-side offsets that let Delta and United hold guidance do not exist.

Where Southwest is genuinely improving: managed business revenue +30% to a record, Rapid Rewards at approximately 100 million members with enrollments +35%, Chase acquisitions +28%, and a fifth consecutive J.D. Power economy satisfaction win achieved while monetizing bags and seats. The nine-partner interline strategy monetizes domestic feed to Singapore Airlines, Air Premia, Icelandair and others without widebody capital — these are commercial counterparties in jointly-sold itineraries, not customers or equity affiliates, and no guarantees are disclosed.

Where it is retreating: exiting O'Hare and Dulles and downsizing Atlanta, Fort Lauderdale and Philadelphia reverses the 2022-2024 hub expansion. Landing fees and airport rentals rose 12.2% in Q2, roughly half from higher airport rates for leased space and a quarter from heavier -8 aircraft — airport cost inflation is structural, and the response is to concentrate where density exists rather than defend marginal stations. Combined with roughly 1.5% capacity growth against approximately 60 annual retirements, this is a utilization and mix strategy.

The Boeing dependency is concentrated rather than shared. Being 100% 737 and the -7 launch customer makes FAA certification timing a single point of failure for a fleet plan with 264 firm -7 orders and $14.8B of capital commitments through 2031.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel, fully unhedged.** The 10-Q states the hedging program was discontinued and the portfolio terminated, that the company "does not intend to add additional fuel derivatives," and that it is "fully exposed to fluctuations in fuel prices." Only $79M of legacy AOCI remains to amortize. Company sensitivity: one cent per gallon changes Q3 fuel expense by $5.3M on 532M expected gallons. Annualized on approximately 2,118M gallons, a $0.50/gal move is roughly $1,059M pre-tax, about $822M after tax at the 22.4% non-GAAP rate, or approximately $1.67 per diluted share gross — call it $1.40 net of profit sharing. That is a third to a half of the entire $3.25-$4.25 guide, which is why the guide is a dollar wide. The mitigating context: Delta, United and American are also structurally unhedged, so the decision converged Southwest to industry practice and eliminated a real premium cost. We draw no inference about management foresight from the timing — the exit was disclosed in 2025 and the price move is externally dated to 2026. What is objectively true is that Southwest removed the shock absorber and has the thinnest margin buffer among the majors with which to absorb a shock. **Domestic leisure demand.** Southwest is the most domestic-leisure-concentrated major. [unverified — the research brief does not contain macro data on mortgage rates, July payrolls or mortgage application demand] If a consumer slowdown lands in Q4, it lands precisely in the quarter the full-year guide depends on. This risk is additive to the fuel risk rather than offsetting.

**Back-loaded guidance.** Implied Q4 adjusted EPS of $1.36-$2.11 against a Q3 midpoint of $0.625 is atypical seasonality and the primary reason we treat the low end of the range as the base planning case.

**Breakage estimate.** Roughly $1.8B of non-expiring credits remain; another three-point assumption change is approximately $237M. Exposure is bounded by breakage previously recognized but cannot be computed precisely from the disclosure.

**Liquidity mechanics.** Current maturities of $2,156M against $3,791M cash and an undrawn $1.5B revolver; free cash flow near breakeven; $500M/$1.0B aircraft-secured term loans introduced in 2026. Separately, credit-card processor agreements require cash reserves sized as a percentage of air traffic liability ($8,184M total) if ratings fall below specified sub-investment-grade levels. No holdbacks exist today and Southwest is investment grade at all three agencies, but this is the mechanism by which airline distress becomes self-reinforcing.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Form 4 title changes on August 5, 2026 show Justin Jones as EVP & Chief Commercial Officer (previously EVP Operations) and Anthony Roach as Chief Customer & Brand Officer, and COO Andrew Watterson's August 5 grant was 2,995 shares versus 35,092 in March. An Item 5.02 8-K was filed August 7, 2026 and could not be retrieved. Whether any officer departed and who runs operations is unverified and should not be assumed either way. Separately, the Fifth Amended and Restated Bylaws (effective May 16, 2025) set a 3%-of-shares threshold to bring a derivative action — roughly $690M of stock, a threshold only large institutions clear — and Southwest used it to obtain dismissal with prejudice of the Bags Fly Free derivative suit; the plaintiff is appealing on constitutional grounds with the State of Texas intervening.

**Resolved risks, worth crediting.** The December 2022 meltdown securities class action was dismissed with prejudice with final judgment entered May 20, 2026 (on appeal to the Fifth Circuit, plaintiffs' brief due September 24, 2026). The USERRA military-leave class action settled for $18.5M with final approval May 19, 2026. A California meal-period class action was resolved in the company's favor on summary judgment. The meltdown-era legal overhang has largely cleared in Southwest's favor over the past twelve months, and the convertible notes were retired.

### Options Market Signal

The options market prices a wide near-term distribution without a directional bearish view. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 44.4% sits essentially on top of 43.5% one-year realized (spread 0.8%), so there is no cheap-or-rich entry-timing edge. The term structure is in backwardation (44.4% at one week versus 39.6% at six months), which indicates near-term event risk rather than direction. Positioning is call-tilted: put/call skew −5.5%, put/call volume 0.66, put/call open interest 0.44 — the lowest put tilt across our coverage set. ATM IV has fallen 6.6 points from 51.0% on August 3 as the price rose 1.4%, consistent with post-earnings vol decay rather than accumulating protection. The one-month tenor is the exception (put/call volume 3.22, +6.1% skew), suggesting some September hedging around macro dates. Implied moves of ±10.2% at three months and ±20.5% at six months are consistent with our own view that fuel dominates the distribution. Short interest of 8.6% of float and 4.7 days to cover is moderate — the bear case is not crowded, and neither is it being expressed in options.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $65 (+38%)

Jet fuel normalizes toward $2.75-$3.00/gal. On roughly 2,118M annual gallons, a $0.80-$1.00 decline from 2026 levels is worth $2.20-$2.75 of adjusted EPS gross of profit sharing. Added to a 2026 base near $3.50 and layered with the first full year of assigned and extra-legroom seating, four-tier fares, corporate revenue compounding off a +30% base, the annualized savings from the O'Hare/Dulles exits, and continued -700 retirement (which cuts maintenance per ASM), FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $6.00. At 11x that is $66, matching the top of the analyst range at $67. Supporting evidence: 100 million loyalty members, Chase acquisitions +28%, the fifth consecutive J.D. Power win demonstrating the brand survived monetization, and a company that produced a 6.7% adjusted operating margin with fuel at $3.92 — implying substantial earnings power at any normal fuel price.

### Bear Case — $33 (−30%)

Fuel re-accelerates above $4.25, or domestic leisure demand cracks. Either alone removes $1.40-$2.00 of adjusted EPS. Southwest has no premium cabin, cargo or long-haul mix to offset, and Q4 is where the entire guide is concentrated. Revenue passengers are already down 3.3%, so the yield-led model reaches its elasticity ceiling exactly when it needs volume to absorb the gauge-up from -7-to-8 conversions. Strip the $105M quarterly asset disposal gains and part of the Chase-reclassified ancillary revenue and the underlying margin expansion is smaller than reported. FY2027 adjusted EPS falls to $2.00-$2.50, and at 9-10x with free cash flow negative, the stock returns toward the $33-$35 area — near the low analyst target of $35 and above the 52-week low of $28.98, with book value of $14.48/share and $15.7B of unencumbered aircraft providing the floor.

### Base Case — $51 (+8.4%)

Fuel averages $3.10-$3.30 in 2027, FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $4.60, and the stock trades at 11x — a modest premium to United's current multiple for the investment-grade balance sheet, offset by the inferior fuel-shock mix. Free cash flow turns modestly positive as capex runs at or below the low end of $3.0-$3.5B. Corporate and loyalty revenue continue compounding, but ROIC improvement now has to come from earnings rather than balance-sheet shrinkage, and the remaining $450M buyback authorization is worth only about 1.9% of shares.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: approximately +6% price return plus a 1.53% dividend, or roughly 8% total. For a stock with 43.5% realized volatility and beta near 1.25, that is below a fair required return, which is why we hold rather than accumulate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (upgraded from AVOID). Rationale: uncertain — the two dominant variables (jet fuel and domestic leisure demand) are exogenous and unresolved, and our base-case upside sits inside the noise of a stock that prices a ±10.2% three-month move.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in late October 2026 is the decisive checkpoint. It resolves (a) whether Q3 lands in the $0.50-$0.75 guide, (b) whether management reaffirms a full-year range that requires Q4 adjusted EPS near $1.74, and (c) what fuel per gallon actually printed against the $3.70-$3.75 assumption. A secondary near-term item is disclosure of the August 7 Item 5.02 8-K contents.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Fuel and guidance together.** Q3 fuel at or below $3.60/gal *and* the full-year adjusted EPS floor raised above $3.50. That combination would validate the FY2027 consensus of $4.95 and move our base case toward $57-$60 (11.5-12x), restoring a double-digit margin of safety.
2. **Earnings quality improvement.** Two consecutive quarters in which adjusted operating income grows with asset-disposition gains contributing less than 10% of the total, and free cash flow (operating cash flow less gross capex) positive excluding air-traffic-liability build.
3. **Price.** A decline to $38-$40 (roughly 8.3-8.7x FY2027 consensus, near the April 2026 trading area) without fundamental deterioration would create the margin of safety this thesis currently lacks.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 adjusted EPS below the $0.50 guidance floor, or the full-year range cut a second time. A second cut would establish that the transformation cannot outrun input costs.
2. Jet fuel sustained above $4.25/gal for a full quarter, which on our sensitivity removes roughly $1.20-$1.40 of annualized adjusted EPS and puts the company near operating breakeven.
3. Revenue passengers carried declining more than 5% year-over-year with load factor falling, which would indicate the fare-led model has hit its elasticity limit rather than deliberately shedding unprofitable traffic.
4. Any credit rating action toward the bottom of investment grade, given the air-traffic-liability cash-reserve trigger with card processors.

**Thesis-invalidating evidence for existing holders:** a further breakage-assumption reduction of comparable size to the $285M reversal, or additional secured debt drawn beyond the remaining $1.0B uncommitted incremental capacity without a corresponding free-cash-flow improvement.

## Conclusion

Four consecutive AVOID ratings on Southwest rested on a specific proposition: that a carrier with a 1.9% adjusted operating margin and no fuel hedges would be unable to absorb a fuel shock. Q2 2026 tested that proposition directly — fuel per gallon rose 69% and cost $889M year-over-year — and Southwest expanded adjusted operating margin 3.3 points to 6.7%, posted record revenue and beat consensus adjusted EPS by 82.5%. Consensus current-year EPS has risen 29.9% in 90 days. That evidence does not support continuing to recommend against owning the stock, and we are upgrading to HOLD.

It also does not support buying it here. The company cut the floor of its own guidance 19% in the same weeks Delta reaffirmed and United raised, for a structural reason that will not change: no premium cabin, no long-haul international, no cargo, no refinery. Roughly 18% of Q2 adjusted operating income came from selling retired airframes and engines. Part of the ancillary revenue step-up is a reallocation of Chase consideration rather than new customer cash. Free cash flow was negative $831M in 2025 and is near breakeven in 2026. The buyback lever that drove half the ROIC improvement is down to $450M. And at 13.8x current-year consensus, Southwest trades at a 38% premium to United, which has the better mix, a raised guide and the same unhedged fuel exposure.

Our base case of $51 offers 8.4% upside against a 30% bear case that requires nothing more exotic than fuel returning to $4.25 or a domestic leisure slowdown arriving in the quarter the guidance depends on. Existing holders are being paid a 1.53% dividend to wait for the October print, which will resolve both the Q3 guide and the credibility of an implied Q4 running 2.8x Q3. New capital should wait for either $38-$40 or a raised full-year floor with fuel below $3.60.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the bull-case narrative citing an OPEC+ voluntary-cut rollback, and the risk-section macro assertions (Brent ~20% move, Middle East transit conditions, US mortgage rates at a one-year high, a July payroll contraction of 23,000, mortgage-application demand) are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed or tagged `[unverified]`; the demand-slowdown leg of the bear case rests on macro claims that could not be sourced.
