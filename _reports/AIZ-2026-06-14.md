**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Assurant is a global B2B2C specialty protection company that partners with mobile carriers, auto dealers, and mortgage servicers to insure connected devices, vehicles, and homes. The business has compounded well: revenue rose from $10.19B (2022) to $12.81B (2025), net income climbed to $872.7M (+15% YoY), operating cash flow surged to $1.83B, and the company returned $468M to shareholders in 2025 while raising the dividend 10% in January 2026. Q1 2026 was, per management, "the strongest performance in Assurant's history," with adjusted EPS of $5.95 (an 11.3% beat) and a raised full-year outlook. This is a high-quality, well-capitalized franchise with a defensible niche position and no balance-sheet concern; the Altman Z-Score of 0.7 is a model artifact that misclassifies insurers and should be overridden.

The investment problem is timing and price, not quality. AIZ trades at $260.99, fractionally below its 52-week high of $261.10, after a 31% twelve-month advance. At ~12.4x the current-year consensus EPS of $21.04 it screens cheap against its own history, but the more telling signals are forward: the current-quarter consensus has been cut 2.7% over 90 days and the next-quarter consensus 1.4%, even as the full-year number drifted up. Management itself guides only to low-single-digit adjusted EBITDA and EPS growth ex-catastrophes for 2026, a deceleration from the 2025 pace. The 28% Global Housing EBITDA surge that powered 2025 benefited from $46M of lower catastrophe losses ($199M vs. $245M), a tailwind that mean-reverts in a normal hurricane season and is structurally exposed to Florida/California/Texas concentration.

The stock now sits below the analyst target band ($270 low, $290 high, $280.5 mean), sentiment is classified as degrading, and there is no near-term catalyst that re-rates the multiple beyond an in-line earnings cadence. The combination of a full price at the top of its range, a soft near-term earnings revision trend, a low-cat comparison that gets harder, and dense senior-leadership turnover in a business whose value rests on client-relationship continuity keeps this a HOLD with a growth bias. I would add on a pullback toward the low-$230s where the risk/reward inverts in the buyer's favor.

## Company Overview

Assurant (incorporated in Delaware in 2004; headquartered in Atlanta; ~14,800 employees across 21 markets) operates a specialty protection model rather than a traditional life or P&C insurer. It embeds protection products into the distribution channels of major brands and shares the economics. Two reportable segments:

**Global Lifestyle (~78% of revenue; $9.94B revenue, $801M adjusted EBITDA in 2025).**
- *Connected Living* ($5.38B): mobile device protection (53% of segment revenue), extended service contracts for electronics and appliances (34%), and financial services/card benefits (13%). The mobile business is vertically integrated across trade-in, reverse logistics, repair (~1,150 partner/repair locations), refurbishment, and disposition, anchored by a Nashville device-care center using automation and robotics.
- *Global Automotive* ($4.2B): vehicle service contracts, GAP products, and commercial equipment protection.

**Global Housing (~22% of revenue; $2.91B revenue, $859M adjusted EBITDA in 2025).**
- *Homeowners* ($2.19B): primarily lender-placed (force-placed) insurance, automatically issued when a borrower's voluntary policy lapses, distributed through mortgage servicers under exclusive multi-year contracts and proprietary insurance-tracking systems.
- *Renters and Other* ($576M): renters insurance plus administration of the NFIP flood program (100% reinsured to the U.S. government).

The competitive moat rests on scale and incumbency in fragmented niches where no single competitor competes across all of Assurant's lines, integration-heavy multi-year (3–5 year) client contracts, vertical integration in the mobile value chain, and superior financial-strength ratings (A.M. Best A+/A on most domestic insurers).

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory is strong and improving:

- **Revenue:** $10.19B (2022) → $11.13B (2023) → $11.88B (2024) → $12.81B (2025), +7.9% YoY.
- **Net income:** $276.6M (2022, depressed by elevated catastrophes) → $642.5M (2023) → $760.2M (2024) → $872.7M (2025), +15% YoY.
- **Segment drivers (2025):** Global Housing adjusted EBITDA +28% to $859M, driven by lower catastrophes, lender-placed policy/premium growth, and favorable non-cat loss experience. Global Lifestyle adjusted EBITDA +4% to $801M on mobile and financial-services growth plus improved auto loss experience.
- **Cash flow:** Operating cash flow rose to $1.83B from $1.33B; FCF approximately $1.6B (~12.5% margin). OCF exceeds net income, indicating high earnings quality.
- **Balance sheet:** $36.3B total assets; equity $5.87B (up from $5.11B); debt/total capital a conservative 27.3%; long-term debt $2.21B, well-laddered with no maturities until 2028/2030. Holding-company liquidity $887M, $662M above the $225M internal minimum. Senior debt BBB (S&P)/Baa2 (Moody's), stable.
- **Capital return:** Dividend raised 10% to $0.88/quarter (January 2026); $468M returned in 2025; $774.6M remaining on buyback authorizations (including a $700M plan authorized November 2025). Share count fell from 54.4M to 50.5M basic over three years. 2026 buybacks raised to $300M–$350M.
- **Investments:** $10.06B portfolio, ~85% fixed maturity, 93% investment-grade or better; net unrealized loss improved to $55.7M from $349.7M as rates fell.

On the headline distress flags: the Altman Z-Score of 0.7 and the 5.18 debt/equity ratio are reserve-driven artifacts. The Altman model is calibrated for industrials and structurally misclassifies insurers because policy reserves and unearned premiums inflate "liabilities" and depress asset turnover. ROE of 14.9%, $1.6B FCF, rising equity, 27.3% debt/capital, investment-grade ratings, and $791.9M of permitted subsidiary dividend capacity for 2026 all confirm a financially healthy company. I score Financial Health 7.5.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is solid but decelerating, and the near-term cadence is the key tension. Q1 2026 delivered record results — net income $274.1M, adjusted EPS $5.95 (+9% ex-cat YoY), revenue $3.35B (+7.9%) — and an 11.3% earnings beat extends a four-quarter beat streak (12.1%, 32.7%, 0.9%, 11.3%). The beat track record warrants confidence in the company's ability to clear the bar.

The forward picture is more measured. Management guides to low-single-digit adjusted EBITDA and EPS growth ex-catastrophes for 2026, a step down from the double-digit net-income growth of 2025. The estimate revision data corroborates the deceleration: the current-quarter consensus has been cut 2.7% over 90 days (to $5.16) and the next-quarter consensus 1.4% (to $4.43), while the full-year number was nudged up 1.5% and next year up 0.9% (to $22.47). The near-term revisions are negative; the back-half revisions are modestly positive. This is a steady-compounder cadence with soft near-term quarters, not the +30% earnings-inflection profile that signals a structural re-rating.

By segment, growth quality is uneven. Housing's 28% EBITDA jump is partly structural — a hardening voluntary homeowners market (insurers retreating from California, Texas, and Florida) lifts lender-placed placement rates — and partly cyclical, with lower catastrophe losses flattering the comparison. Normalizing for a heavier catastrophe year compresses that growth materially. Lifestyle, the larger segment, grew adjusted EBITDA only 4%, with mobile exposed to device trade-in economics, a $7M Q4 2025 inventory write-down, and tariff/supply-chain sensitivity. New U.S. carrier wins and a reverse-logistics expansion support Connected Living, but the segment's top-line growth outpaces its margin growth. Growth Prospects: 6.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At $260.99, AIZ trades at ~12.4x current-year consensus EPS of $21.04 and ~11.6x next-year $22.47. Against the multi-line insurance peer set the multiple is roughly in line: peer median P/E is 12.6 (AFG 12.6, L 13.6, HMN 12.2, GNW 17.0, LNC 4.3, the latter an outlier). On a peer-relative basis AIZ is fairly valued, not cheap. Its 1.4% dividend yield trails the peer median of 2.7%, so there is no yield-support cushion to the valuation.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the bull case here. The cycle-adjusted-valuation framework applies when a stock shows earnings inflection — estimate revisions above +30% or revenue growth above 40% with expanding margins. AIZ shows neither: near-term EPS revisions are negative, full-year revisions are +1.5%, and revenue growth is ~8%. Trailing earnings are not at a trough; 2025 was a record year aided by benign catastrophes, so the trailing multiple understates rather than overstates earnings power. The risk is the opposite of trough-multiple cheapness: a normal hurricane season pressures the comparison.

The stock sits below the analyst target band — low $270, high $290, mean $280.5 — meaning consensus sees ~3.5% to ~11% upside to the band. The analyst-targets-as-floors principle applies only to inflection stocks with positive revisions; AIZ's near-term revisions are negative and sentiment is classified as degrading, so I treat the consensus band as a genuine ceiling rather than a stale floor. There is no specific identified catalyst — no spin, no divestiture re-rating, no inflection — that would force the multiple higher beyond an in-line earnings cadence. Fair value, not mispricing. Valuation: 6.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Assurant occupies defensible niches with structural staying power, but its competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than clearly widening. The moat sources are real: incumbency in fragmented specialty markets, exclusive integration-heavy multi-year contracts, vertical integration in mobile (repair, trade-in, refurbishment), and top-tier financial-strength ratings.

The countervailing forces cap the score. The 10-K's most candid disclosure is that clients may "disintermediate us by developing internal capabilities… which has occurred from time to time" — carriers and servicers have pulled programs in-house before, and the 3–5 year contract structure creates renewal-cliff risk, sometimes requiring concessions or up-front payments at renewal. AI is framed in the filing as both opportunity and threat: lower barriers for new insurtech entrants and easier client self-build. In Housing, the hard voluntary market is a current tailwind but raises catastrophe concentration and regulatory attention under the 2017 multistate Regulatory Settlement Agreement, which mandates more frequent rate filings and exerts structural downward pressure on lender-placed premium rates. The relative-TSR data reinforces the read: cumulative TSR (192.81) has trailed the peer group (221.66) in every year from 2022 to 2025; the company executes well operationally but the market has rewarded multi-line peers more. Competitive Position: 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risks are client-side and catastrophe-side, not balance-sheet-side:

1. **Client concentration / disintermediation** — each segment derives a substantial portion of revenue from a few mobile carriers/MSOs (Lifestyle) and mortgage servicers (Housing). The existential risk is a major client building in-house or consolidating business to a competitor, which the 10-K confirms has happened before.
2. **Catastrophe concentration** — lender-placed homeowners automatically concentrates exposure in Florida, California, and Texas, the states from which voluntary insurers are withdrawing (adverse selection). 2025 cat losses were $199M against $1.76B of reinsurance over a $160M retention; the low-cat year flatters the comparison entering 2026 hurricane season.
3. **Lender-placed regulatory pressure** — the 2017 RSA creates structural downward rate pressure and persistent scrutiny of LPI rates, agent compensation, and tracking practices on the now-very-profitable Housing segment.
4. **Mobile device/inventory risk** — exposed to trade-in values, carrier promotions, and U.S.-China/Taiwan tariff dynamics; a $7M write-down hit Q4 2025.
5. **Goodwill concentration** — $2.65B, 45% of equity (most in Global Automotive, $1.52B); no impairment in Q4 2025, but a reporting-unit downturn could force a write-down.
6. **Reinsurance "boomerang" risk** — a held-for-sale subsidiary carries a $472M reinsurance recoverable from John Hancock tied to a divested long-term-care book; reinsurer insolvency could revert a 30+ year runoff liability Assurant lacks the systems to service.

Management transition adds a softer risk. A dense cluster of Item 5.02 leadership-change filings (January 2026, two in August 2025, July 2025, November 2024) coincides with a new COO, a new Global Housing president, and a Management Committee expansion. Framed as bench strength, the density warrants monitoring given how much franchise value rests on client-relationship continuity. Risk Factors: 6.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sanguine and confirms no near-term binary. Near-term ATM IV is 20.4% versus 24.4% one-year historical volatility (a -4.0% spread, cheap), IV percentile 2%, put/call skew a balanced 1.4%, and the term structure is flat (20.4% near vs. 22.1% far). IV fell from 23.8% to 20.4% as the stock rose 3.5% since late May; falling vol into rising price reflects complacency, not informed hedging. Short interest is low at 2.9% of float with 2.3 days to cover. The market sees neither an imminent catalyst nor elevated downside risk, consistent with the in-line, full-price read. Cheap options make protective puts inexpensive for holders concerned about a heavy hurricane season, but there is no positioning signal pointing to a directional move either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300):** Mobile carrier wins and reverse-logistics expansion accelerate Connected Living, the hard voluntary housing market continues lifting lender-placed placement rates without triggering punitive regulatory action, catastrophe losses stay benign, and the $300M–$350M buyback plus a sub-13x multiple compounds book value and EPS. Re-rating toward the high end of the peer range on ~$22.50 EPS at ~13.3x yields ~$300, roughly +15%.

**Bear case ($210):** A normal-to-heavy hurricane season reverses the catastrophe tailwind and pressures Housing's outsized EBITDA, a major mobile or servicer client renews on worse terms or builds in-house, lender-placed regulatory action tightens rates, and the multiple compresses toward the lower peer band. At ~11x a normalized ~$19–20 EPS, the stock falls to ~$210, roughly -20%.

**Base case ($268):** Low-single-digit ex-cat EBITDA/EPS growth as guided, an in-line earnings cadence, continued buyback support, and a multiple that holds near 12.5x on rising full-year EPS. ~$268, roughly +3%, inside the consensus band.

The asymmetry at $260.99 is not attractive: ~+15% to the bull case against ~-20% to the bear case, with the base case offering only modest upside. The business deserves to be owned; the entry does not compel adding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale: a high-quality compounder worth holding through a normalizing cycle, but at a full price that does not warrant fresh capital at the top of its range.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (early August), which will reveal whether the negative near-term EPS revisions materialize and provide the first read on 2026 hurricane-season catastrophe exposure.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$230s (~11x current-year EPS) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS revisions turning positive alongside a confirmed major carrier/servicer renewal or net-new win that durably lifts Lifestyle EBITDA growth back toward double digits.
- Evidence the Housing margin gains are structurally sticky (placement-rate gains holding through a normal-cat quarter) rather than catastrophe-driven.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Loss or in-housing of a top mobile carrier or major mortgage-servicer relationship, or a renewal on materially worse economics.
- A material adverse lender-placed regulatory action under the RSA that compresses Housing rates.
- A goodwill impairment in Global Automotive or a Housing combined-ratio deterioration that pushes full-year EPS estimates below ~$19.

## Conclusion

Assurant is a financially robust, well-managed specialty protection company with record results, $1.6B of free cash flow, conservative leverage, and an accelerating capital-return program. The fundamentals justify ownership and rebut any balance-sheet concern flagged by industrial-calibrated screens. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: the stock trades fractionally below its 52-week high after a 31% run, at a peer-in-line ~12.4x multiple with no yield cushion, while near-term EPS estimates are being revised down (-2.7% current quarter, -1.4% next quarter over 90 days), management guides only to low-single-digit ex-cat growth, the 2025 Housing surge was partly a $46M low-catastrophe artifact that gets harder to lap entering hurricane season, the stock sits below a consensus target band with degrading sentiment, and there is no identifiable catalyst to re-rate the multiple beyond an in-line cadence. The score trajectory (7.0 → 7.0 → 6.9, with Management Quality and Valuation declining) is consistent with this read: a good business at a fair-to-full price. Hold existing positions; add on a pullback toward the low-$230s where the risk/reward turns favorable.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth) — Overall score 6.4.**
