**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Texas Roadhouse is the highest-quality operator in casual dining and, as of mid-2026, the largest U.S. casual-dining chain by revenue, having surpassed Olive Garden. The business is company-operated rather than franchise-heavy, generates industry-leading unit volumes (~$8.7M AUV at the core concept), carries essentially no long-term debt, and produces strong returns on capital (ROE 28.4%). Its distinguishing strategy is deliberate under-inflation menu pricing, which sacrifices near-term margin to widen its price/value gap and drive traffic. That strategy is working: Q1 2026 comparable sales grew 7.1%, of which 4.5% was traffic, a high-quality mix at a time when many peers show flat-to-negative traffic.

The central operating story of 2025 was margin compression from beef-led commodity inflation. FY2025 restaurant margin fell from 17.1% to 15.5%, operating income declined from $516.5M to $474.7M, and diluted EPS fell 5.8% to $6.10. Q1 2026 reversed the trend: the margin decline narrowed to just 30bps (16.6% → 16.3%), restaurant-margin dollars grew 10.5% to $264.4M, and EPS rose 9.6% to $1.87. Sales leverage from record volumes is beginning to absorb the commodity pressure, and consensus estimates have been revised modestly higher with no cuts.

The problem is price. TXRH trades at roughly 30x trailing and ~28x forward earnings, near its 52-week high of $200.11, against a mid-teens forward EPS growth outlook. On growth-adjusted terms the multiple is defensible for a franchise of this quality, but it is not cheap, and the setup carries a specific, live downside driver: ~half of food costs are beef, 2026 commodity inflation is guided to 6-7% with only ~65% of remaining costs locked, and the New World Screwworm outbreak is spreading across Texas, the company's largest state at 101 restaurants. We rate TXRH a HOLD with a growth bias. The business quality and margin recovery support ownership, but the valuation offers limited margin of safety, and the beef cost/supply tail keeps the near-term risk/reward roughly balanced.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1993 by the late W. Kent Taylor, Texas Roadhouse operates three concepts across 822 restaurants (723 company-owned, 99 franchised) as of Q1 2026, spanning 49 states, one U.S. territory, and ten foreign countries:

- **Texas Roadhouse** (749 units) — the core value-oriented steakhouse, ~94% of restaurant margin.
- **Bubba's 33** (56 company units) — a sports-bar/burger concept, lower-margin and still scaling.
- **Jaggers** (17 units) — a sub-scale fast-casual concept.

Two features distinguish the model within casual dining. First, TXRH owns and operates the vast majority of units and is actively repurchasing domestic franchises to internalize their economics: 20 acquired in 2025 for $107.6M and 5 more in Q1 2026 for $71.7M. This is a capital-allocation choice, not a sign of franchise distress. Second, management holds menu price increases deliberately below inflation (roughly 1.4%-1.9% per increase), trading margin for traffic and share. The result is very high unit volumes and industry-leading traffic, at the cost of margins that remain hostage to commodity cycles.

Off-premise/to-go continues to grow as a capacity-light channel, reaching 13.6% of sales in 2025 and 14.6% in Q1 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong and re-accelerating.** FY2023-2025 revenue progressed $4.63B → $5.37B → $5.88B (+9.4% in 2025, partly muted by lapping a 53rd week in 2024 worth ~$114.7M). Q1 2026 revenue grew 12.8% to $1.63B on the 7.1% comps.

**The 2025 margin compression is the key nuance.** Restaurant margin fell 160bps to 15.5% as 6.1% commodity inflation (beef-led) and 3.7% labor inflation outran modest menu pricing. Restaurant-margin dollars declined 1.1% despite the 9.4% revenue growth, operating income fell to $474.7M, and net income declined 6.5% to $405.6M. Read from the 10-K alone, the trend looks alarming. The Q1 2026 10-Q reframes it: the margin decline narrowed to 30bps, margin dollars grew 10.5%, and net income rose 8.6%. Volume leverage is beginning to absorb commodity pressure without abandoning the value-pricing strategy.

**Cash flow and the balance sheet are healthy.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $730.1M against $388.0M capex, leaving ~$342M FCF (5.8% FCF margin). The company ended 2025 essentially debt-free, drawing just $50M on its new $450M revolver in Q1 2026 to fund franchise acquisitions and buybacks, with ~$398M revolver availability plus $215M cash. Superficially weak liquidity metrics (current ratio 0.5, Altman Z 2.94) are structurally normal for a cash-sales, negative-working-capital restaurant with a $330-449M gift-card float and minimal debt; they are not distress signals. ROE of 28.4% and ROA of 11.7% confirm high capital efficiency.

**Earnings track record is mixed.** The company missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (June '25 -4.4%, Sept '25 -4.3%, Dec '25 -16.7%) before beating narrowly in Q1 2026 (+1.0%). The misses were commodity-driven margin shortfalls, not demand problems; comps and traffic held up throughout. Still, the pattern tempers confidence that estimates fully capture near-term cost volatility.

The financial-health picture is genuinely strong — the debt-free balance sheet, $730M operating cash flow, and 28% ROE are the most reliable positives here — but the recent GAAP earnings decline and margin compression keep this from a top-tier score.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is straightforward and durable: 5-6% store-week growth (~35 company openings targeted for 2026), positive traffic-led comps, and franchise buybacks that add owned-unit economics. The 4.5% traffic comp is the standout: in a low-growth, fragmented category where many operators are fighting flat-to-negative traffic, TXRH is taking share.

Estimate revisions are constructive but not explosive: current-year consensus of $6.43 has risen +2.3% over 90 days (+0.2% over 30 days), current-quarter estimates +5.4% over 90 days, with next-year at $7.63 essentially flat. This is stable-to-modestly-positive momentum, well short of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a re-rating catalyst. The margin recovery from a compressed FY2025 base is the real driver of forward EPS growth: if commodity inflation moderates as management expects into 2H 2026, operating leverage on 5-7% comps produces mid-teens EPS growth.

The primary uncertainty is beef. With only ~65% of remaining costs locked, the margin-recovery timeline is not fully under management's control. The under-inflation pricing strategy means the company cannot simply price its way out. Growth prospects are solid and traffic-led, but the recovery is conditional on the commodity curve cooperating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $190.86, TXRH trades at ~30x trailing EPS ($6.10) and ~28x forward consensus ($6.43 for 2026, ~$7.63 for 2027 implying ~25x). Against a mid-teens forward EPS growth rate, the forward PEG sits around 1.8-2.0, a premium, not a bargain.

Peer context is muddied by dispersion. The restaurant peer median P/E is 38.3x, but that median blends high-growth concepts (CAVA 169.7x, BROS 102.6x, WING 38.3x) with cheaper legacy operators (DPZ 18.0x, EAT 15.5x). TXRH's ~30x sits below the growth-concept cohort and above the mature-brand cohort, which is roughly where a share-gaining, debt-free, industry-leading casual-dining operator should trade. Analyst targets frame the range: mean $198, median $200, low $125, high $238, with the mean and median sitting only 4-5% above the current price. With the stock at ~28x forward and estimate revisions stable rather than surging, consensus targets are more likely fair markers than lagging floors.

The valuation dimension warrants discipline. The multiple is not stretched to the point of structural risk, but it prices in the margin recovery already, and the stock trades near its 52-week high. There is no identified mispricing catalyst: no compressed multiple versus growth, no absolute yield support (1.5% dividend), no cycle-adjusted earnings power dramatically above trailing. What upside exists depends on commodity relief flowing through faster than modeled. We score valuation below neutral: quality justifies the multiple, but the multiple does not offer asymmetry today.

## Competitive Landscape

TXRH's competitive position is improving, which is the trajectory that matters more than absolute level. Becoming the largest U.S. casual-dining chain by revenue is a genuine share-gain milestone in a category with little organic growth. The moat is traffic-driven value leadership: by holding menu increases below inflation, TXRH widens its price/value advantage versus both casual-dining peers and trade-down alternatives, driving +4.5% traffic when much of the field is flat.

The industry-wide beef cost surge is a sector headwind, not a company-specific one, and it creates a competitive asymmetry that favors TXRH. Its scale, high AUVs, and disciplined pricing let it absorb commodity inflation while sub-scale competitors struggle. The same input pressure that compresses TXRH's margin compresses weaker operators' viability, accelerating share migration to the value leader.

The 5-year TSR of 230.8 versus a peer group's 136.5 corroborates durable outperformance. Gross-margin trajectory is the one caution — margins compressed in 2025 and are only now recovering — but the underlying share and traffic trends are strengthening. The concept-diversification effort (Bubba's 33, Jaggers) remains unproven; Bubba's Q1 2026 margin fell to 15.2%, and neither has replicated the core's economics.

## Risk Assessment

**Beef/commodity concentration is the dominant, live risk.** Beef is sourced primarily from four U.S./Canada suppliers, adding supplier concentration on top of price risk.

**New World Screwworm is a current, under-appreciated tail.** As of July 2026, the flesh-eating parasite has spread to 31+ confirmed Texas locations, threatening a U.S. cattle herd already at multi-decade lows. Mizuho has argued there is no near-term cost impact, but this is an evolving supply-side risk that could compound beef inflation into 2H 2026 and is not fully priced.

**The value-pricing double-edge** means margins remain structurally hostage to commodity swings; management cannot fully price-protect without abandoning the positioning that drives its share gains.

**Other risks:** geographic concentration (~21% of company units in Texas and Florida, exposing wage and weather risk); 3-4% guided wage inflation plus state minimum/tipped-wage changes; sub-scale, lower-margin concept diversification; and GLP-1 agonists as a potential structural shift in food consumption, explicitly cited in the 10-K.

**Governance:** CEO compensation rose ~75% to $10.8M in a year when net income fell, a pay-for-performance disconnect worth monitoring, though "comp actually paid" was more moderate and NEO comp-actually-paid went negative (the mechanism working in the other direction). An elevated cadence of officer/director change 8-Ks (at least eight since mid-2025) signals an ongoing post-founder leadership transition to track. Insider Form 4 activity is routine RSU vesting and modest scheduled sales with no open-market buying.

Most of these are recurring sector risks that the market prices. The two that could genuinely move the stock beyond what is priced are the beef cost curve and the screwworm supply shock, both binary-ish and both concentrated in the company's largest market, which is why the risk score sits at neutral rather than higher.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 51.9% is rich versus 29.7% one-year historical volatility (a 22pp spread, 71st percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (51.9% near-term vs 27.2% at six months). Put/call skew is elevated at 4.2% and the one-month tenor shows 9.3% put skew, indicating hedging demand into the August 6 Q2 report. IV has collapsed from 64.5% to 47.1% since mid-June even as the stock rose ~9.6%, and put/call OI fell from 1.29 to 0.43; positioning has grown less bearish as the stock recovered toward its highs. The takeaway: the market is pricing a meaningful move around Q2 earnings (the beef/screwworm cost read-through), but overall positioning is not aggressively bearish. This is consistent with a HOLD: the near-term binary is Q2 margins, and options are appropriately hedged rather than one-sided.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** TXRH is the value leader in casual dining, taking share through disciplined under-inflation pricing that drives industry-leading traffic. The FY2025 margin compression was cyclical and commodity-driven; Q1 2026 already shows the reversal (decline narrowing to 30bps, margin dollars +10.5%). If beef inflation moderates in 2H 2026, operating leverage on 5-7% comps produces mid-teens EPS growth, and a debt-free balance sheet funds openings, franchise buybacks, buybacks, and a growing dividend simultaneously. The bull target rests on ~30x a recovering ~$7.63 2027 EPS, or roughly $225-238 (the high analyst target), as consensus revises up on confirmed margin recovery.

**Bear case.** The screwworm outbreak and structurally short cattle supply keep beef inflation elevated or push it higher, stalling the margin recovery the market has already priced at ~28x forward. The value-pricing strategy caps the company's ability to offset, and another string of margin-driven misses (as in three of the last four quarters) triggers multiple compression on a stock near its 52-week high. A de-rating toward the mid-teens/low-20s P/E that mature casual-dining peers (DPZ 18x, EAT 15.5x) command implies downside to the $130-150 range; the $125 low analyst target reflects this scenario.

**Base case.** Traffic-led comps hold in the 5-7% range, commodity inflation stays elevated but manageable, and EPS grows toward $6.43 in 2026 and ~$7.60 in 2027. At a ~28x forward multiple, that supports a base value around $200-210, roughly in line with the current price plus the dividend, implying total return driven mostly by earnings growth rather than re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings on August 6, 2026**, the decisive near-term read on whether the beef-cost recovery is holding.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 restaurant margin decline continues to narrow or turns positive, confirming the commodity recovery, alongside comps sustained above 5%.
- Estimate revisions accelerate to +5% or more over 30 days on the current year (versus the current stable +0.2%/30d), signaling consensus catching up to a faster recovery.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $160s (~25x forward, near the 52-week low) that restores margin of safety without any fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Screwworm/beef supply disruption materially worsens, pushing 2026 commodity inflation above the 6-7% guide and forcing restaurant margins back below FY2025's 15.5%.
- Two or more consecutive quarters of margin-driven EPS misses combined with comps decelerating below 3%, indicating the value strategy is no longer offsetting cost pressure.
- Multiple compression accompanied by a dividend/buyback pullback or a governance escalation beyond the current transition cadence.

**From the current $190.86:** base case ~$205 (+7% plus 1.5% yield, 12-month), bull case ~$230 (+21%), bear case ~$140 (-27%). The roughly symmetric-to-slightly-favorable skew, with a live binary cost risk into August, supports holding rather than adding at current levels.

## Conclusion

Texas Roadhouse is a genuinely excellent business: the value leader and now revenue leader in U.S. casual dining, debt-free, cash-generative, and gaining share through a coherent under-inflation pricing strategy that drives industry-leading traffic. The FY2025 margin compression that spooked earnings has reversed in Q1 2026, and the fundamentals are improving, not deteriorating. The trajectory of scores — financial health strengthening, valuation and overall recovering — reflects a fundamentally healthy franchise.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of a full valuation (~28x forward, near the 52-week high, with no identified mispricing catalyst and stable-not-surging estimates) and a specific, live downside driver in beef costs and the Texas screwworm supply threat. The quality justifies the multiple; it does not create asymmetry at today's price. We would upgrade to BUY on confirmed margin recovery at the August 6 print or on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $160s. For existing holders, the growth thesis remains intact and the business deserves patience; for new capital, the risk/reward is better entered on weakness than chased near the highs.
