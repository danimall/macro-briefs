**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CoreCivic is the largest U.S. owner of partnership correctional and detention facilities, operating ~80,000 beds across 44 Safety facilities, 20 reentry centers, and 5 leased properties. The investment story is a federal immigration tailwind: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025) appropriated $45B for adult and family detention capacity through September 2029, and federal customers rose to 58% of revenue in Q1 2026 from 49% a year earlier. The company reactivated five idle facilities (Dilley, California City, Diamondback, Midwest/Leavenworth, West Tennessee) carrying ~$500M of combined annual revenue capacity, most of which was still ramping in Q1. The $148M Clinical Solutions Pharmacy acquisition (closed April 1) adds $215-230M of recurring, less politically exposed revenue and signals deliberate diversification.

The growth is real but already cooling, which the market does not appear to be pricing. The single most important disclosure in the Q1 10-Q is that nationwide ICE detention peaked near 70,800 in late January 2026 and fell ~10,500 by early April on a DHS-centered government shutdown, a leadership reorganization, and redeployment of ICE agents to TSA checkpoints. CoreCivic's own ICE population fell ~3,000 over that window. Management expects demand to "ultimately increase" but "cannot predict how quickly population growth will resume." Q1's +25.8% revenue print likely marks a near-term high-water mark in momentum, with Q2 at risk of sequential softness. Against this, estimates are falling — current-quarter consensus has been cut 15% over 90 days, next-quarter 10.4% — which contradicts the headline growth narrative.

The stock has appreciated 38% in three months to $28.93, now sitting at the top of the $28-32 analyst target range, on a ~27x trailing P/E and ~18x forward consensus EPS of $1.60. The fundamentals justify a holding position: untapped operating leverage in the under-occupied reactivated facilities provides a genuine 12-month appreciation path if ICE demand re-accelerates. But the policy concentration, rising leverage (debt up to $1.41B, leverage-ratio step-up triggered), no insider buying despite the run, and a valuation that has caught up to the thesis cap the case at HOLD rather than BUY. The asymmetry that existed at $16-20 has largely been realized.

## Company Overview

CoreCivic (formerly Corrections Corporation of America; revoked REIT status in 2021) is a Maryland corporation operating three segments:

- **CoreCivic Safety (~92% of segment NOI):** 44 correctional/detention facilities, ~68,000 design beds, 40 owned or long-term leased. Includes TransCor prisoner transport. This segment houses the ICE exposure.
- **CoreCivic Community (~5% of segment NOI):** 20 residential reentry centers (~4,000 beds) plus electronic monitoring and case management.
- **CoreCivic Properties (~3% of segment NOI):** 5 properties (~8,000 beds) leased to government agencies.

The competitive moat is ownership of the majority of beds it operates, an asset base that is hard to replicate given the political difficulty of permitting and building new prisons. Management cites a structurally constrained supply of correctional beds as the reason renewal rates stay high. The principal competitor is GEO Group; together they form a duopoly serving government customers — ICE, the U.S. Marshals Service, the Bureau of Prisons, states, and localities.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY2025 revenue was ~$2.21B, up ~12.7% YoY. Q1 2026 revenue reached $614.7M, +25.8% from $488.6M. Q1 net income was $37.9M ($0.38 diluted EPS), up from $25.1M ($0.23), a 51% increase. Revenue per compensated man-day rose to $116.53 (Safety: $118.18) from $102.71, on per-diem increases plus mix shift toward higher-rate federal contracts. Average occupancy improved 2.6 points YoY to 79.6%. The operating margin on a per-man-day basis was 24.0%, with Safety operating income per man-day at $27.96.

**The reactivation story and operating leverage.** Five idle facilities are the growth engine: Dilley (~$180M est. annual revenue), California City (~$130M), Diamondback (~$100M), Midwest/Leavenworth (~$60M), and West Tennessee (~$30M). These were materially under-occupied in Q1 — Midwest at 94 of 1,033 beds (9%), Diamondback at 735 of 2,160 (34%), California City at 1,817 of 2,560 (71%). If ICE demand resumes, margins should expand as fixed costs are absorbed across higher utilization. This untapped leverage is the core of the constructive 12-month case.

**Guidance.** On the Q1 call, management guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA to $453.8M-$461.8M, plus $215M-$230M of new revenue from CSP. The earnings track record supports modest confidence: three beats in the last four quarters (Q1 2026 +36.0%, Q4 2025 +39.9%, Q2 2025 +78.2%), with one miss (Q3 2025 -11.3%).

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $13.8M from $44.5M, driven by a $78.2M unfavorable working-capital swing as accounts receivable climbed to $479.8M, partly from government-shutdown payment delays. This appears timing-related rather than structural — Normalized FFO rose to $65.1M from $49.7M. Federal customers owe back-payment with interest. The AR balloon bears monitoring but is not a red flag at this stage.

Total debt rose to $1.41B from $1.23B at YE2025 on $310M of revolver draws for growth, acquisitions, and buybacks. Weighted average rate is 7.3%, weighted average maturity 3.6 years, with no maturities until October 2027 ($238.5M of 4.75% notes), then $500M of 8.25% notes in April 2029. Cash was $209.7M with $131.3M available on the revolver (expanded from $275M to $575M in December 2025; a $100M incremental term loan added in April 2026). The company is in compliance with its ≤4.50x leverage covenant but reached a level that triggered a +0.25% rate step-up at YE2025, and $425M sits on the revolver at 7.6% floating. The Altman Z-Score of 1.83 places it in the grey zone — typical for a capital-intensive operator, but leaving limited cushion given the leverage trajectory. D/E is 1.32, ROA 3.6%, ROE 8.3%, net margin 5.3%.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks have been aggressive: 2.3M shares for $44.7M in Q1 2026 (~$19.08/share); 28.1M shares retired for $444.2M (~$15.82 average) since 2022, taking the count from ~109.9M (YE2024) to 98.9M. The total authorization was raised to $700M (November 2025) with $255.8M remaining. No dividend currently.

## Growth Analysis

The structural demand driver is the strongest growth signal: OBBBA's detention appropriation (of $75B total to ICE) is a multi-year funding floor for the duopoly. CoreCivic and GEO are the obvious beneficiaries given the constrained supply of beds. CoreCivic's five reactivated facilities plus the Farmville acquisition (July 2025, 736 beds, ICE IGSA to March 2029) position it to capture incremental demand at ~3-6 month reactivation lead times.

The CSP pharmacy acquisition is a deliberate de-risking move, pivoting capital into recurring correctional pharmacy revenue (600+ facilities, 28 states) that is less politically exposed than ICE detention.

The countervailing signal is that growth is cooling in real time. The ICE population decline directly tempers the Q1 momentum. Estimate revisions corroborate the deceleration: current-year consensus EPS has been cut 1.7%. These are not the rising estimates that accompany durable growth; the Street is marking down near-term assumptions even as the multi-year policy backdrop stays favorable. The growth thesis therefore hinges on re-acceleration of ICE utilization that management explicitly cannot time. I weight the structural funding and untapped operating leverage as genuine positives against the near-term population decline and falling estimates, arriving at a moderately positive growth score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.93, CoreCivic trades at ~27x trailing EPS of $1.08 and ~18x forward consensus EPS of $1.60. The peer set in Diversified Support Services shows a median P/E of 37.3 (UNF 37.3, LQDT 40.8), but that median is distorted by services businesses with different economics; the cleaner comparison is GEO Group, the direct duopoly peer, which trades on a similar policy-driven multiple. On EV/EBITDA, the ~$2.9B equity plus $1.41B total debt less $209.7M cash (~$1.20B net debt) against guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$458M implies roughly 9x EV/EBITDA, a full multiple for a leveraged, single-customer-concentration business.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the valuation here. ICE demand surged in 2025, but the most recent data shows that demand declining and estimates falling rather than rising, so the conditions that justify valuing on normalized rather than trailing earnings (rising estimates, accelerating revenue with expanding margins) are not present. The stock now sits at the very top of the $28-32 analyst target range, with a $29.80 mean and $30.00 median offering ~4% to the median. On a cheap-multiple business the principle that analyst targets are floors would apply, but here estimates are being revised down, not up, which removes the basis for treating consensus as stale-low.

The valuation score is below average because the multiple has caught up to and arguably overshot the near-term fundamentals. The asymmetry that existed at $16-20 (where buybacks were executed at ~$15.82) has largely been realized. The score is not a deep value-trap rating because the operating-leverage upside remains genuine if ICE re-accelerates; it reflects that today's price already discounts a substantial portion of that re-acceleration while the most recent data points the other way.

## Competitive Landscape

CoreCivic's position is structurally advantaged within its niche. It owns most of its beds, and the political difficulty of permitting new prison capacity means the existing asset base is difficult to replicate. The five idle facilities (~7,000 beds, $149.8M net carrying value) function as a call option on further ICE demand, reactivatable in 3-6 months. The duopoly structure with GEO limits direct competition, and state/county demand (overcrowding, aging public infrastructure, public-sector staffing shortages) is a secondary tailwind.

The competitive risk is that the customer can bypass CoreCivic's beds entirely. Management explicitly warns ICE is using military bases (including Guantanamo), soft-sided facilities, BOP facilities, state and local facilities, international options, and is considering warehouse-based detention. Against a near-monopsony government customer, pricing power is constrained, and the ICE population decline shows the customer can redeploy resources away from CoreCivic capacity quickly. The trajectory is improving on the asset-base and reactivation optionality but is exposed to a customer that can substitute supply, which keeps the score moderate rather than high.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is policy concentration: the federal growth thesis rests on Trump-administration executive orders plus OBBBA, which can reverse with an administration change. The prior whiplash is instructive — ICE terminated the Dilley IGSA in 2024, then reinstated it in 2025.

Near-term, the ICE population decline in CoreCivic's care is the most material datapoint and is not fully reflected in the trailing financials. Q2 could show sequential softness. The bypass risk (military bases, soft-sided, warehouse detention) compounds this.

Litigation exposure is material and partly unestimable: the California City class action (provisional class certified, ICE ordered to ensure medical care, accommodations, and recreation — injunctive relief could hurt facility economics), the Otay Mesa forced-labor class action (nationwide anti-trafficking class certified, loss unestimable), a DOJ CRIPA investigation of Trousdale Turner, and the Elizabeth Detention Center contract on short-term extensions through May 31, 2026 with the site lease expiring June 30, 2027 and no renewal assurance. A $27.8M Crossroads jury verdict was settled in Q1 (insured, not material).

Leverage is creeping up while management simultaneously buys back stock and makes acquisitions — an aggressive posture that amplifies both upside and downside. Labor is ~63% of operating expense, a structural cost pressure. The risk score is low because several of these are specific, partly binary catalysts (litigation rulings, contract non-renewal, an ICE policy reversal) that the current full valuation does not appear to discount.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 70.2% against a 1-year historical vol of 37.9% — rich, with IV in the 80th percentile of the current chain and an implied move of ±15.5% on the nearest expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (70.2% near-term vs 59.9% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the cooling ICE population and the Q2 earnings setup. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-9.4%), so positioning is not aggressively bearish, but the elevated IV and backwardation indicate the market expects a binary near-term move. The data point showing ATM IV spiking to 744.9% intraday is a thin-options artifact and is treated as non-informative. Option pricing corroborates near-term uncertainty around the next print and tempers conviction for a fresh long at this level.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($35-38).** ICE detention re-accelerates after the shutdown disruption resolves, filling the under-occupied reactivated facilities and driving the operating leverage management has built. FY2026 EBITDA lands at the top of the guide, CSP adds recurring revenue, and OBBBA funding underwrites multi-year demand. Continued buybacks at a falling share count compound EPS. At ~10x a re-accelerating ~$480M EBITDA, the equity re-rates toward $35-38.

**Bear case ($18-20).** The ICE population decline persists or deepens as agents stay redeployed and ICE substitutes military bases, soft-sided, and warehouse detention. An adverse California City or Otay Mesa ruling impairs facility economics; Elizabeth Detention Center is not renewed. Estimates continue falling, and the leveraged balance sheet (7.6% floating revolver, Z-score 1.83) amplifies the de-rating. A 2028 administration change becomes a tail overhang. The stock retraces toward its prior trading range of $18-20, roughly where it spent late 2025.

The probability-weighted outcome sits closer to the current price than to either tail, which is the essence of the HOLD. The structural funding and operating-leverage optionality are real, but the near-term population decline, falling estimates, and a valuation that has run to the top of the target range remove the asymmetry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis for holding rather than exiting is specific: the five reactivated facilities carry ~$500M of incremental annual revenue capacity and were materially under-occupied in Q1. As OBBBA detention funding flows and ICE utilization normalizes off the shutdown trough, occupancy of these fixed-cost facilities should drive margin expansion and EPS growth over the next 12 months. This is a concrete, monitorable operating-leverage path to appreciation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (early August) is the critical checkpoint — it will reveal whether the ICE population decline translated into sequential revenue/occupancy softness or whether demand re-accelerated.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 occupancy at the reactivated facilities (Diamondback, Midwest, California City) shows clear sequential improvement, with CoreCivic's ICE population stabilizing or recovering toward the late-January level.
- Forward EPS estimates reverse from falling to rising (current-quarter and next-quarter revisions turn positive), confirming re-acceleration rather than deceleration.
- A pullback toward $22-24 that restores asymmetric upside to the $30+ target while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CoreCivic's ICE population declines further in Q2, or ICE materially shifts detention to bypass capacity (military bases, warehouse detention), with management guiding EBITDA below the $453.8M floor.
- An adverse California City or Otay Mesa ruling that impairs facility economics, or non-renewal of Elizabeth Detention Center.
- Leverage breaches comfort (revolver utilization rising further with the leverage ratio approaching the 4.50x covenant) while buybacks continue.

## Conclusion

CoreCivic owns a genuinely scarce asset base and is the prime beneficiary of a multi-year federal detention appropriation, with real operating leverage embedded in under-occupied reactivated facilities and a CSP acquisition that diversifies away from pure ICE exposure. Those are the reasons to hold. The reasons not to add at $28.93 are equally concrete: the ICE population that drives the thesis fell in CoreCivic's care between late January and late April, consensus estimates are being cut, the stock has run to the top of the analyst target range, option markets are pricing imminent stress, and there is no insider buying despite the run (the only recurring genuine sale is the CAO's programmatic monthly disposition). The valuation has caught up to the thesis, and the near-term data points the wrong way. Hold for the operating-leverage re-acceleration, monitor the Q2 print closely, and require either a fundamental re-acceleration or a meaningful pullback before upgrading.
