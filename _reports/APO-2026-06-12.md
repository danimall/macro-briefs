**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Apollo is two businesses fused into one ticker: a capital-light alternative asset manager that earns recurring fee-related earnings (FRE), and Athene, a capital-intensive annuity writer that earns spread-related earnings (SRE). The asset-management half is performing strongly — FRE rose 30.2% YoY to $728M in Q1 2026 on a 23.6% jump in management fees and a 59.7% surge in capital-solutions fees, and total AUM reached $1.03 trillion, up 9.4% in the quarter. The Athene half is the soft spot. SRE fell 10.6% to $719M as net investment spread compressed 31bp to 1.34%, and the alternative net investment earned rate collapsed from 10.08% to 5.79% on tough 2025 comparables plus an Atlas asset impairment. Because SRE is roughly half of segment income, this compression caps the consolidated growth rate even as the fee engine accelerates.

The headline GAAP loss of $(3.27)/share is a distraction. It is driven almost entirely by a $1.7B non-cash full valuation allowance against Bermuda deferred tax assets, taken after Apollo revoked ACRA's Bermuda CIT election once the OECD exempted U.S.-parented groups from Pillar Two on January 5, 2026. Economically this is a positive — it signals lower future cash taxes — and adjusted net income (ANI) was up 8.0% to $1,208M ($1.94/share). Investors should anchor on segment metrics, not the consolidated GAAP statements, which are ~96% insurance assets and produce meaningless leverage and tax-rate optics.

The stock trades at ~12.6x forward earnings against an $8.88 current-year and $10.61 next-year consensus, with a $4.0B buyback authorization and a recently raised dividend. The setup is reasonable but not compelling. The fee engine justifies a premium; the spread engine is mid-correction; private-credit redemption pressure is now management's own acknowledged headwind; and a new IRS NOPA on 2019–2021 income, active Epstein-related securities class actions, and the Up-C derivative suit are unresolved overhangs. With the same HOLD having been issued four times running, the bar to either upgrade or downgrade is higher. We maintain HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, but the conviction rests on the spread engine finding a trough rather than on a near-term catalyst.

## Company Overview

Apollo operates through three reportable segments:

1. **Asset Management** — credit ($834B AUM) and equity ($192B AUM) strategies, earning FRE. Credit is the engine, built on four pillars: direct origination, asset-backed, multi-credit, and opportunistic credit. Roughly 68% of fund investments are valued by models or comparables rather than market quotes.
2. **Retirement Services (Athene)** — a leading annuity and retirement-savings writer with $300.3B of net invested assets, earning SRE by sourcing long-dated liabilities and investing in higher-yielding, complexity-premium assets.
3. **Principal Investing** — realized performance fees plus balance-sheet investment income; inherently volatile (PII was $75M in Q1 vs $14M a year earlier).

The flywheel is the franchise: Athene's growth generates fee-paying AUM for the asset manager (Apollo managed $400.9B on behalf of Athene and $125.9B on behalf of Athora at quarter-end), while Apollo's origination platforms source assets for Athene's portfolio. This vertical integration is the core competitive advantage and also the core concentration risk, because much of Athene's investment book sits in Apollo-affiliated, model-valued structures.

The consolidated balance sheet ($467.5B total assets) is ~96% insurance assets, so standard leverage and liquidity ratios computed on the consolidated entity are not informative. Management runs the company on FRE, SRE, and ANI, and so should investors.

## Financial Analysis

**Segment earnings, Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025:**
- **Segment Income: $1,522M, +10.5%**
- **Adjusted Net Income: $1,208M, +8.0%** ($1.94/share on 623.8M ANI shares)
- **FRE: $728M, +30.2%** — management fees +23.6% to $952M, capital solutions fees +59.7% to $246M, aided by the Bridge acquisition. This is the recurring, high-quality stream, and it is accelerating.
- **SRE: $719M, −10.6%** — net investment spread −31bp to 1.34% as cost of funds rose 33bp to 3.79% on higher new-business rates and growth. The alternative net investment earned rate fell from 10.08% to 5.79%, reflecting outsized 2025 marks (MidCap, Redding Ridge, Wheels) against a 2026 Atlas impairment.
- **PII: $75M vs $14M** — realized performance fees from a portfolio sale and Fund X.

The earnings surprise record supports modest confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one near-line miss in the trailing four quarters (Q4 2025 +19.3%, Q3 2025 +12.4%, Q2 2025 +3.1%, Q1 2026 −0.5%). The slight Q1 miss coincides with the spread compression, which is the appropriate place to focus.

**GAAP optics vs. economic reality.** The GAAP net loss to common of $(1,930)M / $(3.27)/share is almost entirely the Bermuda DTA writedown plus mark-to-market noise on Athene's portfolio from rising rates. The ~599% effective tax rate confirms the distortion. The DTA charge is economically favorable: the DTA built to offset Pillar Two tax became worthless once that tax exposure was removed. Management states this carries "favorable implications for the Company's overall tax position over the longer term." A naive screen sees a giant loss; the cash-tax reality is better.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Athene capitalization is strong: U.S. RBC 436%, Bermuda RBC 454%, consolidated 441% (12/31/25), all up YoY. Asset-management recourse debt is only $6.4B; Athene debt $7.8B; nothing drawn on $5.1B of credit facilities; the AGM net-leverage covenant of ≤4.0x has ample headroom. Unrestricted cash is $21.4B. Total equity fell to $39.5B from $42.5B, driven by the DTA charge and a $2.7B negative AOCI swing from rate-driven AFS marks.

**Capital allocation.** The buyback authorization was raised to $4.0B in February 2026, with $632M repurchased in Q1. The common dividend was raised to $0.5625/quarter. Athene's organic net flows were $39.7B over the trailing twelve months, so the underlying growth engine is intact even as the earned rate on the invested assets compresses.

A built-in Q2 tailwind partly offsets the alt-income softness: AP Grange called its ABS debt, producing a $673M GAAP gain ($458M net of NCI) next quarter, and the ~$9B ARI commercial-mortgage portfolio purchase closed April 24.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated. FRE growth is durable and high-quality, and the AUM trajectory reflects both organic flows and the inorganic Athora–PIC step-up ($65.3B of credit AUM at one stroke). Management frames private credit as a ~$40T, largely investment-grade addressable market and positions Apollo's origination platforms as the differentiator. The ~60% YoY growth in capital-solutions fees confirms the origination machine is monetizing.

The drag is the retirement-services spread. SRE fell despite asset growth because the earned rate on alternatives reverted sharply. This is not a structural impairment of the model, but it does mean consolidated growth will be capped until the alt earned rate stabilizes. The +8% ANI growth in Q1 is the blended result, and consensus estimate revisions reflect this two-sided picture: current-quarter EPS revised +1.5% over 30 days but −1.9% over 90 days; current-year revised +0.2% / −4.0%; next-year −0.1% / −2.4%. The 30-day revisions have firmed while the 90-day trend is still negative, consistent with the FRE acceleration beginning to offset the SRE markdown in analysts' models. This is a stabilizing, not inflecting, revision picture.

The most important forward signal in the filing is management's own acknowledgment of "increased redemption requests within perpetual non-traded BDC structures across the industry, resulting in lower growth." Apollo Debt Solutions (~$30B) posted a 0% net return in Q1 2026 vs +2% a year earlier. This is the sector-wide private-credit cooling hitting Apollo's retail-distribution growth vector directly. The long-term wealth-channel opportunity is plausibly intact across cycles, but near-term BDC growth is impaired.

## Valuation Assessment

At $133.91, APO trades at ~12.6x forward earnings (consensus $8.88 current-year, $10.61 next-year). Trailing P/E of 84x is meaningless given the GAAP loss quarter; forward and ANI-based metrics are the right lens.

The peer set the screen offers (OWL at 82x, KLAR at negative earnings) is not a usable comparable group, since neither is an insurance-led alternative manager. The relevant cohort is Blackstone, KKR, and Ares. Against those, Apollo's ~12.6x forward multiple reflects a discount that the market applies for two reasons: the SRE volatility introduced by the insurance overhang (a +100bp rate shift would reduce pre-tax income by $4.7B in mark-to-market terms, up from $4.2B at year-end on longer-duration purchases), and the related-party/Level-3 valuation concentration in Athene's book. A pure-play manager with the same FRE growth would command a higher multiple; Apollo trades cheaper precisely because the captive insurer that fuels the flywheel also injects spread and mark volatility.

The valuation is reasonable rather than mispriced. The case for a higher multiple rests on FRE durability and the structural tax benefit from the Bermuda/Pillar Two reversal flowing through to lower cash taxes over time. The case against is that consensus current-year estimates have been cut 4% over 90 days and the SRE engine is mid-correction, so the "E" in the forward P/E carries downside risk if the alt earned rate stays depressed. We do not see a specific, falsifiable mispricing that would justify a valuation score above the mid-point. Analyst targets span $125 (low) to $173 (high), mean $150.4, median $146 — implying ~9% upside to the median from current levels, which is consistent with a hold rather than a conviction long.

## Competitive Landscape

Apollo sits among the big four alternatives managers but is uniquely insurance-led. Athene provides a permanent, growing, captive capital base that pure-play peers lack, making FRE and SRE structurally more durable than carry-dependent earnings. The origination platforms (Atlas, MidCap, Redding Ridge, Wheels, PK Air/Skylign) are the differentiator in the private-credit market, allowing Apollo to source proprietary, complexity-premium assets rather than competing on spread in syndicated markets.

The competitive trajectory is improving on the institutional and origination side and pressured on the retail side. The FRE and capital-solutions growth indicates share gains in origination-led credit. The redemption pressure in non-traded BDCs is an industry-wide phenomenon that Apollo shares with peers; it is not Apollo-specific erosion, but it does cap a growth vector the market had been pricing optimistically across the sector.

Apollo's institutional and insurance-anchored funding base is a relative advantage when wealth-channel flows soften, which is why we score competitive position moderately above neutral despite the BDC headwind.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Spread compression in Retirement Services.** SRE fell despite asset growth. Alternatives income is volatile and was a 2025 tailwind that reversed. If the alt earned rate stays near 5.8% and cost of funds keeps rising, roughly half of segment income stays under pressure. This is the central near-term risk.

2. **Athora concentration and funding.** Athene's investment in Athora more than doubled to $3.15B in Q1 to finance Athora's PIC acquisition. Athora is now one of Athene's largest single-issuer exposures, and the relationship increases reliance on a related, less-liquid European affiliate. Note the attribution nuance: Apollo does not control Athora (it holds ~16% AAM voting power plus ~10% via Athene), so the PIC AUM is advised, not consolidated, but the credit exposure is real.

3. **Private-credit redemption pressure.** Management's explicit acknowledgment of rising non-traded BDC redemptions, plus ADS's 0% Q1 return, is the most important forward growth signal. This connects to broader cooling in the retail/perpetual private-credit complex as redemptions rise and valuation/liquidity scrutiny intensifies.

4. **Single-issuer / Level-3 concentration.** Athene holds >10%-of-equity positions in AP Grange ($5.7B), Atlas ($3.3B), Fox Hedge, multiple AMAPS vehicles, and Athora — many Apollo-affiliated, model-valued. The Atlas impairment that dented Q1 alt income illustrates the risk.

5. **IRS NOPA (new, post-quarter).** On April 13, 2026, Apollo received a draft Notice of Proposed Adjustment proposing a significant increase to taxable income for 2019–2021, with 2022–2023 under exam on the same issue. Apollo disagrees and cannot estimate the loss, but states the amount "could be material." This is a new, unquantified risk surfacing after the quarter.

6. **Litigation overhang.** Active Epstein-related securities class actions (Feldman, Perez) against AGM, Rowan, and Black; the Anguilla derivative suit over $570M Up-C elimination payments to founders (motion to dismiss denied, Special Litigation Committee review stayed to May 30, 2026); a Moelis-style challenge to the founder stockholders' agreement; and German tax assessments on fund reorganizations.

7. **Mandatory Convertible dilution.** 28.75M shares of 6.75% Series A Mandatory Convertible (~$1.4B) convert into 14.6M–17.5M common shares on July 31, 2026. ANI share count already reflects ~14.6M, but modelers should ensure the count steps up this summer.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning has turned more cautious. Between May 19 and June 3, ATM implied volatility rose from 43.1% to 53.0% (+10pp) while the stock fell 6.1%, and the put/call open-interest ratio rose from 0.83 to 1.34. Rising IV alongside a falling price and put accumulation indicates the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty rather than complacency. Short interest is modest at 5.7% of float with 8.8 days to cover, so this is hedging and caution, not a crowded directional bet. The IV richening is consistent with the spread-compression and litigation overhangs and argues against treating the recent dip as a clean entry, since the market is paying up for protection.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** FRE growth is durable and the AUM base crossed $1T, so the high-quality recurring earnings stream is compounding. The Bermuda/Pillar Two reversal lowers future cash taxes. Q2 carries a built-in $458M net AP Grange gain plus the closed $9B ARI portfolio. Athene's $39.7B trailing organic net flows show the growth engine is intact and the spread compression is a comp/mark phenomenon that mean-reverts as 2025's outsized alt marks roll off. At ~12.6x forward earnings with a $4.0B buyback and rising dividend, the stock re-rates toward the peer cohort as the spread trough forms. Bull target ~$165 (~14x next-year ANI as SRE recovers and FRE durability is rewarded).

**Bear Case.** The alt earned rate stays depressed near 5.8%, cost of funds keeps rising, and SRE — half of segment income — remains under pressure through 2026, capping consolidated growth. Private-credit redemptions accelerate, impairing the retail-distribution growth vector and forcing further estimate cuts beyond the 4% current-year reduction already booked. The IRS NOPA crystallizes a material liability, and the securities litigation produces a settlement. Rate volatility drives further AOCI erosion and Level-3 mark uncertainty. Bear target ~$110 (~10x a downward-revised current-year ANI, near the recent February–March trading lows), roughly −18% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The fee engine supports a constructive medium-term view, but the spread engine and the redemption/litigation overhangs prevent a higher-conviction call until the SRE trough is confirmed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of SRE stabilization or recovery — alternative net investment earned rate recovering toward the 7–9% range and net investment spread holding at or above 1.34%.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turning decisively positive over a 90-day window (currently −4.0%), confirming the FRE acceleration is outpacing the SRE drag.
- Resolution or clear quantification of the IRS NOPA at an immaterial level.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Acceleration of non-traded BDC redemptions producing negative net flows at ADS or a broader Athene flow reversal.
- A material adverse IRS NOPA determination or an adverse securities-litigation settlement.
- SRE declining a further 10%+ YoY with the alt earned rate failing to recover above ~6%, signaling structural rather than cyclical compression.
- Athene RBC ratios deteriorating meaningfully below current ~440% levels.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (the AP Grange gain and ARI portfolio land here, alongside the July 31 mandatory-convertible conversion), plus the IRS NOPA process and the Special Litigation Committee outcome after the May 30 stay.

## Conclusion

Apollo is a high-quality franchise trading at a reasonable forward multiple, but it is at an awkward point in its cycle: the asset-management engine is accelerating while the insurance spread engine is correcting, and the net is single-digit ANI growth rather than the double-digit compounding the bull case requires. The GAAP loss masks a structural tax benefit, and the balance sheet and capital return are sound. What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of unresolved spread compression, management's own acknowledgment of private-credit redemption pressure, and three distinct unquantified overhangs (IRS NOPA, securities litigation, Up-C derivative suit). The same HOLD has now been issued four times, and the bar to change it should be evidence — specifically, an SRE trough and positive estimate-revision turn — not price action. At ~12.6x forward with ~9% upside to the analyst median and a defined −18% bear path, the risk/reward is balanced. We maintain HOLD and would revisit toward BUY on confirmation the spread engine has bottomed, or on a pullback that widens the asymmetry below the recent low-$110s lows.
