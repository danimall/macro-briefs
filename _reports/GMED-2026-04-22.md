## Executive Summary

Globus Medical has completed a multi-year transformation from a mid-cap spine implant pure play into one of the largest musculoskeletal device companies globally, with ~$2.9B in revenue and a comprehensive platform spanning spine, trauma, joints, robotics, and — following the April 2025 Nevro acquisition — spinal cord stimulation. FY2025 represents the first clean year of post-NuVasive financial results, with operating margins expanding to 16.3%, FCF reaching $589M (20% margin), and EPS of $3.92 up from $0.75. The company carries zero debt, $526M in cash, and an undrawn $400M revolver.

The stock trades at approximately 21x FY2026E EPS of $4.47, a 55% discount to the Health Care Equipment peer median P/E of 46.6x. This discount reflects legitimate concerns: the company is simultaneously integrating two major acquisitions, experienced a CEO transition in mid-2025, operates under a controlled-company governance structure with 66.1% voting concentration, and carries an open FDA warning letter on its flagship robotics platform. However, the magnitude of the discount overstates these risks given the demonstrated margin recovery, consistent earnings beats (three of the last four quarters), and upward estimate revisions (+6.8% for FY2026E over the past 90 days).

At current prices, the base case path to fair value delivers 15-20% upside with limited downside support from the fortress balance sheet and ongoing $500M buyback authorization. The key variable is whether the dual integration can sustain the margin trajectory visible in FY2025; we believe the evidence favors it.

## Company Overview

Globus Medical, headquartered in Audubon, Pennsylvania, develops and commercializes musculoskeletal healthcare solutions across 65 countries. The company operates through two segments:

**Musculoskeletal Solutions (~95% of revenue, $2.80B):** Spinal implants, orthopedic trauma products, hip and knee arthroplasty, biologics/allografts, spinal cord stimulation therapy (via Nevro's Senza® HFX™ platform), and intraoperative neuromonitoring services. This segment was assembled through the 2023 NuVasive merger ($2.6B) and 2025 Nevro acquisition ($253M).

**Enabling Technologies (~5% of revenue, $141M):** The ExcelsiusGPS® robotic guidance and navigation system, Excelsius3D™ imaging, ExcelsiusFlex® for total knee, ExcelsiusXR® extended reality headset (launched 2025), and Surgimap® surgical planning software. This platform is the company's key differentiator and the front door for implant pull-through.

The company holds 3,374 U.S. patents and operates manufacturing in Pennsylvania, Ohio, Texas, Massachusetts, and Costa Rica. Founded in 2003, GMED went public in 2012.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Margin Recovery

FY2025 revenue of $2.94B grew 16.7% YoY, driven by: (1) Nevro's $293.6M contribution over ~9 months, (2) $126M in organic MSK growth in the U.S., and (3) $50M in European/Middle East expansion. Domestic revenue of $2.37B grew 18.4% while international revenue of $571M grew 10.0%.

FY2023 and FY2024 margins were severely distorted by NuVasive purchase accounting — $72M and $215M in inventory fair value step-up amortization, plus $51M and $119M in intangible amortization. This depressed gross margins to 65.0% and 58.9% respectively, making the business appear structurally less profitable than it is. FY2025 gross margins recovered to 67.4% with step-up costs largely flushed, and operating margins expanded to 16.3% from 6.6%.

Adjusted for the $118M Nevro bargain purchase gain, net income was approximately $420M, or ~$3.07 per diluted share. Reported EPS of $3.92 includes this non-recurring item, but even the adjusted figure represents a substantial improvement from $0.75 in FY2024.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Free cash flow of $589M (20.0% margin) on $753M in operating cash flow and $165M in capex represents high-quality earnings generation. OCF exceeds reported net income even excluding the bargain purchase gain, reflecting strong working capital management. The company funded the $253M Nevro acquisition, repaid $450M in NuVasive convertible notes, and repurchased $301M in shares, yet ended FY2025 with $526M in cash and zero debt.

The Altman Z-Score of 6.19 places GMED firmly in the safe zone. Current ratio of 4.26x and debt-to-equity of 0.16x provide extraordinary financial flexibility. The $400M undrawn revolver adds further capacity. Goodwill of $1.44B and intangible assets of $745M represent 48% of total assets — elevated but typical for a serial acquirer in medtech.

### Capital Allocation

Management's capital allocation priorities are clear: internal R&D ($147M, 5.0% of sales), tuck-in acquisitions, and aggressive share repurchases. The $500M buyback authorization (with $390M remaining) at an average purchase price of $70.06 in FY2025 represents accretive capital return at valuations well below current prices. No dividend is paid; the company prioritizes growth reinvestment.

One concern: R&D spend declining from 6.5% to 5.0% of revenue. While partially explained by NuVasive integration efficiencies, a medtech company competing against Medtronic and Stryker — both of which spend materially more on R&D as a percentage of revenue — needs to monitor this carefully.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Trajectory

U.S. MSK organic growth of $126M on a base of approximately $1.87B (excluding Nevro and Enabling Tech) implies organic growth in the mid-single-digit range, consistent with the broader spine and trauma markets. Management guided FY2026 revenue to $3.18-3.22B, implying 8-10% growth, with Nevro contributing a full-year comparison and continued organic expansion.

### Estimate Revisions

FY2026 EPS consensus has risen 6.8% over the past 90 days (from $4.19 to $4.47), and FY2027 estimates are up 8.6% (from $4.57 to $4.96). Revisions have stabilized over the past 30 days (flat), suggesting the post-Q4 earnings upgrade cycle has been fully digested. This positive revision momentum — combined with the Q4 earnings beat of 9.2% ($1.28 vs. $1.17 estimate) and the massive Q3 beat of 47.8% — indicates analysts have been systematically underestimating the company's normalized earning power.

### Growth Catalysts

1. **Nevro SCS integration and turnaround:** The $5B+ spinal cord stimulation market is adjacent to GMED's spine customer base. Nevro's HFX™ platform is differentiated (10kHz high-frequency therapy) but was financially distressed pre-acquisition. If GMED can leverage its spine sales relationships to cross-sell SCS products, the revenue synergy opportunity is material. Nevro's current net loss of $37.5M (annualized) must reverse for this thesis to work.

2. **ExcelsiusGPS® ecosystem expansion:** The ExcelsiusXR® extended reality headset launched in 2025 extends the robotics platform. While domestic Enabling Technologies revenue declined $17.3M in FY2025, this segment is lumpy by nature (large capital equipment sales cycles). The ecosystem approach — robotics + imaging + XR + planning software — creates switching costs that drive implant pull-through.

3. **International expansion:** International revenue at $571M (19% of total) has room to grow. The NuVasive merger added a global commercial infrastructure that legacy Globus lacked. European and APAC markets are earlier in robotic-assisted surgery adoption curves.

4. **Share buyback:** $390M remaining authorization provides earnings accretion support even in the absence of organic growth acceleration.

- ~18x trailing FCF ($589M / $10.7B market cap)
- 3.5x FY2025 revenue

## Competitive Landscape

Post-NuVasive, GMED is the #3 or #4 global spine company behind Medtronic, DePuy Synthes (J&J), and arguably Stryker. The competitive dynamics break down as follows:

**Spine/MSK:** The combined Globus-NuVasive portfolio offers the broadest spine product line outside of Medtronic. The integration added NuVasive's MIS expertise, IONM services, and the PRECICE™ limb lengthening platform. Competitors include Alphatec (ATEC, smaller but growing fast in lateral spine) and Orthofix.

**Robotics:** The ExcelsiusGPS® platform competes directly against Medtronic's Mazor X Stealth™ and Stryker's ROSA. GMED's ecosystem approach (robotics + imaging + XR + planning) is a strength, but the domestic Enabling Technologies revenue decline raises questions about placement momentum. Medtronic's scale advantage in capital equipment sales is meaningful.

**SCS/Neuromodulation:** Entering the SCS market via Nevro positions GMED against Boston Scientific (dominant), Medtronic, and Abbott. Nevro's HFX™ platform has clinical differentiation but was losing market share pre-acquisition. GMED's ability to cross-sell SCS through its spine surgeon relationships is the strategic thesis, but this has not yet been validated.

**Moat Assessment:** GMED has a moderate competitive moat based on: (1) surgeon training and familiarity with proprietary implant systems, (2) the ExcelsiusGPS® installed base creating switching costs, (3) patent portfolio (3,374 U.S. patents), and (4) manufacturing scale. The moat is narrower than Medtronic's or J&J's due to smaller scale and less diversification, but wider than small-cap competitors.

## Risk Assessment

### Integration Execution (HIGH)
Simultaneous integration of NuVasive (still ongoing — 2025 Strategic Integration Plan added $12M in restructuring costs) and Nevro (acquired April 2025) represents the primary operational risk. Nevro's internal controls were excluded from the FY2025 ICFR assessment. Nevro contributed a $37.5M net loss excluding the bargain purchase gain. Different commercial cultures (three organizations merging), overlapping sales forces, and a fundamentally different business model in SCS (direct-to-payor billing) create execution risk.

### Governance Concentration (MODERATE-HIGH)
David Paul's 66.1% voting control through dual-class shares creates classic minority shareholder risk. The controlled-company exemption from NYSE independence requirements means limited board oversight. With 192.6M additional Class B shares authorized, dilution of economic interest without corresponding voting dilution remains possible.

### FDA Warning Letter (MODERATE)
The July 2024 FDA warning letter citing ExcelsiusGPS® complaint handling and MDR deficiencies remains open as of February 2026. While no safety or manufacturing issues were identified, an unresolved warning letter can delay 510(k) clearances for new products and create procurement hesitancy among hospital systems evaluating capital equipment.

### Pimenta Litigation ($43M Accrual)
The $28.7M jury verdict (accrued at $43.1M including interest/costs) in the Pimenta royalty dispute is an inherited NuVasive liability. The original claim was $82M; GMED is appealing. This is manageable relative to the company's cash position but represents ongoing legal risk.

### CEO Transition
Daniel Scavilla's mid-2025 resignation and replacement by Keith Pfeil represents leadership uncertainty. Pfeil's prior COO/CFO role provides continuity, but the lack of non-compete agreements for departing executives creates risk of competitive talent drain.

### Tariff Exposure
Costa Rica manufacturing (Nevro SCS products) creates tariff exposure. The 10-K discusses the impact of 2025 U.S. tariffs and notes ongoing policy uncertainty. In the current elevated global trade tension environment, this warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 48.6% is essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 50.4% (spread of -1.8%), indicating the options market is not pricing unusual near-term risk or opportunity. The term structure is in backwardation (48.6% near-term vs. 36.0% at 6 months), suggesting elevated near-term uncertainty the market expects to resolve. Put/call OI ratio of 0.36 is call-heavy, consistent with the stock's recent uptrend from the $52 low. Short interest at 2.3% of float with 2.9 days to cover is minimal — no crowding risk on either side.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$125, +33% from current)
- Nevro integration succeeds: SCS cross-selling through spine surgeon relationships generates $400M+ in annual SCS revenue at positive operating margins by FY2027
- Gross margins expand toward 70%+ as all purchase accounting step-ups are fully amortized
- ExcelsiusGPS® placement momentum recovers, driving implant pull-through and competitive wins against Medtronic
- FY2027 EPS reaches $5.50+ with buyback accretion; market re-rates toward 23-25x as integration risk diminishes
- FDA warning letter resolved, removing regulatory overhang

### Bear Case (~$70, -25% from current)
- Nevro integration fails: SCS business continues losing market share and remains loss-making, dragging consolidated margins
- ExcelsiusGPS® placement decline worsens; hospitals defer capital spending in recessionary environment
- FDA warning letter complicates new product launches, competitive positioning
- Operating margins plateau at 14-15% rather than expanding, and FY2026 EPS comes in below $4.00
- Governance concerns and CEO transition keep the valuation discount persistently wide

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $112 (25x FY2026E EPS of $4.47). This represents a narrowing of the peer discount from 55% to 46%, achievable as integration execution is demonstrated over the next two to three quarters. **Upside: +19.4%.**

**Bull case target:** $125 (25x FY2027E EPS of $4.96, or 28x FY2026E). Requires Nevro turnaround evidence and robotics placement recovery. **Upside: +33.2%.**

**Bear case target:** $70 (17.5x a downside FY2026E EPS of $4.00). **Downside: -25.4%.**

**Risk/reward ratio:** Base case +19.4% vs. bear case -25.4% = 0.76:1 on a base-case basis, improving to 1.3:1 on a bull-vs-bear basis. The asymmetry is not compelling on a pure ratio basis, but the probability weighting favors the upside: three consecutive earnings beats, positive estimate revisions, and a clean balance sheet reduce the likelihood of the bear case materializing.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalysts: Q1 FY2026 earnings (expected May 2026), FDA warning letter resolution status, and Q2 FY2026 earnings (expected August 2026) providing the first evidence of full-year Nevro integration economics.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. FY2026 quarterly EPS misses consensus by >10% for two consecutive quarters, signaling integration or operational deterioration
2. Enabling Technologies domestic revenue declines for a third consecutive year, indicating structural loss of robotics competitive position
3. FDA escalation (consent decree, additional warning letters, or product recall) materially impairing the ExcelsiusGPS® franchise

## Conclusion

Globus Medical at ~21x forward earnings represents a discounted entry into a medtech franchise with a fortress balance sheet, 20% FCF margins, and a product portfolio spanning the full musculoskeletal continuum. The governance discount and integration complexity are real, but the market is pricing these risks at a level that implies permanent impairment rather than temporary execution uncertainty. With estimate revisions trending upward, three of four quarters beating expectations, and $390M in remaining buyback capacity, the path to $110-$125 over the next 12 months is credible. We rate GMED a BUY with a base case target of $112.
