**Research Date: August 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The ticker NVRI no longer represents the company most databases describe. On June 1, 2026, the former Enviri Corporation was dismantled: Clean Earth — the highest-margin segment and the entire environmental-solutions growth story — was sold to Veolia as part of a transaction valued above $3.0 billion, of which approximately $1.3 billion went directly to old shareholders in cash and $1,724.8 million went to Enviri LLC through an intercompany note. What trades today is a new registrant holding Harsco Environmental (HE, roughly 80% of revenue) and Harsco Rail, with 28,103,750 shares outstanding versus roughly 84 million before. Any trailing multiple, historical chart, or peer screen built on the old share count describes a different company. The reported trailing and forward P/E of -9.4 and the 149.9% one-year "price change" in the data are artifacts of that recapitalization and carry no information.

Two things happened since the last review that change the analysis, and neither is price action; the stock is essentially flat at $21.61 versus $22.01 in mid-June. First, the Q2 results release quantified HE's earnings power precisely: management's own FY2026 reconciliation puts the segment at $1,018M of adjusted revenue, $175M of Adjusted EBITDA and **$54M of projected operating income**, because projected depreciation is $114M. Second, the August 10 exit from the Deutsche Bahn and Network Rail engineered-to-order contracts produced a $207.4M pre-tax charge of which roughly $75M is a non-cash write-off and roughly **$133M is an accrued liability that converts to future cash outflows**. Put together with an annualized corporate drag near -$32M, consolidated FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA derives to approximately $120M, against a maintenance capital requirement that plausibly tracks HE's $114M depreciation line. The deleveraging is real; the free cash flow is not.

At $21.61 the market capitalization is $607.3M and enterprise value $743.0M, or 6.2x my derived $120M of consolidated Adjusted EBITDA — 7.3x if the $133M of committed exit obligations is treated as debt-like, which it should be, since the cash on the balance sheet was purposefully sized to fund them. My sum-of-parts band is roughly $19-23. Against it sit an unprovisioned R$431M (~$82M) Brazilian criminal claim equal to 13.5% of market cap, an unresolved counterparty response to a contract Enviri walked away from unilaterally, consensus loss estimates that have widened from -$0.09 to -$0.39 for the current year in thirty days, and roughly 9.7% of shares sold short. **REDUCE.**

## Company Overview

Harsco Environmental embeds crews and capital equipment inside customers' steel and metals plants under multi-year site contracts, handling slag and byproduct streams, recovering metal, and providing materials handling and logistics. It also converts those waste streams into ecoproducts — road surfacing, metallurgical additives, cement additives, agricultural and turf products — and supplies aluminum dross and scrap processing systems through Altek. Q1 2026 splits HE into environmental services and logistics $240.4M, ecoproducts $12.2M, and aluminum systems $4.1M.

The geography is the central fact about HE. Western Europe is $96.4M of Q1 HE revenue, the largest region at roughly 38% of the segment, followed by North America $59.1M, Latin America $40.6M, Asia-Pacific $30.4M, and Middle East/Africa $25.5M. Roughly three-quarters of HE revenue is generated outside North America, concentrated in the world's most structurally challenged steel region. Management's own description of demand is that "end-markets have remained subdued."

Harsco Rail supplies track-maintenance equipment, aftermarket parts and services, safety and diagnostics systems, and railway contracting. Q1 2026: equipment $20.7M, aftermarket and diagnostics $29.0M, contracting services $17.7M. Following the August exits, the residual is what management calls the "core maintenance-of-way businesses."

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet.** The transformation is genuine. Long-term debt fell from $1,480.1M at December 31, 2025 to $380.5M at June 30, 2026. Adding short-term borrowings of $0.079M and current maturities of $8.469M gives total debt of $389.1M; against $253.4M of cash, net debt is $135.7M. Financing outflows in the quarter included -$557.0M on the revolver, -$105.6M on the term loan and -$475.0M redeeming the 5.75% Senior Notes, plus $160.0M to repurchase securitized receivables and terminate the AR facility. Total assets fell from $2,742.8M to $1,722.8M and total liabilities from $2,447.2M to $1,040.1M. The revolver has $152.0M of availability to September 5, 2029; the term loan matures March 10, 2028, with $300M of exposure swapped at a 3.06%-3.12% base through that date.

Three qualifications matter. First, restricted cash rose from $21.7M to $49.9M and investing activities include a $25.0M "deposit for commercial commitments," so roughly $75M of the headline cash appears tied to performance obligations. Second, the reserve for contracts (current) rose from $61.0M to $189.5M — that reserve, not the debt schedule, is now the largest claim on cash. Third, management's reported credit-agreement net leverage of 1.9x cannot be reproduced from the disclosed balance sheet: $135.7M of net debt at 1.9x implies a covenant EBITDA base near $71M, roughly $50M below the company's own Adjusted EBITDA basis. Plausible reconciliations include a cap on cash netting, inclusion of letters of credit or pension obligations in the debt definition, or restricted add-back of the exit charge. If the covenant base really is near $71M, headroom to the 3.00x ceiling is only about $77M of incremental net debt. This is unresolved pending the Q2 10-Q covenant note and should be treated as a hypothesis, but the direction, from 4.98x at March 31 to under the ceiling, is unambiguous.

The covenant history is a warning worth keeping in view. A February 2025 amendment reset maximum net leverage to 5.00x; a November 2025 amendment loosened it to 5.50x through Q3 2026 with the stated rationale that "forward-looking projections indicated that it may not meet the minimum level required." At March 31, 2026 the company sat at 4.98x against 5.50x with cushion of $28.5M of EBITDA. The Clean Earth sale was executed by a company approaching its covenant ceiling, not from a position of strength.

**Q2 earnings.** GAAP revenue of $187.3M and a $244.4M operating loss are almost entirely a function of one event: the Rail exits reversed $136.5M of previously recognized percentage-of-completion product revenue and added $70.9M to cost of products sold. Rail's GAAP revenue for the quarter was negative $78.8M. On an adjusted basis, revenue was $323.8M versus $316.0M, Adjusted EBITDA $33.5M versus $27.4M, and margin 10.4% versus 8.7%. Adjusted diluted EPS from continuing operations was -$0.63 against a -$0.75 consensus, the third beat in four quarters — but all four quarters are losses, so the beat record calibrates the size of the loss rather than confirming earnings power.

The charge's cash character reconciles cleanly. On the cash flow statement, "contract exit charges" is added back as non-cash at $74.969M and the reserve-for-contracts working-capital line increases $132.923M, summing to $207.9M against the $207.4M pre-tax charge. Independently, the reserve balance rose $128.5M. So roughly $75M is a write-off and roughly $133M is an accrued obligation that will consume cash over 2026-2028.

**Segment trajectory.** HE improved: Q2 revenue $266.2M (+3%), GAAP operating income $13.0M versus $4.3M, Adjusted EBITDA $45.7M versus $39.9M, margin 17.2% versus 15.5%, driven by services and ecoproducts volume, higher services pricing, and internal actions. First-half HE Adjusted EBITDA was $84.2M at 16.1% versus 15.8%. Rail deteriorated on an adjusted basis: revenue $57.7M roughly flat, Adjusted EBITDA -$4.6M versus -$3.3M. Corporate improved to -$7.6M from -$9.2M, but now carries $1.9M per quarter of central cost previously allocated to Clean Earth with no offsetting revenue.

**Guidance and the consolidated derivation.** Reaffirmed August 11 at segment level only: HE Adjusted EBITDA $170-180M, Rail -$26M to -$19M. No consolidated Adjusted EBITDA and no free cash flow guidance were provided. Summing midpoints ($175M less $23M) and annualizing the -$16.1M first-half corporate run rate gives approximately **$120M of consolidated FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA**, in a range of roughly $110M to $131M. The implied second-half shape: first-half consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was $62.4M, so H2 is approximately $57.6M — lower — with HE improving from $84.2M to $90.8M while Rail deteriorates from -$5.7M to -$17.3M. That Rail step-down either embeds wind-down conservatism or signals that the residual maintenance-of-way business is weaker than the first half suggested. The disclosures do not distinguish these.

**Cash flow is the binding constraint.** Reported Q2 operating cash flow of -$296.9M is not an operating signal; it includes the $160M receivables repurchase and $131.9M of transaction expenditures. Management's adjusted free cash flow was -$8.9M in Q2 and -$32.3M in the first half, both improvements on prior year. The structural question is what a normalized year looks like: approximately $120M of EBITDA, less capex that plausibly tracks HE's $114M depreciation for a business continuously redeploying equipment onto customer sites (call it $110-130M), less cash interest of roughly $20-25M on $389M of debt with $300M swapped, less cash taxes of perhaps $15-25M given profitable foreign operations. That arithmetic does not clear before any exit-liability outflow. Label it a sensitivity rather than a forecast — FY2026 capex was not guided, and there are real offsets: "significant cash payments" expected from the SBB contract in 2027, possible GBM proceeds, working capital release from the Rail wind-down (inventories already down $37.6M since December), and the collapse of interest expense from roughly $107M annualized on the pre-spin carve-out basis. But the burden of proof sits with the bulls, and management chose not to guide free cash flow.

Two accounting items need care. Stockholders' equity rose from $255.1M to $639.1M while the company reported a $406.7M first-half net loss; retained earnings fell only $77.6M, implying roughly $329M of recapitalization credits from the reverse-spin accounting. Do not read the equity increase as value creation. Separately, income tax expense of $40.5M was recorded on a $256.3M pre-tax loss while the deferred tax asset fell from $45.4M to $10.1M — most plausibly valuation allowances against UK and German Rail losses, which would mean the $207M of exit losses generates little future cash tax shield. Both items require the 10-Q notes to settle.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth story here worth underwriting at present. HE revenue grew 3% year over year in Q2 and FY2026 guidance describes segment EBITDA as "modestly above prior-year results at the mid-point," with site exits and the non-repeat of 2025 Brazilian sales tax recoveries named as headwinds. Rail is guided down on lower standard equipment and contracted-services demand plus manufacturing inefficiencies. Consolidated adjusted revenue of roughly $1,245M is a shrinking base once ETO revenue is gone.

The earnings lever is cost, not volume: management said on August 11 that it "recently begun implementing broad restructuring actions across the Company," booking $9.9M of restructuring in Q2 ($7.4M Rail, $2.5M HE). Neither program size nor annualized savings were quantified, and the call transcript was not obtainable. If restructuring cuts corporate drag from -$32M to -$20M, consolidated EBITDA rises to roughly $132M and the multiple compresses to 5.6x; that is the credible near-term upside, and it is unquantified.

The highest-torque external catalyst is European trade policy. The Q1 10-Q records the European Commission's October 2025 proposal for a sizable reduction in steel import quotas plus a meaningful tariff increase on above-quota imports, with parliament and council approval anticipated during 2026. Higher European mill utilization would flow to HE volumes with essentially no incremental fixed cost. It is a free option, but it is a legislative outcome outside the company's control and outside its guidance.

Consensus is moving the other way, hard. Current-year EPS estimates went from $0.00 ninety days ago to -$0.09 thirty days ago to -$0.39 now; next year from -$0.06 to -$0.15. On three analysts these are thin readings, and much of the revision mechanically incorporates the Rail exit and Rail's guided second half. But the sign is unambiguous and the pattern of widening losses and degrading rating sentiment argues against paying up for a restructuring option.

## Valuation Assessment

The supplied peer table is not usable. Median P/E of 70.5 across TNC (71.9), EPAC (21.4), PRLB (70.5) and AIN (-34.9) reflects each company's own earnings distortions, and NVRI has no positive earnings to compare. Trailing and forward P/E of -9.4 are recapitalization artifacts. The analytically defensible frames are EV/EBITDA on derived guidance and a sum-of-parts.

**EV/EBITDA.** Market cap of $607.3M (28,103,750 shares at $21.61) plus $135.7M net debt gives EV of $743.0M, or **6.2x** my derived $120M. Including the $133M of committed exit obligations as debt-like — appropriate, since management explicitly states the opening capital structure "included cash sufficient to address these liabilities," meaning that cash is pre-committed rather than available — EV becomes roughly $876M and the multiple **7.3x**. For an asset-heavy industrial services business with a loss-making second segment and no free cash flow, 6-7x is not obviously cheap.

**Sum-of-parts.** HE at $175M of EBITDA is the whole asset. At 6.0x that is $1,050M, which is already 19x the segment's $54M of projected operating income. Capitalizing an annualized -$32M corporate drag at 6x subtracts $192M. Rail, running -$23M with a customer relationship it just exited, is worth somewhere between negative $100M and zero; call it -$50M. Less $136M net debt and $133M of exit obligations, equity value is approximately $539M, or $19.2 per share. At 7.0x HE the same build gives roughly $682M, or $24.3. **The defensible band is $19-24, with the current $21.61 sitting mid-band.**

That is a fair-value conclusion, not a mispricing, and the valuation dimension deserves no credit for optical cheapness: a 6.2x multiple on a business whose EBITDA is fully consumed by the capital required to earn it is not a discount. Analyst targets (low $21, median $21, mean $26, high $36) bracket the same range from three estimates, and the median sitting exactly at spot with a "degrading" sentiment trend is consistent with the sum-of-parts. Score: 4.8.

## Competitive Landscape

HE's structural advantage is physical embedment: capital deployed inside the customer's mill under multi-year contracts creates switching friction and long tenure. Q2's margin expansion to 17.2% came partly from "higher services pricing," which is meaningful evidence of bargaining position at the contract level.

The offsetting realities are the ones that matter for trajectory. Demand is a derivative of individual mill utilization in the world's weakest heavy-industry region; the competitive question is not share gain but whether the site keeps running and what the mill will pay. Guidance names "site exits" as a revenue headwind, Q2 2025 carried $10.3M of site exit costs plus a $7.4M PP&E impairment, and Bahrain operations have ceased entirely; the portfolio contains sites that cannot earn their cost of capital. Goodwill of $374.6M sits against tariffs and cost inflation identified as potential future impairment triggers.

Rail's position is weaker than the framing suggests. Adjusted EBITDA has been negative and worsening (-$3.3M to -$4.6M year over year) excluding all ETO effects, and the exits leave a business consuming roughly $20M+ of EBITDA annually on $227M of revenue. Enviri closed manufacturing facilities and unilaterally ceased work for Network Rail while simultaneously proposing to keep servicing and life-extending that customer's existing stoneblower fleet — a commercial position that requires the customer's cooperation and does not yet have it. Independent competitor and share data was not obtainable; the characterization above rests on the company's own filings. Score: 4.5 — HE improving on price and margin, deteriorating on footprint; Rail deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

**Network Rail counterparty response (unquantified).** The exits were unilateral. Harsco Rail Limited "informed Network Rail that it has ceased all activities" and closed the associated facilities. The company's own first-listed forward risk factor is "counterparties' response to the actions taken by us, including potential commencement of litigation," followed by "higher than expected liabilities and obligations" and "potential reputational damage." The $133M accrual is management's estimate, not a settlement or release, and prior forward-loss estimates on these same contracts were revised upward repeatedly through Q4 2024, Q1 2025 and Q2 2025. No public response from Network Rail or Deutsche Bahn had appeared as of August 11. The benign reading — that the GBM asset sale is definitive and the fleet-extension proposal implies a continuing relationship — is plausible and would make $133M adequate or conservative.

**Brazil criminal proceeding.** A criminal action filed September 30, 2025 seeks R$431M (approximately $82M, or 13.5% of market cap) jointly from CSN and Enviri over Volta Redonda slag, largely overlapping a civil claim whose May 2025 settlement proposal contemplated roughly $13M from Enviri. Defenses were submitted in January 2026. **No loss provision is recorded** because loss is not deemed probable.

**Other long-tailed liabilities.** Approximately 17,000 pending asbestos personal-injury actions, mostly on the New York Deferred/Inactive Docket with defense and settlement paid by insurers and no provision recorded; Bahrain salt cakes reserve of $27.0M with removal being tendered to third parties; Newtown Creek Superfund with an EPA sitewide decision no sooner than 2028; a revived $28.5M Orion Metals claim; $7.6M of unprovisioned Brazilian municipal tax. Recorded environmental liabilities total $27.7M.

**Pension.** Cumulative unrecognized actuarial losses of -$305.5M sit in AOCI (total AOCI -$509.8M). During FY2025 management identified errors in the actuarial estimation of UK plan liabilities for acquired plans merged into the plan, found during diligence for a potential insurer buy-out; the revision reduced December 2024 retained earnings by $21.5M. The error is modest and the benign explanation — legacy documentation never stress-tested until an insurer's actuaries looked — is the most likely one. That it surfaced only in third-party diligence is a control-environment observation. A buy-out would simplify the structure but would likely require an unsized cash contribution.

**Disclosure immaturity.** No audited standalone fiscal year, no 10-K with Item 1A risk factors for the new registrant, no proxy, no consolidated EBITDA or FCF guidance, one reported quarter, a new CEO and CFO, and Q2 figures sourced from a press release rather than a filed 10-Q. The covenant note, tax note and equity roll-forward are all unavailable. Score: 3.5 — several of these are binary and large relative to a $607M capitalization, and none is obviously priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($29-30).** Restructuring cuts corporate drag toward -$20M and HE's pricing and internal actions push segment EBITDA to $185M in FY2027 while Rail's residual maintenance-of-way business reaches roughly -$10M. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $145M at 7.0x gives $1,015M EV; less $136M net debt and roughly $70M of remaining exit obligations gives $809M of equity, or $28.8 per share. Requires the EU steel quota reduction to pass and lift European mill utilization, Network Rail to settle within the accrual, and HE capex to prove partly discretionary so that free cash flow inflects positive in 2027 alongside SBB inflows and Rail working-capital release.

**Bear case ($12-14).** Network Rail litigates and total obligations reach $200M; Rail's guided second-half deterioration proves structural rather than conservative; European mill closures accelerate site exits and HE volumes fall. FY2027 EBITDA of $105M at 6.0x gives $630M EV; less $136M net debt and $170M of liabilities leaves $324M, or roughly $11.5-12.2 per share. The Brazilian criminal claim landing anywhere near R$431M would be additive to this, and goodwill of $374.6M becomes an impairment candidate.

**Base case ($21-22).** HE delivers the $175M midpoint, Rail lands near -$23M, corporate improves modestly, and consolidated EBITDA settles near $125M in FY2027. At 6.25x, EV of $781M less $136M net debt and roughly $100M of remaining committed obligations gives $545M of equity, or $19.4; on the less conservative framing that treats the exit reserve as ordinary working capital offset by on-balance-sheet cash, the same EBITDA and multiple give $22.95. Midpoint approximately **$21.50, essentially spot.**

Probability-weighting 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear yields roughly -1.5% expected return with a -40% left tail against +34% upside. That asymmetry, not the level of the multiple, drives the rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into post-spin strength. The shares are 11.2% above the CEO's June 3 purchase price of $19.43 and within 11% of the August 5 post-spin intraday high of $24.20; existing holders should reduce to a residual or zero position over the next four to six weeks rather than hold through the Q2 10-Q, the pending Network Rail response, and a restructuring program of unstated size. New capital should wait. Near-term implied volatility at 66.3% against 44.4% realized means optionality is expensive; there is no reason to pay for it.

**Base case target: $21.50** (6.25x $125M FY2027 consolidated Adjusted EBITDA = $781M EV, less $136M net debt, averaged across the two treatments of the $100M residual exit obligation), 0% from $21.61. **Bull $29** (+34%). **Bear $13** (-40%).

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.** (1) A Q2 10-Q covenant note reconciling the 1.9x figure to a covenant EBITDA base above $110M, confirming genuine headroom to the 3.00x ceiling. (2) A settlement, standstill or definitive service agreement with Network Rail confirming total obligations at or below the $133M accrual. (3) FY2026 or FY2027 free cash flow guidance implying positive adjusted FCF, or two consecutive quarters of positive adjusted FCF with capex disclosed below $110M annualized. (4) A quantified restructuring program with annualized savings above $25M and consolidated Adjusted EBITDA guidance above $135M.

**Avoid triggers.** (1) Network Rail or Deutsche Bahn commencing litigation, or any upward revision of the contract reserve above $189.5M. (2) An adverse development on the Brazilian criminal claim requiring a provision above $30M. (3) Rail FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below -$26M, or HE guidance cut below $170M. (4) Goodwill impairment testing triggered against the $374.6M balance. (5) Any covenant amendment request, which would signal the covenant EBITDA base is closer to $71M than to $120M.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q2 2026 10-Q is the immediate decision point; it should settle the covenant reconciliation, the deferred-tax valuation-allowance question, and the equity roll-forward. The Q3 report (early November) tests whether Rail's guided second-half deterioration of -$17.3M was conservatism, and whether restructuring has been sized. The EU steel quota legislation is expected to complete during 2026 and is the single largest external variable.

## Conclusion

Enviri's management did the right things under pressure: the Clean Earth sale removed roughly $1.1 billion of debt from a company that had amended its leverage covenant twice in twelve months, and the Rail exits converted an open-ended percentage-of-completion loss machine into a bounded, funded accrual. The CEO, CFO and a director bought approximately $1.415M of stock on the open market in the first week of trading with no offsetting sales. Those are real positives, and they are why the score sits near the middle rather than at the bottom.

They are not sufficient. The Q2 disclosures established that HE earns $54M of operating income on $114M of depreciation, which means the roughly $120M of consolidated Adjusted EBITDA the company declined to guide is consumed by the capital required to produce it — before the $133M of accrued exit obligations begin converting to cash, before the unprovisioned $82M criminal claim, and before whatever Network Rail does about a contract Enviri exited unilaterally. Against that, $54.6M of cash left the company in the first half settling equity compensation and related taxes, equal to 9.0% of the current market capitalization, and founder grants transferred 2.05% of ownership within two weeks of the spin.

At $21.61 the shares sit in the middle of a $19-24 sum-of-parts band with roughly -1.5% probability-weighted expected return and a bear case 40% below spot. The balance-sheet problem has been solved; the earnings-power problem has not, and the market is not offering a discount for waiting to find out which way it resolves. **REDUCE.**
