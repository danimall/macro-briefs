**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Paramount Skydance is an event-driven security whose price is dominated by the fate of the pending $80.9 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. At $8.49, the Class B shares trade roughly 29% below the $12.00 floor of the equity syndication that would fund the deal, and roughly 47% below the $16.02 ceiling. That spread is the entire story: the market is assigning material probability to one of two adverse outcomes — a close that layers ~$85 billion of pro-forma debt onto a company whose core linear business is shrinking, or a deal failure that leaves a standalone entity carrying a $2.8 billion advance to Netflix, $16.8 billion of existing debt, and legacy liabilities including ~18,050 asbestos claims.

The transaction is now legally frozen. Despite DOJ clearance (reportedly over career-staff objections) and conditional EU approval, a 12-state attorney-general coalition secured a temporary restraining order in late July 2026, and Paramount agreed to halt the merger until five days after an antitrust trial ruling or June 1, 2027, whichever comes first. This removes the near-term catalyst and extends uncertainty by up to a year, during which ticking fees and carrying costs accrue and the standalone business continues to erode on the linear side.

The standalone fundamentals do not rescue the story. Q1 2026 reported +59% Adjusted EBITDA growth, but the research work confirms much of that is a pushdown-accounting artifact — programming inventory and debt were marked to fair value in the NAI Transaction, lowering content and interest carrying costs mechanically rather than through operating improvement. Operating cash flow was only $185 million in the quarter. GAAP full-year 2025 showed a net loss of $586 million. Estimate revisions are flat-to-negative (current-year EPS revised down 18.4% over 90 days). We maintain AVOID, consistent with the four prior reports, with an overall score of 3.5.

## Company Overview

Paramount Skydance Corporation was created on August 7, 2025, when Skydance Media and Paramount Global combined under a new holding company. The combination followed the NAI Transaction, in which Ellison-family and RedBird Capital entities acquired National Amusements, Inc., Paramount's former controlling shareholder. David Ellison, son of Oracle founder Larry Ellison, is CEO and Chairman, and the Ellison family is the controlling stockholder, indirectly holding approximately 77.5% of the voting Class A shares. Publicly traded Class B shares (PSKY, ~1.09 billion shares) carry no vote; the company is a "controlled company" exempt from certain Nasdaq governance requirements.

The portfolio spans Paramount Pictures, CBS (Network, News, Sports, Stations), the cable networks (Nickelodeon, MTV, BET, Comedy Central, Showtime), the streaming assets (Paramount+, Pluto TV, BET+), and Skydance's animation, film, TV, and interactive-games units. Operations recast into three segments in 2026:

- **TV Media** — the largest profit center and cash engine, but in secular decline. Q1'26 revenue $3.67 billion, Adjusted EBITDA $1.06 billion (~29% margin), with advertising down ~6% and affiliate/subscription down ~6% on a comparable basis.
- **Direct-to-Consumer** — Q1'26 revenue $2.40 billion, Adjusted EBITDA $251 million, a swing to profitability from a −$4 million comparable prior-year figure. Paramount+ reached 79.6 million subscribers (+1.8 million YoY), with ARPU up 14% to $8.30.
- **Studios** — combined film and TV studio operations plus Skydance. Q1'26 revenue $1.28 billion, Adjusted EBITDA $164 million.

## Financial Analysis

Financials are bifurcated into "Predecessor" (Paramount Global pre-August 7, 2025) and "Successor" (post-merger), with pushdown accounting restating assets to fair value. This materially distorts period-over-period comparisons and inflates reported margin improvement.

**Q1 2026 (Successor):** Revenue $7.35 billion, +2% versus the $7.19 billion prior-year comparable. Operating income $616 million (+12%). GAAP net income to parent $168 million, or $0.15 diluted EPS, down from $0.22 because of the much larger post-merger share count. Adjusted EBITDA rose 59% to $1.16 billion, but the mechanism matters: lower content and comp/marketing costs, a meaningful portion of which reflect the fair-value markdown of programming assets and debt rather than organic cost discipline. Revenue mix was advertising $2.44 billion (33%, declining), affiliate/subscription $3.50 billion (48%, growing on streaming), theatrical $152 million, and licensing/other $1.25 billion.

**Full-year 2025 (blended, XBRL):** Revenue $12.27 billion, net loss −$586 million, operating loss −$95 million, reflecting merger-year charges and amortization.

**Balance sheet (3/31/26):** Cash fell to $1.94 billion from $3.27 billion at year-end 2025, drained by the Netflix termination payment. Total debt face value is $16.78 billion (carrying value $15.48 billion after a $1.30 billion pushdown discount), including a fresh $2.15 billion revolver draw. Stockholders' equity is $11.7 billion, programming inventory $16.5 billion. The company reports compliance with its 4.50x maximum leverage covenant as of Q1'26.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow was $185 million in Q1'26, burdened by $172 million of restructuring/transformation payments and working-capital drag. Investing cash flow was −$2.98 billion, dominated by the $2.8 billion advance to Netflix. Capex was modest at ~$89 million.

**Capital allocation:** The company maintains a token $0.05/quarter dividend (~$60 million annualized), a plausible early cut candidate if the WBD deal closes given the leverage that would follow.

The financial-health score of 2.8 reflects weak operating cash conversion, a cash balance halved in one quarter, a fresh revolver draw, a standalone debt load already at 16.8x the current tangible cushion of cash, and headline EBITDA gains that do not survive scrutiny. This dimension is our most reliable discriminator, and it points firmly negative here. The prior trajectory (4.5 → 3.0) is confirmed, not reversed, by the Q1 cash-flow and balance-sheet detail.

## Growth Analysis

The DTC swing to positive Adjusted EBITDA is the one genuinely constructive data point, driven by UFC rights (from January 2026) and content such as *Landman*. That gain is real but modest in absolute dollars ($251 million of segment EBITDA against a $16.8 billion standalone debt stack), and it is partly offset by the non-renewal of international distribution deals (−1.2 million subs).

Against that, TV Media — the dominant profit center — is contracting on both advertising and affiliate lines, consistent with the broader linear decline that has pressured every legacy media operator. Management projects "significantly lower" 2026 box-office revenue despite nearly doubling the theatrical slate.

Estimate revisions do not support a growth thesis. Current-year EPS consensus fell 18.4% over 90 days to $0.63, and next-quarter estimates fell 25.1% over the same window. Near-term revisions are essentially flat (current quarter −0.7% over 30 days). This is the opposite of the +30% revision velocity that would flag an underappreciated turn; consensus is drifting lower, not catching up to an improving story. The +51.8% Q1 EPS beat is encouraging on visibility, but sits against two prior-quarter misses (Q3'25 −131%, Q4'25 −1,279%), so the beat/miss record offers no basis for confidence in forward estimates.

Growth prospects score 3.8: a genuine streaming inflection embedded in a shrinking linear core, with consensus estimates declining and the entire growth narrative subordinated to the merger outcome.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples are meaningless here. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (−13.4x on −$0.73 EPS). The peer set (WMG 33.7x, CNK 26.4x, TKO 79.4x, with LYV and MSGS negative) provides little anchor for a controlled, litigation-frozen, negative-earnings holding company. Consistent with the lesson that a "cheap" screen is more often a value trap than a signal, we place no weight on the deeply negative headline multiple.

The relevant valuation reference is the deal architecture. The stock at $8.49 sits ~29% below the syndication floor. Two readings follow, and both cap upside:

- **If the deal closes**, existing Class B holders are diluted (syndication parties take ~39–42% of Class B shares), and the entity carries ~$85 billion of pro-forma debt. Arete Research's Street-low $2.00 target (July 9, 2026) captures the downside of a leveraged close managed by a team with limited experience at that debt scale.
- **If the deal fails**, the standalone entity avoids the leverage and dilution but must resolve the recoverability of the Netflix advance, absorb the regulatory termination-fee mechanics ($7.0 billion owed to WBD if antitrust approval fails, contractually backstopped by the Ellison Parties via Class B at $16.02), and return to being a declining linear business with an improving streaming line. The MarketWatch "could win if it loses" thesis (July 21, 2026) argues shareholders may prefer this path, but it is not clearly value-accretive at the equity level.

The analyst mean target of $11.79 and median $12.00 straddle the deal floor; the range from $2.00 to $20.00 is one of the widest in large-cap media, reflecting a bimodal outcome rather than a central tendency. We do not treat these targets as floors: this is a binary event with falling estimates. Valuation scores 4.2: the price embeds real deal risk, but there is no identifiable catalyst to unlock a mispricing, and both scenario branches cap or impair the equity.

## Competitive Landscape

The strategic rationale for the WBD combination is scale: pairing Paramount+ (~80 million subs) with HBO Max, and building toward a ~30-film annual theatrical slate to compete with Netflix and Disney. On a standalone basis, Paramount lacks the scale to win the streaming arms race and is losing share of viewing to Netflix, Disney, Amazon, and Apple across its linear franchises.

The competitive trajectory is the core problem. TV Media revenue declines on both advertising and affiliate lines mark a moat that is compressing, not widening. DTC is gaining, but from a subscale position and at ARPU levels well below Netflix. The 30-film slate ambition is contingent on the WBD deal that is now frozen. We score competitive position 4.3, in line with the prior report's decline from 6.5; the deterioration reflects a leader in a structurally shrinking format whose scale solution is legally blocked.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by binary, dated, and asymmetric factors that are only partially priced:

1. **Pro-forma leverage.** A closed deal implies ~$85.2 billion of total debt (full $49 billion bridge draw, $5 billion term loans, ~$17.8 billion assumed WBD notes). This is the single largest risk, and management's ability to service it is openly questioned by the Street.
2. **Deal freeze and antitrust trial.** The 12-state suit and TRO stop the clock until a trial ruling or June 1, 2027, deferring any resolution catalyst by up to a year.
3. **Termination-fee exposure.** $7.0 billion regulatory break fee owed to WBD on antitrust failure, plus a ticking fee of up to $0.25/WBD share per 90 days after 9/30/26.
4. **Governance and related-party density.** A controlled-company structure with no public voting rights; a $300 million Oracle cloud agreement and a ~$169 million building lease from entities Larry Ellison controls; RedBird's $80 million advisory fee plus 20% of any termination fee with its founder on the board; sovereign-wealth co-investors (Saudi PIF, Qatar QIA, Abu Dhabi); and the appointment of former DOJ antitrust chief Makan Delrahim as CLO alongside DOJ clearance "over staff objections." The optics are a real qualitative risk that could surface in the AG trial.
5. **Netflix advance recoverability.** The $2.8 billion paid to Netflix on WBD's behalf becomes a live accounting question if the deal collapses.
6. **Legacy liabilities.** ~18,050 asbestos claims (rising QoQ, ~$1.4 billion fair-valued contingent liability), Nokia HEVC patent litigation, and multiple Skydance-transaction fiduciary suits.

Risk factors score 2.5 — an unusual concentration of binary, dated, and governance-linked exposures, several of which are not fully priced given the frozen catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 140.5% against 64.9% one-year historical vol — richly priced and in steep backwardation (near-term 140.5% versus 55.8% long-dated), signaling the market expects imminent, event-driven stress. The put/call open-interest ratio jumped to 2.22 (from 0.79 in mid-June), and put skew of 16.5% confirms elevated downside hedging. The nearest expiry prices a ±16% move. This positioning is consistent with the fundamental thesis — the market is pricing a binary — but it also raises the cost and danger of any directional bet. The rich, backwardated vol structure argues against buying premium, and both a squeeze and a gap-down are live.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The antitrust suit is dismissed or settled quickly (Paramount calls it "one of the weakest merger challenges in modern antitrust history"), the deal closes on terms that let scale synergies and streaming profitability compound, and the combined entity re-rates toward the $12–$16 syndication band and beyond. Alternatively, the deal cleanly fails, the standalone entity sheds the leverage/dilution overhang, DTC profitability continues to improve, and the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean of ~$11.79. Bull target: $16–$20 (deal closes and re-rates toward ceiling / market assigns synergy value).

**Bear Case.** The deal closes into ~$85 billion of debt with diluted equity holders and uncertain synergy realization, and the market prices the leverage risk toward Arete's $2.00 Street-low. Or the deal drags to the June 2027 deadline, ticking fees and carrying costs accrue, the linear business erodes further, and the Netflix advance and termination-fee mechanics impair the standalone equity. Bear target: $2–$5 (leveraged close or disorderly failure).

The distribution is bimodal with a wide range, and both tails skew adverse for the current equity holder: a leveraged close impairs the equity through debt and dilution, and a failure impairs it through break-fee and advance-recoverability questions. There is no clean path to a base-case return that justifies ownership.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The WBD deal outcome has been deferred to five days after an antitrust trial ruling or June 1, 2027. There is no dated near-term event that reliably drives the stock materially lower; the next earnings print is a data point, not a binary. The bearish thesis (leverage/dilution or failure impairment) is directionally sound but lacks a timed trigger within a normal short horizon.

**Timing window.** The primary resolution is now up to 10-plus months out. Carry over that horizon — borrow fee plus 140% near-term implied vol — is prohibitive relative to the deferred payoff.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 14.7% of float with 8.7 days to cover — elevated, and enough to fuel a violent squeeze on any positive antitrust or deal-timing headline. The controlling Ellison stake and sovereign-wealth backers create a bid that could reprice the equity sharply upward if the deal path clears.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market confirms the bearish tilt (P/C OI 2.22, 16.5% put skew), but near-term IV at 140.5% in backwardation makes premium expensive and the setup two-sided, reducing the residual edge for a new short.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Downside on a leveraged close or failure could reach $2–$5; upside on a deal-path clearing could reach $12–$16 (the syndication band) with squeeze acceleration. With the catalyst frozen 10+ months out, ~15% short interest, and 140% near-term vol, the reward:risk does not clear the 2:1 bar for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental case is real, but the catalyst is frozen, carry is expensive, and squeeze risk is meaningful. This is a name to avoid owning, not one to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is the fifth consecutive AVOID, and the additional data — the deal freeze, the halved cash balance, weak Q1 operating cash flow, and 90-day negative estimate revisions — confirms rather than challenges the declining score trajectory (5.2 → 3.5). We are explicit that the recent price decline from ~$9.94 to $8.49 does not by itself justify the cautious view; the fundamental deterioration (frozen catalyst, cash drain, negative revisions, extended uncertainty) is what does. At a >30% drawdown from the 52-week high, the burden is on identifying what the market may be wrong about, and here the market's skepticism (price ~29% below the deal floor) appears well-founded rather than an overshoot.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Clarity on the antitrust trial outcome, and specifically deal terms that preserve equity value — either a failure resolution that leaves a deleveraged standalone entity with a clear path to DTC profitability, or a close with materially less leverage/dilution than the current ~$85 billion pro-forma structure.
- A durable turn in estimate-revision velocity (current-year EPS revisions turning positive over 60–90 days) alongside evidence that DTC EBITDA gains are organic rather than pushdown-driven.
- Resolution or quantification of the Netflix advance recoverability and the termination-fee exposure, removing the balance-sheet tail.
- Open-market insider buying by the controlling family or C-suite, which is conspicuously absent (only routine RSU vesting to date).

**Timeline.** The headwinds persist until the antitrust matter resolves — up to June 2027 by the terms of the merger freeze. We do not expect a cleaner risk/reward before a trial ruling or settlement, and we would reassess at each quarterly print for evidence of standalone deterioration or improvement in the interim.

## Conclusion

PSKY is a special-situation equity whose value is a function of a legally frozen $80.9 billion transaction, not of its operating fundamentals. The standalone business pairs a declining linear cash engine with an improving-but-subscale streaming segment, headline margin gains substantially flattered by pushdown accounting, weak operating cash flow, a halved cash balance, and a governance structure dense with related-party conflicts. Both merger outcomes — a leveraged close or a failure — skew adverse for current equity holders, and the price already sits ~29% below the deal floor. With the resolution catalyst deferred up to a year and estimate revisions drifting lower, we see no compelling reason to own the equity and insufficient asymmetry to short it. We maintain **AVOID** with an overall score of 3.5.
