**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Morningstar has fallen 38% from its July 2025 high of $289.63 to $172.61, and touched $141.49 in late June, entirely on sentiment. The operating business is not deteriorating. Q1 2026 revenue rose 10.8% to $644.8M, operating income rose 36.6%, and diluted EPS grew 50% to $2.73. The company has beaten consensus in four consecutive quarters, most recently by 17.2%. What has changed is the market's willingness to pay for financial-data franchises, which have been repriced across the board on the thesis that large language models will disintermediate paid data and research. Morningstar's own 10-K acknowledges this directly, noting that AI sentiment "has driven meaningful sector-wide stock price declines, including Morningstar's, despite strong operating performance."

The central question for the stock is whether the AI-substitution fear is a permanent impairment of the moat or a sentiment overshoot against a business that is still compounding at high-single-digit organic growth with expanding margins. Two pieces of evidence lean toward overshoot. First, the CRSP acquisition (closed February 2, 2026) converted a ~$88M index business into a scale asset-linked fee engine: assets linked to Morningstar indexes jumped from ~$209B a year ago to ~$3.17 trillion, driven by CRSP indexes that underpin many of the largest Vanguard ETFs. That is a durable, high-margin, passively-driven revenue stream that AI does not threaten. Second, management is putting capital behind its own view: a $1.0B buyback authorization deployed into the decline (shares down ~10% in twelve months), plus an open-market purchase by the new CFO at $186.59 in March 2026.

At ~13-14x forward EPS ($11.92 current-year, $13.46 next-year consensus), Morningstar trades near the bottom of its own historical range and below its higher-growth data peers on a growth-adjusted basis. A >30% drawdown on a business with accelerating results, insider buying, and a structural offset (CRSP) to the fear driving the selling supports a BUY, though a real AI-disruption debate and newly-elevated floating-rate leverage keep this a disciplined-sizing call rather than maximum conviction.

## Company Overview

Morningstar is a global provider of independent investment data, research, ratings, indexes, credit ratings, and investment-management services. FY2025 revenue was $2,445.5M, up 7.5% reported and 8.0% organic. Revenue mix is roughly 70% license-based, 14% asset-based, and 16% transaction-based, with the largest single customer representing under 3% of revenue and ~28% of revenue generated internationally. The company employs ~10,973 people, 43% in India and 29% in the US.

The business runs across five reportable segments plus a corporate/other bucket:

- **Morningstar Direct Platform** (FY25 rev $830.6M, +5.4%): The legacy data/research franchise (Morningstar Data, Direct, Advisor Workstation), sold on subscription to asset managers, advisors, and redistributors. Adjusted margin ~44-45%, renewal rates ~101-104%.
- **PitchBook** (FY25 rev $671.8M, +8.6%): Private-market data covering PE/VC deals and funds. ~10,200 client accounts. Renewal rate slipped to ~103% from 108% on corporate-segment softness, and growth decelerated to +5.3% in Q1 2026.
- **Morningstar Credit** (FY25 rev $354.4M, +21.7%): Morningstar DBRS, the #4 global credit rating agency, plus credit analytics. Fastest-growing segment; margin expanded to 32.4% from 26%.
- **Morningstar Wealth** (FY25 rev $251.4M, +1.2%): Model portfolios and platforms; turned profitable after exiting the US TAMP and sunsetting Morningstar Office.
- **Morningstar Retirement** (FY25 rev $137.6M, +8.3%): Managed retirement accounts with ~$305B AUMA; the highest-margin segment (~49%).
- **Corporate/Other**: Includes Sustainalytics (ESG, declining ~$112M) and Morningstar Indexes (~$88M pre-CRSP, now transformed).

The moat rests on trusted brand independence, proprietary IP (Star and Medalist Ratings, Style Box, indexes), deep and hard-to-replicate public-and-private datasets, and embeddedness in advisor and asset-manager workflows with historically >100% net renewal rates.

## Financial Analysis

The margin recovery is the defining feature of the recent trajectory. Operating income moved from $167.8M in FY22 (a ~9% margin, depressed by over-hiring and integration costs) to $526.6M in FY25 (a 21.5% margin; 23.8% adjusted, up 2.1pp YoY). Revenue over the same window grew from $1,870.6M to $2,445.5M. This is a genuine efficiency turnaround driven by targeted reorganizations, product sunsets, and roughly flat headcount against rising revenue, not a fragile one-off.

FY25 profitability metrics are strong for an asset-light model: net margin 15.3%, ROE 30.6%, ROA 10.4%. Operating cash flow was $589.7M and free cash flow $442.6M, an ~18% FCF margin. FCF was flat YoY on higher tax and bonus payments rather than any operating decay.

Q1 2026 showed acceleration rather than a plateau. Revenue rose 7.6% organic, the operating margin expanded to 24.2% from 19.6%, with CRSP accretive. Segment divergence is the thing to watch: Credit +38.4% and Retirement +17.9% are firing, while PitchBook decelerated and Wealth was down 5.4% reported on deliberate runoff.

The balance sheet is where the profile has changed most. Debt rose from effectively zero short-term at YE24 to $1,072.6M at YE25 and $1,712.8M by Q1 2026, a deliberate leveraging to fund the CRSP purchase (~$365M cash) and aggressive buybacks. Net leverage was ~1.3x EBITDA at YE25, comfortably within covenants. The debt is floating-rate (SOFR-based, 1.05-1.425% margin), with facilities maturing October 2028 and 2030 plus 2.32% private notes due 2030. This introduces rate sensitivity: net interest expense jumped to $13.7M in Q1 2026 from $5.4M a year earlier.

One point of caution on published screens: the Altman Z-Score of 1.46 flags "distress," but this is a false signal for this business. The model penalizes Morningstar's negative working capital, which is driven by ~$586M of deferred revenue, a favorable feature of a subscription model, and its intangible/goodwill-heavy balance sheet from decades of acquisitions. With 30.6% ROE, $442M FCF, and 1.3x net leverage, bankruptcy risk is not a live concern.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth of ~8% is the honest run-rate for the consolidated business, with headline EPS growth amplified by the ~10% share-count reduction and margin leverage. The estimate revision data confirms modest positive momentum: current-year EPS revised +4.9% over 90 days to $11.92, next-year +3.8% to $13.46, with near-term quarters roughly flat. This does not meet the >30% revision threshold that would flag a trough-earnings inflection, and it should not be treated as one — Morningstar is a steady compounder, not a cyclical recovery.

The growth engines are well-positioned. Morningstar Credit (DBRS) is the clear standout, growing on healthy debt issuance. The caveat is that this same driver is cyclical: a fixed-income slowdown would reverse the surge quickly, so it should not be extrapolated at 2025-2026 rates.

Retirement is a durable, high-margin annuity tied to managed accounts. The most important structural development is CRSP. Converting the index business into a franchise underpinning ~$3.17T of linked assets, much of it via the largest Vanguard ETFs, gives Morningstar a passively-driven, asset-based fee stream that grows with market levels and fund flows and is largely immune to AI disintermediation. It also puts Morningstar at the center of high-visibility index decisions (CRSP inclusion calls, new CLO index suites with Houlihan Lokey).

Two areas are softening. PitchBook's deceleration with a falling renewal rate matters because it was previously a double-digit grower, and VC/corporate-segment softness could persist. Wealth is in deliberate managed decline. The bull case on growth depends on Credit, Retirement, and CRSP durability offsetting PitchBook normalization, a reasonable but not risk-free bet.

## Valuation Assessment

At $172.61 against consensus EPS, Morningstar trades at ~14.5x current-year and ~12.8x next-year earnings. (The 17.1x trailing figure in the headline data reflects a lagging EPS base; forward is the relevant metric for a compounder with positive revisions.) That is a substantial de-rating for a business generating 30.6% ROE with 70% recurring revenue and expanding margins.

The peer set validates the discount as unusual. Median financial-exchange/data peer P/E is 15.2x (FactSet), with the group ranging from VIRT at 9.2x to DFIN at 29.3x. Morningstar's forward multiple sits below the median despite superior returns on equity and a stronger recurring-revenue mix than most of the group. The entire cohort has been hit — FactSet is 43% below its 52-week high, MarketAxess 48% below — confirming this is a sector-wide AI-substitution repricing rather than a Morningstar-specific problem. When a whole re-rating cohort moves together on a thematic fear, individual high-quality names within it tend to be mispriced on the way down.

The analyst target range ($203 low, $215 median, $226 mean, $260 high) sits 18-51% above the current price. Given the stock trades below 15x forward with positive estimate revisions, these targets are more likely floors than ceilings; consensus targets on cheap, positively-revising names lag reality. On a growth-adjusted basis, ~13x next-year earnings for ~8% organic growth plus buyback-amplified low-double-digit EPS growth and margin leverage is a PEG comfortably below the market.

I resist scoring valuation at the top of the range. The AI-disruption debate is genuine, not fully resolved, and cheapness alone has been a poor predictor of returns; it frequently marks value traps rather than opportunities. Here, though, the cheapness is paired with a specific catalyst (CRSP's structural offset to the AI fear, plus an active $1B buyback and management signaling), which is what distinguishes an asymmetric setup from a trap. That combination supports a valuation score of 6.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Morningstar competes on fragmented fronts rather than against a single peer: Bloomberg, LSEG, FactSet, and S&P Global in data/research; Preqin (now BlackRock), Capital IQ, and FactSet in private markets; Moody's, S&P, Fitch, and Kroll in credit ratings; S&P DJI, MSCI, and FTSE Russell in indexes.

The competitive trajectory is mixed, which is why I mark this dimension down to 6.0. On the positive side, DBRS is taking share as a legitimate #4 in an ~$11.8B credit-ratings market with long runway, and CRSP materially strengthens the index franchise's scale and durability. On the negative side, the AI overhang is a real structural threat to the value of aggregated data and research, and the in-housing trend — broker/dealers and custodians building proprietary tools — chips at the redistribution model. PitchBook's declining renewal rate is a concrete data point that competitive intensity in private-market data is rising, with Preqin now backed by BlackRock's balance sheet.

Morningstar's response — embedding its data through LLM integrations (Claude, Copilot, ChatGPT; PitchBook collaborations with Anthropic, OpenAI, Hebbia, Rogo) — is the right defensive posture, and these arrangements make Morningstar's proprietary data accessible through third-party models while Morningstar retains ownership. That is a channel expansion, not a surrender of IP. The moat is intact but no longer unambiguously widening, which the score reflects.

## Risk Assessment

**AI disruption to the data/research model** is the central strategic risk and the reason the stock is where it is. The threat is twofold: free or low-cost AI tools that aggregate data (including Morningstar's own content) could erode paid-product value, and there is execution risk in becoming AI-native. This risk is partly priced but not resolved, which is why risk factors score below neutral at 5.5.

**Credit segment cyclicality** is a near-term concern. DBRS's 2025-2026 surge rode a healthy issuance market; a fixed-income slowdown, wider spreads, or reduced refinancing would sharply reverse the fastest-growing, highest-momentum segment.

**Floating-rate leverage** is newly elevated after CRSP and buybacks. $1.71B of SOFR-based debt in a higher-for-longer rate environment means interest expense could erode the buyback benefit if rates stay high.

**Independence/conflict exposure** — rating funds while running its own funds and using an issuer-pay ratings model — is a structural brand risk where any perceived lapse would damage the core asset.

**Governance concentration**: founder Joe Mansueto owns ~37.5%, aligning him with long-term value but creating an overhang through ongoing programmatic diversification selling. **Regulatory breadth** (NRSRO/DBRS oversight, EU ESG Rating Provider Regulation effective 2026, EU AI Act, potential index/model-portfolio adviser regulation, one active ERISA managed-accounts class action) and **goodwill impairment risk** ($1.6B+ goodwill, vulnerable if the valuation decline persists) round out the risk profile.

For a fundamental buyer, the rich near-term IV argues for patience on entry timing or the use of the earnings-driven volatility crush, rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($226-260):** The AI-substitution fear is a sentiment overshoot, and CRSP proves the moat is more durable than the market credits. Credit and Retirement compound at double digits, CRSP's asset linkage grows with markets, and organic growth holds near 8% while margins expand toward the mid-20s. The buyback retires shares at depressed prices, amplifying EPS toward $13.46+ next year. Multiple re-rates back toward the peer median-plus (18-20x) on ~$13.50 EPS as consensus targets prove to be floors. This maps to the $226 mean and $260 high analyst targets.

**Bear Case (~$120-130):** AI genuinely disintermediates paid data and research, PitchBook's deceleration spreads to the Direct Platform and renewal rates fall below 100%, and the Credit surge reverses as debt issuance cools. Floating-rate interest expense eats into FCF, a goodwill impairment forces a writedown, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of peers (10-11x) on flattening EPS. Mansueto's ongoing selling adds supply. This implies ~25-30% downside from current levels.

**Base Case (~$210):** Organic growth holds near 8%, CRSP and Credit offset PitchBook normalization, margins stay in the 23-24% range, and buybacks drive EPS to ~$13.50 next year. The multiple recovers modestly toward the peer median (~15.5-16x forward), yielding a fair value around $210, in line with the analyst median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $210.** 15.6x on ~$13.50 next-year EPS, a modest recovery toward the peer-median forward multiple, representing ~22% upside from $172.61.

**Bull case target: $260.** ~19x on ~$13.75 EPS if the AI fear fully reverses and CRSP/Credit durability drives re-rating — ~51% upside, matching the high analyst target.

**Bear case target: $125.** ~10x on ~$12.50 flattening EPS if AI disruption bites and the Credit tailwind reverses — ~28% downside.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored by quarterly prints (next earnings drives the near-term ±10.9% implied move) and the accumulation of evidence on CRSP contribution, PitchBook stabilization, and Credit durability.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Organic revenue growth decelerates below 5% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the AI/in-housing threat is materializing in results.
2. Direct Platform or overall net renewal rates fall below 100%, indicating churn rather than expansion in the core recurring base.
3. A goodwill impairment charge or a covenant-pressuring rise in floating-rate interest expense that meaningfully erodes FCF.

## Conclusion

Morningstar offers an asymmetric setup that the score trajectory (6.3 to 6.9 to 6.7, HOLD to BUY to BUY) has been building toward. The stock is down on a sector-wide AI-substitution repricing, while the operating business accelerated. The price decline reflects sentiment and multiple compression, not fundamental deterioration; on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, a lower price is a stronger buy case.

What distinguishes this from a value trap is a specific offset to the fear driving the selling. The CRSP acquisition transformed the index business into an asset-linked fee engine underpinning major Vanguard ETFs, a passively-driven, high-margin revenue stream that AI does not threaten. Management is corroborating its own view with a $1B buyback into weakness and a CFO open-market purchase. At ~13-14x forward earnings against a 15.2x peer median, with 30.6% ROE and 70% recurring revenue, the discount is unusual for the quality.

I temper conviction for legitimate reasons: the AI-substitution debate is real and unresolved, PitchBook is softening, the Credit surge is cyclical, and floating-rate leverage has risen materially. This is a BUY with disciplined sizing, best accumulated on weakness or after the near-term earnings volatility resolves, with the invalidation triggers monitored closely. Overall score 6.4, recommendation **BUY**.
