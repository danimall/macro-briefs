## Executive Summary

Comstock Resources is the largest pure-play Haynesville natural gas producer, currently trading at $14.83, down 37% over the past year and 52% from its 52-week high of $31.17. The stock collapsed ~15% following a Q1 2026 earnings miss of -42% vs. consensus, driven by production shortfalls (down 15% YoY), hedging losses of $80.4M, and weather-related disruptions.

The company has a genuinely differentiated long-term asset in its data center power generation partnership with NextEra Energy Resources for 5.2 GW+ of data center power generation, targeting ~1 Bcf/day of gas demand by 2031. This would effectively double current production into a captive, premium-price customer. However, this optionality sits 5+ years out and requires sustained heavy capital spending ($1.4-1.5B annually) through a period of pronounced financial fragility — negative FCF of $223M in Q1 alone, Altman Z-Score of 1.48, and a credit facility maturity in November 2027 that creates a near-term refinancing hurdle with $2.8B+ in debt outstanding.

Consensus estimates are collapsing: current quarter EPS revised down 65% over 90 days, current year down 38%. Analyst sentiment is overwhelmingly cautious (9 Hold, 3 Sell, 1 Buy). The 26.7% short interest with 10.2 days to cover reflects broad institutional conviction that the near-term trajectory is negative. While the forward P/E of 7.0x on current year estimates appears optically cheap, those estimates carry substantial downside risk given the production decline trajectory and commodity sensitivity.

## Company Overview

Comstock Resources operates exclusively in the Haynesville and Bossier shale formations across North Louisiana and East Texas. The company operates ~1,074 of its 1,724 producing wells (99% of proved reserves operated) and had proved reserves of 7.0 Tcfe with a PV-10 value of $4.5 billion at year-end 2025 SEC pricing.

CRK is majority-controlled by entities associated with Jerry Jones, who acquired control in 2018. The company's strategy is focused on three pillars: (1) maintaining legacy Haynesville production, (2) delineating the Western Haynesville extension with 3,343 identified drilling locations, and (3) building out a captive gas demand source via the data center power hub partnership.

Current production runs approximately 1.09 Bcf/day (Q1 2026 annualized), down from 1.4 Bcf/day in 2024. Revenue is virtually 100% natural gas, creating extreme commodity price sensitivity — demonstrated by the swing from $3.63B revenue (2022, ~$6.50/Mcf) to $1.25B (2024, $1.98/Mcf) to $2.22B (2025, $3.17/Mcf).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability:**
Q1 2026 revenue of $587M (+51% YoY) was driven entirely by higher realized gas prices ($4.27/Mcf market, $3.45/Mcf after hedging losses). Adjusted EBITDAX of $251M annualizes to approximately $1.0B. Operating margins recovered to 29.1% in FY2025 from negative territory in FY2024, but this is entirely a gas price function.

**Free Cash Flow (Critical Deficit):**
- FY2025: Operating CF $900M minus capex $1.35B = FCF of approximately -$450M
- Q1 2026: FCF deficit of -$223M (worse annualized pace)
- 2026E capex guidance: $1.4-1.5B E&D + $100-150M midstream + $440M Quantum buyout

The company is structurally burning cash. This spending funds Western Haynesville delineation and infrastructure but creates genuine financial fragility. The gap between OCF and spending is covered by credit facility drawings.

**Balance Sheet Stress:**
- Total debt: $2.8B (pre-Quantum buyout; post-buyout likely ~$3.2B)
- Current ratio: 0.49 (severely below 1.0)
- Cash: $24M
- Available liquidity: $1.3B (revolver capacity)
- Annual interest expense: $223M (consuming ~25% of OCF)
- Altman Z-Score: 1.48 (distress zone)
- Credit facility maturity: November 2027

The Altman Z-Score in the distress zone is driven by structural factors (negative working capital, high debt-to-assets) rather than imminent bankruptcy risk — liquidity of $1.3B provides near-term runway. However, the November 2027 credit facility maturity creates a refinancing event within 18 months at a time when leverage will be higher due to the Quantum buyout.

**Reserve Economics:**
Proved undeveloped reserves swung from 4.2 Tcf (year-end 2023) to 1.0 Tcf (year-end 2024 at $1.84/Mcf SEC pricing) back to 4.2 Tcf (year-end 2025 at $3.07/Mcf). This means 75% of CRK's reserve base effectively disappears at low gas prices — an extreme form of commodity sensitivity embedded in the asset base itself.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (negative):** Production is declining. Q1 2026 output of 98 Bcfe (1.09 Bcf/day annualized) represents a 15% decline from Q1 2025. Management attributes this to weather and timing of Western Haynesville well completions (wells turned to sales "late in the quarter"), but the trajectory requires reversal in Q2-Q3 to validate the capital spending program.

**Medium-term (uncertain):** The Western Haynesville play has delivered 30 successful wells with strong IP rates (29 MMcf/day average). The 3,343 identified locations represent decades of drilling inventory. However, these are among the deepest horizontal shale wells in the industry (14,000-19,000 ft) at ~$14-18M per well, creating high capital intensity per flowing barrel-equivalent.

**Long-term (transformational optionality):** The NextEra Energy Resources partnership for 5.2 GW+ of data center power generation in East Texas represents the most differentiated growth catalyst in the Haynesville. Gas supply targeting ~1 Bcf/day by 2031 would approximately double current production into a captive customer. Initial capacity is 2 GW with expansion potential to 8 GW. The $16B project scale suggests NextEra provides power generation capital while CRK supplies gas.

The challenge: this optionality requires 5+ years of heavy spending, successful well execution at scale, data center buildout proceeding on schedule, and sustained gas prices above $3/Mcf throughout. Multiple failure points exist along this timeline.

## Valuation Assessment

At $14.83, CRK trades at:
- 7.0x forward P/E (on current year consensus of $0.57, this implies ~26x; however, Phase 1 data reports forward P/E of 7.06x)
- Forward P/E on FY27 consensus ($1.24): 12.0x — but this estimate has been declining
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 6-7x annualized Q1 EBITDAX

Against peers: CNX trades at 4.9x P/E, MGY at 16.6x. The peer median P/E of 16.6x would imply significant upside, but CRK's structural FCF deficit, higher leverage, and production decline differentiate it negatively from peers generating positive free cash flow.

The analyst consensus target of $18.50-$18.75 implies approximately 25% upside. The range of $13-$29 is extremely wide, reflecting the binary nature of the gas price and execution thesis. CRK's valuation is "cheap" only if: (1) gas prices sustain $3.50+/Mcf, (2) production reverses its decline in H2 2026, and (3) Western Haynesville capital efficiency proves out at scale. The current estimate revision trajectory (-65% on current quarter, -38% on current year) suggests consensus is still catching down to reality.

The stock has declined 52% from its high. What sophisticated sellers (26.7% short interest) see: chronic FCF consumption, declining production despite massive capex, a missed earnings quarter that exposed hedging damage and weather vulnerability, and a credit facility maturity 18 months away with higher leverage pending. The bear case extends beyond gas price risk to the possibility that the company's growth spending may not generate adequate returns before financial constraints force curtailment.

## Competitive Landscape

CRK holds a defensible position as the largest pure-play Haynesville operator with:
- Scale and operating expertise (since 2007 in the basin)
- Company-owned midstream in Western Haynesville (Pinnacle, soon fully owned)
- Proximity to Gulf Coast LNG export and petrochemical demand
- Unique data center power partnership (no comparable deal among E&P peers)

However, competitive position is weakening near-term:
- Production declining while Expand Energy (CHK/SWN merged) scales up in the same basin
- Capital efficiency unclear — spending $1.35B to achieve negative production growth
- The Shelby Trough divestiture removed legacy low-cost production, concentrating risk on Western Haynesville execution

The data center power partnership is genuinely unique among E&P peers and provides a potential moat if executed. No other gas producer has a captive 1 Bcf/day demand anchor from data centers.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Natural gas price collapse:** Revenue is 100% gas. At sub-$2/Mcf (2024 reality), the company loses money and reserves contractually "disappear." The current macro environment is supportive of gas demand growth, but Haynesville gas supply has also grown materially.

2. **Chronic negative FCF with rising leverage:** Adding the $440M Quantum buyout pushes total debt toward $3.2B.

3. **November 2027 credit facility maturity:** If gas prices decline or production fails to grow, refinancing terms could be materially worse (higher rates, tighter covenants, or forced asset sales).

4. **Production decline trajectory:** If Western Haynesville wells don't deliver production growth by Q3 2026, the capital program's ROI comes into serious question.

5. **Controlled company governance:** Jerry Jones control limits shareholder influence. CEO comp of $34.5M (actually paid) is aggressive for a $4.4B market cap company burning cash. No dividend despite mature E&P operations.

6. **Lease expiration pressure:** 54,529 net acres expiring in 2026 forces drilling activity regardless of economics — a "drill or lose" dynamic that constrains capital discipline.

## Conclusion

Comstock Resources presents a classic "growth trap" dynamic: the long-term asset quality (Western Haynesville + data center power partnership) is genuinely differentiated, but reaching that destination requires traversing 5+ years of heavy spending through a period of elevated financial fragility. The Q1 2026 earnings miss, production decline, chronic negative FCF, rapidly falling estimates, and rising leverage with a 2027 refinancing event create a configuration where fundamental deterioration outweighs the long-term optionality discount.

The score trajectory (5.6 → 4.5 → 4.5 → 4.4) reflects continued deterioration. The Q1 miss and estimate revisions confirm the declining trend rather than reversing it. At $14.83, the stock may eventually prove cheap if gas prices rise significantly and production reverses — but the probability-weighted near-term path remains unfavorable given structural FCF consumption, declining production, and a balance sheet that provides limited margin for error.

Maintain AVOID. The asymmetry favors patience until production growth or FCF generation provides evidence that the capital spending program is generating returns.
