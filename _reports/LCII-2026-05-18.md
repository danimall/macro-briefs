## Executive Summary

LCI Industries is the dominant supplier of engineered components to the North American RV industry, with $5,670 of content per towable RV unit and over 100 manufacturing facilities. The company has executed well on diversification (56% of revenue now comes from outside North American RV OEM) and has demonstrated pricing power by passing through $80.9M of tariff-related costs in 2025 while maintaining volume. At $111 per share (~12.5x forward earnings, 4.1% dividend yield), the stock offers reasonable value for a cyclical recovery play.

The core problem is timing. RV retail sales fell 17% in Q1 2026, management cut their 2026 wholesale shipment forecast to 315K-330K units, and the macro environment directly pressures the discretionary consumer spending that drives RV purchases. The failed merger with Patrick Industries removed a potential catalyst and introduced questions about management's confidence in the standalone growth path. Short interest at 12.6% of float reflects meaningful institutional skepticism.

The stock has recovered from its May 4 post-merger-termination trough of ~$103 to $111, suggesting the event overhang is partially absorbed. But with current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates declining (-1.8% and -3.9% over 30 days respectively), and consumer spending headwinds intensifying, the risk/reward from here is balanced rather than compelling.

## Company Overview

LCI Industries operates through Lippert Components as the leading manufacturer of engineered components for the recreational vehicle, marine, transportation, and housing industries. The business generates $4.1B in annual revenue across two segments:

**OEM Segment (77% of sales, 66% of operating profit):** Manufactures chassis, axles, windows, furniture, appliances, slide-outs, awnings, and doors primarily for RV OEMs (Thor Industries, Forest River/Berkshire Hathaway, Winnebago) and adjacent markets (marine, bus/transportation, manufactured housing).

**Aftermarket Segment (23% of sales, 34% of operating profit):** Replacement parts, accessories, and towing products (CURT Manufacturing) sold through dealers, distributors, and direct-to-consumer channels.

The company is headquartered in Elkhart, Indiana — the center of the North American RV manufacturing cluster — which provides logistics advantages and proximity to customers.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Recovery:**
Revenue peaked at $5.21B in 2022, collapsed 27% to $3.78B in 2023, stabilized in 2024 ($3.74B), and grew 10% to $4.12B in 2025. Q1 2026 revenue of $1.09B (+4% YoY) suggests continued modest growth, though the composition matters: adjacent markets (transportation +24% from acquisitions) and aftermarket (+7%) offset RV OEM weakness.

**Margin Trajectory:**
- Gross margin: 23.8% (2025), recovering from 20.5% trough (2023)
- Operating margin: 6.8% (2025), with management targeting 7.5-8.0% in 2026
- Q1 2026 operating margin: 8.7% (up 90bps YoY) — tracking above full-year guide

The margin expansion is driven by three identifiable sources: (1) tariff pass-through net positive by $5.1M in OEM segment, (2) materials sourcing optimization contributing $58M of savings, and (3) footprint consolidation (8-10 facility closures planned for 2026).

**Balance Sheet:**
Net debt/adjusted EBITDA at 1.9x is conservative for a capital goods business. Long-term debt of $942M is primarily composed of a Term Loan B (due 2031/2032) and $450M of 2030 Convertible Notes at 3.0% coupon. No significant maturities until 2030. Current ratio of 2.85 provides substantial liquidity cushion.

**Cash Flow:**
Full-year 2025 FCF was $278M (6.8% of revenue). Q1 2026 FCF was negative $43M, which is seasonal (inventory build ahead of the spring/summer RV production season). The pattern of $250-300M annual FCF supports the dividend ($114M) and buyback ($128.6M in 2025) comfortably.

**Capital Return:**
The company returned $242.6M to shareholders in 2025 (dividends + buybacks). The $4.60/share annualized dividend yields 4.1% at current prices. A $300M buyback program (authorized May 2025, $200M remaining) provides ongoing share count reduction.

## Growth Analysis

**Content Per Unit — The Secular Growth Engine:**
LCII's content per towable RV unit grew 11% to $5,670 in 2025. This growth reflects pricing power, mix shift toward higher-content fifth-wheels (23.7% of chassis shipments in Q1 2026, up from 15.5% in Q1 2022), and genuine share gains through innovation. This mechanism allows revenue to grow even when unit volumes decline modestly — a critical differentiator for a cyclical business.

**Diversification:**
Adjacent market OEM revenue reached $1.25B in 2025 (+12% YoY), representing 39% of the OEM segment. The transportation vertical is the fastest-growing — acquisitions of Trans Air (bus HVAC) and Freedman Seating (bus seats) drove 24% Q1 2026 growth. International represents ~9% of revenue against an estimated $2B+ addressable market, providing a long runway.

**Near-Term Growth Headwinds:**
- RV wholesale shipment forecast cut to 315K-330K for 2026 (from 335K-350K)
- Q1 2026 retail down 17% YoY — a deteriorating trend
- Current-quarter EPS estimates declining 1.8% over 30 days
- Next-quarter estimates declining 3.9% over 30 days
- April 2026 sales down 4% YoY per management commentary

The estimate revision trend is modestly negative, not alarming but inconsistent with a BUY thesis. Full-year 2026 estimates ($8.92) are still up 6% over 90 days, suggesting confidence in the full-year recovery even as near-term visibility deteriorates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $111, LCII trades at:
- ~12.5x 2026E EPS ($8.92 consensus)
- ~11.0x 2027E EPS ($10.06)
- ~8.0x EV/EBITDA (estimated, using ~$480M 2026E EBITDA)

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E is 24.2x, making LCII's ~12.5x appear significantly discounted. However, context matters: Dorman Products (19.3x) operates in the less-cyclical auto aftermarket, Patrick Industries (24.2x) trades at a premium following the failed merger, and Adient (30.3x) has negative trailing earnings inflating its forward ratio.

The discount to peers reflects the RV cyclicality and the current-cycle uncertainty. At normalized mid-cycle earnings power of $10-12/share (achievable if RV shipments recover toward 350K-375K units), LCII at $111 trades at 9-11x — genuinely cheap. The timing of cycle recovery is the key unknown.

**Dividend Support:** The 4.1% yield provides a meaningful floor. At $278M annual FCF and $114M in dividends, the payout ratio is 41% of FCF — well-covered even in a downturn scenario.

**Why the market may not be wrong:** The stock is 30% below its February high of $159.66. The decline reflects (1) failed PATK merger premium reversal, (2) deteriorating RV retail data, (3) tariff uncertainty, and (4) broader consumer discretionary selling. These are legitimate fundamental concerns, not mere volatility. The market is pricing a delayed RV recovery and potential consumer recession — both of which are plausible given the macro backdrop.

## Competitive Landscape

LCII's competitive position is strong but faces structural challenges:

**Strengths:**
- Dominant scale: 100+ facilities, largest RV component supplier
- Content growth: ability to increase revenue per unit through innovation and cross-selling
- Proximity: manufacturing cluster in Elkhart provides logistics advantages
- Aftermarket recurring revenue stream (23% of sales, higher margins)
- Diversification reducing RV dependency year-over-year

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Barriers to entry are "generally low" per the 10-K — moat is scale-based, not IP-based
- Customer concentration: 33% from two customers (Berkshire/Thor) with no long-term contracts
- Vertical integration risk: large OEM customers could bring component manufacturing in-house
- Patrick Industries remains an aggressive competitor that may continue pursuing consolidation

The failed merger with Patrick suggests both companies recognize that industry consolidation is strategically valuable. LCII must now execute its growth strategy without the scale benefits a merger would have provided.

## Conclusion

LCI Industries is a well-managed, dominant-position company in a cyclical industry at the wrong point in the consumer spending cycle. The valuation at ~12.5x forward earnings and 4.1% dividend yield provides downside cushion, and the content-per-unit growth story offers structural revenue growth independent of unit volumes. Management has executed competently on diversification, margin improvement, and capital return.

However, the macro environment for consumer discretionary purchases is under pressure, and estimate revisions are turning negative. The failed PATK merger removes a near-term catalyst. The stock needs either a macro improvement (lower rates, stronger consumer) or clear evidence of RV cycle trough to justify a more constructive stance.

At $111, the risk/reward is balanced. The downside is limited by valuation support, dividend yield, and buyback activity. The upside requires a catalyst that current data does not yet support. Hold, with a bias toward upgrading if Q2 data confirms cycle stabilization.
