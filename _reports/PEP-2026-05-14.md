## Executive Summary

PepsiCo is navigating a transition year. FY2025 delivered flat core EPS ($8.14 vs. $8.16 prior year), nearly $2B in brand impairments (Rockstar), and persistent North America volume declines (-2% food, -3% beverages). The stock has rallied 14.5% from its 52-week low of $127.60 in late June 2025 but remains 13% below its February 2026 high of $171.48. At $149 and approximately 17.2x forward core EPS ($8.66 for FY2026), PEP trades at a meaningful discount to Coca-Cola (25.2x) and a premium to Keurig Dr Pepper (21.6x), reflecting uncertainty about whether the Elliott-driven restructuring will restore volume growth.

The bull case rests on the December 2025 shareholder value plan — 20% SKU rationalization, Frito-Lay plant closures, aggressive price cuts on mainstream brands, and 100+ bps of margin expansion over three years. Q1 2026 delivered early evidence of traction (revenue +5.6%, beat on both lines). The bear case centers on structural headwinds: GLP-1 drug impact on snacking, private label encroachment, $49B in debt with rising interest costs, 20% of cash trapped in Russia, and a consumer environment deteriorating under tariff uncertainty and elevated inflation. The 4% dividend yield provides meaningful downside support, but total return upside depends on execution of a turnaround that is still in early innings.

## Company Overview

PepsiCo is a $204B market cap global food and beverage company generating $93.9B in FY2025 revenue. The business operates across six segments: PepsiCo Foods North America (29% of revenue), PepsiCo Beverages North America (30%), International Beverages Franchise (5%), EMEA (19%), Latin America Foods (11%), and Asia Pacific Foods (5%). The company owns over a dozen billion-dollar brands including Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Pepsi, Mountain Dew, and Gatorade. North America represents ~59% of revenue. Walmart is the single largest customer at ~14% of consolidated net revenue.

The company's Direct Store Delivery system provides competitive advantages in shelf placement and freshness but operates at higher cost than warehouse distribution, creating a structural margin tension that management is actively addressing through its productivity program.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Growth has decelerated from +5.9% in FY2023 to +0.4% in FY2024 and +2.3% in FY2025. FY2025 organic revenue was +2%, composed of +4% effective pricing and -2% volume. The pricing-without-volume dynamic was the central problem that attracted Elliott's attention.

**Profitability:** GAAP operating income declined 11% to $11.5B due to $1.95B in impairment charges. GAAP operating margin compressed from 14.0% to 12.2%. Core (non-GAAP) EPS was effectively flat at $8.14 vs. $8.16. This implies zero real earnings growth for shareholders over two years.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $12.1B (-3% YoY). Free cash flow improved to $8.2B (+9%) primarily because capex was cut from $5.3B to $4.4B. FCF yield at current market cap is approximately 4.0%, consistent with the dividend yield and suggesting the company is distributing nearly all of its free cash flow to shareholders ($7.6B dividends + $1.0B buybacks = $8.6B total vs. $8.2B FCF).

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $49.2B against $20.4B in equity implies a debt/equity ratio of 2.4x. Net debt increased as PepsiCo funded the poppi ($1.95B) and Siete ($1.2B) acquisitions. New long-term debt was issued at 4.1-5.0% coupon rates. Net interest expense grew $202M to $1.12B. The Altman Z-Score of 1.97 (grey zone) reflects this leverage, though investment-grade ratings and $10B in available credit facilities mean refinancing risk is low. The more concerning issue is that 20% of consolidated cash (~$1.8B+) sits in Russia with limited repatriation prospects.

**FY2026 Guidance:** Organic revenue +2-4%, core constant currency EPS +4-6%, 100+ bps margin expansion over three years, FCF conversion ≥80%. This implies core EPS of approximately $8.47-$8.63, broadly consistent with consensus at $8.66.

## Growth Analysis

PepsiCo's growth challenge is structural rather than cyclical. North America organic volume has been negative for two consecutive years. The company faces a trilemma: (1) raise prices to protect margins but lose volume to private label, (2) cut prices to recover volume but compress margins, or (3) innovate into higher-growth categories.

Management is pursuing all three simultaneously: price cuts on mainstream brands (Lay's, Doritos), continued premiumization in new segments (poppi, Siete), and category pivots (Celsius/Alani Nu distribution in energy).

Q1 2026 provided the first positive datapoint — revenue of $29.34B (+5.6%) and an EPS beat, with analysts noting "volume recovery finally showing" in PFNA. However, one quarter does not confirm a trend reversal, especially when the comparison period was weak.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: FY2026 EPS revised up 0.5% over 90 days to $8.66, and FY2027 up 0.2% to $9.15. This represents ~6% core EPS growth, which if sustained would be a meaningful improvement from the prior two years of stagnation. The revision velocity is insufficient to trigger the BUY signal for inflection stocks — it is more consistent with stabilization than acceleration.

International segments offer better growth (EMEA +10%, Asia Pacific +20% in constant currency) but represent only ~41% of revenue. The structural growth rate of the consolidated business appears to be mid-single-digits at best, consistent with a mature consumer staples company.

## Valuation Assessment

At $149.27, PepsiCo trades at:
- 17.2x forward core EPS ($8.66 for FY2026)
- 16.3x FY2027 core EPS ($9.15)
- 24.9x trailing GAAP EPS ($6.00, depressed by impairments)
- ~4.0% dividend yield
- ~4.0% FCF yield

Relative to peers:
- KO trades at 25.2x with a 2.7% yield — the 8x P/E premium reflects KO's pure-play beverage focus, superior organic growth, and lighter leverage
- KDP trades at 21.6x with 3.2% yield — PEP's discount vs. KDP reflects weaker near-term volume trends and restructuring uncertainty
- MNST at 41.5x reflects growth premium in energy drinks

PEP's discount to KO is justified by (a) weaker volume trends, (b) higher leverage, (c) Russia exposure, and (d) restructuring uncertainty. However, if the turnaround executes and PepsiCo delivers the guided margin expansion with volume recovery, a re-rating toward 20x forward EPS ($8.66 × 20 = $173) is plausible — consistent with the analyst median target of $173.

At current levels, the stock appears fairly valued for the risk profile. The 4% yield provides a floor, and the restructuring provides optionality, but the valuation does not offer a clear margin of safety given the execution risks.

## Competitive Landscape

PepsiCo's competitive position is stable but under pressure at the margins:

**Strengths:** Frito-Lay commands 33%+ U.S. salty snack market share. The DSD network provides execution advantages in convenience and grocery channels. The food+beverage portfolio enables cross-merchandising. Scale across 200+ countries creates distribution barriers.

**Pressures:** Private label growth is eroding branded share in core snacking categories. Retailers are reducing shelf space for national brands. Health/wellness trends are structural headwinds for traditional salty snacks and sugary beverages. The energy drink pivot — from ownership (Rockstar) to distribution (Celsius, Alani Nu) — surrenders operating economics in the highest-growth beverage category.

**Trajectory:** The competitive position is slightly deteriorating in North America (volume losses to private label, DSD system costs) but stable to improving internationally. The poppi and Siete acquisitions attempt to address the health/wellness gap. Celsius distribution provides access to energy drink growth but at lower margins than brand ownership. The net competitive trajectory is flat to slightly negative, warranting a score in the mid-6 range rather than the 7+ assigned to companies gaining share.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Volume Recovery Execution (HIGH):** The entire investment case depends on whether price cuts drive sufficient volume to offset margin compression. If Q2-Q3 2026 show volume recovery flattening, the turnaround narrative weakens.

**2. Tariff and Input Cost Inflation (MODERATE-HIGH):** PBNA already absorbed a 6-point tariff headwind. With ongoing trade policy uncertainty around China, EU, Canada and Mexico, input costs remain elevated and will likely persist as a headwind through 2026.

**3. Russia Exposure (MODERATE):** 5% of revenue, 5% of assets, and 20% of cash are in Russia. Repatriation prospects are unclear. Any escalation of Western sanctions or Russian capital controls could trigger impairment.

**4. Consumer Spending Deterioration (MODERATE):** The broader consumer environment is weakening. While staples are relatively defensive, trading down to private label accelerates during consumer stress — exactly the behavior that caused PEP's volume problem.

**5. GLP-1 Drug Structural Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** Long-term reduction in snacking occasions from weight-loss drugs remains difficult to quantify but represents a potential 2-5% volume headwind over 3-5 years. The market may be under-pricing this for snack-heavy companies.

**6. Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE):** As a bond-proxy with 4% yield, PEP benefits from rate cuts and suffers during rate hikes. With the rate-cut timeline uncertain, the yield-driven re-rating that would normally support staples multiples is absent.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.8% is nearly double the 1-year historical volatility of 21.4% — a 17.4pp spread indicating the market is pricing significant uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (38.8% near-term vs. 27.0% at 6-month), consistent with a specific near-term catalyst being priced. Put/call skew of 5.0% at the front end suggests moderate put demand. IV has risen 11.6pp since late April while the stock declined 4.5%.

This options structure is unusual for a consumer staples name and may reflect (a) positioning ahead of macro catalysts, (b) concern about sector rotation out of defensive names if risk-on sentiment persists, or (c) hedging by large institutional holders. The backwardation resolves by the 1-month tenor (23.4%), suggesting the stress is concentrated within days, not weeks.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($175-185, +17-24%)
- Elliott-driven restructuring delivers 100+ bps margin expansion on schedule
- Volume recovery sustains through 2026, proving price cuts effective
- Multiple re-rates from 17x to 20x as execution de-risks
- International growth accelerates (EMEA/LatAm/APAC)
- Rate cuts eventually arrive, lifting bond-proxy multiples
- poppi and Siete acquisitions succeed, addressing health/wellness positioning

### Bear Case ($125-132, -11-16%)
- Volume recovery stalls — price cuts compress margins without driving sufficient volume
- Consumer spending deterioration accelerates private label migration
- Russia cash trap worsens or triggers impairment
- Interest rates remain elevated, keeping yield premium insufficient vs. risk-free alternatives
- GLP-1 drug adoption materially reduces snacking occasions
- SodaStream impairment adds to write-off history

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive North America organic volume growth (PFNA and PBNA both positive)
2. Stock pulls back to $135-140 range (16x forward EPS) while estimates remain stable, creating clear valuation margin of safety
3. Rate cut cycle begins, creating tailwind for yield-oriented equities

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 organic revenue growth comes in below +1% for two consecutive quarters
2. Core operating margin expansion fails to materialize by Q3 2026 results
3. Management cuts dividend growth rate below 3% or freezes the dividend

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next natural reassessment point is Q2 2026 earnings (expected July 2026), which will provide the second data point on whether Q1 2026 volume recovery is sustainable.

## Conclusion

PepsiCo at $149 is a reasonably priced income investment yielding 4% with 53 years of consecutive dividend increases. The Elliott-driven restructuring provides optionality for re-rating, and Q1 2026 showed early execution. However, the business faces genuine structural headwinds (health trends, private label growth, GLP-1 drugs), cyclical headwinds (inflation, tariffs, consumer weakness), and financial headwinds (rising interest costs on $49B debt, trapped Russian cash).

The stock is neither cheap enough to offer a margin of safety against these risks, nor growing fast enough to justify a premium multiple. Analyst consensus (median target $173, 16% upside) reflects the bull case but requires sustained execution that has only one quarter of evidence. The income characteristics and defensive positioning provide portfolio utility, but the risk/reward does not justify new capital at current levels. HOLD for income, with defined upgrade and downgrade triggers.
