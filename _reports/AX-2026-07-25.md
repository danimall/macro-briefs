**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Axos Financial is a technology-driven, branchless bank holding company with roughly $29.2B in assets that consistently out-earns regional-bank peers on the strength of a low-cost operating model. Its efficiency ratio of ~47% consolidated (~41.5% at the Banking segment) and net interest margin of ~4.6% translate into a 1.77% return on assets and 16.3% return on equity — top-decile figures for a depository. The business runs on nationwide digital deposit gathering funding a diversified, higher-yielding commercial and specialty loan book, supplemented by a securities-clearing and RIA-custody franchise.

The story over the past two quarters has become more nuanced. Q3 FY26 (quarter ended 3/31/2026) net income of $124.7M (+18.5% YoY) and reported diluted EPS of $2.15 looked strong on the headline, but the beat was flattered by a $22M one-time legal settlement worth roughly $0.38 per share. On an adjusted basis, EPS growth was closer to 5%. Beneath the beat, net interest margin compressed to 4.57% from 4.78% a year prior, the provision for credit losses tripled to $41.0M from $14.5M, and the net charge-off rate jumped to 0.31% from 0.09%, driven substantially by a single problem commercial-and-industrial loan. Consensus EPS estimates have been drifting lower across every window (current year -4.3% over 90 days), and the stock reacted negatively to the "beat" — the market is discounting the earnings quality rather than rewarding it.

We rate AX a HOLD (growth_hold, overall 5.85). The bank remains a high-quality compounder, and its deposit-book acquisitions (Jenius ~$2.3B, Capital One IRAs ~$3.2B) offer a credible path to lower funding costs that a standard screen would miss. But at ~11.6x forward earnings and ~1.9x tangible book, near its 52-week high, the valuation fairly reflects the balance of top-tier returns against rising credit costs, thinning capital, and an asset-sensitive balance sheet that has more NIM to give back than peers as rates fall. We would own the name for the compounding, not for asymmetric upside at current levels.

## Company Overview

Axos operates two reportable segments. The **Banking Business** (~95% of pre-tax income) gathers deposits nationally through digital channels — consumer and business checking/savings, time deposits, and specialty verticals like Title & Escrow and treasury management — and deploys them across a diversified lending book: single-family and multifamily mortgage, commercial real estate, commercial & industrial (including fund finance, lender finance, asset-based lending, and, post-Verdant, equipment leasing), auto/consumer, and a bankruptcy-trustee fiduciary niche. The **Securities Business** houses Axos Clearing (clearing broker-dealer), Axos Advisor Services (RIA custody), and Axos Invest (digital wealth), generating fee income and margin/stock-lending net interest.

The company is supervised by the Federal Reserve as a savings-and-loan holding company; its principal subsidiary, Axos Bank, is a federal savings association regulated by the OCC. The fiscal year ends June 30. As of the most recent reporting, gross loans were $25.5B (+18% since June 2025), deposits $22.4B (+7.5%), and assets under custody/administration roughly $44B.

The differentiator is a genuinely low-cost, branchless model. The absence of a physical branch network produces an efficiency ratio far below traditional regional peers and supports a structurally elevated NIM. Axos competes on technology, niche commercial lending, and disciplined low-LTV underwriting rather than geographic presence.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is strong but decelerating on a core basis.** Nine-month net income was $365.4M with EPS of $6.33. Adjusting for the one-time favorable legal settlement, adjusted quarterly EPS was $1.90 versus $1.81 in the prior-year period — roughly 5% core growth. ROA of 1.77% and ROE of 16.3% remain best-in-class for a bank.

**Net interest margin is compressing but stays well above peers.** NIM fell to 4.57% in Q3 from 4.78% a year earlier (nine-month 4.76% vs 4.93%). Three drivers: runoff of the very high-yielding FDIC purchased loans acquired December 2023 (yielding 12–17%, with average balances down to ~$630M from ~$975M), lower yields on excess cash, and higher borrowing costs. The purchased-loan amortization is a structural drag that will continue pulling NIM lower independent of the rate path.

**Loan growth is aggressive and capital-consuming.** Gross loans rose 18% (~$3.9B) since June 2025, of which ~$1.0B came from the Verdant equipment-leasing acquisition. Rapid RWA growth drove CET1 down to 11.65% at the holding company and 10.90% at the bank, from 12.52%/12.42% at June 2025. Both remain "well capitalized," but the buffer is thinning. There were no share repurchases in the last nine months despite $148M of remaining authorization and an unused $150M ATM — capital is being retained to fund loan growth and acquisitions.

**Funding composition warrants a flag.** To bridge to the pending Jenius deposit acquisition, Axos took on $1.745B of overnight FHLB advances at ~3.98%, pushing total borrowings to 9.6% of assets from 1.5%. Brokered deposits stood at ~$2.0B. This is temporary and should reverse as the acquired deposit books arrive, but it adds near-term funding-cost and rollover risk.

**Earnings-quality track record is solid at the headline.** The company has beaten consensus in four consecutive quarters (Q3 FY26 +16.0%, though partly one-time; prior three beats of 0.4–4.0%). The more revealing signal is that estimates are falling despite the beats — current-quarter consensus is down 5.0% over 90 days, current-year down 4.3%, next-year down 1.2%. The market is pricing NIM compression and credit costs faster than the beats are lifting near-term numbers.

Book value per share is $53.89; tangible book value per share $49.72. At $95.83, the stock trades at ~1.9x tangible book.

## Growth Analysis

Axos's growth engine has two components. The first is organic origination in higher-yielding commercial niches — C&I/fund finance, lender finance, and equipment leasing — which supports the elevated NIM but also concentrates credit risk. The second, and increasingly the strategic core, is opportunistic acquisition of deposit books and specialty lending platforms.

The deposit M&A pipeline is the most credible near-term catalyst. The Jenius Bank consumer deposit book (OCC-approved 3/19/2026, expected to close by quarter ending 6/30/2026) and the Capital One IRA deposits (P&A agreement signed 4/22/2026, pending OCC, expected to close CY2026) together bring ~$5.5B of incoming deposits at negotiated discounts. If these close on terms, they should lower funding costs and reduce reliance on FHLB advances and brokered deposits — a genuine offset to NIM compression that standard screening would miss. Verdant, acquired 9/30/2025 for ~$566.9M, added ~$1.0B of loans/leases and contributed $7.1M of net income in Q3 FY26.

The counterweight is that growth is being funded by shrinking capital buffers and (potentially) the ATM, and that the higher-yielding commercial mix carries the credit exposure now surfacing in provisions. Consensus captures this tension: next-year EPS of $9.61 implies ~16% growth off the current-year $8.32, but the 90-day revision trend is negative across all windows. The growth is real and multi-pronged, but the near-term earnings momentum has cooled, which is why we score Growth Prospects at 5.8 rather than higher. The estimate-revision signal here is mildly negative, not the +30% inflection that would flag an aggressive buy.

## Valuation Assessment

At $95.83, AX trades at ~11.6x trailing/forward earnings (EPS $7.43; forward on $8.32 current-year consensus implies ~11.5x) and ~1.9x tangible book value.

Against regional-bank peers, the picture is mixed. The peer set — OZK (8.4x), ASB (10.1x), FFIN (18.1x), HWC (14.7x), AUB (16.8x) — carries a median P/E of 14.7x. On that basis AX screens cheap. But two adjustments matter. First, AX pays no dividend versus a peer-median 3.4% yield, so a portion of the multiple discount is the absence of income return (capital is retained for growth). Second, AX's tangible-book multiple is a premium to the group, appropriately reflecting its 16.3% ROE and ~47% efficiency ratio — a bank earning double-digit-above-peer returns should trade above book, and it does.

The valuation question is whether the P/E discount to the peer median reflects genuine mispricing or fairly prices the emerging risks. We lean toward the latter. The earnings that anchor the multiple are of somewhat lower quality than the headline suggests (the $22M legal gain, tripled provisions), NIM is structurally compressing (purchased-loan runoff plus rate-cut sensitivity), and estimates are being revised down. A cheap multiple on earnings that consensus is trimming is the classic value-trap configuration, and we treat the apparent cheapness with skepticism rather than as a buy signal.

Analyst price targets ($105 low, $115 high, $110.9 mean) sit 10–20% above the current price, and the consensus has upgraded (buy ratings 4→5). On a lower-multiple, positive-revision inflection name we would treat those targets as floors; here, with estimates falling, we treat them as reasonable but not conservative. Applying ~13x to a normalized ~$8.75 forward EPS (blending current and next-year consensus, haircutting for one-time items) yields a base case around $110 — roughly in line with the analyst mean and ~15% above spot. That upside exists but is not asymmetric given the credit and rate risks, supporting a Valuation score of 5.5 and a HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

Axos occupies a distinctive position within regional banking: a digital-first, high-NIM, high-ROE outlier whose cost structure lets it out-earn traditional peers on a sustained basis. The branchless model is a durable structural advantage in efficiency — the efficiency ratio is well below the peer group and hard to replicate for incumbents carrying branch overhead.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the platform level. Axos is effectively arbitraging its low-cost deposit-servicing infrastructure and excess origination capacity by acquiring deposit books (Jenius, Capital One IRAs) and lending platforms (Verdant) at negotiated prices. This is a repeatable playbook that widens the funding-cost and scale advantages over time.

The mirror-image risks temper the score. Because Axos competes for deposits nationally on rate, its funding is price-sensitive — an inherent vulnerability of the branchless model versus banks with sticky, low-cost core-branch deposits. It sustains its NIM by leaning into higher-risk, higher-yield commercial niches (C&I, fund finance, equipment leasing), which is precisely where credit is now deteriorating. And it faces the standing threat of FinTech disintermediation in digital deposit gathering. On balance, the moat is widening in efficiency and scale but is being extended into riskier lending, so we score Competitive Position at 6.5 — an improving trajectory with a caveat, consistent with the recent upward drift in this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit quality in C&I is the most important watch-item.** The provision for credit losses spiked in Q3 (nine-month $83.3M vs $40.7M), and the charge-off rate rose. C&I Non-RE nonaccruals rose to $98.1M from $61.8M at June 2025, driven by management's disclosed "one Commercial & Industrial – Non-RE loan with unique credit risk characteristics." C&I Non-RE is now the largest loan bucket (35% of loans, $8.95B) and includes fund-finance/capital-call lines and equipment leases. The mitigant: total nonaccruals/loans actually improved to 0.71% from 0.79%, and the allowance covers nonaccruals at a healthy 192%. This reads as an idiosyncratic event, not yet a portfolio-wide problem, but given C&I is the single largest book, it merits close monitoring at the July 30 (FY26 Q4) and subsequent prints.

**Rate sensitivity cuts against Axos in an easing cycle.** The balance sheet is asset-sensitive (NII +5.2% in a +200bp scenario, but -5.3% over months 13–24 in a -100bp scenario). Because Axos runs an above-peer NIM, it has more margin to give back than lower-margin banks as rates fall. On a two-sided rate path, this is a genuine headwind if the Fed eventually eases.

**Concentration.** Roughly 35.8% of real-estate loans are in California and 28.2% in New York; CRE is 34% of loans ($8.7B). Office exposure is small (~$389M, 7% of CRE specialty) at conservative ~50% weighted-average LTVs, which meaningfully limits severity.

**Growth/integration and capital.** Assets grew 18% in nine months via organic origination plus multiple acquisitions; CET1 fell ~90bp and buybacks stopped. If growth outpaces retained earnings, the ATM could be tapped, introducing dilution risk. Roughly 15% of bank deposits ($3.6B) are uninsured/uncollateralized, and near-term reliance on FHLB advances adds funding-cost and rollover risk.

**Key-man and governance.** Heavy dependence on long-tenured CEO Gregory Garrabrants. Anti-takeover features (classified board, 75% supermajority provisions, no action by written consent) could suppress any takeover premium. A December 2025 8-K (Item 5.02) disclosed a director/officer change, the succession context of which is not detailed in the available filings. Legacy litigation is winding down — the BofI/Erhart-origin derivative suit was dismissed with prejudice (9/18/2025, though under appeal to the 9th Circuit) — but UFB Direct deposit-rate class actions and a COR Securities indemnification matter (~$7.4M paid April 2026) persist; none is deemed material by management.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing near-term caution. ATM implied volatility of 43.6% is rich versus one-year historical vol of 32.2% (an 11.4-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (43.6% near-term vs 35.8% at six months) — the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the July 30 FY26 Q4 earnings date. The put/call open-interest ratio surged from 0.21 to 2.63 over the past month, and the near-term put/call skew is elevated at 3.3%, indicating hedging demand for downside. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.9%. For a BUY thesis this positioning would be a warning; for a HOLD it reinforces waiting for the earnings binary to resolve before adding, and it argues that near-term IV is expensive relative to fundamentals (a favorable environment for selling premium rather than buying calls).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($115–125).** Jenius and Capital One IRA deposits close on terms, lowering funding costs and unwinding the FHLB reliance; NIM stabilizes above 4.5% as the cheaper deposit base offsets purchased-loan runoff. The single problem C&I loan resolves without spreading, provisions normalize back toward the 0.10–0.15% charge-off range, and the credit scare proves idiosyncratic. ROE holds near 16%, EPS re-accelerates toward the next-year $9.61 consensus, and the market re-rates the multiple toward the peer median (14.7x). At ~13x normalized $9.00+ EPS, the stock reaches the mid-$110s to $125.

**Bear Case ($72–78).** The C&I credit event proves the leading edge of broader deterioration in fund finance and equipment leasing; provisions stay elevated and charge-offs climb. The Fed eases, and Axos's asset-sensitive balance sheet gives back 5%+ of NII while the above-peer NIM compresses toward the group. Growth continues to consume capital, forcing ATM issuance and dilution. Estimates continue falling, the multiple de-rates toward 9–10x (OZK/ASB territory) on a lower earnings base, and the stock retraces to its 52-week low around $75 or below.

**Base Case ($108–112).** Deposit deals close and partially offset NIM pressure; the credit event stays contained but keeps provisions modestly elevated; core EPS grows high-single digits toward ~$9.00; the multiple holds around 12–13x. The stock drifts toward the analyst mean of ~$111 over 12 months, roughly 15% above spot but with meaningful two-way risk into the July 30 print.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: FY26 Q4 earnings (reported 7/30/2026) and the subsequent quarter, which will show the initial impact of the Jenius deposit book and the trajectory of C&I credit.**

This is a growth-oriented hold rather than a fresh-money buy. The bank is a genuine high-quality compounder, but with estimates falling and near-term options positioning defensive, the risk/reward does not justify chasing at current levels. The market has increasingly caught up to the earnings-quality and credit realities we describe, driven by valuation compression, and the current 5.85 score reflects that read.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Jenius and Capital One IRA deposits close on terms and management confirms funding-cost reduction that stabilizes NIM at or above 4.5%.
- C&I Non-RE nonaccruals decline and the net charge-off rate reverts toward 0.15% or below, confirming the credit event was idiosyncratic.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $80s that restores clear asymmetry (forward P/E toward ~10x on stable-to-rising estimates).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Provisions remain elevated (>$40M/quarter) with C&I Non-RE nonaccruals rising further, signaling portfolio-wide deterioration rather than a single loan.
- The company taps the ATM to fund growth, diluting per-share metrics without a commensurate earnings step-up.
- NIM compresses below ~4.3% on rate cuts before the deposit deals offset it, and CET1 slips below ~11% at the holding company.

## Conclusion

Axos Financial is one of the better-run banks in the regional universe — a branchless, high-efficiency, high-return franchise with a credible and repeatable deposit-book M&A engine. That quality is not in dispute. What has changed over the last two quarters is the risk profile: NIM is structurally compressing, provisions have tripled on a disclosed idiosyncratic C&I credit, capital buffers are thinning to fund aggressive growth, and consensus estimates are drifting lower even as the company posts headline beats. The GAAP earnings momentum overstates the core, and the market has correctly discounted rather than rewarded the recent "beat."

At current levels near the 52-week high, the valuation fairly prices the balance between top-tier returns and these emerging risks. The ~15% upside to the analyst mean of ~$111 is real but not asymmetric, and it comes with meaningful downside if credit spreads beyond one loan or the Fed's easing bites the asset-sensitive balance sheet. We rate AX a HOLD, favoring reassessment after the July 30 FY26 Q4 print and confirmation that the deposit acquisitions are lowering funding costs and the C&I credit event is contained. We would become more constructive on a pullback into the low-to-mid $80s or on clear evidence that NIM has stabilized and credit costs have normalized.
