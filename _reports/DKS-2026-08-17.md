## Executive Summary

DICK'S Sporting Goods is no longer the company that compounded 11% operating margins on a US big-box footprint. The September 8, 2025 acquisition of Foot Locker added 2,227 owned stores across roughly 20 countries, pushed footwear from 28% of consolidated sales in fiscal 2024 to 50.4% in the quarter ended May 2, 2026, and converted a high-return domestic retailer into a two-segment operator where the second segment earned a 0.98% margin in its best quarter to date. Fiscal 2025 revenue rose 28.1% to $17,215.1M while operating income fell 25.6% to $1,095.9M — 298 basis points of gross margin compression, of which 215bps was Foot Locker's structurally lower merchandise margin and 127bps was a Foot Locker inventory write-down and liquidation, partly offset by 44bps of genuine improvement in the legacy business.

The most important correction to any screen-driven view of this stock is the share count. The 10-Q cover page reports 65,931,904 common shares and 23,570,633 Class B shares outstanding — 89,502,537 total. The $12.9B market capitalization circulating in market-data feeds reflects only the common float. At $201.97 the correct equity value is $18,081M, and enterprise value is $18,989M excluding leases ($24,873M including $5,883.6M of operating lease liabilities). That moves the forward multiple from a spurious ~10x to 14.7x the midpoint of company GAAP guidance ($13.27–14.27) and 14.1x consensus current-year EPS of $14.29. Wells Fargo's independently derived "14–15x on 2027" corroborates the full-count basis.

On that corrected basis the asymmetry is thin. The core DICK'S segment grew sales 6.4% in Q1 and grew segment profit 0.2% — $360.975M versus $360.408M, a 0.3% incremental margin, with roughly $61.6M of the $62.2M gross-profit increment absorbed by segment costs. Adjusted EPS of $2.90 (reported $3.54 less $1.45 of interchange-settlement and lease-termination income, plus $0.81 of acquisition charges) missed the $2.93 consensus and fell approximately 14% from the prior-year comparable. Management has pre-announced that Q2 will carry the year's largest operating margin decline, and consensus for the current quarter is $3.80 against $4.38 a year ago. Consensus for the full year sits at $14.29, essentially at the top of the adjusted equivalent of company guidance. That is a demanding setup one week ahead of the August 25 print, which is also the first read on the back-to-school season management has called "an operational inflection point" for Foot Locker. Fifth consecutive HOLD, and the reasons have shifted from valuation to earnings quality.

## Company Overview

**Segment 1 — DICK'S Business.** 888 stores and 45.6M square feet as of May 2, 2026: 640 traditional DICK'S, 44 Field House, 36 House of Sport, 113 Golf Galaxy, 51 Going Going Gone!, 3 Public Lands. Fiscal 2025 segment sales $14,108.9M (+5.0%) at an 11.12% segment margin, essentially flat against 11.14% the prior year, on +4.5% comps. Also houses GameChanger, a youth-sports streaming/scorekeeping app with ~10M unique active users and nearly $150M of fiscal 2025 revenue.

**Segment 2 — Foot Locker Business.** 2,227 owned stores and 11.7M square feet: Foot Locker North America (716), Champs (364), Kids Foot Locker (357), WSS (100), Europe (567), Asia Pacific (94), atmos (29), plus 256 licensed locations generating royalties. Contributed $3,106.2M of sales and a $60.0M net loss over the ~5 months post-close, with a $(52.2)M segment loss — a stub period that excluded the August back-to-school peak.

Differentiated assets worth enumerating: vertical brands (CALIA, DSG, VRST, ETHOS, MAXFLI and licensed adidas/Cobra/Marucci marks) at $1.8B, ~13% of DICK'S Business sales at above-average margins, described in the filing as the second-largest "vendor" inside the segment; ScoreCard loyalty with ~30M members representing over 75% of DICK'S Business sales and ~8M Gold members driving over 50%; over 80% of DICK'S online orders fulfilled from stores; and lease optionality on roughly three-quarters of DICK'S locations coming up for renewal at the company's option over five years.

The dependency: Nike is ~31% of consolidated merchandise purchases with **no long-term purchase contracts** — all buying is on short-term purchase orders across ~1,500 vendors. Foot Locker's mix is more Nike-weighted than DICK'S, so the acquisition increased single-vendor concentration.

The practical implication is that incremental buybacks in fiscal 2026 draw on the $998M cash balance, the revolver, or commercial paper rather than on free cash flow. The $3.0B five-year authorization behind the $28.2M remaining on the 2021 program should not be read as near-term capacity. The dividend itself looks secure given the balance sheet, but it is being funded at the edge of internal cash generation during the capex peak.

**Financial health: 6.2.** Investment-grade, 17x interest coverage, 0.57x net funded leverage, $2B undrawn revolver, and accrual quality that is fine (operating cash flow exceeds net income). Offset by free cash flow that barely covers the dividend at the current capex run-rate, asset turnover falling from ~1.36x to ~0.99x on the enlarged base, and $5.88B of largely non-cancellable lease obligations.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is largely acquisitive. Fiscal 2026 sales guidance of $22.1–22.4B represents another ~29% increase, almost entirely the annualization of Foot Locker. The organic picture:

- **DICK'S comps** +4.5% in fiscal 2025 (ticket +4.2%, transactions +0.3%), +6.0% in Q1 fiscal 2026, guided +2.5–4.0% for the year (raised from +2–4%).
- **DICK'S segment profit** guided $1.60–1.68B, raised from $1.58–1.66B — implying an 11.0–11.4% margin, flat to fiscal 2025.
- **Foot Locker pro forma comps** −3.3% in fiscal 2025, +0.6% in Q1, guided +1.5–3.0% for fiscal 2026 (raised from +1–3%). International pro forma comps were −8.1% in fiscal 2025, the weakest disclosed operating metric in the filings.
- **Foot Locker segment profit** guided $110–150M, raised from $100–150M. Backing into implied segment revenue of roughly $6.8–8.4B, that is a guided margin of ~1.5–2.0%.

The unit growth program is heavy: 16 House of Sport opened in fiscal 2025 (36 at May 2), ~14 more planned for fiscal 2026 with ~18 in construction for 2027; ~22 Field House and ~15 Golf Galaxy Performance Centers in 2026; a sixth regional distribution center in Fort Worth. Over 75% of DICK'S openings are relocations or remodels. Pre-opening expense rose to $69.0M from $57.5M.

The problem is conversion to profit. In Q1, DICK'S segment sales rose $202.7M and segment profit rose $0.567M. Gross profit rose $62.2M — a respectable 30.7% incremental gross margin — and segment-level operating costs consumed nearly all of it. Management attributes Q1–Q2 SG&A pressure specifically to the timing of World Cup marketing and pre-opening expense for a heavier House of Sport calendar, which is a pre-announced and plausible explanation; pre-opening cost is investment in future units, not operating failure. One quarter cannot separate timing from a structural cost plateau. Q2 and Q3 will.

Consensus models the recovery: current-year $14.29, next-year $16.35 (+14.4%). Revisions are flat — current year −0.0% over 30 days and −0.1% over 90 days; next year +0.1% and +1.2%. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here, which matters: the strongest forward indicator in this framework is a large positive revision cluster, and it is absent.

**Growth prospects: 6.0.** Real organic comp momentum in a franchise with credible format innovation, plus a genuine Foot Locker inflection from a $(52.2)M segment loss to +$17.5M of profit. Discounted by zero incremental core profit last quarter, flat guided core margins, and −8.1% international comps that also gate the tax-rate normalization.

## Valuation Assessment

**Corrected basis.** 89,502,537 shares × $201.97 = $18,081M equity value; Q1 weighted-average diluted shares were 90,408K. Enterprise value $18,989M ex-leases, $24,873M including operating leases.

| P/E on consensus current year ($14.29) | 14.1x |
| P/E on consensus next year ($16.35) | 12.4x |
| EV/EBITDA (FY2026E ~$2.4B, ex-leases) | 7.9x |
| Dividend yield ($5.00) | 2.48% |
| FCF yield (guided $300–600M range) | 1.7–3.3% |

**Guidance is a GAAP guide.** It includes both the ~$200M of acquisition charges and the $174.5M of settlement income. Converting to an adjusted equivalent — add back ~$1.66/share of after-tax acquisition costs, subtract $1.45/share of after-tax settlement income — gives roughly $13.48–14.48. Consensus at $14.29 sits at 98.7% of that top end. The Q1 data point validates the basis: Street estimate $2.93 against adjusted actual $2.90, a 1.0% miss, the first in four quarters after beats of 19.1%, 1.5% and 0.4%.

**Peer context.** The Specialty Stores comparable set is heterogeneous: FIVE 30.2x, EYE 30.2x, SIG 12.8x, ASO 8.4x, SBH 8.3x, median 12.8x. The closest business comparable, Academy Sports, trades at 8.4x. DKS at 14.1x forward carries a premium to the median and to its nearest sporting-goods peer, which is defensible on scale, House of Sport differentiation, vertical brands and investment-grade credit, but it is not a discount that requires explaining.

**Where a mispricing could exist.** Analyst mean target is $251.05 (median $262, low $169, high $300), implying 24% upside, with 16 of 27 covering analysts positive. Wells Fargo upgraded to Overweight on August 10 with a $240 target, asserting a path to 7–8% Foot Locker margins. That figure is an analyst projection, not company guidance, and the arithmetic is large: roughly 6 points of margin on ~$7.5B of Foot Locker revenue is ~$450M pre-tax, ~$338M after tax, ~$3.73 per share — capitalized at 14x, about $52 per share or 26% of the current price. That is the entire bull case, and it is what the $251 consensus target embeds.

I do not credit consensus targets as an independent floor here. The conditions under which analyst targets systematically lag reality — rising estimates against a cheap multiple — are only half met: the multiple is moderate, but estimates are flat, not rising. When targets sit 24% above spot while estimates are unchanged, the gap reflects a scenario assumption rather than a revision cycle.

**Vesting conditions on the bull case,** none of which is contractually assured: sustained Nike wholesale re-engagement including improved premium allocation; Fast Break scaling from ~21 stores to ~250 before back-to-school with measurable comp lift; international comps recovering from −8.1%, which also unlocks roughly 430bps of tax drag (~$0.79 per share annualized on ~$1.66B of guided pre-tax income); integration charges landing inside the $750M ceiling against ~$590M cumulative expectation; and fleet rationalization completing without further eroding fixed-cost leverage.

**Valuation: 5.5.** Not expensive, not demonstrably mispriced. Paying 14–15x for a good core franchise whose incremental margin was zero last quarter, plus an unpriced-but-unproven option on a turnaround that requires a third party's allocation decisions. There is one unmodelled positive: the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized, with CBP's refund platform opened April 20. DKS applies gain-contingency accounting, has received nothing, and has excluded any refund from guidance. Amount, eligibility and whether recoveries accrue to DKS or to importers of record are all undisclosed. It carries zero in my base case.

## Competitive Landscape

The strategic logic of the acquisition is a bet on the partial reversal of brand direct-to-consumer disintermediation. Externally reported context indicates Nike delivered flat revenue in its fiscal 2026 with a difficult Q4, has underperformed the S&P 500 by the widest margin in roughly 25 years, and that its DTC pivot improved digital while weakening retail-partner relationships — with a reported wholesale re-engagement underway under CEO Elliott Hill. If that re-engagement is durable, DKS bought the largest global sneaker specialty platform at a cyclical trough in wholesale allocation for ~0.31x sales ($2,512.7M total consideration against ~$8.0B of standalone Foot Locker revenue). If Nike's own recovery stalls, DKS owns 2,227 leased stores levered to it. The 10-K competition section also names "vendors selling directly to consumers" — the same supplier is a competitor.

Management has moved deliberately on the personnel side: Ann Freeman, President Foot Locker North America, spent 26 years at Nike including GM/VP for North America; EVP-CTO Vlad Rak came from Nike enterprise architecture; Matthew Barnes runs International after leading Aldi Süd and Tesco UK. Rational and observable steps toward the thesis, with no disclosed allocation commitment behind them.

The restructuring is more aggressive than "turnaround" language implies. Foot Locker owned stores fell from 2,307 to 2,227 in one quarter — 85 closures against 5 openings, 62 attributed to a review of unproductive assets — with square footage down 5.6%. WSS was cut from 143 stores to 100, a 30.1% reduction in a single quarter. A March 23, 2026 reorganization eliminated positions and required relocations to Foot Locker's New York headquarters, at $45–55M of expected charges with $38.4M already taken. This is shrinking to a profitable core rather than attempting broad fleet recovery — decisive, and also an admission that the acquired asset base was worse than scoped.

Against that, the DICK'S franchise is genuinely strong: 11.1% segment margins, 44bps of gross margin expansion in fiscal 2025, $1.8B of higher-margin owned brands, 30M loyalty members, and House of Sport as a demonstrated real-estate differentiator the filing says drives engagement with athletes, brand partners and landlords.

**Competitive position: 5.5.** Trajectory is genuinely mixed rather than clearly improving. Core segment stable-to-improving; acquired segment being shrunk toward profitability with rising single-vendor dependency and a mix shift toward a lower-margin, more fashion-cyclical category.

## Risk Assessment

**Nike concentration.** ~31% of consolidated merchandise purchases with no long-term contracts, and the dependency rose with the acquisition. Vendor return privileges, volume allowances, co-op advertising and markdown allowances are discretionary. The recovery thesis is substantially a bet on a decision made outside the company.

**Impairment.** Foot Locker goodwill of $567.2M at May 2, 2026 (after a $46.0M measurement-period adjustment and $5.0M of currency) plus $710.0M of indefinite-lived trade names — roughly $1,277M, or 22.8% of book equity — is disclosed as carrying value *approximating* fair value, with no cushion, while the remainder of goodwill and intangibles is described as substantially in excess. Five months after an acquisition that is the expected disclosure and is not itself evidence of overpayment, but a stalled turnaround produces a large non-cash charge. It would hit GAAP EPS, book equity, ROE and screening scores; it would not affect operating cash flow, covenants, ratings or the dividend. Acquisition accounting is not final on litigation contingencies and income taxes.

**Integration cost trajectory.** Total expected pre-tax charges $500–750M; $390M incurred in fiscal 2025; the fiscal 2026 estimate raised from ~$150M to ~$200M within ten weeks with $96.5M already taken in Q1. Cumulative expectation ~$590M against a $750M ceiling. Guided synergies are only $100–125M in the medium term, primarily procurement and direct sourcing — restructuring cash cost is roughly five times the annual synergy run-rate. The economics rest on revenue and margin recovery, not cost extraction.

**Tax.** Q1 effective rate 28.3% versus 24.0%, driven by unbenefited Foot Locker foreign losses. Each 100bps is ~$16.6M or ~$0.18 per share on guided pre-tax income; the 430bps increase is roughly $0.79 per share of drag that persists until international profitability, against −8.1% international comps.

**Litigation.** *In re Dick's Sporting Goods Securities Litigation* (W.D. Pa.) was partially dismissed April 3, 2026 — shrinkage, risk-factor and certain inventory claims dismissed, some inventory statements surviving; defendants answered June 3. A derivative action was stayed June 2. Three §220 books-and-records demands were received and all three objected to; *Anderson v. DICK'S* (Del. Ch.) remains stayed. No loss estimate or accrual is disclosed.

**Governance.** Class B holders — Edward Stack, relatives and family trusts — control a majority of combined voting power. The Chief Operating Decision Maker is disclosed as the Executive Chairman and the President/CEO acting jointly, an atypical arrangement consistent with the risk-factor statement that Stack (71) continues to oversee merchandising and the Foot Locker business. Key-person risk is named for both Hobart and Stack.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 38.1% against 35.9% one-year realized is fair, at the 1st percentile within the current chain — no near-term stress premium in the three-day tenor. The information is in the term structure: one-month IV of 49.2% with an ±11.9% implied move brackets the August 25 print, and three- and six-month tenors sit at 41.2%/41.3% with a ±20.2% six-month implied move. Contango, so no acute near-term dislocation is priced. Put/call open-interest ratio rose from 2.94 to 3.88 between July 23 and August 17 while price fell 3.8% — protection or short-related structure accumulating in size, consistent with 15.1% short interest and 6.8 days to cover. Skew values are reported as balanced at the front (−1.8%) and larger at six months (−5.5%); I make no directional claim from the sign convention. Unusual activity is two-sided: 1,127 September $200 calls (7.2x open interest), 1,134 September $180 puts (4.0x), and 1,096 September $220 calls (3.4x). Read together: the market prices a large event a week out without a consensus direction, and the growing put open interest raises the cost of being wrong on the long side without confirming the bear case.

**Risk factors: 4.6.** Multiple identifiable, dated downside catalysts — an imminent print into which consensus sits at the top of the adjusted guide, a $1.28B impairment tail with no cushion, an integration-cost ceiling being approached, and a single-vendor dependency outside management's control — against a 15.1% short base that cuts both ways.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($275, +36%).** Foot Locker's Q1 inflection to +$17.5M of segment profit on +0.6% pro forma comps is the first real evidence, and back-to-school 2026 — which management has framed as an operational inflection point — delivers the second. Fast Break scales to ~250 stores with measurable comp lift; Nike wholesale re-engagement improves premium allocation; the 85 closures and the WSS reduction remove the loss-making tail; international comps recover from −8.1%, which also unwinds ~430bps of tax drag worth ~$0.79 per share. Foot Locker margin works toward 4–5% by fiscal 2028 rather than the 1.5–2.0% guided for fiscal 2026, adding roughly $2.00–2.50 per share; combined EPS reaches $18–19 and the multiple holds at ~15x on a de-risked story. Q1–Q2 SG&A pressure proves to be the timing effect management described, DICK'S incremental margins normalize toward 25–30%, capex steps down after the 2027 House of Sport pipeline is delivered, and free cash flow re-expands above $1B. An unquantified IEEPA tariff refund would be incremental.

**Bear case ($155, −23%).** Foot Locker stalls near breakeven; Nike's own flat revenue trajectory means allocation improvement is slower and less premium than assumed; the DICK'S 0.3% incremental margin proves structural rather than timing, so a +3% comp produces no profit growth; integration charges breach $750M; a goodwill and trade-name charge against the $1,277M with no cushion hits book equity and headline EPS; consumer discretionary spending weakens into holiday. EPS resets to ~$13.00 and the multiple compresses toward 11.5–12x on a lower-quality, more fashion-cyclical mix — $150–160, against a 52-week low of $186.67 that would not hold.

**Base case ($215, +6.4%).** Fiscal 2026 lands near the guidance midpoint with Q2 optically weak as pre-announced; Foot Locker delivers $110–150M of segment profit; DICK'S comps come in inside +2.5–4.0% with flat margins; fiscal 2027 EPS approaches consensus $16.35 and the market pays ~13.1x on a mix that is now half footwear and lower-margin than the pre-deal franchise. Total return including the 2.48% dividend is roughly 9%.

Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives an expected price return of approximately +6% plus dividend — modestly positive, with a wide distribution and low confidence on the single variable (Nike allocation) that dominates the outcome.

**On the trajectory.** Scores have declined from 5.8 in March to 5.5 in July, with valuation down 1.1 points and competitive position down 1.0 while risk factors improved 0.8. Current evidence stabilizes rather than reverses that: price is essentially unchanged from the July snapshot ($203.97 then, $201.97 now), estimates are flat, and the new information since — the confirmation that Q1 adjusted EPS of $2.90 was a small miss, the Wells Fargo and JPMorgan upgrades, and the still-pending Q2 print — nets to neutral. Four prior HOLDs and this fifth one, but for a different reason: earlier reports rested on valuation being full, whereas the binding constraint now is core earnings quality with a pre-warned print a week away.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders maintain exposure; prospective buyers have no urgency ahead of August 25.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q2/Q3 evidence that DICK'S segment incremental margin recovers above 15% — specifically, segment profit growth of at least half the rate of segment sales growth. Q1's 0.3% incremental margin on +6.4% sales is the single fact that most constrains the rating.
2. Foot Locker segment margin above 3% on a back-to-school quarter, with pro forma comps at or above the +3% top of guidance, evidencing that the margin path toward mid-single digits is real rather than an analyst assumption.
3. Price near $170–178 (roughly 10.5–11x next-year consensus of $16.35, near the $169 low analyst target) with fundamentals intact, which would restore a genuine margin of safety and make the Foot Locker option close to free.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Fiscal 2026 EPS guidance cut for operating reasons — DICK'S segment profit guidance below $1.60B or Foot Locker below $110M — as distinct from the last cut, which reconciled almost entirely to $50M of additional integration spend ($50M × 0.75 ÷ 90.4M shares = $0.41 against a $0.43 reduction).
2. Any goodwill or trade-name impairment against the $1,277M with no disclosed cushion, or cumulative acquisition charges guided above the $750M ceiling.
3. DICK'S comps below +2.5% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the core franchise is participating in a broader discretionary slowdown rather than taking share.
4. Evidence of adverse Nike allocation — declining footwear gross margin alongside falling footwear comps, or disclosure of reduced vendor allowances.

**Reassessment date: August 25, 2026** (Q2 fiscal 2026 results, ±11.9% implied move). This release carries the pre-announced worst margin optic of the year alongside the first substantive back-to-school evidence on Foot Locker — the most informative disclosure available in the next twelve months. I would not add ahead of it.

**Position management.** For holders, the 2.48% dividend is well covered by earnings (36% of guided midpoint) and secure against a $908M net funded debt position, an undrawn $2.0B revolver and a $500M commercial paper program, even though it consumes most of guided free cash flow during the capex peak. That supports holding through the print rather than de-risking into it.

## Conclusion

The corrected share count changes the framing more than any operating datapoint: at $201.97 on 89.5M shares this is an $18.1B equity, $19.0B enterprise value business trading at 14.7x GAAP guidance and 12.4x next-year consensus, not the ~10x a float-only market cap implies. On that basis the stock is fairly priced for what is currently verifiable — a strong domestic franchise generating 11% segment margins with flat guided margins and zero incremental profit last quarter, plus an acquired business guided to a 1.5–2.0% margin that has produced exactly one quarter of positive segment profit.

The $251 mean analyst target and the Wells Fargo $240 both embed a Foot Locker margin recovery toward 7–8% that company guidance does not support for fiscal 2026 and that depends on Nike allocation decisions disclosed nowhere in the filings — the 10-K states affirmatively that no long-term purchase contracts exist with any vendor. That option may well pay; it is worth roughly $52 per share if it does. But it is an option on a third party's strategy, purchased with $590M of expected restructuring cash against $100–125M of annual synergies, priced into a stock at 14x forward earnings with free cash flow at or below the dividend and $1.28B of goodwill and trade names carrying no valuation cushion.

Expected return is modestly positive at roughly 9% including the dividend, with a −23% bear case that a stalled turnaround makes real. That is a HOLD, and the classification is honestly uncertain rather than a confident growth thesis.
