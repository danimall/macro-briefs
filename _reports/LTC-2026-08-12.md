## Executive Summary

LTC Properties is roughly halfway through the largest business-model change in its 34-year history, converting a triple-net-lease and mortgage-lending portfolio into a Senior Housing Operating Portfolio (SHOP/RIDEA) built from a zero base in Q2 2025. SHOP went from $0 at 12/31/24 to $565.3M at 12/31/25 to $801.0M and 34 communities at 6/30/26, with management targeting 50% of pro-forma NOI by year-end 2026 and 75% by end-2028. The strategic logic is defensible: senior housing faces a demographic demand wave against roughly two-decade-low new supply, LTC's remaining triple-net seniors housing book covers rent at only 1.16x EBITDAR (so the fixed-rent claim provides little downside protection anyway), and the company is exiting skilled nursing at large premiums to a deeply depreciated book — anticipated 2026 gains on sale exceed $300M, and a single post-quarter Oregon SNF sold for $34.2M against a ~$33.0M gain.

The problem is that none of this is currently reaching per-share cash flow. Q2 2026 Core FFO/share was $0.68, exactly flat year over year, and Core FAD/share fell to $0.70 from $0.71, on 13.4% more diluted shares. Full-year 2026 guided Core FAD of $2.83-$2.85 (midpoint $2.84) sits ~1.0% below the 2025 actual of $2.87 disclosed in the proxy's pay-versus-performance table. The arithmetic is straightforward: ~$730M of dispositions and loan payoffs carrying $53M of annualized income (a 7.26% surrendered yield) are being recycled into SHOP assets at a 7.10% weighted cap rate, funded in part by 5,257,220 shares issued for $198.1M net at an average $37.68 — a 7.35% implied Core FFO yield, or a negative ~25bp day-one spread on the metric that governs reported results. Co-CEO Pam Kessler attributed "limited near-term funds from operations growth" to the transition on the Q2 call and called 2026 "a heavy lift transformation year."

At $38.82 the shares trade at ~14.0x guided Core FFO and ~13.7x Core FAD, with an 80% Core FAD payout and a balance sheet at 29.7% debt-to-gross-asset-value against a 60% covenant limit. That combination supports the price and the dividend, but it does not offer asymmetry: the price sits 6.7% above where we last reviewed it in June, essentially at the low end of the sell-side target range ($38.00-$47.00, mean $41.57), and a dated Q4 2026 event — Prestige Healthcare's expected par repayment of a $179.875M mortgage carrying $20.3M of annualized interest — removes roughly $0.14/share of income if redeployed at observed cap rates. We rate LTC a HOLD on income grounds: the expected 12-month total return is close to the dividend, with a lower risk-adjusted case than in June.

## Company Overview

LTC is a self-administered Maryland REIT headquartered in Westlake Village, California, with 25 employees as of 12/31/25 — a genuinely tiny corporate platform managing $2,492.6M of gross investments across 185 properties, 16,291 units/beds, 23 states and 31 operators at 6/30/26. Portfolio composition by gross investment: seniors housing $1,686.7M (67.6%), skilled nursing $777.5M (31.2%), other/development $28.4M.

The structural feature that distinguishes LTC from other health care REITs is that it is nearly as much a lender as a landlord. At 6/30/26, gross investment splits across triple-net leases ($982.8M, 46.6% of TTM NOI), SHOP ($801.0M, 21.0%), mortgage loans ($396.1M, 19.8%), financing receivables ($286.9M, 11.3%) and notes receivable ($25.7M, 1.3%). Roughly 28% of NOI arrives through credit instruments rather than owned real estate. The financing-receivables bucket is JV-owned property leased to ALG Senior Living under master leases containing purchase options with specified exit IRRs, which forces financing rather than lease accounting.

The pro-forma transformation LTC quantifies in its Q2 supplemental: NOI from seniors housing rising from 43% (2024) to 77%; skilled nursing falling 57% to 22%; owned real estate from 76% to 89%; mortgage loans from 20% to 9%.

## Financial Analysis

**Headline numbers are unusable without adjustment, and the distortions run in both directions.** FY2025 revenue rose 25.3% to $262.9M, but the bridge from MD&A shows resident fees and services contributing +$72.1M — a brand-new line created when triple-net leases converted to SHOP and LTC began consolidating gross resident revenue and operating expense — against rental income −$16.1M and mortgage interest −$6.2M. Total NOI grew from $199.4M to $204.7M, or 2.7%. Any screen keying on revenue growth overstates LTC by roughly 23 percentage points.

Reported Nareit FFO/share fell 42%, from $2.84 (FY2024) to $1.66 (FY2025), driven overwhelmingly by a single non-cash item: a $41.455M write-off of effective interest receivable recorded in Q3 2025 alongside a modification of the Prestige mortgage. The reliable anchor for underlying earnings power is the proxy's company-selected measure, Diluted FAD excluding non-recurring items per share: $2.43 (2021), $2.68, $2.75, $2.67, **$2.87 (2025)** — a 4.25% four-year CAGR and confirmation that 2025 was a good underlying year despite the FFO optics.

**The 2026 guide is where the thesis lives.** Guided Core FFO $2.76-$2.78, Core FAD $2.83-$2.85, GAAP diluted EPS $8.08-$8.10 — of which $6.36 is gain on sale net of impairment and $1.04 is D&A add-back. EPS-based valuation of LTC is meaningless; the supplied profile fields (trailing and forward P/E both 14.39 on EPS of $2.52, market cap computed off 51.18M shares) are internally inconsistent with each other and with the 53.9M shares outstanding at 6/30/26. We use Core FFO and Core FAD throughout.

Interim results corroborate the flat-per-share picture without ambiguity. Q2 2026: total revenue $98.9M versus $60.2M; net income attributable to LTC $29.6M versus $15.1M; diluted EPS $0.56 versus $0.32 (helped by $7.562M of gains); Core FFO/share $0.68, flat; Core FAD/share $0.70 versus $0.71. Six-month Core FFO/share $1.37 versus $1.34 (+2.2%) and Core FAD/share $1.43 versus $1.41 (+1.4%), on 9.6% more shares.

**Recycling economics.** The disposition program of ~$730M carries $53M of 2026 annualized income, a 7.26% surrendered yield. The 2026 SHOP acquisition vintage prices at a 7.10% weighted cap rate (computed from the disclosed deal-level rates: $108.0M at 7.0%, $9.2M at 8.1%, $54.25M at 6.8%, $72.5M at 6.9%, $40.0M at 7.2%, $95.35M at 7.4%). The 2025 vintage priced at 7.42% on the same method. The recycling is modestly yield-dilutive on day one, before the maintenance capex and G&A that owned operating assets require and mortgage loans do not, and the acquisition spread is compressing about 32bp per vintage. The entire economic case rests on the growth differential: core SHOP NOI is guided +14% in 2026 with REVPOR +5.5% against EXPOR +3.0%, versus low-single-digit escalation on the surrendered rent and fixed mortgage interest.

**Balance sheet — the strongest part of the story.** Total debt $777.1M at a 4.31% weighted-average rate ($200.0M revolver, $198.4M term loans, $378.7M senior unsecured notes). Debt/gross asset value 29.7% (from 39.5% at 12/31/23); debt/Adjusted EBITDAre 4.2x; fixed-charge coverage 4.9x; debt/enterprise value 27.0%. The credit facility was upsized $300M to $1.1B in Q2 2026 with a $2.0B accordion, maturing July 2029; post-quarter draws took the revolver to $356.1M with $543.9M available. Covenant headroom is wide (total debt/asset value ≤0.60 against ~0.297; fixed-charge coverage ≥1.50x against 4.9x). $400M notional of swaps at 4.95%-4.99% is in place.

One data-integrity note: any distress screen applied to LTC will mis-fire. A REIT that must distribute taxable income carries permanently negative retained earnings, and real estate turns assets at roughly 0.13x — both are penalized heavily by models calibrated on manufacturers. The direct solvency evidence (4.9x fixed-charge coverage, 4.2x leverage, half the permitted covenant leverage, ~$829M of liquidity) describes a conservatively financed company. The $252.9M of "short-term debt" reported at 12/31/25 is the revolver, which is not contractually current.

**Dividend coverage is adequate and static.** FY2025 operating cash flow $136.0M against $107.4M of dividends (1.27x). Six-month 2026 OCF $65.1M against $59.0M (1.10x), but that ratio should not be annualized — the 10-Q shows roughly $12.3M of six-month working-capital drag from interest-receivable and accrual timing. The cleaner measure is six-month Core FAD of $72.0M against $59.0M of dividends, 1.22x. On full-year guidance the $2.28 dividend is 82.3% of Core FFO and 80.3% of Core FAD. The distribution has been $0.19/month across every period disclosed. Coverage is real; incremental coverage is not being created, since guided 2026 Core FAD/share does not exceed 2025's.

**Earnings surprise history is not informative here.** The reported record (Q3 2025 −242%, Q4 2025 +69%, Q1 2026 −10.0%, Q2 2026 +11.3%) tracks GAAP EPS dominated by the Prestige write-off in one quarter and disposition gains in others. Estimate revisions are similarly muddled — current-year consensus of $2.06 reconciles to neither the $8.08-$8.10 GAAP guide nor the $2.77 Core FFO guide, and coverage is 1-3 analysts per period. The next-year figure of $1.50, down 8.5% over 90 days, directionally matches the Prestige income cliff, but the basis mismatch makes it too unreliable to weight.

## Growth Analysis

Gross investment growth is unambiguous and large: $700M-$1,100M of 2026 investment guidance (raised $300M at the midpoint), with $171M closed through Q2, $208M in July, $321M in the August-September pipeline and ~$200M anticipated in Q4. Post-quarter SHOP acquisitions totaled $207.85M across Minnesota (Lifespark, 7.4% cap), New Mexico/Colorado (MorningStar, 6.9%) and Wisconsin (Health Dimensions Group, a new operator relationship, 7.2% including budgeted renovation).

Portfolio quality is improving: average SHOP property age is 10 years against 18 for the triple-net book, over 75% of expected acquisitions sit in NIC primary markets, ~68% of the total portfolio is in the top 100 MSAs, and average community size is ~110 units with more than half offering the full IL/AL/MC continuum.

The industry backdrop is the strongest element of the growth case. Occupancy in the strongest NIC primary markets has reached the mid-90s, and new supply is running at roughly two-decade lows, which is what allows management to guide rate growth (REVPOR +5.5%) above cost growth (EXPOR +3.0%). There is also a genuine organic positive: a ten-property portfolio with market-based rent resets contributed $1.2M in Q2 with $2.72M anticipated over the balance of 2026, a full-year $5.12M representing a 24% increase over 2025.

Against that, three specific brakes on per-share growth:

1. **Occupancy is running behind plan.** Year-to-date SHOP occupancy of 89.7% was flat versus 2025 and roughly 90bp below LTC's own expectations, though +145bp year over year. Core SHOP (27 properties) occupancy was 90.0% in Q2 with a 26.9% NOI margin; total SHOP margin declined to 24.8% from 28.1% in Q4 2025 as acquisitions dilute the average. SHOP is an operating business now, so the NOI margin, not the cap rate, determines the return.
2. **The Prestige repayment.** LTC expects Prestige to repay the $179.875M mortgage at par in Q4 2026 (contingent on Prestige obtaining replacement financing). That loan represents $20.312M of annualized GAAP interest. Redeploying $179.875M at the observed 7.10% weighted cap rate generates $12.77M — a $7.54M annualized shortfall, roughly $0.14 per diluted share on Q2's 52.198M shares. Using the 4.33% revolver rate instead produces a worse outcome, which is why redeployment into SHOP is the superior use. This is a static single-variable sensitivity that excludes releveraging and SHOP NOI growth.
3. **Equity funding.** Shares outstanding rose from 48.48M to 53.91M in six months (+11.2%). Only $88.6M of ATM capacity remained at 6/30/26 against roughly $500M of remaining 2026 acquisitions; an 8-K under Items 1.01/1.02 and a 424B5 filed simultaneously on August 6 match LTC's prior ATM-renewal pattern, though the size of any new program is unverified. Management indicated 2027 funding should revert to roughly 70% equity / 30% debt.

Netting these, our 2027 Core FFO estimate is $2.80-$2.90 — flat to +4% versus 2026 — with the SHOP NOI compounding and full-year contribution from H2 2026 acquisitions roughly offsetting the Prestige cliff and continued equity issuance. Growth prospects score 5.5: real medium-term potential in SHOP operating leverage, no per-share growth visible over the next four to six quarters.

Peer P/E comparisons are uninformative in this sector (MPT −22.2x, HR −76.7x, SBRA 80.4x, UHT 30.7x on the supplied data) because GAAP earnings for health care REITs are dominated by depreciation and gains. The usable relative metric is yield: peer median 5.7% against LTC's 5.87%. LTC therefore trades at a modest yield premium to the group and a wide premium to NHI (4.9%), the closest structural comparable as a small-cap SNF/seniors-housing REIT. That premium is consistent with, rather than corrective of, LTC's flat near-term per-share trajectory and five consecutive years of TSR underperformance (indexed +21.7% since 2020 versus +37.8% for its proxy peer group, lagging in every single year).

Two NAV-side supports deserve weight. First, mortgage loans carry at $392.1M against a disclosed fair value of $473.7M at an 8.8% discount rate — an $81.6M premium (~$1.51/share) driven by the 10.3%-11.3% Prestige coupons. Par repayment surrenders a demonstrably above-market asset. Second, the disposition program's anticipated gains exceeding $300M on roughly $730M of proceeds establish that the legacy portfolio's market value materially exceeds depreciated book equity of $1,075M (~$19.9/share at 12/31/25). Book value is not the constraint on this stock.

What is missing is a mispricing catalyst. At 14.0x Core FFO with a flat guide, LTC is priced approximately where a small-cap health care REIT with 30% leverage, an 80% payout and no per-share growth should be priced. The equity-issuance overhang mechanically caps upside: strength in the shares is met with ATM supply funding the remaining ~$500M acquisition program.

The sell-side distribution (1 strong buy, 7 holds; price at the low target, 7% below the mean) corroborates this read. On a stock at 14x with flat estimates, targets are not a leading indicator either way.

**Valuation score: 5.3.** Fairly priced with yield support and an NAV floor, but no identified asymmetry and a 6.7% higher price than at our June review on unchanged per-share earnings power.

## Competitive Landscape

LTC's durable edge is relationship-driven origination with small and mid-sized private regional operators, plus willingness to deploy structured capital (mortgage loans, mezzanine, preferred equity, JVs with retained non-controlling interests) in sizes larger REITs find uneconomic. Of the top ten non-SHOP operators, only Brookdale is public; Prestige, ALG, Encore, HMG, Carespring, Fundamental, Ignite and Juniper are private. On the Q2 call, EVP Gibson Satterwhite said most transactions have been or will be with existing senior-living operating partners, which reduces underwriting and transition friction versus broadly marketed portfolios.

The disadvantage is scale and cost of capital. At $2.5B of gross investment and 25 employees, LTC is roughly an order of magnitude smaller than Welltower or Ventas and has no internal operating platform, so its SHOP build depends entirely on third-party managers. It is also executing a consensus trade: National Healthcare Properties is running the same pivot, American Healthcare REIT is expanding SHOP aggressively, and Welltower and Ventas have been at it for years. The consequence appears directly in LTC's own disclosures: acquisition cap rates compressed from 7.42% (2025 vintage) to 7.10% (2026), with the two most recent MorningStar deals at 6.8% and 6.9%. Buying into a crowded trade at tightening spreads against lower-cost-of-capital acquirers is the central competitive question.

On the sell side, LTC is exiting skilled nursing into what looks like a strong bid, realizing gains exceeding $300M. That is good timing on price. It is also an exit from LTC's better-covered asset class (SNF EBITDAR coverage 1.99x versus seniors housing 1.16x) at a moment when SNF reimbursement is improving (FY2027 Medicare +2.4%; the CMS minimum staffing mandate suspended through September 30, 2034 by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act) into an asset class where LTC will absorb 100% of operating volatility at 25%-27% NOI margins.

**Competitive position score: 5.2** — asset quality and private-pay mix improving, but no scale or cost-of-capital advantage in an increasingly contested acquisition market, and a five-year record of relative underperformance.

## Risk Assessment

**Prestige repayment (dated, Q4 2026).** The largest identified single earnings event. Prestige was 7.8% of revenues and 11.9% of total assets at 6/30/26 (11.9% and 12.6% at FY2025), with $267.8M of total gross investment. Effective 7/1/25 LTC raised current pay on the loan from 8.5% to the full contractual 11.14% and granted a 12-month par-prepayment window beginning July 2026, contingent on replacement financing; in conjunction, LTC wrote off $41.455M of accrued effective interest receivable. Prestige has given notice of intent to repay and was current through August 2026. Two-sided: if Prestige cannot refinance, LTC keeps an 11.3% asset (better for near-term FFO) but retains concentrated exposure to a private SNF operator whose accrued interest has already been written off once.

**Thin seniors housing coverage.** Same-property EBITDAR coverage through 3/31/26 was 1.16x for seniors housing (1.40x EBITDARM) at 85.1% occupancy — a ~14% operator-level NOI decline breaches 1.0x. This is the quiet risk in the remaining $885.6M triple-net seniors housing book, and a plausible reason management is converting it.

**SHOP execution.** The FY2025 10-K moved SHOP risks to the front of Item 1A with three new risk factors (operational/legal liability, dependence on third-party managers, difficulty replacing managers). Roughly 32% of core SHOP units are standalone memory care — higher acuity, higher labor intensity, greater staffing sensitivity. A meaningful slice of SHOP is a turnaround of assets recovered from a failing tenant: the 12 Anthem Memory Care communities (732 units, $156.0M, 19.5% of SHOP) entered SHOP because LTC terminated Anthem's leases in Q2 2025 and wrote off a $2.7M working-capital note.

**Concentration.** The 10-Q's statement that no operator exceeded 10% of revenues in 6M26 is substantially a measurement artifact — consolidating $105.7M of gross SHOP resident revenue enlarged the denominator, while Prestige gross investment was flat at ~$268M and ALG at ~$298M. Non-SHOP top five operators are 55.4% of annualized cash NOI (Prestige 18.3%, ALG 13.7%); SHOP top three are 60.4% of SHOP gross investment (Lifespark 24.3%, Anthem 19.5%, Arbor 16.6%); Wisconsin alone is 31.1% of SHOP.

**Callable income.** Thirty properties carry outstanding tenant purchase options ($142.9M gross, windows 2027-2029), and all $286.9M of ALG financing receivables are subject to purchase options with specified exit IRRs of 8.0%-9.0%. Combined with the Prestige prepayment right, a material portion of the income base is callable at counterparties' discretion, a persistent reinvestment headwind that partly explains why gross investment growth does not convert to per-share growth.

**Regulatory.** Mixed. Positive: FY2026 SNF PPS +3.2%, FY2027 +2.4%, staffing mandate suspended to 2034. Negative: a CMS final rule dated January 29, 2026 tightens state use of provider taxes to fund Medicaid, a direct threat to SNF Medicaid reimbursement; Medicare 2% sequestration runs through February 2033. A new risk factor cites legislative proposals ranging from added oversight up to prohibitions on investors acquiring or increasing ownership/control in nursing homes — a tail risk for SNF-owning REITs that LTC's pivot reduces.

**Funding sequencing.** The Q4 2026 acquisition plan (~$200M) leans on the Q4 disposition/payoff schedule ($443.0M, which by magnitude and stated timing very likely includes Prestige, though the composition is not itemized) and on a replacement ATM whose size is unverified. This is a pacing question, not a solvency one — $543.9M of revolver availability and 29.7% leverage against a 60% covenant give ample flexibility.

**Genesis** filed Chapter 11 in Q3 2025 (LTC wrote off $1.3M of straight-line rent) but has paid contractual rent through August 2026; exposure is six SNFs, $53.3M gross investment, $10.0M annualized cash NOI (6.2% of non-SHOP NOI). Economically a non-event so far.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 21 contracts across three expirations — so treat this as low-confidence color. Near-term ATM IV of 29.0% against 19.9% one-year realized is rich, with backwardation (29.0% at eight days versus 23.2% at 36 days) and an implied move of ±4.9% on the nearest expiry. That is unusual given the Q2 print already landed on August 5. Positioning is call-tilted: put/call skew −9.8%, put/call open-interest ratio 0.32 (up from 0.06 in June), no meaningful put volume. Short interest is 5.1% of float at 4.0 days to cover — uncrowded. Read together: no evidence of institutional downside hedging, some upside-call accumulation, and near-term vol priced above realized. Nothing here contradicts the fundamental view; the rich near-term vol against a fairly-valued equity argues that selling covered calls is a more attractive expression than buying the stock outright for holders comfortable capping upside.

**Risk factors score: 4.9** — the balance sheet insulates solvency, but the density of company-specific, partially-priced binaries (Prestige, 1.16x coverage, SHOP execution below plan, callable income, Medicaid provider-tax rule) is above average for a REIT at this multiple.

## Management Quality

Forward-looking evidence is genuinely mixed and, on balance, modestly positive.

**Alignment is well constructed.** Performance measures are Diluted Core FFO/share, Diluted Core FAD/share, Adjusted FAD/share, ROE and cumulative TSR, with the company-selected measure being Diluted Core FAD/share. That is unusually appropriate for a company deploying ~$900M in a single year — management is not paid to grow the balance sheet. It also means the guided 2026 Core FAD/share decline to $2.83-$2.85 from $2.87 registers on their own scorecard. CEO compensation is modest for the sector: Kessler's FY2025 total was $4.03M with a CEO/NEO average ratio of ~3.0x. Insider ownership is roughly 1.0M shares among the top six executives, ~1.9% of shares outstanding.

**Insider buying is the right people at the right time.** CIO David Boitano bought 10,000 shares at $34.69-$34.85 on June 4, 2026 (~$347,700), near the apparent 2026 low. Investment Committee chairman David Gruber bought 11,000 shares at $38.00 and $38.41 in March and May (~$419,640). Together ~$767K from the two individuals with the most direct visibility into the acquisition pipeline. The 10-Q states no director or officer adopted, modified or terminated a Rule 10b5-1 arrangement during the six months, which supports discretionary timing. All other dispositions in the record are code F tax withholding, except one small director sale offsetting an annual grant. The absolute amounts are modest, so this is corroborative rather than decisive.

**Against that:** equity has been issued at a negative day-one Core FFO spread to acquisition cap rates; the proxy shows relative TSR underperformance in each of five consecutive years; and the Prestige episode means roughly $41.5M (~$0.89/share) of interest income recognized in FFO over approximately 2016-2025 was never collected. The two readings of that write-off — crystallization of a pre-existing impairment, or a trade of a nominal receivable for +264bp of current cash pay plus a clean exit — are both consistent with the filings, but the arithmetic favors the first: at ~1.5 years to repayment, the incremental cash from the higher pay rate is only about $7.1M against $41.5M surrendered. One ambiguous governance item: Executive Chairman Simpson received 67,721 shares in the March 2026 round versus 33,226 each for the Co-CEOs, though she received no February annual grant, which is consistent with settlement of previously earned multi-year awards.

**Management quality score: 6.0.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $46.50 (+19.8% price, ~+25.7% total return)

SHOP occupancy inflects from 90.0% core toward 92%+ as the roughly two-decade-low new-supply backdrop lets operators push REVPOR ahead of EXPOR; core SHOP NOI delivers the guided +14% and total SHOP margin recovers toward 28%+ as the 2026 vintage seasons. Prestige repays and the $179.875M is redeployed at 7.4%+ with releveraging, limiting the income gap to well under $0.14/share. Market-based rent resets add $5.12M in 2026 and continue escalating. 2027 Core FFO reaches $2.90-$3.00, the equity market re-rates a 77%-seniors-housing, 89%-owned-real-estate portfolio toward 16x, and the ~$300M+ of realized disposition gains validate that NAV sits well above book. $46.50 is 16.0x $2.90 and sits just below the high sell-side target of $47.

### Bear Case — $32.50 (−16.3% price, ~−10.4% total return)

SHOP occupancy continues running below plan (currently ~90bp light) while the memory-care concentration (32% of core units) exposes LTC to labor-cost pressure; total SHOP margin drifts toward 22-23% and the guided $71.2M-$79.9M SHOP NOI comes in at the low end. Prestige repays and redeployment lags, so 2027 Core FFO falls to $2.65-$2.75. Cap-rate compression continues (recent deals at 6.8%-6.9%) while the cost of equity stays near 7.3%, extending the negative-spread funding. Seniors housing EBITDAR coverage slips below 1.10x and a triple-net operator requires restructuring. Core FAD coverage of the $2.28 dividend approaches 1.05x, removing the yield-support argument. The multiple compresses to 11.5x on $2.82 — $32.50, below the 52-week low of $33.64.

### Base Case — $41.00 (+5.6% price, ~+11.5% total return)

2026 lands within guidance (Core FFO $2.77, Core FAD $2.84). Prestige repays in Q4 2026 and is redeployed at approximately observed cap rates; SHOP NOI growth and full-year contribution from H2 acquisitions roughly offset the income gap and continued issuance, producing 2027 Core FFO of ~$2.85. The multiple holds at ~14.4x, giving $41.04 — between the sell-side median of $40 and mean of $41.57. Total return is dominated by the 5.87% dividend.

**Probability-weighted:** 55% base (+11.5%), 20% bull (+25.7%), 25% bear (−10.4%) → approximately +9% expected 12-month total return, of which roughly two-thirds is the dividend. That is a fair, not compelling, risk-adjusted return, and it is a lower expected return than at our June review because the price is 6.7% higher on unchanged per-share earnings power.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income hold). Horizon: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 print (early November 2026).**

## Conclusion

LTC is doing something strategically reasonable: rotating out of skilled nursing at large premiums to a depreciated book, exiting a triple-net seniors housing structure where 1.16x rent coverage gave it little protection, and building exposure to senior housing operating income against the tightest new-supply backdrop in roughly two decades. The balance sheet supporting that rotation is genuinely conservative: 29.7% debt to gross asset value, 4.2x debt/EBITDAre, 4.9x fixed-charge coverage, no maturity wall before July 2029, and $543.9M of revolver availability.

What the strategy is not yet doing is compounding per-share cash flow. Core FFO/share was flat in Q2 on 13.4% more shares; guided 2026 Core FAD of $2.84 is ~1% below 2025's $2.87; the recycling surrenders a 7.26% yield to buy at 7.10%; equity is being issued at a 7.35% implied Core FFO yield; and a dated Q4 2026 Prestige repayment removes roughly $0.14/share of income if redeployed at observed cap rates. At 14.0x Core FFO with a 5.87% dividend covered 1.22x on Core FAD, the shares are priced about right for that profile, sitting at the low end of the sell-side range with a consensus that is 7 holds to 1 strong buy, and with an active ATM program that mechanically caps near-term upside.

Own LTC for the covered 5.87% distribution and the balance-sheet resilience; the expected 12-month total return is close to the yield. We would pay up only on two consecutive quarters of SHOP occupancy and margin expansion, or on 2027 guidance that shows the transition trough is over. We would add on weakness toward $34-$35, where the CIO bought in June and where the yield alone does the work. **HOLD.**
