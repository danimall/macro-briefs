## Executive Summary

The Mosaic Company is experiencing a structural margin collapse in its phosphate segment driven by a 141% year-over-year surge in sulfur costs tied to Strait of Hormuz shipping disruptions. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS came in at $0.05 versus $0.24 consensus (a 79% miss), the third significant miss in four quarters. Management has withdrawn phosphate production guidance and curtailed operations across three facilities. The stock has declined 35.7% over the past 12 months to $22.51, near its 52-week low of $20.89.

At current levels, Mosaic trades at approximately 0.66x tangible book value and carries a 3.9% dividend yield, suggesting meaningful asset-value support. The $1.9B Ma'aden equity investment alone represents approximately $6/share. However, the combination of negative free cash flow (-$535M in FY2025), a thin cash position ($282M), heavy reliance on short-term borrowing facilities, new antitrust class action lawsuits, and the absence of insider buying despite five-year low prices collectively argue against establishing a position here.

The key binary variable is Hormuz resolution. If shipping normalizes and sulfur prices collapse, margins could snap back rapidly. But current consensus estimates continue falling — current-year EPS revised down 65% in 90 days to $0.82 — and the company's balance sheet provides limited margin for error while waiting.

## Company Overview

Mosaic operates through three segments: **Phosphate** (North American mines and processing), **Potash** (Canadian and formerly U.S. mines, international sales via Canpotex), and **Mosaic Fertilizantes** (Brazilian integrated mining, processing, and distribution). The company is one of the world's largest concentrated phosphate producers and among the top potash producers globally.

Revenue peaked at $19.1B in FY2022 during the Russia-Ukraine fertilizer supply shock, fell to $11.1B in FY2024, and partially recovered to $12.1B in FY2025. Q1 2026 revenue of $3.0B showed 14% YoY growth, but this top-line improvement was overwhelmed by input cost inflation.

## Financial Analysis

Phosphate segment gross margin fell from $167M in Q1 2025 to $3.4M in Q1 2026 — functionally zero. Sulfur costs surged from $157 to $379/long ton (+141%), and ammonia from $417 to $626/tonne (+50%). At these input economics, phosphate production destroys value, hence the curtailments.

**Quarterly earnings trajectory:**
- Q2 2025: $0.51 actual vs. $0.73 estimate (-30.5% miss)
- Q3 2025: $1.04 actual vs. $1.00 estimate (+4.5% beat)
- Q4 2025: $0.22 actual vs. $0.47 estimate (-53.3% miss)
- Q1 2026: $0.05 actual vs. $0.24 estimate (-79.1% miss)

**Balance sheet stress indicators:**
- Cash: $282M
- Short-term debt: $1.2B (including $789M commercial paper, $401M inventory financing)
- Long-term debt: $4.3B
- Debt/equity: 0.46x (moderate but deteriorating)
- Current ratio: 1.25x (adequate but tightening)
- FY2025 FCF: -$535M (operating cash flow $825M minus capex $1.36B)

**Inventory at $3.4B** signals potential further writedowns if fertilizer prices decline. The capex cut to $1.25B from $1.5B helps but doesn't resolve the negative FCF trajectory given current commodity dynamics.

**Asset retirement obligations of $2.5B** represent a long-tail liability for gypstack closure in Florida/Louisiana that has no revenue-generating potential.

Long-term potash demand remains supported by global population growth and agricultural intensification, but near-term Mosaic cannot profitably produce its largest segment's products.

**Asset value floor:**

The Arax/Patrocnio writedowns ($442M) demonstrate management willingness to impair when economics deteriorate.

## Competitive Landscape

Mosaic benefits from structural advantages that persist regardless of the current margin squeeze:
- **CVD protection:** Import duties on Moroccan and Russian phosphate provide U.S. pricing power
- **Integrated operations:** Mining through processing in North America
- **MicroEssentials:** Premium-priced performance products with higher margins than commodity MAP/DAP
- **Canpotex membership:** Efficient international potash distribution
- **Brazilian distribution network:** Extensive reach into the world's largest agricultural market

Offsetting factors:
- **CVD political risk:** The American Soybean Association is actively lobbying for duty removal, framing Mosaic's production cuts as evidence that domestic supply is unreliable
- **No input cost control:** Unlike integrated chemical producers (CF Industries with natural gas-based nitrogen), Mosaic must purchase sulfur and ammonia at market prices
- **Brazilian retreat:** The Arax/Patrocnio divestiture and idling signal competitive challenges in Brazil's phosphate mining

Competitive position is stable but not improving. CVD removal represents a potential structural negative if the political environment turns against Mosaic.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Sulfur cost normalization timing (CRITICAL):** The entire near-term investment case depends on sulfur prices reverting. Current $379/long ton is uneconomic for phosphate production. Resolution requires Hormuz reopening or alternative supply development — neither is within Mosaic's control.

**2. Liquidity deterioration:** With negative FCF and $1.2B short-term maturities, credit facility drawdowns will increase, potentially approaching covenant limits. The $2.5B commercial paper program provides capacity, but this is not a comfortable position.

**3. Dividend sustainability:** The $0.88/year dividend costs approximately $280M annually. With negative FCF, this is funded by borrowing. If conditions persist through H2 2026, a dividend cut becomes probable (40-50% probability within 12 months).

**4. Antitrust litigation:** March 2026 class actions seeking treble damages across the fertilizer industry. Resolution timeline is 3-5 years, but if discovery produces damaging evidence, settlement costs could be material.

**5. CVD removal:** If the Trump administration sides with the farming lobby and removes phosphate import duties, Mosaic faces direct price competition from OCP (Morocco) and PhosAgro (Russia), compressing domestic margins even after sulfur normalizes.

## Conclusion

Mosaic presents a classic value trap: cheap on assets (~0.66x tangible book), cheap on theoretical normalized earnings (11.5x FY2027), but burning cash, missing estimates by wide margins, facing new litigation, and entirely dependent on an external geopolitical event for margin recovery. The absence of insider buying at five-year lows is telling — management sees no reason to commit personal capital despite apparent "cheapness."

The score trajectory confirms deteriorating fundamentals: Financial Health declined from 5.5 to 3.8, Management Quality from 5.5 to 4.0. Valuation has improved mechanically as the stock fell, but the business has deteriorated faster than the price suggests.

For investors with geopolitical conviction on Hormuz resolution and patience for binary outcomes, Mosaic offers asymmetric upside potential. For portfolio construction purposes, the risk profile — negative FCF, litigation, political CVD risk, management uncertainty — warrants avoidance until the operating business demonstrates it can generate cash again.
