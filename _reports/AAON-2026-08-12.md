## Executive Summary

AAON has, over eighteen months, converted itself from a premium commercial rooftop-unit manufacturer into a company where 38% of FY2025 revenue and 73% of order backlog sits in custom data-center and cleanroom cooling under the BASX brand. The mechanics are now visible in reported results: Q2 2026 revenue of $627.0M rose 101.2% year over year, H1 revenue of $1,123.9M rose 77.4%, operating margin expanded 340bp to 11.0% on 570bp of SG&A leverage, and operating cash flow swung to +$55.0M in H1 2026 from -$31.0M a year earlier. Backlog reached $1,970.8M at 6/30/26 (BASX-branded $1,430.4M, AAON-branded $540.5M), roughly 1.02x trailing-twelve-month revenue of $1,932M. This is a genuine participant in the data-center capex cycle.

Three things keep it from being a buy at this price. First, the arithmetic of FY2026 guidance. Management's 55-60% revenue growth target on the FY2025 base of $1,442.1M implies $2,235-2,307M, which against H1 actual of $1,123.9M leaves H2 at $1,111-1,183M, or $556-592M per quarter versus Q2's $627M. The guided 25-26% full-year gross margin against H1's ~24.5% simultaneously requires H2 gross margin of roughly 26-27.5%. Lower revenue and materially higher margin in the same half is a specific, testable claim, and the Q3 print is the test. Second, warranty cost is escalating precisely where the ramp is: Q2 2026 warranty expense of about $11.0M against $3.6M a year earlier (roughly 1.75% of sales versus 1.39% for FY2024), with the accrual building from $29.97M to about $36.6M, after AAON Coil Products' warranty expense rose 265% in FY2025 while its BASX-branded output went from $26.9M to $229.3M. Third, EPS quality: Q2's 9.8% effective tax rate included $11.6M of excess share-based-compensation tax benefits worth roughly $0.10 of the reported $0.68 diluted EPS. That benefit is pro-cyclical and shrinks mechanically as the share price falls.

At $87.27 the stock trades at ~37x the $2.35 FY2026 consensus, ~24.7x the $3.53 FY2027 consensus, ~3.35x FY2026E EV/sales and ~20.6x FY2026E EV/EBITDA, against a Building Products peer median P/E of 25.4x. The 42% decline from the June 3 high of $150.46 has removed the extreme premium without creating a margin of safety: FY2025 free cash flow was -$190M, cash on hand at year-end was $13 thousand, and $435M of a $600M revolver was drawn at 6/30/26. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; valuation improved, growth visibility narrowed, and risk deteriorated. We hold with a defined upgrade level in the low $70s and a defined test at the Q3 report.

## Company Overview

AAON engineers and manufactures premium commercial HVAC and thermal-management equipment from Tulsa, Oklahoma, through three reportable segments: AAON Oklahoma (configurable rooftop units, controls, aftermarket parts), AAON Coil Products in Longview, Texas (semi-custom systems, coils, heat pumps), and BASX in Redmond, Oregon (custom high-performance data-center cooling, cleanroom ventilation, modular air handlers), acquired in December 2021.

The brand view is more informative than the segment view:

| Brand revenue | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY25 mix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AAON-branded | $1,001.99M | $975.64M | $894.28M | 62.0% |
| BASX-branded | $166.53M | $224.99M | $547.79M | 38.0% |
| Total | $1,168.52M | $1,200.64M | $1,442.08M | |

BASX-branded revenue grew 143.5% in FY2025 while AAON-branded revenue declined 8.3%, its second consecutive annual decline. The structurally important detail is where the growth is built: BASX-branded output from the Longview, Texas plant went from $8.2M (2023) to $26.9M (2024) to $229.3M (2025). Longview, not Redmond, is the marginal data-center capacity engine, and Longview is also where warranty expense is escalating fastest.

Two economically different businesses now sit in one P&L. Legacy AAON-branded equipment is sold through independent manufacturer representatives, with AAON setting a minimum price and the representative negotiating the end price; representative fees and third-party content are booked as liabilities rather than revenue ($30.5M "due to representatives" at 12/31/25). R&D of $58.2M was 4.0% of sales, high for building products and consistent with a spec-driven engineering position. BASX product is highly customized with no alternative use and is therefore recognized over time on percentage-of-completion, which is why the balance sheet now resembles an engineering-and-construction contractor's.

## Financial Analysis

### FY2025: growth purchased at a steep margin cost

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $1,168.52M | $1,200.64M | $1,442.08M |
| Gross margin | 34.15% | 33.08% | 26.75% |
| SG&A % of sales | 14.68% | 15.66% | 16.61% |
| Operating margin | 19.47% | 17.42% | 10.14% |
| Net income | $177.62M | $168.56M | $107.59M |
| Diluted EPS | $2.13 | $2.02 | $1.29 |

Revenue grew 20.1% while gross profit fell 2.9% and net income fell 36.2%. Segment data localizes the damage entirely to legacy: AAON Oklahoma gross margin fell 836bp to 28.97% on declining sales, while AAON Coil Products (+225bp to 21.42%) and BASX (+190bp to 26.61%) both expanded. Management attributed the Oklahoma deterioration to lower volumes and overhead absorption, an ERP transition, the low-GWP refrigerant platform change, coil supply constraints, higher warranty costs, and Memphis start-up expense. Most of that list is transitional. The item that is not obviously transitional is three consecutive years of AAON-branded revenue decline, which is equally consistent with share loss during the lead-time and ERP disruption. AAON-branded backlog of $540.5M at 6/30/26 versus roughly $530M at 12/31/25 indicates stabilization rather than recovery.

| Cash | $0.014M | $0.013M |
| Accounts receivable, net | $147.43M | $314.39M |
| Contract assets, net | $135.42M | $247.04M |
| Inventories, net | $187.42M | $261.15M |
| PP&E, net | $510.36M | $631.26M |

Days sales outstanding rose from 44.8 to 79.6; receivables plus contract assets rose from 86 to 142 days of revenue, partly offset by payables stretching from 20 to 38 days. Total debt of $414.6M against $894.99M of equity is 0.46x debt/equity, an important correction to screens that report ~1.88x, which is the assets-to-equity multiplier rather than a debt ratio. Covenant leverage was 1.77x at 12/31/25 and 1.46x at 6/30/26 against a 3.00x maximum. Solvency is not the issue; funding capacity is. The revolver went from $200M (Dec 2024) to $230M (April 2025, explicitly for working capital) to $500M (May 2025) to $600M (Dec 2025), with $435M drawn and roughly $163.7M available at 6/30/26 against a zero-cash treasury.

### The cash flow statement is the anchor

FY2025 operating cash flow was $0.534M against net income of $107.59M. The reconciliation confirms this is working capital, not earnings quality: $237.0M of net income plus non-cash addbacks less $236.4M of net working-capital use. Capex of $190.56M plus $14.33M of intangibles produced free cash flow of approximately -$190M (-$204M including intangibles). In that year AAON distributed $72.3M ($39.7M buybacks, $32.6M dividends), raised the dividend 25%, and funded the gap with $243.2M of net revolver borrowing. Shareholder distributions in FY2025 were debt-financed.

Cash taxes paid were $12.93M in 2025 versus $49.92M in 2024 and $63.38M in 2023, the result of the July 2025 tax act (100% bonus depreciation, permanent §174A domestic R&E expensing with an election to deduct the 2022-2024 unamortized balance), generating $57.0M federal and $22.5M state NOLs that management expects to reverse in 2026. At 2024 cash-tax levels, FY2025 operating cash flow would have been roughly -$36M.

### Q2 2026: the inflection is real, with two asterisks

| | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $311.6M | $627.0M (+101.2%) | $633.6M | $1,123.9M (+77.4%) |
| Gross margin | 26.6% | 24.3% | — | ~24.5% |
| SG&A % of sales | 19.0% | 13.3% | — | ~13.5% |
| Operating margin | 7.6% | 11.0% | — | ~11.2% |
| Diluted EPS | — | $0.68 | ~$0.54 | $1.15 |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | -$31.0M | +$55.0M |

Gross margin fell about 230bp year over year while SG&A leverage improved about 570bp, netting 340bp of operating margin expansion. That is the signature of a fixed-cost absorption inflection where cost was built in 2025 ahead of revenue that has now arrived. Two asterisks. First, roughly $0.10 of the $0.68 EPS came from the tax line: a 9.8% effective rate including $11.6M of excess share-based-compensation benefits. Normalized at 23%, Q2 EPS is approximately $0.578 and H1 approximately $1.055. This treatment is mandatory under ASC 718, not aggressive, but it is share-price dependent, and with the stock 42% below its June high the benefit will compress in H2. Second, the H1 cash inflection was achieved while customer advances (contract liabilities) drained from $80.67M to roughly $12.8M, a roughly $68M cash use. That makes the H1 cash performance more credible and also means the prepayment cushion that helped fund the 2025 build has largely been consumed; H2 2026 and 2027 working capital must come from collections or the revolver.

The earnings-surprise record — +12.8% (Sep-25), -14.8% (Dec-25), +62.3% (Mar-26), +33.2% (Jun-26) — shows genuine positive momentum but also high estimate dispersion around a business whose revenue recognition is now partly percentage-of-completion. The reported "beats" are measured against adjusted estimates while the $0.68 is GAAP diluted; we do not treat the magnitude as established.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three verifiable items and one guided item.

Verifiable: backlog of $1,970.8M at 6/30/26, up from $1,830M at 12/31/25 (itself +110.9% year over year), with BASX-branded at $1,430.4M; H1 revenue +77.4%; and rising out-year consensus — FY2027 EPS estimates up 7.2% over 90 days to $3.53, FY2026 up 4.2% to $2.35. Headcount grew 52.9% to 5,897 (Feb 2026 from Feb 2025), which is why absorption is the dominant swing factor: revenue per employee fell from about $311K to about $245K, and recovers mechanically as the ramp lands.

Guided and unverified against a filing: FY2026 revenue growth of 55-60% with a 25-26% gross margin. The revenue math implies H2 of $1,111-1,183M against H2 2025 of $808.5M — still +37% to +46% growth, but a sharp deceleration from H1's +77% and a 6-11% sequential decline from the Q2 run rate. Consensus is internally consistent with this: Q3 $0.56 and Q4 $0.64 sum to H2 EPS of $1.20 against H1's $1.15 on roughly flat revenue. With a normalized tax rate replacing H1's 16.0% effective rate, delivering $1.20 requires roughly 120bp of pretax margin expansion at flat revenue, which in turn requires the guided gross-margin step-up. The near-term estimate pattern confirms the market has absorbed this: current-quarter consensus was cut 15.2% (from $0.66 to $0.56) over the last 30 days even as full-year and next-year numbers rose.

The composite growth signal is strong but no longer accelerating in the way the H1 prints implied. Backlog conversion supports 2027 revenue in the $2.7-2.9B range at 20-25% growth. The binding constraint is margin, not demand.

The demand backdrop is corroborated externally. [unverified] Hyperscaler capex confirmations through the July-August reporting season, and industrial confirmation in billed revenue at the large equipment makers, support the durability of data-center thermal demand into 2027. Working the other way, copper and electrical input costs are rising, and AAON carries fixed purchase commitments — $10.5M (2026) and $11.2M (2027) minimums on a five-year refrigerant contract, plus $27.4M of 2026 raw-material commitments.

Peers: AOS 17.8x, SSD 21.7x, AWI 25.4x, FBIN 40.9x, BLDR 80.4x — the dispersion reflects different points in the residential/non-residential cycle rather than a clean comparable set.

The trailing 45.9x is not the right lens: FY2025 was a trough distorted by the Oklahoma absorption collapse. Forward is the correct basis. On FY2026 consensus, 37.1x is a 46% premium to the peer median for a company guiding 55-60% revenue growth — defensible but not cheap. On FY2027 consensus, 24.7x is at the peer median against implied earnings growth of ~50%, which produces a PEG below 0.5 and looks compelling in isolation. The FY2027 number is levered to two unresolved variables: whether the guided H2 2026 gross-margin step-up is real, and what a normalized tax rate does to reported EPS once excess share-based-comp benefits compress (those benefits were $16.4M in FY2024, $11.5M in FY2025, and $11.6M in Q2 2026 alone).

Our own FY2027 build — revenue $2.78B, gross margin 26.5%, SG&A 13.0%, interest $22M, 23% tax, 84.5M diluted shares — yields EPS of roughly $3.21, about 9% below consensus. Applying 28-30x (a premium to slower-growing peers, a discount to AAON's own 2021-2024 range) gives $90-97. Cross-checked on EV/EBITDA: FY2027 EBITDA of $450-485M at 16-18x implies $82-101 per share. Both routes centre near $95.

Consensus price targets of $135 low and $154 high (mean $143) sit 55-76% above spot. Those targets were almost certainly set when the stock traded between $130 and $150 in May-June, and the recommendation mix has since degraded (one Buy moved to Hold in the last month, now 1 Strong Buy / 3 Buy / 1 Hold). At 37x current-year earnings we do not treat analyst targets as independent floors; at that multiple, targets and price tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation.

The 42% decline from $150.46 requires an explanation. Our read is that the market repriced three specific items disclosed or clarified since early June: guidance implying a sequential revenue plateau in H2, warranty expense tripling year over year in Q2, and the consumption of the contract-liability cushion that had been funding working capital. The stock fell about 10% across the two sessions following the record Q2 report, which is the cleanest evidence that the sellers are reacting to guidance composition rather than to the headline. That is a rational repricing, not an obvious mispricing, and it is why the valuation score improves to 5.5 rather than to a level that would support ACCUMULATE.

## Competitive Landscape

Legacy AAON competes against Carrier, Trane, Daikin, Lennox, Johnson Controls and Rheem/Nortek with a fraction of their scale, differentiating on configurability, lead times, an AMCA-accredited test lab, controls integration and 4%-of-sales R&D. It sells through an independent representative channel it does not own — a structural fragility illustrated by the Havtech matter, where a terminated representative sought $48.6M and settled for $7.5M in 2023. Two consecutive years of AAON-branded revenue decline against a soft non-residential construction market leave open whether the volume shortfall is cyclical or partly permanent share loss.

BASX competes in custom data-center cooling, where the trajectory is clearly improving: backlog up to $1.43B, segment gross margin up 190bp in FY2025, and capacity extended by shifting BASX-branded production into Longview. The competitive question here is different: [unverified] the market is attractive enough to attract capacity from Vertiv, Munters, Johnson Controls and Modine, and the pricing achieved on first-of-kind units at newly commissioned plants is where cost-to-complete estimates and warranty accruals get tested. The implied direct-cost margin on in-process contracts rose about 300bp (to 45.1% earnings on costs incurred) while consolidated gross margin fell 633bp. AAON's accounting policy excludes indirect labor, small tools, field rework and back charges from contract costs, so the two are not comparable, but the divergence in direction is worth monitoring alongside warranty.

Trajectory overall: improving in the segment that now drives growth, stabilizing-at-best in the segment that still contributes 62% of revenue. That mix supports a mid-range competitive score rather than a high one.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is worsening while mix diversifies.** Three customers were each ≥10% of FY2025 revenue, up from two in FY2024, and three customers were ≥10% of accounts receivable at both year-ends. Revenue mix diversified (BASX-branded from 18.7% to 38.0% of sales) while counterparty dependence increased; these measure different things and the second moved the wrong way. AAON does not name the ≥10% customers. The 10-K's reference to Microsoft, AWS, Google Cloud, QTS and Applied Digital appears in a passage describing companies operating in the data-center market, not as a customer list; we treat those names as illustrative of the end market and unconfirmed as AAON counterparties. That distinction is material to the credit quality of a $314M receivable book.

**Receivable reserve coverage thinned as the book doubled.** Gross AR rose from $148.5M to $315.7M while the allowance rose only from $1.038M to $1.308M — coverage fell from 0.699% to 0.414% — and the provision fell to $283K from $720K. Write-offs remain immaterial ($13K in 2025), so there is no realized deterioration. The reserve is defensible if the incremental counterparties are investment-grade; it is thin if a developer-class counterparty sits among the ≥10% names.

**Warranty escalation, concentrated at the ramp.** FY2025 warranty expense rose 42% to $23.83M (1.65% of sales from 1.39%), with AAON Coil Products up 265% to $7.05M (2.17% of ACP sales). Q2 2026 warranty expense of about $11.0M versus $3.6M, with the accrual at roughly $36.6M, is attributed in the 10-Q to an increase in historical claims. Either early-life quality issues on newly transferred product lines, or a genuine actuarial true-up plus richer service content on data-center awards, would produce this result. This is the single most concrete leading indicator of whether the guided H2 gross margin is achievable.

**Percentage-of-completion estimate risk.** Grant Thornton designated BASX-branded revenue recognition as the sole Critical Audit Matter. Contract assets net were $247.0M, or 17.1% of FY2025 revenue. A CFO transition during 2026 (Andy Cheung signing as CFO, prior finance leader Rebecca Thompson now Chief Accounting Officer, with Item 5.02 8-Ks dated 2026-04-02 and 2026-07-30) is a defensible response to a changed business model, and it removes continuity in exactly the estimates — cost-to-complete, warranty, receivable reserves — that now carry the most weight.

**Funding capacity.** Zero cash, $435M drawn on a $600M revolver maturing May 2030, roughly $163.7M available, and a consumed prepayment cushion. Covenant leverage of 1.46x against a 3.00x maximum provides headroom, but continued growth at current working-capital intensity consumes availability. A recomputed Altman Z of ~7.7 on market equity (2.24 on the private-firm variant) confirms this is a liquidity-management question, not a solvency one.

**Governance and alignment.** No open-market executive purchases were observed in the period reviewed. Former CEO Gary Fields, now a director, executed a large series of option exercises and associated sales in May 2026. Founder Norman Asbjornson made two large gifts while retaining roughly 11.6 million shares (gifts carry no valuation signal). FY2025 shareholder distributions of $72.3M were made in a year of -$190M free cash flow, funded by borrowing, alongside a 25% dividend increase. The 2022-vintage PSUs vested at 196.4% of target on performance measured at 12/31/2024, immediately before a -52% calendar-2025 total shareholder return against a peer group that returned +7.6%; we note the timing as a design artifact of a three-year cliff without inferring intent. Offsetting: absolute CEO pay of $3.66M is modest for a $7.1B company, NEO compensation actually paid was roughly 87% below granted value (real downside participation), and PSUs use relative TSR against S&P SmallCap 600 capital goods constituents. The number of distinct related-party channels — representative entities affiliated with a director's family, supplies purchased from entities controlled by two directors and an executive, a director consulting agreement, and flight time on an aircraft partly owned by the CEO — is above what we consider typical, though individually immaterial against $1.44B of revenue ($7.77M of affiliate sales).

**Structural offsets worth naming.** AAON matches 175% of employee 401(k) and HSA contributions and distributes roughly 8.5% of pre-tax profit quarterly to eligible non-exempt employees. Roughly $62M of benefit spend is largely fixed, which is part of why gross margin is so volume-sensitive; the profit-sharing plan is a genuine stabilizer that fell $7.1M as profits fell in 2025 and will expand as profits recover, muting some 2026 operating leverage.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 73.6% against 61.6% one-year realized is rich (12pp spread, 86th percentile within the chain), with a flat term structure (near 73.6% versus far 71.7%) and an implied move of ±9.4% on the 8-day expiry. Positioning is call-tilted: put/call skew of -11.3% at the front, put/call open interest of 0.40, and open interest shifting from 0.73 to 0.40 in a single session while ATM IV rose 9.2pp. Short interest of 5.8% of float with 3.6 days to cover is uncrowded. Options participants are paying up for upside optionality after the print rather than buying protection, and the market is pricing large moves in both directions without a directional bearish tilt. That does not corroborate a bear case, but the premium level makes long optionality an expensive way to express the Q3 catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($130, +49%)

Q3 validates the guided margin step-up: gross margin prints 26%+ as Longview and Memphis absorption improves, warranty normalizes back toward 1.2-1.3% of sales as first-of-kind issues clear, and H2 revenue exceeds the guidance-implied $556-592M quarterly run rate. Backlog conversion carries FY2027 revenue to ~$3.0B at a 27%+ gross margin, producing EPS near $3.80. The legacy Oklahoma segment recovers absorption as the ERP and refrigerant transitions anniversary, adding a second margin lever that has nothing to do with data centers. On $3.80 at 34x — a multiple AAON has sustained before — the stock reaches $129. Working capital normalizes as DSO retraces from 79.6 days, free cash flow turns positive, and the revolver draw stabilizes, removing the funding overhang.

### Bear Case ($62, -29%)

The H2 guide is an accurate read of a production plateau, and the margin step-up does not arrive: warranty stays near 1.75% of sales, cost-to-complete estimates on first-of-kind Longview units true up adversely, and gross margin holds near 24%. FY2026 EPS lands near $2.05 rather than $2.35, with the shortfall amplified because excess share-based-comp tax benefits compress with the lower share price. FY2027 consensus resets toward $2.50, the multiple compresses to 24x on a business now viewed as a project contractor rather than a compounder, and the stock retests the $62 52-week low. Secondary paths to the same outcome: one of the three ≥10% customers defers or cancels, requiring a receivable provision against a 0.414% reserve; or working-capital intensity persists and revolver availability falls below $100M, forcing a capital-raise conversation.

### What we think happens

Base case $95 (+8.9%). We build FY2027 EPS of roughly $3.21 — revenue $2.78B, 26.5% gross margin, SG&A 13.0%, $22M interest, 23% tax, 84.5M shares — and apply 29x, cross-checked against 17x EV/EBITDA on $465M. Demand is confirmed by backlog and by external capex evidence; the uncertainty is margin conversion and cash generation, and the current multiple pays for a favorable resolution of both. The probability-weighted return across our three cases (roughly 45/22/33) is about +5%, a fair rather than asymmetric return.

## Conclusion

AAON is a legitimate beneficiary of data-center thermal demand with a $1.97B backlog, doubled revenue, restored operating leverage, and positive operating cash flow in H1 2026. The FY2025 margin collapse was concentrated in the legacy rooftop segment and was largely self-inflicted and transitional, which supports the case that normalized earnings power is well above trailing results.

What prevents a stronger rating is the specific shape of the next two quarters. Management's own guidance implies revenue roughly flat-to-down sequentially while gross margin expands 150-300bp, at a time when warranty expense has tripled year over year at the plant driving the ramp, the customer-prepayment cushion has been spent, cash on hand is negligible, and $435M of a $600M revolver is drawn.
