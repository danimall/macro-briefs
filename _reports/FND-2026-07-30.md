## Executive Summary

Floor & Decor is a 276-store warehouse-format hard-surface flooring retailer executing a long-term expansion toward "at least 500" U.S. stores while its comparable-store base contracts. The two forces collided in Q1 FY2026: new-store (non-comparable) sales added $32.3M, comparable sales subtracted $40.8M, and total net sales fell 0.7% year over year — the first total-revenue decline of this cycle. Comparable transactions fell 5.5%, extending a traffic decline that has now run at least eight quarters. Operating margin has moved from 7.3% (FY2023) to 5.8% (FY2024 and FY2025) to 4.5% in Q1 FY2026 while the store count went from 251 to 276. The mechanism is explicit in the FY2025 MD&A: non-comparable-store SG&A rose $126.8M while comparable-store SG&A fell $24.8M. Management is cutting cost in the mature base; the new-store cost load is simply larger.

Two things keep this from being a straightforward avoid. First, gross margin has expanded every year through the downturn — 40.5% (FY2022) to 44.0% (Q1 FY2026) — which is unusual for a discretionary retailer absorbing tariffs against falling traffic, and is real evidence that the 240-vendor direct-sourcing model works. Second, the balance sheet is not the problem: $293.6M cash, an undrawn $800M ABL, $1.0B total liquidity, no financial maintenance covenants on the term loan, and stable BB/Ba3 ratings. Reported FY2025 free cash flow of $64M is misleading — trade payables swung $237.3M between FY2024 and FY2025, and excluding that single line operating cash flow was roughly $481M and $497M in the two years respectively. Underlying cash generation is near $500M, not $382M.

What is missing is a reason to pay a growth-retailer multiple for it. Consensus stands at $1.90 for FY2026 and $2.16 for FY2027, cut 7.5% and 10.0% respectively over 90 days. The FY2026 figure sits *below* the low end of management's own $1.98–2.18 guide, and stripping the 53rd week (~$0.08) the comparable-basis guide of $1.90–2.10 has a floor beneath FY2025 actual EPS of $1.92. Management is guiding to roughly flat Adjusted EBITDA margin, not recovery. The stock trades essentially on top of the consensus mean target ($54.77) and near the median ($55.50), with 13 holds, 3 sells (1 sell + 2 strong sell) and 7 buys. Q2 results land within days against the strongest comparison of the recent cycle (Q2 FY2025: EPS $0.58, revenue +7.1%, comps +0.4%), and the options market is pricing a ±13.6% move at 70.2% implied volatility. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Floor & Decor operates warehouse-format stores averaging roughly 75,000–76,000 square feet, carrying ~4,200 SKUs and ~$2.7M of inventory at cost per store — roughly 1.0M square feet of flooring product per location. The structural differentiator is job-lot in-stock depth: big-box home improvement centers allocate limited floor space to hard surface flooring, and independent specialty retailers lack the scale to hold job-lot quantities. Product is sourced direct from more than 240 vendor relationships (largest ≈10% of FY2025 net sales), bypassing importers, which supports an everyday-low-price model rather than a promotional cadence.

Footprint at FYE2025 was 270 warehouse stores plus five design studios across 39 states, rising to 276 after six Q1 FY2026 openings; 20 openings are planned for FY2026. Five port-adjacent distribution centers (Savannah, Houston, Los Angeles, Baltimore, Seattle) plus an LA transload facility support the model, with a sixth DC near Baltimore anticipated. Headcount was 13,723 at FYE2025, non-union. Spartan Surfaces, the commercial subsidiary, is not a reportable segment; its Q1 FY2026 revenue fell ~9.0% to $49.8M and it swung to a $2.4M operating loss from a $2.3M profit.

Retail mix shifted from roughly 50/50 homeowner/Pro in FY2024 and FY2025 to approximately 45/55 in Q1 FY2026. Management frames rising Pro share as strategic. The more likely near-term explanation is that the DIY homeowner is retrenching faster than the Pro, which makes the mix shift partly a symptom of consumer weakness. It does not reduce concentration risk: supplier concentration is unchanged, and dependence on the single macro driver — existing home sales — is unchanged.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue decomposition.** FY2025 net sales of $4,684.1M grew 5.1%, composed of +$305.7M non-comparable and −$77.4M comparable. Comps were −1.8% (transactions −3.5%, ticket +1.8%). The quarterly path deteriorated through the year: Q4 FY2025 comps −4.8% with operating income down 12.3% to $51.9M, then Q1 FY2026 comps −3.7% on transactions −5.5%.

| Metric | Q1 FY2026 | Q1 FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $1,152.3M | $1,160.7M | (0.7)% |
| Comparable store sales | (3.7)% | (1.8)% | worse |
| Gross margin | 44.0% | 43.8% | +20 bps |
| SG&A % of sales | 39.5% | 38.3% | +120 bps |
| Operating income | $52.4M | $64.2M | (18.4)% |
| Diluted EPS | $0.37 | $0.45 | (17.8)% |
| Adjusted EBITDA margin | 10.5% | 11.2% | (70) bps |

The quality of ticket growth deserves attention. FY2025 ticket gains were attributed to mix; Q1 FY2026 gross margin expansion was attributed to "strategic pricing initiatives." Raising price to offset tariffs defends the margin *rate* while plausibly contributing to the 5.5% transaction decline. The 20bp gross margin gain and the traffic loss may be two sides of the same decision.

**Cash flow.** Reported FCF (OCF less capex) was $156.3M in FY2024 and $64.1M in FY2025. The collapse is almost entirely a payables artifact: trade accounts payable contributed +$122.3M to FY2024 operating cash flow and −$115.0M in FY2025, a $237.3M swing against a $221.3M OCF decline. Excluding that line, OCF was approximately $481M and $497M. I cannot determine why payables moved — benign purchase cadence, tighter supplier terms amid tariff disruption, and deliberate paydown are all consistent with the data. Disclosure argues against a supply-chain-finance wind-down, since amounts owed to those intermediaries within trade AP *grew* from $133.2M at FYE2025 to $143.9M at 3/26/26. The honest read is that this line is volatile and single-year FCF comparisons for FND carry little information.

Q1 FY2026 OCF of $109.2M and FCF of $45.8M should not be annualized. Q1 carried a +$46.5M payables inflow, a much smaller inventory build than prior year (−$15.9M vs −$56.7M), and management noted capex was depressed by second-use sites and store-size optimization. Full-year capex guidance of $250–300M is roughly 4x the Q1 run rate. As sensitivities only: FY2026 OCF at FY2025's reported level (~$382M) less midpoint capex ($275M) implies ~$107M FCF; on the normalized ~$490M basis it implies ~$215M. Against a $400M buyback authorization, that framing matters — full utilization would consume between roughly 2 and 4 years of free cash flow, so any repurchase pace is likely to be measured.

**Balance sheet.** Funded debt is trivial: a $197.7M term loan at 5.67% variable, plus zero drawn on the $800M ABL ($713.6M net availability). Against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $538.2M that is 0.37x. Capitalizing $1,810.5M of operating lease liabilities produces adjusted debt of ~$2,008M, or ~3.7x Adjusted EBITDA (3.2x net of cash) — a materially levered structure in substance. Undiscounted minimum lease payments total $2,626.1M at a ~12-year weighted-average remaining term. This is why the Altman Z-Score of 2.19 sits in the grey zone despite genuine solvency comfort; the metric penalizes lease-heavy retailers and is depressed here primarily by low EBIT/assets, which is a returns problem rather than a solvency problem.

A less visible item: "buildings and equipment acquired under operating leases" was $439.7M in FY2025 versus $186.9M in FY2024. Total capital deployed into the store base in FY2025 was therefore roughly $758M ($318M cash capex plus $440M lease-financed), not the $318M in investing activities. Any incremental-return analysis using capex alone overstates returns substantially. That line collapsed to $53.5M in Q1 FY2026 from $303.5M in Q1 FY2025, consistent with the second-use-site strategy genuinely reducing per-unit capital intensity — but lease commencements are lumpy and driven by landlord delivery timing, and this line moved 135% in the opposite direction the prior year. One quarter is not a trend.

The $197.7M term loan matures February 14, 2027 and was reclassified entirely to current liabilities at 3/26/26. A June 25, 2026 8-K carrying Items 1.01, 1.02 and 2.03 — the signature pattern of a refinancing — was not retrievable. I treat the refinancing as unverified. Practically, with $1.0B liquidity and an undrawn ABL, this is a manageable obligation rather than a cliff, but it should not be assumed termed out.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative and consensus is moving the wrong way. Current-year EPS estimates fell 7.5% over 90 days to $1.90; next-year fell 10.0% to $2.16. Both were roughly flat over the trailing 30 days, so the cuts have paused rather than reversed. Critically, $1.90 sits below the $1.98 floor of management's own guide — the sell side is not underwriting the guidance.

Management's FY2026 framework is itself unambitious. Net sales $4,880–5,030M and Adjusted EBITDA $560–590M include roughly $65M and $11M respectively from the 53rd week. Stripping that, comparable-basis sales growth is +2.8% to +6.0%, EPS is $1.90–2.10 (−1.0% to +9.4% versus FY2025's $1.92), and implied Adjusted EBITDA margin of 11.4–11.7% brackets FY2025's 11.5%. Management is guiding to flat margins with comps of −2.0% to +1.0%. The 20-store opening plan and $250–300M capex are unchanged.

The most consequential growth disclosure is management's own admission in the FY2025 MD&A that "our more recent new store classes are experiencing lower first year sales and initial returns compared to new stores opened in years prior to 2022," reinforced by a risk factor noting recent stores have higher construction, occupancy and operating costs and "may have lower profitability." The 500-store target is restated unchanged in Item 1. The evidence does not yet distinguish between value-destructive overexpansion and well-timed counter-cyclical investment in second-use real estate at depressed rents. The Q1 collapse in lease-financed capital deployment supports the charitable reading; four years of operating-margin compression supports the skeptical one.

Category detail adds a question the filings do not answer. Laminate and vinyl — historically the engine of the carpet-to-hard-surface conversion story, and FND's most commoditized and price-transparent category — fell 10.4% to $261.1M and dropped from 25% to 23% of mix. Adjacent categories fell 14.4%. Tile rose 5.0% and installation materials rose 7.0%. The strength in installation materials, an attach-rate product, is difficult to reconcile with collapsing project volumes. No management explanation appears in the 10-Q. This warrants a direct question on the Q2 call.

The structural drivers — aging housing stock, home equity, carpet-to-hard-surface conversion — remain intact and slow-moving. The binding constraint is existing home sales, which the FY2025 10-K and Q1 FY2026 10-Q both cite as the direct cause of the comp decline. With roughly half the store base under five years old and heavy fixed operating leverage, FND is a high-amplitude call option on housing turnover. That option is currently out of the money.

## Valuation Assessment

On FY2025 diluted EPS of $1.92, the stock at $54.77 trades at ~28.5x trailing (the supplied 30.3x figure implies either a higher reference price or a different EPS base; the difference does not change the conclusion). Forward multiples are ~28.8x on FY2026 consensus of $1.90 and ~25.4x on FY2027 consensus of $2.16. Peer medians in the supplied comparable set sit at 15.9x, though that set (CROX, CHDN, URBN, GOLF, CZR) is a market-cap screen rather than a business-model match and should be treated as loose context only. The more relevant comparison is that FND carries a growth-retailer multiple while delivering negative comparable sales and falling estimates.

The cycle-adjusted case deserves a fair hearing, because trailing EPS is plainly depressed. Suppose the cycle turns and FND reaches roughly 300 stores generating ~$5.5B of revenue at the FY2023 operating margin of 7.0–7.3%. That yields $385–401M of EBIT, ~$5M of net interest, a 21.75% tax rate, and roughly $297–310M of net income. On ~104M shares (assuming partial buyback execution), normalized EPS is $2.86–2.98. At 22x that is $63–66; at 25x, $71–74 — which is where the analyst high target of $75 sits. So a full multi-year cycle recovery, achieved, delivers roughly 15–35% upside from here.

The downside arithmetic is comparable in magnitude and requires less to go right. If comps run −4% to −6% and operating margin drifts toward 4.0%, FY2027 EPS lands near $1.60–1.75; at 20x that is $32–35, near the analyst low of $37. The distribution is roughly symmetric with a slight tilt against, and the stock sits within roughly $0.75 of the consensus mean and median.

The standard argument that consensus targets lag reality on inflecting names does not apply here, because there is no inflection: estimates are falling, not rising, and the guide implies flat margins. Nor does the "cheap on trough earnings" argument carry much force at 25x forward. A high multiple on trough earnings is only attractive when the trough is identifiable and the recovery path is funded; here the recovery depends on mortgage rates the company does not control, and the intervening years require $250–300M of annual capex plus lease-financed capital into store vintages management has told us earn less.

The $400M buyback is the one genuine, company-controlled valuation support. At $54.77 full utilization retires ~7.3M shares, taking share count from ~108.1M to ~100.8M — roughly 6.8% of shares outstanding, and 6.8% of market cap. Zero shares had been repurchased as of 3/26/26, and the ~109M diluted share count in FY2026 guidance predates the authorization. Executed at these prices, that is accretive and would put a bid under the stock. It is an authorization, not a commitment.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** The multiple is stretched relative to deteriorating near-term fundamentals with no identified catalyst for mispricing, partially offset by real cycle-recovery optionality and the unstarted buyback.

## Competitive Landscape

The strongest competitive evidence is gross margin: 40.5% (FY2022) → 42.1% → 43.3% → 43.6% → 44.0% (Q1 FY2026), achieved through eight quarters of falling traffic and a materially worse tariff environment. Very few discretionary retailers manage that. Direct sourcing across 240-plus vendors, no importer layer, and job-lot in-stock depth appear to be doing what management claims.

The most uncomfortable datapoint runs the other way. In the same Q1 period, Home Depot grew revenue 4.8%, Lowe's 10.3% and Williams-Sonoma 4.4%, while FND declined 0.7% and RH declined 1.7% (externally reported peer figures; HD and LOW growth includes acquisition contribution not disaggregated in the source, and comparable sales are not disclosed there). Read carefully, this establishes that FND's *total* revenue underperformed the largest competitors, not that its comparable sales did. It nonetheless undercuts any claim that the weakness is purely sector-wide. Combined with the 10.4% laminate-and-vinyl decline in FND's most price-transparent, most import-dependent category, the share trajectory looks flat at best.

Management's FY2025 claim of market share expansion is not quantified in any filing and remains unverified. Growing revenue 5.1% in FY2025 against a weak transaction market is at least directionally consistent with share gain through square footage rather than through the existing base.

New competitive risk language in the 10-K is worth noting: growing omnichannel competition, an explicit statement that FND could be disadvantaged if competitors are more effective at integrating artificial intelligence, and an acknowledgment that low barriers to entry allow FND's own suppliers to enter the market directly — disintermediation running in the opposite direction from FND's sourcing advantage.

**Competitive position score: 5.0**, down modestly. Margin evidence is genuinely strong; volume and category-mix evidence is weak.

## Risk Assessment

**Housing turnover is the master variable and is outside management's control.** Comparable transactions have declined for at least eight consecutive quarters (−4.7% FY2024, −3.5% FY2025, −5.5% Q1 FY2026). Ticket growth is masking the traffic problem, and in Q1 that ticket growth came from price increases, a lower-quality source because it can suppress units.

**Lease-embedded operating leverage.** Fixed operating lease cost rose 12.7% to $67.0M in Q1 FY2026 (total lease cost $91.0M) while net sales fell 0.7%. With a ~12-year weighted-average remaining term and $2.63B of undiscounted commitments, occupancy is close to irreducible in a downturn. The risk factor is candid about using a significant portion of operating cash flow to satisfy fixed lease obligations.

**Tariffs, two-sided and entirely unquantified.** The Q1 10-Q discloses that the Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA-authorized tariffs in early 2026, that the ruling "did not specifically require these tariffs to be refunded, resulting in uncertainty regarding potential tariff refunds," and that the government is implementing replacement tariffs under other authorities. No refund asset is recognized. The company quantifies no gross tariff cost, no mitigation offset, and no refund estimate. Both tails are wide and neither can be modeled from the filings.

**Leadership transition, a new risk factor.** Bradley Paulsen became CEO in fiscal 2026, the first CEO change since 2012; Thomas Taylor moved to Executive Chairman. The risk factor itself flags the hazard of the overlap and the possibility that other key personnel reevaluate their positions. Six Item 5.02 8-K filings in roughly 14 months indicate a broad leadership refresh rather than a clean single handoff.

**ERP replacement running through 2027.** Cloud financial and merchandising modules went live in Q1 FY2026, with disclosed changes to internal control over financial reporting (controls "automated, modified, or implemented"). Management concluded disclosure controls remain effective with no material weakness. The concern is specific: FND's stated advantage depends on SKU-level in-stock forecasting, so replacing the merchandising system puts the competitive advantage itself at execution risk (stockouts, markdowns), alongside elevated reporting risk for several quarters.

**Term loan refinancing unverified.** $197.7M sits in current liabilities. The June 25, 2026 8-K pattern (1.01/1.02/2.03) is consistent with a refinancing, but the document was inaccessible; amount, tenor, pricing and covenants are unknown.

**Q2 FY2026 print, imminent and binary.** Results are due approximately July 30, 2026, lapping the strongest quarter of the recent cycle (Q2 FY2025 EPS $0.58, revenue $1.21B, comps +0.4%) from a −3.7% exit rate.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 70.2% sits 21.6 points above one-year historical volatility of 48.6%, at the 89th percentile within the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (70.2% at one month versus 51.9% at six months). The one-month expiry prices a ±13.6% move. Implied volatility rose 9.6 points over the prior week while the stock gained 3.3% — a pattern consistent with event risk being priced ahead of the print rather than a directional signal. Put/call open interest collapsed from 10.06 to 1.20 as a large put position rolled off; put/call volume of 3.56 (9.75 at the one-month tenor) shows heavy near-dated put activity, while the data provider's skew reading of −11.3% is characterized as call-heavy. The composite read is two-sided event positioning at a rich volatility price, not a clean directional tell. For a fundamental investor the practical implication is that options are an expensive way to express either view right now, and that entering a directional equity position 24–48 hours ahead of a print with a ±13.6% implied move is a coin-flip on timing rather than a fundamental decision.

Short interest of 14.7% of float at six days to cover is meaningful squeeze fuel on any upside surprise, and equally reflects that a substantial cohort of institutional investors shares the bear view.

**Risk factors score: 4.8.** Housing exposure and multiple compression are well priced after a substantial decline from the 52-week high, but the imminent print, the unverified refinancing, the unquantified tariff position and the ERP transition are specific, live items.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($65–75)

Housing turnover is the only thing that needs to change. FND has kept gross margin expanding through the worst of the cycle, has cut comparable-store SG&A in absolute dollars for two consecutive periods, and is adding stores at what may prove to be trough real-estate and construction cost — the collapse in lease-financed capital deployment to $53.5M in Q1 from $303.5M supports genuine reduction in per-unit capital intensity. With roughly half the base under five years old, comp maturation alone provides multi-year same-store growth once transactions stabilize. Normalized operating margin back to 7.0–7.3% on ~$5.5B of revenue at ~300 stores supports $2.85–3.00 of EPS, worth $63–74 at 22–25x. The $400M authorization (6.8% of shares) executed at these prices adds accretion the guidance does not yet reflect. The CEO and CFO bought stock in the open market four days after a disappointing print at prices below today's. Normalized operating cash flow near $490M against a $5.9B market cap is a ~8% cash yield before growth capex, and any easing of the long end of the curve would feed directly into mortgage rates and existing home sales.

### Bear Case ($34–40)

The growth algebra has already flipped: Q1 was the first quarter in which new-store contribution failed to cover comp erosion, and there is no reason it reverses while transactions fall 5.5%. Management has stated in the 10-K that post-2022 store vintages produce lower first-year sales and lower initial returns, yet FY2026 adds 20 more stores and $250–300M of capex, converting flexible capital into ~12-year fixed lease obligations at declining incremental returns. Operating margin has gone 7.3% → 5.8% → 5.8% → 4.5% as the store count rose from 251 to 276, and the FY2026 guide contemplates no margin recovery. Consensus already sits below the guidance floor. Home Depot and Lowe's grew while FND shrank in the same quarter, and laminate and vinyl — the category that carried the hard-surface conversion story — fell 10.4%. Price-led ticket growth is suppressing units. If FY2027 EPS lands near $1.65 and the multiple compresses toward 20x, the stock is worth $33–35, close to the $37 analyst low, roughly 35–40% below current levels. Lease-adjusted leverage of 3.7x and a grey-zone Z-Score leave less cushion than the pristine funded-debt optics suggest.

### Base Case ($54–58)

Comps run between −2% and +1% as guided, FY2026 EPS lands $1.90–2.05 including the 53rd week, FY2027 reaches $2.15–2.25 with modest buyback accretion, and the multiple holds near 25x. That is $54–56, essentially the current price and the consensus mean. The buyback provides support; the absence of comp inflection prevents re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be higher. The reason it repeats is that the two sides of the case have both strengthened in ways that offset: the balance sheet, buyback and insider signals have improved, while the operating trajectory (first total-revenue decline, comps −3.7%, laminate/vinyl −10.4%, Spartan to an operating loss, consensus below the guidance floor) has worsened. The overall score edges down to 5.0 from 5.2 on the operating evidence, not on the price, which is modestly higher than at the prior review.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate. Q2 FY2026 results are due approximately July 30, 2026. Given a ±13.6% implied move and the toughest comparison of the fiscal year, no directional position should be initiated in the 48 hours before the print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Comparable-store sales inflect to −1.0% or better with transactions improving sequentially** (Q2 or Q3 FY2026). Transactions, not ticket, are the variable that matters; price-led ticket growth does not qualify.
2. **Meaningful buyback execution** — $75M+ repurchased in a single quarter at prices below $60, confirming management is willing to convert the authorization into share-count reduction rather than holding it as optics.
3. **Estimate-revision inflection**: FY2027 consensus revised upward from $2.16, combined with a re-rating opportunity at or below roughly 20x forward (~$43–45), which would create the asymmetry currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Comps deteriorate below −5% or FY2026 guidance is cut**, particularly if the low end of EPS guidance moves below $1.85 — which would confirm the market's current sub-guide positioning and put the $34–40 bear case in play.
2. **Gross margin compresses below 43.0%**, which would remove the one genuinely differentiating element of the story and indicate the pricing/sourcing advantage is spent.
3. **Capital allocation fails to adjust**: FY2027 store openings held at 20-plus with capex above $250M while comps remain negative and returns keep falling, or a refinancing on materially worse terms with new maintenance covenants.
4. **Any ERP-related disruption** appearing as stockouts, markdowns, inventory write-downs, or an internal-control deficiency in a subsequent filing.

**Monitoring items with no current resolution:** the June 25, 2026 8-K contents (term loan status), the laminate/vinyl decline explanation, Spartan's operating loss, and any tariff quantification or refund recognition. Each should be raised on the Q2 call.

## Conclusion

Floor & Decor is a well-run specialty retailer with a genuine sourcing advantage, a defensible in-stock assortment model, an ample liquidity position, and management whose two most senior officers bought shares with their own money below today's price. It is also a high-fixed-cost operator whose comparable base has been shrinking for eight quarters, whose operating margin has fallen from 7.3% to 4.5% while it added 25 stores (251 at FYE2024 to 276 at Q1 FY2026), and which has told shareholders in plain language that its newer store vintages earn less than their predecessors — while continuing to fund 20 more of them.

At roughly 25x FY2027 consensus of $2.16, with those estimates cut 10% over 90 days and sitting below management's own guidance floor, the price does not compensate for that combination. A full cycle recovery is worth roughly $65–75; the guided path is worth roughly the current price; a continued transaction decline with modest multiple compression is worth $34–40. That distribution is symmetric at best, and it resolves on mortgage rates rather than on anything management does. A binary earnings print within days at 70% implied volatility is not the moment to take a side. HOLD, reassess on the Q2 print, and require comparable-transaction stabilization or a materially lower entry price before upgrading.
