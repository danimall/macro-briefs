## Executive Summary

Aramark is a $12B pure-play food and facilities services company holding the #2 position in North America. Following its 2023 spin-off of the Uniform segment (Vestis), the company has simplified into a focused operator serving education, healthcare, B&I, sports/corrections, and international markets across 16 countries. FY2025 revenue grew 6.4% to $18.5B, Covenant Adjusted EBITDA reached $1.47B (+9.7%), and free cash flow improved to $432M from $299M.

The stock has rallied 41% from its April 2025 lows to trade at $45.54, near its 52-week high of $45.60. At 38x trailing P/E and ~20x forward consensus, the stock prices meaningful earnings acceleration that the company has failed to deliver in recent quarters (1 beat, 2 misses, 1 inline over four quarters). Combined with $5.4B in debt (4.2x D/E), rising weighted-average interest rates, and P&L contract cost exposure in an inflationary environment, the risk/reward skews neutral at current levels despite the sound underlying business thesis.

## Company Overview

Aramark provides outsourced food services and facilities management to institutions across education, healthcare, B&I, sports/entertainment, and corrections. The business operates through two segments: FSS United States ($13.2B, 71% of revenue) and FSS International ($5.3B, 29%). Approximately two-thirds of revenue comes from profit-and-loss contracts where Aramark bears cost inflation risk, and one-third from management fee/cost-plus arrangements. No single client exceeds 2% of revenue, though the U.S. government collectively represents meaningful exposure. The company employs ~278,000 people and competes primarily against Compass Group (significantly larger globally) and Sodexo.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $18.5B grew 6.4% reported, though approximately 2 percentage points came from a 53rd fiscal week. Organic growth of ~4.4% was driven by B&I (+18.0%), Healthcare (+4.5%), and International (+9.8% reported, +11.2% constant currency). The Facilities & Other segment declined 7.1% as management exited lower-margin accounts.

**Profitability:** Operating income of $792M (4.3% margin) improved from $707M (4.1%) in FY2024. Cost of services improved 20bps to 91.6% of revenue, but personnel costs rose to 41.8% from 40.9%, driven by business expansion, severance (+$23.5M), and medical claims (+$19.8M). Interest expense of $342M consumed 37% of operating income — a structural headwind. Net income of $326M (1.8% margin) translated to EPS of approximately $1.22.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow improved to $921M from $727M. Capex of $489M yielded FCF of $432M (2.3% margin), a 44% improvement over FY2024's $299M. The company also spent $264M on acquisitions (up from $149M), indicating inorganic growth is accelerating.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $5.4B against equity of $3.1B produces a D/E ratio of 4.23x. Goodwill ($4.9B) and intangibles ($1.9B) represent 51% of total assets. Liquidity is adequate at ~$2.4B (cash + revolver + receivables facility). Covenant metrics are comfortable: Secured Debt Ratio 2.26x vs. 5.125x limit, Interest Coverage 4.12x vs. 2.0x minimum. Weighted-average interest rates will rise from 4.03% in FY2026 to 5.73% by FY2029 as lower-rate tranches mature and are refinanced at higher market rates.

**Capital Return:** $500M buyback authorized November 2024; ~$170M executed in FY2025. Dividends of $111M. Current dividend yield ~1.1%.

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth thesis rests on three pillars: (1) first-time outsourcing acceleration — 43% of competitor Sodexo's North American wins came from organizations outsourcing for the first time; (2) market consolidation favoring the top 4 players growing at ~10% vs. the overall market at 5.3%; and (3) the B&I sector return-to-office trend, where Aramark's U.S. B&I revenue grew 18% in FY2025.

Management has guided consensus toward FY2026E EPS of $2.23 and FY2027E of $2.62, implying 83% and 17% growth respectively. The FY2026E figure requires a significant step-up from the $1.22 trailing, and analysts appear to be incorporating expectations for margin expansion, lower tax rates (the FY2025 effective rate dropped to 24.1% from 28.2%), and share count reduction from the buyback program.

The challenge: Aramark has missed EPS estimates in 2 of 4 recent quarters, including a material 14.4% miss in Q1 FY2026 (September quarter). This pattern undermines confidence in the forward estimates that support the current valuation. The estimate revision trend is mildly positive (FY2027E up 1.0% over 90 days) but far from strong.

Management's argument that tariff-driven economic uncertainty accelerates outsourcing decisions has some logic — operational complexity and cost pressure do push organizations toward third-party providers — but remains unproven in revenue data thus far.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.54, ARMK trades at:
- 38x trailing EPS ($1.22)
- ~20.4x FY2026E EPS ($2.23)
- ~17.4x FY2027E EPS ($2.62)

Against distributor peers: POOL trades at 20.8x, LKQ at 13.2x, GPC at 236.6x (distorted). The more relevant peer comparison is the contract food services industry: Compass Group trades at roughly 23-25x forward earnings with superior margins (6-7% operating margin vs. Aramark's 4.3%), stronger balance sheet, and larger scale. Sodexo trades at approximately 16-18x forward.

Aramark's premium to Compass on a forward basis is unjustified given Compass's superior margin profile, lower leverage, and larger global footprint. The 38x trailing multiple reflects the market's confidence in the EPS step-up from $1.22 to $2.23; if that acceleration materializes, the stock is fairly valued at ~20x forward. If Aramark delivers another earnings miss, the trailing P/E compression will be severe.

Analyst consensus target is $47.31 (median $48.00), implying 4-5% upside from current levels. The range of $35-$51 brackets the current price near the top quartile.

## Competitive Landscape

Aramark holds a defensible #2 position in North America with particular strength in sports/entertainment (26 major professional sports teams), education (~1,345 institutions), and corrections. The moat derives from: (1) multi-year contracts with high switching costs (especially in education and sports where 5-15 year terms are standard and require upfront capital investment); (2) supply chain scale — the long-standing Sysco relationship provides purchasing efficiencies; (3) institutional relationships and reference accounts that create barriers to entry.

The primary competitive risk is Compass Group's scale advantage and M&A-driven consolidation strategy (three European acquisitions totaling >€1.3B in 2024 alone). Compass can outbid Aramark on large multinational contracts and invest more in digital capabilities. Aramark's international operations (29% of revenue) are subscale relative to Compass, creating a structural disadvantage outside North America.

The offsetting positive: 43% of industry growth comes from first-time outsourcing penetration, meaning the market is expanding rather than purely zero-sum.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage risk (HIGH):** Interest expense consumes 37% of operating income. Rising weighted-average rates from 4.0% to 5.7% by FY2029 create a ~$90-100M annualized headwind on debt refinancing at maturity. In the current rate environment (Fed funds at 3.64%, no cuts expected), this is a persistent drag on earnings growth.

**Cost inflation risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** Two-thirds of revenue on P&L contracts means Aramark absorbs food, labor, and energy cost increases directly. Personnel costs already rose 90bps YoY to 41.8% of revenue. With oil above $95, diesel at records, and food inflation accelerating (cattle +25% YoY, urea doubling), Aramark's cost base is under meaningful pressure.

**Earnings delivery risk (MODERATE):** 1 beat in 4 quarters. The 14.4% miss in Q1 FY2026 is particularly concerning given the magnitude of earnings acceleration embedded in consensus estimates.

**Sysco concentration risk (MODERATE):** 43% of U.S./Canadian purchases through a single distributor. Low probability but high impact if Sysco experiences disruption.

**Goodwill risk (LOW-MODERATE):** $4.9B goodwill (51% of assets with intangibles). Recent test showed fair value "substantially exceeds" carrying value, but this is growth-assumption dependent.

### Options Market Signal

The backwardated vol structure warrants caution at the 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-51):** FY2026 earnings accelerate as guided ($2.23 EPS), driven by B&I momentum, outsourcing pipeline conversion, margin expansion from supply chain optimization, and share count reduction from buybacks. If Aramark delivers 22-23x on $2.23 EPS, the stock reaches $49-51. The tariff-driven outsourcing acceleration thesis materializes in bookings data.

**Bear Case ($35-38):** Another earnings miss triggers multiple compression back toward 16-17x forward (in line with Sodexo). Rising interest costs and food/labor inflation compress margins. The P&L contract structure becomes a liability in an inflationary environment. Stock returns to the $35-38 range that prevailed for much of late 2025.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback to $38-40 range (providing 15%+ upside to $48 median target) combined with evidence of earnings delivery in next quarter
- Two consecutive EPS beats demonstrating the step-up to $2.23 run rate is achievable
- Meaningful debt reduction (net leverage below 3.5x) reducing interest expense headwind

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Another EPS miss of >10% in next reported quarter, suggesting FY2026E of $2.23 is unreachable
- Covenant Adjusted EBITDA stagnation or decline for 2+ consecutive quarters
- Debt increase above $6B or covenant metrics approaching warning levels (Secured Debt Ratio >4x)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely May 2026 for Q2 FY2026). The Q1 FY2026 miss needs to be followed by evidence of recovery.

## Conclusion

Aramark is a high-quality franchise in a structurally favorable industry (outsourcing penetration expanding, consolidation benefiting scale players, multi-year contracts providing revenue visibility). Operational execution is improving, as demonstrated by 9.7% EBITDA growth and 44% FCF improvement in FY2025. The growth thesis is sound on a 2-3 year timeframe.

However, at $45.54 — essentially the 52-week high — the stock offers 4-5% upside to consensus targets, trades at 38x trailing (requiring the market to extend significant trust in forward estimates), and carries rising interest costs on $5.4B in debt. The recent earnings miss pattern directly contradicts the confidence required to pay this multiple. Aramark's P&L contract structure faces meaningful margin pressure in the current inflationary environment.

The stock is a HOLD: wait for either (a) a pullback providing better entry or (b) demonstrated earnings delivery confirming the step-up to $2.23. At current levels, the risk/reward is approximately balanced and not compelling enough for capital allocation against alternatives offering wider margins of safety.
