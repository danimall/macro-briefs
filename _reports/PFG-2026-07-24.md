**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Principal Financial Group is a diversified global financial-services company built on three engines: Retirement & Income Solutions (RIS), Principal Asset Management (PAM), and Benefits & Protection (B&P). The investment case rests on a distinction the headline GAAP numbers obscure. Reported net income swung from $48.1M in Q1 2025 to $424.6M in Q1 2026, and full-year 2025 net income fell to $1.19B from $1.57B — both moves dominated by the Talcott/Sixth Street funds-withheld embedded derivative, a non-operating mark that has nothing to do with underlying performance. The number to underwrite is segment operating earnings, and those grew across all three units in both periods: RIS +6.5% in Q1'26, PAM +11.2%, B&P +42% on improved claims experience. Full-year 2025 operating earnings rose in every segment (RIS $1,186M, PAM $930M, B&P $523M).

The counterweight is PAM's structural drag. Asset-management net outflows ran -$10.6B in 2025, -$5.9B in 2024, and continued at -$2.4B in Q1'26. AUM growth to $737.6B has come entirely from markets and FX, not organic flows; the active-to-passive migration and fee compression that pressure the entire industry are visible directly in PFG's numbers. The thesis hinges on whether the sticky, SMB-focused workplace franchise (retirement, group benefits, business-owner life) and a growing pension-risk-transfer market can outrun the asset-management bleed. Both can be true at once, which is why this is a compounding-but-not-re-rating story.

At $107-108 the stock has risen ~33% over the trailing year and sits near its 52-week high of $114.90. It trades at roughly 11.5x forward operating EPS (consensus current-year $9.42, next-year $10.31) with a ~3% dividend yield raised to $0.80/quarter, plus a buyback that has retired ~10M shares over the year. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (+2.1% current-year over 90 days) but well short of a signal that the price is mispriced. The near-term shareholder return is disciplined capital return supported by ~$4.5B of operating cash flow; the ceiling on the multiple is the outflow narrative. We rate PFG HOLD as an income hold.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1879 and headquartered in Des Moines, Iowa, Principal employs roughly 19,700 people and manages ~$333B in total assets, of which ~$186B are separate accounts (client risk) and $110.9B are general-account invested assets. The company operates three reportable segments plus Corporate:

- **Retirement & Income Solutions (RIS)** — the earnings engine, spanning 401(k)/403(b) recordkeeping, trust/custody, pension risk transfer (single-premium group annuities), funding agreements, and individual variable annuities/RILAs. Q1'26 segment assets $231.6B, roughly 70% of the total.
- **Principal Asset Management (PAM)** — Investment Management (public/private markets, real estate, alternatives) plus International Pension (Chile Cuprum AFP, Brazil Brasilprev JV, Mexico Afore, Asia JVs). Total AUM $737.6B at Q1'26.
- **Benefits & Protection (B&P)** — Specialty Benefits (group dental, group life #1 in the US, LTD, supplemental health, individual disability) and Life Insurance (business-owner-focused UL/VUL/term).

Principal's differentiator is a deliberate focus on small-and-medium-sized businesses across retirement, benefits, and business-owner life insurance, an integrated workplace ecosystem where it cross-sells. Financial-strength ratings anchor distribution: Principal Life is rated A+ (AM Best), AA- (Fitch), A1 (Moody's), and A+ (S&P), all stable. A downgrade would trigger surrenders and lost distribution, so the ratings are a load-bearing element of the franchise.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Reported revenue moved from $17.5B (2022, inflated by divestitures) to $13.7B (2023), $16.1B (2024), and $15.6B (2025). The swings are largely non-operating: funds-withheld embedded-derivative movements and PRT premium timing. Net income to PFG followed a similar path: $4.8B (2022), $0.62B (2023), $1.57B (2024), $1.19B (2025), for an ROE near 10.6%. The operating layer beneath is steadier and directionally positive: 2025 segment pre-tax operating earnings grew in all three units (RIS $1,186M from $1,056M; PAM $930M from $861M; B&P $523M from $463M).

**Q1 2026.** Net income to PFG was $424.6M ($1.93 diluted EPS) versus $48.1M a year earlier, and the difference is almost entirely the funds-withheld embedded derivative swinging +$177.4M versus -$209.7M. Total segment operating earnings were $558.5M versus $485.1M, with RIS $302.1M (+6.5%), PAM $208.5M (+11.2%), and B&P $170.0M (+42%). The B&P strength came from improved dental, disability, and life claims experience — genuinely favorable but partly a benign-claims tailwind that mean-reverts and should not be extrapolated at a 42% rate.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** 2025 operating cash flow was $4.5B, an FCF margin near 28%. Q1'26 operating cash was seasonally light at $187M (versus $977M) on settlement timing. 2025 capital return totaled $1,585M: $684M dividends plus $901M buybacks, roughly 7% of the current market cap. A $1.5B buyback authorized in February 2025 has ~$1,235M remaining; the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.80 from $0.75; share count fell from ~226M to ~216M over the year. Capital structure is conservative for a life insurer at 25% debt/capital and 33% debt/equity, with the next debt maturity not until November 2026 and $1.8B of holdco liquidity.

**Balance-sheet nuance.** As an insurer, PFG carries ~$333B in assets dominated by separate accounts and general-account investments. Standard bankruptcy/leverage screens (Altman Z near 0.1) are meaningless here and should not drive risk scoring. The relevant balance-sheet watch item is the AFS unrealized loss position, which widened to $2,976M at Q1'26 from $2,448M at YE2025 as rates rose and spreads widened. Gross unrealized losses are $3.6B; 97% of the underwater book is investment grade and management does not intend to sell, so there is no forced realization, but it depresses book equity and reflects real duration exposure in a higher-for-longer environment.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is genuinely two-speed. On the positive side, RIS operating earnings compounded ~12% in 2025, benefiting from a growing pension-risk-transfer market as corporate plan sponsors continue de-risking, and from net investment income supported by a 4.8-4.9% portfolio yield in a higher-for-longer rate regime. B&P has strong US positions (group life #1) and favorable 2025-2026 claims trends, though the 42% Q1 jump overstates the durable run-rate. Consensus expects Q2'26 EPS of ~$2.33, up ~7.4% YoY, and full-year operating EPS estimates have crept higher: current-year $9.42 (+2.1% over 90 days) and next-year $10.31 (+1.9%). These are steady mid-single-digit-plus revisions.

The drag is PAM. Net outflows mean that AUM growth is entirely market appreciation and FX; organic flows are negative. This is consistent with the industry-wide active-to-passive migration and fee compression, and management is responding by leaning into private markets and alternatives while pruning subscale units (Hong Kong MPF, Chile Vida, Post Advisory, Origin Asset Management). Whether the private-markets pivot arrests the outflow trend is the central open growth question; there is no evidence yet that it has.

The composite growth profile is mid-single-digit operating-earnings growth per share, amplified modestly by buybacks. That is a respectable but unremarkable trajectory that does not, on its own, justify multiple expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $107-108, PFG trades at ~11.5x forward operating EPS, or ~15.4x on the trailing/GAAP EPS figure of $5.25 that is distorted by the derivative mark. The combined dividend-plus-buyback yield is ~7% of market cap. Analyst targets bracket the stock tightly: low $92, mean $106.42, median $108.50, high $125, placing the current price essentially at consensus median.

The valuation debate has shifted from "cheap" to "fully priced." A year ago the stock was in the high $70s to low $80s; the +33% move to near 52-week highs has closed most of the obvious discount. On forward operating earnings, PFG is not expensive relative to diversified life-insurance peers, and the yield support is real. But the case for a re-rating is weak: estimate revisions are positive but modest (well below the +30% threshold that signals a mispriced setup), and the PAM outflow narrative caps the multiple the market will pay. The compressed multiple reflects the structural asset-management headwind, not a market error waiting to be corrected. There is no specific catalyst to argue the market is systematically underpricing the shares here; the price sits within a fair band, offering symmetric risk/reward rather than asymmetric upside.

We assign a valuation score of 5.0 — fairly valued, with no identifiable mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Principal's competitive position is stable-to-mixed. The retirement and benefits businesses are structurally advantaged: PRT is a growing market, and the SMB workplace-benefits niche is sticky and less commoditized than large-plan recordkeeping. B&P's #1 US group-life position and favorable claims trends reinforce a durable, cross-sold franchise. Strong financial-strength ratings support distribution and reduce surrender risk.

The asset-management arm is where the position is deteriorating. Persistent net outflows track the broader active-management erosion, and the fee mix is under pressure from passive competition. Management's pivot toward private markets and alternatives is the correct strategic response, but it is unproven at scale and the outflows have not yet stabilized. Portfolio pruning (HK MPF, Chile Vida, Post, Origin) cleans up the story and exits low-return blocks, but it also shrinks the base. The net effect is a company with an improving-to-stable workplace franchise offset by a slowly eroding asset-management position, a wash that lands at a competitive-position score of 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

The most important idiosyncratic risk is PAM net outflows, which are already visible in the data and partly priced. Beyond that:

- **AFS unrealized losses / duration.** No forced realization (97% investment grade, no intent to sell), but the position depresses book equity and reflects genuine rate sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment.
- **CRE / office exposure.** A $13.9B commercial mortgage book with California at 21% and office at 16.6% of CML / 35% of equity real estate. Underwriting is conservative (weighted-average LTV 48%, DSCR 2.3x, 89% balloon), and problem-plus-potential loans are 1.27% ($229.5M carrying, $53M allowance). Contained but worth monitoring given ongoing office stress.
- **Talcott/Sixth Street funds-withheld reinsurance.** The $2.8B embedded-derivative asset drives large quarterly GAAP swings unrelated to operations and introduces reinsurer counterparty risk.
- **Iowa dividend restrictions.** Distributions from Principal Life are capped by Iowa law (2026 ordinary limit ~$1,234M); Q1'26's $232M was classified extraordinary and required Commissioner approval, a recurring structural constraint on the capital-return story.
- **International/EM political risk.** Chile pension reform, Mexico Afore/judicial reform, Brazil. A 10% adverse FX move would cut ~$281M (7%) from international equity.
- **Ratings-downgrade cascade** and equity-market guarantees (GMWB/RILA) round out the list.

The 10-Q states no material change to risk factors since the 10-K. None of these is a near-term binary catalyst; most are slow-moving and partly priced, which supports a middling risk score of 5.5 rather than a low one.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 33.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 22.3% — rich by 10.8 points, with the term structure in mild backwardation (33.1% near-term versus 30.6% at six months), signaling the market is pricing some imminent stress, likely the next earnings print. Put/call skew is elevated at 5.5% (though skew flattens and turns negative at longer tenors), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.25 with an OI ratio of 0.14 indicates net light hedging demand. The drop in reported ATM IV from 563.8% (a corrupted prior reading) to 33.1% is a data-normalization artifact, not a real vol collapse. Options do not confirm a strong directional view either way, but the rich near-term IV and elevated short-dated put skew make downside protection expensive right now, a marginal argument against chasing the stock near its highs.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$125, +16%).** Operating earnings compound in the mid-to-high single digits as RIS rides PRT growth and net investment income, B&P sustains favorable claims trends, and PAM's private-markets pivot finally stabilizes flows. Continued ~$1.6B annual capital return shrinks the share count ~4-5% per year, and the market awards a modest re-rating toward 13x forward operating EPS as the outflow overhang eases. Combined with the ~3% dividend, total return reaches the low teens.

**Bear case (~$92, -14%).** PAM outflows accelerate as fee compression bites, dragging PAM operating earnings; the benign-claims tailwind in B&P mean-reverts, giving back part of the Q1 jump; the AFS unrealized loss position widens further on higher rates, pressuring book value; and the Iowa dividend constraint or a CRE deterioration forces a more cautious capital-return posture. The multiple compresses back toward 10x and the stock retraces toward the analyst low.

**Base case (~$110, ~+3% plus ~3% yield).** Mid-single-digit operating-earnings growth, steady buyback support, and a stable multiple around 11.5x forward operating EPS keep the stock roughly range-bound with the dividend providing most of the total return. This is the most likely path given positive-but-modest revisions and an unresolved outflow story.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2'26 earnings report (consensus ~$2.33 EPS), with the Chile Vida sale expected to close in Q3'26 (~$280M pre-tax net realized capital loss, no material impact to PAM operating earnings).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- PAM net cash flow turns positive (or outflows narrow materially below the ~$2-3B quarterly run-rate) for two consecutive quarters, removing the primary ceiling on the multiple.
- Consensus operating EPS revisions accelerate above +5% over 90 days alongside a pullback to the mid-$90s, restoring an asymmetric entry.
- A durable step-up in RIS/PRT operating earnings or a private-markets flow inflection that de-risks the growth trajectory.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- PAM outflows accelerate meaningfully (e.g., beyond -$15B annualized) or fee rates compress in a way that reduces PAM operating earnings.
- A ratings downgrade at Principal Life, or a material deterioration in the CRE/office book (problem loans rising well above the current 1.27%).
- A dividend/buyback cut, or a binding Iowa dividend constraint that forces the company to curtail capital return.
- B&P claims experience reverses sharply, giving back most of the recent operating-earnings gains.

## Conclusion

Principal is a well-run, conservatively capitalized diversified insurer whose GAAP optics obscure a steadier operating reality: all three segments grew operating earnings in 2025 and again in Q1'26, and the company returns ~7% of its market cap annually through dividends and buybacks. The problem is not quality; it is the absence of an asymmetric setup. After a +33% run to near 52-week highs, the stock trades at fair value against a mid-single-digit operating-growth profile capped by a persistent asset-management outflow that the market correctly discounts. Estimate revisions are positive but too modest to signal a mispricing, and the score trajectory has declined (6.6 → 6.4 → 5.8) as the valuation cushion eroded on the rally, a change in risk/reward rather than in business quality.

We rate PFG **HOLD (income hold)**. The ~3% dividend plus buyback provides a reasonable total-return floor, and the operating engine is compounding, but there is no catalyst to justify a re-rating from here. Owners should hold for the yield and capital return; new capital should wait for PAM flow stabilization or a pullback toward the mid-$90s before treating this as a buy.
