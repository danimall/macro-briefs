**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

OGE Energy is a pure-play regulated electric utility holding company whose sole material operating subsidiary, Oklahoma Gas and Electric (OG&E), is the largest electric utility in Oklahoma, serving roughly 915,000 customers across Oklahoma and western Arkansas. The company's strategic advantage is straightforward: OG&E's residential rates sit approximately 19% below the regional average and 34% below the national average, which allows it to attract large industrial and data-center load without triggering the ratepayer backlash that has raised bills sharply on higher-cost coastal grids. That rate positioning has translated into a concrete catalyst: an April 2026 contract to power three Google data centers, ramping to roughly 1 GW by 2031, structured with 100% customer-funded interconnection and take-or-pay payments regardless of actual consumption.

The investment case is a rate-base compounding story de-risked by contract structure, targeting 5-7% consolidated EPS growth through 2030. The Google deal and a June 2026 large-load tariff filing (which credits residential customers $25-30M/year and imposes collateral requirements, billing minimums, and early-termination fees on large loads) convert speculative data-center demand into contracted, low-risk, ratepayer-neutral-to-positive load growth. Moody's revised its outlook on both OGE Energy and OG&E to stable from negative in April 2026, resolving a prior credit overhang.

The problem is price. At $49.95, OGE trades within pennies of its 52-week high of $50.32, at roughly 20.6x the FY26 guidance midpoint and above the 19.3x multi-utility peer median. Near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-negative (current-quarter EPS cut 1.9% over 90 days, current-year essentially unchanged), the earnings track record has softened (two misses and one in-line in the last four quarters), and the growth model is funded by ongoing equity dilution into a higher-for-longer rate environment that raises the cost of the negative-free-cash-flow financing structure. The load-growth narrative is real and durable, but it is largely reflected in the multiple. We rate OGE a HOLD (growth_hold): a quality compounder worth owning for the yield-plus-growth profile, but without an asymmetric entry point at current levels.

## Company Overview

OGE Energy operates one of the cleanest structures in the utility sector. Despite its "Multi-Utilities" classification, it is effectively a single-segment, vertically-integrated regulated electric utility; the "other operations" segment is just the holding company and consolidating eliminations, which run a small loss. OG&E was incorporated in 1902 and exited natural gas distribution in 1928, leaving it as a focused electric generation, transmission, and distribution business.

Rates are regulated across three jurisdictions: the Oklahoma Corporation Commission (OCC), the dominant driver; the Arkansas Public Service Commission (APSC); and FERC for transmission. The company operates within the Southwest Power Pool (SPP). Reliability is strong, at 99.96% grid uptime in 2025, and the low-rate positioning is the strategic linchpin, enabling load growth by attracting large industrial and data-center customers without the political friction faced by higher-cost utilities.

FY2025 net income was $470.7M, up from $441.5M in 2024, though the multi-year path has been volatile ($737M in 2021, $666M in 2022, $417M in 2023). Management guides to 2026 EPS of $2.38-2.48 (consolidated net income $494-514M), with OG&E itself projected to earn ~$533M / $2.57 per share against a ~$(30M) / $(0.14) holding-company drag.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is that of a capital-intensive regulated utility in a heavy investment cycle. Total assets stand at $14.5B, with net PP&E of $12.9B. Total debt comprises OG&E senior notes of roughly $4.85B, OGE Energy senior notes of $348M, a $60M term loan, and $103M of industrial authority bonds, against total stockholders' equity of $4.94B. Short-term debt rose to $492M at March 31, 2026 (from $292M at year-end 2025) for working capital, and the company issued $350M of 5.90% senior notes due 2056 on April 1, 2026 to repay short-term debt and fund capex.

The Altman Z-Score of ~1.19 nominally screens as "distress zone," but this is a well-understood artifact of the regulated-utility model: a high fixed-asset base, low asset turnover, and heavy but recoverable leverage produce a low Z-Score for financially sound utilities. The more relevant credit signal is the Moody's stable-outlook revision and full compliance with all debt covenants. The balance sheet is investment-grade and improving.

Q1 2026 illustrated the seasonal and weather volatility inherent in the business. Revenue was $752.6M (+0.7% YoY), but net income fell to $50.2M / $0.24 diluted from $62.7M / $0.31 a year earlier. The decline was almost entirely weather-driven: heating degree days fell 27% YoY (1,383 actual vs. 1,900 prior year and 1,889 normal), cutting roughly $26.7M of quantity-related revenue. Customer count grew, non-residential demand was favorable, and Oklahoma PISA interest and depreciation deferrals to regulatory assets provided offsets. Management reaffirmed full-year guidance, signaling that the miss was weather rather than fundamentals. Operating cash flow jumped to $175.5M (from $15.9M) on fuel-recovery timing and customer collections.

The defining financial reality is capital intensity. Capex was $266.8M in Q1 alone, and the company is in a multi-year build-out. This model runs on structurally negative free cash flow funded by debt and equity issuance. The November 2025 public offering of 9.2M shares, including forward equity sale agreements on 4.6M shares at an initial forward price of $41.71 (settleable through May 2027 for ~$192M), pre-funds the program. Share count rose from 201M (Q1 2025) to 206M (Q1 2026). The forward price sits below today's $49.95, so settlement is currently accretive to the company, but EPS growth of 5-7% must be delivered over an ongoing dilution headwind, and higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of the debt leg of the financing stack. The dividend was raised modestly to $0.425/quarter ($1.70 annualized), a ~3.4% yield, well-covered by earnings.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on rate-base expansion driven by generation build-out and data-center load. The 2026 Integrated Resource Plan identifies 1,922 MW of capacity needs by 2031/32, up from 1,647 MW in the 2025 IRP, a ~17% one-year increase, roughly 800 MW of which is driven by tighter SPP resource-adequacy rules. That capacity need translates directly into rate base, the core driver of the 5-7% EPS growth target, with management guiding to the top half of the range in 2027-2028.

The Google contract is unusually high-quality for a utility. The three data centers (in Muskogee and Stillwater) are owned and operated by Google, with OG&E as the electricity supplier rather than a developer, insulating it from Google's own capex and technology risk. Google pays 100% of grid connection costs and makes guaranteed contracted payments regardless of actual energy consumption. Google will also make solar capacity available via capacity purchase agreements to be filed with the OCC. This structure is precisely the kind of contracted, take-or-pay load that de-risks a rate-base story.

The broader macro tailwind is real. The research brief cites EIA projections of data-center electricity demand rising sharply over the next 25 years. OGE's differentiator is its combination of superior structural positioning (below-market rates, land availability) and a proactive tariff design that makes large loads subsidize rather than burden residential ratepayers. The June 2026 large-load tariff filing runs a 15-year term with a 5-year ramp, and T&D World's independent reviewer described its customer protections as among the most robust in the country. This is regulatory and political de-risking that supports load-growth approval.

The near-term counterweight is estimate momentum. Consensus current-quarter EPS was cut 1.9% over 90 days, current-year is flat ($2.43), and next-year is essentially unchanged ($2.60). The load-growth story is not yet showing up as positive revision velocity. Until estimates begin rising, the growth thesis is a multi-year rate-base compounding case rather than a near-term earnings inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

OGE trades at ~20.6x the FY26 guidance midpoint (~$2.43) and ~22.1x trailing EPS of $2.32, with a 3.4% dividend yield. Against the multi-utility peer set, this is a full-to-premium valuation. Peer P/E medians sit at 19.3x (PNW 19.3x, BKH 18.5x, AVA 16.7x, MDU 22.7x, NWE 25.5x), and the peer median dividend yield of 3.8% exceeds OGE's 3.4%. The stock sits within pennies of its 52-week high, having appreciated ~11% over the trailing year.

The case for a premium multiple is the superior structural load-growth runway. OGE's contracted Google load and ratepayer-protective tariff design justify trading above utilities without a comparable catalyst. Analyst targets span $44 low to $60 high, with a $50.5 median, placing the current price essentially at consensus median. The $60 high target implies ~20% upside if the data-center thesis compounds without regulatory friction.

The case against paying up is more compelling at this entry point. Near-term estimates are flat-to-falling, so the multiple is not being pulled up by rising forward earnings. The financing model requires ongoing equity dilution, which caps per-share growth relative to the rate-base narrative, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of the negative-FCF model. A "cheap" print on a low-multiple utility peer would not, on its own, be a buy signal; compressed utility multiples frequently reflect the structural dilution-and-financing drag rather than genuine mispricing, and cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way in this environment. OGE is not even cheap: it trades above peers and at its own high, with the primary catalyst already announced and partially priced. There is no asymmetric upside at $49.95. We score valuation below neutral: the multiple reflects the growth story, and the risk/reward is symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable near the 52-week high.

## Competitive Landscape

OGE's competitive position within the utility sector is improving, driven by structural tailwinds it is well-positioned to capture. Oklahoma is an attractive data-center destination on the strength of low power costs and land availability, and OG&E's below-regional and below-national rates are the direct draw. Unlike coastal grids where data-center demand has pushed consumer bills up sharply, OGE is proactively structuring tariffs so large loads spread fixed costs across a wider base and put downward pressure on residential rates. This is a genuine regulatory and political moat that neutralizes the ratepayer backlash slowing load-growth approvals elsewhere.

The countervailing dynamic is real. Oklahoma City passed a moratorium blocking new data centers through 2026, and state officials have publicly emphasized protecting residential ratepayers. The large-load tariff is designed precisely to defuse this friction, and the independent review of its protections suggests OG&E is managing the politics competently. The 765kV Seminole-to-Shreveport transmission line added to SPP's 2025 Integrated Transmission Plan, which OG&E will construct and own a significant portion of, adds a further rate-base growth vector. On balance, OGE is a market leader positioned for structural tailwinds it is partly protected from through smart tariff design; the trajectory is favorable, though the level of the moat (a single-state regulated monopoly) is inherently capped by the pace of regulatory approval.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory recovery risk** is the core utility risk and is currently elevated by several live matters. The OCC dismissed OG&E's construction-work-in-progress (CWIP) application for Horseshoe Lake Units 13 & 14, now on appeal to the Oklahoma Supreme Court, and the Arkansas Attorney General is opposing Strategic Investment Rider eligibility for Horseshoe Lake/Tinker generation. Neither threatens solvency, but delays or denials push cost recovery into future rate cases and pressure returns.

**Capex and financing risk** is structural. Funding the capacity build-out through debt and equity issuance dilutes shareholders and depends on continued capital-market access. In a higher-for-longer rate regime, the cost of the negative-FCF model rises, and the dilution headwind must be overcome to deliver the 5-7% EPS target.

**Environmental compliance** is a muted but binary tail risk. OG&E previously estimated $2.4-2.8B in potential compliance costs tied to the EPA "Good Neighbor" ozone FIP (including $100-300M in the first 12-18 months), a large number against a ~$10B market cap. The current EPA is proposing to repeal or reconsider many of these rules, which reduces near-term pressure but creates litigation and reversal risk under a future administration.

**Large-load counterparty concentration** is a newly emphasized risk factor. The forward-looking disclosures flag the creditworthiness of large new customers in cryptocurrency and data centers, plus generative-AI-related regulatory and technology risks, directly reflecting the Google exposure. The take-or-pay structure and collateral requirements substantially mitigate this, but concentration in a single large offtaker is a monitorable.

**Weather/seasonality** adds quarter-to-quarter volatility, as Q1 2026 demonstrated. **Wildfire liability** is a sector-wide concern; a $60M+ Oklahoma City apartment-fire lawsuit names OG&E and its contractor, though OG&E disputes it and believes insurance covers it beyond a non-material retention.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly benign. Near-term ATM IV of 18.4% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 16.6% (a 1.9pp spread, fair) and at only the 6th percentile within the current chain; the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst. IV has collapsed from 30.5% on June 3 to 18.4%, consistent with resolved uncertainty following the Google announcement and Moody's revision. There is a modestly elevated near-term put/call skew of 13.7%, indicating some hedging demand, but the term structure is flat (near 18.4% ≈ far 19.1%), suggesting uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than clustered around a near-term event. The falling IV and low percentile are consistent with a period of complacency after the primary catalysts have been announced, supporting the view that the growth story is now largely priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($58-60):** The Google contract ramps to 1 GW as planned, the OCC approves the large-load tariff within its ~6-month review, and additional data-center offtakers sign on given Oklahoma's structural cost advantage. Rate-base expansion drives EPS to the top half of the 5-7% range in 2027-2028, estimate revisions turn positive as load growth converts to earnings, and the multiple holds at a premium to peers on the strength of the highest-quality contracted growth in the group. At ~23x $2.60 next-year EPS, the stock reaches ~$60, matching the analyst high target.

**Bear Case ($42-44):** Regulatory friction intensifies (the Oklahoma Supreme Court upholds the CWIP dismissal, the Arkansas AG's rider opposition succeeds, or the large-load tariff is watered down), delaying cost recovery and pressuring returns. Higher-for-longer rates raise financing costs on the negative-FCF model, equity dilution continues to dilute per-share growth, and a weak weather year compounds the drag. The multiple compresses toward the peer median of 19.3x on $2.30-2.40 of achieved EPS, taking the stock to $44 (the analyst low), roughly 12% downside from current levels.

The base case sits in between: OGE delivers mid-single-digit EPS growth, the Google and tariff catalysts play out constructively but incrementally, and the stock compounds at roughly the EPS growth rate plus the 3.4% yield, with limited multiple re-rating from an already-full starting point.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** OGE is a quality regulated compounder with a genuine, contracted data-center load-growth catalyst, but the stock trades near its 52-week high at a premium to peers with no asymmetric upside at the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive estimate-revision velocity — current-year or next-year consensus EPS rising meaningfully (turning from the current flat-to-negative trend) as data-center load converts to earnings.
- OCC approval of the large-load tariff on constructive terms, de-risking the load-growth pipeline.
- A pullback toward the mid-$40s (roughly 18-19x forward, at or below the peer median), where the growth runway would be available at a non-premium multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse Oklahoma Supreme Court ruling on the CWIP appeal combined with an unfavorable large-load tariff outcome, materially delaying cost recovery.
- A guidance cut below the 5-7% EPS growth target, or evidence the equity dilution is outrunning rate-base earnings accretion.
- Revival of the $2.4-2.8B Good Neighbor FIP overhang under a regulatory reversal, or a material credit-rating action reversing the Moody's stable outlook.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (a seasonally stronger cooling-season quarter, which will test whether the Q1 weather miss was fully transitory) and the OCC's ~6-month review of the large-load tariff filed in June 2026, resolving around year-end 2026.

## Conclusion

OGE Energy is a well-run, cleanly-structured regulated electric utility positioned in the AI/data-center load-growth theme, with the added advantage of below-market rates and a proactive tariff design that makes large loads accretive rather than burdensome to residential ratepayers. The Google contract is nearly ideal for a utility, converting speculative demand into contracted, low-risk rate-base growth, and the Moody's stable-outlook revision removes a prior credit overhang.

The obstacle is entry price. At $49.95, OGE trades within pennies of its 52-week high, at ~20.6x forward EPS (a premium to the 19.3x peer median), with a below-peer 3.4% yield, flat-to-falling near-term estimates, a softened earnings track record, and a financing model dependent on ongoing equity dilution into a higher-for-longer rate environment. The load-growth narrative is durable and largely priced; there is no asymmetric upside here. We rate OGE a HOLD (growth_hold), constructive on the multi-year rate-base compounding case but recommending patience for a mid-$40s entry or a turn in estimate-revision momentum before adding.
