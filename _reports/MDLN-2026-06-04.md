**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Medline is the largest provider of medical-surgical products and supply-chain solutions in the United States, with roughly $28.4B of FY2025 revenue and a vertically integrated model that combines self-manufacturing, third-party distribution, and direct customer relationships through five-year "Prime Vendor" contracts that retain customers at a 98%+ rate. The company went public on Nasdaq in December 2025 at $29/share in the largest U.S. IPO of the year, ran to roughly $50 by late February 2026, and has since retraced to $34.47, about 32% below the 52-week high. The pullback coincides with a tariff-driven margin event and a steady supply of sponsor and insider stock hitting the market.

The central tension is that the top line is strong and the bottom line is not. Q1 2026 net sales grew 10.7%, almost entirely on volume, and U.S. Prime Vendor sales grew 15.3%, clear evidence of continued share gains. Adjusted EBITDA fell 10.6% to $776M, operating income fell 26%, and net income fell 25.8%, with roughly $120M of tariff cost in the quarter the primary driver. Management raised FY2026 organic sales guidance to 8.5%–9.5% while only maintaining Adjusted EBITDA at $3.5–3.6B. Medline is buying market share at the expense of current margin.

We rate MDLN a HOLD. This is a high-quality, share-gaining franchise facing a poor near-term profit setup driven by an exogenous and potentially reversible cost (tariffs, with a CBP refund process pending), not a structurally impaired business. Consensus EPS estimates are rising across current-year, next-quarter, and next-year horizons, the company has beaten both reported quarters, and 24 of 28 covering analysts rate it Buy or Strong Buy with a target low ($40) that sits 17% above the current price. That forward picture rules out a negative call. The stock trades at roughly 30x forward earnings on EBITDA that is currently declining, carries a multi-billion-dollar Tax Receivable Agreement that diverts cash to pre-IPO owners, faces a persistent secondary-offering and insider-selling overhang into mid-2026, and has first-year internal-control risk. That combination is genuinely two-sided and prevents a confident directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1966 by the Mills family, Medline offers approximately 335,000 products across the full continuum of care: hospitals, ambulatory surgery centers, physician offices, and post-acute and nursing facilities. It operates two reportable segments:

- **Medline Brand** (~47% of sales, the large majority of segment EBITDA): self-manufactured and self-sourced products. Medline manufactures roughly one-third of these in 30 facilities and sources the rest from 600+ partners across ~40 countries. Three categories: Front Line Care (wound care, exam gloves, incontinence), Surgical Solutions (procedure kits, drapes, PPE), and Lab & Diagnostics. This is the higher-margin segment, with Q1 2026 segment EBITDA margin of 22.1%.
- **Supply Chain Solutions** (~53% of sales, ~one-fifth of segment EBITDA): distribution of 145,000 third-party-branded products from 1,300+ suppliers, plus logistics. Low margin, with Q1 2026 segment EBITDA margin of 4.8%.

The franchise rests on the Prime Vendor model: five-year exclusive distribution agreements that accounted for $18.0B (63.4%) of FY2025 sales at a five-year retention rate above 98%. Medline is distinctive in being both manufacturer and distributor with direct customer relationships, in contrast to pure distributors (McKesson, Cardinal Health, Owens & Minor, Henry Schein) and pure manufacturers. Its physical network includes 70 distribution centers, 30 manufacturing plants, 2,100+ owned delivery trucks, and next-day delivery to 95% of U.S. customers. The core profit-growth engine is converting third-party Supply Chain volume into higher-margin Medline Brand product within existing Prime Vendor accounts.

The company emerged from a ~$34B 2021 leveraged buyout by Blackstone, Carlyle, and Hellman & Friedman, alongside the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority and the Mills family. It is structured as an Up-C holding company with dual-class shares; the sponsors, Mills family, and ADIA together control roughly 67% of voting power.

## Financial Analysis

**Top line.** Q1 2026 net sales were $7,352M, up 10.7% YoY, with the gain coming almost entirely from volume (pricing was immaterial). U.S. Prime Vendor sales rose 15.3%. This volume momentum prompted the raise in FY2026 organic sales guidance, above prior expectations.

**Profitability — the near-term issue.** Margin compression dominates the quarter:
- Gross margin fell to 25.0% from 27.5% a year earlier.
- Operating income fell 26% to $422M.
- Net income fell 25.8% to $239M.
- Adjusted EBITDA declined 10.6% to $776M; Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 10.6% from 13.1%.
- Medline Brand segment EBITDA margin compressed to 22.1% from 25.4%.

The dominant cause is tariffs, which cost roughly $120M of pre-tax income in the quarter. China represents about 5% of COGS, with additional exposure to Mexico and Southeast Asia. The guidance pattern is the clearest read on the dynamic: management raised sales guidance but only maintained Adjusted EBITDA, confirming that tariff costs are absorbing the incremental volume profit.

**Capital structure.** This is an LBO-legacy balance sheet. Total debt was $12,755M against $2,236M of cash at quarter-end (3/28/26). IPO proceeds repaid roughly $4.0B of term loans and Euro-denominated loans, which cut Q1 interest expense to $136M from $210M, a 35% reduction that materially improves the path to net income. There is a maturity wall of about $8.5B due in 2029. Goodwill ($8.1B) and intangibles ($13.7B) together total $21.8B, roughly 56% of $39B of assets, which creates impairment exposure if performance deteriorates.

Layered on top of the debt is a Tax Receivable Agreement liability of $4.0B (up from $3.5B at IPO and growing with each unit exchange in secondary offerings), which could rise toward ~$7.5B over time. Under the TRA, pre-IPO owners receive 90% of realized tax benefits, a recurring cash claim that diverts cash from public shareholders and is independent of operating performance.

Q1 operating cash flow was $412M, down from $682M a year earlier on a working-capital build. FY2026 capex is guided to roughly $500M.

## Growth Analysis

The volume story is real and broad-based. Double-digit sales growth driven by volume rather than price, 15.3% Prime Vendor growth, and a 98%+ retention rate indicate Medline is winning share even while its profitability is suppressed. The structural tailwinds support durability: an aging population, care shifting to lower-cost non-acute settings where Medline's portfolio penetration is higher-margin, and the ongoing Brand-conversion lever inside existing accounts.

The forward earnings signal is constructive, and we weight it explicitly because it directly bears on the recommendation. Consensus EPS estimates have moved up across the board over the past 90 days: current-year EPS to $1.51 from $1.47 (+2.9%), next-quarter to $0.39 from $0.37 (+3.7%), and next-year to $1.67 from $1.65 (+0.9%). The company has beaten in both reported quarters as a public company (+9.5% and +69.2%). This positive earnings momentum is inconsistent with a thesis that profit is "structurally capped." The qualifier is that this is not the +30% inflection velocity that signals a trough-to-recovery re-rating; it is modest upward drift in estimates against a backdrop where reported EBITDA is still declining YoY. The growth thesis is therefore a "share gains plus eventual margin normalization" story, not an earnings explosion.

The path to renewed profit growth runs through three levers: tariff normalization or refunds (a Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and a pending CBP refund process create real but uncertain optionality), continued Brand conversion lifting mix, and ongoing deleveraging reducing interest expense. If even one or two of these reassert, the current EBITDA decline can reverse and earnings growth can re-accelerate toward the revenue growth rate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.47, MDLN trades at roughly 30x trailing and forward earnings (EPS $1.43 trailing, $1.51 current-year consensus). That multiple is full for a business whose EBITDA is currently declining. The bull framing is that current profit is artificially depressed by exogenous tariff cost; on normalized EBITDA the forward multiple would look more reasonable, and the analyst target range ($40 low, $51.6 mean, $53 median, $62 high) implies 17%–80% upside that is built on tariff normalization and continued share gains.

We do not treat the analyst consensus as stale. The strong-buy count moved from 6 to 7 and the hold count from 5 to 4 in the most recent period, after the May 6 Q1 print and the tariff disclosure, and after the stock fell. Analysts maintained a constructive distribution into a falling tape, and zero of 28 rate the stock Sell. That is a meaningful counterweight to any negative thesis and a primary reason we are not recommending against the stock. With the stock at 30x forward on declining near-term profit, we do not treat those targets as floors in the way the methodology reserves for cheap, inflecting names; the targets embed an expectation of margin normalization that is real but not yet visible in the financials.

The honest conclusion is that the stock is near fair value with two-sided risk. There is no specific reason today to believe the market is materially mispricing it lower; equally, the multiple does not offer asymmetric upside until the tariff drag eases. That balance is what produces a HOLD rather than either a BUY (no margin-of-safety, full multiple on falling profit) or an AVOID (no structural impairment, rising estimates, broadly constructive Street).

## Competitive Landscape

Medline's competitive position is strong and improving. It is the largest U.S. med-surg supplier by net sales, and its vertical integration in owning both the brand and last-mile distribution lets it compete on total delivered cost and capture Brand-conversion margin in ways distributors and pure manufacturers cannot replicate. The Prime Vendor retention and growth indicate the moat is widening, not eroding, even during a margin-pressured quarter.

Sector headwinds are real but apply to the whole industry: healthcare-provider consolidation strengthens buyer negotiating power, GPOs compress pricing (roughly 69% of sales flow through Vizient, HealthTrust, and Premier), and tariffs hit the entire import-dependent supply chain. Medline argues that diversified global sourcing and owned manufacturing position it to mitigate tariffs better than peers, and its continued volume share gains support that claim. The competitive trajectory is the strongest single element of the investment case.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs (most acute, partly reversible).** The Q1 pre-tax cost is the dominant driver of the EBITDA decline. Mitigation through re-sourcing and selective price increases lags. Legal status is fluid after the February 2026 IEEPA ruling and subsequent re-imposition under other authority, with a CBP refund process that creates upside optionality but no certainty.

**Tax Receivable Agreement.** A cash obligation to pre-IPO owners that grew ~$467M in Q1 alone from the March secondary (from $3,542M at 12/31/25 to $4,009M at 3/28/26). It diverts 90% of realized tax benefits away from public shareholders and complicates change-of-control economics.

**Supply overhang.** Two large sponsor secondaries (March and May 2026) raised no capital for Medline, and coordinated executive 10b5-1 plans begin near the June 16, 2026 lockup expiry. This is a structural source of selling pressure and a plausible explanation for the $50→$34 retracement independent of fundamentals.

**Leverage and holding-company structure.** $12.8B of debt, covenant restrictions on distributions, dependence on subsidiary distributions to fund taxes and TRA payments, and the 2029 maturity wall. A June 2, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/2.03/8.01) indicates active liability management ahead of that wall, which is constructive if it terms out or reprices the 2028/2029 maturities.

**EtO litigation insurance tail.** Of ~380 ethylene-oxide plaintiffs, most settled (2023–2025) with five remaining; excess insurers have denied coverage on settlements already paid, and recovery is being litigated with no receivable booked, an uncapped, hard-to-quantify tail.

**First-year public-company control risk.** Medline has self-identified control deficiencies under remediation and has not yet had an auditor attestation; the first SOX 404(b) test lands in the FY2026 10-K. A material-weakness disclosure would be a genuine negative catalyst.

**Concentration and governance.** GPO concentration (~69% of sales), top-5 customers at 11.3%, sponsor voting control (~67%), dual-class structure, and an opt-out of DGCL §203.

Several of these — control risk, the EtO insurance tail, and the secondary/insider supply overhang — are not obviously fully priced and are the reason the Risk Factors score sits below 5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 51.8% is rich versus 42.6% one-year historical vol, the term structure is in backwardation (51.8% near-term vs 43.9% six-month), put/call skew is elevated at 7.3% with a put/call open-interest ratio of 1.91, and the nearest expiry implies a ±8.4% move. This is consistent with a defensive lean into the mid-June lockup expiry and the coordinated insider-selling start dates, plus general post-IPO uncertainty. It does not signal a fundamental collapse — short interest is uncrowded at 5.7% of float with 3.5 days to cover — but it confirms that the market expects a bumpy near-term window, which supports a wait-and-watch posture rather than initiating a position now.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Tariffs are an exogenous, potentially reversible cost; if CBP refunds materialize or sourcing/pricing mitigation catches up, the quarterly drag eases and EBITDA growth re-accelerates toward the 10%+ revenue growth rate. Deleveraging is already cutting interest expense 35% YoY, and active liability management addresses the 2029 wall. The Prime Vendor moat keeps compounding share gains at 98%+ retention, and Brand conversion lifts mix. On normalized EBITDA the 30x forward multiple looks reasonable, and the Street's $51.6 mean target (50% above spot) reflects that recovery scenario. Bull case ~$53–62.

**Bear case.** Tariffs persist or worsen with no refund, keeping EBITDA flat-to-down while revenue grows, share bought at the expense of profit indefinitely. The TRA siphons billions in cash to insiders, the secondary and insider-selling overhang caps the stock through 2026, a material-weakness disclosure lands in the FY2026 10-K, or the EtO insurance dispute crystallizes into a real liability. On a 30x multiple, any of these compresses the stock back toward the high-$20s. Bear case ~$27–30.

**Base case.** Volume share gains continue, tariffs ease only gradually, EBITDA growth stays muted through 2026 before reaccelerating, and the supply overhang clears after mid-2026. The stock trades roughly sideways to modestly higher as estimates drift up but the multiple does not expand against still-suppressed near-term profit. Base case ~$36–42 over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 FY2026), with the FY2026 10-K SOX 404(b) attestation and the mid-June lockup expiry / insider-selling start as interim checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Evidence the tariff drag is reversing — a CBP refund recognized, or sequential Adjusted EBITDA margin recovery toward 12%+ — such that EBITDA returns to YoY growth.
2. The stock de-rates into the $27–30 range on the secondary/insider overhang while fundamentals (volume growth, retention, rising estimates) remain intact, creating genuine margin-of-safety.
3. Confirmation the 2029 maturity wall has been termed out or repriced on favorable terms with no covenant pressure, and the FY2026 10-K shows clean internal-control attestation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A material-weakness disclosure in the FY2026 10-K, or an EtO insurance-recovery loss that converts the denied coverage into a booked liability.
2. Tariff costs escalate and FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance is cut below $3.5B, breaking the "share gains plus eventual normalization" thesis.
3. Revenue volume growth decelerates below mid-single digits, signaling the Prime Vendor share-gain engine is stalling rather than the margin issue being transient.

## Reconciliation of the Recommendation

We are calling HOLD rather than a negative rating, and we want to be explicit about why, because the near-term setup is poor. An AVOID requires either structural impairment or a demonstration that a bear case is not already priced and that the stock has negative momentum with limited recovery optionality. Medline does not fit. The franchise is gaining share, the sole driver of the EBITDA decline is an exogenous and potentially reversible tariff cost with a refund process pending, interest expense is falling, and the forward-looking evidence runs the other way: consensus EPS estimates are rising across all horizons, the company has beaten both reported quarters, and 24 of 28 analysts rate it Buy or Strong Buy with a target low 17% above the current price. Calling AVOID on a share-gaining franchise already 32% below its high, where the impairment is a transient margin event, would be the classic error of fading a depressed quality name into a rebound. The bear case here is well-telegraphed and partly priced, which is the definition of a HOLD rather than an actionable short.

We are equally clear about why this is not a BUY. The stock trades at ~30x forward earnings on EBITDA that is currently declining, the multiple offers no margin of safety, and the next twelve months carry a persistent supply overhang from sponsor secondaries and coordinated executive selling, a multi-billion-dollar TRA cash drain, the 2029 maturity wall, and unattested first-year controls. The rising estimates are modest drift, not the kind of inflection that would justify paying up. We classify this as an uncertain hold: there is a credible 12-month appreciation thesis, but it is genuinely offset by near-term headwinds and structural overhangs, and the evidence does not support a confident directional bet either way at $34.

## Conclusion

Medline is a dominant, share-gaining medical-surgical franchise with a wide and widening Prime Vendor moat, currently in a phase where revenue is growing double digits but profit is going backwards because of tariffs. The forward signals — rising estimates, two earnings beats, a constructive Street, falling interest expense, and a potentially reversible tariff driver — are good enough to keep this out of negative territory. The headwinds — a full 30x forward multiple on declining EBITDA, a $4B-and-growing TRA, a heavy secondary and insider-selling overhang into mid-2026, first-year control risk, and options pricing near-term stress — are real enough to keep it out of buy territory. We rate MDLN a HOLD and would become buyers on a de-rating toward $27–30 or on clear evidence the tariff drag is reversing and EBITDA growth has resumed.
