## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group is a Bermuda-domiciled specialty insurer and reinsurer generating 17%+ ROE at approximately 1.45x book value. The company reported Q1 2026 operating EPS of $2.50, an 81.7% combined ratio, and repurchased $783 million in stock — a quarterly buyback pace that reduced share count by 5.8% year-over-year. These are best-in-class capital returns from a business that has delivered strong book value per share compounding over many years.

The primary near-term headwind is management's acknowledgment that market conditions are "clearly more competitive," with Q1 2026 net premiums written declining 3.7% year-over-year ($167 million). This explains the modest negative estimate revision trend (-1.1% on current year, -2.2% on next year over 90 days). The market appears to be interpreting this as growth deterioration when it is actually disciplined cycle management — the same behavior that protected book value during the 2017-2019 soft market and positioned Arch to capitalize on the subsequent hard market.

At current prices, investors acquire a proven compounder at a valuation that appears attractive relative to its historical ROE generation, with the aggressive buyback program providing a floor on per-share earnings growth even as top-line premium contracts.

## Company Overview

Arch Capital operates three segments: Insurance (Q1 2026 GPW of $2.7B), Reinsurance (Q1 2026 GPW of $3.4B, though this quarter includes heavy January renewal activity), and Mortgage (Q1 2026 GPW of $316M). Total FY2025 gross premiums written were $22.9 billion. The company completed the MCE Acquisition from Allianz in August 2024, materially expanding its U.S. mid-corporate and entertainment insurance platform.

The business model is fundamentally asset-light with minimal capex ($44M in FY2025 against $6.1B in free cash flow), and the Bermuda domicile historically provided tax efficiency — though the new 15% corporate income tax effective January 2025 partially erodes this advantage.

Arch was added to the S&P 500 in November 2022 and was designated as an Internationally Active Insurance Group (IAIG), reflecting its scale with $26.9 billion in total capital.

The growth score of 6.0 reflects the near-term premium contraction reality offset by structural per-share compounding. FY2025 book value per share grew 22.6%, and management has historically targeted continued strong BVPS compounding through retained earnings and buybacks.

## Competitive Landscape

Arch's competitive position is improving on a relative basis despite the overall market becoming more competitive:

1. **Cycle management discipline** differentiates Arch from volume-driven competitors. While peers maintain premium volume in soft markets (destroying book value), Arch contracts and preserves capital for redeployment when pricing improves. This is documented across multiple market cycles since 2001.

2. **Three-platform diversification** — the mortgage segment generates approximately 22% combined ratios, providing a highly profitable earnings stream. However, this segment is exposed to housing affordability, mortgage rates, home prices, unemployment, and GSE policy dynamics, and new originations have remained modest due to affordability challenges.

3. **MCE Acquisition** expanded the insurance platform into mid-corporate and entertainment lines, diversifying away from catastrophe-heavy reinsurance.

4. **Financial strength ratings** — Arch's strong capitalization enables access to preferred business that smaller competitors cannot access.

5. **Geopolitical risk environment** — elevated specialty insurance demand related to geopolitical tensions may support pricing in certain lines where Arch has expertise, though the magnitude of this benefit is difficult to quantify precisely.

The competitive position trajectory is stable-to-improving: while the broader market is more competitive (hence premium contraction), Arch's relative positioning strengthens as weaker competitors extend into inadequately priced business and face future loss development.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Catastrophe Exposure (Moderate-High):**
Q1 2025 California wildfire losses demonstrated the quarterly volatility inherent in the business. A major hurricane season or geopolitical infrastructure attack could produce $500M+ losses in a single quarter. Management's reduction in property catastrophe writings partially mitigates this.

**2. Insurance Cycle Turning (Moderate):**
The hard market that drove revenue from $9.6B to $19.9B may be peaking. If pricing deteriorates faster than expected, the premium headwind could exceed Q1 levels. Arch's demonstrated willingness to shrink protects book value — the risk is stagnation rather than impairment.

**3. Reserve Adequacy (Low-Moderate):**
Approximately $24.4B in net reserves creates exposure to social inflation and nuclear verdicts on long-tail casualty lines. While Arch has consistently released reserves (indicating conservative initial picks), an adverse development scenario could consume 2-3 quarters of earnings.

**4. Bermuda Tax Regime Change (Moderate):**
The new 15% CIT partially offset by tax credits produced an 8.6% effective rate in Q1 2026. OECD Pillar II retroactive changes could increase this rate further. Full impact unclear for 12-18 months.

**5. GSE Reform (Uncertain probability, High impact):**
If mortgage insurance requirements are eliminated through GSE privatization, the mortgage segment's substantial annual underwriting income faces material risk. The timing and probability of such reform remains uncertain.

## Conclusion

Arch Capital at approximately 8x trailing earnings offers a compelling combination of quality (17%+ ROE, best-in-class cycle management, $6B+ annual free cash flow), capital return (5-6% annual share count reduction), and valuation support (1.45x book value below historical average). The negative estimate revision trend is a function of deliberate premium contraction — precisely the behavior that protects and compounds long-term book value.

The score trajectory shows a decline from 7.9 to 7.5 over recent reports, primarily driven by growth prospects deterioration as the cycle turns. This decline is appropriate: top-line growth is genuinely decelerating. At current multiples, the stock compensates adequately for flat-to-declining premium volume because per-share compounding continues through buybacks and investment income growth.

The mortgage segment provides highly profitable earnings, MCE integration should improve over the next 12-18 months, and Arch's proven discipline positions it to capitalize when weaker competitors inevitably generate losses in the current competitive environment. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $110.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The $110 target is primarily supported by a book-value multiple approach (~1.65x projected year-end BVPS); the P/E-based derivation (10.5x $9.90 FY27E EPS) yields approximately $104, and the specific $250M premium headwind figure cited in the original thesis could not be verified against SEC filings (Q1 2026 NPW declined $167M YoY). These corrections suggest the base case may be closer to $104-$112.
