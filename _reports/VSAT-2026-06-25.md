**Research Date: June 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Viasat is a global satellite communications company in the middle of a financial transition that the market has already rewarded aggressively. The stock has risen roughly 380% from its 52-week low of $12.78 to the current $62.23, driven by the long-delayed ViaSat-3 capacity ramp, a $437.7M Space Force Protected Tactical SATCOM award, and a deleveraging trajectory that turned 9-month operating cash flow from $610M to $1.27B. The genuine improvement is in cash generation and debt reduction, not earnings; both recent "profitable" GAAP quarters were inflated by non-recurring items, most notably $152.5M of Ligado settlement interest income. On an adjusted basis the company still essentially broke even, with Q4 FY26 adjusted EPS of –$0.02 missing the ~$0.25 consensus.

The defining strategic question is whether Viasat's GEO-centric architecture can defend its single largest growth driver, in-flight connectivity (IFC), where 9-month aviation revenue grew 15% to $897M, against LEO competitors. SpaceX Starlink (~10,000 satellites) leads the airline Wi-Fi market, and Amazon Leo/Kuiper is racing to commercial launch in 2026. Delta's May 2026 decision to select Amazon Leo over Starlink for in-flight Wi-Fi, with Viasat not chosen, is the most important competitive datapoint in the file: it confirms Viasat's home turf is now a three-way LEO-dominated fight. Viasat's installed base of ~4,460 commercial aircraft plus ~2,100 business jets and ~1,100 aircraft under contract provides backlog protection, but future mandate wins are at structural risk.

We rate VSAT a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The deleveraging story is real and the activist involvement (a Carronade Capital cooperation agreement referenced on the Q4 FY26 call) introduces genuine sum-of-the-parts optionality. But consensus EPS estimates are falling hard (current-year down 53% over 90 days), the balance sheet sits in the Altman Z distress zone (~1.17), and the stock's prior re-rating has compressed the asymmetry that existed at lower prices. This is a high-beta, event-driven equity where the next several quarters of ViaSat-3 service entry, FCF delivery, and any activist action will determine direction.

## Company Overview

Viasat (incorporated 1986, headquartered in Carlsbad, CA, fiscal year ending March 31) operates two reportable segments:

**Communication Services (~72% of revenue; $2.49B of 9-month FY26 revenue):**
- **Aviation / IFC** — the primary growth driver: ~4,460 commercial aircraft + ~2,100 business jets in service, ~1,100 more under contract; 9-month aviation revenue $897M, +15% YoY.
- **Government satcom** — $590M, +6%.
- **Maritime** — $350M (including NexusWave managed service), slightly declining.
- **Fixed broadband & other** — $475M, –16%, as Viasat deliberately reallocates constrained bandwidth toward higher-value IFC. US fixed broadband down to ~143,000 subscribers at $112 ARPU.

**Defense & Advanced Technologies (~28%; $980M, +9% YoY):** Encryption/information security (HAIPE Type 1), space and mission systems, tactical networking (majority-owned TrellisWare), and emerging Direct-to-Device (D2D).

The May 2023 acquisition of Inmarsat transformed the company's scale, adding L-band/S-band satellites, maritime and aviation safety services, global mobility customers, and roughly $3.4B of debt. The competitive moat rests on a vertically integrated multi-band (Ka/L/S) GEO fleet of 23 operational satellites, Inmarsat's regulated L-band safety-of-life spectrum, a defense encryption franchise, and orbital slots/spectrum assets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** 9-month FY26 (ended Dec 31, 2025) revenue was $3.47B, +3% YoY. Q4 FY26 (reported May 28, 2026) was ~$1.17B, +2.1%. Growth is constrained by limited satellite capacity pending ViaSat-3 service launches. FY27 guidance calls for mid-single-digit revenue growth.

**Profitability.** 9-month operating income was $108.7M (up from $56.3M), an operating margin of only ~3%. The 9-month net loss attributable to Viasat was –$92.9M, materially improved from –$328.9M a year earlier. Q3 FY26 swung to GAAP net income of +$25.0M, but that was flattered by $152.5M of Ligado settlement interest income. Q4 FY26 GAAP net income was $58.8M ($0.41 EPS), again boosted by non-recurring items; however, the reported Q4 FY26 (quarter ended Mar 31, 2026) actual EPS was –$0.82 against a –$0.36 estimate, a –125.4% surprise; [unverified — the report's claim of positive GAAP net income and –$0.02 adjusted EPS for Q4 FY26 cannot be reconciled with the Phase 1 earnings-surprise data showing a –$0.82 actual]. Non-cash drag is heavy: 9-month depreciation of $772M plus intangible amortization of $236M.

**Cash flow — the positive surprise.** 9-month operating cash flow more than doubled to $1.27B (from $610M), aided by the Ligado deferred-revenue receipt and working-capital timing. Capex of ~$694M is declining as the ViaSat-3 build-out crests. Free cash flow turned solidly positive, and the FY27 FCF target is ~$180M, modest but a meaningful improvement after years of cash burn on the satellite build.

**Balance sheet and leverage — the central concern.** Total debt is $6.4B, down from $7.2B a year earlier following the redemption of the 2025 Notes, repayment of the Original Inmarsat term loan, and repayment of the Ex-Im facility. Cash stands at $1.35B. The cost of capital is high: term loans carry floating rates of 8.87%–9.55%, the Inmarsat 2029 notes are at 9.0%, and the 2031 notes at 7.5%, producing 9-month interest expense of $281M. Reported equity of $4.63B includes roughly $3.7B of goodwill and intangibles, with an accumulated deficit of –$418M. A $481.9M valuation allowance against deferred tax assets drives erratic effective tax rates that occasionally exceed 60%. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.17 places the company in the distress zone. Liquidity is adequate (>$1.1B revolver availability, no near-term maturities drawn), but the maturity ladder is demanding: 2027 Notes ($600M), 2028 Notes ($400M), 2029 Inmarsat Notes ($1.975B), 2031 Notes ($733M), plus term loans in 2029–2030. Refinancing the 2027–2029 stack at acceptable rates is the gating item for the financial-health thesis.

**Backlog.** Firm backlog of $3.97B and funded backlog of $3.71B provide visibility, with roughly half delivered within 12 months. This excludes recurring IFC service revenue, so it understates aviation economics.

The financial-health picture is bifurcated: a clearly improving cash-flow and deleveraging trend sits on top of a distressed capital structure with little margin for error. A score above the midpoint is not warranted while the Z-Score remains in the distress zone and high floating-rate debt compounds in a higher-for-longer environment, but the cash-flow trend lifts the score above where a pure burn-rate read would put it.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two distinct engines moving in opposite directions on credibility.

Aviation/IFC is the structural growth driver, with the installed base expanding and ~1,100 aircraft under contract. Government satcom (+6%) and Defense & Advanced Technologies (+9%, with the PTS award validating the franchise) provide steadier, higher-margin growth. The near-term capacity catalyst is ViaSat-3 F2, launched November 13, 2025, with commercial service expected in H1 FY27; this unlocks the bandwidth that has throttled growth since FY2023 and forced the deliberate sacrifice of fixed broadband.

Working against this, consensus EPS estimates are falling sharply. Current-quarter estimates were cut 45.7% over 90 days, next-quarter 47.1%, current-year 52.9% (to $0.30), and next-year 12.3% (to $0.40). This is the opposite of the positive estimate-revision velocity that signals a durable upturn. The growth here is a deleveraging-and-cash-flow story layered on low-single-digit revenue growth, not an earnings-acceleration story. Management's FY27 guide of mid-single-digit revenue growth and ~$180M FCF is modest in absolute terms relative to a $6.4B debt load.

We score growth prospects at the midpoint. The aviation and defense engines are real and the ViaSat-3 capacity unlock is a genuine catalyst, but the falling estimate trend, the LEO threat to the primary driver, and the dependence on accounting-noise items for headline "profits" cap the score. This is not the profile (revenue >40% with rising estimates) that warrants a high growth mark.

## Valuation Assessment

VSAT screens as un-valuable on conventional earnings metrics — trailing and forward P/E are both negative given the GAAP loss base, and the company pays no dividend (covenants restrict it). The meaningful framework is enterprise value against cash flow and a sum-of-the-parts lens.

At $62.23 the equity is ~$8.5B; adding net debt of roughly $5.05B ($6.4B debt less $1.35B cash) puts enterprise value near $13.6B. Against the FY27 FCF target, the equity FCF yield is thin (~2%), and the EV-to-FCF multiple is unattractive on a standalone basis. The cash-flow improvement is real but the absolute level does not support the current equity valuation on a steady-state basis. Analyst targets span a wide $49–$130, with a mean of $88.875 and median of $94; the dispersion reflects how binary the outcome is and how much weight the bulls place on either ViaSat-3 economics or an activist-driven structural unlock.

The sum-of-the-parts argument is the strongest bull valuation case. Defense & Advanced Technologies posts 16%+ segment margins and is not exposed to the LEO threat that overhangs the comms segment. Separating it could surface value the consolidated, leveraged structure obscures, the kind of thesis a Carronade-style activist would pursue. This is optionality, not a base case.

We score valuation at 4.5. The stock is not cheap on cash flow, the standalone multiple is rich for the growth and risk profile, and the cleanest "value" angle (SOTP) is contingent on an activist outcome that is not yet confirmed. We are not penalizing the stock for having already risen ~380% off its low; that appreciation reflected fundamental deleveraging progress. The valuation mark reflects current EV against current cash-flow power and the falling estimate trend, not the prior run. A "cheap" optical read on EV/EBITDA would be a value-trap signal here given the distress-zone balance sheet and the structural competitive overhang; we treat it accordingly.

## Competitive Landscape

The satellite-communications sector is bifurcating along orbital architecture. LEO constellations are winning the low-latency consumer and aviation broadband race on price and performance: Starlink operates ~10,000 satellites and leads airline Wi-Fi, and Amazon Leo/Kuiper has 300+ satellites and is racing to first commercial customers in 2026. Viasat's GEO-centric architecture faces structural latency and per-bit-cost disadvantages in exactly the premium IFC market that is its largest growth engine.

The Delta decision crystallizes the threat: Delta cited price and a streaming bundle, and Viasat was not in contention. This is the clearest evidence that Viasat must prove its multi-orbit orchestration model (reselling and integrating third-party LEO/MEO capacity alongside its own GEO fleet) can win new airline mandates rather than merely service its existing installed base.

Where Viasat retains a genuine moat is in regulated safety-of-life services. Inmarsat's L-band maritime and aviation safety franchise is difficult for LEO players to replicate, government/defense satcom rewards wide-area coverage and security (the PTS award validates this), and the encryption franchise (HAIPE Type 1) is sticky. Direct-to-Device is an early growth bet within Defense & Advanced Technologies.

The competitive trajectory is the binding constraint on the equity. The defensible segments are real but slower-growing; the growth engine is under structural attack. We score competitive position at 4.5 — the moat in safety-of-life and defense is intact, but the trajectory in the most economically important market (premium IFC) is deteriorating, and trajectory matters more than level. We hold the score at the prior 4.5–5.0 range rather than advancing it, because the Delta datapoint and the Starlink/Amazon momentum are net-negative evidence on trajectory since the last review.

## Risk Assessment

**LEO competition — the existential strategic risk.** Covered above; the structural threat to IFC economics is the dominant risk and is only partially priced.

**Leverage and interest burden.** High floating and fixed rates with a 2027–2029 maturity ladder. If rates stay elevated or equity sentiment sours, refinancing risk is material against a distress-zone balance sheet.

**Satellite execution risk.** Viasat took a $1.67B writedown in FY2024 from the ViaSat-3 F1 antenna anomaly and Inmarsat-6 F2 issue (partly offset by $770M of insurance, fully collected by Q1 FY26). The remaining fleet (F3, GX7/8/9, four Inmarsat-8) is still under construction; any further anomaly would be severe given the leverage.

**Near-term capacity constraints.** Management explicitly cites bandwidth constraints since FY2023 until ViaSat-3 enters full service, throttling growth and forcing trade-offs.

**US Government concentration (~16% of revenue).** Exposure to budget delays, shutdowns, and DCAA/DCMA audits (costs approved only through FY2022). [unverified — the research brief does not establish a broader federal procurement reform context, so that connection is removed.]

**Ligado counterparty risk.** The $568M settlement, including ~$16M/quarter escalating 3% through 2107, depends on Ligado's compliance and restructuring solvency — an underappreciated annuity, but a contingent one.

**Governance.** Combined Chairman/CEO role (co-founder Mark Dankberg), CFO transition (Shawn Duffy out, Garrett Chase in), and multiple 2025 officer-departure 8-Ks. The Carronade cooperation agreement is both a risk (activist disruption) and a potential catalyst (value unlock).

The risk score of 4.0 reflects the combination of a distress-zone balance sheet, a structural competitive threat to the primary growth driver, and dependence on non-recurring items for headline profitability — risks that are not fully priced into a stock that has re-rated nearly 4x off its low.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is currently complacent rather than fearful. Near-term ATM IV of 75.0% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 81.2% (a –6.3% spread, IV percentile in the 2nd percentile), and the put/call skew is call-heavy at –6.3% with a put/call OI ratio of 0.57. The term structure is in contango (75.0% near vs 77.4% long). ATM IV has collapsed 30.4 points (from 105.4% to 75.0%) since late May as the post-earnings event passed, even as the stock fell 16.5% over the same window. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±15.4%, confirming this remains a high-beta, headline-driven equity. The call-heavy positioning and cheap IV suggest the options market is not pricing the LEO/leverage risks with much fear — a mild divergence from our fundamental caution, and a reason not to read the recent price drop as a market verdict of imminent distress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** ViaSat-3 F2 enters commercial service in H1 FY27 without further anomalies, unlocking the bandwidth that has throttled growth and allowing aviation revenue to compound off the installed base plus contracted backlog. FCF delivers at or above target, debt continues falling, and the Altman Z-Score climbs out of distress, re-rating the equity as a deleveraging turnaround. The Carronade activist agreement catalyzes a sum-of-the-parts separation, surfacing value in the 16%+-margin Defense & Advanced Technologies segment that the consolidated structure obscures. Multi-orbit orchestration proves it can defend IFC economics, and the high-margin Ligado annuity plus the PTS defense award provide steady cash. In this scenario the stock approaches the upper analyst range toward $130, supported by SOTP math and a cleaner balance sheet.

**Bear Case.** LEO competition accelerates — Amazon Leo launches commercially and, following Delta, additional airlines bypass Viasat for direct Starlink/Amazon deals, eroding the IFC growth engine as the existing backlog runs off. A ViaSat-3 execution issue or refinancing stress at the 2027–2029 maturities forces dilutive action against the distressed balance sheet. Adjusted earnings continue to disappoint (the most recent quarter's actual EPS of –$0.82 against a –$0.36 estimate is the warning), estimates keep falling, and the activist effort fails to produce a structural unlock. In this scenario the stock retraces toward the lower analyst target of $49 or below, a ~21%+ decline from current levels, with a deeper move possible if refinancing sentiment sours.

The base case sits between these: ViaSat-3 enters service roughly on schedule, deleveraging continues, but IFC growth decelerates as LEO competition bites and earnings remain thin. The activist creates optionality without a confirmed catalyst, and the stock trades in a wide, volatile band around current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction at the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- ViaSat-3 F2 enters full commercial service on schedule (H1 FY27) and management confirms a clear path to FY27 FCF ≥$180M, with EPS estimate revisions turning positive after several quarters of cuts.
- A confirmed activist-driven structural action (segment separation or material asset sale) that surfaces SOTP value, particularly monetizing Defense & Advanced Technologies.
- A new marquee airline IFC mandate win that demonstrates the multi-orbit model can compete against LEO offerings on economics.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further ViaSat-3 satellite anomaly, or refinancing of the 2027–2029 maturities at materially higher rates or on dilutive terms.
- Loss of additional major airline IFC mandates to Starlink/Amazon Leo, signaling backlog erosion in the core growth engine.
- Continued adjusted-EPS misses with falling estimates and no improvement in the Altman Z-Score out of the distress zone.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY27 earnings report (expected early August 2026), which should provide the first read on ViaSat-3 F2 service entry, updated FCF trajectory, and any concrete development on the Carronade agreement. Given the ±15.4% implied move and the event-driven nature of the equity, position sizing should reflect the elevated volatility.

## Conclusion

Viasat is a leveraged, event-driven turnaround where the genuine improvement — operating cash flow doubling to $1.27B over nine months and $0.8B of YoY debt reduction — is partially obscured by GAAP "profits" that rest on non-recurring Ligado items, and is overhung by a structural LEO threat to its single most important growth driver. The Delta-to-Amazon-Leo decision is a real warning about the future of premium in-flight connectivity, and falling consensus estimates (current-year –53% over 90 days) argue against treating this as a clean earnings inflection.

At the same time, the stock has already re-rated ~380% off its low on legitimate deleveraging progress, the defense and safety-of-life franchises are intact, the ViaSat-3 capacity unlock is a near-term catalyst, and the Carronade activist involvement introduces credible sum-of-the-parts optionality. The balance sheet remains in the distress zone (Z ~1.17), which caps how much credit we can extend to the bull case. The trajectory of scores has improved modestly (4.3 → 4.5), driven by financial health, but valuation has deteriorated as the prior run compressed asymmetry, and the competitive datapoints since the last review are net-negative.

We move from AVOID to HOLD. The improving cash-flow story and the activist/SOTP optionality no longer justify an outright avoid at current fundamentals, but the distressed balance sheet, falling earnings estimates, and the unresolved LEO competitive question prevent a constructive call. This is a stock to monitor against the specific triggers above rather than to own with conviction. Overall score: 4.5 / 10.
