## Executive Summary

Casey's General Stores is the third-largest U.S. convenience store chain with 2,924 locations, differentiated by its small-town geographic focus (71% of stores in communities under 20,000 people), made-from-scratch pizza program, and vertically-integrated distribution. The company has beaten analyst EPS estimates for four consecutive quarters, including a 32% beat in Q1 FY2026 and 14.5% beat in Q3 FY2026, demonstrating that consensus has systematically underestimated the earnings power of this business.

The stock has appreciated 90% over the past 12 months, reflecting the Fikes/CEFCO acquisition integration, structurally expanding fuel margins (41.0 cents/gallon in Q3 FY2026 vs. historical norms of 15-25 cents), and inclusion in the S&P 500 index (announced April 2026, replacing Hologic). At ~49x trailing twelve-month EPS (TTM EPS of approximately $17.42 through Q3 FY2026), the multiple appears elevated in isolation. However, the forward earnings trajectory — FY2027 consensus of $20.17 growing at 11%+ — combined with 20.9% EBITDA growth through nine months of FY2026 (which prompted management to raise FY2026 EBITDA guidance to 18–20% from the original 10–12%), suggests meaningful operating leverage.

The specific opportunity lies in the margin expansion story: same-store labor hours declining for 12 consecutive quarters while same-store sales grow, fuel margins remaining elevated with the added benefit of a newly-acquired fuel terminal, and food margins benefiting from product mix optimization. Two directors purchased shares in open-market transactions at $555 and $665 — well below current levels — providing informed insider confirmation of value at lower prices, though broader insider activity over the past year has been more mixed.

## Company Overview

Casey's operates across three revenue pillars: fuel (~61% of revenue, ~12-15% gross margin), grocery and general merchandise (~26% of revenue, ~36% gross margin), and prepared food and dispensed beverages (~10% of revenue, ~58% gross margin). The inverted margin structure means the highest-margin category (prepared food, centered on made-from-scratch pizza) is the primary strategic driver, while fuel serves as a traffic generator.

The company's competitive moat derives from three structural advantages:

1. **Geographic positioning:** Operating predominantly in communities national chains avoid due to population thresholds. This creates effective local monopolies.
2. **Vertical integration:** Three distribution centers supplying ~70% of in-store products and ~60% of fuel, plus a fleet of ~500 fuel tractors and a newly-acquired fuel terminal in Waco, Texas.
3. **Real estate ownership:** The company owns substantially all of its stores (leasing land and/or buildings at only 245 of 2,904 locations as of April 2025), distribution infrastructure, and support center — providing asset backing and significantly reducing lease escalation risk.

The over 10 million Casey's Rewards loyalty members (surpassing that milestone during Q3 FY2026) provide a data advantage for targeted promotions, creating customer stickiness in markets with limited alternatives.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability:**

Total revenue for FY2025 was $15.94B, with EBITDA of $1.2B (7.5% margin). Through nine months of FY2026, EBITDA reached $1.133B (+20.9% YoY). Net income for the nine-month period was $551.8M (+23.1%).

Q3 FY2026 demonstrated the margin expansion thesis: total gross margin expanded from 23.4% to 25.7% YoY, driven by fuel margin expansion (41.0 vs. 36.4 cents/gallon) and inside-store mix improvement. Net income grew 49.3% to $130.1M on only 0.3% revenue growth.

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**

Operating cash flow through nine months of FY2026 was $979M, on pace for >$1.3B annually. Free cash flow in FY2025 was $585M. Long-term debt of $2.43B (reduced from $2.51B) with an undrawn $850M revolver and $465M cash on hand provides ample liquidity. The current ratio improved from 0.92 to 1.04 over the fiscal year.

Net debt-to-EBITDA is approximately 1.4x on a trailing basis (net debt of ~$1.97B against TTM EBITDA of ~$1.4B) — a comfortable level for a cash-generative retailer with owned real estate.

**Capital Allocation:**

Management is executing on all three levers simultaneously: repurchasing 134,608 shares at an average of $563 in Q3 ($75.8M), paying quarterly dividends of $0.57/share, and investing $465M in capex through nine months. The $156.8M remaining under the current buyback authorization will likely be refreshed.

## Growth Analysis

Casey's growth comes from three distinct channels:

1. **Same-store sales growth:** Prepared food +4.3%, grocery/merchandise +4.0%, fuel gallons +0.4% in Q3 FY2026. Management's updated FY2026 guidance calls for 3.5–4.5% inside same-store sales growth.

2. **New store additions:** At least 80 new stores per year through organic builds and acquisitions. The strategic plan targets ~500 stores added during FY2024-FY2026. Store count reached 2,924 at January 2026, approaching 3,000.

3. **Margin expansion:** The 12 consecutive quarters of same-store labor hour reductions while growing sales represents structural operating efficiency improvement that has not yet been fully reflected in forward estimates. Total gross margin expanding 230bps YoY in Q3 is a powerful compounding factor.

Average store operating income (defined as store-level retail sales less store-level COGS, operating expenses, and D&A, excluding corporate-level expenses) has grown from $445K (FY2023) to $473K (FY2024) to $496K (FY2025) — approximately 5-6% annual improvement. With estimated new-build costs in the range of $6–7M per store, this implies attractive store-level returns on incremental capital, though full unlevered returns would be somewhat lower after accounting for corporate overhead allocation.

Consensus estimates project FY2027 EPS of $20.17, representing ~11% growth over FY2026's $18.14. Given the company's pattern of beating estimates (average surprise of +16% over four quarters), realized FY2027 EPS may land above $21, though such extrapolation carries inherent uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

At the current price of approximately $860-895, Casey's trades at:
- ~49x trailing twelve-month EPS (~$17.42 TTM through Q3 FY2026)
- ~59x FY2025 full-year EPS ($14.64)
- ~47x FY2026E EPS ($18.14)
- ~44x FY2027E EPS ($20.17)
- ~22x EV/EBITDA (on ~$1.5B forward EBITDA)

The multiple is at a significant premium to the broader consumer staples/retail sector. Direct peer comparisons are imperfect: traditional grocers like Kroger trade at materially lower forward P/E multiples (~12-13x forward earnings), while Casey's growth rate and margin profile differ substantially from mature grocery operators. Casey's growth rate and capital-light expansion (owned real estate appreciates) justify a premium, but the magnitude reflects both fundamental quality and technical factors including recent S&P 500 index inclusion.

The stock now trades above the 52-week high of $877.53 and above the mean analyst target of $775. The $962 high target provides 7-12% upside from current levels on the bull case.

The valuation is the primary constraint on the thesis. At 44-49x trailing/forward earnings, the stock prices in meaningful execution on the Fikes integration, sustained fuel margin expansion, and continued efficiency gains. However, the consistent earnings beat pattern suggests consensus may still underestimate earnings power, and the forward multiple on realized EPS (if >$21 for FY2027) would be closer to 40-42x — more reasonable for a company compounding EBITDA at 10-15%.

The PEG ratio on 11% growth is approximately 4.0x — elevated but comparable to high-quality consumer staples compounders. The FCF yield of approximately 2.0% on FY2026E earnings provides limited margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Casey's occupies a defensible niche: the small-town convenience store with food-as-differentiator positioning. Three key competitive dynamics support durability:

1. **Barriers to competitive entry are high in small towns.** A community of 5,000-15,000 people typically supports one well-positioned convenience store. The economics don't support a second entrant when Casey's already owns the real estate and operates with distribution-cost advantages from vertical integration.

2. **Dollar stores (DLGO, DG) are the primary competitive threat for merchandise** but lack fuel and prepared food capabilities. The cross-traffic between fuel and food creates a demand bundle that pure-play merchandise stores cannot replicate.

3. **QSRs compete on food** but lack the convenience of a single-stop for fuel + food + grocery. Casey's pizza at $6-8 competes favorably on price with Domino's or Pizza Hut while capturing customers already stopping for gas.

The acquisition of Fikes/CEFCO expands Casey's into Texas and the Southeast, diversifying geographic concentration while adding a wholesale fuel network. This represents a new growth avenue with incremental revenue and margin opportunity as the CEFCO stores are eventually upgraded with full kitchen capabilities.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Fuel margin normalization (HIGH):** The 41 cents/gallon margin is historically elevated. Management acknowledges this can fluctuate significantly. Casey sold 3.197 billion retail fuel gallons in FY2025; a reversion to 28–30 cents/gallon (a decline of approximately 11–13 cents/gallon) would imply gross profit/EBITDA exposure of approximately $350–415M before any offsetting factors. While management believes the post-COVID structural shift in fuel margins will persist, and the fuel terminal acquisition may provide incremental supply-side economics, the filings do not explicitly attribute the current margin level to the terminal or wholesale network. The magnitude of potential downside from fuel-margin reversion is the single largest risk to the thesis.

2. **Valuation compression on any execution stumble:** At 44-49x earnings, the stock has minimal margin for error. A guidance miss or deceleration in same-store trends would likely produce a 15-20% drawdown.

3. **Fikes integration complexity:** 198 stores in new geographies (Texas, Alabama, Florida, Mississippi) with different competitive dynamics than the Midwest. The $1.145B purchase price implies ~$5.8M per store — reasonable but requires successful food program expansion to generate adequate returns.

4. **EV adoption (LOW probability, HIGH impact, LONG-DATED):** Casey's rural Midwest footprint is insulated from near-term EV penetration, but a 10-15 year structural shift toward electrification would gradually erode the fuel traffic model. Only 269 charging stations across 58 stores currently. The timeline provides runway to adapt.

5. **Interest rate environment:** $2.43B in debt at 5.2-5.4% rates against a rate environment where refinancing in 2027-2028 may not produce materially lower rates. Interest expense rose 57% in FY2025.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($960-1,000, ~8-12% upside):**
- FY2027 EPS comes in at $21-22 (above $20.17 consensus) given the 4-quarter pattern of 5-32% beats
- Fuel margins sustain above 38 cents/gallon through FY2027 as the elevated post-COVID margin environment persists
- CEFCO stores begin remodeling in FY2027, adding 50-100bps to same-store food sales growth
- Same-store labor efficiency gains continue (13th, 14th, 15th quarters), driving 100-150bps of additional margin expansion
- Multiple sustains at 44-46x on demonstrated compounding quality

**Bear Case ($620-680, ~25-30% downside):**
- Fuel margins revert toward 28-30 cents/gallon, reducing EBITDA by $350-415M before offsets — representing roughly 25-30% of current EBITDA
- Consumer weakness in small-town Midwest (which correlates with agricultural income) depresses inside-store traffic
- CEFCO integration proves costlier/slower than expected, goodwill impairment risk emerges on the $1.245B balance
- Multiple compresses to 33-35x as growth decelerates toward mid-single-digits
- S&P 500 index-inclusion demand normalizes, removing a technical tailwind

**Base Case ($940-960, ~7-10% upside over 12 months):**
- FY2027 EPS of $20.50-21.00 (slight beat to consensus)
- EBITDA growth of 9-11% as fuel margins moderate slightly but inside-store growth persists at 3-4%
- Store count reaches 3,050-3,100 by FY2027 end
- Multiple sustains at 44-46x given continued execution and limited quality alternatives in consumer staples

## Addressing the Valuation Concern

The specific reason to issue a BUY despite the 44-49x multiple: Casey's has beaten consensus EPS by an average of 16% over the last four quarters (range: 5-32%), based on available consensus data. This pattern reflects systematic under-estimation of (a) fuel margin durability, (b) operating leverage from labor efficiency, and (c) the Fikes acquisition contribution. However, management's decision to raise FY2026 EBITDA guidance to 18–20% (from the original 10–12%) means much of this outperformance is now incorporated in the company's own outlook. The remaining upside potential depends on whether the beat pattern continues into FY2027.

When consensus at $20.17 for FY2027 is adjusted for a conservative 8-10% beat rate, EPS could reach $21.50-22.00, implying a true forward multiple of 40-41x. For a company growing EBITDA 10-15% annually with owned real estate, 10M+ loyalty members, and expanding geographic footprint, 40x is a reasonable premium-compounder multiple — particularly in an environment where consumer staples quality is scarce.

Two directors made open-market purchases at $555 and $665 (30-37% below current price) within the last 4 months, providing informed insider validation at lower price levels. However, broader insider activity over the trailing twelve months has included more substantial selling (including by senior executives), which is common for a stock that has appreciated 90% but tempers the bullishness of the director purchases alone. Short interest at 3.5% with 1.4 days to cover indicates limited institutional skepticism. The recent S&P 500 index inclusion represents a technical catalyst that may have contributed to demand beyond what fundamentals alone would support.

## Conclusion

Casey's General Stores is a high-quality compounder with structural competitive advantages, demonstrated operational excellence (12 consecutive quarters of labor hour reductions), and a pattern of exceeding consensus expectations. The valuation is the primary constraint — at 44-49x earnings, the stock requires continued execution. However, the combination of rising estimates (+4.7-5.0% over 90 days), strong beat record, director purchases below current levels, and a credible path to $21+ FY2027 EPS creates sufficient asymmetry to justify a BUY with a 12-month horizon. Position sizing should reflect the elevated multiple: 1.5-2% allocation is appropriate given the narrower margin of safety relative to lower-multiple opportunities.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The fuel-margin downside exposure is materially larger than initially presented ($350–415M vs. $150–200M), management has already raised FY2026 EBITDA guidance to 18–20% (reducing the "guidance beat" catalyst), and S&P 500 index inclusion may account for a portion of the valuation re-rating — collectively these corrections weaken the risk/reward underpinning the BUY recommendation at current prices.
