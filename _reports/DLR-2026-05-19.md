## Executive Summary

Digital Realty Trust is the world's largest data center REIT by total capacity, operating 310 facilities across 30+ countries with 57.6 million rentable square feet. The company reported a record Q1 2026 with Core FFO of $2.04/share (+15% YoY), raised full-year guidance to $8.00-$8.10/share, and signed its largest-ever lease — a 200MW AI inference deployment in Charlotte with a double-A-rated hyperscaler. Leverage declined to a multiyear low of 4.7x debt-to-EBITDA while the development pipeline expanded 50%+ to 1.2 GW under construction.

The fundamental trajectory is unambiguously positive: AI demand is converting from pilot to production, renewal spreads are running +27% on hyperscale leases, and DLR's capital-light JV strategy (DC Partners NA Fund raising $3B+ equity supporting $10B investment) is de-risking growth while generating fee income. The stock has appreciated ~22% from its December 2025 lows.

The binding constraint on a more aggressive recommendation is valuation: at $188/share, DLR trades at ~23-24x forward Core FFO guidance ($8.05 midpoint) in an environment where the 30-year Treasury yield exceeds 5.1%. For a REIT with ~$18.6B of total debt, rising long-end rates create direct refinancing headwinds (despite the current 2.90% effective rate) and compress the spread between DLR's FFO yield (~4.3%) and risk-free alternatives. Until either yields stabilize meaningfully or DLR's FFO growth trajectory re-rates the multiple to below 22x, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Digital Realty Trust operates through Digital Realty Trust, L.P. as a global data center platform (PlatformDIGITAL®) providing power, space, cooling, and connectivity infrastructure. The business spans the full deployment spectrum: single-cabinet colocation (0-1 MW) with 232,000+ cross-connects globally, and multi-hundred-megawatt hyperscale deployments for the world's largest cloud and AI companies.

Key metrics as of Q1 2026:
- **310 data centers** in 55+ metros, 30+ countries
- **5,000+ customers** including Fortune 50 technology companies, financial institutions, and network operators
- **3 GW operating portfolio** with 6+ GW developable capacity
- **1.2 GW under construction** (61% pre-leased)
- **$1.8B annualized rent backlog** ($1.0B at DLR share)

The top customer represents 11.7% of annualized recurring revenue; the top 20 represent 51%. Northern Virginia accounts for 21.4% of total annualized rent, creating meaningful geographic concentration.

## Growth Analysis

**AI Demand as Primary Growth Vector:** Management explicitly states AI demand is converting "from pilot to production." The 200MW Charlotte lease — DLR's largest ever — is for AI inference workloads with a double-A-rated hyperscaler on a 15-year term with 3%+ annual escalators. At typical market rates, this single lease could represent $200-400M+ in annual rent when fully deployed (4-7% of current revenue).

21% of 0-1 MW bookings are now AI-oriented. The company signed 116 new customer logos in Q1 2026 and achieved record interconnection bookings ($98M in 0-1 MW).

**Development Pipeline:** 1.2 GW under construction (up 50%+ sequentially) with 61% pre-leased and a total backlog of $1.8B annualized rent. The 6+ GW of developable capacity beyond the current 3 GW operating portfolio provides multi-year visible growth runway.

**Capital-Light Platform Evolution:** The DC Partners NA Fund ($3B+ equity, ~$10B investment capacity), combined with JVs with Blackstone (500MW across Frankfurt/Paris/NoVA), Mitsubishi, GI Partners, and Brookfield, allows DLR to participate in growth while reducing balance sheet intensity. Fee income grew 98% in 2025 and will continue scaling as these vehicles deploy capital.

**Estimate Revisions:** Quarterly estimates are modestly positive (+8-10% over 30/90 days for upcoming quarters). Annual estimates show a more complex picture — the -12.5% revision in current year EPS likely reflects GAAP adjustments rather than operational deterioration, given the simultaneous guidance raise. With only 2 analysts providing annual EPS estimates, these figures lack statistical significance. Core FFO guidance of $8.00-$8.10 (raised from $7.90-$8.00) is the authoritative forward metric.

## Valuation Assessment

At $188/share and forward Core FFO guidance of $8.05 (midpoint):
- **P/FFO (forward):** 23.4x
- **Dividend yield:** 2.6%
- **Trailing P/E (GAAP):** 50.3x (distorted by property gains/impairments — not the relevant metric for REITs)
- **FFO yield:** 4.3%

**Peer Comparison:**

| Metric | DLR | Equinix (EQIX) | AMT | PSA | Peer Median P/E |
|--------|-----|-------|-----|-----|------|
| P/FFO (approx) | 23.4x | ~27x | ~25x | ~22x | — |
| Dividend Yield | 2.6% | 1.9% | 4.2% | 4.1% | 4.2% |

| Leverage | 4.7x | ~5.5x | ~5.0x | ~4.5x | — |

DLR trades at a premium to storage/tower/net-lease peers on a P/FFO basis, justified by its faster growth rate. It trades at a discount to Equinix, arguably justified given EQIX's higher-margin interconnection density. The 2.6% dividend yield is the lowest among peers, reflecting the market's expectation of growth over income.

**The Duration Problem:** At $188/share with a 4.3% FFO yield, DLR's spread over the 30-year Treasury is compressed. Investors are paying for growth, not current income. This is rational if FFO grows 10%+ annually, but creates vulnerability to further yield increases. Every 50bp rise in the 10Y mechanically compresses REIT valuations by ~5-8% through the cap rate expansion channel.

The stock's decline from $208 (52-week high, mid-April 2026) to $188 partially reflects this repricing.

**Valuation Verdict:** DLR is not expensive relative to its growth rate (PEG ~2.3x on 10% Core FFO growth), but it offers limited margin of safety given the yield environment. At $165-170 (~20x forward FFO), the risk/reward becomes asymmetric. At $188, it is fairly valued assuming 10% Core FFO growth sustains and yields do not continue rising.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural Position — Improving:**
- #1 global data center REIT by total capacity
- Supply constraints (power availability, permitting, labor) are widening the gap between demand and deployable capacity, benefiting incumbents with existing land banks and utility relationships
- 6+ GW developable capacity provides multi-year supply advantage
- 232,000+ cross-connects create network effects and switching costs
- Global platform (30+ countries) vs. single-market competitors

**Competitive Threats:**
- Hyperscalers (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta) building their own data centers — DLR explicitly acknowledges this risk
- Private capital (Blackstone, Brookfield) funding competitive builds and compressing development yields
- Equinix remains the premier interconnection platform with higher revenue per kW
- Technology obsolescence risk from AI-driven power density requirements potentially rendering older facilities sub-optimal

**Pricing Power Evidence:** The +27% cash rent change on >1MW renewals confirms supply/demand imbalance and customer switching costs (data gravity, interconnection dependencies). In a commoditized market, 27% rent increases on renewal would not be achievable.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Interest Rate / Duration Risk (PRIMARY — CONCRETE AND CURRENT):** With long-end yields elevated and structural selling pressure, DLR faces multiple headwinds: (a) its FFO yield offers limited spread over risk-free, making valuation dependent on growth delivery; (b) $18.6B of debt, while 92% fixed today, will face repricing as maturities come due — the 2.90% effective rate will inevitably rise; (c) cap rate expansion in real estate broadly compresses asset values.

**2. Execution Risk on Development Pipeline:** $5.8B of future development investment needed across the 1.2 GW pipeline. Construction cost inflation (tariffs explicitly flagged) could compress development yields. If new supply comes online simultaneously across the industry, utilization rates and pricing power could erode.

**3. Customer Concentration:** Top customer at 11.7% of ARR. The 200MW Charlotte lease, once stabilized, will likely increase concentration in a single customer. If that hyperscaler's AI capex cycle moderates, DLR's growth rate is directly impacted.

**4. Geographic Concentration:** Northern Virginia at 21.4% of rent. Overbuilding risk in NoVA has been a recurring sector concern; DLR's diversification into Charlotte and Atlanta partially mitigates but takes years.

**5. Dilution:** ~19% share count growth over 3 years. ATM issuance at $173/share (below current price) means recent dilution was accretive on a per-share basis, but continued equity issuance at scale is probable given the capital-intensive growth plan.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($220-250, +17-33%):** AI demand continues accelerating through 2027-2028. Core FFO reaches $9.00+/share by 2027 as the Charlotte lease, development pipeline, and renewal spreads compound. Long-end yields stabilize. DLR re-rates to 25x forward FFO on scarcity premium (limited ways to get direct AI infrastructure exposure in REIT wrapper). Fee income from JV platform becomes meaningful P&L contributor ($300M+ by 2027).

**Bear Case ($155-165, -12% to -18%):** 30Y yields rise further, mechanically compressing REIT multiples by 15-20%. Hyperscaler capex cycles moderate after 2026 (Meta/Microsoft rationalization), slowing new leasing velocity. Construction cost inflation (tariffs) compresses development yields to below cost of capital. DLR re-rates to 19-20x forward FFO.

**Base Case ($195-210, +3-12%):** Core FFO delivers at $8.00-$8.10 as guided. Yields remain elevated but do not spike further. Development pipeline progresses on schedule. Multiple compresses slightly to 24-26x forward FFO but is supported by visible growth. Dividend grows modestly (+3-5%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (→ BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $165-170 (20-21x forward FFO) on rates-driven selling without fundamental deterioration — this would create asymmetric upside
2. 30Y yield stabilizes below 5.0% and term structure normalizes, relieving the primary valuation headwind
3. Q2/Q3 2026 results show Core FFO run-rate exceeding $8.20/share with further guidance raises, making the growth premium more defensible

**Downgrade Triggers (→ AVOID):**
1. New leasing velocity declines >30% sequentially for 2+ quarters, signaling demand saturation
2. Hyperscaler capex guidance cuts (Google/Microsoft/Meta) reducing future demand pipeline
3. 10Y Treasury breaches 5.5% sustained, indicating multiple turns of compression ahead
4. Core FFO guidance is reduced below $7.80/share

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July). The combination of quarterly results + 30Y yield trajectory by then will determine whether the growth-vs-duration tradeoff has shifted.

## Conclusion

Digital Realty is executing well against the strongest demand backdrop in data center history. Record leasing, declining leverage, accelerating Core FFO growth, and structural supply constraints all favor the business. The management team's capital-light JV strategy de-risks growth while creating a new fee income stream.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: **at 23-24x forward FFO with a compressed spread to the 30-year Treasury (4.3% FFO yield vs. risk-free alternatives), DLR requires 10%+ sustained FFO growth to justify its current price. If yields rise further, the multiple compresses before the growth materializes.** A 100bp rise in 10Y yields historically correlates with a 10-15% REIT price decline, all else equal.

The stock is worth owning for exposure to AI infrastructure buildout, and the fundamental trajectory supports holding an existing position. But initiating new positions at $188 offers ~12% upside to base case versus ~15% downside to bear case — insufficiently asymmetric for a BUY given the macro yield regime.
