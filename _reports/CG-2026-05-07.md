## Executive Summary

The Carlyle Group manages $477 billion in assets across private equity, credit, and solutions strategies. Under CEO Harvey Schwartz (since early 2023), the firm has pivoted toward fee-related earnings (FRE) growth as its primary value driver, with ambitious three-year targets of $1.9B+ FRE and $6.00+ distributable earnings per share by end-2028. FRE grew 12% in FY2025 to a record $1.24B, driven by Global Credit (+21%) and AlpInvest (+58%), while GPE fees declined 6% on smaller fund sizes.

The valuation appears optically cheap at 10x distributable earnings and 14x FRE, but the stock faces material headwinds: four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, broadly declining analyst estimates (current quarter EPS revised down 16.1% in 30 days), a deeply stressed options term structure pricing near-term risk, and structural exposure to a credit market environment where private credit stress is building across the industry. The stock has rallied 27% from its 52-week low but remains 27% below its September 2025 high, reflecting market skepticism about both the macro environment for alternative managers and Carlyle's ability to execute its growth plan.

The risk/reward at $50.80 is balanced rather than compelling. The 2028 targets, if achieved, imply substantial upside, but the path requires sustained credit market fundraising in an environment of rising private credit defaults, normalized PE exit activity that remains historically depressed, and margin expansion from a platform that still faces structural GPE fee headwinds. Execution must precede re-rating.

## Company Overview

Carlyle Group is one of the world's largest alternative asset managers with three operating segments:

- **Global Private Equity (34% of AUM, $164B):** Buyout, growth, real estate, infrastructure, and natural resources funds across 275+ portfolio companies. Flagship U.S. Buyout fund ($52.8B) and Carlyle Partners franchise.
- **Global Credit (44% of AUM, $211B):** The fastest-growing segment, having nearly quadrupled AUM over five years. Includes insurance solutions via the Fortitude advisory relationship ($80.4B), liquid credit/CLOs ($50.1B), and private credit ($74.4B).
- **Carlyle AlpInvest (21% of AUM, $102B):** Secondaries, co-investments, and primary fund investments. Grew 20% YoY in AUM.

The firm operates from 27 offices across four continents with 2,500+ employees including 770 investment professionals, serving 3,200+ investors from 87 countries. 93% of commitments come from investors in two or more products.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:**

Total segment revenues grew 7% to $3.9B in FY2025. The critical distinction for Carlyle is between recurring fee income (FRE) and volatile performance allocations. FRE reached a record $1.24B (+12% YoY), while GAAP performance allocations fell 39% to $1.22B. Distributable earnings totaled $1.69B (+11%), translating to approximately $4.70/share.

FRE by segment:
- GPE: $561M (-6%) — pressured by smaller fund sizes and fee step-downs
- Global Credit: $402M (+21%) — driven by direct lending, CLOs, and capital markets fees
- AlpInvest: $274M (+58%) — inflated by $55.7M in non-recurring catch-up management fees

**Fee-Earning AUM:** $337B (+11% from $304B), with pending fee-earning AUM of $16.8B not yet activated. This pending amount provides near-term visibility into fee growth.

**Balance Sheet:** Cash of $2.0B, debt of approximately $3.0B in senior/subordinated notes (3.5%-5.65% coupons, maturities 2029-2061), and a $1.0B undrawn revolver extended to May 2030. The reported D/E ratio of 3.13x is misleading due to fund consolidation — the operating balance sheet is considerably more conservative. Net accrued performance revenues of $2.86B (attributable to Carlyle) represent significant stored value, approximately 16% of market cap.

**Capital Return:** $1.2B returned in 2025 ($505M dividends + $687M buybacks), representing 71% of distributable earnings. A fresh $2.0B repurchase authorization was announced in February 2026. The dividend yields approximately 2.7% at current prices.

**Cash Flow:** The negative operating cash flow in reported data is entirely driven by consolidated fund activities (CLO purchases, fund investments). Company-only operating cash flow was +$1.09B in both 2024 and 2025.

**Record Fundraising:** $53.7B of capital inflows in 2025 (+32% YoY), with particular strength in AlpInvest and Global Credit.

## Growth Analysis

**Three-Year Targets (through end-2028):**
- FRE: $1.9B+ (from $1.24B — implies ~15% CAGR)
- Cumulative inflows: $200B+
- DE/share: $6.00+ (from ~$4.70)

**Growth Drivers:**
1. Global Credit expansion: The insurance solutions relationship with Fortitude ($80.4B), direct lending, and CLOs provide the highest-visibility growth. Credit FRE margin is lower (0.36% fee rate vs. GPE's 1.17%), requiring significant AUM scale to drive FRE dollars.
2. Private wealth distribution: $18B across Global Wealth products with 45,000+ investors. Near-doubling of evergreen product AUM in 2025 ($7B+ inflows). MAI Capital acquisition advances this channel.
3. AlpInvest secondaries: Benefiting from LP liquidity demand in a constrained exit environment.
4. Pending fee-earning AUM: $16.8B not yet activated provides near-term fee conversion upside.

**Growth Headwinds:**
1. GPE fund size compression: CP VIII at $14.8B is materially smaller than CP VII's $18.5B. Fee step-downs compound this.
2. AlpInvest catch-up fees ($55.7M) will not recur in 2026, creating a mechanical headwind.
3. PE exit environment remains historically weak — only 410 full divestitures globally in Q4 2025, constraining carry realization.
4. CEO Schwartz himself noted "credit uncertainty could persist for a while" in April 2026.

**Estimate Revision Trend:** Negative and accelerating. Current quarter EPS revised down 16.1% over 30 days ($1.09 → $0.92). Current year revised down 9.0% over 90 days ($4.69 → $4.27). The decline reflects both macro headwinds (credit stress, PE exit delays) and Carlyle-specific execution questions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.80, Carlyle trades at:
- 23.3x trailing P/E (on $2.18 GAAP EPS, which is depressed by unrealized performance allocation volatility)
- ~14x 2025 FRE ($1.24B on ~$17.4B market cap)
- ~10x 2025 Distributable Earnings ($1.69B)
- ~12x FY2026E consensus EPS of $4.27
- ~9.5x FY2027E consensus EPS of $5.32

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E of 15.5x (SEIC, AMG, JHG) applies to a different category — traditional asset managers with lower growth profiles and no performance fee upside. Carlyle's more relevant comparables are Blackstone (BX, ~30x+), KKR (~25x), and Apollo (APO, ~20x). Carlyle's discount to these peers reflects: (a) smaller absolute scale, (b) declining GPE franchise perception, (c) higher performance revenue volatility, and (d) less proven wealth distribution platform.

If Carlyle achieves its 2028 targets ($6.00 DE/share), the stock at $50.80 trades at 8.5x a number three years out. At the peer-implied 12-15x DE multiple, that would imply $72-90/share — material upside if targets are met. The market's willingness to pay for these targets is limited given four consecutive earnings misses and declining estimates.

The analyst consensus mean target of $61.75 (median $63.50) implies 22-25% upside, but the low target of $48.00 (essentially current price) and the presence of one strong-sell rating indicate meaningful disagreement.

## Competitive Landscape

Carlyle sits in the second tier of global alternative managers, materially smaller than Blackstone ($1.1T+ AUM), Apollo ($700B+), and KKR ($600B+), but among the largest pure-play alternatives firms globally at $477B.

**Competitive Advantages:**
- Global geographic footprint with local investment teams (particularly strong in Asia — CJP IV delivered 38% gross IRR)
- Deep investor cross-selling (93% in 2+ products, 76% in 5+)
- Fortitude insurance relationship provides captive capital source analogous to Apollo's Athene (though advisory rather than owned)
- AlpInvest secondary platform is among the world's largest

**Competitive Vulnerabilities:**
- PE fund sizes declining while peers raise record funds
- Wealth distribution materially behind BX and APO
- Insurance relationship concentrated in single counterparty vs. Apollo's ownership model
- CEP V underperformance (-IRR, 1.1x MOIC) damages European fundraising credibility
- Scale disadvantage in credit relative to Apollo and Ares

The competitive trajectory is mixed. Credit and solutions growth is gaining share, but the core GPE franchise — historically the firm's identity — is losing competitive ground. The three co-president structure announced in April 2026 may improve execution but also introduces organizational complexity.

## Risk Assessment

**Idiosyncratic Risks:**

1. **Credit Market Stress Exposure (HIGH):** With 44% of AUM in credit and credit being the primary growth engine, Carlyle is directly exposed to building private credit stress across the industry (rising non-accruals). CEO Schwartz's own cautionary comments on credit uncertainty reinforce this concern.

2. **Execution Risk on 2028 Targets (MODERATE-HIGH):** FRE must grow from $1.24B to $1.9B+ (~53% in 3 years) against declining GPE fees, catch-up fee cliffs, and uncertain credit market fundraising. Four consecutive earnings misses suggest forecasting credibility issues.

3. **CEP V Reputational Risk (MODERATE):** A negative-IRR €6.4B fund is a visible failure that constrains European fundraising and earns zero carry.

4. **Fortitude Concentration (MODERATE):** $80.4B advisory AUM from a single insurance relationship. Disruption of this relationship would materially impair credit segment economics.

5. **PE Exit Environment (MODERATE):** Carry realization depends on exits. Despite $5.1T in M&A volume in 2025, PE exit counts remain at multi-year lows. Higher rates and tighter credit conditions could persist.

6. **Regulatory (AIFMD II, April 2026):** European fund marketing and delegation requirements may increase costs and limit flexibility.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing severe near-term stress. ATM IV of 70.1% on the 1-week expiry is nearly double the 36.7% realized historical volatility — a 33.4pp premium that places IV at the 89th percentile. The term structure is in steep backwardation (70.1% near-term vs. 40.9% six-month), which typically signals the market is pricing an imminent binary event or acute uncertainty. The put/call OI ratio of 0.63 (call-heavy) and -5.3% skew suggest some speculative upside positioning, but the extreme IV levels indicate that market-makers and institutions are demanding significant premium for risk exposure. The implied 1-week move of ±8.8% on a $50 stock is substantial.

This options signal is consistent with broader credit sector stress: institutions are pricing uncertainty around private credit marks, exit timing, and the macro environment for alternative managers. The divergence between the relatively orderly spot market and the extreme options pricing warrants caution.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($65-75):**
- FRE growth trajectory continues toward 2028 targets via credit and wealth channels
- PE exit environment normalizes, unlocking $2.86B in net accrued performance revenues (Medline alone represents $5B+ in remaining holdings)
- Multiple re-rates from ~12x forward earnings toward peer-implied 15-18x as execution builds confidence
- $2B buyback authorization provides floor support and EPS accretion
- Private wealth channel scales to $50B+ within 3 years

**Bear Case ($38-42):**
- Credit market stress impairs the fastest-growing segment; CLO spreads widen, direct lending defaults rise
- 2028 targets are missed as GPE fee decline accelerates and credit growth stalls
- PE exits remain depressed through 2026-2027, freezing carry realization
- Market re-rates alternative managers lower on credit stress contagion fears
- Stock returns to its 52-week low (~$39.50) on 8-9x distributable earnings

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Positive earnings surprise (breaking the 4-quarter miss streak) combined with estimate revisions stabilizing or inflecting positive
2. Stock declines to $42-44 range (approximately 9-10x trailing DE) providing clear margin of safety to 2028 targets
3. Evidence of credit segment resilience in next 2 quarters (fee-earning AUM growth sustained >10% annualized despite industry stress)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Current quarter EPS misses consensus by >10% AND management reduces or withdraws 2028 targets
2. Fortitude relationship restructuring or material AUM withdrawal (>$10B)
3. Credit segment fee-earning AUM declines sequentially for 2+ quarters

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July/early August 2026). The combination of credit segment performance, PE exit activity, and estimate revision trajectory at that point will determine whether the 2028 targets remain credible.

## Conclusion

Carlyle Group presents a classic "cheap for a reason" value proposition. The stock trades at meaningful discounts to larger alternative manager peers and to its own 2028 target-implied valuation. However, four consecutive earnings misses, declining estimates across all time horizons, a stressed options market, and direct exposure to building private credit turbulence make this a position where patience is required before capital commitment.

The FRE growth story under Schwartz is directionally sound — credit, wealth, and solutions are gaining share — but the execution track record hasn't yet earned the benefit of the doubt. At $50.80, the market is pricing approximately 12x forward earnings, which assumes modest but not exceptional growth. A sustained inflection in estimate revisions, or a pullback to the low-$40s range that provides genuine margin of safety, would shift the risk/reward toward actionable territory. Until then, this is a monitoring position with defined upgrade criteria.
