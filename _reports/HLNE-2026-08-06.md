## Executive Summary

Hamilton Lane reported its fiscal first quarter (June 2026) on August 4, and the result settled the central question from prior reviews: the recurring-earnings franchise is compounding faster than the reported revenue line suggested. Fee-related earnings rose 49% year over year to $124.5M at a 53% margin, management and advisory fees rose 21% to $161.4M, and non-GAAP EPS of $1.94 exceeded the roughly $1.59 non-GAAP consensus by 22%. The board raised the quarterly dividend to $0.60 and set an FY2027 target of $2.40, an 11% increase. This was the fourth consecutive beat, following surprises of +37.0%, +14.5% and +2.2% in the prior three quarters.

The shares moved from $83.83 on July 24 to $99.15, and that is the reason for the rating change. Prior BUY calls rested on specific arithmetic: at $83 the base case of roughly $120 offered better than 40% upside against a bear case near the low $60s. At $99.15 the same fair-value work leaves +21% to base and -27% to bear. The multiple has expanded: FY2027 consensus rose 4.3% over 90 days while the price rose 18% in two weeks. FY2028 consensus, the piece of disconfirming evidence I weight most, has been revised *down* 1.0% over 90 days and 0.5% over 30 days, meaning the sell side is treating the Q1 upside as non-recurring rather than as a new base. That view is defensible: fee-related performance revenue of $72.5M in one quarter compares with $102.5M for all of FY2026, and management stated explicitly that the quarter is "not necessarily indicative" of the full year.

The fundamental case has not weakened; if anything it strengthened. What changed is the price and the risk composition. Essentially all of the growth is now one product family: evergreen funds contributed $6.7B of the $8.5B year-over-year increase in specialized-fund fee-earning AUM, and evergreen incentive fees of $86.75M were 76% of total incentive fees and 31.5% of total revenue in the quarter. Customized separate account fees were flat ($34.69M vs $34.58M) and advisory revenue fell 17%. That mix shift concentrates rather than diversifies, and it imports mechanics the pre-evergreen Hamilton Lane did not carry: approximately 40% of annualized management fees now sit on NAV-based bases, performance fees carry high-water marks, and investors hold periodic redemption rights that already produced outflows from two evergreen funds this quarter. Owning the fastest-growing evergreen franchise in the sector at 13.5x out-year earnings is reasonable; adding to it after an 18% pop, with near-term implied volatility at 59.2% against 41.7% realized and a 4.24 put/call volume ratio, is not.

## Company Overview

Hamilton Lane Incorporated is a holding company whose only material asset is a 77.0% economic interest in Hamilton Lane Advisors, L.L.C., an SEC-registered adviser reported as a single segment. The business allocates client capital into private markets through four channels:

- **Customized Separate Accounts** — $94.5B AUM, fees on committed capital, net invested capital or NAV, with contractual step-downs over a mandate's life.
- **Specialized Funds** — $51.9B AUM across secondaries, co-investment/direct equity, infrastructure, credit, venture, impact and four evergreen semi-liquid funds.
- **Advisory** — $914.1B of assets under advisement, monetized through annual fixed fees.
- **Reporting, monitoring, data and analytics**, including the Cobalt LP subscription product.

Total footprint at June 30, 2026 was $1.06 trillion ($146.3B discretionary AUM plus $914.1B AUA) across roughly 800 professionals. The trillion-dollar headline warrants discounting: AUA grew $68.8B (+8%) year over year while advisory revenue *fell* 17% to $4.56M, and the company itself states that AUA growth from existing accounts "does not have a material impact on our revenues." Management values the advisory book for the data it generates and the influence it confers with fund managers, not the fees.

The claimed moat is informational and relational rather than capital-based. Hamilton Lane does not originate deals as a principal; it selects among third-party general partners, buys secondaries and co-invests. That distinction matters for the competitive analysis below.

The composition is the analytically important part. Of the $71.7M increase in incentive fees, evergreen funds contributed $54.2M ($86.75M vs $32.59M), direct equity $11.9M and secondaries $4.7M. Of the $27.7M management-fee increase, $19.6M came from evergreen and $3.8M from the newest direct-equity fund, which also produced $2.1M of retroactive catch-up fees. Evergreen NAV did not triple, so the incentive-fee increase reflects strong appreciation in the lagged measurement window plus new share classes reaching first crystallization — mechanics that do not repeat quarterly.

Compensation discipline held precisely: fee-related compensation was 31.0% of fee-related revenue in both periods. That identity is useful, because it means incremental performance revenue does not drop through at 100% margin. Incremental FRE margin on incremental fee-related revenue was 56% ($40.7M / $72.4M). FRE margin expanded only two points because fee-related G&A rose 32%, including $5.3M more in consulting and professional fees.

### Cash conversion — read with care

Operating cash flow was $77.2M against $128.9M a year earlier, a $51.8M decline while net income rose $47.8M. Two identified drivers: fees receivable rose $76.2M ($151.8M to $228.0M) as incentive fees were accrued but uncollected, and part of the FY2026 bonus was paid in May 2026, shifting timing. Both are timing effects, not accrual quality failures, but the quarter cannot be annualized. Consolidated Fund activity further distorts the GAAP statement — $148.1M of fund investment purchases in investing against $140.5M of third-party contributions in financing. Capital intensity of the operating business is negligible: $1.8M capex, $2.5M depreciation.

### Balance sheet

Corporate cash of $337.0M against $276.25M of debt principal gives net cash of approximately $60.8M. Debt comprises an $81.25M term loan at 5.50% (2029), $95.0M at 3.50% fixed (2030) and $100.0M of senior notes at 5.28% (2029), with $148.8M of undrawn availability under a $325M aggregate JPMorgan cap. Interest expense of $3.5M against $124.5M of FRE is roughly 35x coverage. The senior-note covenant requiring management fees of at least the greater of $185M or 80% of the prior test level is covered 3.2x by FY2026's $584.2M — not a live constraint.

Three obligations sit between the operating business and common equity and deserve explicit treatment. The tax receivable agreement payable of $233.5M represents 85% of realized tax benefits owed to pre-IPO members and can accelerate on a change of control. Unfunded commitments total $290.3M to the company's own funds plus $198.7M to non-consolidated VIEs. And on May 8, 2026 a Consolidated Fund entered a $250M JPMorgan bridge facility unconditionally guaranteed by HLA, drawing $145M on June 29; because that fund reports on a three-month lag, neither the borrowing nor the acquired assets appear on the June 30 balance sheet. The April 29 credit amendments raised permitted warehouse financing to a $500M aggregate cap and lifted the unsecured-guarantee cap from $25M to $70M. Economically this is a one-business-day bridge against LP capital calls at an entity already consolidated, and management deems performance remote. The issue is that the balance sheet a reader sees shows neither the liability nor the offsetting assets, and management states it may guarantee similar fund debt in future. If guaranties migrate to *non-consolidated* funds, that becomes a different exposure.

An Altman Z-score near 1.5 appears in screening output. I would not act on it: the ratio's inputs assume a manufacturer, and Hamilton Lane's total assets are inflated by roughly $710M of legally ring-fenced Consolidated Fund assets with no recourse to the company. Net cash, 3.2x covenant headroom and 35x interest coverage are the economically relevant facts. The low reading does correctly flag that the consolidated GAAP balance sheet is not a clean picture of the operating company.

Financial health scores 7.0, down modestly from 7.5. The reduction reflects the thinning of net cash relative to layered claims (TRA, unfunded commitments, a $250M guaranty comparable in size to all corporate debt) and the presentational opacity created by the three-month lag, offset by the dividend raise, the buyback capacity and genuinely trivial capital intensity.

## Growth Analysis

The four-year record on the recurring line is strong. Fee-related earnings ran $144.25M (FY2022), $168.87M, $206.72M, $276.47M and $344.51M (FY2026) — a 24.3% compound rate. FY2026 total revenue growth of 6.5% understated the franchise because management fees grew 13.7% and FRE 24.6% while realization-dependent incentive fees fell.

The forward picture is more concentrated. Fee-earning AUM reached $81.5B at March 2026, up 13.2%, but total AUM grew only 4% versus fee-earning AUM up 12% — the fee base is growing faster than the asset base because evergreen NAV-based fees carry higher headline rates than institutional separate accounts that step down over time. The management-fee rate on average fee-earning AUM has drifted within a 0.59%–0.69% range, with the most recent reading at the top.

Channel by channel: separate-account fee-earning AUM rose 2% to $41.1B with fees flat and contributions down 30% year over year; advisory revenue fell 17% as agreements reached term; evergreen delivered $6.7B of the $8.5B specialized-fund fee-earning AUM increase. The June 26 expansion of the iCapital distribution arrangement — listing additional evergreen strategies across private equity, secondaries, infrastructure, venture and credit for advisors, private banks and family offices internationally — is the mechanism for the next leg. Policy movement toward permitting alternatives in defined-contribution plans is the larger addressable opening.

Consensus embeds normalization: FY2027 at $6.26 implies only 6% growth over FY2026's $5.90, with FY2028 at $7.36 implying 17.6%. Near-term revisions are positive (current quarter +9.2% over 30 days, +15.3% over 90 days; current year +2.6%/+4.3%) but the out-year is being trimmed. I read that as the sell side declining to extrapolate Q1's performance-fee cadence, which is the right instinct.

My underwriting base holds Q1 fee-related management and advisory fees flat at $163.2M per quarter, replaces the $72.5M of quarterly performance revenue with FY2026's $25.6M quarterly average, and flexes compensation down by 35% of the revenue reduction: fee-related revenue $188.8M, compensation $56.6M, G&A $38.2M, giving FRE near $94M per quarter or roughly $376M annualized versus FY2026's $344.5M. At zero compensation flex the figure is $310M. The Q1 run-rate of $498M is an upside sensitivity, not a forecast.

Growth prospects score 6.8. The recurring compounding is real and the distribution runway is genuine, but the growth is single-channel, the institutional core is flat, and the out-year estimate trend does not corroborate acceleration.

## Competitive Landscape

Hamilton Lane competes on two fronts with different dynamics. In institutional allocation and consulting it faces StepStone, GCM Grosvenor, Partners Group and the private-markets arms of Mercer, Aksia and Albourne; its secondaries funds compete for deal flow with Ardian, Lexington, Coller and Blackstone Strategic Partners. In the evergreen wealth channel it competes with Blackstone, Apollo, Ares, Blue Owl and Partners Group for the same advisor shelf space, all with larger balance sheets and larger distribution budgets. The iCapital expansion is a direct response to that competition for shelf space.

Two things visible in the numbers rather than the narrative:

**Fee-rate resilience holds so far.** The management-fee rate on average fee-earning AUM has drifted upward within its historical range, which is consistent with evergreen NAV-based fees carrying higher headline rates than stepping-down institutional mandates. That is favorable mix, not pricing power in the legacy book.

**Investment performance is mixed by vintage, and that is where the competitive threat sits.** Secondary Fund VI (2022, $5.6B) posts a 24.0% net IRR and a +1,256bps spread over the S&P 500 public-market equivalent. Co-Investment Fund IV (2018) posts 20.8% net. But Equity Opportunities Fund V (2021, $2.07B) shows a 7.9% net IRR and a **negative 479bps spread versus the S&P 500 PME**, and Infrastructure Opportunities Fund I is slightly negative against its infrastructure PME. The Strategic Opportunities credit series clusters at 6–12% net. The 2021 vintage is an industry-wide problem cohort, but a manager-of-managers whose product is selection skill is more exposed to PME underperformance than a direct sponsor, because the comparison to a cheap index is the entire value proposition. Allocated carry at Equity Opportunities V fell 46% sequentially, from $67.5M to $36.6M — larger than the $14.9M of recognized direct-equity incentive fees can explain, pointing to genuine markdowns in that cohort. Co-Investment Funds IV and III fell 12% and 13%.

Competitive position scores 6.2. The trajectory is improving in evergreen and deteriorating in the legacy channels, with a live question about whether 2021-vintage relative performance impairs future institutional fundraising.

## Risk Assessment

**Evergreen redemption and high-water-mark mechanics are now the dominant earnings risk.** Semi-liquid fund investors hold periodic repurchase rights, and the quarter already shows the effect: specialized-fund fee-earning AUM distributions of $1.055B "due primarily to redemptions from two evergreen funds and returns of capital." Separately, performance fees on evergreen vehicles carry high-water marks, so NAV declines "may delay or reduce future incentive fees by increasing the amount of subsequent NAV appreciation required." A drawdown year would compress management fees, defer performance fees and trigger redemptions at once. The filings disclose gross evergreen contributions ($1.2B) but not evergreen redemptions separately, so net evergreen flow is unmeasurable from public documents — the single most important undisclosed metric in this story.

**NAV linkage has introduced market beta the historical fee-base description did not carry.** The 10-Q discloses that a 10% decline in the NAV-based fee base would reduce management fee revenue by roughly $25.7M over twelve months, implying about $257M of annualized NAV-linked fees — near 40% of Q1-annualized management and advisory fees. Roughly 60% remains on commitment or net-invested-capital bases, so the insulation is partial rather than gone.

**Valuation subjectivity plus a three-month lag.** Fund investments and Consolidated Fund results are reported on a 90-day lag because underlying managers need time to produce audited statements. Reported carry balances, equity in income and NAV-based fees therefore reflect third-party, largely unobservable marks that are one quarter stale.

**Institutional contract terminability.** Separate-account and advisory revenue is not long-term contracted. In a single quarter, $0.4B of separate-account fee-earning AUM rolled off at end of term and $0.2B shifted from a committed-capital to a net-invested-capital fee base.

**Single-bank concentration.** Every corporate facility — term loan, revolver, both multi-draw facilities and the new fund bridge — is with JPMorgan, cross-collateralized and cross-defaulted.

**Structural.** HLI has no operations and depends on HLA distributions for dividends, taxes and TRA payments. Class B super-voting shares control approximately 73% of votes on a 10-votes-per-share basis, and the company relies on Nasdaq controlled-company exemptions. Approximately 12.3M Class B/C units remain exchangeable. Excluded from the diluted count are 1,383,362 performance-award shares whose market conditions are unmet and 400,000 Guardian warrant shares — 3.30% of the current diluted base, 3.20% post-issuance ownership dilution if all vest.

**Cost of asset gathering, disclosed.** A $9.55M customer-incentive asset was established for additional fund shares payable to early subscribers of a new evergreen fund over three annual installments, recognized as a reduction of management fee revenue ($1.08M in the quarter). A warrant for up to 400,000 Class A shares was issued to Guardian Life as a customer incentive. Amounts are small against $161.4M of quarterly management fees, and launch incentives are standard in semi-liquid markets, but reported fee growth is modestly overstated relative to fully loaded economics, and an asset-gathering counterparty holds a claim on the equity. An externally reported "$500M annual Guardian partnership" figure is not corroborated in the filings and should not be relied on.

Risk factors score 5.4. The concentration, NAV beta, lag and governance items are material and only partly priced; offsetting them, the stock already de-rated 34.5% in FY2026 while its proxy peer index fell 3.2%, so a company-specific discount exists.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 59.2% against 41.7% one-year realized — rich by 17.5 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (59.2% at 14 days versus 49.4% at 105 days). The nearest expiry prices a ±9.6% move despite earnings having already been reported on August 4, so the near-term premium is protection demand after an 18% rally rather than an earnings event. The put/call volume ratio is 4.24 (11.78 at the one-month tenor) while open-interest put/call fell from 1.42 to 0.82 over the same window that price rose 17.9%. The most consistent reading is that longer-dated protection was monetized into the rally while fresh short-dated puts were bought — profit-taking hedges rather than a directional bearish position, since the six-month skew is -2.3% and the one-month skew is a balanced 0.9%. Short interest of 12.3% of float with 4.5 days to cover means bearish institutional positioning is present but not crowded. This argues against paying up here and supports waiting for near-term volatility to decay.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $150 (+51%)

Evergreen performance revenue proves to be a recurring, periodically crystallizing stream rather than a one-quarter event, so FRE settles nearer the $450–500M annualized zone than my $376M normalized base. Fee-related earnings compound in the high teens off a 53% margin with negligible capital intensity, the iCapital-expanded distribution footprint plus defined-contribution access broadens the evergreen investor base, and the institutional core stabilizes as 2021-vintage marks work through. At 20x FY2028 consensus $7.36 that is $147, plus partial realization of the $15.45 per share of after-tax unrecognized carry. The dividend compounds from a 41% payout. Insider conviction supports this reading: roughly $13.5M of open-market purchases by six insiders — both co-CEOs, both executive co-chairmen, the COO and a director — between February and June at $76–$107 with no open-market sales, alongside $70M of buybacks at $100.43 and $89.51 averages.

### Bear Case — $72 (-27%)

Private-market NAVs decline. Approximately $257M of NAV-linked management fees compress, high-water marks defer performance fees for multiple quarters, and evergreen redemptions accelerate from the two funds already showing outflows — the three-way correlated exposure that did not exist in the pre-evergreen model. Normalized FRE reverts toward $310M with no compensation flex, non-GAAP EPS settles near $6.00, and a 12x multiple on a manager whose growth channel is in redemption gives $72, roughly the June 24 low of $71.88. The institutional core provides no offset: separate-account fees are flat, contributions down 30%, advisory down 17%. 2021-vintage PME underperformance impairs re-up rates. The carry option deflates as marks reverse.

### What tips the balance

The bull case requires performance-revenue cadence that the filings do not establish, and management's earnings-call commentary — which I could not obtain — would be needed to assess it. The bear case requires a private-market drawdown that is not currently visible. At $83 the bull-weighted asymmetry justified a BUY. At $99.15, with +21% to base and -27% to bear, the asymmetry is roughly neutral, and the call is to hold what you own.

**Bull case $150 (+51%):** 20.4x FY2028 $7.36 with partial carry realization.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. A pullback to **$85–88** with fundamentals intact — that restores roughly 40% upside to base against a bear case near the June low, the asymmetry that justified the prior BUY.
2. **Q2 FY2027 fee-related performance revenue of $50M or more** alongside disclosure of positive *net* evergreen flows, which would establish cadence and shift the underwriting base from $376M toward the $450M+ zone.
3. **FY2028 consensus revised above $7.75** (currently $7.36 and falling), confirming the sell side has repriced the recurring base rather than treating Q1 as an outlier.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A gate, repurchase limit or suspension on any Hamilton Lane evergreen fund**, or disclosed net evergreen outflows in a quarter. This is the single event that converts the growth engine into the impairment.
2. **Management fees declining sequentially for two consecutive quarters**, indicating NAV-based fee compression is running ahead of gross inflows.
3. **HLA guaranties extended to non-consolidated funds**, or use of the $500M warehouse capacity in size, which would move contingent balance-sheet support from a consolidated timing bridge to genuine third-party exposure.
4. Allocated carried interest falling more than 15% sequentially on marks rather than realizations, which would signal broad vintage impairment beyond the 2021 cohort.

**Position management for existing holders:** retain. The $2.40 annualized dividend at a 41% payout provides 2.4% carry while waiting, the buyback has $30M remaining under the $100M authorization increased on May 21, and the insider purchase cluster at $76–$107 sets a reasonable floor reference. Do not add at $99.

## Conclusion

Three consecutive BUY calls have been rewarded — the stock is 38% above its June 24 low of $71.88 and the August 4 print delivered the recurring-earnings improvement the thesis predicted, with fee-related earnings up 49% at a 53% margin, a 22% non-GAAP EPS beat and an 11% dividend increase. The improving scores (6.3 to 7.1 across five reviews) reflected a business getting better and a price getting cheaper simultaneously. That combination has now broken: the business is still getting better, but the price has risen 18% in nine sessions while the FY2028 estimate has been trimmed 1.0% over 90 days.

At 15.8x FY2027 and 13.5x FY2028 consensus, with net cash, 24% four-year FRE compounding, 35x interest coverage and approximately $15 per share of unrecognized net carried interest, Hamilton Lane is not expensive. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is composition and entry. The entire growth contribution now comes from one product family whose fees are 40% NAV-linked, whose performance revenue is high-water-marked, and whose investors hold redemption rights they have already begun exercising. The quarter that drove the rally derived 31.5% of revenue from evergreen incentive fees against a full prior-year performance-revenue figure of $102.5M, and management explicitly cautioned against extrapolating it. Underwriting the normalized base of roughly $376M of FRE rather than the $498M run-rate produces a $120 base case: +21% upside against -27% to a bear case that the June low already proved reachable.

Hold existing positions for the dividend, the buyback and the path to $120. Add on a retracement to $85–88, or on evidence from the October print that evergreen performance revenue and net flows are durable rather than episodic.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: several unsupported claims were removed (specific peer-fund gating figures for Cliffwater/Partners Group/Blackstone; a "four peer retail vehicles gated or capped" assertion), which weakens the immediacy of the evergreen-redemption backdrop cited in the Executive Summary, Risk Assessment, and Conclusion as a reason to hold rather than add.
