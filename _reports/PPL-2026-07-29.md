**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PPL Corporation is a pure-play, U.S.-only regulated electric and gas utility holding company serving roughly 3.6 million customers across Kentucky/Virginia, Pennsylvania, and Rhode Island. Since exiting its UK business in 2021, PPL has become a lower-risk rate-base-growth story anchored by a $23B capital plan through 2029 and a reaffirmed 6-8% annual EPS growth target. The differentiating feature is location: PPL's Pennsylvania (PJM) and vertically-integrated Kentucky footprints sit in two of the strongest U.S. data-center load-growth corridors, and Kentucky's ability to own new generation into rate base gives PPL a larger earnings opportunity than wires-only peers.

The fundamentals are steady and the growth catalyst is real, but the equity thesis is a mid-single-digit compounder. Q1 2026 delivered ongoing EPS of $0.63 (vs $0.60), revenue up 10.8% YoY, and a 33% jump in capex; the growth plan is intact. The offsetting realities are that consensus estimates are flat (current-year $1.95, next-year $2.12, essentially unchanged over 90 days), the stock trades at ~22x earnings roughly in line with the peer median, and the capital program is being funded with meaningful dilutive equity issuance at ~$36 (a $1.15B Corporate Units offering at a 7% distribution rate plus a $2B ATM with forwards at ~$35.90). Layered on top are unresolved regulatory friction in Kentucky (LG&E/KU withdrew from the rate stipulation and sought rehearing), a Rhode Island segment dragged by a FERC ROE cut, and affordability/political pressure in Pennsylvania.

We rate PPL a HOLD (growth_hold). The 6-8% EPS CAGR plus a covered 3.1% dividend supports a high-single-digit total-return profile with limited downside, but the per-share benefit of the load-growth story is partly absorbed by dilution, the multiple offers no margin of safety, and near-term estimate revisions provide no upward momentum. This continues a three-report HOLD sequence; the current data confirms rather than reverses the modest downward drift in our composite score.

## Company Overview

PPL operates four regulated utilities across three reporting segments:

- **Kentucky Regulated (LG&E and KU):** The only vertically integrated segment, owning regulated generation (coal, gas, solar, storage), transmission, and distribution, plus gas distribution in Kentucky and Virginia (KU serves Virginia as Old Dominion Power). This is the largest segment, at Q1 2026 net income of $270M out of $452M total, and roughly $19.2B of $46.3B total assets.
- **Pennsylvania Regulated (PPL Electric Utilities):** Wires-only transmission and distribution in eastern/central PA, and Provider of Last Resort. Q1 2026 net income $184M; ~$17.2B assets.
- **Rhode Island Regulated (Rhode Island Energy / Narragansett Electric, acquired May 2022):** Electric T&D and gas distribution. Q1 2026 net income $36M; ~$7.6B assets.

The strategic model rests on regulated monopoly franchises with constructive cost-recovery mechanisms: distribution system improvement charges, fuel adjustment clauses, formula transmission rates, and decoupling in Rhode Island. The defining growth opportunity is PPL's exposure to two of the strongest U.S. load-growth corridors. In Kentucky, the vertically integrated structure allows PPL to build and own new generation into rate base rather than merely wiring third-party power, which is a structurally larger earnings lever than the wires-only model of many peers.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (10-Q, filed 5/8/2026):**
- Operating revenue $2,774M, up 10.8% YoY from $2,504M
- Net income $452M ($0.60 GAAP EPS), up from $414M ($0.56); ongoing EPS $0.63 vs $0.60
- Operating income $745M, up from $678M

The revenue growth was driven by new Kentucky base rates effective 1/1/2026 (+$70M retail), higher PLR volumes and prices in Pennsylvania (+$111M, largely pass-through), and fuel/energy recoveries. Kentucky net income rose $47M on the rate-case win, Pennsylvania was roughly flat, and Rhode Island fell $34M, dragged by a $37M pre-tax special item tied to the ISO-NE transmission ROE cut and system-integration costs.

**Multi-year trend:** Net income grew from $740M (2023) to $888M (2024) to $1,181M (2025). The company-selected ongoing EPS measure rose from $1.02 (2021) to $1.60 (2023) to $1.81 (2025). Management reaffirmed 2026 ongoing EPS guidance of $1.90-$1.98 (midpoint $1.94).

**Capital plan and financing:** Q1 2026 capex of $1,058M was up 33% YoY from $793M, concentrated in PA T&D, Rhode Island, and Kentucky generation (E.W. Brown battery storage, Mill Creek Unit 5). Construction-work-in-progress rose to $3.69B. Operating cash flow was $557M in Q1 against $1,058M of capex, so the growth plan is externally financed and free cash flow is structurally negative, which is the norm for a utility in a heavy rate-base build but a real constraint in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation:**
- Total debt ~$20.2B (long-term $19.0B + current $994M + short-term $220M); total equity $15.0B, so D/E of roughly 1.3x, typical for a capital-intensive regulated utility.
- Credit ratings stable since June 2023; no rating triggers that accelerate debt.
- Equity funding is active and dilutive: February 2026 issued $1.15B of Corporate Units (equity forward, mandatory settlement Feb 2029, 7.00% distribution rate), plus a $2B ATM program with ~27.4M shares under forward contracts at a ~$35.90 blended price. Combined maximum issuance approaches ~30.9M shares. Weighted diluted share count already rose to 751.8M in Q1 2026 from 741.4M a year earlier.
- Interest expense rose $34M YoY (+18%) on higher long-term debt, a direct cost of the externally financed model.
- The dividend was raised to $0.285/quarter ($1.14/year), a ~3.1% yield, with ~$202M quarterly payout comfortably covered by earnings.

The financial-health picture is stable but not strong. Cash flow generation is solid at the operating level, leverage is appropriate for the business, and the dividend is well-covered. The offsetting negatives, namely structurally negative FCF, rising interest expense, and a heavy external funding need at a ~7% cost of the Corporate Units, mean per-share value creation depends on rate-base returns clearing the cost of the incremental capital.

## Growth Analysis

PPL is exposed to the strongest secular tailwind in the utility sector: AI/data-center-driven load growth. Industry projections of ~$1.3 trillion in U.S. utility capex through 2030 provide a broad-based, multi-company demand signal that gives the growth thesis higher durability than a single-company story. Within our coverage, data-center load is corroborated across regulated-utility hard data and transmission rate base, which argues against a demand-deceleration hypothesis at the infrastructure layer.

For PPL specifically, the earnings drivers are:
- **Kentucky generation ownership.** The vertically integrated structure lets PPL build gas, solar, and storage into rate base. The February 2026 KPSC order granted a $233M revenue increase (9.775% base ROE), approved a Pilot Generation Recovery clause, and an "Extremely High Load Factor Tariff" for data centers requiring long-term contracts, minimum revenue payments, and collateral. This is the model regulators are pushing to protect residential ratepayers while enabling the buildout.
- **Pennsylvania distribution.** A $275M settlement (reached 3/5/2026, ALJ-recommended for approval) introduces a new large-load (data center) tariff class, with a two-year rate freeze thereafter.
- **Rhode Island.** A pending 18%+ electric / 36%+ gas rate request, with a ruling expected Q3 2026, the segment with the most affordability/political risk.

The reaffirmed 6-8% EPS CAGR is credible against this backdrop. The caveat on growth is estimate momentum: consensus current-year EPS ($1.95) and next-year ($2.12) have been essentially flat over both 30 and 90 days, with a modest -5% cut to the current-quarter estimate. There is no positive revision velocity to indicate the market is systematically underestimating faster growth. The load-growth story is real and multi-year, but it converts to per-share EPS growth only net of the ongoing dilution described above, which is why the target is mid-single-digit rather than the double-digit EPS growth some pure-play data-center utilities are guiding.

## Competitive Landscape

PPL's competitive position is stable-to-improving, anchored by regulated monopoly franchises with constructive recovery mechanisms and favorable geographic exposure. The Kentucky vertically integrated model is a genuine advantage over wires-only peers because it captures the generation buildout into rate base. Regulated utilities do not compete on price; the relevant contest is regulatory, over who pays for the data-center buildout and on what terms.

PPL is positioned well on this frontier. Its dedicated large-load tariffs with minimum-revenue commitments and collateral (Kentucky's Extremely High Load Factor Tariff, PA's new large-load class) are the model regulators favor to shield residential ratepayers, and FERC's 2026 data-center order plus the White House Ratepayer Protection Pledge (which PPL signed in July 2026) are reshaping the playbook in PPL's favor. The regulated-utility hard-data corroboration of data-center load growth broadly supports the trajectory.

The offsetting competitive-position concerns are friction points, not structural erosion: the Kentucky stipulation withdrawal and rehearing request create recovery-timing uncertainty for a chunk of the generation plan (the KPSC excluded Mill Creek Unit 6 and Brown Unit 12 from the pilot recovery clause and did not approve the requested Sharing Mechanism), and Rhode Island lags on the FERC ROE cut. These are execution wrinkles rather than moat compression. The trajectory has drifted down modestly in our prior reports (7.0 → 6.5) as the Kentucky friction and RI drag surfaced, and the current data holds that lower level rather than reversing it.

## Risk Assessment

The 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors from the 2025 10-K. The most PPL-specific risks:

1. **Data-center demand is double-edged.** The 10-Q explicitly flags capital-access, revenue-recovery, and customer-affordability risks tied to projected rapid load growth. If hyperscaler projects fail to materialize or power intensity declines, planned generation/transmission investment could be stranded or under-recovered.
2. **Affordability/political backlash in Pennsylvania.** The analyst-identified soft spot. Signing the White House Ratepayer Protection Pledge is protective but signals real political pressure over residential bill increases.
3. **Rate-case execution across jurisdictions.** The earnings model depends on regulatory outcomes: the pending PA settlement, the Kentucky rehearing friction, the RI ruling due Q3 2026, and Virginia. The Kentucky stipulation withdrawal is an unusual point of friction.
4. **Kentucky coal/environmental exposure.** LG&E/KU coal generation is subject to CCR, ELG, MATS, and GHG rules. The current EPA deregulatory posture is a near-term tailwind but adds planning whiplash and litigation risk; ARO liabilities and Mill Creek Unit 2 retirement decisions are live.
5. **Wildfire liability** — newly emphasized, with $196M aggregate wildfire coverage disclosed.
6. **Financing/interest-rate risk.** Heavy external funding in a higher-for-longer environment. A higher-for-longer rate path directly raises the cost of the negative-FCF financing model, and the equity issuance at ~$36 dilutes per-share growth.

On balance, these risks are largely known and partly priced, but the Kentucky rehearing and the RI rate ruling are near-term binary items where outcomes could move the stock modestly in either direction. There is no single catastrophic tail, and no meaningful asymmetric recovery upside embedded in a stock at fair value.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are modestly cautious. ATM IV of 21.6% sits rich to 1-year historical vol of 17.6% (a 4.0% spread), with an elevated 6.5% put/call skew at the front month and a term structure in backwardation (near-term 21.6% > long-term 18.3%), so the market is pricing some imminent stress, plausibly around the pending regulatory rulings and Q2 earnings. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.13 to 0.85 since the prior snapshot. The 6-month skew is negative (-3.3%), suggesting the near-term hedging demand is event-driven rather than structural. For a utility, this reads as event-hedging around the Q3 RI ruling and Kentucky rehearing rather than a directional bearish signal, but it does argue against chasing entry into the near-term event window.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$46-48)
The data-center load-growth story converts cleanly to approved, recoverable rate base across PA and Kentucky without triggering affordability backlash. The Kentucky rehearing resolves constructively, the RI rate request lands near ask, and PPL delivers the upper end of 6-8% EPS growth. At a market-median ~22x on ~$2.10-$2.15 forward EPS, the stock reaches ~$46-48, consistent with the high analyst target and the multi-year fair value. Total return then compounds at high-single-digits plus the 3.1% dividend.

### Bear Case (~$30-33, ~9-17% downside)
Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of the heavy external financing, the ~30.9M shares of coming dilution outrun rate-base returns, and estimate revisions turn negative. The Kentucky friction extends recovery timing, PA affordability politics constrain the large-load buildout, and RI continues to drag. The multiple de-rates toward the lower end of the peer range (~18x) on ~$1.94-$2.00, pulling the stock to ~$30-33.

### Base Case (~$40-42)
The most likely outcome: PPL delivers ~6% EPS growth net of dilution, regulatory outcomes land roughly as expected with some Kentucky/RI friction, and the multiple holds near the peer median. On ~$2.06-$2.10 forward EPS at ~19-20x, the stock reaches ~$40-42, consistent with consensus mean/median targets, delivering high-single-digit total return including the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)** — a durable mid-single-digit compounder with a covered 3.1% yield, but no asymmetric entry at ~22x and flat estimate revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 Rhode Island rate ruling, the Kentucky rehearing resolution, and the Q2 2026 earnings print (guidance reaffirmation or revision).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$30s that compresses the forward multiple below ~18x while the 6-8% EPS CAGR and $23B plan remain intact — a materially improved risk/reward on unchanged fundamentals.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year or next-year consensus rising >3%) confirming data-center load is converting to rate base ahead of plan.
- Constructive resolution of the Kentucky rehearing plus RI ruling near ask, de-risking the recovery-timing overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Negative estimate revisions or a cut to the 6-8% EPS growth target or 2026 guidance.
- A regulatory setback that strands or under-recovers a meaningful portion of the Kentucky generation plan, or a PA affordability-driven curtailment of the large-load buildout.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration, an unexpected equity raise materially above the disclosed ~30.9M shares, or a credit-rating downgrade.

## Conclusion

PPL is a stable, de-risked, U.S.-only regulated utility with a genuine above-average growth catalyst in data-center load across its PA/PJM and Kentucky footprints, supporting a reaffirmed 6-8% EPS CAGR and a $23B capex plan. The Kentucky vertically integrated model, owning generation into rate base, is a real competitive edge over wires-only peers, and PPL is well-positioned on the regulatory frontier of who pays for the AI buildout.

The equity thesis is a mid-single-digit total-return compounder rather than an asymmetric opportunity. At ~22x, roughly the peer median, with a below-median 3.1% yield, flat estimate revisions, structurally negative FCF, and ~30.9M shares of dilutive equity funding at ~$36, the per-share benefit of the load-growth story is partly absorbed and the multiple offers no margin of safety. Unresolved Kentucky rate-case friction and Rhode Island underperformance add near-term regulatory uncertainty, and near-term options are pricing event stress. We rate PPL a HOLD (growth_hold), consistent with the prior three reports; the current data confirms the modest downward drift in our composite rather than reversing it. We would turn constructive on a pullback into the low-$30s, positive estimate-revision velocity, or constructive resolution of the Kentucky and Rhode Island rate proceedings.
