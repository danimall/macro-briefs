## Executive Summary

TSMC reported its fourth consecutive record quarter in Q1 2026: revenue of $35.9B (+40.6% YoY in USD terms), net income growth of 58.3%, and gross margins of 66.2%. The company has raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to >30% in U.S. dollar terms and is spending $52-56B in capex this year alone. 2nm capacity through 2026 is reported to be fully allocated, CoWoS advanced packaging capacity is overflowing to external partners, and HPC (including AI-related products) now constitutes 61% of revenue.

The investment case centers on a dominant, supply-constrained market leader with quasi-monopoly economics at the leading edge, operating in a structural demand upcycle. TSMC holds approximately 70% foundry market share and is the dominant volume producer at leading-edge nodes, with the clearest leadership position at 3nm/2nm. Neither Samsung nor Intel poses a credible near-term threat to this position. The stock trades at approximately 35x trailing earnings with a forward P/E of ~26x FY2026 consensus — a reasonable multiple for a company delivering 58% profit growth with multi-year visibility into AI-driven demand.

The principal risk remains geopolitical: the vast majority of the world's most advanced chip production sits in Taiwan. Arizona's reported profitability (approximately $598M in Q1 2026 based on subsidiary disclosures) is encouraging but does not eliminate this risk. Secondary risks include the sustainability of AI capital spending and the massive capex cycle's execution demands.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, manufacturing chips designed by customers including Nvidia (widely reported as the largest customer), Apple (widely reported as the second-largest), AMD, Broadcom, and Qualcomm. The pure-play foundry model eliminates competitive conflicts with customers, enabling TSMC to consolidate the most advanced manufacturing capacity globally.

Revenue mix has shifted decisively toward high-performance computing: HPC represents 61% of Q1 2026 revenue (up from 57% in Q3 2025), encompassing AI accelerators, CPUs, GPUs, and networking chips. Advanced nodes (7nm and below) generate 74% of wafer revenue, with 3nm and below at 25%. The company is ramping 2nm production — which entered volume production in 2025 — with capacity reported to be fully allocated to customers through 2026.

TSMC trades on the NYSE as an ADR. Market capitalization is approximately $2.1 trillion based on recent trading levels.

**Profitability:**

Gross margin of 66.2% and operating margin of 58.1% in Q1 2026 are extraordinary for a manufacturing business. These margins reflect three factors: (1) near-100% utilization at advanced nodes, (2) pricing power enabled by sold-out capacity, and (3) the structural economics of leading-edge manufacturing where yields compound with scale.

**Earnings:**

Trailing EPS of approximately $10.43 (reflecting ADR economics). Consensus estimates: $15.61 for FY2026 and $19.52 for FY2027. Both figures have been revised upward — current year estimates are +9.6% vs. 90 days ago; next year +9.0%. The revision trend is uniformly positive across all forecast periods.

The 4-quarter earnings beat streak (ranging from 3.7% to 8.0% surprise) suggests consensus still lags actual performance, though beat magnitude is narrowing.

**Capital Expenditure:**

$52-56B capex guided for 2026, up ~37% YoY. TSMC is in the most aggressive investment cycle in semiconductor history. This spending funds: 2nm production ramp in Taiwan, Arizona expansion (4th fab + first US advanced packaging plant), Japan (JASM) expansion, and CoWoS packaging buildout. Management commentary suggests continued elevated investment may be required in subsequent years given the AI demand trajectory.

**Balance Sheet:**

Cash generation remains robust given 58% operating margins on $35.9B quarterly revenue. The company approved $20B capital injection into TSMC Arizona and $31.3B in broader capital appropriations. While the capex cycle absorbs significant cash flow, the sold-out demand environment provides high confidence in return on investment.

## Growth Analysis

TSMC's growth outlook is anchored by three structural drivers:

**1. AI Demand (Primary):** Management has indicated expectations for strong AI chip revenue growth through the end of the decade. Nvidia's Blackwell architecture, custom AI accelerators from hyperscalers, and the training-to-inference transition all require TSMC's most advanced nodes and packaging. Reports of CoWoS overflow to external partners suggest demand exceeds even TSMC's aggressive capacity expansion.

**2. Process Technology Leadership:** 2nm entered volume production in 2025 and is ramping aggressively in 2026. 2nm and A16 capacity is projected to grow at approximately 70% CAGR from 2026 to 2028. Each node transition drives ASP increases and locks in customers who cannot find equivalent manufacturing elsewhere.

**3. Geographic Expansion Proving Economically Viable:** Based on TSMC's Q1 2026 consolidated subsidiary disclosures, TSMC Arizona generated approximately NT$18.8B (~$598M) in profit in Q1 2026. Japan (JASM) also turned profitable in Q1 2026 (NT$951M). These results are encouraging for the overseas expansion model, though whether Arizona economics will hold at full scale across multiple fabs remains to be demonstrated.

Estimate revisions confirm growth momentum: current year EPS revised up 9.6% over 90 days, next year up 9.0%. These are meaningful positive revisions for a ~$2.1T market cap company, though they fall below the 30%+ threshold that would indicate a true inflection-stage buy signal. Growth here is accelerating from already-elevated levels rather than recovering from a trough.

## Valuation Assessment

At $404.52, TSM trades at approximately 26x FY2026 consensus EPS of $15.61 and 20.7x FY2027 EPS of $19.52.

**Relative to growth:** FY2026 revenue growth >30%, profit growth ~58% in Q1. A 26x forward multiple on this growth profile implies a PEG ratio of approximately 0.5-0.8x depending on how sustained the growth is. This is reasonable for a company with TSMC's quality characteristics.

**Relative to peers:** At approximately 35x trailing P/E, TSM trades at a premium to broad semiconductor peers but at a discount to NVDA. Given TSMC's higher margin stability (66% gross vs. NVDA's fluctuating), lower customer concentration risk (broader customer base), and essential infrastructure positioning, a premium to peers is warranted.

**Relative to history:** The stock has appreciated significantly over the past year. However, this appreciation is fundamentally justified — forward estimates have risen proportionally. The multiple has expanded modestly, but most of the move reflects earnings growth.

**Market is already pricing significant growth:** The 3% decline on record Q1 results and the narrowing of earnings beat magnitude (8.0% → 5.2% → 4.4%) suggest consensus is catching up to reality. Analyst consensus target of approximately $468 (mean $467.84, median $472.50) implies roughly 16% upside from the $404.52 level.

The valuation is not cheap in absolute terms, but it is reasonable for the quality and growth profile. I would not call this a deep value opportunity — the market broadly understands TSMC's competitive position. The mispricing, to the extent it exists, relates to the durability of AI demand and the partial de-risking from Arizona profitability.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's competitive position is among the strongest of any company in any industry globally.

**Market Share:** Approximately 70% of global foundry revenue. At advanced nodes (sub-5nm), the share is effectively 90%+.

**Yield Advantage:** >80% at advanced nodes vs. Intel's 50-60% targets and Samsung's persistent struggles. This yield gap compounds into massive cost advantages per functional die.

**Customer Lock-in:** Designing a chip for TSMC's process nodes requires 12-24 months of development. Switching costs are measured in billions of dollars and years of engineering time. The ecosystem effect (EDA tools optimized for TSMC, IP libraries designed for TSMC, engineering talent trained on TSMC processes) creates an increasingly wide competitive gap.

**Emerging Threats:**

1. Intel Foundry under Lip-Bu Tan represents the most credible medium-term challenger. Apple has reportedly reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to manufacture some chips, which validates Intel's direction but does not threaten TSMC's primary volumes. Intel's 18A node targets 2027 volume production. Yield parity with TSMC is unlikely before 2028-2029 at earliest.

2. Apple's reported second-sourcing effort is actually a bullish signal for TSMC in the near term — it suggests that TSMC is supply-constrained rather than demand-constrained. It creates legitimate longer-term risk if Intel achieves competitive yields, but this is a 3-5 year development.

3. Samsung Foundry continues losing share (approximately 7% market share) and does not represent a credible threat at leading-edge nodes.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical Concentration (Severity: High, Probability: Low-to-Moderate):**
Taiwan Strait tensions represent the existential risk. The vast majority of the world's most advanced chip production is in Taiwan. China has not renounced its claim. Uncertainty around approval of a $14B Taiwan arms package following the US-China summit in May 2026 reminded markets of the unresolved situation. A blockade scenario (per Stanford's Eyck Freymann) is more likely than invasion but still carries low near-term probability given the economic interdependencies involved. Arizona/Japan expansion provides partial mitigation but not elimination.

**AI Demand Sustainability (Severity: High, Probability: Moderate):**
61% revenue concentration in HPC (including AI-related products). Management's expectation for strong AI revenue growth through the end of the decade requires sustained hyperscaler capital spending at unprecedented levels. Any meaningful pause or reduction in AI capex would impact TSMC disproportionately. The DeepSeek 75% price cut on inference suggests commoditization pressure that could eventually moderate training/inference hardware demand growth — though the Jevons paradox may increase total compute demand as costs fall.

**Capex Execution (Severity: Moderate, Probability: Moderate):**
$52-56B this year with continued elevated investment likely required in subsequent years. CEO Wei's candid admission of nervousness is appropriate. Arizona faces water constraints, visa delays, labor shortages. Any sustained yield issues at new fabs or construction delays would impact capacity delivery into a sold-out environment.

**Tariff/Trade Policy (Severity: Moderate, Probability: Moderate):**
Taiwan negotiating for lower reciprocal tariff rate (15% vs. 20%). Semiconductor-specific tariffs possible. US policy may require additional domestic fab investment. Export controls limit China revenue.

**Current Macro Environment:**
Rising US interest rates and potential hikes create valuation headwinds for growth stocks broadly. However, TSMC's capital-intensive model generates real-asset value less vulnerable to multiple compression than pure-software businesses.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($500-550, +24% to +36%):**
AI demand sustains at high growth rates through 2027-2028. 2nm ramp executes flawlessly. Arizona profitability expands as second and third fabs come online. Pricing power continues with annual ASP increases. FY2027 EPS reaches $20+ with expansion toward 25x+ forward multiple. Geopolitical risk decreases as geographic diversification proceeds.

**Bear Case ($300-330, -26% to -18%):**
AI capex cycle moderates meaningfully in H2 2026 or 2027. Tariffs compress margins. Intel achieves competitive yields at 18A sooner than expected. Geopolitical escalation (blockade scenario or significant Taiwan Strait incident) triggers risk premium expansion. Multiple compresses to 17-18x forward.

**Base Case ($460-480, +14% to +19%):**
AI demand continues at slightly below guidance levels. TSMC delivers >30% revenue growth in 2026, moderating to 20-25% in 2027. Margins remain above 60% gross. Arizona/Japan continue profitable scaling. Geopolitical risk remains priced as tail risk without acute escalation. Stock re-rates modestly toward consensus target as execution continues.

## Conclusion

TSMC is the highest-quality semiconductor business globally, operating in a structural demand upcycle with dominant market position, demonstrated pricing power, and multi-year visibility into customer demand. The stock at $404 (~26x FY2026 forward EPS) prices significant growth but does not appear stretched relative to the 58% profit growth rate and >30% revenue growth guidance.

The score trajectory shows modest compression from 8.0 to 7.6 over recent weeks — I assess this reflects legitimate concerns about semiconductor correction timing and options market positioning rather than fundamental deterioration. Estimate revisions remain uniformly positive (+9.6% over 90 days), the Q1 beat continued the streak, and Arizona subsidiary profitability represents a positive development for the long-term thesis.

The primary risk — geopolitical concentration in Taiwan — is structural and unhedgeable through position sizing alone. It warrants a 2-3% portfolio allocation cap rather than avoidance. The recommendation is BUY with the understanding that this is a high-quality compounder facing a known tail risk, with 14-33% upside potential over 12-18 months against 23% downside in a bear scenario.
