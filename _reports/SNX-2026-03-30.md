## Executive Summary

TD SYNNEX is the world's largest IT distributor by revenue ($62.5B in FY2025), connecting ~2,500 OEM vendors and 150,000+ reseller customers across 100+ countries. The company is trading at $157, representing 15.3x trailing EPS of $9.95 — a discount to the peer median of 16.8x — despite posting 18.5% operating income growth and generating $1.39B in free cash flow in FY2025.

The most important near-term catalyst is the March 2026 segment reorganization that breaks out Hyve Solutions (the hyperscale/AI infrastructure design and manufacturing business) as a standalone reporting segment. This provides investors with the first clean look at the growth rate and margin profile of what is likely the highest-value piece of the business. Combined with a PC refresh cycle driven by Windows 10 end-of-life, underlying gross billings growth of approximately 12%, and a capital return program that has reduced shares outstanding by 11.3% over two years, the stock offers a concrete path to the analyst consensus target of $180.

Key risks include significant vendor concentration (Apple 12%, HP 10% of revenue), customer concentration in the hyperscale business (~11% from one unnamed customer), margin compression in strategic technologies, and macro sensitivity to enterprise/government capex cycles in a stagflationary environment. The low-margin business model (~7% gross, ~2.3% operating) amplifies any revenue or pricing disruption.

## Company Overview

TD SYNNEX was formed from the September 2021 merger of SYNNEX Corporation and Tech Data Corporation, creating the global #1 IT distributor. The company aggregates and distributes IT products across two primary portfolios: Endpoint Solutions (PCs, peripherals, mobile, printers) and Advanced Solutions (data center infrastructure, hybrid cloud, security, networking, software). The Hyve Solutions division designs and manufactures custom infrastructure for hyperscale customers.

Effective Q1 FY2026, the company reorganized into four reportable segments: Americas distribution, Europe distribution, APJ distribution, and Hyve Solutions (global). This reorganization isolates the AI/hyperscale infrastructure business for separate reporting and potentially separate valuation analysis.

The company operates 168 distribution facilities, maintains $200,000+ active SKUs, and employs approximately 24,000 people. 48% of revenue is international.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $62.5B grew 6.9% YoY from $58.5B. Management disclosed that approximately $2.8B in revenue was reported on a net basis (as an agent) rather than gross, meaning underlying gross billings growth was approximately 12%. Q3 FY2025 (August quarter) showed 6.6% reported growth with constant-currency growth of 4.4%.

**Profitability:** Gross margin expanded 18bps to 6.99%, partially from the net revenue presentation shift (30bps benefit) offset by lower strategic technologies margins. Operating income grew 18.5% to $1.41B, reflecting operating leverage. Non-GAAP diluted EPS grew 12.9% from $11.68 to $13.19, with share buybacks providing additional per-share lift. The quarterly earnings trajectory shows acceleration: Q2 FY2025 $2.99 (+9.0% beat), Q3 $3.58 (+16.4% beat), Q4 $3.83 (+1.7% beat).

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $1.53B and estimated free cash flow of ~$1.39B represent strong cash conversion. The cash conversion cycle of 16 days (down from 18) reflects the business model's working capital efficiency — $17.6B in accounts payable and $3.7B in supplier finance programs largely fund the $11.7B inventory position.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt declined to approximately $1.16B from $2.68B in FY2024, driven by strong cash generation. Cash on hand of $2.44B more than doubled. Debt-to-equity of 0.43x is conservative. The company completed a refinancing in October 2025, issuing $1.15B in senior notes (4.30% due 2029, 5.30% due 2035) and repaying the $581M term loan. The $700M in 1.75% senior notes due August 2026 is a near-term item but is well-covered by $3.5B in undrawn revolver capacity.

**Capital Return:** The company repurchased $596M in stock in FY2025 and $612M in FY2024, reducing diluted shares from 92.9M to 82.4M (11.3% over two years). $1.2B remains authorized. The quarterly dividend was raised 9.1% to $0.48/share ($1.92 annualized, ~1.2% yield) in January 2026. Combined buybacks and dividends returned approximately $740M to shareholders in FY2025 against $1.39B in FCF — a 53% payout ratio with ample room for continued returns.

## Growth Analysis

Three identifiable growth drivers support the forward outlook:

**1. AI/Hyperscale Infrastructure (Hyve Solutions):** Separating Hyve as a standalone segment reflects management's view that this business warrants separate investor scrutiny. Goldman Sachs reportedly initiated coverage with an FY2026 billings growth expectation of 10%. Hyperscaler capex spending remains elevated globally, and Hyve's custom server/infrastructure design capabilities position it to benefit from sustained hyperscaler spending growth. However, margins in this segment appear to be compressing (the 10-K references declining "strategic technologies" margins), and customer concentration is high.

**2. PC Refresh Cycle:** Windows 10 end-of-life combined with AI PC introduction should drive endpoint demand through FY2026-2027. This is a cyclical tailwind with relatively high visibility given Microsoft's timeline.

**3. Cloud/SaaS Distribution Evolution:** The shift from hardware to cloud and as-a-service models changes revenue recognition (net vs. gross) but creates recurring revenue streams and margin improvement over time. The Apptium acquisition ($84M, cloud commerce platform) reflects management's investment in this transition.

The earnings beat trajectory (3 of 4 quarters, with Q3 showing a 16.4% surprise) suggests estimates may still embed conservatism. The forward P/E of 15.3x implies approximately $10.25 in consensus EPS for FY2026 — representing modest single-digit growth from FY2025's $9.95 GAAP EPS and potentially conservative given the growth drivers above.

## Competitive Landscape

TD SYNNEX holds the #1 position in global IT distribution following the 2021 Tech Data merger. The competitive structure favors incumbents: scale advantages in logistics and vendor rebate programs, deep integration with customer procurement systems, and working capital efficiency create meaningful barriers to entry. Vendor rebates and back-end incentive programs represent a significant portion of gross profit — these programs are relationship-intensive and difficult for smaller distributors to replicate.

The primary competitive threat is disintermediation. Large enterprises and hyperscalers increasingly purchase directly from OEMs or ODMs. This is partially mitigated by the value-add services SNX provides (credit extension, logistics, technical support, demand aggregation for smaller customers). The Hyve business directly addresses the hyperscale market but competes against Celestica, Jabil, Flex, Quanta, and Wiwynn — a more fragmented and competitive landscape than traditional distribution.

Ingram Micro's recent re-IPO adds a newly public competitor, though the industry structure remains an oligopoly. The top two distributors (SNX and Ingram Micro) control a dominant share of global IT distribution.

## Risk Assessment

**Vendor Concentration:** Apple (12%) and HP (10%) together represent 22% of revenue on short-term, non-exclusive, terminable-without-cause agreements. Loss of either would be material. The French antitrust fine (€24.9M) specifically involved Apple distribution practices.

**Customer Concentration in Hyve:** One unnamed customer (likely a major hyperscaler) accounts for 11% of total company revenue. Contracts in the hyperscale business offer "limited or no volume guarantees." This concentration is a material risk to the Hyve growth thesis.

**Tariff/Trade Exposure:** With 48% international revenue and significant IT hardware crossing borders, tariff escalation directly impacts the supply chain. The company has IT operations in China, adding geopolitical risk. In the current stagflationary macro regime with trade policy uncertainty, enterprise customers may delay capex decisions.

**Margin Sensitivity:** At 7% gross margin and 2.3% operating margin, the business has minimal buffer against pricing pressure, rebate changes, or cost inflation. A 50bp deterioration in gross margin would reduce operating income by approximately 7%.

**Management Transition:** A new CFO (David Jordan, October 2025) and CEO (Patrick Zammit, September 2024) mean the top two positions have been filled within the past 18 months. Multiple officer departures in 2025 suggest organizational churn.

**Cybersecurity:** Network intrusions in 2021, 2022, 2023, and a 2024 partner breach represent a pattern. While not yet material, repeated incidents raise questions about the security posture of a company handling sensitive supply chain data.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

1. **Hyve segment visibility catalyst:** The Q1 FY2026 reporting under the new four-segment structure will give the market its first look at Hyve's standalone growth rate, margin, and return profile. If Hyve is growing at 15-20%+ (consistent with hyperscaler capex trends) with above-company-average margins, a sum-of-the-parts re-rating toward 17-18x earnings is plausible, implying $170-$180.

2. **Earnings momentum:** Three consecutive quarterly beats (9%, 16.4%, 1.7%) with upward trajectory in absolute EPS ($2.99, $3.58, $3.83) suggest consensus estimates remain conservative. A 5-10% beat in Q1 FY2026 would put the stock on a path toward the $180 consensus target.

3. **Capital return acceleration:** With net debt down to $1.16B, $2.44B in cash, and $1.39B annual FCF generation, management has capacity to accelerate buybacks and dividend growth. At current prices, the 11% FCF yield provides a strong floor.

4. **PC refresh cycle:** Windows 10 end-of-life provides multi-quarter visibility into endpoint demand — this is a known, quantifiable tailwind.

### Bear Case

1. **Hyperscale capex deceleration:** If hyperscaler spending growth decelerates (macro slowdown, budget reallocation, competitive ODM pricing), Hyve's growth rate falls and the re-rating catalyst fails to materialize.

2. **Vendor disintermediation:** Apple or HP deciding to shift distribution strategy (more direct sales, fewer partners) could remove 10-12% of revenue with limited recourse.

3. **Margin compression accelerates:** Strategic technologies margins are already declining. If Hyve's competitive position weakens or pricing pressure intensifies from Asian ODMs, operating leverage reverses.

4. **Macro demand destruction:** In a stagflationary environment with oil at $116 and enterprise confidence declining, IT capex budgets could face cuts, reducing demand across both endpoint and advanced solutions.

5. **Management execution risk:** New CEO and CFO navigating a major segment reorganization introduces execution uncertainty during the segment reorganization.

## Conclusion

TD SYNNEX offers a specific, identifiable catalyst (Hyve segment separation and first standalone reporting expected in coming quarters) combined with favorable fundamental momentum (18.5% operating income growth, 3 of 4 quarterly beats, 11% FCF yield) at a discount to peer median valuation (15.3x vs. 16.8x). The 9/11 analyst Buy/Strong Buy consensus with $163-$200 target range and a median of $180 provides 14.6% upside from the current price.

The risks are real: vendor concentration, hyperscale customer dependence, low-margin amplification, and macro sensitivity in a hostile environment. The management transition adds uncertainty. These risks are partially reflected in the valuation discount to peers. The FCF yield of 11%, combined with the aggressive buyback program and growing dividend, limits downside.

**Recommendation: BUY.** Below-peer valuation, accelerating earnings trajectory, a concrete near-term catalyst (Hyve segment reporting), and strong capital returns offer favorable risk/reward at $157. The primary risk to monitor is whether Q1 FY2026 Hyve segment data confirms the growth thesis or reveals margin and concentration concerns that justify the current discount.
