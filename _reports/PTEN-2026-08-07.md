## Executive Summary

Patterson-UTI's second quarter of 2026 contained the first operational turn in the company's post-merger history visible in segment-level GAAP data rather than in management commentary alone. Completion Services — 61.4% of revenue and the source of the $885.2M goodwill write-off taken in FY2024 — swung from a $20.7M segment operating loss in Q1'26 to $8.2M of positive segment operating income in Q2'26, with adjusted gross margin rising from 14.4% to 16.3%. Frac revenue rose $55.1M sequentially (+10%) on a 6% increase in pumping hours, meaning roughly four points of the gain came from price and mix. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA rose 13.1% sequentially to $231.9M, US contract drilling backlog rose 25.4% in six months to $365M (the first increase in four years of disclosure), and management guided Q3 adjusted gross profit higher in all three segments on a US rig count moving from 92 to approximately 100. FY2027 EPS estimates went from $0.04 to $0.24 over ninety days.

The cash statement tells a harder story. H1'26 operating cash flow was $119.9M against $272.6M of capex, producing free cash flow of negative $152.6M and a $217.5M decline in cash and restricted cash to $203.2M. The company paid $76.0M of dividends out of that balance sheet, having raised the quarterly dividend 25% on February 4, 2026 into a declining-EBITDA year, and made zero open-market repurchases despite $694M of remaining authorization and a stock at 1.24x book. Roughly $196.9M of the cash drag is a receivable build management attributes to a mid-quarter ERP cutover covering a third of the business, plausibly reversible. About $70.7M is a deferred-revenue unwind that is not reversible: rig reactivation and mobilization prepayments collected in prior periods were earned as H1 revenue, and the contract-liability balance is down 88% to $9.9M.

At 5.6x EV/TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $876.6M, 1.24x book value of $8.13/share, and 41.9x the $0.24 FY2027 consensus, the price sits between a credible recovery and a credible disappointment. Base case is $11.25 (+11.9%), bull $14.50 (+44%), bear $6.75 (-33%). The rating stays HOLD. The operating turn is now confirmed in filed segment data, which supports the growth and valuation dimensions moving up, while the H2 cash-conversion test, the undisclosed July 31 goodwill result, and management's capital-allocation choices keep financial health and management quality where they are.

## Company Overview

Patterson-UTI is a North American land drilling and completion services provider operating three segments. Its current shape derives from two Q3 2023 transactions, the NexTier Oilfield Solutions merger and the Ulterra acquisition, which converted a land driller into a majority-completions business by revenue.

**Completion Services (61.4% of Q2'26 revenue, $753.6M)**: hydraulic fracturing, wireline and pumpdown, cementing, a natural-gas fueling business ("Power Solutions"), and proppant last-mile logistics. Active in the Permian, Marcellus/Utica, Eagle Ford, Mid-Continent, Haynesville and Bakken/Rockies. Perforating guns and switches are manufactured in-house. The strategic thrust is the "Emerald" 100% natural-gas fleet, targeted at 90% of active horsepower by year-end 2026.

**Drilling Services (30.4%, $373.5M)**: 148 marketed land rigs at 6/30/26 (West Texas 63, Appalachia 21, Rockies 22, Oklahoma 16, South Texas 13, East Texas 7, Colombia 3, Ecuador 1, Argentina 2), of which 137 meet the company's Tier-1 super-spec definition (≥1,500 hp, AC, ≥750,000-lb hookload, 7,500-psi circulation, pad-capable, third mud pump, raised drawworks). Proprietary technology includes the APEX rig platform and Cortex operating system.

**Drilling Products (7.4%, $91.3M)**: Ulterra PDC drill bits and downhole tools, sold into 30+ countries, manufactured in Fort Worth, Leduc and Saudi Arabia. Bits are predominantly rented rather than sold outside select international markets, producing recurring revenue at a 40.7% adjusted gross margin in Q2'26.

Two structural facts frame everything below. First, the highest-margin segment (Drilling Products, 40.7%) is 7.4% of revenue, while the lowest-margin segment (Completion Services, 16.3%) is 61.4%. Second, in January 2026 the company leased two rigs to DLS Archer Ltd. S.A. in Argentina under sales-type lease accounting, with PTEN as lessor and DLS Archer the lessee operating in Vaca Muerta. That arrangement contributed roughly $7.8M of Q2 Drilling Services revenue with $8.942M of net investment in sales-type leases recognized as a non-cash item in H1, so a small portion of the segment's reported improvement is revenue recognized ahead of cash.

The FY2022–FY2024 revenue growth was acquisition-driven. Within that decline, Q2'26 is the sequential turn: consolidated revenue $1,227.97M (+9.9% sequentially, +0.7% year over year), Adjusted EBITDA $231.9M (18.88% margin) versus $205.0M in Q1, and a GAAP net loss attributable to common of $19.6M, or $(0.05)/share. Adjusting only for Colombia, Drilling Services adjusted gross margin was 35.8% rather than the reported 30.5%.

### The cash-flow question

|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow ($M) | 119.9 | 347.9 | 961.2 |
| Capex ($M) | (272.6) | (306.0) | (589.0) |

Two items explain most of the $228.0M year-over-year operating cash flow decline, and they behave differently. Accounts receivable rose $196.9M (versus $6.9M in H1'25), from $723.3M to $919.7M — 27.2% growth against 9.9% sequential revenue growth, implying roughly 68 days of sales outstanding versus approximately 58 at year-end. Management attributes this to a mid-quarter ERP cutover covering one-third of the business that delayed billing; the allowance for credit losses rose only $2.0M, consistent with a billing rather than a credit problem, though credit loss expense did increase $2.2M in the quarter. Separately, contract liabilities fell from $79.5M to $9.9M, with $89.7M of revenue recognized against $20.0M of new payments received. Roughly $73.0M of H1 revenue came out of the opening balance. That was cash collected in earlier periods and will not repeat unless new mobilization prepayments arrive at similar scale, which is plausible given the 92-to-100 rig reactivation path but is not assured.

Adding back only the receivable build, H1 operating cash flow would have been roughly $317M and free cash flow roughly +$44M, still short of the $76.0M of dividends paid. For the $152.6M annual dividend run-rate ($0.40 × 381.4M shares) to be covered by full-year free cash flow, H2 free cash flow must reach roughly +$305M, implying an approximately $457M half-over-half swing in operating cash flow. That is achievable if receivables fully convert and earnings rise as guided, but it is a demanding bar, and the company's own capex guide of "under $500M net of asset sales" suggests the "adjusted free cash flow" metric management uses for its return policy may net disposal proceeds. That definition is not verifiable from the filings.

### Capital intensity and asset base

FY2025 capex of $589.0M equalled 63% of DD&A plus impairment; H1'26 capex of $272.6M equalled the same 63% of $436.2M. Excluding roughly $63.7M of purchase-accounting intangible amortization, capex is about 73% of property depreciation and depletion. Net PP&E fell 4.2% in six months to $2,598.4M and gross equipment fell $241.7M to $7,983.3M, reflecting active retirement consistent with the diesel-to-gas frac conversion and the 152-to-148 rig reduction. Two observations cut against reading this purely as high-grading: commitments to purchase major equipment jumped from $47.5M at 12/31/25 to $169M at 6/30/26, and Completion Services capex rose 66.3% sequentially in Q2 on Emerald fleet expansion. Capex is set to rise materially, which raises the bar for the H2 free-cash-flow swing.

### Balance sheet

Debt of $1,244.9M face: $344.9M 5.15% due 2029, $400M 7.15% due 2033, $500M 6.05% due 2036. In May–June 2026 the company issued the $500M 2036 notes and redeemed roughly $483M of 3.95% 2028 notes at par, taking a $0.9M non-cash extinguishment loss. Run-rate cash interest is now approximately $76.6M versus $65.4M on the prior mix, a permanent $11.2M annual earnings headwind (roughly $0.029/share pretax) for $17.5M more principal, with that tranche repricing 210bp higher. Liquidity is $201.0M cash plus roughly $498M of undrawn revolver, or approximately $699M; the revolver was amended in April 2026 to extend $450M of commitments to January 2031. Total debt/capitalization is 28.6% against a 50% covenant. Net debt/TTM Adjusted EBITDA is 1.19x, up from roughly 0.88x at year-end because EBITDA fell and cash was consumed. Adjusted EBITDA/cash interest is roughly 11.4x. Nothing matures before 2029.

One conditional term deserves attention: PTEN is investment grade at both Moody's and S&P, and if it falls below investment grade at both, a restricted-payment covenant activates requiring a pro forma debt service coverage ratio of at least 1.50x before dividends or buybacks. That creates a direct mechanical link between rating migration and the dividend.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.61 sits in the grey zone, dragged there almost entirely by EBIT/assets near zero. With $699M of liquidity, 28.6% debt/capitalization against a 50% covenant and no maturities for three years, the score is flagging trough earnings rather than a funding problem.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three sequential data points visible in the filings rather than only in commentary.

First, **completion pricing recovery**. The Completion Services segment turned to positive operating income, and Q3 guidance cites near-full utilization of active frac equipment plus additional pricing improvement. Management reports completion pricing fell roughly 30% over three years, so the recovery starts from a deeply depressed base. The company holds 2027 manufacturing slots but has declined to commit to adding total fleet horsepower; capacity discipline is the mechanism by which price recovery holds, and PTEN controls only its own share of it.

Second, **drilling backlog and pricing**. New-contract rig pricing is reported up 10–15% sequentially, with high-quality rigs described as nearly sold out outside the Permian, where PTEN's footprint (79 rigs across Appalachia, the Rockies, Oklahoma, South and East Texas) exceeds its 63-rig West Texas book. The $365M backlog rose from $291M at 12/31/25, the first increase after four years of decline from roughly $830M in 2022. Three scope limits apply: the backlog covers only US term contracts of six months or longer, excludes both other segments and all well-to-well work, only about 15% is expected to remain at 6/30/27, and term contracts are terminable on short notice. The FY2025 10-K guided to an average of just 27 rigs on term contracts across 2026 versus 49 in Q1'26.

Third, **Drilling Products international expansion**. Q2 revenue rose 14.5% sequentially with segment operating income up 63%, the highest revenue since the Ulterra acquisition, and share gains cited in Oman and Saudi Arabia. This is the highest-quality growth in the portfolio and it is compounding despite Middle East logistics disruption.

Consensus reflects this. FY2027 EPS revision of +480% is large because the base is near zero, not because absolute earnings power has re-rated much — $0.24 of FY2027 EPS on $10.05 is 41.9x. The current year improved from $(0.18) to $(0.06); the current quarter went from $(0.02) to $0.03. Revision magnitude of that scale usually precedes further upgrades. The estimate cycle rose alongside a WTI price averaging $95.65 in Q2 that has since fallen toward the mid-$80s, with a forward strip near $70.

The constraint on the growth case is the demand response function. WTI averaged $95.65 in Q2'26 versus $72.74 in Q1, and PTEN's average US rig count was 92, flat sequentially. Baker Hughes' US oil-directed count fell to 450 in late July even as prices climbed. Customers budgeted 2026 at roughly $60/bbl, the strip is roughly $70, and management expects the activity response in late 2026 and 2027, led by private E&Ps. Simultaneously Henry Hub fell from $4.71 to $2.95 and closed near $2.63, a direct negative for the 21 rigs in Appalachia and the Haynesville-exposed completion fleet. PTEN's Q3 guide to roughly 100 rigs therefore implies share gain against a flat-to-declining industry count rather than participation in a rising one. That is consistent with the bifurcation thesis — 137 of 148 rigs sit in the tightening premium tier — but it makes the growth path narrower and more execution-dependent than a normal cyclical upswing.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is genuinely mixed by segment, and the aggregate trajectory is modestly improving.

**Where PTEN is strong.** 137 of 148 marketed rigs are Tier-1 super-spec, in a market management describes as bifurcating toward high-hookload rigs for laterals beyond four miles. The upgrade program — 10 to 15 rigs to one-million-pound hookload at roughly $2M each, targeting one-year payback and secured by six-month to three-year term contracts — is aimed precisely at the tightening tier, and implies only $20–30M of capex. Ulterra's rental drill-bit model delivered 40.7% adjusted gross margin at a cyclical trough with international share gains. Vertical integration in completions (in-house perforating manufacture, owned sand logistics, owned gas fueling) provides cost advantage the pure-play pumpers lack.

**Where PTEN is weak.** Completion Services is 61% of revenue in a market where price fell roughly 30% over three years, the segment produced negative $79.4M of operating income in FY2025, and $885.2M of goodwill was written off in FY2024. That is the signature of persistent capacity oversupply. PTEN's response — retiring rather than adding horsepower, converting to 90% gas-powered active horsepower, differentiating on customer fuel cost — is strategically correct but depends on competitors exercising similar restraint. Drilling Services adjusted gross margin has eroded roughly 440bp from FY2023 on a comparable basis even as fleet quality improved, which suggests day-rate pressure has been running ahead of the premium-tier tightening.

One strategic gap deserves flagging. Reporting on 7/16/26 describes SLB and Liberty Energy forming an alliance to supply modular power generation to data centers, two independent companies converting gas-power capability into behind-the-meter merchant power. PTEN owns a Power Solutions natural-gas fueling business, but per the 10-Q it is oriented to fueling PTEN's own completion fleets, and its H1'26 revenue fell 20% ($13.0M) on fewer working days. I found no filing evidence that PTEN is pursuing data-center or merchant power. The inference that PTEN lacks a non-cyclical growth option some peers are building is mine, not the company's; the defensible alternative reading is deliberate focus on core oilfield returns, consistent with the Colombia exit to reallocate capital to higher-return US opportunities. Given the current bid for dispatchable generation, the absence of that optionality is a competitive consideration rather than a decisive one.

Customer concentration is only disclosed as of 12/31/25: largest customer roughly $597M or 12.4% of revenue, top five roughly 39%, top ten roughly 57%. The H1'26 shift in segment mix toward Completion Services (61.1% versus 59.4%) does not establish improved customer diversification, and ongoing E&P consolidation mechanically concentrates the buyer side.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill concentration with a thin, stale cushion.** Of $487.4M total goodwill, $450.5M sits in Drilling Products. The FY2025 10-K disclosed that the Q2 2025 quantitative test found fair value exceeded carrying value by only about 8%, with a 100bp reduction in long-term growth cutting fair value roughly 7% and a 100bp increase in discount rate cutting it roughly 10%. Either single move roughly eliminates the cushion. Subsequent assessments were qualitative. The annual test date is July 31, 2026 — days before the 10-Q was filed on 8/4/26 — and the result is not disclosed. The Q3'26 10-Q, expected in early November, is a dated catalyst for a non-cash charge of up to roughly $450M. Offsetting: Drilling Products fundamentals improved materially since the 8% figure was struck, which raises fair value, and an impairment would not affect Adjusted EBITDA, cash flow, or the 28.6%-versus-50% covenant. It would reduce book equity by roughly $1.18/share and remove part of the price floor.

**Dividend coverage.** The dividend was raised 25% in February 2026 into a year of declining EBITDA and negative H1 free cash flow. Full-year coverage requires roughly +$305M of H2 free cash flow. The receivable reversal is the mechanism; rising capex (equipment commitments up 3.6x to $169M) works against it. A cut is not the base case given $699M of liquidity and 1.19x leverage, but the dividend is currently a claim on the balance sheet rather than on operations.

**Oil and gas price normalization.** The Q2 activity environment carried a war premium that is fading: WTI closed at $84.25 on 7/27/26, fell about 5% to a two-week low on 7/28 as fighting paused, and the strip is near $70 while OPEC+ restores supply and Saudi Arabia cuts official selling prices. Henry Hub at roughly $2.63 pressures the 21-rig gas-directed footprint directly. The more troubling observation is that $95 oil produced zero rig growth for PTEN and a decline in the industry oil-directed count, so the downside from normalization is not symmetric with a proven upside from strength.

**ERP execution.** A risk factor in the FY2025 10-K materialized as a cash-flow event. Management says the system is now live for the majority of the business; the remaining rollout is unfinished execution risk with a demonstrated cash consequence.

**Litigation.** NOV subsidiaries sued Ulterra in February 2023 over patent royalties. Ulterra won final judgment on 11/25/2025; NOV appealed to the Federal Circuit, with briefing completed 7/31/26. No amount is quantified in either filing and the FY2025 10-K states an unfavorable resolution not covered by indemnity could be material. Posture is favorable; a 2027 decision is plausible.

**Other.** Section 382 limits on roughly $1.4B of federal NOLs push cash taxes earlier. The company self-insures loss of earnings, business interruption and most cybersecurity risk. Share count is rising for the first time in three years (+2.08M in H1'26), with 8.38M shares of reported dilutive overhang (2.20% of shares, 2.15% post-issuance ownership dilution) and a newly authorized 28.9M-share addition to plan capacity that, if fully issued over time, would equal 7.04% post-issuance dilution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 58.8% sits 8.2 points above 50.7% one-year realized volatility, at the 64th percentile of the current chain, with a contango term structure (58.8% near versus 61.0% at six months). Positioning is strongly call-tilted: put/call volume ratio 0.04, put/call open interest 0.17, and the data labels the skew call-heavy at every tenor. The nearest expiry prices a ±9.1% move; the six-month tenor prices ±25.1%. Short interest is 9.0% of float at 2.7 days to cover, moderate and not a squeeze setup.

The options market is positioned for upside and is pricing no near-term dislocation, which corroborates the recovery narrative but also means there is no cheap protection and little skepticism embedded in the structure. For a stock facing an undisclosed goodwill test and an unresolved dividend-coverage question inside the next quarter, the absence of put demand is a caution rather than a confirmation. Elevated IV relative to realized also means selling covered calls against existing positions is better compensated than buying upside exposure outright.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $14.50 (+44%)

Completion Services pricing recovery compounds from a base 30% below the 2023 peak, with segment adjusted gross margin moving from 16.3% toward the low 20s as the industry declines to add horsepower. Drilling rig count reaches 100+ with new-contract pricing up 10–15% and the one-million-pound upgrade program capturing the four-mile-lateral premium at one-year paybacks. Ulterra continues compounding internationally, and the July 31 goodwill test passes on improved Drilling Products fundamentals. The H2 receivable reversal delivers the cash swing, full-year free cash flow covers the $152.6M dividend, and buybacks resume against the $694M authorization at a price near book. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches $1.05B; at 6.3x, equity value is $5.57B, or $14.60/share. Analyst mean target of $13.36 and high of $19.00 sit in this zone.

### Bear Case — $6.75 (-33%)

The Q2 activity uplift proves to have been a war premium. Hormuz transit normalizes, OPEC+ completes its rollback, WTI settles near $70 or below, and US land activity stays flat through 2027 as public E&Ps continue budgeting off multi-year strips. Henry Hub near $2.60 keeps gas-basin rigs idle. Completion pricing recovery stalls as a competitor reactivates horsepower. H2 receivables convert more slowly than guided while capex rises on the $169M of equipment commitments, forcing either a dividend cut or revolver drawdown. The November 10-Q discloses a Drilling Products impairment, taking book value to roughly $6.95 and confirming the 2023 acquisition price was too high. EBITDA holds near $825M and the multiple compresses to 4.5x, producing roughly $6.75/share against a 52-week low of $5.10 and an analyst low target of $8.00.

### Base Case — $11.25 (+11.9%)

Q3 delivers the guided sequential improvement across all three segments and the rig count reaches roughly 100, but the aggregate US count stays flat and PTEN's gains come from share within the premium tier. Receivables largely convert in H2, producing full-year free cash flow near but not comfortably above the dividend; buybacks stay dormant. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $980M. At 5.5x, EV is $5.39B, equity $4.35B, and per-share value $11.41, rounded to $11.25 to account for the modest ongoing share creep. Total return with the dividend is roughly 16% over twelve to eighteen months, which is fair compensation for the risk profile but not asymmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders are compensated for waiting through the H2 cash-conversion test; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $10.05 with the base case only 12% above spot and a 33% bear leg carrying identifiable, dated catalysts.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results and 10-Q, expected late October/early November 2026. That single filing resolves three of the four open questions simultaneously — the H2 free cash flow swing, the July 31 goodwill test result, and whether the completion pricing recovery extended a second quarter.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 operating cash flow above roughly $290M with capex under $150M, demonstrating that the receivable build reversed and that the full-year dividend is covered from operations rather than the balance sheet.
2. Completion Services adjusted gross margin at or above 18% in Q3 with segment operating income positive for a second consecutive quarter, confirming the price recovery rather than a one-quarter mix effect.
3. Resumption of open-market repurchases against the $694M authorization at or below 1.3x book, which would both validate management's own view of value and reverse the share-count creep.
4. Alternatively, a price near $8.00–$8.50 (roughly 1.0x book, 4.9x EV/EBITDA on $950M) would restore the margin of safety independent of any of the above.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A Drilling Products goodwill impairment disclosed in the Q3 10-Q, which would cut book value per share to roughly $6.95 and remove the primary valuation floor while confirming the 2023 acquisition thesis failed.
2. Q3 US average rig count below 95 versus the roughly 100 guided, or any softening of the Q3 completion pricing language — evidence that the premium-tier tightening was a war-premium artifact.
3. Dividend reduction, revolver drawdown, or a ratings action toward the sub-investment-grade threshold that activates the 1.50x pro forma debt service coverage restricted-payment covenant.
4. Henry Hub sustained below $2.50 into the winter with the gas-directed rig count declining, which would pressure roughly 21 rigs plus the Haynesville and Marcellus completion book.

**What would confirm this is a value trap rather than a trough:** two consecutive quarters in which Adjusted EBITDA rises while free cash flow stays negative and capex rises to meet the $169M of equipment commitments. That combination would establish that the recovery requires reinvestment at a rate the business cannot self-fund at 5.6x EV/EBITDA.

## Conclusion

Patterson-UTI has produced its first credible operating turn since the 2023 acquisitions, and the evidence is in filed segment data rather than in management framing alone: Completion Services operating income turned positive, adjusted gross margin rose 190 basis points sequentially, US drilling backlog rose 25% after four years of decline, and Q3 guidance calls for higher adjusted gross profit in all three segments. Consensus has responded with a 480% upward revision to FY2027 EPS. The stock at 5.6x EV/TTM Adjusted EBITDA and 1.24x book is not expensive.

The offsetting evidence is equally specific. H1 free cash flow was negative $152.6M and the dividend was paid from the balance sheet after a 25% increase into a declining-EBITDA year; roughly $73M of H1 revenue drew on a prepayment reservoir now 88% depleted; buybacks stopped entirely at 1.24x book while the share count began rising and six insiders sold approximately 1.58M shares for $17.0M at $11.47–$12.29 with no offsetting purchases; the Drilling Products goodwill cushion was last measured at roughly 8% and the July 31 test result is unpublished; and $95 oil in Q2 produced zero net rig growth, so the demand response function that would normally carry a land driller through a price spike did not operate.

The two dimensions that most reliably separate winners from losers in this business, cash generation and capital allocation, are the two where PTEN currently scores worst. That is why the recommendation stays HOLD despite genuinely improved operating data, and why the score moves only fractionally. The November 10-Q is a clean, dated discriminator. Existing holders should hold through it and consider writing covered calls given implied volatility 8 points above realized. New capital should wait for either the cash conversion to verify or a price near $8.00–$8.50.
