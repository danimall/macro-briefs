**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nucor is emerging from a cyclical trough, and the turn is now visible in reported results rather than projected. Q1 2026 (quarter ended April 4, 2026) delivered net earnings of $743M ($3.23 diluted EPS) versus $156M ($0.67) a year earlier — nearly a fivefold jump — as consolidated gross margin doubled from 8% to 16% and steel-mill shipments hit a record 7.0M tons. The proximate cause is trade policy: the full 2025 reinstatement of Section 232 tariffs (no exemptions, broadened to roughly 600 fabricated products) drove finished-steel import share down from over 22% in Q1 2025 to about 15% in Q1 2026, tightening the domestic market and expanding metal margins as mill selling prices rose 14% YoY against a 2% scrap-cost increase. Current-year consensus EPS has risen 30.9% over 90 days to $16.99, and next-quarter estimates are up 40.8%.

The quality of the business is not in question. Nucor runs 100% electric-arc-furnace capacity, carries the highest credit ratings in North American steel (A-/A-/A3, all stable), sits at ~0.9x net debt/EBITDA with a 2.9x current ratio, and has raised its dividend every year since 1973. Management's pay-for-performance model works: CEO "comp actually paid" fell to roughly $0.3M at the 2024 trough, and the "Expand Beyond" diversification into doors, racking, insulated panels, and towers is already providing a more stable earnings floor beneath the volatile mill segment.

The debate is about price and durability, not quality. At $241–244 and roughly 24x trailing / ~14x this year's rising consensus EPS, the market is pricing off a recovering earnings base, not the trough. The tariff regime that produced the margin expansion is policy-dependent and fragile, and the stock trades about 10% below its 52-week high of $270.90 with balanced options positioning. We rate NUE a HOLD (growth hold), with a bias to accumulate on pullbacks toward the low-$200s where the cyclical risk/reward turns more asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Nucor is North America's largest and most diversified steel producer and the continent's largest metal recycler, processing roughly 20 million gross tons of scrap in 2025. Its entire fleet runs on electric-arc-furnace technology, which gives it a lower fixed-cost base, greater production flexibility across the cycle, and roughly one-third the greenhouse-gas intensity of blast-furnace integrated competitors. The company is organized in three segments:

- **Steel Mills (~62% of external sales):** sheet, bar, structural, and plate steel — the largest and most cyclical segment, including 51%-owned JVs (Nucor-Yamato structural, California Steel Industries, Nucor-JFE Mexico).
- **Steel Products:** joist and deck (Vulcraft/Verco, #1 in the U.S.), rebar fabrication, tubular, metal buildings, insulated panels, steel racking, overhead doors (C.H.I., Rytec), and utility towers and structures. This is the "Expand Beyond" engine — higher-margin, less-commoditized products tied to data centers, warehousing, and infrastructure.
- **Raw Materials:** DJJ (the largest North American ferrous-scrap broker), two DRI plants (Trinidad and Louisiana, ~3.3M mt/yr), natural-gas wells, and industrial gases — mostly captive supply, providing vertical integration against scrap-price volatility.

Nucor employs roughly 33,000 largely non-union teammates under a decentralized, incentive-heavy culture that automatically flexes labor cost down in troughs. It is the leading domestic supplier in most categories it serves.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year picture shows a cyclical descent followed by a sharp turn:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $41.5B | $34.7B | $30.7B | $32.5B (+6%) |
| Net income | $7.6B | $4.5B | $2.0B | $1.74B |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $8.46 | $7.52 |
| Gross margin | ~30% | ~22.5% | 13% | 12% |

The Steel Mills segment produced Q1 2026 EBT of $1,128M versus $231M a year earlier. Record mill shipments of 7.0M tons eclipsed the prior Q2-2021 peak of 6.7M. Mill utilization ran 86% versus 80% a year earlier, backlogs rose 20% sequentially, and rebar and structural backlogs are at record highs. The margin mechanics are clean: average mill selling price rose 14% YoY to $1,074/ton while scrap cost rose only 2% to ~$403/gross ton. Metal-margin expansion is the profit driver, and it is a direct function of the domestic supply-demand tightening produced by tariffs.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed but recently strong: Q1 2026 beat by 13.5% and Q3 2025 by 19.1%, while Q4 2025 missed by 10.3% and Q2 2025 by a hair. Visibility is improving as the recovery gathers pace, but the business remains lumpy quarter-to-quarter.

The balance sheet is a differentiated strength. Altman Z ~2.99, current ratio 2.9, D/E ~0.62, funded debt/total capital ~24%, net debt/EBITDA ~0.9x, and the highest credit ratings in North American steel (S&P A-, Fitch A-, Moody's A3, all stable). Liquidity comprises ~$2.5B in cash and short-term investments plus an undrawn $2.25B revolver. This balance sheet lets Nucor stay profitable and keep investing while fixed-cost integrated competitors bleed in downturns.

Cash flow swung negative in 2025 (FCF –$188M) as capex peaked at $3.42B during the build-out. Management guides capex down to ~$2.5B in 2026, which, combined with the earnings recovery, should restore strong free cash flow. Nucor has now paid 212 consecutive quarterly dividends and, in February 2026, authorized a new $4.0B buyback (~$3.97B remaining), replacing the prior program. The company returns a minimum 40% of net earnings to shareholders and returned roughly 73% over the trailing three years.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth thesis rests on the earnings turn and its confirmation in estimate revisions. Consensus current-year EPS has risen from $12.98 to $16.99 over 90 days (+30.9%), next-quarter from $3.64 to $5.13 (+40.8%), and next-year from $14.12 to $17.38 (+23.1%). Revision magnitude of this size, combined with revenue re-acceleration off a depressed base, is one of the stronger forward indicators available and argues that consensus is still catching up to the recovery. This is a cyclical recovery off a trough, driven substantially by a policy tailwind, not a secular growth story. The estimate-revision velocity supports the near-term earnings trajectory but does not by itself justify a premium multiple on peak-cycle earnings.

The medium-term growth drivers are more structural. The West Virginia sheet mill — a 3M-ton greenfield project costing ~$4B gross (~$3.65B net of a $350M state incentive) — is commissioning through 2026 with a production ramp in 2027; management says it is on time and on budget. Smaller growth projects are already contributing: the Lexington NC rebar micro mill turned EBITDA-positive in March 2026, and the Kingman AZ melt shop, Berkeley SC second galv line, and Towers & Structures greenfields are ramping.

Diversification is the most important strategic point. Through the Q1'26 downturn-to-recovery, the Steel Products segment held roughly flat (~$285–307M EBT across quarters) while Steel Mills swung from $231M to $1,128M. The "Expand Beyond" businesses — doors, racking, insulated panels, towers — are deliberately shifting mix toward less-cyclical, higher-margin products tied to secular demand: data centers, IIJA infrastructure, CHIPS reshoring, and energy. This is a credible multi-year effort to structurally reduce earnings volatility, and it is beginning to work.

## Valuation Assessment

At $241–244 with a ~$55.7B market cap, NUE trades at roughly 24x trailing EPS ($7.52) but only ~14x the rising current-year consensus of $16.99 and ~14x next-year $17.38. The trailing multiple is misleading at a cyclical turn: Q1 2026 annualized to a run-rate of ~$13 in EPS, and consensus sees $17 this year. On forward earnings the stock is not expensive in absolute terms.

The difficulty is that "forward earnings" here means recovering-to-peak-cycle earnings, and steel is a business where the market rightly refuses to capitalize peak margins at trend multiples. The whole earnings uplift from 8% to 16% gross margin traces to import share falling on the back of Section 232 tariffs — a policy-dependent margin structure, not a durable moat. Adjusted for where we are in the cycle, the stock is priced appropriately for a high-quality producer earning above-mid-cycle margins with meaningful tail risk to the tariff regime.

Analyst targets bracket the stock: low $224, mean $267, median $274, high $285. On an inflecting stock with a forward P/E well under 30 and rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag and act as floors. But much of the analyst optimism embeds the same tariff-driven margin extrapolation the price does, so the targets are not fully independent confirmation. The median target implies ~12% upside, which is real but not the asymmetric setup that would justify a BUY at these levels.

A "cheap" trailing multiple is frequently a value-trap flag in the steel and materials complex, where cyclical earnings capitalized at low multiples near the top of a cycle mark the wrong entry point. NUE is a quality compounder trading at fair value on cycle-adjusted earnings, which supports a HOLD rather than either a BUY or an AVOID. The valuation score reflects the absence of a clean mispricing at the current price, tempered by the recognition that this is not a stretched multiple relative to the earnings power on display.

## Competitive Landscape

Nucor competes against domestic integrated producers (higher fixed cost, less flexible), other EAF minimills (Steel Dynamics, Cleveland-Cliffs, Commercial Metals), imports, and substitute materials (aluminum, concrete, wood, composites). Its competitive edges are durable: lowest-cost EAF flexibility, captive raw-material supply through DRI and DJJ, the broadest product portfolio in North America, the strongest balance sheet and credit ratings in the sector, and a variable-cost labor model. These allow Nucor to stay profitable in downturns that push fixed-cost mills into losses, and to keep funding growth projects when peers are retrenching.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than sharply improving. Nucor is a market leader holding or modestly extending its position through the "Expand Beyond" mix shift, but it is not a challenger rapidly taking share, and its 5-year cumulative TSR of 331 slightly trails its peer group's 342 despite superior balance-sheet quality. The near-term industry setup is favorable — firm trade enforcement, lean service-center inventories, record long-product backlogs, and mega-project demand — but that favorability is cyclical and policy-linked, not a permanent widening of the moat. This warrants a solid but not premium competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**Trade-policy dependence (the fulcrum).** The margin expansion is materially levered to the full 2025 reinstatement of Section 232 tariffs. Any relaxation, expiry, or successful legal challenge would allow import share to recover from ~15% and compress the metal margins that drive the entire recovery. This is the single largest downside risk and it is not company-controllable.

**Global overcapacity.** OECD estimates 704M net tons of excess capacity in 2025 (roughly 8x total U.S. output), projected to grow ~20% to 795M tons by 2027. China produced over 1B tons for the eighth straight year and exported a record 131M tons in 2025, and is building capacity in Southeast Asia and Africa to circumvent duties. This is a permanent structural overhang on global steel pricing and keeps import pressure a live threat if U.S. trade enforcement softens.

**Cyclicality and end-market sensitivity.** Nonresidential construction is the largest end market and is interest-rate-sensitive; automotive and residential remain soft in a higher-for-longer rate environment. Scrap price volatility (the largest input cost) is partially mitigated by captive DRI and DJJ brokerage, and energy (~10% of conversion cost) is hedged.

**Cybersecurity — open item.** Nucor filed two Form 8-K Item 1.05 (Material Cybersecurity Incident) reports in 2025 (May 14 and a June 20 amendment). The 10-K addresses cyber risk only generically, and the incident does not appear to have surfaced as a financial charge or operational disruption. The specifics are unresolved in the available filings and should be confirmed; on current evidence it appears immaterial to financials but is the one genuinely unresolved item.

**Legal and impairment.** The Louisiana DRI facility faces EPA Clean Air Act allegations (2022) with a combined DOJ/EPA/LDEQ settlement under negotiation, not expected to be material. Goodwill of $4.3B includes the NJSM (Mexico) JV, which triggered impairment testing in Q4'25 (passed, but monitored).

The risk score reflects that the dominant risk — tariff durability — is a binary policy variable that could move the stock materially and is not fully de-risked at current levels. This is a specific, asymmetric downside catalyst rather than a generic "steel is cyclical" truism, which is why we score risk toward the middle rather than higher.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly balanced but flags near-term caution. Near-term ATM IV of 38.3% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 30.1% (an 8.3-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (38.3% near vs. 34.4% at six months), which the market typically uses to price an imminent event — here, the late-July / early-August earnings release. Put/call skew is essentially neutral (-0.7% near-term), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.10 and OI ratio of 0.41 show no bearish tilt. IV has risen 2.4 points since late June as the stock climbed ~9.7%, and the implied one-month move is ±8.8%. The market is pricing an earnings-driven move but is not positioned bearishly, which is consistent with a HOLD into the print rather than a directional lean.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($285+).** Section 232 tariffs hold, import share stays near 15% or falls further, and mega-project demand (data centers, reshoring, infrastructure) keeps mill utilization above 85% with record backlogs converting to shipments. Metal margins stay elevated, the West Virginia mill ramps into a firm pricing environment through 2027, and the "Expand Beyond" segments continue to grow higher-margin, less-cyclical earnings. Consensus current-year EPS of $16.99 proves conservative, next-year approaches or exceeds $18, and the $4B buyback shrinks the share count into rising earnings. On ~16x $18 EPS the stock reaches the high-$280s.

**Bear Case (~$135–150, roughly -40% from current).** Trade policy softens — exemptions return, tariffs are challenged, or global overcapacity forces import share back toward 22%. Metal margins compress as selling prices fall faster than scrap costs, mill utilization slips, and nonresidential construction weakens in a higher-for-longer rate environment. EPS reverts toward the $8–9 trough range, and the market re-rates cyclical earnings to a low-teens multiple, sending the stock toward the mid-$130s to $150s (below the 52-week low of $131.32 in a severe case).

**Base Case (~$260).** Tariffs hold through 2026 but the market declines to fully capitalize peak-cycle margins. EPS lands near consensus $17 this year, the WV mill ramps roughly on schedule, and the stock trades at ~15x forward earnings, delivering a mid-single to low-double-digit total return including the ~1% dividend. This sits between the bull and bear cases and reflects a fairly valued high-quality producer earning above-mid-cycle margins with a policy-dependent tail risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The trajectory has been a persistent HOLD across four prior reports (6.6 → 5.8 → 6.9 → 6.6), and the bar for continuing it unchanged is appropriately high. The current call is unchanged because the fundamentals have genuinely improved (Financial Health 7.0 → 8.0, Growth 6.5 → 7.5 across the trajectory) while valuation has re-rated to match: the stock has done what the improving fundamentals warranted, leaving risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric at $241–244.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$200s (roughly $200–210) that restores cycle-adjusted asymmetry, particularly if backlogs and estimate revisions remain intact.
- Concrete evidence the tariff regime is entrenched beyond a single administration cycle (durable legislative backing or multi-year enforcement commitment), which would de-risk the margin structure and justify a higher multiple.
- Q2/Q3 2026 prints confirming metal margins holding above the 15%+ gross-margin level with the WV mill ramping on schedule.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material relaxation, expiry, or successful legal challenge to Section 232 tariffs, or import share recovering back above ~20%.
- Estimate revisions turning negative for two consecutive quarters alongside compressing metal margins.
- WV mill cost overruns or schedule slippage that impair the medium-term FCF recovery, or a materialized cybersecurity/legal charge.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (late July / early August 2026), which the options market is already pricing an ~8.8% move around. The Q2 print will confirm or challenge whether the Q1 record shipments and margin expansion are sustaining.

**Base case target: ~$260** (~15x forward ~$17.38 EPS), implying ~7% upside from $241. **Bull case: $285** (~18% upside). **Bear case: ~$135–150** (roughly -40%). The consensus median target of $274 sits above our base case and embeds a more optimistic tariff-durability assumption than we are willing to underwrite at these levels.

## Conclusion

Nucor is a high-quality, best-in-class producer executing well through a genuine cyclical recovery. The Q1 2026 results are real, and the estimate-revision velocity confirms consensus is still catching up. The balance sheet is the strongest in the sector, the dividend record is unmatched, capital allocation is disciplined, and the "Expand Beyond" diversification is measurably reducing earnings volatility. These are the ingredients of a company worth owning through the cycle.

The reason for HOLD rather than BUY is price and durability. At ~14x rising forward earnings the stock is fairly valued on cycle-adjusted earnings, not cheap, and the entire margin uplift is levered to a policy-dependent tariff regime that could reverse. With the stock ~10% off its 52-week high, balanced options positioning, and a consensus median target implying ~12% upside, the risk/reward is symmetric here. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$200s or on evidence that the tariff structure is entrenched beyond the current policy cycle. For now, we would hold the position, wait for a better entry, and monitor the Q2 print and trade-policy developments.
