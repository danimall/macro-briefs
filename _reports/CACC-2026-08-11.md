## Executive Summary

Credit Acceptance reported Q2 2026 diluted GAAP EPS of $12.66, up 70.6% year over year, on revenue of $587.4M that rose 0.6%. That gap between revenue and earnings growth is the whole analytical problem with this name. Reconstructing the comparison on a like-for-like basis — adding back the $23.4M legal contingent loss and the $16.5M provision-methodology adjustment that both landed in Q2 2025 — takes prior-year pre-tax income from $117.8M to roughly $157.7M against $168.6M this quarter, or approximately +7% normalized pre-tax growth versus +43.1% reported. The durable improvements are real but modest: average cost of debt fell 40bp to 6.8%, portfolio yield rose 50bp to 27.5%, and expected net loan income per dollar deployed at assignment improved to 44.96% in H1 2026 from 44.03% a year earlier. Beyond that, the earnings line was flattered by a 640bp drop in the effective tax rate (including a first-time R&D credit worth roughly $4.8M in the quarter) and by an 8.8% reduction in diluted shares.

Two things have genuinely improved. First, secured lenders — the counterparties with monthly visibility into collateral performance — cut spreads and extended maturities across three separate facilities in six months: Term ABS 2021-1 from SOFR+220bp to +140bp with the revolving end pushed to January 2028, the revolver from SOFR+197.5bp to +175bp with maturity to June 2029, and Term ABS 2026-1 priced at a 5.2% all-in cost against a 6.8% portfolio average. Second, volume has inflected. Year-over-year unit volume went from a −16.5% trough in Q3 2025 to −1.0% in Q2 2026, with June positive and July reported up 28.0%. That 28% figure sits on a depressed base — indexing forward from July 2024 using quarterly rates as a monthly proxy puts July 2026 roughly 7% above July 2024 — but the direction is not in doubt.

What holds us at HOLD is the combination of what is still marking down and what is entirely unproven. The 2023, 2024 and 2025 vintages each declined again in Q2 2026 (−0.2%, −0.2%, −0.3% versus March 31), and those three vintages carry $8,018.5M, or 68.2%, of the $11,753.7M of total forecasted future net cash flows; management explicitly warns that changes in recent vintages have a disproportionate cash-flow effect. Forecast-change provision worsened to −$39.1M in Q2 from −$9.1M in Q1. Meanwhile the company has replaced its CEO, CFO, CMO and CTO, shrunk the board from six to five with no plan to add, and forfeited 20.7% of its opening RSU pool to executive retirements — at a company whose competitive advantage is a thirty-year statistical scorecard. At 11.8x TTM earnings with 11.1% of shares still authorized for repurchase, the price is not demanding. It is also not cheap enough to pay for a leadership transition and a seasoned book that is still being marked lower.

## Company Overview

Credit Acceptance is a deep-subprime indirect auto lender operating in one reportable segment from Southfield, Michigan. It does not lend to consumers. Dealers originate retail installment contracts and assign them to Credit Acceptance, which then services and collects. In Q2 2026, 79.2% of unit volume carried FICO scores below 650 or no score at all (80.9% for the first half).

The structure that matters is the split between two programs sitting in the same income statement line:

- **Portfolio Program (Dealer Loans).** Credit Acceptance advances cash against a pool of 50 or 100 loans. Collections flow through a waterfall: collection costs, then a servicing fee of generally 20% of collections, then repayment of the advance, then Dealer Holdback. Pools are cross-collateralized across a dealer's other pools. If a vintage underperforms, the dealer absorbs much of the shortfall through forgone Holdback. Dealers also pay a $599 monthly program fee.
- **Purchase Program (Purchased Loans).** A single payment; Credit Acceptance keeps every dollar collected and, in the 10-Q's own words, "is impacted by the full amount of negative variances in Consumer Loan performance." No Holdback buffer.

Ancillary income comes from vehicle service contracts and GAP products sold through third-party providers, a consolidated captive reinsurer (VSC Re, holding $116.6M of restricted securities), and Vehicle Remarketing Services ($3.5M of fees in Q2).

The moat has three components: a proprietary loss-forecasting scorecard that prices each loan to a target economic profit; advance-rate discipline explicitly designed to produce acceptable returns "even if collection rates are less than we initially forecast"; and distribution, at a record 11,004 active dealers with 1,456 newly enrolled in the quarter. No single dealer accounts for 10% or more of revenue or receivables.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 and H1 2026 (filed figures).** Revenue $587.4M (+0.6%) and $1,167.4M (+1.1%). Operating expenses $134.1M (−13.8%) and $275.3M (−5.4%). Provision for credit losses $159.2M (−7.8%) and $298.8M (−10.7%). Interest expense $107.4M (−9.1%) and $215.8M (−7.3%). Net income $135.9M (+55.5%) and $271.7M (+40.3%). Diluted EPS $12.66 and $25.04. Diluted weighted-average shares 10.73M (−8.8%) and 10.85M (−9.8%). Effective tax rate 19.4% versus 25.8%.

The composition of the earnings improvement, ranked by durability:

1. **Interest expense (durable).** Average cost of debt 7.2% → 6.8%, average debt down $231.0M. New paper at 5.2% against a 6.8% average means this trend continues mechanically as the $600M 9.250% 2028 notes approach refinancing.
2. **Portfolio yield (durable).** 27.0% → 27.5% on higher contractual yields in recent assignments.
3. **G&A −$19.1M (not durable).** $23.4M of it is the non-recurrence of a prior-year legal accrual; underlying G&A rose roughly $4.3M on professional services tied to "strategic market analysis initiatives."
4. **Provision on forecast changes, $81.6M versus $101.3M (partly base effect).** Roughly $16.5M of the $19.7M improvement is the non-recurrence of a 2025 methodology adjustment.
5. **Tax rate (mostly not durable).** First-time R&D credit, absence of prior-year state tax law changes, and excess tax benefits from a wave of option exercises tied to executive retirements.

**Balance sheet.** Gross loans receivable $11,608.7M with a $3,649.5M allowance (31.4%) and net loans $7,959.2M. The 31.4% allowance ratio is a CECL presentation artifact — gross receivables accrete finance charges over the loan life while the allowance is set so net carrying value equals the present value of expected net cash flows. Management states outright that GAAP "does not provide sufficient transparency into the economics of our business." Average net loans of $7,953.2M actually declined from $8,011.6M a year earlier; the book is flat to shrinking.

Total debt principal was $6,314.8M ($6,286.2M carrying): $177.8M revolving, $5,019.0M secured financings, $1,089.4M senior notes. Against $1,588.9M of equity that is 3.96x balance-sheet debt/equity — reasonable for a securitized lender, and distinct from the company's covenant-style "funded debt to equity" measure of 4.2x at year-end 2025. Warehouse and Term ABS obligations sit in bankruptcy-remote subsidiaries and are non-recourse to the parent, with the parent collecting a 4% servicing fee on collections. The company was in compliance with all covenants at June 30.

**Liquidity is the sharpest edge on this balance sheet.** Unrestricted cash was $1.4M at June 30, down from $22.8M at December 31, 2025 and $343.7M at December 31, 2024. Management's stated $1.4B of liquidity is almost entirely unused revolver capacity of $1,432.2M, itself down from $1,627.7M six months earlier. Scheduled principal maturities are $1,352.2M for the remainder of 2026, $2,190.0M in 2027 and $1,650.1M in 2028. The majority of ABS "maturities" are revolving-period end dates after which debt amortizes from pledged collateral, so this is not a bullet-refinancing wall. It nonetheless leaves essentially no parent-level buffer if collateral performance tests tighten.

**Cash-flow caution.** H1 2026 operating cash flow of $656.3M versus $485.9M reflects cash taxes of only $13.8M against $183.4M in H1 2025. That timing swing will not repeat and should not be annualized.

**Returns.** Annualizing H1 net income gives ROE of 34.9% on average equity of $1,556.3M and ROA of 6.3% on average assets of $8,627.2M. Those are exceptional numbers for a lender and are the core of any bull case here.

## Growth Analysis

The franchise is not growing; the share count is shrinking. Loans receivable rose 0.8% in the first half and average net loans fell year over year. Revenue growth has been under 1.5%.

Volume, however, has turned. Year-over-year unit volume: Q3'24 +17.7%, Q4'24 +0.3%, Q1'25 −10.1%, Q2'25 −14.6%, Q3'25 −16.5%, Q4'25 −9.1%, Q1'26 −4.3%, Q2'26 −1.0% (dollar volume +0.1%). June returned to growth and July was disclosed at +28.0%. The quality of that recovery is mixed: active dealers hit a record 11,004 (+3.3%) with 1,210 new active dealers (+10.6%), but average volume per active dealer fell 3.8% and volume from dealers active in both periods fell 5.3%. Growth is coming from breadth, not depth.

Unit economics at assignment are the better forward indicator. In H1 2026 the company recorded $2,067.8M of loans receivable against expected net cash flows of $2,997.4M, for expected net loan income of $929.6M — the same absolute expected profit as H1 2025's $929.3M, generated from 2.0% less capital deployed. Expected net loan income per dollar advanced improved from 44.03% to 44.96%.

The composition of that improvement is less comfortable. Purchased Loans rose from 20.5% of unit volume in Q3 2024 to 31.5% in Q2 2026 (21.6% to 34.8% of dollar volume). The 2026 Purchased Loan spread fell to 20.3% from 21.1%, while the Dealer Loan spread improved to 22.4% from 22.1%. Mix is shifting toward the structure with no Holdback buffer, full retention of downside variance, and a narrowing spread. The counterargument is legitimate: Purchased Loans carry higher advance rates (49.9% versus 43.3% for 2026 Dealer Loans) and higher forecasted collection rates (70.2% versus 65.7%), and Credit Acceptance keeps all upside above expectation, whereas Portfolio upside is largely offset by additional Holdback. This may be a considered risk-return choice rather than deterioration. It is not, however, evidence of diversification — dealer concentration disclosure is unchanged.

Consensus EPS of $47.72 for FY2026 and $55.45 for FY2027 implies 16.2% growth, most of which is arithmetic from continued repurchase rather than portfolio expansion. Estimate revisions are essentially flat (+0.8% current year, +1.4% next year over 90 days), well short of any inflection signal, and the four-quarter track record is one beat and three misses, including a −12.6% Q4 2025 miss.

The AI deployment deserves credit as a genuine operating-leverage item: an AI-enabled agent handled 67% of inbound customer service and account solutions calls in June, up from 27% in March, with salaries and wages down 6.7% on lower headcount. A deal-optimization tool was used by 90% of active dealers. If cost-to-serve keeps falling while the book stays flat, that supports the ROE even without volume.

## Valuation Assessment

At $571.64 against 10,376,049 shares, market capitalization is $5,931M.

- **TTM net income:** FY2025 $423.9M + H1'26 $271.7M − H1'25 $193.7M = **$501.9M**. P/E = **11.8x**.
- **FY2026 consensus $47.72:** **12.0x**. **FY2027 consensus $55.45:** **10.3x**.
- **Price/book:** $5,931M ÷ $1,588.9M = **3.73x**. On 34.9% ROE that is internally consistent, not stretched.
- **Fair-value-adjusted book:** loans carried at $7,959.2M carry a disclosed estimated fair value of $9,010.3M; tax-effecting the $1,051.1M excess at 21% federal gives adjusted equity near $2,419M and **~2.45x**. Treat this as illustrative — the fair value is a Level 3 output discounted at the company's own non-GAAP floating yield, and the 21% rate ignores state taxes.

A note on data hygiene: the supplied profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 12.62 against EPS of $36.38, which implies a price near $459 and does not reconcile to the current price. The $36.38 corresponds to FY2025 diluted EPS. We use the aggregate TTM net income calculation above. Third-party revenue figures for this company are also unreliable — one vendor reported "sales" of $415M against GAAP revenue of $587.4M — because CECL inflates the finance-charge line while front-loading provision. The reported GAAP revenue "miss" of roughly $92M versus consensus is close to economically meaningless for the same reason; the non-GAAP EPS of $12.12 versus $11.85 consensus is the comparable pair.

**Against peers**, the Financials median is 12.5x P/E with a 1.2% yield. CACC at 12.0x forward pays no dividend but earns 34.9% ROE against banks and insurers typically in the 10-14% range, and is retiring 8-14% of its shares annually. On a quality-adjusted basis that looks like a discount. The offsetting reasons the discount exists are specific rather than sentimental: earnings visibility depends entirely on a loss-forecasting model whose residual error the company concedes is unmodellable ("we are not able to use any specific credit quality indicators to predict or explain variances"); 68.2% of forecasted future cash flows sit in vintages still being revised down; and the parent holds $1.4M of unrestricted cash.

CACC has also traded in an 8-13x band for years. Twelve times is mid-range for its own history, not a compressed multiple awaiting normalization. The 52-week range of $401.90 to $668.86 — a 66% spread on a business whose book barely moved — shows how violently the multiple reprices on perceived credit direction. Three analysts cover the name, all at HOLD, with a target range of $600-660 and a mean of $637. Targets above price with hold ratings on three-analyst coverage is weak corroboration and we do not weight it heavily.

Our conclusion: modestly undervalued on quality-adjusted metrics, fairly valued once the unpriced binaries are charged for. Valuation score 6.0.

## Competitive Landscape

The 2024-2026 volume cycle is best explained as a capacity cycle in deep-subprime auto. Capital re-entered in 2023-24, compressing available spreads; Credit Acceptance held price and lost volume for seven quarters; as 2022-2023 vintage losses became visible industry-wide, that capital retreated and volume returned.

The filings support this reading. Average consumer loan size fell from $27,242 (2022) to $25,355 (2026), average advance from $12,924 to $11,449, advance rate from 47.4% to 45.2%, while the spread between forecasted collection rate and advance rate widened from 11.9% to 21.9%. That is a company tightening price and terms and accepting the volume loss — the opposite of chasing share.

An alternative explanation cannot be ruled out: management states pricing is set "within the confines of capital constraints," and $1,045.7M went to buybacks over the same period that volume fell. Capital rationing and industry price competition could both be operating.

Competitive trajectory is mixed. Pricing discipline and scorecard performance have improved (2025 and 2026 vintages within 10bp of initial forecast versus 2022's −8.2% miss). Distribution is at a record. But penetration of existing dealers is falling, mix is drifting toward the lower-spread program, and nothing structural prevents capital from re-entering the segment the moment 2025-2026 vintages season well. Score 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Forecast accuracy is the business.** The 2022 vintage missed its initial forecast by 8.2%, compressing spread from roughly 20% to 11.9%. Because advances are non-recourse to the dealer, forecasting error is absorbed, not recovered. Management ranks forecast accuracy first among critical success factors and concedes it cannot attribute variances to observable indicators.

**Seasoned vintages are still marking down.** Q2 2026 declines of 20-30bp across 2023, 2024 and 2025 are small individually but sit against $8,018.5M of forecasted future net cash flows. Forecast-change provision worsened sequentially to −$39.1M from −$9.1M. Write-offs rose 9.5% year over year in H1 to $736.8M. Past-due status: of $11,608.7M gross, $8,111.3M current (69.9%), $1,826.3M 11-90 days past due, $1,413.5M over 90 days. Slower-than-historical prepayments extend duration and, per the filing, historically coincide with tighter consumer credit availability.

**Macro fit is poor.** Deep-subprime auto borrowers are the most sensitive cohort to macro stress. Slower-than-historical prepayments, per the filing, "historically have been lower in periods with less availability of consumer credit," which is consistent with a still-tight credit environment for the subprime borrower. Nothing in the Q2 vintage data suggests deterioration is accelerating; nothing in the provided sources establishes a macro tailwind. [Specific macro indicators — payrolls, the 10-year yield, mortgage rates, core PCE, fuel and repair inflation — are not in the provided sources and have been removed.]

**Settlement is agreed but not consummated.** The New York AG matter and the parallel multi-state investigation are covered by $75.5M cash against an $82.6M accrual — reserves exceed the contemplated payment. Material terms were agreed June 5, the action was dismissed June 6 subject to reopening, and the AG obtained a 45-day extension on July 23 to align with the multi-state resolution. Definitive documentation is unexecuted, and non-monetary terms — injunctive relief, business-practice undertakings, origination or collection restrictions — are not disclosed. Those could matter more than the cash. A separate TCPA class action in E.D. Michigan survived in part on February 4, 2026.

**Leverage with no parent buffer.** $6.3B of debt, $1.59B of equity, $1.4M of unrestricted cash, and revolver availability that fell roughly $195M in six months. Warehouse and ABS facilities contain covenants measuring conveyed-asset performance, so a deep enough collection-rate decline could trip collateral tests independently of parent solvency. We could not reproduce covenant headroom — the fixed-charge and funded-debt definitions are contractual — so remaining repurchase and leverage capacity is unquantified.

**Governance and key-person risk.** Complete C-suite replacement: Vinayak Hegde as CEO succeeding a 20-plus-year incumbent who left the board July 21; a new CFO who signed the Q2 10-Q while his predecessor was still filing Form 4s five weeks earlier; a new CMO from T-Mobile; a CTO to be named; the outgoing Chief Product and Marketing Officer and CTO departing August 14; the board reduced from six to five with no replacement plan; five Item 5.02 8-Ks since October 2025; and 144,543 RSUs (20.7% of the opening pool) forfeited on executive retirements. The company frames this as a digital-first transformation, which the AI results partly corroborate. The risk is that institutional knowledge of a proprietary underwriting model is concentrated in people who have left.

**Also named and non-boilerplate:** dealer concentration in several states (magnitude not retrieved), dependence on foreign engineering personnel subject to immigration restrictions, potential impairment of security interest perfection via electronic contracts, filing-fee exposure from mass individual arbitration demands, and concentrated shareholders (Prescott General Partners at ~8.7%, plus the founding Foss family) with the ability to influence matters requiring shareholder approval.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 47.4% against 40.0% one-year realized, with a 5.9% put skew indicating elevated near-term put demand and a ±6.6% implied move on the August 21 expiry. The term structure is in severe backwardation (47.4% near-term versus 11.8% at six months). We treat the long-dated readings with low confidence: the entire chain is 89 contracts across four expirations, and an 11.8% six-month IV on a stock with 40% realized vol is not a credible market price. Put/call open interest of 0.28 is call-tilted, and the same ratio fell from 0.67 in early June, which argues against a coordinated bearish options build. The honest read is that near-term vol is rich into a thin chain with no scheduled catalyst before August 21 (Q2 already reported), and that the options market is not confirming a directional bearish view.

Short interest at 34.5% of float with 6.0 days to cover is the louder signal. This is a long-standing structural short built on CECL opacity and subprime cyclicality, and the percentage is inflated by concentrated insider and 10%-owner holdings shrinking the effective float. It creates two-sided risk: any clean quarter on vintage data can force a violent rally, and any vintage re-deterioration finds a crowded exit. It is a reason to size positions modestly, not a directional input.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $720 (+26%)

The scorecard fix implemented in Q3 2024 is working: 2025 and 2026 vintages are within 10bp of initial forecast with 21.9% spreads back to 2019-2020 levels. Funding costs decline mechanically as the 9.250% 2028 notes are refinanced toward the 5.2% marginal ABS cost. AI servicing takes cost-to-serve down further from the 67% call-handling rate. Volume growth from a record 11,004 dealers converts the flat book to growth in 2027, restoring net interest income growth on top of the 44.96% expected loan income per dollar deployed. The remaining 11.1% repurchase authorization at prices near book-adjusted 2.5x compounds EPS. Settlement closes with cash inside the existing $82.6M accrual and no material operating restrictions. On FY2027 consensus of $55.45 at 13x — a modest premium to the 12.5x peer median justified by 35% ROE — the stock is $721.

### Bear Case — $430 (−25%)

Sequential vintage markdowns in 2023-2025 continue and accelerate as subprime borrowers absorb rising costs against a tight consumer-credit backdrop. Because those vintages hold 68.2% of forecasted future net cash flows, each incremental point of collection-rate decline flows straight through provision. Purchase Program mix, now 34.8% of dollar volume with no Holdback buffer and a narrowing 20.3% spread, amplifies the effect. Warehouse collateral performance tests tighten, revolver availability compresses further from $1,432M, and with $1.4M of parent cash the buyback stops — removing the primary EPS driver. FY2027 EPS resets toward $45 and the multiple compresses to 9.5x, giving roughly $428, consistent with the $401.90 52-week low. The new management team, six months into the job, has no track record managing a credit downturn at this company.

### Base Case — $610 (+6.7%)

Vintage markdowns continue at 20-30bp per quarter without accelerating; volume growth persists at high single digits on a normalized two-year stack rather than the headline 28%; funding costs decline 30-40bp over twelve months; the buyback continues but slows as revolver capacity is preserved; settlement closes within accrual. FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $55.45, and the market pays 11.0x — a discount to the 12.5x peer median for credit-cycle and earnings-quality opacity, supported by the ROE and share shrinkage. That is $610.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives an expected return of roughly +2%. That is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain — the leadership transition and forecast-model dependency prevent a confident directional call in either direction).

- **Base case: $610** (11.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $55.45), +6.7% from $571.64.
- **Bull case: $720** (13.0x $55.45), +26.0%.
- **Bear case: $430** (9.5x a reset $45), −24.8%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026) and the Q4/FY2026 report (February 2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters in which the 2023, 2024 and 2025 forecasted collection rates are flat or higher sequentially, with quarterly forecast-change provision inside −$20M. This is the single most decisive data point in the filing and is disclosed every quarter in Note 6.
2. Unit volume growth sustained above 10% year over year through Q4 2026 *with* volume from dealers active in both periods turning positive — confirming penetration rather than dealer-count arithmetic.
3. A price below $480, which would put the stock near 8.7x FY2027 consensus and roughly 2.0x fair-value-adjusted book, a margin of safety sufficient to absorb one full vintage re-marking cycle.
4. Open-market purchases by the incoming CEO, CFO or CTO. There have been none from any officer or director in the transactions reviewed.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Quarterly forecast-change provision worse than −$75M, or any single-quarter decline of 0.5% or more in the 2024 or 2025 vintage forecasted collection rate.
2. Suspension or material slowdown of repurchases without a corresponding increase in loan volume, or revolver availability falling below roughly $1.1B — either would indicate capital or covenant capacity is binding.
3. Settlement documentation disclosing non-monetary terms that restrict origination practices, advance rates, or collection methods, or a settlement figure materially above the $82.6M accrual.
4. Purchased Loans exceeding 40% of dollar volume with the 2027 Purchased Loan spread below 19%.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediate checkpoints are the expiry of the New York AG's 45-day extension (early September 2026) and the announcement of the new CTO in late August. The substantive reassessment is the Q3 2026 10-Q in late October, which will provide the third consecutive quarter of vintage data under the current scorecard and the first full quarter under the new operating team.

## Conclusion

Credit Acceptance is a high-return, high-opacity lender priced at approximately 11.8x trailing net income and 10.3x FY2027 consensus, earning 34.9% ROE, with 11.1% of its shares authorized for repurchase and a funding curve that its own secured lenders have repriced favorably three times in six months. Those are real positives, and they explain why the stock is up 25% over twelve months despite a flat loan book.

The reasons not to pay up are equally specific. Reported earnings growth of 55.5% normalizes to roughly 7% pre-tax; roughly 16 of the 70.6 points of diluted EPS growth came from a share count reduced by repurchases funded partly by drawing parent cash from $343.7M to $1.4M over eighteen months. The vintages carrying 68.2% of forecasted future cash flows all marked down again in the quarter, and the forecast-change provision worsened sequentially. Mix is drifting toward the program where the company retains 100% of credit downside at a narrowing spread. And the entire senior operating team plus a board seat has turned over at a business whose only durable edge is a statistical model built by the people who left, with a settlement whose non-monetary terms are still undisclosed and a 34.5% short base ready to move violently on either resolution.

Expected return from $571.64 is approximately fair. Existing holders have no reason to sell into a book that is repricing better on the funding side and a volume line that has inflected. New capital has no urgency: the two conditions that would change the calculus — two clean quarters of vintage data, or a price near $480 — are both observable and dated. **HOLD.**
