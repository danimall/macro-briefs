**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TKO Group Holdings owns and operates UFC and WWE, two fully-controlled live-sports IP franchises, plus a lower-margin sports-marketing and hospitality arm (IMG / On Location / PBR). The core assets are among the highest-quality media properties in existence: UFC posted a 63% Adjusted EBITDA margin in Q1'26 and WWE 54%, both because TKO owns the entire value chain (talent, production, promotion) rather than running a franchise revenue-share model. Live combat sports and wrestling are DVR-proof, ad-friendly content that streamers are bidding aggressively to acquire, and TKO has converted that scarcity into a $16.6B contracted revenue backlog laddered through 2030+. The new media era — UFC on Paramount (reported ~$7.7B over seven years), WWE Raw on Netflix, WWE premium events on ESPN — began contributing in Q1'26 and drove revenue +26% YoY to $1.597B and Adjusted EBITDA +32% to $549.8M.

The bull case on business quality is not in dispute. Three separate issues temper the rating. First, the UFC antitrust cluster is structural, not resolved: after the $375M *Le* settlement (paid June 2025), a widening set of suits (*Johnson*, *Cirkunovs*, *Davis* — motion to dismiss denied March 31, 2026 — and the new February 2026 *Costantino* consumer class action) targets the fighter-pay model that generates UFC's margins. Second, TKO Group Holdings (the public Class A entity) owns only 39.2% of the operating company, with ~64% of net income accruing to Silver Lake/Endeavor-held non-controlling interests, and a new derivative suit (*Jordan*, March 2026) alleges the IMG acquisition was conflicted self-dealing. Third, consensus EPS estimates were cut roughly 16% over the trailing 90 days and the company has missed in three of its last four quarters, undercutting confidence in near-term visibility even as the topline accelerates.

The stock has de-rated ~21% from its February high of $226.94 to ~$179, and roughly 12% since our prior review at ~$204. Recent-window fundamentals have stabilized (30-day estimate revisions are roughly flat), the CEO, COO and CFO bought stock on the open market in May, and the price now sits below the lowest published analyst target of $185. That combination improves risk/reward at the margin. The open-ended legal exposure, the premium whole-company multiple, and the price-insensitive capital-allocation record (an $800M accelerated repurchase executed at ~$255) keep this a HOLD with a growth orientation rather than a BUY.

## Company Overview

TKO was formed in September 2023 by combining UFC (Endeavor's Zuffa) with WWE, and in February 2025 acquired IMG, On Location and Professional Bull Riders from Endeavor for ~$3.3B in an all-equity, common-control transaction. The company monetizes its IP through media rights and content, live events and hospitality, partnerships and marketing, and consumer-products licensing, organized in three reportable segments plus Corporate/Other:

- **UFC** — the crown jewel. Q1'26 revenue $401M, Adjusted EBITDA $255M (63% margin). Owned IP with no league revenue-share, making it extraordinarily profitable.
- **WWE** — Q1'26 revenue $476M, Adjusted EBITDA $256M (54% margin, up from 50%), with recurring weekly TV content.
- **IMG / On Location** — Q1'26 revenue $655M but Adjusted EBITDA only $97M (15% margin). Low-margin, working-capital-intensive, and cyclical (Olympics, FIFA World Cup, Serie A).
- **Corporate & Other** — PBR plus the Zuffa Boxing JV with Saudi Arabia's Sela; a net drag of roughly $58M/quarter.

The competitive edge is ownership of non-fungible live-sports IP with control of the full value chain, driving segment margins far above traditional media peers. UFC has no scaled competitor; WWE's nearest rival, AEW, is a distant second in wrestling.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** The headline FY2025 revenue figure (~$4.7B vs ~$4.9B in FY2024 on a recast basis) looks like a decline but is entirely a function of On Location's Olympics cyclicality — 2024 was a Paris Olympics year. The underlying UFC and WWE segments grew double digits: in Q1'26, UFC revenue rose ~12% and WWE ~22%. Q1'26 was the first clean quarter with new media deals live, with net income $249.8M and diluted EPS $1.12 (vs $0.69).

**Backlog.** Remaining performance obligations ladder through 2030+ (~$2.7B for the remainder of 2026, $3.3B in 2027, $3.1B in 2028, $2.7B in 2029). For a media business, this visibility is exceptional and directly reflects the new long-term rights contracts.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$4.64B (First Lien Term Loan due November 2031, floating at SOFR+2.00%), against ~$789M cash. In March 2026 the company added a $900M incremental term loan and upsized the revolver to $350M, using proceeds largely to fund buybacks. The Altman Z-Score screening TKO as "distress" (~1.2) is a false signal driven by the enormous goodwill ($8.4B) and intangible ($3.2B) base and NCI-heavy equity from the acquisitions. The covenant structure is light — an 8.25x first-lien leverage covenant that only springs if revolver draw exceeds ~$140M, currently untested. Interest expense is rising (Q1'26 $60.6M, +35% YoY) on the added debt and floating rates, with a ~$46M annual sensitivity to a 1% rate move; in the higher-for-longer environment this is a genuine incremental headwind, though modest against annual EBITDA.

**Cash flow optics.** Q1'26 operating cash flow surged to $694.5M from $162.8M, but roughly $582M of that is a restricted-cash inflow of FIFA World Cup 2026 hospitality collections held on behalf of third parties. Normalized free cash flow is materially lower than the headline; investors should back this pass-through out when modeling.

**Capital allocation.** Aggressively shareholder-friendly and price-insensitive. The dividend doubled to $0.78/quarter, a $2.0B buyback program was supplemented by an incremental $1.0B authorized May 6, 2026, and the company executed an $800M accelerated share repurchase in March 2026 at an average ~$255/share — well above today's ~$179. Q1'26 alone saw $838M of repurchases, $58M of dividends, and $91M of distributions to Endeavor's NCI. Debt was raised to fund returns rather than growth. The willingness to lever up to buy back stock near cyclical highs is a mark against capital-allocation discipline.

**Ownership structure.** TKO Group Holdings owns only 39.2% of TKO OpCo; Endeavor/Silver Lake owns 60.8% via Class B / OpCo units. As a result, ~64% of net income accrues to non-controlling interests ($160M of $250M in Q1'26). Diluted share count is ~194.6M versus ~77M Class A basic. Valuation must be done on a fully-diluted, whole-enterprise basis, which makes the effective multiple considerably richer than a Class A per-share screen implies.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on the new media-rights cohort now ramping. UFC's Paramount deal became effective January 2026 (reported roughly double competing bids); WWE's Netflix (Raw) and ESPN (premium live events) deals are contributing. These drove the revenue quarter and are the primary support for the backlog. Early engagement data is encouraging but unproven — a UFC event on Paramount drew a reported 7M initial U.S. viewers (34M globally), and the market will watch whether the streaming-forward model sustains reach and drives subscriber acquisition, which is the proxy for eventual renewal leverage.

Two facts complicate the growth score. First, consensus estimates were cut sharply over 90 days: current-quarter EPS -20.5%, current-year -15.8%, next-year -15.5%. That is genuine deterioration in forward expectations, not price noise. The encouraging offset is that the last 30 days have essentially flatlined (-0.4% CY, -0.2% NY), suggesting the cuts have run their course rather than compounding. Second, execution has been erratic — three misses in the last four quarters, including a small Q1'26 miss ($1.12 vs $1.20) and a large Q4'25 miss. IMG/On Location's cyclicality contributes to this lumpiness, but repeated misses lower the confidence one can place in the current consensus.

The EPS estimate revision profile does NOT meet the strong-BUY signal (rising estimates >30% over 90 days). Here the 90-day revision trend is negative, only recently stabilizing. Growth prospects are therefore solid but not accelerating in the way that would warrant maximum weight — the topline is strong, the backlog is real, but the earnings translation has been inconsistent.

## Valuation Assessment

TKO trades at ~$179, giving a Class A market cap of ~$13.1B. The trailing/forward P/E of 66.6x on Class A EPS ($2.26) overstates richness because Class A captures only ~39% of OpCo economics. On consensus whole-company estimates ($4.47 current year, $4.92 next year), the multiple is closer to ~36-40x forward, with a PEG of roughly 1.5 against ~26% revenue growth. That is a premium multiple, but for a scarce-IP annuity with 54-63% core-segment margins and multi-year contracted visibility, it is defensible rather than egregious.

Peer context is muddied because most Movies & Entertainment comparables carry negative or distorted P/Es (PSKY -16.6x, MSGS -425x, LYV -97x) reflecting their own accounting and cyclicality; the cleaner comparables trade at 26-34x (CNK 26.4x, WMG 33.7x, peer median 33.7x). TKO's whole-company multiple sits above that median, justified by materially higher margins and growth but leaving little room for disappointment.

The specific mispricing argument is that the stock trades below the lowest published analyst target ($185) against a mean of $233 (~30% implied upside) and a high of $275. Analyst targets function as floors only when a stock is cheap relative to improving estimates; here estimates were cut over 90 days, which is why the price and the (still elevated) targets have converged rather than the price simply lagging optimism. The insider cluster buy at $184-190 in May provides a real-money support level from the people with the best information.

On balance, the valuation offers modest asymmetry — the de-rating looks to have overshot near-term fundamentals, and the sub-floor price plus insider buying give downside support — but the premium whole-company multiple, the NCI structure, and the execution misses prevent this from being a high-conviction value case. The valuation score reflects a business fairly-to-slightly-attractively priced today, not a clear bargain.

## Competitive Landscape

TKO's moat is genuinely wide and, operationally, intact. UFC has no scaled competitor (PFL is far smaller), and the antitrust suits themselves underscore its monopsony-adjacent dominance over fighter labor. WWE dominates wrestling, with AEW a distant second. The structural tailwind is durable: live sports is the last must-watch, DVR-proof, ad-friendly content, and streamers (Netflix, Paramount+, ESPN's DTC) are competing for scarce large-scale U.S. rights to acquire and retain subscribers. The Paramount deal, reported at roughly 2x competing bids, demonstrates that TKO's IP is a strategic asset buyers will overpay for.

The competitive-position trajectory has moderated from prior reviews (previously scored 9.0, then 7.5) for two defensible reasons. First, the antitrust litigation threatens the economic engine of the moat: UFC's margins depend on a fighter-pay model now under legal attack. A structurally adverse outcome would not remove UFC's market dominance but would compress the economics that make it so valuable. Second, media concentration cuts both ways — today's premium rights valuations are being set during a streaming land-grab, and the eventual need to re-negotiate at or above these levels is the key long-term cyclical risk. If bidding intensity cools by the time these deals renew, growth could plateau. Neither issue reflects share loss today, so the position remains strong, but the risk-adjusted trajectory is no longer strengthening.

## Risk Assessment

**UFC antitrust cluster (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** The *Le* settlement did not end the problem. A widening set of copycat suits alleges monopsony over fighter pay: *Johnson* (damages, in discovery), *Cirkunovs* (challenging arbitration/class-waiver clauses), *Davis* (injunctive; motion to dismiss denied March 31, 2026), and the *Costantino* consumer class action alleging UFC inflated prices for PPV buyers and Paramount+ subscribers. Aggregate exposure is open-ended, and because the fighter-pay model is the source of UFC's ~63% margins, an adverse structural outcome would strike the core economics. This is the risk a financial screen misses entirely.

**Governance and related-party risk.** Silver Lake controls TKO (~64% of voting power via its take-private of Endeavor). TKO is a "controlled company," limiting minority governance leverage. The *Jordan* derivative suit alleges the IMG acquisition was conflicted self-dealing benefiting Endeavor/Silver Lake at minority-holder expense, naming the entire board and Moelis. The Delaware stockholder merger litigation over the original TKO Transactions goes to trial June 2026, though TKO/WWE/EGH have been dropped as defendants (exposure now to individual directors).

**WWE/McMahon legacy litigation.** Multiple sexual-misconduct suits tied to founder Vince McMahon (resigned January 2024) and a former ring announcer carry headline and financial tail risk.

**Media concentration and renewal risk.** The bull case rests on a handful of mega-contracts. Concentration and the need to re-negotiate at or above elevated levels is the key long-term cyclical risk.

**Financial risks.** Floating-rate debt with rising interest expense; IMG/On Location cyclicality and large working-capital swings (the ~$937M restricted cash for FIFA is pass-through, not profit).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 42.1% is rich versus 32.7% one-year historical volatility (a 9.4pp spread) and sits in the 69th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (42.1% near-term vs 36.5% at six months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst — the next earnings report is due in early August. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.1% near-term with an implied move of ±9.6%. Since early June, ATM IV rose 2.9pp while the price fell 11.7% and the put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.11 to 0.65. Short interest is elevated at 12.8% of float (5.2 days to cover). The composite reads as institutional hedging/bearish positioning into the print, consistent with the erratic earnings track record. This does not override the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($260-275)

TKO owns two of the most valuable, non-fungible live-sports franchises in the world, generating 54-63% core-segment EBITDA margins with a $16.6B contracted backlog through 2030+. The new media cohort (Paramount, Netflix, ESPN) is just beginning to contribute and drove +26% revenue and +32% Adjusted EBITDA in Q1'26. As the streaming shift proves it sustains reach and drives subscriber acquisition, renewal leverage compounds and estimates stabilize then rise. Estimate cuts have already flattened over 30 days; a clean beat re-rates the stock back toward analyst targets ($233 mean, $275 high). The May open-market buy cluster from the CEO, COO and CFO at $184-190 signals management sees value below current levels. On these assumptions, the whole-company multiple holds at ~35-40x on rising forward EPS, supporting $260-275.

### Bear Case ($130-150)

The antitrust cluster produces a structurally adverse outcome or another large settlement, forcing a fighter-pay model change that compresses UFC's ~63% margin, the single most important driver of enterprise value. Estimate cuts resume as IMG cyclicality and inconsistent execution (three misses in four quarters) persist, and the premium multiple compresses toward the peer median (~33x) on lower forward EPS. Rising floating-rate interest expense and price-insensitive debt-funded buybacks at ~$255 look increasingly like value destruction as the stock trades at $179. Governance overhang from the *Jordan* derivative suit and Silver Lake control weighs on the multiple. This combination takes the stock to $130-150, roughly 16-27% below current levels.

### Base Case ($195-205)

The media-deal ramp continues to deliver double-digit UFC/WWE growth, estimates stabilize near current levels, and the antitrust litigation grinds on without a near-term structural resolution but also without a catastrophic ruling. The stock recovers part of its de-rating as the market prices the backlog and margin durability, but the multiple stays capped by legal and governance overhangs. This supports ~$195-205 on a 12-month view, roughly 9-14% above the current price plus a ~1.7% dividend yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).** The 12-month thesis is positive — media-deal ramp, backlog realization, and a plausible estimate-stabilization-then-recovery path — but the open-ended antitrust exposure, the controlled-company structure, and inconsistent execution keep this short of a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of consensus EPS estimate stabilization or upward revision (the 30-day trend is already roughly flat; sustained positive revisions would confirm the turn), combined with a Q2/Q3 earnings beat.
- A favorable or bounded resolution in the UFC antitrust cluster (e.g., a *Davis*/*Costantino* dismissal, or a settlement whose terms leave the fighter-pay economics substantially intact).
- A pullback toward the mid-$150s that widens the margin of safety while fundamentals remain intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse structural ruling in the antitrust suite that credibly threatens UFC's fighter-pay economics, or an unbounded settlement.
- Resumption of estimate cuts (another leg of -10%+ over 90 days) alongside a fourth miss, signaling the media-deal ramp is failing to convert to earnings.
- Signs of governance escalation from the *Jordan* derivative suit or further conflicted related-party transactions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report in early August (options market pricing a ±9.6% move), followed by the June 2026 Delaware trial and ongoing antitrust docket updates.

## Conclusion

TKO is a genuinely high-quality business — scarce, DVR-proof live-sports IP with 54-63% core-segment margins and a $16.6B contracted backlog — trading at a defensible but full whole-company multiple after a ~21% de-rating from its February high. The recent decline came against stabilizing near-term fundamentals and drew open-market buying from the CEO, COO and CFO, which improves risk/reward and places the price below the lowest published analyst target. Those are the ingredients of a constructive setup.

The rating stays at HOLD rather than BUY because of the combination of a structural, open-ended UFC antitrust cluster that directly targets the source of UFC's margins; a controlled-company structure where public holders own only 39% of the operating economics and a new derivative suit alleges conflicted dealing; a 90-day stretch of ~16% estimate cuts against three misses in four quarters; and a capital-allocation record that included an $800M repurchase at ~$255 while the stock now trades at $179. The overall score of ~6.0 marks a modest improvement over the prior 5.8, reflecting the better entry point and insider signal, but the legal and governance overhangs cap conviction. Own it for the growth thesis and the backlog-backed compounding, but size for the tail risk in the litigation and reassess after the August print.
