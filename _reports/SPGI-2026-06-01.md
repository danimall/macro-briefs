**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

S&P Global is one of the highest-quality franchises in the public markets: a ratings/index/energy-data oligopolist with 68–72% margins in its two crown-jewel segments, ~48% recurring-subscription revenue, minimal capex, and ~$919M of quarterly free cash flow. Q1 2026 was strong across the board: revenue +10% to $4,171M, all five segments growing, diluted EPS +32% to $4.69 (aided by buybacks), and ETF AUM linked to S&P indices up 25% to $5.39 trillion. The business is being actively high-graded through the planned mid-2026 Mobility spin-off (removing the lowest-margin 21% segment) and a series of sub-scale software divestitures, while bolting on AI capabilities (Enertel). At $426.73, the stock sits roughly 26% below its August 2025 high of $579 and trades at 19.2x next-year consensus EPS of $22.20, a discount to its own multi-year range and to data-franchise peers like MCO (32.4x), MSCI (34.5x), and NDAQ (27.3x).

The bull case is well-articulated and largely correct: this is a quality compounder that has de-rated alongside a broader Financials/data pullback, and the recent open-market insider purchases by CEO Cheung (~$1M), the Indices CEO (~$1.08M), and a director (~$500K) are a genuine, rare signal at a mega-cap. The fundamentals are not deteriorating; the price decline reflects multiple compression rather than estimate cuts.

The reason I hold rather than buy comes down to two specific, falsifiable concerns that cluster in the near term. First, on May 26, 2026, Saugata Saha — President of Market Intelligence and Chief Enterprise Data Officer — announced his departure (effective July 30), during the active strategic reshaping of the segment most exposed to AI substitution. Losing the leader of the slowest-growing, most-threatened business mid-restructuring is concrete execution risk. Second, the options market is pricing imminent stress: near-term ATM IV of 39.9% against 27.6% realized vol, term-structure backwardation (39.9% near vs 29.3% at six months), an 8% put skew, and put/call OI of 1.51 rising from 1.23 intraday. That combination signals informed positioning ahead of a near-term event into a window where there is no clean positive catalyst until the spin completes. The risk/reward is favorable on a 12-month horizon but not asymmetric enough today to clear the elevated BUY bar.

## Company Overview

S&P Global provides benchmarks, data, analytics, and workflow tools across capital markets, energy/commodities, and automotive. It runs five reportable segments (Q1 2026):

| Segment | Q1'26 Revenue | Op. Margin | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ratings | $1,302M | 68% | Credit ratings duopoly; issuance-driven, cyclical |
| Market Intelligence | $1,296M | 34% | Capital IQ Pro, data feeds; most AI-exposed |
| Energy | $652M | 44% | Platts price benchmarks, CERAWeek |
| Indices | $519M | 72% | S&P 500/DJIA; asset-linked ETF fees |
| Mobility | $454M | 21% | Automotive data — being spun off mid-2026 |

The moat is genuine and multi-source. Ratings is an effective duopoly with Moody's, protected by NRSRO regulatory barriers and reputational incumbency, and acts as a structural toll on global debt issuance. Indices owns the S&P 500 and DJIA brands and rides the secular shift to passive investing. Platts is the dominant energy price benchmark. These are quasi-toll-road businesses with high switching costs and pricing power. The 2022 IHS Markit merger (~$44B) added scale to Market Intelligence and Energy but loaded the balance sheet with goodwill and intangibles.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 was a high-quality quarter. Operating profit rose 27% to $2,002M, lifting margin to 48% from 42%, though that includes a $175M disposition gain; excluding one-offs, operating profit grew ~12%. Net income to SPGI was $1,395M, helped by a reduction in share count from 307.7M to 297.6M. Free cash flow was up 13% YoY on capex of only ~$27M, reflecting the asset-light economics that define this business.

Segment detail matters here. Ratings, the profit engine, grew revenue 13% on a 41% jump in investment-grade billed issuance ($621B vs $440B), much of it financing AI/data-center capex and M&A. Bank-loan ratings declined on "AI-disruption concerns affecting software and tech-adjacent leveraged loans," the same macro force helping and hurting different parts of the franchise. Indices grew 17%, the most operating-leveraged business. Market Intelligence grew only ~8% (reported 34% margin inflated by a one-time gain; organic growth is mid-single-digit), and it is both the structural laggard and the segment most exposed to AI substitution.

Balance sheet: total debt $13.3B, cash $1.8B, long-term debt $10.6B. The dominant feature is $36.4B goodwill plus $16.0B intangibles, totaling $52.4B of $60.8B assets — the IHS Markit legacy. This is why the Altman Z-Score sits near 2.0 (grey zone), which is a false negative for credit risk: with 48% operating margins, $919M quarterly FCF, minimal capex, and a single financial covenant (debt/cash flow ≤ 4:1) that has "never been exceeded," bankruptcy risk is negligible. Capital returns are aggressive: $1.0B buyback in Q1'26 via ASR at $426.70, $288M in dividends (raised to $0.97/quarter), and a new 30M-share (~10%) authorization from November 2025. Commercial paper rose to $951M, so buybacks are partly leverage-financed — fine given the FCF profile, but worth monitoring.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent (Q1'26) a 1.3% beat ($4.97 vs $4.91 estimate).

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth drivers are durable: secular debt-issuance growth flowing through Ratings, the active-to-passive shift and rising global ETF AUM flowing through Indices, and the high-graded margin profile that emerges after Mobility (21% margin) is removed. Management guided to ~9% organic growth and margin expansion, trimming FY guidance only for FX.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive. Next-year EPS has been revised up +0.6% over both 30 and 90 days to $22.20; current-year is essentially flat at $19.62. This is steady, not accelerating — far from the +30% revision velocity that signals a market systematically underestimating an acceleration. The growth case here is durability and quality, not a sudden acceleration the market has missed.

The two-sided AI dynamic is the key forward variable. On the tailwind side, AI-infrastructure financing is driving record IG issuance through Ratings. On the headwind side, AI threatens to commoditize Market Intelligence's data/analytics, and the 10-K dedicates unusual space to warning that the company's data-protection approach may prove "inadequate" as third parties use SPGI data with AI tools to "supplant our products and services." Market Intelligence is ~31% of revenue, so durable mid-single-digit growth there caps the consolidated rate even as Ratings and Indices compound faster.

## Valuation Assessment

At $426.73 on next-year EPS of $22.20, SPGI trades at 19.2x forward (26.8x trailing on $14.66 reported EPS, which is depressed by below-the-line items relative to adjusted figures). Against the peer set, this is a discount: MCO 32.4x, MSCI 34.5x, NDAQ 27.3x, ICE 21.8x, CME 23.9x, with a peer median of 27.3x. SPGI's mix is at least as high-quality as MCO's (it adds the dominant index franchise and Platts to a comparable ratings duopoly), yet it trades meaningfully cheaper than MCO. The de-rating is driven by multiple compression rather than estimate cuts; the fundamentals that produced the August $579 print are intact or improved.

The discount has identifiable reasons rather than being a pure mispricing: (1) Market Intelligence's AI-substitution overhang, which the market is stress-testing across the data complex; (2) Mobility spin-off execution uncertainty; (3) the CME redemption feature on the Indices business. These are real, but at 19.2x forward for a franchise of this quality, a fair amount of that concern is priced. A re-rating toward 24–25x forward (still below MCO/MSCI) on $22.20 implies $533–555, consistent with the analyst mean target of $534 and median of $540. The valuation is attractive on a 12-month view; it is not so cheap that the downside is trivial, which is the relevant test for the BUY bar.

## Competitive Landscape

Ratings is a structurally advantaged duopoly with Moody's; barriers (NRSRO status, reputation, decades of rating history) are among the highest in financial services. Indices is a dominant franchise competing with MSCI, FTSE Russell, and Nasdaq, benefiting from the passive shift, though the CME 27% ownership caps SPGI's economic share. Platts leads energy price benchmarking against Argus and ICE.

The competitive trajectory is mixed by segment, which is why I bring the competitive score down from the prior 8.5 to 8.0. Ratings and Indices positions are stable-to-improving. Market Intelligence faces the most credible deterioration risk: it competes with Bloomberg, FactSet, Moody's Analytics, and increasingly AI-native entrants and clients self-sourcing data. The strategic response — divesting sub-scale software (EDM/thinkFolio, the SLB geoscience portfolio) and bolting on AI (Enertel) — is sensible, but the segment's organic growth is decelerating relative to the rest of the company, and the departure of its president mid-reshaping raises the odds of a slower turnaround.

## Risk Assessment

This is where the case for restraint concentrates, and why the risk score sits at 5.5.

**Market Intelligence leadership gap (concrete, dated).** Saugata Saha's departure during the active reshaping of the AI-most-exposed segment is execution and continuity risk with a specific date attached. The stock fell on the news.

**AI substitution.** The 10-K's expansive AI section is the dominant emerging risk: AI plus off-the-shelf models can replicate Market Intelligence data/analytics cheaply, and third parties can use SPGI's own data to supplant its products. This is a multi-year structural threat to ~31% of revenue.

**CME redemption feature.** CME owns 27% of S&P Dow Jones Indices with the right to put at least 20% of its stake to SPGI at fair value at any time, and the full stake within 15 days of a change of control. This $4.9B redeemable NCI sits outside equity and is a contingent cash claim on the crown-jewel franchise.

**Mobility spin execution.** Form 10 filed May 7, board named May 8, $2.5B Mobility-entity financing arranged in May 2026. The spin may not complete on timeline, may not be tax-free, and could divert management. The catalyst is real but binary in timing.

**Ratings cyclicality and regulation.** A large slice of Ratings is transaction-based and tied to issuance volumes, rates, spreads, and M&A. Heavy regulation (Dodd-Frank, SEC NRSRO, ESMA, FCA), with ongoing SEC compliance communication noted in the 10-Q (routine) and an open pre-2008 Australian CDO class action.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress, which directly informs the HOLD. Near-term ATM IV is rich at the 71st percentile within the chain, and the term structure is in backwardation, which typically signals a near-term event. Put skew is elevated at 8%, put/call OI rose to 1.51 from 1.23 in a single intraday window, and the one-month tenor shows a P/C volume ratio of 3.34. This is the market positioning for downside into the next few weeks. It does not override the fundamental case, but on a stock I would otherwise size as a BUY, this much put demand and IV richness argues for patience rather than chasing into the event window.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($533–575).** SPGI re-rates toward data-franchise peers as the Mobility spin completes mid-2026, lifting consolidated margins and refocusing the story on the 68–72% ratings/index moat. Ratings rides continued AI-infrastructure debt issuance, Indices compounds on ETF AUM growth, the insider purchases prove well-timed, and Market Intelligence stabilizes under new leadership. 24–25x forward on rising estimates gets to $533–555; the Street high of $575 assumes a fuller re-rating.

**Bear Case ($360–390).** AI substitution begins to show in Market Intelligence organic growth, the leadership gap slows the turnaround, Ratings issuance softens as the AI-capex financing wave matures (the bank-loan softness is an early tell), and the spin disappoints or slips. Multiple compresses toward 16–18x forward, implying the high-$300s — roughly 9–16% downside from current.

The asymmetry on a 12-month view favors the upside, but the near-term downside is real enough that the setup is favorable rather than asymmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth-oriented HOLD** with a 12-month horizon centered on the mid-2026 Mobility spin and subsequent Market Intelligence stabilization.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Mobility spin completes cleanly and tax-free, confirming the high-graded margin profile, with the stock still below ~24x forward.
- Market Intelligence organic growth re-accelerates above high-single-digits OR a credible new segment leader is named with a clear AI strategy, removing the continuity overhang.
- Near-term options stress resolves (IV term structure normalizes from backwardation, put/call OI falls back below ~1.2) with the price still in the low-$400s, confirming the event passed without fundamental damage.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Market Intelligence organic growth turns negative or a guidance cut signals AI substitution is materializing in revenue.
- Spin-off is abandoned or delayed beyond 2026, or completes in a non-tax-free structure that destroys value.
- Ratings issuance contraction (IG billed issuance turning negative YoY) signaling the cyclical tailwind has reversed.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026) and the Mobility spin milestones over the summer.

## Conclusion

S&P Global is a wide-moat compounder trading at a defensible discount to its history and to data-franchise peers, with a real catalyst (the Mobility spin), durable double-digit profit growth in its best segments, and a rare insider-buying signal. On fundamentals alone this is close to a BUY. I hold rather than buy because of a specific, near-term risk cluster that the system's elevated quality bar should respect: the May 26 departure of the Market Intelligence president during the AI-most-exposed segment's reshaping, and an options market actively pricing imminent stress into a window with no clean upside catalyst until the spin completes mid-year. The 12-month risk/reward is favorable, but the entry is better characterized as patient accumulation through the spin than as an asymmetric buy today. I expect to upgrade to BUY on spin completion or a stabilization in Market Intelligence, particularly if the stock is still in the low-$400s when the near-term options stress resolves.
