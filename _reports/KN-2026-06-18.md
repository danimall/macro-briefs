**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Knowles is a misunderstood security in the standardized data layer and a clean, profitable specialty-components business underneath it. The pre-collected market feed tags it "Information Technology / Electronic Components" and shows a -$237.8M net loss in 2024 with revenue "falling" from $765M to $554M. Those figures reflect the December 2024 divestiture of the consumer MEMS microphone business and a one-time goodwill impairment, not the operating reality. On a continuing-operations basis, the company generated FY2025 revenue of $593.2M (+7.2%), adjusted EBIT margins of ~20%, and adjusted free cash flow of $114.4M (19.3% of revenue). Q1 2026 accelerated to +15.8% revenue growth with 350bps of gross-margin expansion. This reflects a working five-year pivot out of volatile consumer hardware into higher-margin, stickier defense, industrial, electrification, and medtech end markets.

The investment debate has shifted. Twelve months ago the question was whether the turnaround was real; the answer is yes. The question now is whether the re-rating is full. The stock has more than doubled from its 2025 lows near $17 to roughly $40-41, a 137% gain over twelve months, lifting it to ~56x trailing GAAP EPS ($0.58) and ~32x the current $1.29 consensus FY EPS. The current price sits above every published analyst target — the consensus high is $39, the mean $36.25, and the low $33 — against a last price of ~$41.5. Estimate revisions are positive (+4-6% over 90 days for the current and next years) and the business is genuinely compounding, but a stock trading above its entire analyst target range with a ~32x forward multiple offers limited near-term asymmetry.

We rate Knowles a HOLD with a constructive bias toward the underlying business. The balance sheet is clean (sub-1x net leverage, seller note repaid, falling interest expense), the buyback engine retires ~5% of the float annually, and both segments are accelerating into structural tailwinds. The price discounts much of the success that has already been delivered, and the most likely path is digestion rather than further rapid re-rating. We would add on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $30s, where the forward multiple compresses toward 25x and the analyst range becomes a floor rather than a ceiling.

## Company Overview

Knowles (founded 1946, headquartered in Itasca, IL, ~5,200 employees across 11 countries) manufactures specialty electronic components that perform critical functions in demanding applications. Following the sale of its consumer MEMS microphone business to Syntiant, the company runs two segments:

- **Precision Devices (PD) — 55% of FY2025 revenue ($328.9M).** High-performance ceramic and film capacitors plus RF/microwave filters serving defense, industrial, medtech, and electrification/energy markets. The November 2023 acquisition of Cornell Dubilier (~$247M) added film-capacitor capability now being expanded into power and energy applications. Adjusted EBIT margin ~19% and rising.
- **MedTech & Specialty Audio (MSA) — 45% ($264.3M).** Balanced-armature speakers and microphones for hearing aids and premium audio. This is the margin anchor at ~38-41% adjusted EBIT margins.

The competitive structure differs by segment. PD competes in a fragmented capacitor/filter market against Kyocera AVX, Yageo/Kemet, and Vishay, with lower pricing pressure and a reliability-premium moat. MSA operates in an effective duopoly with Sonion for hearing-aid balanced-armature components, where switching costs and qualification cycles are high. The common thread is custom engineering plus manufacturing scale for small, high-criticality components where a defect can disable an expensive end product, a profile that supports durable pricing for reliability.

## Financial Analysis

On continuing operations, the numbers are sound and improving:

- **Revenue:** FY2023 $456.8M → FY2024 $553.5M → FY2025 $593.2M. Q1 2026 accelerated to $153.1M, with PD +17.4% and MSA +13.9% — growth is broad-based, not concentrated in one end market.
- **Margins:** GAAP gross margin expanded from 43.2% (FY25) to 43.8% in Q1 2026 (vs 40.3% prior year); non-GAAP gross margin reached 45.5% in Q1. Adjusted EBIT margin moved from 16.4% to 19.7% year-over-year in Q1.
- **Profitability:** Continuing-ops net earnings of $50.9M in FY2025 ($0.58 GAAP diluted EPS, $1.11 non-GAAP). Q1 2026 continuing EPS of $0.13 ($0.27 non-GAAP), up from roughly breakeven a year earlier. The earnings surprise record is solid but not explosive — 2 beats and 2 in-line prints over the last four quarters, with the most recent (Q1 2026) a 10.4% beat.
- **Cash flow:** FY2025 adjusted free cash flow up from 16.9% of revenue. Q1 2026 FCF was -$3.1M, but that reflects a non-recurring 2025 customer prepayment comparison, working-capital build, and elevated capex for specialty-film capacity, a timing item rather than deterioration.
- **Balance sheet:** As of 3/31/26, $41M cash and $131M drawn on a $400M revolver (matures Feb 2028), against $780M equity. The Cornell Dubilier seller note ($122.9M) was fully repaid in October 2025, cutting interest expense from $16.3M (FY24) to ~$9.3M (FY25) toward a ~$6M run-rate. Net leverage sits well under 1x EBITDA, comfortably inside the 3.75x net-leverage covenant.
- **Capital allocation:** Value-conscious buybacks — 3.6M / 3.0M / 2.9M shares repurchased in 2025/24/23 ($65M/$54M/$48M), with $121.5M remaining authorized at 3/31/26. Share count fell from ~91.7M (2022) to ~85.4M. No dividend.

One earnings-quality flag: the $6.2M "dividend income" recognized in FY2025 was a non-cash paid-in-kind preferred dividend from the Syntiant investment, and it flatters reported EBIT. Stripping it leaves the operating story intact but tempers the headline.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on two engines with credible structural tailwinds.

Precision Devices benefits from rising global defense spend on electronic warfare, where RF filters and high-reliability capacitors are gating components and customers increasingly demand domestic/onshore supply chains. The reshoring impulse — amplified by the current tariff regime — is a tailwind here, not a headwind. Electrification and power demand is driving specialty film-capacitor growth, where Knowles is ramping new capacity (currently a yield drag, but a deliberate growth investment). Defense and industrial demand is the kind of multi-year, program-driven backlog that supports the +17.4% PD growth seen in Q1.

MSA rides aging demographics, expanding global hearing-health access, and the new OTC hearing-aid regulatory channel in the US, which broadens the addressable market and reduces stigma. As a high-margin near-duopoly, MSA offsets mature-product price erosion with cost-downs and premium specialty-audio mix; the +13.9% Q1 growth shows the channel expansion is contributing.

Estimate revisions confirm the momentum. The current-year consensus has risen +4.2% over 30 days and +5.7% over 90 days to $1.29; next-year estimates are up +4.1%/+6.2% to $1.56. These are healthy, broadening revisions consistent with a business executing, but they fall short of the revision velocity that would mark a trough-to-recovery inflection deserving aggressive re-rating. The transformation has already played out in the numbers; the forward growth is high-single to low-double-digit revenue with steady margin gains, which is good but not the kind of acceleration that justifies a premium multiple from here.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the case for patience is strongest. At ~$41.5, Knowles trades at ~26.6x the $1.56 next-year consensus. On non-GAAP earnings ($1.11 FY2025, with Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $0.27 up ~50% YoY), the trailing multiple is ~37x and forward closer to ~30x. None of these are trough-earnings distortions that would justify ignoring the headline multiple — the business is past its inflection, margins are near their improved run-rate, and the forward P/E of ~27-32x already embeds continued execution.

The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited use: Rogers (-47x, loss-making), Belden (18.9x), Vishay (5,518x, depressed earnings), Littelfuse (-287x), and Corning (85x) produce a median P/E of 85 that says more about cyclical earnings troughs across the group than about a fair multiple for Knowles. On growth-adjusted terms, a low-double-digit grower at ~26.6x next-year earnings carries a PEG well above 2 — full, not cheap.

The most concrete valuation signal is the analyst target range, with the median at $36.5. On a genuine inflection stock with a sub-30x multiple, analyst targets lag reality and function as floors. Here the multiple is at the upper end of that range and the targets are below the current price, which is the opposite configuration: the price has run ahead of where the sell-side sees fair value, and the burden of proof is on continued upside, not on the market being wrong. We assign a valuation score of 4.5 — the business quality argues against a lower mark, but the absolute and relative multiple, combined with a price above the entire target range, prevents a higher one.

## Competitive Landscape

Knowles holds defensible positions in both segments, with the trajectory stable-to-improving rather than rapidly gaining share. In MSA, the balanced-armature duopoly with Sonion is a durable structure with high qualification barriers; the OTC channel could modestly expand the pie. In PD, the company is a credible specialist in a fragmented market, and the reshoring/defense tailwind plays to its domestic-supply and high-reliability strengths. The Cornell Dubilier integration extends its film-capacitor reach into electrification, an adjacency with secular demand.

The moat is real but not widening dramatically — these are engineering-and-reliability advantages in niche markets, not network effects or platform lock-in. Continued gross-margin expansion is evidence of pricing discipline and mix improvement, supporting a 6.5 competitive-position score. The five-year cumulative TSR of 116.3 versus a peer group at 236.6 is a reminder that the company spent years underperforming during the consumer-MEMS era; the recent re-rating is the market finally crediting the cleaner business, but it also means the easy catch-up has largely happened.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer and distributor concentration.** WS Audiology is ~11% of total revenue and 24% of MSA; MSA's top five customers are 76% of the segment. In PD, distributor TTI crossed 10% of total revenue (19% of PD) in 2025 — a newly modified disclosure. A pullback from either would hit results materially.

**China and tariffs.** A majority of manufacturing sits in China and Southeast Asia, and the 10-K states China's domestic-sourcing policy has "adversely impacted" PD revenue. A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidated IEEPA tariffs, opening a potential refund of historically paid duties (amount and timing uncertain), but a new temporary 10% global tariff under Section 122 was implemented. The regime is fluid; reshoring demand is a partial offset on the defense side.

**Syntiant entanglement.** Knowles carries an $83.4M cost-method investment in Syntiant Series D-2 preferred (illiquid private AI-chip company), a $5.9M junior note, residual separation-cost liability (~$4.3M), and supplies metal cans to Syntiant. A Syntiant impairment could trigger a non-cash writedown; an up-round could create upside. Roughly $89M of book value is exposed to a single private startup.

**FX and supply.** A sustained 10% USD weakening would reduce pre-tax earnings ~$14.8M. Sole-source ASIC/MEMS foundries are single points of failure. Defense exposure adds ITAR/debarment compliance risk.

**Governance/succession.** The February 2026 special performance award to COO Daniel Giesecke (5-year EPS/revenue PSU, 0-400% payout) plus new CEO-specific severance/change-in-control plans signal an engineered leadership transition over the medium term — a key-person item to monitor. Insider activity is routine: CEO Niew exercised 142,857 options at $16.77 and sold at $33.25 in May 2026, retaining ~884K shares; the COO sold ~$754K. No open-market buying, so no conviction signal, but nothing alarming either.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is leaning bullish, not defensive. Near-term ATM IV of 48.1% is rich versus 34.5% one-year historical vol (a 13.6pp spread), but the IV percentile within the current chain is low at 14%, and skew is call-heavy (-4.6%), with a put/call volume ratio of 0.03 and OI ratio of 0.01. There is no put-demand divergence to flag against our view; if anything, positioning is sanguine, which is a mild caution for a stock that has more than doubled and trades above analyst targets. Rising IV alongside the price advance (82.3% → 100.5% on one delta read) suggests the market expects continued movement but is not hedging downside aggressively.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The transformation is durable and the market keeps re-rating it. Both segments compound at low-double-digit revenue with adjusted EBIT margins holding near 20% (PD) and ~40% (MSA), FCF margin stays near 19%, and the buyback engine retires ~5% of the float annually. Defense electronic-warfare and electrification tailwinds prove multi-year and PD growth sustains mid-teens. A potential IEEPA tariff refund delivers a one-time cash windfall, and a Syntiant up-round marks the $83M investment higher. On $1.56 next-year EPS at ~30x, the stock reaches the high $40s; on continued estimate revisions and 33x, mid-$50s. Bull case: ~$52-55.

### Bear Case

The multiple compresses as growth normalizes toward high single digits and the market re-anchors to a more reasonable ~22-24x forward. Customer concentration bites (a WS Audiology or TTI pullback), the Syntiant stake takes a non-cash impairment, China/tariff costs pressure margins, or the new film-capacitor capacity remains a yield drag longer than expected. At 22x the $1.29 current-year EPS, the stock falls to ~$28-30, roughly 28-32% below the current price. Bear case: ~$28-30.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The business merits ownership for its compounding cash flow and disciplined buyback, but the current price offers limited near-term asymmetry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $30s, compressing the forward multiple to ~25x or below and bringing the price back within the analyst target range, where downside is better supported.
- Estimate-revision acceleration above +10% on next-year EPS combined with sustained mid-teens PD growth, confirming an inflection rather than a normalization.
- A confirmed IEEPA tariff refund or Syntiant up-round that adds quantifiable value not yet in the price.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Syntiant impairment or evidence of distress in the $89M exposure.
- Loss or material reduction of a top customer/distributor (WS Audiology or TTI), or PD growth decelerating below mid-single digits on China/tariff pressure.
- Margin reversal — gross margin retreating back toward 40% — signaling the mix/pricing gains were transient.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (~late July 2026) for confirmation that both segments hold double-digit growth and margins, and for any update on the tariff refund and Syntiant marks. A disorderly leadership transition would warrant earlier review.

**Risk/reward from current ~$41.5:** Base case ~$44-46 (≈28-29x next-year EPS of $1.56, +6-11%); bull ~$52-55 (+25-32%); bear ~$28-30 (-28-32%). The roughly symmetric downside-to-base-case skew, against a price already above the analyst target range, is what holds us at HOLD rather than BUY.

## Conclusion

Knowles is a genuinely better company than its standardized data tags suggest — a debt-light, cash-generative specialty-components maker that has successfully pivoted into defense, industrial, electrification, and medtech, with both segments accelerating and margins expanding. The fundamentals support ownership: ~20% adjusted EBIT margins, ~19% FCF margin, sub-1x leverage, falling interest expense, and a buyback that retires ~5% of the float each year.

The constraint is price. A 137% twelve-month run has lifted the stock above every published analyst target, with estimate revisions that are positive but not at inflection velocity. The asymmetry that existed when the stock traded in the high teens has largely been captured. We rate Knowles HOLD, would hold existing positions for the compounding, and would deploy fresh capital on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $30s where the multiple and the analyst range provide better support.
