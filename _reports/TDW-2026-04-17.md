## Executive Summary

Tidewater is the world's largest offshore support vessel (OSV) operator with 208 vessels deployed across five geographic segments. The stock has rallied 161% over the past 12 months from a ~$32 base to ~$84, driven by elevated oil prices tied to Middle East conflict and a structural supply thesis in offshore vessels. At 12.7x forward P/E, Tidewater trades at a 33% discount to the 19.0x OFS peer median despite superior free cash flow generation ($353M, 26% FCF margin) and a genuine supply-side moat — newbuild construction costs remain financially unviable for competitors, locking in pricing power for the existing fleet.

The near-term catalyst is the $500M Wilson Companies acquisition (22 PSVs in Brazil, expected Q2 2026 close), which should add $100-130M in incremental revenue and positions Tidewater in the largest offshore vessel market globally. Management guides FY2026 revenue of $1.43-1.48B (+6-9% YoY) with gross margin expansion to 49-51%. Operational quality deteriorated in 2025: utilization declined to 76.1%, West Africa revenue softened materially, and G&A surged 21%. The stock is pricing in execution that hasn't yet been delivered. The combination of a meaningful valuation discount to peers, a supply-constrained industry, and a specific acquisition catalyst supports a BUY, but position sizing should reflect the oil price sensitivity and the elevated short interest (12.2% of float) that signals institutional skepticism.

## Company Overview

Tidewater provides marine support and transportation services to offshore energy companies worldwide. Its fleet of 208 vessels (average age 13.1 years) includes platform supply vessels (PSVs), anchor handling towing supply vessels (AHTS), and specialty vessels. Operations span five segments: West Africa (66 vessels, $363M revenue), Europe/Mediterranean (50 vessels, $344M), Americas (34 vessels, $270M), Asia Pacific (19 vessels, $190M), and Middle East (43 vessels, $173M).

The company emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2017 and has since pursued aggressive consolidation, acquiring Solstad's fleet (37 PSVs, $594M) in 2023 and now Wilson Companies (22 PSVs, $500M) in 2026. This consolidation strategy exploits a structural supply shortage: high newbuild costs (estimated $30-40M+ per PSV) mean the global fleet is aging and shrinking, giving existing operators pricing power as long as offshore E&P activity holds.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability**

Revenue grew from $648M (2022) to $1,346M (2024) to $1,353M (2025) — a deceleration from +33% growth to essentially flat. Average day rates continued rising (+6.1% YoY to $22,573), but this was offset by lower vessel count (213 average vs. 217) and declining utilization (76.1% total, down 2.9pp). Operating income fell 9% to $283M.

Headline net income of $335M and EPS of $6.64 are misleading. A $201.5M non-cash deferred tax benefit from an internal vessel realignment inflated the figures. Adjusted pretax income was approximately $220M, down 4% from $229M in 2024. The Q4 2025 earnings "beat" that drove the stock from ~$80 to ~$88 was almost entirely this tax event.

Gross margin of 48.7% in Q4 2025 was genuinely strong. Management guides 49-51% for FY2026, implying further improvement.

**Cash Flow**

Operating cash flow of $379M (+34% YoY) is the standout. Capital expenditure was only $25.8M (drydocking of $98.6M is the primary maintenance spend), producing free cash flow of $353M. Even including drydocking, adjusted FCF was ~$254M — roughly 19% of market cap. This cash generation is well above industry norms.

**Balance Sheet**

Cash of $579M provides a substantial buffer, and the $250M undrawn revolver adds liquidity. Long-term debt of $649M (9.125% Senior Notes due 2030) carries a high coupon. Interest expense was $66M in 2025, representing 17.4% of adjusted pretax income. The Wilson acquisition ($500M + ~$261M assumed debt) will consume most of the cash cushion and increase total debt to ~$910M. Post-acquisition, the balance sheet will be meaningfully more leveraged.

Retained earnings remain negative at -$305M, reflecting the legacy bankruptcy. Debt/equity of 1.75x is moderate for an asset-intensive business but will rise post-Wilson.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth is acquisition-dependent.** Organic revenue was flat in 2025, and the Q1 2026 estimate revision trend is negative (-6.8% over 90 days for the current quarter). The Wilson acquisition provides the mechanical uplift: 22 PSVs in Brazil should add $100-130M in annualized revenue, driving the $1.43-1.48B guidance.

**Day rate trajectory is the key organic variable.** Rates rose 6.1% in 2025 but are decelerating. CEO commentary from the March 2026 earnings call was optimistic, calling the 2026 outlook "as good as it's been since the offshore recovery began" and citing leading indicators of tendering and new contract awards that suggest a drilling recovery "as we progress through the year and into 2027." This is forward-looking commentary, not yet confirmed by data.

**Full-year estimate revisions are mixed.** FY2026E EPS of $4.07 is down 3.3% over 30 days but up 12.0% over 90 days. FY2027E EPS of $6.03 is up 22.2% over 90 days. The market is pricing in meaningful earnings acceleration in the back half of 2026 and into 2027. This acceleration depends on (a) Wilson closing on schedule, (b) offshore drilling activity improving, and (c) day rates continuing to rise.

**Utilization softening is the counter-signal.** Eight vessels were stacked in 2025 versus one in 2024. West Africa utilization fell to 65.9%. If this trend continues, it suggests demand may be rolling over rather than inflecting higher.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.89, TDW trades at 12.7x trailing and forward P/E versus a peer median of 19.0x. The closest comparable, Oceaneering International (OII), trades at 6.9x, while most OFS peers trade at 14-42x. The discount reflects the cyclical nature of offshore services and the mixed Q4 2025 earnings (53% miss on adjusted EPS before the tax benefit).

On a cash flow basis, the valuation is more compelling. At ~6x EV/EBITDA (using approximately $600M FY2025 EBITDA and $4.2B market cap + $649M debt - $579M cash = ~$4.3B EV), Tidewater trades in line with or below historical OFS multiples in a mid-cycle environment.

The consensus analyst price target range is $52-117, with a median of $80 and mean of $82.29 — essentially at current levels. The distribution of analyst recommendations (2 Strong Buy, 5 Hold, 1 Sell) reflects divided opinion. The stock has already more than doubled in 12 months, and analysts appear to view most of the near-term upside as captured.

If the structural supply thesis holds and day rates continue rising, FY2027E EPS of $6.03 at a 15x multiple (still below peer median) implies $90+ within 12-18 months. At the peer median of 19x, the math yields $114. Whether the industry cycle supports that multiple expansion depends on sustained offshore drilling recovery and oil prices above $75.

## Competitive Landscape

Tidewater's primary competitive advantage is structural and quantifiable: it operates the largest OSV fleet in an industry where supply cannot meaningfully increase. Newbuild costs of $30-40M+ per vessel, combined with 2-3 year construction timelines, mean the global fleet will continue aging and shrinking absent a sustained multi-year oil price supercycle. Tidewater has positioned itself as the consolidator of record, acquiring distressed fleets at attractive valuations.

The company also benefits from geographic diversification across 30+ countries, scale economies in procurement and crew management, and Jones Act protections for U.S. coastal operations. Competitors (Bourbon Offshore, DOF Group, regional operators) generally lack the fleet size, financial resources, or geographic reach to compete effectively for large-scale, multi-vessel contracts.

The risk to this moat is demand-driven, not supply-driven. If oil prices fall sustainably below $65, E&P capex contracts, drilling programs are delayed, and day rates compress regardless of fleet supply dynamics. The moat protects pricing on the upside but offers limited defense against cyclical demand declines.

## Risk Assessment

**Oil Price Sensitivity (High).** The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that "sustained oil prices in the low $60s per barrel may delay some drilling projects initially expected to commence in 2026." Oil currently trades around $83 following ceasefire-driven volatility, but the range of outcomes is wide. Any sustained decline below $70 would likely compress day rates and utilization.

**Wilson Acquisition Execution (Moderate-High).** The deal introduces Brazilian antitrust risk, ~$261M of additional debt, integration complexity, and BRL currency exposure. Operating in Brazil requires navigating regulatory requirements around Brazilian-built tonnage and local content rules.

**West Africa Deterioration (Moderate).** The company's largest segment by vessel count saw a 21% decline in operating profit with utilization dropping to 65.9% and 5 vessels stacked. If this represents the leading edge of broader demand softening rather than a transient dip, the earnings trajectory could disappoint.

**G&A Creep (Moderate).** G&A rose from $95M (2023) to $135M (2025) — up 42% over two years while revenue grew 34%. Corporate G&A is now 55% of total G&A (from 46%). This overhead inflation dilutes the operating leverage that should accompany day rate increases.

**Middle East Conflict (Moderate).** The 10-K specifically cites the Iran conflict as a risk to the Middle East segment. War-risk insurance cancellation, vessel seizure, and operational disruption in the Strait of Hormuz are explicitly flagged. Current geopolitical conditions make this risk live.

**High-Cost Debt (Moderate).** The 9.125% coupon on $649M of senior notes generates $66M in annual interest expense. Post-Wilson, total debt rises to ~$910M. With rates at 3.64% and no prospect of cuts, refinancing at lower rates is unlikely before maturity in 2030.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-117, +31-39%)**
Wilson acquisition closes on schedule in Q2 2026. Offshore drilling recovers in H2 2026 as management projects, driving day rates above $24,000 and utilization back above 80%. FY2027E EPS of $6.03 at 18-19x (approaching peer median) yields $108-114. The $500M buyback authorization provides price support and EPS accretion. Oil remains above $80, and short interest unwinds, creating additional buying pressure.

**Bear Case ($52-58, -31-38%)**
Oil prices decline below $70 on ceasefire extension or global demand weakness. E&P capex is cut, drilling programs delayed, and day rates compress toward $18,000-19,000. Utilization falls below 75% as more vessels are stacked. Wilson closes but at suboptimal timing — Brazilian demand softens, integration costs exceed expectations, and incremental debt weighs on the balance sheet. West Africa deterioration accelerates. G&A continues rising. FY2027E EPS is revised down to $3.50-4.00, and the market applies a 14-15x multiple: $49-60. This is consistent with the analyst low target of $52.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalyst dates: Wilson acquisition close (Q2 2026), Q1 2026 earnings (late May/early June), Q2 2026 earnings (August/September).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Average day rates decline below $20,000 for two consecutive quarters (signals cycle turn)
2. Total utilization falls below 74% (indicates demand destruction beyond seasonal/regional softness)
3. Wilson acquisition fails to close or purchase price is meaningfully revised upward
4. Oil prices sustain below $65 for more than 60 days

## Conclusion

Tidewater offers a structural supply moat, strong free cash flow generation, a specific acquisition catalyst, and a 33% valuation discount to OFS peers. At 12.7x forward earnings and ~6x EV/EBITDA, the stock retains upside if offshore drilling activity recovers as management projects. The principal risks — oil price sensitivity, Wilson execution, and softening utilization — are real but known, reflected in divided analyst consensus and elevated short interest. BUY with a 12-18 month base case target of $100, sized at 2-3% of portfolio given the cyclical sensitivity, and with strict discipline on the invalidation triggers defined above.
