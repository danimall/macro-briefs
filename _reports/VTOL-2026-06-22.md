**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bristow is the leading global provider of offshore-energy and government helicopter services, operating roughly 214 aircraft across 15 countries under 12 Air Operator Certificates. The investment case rests on a 2026 earnings inflection that management has labeled "transformational": Government Services margins, which collapsed 76% in 2025 to $5M of operating income on UKSAR2G and Irish Coast Guard transition costs, should recover sharply as those start-up costs roll off, while only about half of Offshore Energy Services (OES) contracts have repriced to today's leading-edge rates with another ~50% set to renew in 2026 against near-100% heavy/medium helicopter utilization. The company has de-risked its balance sheet through a January 2026 refinancing that pushed out maturities and cut its cost of debt, and initiated its first-ever dividend in February 2026.

The complication is that the forward picture has softened in the data even as the structural story remains intact. Current-year consensus EPS has been cut 22.5% over 90 days and the current-quarter estimate is down 39.6% over 90 days (down 37.3% over 30 days), the Q1 2026 GAAP EPS print missed consensus by ~56%, and the stock has retraced from a $50.38 high to $42 since the May 5 earnings reaction. The forward repricing tailwind is real and filing-supported, but the S92 parts shortage, affecting ~29% of the fleet and forcing groundings, is a concrete, company-specific constraint on the company's ability to monetize the tight market. Insiders have trimmed into strength with no offsetting open-market buying.

We rate VTOL a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 5.85, a continuation of the four-report HOLD streak. The growth thesis is credible and the balance sheet is sound, but falling near-term estimates, a recent earnings miss, and a stock trading below the analyst target range without the catalyst yet confirmed in reported numbers argue for waiting for the Government Services margin recovery to appear in actual results before adding. We see better risk/reward on a pullback toward the mid-$30s or on confirmation of the inflection in the next two prints.

## Company Overview

Bristow emerged from the predecessor "Old Bristow's" 2019 Chapter 11 reorganization and its merger with Era Group, from which CEO Christopher Bradshaw came. The business runs through three segments:

- **Offshore Energy Services (~66-67% of revenue):** Transports personnel to and from offshore production platforms and drilling rigs in the US Gulf, Brazil, Nigeria, the North Sea, Trinidad, and Suriname. Revenue is production-weighted rather than exploration-weighted, which makes it structurally less cyclical than typical oilfield services: production platforms operate over decades at low marginal cost regardless of near-term oil price swings.
- **Government Services (~25-26%):** Long-duration (10+ year) public search-and-rescue contracts. The anchor is UK SAR / UKSAR2G (~19% of total revenue), alongside the Irish Coast Guard, Netherlands, and Dutch Caribbean contracts. Counterparties are high credit quality; cash flows are stable.
- **Other Services (~8%):** The Airnorth regional airline in Australia, Humberside Airport, and dry-leasing.

The competitive structure is a consolidated, high-barrier oligopoly. Primary competitors are CHC Group, NHV Group, Omni Helicopters, and PHI. Moats include a strong safety record, incumbency advantage in contract re-bids, scale, certifications, and the cost and difficulty of replicating a global AOC, maintenance, and pilot infrastructure. Customer concentration is meaningful: the top 10 customers represent 63% of revenue and the top 3 represent 36%.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year turnaround is clear in the trend data:

| Net income | –$16M | –$7M | $95M | $129M |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $177M | $198M |
| Free cash flow | — | — | — | $56M |

FY2025 revenue rose 5.3% to $1.49B, operating income rose 19.8% to $159M, and net income rose 36% to $129M. The OES operating margin expanded from 14% to 17% on higher leading-edge rates, lower repairs and maintenance via vendor credits, and lower fuel. Profitability ratios are respectable for a capital-intensive operator: ROE 12.2%, ROA 5.6%, operating margin 10.7%, net margin 8.7%.

The Government Services margin compression is the most important nuance in the financials. Operating income in that segment fell 76% to $5M in 2025 on transition costs for UKSAR2G and IRCG: subcontractor costs up $28M, plus higher start-up and personnel expenses. This is temporary and expected to reverse in 2026 as transitions complete (the last Irish SAR base went live February 1, 2026). A mechanical read of "Government margin down 76%" badly misreads the situation; these are 10-year, high-credit-quality annuities ramping into profitability.

The balance sheet is sound following the January 2026 refinancing. Bristow issued $500M of 6.750% Senior Secured Notes due 2033, redeemed the 6.875% notes due 2028, and extended its ABL maturity to 2031 while cutting pricing 25bps. Total debt is ~$774M principal against ~$342-344M cash, leaving net debt of ~$432M. D/E is 1.18, current ratio 1.9, and the Altman Z of 2.19 sits in the "grey zone" typical of a capital-intensive aircraft operator rather than indicating distress. There are no material near-term maturities. Maintenance capex is low at $20-25M annually, which supports strong FCF conversion potential once transition costs roll off.

The earnings surprise record warrants attention. After two large beats in mid-2025 (Q2 +108%, Q3 +98%), the company missed by 31.7% in Q4 and 56.5% in Q1 2026. Management attributes the Q1 miss to seasonality (Australia wet season) and elevated transition costs rather than demand deterioration, and affirmed full-year guidance the same day. The pattern shows lumpy quarterly visibility, with large beats and misses in both directions, which lowers confidence in any single-quarter forecast.

## Growth Analysis

The 2026 guidance frames the inflection: revenue of $1.58-1.69B and Adjusted EBITDA of $295-325M versus ~$247M LTM. The implied EBITDA growth is substantial and rests on two engines.

First, the Government Services recovery. As transitions complete, this segment should swing from ~$5M of operating income to a guided ~$70-80M of adjusted operating income, the single largest earnings catalyst and the reason management calls 2026 "transformational." Q1 2026 already showed Government Services revenue up $7.8M sequentially as IRCG ramped.

Second, the OES repricing runway, with ~50% of contracts renewing in 2026 at higher rates and improved terms (better cancellation provisions, risk-sharing). With heavy and medium offshore helicopters running near 100% effective utilization, the result of the 2014-2021 downturn purging capacity and new deliveries being absorbed by closed markets and military orders, this is a structural pricing tailwind independent of any near-term oil-price upside. Rystad data cited by management projects global floater demand, offshore capex/opex, and floating rig demand all growing through 2030.

The tension is that consensus estimates have moved the wrong direction. Current-year EPS has been cut from $4.97 to $3.91, the current-quarter estimate is down 39.6%, and next-quarter is down 10.1% over 90 days. This is negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the positive revision velocity that would signal an underappreciated inflection. The estimate cuts likely reflect the Q1 miss and analysts' recognition that the Government margin recovery is being pushed later into 2026 than initially modeled, plus the S92 supply constraint limiting near-term revenue capture. The structural growth story is intact, but the timing has slipped, and falling estimates are a reliable forward warning that argues against treating this as a high-conviction growth name today. We weight growth prospects as the most predictive dimension in our framework, and here the qualitative thesis (real repricing runway, durable Government annuities) is partly offset by deteriorating near-term revision velocity.

## Valuation Assessment

VTOL trades at ~11x trailing EPS of $4.32 and ~11x the current $3.91 forward consensus. On the company's own 2026 EBITDA guidance against an enterprise value of roughly $1.68B (market cap $1.25B plus net debt $432M), the stock trades at ~5.2-5.7x EV/EBITDA, undemanding for a business with a credible path to EBITDA growth and low maintenance capex.

The peer set is not directly useful as a multiple anchor. Bristow's listed Oil & Gas Equipment & Services peers (HLX at 100.5x, RES at 76.6x, CLB at 21.5x, AESI negative, INVX at 38.2x) carry a median P/E of 76.6x, reflecting trough or distressed earnings rather than a clean comparison; Bristow's production-weighted, contract-backed cash flows are structurally different from the exploration-levered services names. The cleaner read is that VTOL is cheap on EV/EBITDA and trailing P/E in absolute terms.

The analyst price targets (low $56, high $70, mean $62, median $60) sit 33-66% above the current price, but coverage is thin (three analysts) and those targets were likely set against the higher pre-cut estimate base. With current-year EPS revised down in 90 days, the gap between price and target reflects stale models as much as a clear mispricing. Our framework treats analyst targets as floors only on inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples; here estimates are falling, so the targets are more likely to compress toward the price than the reverse, at least until reported numbers confirm the recovery.

We assign a valuation score of 5.5. The stock is genuinely inexpensive on cycle-adjusted EBITDA, and the dimension is anti-predictive in our experience (cheap names have underperformed), so a low P/E alone does not earn a high score. The catalyst (Government margin recovery) is identifiable but not yet visible in reported results, and the estimate-revision direction is negative. That combination of cheap valuation, falling estimates, and an unconfirmed catalyst is the textbook value-trap warning that requires an independent, confirmed catalyst before rewarding the cheapness. The valuation is supportive, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Bristow's competitive position is improving, and this is the strongest pillar of the case. The offshore helicopter market sits in a structurally tight supply/demand position that favors operators. The prior downturn purged capacity, few new offshore-configured medium, super-medium, or heavy helicopters were built, and effective utilization on these models is now near 100%. New deliveries are being absorbed by closed markets (China, Middle East) and military orders that share OEM production lines, lengthening civilian lead times to ~24 months and tightening supply further. The result is rising leading-edge rates with a multi-year repricing runway.

On the government side, Bristow is incumbent on long-duration SAR contracts that re-bid favors incumbents, and NATO/government defense spending trends (a 5% of GDP target by 2035) support SAR and ISR outsourcing demand. The company also holds genuine, low-cost Advanced Air Mobility optionality through agreements with Electra (EL9, 5 delivery slots), Vertical Aerospace/Skyports (UK air-taxi network), and BETA Technologies, where Bristow's role is operator and launch partner leveraging its AOCs, not aircraft developer, with refundable deposits limiting downside. This should be valued as a call option rather than a core earnings driver.

The principal competitive constraint is internal: the S92 supply chain problem. With ~29% of the fleet being S92 and parts delays forcing groundings, Bristow may be unable to fully monetize the tight market it is well-positioned to exploit, and faces availability penalties on both OES and government contracts. Whether management accelerates the planned early-2027 S76D exit and AW189 build-out is the operational variable to monitor. We score competitive position 6.8: the structural trajectory is favorable and the moat is widening, tempered by the self-inflicted supply constraint and a TSR that has lagged the peer group over the measurement period.

## Risk Assessment

**S92 parts shortages (company-specific, acute):** The most concrete constraint on upside. Groundings and forgone business cap revenue capture in a tight market and expose the company to availability penalties.

**Customer and contract concentration:** UK SAR alone is terminable at will by the UK Department for Transport (for a cancellation fee). Government appropriation and shutdown risk was flagged in Q1 forward-looking statements.

**FX exposure:** 83% of revenue is non-US, with GBP/USD the dominant exposure. FY2025 benefited from $22.5M of FX gains in "Other, net," a tailwind that can reverse and pressure both translated results and margins.

**Offshore cyclicality:** Management notes drilling and exploration activity is in a "mid-cycle activity plateau that may persist for much of 2026," with a pickup expected late 2026 onward. Mature-basin dependence (North Sea, US Gulf) is a structural feature.

**Labor:** ~60% of employees are unionized, with collective bargaining agreements that expired in late 2025 or expire in 2026 (UK engineers, Nigeria, Norway, Brazil). Renegotiation risk is live.

**Foreign-ownership constraints:** US law requires ≥75% US-citizen voting control; the charter auto-reduces non-US voting rights above 25%, structurally limiting the shareholder base and liquidity.

We score risk factors 5.0. The risks here are specific and partly unpriced: the S92 constraint, the at-will UK SAR contract, and the FX tailwind reversal are not sector truisms but identifiable, company-level exposures that could move the stock against an unconfirmed earnings recovery.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 64.7% is rich against 32.9% one-year historical volatility (a 31.8-point spread), with the term structure in backwardation (64.7% near-term vs 38.9% at six months); the market is pricing an imminent event, most likely the next earnings print. Put/call skew of 7.4% indicates elevated put demand, and the nearest-expiry implied move is ±13.2%. ATM IV has fallen 16 points since early June (80.8% to 64.7%) while the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.20 to 0.57, suggesting some de-escalation of near-term hedging but still-elevated downside protection demand. For a fundamental investor, the rich near-term IV argues against buying premium and supports patience; the backwardation flags that the next quarter is a binary the market is wary of.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Government Services transition costs roll off through 2026 and the segment swings to ~$70-80M of adjusted operating income; OES contracts reprice at near-100% utilization with ~50% renewing in 2026 at higher rates; full-year Adjusted EBITDA hits the upper end of guidance. The de-risked balance sheet (no near-term maturities, lower cost of debt), maiden dividend, and $121M remaining buyback support a re-rating. On normalized EBITDA of ~$320M and a 7-8x EV/EBITDA multiple, equity value approaches $60-70, consistent with analyst targets. AAM optionality provides additional, uncapitalized upside.

### Bear Case

S92 groundings and parts shortages prevent Bristow from capturing the tight market, the Government margin recovery slips further into 2027, and continued GBP weakness reverses the FX tailwind. Estimate cuts continue, the Q2 and Q3 prints disappoint against the seasonal and transition-cost overhang, and the stock derates toward the mid-$30s, roughly its 52-week low of $31.76 and ~15-25% below current levels. The offshore "mid-cycle plateau" persists, and UK SAR concentration becomes a focal worry if any contract renegotiation turns adverse.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale: the 12-month thesis is positive but the catalyst is not yet confirmed in reported numbers, and near-term estimate momentum is negative.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 or Q3 2026 results confirming the Government Services margin recovery, with the segment moving toward the guided run-rate and consensus current-year EPS revisions turning positive.
- Evidence the S92 supply constraint is easing (fewer groundings, restored availability) or accelerated AW189/S76D fleet transition de-risking the upside capture.
- A pullback toward the mid-$30s on no fundamental deterioration, which would restore clear asymmetry to the EV/EBITDA case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of EBITDA tracking below the guidance run-rate, or a guidance cut to the Adjusted EBITDA range.
- Adverse UK SAR contract development or a material loss of a top-3 customer.
- Continued negative estimate revisions (current-year EPS falling below ~$3.50) combined with deteriorating offshore activity beyond the "plateau" framing.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), with the full-year guidance and Government margin trajectory the decisive inputs.

## Conclusion

VTOL is a credible post-bankruptcy turnaround with a real, filing-supported 2026 earnings inflection, a freshly de-risked balance sheet, a maiden dividend, near-100% market utilization, and low-cost AAM optionality. On cycle-adjusted EBITDA the stock is inexpensive, and the structural OES repricing and Government Services recovery offer genuine upside to the analyst target range.

The reasons for patience rather than fresh accumulation are concrete: estimates are falling, the Q1 print missed, the S92 parts shortage caps the company's ability to monetize a tight market, and the inflection has not yet shown up in reported results. Cheapness alone is not a buy signal here; it requires the Government margin recovery to convert from guidance into actuals. The score trajectory has declined from 6.9 in April to 5.85 now, and the new data (estimate cuts, the earnings miss) confirms rather than reverses that softening, while the structural story keeps it well above an AVOID. We maintain HOLD and look to the next two prints to confirm the inflection or to a pullback toward the mid-$30s for a more asymmetric entry.
