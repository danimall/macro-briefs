**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Garrett Motion is a global leader in turbocharging, air/fluid compression, and high-speed electric-motor technologies, supplying automotive OEMs and the independent aftermarket. The equity has re-rated dramatically over the past year, rising roughly 219% from a 52-week low of $9.57 to ~$32.34, with the bulk of the move concentrated in 2026 (from ~$18 in January to a 52-week high of $34.34 in May). The catalyst sequence is identifiable: four consecutive double-digit earnings beats, a Q1'26 print showing +12% revenue and +53% net income with raised guidance, the May 12 Ingersoll Rand strategic partnership validating Garrett's oil-free compressor technology for industrial/data-center applications, and a May 20 Technology & Investor Day at which management leaned into a re-rating narrative around non-automotive optionality.

The investment question is no longer whether Garrett is a viable business; it generates ~$340M of free cash flow, ~20% gross margins, 13% ROA, and is deleveraging with cost of debt falling. The question is what an investor is paying for at $32 after the stock has doubled in five months. Garrett is fundamentally a flat-to-cyclical top-line business (2025 revenue of $3.58B sits below 2023's $3.89B) operating in a turbocharger unit market that S&P expects to peak around 50M units in 2025 and decline toward 2022 levels by 2030 as battery-EV penetration rises. The equity thesis rests on three legs: management's exceptional margin and FCF defense, the hybrid powertrain bridge extending turbo relevance into the 2030s, and aggressive share-count reduction (~7% fewer diluted shares YoY) re-rating against a recovering multiple.

At ~16x forward EPS ($1.99 current-year consensus) the stock is no longer pricing terminal decline, but neither is it cheap relative to a structurally maturing core business. Options markets are signaling near-term caution: 70.2% near-term implied vol against 45.3% realized, backwardation in the term structure, and a 16.3% put skew. The risk/reward after the double is balanced. We rate GTX a HOLD on a growth-hold basis, with upgrade conviction contingent on confirmation that the industrial/data-center optionality is converting to revenue rather than press releases.

## Company Overview

Garrett Motion emerged as a Honeywell spin-off in 2018 and completed a Chapter 11 reorganization in 2021. The business is the global leader in the roughly $10B OEM turbocharger industry, supported by 70+ years of OEM relationships and approximately 1,350 patents. Its technical differentiation centers on variable-geometry turbines (VNT), high-temperature materials, oil-free foil bearings, and power electronics.

The company operates as a single segment with ~6,700 employees and ~1,330 engineers across six R&D centers. A genuine structural cost advantage underpins the model: 89% of products are made in low-cost countries (China, India, Mexico, Brazil, Romania, Slovakia) and roughly two-thirds of materials are sourced from low-cost countries. This footprint matters in a Tier-1 supplier industry subject to perpetual OEM pricing pressure, including mandatory annual price step-downs.

2025 revenue mix: gasoline 45%, diesel 23%, commercial vehicle 18%, aftermarket 12%, other 2%. OEM revenue is 86% of the total; the aftermarket is 14% and represents a higher-margin, more stable annuity stream sitting on a ~150M-vehicle installed base. Strategy is two-pronged: defend and extend turbo leadership (including a new "Garrett MEG" large-bore industrial/marine turbo line targeting data-center backup power), and pivot roughly half of RD&E spend into zero-emission technologies (fuel-cell compressors, E-Powertrain, E-Cooling oil-free compressors).

## Financial Analysis

Garrett is profitable and strongly cash-generative, with a balance sheet whose headline distress signals are accounting artifacts rather than operational warnings.

| Net income ($M) | 390 | 261 | 282 | 310 | 95 (+53% YoY) |

| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | — | 408 | 413 | 98 |
| Free cash flow ($M) | — | — | — | 341 | ~69 |

The top line is cyclical and flat, not a growth story, reflecting soft light-vehicle and commercial-vehicle markets in 2024 and a maturing turbocharger unit market. FY2025's +3% growth was partly FX (+2%) and tariff recoveries (~$40M, a pass-through, not a margin gain).

The actual story is margin discipline and cash conversion. Management has repeatedly offset volume, price, and mix headwinds with productivity ($82M of fixed-cost productivity in 2024), commodity-deflation pass-through, and variable-cost flexing. Q1'26 accelerated on all axes, with growth across every vertical (gasoline +10%, diesel +12%, CV/industrial +17%, aftermarket +16%), net margin to 9.6%. Operating cash flow consistently exceeds net income, capex is modest (~$72M, ~2% of sales), and the FCF margin runs near 9.5%. Interest expense fell from $156M in 2024 to $108M in 2025 on deleveraging and the January-2025 credit repricing.

On the balance sheet, the Altman Z-Score of 1.48 (distress zone) and negative stockholders' equity (-$802M at YE2025, -$781M at Q1'26) are legacies of bankruptcy-emergence accounting, the Honeywell asbestos/indemnity settlement, and aggressive buybacks booked to treasury stock (-$625M) on top of a retained deficit (-$1.3B). This is a capital-structure characteristic, not operational insolvency. Gross debt is $1.44B (a $635M term loan due 2032 at SOFR+200bps and $800M 7.75% senior unsecured notes due 2032). Net debt of ~$1.26B against ~$510M+ annual Adj. EBIT implies leverage around 2.0–2.5x, with an undrawn $630M revolver maturing 2030, covenant compliance confirmed at Q1'26, and nothing material maturing until 2032. The 7.75% notes trade above par (~$828 on $800 face), and the undrawn revolver confirms healthy capital access. Any screen-driven "bankruptcy risk" read on this name is a false positive.

Capital allocation is heavily shareholder-return oriented. In 2025 the company repaid $50M of term loan, paid $52M in dividends, and repurchased $208M of stock (including $103M directly from Oaktree). A new $250M buyback was authorized for 2026, with $87M already executed in Q1'26 (including $50M from Oaktree). The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.08/share. Diluted share count fell from ~207M in Q1'25 to ~193M in Q1'26.

## Growth Analysis

Garrett presents a divergence between earnings momentum and structural top-line trajectory that needs to be reconciled rather than averaged.

The near-term momentum is genuine. Four straight quarters of double-digit beats (13.9% to 16.8% surprise), a Q1'26 print with broad-based vertical growth, and raised 2026 guidance constitute real positive earnings momentum. Estimate revisions confirm it: current-year EPS has been revised +8.1% over 90 days (to $1.99) and next-year +6.0% (to $2.22). These revisions are positive but below the +30% velocity threshold that would mark a true earnings inflection; they reflect a healthy upgrade cycle, not a regime change.

The structural picture is more sober. Global turbo production is forecast to decline toward 2022 levels by 2030 as BEV share rises (S&P sees BEVs at ~28% of light-vehicle production by 2030, with light-vehicle production flat-to-slightly-down in 2026). Battery EVs need no turbocharger, and Garrett states its zero-emission investments are funded by turbo profits, a self-cannibalization timing problem.

The swing factor is the hybrid bridge. S&P sees hybrids (including range-extended EVs) growing from ~16M units in 2025 to ~26M units in 2029, an ~11% CAGR, most of which still use turbos and can incorporate E-turbos. This is the medium-term lever that keeps the core relevant into the 2030s, and it is the crux of the durability case.

The non-consensus growth angle sits outside autos. Garrett is applying high-speed motors, oil-free foil bearings, and power electronics to industrial and data-center cooling and backup power generation, larger and secularly growing TAMs less exposed to the auto cycle. The Ingersoll Rand partnership validates the E-Cooling/oil-free compressor technology through a credible counterparty, and the MEG large-turbo line targets data-center backup power. These are concrete steps, but the economics of the Ingersoll Rand deal are not yet disclosed and E-Powertrain/E-Cooling remain largely pre-development. For now, this is optionality, not earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.34 the stock trades at ~16x current-year EPS ($1.99) and ~14.6x next-year EPS ($2.22). The reported trailing/forward P/E of 18.5x on $1.52 trailing EPS reflects the prior period; on consensus forward estimates the multiple is lower. Against the ~9.5% FCF margin and ~$340M of annual FCF, the equity yields roughly 5.7% on a ~$6.0B market cap before share-count shrink, which is a reasonable absolute yield for a cash-generative supplier.

The peer reference set (BorgWarner, Continental, IHI, Mitsubishi Heavy) typically trades in the low-double-digit forward P/E range, reflecting the same auto-cyclical and ICE-exposure concerns. Garrett at ~14.6x next-year EPS sits modestly above the cheapest Tier-1 ICE-exposed peers, and the premium is being assigned to its superior margin profile (20%+ gross margin), its FCF conversion, and the non-auto optionality. That premium is defensible but not deeply discounted.

The core difficulty is that this is a flat-to-declining-volume business whose multiple has re-rated from terminal-decline pessimism (sub-$10 a year ago) to durability-plus-optionality. The re-rating has already happened. Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly — low $33, median $35, mean $35.67, high $42 — implying limited consensus upside (~8% to the median) and a floor essentially at spot. On a stock at ~15x forward with mid-single-digit estimate revisions, analyst targets are closer to fair-value markers than to lagging floors; this is not the >30% revision, sub-30x setup where consensus is mechanically stale.

The bull mathematics work only if two things compound: the share count keeps shrinking ~7% annually against a stable or modestly growing FCF base, and the industrial/data-center optionality re-rates the multiple toward a less auto-cyclical comp set. The bear mathematics work if turbo volumes roll over faster than the hybrid bridge supports, compressing both EPS and the multiple. At $32, both outcomes are roughly equally plausible, which is why valuation does not score above neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Garrett's competitive position in turbochargers is strong and durable: scale leadership in a ~$10B market, VNT and high-temperature-materials technology leadership, a deep patent estate (~1,350 patents), and a global low-cost manufacturing footprint that is genuinely hard to replicate. Within turbos, the trajectory is stable-to-favorable, with Q1'26 vertical growth across gasoline, diesel, and CV suggesting share is at least holding as the broader unit market plateaus.

The competitive question is whether Garrett can transfer this strength to adjacencies where it is a challenger rather than a leader. In E-Powertrain and E-Cooling, Garrett competes against well-funded EV-tech startups and vertically integrated OEM units, with commercialization and scale unproven. The Ingersoll Rand partnership is meaningful precisely because it pairs Garrett's oil-free compressor technology with an established industrial-flow franchise, de-risking the go-to-market in industrial/data-center applications. If that partnership converts to material revenue, Garrett's competitive position shifts from "ICE-cycle leader managing decline" to "diversifying technology platform," a higher-multiple identity.

Customer concentration is a competitive vulnerability: the top 10 customers are ~62% of sales, with Stellantis at 12%, BMW at 11%, and Ford at 11% in 2025. OEM contracts are largely non-binding open POs, terminable for convenience, with mandatory annual price step-downs. The relationships are sticky on engineering integration, but the contractual structure gives OEMs leverage.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the secular ICE-to-BEV transition. Garrett's most profitable products serve ICE and hybrid powertrains; battery EVs eliminate the turbocharger entirely. The bull case depends on slower-than-expected BEV adoption, the hybrid bridge, and regulatory pressure keeping turbo penetration high (~57% of ICE light vehicles by 2026). If BEV adoption accelerates or turbo penetration is overestimated, the core profit engine erodes while the EV-pivot investments are still pre-commercial, a timing mismatch funded by the very profits at risk.

Second, customer concentration means loss of a platform or OEM financial distress would be material, amplified by terminable, price-step-down contract structures.

Third, capital-structure optics. Negative book equity and a distress-zone Z-score will screen poorly in quantitative models and may limit certain institutional ownership, and continued buybacks deepen the deficit. This is not an operational risk but it is a real constraint on the ownership base.

Fourth, geographic and tariff exposure. Significant operations and supply chain in China and Mexico, with the 10-K flagging 2025 U.S. tariff actions and the 2026 USMCA renewal as potentially "significant." Renewed tariff escalation could pressure timing and working capital even if ultimately recovered.

Fifth, the Oaktree overhang. Oaktree is a large related-party shareholder methodically exiting through company buybacks. This provides a known liquidity sink and de-risks the overhang, but it also means a portion of "buyback support" offsets insider supply rather than purely shrinking float against natural demand.

Two unresolved items deserve monitoring: the August/September 2025 Item 5.02 executive/director departures (no indication of accounting or governance turmoil; disclosure controls certified effective in Q1'26), and the May 20, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/2.03/7.01) indicating a new material agreement creating a financial obligation, most consistent with a debt transaction tied to the Investor Day and possibly to fund buybacks or the Ingersoll Rand partnership. The specifics of this filing could not be confirmed from the available sources and remain an open item.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 70.2% against 45.3% one-year realized, an unusually rich 24.9-point spread sitting in the 82nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (70.2% near-term vs. 57.0% at six months), which typically signals an anticipated near-dated catalyst rather than a stable elevated-uncertainty regime. The put/call skew of 16.3% indicates elevated demand for downside protection, and the nearest expiry implies a ±11.2% move. Reported put/call volume and OI ratios near zero suggest thin, illiquid options rather than directional positioning, so the skew and IV readings carry more weight than flow. The market is hedging a near-term event (likely the next earnings print or a follow-through from the Investor Day financing) into a stock that has already doubled, which argues for patience on new long entry rather than chasing strength.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$42).** The hybrid bridge extends turbo relevance through the 2030s, keeping the core FCF base stable while the ~7% annual share-count reduction compounds EPS. The Ingersoll Rand partnership and MEG data-center line convert into material non-auto revenue, re-rating the multiple toward a less cyclical comp set. Continued double-digit beats and raised guidance push next-year EPS toward and above $2.22. At a modestly higher multiple on that base, the stock reaches the Street high of $42, roughly +30% from current. Management's demonstrated margin defense (a 5-year TSR meaningfully above its peer group per the proxy) gives credibility to flawless execution.

**Bear Case (~$22-24).** Turbo unit volumes roll over faster than the hybrid bridge supports, light-vehicle production declines in 2026 as S&P forecasts, and an OEM platform loss or USMCA tariff disruption pressures revenue. EPS stalls near $1.85-1.95 and the multiple compresses toward the cheapest ICE-exposed peers (~11-12x) as the durability narrative loses momentum. The negative-equity optics and Oaktree supply cap institutional demand. The stock retraces toward $22-24, roughly -25% to -30%, reflecting that a re-rating built on optionality reverses when the optionality stays a press release.

**Base Case (~$35).** EPS lands around $2.10-2.20 for next year as beats continue but at a decelerating pace, the buyback shrinks the share count, and the optionality remains promising but unproven. The multiple holds around 16x. The stock trends to roughly the analyst median of $35, ~8% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive but modest — base case ~$35, roughly +8% from $32.34 — with a credible but unconfirmed re-rating optionality that could push toward the $42 bull case if industrial/data-center revenue materializes. The risk/reward after a 219% move is balanced rather than asymmetric, and options markets are pricing near-term stress, which favors patience.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The Ingersoll Rand partnership (or MEG data-center line) discloses concrete revenue/order economics that establish a non-auto growth line of material size, justifying multiple expansion toward a less cyclical comp set.
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions accelerate beyond +15% over 90 days while the stock pulls back to the high-$20s, compressing forward P/E below ~12x and creating genuine asymmetry.
- Clarity on the May 20 financing confirms it funds accretive buybacks or growth rather than balance-sheet stress, removing the open-item overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quarter showing turbo volume decline outpacing the hybrid bridge, with management guiding revenue lower and gross margin compressing below ~19%.
- Loss or material reduction of a top-three OEM platform (Stellantis, BMW, Ford), or a USMCA/tariff event that is not recoverable as a pass-through.
- Estimate revisions turn negative for two consecutive quarters alongside a break of the rising-EPS streak.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the likely catalyst the options market is hedging), plus resolution of the May 20 material-agreement disclosure and any follow-up disclosure on the Ingersoll Rand economics.

## Conclusion

Garrett Motion has executed an impressive transition in market perception over the past year, moving from a terminal-decline turbocharger play priced under $10 to a durable-cash-flow-plus-optionality story near its all-time high. The fundamentals support a re-rating to a point: ~$340M FCF, 20%+ gross margins, falling cost of debt, aggressive share-count reduction, four consecutive beats, and a genuine emerging angle in industrial/data-center applications validated by the Ingersoll Rand partnership. The negative book equity and distress-zone Z-score are accounting legacies, not operational warnings, and should be disregarded as screening false positives.

What stops this from being a buy is the combination of a structurally flat-to-declining core volume market, a multiple that has already re-rated to ~16x forward with limited consensus upside, and an options market pricing imminent near-term stress. The bull case requires the non-auto optionality to convert from announcement to revenue, which has not yet happened. At $32, the prudent posture is to hold for the continued FCF and share-shrink mechanics while waiting for either a pullback that restores asymmetry or confirmation that the industrial/data-center thesis is earning its multiple. We rate GTX a HOLD with a constructive growth lean, and would upgrade on a meaningful pullback into the high-$20s or on hard evidence that the diversification optionality is converting to earnings.
