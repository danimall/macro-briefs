**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

STAG Industrial owns 601 buildings across 41 states totaling roughly 120.3 million rentable square feet, built on a strategy that distinguishes it from coastal in-fill peers: acquiring single- and small-portfolios of predominantly single-tenant industrial assets in secondary and tertiary Tier-1 markets to harvest higher cap rates and idiosyncratic value. The portfolio is 96.4% leased, diversified (no tenant above 2.8% of ABR, no industry above 11.1%), and financed conservatively (net debt to real estate cost basis ~38%, blended debt cost 4.42%, floating exposure limited to a ~$200M revolver). The current internal-growth engine is the re-leasing of years of accumulated below-market in-place rents at +18-20% cash spreads, which management has guided to maintain through 2026.

The numbers behind the operating thesis are solid. Revenue grew 10.1% to $845M in 2025, Core FFO/share compounded from $2.29 (2023) to $2.40 (2024) to $2.55 (2025), and Q1 2026 FFO rose 8.1% YoY with same-store NOI up 4.1%. GAAP net income swings are noise driven by lumpy disposition gains: Q1 2026 net income fell 32% only because the prior-year quarter booked a $49.9M sale gain versus $15.1M this period, while underlying FFO and NOI both grew. Management's commentary that "the multiyear weakness in demand for big-box product has reversed" is a genuine tailwind for STAG's larger distribution assets, and low speculative supply supports landlord pricing power.

The case against owning this for appreciation rather than income centers on three items: a heavy lease-expiration schedule (21.8% of ABR rolling through 2027, with 2028 and 2029 also heavy), a 120bp YoY same-store occupancy decline (97.8%→96.6%) in Q1 2026 that warrants watching, and a valuation that is rate-sensitive. At ~30x trailing P/E (a misleading metric for a REIT) and roughly 15.2x Core FFO, STAG trades below the industrial-REIT FFO-multiple cohort but offers no specific re-rating catalyst. The ~4.1% yield is well-covered, but solid operations, a conservative balance sheet, and no catalyst support a HOLD for income rather than a BUY.

## Company Overview

STAG operates as an UPREIT (Maryland corporation) running a single reporting segment with just 93 employees, a lean, externally-scalable platform. Its differentiated approach targets granular acquisitions of individual industrial assets across CBRE-EA "Tier 1" markets rather than concentrating in super-prime coastal logistics hubs. The portfolio carries a triple-net-leaning lease structure under which most operating expenses are passed through to tenants.

Diversification is the structural risk-mitigant: top 20 tenants represent only 16.3% of ABR (Amazon the largest at 2.8%), the top 20 markets account for 59.2% (Chicago largest at 8.2%), and no single industry exceeds 11.1% (Air Freight & Logistics). Operating Portfolio occupancy stood at 97.2% at year-end 2025, with same-store occupancy 96.6% at Q1 2026.

The stock currently trades near $38.88, with a ~$7.5B market cap, against a tight 52-week range of $33.72-$39.99. The shares have moved sideways over the past twelve months, oscillating between the mid-$34s and high-$39s without establishing a directional trend.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and FFO.** Revenue progressed from $657M (2022) to $708M (2023), $767M (2024), and $845M in 2025. Nareit FFO rose from $421M (2023) to $459M (2024) to $488M (2025). The cleaner per-share metric, Core FFO/share, compounded steadily. Q1 2026 produced FFO of $126.6M.

**GAAP net income is noise.** FY2025 net income of $279M (+44.5% YoY) was inflated by a $93.8M gain on property sales versus $32.3M in 2024. The earnings-surprise record shows four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 actual EPS $0.32 vs $0.23 estimate; Q4 2025 $0.44 vs $0.23), but these GAAP figures are distorted by disposition timing and should not be read as operating acceleration. FFO/share and same-store NOI are the metrics that matter.

**Leasing power is the standout.** FY2025 cash rent change was +24.0% and straight-line +38.2% (new plus renewal). Q1 2026 delivered +20.9% cash and +39.6% straight-line, with new-lease cash spreads hitting +35.5%. These spreads exist because years of below-market in-place rents mark up sharply on renewal, the core driver of organic NOI growth in a low-acquisition environment.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$3.2B at a weighted-average rate of 4.42% and weighted-average maturity of 4.3 years, with net debt to real estate cost basis at ~38%, conservative for the sector. Only 0.1% of debt is secured, and essentially all floating exposure has been swapped to fixed via 18 interest-rate swaps, leaving just the ~$200M revolver exposed (a 100bp move costs roughly $2M/year). Liquidity was $805.7M at Q1 2026 ($8.9M cash plus $796.8M undrawn revolver), with only ~9.4% of debt maturing in the next 12 months and full covenant compliance (the 0.60x max leverage ceiling is comfortably distant). The Altman Z-Score of 1.43 ("distress zone") is a false signal that penalizes the asset-heavy, leveraged, low-turnover REIT model; STAG is investment-grade and well-capitalized.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was ~$463M in FY2025. Growth is funded through a blend of dispositions (recycling lower-conviction assets at gains), ATM equity (forward sales), and unsecured notes ($550M issued in 2025 at 5.50-5.99%, with maturing Series D and G redeemed and Term Loan G extended to 2030). The financing is disciplined and well-laddered, with no distressed refinancing.

Strong cash flow generation, conservative and well-hedged leverage appropriate to the REIT model, and ample liquidity support a Financial Health score of 7.0, consistent with the trajectory's prior improvement and confirmed by the Q1 data.

## Growth Analysis

STAG's growth has shifted from external (acquisitions) to internal (re-leasing). Acquisition pace has been slow since 2022 as better-capitalized institutional buyers compress cap rates below STAG's cost of capital, blunting accretive external growth. What remains is the embedded below-market rent roll, which marks up at +18-40% on renewal and drives mid-single-digit FFO/share compounding.

The industry backdrop is supportive at the margin. Speculative construction starts remain low, which strengthens existing-landlord pricing power, and the supply pipeline is increasingly build-to-suit. Big-box demand has reversed off its multiyear weakness, a positive for STAG's larger distribution assets. Secular drivers — e-commerce, onshoring/nearshoring, and rising inventory-to-sales ratios that lift US warehouse demand — provide a durable demand floor.

Two qualifications temper the growth case. First, the same-store occupancy decline is the single metric most worth interrogating: if it reflects known timing/move-outs it is benign, but if it marks the start of softening it would compress the leasing spreads the entire thesis rests on. Second, STAG is pivoting toward development/build-to-suit (a Dallas project targeting ~7.4% yield, 7 projects with ~$149M of construction-in-progress at YE 2025), a sensible capital-allocation response to a competitive acquisition market that introduces lease-up and execution risk versus the traditional stabilized-acquisition model.

Estimate revisions are mixed in a way that aligns with the disposition-driven GAAP volatility: current-year EPS estimates rose +9.8% over 90 days (to $1.12) while near-term quarterly estimates were cut ~17-20% (reflecting disposition-gain timing rather than operating deterioration). Next-year estimates are essentially flat (-0.3% over 90 days). This is steady, not accelerating, and the growth thesis is one of durable compounding. Growth Prospects score: 6.3.

## Valuation Assessment

On the surface, STAG trades at ~30x trailing P/E (and the same forward figure per the data), but P/E is a poor lens for a REIT given depreciation and disposition-gain noise. The relevant metric is Core FFO/share of $2.55 (2025), placing the stock at roughly 15.2x trailing Core FFO. On consensus current-year EPS of $1.12 the P/E is ~35x, again distorted by REIT accounting.

Against the industrial-REIT peer set, the picture is mixed. Peer P/E medians sit at 29.2x (REXR 36.8x, EGP 36.8x, CUBE 29.2x, FR 24.3x, TRNO 16.3x), so STAG's ~30x P/E is roughly in line on a headline basis. On yield, STAG's ~4.1% sits modestly above the 3.2% peer median, reflecting its higher-organic-growth-but-smaller-and-more-rate-sensitive profile. The Core FFO multiple of ~15x is a discount to the higher-quality coastal in-fill names, justified by STAG's secondary-market positioning, single-tenant binary risk, and slower external growth.

The discount is defensible but does not constitute a mispricing with a catalyst. There is no identified event that would force a re-rating: no spin, no asset sale program at a premium to public NAV, no estimate inflection beyond steady compounding. The valuation framework rewards a high score only when there is a specific reason the market is wrong; here the market is pricing STAG appropriately as a steady, yield-supported compounder with a rate-sensitive multiple. The rate path is a live headwind, as yield-driven valuations and cap rates both face pressure if the 10Y stays elevated. Analyst targets bracket $38-$45 (mean $41.55, median $41.00). With the stock at $38.88, that implies modest upside to the mean (~7%) and to the high (~16%). The guidance for treating targets as floors applies only to cheap, inflecting stocks with rising estimates; STAG is a steady compounder near the low end of its analyst range, so the consensus target functions more as a fair-value anchor than a floor. ## Competitive Landscape

STAG occupies a distinct and defensible niche. Where Prologis and Rexford concentrate in coastal in-fill logistics and Realty Income competes on diversified net-lease scale, STAG harvests relative value in secondary/tertiary Tier-1 markets and single assets. This granular approach lets it capture higher cap rates and idiosyncratic opportunities while extreme tenant, industry, and geographic diversification dampens portfolio-level risk.

The competitive position is stable-to-improving. The leasing-spread data demonstrates real pricing power on the existing book, and the reversal of big-box weakness plus low speculative supply favor incumbent landlords. STAG is the higher-organic-growth, smaller, more rate- and leasing-sensitive name relative to Realty Income, which competes on scale and diversification.

The persistent constraint is acquisition competition from better-capitalized institutional buyers, which has kept external growth subdued since 2022 and is pushing STAG toward development. The trajectory shows competitive position improving from 6.0 to 7.0 across prior reports; the Q1 leasing data confirms the pricing power but the same-store occupancy slip and the structural inability to grow externally on accretive terms keep this from a higher mark. Competitive Position score: 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term lease-expiration wall.** Roughly 21.8% of ABR expires through 2027, with 2027 (12.9%), 2028 (13.3%), and 2029 (16.1%) all heavy. The entire internal-growth thesis depends on re-leasing this volume at the guided spreads. If industrial demand softens or new supply pressures rates, spreads compress and same-store growth fades. The same-store occupancy decline is the early metric to monitor.

**Single-tenant vacancy binary.** With most assets net-leased to single tenants, a vacancy takes a property from full rent to zero plus carrying costs until re-let. Diversification mitigates aggregate impact, but individual-asset cash-flow cliffs are real.

**Tariffs and trade policy.** The FY2025 10-K expanded its tariff/trade-policy risk language. STAG's tenant base is goods-handling (logistics, distribution, manufacturing components), and 2025 tariffs plus retaliatory measures could weaken tenant cash flows and space demand, directly relevant given the Air Freight/Logistics concentration and import-exposed industries.

**Interest-rate and refinancing.** Debt is well-laddered and hedged, but higher rates raise the cost of continuously rolling maturities and pressure cap rates and NAV. The stock's yield-driven valuation is directly exposed to the rate path.

**External-capital dependency.** As a REIT distributing 90%+ of taxable income, STAG must tap equity and debt markets to grow. ATM equity issuance is dilutive, and when cost of capital exceeds acquisition cap rates accretive external growth stalls, which is why acquisition pace has slowed.

The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors. None of these risks is a binary near-term catalyst, but the lease-roll dependency and rate sensitivity are not fully priced into a multiple that still sits near the upper half of its 52-week range. Risk Factors score: 5.8.

There is no informed positioning signaling an imminent catalyst in either direction, with mild downside hedging present but not significant.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** STAG's embedded below-market rent roll continues to mark up at the guided cash spreads through 2026 and beyond, sustaining ~8% FFO/share growth. The big-box demand reversal and low speculative supply extend landlord pricing power, the development pivot (7.4% Dallas yield) adds higher-return growth, and a stable-to-falling rate path eventually compresses the yield-driven discount to peers. Core FFO/share reaches ~$2.75-2.85 by 2027; at a re-rated ~16x Core FFO multiple the stock approaches $44-45, matching the high analyst target, with the dividend on top.

**Bear Case.** The same-store occupancy slip marks the start of softening; demand moderation and tariff-driven tenant stress compress leasing spreads below guidance, and the 21.8% expiration wall through 2027 re-leases at low-single-digit rather than +20% spreads. Higher rates expand cap rates, pressure NAV, and de-rate the yield multiple. FFO/share growth stalls toward flat, the multiple compresses to ~13x Core FFO, and the stock drifts toward $33-34 (the 52-week low), a ~13-15% decline, with the dividend the only return support.

The base case is steady compounding within the existing range: leasing spreads hold in the mid-teens, FFO/share grows mid-single-digits, and the stock tracks toward the low end of the analyst range (~$41) over twelve months while paying its yield. The risk/reward is roughly balanced, with the dividend providing a floor and the absence of a catalyst capping the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD for income, with a 12-month reassessment horizon anchored to the quarterly FFO and same-store occupancy cadence.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Same-store occupancy stabilizes or recovers above 97% AND cash leasing spreads hold at the guided 18-20% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the internal-growth engine through the 2027 expiration wall.
- The shares de-rate to a Core FFO multiple below ~13x (roughly $33-34) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring an attractive yield-plus-growth entry (yield approaching ~4.7%).
- A sustained shift in the rate path (10Y easing materially) that would compress the yield-driven discount and re-rate the multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store occupancy declines below ~95.5% AND leasing spreads compress to single digits, signaling the demand reversal has stalled and the growth thesis is breaking.
- A dividend coverage problem emerges (FFO payout climbing toward unsustainable levels) or the company cuts the distribution.
- A material tariff-driven tenant default cluster or a refinancing executed at distressed terms.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the Q2 2026 print), with particular attention to the same-store occupancy trajectory and any update to the 18-20% spread guidance.

## Conclusion

STAG Industrial is a well-run, conservatively-financed industrial REIT with a genuine and durable internal-growth engine: re-leasing years of below-market rents at the guided cash spreads. The balance sheet is healthy, the dividend is well-covered, and Core FFO/share has compounded reliably. The trajectory has improved across recent reports, and the Q1 2026 data confirms the operating thesis rather than reversing it.

The case for HOLD rather than BUY rests on three things the bulls must respect: a heavy lease-expiration schedule on which the entire growth story depends, a same-store occupancy slip that bears watching, and a yield-sensitive valuation with no specific re-rating catalyst. The stock trades at a fair-to-modest discount to peers, justified by its secondary-market and single-tenant profile, and sits near the low end of an analyst range that brackets it tightly. This is a quality income holding to own for its covered, growing dividend and steady FFO compounding, without the asymmetric upside that would warrant a BUY. **Recommendation: HOLD (income).**
