## Executive Summary

Crane Company is a focused industrial manufacturer executing a multi-year portfolio transformation through separation, divestiture, and acquisition. The company reported Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.65 (beating consensus by 10.6%), raised full-year guidance to $6.65-$6.85, and demonstrated strong backlog growth (+25% QoQ to $1.79B). Operating margins have expanded from 13.4% in 2023 to 19.8% (adjusted) in Q1 2026, driven by pricing power in mission-critical aerospace and process applications.

The investment case rests on continued margin expansion, acquisition synergy realization, and aerospace cycle tailwinds. However, valuation at ~26.5x forward adjusted EPS ($6.75 midpoint) is near industrial peers (median P/E 28.6x) for a company delivering mid-single-digit organic growth. The company also carries $1.2B of debt from a $1.36B acquisition completed only five months ago, with integration still in its early stages. The market has partially de-rated the stock from its January 2026 highs ($214) but the current price still embeds successful integration and sustained margin expansion with limited discount for execution risk.

This is a quality industrial business with demonstrable pricing power and improving competitive positioning, best held for its growth trajectory but not actionable as a new position at current levels given the integration overhang and sector multiple compression risk in a rising-rate environment.

## Company Overview

Crane Company is a 170-year-old industrial manufacturer with two segments: Aerospace & Advanced Technologies (AAT, ~45% of revenue) and Process Flow Technologies (PFT, ~55%). The company became independent in April 2023 following a separation from Crane Holdings and has since divested non-core assets (Engineered Materials, January 2025) while acquiring sensor-based technology businesses (Druck, Panametrics, Reuter-Stokes, Optek from Baker Hughes, $1.36B, closed January 2026).

The AAT segment supplies pressure sensors, braking systems, power conversion, and lubrication systems for commercial and military aircraft, with a 61%/39% commercial/military split and 66%/34% OEM/aftermarket split. PFT manufactures process valves, pumps, vacuum-insulated pipe, and sampling systems for pharmaceutical, cryogenic, chemical, nuclear, and water/wastewater applications.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is strong but acquisition-dependent.** Revenue grew from $2.09B (2023) to $2.31B (2025, +8.2% core + pricing) and is tracking approximately $2.8B annualized in Q1 2026 ($696.4M, +24.9% YoY). Of that growth, 18.3% was acquisition-driven, 3.8% core organic, and 2.7% FX. The organic growth rate, while positive, is mid-single-digit — consistent with guidance for the full year.

**Margin expansion is the primary earnings driver.** Adjusted operating margin reached 19.8% in Q1 2026, up from 16.7% in 2024 and 13.4% in 2023. AAT margins are particularly strong at 24.6%. Adjusted EPS has compounded from $4.16 (2023) to $6.12 (2025) to a guided $6.65-$6.85 for 2026 — a 28% CAGR since separation.

**Cash flow is strong on a full-year basis but seasonal.** Operating cash flow improved from $162M (2023) to $395M (2025). The light capex model (~$53M/year) produces healthy free cash flow. Q1 2026 was negative (-$40M), which is typical seasonal pattern for Crane.

**Balance sheet transformation carries risk.** Long-term debt rose from $248M to $1.19B following the Baker Hughes acquisition. Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 2.0x on a pro-forma annualized basis (~$600M estimated adjusted EBITDA). The maturity profile is favorable — $900M term loan and $900M revolver both mature in 2030. Interest expense quadrupled from $4.5M to $16.8M quarterly ($67M annualized).

**Backlog provides 12+ months of visibility.** Total backlog of $1.79B represents approximately 7.7 months of forward revenue at current run rates. AAT backlog of $1.19B grew 14% core YoY; PFT backlog of $606M is now supported by $222M from acquired businesses.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is anchored by: (1) acquisition contribution ramping through 2026 (Baker Hughes assets contributed $102M in Q1), (2) aerospace build-rate recovery (Boeing/Airbus production increases) driving AAT, and (3) aftermarket demand from high aircraft utilization rates.

The organic growth rate of mid-single digits is the critical variable. AAT core grew 9.4% in Q1 2026, but PFT core declined 0.6% on chemical market sluggishness. Management's 2026 guidance implies total growth of low-to-mid 20% (primarily acquisition) with mid-single-digit core. This makes the earnings trajectory highly dependent on successful integration and eventual PFT recovery.

Longer-term growth vectors include hydrogen economy infrastructure (CryoWorks acquisition positions for this), nuclear power generation (Reuter-Stokes acquisition), and pharmaceutical processing. These are legitimate secular tailwinds but are currently more thematic than revenue-material.

The 4-quarter earnings beat streak (average surprise +7.6%) and rising current-year estimates (+1.6% in 90 days to $6.78) provide confidence in near-term execution. However, next-quarter estimates have declined 3.1%, suggesting the market expects some deceleration from Q1's strong performance.

## Valuation Assessment

At $178.93, Crane trades at approximately 26.5x forward adjusted EPS ($6.75 midpoint) and 33.3x trailing GAAP EPS ($5.66, which includes acquisition-related charges). The peer median P/E is 28.6x (Valmont), with peers ranging from 25.6x (Flowserve) to 39.8x (SPX Technologies).

Crane's forward multiple is below the peer median trailing P/E of 28.6x. The question is whether Crane deserves a premium to peers on a forward basis given its growth profile. Arguments for: higher organic growth rate than most industrials (mid-single vs. low-single), margin expansion trajectory, mission-critical applications with pricing power. Arguments against: acquisition integration risk that peers don't carry, PFT segment facing chemical market headwinds, and interest expense headwind from quadrupled debt.

Analyst consensus price target is $219.67 mean / $225 median, with a range of $185-$235. The low target of $185 implies only 3.4% upside, indicating even the most bearish covering analyst sees limited downside. The high target of $235 implies 31% upside.

At 26.5x forward EPS, the stock is priced for successful execution. If integration falters or organic growth disappoints (chemical markets remain sluggish, aerospace slows), the multiple could compress to peer-level 24-25x forward, implying ~$163-169 — roughly 7-9% downside.

**Why not BUY despite 6.5 overall score:** The specific barrier is valuation relative to risk. Crane trades near peer multiples while carrying 2.0x net leverage (up from near-zero pre-acquisition) and integrating a complex multi-asset carve-out that closed five months ago. In prior calibration, Industrials BUYs have produced negative returns at aggregate level. At current valuation, the risk/reward is approximately symmetric — upside to $220 (23%) vs. downside to $163-169 (6-9%) in base and bear cases respectively. While the upside/downside ratio appears favorable, the bear case probability is elevated given the integration complexity, rising rate environment increasing interest burden on variable-rate debt, and CEO transition occurring simultaneously.

## Competitive Landscape

Crane occupies technology-differentiated niches in aerospace sensing, precision fluid handling, and industrial instrumentation. The company's competitive advantages include: (1) proprietary technology with long certification cycles creating switching costs, (2) aftermarket revenue streams (34% of AAT), (3) positioning in regulated applications requiring qualification and compliance, and (4) the Crane Business System for margin improvement.

The Baker Hughes acquisitions (Druck, Panametrics) strengthen Crane's sensor portfolio and add nuclear/energy applications. Post-acquisition, Crane has meaningfully broader instrumentation capabilities spanning pressure, flow, temperature, and radiation measurement.

Competitive risks include: Parker Hannifin and Emerson competing in overlapping process applications with larger R&D budgets; Boeing/Airbus production schedule dependency; and the potential for customers to qualify alternative suppliers during extended delivery timelines.

Pricing power evidence is strong: Crane offset $110.7M in tariff costs in 2025 through pricing and productivity, with net positive price impact of $66.3M. This demonstrated ability to pass through costs confirms genuine differentiation.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration Risk (HIGH).** The $1.36B Baker Hughes acquisition closed January 2026 — only five months of integration experience. These are carve-outs from a larger organization requiring standalone operations, systems separation, and cultural integration for ~1,900 employees. While Q1 results showed "above expectations" performance, the first year of a carve-out integration frequently encounters hidden costs (IT separation, stranded overhead, customer transition friction). Goodwill nearly doubled to $1.35B, creating impairment risk if returns disappoint.

**Interest Rate/Leverage Risk (MODERATE-HIGH).** Variable-rate debt of ~$1.15B means every 100bp rate increase costs ~$11.5M pre-tax. With the Fed potentially hiking, interest expense could continue rising. The company's interest coverage is still comfortable (>10x on adjusted EBITDA) but represents a margin headwind.

**Aerospace Cycle (MODERATE).** While current backlogs are at record levels, the aerospace aftermarket is tied to flight hours and the OEM business to production rates. A recession-driven decline in air travel or further production delays at Boeing could reduce AAT revenue growth. Defense budgets are structurally supported but subject to political dynamics.

**CEO Transition (LOW-MODERATE).** Alejandro Alcala assumed CEO role April 27, 2026. Max Mitchell remains as Executive Chairman. The planned transition reduces abrupt disruption risk, but the timing — during the largest acquisition integration in company history — adds execution concern.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($220-235, +23-31%):** Baker Hughes integration delivers above-plan synergies through 2026-2027. Aerospace build-rate recovery accelerates AAT organic growth to high-single-digits. Chemical market recovery lifts PFT core growth. Adjusted EPS reaches $7.50-8.00 in 2027. Multiple sustains at 28-30x forward earnings on premium growth profile. Stock re-rates to $220+ as integration risk fades.

**Bear Case ($155-165, -9-13%):** Integration of Baker Hughes assets encounters unexpected costs (IT separation overruns, customer attrition, cultural friction). Chemical market sluggishness persists, PFT organic declines. Interest rates rise further, compressing earnings by $5-10M annually. Multiple compresses to 22-24x on disappointing organic growth + rising leverage concerns. Stock trades to $155-165.

**Base Case ($195-210, +9-17%):** Integration proceeds on-plan through 2026. AAT growth remains mid-to-high single-digit. PFT stabilizes with flat core growth supplemented by acquisitions. Full-year 2026 adjusted EPS lands at $6.70-6.80 (within guidance). Stock grinds toward analyst consensus median of $225 over 12 months but faces headwinds from sector multiple compression in a rising-rate environment, settling in the $195-210 range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $160 or below, creating 24x forward P/E with unchanged estimates — providing genuine margin of safety against integration risk
2. Two consecutive quarters of Baker Hughes acquisition contribution exceeding plan by >10%, demonstrating clear synergy realization
3. PFT core organic growth returns to positive 3%+ on chemical market recovery, confirming both segments growing

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Full-year 2026 EPS guidance lowered below $6.40 (implying integration or organic growth challenges)
2. Net leverage exceeds 2.5x with no clear deleveraging trajectory
3. AAT book-to-bill ratio falls below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters, signaling aerospace deceleration

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026) — the next data point on integration execution, organic growth trajectory, and backlog conversion.

## Conclusion

Crane Company is a well-managed industrial business executing a thoughtful portfolio transformation with genuine competitive differentiation and demonstrated pricing power. The 4-quarter earnings beat streak, margin expansion trajectory, and $1.79B backlog support a constructive medium-term view. However, the stock's 26.5x forward multiple assumes successful integration of a complex $1.36B carve-out while carrying 2.0x net leverage in a rising-rate environment. If integration costs exceed management estimates by even $15-20M annually (a realistic outcome for a multi-asset carve-out), EPS falls to $6.40-6.50 and the multiple likely compresses to 24x = $154-156, representing 13-14% downside from current levels. At $160 or below, the risk/reward shifts meaningfully in favor of buyers. At current levels, hold and wait for either a better entry or two additional quarters confirming integration execution.
