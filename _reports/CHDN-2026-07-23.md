**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Churchill Downs Incorporated is a gaming and racing company built around the Kentucky Derby, one of the most durable brand assets in American sport, supplemented by a rapidly expanded portfolio of Historical Racing Machines (HRMs) concentrated in Kentucky and Virginia, an online wagering and gaming-technology segment, and ten regional casinos plus two high-value equity affiliates. The company delivered record FY2025 revenue of $2,926M (+7.0%) and record Adjusted EBITDA of $1,205M (+4.0%), yet the stock has fallen ~24% since its late-April high near $99.50 to ~$82, and now sits near its 52-week low of $80.24. It trades at roughly 12x consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.92, a wide discount to the analyst consensus target range of $110-155 and to the casino-and-gaming peer median P/E near 20x.

The drawdown does not reflect fundamental collapse. Q1 2026 was solid: revenue $663M (+3.1%), net income $83M (+7.8%), EPS $1.16 versus $1.02, Adjusted EBITDA $257M (+4.9%), and operating margin expanding to ~22% from ~21%. Consensus estimates have risen modestly across every window (current-year EPS +5.7% over 90 days). The market is pricing three distinct overhangs simultaneously: a $1.3B stack of 2027/2028 senior notes that must be refinanced into a higher-for-longer rate environment; the May 2025 Louisiana Supreme Court ruling that HHR is unconstitutional in that state, which establishes a legal template that could be applied to the far more important Kentucky and Virginia HRM fleets; and a deceleration in organic growth as the acquisition-and-build-out phase matures.

We rate CHDN a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.05, extending a four-report HOLD sequence. The valuation is genuinely attractive on forward and normalized metrics, the moat is real, and rising estimates argue against reading the price decline as thesis deterioration. What prevents an upgrade to BUY is the concentration of the growth engine in a legally contingent revenue stream (HRM), a refinancing wall that will raise interest expense regardless of outcome, and consumer-discretionary sensitivity into a probable H2 2026 spending softening. Accumulate on further weakness rather than chase; the bar to a full BUY is a resolution or clarification on one of the two binary overhangs.

## Company Overview

Churchill Downs is a ~150-year-old company headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky, whose flagship asset, the Kentucky Derby, run annually since 1875, is the longest continuously held annual sporting event in the United States. The business today is organized into three reportable segments:

**Live and Historical Racing (L&HR)** is the growth engine. It comprises Churchill Downs Racetrack and a portfolio of ~10,190 HRMs across Kentucky, Virginia, and New Hampshire. FY2025 segment revenue was $1,442M (+13.8% YoY) with segment Adjusted EBITDA of $637M (+10.9%), a ~44% margin, the highest-margin and fastest-growing part of the company.

**Wagering Services and Solutions** (formerly TwinSpires) includes online horse-race ADW wagering, the Exacta central-determinant HRM technology platform, a residual stake in United Tote, and sports betting. FY2025 revenue was $526M with Adjusted EBITDA of $177M. TwinSpires operates its own ADW platform, and CDI provides white-label ADW technology to sports-betting operators such as FanDuel and DraftKings, which are its customers/partners.

**Gaming** operates ten wholly-owned regional casinos across nine states, plus two equity investments carried under the equity method: 61.3% of Rivers Casino Des Plaines (Chicago) and 50% of Miami Valley Gaming (Ohio). FY2025 revenue was $1,049M with Adjusted EBITDA of $483M (–4.7%), the only segment in decline.

The economic core is two moats. The Derby is a non-replicable brand with global TV reach and pricing power; the 151st running set records for handle and attendance, and the 152nd (May 2026) drew a record TV audience. The HRM business is a second, subtler moat: CDI has secured HRM licenses in Kentucky and Virginia, states that restrict full commercial casinos, effectively building casino-like economics behind a regulatory barrier. Vertical integration through Exacta, which supplies the HRM technology, captures additional margin as CDI opens more owned venues.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** CDI has grown revenue meaningfully since 2022 ($1,810M → $2,462M → $2,734M → $2,926M, up ~62% over three years), driven by the 2022 Peninsula Pacific acquisition, the 2023 Exacta acquisition, and a wave of new HRM openings (The Rose, Terre Haute, Owensboro, Roseshire, Rosie's Richmond expansion). That growth is now transitioning from acquisition-and-build-out to organic HRM ramp, and the rate is decelerating: Q1 2026 revenue grew 3.1% versus double-digit prior-year comparisons.

**Margins and profitability.** FY2025 operating margin compressed to 23.4% from 25.9%, but the decline is largely explained by an $85.1M Chasers gaming-rights impairment (a $47.5M asset impairment line item) plus transaction and pre-opening costs, not core deterioration. Q1 2026 operating margin expanded to ~22% from ~21%. ROE is a headline-flattering 38.2%, buoyed by a thin, buyback-eroded equity base; ROA at 5.2% is the more sober read on asset efficiency for a capital-intensive operator.

**Cash flow quality is high.** Operating cash flow was $769.8M in FY2025, comfortably exceeding net income of $383M, a positive earnings-quality signal. Q1 2026 OCF jumped to $295M (+$48M) on working-capital timing tied to Derby advance ticket sales; deferred revenue rose to $185M. Project capex of $204.7M in 2025 is guided down to $180-220M for 2026, indicating the peak build-out spend is behind the company and free cash flow generation should improve.

**The balance sheet is the central analytical issue.** Total debt was $5.16B at year-end 2025, reduced to $4.95B by Q1 2026 through revolver paydown. On a screen, debt/equity of 6.37x and an Altman Z-Score of 0.93 ("distress zone") look alarming. Both are artifacts. The Z-Score model is calibrated for manufacturers, not asset-heavy gaming operators with deliberately thin book equity. The credit metrics that actually govern the company are healthy: consolidated secured net leverage of 1.4x against a <4.0x covenant, and interest coverage of 3.9x against a >2.5x requirement. Gross leverage of ~4.2x ($1.2B Adjusted EBITDA against ~$5B debt) is normal for the sector, and the company was fully covenant-compliant. The current ratio of 0.6 is likewise normal for gaming, where customers pay upfront and inventory is minimal.

The real financial headwind is twofold. First, interest expense of $297.7M in FY2025 consumed ~10% of revenue, on debt that is ~37% floating-rate; a 100bp SOFR move cuts net income and OCF by ~$14M. Second, and more consequential, a refinancing wall is approaching: $600M of 2027 notes (5.50%) and $700M of 2028 notes (4.75%) must be refinanced, plus a $1.1B revolver and Term Loan A due 2029 with a springing maturity. Management has stated it is exploring options to fund the upcoming senior note maturities. Refinancing at current rates will raise interest expense materially; the 2031 notes already carry 6.75%. With the higher-for-longer rate path now the base case, this is a quantifiable EPS drag rather than a solvency question.

**Capital allocation** has been shareholder-friendly but is pivoting. CDI repurchased $425.3M of stock in 2025, shrinking the basic share count from 74.0M to 71.4M, and raised the dividend for the 15th consecutive year (+7% to $0.438/share, a modest ~0.5% yield). Roughly $430M of buyback authority remained at Q1 2026. Buybacks then slowed sharply to ~$3M in Q1 2026 as management prioritized $188M of revolver paydown ahead of the note maturities, a prudent shift that also tempers the per-share growth support from repurchases.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case rests on the L&HR segment. HRM openings and ramps—Owensboro (Feb 2025), Roseshire (Sep 2025), the Rosie's Richmond expansion, Marshall Yards (Feb 2026), and the New Hampshire Rockingham Grand Casino (825 HRMs plus 32 table games, opening mid-2027)—provide a multi-year pipeline of new capacity in limited-competition markets. Exacta compounds this internally: it both supplies the HRM technology and benefits as CDI opens more owned venues, a self-reinforcing margin loop that drove most of the Wagering segment's FY2025 growth.

The Derby franchise continues to premiumize. Churchill Downs Racetrack reinvestment includes a $280-300M "Victory Run"/first-turn building (2026-2028) and a $30M Finish Line Suites/Mansion renovation for the 152nd Derby, both aimed at sustaining pricing power. NBC's move of the Oaks to prime time in 2026 extends the media reach. The April 2026 agreement to acquire the intellectual property of the Preakness Stakes and Black-Eyed Susan Stakes from 1/ST Racing for $85M, then license it back to Maryland for an annual fee, is a low-capital, annuity-like play that gives CDI the trademarks of two of the three Triple Crown races. It is a high-ROIC brand consolidation that deepens the moat with minimal balance-sheet impact.

Estimate revisions are supportive rather than dramatic. Current-year EPS consensus has risen 5.7% over 90 days to $6.92, next-quarter estimates are up 5.0%, and current-quarter and next-year estimates are marginally higher. This is positive earnings momentum, but it falls well short of the >30% revision velocity that would signal a mispriced re-rating. The growth here is durable and moat-supported, compounding in the mid-single-digits to low-double-digits, not accelerating.

Two structural headwinds temper the growth score. Thoroughbred racing is shrinking—~29,000 U.S. races in 2025, down 4.7% YoY—which is why CDI is concentrating value in premium events rather than volume racing. The Gaming segment faces secular pressure from new regional casino supply and iGaming cannibalization (live in seven states, with Maine authorized in January 2026), plus prediction markets as an emerging, unregulated competitive frontier. Q1 2026 already showed Central Virginia softness from increased competition and unfavorable weather, and Rivers Des Plaines saw a $7.8M FY2025 EBITDA decline from competition.

## Valuation Assessment

CHDN trades at ~$82, or ~11.3x FY2027 EPS of $7.28. This is a wide discount to the casino-and-gaming peer median P/E of 19.9x (RRR 19.9x, MCRI 21.6x; CZR and PENN show negative earnings, and BYD's 3.8x is an outlier likely distorted by one-time items). Adjusting for CDI's superior moat quality, the Derby's irreplaceable brand, and the higher-margin HRM growth engine, the stock arguably deserves a premium to that median rather than a ~40% discount.

The consensus analyst target range is $110 (low) to $155 (high), with a mean of $136 and median of $137; the entire range sits well above the current price. Truist reiterated a Buy with a $145 target in June 2026. On stocks with rising estimates and a sub-15x forward multiple, analyst targets tend to lag rather than lead, which argues the consensus range is a floor rather than a ceiling. The guidance here is measured: the estimate revisions are positive but modest, so we treat the target range as a genuine gap the market is discounting for specific reasons rather than pure mispricing.

Applying our own math: a 15x multiple on FY2026 EPS of $6.92, still below the peer median and well below where a franchise of this quality historically trades, implies ~$104, roughly 27% above the current price. A more normalized mid-cycle multiple of 16-17x on FY2027 EPS implies $116-124. On an EV/EBITDA basis, ~$5.8B market cap plus ~$4.95B net debt against $1.2B Adjusted EBITDA is ~9x, cheap for a business with two moats and a growing high-margin segment. The Rivers Des Plaines equity investment is a further under-appreciated value driver: CDI's carrying investment is $573M but the net basis difference to underlying equity is $833M, and it contributes ~$36M/quarter in equity income and ~$25M/quarter in cash distributions.

We assign a valuation score of 6.8. The forward and normalized metrics are genuinely attractive, and the cheap multiple does not reflect structural impairment: the business is growing and cash-generative. We stop short of a higher score because the discount is not pure mispricing: the refinancing wall and the HHR regulatory tail are real reasons a rational buyer would demand a lower entry multiple, and a low P/E alone, per our discipline, warrants skepticism absent a catalyst that closes the gap. The catalyst path here is refinancing clarity and HHR precedent containment, both of which are unresolved.

## Competitive Landscape

CDI's competitive position is strong but its trajectory is mixed, which is why we hold this dimension at 7.0 after a decline from 7.5 in prior reports. The Derby is a genuine economic moat—a non-replicable brand with global reach, luxury sponsor appeal, and demonstrated pricing power. The Preakness IP acquisition extends that moat across two of three Triple Crown races. Within HRM, CDI has built a first-mover position in Kentucky and Virginia, capturing casino economics in markets closed to full commercial casinos, and its Exacta ownership integrates the technology layer.

The offsetting pressures are real. The Gaming segment is losing share to new regional supply and iGaming; it is the only shrinking segment. Central Virginia and Rivers Des Plaines both cited competitive intensification. Thoroughbred racing volume is in secular decline. iGaming legalization is spreading into states where CDI operates brick-and-mortar and HRM venues, a direct cannibalization threat the 10-K explicitly flags. Prediction markets are an emerging, unregulated competitive vector.

The net assessment: CDI's premium-brand and HRM-arbitrage positions are widening, but its regional-casino position is eroding at the margin, and the growth engine is increasingly concentrated in a revenue stream (HRM) whose legal foundation is contested. The trajectory is stable-to-improving at the franchise core and deteriorating at the periphery.

## Risk Assessment

**HHR legal and constitutional risk is the single most important company-specific risk.** In May 2025 the Louisiana Supreme Court affirmed that the state's 2021 HHR Act is unconstitutional, ruling HHR a "new form of gaming" requiring parish referendums. CDI discontinued all Louisiana HRM operations and relocated ~500 machines to Virginia, costing ~$18.9M of revenue and ~$8.1M of Adjusted EBITDA in FY2025 and dragging Gaming comps into 2026 (–$9M revenue in Q1 2026). The direct financial hit is manageable, but the precedent is the concern: opponents could attempt to replicate this challenge in Kentucky or Virginia, which now house the bulk of the HRM fleet and profit growth. An adverse ruling in either state would be materially damaging to the core growth thesis. This is a low-probability, high-impact binary that is difficult to price and is the primary reason the risk score sits at 5.0.

**Refinancing and rate risk** is the second material factor. Refinancing the 2027/2028 notes and the 2029 revolver/Term Loan A at today's rates raises interest expense; management is actively exploring funding options. The covenant metrics remain comfortable, so this is a quantifiable EPS drag rather than a solvency question.

**Consumer-discretionary sensitivity** is a live macro risk. Regional gaming and HRM revenue track consumer confidence, and the probability of an H2 2026 consumer spending softening is meaningful. Q1 2026 already showed Central Virginia weakness.

**Impairment risk** on the $900M goodwill and $2.5B of intangibles (mostly indefinite-lived gaming rights valued via the assumption-heavy Greenfield Method) is demonstrated by the $85.1M Chasers write-off; the 10-Q flags Presque Isle (PA) as being monitored for potential future impairment. **Single-event concentration** on Derby weekend carries outsized weather, horse-safety, and security tail risk.

A June 26, 2025 8-K (Item 5.02, officer/director change) warrants a flag for follow-up. The item type indicates an officer/director change that could not be fully characterized from the filing index, and a scoring analyst should confirm whether it was a routine board rotation or a more significant departure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV has risen from 35.7% to 47.2% over the past six weeks while the stock fell 4%, and the term structure is in backwardation (47.2% near-term versus 44.1% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print. IV is rich relative to 32.9% historical volatility (a 14.3pp spread), so premium sellers are favored and buyers are overpaying for protection. The put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.1%): despite the drawdown, there is no pronounced downside-hedging panic. The elevated put/call OI ratio of 6.61 (up from 4.22) reflects accumulated open interest rather than fresh directional bearishness, given the muted put/call volume ratio of 0.34. Net read: the market expects a volatile earnings event but is not positioned aggressively bearish, consistent with our fundamental view that the drawdown reflects overhang-pricing rather than thesis collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($116-145).** The refinancing wall clears at manageable rates, removing the largest overhang, and the market re-rates the stock toward its peer multiple and analyst target range. HRM ramps (Rockingham Grand mid-2027, Marshall Yards, Roseshire) compound, Exacta margins widen, and the Preakness IP deal deepens the Derby franchise. No HHR challenge succeeds in Kentucky or Virginia. Consumer spending holds. At 16-17x normalized FY2027 EPS of $7.28, the stock reaches $116-124; a full close of the gap to the Truist $145 target would require multiple re-rating to ~20x, in line with peers. Upside of 40-77% from current levels.

**Bear case ($60-70).** An HHR challenge surfaces in Kentucky or Virginia, threatening the core growth engine and triggering a valuation de-rating on the most profitable segment. Refinancing at 6.75%-plus rates adds annual interest expense, compressing EPS. A consumer recession hits regional gaming and HRM volumes, and further intangible impairments (Presque Isle) dent reported earnings. At ~10x depressed EPS with a growth-thesis crack, the stock falls to $60-70. Downside of ~15-27% from current levels.

**Base case ($104).** The refinancing is completed at a modestly higher blended rate, HHR challenges do not materialize in Kentucky or Virginia in the coverage window, and HRM growth continues in the mid-single-digits to low-double-digits. Estimates continue to grind higher, and the multiple re-rates partway toward peers. At ~15x FY2026 EPS, the stock reaches ~$104. This sits between the bull and bear scenarios and is our most-likely outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The forward valuation is attractive and the moat is real, but the binary HHR regulatory tail and the refinancing overhang cap conviction below BUY. Accumulate on further weakness toward the high-$70s rather than chase.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Successful refinancing of the 2027/2028 notes at a blended rate that does not materially raise interest expense (e.g., within ~50bp of the maturing coupons), removing the largest single overhang.
- Legislative or judicial affirmation of HHR legality in Kentucky and/or Virginia, or a full coverage window passing with no credible constitutional challenge surfacing in either state.
- A pullback toward the high-$70s that widens the risk/reward, combined with continued positive estimate revisions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A filed constitutional or legislative challenge to HHR in Kentucky or Virginia that reaches a state supreme court or credible referendum path.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turning negative, or Q2/Q3 2026 revenue growth decelerating below ~2% YoY on core (ex-Louisiana) comps.
- A refinancing outcome that adds >$50M of annual interest expense, or a dividend/buyback suspension signaling balance-sheet stress.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next earnings print (implied by the options backwardation to be within weeks) is the immediate checkpoint. The more consequential reassessment points are any refinancing announcement on the 2027 notes and any HHR-related legislative or litigation news from Kentucky or Virginia over the next 6-12 months.

## Conclusion

Churchill Downs is a high-quality franchise trading at a distressed-looking multiple for reasons that are real but not fatal. At ~12x forward EPS, ~9x EV/EBITDA, and a ~40% discount to both peers and the full analyst target range, the market is discounting a refinancing wall, an HHR regulatory precedent, and decelerating growth. The refinancing is a quantifiable rate drag rather than a solvency issue; the HHR precedent is a genuine binary tail; and the growth, while decelerating, remains moat-supported and is producing rising estimates. The 24% drawdown reflects overhang-pricing rather than fundamental deterioration: Q1 2026 was solid, margins expanded, and consensus estimates rose across every window.

We maintain the four-report HOLD with an overall score of 6.05, a marginal continuation of a gently declining trajectory. The score reflects an attractive valuation (6.8) and durable moat (7.0) offset by a leveraged, refinancing-exposed balance sheet (5.5), a binary regulatory tail (5.0), and neutral insider signals (5.8). The path to a BUY is refinancing clarity and HHR containment. Until one of those resolves favorably, or the price falls further into the high-$70s, hold and accumulate on weakness rather than press at current levels. The asymmetry is favorable but not yet compelling enough to override the two overhangs the market is pricing.
