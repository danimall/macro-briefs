## Executive Summary

Copart operates at the center of the U.S. salvage vehicle auction market with an essentially irreplaceable physical infrastructure moat (over 250 locations across 11 countries, with substantial owned land holdings), a global buyer network spanning 185+ countries, and the strongest balance sheet in the specialty industrial services space. The stock has declined 47% from its 52-week high of $63.85 to $33.44, driven by a confluence of hurricane lapping effects (Q2 FY2026 revenue -3.6%), an unresolved DOJ money laundering investigation, rising uninsured driver headwinds, and tariff/export risk affecting nearly 40% of U.S. vehicle unit sales.

The fundamental quality of this business remains exceptional: 36.5% operating margins, 33% net margins, 15% ROA, zero debt, and a structural growth tailwind from rising total loss frequency. Management's pivot to aggressive buybacks ($1.1B in ~3 months at $37-40/share) represents a significant capital allocation signal and a genuine shift in shareholder return philosophy. At 21x forward earnings with substantial cash backing the valuation, the stock is cheap relative to its quality — but two specific unresolved risks (DOJ investigation with unpredictable outcome; export buyer vulnerability to tariffs in a trade-war environment) create binary downside scenarios that prevent full conviction until clarity emerges.

## Company Overview

Copart is the world's largest provider of online vehicle auctions and remarketing services, processing salvage, used, and wholesale vehicles through its proprietary VB3 platform. The company serves insurance companies (81% of volume) under customized agreements that generally provide Copart with designated geographic areas for vehicle processing. It operates on a predominantly agency model — Copart does not own most vehicles it sells, eliminating inventory risk.

The business operates in 11 countries with over 250 locations. Copart's significant owned land holdings (PP&E of $3.6B at July 31, 2025) represent a critical competitive barrier: zoning restrictions make new salvage yard development nearly impossible in urbanizing markets, giving Copart a physical infrastructure advantage that cannot be readily replicated by a well-funded competitor.

Revenue split: U.S. 83%, International 17%. Adjacent businesses include National Powersport Auctions (motorcycles), Purple Wave (heavy equipment), and CashForCars (direct consumer purchases).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2022 $3.50B → FY2023 $3.87B → FY2024 $4.24B → FY2025 $4.65B. The ~10% annual growth rate was remarkably consistent until Q2 FY2026 (-3.6% YoY), which management attributes to lapping Hurricanes Helene and Milton from fall 2024.

**Profitability:** Operating margin of 36.5% (FY2025). Net margin of 33.3%. FCF margin of 26.5% ($1.23B on $4.65B revenue). These margins are extraordinary for an inventory-light but land/facility-intensive services business, reflecting the structural operating leverage of the platform model.

**Balance sheet:** Cash of $5.1B as of January 31, 2026, against total liabilities of $788M. Zero drawn debt. Current ratio of 8.42. Altman Z-Score of 9.13. The company replaced its secured revolver with a new unsecured $1.25B facility in January 2026 (maturity 2031), reflecting pristine credit quality. After quarter-end through March 2, 2026, Copart deployed $898.7M in share repurchases, reducing the pro forma cash position to approximately $4.2B before accounting for interim cash generation.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow was $1.80B in FY2025, up 22% YoY. Interest income alone ($104M in H1 FY2026) from Treasury Bills on the cash hoard contributes meaningfully to earnings.

**Capital allocation:** After years of hoarding cash, Copart deployed approximately $1.1B in buybacks between January and March 2026, purchasing ~30M shares at average prices of $37-40. At the current price of $33.44, management bought significantly above where the stock trades now — either they misjudged, or the stock has overshot to the downside.

**EPS trajectory:** Consensus is $1.57 current year (FY2026) and $1.68 next year (FY2027). Estimates have declined 5-7% over the past 90 days, stabilizing in the past 30 days. The most recent quarter (Q2 FY2026) saw diluted EPS decline 10% year-over-year to $0.36 from $0.40 in the prior-year period, breaking a pattern of consistent EPS growth.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular tailwinds remain intact:**
1. Total loss frequency continues rising — vehicle repair costs escalate as cars incorporate more sensors, cameras, aluminum body panels, and increasingly complex electric drivetrains. This structurally grows Copart's addressable volume.
2. Average vehicle age has risen to 12.8 years (2025), meaning older vehicles are more likely to be totaled rather than repaired.
3. International demand expansion from developing-world buyers seeking affordable mobility drives bidding intensity.

**Near-term headwinds are real but cyclical:**
- Uninsured driver increase (from premium inflation) reduces the insured vehicle pool. This self-corrects as insurance rates stabilize — ClearBridge Investments explicitly calls this "temporary and self-correcting."
- Hurricane lapping effect mechanically depresses H1 FY2026 comparisons.
- Estimate revisions are -5% to -7% over 90 days but have stabilized (flat to -0.3% over 30 days), suggesting the downward revision cycle may be largely complete.

**International growth requires nuance:** International service revenue grew from $357M (FY2023) to $517M (FY2025) — 44% in two years. Q2 FY2026 international service revenue increased 7.7%, though Copart's MD&A notes that after excluding $8.4M of favorable foreign exchange impact, international growth was driven primarily by higher revenue per car, offset by a decrease in volume. The company opened 12 new international locations in the past 3 years, with particular focus on Spain, but the quality of international growth is more mixed than headline numbers suggest.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.44 with EPS of $1.59 (trailing) and $1.57 estimated for FY2026, CPRT trades at approximately 21x earnings. This metric understates the value proposition because it ignores the substantial cash position. On a pro forma basis, cash is approximately $4.2B (before interim cash generation), or roughly $4.4-4.5/share. On an enterprise value basis, EV is approximately $27-28B, and EV/operating income of $1.70B equals ~16-17x — reasonable for a business with this margin profile and growth trajectory.

Analyst consensus targets range from approximately $32 to $55, with a median around $44. The current price near the low end of the analyst range implies either the bear case is fully priced or the market is pricing worse-than-bear-case outcomes. The median target implies approximately 32% upside.

Peer comparison: Copart has historically traded at 30-40x earnings reflecting its quality and growth. At 21x, this is the cheapest the stock has been on a P/E basis in over a decade. The de-rating is substantial but appears to be pricing in significant structural headwinds rather than a cyclical trough.

The substantial cash position provides meaningful downside support: even adjusting for the post-quarter buybacks and ongoing capital needs, the operating business at the current price is being valued at approximately 18-19x current earnings — inexpensive for a 33% net margin franchise with structural growth tailwinds. However, cash can be deployed for capex, acquisitions, legal settlements, or further buybacks, and should not be treated as a guaranteed valuation floor.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. salvage insurance auction market is dominated by Copart (#1) and IAA/RB Global (#2), though Copart's 10-K also identifies Carvana, Openlane, Manheim, ACV Auctions, and LKQ as competitors across various vehicle remarketing segments.

**Moat components:**
- Physical land assets: Over 250 locations with significant owned property and zoning protections that prevent competitive replication.
- Two-sided marketplace: ~1M registered bidders in 185+ countries create network effects where more buyers attract more sellers (insurance companies), and vice versa.
- Catastrophe response infrastructure: Purpose-built capacity to absorb vehicle surges from storms creates switching costs with insurers who cannot risk service failures during high-volume events.
- Technology platform: VB3 system with proprietary patents.
- Regulatory expertise: Deep DMV integration and title processing capabilities across all 50 states.

The competitive position is stable to improving. The shift from principal to consignment models internationally aligns with the higher-margin U.S. approach.

## Risk Assessment

**DOJ Money Laundering Investigation (PRIMARY RISK — BINARY, UNQUANTIFIABLE):** Since October 2023, the DOJ has been investigating Copart's practices for preventing and detecting money laundering by auction platform members. A substantial portion of U.S. vehicle unit sales go to international buyers, often in cash-intensive markets with known money laundering associations. The company states it "is unable to predict the duration, scope, or result" and "the range of possible loss." After 2+ years with no charges filed, a negotiated resolution (consent decree with enhanced compliance requirements and a fine) represents one plausible outcome, but the filing explicitly does not support any probability-weighted conclusion. The tail scenario (criminal charges, restrictions on international buyer access) would fundamentally impair the business model.

**Export/Tariff Vulnerability:** In the current trade-war environment, the risk that vehicle exports face new duties or restrictions is elevated. Copart's 10-K discloses risks from import duties, tariffs, trade restrictions, and regulatory actions that could reduce demand or increase buyer costs. In a hypothetical scenario where international bidding declined 10-20% (analyst estimate), auction prices could fall, reducing Copart's percentage-based fees and compressing margins.

**Estimate Revision Momentum:** Negative. Current year estimates down 5.1% over 90 days, next year down 6.6%. While stabilized at the 30-day mark, there is no positive revision catalyst visible until hurricane comparisons normalize in H2 FY2026 or insurance rates stabilize enough to reduce the uninsured driver cohort.

**G&A cost trajectory:** G&A grew 20% in FY2025 and 34% in FY2024. Although showing signs of moderation (U.S. G&A ex-D&A declined 2.6% in H1 FY2026), sustained cost pressure — particularly if DOJ compliance requirements expand — could compress the margin structure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($44-55, +32% to +64%):**
- DOJ investigation resolves with a manageable fine ($100-300M) and no operational restrictions on international buyers.
- Hurricane lapping effects roll off in H2 FY2026, restoring positive revenue comps.
- Insurance rates stabilize, reversing uninsured driver headwind and restoring volume growth.
- Continued buybacks at depressed prices create per-share value accretion. While the company has demonstrated willingness to repurchase aggressively, the pace of future buybacks remains a management decision, not a commitment.
- Secular total loss frequency trends reassert, driving 8-12% annual revenue growth.
- Stock re-rates toward 25-30x as growth resumes, implying $39-50 on FY2027 EPS of $1.68.

**Bear Case ($25-28, -16% to -25%):**
- DOJ imposes restrictions on international buyer access or files criminal charges, structurally impairing the substantial portion of U.S. unit sales going to foreign bidders.
- New tariffs/export restrictions reduce international demand, compressing auction prices and Copart's percentage-based fees.
- Uninsured driver headwind persists longer than expected as insurance rates remain elevated in a stagflationary environment.
- G&A costs re-accelerate on compliance requirements.
- Multiple compresses to 15-17x on slowing growth and regulatory uncertainty.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. DOJ resolution announcement — specifically, a settlement without operational restrictions on international buyers.
2. Positive EPS revision momentum (estimates turning upward over 30 days) indicating volume recovery has begun.
3. Q4 FY2026 or Q1 FY2027 earnings demonstrating resumed revenue growth as hurricane lapping effects fully normalize.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. DOJ files formal charges or imposes restrictions on international buyer participation.
2. Revenue declines persist beyond H1 FY2026, indicating secular rather than cyclical headwinds.
3. Management slows or halts buyback program despite continued price weakness, signaling reduced confidence.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late May/early June 2026 for Q3 FY2026, which ended April 30, 2026). The hurricane lapping effect should be largely complete by then, providing cleaner underlying trend visibility.

## Validation Rebuttal: Why HOLD at 7.17 Overall Score

The specific risk preventing a BUY recommendation is the **DOJ money laundering investigation**, which is: (a) binary in outcome, (b) explicitly unquantifiable by the company itself ("unable to predict the range of possible loss"), (c) directly threatens the portion of U.S. unit sales going to international buyers if operational restrictions are imposed, and (d) has been unresolved for 2+ years with no visible timeline for closure. This is falsifiable: if the DOJ announces a resolution, the HOLD converts to BUY immediately.

The secondary factor is **export/tariff vulnerability in the current geopolitical environment.** With trade tensions elevated and tariffs being applied across multiple categories, the probability that international vehicle exports face new restrictions is above historical norms. A material decline in international bidding would reduce Copart's revenue through lower auction prices on its percentage-incentive contracts. This risk is amplified by compressed purchasing power in developing economies that constitute Copart's international buyer base.

These are specific risks affecting the company's largest growth driver (international buyers) that have demonstrably contributed to the 47% stock decline and negative estimate revisions. The business quality warrants a BUY at this price absent these overhangs; the overhangs are what prevent it.

## Conclusion

Copart is an exceptional business trading at a decade-low valuation due to a combination of cyclical headwinds (hurricane lapping, uninsured drivers) and structural overhang (DOJ investigation, export vulnerability). The pro forma cash position (~$4.2B) and zero-debt balance sheet provide meaningful downside support, and management's aggressive buyback initiation signals conviction in the stock's undervaluation. The competitive moat (owned land, market dominance, global network) is as wide as ever.

The investment recommendation is HOLD with bullish lean and a clear upgrade path. The stock likely represents an excellent entry point for investors with a 12-18 month horizon and tolerance for DOJ resolution risk. However, with the investigation unresolved after 2+ years, and with international buyer vulnerability elevated in the current trade-war environment, the risk-adjusted case does not yet clear the threshold for a new BUY at the required confidence level. Monitor DOJ resolution and Q3 FY2026 earnings (April quarter) for upgrade catalysts.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Pro forma cash position is approximately $4.2B (not $5.1B) after disclosed post-quarter buybacks of $898.7M; the DOJ outcome characterization and tariff demand-impact scenarios are analyst estimates, not sourced from primary filings; international growth quality is weaker than headline figures suggest due to FX benefit and volume decline — collectively these reduce confidence in the downside-support and upside-accretion elements of the thesis.
