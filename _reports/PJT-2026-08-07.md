## Executive Summary

PJT Partners is an advisory-only investment bank with three revenue lines — strategic advisory, restructuring and special situations, and PJT Park Hill private capital advisory — no balance sheet, no funded debt, and no recurring revenue. The operating story through H1 2026 is good: revenue +23.7% to $904.5M, Q2 revenue +19.5% to $486.3M, adjusted if-converted EPS +35.5% to $3.51, and a compensation ratio that has fallen from 69.8% (FY2023) to 67.0% (H1 2026). Pretax margin has expanded roughly 460bp over three years to 20.2% on 49% cumulative revenue growth. That margin expansion, achieved on a fixed senior-partner base, is the mechanism that turns cyclical revenue into per-share compounding.

The central analytical issue in PJT is share count. The quoted $4.4B market cap reflects Class A shares only (25.56M), while the economic enterprise carries 45.08M fully diluted shares as of 2026-06-30 — PJT Partners Inc. owns only 64.7% of the operating partnership, with 35.3% held by partners through exchangeable units. Dividing the $4.4B Class A capitalization by consolidated FY2025 net income of $309.7M produces a ~14x multiple that is arithmetically incoherent: it divides roughly 57% of the equity value by 100% of the earnings. On the company's own if-converted basis the correct figures are ~21.6x LTM adjusted EPS (~$7.96), 21.5x FY2026 consensus ($7.99) and 19.0x FY2027 consensus ($9.05), against a 23.0x peer median. PJT is modestly cheap relative to peers for materially faster growth, which is neither the deep-value situation a naive screen suggests nor the expensive one the quoted 28.1x trailing P/E implies.

We maintain HOLD, with the overall score easing to 6.6 from 6.8. Fundamentals confirmed the improving trajectory: the Q2 print beat by 24%, estimates rose 6.3% (current year) and 5.9% (next year) over 90 days, and the balance sheet remains debt-free with $535M of cash and investments. What has changed is entry price. The stock has recovered 34.7% from the March 13 low of $127.73 and now sits 8.0% below the analyst mean of $187.20 and 12.0% below its 52-week high. Our base case of $190 implies +10.4% over twelve months against a $125 bear case (-27.4%); probability-weighted expected return of roughly +6% is positive but sits below the bar for committing new capital in a sector where selection has historically added little above beta.

## Company Overview

PJT was spun out of Blackstone on October 1, 2015, combining Blackstone's advisory businesses with Paul J. Taubman's PJT Capital LP. Taubman remains Chairman and CEO. The firm employed 1,224 people including 133 partners at 2025-12-31, served 436 clients in FY2025 of which 255 generated fees of $1M or more, and no client exceeded 10% of revenues, a genuine structural strength versus most advisory boutiques.

The three franchises:

**Strategic advisory** — M&A, divestitures, shareholder-engagement defence (the 2018 CamberView acquisition), special committee work. Management describes this as the primary growth engine and posted record H1 revenue.

**Restructuring and special situations** — liability management, debtor and creditor advisory, distressed M&A. Management stated on the July 28 call that the team ranked #1 in global and U.S. announced and completed restructurings and delivered record Q2 and H1 revenue. The league-table provider was not named in the summaries available; treat the ranking as an issuer claim.

**PJT Park Hill** — primary fund placement, secondaries, GP stakes, continuation vehicles, now branded "private capital solutions." Management disclosed that private capital solutions growth "more than offset declines in primary fundraising," and that the primary market "remains challenging."

The structure is an Up-C. The public company holds a fraction of PJT Partners Holdings LP — 64.7% at 2026-06-30, up from 62.0% at 2025-12-31 and 60.3% at 2024-12-31. The productive assets are people, and the capital structure is built around that: partners hold Partnership Units in the operating LP rather than only public stock, creating multi-year economic lock-in and a firm whose value can walk out the door.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and margin

| Period | Revenue | Y/Y | Comp/Revenue | Pretax margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $1,153.2M | — | 69.8% | 15.4% |
| FY2024 | $1,493.2M | +29.5% | 69.1% | — |
| FY2025 | $1,713.7M | +14.8% | 67.6% | 20.0% |
| H1 2025 | $731.4M | — | 68.1% | 17.6% |
| H1 2026 | $904.5M | **+23.7%** | **67.0%** | **20.2%** |
| Q2 2026 | $486.3M | +19.5% | — | 21.0% |

FY2025 detail: advisory fees $1,500.4M (+14%), placement fees $181.6M (+24%), interest and other $31.7M. Growth re-accelerated in H1 2026 after decelerating in FY2025, and management attributes the increase to all three business lines simultaneously.

The compensation ratio is the dominant cost lever in advisory, and management described the H1 improvement as reflecting "a lower accrual rate," a deliberate decision rather than pure revenue leverage. Non-compensation expense fell from 14.3% to 12.8% of revenue. The mechanism is revenue growth on a roughly fixed partner headcount, with the accrual rate set below prior-year levels, dropping disproportionately to pretax income.

### Balance sheet — solvency is not the risk

At 2025-12-31: total assets $1,843.3M; total liabilities $834.2M, composed of accrued compensation $342.4M, operating lease liabilities $413.9M, accounts payable $34.0M, TRA $30.3M, and small tax and deferred-revenue items. **Zero funded debt. Interest paid in FY2025 was $0. The $100M revolver was undrawn.** Total equity $1,009.0M (PJT Partners Inc. $308.2M plus non-controlling interests $700.8M). Cash and short-term investments $585.8M at year-end, $535M at 2026-06-30 ($235.5M cash plus $299.3M investments).

Screens that compute debt/equity of 2.71 for PJT are dividing total liabilities by PJT Inc.-only equity, omitting the $700.8M NCI. Liabilities to total equity is 0.83, and essentially none of it is financial leverage. Similarly, an Altman Z-Score in the "distress" zone is an artifact; recomputed on FY2025 inputs it lands at 5.2 (Class A equity) to 7.8 (fully diluted equity), both above the 2.99 threshold. The narrower and more defensible statement: with no funded debt, $535M of net cash and an undrawn facility, insolvency is not a plausible risk here.

The real fixed-cost exposure is the lease book: $413.9M of lease liabilities against $339.0M of right-of-use assets, weighted-average remaining term 13.7 years at a 6.9% discount rate, and $663.8M of undiscounted payments running to 2041 with the schedule stepping from $35.8M (2026) to $50.3M (2028). Occupancy expense rose 18% in FY2025 to $59.7M. For a firm with $8.4M of deferred revenue against $1.71B of annual revenue, a 13.7-year escalating lease commitment is the closest thing on the balance sheet to leverage.

### Cash flow — one genuine soft spot

Operating cash flow: $441.5M (FY2023) → $530.9M (FY2024) → $526.3M (FY2025). OCF was flat while pretax income grew 27%. The reconciliation is visible in the cash flow statement: the accrued-compensation contribution to OCF fell from roughly $118.1M to roughly $36.0M, an $82M swing in working-capital tailwind, while receivables grew $75.9M. Capex stepped up from $3.3M to $45.9M on leasehold build-out, so free cash flow fell from $527.7M to $480.4M despite the earnings growth. OCF/revenue fell from 35.6% to 30.7%.

This is a timing-driven divergence rather than deteriorating conversion quality, but it establishes that FY2024's ~35.6% OCF margin was not a sustainable baseline and that capex is now a real claim. H1 2026 OCF of $301.2M should not be annualized: bonus accruals build through the year and pay out in Q1, so a single-half run rate is uninformative without normalizing the compensation cycle. Non-cash equity compensation of roughly $234.3M in FY2025 (13.7% of revenue) is the main reason OCF exceeds net income, a real shareholder cost but not a cash one.

On the fully diluted economic equity value of $7,760M (45.084M × $172.12), FY2025 FCF of $480.4M is a 6.2% yield.

### Earnings basis and the reconciliation problem

The supplied data quotes trailing and forward P/E of 28.06 and EPS of $6.68. Those figures do not reconcile with each other ($172.12 ÷ $6.68 = 25.8x), and neither is the right basis. GAAP diluted EPS is itself non-comparable across periods: Q2 2026 GAAP diluted shares were 28.51M versus 43.44M in Q2 2025, because the 15.33M dilutive impact of Partnership Units became anti-dilutive and was excluded. FY2025 diluted EPS of $6.68 was struck on ~28.6M shares; FY2024's $4.92 on ~44.1M. The headline +35.8% GAAP diluted EPS growth substantially reflects a denominator that shrank 35% for methodological reasons. Basic GAAP EPS ($5.28 → $7.00, +32.6%) is the clean GAAP comparison; adjusted if-converted EPS ($2.59 → $3.51 for H1, +35.5%) is the cleanest economic one.

A further caution on GAAP: FY2025's 9.7% effective tax rate included $37.3M of excess tax benefits on equity vesting, roughly 10.9 percentage points of the rate and approximately $1.30 per diluted share, or ~19% of the $6.68. These benefits arise when shares vest above grant-date value, making GAAP EPS partly reflexive to PJT's own share price. The company's adjusted if-converted tax rate has already normalized from 14.1% (FY2025) to 20.5% (H1 2026), a 6.4pp headwind that H1 adjusted EPS grew 35.5% through, which strengthens rather than weakens the earnings-quality read. Model ~20-21% going forward and treat excess benefits as upside.

### Capital allocation

FY2025 uses of cash: $195.2M of Class A repurchases (1.3M shares at $150.15 VWAP), $133.7M to cash-settle 0.8M Partnership Units, $24.5M of dividends, $40.8M of tax distributions to NCI, $54.9M of employee tax withholding. H1 2026: $114.5M of Class A repurchases (761,852 shares at $150.29 VWAP) plus $158.6M to cash-settle roughly 1.0M Partnership Units, with a further 0.9M units cash-settled on 2026-02-05 at $159.92 for $136.0M.

The unit cash-settlement mechanism matters. When holders present units for exchange, the Board may elect cash rather than Class A shares, and it has consistently elected cash. That permanently retires economic units, which is why the NCI fell 39.7% → 38.0% → 35.3% and why fully diluted shares fell 1.86% y/y from 45.94M to 45.08M. Most screens never see this reduction because it does not touch the Class A count, while simultaneously reporting a spurious 35% collapse in "diluted shares" that did not economically occur.

The Board authorized a new $800M repurchase program on 2026-04-28, with $759.6M remaining at 2026-06-30 — equal to 4.41M shares at $172.12, or 9.8% of the fully diluted base. At the H1 deployment pace (~$323M per half) that is roughly 15 months of capacity. Repurchases have been executed at VWAPs of $150.15 and $150.29, below the current price. The dividend is $0.25 quarterly, a 0.58% yield; this is not an income story.

## Growth Analysis

Three things support the growth case, and each has a specific offset.

**Revenue re-acceleration is real but partly cyclical.** H1 +23.7% is strong, but calendar-aligned Q2 CY2026 peer results (per StockStory summaries, not verified against peer filings) show Evercore at +18% and Moelis at +12%. PJT's +19.5% sits at the top of a strong cycle rather than representing predominantly idiosyncratic share gain. Houlihan Lokey's -15.6% reflects its fiscal-quarter timing, mid-market skew and a high base, and should not be read as industry contraction. Where PJT genuinely separated was earnings: a reported 25.6% non-GAAP beat while Evercore and Houlihan Lokey both missed, consistent with comp-ratio discipline being the differentiator.

**Growth is coming from larger mandates, not more clients.** H1 revenue rose 23.7% while total clients rose to 312 from 294 (+6.1%) and clients paying $1M or more rose to 137 from 133 (+3.0%). Average fee per mandate is rising sharply, evidence the strategic advisory build is landing bigger, more complex transactions. The same fact makes revenue lumpier and more concentrated. Rising average fee per client is directionally concentrating, and the 10-Q does not update client-level concentration, so improving diversification cannot be inferred from the growing revenue mix.

**Management guided H2 deceleration.** FY2026 revenue growth will be below the 24% H1 rate, and non-compensation expense is guided to grow ~14% for the full year versus +11% in H1, implying cost acceleration from occupancy, office build-out, travel, and AI/technology investment. PJT is materially back-half weighted (FY2025 was 42.7% H1 / 57.3% H2 on revenue, 37.6% / 62.4% on pretax), so naively annualizing H1's $3.51 to $7.02 would understate the year. Consensus at $7.99 (up 6.3% in 90 days) and $9.05 for FY2027 (up 5.9%) is internally consistent with mid-to-high-teens revenue growth at a 21-22% pretax margin.

Two structural offsets deserve weight. First, unrecognized RSU compensation rose from $283.0M (1.2-year recognition period) at year-end to $378.9M (2.2 years) at 2026-06-30 even as unvested units fell from 6.28M to 5.39M. Longer vesting schedules are the most likely explanation, a retention response and a committed future expense that will land in the comp ratio regardless of revenue, modestly capping further margin expansion. Second, international pretax income was -$69.5M in FY2025 versus -$38.9M in FY2024 and -$4.2M in FY2023, with $53.2M of foreign NOL deferred tax assets fully valuation-allowanced. Geographic revenue is assigned by office location while costs follow legal-entity and transfer-pricing conventions, so part of that loss may be an allocation artifact, but the valuation allowance confirms the tax losses in the UK entities are real. UK revenue by office rose 39% in H1 2026 ($124.5M vs $89.6M) and 89% in Q2, consistent with the drag beginning to reverse, though the 10-Q provides no interim geographic pretax split to confirm it.

The estimate-revision signal here is positive but modest — roughly +6% over 90 days on both current and next year, well short of the magnitude that reliably precedes sustained re-rating. Four consecutive beats, including the last at +24%, argue for confidence in consensus rather than for the view that consensus is dramatically too low.

Peer set: BGC 30.4x, Moelis 23.2x, StoneX 23.0x, Houlihan Lokey 21.8x, Piper Sandler 19.7x. PJT at ~21.5x forward on adjusted if-converted earnings sits at a modest discount to the 23.0x median while growing revenue faster than any of them in the most recent quarter and expanding margin. That is a defensible relative-value case: a mid-single-digit to low-double-digit percentage discount, not a mispricing of a magnitude that compels action.

Free cash flow yield of 6.2% on fully diluted equity is adequate for a business with no debt and a 9.8%-of-shares repurchase authorization, but it is not distressed-cheap, and it is calculated on FY2025 FCF that already declined y/y.

Note the definitional hazard on EV/EBITDA. A third-party "11.5x forward EV/EBITDA, well below its recent peak of 19x" claim circulating in July cannot be reproduced: Class A-only equity less $535M net cash against plausible FY2026 EBITDA of ~$435M gives ~8.4x, while fully diluted equity on the same basis gives ~16.6x. The cited figure implies a partially diluted count. Any EV multiple quoted for PJT without stating NCI treatment should be discarded.

**Base case $190.** This is 21.0x FY2027 consensus of $9.05, holding the current forward multiple roughly constant and letting one year of estimate growth plus ~2% annual reduction in the economic share base carry the return. Upside of 10.4% plus a 0.58% dividend.

**Bull case $228.** 24x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $9.50 — requires the comp ratio to hold near 67% or improve, the international drag to reverse visibly, private-equity M&A recovery to deliver the mandate volume Moelis' reported +80% pipeline build implies, and full deployment of the $759.6M authorization. Upside 32.5%.

**Bear case $125.** A simultaneous downturn in M&A and restructuring — the 2021 configuration of cheap credit and scarce deals — with revenue down mid-teens, the comp ratio reverting toward 69-70% and non-comp costs fixed. Adjusted EPS of roughly $6.25 at 20x gives $125, near the March 13 low of $127.73. Downside 27.4%.

Weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives roughly +6% expected return. The analyst mean of $187.20 (+8.8%) and median of $194.00 (+12.7%) bracket our base case; the low target of $171.00 is essentially spot. With one sell rating and three holds against three buy-side ratings, consensus is not a source of independent conviction here. Because PJT trades near, rather than well below, growth-adjusted peer multiples, the argument that analyst targets are stale floors does not apply with force.

## Competitive Landscape

PJT competes with independent advisers (Evercore, Moelis, Houlihan Lokey, Perella Weinberg, Lazard) and bulge-bracket banks on two dimensions: senior-banker quality and conflict-free independence. Without a financing arm there is no tension between advising and underwriting, which matters most in creditor-side restructuring and special-committee work.

The evidence of improving position: record H1 strategic advisory revenue; management's claim of #1 restructuring rankings; the earnings differential versus peers in the June quarter; and a comp ratio 280bp below where it stood three years ago while revenue grew 49%, which indicates pricing and productivity gains rather than headcount buying.

The evidence against extrapolating too far: revenue growth is close to Evercore's, so the cycle is doing much of the work; primary fund placement inside Park Hill is shrinking, with private capital solutions offsetting it; the separate disclosure of advisory versus placement fees was discontinued beginning Q1 2026 (the stated rationale of platform integration is plausible and PJT reports a single segment, but investors lost the ability to track Park Hill economics at the moment that mix was shifting); and the lengthening equity-vesting tail suggests the competitive cost of retaining partners is rising. Restructuring's counter-cyclicality to M&A is a genuine hedge, but with both strong simultaneously the hedge is currently untested.

Macro context cuts both ways. Tight credit terms have suppressed default-driven restructuring, yet liability management in a higher-for-longer rate environment is expanding the addressable market. Management frames rising demand for proactive liability management in a more normalized rate environment as an expanding opportunity, and AI is framed on the earnings call as a demand driver — companies reassessing competitive position, driving take-privates, strategic mergers and creative financing — which is the kind of activity that eventually generates restructuring mandates as well.

## Risk Assessment

**No contractual revenue visibility.** Engagements are non-recurring, terminable, and largely completion-contingent. Deferred revenue was $8.4M at 2025-12-31 against $1.71B of annual revenue, and the 10-K states that unsatisfied performance obligations with an original duration over one year were not material. Management's reference to a "growing M&A backlog" is a qualitative pipeline statement, not a contractual figure, and should not be treated as equivalent to RPO.

**Key-man concentration.** The 10-K names dependence on Taubman explicitly. He holds approximately 5.35M Partnership Units, and executive officers and directors controlled 23.5% of voting power for director elections and 33.0% for other matters at 2025-12-31.

**Tax Receivable Agreement tail.** The recorded liability is $30.3M with modest scheduled payments, but early termination or a change of control would trigger an estimated $402.2M (calculated at a $167.20 share price and 5.13% discount rate), roughly $8.92 per fully diluted share. PJT Partners Inc. is the payer and exchanging unit holders, plausibly including the CEO, are the recipients. Model this as a conditional obligation and as an effective takeover deterrent, not as current net debt.

**Fixed costs against variable revenue.** The 13.7-year, $663.8M undiscounted lease book plus guided ~14% FY2026 non-comp growth means a revenue shortfall compresses margin quickly. Non-comp is 12.8% of revenue; a 10% revenue decline with flat non-comp costs costs roughly 140bp of margin before any compensation offset.

**Tax-rate reflexivity.** FY2025's 9.7% GAAP rate is not repeatable. The adjusted if-converted rate has already moved to 20.5%, and the GAAP rate has swung from -5.1% (H1 2025) to +6.5% (H1 2026) to +20.4% (Q2 2026 alone). Investors extrapolating FY2025 GAAP EPS would be badly wrong.

**Governance and disclosure items.** No open-market insider purchases were observed through 2026-07-30. Taubman's 36,000 units per quarter (transaction code M) are programmatic exchanges through the quarterly mechanism, not discretionary sales, and represent roughly 2.7% of his stake annually. Modest sales by the CFO (8,000 shares at $153.195), General Counsel and one director are consistent with routine post-vest liquidation. The CFO transition — Arun Kalra effective 2026-10-01, with Helen Meates staying through year-end after more than a decade — has the structure of planned succession; no restatement, auditor change or material weakness accompanies it. Against that, the absence of buying alongside a CFO change is a point to weigh rather than dismiss. The Q2 exchange notice covered only 65,000 units versus roughly 1.0M across H1, which slows the unit-retirement accretion mechanism if it persists.

**Short interest of 10.8% of float with 4.1 days to cover** is elevated for a profitable, debt-free advisory firm, and is measured against the Class A float only. Some of this is likely structural (Up-C arbitrage, index-related), but it also signals a non-trivial cohort positioned for cycle deceleration.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.6% against 32.3% one-year realized is rich (an 8.3pp spread) and sits at the 65th percentile within the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (40.6% at 13 days, 35.2% at 41 days, 34.9% at 104 days). ATM IV rose 2.3pp since August 2 even as the stock gained 2.4%. Since Q2 earnings already cleared on July 28, the near-term premium is not an earnings hedge; it more likely reflects thin liquidity (124 contracts across four expirations) plus macro event risk. Skew is balanced at -2.6% and put/call open interest is call-tilted at 0.31, so options positioning is not corroborating the elevated short interest. The practical read: near-dated premium is expensive for buyers of protection, which argues against paying up for hedges here and mildly favours patience on entry, and the absence of put-skew stress means the options market is not flagging a specific identified downside catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($228, +32.5%)

The comp ratio is the whole argument. Every 100bp of compensation ratio improvement on ~$2.0B of revenue is roughly $20M of pretax income, or about $0.37 of if-converted EPS after 20.5% tax — a 4-5% earnings uplift with no revenue growth required. PJT has taken 280bp out of the ratio in three years and management describes the H1 improvement as a deliberate lower accrual rate. Layer on: three business lines growing simultaneously; strategic advisory that Taubman describes as "still in the early stages"; a private capital solutions franchise offsetting primary-placement weakness; UK revenue up 89% in Q2, consistent with the international drag reversing; and a $759.6M authorization equal to 9.8% of the fully diluted base being deployed at prices near $150. If FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $9.50 and the market pays 24x for a debt-free franchise compounding the economic share base down ~2% annually, the stock is $228. Rising liability-management demand as corporate debt matures provides a multi-year restructuring option that costs nothing today.

### Bear Case ($125, -27.4%)

Revenue is 100% transaction-contingent with $8.4M of deferred revenue and no disclosed backlog, set against a 13.7-year, $663.8M lease commitment and guided ~14% non-comp growth. Management has already guided FY2026 growth below the 24% H1 rate. If M&A and restructuring decelerate together — the 2021 configuration — revenue could fall mid-teens while the comp ratio reverts to 69-70% and non-comp costs stay fixed, taking adjusted EPS to roughly $6.25. At 20x that is $125, essentially the March low. Additional pressure points: FY2025 FCF already declined y/y on a $42.6M capex step-up and the absence of the prior accrual tailwind; the adjusted tax rate has risen 6.4pp with no obvious reversal; unrecognized RSU expense grew to $378.9M over a 2.2-year tail, a committed cost regardless of revenue; the international pretax loss widened to -$69.5M for the third consecutive year; and the loss of separate placement-fee disclosure removes the ability to verify Park Hill economics from filings. Key-man dependence on the founder-CEO and a $402.2M change-of-control TRA cap the terminal-value case.

### Which dominates

The bull case is mechanically credible and the balance sheet removes the tail that would make a downturn existential. But at 21.5x FY2026 and 19.0x FY2027 against a 23.0x peer median, the market already prices a good outcome. The asymmetry (+10.4% base, +32.5% bull, -27.4% bear) is roughly balanced, and the honest read is that the March-to-August 34.7% rally has absorbed most of the mispricing that existed when the correct share-count arithmetic was less widely applied.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold). Twelve-month horizon.**

- **Base case: $190** — 21.0x FY2027 consensus adjusted if-converted EPS of $9.05, holding the current forward multiple constant. +10.4% from $172.12, plus a 0.58% dividend.
- **Bull case: $228** — 24x on $9.50 FY2027 adjusted EPS. +32.5%.
- **Bear case: $125** — 20x on $6.25 in a joint M&A/restructuring downturn. -27.4%.
- **Consensus reference:** mean $187.20, median $194.00, range $171.00-$200.00.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price at or below $150-155 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $9.00 — that is 17x or less forward on unchanged estimates, and it coincides with the level at which management repurchased in FY2025 and H1 2026.
2. Q3 2026 (late October) print showing the comp ratio at or below 67.0% with FY2026 revenue growth of 18% or better, confirming that guided H2 deceleration and ~14% non-comp growth do not reverse margin expansion.
3. Disclosure of a narrowing international pretax loss in the FY2026 10-K (better than -$40M versus -$69.5M), which would convert the UK revenue surge from a hypothesis into a confirmed earnings lever, or a partial release of the $53.2M valuation allowance.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Comp ratio rising above 68.5% in any quarter, or FY2026 non-comp expense growth exceeding the guided ~14% while revenue growth falls below 12%.
2. Two consecutive quarters of Partnership Unit exchange notices below 150,000 units combined with repurchase deployment below $75M per quarter — the unit-retirement accretion is a core part of the per-share thesis and would be stalling.
3. FY2027 consensus falling below $8.25 (a 9% cut from the current $9.05), or a quarterly revenue decline y/y, either of which would indicate the cycle has turned rather than merely decelerated.
4. Unplanned senior departures — Taubman, or a cluster of restructuring partners — or a disorderly development around the October 1 CFO transition.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 print in late October, which is the first quarter under H2 cost acceleration and the last with Meates as CFO. The FY2026 10-K (late February 2027) is the next opportunity to verify the international pretax split, client concentration, and lease-schedule progression.

## Conclusion

PJT is a high-quality, debt-free advisory franchise whose per-share economics are better than its GAAP presentation suggests and whose valuation is better than a naive screen suggests in one direction and worse in the other. The screens that show 14x on Class A market cap divided by consolidated earnings are wrong; so is the quoted 28.1x trailing P/E. The correct figure — 21.5x FY2026 and 19.0x FY2027 consensus on the company's own if-converted basis, versus a 23.0x peer median — describes a modestly discounted, faster-growing operator with 6.2% FCF yield, $535M of net cash, no funded debt, a 9.8%-of-shares repurchase authorization, and a CEO taking $1.03M in annual compensation against roughly $920M of Partnership Units.

Three dimensions genuinely support the name: financial health (7.5), management quality (7.2) and, at the margin, growth (6.8). Two constrain it: valuation (5.9), because the 34.7% recovery from the March low has closed most of the gap to a defensible base case, and risk factors (5.8), because revenue is entirely transaction-contingent against a 13.7-year lease book, the adjusted tax rate has risen 6.4pp, international operations have lost money for three consecutive years at an accelerating rate, and $402.2M of contingent TRA exposure sits behind any change of control.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be higher. The justification is specific rather than default: fundamentals improved (four straight beats, the last by 24%; estimates up ~6% over 90 days; comp ratio at a multi-year low), while the entry price moved against the buyer by more than the fundamentals moved in the buyer's favour. Existing holders should stay long, because the buyback, the unit-retirement accretion and the margin trajectory all work in their favour. New capital should wait for $150-155, or for the October print to demonstrate that H2 cost acceleration does not consume the margin gain.
