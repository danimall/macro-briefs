**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

KLA is the dominant process-control supplier to the semiconductor industry, owning roughly 85% of the optical wafer-inspection market and facing limited direct competition in inspection and metrology, the least-contested niche within the wafer-fab-equipment (WFE) oligopoly that also includes ASML, Applied Materials, and Lam Research. The business is performing well: Q3 FY26 (quarter ended March 31, 2026) revenue rose 11% to $3.42B, nine-month revenue reached $9.92B (+10%), gross margin held at 61.3%, and operating cash flow of $3.24B on $3.47B net income represents ~93% cash conversion. A growing services layer (~23% of revenue, +16% YoY) tied to the installed base adds a recurring, partially counter-cyclical revenue stream that dampens the historical cyclicality of equipment sales.

The complication is price. KLA executed a 10-for-1 forward split effective June 11/12, 2026, and the stock has rallied roughly 45% in the six weeks since, closing at $238.73 against a 52-week post-split high of $267.17. The prior-report comparison showing an 86% loss is entirely a split artifact; on a split-adjusted basis the shares are up substantially from the early-May reference. On real economics the forward multiple now sits in the low-to-mid-30s, above the analyst target band (mean $194, median $200, high $250), with at least one firm rating it Hold on the grounds the price has outrun targets after the rally.

The combination of an excellent franchise and a stretched near-term entry argues for patience rather than chasing. Estimate revisions are positive but modest (current-year EPS +1.5% over 90 days, next-year +6.3%), nowhere near the 30%+ velocity that would justify ignoring the multiple. China export controls (31% of nine-month revenue), a quantified DRAM-cost gross-margin headwind into CY2026, and rare-earth supply risk are specific, identifiable overhangs. I rate KLAC a HOLD (growth_hold) and would become a buyer on a pullback into the high-$190s to low-$200s.

## Company Overview

KLA supplies inspection, metrology, software, and services that detect and measure nanometer-scale defects on wafers and reticles, helping foundry/logic and memory chipmakers raise and stabilize die yields. The company reports three segments on nine-month FY26 revenue of $9.92B:

- **Semiconductor Process Control — ~91% of revenue ($8.99B).** The core franchise, comprising Wafer Inspection (~49% of total) and Patterning (~20%). This is where the ~85% optical-inspection share resides.
- **Specialty Semiconductor Process — ~4% ($424M).** Vacuum deposition/etch for MEMS, RF, and power semiconductors.
- **PCB and Component Inspection — ~5% ($509M).** Inspection/test for PCBs and advanced packaging; KLA exited the Display business in 2024 and took a $230.4M goodwill impairment on the legacy PCB unit in Q2 FY25.

Process-control intensity rises with node complexity: more EUV layers, the 2nm transition, and DRAM/HBM stacking all multiply the number of inspection and metrology steps per wafer. Each generation of AI-driven logic and memory requires proportionally more of KLA's tools per unit of capacity, which is the mechanism behind KLA's secular tailwind.

## Financial Analysis

The financials are high-quality and consistent. Q3 FY26 net income was $1.20B (+10% YoY). Nine-month net income grew 21% with gross margin improving from 60.5% a year earlier. Capex is light at $287M (~3% of revenue), reflecting an asset-light model that converts nearly all net income to cash.

The balance sheet carries $4.96B in cash and marketable securities against $5.95B in fixed-rate senior notes laddered out to 2063 at effective rates of 3.3%–5.67%. Net leverage is 0.54x versus a 3.25x covenant, substantial headroom, supported by a new, undrawn $1.5B revolver and A/A2/A- credit ratings. The Altman Z-Score "grey zone" some screens flag is a function of the leveraged capital structure, not the underlying cash generation; for a business converting 93% of income to operating cash flow, refinancing and solvency risk are minimal.

The earnings track record is steady rather than explosive: over the last four quarters, two beats, one in-line, one small miss, with surprises clustered within ±1.5% except a 7.4% beat in June 2025. This is a high-visibility, well-modeled business, which means consensus estimates are reliable but also that large upside surprises are unlikely.

The single most company-specific margin risk is DRAM cost inflation. KLA buys DRAM chips for its image computers, and a supply-side spike (some suppliers discontinued certain chips) has lifted purchase commitments to $4.83B and is pressuring gross margin. Management calls the drag "transitory" but explicitly warns it "will continue to negatively impact gross margin in calendar 2026." A customer-end boom (DRAM/HBM capex driving KLA tool sales) creates an input-cost headwind on the same product line, net positive for revenue and net negative for near-term margin.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is AI-aligned and multi-pronged. Korea revenue rose 80% YoY in Q3 FY26 to $681M on DRAM/HBM investment, a direct read on the memory cycle. Management targets ~$1B in advanced-packaging revenue in 2026, growing mid-to-high-teens percent YoY on AI/HBM packaging complexity. The services business compounds with the installed base independent of new-tool order timing.

Estimate revisions are positive but measured: current-quarter EPS +2.1% over 90 days, current-year +1.5%, next-year +6.3%. The next-year revision is the most encouraging signal, consistent with the AI WFE cycle extending into 2027–2028. This is steady upgrade momentum from a company already executing well, not the 30%+/90-day velocity that would flip valuation discipline. I weight it as confirmation of thesis durability, not as a standalone catalyst to override the multiple.

Industry breadth supports durability: multiple WFE peers and hyperscaler capex commitments confirm a broad-based, structural demand shift rather than a single-customer story. The countervailing constraint is China, where advanced-node export controls cap one of KLA's largest markets even as legacy-node demand keeps the China line roughly flat.

## Valuation Assessment

On split-adjusted economics, run-rate diluted EPS is ~$0.91/quarter (~$3.6 annualized; consensus current-year $3.71, next-year $5.05). At $238.73 the stock trades at roughly 64x current-year and ~47x next-year EPS. The forward_pe field of 67.6 in the raw data reflects a trailing-EPS basis ($3.04 trailing); on forward consensus the multiple is mid-60s on current-year and mid-40s on next-year.

Peer multiples cluster high: LRCX 68.5x, AMAT 52.0x, TER 70.8x, with a peer median P/E of 68.5x. On a trailing basis KLA screens in line with or below peers, but the sector is broadly re-rated on the AI WFE theme, so peer parity is not independent evidence of cheapness; it reflects a cohort that has all moved together. The relevant question is price versus the company's own forward earnings power, and there the stock sits above the analyst range (mean $194, median $200, high $250). One firm rated it Hold on June 16, citing the price "running far ahead of Wall Street price targets" after the rally.

The dimension does not earn a high score here. KLA is a premium business deserving a premium multiple, but at ~47x next-year with positive-but-modest estimate revisions and two quantified near-term headwinds (DRAM cost, China controls), there is no specific, falsifiable reason the market is underpricing it today. Prior appreciation alone does not make it expensive; the risk/reward is unattractive at this price because the multiple already discounts the durable AI WFE thesis while the next-twelve-month risks skew to the downside. A pullback into the high-$190s to low-$200s would restore a more attractive entry.

## Competitive Landscape

KLA's moat is the strongest dimension. It owns ~85%+ of optical wafer inspection and faces limited direct competition; Applied Materials' process-diagnostics-and-control unit is materially smaller. Process control is the least-contested niche in the WFE oligopoly because it is software- and algorithm-intensive, benefits from a decades-long defect-library and installed-base data advantage, and carries high switching costs once embedded in a customer's yield-management flow.

The trajectory is improving, not merely stable: rising process-control intensity per node means KLA captures a growing share of each customer's capex even at flat unit volumes, and the services layer deepens the lock-in. Five-year TSR of 489% versus a peer group's 278% quantifies the franchise outperformance. The competitive risk is geographic: export controls "make it easier for China-based competitors to develop and sell their own products," per management, but indigenization of high-end inspection is a multi-year challenge given the technology lead.

## Risk Assessment

1. **China export controls (most material).** China was 31% of nine-month FY26 revenue ($3.09B). Layered BIS rules plus the September 2025 "Affiliates Rule" (suspended to November 2026) restrict advanced-node and advanced-DRAM sales. Some products are being held at U.S. Customs over end-use questions. The resilience offset: China revenue was roughly flat over nine months on sticky legacy-node demand, so the risk is a step-down if controls tighten, not an immediate cliff.

2. **DRAM cost inflation.** Direct, quantified gross-margin pressure into CY2026, per management. Partially offset by the DRAM/HBM capex tailwind on the revenue side.

3. **Rare-earth supply.** China's April/October 2025 export controls on rare earths (it controls ~70% of mining, ~90% of processing) are a retaliation lever that could disrupt KLA's component supply, newly added to the 10-Q and under-discussed by the market.

4. **Tariffs.** A Section 232 semiconductor-equipment investigation could bring new duties; the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling created refund uncertainty (KLA expects the impact immaterial).

5. **Customer concentration.** In Q3 FY26 two customers were ~19% and ~11% of revenue. Typical of the foundry/memory oligopoly but a genuine concentration exposure.

6. **Cyclicality.** WFE spending tracks customer capex cycles; the 23% services mix dampens but does not eliminate this.

Most of these are partially priced; the stock would not be at a record multiple if the China and cyclicality risks were unknown. Less fully reflected is the convergence of the DRAM margin drag and a potential further China step-down arriving while the stock sits above its target range. That asymmetry is why risk_factors scores at the midpoint rather than higher.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($300+).** AI-driven WFE intensity compounds through 2028; advanced packaging hits the ~$1B target and grows mid-to-high teens; DRAM/HBM memory capex sustains the Korea surge; services keep compounding at mid-teens, lifting the recurring mix toward 25%+. Next-year EPS of $5.05 proves conservative, the China headwind stays contained at legacy-node demand, and the stock holds a premium forward multiple on rising estimates. Re-rating plus earnings growth carries it past $300.

**Bear Case (~$150).** China controls tighten or China indigenizes inspection, stepping the 31% revenue contribution down materially; the DRAM cost drag proves stickier than "transitory"; a WFE cyclical pause coincides with multiple compression on the forward EPS base. The low analyst target of $150 captures this scenario, roughly 37% below the current price.

The franchise quality is not in dispute. The debate is entirely about whether ~47x next-year earnings adequately compensates for the China/DRAM overhangs at a record price. Today it does not offer asymmetric upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is a quality franchise worth owning through the AI WFE cycle, but the entry price above the target range, against two specific near-term headwinds, does not support adding here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback to the high-$190s–low-$200s, restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Next-year EPS revisions accelerating above +10% over a 90-day window alongside confirmation the DRAM cost drag is resolving (gross margin re-expanding toward 62%+).
- A durable de-escalation of China export-control risk (e.g., the Affiliates Rule lapsing without a tightening replacement) that de-risks the 31% revenue base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A China revenue step-down of >25% QoQ from a tightening of controls.
- Gross margin compressing below ~59% on a sustained DRAM/tariff cost squeeze with no recovery path.
- Next-year estimate revisions turning negative alongside a WFE order-rate rollover.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (FY26 Q4, expected late July/early August 2026) for the DRAM-margin and China-revenue trajectory, and the November 2026 Affiliates Rule deadline for the China-control overhang.

## Conclusion

KLA is one of the highest-quality businesses in the semiconductor supply chain: a near-monopoly in process control, 61%+ gross margins, ~93% cash conversion, a 17-year dividend-growth record, and a recurring services layer that softens the historical cyclicality. The structural AI/HBM/advanced-packaging demand thesis is broad-based and durable, and the company is executing against it. None of that is the constraint.

The constraint is that the post-split stock has rallied ~45% in six weeks to a record forward multiple that already discounts the durable growth, while the next twelve months carry two specific, quantified headwinds (a DRAM-cost gross-margin drag and China export-control risk on 31% of revenue) that skew the near-term risk/reward to the downside. With estimate revisions positive but well short of inflection velocity, there is no specific reason to believe the market is underpricing the shares today. I rate KLAC a HOLD and would add on a pullback into the high-$190s to low-$200s, where the franchise quality and the entry price align.
