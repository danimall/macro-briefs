## Executive Summary

Intercontinental Exchange has delivered four consecutive earnings beats and a first half of 2026 that looks strong in aggregate: net revenue $5,643M (+12.5% y/y), operating income $3,056M, adjusted diluted EPS $4.25 versus $3.54. Inside that half, the trajectory reversed. Q1 2026 was a record (adjusted EPS $2.35, +37%; net revenue $3.0B, +18%). Q2 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $1.90 (+5%) on net revenue of $2,666M (+5%), sequentially below Q1, with energy revenue down 13% y/y to $518M on volumes down 21%. Adjusted operating expense grew roughly 5.8% to $1.04B against that 5% revenue growth, and management raised full-year expense guidance. That is the first quarter in the observable window in which cost growth exceeded revenue growth, and it is the most plausible explanation for the stock's slide from $169 in January to a $121.79 low on June 29.

The second new fact is the July 29 agreement to acquire MarketAxess for $167.00 per share in cash — roughly $6.0B of equity value, $5.7B enterprise value, funded entirely with new debt via a $6.2B bridge commitment. The headline 10.6x LTM EBITDA is stated pro forma for the full $100M of promised expense synergies; reversing that arithmetic ($5,700M ÷ 10.6 = $537.7M, less $100M) implies MarketAxess LTM EBITDA near $438M and an unsynergized multiple of ~13.0x. No revenue synergies were quantified. Pro forma gross leverage begins at 3.4x with a stated return to ≤3.0x in 18-24 months, and management simultaneously raised the baseline quarterly buyback to $400M from $350M. At today's financing costs the deal is only marginally cash-accretive before synergies land: $438M of acquired EBITDA less roughly $50M of maintenance depreciation less incremental interest on $6.0-6.2B of new debt (above ICE's 3.7% embedded cost) leaves a modest positive per-share contribution before synergies, rising as the $100M of cost synergies land over three years. [The precise accretion figures are unverified — the brief provides no financing coupon or maintenance-depreciation figures for MarketAxess; the deal is disclosed as accretive to adjusted EPS in the first full year post-close.]

On the numbers that matter for a scored decision, ICE trades at 21.1x TTM GAAP EPS of ~$7.12, 23.0x after stripping $452M of non-cash investment marks, 18.3x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $8.16, and 17.0x FY2027 consensus of $8.81. The peer median P/E is 26.2. The discount is real but largely earned: forward earnings growth of ~8% against MCO/SPGI/MSCI in the mid-teens, leverage rising by roughly two-thirds of a turn, and a mortgage technology segment whose recurring revenue grows 2%. Base case fair value is $170, 13.5% above spot; the bear case is $128, 14.5% below. That is a fair, not asymmetric, setup, and it argues for holding rather than adding. The low-$120s is the accumulation zone; the stock traded there in late June and has since recovered ~23%.

## Company Overview

ICE operates three reported segments.

**Exchanges (~86% of H1 2026 gross revenue)** houses the global futures and options franchise plus the NYSE complex, and generated $2,481M of H1 segment operating income at a 76% GAAP margin. H1 revenue lines: energy futures and options $1,332M (+16%), agricultural and metals $168M (+31%), financial futures and options $448M (+43%), cash equities and equity options $1,897M (+10%), OTC and other $213M (+7%), data and connectivity $564M (+13%), listings $257M (+5%). Total open interest reached 118.1 million contracts at June 30, up 20% y/y, with financial F&O open interest up 46% and agricultural/metals up 43%.

**Fixed Income & Data Services (~18%)** produced $1,302M of H1 revenue and $543M of operating income (42% GAAP, 47% adjusted). Annual Subscription Value was $2.080B at June 30, up 7.9%, and the company states ASV represents approximately 100% of segment data-services revenue — a near-fully recurring book. CDS clearing notional was $16.1 trillion in H1 2026 versus $12.8 trillion a year earlier. Fixed-income execution (ICE Bonds) was only $62M and declined 2%, which is precisely the gap MarketAxess is intended to fill.

**Mortgage Technology (~15%)** produced $1,096M of H1 revenue (+5%) but only $32M of GAAP operating income — a 3% margin — against $451M adjusted (41%). The $419M difference is $369M of acquisition-intangible amortization plus $50M of Black Knight integration cost. Recurring revenue of $807M grew 2%; servicing software, the largest line at $448M, grew 2%; growth came from transactional revenue (+16%) and closing solutions (+17%), the rate-sensitive lines.

The clearing complex held $235.3B of total collateral at June 30, including $116.9B of cash. ICE's own skin-in-the-game contributions total $396M plus $400M of default insurance, and no ICE clearing house has ever required either.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trend.** Gross revenue grew from $9,636M (FY2022) to $12,640M (FY2025), with the FY2024 step-up of 18.8% reflecting Black Knight's first full year and FY2025 growth of 7.5%. Operating income rose from $3,638M to $4,929M. Net income reached $3,315M in FY2025.

**H1/Q2 2026.**

| | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net revenue (ex-TBE) | $2,666M | $2,543M | $5,643M | $5,016M |
| Operating income | $1,391M | $1,297M | $3,056M | $2,518M |
| Operating margin | 52% | 51% | 54% | 50% |
| Net income (to ICE) | $958M | $851M | $2,371M | $1,648M |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $1.69 | $1.48 | $4.18 | $2.86 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $1.90 | $1.81 | $4.25 | $3.54 |

H1 should not be annualized. Listings revenue is billed largely upfront in Q1 and amortized (deferred revenue moved from $292M at January 1 to $653M at June 30 on $837M of additions against $476M of amortization), and volatility-driven derivative volumes clustered in Q1. Consensus of $8.16 for FY2026 implies H2 adjusted EPS of $3.91 against H1's $4.25, consistent with that seasonality and with the Q3/Q4 quarterly estimates of $1.93 and $1.97.

**Cash generation.** H1 operating cash flow was $3,324M (+34%); capex of $208M plus capitalized software of $230M gives $2,886M of free cash flow. Two items inflate the comparison: a $286M working-capital benefit from Section 31 fees payable (the SEC reinstated the rate in April 2026 after zeroing it in May 2025, so Q2 collections are remitted later), and the add-back of $452M of non-cash investment fair-value gains. ICE's own adjusted free cash flow figure of $2,600M captures the first adjustment. On the company's definition, FY2025 free cash flow was approximately $3.8-3.9B, a 4.6% yield on the current market capitalization.

**Balance sheet, correctly framed.** Total assets rose from $136,887M to $174,247M in six months, but essentially the entire increase is clearing-member margin and guaranty funds (from $76,789M to $114,599M), matched dollar-for-dollar by a liability. Screening ratios built on gross assets — current ratio, Altman Z — measure custodial float rather than ICE's solvency and should be discarded. The metrics that inform credit:

- Total debt $19,846M ($18,628M senior notes, $1,218M commercial paper), 13-year weighted-average maturity at a 3.7% weighted-average cost
- Interest coverage: H1 operating income $3,056M ÷ interest expense $408M = 7.5x (FY2025: 6.1x)
- Gross leverage ~2.77x LTM adjusted EBITDA (derived: management's 3.4x pro forma figure reconciles exactly at $25,846M ÷ $7,598M, implying ~$7.16B standalone ICE EBITDA)
- Debt/equity 0.67x
- Fair value of debt $17,791M versus $19,846M carrying — a $2.06B economic asset that book leverage does not reflect, because ICE locked in low long-term coupons before the current rate regime
- Goodwill $30,632M plus net intangibles $14,870M = $45.5B, or 154% of $29.6B equity

**Capital allocation.** H1 buybacks were $1,202M (7.9M shares) versus $496M a year earlier, plus $591M of dividends — $1,793M returned against $2,886M of FCF, a 62% payout. Share count fell to 561M from 567M. The monthly repurchase table shows average prices of $168.90 in January stepping down to $142.39 in June, with $651M repurchased in Q2 versus $551M in Q1. Buying more aggressively as the price fell is the correct behavior and the strongest single piece of evidence in management's favor.

Financial health scores 6.5: coverage and maturity structure are comfortably investment-grade, but leverage is rising by two-thirds of a turn, and the goodwill/intangible load is the largest single downside asymmetry on the balance sheet.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth engines are visible and quantified. Energy rate-per-contract rose 10% to $1.88 while open interest rose 20% — more positions held on the venue and a higher price per position is what pricing power looks like in a derivatives franchise. Financial futures revenue grew 43% with open interest up 46%, which is ICE monetizing the rates-volatility regime. ASV growth of 7.9% on a book that equals roughly 100% of segment data revenue is genuinely recurring. CDS clearing notional grew 26%.

Against that, three drags are now measurable.

**Q2 energy volumes fell 21% y/y.** The mechanism can cut both ways: higher volatility raises initial margin per contract, which reduces the position size a given pool of capital can carry, and basis dislocation can push activity toward OTC and physical channels rather than screen futures; a strong Q2 2025 comparison compounds it. Anyone underwriting ICE as a levered play on energy-market disruption should reconcile with this print. [The specific geopolitical drivers — Kharg Island, Hormuz transit — are not stated in the brief and are removed as unverified.]

**Equity execution economics are eroding while share rises.** Cash equities matched share improved to 20.0% from 19.0% with handled ADV up 24%, but rate-per-100-shares fell 9% to $0.037. Equity options share rose to 20.5% from 18.8% with ADV up 33%, but rate-per-contract fell 27% to $0.05 for the half and 33% to $0.04 in Q2 alone. Liquidity rebates paid rose to $1.1B from $842M. Rising share and falling capture measure different things and here point in opposite directions. Part of this may be industry mix shift toward lower-fee tiers rather than ICE-specific price competition; the filings do not disaggregate enough to settle it.

**Mortgage Technology recurring revenue grows at roughly the rate of inflation.** Nearly three years after the Black Knight close, servicing software (+2%) and data and analytics (+3%) are static. Headcount fell to 12,725 from 12,806 on synergy reductions, so cost execution is working; revenue synergies are unproven. The counter-argument is cyclical: the 10-K names elevated mortgage rates, affordability and availability as current headwinds, which suppress both transactional volume and seat growth, so 2% may be a trough reading. A rate-cut cycle would lift it. [The specific mortgage-rate level and Fed-path characterizations are not in the brief and are removed as unverified.]

Consensus captures the deceleration: FY2027 EPS of $8.81 is only 8% above FY2026's $8.16. Estimate revisions are positive but marginal: +0.4% for the current year and +0.2% for next year over 90 days. That falls far short of the revision velocity that would justify overriding a valuation concern; it confirms that the near-term path is intact rather than accelerating.

Growth prospects score 5.5.

Peer detail: CME 22.4x, NDAQ 26.1x, SPGI 26.2x, MCO 31.7x, MSCI 34.0x. ICE is the cheapest name in the cohort on trailing earnings, and it sits 20.9% below its $189.35 52-week high.

The question is whether the discount is a mispricing. Three considerations argue it is mostly earned. First, forward earnings growth of 8% against mid-teens at MCO, SPGI and MSCI produces a PEG above 2.0 — the multiple discount is roughly proportional to the growth gap. Second, pro forma leverage of 3.4x is meaningfully above the peer group's typical structure, and the incremental $6.0-6.2B is being raised at a cost above ICE's 3.7% embedded average, so interest expense rises in a business where EBITDA growth is decelerating. Third, the GAAP/adjusted wedge is about to widen: a $6.0B purchase of a data-and-technology business will generate large intangible amortization, and Mortgage Technology is the live precedent ($32M GAAP versus $451M adjusted). Adjusted EPS accretion in year one is credible; GAAP EPS could be flat to down.

Two considerations argue the discount overshoots. ICE bought $651M of its own stock in Q2 at prices averaging down to $142.39 and then raised the authorization to $4.0B and the quarterly baseline to $400M; the issuer is a large, price-sensitive marginal buyer below $150. And the $2.06B mark-to-market discount on ICE's own debt is real economic value that neither book leverage nor the P/E captures.

There is also a ~$3B venture book (Polymarket $1.64B invested and carried near $2.0B, OKX parent $200M, Bakkt $62M) representing about 3.5% of market capitalization, carried largely at Level 3. Reported private rounds valuing Polymarket at $15B and above $20B are unconfirmed by any filing, and preferred liquidation preferences mean ICE's ~14% fully diluted stake is not a clean claim on enterprise value. Bakkt's path — $9M at year-end 2025, $62M six months later, with going-concern language disclosed since 2023 — shows how violently these marks move. Treat the position as optionality with no value ascribed above carrying.

**Base case: $170.** 19.3x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $8.81. The multiple sits below the 26.2x peer median and below ICE's own recent history, reflecting 8% growth, 3.4x leverage and unresolved MarketAxess antitrust review. Upside 13.5% plus a 1.4% dividend.

**Bull case: $205.** 23x on $8.90-9.00, requiring energy volumes to normalize in H2, opex growth to fall back below revenue growth, MarketAxess to clear without conditions, and mortgage transactional revenue to lift on any rate relief. Upside 36.9%.

**Bear case: $128.** 15.5x on $8.20 — MarketAxess delayed or conditioned, energy volumes remaining soft, expense growth continuing to outpace revenue, and a Mortgage Technology impairment against the $45.5B intangible balance. Downside 14.5%.

Probability-weighting at 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives roughly +11% price return plus dividend, or about 12.5% expected total return over twelve months. Positive, but the base-to-bear reward:risk is only about 0.93:1, which is below the bar for adding capital.

A note on analyst targets: 14 of 15 ratings are buy or strong-buy, the low target is $163 (8.8% above spot) and the mean is $185.86. Every published target is above the current price. On stocks with rapidly rising estimates and compressed multiples, consensus targets lag reality and function as floors. That condition does not hold here — revisions are +0.4%, revenue growth decelerated to 5%, and expense guidance was raised. Uniformly positive consensus on decelerating fundamentals is more likely to produce target reductions than to pull the stock up.

Valuation scores 5.9. Cheap relative to peers, appropriately cheap relative to its own growth and leverage.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is evidenced rather than asserted in three places: clearing-house network effects and margin efficiency across six clearing houses holding $235.3B of collateral; a subscription-locked data franchise where ASV equals roughly 100% of segment data revenue; and regulatory positioning, with ICE Clear Credit designated a systemically important financial market utility and IBA administering ICE Swap Rate as a critical UK benchmark. The charter's 10% voting and 20% ownership caps function as a structural takeover defense.

Trajectory is mixed. Improving: energy and financial derivatives, where open interest and rate-per-contract are rising together; CDS clearing; the ASV book. Deteriorating: NYSE execution economics, where share is being purchased with $1.1B of rebates against falling capture; and mortgage technology, where the 10-K itself concedes "vigorous competition" and that ICE "may be unsuccessful in differentiating our services to the extent necessary to effectively compete."

MarketAxess places ICE in direct competition with Tradeweb (majority-owned by LSEG) and Bloomberg in institutional credit execution. The strategic logic is coherent: bundle execution with the evaluated pricing, reference data and indices that MarketAxess's users already consume, replicating what ICE did in energy and CDS. Two cautions. The same bundling gives antitrust authorities a vertical-foreclosure theory, and ICE has precedent here: Black Knight closed only after an FTC consent order under which ICE remains subject to periodic reviews. And a 33% premium with zero quantified revenue synergies means the return depends on credit e-trading penetration continuing to compound; if institutional credit volumes soften, ICE will have paid ~13x an unsynergized EBITDA base. [The "AI-debt wave" and "$100bn of investment-grade AI-related paper" characterizations are not in the brief and are removed as unverified.]

Two other contests matter. ICE Clear Credit's new U.S. Treasury clearing service (SEC-approved February 3, cash live, repo targeted Q4 2026) competes against DTCC's entrenched FICC, and ICE's own risk factor concedes "there is no assurance that the service will achieve meaningful market adoption." The CFTC's June 2026 request for comment on perpetual contracts explicitly asks whether perpetuals on physically-delivered or storable energy commodities should be treated as futures. Energy derivatives are ICE's highest-value franchise; an affirmative outcome opens it to an always-on, capital-efficient competing structure. CME is separately litigating against the CFTC's approval of Kalshi's bitcoin perpetuals, and Kalshi is Polymarket's principal U.S. rival, so ICE and CME sit on opposite sides of that regulatory contest.

Competitive position scores 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**MarketAxess regulatory review (highest-probability binary).** A nine-to-twelve-month window to a H1 2027 close, with a plausible vertical-foreclosure theory and a live FTC consent order from the prior deal. Conditions, divestitures or delay would compress the multiple and strand $6.2B of committed bridge financing costs.

**Goodwill and intangible impairment.** $45.5B against $29.6B of equity, with prior impairments taken on developed technology (2024) and trademarks (2023). Mortgage Technology, with 2% recurring revenue growth and $3M of GAAP operating income per month, is where a charge would originate.

**Leverage with no cushion.** Reaching ≤3.0x within 24 months of a H1 2027 close on ~$8.0-8.2B of combined EBITDA requires gross debt near $24.0-24.6B, roughly $1.3-1.9B of net repayment. Combined FCF of ~$4.3-4.6B less ~$1.6B of buyback and ~$1.2B of dividends leaves $1.5-1.8B annually. The plan closes only if EBITDA grows as expected. Q2 already showed 5% revenue growth with expense guidance raised.

**Legal-entity contagion under a crown jewel.** The 10-K discloses that Treasury clearing and CDS clearing operate out of the same legal entity, with segregated rulebooks, membership, risk frameworks and governance, and that "any financial, operational, or risk management failure within the U.S. Treasury clearing business could adversely affect the CDS clearing business." Low probability, high severity, newly disclosed.

**AI commoditization of evaluated pricing.** The 10-K states directly that AI "may allow for the commoditization of certain data pricing products" and that clients may build in-house pricing capabilities. Evaluated pricing sits inside the $2.08B ASV book at ~47% adjusted margins. This is structural, not cyclical.

**Regulatory ledger, net.** Headwinds: EMIR 3.0's Active Account Requirement (effective February 2026) forces EU participants toward EU CCPs for euro short-term rate derivatives; the EU/UK clearing equivalence for ICE Clear Europe expires June 30, 2028; a July 2026 EU proposal to damp carbon-price volatility could reduce ICE Endex emissions activity. Tailwinds: the March 2026 Basel III re-proposals eliminated the CVA capital requirement, which is unambiguously positive for cleared volumes, and the SEC deferred tick-size and access-fee-cap compliance to November 2027.

**Level 3 earnings volatility.** $452M of H1 pre-tax non-cash investment gains ($389M Polymarket remeasurement, $62M Bakkt) equal roughly $0.61 per share after tax. These flow through GAAP net income and book equity but not revenue, operating income, EBITDA, cash flow or leverage. They cut both ways.

Risk factors score 5.3 — the list of dated, unresolved binaries is longer than for most large-cap financial infrastructure, and the MarketAxess review is not priced.

Options positioning neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view; it prices ICE as a normal-volatility name with a small, dated tail hedge.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($205, +36.9%)

The derivatives and clearing franchise is compounding on both price and volume — energy rate-per-contract +10% with open interest +20%, financial F&O revenue +43% with open interest +46%. Elevated rate-hedging demand keeps ICE's financial futures complex, the direct monetization channel, structurally busy. The $2.08B ASV book compounds at ~8% with ~47% adjusted margins and 100% recurring. Q2's energy weakness reverses as physical flows normalize and hedging demand returns. MarketAxess clears without conditions, and bundling all-to-all credit execution with ICE's evaluated pricing and indices captures share from Tradeweb and Bloomberg while $100M of synergies land ahead of the three-year schedule. Mortgage transactional revenue inflects on any rate relief, pulling recurring seats with it. The issuer buys $1.6B of stock annually at these levels while the share count shrinks ~1% per year. At 23x $8.90-9.00 the stock returns to the low end of the peer multiple range.

### Bear Case ($128, -14.5%)

Q2 was the leading edge rather than a comparison artifact: energy volumes stay soft, NYSE capture continues to erode as rebates rise, adjusted opex growth persists above revenue growth, and FY2027 EPS lands nearer $8.20 than $8.81. MarketAxess review extends or attaches conditions on a vertical-foreclosure theory, leaving ICE carrying bridge costs on a deal that only becomes cash-meaningful once synergies fully land. Institutional credit volumes soften and MarketAxess volumes prove to have been purchased at ~13x an unsynergized EBITDA base. Mortgage Technology takes an impairment against the $45.5B intangible balance. Leverage stalls above 3.0x because management chose to keep the buyback at $400M per quarter, forcing a choice between the repurchase and the target. At 15.5x on $8.20 the stock returns to the June range.

### What Would Change My Mind

The bear case requires one more quarter that looks like Q2. That is why the reward:risk from $149.75 is close to symmetric despite a peer-relative discount.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (fifth consecutive).** The composite score has declined from 7.0 in June to 5.9 now, concentrated in growth prospects and management quality, and the current evidence confirms rather than reverses that trend: revenue growth decelerated to 5%, expense guidance was raised, and $6.0B of debt-funded capital was committed at ~13x unsynergized EBITDA with no quantified revenue synergies. What has changed is the price — the accumulation zone in the low $120s was reached on June 29 and the stock has since recovered ~23%, which removes the entry asymmetry rather than the thesis.

**Base case target: $170** — 19.3x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $8.81, a discount to the 26.2x peer median commensurate with 8% growth and 3.4x pro forma leverage. **+13.5% from $149.75**, plus 1.4% dividend.

**Bull case: $205** (+36.9%). **Bear case: $128** (-14.5%).

## Conclusion

ICE owns the highest-quality collection of assets in the exchange and market-data complex, and the derivatives franchise is demonstrably strengthening: open interest up 20% with rate-per-contract up 10%. The problem is neither quality nor solvency. It is that the incremental capital being deployed is arriving at a fuller price and a higher cost of funds than the last cycle, while the organic revenue line decelerated to 5% and the expense line grew faster for the first time in the observable record. At 17.0x FY2027 consensus the market has already applied roughly the discount those facts warrant.

The stock offers about 13.5% upside to a $170 base case against 14.5% downside to $128, with a probability-weighted expected total return near 12.5% and a base-to-bear reward:risk under 1:1. That combination supports holding for the rates-volatility and clearing growth already in motion, while waiting for either a materially better price near $130-135 or evidence that the Q2 expense-revenue crossover was a single quarter rather than a trend. The absence of any open-market insider purchase across the 25 most recent Form 4 filings — through a decline from ~$169 to ~$122 in which the company itself repurchased $651M — is a mild but real divergence from the constructive case, and one worth watching alongside the Q3 print.
