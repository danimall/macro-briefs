**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

General Dynamics enters mid-2026 with the strongest backlog in its history and two converging margin tailwinds, set against a defense-spending backdrop that remains supportive across nearly its entire portfolio. FY2025 revenue grew 10.1% to $52.55B, diluted EPS rose 13.4% to $15.45, and total backlog jumped 30% to $118B at year-end before surging again to $130.8B in Q1 2026 on a defense book-to-bill of 2.2-to-1. The headline driver is the nuclear-submarine franchise at Electric Boat, which received a $15.38B Columbia-class contract modification in March 2026 plus a ~$2.3B Virginia-class long-lead award in May. This is funded, multi-year, highest-priority Navy demand, and it de-risks the revenue trajectory through the back half of the decade.

The earnings quality is high: 2025 operating cash flow was 122% of net earnings, free cash flow converted at 94%, and ROIC rose for a third consecutive year to 14.2%. Two margin levers are working in GD's favor. Aerospace is climbing into the mid-teens (15.0% in Q1 2026, up 70bps YoY) as the G700/G800 fleet matures down the learning curve and R&D rolls off, while Marine Systems is recovering off a structurally depressed ~7% base as submarine volume scales and supplier cost issues abate. Management guides FY2026 EPS to $16.45–$16.55, a high-single-digit increase, and consensus estimates have drifted modestly higher (+2.3% over 90 days for the current year).

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable. At $341.48 the stock trades at ~22x FY2025 EPS and ~20.7x the FY2026 guidance midpoint, against an EPS growth rate in the low-double-digits, a PEG near 1.7. That leaves roughly 10-14% upside to a reasonable base case, below the asymmetric threshold I require for a BUY. Two company-specific contingencies the 10-Q explicitly flags as potentially "material" create downside that the current valuation does not discount: a revived Sherman Act no-poach class action and an international tracked-vehicle contract with variable consideration that could be "reasonably possible" to come in below estimate through early 2028. The business is excellent; the entry price offers balanced rather than skewed risk/reward.

## Company Overview

General Dynamics is a diversified aerospace and defense prime contractor with ~117,000 employees, organized into four segments. FY2025 revenue of $52.55B breaks down as follows:

- **Marine Systems (32%, $16.7B):** The franchise asset. Electric Boat is the prime contractor and lead shipyard on every U.S. Navy nuclear submarine program: the Columbia-class ballistic-missile program (12 boats, >$125B program of record, first delivery 2028) and the Virginia-class (14 boats in backlog through 2034). Bath Iron Works builds Arleigh Burke destroyers; NASSCO builds auxiliaries and oilers. The only primary competitor is Huntington Ingalls, with whom GD partners on Virginia-class and subcontracts on Columbia-class.
- **Technologies (26%, $13.5B):** GDIT (federal IT services/modernization) and Mission Systems (C5ISR defense electronics), diversified across the Department of War, intelligence, and federal civilian customers.
- **Aerospace (25%, $13.1B):** Gulfstream business jets and Jet Aviation services. The fleet refresh is largely complete, with the G700 (2024) and G800 (certified April 2025) in service and the G400 and super-midsize G300 in development. Deliveries rose to 158 aircraft in 2025 from 136 in 2024.
- **Combat Systems (17%, $9.2B):** Abrams tanks, Stryker vehicles, European LAVs, and munitions/propellants. Sole-source producer of Abrams and Stryker for the U.S. Army.

Customer mix is ~68% U.S. government, 15% U.S. commercial, 17% non-U.S. Production contracts are predominantly fixed-price (51% of U.S. government revenue), which matters for the execution-risk discussion below.

## Financial Analysis

**Top line.** Revenue progressed $39.4B (2022) → $42.3B (2023) → $47.7B (2024) → $52.55B (2025). Q1 2026 grew 10.3% YoY to $13.5B with all four segments contributing; Marine led at +21%. Operating earnings rose 11.7% to $5.36B and operating margin expanded 10bps to 10.2%.

**Backlog.** Total estimated contract value sits at $188.4B. Roughly 55% of backlog is scheduled to convert by YE2027, providing multi-year revenue visibility backed by funded, high-priority Navy demand rather than soft indefinite-delivery ceilings.

**Cash flow and returns.** Q1 2026 OCF swung to +$2.16B from -$148M a year prior as Combat Systems working capital unwound. ROIC has risen steadily: 12.3% (2023) → 13.2% (2024) → 14.2% (2025), with no accrual flag against the high earnings quality.

**Segment margin dynamics.** Three distinct trajectories matter:
- Aerospace margin is the best-quality story, reaching 13.3% FY2025 and 15.0% in Q1 2026 (+70bps YoY) as new models mature and R&D rolls off post-G800 certification. Tariffs shaved ~30bps off Aerospace in 2025.
- Marine Systems margin is structurally low (7.0% FY2025, 7.3% Q1 2026) but improving from a 2024 trough hurt by supplier cost growth. Margin recovery here is the central earnings lever as submarine volume scales.
- Technologies margin is the soft spot, flat-to-declining at 9.5%, guided to 9.2% in 2026 on government-efficiency pressure.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$8.0B and declining; net interest expense was $314M in 2025, guided to ~$340M in 2026 on refinancing. A naïve screen flags an Altman Z of 2.28 ("grey zone") and D/E of 2.23, but these are misleading for a defense prime. The leverage reflects $21B of goodwill (largely from the 2018 CSRA/IT acquisitions) and $10.8B of customer-advance liabilities (a funding source, not distress), not financial fragility. Interest coverage is ~17x, the company holds investment-grade ratings, and the $4–5B revolver carries zero commercial-paper draw. The Z-score's depressed working-capital and equity-to-asset components are an artifact of contract accounting, not genuine risk.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, two of which are now visible in reported results rather than forecast.

**Submarine volume.** The Navy is ramping toward a target cadence of one Columbia plus up to two Virginia boats per year. FY2025 included $20.1B of combined Virginia/Columbia awards. Marine grew 21% in Q1 2026 with management guiding the segment to $17.3–17.7B for the year. This is a multi-decade annuity backed by the highest national-priority funding rating.

**Aerospace maturation.** With the G700 and G800 in service and certification R&D behind it, Gulfstream delivered 158 aircraft in 2025. Management guides Aerospace to ~$13.6B at ~14% margin in 2026, with the margin path toward the mid-teens already demonstrated at 15% in Q1.

**European rearmament.** Combat Systems faces a near-term U.S. headwind, as the M10 Booker program was terminated and Stryker volume is declining while the Army transitions to XM30 and M1E3 Abrams, but surging European demand and munitions partially offset this. Management guides Combat Systems to $9.6–9.7B at ~14.1% margin.

The estimate-revision picture is positive but modest: FY2026 EPS consensus moved +2.1% over 30 days and +2.3% over 90 days to $16.65, with FY2027 at $18.22. This is steady upward drift, not the +30% jump that signals systematic underestimation. The earnings track record is solid, with three beats and one narrow miss over the last four quarters and an 8.2% beat in Q1 2026, supporting confidence in the guidance trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $341.48, GD trades at ~22x trailing EPS of $15.45 and ~20.7x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $16.50. Against EPS growth in the low-double-digits (FY2026 guidance implies ~7%, FY2027 consensus implies ~9%), the PEG sits near 1.7.

**Peer context cuts in GD's favor on a relative basis but does not make it cheap.** The aerospace and defense peer median P/E is 38.7x (HWM 59.9x, TDG 38.7x, LMT 25.8x), so GD trades at a meaningful discount to the group median. But the most apt comparable is NOC at 17.3x, also a pure-play defense prime with a sole-source franchise, which trades several turns below GD. GD's premium to NOC is justified by its submarine franchise, superior cash conversion, and rising ROIC, but it caps how much re-rating room exists. HWM and TDG carry inflated multiples on aftermarket-heavy commercial-aero exposure and are not clean comparables for a shipbuilder.

**The defense theme is supportive but does not override valuation here.** Multiple confirming signals — elevated U.S. defense spending, stated FY2027 budget increases, European rearmament — give the growth thesis durability. But GD is a high-quality compounder fairly valued for its growth, not a cheap stock at <30x forward with a PEG below 1.5. On a base case of ~22x the FY2027 consensus of $18.22, fair value is ~$400, roughly 17% above spot over an 18-24 month horizon. Discounting that back and netting the two material contingencies, the 12-month asymmetry is closer to 10-14% upside against a credible 10-15% downside if either contingency crystallizes. That is balanced, not skewed.

The dividend yield of ~1.8% (29th consecutive annual increase, raised to $1.59 quarterly in March 2026) matches the peer median and provides modest support but is not the thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

GD occupies one of the most defensible positions in U.S. defense. It is the lead designer and builder of every U.S. nuclear submarine, a duopoly participant in destroyers, and the sole-source producer of the Army's Abrams and Stryker. GovCon Wire and Janes reporting indicates GD captures ~78% of the ~$126B Columbia program versus ~22% for Huntington Ingalls. The submarine franchise is effectively irreplaceable on any practical timeline: the industrial base, security clearances, and skilled workforce cannot be replicated by a new entrant.

In business jets, Gulfstream competes at the ultra-long-range, ultra-large-cabin high end against Bombardier and Dassault, and has just completed a rare full fleet renewal that should drive both delivery growth and a maturing-margin tailwind. The competitive trajectory is improving in Aerospace (new fleet, expanding margin) and stable-to-strengthening in Marine and Combat (sole-source, surging demand). The one soft spot is Technologies, where federal IT spending faces government-efficiency pressure and margin is guided lower. On balance the portfolio's competitive position is improving, which supports a high score on this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the binding constraint on the rating. Several items are genuinely company-specific and not fully priced:

1. **No-poach antitrust class action.** A Sherman Act putative class action (filed Oct 2023) alleging a no-solicit conspiracy among shipyards and consultancies was revived by the 4th Circuit in May 2025. GD petitioned the Supreme Court for certiorari in September 2025, cannot estimate the loss, and states an adverse outcome "could have a material impact." This is a live binary with no clear resolution date.

2. **International tracked-vehicle contract.** The Q1 2026 10-Q explicitly flags variable consideration on a large Combat Systems contract as "reasonably possible" to come in below estimate, with a potential "material unfavorable impact." ~$1.3B of net unbilled receivables are tied to this contract, winding down through early 2028. On fixed-price work, this is a real downside lever.

3. **Submarine industrial-base execution.** The Navy's submarine ramp has strained a supply chain hit by post-pandemic labor issues. GD is hiring aggressively (~3,300 planned hires at Quonset, RI) and capex rose ~30% to $1.2B in 2025. Margin recovery and on-schedule delivery on fixed-price work depend on stabilizing ~3,000 suppliers. This is simultaneously the upside (margin recovery) and downside (cost overruns) case.

4. **Government concentration and budget cycle.** With ~70% of revenue from the U.S. government, GD remains exposed to appropriations timing (FY2026 began under a continuing resolution; full-year appropriations enacted early February 2026) and government-efficiency actions, which hit GDIT but are expected to have "limited ongoing impact."

5. **Combat Systems program transitions.** M10 Booker termination and Stryker volume declines create a near-term U.S. headwind, partially offset by European demand.

These are specific, dated, and flagged by management as potentially material. The combination justifies a below-average risk score even for a high-quality franchise.

Near-term ATM IV of 23.3% sits roughly fair to 1-year HV of 20.7% (2.6pp spread, 21st percentile within the current chain). The put/call skew is -8.6% (call-heavy), the put/call OI ratio is a low 0.54, and the term structure is flat. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($400–444):** Marine margin recovers toward 8%+ as submarine volume scales and supplier costs abate, Aerospace sustains mid-teens margins on a maturing fleet, and the $130.8B backlog converts on schedule. FY2027 EPS reaches the consensus $18.22 or higher, and the multiple holds at ~22-24x as the defense cycle and European rearmament reinforce durability. The no-poach case is resolved without material liability and the tracked-vehicle contract lands at estimate. This delivers $400-444, the upper analyst target.

**Bear Case ($290–313):** The no-poach class action produces a material adverse ruling, and/or the international tracked-vehicle contract's variable consideration comes in below estimate, taking a charge through 2027-28. Submarine supply-chain execution stumbles, producing cost overruns on fixed-price work that compress Marine margins. Technologies softness deepens on government-efficiency pressure. EPS growth decelerates to mid-single-digits and the multiple compresses toward NOC's ~17-18x, taking the stock to ~$290-313 (the low analyst target), roughly 8-15% below spot.

**Base Case (~$380–400):** Steady mid-to-high-single-digit EPS growth in line with guidance, modest margin progress in Marine and Aerospace, and no crystallization of the material contingencies. The multiple holds near 21-22x on rising FY2027 estimates, producing ~$380-400 over 18-24 months, with appreciation front-loaded by the contingency overhang.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive growth lean (growth_hold). The 12-month risk/reward is balanced; the longer-term compounding case is intact.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The no-poach antitrust case is resolved or dismissed without material liability, removing the largest binary overhang.
- A pullback toward $300-310 (below ~19x FY2026 EPS), which would restore the >15% asymmetric upside a BUY requires on unchanged fundamentals.
- Confirmed Marine Systems margin inflection above 8% sustained across two quarters, validating the volume-scaling earnings lever.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse ruling in the no-poach litigation, or a material unfavorable charge on the international tracked-vehicle contract.
- Two consecutive quarters of Marine margin contraction below 7%, signaling supply-chain cost overruns on fixed-price work.
- FY2027 EPS revisions turning negative alongside deeper Technologies/GDIT erosion.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) for segment margin confirmation; the Supreme Court cert decision on the no-poach case is the key event-driven catalyst with no fixed date.

## Conclusion

General Dynamics is a high-quality defense compounder with a near-irreplaceable submarine franchise, a record backlog at 2.2x book-to-bill, converging margin tailwinds in Aerospace and Marine, and excellent cash conversion. The score trajectory has drifted lower (7.3 in February to 6.8 in May) primarily on risk-factor reassessment, and the prior three BUYs gave way to a HOLD as the stock re-rated. New data partially reverses the negative trend, as Q1 backlog and Aerospace margin both surprised positively, but it does not clear the valuation and contingency hurdles that prevent a return to BUY.

At ~22x EPS with a PEG near 1.7, GD is fairly valued for its growth rather than cheap. The two material contingencies the 10-Q flags create real, unpriced downside while offering no offsetting near-term upside. The call is HOLD with a growth lean: own it for the multi-year compounding, but the current entry point offers balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward. I would become a buyer on a pullback toward $300-310 or on resolution of the litigation overhang.
