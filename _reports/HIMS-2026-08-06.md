## Executive Summary

Hims & Hers has transitioned from a high-margin telehealth franchise with a regulatory arbitrage to a lower-margin distributor of branded pharmaceuticals with an escalating federal enforcement file. The financial signature of that transition is unambiguous. Gross margin went 82% (FY2023) → 79% (FY2024) → 73.8% (FY2025) → 65.3% in Q1 2026, and the Q1 10-Q states plainly that margin is expected to decrease further through FY2026. US revenue fell 8.4% year over year in Q1 2026 to $529.9M; consolidated revenue rose 3.8% only because acquired Rest-of-World revenue went from $7.3M to $78.2M. Subscriber net adds collapsed from 692K in FY2024 to 282K in FY2025 to 73K in Q1 2026. On a trailing-twelve-month basis through Q1 2026, operating income is −$30.6M versus +$105.6M for FY2025, and net income is −$13.2M.

Management's FY2026 guidance concedes the margin problem. Revenue of $2.8-3.0B against FY2025's $2,347.6M implies ~23.5% growth at the midpoint, but Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $275-350M brackets a $312.5M midpoint that sits *below* FY2025's $318.0M actual. Guidance is also severely back-half loaded: with Q1 delivered at $608.1M revenue / $44.3M Adjusted EBITDA and Q2 guided to $680-700M / $35-55M, the implied second half must produce roughly $1,602M of revenue (~$801M per quarter, a 16% sequential step from Q2's midpoint) and roughly $223M of Adjusted EBITDA — 71% of the full year at a ~13.9% margin against ~6.9% in the first half. That inflection has not been demonstrated. Q2 results on August 10, 2026 are the near-term test.

The regulatory position deteriorated after the last reported balance sheet. The 10-Q filed May 11 disclosed a $15.0M FTC accrual amid "active settlement negotiations," with the explicit caveat that final liability could be materially higher. On July 29, per Reuters, the FTC sued, joined by California and Utah, alleging that user health data was shared with Meta, Snap and other advertisers contrary to privacy representations, alongside deceptive billing and cancellation practices. Beyond money, the requested injunctive relief bears on marketing, subscription enrollment, cancellation and data handling, the operating core of a company that spent $919.3M, or 39% of revenue, on marketing in FY2025. This sits alongside an HHS criminal referral to the DOJ (February 2026), an SEC enforcement investigation into public statements about compounded semaglutide (February 2026), FDA warning letters in September and December 2025, a consolidated securities class action, and a February 2026 cybersecurity incident. At 2.65x guided forward revenue and 24.6x guided Adjusted EBITDA — above the $29.23 analyst mean and $27.00 median — we do not find compensation for that stack. **AVOID.**

## Company Overview

HIMS operates a direct-to-consumer telehealth and pharmacy platform. In most jurisdictions it accepts no third-party payor or insurance reimbursement, which exempts it from the Anti-Kickback Statute, Stark Law and False Claims Act, and which the company believes places it outside HIPAA covered-entity status. The corollary is that every revenue dollar is discretionary consumer spend and every acquisition dollar is paid out of pocket.

Three operating legs: demand generation ($919.3M marketing in FY2025, of which $798.5M disclosed customer acquisition cost, across social, influencer, TV and email/SMS); a clinical layer of physician-owned Affiliated Medical Groups consolidated as variable interest entities under administrative services agreements, with the majority of consultations asynchronous rather than live video; and fulfillment through owned facilities plus third-party Partner Pharmacies. Two former VIEs, Apostrophe Pharmacy and XeCare, became wholly-owned in April and November 2025.

Mix has genuinely broadened. Personalized offerings exceeded 70% of US revenue in FY2025 versus roughly half in FY2024; the Hers brand reached ~40% of US revenue from under 30%; management states a majority of US revenue came from non-GLP-1 offerings. Subscribers reached 2,584K at 3/31/26, up 9% year over year.

International expansion has been entirely acquisitive: Zava (July 2025, UK/Germany/Ireland/France/Spain), Medici (November 2025, Canada), and the pending Eucalyptus transaction. One structural disclosure limits how international revenue should be read: for certain prescription products accessed through third-party Platform Partners, HIMS acts as agent rather than principal and recognizes revenue net. Zava contributed a $100.2M "platform partnerships" intangible, the largest single intangible from any acquisition. International revenue is therefore not economically comparable to US gross-basis revenue on a per-dollar basis.

Reporting granularity is minimal: one operating segment, CEO as CODM, net income as the segment profit measure, no margin disclosure by specialty or geography. The Q1 release also announced a shift from quarterly to annual shareholder letters, reducing disclosure precisely as mix, geography and margin structure change most.

Of the $22.1M consolidated revenue increase in Q1, +$70.9M came from Rest of World against −$48.8M in the US. Adjusting out $28.5M of non-cash inventory write-downs booked in cost of revenue, Q1 adjusted gross margin was 69.9%.

The mechanism behind the margin step-down is straightforward. Compounded semaglutide produced through affiliated 503A pharmacies and an owned peptide facility carried very low cost of goods. Branded GLP-1s must be purchased from a manufacturer at negotiated wholesale prices, converting a vertically integrated product into a resale product. The corroborating balance-sheet item is $131.5M of "manufacturer's discount and rebate receivables" appearing at 3/31/26 (immaterial at 12/31/25), described as arising under vendor supply agreements and driven primarily by inventory shipment volume. The counterparty is not named in any filing; Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly are the only branded manufacturers, but we do not assert which, if either.

**A tax artifact worth correcting.** FY2024 net income of $126.0M included a $54.3M valuation-allowance release; FY2025 included $4.4M. Pretax income was $71.7M and $123.9M respectively, so pretax earnings grew 72.8% rather than the 1.8% the net line implies. FY2024 reported profitability was flattered, and the "flat earnings" framing across 2024-25 is a tax-line effect.

**Trailing profitability has turned negative.** LTM through Q1 2026: revenue $2,369.7M, operating income −$30.6M, net income −$13.2M, Adjusted EBITDA $271.2M. Some of the Q1 deterioration is genuinely one-time ($33.5M restructuring, $15.0M legal accrual, $13.4M acquisition costs, $27.3M of fair-value marks; adjusted net loss −$43.6M). But the FY2025 Altman Z-Score of 2.01 already sat in the grey zone and is driven materially by an EBIT term that is now negative on trailing data.

**Cash flow quality is the most important finding.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $89.4M and company-defined free cash flow of $53.0M look resilient against a $92.1M net loss. The composition does not support that reading. Reconciling: net loss −$92.1M, non-cash add-backs +$114.0M (D&A $22.0M, SBC $36.9M, fair-value changes $27.3M, non-cash restructuring in COGS $28.5M, deferred tax −$14.0M, other items), subtotal +$21.9M before working capital; working capital contributed +$67.5M, dominated by a $167.6M increase in accounts payable against a $116.2M receivables build and $38.0M of deferred revenue. Roughly 75% of reported operating cash flow was working-capital movement. The equivalent pre-working-capital figure a year earlier was $84.8M, a ~$63M year-over-year deterioration in cash generation before timing. As a labeled sensitivity only: pre-working-capital operating cash of $21.9M less $36.3M of capex and capitalized software is −$14.4M. Q1 OCF and FCF should not be annualized.

Whether the payables benefit is timing or structural cannot be resolved yet. New vendor supply agreements may carry longer terms than the prior compounding supply chain, making part of it permanent. Two to three quarters are needed. The July 1, 2026 master receivables purchase agreement with JPMorgan, entered by XeCare and Apostrophe Pharmacy (8-K Items 1.01 and 2.03), is almost certainly a vehicle to monetize the growing rebate receivables; the reported ~$400M size is single-source secondary reporting we could not verify, and whether transfers qualify as sales or secured borrowings materially affects how the next balance sheet reads.

**A second classification point.** Cash paid for taxes on net share settlement of equity awards — $116.7M in FY2025 and $39.2M in Q1 2026 — sits in financing activities and is therefore excluded from company-defined FCF. Treating it as an operating compensation cost turns FY2025 FCF of $57.4M into −$59.3M and Q1's $53.0M into +$13.8M. The classification is GAAP-compliant and buys real benefit (1,581,656 of 3,808,285 shares released in Q1 were withheld and never issued, substituting cash for dilution). We present it as an alternative measure, not an error, but the magnitude is decision-relevant.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/26: total assets $2,267.0M, liabilities $1,820.9M, equity $446.2M — debt/equity 4.08x, up from 2.98x at year-end; current ratio 1.69 from 1.90. Equity fell $94.8M in the quarter. Debt consists of $1.0B of 0% convertible notes due May 2030 (conversion price $70.67, no financial covenants, carrying value $974.1M, capped calls to $89.95) and a $175M secured revolver with total leverage ≤3.50x and interest coverage ≥3.00x covenants, undrawn but for $12.6M of letters of credit. Cash of $222.3M plus short-term AFS investments of $528.6M gives $750.9M of liquid assets, so net debt is $249.1M, or ~$338.8M including $89.7M of acquisition earn-out obligations.

Gross leverage on LTM Adjusted EBITDA is 3.69x, above the 3.50x covenant; net leverage using the full $750.9M is 0.92x. The covenant is satisfied only through netting, which makes the definition of what may be netted consequential, and Amendment No. 2 (May 7, 2026) broadened "cash equivalents" in the ratio numerator without changing the threshold. This is plausibly routine documentation so that a normal board investment policy holding AFS securities is not penalized; we have no evidence of a near-breach and infer no motive. The point is that headroom is a function of the treasury portfolio rather than of earnings.

The convertible notes were marked at $745.9M against $1,000M principal at 3/31/26 — 74.6 cents, implying roughly a 7.4% yield to maturity on a 0% coupon over 4.125 years. Most of that discount is the collapse of embedded equity option value, with the stock 123% below the $70.67 conversion price rather than near parity at issuance in May 2025. It should not be read as a credit-implied default probability.

**Capital intensity.** Gross PP&E was $395.7M at 3/31/26, of which $222.9M (56%) is classified as assets not placed in service — not yet depreciating, not yet generating revenue. FY2025 capex plus capitalized software was $242.6M, 10.3% of revenue, and Q1 D&A already tripled year over year to $22.0M. A depreciation step-up is coming, and there is latent impairment exposure to the extent the peptide and compounding buildout cannot be redeployed. No impairment was recorded in Q1; management frames the capacity as fungible to diagnostics, international and non-GLP-1 personalization.

**Unresolved verification gap.** An 8-K filed 2026-05-21 carries Items 1.01, 2.03 and 3.02 (unregistered sales of equity securities) and was submitted through a capital-markets filer agent rather than the company's usual ID — a pattern matching the May 2025 8-K for the $1B convertible. A further 8-K on 2026-06-02 carries Items 1.01, 2.03 and 8.01. If a second notes offering occurred, net debt, leverage and contingent dilution in this report are understated. Every net-debt and enterprise-value figure here should be read as of 3/31/2026.

## Growth Analysis

Growth on a consolidated line is intact; growth on the underlying business is not. Three measures make the point.

**Domestic revenue is contracting.** US revenue −8.4% year over year in Q1 2026. The consolidated +3.8% is arithmetic from acquisitions, part of which is recognized net as agent.

**Subscriber acquisition efficiency has collapsed.** Net adds of 692K (FY2024) → 282K (FY2025) → 73K (Q1 2026). Dividing disclosed CAC by net adds gives approximately $859 (FY2024) and $2,831 (FY2025), and $2,641 for Q1 2026 ($192.8M ÷ 73K). This ratio is directional only — it inflates mechanically if churn rose or if spend increasingly converted one-time purchasers who are excluded from the subscriber count. The company's own disclosure supports the latter mechanism, which is itself the problem.

**Subscription ARPU is deteriorating faster than the headline.** Monthly revenue per average subscriber fell to $80 from $85, −6%. The 10-Q discloses that excluding one-time purchasers the metric would be roughly $10 lower in Q1 2026 versus under $5 lower a year earlier, implying subscription-only ARPU of roughly $70 versus $80+, a decline of 13% or more. The membership program launched only at end-March 2026 and may reclassify purchases across the subscriber boundary, so Q2 is the first clean read.

**Estimate revisions are sharply negative and thinly covered.** Current-quarter consensus moved from +$0.09 ninety days ago to −$0.05; next quarter from $0.19 to $0.07. Current-year consensus stands at −$0.50, next year at $0.42, on two and three contributing analysts respectively. Coverage is 12 holds against three buys (two strong buy, one buy). The strongest positive forward-estimate signal — a large upward revision cycle — is absent here; the revisions run the other way.

Management's 2030 targets of at least $6.5B revenue and $1.3B Adjusted EBITDA imply a 22.6% five-year revenue CAGR from FY2025 and a 20% Adjusted EBITDA margin against 10-12% guided for 2026. That is not an organic target; it necessarily assumes Eucalyptus and further M&A, and it requires margin to roughly double in a business whose gross margin has fallen 17 points in three years.

The credible growth vectors are branded GLP-1 ramp (BofA cited early traction when raising its target to $36 while maintaining Neutral), full-quarter contributions from Zava, Medici and YourBio, diagnostics, and Eucalyptus. Against them: the UK, EU and Canada prohibit direct-to-public advertising of prescription medicines, which removes the social/influencer playbook that built the US business. That HIMS bought rather than built in every international market is consistent with that constraint.

Three adjustments matter. Second, the Adjusted EBITDA definition was revised twice in four quarters without recasting priors: Q2 2025 added payroll tax on SBC, and Q1 2026 added restructuring charges deemed non-recurring, the latter capturing $33.5M of charges from a strategy the company itself chose. A multiple built on a moving definition needs recomputation on a consistent basis before use. Third, guidance excludes Eucalyptus, which if closed mid-2026 adds revenue not in the base along with costs and cash obligations.

Peer context is imperfect. Health Care Services comparables show a median P/E of 40.1 (CHE 23.0, SHC 40.1, CON 19.9, VCYT 43.9, RDNT negative), but those are profitable or differently structured businesses; HIMS has negative trailing GAAP earnings and a decelerating top line, so the peer P/E median offers no support. On revenue, 2.65x forward for a business with declining domestic revenue and gross margin guided lower is not a discount to anything identifiable.

The market's own read is instructive. Analyst targets span $21 to $40 with a $29.23 mean and $27.00 median — the current price is 10% above the mean and 19% above the median. Where a low multiple on rising estimates would argue that consensus targets are stale floors, here the multiple is high and estimates are falling, so proximity to (and exceedance of) consensus carries no upside signal.

We can find no specific mispricing to underwrite. The bull argument reduces to the 2030 targets, which require both M&A and a doubling of margin, discounted at a price that already embeds ~24x a non-GAAP earnings measure the company has redefined twice. **Valuation: 3.8.**

## Competitive Landscape

The asset that drove 2024-25 growth was regulatory, and it has closed. The chronology from the filings: compounded injectable semaglutide launched May 2024; all Ozempic/Wegovy doses were on the FDA shortage list as of October 30, 2024; the FDA resolved the semaglutide shortage on February 21, 2025, limiting use of 503B outsourcing facilities; HIMS now uses only 503A compounding pharmacies for GLP-1s. Section 503A bars products that are essentially copies of commercially available approved drugs with the same route of administration, and manufacturers have petitioned the FDA to add semaglutide and tirzepatide to the Demonstrable Difficulties for Compounding List, which if finalized would end compounding of those products. In February 2026 the FDA named the company in a statement of intent to restrict GLP-1 APIs for mass-marketed compounded alternatives. Fourteen months from launch to the March 2026 strategic reversal.

What remains: brand, a large first-party customer dataset, and vertical integration into pharmacy, peptides and diagnostics. Those are real but not obviously defensible against the entrant class the 10-K itself names — "pharmaceutical companies that have entered the direct-to-consumer healthcare industry," with the acknowledgment that HIMS "may also increasingly be viewed by pharmaceutical companies as competing with them." If branded manufacturers sell directly at reference prices, the HIMS proposition in weight loss compresses toward convenience and bundling.

Supplier concentration has worsened with the pivot, not improved. The 10-K states that certain weight-loss offerings are primarily fulfilled by one supplier, and that if that supplier stopped fulfilling orders it could significantly slow order fulfillment. Revenue mix has genuinely diversified; supply-side concentration is the meaningful exposure and it is single-sourced.

The diagnostics pivot (Trybe Labs, laboratory testing launched November 2025, YourBio's $89.3M of capillary blood sampling IP) introduces an entirely new regulatory surface: specimen-collection kits potentially requiring 510(k) clearance, the vacated-then-rescinded LDT rule leaving the framework in flux, and state prescription-device distribution licensing. Trajectory across gross margin, share of organic domestic growth and supplier leverage points the same direction. **Competitive position: 3.8.**

## Risk Assessment

**Enforcement stack.** Five simultaneous federal matters, all disclosed in the FY2025 10-K or Q1 10-Q:

1. **FTC litigation.** The October 2023 CID escalated to communicated findings in April 2026 and active settlement negotiations. On July 29, 2026 the FTC sued, joined by California and Utah, with allegations affecting roughly 2.5 million subscribers; shares fell ~10%. We have not read the complaint — venue (reported as N.D. Cal. by most outlets) and counts come from secondary reporting. The injunctive dimension matters more than the monetary one: the 10-Q lists potential ongoing compliance obligations affecting marketing, subscription enrollment, cancellation and data handling.
2. **DOJ criminal referral.** In February 2026 the HHS General Counsel stated publicly that HHS referred the company to the DOJ regarding potential FDCA violations and applicable Title 18 provisions. The 10-K states it is unclear what actions the FDA, HHS or DOJ may take. Highest severity, lowest visibility item in the file; no enforcement action disclosed.
3. **SEC enforcement investigation** opened February 2026 concerning public statements about compounded semaglutide and "related business relationships."
4. **FDA actions:** warning letters in September 2025 (website statements on compounded semaglutide) and December 2025 (503B facility, adverse-event reporting deficiencies), plus the February 2026 statement naming the company.
5. **Securities litigation.** *In re Hims & Hers Health Securities Litigation*, N.D. Cal., consolidated, amended complaint January 2026, class period April 29 – June 22, 2025, centered on representations about the Novo Nordisk relationship. Three derivative suits stayed.

Novo Nordisk's February 2026 patent suit over compounded semaglutide was voluntarily dismissed without prejudice on March 9, 2026, within a month of the US weight-loss reversal. Dismissal without prejudice preserves the right to refile; we have no evidence of a settlement or license, and the rebate receivables do not establish one.

**Structural regulatory dependencies** any one of which could impair the model: corporate-practice-of-medicine and fee-splitting doctrines underpinning the entire Affiliated Medical Group structure, which the 10-K notes have seen a recent increase in state legislative focus; asynchronous telehealth, which covers the majority of consultations while some states require synchronous care; and 503A compounding limits.

**Cybersecurity.** A February 2026 social-engineering incident on two employees gave an unauthorized third party access to certain systems; notifications are substantially complete. It landed in the same month as the FDA statement, DOJ referral and SEC investigation, and five months before a privacy lawsuit, for a business whose product includes discretion about sensitive conditions.

**Eucalyptus.** Up to $1.15B: ~$240M cash upfront, ~$710M deferred across six quarterly installments through the 18-month anniversary, and up to ~$200M of earn-out on FY2026-28 results. HIMS may elect to settle roughly 60% of deferred and earn-out amounts in Class A stock (the 10-Q says "a significant majority"; we use 60%). At $32.16, 60% of ~$910M is ~$546M, or ~17.0M shares — 7.3% of current shares, leaving existing holders with ~93.2% and thus ~6.8% ownership dilution. Settled entirely in cash, the ~$910M compares against $750.9M of liquid assets plus $162.4M of revolver availability, before the $240M upfront and before Zava's €40M earn-out. Convertible dilution is separate and conditional: 14,149,300 shares only above $70.67, with capped calls to $89.95. These maxima should be probability-weighted, not summed.

**Governance.** Dudum controls approximately 90% of voting power through 8,377,623 Class V shares at 175 votes each, on ~3.6% of economic shares. HIMS is a controlled company under NYSE rules; it currently maintains 7 of 10 independent directors but no shareholder vote — on directors, charter amendments, or a sale — is contestable. The 2020 Plan carries an evergreen 5%-of-outstanding annual reserve addition through 2031 (11,362,276 shares added January 1, 2026). Unvested RSUs grew from 13,075K to 19,579K during Q1 2026.

**Concentration of near-term binary risk.** Q2 results on August 10, 2026 test the required H2 inflection four days after this report's information cutoff.

For a fundamentally negative view, this configuration argues for avoiding the shares rather than shorting them. A single unusual print — 3,163 August 7 $31 puts against 961 open interest, 3.3x — is same-week positioning around the earnings date and carries little directional information.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($44, +37%)

The FTC matter resolves as a consent order with a monetary component near the $15.0M accrual and remedies HIMS can operationalize without materially raising acquisition friction, as several prior telehealth privacy matters have. Branded GLP-1 traction ramps through H2, validating the shape of guidance; the membership program stabilizes subscription ARPU on the first clean quarter; Zava, Medici and YourBio contribute full quarters; Eucalyptus closes and adds revenue outside the guided base. Adjusted EBITDA reaches the $350M high end for FY2026 and $450M in FY2027 as branded supply terms scale and the rebate mechanism improves realized product margin. At 25x FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of $450M, EV is $11.25B, equity ~$11.0B, or ~$47 per share before Eucalyptus dilution — call it $44 after issuance. A 31.9% short base amplifies any upside surprise on August 10. Director Wells' $1.17M open-market purchase at $24.235 is the one insider datapoint pointing this way.

### Bear Case ($15.50, −52%)

The H2 inflection fails to materialize: revenue lands near the $2.8B low end and Adjusted EBITDA near $275M, with gross margin continuing toward 62-64% as branded mix rises and shipping cadences shorten. US revenue keeps contracting; subscriber net adds stay near zero and subscription ARPU falls below $70. FTC remedies impose enrollment and cancellation friction that raises effective CAC on a base already at ~$2,600 per net add, and mandated changes to pixel-based and customer-list advertising degrade the acquisition channel. Working-capital tailwinds reverse as branded purchase growth flattens, exposing pre-working-capital operating cash of roughly $22M per quarter against ~$36M of quarterly capex. Eucalyptus consumes cash into a tightening liquidity position, or dilutes ~7%. A portion of the not-in-service PP&E is impaired. The DOJ referral or SEC investigation produces an action. At 1.3x FY2026 revenue of $2.8B, EV is $3.64B, equity ~$3.39B, or ~$14.65; allowing for the liquidity cushion, $15.50.

### Base Case ($24, −25%)

Q2 lands within guidance and FY2026 finishes near the midpoint — $2.9B revenue, ~$310M Adjusted EBITDA — with gross margin settling near 66-68%. US revenue returns to roughly flat rather than growing; international carries mix. The FTC matter costs meaningfully more than $15M and imposes compliance obligations that raise acquisition cost without ending the model. Eucalyptus closes and is settled largely in stock. On that outcome the market applies 18x forward Adjusted EBITDA — a discount to today's 24.6x, appropriate for a business with negative trailing GAAP operating income, a definition-mobile non-GAAP metric, an open criminal referral, and no organic domestic growth. 18x $312.5M = $5.63B EV; less $249M net debt gives $5.38B equity, or **$23.2**. Rounding to $24 for the liquidity cushion.

Expected return to base is negative. That, rather than any single risk item, drives the recommendation.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Q2 2026 results on August 10, 2026 test whether the second-half inflection embedded in guidance is achievable. The specific measurable bar: revenue of $680-700M and Adjusted EBITDA of $35-55M, plus whatever commentary is offered on the implied ~$801M quarterly H2 run rate and ~13.9% H2 margin. Secondary catalysts lack dates: FTC litigation milestones, a possible DOJ or SEC action, and the closing or repricing of Eucalyptus.

**Timing window.** Four days to the primary catalyst, which is favorable carry-wise. But the catalyst is two-sided: management guided Q2 in May with the branded pivot already underway, and BofA reported early branded traction in July. A guidance-consistent print with constructive H2 commentary is a plausible outcome that would produce a violent move against a short.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory here has been 5.0 → 5.6 → 5.0 → 5.0 → 5.0 → 4.7, with three consecutive HOLDs. Continuing a HOLD requires evidence that the deterioration has stopped, and the evidence since the July 19 assessment runs the other way at a roughly unchanged price. The FTC matter moved from settlement negotiation to filed litigation joined by two state attorneys general, adding injunctive exposure to the marketing, enrollment, cancellation and data-handling functions that constitute the company's demand engine. The price is essentially where it was, so risk-adjusted value has fallen.

The fundamental case does not rest on the lawsuit. US revenue is contracting 8.4% year over year, subscriber net adds have fallen 89% from FY2024, subscription-only ARPU is down more than 13%, gross margin has compressed 17 points in three years with more guided ahead, and FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance brackets a midpoint below FY2025 actual on 23% revenue growth. Trailing operating income is negative. Reported operating cash flow is three-quarters working-capital timing, and pre-working-capital cash generation fell ~$63M year over year. Against that, the equity trades at 2.65x forward revenue, 24.6x a non-GAAP earnings measure redefined twice in four quarters, and 76x a three-analyst FY2027 estimate — above the analyst mean and median.

There are genuine assets: $750.9M of liquid securities against covenant-free 0% convertible paper, a real brand, 2.6 million subscribers, and a director committing $1.17M of personal capital at $24.235. Those support the view that the equity is not going to zero. They do not support paying 24x a moving non-GAAP metric for a business whose domestic revenue is shrinking while five federal matters remain open. With 31.9% of the float short and options positioning call-tilted, the appropriate expression is to own neither side. **AVOID.**
