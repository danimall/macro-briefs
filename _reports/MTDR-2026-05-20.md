## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a Delaware Basin-focused E&P with integrated midstream operations, trading at $59.52 per share — roughly 8.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $7.28 and 4.4x EV/EBITDA. The stock has appreciated 39% from its November 2025 low of $37.14 but remains 11% below its 52-week high and substantially undervalued relative to the current oil price environment. MTDR's FY2025 realized oil price was only $64.99/bbl. Consensus estimates have risen 71.5% over the past 90 days (from $4.24 to $7.28 for FY2026), but even at $7.28, these estimates likely embed realized oil prices far below current spot levels.

The company demonstrated operational excellence with a 21% production increase in FY2025, an 18.3% Q1 2026 EPS beat ($1.53 vs. $1.29 estimate), and a clean insider buying pattern from the CEO, CFO, and Co-President near the cycle low. The primary non-consensus catalyst is San Mateo midstream monetization (potentially worth $2-4B at peer multiples against MTDR's $7.4B equity cap) and the embedded oil price lag — if WTI averages significantly above FY2025 realizations in 2026, MTDR's actual earnings power could substantially exceed the $7.28 consensus.

Key risks include commodity price volatility (minimal hedging), geographic concentration in the Delaware Basin, customer concentration (72% of revenue from three purchasers), and post-acquisition leverage of ~$2.55B in corporate debt. However, $2.4B in annual operating cash flow, $1.9B in available liquidity, and rapid deleveraging trajectory mitigate the balance sheet concern.

## Company Overview

Matador Resources is an independent E&P company founded in 2003, focused on the Delaware Basin (98% of production) in Southeast New Mexico and West Texas. The company targets the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring formations across approximately 197,600 net acres. FY2025 production averaged 207,070 BOE/d (58% oil), representing 21% growth over FY2024 driven primarily by the September 2024 Ameredev acquisition.

A key differentiator is MTDR's 51% ownership of San Mateo Midstream (49% Five Point Energy), providing 720 MMcf/day gas processing capacity, ~120 miles of oil gathering pipelines, and 475,000 Bbl/day produced water disposal capacity. San Mateo generated $164.7M in third-party revenue in FY2025 (+17% YoY) and provides flow assurance in a capacity-constrained basin.

## Competitive Landscape

MTDR operates in the premier U.S. oil basin alongside much larger operators (ExxonMobil, Chevron, Diamondback, Devon). Despite smaller scale ($7.4B market cap), the company has consistently outperformed peers on TSR (approximately 1.8x peer group over 5 years) through:

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH):** MTDR carries minimal oil hedges. While this provides maximum upside exposure to elevated oil prices, a reversion to $60-70 WTI would compress earnings significantly. The company's operating costs (~$24/BOE all-in) provide a cash flow breakeven near $40 WTI, but equity valuation would compress materially.

**Geographic Concentration:** 98% of production from a single basin creates concentrated exposure to regional infrastructure disruptions, weather events, and regulatory changes (particularly on 33% federal acreage).

**Customer Concentration:** Three purchasers = 72% of revenue. Loss of any single offtaker would create significant short-term disruption.

**Leverage:** Post-Ameredev debt of $2.55B corporate + $883M San Mateo is meaningful but manageable at ~1.1x net debt/EBITDA. Rapid deleveraging trajectory reduces this risk quarterly.

**Induced Seismicity:** New Mexico protocols could curtail produced water disposal volumes, potentially restricting production growth in specific areas.
