**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dentsply Sirona is the world's largest diversified manufacturer of professional dental products, and it is in the middle of a multi-year decline that has erased roughly two-thirds of book value through serial impairments. The company has posted net losses in each of the last five years (2025 net loss of $598M after $650M of goodwill/intangible write-downs, following $910M of losses and $1,014M of impairments in 2024). Revenue has fallen from $3,922M in 2022 to $3,680M in 2025, and the deterioration accelerated underneath the headline in Q1 2026: reported revenue was flat at $880M, but constant-currency sales fell 6.7%, with a dollar tailwind masking the true demand picture. Peer Henry Schein grew 6.3% in the same quarter, which points to company-specific share loss in CAD/CAM, imaging, and implants rather than pure sector weakness.

The balance sheet is the binding constraint. Net debt of roughly $2.0B sits against shrinking equity ($1,339M at year-end 2025, down from $3,811M in 2022) and a market cap of about $2.0B, so the enterprise is split roughly half equity, half debt. The Altman Z-score of 1.11 places the company in the distress zone. The 2025 financing actions reflect the strain: a $435M bridge loan, $550M of 8.375% junior subordinated notes due 2055, and December 2025 covenant amendments, all signs of tight financial flexibility and reliance on expensive capital. The dividend was eliminated starting Q1 2026 to preserve roughly $130M a year for deleveraging.

The bull narrative — a new CEO, a freshly installed board, a permanent CFO (named June 11, 2026), a $120M cost program, increased R&D, and the DS Core/Primescan 2 "connected dentistry" bet — is structurally coherent but entirely unproven in the numbers. Q1 2026 showed no operational improvement, CTS swung to a segment operating loss, and management itself warns that its deliberate distribution-model overhaul will add quarter-to-quarter volatility on top of soft demand. The stock has fallen 37% over twelve months to about $10.14. We rate XRAY AVOID. The valuation is not absurd against a normalized earnings power that may eventually materialize, but the combination of secular revenue decline, distress-zone leverage, self-inflicted channel disruption, and an unproven turnaround means the risk/reward does not favor ownership here.

## Company Overview

Dentsply Sirona operates four segments. Essential Dental Solutions (EDS, ~$1.47B 2025 sales) is the profit engine — endodontic, restorative, and preventive consumables generating $514M of segment adjusted operating income. Connected Technology Solutions (CTS, ~$1.04B) houses dental equipment and technology including CEREC CAD/CAM, imaging, treatment centers, the DS Core cloud platform, and the Primescan 2 scanner; it is the most troubled segment, generating only $52M of adjusted operating income in 2025 and slipping to a loss in Q1 2026. Orthodontic and Implant Solutions (OIS, ~$850M, down 12.6% in 2025) covers implants, prosthetics, and clear aligners and has been the hardest hit. Wellspect Healthcare (~$325M) is continence care — catheters and bowel irrigation — and is the only growing segment (+6.6% in 2025, +15% reported in Q1 2026), and sits outside dental.

About two-thirds of sales come from outside the United States, and roughly two-thirds of dental product distribution runs through third-party distributors. The company is the market leader by size but is losing share in core equipment categories to competitors on price, against an industry backdrop of soft US clinician capex, tariffs, and the rise of Dental Support Organizations (DSOs) that increase buyer price sensitivity.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is sustained value destruction punctuated by large non-cash impairments. The four-year trend:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 3,922 | 3,965 | 3,793 | 3,680 |
| Operating income ($M) | (937) | (85) | (879) | (422) |
| Net income ($M) | (950) | (132) | (910) | (598) |
| Goodwill/intangible impairment ($M) | — | — | 1,014 | 650 |
| Stockholders' equity ($M) | 3,811 | 3,293 | 1,942 | 1,339 |

Goodwill fell from $2,688M (2022) to $1,148M (2025), and the CTS and OIS reporting units are now fully impaired of goodwill (accumulated impairment losses of $1,589M). The remaining indefinite-lived intangibles in OIS and CTS "continue to approximate carrying value," meaning any further deterioration triggers additional write-downs.

Margins are compressing. Gross margin fell to 50.0% in 2025 from 51.6% in 2024, and to 48.5% in Q1 2026 from 53.0% a year earlier, driven by unfavorable mix, CAD/CAM and implant pricing pressure, and tariff costs that hit all four segments. EU-manufactured equipment sold into the US and US consumables sold into Europe are both tariff-exposed, so this is a structural rather than one-time headwind.

The balance sheet is the dominant risk. Debt/equity is 3.05x, and net-debt-to-total-cap rose to 59.9% (from 48.9% in 2024) purely because equity shrank. Equity of $1.3B sits against more than $2.3B of debt. Interest expense rose 28% to $88M in 2025 and will climb further given the 8.375% coupon on the new junior notes. A $2,103M valuation allowance against deferred tax assets produces erratic effective tax rates (-23.1% in 2025, 76.3% in Q1 2026) because the company cannot reliably monetize its losses.

Cash flow is the relative bright spot but is weakening. Operating cash flow fell to $235M in 2025 from $461M in 2024, with free cash flow of only $104M (a 2.8% margin). Q1 2026 operating cash flow was a thin $40M, and cash fell from $326M to $190M in the quarter. Liquidity is adequate but not comfortable — about $665M available under the revolver/commercial paper at Q1 2026 — against $228M due within a year of fiscal year-end and $914M due in years four and five. The need to amend covenants in December 2025 underscores limited headroom. The dividend elimination and absence of buybacks (despite a $1.2B authorization) reflect a capital-allocation pivot toward survival.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth here to score, only the question of whether decline can be arrested. Revenue has fallen about 6% cumulatively since 2022, and the underlying trend is worse than reported because FX is currently a tailwind. The 4.3% constant-currency decline in 2025 is the relevant number alongside the Q1 figure. Estimate revisions confirm negative momentum: current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 11.5% over 90 days and 4.4% over 30 days, and current-year estimates are drifting lower to $1.42. This is the opposite of the revision velocity that would signal a genuine turn.

The growth thesis rests on three unproven legs. First, the $120M annualized cost program (approved February 2026, $60-65M of cash charges, $60M booked in Q1) should support adjusted EBITDA even on flat-to-declining revenue. Second, R&D is being raised from roughly 4% to at least 5% of sales to rebuild competitiveness in CAD/CAM and imaging through the DS Core platform and Primescan 2 — capital-intensive and slow to pay off, with a $63M minimum cloud-services commitment through 2028. Third, Wellspect's mid-teens growth provides a real, if small, organic offset and a potential divestiture lever.

Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $3.5-3.6B in sales (about 0.7% below consensus at the midpoint) and $1.40-$1.50 adjusted EPS. The wide gap between adjusted EPS guidance and GAAP losses, combined with FX-flattered headlines, makes the reaffirmed guidance look optimistic against the negative constant-currency Q1. Henry Schein's growth against XRAY's negative constant-currency result is the most damaging single comparison: the sector is soft, but XRAY is losing share within it.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are both negative and meaningless given GAAP losses. The honest valuation anchor is the adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $1.45 for FY2026 and consensus of roughly $1.55 for next year. At $10.14, the stock trades at about 7.0x FY2026 adjusted EPS and 6.5x next-year estimates. On an enterprise basis, with roughly $2.0B net debt and a $2.0B equity value, EV is about $4.0B against an adjusted EBITDA figure that is [unverified — actual: N/A] in the provided sources, so an EV/EBITDA multiple cannot be reliably derived here.

That is not expensive in isolation, but the adjusted figures exclude very real, recurring cash and non-cash costs (restructuring, impairments, litigation), so the "earnings" being multiplied are of low quality. Peer comparison is muddied because most Health Care Supplies comparables (NEOG -3.2x, AVNS -15.6x, QDEL -0.8x, BLFS -109.9x) also carry negative or distorted P/Es; only ICU Medical at 73.8x is positive, reflecting a heterogeneous, troubled sub-sector rather than a clean benchmark.

The case for a higher valuation score rests on hidden value: Wellspect is a growing, higher-quality, non-dental medical-device business that the market arguably under-credits inside a struggling dental conglomerate, and the 10-K flags an ongoing portfolio review for dispositions. A Wellspect sale at a healthcare-device multiple could meaningfully delever and re-rate the equity. Against that, equity is a thin sliver atop $2.0B of net debt, so small changes in enterprise value swing the equity disproportionately; the same leverage that creates upside optionality magnifies downside. The analyst range of $9.00 low to $17.00 high (mean $13.40, median $13.00) captures this binary: meaningful upside if the turnaround takes and Wellspect is monetized, with a floor near the current price if it does not. We score valuation 4.5 — cheap on normalized earnings but with no identifiable near-term catalyst to force the re-rating and a balance sheet that caps how high the score can go.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive trajectory is negative, and that matters more than market-leader status. XRAY is losing share in CAD/CAM, imaging, and implants to lower-priced competitors, and the Q1 2026 gap against Henry Schein quantifies the problem. The dental supplies market is fragmenting and consolidating around DSOs, which increases buyer price sensitivity precisely where XRAY's premium equipment positioning is most exposed. Gross margin compression is the financial fingerprint of this pricing pressure.

The bull-case moat is the DS Core cloud platform plus Primescan 2 building a sticky "connected dentistry" ecosystem with recurring software revenue. The logic is sound — recurring revenue and hardware lock-in would stabilize CTS — but it is unproven, capital-intensive, and being built while the company simultaneously overhauls its distribution model. EDS consumables remain a genuine competitive strength (the segment's $514M adjusted operating income is the company's backbone), and Wellspect competes well in continence care. Those two segments anchor the franchise; the dental equipment and implant businesses are where share is bleeding.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is leverage colliding with declining EBITDA. With 3.05x debt/equity, 59.9% net-debt-to-cap, a distress-zone Z-score, and a maturity schedule that includes $914M in years four and five, the December 2025 covenant amendment and the 8.375% note issuance signal that financial flexibility is thin. A further demand leg down or another impairment would tighten covenant headroom quickly.

Further impairments are a live risk given that OIS and CTS indefinite-lived intangibles approximate carrying value. Distributor concentration compounds this: revenue runs through a limited number of distributors, and the company is simultaneously renewing US distribution (new non-exclusive Patterson agreement, January 2026), diversifying its distributor base, and reducing distributor-held inventory — a self-inflicted source of sales variability that management explicitly flags. This is unusual for a distressed company and raises the odds of lumpy, disappointing quarters even if end-demand stabilizes.

Litigation tails are real but largely reserved and insured. Two securities class actions are outstanding — one tied to the 2021-22 revenue-recognition/distributor-rebate scandal (class certified July 2025, now at summary judgment) and one tied to alleged misrepresentations about the Byte aligner business (partial motion-to-dismiss denial January 2026). A German criminal tax investigation over 2016-17 intercompany loans and an IRS dispute over a $451M 2016 stock-redemption distribution are lower-probability, higher-severity tails. None appear imminent enough to threaten solvency, but they consume cash and management attention. Tariff exposure across all four segments and the discretionary, financing-dependent nature of implants and aligners round out the company-specific risk set.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 52.7% to 68.1% over the two-and-a-half weeks to June 12 while the stock was roughly flat, indicating the market is pricing in increasing uncertainty rather than complacency, consistent with an unresolved turnaround and a stretched balance sheet. The put/call open-interest ratio remains elevated at 2.06 (down from 2.28), reflecting persistent hedging/bearish positioning. Short interest is 12.2% of float with 3.4 days to cover, elevated but not at squeeze-risk extremes. The combination of rising IV and heavy put positioning argues against this being a clean, low-uncertainty entry point.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Return-to-Growth plan works: the $120M cost program lifts adjusted EBITDA, R&D reinvestment stabilizes CTS share, the distribution overhaul completes without lasting damage, and Wellspect is divested at a premium multiple to delever. The new CEO and freshly installed board execute, the new CFO restores financial discipline, and the equity re-rates from roughly 7x toward the analyst high of $17.00 (+68%) as the market re-credits normalized earnings power of $1.40-$1.55 and the balance sheet repairs. Insider buying by the Chairman and a director at ~$12.45 in March supports this read.

**Bear case.** Constant-currency decline continues, FX turns from tailwind to headwind, the distribution overhaul produces lumpy misses, another impairment hits, and covenant headroom tightens. With $2.0B of net debt against a $2.0B equity value, a 15-20% decline in enterprise value roughly halves the equity. The stock revisits the 52-week low near $9.40 and below, toward $8.00 (-21%), as the distress premium widens. The recurring miss pattern (three of the last four quarters) and falling estimates point toward this outcome.

The base case sits closer to the bear path: a company that arrests some of the decline through cost cuts but cannot reignite growth, trading in a $9-12 range as the turnaround drags through 2026-2027 without a decisive catalyst.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The cleanest bearish catalyst is the Q2 2026 earnings report, where continued constant-currency revenue decline and distribution-overhaul disruption could drive another miss against already-cut estimates. A further impairment of the remaining OIS/CTS intangibles is a second potential trigger, though its timing is not dated. There is no single hard-dated event (no near-term debt wall forcing refinancing in the next two quarters), which weakens the short setup.

**Timing window.** Q2 2026 earnings fall in the late-July/early-August window. That is a reasonable near-term window, but the underlying thesis (slow decline, balance-sheet drag) is a grind that could take 12-18 months to play out, raising carry cost relative to the payoff.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 12.2% of float with 3.4 days to cover. This is elevated but below the >20% level that creates acute squeeze risk. The positioning leaves room, but it also means the bearish view is partly shared.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning confirms bearish sentiment (P/C OI 2.06, rising IV to 68.1%), which raises the cost of expressing the short through puts. Rich near-term IV signals elevated carrying cost and that the market already discounts downside.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A bear-case target near $8.00 implies roughly 21% downside from $10.14. The upside risks are material and specific: a Wellspect divestiture announcement, a credible turnaround quarter, modest insider buying already on the tape, and acquisition interest in a beaten-down market leader. With downside of about 21% against squeeze, divestiture, and acquisition upside, the reward:risk does not reach 2:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamentals are weak and the trajectory is negative, but there is no dated hard catalyst forcing a near-term decline, the equity carries real divestiture and acquisition optionality on top of $2.0B of net debt, and rising IV makes the short expensive to carry. This is a stock to avoid owning, not one to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit a constructive view if: (1) constant-currency revenue turns positive for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the turnaround is reaching the top line rather than only the cost line; (2) net-debt-to-total-cap falls back below 50%, whether through a Wellspect divestiture or sustained free-cash-flow-driven deleveraging; or (3) gross margin recovers above 51% on a sustained basis, signaling that pricing pressure and tariff costs have stabilized. A clear divestiture of Wellspect at a premium multiple, with proceeds applied to debt, would be the single most likely catalyst to upgrade.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — secular dental-equipment share loss, tariff drag, leverage, and self-inflicted channel disruption — are likely to persist through 2026 and into 2027. The Return-to-Growth plan is a 24-month program; meaningful evidence of success is unlikely before the second half of 2026 at the earliest. The next natural reassessment point is the Q2 2026 earnings report.

## Conclusion

Dentsply Sirona is a distressed turnaround. Five years of net losses, $1.66B of impairments in 2024-2025, an Altman Z-score of 1.11, $2.0B of net debt against a $2.0B equity value, and constant-currency revenue still declining in Q1 2026 while a key peer grew define a business losing share within a soft sector. The new leadership, $120M cost program, dividend elimination, and June CFO appointment make the recovery plan structurally credible, but Q1 showed no operational improvement, estimates are falling, and management's own distribution overhaul adds volatility precisely when the company can least afford it.

The valuation is not stretched — roughly 7x normalized adjusted EPS with embedded Wellspect divestiture optionality — but it sits on low-quality adjusted earnings, with no near-term catalyst to force a re-rating and a balance sheet that magnifies any downside. We rate XRAY **AVOID**. It is not a clean short given partly-reserved litigation, modest insider buying, and acquisition/divestiture optionality, but it does not belong in a long portfolio until constant-currency growth turns positive and leverage demonstrably falls.
