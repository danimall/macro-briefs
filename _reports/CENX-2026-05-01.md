## Executive Summary

Century Aluminum trades at $59.44, up 262% over the trailing twelve months, driven almost entirely by the escalation of Section 232 tariffs on aluminum imports from 10% to 50%. This policy shift tripled the Midwest Premium (MWP) from $427/t to $1,295/t in FY2025, adding approximately $416M in revenue. Consensus EPS estimates for FY2026 have surged 76% over the past 90 days to $10.51, implying the stock trades at approximately 5.7x forward earnings — a substantial discount to peer median P/E of 16.2x and the analyst consensus target range of $75-$86.

The growth thesis is specific and catalyzed: Grundartangi's second potline restarted ahead of schedule in April 2026 and should reach full production by July, adding roughly 70,000+ tonnes of annualized capacity. Mt. Holly's 50,000-tonne expansion is producing metal. Combined, these two events could add 15-20% to annualized shipment volumes by H2 2026, at a time when realized pricing is at multi-year highs. The Inola JV and Hawesville data center equity represent longer-duration option value.

The thesis has material vulnerabilities. Century has missed earnings estimates in all four reported quarters by an average of 22%. Every named executive officer sold shares in Q1 2026, with CEO Jesse Gary liquidating 300,000 shares for ~$15.5M. Glencore — which accounts for 54% of revenue and owns 36.4% of equity — sold a $327M block in March 2026. The company has an unremediated material weakness in internal controls that already produced a financial restatement. Most critically, the entire profitability structure rests on a tariff regime that could be modified by executive order. These risks are severe enough to preclude a BUY rating despite the attractive forward valuation.

## Company Overview

Century Aluminum is one of the few remaining primary aluminum producers in the United States, operating smelters in Sebree, Kentucky and Mt. Holly, South Carolina, along with the Grundartangi (Norðurál) smelter in Iceland and a 55% interest in the Jamalco bauxite mining and alumina refinery joint venture in Jamaica. FY2025 shipments totaled 647,112 tonnes (57% U.S., 43% Iceland), down from 677,967 tonnes in FY2024 due to the Grundartangi transformer failure in October 2025.

The company sold its idled Hawesville, Kentucky smelter to a TeraWulf affiliate in February 2026 for $200M cash plus 6.8% equity in a planned data center venture. In January 2026, Century announced a 60/40 joint venture with Emirates Global Aluminium (EGA) to build a 750,000 tonne/year smelter in Inola, Oklahoma, which would be the first new U.S. aluminum smelter in nearly 50 years, backed by a $500M DOE grant.

Glencore is both the dominant customer (54% of FY2025 revenue) and the largest shareholder (~36.4% of outstanding shares), creating an inherently conflicted governance structure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** FY2025 revenue of $2,528M (+13.9% YoY) was driven by $416M in favorable LME and regional premium pricing, partially offset by $116M in higher raw materials, $93M in higher power costs, and $42M in lower volumes from the Grundartangi outage. Gross margin improved to 10.1% from 8.3%, and operating income reached $158M (6.3% margin), the best operating result since at least FY2022.

Net income of $42M ($0.42 EPS) was depressed by $94.7M in mark-to-market derivative losses on MWP and LME hedges — the company hedged volumes at prices that subsequently rose well above strike levels, creating paper losses that will reverse as hedges roll off. Operating performance was strong, but the reported bottom line understates the earnings power of the current pricing environment.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $185M substantially exceeded net income, reflecting depreciation and non-cash hedge losses. Free cash flow of approximately $85M after $100M in capex represents a 3.4% FCF margin. Capex is guided at $170-180M for FY2026, elevated by $60-70M in Grundartangi repair costs expected to be insurance-reimbursed and $45M for the Mt. Holly restart.

**Balance Sheet:** Post-Hawesville sale, effective liquidity stands at approximately $618M ($134M cash + $200M sale proceeds + $284M credit facility availability). Long-term debt of $480M (primarily $400M 6.875% Senior Secured Notes due 2032) was refinanced in July 2025, extending the maturity runway significantly. Debt-to-equity of ~0.60x is moderate for a commodity producer. The accumulated deficit of -$1,626M and $471M deferred tax asset valuation allowance reflect decades of cyclical losses. The company holds $1,545M in federal NOL carryforwards that could shield future taxable income.

**Earnings Quality Concerns:** The four consecutive quarterly earnings misses averaging -22% vs. consensus are a significant red flag. Even as the pricing environment improved materially, Century consistently delivered below expectations. Contributing factors include the Grundartangi outage, derivative losses, and the Jamalco consolidation restatement, but the pattern suggests either poor operational forecasting or structural cost overruns that the market has not fully calibrated to.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth trajectory is driven by three identified catalysts with specific timelines:

1. **Grundartangi Full Restart (by July 2026):** The second potline restarted ahead of schedule in April 2026. Full production recovery of approximately 70,000+ annualized tonnes would restore the facility to pre-outage capacity. At current LME prices (~$2,600-2,700/t), this represents approximately $180-190M in incremental annual revenue.

2. **Mt. Holly Expansion (H2 2026):** The $50M investment to restart 50,000+ tonnes of idled capacity is producing first metal. At current MWP levels, the incremental revenue contribution is approximately $130-150M annualized, with margins likely above the smelter's current run rate given the fixed-cost absorption benefit.

3. **Inola JV (2027-2030+ timeline):** The 750,000 tonne/year smelter would substantially expand Century's production base, but execution risk is high. The power supply agreement with PSO/AEP is not yet secured — this is the critical gating item. Century's 40% share of a $4B+ project requires substantial capital beyond the $500M DOE grant. Construction must begin by year-end 2026.

Consensus estimates reflect these catalysts: FY2026E EPS of $10.51 has been revised upward 76% over the past 90 days, and FY2027E of $9.96 has been revised up 133%. The next quarter estimate of $3.02 (revised up 66% over 90 days) implies a material step-up from $1.25 in Q4 2025. The earnings revision velocity is among the strongest in the Materials sector.

## Valuation Assessment

At $59.44, CENX trades at approximately 5.7x FY2026E EPS of $10.51 and 6.0x FY2027E EPS of $9.96. Against the peer median P/E of 16.2x, this appears deeply discounted. All three covering analysts rate the stock BUY with targets ranging from $75 to $86 (median $77), implying 26-45% upside.

However, the low multiple reflects genuine structural risk, not market oversight. The earnings stream is a direct function of Section 232 tariff policy. If tariffs were reduced from 50% to 25% (their pre-May 2025 level), the MWP would likely compress by 40-60%, potentially taking $400M+ out of annual revenue. At pre-tariff economics, normalized EPS would be closer to $2-4, implying a current-price P/E of 15-30x — fair to expensive relative to peers. The market is assigning a substantial probability to some tariff moderation over the cycle, which is rational.

Additionally, the stock has already rallied 262% in twelve months. The 52-week low of $14.77 reflects the pre-tariff escalation reality; the current price incorporates most of the known catalysts. At 5.7x forward earnings, there is a margin of safety if current policy persists, but the rally from the low leaves limited room for positive surprise.

Valuation score is constrained because the low multiple accurately reflects policy dependency risk rather than market mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Century Aluminum operates in a structurally favorable competitive position within the U.S. market. The domestic primary aluminum industry shrank from 33 smelters in 1980 to 4 operating facilities today. Century operates 2 of these. This scarcity creates strategic value for national security and domestic supply chains — the U.S. produces only a fraction of its aluminum needs.

The 50% Section 232 tariff creates an effective moat that renders foreign competition largely non-competitive in the U.S. market. The resulting MWP of $1,295/t is the primary driver of CENX's current profitability. Section 45X production tax credits from the IRA provide additional subsidy, though the OBBBA phases these out starting 2031 (25% annual reduction, zero by 2034).

On a cost basis without tariff protection, U.S. smelting is globally uncompetitive due to higher energy prices. Grundartangi benefits from Iceland's geothermal/hydroelectric power at competitive rates (~70% LME-indexed, providing a natural hedge), but the U.S. operations face market-rate electricity exposure. Sebree's power costs are particularly vulnerable to market disruptions.

The Inola JV, if executed, would more than double U.S. primary aluminum capacity and position Century as a dominant domestic producer. The competitive trajectory is improving, contingent on policy continuity and Inola execution.

## Risk Assessment

### Tariff Reversal (Existential Risk)
The 10-K notes Section 232 tariffs were not directly impacted by the Supreme Court's February 2026 IEEPA ruling, but they can be modified by executive order. A new administration, a trade deal with key aluminum-exporting nations, or pressure from downstream aluminum consumers could reduce tariffs. A reduction to 25% would likely compress MWP by $500-700/t, wiping out approximately $350-500M in annual revenue and making the current valuation appear fully priced or expensive. This risk is unhedgeable and binary.

### Earnings Execution
The Q1 2026 report (likely May-June 2026) will test whether the Grundartangi restart and Mt. Holly expansion translate to bottom-line improvement, given the pattern of operational underdelivery.

### Insider Selling and Glencore Block Sale
The broad, coordinated selling pattern following the rally suggests management and the controlling shareholder view the current price as reflecting substantial future value. No insider purchases are on record.

### Material Weakness / Restatement
The unremediated material weakness in Jamalco business process controls, combined with a financial restatement for incorrect consolidation accounting, represents a governance and reporting reliability concern. The company's financial statements require additional scrutiny until remediation is confirmed.

### Customer Concentration
54% of revenue from Glencore, which is simultaneously the largest shareholder and has a board seat. Any deterioration in the commercial relationship, or Glencore's continued reduction of its stake, could create instability.

### Inola Execution Risk
The $4B+ smelter project depends on securing a power supply agreement (not yet done), DOE grant compliance, JV partner EGA's operational stability, and massive capital deployment. Century's 40% share still requires substantial equity contribution beyond the DOE grant.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 86.0% is 22.9pp above realized volatility of 63.1%, placing CENX options in the 88th percentile of implied richness. The term structure is in backwardation (86.0% near-term vs. 79.1% long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress or a binary event. Put/call OI ratio of 0.26 is call-heavy, suggesting speculative positioning. The 3-month tenor shows elevated put volume (P/C vol ratio 2.86), indicating institutional hedging at a specific horizon. The options market is pricing substantially more uncertainty than the consensus BUY rating implies. Short interest at 13.9% of float with 3.4 days to cover confirms meaningful bearish institutional positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($75-86, +26% to +45%)
Tariffs remain at 50% through 2027+. Grundartangi reaches full production by July 2026. Mt. Holly expansion ramps successfully. FY2026 EPS of $10-12 is achieved. Inola power agreement is secured and construction begins, providing longer-duration growth optionality. Hawesville data center equity appreciates as AI infrastructure buildout continues. Insurance reimbursement for Grundartangi repairs is received. The stock re-rates from 5.7x to 7-8x forward earnings as execution risk diminishes, reaching $75-85.

### Bear Case ($25-35, -41% to -58%)
Tariffs are reduced to 25% (or lower) through trade negotiations or executive action. MWP compresses to $500-700/t. Earnings normalize to $2-4 EPS. Inola power agreement fails. Grundartangi restart encounters further setbacks. Glencore continues reducing its stake, pressuring the stock. The material weakness leads to further restatements. At 12-15x normalized EPS of $2-4, the stock trades at $25-50.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Two consecutive quarterly earnings beats (would break the four-miss streak and validate forward estimates)
2. Cessation of insider selling, or any open-market purchases by C-suite
3. Inola power supply agreement announced with PSO/AEP
4. Stock pullback to $45-48 range (improving risk/reward to ~60% upside to consensus target)

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Any reduction in Section 232 aluminum tariffs below 50%
2. FY2026E consensus EPS revised below $7.00 (would indicate catalysts failing to materialize)
3. Glencore reduces stake below 25% or signals intent to exit entirely
4. Additional financial restatement or SEC enforcement action related to material weakness

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected May-June 2026). The Q1 2026 report will capture the first full quarter impact of the Mt. Holly expansion. A beat would substantially strengthen the growth thesis; a fifth consecutive miss would weaken it materially.

## Conclusion

Century Aluminum presents a genuine growth thesis supported by specific, near-term catalysts: the Grundartangi restart, Mt. Holly expansion, and a pricing environment that generates FY2026E EPS of $10.51 at 5.7x forward earnings. Analyst consensus is unanimously bullish with $75-86 targets. Estimate revisions are sharply positive across all periods.

The rating is HOLD rather than BUY because the risk profile is severe and asymmetric on the downside. The consecutive earnings misses, universal insider selling, unremediated material weakness, and existential dependence on Section 232 tariffs collectively prevent a high-conviction long recommendation. The options market is pricing near-term uncertainty well above the fundamental consensus.

The position should be held for the identifiable near-term catalysts (capacity restarts ramping through H2 2026) while closely monitoring the specific upgrade and downgrade triggers outlined above. If execution confirms the earnings trajectory and insider selling abates, the stock merits upgrade to BUY. If tariff policy shifts or earnings continue to disappoint, the downside is substantial.
