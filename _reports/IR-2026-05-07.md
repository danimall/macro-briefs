## Executive Summary

Ingersoll Rand is a high-quality industrial compounder built on mission-critical flow creation products (compressors, pumps, vacuum systems) with 36.5% aftermarket revenue, 16% FCF margins, and an aggressive M&A engine that has added 15+ acquisitions in a single year. The company's installed base flywheel — where cumulative aftermarket revenue exceeds original equipment price over a 10-12 year useful life — creates genuine recurring earnings power that most industrials lack.

The problem is valuation and recent execution. At $78.72, the stock trades at 53x trailing GAAP earnings and approximately 22x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($3.51 consensus), a premium to peers trading at a median 28x trailing P/E. This premium historically reflected organic growth and margin expansion, but both have deteriorated: organic revenue was negative in FY2025 and Q1 2026, margins are compressing (EBITDA margin fell 140bps YoY in Q1), and the $2.96B ILC Dover acquisition produced a $230M impairment within 12 months. Estimate revisions are modestly negative across all periods (-1.4% to -4.3% over 90 days), and the earnings beat record is only 50% over the past four quarters.

The stock sits 22% below its February 2026 high of $100.96, and the analyst consensus target of $94.67 implies 20% upside. However, the fundamental case for re-rating requires organic growth to inflect positive and margin compression to reverse, neither of which is clearly visible in Q1 2026 data. At current prices, this is a quality company at a premium multiple during a period of deteriorating operational momentum. The right entry is lower, or after evidence of organic acceleration materializes.

## Company Overview

Ingersoll Rand operates through two segments:

**Industrial Technologies and Services (ITS, ~79% of revenue):** Air and gas compression, vacuum/blower products, fluid transfer, loading systems, and power tools. Brands include Ingersoll Rand, Gardner Denver, Nash, CompAir, and Elmo Rietschle. Aftermarket represents 40.6% of this segment.

**Precision and Science Technologies (PST, ~21% of revenue):** Positive displacement pumps, automated liquid handling, single-use pharma systems (ILC Dover), and medical device components (Flexan). Brands include Milton Roy, Seepex, Thomas, and ARO.

The company generates 54-57% of revenue internationally (significant EUR, GBP, CNY exposure), operates 60+ manufacturing facilities across 6 continents, employs 21,000+ people, and has no single customer exceeding 10% of revenue. The core competitive claim is the broadest product portfolio in flow creation technologies, enabling cross-selling across the installed base.

## Growth Analysis

Organic revenue is negative, estimate revisions are falling across all timeframes, and management guidance for FY2026 implies only ~5% adjusted EPS growth at the midpoint ($3.45-$3.57).

**Structural growth drivers remain intact but unproven at current prices:**
1. **Aftermarket flywheel:** 36.5% of revenue is recurring, higher-margin service/parts revenue tied to installed base expansion
2. **M&A compounding:** 200+ targets in pipeline, 10 LOIs outstanding, 400-500bps annualized inorganic revenue addition targeted
3. **Secular tailwinds:** Carbon capture (Q1 2026 win), hydrogen compression, water/wastewater treatment, life sciences automation
4. **Book-to-bill of 1.07** in Q1 suggests demand is not collapsing

**The organic growth problem is the central uncertainty.** If ISM continues stabilizing above 50 and short-cycle demand recovers in H2 2026, organic revenue could inflect positive and validate the premium multiple. If tariffs and macro uncertainty depress capex decisions further, the company remains dependent on M&A for any growth — and acquisitions alone do not justify a 22x forward P/E.

**Life Sciences recovery:** PST orders grew double-digits in Q1 2026, and the biopharma spending recovery could partially rehabilitate the ILC Dover thesis. However, the 30% fair value cushion in the Life Sciences reporting unit means further impairment risk persists.

The trailing P/E of 53x is misleading due to GAAP impairments. On adjusted forward earnings ($3.51 FY2026E), IR trades at ~22.4x — below higher-quality peers like Dover (28.4x) and Xylem (29.5x), which have stronger organic growth profiles. Snap-on at 20x and Otis at 20.4x represent the "mature/stable" comparables with higher yields.

The valuation implies a growth premium that the current organic trajectory does not support. At 22x forward earnings with negative organic revenue, falling estimates, and compressed margins, the stock is priced for recovery that has not yet materialized. FCF yield of ~4% provides floor support, and the analyst consensus median target of $94 (20% upside) reflects belief in H2 recovery plus continued M&A value creation.

**Base case fair value:** $88-92 (25-26x FY2026 adjusted EPS of $3.51), requiring organic growth to return to positive territory and margins to stabilize.

## Competitive Landscape

Ingersoll Rand competes against Atlas Copco (Swedish, ~3x IR's revenue in relevant segments), Flowserve, IDEX, Dover, Kaeser, and numerous regional competitors in a highly fragmented market. IR's competitive advantage rests on portfolio breadth — no other single company spans compressors, vacuum, pumps, and life science automation with equivalent depth.

The aftermarket model creates genuine switching costs: once equipment is installed with IR's technology, the customer faces significant cost/risk from changing platforms over the 10-12 year useful life. This is the source of the ~40% aftermarket revenue in ITS.

Competitive position is stable but not demonstrably improving. Organic revenue decline suggests IR is not gaining share in the current environment. Management attributes this to macro/project timing rather than competitive displacement, and China "outperformance" (positive organic orders despite a weak market) partially supports that claim.

Tariff dynamics could temporarily benefit IR versus European-headquartered Atlas Copco on US-sold equipment, though IR's own significant international manufacturing creates offsetting exposures.

## Risk Assessment

**1. ILC Dover Acquisition Value Destruction (HIGH):** $230M impairment within 12 months of a $2.96B acquisition, insurance claim filed, Life Sciences reporting unit has only 30% fair value cushion. If biopharma capex remains weak, another $100-200M impairment is plausible.

**2. Organic Growth Remains Negative (MODERATE-HIGH):** Two consecutive periods of negative organic revenue raise the question of whether the market is saturated at current pricing/capacity or if macro headwinds are temporary. Recovery is assumed in guidance but not proven.

**3. Tariff and Supply Chain Risk (MODERATE):** Significant steel, aluminum, and copper inputs with some single-sourced castings. "One-for-one" pricing offset is margin-neutral in theory but creates customer friction and volume risk. Tariff escalation beyond current assumptions would pressure guidance.

**4. Serial Acquirer Integration Risk (MODERATE):** 15+ acquisitions per year creates integration complexity. One potential >$1B deal in pipeline could move the needle positively or negatively depending on execution.

**5. Goodwill Concentration (MODERATE):** 69% of assets in goodwill/intangibles, with $387.5M annual amortization. Discount rates of 8-10% are sensitive to the current elevated rate environment.

**6. Executive Turnover (LOW-MODERATE):** Multiple 8-K filings for executive departures in 2025. Not alarming in isolation but bears monitoring.

## Conclusion

Ingersoll Rand is a structurally sound industrial compounder with exceptional FCF generation, a defensible aftermarket model, and real secular growth exposure across clean energy, water, and life sciences. The business quality merits a premium to industrial peers.

However, the current price of $78.72 already reflects a meaningful premium despite deteriorating operational momentum. The valuation at ~22x forward adjusted EPS offers insufficient margin of safety when the near-term growth outlook is uncertain and the bear case is quantifiable at 13-17% downside.

The correct approach is to wait for either a cheaper entry point ($70-72, where the FCF yield provides genuine cushion) or fundamental evidence that organic growth has inflected. Until one of those conditions is met, the risk/reward at current levels is approximately symmetric, which does not justify deploying capital.

**Rating: HOLD.** Reassess after Q2 2026 earnings.
