## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a Delaware Basin-focused E&P company producing 207,000 BOE/d, trading at 4.6x EV/EBITDA. Using the company's own adjusted free cash flow definition (which accounts for midstream capex, capital-accrual adjustments, and San Mateo non-controlling interest), FY2025 adjusted FCF attributable to Matador shareholders was approximately $437M, implying a P/FCF of ~16x and FCF yield of ~6.2%. The stock has appreciated 30% over the past year from its October 2025 lows (~$37) but remains well below its 52-week high of $66.84. Consensus estimates were revised upward significantly over the 90-day period through early 2026, though post-Q1 2026 analyst revisions have moderated this trend.

The investment case rests on three identifiable catalysts: (1) the Hugh Brinson Pipeline expected to come online in Q3-Q4 2026, which will shift gas volumes from severely depressed Waha pricing to Henry Hub, adding an estimated ~$90M in annual revenue per $0.50/MMBtu improvement with zero incremental capital; (2) the Woodford well currently being completed, representing potential inventory expansion not reflected in current reserves or drilling location counts; and (3) San Mateo midstream monetization optionality, which at peer midstream multiples could represent significant value to Matador's 51% interest. With 15 Buy ratings, zero Sells, insider buying during weakness, and a median analyst target of approximately $73 (30% above current price), the evidence supports a BUY recommendation.

The primary risks are commodity price sensitivity (the company has meaningful derivative exposure, as evidenced by Q1 2026's $255M unrealized derivative loss), Waha gas basis volatility (which worsened to approximately ($6.00)/MMBtu in Q1 2026), and leverage of ~1.3x net debt/EBITDA.

## Company Overview

Matador Resources is an independent E&P company founded in 2003, operating primarily in the oil and liquids-rich Wolfcamp and Bone Spring formations of the Delaware Basin across Southeast New Mexico and West Texas. The company controls 212,500 net acres (81% held by production), producing approximately 58% oil with the remainder in natural gas and NGLs.

A key differentiator is Matador's vertically integrated midstream subsidiary, San Mateo (51% owned), which provides 720 MMcf/day of gas processing capacity, 195 miles of water pipelines, and third-party revenue of $165M in 2025. This integration provides flow assurance, cost savings, and substantial monetization optionality.

The company has identified 5,568 gross (1,852 net) drilling locations representing 10-15 years of inventory, with 76% expected to feature 2+ mile laterals. The September 2024 Ameredev Acquisition ($1.8B) significantly expanded acreage and production capacity.

- Operating cash flow: $2.43B (+8% YoY)
- Operating cash flow less D/C/E capex: $883M; however, Matador's adjusted free cash flow attributable to shareholders (which includes midstream/other capex and San Mateo NCI adjustments) was $437M for FY2025

**Production Growth:**
Production scaled from 131,813 BOE/d (2023) to 170,751 (2024) to 207,070 (2025), a 57% increase over two years. Oil production reached 119,723 Bbl/d in 2025. This growth rate is exceptional among mid-cap E&Ps.

**Balance Sheet:**
- At year-end 2025, total MTDR-only debt was approximately $2.55B in senior notes plus $398M on the revolving credit facility, with an additional $883M in non-recourse San Mateo debt
- Cash: $15M (revolver-dependent working capital model)
- Net debt/EBITDA: ~1.3x
- Available borrowing capacity: $1.8B under the credit facility at year-end 2025
- Current ratio: 0.79 (note: the Credit Agreement's covenant current ratio, which includes unused borrowing availability, must exceed 1.0 and is distinct from the simple balance-sheet ratio)
- Fitch upgraded to BB (from BB-) during 2025

**Post-Year-End Debt Actions (March 2026):** In March 2026, Matador issued $750M of 6.000% senior notes due 2034, used proceeds to tender for $419.8M of the 2028 notes, and irrevocably deposited funds to redeem the remaining $80.2M on April 15, 2026. This effectively eliminated the 2028 maturity wall. Additionally, borrowings under the Credit Agreement declined to $185M at March 31, 2026, and further to $45M by May 6, 2026. San Mateo borrowings declined to $860M by May 6. The company's liquidity position improved materially in Q1 2026.

**Capital Allocation:**
- 2026 D&C capex guided at $1.35-1.44B (7-12% reduction from 2025's $1.53B)
- Dividends: $1.50/share annually (2.6% yield)
- Share repurchases: $55.8M in 2025 at avg $41.31; $344M remaining under authorization
- Capital spending front-loaded (55-60% in H1 2026), creating H2 free cash flow acceleration

**Q1 2026:**
The GAAP loss of $35.9M ($-0.29/share) was primarily driven by a $255M unrealized loss on derivatives (mark-to-market on oil costless collars as prices moved above collar ceilings) and a $15.6M loss on debt extinguishment related to the 2028 notes tender. Production continued growing, debt was reduced, and operational metrics improved. Adjusted EPS of $1.53 beat consensus by approximately 18-21%.

**Note on Forward P/E Discrepancy:** The company profile shows forward P/E of 14.58, which at $56.96 implies forward EPS of ~$3.91. However, the estimate revision data shows current year consensus of $7.34 from 18 analysts. The discrepancy likely reflects a stale or differently-sourced forward EPS figure in the profile data. These are the operative figures for valuation purposes, though post-Q1 estimate cuts may lower these figures.

**Near-Term Catalysts:**
1. **Hugh Brinson Pipeline (Q3-Q4 2026):** Energy Transfer's 500,000 MMBtu/day pipeline will connect Delaware Basin gas production to Gulf Coast markets. Energy Transfer expects the line to begin flowing gas in Q3 or Q4 2026 and be fully in service by year-end 2026. With the Waha-Henry Hub differential averaging ($5.99)/MMBtu in Q1 2026 (and approximately ($6.57)/MMBtu between March 31 and May 6, 2026), Matador estimates that a $0.50/MMBtu improvement in average realized gas price could add approximately $90M annually. This is purely incremental with zero capital spend by MTDR, though related transportation agreements include minimum volume commitments and potential deficiency fees.

2. **Woodford Well Results:** The company's first Woodford well was drilled and cased in Q1 2026 with completions ongoing. No Woodford locations are included in current inventory (5,568 locations) or reserves (667M BOE). Success expands the runway without acquisition spending.

3. **San Mateo Monetization:** Management has stated they "do not need the cash" but want to "pull value forward" at the right time. At peer midstream multiples, Matador's 51% interest in San Mateo could represent significant value, though the company has explicitly stated such monetizations are difficult to estimate and has not provided estimated proceeds for 2026. An IPO or strategic sale would crystallize embedded value not fully reflected in MTDR's share price.

4. **H2 2026 FCF Acceleration:** With 55-60% of capex front-loaded to H1, second-half free cash flow generation should meaningfully exceed first-half rates, supporting dividend coverage and continued buybacks at $344M remaining authorization.

**Reserve Valuation Context:** Proved reserves are valued at $8.24B (PV-10, using SEC pricing) vs. a market cap of $7.1B and enterprise value of ~$10.5B. Comparing PV-10 to enterprise value (which exceeds PV-10) rather than equity market cap alone provides a more analytically sound perspective. The stock is trading at a level where proved reserves alone account for a substantial portion of enterprise value, before assigning value to 5,568 identified but unbooked drilling locations or Woodford upside.

The low target of $62.00 sits above the current price of $56.64, meaning even the most conservative analyst covering the stock sees meaningful upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Matador's competitive position is improving. Key evidence:

1. **Production growth of 21% YoY** substantially exceeds mid-cap E&P peers, most of which are maintaining flat to low-single-digit growth under capital discipline frameworks.

2. **Integrated midstream** is unusual for a company of this size. San Mateo's 720 MMcf/d processing capacity eliminates third-party bottleneck risk and provides $165M in third-party revenue, effectively a separate business embedded within the E&P.

3. **Operational efficiency gains** are documented: 13% YoY reduction in cycle times, Simul-Frac/Trimul-Frac deployment, AI-driven completions optimization processing 40M data points/day, and D&C cost guidance of $785-805/lateral foot (6% below 2025).

4. **Inventory depth of 10-15 years** at current development pace, with 76% of locations featuring 2+ mile laterals (higher productivity per well).

5. **Water recycling at >70%** provides both cost and regulatory advantages in an increasingly water-constrained basin.

The primary competitive weakness is geographic concentration: 98% Delaware Basin exposure means no diversification against basin-specific risks (pipeline outages, seismicity regulations, state-level policy changes).

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (High):**
The company has meaningful oil derivative exposure. Q1 2026 demonstrated this clearly, with oil costless collars generating a $312M liability at March 31, 2026, and a $51.8M net realized/unrealized loss in Q1 related to oil collars (as prices exceeded collar ceilings). For downside scenarios, a $10/Bbl decline in WTI from Matador's Q1 2026 realized price of $72.83/Bbl would compress EPS by approximately $2.00-2.50/share. This remains the dominant risk factor.

**Waha Gas Pricing (Moderate-High):**
Natural gas at Waha traded at a Waha-Henry Hub differential averaging ($5.99)/MMBtu in Q1 2026, worsening to approximately ($6.57)/MMBtu between March 31 and May 6, 2026. Matador has at times received negative gas prices. The Hugh Brinson Pipeline partially mitigates this from late 2026, but until operational, gas realizations represent a significant drag on margins.

**Leverage & Maturity Risk (Moderate — Reduced):**
Net debt/EBITDA of ~1.3x is manageable. The March 2026 refinancing eliminated the 2028 maturity wall, with the next major note maturity now being the $900M 2032 notes. Revolver borrowings declined to $45M by May 6, 2026. The credit upgrade to BB is directionally positive and the maturity profile has improved materially.

**Federal Lands Exposure (Moderate):**
33% of Delaware Basin acreage sits on federal BLM lands. While the current regulatory environment is favorable, any policy shift could create permitting delays. This is a known structural risk that the market has historically penalized MTDR for during periods of regulatory uncertainty.

**Key-Man Risk (Moderate):**
CEO Joseph Foran, 40+ year industry veteran and founder, has no named successor and no key-man insurance. The 8-K filings from January 23 and April 27, 2026 (both Item 5.02) likely reflect the ongoing management succession buildout. Christopher Calvert's elevation to EVP/CFO and the Co-President structure (Erman and Singleton) suggest the board is actively addressing succession planning, though the 10-K still explicitly identifies this risk.

**Customer Concentration:**
Three purchasers account for 72% of revenues. While common in the Permian Basin given limited marketing outlets, this creates counterparty dependency.

**Seismicity:**
New Mexico regulations require enhanced reporting and potential shut-ins of disposal wells. San Mateo operates 16 commercial SWD wells. A major seismic event could temporarily curtail production.

## Conclusion

Matador Resources presents a compelling risk/reward at $56.64. The stock trades at 7.8x forward earnings (vs. 34.5x peer median), 4.6x EV/EBITDA, and approximately 16x company-defined adjusted free cash flow. EPS estimates were revised upward significantly through early 2026, the company has beaten on earnings in 4 consecutive quarters (most recently by ~18-21%), and the CEO purchased shares in the open market at $49.78 during a period of weakness.

The score trajectory shows improving fundamentals (financial health +0.5, competitive position +0.5 over recent assessments) with oscillation between BUY and HOLD recommendations. The current evidence — the depth of valuation discount, the specificity of near-term catalysts (Hugh Brinson Pipeline, Woodford, San Mateo), insider buying, and materially improved debt maturity profile — supports upgrading conviction to BUY. The primary risk is commodity price exposure (both direct and through derivative mark-to-market impacts), which warrants position sizing at 2-3% rather than maximum conviction.
