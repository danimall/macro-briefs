## Executive Summary

Kaiser Aluminum converts purchased primary aluminum and scrap into specialty mill products — aerospace heat-treat plate from Trentwood, coated can and closure stock from Warrick, general engineering extrusions and rod, and automotive extrusions. Because metal cost is substantially passed through, the economically meaningful top line is Conversion Revenue (net sales less the hedged cost of alloyed metal), and that figure was essentially flat across 2023-2025: $1,465.9M, $1,456.2M, $1,453.2M. FY2025 headline net sales rose 11.5% to $3,373.0M almost entirely on metal-price pass-through after the Section 232 aluminum tariff went to 50% in June 2025 and the Midwest Premium moved from roughly $0.19/lb to $0.59/lb.

Two things then happened simultaneously. First, an operating inflection arrived as two multi-year capital programs, Trentwood Phase VII plate expansion and a fourth Warrick coating line, came into service. Q2 2026 Conversion Revenue rose 16.8% year over year to $437.0M with conversion revenue per pound up to $1.43 from $1.30, and Packaging conversion revenue per pound up 22% to $1.12 from $0.92. Metal is stripped out of that metric by construction, so the Packaging move is price/mix, not pass-through. Second, the rising Midwest Premium produced a large timing gain: Adjusted EBITDA of $310.2M in FY2025 included $93.0M of favorable Metal Price Lag, and 1H26 Adjusted EBITDA of $294.8M included $64.0M more. On a trailing-twelve-month basis, roughly $121M of ~$464M of Adjusted EBITDA (about 26%) is a mark on inventory in the pipeline rather than operating cash generation, and the company's own risk disclosures confirm the mechanism is symmetric when metal prices fall.

Management is guiding to that reversal. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of +45-55% on the $310.2M base implies $449.8M-$480.8M; against 1H26 actual of $294.8M, the implied second half is $155.0M-$186.0M, a 37-47% decline from the first half. Consensus has followed: current-year EPS has been revised up 32.8% in 90 days to $13.52, while next-year consensus sits at $11.29, or 16.5% *below* the current year. At $184.15 the shares trade at 13.6x the FY2026 windfall year, 16.3x the FY2027 normalization year, and above the highest published analyst target of $183 (mean $169.25, low $146). Probability-weighting a $115 bear, $178 base and $225 bull produces roughly $171 of expected value, a negative total return including the 1.67% dividend, with downside asymmetry of about -37% versus +22%. This is the fifth consecutive review and the score has declined from 6.1 to 5.3; the operating story has improved while the price has moved further ahead of a normalized earnings base. That combination warrants trimming rather than another hold.

## Company Overview

Kaiser is a converter, not a smelter. It buys primary aluminum and scrap, alloys and fabricates it, and sells engineered rolled plate and sheet, bare and coated coil, and extruded and drawn products from U.S. plants plus one Canadian facility in London, Ontario. It reports a single operating segment with Net income as the disclosed measure reviewed by the CODM, which limits visibility into end-market profitability.

FY2025 net sales by end market: Packaging $1,489.6M (44.2%), Aero/High Strength $837.8M (24.8%), General Engineering $759.2M (22.5%), Automotive Extrusions $286.4M (8.5%). Domestic sales were 96.4% of the total, and exports fell to 7% of net sales from 10% in each of the prior two years. Because Aero/HS carries conversion revenue of $2.23/lb versus Packaging's $1.12/lb, aerospace is roughly 31% of Conversion Revenue against 25% of net sales; the mix matters more to profit than to reported revenue.

The 2025 capital cycle is complete. Construction in progress fell from $297.5M at 12/31/24 to $66.7M at 12/31/25 while gross machinery and equipment rose $249.3M. The Sherman, Texas soft-alloy extrusion plant was exited under the 2024 restructuring plan (including a $4.6M multiemployer withdrawal obligation payable in 2027) and sold in Q4 2025, contributing to $53.1M of proceeds from property disposals.

Concentration is structural. One customer was 16% of FY2025 net sales and a second 15%, unchanged from 2024; the top five were approximately 56%. At year-end one customer represented 21% of trade receivables, up from 17%. On the input side, the top five primary-aluminum suppliers provided 82% of pounds and the largest 24%, up from 21%. Sixty-five percent of employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements; the critical Trentwood and Newark USW agreements run to September 2030, while Richmond, Virginia expires November 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 reported results (weighted-average-cost basis, prior years retrospectively adjusted).** Net sales $3,373.0M; operating income $188.8M (5.6% margin, up from 3.7%); interest expense $50.1M; pretax income $150.0M; tax provision $37.5M (25.0% ETR); net income $112.5M; diluted EPS $6.77 on 16.62M diluted shares. Gross margin ex-D&A improved to 13.12% from 11.82%. ROE was 14.3% on average equity of $784.6M; ROA 4.5%.

**The accounting-basis question is the largest single fact in the file.** Effective January 1, 2025 Kaiser changed inventory costing for finished goods, WIP and raw materials from LIFO to weighted average cost, applied retrospectively, with an unqualified Deloitte opinion including a change-in-accounting-principle paragraph. The company's own Note 18 comparison for FY2025: under LIFO, COGS ex-D&A would have been $3,114.6M rather than $2,930.6M, operating income $4.8M rather than $188.8M, and net income a **loss of $(29.1)M, or $(1.80) per diluted share**, rather than $112.5M and $6.77. Prior-year effects were an order of magnitude smaller (+$24.5M pretax in 2024, +$26.7M in 2023). Cumulative balance-sheet effect at 12/31/25: inventory of $725.2M versus $443.2M under LIFO, a $58.2M incremental deferred tax liability, and retained earnings of +$142.5M versus a $(74.2)M deficit.

The mechanism is unremarkable: WAC defers recognition of current-period input cost inflation into COGS relative to LIFO, so in a year when the Midwest Premium roughly tripled the effect was extreme. Operating cash flow was identical under both methods. The analytical consequence is symmetry: when metal prices decline, WAC will produce higher COGS than LIFO would have. The $6.77 FY2025 EPS and the WAC-basis margin trajectory are basis-dependent and directionally levered to the aluminum price.

**Metal Price Lag, quantified.** FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $310.2M included $93.0M of favorable lag against $45.0M in FY2024, so ex-lag Adjusted EBITDA was roughly $217.2M versus $172.4M, a real improvement of about $45M, or 26%. Adjusted EBITDA margin on Conversion Revenue was 21.3% reported and 14.9% ex-lag, versus 14.9% and 11.8% respectively in FY2024, so approximately 310bp of genuine margin expansion sits underneath about half of the reported increase being timing. Cross-checking magnitude: Q2 2026 Conversion Revenue of $437.0M at $1.43/lb implies roughly 306M lbs shipped, or ~1.15-1.22bn lbs annualized. $93.0M of lag on ~1.15bn lbs is ~$0.081/lb against a ~$0.40/lb Midwest Premium move, implying effective exposure on roughly 20% of annual volume, about 2.4 months of metal in the pipeline, consistent with the inventory turn. On the same exposure, a $0.20/lb premium retracement implies on the order of $45-50M of unfavorable lag spread over roughly two quarters. That is a sensitivity, not a forecast.

**Q2 2026.** Net sales $1,256.6M (+52.7%); Conversion Revenue $437.0M (+16.8%); Adjusted EBITDA $166.3M (+146%, including $27.0M of favorable lag versus $14.0M a year earlier); net income $96.8M; diluted EPS $5.72. Hedged cost of alloyed metal per pound rose 73% to $2.68. Automotive Extrusions shipments fell 11%.

The quality caveat: Q2'25 Adjusted EBITDA ex-lag was $53.7M and Q2'26 ex-lag was $139.3M, an $85.6M improvement on $62.8M of incremental Conversion Revenue. Incremental margins above 100% are not sustainable and indicate the ex-lag split is imperfect. Realized hedge settlements ($27.0M embedded in the FY2025 metal line; $32.1M of hedge gains reclassified into COGS in FY2025) overlap with the lag definition, and prior-year comparatives included depressed aerospace volumes. I would not extrapolate the ex-lag run rate at face value.

**Cash flow is the weak link.** Operating cash flow fell to $111.4M in FY2025 from $167.1M and $211.9M; capex was $136.9M; free cash flow was **-$25.5M**, following -$13.7M in FY2024 and +$68.7M in FY2023. Two consecutive years of negative FCF were paid through while distributing a flat $3.08/share dividend ($51.3M). Working capital was the cause: a $125.3M inventory build and $81.9M receivables build, with metal inflation the proximate driver. Year-end cash fell to $7.0M with $22.3M drawn on the revolver.

That position reversed by 6/30/26: revolver at zero, cash $58.5M, total liquidity $628.4M. 1H26 capex was only $43.8M against FY2026 guidance of $120-130M, so $76-86M lands in the second half against guided EBITDA of $155-186M.

**Capital structure is de-risked.** $550M of 4.50% notes due June 2031 and $500M of 5.875% notes due March 2034 (issued at par in November 2025 to redeem all $500M of 4.625% notes due 2028 at par). The ABL revolver was amended in October 2025 to $575M maturing October 2030, with $540.2M available at year-end. No maturities before 2031 other than the revolver. The refinancing cost roughly $6.3M per year of incremental interest to term out the nearest maturity by six years. FY2026 interest expense of $58-62M is a reasonable sensitivity given the coupon swap and shrinking capitalized interest (only $4.4M in FY2025 versus $9.1M in FY2024, with CIP now $66.7M).

Net debt at 6/30/26 was approximately $1,006M ($1,050M notes plus $14.7M finance leases less $58.5M cash), or ~2.2x LTM Adjusted EBITDA of ~$464M and ~2.9x LTM ex-lag of ~$343M. On the FY2025 base alone it was 3.4x reported and 4.9x ex-lag. Interest coverage was 6.2x reported and 4.3x ex-lag.

**Tax is an underappreciated support.** FY2025 book provision was $37.5M but cash taxes paid were only $7.6M, with $33.1M deferred. Federal NOLs are $123.9M (indefinite, usable against 80% of taxable income), federal R&D credits $22.1M, and the PP&E deferred tax liability grew from $133.3M to $183.9M consistent with 100% bonus depreciation. Cash taxes should remain materially below book tax for several years.

**Off-balance-sheet working-capital reliance.** Kaiser sold $1,042.6M of trade receivables in FY2025 under customer-sponsored supply chain financing programs (31% of net sales) with $24.1M of discount fees charged by the customers' financial institutions that Kaiser states it has been or expects to be substantially reimbursed for by those customers. Q2 2026 sales were $334.3M with $7.7M of fees. The transfers qualify as sales and Kaiser retains no servicing obligation, but reported receivables of $395.2M understate gross credit exposure generated during the year, and the tool's availability depends on customers maintaining their programs.

Solvency screens are basis-dependent: Altman Z of 3.06 on the WAC basis (marginally in the safe zone) versus approximately 2.79 on a LIFO basis (grey zone); Piotroski F-Score of ~7 on WAC versus ~4 on LIFO. The more decision-relevant facts are unambiguous: no maturities before 2031, $628.4M liquidity, 2.2x LTM leverage.

**Data note:** the profile field reports forward and trailing P/E both at 12.68 with EPS of $6.77, which is internally inconsistent ($184.15/$6.77 = 27.2x). LTM EPS from the four reported quarters is $12.85 (Q3'25 $1.86, Q4'25 $1.53, Q1'26 $3.74, Q2'26 $5.72), giving 14.3x trailing. All multiples below are computed from the disclosed figures.

**Financial Health: 6.0.** Maturity profile, liquidity and cash-tax position are strong; two years of negative FCF against an uncovered dividend, working-capital sensitivity to metal prices, receivables-program reliance, and earnings-quality dependence on the WAC change hold this at the prior level rather than above it.

## Growth Analysis

The distinction between revenue growth and value-added growth is decisive here. Conversion Revenue fell 0.9% cumulatively from 2023 to 2025 while net sales rose 9.3%. All of the reported FY2025 revenue growth was metal pass-through.

What changed in 2026 is the Warrick and Trentwood capacity coming online. Packaging conversion revenue per pound rose 22% to $1.12, a metal-neutral metric, which is the strongest single operating datapoint available. The most likely explanation is mix upgrade into coated end/tab and closure stock, which is more specification-controlled and higher-barrier than bare body stock. An alternative reading is that part of the increase reflects contractual pass-through of energy and freight inflation or annual price resets rather than durable mix shift; the 10-Q does not decompose it. Even a partial mix explanation implies real progress against the earlier bear view that Warrick was an expensive, low-return acquisition.

FY2026 guidance is Conversion Revenue +10-15% ($1,598M-$1,671M) and Adjusted EBITDA +45-55% ($449.8M-$480.8M). The implied second half of $155.0M-$186.0M against 1H26's $294.8M is the crux. If second-half guidance embeds essentially zero incremental lag, consistent with management framing the outlook on aluminum prices remaining stable at current levels, then implied 2H26 ex-lag EBITDA of $155-186M is well above the ex-lag 2H25 run rate, making guidance operationally constructive and metal-conservative at once. That is the most favorable reading. The less favorable reading is that 2H26 ex-lag EBITDA of $155-186M is still 20-33% below 1H26's ex-lag $230.8M, and that other reversals are landing: an expected ~$17.6M pretax hedge loss to reclassify over the following twelve months (against a +$5.2M gain expected a year earlier), higher interest, and second-half-weighted capex.

End-market trajectory is mixed. Aero/HS net sales declined three consecutive years ($899.3M → $883.0M → $837.8M), with FY2025 Aero/HS conversion revenue down roughly 14% on ~16% lower shipments as plate customers destocked, this against an improving commercial aerospace build backdrop, which raises but does not resolve the question of whether some of it was share loss or a deliberate mix choice during the Phase VII ramp. Q2'26 Aero/HS conversion revenue per pound was +5%, the slowest of the disclosed end markets. Automotive Extrusions shipments fell 11%. Packaging and GE are carrying the growth.

**On the estimate-revision signal.** Current-year consensus rose 32.8% in 90 days to $13.52 and next-quarter estimates 14.6%. That magnitude of revision is normally a strong forward indicator, but the qualifying conditions are not met here. First, next-year consensus of $11.29 is 16.5% below the current year, so forward earnings growth is negative and no PEG-based conclusion is supportable. Second, the revision reflects results already banked in 1H26, roughly $64M of which was a timing gain management does not expect to repeat. Third, the out-year revision is only +8.6% over 90 days; consensus is marking up the windfall year, not the normalized year. I am therefore not treating this as a buy signal.

**Growth Prospects: 6.2**, slightly below the 6.5 of the prior review. The Packaging price/mix evidence is a real positive; the offsets are a completed capex cycle with no new announced capacity program, guided second-half contraction, three years of aerospace decline, and a consensus out-year that steps down.

## Competitive Landscape

Kaiser sits in the converter tier between primary producers and end customers, earning a spread on conversion rather than a price on metal. Three dynamics dominate.

**Tariff-driven metal inflation is a working-capital tax on converters.** The Midwest Premium tripling inflated net sales by roughly $350M with essentially no change in Conversion Revenue, while forcing a ~$207M combined inventory and receivables build and drawing the revolver. Converters experience a tariff regime as a one-time reported earnings windfall through lag plus a permanent increase in the capital required to run the same physical volume. Kaiser's response, selling 31% of net sales as receivables into customer-sponsored programs, is disclosed and arm's-length but adds a dependency.

**Packaging is where the competitive win is.** The 22% increase in Packaging conversion revenue per pound is the clearest evidence in the file that the Warrick roll-and-coat transformation and fourth coating line are converting into mix rather than volume alone. Coated end/tab and closure stock is qualified, specification-controlled work with meaningful switching friction, and beverage packaging demand is among the more defensive industrial end markets.

**Aerospace plate is the offsetting drag.** Aero/HS is the highest-value business at $2.23/lb of conversion revenue and roughly 31% of Conversion Revenue. Three straight years of declining sales and a ~16% shipment decline in 2025 ran counter to commercial build rates. Destocking in the plate distribution channel is the most likely explanation and the Q2'26 +5% per-pound recovery is consistent with it, but share loss or a deliberate Trentwood mix decision during the Phase VII ramp cannot be excluded from the disclosed data.

Customer concentration has not improved. The mix shifted toward Packaging and GE, but top-customer concentration was 16%/15% in both 2024 and 2025 and receivables concentration worsened, so mix shift should not be described as diversification. Supplier concentration tightened, with the largest supplier rising to 24% from 21%.

**Competitive Position: 6.2.** Improving in Packaging on hard metal-neutral evidence; flat-to-deteriorating in aerospace and automotive; concentration measures unimproved on both ends of the chain.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Metal Price Lag reversal — the dominant swing factor.** The company's risk factors state explicitly that decreases in primary aluminum prices create adverse lag. A $0.20/lb retracement implies $45-50M of unfavorable lag; a full retracement toward $0.19 implies roughly double that.

**2. Trade-policy dependence, with a specific new development.** A July 20, 2026 Section 232 proclamation reportedly creates onshoring incentives permitting companies that commit to new U.S. primary capacity to import at half the tariff rate (externally reported legal commentary, not a company disclosure). Admitting metal at 25% rather than 50% would increase effective U.S. supply and could compress the Midwest Premium over time. Counterweights: the relief is conditional on multi-year commitments and approvals, new smelting takes years, and the proclamation reaffirms the 50% baseline. Kaiser is a buyer of primary metal and would not qualify for onshoring credits; its exposure is to the premium's level and direction.

**3. Accounting-basis leverage.** WAC amplified reported FY2025 earnings by $141.6M of net income versus LIFO and will work in reverse if metal falls. Consensus models may not fully capture this.

**4. Hedge position turning.** The cash-flow-hedge component of AOCI was +$4.3M at 12/31/25 with a $5.2M pretax gain expected to reclassify within twelve months; per the Q2 2026 10-Q that position swung to a net loss with approximately $17.6M of pretax loss expected to reclassify over the following twelve months.

**5. Concentration.** Two customers at 16% and 15%, top five ~56%, one customer at 21% of receivables; top five suppliers at 82% of pounds. Annual pricing negotiations with consolidated can-maker and aerospace buyer bases.

**6. Free cash flow and dividend coverage.** Two consecutive years of negative FCF with a flat $51.3M dividend. Second-half 2026 carries $76-86M of capex against $155-186M of guided EBITDA.

**7. Labor and legacy.** Sixty-five percent unionized with Richmond expiring November 2026; core Trentwood/Newark agreements run to September 2030, which limits the tail. Environmental accrual $17.9M with disclosed potential excess of up to $14.1M across Trentwood PCB remediation and Newark consent-agreement work on 30-plus-year horizons. Pension/OPEB liability of $73.4M is modest for the legacy.

**8. Governance.** The United Steelworkers holds contractual board-nomination rights (approximately 40% of directors through 2030 on my reading of the risk factors), originating in the 2006 post-bankruptcy emergence. This plausibly reduces labor-disruption risk while constraining board refreshment and complicating any change-of-control scenario. Single-segment reporting with Net income as the CODM measure limits end-market profitability transparency. Only 452,688 shares remained available under the 2021 equity plan at year-end, implying a shareholder-approved refresh reasonably soon.

**Risk Factors: 4.2.** The risks are disclosed, but with the stock at 94% of its 52-week high and above the top analyst target, the market is not pricing the lag-reversal and accounting-symmetry channels with much margin.

IV compressed from 56.4% to 48.4% since July 22 while the price rose 10.6%, reflecting post-earnings uncertainty resolution. The term structure is in backwardation (48.4% at 13 days versus 44.8% at six months), and the near-term put/call skew of 6.7% is characterized as elevated put demand, with a ±7.4% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Against that, put/call volume of 0.22 and open-interest of 0.18 are decidedly call-heavy, and open-interest ratio ticked up only marginally from 0.15. The composite reading: positioning is bullish and lightly hedged, while near-dated pricing reflects event risk. Short interest of 5.8% of float with 2.4 days to cover is uncrowded, so there is no squeeze dynamic supporting the price and no confirming bearish crowd. Sell-side distribution has turned negative: one buy, one hold, one sell, one strong sell, with a hold converted to a sell in the past month.

**Management Quality: 4.8.** Capital allocation includes several sound decisions: the November 2025 refinancing removed all maturities before 2031 for roughly $6.3M per year, the Sherman exit was disciplined, and the Warrick/Trentwood capex is now demonstrably converting into Packaging price/mix. Offsetting these: insider activity is one-directional, with CEO Keith Harvey selling 47,001 shares at ~$175.09 (~$8.23M) on April 27, 2026 plus 3,031 more on April 29, alongside CFO Neal West (5,000 shares at ~$173.29) and General Counsel John Donnan (2,743 shares plus a gift), following February sales by Donnan (18,461) and West (5,000) at $121-125 — three C-suite officers selling in two windows shortly after the 10-K and proxy, with no observed open-market purchases. The clustering pattern is the signature of pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plans and post-vest diversification, and the CEO sold before the Q2 beat that lifted the stock, so this is suggestive of valuation discipline rather than evidence of adverse information. More substantive is that both incentive metrics, Adjusted EBITDA for short-term and Adjusted EBITDA margin on Conversion Revenue for long-term, are inflated by Metal Price Lag and by the LIFO-to-WAC change, so the FY2025 STI accrual of $23.8M was earned on a metric that includes a $93.0M timing gain and a basis change that turned a $(29.1)M LIFO loss into a $112.5M reported profit. Deloitte issued an unqualified opinion and the stated rationale (comparability, physical goods flow, close simplification) is reasonable; I am noting the coincidence of effects, not asserting motive. The compensation plan does bite: 98,825 performance shares were cancelled in 2025 for falling below maximum thresholds, and the CEO-to-NEO-average ratio of 3.4x is unusually compressed. Five-year indexed TSR of 140.26 still trails the company's own selected peer group at 154.36 despite the 2025-26 surge.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (25% probability, $225, +22.2%)

The 50% Section 232 baseline persists, keeping the Midwest Premium elevated near $0.59/lb and preventing lag reversal, possibly extending it. Conversion Revenue grows at the high end of the +10-15% guide as Trentwood Phase VII fills and the fourth Warrick coating line ramps, with Packaging conversion revenue per pound holding above $1.10 as a permanent mix upgrade. Aerospace destocking completes and Aero/HS shipments recover toward 2023 levels, restoring the highest-margin mix. Ex-lag EBITDA sustains above $450M, cash taxes stay near 20% of book on $123.9M of NOLs and bonus depreciation, capex falls back toward maintenance levels post-program, and free cash flow inflects to $250M+, an 8%+ yield that funds the dividend with room to add buybacks. On $470M of EBITDA at 10x, equity is worth roughly $225.

### Bear Case (30% probability, $115, -37.6%)

The onshoring provisions of the July 2026 proclamation, combined with normal supply response, compress the Midwest Premium back toward $0.30-0.40/lb. Insiders sold into the rally in two clustered windows without offsetting purchases, and the incentive metrics on which they are paid are inflated by the same two effects.

The score declines from 5.6 to 5.3, extending a trajectory from 6.1 in May, and after four consecutive HOLD ratings the rating moves to REDUCE. The change is not driven by fundamental deterioration, since the operating evidence improved this quarter, but by price moving further ahead of a normalized earnings base while the identified reversal moved closer. Trim into strength, keep the residual small, and revisit at $150 or on a Q3 print that demonstrates the ex-lag run rate without the metal windfall.
