## Executive Summary

Autodesk is a dominant design and engineering software franchise generating $2.4B in annual free cash flow on $7.2B in revenue, with 97% recurring revenue and $8.3B in remaining performance obligations providing multi-year visibility. The stock has declined 26% from its September 2025 high of $329 to $243, driven by macro concerns (tariff impacts on construction/manufacturing customers), a Citi downgrade, and broader tech multiple compression. During this drawdown, fundamentals have improved: Q4 FY2026 billings grew 33%, FCF expanded 54% YoY, and EPS estimates have risen 6.8% over 90 days.

The current valuation of ~21x trailing FCF and ~19x forward FCF represents a discount to Autodesk's historical trading range and to application software peers. The key analytical question is whether the AECO product family (50% of revenue, +22% growth) faces cyclical contraction from rising rates and tariff uncertainty, or whether secular digital adoption in construction sustains growth through a macro slowdown. At 21x FCF with 18% revenue growth and expanding margins, the risk/reward skews favorably even under moderately bearish assumptions.

## Company Overview

Autodesk provides design, engineering, and construction software across four product families: AECO ($3.58B, 50% of revenue), AutoCAD ($1.79B, 25%), Manufacturing ($1.38B, 19%), and Media & Entertainment ($332M, 5%). The company operates as a single reportable segment. Autodesk completed a multi-year transition to a subscription model and is now executing a second transformation: shifting from indirect distribution (resellers) to direct customer relationships. This channel shift distorts reported revenue growth, and management has stated it increases direct customer data access for upsell/cross-sell opportunities, though the accounting impact is expected to be broadly neutral to operating profit and free cash flow.

Core competitive advantages include deep workflow integration (architects, engineers, and contractors build projects around Autodesk tools), file format dominance (DWG standard), high switching costs (retraining entire teams on new software is prohibitive), and network effects (collaboration requires compatible tools). The company serves a large global subscriber base across its product families.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:** FY2026 revenue reached $7.21B (+18% YoY), accelerating from +12% in FY2025 and +10% in FY2024. However, a portion of reported growth reflects the new transaction model's accounting treatment (reseller commissions reclassified from contra-revenue to opex). Autodesk has disclosed that this reclassification "positively impacts calculated revenue growth" while being broadly neutral to operating profit and FCF, though the company has not quantified the precise revenue growth impact. Investors should focus on operating income and FCF growth as more reliable indicators of underlying momentum.

**Profitability:** Non-GAAP operating margin expanded to 38% (from 36% in FY2025). GAAP operating margin is 22%, suppressed by $788M in stock-based compensation and $216M in restructuring charges. The SBC load at ~11% of revenue is elevated relative to mature software peers.

**Free Cash Flow:** $2.41B in FY2026 (+54% YoY from $1.57B in FY2025), on only $43M in capex. FCF margin of 33.4% is exceptional and demonstrates the capital-light nature of the business. Full-year operating cash flow expanded 53% YoY to $2.45B. This is the most reliable financial metric given accounting complexities elsewhere.

**Balance Sheet:** $2.97B in cash/securities against $2.48B in long-term notes payable. The company is in a modest net cash position. The Altman Z-Score of 1.4 is structurally misleading — $4.69B in deferred revenue (a non-cash liability that converts to recognized revenue) inflates current liabilities. Autodesk has limited near-term refinancing risk with no current portion of long-term notes payable and ample liquidity ($1.5B undrawn revolver).

**Capital Return:** $1.4B in share repurchases in FY2026 with $7.48B in remaining authorization. No dividend.

**Forward Visibility:** Current RPO of $5.48B (+23% YoY) and total RPO of $8.30B (+20% YoY) provide strong revenue predictability over 12-24 months.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is driven by three vectors:

1. **AECO Digitization (50% of revenue, +22% growth):** Construction remains early in its digital adoption curve. Autodesk's platform connecting design (Revit), construction management (Construction Cloud/Forma), and operations creates a lifecycle value proposition that competitors offer only in fragments. Government infrastructure spending globally provides a secular tailwind, though near-term cyclical risk exists from higher interest rates constraining private construction.

2. **Manufacturing Platform Expansion (+16% growth):** Fusion 360 is gaining traction as a cloud-native manufacturing platform, driven by growth in MFG Collections, Fusion, and Inventor products.

3. **Pricing and Packaging (+14% AutoCAD growth):** AutoCAD and AutoCAD LT revenue growth reflects a combination of pricing, mix, new transaction model accounting effects, and volume, providing reliable growth on a sticky installed base.

**EPS Growth Path:** Consensus estimates project $12.42 in FY2027 EPS growing to $14.10 in FY2028 (+14% YoY). With continued margin expansion (target of 40%+ non-GAAP operating margin) and share buybacks reducing dilution, double-digit EPS growth appears sustainable over the next 2-3 years absent a construction recession.

**AI Strategy:** Autodesk is integrating generative AI into design workflows (Forma, Fusion) and has implemented Model Context Protocol servers for third-party AI integration. AI represents both opportunity (increased platform value, new use cases) and risk (potential disintermediation of manual design workflows by AI-native tools).

## Valuation Assessment

At $243, Autodesk trades at:
- 21x trailing FCF ($2.41B / 211M recent shares outstanding = $11.42 FCF/share)
- ~19.6x FY2027E EPS of $12.42 (non-GAAP)
- ~17.2x FY2028E EPS of $14.10
- ~7.2x trailing revenue
- ~47x trailing GAAP P/E (based on reported EPS of $5.23)

Versus peers (trailing P/E basis, as of mid-April 2026): PTC trades at approximately 20-21x (lower growth, though GAAP net margins may exceed Autodesk's depending on the metric used), WDAY at approximately 47-49x (comparable growth but deteriorating competitive position in HCM), FICO at approximately 33x. Autodesk's ~47x trailing GAAP P/E reflects FY2026 GAAP EPS depressed by $216M in one-time restructuring charges. On normalized GAAP earnings, the multiple is closer to 35-38x.

The 21x FCF multiple is below Autodesk's 5-year average of 28-35x FCF. This compression reflects: (a) macro uncertainty on construction spending, (b) channel transition complexity reducing analytical confidence, and (c) the April 2026 Citi downgrade. Analyst consensus targets as of mid-April 2026 imply meaningful upside from the current price, with even lower-end targets near current levels.

The valuation is not cheap on an absolute basis at ~47x GAAP P/E, but it is compressed relative to growth (PEG of ~2.7 on forward estimates), relative to application software peers, and relative to its own history. The 21x FCF provides adequate margin of safety for a business growing FCF at 15-20% annually.

## Competitive Landscape

The direct transaction model gives Autodesk first-party data on customer usage patterns, enabling better upsell targeting and reducing dependency on reseller relationships. The platform strategy (connecting design → build → operations) creates cross-selling opportunities that point-solution competitors cannot match.

**Moat sources:**
- Switching costs: Extremely high for enterprises (workflow retraining, file migration, certification requirements)
- Network effects: Collaboration requires file compatibility; DWG is the industry standard
- Installed base: Large global subscriber base creates recurring revenue regardless of new customer acquisition
- Platform breadth: No competitor offers equivalent coverage across AEC + manufacturing + media

**Competitive risks:** PTC competes effectively in manufacturing CAD/PLM. Procore targets construction management directly. Bentley Systems is strong in civil infrastructure. AI-native generative design tools could reduce the need for manual CAD work, though this risk is more relevant on a 5-10 year horizon than a 12-month investment timeframe.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Cyclical AECO Exposure (MODERATE-HIGH):** With AECO at 50% of revenue, Autodesk has meaningful exposure to construction activity. Higher interest rates constrain private construction starts. Tariff-driven cost increases could delay projects. However, government infrastructure spending provides a partial offset, and digital adoption in construction is sufficiently early that Autodesk can grow market penetration even if total construction spending stagnates.

**2. Accounting Complexity (MODERATE):** The new transaction model makes revenue growth comparisons unreliable for another 2-3 quarters. This creates analytical noise that may keep some institutional investors on the sideline. The SEC and USAO closed their matters related to the 2024 audit investigation in August 2025 with no action required, and no financial restatement was necessary, but pending shareholder lawsuits and the CFO replacement (Deborah Clifford transitioned to Chief Strategy Officer in May 2024; Janesh Moorjani appointed CFO effective December 2024) create residual uncertainty.

**3. Macro Slowdown (MODERATE):** A recession would slow enterprise software spending, though subscription renewals are more resilient than new license sales. Autodesk's 97% recurring revenue base provides downside protection relative to transactional software models.

**4. Insider Selling Pattern (LOW-MODERATE):** Executives sold shares at $305-$325 in mid-2025, and no insider has purchased shares during the 25% decline. This is not necessarily bearish — executives at subscription software companies rarely buy shares, and selling at higher prices is routine diversification.

**5. Tariff Impact on Customers (MODERATE):** Global customers in construction and manufacturing face cost inflation from tariffs. This could reduce project volumes or delay expansion decisions, indirectly affecting software spending.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($310-330, +28% to +36%):** Construction digitization continues at 20%+ growth rates regardless of macro headwinds. FCF expands to $2.8-3.0B in FY2027 as restructuring costs roll off and margins expand. Multiple re-rates to 25-28x FCF as accounting complexity resolves and investors regain confidence. AI features increase platform stickiness and enable price increases.

**Bear Case ($195-210, -14% to -20%):** Construction recession materializes as rates stay elevated. AECO growth decelerates to 8-10%. Tariff impacts compress manufacturing product family. Multiple compresses to 17-18x FCF on cyclical fears. Shareholder lawsuits result in settlement costs.

**Base Case ($290-310, +19% to +28%):** AECO growth moderates to 15-18% as rate sensitivity partially offsets secular adoption. FCF grows to $2.7B as margins expand. Multiple normalizes to 22-24x FCF as transition uncertainty diminishes. Stock re-rates toward consensus target over 12 months.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. AECO product family revenue growth decelerates below 10% YoY for two consecutive quarters (would signal cyclical contraction overwhelming secular adoption)
2. FCF declines below $2.0B on a trailing-twelve-month basis (would indicate margin compression or operational deterioration)
3. RPO growth turns negative or decelerates below 5% (would signal demand deterioration that hasn't yet appeared in revenue)

## Conclusion

Autodesk presents a compelling risk/reward at $243. The stock is down 26% from highs on concerns that are legitimate but appear over-discounted: AECO cyclicality is real but secular digitization provides a strong offset, and accounting complexity from the channel transition will resolve within 2-3 quarters.

The specific BUY catalyst is the convergence of: (1) estimate revisions up 6.8% over 90 days confirming fundamental momentum, (2) valuation compressed 26% below recent peaks on macro fears rather than fundamental miss, and (3) $5.48B in current RPO providing near-term revenue visibility that limits downside surprise probability. The primary risk is a construction recession that would slow AECO growth below 10%, which would warrant position exit.

Recommended position size: 2-3% of portfolio. The governance concerns (audit investigation history, insider selling, elevated CEO compensation vs. TSR underperformance) prevent maximum conviction sizing.
