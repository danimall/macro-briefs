## Executive Summary

Sabre reported Q2 2026 revenue of $712.0M (+3.6% year over year) and Adjusted EBITDA of $143.0M (+20.9%), with H1 Adjusted EBITDA of $301.7M (+21.1%) and GAAP operating income of $208.7M on a 14.2% margin versus 13.0% a year earlier. The EBITDA improvement is genuine and not a non-GAAP artifact. Two days before the print, Sabre amended its securitization facility to extend maturity to September 2029, upsize the commitment to $250M, and cut spreads (AR tranche from SOFR+375-400bp to SOFR+275bp; FILO from SOFR+800bp to SOFR+625bp); a secured lender reducing pricing is a mild credit-improvement signal, and it removes the last maturity inside three years. The remaining $50M of 2026 exchangeables was settled in cash on August 1. Interest coverage improved from 1.12x for FY2025 to 1.22x for H1 2026.

Against that, the core economic driver stalled. Air direct billable bookings grew 0.7% in Q2, down from roughly 5.8% in Q1, with management attributing the deceleration to Middle East conflict and fuel prices and guiding that pressure continues into Q3. Airline Technology, the higher-value, contract-based, passengers-boarded revenue line, declined 4.4% on license-fee and other-revenue losses. Incentive consideration paid to travel agencies rose $42M in H1 against an $83M revenue increase, and in Q2 alone rose $15M against a $25M increase in transaction-based revenue. Management's own disclosure attributes the increase partly to "an increase in **rates**," meaning agencies are extracting better per-booking economics. EBITDA growth is coming from the fixed-cost side — technology costs fell 1% in Q2 despite volume growth — and management's stated goal for that program is to hold costs "relatively flat," which stops contributing to growth once complete.

The capital structure dominates everything. Face debt of $4,484.4M against $676.0M of cash gives net debt of $3,808.4M and an enterprise value of roughly $4,664M, or 5.45x the $856M equity value. On a seasonally scaled FY2026 EBITDA estimate of approximately $606M (derived, not company-guided), that is 7.7x EV/EBITDA and 7.4x gross leverage. A ±10% swing in forward EBITDA at a constant multiple maps to roughly -55%/+55% in the share price. Consensus next-year EPS has been cut from $0.27 to $0.14 over 90 days (-49.4%), and the stock at $2.12 sits above the $1.99 analyst mean and $2.00 median. This is the fourth consecutive AVOID; the overall score is essentially flat at 3.0 versus 3.1 in June, with a modest financial-health upgrade offset by deteriorating growth and competitive trajectory.

## Company Overview

Sabre is a one-segment B2B travel-technology company following the July 3, 2025 divestiture of Hospitality Solutions to a TPG affiliate for $969M net proceeds ($821M pre-tax gain, $723M after tax; $822M applied to debt reduction). In the Q2 2026 10-Q the company renamed its two revenue lines — Distribution became **Marketplace** and IT Solutions became **Airline Technology** — stating explicitly that the underlying products and services are unchanged. Analysts comparing to prior commentary should map accordingly.

**Marketplace** (Q2 2026: $576.8M, 81% of revenue, +5.7%) is the global distribution system. Sabre earns transaction fees on direct billable bookings by travel agencies across air, lodging, car, rail and tour content, plus agency solutions including the Conferma virtual-payments business and media. Revenue is tied to transaction volume rather than ticket price, and the filings state it "is generally not contractually committed to recur annually."

**Airline Technology** (Q2 2026: $135.1M, 19%, -4.4%) sells SaaS and hosted airline reservation and retailing systems billed largely on passengers boarded, plus implementation fees, professional services and periodic license fees. Implementations typically take 12 to 18 months, making this the stickier of the two lines.

Corporate structure matters for equity holders. Sabre Corporation is a holding company with no material direct operations; Sabre GLBL Inc. is the operating subsidiary, and Sabre Financial Borrower, LLC and Sabre Securitization, LLC are financing entities. The securitization vehicle's assets "are not available to satisfy the obligations of Sabre Corporation or any of its affiliates," and its AR/FILO lenders hold senior priority on $336M of receivables. Structural subordination of the common equity is severe.

One framing note: the security is classified in market data as Consumer Discretionary / Hotels, Resorts & Cruise Lines. Sabre is a software and transaction-processing business with no hotel or cruise assets, and it just sold its hospitality software unit. Sabre made this same argument in its April 2026 proxy defense against ISS benchmarking it to "Six Flags and Cracker Barrel." The peer table supplied — median P/E 27.8x across PRSU, VAC, HGV, TNL and CHH — is not a usable comparable set for a levered GDS operator with negative EPS, and I place no weight on it.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 continuing-operations revenue was $2,771.0M, up 1.0% from the restated FY2024 figure of $2,744.8M. A standardized data pull comparing FY2025 to as-reported FY2024 revenue of $3,029.6M produces an apparent 8.5% decline; that is a divestiture artifact, not organic contraction. In 2026, H1 revenue of $1,472.3M grew 6.0%, but the composition is unfavorable: Q1 grew 8.3% (derived: $1,472.3M − $712.0M = $760.3M against $702.1M) and Q2 slowed to 3.6%.

**Volume.** Air direct billable bookings, the primary economic driver, grew 0.3% in FY2025, 3.2% in H1 2026 and only 0.7% in Q2 2026. Lodging/ground/sea grew 5.3% in Q2 and passengers boarded 1.6%. Management guides low-to-mid single-digit air bookings growth for FY2026 and expects Q3 pressure. The 10-Q attributes H1 growth to "implementation of certain commercial wins from recent years" — backlog conversion rather than market expansion.

**Margins and the incentive problem.** H1 revenue rose $83.0M while cost of revenue excluding technology rose $43.6M, of which incentive consideration accounted for $42M, "due to an increase in rates, transaction mix and volume." In Q2, incentive consideration rose $15M against a $25M increase in transaction-based revenue. Accrued subscriber incentives stood at $300.4M at 6/30/26 versus $289.1M at year-end, and amortization of capitalized upfront incentives rose 39% to $24.7M in H1 from $17.7M. The mechanism is straightforward: Sabre pays agencies to keep volume on its network, and when the required rate rises faster than revenue per booking, incremental gross margin compresses.

The offset is fixed-cost reduction. Technology costs fell 1% in Q2 to $171.1M and were flat in H1 despite volume growth, under an inflation offset program launched in Q4 2025 targeting flat technology and SG&A over two to three years. SG&A also carried a roughly $9M H1 benefit from reversing the Canadian digital services tax accrual after Canada repealed the DST on March 26, 2026.

Adjusted EBITDA: $289.5M (FY2023) → $447.2M (FY2024) → $500.2M (FY2025) → $143.0M in Q2 2026 (+20.9%) and $301.7M in H1 (+21.1%). Applying the 2025 H1/FY split of 49.8% implies FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA near $606M. That figure is my seasonal scaling, not company guidance — no FY2026 EBITDA guide appears in the filings, and Q2's revenue deceleration argues the H2 seasonal pattern may not repeat precisely.

**Interest expense consumes almost all of EBITDA.** FY2025: $500.2M EBITDA against $447.8M net interest, coverage 1.12x. H1 2026: $301.7M against $246.7M, coverage 1.22x. Q2: $143.0M against $123.8M, coverage 1.15x. H1 2026 left $55.0M of Adjusted EBITDA after interest to cover $47.6M of capex, $36.3M of severance disbursements and cash taxes. Coverage is improving, but the absolute cushion is thin.

Applying stated tranche rates to face balances at 6/30/26 with SOFR near 4.0% gives roughly $477M of annual interest on $4,484M face, a blended rate of about 10.6%. Cross-checking against H1 reported interest of $246.7M less $19.9M of non-cash discount and issuance amortization gives roughly $227M of cash interest, or about $454M annualized — consistent with a 10.1-10.6% blended rate. Every 100bp of blended-rate reduction is $44.8M of annual pre-tax cash flow, equal to $0.11 per share and roughly 5% of the current share price. Refinancing spread is a larger equity lever than booking volume.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 free cash flow was -$191.8M, including a $227M June 2025 payment of previously paid-in-kind and accrued interest in connection with retiring the 2028 term loan; excluding that catch-up, FY2025 FCF would have been roughly +$35M (an illustrative normalization, not a reported measure). H1 2026 operating cash flow was -$98.0M with $47.6M of capex, giving -$145.6M of FCF. Management guides FY2026 operating cash flow of approximately +$25M and FCF of approximately -$65M.

That requires H2 operating cash flow of +$123.0M and H2 FCF of +$80.6M — a half-over-half free-cash-flow swing of roughly $226M (derived). Identifiable reversing items support part of it: a $69.4M H1 receivables build that historically unwinds, a $44.2M decline in accrued compensation from annual bonus payment, and $36.3M of severance paid against a remaining accrual of only $22.2M. Against that, the coupon calendar is not evenly split across halves. I treat the FY2026 FCF guide as achievable but not de-risked.

A quality-of-cash-flow point deserves emphasis: the new 2026 short-term incentive program for non-executive employees pays in fully vested shares to be issued in early 2027 and is liability-classified. Q2 stock compensation jumped to $20.6M from $11.3M, with $13M of H1's $26.2M attributable to this program. Some of the improvement in 2026 operating cash flow is a substitution of 2027 share issuance for 2026 cash.

**Balance sheet.** Cash of $676.0M at 6/30/26 versus $791.6M at year-end; restricted cash fell from $118.6M to $21.0M, fully explained by the $98M held in trust to redeem the 8.625% June 2027 notes, completed March 1, 2026. Total assets $4,372.4M, of which goodwill and intangibles are $2,652.3M (61%). Total stockholders' deficit -$1,055.2M, widened $18.7M in H1. The improvement from -$1,604.7M at 12/31/24 to -$1,036.5M at 12/31/25 came entirely from the $723M after-tax Hospitality gain, not operating profitability.

**The maturity schedule is the decisive table.** $4,234.6M of $4,484.4M face debt — 94% — matures in 2029-30: $2,289.8M in 2029 (two term-loan tranches, $1.0B of 11.125% June 2029 notes, $445.7M of 10.75% November 2029 notes), plus a $150M holder put on the 2031 exchangeables on May 15, 2029, and $1,794.8M in 2030. Near-term maturities are now trivial: $50M settled in cash on August 1, and $199.8M of securitization debt extended to September 2029 (effective September 30, 2026, subject to conditions precedent). Sabre bought roughly three years of runway through 2024-26 liability management, at the cost of higher coupons and a single concentrated refinancing decision.

Credit markets have repriced that runway. The 11.125% July 2030 notes were marked at 84.1 (fair value $1,114.4M on $1,325.0M) at 12/31/25 and 96.1 at 6/30/26, a roughly 12-point rally in six months. The June 2029 notes trade near 106, March 2030 notes near 109, and the 7.00% 2031 exchangeables near 130 (largely equity-option value on the $2.24 conversion right). Term loans at 91-95 reflect floating-rate repricing. Secured paper at or above par is not the pricing of an imminent restructuring; it is also consistent with strong collateral coverage in a scenario where the equity is impaired, so it should not be read as a clean endorsement of enterprise value.

## Growth Analysis

There are two growth stories running in opposite directions, and the reconciliation determines the equity outcome.

The EBITDA story is real: +54.5% in FY2024, +11.9% in FY2025, +21.1% in H1 2026, with GAAP operating margin expanding 120bp year over year in H1. Interest coverage improved on this measure. Cost discipline is delivering.

Revenue is deteriorating. Air bookings at +0.7% in Q2 against +5.8% in Q1 is a sharp deceleration in the single metric that determines transaction-fee revenue. Airline Technology at -4.4% is contraction in the higher-quality line. Sequential revenue growth halved from 8.3% to 3.6%. The Middle East conflict and elevated fuel prices are sector conditions rather than Sabre execution failures, but Sabre absorbs sector softness with 1.15x coverage and no cushion.

The durability question is arithmetic. EBITDA growth is being generated by holding technology and SG&A flat against modest revenue growth while incentive consideration absorbs roughly 60% of incremental transaction revenue. Once the cost program reaches its "relatively flat" target — management's own horizon is two to three years from Q4 2025 — the fixed-cost contribution stops. From that point, EBITDA growth requires either volume acceleration or a reversal in incentive rates, and neither is currently evident.

Consensus reflects this. Next-year EPS has been cut from $0.27 to $0.14 over 90 days (-49.4%), current-year from $0.12 to approximately breakeven (-102.8%), current-quarter from $0.00 to -$0.07. The earnings surprise record is 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters, with the most recent quarter reported below non-GAAP consensus. Rising estimates are a strong forward signal; falling estimates of this magnitude, occurring while the share price rose 12.8% since the June snapshot, is the inverse configuration and a genuine warning.

One point of fairness on the reported "miss": Adjusted Net Loss per share widened to -$0.17 from -$0.02, but that reconciliation is dominated by a $149.9M reversal in the "tax impact of adjustments" line (from +$94.2M to -$55.7M) and the absence of the prior year's $85.2M extinguishment loss, while Adjusted EBITDA improved $24.7M. The adjusted-EPS line for this company is close to uninformative about operating performance. The market's positive reaction to the print reflected the EBITDA line, and that read was reasonable.

## Valuation Assessment

Derived enterprise value: $856M equity (403.6M shares × $2.12) plus $3,808.4M net debt at face = **$4,664M**.

Against the derived FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA of ~$606M:
- EV/Adjusted EBITDA ≈ **7.7x**
- Gross debt/EBITDA ≈ **7.4x**; net ≈ **6.3x**

Next-year consensus EPS of $0.14 puts the shares at 15.1x, but that estimate has been cut 49% in 90 days and is produced by three analysts on a business whose GAAP result is dominated by valuation-allowance-driven tax noise. I do not treat it as a reliable anchor.

The structurally decisive feature is that enterprise value is **5.45x** equity value. Holding the 7.7x multiple constant:

| FY2027 Adj. EBITDA | EV at 7.7x | Less net debt $3,808M | Implied per share |
|---|---|---|---|
| $545M (−10%) | $4,197M | $389M | **$0.96** (−55%) |
| $606M (base) | $4,666M | $858M | **$2.13** (+0%) |
| $667M (+10%) | $5,136M | $1,328M | **$3.29** (+55%) |

The equity is a levered call on forward EBITDA and on the 2029 refinancing spread. Levered equities can be excellent risk/reward when the option is struck favorably. The question is whether $2.12 is a favorable strike. Three observations say no.

First, 7.7x EV/EBITDA is not a distressed multiple for a business growing revenue ~1-4% with negative free cash flow, contracting high-value revenue, and a ~10.5% cost of debt. Amadeus-class competitors funded at investment-grade rates command higher multiples on better organic growth; Sabre's discount to them is explained by its leverage rather than mispriced by it.

Second, the shares trade **above** the analyst mean ($1.99) and median ($2.00), with a high of $2.30 and a low of $1.75, and the coverage split is 1 buy / 5 holds. The principle that analyst targets act as floors rather than ceilings applies to stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples; here estimates are falling hard, so the targets carry their normal meaning and the stock is already through them.

Third, the equity has risen from approximately $1.16 in late February to $2.12, roughly in lockstep with the 12-point rally in the July 2030 notes. That was the correct trade, and much of the credit-normalization optionality has now been monetized. The 2031 exchangeables at ~$2.24 conversion (with a 130%-of-strike trigger near $2.91) also cap the equity's benefit from further appreciation: 67.08M maximum exchange shares equal 16.6% of current shares outstanding and 14.3% ownership dilution post-issuance, though settlement is at Sabre's election in cash, shares or a combination, and no exchange condition was met at 6/30/26.

I score valuation 3.0. There is no identifiable catalyst that makes the market wrong at this price, and the compressed EV/EBITDA multiple reflects a real structural constraint rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Three dynamics emerge from the filings.

**Supplier and agency squeeze from both sides.** A dedicated risk factor describes airlines negotiating lower fees, withholding content for direct channels, and using multiple distribution channels. On the other side, incentive consideration rose partly on "an increase in rates." Sabre pays more to agencies while defending fee levels against airlines. The bull reading — that Sabre is paying up to win profitable share, consistent with the 10-Q's attribution of H1 growth to prior commercial wins — is plausible, and the disclosed data (no transaction-revenue-per-booking detail) does not permit distinguishing the two. Either way, gross-margin expansion is absent.

**Reinvestment capacity is constrained by the balance sheet, and management says so.** The risk factors state Sabre may be "required to make changes to our technology platforms or increase our investment in technology, increase marketing, adjust prices or business models" and then cross-reference the indebtedness risk factor in the same passage. NDC build-out is a required investment to remain the aggregation layer rather than be bypassed; the filings disclose no NDC volume penetration. A competitor funded at investment-grade rates faces a different reinvestment calculus than one paying ~10.5% blended.

**AI positioning is partly licensed-in.** Sabre markets itself as "AI-native" with "one of the world's largest travel data clouds" and, in proxy defense materials, as "powering the agentic revolution in travel." The risk factors are more measured: Sabre "utilize[s] various generative AI solutions from third-party providers as part of some of our software products," and competitors "may incorporate AI into their products more quickly or more successfully." No filing quantifies AI-attributable revenue. Whether agentic booking agents make a normalized aggregation layer more valuable or make direct API access cheap enough to bypass it is genuinely open and is the core long-term durability issue.

The Airline Technology decline of 4.4% is the concrete evidence available on trajectory, and it points the wrong way. I score competitive position 3.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing concentration.** $2,289.8M due in 2029 plus a $150M holder put, and $1,794.8M in 2030, at coupons of 10.75-11.125% fixed and SOFR+600-625bp floating. The filings note Sabre's ability to incur additional *secured* debt is "significantly limited," so incremental borrowing "may be unsecured," and state three separate times that refinancing at current rates "may negatively impact our interest expense **or result in higher stock dilution**." Management is signalling equity or equity-linked issuance as a live option.

**Volume sensitivity at 1.15x coverage.** Air bookings at +0.7% with Q3 pressure guided leaves little tolerance for a decline. Revenue "is generally not contractually committed to recur annually."

**Goodwill.** $2,652.3M of goodwill and intangibles against $4,372.4M of total assets and -$1,055.2M of equity. No triggering event was identified in H1 and no impairment was recorded, but the disclosed impairment inputs — booking volumes, discount rates, long-term growth rates — are the variables currently deteriorating. An impairment would be non-cash and would not breach the incurrence-based covenants identified, but it would deepen the deficit and affect the pricing of any equity raise.

**Dilution channel.** Issued shares rose from 427.4M at 12/31/25 to 439.3M at 6/30/26 (+2.8% in six months), 11.5M of it in Q2 from award settlements. May 2026 executive grants appear to be fixed-dollar awards struck near $1.65, mechanically issuing more shares at a depressed price. The 2026 employee incentive program settles in shares in early 2027. Offsetting: ISS's own calculation puts Sabre's three-year burn rate at 4.95% versus a 6.77% software benchmark, so the absolute pace is not egregious for a technology issuer.

**Credit-loss build.** Allowance rose from $18.0M to $21.1M with a $5.2M H1 provision versus $1.1M a year earlier, against only $2.0M of write-offs. That may signal travel-agency or airline customer stress; it may also be mechanical reserve growth on receivables that expanded from $311.9M to $378.1M. Worth monitoring.

**Tax and regulatory.** Indian income tax litigation with ~$20M of exposure and no provision recorded; an IRS examination of the FY2023 federal return opened in June 2026 (new since the 10-K); OECD Pillar Two phasing in; a UK OFSI sanctions penalty accrued in Q4 2025 following a January 2026 notice of intent, amount not disclosed in the sections reviewed.

**Governance uncertainty.** Constellation Software Inc. filed as a 10% owner, purchasing 10,634,702 shares at $1.1605 on 2026-02-27 and holding 50,157,523 shares (12.4% of 403.6M) — established filing fact. A March 2-5, 2026 8-K cluster reported Items 1.01, 1.02, 3.03, 5.02, 5.03 and 7.01; director Damian McKay joined March 19; and the Q2 10-Q introduces "Stockholder matter costs" of $3.5M for "external legal and professional advisory fees associated with a strategic governance agreement." The content of that agreement, McKay's affiliation, and whether Item 3.03 reflects a cooperation agreement, a stockholder rights plan, or a Section 382 NOL protection plan are **unverified** (8-K text inaccessible). Those alternatives carry opposite implications — accommodating an accumulation versus limiting one — and I therefore treat the Constellation stake and purchase price as facts and everything about intent and agreement terms as an open gap.

**Delisting language.** A risk factor explicitly contemplates Nasdaq delisting and OTC trading. At $2.12 the shares are comfortably above the $1.00 minimum bid, but the inclusion is notable.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 133.0% against 94.1% one-year realized — a 38.8-point spread at the 93rd percentile of the current chain, with backwardation (133.0% at one month versus 93.3% at six months) indicating the market prices imminent stress. The nearest expiry (August 21, 14 days) implies a ±21.2% move. The nearest tenor shows put/call skew of 8.2%, characterized as elevated put demand, while the three- and six-month tenors show -6.3% and -5.3%. Put/call open-interest ratio is 0.60, up from 0.02 at the June snapshot — a large increase in absolute terms but still call-tilted, so aggregate open positioning does not corroborate a directional bearish view. Short interest is 11.0% of float at 5.9 days to cover.

The options market is pricing high near-term event risk without a settled direction, and near-term protection is expensive. For an equity that is structurally a levered option on EBITDA, paying 133% implied volatility on either side is unattractive. This confirms the AVOID and specifically argues against expressing the bearish view through purchased puts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — approximately $3.29 (+55%)

Adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $667M in FY2027 (about 10% above my $606M FY2026 base) as the inflation offset program completes, Middle East-driven booking pressure reverses, and Airline Technology returns to growth on backlog conversion. At a constant 7.7x EV/EBITDA, equity value rises to $1,328M or $3.29 per share. Supporting elements: interest coverage rises toward 1.4x; the securitization amendment demonstrates secured lenders will transact at *tighter* spreads, which extrapolates favorably to the 2029 refinancing; credit spreads compress further from the current 96-109 range, and each 100bp of blended-rate reduction adds $0.11 per share; Constellation Software's 12.4% position, accumulated at prices near $1.16, creates strategic or take-private optionality absent from most distressed-leverage equities. In this path the 2031 exchangeables convert above ~$2.91 and existing holders take 14.3% ownership dilution — an acceptable cost against a 55% gain.

### Bear Case — approximately $0.96 (−55%)

Air bookings stay near flat or decline as fuel costs persist, FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA lands near $545M, and the same 7.7x multiple leaves $389M of equity value, or $0.96 per share. Multiple compression on refinancing doubt would take it lower. Supporting elements: incentive rates continue rising and absorb the majority of incremental transaction revenue, capping gross margin; the fixed-cost program's contribution ends by 2027 as designed; interest coverage falls back toward 1.1x; the FY2026 FCF guide is missed because the required $226M half-over-half swing does not fully materialize against a heavy H2 coupon calendar; a goodwill impairment against $2,652M of intangibles deepens the deficit; and Sabre pre-funds part of the 2029 wall through equity or equity-linked issuance at a depressed price, as the 10-Q's repeated "higher stock dilution" language contemplates.

### Base Case — approximately $1.90 (−10%)

FY2026 EBITDA near $606M, FY2027 roughly flat as the cost program's contribution fades and volume growth stays low-single-digit, with the multiple drifting to ~7.5x on unresolved refinancing questions. That implies equity value near $770M, or roughly $1.90 per share — consistent with the $1.99 analyst mean and $2.00 median. The most likely 12-month outcome is a modest decline from the current price, not a collapse.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** No dated catalyst inside six months carries the required conviction. The nearest candidates are the Q3 2026 report (likely late October/early November), where management has pre-guided air-bookings pressure, and the FY2026 FCF guide, which requires a $226M half-over-half free-cash-flow swing. Both are plausible disappointments, but management has already lowered the bar on bookings, and a substantial share of the H2 cash swing is mechanically supported by identifiable H1 reversals ($69.4M receivables build, $44.2M bonus payment, $36.3M severance disbursement). The decisive event — the 2029 refinancing — is roughly three years out.

**Timing window.** The binding constraint resolves in 2028-29. A short held for that is paying carry for years against a security whose near-term direction is driven by credit-spread normalization that has been running in the wrong direction for the bear since February.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 11.0% of float short, 5.9 days to cover, 30.77M shares. Elevated but not extreme. The larger issue is structural: with enterprise value at 5.45x equity value, a 1% move in EV produces a 5.4% move in the share price. The stock rose approximately 83% from $1.16 in February to $2.12 and gained on the Q2 print. Constellation Software's 12.4% stake, plus an unverified "strategic governance agreement," creates real headline and take-private gap risk.

**Borrow and options signal.** Near-term implied volatility of 133.0% versus 94.1% realized makes put protection expensive, and put/call open interest at 0.60 is call-tilted, so options positioning does not confirm the bearish thesis. Three- and six-month skew is negative. This is the configuration in which shorts pay the most and get the least confirmation.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear case $0.96 (−55%) against bull case $3.29 (+55%) — approximately 1:1 before borrow cost, and closer to unfavorable after it. A levered equity option cuts symmetrically.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is an unattractively levered business with decelerating volume, not an actively collapsing enterprise with a dated trigger. Secured notes trade at or above par, near-term maturities have been cleared, coverage is improving, and a disciplined strategic buyer owns 12.4%. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID at $2.12.**

**Re-entry criteria** — any two of the following would move me to HOLD; the first plus either of the next two would justify a BUY:

1. **Air direct billable bookings growth returns to 3%+ for two consecutive quarters** with Airline Technology revenue back to flat or better. This is the single most important reversal, because it is what would make the EBITDA trajectory independent of a finite cost program.

## Conclusion

This is the fourth consecutive AVOID, and the overall score is essentially unchanged at 3.0 versus 3.1 in June. The composition shifted. Financial health improves modestly from 2.0 to 2.5: the securitization amendment extended the last near-term maturity to September 2029 at spreads 100-175bp *tighter*, the 2026 exchangeable was settled in cash, interest coverage rose to 1.22x for H1, and secured notes rallied roughly 12 points in six months. Those are real, verifiable improvements in the survival probability that a levered equity should be credited for.

They are offset by deterioration in the two dimensions that determine whether the equity is worth owning rather than merely surviving. Air direct billable bookings — the metric that drives 81% of revenue — decelerated to 0.7% in Q2, with management guiding continued Q3 pressure. Airline Technology, the higher-quality contract-based line, contracted. Roughly 60% of incremental Q2 transaction revenue went back to travel agencies as incentive consideration, partly on rising rates. EBITDA growth is being produced by a fixed-cost program with a stated flat-cost endpoint, and next-year consensus EPS has been cut 49% in 90 days while the share price rose.

At $2.12 the shares trade above the $1.99 consensus mean and $2.00 median, at 7.7x EV/EBITDA on a derived FY2026 estimate, with enterprise value at 5.45x equity value. The scenario band is approximately $0.96 to $3.29 around a base case of $1.90 — symmetric magnitude, unfavorable skew from the current price. Near-term implied volatility of 133% against 94% realized makes option expression expensive in either direction. Constellation Software's 12.4% position, accumulated near $1.16, is the most interesting fact in the file and a genuine reason not to be short; it is not a reason to buy at 83% above that buyer's disclosed entry, particularly while the terms of the associated governance arrangement remain unverified.

The credit is being priced as stressed-but-performing with adequate runway. The equity is being priced as though the 2029 refinancing is largely solved. The gap between those two views is where the risk sits.
