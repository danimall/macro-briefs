**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer and a leading North American nitrogen manufacturer. The stock has risen roughly 27% over the past year and traded as high as $142 in March 2026, driven almost entirely by a geopolitical supply shock: the closure of the Strait of Hormuz curtailed Middle East nitrogen exports (25–30% of traded ammonia, 35–40% of traded urea) and Qatari LNG (~18% of global LNG), tightening global nitrogen markets and lifting CF's realized prices. Q1 2026 net sales reached $1,986M (+19%), gross margin 37.6%, operating earnings $863M (+90%), and diluted EPS $3.98 versus $1.85 a year earlier. Consensus estimates have been revised upward rapidly: current-quarter EPS estimates are up 173.6% over 90 days, current-year up 115.2%.

The earnings base rests on a reversible event. Approximately $220M of Q1 pre-tax profit was non-recurring (a $170M Orica litigation settlement, $25M of insurance recovery, $24M of 45Q tax credits), so the headline $3.98 overstates run-rate earnings. The pricing tailwind depends on the supply shock persisting, and the conflict continues. Layered on top are an active multi-defendant antitrust class action (JPML consolidation heard May 28, 2026), a White House executive order directing DOJ/FTC to probe fertilizer price-fixing, and a leadership vacuum: a CEO transition (Will to Bohn) and no permanent CFO (Cameron separated in January, Hoker serving interim) during a $3.7B greenfield build.

We rate CF a HOLD. The forward multiple is undemanding (about 10.4x next-year consensus EPS of $11.16 versus a much higher peer-group median), the company is repurchasing shares aggressively, and the supply shock has held while Qatar's LNG capacity takes years to restore. That supports holding rather than selling at a single-digit-to-low-double-digit multiple. We do not rate it a buy because the earnings are cyclical-peak and geopolitically contingent, the catalyst is reversible, and the litigation and political overhangs are asymmetric. The classification is a tactical growth hold with a clean exit if Hormuz reopens.

## Company Overview

CF produces anhydrous ammonia via the Haber-Bosch process using natural gas as both feedstock and fuel, then upgrades it into granular urea, urea ammonium nitrate (UAN), ammonium nitrate (AN), and industrial products. Q1 2026 net sales split across Ammonia ($627M), Granular Urea ($590M), UAN ($583M), AN ($58M), and Other ($128M).

The asset base comprises six U.S. complexes (including Donaldsonville, the world's largest ammonia complex), two Canadian complexes (including Medicine Hat, Canada's largest), a UK complex at Billingham, a 50% interest in Point Lisas (Trinidad), and a 40% interest in the Blue Point JV. The structural advantage is cost-advantaged North American natural gas feedstock versus higher-cost producers reliant on imported LNG or Middle East gas, paired with an extensive North American distribution and logistics network. CF has also moved early on low-carbon ammonia: carbon capture at Donaldsonville came online in July 2025, generating ~1.9M tons/year of low-carbon capacity qualifying for 45Q credits.

The ownership structure carries meaningful noncontrolling interests: CHS Inc. owns ~10% of CF Industries Nitrogen, and JERA (35%) and Mitsui (25%) co-own the Blue Point JV. These translate into roughly $61M/quarter of earnings attributable to minorities and $200M+ in semi-annual distributions out of the consolidated entity.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 net sales rose 19% to $1,986M on an average selling price of $424/ton (+28%), partially offset by 6% lower volume. Gross margin expanded 320bps to 37.6% ($746M, +30%). Operating earnings rose 90% to $863M, and net earnings to common rose 97% to $615M. Adjusted EBITDA was $983M.

Segment dynamics confirm the pricing story. UAN was the standout, with gross margin up 76% to $250M as margin percentage rose from 30% to 43%. Granular urea margin reached 43.2%. AN swung to an $11M loss (–19% margin) as the idled Yazoo City plant absorbed unabsorbed fixed costs. Natural gas cost rose to $4.57/MMBtu (+24%), a roughly $76M margin headwind that pricing gains more than offset.

The quality of the headline EPS deserves emphasis. Roughly $220M of Q1 pre-tax profit was non-operating or non-recurring (the Orica settlement received April 30, 2026, $25M of Yazoo City insurance recovery, and 45Q credits). The underlying pricing-driven margin expansion is real, but the $3.98 EPS should not be annualized. Higher selling price widened gross margin and lifted operating earnings, but a meaningful slice of the bottom line came from one-time items.

The balance sheet is adequate for a commodity cyclical. Cash stands at $2.04B, though $254M is restricted within the Blue Point VIE and discretionary capital is further constrained by NCI distributions and Canadian tax deposits. Long-term debt is $3.25B in principal across four senior notes maturing 2034–2044 at roughly 5% coupons, with no revolver borrowings and a $750M undrawn revolver. Debt/equity is elevated (~2.9), placing the Altman Z-score in the grey zone, but the maturity ladder is distant and coupons are low. Q1 operating cash flow was $496M (down from $586M on working-capital build and a ~$95M Canadian tax deposit). FY2026 capex is guided to ~$1.3B (about $550M existing ops, ~$240M CF share of Blue Point, ~$150M Common Facilities), so free cash flow is being absorbed by growth investment.

Across the cycle, net income has ranged from a ~$3.9B peak in the 2022 gas-spike super-cycle down to ~$1.5B in 2024 and ~$1.8B in 2025. The current quarter reflects another spike, not a new baseline.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth profile is exceptional on paper. Consensus revisions are among the steepest we track: current-quarter EPS up 48.5% in 30 days and 173.6% in 90 days; next-quarter up 92.0% and 286.1%; current-year up 24.0% and 115.2%; next-year up 3.5% and 66.0%. Revenue grew 19% year over year. In isolation, this combination of rising estimates and accelerating revenue is a powerful forward signal.

The durability is the problem. The revisions are not the product of a structural earnings recovery from a trough; they are the product of a commodity at a geopolitically driven peak. The forward signal that carries the most weight when a stock is recovering from depressed earnings carries the opposite weight here, because the market is extrapolating peak pricing.

The genuine long-term growth vector is low-carbon ammonia, supported by Blue Point offtake commitments from JERA and Mitsui and Japanese METI low-carbon certifications. This is real optionality for European CBAM-exposed customers and new applications (power, marine fuel, steel), but it is multi-year and capital-intensive, with Blue Point production not until 2029. It does not support the next twelve months of earnings.

We weight the strong near-term revisions but discount heavily for the non-recurring component and the geopolitical contingency, landing on a Growth score that reflects real momentum with low durability.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at roughly 10.5x trailing and forward earnings on the headline figures, against a peer-group median P/E of 26.3x in the supplied Materials comparables (Amcor, International Paper, Royal Gold, Reliance, Packaging Corp). On its face the stock looks deeply discounted. The peer set is a poor comparison — packaging, paper, and gold royalty businesses are not nitrogen producers — but even against a cleaner nitrogen comp set, CF screens reasonably.

The more relevant lens is normalized earnings. The market is not pricing CF at 10.5x because it believes peak earnings will persist; it is pricing the reversion. On next-year consensus EPS of $11.16, the multiple is about 10.4x, and that estimate already incorporates partial normalization. On 2024–2025 net income of $1.5–1.8B (roughly $10–12 normalized EPS at the current share count), the stock sits around 10–12x mid-cycle earnings. For a quality nitrogen producer with a durable cost advantage buying back ~8% of shares annually, that is fair to modestly cheap, but it is not a screaming mispricing. A low P/E at the top of a commodity cycle is a warning as often as it is a bargain, because the earnings denominator is elevated.

The asymmetry is two-sided. If pricing holds, the multiple and the buyback compound value toward the $130 analyst median and potentially the $150 high. If Hormuz reopens, next-year estimates get cut and the stock reverts toward the $94 analyst low. The current price embeds genuine uncertainty about which path resolves, which is why we do not score valuation as a clear positive.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive position is the strongest part of the story and is stable-to-improving. In a global commodity market where delivered prices are set at the margin by the highest-cost producer, CF's structural edge is cheap, stable North American natural gas versus producers dependent on imported LNG (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh) or Middle East gas — precisely the producers now curtailed. CF is therefore a primary beneficiary of the supply shock rather than a victim of it. Scale leadership in ammonia, the North American distribution network, and the early low-carbon position widen the moat.

Key peers named in filings and news include Nutrien, Mosaic, Yara, Koch, and Canpotex. Nitrogen-focused players (CF, Nutrien) benefit most from gas-driven tightness; phosphate/potash-exposed Mosaic faces relative headwinds. The low-carbon ammonia push, validated by JERA/Mitsui offtake and Japanese certifications, differentiates CF for CBAM-exposed and new-application demand, though that payoff is years out.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is that the entire earnings surge rests on a reversible geopolitical accident. Management states the supply constraints "will persist in the near-term," and the multi-year nature of Qatar's LNG restoration supports tightness for now. But any physical reopening of Hormuz would compress nitrogen prices and margins rapidly, and the earnings power that justifies the current price would erode within one to two quarters.

Three additional risks are specific, dated, and asymmetric, and are not fully reflected at current levels:

1. **Antitrust and political pressure.** A putative direct-purchaser class sued CF, Mosaic, Nutrien, Koch, Yara, and Canpotex on March 7, 2026 alleging a Sherman Act conspiracy, seeking treble/punitive damages, with a JPML consolidation hearing on May 28, 2026. Separately, a December 6, 2025 executive order created DOJ/FTC fertilizer price-fixing task forces, and the administration has signaled intent to lower fertilizer costs for farmers. The same high prices boosting earnings invite scrutiny that could cap them.
2. **Leadership vacuum.** CF is simultaneously executing a CEO succession (Will to Bohn) and operating without a permanent CFO (Cameron separated January 2026, Hoker interim), amid a $3.7B greenfield build. A dense run of officer-change filings since early 2025 suggests broader reshuffling during a critical capital-deployment phase.
3. **Operational and execution items.** Yazoo City AN production will not resume until late Q4 2026 at the earliest. Blue Point carries schedule and budget risk, with about a third of cost in imported materials exposed to steel/aluminum tariffs. Ammonia, CF's base export product, is not exempt from the new tariff regime even though most fertilizers are.

Natural gas cost volatility (gas is ~39% of production cost) and NCI cash leakage round out the risk set.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 85.8% against 41.5% historical volatility — a 44.3-point spread, in the 84th percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in backwardation (85.8% near-term versus 47.3% at six months). IV jumped from 74.0% to 85.8% over two sessions while the price rose only 1.2%. Put/call skew is elevated at 16.5%, signaling demand for downside protection. The concentration of near-term vol around the front expiry is consistent with event risk tied to the late-May antitrust consolidation hearing and the broader Hormuz de-escalation watch. The P/C open-interest ratio fell to 0.68, a partially offsetting signal, but the dominant read is that the market is paying up for protection around a known near-term catalyst. This confirms the asymmetric, event-driven risk profile that keeps us at HOLD rather than a more constructive stance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The supply shock proves durable. Hormuz-related curtailments persist, Qatar's LNG restoration keeps global nitrogen tight, and CF's North American cost advantage continues to capture an outsized pricing premium through the 2026 application season. Underlying pricing-driven margin expansion (real even after stripping the one-time items) sustains EBITDA well above mid-cycle. The $2B buyback (through 2029) compounds per-share value on a reduced share count, and the cheap forward multiple re-rates toward the analyst median and high. Low-carbon ammonia optionality adds long-dated upside. Stock reaches $150 (the analyst high), roughly 29% above current.

### Bear Case

Hormuz physically reopens and nitrogen prices revert toward mid-cycle. Next-year consensus EPS, already at $11.16, gets cut as the windfall fades. The antitrust litigation advances and the political push to cap fertilizer prices gains traction, compressing both the multiple and the pricing. The leadership vacuum complicates execution on Blue Point. Stock reverts toward the $94 analyst low, roughly 19% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)**

The specific 12-month thesis: CF generates substantial free cash flow and is reducing its share count ~8% annually under a fresh $2B authorization. The supply shock has held, and Qatar's LNG restoration takes years, supporting elevated pricing through the 2026 application season. Even excluding the non-recurring Q1 items, the underlying pricing-driven margin expansion is real, and the structural North American cost advantage is durable regardless of the catalyst. That combination supports holding at a single-digit-to-low-double-digit multiple rather than selling. We stop short of a buy because the earnings base is cyclical-peak and geopolitically contingent, and the litigation and leadership overhangs are active.

**Scenario reference prices** (anchored to the Investment Thesis): base case ~$130 (analyst median, ~12% upside) on sustained-but-moderating pricing plus buyback support; bull case ~$150 (~29% upside); bear case ~$94 (~19% downside).

**Upgrade triggers** (toward a more constructive stance):
- A permanent, credible CFO is appointed and management churn stabilizes, removing the governance overhang during the Blue Point build.
- Q2/Q3 2026 results demonstrate that run-rate margins (excluding one-time items) are holding even as Hormuz tensions ease, indicating the pricing strength is broader than the geopolitical premium.
- The antitrust consolidation resolves favorably or is narrowed materially, removing a downside catalyst.

**Downgrade triggers** (toward AVOID):
- Physical, sustained reopening of Hormuz (multiple weeks of uninterrupted commercial transit) that begins compressing nitrogen prices, with next-year consensus EPS revised down by more than 15%.
- The antitrust class action survives a motion to dismiss with a credible treble-damages exposure, or the DOJ/FTC probe escalates into formal pricing remedies.
- Average selling price declines for two consecutive quarters alongside a renewed slide in segment gross margins, signaling the supply premium has unwound.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August), which will reveal how much of the Q1 margin strength is run-rate versus windfall, plus any update on the CFO search, Yazoo City restart, and antitrust proceedings. The Hormuz situation should be monitored continuously given its outsized weight on the thesis.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a high-quality nitrogen producer with a genuine structural cost advantage, trading at an undemanding multiple while returning capital aggressively. Those attributes argue against selling. But the trailing results and the steep estimate revisions are built on a reversible geopolitical supply shock and partly on non-recurring items, and the stock faces a stack of specific risks: active antitrust litigation, direct political pressure on pricing, and a leadership vacuum during a major capital build. The options market is paying up for near-term protection, reinforcing the asymmetric, event-driven nature of the setup. The result is a balanced, two-sided situation best held tactically with disciplined exit triggers. We rate CF a HOLD with an overall score of 6.0.
