## Executive Summary

Kroger is the largest pure-play supermarket operator in the US, serving 63 million households across 2,697 stores. The stock trades at $66.01, representing approximately 12.7x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($5.20), a meaningful discount to large-cap consumer staples and retail leaders such as Walmart and Costco. This discount partially reflects the $2.5B fulfillment network impairment reported in FY2025, the failed $24.6B Albertsons merger, and a CEO transition — but the underlying business is improving: identical sales grew 2.9%, adjusted EPS grew 9%, and the company returned $4.3B to shareholders in a single year.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a new CEO with Walmart operational DNA signals a pragmatic shift away from expensive technology bets toward execution and price competitiveness; (2) aggressive capital return ($7.5B buyback completed in FY2025, with an additional $2.0B authorized in December 2025) at a valuation that is accretive to per-share earnings; and (3) a growing high-margin alternative profit stream ($1.5B in operating profit, including retail media, data analytics, and other services) that structurally improves Kroger's margin profile over time. The primary risks are competitive intensity from Walmart and discounters, the AI-driven agentic shopping threat to Kroger's data moat, and ongoing Albertsons litigation costs.

## Company Overview

Kroger operates 2,697 supermarkets (2,250 with pharmacies, 1,731 with fuel centers) across 35 states under banners including Kroger, Ralph's, Fred Meyer, Harris Teeter, and King Soopers. The company generates approximately $148B in annual revenue, owns 33 food production plants supplying its $39B private-label portfolio ("Our Brands"), and operates one of the largest loyalty programs in US retail with 95%+ transaction identification rates.

The business model generates thin margins on core grocery (FIFO gross margin ~23%) supplemented by higher-margin pharmacy, fuel, and increasingly, alternative profit streams. Kroger Precision Marketing (the retail media business) leverages its 63M-household data asset to sell targeted advertising to CPG brands, contributing to $1.5B in operating profit from "alternative profits" in FY2025 — a category that also includes data analytic services, other pharmacy services, and external food-production sales.

## Growth Analysis

Kroger is a low-growth business generating mid-single-digit earnings expansion through a combination of:

1. **Same-store sales growth:** Identical sales ex-fuel accelerated from 1.5% to 2.9% in FY2025, driven by customer count growth and improved digital engagement. FY2026 guidance is more conservative at +1.0% to +2.0% (including approximately 130 bps of unfavorable IRA impact). Sustained food inflation (LIFO charge rose to $157M) provides a modest tailwind.

2. **eCommerce expansion (+16% in FY2025):** Digital sales continue growing at multiples of total company growth. The pivot from expensive Ocado warehouses to store-based fulfillment should improve the unit economics of this channel.

3. **Alternative profits ($1.5B in operating profit, growing):** This is the most valuable growth vector. Kroger's alternative profit businesses — including retail media, data analytics, pharmacy services, and external food production — contributed $1.5B in operating profit in FY2025. The company does not disclose specific margins or revenue for individual components, but the high-margin nature of data and media businesses suggests growing this contribution to $2B+ over 3 years would add meaningful incremental EPS without requiring top-line acceleration.

4. **Share count reduction:** Kroger completed its entire $7.5B December 2024 buyback authorization in FY2025 (including a $5B ASR at ~$66.68/share and $2.5B in open-market repurchases), reducing shares outstanding from ~720M to ~655M diluted (~9% reduction). An additional $2.0B authorization was approved in December 2025, expected to be completed by FY2026 year-end. The pace of future buybacks beyond this $2.0B program will depend on further board authorization.

Combined, these produce a credible 7-10% adjusted EPS growth trajectory — consistent with FY2026 guidance and consensus estimates of $5.24 (current year) and $5.62 (next year, +7.3%).

The constraints on growth are structural: the US grocery market grows ~3% nominally, Kroger is already the #2 player, and the Albertsons merger that would have provided step-function share gains is dead. Organic share gains are possible but will be ground out through execution, not transformational events.

## Valuation Assessment

At $66.01, Kroger trades at:

- ~12.7x midpoint FY2026 guidance ($5.20 adj. EPS)
- 12.6x consensus FY2026 ($5.24)
- 11.7x consensus FY2027 ($5.62)
- ~11.7x trailing free cash flow ($3.46B / $40.4B market cap); note FY2026 FCF guidance of $2.7B-$2.9B implies a forward FCF yield closer to 6.7%-7.2%
- 2.1% dividend yield + buyback yield = attractive total shareholder return

**Peer Comparison:** Kroger trades at a meaningful discount to large-cap staples and retail leaders such as Walmart (~34x forward) and Costco (~50x forward), which reflects those companies' faster growth and broader business models. Among direct grocery peers, the comparison is more nuanced — Albertsons trades at a low forward P/E (high single digits) reflecting its own operational challenges post-merger collapse. A more relevant framing is that Kroger's 12.7x forward multiple is cheap for the broader consumer staples universe, where stable growers typically trade at 18-22x forward earnings.

Kroger's discount reflects the failed merger, the Ocado impairment, the CEO transition, and the perception that grocery is a structurally low-return business. At ~12.7x forward earnings with strong total shareholder yield and improving comps, the risk/reward skews positive.

**The specific mispricing:** Data feeds displaying trailing P/E based on GAAP EPS of $1.54 (showing ~42.9x) may create a perception of expensive valuation among screens and quantitative models, when the actual recurring earnings power is $4.85 adjusted EPS. The $2.5B impairment is a sunk cost on a failed technology bet, not an indicator of the core business trajectory.

Analyst consensus target is approximately $73-$75 (average), with a range of approximately $59-$86.

## Competitive Landscape

Kroger's competitive position is stable with pockets of improvement:

**Advantages:**
- Unmatched first-party data (63M households, 95%+ identification)
- $39B private-label portfolio providing both margin and loyalty differentiation
- Geographic diversification reduces regional concentration risk
- Growing alternative profit business monetizes data at high margins
- Store-level pharmacy/fuel drives traffic at low incremental cost

**Threats:**
- Walmart's grocery dominance (~25% share) with aggressive delivery expansion
- Aldi/Lidl capturing price-sensitive shoppers through aggressive US expansion
- Amazon's continued investment in grocery (despite uncertain profitability)
- AI-powered agentic shopping (newly disclosed risk) could disintermediate Kroger from the customer relationship if AI agents optimize on price/convenience rather than retailer preference
- Dollar stores expanding fresh/frozen food

The new CEO's Walmart background may enable Kroger to better compete on operational efficiency — the core competency of its largest competitor.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Competitive Margin Pressure (MODERATE):** Sustained investment in price to match Walmart and discounters could compress margins. Identical sales growth requires ongoing promotional spending.

**2. Albertsons Litigation (MODERATE):** Multiple lawsuits ongoing. $161M already charged in FY2025. Albertsons seeks a $600M termination fee plus additional damages including merger expenses and alleged lost premium. While Kroger denies the claims and has filed counterclaims, the outcome is uncertain. A $600M+ adverse judgment, while manageable relative to Kroger's enterprise value, would be material relative to FY2025 GAAP net earnings of ~$1.0B and FY2026 FCF guidance of $2.7B-$2.9B.

**3. AI Agentic Shopping (LOW PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT):** If AI shopping agents gain adoption, they could commoditize the grocery purchase decision, reducing Kroger's ability to monetize its data/personalization advantage. Timeline is uncertain (2-5 years for meaningful adoption) but the risk is real and newly acknowledged by management.

**4. Multi-Employer Pension Obligations (~$1.2B underfunding):** Contributions rising ($496M in FY2025, up ~$100M YoY). Manageable but a persistent cash drag with no strategic benefit.

**5. CEO Transition Execution Risk:** Foran has limited grocery-specific experience outside Walmart. Strategic direction may shift, creating near-term uncertainty. Early signals suggest emphasis on price and efficiency, which is constructive.

**6. Macro/Consumer:** In a stagflationary environment with savings rates at 3.6%, grocery is relatively defensive — consumers trade down to home cooking. However, private-label competition intensifies as consumers seek value.

- Alternative profit/retail media scales to $2B+ within 2 years
- Continued aggressive buybacks (subject to new board authorization beyond current $2B program) at accretive valuations

- Buybacks reduce shares ~3% via current $2B authorization

- $5.24 EPS × 14.3x = $75 (aligned with analyst consensus)

## Conclusion

Kroger at $66.01 offers a compelling risk/reward for a consumer staples investor. The stock screens as expensive on GAAP P/E (~42.9x) due to the non-recurring $2.5B impairment, but the underlying business trades at ~12.7x forward adjusted earnings — cheap for a company with strong cash generation, attractive shareholder returns, and 7-10% EPS growth.

The Ocado impairment was a capital allocation mistake under prior management. The new CEO (ex-Walmart US) brings operational credibility. The alternative profit business provides a structural margin improvement path not fully reflected in current multiples. The buyback program at current valuations is meaningfully accretive.

The primary concern preventing a higher-conviction recommendation is the structural growth ceiling in US grocery. Additionally, FY2026 FCF guidance of $2.7B-$2.9B (below FY2025's $3.46B due to higher capex of $3.8B-$4.0B) and identical sales guidance of only +1.0% to +2.0% temper the near-term outlook. At ~12.7x forward earnings with visible catalysts (new CEO, buybacks, alternative profits), the valuation compensates for limited top-line growth. Recommend BUY with a 12-month base case target of $75.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** FY2026 FCF guidance ($2.7B-$2.9B) is materially below the trailing $3.46B used in parts of the valuation; only $2.0B in buyback authorization remains (not an ongoing 5-7% annual reduction without new authorization); and the peer-comparison discount is primarily versus large-cap staples leaders, not direct grocery peers — collectively these weaken the capital-return pillar of the thesis and narrow the margin of safety.
