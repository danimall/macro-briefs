## Executive Summary

Equitable Holdings trades at approximately 6x forward operating earnings, a meaningful discount to diversified financial services peers and analyst consensus price targets. The primary driver of this discount is the pending all-stock merger with Corebridge Financial (CRBG), which introduces binary regulatory/completion risk alongside a pattern of quarterly earnings results below consensus expectations that has eroded near-term confidence.

The underlying franchise generates a 22.4% non-GAAP operating ROE, is systematically de-risking legacy liabilities through reinsurance transactions, and holds a strong position in the structurally growing U.S. retirement market. The SCS (Structured Capital Strategies) product — now $77.2B in account value as of Q3 2025, up 18% in nine months — positions EQH at the center of baby boomer retirement demand. AllianceBernstein (approximately $839B AUM as of Q1 2026) provides fee-based earnings diversification with a capital-light profile.

The risk-reward remains favorable at current prices despite declining estimate revisions and merger uncertainty. A failed merger would likely push the stock toward $35-38 initially before the standalone business re-rates on capital return execution. A successful close at year-end 2026 should drive the combined entity toward management's accretion targets, though specific price outcomes depend on pro-forma EPS and the multiple assigned by the market.

## Company Overview

Equitable Holdings operates through three segments: **Retirement** (approximately $176B in assets as of Q1 2026, including the strong-selling SCS structured annuities), **Asset Management** (approximately 68% economic interest in AllianceBernstein, ~$839B AUM as of Q1 2026), and **Wealth Management** (Equitable Advisors, ~4,600 professionals, approximately $131B AUA as of Q1 2026). The Corporate & Other segment houses legacy variable annuity blocks and the recently reinsured life/closed block business.

The company is executing a multi-year transformation from capital-heavy insurance guarantor to fee-oriented, capital-light financial services firm. Key transactions include: RGA reinsurance (ceded 75% of individual life, July 2025), Venerable VA novation (January 2025), and the AB tender offer increasing ownership from 62% to approximately 68-69%.

On March 26, 2026, EQH announced an all-stock merger with Corebridge Financial at a 1.55516 exchange ratio per EQH share, expected to close by year-end 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP vs. Operating Reality:** The trailing GAAP P/E is negative, reflecting GAAP losses driven by $1.1B in realized losses from the RGA asset transfer, $499M Venerable novation loss, and derivative mark-to-market volatility. These distortions obscure a business earning ~$1.7B annually on an operating basis (FY2025 Non-GAAP operating earnings: $1.741B, or $5.64/share).

**Operating Metrics (9M 2025):**
- Non-GAAP operating earnings: $1,228M ($3.90/share)
- Non-GAAP operating ROE: 22.4%
- Segment breakdown: Retirement $1,129M, Asset Management $411M (+29% YoY), Wealth Management $154M (+14%)

**Q1 2026 Update:** Non-GAAP operating earnings of $472M ($1.62/share), up from $1.30 in Q1 2025. Retirement assets grew to $175.7B, Wealth AUA reached $131.0B (aided by market performance, inflows, and the Stifel Independent Advisors acquisition), and AB AUM was $838.6B.

**Capital Deployment:** Management deployed approximately $2.7B in capital in the first nine months of 2025, including $1.17B in buybacks (reducing shares 6.7% from 310M to 289M), $758M AB tender offer, $237M common dividends, $48M preferred dividends, and $453M preferred redemption. The AB tender offer and preferred redemption represent growth investments and capital structure optimization, respectively, rather than direct shareholder returns. The company reported returning $1.8B to shareholders in FY2025. This pace is aggressive but supported by the Equitable America extraordinary dividend of $1.7B and capital freed by the RGA transaction (which freed approximately $2B of capital and reduced mortality exposure by 75%).

**Balance Sheet Concerns:** Total equity attributable to Holdings was $273M as of March 31, 2026 (on approximately $310B of total assets), having recovered from negative $74M at December 31, 2025. This low figure is driven by AOCI (unrealized losses on fixed income securities in the rate environment). Book value per common share excluding AOCI was $19.56 as of Q1 2026, and the combined NAIC RBC ratio was approximately 475% at year-end 2025, well above the company's 400% target. Investment portfolio quality remains strong — 97.7% investment grade, average mortgage LTV 67%. The GAAP equity figure reflects accounting treatment of unrealized fixed income losses rather than statutory insolvency risk, though it does create vulnerability if assets must be sold or if additional impairments arise.

**Liquidity:** $868M operating cash flow (9M 2025). Long-term debt of $3.83B with recent $500M junior subordinated notes at 6.7% replacing preferred equity.

## Growth Analysis

- SCS account value grew from $65.3B to $77.2B in 9 months through Q3 2025 (18%), driven by $10.1B net transfers
- Wealth Management advisory assets grew from $66B to $79B (+20%) through Q3 2025, reaching $131B AUA by Q1 2026
- AB AUM supports fee growth as Corebridge assets (management expects over $100B to shift to AB over time) represent a concrete growth opportunity

**Growth Concerns:**
- Sell-side consensus EPS estimates for FY2026 have declined approximately 10% over 90 days, per data vendor estimates
- Recent quarters have seen operating EPS come in below consensus expectations, per data vendor tracking, though the magnitude of misses has been narrowing
- AB experienced net long-term outflows of $(6.6)B in 9M 2025, and Q1 2026 saw $7.1B in asset management outflows
- Retirement segment operating earnings declined 7% YoY in 9M 2025 as interest credited growth offset NII gains

**Merger Growth Thesis:** The combined entity targets >$5B operating earnings, >$4B cash generation, and >10% EPS accretion by 2028 with more than $500M run-rate expense synergies by end of 2028. These are management expectations and remain subject to execution risk.

The declining estimate trajectory is the primary negative signal. If the narrowing of quarterly miss magnitudes continues, it would suggest the business is stabilizing closer to consensus, but the miss streak indicates sell-side models may still overestimate EQH's earnings power under current conditions.

The exact current discount depends on the market price at time of analysis and the FY2026 consensus estimate used.

**Valuation Framework:**
- FY2025 reported operating EPS: $5.64; adjusted operating EPS: $6.21
- Q1 2026 operating EPS: $1.62 ($1.68 adjusted)
- Analyst consensus targets suggest meaningful upside from recent trading levels
- Dividend yield: approximately 2.5-3.0% at recent price levels

**Why the market may be undervaluing EQH:** The market appears to be pricing merger failure risk AND earnings deterioration simultaneously. If the merger fails, EQH reverts to a standalone entity generating $5.60+ operating EPS (with growth potential) with a $1.3B remaining buyback authorization and a track record of 22%+ ROE. If the merger succeeds, the 1.55516 exchange ratio into a combined entity with higher earnings and synergy potential should drive re-rating.

**Scenario math (illustrative):** A standalone EQH at 8-9x operating earnings (assuming FY2026 consensus near $7) would imply $56-63. A successful merger with the combined entity delivering on management's targets should support similar or higher EQH-equivalent value, though this requires a detailed pro-forma EPS bridge (combined operating EPS, synergy timing, new share count) that is not yet fully calculable from public filings.

## Competitive Landscape

EQH holds a strong position in U.S. structured annuities (SCS), a product category growing rapidly as retirees seek principal protection with equity participation. Competitors include Jackson Financial, Lincoln Financial, and Athene/Apollo, but SCS's distribution advantage through Equitable Advisors and third-party channels creates competitive positioning advantages.

AllianceBernstein provides competitive differentiation through vertically integrated asset management — managing both General Account assets and external client capital. The post-merger potential to shift over $100B of Corebridge assets to AB management represents a concrete growth opportunity unavailable to asset-management-only competitors.

The merger with Corebridge, if completed, would create one of the largest U.S. retirement/life platforms (>$1.5T AUM/AUA, >12M customers), providing scale advantages in technology investment, regulatory compliance, and distribution that smaller competitors cannot match.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Merger Execution (HIGH):** The Corebridge merger requires state insurance department approvals across multiple jurisdictions, potential DOJ/FTC review for combined market share in certain annuity categories, and shareholder approval from both sides. The exchange ratio is fixed, meaning EQH shareholders bear the risk of Corebridge value fluctuation. Expected close by year-end 2026 leaves significant regulatory uncertainty. The company has indicated that 2026 annual meetings are expected to be deferred to allow special meetings for shareholder votes.

**2. Earnings Trajectory (MODERATE-HIGH):** The pattern of quarterly results below consensus with declining estimates signals a challenge in hitting consensus expectations. Whether this reflects temporary noise from de-risking transactions or structural overestimation of the business's earnings power is unclear. Q1 2026 operating EPS of $1.62 (up 25% YoY from $1.30) suggests underlying growth remains intact.

**3. Legacy VA Guarantee Exposure:** Net amount at risk on GMxB of $15.0B with $129B notional derivative book. A simultaneous equity decline and rate move could stress both the guarantee liability and the derivative hedge effectiveness.

**4. GAAP Equity Concerns:** Low GAAP equity ($273M as of Q1 2026) means additional impairments or forced asset sales could pressure the GAAP capital position. Statutory capital remains robust (RBC ~475% vs. 400% target), and the risk should not be conflated with operational insolvency, but it does create rating agency and counterparty perception risk.

**5. Regulatory Risk:** NAIC CLO capital requirements (effective 2025/2026), AG 55 reinsurance guidelines, and potential DOL fiduciary rule changes could increase capital requirements or constrain business practices.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (+25-40% from recent levels):** Merger closes on schedule with regulatory approval by year-end 2026. Combined entity achieves initial synergy targets, AB captures Corebridge asset flow. Market re-rates combined entity at 8-9x forward operating EPS. Standalone SCS growth continues at 15%+ annually regardless of merger outcome.

**Bear Case (-15-20% from recent levels):** Merger blocked or abandoned on regulatory grounds. Earnings continue declining as de-risking transactions reduce fee income faster than growth offsets. Legacy VA guarantees stressed by adverse market conditions. GAAP equity concerns trigger rating agency action.

**Base Case (+15-25% from recent levels):** Merger progresses with modest regulatory delays (close Q1 2027). Earnings stabilize as de-risking transaction drags dissipate. Market assigns partial credit for synergy potential. Standalone valuation floor of 7-8x earnings provides downside support.

## Conclusion

EQH presents a favorable risk-reward at current levels despite legitimate concerns about the earnings miss pattern and merger execution risk. The stock trades at a significant discount to forward operating earnings relative to financial sector peers, with analyst consensus targets implying meaningful upside.

The valuation discount appears inconsistent with probability-weighted outcomes. Even assigning substantial probability to merger failure, the standalone business's operating earnings power, capital return capacity, and 22%+ ROE provide a valuation floor well above bear-case levels. The market appears to be pricing merger risk as near-binary when it is more accurately a timing question with a favorable base case.

The key offsetting risk: the pattern of quarterly earnings below consensus combined with declining estimate revisions indicates the underlying business may be generating less than consensus expects. However, the improving trajectory in Q1 2026 (operating EPS up 25% YoY) and the transactional nature of prior miss drivers (RGA drag, Venerable impact) suggest stabilization is likely by H2 2026.
