**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Crane NXT is an industrial-technology company built around two franchises: Security & Authentication Technologies (SAT), anchored by the differentiated Crane Currency micro-optics banknote business, and Detection & Traceability Technologies (DTT), the legacy Crane Payment Innovations hardware franchise now combined with the newly acquired Antares Vision. Since spinning from Crane Holdings in April 2023, management has executed three acquisitions in roughly 24 months (OpSec, De La Rue Authentication, Antares Vision) and reorganized its reporting segments twice in 15 months. The result is a company whose headline revenue growth (+11.4% in FY2025) is almost entirely purchased: of the +$169.9M in FY2025 revenue growth, only +$10.1M (0.7%) was organic, with +$133M from acquisitions and +$26.8M from FX.

The central analytical problem is the gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings. GAAP net income has declined three consecutive years ($188M → $184M → $145M) even as revenue rose, and Q1 2026 GAAP EPS collapsed to $0.11 from $0.38 a year earlier. Management bridges this to $0.60 adjusted EPS (+11% y/y), attributing the difference to roughly $21M of Antares deal costs, Class-B stock compensation, and amortization. Full-year 2026 adjusted guidance is $4.10–$4.40, which puts the stock at approximately 12–13x adjusted EPS at $52.86. That is cheap if the adjustments are transitory noise, and a value trap if the bear thesis is correct that the "adjusted" story permanently papers over a decaying, low-organic-growth core carrying rising leverage.

We rate CXT a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The most credible bullish evidence is that both the CEO and CFO bought stock on the open market in June 2026 at ~$42 — roughly 20% below the current price — a conviction signal from the two people with the best information. That materially offsets, but does not resolve, the earnings-quality and leverage concerns. The near-term thesis hinges on a binary: deleveraging from 2.9x to a targeted 2.3x net leverage by year-end 2026, against a backdrop where the Authentication reporting unit's goodwill cushion is disclosed at under 10%.

## Company Overview

Crane NXT operates in secure, detect, and authenticate technologies with roughly 4,800 employees across 35 countries and approximately half of revenue generated outside the United States. The company was formed on April 3, 2023 when the former Crane Holdings split into Crane NXT (retaining Payment & Merchandising Technologies) and Crane Company (Aerospace & Electronics, Process Flow, Engineered Materials). For accounting purposes, Crane Company was the "accounting spinnor," so pre-2023 combined carve-out financials — including the ~$3.37B figure that appears in some historical XBRL data — are not comparable to today's standalone ~$1.6–1.7B business. Any trend analysis must begin at FY2023.

The two operating segments as of Q1 2026:

- **SAT (Security & Authentication Technologies):** Banknote design and production and security features for central banks via Crane Currency (micro-optics, anti-counterfeiting), plus brand protection, physical and digital authentication, and government tax stamps via OpSec and De La Rue (combined into "Crane Authentication"). FY2025 sales of $810.1M. This is the differentiated, high-barrier franchise, with sticky central-bank relationships and long qualification cycles.

- **DTT (Detection & Traceability Technologies):** Electronic payment acceptance and dispensing hardware and software (bill validators, coin and cash handling) for vending, gaming, retail, and banking, plus the acquired Antares Vision inspection and track-and-trace systems for life sciences and food and beverage. FY2025 legacy CPI sales of $846.6M at a strong ~26% operating margin, though the underlying payments hardware demand is soft.

The segment restructuring cadence (CPI + Crane Currency → CPI + SAT → SAT + DTT) reflects the pace of portfolio reshaping and complicates comparability of the "growth story" against genuine operating momentum.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** Standalone revenue grew FY2023 $1.39B → FY2024 $1.49B → FY2025 $1.66B. The composition of that growth is the issue: acquisitions and FX did nearly all the work. In Q1 2026, revenue rose 17% to $388M with +5.6% organic, but the organic figure masks divergence — SAT organic +22% (Currency strong) versus DTT/CPI organic −5% on weak vending and gaming hardware.

**Margins.** Total operating margin has compressed materially: 20.6% (2023) → 18.1% (2024) → 14.9% (2025). The SAT margin fell from 23.0% to 18.1% to 12.0% as higher-margin Currency was diluted by lower-margin OpSec/De La Rue authentication, acquisition amortization, fair-value step-ups, and $12.1M of integration restructuring. CPI held its ~26% margin but on a shrinking sales base (−3.0% in 2025). The mechanism is straightforward: the company is buying revenue at lower incremental margins while its highest-margin legacy line stagnates, so consolidated profitability declines even as the top line grows.

**Earnings quality — the crux.** GAAP net income was pressured by interest expense rising to $60.3M (from $47.8M) on acquisition debt and an $11.5M equity-investment loss tied to Antares stock comp. Q1 2026 operating margin fell to 5.7% from 11.3%, driven by ~$10.3M acquisition costs, a $10.7M Antares Class-B stock-comp charge, amortization, and higher interest of $17.8M. Management's adjusted EPS strips these items out, alongside a ~19% adjusted EBITDA margin (+80bps). The bear correctly notes that the Class-B stock-comp mechanism is recurring through 2028, so the "adjustments" are not purely one-time; they are a multi-year feature that will keep GAAP results volatile and hard to model.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt surged from $750.6M (2024) to $1,139.5M (2025) to approximately $1.5B by Q1 2026, including a $366.9M Term Loan B draw and $123.5M of assumed Antares debt. Credit ratings are sub-investment grade (S&P BB+ / Moody's Ba1, stable), and the Altman Z-Score of 1.87 sits in the grey zone. Goodwill and intangibles total $2.19B at Q1 2026 — roughly 60% of $3.64B total assets, with goodwill alone at $1.40B.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow has been reasonably stable ($276M in 2023, $214M in 2024, $242M in 2025), with FY2026 FCF conversion guided at 90–110%. Capex is stepping up to $75–80M in 2026 (from ~$43M in 2025) to expand banknote capacity — a call on near-term cash that pressures the deleveraging math. The dividend (~$0.18/quarter, ~$40M/year) is well covered at a 1.4% yield, and there were no buybacks in Q1 2026 as capital is directed to debt reduction and M&A integration.

The Financial Health score of 4.5 reflects sub-IG ratings, a grey-zone Z-score, net leverage near 3x, goodwill-heavy assets, and three years of declining GAAP net income, partially offset by stable operating cash flow and a covered dividend. This is the single most reliable discriminator of return outcomes in our framework, and the evidence here points to below-average resilience.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question separates into two very different stories. SAT/Currency is genuinely growing — +22% organic in Q1 2026, driven by international banknote demand, with capacity expansion underway. This is durable, high-barrier demand tied to central-bank contracts. The problem is that the reported growth of the whole company is dominated by acquisitions, and the payments core (DTT/CPI) is in structural decline: hardware sales fell 4.1% in 2025 and DTT organic fell ~5% in Q1 2026 on soft vending and gaming capital spending, compounded by the secular decline in cash usage. Management guides CPI "flat" for 2026, which looks optimistic against the trend.

Antares Vision (fully acquired March 31, 2026 for $418.2M) moves CXT into faster-growing regulated end markets — pharma serialization and food traceability, roughly a $3B addressable market — and is expected to add $200–210M of revenue over nine months of 2026 (Q4-weighted). But it comes at lower initial margins, is dilutive in 2026, and is only guided accretive by 2027. FY2026 guidance of 15–17% sales growth is therefore largely inorganic; the underlying organic growth rate of the combined business is low-single-digit at best, held up by SAT and dragged by payments.

Estimate revisions are broadly negative: current-quarter EPS revised −1.2% over 90 days, next-quarter −3.1%, current-year −0.9%. Only next-year sits slightly positive (+0.4%). This is the opposite of the +30% revision velocity that would flag an inflection buy — consensus is drifting down, not up. The earnings-surprise record (2 beats, 2 in-line over four quarters, all beats under 5%) shows management delivers roughly in line without upside momentum. The Growth Prospects score of 5.0 reflects real strength in one segment offset by a decaying core, negative revision trend, and growth that is purchased rather than compounded organically.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.86 the equity carries a ~$3.0B market cap and trades at approximately 12–13x adjusted EPS guidance (23.3x trailing, but trailing GAAP is distorted by deal noise). On adjusted earnings, that is a discount to the peer median P/E of 26.5x in Electronic Equipment & Instruments. Peer multiples are widely dispersed, however (VNT 10.1x, ITRI 12.9x, OSIS 26.5x, BMI 29.5x, MIR 167.8x), so the median is not a clean anchor. Against the lower-multiple, lower-quality peers (VNT, ITRI), CXT is roughly in line; against the higher-quality authentication franchises it looks cheap.

The analyst price-target range is $50 to $85, mean $66.83, median $66 — implying meaningful upside to consensus. Under our framework, analyst targets function as floors only on cheap inflection stocks with rising estimates; here estimates are falling, so consensus targets are more likely stale on the high side than the low side, and the $50 low is the more informative datapoint (essentially at the current price).

The valuation case is genuinely two-sided. If adjusted EPS is a fair proxy for cash earnings power — and the ~90–110% FCF conversion guide supports that — then 12–13x for a business with a differentiated Currency franchise and a credible 2027 accretion story is attractive. If the bear is right that the Class-B comp and amortization are permanent features masking a stagnant, over-levered roll-up, then the "cheap" multiple is a value trap. Our framework treats a low multiple as a warning flag absent an independent catalyst, and cheapness has reliably sorted the wrong way in recent regimes. The one identifiable catalyst — deleveraging to 2.3x re-opening capacity and confirming the adjusted bridge — is binary and unproven. We score Valuation 5.7: modestly above neutral to reflect the discount and the insider-buying signal, but well short of a high score given the earnings-quality debate and falling estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is bifurcated. Crane Currency's micro-optics banknote security is a genuinely differentiated, high-barrier niche with central-bank customers and multi-year qualification cycles; competitors include Giesecke+Devrient, De La Rue's remaining currency business, and SICPA in authentication and tax stamps. This franchise is gaining, not losing, with capacity expansion signaling management's confidence in sustained demand. Antares moves the company into faster-growing regulated traceability markets, though against established European peers and larger inspection players, and at lower margins initially.

The payments core (CPI) is the clear competitive weak point. Vending and gaming hardware demand is structurally challenged by restrained customer capex and the secular decline in cash usage, and organic sales are shrinking. This is the "decaying core" the bear thesis targets, and it drags on both the growth and the multiple. Level-wise, CXT describes itself as a principal competitor in most markets, but trajectory is what matters: one segment improving, one deteriorating, netting to a stable-to-slightly-positive overall competitive trajectory. The Competitive Position score of 5.5 reflects that mixed picture, and our framework treats this dimension as low-signal, so it carries limited weight.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is elevated and concentrated in specific, disclosed items rather than generic sector risk:

1. **Leverage and earnings quality.** Sub-IG ratings, Z-score in the grey zone. The deleveraging-to-2.3x plan depends on synergies materializing and FCF conversion holding while integration costs and rising capex pressure near-term cash.

2. **Goodwill impairment — specifically flagged.** The 10-K discloses the Authentication reporting unit's fair-value cushion is under 10% in a hypothetical stress test. With more goodwill added from Antares, this is the single most concrete impairment watch-item; a De La Rue/OpSec integration stumble could trigger a real writedown against $1.40B of goodwill (~38% of assets).

3. **Antares Class-B stock-comp overhang.** A $36.7M (and growing) liability-classified comp arrangement tied to Antares' 2028 revenue/EBIT targets, marked-to-market each quarter, will keep GAAP EPS volatile and hard to model for years.

4. **Decaying payments core.** Management's "flat CPI in 2026" guide looks optimistic against the secular trend.

5. **Lumpy government/central-bank revenue** with FCPA/anti-bribery exposure in high-corruption-index countries.

6. **FX and tariffs.** ~50% non-U.S. sales create tough H2 2026 comps against strong 2025 FX, plus Mexican Maquiladora tariff exposure.

7. **Separation tax risk** — a low-probability, high-severity tail if the 2023 spin were deemed taxable.

8. **Integration execution** across three acquisitions and two segment reorganizations in ~24 months, in a decentralized structure.

Short interest is elevated at 19.8% of float (6.5 days to cover), confirming institutional skepticism. The Risk Factors score of 4.5 reflects the specific goodwill-cushion disclosure, the binary deleveraging catalyst, and the earnings-quality opacity — risks that are not yet fully resolved in the current price.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 46.4% versus 38.7% historical volatility (rich by 7.7 points), with the term structure in backwardation (46.4% near-term versus 41.5% at six months) — the market expects imminent event risk, consistent with the next earnings print and the deleveraging watch. Put/call skew is elevated at 7.0% at the front expiry, reflecting demand for downside protection, though it flips negative at the 3-month tenor. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.7%. For a fundamental HOLD, this configuration argues against establishing a large position ahead of the print; the rich near-term IV and put skew corroborate the bearish institutional positioning reflected in the short interest.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Adjusted EPS is a fair proxy for cash earnings power, supported by 90–110% FCF conversion, and the GAAP collapse is transitory deal noise that fades as integration completes. SAT/Currency keeps growing double-digits organically with capacity expansion, Antares turns accretive in 2027, deleveraging hits 2.3x by year-end 2026 and re-opens M&A capacity, and the multiple re-rates from ~12–13x toward the peer median. The CEO and CFO open-market purchases signal conviction that the stock is undervalued. Bull case ~$70–75 (16–17x the midpoint of adjusted EPS as the deleveraging de-risks the story), roughly +32–42% from $52.86.

### Bear Case

The "adjusted" bridge permanently masks a stagnant, over-levered roll-up; the Class-B comp charges recur through 2028; the payments core continues its ~5% organic decline; SAT margin recovery to a targeted 25% disappoints; and a goodwill impairment against the sub-10%-cushion Authentication unit crystallizes. Deleveraging slips on weak vending FCF or capex overruns, pressuring the sub-IG credit and the equity. Bear case ~$38–40 (near the June 2026 lows and where insiders bought), roughly −24–28% from current levels.

### Base Case

Deleveraging progresses but perhaps short of 2.3x, SAT continues to carry the organic story while payments stays soft, adjusted EPS lands in the guided range, and the stock oscillates in the low-to-mid $50s as the market waits for proof of the adjusted-to-cash-earnings conversion. The insider buying and the discounted multiple cap the downside near the June lows; the earnings-quality overhang and falling estimates cap the upside. Base case ~$55–58.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (uncertain)**. The evidence is genuinely balanced — a cheap adjusted multiple and a strong insider signal against a decaying core, rising leverage, negative estimate revisions, and a specific goodwill-impairment watch-item.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net leverage confirmed at or below 2.3x by year-end 2026 (or a clear glidepath in Q3 2026 results), validating the deleveraging catalyst.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive DTT/CPI organic growth, breaking the "decaying core" thesis.
- Consensus EPS revisions turning positive (reversing the current −1% to −3% drift), signaling the adjusted story is being validated by the sell-side.
- Confirmation of SAT margin recovery toward the targeted 25% and adjusted EBITDA margin sustained near/above 19%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment at the Authentication unit, or a widening of the disclosed sub-10% cushion into negative territory.
- FCF conversion falling below the 90% guide floor, jeopardizing deleveraging and the sub-IG credit.
- Adjusted EPS guidance cut below the $4.10 floor, which would collapse the valuation support.
- Any deterioration in the sub-IG rating outlook to negative.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the ±10.7% implied move and IV backwardation flag this as a genuine catalyst), with the year-end 2026 leverage figure as the decisive test of the thesis. The prior three reports have all been HOLD with a declining score trajectory (6.0 → 5.3 → 5.2); this report keeps the HOLD but stabilizes the score at 5.25. The stabilization reflects that the ~10% price decline since the last report has occurred against unchanged-to-slightly-improving fundamentals (adjusted guidance intact, insider buying at lower levels, deleveraging on track per Q1), which improves risk/reward at the margin rather than confirming further deterioration. It is not an upgrade because the core debate — whether adjusted earnings represent real cash power or mask a decaying franchise — remains unresolved.

## Conclusion

Crane NXT is a leveraged industrial-tech roll-up with one genuinely differentiated franchise (Crane Currency micro-optics), one structurally challenged core (payments hardware), and a portfolio reshaping that has purchased revenue at declining incremental margins while GAAP earnings fell for three straight years. At ~12–13x adjusted EPS guidance it is cheap if the adjusted bridge is real and a value trap if it is not, and the recurring Class-B stock-comp mechanism gives the bear a legitimate argument that the "adjustments" are a permanent feature rather than one-time noise. The most credible offset is the CEO and CFO open-market purchases at ~$42 in June 2026. Weighing the cheap multiple and insider conviction against sub-IG leverage, negative estimate revisions, a decaying organic core, and a specifically disclosed sub-10% goodwill cushion, we rate CXT a HOLD. The deleveraging path to 2.3x by year-end 2026 is the binary that will resolve the debate; until it does, the risk/reward is symmetric and the position is one to hold and monitor rather than to add to.
