## Executive Summary

Enerpac is a high-quality niche industrial — 54.5% product gross margins, 27.5% IT&S segment operating margins, 21.7% ROIC, 99% free-cash conversion, and a largest customer worth roughly 3% of sales — whose self-help earnings engine has run out. The ASCEND program that cut SG&A from $205.1M to $166.9M and lifted operating margin from 14.0% (FY2023) to 21.6% (FY2025) completed on August 31, 2024. What remains is a business growing organic revenue at 1–3% with a services franchise in double-digit decline, guiding to flat absolute EBITDA on 4% higher revenue.

The reported numbers obscure this. Q3 FY2026 looked strong (sales +6%, gross margin +260bp to 53.0%, diluted EPS +41% to $0.58), but $5.7M of net IEEPA tariff refund landed in cost of goods sold — 3.4 points of gross margin, taking underlying gross margin to roughly 49.6%, below the prior year's 50.4% — while the year-ago quarter carried $5.862M of restructuring. On a comparable basis, operating profit fell from about $37.5M to $35.7M, roughly –5%, on +6% reported revenue, and adjusted EPS was approximately flat at $0.49–$0.50. Management's own FY2026 guidance cut on July 7 confirms the direction: adjusted EBITDA reduced from $158–163M to $151–156M, with the implied Q4 range of $38.8–43.8M below the ~$44.5M delivered in Q4 FY2025 at every point. The $0.02 EPS guidance cut against a $7M EBITDA cut is bridged by a ~4.6% lower share count and lower interest, not by operations.

Against that, the $472M SFE Group acquisition (~$451M cash plus ~$21M of RSUs) is defensible capital allocation — $44M of EBITDA at ~10.7x, at a 25.9% margin slightly above Enerpac's own, in genuinely adjacent portable machining, weld prep and pipe handling niches, with modest 13%-of-EBITDA synergy assumptions. It also transforms the risk profile: net debt goes from $69.1M to roughly $540M, or ~2.8x pro forma, entirely floating after the $60M interest-rate swap matured November 30, 2025, with the amended credit agreement's maturity date unverified against a 10-K-stated September 2027 term. At 20.2x FY2026 adjusted EPS, a FY2027 consensus of $2.06 that has been cut 5.5% in 90 days (~18.3x), and 13.0x EV/EBITDA, the stock is priced roughly where the fundamentals justify. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on this name; the score decline from 5.6 to 5.26 reflects the guidance cut, negative revisions, and leverage step-up, and marks drift toward REDUCE rather than toward a buy.

## Company Overview

Enerpac designs and manufactures high-force hydraulic and mechanical tools — cylinders, pumps, torque wrenches, bolting tools, synchronous-lift systems, gantries and skidding systems — sold predominantly through global industrial distributors into refining and petrochemical, general industrial MRO, machining, power generation, infrastructure, mining and civil construction. One reportable segment (Industrial Tools & Services, $595.8M of FY2025 revenue) plus Cortland Biomedical in "Other" ($21.1M).

Two structural features matter. First, the revenue is genuinely diversified: largest customer ~3% of sales in each of the last three years, US sales 37.1% of total, export sales from domestic operations only 7.3%. This is local-for-local manufacturing and selling, which mutes tariff exposure on the revenue line while creating FX translation sensitivity. Second, the business has almost no forward visibility: $472.7M of FY2025 revenue was recognized at a point in time, contract liabilities were $3.4M, and the $54M "backlog" is a management-defined metric under 9% of annual sales — and per MD&A, its increase from $41M was driven primarily by the global inventory reduction program, meaning reduced fill rates rather than stronger demand.

The two halves of IT&S behave differently. Product gross margin was 54.5% in FY2025; service and rental (largely Hydratight bolting and joint-integrity work tied to refinery turnarounds) was 33.7%, down from 34.5% and 37.0% in the two prior years. Management flags in Note 1 that the product/service cost allocation involves judgment, so the level is approximate — but the three-year direction is consistent.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (the last audited year) was strong on every quality metric.** Revenue $616.9M (+4.6%), operating profit $133.5M (21.6% margin), net earnings $92.7M (15.0% net margin — note that a common screen field reports 21.6% here, which is the operating margin, not net). ROE on average equity 22.5%; ROA 11.6%; ROIC approximately 21.7% on ending invested capital. Operating cash flow $111.3M less $19.3M capex gives free cash flow of $91.9M, or 14.9% of sales and 99% of net income. Current ratio 2.74x, debt/equity 0.44x, net debt $38.4M (0.25x adjusted EBITDA).

Two quality-of-earnings items sit inside that year. A LIFO layer liquidation, a byproduct of the inventory reduction, added roughly $3.7M to pre-tax income (3.1% of $120.7M pre-tax) and does not repeat. Separately, the allowance for credit losses fell from $15.9M to $3.8M as ~$12.9M of pre-FY2023 reserves were written off — a balance-sheet clean-up with essentially no P&L effect, though it reveals that reserves equal to roughly 15% of the receivable book sat unresolved for years. The tax valuation allowance reduction from $57.7M to $27.7M was likewise a simultaneous derecognition of DTAs and their matching allowance, contributing 0.0% to the FY2025 rate reconciliation. FY2024, by contrast, did take a –4.1% rate benefit from valuation allowance releases, so the FY2024 base is the flattered one.

**FY2026 year-to-date is where the thesis turns.** Nine-month sales of $466.6M grew ~4% reported but only 1% organic. The composition:

| Organic growth | Q1 FY26 | Q2 FY26 | Q3 FY26 | 9M FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product | +4% | +6% | +5% | — |
| Service & rental | –26% | –17% | –8% | –18% |
| Cortland Biomedical | +27% | +27% | +25% | — |

Service sales fell from $90.0M to $76.5M over nine months. Management attributes this to Middle East disruption and deferred refinery/petrochemical turnarounds, and has initiated a FY2026 restructuring plan to rightsize Hydratight EMEA — an admission that the recovery is not expected to be fast or complete. Two readings coexist: the rate of decline is decelerating sharply and Q3 service sales rose 17% sequentially off what looks like a Q2 trough; and the base has reset roughly $17M lower on an annualized basis. Because service carries ~34% gross margin versus ~55% for product, the mix shift is arithmetically gross-margin accretive, which is part of why reported gross margin held while the business weakened.

**Guidance mathematics.** FY2026: sales $635–650M, adjusted EBITDA $151–156M, adjusted EPS $1.84–1.89, FCF $100–110M (maintained). Subtracting 9M actuals ($466.6M sales, $112.2M adjusted EBITDA, $1.35 adjusted EPS) implies Q4 sales of $168.4–183.4M, adjusted EBITDA of $38.8–43.8M against ~$44.5M a year ago, and adjusted EPS of $0.49–0.54 against ~$0.52. Full year: $153.5M midpoint EBITDA on $642.5M, a 23.9% margin, versus $153.6M on $616.9M and 24.9% in FY2025. Excluding the ~$5.7M tariff credit, the underlying figure is ~$147.8M and 23.0% — roughly 190bp of margin compression.

The $7.3M refund is a receivable in other current assets, not cash. Nine-month operating cash flow of $69.3M is clean of it, so EBITDA-based and cash-based views diverge this year, and reversal risk exists if recovery is delayed or partial (management recorded it because collection is deemed probable).

**Balance sheet, pre and post deal.** At May 31, 2026: debt $184.8M, cash $115.7M, net debt $69.1M. The credit facility (September 2022) comprised a $400M revolver and $200M term loan maturing September 2027, priced at SOFR plus 1.125%–1.875% on a net-leverage grid, with covenants at 3.75x net leverage (electable 4.25x around material acquisitions for the period in which they occur and the next four testing periods) and 3.00x interest coverage. Revolving commitments were upsized to $625M in July 2026. Post-close net debt of roughly $540M against combined adjusted EBITDA near $197.5M gives ~2.7–2.8x, matching management's guide. Covenant headroom at 3.75x implies minimum EBITDA of ~$147.5M, a ~25% cushion — adequate, no longer comfortable. [Pro forma interest coverage is not derivable from the sources — the amended pricing grid and revised term-loan sizing are unverified; the brief's incremental-interest estimate is a $19–23M sensitivity, not a stated figure.]

Goodwill ($289.8M) plus other intangibles ($46.9M) already equal 77.6% of equity; post-deal, tangible book goes materially negative. Pensions are near fully funded and leases are modest at $38.1M — neither is a risk.

## Growth Analysis

The honest growth picture is inorganic. Organic revenue growth is 1% year-to-date and organic EBITDA is declining. SFE adds ~$170M of revenue (+26% on FY2026 guidance) and $44M of EBITDA, and management claims ~$1B of incremental addressable market. Consensus reflects this: current-year EPS of $1.86 (matching guidance) and next-year $2.06, implying ~11% growth almost entirely from the acquisition, lower interest on a smaller share count, and cost synergies.

Estimate momentum is negative and should not be soft-pedaled. Current-quarter consensus fell 19.3% over both 30 and 90 days (to $0.52 from $0.64); next-year fell 5.5% (to $2.06 from $2.18); current year fell 1.5%. The earnings track record is unimpressive: over four quarters, one beat (Q3 FY2026, entirely explained by the tariff credit), one in-line, two misses. This is the mirror image of the estimate-revision setup that supports a constructive call — falling estimates on a business already trading at 18–20x forward earnings offers no valuation cushion.

The bull-side growth evidence is narrower but real. Product organic growth accelerated to the strongest rate since Q4 FY2023 on improving US manufacturing and distributor sentiment. Cortland Biomedical is compounding at ~25–27% at a 28.8% segment operating margin, though at ~3% of revenue it cannot move the consolidated line. R&D rose to $14.5M (2.4% of sales) from $9.0M in FY2023, a genuine step-up. And the service decline is decelerating on a clear path (–26%/–17%/–8%), which is the single most important variable for FY2027: if turnaround activity normalizes, FY2026 reads as a trough; if it does not, the ~$100M annualized service run-rate is the new base.

Two macro constraints deserve naming. Roughly 63% of revenue is non-US, with the UK, Germany and the Netherlands prominent. And Saudi Arabia alone was $26.7M of FY2025 sales — service demand is levered to the same Gulf disruption that has kept energy markets unsettled, which cuts both ways: sustained disruption defers turnarounds, verified normalization would release them.

The peer table is not usable as presented. The FCF yield is the most supportive metric. 5.4% on guided cash flow, with FY2025 conversion at 99% of net income, is respectable for a 21.7%-ROIC franchise. But that yield will be substantially redirected to de-levering for the next two to three years, converting an equity return into balance-sheet repair. Buybacks of ~$188M over 32 months at an average near $39 (above the current price) are the mechanism that has been delivering EPS growth; capacity, not the ~$119M of nominal remaining authorization, is now the constraint.

**Cycle-adjusted view.** The strongest bull argument is that FY2026 is a trough year distorted by a service downturn that is visibly decelerating, and that normalized EBITDA on a recovered service base plus SFE is closer to $210–215M. At 13x that is an EV of $2,730–2,795M, or $43–44 per share after ~$540M of net debt. That is real upside, but it requires a service recovery that management's own EMEA restructuring implies it is not underwriting.

There is no identifiable mispricing to underwrite a high valuation score. Analyst targets of $46–50 (mean $48) imply 23x FY2027 consensus, but the brief documents that the standing targets predate two guidance revisions and the SFE announcement, with no post-deal revisions located. On a name with falling estimates and no premium-growth profile, stale targets are not independent confirmation.

**Scenarios:**
- **Bear $28 (–25.8%):** service fails to recover, distributor destocking returns, FY2027 EBITDA ~$175M, multiple compresses to 10.5x, refinancing pressure if the September 2027 maturity stands.
- **Base $41 (+8.7%):** service stabilizes near a $100M annualized run-rate, SFE delivers guided synergies, FY2027 adjusted EPS ~$2.10 at ~19.5x, cross-checked at ~13.2x pro forma EBITDA of ~$200M.
- **Bull $50 (+32.6%):** turnaround activity normalizes, synergies exceed $5.7M, FY2028 EBITDA above $220M at 14x with net debt down toward $480M.

Probability-weighted at 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives roughly $39.35, about +4%. At 50/25/25 it is $40.00, about +6%. Either is a fair-return outcome, not an attractive one.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is brand, channel and installed base in a niche where tool failure is catastrophic — lifting a bridge span or torquing a reactor flange. The evidence is quantitative rather than assertive: 54.5% product gross margin, 27.5% IT&S segment operating margin (up from 24.5% in FY2023), and warranty reserves of $1.1M on $617M of revenue. Competition is fragmented — large diversified tooling players in adjacent categories and numerous regional specialists.

Trajectory is mixed. On the product side, position is stable to improving: organic growth accelerating to +5%, margins expanding, R&D spend up 61% in two years. On the service side, position is contracting by choice — Hydratight EMEA is being rightsized, and skilled service labor is easier to shed than to rebuild, so a share loss that persists through the eventual turnaround upcycle is a live possibility.

SFE's strategic logic holds up. Portable machining, weld prep, orbital welding and cutting, pipe handling and material lifting sell into the same pipeline construction, fabrication, refinery and power customers as Hydratight and core Enerpac, and the seven rental depots add recurring-revenue characteristics. Buying $44M of EBITDA at 10.7x when the acquirer trades at 13.0x is accretive on an EV-multiple basis and is a better use of a strong balance sheet than repurchasing stock at 20x earnings. The counter-consideration is that SFE is a sponsor-owned twelve-brand roll-up assembled since 2019 — prepared for sale, with a seller-presented unaudited $44M EBITDA figure investors cannot inspect — and integrating twelve brands across four plants while simultaneously restructuring Hydratight EMEA is a heavy concurrent execution load. Enerpac's own acquisitive record is thin: DTA at $26.7M in FY2025 is the only recent comparison, and it is too new to judge.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Refinancing verification gap (highest-priority unresolved item).** The FY2025 10-K states a September 2027 maturity for the $600M facility. The July 8, 2026 8-K discloses the revolver upsize to $625M but the amendment text was not retrievable, so maturity, revised pricing grid and whether the 4.25x material-acquisition covenant election was made are unverified. Refinancing ~$550M of secured debt roughly twelve months out is a materially different proposition than a 2030 maturity.

**2. Floating-rate exposure with no hedge.** The $60M swap at 4.022% matured November 30, 2025; no replacement is disclosed. With ~$400M of incremental revolver borrowings at SOFR plus a margin appropriate to ~2.8x leverage, incremental interest runs roughly $19–23M annually, and every 100bp of SOFR movement is about $4M pre-tax, or ~$0.06 of EPS.

**3. Service end-market and Gulf geopolitics.** Nine-month service organic decline of 18% on deferred turnarounds; Saudi Arabia was $26.7M of FY2025 sales. The restructuring response signals management's own expectation of a slow recovery.

**4. Short-cycle visibility.** $3.4M of contract liabilities, a $54M management-defined backlog under 9% of sales, and $472.7M of point-in-time revenue mean a quarter can swing on distributor inventory decisions independent of end demand.

**5. Tariff refund reversal.** The $7.3M IEEPA receivable rests on a probability judgment. Delay or partial recovery reverses ~$0.085 of EPS and, more importantly, removes the item that made Q3 look like an inflection.

**6. Goodwill and intangibles.** $336.7M today (77.6% of equity) rising sharply post-deal against deeply negative tangible book, in a single-reportable-segment structure. Cyclical impairment risk is non-trivial.

**7. Unquantified Dutch sanctions matter.** The 2022 Dutch conclusion that Crimea-linked sales violated EU sanctions remains subject to further proceedings; the FY2021 accrual was set at the low end of a range that is not disclosed, so residual exposure cannot be sized.

**8. Unexplained Item 5.02 8-K of July 23, 2026.** An officer or director change two weeks after a $472M acquisition, with content not retrievable. Not evidence of a problem, but an open item.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 22 contracts across three expirations, zero put/call volume — so the readings should be treated as low-confidence quote artifacts rather than informed positioning. Taken at face value, near-term ATM IV of 113.2% against 32.1% one-year realized, in steep backwardation (113.2% at 11 days versus 44.6% at 39 days and 39.9% at 102 days), with a ±14.8% implied move on the August 21 expiry, is anomalous given that fiscal Q4 ends August 31 and results are not due until roughly mid-October. Two features argue against reading this as fundamental stress: the skew is call-heavy (–58.6% near-term), and the longer tenors — the more meaningful readings — sit only modestly above realized vol. The IV increase from 69.2% to 113.2% since June 22 tracks the deal announcement. Net: no put-side confirmation of the bearish operating case, no options-based reason to alter the fundamental view, and a chain too illiquid to trade around. Short interest at 4.5% of float and 2.3 days to cover is unremarkable and indicates no crowded bear positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($50, +32.6%).** Service is troughing, not permanently reset — the –26%/–17%/–8% sequence and Q3's 17% sequential recovery argue turnaround deferrals are timing, not demand destruction. Product organic growth at its best level since FY2023 confirms the industrial cycle is cooperating. SFE brings $44M of EBITDA at a 25.9% margin for 10.7x, with only 13%-of-EBITDA synergies assumed, and management's long-term incentives include a three-year average ROIC gate, so the acquisition is measured against capital efficiency rather than EBITDA growth alone. FY2028 EBITDA above $220M at 14x, with de-levering restoring buyback capacity, supports $50. The November 4 investor day is a scheduled venue to quantify synergies and set multi-year targets.

**Bear case ($28, –25.8%).** The ASCEND margin engine is spent: SG&A cannot fall another $38M, and FY2026 EBITDA is flat on 4% higher revenue even with a $5.7M tariff windfall and a $3.7M LIFO benefit in the prior-year comparison. Comparable Q3 operating profit fell ~5% on +6% revenue; adjusted EPS was flat. Estimates are being cut. The company is levering to ~2.8x, unhedged, into a sponsor-prepared twelve-brand roll-up whose $44M EBITDA is unaudited, while simultaneously restructuring a shrinking service franchise. If service does not recover and distributors destock, FY2027 EBITDA lands near $175M, the multiple compresses toward 10.5x, and a September 2027 maturity becomes a live problem. The $500M+ of cash formerly available for buybacks is gone.

**Where the evidence sits.** The bear case is grounded in reported filing facts and management's own guidance; the bull case rests on a service recovery that management is restructuring around and on an acquisition whose economics investors cannot independently verify. At 20.2x adjusted earnings and 5.4% FCF yield, the shares neither discount the bear case nor leave room for the bull case to be paid for twice.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on EPAC, and the bar for repeating it should be higher. What justifies it is that the price has moved little since the prior review ($36.33 on June 22 to $37.72) while a defensible base case sits ~9% above spot — so the call is genuinely fair-value rather than inertial. What has changed for the worse is fundamental, not price-driven: a guidance cut on July 7, a 19.3% cut to current-quarter consensus and 5.5% to next year, flat absolute EBITDA on 4% revenue growth, and a balance sheet moving from 0.25x to ~2.8x net leverage with an unverified maturity. The overall score declines from 5.6 to 5.26 for those reasons, and the direction of travel is toward REDUCE, not toward accumulation.

- **Base case $41** — 19.5x FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$2.10, cross-checked at ~13.2x pro forma EBITDA of ~$200M less ~$540M net debt. Upside +8.7%.
- **Bull case $50** — +32.6%.
- **Bear case $28** — –25.8%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the FY2026 Q4 report (~mid-October 2026), the November 4 investor day at the NYSE, and the first two quarters of SFE consolidation.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of:**
1. Service and rental organic growth returns to flat or positive for two consecutive quarters (Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027), confirming the trough rather than a reset base.
2. Disclosure that the amended credit facility matures in 2029 or later, removing the near-term refinancing question, ideally with a replacement interest-rate hedge on at least half the floating balance.
3. A November 4 investor day that quantifies SFE synergies above the implied $5.7M and sets a credible FY2029 EBITDA margin target at or above 25% on a post-deal revenue base — with the price still below $36.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. FY2027 guidance (given with the October print or at the investor day) implies adjusted EBITDA below $185M pro forma for a full year of SFE, which would mean SFE's $44M is not translating.
2. The July 2026 amendment is confirmed to leave a 2027 maturity, or pro forma net leverage is reported above 3.2x at close.
3. Product organic growth decelerates below +2%, indicating the one cooperating engine has stalled and that distributor restocking was the driver rather than end demand.
4. The $7.3M IEEPA receivable is written down, or a material purchase-accounting or goodwill charge emerges in the first two post-close quarters.

**Reassessment date:** mid-October 2026 (Q4 FY2026 results and initial FY2027 guidance), with a second checkpoint November 4.

For existing holders: the 5.4% FCF yield, 21.7% ROIC and low customer concentration justify continued exposure through the trough test. The $0.04 annual dividend is a token and provides no income support. New capital has no urgency here; the low-$30s — near the $32.35 fifty-two-week low, roughly 17x FY2027 consensus with the service trough confirmed — would offer the margin of safety this setup currently lacks.

## Conclusion

Enerpac remains a good business at a price that reflects it. The franchise metrics — 54.5% product gross margin, 27.5% segment operating margin, 21.7% ROIC, 99% cash conversion, largest customer at 3% of sales — are genuinely above average for a $1.9B industrial. The problem is that the earnings growth of the past three years came from a completed cost program, and FY2026 shows what the business looks like without it: 1% organic revenue growth, flat absolute EBITDA on 4% higher revenue, ~190bp of underlying margin compression, and EPS growth manufactured by buybacks and a one-time tariff refund. The SFE acquisition is a rational response — $44M of EBITDA at 10.7x is better value than repurchasing stock at 20x — but it converts a fortress balance sheet into a 2.8x levered, unhedged one with an unverified maturity, and it redirects the free cash flow that had been the shareholder return mechanism toward de-levering.

At 20.2x FY2026 adjusted EPS, 18.3x a falling FY2027 consensus, and 13.0x EV/EBITDA, the shares are priced for the base case. Expected return of roughly 4–6% against a 26% bear case is fair, not attractive. HOLD, with the October print and the November 4 investor day as the decision points.
