## Executive Summary

Core Natural Resources was formed in January 2025 through the merger of CONSOL Energy and Arch Resources, creating the largest publicly traded pure-play U.S. coal producer. The combined entity operates 11 mines across six states, owns export terminal infrastructure, and produces thermal, metallurgical, and Powder River Basin coal. The stock trades at $87 against a $4.4B market cap, with all four covering analysts rating it a Buy and a consensus price target of $111.50.

The investment case rests on normalization: FY 2025 was heavily distorted by the Leer South mine fire (which idled the company's best met coal asset for nearly the entire year), $621M in purchase-accounting DD&A (vs. ~$285M actual capex), and merger-related G&A. If Leer South runs at targeted rates through 2026 and met coal prices hold near current ~$250/mt levels, EBITDA could reach $700-800M — putting the stock at roughly 5.5-6.3x EV/EBITDA, cheap for a company with negative net leverage. The problem is that normalization has been promised but not yet delivered: CNR has missed EPS estimates in 3 of its 4 reported quarters, estimates have been revised sharply downward, and management sold significant stock at $100-$110 in March 2026.

CNR has a structurally advantaged asset base, zero net debt, and a credible path to $8+ in normalized EPS, but the confirmation data points are still ahead of us, not behind us. The right posture is to hold and upgrade on evidence of sustained Leer South operations and met segment margin recovery.

## Company Overview

Core Natural Resources operates through three production segments and an owned logistics platform:

**High CV Thermal** — The Pennsylvania Mining Complex (PAMC) is one of North America's largest and lowest-cost longwall thermal operations, producing high-calorific-value coal for domestic utilities and export markets. West Elk in Colorado recently completed a seam transition to B-Seam coal with better thickness and quality. Combined, this segment is the earnings anchor.

**Metallurgical** — Leer, Leer South, Beckley, Mountain Laurel, and Itmann mines in West Virginia produce premium high-vol and low-vol coking coals for the global steel industry. This is the swing factor for 2026 earnings, with 4.3M of 6.7M committed tons still unpriced and leveraged to benchmark price movements.

**Powder River Basin** — Black Thunder and Coal Creek surface mines in Wyoming produce sub-bituminous thermal coal at $1-$1.55/ton margins. Structurally declining but cash-flow-positive with 47.4M tons committed at $14.15/ton for 2026.

**Baltimore Marine Terminal** — Provides direct export access, a meaningful competitive advantage given that ~56% of coal revenue derives from exports.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Distortions**

FY 2025 reported a $153M net loss, but the loss is almost entirely artificial:

- DD&A: $621M (driven by $1.4B in fair-value step-ups from purchase accounting) vs. $285M capex
- Merger-related G&A: $215M (guided to $85-$100M cash basis in 2026)
- Leer South fire costs: ~$112M gross ($76M net of $19M insurance in 9M, plus $36M in Q4)
- Loss on debt extinguishment: $12M

Adjusted EBITDA was approximately $512M ($409M for 9M plus $103M in Q4). This is the relevant profitability metric.

**Segment Cash Margins (Q3 2025)**

| Segment | Revenue/Ton | Cash Cost/Ton | Cash Margin/Ton |
|---------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| High CV Thermal | $59.78 | $40.53 | $19.25 |
| Metallurgical | $101.60 | $94.18 | $7.42 |
| PRB | $14.09 | $13.04 | $1.05 |

The met segment was effectively break-even for most of 2025 (EBITDA negative at $-4.8M for 9M). With Leer South back online and 2026 cash cost guidance of $88-$94/ton, the met segment should swing to meaningful positive contribution if benchmark prices hold above $200/mt.

**Balance Sheet**

The balance sheet is unusually strong for a coal producer:
- Cash: $432M
- Total debt: ~$425M (mostly tax-exempt bonds at 5.0-5.45%, 10+ year maturities)
- Net debt: Approximately zero
- Undrawn revolver: $521M
- Total net leverage: Negative (net cash)
- Interest coverage: 50.1x

This eliminates refinancing risk entirely and provides substantial flexibility for counter-cyclical buybacks.

**Cash Flow & Capital Returns**

FY 2025 operating cash flow was $306M against $285M capex, yielding just $21M in FCF — depressed by merger costs and Leer South. The company nonetheless returned $245M to shareholders ($224M buybacks + dividends), funded partly from the cash balance. The $1B buyback authorization has $776M remaining; shares were repurchased at an average of $72.61, meaningfully accretive at current prices.

2026 capex is guided at $325-$375M. If EBITDA normalizes to $700-800M, FCF could reach $300-400M, supporting continuation of the aggressive buyback program.

**Earnings Quality Concern**

The EPS track record is poor: 3 misses in 4 quarters, including a -334% surprise in Q4 2025 ($-1.54 actual vs. $-0.35 estimated). The misses are largely explained by purchase-accounting distortions and the Leer South fire, but the pattern means consensus estimates carry low credibility. Current-quarter estimates have been revised down 55% in 90 days, and next-quarter estimates are down 87% over 90 days.

## Growth Analysis

Growth at CNR is defined by asset normalization and commodity price recovery, not organic business expansion.

**Catalysts for Improvement:**
1. **Leer South full-year contribution.** The mine resumed longwall operations in December 2025 and entered 2026 at targeted rates. A full year of production at 3.5-4.0M tons would add $35-60M in segment EBITDA at current met prices.
2. **Met coal price recovery.** Premium low-vol coal has rallied ~25% from December lows to ~$250/mt. With 4.3M unpriced tons, every $10/mt improvement in realization adds approximately $40M in revenue.
3. **Synergy realization.** G&A guided to $85-$100M cash basis in 2026, down from $215M in 2025, capturing merger synergies.
4. **Section 45X tax credit.** The 2.5% production cost credit on metallurgical coal (2026-2029) is worth an estimated $18-$22M annually.
5. **Purchase-accounting DD&A normalization.** DD&A will remain elevated ($600-$650M guidance for 2026) but the non-cash overhang will diminish over time, improving GAAP comparisons.

**Headwinds:**
- U.S. domestic thermal demand is supported near-term (AI/data center power demand, coal fleet below 50% utilization) but faces secular decline longer-term as retirements proceed.
- PRB operates at razor-thin margins and is being managed as a cash-generating wind-down.
- The met coal market is cyclical and currently in surplus, with recovery depending on Australian supply rationalization and Southeast Asian steel demand.

Consensus next-year EPS estimate is $8.49, up 8% over 30 days, suggesting some positive revision momentum on an annual basis. But the quarterly estimates paint a much more pessimistic near-term picture.

## Valuation Assessment

At $87 with approximately $512M trailing adjusted EBITDA and near-zero net debt, CNR trades at roughly 8.6x trailing EV/EBITDA. Against normalized EBITDA of $700-800M, the multiple compresses to 5.5-6.3x — cheap for a coal company with this balance sheet quality and asset diversification.

Peer comparisons are imperfect because CNR is uniquely diversified across met, thermal, and PRB coal with owned logistics. The broader energy peer group shows a median P/E of 13.3x, but CNR's negative trailing P/E is meaningless given the purchase-accounting distortions. On a forward basis, at $8.49 consensus FY2027 EPS, the stock trades at 10.2x — a premium to peers but reasonable given the superior balance sheet and logistics moat.

The analyst consensus target of $111.50 (low $105, high $116) implies 21-34% upside. All four analysts rate it Buy with no Holds or Sells. This unanimity also means there is no bearish institutional voice providing a check on the thesis.

The key valuation question is whether GAAP profitability can normalize quickly enough to attract generalist investors. At current DD&A levels ($600-$650M guided), GAAP earnings will remain depressed relative to cash earnings for at least 2-3 more years. This is a cash-flow story that requires investors to look through reported earnings.

CEO Brock's sale at ~$101 in March 2026 and SVP Klein's sales at $105-$111 provide a rough upper bound for management's perception of fair value in the near term.

## Competitive Landscape

CNR has assembled the strongest competitive position in U.S. coal through the merger:

- **Scale:** 85-91M tons annual production capacity across thermal, met, and PRB — largest U.S. pure-play producer
- **Cost position:** PAMC's cash costs of $38-$39.50/ton are among the lowest for high-CV thermal coal in North America
- **Logistics:** The Baltimore Marine Terminal, plus connectivity to Gulf and West Coast ports, provides export optionality that pure domestic producers lack. CNR can redirect volume between domestic and international markets as relative pricing shifts.
- **Product diversification:** The only major U.S. producer spanning all three coal product categories (met, high-CV thermal, PRB). This provides natural diversification against any single commodity price cycle.

Competitor comparison: Peabody Energy (BTU) is larger in thermal but lacks CNR's met portfolio. Warrior Met Coal (HCC) is a pure-play met producer without thermal diversification or export terminal ownership. Alliance Resource Partners (ARLP) is smaller and entirely domestic-focused.

The merger has improved CNR's competitive trajectory. Whether the coal industry's secular decline in U.S. thermal demand offsets these positioning advantages over a multi-year horizon remains an open analytical question.

## Risk Assessment

**Leer South Operational Continuity (HIGH IMPACT, MODERATE PROBABILITY)**

The mine experienced a combustion event on January 13, 2025 that shut it for essentially all of 2025. A CO spike forced re-evacuation in June. The mine resumed longwall operations in December 2025 and entered 2026 at targeted rates, but underground combustion risks remain. A recurrence would eliminate the primary source of 2026 earnings improvement. This was flagged as a NEW risk factor in the 10-Q.

**Met Coal Price Cyclicality (HIGH IMPACT, MODERATE PROBABILITY)**

The met segment generated negative EBITDA in 9M 2025. While prices have rallied, the market remains in surplus. A reversal back toward 2025 lows (~$180-$200/mt) would keep the met segment near break-even. With 4.3M unpriced met tons, high leverage cuts both ways.

**Trade Policy / Export Risk (MODERATE IMPACT)**

With 56% of coal revenue from exports, retaliatory tariffs or trade restrictions could reduce demand for U.S. coal. This was added as a new risk factor in the most recent filings.

**Legacy Liabilities**

$492M in asset retirement obligations, $250M in pneumoconiosis benefits, $209M in post-retirement health benefits, $74M in workers' compensation, plus the UMWA 1992 Benefit Plan lawsuit alleging $64.8M in unpaid premiums plus $25.6M in interest/damages (no accrual recorded; company vigorously defending). These are long-duration obligations but represent a meaningful drag on equity value.

**Cybersecurity Incident**

An Item 1.05 8-K was filed January 12, 2026, indicating a material cybersecurity event. Details remain limited, and the financial impact is unquantified.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($105-$116, +20-33%)

Leer South operates at full capacity through 2026, met coal benchmark prices sustain above $230/mt, and the met segment swings from break-even to $150-250M EBITDA. Combined with stable High CV Thermal earnings ($350-400M segment EBITDA) and synergy capture, total adjusted EBITDA reaches $750-850M. At 5.5-6.0x EV/EBITDA, enterprise value reaches $4.1-5.1B; with near-zero net debt, equity value per share reaches $80-100. On a normalized EPS basis, $8-$10 at 12x = $96-$120. The aggressive buyback ($776M remaining) provides a floor mechanism by reducing share count 5-8% annually. The Section 45X tax credit and favorable trade dynamics (India cement demand, Southeast Asian steel) provide incremental support.

### Bear Case ($65-$72, -18-25%)

Leer South experiences another combustion event or fails to sustain targeted production rates. Met coal prices revert toward 2025 lows (~$180-$200/mt), and the met segment remains near break-even. PRB margins compress further on declining domestic demand. Export demand weakens on trade policy disruption. EBITDA remains around $500-550M, and the stock trades at 7-8x — fair for a coal producer without demonstrated earnings growth. GAAP losses persist, deterring generalist capital. The $87 price already embeds meaningful skepticism, which limits downside, but the stock's 52-week low of $63.36 shows the market is willing to go lower.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of met segment EBITDA exceeding $50M, confirming Leer South operational sustainability and met price translation
2. Consensus current-year EPS estimate stabilizing and reversing the downward revision trend (30-day revision turning positive)
3. Stock pullback to $75-$78 (8.5-9.0x normalized EBITDA), which would provide a margin of safety sufficient to compensate for execution risk

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Leer South production halt or material operational disruption lasting >30 days
2. Met coal benchmark prices falling below $190/mt (sustained 60+ days)
3. Management cutting the buyback authorization or signaling reduced capital returns

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected May 2026) is the critical data point. Leer South's first full-quarter contribution will be visible, and Q1 2026 met segment margins will provide the clearest signal yet on whether the normalization thesis is playing out.

## Conclusion

Core Natural Resources holds the best competitive position in U.S. coal production: lowest-cost thermal operations, a diversified met portfolio, owned export logistics, and a fortress balance sheet. The stock at $87 already reflects partial credit for a normalization that has been promised but not yet delivered. Three-of-four quarterly EPS misses, sharply declining near-term estimates, and insider selling at $100-$110 all argue for patience.

The base case fair value is $95-$105 (11-13x normalized EPS of $8-$8.50, cross-checked against 6.0-6.5x $700-750M normalized EBITDA). That represents 9-21% upside from current levels — attractive but not exceptional given the execution risks around Leer South and met coal prices. The downside to $65-$72 in the bear case (18-25%) means the risk/reward is roughly symmetric at current prices.

HOLD with a growth orientation. The next two quarters will determine whether this becomes a high-conviction BUY as normalization materializes in actual reported numbers.
