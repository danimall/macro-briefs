## Executive Summary

Brookfield Corporation is a hybrid: roughly two-thirds of its distributable earnings come from a capital-light asset-management franchise and an insurance spread business, and roughly one-third from distributions received on principal stakes in listed infrastructure, renewables, industrial and real-estate platforms. The recurring portion is compounding. Distributable earnings before realizations (DE-BR) grew from $2.05/share in FY2024 to $2.27 in FY2025 (+10.7%) and $2.32 on an LTM basis through Q1 2026 (+6.4%), on fee-bearing capital of $614B (+12% YoY) and insurance assets that stepped from $143B to roughly $180B when the Just Group acquisition closed after Q1 quarter-end.

The complication is that total distributable earnings have not grown. Total DE was $6,274M in FY2024, $6,008M in FY2025 and $6,009M LTM — flat for two years — because principal disposition gains fell from $1,000M to $62M to $3M. Realized carried interest actually improved ($403M → $560M → $528M). Management headlines the record recurring figure, which is the better run-rate metric, but an owner measuring cash generation sees a franchise that has been reinvesting rather than crystallizing. Meanwhile real estate distributions, about 11% of DE-BR, fell 29% LTM and 44% year over year in Q1 2026 — most plausibly because BN sold $26B of property, though the decomposition is not disclosed.

At $44.08 the shares trade at 19.0x LTM recurring DE, 17.4x total DE, 2.31x IFRS book value and a 33.2% discount to management's own $66 intrinsic-value mark. That discount was 47% when BN was repurchasing at an average $36 in 2025; it has compressed as the stock rose, and management's mark itself ticked down from $68 to $66 with no disclosed cause. Two unresolved items keep this from being an add: the BN/BNT combination that both shareholder bases approved on July 16, 2026 has undisclosed exchange terms, so dilution to existing holders cannot be sized; and the conversion to U.S. GAAP in Q1 2027 will likely remove fair-value accounting for the $85.7B investment-property book, with unquantified effect on a stock priced at 2.3x reported equity. Base case $50 (+13.4%), bull $60 (+36.1%), bear $34 (-22.9%). **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Brookfield Corporation is an Ontario-incorporated foreign private issuer reporting to the SEC on Form 40-F and 6-K under IFRS. It does not file 10-Ks, 10-Qs, 8-Ks or DEF 14As, and Section 16 insider reporting does not reach its executives. Three practical consequences follow: standard U.S. filing-text analysis is unavailable, multi-factor screens built on U.S. GAAP inputs are unreliable here, and **there is no insider-transaction signal for BN in either direction** — the Form 4 records surfacing under the ticker are BN and Brookfield Asset Management Ltd. reporting as owners of a third party's NAV-priced Class I fund shares at ~$10.36–10.43, not executives trading BN stock. The absence of insider buying carries no information.

Three core businesses:

**Asset Management (via listed BAM).** Fee-bearing capital $614B at March 31, 2026, +12% YoY. Fee-related earnings $3.0B in FY2025, +22%; +11% YoY in Q1 2026. FY2025 fundraising $112B including $24B from retail and wealth channels.

**Wealth Solutions (via listed BNT).** Insurance assets $143B at YE2025 rising to ~$180B post-Just Group; book equity $13.2B generating ~$2.0B of annualized cash flow at a stated 15% ROE. FY2025 annuity sales $20B, 85% written at five years or longer.

**Operating Businesses.** Distributions from BEP (energy/renewables), BIP (infrastructure), BBU/BBUC (private equity), BPG (real estate). Real estate operating metrics at Q1 2026 were solid: 96% super-core and 95% core-plus occupancy, 5.5M sq ft leased at office net rents 15% above expiring.

The single most important structural fact: **BN consolidates affiliates in which it owns economic minorities.** Of $165.6B of total equity at March 31, 2026, $118.9B (71.8%) is non-controlling interests and only $42.7B (25.8%) is common equity attributable to BN shareholders. In Q1 2026, $940M of $1,042M of consolidated net income (90.2%) accrued to NCI. Consolidated revenue, assets and debt materially overstate what BN owners hold; consolidated net income materially understates their economics after $10.4B of annual IFRS depreciation.

## Financial Analysis

### The revenue decline is a margin-free divestiture, not deterioration

Audited annual revenue from SEC XBRL (ifrs-full:Revenue, CIK 1001085): $95,924M (FY2023) → $86,006M (FY2024) → $75,100M (FY2025), a cumulative 21.7% decline. Any screen keying on top-line growth will mis-rate this company badly. The reconciling arithmetic:

| US$M | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 86,006 | 75,100 | −10,906 |
| Direct costs (ex-D&A) | 58,199 | 46,594 | −11,605 |
| **Revenue less direct costs** | **27,807** | **28,506** | **+699 (+2.5%)** |

Revenue fell $10.9B while direct costs fell $11.6B, so gross spread *rose* $699M. The lost revenue carried approximately zero contribution margin. Q1 2026 revenue then grew 3.5% to $18,580M from $17,944M. The specific driver — sale versus deconsolidation versus sell-down into equity accounting — is inference rather than established fact; the segment note was not accessible. Equity-accounted investments rose $11.6B during 2025 while investment properties fell $18.1B, which is consistent with sell-downs to equity-accounted stakes. The margin arithmetic holds regardless of mechanism.

I underwrite the recurring $2.32 and treat realizations as optional upside, because a $1.0B disposition-gain year is not a repeatable annuity and the company chose not to generate one in FY2025 or LTM.

A same-store distribution decline cannot be ruled out. **Wealth Solutions DE was exactly flat YoY at $430M**, plausibly a timing artifact since Just Group's $40B closed after quarter-end, plausibly spread compression — undetermined. **Corporate cost savings supplied roughly $77M of the $92M YoY increase in Q1 DE-BR.** That is cost control, and it is not repeatable indefinitely.

### IFRS earnings and the 87x headline

Net income attributable to BN shareholders: $641M (FY2024) → $1,307M (FY2025); LTM diluted EPS ~$0.50, matching the $0.487 trailing EPS in the market data. The 87x trailing P/E in the data feed — reported identically as "forward" P/E, which is a mapping artifact rather than a forward estimate — is an IFRS figure that should not be used for valuation. The FY2025 reconciliation bridges $3,235M of consolidated net income to $5,386M of DE-BR by adding back $10,379M of D&A, $3,503M of equity-accounted fair-value changes and $2,189M of other fair-value changes, then subtracting $10,860M attributable to NCI. Note also that the consensus EPS estimates in the earnings-surprise record ($0.64 estimate versus $0.66 actual for Q1 2026) are on a DE-per-share basis, not IFRS — the two must not be compared.

The Corporation's recourse debt has been static at $14.2–14.3B across five quarters, at a 15-year weighted-average term, A− or equivalent senior unsecured across major agencies, and an implied average cost of roughly 5.2% ($742M FY2025 interest against a near-flat $14.3B balance). Corporate debt to common equity is 33.4%. BN funded a decade of growth without adding recourse leverage, which is genuinely unusual for an entity of this scale and is the strongest single element of the financial-health case.

Group indebtedness is growing elsewhere: non-recourse borrowings of managed entities rose $24.8B in 2025 and a further $4.2B in Q1 2026 to $249.5B. Lender recourse runs to specific assets and managed entities, not the Corporation, so this is not BN leverage — but Operating Businesses DE depends on those entities servicing that debt. Financing access appears strong ($175B of financings franchise-wide in FY2025, $45B in Q1 2026), so this is manageable rather than acute.

Two cautions on liquidity language. "Deployable capital of $188B" comprises $74B of cash, financial assets and undrawn lines aggregated across the Corporation, affiliates **and** the wealth solutions business — three separate creditor groups — plus $114B of uncalled *client* fund commitments. Neither figure is Corporation-level liquidity, which is not disclosed. Separately, common equity fell $1,099M in Q1 2026 despite $102M of attributable net income; $598M was returned to shareholders, and the residual ~$600M is presumably OCI (FX and revaluation), unverified.

Book value per share is $19.05 on basic weighted shares ($18.54 on shares and equivalents), so the stock trades at 2.31x IFRS book. That book fair-values investment properties but carries the asset-management franchise near historical cost, so it is not comparable to a U.S. GAAP financial's book.

## Growth Analysis

The recurring earnings base is growing at 6–11% per share, and the composition is improving in quality. Fee-bearing capital +12%, FRE +22% in FY2025 decelerating to +11% in Q1 2026, insurance assets +26% to ~$180B, and a $13B FY2025 / $4B Q1 2026 insurance deployment program at 8.5% realized / ~10% targeted yields. BAM's Q2 2026 report (secondary source, unverified against a filing) cited a record $77B raised and AUM above $1T, which reads through constructively to BN's Asset Management DE but says nothing about Wealth Solutions or real estate.

Three qualifications temper the growth case.

**Fundraising mix has shifted toward affiliated capital.** Of $67B raised in 2026 through mid-May, $40B (60%) was an investment mandate from Just Group — the U.K. insurer Brookfield itself acquired — and only $5B came from retail and wealth clients, against $24B for full-year 2025. The fees are economically real and this flywheel is standard in the sector (Apollo–Athene, KKR–Global Atlantic), but $40B of self-generated mandate is not third-party franchise validation and should not be extrapolated as a run rate. The 2026 retail/wealth pace is the number to watch, because that channel is the industry's growth vector.

**Accumulated unrealized carried interest is converting slowly.** The balance was $11.6B at YE2025 and $11.8B at Q1 2026; realizations into income were $560M in FY2025 and $528M LTM — roughly 4.5–4.8% per year. Management states it is "well positioned to realize significant carried interest into income over the next three years," which implies sharp acceleration. This balance is explicitly unrealized: it is not backlog, not deferred revenue, not a remaining performance obligation, and it remains reversible until crystallized. Carry realization is exit-driven and non-linear, so acceleration is plausible against $91B of FY2025 monetizations — but it cannot be underwritten.

**Real estate is shrinking by design and dragging reported DE.** If the 29% LTM decline is portfolio shrinkage, the per-dollar economics are intact and proceeds are being redeployed at higher returns. If it is same-store distribution erosion, roughly 11% of recurring DE is impaired. The occupancy and re-leasing data favor the first reading.

The structural growth vector I find most interesting is energy and AI infrastructure, where owning generation is a real advantage over capital-light managers. The disclosed Google hydroelectric supply agreement is clean — Brookfield sells power, Google offtakes. The NVIDIA agreement is described only as supporting "the build-out of AI factories," with no disclosure of who pays whom, who owns the assets, or what is contracted; I assign it no value. The U.S. government nuclear-development agreement likewise has no disclosed terms. BAM's inaugural AI infrastructure fund is the vehicle through which this converts to fee-bearing capital, and it is too early to size.

Nineteen times recurring distributable earnings for a franchise compounding recurring DE per share at 6–11%, with an A− corporate balance sheet and a 12%-of-DE dividend payout, is a fair-to-modestly-attractive multiple against large-cap alternative managers, which have generally traded from the high teens to the mid-twenties on comparable earnings measures. It is not obviously cheap. The PEG-equivalent on recurring DE growth of roughly 8% is around 2.4x, which requires either an acceleration in carry realization or multiple expansion to work.

The mispricing argument rests on the discount to intrinsic value, and it has weakened materially. Management repurchased at an average $36 in 2025 against a stated $68 mark (a 47% discount) and at an average $41 in 2026 against $66 (38%). At $44.08 the discount is 33.2%. Two features of that series deserve weight: the gap has closed by roughly 14 points in two quarters as the price rose, and management's own estimate declined 2.9% while recurring earnings grew. No cause for the $68 → $66 revision is disclosed; benign explanations (marks on listed affiliate stakes, FX, discount-rate updates) are plausible and I attribute no intent. But the input is self-assessed by the same party deciding whether to buy the stock, which limits how much weight it can carry.

Sell-side is applying its own haircut to management's mark: TD Securities $60 (raised from $59 on May 19, 2026), J.P. Morgan $56 (reaffirmed Buy, ~July 23, 2026), consensus mean $54.20 and median $56.00, with a high of $61 and a low of $31. Ten of eleven ratings are Buy or Strong Buy with one Sell — and the $31 low target, 30% below spot, marks the dispersion honestly. On a stock trading at 19x recurring earnings rather than at a premium multiple, I treat the mean target as a reasonable if lagging anchor rather than a ceiling; I do not treat it as independent confirmation of the thesis.

**Scenario framework.**

- **Base $50 (+13.4%).** FY2027 DE-BR of $2.55–2.60/share (recurring growth of 10–12% from $2.32 over six to seven quarters, driven by the Just Group step-up in Wealth Solutions, low-double-digit FRE growth, and stabilizing real estate distributions) at 19–20x. $2.60 × 19.2 = $49.9.
- **Bull $60 (+36.1%).** DE-BR of $2.75 at 21x, plus carry realization accelerating toward $1.0B+ annually (roughly $0.30/share of additional total DE) and the discount to management's mark narrowing to ~10%. Consistent with TD's $60 target.
- **Bear $34 (−22.9%).** Recurring DE-BR flat to down at $2.25 on continued real estate distribution erosion and insurance spread compression, at 15x = $33.8. Cross-check: if the U.S. GAAP transition reduces book value per share from ~$19 to ~$16, 2.1x that book is also ~$34.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives an expected return of roughly +8% over twelve months. Positive, but against 28% realized volatility and two unquantified structural items, that does not clear an ACCUMULATE bar. Valuation scores mid-range: the discount is real on management's numbers, but it is no longer wide, the input is self-marked, and the multiple on recurring earnings is unremarkable.

## Competitive Landscape

BN sits among the four or five largest global alternative-asset platforms by fee-bearing capital ($614B at BN's level; BAM AUM reported above $1T), paired with a ~$180B insurance balance sheet and direct ownership of listed infrastructure, renewables, industrial and real-estate platforms. The closest structural comparables are Blackstone, KKR, Apollo and Ares, but the comparison is imperfect in a way that matters for the multiple: pure-play managers are valued on fee-related earnings, which are capital-light and command high multiples, while BN's DE blends FRE, insurance spread and affiliate distributions. The ongoing simplification program — BBU/BBUC already converted, BN/BNT approved, BIP/BIPC and BEP/BEPC under evaluation — is management's answer to exactly this discount, on the explicit reasoning that "the dominance of index investing... reinforced our view that simpler structures with larger market capitalizations are now the most effective way to position these businesses."

Industry conditions in the disclosures are favorable and specific enough to be credible: FY2025 saw $91B of monetizations, $126B deployed and $175B of financings, with the repeated assertion that "substantially all sales were completed at or above our carrying values." That claim is management's characterization and unaudited, but it is falsifiable and directly rebuts the long-standing bear argument that IFRS marks on private real assets are stale. Real estate credit markets have clearly reopened for quality assets — the Two Manhattan West 107bp spread, a $1.5B term-loan refinancing, a $1.2B logistics CMBS, a €500M German office bond.

The trajectory reading is mixed rather than clearly improving. Fee-bearing capital growth is holding at +12% and the energy/AI-infrastructure position is a genuine structural advantage. Against that, FRE growth decelerated from +22% to +11%, and the retail/wealth channel — where the franchise competes most directly for the industry's incremental dollar — contributed $24B in FY2025 but only $5B through mid-May 2026.

## Risk Assessment

**BN/BNT combination terms are undisclosed.** Both shareholder bases approved the transaction on July 16, 2026 (corroborated across three independent outlets). The exchange ratio, share issuance and therefore dilution to existing BN holders are unknown to me. BNT was created as a paired security to BN, which *may* imply near-economic-neutrality, but that is unverified and the arithmetic is material to per-share value. This is the largest single open item in the analysis. Whether closing conditions remain outstanding is also unconfirmed.

**U.S. GAAP adoption from Q1 2027.** IFRS carries $85.7B of investment properties at fair value; U.S. GAAP generally does not permit that for real estate held for use. My inference is that reported assets, equity and book value per share could decline materially, while earnings volatility from fair-value marks diminishes and peer comparability improves. The magnitude depends on transition elections and how much property sits in equity-accounted vehicles. No company quantification exists. For a stock at 2.31x reported book this is a mechanical repricing risk, not a change in economics — but reported book is one of the anchors bears will use.

**Insurance risk migrating onto the Corporation's balance sheet.** Management states the combination will let it deploy "an incremental approximately $145 billion of cash, equities, real estate, and other investments" to support insurance growth. Read literally, parent capital backing policyholder obligations would include equities and real estate, raising correlation between investment-portfolio drawdowns and insurance capital adequacy. Management's counter-rationale — removing an inefficient double-holding structure, lowering cost of capital, formalizing economics BN already bore under the paired-security arrangement — is coherent. No regulatory-capital disclosure was available to test either view.

**Real estate distribution decline is not decomposed.** Roughly 11% of DE-BR is at issue if the driver is same-store cash-flow deterioration rather than portfolio shrinkage.

**Structural analytical hazard.** With 71.8% of consolidated equity in NCI and 90.2% of Q1 net income accruing to NCI, any consolidated metric mis-sizes the equity. This is permanent, not a discrete risk.

**Governance is unverified.** No management information circular was obtained: no CEO compensation, pay-versus-performance, board independence, related-party schedule, or share-class/voting-control data. BN's dual-class arrangements are widely described in secondary commentary but were not verified from a filing, and I do not assert them. The 40-F risk-factor narrative, MD&A and legal proceedings were also unread (EDGAR archive access blocked), so company-specific litigation exposure is unexamined. Among the 25 enumerated forward-looking-statement factors, one is notable for this structure: "the existence of information barriers between certain businesses within our asset management operations" — the conflict-management architecture of a firm that simultaneously manages third-party funds, owns an insurer investing into those funds, and holds principal positions.

**Macro.** Interest-rate and FX fluctuations and inflationary pressure are enumerated by management among its risk factors, and the availability of equity and debt financing/refinancing is a direct input to the alternatives-manager multiple and to real-estate cap rates. Higher-for-longer rates also raise the bar on the insurance spread business's reinvestment assumptions, though they help annuity pricing. [Specific macro figures — a Fed stance, a 30-year yield level — are not traceable to the provided sources and are omitted.]

Offsetting all of the above: the non-recourse structure genuinely insulates the Corporation, corporate debt is flat with a 15-year average term and A− ratings, there is no near-term recourse maturity cliff in evidence, and short interest is trivial at 15.8M shares, roughly 0.7% of shares outstanding.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 30.1% against 28.0% one-year realized — a 2.1-point spread the data characterizes as fair, so there is neither a cheap-vol entry edge nor evidence of extreme priced uncertainty. The term structure is backwardated (30.1% at 10 days, 34.2% at 38 days, 25.4% at 157 days), which is straightforwardly explained by the August 13 Q2 print falling inside the front two tenors; the 38-day tenor prices a ±9.4% move. The near-term skew of 9.7% is characterized as elevated put demand into that event. That does not extend across the surface: put/call volume is 0.41 and put/call open interest 0.29, both call-tilted, and the six-month skew carries the opposite sign (−10.0%). The composite read is event hedging around a specific earnings date rather than a directional bearish position, which is consistent with a HOLD and argues against paying up immediately ahead of August 13.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($60, +36%)

The recurring franchise compounds at 10%+ per share as Just Group's $40B of insurance assets contribute a full run rate to Wealth Solutions (which was flat YoY in Q1 only because the deal closed after quarter-end), FRE growth reaccelerates toward the FY2025 +22% pace on BAM's reported record $77B fundraising quarter and >$1T AUM, and corporate cost discipline holds. Carry realization accelerates as management guides, converting even 10–12% of the $11.8B accumulated balance annually — roughly $1.2–1.4B versus $528M LTM — restoring total DE growth and re-anchoring the multiple. The BN/BNT combination proves near-economically-neutral, simplifies the story for index and generalist capital, and closes part of the discount to management's $66 mark. Buybacks continue at prices management deems 30%+ below intrinsic value. Owned hydro, nuclear and infrastructure convert the AI-power demand cycle into fee-bearing capital at attractive economics. $2.75 of DE-BR at 21x supports $58, and a narrower discount to intrinsic value carries it to $60 — matching TD's target and 91% of management's own mark.

### Bear Case ($34, −23%)

Total distributable earnings stay pinned near $6.0B for a third year: real estate distributions keep eroding, insurance spread compresses as the reinvestment yield advantage narrows, and carry realization continues at ~5% per year rather than accelerating, revealing the recurring-DE growth story as partly a mix effect from shrinking a lower-multiple business. The U.S. GAAP transition strips fair-value accounting from the $85.7B property book, cutting reported equity and removing the book-value floor bears currently must argue against. The BN/BNT exchange ratio proves dilutive by a mid-single-digit percentage of existing shares, resetting per-share metrics. Rates stay higher for longer, the alternatives complex derates further on redemption-gate and NAV-mark concerns, and the retail/wealth fundraising channel stays at the 2026 pace rather than the 2025 pace, exposing how much of headline capital formation was self-generated. $2.25 of DE-BR at 15x is $34, consistent with the $31 low sell-side target and 10% below the 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** The thesis is per-share earnings compounding, not yield — the $0.28 dividend is 12.1% of recurring DE and yields 0.635%.

**Targets from $44.08:**
- Base case **$50** (+13.4%) — 19.2x estimated FY2027 DE-before-realizations of $2.60
- Bull case **$60** (+36.1%) — 21x $2.75 plus accelerated carry realization and discount narrowing
- Bear case **$34** (−22.9%) — 15x $2.25, cross-checked against ~2.1x a post-transition book of ~$16

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, spanning the August 13, 2026 Q2 print, the BN/BNT combination close, and the Q1 2027 U.S. GAAP transition.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY on any of:**
1. Disclosure of BN/BNT exchange terms showing dilution below roughly 2% of existing shares, combined with a Wealth Solutions DE step-up above $470M in a quarter (from $430M) confirming the Just Group contribution.
2. Realized carried interest running above $250M in a single quarter (roughly $1.0B annualized, versus $528M LTM), which would restore total DE growth and justify a higher multiple.
3. Price toward **$38–40** on unchanged recurring earnings, which would restore a 40%+ discount to management's mark and put the multiple near 16–17x recurring DE with the base case ~25% above spot.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. Real estate DE falling below $100M in a quarter with occupancy or re-leasing spreads deteriorating, which would convert the portfolio-shrinkage explanation into a cash-flow-erosion explanation for ~11% of recurring earnings.
2. BN/BNT exchange terms implying dilution above roughly 5% of existing shares, or a further downward revision of management's intrinsic-value mark below $64 while recurring earnings grow.
3. Corporate borrowings rising materially above $16B, or a ratings action below A−, either of which would remove the balance-sheet pillar of the case.
4. Q1 2027 U.S. GAAP restatement reducing common equity by more than roughly 20% without an offsetting narrative on earnings quality.

**Reassessment date:** August 13, 2026 (Q2 2026 results), with a second checkpoint at the combination close and a third at the first U.S. GAAP reporting period.

## Conclusion

Brookfield's recurring earnings engine works. Distributable earnings before realizations reached $2.32 per share LTM on $614B of fee-bearing capital, a ~$180B insurance balance sheet earning a stated 15% ROE, and affiliate distributions — all supported by a Corporation balance sheet that has carried a flat $14.3B of A−-rated recourse debt at a 15-year average term through a decade of growth. The 21.7% consolidated revenue decline since FY2023 is a divestiture of essentially zero-margin activity, verified by the fact that direct costs fell more than revenue did; screens keying on the top line will read this company wrong.

What keeps the rating at HOLD rather than higher is the combination of a discount that has already substantially closed and two unquantified structural events. Total distributable earnings have been flat at roughly $6.0B for two years because the company generated $3M of principal disposition gains in the last twelve months against $1.0B two years ago, and the $11.8B of accumulated unrealized carry is converting at under 5% per year against management's guidance of significant acceleration. The discount to management's self-assessed intrinsic value has narrowed from 47% to 33% while that self-assessed value itself declined from $68 to $66. Nineteen times recurring earnings for 6–11% recurring growth is fair, not cheap. And the BN/BNT combination — already approved by both shareholder bases — has undisclosed exchange terms, while the Q1 2027 U.S. GAAP conversion is likely to reduce reported book value for a stock priced at 2.31x that book.

Expected return of roughly +8% probability-weighted, with base-case upside of 13% against bear-case downside of 23%, does not offer the asymmetry required to add. Existing owners should hold through August 13 and the combination close; prospective buyers get a materially better entry at $38–40, or on confirmation that the exchange ratio is near-neutral and Just Group is contributing to Wealth Solutions earnings.
