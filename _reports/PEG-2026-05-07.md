## Executive Summary

Public Service Enterprise Group operates two complementary businesses: PSE&G, a regulated electric and gas utility serving 74% of New Jersey's population, and PSEG Power, a nuclear generation fleet of 3,758 MW operating at 91.2% capacity factors. The company benefits from two structural tailwinds: massive infrastructure investment needed to accommodate data center and electrification load growth in one of the most densely populated U.S. service territories, and the revaluation of carbon-free nuclear baseload generation in PJM's capacity-constrained market.

At $79.35, the stock trades at 18.9x forward earnings versus the electric utility peer median of 24.3x, offering a 3.4% dividend yield with 5-7% operating EPS growth visibility through 2030. The discount reflects legitimate concerns: (1) New Jersey regulators are blocking the nuclear-to-data-center contracting model that re-rated Constellation Energy, (2) interest expense rose 60% from 2022-2025 and will continue rising as the $24-28B capex program requires external debt financing, and (3) PJM's unprecedented capacity market failure is provoking political responses that could restructure the market in unpredictable ways. The stock is reasonably valued for an income-oriented holding but lacks a clear catalyst to close the peer valuation gap in the near term.

## Company Overview

PSEG is a diversified energy holding company headquartered in Newark, New Jersey, operating through two segments:

**PSE&G (Regulated Utility, ~83% of net income):** Serves 2.4 million electric and 1.9 million gas customers across ~2,600 square miles of northern and central New Jersey. Operates under BPU-approved distribution rates (9.6% ROE, 55% equity) and FERC-approved transmission formula rates (9.90% base ROE + 50bp RTO adder). The Conservation Incentive Program decouples >90% of distribution margin from volumetric sales, insulating earnings from weather and conservation effects.

**PSEG Power (Nuclear Generation, ~17% of net income):** Operates Salem 1, Salem 2, Hope Creek (100% owned, NJ) and Peach Bottom 2/3 (50% owned, PA). Total capacity 3,758 MW. Revenue from PJM wholesale energy, capacity, and ancillary services. Nuclear Production Tax Credit provides $15/MWh floor protection through 2032 (currently out-of-the-money as market prices exceed the threshold).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:**
Operating EPS (non-GAAP) grew from $3.47 (2022) to $4.05 (2025), a 5.3% CAGR. GAAP EPS is distorted by mark-to-market effects ($5.13 in 2023 on MTM gains; $3.54 in 2024 on MTM losses). Forward consensus sits at $4.37 for FY2026 and $4.69 for FY2027, implying 7.9% and 7.3% growth respectively.

**Cash Flow Profile:**
Operating cash flow improved materially to $3,298M in 2025 (up $1,165M YoY), but capex of $3,272M consumed virtually all of it, leaving free cash flow of approximately $26M. The $1.26B annual dividend is effectively funded through a combination of operating cash flow and incremental debt issuance — a standard but not risk-free utility model during a high-capex cycle.

**Balance Sheet:**
Long-term debt expanded from $16.5B (2022) to $21.7B (2025). Debt-to-equity of 3.39x is high even for a regulated utility. Interest expense reached $1,005M in 2025, up 60% from $628M in 2022. With the planned $4.6-4.8B annual capex (2026-2028), additional debt issuance of $1.5-2.0B annually is likely, pushing interest expense toward $1.2-1.4B by 2027-2028.

**Rate Base Growth:**
Rate base grew to approximately $36B at year-end 2025, with management guiding to 6.0-7.5% CAGR through 2030 ($22.5-25.5B regulated capex over five years). This rate base growth is the primary earnings driver, as allowed ROEs are applied to the growing investment base.

## Growth Analysis

PSEG's growth profile is anchored by three drivers with differing timelines:

**Near-term (1-2 years):** Rate base growth from ongoing T&D infrastructure programs drives PSE&G earnings growth of 6-8% annually. The October 2024 distribution rate case settlement (9.6% ROE) provides visibility. Transmission formula rates adjust mechanically with investment. PSEG Power benefits from elevated PJM energy and capacity prices — the December 2025 capacity auction cleared at the $333.44/MW-day cap (which would have been $530 without the cap), providing substantial revenue uplift.

**Medium-term (2-4 years):** Load growth from data centers and electrification in PSEG's service territory. Electric customer growth of 0.9% annually has room to accelerate if data center construction proceeds. Salem Unit 1 and 2 power uprates add generation capacity at low marginal cost. PJM's "reliability backstop auction" (September 2026) could provide 15-year forward price certainty for nuclear generation.

**Long-term (5+ years):** New Jersey's April 2026 lifting of the nuclear moratorium enables advanced nuclear development on PSEG's existing sites. This creates optionality for dedicated data center nuclear generation without diverting existing assets from ratepayers — resolving the regulatory tension that blocked near-term data center deals.

**Headwinds to growth:** Estimate revisions are modestly negative (current year -0.7% over 90 days, next year -0.7%), reflecting the Jefferies downgrade thesis and interest expense headwinds. The current quarter estimate has been cut 3.7% in 30 days.

## Valuation Assessment

At $79.35, PEG trades at 18.9x trailing and forward P/E, a significant discount to the peer median of 24.3x:

| Metric | PEG | Peer Median |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| P/E | 18.9x | 24.3x |
| Dividend Yield | 3.3% | 3.3% |
| 52-wk Discount | -13% | -6% |

The discount widened following Jefferies' April downgrade and reflects the market's recalibration of the nuclear data center premium. Constellation Energy (CEG) trades at substantially higher multiples due to confirmed data center contracts; PSEG's inability to replicate that model in NJ's regulatory environment has created a valuation gap.

The discount appears modestly excessive. PSEG's 6-7.5% rate base CAGR is above-average for the sector, its nuclear fleet generates earnings at high margins with PTC downside protection, and the territory is positioned for structural load growth. The analyst consensus target of $90.64 (median $90.50) implies 14% upside, with a range of $73-$102.

At 18.9x on $4.37 FY2026 consensus EPS, a move to 20x (still below peers) would imply ~$87. A move to peer median 24x is unlikely without a data center deal catalyst but would imply ~$105.

The valuation is neither cheap enough to compel aggressive buying nor expensive enough to warrant selling. It reflects a regulated utility growing earnings at mid-single-digits with dividend support and some dormant optionality.

## Competitive Landscape

**Regulated position: Stable to improving.** PSE&G operates in one of the most attractive utility territories in the United States. Northern/central New Jersey combines high population density, affluent demographics (commercial loads = 57% of electric sales), growing customer counts, and proximity to the data center corridor developing along the Eastern Seaboard. The franchise is a natural monopoly with essentially zero competition risk.

**Nuclear position: Structurally advantaged but competitively constrained.** PSEG Power's nuclear assets are carbon-free, baseload-capable, and sitting in a capacity-constrained region. PJM's failure to procure adequate capacity in December 2025 (6,625 MW shortfall) structurally benefits existing generators. Compared to Constellation Energy (~21 reactors across multiple states), PSEG's 3,758 MW in NJ faces state-specific regulatory constraints that limit premium contracting opportunities.

**Data center opportunity: Behind peers.** Constellation has signed a deal with Microsoft (Three Mile Island restart) and other hyperscaler deals. PSEG's "exploring opportunities" language from the 10-K has not materialized into announced contracts. NJ's ratepayer protection stance limits hyperscaler interest in contracting with existing assets. The nuclear moratorium lift (April 2026) creates a longer-term path but puts PSEG years behind Constellation on this theme.

**PJM capacity market: Shared tailwind.** All generators in PJM benefit from structural capacity shortage. This supports PSEG Power's earnings base but is not a competitive differentiator.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Regulatory Affordability Backlash (High Impact, Medium Probability)**
PJM capacity costs flowing through to retail bills have triggered legislative moratoriums, BPU-directed credits, and political scrutiny. The 10-K explicitly flags this as potentially impacting "business, strategy, growth rates, cash flows." If regulators restrict cost recovery or impose rate caps, PSE&G's allowed ROE or investment recovery could be compressed.

**2. Interest Expense Escalation (High Impact, High Probability)**
Virtually certain to continue given the capex program and near-zero FCF. By 2028, interest expense likely consumes ~30% of operating income versus ~24% today, directly pressuring EPS growth.

**3. Nuclear Data Center Deal Failure (Medium Impact, Medium Probability)**
If PSEG cannot monetize its nuclear assets at premium rates through data center contracts, the stock will continue trading at a discount to Constellation and nuclear peers. The market currently prices minimal premium for this optionality, so downside is limited but the upside catalyst is absent.

**4. PJM Market Reform (High Impact, Medium Probability)**
The "reliability backstop auction" with 15-year pricing could restructure how all PJM generators earn revenue. Depending on design, this could benefit (long-term price certainty for nuclear) or harm (if existing assets are not eligible for new contract structures) PSEG Power.

**5. FERC RTO Adder Elimination (~$40M annual impact)**
FERC has proposed eliminating the 50bp RTO membership adder. This would reduce PSE&G net income by approximately $40M annually — meaningful but not thesis-changing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90-102, +13% to +29%):**
- Rate base CAGR of 7.5% compounds through 2030, driving consistent PSE&G earnings growth
- PJM capacity market structure provides sustained elevated pricing for nuclear fleet
- Nuclear moratorium lift leads to announced data center partnership (3-5 year timeline) that re-rates the stock toward Constellation-like multiples
- PJM reliability backstop auction (September 2026) provides 15-year price certainty for nuclear generation, unlocking higher valuation
- Reversion to peer median P/E of 24x on $4.37 EPS = $105

**Bear Case ($73-76, -4% to -8%):**
- NJ regulatory affordability response compresses PSE&G's allowed ROE or slows capex program approvals
- Rising interest costs offset rate base growth, flattening EPS trajectory
- Nuclear data center deals remain structurally blocked; market assigns no premium
- PJM capacity market reform reduces nuclear capacity revenues from current elevated levels
- De-rating to ~17x on $4.37 EPS = $74

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock falls below $73-74, creating >20% upside to consensus target with 3.6%+ yield
2. PSEG announces a signed data center power agreement (any structure) — this would justify re-rating toward 22-24x
3. PJM reliability backstop auction (September 2026) provides favorable long-term pricing structure for existing nuclear

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NJ BPU explicitly caps or reduces PSE&G's allowed ROE below 9.0%
2. Interest expense growth outpaces rate base earnings growth for two consecutive quarters
3. Management materially reduces the 2026-2030 capex program (signaling regulatory obstruction)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely July 2026 for Q2), September 2026 PJM reliability backstop auction, and any announced nuclear data center contract developments.

## Conclusion

PSEG is a well-run regulated utility with above-average rate base growth, a structurally advantaged nuclear fleet, and a premium service territory positioned for long-term load growth. At 18.9x forward earnings with a 3.3% yield, the stock trades at a meaningful discount to peers that reflects specific, legitimate concerns: NJ regulatory constraints on data center contracting, rising interest costs from a massive capex program, and capacity market uncertainty.

The discount is not large enough to represent a compelling buying opportunity absent a specific catalyst, and the negative estimate revision trend argues against aggressive positioning. The combination of above-inflation dividend growth, mid-single-digit earnings growth from rate base expansion, and dormant nuclear optionality makes this a reasonable income-oriented holding. HOLD with a bias toward upgrading on either valuation compression (below $73-74) or the emergence of a data center contracting pathway.
