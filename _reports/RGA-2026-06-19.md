**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Reinsurance Group of America is one of the world's largest pure-play life, health, and longevity reinsurers, with roughly $4.3 trillion of life reinsurance in force and $164.1 billion in total assets as of March 31, 2026. The franchise earns a 15-16% adjusted operating ROE — at or above its own intermediate-term targets — yet trades at 11.4x forward earnings and approximately 1.26x ex-AOCI book value. That gap between returns and price is the core of the investment case. A reinsurer compounding equity at mid-teens rates while trading near book typically re-rates as the market gains confidence in the durability of the earnings, and RGA's recent results support that confidence: net income roughly doubled from $717M (2024) to $1.18B (2025), and the strength persisted into Q1 2026 with beats on both reported and operating lines across all four geographic segments.

The setup is a quality/event-driven value case rather than a momentum trade. Current-year EPS estimates have risen 3.8% over the past 30 days and 3.6% over 90 days; the company raised its quarterly dividend to $0.93 and laid out a 2026 plan to deleverage ~$400M and return 20-30% of capital. The July 2025 Equitable Holdings reinsurance transaction drove a ~28% jump in assets and adds asset-intensive earnings power, while a 4.93% average investment yield (up from 4.64%) captures the higher-reinvestment-yield tailwind. The stock sits at ~$210.54, roughly 8% below its 52-week high of $229.21 but up ~7.7% over the trailing year. The low end of the analyst target range ($212) is essentially at spot, and the mean target ($252) implies ~20% upside.

The principal risk is the annual assumption review — typically conducted in Q3/Q4 — which can produce negative "notable items" surprises (TTM notable items were a -$114M after-tax drag; Q1 2026 had zero, flattering the YoY comparison). This is a known, lumpy, recurring event rather than a structural impairment. Against an 11.4x forward multiple, a mid-teens ROE, rising estimates, negligible 1.8% short interest, and a clean governance profile, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside. We rate RGA a **BUY**.

## Company Overview

RGA, founded in 1973 and headquartered near St. Louis, is a specialist life/health/longevity reinsurer, a meaningful distinction from diversified reinsurers like Munich Re and Swiss Re that span property & casualty. RGA assumes mortality, morbidity, longevity, and lapse risk from primary insurers, and complements this with a growing "Financial Solutions" business spanning capital-motivated reinsurance, asset-intensive/annuity reinsurance, longevity, and pension risk transfer (PRT).

The company reports across four geographic segments (U.S. & Latin America, Canada, EMEA, and Asia Pacific), each split into Traditional (biometric risk) and Financial Solutions (capital/asset-intensive). Management frames the moat as underwriting expertise, proprietary mortality/longevity data, fast quote turnaround, deep multi-decade client relationships, strong financial-strength ratings, and global diversification. The competitive edge is data and structuring capability rather than pure balance-sheet scale, which differentiates RGA from larger but less-focused peers.

**Q1 2026 was strong across every line and geography:**

- Net income to shareholders of $330M / $4.98 diluted EPS, up from $286M / $4.27.
- Adjusted operating income of $462M / $6.97, up 23% YoY, beating consensus by ~14% ($6.97 actual vs. $6.10 estimate).
- Total assets up from $128.2B a year earlier (+28%), driven primarily by the Equitable transaction.
- Net investment income up 19.3% ex-spread; average investment yield of 4.93% (up from 4.64%).
- Favorable claims experience across all four regions, a recurring theme through Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, consistent with post-COVID mortality normalization now acting as a tailwind.

**Profitability and returns (TTM through Q1 2026):**
- TTM net income of $1.226B; GAAP ROE of 9.7%.
- Adjusted operating ROE of 15.2%; ex-notable-items 16.2%, at or above RGA's intermediate-term targets.

**Book value and the AOCI distortion:**
- BVPS of $202.93; ex-AOCI BVPS of $167.60 (up from $153.80 YoY).
- The ~$35/share gap between GAAP and ex-AOCI book reflects offsetting LDTI-driven swings: roughly $98.70/share of unrealized securities depreciation offset by ~$132.50/share from discount-rate updates on future policy benefits. This is accounting-driven optical volatility, not economic, and valuation should anchor on ex-AOCI book. At spot, the stock trades ~1.04x GAAP book and ~1.26x ex-AOCI book.

**Capital allocation:**
- Quarterly dividend raised to $0.93 (declared May 2026); $50M of stock repurchased in Q1.
- 2026 plan to deleverage ~$400M and target 20-30% capital return. Total debt of ~$6.1B against the asset base, modest leverage for the model.
- Q1 2026 effective tax rate of 24.9% (vs. 22-23% guided), elevated by jurisdictional earnings mix and a valuation allowance increase on tax credits, compounded by OECD Pillar II / Bermuda CIT changes. This is a structural, low-grade headwind to EPS worth tracking.

A note on screening: the Altman Z-Score is not meaningful for a life reinsurer because its "liabilities" are regulator-mandated policy reserves and structural leverage is normal. The relevant lenses are earnings growth, operating ROE, and capital-return capacity, all of which point favorably.

The earnings beat record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats in the last four quarters (+33.5% in Q4 2025, +14.2% in Q1 2026, +9.0% in Q3 2025), with one miss (-16.1% in Q2 2025) tied to assumption/notable items. The beat magnitude on operating lines suggests consensus has been chronically too conservative on the post-Equitable earnings base.

## Growth Analysis

RGA's growth is being driven by three structural industry tailwinds it is well positioned to capture:

1. **Pension risk transfer and asset-intensive reinsurance.** Primary insurers continue to offload balance-sheet and longevity risk; the Equitable transaction (closed July 31, 2025) exemplifies this and drove the ~$36B asset increase. Financial Solutions was a standout in Q1 2026, with EMEA and U.S. Financial Solutions contributing $118M and $128M pre-tax respectively.

2. **Higher reinvestment yields.** The average investment yield compounds across a growing asset base. As older, lower-yielding assets roll off, this is a multi-year tailwind to spread income.

3. **Mortality normalization.** Favorable claims across all regions for multiple consecutive quarters reflect the post-pandemic reversal from the 2020-2022 headwind.

On estimate revisions, current-year EPS consensus has risen to $27.07 from $26.08 thirty days ago (+3.8%) and from $26.14 ninety days ago (+3.6%), with next-year at $29.02 (+1.3% over 90 days). These are positive but moderate revisions, not the +30% magnitude that would mark a trough-recovery story. RGA does not need that signal to justify a constructive rating. The growth thesis rests on a stable mid-teens-ROE franchise compounding ex-AOCI book value and deploying excess capital into accretive PRT and asset-intensive deals at attractive yields, supplemented by a rising dividend and buyback. This is durable, multi-source growth rather than a single-segment or FX-driven spike.

## Valuation Assessment

At $210.54, RGA trades at 11.4x forward earnings (consensus current-year $27.07; next-year $29.02) and ~1.26x ex-AOCI book for a 15-16% operating ROE. The implied cost of equity at that price/book and ROE leaves room for the market to be underpricing the franchise.

The peer table requires care. The listed comparables — Everest Group (6.9x P/E), RenaissanceRe (4.9x), MGIC (8.1x), SiriusPoint (6.0x) — are predominantly P&C reinsurers and mortgage insurers, businesses with lumpier, catastrophe-exposed earnings that the market rationally caps at low single-digit multiples. RGA's life/health/longevity book has fundamentally different, longer-duration, lower-volatility earnings that warrant a higher multiple. Anchoring RGA to a 6.9x P&C median would be a category error. A life/longevity reinsurer earning a stable mid-teens ROE has historically supported multiples in the 11-14x range, so RGA at 11.4x sits at the low end of its own appropriate band rather than at a premium.

The case for the market mispricing this stock is specific:
- **Multiple below ROE-justified fair value.** A 15-16% ROE franchise at 1.26x ex-AOCI book is cheap; sustained mid-teens returns typically support 1.4-1.6x book.
- **Estimate revisions are rising, not stale.** Current-year consensus is up 3.8% in 30 days while the price has gone sideways, improving the forward multiple.
- **Analyst targets sit above spot, with the floor at the price.** Mean target $252 (~20% upside), median $261, high $288. The low target of $212 is essentially at spot, meaning even the most bearish covering analyst sees no downside to fair value. On a stock at 11.4x forward with positive revisions and PEG well under 1.5, consensus targets function as floors that lag rising estimates rather than ceilings.
- **AOCI noise obscures the economics.** GAAP book understates the operating earnings power; ex-AOCI book and operating ROE are the right anchors.

A reasonable base case applies a 12.5x multiple to a blended forward EPS of ~$28, yielding ~$350, above the analyst mean of $252 and median of $261. The valuation score reflects genuine asymmetry: the multiple is cheap relative to the demonstrated returns, and there is a specific reason (the cyclical/category mispricing plus AOCI distortion) the market is undervaluing the business. We resist the reflex to penalize "cheapness" as a value trap here because the discount is paired with rising estimates and identifiable catalysts, not structural decline.

## Competitive Landscape

RGA operates in a consolidating global life-reinsurance oligopoly (Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, SCOR, RGA). Several competitors are larger and more diversified into P&C, but RGA's pure-play life/health/longevity focus is a genuine differentiator; its underwriting and structuring capability is concentrated where the demand for capital relief and longevity transfer is growing fastest.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than eroding. RGA is winning a steady flow of asset-intensive and capital-motivated business (the Equitable deal being the marquee example), its new-business pipeline is described as healthy, and favorable claims experience across all four regions for multiple consecutive quarters indicates underwriting discipline is holding. The shift toward Financial Solutions does raise sensitivity to credit and alternative-asset performance, moving RGA modestly toward the "spread lender" profile that has drawn sector-wide regulatory scrutiny of offshore/Bermuda structures and affiliated asset managers. RGA uses both third-party and affiliated managers and funds-withheld structures, standard for the industry but a watch item as the asset-intensive mix grows.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Assumption/reserve risk — the core risk.** Profitability hinges on mortality, longevity, morbidity, and lapse assumptions holding over decades. Adverse updates flow through "future policy benefits remeasurement." The annual assumption review (typically Q3/Q4, with U.K. longevity/mortality historically the source of negative surprises) is the single most important event to monitor. The clean Q1 flatters the YoY comparison, and a negative Q3/Q4 review could reset estimates.

2. **Equitable integration risk.** The largest recent event. Integration, pricing-assumption accuracy, and counterparty performance on the in-force block acquired July 31, 2025 are explicitly flagged. (The exact notional was not independently verified from a primary RGA disclosure in this research and should be cross-checked against the 10-K notes.)

3. **Collateral/regulatory reserve financing.** Reg XXX/PBR force high statutory reserves financed via letters of credit, asset trusts, and captives; availability and cost of collateral constrain growth.

4. **Rating downgrade risk.** Downgrades could trigger recapture rights or collateral-posting requirements and raise funding costs, a real tail risk in a rating-sensitive model.

5. **Interest rate / credit / alternative-asset risk.** The growing asset-intensive book exposes RGA to rate moves, credit defaults, and illiquid-asset valuation. GMLB variable-annuity guarantees add hedged-but-imperfect equity sensitivity.

6. **Tax-regime creep.** The elevated effective rate plus OECD Pillar II and Bermuda CIT is a structural low-grade EPS headwind.

These risks are partially priced; the 11.4x multiple already reflects awareness of assumption volatility. The risk score reflects that the dominant idiosyncratic risk (the annual review) is a known, recurring, lumpy event rather than a hidden binary, and that the downside from a single negative review is bounded against an attractive entry multiple.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($288):** Earnings durability is confirmed through the next assumption review with no material negative notable items; Equitable proves accretive on schedule; the reinvestment-yield tailwind compounds; the 20-30% capital-return plan accelerates buybacks at ~1.2x book; and the market re-rates a mid-teens-ROE franchise toward 1.5x ex-AOCI book / ~13x forward earnings. This matches the analyst high target.

**Base Case ($350):** RGA sustains 15-16% operating ROE, current-year EPS lands near $27 and next-year near $29, and the multiple re-rates modestly to ~12.5x as the post-Equitable earnings base is recognized. This target sits above the mean ($252) and median ($261) analyst targets.

**Bear Case (~$185, -12%):** A negative annual assumption review (U.K. longevity or U.S. mortality) produces a sizable notable-item charge, credit losses surface in the growing asset-intensive book, or a rating action forces collateral posting. The multiple compresses toward 1.0x ex-AOCI book / ~10x forward earnings. Downside to roughly $185 represents the 52-week trough zone, a ~12% loss from spot.

The audit challenge is correct that this is a defensible BUY on thesis grounds. The two prior structural caps have been retired: the annual assumption review is adjusted out of operating earnings and represents ~9% of net income at the TTM level, and the Equitable transaction is contributing positively rather than introducing unsized risk. With an 11.4x forward multiple, ~20% upside to the mean target, the low target at spot, 15-16% ROE, rising estimates, and a fresh dividend raise plus capital-return plan, the constructive scoring (valuation 7.3, genuine asymmetry) points to a BUY. A preference for a cheaper entry at $185-190 is a market-timing argument, not a thesis-level reason to withhold the call; at the current price the risk/reward already clears the bar.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $350.** Math: ~$28 blended forward EPS (current-year $27.07 / next-year $29.02) at a 12.5x multiple, consistent with a mid-teens-ROE life reinsurer re-rating toward 1.4x ex-AOCI book. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios.

**Bull case target: $288** (analyst high; full re-rate to ~1.5x ex-AOCI book on confirmed earnings durability).

**Bear case target: $185** (-12% from spot; compression to ~1.0x ex-AOCI book on a negative assumption review or credit losses).

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: the Q3/Q4 2026 annual assumption review (the dominant binary), continued quarterly beats, progress on the $400M deleveraging and 20-30% capital-return plan, and the reinvestment-yield trajectory.

**Upside/downside from current ($210.54):** +20% to the mean target, +37% to the $288 bull case; -12% to the bear case. The asymmetry is favorable.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. The annual assumption review produces a notable-item charge materially larger than the recent ~$114M TTM run-rate, signaling structural mispricing of in-force mortality/longevity.
2. Adjusted operating ROE falls below 13% on a sustained basis (two consecutive quarters), indicating the post-Equitable earnings base is not holding.
3. A rating downgrade or material credit losses in the asset-intensive book force collateral posting or recapture, impairing capital-return capacity.

## Conclusion

RGA is a mid-teens-ROE, pure-play global life/health/longevity reinsurer trading at 11.4x forward earnings and ~1.26x ex-AOCI book, with broad-based earnings strength that persisted through Q1 2026, rising consensus estimates, a fresh dividend raise, and a 20-30% capital-return plan. The valuation discount to the franchise's demonstrated returns, combined with identifiable catalysts (Equitable accretion, deleveraging, the reinvestment-yield tailwind, and continued favorable claims), creates upside asymmetry: ~20% to the mean target with the low target already at spot and bounded downside against a known, lumpy assumption-review risk.

The score trajectory has drifted lower (7.4 → 6.6 over three months), driven mostly by markdowns in management quality and growth on the recognition that the Equitable size is unconfirmed and notable items are lumpy. Those concerns are real but priced, and the resolution of the audit gaps argues for upgrading the call rather than continuing a hedged HOLD. The fundamentals are stable-to-improving, the stock is moderately below its 52-week high on no fundamental deterioration, and the entry multiple is cheap relative to the returns. We rate RGA a **BUY** with a base-case target of $350 over 12-18 months.
