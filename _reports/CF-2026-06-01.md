**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer and the lowest-cost nitrogen platform in North America, anchored by the Donaldsonville, Louisiana complex. The business is fundamentally healthy: FY2025 revenue of $7.08B (+19% YoY), operating income of $2.3B, ~$1.8B of free cash flow (25% FCF margin), $1.98B of cash, and a manageable $3.25B of all-unsecured notes laddered out to 2034-2044 with no near-term maturities. Management has retired roughly 20% of the share count since 2022 and launched a fresh $2B buyback through 2029. On structural quality, CF is among the best-positioned cyclical commodity companies in the market.

The problem is the source of current earnings power. The FY2025 boom and the 2026 estimate revisions (current-quarter EPS estimate +173.6% over 90 days, current-year +115.2%) are driven primarily by price (+19% selling-price gain on +1% volume in 2025), and that price strength reflects a geopolitical nitrogen supply shock — outages in Egypt, Iran, and Russia plus Middle East gas disruptions. A confirmed de-escalation would compress the disruption premium embedded in nitrogen prices precisely as Henry Hub gas costs have risen sharply (to ~$6.32/MMBtu by February 2026 versus a $3.53 average in 2025), with gas at 34% of production cost. Q1 2026's headline strength also leaned on a one-time $170M litigation gain.

The stock has round-tripped much of its run, falling from a 52-week high of $141.96 to ~$112.35, reflecting that the market understands these margins are partly transient; Morningstar noted CF shares fell on Middle East ceasefire progress. Layered on top are a CEO succession (Tony Will to Director; Christopher Bohn now President & CEO), ~$30M of executive selling in early 2026 with zero open-market buying, the Yazoo City complex idled until at least Q4 2026, and Blue Point JV execution risk compounded by Louisiana's Class VI sequestration moratorium. The combination of a fortress balance sheet against cyclical-peak, geopolitically contingent earnings produces a balanced setup. We rate CF a HOLD, with the cleanest exit being a confirmed Middle East de-escalation.

## Company Overview

CF Industries Holdings is a pure-play nitrogen company, having exited phosphate in 2014. It uses the Haber-Bosch process to convert cheap North American natural gas and atmospheric nitrogen into anhydrous ammonia, then upgrades the ammonia into granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate, and industrial nitrogen products (DEF, nitric acid, urea liquor).

The asset base is the moat. CF operates nine nitrogen complexes — six in the U.S., two in Canada, one in the U.K. — led by Donaldsonville, the world's largest ammonia complex at roughly 40% of CF's ammonia capacity. The company holds approximately 40%, 41%, 44%, and 19% of North American ammonia, granular urea, UAN, and ammonium nitrate capacity respectively. It controls an extensive logistics network: ~5,000 leased railcars, river barges, a connection to the 2,000-mile Sunoco ammonia pipeline, and deep-water export docks at Donaldsonville and Billingham.

The central pillar of the bull thesis is cost position. CF's facilities sit on Henry Hub gas, which averaged ~$3.53/MMBtu in 2025 against multiples of that for marginal European and Asian producers. In a globally priced, low-differentiation commodity, being the low-cost producer is the durable advantage. The ownership structure has nuances: five U.S. plants are held through CF Industries Nitrogen (89% CF / 11% CHS Inc.), with CHS also being CF's largest customer (~13% of 2025 net sales) and receiving semi-annual distributions of $330-375M/year.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is strong on the metrics that matter for resilience. FY2025 results:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 6,631 | 5,936 | 7,084 |
| Gross margin | 38.4% | 34.6% | 38.5% |
| Operating income ($M) | 2,230 | 1,746 | 2,300 |
| Net earnings to common ($M) | 1,525 | 1,218 | 1,455 |
| Diluted EPS | 7.87 | 6.74 | 8.97 |

Essentially all of the 2025 revenue gain came from price (+19% to $372/ton) rather than volume (+1%). Segment profitability is strongest in granular urea (~47% gross margin) and UAN (42.6%), with ammonia at 31% and ammonium nitrate weakest at ~19%, hurt by the Yazoo City idling.

Cash generation is the headline strength: operating cash flow of $2.75B against $950M of capex produced ~$1.8B of FCF. The balance sheet carries $3.25B of total debt, all unsecured senior notes, after the 2026 notes were redeemed. The current ratio is 3.37 and the $750M revolver is undrawn.

A naive screen flags CF with an Altman Z-Score of 1.37 ("distress") and elevated D/E of 2.91. Both are misleading. The EBIT/assets component reads near-zero despite $2.3B of actual operating income — a classification artifact — and the high leverage ratio reflects aggressive buybacks shrinking the equity base, not financial weakness. With $1.8B of FCF, $2.75B of OCF, $1.98B of cash, and a 3.4x current ratio, CF is robustly healthy. The leverage profile is a feature of shareholder-friendly capital return.

The earnings-quality caveat is that the trailing $8.97 EPS and the forward estimates embed a transient supply shock. Q1 2026 net earnings of $615M and adjusted EBITDA of $983M beat estimates but included a one-time $170M litigation gain. The earnings beat track record is solid (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters, including a +24.9% beat in Q4 2025), which lends confidence to near-term consensus, but the forward path depends on commodity prices that may not be durable.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate revisions must be addressed directly: current-quarter EPS estimate of $5.91 is up 48.5% in 30 days and 173.6% in 90 days; next-quarter of $4.75 is up 92.0% and 286.1%; current-year of $18.23 is up 24.0% and 115.2%. Revision velocity of this magnitude is normally one of the strongest forward indicators of continued outperformance.

The critical qualification is that this is cyclical-peak inflection driven by a geopolitical price spike, not a durable secular re-rating. The same supply disruptions inflating these estimates (Egypt/Iran/Russia outages, Middle East gas) are precisely what could reverse on a resolution. When the disruption premium compresses, these estimates revise down as sharply as they have revised up. CF is a cyclical commodity producer riding a temporary supply shock, not an inflection stock recovering from a structurally improving demand environment. The next-year estimate ($11.16, up only 3.5% over 30 days) implicitly reflects this normalization: consensus already expects 2027 earnings well below the 2026 peak.

Genuine structural growth levers exist but are unproven and long-dated. The Donaldsonville CCS facility came online in July 2025 (capacity for ~1.9M tons/year of low-carbon ammonia, ~$42M of 45Q credits in 2025), and the Blue Point JV (CF 40%, JERA 35%, Mitsui 25%) targets low-carbon ammonia for Japanese power and steel offtake. EU CBAM (effective January 2026) is a structural tailwind for low-carbon product. But management itself says low-carbon ammonia demand could take 10+ years to materialize, the economics depend on 45Q credits and policy support that is being actively rolled back, and CF wrote off its $51M Donaldsonville electrolyzer project in December 2025. Near-term, the Yazoo City idling cuts ~600k tons of 2026 ammonia production, with gross production guided down to ~9.5M tons from 10.1M in 2025.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at ~10.1x trailing EPS of $8.97 and a similar forward multiple on near-term estimates. Against the Materials peer median P/E of 26.3 (IP, AMCR, RGLD, RS, PKG), CF looks deeply discounted. The discount is appropriate, not an opportunity.

The peer set is heterogeneous and not directly comparable, since paper/packaging and royalty companies carry different cyclicality. CF's apparent cheapness is a function of earnings being at cycle peak. The correct way to value a commodity cyclical at peak is on normalized mid-cycle earnings, not trailing or trough-relative forward EPS. When a stock prints peak earnings driven by a transient supply shock, a low trailing P/E is the textbook signal of a top, not a bottom. The 2024 trough showed EPS of $6.74 in a more normal environment; a de-escalation would push 2027 earnings back toward that range or below as both nitrogen prices fall and gas costs stay elevated.

The market is not mispricing this. The stock has already retraced to ~$112, and Morningstar explicitly noted CF fell on ceasefire progress. Analyst consensus reflects the uncertainty: a wide $94-$150 range with a $130 median and $126.5 mean, against 14 holds, 4 buys (1 strong buy, 3 buy), and 3 sells (2 sell, 1 strong sell). The dividend yield of 1.78% provides only modest support.

There is no specific, identifiable reason the market is wrong here. The bull case requires the supply shock to persist or worsen; the bear case requires it to resolve. Both are live. That balance, applied to a stock at cyclical-peak earnings, supports a valuation score in the middle of the range rather than a discount-driven high score.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive position is strong and stable. It competes with Nutrien, Koch Fertilizer, LSB Industries, CVR Partners, and Yara, plus subsidized and state-owned producers across the Middle East, Russia, Trinidad, Africa, and China. CF's edge is durable: lowest-cost North American gas feedstock, unmatched logistics scale, and first-mover positioning in low-carbon ammonia.

The 2025-26 strength has been amplified by EU anti-dumping duties on Russian/Trinidad/U.S. UAN and EU tariffs on Russian nitrogen, which tighten supply and favor CF. CBAM is a structural tailwind, though France and Italy are lobbying to suspend it for fertilizers. The competitive risks are cyclical rather than structural: China relaxing nitrogen export restrictions, or new global capacity (including low-carbon ammonia) arriving over the next 12 months, would pressure prices. The 10-K explicitly recalls nitrogen prices falling 34% in 2015-2017 as new capacity came online. CF's market position will not erode, but the price environment that position monetizes is cyclical.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the weakest dimension and is the primary driver of the HOLD.

**Commodity cyclicality / geopolitical contingency (dominant risk).** If de-escalation is confirmed and holds, the supply premium inflating nitrogen margins compresses, and CF's forward estimates revise down sharply. This is the central asymmetry: the same conflict premium that drove the estimate surge can reverse. (Probability is not forecastable with conviction; consequence is a retrace toward the $94 bear-case level.)

**Natural gas cost headwind.** With gas at 34% of cost, the rise in Henry Hub is a material 2026 margin compressor not captured in trailing numbers, and the squeeze is worst if nitrogen prices fall while gas stays high.

**Yazoo City idling (new).** A November 2025 incident idled the entire complex with no restart expected before Q4 2026, driving a $25M impairment and reducing 2026 production guidance.

**Blue Point execution risk.** The ~$3.7B JV uses ATR technology not widely deployed at the contemplated scale and depends on third-party Class VI CO₂ sequestration. Louisiana imposed an indefinite moratorium on new Class VI well applications in October 2025, directly threatening the infrastructure Blue Point's 45Q economics depend on. Roughly one-third of cost is imported materials exposed to steel/aluminum tariffs.

**Policy and litigation overhang.** Low-carbon economics depend on 45Q, CBAM, and Japanese subsidies, all subject to reversal (OBBBA already modified credits; the administration is rolling back GHG regulation). The Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and subsequent 10% tariff create ongoing uncertainty for capital equipment costs and trade flows, plus the possibility of China urea export resumption.

**Management transition.** The CEO succession plus a new CFO (Scribner) introduces strategic-continuity risk, layered on the early-2026 executive selling.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 62.7% versus 41.6% historical volatility — rich by 21 points, in the 84th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (62.7% near-term versus 47.4% at six months), the signature of an expected near-term catalyst. IV has already cooled from 85.8% to 62.7% over three days as the price slipped, but the elevated front-end vol and ±5.6% implied weekly move confirm the market expects volatility around the next set of nitrogen-price and geopolitical data points. Put/call skew is balanced (-2.2%) and put/call OI is 0.79, so the positioning is not aggressively bearish; the market is hedging a two-sided binary. Short interest at 7.0% of float (2.8 days to cover) is modest and does not signal a crowded bearish bet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
A persistent or worsening supply shock keeps the nitrogen supply premium intact through 2026. CF compounds peak FCF into the buyback, retiring shares at low multiples. CBAM and the EU duty regime structurally favor CF; low-carbon ammonia sales ramp at a premium. Estimates hold near the current $18.23 current-year path, and the stock re-rates toward the $150 analyst high. Bull case ~$150 (+34% from $112.35).

### Bear Case
A confirmed Middle East de-escalation compresses the disruption premium; nitrogen prices normalize toward 2024 levels while Henry Hub stays elevated near $6/MMBtu, squeezing margins from both ends. Yazoo City remains offline, China resumes urea exports, and 2027 EPS reverts toward the 2024 range. Stock retraces toward the $94 analyst low. Bear case ~$94 (-16% from $112.35).

### Base Case
The conflict premium partially persists but erodes through H2 2026 as supply disruptions ease. CF earns elevated-but-fading margins, returns cash, and trades in a range as the market discounts the peak. Base case ~$115-120.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale: the binary geopolitical outcome dominates the 6-12 month return distribution and is not forecastable with conviction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A sustained supply disruption that durably re-anchors nitrogen prices higher AND a normalization of Henry Hub back below ~$4/MMBtu, restoring margin on both ends.
- Open-market insider buying by the new CEO/CFO, confirming management conviction at current prices.
- Stock dislocating toward the low-$90s on macro (not fundamental) selling while next-year estimates hold above ~$11, compressing the forward multiple below mid-cycle earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A confirmed, sustained Middle East de-escalation that alleviates the supply shock, as nitrogen margins compress.
- A guidance cut or a >20% downward revision to next-year EPS, signaling the peak has passed.
- China urea export resumption confirmed at scale.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, and continuous monitoring of the Middle East supply situation and the broader energy-price complex as secondary catalysts given the gas-cost linkage.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a high-quality, lowest-cost nitrogen producer with a genuine cost-and-logistics moat, ~$1.8B of FCF, a fortress balance sheet (the "distress" Z-Score is a screening artifact), and disciplined, shareholder-friendly capital allocation. None of that is in dispute. The issue is that the trailing earnings and the +173% estimate revisions are inflated by a geopolitical supply shock that could reverse on resolution, while gas costs have risen sharply and one-time items (the $170M litigation gain) flattered the latest quarter. Buyers at current levels are paying a low headline multiple on cyclical-peak earnings.

New data confirms the cautious trend: the CEO transition with concurrent insider selling, the Yazoo City idling, the gas-cost headwind, and the widening downside tail on a supply-shock resolution all argue for caution. We maintain HOLD. The cleanest exit is a confirmed, sustained Middle East de-escalation; absent that, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, and there is no specific reason the market is mispricing the stock at ~$112.
