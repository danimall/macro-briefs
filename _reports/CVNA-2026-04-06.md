## Executive Summary

Carvana has executed a sharp operational turnaround: from a $1.6B net loss and negative equity in 2022 to $1.4B net income and $4.2B stockholders' equity in 2025. Revenue grew 49% to $20.3B on 596,641 retail units, with total GPU of $7,026 and SG&A per unit declining 12% YoY. Adjusted EBITDA of $2.24B (11% margin) demonstrates genuine scale economics in a highly fragmented $1.3T used car market where Carvana holds only 1.6% share.

The problem is everything surrounding the operations. An active SEC investigation into Gotham City Research's allegations of $1B+ in earnings overstatement through related-party subsidies from DriveTime (controlled by the same Garcia family) raises unresolved questions about reported earnings quality, particularly the $1.19B gain-on-sale line. The Garcia family controls 83% of voting power through dual-class shares and stands to receive $2.3B through the Tax Receivable Agreement. Short interest at 61.9% of float reflects deep structural skepticism. At ~37x trailing earnings and ~45x forward, the stock prices in sustained high-growth execution while the macro environment — stagflation, $107 oil, consumer confidence down 12.5% YoY, mortgage rates above 6.5% — works against discretionary large-ticket purchases.

The calibration data explicitly mandates Consumer Discretionary BUY suspension given negative average returns across 12 prior BUY calls in the sector. The governance risks, SEC investigation, macro headwinds, and elevated valuation create asymmetric downside that warrants an AVOID rating despite the strong operational trajectory.

## Company Overview

Carvana operates the largest e-commerce platform for buying and selling used vehicles in the United States, serving 316 metropolitan statistical areas covering ~80% of the U.S. population. The company's vertically integrated model spans vehicle acquisition, reconditioning at a network of Inspection and Reconditioning Centers (IRCs) with ~1.5M vehicles/year capacity, online merchandising of a nationally pooled inventory (~75,000 vehicles), proprietary financing origination, complementary product sales (VSCs, GAP coverage, insurance via Root), and last-mile delivery through a hub-and-spoke logistics network including 39 branded vending machines.

Since inception, Carvana has sold 2.8 million retail vehicles generating $84.1B in cumulative revenue. The company employs 23,100+ people. Its 2022 acquisition of ADESA's U.S. physical auction business for $2.2B provided reconditioning infrastructure — 16 of 56 ADESA sites now have IRC capabilities — and a wholesale marketplace that generates additional revenue.

Carvana Co. is a holding company controlling Carvana Group, LLC. Ernest Garcia II (father, founder of DriveTime) and Ernest Garcia III (son, CEO) hold approximately 83% of voting power through super-voting Class B shares carrying 10 votes per share. Carvana is a NYSE-listed "controlled company."

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Unit Economics

Revenue grew from $10.8B (2023) to $13.7B (2024) to $20.3B (2025) — a 49% YoY increase driven by 43% unit growth (596,641 units vs. 416,348) and 4.8% higher average revenue per unit ($24,365 vs. $23,252). Quarterly acceleration was evident: Q1 $4.23B, Q2 $4.84B, Q3 $5.65B.

Total GPU expanded from $5,511 (2023) to $6,908 (2024) to $7,026 (2025). "Other" gross profit (finance receivables + complementary products) contributed $2,905/unit in 2025, representing 41% of total GPU. Gain on loan sales alone was $1.19B in 2025, up from $755M in 2024. This finance-driven profit stream is the component most directly questioned by the Gotham City allegations.

### Profitability

Gross margin was 20.6% in 2025 ($4.19B), modestly below 2024's 21.0% as unit growth slightly outpaced margin expansion. Operating income reached $1.88B (9.3% margin) vs. $990M (7.2%) in 2024 — demonstrating operating leverage as SG&A per unit declined from $3,886 to $3,433 (non-GAAP). The company reported $1.407B net income attributable to Carvana Co. in 2025, though this was depressed by a $2.2B TRA expense (non-cash) offset by a $2.2B deferred tax asset release. Adjusted EBITDA of $2.24B (11.0% margin) is the cleanest profitability metric.

### Balance Sheet and Cash Flow

Cash of $2.33B at year-end 2025, up from $1.72B. Total liquidity of $6.74B including credit facilities. Total debt of $5.0B, down from $5.5B following retirement of $611M in 2028 Senior Secured Notes. Net debt of ~$2.7B. Interest expense declined to $505M from $651M, but remains substantial — the Senior Secured Notes carry 9-14% rates and some tranches continue accruing PIK interest (the 2031 notes grew from $1.98B to $2.27B in 2025 via PIK accrual).

Operating cash flow was $1.04B, up from $918M. CapEx was only $147M, yielding free cash flow of ~$890M. Capital expenditure intensity is low for a company of this scale, reflecting the asset-light e-commerce model outside of the IRC network.

Stockholders' equity turned positive at $4.2B, up from negative equity as recently as 2022. This recovery was substantially driven by the DTA release.

### Finance Receivable Quality

Carvana has secured $18B+ in loan purchase commitments through 2027 (primarily Ally Financial MPSA), providing forward visibility on its ability to originate and sell loans. However, the quality and arms-length nature of these transactions — particularly involving Bridgecrest (Garcia-controlled) as servicer — is the central question raised by Gotham City. If DriveTime is absorbing losses on loans originated by Carvana, the reported gain-on-sale ($1.19B in 2025) would overstate Carvana's standalone economics.

## Growth Analysis

### Addressable Market

The U.S. used vehicle retail market is ~37 million annual transactions, one of the largest retail categories. E-commerce penetration in automotive remains far below the ~20% level in other retail segments. Carvana's 1.6% market share implies significant runway if the secular shift to online car buying continues. The top 10 used car retailers collectively hold less than 10% share.

### Growth Trajectory

Unit growth of 43% in 2025 on reconditioning capacity of ~1.5M vehicles/year means Carvana is operating at roughly 40% utilization. This capacity headroom supports continued volume growth without proportionate capital investment. Management has demonstrated ability to grow units while expanding GPU — the key proof point of the e-commerce thesis.

### Forward Estimates

Consensus expects FY2026 EPS of $6.90 (current year) and FY2027 EPS of $10.10. The 90-day revision trend is mixed: current quarter estimates fell 4.1%, next quarter fell 5.7%, but next year estimates rose 11.0%. The 30-day trend shows modest deterioration across all periods (1.3-1.8% declines). Two of the last four quarters missed estimates, including a 19.9% miss in Q3 2025 and a 5.5% miss in Q4 2025. The miss pattern after prior beats suggests deteriorating earnings visibility.

### Potential Tariff Tailwind

If tariffs on new vehicles increase prices, the new-to-used price spread widens, potentially driving incremental demand toward used vehicles. Carvana's 10-K acknowledges tariff risk but notes FY2025 was not materially impacted. This is a conditional positive that depends on tariff policy outcomes.

## Valuation Assessment

At $313.91 and a market cap of ~$45.2B, Carvana trades at:
- ~37x trailing P/E (based on $8.45 EPS)
- ~45x FY2026 consensus EPS of $6.90 (lower than trailing due to TRA/DTA adjustments in FY2025)
- ~31x FY2027 consensus EPS of $10.10
- ~20x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $2.24B
- ~17x net debt-adjusted EV/EBITDA

Against peer medians: Automotive Retail peers trade at a median P/E of 16.7x (AutoZone 27.2x, O'Reilly 30.7x, Penske 11.2x, AutoNation 12.1x). Carvana's trailing P/E of 37x is more than double the peer median. The premium reflects the growth differential — Carvana grew revenue 49% vs. low-to-mid single digits for most peers — but also embeds assumptions about sustained market share gains and margin expansion that may not materialize in a recessionary environment.

The stock is 35% below its January 2026 high of $486.89 and 112% above its April 2025 low of $148.25. Analyst consensus median target is $445 (42% upside), with a range of $300-$519. The low target at $300 is approximately the current trading level.

The 20x EBITDA multiple assumes continued growth at current margins. If unit growth decelerates to 15-20% (plausible in recession) and GPU compresses by $500-700/unit (historical range during downturns), EBITDA could contract to $1.5-1.7B, implying a stock price of $200-250 at the same multiple, or lower if the multiple compresses.

## Competitive Landscape

Carvana has concrete competitive advantages: nationally pooled inventory (broader selection per customer), vertically integrated reconditioning, proprietary financing with 10+ years of data, and a scaled logistics network. These create genuine barriers to replication. CarMax (KMX), the largest traditional competitor, has invested in omnichannel but lacks Carvana's end-to-end digital architecture.

The fragmented market structure (top 10 players <10% share) supports the consolidation thesis. However, competitive response is accelerating: Amazon has entered automotive, CarGurus and other platforms are adding transaction capabilities, and traditional dealers are building digital tools. The competitive moat is narrowing over time, though Carvana's infrastructure lead likely persists for 3-5 years.

The recent acquisition of five franchise dealerships for $160M signals strategic optionality into new vehicle sales, though this introduces execution complexity and is immaterial at current scale.

## Risk Assessment

### Related-Party / SEC Investigation Risk (CRITICAL)

Gotham City Research's January 2026 report alleged Carvana overstated 2023-2024 earnings by $1B+ through undisclosed subsidies from DriveTime. Specific allegations: DriveTime absorbing losses on Carvana-originated loans (inflating gain-on-sale), DriveTime operating under severe financial stress ($1B+ cash burn, 20-40x leverage), and loans appearing on Bridgecrest's balance sheet that were sold by Carvana. Carvana received an SEC subpoena in June 2025 and states it is cooperating while denying the allegations.

Gain-on-sale was $1.19B in 2025 (30% of gross profit). If even a fraction of this is artificially supported by DriveTime absorbing below-market losses, Carvana's standalone economics are materially weaker than reported. The Garcia family's control of both entities creates an inherent conflict acknowledged in the 10-K: arrangements "cannot be assumed to have been negotiated at arm's length."

The $2.3B TRA liability — requiring Carvana to pay 85% of realized tax benefits to the Garcia family — represents an additional wealth transfer mechanism from public shareholders to controlling insiders.

### Macro Sensitivity

Carvana sells a high-ticket item ($24,365 average) frequently financed at elevated rates. The world model documents: consumer sentiment down 12.5% YoY, 30.5% of car trade-ins underwater (+4.2pp YoY), oil at $107 (increasing transportation costs and reducing disposable income), recession probability 40-45%, and no rate cuts expected in 2026. BofA's April 2026 downgrade to Neutral specifically cited oil prices and rising yields pressuring Carvana's core customer base. Diesel at $5.29/gallon (+40%) directly increases Carvana's vehicle transport costs.

### Short Interest

61.9% of float is short with 3.1 days to cover. This creates two-way risk: potential squeeze dynamics on positive catalysts, but also reflects deep structural skepticism from informed participants who have maintained large short positions through a period when the stock more than doubled. The short thesis appears centered on related-party concerns and valuation.

### Capital Structure

$5.0B in debt at 9-14% rates with ongoing PIK accrual on some tranches. Interest expense of $505M/year consumes roughly half of operating cash flow. While deleveraging is underway ($611M retired in 2025), the balance sheet remains stressed relative to cyclical downside scenarios.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 84.7% vs. 68.9% HV (15.8pp rich) with the term structure in backwardation — the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The 3-month tenor shows 87.1% IV with a put/call volume ratio of 1.73 (heavy put buying), consistent with institutional hedging or directional shorting. The 6-month tenor at 77.1% with a put/call skew of -1.2% (slightly negative skew = puts cheaper than calls) suggests the medium-term options market sees the current risk as binary rather than structurally bearish. The near-term elevated put demand aligns with macro uncertainty (Tuesday ultimatum effects on consumer stocks) and SEC investigation overhang.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- 1.6% market share in a $1.3T market with <10% e-commerce penetration implies multi-year unit growth runway
- Reconditioning capacity at ~40% utilization supports volume doubling without proportionate capex
- SG&A per unit declining 12% YoY demonstrates operating leverage with further room to scale
- $6.74B total liquidity and positive FCF enable continued deleveraging
- Gotham City allegations prove unfounded; SEC investigation closes without action
- Tariffs on new vehicles widen the new-used spread, accelerating used vehicle demand
- FY2027 consensus EPS of $10.10 implies stock trades at ~31x forward — reasonable if growth sustains
- Short squeeze potential at 62% SI could drive substantial upside on positive catalysts

### Bear Case
- SEC investigation validates some or all Gotham City allegations, requiring earnings restatement and destroying investor confidence
- Gain-on-sale profitability ($1.19B, 30% of gross profit) is partially supported by non-arms-length DriveTime subsidies
- Stagflationary macro (recession probability 40-45%, oil at $107, no rate cuts) pressures both unit volumes and GPU as consumers defer purchases or shift to lower-priced vehicles
- $5.0B in high-cost debt (9-14%) amplifies earnings volatility in a downturn; PIK accrual continues growing principal
- Two consecutive earnings misses (Q3 -19.9%, Q4 -5.5%) signal deteriorating visibility as growth normalizes
- Estimate revisions trending negative across all near-term periods (current quarter -4.1%, next quarter -5.7%)
- Garcia family governance (83% voting control, $2.3B TRA, DriveTime conflicts) creates permanent discount to fair value for public shareholders
- Competitive moat narrowing as CarMax, Amazon, and traditional dealers invest in digital capabilities

## Conclusion

Carvana's operational execution since 2022 has been strong. Growing units 43% while expanding GPU and reducing SG&A per unit is a combination few competitors have achieved and supports the e-commerce used car thesis. Capacity headroom and market share opportunity remain substantial.

However, the investment case requires accepting: (1) that the SEC investigation will resolve favorably despite legitimate questions about $1.19B in gain-on-sale revenue flowing through Garcia-controlled entities, (2) that a Consumer Discretionary company selling a $24,365 average ticket will continue growing units 30%+ in a stagflationary environment with 40-45% recession probability, (3) that 37x trailing earnings is an appropriate multiple given two consecutive misses and falling near-term estimates, and (4) that 83% insider voting control with $2.3B in TRA obligations to the controlling family does not create a governance discount.

The risk/reward is unfavorable at current levels. The stock prices in continued high-growth execution while multiple fundamental and structural risks remain unresolved. The earnings surprise pattern has deteriorated (two consecutive misses after a 131% beat in Q1 2025), estimates are being revised downward, and the macro environment is actively hostile to the company's customer base. Insider selling is systematic (option exercises at $10-52 followed by sales at $296-334) with zero open-market purchases.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The stock's 112% rally from April 2025 lows discounts a turnaround that is already substantially realized. Downside scenarios (SEC enforcement, recession, earnings compression) carry larger magnitude than remaining upside, particularly given the governance structure that limits minority shareholder value capture. The calibration data's mandatory Consumer Discretionary BUY suspension reinforces this conclusion. A more attractive entry point would require either: (a) SEC investigation closure with clean findings, (b) price decline to 15-20x forward EBITDA (~$200-250), or (c) demonstrated volume resilience through at least two quarters of macro deterioration.
