# Investment Research Report: BankUnited, Inc. (BKU)

*Price basis: $46.765 (NYSE, August 7, 2026 regular session; official prior close $46.63). Market capitalization ~$3.38B on 72.28M diluted shares.*

## Executive Summary

BankUnited has done the hard part of a regional-bank funding repair. Non-interest-bearing demand deposits (NIDDA) rose $825M in the first half of 2026 to $9,935M, taking NIDDA to **34.4% of total deposits** from 28.8% at year-end — a company record above the COVID-era peak. Brokered deposits fell from $4.9B to $3.0B and time deposits from $3.9B to $2.4B, so while headline deposits declined $472M, non-brokered deposits rose $1.4B. Average cost of deposits fell to 2.05% in Q2 from 2.47% a year earlier, and tax-equivalent NIM expanded to 3.06% from 2.93%. Decomposing the average-balance tables, essentially all of that margin gain is funding-side: earning-asset yields *fell* 29bp year over year (loans 5.55%→5.31%), while liability cost fell 47bp and interest-bearing liabilities shrank from 68.5% to 65.5% of earning assets.

The problem is that none of this is reaching shareholders through operating earnings. First-half non-interest expense rose 5.4% to $342.0M, with compensation up **12.2%** to $186.1M on revenue-producer hiring and 2025 incentive payouts. The derived efficiency ratio was **61.3% in 1H26 and 61.3% in 1H25** — dead flat. Pre-provision net revenue grew only 5.6% ($216.2M vs $204.8M), and Q2 PPNR of $109.9M was barely above Q2'25's $109.6M. Reported EPS improvement owes as much to a lower effective tax rate (24.7% vs 26.9%) and a 2.5% smaller share count as to operating leverage. Meanwhile the asset side is not growing: core loans (CRE, C&I, warehouse, Pinnacle) went from $17,188M to $17,202M in six months, with C&I *down* $367M and $230–240M of deals walked away from on price and structure. Management cut full-year core loan growth guidance from 6% to 4–5% and NII growth from 9% to 5–6%.

At 1.16x tangible book ($40.48 TBVPS) for a 9.3% ROE, the stock is priced roughly in line with the returns it generates. The nearest peer on the comp sheet, First Hawaiian, trades at 12.3x earnings; BKU trades at 11.9x current-year and 10.4x next-year consensus, a discount that is defensible given falling estimates rather than an obvious mispricing. Consensus for the current year has been cut 4.4% in 90 days and the last two quarters both missed. The capital-return program — $110.6M of 1H buybacks plus $48.3M of dividends against $132.5M of net income, roughly 120% of earnings — supports the price but is funded partly by balance-sheet shrinkage and has a finite runway (~$204M of excess capital above management's "mid-11s" CET1 target, about 6% of market cap, plus retained earnings). We maintain HOLD, with the score continuing to drift lower (6.1 → 5.7 → 5.4).

## Company Overview

BankUnited, Inc. is a Delaware bank holding company whose sole subsidiary, BankUnited, N.A., held $34.9B of assets at June 30, 2026 (down from $35.0B at year-end 2025). It operates as one reportable segment. Florida is the largest market, followed by the New York tri-state area, with newer entries in Dallas (footprint doubled in Q2), Atlanta, Charlotte (wholesale office opened weeks before the July call) and Morristown, NJ. Headcount was 1,785 at year-end 2025.

The business is a commercially-funded balance-sheet lender: roughly **75% of deposits are commercial or municipal**, and two national verticals dominate — title insurance at ~$4.9B (~17% of deposits) and homeowner associations at ~$2.4B (~8%). Commercial loans are 72% of the book across C&I, CRE, construction, mortgage warehouse, and two national subsidiaries: Pinnacle (municipal essential-use equipment and private-placement bond finance) and Bridge/BFG (franchise and transportation equipment finance, explicitly de-emphasized and down to $71.7M from $102.7M in six months).

Two structural features matter for interpretation. First, BKU does not originate residential mortgages; the $5.8B 1-4 family book is prime jumbo purchased through correspondents (California 29.9%, New York 20.3%, Florida only 7.1%). Second, it buys defaulted FHA/VA "Buyout Loans" out of GNMA securitizations, cures or modifies them, and shares re-securitization economics with the servicer — $814M outstanding at June 30. That business is the reason $176M of loans are 90+ days past due and still accruing; the credit risk sits with the government guarantee, and any screen reading total 90+ delinquency off the aging table overstates BKU's asset-quality problem by roughly 70bp of loans.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings.** Net income ran $415.0M (FY21) → $285.0M (FY22) → $178.7M (FY23) → $232.5M (FY24) → $268.4M (FY25), so roughly half the rate-shock decline has been recovered. FY2025 diluted EPS was $3.53 on ROAA 0.77% and ROAE 9.0%. First-half 2026 net income was $132.5M ($1.79 diluted) versus $127.2M ($1.68). Q2 alone: $70.7M, $0.97 diluted, ROAA 0.81%, ROAE 9.3%, up from Q1's $61.9M/$0.83/0.72%/8.1%.

**Margin mechanics.** My decomposition of Q2'26 versus Q2'25 attributes about +30.8bp to lower liability cost and +9.3bp to mix shift, offset by falling asset yields, netting roughly +11bp against the reported +13bp (the residual is rounding and tax-equivalent adjustments). The runway is therefore tied to how much more interest-bearing funding can be converted. The $1.03B year-over-year increase in *average* NIDDA, if it displaced interest-bearing deposits at Q2's 2.99% rate, is worth roughly $30.8M pre-tax, ~$23M after tax, ~$0.32 per diluted share annually. That is the engine, and it is real — but at 34.4% NIDDA the low-hanging conversion is largely done, and management's own raised target is 13% average NIDDA growth, not a step-change.

**A comparability adjustment.** Deposit rebate and commission costs correlated with account balances — predominantly earnings-credit arrangements in the title vertical — totaled $14.3M in Q2 and $27.5M in 1H, booked in *other non-interest expense* rather than interest expense. Annualized against $33,903M of average earning assets, that is ~16.9bp of NIM; against $28,728M of average deposits, ~20bp of deposit cost. Economically comparable NIM is therefore closer to **2.89%** and all-in deposit cost nearer **2.25%**. The two distortions offset in pre-tax income, so this is a screening-comparability issue, not an earnings-quality issue — but it means BKU's NIM looks better and its efficiency ratio looks worse than a peer routing the same economics through interest expense.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Equity $3,003M; tangible common equity $2,925.8M (goodwill $77.6M is the only intangible); **TBVPS $40.48** versus $40.14 at year-end — a 0.85% six-month increase against a 9.3% ROE, the drags being $47.9M of dividends, buyback above book, and $27.3M of adverse AOCI. CET1 is 12.30% at the holding company (13.01% at the bank), Tier 1 leverage 8.91%, TCE/TA 8.4%.

Securities are $9.32B, **entirely AFS** — there is no held-to-maturity bucket, so marks already sit in tangible book. Net unrealized loss widened to $282.3M from $267.1M, equal to **9.6% of TCE**; duration is only 2.02 years with ~65% floating. The largest bucket is private-label residential MBS/CMOs at −$202.3M (86% AAA, 3.3% projected collateral losses against 19.2% credit support). Private-label CMBS is −$20.9M with acknowledged office-sector collateral deterioration but 39.3% credit support against 7.8% projected losses. No credit impairment was recognized. Pledged securities fell from $8.0B to $5.6B, freeing collateral.

**Capital return.** 1H26 repurchases were 2,363,980 shares for $110.586M (average $46.78, or **1.156x** the June TBVPS), reducing the count 74.14M → 72.28M. $145.8M remains on the January 2026 $200M authorization, which the CFO expects to exhaust "somewhere around year end" before returning to the board in November/December. The dividend was raised to $0.33 quarterly, $1.32 annualized, a **2.82% yield** at $46.765. Combined 1H distributions of $158.9M equal ~120% of 1H net income. On an implied RWA of $25,544M ($3,141.9M ÷ 12.30%), the stated mid-11s CET1 target implies roughly **$204M of excess capital, ~6.0% of market capitalization**. Annualizing the 1H buyback pace gives ~6.5% share-count reduction plus 2.8% dividend — a ~9.3% gross shareholder yield, which is the single most reliable near-term support for the shares and also the clearest evidence that management has no better use for capital.

**Credit.** The direction of travel is mixed. Non-accruals fell 40% to $223.9M (NPL/loans 1.54% → 0.94%) and ACL/NPL coverage rebuilt from 59% to 97%, with Q2 net charge-offs of just $6.4M (0.11% annualized) after $36.1M (0.61%) in Q1. But the improvement was substantially *paid for*: 1H gross charge-offs were $47.2M ($40.0M C&I, $7.2M CRE), and total criticized/classified fell only $139M to $1,059.4M, with **substandard-accruing rising** to $686.3M from $674.4M. My rotation of the risk-rating tables shows C&I criticized-*accruing* up 58% to $305.4M while non-owner-occupied CRE plus construction criticized-accruing fell $112M. Construction ($28.9M → $64.1M) and industrial ($28.3M → $40.3M) worsened. Collateral-dependent loans shrank from $285.2M to $182.6M, but the unsecured shortfall widened from $28.3M (9.9%) to $43.9M (24.0%), with C&I the driver ($145.3M amortized cost against $102.1M of collateral). Mitigating context: 1H charge-offs were vintage-concentrated ($16.5M from 2023 originations, $15.4M from 2022, ~80% of C&I charge-offs), consistent with a peak-rate cohort resolving rather than a broad turn. Two more quarters are needed before the C&I migration counts as established deterioration.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has narrowed to one variable and it is decelerating in aggregate terms.

**Loans are flat.** Total loans fell $345M to $23,929M in six months. Core loans rose $14M. CRE added $120M and mortgage warehouse $72M; C&I fell $367M. Non-core runoff (residential plus franchise/equipment finance) accounted for $358M of decline and was intentional. Loan-to-deposit is 82.9%, so there is capacity — the constraint is pricing, not funding.

**Guidance was cut for the right reasons, which does not make it less of a cut.** Core loan growth went 6% → 4–5%; NII growth 9% → **5–6%**; total revenue growth to 5–6%; NIDDA average growth *raised* 12% → 13%; expenses raised slightly; provision "around original guidance to a little bit higher." Management attributed the NII reduction largely to timing (second-half closings not producing full-year effect) and mix rather than demand, and described pipelines as "fantastic." The competitive diagnosis was explicit: banks have re-entered CRE "at spread levels that we have not seen in quite some time," and the CEO said "our credit box has not changed… the market has moved meaningfully in terms of pricing credit. So we're winning less," adding "we could be wrong… we could be maybe a little too pessimistic."

**Consensus is following guidance down.** Current-quarter estimates fell 5.6% in 30 days and 5.7% in 90 days to $1.06; next quarter −5.2%/−5.3% to $1.12; current year −4.2%/−4.4% to $3.92; next year −2.3%/−1.9% to $4.48. The last two prints missed (−14.5% in Q1, −5.0% in Q2 on the Zacks adjusted basis; roughly −2% on a GAAP basis, with the two bases not reconcilable and not blendable). This is negative earnings momentum, the inverse of the pattern that supports a constructive rating.

**What growth remains.** NIDDA conversion still has runway toward management's 13% average growth target and a stated year-end NIM near 3.15%. Fee income is improving — service charges +18.6% year to date, capital-markets fees $8.1M driven by $6.8M of derivative income — though loan syndication fees were weak at $0.6M versus $3.1M a year earlier. Deposit insurance expense fell to $4.3M in 1H from $14.2M, including a non-repeating $6.7M FDIC special-assessment release. Net of all this, the durable per-share growth engine is the buyback, and per-share earnings growth of high-single digits is achievable in 2027 even with flat PPNR, provided the repurchase pace persists.

BKU trades at roughly a 25% earnings-multiple discount to the regional-bank comp median and yields 78bp less. The discount is not obviously an anomaly: BKU earns ~9% ROE against a plausible cost of equity at or slightly above that level, its estimates are falling while peers are not visibly cut, and its funding franchise carries a concentration profile (49% uninsured/uncollateralized, ~25% of deposits in two real-estate-linked verticals) that a diversified consumer-funded peer does not. At 1.16x tangible book for 9% ROE, the market is paying approximately what the returns justify. The bull case requires ROE moving toward the low-to-mid teens; the bear case is that 9% is the ceiling.

On run-rate sensitivities rather than forecasts: 1H26 annualized gives $3.58 (13.1x) and Q2 annualized gives $3.88 (12.1x). Both overstate normalized power in one respect — Q2's 0.11% annualized charge-off rate is well below the 0.35% trailing-twelve-month rate, and normalizing provision toward TTM losses trims roughly $0.14–0.18 per share per quarter of pre-tax equivalent, putting normalized run-rate EPS nearer $3.30–3.50. Management notes Q3/Q4 are seasonally the strongest lending quarters and Q2 the strongest deposit quarter, so simple doubling of the half is unlikely to be right in either direction.

**Scenario values.**

- **Bull $57** — 1.38x TBV / 12.7x $4.48. Requires the Fed to hike as management expects (year-1 +200bp sensitivity is +4.7% of NII, year-2 +9.5%), NIM through 3.15%, C&I criticized balances rolling back, and expense growth decelerating below revenue growth so PPNR inflects. Upside +21.9%. The published analyst high is $55.
- **Bear $37** — 0.91x TBV / ~10.3x depressed EPS near $3.60. Requires some combination of Fed cuts (year-2 −200bp sensitivity of −10.9% on ~$1.02B NII ≈ −$111M pre-tax, ~$1.15/share after tax), title-vertical deposit outflow on housing weakness, and C&I criticized migration converting to charge-offs. Downside −20.9%. The published analyst low is $42, above our bear.

Probability-weighting bull 20%, base 50%, a shallow bear (−10%) 20% and the deep bear 10% gives roughly +4% price return plus the dividend, or ~+7% total — approximately fair for a 0.9-beta bank, with the distribution skewed such that the downside tail is twice the base-case upside. That is a HOLD, not an ACCUMULATE.

## Competitive Landscape

BKU's differentiated asset is the deposit franchise, not the loan book. Management's claim that deposits grew ~$10B over ten years with ~$7B of that non-interest-bearing, entirely organically, is corroborated by the $77.6M goodwill balance on a $34.9B balance sheet — this was built, not acquired. The title-insurance and HOA verticals are national, treasury-management-intensive businesses with genuine switching costs, and 75% commercial/municipal deposits with 34.4% NIDDA is a better funding mix than most $30B-asset peers.

The corresponding weakness is that BKU is a price-setter on deposits and a spread-taker on assets. With 27.8% of loans in purchased residential and government-insured paper and a de-emphasized equipment finance book, the franchise-linked earning assets are ~72% of loans and are not growing. The 10-K's competition section names private credit funds and fintechs alongside banks and life insurers — and the Q2 commentary that competitors are mispricing credit is, from the other side, an admission that BKU's volume is hostage to industry pricing discipline it cannot influence. Trajectory is therefore split: funding position strengthening, asset-side share deliberately shrinking. BKU's decision to cut NDFI ("private credit") exposure from $1.5B (6% of loans) to $1.3B (5%) and to de-emphasize data-center lending is a prudent risk choice, but it also removes one of the few growing commercial verticals available.

Where BKU screens better than the regional-bank average: no HTM bucket (no hidden mark), AFS duration 2.02 years, ~65% floating securities, office CRE only 5.8% of loans with weighted-average DSCR 1.76 and LTV 65.6% (25% medical office), only ~$99M of New York rent-regulated multifamily, CET1 12.3%. Where it screens worse: ~49% uninsured/uncollateralized deposits, ~25% of deposits real-estate-activity-linked, 9% ROE, and five-year TSR of 150.72 against its own selected peer group at 152.71 — slight underperformance despite a strong 2025.

## Risk Assessment

**Deposit concentration in the residential real-estate ecosystem is the most specific, least-priced risk.** Title-insurance escrow deposits are ~$4.9B (~17% of total) and HOA ~$2.4B (~8%). Title escrow balances are a direct function of transaction volume. A 25% decline in transactions flowing proportionally to escrow would imply ~$1.2B of outflow; replacing it with brokered/FHLB funding at 3.5–3.8% costs ~$42–46M pre-tax annually, or roughly $0.44–0.48 per share after tax, partially offset by lower earnings-credit rebates. This is the channel through which a housing slowdown would reach BKU's income statement, and it is not visible in trailing data.

**The rate bet is two-sided and management has leaned into one side.** Year-1 NII sensitivity moved from +3.4%/−4.7% (±200bp) at year-end to **+4.7%/−5.5%** at June 30; year-2 from +6.2%/−8.8% to **+9.5%/−10.9%**. The CEO corrected an analyst on the call: "We're not expecting a cut, we're expecting a hike" — one hike in Q4 2026 and more in 2027, which is a distinctly non-consensus macro stance. If the Fed cuts 200bp, year-2 damage is roughly a third of current EPS, and the model assumes an 80% down-beta on non-maturity interest-bearing deposits — if achievable down-beta is lower, the outcome is worse than modeled. Whether the increased asset sensitivity is deliberate positioning or an arithmetic byproduct of hedge maturities (notional fell $4,680M → $3,630M) and the NIDDA mix shift cannot be determined from the disclosures, and management motive should not be inferred from the positioning.

**Uninsured deposits and liquidity cushion.** Insured *and collateralized* deposits are 51% of total. Every disclosed liquidity measure is within policy, but the thinnest relative cushion is available liquidity to uninsured/uncollateralized deposits at 119% against a 105% minimum. Others are comfortable: wholesale funding/assets 17.0% (limit 37.5%), stress coverage 2.59x (min 1.50x), one-year liquidity 3.33x (min 1.15x), top-20 uninsured depositors 12.5% (limit 15%).

**CRE maturity wall.** $1,918M of CRE (27%) matures within 12 months, of which $925M is fixed-rate to the borrower at a weighted-average coupon of only 4.29% — loans that reprice sharply higher at renewal. Office is heaviest, with $482M (35% of the office book) maturing inside a year, and all non-performing CRE excluding $30M of SBA is office.

**Credit trajectory.** The C&I criticized-accruing build (+58% to $305.4M) and the widening collateral shortfall on collateral-dependent loans (9.9% → 24.0% unsecured) are the two data points that argue the NPL improvement bought time rather than resolving the cycle. Both have benign alternative explanations (re-grading conservatism, mix shift toward receivables/inventory-secured C&I) and specific reserves are already in the 0.91% ACL (1.30% commercial). Treat as a hypothesis requiring two more quarters.

**Governance verification gap.** A cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks (November 10 and 21, 2025; May 12 and 21, 2026) coincides with an Amended and Restated Retention Letter for CFO James Mackey originally dated July 22, 2025 and amended May 11, 2026, identified only via the 10-Q exhibit index. A May 21 amended-and-restated omnibus equity plan suggests a broader compensation refresh that could require conforming amendments. The underlying documents were not retrievable. Whether these reflect departures, succession planning, or administrative amendments is unresolved and should not be treated as established in either direction.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director in the ~14 months through June 2026. Sales were small relative to holdings (director and officer lots of 1,000–5,000 shares at $34–$51), and the large March 2026 dispositions at $44.76 were net-of-tax vesting settlements, not sentiment. The CEO retains 280,407 shares. Absence of buying at 1.16x tangible book is a mild negative but weak evidence given blackout calendars and large unvested equity positions.

**Idiosyncratic strengths that offset.** No HTM overhang, 2.02-year AFS duration, government guarantee behind the delinquent Buyout Loan book, REO nearly cleared ($1.5M), and $204M of identified excess capital.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 28.3% against 29.0% one-year realized — fair, and in only the 12th percentile of the current chain. IV has compressed from 35.1% in late May with the price essentially unchanged ($46.64 → $46.81), consistent with resolved event risk after the July 22 print rather than accumulating concern. Term structure is flat (28.3% near versus 27.6% at six months), put/call open interest is 0.33 (call-tilted), and near-term skew is balanced at −1.8%. The one item worth noting is the six-month tenor's −13.1% skew reading alongside a +2.9% three-month reading; with only 34 contracts across four expirations, the sign convention and thin liquidity make directional inference unreliable, and we do not draw one. Net: the options market prices no near-term dislocation and does not corroborate either the bull or bear case. Short interest at 6.1% of float with 2.8 days to cover is uncrowded.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($57, +21.9%)

The funding transformation still has runway. NIDDA at 34.4% is a record, and management's raised 13% average-growth target implies continued displacement of ~3%-cost interest-bearing funds — each $1.0B of conversion is worth roughly $0.32 per share annually. Year-end NIM of ~3.15% is achievable, and if the Fed hikes as management expects, the +9.5% year-two asset sensitivity turns a headwind into a tailwind that consensus does not model. Expense growth was front-loaded on revenue-producer hiring in Dallas, Charlotte and Atlanta; if those hires produce in 2027, PPNR inflects and the efficiency ratio finally breaks below 60%, on which the 61.3% reported figure understates true efficiency by ~17bp of NIM-equivalent given earnings-credit classification. Credit resolves as a 2022–2023 vintage problem rather than a cycle, with Q2's 0.11% charge-off rate closer to the truth than Q1's 0.61%. Capital return continues at ~9% gross shareholder yield, retiring 6%+ of shares a year at 1.16x book. A 34% NIDDA franchise with no HTM overhang, organically built, is also a logical participant in regional-bank consolidation in either direction — though no filing or company statement indicates any transaction is contemplated.

### Bear Case ($37, −20.9%)

Nine percent is the ceiling on ROE, and 1.16x book is the ceiling on the multiple. The margin gain has been fully consumed by compensation, PPNR is flat, and the asset side cannot grow at acceptable spreads because competitors have abandoned pricing discipline — a condition BKU cannot fix and management concedes is "harder and harder to hold the line" against. EPS growth is manufactured by a buyback running at 120% of earnings against a shrinking balance sheet, with ~$204M of excess capital plus retained earnings supporting perhaps two to three more years at this pace. Then the Fed cuts instead of hiking, costing up to $1.15 per share in year two on a balance sheet that got *more* asset-sensitive; and a housing slowdown drains title escrow balances that fund 17% of the bank, forcing brokered replacement at 3.5%+. Underneath, C&I criticized-accruing has already risen 58% and the collateral shortfall on workout loans has widened from 10% to 24%. Estimates keep grinding lower, and 2 of 11 analysts already carry sell ratings, up from 1 three months ago.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $50 (+6.9% price, ~+9.7% with the dividend), bull $57 (+21.9%), bear $37 (−20.9%) from $46.765. Probability-weighted total return of roughly +7% over 12 months is approximately fair compensation for the risk, with the downside tail about twice the base-case upside. Existing holders are paid a well-covered 2.82% dividend (34% of current-year consensus) plus a buyback retiring 6%+ of shares annually; prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at 1.16x book on falling estimates. This is the third consecutive HOLD with a declining score (6.1 → 5.7 → 5.4); the deterioration is located specifically in growth prospects (guidance cut, flat PPNR, C&I contraction) rather than in balance-sheet strength or credit, and the price has been flat while estimates fell 4.4%, so risk/reward is modestly worse than at the prior review, not better.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **PPNR inflection**: two consecutive quarters in which pre-provision net revenue grows faster than 8% year over year with the efficiency ratio printing below 60% — evidence that the 12.2% compensation growth is producing revenue rather than absorbing margin.
2. **Core loan growth returning to guidance with spread intact**: core loans (CRE + C&I + warehouse + Pinnacle) growing at a 4%+ annualized pace for two quarters *without* a decline in new-origination loan yields, plus C&I balances stabilizing after the $367M six-month decline.
3. **Price at or below $41–42** (approximately 1.02–1.04x current TBVPS), where the ~9% shareholder yield and tangible-book support provide margin of safety against the rate and title-deposit risks.
4. **Estimate revisions turning positive**: current-year consensus rising above $4.00 after two quarters of stabilization.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Title/HOA deposit outflow**: a decline of more than $500M in combined title-insurance and HOA deposits in any single quarter, or NIDDA/total deposits falling below 31%, indicating the funding engine has reversed.
2. **C&I credit confirmation**: criticized-accruing C&I balances rising above ~$350M, or net charge-offs exceeding 0.45% annualized in any quarter, which would validate the migration hypothesis and force provision above guidance.
3. **Rate path against the positioning**: two Fed cuts totaling 50bp or more without offsetting hedge additions, given the −5.5% year-one and −10.9% year-two NII sensitivity to −200bp.
4. **Buyback suspension** or CET1 target revised upward, which would remove the primary per-share earnings lever.
5. **Resolution of the Item 5.02 cluster** revealing an unplanned CFO or senior-management departure.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (expected late October) is the natural decision point — it covers management's stated seasonally strongest lending quarter, will show whether the 4–5% core loan guidance is being met, whether NIM is tracking toward ~3.15%, and whether C&I criticized balances continued to build. The November/December board discussion of the next buyback tranche is a secondary checkpoint on capital-return durability. The September FOMC meeting is the relevant macro checkpoint for the asset-sensitivity bet.

## Conclusion

BankUnited's deposit franchise is better than its earnings. Converting $1.9B of brokered funding into $825M of new non-interest-bearing balances in six months and taking NIDDA to a record 34.4% is genuine franchise work, and the compensation plan is unusually well matched to it — NIDDA/total deposits is a named performance measure, and the CEO's stated priority, the pay metric and the earnings driver are the same variable. Credit also improved on the surface, with non-accruals down 40% and coverage rebuilt to 97%.

None of it is compounding into shareholder value at the pace the funding progress implies. The efficiency ratio was 61.3% in both halves; PPNR grew 5.6%; Q2 PPNR was flat year over year; core loans grew $14M in six months while C&I fell $367M; and guidance for both loan growth and NII was cut. Per-share progress now depends on retiring 6%+ of the shares annually with capital that is finite (~$204M above the mid-11s CET1 target) and distributions running at 120% of earnings against a shrinking balance sheet. Layered on top are two specific, unpriced exposures: ~25% of deposits keyed to residential real-estate activity, and a balance sheet that has become *more* asset-sensitive while management bets publicly on rate hikes, a distinctly non-consensus stance.

At 1.16x tangible book for a 9.3% ROE, 11.9x current-year and 10.4x next-year consensus, the price is about right for what the business earns. The 25% multiple discount to peers is explained by the lower return, the falling estimates and the concentration profile, not by a mispricing we can identify. **HOLD**: own it for the capital return and the funding franchise if you already do, but wait for either PPNR inflection or a price near tangible book before adding.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: several macro claims presented as fact (mortgage rates at one-year highs, existing home sales ~4.09M, five consecutive Fed holds with three dissents, core PCE 3.3%, July payrolls −23,000 vs +83,000 consensus) are not supported anywhere in the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed — the bear-case rate and housing arguments now rest only on management's disclosed sensitivities and the brief's own risk factors, not on external data points; this weakens some of the downside timing arguments used to justify the HOLD's skewed risk/reward.
