**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UL Solutions is the commercial entity descended from Underwriters Laboratories, operating the most recognized safety certification mark in North America and running a global Testing, Inspection and Certification (TIC) business with a large recurring revenue base. The investment case rests on two real, observable developments: a fast-moving margin inflection (Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 320 basis points year-over-year to 26.0% in Q1 2026, with free cash flow margin moving from ~15% to ~20%) and disciplined capital allocation that has cut total debt from $747M at YE2024 to $360M by Q1 2026 while management prunes non-core software and reinvests in the core certification franchise. Consensus EPS estimates have risen +11.0% for the current year over 90 days, confirming that the operating momentum is being recognized by the sell-side.

The constraint is valuation and structure. At $89.70 the stock trades around 56x trailing EPS of $1.60 and roughly 39x the current-year consensus of $2.30, which is full for a high-single-digit organic grower even after normalizing for quality. The peer-median P/E of 46.8x flatters the comparison, since that median is distorted by negative- and triple-digit-multiple names; on a growth-adjusted basis ULS is priced for sustained margin gains to continue, not merely to hold. Layered on top is a controlled-company governance structure in which UL Standards & Engagement (ULSE) holds 61.6% of equity but 94.1% of voting power, with consent rights extending to CEO succession, large M&A, and dividend policy. The dual-class structure makes ULS ineligible for certain indices, mechanically capping passive demand and acting as a structural ceiling on the multiple.

The stock has retraced roughly 17% from its 52-week high of $107.54 (reached in early May) to $89.70. That improves the risk/reward at the margin but does not resolve the core tension: a genuinely good business at a price that already discounts the good news, with idiosyncratic governance and China-concentration risks that the index exclusion and analyst dispersion (one strongSell among 13 ratings until recently) reflect. We maintain a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, looking for either a better entry or confirmation that the margin step-up is durable rather than restructuring-driven.

## Company Overview

UL Solutions provides independent third-party TIC services, advisory offerings, and risk-and-compliance software across roughly 87 lab sites in 27 countries. Approximately 59% of 2025 revenue was generated outside the United States. The business reorganized into three segments effective Q1 2026:

- **Industrial** (~$375M Q1 revenue, ~50% of total, ~27% operating margin): TIC and advisory for energy, industrial automation, engineered materials, and the built environment. The highest-margin, fastest-growing segment, up 10.3% YoY (+8.2% organic).
- **Consumer** (~$318M Q1, ~42%, ~9% operating margin): consumer electronics, medical devices, appliances, HVAC, lighting. Lower margin but improving sharply, with operating margin moving from 7.2% to 9.4% YoY.
- **Risk & Compliance Software** (~$65M Q1, ~9%, ~11% operating margin): SaaS for regulatory compliance and supply-chain transparency.

The moat sits in the recurring "Ongoing Certification Services" line (revenue customers pay to keep previously certified products compliant and to retain the right to use the UL Mark), which was $265M of $758M total Q1 revenue. The UL-in-a-circle mark is embedded in U.S. state and local building codes, retailer requirements, and the demands of Authorities Having Jurisdiction, and is reinforced by accreditations (ISO/IEC 17025/17020/17065). That regulatory embeddedness, particularly in electrical and built-environment categories in North America, is the source of durable pricing power and high switching costs.

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 results show broad-based profitability gains with concurrent deleveraging:

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $705M | $758M | +7.5% (+5.7% organic) |
| Operating income | $109M | $138M | +27%; margin 15.5%→18.2% |
| Net income | $71M | $97M | +37% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $161M | $197M | margin 22.8%→26.0% |
| Diluted EPS | $0.33 | $0.45 | +36% |
| Free cash flow | $103M | $150M | margin 14.6%→19.8% |

FY2025 net income was ~$345M and company-selected Adjusted EBITDA rose from $656M (2024) to $792M (2025). The margin expansion has three identifiable drivers: organic growth, the November 2025 restructuring (~$40M total pre-tax, mostly cash severance concentrated in Consumer), and operating leverage on a fixed lab-cost base. Margins improved in all three segments simultaneously, which argues the gain is structural rather than a one-segment artifact, though part of the Consumer step-up is cost-out that does not repeat.

The balance sheet has improved materially, with the revolver paid down from $191M to $57M in the quarter. The capital structure consists of $300M of 6.500% senior notes due 2028 plus a revolver on a new $1.0B facility (October 2025, matures 2030); cash was $258M with ~$937M of revolver availability. Interest expense is falling ($12M to $8M YoY). Operating cash flow of $219M in Q1 substantially exceeded net income, helped by contract-liability growth as customers prepay ongoing certification, a favorable working-capital dynamic characteristic of recurring TIC models and a marker of high earnings quality. Capex is rising ($51M to $69M) as the company expands lab capacity in Singapore, the U.S., Germany, and Korea, which is appropriate reinvestment behind demand.

ROE is approximately 27% and D/E ended FY2025 around 1.3x, now lower after Q1 paydown. The grey-zone Altman Z-Score that screens flag is a function of moderate leverage rather than operational distress, and that leverage has been actively reduced. The earnings beat track record is consistent (three beats of 16-20% in the last four quarters, one small miss), which supports confidence in forward estimates.

One critical normalization: reported FY2026 net income and EPS will be flattered by approximately $291M of one-time pre-tax gains — the EHS software divestiture (~$191M, recorded Q2 2026) and the DQS stake sale (~$100M, 2H 2026). Both are non-operating. Underlying operating momentum is strong on its own, but the headline FY2026 numbers will overstate the run-rate, and any valuation work should use normalized operating earnings, not reported.

## Growth Analysis

Organic revenue growth was 5.7% in Q1, within a high-single-digit corridor that the Industrial segment (+8.2% organic) leads. Secular tailwinds are genuine and visible in the segment drivers: electrification and the energy transition, industrial automation, battery and lithium-ion safety, connected/IoT device proliferation, and cybersecurity certification all drive demand for new and ongoing testing. The recurring ongoing-certification base provides a compounding floor independent of new-product cycles.

Estimate revisions reinforce the trajectory. Current-year consensus rose to $2.30 over 90 days and next-year +9.5% (to $2.58), with smaller but positive near-term quarter revisions. These are meaningful, but they sit below the +30% inflection threshold that would signal the market is systematically underestimating a trough-to-recovery earnings swing. ULS is a steady compounder whose margins are stepping up, not a depressed cyclical snapping back. The distinction matters: rising estimates on a steady grower at ~39x forward earnings are largely already in the price, whereas the strongest estimate-revision buy signal applies to cheap stocks (forward P/E <30x) where consensus lags reality.

The E&E acquisition (Electrical and Electronics Testing LUX Holding, €575M enterprise value, expected Q4 2026 close) is a sizable bolt-on directly into the core electrical/electronics testing franchise, funded with cash and revolver. Combined with the EHS divestiture and folding Advisory into Industrial, the strategic direction is a deliberate concentration into the highest-margin, highest-moat certification business. That is the right capital-allocation instinct, and it should support both growth and margin durability if integration goes cleanly.

The principal growth question is whether the 320bp margin jump is a one-time reset (restructuring + favorable working capital) or a new plateau that compounds. If it plateaus, forward EPS growth reverts toward mid-to-high single-digit organic plus bolt-on M&A — solid, but not enough to justify a 39x forward multiple expanding further.

## Valuation Assessment

At $89.70, ULS trades at roughly 56x trailing EPS of $1.60 (skewed low by prior-period timing) and approximately 39x the $2.30 current-year consensus, 35x the $2.58 next-year consensus. The dividend yield is ~0.65% after the raise to $0.145/quarter, so income provides little support.

The peer table shows a median P/E of 46.8x, but that median is unreliable: Mercury Systems screens negative (-502x), Enpro at 163x, MYR Group at 47x, while Air Lease (a different business model) sits at 7x. The dispersion means there is no clean sector multiple to anchor against. Against the relevant global TIC comparables — SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek — ULS commands a premium that its faster margin trajectory and stronger North American regulatory moat partly justify, but the premium also reflects scarcity value as the only U.S.-listed pure-play of scale.

Applying our calibration discipline: a high valuation score requires a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock. The estimate-revision velocity is real but below the inflection threshold, and at ~39x forward it is largely priced. The cycle-adjusted argument does not apply forcefully here — ULS is not at a trough; FY2026 reported earnings will be inflated by one-time gains, not depressed. The analyst-targets-as-floors principle is muted because the stock, while not at 50x forward, is not cheap relative to its growth either (forward PEG roughly 4-5x on ~9% EPS growth). Consensus targets (mean $108.95, median $112.00, range $78-$120) imply ~25% upside to the median, but the dual-class index exclusion is a persistent reason the multiple may not re-rate to those levels absent continued margin beats.

We score valuation at 5.0 — neither a clear mispricing nor a value trap. The quality is real and the price is full. Treating a stock as "cheap" because the distorted peer median is higher has historically sorted the wrong way; the more honest read is that ULS is priced for the margin inflection to hold and continue, which is a demanding bar.

## Competitive Landscape

The TIC industry is, in management's own words, "highly competitive and fragmented." Global competitors include SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, the TÜV organizations, DEKRA, and Eurofins, alongside regional players and PE-backed entrants. ULS's differentiator is the UL Mark's embeddedness in North American building codes, AHJ requirements, and retailer mandates, which is strongest in electrical and built-environment categories. That embeddedness creates switching costs that are regulatory and reputational, not merely commercial.

Competitive trajectory is improving at the margin: Industrial is growing faster than the broader TIC market, the company is gaining operating leverage, and it is consolidating the field through the E&E acquisition. The recurring ongoing-certification revenue line provides annuity-like stickiness that pure project-based competitors lack.

Two structural threats to the moat warrant respect rather than dismissal. First, regulatory commoditization — a shift toward self-declaration of conformity, global standards harmonization/mutual recognition, or post-*Loper Bright* deregulation — could reduce mandated third-party testing. Second, management's own 10-K elevates AI/automation as a risk that could replicate or bypass human-performed testing, and raises the possibility that AI-designed products embedding safety standards become the trusted norm over human-driven design. These are long-tail risks, but for a labor-intensive certification business they are not boilerplate, and they distinguish ULS's competitive risk profile from a typical industrial.

## Risk Assessment

**Controlled-company / dual-class structure (the defining risk).** ULSE controls 94.1% of voting power via 10-vote Class B shares, with consent rights over M&A above 15% of market cap, new business lines, debt that would trigger a sub-IG downgrade, dividend policy, and the explicit right to approve any CEO other than the incumbent. Sunset is the earlier of seven years post-IPO (April 2024) or Class B falling below 35% of the IPO level, so public holders have minimal governance power until at least 2031. ULSE may also sell a control stake privately with no change-of-control premium to public holders. The controller is a nonprofit with a public-safety mission, which creates a genuine, disclosed conflict: UL Research Institutes could publish research or ULSE could write standards that harm ULS's own customers.

**China / CCIC concentration.** ULS owns 70% of UL-CCIC; the 30% partner CCIC is a Chinese state-owned enterprise that performs ~36% of global ongoing-certification inspections, with the JV agreement expiring January 2033. The post-August 2025 FCC rule under which UL-CCIC withdrew as an FCC-recognized testing lab is a concrete, if individually immaterial, example of U.S.-China decoupling touching the business. SAFE foreign-exchange controls can restrict repatriating UL-CCIC earnings. This is a large, underappreciated dependency.

**Litigation and brand.** The Martucci putative class action (N.D. Ill., filed February 11, 2026) alleges UL-listed combination burglar/fire alarm control units had concealed defects non-compliant with UL/NFPA 72 standards. The company calls it meritless with no loss estimate, but the entire enterprise value rests on the UL Mark's credibility, so brand-impairment litigation carries outsized reputational tail risk.

**Other.** Conflict-of-interest exposure between TIC and Advisory under ISO/IEC 17065 (sharpened by folding Advisory into Industrial), and the AI/self-declaration commoditization threats noted above.

We score risk at 5.0. These are specific, identifiable, partly idiosyncratic risks — index exclusion and a 36% China-JV dependency are not generic sector truisms, and they are not fully priced given the premium multiple. The risks are balanced against the strong balance sheet and recurring revenue base.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.6% sits slightly below 1-year historical volatility of 40.8% (a -2.3% spread), so options are fairly priced with no elevated fear premium. IV percentile is at the 6th percentile within the current chain — complacent. Put/call skew is balanced (0.4%), and the term structure is in contango (38.6% near vs. 43.8% long-dated), the normal/complacent configuration. The put/call volume ratio of 1.50 and OI ratio of 1.24 lean modestly defensive, and the OI ratio has declined from 1.45 a month ago as the price fell ~9%, suggesting some bearish positioning was unwound into the decline rather than added. The options market is not signaling an imminent catalyst or directional conviction in either direction, consistent with a fairly-valued stock between earnings events. The implied ±7.9% move for the nearest expiry frames the near-term range.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$120, ~34% upside).** The margin inflection proves durable — 26% Adjusted EBITDA margin holds and grinds higher as the restructuring fully annualizes and Industrial mix shifts up. Organic growth sustains high-single-digits on electrification/automation/IoT tailwinds, the E&E acquisition integrates accretively, and FCF margin holds near 20%. Continued double-digit estimate revisions force the sell-side to chase, and the scarcity premium as the only U.S.-listed pure-play TIC name of scale supports a ~45x forward multiple on ~$2.65+ normalized EPS. This is the high analyst target.

**Bear case (~$70, ~22% downside).** The 320bp margin jump is revealed as a restructuring/working-capital reset that plateaus; organic growth decelerates toward mid-single-digits; a China/CCIC disruption (further decoupling, JV friction, repatriation constraints) impairs ~36% of inspection capacity; or the index-exclusion overhang reasserts as growth normalizes. The multiple compresses toward 28-30x forward on the realization that this is a good but not exceptional compounder paying a ~0.65% yield. The Martucci litigation or an adverse standards/self-declaration development adds a brand-risk discount.

**Base case (~$100, ~12% upside).** Margins hold near the new plateau without dramatic further expansion, organic growth stays mid-to-high single digits, the E&E deal closes and contributes, and the stock re-rates modestly as estimates continue rising but the index/governance ceiling caps the multiple around 38-40x normalized forward EPS of ~$2.55-$2.60.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 6-12 months, with the next natural checkpoints being the Q2 2026 print (which will carry the EHS gain and require careful normalization), the E&E acquisition close (expected Q4 2026, regulatory-contingent), and the DQS sale completion in 2H 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence the 26% Adjusted EBITDA margin is a durable plateau (two-plus consecutive quarters holding or expanding ex-restructuring), combined with organic growth holding ≥6%.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s, which would compress forward P/E toward ~30x and create genuine asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.
- A clear de-risking of the China/CCIC dependency, or any index-eligibility development (Class B reduction toward the sunset threshold) that removes the passive-demand cap.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic revenue growth decelerating below ~4% YoY, signaling the secular tailwind is fading.
- A material China/CCIC disruption affecting ongoing-certification inspection capacity, or a forced JV restructuring before 2033.
- A meaningful adverse development in the Martucci litigation or a regulatory shift toward self-declaration that visibly reduces certification demand.
- Evidence the margin gains reverse as restructuring benefits lap and cost re-enters the base.

## Conclusion

UL Solutions is a high-quality, moat-protected compounder demonstrating a real and fast margin inflection, supported by rising estimates, deleveraging, and coherent capital allocation toward its core certification franchise. The score trajectory (6.2 → 6.3) and the modest improvements in management-quality and risk perception across prior reports are consistent with a business executing well. None of that is in dispute.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a full ~39x forward multiple that already prices the inflection, a ~0.65% yield that provides little downside support, and structural overhangs — the dual-class control structure with index exclusion and the ~36% China-JV inspection dependency — that are idiosyncratic and not fully discounted. The ~17% pullback from the May high improves entry and is not, by itself, a reason to turn negative, since fundamentals are intact and estimates are still rising. But it does not yet create the asymmetric upside that would justify upgrading, particularly given that the strongest estimate-revision and cycle-adjusted buy signals do not apply cleanly to a steady grower whose FY2026 reported earnings will be inflated by ~$291M of one-time gains. We rate ULS a HOLD (growth-hold), with a clear path to a BUY on either a better entry in the low-to-mid $70s or confirmation that the margin step-up is a durable plateau rather than a restructuring-driven reset.
