**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Everus Construction Group is a specialty electrical, mechanical, and utility contractor spun off tax-free from MDU Resources in October 2024. The company has become one of the more direct mid-cap public expressions of the data-center construction cycle: FY2025 revenue grew 31.5% to $3.746B, Q1 2026 revenue grew 25.4% to $1.037B, and backlog rebounded to $3.68B. The Electrical & Mechanical segment, which is roughly 78% of revenue and driven overwhelmingly by data centers, produced nearly all of that growth (E&M +43.8% in 2025), while Transmission & Distribution was roughly flat and institutional work (healthcare, education) is now declining. The top-line story is real and demand-driven, but increasingly narrow.

Two facts complicate the clean-beat narrative and hold the rating at HOLD rather than BUY. First, customer concentration has intensified rapidly: a single customer moved from under 10% of revenue in 2024 to 17% in FY2025 and 19.1% in Q1 2026 (23.6% of the E&M segment, and 19.7% of trade receivables). This is both the growth engine and the single largest point of failure. Second, earnings quality is softer than the headline suggests. Cumulative catch-up changes in percentage-of-completion estimates added $83.7M to FY2025 net income (roughly $1.64 of diluted EPS, or about 42% of the full year) and $41.5M to Q1 2026 net income ($0.81, or about 71% of the quarter). Reported margins are running ahead of cash conversion; FY2025 free cash flow margin was only about 2.4%.

The stock has fallen roughly 22% from its early-May high of $171.58 to $133.23 while consensus estimates held flat-to-up over the trailing 90 days (FY26 EPS revised +10.5% to $4.62, FY27 +7.1% to $5.12). A lower price on stable-to-improving fundamentals improves the risk/reward, and analyst targets ($157 low, $185 high, $172 median) sit well above the current price. But at roughly 29x FY26 consensus EPS the multiple embeds continued data-center strength, and the concentration plus estimate-driven earnings quality argue for a growth-hold posture: own it for the compounding, but size it for the binary risk that a single hyperscaler-adjacent client and one capex cycle carry.

## Company Overview

Everus is headquartered in Bismarck, North Dakota, and traces its operating history to 1997 as MDU's construction segment. It became an independent public company on October 31, 2024 (one ECG share per four MDU shares). The company runs a decentralized model of 16 wholly-owned operating companies under 19 local brands, employs roughly 9,400 people (about 85% unionized, largely IBEW), and serves approximately 4,000 customers across 44,000-plus projects. It ranks #12 on the ENR Top 600 Specialty Contractors and #5 on the EC&M Top 50 Electrical Contractors.

Two segments define the business:

- **Electrical & Mechanical (E&M)** — approximately 78% of 2025 revenue at a 7.5% operating margin. Electrical and communication wiring, fire suppression, mechanical piping, and renewables work. Demand is driven overwhelmingly by data centers, with secondary exposure to hospitality, high-tech reshoring, healthcare, and education.
- **Transmission & Distribution (T&D)** — approximately 22% of revenue at a higher 10.6% operating margin. Overhead and underground electric, gas, and communications infrastructure for utilities, plus line-stringing equipment manufacturing and rental (Wagner Smith Equipment). Concentrated in Missouri, California, Montana, and Oregon, and MSA-driven, which gives it a more recurring revenue profile than E&M.

Competitors include Comfort Systems (FIX), EMCOR (EME), IES Holdings (IESC), MasTec (MTZ), MYR Group (MYRG), Primoris (PRIM), Quanta (PWR), and Sterling Infrastructure (STRL), alongside large private players such as Rosendin and M.C. Dean. The specialty-contracting market is highly fragmented and competes on price, safety record, and reliability.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials show strong growth and improving headline margins:

- **FY2025:** revenue $3.746B (+31.5% from $2.85B), net income $201.8M (+40.7%), diluted EPS ~$3.95, EBITDA $319.8M (8.5% margin, up from 8.1%). Gross margin 12.1%, operating margin 7.1%, net margin 5.4%.
- **Q1 2026:** revenue $1.037B (+25.4%), net income $58.3M (+58.9%), diluted EPS $1.14 (versus $0.72), operating margin expanded to 7.5%, EBITDA margin 8.6%.

The growth is concentrated. E&M commercial revenue rose $876.8M in 2025 while T&D grew only 1.4%. In Q1 2026, E&M commercial grew $235.5M while institutional work declined $41.6M. The company is growing by leaning harder into one end market, not by broadening its base.

The balance sheet is healthy. Altman Z-Score is 3.92 (safe zone), Piotroski 4/5, current ratio 1.76. Debt is modest: a $285M term loan at 5.70% plus an undrawn $225M revolver. Net leverage is well under the 3.0x covenant and interest coverage is far above the 3.0x minimum. Cash grew to $293.4M by Q1 2026. ROE is 32% and ROA 11.7%. There is no dividend, and buybacks and large equity actions are restricted until around October 2026 under the tax matters agreement with MDU.

Cash flow is the caveat. FY2025 free cash flow was only roughly $90-100M against $201.8M of net income, because operating cash flow of $156.8M trailed net income as rapid growth consumed working capital (contract assets and receivables). Q1 2026 operating cash flow rebounded to $143.7M from $7.1M on favorable contract-liability and billing timing. Capex is guided to $90-100M for 2026, up from $66.8M. Lumpy, billing-timing-dependent cash flow is normal for the sector, but the gap between reported earnings and cash conversion is wider here than the headline growth implies, partly because of the estimate-revision dynamic discussed below.

The earnings-quality question is the most important input for scoring. Everus uses cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion accounting. Favorable estimate revisions are a large and recurring component of reported profit. This is standard for large-project contractors, but it means a meaningful slice of the reported margin expansion is estimate-driven rather than structural, and it introduces the risk that revisions could swing unfavorably in a weaker execution period.

The earnings surprise record is strong: four consecutive beats, ranging from 39.2% to 77.8% above consensus. That track record supports confidence in near-term estimates, though it partly reflects the same conservative-bid, favorable-catch-up mechanics that make the earnings lumpy.

## Growth Analysis

The secular tailwind is real at the industry level. Data-center demand is the dominant growth driver across Everus's end markets, and the buildout is broad-based rather than a single-company story. Everus is a direct picks-and-shovels beneficiary through its E&M segment, and its Q1 2026 beat came in the same quarter peer Primoris missed, which points to strong project execution.

Backlog supports the near-term trajectory: $3.68B at Q1 2026 (E&M $3.29B, T&D $389M), up from $3.23B at YE2025 and $3.06B a year earlier. FY2026 revenue is guided to $4.3-4.4B, incorporating the SE&M acquisition. Estimate revisions are supportive but not explosive, with 30-day revisions flat. This is positive momentum that has stabilized rather than the +30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine inflection buy signal. The estimate-revision picture is constructive but does not, on its own, force a BUY.

The concern within growth is narrowing. Nearly all of it flows from E&M commercial and data centers; T&D is flat and institutional is declining. Most contracts run under 12 months, so backlog visibility is real but short. The thesis rests heavily on the AI/data-center capex cycle continuing at its current pace. Everus itself added a dedicated risk factor acknowledging that its revenue mix has shifted heavily toward data centers and that it cannot guarantee this demand will be sustained.

The SE&M acquisition (closed April 1, 2026; $158M cash plus an earnout up to 8% of price) is the first deal as a standalone public company and signals a return to the historical roll-up strategy. It expands the Southeast footprint into pharmaceutical, complex-industrial, and healthcare MEP work, which modestly diversifies away from data centers, and it was funded from cash with no new debt. Because the deal closed after Q1 2026 ended March 31, Q1 2026 results do not include any SE&M contribution; the first reporting quarter with SE&M contribution is Q2 2026. With the two-year post-spin M&A restriction lapsing around October 2026 and ample balance-sheet headroom, further M&A is the most credible path to broadening the growth base — a key thing to track.

## Valuation Assessment

At $133.23 the stock trades at roughly 29x FY26 consensus EPS of $4.62 and about 26x FY27 of $5.12, with a PEG in the 1.5-2.0 range depending on the growth rate applied. Trailing P/E is 30.8x on $3.95.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E in Construction & Engineering is 28.4x (MYRG 46.8, GVA 37.5, ACA 28.4, FLR 22.8, TTEK 16.6). ECG at ~29x forward sits near the peer median, not at a discount. Given its faster revenue growth (25-31% versus most peers in the single-to-mid teens), a market-median multiple is arguably reasonable, but it is not the kind of gap that signals clear mispricing. The dispersion in the peer set is wide, reflecting a heterogeneous group where end-market exposure (data centers versus environmental services) matters more than the average.

Analyst targets support upside from here: low $157, high $185, mean $169.60, median $172. On the inflection-stock framework, these would function as floors that lag reality, but that logic applies most cleanly when a stock is cheap relative to rising estimates (forward P/E under 30x, PEG under 1.5). ECG sits right at the boundary, so the targets are informative as a directional signal (all above current price, sentiment improving with a recent buy-rating upgrade) without being a decisive undervaluation case.

I score valuation 5.5. The stock is not stretched, the ~22% pullback from the May high has improved the entry, and estimates held or rose while price fell — a better risk/reward than at $171. But two factors cap the score. First, a meaningful slice of the earnings that anchor the multiple is estimate-revision-driven rather than cash-backed, so the "E" in the P/E is softer than it looks. Second, the ~29x forward multiple embeds continued data-center strength, and I treat a cheap-looking multiple as a buy signal only when there is a specific catalyst the market is missing; here the catalyst (data-center demand) is well understood and already in the price. This is fair value with modest upside, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Everus is a mid-cap consolidator in a fragmented specialty-contracting market riding secular tailwinds: data-center and AI buildout, high-tech reshoring, grid modernization, and utility infrastructure investment. Its differentiation is a decentralized local-brand model (19 brands) combined with corporate scale for bonding capacity, national-customer relationships, and a safety and execution reputation. The Q1 2026 beat against Primoris's miss is evidence of execution quality.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on execution but carries a structural vulnerability. Everus is more concentrated in data centers than most of its larger, better-capitalized peers (Quanta, EMCOR, MasTec, Comfort Systems). Those peers can win the same hyperscaler work with deeper balance sheets and broader geographic footprints. The T&D segment provides some higher-margin, MSA-driven recurring revenue that offsets E&M cyclicality, but T&D is not growing, so the segment mix is drifting toward the more competitive, more concentrated part of the business.

I score competitive position 6.2, consistent with the prior read. Execution is strong and the company is winning share in a growing end market, but the moat is not widening relative to larger peers, and the increasing customer concentration is a competitive-position weakness dressed as a growth story: winning more of one hyperscaler's business is not the same as durable, diversified share gains.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is where this name earns its HOLD rather than BUY.

1. **Customer concentration is intensifying and is the single biggest risk.** Top 10 customers are 43% of revenue. Loss or a capex pullback from the dominant client — almost certainly a hyperscaler data-center customer — would be materially damaging, and the receivables concentration compounds the credit exposure.

2. **Data-center/AI cycle dependence.** The dedicated new risk factor is candid: the revenue mix has shifted heavily to data centers, and demand sustainability is not guaranteed. This is the same open question hanging over the entire AI-capex complex. A demand-side slowdown would hit ECG's core growth driver directly while institutional work is already contracting.

3. **Earnings quality / estimate reliance.** Favorable cost-to-cost estimate revisions are the most important item to weigh against the clean-beat narrative; unfavorable swings could sharply pressure reported earnings even if revenue holds.

4. **Fixed-price and input-cost exposure.** About 52% of revenue is fixed-price, exposing the company to labor, copper/aluminum/steel, and tariff-driven material inflation that is not always passable through.

5. **Surety bonding dependence.** About $2.1B in bonds outstanding; a reduction in bonding capacity would constrain the business.

6. **Spin-related items.** Limited standalone history, an uncapped tax indemnity to MDU, two-year restrictions on M&A and equity actions through roughly October 2026, and Section 355(e) tax risk. A $31.3M customer payment dispute (open since October 2023) remains unresolved and litigable.

7. **Auditor change.** An Item 4.01 8-K in January 2026 with a March 2026 amendment is consistent with a newly independent spin-off transitioning off its parent's audit relationship. The Q1 2026 10-Q confirmed effective disclosure controls and no material weaknesses, so this is not treated as a red flag, but it reduces the length and comparability of the audited track record.

I score risk factors 4.8. The concentration and earnings-quality issues are specific, quantified, and not yet fully reflected in a multiple that assumes continued data-center strength. These are idiosyncratic, binary-adjacent risks that warrant a below-neutral score rather than a generic "construction is cyclical" framing.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV rose from 53.5% to 73.6% over the past month as the stock fell 15.5%, and near-term IV sits rich versus one-year historical volatility of 59.6% (a 14-point spread, 77th percentile within the current chain). The term structure is in backwardation (73.6% near-term versus 66.8% at six months), consistent with the market pricing an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings report. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±17.9%. Skew is call-heavy (-3.3%): despite the sharp drawdown, the options market is not showing the elevated put demand that typically accompanies a deteriorating fundamental view. That reads as event uncertainty rather than directional bearishness, consistent with the fundamental picture — a good business facing a binary concentration/cycle question at earnings, not a collapsing one. Short interest is low at 4.9% of float (2.9 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($185-200):** Data-center capex continues at pace, backlog keeps building above $3.7B, and the SE&M deal plus the post-October-2026 return to M&A broadens the revenue base away from the single dominant customer. Estimate revisions continue positive, the four-quarter beat streak extends, and cash conversion normalizes as working-capital growth moderates. On $5.12+ FY27 EPS at a 32-36x multiple justified by 20%+ growth and index inclusion, the stock reaches the high-$180s to low-$200s. This is roughly the Oppenheimer $180 case.

**Bear Case ($95-110):** The dominant customer cuts capex or shifts work to a larger peer, E&M commercial growth stalls while institutional keeps declining, and an unfavorable swing in percentage-of-completion estimates exposes how much of reported profit was catch-up-driven. Multiple compresses toward the low-20s on decelerating growth and a re-rating of earnings quality. On roughly $4.50-4.75 of more conservatively-recognized EPS at 22-24x, the stock falls to the $95-110 range, a decline of roughly 17-29% from current levels.

**Base Case ($150-160):** Data-center demand holds but growth decelerates from 25-31% toward the mid-teens as comparisons harden. Backlog remains healthy, SE&M adds modest diversification, and estimates drift up in line with the current +7-10% annual revision pace. On ~$5.10 FY27 EPS at ~30x, the stock reaches the low-to-mid $150s, roughly 13-20% above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $155**, derived from ~30x FY27 consensus EPS of $5.12, a multiple in line with the peer median and supported by 15-20% forward growth. This sits between the bear and bull cases.

**Bull case target: $190** (roughly 37x FY27 EPS on sustained 20%+ growth plus continued M&A and index-flow support).

**Bear case target: $105** (roughly 22x more conservatively-recognized earnings on a growth deceleration and earnings-quality re-rating), about 21% below the current price.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two to three earnings reports (the next report is the near-term catalyst the options market is pricing) and the October 2026 lapse of the M&A restriction.

**Upside/downside from current $133.23:** +16% to base case, +43% to bull case, -21% to bear case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The dominant customer's share of revenue stabilizes or declines while total revenue growth stays above 20%, evidence the base is broadening rather than concentrating.
- Free cash flow conversion improves toward 60%+ of net income for two consecutive quarters, closing the gap between reported earnings and cash.
- A pullback toward the mid-$110s to low-$120s that would push the forward multiple below the peer median and PEG below 1.5.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The dominant customer share exceeds ~25% of revenue, or a disclosed loss/reduction of that relationship.
- Any quarter showing an unfavorable net cost-to-cost estimate revision, or revenue growth decelerating below 10% YoY while backlog contracts.
- Bookings/backlog erosion for two consecutive quarters signaling the data-center cycle is rolling over.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report, with a hard check on customer concentration, backlog, and cash conversion.

## Conclusion

Everus is a well-run specialty contractor riding a real infrastructure tailwind, with a strong balance sheet (Altman Z 3.92, net leverage under 3.0x, $293M cash), a rebounding $3.68B backlog, and a four-quarter beat streak. The score trajectory has drifted lower (6.8 in late May to 6.2) as the market and this analysis have grown more aware of the two structural caveats: growth has narrowed to one end market and, increasingly, one customer (~19% of revenue), and 42-71% of reported EPS derives from favorable percentage-of-completion estimate revisions that outrun the ~2.4% free cash flow margin. The ~22% pullback from the May high on stable-to-rising estimates improves the risk/reward and is why the rating stays a growth-hold rather than sliding toward AVOID.

The rating is **HOLD (growth_hold)**. The compounding case is intact and the analyst targets ($172 median) sit well above the current price, so this is worth owning for the data-center exposure and the M&A optionality that opens after October 2026. But at ~29x forward earnings with concentration intensifying and earnings quality softer than the headline, the setup does not clear the bar for a high-conviction BUY. The path to an upgrade is concrete: a broadening customer base, better cash conversion, or a cheaper entry. The path to a downgrade is equally concrete: rising single-customer share, an unfavorable estimate-revision swing, or backlog erosion. Own it sized for a binary concentration risk, and reassess at the next print.
