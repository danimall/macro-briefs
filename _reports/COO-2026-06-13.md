**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cooper Companies is a two-segment medical device company whose investment debate now hinges almost entirely on a single divergence: CooperVision, a high-margin contact-lens consumable franchise inside a rational four-player oligopoly, is performing well, while CooperSurgical, the acquisition-built fertility and women's-health segment, is dragging on margins and carrying an open-ended product-liability tail. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026) reported a GAAP net loss of $(77.9)M / $(0.40) EPS, but that result was produced entirely by a one-time $271.6M net litigation charge for the LifeGlobal embryo-culture-media recall. Stripping that out, the underlying business was healthy: revenue of $1,081.5M (+8% reported, +5% organic), non-GAAP EPS of $1.21 (+26% YoY), beating the $1.11 consensus and extending the beat streak to ten consecutive quarters.

The stock at $67.88 sits ~24% below its December 2025 high of $89.83 and well below the $80–84 range where the CEO and a cluster of directors bought stock on the open market in mid-December 2025, and below the ~$81 average at which the company repurchased $105.6M of shares in the first half of FY26. On forward consensus EPS of $4.63 (FY26) and $5.00 (FY27), the stock trades at ~14.7x and ~13.6x respectively, roughly half the 29.7x peer median. The trailing P/E of 57x is a litigation artifact and should be disregarded.

Two facts keep this at HOLD rather than BUY. First, the near-term estimate revision trend is negative: current-quarter consensus has been cut 5.9% over 30 days and next-quarter 3.0%, driven by Asia Pacific/Japan contact-lens softness (-6% in the region). Earnings momentum is decelerating at the margin even as full-year estimates hold. Second, the LifeGlobal litigation is a genuinely open-ended liability: 140+ lawsuits including three putative class actions and 1,500+ claimants as of June 5, 2026, in an emotionally charged, jury-sympathetic product category where management was forced to reverse its prior "not estimable" stance and accrue $324.1M. The accrual could prove insufficient if claim growth continues, and that risk is not capturable in a forward multiple. The combination of a real but discounted franchise with an uncapped legal tail produces a balanced 12-month setup with a credible re-rating path that depends on litigation clarity and a Japan recovery.

## Company Overview

Cooper Companies (HQ San Ramon, CA; ~15,000 employees; products in 130+ countries) operates two segments:

- **CooperVision (~67% of sales)** — A top-tier participant in the global soft contact lens oligopoly alongside Johnson & Johnson Vision, Alcon, and Bausch + Lomb. Franchises include Biofinity/Avaira Vitality (monthly/two-week), MyDay and clariti (single-use silicone hydrogel), and the differentiated **MiSight 1 day**, the only FDA-approved lens to slow myopia progression in children ages 8-12, now also approved in China (2021) and Japan (August 2025). This is a recurring-consumable business with secular tailwinds: the mix shift to single-use lenses and the emergence of pediatric myopia management as a standard of care.
- **CooperSurgical (~33% of sales)** — Fertility and women's health: IVF consumables and equipment, donor gametes, genomic/genetic testing, cryostorage, medical devices, and the Paragard hormone-free IUD (exclusive U.S.). Built through 600+ acquired products. Competitors include Vitrolife and Hologic; a coming non-hormonal IUD competitor (Miudella, FDA-approved, not yet launched) threatens Paragard.

A 4-for-1 stock split (February 2024) explains the ~199M share count and ~$68 price. The dividend was eliminated in December 2023; capital return now runs entirely through buybacks.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory (FY ends Oct 31):** Revenue grew $3.31B (FY22) → $3.59B (FY23) → $3.90B (FY24) → $4.09B (FY25, +5%) — steady mid-single-digit organic growth, FX-aided. FY25 operating income fell 3% to $682.9M and net income fell 4% to $374.9M despite revenue growth, because of inventory and long-lived-asset write-offs plus severance from a workforce-optimization program concentrated in CooperSurgical.

**The segment divergence is the central financial fact.** In FY25, CooperVision operating income grew 8% to $729.6M (27% segment margin), while CooperSurgical operating income collapsed 63% to $43.4M (3% margin) on write-offs, severance, and higher amortization. CooperVision generates more than 100% of consolidated operating profit; CooperSurgical is currently a net drag.

**Q2 FY2026 detail:** CooperVision +8% (Toric/multifocal +11%, reflecting the favorable mix shift); CooperSurgical +8% (Fertility +13%). The $271.6M net litigation charge ($324.1M accrued, $52.5M insurance recovery) was booked to CooperSurgical SGA. The geographic soft spot was Asia Pacific contact-lens sales -6% on Japan weakness; EMEA was strong (+17%).

**Balance sheet and cash flow:** Total debt is ~$2.46B with D/E ~0.5, in compliance with all covenants. In February 2026 the company extended $950M of term loans to 2031, terming out the December 2026 maturity and removing the near-term refinancing wall. FY25 FCF was $433.7M (10.6% margin); 1H-FY26 operating cash flow was $443.7M, elevated partly by the non-cash litigation add-back. The Altman Z-score of 2.73 ("grey zone") reflects a goodwill-heavy balance sheet ($3.89B goodwill + $1.49B intangibles, ~43% of assets) from CooperSurgical's roll-up, not financial distress. Working capital fell from $993.6M to $495.1M in 1H-FY26, but this is largely classification: the $550M term loan due December 2026 moved to short-term debt and the $324.8M litigation accrual hit current liabilities. It is not a liquidity alarm.

The four-quarter beat record (1.9%, 2.3%, 5.7%, 9.3% surprises) shows management guiding conservatively and delivering, which supports confidence in non-GAAP estimates as the operating reality.

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth case sits in CooperVision. The mix shift to single-use silicone hydrogel lenses and to higher-value toric/multifocal designs carries pricing and margin benefits, and the MiSight myopia-management franchise is a differentiated growth vector as pediatric myopia control becomes standard of care. The August 2025 Japan MHLW approval for MiSight expands the addressable market for a product Cooper effectively pioneered.

CooperSurgical rides favorable demographics — rising infertility rates, later maternal age, expanding clinic counts, and broader family formation. The segment's problem is margin, not top line.

The near-term qualifier is real and quantified. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut from $1.19 to $1.12 over both 30 and 90 days (-5.9%/-5.8%), and next-quarter from $1.23 to $1.19 (-3.0%/-3.2%). Full-year FY26 estimates are essentially flat at $4.63 (+0.2%) and FY27 at $5.00 (-0.4%), so the cuts are timing/regional (Japan, FX) rather than a structural demand crack — management trimmed FY26 revenue guidance to $4.28–4.32B while raising EPS and FCF guidance on MyDay/MiSight momentum. But the direction of near-term revisions is negative, and that matters for the growth score: this is a steady compounder with a near-term soft patch rather than a stock with rising estimates. The strong estimate-revision buy signal is not present here; the opposite is.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline trailing P/E of 57x on $1.87 EPS is meaningless because trailing EPS is suppressed by the one-time litigation charge. The operating multiple is what matters.

Against the peer set in Health Care Supplies, the discount is stark:

A consumable franchise with mid-single-digit organic growth and 27% segment margins in its core business trading at roughly half the peer median is a defensible mispricing argument, and it is why the valuation score is above neutral. The analyst consensus reinforces this: mean target $80.57, median $85.00, high $92.00, with a low of $66.00 essentially at the current price — the distribution skews materially to the upside.

The discount is not purely an error, however. It reflects two real items the market is pricing: the uncapped CooperSurgical litigation tail and the segment's depressed margins. A sum-of-the-parts that values CooperVision at a market-supplier multiple would imply CooperSurgical is being assigned little or negative value — which is the bull thesis and also a coherent market judgment given the open legal exposure. I score valuation 7.0: the discount is real and the analyst targets function as floors given the sub-15x forward multiple, but the discount is partly earned by a risk that a forward multiple cannot capture.

## Competitive Landscape

CooperVision operates in a rational four-player global oligopoly. Its larger rivals (J&J Vision, Alcon, Bausch + Lomb) carry deeper R&D budgets and adjacent eye-care portfolios — lens care, pharma, surgical — that Cooper lacks. Cooper's competitive edge is breadth of SKUs and fitting parameters (high fitting success), silicone hydrogel manufacturing scale, and the MiSight first-mover position in myopia management. The Toric/multifocal mix gain indicates Cooper is capturing the higher-value end of the mix. Competitive trajectory in this segment is stable-to-improving.

CooperSurgical is the weaker competitive story: a more fragmented, acquisition-dependent, lower-margin business with an explicit margin and ROE gap versus peers, now compounded by litigation. The pending Miudella non-hormonal IUD launch is a direct threat to the high-margin Paragard franchise. The company's disclosed "strategic review" of CooperSurgical is an acknowledgment that the segment's competitive and financial positioning needs to change. The structural risks of customer concentration (consolidating optical-retail and fertility-clinic networks gaining negotiating leverage) and sole-source silicone hydrogel raw-material supply apply across both segments and are genuine single-point-of-failure exposures for the crown-jewel franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**1. LifeGlobal embryo-media litigation (the dominant near-term risk).** Management reversed its prior "not probable/not estimable" stance and accrued $324.1M ($271.6M net of insurance). The accrual could prove insufficient if claims keep accelerating, and embryo loss is a category that produces large, sympathetic jury verdicts. This is an uncapped tail that a forward earnings multiple structurally cannot price, and it is the primary reason the risk score is below 5.

**2. UK HMRC tax dispute (Sauflon).** On March 2, 2026 the UK First-tier Tribunal ruled largely for HMRC on payroll-tax withholding. Cooper is appealing; exposure is £0–71.7M plus interest, currently unaccrued ("reasonably possible," not "probable").

**3. CooperSurgical strategic review** could unlock value (restructuring, portfolio pruning, separation) or signal deeper impairment — a two-sided catalyst with no set date.

**4. Goodwill/intangible concentration (43% of assets)** makes future write-offs structural; FY25 already saw write-offs, and a review concluding value is impaired would trigger more.

**5. Customer concentration, sole-source supply, FX, and channel disruption.** A 10% FX move equals ~$35.5M of quarterly operating-income swing (GBP, EUR, JPY). DTC online lens fulfillment and telemedicine could erode the eye-care-practitioner channel over time.

**6. Remediated material weakness.** The CooperSurgical U.S. IT general-controls weakness disclosed at FY24 was remediated as of October 31, 2025, and Q2 controls are effective, but the multi-year ERP implementation remains an execution watch-item overlapping the segment's broader troubles.

### Options Market Signal

The options market does not corroborate acute fear. Near-term ATM IV is 39.2% against 30.5% one-year historical vol (rich by 8.6 points), but the term structure is flat (39.2% near vs 37.6% far) and put/call skew is balanced (1.0%), with longer tenors showing mild put skew (-2.9% to -3.9%). The P/C OI ratio collapsed from 1.42 to 0.10 and IV fell 15.6 points (54.8% → 39.2%) as the stock rallied 10.9% off the post-earnings low, indicating that the heavy hedging present before the June 4 print was unwound once the litigation charge was understood as a known, bounded-for-now event. Short interest is low at 3.0% of float (2.1 days to cover). The options market is not pricing a binary collapse; it reflects modest residual uncertainty consistent with the litigation overhang rather than imminent distress.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
CooperVision is a durable, high-margin consumable franchise growing mid-single-digits with a differentiated myopia-management vector (MiSight, now approved in the U.S., China, and Japan) and a favorable mix shift to single-use and toric/multifocal lenses. The market is assigning little value to CooperSurgical at the current forward multiple. The CEO bought $808K of stock on the open market at $80.80 in December 2025, joined by a cluster of directors and a segment president at $80–84, and the company repurchased stock at ~$81 — all ~18-20% above today's price. If the CooperSurgical review unlocks value (restructuring or separation), Japan recovers, and the litigation accrual proves adequate, the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean/median ($80.57/$85.00) and beyond. Bull case ~$92 (analyst high, ~36% upside), reflecting a partial closure of the peer-multiple gap on stable estimates.

### Bear Case
The LifeGlobal litigation accelerates beyond the $324M accrual, forcing additional charges and reopening the GAAP-loss narrative; an adverse class-certification or large early verdict could drive headline risk well beyond the current reserve. Japan/Asia Pacific softness persists, near-term estimate cuts continue, and the CooperSurgical review reveals impairment rather than hidden value, triggering goodwill write-offs against the intangible base. In that scenario the stock revisits the 52-week low near $58.89 (~-13%), with the analyst low of $66 (~-3%) the more benign downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — a discounted high-quality franchise with a credible re-rating path and strong insider alignment — but the open-ended litigation tail and negative near-term revisions prevent a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Litigation clarity — a global settlement framework, claim-count stabilization, or a court ruling that bounds the LifeGlobal exposure at or near the $324M accrual, removing the uncapped tail.
2. Estimate revisions turn positive — current/next-quarter consensus stops falling and revises upward for two consecutive prints, confirming Japan/Asia Pacific recovery (Q3 FY26 reports ~early September 2026).
3. The CooperSurgical review delivers a value-accretive outcome (separation, margin-restoring restructuring) with a quantified margin path.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Additional material litigation charges that signal the $324M accrual is inadequate, or an adverse class certification with a multi-hundred-million incremental estimate.
2. Goodwill impairment from the CooperSurgical review (the 43%-intangible base makes this material).
3. CooperVision organic growth decelerating below ~3% YoY, signaling the core franchise — not just the surgical segment — is softening.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q3 FY2026 earnings (~early September 2026), with the CooperSurgical review update and litigation claim-count progression as the key intra-quarter monitorables.

## Conclusion

Cooper Companies is a high-quality contact-lens franchise carrying a litigation-burdened, margin-impaired fertility segment, trading at roughly half its peer multiple after a drawdown that was driven by a one-time GAAP charge rather than operating deterioration. The insider buying cluster and buybacks signal that the people with the best information saw value 18-20% above today's price. Against that, the LifeGlobal litigation is a genuinely open-ended liability that a forward multiple cannot capture, and near-term estimate revisions are falling. The forward valuation, the analyst target distribution skewed to the upside, and the insider signal argue for owning the stock; the litigation tail and decelerating near-term momentum argue against paying up for it as a high-conviction long. The balance is a growth-oriented HOLD with a monitorable path to a BUY upgrade once the legal exposure is bounded and Japan stabilizes.
