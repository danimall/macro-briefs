## Executive Summary

Central Garden & Pet is a consumer staples company undergoing a fundamental transformation from a diversified manufacturer/distributor into a higher-margin branded products business. The stock trades at approximately 7.3x EV/EBITDA and ~12.9x forward non-GAAP P/E — a significant discount to both the household products peer median of 18x and the broader consumer staples sector. This discount persists despite 450bps of gross margin expansion since FY2023, a return to revenue growth in H1 FY2026 (+2.3% consolidated, +8.7% in Q2), and a balance sheet with $653M cash, zero revolver draws, and approximately 1.5x net leverage.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) the ongoing "Cost and Simplicity" restructuring that has driven full-year operating margins from 5.8% (FY2024) to 8.0% (FY2025), with H1 FY2026 non-GAAP operating margin reaching 9.1% and still having room to run as facility consolidation and ERP migration complete, (2) the Phillips Pet Food JV (April 2026) that removes low-margin distribution revenue and concentrates the portfolio on higher-margin branded manufacturing, and (3) an accumulating capital deployment capacity ($300M annual FCF + approximately $1.25B liquidity) that the company has explicitly signaled may be used for material acquisitions.

The primary risks are the controlled company structure (84-year-old founder holds 56% voting power), customer concentration (top 5 = 54% of sales), and tariff exposure on the ~15% of COGS sourced internationally. The consistent earnings beat streak and modestly rising consensus estimates suggest the operational execution is real and gaining credibility.

## Company Overview

Central Garden & Pet operates two segments across the U.S. consumer market:

- **Pet Segment** (~58% of revenue, ~$1.8B): Dog/cat treats, chews, toys, beds, grooming products; aquatics/reptile supplies; small animal products; equine/livestock health products; live fish. Key brands include Nylabone, Kaytee, Farnam, Aqueon, and Best Bully Sticks. The company is the sole domestic provider of (S)-Methoprene, a key ingredient in Frontline Plus.

- **Garden Segment** (~42% of revenue, ~$1.3B): Grass seed, wild bird feed, herbicides/pesticides, fertilizers, live plants, packet seeds. Key brands include Pennington, Amdro, Ferry-Morse, and Sevin. This segment is highly seasonal (64% of sales in Q2/Q3).

Distribution reaches nearly 10,000 retail locations and 5,600+ veterinary offices. Top customers: Walmart (~17%), Home Depot (~16%), Costco, Lowe's, and Amazon collectively represent ~54% of total sales.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: Stabilization to Growth

Revenue declined from a $3.34B post-COVID peak (FY2022) to $3.13B (FY2025) — a cumulative 6% contraction driven by industry normalization, deliberate portfolio pruning (exiting U.K. operations, pottery), and some competitive share loss. The inflection came in H1 FY2026: consolidated sales grew 2.3% YoY, with Q2 FY2026 specifically delivering $906M (+8.7%). However, management attributed Q2 strength partly to shipment timing shifts from Q1 (which declined 6.0%) and new Garden listings, so sustained organic acceleration has not yet been definitively confirmed. The Phillips JV will reduce 2H FY2026 reported revenue, though the company has not publicly quantified the magnitude.

### Margins: The Core Thesis

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 FY2026 | Q2 FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Gross Margin | 28.6% | 29.5% | 31.9% | — | 33.1% |
| Operating Margin (GAAP) | 6.3% | 5.8% | 8.0% | 8.6% | 12.6% |
| Non-GAAP Operating Margin | — | 7.0% | 8.5% | 9.1% | — |

Gross margin expanded 450bps in 2.5 years through facility consolidations (Ontario CA → Salt Lake City), SKU rationalization, procurement improvements, ERP consolidation (from 46 systems to 6), and commodity deflation. Full-year operating margin improved from 5.8% (FY2024) to 8.0% (FY2025), with H1 FY2026 reaching 8.6% GAAP (9.1% non-GAAP). Q2 FY2026's 12.6% operating margin reflects peak Garden seasonality and should not be compared directly to full-year figures. The exit of distribution activities through the Phillips JV will structurally lift future margins further, since distribution was the segment's lowest-margin activity.

### Cash Flow & Balance Sheet

Free cash flow of $291M in FY2025 represents a 9.3% FCF margin — exceptional for a consumer products business. Capex is modest at $41M (1.3% of revenue), reflecting capital discipline, though Central manufactures the majority of its branded products in U.S.-based facilities.

The balance sheet is strong: $653M cash (as of March 28, 2026), a fully undrawn $600M credit facility, and $1.2B in long-dated debt ($300M at 5.125% due 2028, $500M at 4.125% due 2030, $400M at 4.125% due 2031). Net leverage is approximately 1.5x adjusted EBITDA as of March 2026 (reflecting seasonal working capital build and $57M acquisition spend since FY2025 year-end). Total liquidity (cash plus undrawn facility) is approximately $1.25B. Current ratio of approximately 3.45 as of March 2026 provides ample working capital coverage.

### Capital Return

Share repurchases totaled $148.4M in FY2025, retiring 4.6M shares. An additional $100M authorization was approved in February 2026. The diluted share count has declined from 66.9M (FY2024) to 61.9M (H1 FY2026) — a 7.5% reduction in approximately two years. At the current price of ~$34, remaining buyback authorization of $128M could retire another 3.7M shares (6% of outstanding), providing a mechanical EPS tailwind of ~6%.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Growth Drivers

1. **Margin expansion runway**: ERP consolidation ongoing (46→6 systems), additional distribution facility closures in progress, grass seed oversupply beginning to moderate. These actions have multi-year tails.

2. **Phillips JV margin uplift**: Removal of low-margin distribution revenue lifts the remaining portfolio's blended margin. The company maintained $2.70+ non-GAAP EPS guidance despite the JV transition, and management stated it does not expect the distribution partnership to have a significant impact on EPS, implying underlying earnings power is stronger than headline guidance suggests.

3. **Buyback-driven EPS growth**: At $300M annual FCF and current authorization pace, 5-7% annual share count reduction provides compounding EPS growth independent of revenue.

4. **M&A pipeline**: The 10-K states management anticipates that one or more potential acquisition opportunities, including those that would be material, may become available in the near future. With approximately $1.25B liquidity, a $200-400M acquisition is well within capacity.

### Medium-Term Concerns

Revenue growth excluding the Phillips deconsolidation is positive but its durability is unconfirmed, given Q1-to-Q2 timing shifts. The pet supplies market is approximately $40B and growing (industry estimates suggest around 5% annually); garden is closer to GDP growth. Central's organic growth ceiling is probably 3-5% in a normal environment, supplemented by M&A and buybacks to deliver high-single-digit EPS growth.

Consensus estimates reflect this: FY2026E non-GAAP EPS of $2.91 (+7% vs. FY2025's $2.73) and FY2027E of $3.07 (+5.5%). These are reasonable given the margin trajectory and buyback pace.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.74 per share with approximately 62.5 million shares outstanding, CENTA's equity value is approximately $2.17B. Using March 2026 net debt of approximately $539M, enterprise value is approximately $2.71B. Key metrics:

- **13.6x trailing P/E** (on $2.55 GAAP EPS)
- **12.9x forward P/E** (on management's $2.70+ non-GAAP guidance)
- **11.9x FY2026E P/E** (on consensus $2.91)
- **~7.3x EV/EBITDA** (on FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of ~$371M)

Against the peer median P/E of 18.0x, CENTA trades at a 34% discount. This discount partially reflects the controlled company structure (suppresses takeover premium), the multi-year revenue decline narrative, and small-cap neglect (limited analyst coverage).

At approximately 7.3x EV/EBITDA for a consumer staples company generating 9%+ FCF margins with moderate net leverage and margin expansion still in progress, the stock appears meaningfully undervalued. Applying a still-discounted 14-15x multiple to $2.91 consensus FY2026 EPS yields $40.75-$43.65, representing 17-26% upside. Available analyst consensus targets suggest a similar range.

## Competitive Landscape

Central operates in a fragmented market (2,500+ manufacturers in pet supplies alone) where scale provides meaningful advantages in retailer relationships, procurement, and distribution. The company's dual-segment model (pet + garden) provides diversification that few competitors match:

- **Scotts Miracle-Gro** (main garden rival) has ~$3.6B revenue but carries significantly more leverage and recently underwent aggressive cost-cutting after over-investing in cannabis during 2020-2021.
- **Spectrum Brands** competes in both categories but is undergoing its own strategic restructuring.
- **Mars/Nestlé/J.M. Smucker** dominate pet food but have less presence in pet supplies where Central specializes.

Central's competitive position is stable rather than improving. The company is not gaining material share in either segment, but the Phillips JV strategically refocuses on branded manufacturing where barriers to entry (brand recognition, retailer relationships, formulation IP for chemicals like (S)-Methoprene) are higher than in distribution.

The pet industry structural tailwinds (humanization, premiumization, Gen Z/Millennial pet ownership growth) provide a supportive backdrop.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Controlled Company / Founder Age**: William Brown (84) holds 56% of voting power through Class B shares. No disclosed succession plan for governance control. His departure could trigger a sale (upside scenario) or protracted uncertainty (downside scenario). The dual-class structure definitively caps takeover premium optionality.

2. **Customer Concentration**: Home Depot + Walmart = 33% of total sales; 66% of Garden segment sales. Any meaningful shift in these relationships (private label expansion, shelf space reallocation) would materially impact revenue.

3. **Tariff Exposure**: Less than 15% of COGS sourced internationally (China, Brazil, Mexico). Management has already flagged tariff impacts in FY2025/2026. In a protracted trade war scenario, input costs could rise above current assumptions.

4. **Garden Seasonality**: Weather-related disruptions can meaningfully impact quarterly Garden sales. In FY2025, cool/wet weather hurt controls and live plants performance. Climate volatility adds persistent quarter-to-quarter uncertainty.

5. **Revenue Trajectory Post-Phillips**: The JV removes an estimated $200-250M+ in annualized revenue (analyst estimate based on segment structure; not disclosed by the company). Forward comparisons will look weaker until fully lapped. Market may penalize headline "declines" despite improved quality of remaining revenue.

### Mitigating Factors

- Consistent earnings beat pattern with substantial magnitude in recent quarters
- No near-term debt maturities requiring refinancing at elevated rates (nearest maturity February 2028 with substantial cash on hand)

## Conclusion

CENTA offers a compelling value opportunity within consumer staples at a point when the business model is actively improving. The combination of approximately 7.3x EV/EBITDA, 9%+ FCF yield, moderate net leverage, a consistent earnings beat streak, and still-rising consensus estimates creates an asymmetric setup where the downside is protected by balance sheet strength and the upside is driven by continued margin expansion plus capital deployment. The 34% P/E discount to household products peers is partially justified by the controlled company structure but overstates the discount appropriate for a business generating $300M+ annual free cash flow with expanding margins.

The key monitoring point is whether management deploys the substantial accumulated cash in a value-creating manner. The 10-K language about "material" near-term acquisition opportunities suggests management is actively pursuing deployment. At current valuation, the stock has room to absorb moderate M&A dilution while still offering upside if the operational transformation continues executing.
