## Executive Summary

Zurn Elkay is a genuinely improved business trading at a price that reflects it. Core revenue growth has accelerated from +2.4% in FY2024 to +8% in FY2025 to +11% in the first half of 2026, driven by institutional (+12.2%) and commercial (+14.8%) construction demand plus price realization. The balance sheet is now one of the cleanest in mid-cap industrials: total net leverage of 0.36x against a 4.00x covenant, $905.3M of liquidity, 15.2x interest coverage on ex-refund operating income and no maturity before October 2028. Three legacy overhangs — the Process & Motion Control platform, asbestos liabilities and the U.S. defined benefit pension — have been sequentially removed since 2021, and the Q2 asbestos-related insurance settlement produced a $4.4M receipt rather than another charge. The company earns roughly 48% on tangible invested capital.

The obstacle is entry arithmetic, and it has moved against the buyer. At $51.84 the stock trades at 28.3x the $1.83 current-year consensus, 25.4x the $2.04 next-year consensus (a PEG of roughly 2.2), 18.5x TTM credit-agreement Adjusted EBITDA of $478.3M on a pro-forma EV of ~$8.84B, and a 3.85% free cash flow yield once the non-repeating $47.6M IEEPA tariff refund is removed. Reported Q2 gross margin of 58.7% is not a usable number: excluding the refund it was 49.0%, and stripping the additional $8.4M favorable LIFO swing (a $7.0M charge in 1H25 versus a $1.4M credit in 1H26) leaves underlying 1H26 gross-margin expansion of roughly 140bps rather than the 750bps the headline implies. The market performed this arithmetic itself on July 29: the stock opened at $54.44, printed the 52-week high of $55.00, and closed at $51.55.

We are downgrading from BUY to HOLD after three consecutive buy ratings. Nothing in the Q2 filing impairs the franchise: estimates are rising (+4.2% current year, +5.9% next year over 90 days), the beat streak is now four quarters, and the Intellihot tuck-in adds a credible growth option at ~2.9x 2026E sales. But base-case fair value of about $55 sits only 6% above spot against a bear case near $41, and the two engines of the last three years of margin gain — restructuring self-help (charges down from $15.3M in 2023 to $2.7M in 1H26) and a favorable price/cost spread producing 41-50% incremental margins — are both closer to exhaustion than to acceleration. Existing holders should stay; new capital should wait for the mid-$40s or for evidence that incremental margins hold above 35% with capex normalized.

## Company Overview

Zurn Elkay is a pure-play North American water management manufacturer serving institutional and commercial building construction, with smaller waterworks and residential exposure. Revenue is 92.1% U.S. (1H26: $850.7M), 5.4% Canada, 2.5% rest of world. Four product families span backflow preventers and pressure-reducing valves (Zurn, Wilkins), drainage and wastewater pre-treatment (Green Turtle, Wade), hygienic and environmental products including sensor flush valves, commercial faucets, sinks, hand dryers and restroom partitions (Elkay, JUST, World Dryer, Hadrian), and drinking water (Elkay ezH2O, Halsey Taylor).

The competitive mechanism is a two-stage gate. Products must first clear third-party certification (USC Foundation for Cross-Connection Control, IAPMO, NSF, ANSI, ASTM, PDI, UL, FM, AWWA), then be written into a project specification by an architect, engineer, contractor or owner. Once specified with satisfactory results, specifiers generally re-specify. The products are project-critical but a low share of total project cost, a structurally low-scrutiny, high-friction position. Distribution runs through ~1,000 independent sales representatives across ~120 agencies plus ~65 third-party warehouses supporting same-day service. IP consists of ~240 active U.S. patents and ~90 foreign patents; ~180 engineers and technical staff support ~2,600 employees across 21 principal facilities, only ~160 of whom are unionized under four contracts expiring November 2026, October 2027 and January 2028.

Corporate history matters for reading the financials. This is the residual of Rexnord: Process & Motion Control was spun to Regal Rexnord in October 2021 (still reported as discontinued operations), Elkay was merged in July 2022, asbestos liabilities were divested in December 2023, and the pension was terminated in 2025. The company reports one segment with the CEO as chief operating decision maker measuring consolidated net income from continuing operations, so Zurn versus Elkay economics are not visible to investors.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2022 $1,281.8M → FY2023 $1,530.5M (+19.4%, Elkay full-year effect) → FY2024 $1,566.5M (+2.4%) → FY2025 $1,695.9M (+8.3%) → 1H26 $924.0M (+10.9%). Q2 2026 was $491.0M, +10.5% with core growth of 10%. The acceleration off the 2024 trough is the most important fact in the file.

Growth is not uniform across end markets. Q2 disaggregation: institutional $245.1M (+12.5%, 49.9% of sales), commercial $142.6M (+13.5%, 29.0%), all other including waterworks and residential $103.3M (+2.2%, 21.0%). Roughly one fifth of the business is growing at about 3%. Management's "growth in all product categories" refers to product families, not end markets.

**Margins.** Gross margin went 36.3% (FY22) → 42.3% → 45.1% → 45.1% (FY25) → 53.5% reported in 1H26. The reported figure is unusable. Excluding the $47.6M tariff refund recognized as a reduction of cost of sales, 1H26 gross margin was 48.3%; excluding the additional $8.4M favorable LIFO swing, underlying 1H26 gross-margin expansion was roughly 140bps over the 46.0% in 1H25. Operating margin: 16.4% FY2025 → 25.4% reported / 20.2% ex-refund in 1H26, reproducing management's stated +330bps.

Incremental margins deserve scrutiny. 1H26 revenue rose $90.7M while ex-refund operating income rose $45.8M, a 50.5% incremental margin; excluding the LIFO swing, roughly 41%. For a business that outsources component fabrication and assembles, this reflects a favorable price/cost spread landing simultaneously with productivity, not a new structural level. Secondary reporting of the July 29 call indicates management points to ~35% normalized incrementals, which is the right direction of travel. SG&A was 24.6% of sales in both FY2025 and 1H26 (25.1% in 1H25), essentially no operating leverage on that line. All margin gain is in gross margin, which is where the price/cost spread and LIFO live.

**Adjusted EBITDA (credit-agreement definition).** FY2023 $339.5M (22.2%) → FY2024 $390.4M (24.9%) → FY2025 $442.2M (26.1%) → TTM to 6/30/26 $478.3M on derived TTM revenue of $1,786.6M, a 26.8% margin. This definition excludes the tariff refund, LIFO, restructuring and stock compensation. It is therefore cleaner than GAAP for run-rate purposes but flatters the business by excluding roughly $42M per year of stock compensation, a real economic cost equal to 2.3-2.4% of sales.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $346.5M on $29.9M capex produced $316.6M of FCF against $198.0M of net income (160% conversion, bridged by $88.7M of D&A including $58.6M of non-cash intangible amortization). TTM to 6/30/26: OCF $401.4M, capex $22.9M, FCF $378.5M; excluding the cash refund, approximately $330.9M.

Two adjustments to that figure. First, 1H26 should not be annualized: Q2 and Q3 are seasonally strongest, receivables built $86.6M in the half and will unwind, and the refund does not repeat. Second, 1H26 capex of $6.3M was 0.68% of sales and only 48% of first-half depreciation ($13.0M), against 86% in 1H25 and roughly 100% for FY2025. Net PP&E fell from $157.6M to $152.0M. The benign reading is back-half phasing in a structurally asset-light model; deferral cannot be excluded on half-year data. If capex normalizes toward $30M, ex-refund FCF is roughly $15M below a naive annualization.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26: cash $365.0M, total debt $499.1M (term loan $477.1M net, finance leases $22.0M), net debt $134.1M. Total Net Leverage Ratio 0.36x against a 4.00x covenant (electable to 4.50x for a material acquisition). Current ratio 3.05x. Term loan matures October 2028 at Term SOFR plus 200bps, effective 5.77%, no required amortization. A +100bps move in SOFR costs about $4.9M annually, largely offset by interest income on $365M of cash ($4.2M in 1H26 alone).

The February 19, 2026 Amendment No. 3 is the most consequential balance-sheet event since the Elkay merger: the revolver was increased from $200.0M to $550.0M, maturity extended to February 2031, and JPMorgan replaced Credit Suisse as agent. Total liquidity rose to $905.3M from $488.4M at year-end. Against $172.2M of credit-agreement indebtedness, the 4.00x covenant implies roughly $1.7B of theoretical debt capacity. That is M&A firepower. The alternative reading is defensive refinancing that de-risks the 2028 wall and replaces a defunct agent, and is equally consistent with the facts. Capacity is not intent.

The intangible-heavy balance sheet is structural. Goodwill $793.2M plus intangibles $805.5M equals $1,598.7M, or 57.5% of total assets and 96.4% of stockholders' equity. Tangible book value is approximately $60.4M. Amortization runs a flat $58.5-58.6M annually through 2031, dominated by $593.8M net of 16-year Elkay customer relationships, roughly $0.35 per share pre-tax of non-cash GAAP drag. The retained deficit of $(1,059.6)M is a Rexnord LBO artifact offset by $2,797.8M of paid-in capital, not evidence of current losses; because retained earnings is negative, buybacks are charged against APIC.

Two consequences follow. Reported ROIC of roughly 11.6% (NOPAT ~$243.5M on invested capital of ~$2,105M) sits well below return on tangible invested capital of approximately 48%. The operating franchise is excellent; the price paid for Elkay dilutes the reported figure. And the FY2025 goodwill test used a qualitative rather than quantitative assessment, so no fresh fair-value cushion was disclosed.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.96 is a mechanical artifact here, depressed by purchase-accounting intangibles that inflate assets without generating revenue. Actual credit metrics — 0.36x leverage, 15x coverage, 3.05x current ratio, $905M liquidity — are investment-grade-like. The screen is detecting a real feature (intangible concentration versus $60M of tangible equity) and mislabeling it as distress risk rather than impairment risk.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025 returned $223.8M (71% of FCF) via $159.9M of buybacks and $63.9M of dividends with no acquisitions. 1H26 returned $136.4M ($99.6M buybacks at an average $47.98, $36.8M dividends), roughly 88% of ex-refund FCF, then $108.5M for Intellihot in Q3 from cash. The dividend was raised 22% to $0.11 quarterly ($0.44 annualized, 0.86% yield). $382.5M of a $500M authorization remains.

Buyback optics require care. The company repurchased 2,075,825 shares in 1H26 (1.24% of the opening count) but issued 1,158,270 on option exercises and RSU/PSU vesting. Net reduction was 910,755 shares, or 0.55%; roughly 56% of buyback dollars funded dilution offset rather than shrinking the base. With ~$42M of annual stock compensation excluded from the Adjusted EBITDA metric management is paid on, the per-share cost of the comp program surfaces as reduced buyback efficacy rather than as expense in the headline metric. The repurchases were nonetheless executed 8% below the current price.

## Growth Analysis

The demand case rests on three legs, of which two are verifiable and one is not.

**Verifiable: end-market volume plus price.** Institutional demand (education, healthcare, government, half of revenue) grew 12.2% in 1H26 and commercial 14.8%. The specification-to-revenue lag means projects specified in mid-2025 convert through 2026-2027, giving a modest visibility cushion. Q2 non-GAAP EPS of $0.50 beat the $0.48 consensus by 4.4%, the fourth consecutive beat (prior three: +4.9%, +11.6%, +7.4%). Consensus has moved up accordingly: current quarter +4.3% over 30 days, current year +4.2%, next year +5.9%. These are healthy revisions but well short of a magnitude that would signal a genuine earnings inflection, and they are consistent with a company beating modestly on a steady base rather than re-rating.

**Verifiable: regulatory expansion of addressable market.** This is a rare industrial where tightening regulation grows the market. Pro Filtration for the ezH2O line carries NSF/ANSI 42, 53 and 401 certifications for PFAS, lead, microplastics and asbestos reduction, with 10,000-gallon filter capacity and sub-30-second replacement. Water-conservation code compliance drives EcoVantage and Zurn One. Intellihot's tankless water heating benefits from energy-efficiency regulation displacing storage tanks. Elimination of single-use plastic bottles supports bottle-filling stations. Management's claim that these can support market growth above U.S. industrial production is credible.

**Not verifiable: the M&A engine.** The revolver upsize creates roughly $1.7B of theoretical debt capacity, and the 10-K names M&A as critical to the growth strategy. Intellihot, closed July 21 for $108.5M against approximately $37M of 2026E net sales (~2.9x sales), is the first step. Management aspirations relayed through secondary sources point to $100M of sales at a 30% EBITDA margin in five to six years, a ~20% revenue CAGR, which would imply roughly 3.6x entry EV/EBITDA on the outcome. The sales-based rather than EBITDA-based multiple and the "earlier-stage technology disruptor" framing imply limited current profitability. The company states the acquisition is not expected to have a material impact on consolidated financials. Treat this as a growth option with unverified terms, not a cash-flow accretion event.

Three constraints on the growth case. Backlog is never quantified in any filing; contract assets and liabilities are "not material," customers are billed on shipment with ~30-day terms, and performance obligations have a duration of one year or less. Demand can inflect within a quarter. The self-help runway is narrowing — restructuring charges fell from $15.3M (2023) to $13.5M to $9.6M to $2.7M in 1H26 — so future margin gains must come from price/cost spread, mix and volume leverage, which are more cyclical sources. And roughly 21% of revenue (waterworks, residential) is growing at about 3%, capping the blended rate.

Macro cuts both ways. Institutional demand depends on municipal and state budgets, and higher municipal financing costs are a plausible headwind to project starts on a 12-24 month lag. Against that, non-residential construction tied to data centers and warehousing sits inside the commercial bucket, though the filings do not break it out and no inference should be drawn about magnitude. [Specific interest-rate and Fed-policy figures are unverified — not present in the sources.]

## Competitive Landscape

The market is fragmented; management states no single competitor competes across the breadth of its product lines, and that it avoids commoditized segments. The durable advantage sits in three places rather than in manufacturing scale, which is largely outsourced to a global network of independent component suppliers.

First, the certification-then-specification gate. A rival must both certify a product and get it re-specified, which takes years per product family. Second, the distribution and local-inventory system — ~1,000 reps, ~120 agencies, ~65 warehouses — which management frames as a lead-time advantage on project work where schedule slippage is expensive. Third, replacement inertia: the 10-K cites a strong propensity to replace like-for-like, converting the installed base into recurring retrofit demand. The bundling logic is credible: Elkay sinks, JUST stainless, World Dryer, Hadrian partitions and Zurn flush valves cover most of a commercial restroom's fixture content from one supplier.

Two things temper the trajectory read. The same distribution network that constitutes the moat is the concentration risk: three independent distributors move approximately 32% of revenue, the largest 18%. The 10-K's own risk factor warns about distributors increasing sales of competitors' products, and the channel's bargaining power is visible in the $83.3M sales-rebate accrual against $270.9M of receivables. And converting the disclosed percentages to dollars, the largest relationship has been flat at roughly $300M for three years (20% of $1,530.5M in 2023, 19% of $1,566.5M in 2024, 18% of $1,695.9M in 2025) while total revenue grew 10.8%. The declining percentage is a denominator effect. Essentially all incremental growth came from other customers, which is a genuine mix improvement, but absolute exposure has not fallen. Rounding to whole percentages leaves ±$8-17M of imprecision, so the underlying trend could be modest growth or modest decline.

plumbSMART, the connected-products portal offering wireless monitoring of usage, filter status and alerts with building-automation integration, is a plausible differentiator. No attach rates, connected-unit counts or recurring revenue are disclosed, so it carries no valuation weight here.

Competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving: share gains in fiberglass-reinforced interceptors displacing concrete, 12-14% growth in the two largest end markets, and a widening product envelope via Intellihot, set against flat dollar concentration in the largest channel relationship and a 3%-growth fifth of the business.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer and distributor concentration.** Largest customer 18% of FY2025 sales, top five ~38%, three largest distributors ~32%. Because the 18% figure appears in both the customer and distributor disclosures, the largest relationship is a channel partner, not an end user. Disruption at one distributor puts roughly $300M of annual revenue at risk. The counterparty is not named in any filing.

**No revenue visibility.** Short-cycle business, billed on shipment, ~30-day terms, immaterial contract assets and liabilities, performance obligations under one year, backlog never quantified. There is no deferred revenue cushion. This is the single largest structural risk at a 28x multiple: a two-quarter demand inflection has nothing to absorb it.

**Residual tariff exposure, unquantified.** Material cost is ~30% of net sales (bronze, iron, brass, stainless and carbon steel, zinc, engineered plastics), and the company does not typically hedge commodity purchase-price risk. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling addressed IEEPA authority; the report's statement that Section 232 and Section 301 tariffs rest on different statutory bases and were not addressed is an inference from the ruling's stated statutory scope, not a filing fact. The Q2 MD&A still states costs are impacted by trade policies. No filing quantifies residual exposure, original IEEPA amounts paid, or potential further refunds. The refund is unambiguously non-repeating in its recognized form; whether the underlying cost pressure has been eliminated is unknown.

**Impairment risk on $1,598.7M of goodwill and intangibles** against $60.4M of tangible book equity, tested qualitatively in FY2025 with no disclosed fair-value cushion. No goodwill impairment has been taken in 2023-2025.

**Warranty trend.** The liability rose from $6.6M to $9.4M during 1H26 and was split for the first time into $4.9M current and $4.5M long-term. Charges to operations doubled to $4.0M from $2.0M while claims settled were flat at $1.2M. The appearance of a long-tail component alongside doubled charges is consistent with recognition of a multi-year claim expectation; it is equally consistent with the Pro Filtration launch warranting a conservative new accrual or simple scaling on 11% growth. At $9.4M against $478M of Adjusted EBITDA the amount is immaterial; the direction and the new classification are the watch items.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Todd Adams holds combined Chairman and CEO roles. FY2025 CEO compensation was $11.60M with "compensation actually paid" of $19.06M, against an NEO average of $1.98M, a 5.9x ratio, wide even for large-cap industrials. Equity leverage in the pay structure is extreme (actually-paid swings of +$56.8M in 2021 and -$5.9M in 2022), which produces high alignment and high volatility. The CFO transitioned from David Pauli to Daniel Klun, with the May 27, 2026 8-K carrying both Item 2.02 and Item 5.02, an unusual pairing whose text could not be retrieved, leaving the circumstances unresolved. Director Timothy Jahnke retired effective immediately on July 23, 2026 for stated personal reasons with the standard no-disagreement language; he had sold 25,000 shares at $51.18 five months earlier, which is weak evidence of anything. Single-segment reporting prevents investors from seeing Zurn versus Elkay or valve versus drinking-water economics across four product families and ten brands.

**Insider activity.** The Form 4 record shows sales and grants only, no open-market purchases, from February through August 4, 2026. The CEO acquired 569,734 shares on February 6 (no price reported, consistent with exercise or settlement) and sold 538,397 shares for approximately $28.1M on February 9-10 at $51.78-$52.68, retaining 2,256,684 shares worth ~$115M at the current price. The most recent transaction is the Chief Accounting Officer selling 25,000 shares at $53.02 on August 4, six days after the Q2 print and near the 52-week high, retaining 308,814. Clustered small sales in mid-February by the President, then-CFO, General Counsel, CIO and a VP were sized consistently with tax withholding around vesting. Whether the February CEO sales occurred under a pre-existing 10b5-1 plan cannot be confirmed; the 10-Q's Item 5 disclosure covers only Q2 adoptions and terminations. The pattern is most consistent with scheduled exercise-and-sell monetization inside an open window, and the residual stake is large. What is established: no insider bought stock between $47 and $53 during a period when the company itself repurchased at an average of $47.98.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV is 38.5% against 27.9% one-year realized volatility, rich by 10.6 points, with the term structure flat (38.5% at one month versus 38.2% at six months) and put/call skew balanced at 2.9%. IV compressed from 44.9% to 38.5% since July 26 as the price rose 6.3%, the normal post-earnings pattern, but it has not fully reverted to realized levels. The nearest expiry prices a ±6.0% move over 13 days and the six-month tenor prices ±15.5%. The options market is charging a persistent premium to realized volatility without directional bias, consistent with uncertainty about the durability of the margin level rather than a view on direction. For a HOLD this is mildly discouraging to buyers: long exposure is being asked to pay for volatility that the underlying has not delivered. Short interest at 4.6% of float with 5.1 days to cover is uncrowded and carries no signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($64.50, +24.4%).** Core growth holds at 10%+ through 2027 as the mid-2025 institutional project step-up converts through the specification lag, with commercial construction adding a second leg. Incremental margins settle at 35-40% rather than reverting to the low 30s, pushing Adjusted EBITDA margin above 28% and FY2027 EPS above $2.15. The company deploys part of the $550M revolver into one or two acquisitions on Intellihot-like terms — 3x sales for specifiable products with regulatory tailwinds sold through the existing rep network — adding $75-150M of revenue at accretive multiples. Additional IEEPA refunds materialize (the company is still evaluating and has accrued nothing). The multiple holds at 30x on the strength of a 0.36x-levered balance sheet, 48% return on tangible capital, and the completed removal of asbestos, pension and PMC legacy exposures. Analysts revise the $58 mean toward the $65 high.

**Bear case ($41, -20.9%).** The 140bps of underlying gross-margin expansion in 1H26 is the true run-rate, and it stalls as bronze, brass, copper and steel re-inflate against an unhedged book while price realization decelerates on easier comps. Incremental margins revert to 30%. Municipal and state budget pressure slows institutional project starts on a 12-18 month lag into 2027, and the commercial bucket, the faster-growing and more cyclically fragile one, turns first. With no backlog and no deferred revenue, the deceleration appears in a single quarter. Restructuring self-help is spent (charges down 82% from 2023), so there is no cost offset. Capex normalizes toward $30M+, taking ex-refund FCF below $320M and the yield below 3.7% on unchanged price. FY2027 EPS lands at $1.85 and the multiple compresses to 22x, the low end of the FLS/GTES/VMI cluster, putting the stock near the $43 March low.

**What tips the balance to HOLD.** The bull case is plausible but requires two things the filings do not support today: incremental margins above the ~35% management itself points to, and successful capital deployment from a revolver whose purpose has not been stated. The bear case requires only mean reversion in the price/cost spread plus a normal cyclical slowdown in publicly funded construction. At 25.4x next-year earnings with a 3.85% ex-windfall FCF yield, the price already embeds the bull path's first stage. The 8% appreciation since late July, combined with the July 29 gap-and-fade that showed the market re-parsing the quality of the headline beat, has consumed the margin of safety that justified the prior buy ratings without any offsetting fundamental improvement.

**On the score trajectory.** Scores have declined 7.0 → 6.4 → 6.6 → 6.28 across four reports. ## Conclusion

Zurn Elkay is a high-quality, low-leverage water management franchise with a defensible specification-based moat, a regulatory tailwind that expands rather than taxes its addressable market, and a balance sheet cleaned of three legacy overhangs. Core revenue growth has accelerated to 11%, estimates are rising, and the company has beaten consensus four consecutive quarters. None of that is in question.

What has changed is the price relative to the substance. The reported Q2 margin performance that drove the stock to a 52-week high was roughly 80% attributable to a non-repeating $47.6M tariff refund and a favorable LIFO swing; underlying gross-margin expansion in the first half was approximately 140bps. At $51.84 the stock capitalizes 10-11% growth, 27% EBITDA margins and continued expansion at 25.4x next-year earnings, 18.5x TTM Adjusted EBITDA and a 3.85% free cash flow yield before capex normalization, for a business with no quantified backlog, roughly half its revenue tied to publicly funded construction, 32% of revenue through three distributors, and a self-help program whose spending has fallen 82% since 2023. Base-case value of $55 leaves about 6% of upside against a bear case near $41 that requires only mean reversion in the price/cost spread and a normal cyclical slowdown.

Hold existing positions for the growth trajectory and the modest dividend. Direct new capital elsewhere until the mid-$40s, or until the October print demonstrates that incremental margins hold above 35% with capex at a normalized level.
