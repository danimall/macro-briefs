**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Netflix has de-rated hard. The stock trades near $68.58, down roughly 46% from its 52-week high of $126.71 and sitting at a 52-week low after a ~9-11% single-day drop following the July 16, 2026 Q2 print. The catalyst was soft Q3 revenue guidance of ~11% growth against a ~$13B consensus, layered on top of a company decision made in 2025 to stop reporting subscriber counts and ARM (Q2 EPS of $0.80 was in line, revenue +13% to $12.56B). The combination of decelerating revenue plus reduced disclosure has cost Netflix what one set of analysts called "narrative control," and the multiple has compressed accordingly.

The underlying business remains high quality. FY2025 delivered $45.18B revenue (+16%), a 29.5% operating margin (up from 26.7% in 2024 and 20.6% in 2023), $10.98B net income, and $9.46B of free cash flow. The balance sheet is manageable: ~$9.1B cash against ~$14.3B of fixed-rate notes, full covenant compliance, and $27.1B of remaining buyback authorization after Netflix repurchased 66.4M shares in H1 2026. The failed Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition left Netflix with a $2.8B termination fee, no new debt, and its chief content rival (Paramount-Skydance) now tied up in a litigated $110B deal that a federal judge paused on July 20, 2026. On strategic optionality, that is a favorable outcome.

The problem is momentum, not solvency. Revenue growth is slowing (16% FY25 → ~11% Q3'26 guide), Q2 operating margin dipped year-over-year for the first time in a while as tech/development and marketing outgrew revenue, consensus estimates are drifting lower across every window, and the two most recent surprise-relevant quarters showed misses of -17.4% and -10.2%. At ~22x trailing / ~19x forward earnings, Netflix is no longer priced for perfection, but there is no near-term catalyst to re-rate it and the loss of the subscriber-metric window makes it harder to time the trough. We rate NFLX a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Netflix is the largest global subscription streaming service, offering TV series, films, games, and live programming across four reporting regions (UCAN, EMEA, LATAM, APAC). The company operates as a single reporting segment managed by co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters to a consolidated operating-margin target, with Reed Hastings as Executive Chairman.

Revenue is predominantly monthly subscription fees across plan tiers ranging from roughly $1 to $38/month. A growing ad-supported tier and nascent advertising, consumer-products, and live-experience revenues supplement subscriptions; advertising is on track for approximately $3B in 2026 per the Q2 shareholder letter, though it remains sub-scale. Netflix's moat rests on global content scale, its proprietary Open Connect content-delivery network, a mature recommendation engine, and a subscriber base that funds a largely fixed content budget monetized in aggregate rather than per title.

In 2025 Netflix discontinued reporting membership and ARM figures, directing investors to revenue and operating margin instead. Netflix also executed a 10-for-1 forward stock split on November 14, 2025; all per-share and share-count figures here are split-adjusted, consistent with the ~$68.58 price and ~4.16B shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

Netflix's financials are strong on the levels and softening on the derivatives.

**Revenue and profitability.** Operating income of $13.33B produced the 29.5% margin. Net income was $10.98B (+26%), a 24.3% net margin. The 2026 deceleration is now visible: Q2 2026 revenue grew 13% to $12.56B, Q3 is guided to ~11%, and Q2 operating margin slipped to 33.4% from 34.1% as tech/development and sales & marketing outgrew revenue. All four regions still grew, with LATAM and APAC strongest.

**Cash flow and returns.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $10.1B and FCF $9.46B, an FCF margin of ~21%. ROE was 41.3% and ROA 19.8% — high-quality returns on a content-heavy asset base. FCF conversion (FCF/net income ~86%) is solid for a business that continuously capitalizes and amortizes content ($8.5B content amortization in H1 2026 alone).

**Balance sheet.** As of June 30, 2026, total assets were $58.5B, dominated by $33.8B of net content assets. Cash sat at ~$9.1B against ~$14.3B of fixed-rate senior notes maturing 2026–2054 (some Euro-denominated). The Altman Z-score of 2.87 places Netflix in the "grey zone" and D/E is ~1.1, but both metrics understate the credit quality of a business generating $9B+ FCF, carrying $9B+ cash, in full covenant compliance (minimum EBITDA/interest coverage 3.0x), with no near-term liquidity stress. Off-balance-sheet content obligations are large: $25.1B total content obligations ($19.6B not yet on the balance sheet), plus an estimated $1–4B of unknown future-title obligations over three years, which is the source of the operating-leverage risk if revenue disappoints.

**Capital allocation.** Netflix repurchased 86.5M split-adjusted shares for $9.1B in FY2025, then 66.4M shares for $5.9B in H1 2026 at average prices declining from ~$97 in April to ~$79 in June. The Board authorized an additional $25B in April 2026, leaving $27.1B available at quarter-end. No dividend. Management is buying aggressively into weakness, a per-share tailwind if the stock is genuinely cheap, but the declining average prices show the buyback has not yet defended the shares.

**Earnings surprise track record.** The recent record is a caution: over the last four quarters Netflix delivered two in-line prints ($0.80 and $0.56) and two misses (-17.4% in Q3 2025, -10.2% in Q1 2026). This weakens confidence in forward consensus and is inconsistent with the "reliable beat" pattern Netflix carried in prior years.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is intact in direction but eroding in slope. Revenue growth has stepped down from 16% (FY25) toward ~11% (Q3'26 guide), and operating-margin expansion — the second leg of the earnings-growth story — showed its first year-over-year dip in Q2 2026. The company frames growth within an operating-margin target; the Q2 margin compression suggests that framework may be bumping against the limits of pricing power and content-cost inflation.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration rather than a reacceleration. Current-quarter consensus fell 2.5% over both 30 and 90 days to $0.82; next-quarter is down 1.1% to $0.73; current-year down ~0.9% to $3.56; next-year down 0.5% to $3.81. This is broad-based negative momentum, not the +30% revision velocity that would flag a positive turn. Applying the growth-prospects dimension conservatively is warranted: the directional signal is still positive, but the recent separation between improving and deteriorating names is thin, and Netflix's revisions sit on the wrong side of the line.

The genuine incremental growth levers are advertising (~$3B in 2026, still sub-scale but scaling), the ad-supported tier's contribution to net additions, live programming, and gaming. None is yet large enough to re-accelerate consolidated revenue back to mid-teens in the near term. The March 2026 ~$587M tuck-in acquisition (undisclosed target, likely gaming, ad-tech, or production) is too small to move the growth trajectory. On balance, Netflix is a decelerating high-quality compounder, and the market has repriced it as such.

## Valuation Assessment

At $68.58 Netflix trades at ~21.6x trailing earnings and ~19.3x forward consensus ($3.56 current-year, $3.81 next-year). This is a material compression from the multiples the stock carried at its 52-week high, and it reflects fundamental re-rating rather than mechanical overshoot: growth slowed, estimates fell, and disclosure narrowed.

Peer comparison is of limited use here: the "Movies & Entertainment" cohort (DIS at 16x, LYV and PSKY at negative earnings, TKO at 79x, MSGS distorted) has a peer-median P/E of 79.4x driven by loss-makers and event-driven names. Netflix is not a linear-media asset and should not be anchored to Disney's 16x nor to the distorted median. On its own merits — a business still growing double-digits with a 29.5% operating margin, ~21% FCF margin, and $27B of buyback capacity — ~19x forward is a reasonable multiple for a decelerating-but-durable compounder. It is neither obviously cheap nor stretched.

The trajectory here matters: prior reports rated valuation as high as 7.0 in April, compressing to 5.5 by June. The stock has fallen further since, which mechanically improves the entry, but that improvement is offset by the falling estimates. We would resist scoring valuation as a strong buy signal on the multiple alone. Cheapness without a catalyst has historically been a value-trap warning, and Netflix currently lacks a specific re-rating catalyst — the reduced-disclosure overhang and decelerating top line are precisely what the market is penalizing. Analyst targets (median $94, high $135, low $70) sit well above spot, but with estimates drifting lower these targets are more likely to compress toward the price than to pull the price up. We assign a moderate valuation score of 6.5: the price is defensible, but there is no identified mispricing to exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

Netflix remains the scale leader in global streaming, and the WBD outcome strengthened its relative position more than the lost content assets weakened it. Netflix walked away with a $2.8B termination fee, no ~$42B bridge-financed integration burden, and clean focus, while Paramount-Skydance's WBD acquisition is now enmeshed in antitrust litigation: a 12-state coalition led by California sued to block it, a federal judge issued a temporary restraining order, the WGA sued, and the UK signaled possible intervention. Netflix's largest content rival is absorbed in a multi-front legal battle over the very assets Netflix pursued.

Competitive trajectory is nonetheless mixed rather than clearly improving. The streaming market is intensely competitive across subscription, ad-supported, transactional, and piracy models, and generative AI is cited repeatedly in filings as both a content-creation threat and a discovery-interface threat. Netflix's differentiation — content scale, Open Connect delivery, recommendation engine, and a maturing ad-tech stack — is durable, but the loss of subscriber-metric disclosure makes it harder to verify whether Netflix is gaining or holding engagement share as growth slows. The prior-report competitive-position score fell from 8.5 to 6.0; the WBD outcome argues against further deterioration, but the engagement-visibility gap and the deceleration keep this dimension at a stable-not-improving 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Growth deceleration plus reduced disclosure (primary overhang).** Netflix removed the subscriber/ARM metrics investors used to diagnose whether the slowdown reflects pricing, engagement, or saturation. The market is penalizing this opacity, and it caps the near-term re-rating.

**Fixed content cost structure.** ~$25B of non-cancelable content commitments mean margins are exposed if revenue growth disappoints — management explicitly warns it may not be able to cut spend commensurate with lower growth.

**2026 guild-contract labor risk.** WGA (May 1, 2026), SAG-AFTRA and DGA (June 30, 2026) agreements all expire in 2026. Strikes could halt productions and delay content, as in 2023 — a live binary this year.

**AWS single-vendor dependency.** Netflix runs the vast majority of computing on AWS and cannot easily switch, with the added wrinkle that Amazon Prime Video competes directly.

**FX exposure.** 57% of revenue is non-USD; a $91M FY2025 hedging loss and remeasurement volatility affect reported results.

**Tax/regulatory.** Effective tax rate rising (14% FY25 → 18% H1'26); ~$700M+ of Brazil non-income-tax deposits; EU content quotas and CMO music-licensing disputes are ongoing.

**Earnings quality caveat.** H1 2026 net income was inflated by the $2.8B pre-tax termination fee booked in interest and other income. Underlying operating momentum, stripped of that one-timer, is decelerating but healthy.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV rose from 27.9% to 40.8% since the prior snapshot as the stock fell 11.4%, and the term structure is in backwardation (40.8% near-term vs. 36.6% at six months), consistent with the market expecting an imminent move to resolve. IV is rich relative to 34.8% one-year historical vol (a 6-point spread), so options are not a cheap entry vehicle here. Put/call OI ratio climbed to 1.52 (from 1.05), and the six-month put/call volume ratio of 3.49 shows meaningful downside hedging further out, though the one-week skew (-2.8%) is balanced rather than panicked. Near-dated call buying at the $71 strike (3.5x OI) suggests some positioning for a bounce. Net read: the options market corroborates the fundamental caution — elevated uncertainty, downside protection being bought, no cheap-vol entry signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($100–125).** Netflix is a high-quality, FCF-generative compounder that has de-rated to ~19x forward. Advertising scales toward and past $3B, the ad-supported tier reaccelerates net additions, the Paramount-WBD deal stays mired in litigation and hands Netflix competitive breathing room, and the $27B buyback (at ~4.16B shares, roughly 10% of the cap) compounds per-share value at depressed prices. Operating margin resumes expansion as the ad business matures, revenue reaccelerates toward mid-teens, and the multiple re-rates back toward 25x on rising estimates. Analyst high target of $135 anchors this scenario.

**Bear Case ($52–60).** Growth continues to decelerate through 2026 (Q3 ~11%, trending lower), the reduced-disclosure overhang persists and analysts keep cutting estimates, a 2026 guild strike delays the content slate, margin compression continues as content and marketing costs outgrow a slowing top line, and the buyback fails to defend the stock. On flat-to-declining EPS the multiple compresses toward 15–16x, implying $52–60. The two recent double-digit earnings misses show this risk is not hypothetical.

The base case sits between: a decelerating-but-durable business at a fair multiple, with the buyback providing per-share support and the WBD outcome providing competitive optionality, offset by falling estimates and no catalyst to re-rate. That balance is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The risk/reward is roughly balanced. The stock is fairly valued at ~19x forward and the business is high-quality and FCF-rich, but the growth deceleration, falling estimates, reduced disclosure, and absence of a near-term catalyst prevent a confident BUY, while the balance-sheet strength, buyback support, and improved competitive position prevent an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Revenue growth stabilizes or reaccelerates above ~12% in a reported quarter, or advertising revenue visibly inflects toward a run-rate materially above $3B.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive across the current-year and next-year windows for two consecutive months (reversing the current -0.5% to -2.5% drift).
- A pullback into the low-to-mid $60s on unchanged fundamentals, which would push forward P/E below ~17x against a still-double-digit grower.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q3 or Q4 2026 revenue growth prints below ~9% with continued operating-margin compression, signaling structural saturation rather than a soft patch.
- A prolonged 2026 guild strike that visibly delays the content slate and forward estimates.
- A third consecutive double-digit earnings miss, or evidence the buyback is being funded by rising leverage rather than FCF.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings report (October 2026), which will test whether the ~11% Q3 guide was conservative or the start of a lower growth regime, and will provide the first clean look at underlying momentum stripped of the WBD termination fee. The 2026 guild-contract negotiations (WGA/SAG-AFTRA/DGA) are a concurrent binary to monitor.

## Conclusion

Netflix is a strong business trading at a fair-but-not-cheap price with a genuine growth-visibility overhang. The balance sheet is sound and the buyback capacity provides per-share support. The failed WBD deal was, on balance, a favorable outcome: a $2.8B windfall, no new debt, and a competitor now consumed by antitrust litigation.

Against that, revenue growth is decelerating to ~11%, operating margins dipped year-over-year in Q2, consensus estimates are falling across every window, and two of the last four quarters missed by double digits. The 2025 decision to stop reporting subscriber metrics has collided with slowing growth to erode market confidence, driving the stock to a 52-week low. At ~19x forward earnings the multiple already reflects much of this, but there is no identified catalyst to re-rate it and no positive estimate-revision turn to lean on. The score trajectory (6.3 → 6.1 over four months, with competitive position, growth, and valuation all stepping down) reflects a real fundamental softening, not merely price action.

We rate NFLX a **HOLD**. The quality and the buyback keep it off the AVOID list; the deceleration, falling estimates, and lack of a catalyst keep it off the BUY list. We would turn constructive on a stabilization or reacceleration in revenue growth, a positive estimate-revision turn, or a pullback into the low-to-mid $60s.
