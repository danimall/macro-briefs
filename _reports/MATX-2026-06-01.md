**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Matson is a 144-year-old Hawaii-based ocean shipping and logistics operator running two businesses with sharply different economics: a defensible, Jones Act-protected domestic franchise (Hawaii, Alaska, Guam — roughly 51% of ocean revenue) and a premium, speed-differentiated China-to-Long Beach expedited service (CLX/MAX, ~33% of ocean volume) that carries the majority of the company's earnings variability. The stock has rallied 62% over the past twelve months, from a 52-week low of $86.97 to $181.13, and trades at 13.3x forward earnings against an analyst target range of $217-230.

The investment case turned more constructive at the May 4, 2026 Q1 report. EPS of $1.85 beat consensus by ~10% (consensus $1.69), ocean transportation operating income exceeded internal plan on stronger-than-expected post-Lunar New Year China demand, and management raised FY2026 guidance from "approach" to "modestly exceed" the $499.8M operating income posted in 2025. Estimate revisions have followed: current-year consensus is up 16.5% over 90 days, next year up 12.6%. The balance sheet is conservative (only $361M of financial debt against $2.76B equity and ~$500M operating income), capital allocation is best-in-class (26% share-count reduction since 2021, $300M+ annual buybacks, a growing dividend, all funded alongside a ~$1B fleet renewal through the tax-advantaged Capital Construction Fund), and the Jones Act routes provide a structural moat.

The constraint on the rating is that the 13.3x forward multiple reflects, rather than mistakes, the cyclicality of the business. The expedited China service drives the majority of earnings variance, and the FY2026 guidance rests on peak-season transpacific demand holding through the year. The 2022-to-2023 sequence (net income $1.06B → $297M, -72%) shows the downside when that service rolls over. China volume was already down 9.5% in FEU terms in 2025, a known Q2 2026 fuel-cost-recovery lag is a near-term drag, and insiders have sold across 15+ transactions in the past six months with zero open-market buying. The quality is real and the franchise is durable, but at the current price the risk/reward is balanced. We rate the shares HOLD with a positive growth lean.

## Company Overview

Matson operates two reportable segments:

**Ocean Transportation (~82% of revenue, ~91% of operating income).** Two sub-businesses with different risk profiles:
- *Jones Act domestic franchise:* Matson is the largest carrier between the U.S. West Coast and Hawaii, operating an effective duopoly versus Pasha in Hawaii and a competitive but entrenched position versus TOTE and barge operators in Alaska. These routes are protected by the Jones Act, which requires domestic waterborne freight to move on U.S.-built, U.S.-flagged, U.S.-crewed, and 75% U.S.-owned vessels. High barriers, rational competition, quasi-regulated rates (Surface Transportation Board safe harbor of ±7.5%/-10%) that cap both upside and downside.
- *Expedited China-Long Beach (CLX/MAX):* A premium transpacific service competing on speed and reliability rather than price, carrying e-commerce, garments, and electronics. This is the high-beta, high-margin, highly cyclical engine that drove the 2021-2022 boom and the subsequent normalization.

**Logistics (~18% of revenue, ~9% of operating income).** Asset-light freight brokerage, forwarding (Span Alaska), warehousing, and supply-chain management at 7-8% operating margins, competing in a fragmented field (C.H. Robinson, Hub Group, J.B. Hunt, RXO).

A structurally important asset sits between the two: a 35% stake in the SSAT West Coast stevedoring joint venture, which gives Matson three exclusive terminals enabling the faster, more reliable port calls that differentiate the expedited China service.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The 2022 peak ($4.34B) was a non-repeatable transpacific rate spike. The path since is the relevant baseline: $3.93B (2021) → $4.34B (2022) → $3.09B (2023, -29%) → $3.42B (2024) → $3.34B (2025, -2.3%). Normalized revenue runs well below the boom.

**Profitability.** Net income normalized from $1.06B (2022) to $297M (2023), recovered to $476M (2024), and settled at $445M in 2025. FY2025 operating income was $499.8M, down 9.3% YoY, driven by a $45.3M decline in Ocean Transportation (China volume -9.5% FEU amid 2025 tariff and trade volatility), partly offset by a $33.5M positive swing in the SSAT contribution. FY2025 metrics: operating margin 14.9%, net margin 13.3%, ROE 16.1%, ROA 9.6% — solid for a capital-intensive shipper, well below boom levels.

**Balance sheet.** Conservatively levered. Total financial debt of $361M against $2.76B equity is roughly 0.13x debt/equity. Interest expense of $6.8M against ~$500M operating income produces coverage above 70x. The reported "D/E of 1.68" reflects total liabilities (including pensions and leases) rather than financial debt, and the Altman Z of 2.25 is depressed by the asset-heavy, structurally negative-working-capital nature of a Jones Act shipper, not by distress. The standard distress screens materially understate this company's resilience.

**The hidden liquidity asset.** The Capital Construction Fund holds $532.7M in U.S. Treasury securities, tax-advantaged and earmarked for vessel construction. Combined with $142M of cash, it covers roughly 93% of remaining newbuild milestone payments. This explains how Matson funds a ~$1B fleet renewal, repurchases $300M+ of stock annually, and grows its dividend simultaneously without meaningful incremental debt.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $547M; FCF after maintenance capex was ~$398M (~11.9% FCF margin, ~7.3% FCF yield on current market cap). Share count fell from 41.0M (2021) to 30.4M (2025), a 26% reduction; $303M was repurchased in 2025, with a new 3.0M-share authorization added in April 2026. The dividend grew from $1.06 (2021) to $1.40 (2025). Capex steps up to $550-595M in 2026 for the three LNG-ready Aloha Class newbuilds (deliveries Q1'27, Q3'27, Q2'28), then tapers to ~$305-325M (2027) and ~$125-145M (2028), funded largely from the CCF.

**Earnings track record.** Four consecutive beats: +38.8% (Q2'25), +27.8% (Q3'25), +0.9% (Q4'25), +9.6% (Q1'26). The pattern supports confidence in current consensus, though the magnitude of the earlier beats reflects the China demand surprises that also create the downside variance.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs, of which one is durable and two are cyclical.

The durable leg is the Jones Act franchise: stable lifeline volumes in Hawaii, Alaska, and Guam, with a modest long-term tailwind from Pasha's planned 2027 relocation to Kapalama freeing Sand Island terminal space for Matson's Phase 3 expansion. This grows in the low single digits at best — Hawaii's economy is sluggish, with visitor arrivals projected to decline in 2026 before recovering in 2027.

The cyclical legs carry the upside and the risk. Management is extending the China service into Southeast Asia (Vietnam and Thailand feeder networks) and positioning e-commerce as a structural growth driver for CLX/MAX, diversifying away from pure-China origination. The raised FY2026 guidance — with Q2 operating income guided ~$20M above the prior year's $113.0M — is the key forward signal, and it implies the China service holds through peak season.

Estimate revisions confirm the positive momentum: current quarter $3.65 (+5.4% over 90 days, though -1.8% over 30 days), next quarter +10.2%, current year up to $13.98, next year up to $15.15. These are strong and broadly accelerating, but they sit below the +30% velocity threshold that typically signals a genuine trough-to-recovery turn. Matson is not recovering from a depressed trough; it is operating near a cyclically solid level, and the revisions reflect the market catching up to that level rather than discounting a multi-year re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $181.13, MATX trades at 13.3x trailing/forward earnings ($13.81 trailing EPS, $13.98 CY consensus). On the surface this is cheap: the Industrials peer set shown trades at a median P/E of 38.6x (a heterogeneous group — Genpact at 9.7x, ESAB at 24.8x, Alaska Air at 74.5x, Casella at an outlier 785.9x), and the analyst target range of $217-230 (mean $224, median $225) implies 20-27% upside.

The peer comparison is not the right frame. Jones Act and transpacific shippers structurally trade at low single-digit-to-low-teens multiples because the market discounts the cyclicality of the freight-rate-sensitive transpacific leg. A 13.3x multiple on earnings that include a meaningful China contribution is not obviously a discount to fair value; it is the multiple the market consistently assigns to this earnings stream because it knows the stream is volatile. The 2022-to-2023 collapse is the reason the multiple stays compressed.

The relevant valuation question is whether current earnings are above, at, or below mid-cycle. FY2026 guidance points to a modest improvement over the $445M net income and $499.8M operating income of 2025, which sit well below the 2022 peak but above the 2023 trough. If the China service holds, the forward FCF yield of ~7.3% and the 13x multiple are reasonable and provide some support. If the transpacific leg softens — as it did in 2023, and as the 2025 -9.5% FEU decline already foreshadowed — earnings can fall faster than the multiple, and the "cheap" stock re-rates downward on lower earnings. The absence of a margin of safety against that scenario at the current price is what caps the valuation score.

The analyst targets would normally be treated as floors on a cheap, momentum-positive name. Here they are tempered: the cohort is only three analysts, and the targets assume the cyclical leg holds. The targets are credible base-case fair-value estimates rather than independent confirmation of asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

**Domestic (Jones Act):** A near-duopoly/oligopoly with rational competition and quasi-regulated rates. Hawaii is Matson versus Pasha; Alaska is Matson versus TOTE plus barge operators. Barriers are formidable — the U.S.-build/flag/crew/own requirements make new entry economically prohibitive. This position is stable-to-improving (Pasha's Kapalama move helps modestly).

**China/Transpacific:** Matson competes against global mega-carriers (CMA CGM, Cosco, ZIM, Hede) not on price but on speed and reliability, charging a premium enabled by the SSAT exclusive-terminal access and dedicated equipment. This is a genuine differentiator and a real moat, but it is exposed to global overcapacity cycles, transpacific freight-rate swings, U.S.-China tariff and port-fee policy (the October 2025 trade deal reduced this uncertainty), and customer supply-chain diversification away from China — which the Southeast Asia expansion is designed to address.

**Logistics:** A fragmented, competitive, supporting business that does not differentiate the franchise.

Cumulative relative TSR of 232 versus a peer-group index of 136 confirms Matson has materially outperformed its competitive set over the measured period. The trajectory of the competitive position is stable-to-improving, which supports a score above the neutral line.

## Risk Assessment

**China/transpacific cyclicality (the dominant risk).** Roughly 33% of ocean volume drives the majority of earnings variance. FY2026 guidance is entirely dependent on this service holding through peak season; the 2025 FEU decline shows the volume can move against the company quickly.

**Jones Act challenge (low probability, high severity).** A February 2025 lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for D.C. challenges the Jones Act under the Port Preference Clause. Matson has intervened and calls it meritless. Success would expose ~51% of ocean revenue to foreign-flagged competition. Probability is low given bipartisan, executive, and national-security support, but this is now active, named litigation and a genuine tail risk that was not present in prior filings.

**Newbuild execution.** The ~$1B Philly Shipyard program (now under Hanwha ownership after the 2024 acquisition) carries delivery-delay, spec, subcontractor, and integration risk. Delays would impede the planned CLX upsizing and Alaska fleet cascade.

**Q2 2026 fuel drag (near-term, known).** The Iran-related fuel-price spike, combined with the lagging surcharge-recovery mechanism, will produce a negative fuel drag in Q2 2026, with most recovery expected in Q3. In-year recovery is historically reliable, but it pressures the next print.

**Soft domestic end-markets.** Sluggish Hawaii economy, declining 2026 visitor arrivals, challenged Guam tourism.

**Labor/pension.** 68% unionized; ILWU/PMA West Coast exposure; multi-employer pension withdrawal liability the company concedes "may be material."

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing is consistent with a balanced, moderately elevated-uncertainty setup rather than directional conviction. Near-term ATM IV of 42.5% sits above 1-year HV of 35.4% (a 7.0pp rich spread), with the implied move at ±7.5% into the June 18 expiry. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.8%, the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.13 (no structural put dominance), and the term structure is flat (near 42.5% ≈ far 41.0%), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly rather than concentrated around a single catalyst. IV ticked up 0.9pp into a 1.8% price decline. Short interest is low at 4.3% of float (3.7 days to cover). The options market is not flagging a directional bearish bet, but the rich IV signals the market is pricing meaningful two-sided uncertainty around the China-dependent earnings path, aligning with the fundamental view that the risk/reward is balanced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** China e-commerce demand and the Southeast Asia expansion sustain expedited-service volumes through 2026 peak season; the Q2 fuel drag recovers in Q3 as expected; FY2026 operating income comfortably exceeds guidance and estimate revisions continue upward. The market re-rates the multiple modestly toward the analyst target range as the cyclical fear premium compresses. EPS reaches the $15.15 next-year consensus and beyond. The buyback (now with a fresh 3.0M-share authorization) continues shrinking the share count at ~13x earnings, compounding per-share value. Target: $217-230 (analyst range), ~13-15x on $15+ EPS plus buyback accretion.

**Bear case.** Transpacific demand softens into H2 2026 as front-loaded tariff-avoidance shipments fade and global overcapacity weighs on the premium-service spread; China FEU volume continues the 2025 decline; the Q2 fuel drag proves stickier than guided. Operating income falls toward the FY2023 normalization level. The 13x multiple does not protect the stock because earnings fall faster than the multiple compresses, producing a -15% to -20% drawdown toward the low-$150s, consistent with the early-2026 trading range, with the Jones Act lawsuit as a low-probability tail that could amplify any selloff.

The franchise quality, balance-sheet strength, and capital discipline anchor the downside, but the dominant earnings driver is cyclical and the entry multiple offers limited cushion against a China disappointment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive growth lean (growth_hold). The Q1 2026 turn, raised guidance, and rising estimates are genuine and constructive, but the score (6.5) and the cyclical/valuation profile do not clear the bar for a high-conviction BUY in a sector where the multiple is compressed for structural reasons.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (early August 2026), which will reveal both the magnitude of the fuel drag and whether peak-season China volume is holding.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY2026 China/expedited-service FEU volume turns positive YoY (reversing the 2025 decline), confirming the demand thesis is durable rather than a post-Lunar-New-Year one-off.
- Management raises FY2026 operating-income guidance a second time, with current-year estimate revisions accelerating above +20%.
- A pullback toward the mid-$150s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a margin of safety and pushing the FCF yield toward 8.5%+.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of China FEU volume declines steeper than 2025's, signaling structural transpacific share loss rather than cyclical softness.
- FY2026 operating income guided or tracking below the FY2023 level (~$400M or worse), confirming a roll-over rather than a stabilization.
- Material adverse development in the Jones Act litigation (e.g., the court declining Matson's motion and allowing the challenge to proceed on the merits).

## Conclusion

Matson is a well-run, conservatively financed, structurally advantaged shipper with a defensible Jones Act franchise, a genuine speed-based moat in its expedited China service, and capital allocation that is among the best in the sector. The Q1 2026 beat, the raised FY2026 guidance, and rising estimates have improved the picture, consistent with the upward score trajectory (6.5 in March to a constructive footing today). The improvement in competitive position and management-quality assessments over the past two quarters is supported by the relative TSR, the disciplined buyback, and the franchise durability.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: the majority of earnings variance comes from a cyclical China transpacific service, FY2026 guidance depends on that service holding through peak season, China volume already fell 9.5% in 2025, a known Q2 fuel-cost-recovery lag pressures the next print, and the 13.3x forward multiple reflects rather than misprices that cyclicality. Shippers trade cheap because their earnings are volatile; a low multiple on possibly-near-peak earnings does not create the asymmetry that a BUY requires, and a bear case carries 15-20% downside that the entry price does not cushion. The complete absence of open-market insider buying across 15+ recent transactions reinforces that those closest to the company are not signaling the stock is cheap after a 62% run.

We rate MATX **HOLD** with a positive growth lean. We would become more constructive on confirmation that China/expedited volumes are growing again, on a second guidance raise, or on a pullback toward the mid-$150s that restores a margin of safety.
