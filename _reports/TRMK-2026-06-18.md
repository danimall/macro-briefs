**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Trustmark Corporation is a roughly $18.9B-asset bank holding company based in Jackson, Mississippi, operating Trustmark National Bank across six Southeastern markets. FY2025 was a record year on an adjusted basis: net income reached $224.1M (diluted EPS $3.70), with FTE net interest margin expanding to 3.80% from 3.51% in 2024 and 3.32% in 2023. The margin expansion is the direct payoff of a deliberate 2024 securities restructuring in which management sold $1.56B of AFS securities yielding 1.36% and reinvested in 4.85% paper, absorbing a $137.1M after-tax loss that distorted 2024 GAAP and makes the flat headline net income (2024 $223.0M → 2025 $224.1M) misleading. Adjusted for that one-time loss, continuing-operations earnings rose roughly 20%.

The forward setup is more balanced than the trailing record suggests. The balance sheet is now modestly liability-sensitive into a falling-rate cycle: management's own model shows a 100bp rate decrease cuts net interest income about 2.1% and a 200bp decrease cuts it about 4.8%. With the Fed having cut in late 2025 and the rate path uncertain, NIM defense via deposit repricing becomes the swing factor, and 2026 guidance pins NIM in a tight 3.80–3.85% band, implying the margin tailwind has largely played out. Credit, while genuinely improving on criticized/classified migration, benefited in 2025 from an artificially low 13bp net charge-off rate and a released qualitative reserve; management guides provisioning to normalize to 14–18bps in 2026, which will modestly compress EPS growth. Mississippi-region mortgage nonaccruals jumping to $73.0M from $42.2M YoY is a specific, monitorable soft spot.

We rate TRMK a HOLD on an income basis. The bank is well-run, well-capitalized (CET1 11.72%), and returning ~62% of earnings to shareholders, and its strategy of harvesting talent and clients from heavy M&A disruption in its footprint (Synovus/Pinnacle, Cadence, Stellar, a Houston deal) is credible and capital-light. But the stock has re-rated ~32% over the trailing year to $44.31, trades near its $46.34 52-week high at roughly 11x forward earnings and ~1.5x tangible book, and consensus price targets ($45.50 low / $52.25 high, $47.45 mean) leave only single-digit base-case upside. There is no asymmetric entry at this price.

## Company Overview

Trustmark has operated Trustmark National Bank for over 130 years. After exiting the insurance business with the 2024 sale of its FBBI subsidiary, the company now reports two segments:

- **General Banking** — the engine, generating $212.2M of 2025 net income. Traditional commercial and consumer lending, deposit-taking, and a centralized mortgage-banking operation in Jackson that services an $8.96B third-party loan portfolio (carrying a $131.3M MSR asset).
- **Wealth Management** — $11.9M net income (+53% YoY, record revenue), with $10.9B AUM/AUA and $2.6B brokerage assets.

The footprint spans Mississippi (home, ~51% of loans), Alabama, Texas, Georgia (an Atlanta equipment-finance hub), Florida, and Tennessee. The $13.7B loan book is reasonably diversified: nonfarm-nonresidential CRE (24%), 1-4 family residential (17% plus 5% other), C&I (15%), multifamily/other real-estate-secured (15%), and a growing equipment-finance lease book. The competitive edge is a low-cost core deposit base — deposit cost was just 1.72% in Q4 2025 — supported by an "Outstanding" CRA rating upgraded from "Needs to Improve."

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and margin.** The flat 2024→2025 headline is an artifact: 2024 GAAP absorbed the $182.8M pre-tax securities-swap loss. On a continuing, adjusted basis the trajectory is up roughly 20%. 2023 net income was $165.5M. Net interest income grew 8.8% to $636M (Q4 NIM 3.81–3.83%) and interest expense fell 16.9% YoY on falling deposit costs.

**Profitability and efficiency.** ROA was 1.21% and ROTE 12.97% — solid for a regional bank — supported by disciplined expense growth of 5.5%.

**Capital and returns.** CET1 stood at 11.72% and total risk-based capital at 14.41%, comfortably well-capitalized. Tangible book value per share grew 13.5% to $30.28. The company returned roughly 62% of net income in 2025: ~$80M of buybacks (2.2M shares, ~3.5% of the count) plus $58.5M of dividends, with the quarterly dividend raised 4.2% to $0.25. The trailing dividend yield is ~2.2%.

**Balance sheet.** Loans grew 4.5% to $13.7B; deposits grew 2.6% to $15.5B; uninsured deposits were a manageable 35.3%. The company issued $175M of 6.00% sub debt in Q4 2025 to refinance $125M of 2020 notes.

A note on screening artifacts: standardized credit-stress tools flag TRMK's Altman Z-Score (0.24) and D/E (7.92x) as alarming, but these are not meaningful for a bank, where deposits are the liabilities that make the structure inherently "leveraged." The relevant capital metrics — CET1 11.72% and TCE/TA 9.61% — are healthy, and Piotroski signals (positive NI, ROA, OCF, OCF>NI) are all favorable.

**Recent momentum.** Q1 2026 EPS was $0.95 (adjusted), beating the $0.90 consensus and up from $0.88 a year earlier, on revenue of $205.9M (+4.3% YoY). The earnings-surprise record is consistent: three beats and one narrow miss over the last four quarters, with beats of 3.9–6.0%. This supports moderate confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is mid-single-digit and decelerating from the restructuring-driven gains. 2026 guidance calls for loans and ex-brokered deposits both up mid-single digits, NII up mid-single digits, and noninterest income and expense both up mid-single digits — a steady-state regional-bank profile.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year EPS revised to $3.90 (+2.4% over 30 days, +2.8% over 90 days), and next-year EPS to $4.10 (+0.5%/+0.8%). This is healthy directional confirmation, well short of the +30% revision threshold that would flag a high-conviction re-rating.

The genuine growth lever is organic share-taking. Management is funding C&I and equipment-finance expansion with production hires — 21 in Q3 2025 and 13 in Q4 — explicitly to capture talent and clients dislocated by the wave of consolidation across Trustmark's exact footprint. Flat NIM removes the two-year tailwind that drove the trailing record, and provision normalization will trim what would otherwise flow to the bottom line. Net result: a high-single-digit pre-provision pace that converts to lower-single-digit reported EPS growth in 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

At $44.31, TRMK trades at roughly 11.4x consensus 2026 EPS of $3.90 and ~1.5x tangible book value. Against the regional-bank peer set, TRMK at ~11.4x forward sits modestly below the peer median P/E of 13.5x, while its profitability (ROA 1.21%, ROTE ~13%) is at or above the group. The peer set is dispersed (CUBI 9.8x, WAFD 12.0x, NBTB 13.5x, FBNC 21.0x, BBT 22.1x), so the median is a loose anchor.

The modest discount to peer-median P/E is not a clear mispricing. It is consistent with TRMK's liability-sensitivity into a falling-rate cycle, the Mississippi mortgage credit watch item, and the fact that NIM expansion — the engine of the trailing re-rating — is guided flat. A compressed multiple on a bank facing a NIM ceiling and provision normalization is a fair multiple, not a value opportunity.

We resist treating the prior 32% appreciation as a reason to be bearish; the move was driven by genuine NIM expansion and adjusted earnings growth, not multiple inflation alone. The re-rating has already captured the restructuring payoff. Consensus targets ($45.50 low, $46.50 median, $47.45 mean, $52.25 high) bracket the current price tightly, implying roughly 3–7% base-case upside to the mean before the ~2.2% dividend. The dividend yield is below the peer median of 3.0%, so the income case is sound but not standout. We do not score cheapness highly here because the low multiple reflects identifiable forward headwinds rather than an unrecognized catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Trustmark is a mid-cap Southeastern regional competing against larger banks, community banks, and credit unions. Its durable advantage is the low-cost core deposit franchise, which underpins NIM resilience as the Fed eases.

The differentiated dynamic is the M&A wave reshaping its footprint: Synovus/Pinnacle, Cadence, Stellar, and a Houston-market deal all sit in or adjacent to Trustmark's markets. CEO Dewey has framed this as an organic-growth opportunity — recruiting dislocated bankers and winning disrupted clients rather than chasing acquisitions — while retaining optionality for opportunistic $1B–$10B deals in TX, AR, LA, and TN. This is a credible, capital-light path given the strong capital position and the recent production-hire surge. Wealth Management's net-income growth and record revenue show the franchise can compound fee income alongside the core bank.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than sharply widening. Trustmark is taking share at the margin in a fragmenting regional landscape, but it remains a price-taker on deposits in a competitive Southeastern market and faces the same NIM and CRE pressures as the group.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Interest-rate / NIM sensitivity (dominant).** With NII the bulk of revenue and the balance sheet modestly liability-sensitive in a falling-rate environment, the macro backdrop is genuinely two-sided: the rate path is uncertain into 2026, and either a renewed inflation print or a faster easing cycle stresses the tight NIM guide.

2. **Mississippi mortgage credit.** Total nonaccruals rose to $84.4M, concentrated in Mississippi-region mortgage loans; all mortgage lending is centralized in Jackson, concentrating the risk. Total NPAs were $91.3M (+6.2%). Partially offset by resolution of three large AL/TX commercial nonaccruals.

3. **Provision normalization.** A qualitative reserve was released in Q3 2025, flattering earnings. Management guides NCOs to 13–15bps and provision to 14–18bps in 2026 — a real, guided EPS headwind.

4. **CRE/office/hospitality.** NFNR CRE is the largest commercial class at $3.3B, including ~$226M nonowner-occupied office and ~$235M hotel/motel. Management is actively running this down (NFNR -6.5% in 2025).

5. **Hurricane concentration.** A genuine company-specific exposure given the Gulf Coast AL/FL/MS/TX footprint.

6. **AOCI / HTM unrealized losses.** HTM securities carry a $26.9M unrealized loss; transferred-securities AOCI loss is $36.3M (improving from $46.6M). Portfolio credit quality remains 100% Treasury/agency/Aaa-Aa3.

Many of these — rate sensitivity, CRE, hurricane risk — are sector-permanent and already broadly priced. The two that are most specific and least-priced are the Mississippi mortgage migration and the guided provision step-up, which is why we hold the risk score at the middling level rather than higher.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options picture is noisy and likely distorted. Reported near-term ATM IV of 56.4% against 23.5% one-year historical vol, with backwardation (1m 56.4% vs 3m 19.6%), nominally signals imminent stress. However, the prior-snapshot delta shows ATM IV jumping from 61.3% to 480.6% — a thin, illiquid options chain (only 20 contracts across four expirations) producing corrupted readings rather than informative positioning. We treat the headline IV as a thin-options artifact. The put/call OI ratio sitting flat at 1.00 is neutral. Short interest at 7.5% of float with 8.7 days to cover is moderate and not a crowded-short setup. The options data is not a reliable input here and does not contradict the fundamental HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($52).** Deposit costs continue to reprice faster than asset yields decline, defending NIM at the top of the guided range or above. The organic share-taking from peer M&A disruption accelerates, pushing loan growth toward high-single digits with C&I and equipment-finance mix improvement. Mississippi mortgage nonaccruals stabilize, provisioning normalizes at the low end (14bps), and Wealth Management compounds fee income. Buybacks at ~1.5x TBV remain accretive, and the stock re-rates toward the ~13.5x peer median on ~$4.10 next-year EPS, supporting the $52.25 analyst high.

**Bear case ($36).** A faster Fed easing cycle exposes liability-sensitivity, NII falls 3–5%, and NIM slips below 3.80%. Mississippi mortgage credit deteriorates further, provisioning runs above the 18bp guide, and CRE office/hotel marks add pressure. EPS lands flat-to-down for 2026, the multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range, and the stock retraces toward its prior-year base around the $36 area (~19% downside from current).

The asymmetry is roughly symmetric-to-unfavorable from $44.31: ~17% to the bull case versus ~19% to the bear, with the most likely outcome a low-single-digit total return driven mostly by the dividend and buyback.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on a ~2.2% dividend plus a ~3.5% buyback yield against stable, well-capitalized cash generation, with limited near-term appreciation potential given the re-rating and flat NIM guide.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to ~$38–40 (≈1.25x TBV / <10x forward EPS) that restores asymmetric upside.
- Evidence that NIM is holding above 3.85% for two consecutive quarters despite Fed easing, confirming deposit-repricing discipline beats the liability-sensitivity model.
- Mississippi mortgage nonaccruals declining for two consecutive quarters alongside continued criticized/classified improvement, removing the credit overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut taking NIM below 3.75% or NII to flat/negative for 2026.
- Provision/charge-offs running materially above the 18bp guide, or Mississippi mortgage nonaccruals breaching ~$100M.
- A debt-funded acquisition above ~2x tangible book that dilutes capital return without clear synergies.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), with the rate path (Fed guidance) and the Mississippi mortgage nonaccrual trend as the two variables to monitor in the interim.

## Conclusion

Trustmark is a well-managed, well-capitalized regional bank that engineered a real 50bp NIM expansion through a disciplined 2024 securities restructuring, delivered record adjusted earnings, and is returning roughly 62% of earnings to shareholders while building organic growth from peer M&A disruption. The franchise quality and capital position are not in question. The forward risk/reward is the open question: NIM is guided flat, provisioning is set to normalize higher, the balance sheet is liability-sensitive into an easing cycle, Mississippi mortgage credit is a live watch item, and the stock has already re-rated ~32% to trade near its 52-week high at ~11x forward earnings with consensus targets only modestly above the current price. We rate TRMK a HOLD for income, with an upgrade contingent on either a meaningful pullback or hard evidence that NIM and Mississippi credit hold up better than the bear case fears.
