**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Northrop Grumman has fallen roughly 26% from its early-March 2026 peak of ~$745 to $563, even as the company posted a clean Q1 2026 (EPS +85% to $6.14, operating margin recovering to 10.0%), raised its dividend, and reached an agreement with the U.S. Air Force to accelerate B-21 production. The drawdown is driven by capital-allocation and overhang concerns rather than a deterioration in the core business: the market is discounting a ~$2.5B company-funded B-21 capacity investment that will depress near-term free cash flow, unresolved pricing on not-to-exceed (NTE) production lots through unit 40, and a new executive-order risk that could constrain share repurchases and dividends. At ~18x trailing and forward EPS, NOC trades at a meaningful discount to the aerospace and defense peer median of 38.7x, though that median is inflated by component suppliers (TransDigm, Howmet) that operate on different margin structures than a prime contractor.

The bull case rests on durable demand — a $95.6B backlog (~2.3x revenue), sole-source positions in the B-21 Raider and Sentinel ICBM, and the OBBBA's $150B in supplemental defense funding directed at missile defense, munitions, and deterrence — combined with a valuation that has compressed below the company's own recent history. The bear case is that NOC is a fixed-price-exposed prime with an Altman Z-score of 1.88, debt/equity of 2.08, $1.0B of remaining B-21 loss accruals, an unbounded DOJ criminal pension-CAS investigation, and an executive-order threat to the capital-return story that has historically anchored the stock.

We rate NOC a HOLD. The valuation discount is genuine but is matched by a cluster of company-specific risks that are difficult to quantify and that the market is correctly assigning a discount to. Estimate revisions are flat, there is no insider buying, and the largest near-term free-cash-flow swing factor (B-21 NTE economics) remains unresolved. We see the stock as range-bound until B-21 NTE-lot pricing and the regulatory overhangs clarify.

## Company Overview

Northrop Grumman is one of the five large U.S. defense primes, deriving 84% of FY2025 sales (86% in Q1 2026) from the U.S. government, principally the Department of Defense and the intelligence community. The company operates four reporting segments:

- **Aeronautics Systems** ($12.99B, 31% of FY2025 sales): B-21 Raider strategic bomber, F-35 components, E-2, TACAMO, and HALE ISR platforms (Global Hawk/Triton). Segment margin was depressed to 6.3% by B-21 loss provisions.
- **Mission Systems** ($12.51B, 30%): radars, electronic warfare, C4ISR, advanced microelectronics, and marine power/propulsion. The highest-margin segment at 14.6% and the strongest grower (+10%).
- **Space Systems** ($10.77B, 26%): satellites, missile defense, launch and propulsion (GEM solid rocket motors, SLS boosters). Sales fell 8% on the wind-down of restricted programs and the Next-Generation Interceptor.
- **Defense Systems** ($8.00B, 19%): Sentinel ICBM, tactical weapons, ammunition, IBCS, and hypersonics. Margin expanded to 10.9%.

The defining competitive feature is NOC's sole-source ownership of two of the highest-priority nuclear modernization programs: the B-21 Raider (next-generation stealth bomber) and the Sentinel land-based ICBM. These are multi-decade national-security priorities with extremely high barriers to entry. Total backlog stands at $95.6B, approximately 2.3x annual revenue, with $14.8B of restricted awards booked in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue grew from $36.6B (2022) to $41.95B (2025), a low-single-digit organic trajectory (+2% in 2025, ~3% organic). Q1 2026 revenue was $9.88B, up 4%. Net income was $4.18B in 2025, essentially flat versus $4.17B in 2024, with the 2023 figure of $2.06B depressed by the $1.56B B-21 charge. Diluted EPS was $29.08 for 2025. Operating margin recovered to 10.8% in 2025 from 6.5% in 2023; Q1 2026 operating margin was 10.0% versus 6.1% in the prior-year quarter, which carried a $477M B-21 charge. The Q1 2026 EPS jump of +85% therefore largely reflects the absence of a prior-year charge rather than underlying acceleration. ROE of 25% and ROA of 8.1% are strong, though ROE is flattered by the leveraged balance sheet.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Free cash flow grew to $3.31B in 2025 (+26%), an FCF margin of roughly 7.9%, with capex declining to $1.45B. The company returned $2.9B to shareholders in 2025 ($3.7B in 2024) through buybacks and dividends. The quarterly dividend was raised 12% in May 2025 (to $2.31) and raised again ~7% in 2026, per the FY2025 filing. Share count has declined steadily from 155M (2022) to 142M (Q1 2026), with $2.5B remaining on the 2024 buyback authorization. Q1 2026 FCF was -$1.82B, which is normal seasonality for NOC (cash flow is heavily second-half weighted), but cash fell from $4.4B at year-end to $2.1B in Q1.

**Balance sheet.** The leverage profile is the constraint. The Altman Z-score of 1.88 sits in the grey zone, driven by debt/equity of 2.08 and equity at only 32.5% of assets. Goodwill of $17.4B is 34% of total assets, a legacy of the Orbital ATK acquisition. Long-term debt is $14.4B and the current ratio is a thin 1.1. This leverage is typical for a defense prime backed by stable government cash flows, but it caps financial flexibility, and it interacts unfavorably with the ~$2.5B B-21 capacity investment and the $2.5B remaining buyback authorization: NOC cannot fully fund both, the dividend growth, and debt service without leaning further on its credit profile. The earnings quality is solid (FCF conversion above net income in 2025), but the balance sheet has less cushion than the steady earnings stream implies.

**Earnings surprise track record.** Four consecutive beats: Q1 2026 +0.4%, Q4 2025 +2.8%, Q3 2025 +17.6%, Q2 2025 +3.1%. The Q3 2025 outsized beat reflected favorable EAC adjustments rather than a structural change. The recent surprises have been modest, consistent with a mature business where consensus is well-calibrated.

## Growth Analysis

NOC's growth is durable but slow. Revenue compounds in the low single digits, with Mission Systems (+10%) and Defense Systems (Sentinel) the growth engines, partly offset by Space Systems declines (-8%) as restricted programs and NGI wind down. The structural tailwinds are real: a global threat environment (Ukraine, the Middle East and Iran, the Western Pacific), the OBBBA's $150B supplemental defense funding directed at missile defense, munitions, deterrence, and shipbuilding, and the ~$859B FY2026 defense appropriation enacted in February 2026.

The estimate-revision picture, however, is flat rather than positive. Current-year EPS consensus is $27.94 (versus +0.0% over 90 days), next-year $30.14 (-0.2% over 90 days), and current-quarter $6.81 (-0.8% over 90 days). There is no positive revision momentum to support an inflection thesis; estimates are stable, which is consistent with a mature prime growing earnings in the mid-single to low-double digits through buybacks and margin normalization rather than a re-rating catalyst.

The B-21 production-rate acceleration agreement is the most consequential growth and capital item. NOC reached an agreement with the USAF to accelerate the rate and expand capacity, requiring ~$2.5B of company investment over a multi-year period in exchange for "the opportunity to earn improved returns." This shifts B-21 from a loss program (cumulative LRIP losses, with a $1.0B accrual remaining at March 31, 2026) toward an investment program. Whether the NTE lots through unit 40 are negotiated profitably determines whether this investment generates the improved returns management is targeting. Until that pricing is set, the growth contribution is genuinely uncertain: it could be a meaningful margin tailwind in Aeronautics or a continued drag.

## Valuation Assessment

At $563.68 and $29.08 trailing EPS, NOC trades at 19.4x trailing earnings and approximately 18.7x forward consensus ($30.14 next-year EPS, or ~20x on current-year $27.94). The dividend yield is ~1.75%.

Against the peer set, NOC looks inexpensive: the aerospace and defense peer median P/E is 38.7x. That median is distorted by component suppliers operating on different economics — TransDigm at 38.7x, Howmet at 59.9x, L3Harris at 33.6x — none of which is a clean comparable for a prime. The most relevant peer is General Dynamics at 21.6x, also a diversified prime; NOC trades at roughly a 10% discount to GD. On a like-for-like prime basis, NOC is modestly cheap but not dramatically mispriced.

The case for the discount being excessive: NOC has compressed from a higher multiple at its peak to ~19x now, on a business with sole-source franchises that are essentially non-cancellable. The stock fell while fundamentals held and the dividend was raised. The case against: the discount reflects identifiable concerns rather than irrational selling. The B-21 investment dilutes near-term FCF (the metric defense investors weight most heavily), NTE-lot pricing is unresolved, and the executive-order risk threatens the buyback that supports per-share earnings growth. A prime whose capital-return story is under regulatory threat and whose largest program's economics are unpriced does not warrant a premium multiple.

We assign a valuation score of 6.8. The discount is real and provides some downside support (consensus low target of $603 sits ~7% above the current price), but the multiple is not so compressed that it overrides the offsetting risks. The consensus mean target of $697 (median $691) implies ~24% upside, but these targets predate or only partially reflect the post-earnings decline and the investment commitment; they are anchored to a higher-FCF assumption than the current trajectory supports.

## Competitive Landscape

NOC's moat is concentrated in the segments with the highest barriers: strategic deterrence (B-21, Sentinel), restricted and classified programs (a large and growing share of backlog), and advanced microelectronics. As sole-source provider of the next-generation bomber and the land-based ICBM, NOC holds positions that competitors cannot realistically contest within the relevant program timelines. The threat environment and OBBBA funding reinforce demand for exactly these capabilities.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The FY2025 10-K explicitly flags commercial and new entrants — SpaceX, Anduril, Palantir — taking share in space and software-defined defense via OTA and non-FAR procurement vehicles. This is a structural risk in Space Systems (already shrinking 8%) and in software-intensive mission areas, where the traditional prime cost structure and procurement model are disadvantaged versus agile commercial entrants. NOC's response of pruning lower-value businesses (the $333M Immersive Mission Solutions divestiture in May 2025) and concentrating on franchise programs is sensible but does not fully insulate the company from the share shift in space.

Net, the competitive position is stable-to-improving in deterrence and microelectronics and stable-to-eroding in space and software. We score competitive position 7.0, weighted toward the durable franchise positions that anchor the backlog.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the central reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY. The company-specific risks are material and several are unbounded.

**B-21 fixed-price production risk.** The five LRIP lots are largely fixed-price. NOC took $1.56B in Q4 2023 and $477M in Q1 2025; the remaining loss accrual was $1.0B at March 31, 2026. The new production-rate agreement requires ~$2.5B of company investment, and NTE-lot pricing through unit 40 is not fully negotiated. This is the single largest swing factor for the equity in either direction.

**Sentinel ICBM restructure.** The program breached Nunn-McCurdy cost thresholds in January 2024, was certified to continue in July 2024 but ordered restructured, and must re-accomplish Milestone B. Production and deployment phases are unpriced, leaving multi-decade execution risk on a major Defense Systems program. NOC recorded a small favorable $76M EAC adjustment on the program in Q2 2025.

**DOJ criminal pension-CAS investigation.** NOC received a DOJ criminal subpoena (December 2022) and civil investigative demand (February 2023) regarding pension interest-rate assumptions used to set CAS expense for 2013-2019, followed by a DCMA interim noncompliance determination (February 2024). Management explicitly states the outcome "could have a material adverse effect" and cannot estimate a loss range. An unbounded criminal exposure that does not appear in any quantitative screen is a genuine tail risk.

**Capital-return executive-order risk.** The FY2025 filing adds emphasis to a recent executive order targeting defense-contractor underperformance and compensation that could, under certain conditions, prohibit share repurchases or dividends. This directly threatens the per-share earnings-growth mechanism that has supported the stock.

**Budget and appropriations uncertainty.** A government shutdown ran October 1 to November 12, 2025; FY2026 appropriations (~$859B defense) were enacted February 3, 2026, but a partial (DHS) shutdown was ongoing at the 10-Q date and the funding environment remains episodic. The OBBBA's $150B supplemental partially offsets this.

**Execution hazards.** The Q1 2026 GEM 63XL solid rocket motor launch anomaly triggered a $71M unfavorable EAC, a reminder of propulsion-program execution risk.

We score Risk Factors 5.0 — below neutral, reflecting that the B-21 NTE outcome, the DOJ probe, and the EO overhang are all unresolved and the first two are genuinely difficult to bound.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 31.8% sits 6.0pp above 1-year historical volatility of 25.8% (rich), and the term structure is in backwardation (31.8% near-term versus 28.6% at six months), which indicates the market expects a near-term event to move the stock more than the longer horizon. Put/call skew of 3.9% shows elevated put demand, and the put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.62 to 1.04 in recent days. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.4%. This positioning is consistent with the unresolved overhangs and reinforces the view that the market is hedging downside rather than positioning for a recovery. It supports the assessment that near-term risk is two-sided and weighted toward caution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

NOC is a sole-source franchise holder in two non-cancellable nuclear modernization programs, sitting on its backlog and trading at ~19x earnings, a discount to its prime peer GD and well below the broader A&D median. The drawdown from peak is sentiment-driven; fundamentals held through Q1 and the dividend was raised. The B-21 production agreement signals USAF demand and de-risks the bomber program operationally; if NTE lots are priced to "improved returns," Aeronautics margin (currently depressed to 6.3%) recovers toward the corporate average, adding several hundred million dollars of annual operating income. OBBBA's supplemental drives munitions, missile defense, and deterrence demand directly into NOC's franchises. Continued buybacks at a compressed multiple compound per-share earnings. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward $700, in line with consensus mean.

### Bear Case

The B-21 investment depresses FCF precisely when the balance sheet (Z-score 1.88, D/E 2.08, 34%-goodwill assets) has limited flexibility. NTE lots through unit 40 are negotiated at thin or negative margins, extending the loss-program era. The DOJ criminal pension-CAS probe produces a material settlement or charge. The executive order restricts buybacks or dividends, removing the support that has anchored the stock and the per-share growth story. Space Systems continues to lose share to commercial entrants. In this scenario the multiple compresses toward 15x on a flat-to-lower EPS base, taking the stock toward $470 (the 52-week low).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD; the offsetting forces are too balanced to support a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- B-21 NTE-lot pricing (units up to 40) negotiated at terms that NOC and analysts confirm support margin recovery in Aeronautics, with estimate revisions turning positive (+5% or more over 90 days).
- Resolution of the DOJ pension-CAS investigation with no material adverse charge, or a clearly bounded, immaterial settlement.
- Clarification that the capital-return executive order does not restrict NOC's buyback/dividend program, removing the overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material additional B-21 loss provision (beyond the existing $1.0B accrual) or NTE lots priced at a loss.
- An executive-order action that prohibits or materially restricts share repurchases or dividends.
- A DOJ pension-CAS resolution implying a charge or penalty large enough to affect the balance sheet (the company's inability to bound this is itself the concern).
- FCF guidance cut materially below the ~$3.3B 2025 level on a sustained (not seasonal) basis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026), which should provide updated B-21 NTE commentary and an interim read on the investment cadence. The near-term options backwardation points to a catalyst around the mid-June expiry; monitor for any DOJ or executive-order developments.

## Conclusion

Northrop Grumman is a high-quality defense prime trading at a defensible discount after a drawdown that reflects capital-allocation and regulatory concerns rather than operational deterioration. The backlog and sole-source B-21/Sentinel franchises provide durable demand, and the ~19x multiple offers some downside support, with the consensus low target ~7% above the current price.

The reason this is not a BUY is the combination of three things: estimate revisions are flat rather than rising (no inflection signal), there is no insider buying and CEO/officer programmatic selling occurred near the $720-745 highs, and the largest near-term FCF swing factor (B-21 NTE economics) plus two unbounded overhangs (the DOJ criminal pension probe and the capital-return executive order) remain unresolved. The valuation discount is matched by quantifiable and unquantifiable risks the market is correctly discounting. We rate NOC a HOLD and would revisit on clarity around B-21 NTE pricing, the DOJ probe, or the buyback overhang.
