**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Haemonetics is a global medical technology company mid-way through a deliberate portfolio pivot away from concentrated, commoditized blood-collection legacy revenue toward higher-margin Hospital device franchises. The mechanics of that pivot are visible in the FY2026 numbers: gross margin reached a record 59% (up from 55% in FY25 and 52.6% in FY23), operating cash flow rose to $293M, and free cash flow of ~$260M represents a 19.5% FCF margin on a light-capex, disposables-driven model. The problem is that the revenue base is not growing — FY26 revenue fell 2.0% to $1,334M and GAAP net income dropped 42% to $97M ($2.05 diluted EPS), the latter largely on an $86.5M intangible impairment.

The stock has round-tripped violently over the past year: an August 2025 gap-down from ~$76 to ~$56 on the CSL Plasma transition news, a slide to a $47.32 low in late September, a sharp November recovery to an $87.32 high on strong quarterly results, and a fresh drawdown to ~$52 in May 2026 before recovering to the current $77.86. That volatility reflects a genuine debate the market has not resolved: whether the FY26 revenue air-pocket was a one-time reset (CSL insourcing plus the Whole Blood divestiture removed ~$153M of revenue that is now fully out of the base) or the leading edge of structural erosion in both plasma demand (FcRn therapeutics) and the Hospital growth engine (pulsed-field ablation disrupting the ensoETM esophageal-cooling franchise).

We rate HAE a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull case — a clean FY27 comp, record margins, aggressive buybacks, and a BofA upgrade citing conservative guidance — is credible but rests entirely on Interventional Technologies re-accelerating, and that segment fell 8.2% in FY26 with only 6% goodwill headroom. Forward estimates are being cut across every period, which is the wrong direction for a story that needs re-acceleration. At roughly 15x forward adjusted EPS the risk/reward is balanced, not asymmetric. We want to see IT revenue stabilize and estimate revisions turn before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Haemonetics operates three reporting segments (fiscal year ends the Saturday closest to March 31; FY2026 ended March 28, 2026):

- **Plasma (~$524M, ~39% of FY26 revenue):** Collection devices, disposables, and donor-management software sold to biopharmaceutical fractionators who convert source plasma into therapies, principally immunoglobulin. The core platform is NexSys PCS with Persona/YES technology. The customer base is highly concentrated — a handful of large fractionators (CSL, Grifols, Takeda, Octapharma) dominate.
- **Blood Center (~$221M, ~17%):** Automated apheresis blood component collection. A structurally declining, competitive, mature-market business that management explicitly de-prioritizes; the Whole Blood line was divested to GVS in January 2025.
- **Hospital (~$588M, ~44%):** The intended growth engine, split into Interventional Technologies (VASCADE vascular closure, SavvyWire/OptoWire sensor-guided tech from OpSens, ensoETM esophageal protection from Attune, and the new PerQseal large-bore closure from Vivasure) and Blood Management Technologies (TEG hemostasis analyzers, Cell Saver cell salvage, and transfusion-management software).

The strategic thesis is a portfolio migration executed through M&A (OpSens 2023, Attune 2024, Vivasure 2026) and divestiture (Whole Blood 2025). The margin story is working; the revenue story is not yet.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year trend frames the transition clearly:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,169M | $1,309M | $1,361M | $1,334M (−2.0%) |
| Gross margin | 52.6% | 52.8% | 55.0% | 59.0% |
| Operating income | $156M | $165M | $222M | $157M (−29%) |
| Net income | $115M | $118M | $168M | $97M (−42%) |
| GAAP diluted EPS | — | — | $3.31 | $2.05 |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $182M | $293M |
| FCF | — | — | — | ~$260M |

Three points matter for interpreting these numbers.

First, the FY26 revenue decline is largely deliberate and non-recurring. Management expects zero North America disposable sales to CSL Plasma in FY26, so that headwind is now fully in the base. On a normalized organic ex-CSL basis, Q4 grew ~9% and FY26 adjusted EPS was $4.96, up 9% year-over-year. The gap between $2.05 GAAP and $4.96 adjusted EPS is dominated by the impairment ($77.2M on Attune plus HemoAssay/viscoelastic IP), not by cash operating deterioration.

Second, cash-flow quality is genuinely strong. OCF comfortably exceeds net income, FCF conversion is well above 100% of GAAP net income, and capex is light (~$33M). This is a razor/blade model with a high recurring-disposables component (plus ~3% of revenue from operating leases of Haemonetics-owned equipment placed at customer sites). Cash generation is durable even through the revenue air-pocket.

Third, the balance sheet screens poorly but is not distressed. Total debt is ~$1.22B, dominated by $700M of 2.5% convertible 2029 notes (conversion price ~$117, deeply out of the money), a ~$239M term loan at 5.6–5.8%, and $300M drawn on the revolver in March 2026 to repay the maturing $300M 2026 notes at par (no conversions). Book D/E is ~3.0x, which is why an Altman Z-Score of 1.71 flags "distress." That reading is misleading: the company is in compliance with all covenants (net leverage cap 4.0x, temporary 4.5x for acquisitions), retains ~$449M of revolver availability, runs a ~3.0x current ratio, and produces ~$260M of FCF. The equity cushion is genuinely thin and leverage limits flexibility if Hospital falters, but this is not a solvency situation.

Capital allocation has been aggressive on buybacks: ~$175M repurchased in FY26 via multiple ASRs at $52–69/share, with $325M remaining on the $500M 2025 authorization. Share count fell from ~50.7M to ~47.2M. No dividend. The buybacks were executed near the lows, which is disciplined; the M&A track record is more mixed (see below).

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth signal is negative, and the estimate revisions confirm it. Consensus is falling across every forward period: current-quarter EPS was revised to $1.08 from $1.16 90 days ago (−7.5%), next-quarter to $1.27 from $1.32 (−3.9%), current-year to $5.22 from $5.29 (−1.4%), and next-year to $5.63 from $5.91 (−4.8%, and −2.3% in just the last 30 days). This negative earnings momentum is the single most important reason we do not apply the strong-buy inflection framework here — there is no +30% revision velocity to reward; the revisions run the wrong way.

The forward growth case therefore rests on the composition beneath the flat top line:

- **Blood Management Technologies** (TEG hemostasis, Cell Saver, transfusion-management software) grew +14.6% in FY26 and is the genuine bright spot. TEG is the standard of care in liver transplant with a durable installed base.
- **Plasma ex-CSL** is stable-to-growing, underpinned by aging demographics and rising immunoglobulin demand, though structurally concentrated.
- **Interventional Technologies** fell 8.2% in FY26 and 11.4% in Q3. This is the segment that was supposed to drive growth. Two pressures: pulsed-field ablation (PFA) is tissue-selective and spares the esophagus, which management concedes has "substantially diminished" the esophageal-cooling opportunity that justified the Attune deal; and VASCADE vascular-closure volume has been soft against hospital value-analysis-committee pricing scrutiny.

The bull case for FY27 is a "worst is behind us" reset: CSL fully out of the base, Whole Blood divested, restructuring underway, record gross margin, and an easier comp. That is plausible on the margin and revenue-line arithmetic. The swing factor is whether VASCADE plus a Vivasure/PerQseal ramp can re-accelerate IT to offset ongoing PFA-driven ensoETM losses, and PerQseal Elite is still pending FDA PMA approval in the U.S. Given growth prospects are the most reliable winner-selection dimension in our framework, the flat-to-declining top line and falling estimates keep this score below the midpoint of our range rather than above it.

## Valuation Assessment

At $77.86 the stock trades at ~15x current-year adjusted EPS consensus of $5.22, or ~38x trailing GAAP EPS of $2.05. The GAAP multiple is not the right anchor because trailing GAAP is depressed by the non-cash impairment; the adjusted-EPS multiple is the fair lens. On that basis HAE is neither expensive nor cheap.

The peer table in Health Care Supplies shows wide dispersion — ICUI at 77x, NVST at 60x, MMSI at 29x, with XRAY and NEOG at negative (loss-making) multiples and a stated peer-median P/E of 60.1x. That median is inflated by loss-makers and premium-multiple names and is not a clean comparable for HAE's low-single-digit-growth profile. On growth-adjusted terms, ~15x forward for a company delivering ~9% adjusted EPS growth but flat-to-declining reported revenue is a full-but-not-stretched multiple. The absence of a dividend removes yield support.

We are explicit about not rewarding the low trailing GAAP multiple as a value signal — a compressed multiple here reflects genuine uncertainty (IT deceleration, goodwill-impairment risk, plasma concentration), not a mispricing the market has overlooked. Cheapness on trailing metrics has historically been a poor buy signal in this framework, and it is particularly hollow when the compression sits on impaired earnings. The analyst consensus (mean $86.6, median $88.5, high $104, low $62) implies modest upside, but sentiment is degrading — one firm moved from buy to hold in the last cycle — so we treat the targets as bracketing rather than confirming an edge. The valuation score sits below the midpoint: the multiple is fair, but there is no identifiable catalyst forcing a re-rating and the falling estimates argue against paying up.

## Competitive Landscape

Haemonetics holds a leadership position in plasma collection technology, protected by high switching costs and installed-base economics, and it is actively defending that moat as the plaintiff in plasma-patent suits against Terumo BCT (Colorado) and Fresenius Kabi/Fenwal (Illinois) over NexSys PCS with YES/Persona. The offensive litigation posture signals both a real competitive threat (encroachment by two credible rivals) and management's willingness to protect the franchise. The OpSens/Philips sensor-guidewire suit was settled at immaterial cost.

The trajectory is mixed. Blood Center is in secular decline and management is harvesting it. Blood Management Technologies (TEG, transfusion software) is gaining. But Interventional Technologies — the segment where competitive positioning was supposed to improve — is where the moat is most exposed. PFA adoption is a genuine technology shock to the esophageal-cooling thesis, and it materialized fast enough to force a $77M write-down roughly 24 months after Haemonetics paid $176M for Attune. Vascular closure faces physician-preference-device pricing pressure. On balance, competitive position is stable rather than improving: durable in plasma and blood management, deteriorating in the very franchise that was meant to be the growth story. Given this dimension has shown near-zero recent predictive power in our framework, we weight it lightly and score it at the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific and material, and several are not fully priced:

1. **Plasma customer concentration.** One plasma customer is ~13% of total revenue; the top 10 are ~44%. The company just lived this risk with CSL insourcing. Any further loss or repricing among the handful of fractionators would be highly material and is the single largest near-term risk.

2. **FcRn therapeutics as a demand-side threat.** The 10-K explicitly names FcRn-targeted therapies as an alternative to plasma-derived immunoglobulin. If subcutaneous/monoclonal alternatives erode Ig demand, the core plasma collection franchise could face structural volume decline. This is the long-term bear case and it is difficult to quantify.

3. **Interventional Technologies goodwill impairment risk.** MD&A discloses the IT reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only 6% ($867.8M vs $821.6M). A 50bp discount-rate increase or a 1% cut to projected revenue growth could eliminate headroom. With IT revenue down 8%, this is a live GAAP overhang for the next annual test.

4. **M&A execution.** The Attune write-down is a real capital-allocation blemish and raises the bar on the fresh Vivasure deal (~$116M upfront plus up to ~$99M contingent), whose flagship PerQseal Elite is still pre-FDA-approval in the U.S. — the same early-stage, single-product profile that went wrong with Attune.

5. **Convertible/refinancing and tariff/supply-chain exposure.** The 2029 converts and revolver draw create refinancing and potential dilution risk, though near-term maturities are light and the converts are far out of the money. Manufacturing in Mexico (Guaymas/Tetakawi) and Asian component sourcing were flagged as tariff pressure on SG&A in FY26.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 59.9% is rich to 1-year historical volatility of 53.8%, sits at the 80th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (59.9% near-term vs 53.0% at six months). The market is pricing an implied ±14.8% move on the nearest expiry, consistent with the next earnings print being hedged as an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced near-term (+0.3%) but turns negative at the 3-month and 6-month tenors (−5.0% and −5.7%), indicating some downside protection demand further out. ATM IV has jumped from 40.6% to 59.9% since the prior snapshot as the stock rose 32.3%, so the elevated vol reflects a stock that has moved sharply and now faces an event, not a directional bearish tell. Short interest is moderate at 8.7% of float with only 2.8 days to cover — no meaningful squeeze dynamic in either direction. The rich near-dated IV argues against paying up ahead of the print and favors patience on entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$95–104):** The FY26 revenue trough is fully in the base and FY27 delivers clean organic growth on an easy comp. Gross margin holds at a record 59%, Blood Management Technologies compounds mid-teens, and Interventional Technologies stabilizes as VASCADE recovers and PerQseal clears FDA to reinvigorate the large-bore closure opportunity. Adjusted EPS grows toward the ~$5.63 next-year consensus, the market pays ~17x on a re-accelerating story, and buybacks accrete per-share value. BofA's upgrade to Buy rests on this thesis and on FY27 guidance it considers conservative. This maps to the analyst high of $104.

**Bear case (~$52–58, roughly −25% to −33% from current):** FcRn therapeutics and PFA prove to be the leading edge of structural erosion, not one-offs. IT revenue keeps declining, forcing another goodwill impairment against the 6% headroom. A further plasma-customer loss or repricing among the concentrated fractionators hits the core. Falling estimates continue, the adjusted-EPS growth narrative breaks, and the stock retests the May 2026 low near $52. This maps to the analyst low of $62 and below.

The current setup is genuinely balanced. The margin transformation and cash generation are real; the revenue re-acceleration is unproven and the near-term data (IT −8%, estimates falling across every period) points the wrong way.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q1 FY27 earnings report (typically early August), which will show the first clean, CSL-free organic growth print and IT revenue trajectory. The annual goodwill test and any impairment disclosure are the second key input.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Interventional Technologies revenue returns to positive year-over-year growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming VASCADE stabilization and Vivasure contribution.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive over a 30–90 day window (currently −4.8% next-year over 90 days).
- FDA PMA approval of PerQseal Elite for the U.S. arterial indication, de-risking the Vivasure bet.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second material goodwill impairment against the IT reporting unit, confirming the 8% revenue decline was structural rather than transitional.
- Loss or adverse repricing of another top plasma fractionator, or explicit evidence that FcRn therapies are cutting immunoglobulin-driven plasma collection volumes.
- Adjusted EPS growth turning negative or guidance cut, breaking the "worst is behind us" reset.

## Conclusion

Haemonetics is a durable, high-FCF medical technology franchise executing a real margin transformation while its revenue base sits in an air-pocket that management argues is now behind it. The evidence is genuinely two-sided: the CSL headwind is fully absorbed and Blood Management Technologies is compounding, but Interventional Technologies — the intended growth engine — fell 8% with only 6% goodwill headroom, the Attune impairment exposes M&A discipline, plasma concentration and FcRn therapeutics remain live structural threats, and forward estimates are being cut across every period. At ~15x forward adjusted EPS the stock is fairly valued for a low-single-digit organic grower with unresolved segment risk, not a bargain.

We rate HAE a HOLD. The score has drifted lower across recent reports (5.8 in March to 4.9 in June), and the recovery in price back to $77.86 has come without a corresponding turn in the underlying estimate trend. We are marginally more constructive than the prior report given the record margin and clean FY27 comp arithmetic, but the falling estimates and unproven IT re-acceleration keep us on the sidelines. This is an uncertain hold: we need to see IT revenue stabilize and consensus revisions turn positive before the risk/reward becomes asymmetric enough to buy, and we would step aside on evidence of a second impairment or further plasma-customer erosion.
