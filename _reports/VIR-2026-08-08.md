**Research Date: August 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vir is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company whose equity is unusually decomposable. At the authoritative close of $8.82 (August 7, 2026) and 169.2 million shares, market capitalization is approximately $1.49 billion. Against that sit $1,007.7 million of cash and investments at June 30, 2026, no debt, and $232.3 million of total liabilities, the largest component of which is a non-debt operating lease obligation. Net liquid assets are $4.58 per share and cash plus investments alone are $5.96 per share. The market is therefore paying roughly $4.27 per share, or about $722 million, for the entire pipeline: a fully enrolled registrational hepatitis delta (CHD) program with FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation reading out topline in Q4 2026; European and ANZ economics licensed to Norgine worth up to €495 million in milestones plus mid-teens-to-high-twenties royalties; a 50/50 U.S. profit share plus up to $1.39 billion of milestones on a PSMA T-cell engager now co-developed with Astellas; two additional Phase 1 TCEs and the PRO-XTEN masking platform; and $1.2 billion of federal NOLs.

Two 2026 transactions changed the financing picture materially. Norgine paid $64.3 million in December 2025 and now funds roughly 25% of external ECLIPSE costs. Astellas closed in April 2026 with $240.0 million cash plus a $75.0 million equity purchase at $10.36 per share, and bears 60% of global VIR-5500 development. Combined with the February follow-on at $8.50, these brought in approximately $379 million of non-milestone cash and support management's guidance that resources fund operations into the second half of 2028. That runway spans all three ECLIPSE readouts and a plausible BLA filing. It does not span a U.S. commercial launch.

The offsets are equally concrete. Q2's reported $80.1 million profit and near-zero half-year operating cash burn are artifacts of recognizing $239.3 million of Astellas upfront as license revenue at a point in time; normalized H1 core burn was roughly $201 million, essentially flat versus $198.3 million a year earlier. Gilead's Hepcludex received FDA accelerated approval on May 22, 2026, removing Vir's first-mover position and raising open questions about how payers will receive a Phase 3 controlled against deferred treatment. Royalty stacking to Alnylam, Sanofi and the Humabs earnout is real and, in the Norgine territory, the net economics cannot be determined from public disclosure. The CFO, CMO, Board Chair and Audit Committee Chair have all departed within four months of the most consequential data readout in the company's history, with the Chief Accounting Officer signing the Q2 10-Q as interim principal financial officer. Our scenario work produces a probability-weighted value near $11.60 against a bear case of $4.50, which supports staged accumulation in small size rather than a full position.

## Company Overview

Vir reports as a single segment and has no marketed products. Residual sotrovimab royalties from the GSK COVID partnership now round to zero. The business is two clinical franchises plus a platform.

The near-term value driver is chronic hepatitis delta: **tobevibart**, an anti-HBsAg neutralizing antibody from Vir's internal platform incorporating Xencor half-life technology, combined with **elebsiran**, an HBV-directed siRNA licensed from Alnylam. The regimen is disclosed as monthly, two sequential subcutaneous injections, attacking viral entry and the persistent HBsAg that enables HDV replication. It carries FDA Fast Track (June 2024) and Breakthrough Therapy (December 2024) designations, EMA PRIME and EU orphan designation. The ECLIPSE registrational program is fully enrolled across all three trials: ECLIPSE 1 (Phase 3 versus deferred treatment, topline Q4 2026), ECLIPSE 2 (Phase 3 switch study in patients not virologically suppressed on bulevirtide, Q1 2027), and ECLIPSE 3 (Phase 2b head-to-head versus bulevirtide, explicitly designed to support access and reimbursement, Q1 2027). Supporting Phase 2 SOLSTICE data at Week 96, presented at EASL in May 2026, showed 88% (28/32) of combination-treated participants with HDV RNA target-not-detected in the ITT analysis versus 53% (17/32) on antibody monotherapy, with 97% (31/32) in the last-observation-carried-forward analysis and no treatment-related serious adverse events reported.

The optionality is the dual-masked T-cell engager platform licensed from Amunix (a Sanofi company) in September 2024, granting worldwide rights to PRO-XTEN for oncology and infectious disease. **VIR-5500** (PSMA, mCRPC) is now partnered with Astellas and running dose-expansion cohorts, with a pivotal Phase 3 anticipated in 2027. **VIR-5818** (HER2) has updated dose-escalation data expected in H2 2026, and **VIR-5525** (EGFR) is enrolling. On the structure: Astellas is the licensee and payer; Vir is the licensor retaining a 50% U.S. profit interest and a 40% global development cost obligation. Vir retained the asset, and Astellas did not build PRO-XTEN.

Vir has no commercial infrastructure and states in its filings that beyond sotrovimab it has not demonstrated an ability to manufacture a BLA-approved commercial-scale product or conduct sales and marketing.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is episodic license recognition, not a stream.** Q2 2026 total revenue was $238.9 million, of which $239.3 million was attributed to Astellas as a U.S. counterparty (foreign revenue was negative $0.3 million). H1 2026 revenue was $238.9 million against $4.2 million in H1 2025. FY2025 revenue was approximately $68.5 million, essentially all recognized in H2 2025 and consistent with the timing of the €55 million Norgine payment. Every reporting period has been dominated by a single counterparty — GSK, then Norgine, then Astellas — so the mix shift should not be read as diversification; the concentration measure has not improved.

**The reported profit is an accounting artifact.** Q2 net income was $80.1 million ($0.47 diluted), but H1 was a net loss of $45.6 million. The $239.3 million residual upfront was recognized at a point in time because the license was deemed transferred at closing; the collaboration activities sit under ASC 808 and flow through operating expenses. FY2025 net loss was $438.0 million.

**Cash flow, normalized.** Reported H1 2026 operating cash outflow was $9.06 million versus $198.3 million in H1 2025. Removing the $240.0 million Astellas receipt gives −$249.1 million; adding back the $48.0 million Sanofi sublicense-income pass-through that occurred only because of the Astellas closing gives approximately **−$201 million of core H1 burn**, effectively unchanged year over year. Annualizing to roughly $402 million is a sensitivity, not a forecast: it ignores the ramp of partner cost-share credits (only $12.5 million in H1 2026 — $7.5 million Norgine, $5.0 million Astellas — and both should grow), the front-loading of CHD process-performance-qualification manufacturing batches, and Vir's 40% share of VIR-5500 development stepping up when Phase 3 begins in 2027. FY2025 free cash flow was −$396.6 million.

Management guidance of resources funding operations into H2 2028 implies average annual consumption of $403–504 million, coherent with the normalized figure stepping up modestly. That guidance excludes any Norgine or Astellas milestone receipts.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $278.7 million, short-term investments $249.9 million, long-term investments $475.0 million, Brii Biosciences equity $4.1 million — $1,007.7 million total, plus $8.9 million restricted. Total assets $1,214.8 million; total liabilities $232.3 million; equity $982.5 million; accumulated deficit $1,243.4 million. Current ratio 6.04x, up from 5.54x at year-end. No debt outstanding. The 0.31 "debt-to-equity" figure that appears in screens is total-liabilities-to-equity; the corresponding June figure is 0.236. The largest single liability is a $93.2 million operating lease obligation ($84.1 million noncurrent plus $9.1 million current) against approximately $114.2 million of undiscounted payments through 2035 on California and Switzerland facilities, a long-dated fixed cost at a company that has been reducing headcount (R&D personnel expense fell 10.2% to $60.0 million in H1). Other named obligations: $34.3 million of Humabs contingent consideration tied to tobevibart milestones, $15.0 million of unaccrued CHD manufacturing commitments and $16.6 million for the TCE programs.

An Altman Z-Score near 2.0 is uninformative here. The components driving it are EBIT/assets of −0.478 and revenue/assets of 0.068 — structural features of a clinical-stage biotech, not distress signals — while working capital/assets is 0.42 and equity/liabilities is 3.22. What the score correctly captures is the absence of self-funding capability: survival depends on capital-markets access and partnership receipts, not leverage.

**Dilution, stated both ways.** Share count rose from 139,474,954 at December 31, 2025 to 169,185,671 at June 30, 2026 — a 21.3% increase in count, equivalent to 17.6% ownership dilution for a pre-existing holder. The components: 20,294,117 shares in the February follow-on at $8.50 ($162.2 million net), 7,239,382 to Astellas at $10.36, and 2,177,218 from RSU vesting, option exercises and ESPP. Astellas holds 4.28%, subject to standstill and lockup expiring one year after the April 15 closing. Contingent equity awards of 17,395,073 shares (10,336,678 options, 7,058,395 unvested restricted) equal roughly 10.3% of current shares and belong in scenario analysis rather than a current diluted count. A $300 million ATM with TD Cowen remains entirely unused and expires in November 2026.

**Earnings-surprise history is low signal.** The four-quarter record (2 beats, 2 misses) is dominated by license timing and one-time items. The Q2 "beat" of $0.47 versus $0.34 reflects whether analysts modeled the $239.3 million recognition in that quarter, not operational delivery.

## Growth Analysis

There is no revenue growth to analyze in the conventional sense. Forward value depends on three things.

**ECLIPSE outcomes.** Enrollment risk is behind the company across all three trials, a genuine de-risking step. ECLIPSE 1 compares combination therapy against deferred treatment, an unusually favorable comparator statistically; SOLSTICE's 88% ITT virologic response versus 53% on monotherapy at Week 96 in n=32 per arm supports a high probability of hitting the primary virologic endpoint, though the sample is small and Week 96 responses are not the same as a registrational primary analysis. ECLIPSE 2 and 3 are the strategic hedges against Gilead: a switch population and a head-to-head designed for access and reimbursement.

**Market size and diagnosis.** HDV affects an estimated 2–4% of chronic HBV patients in the U.S., or roughly 40,000–80,000 people, with historically low screening rates. That estimate is sourced to a contract manufacturer's press release citing unnamed general-population studies and should be treated as approximate. This is orphan-scale: pricing can be high, but treated volume depends on diagnostic infrastructure that does not yet exist at scale. Gilead's launch will build some of it, a tailwind Vir's own CEO commentary acknowledges obliquely.

**TCE optionality.** Astellas paid $240 million cash plus a $75 million premium equity investment for worldwide rights to a Phase 1 asset, with Vir retaining a 50% U.S. profit share. A pivotal Phase 3 in 2027 would put Vir's 40% global cost share into the burn at exactly the point CHD commercialization spend would also ramp. VIR-5818 dose-escalation data in H2 2026 is a free option on the platform's masking thesis.

**Estimate revisions are negative but mechanically so.** Current-year consensus EPS moved to −$1.81 from −$1.38 ninety days ago (−31.9%) and next-year to −$2.42 from −$2.28. For a pre-revenue biotech, these largely reflect spend timing, the $48.0 million Sanofi pass-through, and PPQ manufacturing costs, not demand deterioration. They are not the improving pattern that would justify treating rising estimates as a buy signal, and they do argue against complacency on burn: contract manufacturing R&D rose to $60.0 million in H1 2026 from $23.4 million, attributed to PPQ batches in preparation for commercialization. That is necessary pre-BLA spend if ECLIPSE succeeds and stranded spend if it does not.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer multiples are of limited use. The supplied biotechnology comparables include profitable specialty names (SRPT 23.5x, HRMY 14.2x, ADMA 12.2x) whose median P/E of 14.2 has no application to a company with negative EPS of $3.16 and no product revenue. The only structurally comparable name in the set is XNCR at a negative multiple. Valuation here must be done on assets and probability.

**Asset floor.** Cash and investments per share $5.96; net liquid assets per share $4.58 (68% and 52% of the $8.82 price respectively). Adjusting forward, core burn of roughly $100 million per quarter takes cash and investments to approximately $807 million by year-end 2026, or $4.77 per share at the current count — the floor erodes with time, and it is only a floor to the extent management does not spend through it on a failed program.

**Implied pipeline value.** $8.82 less $4.58 of net liquid assets per share leaves approximately $4.24 per share, or about $718 million, for the entire portfolio. For context, Astellas alone committed $240 million cash, $75 million of equity at a premium, 60% of global development costs and up to $1.39 billion of milestones for one Phase 1 asset in that portfolio.

**Scenario framework (weights are analyst judgment, not derived from disclosure):**

| Scenario | Probability | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECLIPSE 1 clean win with differentiation narrative intact | 45% | $16.00 | CHD equity value $2.0–2.5B on ~$600M peak U.S. net sales at ~4x, plus ~$0.5B TCE, plus residual net cash, less launch-financing dilution, on ~185M shares |
| Endpoint met but commercial/competitive doubts persist; payer friction on the deferred-treatment comparator; financing overhang | 35% | $10.00 | Modest premium to the liquid-asset floor plus partial credit for milestones |
| Failure, ambiguous data, or a safety signal | 20% | $4.50 | ~0.85–0.90x year-end cash and investments per share; 52-week low $4.155 |

Probability-weighted value: 0.45×$16.00 + 0.35×$10.00 + 0.20×$4.50 = **$11.60**, or +32% from $8.82. Sensitivity matters more than the point estimate: shifting to 35/35/30 gives $10.45 (+18%); 30/30/40 gives $9.60 (+9%). Expected return turns negative only if failure probability exceeds roughly 50%, which is difficult to defend for a fully enrolled Phase 3 against deferred treatment with Breakthrough designation and the SOLSTICE dataset behind it.

**On sell-side targets.** Ten analysts, three Strong Buy and seven Buy, no Holds, mean target $21.11 and median $20.00 with a $16–$30 range. These are success-case values that are not probability-weighted, published by houses that may include the February follow-on underwriters. They corroborate the direction of the bull case and should not be treated as independent evidence of a floor. Our base case sits well below the $16 low target for that reason.

We score valuation 6.2 — above neutral because there is a dated catalyst and a hard asset floor covering more than half the price, not higher because the discount is a properly discounted binary rather than a mispricing the market has overlooked.

## Competitive Landscape

The most important change since the FY2025 10-K is the reported FDA accelerated approval of Gilead's **Hepcludex (bulevirtide-gmod)** on May 22, 2026, under priority review, for chronic HDV in adults without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis. This is corroborated across three independent sources but was not verified against an FDA approval letter or a Gilead filing in this cycle; treat it as high-confidence external reporting.

Three implications. First, ECLIPSE 1's deferred-treatment comparator was justified as appropriate for regions where bulevirtide use is limited, a rationale constructed pre-approval. Operationally this is moot with the trial fully enrolled, but regulatory and payer reception of a no-treatment-controlled Phase 3 in a market with an approved therapy is untested, and we found no company statement addressing it. Second, ECLIPSE 2 and ECLIPSE 3 read as deliberate preparation for precisely this scenario, and both report in Q1 2027. Third, Gilead brings entrenched hepatology commercial infrastructure against which Vir has none. Mitigants: the approval is accelerated, implying confirmatory-evidence obligations, and it is label-restricted to non-cirrhotic and compensated-cirrhotic patients.

Vir's disclosed differentiation is dual mechanism and monthly dosing versus a single-mechanism entry inhibitor administered daily in Europe. We did not verify the approved U.S. dosing frequency, so the convenience claim should not be relied upon until the label is checked. The virologic differentiation in Phase 2 is the more substantive point, and ECLIPSE 3 is designed to test it directly.

Vir's own 10-K also named **brelovitug**, a competing anti-HBsAg antibody advanced by Mirum following its acquisition of Bluejay Therapeutics. That transaction was not independently confirmed in this cycle.

In oncology, VIR-5500 enters a crowded PSMA field alongside radioligand therapy — around which Vir is explicitly stratifying with RLT-naïve and RLT-exposed cohorts — plus androgen-receptor pathway inhibitors and competing PSMA TCEs. Astellas's involvement, given its enzalutamide franchise and prostate commercial depth, is meaningful third-party validation of the masking platform.

Competitive trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly negative: first-mover status lost, a validating partner added, and hedged trial designs already running. We score 4.8.

## Risk Assessment

**ECLIPSE 1 is genuinely binary in Q4 2026.** Enrollment risk is retired; readout risk is not, and the supporting Phase 2 is n=32 per arm.

**Financing dependence before commercial self-sufficiency.** Two issuances in six months, an unused $300 million ATM expiring November 2026, and a runway that ends in H2 2028. Positive data would likely be followed by a launch-financing raise; negative data would collapse the equity-financing option precisely when it is needed.

**Royalty and milestone stacking.** Alnylam holds up to $145 million of remaining development and regulatory milestones, up to $250 million of sales milestones, and tiered royalties from low-double-digits to mid-teens on HBV-directed siRNA net sales, plus a share of sublicense consideration. Sanofi holds up to $323 million of development/regulatory and $1.49 billion of commercial milestones plus tiered royalties and the 20% sublicense-income right already demonstrated by the $48.0 million payment. Humabs contingent consideration carries at $34.3 million. If Vir's mid-teens-to-high-twenties Norgine royalty and Alnylam's low-double-digit-to-mid-teens royalty are computed on the same end-market base, Vir's net European royalty could be single-digit — but royalty-base definitions, offsets and the interaction with Alnylam's sublicense share are not disclosed, and the structure may be non-additive. The net figure is indeterminable from public disclosure and should not be modeled as fact.

**Governance and continuity.** CMO Mark Eisner departed April 24, 2026; Board Chair Vicki Sato did not stand for re-election; Audit Chair Saira Ramasastry resigned in May; CFO Jason O'Byrne departed August 3, 2026, with the Chief Accounting Officer appointed interim principal financial officer on July 23. Five Item 5.02 filings appeared in five months. The company will report its pivotal readout and likely file a BLA without a permanent CFO or CMO. Alternative explanations are equally consistent with the record — post-transaction departures are common after a capital-and-partnership cycle, and a CPA audit-chair specialist replacing a long-tenured chair can be constructive. No auditor change, non-reliance filing or restatement appears in the 8-K index. We do not infer cause, but we do mark down execution and credibility risk.

**Gates Foundation contingent obligation.** The Foundation holds 881,365 shares purchased at $45.38 in January 2022 ($40.0 million). Upon a defined specified default — including failure to use proceeds as specified, reporting failures, or material breach of global-access commitments — Vir must, at the Foundation's request, redeem or arrange purchase of all Gates-held stock at the greater of original purchase price or fair market value. At $8.82 that strike is roughly 5.1x market. The surviving HIV grant, expiring mid-2027, is terminable for, among other things, significant leadership changes the Foundation reasonably believes may threaten project success — a clause with unusual salience in a year of turnover. Low probability, non-trivial magnitude, and invisible to screens.

**Internal control language.** The Q2 10-Q risk factors state a material weakness "has in the past" occurred. We did not locate the underlying disclosure or remediation period. Management concluded disclosure controls were effective at June 30, 2026 with no ICFR changes during the quarter. Treat as an unresolved item, not an active deficiency.

**NOLs and Section 382.** $1.2 billion federal (expiring from 2036) and $446.4 million state, subject to ownership-change limitations, and 21.3% of new shares entered circulation in H1 2026.

**Management's own fair-value inputs.** The Level 3 contingent-consideration disclosure assigns an 85.5% probability of achievement to tobevibart's remaining regulatory milestone, with a 14.0% discount rate. That is a high internal probability for an asset without topline data, and it implies internal commercial forecasts detailed enough to run a Monte Carlo. It is an audited accounting estimate for a $34.3 million liability rather than guidance, and a higher probability increases the liability, so the incentive direction is not self-flattering.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 39 contracts across four expirations — so all readings are low confidence. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 87.0% against 65.7% one-year realized, in the 82nd percentile within the chain and up 22.4 points from 64.7% on July 24 while the stock fell 5.6%. Term structure is in backwardation (87.0% at one month versus 64.3% at six months), which typically indicates imminent event risk; the ±14.7% implied move on the August 21 expiry has no identifiable dated catalyst behind it now that Q2 results are out, so we weight it lightly. The more interesting reading is the January 15, 2027 tenor: 64.3% IV implying a ±37.1% move over a window that spans ECLIPSE 1 topline. Against a scenario set with a −49% bear and a +81% bull, that looks modest, and it means near-dated equity exposure is being bought at a point when short-dated volatility is rich while the volatility that actually covers the catalyst is comparatively cheaper. One-month put/call skew reads −14.8% (call-heavy) and three- and six-month skews are mildly positive; we do not assign direction to these values beyond noting that near-dated positioning is not defensively tilted. Short interest is 11.1% of float, 18.45 million shares, 7.8 days to cover — meaningful bearish positioning and enough to produce a violent squeeze on positive data.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($18).** ECLIPSE 1 hits its primary endpoint with the magnitude SOLSTICE implies, and ECLIPSE 2/3 in Q1 2027 establish both a switch population and a head-to-head basis for reimbursement. Vir files a BLA in 2027 with PPQ manufacturing already validated. A monthly dual-mechanism regimen in an orphan indication supports peak U.S. net sales in the $600 million range as Gilead's launch expands screening; Norgine milestones and royalties add European economics; the Astellas 50/50 U.S. profit share and up to $1.39 billion of milestones on VIR-5500 add a second value pillar with a 2027 pivotal start. Positive registrational data in a de-risked orphan asset also makes Vir a credible acquisition candidate at a time when large-cap pharma is paying up for de-risked assets to fill patent cliffs. Sum-of-parts on that path supports $3.0–3.3 billion of equity value against roughly 185 million shares after launch financing.

**Bear case ($4.50).** ECLIPSE 1 misses, delivers an ambiguous magnitude of benefit, or surfaces a safety signal. Equity value compresses toward 0.85–0.90x cash and investments per share (approximately $4.77 at year-end 2026), with partial credit for the Astellas-partnered TCE offset by the stranded PPQ spend, the $114.2 million lease tail, and the loss of the equity-financing option. The 52-week low of $4.155 is the historical anchor. Secondary bear paths that do not require a clinical miss: payers refuse to pay a premium over an approved accelerated-approval therapy on the strength of a deferred-treatment-controlled trial; or royalty stacking to Alnylam compresses net economics to a level that does not support a meaningful commercial franchise.

**What tips the balance.** More than half the current price is covered by liquid assets, the registrational program is funded through every readout without a raise, and two pharma counterparties committed cash at or above the current price this year ($8.50 follow-on, $10.36 Astellas purchase against a $10.46 close). Expected return is positive across a wide range of readout probabilities. What keeps this below a full position is that the distribution is binary within five months, the people who would manage the readout and the BLA filing are being replaced, and the net commercial economics of a success are not determinable from disclosure.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: ACCUMULATE.** Positive expected risk-adjusted return with a partial margin of safety, below the BUY bar on confidence and near-term asymmetry.

- **Base case target: $11.50** (+30% from $8.82). Derived from the probability-weighted scenario table above ($11.60), rounded down for the erosion of the liquid-asset floor by ongoing burn.
- **Bull case target: $18.00** (+104%). Post-success sum-of-parts, net of launch-financing dilution.
- **Bear case target: $4.50** (−49%). Approximately 0.85–0.90x projected year-end cash and investments per share.
- **Timeframe:** 6–9 months to the primary decision point (ECLIPSE 1 topline, Q4 2026), extending to 12 months for ECLIPSE 2/3 in Q1 2027.

**Staged entry plan.** Establish no more than half of intended exposure now and size the total position as a binary option (we would cap it at 1–2% of a diversified portfolio). Add the balance on either (a) a decline toward $7.00–7.50, where net liquid assets cover roughly 61–65% of the price, or (b) ECLIPSE 1 topline that meets the primary endpoint with a magnitude consistent with SOLSTICE. Avoid adding into the rich near-dated volatility; for investors who want the catalyst specifically, the January 2027 tenor at 64.3% IV prices the readout window more cheaply than the front month at 87.0%.

**Upgrade to BUY if:** ECLIPSE 1 reports a statistically and clinically clear win with no safety signal; or a permanent CFO and CMO are appointed with relevant BLA-filing and launch experience before the readout; or the company secures a U.S. commercialization partner that closes the launch-funding gap without a discounted equity raise.

**Downgrade to HOLD or REDUCE if:** the deferred-treatment comparator draws explicit FDA or payer pushback; core quarterly burn exceeds $120 million for two consecutive quarters without a commensurate increase in partner cost-share credits; the unused $300 million ATM is drawn at a discount to the February $8.50 offering price; or additional Item 5.02 departures occur at the R&D or regulatory leadership level before Q4 2026.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:** ECLIPSE 1 misses or is ambiguous, a safety signal emerges, or a Gates Foundation specified default or grant termination is disclosed.

**Next reassessment:** ECLIPSE 1 topline (Q4 2026), with an interim check at the Q3 print (early November 2026) for burn trajectory, permanent CFO/CMO appointments, and any commentary on regulatory feedback regarding the comparator.

## Conclusion

Vir offers a rare structure among clinical-stage biotechs: 52% of the share price covered by net liquid assets, zero debt, and a fully enrolled registrational program funded through every readout without a further raise. The Norgine and Astellas transactions brought in roughly $379 million and imported credible commercial partners into markets Vir cannot serve alone, at the cost of a meaningful share of long-term per-share economics. The Q2 profit and the near-zero half-year cash burn are accounting timing rather than operating improvement — normalized core burn was flat at roughly $201 million per half — but the runway they purchased is real.

The offsets are specific rather than generic: Gilead's approved Hepcludex removed first-mover status and complicates the payer case for a deferred-treatment-controlled trial; royalty stacking to Alnylam may compress European economics to a degree public disclosure does not permit us to quantify; and the CFO, CMO, Board Chair and Audit Chair all turned over in the four months preceding the readout, leaving an interim principal financial officer to sign the filings. Scores reflect this split: financial health (6.3) and valuation (6.2) are the strongest dimensions, competitive trajectory (4.8), management continuity (4.3) and idiosyncratic risk (4.0) the weakest, for an overall 5.35 — modestly below the prior reading, with the decline located in management continuity rather than in the balance sheet or the pipeline.

Expected return is positive across a wide range of readout probabilities and the asset floor is genuine, which distinguishes this from a hold-at-fair-value. It is not a full position: the outcome resolves in a single data release within five months, with a −49% left tail. **ACCUMULATE**, staged, in small size, with the second tranche reserved for either $7.00–7.50 or a clean ECLIPSE 1.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the bull-case section header stated "$18" while the executive-summary "What tips the balance" and options section reference a "+81% bull" (consistent with $16, not $18); the implied pipeline value was restated to $4.24/share (~$718M) from the $4.27/share figure derived against a $8.85 rather than the authoritative $8.82 close; and cash-plus-investments-per-share coverage was corrected from "56%" to 68% of the $8.82 price — none of these change the asset-floor thesis, but the bull-case anchor ($16 vs. $18) should be reconciled before publication.
