**Research Date: August 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Select Medical Holdings ceased to exist as a public company. A consortium led by Executive Chairman and Co-Founder Robert A. Ortenzio, Senior Executive Vice President Martin F. Jackson, and Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe acquired the company for $16.50 per share in cash under a merger agreement dated March 2, 2026; stockholders including unaffiliated holders approved the transaction on June 26, 2026, and the merger became effective July 1, 2026 at 12:01 a.m., at which point the common stock was delisted from the NYSE. This rests on the company's June 30, 2026 press release plus the corroborating pattern that current-period SEC filings, 8-Ks and Form 4 data no longer resolve for the ticker while the February 19, 2026 10-K and March 4, 2026 DEF 14A retrieve normally. A confirming Form 8-K, Form 25 or Form 15 was not obtained, so treat the mechanics as high-confidence but not filing-verified.

The practical consequence governs the rating. The frozen valuation basis for this report is invalid at the provider level (no authoritative Schwab quote, no market timestamp, currency not determinable), with a daily-history close of $16.51 on 2026-08-13 — one cent above the fixed $16.50 cash consideration. A residual quote at or above the merger price on a delisted security is a data artifact, not a tradable market. Buying at $16.51 against $16.50 of already-fixed consideration produces a return of approximately −0.06% before any settlement or transfer friction, with no participation in the private company's subsequent earnings, and with no verified appraisal or contingent-value instrument to underwrite. The label "Class N" in the security description appears to be a vendor artifact; the FY2025 10-K refers to a single class of outstanding common stock with blank-check preferred authorized but unissued.

The fundamental record supports the clearing price rather than a mispricing claim. FY2025 revenue grew 5.1% to $5,452.8M, but company-defined Adjusted EBITDA fell 3.4% to $493.2M, and the 25.3% increase in reported operating income to $336.2M is almost entirely explained by an $82.5M decline in stock compensation expense ($99.2M to $16.7M) following November 2024 restricted-stock modifications tied to the Concentra separation. Operating income excluding stock compensation — Adjusted EBITDA less D&A — fell from $367.5M to $352.9M. Critical illness recovery hospital EBITDA declined 12.0%, outpatient rehabilitation 17.0%. Operating cash flow fell 33% to $346.5M, leaving $117.3M of free cash flow against $221.4M of distributions to shareholders and minority partners. Two subsequent quarters missed consensus badly — Q4 2025 EPS of $0.16 versus $0.23 (−31%) and Q1 2026 EPS of $0.35 versus $0.46 (−24%). The score trajectory from 5.7 in May to 3.2 in late July was directionally correct; the new information this cycle confirms rather than reverses it, and the residual score of 2.75 reflects an extinguished instrument on a deteriorating operating base.

## Company Overview

Select Medical, headquartered in Mechanicsburg, Pennsylvania and operating since 1997, was among the largest U.S. post-acute care operators by facility count. Following the November 2024 separation and distribution of 104,093,503 Concentra shares, it ran three operating segments plus corporate:

| Segment | Footprint (12/31/25) | FY2025 Revenue | % of total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical illness recovery hospitals (LTCHs) | 104 hospitals, 28 states, 4,420 licensed beds | $2,477.8M | ~45% |
| Rehabilitation hospitals (IRFs) | 38 hospitals (26 consolidated), 15 states | $1,289.0M | ~24% |
| Outpatient rehabilitation | 1,917 clinics (1,617 consolidated), 39 states + DC | $1,284.9M | ~24% |
| Other (corporate/shared services) | Mechanicsburg, PA | $401.2M | ~7% |

Total FY2025 revenue was $5,452.8M across roughly 45,300 employees, of whom fewer than 40 were union-represented — a genuine structural cost advantage relative to unionized acute-care systems.

The defining operating feature was the hospital-within-a-hospital model: 70 of 104 critical illness hospitals leased space inside host acute-care hospitals rather than occupying free-standing buildings. This is capital-light and referral-advantaged, and it creates two dependencies invisible in leverage ratios. Each HIH must independently qualify as a hospital separate from its host under Medicare conditions of participation; failure reverts payment to general acute-care IPPS rates materially below LTCH-PPS. And the 10-K states that most critical illness hospitals are leased under terms of less than ten years that "generally cannot be renewed or extended without the written consent of the landlords" — landlords who are frequently the host hospital, simultaneously the referral source, the regulatory co-tenant, and a potential competitor if it opens its own step-down unit. Undiscounted operating lease payments totaled $1,505.2M with $244.6M due within twelve months.

Payor mix in FY2025: commercial 51.6%, Medicare 28.6%, private and other 12.1%, workers' compensation 4.4%, Medicaid 3.3%. Medicare dependence by segment differed sharply — rehabilitation hospitals 45.3%, critical illness 31.8%, outpatient 14.7%.

At March 31, 2026 the footprint was 103 critical illness hospitals, 41 rehabilitation hospitals and 1,912 outpatient clinics, continuing the reallocation toward inpatient rehabilitation visible in the FY2025 capex mix.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 earnings improvement was an accounting artifact.** Reported operating income rose from $268.3M to $336.2M and income from continuing operations from $130.0M to $214.5M. Stock compensation fell $82.5M over the same period. Because Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock compensation, the cleaner read is a 3.4% decline from $510.4M to $493.2M on a $265.7M revenue increase, with margin compressing 79 basis points to 9.0%. Backing out D&A ($142.9M derived for FY2024, $140.3M disclosed for FY2025), operating income excluding stock compensation fell approximately $14.6M.

The pretax bridge ties. FY2024 pretax continuing income of $174.8M rose to $272.7M in FY2025; the $97.9M improvement decomposes into operating income +$67.9M (of which roughly $82.5M is stock-comp normalization), interest expense favorable $10.7M, absence of the prior-year $28.8M debt-retirement loss, and equity in earnings unfavorable $9.4M. The effective tax rate fell to 21.3% from roughly 25.6%, partly attributed in MD&A to reduced executive compensation — the same non-recurring cause.

**Segment detail locates the damage.**

| Segment Adjusted EBITDA ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY25 change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Critical illness | 246.0 | 301.6 | 265.4 | −12.0% |
| Rehabilitation hospitals | 221.9 | 245.7 | 278.6 | +13.4% |
| Outpatient | 111.9 | 108.6 | 90.2 | −17.0% |
| Other | (133.7) | (145.6) | (141.1) | — |

Critical illness admissions rose 1.0% to 36,126 while patient days fell 1.0% to 1,107,387 at a flat 31-day average length of stay; revenue per patient day rose 2.4% to $2,230. So +2.4% price on −1.0% volume produced +1.4% revenue against cost inflation, and EBITDA fell 12%. The mechanism is most plausibly the LTCH high-cost outlier fixed-loss amount, which CMS raised from $59,873 in FY2024 to $77,048 in FY2025 (+28.7%), reducing both eligibility and payment on the most expensive cases. Occupancy of 69% across 104 hospitals indicates meaningful stranded capacity.

Outpatient was worse and cannot be blamed on CMS: visits grew 3.3% to 11.52 million, disclosed revenue per visit fell from $101 to $100, and margin dropped to 7.0% from 9.4% two years earlier, with approximately 83% of segment revenue commercial. This is volume growth at falling price with rising cost per visit in a business whose barriers to entry management itself describes as not substantial.

Rehabilitation hospitals were the genuine growth engine: admissions +9.3%, patient days +8.4%, revenue per patient day +5.9% to $2,260, consolidated hospitals 21 → 23 → 26, at ~21.6% segment EBITDA margins. Occupancy fell from 85% to 82%, consistent with new beds not yet filled. Segment capex was $112.6M versus $53.6M in FY2024 and $21.9M in FY2023.

**Cash flow is the binding constraint.** Operating cash flow fell 33% to $346.5M (MD&A attributes the decline principally to the absence of Concentra's contribution, which is legitimate); capex was $229.2M, leaving $117.3M of free cash flow — a 2.2% FCF margin on $5.45B of revenue. Against that, FY2025 outflows were $100.1M of share repurchases, $89.9M of distributions to and purchases of non-controlling interests, and $31.4M of dividends, totaling $221.4M, roughly 1.9× free cash flow. Funding came from $66.9M of net new borrowings, $23.4M of asset-sale proceeds, $15.9M of NCI issuance, and a $33.2M cash drawdown to a year-end balance of $26.5M. The NCI distributions are largely contractual obligations to health-system joint-venture partners rather than discretionary returns, and $112.6M of the capex was growth spending, so maintenance-basis free cash flow would be higher; neither qualification changes the level of balance-sheet liquidity.

Non-controlling interests absorbed roughly $68.3M of the $214.5M of net income, so net income attributable to Holdings was $146.2M and attributable-basis ROE was approximately 8.6% rather than the 12.6% computed on the consolidated figure. A further $54.5M of pretax income was equity in earnings of unconsolidated subsidiaries — accounting income that reaches the parent only via distribution.

**Leverage was structural but not distressed.** At December 31, 2025: total assets $5,851.6M, goodwill $2,360.9M (40% of assets), stockholders' equity $1,705.6M, current ratio 1.04, net working capital $40.8M, DSO stable at 57 days. Gross debt principal was $1,845.4M (term loan $1,039.5M, revolver drawn $100.0M, 6.250% senior notes due 2032 $550.0M, remainder finance leases and JV borrowings), with only $24.2M due within twelve months and $469.1M of revolver availability. Covenant leverage was 3.67× against a 7.00× maximum; Adjusted-EBITDA interest coverage 4.18×; (Adjusted EBITDA − capex) to interest 2.24×. An Altman Z-Score in the 1.6–1.8 range sits in the distress band, but it is measuring capital-structure architecture — $2.36B of goodwill with no output plus capitalized lease liabilities — rather than default proximity. The accurate characterization is a lease-heavy, structurally levered operator with large covenant headroom and genuinely thin cash buffers, operationally dependent on revolver access.

Post-LBO capital structure is unknown. Wells Fargo Securities and J.P. Morgan acted as joint lead arrangers and bookrunners on committed debt financing, which establishes that leverage was raised; quantum, pricing and pro forma leverage were not obtainable. Do not assume pro forma leverage equals the pre-deal 3.67×.

## Growth Analysis

Consolidated revenue growth decelerated from 7.5% in FY2024 to 5.1% in FY2025, and the composition matters more than the rate. Growth came almost entirely from rehabilitation hospitals (+16.1%), the segment with 45.3% Medicare dependence and direct exposure to CMS's Review Choice Demonstration. Critical illness grew 1.4% on falling patient days. Outpatient grew 2.8% on falling price per visit.

The mix shift away from Medicare (30.8% to 28.6% of revenue over three years) is arithmetic rather than diversification: the fastest-growing segment is the most Medicare-dependent, and the least Medicare-dependent segment is the one where margins collapsed. Commercial exposure is not insulated either — the 10-K notes that reductions in Medicare reimbursement can flow through commercial contracts that follow Medicare methodologies.

Earnings visibility deteriorated through the deal process. The quarterly record shows two beats followed by two sizeable misses: Q2 2025 $0.32 versus $0.25 (+30%), Q3 2025 $0.23 versus $0.15 (+50%), Q4 2025 $0.16 versus $0.23 (−31%), Q1 2026 $0.35 versus $0.46 (−24%). FY2025 EPS of $1.16 was down from $1.66 in FY2024 and $1.91 in FY2023 on the proxy's basis. The Q1 2026 result is the only post-10-K financial data point available and it is negative — relevant because it undercuts the argument that the buyers acquired an accelerating business at a trough price.

For a shareholder, forward growth participation is zero. Whatever operating recovery the private owners engineer — rehabilitation hospital ramp filling the 82% occupancy, expiration of the 4.6% site-neutral per-diem reduction after September 30, 2026, the roughly 2% net CY2026 Medicare therapy increase — accrues to the consortium.

## Valuation Assessment

The relevant valuation exercise is retrospective: what did $16.50 imply, and is there any residual claim worth pricing?

Derived enterprise value: 124.8M shares (from the Q4 2025 dividend of $7.8M ÷ $0.0625) × $16.50 = approximately $2,059M of equity value; plus net debt of $1,845.4M gross principal less $26.5M cash = $1,818.9M; total approximately $3,878M. The press release's "approximately $3.9 billion" therefore describes enterprise value including net debt, not equity value — a distinction the release does not make.

Implied multiples: EV/FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of 7.9×; equity value to attributable net income of 14.1×; 14.2× the proxy's FY2025 EPS of $1.16. The EBITDA multiple excludes operating leases entirely; with $1,505.2M of undiscounted future lease payments and roughly $244.6M of near-term rent, a rent-adjusted view would show a materially lower multiple on a materially larger capitalization. For a business where most of the largest segment's facilities sit on sub-ten-year leases, the lease-inclusive view is the economically correct one, and it was not computable from available disclosure.

Three benchmarks frame fairness. First, Select repurchased 6,375,512 shares in FY2025 for approximately $96.5M, an average of $15.14 — the takeout was 9.0% above the price at which the company itself was a buyer, and 29.3% above the $12.76 program-life average across 54,610,335 shares. Second, Ortenzio's November 24, 2025 Schedule 13D proposal was $16.00–$16.20; the final $16.50 was a bump of only 1.9%–3.1% after roughly three months of negotiation to signing (and a seven-month process to closing). Third, the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows Select's indexed TSR falling from 142.80 to 114.38 in FY2025 (−19.9%) while the peer index rose from 92.81 to 109.97 (+18.5%) — a roughly 38-point relative swing against the company in the year the bid arrived.

That pattern is consistent with a Special Committee holding limited competitive leverage, and equally consistent with an independent advisor concluding $16.50 already sat within its intrinsic-value range. The evidence that would adjudicate — Goldman Sachs's valuation range for the Special Committee, any go-shop or market check, the termination fee — was not obtainable. The process architecture (committee formed within one day, independent counsel and banker, majority-of-the-unaffiliated-holder vote condition, which was satisfied) is the standard structure for business-judgment cleansing. Given the two subsequent earnings misses and the 3.4% Adjusted EBITDA decline, a claim that $16.50 materially underpaid is unsupported on the available record.

**Current-price arithmetic.** The frozen basis carries no authoritative price; the daily-history close is $16.51 against fixed cash consideration of $16.50 already payable to record holders at the effective time. Upside from here is $0.00 in the base case and −$0.01 (−0.06%) against the quoted level, with no dividend, no earnings claim, and no verified appraisal or contingent instrument. The valuation score of 1.5 reflects the absence of any asymmetry, not a judgment that the underlying assets were overpriced at 7.9× EBITDA.

The only potentially live SEM-linked security is the $550.0M of 6.250% senior notes due 2032 issued under the December 3, 2024 indenture. Whether the change of control triggered a put (typically at 101% in comparable indentures) and whether the notes were repaid, refinanced or left outstanding is unverified. That is a credit instrument outside the scope of this equity assessment.

## Competitive Landscape

Select operated in a structurally contracting inpatient niche and a highly fragmented outpatient one.

On the hospital side, named competitors were ScionHealth (private, the former Kindred LTCH business) and Encompass Health, plus rehabilitation and step-down units inside acute-care hospitals. The last is the most consequential given the HIH model: a host hospital that opens its own step-down unit becomes a direct competitor while controlling the lease. The 10-K discloses that site-neutral payments "caused some operators to close LTCHs," which cuts both ways — less local competition, but a shrinking claims dataset that mechanically worsens outlier rate-setting for whoever remains. Select's own critical illness footprint fell from 107 hospitals at end-2023 to 104 at end-2025, with licensed beds down from 4,538 to 4,420.

MedPAC's June 2023 conclusion that a unified post-acute care PPS is feasible but "would disproportionately impact payments for certain PAC provider types, particularly LTCHs" is an explicit statement by Medicare's advisory body that LTCHs are currently paid above what a rationalized system would pay. Public markets discount that regardless of legislative timing, which partly explains a single-digit takeout multiple.

Outpatient rehabilitation is the weakest position, and management says so: competitors include Athletico, ATI Physical Therapy, U.S. Physical Therapy and Upstream Rehabilitation plus physician- and locally owned clinics; "the barriers to entry are not substantial and current customers have the flexibility to move easily to new healthcare service providers." The financial expression was margin compression from 9.4% to 7.0% over two years on commercial revenue.

Inpatient rehabilitation was the one segment with improving competitive standing — 9.3% admissions growth, 5.9% revenue-per-day growth, ~21.6% margins, health-system joint ventures that are capital-efficient for Select while leaking substantial cash to minority partners. Blended across segments, the trajectory was flat-to-deteriorating, which supports a competitive position score of 4.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Instrument risk dominates.** The security is delisted with no verified deregistration filing read, no liquidity, and no forward claim. Any position established at a residual quote faces settlement and transfer-agent friction against consideration already fixed at $16.50.

**Regulatory risks that drove the business decline remain, now borne by the private owner.** The LTCH high-cost outlier fixed-loss amount rose from $38,518 in FY2023 to $78,936 in FY2026, a cumulative 105%, on a rate-setting process CMS has not adjusted for COVID-distorted cost reports, a shrinking standard-rate case population, or labor inflation; management expected a further FY2027 increase. Outlier reconciliation triggers tightened for cost reporting periods beginning on or after October 1, 2025 (from a ≥10 percentage-point cost-to-charge change to ±20%), which the 10-K says will likely increase reconciliations and which also delays final cost-report settlement and thereby blocks reimbursement appeals; first affected reports reach MACs around March 2027. The IRF Review Choice Demonstration, already in Alabama and Pennsylvania, is slated for Texas and California; the 10-K discloses that Pennsylvania IRF Medicare reimbursement statewide fell from $33M to $29M per month when RCD began, roughly −12%, and an HHS-OIG nationwide IRF claims audit report was expected in 2026. LTCH certification thresholds (Medicare average length of stay above 25 days against Select's actual 31; LTCH discharge payment percentage of at least 50%) are step-function rather than gradual.

**Other named exposures.** OBBBA Medicaid reductions (CBO estimate roughly $1 trillion over ten years) touch only 3.3% of revenue directly, with the indirect channel through state rates and host-hospital finances less quantifiable. Labor cost inflation and contract clinical staffing are explicitly cited as margin drags. Insurance limits look thin for the scale of operations — $42.0M professional liability aggregate and $45.0M general liability for wholly owned operations across 104 hospitals, 38 rehabilitation hospitals and 1,917 clinics, with professional liability written claims-made and wage-and-hour and qui tam actions uninsured.

**Governance items specific to the transaction.** The buyer was the insider group. Insider ownership was approximately 16.04% at February 1, 2026 — real influence, short of control, which is why WCAS equity and committed debt were required. A comprehensive anti-takeover structure (classified board, no action by written consent, restricted special meetings, blank-check preferred, DGCL §203 not opted out) insulated the board while it evaluated the proposal and simultaneously made an unsolicited competing bid structurally difficult. Compensation metrics were EPS and return on equity, both leverage-sensitive and poorly matched to a business distributing 1.9× free cash flow. No Form 4 data was obtainable, so there is no evidence in either direction on insider transactions ahead of the November 24, 2025 proposal — the most conspicuous absence in the governance record for an insider-led buyout.

Two items should not be scored as liabilities. Plaintiff-firm releases from Bleichmar Fonti & Auld, Johnson Fistel and Scott+Scott announced "investigations," not filed complaints; no docket was verified, and one release misstates executive titles. And the timing of the closing 19 months after the tax-free Concentra distribution raises a Section 355(e) plan-presumption question that sophisticated counsel would have addressed pre-signing; the 10-K does not connect the two, and the merger proxy's tax disclosure was not read.

### Options Market Signal

The only options data available is a snapshot comparison between May 7 and June 11, 2026, during which the price moved from $16.44 to $16.63 and the put/call open-interest ratio was 0.00 in both readings. Zero open interest on both sides, with the underlying pinned within roughly 1% of the $16.50 consideration, is the signature of a fully deal-arbitraged stock in the final weeks before closing: no directional demand, no hedging demand, no residual optionality being priced. It corroborates the closing rather than offering any independent view of value. There is no current options market to read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (no longer available to public shareholders).** The private owners bought a scale post-acute platform at approximately 7.9× trailing Adjusted EBITDA — a discount that reflects LTCH policy overhang more than asset quality. Behind the headline decline sits one segment compounding at 16% revenue growth on 21.6% margins with $112.6M of accelerating capex and occupancy temporarily diluted to 82% by new beds; three additional rehabilitation hospitals were added by March 31, 2026. Near-term reimbursement offsets exist: the 4.6% site-neutral IPPS-comparable per-diem reduction was scheduled to lapse after September 30, 2026, and management expected a net ~2% CY2026 Medicare increase for physical and occupational therapy. Covenant leverage of 3.67× against a 7.00× limit left room to fund the build-out. Under private ownership, with the outpatient segment either fixed or divested and no quarterly EPS constraint, the rehabilitation-hospital ramp could support a materially higher exit valuation. None of this is investable through SEM common stock.

**Bear case (the operative one).** There is no security. The equity was extinguished at a fixed $16.50 on July 1, 2026, and the residual $16.51 quote offers zero upside and slightly negative expected return before friction. The business being handed over was deteriorating in cash terms: Adjusted EBITDA −3.4%, two of three segments down double digits, operating cash flow −33%, $117.3M of free cash flow against $221.4M of distributions, $26.5M of year-end cash on $5.45B of revenue, and consecutive 31% and 24% EPS misses in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026. The regulatory pressure that caused the decline — a fixed-loss amount up 105% in three years on a self-reinforcing rate-setting mechanism, tightened outlier reconciliation with the appeal remedy removed, RCD expansion toward Texas and California — is structural and unresolved. Sponsor leverage on top of that base raises rather than lowers the risk carried by the capital structure. The only SEM-linked instrument that might still trade is the 6.250% 2032 notes, whose change-of-control treatment is unverified.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: None available. The value-relevant event — the merger becoming effective at a fixed $16.50 cash price — already occurred on July 1, 2026. There is no future dated event that could move the common stock lower, because the consideration is contractually fixed and the listing is terminated. A short thesis requires a security whose price can fall; here the price is a residual data print rather than a market clearing level.

**Timing window**: Not applicable. Any theoretical convergence from $16.51 to $16.50 is $0.01, or 6 basis points, which does not cover a single day of borrow or commission.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Not measurable. Post-delisting short interest and days-to-cover are not reported for the ticker, and institutional ownership positions were cashed out at the effective time.

**Borrow and options signal**: There is no locatable borrow on a delisted security whose float has been cancelled and converted to cash. The last options reading showed zero open interest on both puts and calls with the underlying pinned at the deal price — no bearish positioning to confirm because there is nothing left to hedge.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Downside is $0.01 per share; upside risk is unbounded in the operational sense that an unfillable or mispriced residual quote could print anywhere. Reward:risk is effectively zero-to-negative.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE**. The AVOID rating reflects the absence of an investable instrument, not an actively collapsing business. There is no borrow, no catalyst, and no downside to capture.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: AVOID — absence of instrument.**

- **Current holders of record**: entitled to $16.50 per share in cash. The action is administrative — confirm receipt of consideration through the paying agent — not investment. Verify whether any appraisal rights were perfected under DGCL §262; no evidence of a perfected appraisal claim was obtained, and none should be assumed.
- **Prospective buyers**: do not transact. A purchase at the $16.51 residual print yields approximately −0.06% against fixed consideration, with settlement risk on a delisted security and no forward earnings claim.
- **Coverage action**: remove SEM common stock from the investable universe pending confirmation of deregistration via Form 25 and Form 15. This mirrors the treatment applied to prior completed take-privates in the coverage set.

**Re-entry criteria** — any of the following would restore a monitorable security:
1. Confirmation that a SEM-linked equity instrument remains outstanding and tradable (a stub, contingent value right, or rollover security), with disclosed terms. Nothing in the available record indicates one exists.
2. A future re-IPO of the platform by the consortium, at which point the analysis resets on post-LBO capital structure, segment margin trajectory, and the LTCH policy path.
3. Verified pricing on the 6.250% senior notes due 2032, which would move coverage to the credit desk rather than equity. The threshold facts required are whether the change-of-control put was triggered, whether the notes remain outstanding, and pro forma leverage post-financing.

**Verification items that would change the framing rather than the rating**: a filed Form 8-K, Form 25 or Form 15 confirming the delisting mechanics; the DEFM14A or SC 13E-3 disclosing Goldman Sachs's fairness range, any market check, and the termination fee; the special-meeting vote tally including the separate unaffiliated-holder count; and any actually filed shareholder complaint in Delaware Chancery. None of these creates a claim for a buyer at $16.51.

**Timeline**: indefinite. Absent a re-IPO or a disclosed residual instrument, there is no natural reassessment date.

## Conclusion

**AVOID.** Overall score 2.75, continuing the decline from 5.7 in May through 3.2 in late July, and the new information this cycle is decisive rather than incremental: the equity was extinguished at $16.50 per share in cash on July 1, 2026 in an insider-led buyout by Robert Ortenzio, Martin Jackson and Welsh Carson, and the common stock was delisted from the NYSE. The $16.51 residual quote offers no upside, no income, and no participation in the private company's results.

The rating is not a directional bet against the assets. Select was a scale operator with a defensible inpatient-rehabilitation franchise growing 16% at 21.6% margins, a genuinely capital-light HIH structure in LTCHs, near-zero unionization, and covenant leverage of 3.67× against a 7.00× limit. It was also a business whose FY2025 reported earnings improvement dissolved entirely under an $82.5M stock-compensation swing, whose Adjusted EBITDA fell 3.4% on 5.1% revenue growth, whose two largest segments posted 12% and 17% EBITDA declines, whose free cash flow of $117.3M funded $221.4M of distributions with the help of new borrowings and a cash drawdown to $26.5M, and which then missed consensus EPS by 31% and 24% in the two quarters after the 10-K. A takeout at 7.9× trailing EBITDA — 9.0% above the company's own average FY2025 repurchase price of $15.14, following a 19.9% decline in indexed TSR while the peer index rose 18.5% — is a plausible clearing price for those cash economics under an unresolved LTCH policy overhang, and the available record does not support a claim that shareholders were materially underpaid. Whether it was generous cannot be settled without the fairness-opinion range, which was not obtainable.

For anyone holding, the action is collecting cash. For anyone considering a position, there is nothing to buy.
