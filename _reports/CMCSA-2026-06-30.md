**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Comcast trades at roughly $24.22, down about 28% over the trailing year and near multi-year lows, at a mid-single-digit trailing P/E and a ~5.7% dividend yield. The headline cheapness reflects a genuine and worsening problem: the crown-jewel domestic broadband franchise crossed from "losing subscribers but holding pricing" to "losing subscribers *and* cutting price" in Q1 2026. Domestic broadband revenue fell 5.1% versus roughly flat in 2025, and Residential Connectivity & Platforms adjusted EBITDA margin compressed 160 basis points to 37.1%. Fixed-wireless access (T-Mobile, Verizon) and fiber overbuild (AT&T, Lumen, Fios) are taking share from what was for two decades a near-monopoly on high-speed home internet.

The offsetting story is a de-conglomeration play. Comcast completed the Versant cable-network spin-off in January 2026 and announced on June 29, 2026 that it will separate the entire Content & Experiences business (NBCUniversal, Sky, Universal parks, studios, Peacock) into a second independent public company within roughly a year. The end-state CMCSA becomes a pure-play connectivity company with higher cash-flow quality, lower growth, and more direct exposure to the broadband threat. The stock is a sum-of-the-parts unlock bet layered on top of a structurally challenged asset. Cash generation remains formidable: FY2025 free cash flow of roughly $21.9B (17.7% FCF margin), a well-covered and 17-years-raised dividend, and $6.8B of 2025 buybacks that reduced the diluted share count from 3,908M to 3,617M.

We rate CMCSA a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The valuation embeds severe pessimism, which limits downside, but the fundamental trajectory is deteriorating on the metric that matters most (broadband revenue and margin), consensus estimates are falling across every horizon, and no insider is stepping up to buy at these levels. The spin-off could unlock value, but the pieces most exposed to secular decline stay with RemainCo. We assess this as a coin-flip between value-unlock and levered decline, and we do not yet have enough evidence to take a confident side.

## Company Overview

Comcast operates two primary businesses across five reporting segments post-Versant. **Connectivity & Platforms** (~$81B in 2025) contains Residential Connectivity & Platforms ($70.7B: Xfinity broadband, MVNO wireless over Verizon's network, video, voice, plus Sky in the UK and Italy) and Business Services Connectivity ($10.2B: Comcast Business, +5.5% revenue, ~56% adjusted EBITDA margins, boosted by the April 2025 Nitel acquisition). **Content & Experiences** (~$45.6B) contains Media ($27.1B: NBC, Telemundo, Peacock at 46M paid subs in Q1 2026, Sky Sports), Studios ($11.3B: Universal, Illumination, DreamWorks, Focus), and Theme Parks ($9.8B, +14% on the May 2025 Epic Universe opening in Orlando).

The competitive assets are a scaled HFC network passing 65M+ US homes and businesses, multi-gig capability, the highest-margin business-services franchise in cable, and a valuable content/IP library plus a theme-park flywheel. The structural vulnerability is straightforward: the core residential broadband business is ex-growth and losing share, and the announced NBCUniversal/Sky spin-off will move the two clearest growth vectors (parks and content) into the SpinCo, leaving RemainCo more concentrated in the declining franchise.

## Financial Analysis

Consolidated revenue was flat at roughly $123.7B in both 2024 and 2025, masking a divergence in which connectivity slowly shrinks while content/experiences grows. FY2025 net income attributable was $20.0B, up 23.5% year over year, but this figure is misleading — it was inflated by a one-time $9.4B pre-tax gain on the June 2025 Hulu sale. Operating income fell 11.3% to $20.7B, and adjusted EBITDA fell 1.8% to $37.4B, which is the more honest read on underlying performance.

Cash generation is the strongest part of the story. Operating cash flow was $33.6B, capex $11.75B, and free cash flow roughly $21.9B. ROE was 20.3%, ROA 7.2%, net margin 15.9%. Against a market cap of ~$88B, the FCF yield sits near 25%; the market is not paying for growth here.

On the balance sheet, total debt was $98.9B at year-end, falling to $94.6B by Q1 2026 after Versant-funded note redemptions. Debt/equity is 2.81x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.4 flags "distress," but that is a false positive for a capital-intensive, utility-like cable operator: weighted-average debt maturity is roughly 15 years, 95% is fixed-rate at an approximate 3.8% coupon, the company comfortably meets its leverage covenant, and the 0.88 current ratio is structurally normal for a business where customers pay in advance. Investment-grade status and $21.9B of FCF make the leverage manageable, though it limits flexibility in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

Q1 2026 is where the warning signs sharpen. Consolidated revenue rose 5.3% to $31.5B, but this was flattered by roughly $2.2B of incremental Media revenue from the Milan-Cortina Olympics and Super Bowl. Beneath that, operating income fell 26.9% and adjusted EBITDA fell 16.8% to $7.9B. Domestic broadband revenue declined 5.1%, driven by both customer losses and lower average rates under the new simplified-pricing strategy. Residential Connectivity & Platforms adjusted EBITDA margin fell 160 basis points to 37.1%.

Capital allocation remains shareholder-friendly: $6.8B of buybacks (205M shares) in 2025 plus $1.3B in Q1 2026, a dividend raised to $1.32 annualized with 17 consecutive years of increases, and $7.6B remaining on the authorization. The dividend costs roughly $4.7B annually against ~$21.9B FCF; coverage is not the concern.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is weak and deteriorating, which drives the low Growth Prospects score. The most important operating metric — domestic broadband — is now in outright revenue decline. Domestic broadband customers fell 711K in 2025 (versus 411K in 2024), and penetration of homes passed dropped from 49.8% to 47.6% (48.4% residential in Q1 2026). The simplified/price-locked strategy plus a free-wireless-line offer is stabilizing unit losses (Q1 2026 broadband losses reportedly improved to ~65K) but at the explicit cost of ARPU and margin. Management concedes the pricing moves will "negatively impact average domestic broadband revenue per customer," and the Q1 -5.1% print confirms the trade-off is real.

Domestic video customers fell 1.3M in 2025 to 11.3M, continuing the linear decline. The genuine bright spots are narrower: wireless lines grew from 7.8M to 9.3M in 2025 and hit 9.74M in Q1 2026, with wireless revenue up 16%, and a new T-Mobile MVNO in 2026 diversifies from Verizon-only. Business Services (+5.5% revenue, ~56% margins) and Theme Parks (+14% on Epic Universe) are durable growth vectors, but both parks and content migrate to the SpinCo, so post-spin RemainCo is more exposed to the broadband headwind.

Estimate revisions confirm the negative momentum. Current-quarter EPS consensus fell 5.5% over 90 days, current-year 2.2%, and next-year 2.4%. Every horizon is being revised down. This is the opposite of the profile (>30% upward revisions) that would justify treating trailing multiples as trough earnings. The weak visibility is not a cyclical trough being under-modeled; it is a structural fade being progressively recognized.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$24.22 the stock trades at roughly 4.7x trailing earnings and 6.9x current-year consensus EPS of $3.51. Against Cable & Satellite peers — Charter at 3.8x, Versant at 6.7x — Comcast sits near the peer-median 6.7x. The absolute multiple is low, but the peer set is uniformly depressed (Charter is down 70% from its 52-week high, Cable One down 74%), which tells you the market is repricing the entire cable-broadband cohort rather than singling out Comcast.

Our lessons here counsel caution: a low P/E on its own is anti-predictive and frequently marks a value trap. The correct question is whether there is a specific reason the market is wrong. The candidate catalysts are the sum-of-the-parts unlock from the NBCUniversal/Sky spin-off and the ~25% FCF yield supporting a combined shareholder return (dividend + buyback). Against that, the trailing period is a plateau that is beginning to erode, so cycle-adjusted normalized earnings are more likely below trailing than above. The analyst-target-as-floor logic does not apply here because estimates are falling, not rising.

We score valuation 5.5 — modestly above the midpoint because the FCF yield and yield support provide a real cushion, but well short of a high score because the cheapness reflects a genuine structural impairment without a confirmed re-rating catalyst. The analyst mean target is $32.19 (median $32.00) against a $21 low and $44 high, implying ~33% upside to the mean. We treat the high end as contingent on the spin-off delivering a clean value-unlock, which is far from certain given RemainCo inherits the impaired asset.

## Competitive Landscape

Comcast sits at the center of the cable broadband disruption. The two-decade near-monopoly on high-speed home internet is eroding on two fronts simultaneously. Fixed-wireless access from T-Mobile and Verizon is adding millions of 5G home-internet subscribers at lower price points, taking the price-sensitive segment. Fiber overbuild from AT&T, Verizon Fios, and Lumen is extending symmetrical fiber into Comcast's footprint. The mechanism is direct: a customer with a viable FWA or fiber alternative at a lower price either switches or forces Comcast to cut price to retain them, which shows up as either unit loss or ARPU compression, and Q1 2026 shows both happening at once.

Comcast's counter-strategy is network upgrades (DOCSIS 4.0/multi-gig symmetrical, ~60% of the residential base already has multi-gig downstream), convergence bundling of its own fast-growing MVNO wireless with broadband plus the free-line offer to raise switching costs, and footprint expansion (homes passed +1.3M in 2025). The data so far shows the strategy defending units at the expense of ARPU and margin, the classic maturing-utility dynamic. Business Services remains a durable strength and Theme Parks a genuine growth vector, but both of the latter's upside migrates to the media SpinCo.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating: penetration is falling and the core is now in revenue decline, which supports a below-midpoint Competitive Position score. This reverses the earlier premium (7.0 in February) as the Q1 revenue decline made the share loss quantitatively unambiguous.

## Risk Assessment

The central risk is broadband share loss compounding into accelerating revenue decline; the Q1 2026 -5.1% print is the number to watch, and further deterioration in subsequent quarters would validate the structural-decline thesis. Self-inflicted ARPU compression from simplified pricing is a second, related risk that management has acknowledged will pressure per-customer revenue. Secular video/linear decline continues, with $93.8B of programming obligations (NBA through 2036, NFL through 2034, Olympics through 2036) locking in cost even as the linear base shrinks.

Leverage is manageable given FCF but limits flexibility as rates stay higher for longer and capitalized interest falls post-Epic Universe. The dual-class control structure (Brian Roberts controlling ~33% of the vote via Class B super-voting shares with separate approval rights over major transactions) is a persistent governance overhang. Execution risk on two back-to-back structural breakups in 18 months is real; the NBCUniversal/Sky spin-off leadership specifics (Cavanagh to media, Angelakis to connectivity per news reporting) are not yet confirmed against the primary 8-K, and stranded costs, tax structure, and debt allocation between SpinCo and RemainCo will materially affect the sum-of-the-parts math.

Insider behavior is a negative: no open-market purchases into the weakness. Activity is routine RSU vesting, tax-withholding sales, and a Roberts charitable gift of 202,500 shares in May 2026. With the stock near multi-year lows and a "coiled spring at 4.4x earnings" thesis circulating, the absence of any executive adding shares is telling.

Short interest is low at 2.8% of float (2.9 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded bear position nor a squeeze setup; the market is expressing its pessimism through the multiple, not through shorting.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 46.2% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 29.6% (a 16.7-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (46.2% near-term versus 39.7% at six months), which signals the market expects a discrete near-term event — almost certainly reaction to the NBCUniversal/Sky spin-off details and the next earnings print. Positioning is call-heavy, not put-heavy: the put/call skew is -10.7% (call-weighted), the put/call volume ratio is 0.13, and the unusual-activity table is dominated by call buying ($22, $23, $24, and $27 calls with elevated volume/OI). This is consistent with speculative positioning for a spin-off-driven pop rather than downside hedging. The signal cuts against the fundamental caution; the options market is leaning into an upside catalyst, which is worth respecting but not sufficient to override the deteriorating operating metrics.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Comcast is a de-conglomeration value-unlock at a distressed multiple. The Versant spin and the announced NBCUniversal/Sky/Parks separation crystallize a sum-of-the-parts that the conglomerate structure has masked. The content SpinCo (parks growing double-digit, a 6,500-film library, Peacock) commands a higher multiple standalone, while RemainCo becomes a clean, high-FCF connectivity utility. The ~25% FCF yield funds a combined dividend-plus-buyback return while investors wait, the balance sheet is investment-grade with 15-year laddered maturities, wireless is a genuine growth engine (+16% revenue, 9.74M lines), and Business Services compounds at ~56% margins. Broadband unit losses are already stabilizing. If the spin values the pieces at even modest standalone multiples, the stock re-rates toward the $32–$44 analyst range. Bull case target ~$34.

**Bear Case.** RemainCo is a structurally shrinking, levered asset and the spin-off dumps the impairment squarely into it. Broadband revenue is now declining and margins are compressing as FWA and fiber take share and simplified pricing erodes ARPU, a trend that accelerates rather than stabilizes. Programming obligations keep costs sticky against a shrinking video base. Consensus estimates are falling across every horizon, the low P/E is a value trap, and no insider is buying. Stranded costs and debt allocation could leave RemainCo overleveraged relative to its declining cash flows. In this scenario the multiple stays depressed or compresses further, and the stock drifts toward the analyst low of $21 or below. Bear case target ~$20 (roughly -17% from current).

Base case ~$26, reflecting a modest re-rating on spin-off execution offset by continued core erosion — roughly the midpoint between the two scenarios and consistent with a market that pays the FCF yield but declines to underwrite growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the valuation cushion and the spin-off optionality balance against a deteriorating core and falling estimates, and we lack the evidence for a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Domestic broadband revenue decline moderates to better than -2% YoY for two consecutive quarters, or broadband unit losses turn positive, demonstrating the retention strategy is working without further ARPU destruction.
- The NBCUniversal/Sky spin-off terms (confirmed against the primary filing) allocate debt and stranded costs favorably to RemainCo, with the SpinCo valuation implying a sum-of-the-parts materially above the current ~$88B combined market cap.
- Clustered open-market insider buying by two or more C-suite executives — the missing bullish confirmation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Domestic broadband revenue decline accelerates beyond -6% YoY or residential EBITDA margin compresses another 150+ bps, confirming the structural-decline path.
- Current-year EPS consensus falls below $3.30 (a further ~6% cut) as the negative revision trend accelerates.
- A dividend cut or buyback suspension, or a spin-off structure that leaves RemainCo with disproportionate leverage.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) for the broadband trend, and the NBCUniversal/Sky spin-off Form 10/definitive terms over the next ~12 months. The near-term options backwardation suggests the market expects a resolution point within weeks.

## Conclusion

Comcast is cheap for a reason. The core broadband franchise turned to revenue decline in Q1 2026, margins are compressing under self-inflicted pricing pressure, fixed-wireless and fiber are structurally eroding the moat, and consensus estimates are falling across every horizon. The mid-single-digit multiple and ~5.7% yield price the connectivity business as a structurally declining asset, and the de-conglomeration into content SpinCo and connectivity RemainCo leaves the impaired asset with the RemainCo shareholders. Against that, ~$21.9B of free cash flow, an investment-grade balance sheet, a well-covered dividend, and a real sum-of-the-parts unlock provide a genuine floor and an identifiable upside path — one the options market is positioning for.

The trajectory of our own scoring (6.5 → 5.6 over four reports, with Competitive Position and Management Quality each dropping meaningfully) reflects the same deterioration the numbers now show, and the current data confirms rather than reverses it. We maintain HOLD (uncertain), overall score 4.9. The value-unlock could work, but with the core deteriorating on the metric that matters most and no insider willing to buy the weakness, the burden of proof sits with the bulls. We would want to see broadband stabilization or favorable spin-off terms before treating the cheapness as opportunity rather than trap.
