## Executive Summary

SL Green Realty Corp is Manhattan's largest office landlord, operating ~26.2 million square feet across 44 core properties concentrated in Midtown Manhattan. Q1 2026 delivered the highest first-quarter leasing volume in SLG's 28-year history (929K SF), occupancy rose to 94.4%, average rents hit a record $105.12/SF, and mark-to-market spreads on replacement leases were +16.1%. One Madison Avenue reached 100% leased within three years of completion.

However, consolidated debt stands at $4.04B, with an additional $5.9B in proportionate joint venture debt off-balance sheet. Weighted average debt maturity is just 2.34 years. Approximately $6.4B of combined debt matures in 2026-2027, requiring management to execute what the CFO himself described as a "$7.0 billion 2026 financing plan." Interest expense surged 26.6% in 2025 to $194.7M. FFO guidance for 2026 is $4.40-$4.70/share, down materially from $5.72 in 2025 (which itself was inflated by one-time recoveries). The Altman Z-score of 1.12 places the company in the distress zone. While early 2026 refinancings ($2.0B credit facility, $1.65B One Madison CMBS) have derisked the near-term, the remaining maturity wall is substantial, and any tightening in credit availability or widening of spreads on CRE debt would directly impair SLG's ability to roll its obligations at acceptable rates.

Strong operational execution combined with a precarious capital structure makes this difficult to avoid outright but equally difficult to recommend with conviction.

## Company Overview

SL Green Realty Corp was formed in 1997 and operates as a self-managed REIT focused on commercial office properties in Manhattan. The company operates through three reportable segments: Real Estate (core office/retail/residential portfolio), Debt and Preferred Equity Investments ($168.4M book value), and SUMMIT One Vanderbilt (an observation deck generating ~$122M annually in revenue, with expansion plans to Paris by 2027).

The portfolio consists of 33 consolidated and 11 unconsolidated joint venture properties. Manhattan office space totals 23.3M SF across 26 properties at 93.0% year-end leased occupancy (rising to 94.4% in Q1 2026). The five largest properties — One Vanderbilt, 11 Madison, 420 Lexington, 1515 Broadway, and 245 Park — account for 36.6% of annualized cash rent. Tenant concentration is modest: Paramount Global is the largest tenant at 5.3% of portfolio cash rent, and the top five tenants represent 15.2%.

SLG employs 1,289 people (337 corporate), with a fully integrated platform covering leasing, management, development, construction, and investment management. Management tenure averages 22.9 years across the executive team.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Operating Performance

Revenue grew 13.2% to $1.003B in 2025, driven primarily by a 12.2% increase in rental revenue ($680M vs. $606M) from property consolidations (100 Park Avenue, 10 East 53rd Street, 315 West 33rd Street, 800 Third Avenue) and the acquisition of 500 Park Avenue. Same-store rental revenue grew 6.8%, a solid organic result. Interest income from consolidated securitization vehicles surged to $62.7M from $19M due to CMBS trust consolidations. SUMMIT revenue declined 8.2% to $122.3M as the Ascent experience was offline for maintenance.

| Year | Revenue | YoY Change |
|------|---------|------------|
| 2022 | $827M | — |
| 2023 | $914M | +10.5% |
| 2024 | $886M | -3.1% |
| 2025 | $1,003M | +13.2% |

Operating income before JV equity rose 8.3% to $374.5M. However, same-store operating income was essentially flat ($280M vs. $282M, -0.6%), as a 13.7% increase in property operating expenses — driven by higher real estate taxes — offset the 6.8% same-store revenue growth. This margin compression at the property level indicates that top-line growth is not fully translating to bottom-line improvement on an organic basis.

### FFO Trajectory

FFO of $437.7M ($5.72/share) in 2025 was down from $569.8M in 2024. The 2024 figure was inflated by $208M in JV sale gains, $43.8M in debt extinguishment gains, and $263M in JV impairment recoveries — all non-recurring. Stripping out one-time items, the underlying FFO trajectory is more stable but not growing.

2026 FFO guidance of $4.40-$4.70/share ($4.55 midpoint) represents a 20.5% decline from 2025's reported $5.72. Q1 2026 FFO of $0.84/share (annualized: $3.36) is running below the low end of guidance, though Q1 was penalized by the absence of $25M in mortgage resolution income that boosted Q1 2025.

GAAP net loss widened to -$96.9M in 2025 (from +$30.2M in 2024), reflecting $32.1M in real estate impairments (100 Park Avenue, 760 Madison), $36.2M in negative fair value adjustments, and the $11.9M gaming license write-off.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

Consolidated debt of $4.04B (up from $3.62B) carries a weighted average rate of 5.34% with ~91% fixed or hedged. The weighted average maturity of 2.34 years is dangerously short for a real estate portfolio.

SLG's proportionate share of JV debt adds $5.9B ($12.5B total JV debt), bringing total economic debt exposure to approximately $10B. The maturity schedule is front-loaded:

- **2026:** $555M consolidated mortgages + $100M term loan + $1.08B JV debt = ~$1.74B
- **2027:** $879M mortgages + $640M revolver + $1.05B term loan + $1.75B JV debt = ~$4.32B

That is $6.06B maturing in a 24-month window. The March 2026 credit facility refinancing ($2.0B extended to 2031) and One Madison CMBS ($1.65B at 5.81%) address $3.65B of this. The remaining ~$2.4-3.4B still needs to be refinanced or repaid.

Liquidity stands at $781.9M ($602.5M revolver capacity + $179.4M cash). Operating cash flow was $82.9M in 2025 (down from $129.6M), with $255.6M in capex and leasing costs. The 7 Dey Street disposition ($222.6M, expected Q2 2026) provides an additional liquidity source.

The Altman Z-score of 1.12 falls in the distress zone. While this is characteristic of leveraged REITs broadly, it quantifies that SLG has minimal margin for error on its financing plan.

### Dividend

The annual dividend was reduced to $2.47/share ($0.6175/quarter). At recent prices (~$36-46), this implies a yield of approximately 5.4-6.9%. Management stated the reduction preserves liquidity for "discounted debt extinguishments, share repurchases, or ongoing development projects." The dividend cut is prudent given the refinancing needs but signals that management itself acknowledges balance sheet constraints.

## Growth Analysis

Growth at SLG operates on two distinct vectors: organic leasing and external capital allocation.

**Organic leasing is strong and accelerating.** Manhattan market-wide leasing volume of 31.0M SF in 2025 was up 32.5% from 2024. AI companies are emerging as a new demand driver: Harvey AI and Clay Labs signed significant Q1 2026 leases, and Sigma Computing anchored One Madison in 2025. This diversifies the tenant base beyond traditional financial services (33.6% of Manhattan leasing) and TAMI (23.0%).

**External growth is constrained by the balance sheet.** SLG spent $330.8M on investing activities in 2025 and funded the gap with $252.8M in net financing. Until the $7B refinancing plan is substantially complete, acquisitive growth is unlikely unless funded by dispositions. The 7 Dey Street sale is an example of the asset recycling strategy SLG will likely need to pursue.

**SUMMIT expansion** (Paris 2027, potentially other cities) represents modest growth optionality. Expansion to international markets would diversify revenue away from the core Manhattan office portfolio. However, the gaming license abandonment ($11.9M write-off) suggests that not all diversification efforts will succeed.

The 46.3% of consolidated lease SF expiring by December 2030 ($316.3M of annualized escalated rent) is both a risk and an opportunity. At current mark-to-market spreads, successful re-leasing of this space represents significant embedded revenue growth. However, the volume creates execution risk in a softening macro environment.

## Valuation Assessment

SLG trades at approximately $36-46/share. Using the 2026 FFO guidance midpoint of $4.55/share:

- **P/FFO:** ~7.9-10.1x at $36-46
- **Dividend yield:** ~5.4-6.9%
- **Analyst consensus PT:** $51 (15 analysts), implying approximately 10-42% upside depending on entry

The REIT sector median P/FFO for office REITs has been depressed since 2022, typically in the 8-12x range for quality names. SLG's valuation at the low end of this range reflects the market's assessment of balance sheet risk.

A simple valuation framework: if SLG executes its refinancing plan successfully and FFO stabilizes at the $4.55 midpoint, a normalized 10-12x P/FFO multiple implies $45.50-$54.60/share. If FFO recovers toward $5.00+ as interest expense normalizes post-refinancing and leasing spreads continue at +15%, a $55-65 range is achievable. However, if refinancing costs increase materially or occupancy stalls, FFO could decline toward $4.00, placing the stock at $32-40 at a distressed 8-10x multiple.

The stock appears modestly cheap relative to the quality of the operating business, but the discount exists for a reason: the balance sheet risk is real and quantifiable.

## Competitive Landscape

SLG competes with Vornado Realty Trust (VNO), Brookfield Property Partners, Paramount Group (PGRE), and other Manhattan office owners in a market of approximately 417M SF total inventory. SLG's advantages:

1. **Scale:** Largest Manhattan office landlord at ~26M SF. Scale supports an integrated platform (leasing, management, development, lending) and provides negotiating leverage with contractors and tenants.

2. **Location quality:** Concentration in premier Midtown Manhattan locations near major transit nodes. In a flight-to-quality environment, location quality is the primary differentiator.

3. **Management depth:** Average executive tenure of 22.9 years. The team has navigated multiple market cycles, including the GFC and COVID. The ability to execute complex transactions (One Vanderbilt JV at $4.7B valuation, One Madison development, CMBS investing) demonstrates sophisticated capital markets capabilities.

4. **New supply constraints:** New supply additions are "nominal relative to the size of the overall market," limiting competitive new inventory.

AI companies represent an emerging demand category that favors SLG's premium, well-amenitized buildings near transit. Harvey AI's expansion to 92,663 SF at One Madison and Clay Labs' lease at One Madison are tangible evidence.

The primary competitive risk is from sub-market shifts: if tenants increasingly favor Downtown, Hudson Yards, or other areas over traditional Midtown, SLG's concentration becomes a liability. Current leasing data does not support this concern, but it is a long-term structural risk to monitor.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Refinancing Risk (Critical)
The $3.65B addressed in March 2026 is material progress, but $2.4-3.4B remains. With rates elevated, CRE credit conditions tightening, and recession probability at elevated levels, execution of the remaining refinancing plan is uncertain. Each 100bp increase in refinancing rates on $4B of debt adds $40M to annual interest expense, directly reducing FFO by $0.52/share.

### 2. Interest Rate Sensitivity
Despite 91% fixed/hedged rates, the refinancing of maturing debt at current rates (5.34% weighted average vs. the prevailing 4-5% range on new CRE CMBS) could keep interest expense elevated.

### 3. Remote Work / Office Demand Structural Shift
The 10-K explicitly flags remote work as a risk. Q1 2026 leasing data counters this concern for premium Manhattan office space. The risk is more relevant to SLG's lower-quality suburban portfolio (79.4% occupied vs. 94.4% Manhattan) and to alternative strategy properties (59.3% occupied).

### 4. Single-Market Concentration
Virtually the entire portfolio is in Manhattan. Any severe NYC-specific economic downturn, policy change (e.g., aggressive tax increases on commercial real estate), or disruptive event would disproportionately impact SLG. Local Law 97 climate compliance is expected to be manageable through 2029 but could become material thereafter.

### 5. Off-Balance Sheet JV Exposure
The $5.9B proportionate share of JV debt is larger than consolidated debt. Several JV properties have experienced impairments (5 Times Square, Worldwide Plaza, 2 Herald Square), and alternative strategy JV properties have low occupancy (59.3%). SLG has limited control over capital calls and decision-making in these structures.

### 6. Macro Environment
The current macro regime — elevated uncertainty with rates likely higher for longer — is unfavorable for leveraged real estate. If credit conditions tighten further due to geopolitical developments, SLG's refinancing costs increase directly. The dual threats of inflation (raising office operating costs and real estate taxes) and potential recession (reducing tenant demand) create a stagflationary risk for the company.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
SLG's operating business is performing at peak levels. Record leasing, +16.1% mark-to-market spreads, and One Madison at 100% leased all validate the flight-to-quality thesis for premium Manhattan office. AI companies represent a secular new demand driver. If management successfully executes the remaining $2.4-3.4B of the refinancing plan — early 2026 results suggest they can — balance sheet risk de-escalates rapidly. FFO stabilizes at $4.50-5.00, the stock re-rates to 11-12x P/FFO ($50-60), and the 5-7% dividend yield provides income while waiting. The 7 Dey Street sale and potential further dispositions provide incremental liquidity. Scotiabank's $51 PT and Deutsche Bank's recent upgrade to Buy reflect this view.

### Bear Case
The remaining refinancing is occurring during an environment of tightening CRE credit, elevated geopolitical risk, and potential rate hikes. If credit spreads widen materially, SLG's refinancing costs could increase by 100-200bp over current levels, adding $40-80M in annual interest expense and reducing FFO by $0.52-1.04/share. FFO guidance of $4.40-4.70 already shows limited cushion; a meaningful increase in financing costs could push FFO below $4.00. Same-store operating income was flat despite 6.8% same-store revenue growth, indicating operating cost pressures (particularly real estate taxes) are consuming the benefits of higher occupancy. The $741.9M accumulated deficit leaves minimal margin for error. A recession would reduce leasing velocity and pressure occupancy precisely when refinancing needs are greatest.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. Completion of ≥80% of the $7B financing plan at rates within 50bp of the One Madison CMBS rate (5.81%), demonstrating continued lender appetite for SLG's assets
2. Q2/Q3 2026 FFO tracking at or above the $4.55 midpoint guidance, with same-store NOI growth exceeding 3%
3. Stock price declining to ≤$34 (7.5x FFO midpoint), where the risk/reward shifts meaningfully toward asymmetric upside

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. Any indication that upcoming refinancings are being delayed, repriced above 6.5%, or require additional equity dilution
2. Manhattan office occupancy declining below 92% for two consecutive quarters
3. FFO tracking below $4.00/share annualized

### Reassessment Timeline
Q2 2026 earnings (July/August 2026) will provide visibility into refinancing progress, leasing momentum sustainability, and updated full-year guidance. The 7 Dey Street disposition closing (expected Q2 2026) is an additional checkpoint.

## Conclusion

SL Green presents a genuinely difficult analytical call. The operating business is performing at historically strong levels across every measurable leasing metric, and the flight-to-quality thesis has moved from aspiration to demonstrated reality.

Against this, the balance sheet requires the execution of the largest financing program in the company's history during a period of macro uncertainty, tightening CRE credit, and potential rate increases. The $3.65B completed in March 2026 is meaningful progress, and management's track record provides some confidence. But $2.4-3.4B of remaining maturities in the next 18 months and rising interest expense leave insufficient margin of safety for a buy recommendation at current prices.

The stock is appropriately discounted for its risks but not cheap enough to compensate for the binary refinancing outcomes. HOLD, with close monitoring of Q2 2026 earnings and refinancing progress.
