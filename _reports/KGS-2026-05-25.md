## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services is the largest pure-play contract compression provider focused on large horsepower equipment in the U.S., operating 4.5 million fleet HP at 98% utilization concentrated in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale. The business model generates recurring, contracted revenue with 70.6% adjusted gross margins, insulated from commodity price volatility by fixed-fee structures with inflation escalators. Equipment lead times exceeding 180 weeks create a formidable barrier to entry that supports pricing power.

The strategic pivot into distributed power generation for data centers (via the $675M DPS acquisition closed April 2026) offers substantial TAM expansion — targeting 2 GW by decade-end — but introduces execution risk in an unproven vertical. Meanwhile, the stock has appreciated 115% over 12 months to $73.71, implying ~12x EV/2026E EBITDA, which prices in meaningful success from the power strategy without leaving adequate margin of safety given the DOJ/OFAC investigation, elevated leverage (>4x during investment cycle), and 8% short interest.

The analyst consensus is unanimously bullish (12 Buy/Strong Buy, zero Holds or Sells), with a median target of $82.50. Estimate revisions have turned negative over the past 30 days (-4.3% to -6.5% across periods) after a strong 90-day upward revision cycle, suggesting the initial post-earnings enthusiasm is moderating. This is a quality business at a full valuation — worth holding for the growth trajectory but not offering sufficient asymmetry for new capital at current levels.

## Company Overview

Kodiak Gas Services operates critical midstream infrastructure — natural gas compression equipment deployed under multi-year, fixed-fee contracts to upstream and midstream producers. Without compression, wells cannot produce; this makes KGS's services non-discretionary for its customer base.

**Business Segments:**
- **Compression Infrastructure** (~90% of revenue): Company-owned compression units deployed under 1-7 year contracts (typically 3-5 years for large HP) with annual inflation adjustments
- **Power Infrastructure** (new, ~7% of 2026E revenue): Distributed power generation for data centers and industrial customers, created via DPS acquisition
- **Other Services** (~10%): Station construction, maintenance, parts

**Key Metrics (Q1 2026):**
- Fleet: ~4.5 million HP, 4,736 units
- Utilization: 98.0% (record)
- Revenue-generating HP/unit: 977 (upshift toward larger equipment)
- Geographic concentration: 82.8% Permian + Eagle Ford
- Top 4 customers: ~32% of revenue (all investment-grade)

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

Revenue grew from $850M (2023) to $1.31B (2025), driven by the 2024 CSI acquisition and subsequent organic price increases. Q1 2026 revenue was $346M (+4.9% YoY), with the new power segment beginning contributions.

The margin profile is exceptional for an energy services business:
- Contract Services Adjusted Gross Margin: 70.6% (Q1 2026, new record)
- Adjusted EBITDA Margin: 55.0% (Q1 2026)
- Operating Margin: 26.0% (2025, up from 21.5% in 2024)

Net income is suppressed by ~$198M in annual interest expense on $2.6B of debt and $62M in asset sale losses, yielding only 6.2% net margins. Adjusted EPS was $0.59 in Q1 2026 vs. $0.54 estimate (+10.9% beat).

### Cash Flow Generation

Operating cash flow of $600M in 2025 (up 83% YoY) demonstrates the business model's cash conversion capability. Discretionary cash flow — the metric management uses to fund dividends and growth — was $462M in 2025 and guided to $520-$570M for 2026. This supports the $1.96 annualized dividend while funding growth capex.

### Capital Structure

Total debt of $2.79B (Q1 2026) produces a D/E ratio of 2.58x and credit agreement leverage of ~3.6x (vs. 5.25x covenant max). The $400-$500M power infrastructure capex in 2026 will push leverage above 4x temporarily.

Debt is well-laddered: $750M (2029), $465M (2030), $770M (2033), $630M (2035). The recent $1B refinancing reduced weighted average borrowing cost. Available liquidity is $1.5B on the ABL facility.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.17 is misleading for this business — it was designed for manufacturing companies, not contracted infrastructure operators with 98% utilization and $600M OCF. Covenant compliance is comfortable.

### Capital Allocation

Management guides $690-$830M total capex in 2026 ($245-$275M compression + $400-$500M power + $80-$90M maintenance). The $104M in share repurchases during 2025 (2.7M shares from EQT affiliates) was opportunistic. The 2.7% dividend yield provides a modest income component.

## Growth Analysis

### Compression Infrastructure (Core Business)

Growth here is driven by: (1) Permian Basin production increases supporting LNG export capacity additions, (2) pricing power from 180+ week equipment lead times, (3) continued outsourcing trend from E&P companies seeking capital-light models, and (4) anti-flaring regulations increasing compression demand. The 98% utilization rate implies minimal spare capacity — new unit deployments drive incremental revenue.

Management guided compression revenue of $1.25-$1.28B for 2026, implying low-to-mid single digit organic growth after normalizing for the power segment separation.

### Power Infrastructure (Growth Optionality)

The DPS acquisition positions KGS in the rapidly growing distributed power market, targeting data centers that cannot wait 3-5 years for utility interconnection. Management targets 2 GW of capacity by decade-end (300-500 MW/year additions from current ~260 MW base). At $1.1-$1.5M/MW installed cost with 60-70% targeted gross margins, this could become a $300-$500M revenue segment by 2028-2029.

However, this is entirely unproven at scale. Competition from utilities, Enchanted Rock, and others is intensifying. The capital intensity is enormous — $400-$500M in 2026 alone for power — and returns are uncertain until contracts are signed and facilities commissioned.

### Estimate Revision Trajectory

The 90-day revision trend was strongly positive (+44.4% for current quarter, +21.2% for FY2026). The 30-day trend has turned negative (-4.3% to -6.5% across periods), indicating the initial upward revision cycle has peaked. This shift from positive to negative momentum is a caution signal.

- **P/DCF:** ~13.4x ($545M midpoint discretionary cash flow)
- **Trailing P/E:** 97.4x (distorted by non-cash charges)
- **Forward P/E (FY26E $2.58):** 28.6x
- **Dividend Yield:** 2.7%

KGS's premium to AROC reflects the power growth optionality and superior margin profile (70.6% vs. AROC's lower margins). At 12x EV/EBITDA, KGS trades in line with infrastructure comps but above traditional energy services multiples.

**The Market is Pricing Growth:** At 28.6x forward P/E on $2.58 FY26E EPS, the stock prices meaningful success from the power infrastructure strategy. If power execution disappoints or the compression market weakens, there is limited multiple support below current levels. The analyst consensus target of $82.50 (12% upside) is modest for a 12-Buy unanimous rating — suggesting analysts are updating targets to current trading levels rather than identifying substantial remaining upside.

The 90-day estimate revision of +44% on current quarter meets the threshold for cycle-adjusted valuation treatment. However, at 28.6x forward P/E, the stock has already re-rated to reflect this improvement. The positive revision cycle is now reversing (30-day trend negative), limiting further multiple expansion probability.

## Competitive Landscape

KGS holds a strong competitive position driven by:

1. **Equipment scarcity moat:** 180+ week lead times for large HP compression equipment make fleet expansion nearly impossible for competitors. Existing operators have enormous pricing power, evidenced by 70.6% gross margins.

2. **Scale advantages:** 4.5M HP fleet enables operational efficiencies, supplier pricing leverage, and ability to serve large E&P companies requiring hundreds of units.

3. **Geographic dominance:** 82.8% Permian/Eagle Ford concentration in the highest-growth U.S. basins creates proximity advantages and customer relationships.

4. **Contract structure:** Fixed-fee, multi-year contracts with inflation escalators provide revenue visibility. 97.7-98.0% utilization demonstrates demand exceeds supply.

5. **Power infrastructure first-mover:** The DPS acquisition positions KGS among the first movers in distributed power for data centers, though this advantage is time-limited as others enter.

The competitive position is improving (trajectory from 7.0 to 8.0 over prior reports) as equipment scarcity intensifies and the power strategy differentiates from pure compression peers.

## Risk Assessment

### DOJ/OFAC Investigation (Material, Unquantifiable)
KGS discovered payments to persons associated with designated Foreign Terrorist Organizations at its Mexico operations (acquired via CSI). Self-reported to DOJ, OFAC, and SEC. While described as "not material" in dollar terms and Mexico operations were divested in September 2025, criminal or civil penalties remain possible. This is an active federal investigation with uncertain timeline and outcome — the market appears to be under-weighting this risk.

### Leverage During Power Investment Cycle
Credit agreement leverage will exceed 4x during 2026-2027 as power infrastructure capex is deployed. While covenants allow up to 5.25x and cash flows are strong, any compression market downturn during this investment period could create stress. Interest expense of ~$200M/year consumes ~28% of Adjusted EBITDA.

### Power Strategy Execution Risk
The power strategy is unproven at the scale management targets (2 GW by decade-end). Data center customers are demanding, utilities may compete, and the regulatory environment for distributed generation varies by jurisdiction. The 60-70% gross margin target is a wide range with limited historical data.

### Geographic Concentration
82.8% exposure to Permian Basin and Eagle Ford creates basin-specific risk. Pipeline capacity constraints, regulatory changes (e.g., flaring limits cutting both ways), or production slowdowns in these specific basins would disproportionately impact KGS.

### Texas Sales Tax Contingency
$102.3M accrued for sales/use tax audits with additional open periods under review. While partially reserved, incremental exposure exists.

### Options Market Signal
Options volume is extremely thin (93 total contracts across 3 expirations), making sentiment signals unreliable. The near-zero put/call OI ratio (0.00) and minimal put volume (P/C ratio 0.03) suggest institutional hedging activity is negligible — consistent with either a conviction bull case or simply illiquid options markets for a $7B mid-cap. The 8% short interest with 4.8 days to cover is moderately elevated, suggesting some investors are actively betting against the stock at current levels.

## Conclusion

Kodiak Gas Services operates a high-quality infrastructure business with structural competitive advantages — 98% utilization, 70%+ gross margins, and 180-week equipment lead times that effectively preclude new competition. The power infrastructure strategy offers genuine TAM expansion into the data center buildout theme.

At $73.71 the stock has appreciated 115% over 12 months and trades at 28.6x forward P/E and 12x EV/EBITDA — multiples that price in meaningful power strategy success without providing adequate margin of safety for the DOJ investigation, execution risk on an unproven vertical, and leverage that will exceed 4x during the investment cycle. Estimate revisions have turned negative over the past 30 days, and 8% short interest indicates informed skepticism at these levels.

The correct posture is to hold existing positions for the growth trajectory while awaiting either a valuation pullback to the $62 range or concrete evidence of power strategy success to justify adding exposure. The unanimous analyst consensus (12 Buy, zero Holds) with a modest $82.50 median target suggests limited near-term catalyst for further re-rating.
