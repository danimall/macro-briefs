## Executive Summary

ONEOK has transformed itself from a pure-play NGL midstream operator into one of North America's largest integrated midstream infrastructure companies through $30B+ in acquisitions over two years (Magellan, EnLink, Medallion). The result is a 60,000-mile pipeline network generating ~$8B in adjusted EBITDA, with approximately 90% fee-based earnings. Q1 2026 results demonstrated operational execution: EBITDA grew 13% YoY, management raised full-year guidance, and the Natural Gas Pipelines segment posted 60% EBITDA growth on favorable Waha-Katy spreads.

The core tension is between strong operational performance and an over-leveraged balance sheet. Total debt stands at $34B (4.2x EBITDA), interest expense more than doubled from 2023 to 2025 to $1.78B, and the Altman Z-Score sits in the distress zone at 1.43. While BBB/Baa2 credit ratings remain stable, ONEOK has minimal financial flexibility — $78M cash, reliance on a $3.5B revolver, and competing demands between growth capex ($2.7-3.2B guided), dividends ($2.6B), and deleveraging. At 16x forward earnings with a 4.7% yield, the stock offers adequate income compensation for holding through the integration and deleveraging period, but limited upside catalyst until leverage declines meaningfully.

## Company Overview

ONEOK operates across four segments following its acquisition-driven transformation:

- **Natural Gas Liquids** ($2.78B Adj. EBITDA, 2025): Largest segment. Gathering, fractionation (1.2 MMBbl/d capacity), storage (40 MMBbl), and distribution of NGLs.
- **Refined Products & Crude** ($2.18B): Gained via Magellan acquisition. 9,800 miles of refined products pipelines, crude gathering/transportation, 53 terminals.
- **Natural Gas Gathering & Processing** ($2.14B): Wellhead gathering and processing (7.2 Bcf/d capacity) across Williston, Anadarko, and Permian basins.
- **Natural Gas Pipelines** ($861M): 8,300 miles of intra/interstate pipelines, 74 Bcf storage. Benefits from Waha Hub dislocation.

Total assets grew from $24B (2022) to $67B (2025). Employee count reached 6,326. The company owns strategic infrastructure including the Henry Hub (NYMEX pricing point) and Cushing terminal.

At that pace, reducing leverage from 4.2x to a more comfortable 3.5x would take 3-4 years absent asset sales or equity issuance.

The Natural Gas Pipelines segment's 60% EBITDA growth ($70M benefit from Waha-Katy spreads) is a multi-year tailwind but not structural — it persists until new Permian takeaway capacity arrives (Eiger Express, mid-2028).

## Growth Analysis

ONEOK's growth comes from two sources:

**1. Volume Ramp on Existing/New Infrastructure:**
- Elk Creek pipeline expansion (435 MBbl/d) completed
- 150 MMcf/d processing plant relocation to Permian completed Q1 2026
- Greater Denver expansion (mid-2026)
- NGL raw feed throughput growing 15% YoY

**2. Growth Capital Program ($3B+ annually):**
- Bighorn plant ($365M, mid-2027)
- Medford fractionator rebuild ($485M, 2026-27)
- Texas City LPG export terminal with MPLX ($700M, early 2028)
- MBTC Pipeline ($280M, early 2028)
- Eiger Express Pipeline with consortium (mid-2028)

Consensus estimates project EPS growing from $5.75 (2026) to $6.24 (2027), representing ~8.5% growth. Estimate revisions are modestly positive over 30 days (+2.8% current quarter, +1.4% full year) but slightly negative over 90 days for the full year (-1.3%), suggesting improving near-term visibility offset by macro uncertainty.

The secular tailwinds are real: Permian production growth, LNG export demand, data center power demand, and NGL export growth all drive incremental volumes through ONEOK's integrated system. The 90% fee-based structure means this volume growth translates to earnings with limited commodity price risk.

OKE's discount to peers (16x vs. median 29.5x) is substantial, but reflects legitimate factors: higher leverage (4.2x vs. peers at 3.0-3.5x), integration complexity, diluted per-share growth, and lower organic growth rate. The discount is partially warranted rather than representing clear mispricing.

At current prices, the 4.7% dividend yield provides adequate income compensation, and 8-9% EPS growth plus yield suggests a total return potential of 12-14% annually if the multiple holds or modestly expands. However, multiple expansion requires demonstrated deleveraging, which will take years. The analyst consensus target of $94 (median) offers only 4% upside, suggesting the stock is fairly priced near term.

## Competitive Landscape

ONEOK's competitive position has improved materially through acquisition:

- **Scale:** 60,000 miles of pipeline, among the largest US midstream operators
- **Integration:** Wellhead-to-market capability across gas, NGLs, refined products, and crude
- **Basin diversification:** Permian, Williston, Anadarko, Gulf Coast exposure reduces single-basin risk
- **Strategic assets:** Henry Hub, Cushing terminal, LNG export connectivity, future LPG export terminal
- **Customer stickiness:** Long-term dedications across 3+ million acres, minimum volume commitments

The transformation from NGL-focused to fully diversified creates a competitive moat through integrated customer solutions — a producer can access gathering, processing, NGL fractionation, and now crude takeaway through a single counterparty. The Texas City LPG export terminal (2028) adds international market access.

Competitive position score reflects improvement from prior assessments. The Eiger Express consortium participation (with WhiteWater, MPLX, Enbridge) demonstrates industry recognition of ONEOK's Permian infrastructure importance.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Leverage/Refinancing (Highest Weight):** In a 4.4% 10Y environment with potential for rates to move higher, any credit downgrade from BBB would meaningfully increase borrowing costs and potentially trigger negative cycles with commercial paper access.

2. **Integration Execution:** Managing 6,326 employees across four recently acquired organizations while executing $3B+ annual capex is operationally complex. Cultural integration issues and key personnel departures remain risks.

3. **Volume Risk in Slowdown:** While fee-based, throughput volumes depend on upstream drilling activity. If oil prices decline, Permian/Williston drilling could slow, reducing ONEOK's volume growth trajectory within 6-9 months.

4. **Construction Cost Overruns:** The 10-K explicitly flags tariff-related material cost increases affecting the $3B+ annual capex program. Steel tariffs and supply chain disruptions could push projects over budget.

5. **Waha Spread Normalization:** The Pipelines segment outperformance is a windfall from Permian takeaway constraints. Eiger Express (mid-2028) and other pipes will partially normalize these spreads.

**Options Market Signal:**

Near-term IV at 30.0% sits 4.5pp above historical volatility (25.5%), with the term structure in backwardation (30.0% near-term vs. 25.8% 6-month). Put/call skew at 4.6% shows elevated put demand near-term. The dramatic IV compression from 109.3% to 30.0% since April 17 (coinciding with earnings resolution and 10% price appreciation) suggests the options market had priced significant uncertainty that has now partially resolved. The OI put/call ratio at 0.30 is low, indicating positioning is not structurally bearish. The backwardation likely reflects residual post-earnings repositioning and broader market vol regime rather than OKE-specific stress.

Short interest at 4.6% with 4.8 days to cover is moderate — enough to suggest some structural skeptics but not enough for squeeze dynamics.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-113):**
- Deleveraging proceeds faster than expected (asset sales, organic cash flow growth exceeding capex)
- Waha spread tailwinds persist through 2027-2028
- LPG export terminal drives re-rating as ONEOK is valued as integrated infrastructure
- Multiple expansion from 16x toward 20-22x on demonstrated integration success
- Elevated energy prices support robust Permian/Williston volume growth

**Bear Case ($72-78):**
- Credit downgrade from BBB to BBB- or below on persistent leverage / missed deleveraging targets
- Commodity price decline reduces upstream drilling, causing volume shortfalls
- Integration stumbles — cost overruns on major projects, key personnel departures
- Rising rates push interest expense above $2B, compressing distributable cash flow
- Dividend growth stalls as cash flow prioritizes debt service

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD (Income):**

- **Upgrade triggers to BUY:** (1) Debt/EBITDA declining below 3.8x for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating credible deleveraging path. (2) Stock pulls back to $78-82 range (yielding 5.2%+) on non-fundamental market stress, creating compelling entry. (3) Management announces meaningful asset sales or equity raise specifically earmarked for debt reduction.

- **Downgrade triggers to AVOID:** (1) Credit rating downgraded below BBB-/Baa3 by any agency. (2) Quarterly EBITDA misses guidance midpoint by >10%, suggesting integration problems. (3) Dividend coverage falls below 1.5x (distributable cash flow / dividends) for two consecutive quarters.

- **Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) — first full quarter reflecting guidance raise trajectory. Monitor BDC/credit market stress developments through May for any spillover to IG midstream spreads.

## Conclusion

ONEOK represents a viable income position generating 4.7% yield with moderate growth (8-9% EPS CAGR) supported by secular midstream demand tailwinds and demonstrated operational execution. The Q1 2026 beat and guidance raise confirm near-term trajectory. However, the $34B debt load constrains upside potential and amplifies downside risk in credit stress scenarios. At 16x forward earnings, the valuation discount to peers reflects real leverage risk rather than obvious mispricing.

The prior score trajectory decline (6.8 → 6.2) was driven primarily by balance sheet concerns and elevated risk scoring during a period of stock weakness. With the stock recovering 36% from its November trough and Q1 execution confirming the integration thesis, a modest score stabilization is warranted (6.12 overall). The income thesis holds — ONEOK pays investors adequately to wait through the deleveraging cycle — but a BUY upgrade requires either a significantly cheaper entry point or tangible balance sheet improvement.
