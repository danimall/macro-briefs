## Executive Summary

Steel Dynamics reported Q1 2026 operating income surging 96% YoY to $538M on record quarterly steel shipments of 3.6 million tons and a 30% expansion in metal spreads. The primary catalyst is US tariff policy that has compressed steel imports to multi-year lows, tightening domestic supply and lifting hot-rolled coil (HRC) prices meaningfully above 2025 levels. Forward consensus EPS estimates have risen 8-22% over the past 90 days across all periods, with FY2026E now at $15.04 and FY2027E at $17.16.

The challenge is valuation positioning. At $241.85, the stock trades at approximately 16x FY2026E earnings — a premium to its historical mid-cycle multiple of 8-12x, though defensible if tariff-driven supply tightness persists. The stock has appreciated 102% from its 52-week low of $119.89 and sits within 1% of its 52-week high. The aluminum flat-rolled mill ($4.1B in assets, generating a $65M operating loss in Q1 2026) represents both the largest risk and potential re-rating catalyst; management targets $650-700M through-cycle EBITDA from this segment by late 2026/2027, which would justify the current multiple.

The earnings recovery is genuine and tariff policy appears durable near-term. But the stock's run has captured most of the near-term upside to consensus targets (median $238, mean $228), and the binary risk around aluminum execution and trade policy sustainability warrants patience rather than chasing at current levels.

## Company Overview

Steel Dynamics is one of the largest domestic steel producers and metals recyclers in North America, operating seven electric arc furnace (EAF) steel mills with approximately 16 million tons of annual steelmaking capacity. The company's circular manufacturing model — recycled scrap to steel to fabricated products — gives it a structural cost advantage over blast furnace competitors and aligns with decarbonization trends.

**Segment Breakdown (FY2025):**
- Steel Operations (72% of revenue): Flat-roll, structural, rail, engineered bar, and merchant products
- Metals Recycling (11%): Omni Source supplies 65% of ferrous scrap to STLD's own mills
- Steel Fabrication (8%): Steel joists and deck with ~33% domestic market share
- Aluminum Operations (2%, growing): 650,000 metric ton recycled aluminum flat-roll mill in Columbus, Mississippi — still ramping

The company serves diversified end markets: non-residential construction, automotive, manufacturing, energy, and infrastructure. Approximately 70% of steel products are value-added (cold-rolled, coated, engineered), which reduces commodity price sensitivity relative to commodity hot-roll producers.

## Competitive Landscape

STLD occupies the most favorable structural position among domestic steel producers:

1. **EAF cost advantage:** Lower capital intensity, faster restart capability, significantly lower carbon emissions vs. blast furnace competitors (CLF). As carbon regulations tighten and ESG procurement requirements expand, this advantage widens.

2. **Vertical integration:** Omni Source provides 65% of ferrous scrap internally, creating a natural hedge against scrap price volatility and ensuring supply security.

3. **Value-added mix:** 70% of products are processed beyond commodity hot-roll, reducing price cyclicality relative to peers. Fabrication segment with 33% market share provides demand visibility.

4. **Aluminum diversification:** Unique among steel peers. If successful, creates exposure to the beverage can sheet and automotive markets, which have different cycle dynamics than steel.

5. **Geographic positioning:** Mills located near scrap sources and customers, reducing freight costs in an environment where logistics costs remain elevated.

**Competitive risks:** Nucor and other EAF producers are adding capacity, which could increase scrap competition and reduce domestic supply tightness even with tariffs in place. The tariff-driven competitive advantage is policy-dependent, not structural.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Policy Reversal (HIGH):** Current earnings power is materially dependent on 25% Section 232 tariffs. Any relaxation — through trade negotiations, exemptions, or circumvention — would rapidly compress domestic steel prices and STLD's margins. Downstream manufacturers (automakers, appliance makers) are actively lobbying against tariffs as their input costs rise.

**2. Aluminum Execution Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** Management targets break-even and then $650-700M EBITDA at full capacity, but operational pauses in Q1 2026 and customer concentration create meaningful execution risk. The segment represents 25% of total company assets earning a negative return.

**3. Cyclical Peak Timing:** The 2022-2025 earnings trajectory demonstrates how rapidly margins can compress (operating income dropped from $5.1B to $1.5B in three years). With the stock at all-time highs and a 30x trailing multiple, any disappointment could trigger a sharp rerating.

**4. New EAF Capacity:** Multiple competitors adding domestic EAF capacity will increase scrap demand (potentially raising STLD's primary input cost) and add supply (potentially pressuring selling prices), even in a tariff-protected environment.

**5. Working Capital Volatility:** In rising-price environments, working capital absorbs cash; in falling-price environments, it releases cash but signals deteriorating demand.

## Conclusion

Steel Dynamics is executing well in a favorable environment. The earnings recovery from 2025's trough is real, driven by tariff-induced supply tightening and infrastructure demand flowing through multiple segments. Estimate revisions are strongly positive, fabrication backlog provides demand visibility through Q3 2026, and the aggressive buyback program amplifies per-share earnings growth.

The constraint on upgrading to BUY is straightforward: the stock has appreciated 102% from its 52-week low and trades at all-time highs. Consensus targets cluster below the current price. The earnings recovery is genuine but heavily tariff-dependent, creating binary policy risk that the options market is pricing. The aluminum segment remains operationally challenged.

At 16x FY2026E forward earnings, STLD is not expensive for a recovering cyclical with structural advantages, but it is fully valued for the current consensus view. A pullback to $200-210 or positive aluminum profitability data would shift the risk/reward sufficiently to warrant a BUY upgrade. At current levels, the prudent positioning is to hold existing positions and await either valuation support or additional confirmation of earnings sustainability beyond the tariff tailwind.
