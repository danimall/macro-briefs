**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Acadian Asset Management is a pure-play systematic (quantitative) institutional asset manager that emerged from the 2025 simplification of BrightSphere Investment Group into its single remaining franchise. The company is in the middle of an exceptional operating run: assets under management reached $195.7B in Q1 2026, up 60.5% year-over-year, fueled by $21.4B of net client cash flows in a single quarter against $3.8B in the prior-year period. Management fees rose 41% to $159.3M, ENI diluted EPS rose 94% to $1.05, and the ENI operating margin expanded to 38.1% from 28.3%, demonstrating real operating leverage in the profit-sharing model. The balance sheet is clean (leverage of 1.0x, interest coverage of 46.2x, the $275M 4.80% notes redeemed early in December 2025) and the dividend was raised 10x to $0.10/quarter. Strategies representing 96% of revenue are outperforming benchmarks on 3-, 5-, and 10-year bases, which is the durable core of the moat.

The complication is that the quality of the AUM growth is uneven. The blended fee rate fell to 34.1 bps from 37.9 bps as the lower-fee Enhanced Equity strategy surged to 29.5% of AUM ($57.8B) from 22.5% ($40.0B) in one quarter, and the Wealth/Other channel leapt from $16.2B to $33.0B. Revenue grew 41% while AUM grew 60% because the marginal dollar earns materially less. The flows are real and Acadian is winning share, but each incremental AUM dollar carries a lower revenue-per-dollar yield, so AUM and revenue will continue to diverge if the mix shift persists.

The stock has roughly doubled over the past year to $70.38 and now trades near the published analyst range (mean ~$69, high ~$80), at roughly 17x annualized ENI run-rate earnings, which is not cheap for an asset manager facing active fee-mix compression and a known supply overhang. John Paulson's legacy position is being steadily monetized into strength (1.9M shares sold at $77.25 in June 2026, ~5.84M shares remaining), a structural ceiling until cleared. We rate AAMI a HOLD with a growth bias. The business is genuinely improving and the trajectory of estimate revisions supports continued upside, but the asymmetry that would justify a BUY at this price has largely been arbitraged away.

## Company Overview

Acadian Asset Management (NYSE: AAMI) is a Boston-based holding company whose sole operating business is its majority-owned subsidiary, Acadian Asset Management LLC. The entire enterprise is reported as a single segment, "Quant & Solutions." Acadian uses systematic/quantitative models to identify mispriced securities across Emerging Markets Equity, Non-U.S. Equity, Global Equity, Small Cap Equity, Enhanced Equity, Equity Extensions, Systematic Credit, and Alternatives.

The client base is predominantly institutional (74.8% of AUM in Q1 2026), with a fast-growing Wealth/Other channel and Sub-Advisory business. The company IPO'd out of Old Mutual plc in 2014 as BrightSphere Investment Group, ran a multi-affiliate model, and progressively divested down to the single Acadian franchise. It renamed and redomesticated to Acadian Asset Management Inc. effective January 1, 2025.

Acadian competes against far larger and better-capitalized asset managers. Its differentiation is a long, documented systematic-investing track record, with a 5-year revenue-weighted excess return of 4.1%. After a difficult multi-year stretch for quant strategies broadly, the systematic niche has come back into favor, which is reflected in record inflows.

## Financial Analysis

The Q1 2026 results are strong across nearly every line:

- **AUM: $195.7B**, +60.5% YoY (from $121.9B), +10.3% QoQ, driven by record net inflows and market appreciation.
- **Net client cash flows: +$21.4B** in Q1 2026 versus +$3.8B in Q1 2025. FY2025 gross sales were a record ~$39B.
- **Management fees: $159.3M (+41%); total revenue $167.0M (+39%).**
- **GAAP EPS: $0.68 (+26%); ENI diluted EPS: $1.05 (+94%).**
- **ENI operating margin: 38.1%**, up from 28.3% — the profit-sharing structure converts revenue growth into outsized margin expansion above thresholds.
- **Adjusted EBITDA: $61.8M** for the quarter, versus $35.2M a year earlier.
- **Leverage: 1.0x; interest coverage: 46.2x.**

The single most important nuance is fee-rate compression driven by mix, not price-cutting. Enhanced and extension products are where the flows are, but they earn materially less per dollar. The mechanism: lower-fee Enhanced/Wealth flows grow AUM faster than revenue → blended fee rate falls → revenue compounds at a discount to AUM. Investors should track the spread between AUM growth and revenue growth quarter over quarter as the key tell.

A standardized cash-flow screen will misrepresent this business. GAAP operating cash flow was -$44.3M in Q1 2026, but this is a seasonal artifact: Q1 is when the prior year's accrued incentive compensation is paid out (accrued incentive comp fell from $129.9M to $47.4M). The business is genuinely cash-generative; Adjusted EBITDA of ~$62M/quarter and 1.0x leverage are the accurate signal. We do not penalize the FCF optics.

On capital allocation, management is pivoting from balance-sheet repair to shareholder returns. The company redeemed all $275M of 4.80% Senior Notes (due July 2026) on December 1, 2025, refinancing with a $200M delayed-draw term loan plus a $175M revolver at SOFR+1.5–2.0%, cutting quarterly interest expense to $3.4M from $4.8M. The $80M buyback (authorized Feb 2025) has slowed sharply — only $4.7M repurchased in Q1 2026 with ~$27.3M remaining — a rational response to the stock's run, though it removes a price support.

Financial health scores a 7.0. The downside filter is benign: low leverage, high coverage, real cash generation, and active deleveraging. The cap on the score reflects the inherent earnings volatility of the profit-sharing model (non-cash revaluations swung by $16.4M in a quarter; key-employee distributions rose 103% YoY) and the structural sensitivity of revenue to markets and FX.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the strongest dimension here, and it is corroborated by multiple independent signals. The Wealth/Other channel's +$17B net inflow shows the flows are not the product of a single lumpy mandate. Estimate revisions confirm the momentum: current-year consensus rose +9.6% over 90 days to $4.79, next-year +6.9% to $5.86, with all four tracked periods revising upward. The earnings beat record (Q1 2026 +16.1%, Q2 2025 +18.4%, one in-line, one miss) supports confidence in forward estimates.

The growth durability rests on three legs: the systematic niche is back in favor after years of underperformance; Acadian's 3-to-10-year track record gives institutional allocators a defensible reason to fund it; and the firm has demonstrated it can scale lower-fee Enhanced/extension products that capture share precisely because they are cheaper systematic beta-plus. Operating leverage is the kicker, with ENI margin expanding nearly 10 points YoY as incremental revenue drops through at high rates above the profit-sharing thresholds.

The qualification, which is why this scores 7.5 rather than higher, is the AUM-to-revenue divergence. Growth in dollars under management is strong, but the revenue translation is diluting. If Enhanced flows decelerate or reverse, or if a stronger dollar shrinks the ~70% of AUM denominated in non-USD currencies, the revenue line decelerates faster than the AUM line. We treat growth as the most reliable winner-selection signal and weight it accordingly, but the mix-shift mechanics keep this short of a top-tier score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $70.38, AAMI trades at roughly 31x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.21) and approximately 17x an annualized ENI run-rate (~$1.05 × 4 ≈ $4.20, noting Q1 is seasonally strong). On forward consensus, the stock is near 15x current-year ENI ($4.79) and ~12x next-year ($5.86). Against the asset-management peer set, the picture is mixed: peer median P/E is ~25.2x (APAM at 8.9x, HLNE 13.3x, CNS 25.2x, WT 40.7x), so AAMI does not screen as obviously expensive on a forward ENI basis, but the dividend yield of ~0.5% is far below the 3.1% peer median, removing yield support.

The reasons we do not award a high valuation score are specific rather than reflexive. First, the stock trades near the upper part of the published analyst range (mean ~$69, median ~$66, high ~$80); the consensus has chased the price upward from ~$44 in October 2025 to ~$67 in May 2026, and the current price sits close to where the sell-side has caught up. While analyst targets often lag fast-moving stocks, here the magnitude of the prior re-rating and the fee-mix headwind temper that argument. Second, the headline growth is partly diluted by fee compression, so the forward multiple should be assessed against revenue that grows slower than AUM. Third, a persistent supply overhang (Paulson) caps near-term re-rating regardless of fundamentals.

We are explicitly skeptical of treating a low-ish forward multiple as a buy signal in isolation; cheapness without an independent re-rating catalyst has historically been a poor predictor. The closest thing to a catalyst is the May 2026 Investor Forum's "Next Phase of Growth" framing and continued estimate revisions, but neither overcomes the overhang and mix-shift dilution at the current price. Valuation scores 4.5: not a value trap, but the asymmetry has compressed after the double, and the multiple no longer offers a margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Acadian's moat is its long-duration systematic track record. Having 96% of revenue beating benchmarks across 3-, 5-, and 10-year windows with a 4.1% 5-year revenue-weighted excess return is a credible, hard-to-replicate edge that drives institutional re-allocation and gives the firm pricing power in higher-fee strategies even as it scales lower-fee ones. The firm is gaining share, which matters more than its absolute size relative to giants.

The competitive trajectory is improving but not unambiguously so. On the positive side, Acadian is capturing flows across both ends of its product spectrum and is positioned to benefit from advances in ML/AI for signal generation, a natural fit for a systematic shop. On the cautionary side, industry-wide fee pressure is real and Acadian's own mix shift amplifies its exposure: the firm is winning by offering cheaper systematic products, which is share-accretive but margin-dilutive at the blended level. The 10-K flags AI as both a competitive necessity and a new risk vector, meaning the same technology that powers Acadian's edge lowers barriers for competitors. Single-strategy-family concentration (five strategies = ~46% of AUM, led by Emerging Markets Equity at ~$21B) means a relative-performance stumble in one large strategy would disproportionately hit asset-weighted performance and flows. We score competitive position 7.0, slightly below the prior 7.5, reflecting that the mix-driven fee dilution is now an observable headwind to the franchise's revenue quality rather than a hypothetical.

## Risk Assessment

The risk set is stable per the Q1 2026 10-Q (no material changes to risk factors), but several items are specific and not fully priced:

1. **Paulson supply overhang.** John Paulson's firm continues to monetize its legacy ~21.8% stake, selling 1.21M shares at $47.69 (Sept 2025) and 1.9M shares at $77.25 (June 2026), leaving ~5.84M shares. Selling into strength at progressively higher prices is rational, but it is a persistent structural ceiling on the share price that the company itself flags as a risk. Paulson retains a board appointment right while holding ≥7%.

2. **Fee-mix compression is materializing now.** The blended fee rate has already fallen ~3.8 bps in a year. This is no longer a hypothetical risk factor; it is actively widening the AUM-revenue gap.

3. **Heavy non-USD/FX and EM exposure.** ~70% of AUM is non-USD-denominated and EMEA AUM jumped to 28.2% of the total. A stronger dollar mechanically reduces USD AUM and fees, and EM concentration adds political/liquidity risk.

4. **Profit-sharing earnings volatility.** Key-employee equity is revalued each period, and distributions rise faster than earnings above thresholds (+103% YoY).

5. **Client termination/concentration.** Advisory contracts terminate on ~30 days' notice; top 25 clients = ~33% of run-rate fees; change of control could trigger anti-assignment provisions.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing near-term caution. ATM IV has risen sharply to 46.5% from 32.9% over the past month (price -3.9% over the same window), the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 46.5% > long-term 39.6%), and put/call skew is elevated at 33.7% with an implied move of ±9.7% into the nearest expiry. This combination of rising IV, backwardation, and elevated put demand indicates the market is pricing imminent stress or a near-term catalyst, consistent with the Paulson selling pressure and the proximity to the 52-week high. IV is rich versus 1-year HV (46.5% vs 41.6%), so options are not a cheap hedge. We do not let this override the fundamental read, but it reinforces a wait-for-better-entry posture and argues against adding aggressively here.

Short interest is modest at 4.6% of float (2.5 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic; the bearish positioning is expressed through puts rather than borrow.

Risk factors score 4.5. The Paulson overhang, FX/EM sensitivity, fee-mix dilution, and the elevated near-term options stress are specific, identifiable, and not fully reflected at a price near all-time highs.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($90+)
The systematic niche stays in favor, Acadian compounds AUM through both high- and low-fee channels, and operating leverage keeps ENI margins near or above 38%. Estimate revisions continue upward, the Paulson position clears within 12–18 months removing the overhang, and the market re-rates the stock toward the peer median forward multiple on a clean float. ENI run-rate moves toward $5.50–$6.00 and the stock approaches the high analyst target ($80) and beyond as the franchise demonstrates durable growth. This requires the dollar to stay soft (supportive for ~70% non-USD AUM) and no large-strategy performance stumble.

### Bear Case (~$50, -29%)
Enhanced Equity flows decelerate or reverse, the AUM-revenue divergence widens, and a stronger dollar shrinks USD AUM and fees. A relative-performance stumble in Emerging Markets Equity or another large strategy triggers outflows, the profit-sharing model amplifies the earnings hit on the downside, and the Paulson overhang weighs on the share price into any market weakness. The forward multiple compresses toward the lower end of the peer range (APAM at 8.9x, HLNE at 13.3x) on decelerating revenue, taking the stock back toward the low-$50s where it traded for much of late 2025/early 2026.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August 2026), with the FX/EM environment and Paulson Form 4 filings as ongoing monitoring inputs.**

This is the third HOLD in a rising-score sequence (6.0 → 6.5, now 6.0). The improving trajectory is confirmed by the fundamentals — AUM, flows, margins, and estimate revisions are all genuinely better — but the stock has roughly doubled and the valuation/overhang constraints have reasserted themselves at the current price, which is why we hold the overall score flat rather than continuing to lift it. The price decline from the mid-$80s high to $70.38 does not by itself weaken the thesis, but it coincides with the largest Paulson sale yet and rising put demand, so it reflects identifiable near-term pressure rather than pure noise.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Pullback toward the low-$60s that restores forward asymmetry (sub-13x next-year ENI) while estimate revisions remain positive.
- Evidence the blended fee rate has stabilized (AUM-revenue growth gap narrowing for two consecutive quarters).
- Confirmation the Paulson position is substantially cleared, removing the supply ceiling.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Net flows turn negative for two consecutive quarters, or Enhanced Equity AUM contracts materially.
- ENI operating margin compresses below ~30% on fee-mix dilution or a strategy-performance stumble.
- A relative-performance breakdown in Emerging Markets Equity or another top-5 strategy that drives accelerating outflows.

## Conclusion

Acadian is a high-quality systematic asset manager firing on its operating cylinders: record inflows, 60% AUM growth, ENI margins expanding to 38%, a deleveraged balance sheet, a 10x dividend increase, and rising estimates. The growth is durable enough — backed by a long outperformance track record and a niche back in favor — to justify holding for further appreciation. What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a price that has doubled and now sits near the upper end of the published analyst range, a fee-rate mix shift that is actively diluting revenue per AUM dollar, a persistent Paulson supply overhang, and an options market pricing near-term stress. The risk/reward is more balanced than the headline growth implies. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$60s, on confirmation that the fee rate is stabilizing, or on the clearing of the Paulson position. Until then, hold for the growth trajectory while watching the AUM-revenue gap closely.
