**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lamar Advertising is one of the largest out-of-home (OOH) advertising operators in North America, with ~360,800 displays including the largest U.S. digital billboard network (~5,600 digital faces) and a dominant logo-sign franchise (24 of 28 privatized state contracts). The investment case rests on a regulatory-protected, locally-dominant billboard portfolio that throws off high-margin, GDP-correlated cash flow, converted into a tax-efficient REIT distribution. Q1 2026 delivered the clearest operating acceleration in several quarters: net revenue +4.5% (organic +3.9%), Adjusted EBITDA +7.7%, and diluted AFFO/share +7.5% to $1.72, with management pacing full-year AFFO/share guidance of $8.50–$8.70 toward the top end on strengthening national demand.

The fundamentals are improving, but the price has moved with them. At $150.48, the stock has appreciated ~25% over the trailing year and sits near its 52-week high of $158.69, trading at roughly 17.5x the midpoint of 2026 AFFO/share guidance. That multiple is reasonable for a stable REIT compounding AFFO at mid-to-high single digits, but it does not embed a material discount, and the consensus mean target of $155.60 (median $153.00) implies only modest upside from here with the low target at $145.00. The score trajectory has improved (5.6 → 6.2), driven by financial-health and competitive-position upgrades that the Q1 print confirms.

We rate LAMR a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The quality is real and the AFFO momentum is durable, but the asymmetry required for a higher-conviction call is absent at this entry point. Structural leverage (~$3.5B debt), a springing-maturity watch item tied to the February 2028 notes, dual-class voting control concentrating ~63% of votes in the Reilly family on ~15% economic ownership, and the CFO's complete divestiture of his direct stake near highs all temper enthusiasm. We would become a buyer on a pullback toward the low-$130s, where the AFFO multiple compresses to a more attractive ~15x.

## Company Overview

Lamar has operated under its name since 1902, went public in 1996, converted to a REIT in 2014, and adopted an UPREIT structure in 2022 (enabling tax-deferred property contributions for operating-partnership units — the mechanism behind the recent Verde Outdoor acquisition). The asset base as of year-end 2025: ~159,300 billboard displays across 45 states and Canada (~5,500+ digital), ~144,400 logo displays as the largest U.S. logo-sign provider, and ~40,600 transit displays across 80+ markets.

The FY2025 revenue mix ($2.27B total) was billboard $2.01B (89%, split static $1.38B / digital $632M), transit $163M (7%), and logo $89M (4%). Digital economics are the differentiator: ~5,500 digital displays (3.5% of billboard count) generate roughly a third of billboard revenue, reflecting multiple-advertiser rotation and higher revenue per face.

The competitive positioning centers on small-to-mid-size markets where Lamar holds dominant local share, sidestepping head-to-head intensity with Clear Channel Outdoor and Outfront Media in the largest metros. Approximately 79% of advertising is local, above the industry average, providing pricing control and revenue stability. The Highway Beautification Act and state/local sign controls create high barriers to new billboard permits, making existing inventory scarce. Tenant diversification is strong, with no single advertiser exceeding 2% of billboard revenue.

FY2025 revenue rose 2.7% reported (+2.1% acquisition-adjusted), confirming a mature, low-single-digit organic grower with transit declining (–$3.7M). Margins are strong and stable: operating margin 34.2%, net margin 26.2%, Adjusted EBITDA margin ~47%, with direct advertising expense steady at 33% of revenue.

The headline +63% net income jump in 2025 is largely non-operating and should not be extrapolated. Two distortions account for it: a $75.9M gain on dispositions (~$67.7M from the February 2025 sale of Lamar's minority Vistar Media stake) and a $136.6M YoY decline in D&A driven by a 2024 revision to asset-retirement-obligation cost estimates that had depressed the 2024 comparison. The cleaner growth measure is AFFO +3.4% for FY2025, accelerating to +8.0% in Q1 2026 — that acceleration, attributed to strengthening national demand, is the genuine positive signal.

Cash flow quality is high. The REIT model converts ~38% of revenue to AFFO. Capex is light at ~$181M (~8% of revenue), heavily skewed to digital conversion ($91M in 2025), with 2026 capex guided to ~$186M. The balance sheet carries ~$3.42B total debt at year-end 2025, rising to ~$3.50B at Q1 2026, with D/E ~0.85. The Altman Z of 2.74 ("grey zone") and current ratio of 0.58 reflect the structurally levered, working-capital-deficit REIT model rather than distress — the fully-drawn $250M A/R securitization program (maturing Oct 2027) and $232.5M current operating-lease liabilities explain the sub-1.0 current ratio. Negative retained earnings (–$1.1B) are a normal function of REIT distributions exceeding GAAP earnings.

Interest-rate sensitivity is moderate. Most debt is fixed-rate senior notes (3.625%–5.375%); variable exposure comes via the Term B loans (SOFR+1.50%) and A/R securitization (~4.7%). Interest expense actually fell $11.3M in 2025 on lower rates and Term A repayment. Capital allocation follows a disciplined playbook: FY2025 dividends of $655.9M ($6.45/sh) against AFFO of $847M for a ~77% payout ratio, leaving cushion; $150M of buybacks (1.39M shares) with authorization raised to $400M; and equity-funded bolt-on M&A. The 2026 dividend is reaffirmed at ≥$6.40/sh ($1.60/quarter declared).

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: Q1 2026 beat by 18.2% ($1.00 vs. $0.85), but the prior two quarters missed (–9.7% and –11.2%), with one beat (+2.4%) before that. Two beats and two misses across four quarters argues for measured confidence in consensus rather than strong conviction in upside.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is steady rather than dynamic. Organic billboard revenue growth of ~2.1% in 2025 reflects the GDP-correlated nature of OOH spending. The structural growth levers are digital conversion (higher revenue per face, multiple advertisers per board) and programmatic OOH, which is currently only ~2% of the outdoor business but a stated growth vector.

The industry backdrop is supportive. Per the OAAA, U.S. OOH reached a record Q1 2026 of $2.12B, extending growth to 20 consecutive quarters, with AI companies emerging as a new advertiser category. OOH continues to take share as a resilient, ad-blocker-proof medium. Lamar outperformed the industry over the three months to mid-May 2026, gaining +20.3% versus the industry's +2.8%.

Estimate revisions are mildly constructive. Current-quarter estimates rose +2.5% over 30 days, next-quarter +1.9%, and next-year +5.0% over 30 days — though current-year estimates drifted –0.8% over 30 days and most revisions remain slightly negative on a 90-day basis. The overall tone is "broadly rising," consistent with the Q1 acceleration, but the magnitudes are mid-single-digit at most, well short of the velocity that would constitute a strong inflection signal. This is a mid-single-digit AFFO grower, and the revisions confirm that, no more.

Management's bullish tone is the most important forward signal: CEO Sean Reilly cited "strong demand from local and particularly national customers… pacing to the top end, if not above" the $8.50–$8.70 2026 AFFO/share guidance. National strengthening is the swing factor; if it persists through the back half, AFFO/share growth lands closer to high single digits and supports gradual re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $150.48, Lamar trades at ~27.8x trailing earnings (distorted by REIT depreciation), but the relevant metric is P/AFFO. Against the $8.50–$8.70 2026 AFFO/share guidance midpoint of $8.60, the stock trades at ~17.5x P/AFFO — reasonable for a stable REIT growing AFFO mid-to-high single digits, but not a discount. The 4.2% dividend yield is well-covered at a ~77% AFFO payout ratio.

Peer comparison within Other Specialized REITs shows a heterogeneous group with a median P/E of 17.6 and median yield of 5.9%. The cleanest comparable is Outfront Media (OUT), the other pure-play OOH operator, at 30.5x P/E and a 3.7% yield — Lamar screens favorably against OUT on both quality (higher margins, lower volatility, small-market dominance) and balance-sheet conservatism. Against the broader specialized-REIT set, Lamar's yield (4.2%) sits below the median, reflecting its growth premium and superior cash-flow profile; that is a defensible relative position but not evidence of mispricing.

The analyst range frames the limited near-term upside: mean target $155.60 (+3.4%), median $153.00 (+1.7%), high $170.00 (+13%), low $145.00 (–3.6%). The stock is trading slightly below the mean and above the median, with the high target requiring AFFO growth to exceed guidance and a multiple expansion to ~19–20x. For a stable, mature REIT trading near its 52-week high, treating the consensus as a floor would be inappropriate — at this multiple, beats are largely expected and the re-rating optionality is modest. The asymmetry is balanced: ~13% to the bull target against a clear path to the low-$130s (~15x AFFO) if national demand normalizes or rates back up. We assign a Valuation score of 5.5, reflecting fair value with no embedded margin of safety at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Lamar's competitive position is strong and trajectory-stable to improving. The regulatory moat is the core asset: permit barriers under the Highway Beautification Act make existing billboard inventory scarce and effectively irreplaceable. The small-market focus and ~79% local revenue mix differentiate Lamar from Clear Channel Outdoor (more international, large-metro, more levered) and Outfront Media (transit and large-metro heavy), and have historically delivered superior margins and lower volatility.

The digital and programmatic shift is the durable competitive vector. Lamar operates the largest U.S. digital network and is methodically converting static to digital, capturing higher revenue per face. Programmatic OOH at ~2% of the business offers a multi-year runway. The logo-sign franchise (24 of 28 privatized state contracts) is a near-monopoly niche, though 7 of 25 contracts renew in 2026, creating modest renewal risk on ~4% of revenue.

The industry remains fragmented, supporting Lamar's bolt-on acquisition engine — the Verde Outdoor deal (>1,500 faces across 10 states, $147.6M paid in Lamar LP units) validated the UPREIT structure as a tax-efficient M&A tool. The 10-K candidly notes the pool of suitable targets is dwindling, with rising competition from PE and infrastructure funds and antitrust scrutiny on larger deals, which caps the long-term M&A growth lever. On balance, Lamar is widening its lead in the segments it dominates while facing a maturing acquisition runway — a 7.5 competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and refinancing dependence.** ~$3.5B debt against an obligation to distribute ~90%+ of taxable income means Lamar cannot retain earnings and must refinance maturities with new debt or equity. A springing-maturity provision on the revolver tied to the 3.75% Notes due February 2028 is the key near-term structural watch item. Covenants (secured debt ratio ≤4.5x, total debt ratio <7.0x) are currently compliant, and the company termed out maturities in H2 2025 ($700M Term B due 2032, $400M 5.375% notes due 2033), with ~$701M liquidity at Q1 2026.

**Cyclicality.** Revenue is sensitive to economic conditions; the 2026 earnings release explicitly cited tariffs, trade restrictions, and geopolitical tension as demand risks. The ~79% local mix cushions but does not eliminate this exposure. Advertiser budgets are a discretionary line item, so a back-half demand reversal would pressure the national demand that is currently driving the AFFO acceleration.

**Governance.** The Reilly family controls ~63% of voting power on ~15% economic equity via Class B shares, electing the entire board. Executive Chairman Kevin Reilly Jr. and CEO Sean Reilly are family members. May 2026 approval of expanded equity incentive plans is modestly dilutive against existing concentration. This is a permanent minority-shareholder consideration that caps activist optionality and the takeover premium.

**Other.** Self-insured catastrophe exposure (structures deemed uneconomical to insure) raises remediation costs as climate frequency increases; the company incurred hurricane-redevelopment capex in 2025. A $624.9M asset-retirement obligation is judgment-heavy and, as 2024–2025 demonstrated, materially moves reported earnings. Land-lease dependency is underappreciated: Lamar leases ~71,500 of ~82,700 billboard sites, with annual ground-lease expense ~$348M (~17% of billboard revenue) and 73% of leases renewing within five years — an ongoing cost-inflation and renewal exposure. Digital regulatory risk could impair both existing inventory and expansion. A newly added standalone AI risk factor in the 2025 10-K signals increasing operational AI reliance.

Most of these risks are structural features of the billboard-REIT model and are partly priced. The genuine idiosyncratic items not fully embedded are the 2028 springing-maturity dependency and the back-half cyclicality risk if national demand reverses. Risk Factors score 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.5% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 22.2% (a 9.3pp spread), indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty into the next event window, with a ±7.6% implied move at the 3-month tenor. Put/call skew of +3.7% reflects modestly elevated put demand, but the put/call OI ratio fell to 0.75 (from 1.06 a month earlier) as the stock rose 6%, indicating hedging unwound rather than fresh bearish positioning. The term structure is flat (near 31.5% ≈ far 30.3%), suggesting uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a single catalyst. IV compressed 9.4pp since the May 7 earnings print, consistent with resolved event uncertainty. Net read: neutral-to-mildly-cautious, with no informed bearish positioning that contradicts the fundamental view, but rich IV that argues against paying up for upside via calls here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$170, +13%):** National advertiser demand sustains through 2026, pushing AFFO/share above the $8.70 guidance ceiling toward ~$8.80–$9.00 in 2026 and into the low double digits for 2027 as digital conversion and programmatic scale. The multiple re-rates to ~19–20x P/AFFO as the market rewards the acceleration and the well-covered, growing dividend. Continued bolt-on M&A via the UPREIT structure adds incremental faces accretively. The 20-quarter OOH growth streak and AI-advertiser demand provide a durable industry tailwind.

**Bear case (~$130, –14%):** A back-half demand reversal and corporate ad-budget tightening reverse the national-demand acceleration, dragging AFFO/share growth back to the ~2–3% organic baseline. Rising long-end yields compress the REIT multiple toward ~15x AFFO. The February 2028 springing-maturity refinancing approaches in a tighter credit environment, raising the cost of new debt. Logo-contract renewals or digital regulatory action create incremental drag. The stock de-rates to the low-$130s, near where it traded for much of late 2025.

**Base case (~$155, +3%):** AFFO/share lands at the upper half of the $8.50–$8.70 guidance, dividend grows modestly, and the multiple holds at ~17–18x. The stock delivers a total return of roughly its 4.2% yield plus low-single-digit price appreciation over twelve months — a respectable but unexceptional outcome that does not justify a high-conviction long at the current entry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (expected early August 2026), which will confirm whether national demand is pacing to "the top end, if not above" guidance. The June 16–17 FOMC and the path of long-end yields are secondary macro checkpoints given REIT rate sensitivity.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AFFO/share guidance raised above $8.70, or Q2 AFFO/share growth sustains ≥7% with explicit confirmation of durable national demand.
- A pullback toward the low-$130s (~15x forward AFFO), restoring a margin of safety with the dividend yield approaching ~4.7%.
- Open-market insider buying by the Reilly family or CFO, reversing the current net-selling pattern.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- AFFO/share guidance cut, or two consecutive quarters of decelerating organic revenue below ~1.5%.
- Deterioration in the 2028 refinancing path (a downgrade, covenant pressure, or a materially higher-cost refinancing of the 3.75% notes).
- Dividend coverage erosion (AFFO payout ratio rising above ~90%) or a dividend cut.

## Conclusion

Lamar is a high-quality, regulatory-moated OOH REIT with genuine AFFO acceleration into 2026, a well-covered 4.2% dividend, and a disciplined capital-allocation record. The Q1 2026 print (AFFO/share +7.5%, organic revenue +3.9%) confirms the improving trajectory captured in the rising score history, and management's bullish national-demand commentary is the strongest forward signal in the file.

The constraint is price. At ~17.5x 2026 AFFO and near its 52-week high after a 25% one-year run, the stock is fairly valued, not cheap, with consensus targets implying low-single-digit upside to the mean and only ~13% to the high. Structural leverage, the 2028 springing-maturity watch item, concentrated dual-class control, and the CFO's complete divestiture near highs (with no offsetting open-market buying) cap conviction. The appropriate stance is to hold for the AFFO-growth-plus-yield compounding while waiting for a better entry. We rate LAMR a HOLD and would upgrade to BUY on a pullback toward the low-$130s or a confirmed acceleration of national demand above current guidance.
