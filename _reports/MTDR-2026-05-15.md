## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a Delaware Basin-focused E&P company with integrated midstream operations, currently trading at $57.94 (~8x forward FY26 EPS of $7.27). The company has executed well operationally, beating production guidance, improving capital efficiency (11% capex reduction while growing production), and rapidly deleveraging its balance sheet (RBL expected at zero by May 2026). A cluster of insider purchases from November 2025 through March 2026 across four levels of management underscores internal conviction.

The primary near-term catalyst is the Hugh Brinson Pipeline (H2 2026 in-service), which should eliminate Waha gas price exposure — Q1 2026 realized gas was $0.64/Mcf versus $3.56/Mcf a year prior. Full implementation could add $300-400M in annual revenue. However, the stock has already appreciated 56% from its November 2025 trough of $37.14, reflecting much of this improvement. At $57.94, MTDR sits 13% below the 52-week high ($66.84) and 21% below consensus median ($73), with the remaining upside contingent on oil prices remaining elevated and gas pipeline commissioning proceeding on schedule.

The specific concern preventing a BUY upgrade at current levels: MTDR carries concentrated commodity exposure with earnings highly sensitive to oil price movements. The stock's 56% appreciation from its low means the risk/reward skew has compressed relative to prior assessments at $38-42. The position remains a growth hold with specific upgrade criteria identified below.

## Company Overview

Matador Resources operates primarily in the Delaware Basin (98% of production, 99% of proved reserves), with minor Haynesville shale exposure. The company runs 212,500 net leasehold acres and produced 207,070 BOE/day in 2025 (58% oil-weighted). Its 51%-owned San Mateo midstream subsidiary provides gas gathering/processing, oil transportation, and produced water disposal, generating ~$330M annualized Adjusted EBITDA.

Total proved reserves stand at 667 million BOE with PV-10 of $8.24 billion. The company maintains 800+ net engineered drilling locations, representing 7-8 years of inventory at current pace.

## Growth Analysis

Production growth has been exceptional: ~25% CAGR from 2023-2025, driven by organic development plus the Ameredev acquisition (September 2024). Growth moderates but remains positive:

- 2026 oil production guidance: 123-125K Bbl/d (up from ~120K in 2025 implying ~3-4% growth)
- EPS trajectory: $6.09 (trailing) → $7.27 (FY2026E) → $8.46 (FY2027E), representing 19% and 16% growth respectively
- 90-day EPS estimate revisions: +97% for current quarter, +71% for full year — driven primarily by oil price appreciation from ~$65 average in 2025 to higher levels currently

The Hugh Brinson Pipeline represents a step-function improvement in gas economics starting H2 2026. At 500K MMBtu/day of firm transport to Henry Hub markets, this eliminates Waha exposure that cost the company an estimated $250M+ in 2025 revenue versus Henry Hub pricing.

Longer-term, 800+ drilling locations provide 7-8 years of inventory depth. First 3.4-mile lateral wells (Q2 2026) and Woodford exploratory drilling represent additional potential.

## Competitive Landscape

Matador's competitive positioning in the Delaware Basin is strong and improving:

1. **Well productivity divergence:** +8% improvement in 12-month normalized oil volumes (2021-2024) vs peer average -9% decline (Enverus data). This is a meaningful operational advantage.

2. **Vertical integration:** San Mateo midstream provides flow assurance during constrained takeaway periods. Peers without dedicated gathering/processing face curtailments.

3. **Capital efficiency:** Declining costs per lateral foot ($785-805/ft) while completing increasingly long laterals (3.4-mile record in Q2 2026).

4. **Acreage quality:** Ameredev integration performing well. First wells on Eastern Antelope Ridge acreage being completed.

The primary competitive risk is scale — Matador operates alongside ExxonMobil, Chevron, and Devon in the Delaware Basin. These operators can absorb commodity downturns more readily and compete for services/acreage.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Concentration (PRIMARY):** MTDR's earnings are highly levered to oil prices. The stock's 56% rally from November already reflects the oil move from ~$65 to current levels. A sustained decline in oil prices would produce significant downside given high-beta commodity exposure and leverage.

**Waha Gas Price Risk (mitigating H2 2026):** Q1 realized $0.64/Mcf represents a trough. Hugh Brinson Pipeline commissioning Q3/Q4 2026 is the identified fix. Delay risk exists — pipeline construction can encounter permitting or mechanical delays.

**Geographic Concentration:** 98% in one basin. Federal land exposure (33% of Delaware Basin acreage) creates permitting uncertainty.

**Customer Concentration:** Three purchasers = 72% of revenue. Counterparty risk in a downturn.

**Leverage:** While declining rapidly, $3.4B total debt on a $7.5B market cap means the equity is leveraged to commodity prices. A sustained oil downturn would challenge the deleveraging timeline.

## Conclusion

Matador Resources possesses a clear and specific near-term catalyst (Hugh Brinson Pipeline), strong operational execution (18% earnings beat, production above guidance, insider buying cluster), and attractive forward valuation (8x FY2026E EPS). The company is executing well operationally. However, the stock has already moved substantially (+56% from November low, +26% over the past year) reflecting elevated oil prices. At $57.94, the remaining upside to consensus ($73) is 26% — attractive but not sufficiently asymmetric given concentrated commodity risk. The position merits holding through the pipeline commissioning catalyst with defined re-entry criteria on pullbacks. The specific, falsifiable condition preventing BUY: at current price, a significant oil correction combined with any pipeline delay would produce 25-35% downside, creating a risk/reward profile closer to 1:1 rather than the 2:1+ required for Energy BUY conviction.
