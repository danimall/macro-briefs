## Executive Summary

First Advantage is a global employment background-screening and identity-verification business that recognizes revenue per report delivered, making it a per-transaction levered claim on gross hiring activity. The 2026 operating record is genuinely good: four consecutive quarterly beats (the last two by 21.6% and 19.2%), Q2 revenue of $448.8M (+14.9%), adjusted diluted EPS of $0.35 versus $0.27, guidance raised twice (FY2026 revenue now $1,670–1,710M and adjusted diluted EPS $1.23–1.29), net leverage down from 4.25x at year-end to 3.89x, and $95M of debt retired in 2026 including a $45M voluntary prepayment on August 4. The Sterling acquisition, closed October 2024, is working operationally: the Sterling segment's adjusted EBITDA margin has progressed from 18.7% (two-month stub) to 27.3% (FY2025) to 30.3% in Q2 2026, now above legacy Americas.

Three things temper the case at $23.61. First, the company's own guidance implies a sharp step-down: H2 2025 revenue was $829.2M (FY2025 $1,574.4M less H1 2025 $745.2M), so the guidance range less H1 2026 actual of $834.0M implies H2 2026 revenue of $836–876M, or +0.8% to +5.6% YoY. Consensus has already marked the next quarter down 2.5% while raising the full year 4.3% — analysts are modeling the same comp roll-off. Second, Q2's revenue acceleration was margin-dilutive: adjusted EBITDA margin fell 60bp YoY to 28.6% despite 14.9% growth, because third-party data expense rose $29.8M, consuming 51% of the $58.1M incremental revenue dollar. Incremental adjusted EBITDA margin was 25.1% against a 28.6% reported average. Third, legacy First Advantage revenue declined in 2023 (−5.7%) and 2024 (−2.2%); this is a mature, management-described "penetrated and competitive market," and the underlying growth rate is mid-single-digit, not mid-teens.

The valuation reflects most of the good news. EV of $5,864M is 12.2x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $479M, or 13.9x after deducting the ~$58M of annual capitalized software the non-GAAP measure ignores. At 18.7x guided adjusted EPS and 15.6x next-year consensus of $1.51, with a free-cash-flow yield of 4.3–5.4%, the shares are neither cheap nor stretched. The most informative capital-allocation fact is that management bought 3.24M shares at an average of $11.78 through April, cut repurchase volume 93% by June as the price rose, bought essentially nothing in July, and upsized its August debt prepayment to $45M instead. Retiring SOFR+250bp debt was preferred to buying equity at 12.2x EV/EBITDA. Base case $25.50 (+8%), bull $31 (+31%), bear $17 (−28%). HOLD.

## Company Overview

First Advantage screens employment candidates for more than 80,000 customers, including roughly two-thirds of the Fortune 100, running over 200 million screens annually across 200+ countries against proprietary databases of more than one billion records. The product set spans criminal background checks, credential and employment verification, drug and health screening, identity verification, continuous post-hire monitoring, and adjacent lines (fleet compliance, hiring tax credits, I-9/E-Verify, investigative research). Delivery is concentrated offshore in Manila, Bangalore and Mumbai, with US operations centers in Fishers, Indiana and Atlanta.

The revenue model matters more than the product list. A "substantial majority" of revenue is pre-onboarding screening tied directly to customer hiring volumes, recognized at a point in time on report delivery and billed monthly. Deferred revenue was $5.3M at June 30, 2026 against $448.8M of quarterly revenue; there is no meaningful backlog and no remaining-performance-obligation disclosure. Contracts are typically three years but, in the legacy book, guarantee neither exclusivity nor volumes, and customers may exit with minimal notice and without penalty. Gross customer retention of ~96% and average tenure of roughly 13 years across the top 100 accounts are therefore earned operationally each year rather than contractually secured. One asset acquired with Sterling is that a majority of Sterling's enterprise contracts are exclusive to Sterling or require it to be the primary provider, which is better lock-in than First Advantage's own legacy book.

The defining corporate event is the $1,687.2M acquisition of Sterling Check, closed October 31, 2024, which roughly doubled revenue, added ~$1.32B of goodwill in the Sterling segment, and was funded with a $2,185M first-lien term loan due 2031 plus a $250M revolver due 2029. Silver Lake held 51.4% of the common stock at December 31, 2025 and, under the stockholders' agreement, retains approval rights over CEO appointment and termination, debt incurrence above $100M, and any change-of-control transaction while it holds at least 25%.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 revenue of $1,574.4M reflects the full-year Sterling contribution. Stripping it out is essential to understanding the underlying business: FY2024 revenue of $860.2M included only two months of Sterling ($113.1M), implying legacy First Advantage revenue of ~$747.1M versus $763.8M in 2023 and $810.0M in 2022 — a cumulative ~7.8% contraction from the 2022 peak as post-pandemic hiring normalized. H1 2026 revenue was $834.0M (+11.9%), with Q1 at $385.2M (+8.6%) and Q2 at $448.8M (+14.9%). Management attributes Q2 growth to +$41.6M from existing customers and +$16.6M from new customers, with pricing "relatively stable" — organic, with no acquisition contribution cited.

**Margins.** Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin has compressed for three years: 31.1% (FY2023) → 29.0% (FY2024) → 28.0% (FY2025) → 28.0% (H1 2026). Q2 2026 at 28.6% was down 60bp YoY. Gross margin fell from 46.8% to 45.5% as cost of services rose to 54.5% of revenue from 53.2%, driven primarily by $29.8M of incremental third-party data expense ($46.4M in H1). The arithmetic: $58.1M of incremental Q2 revenue produced $21.2M of incremental gross profit (36.5% incremental margin) and $14.6M of incremental adjusted EBITDA (25.1% incremental margin). Q1 behaved oppositely, with a derived incremental adjusted EBITDA margin of 43.0% and margin up ~134bp YoY, so one quarter does not establish a trend. The interpretation matters a great deal and cannot be settled from the disclosure: mix shift toward data-intensive, high-turnover verticals (transportation, staffing, retail) is neutral-to-positive for dollar profit; supplier price inflation passed through at little markup is a structural margin threat. The FY2025 10-K discloses that some criminal-data suppliers and drug-testing labs "are also owned or may in the future be acquired by one or more of our competitors" and that supplier agreements are non-exclusive, which makes the second reading credible.

**Segments.** Q2 reported Americas revenue +32.6% with margin down 200bp to 29.4%, Sterling +2.6% with margin up 170bp to 30.3%, and International −5.0% with margin down 1,120bp to 5.5%. The Americas/Sterling divergence is almost certainly distorted by migration of Sterling customers onto First Advantage platforms and contracting entities; derived H2 2025 Americas revenue of ~$392.7M is essentially flat against H1 2026 Americas of $389.5M, so the YoY Americas surge is a level shift dated to H2 2025. Only the combined figure is interpretable: Americas plus Sterling grew from $367.2M to $425.8M, or +16.0%. Two signals survive: Sterling is now the highest-margin segment, which is the clearest evidence the acquisition is delivering; and International, at ~6% of revenue, has fallen to ~1.2% of segment adjusted EBITDA, with H1 adjusted EBITDA of $2.8M versus $7.7M on a $1.5M revenue decline. The driver of that decline — FX (a $12.8M H1 translation loss in OCI, $6.3M negative FX effect on cash), genuine EMEA/APAC hiring weakness, or cost reallocation — is not disclosed.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $123.0M versus $56.8M, with Q2 alone at $73.6M. Free cash flow must include capitalized software development, which First Advantage reports separately from PP&E: H1 2026 FCF = $123.0M − $28.1M software − $7.5M PP&E = $87.5M, against $32.9M in H1 2025. FY2025 FCF was approximately $140M, not the ~$188M a PP&E-only capex screen would produce. Capitalized software is running ~$56–58M annualized (~3.4% of revenue), a ~12% haircut to adjusted EBITDA that the non-GAAP measure does not reflect. FY2025 free cash flow was heavily H2-weighted ($32.9M H1 versus ~$107M H2), reflecting the Q1 revenue trough and bonus payments. A reasonable FY2026 band is $175–220M — the low end if H2 merely repeats H1, the high end if H2 2025 seasonality repeats with ~$12M of interest savings and ~$10M of EBITDA growth. That implies an equity FCF yield of 4.3%–5.4%.

**Balance sheet.** Term debt went from $2,185.0M at inception to $2,064.5M at June 30, 2026, and to $2,019.5M after the August 4 prepayment. Cash was $237.9M. Net debt of $1,826.6M against LTM adjusted EBITDA of $469.2M gives 3.89x, down from 4.25x at December 31; against FY2026 guidance midpoint of $479M, ~3.81x. Three levers drove the improvement: EBITDA growth, $95M of 2026 debt reduction, and a July 2025 credit amendment that cut the term-loan spread 50–75bp to SOFR+250–275bp. The maturity and covenant profile is benign, with nothing due until the 2029 revolver and 2031 term loan, and the sole financial covenant is a springing 7.75:1.00 first-lien net leverage test that applies only above 40% revolver utilization, with no revolver borrowings outstanding and $249.3M available.

Two adjustments are important for modeling. Interest expense, net fell $29.9M in H1 to $61.4M, but $14.6M of that decline was non-cash mark-to-market on undesignated swaps (a $8.2M gain in 2026 versus a $6.4M loss in 2025). Cash interest paid fell $14.8M, to $69.3M from $84.1M. The hedge book also shrinks: of $985M notional (~48% of debt), $460M matures December 31, 2026, cutting coverage to ~26%. That is a live 2027 earnings variable. Each 100bp on ~$1.5B of unhedged debt is roughly $15M pretax, or ~$0.06 of adjusted EPS.

The Altman Z-Score of ~1.6 on current figures should not be read as distress. It is depressed by goodwill of $2,135.2M plus intangibles of $785.1M equal to 77.9% of total assets, and by an accumulated deficit of $214.1M that absorbed a $1.50/share special dividend in 2023 and $38.6M of 2026 buybacks charged against it. The accumulated deficit widened during H1 2026 despite $19.1M of net income because of the buyback charge, which is a capital transaction rather than operating deterioration. Tangible book equity of approximately negative $1.63B is a real fact: recovery value in a downside scenario depends entirely on going-concern cash flow, and the company cannot raise meaningful secured debt against assets.

## Growth Analysis

The gap between the Q2 print and the guided second half is the central analytical question. H2 2026 implied revenue of $836–876M against $829.2M in H2 2025 is +0.8% to +5.6%. Two readings are live. The comp-artifact reading: management's "recent large contract wins" and the Americas level shift appear to have ramped in H2 2025 (H2 2025 was +11.3% sequentially over H1 2025), making H1 2026 comps easy and H2 comps hard, with growth reverting toward the underlying market rate once those wins are in the base. The conservatism reading: management has raised guidance twice (May 7: $1,625–1,700M; August 6: $1,670–1,710M) and beaten four consecutive quarters, so H2 may beat again. Both are plausible. The investment-relevant conclusion is the same under either: an investor extrapolating 15% from Q2 is extrapolating a comp, and the company's own arithmetic does not support it.

Consensus corroborates. Analysts raised the current year 4.3% and next year 5.5% over 90 days while cutting the next-quarter estimate 2.5%, a distribution consistent with H1 catch-up rather than H2 acceleration. Implied H2 2026 adjusted EPS is $0.67 (consensus $1.28 less H1 actual $0.61), against $0.68 from the quarterly estimates. H2 EPS improves sequentially despite decelerating revenue, which is the deleveraging and interest-savings contribution rather than operating acceleration.

Estimate revisions of +4.3%/+5.5% are positive but well short of the magnitude that signals a genuine earnings acceleration. This is a mid-single-digit revenue grower converting to mid-to-high-teens adjusted EPS growth through financial leverage: consensus next-year $1.51 is +18% on the current-year $1.28, of which a meaningful share comes from debt reduction, the spread repricing and a 1.9% reduction in shares outstanding rather than from operations.

The macro backdrop is unhelpful. First Advantage's own MD&A cites "uneven hiring activity across industries and geographies," "cautious employer behavior," "slower decision-making, increased cost scrutiny." That language sits in visible tension with a release headlined "Posts Record Quarter and Raises Full Year 2026 Guidance," and the honest synthesis is that the company is outgrowing a soft market through share gains rather than riding a hiring recovery. The corollary is real operating leverage if hiring recovers, and demonstrated revenue contraction (2023–2024) if it weakens further, now against $2.0B of debt.

The longer-duration issue is that AI-driven workforce automation is named in the 10-K as a demand risk. For a business paid per screen, enterprise headcount reduction cuts unit volumes independent of share. Nothing in the quarterly metrics shows this yet, which is why it is underpriced rather than absent.

## Competitive Landscape

Management's own characterization — a "penetrated and competitive market" — is the honest one. North American employment screening is mature; nearly all large employers already buy the service, so growth comes from share shift, cross-sell of additional products per order, and net hiring volumes. That framing explains the Sterling deal: with legacy revenue declining in 2023–2024, consolidation was the available route to scale and cost synergy.

Trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. On the positive side: scale (200M+ screens, 1B+ records) is a genuine cost and match-rate advantage; ~96% gross retention with 13-year top-100 tenure reflects real switching friction (re-integrating an applicant tracking system and re-validating compliance workflows); the acquired Sterling enterprise book carries exclusivity or primary-provider status the legacy book lacks; inherited FTC consent decrees requiring biennially audited security programs raise the compliance bar for subscale entrants; and Sterling's margin now exceeds legacy Americas, with integration add-backs falling to $8.5M in H1 from $17.0M.

On the negative side, ranked by severity: ATS and HCM platforms are simultaneously First Advantage's distribution channel and its most credible disintermediator, and the company's own forward-looking-statement language names those relationships as a risk; data suppliers and drug-testing labs owned by competitors give the supply chain pricing power, which is what Q2's $29.8M data-cost increase looks like in practice; large employers can insource verification; and product-specific secular headwinds (cannabis legalization reducing marijuana screening, state fair-chance laws restricting criminal-history use) shrink specific lines regardless of hiring. Gross margin down 130bp YoY while revenue grew 14.9% is the quantitative expression of the supplier problem.

The company's TSR indexed at 81.91 at December 31, 2025 against a peer group at 112.40 — value destroyed in absolute terms since the 2021 IPO and roughly 30 index points of relative underperformance. The 2026 rally has recovered much of that, but the multi-year record is poor.

## Risk Assessment

**Hiring-volume cyclicality is the dominant risk.** A substantial majority of revenue is pre-onboarding screening. A 10% revenue decline removes roughly $75M of gross profit at 45% margin against ~$140M of annual cash interest. The 2023–2024 legacy contraction is the empirical proof of sensitivity, and it occurred at far lower leverage.

**Rate exposure steps up in 2027.** Hedge coverage falls from ~48% to ~26% of debt when $460M of swaps mature December 31, 2026. An unhedged ~$1.5B floating balance is a material variable in any rising-rate scenario.

**Margin driver unresolved.** If the $29.8M data-cost increase is supplier price inflation rather than mix, the ~28% adjusted EBITDA margin is structurally exposed and the FY2026 guide's implied 28.3% is optimistic.

**Litigation with an insurance gap.** FCRA class actions are the standing exposure, and punitive damages under the FCRA are generally not insured. Accrued probable-and-estimable litigation liability was $8.3M at June 30 with no single named material matter, which suggests diffuse rather than acute exposure. GDPR, CCPA/CPRA and Illinois BIPA add tail risk.

**Governance.** Silver Lake at ~51–52% makes this a controlled company with consent rights over CEO appointment and any change of control; minority holders cannot force change, and no third party can acquire the company without sponsor consent. The $100M buyback expressly excludes Silver Lake, so repurchases mechanically raise the sponsor's stake (roughly 80bp in H1). Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (January 30 and August 6, 2026) could not be retrieved; the identity and nature of those officer or director events is an open gap of moderate importance.

**Balance-sheet quality.** Negative tangible book equity of ~$1.63B with goodwill and intangibles at 77.9% of assets means downside recovery value rests entirely on going-concern cash flow.

**Structural AI demand risk** is the longest-duration item and is invisible in any current metric.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (25 contracts across four expirations), so its information content is limited. Near-term ATM IV of 49.4% sits below 57.9% one-year realized volatility and in the 8th percentile of the current chain, with a contango term structure (49.4% near versus 56.9% at six months) and call-heavy skew (−5.5%; put/call open interest 0.02). Near-term uncertainty is priced as low immediately after the earnings release, with the risk pushed out to the September expiry, which carries 67.1% IV and a ±22.6% implied move. Cheap near-term volatility on a name that has moved 168% in six months reflects post-event complacency rather than a directional signal.

Short interest of 18.4% of float with 5.7 days to cover is the more consequential positioning fact. A short base that large means part of the 168% advance was covering rather than fundamental buying, and residual squeeze fuel remains, which cuts both ways. It supports the price on incremental good news and makes the stock vulnerable to a sharp unwind of the covering bid if H2 comes in at the guidance low end.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($31, +31%).** H2 2026 beats a deliberately conservative guide for a third time, as it did in May and August, confirming that the implied +0.8% to +5.6% was sandbagging rather than forecast. The Q2 margin dilution proves to be a discrete data-intensive mix event (Q1's 43.0% incremental adjusted EBITDA margin supports this), and full-year margin reaches the guided 28.3%. Sterling integration completes, the segment consolidates into Americas, and cross-sell of exclusivity-protected enterprise accounts adds several points of growth. Debt falls toward $1.9B gross, net leverage reaches ~3.4x by mid-2027, and the freed cash interest plus the spread repricing carries FY2027 adjusted EPS to $1.65. A US hiring recovery adds volume operating leverage on a fixed offshore delivery base. At 19x $1.65 the shares reach $31, matching the high analyst target.

**Bear case ($17, −28%).** The H2 guide is a demand forecast, not conservatism, and revenue grows below 1% as the H2 2025 contract wins fully enter the base. The third-party data cost increase turns out to be supplier price inflation from vendors partly owned by competitors, and margin compresses for a fourth consecutive year toward 27%. Gross hiring weakens further, and legacy 2023–2024 revenue behavior repeats at 3.9x leverage. Swap roll-off adds $10–15M of cash interest in 2027. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands at $1.20 and the multiple compresses toward 10.5x EV/EBITDA, giving ~$17.50. The 18.4% short base, having been squeezed on the way up, reasserts. International remains a margin sink and the unexplained officer changes prove disruptive.

**Base case ($25.50, +8%).** Revenue lands near the guidance midpoint of $1,690M (+7.4%), H2 growth of 3–4% confirms mid-single-digit underlying trend, margin holds near 28.3%, and adjusted EPS finishes at $1.27. FY2027 adjusted EPS of $1.51 in line with consensus, driven roughly half by operations and half by deleveraging, buyback and interest savings, capitalized at 17x, gives $25.67; the EV/EBITDA cross-check gives $25.10. Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear yields roughly +5% expected return, approximately fair and below the bar for new capital in a sector where selection has historically added no alpha.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders should keep exposure; the deleveraging path and the option on a hiring recovery are worth retaining. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $23.61, 6% below the 52-week high and 168% above the February low, with management itself having stopped repurchasing near $16.

- **Base case target: $25.50** (17x next-year consensus adjusted EPS of $1.51; cross-checked at 12.0x FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of ~$505M less ~$1,700M net debt over ~174M diluted shares). **+8%** from $23.61.
- **Bull case: $31** (19x $1.65 FY2027 adjusted EPS). **+31%.**
- **Bear case: $17** (10.5x EV/EBITDA on ~$460M with EPS at $1.20). **−28%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 print (early November), which is the first quarter to test the hard comp directly.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 revenue growth of 6% or better YoY with adjusted EBITDA margin at or above 28.5%, demonstrating that the H2 guide was conservatism and that the Q2 margin dilution was mix, not supplier inflation. Combined with a price below ~$21, that would restore asymmetry.
2. Resumption of share repurchases at prevailing prices, or disclosure of the third-party data cost split between volume mix and unit price. Either would resolve a material verification gap in the bull's favor.
3. Net leverage below 3.5x with the December 2026 swap roll-off re-hedged at comparable rates, removing the 2027 interest overhang.
4. A price in the $18–20 range on unchanged fundamentals, which would put FCF yield above 5.5% and EV/EBITDA near 11x.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 revenue growth below 2% YoY combined with adjusted EBITDA margin below 28%, which would confirm the bear reading that the guide is a demand forecast and that margin compression has entered a fourth year.
2. FY2027 guidance (expected February 2027) implying revenue growth below 3% or adjusted EBITDA margin below 28%, particularly if introduced alongside a reduced hedge position.
3. Evidence of the AI-substitution mechanism reaching unit volumes — sequential declines in screens per customer, or a large enterprise account explicitly citing reduced hiring volumes at renewal.
4. Disclosure that either Item 5.02 event involved the departure of the CEO, CFO or President, or an unfavorable resolution of the President's amended employment arrangement.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (early November 2026) is the natural decision point, with the December 31, 2026 swap maturity as a second checkpoint on 2027 interest sensitivity.

## Conclusion

First Advantage in 2026 has done the things a levered post-acquisition roll-up should do: integrate (Sterling's margin now leads the company), deleverage (4.25x to 3.89x with $95M retired), reprice debt (50–75bp of spread), buy stock cheaply and then stop (3.24M shares at $11.78 average, essentially nothing above $16), and beat guidance four consecutive times. The quality of the non-GAAP measure is also improving as add-backs shrink from $37.1M to $19.2M year over year.

What the market has now priced is that improvement. At 12.2x FY2026E EV/adjusted EBITDA (13.9x on a like-for-like basis after the ~$58M of capitalized software the metric excludes), 18.7x guided adjusted EPS, and a 4.3–5.4% free-cash-flow yield, the shares sit inside a defensible range for a transactional business with mid-single-digit underlying revenue growth, 3.9x leverage, negative tangible equity, and a hedge book that halves at year-end. The company's own guidance implies H2 revenue growth of +0.8% to +5.6%, and consensus has already cut the next quarter. Against that, the strongest argument for patience is management's revealed preference: given a choice between buying its own equity at 12.2x and retiring SOFR+250bp debt, the board chose the debt and upsized the prepayment.

Expected return of roughly +5% on a 50/25/25 base/bull/bear weighting, with 28% downside in the bear case and two unresolved analytical questions (the data-cost driver and the two undisclosed officer changes), does not clear the bar for new capital. **HOLD**, with the Q3 print as the checkpoint that determines whether this becomes an accumulate at a better price or a reduce on confirmed deceleration.
