**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UMB Financial has completed its transition from acquirer to operator. The January 2025 all-stock acquisition of Heartland Financial (HTLF) roughly doubled the bank's size to $72.7 billion in assets, and after eleven months of integration drag, Q1 2026 was the first clean quarter of the combined franchise. The numbers validate the deal: net interest income up 34.4% to $534.4M, noninterest income up 23.2% to $204.8M, and total revenue up ~31% against a 1.0% decline in noninterest expense as acquisition costs rolled off. That operating leverage drove ROA to 1.47% and ROCE to 13.70%, both strong for a regional bank, and lifted diluted EPS to $3.35 versus $1.21 in the year-ago quarter (which was depressed by a $62.0M Day-1 provision and $53.2M of acquisition expense).

The stock has appreciated ~31% over the trailing twelve months to ~$141, yet still trades at roughly 11x the $12.95 current-year consensus and ~1.4x its $99.22 book value, a modest discount to the regional-bank peer median P/E of 12.7 that is more than justified by superior returns and a differentiated, capital-light fee franchise. Estimates are rising across every window, with the current-year figure revised up 7.5% over 90 days, and the bank has beaten consensus in three of the last four quarters, including a 17.3% beat in Q1 2026. Consensus targets ($139 low / $170 high, $161 median) frame the stock as fairly-to-modestly-undervalued, and on a cheap, inflecting bank these targets tend to lag.

The bear case is specific and worth taking seriously: UMB carries a double concentration — real-estate-secured loans at ~54% of the book (CRE alone ~42%) layered on top of a price-sensitive commercial/institutional deposit base that is ~65% uninsured — and its balance sheet is liability-sensitive, meaning the recently expanded NIM was funding-cost-led and would reverse in a higher-for-longer rate scenario. We rate UMBF a BUY on the strength of the earnings inflection, rising estimates, and reasonable valuation, but size it as a quality bank compounder rather than a maximum-conviction position, given the concentration and rate-path sensitivity.

## Company Overview

UMB Financial is a Kansas City, Missouri-based financial holding company operating through UMB Bank, N.A. Post-HTLF, it spans the Midwest, Southwest, and West with ~$40.1B in loans, ~$60.0B in deposits, and ~$20.1B in securities as of March 31, 2026. The company runs three reportable segments:

- **Commercial Banking** (largest, Q1 2026 net income $175.5M) — commercial and CRE lending, treasury management, and a specialty group covering asset-based and mezzanine lending.
- **Institutional Banking** (Q1 2026 net income $67.6M) — the differentiated, fee-rich franchise: UMB Fund Services (fund administration for mutual funds and alternatives), corporate trust, institutional custody, fixed-income sales/trading, and a Healthcare Services business (HSA custody, private-label debit cards).
- **Personal Banking** (Q1 2026 net income $18.3M) — consumer banking, wealth management, private banking, cards, and mortgage; a swing from a $31.1M year-ago loss distorted by acquisition charges.

UMB holds #1 deposit market share in Missouri and now ranks top-10 in Colorado, New Mexico, Kansas, and Arizona. Its structural differentiator versus a typical regional bank is the outsized fee engine — fund services, corporate trust, and HSA/healthcare payments — that generated roughly 28% of total revenue in Q1 2026. These are capital-light, scarce among peer regionals, and less rate-sensitive than spread lending, which gives UMB a more diversified revenue profile than its balance sheet alone would suggest.

## Financial Analysis

The merger arithmetic is the whole story, and it is working. Q1 2026 marks the first quarter in which the combined institution's earning power is visible without acquisition noise.

**Earnings and returns.** Net income available to common was $255.6M versus $79.3M a year earlier, though the comparison is flattered by the year-ago HTLF-close charges. ROA of 1.47% (from 0.54%) and ROCE of 13.70% (from 5.86%) sit at the high end of the regional-bank range. Both figures benefit from the acquisition-noise base effect, but even normalizing for that, the run-rate returns are strong.

**Net interest income and margin.** NII rose on an extra month of HTLF, purchase-accounting accretion, favorable deposit repricing, and organic loan growth. NIM (TE) expanded 42 bps YoY to 3.38%. The mechanism matters: cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 54 bps while earning-asset yield was essentially flat (+1 bp). The NIM improvement is funding-cost-led, which confirms the liability-sensitive profile: a tailwind while short rates fall, a headwind if they stay high or rise.

**Fee income.** Noninterest income growth was led by trust/securities processing (+18.7%, with fund services +20.5% and corporate trust +19.4%), brokerage (+16.5%), and bankcard (+9.8%). These are asset-value-linked fees, so they carry market sensitivity, but the breadth of growth across lines confirms a healthy franchise rather than a single-line spike.

**Operating leverage.** Noninterest expense fell to $380.9M as acquisition costs rolled off (legal and consulting down $19.5M, or 68.2%). Revenue up 31% against expenses down 1% is the synergy payoff.

**Capital and balance sheet.** Total shareholders' equity of $7.8B rose 16% YoY. Capital ratios sit comfortably above requirements: CET1 11.16%, Tier 1 11.74%, Total RBC 13.53%, leverage 8.73%, all improved YoY. Book value per share reached $99.22 (from $87.43). The dividend of $0.43/quarter represents a low 12.8% payout, leaving ample room for growth and buybacks; the company repurchased 178,249 shares in Q1 2026 at an average $111.62 and the Board authorized 2M shares in April 2026.

**Credit quality.** The ACL/loans ratio is 1.06% (+3 bps YoY). Nonperforming loans rose $50.4M YoY to $151.3M (0.38% of loans), attributed specifically to acquired HTLF loans. Net charge-offs annualized at just 0.19% of average loans — very low. The provision fell to $27.0M from $86.0M. One item to monitor: after the initial $62.1M PCD ACL at close, UMB added $15.2M (Q2) and $8.0M (Q3) of PCD reserves in 2025, indicating the acquired book carried more embedded credit deterioration than first estimated. Charge-offs remain benign, but the acquired CRE book warrants ongoing attention.

**Tax headwind.** The effective tax rate rose to 21.1% from 12.6% as favorable 2025 discrete items (deferred-tax remeasurement) rolled off. This is a genuine headwind to reported EPS growth going forward and should temper enthusiasm about the raw YoY EPS jump.

The financial health picture is strong — high returns, fortress capital, benign net charge-offs, robust liquidity ($35.4B available) — with the AOCI mark and CRE concentration the offsetting cautions. We score Financial Health 7.3, consistent with the improving trajectory (6.8 → 7.5 in prior reads), trimmed slightly for the securities mark and deposit-mix risk.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth engine is the completion of the synergy phase. Revenue growth against declining expenses implies the combined franchise is now earning at a materially higher run-rate than the pre-merger business. Consensus captures this: current-year EPS is estimated at $12.95, up 7.5% over 90 days; next year at $13.77, up 4.4%; current quarter and next quarter both revised up ~3.7-3.8% over 90 days. This is a broad-based, positive revision pattern rather than a single-line adjustment.

The 90-day current-year revision of +7.5% sits below the +30% threshold that flags a true earnings inflection, so I do not treat this as a maximum-strength buy signal in the way I would for a cyclical trough recovery. What it does confirm is that consensus is steadily catching up to the combined franchise's earning power, and the 3-of-4 beat track record gives reasonable confidence that the current estimates are achievable-to-conservative.

Organic growth drivers beyond the merger: the fee franchise (fund services, corporate trust, HSA/healthcare payments) is compounding at high-teens to low-twenties rates, and it is capital-light and less rate-sensitive. Loan growth is organic-plus-acquired, and the multi-state deposit leadership (top-10 in five states) provides a low-cost funding base to fund it. The chief swing factor is the rate path: falling short rates continue to help funding costs and NIM, while a higher-for-longer regime caps the NIM tailwind.

Growth Prospects score 6.5 — solid and estimate-confirmed, but not the accelerating-inflection profile that would earn a higher mark, and constrained by the tax-rate reset and the reversibility of the funding-led NIM gains.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$141, UMBF trades at ~11x the $12.95 current-year consensus and ~10.3x the $13.77 next-year figure, with a ~1.4x price-to-book multiple against $99.22 BVPS. Against the regional-bank peer median P/E of 12.7 (WTFC 13.1, CFR 14.2, ONB 12.7, ZION 10.3, COLB 12.2), UMB trades at a modest discount on current-year earnings despite generating materially superior returns — 1.47% ROA and 13.70% ROCE.

The valuation case rests on the return differential, not on a low absolute multiple. A bank earning 1.47% ROA with a diversified, capital-light fee engine and fortress capital deserves a premium to a peer group heavier in spread lending; instead it trades in line-to-below. That is the mispricing. Rising estimates reinforce the point: consensus is revising the earnings base upward, so the forward multiple is compressing as the estimates climb. On a stock that is cheap relative to rising estimates (forward P/E ~11x, well under 30x, with a PEG comfortably below 1.5 given mid-single-digit-plus earnings growth), the consensus targets of $139-170 (median $161) function more as floors than ceilings — the median implies ~14% upside, and analysts revise these upward within weeks when a cheap, inflecting name reaches consensus.

I apply appropriate skepticism to the "cheap" signal. Cheapness on its own has been an unreliable buy signal, frequently marking value traps. Here, though, the cheapness is paired with an identifiable catalyst (the synergy payoff now landing in reported results), rising estimates, and a return profile that structurally supports a higher multiple — the independent confirmation required to reward the valuation. The offsetting risk is that the multiple is partly a discount for the CRE concentration and the liability-sensitive rate exposure; if either materializes adversely, the discount is warranted rather than a mispricing.

Valuation score 6.6. This reverses part of the recent decline (7.2 → 6.0) in the prior read: the stock is cheaper relative to a higher estimate base than it was, and price appreciation from fundamental improvement is not a reason to mark valuation down.

## Competitive Landscape

UMB competes with regional banks (Associated Banc-Corp, Huntington, Commerce Bancshares) but differentiates on the fee side. Its institutional franchise — fund services, corporate trust, and especially HSA/healthcare payments — is scarce among peer regionals and generates roughly 28% of revenue with lower rate sensitivity and low capital intensity. The HTLF deal cemented multi-state deposit leadership: #1 in Missouri, top-10 in Colorado, New Mexico, Kansas, and Arizona.

The competitive trajectory is improving. Segment revenue is growing across all three units, fee lines are compounding at high-teens rates, and the acquired footprint expands the deposit-gathering base. Below-industry net charge-offs (0.19%) evidence disciplined underwriting relative to peers. The structural headwind flagged in the 10-K — the 2025 GENIUS Act stablecoin framework as potential competition for bank deposits — is an industry-wide, longer-dated risk rather than a UMB-specific erosion. On balance, UMB is widening its differentiation via the fee engine while holding deposit share; the trajectory earns a Competitive Position score of 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is defined by a specific, UMB-specific double concentration that the market only partly prices.

1. **CRE concentration (the standout).** CRE is ~41-42% of the loan book; combined CRE plus consumer real estate reaches 53.7% of loans. Investment CRE (non-owner-occupied plus construction) is ~26.8% of total loans. The 10-K concedes HTLF *exacerbated* UMB's pre-existing CRE concentration. Geographic exposure clusters in Missouri (12.7%), Arizona (12.4%), Texas (11.3%), and Colorado (11.3%). Office is only ~3.3% of investment CRE — a meaningful mitigant, as office is the most-stressed CRE subsegment.

2. **Price-sensitive, commercial/institutional funding base.** UMB's deposits skew toward commercial/institutional sources that are more price-sensitive than consumer deposits. Estimated uninsured deposits are ~65% of total (~$39.1B); adjusted for affiliate and collateralized deposits, uninsured is ~48.7%. The liquidity backstop is robust — $35.4B available via cash, unpledged collateral, fed funds, the discount window, and IntraFi — which materially blunts the deposit-flight tail.

3. **Liability-sensitive rate profile.** Rate-shock modeling shows NII falls modestly in Year-1 rising-rate scenarios and rises in falling-rate scenarios (+3.0% in a -300 bps Year-1 shock). The recent NIM gains were funding-led, so a higher-for-longer regime would reverse the tailwind.

4. **AOCI / securities mark.** Unrealized pre-tax losses were $376.6M on AFS (2.7% of amortized cost) and $530.7M on HTM (9.3%) at Q1 2026, both worsening as rates rose during the quarter. AOCI sits at a $331.4M loss in equity. Securities are ~28% of assets at a low ~3.8% yield versus ~6.5% on loans.

5. **Residual HTLF credit uncertainty.** The $23.2M of post-close PCD reserve additions and the $50.4M YoY rise in acquired NPLs indicate the acquired book carried more embedded deterioration than initially marked. Charge-offs remain low, but the acquired CRE book is the item to watch.

6. **Fee-business market sensitivity.** The ~28% fee stream is asset-value-linked; a market downturn would pressure fund services, trust, and brokerage revenue.

The double concentration is the single most UMB-specific vulnerability, compounding in a stressed CRE/deposit-flight scenario. Mitigants (strong liquidity, low office exposure, granular geographic CRE spread, fortress capital) prevent this from being a distress risk but keep it from being fully priced away. Risk Factors score 5.4.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 23.9% sits just below one-year historical volatility of 26.7% (a -2.9% spread), so options are fairly-to-slightly-cheaply priced — no elevated event premium. The term structure is flat (23.9% near vs. 22.3% far), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. The 1-month put/call skew of 5.6% shows modestly elevated put demand, but this flips to slightly negative skew (-2.0%) at the 6-month tenor, so the near-term put bias reads as ordinary downside hedging rather than a directional bearish bet. Short interest is moderate at 6.4% of float (5.2 days to cover) — not a crowded short, not a squeeze setup. On balance, the options market is neutral and consistent with the fundamental view; there is no divergence to flag against the BUY thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The HTLF integration is complete and the synergy payoff is now in reported numbers. The bank beats consensus consistently, and it trades at ~11x forward with a differentiated, capital-light fee engine generating ~28% of revenue. Capital is fortress-grade (CET1 11.16%) with a low 12.8% payout leaving room for dividend growth and buybacks. If short rates continue to fall, the funding-led NIM tailwind extends, and consensus targets prove to be floors. Bull target $170 (~12.3x the $13.77 next-year estimate, in line with the peer-premium UMB's return profile warrants).

**Bear case.** The double concentration bites: a CRE stress cycle drives up charge-offs on the acquired book (which already required $23.2M of post-close reserve true-ups), while a higher-for-longer rate regime reverses the funding-led NIM gains and pressures the price-sensitive deposit base. AOCI marks worsen if rates rise. The tax-rate reset to 21.1% caps reported EPS growth. In this scenario the stock derates toward ~9x earnings and ~1.1x book. Bear target ~$118 (~-16% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $158**, roughly 11.5x the blended forward EPS (~$13.36 across current and next year), a modest premium to the peer median P/E justified by UMB's superior ROA/ROCE and fee diversification. This sits between the bear ($118) and bull ($170) scenarios and implies ~12% upside from ~$141, plus a ~1.2% dividend yield.

**Bull case target: $170** (~+20%) — ~12.3x next-year EPS if the falling-rate NIM tailwind persists and estimates continue rising.

**Bear case target: $118** (~-16%) — 9x earnings / ~1.1x book if CRE stress or a higher-for-longer rate path materializes.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the next several quarters of "clean" combined-franchise reporting (Q2 2026 onward) that should confirm the run-rate earning power, and to the rate path over the balance of 2026.

**Upside/downside from current (~$141):** +12% to base, +20% to bull, -16% to bear — a favorable ~2:1 reward/risk.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Net charge-offs rise above ~0.50% of average loans or NPLs climb materially beyond the current $151.3M, signaling the acquired CRE book is deteriorating faster than reserved.
- NIM contracts for two consecutive quarters (a reversal of the funding-led expansion), indicating the liability-sensitive tilt is now a sustained headwind.
- Current-year estimate revisions turn negative, reversing the +7.5%/90-day positive momentum that underpins the valuation case.

## Conclusion

UMB Financial has completed the hard part of the HTLF deal and is now in the payoff phase, with Q1 2026 demonstrating the operating leverage the merger promised. At ~11x forward earnings and ~1.4x book, the market is pricing UMB roughly in line with lower-returning regional peers despite its superior returns and a scarce, capital-light fee franchise. Rising estimates, a consistent beat record, and analyst targets that read as floors support a constructive view. The offsetting concerns — CRE concentration, a price-sensitive uninsured deposit base, and a liability-sensitive balance sheet whose recent NIM gains were funding-led — are real and keep this from maximum conviction. We rate UMBF a **BUY** with disciplined sizing, favoring accumulation on any pullback toward the low-$130s where the reward/risk widens further. The recommendation continues the prior BUY calls, but the current entry is supported by a higher estimate base and completed integration, so the bar for reiteration is met by improved fundamentals rather than repetition.
