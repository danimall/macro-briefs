**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Casey's General Stores is the third-largest convenience-store chain in the U.S., operating 2,924 stores across 19-20 states with a structural advantage that is hard to replicate: roughly 71% of its locations sit in towns of fewer than 20,000 people, where it frequently faces no national competitor and functions as a quasi-grocery and food destination. The prepared-food and dispensed-beverage category, anchored by in-store pizza made since 1984, carries ~58% gross margins and is the centerpiece of a credible three-year growth plan that the company has executed against. Inside sales (prepared food plus grocery) are ~34% of revenue but ~63% of gross profit, the economic engine that justifies a premium to a typical gas-and-snacks retailer.

The investment debate centers on earnings quality rather than business quality. The +49% year-over-year net income growth in Q3 FY2026 was substantially driven by fuel margin expansion to 41.0 cents per gallon from 36.4 cpg a year earlier, a level management itself describes as "historically high" against a low-30s historical norm. Fuel is ~61% of revenue and ~33% of gross profit; a reversion in cents-per-gallon would meaningfully reduce EBITDA. The headline growth and the rising estimate revisions (+3.8% current-year, +5.5% next-year over 90 days) are real, but a portion of the current run-rate reflects a peak-cycle margin that the company does not control and does not promise to sustain.

At $761.91 the stock trades at roughly 37-42x forward EPS (consensus $18.11 current-year, $20.43 next-year), a premium even within a Food Retail peer group whose median P/E is elevated by Kroger and Albertsons trading near 40x. The stock has appreciated ~71% over the trailing year and reports fiscal Q4/FY2026 on June 9, with the options market pricing a ±8.8% implied move and near-term IV at 64.2% versus 25.7% historical volatility. We rate CASY a HOLD. The business is excellent and the execution is genuine, but the combination of a stretched multiple, peak-fuel-margin dependency, and a binary earnings event a few days away leaves the risk/reward balanced rather than favorable.

## Company Overview

Casey's operates a self-distributed convenience-store network concentrated in the Midwest, with roughly half its stores in Iowa, Missouri, and Illinois. The company owns most of its real estate and self-distributes via three owned distribution centers (Iowa, Indiana, Missouri) and a ~500-truck fleet, supporting a 9M+ member Casey's Rewards loyalty program. Fiscal year ends April 30.

Revenue splits into three categories with very different economics:
- **Fuel** — ~61% of revenue, ~33% of gross profit. A low-margin traffic driver.
- **Grocery and general merchandise** — ~26% of revenue, ~35% gross margin.
- **Prepared food and dispensed beverage** — ~10% of revenue but ~58% gross margin. The flagship is Casey's pizza.

The strategic logic is that fuel and the loyalty program drive traffic, while the high-margin food and grocery business converts that traffic into gross-profit dollars. The rural footprint amplifies this: in sub-20,000-population towns, Casey's is often the only food destination, supporting both pricing and customer frequency in a way scaled urban competitors cannot easily match.

The November 2024 Fikes acquisition (~$1.17B, 198 CEFCO stores) extended Casey's into Texas, Alabama, Florida, and Mississippi, outside its Midwest core, and added its first fuel terminal (Waco, TX) plus a wholesale fuel network (~2-3% of revenue), giving some upstream fuel-supply control. The acquired stores currently carry lower food-margin penetration, which is both an integration cost and a multi-year margin-uplift opportunity as Casey's installs full kitchens.

A note on data quality: a standardized XBRL pull shows FY2025 net income of $712M, but the 10-K income statement and EBITDA reconciliation report $546.5M (+8.9% YoY), consistent with the $14.64 diluted EPS. We use the $546.5M GAAP figure throughout. Any model built off the inflated XBRL number would understate the P/E and overstate margins.

Headline revenue is distorted by fuel-price swings (fuel revenue fell in FY2024 on lower pump prices despite volume growth). The cleaner read is gross-profit dollars, which compounded from $3.07B (FY23) to $3.75B (FY25), with EBITDA growing ~12% annually.

**Q3 FY2026 (ended Jan 31, 2026):**
- Net income +49.3% YoY to $130M; 9-month net income +23% to $552M.
- Same-store inside sales reaccelerating: prepared food +4.3%, grocery +4.0%, fuel gallons +0.4%. This reverses the FY25 deceleration when prepared-food SSS slowed from 6.8% to 3.5%.
- Fuel margin expanded to 41.0 cpg from 36.4 cpg, the single biggest profit driver of the quarter.
- Interest expense declined 20.5% as variable-rate debt was paid down.

**Balance sheet and leverage:** Total debt ~$2.43B; D/E ~1.34x; Altman Z of 2.98 (grey zone, normal for an asset-heavy retailer). The Fikes deal was funded with $1.1B of incremental debt (an $850M incremental term loan plus $250M of 5.23%/5.43% senior notes), nearly doubling goodwill from $653M to $1.24B. Deleveraging is proceeding faster than expected: the current ratio improved from 0.92x (April 2025) to 1.04x (Jan 2026), cash grew to $465M, and the revolver is undrawn. The structurally low current ratio reflects rapid inventory turnover and minimal working-capital needs, which is normal for the model.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** Operating cash flow of $1.09B (FY25) and FCF of ~$585M after ~$506M capex. Priorities are reinvestment and M&A (the largest use), a modest dividend (raised ~14% to $0.57/quarter), and opportunistic buybacks ($137M in 9M FY26; $156.8M remaining on a $400M authorization). ROE ~15.6%, ROA ~6.7%. The earnings-surprise record is consistently positive — four straight beats (14.5%, 4.9%, 12.7%, 32.2%) — which raises confidence in near-term consensus but also helps explain why the stock prices in continued execution.

The financial health picture is solid: high-quality cash generation, manageable and declining leverage, and FCF conversion supported by an owned real-estate base. The qualifier is that current profitability embeds a fuel-margin level that is cyclically elevated.

## Growth Analysis

Casey's three-year plan (announced June 2023) has three legs — grow store count, accelerate the food business, enhance operational efficiency — and the company has delivered tangible progress on all three.

**Store growth:** Store count reached 2,924, with 27 new builds plus 27 acquired stores added in 9M FY26 against 34 divested/closed. The c-store industry remains fragmented and consolidating, giving a disciplined acquirer a long runway of organic builds plus tuck-in M&A.

**Food acceleration:** The Q3 FY26 reacceleration of prepared-food SSS to +4.3% is the most important positive trend reversal in the story, validating the highest-margin category. The Fikes CEFCO stores carry lower food penetration, providing a multi-year margin tailwind as Casey's installs kitchens, integration optionality beyond cost synergies.

**Efficiency:** Twelve-plus consecutive quarters of same-store labor-hour reductions is a real operating win that has offset wage inflation.

Estimate revisions are positive but moderate, with current-quarter EPS up +6.6% over 90 days to $3.31. This is healthy earnings momentum, but not the sharp inflection (>30% revisions, >40% revenue growth) that would mark a structurally mispriced compounder. Revenue is growing high-single-digit, much of which is the Fikes store base annualizing. The forward growth algorithm — mid-single-digit organic gross-profit growth plus M&A plus efficiency — supports low-double-digit EPS growth if fuel margins merely hold. If fuel margins normalize, near-term EPS growth would slow materially.

## Valuation Assessment

At $761.91 on $14.64 trailing EPS, CASY trades at ~52x trailing earnings. These are premium multiples on any basis.

The forward multiple is the right lens given the earnings trajectory, and even there the stock is expensive. Peer medians in Food Retail show a P/E of 39.1, but that figure is inflated by Kroger (40.0) and Albertsons (39.1) — both lower-growth, lower-margin grocers — while Sprouts (15.0) and Maplebear (22.8) sit far below. Casey's deserves a premium to that group given its superior unit economics, food-margin engine, and disciplined consolidation. The question is how large a premium is warranted at a point where a meaningful slice of earnings comes from a fuel-margin level management calls historically high.

Stress-testing the multiple: if fuel CPG reverted toward a normalized low-30s level, a substantial portion of recent EBITDA growth would reverse. The bull case (KeyBanc, $860 PT) argues margins go higher, citing structural fuel-retail consolidation and pricing discipline. That may prove correct, but it is the opposite of margin-of-safety positioning — paying ~40x forward for earnings that are partly cyclically peaked, on the assumption the peak extends.

We do not penalize CASY for having appreciated over the trailing year; that move reflects genuine fundamental improvement (gross profit +12% CAGR, food SSS reacceleration, deleveraging). The valuation concern is forward-looking: the current price prices in continued execution and durable fuel margins simultaneously, leaving little cushion. The analyst consensus mean of $842.81 and median of $875 sit above the current price, but with the stock at ~40x forward we treat these as reflecting the same continued-execution optimism embedded in the price, not independent confirmation of upside. The valuation score is 4.0; the multiple is stretched relative to the durable, ex-fuel-margin earnings power.

## Competitive Landscape

Casey's competitive position is genuinely strong and stable-to-improving, which is why the business merits a premium even as the valuation gives us pause.

- **Rural moat:** In towns under 20,000 people, Casey's often has no national competitor and serves as a food destination. This is structurally hard for scaled players to replicate profitably; the unit economics that work for Casey's in a small town do not attract a 7-Eleven or a dollar-store fuel offering.
- **Owned food and distribution infrastructure:** In-house kitchens and self-distribution via owned DCs create margin and quality advantages over c-stores reliant on third-party direct-store-delivery.
- **Loyalty:** Rewards members drive frequency and data.
- **Consolidation lever:** A fragmented, consolidating industry gives a disciplined acquirer with a repeatable kitchen-installation playbook a durable growth path.

The trajectory is improving on the metrics that matter — food SSS reaccelerating, store count growing, labor hours falling. The competitive risks are secular rather than share-loss: long-term fuel-demand decline from EV adoption (mitigated, not eliminated, by the rural footprint where EV penetration lags) and structural tobacco/nicotine decline (~9% of revenue and gross profit). These are slow-moving and partly priced. Competitive position scores 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel-margin dependency (the dominant risk).** Current earnings strength is substantially a function of 41 cpg margins versus a low-30s historical norm. Management explicitly flags CPG as "historically high" and warns it may not persist. The asymmetry is to the downside: bulls assume the peak extends, while a reversion would dent EBITDA in a way the headline numbers currently mask. This is the central, underappreciated cyclical risk in an otherwise stable-looking staples name.

**Secular fuel-demand / EV transition.** Long-term threat to the largest revenue line; rural footprint buys time (only 269 charging stations at 58 stores) but does not eliminate it.

**Tobacco/nicotine decline.** Subject to FDA regulation, excise taxes, and falling consumption, plus dependence on manufacturer rebates.

**Acquisition integration.** Fikes is the largest deal in company history and takes Casey's into new states including hurricane-exposed Florida. Integration is progressing (incorporated into internal controls as of Nov 1, 2025; current ratio already positive), reducing balance-sheet risk faster than expected, but the food-margin uplift thesis on CEFCO stores is unproven.

**Input costs and interchange.** Cheese is the largest food commodity cost; credit-card interchange fees exceeded $250M in FY25 and scale with fuel prices. Neither is controllable.

**Binary near-term event.** Fiscal Q4/FY2026 reports June 9. The key item will be whether fuel margins held in Q4 and what management signals about CPG durability.

No material risk factors were added or modified in the Q3 FY26 10-Q. The two FLSA store-manager misclassification suits (White; Kessler) were settled in Q3 FY26 for an immaterial amount, removing a litigation overhang. Risk factors score 5.0, reflecting the unpriced peak-fuel-margin asymmetry and the imminent binary event rather than any governance red flag.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful imminent stress into the June 9 earnings print. Near-term ATM IV spiked to 64.2% from 37.2% over the past month, against historical volatility of just 25.7% — a rich 38.5-point spread sitting at the 81st percentile of the current chain. The P/C OI ratio flipped from 0.33 to 1.17 as the stock fell ~13% over the same window, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (64.2% near-term versus 36.2% at six months). Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-0.8% near-term, steepening to -5.1% at six months), so this reads as two-sided event hedging into a binary catalyst rather than a directional bearish bet. The market is pricing genuine uncertainty around the upcoming print, and the asymmetry sits with the fuel-margin disclosure. We would not establish a new position immediately ahead of this event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($900-1,000):** Fuel margins hold at or above current levels (KeyBanc's thesis on structural fuel-retail consolidation), inside-sales SSS sustains its FY26 reacceleration, the Fikes CEFCO stores deliver the food-margin uplift as kitchens are installed, and store-count growth plus efficiency gains compound EPS at low-double-digit rates. On ~$20.43 next-year EPS rising toward $22-23, a sustained ~40-45x multiple supports $900-1,000. This requires the fuel-margin peak to persist and the premium multiple to hold.

**Bear case ($560-605):** Fuel CPG reverts toward the low-30s historical norm, compressing EBITDA and revealing that a chunk of recent growth was cyclical. The premium multiple compresses toward the low-30s on lower and more clearly cyclical earnings. On ~$17-18 of normalized EPS at ~32-35x, the stock trades to $560-605 — roughly the consensus low target and ~21-26% below the current price.

**Base case (~$760-800):** Fuel margins hold near current elevated levels for several more quarters but the market discounts their durability, capping multiple expansion. EPS grows to ~$20-21 next year, and a ~38x multiple supports a price roughly in line with today, with modest upside if Q4 reaffirms the food reacceleration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — a high-quality consolidator executing on food and efficiency — but the premium valuation and peak-fuel-margin dependency cap near-term upside, and a binary event is a few days away.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q4/FY2026 (June 9) confirms fuel margins holding near 40+ cpg with management commentary supporting durability, AND inside-sales SSS sustaining the +4% reacceleration, demonstrating the earnings base is more structural than cyclical.
- A pullback toward $650-680 (~32-34x current-year forward EPS) that restores margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals.
- Forward EPS revisions accelerating above +10% over 90 days alongside evidence of CEFCO food-margin uplift.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Fuel CPG reverting toward the low-30s in two consecutive quarters, confirming the peak-cycle thesis, with no offsetting inside-sales strength.
- Inside-sales SSS rolling back below ~2% (loss of the food-acceleration leg).
- A materially dilutive acquisition or integration stumble that reverses the deleveraging trajectory.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediate — fiscal Q4/FY2026 results on June 9, 2026 are the next and most important data point. The fuel-margin disclosure and forward CPG commentary in that report will be the decisive input.

## Conclusion

Casey's is a well-run, structurally advantaged convenience-store consolidator with a defensible rural moat, a high-margin food engine, disciplined capital allocation, and demonstrated execution against a credible three-year plan. The financial health is solid, the competitive trajectory is improving, and management's incentives are well-aligned with shareholders, supported by two recent open-market director purchases.

The constraint is valuation against earnings quality. At ~37-42x forward EPS, the stock prices in continued execution and durable fuel margins at the same time, while management itself flags that those margins are historically high and may not persist. With a binary FY2026 earnings report due June 9, the risk/reward into the print is balanced. The business is one to own at the right price; today's price, ahead of a catalyst that could resolve the central earnings-quality debate in either direction, is not compelling enough to add. We rate CASY a HOLD and would revisit on either a confirmed fuel-margin durability signal or a pullback toward the low-$700s/high-$600s.
