**Research Date: August 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Two Harbors is priced as a nearly-consummated cash merger rather than as a mortgage REIT. Common stockholders approved CrossCountry Mortgage's ("CCM") amended agreement on July 2, 2026 at $12.00 per share in cash. An August 3, 2026 Form 8-K disclosed that all required state and agency approvals had been obtained except one state, with closing scheduled for the business day following receipt, and reset the pro-rated closing-quarter dividend to $0.34 × (days from June 30, 2026 through the day before closing) ÷ 92. That formula accrues $0.0036957 per share per day and is additive to the merger consideration.

At the frozen price of $12.035, the arithmetic is mechanical. A close on August 31 delivers $12.00 + $0.2254 = $12.225 (+1.58%). September 15 delivers $12.281 (+2.04%). September 30 delivers $12.336 (+2.50%). Because the consideration is fixed while the stub accrues, delay increases proceeds. Against that, a break returns the equity to a going concern with $10.68 of common book value per share at June 30, 2026 (−11.3%), and more plausibly to the high-$9s, where the stock traded before the December 17, 2025 announcement ($9.91 on December 16) and where the mortgage-REIT peer set still trades at 31–57% discounts to 52-week highs. UWM Holdings' August 10, 2026 suit seeks more than $500 million (roughly $4.76 per share, or 44.5% of the $1,123 million common equity base), appears in no Two Harbors filing, carries no accrual, and would remain with a standalone TWO if the deal fails. Using a $9.25 break reference, break-even requires roughly a 92% probability of closing.

I judge closing probability at 92–95% given the completed stockholder vote, the near-complete approval slate, CCM's payment of UWM's $25.4 million termination fee on TWO's behalf, and CCM's escalation of the price from $10.80 to $12.00 (approximately $126 million of incremental consideration). That produces expected value of roughly zero to +0.7% over two to six weeks with a fat left tail. Existing holders should hold to collect $12.00 plus the stub; new capital has no reason to underwrite one state regulator's timing for a 2% gross return. This is an upgrade from three prior AVOID ratings, driven by the completed vote and the price now sitting at expected proceeds rather than ahead of an unsettled bidding contest, not by any improvement in the underlying business, which remains in runoff.

## Company Overview

Two Harbors is an internally managed Maryland REIT operating as a single segment, pairing two asset classes plus an operating platform:

- **Mortgage servicing rights**: $2,336.3 million fair value at June 30, 2026 on 655,023 loans and $155.1 billion of unpaid principal balance. Weighted average gross coupon 3.5%, original FICO 760, original LTV 72.9%, 60+ day delinquencies 0.8%, three-month CPR 6.3%. Roughly $70.6 billion of UPB carries coupons at or below 3.25% against a ~6.5% prevailing 30-year rate, so refinance incentive is minimal.
- **Agency RMBS**: $5,091 million fair value, essentially all Fannie ($2,659M) and Freddie ($2,422M) pass-throughs, weighted average coupon 5.45%.
- **RoundPoint Mortgage Servicing**: wholly owned servicer with agency approvals, performing substantially all servicing for TWO's own MSR plus $40.8 billion of third-party subservicing. Total serviced assets 840,537 loans / $196.2 billion UPB.

Derived MSR valuation: $2,336.3M ÷ $155,106.7M UPB = 1.506% of UPB. Against a disclosed 25.3bp net servicing fee, that implies a ~5.95x multiple, consistent with the MD&A's observation that servicing multiples increased in Q2 2026 on higher short rates and float value.

## Financial Analysis

**The 2025 loss was a legal settlement, not operating collapse.** FY2025 pretax loss was −$445.4 million; net loss approximately −$454.3 million, corroborated by the equity statement (cumulative earnings fell from $1,648.785M to $1,194.485M, exactly −$454.300M). After ~$52.8 million of preferred dividends, loss to common was roughly −$507 million, or about −$4.84 per share, which explains the −$4.88 trailing EPS and the meaningless −16.9x reported P/E in the market data.

The driver was the PRCM Advisers / Pine River litigation: a $199.935 million accrual in Q2 2025, settled in Q3 2025 for $375 million cash. That is approximately $3.58 per common share against an observed 2025 common book value decline of $3.34 (from $14.47 to $11.13). My reconciliation closes: comprehensive loss to common of roughly −$1.78 per share plus $1.52 of declared dividends equals −$3.30, within share-count rounding of the observed decline. Absent the settlement, 2025 book value would have been roughly flat to modestly higher.

Two qualifications matter. The reported loss was cushioned by a +$320 million swing in other comprehensive income as AOCI recovered from −$320.5M to −$0.087M; that is reversible mark-to-market, and AOCI deteriorated again to −$45.7M by June 30, 2026. The FY2024 proxy shows indexed cumulative TSR of 36.62 against a peer group at 79.96, roughly half the peer return in every year from 2020 through 2024, well before the settlement.

**Q2 2026 shows operating recovery on a shrinking base.**

Three points. First, net interest margin is structurally negative: the levered Agency book does not cover its own funding at a 4.12% weighted average repo rate, 6.67% on MSR revolvers and 9.375% on senior notes. Earnings come from servicing income and derivative carry. Second, servicing income is eroding — servicing fees fell from $124.4M to $102.3M and float income from $28.7M to $22.2M year over year. Third, merger costs are now material: $13.6 million in Q2 and $19.3 million in H1 2026, pushing the annualized operating expense ratio to 11.8% from 8.5%.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets fell 18.7% in six months to $8,831 million; equity $1,745 million. Debt-to-equity fell from 4.8x to 3.8x, and the company's economic measure including implied TBA debt from 7.0x to 6.0x. The $261.9 million convertible notes were repaid in cash at their January 15, 2026 maturity, eliminating that dilution channel. Covenant headroom is comfortable: indebtedness/tangible net worth 4.0x against an 8.0x maximum, liquidity $685.2M against $149.0M minimum, unrestricted cash $642.7M against $75.0M minimum.

The offsets: $5,084M of the $5,091M AFS book is pledged to repo, leaving roughly $7.1 million of unused capacity against unpledged securities; $5.709 billion of the $6.618 billion total borrowings matures in the remainder of 2026, dominated by repo at a 64-day weighted average; 26.5% of total assets are Level 3, including 100% of the MSR book valued on an internal DCF; and 85.0% of assets are carried at fair value. Dividend coverage is marginal — H1 2026 income to common of $0.65 per share against $0.68 declared, after cuts from $0.45 (Q1 2025) to $0.34.

H1 2026 operating cash flow of $249.3 million should not be annualized. The prior-year comparison includes a non-cash add-back of the $199.9 million accrual (the $375 million cash payment landed in Q3 2025), the current period includes cash merger costs, and investing/financing flows (+$1.591B / −$2.037B) reflect portfolio liquidation rather than recurring generation.

These figures set the floor for a break scenario. They do not determine the return from here, because the equity's cash flows terminate at closing.

## Growth Analysis

The core earning asset is in runoff. MSR additions from purchases were $2.648 million in H1 2026 against $92.849 million in H1 2025 — period flows, not balances. Owned MSR UPB fell from $162.5 billion to $155.1 billion; total serviced UPB from $203.2 billion to $196.2 billion; servicing advances from $103.5 million to $66.8 million. Subservicing grew marginally ($40.49B to $40.83B), which reflects owned-book runoff against roughly flat third-party volume rather than diversification; no counterparty-concentration data for the subservicing business is available.

Two explanations coexist and both fit the data. The 10-Q lists "restrictions on our business activities imposed by the Amended CCM Merger Agreement" among its risks, so operating covenants are the likely primary cause. The MD&A separately notes that MSR bulk and flow pricing tightened in Q2 2026 with demand outstripping supply, so a price-disciplined buyer might have stood aside independently. Neither reading implies distress.

Consensus estimates are noise in this situation. Current-year EPS of $1.20 has been revised −5.8% over 30 days and +18.1% over 90 days, with next-year at $1.18 essentially flat — figures describing a business that will not report as a public company. The Q2 2026 print of $0.28 versus $0.28 estimated (a −1.6% surprise) cannot be reconciled to the $0.46 GAAP diluted result; the basis differs and no beat/miss conclusion should be drawn.

For a common holder, growth participation is capped at $12.00 plus the accruing stub. Any value CCM extracts from recapturing refinancing borrowers into its own $51 billion annual origination channel, or from reducing TWO's modeled $65.03 annual cost to service across a combined servicing book, accrues to the acquirer.

## Valuation Assessment

**Deal arithmetic (primary).** Consideration is $12.00 cash. The stub formula verifies exactly against the original declaration: 33 days × $0.34 ÷ 92 = $0.12196, matching the July 23 declaration for an assumed August 3 close. Daily accrual $0.0036957.

| Closing date | Accrual days | Stub | Total proceeds | Return from $12.035 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aug 31, 2026 | 61 | $0.2254 | $12.225 | +1.58% |
| Sep 15, 2026 | 76 | $0.2809 | $12.281 | +2.04% |
| Sep 30, 2026 | 91 | $0.3363 | $12.336 | +2.50% |

One structural caveat: the disclosed denominator is the 92 days in Q3 2026, so the formula as written contemplates a Q3 closing. A Q4 close would require a recalculation the 8-K does not specify, and I would not extrapolate past September 30 with confidence. The record date is the last trading day immediately before the effective time, so a holder today receives both components provided ownership is continuous.

**Premium to book.** $12.00 ÷ $10.68 (June 30, 2026 common book value) = 1.123x, a 12.3% premium; against $10.57 at March 31, 13.5%. HousingWire's characterization of a "19% premium to end-of-March tangible book value" cannot be reproduced from filing figures and should be treated as unverified; use 12–14%. An above-book cash exit is favorable relative to sector norms, and it was produced by competitive tension rather than a single negotiated price.

**Peer context.** The mortgage-REIT comparables (ABR, ARI, ADAM, PMT, FBRT) trade at a median 8.4x P/E and 13.3% yield, with four of five 31–57% below 52-week highs. Those multiples are irrelevant to TWO's return from here — its terminal value is contractually fixed — but they are highly relevant to the break case, because they describe the multiple environment TWO would re-enter.

**Break reference.** Book value of $10.68 is the optimistic anchor (−11.3%). More realistically, a broken deal leaves a shrinking MSR book with no replenishment pipeline, $19.3 million of sunk merger costs, an unaccrued claim of more than $500 million, and a sector trading at discounts to book. TWO traded at $9.91 on December 16, 2025, the day before the UWM announcement, against $11.13 of year-end book — roughly 0.89x. Applying 0.85–0.90x to $10.68 gives $9.10–$9.60. I use $9.25 (−23.1%).

**Expected value.** Break-even requires p × 2.0% = (1−p) × 23.1%, or p ≈ 92%. At 95%: +0.74%. At 92%: approximately zero. At 90%: −0.5%. The valuation score of 4.0 reflects a security priced within about 1% of its most likely settlement value with no margin of safety and negative payoff skew — fairly priced, not attractively priced.

## Competitive Landscape

The asset quality that attracted two strategic bidders is real. A 760-FICO, 0.8%-delinquent, 6.3%-CPR servicing book with $70.6 billion of UPB at coupons deeply out of the money against a 6.5% mortgage rate has minimal prepayment risk, which is why it was contested. The MD&A describes a seller's market in MSR: demand outstripping supply, bulk and flow pricing tightened, multiples generally increased on higher short rates lifting float value. Agency RMBS conditions were supportive in the quarter — current-coupon nominal spread to swaps tightened 13bp to 128bp, OAS tightened 10bp to 50bp, repo available at 12–15bp over SOFR across 21 counterparties.

CCM originated $51 billion in 2025 (No. 7 overall, first among distributed-retail lenders) and held a $202 billion MSR portfolio at Q1 2026 per Inside Mortgage Finance. Adding TWO's $155 billion owned plus $41 billion subserviced creates a top-tier servicing platform where an originator can recapture refinancing borrowers, cross-sell, and strip duplicate overhead — economics a REIT cannot fully monetize. That explains why two originators paid above book for an asset the public market discounted for years.

The trajectory for the standalone entity is deteriorating: the owned book is in runoff, five-year relative TSR ran roughly half the peer group, and the platform is being absorbed. The competitive-position score of 4.5 balances genuine asset quality against a shrinking franchise with no independent forward path.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal completion (dominant).** One state approval was outstanding as of August 3, closing scheduled for the following business day. The company had guided to an August 3 close that did not occur. CCM is privately held, so the sufficiency of its financing for approximately $1.262 billion of common consideration plus a roughly $622 million preferred redemption cannot be verified from public filings. No commitment letters were reviewed.

**UWM litigation (unquantified).** Per HousingWire and National Mortgage Professional (both August 10, 2026), UWM Holdings and UWM Acquisitions 1 sued TWO in the District of Maryland for alleged willful breach of the December 2025 merger agreement and fraud, arguing the $25.4 million termination fee does not cap liability for willful breach. The suit appears in no TWO filing — the Q2 10-Q (filed July 29, twelve days before the complaint) states the company is not party to any litigation or threatened litigation and Note 13 discloses no material contingency. There is no accrual, no range of reasonably possible loss, and no company characterization of the merits. If the merger closes, this liability transfers with the company and common holders are made whole at $12.00 regardless.

UWM's allegations regarding roughly $35 million of management change-in-control benefits (approximately $0.33 per share, 2.8% of consideration) and shareholder-outreach conduct are unproven claims by an adverse litigant, sourced through secondary reporting; the authoritative golden-parachute table in the merger proxy was not reviewed. Contrary considerations are substantial: UWM's $12.50 headline included a stock election at a fixed 2.3328 ratio, and UWM subsequently reported a $451.9 million Q2 2026 net loss including a $603.2 million derivatives loss connected to the potential acquisition alongside a $2.05 billion capital raise. A board preferring $12.00 of certain cash over partially-stock $12.50 from a bidder with a volatile balance sheet is defensible, and stockholders ratified it. I take no position on the merits.

**Portfolio convexity.** The Item 3 sensitivity table shows total net assets declining $31.9 million at −50bp and $44.0 million at +50bp, against $7.5 million and $14.9 million at ∓25bp. The MSR/RMBS pairing neutralizes first-order duration but is short gamma — book value falls in both rate directions on a large move. Management limits the reliability of its own estimates to moves under 25bp. This matters mainly in a break scenario, and it is live: the MD&A notes market expectations shifted from a possible December 2026 cut to multiple hikes, with the 2-year up 38bp to 4.17% and the 10-year up 15bp to 4.47% in the quarter.

**Funding and valuation concentration.** $5.709 billion maturing in the remainder of 2026, three MSR repo facilities expiring October–December 2026 ($575 million combined), the servicing-advance facility expiring December 14, 2026. MSR sensitivities: a 10% adverse OAS move costs $57.2 million, 10% adverse prepayment $44.9 million, 10% adverse cost-to-service $29.4 million.

**Governance.** No open-market insider purchases in the reviewed window (May 2025–May 2026). Two directors sold on May 15, 2026 at $12.57 and $12.575 — above the $12.00 consideration, two days after the board rejected UWM's $12.50 proposal — while retaining substantial positions (Kasnet 95,993 shares; Abraham 35,039). This is consistent with monetizing a bid-driven premium not expected to persist, and also consistent with routine post-vesting liquidity; motive cannot be determined from transaction structure. Separately, a December 18, 2025 CEO grant of 309,187 restricted shares, one day after the UWM signing, had 154,593 shares immediately withheld for tax at grant — an atypical structure with multiple benign explanations; the award agreement was not reviewed. No common or preferred repurchases occurred in H1 2025 or H1 2026 despite existing authorizations and years of sub-book trading.

**Preferred mechanics.** Three series (24,870,817 shares) carried at $601.5 million against a $621.8 million liquidation preference remain outstanding at the effective time; the surviving company delivers a redemption notice and CCM, acting on the company's behalf, sets aside $25.00 per share plus accrued dividends in trust. The $20.3 million carrying-to-preference gap accrues to preferred holders. Series C already floats at 3-month Term SOFR + 5.011% + 0.26161%; Series A and B convert in April and July 2027.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is the most informative element and it corroborates deal pinning rather than distress. ATM IV runs 151.1% at the 3-day expiry, 7.4% at one month, 5.1% at three months and 2.6% at six months. Six-month implied volatility of 2.6% on a common equity is the market pricing near-certainty that the shares will be cash by December. The 151.1% near-term figure conflicts internally with the reported ±2.1% nearest-expiry implied move and is best read as a thin-chain artifact on a $12-pinned stock with negligible extrinsic value, not as a signal of imminent stress. Put/call open interest of 18.20, up from 1.74 in late May, is consistent with merger-arb downside hedging — buying cheap protection against the break tail — rather than a directional bearish view. Short interest of 5.6% of float at 1.7 days to cover fits the same interpretation. Since May 22 the stock has traded in a $11.98–$12.14 band on elevated volume.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($12.34, +2.5%).** The last state approval arrives in late August or September, the merger closes, and holders receive $12.00 plus a stub of $0.23–$0.34 depending on timing. Delay is accretive under the disclosed formula, so slippage within Q3 improves rather than impairs the outcome. This is the dominant scenario on a probability-weighted basis: the stockholder vote is complete, all approvals but one are in hand, CCM funded UWM's $25.4 million termination fee on TWO's behalf, escalated its price by $1.20 per share (roughly $126 million), reaffirmed $12.00 as best and final, and waived the dividend covenant to permit the stub. A close beyond September 30 introduces formula ambiguity that could add or subtract a few cents.

**Bear case ($9.25, −23%).** The final approval stalls or CCM's financing proves inadequate. The equity reverts to a standalone mortgage REIT with $10.68 of book value, an owned MSR book shrinking at roughly 4.5% per half-year with purchases effectively halted, negative GAAP net interest margin, a dividend already cut 24% and not fully covered in H1, $5.7 billion of 2026 repo rollover, and an unaccrued claim of more than $500 million equal to 44.5% of common equity. The sector trades at 31–57% discounts to 52-week highs and TWO itself traded at 0.89x book pre-announcement, pointing to $9.10–$9.60. A severe outcome in which UWM prevails on willful breach would take book materially lower, though on a multi-year litigation timeline.

**Base case ($12.28, +2.0%).** Close in the first half of September; proceeds of $12.00 plus roughly $0.28.

Weighted at a 93% closing probability: 0.93 × 2.0% + 0.07 × (−23.1%) = +0.25%. That is approximately fair, which is what the HOLD reflects. The dimension scores are low because the underlying entity is a runoff balance sheet with concentrated binary risk and no growth participation; the recommendation is neutral rather than negative because the price sits at expected proceeds, not above them.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon: two to eight weeks.** The thesis resolves on a single unannounced event — receipt of the final state approval, with closing the following business day.

**Base case target $12.28** ($12.00 consideration + $0.28 stub at a mid-September close; +2.0% from $12.035).
**Bull case target $12.34** (+2.5%, September 30 close, maximum in-quarter stub accrual).
**Bear case target $9.25** (−23.1%; 0.87x the $10.68 June 30 common book value, consistent with the pre-announcement 0.89x and current peer discounts). The optimistic break anchor is $10.68 (−11.3%).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE, for arbitrage-mandated capital only):**
1. An 8-K confirming receipt of the final state approval and a dated effective time, which would compress residual uncertainty to settlement mechanics.
2. Disclosure of CCM's committed financing sufficient for the ~$1.262 billion common payment plus the ~$622 million preferred redemption.
3. A price dislocation below $11.75 without a change in deal status, which would widen the gross spread to roughly 4.5% and improve the payoff ratio materially.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any TWO or CCM disclosure of a financing condition, adverse regulatory determination, or extension of the outside date.
2. Company disclosure of the UWM matter with an accrual, a range of reasonably possible loss, or an indication that it affects closing conditions.
3. A Q4 close, which triggers stub-formula ambiguity the August 3 8-K does not resolve.
4. Two consecutive sessions below $11.50 (the low analyst target) on volume, which would signal informed repricing of closing probability.

**Reassessment: immediately on the next 8-K.** Absent one, reassess by September 30, when the disclosed stub denominator ceases to apply cleanly.

Holders who prefer to eliminate the break tail can sell in the market at $12.035, forgoing $0.14–$0.30 of eventual proceeds (1.2–2.5%) to remove a 5–8% chance of a 23% decline. The expected cost of that insurance is roughly one percentage point, which makes the choice close to neutral and a matter of risk tolerance rather than analysis.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** The residual return in TWO is a fixed $12.00 plus a stub dividend accruing at $0.0036957 per share per day, worth $12.22–$12.34 for an August-to-September close against a frozen price of $12.035. That is 1.6–2.5% of gross upside against a break scenario pointing to the high-$9s, requiring roughly 92% closing confidence merely to break even. The evidence supports 92–95%: the vote is done, all approvals but one state are in hand, and CCM has demonstrated financial commitment by paying UWM's $25.4 million termination fee and raising its price by $1.20 per share. Expected value is therefore approximately zero to modestly positive.

That justifies moving off the prior AVOID, because the price no longer sits ahead of an unsettled contest, without justifying anything stronger. The dimension scores remain weak because the underlying business is a runoff balance sheet: MSR purchases have effectively stopped at $2.6 million in H1 2026 versus $92.8 million a year earlier, serviced UPB has fallen from $203.2 billion to $196.2 billion, GAAP net interest margin is negative, and the dividend was not fully covered in H1. The two genuinely unresolved items are one state regulator's timing and a $500 million-plus claim that appears in no company filing; these are the risks a buyer at $12.035 is being paid 2% to absorb. Existing holders should collect the cash. New capital should look elsewhere.
