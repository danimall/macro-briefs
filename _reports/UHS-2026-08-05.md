## Executive Summary

Universal Health Services is a $17.4B-revenue hospital operator with two segments — acute care (58% of revenue) and behavioral health (42% of revenue but roughly 56% of combined segment pretax income at a 19.2% margin). On surface metrics it is one of the cheapest large-cap healthcare franchises available: 7.3x trailing GAAP diluted EPS of $23.10, roughly 7.0x next-year consensus of $24.13, and an enterprise value near $14.9B against FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA less noncontrolling interests of $2.61-2.72B, or about 5.6x. Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 1.7x, unusually conservative for the industry, and covenant margins sit at the lowest tier of the leverage grid.

The reason for the discount is specific and quantified in the company's own filings. UHS disclosed a FY2025 net benefit from state Medicaid supplemental payment programs of approximately $1.339B (the Q1 2026 10-Q cites ~$1.361B; the ~$22M difference is unreconciled). Against FY2025 income before income taxes of $1,972M, that is 67.9-69.0% of pretax income. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, enacted July 4, 2025, reduces that annual net benefit by an estimated $432-480M by 2032, phasing in from state fiscal 2028. At a 23.5% effective tax rate and roughly 60.8M total shares across all classes, that is $5.44-$6.04 of annual EPS at risk on a static basis. The Q2 2026 print made the mechanism concrete: revenue of roughly $4.64B (+8.3%) exceeded expectations and management described a volume rebound in both segments, yet the full-year profit outlook came down, with Reuters reporting the cause as "changes in reimbursements related to certain Medicaid supplemental payment programs."

Consensus has responded. Current-quarter EPS estimates are down 7.5% in 30 days, next-quarter down 8.4%, current year down 3.8% and next year down 4.6%. Meanwhile the shares have rallied 19% off the June 8 low of $140.08. Falling estimates against a rising price is the configuration that most often precedes disappointment, and it is the reason we are not treating the 7x multiple as an entry signal. We rate UHS HOLD with an overall score of 5.05, continuing a declining trajectory (6.8 in February to 5.6 in July to 5.05 now). The decline is driven by new information — a guidance cut, a first adjusted-EPS miss in the most recent quarter, and a fresh round of negative revisions — not by the share price.

## Company Overview

UHS is a holding company; all operations run through subsidiaries, including the management company UHS of Delaware. As of March 31, 2026 it operated 375 inpatient and 168 outpatient facilities across 40 states, Washington D.C., the United Kingdom and Puerto Rico. The facility count is dominated by behavioral health (182 U.S. inpatient, 110 U.S. outpatient, 161 U.K. inpatient, plus Puerto Rico), while revenue is dominated by 29 acute care hospitals plus 35 free-standing emergency departments and a captive commercial health insurer.

Acute care markets are concentrated in Las Vegas/Nevada, South Texas (McAllen), South Florida, Southern California and Washington D.C. Behavioral health is the differentiator: UHS is one of very few national operators of inpatient psychiatric capacity, a fragmented and capital-intensive category with structural supply constraints.

Payer mix at Q1 2026: managed care HMO/PPO 27%, Medicaid 16%, managed Medicaid 14%, managed Medicare 12%, Medicare 11%, U.K. 6%, other patient 7%, other non-patient 7%. Medicaid plus managed Medicaid rose to 30% of consolidated revenue from 28% a year earlier. Inside the behavioral segment the figure is 43%.

Two greenfield acute hospitals define current growth capital: Cedar Hill Regional Medical Center in D.C. (opened April 2025, a $49M pretax loss in FY2025, with the District owning the ~$417M facility and UHS leasing for a nominal amount over 75 years under an obligation to operate) and the Alan B. Miller Medical Center in Palm Beach Gardens, scheduled to open in Q2 2026. Combined pretax losses from the two rose $11M year over year in Q1 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** FY2025 revenue was $17.36B, up 9.7%, with income from operations of $1,994M and an 11.5% operating margin, up from 10.6% in FY2024. Q1 2026 revenue was $4,495.2M (+9.6%) at an 11.2% operating margin; Q2 2026 was approximately $4.64B (+8.3%) at 11.1%, per external reporting not yet corroborated by a filed 10-Q. Margin has therefore been flat to slightly down through the first half of 2026 after a strong FY2025 expansion that was substantially attributable to supplemental payments rather than operating leverage.

**Cash conversion is the weakest element.** FY2025 operating cash flow fell to $1.864B from $2.067B while net income rose. The 10-K attributes this to a roughly $385M receivables drag, of which $145M was Medicaid supplemental payment receivables. Days sales outstanding rose to 55 days at December 31, 2025 from 50 days. Against $1.015B of capex, FY2025 free cash flow was $849M — a 4.9% margin on revenue. Q1 2026 improved: operating cash flow of $401.6M (+11.6%) with the receivables drag narrowing to $123.9M from $218.4M, and capex of $217.2M for roughly $184M of quarterly free cash flow.

We do not annualize that quarter. FY2026 capex guidance of $950M-$1.1B leaves $733-883M after Q1, so capex is back-half weighted; cash taxes paid in Q1 were only $8.3M against a $110.4M book provision, and accrued/deferred taxes added $104.8M to Q1 operating cash flow, which reverses. A normalized FY2026 range of $1.9-2.0B operating cash flow less roughly $1.0B capex gives free cash flow of approximately $0.9-1.0B, or an 8.8-9.8% free cash flow yield on roughly $10.2B of equity value. That is a genuine support for the stock and the strongest single item in the financial-health case.

The interpretive question is whether rising DSO reflects timing (CMS preprint approval lags on supplemental payments, which accrue on approval and collect later) or payer friction and self-pay mix. Both explanations are present in the disclosures. The Q1 improvement supports the timing reading, but one quarter does not settle it, and the Q2 guidance cut on supplemental-payment approvals argues the timing risk is live.

**Balance sheet.** At March 31, 2026: total assets $15,681M, cash $119.0M, net receivables $2,745.1M, goodwill $3,980.7M, net PP&E $7,063.6M, total debt carrying value approximately $4.7B against a $4.5B fair value, and UHS common stockholders' equity of $7,464.9M. Total debt to total capitalization was 39%. Net debt of roughly $4.58B against the $2.665B guided EBITDA midpoint is about 1.72x. Altman Z-Score computed on the FY2025 10-K is approximately 3.56, in the safe zone. Covenant compliance was confirmed at both March 31, 2026 and December 31, 2025, with margins at the lowest grid tier (SOFR + 1.25%).

**Two financing items compress forward earnings.** First, $700M of 1.65% senior secured notes mature September 1, 2026, and the 10-Q states plainly that UHS "will refinance the 2026 Notes at significantly higher interest rates which will significantly increase our interest expense." At 5.5% the incremental cost is $26.95M pretax, roughly $0.34 per share after tax; at 6.0%, roughly $0.38. An 8-K dated July 21, 2026 (Items 1.01/2.03/9.01) is consistent with either this refinancing or Talkspace financing, but the document was not retrievable and no terms are confirmed. Second, the $800M 2030 notes and $500M 2032 notes both carry 2.65% coupons — the reason fair value sits below carrying value — so the cost-of-capital step-up is staged across multiple years, not a single event.

The April 22, 2026 Eleventh Amendment added $900M of capacity: revolver to $1.5B, term loan A to $1.455B, plus a new $400M delayed-draw term loan A to be drawn at Talkspace closing. Assuming full debt funding of the $835M acquisition, pro forma total debt of roughly $5.5B implies about 2.06x — still conservative, but the trajectory is toward more leverage into a legislated revenue step-down.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025 open-market repurchases were $899M (FY2024: $599M). Q1 2026 open-market repurchases were 675,000 shares at an average $188.54 ($127.3M), against $181M in Q1 2025 — a roughly 30% deceleration — with $1.298B of authorization remaining. Those Q1 purchases are marked roughly 11% below cost at the current $167.87. The dividend is a token $0.20 quarterly (0.47% yield).

Weighted average diluted shares fell from 66,037K to 61,668K year over year. Decomposing Q1 2026: net income attributable to UHS grew 10.1% and diluted shares fell 6.6%; 1.101 × (66,037/61,668) = 1.179, against reported diluted EPS growth of 17.7%. Roughly 7 of those 17.7 percentage points — about 40% — came from share count. The Q2 wedge is the same shape: adjusted EBITDA less NCI +5% against adjusted EPS +12%. Investors anchoring on adjusted EPS, which is also the company-selected compensation metric, will overstate underlying momentum by roughly 7 points.

## Growth Analysis

The composition of growth matters more here than the rate.

**Q1 2026 same-facility statistics, acute care:** revenue +8.2%; net revenue per adjusted admission +6.3%; inpatient admissions **−1.5%**; adjusted admissions **0.0%**; patient days −0.7%; occupancy 69% versus 70%. **Behavioral:** revenue +7.3%; revenue per adjusted admission +6.2%; admissions +1.1%; patient days +1.4%; occupancy flat at 74%. FY2025 showed the same shape.

Essentially all revenue growth is rate, mix and supplemental-payment driven, with acute volume flat to negative. Rate is the least durable of these because a material portion is a policy variable. The benign counter-reading is a deliberate inpatient-to-outpatient shift — consistent with 35 free-standing EDs and the outpatient build — and management reportedly described a volume rebound in both segments in Q2 2026. If the Q2 10-Q confirms positive same-facility admissions in both segments, the bearish reading weakens materially. That is the single most important item to check on filing.

**Estimate revisions are the disconfirming evidence.** Current quarter $5.17 versus $5.59 thirty days ago (−7.5%) and $5.61 ninety days ago (−7.8%); next quarter −8.4% and −8.7%; current year $22.54, down 3.8% in 30 days; next year $24.13, down 4.6%. Fourteen analysts cover the full-year lines. This is the inverse of the inflection configuration that supports a valuation-driven long, and it arrived alongside a guidance cut rather than a demand shortfall.

**Structural offsets that genuinely help.** Behavioral health remains supply-constrained — 74% occupancy with staffing-driven volume limitations — supporting mid-single-digit rate growth. Acute same-facility salaries, wages and benefits improved to 38.6% of revenue from 40.0%, evidence the contract-labor unwind is real and largely complete. CMS proposed a 2.4% FY2027 inpatient rate increase (externally reported July 2, 2026; the shares rose 5.3% on the news). Talkspace, announced March 9, 2026 at $5.25 per share for approximately $835M, addresses a real capacity constraint by adding a virtual channel with roughly 6,000 licensed professionals and no bed or nurse requirement. Closing is expected in Q3 2026.

**Structural offsets that hurt.** Behavioral same-facility salaries rose 8.0% in Q1 2026 against 7.3% revenue growth, pushing SWB to 54.6% of segment revenue. California is implementing acute psychiatric staffing standards expected to take effect June 1, 2026, which UHS says may require limiting patient volumes. Acute other operating expenses rose 14.0% against 8.2% revenue growth, including a $20M (12.3%) increase in hospital-based physician expense and a $45M (27.6%) medical cost increase at the captive insurer. And the enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired December 31, 2025 with no extension enacted as of early May 2026, which reduces exchange enrollment and raises uncompensated care.

Netting these, we score growth prospects 4.2. Reported revenue growth of 8-10% overstates the underlying franchise expansion; the volume base is flat, the rate base is partly a policy variable being legislated down, and roughly 40% of EPS growth is share retirement.

## Valuation Assessment

**Share count first, because it changes the arithmetic.** The quoted market capitalization of $9.15B corresponds to 53.3M shares — the publicly traded Class B count. Par-value derivation from the March 31, 2026 balance sheet gives Class A 6.6M, Class B 53.5M, Class C 0.7M, Class D nil, totaling approximately 60.8M, cross-checked against 61,071K weighted-average basic shares. Using 53.5M would overstate per-share value metrics by roughly 14%. All figures below use 60.8M.

At $167.87: equity value approximately $10.21B; plus net debt $4.58B plus NCI and redeemable NCI $0.14B gives EV of approximately $14.93B. Against the FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA less NCI midpoint of $2.665B, EV/EBITDA is **5.6x**. Trailing GAAP EPS of $23.10 gives **7.3x** (note: the provider's quoted 6.83x P/E is not reproducible from the supplied price and EPS and should be disregarded). Current-year consensus of $22.54 gives 7.45x; next-year $24.13 gives 6.96x. Free cash flow yield is approximately 9%.

**Peer context.** Health Care Facilities peers show P/Es of ENSG 24.0, EHC 17.6, NHC 24.9, OPCH 16.2, with ACHC at negative earnings — a median of 24.0. UHS at 7.3x is roughly 70% below that median. The peer set is heterogeneous (post-acute, home infusion, skilled nursing) and none carries UHS's supplemental-payment concentration, so the discount is not directly comparable. But the magnitude requires explanation, and the explanation is available in the filings rather than in sentiment.

**The central valuation question is the multiple on the post-OBBBA earnings base, not the current multiple.** Strip the disclosed $5.44-$6.04 per share of at-risk annual earnings from next-year consensus of $24.13 and the fully-loaded 2032 base is approximately $18.1-$18.7. At $167.87 that is 9.0-9.3x — a discount to peers, but not the 7x headline, and it embeds no offsetting growth over six-plus years. Add the $0.34-$0.38 refinancing headwind and the staged 2030/2032 coupon step-ups and the near-term bridge narrows further.

Working the other direction: the phase-in runs from state fiscal 2028 through 2032, giving UHS six years to grow the non-supplemental base, redeploy $1.298B of buyback authorization, integrate Talkspace, and benefit from state program redesigns — UHS itself notes states "may attempt to modify their respective SDP program in response to the OBBBA," and CMS rulemaking on grandfathered SDP definitions could soften the impact. At 4-5% annual share retirement plus 3% underlying growth, 2032 EPS could plausibly exceed today's. That is the bull arithmetic, and it is not unreasonable.

**Analyst targets are not a floor here.** Mean $193.47, median $191.00, low $162.00, high $290.00, with sentiment characterized as degrading and the ratings distribution at 1 strong buy / 6 buy / 12 hold / 1 strong sell. Targets function as a floor on inflection stocks with rising estimates; UHS has falling estimates across every period, so the mean is a lagging indicator of a cut cycle rather than independent confirmation of upside. The low target of $162 sits 3.5% below spot.

We score valuation 5.5. The stock is cheap in absolute terms with real free cash flow support, but the compression reflects a specific, legislated impairment of the earnings base plus an unresolved litigation tail, and there is no identified catalyst to reverse the revision trend. Cheapness without a catalyst is not asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

UHS's durable advantage is scale in inpatient behavioral health, where national operators are scarce, plus concentrated acute positions in a handful of markets. The behavioral segment's 19.2% pretax margin against acute's 11.0% validates that the moat is real where it exists.

Trajectory is the concern. Acute occupancy slipped to 69% from 70% with admissions down 1.5%. Behavioral occupancy is pinned at 74% by staffing rather than demand. Payer mix is drifting toward Medicaid (30% of consolidated revenue, up from 28%), which is structurally lower-rate and now policy-impaired. The No Surprises Act has raised hospital-based physician subsidies in emergency medicine, anesthesiology and radiology. Physician-owned facilities compete in certain markets, and site-neutral payment proposals remain outstanding.

Two competitive-position developments deserve specific weight. First, effective March 2025 UHS's excess commercial professional and general liability coverage contains exclusions for incidents involving sexual molestation or abuse, with higher premiums and lower aggregate limits; aggregate excess coverage fell from $250M (2014-2020) to $175M (2024), $110M (2025) and $120M (2026), and as of March 1, 2025 the single self-insured retention no longer applies to multi-plaintiff claims against behavioral facilities. Self-insured professional and general liability accruals were $460M at March 31, 2026. The highest-margin segment now retains, rather than transfers, its most severe risk category. This is an industry-wide repricing rather than a UHS-specific failure, but it permanently raises the cost of operating behavioral capacity and should compress the multiple a rational investor assigns to that segment's earnings.

Second, CMS terminated the Medicare provider agreement of Laurel Ridge Treatment Center (330 beds, San Antonio) effective April 30, 2026; a federal TRO request was denied and the facility is terminated from both Medicare and Medicaid while reapplying, with UHS expecting operating losses. Laurel Ridge earned approximately $23M of pretax income in FY2025, about 1.2% of the total — immaterial in dollars. The signal is that Condition-of-Participation enforcement can eliminate a profitable behavioral facility's revenue base within weeks and courts will not stay it. One facility out of 182 is not a systemic thesis, but it changes the risk distribution.

Talkspace is the one clear positive on trajectory, extending the behavioral franchise into a capacity-light channel. We have no visibility into Talkspace's revenue, EBITDA or growth rate, so we cannot assess the purchase multiple or accretion. Competitive position: 5.5 — a real franchise with a stable-to-slightly-deteriorating trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**1. OBBBA Medicaid supplemental step-down (highest materiality, partly priced).** Enacted law, dated phase-in from state FY2028, company-quantified at $432-480M of annual net benefit by 2032 against a base of ~$1.36B — 21.9-24.3% of FY2025 pretax income, or $5.44-$6.04 per share after tax on a static basis. The mechanics: UHS pays provider taxes to states, states fund the non-federal share of state directed payments, and Medicaid managed care organizations pay SDP amounts to UHS facilities. OBBBA ratchets the permissible provider-tax threshold from 6% to 3.5% for expansion states between federal FY2028 and FY2032 and freezes rates for non-expansion states. Separately the April 2024 CMS Managed Care Rule caps SDP payment levels at the average commercial rate, with enforcement discretion on certain hold-harmless arrangements only until calendar 2028. A scheduled $8B federal DSH allotment reduction lands in FFY2028.

**2. Near-term SDP approval and timing risk (now realized).** The Q2 guidance cut was attributed to Medicaid supplemental reimbursement changes, not operations. UHS discloses that CMS SDP preprint approval "and the timing of such approval, if it occurs, are not certain," affecting both payment level and timing of revenue recognition. There is also an unquantified retroactive exposure: if CMS deems a state's financing structure non-compliant, SDP payments "could be subject to recoupment." A truncated report referenced a "$100 million Florida directed payment benefit" in the guidance discussion; direction, period and treatment are unconfirmed.

**3. Cumberland Hospital litigation — near-term binary.** A September 2024 jury verdict for the first three plaintiffs awarded $60M compensatory plus $180M trebled under the Virginia Consumer Protection Act plus punitive damages later reduced to $1.05M, leaving roughly $241M on appeal. Approximately 43 additional plaintiffs have claims pending, with UHS parent and UHS of Delaware still defendants as to those, and the next trial is tentatively set for **August 2026**. Remaining 2020-policy-year commercial coverage above self-insured retentions is approximately $143M. The arithmetic — $241M for three plaintiffs against $143M of coverage with 43 more queued — is why the company states that exhausting coverage or "posting of large bonds or other collateral during the appeal processes" would materially adversely impact results and capital resources. Counterweights: the trebled VCPA theory is precisely what is on appeal, the court already cut punitive damages by roughly 99%, the parent was dismissed from the first trial, and mass-tort bellwethers commonly settle at fractions of headline verdicts. The range of outcomes is wide; we do not model an expected value.

**4. California Short-Doyle Medi-Cal repayment methodology.** A cost-based ceiling on negotiated inpatient psychiatric rates effective December 12, 2023 may apply retroactively. UHS is "awaiting formal guidance" and says "under some scenarios, the adverse financial impact could be material." Unquantified and outstanding for over two years.

**5. EPTC expiration.** Enhanced ACA premium tax credits lapsed December 31, 2025; H.R.1834 passed the House January 8, 2026 but no extension was enacted as of early May 2026. Consequence: lower exchange enrollment, lower revenue, higher uncompensated care.

**6. Financing.** $700M of 1.65% notes due September 1, 2026 with a self-disclosed "significant" interest expense increase, plus $1.3B of 2.65% coupons rolling in 2030 and 2032.

**7. Governance and related-party density.** UHS advises Universal Health Realty Income Trust for 0.70% of average invested real estate assets ($1.4M per quarter), holds roughly 5.7% of its shares (carrying value $4.1M against $31.9M market value), leases five hospitals from it under cross-defaulted unconditional guarantees, and executed the December 2021 asset exchange as a failed sale-leaseback carrying a ~$69M financing liability. Two of those five leases — McAllen Medical Center ($5.485M annual rent) and Wellington Regional ($6.975M) — **expire in December 2026** and will be negotiated across a related-party table. In October 2025 UHS agreed that the Trust would develop and own the 80,000 sq ft "Miller Medical Plaza" on the new Alan B. Miller Medical Center campus, with UHS taking a 10-year master flex lease on roughly 75% of the rentable area and a UHS subsidiary acting as project manager. Add split-dollar life insurance on the Executive Chairman and his wife (approximately $28M of expected UHS premiums, $37M minimum death benefit to UHS) and a director who is Of Counsel to the company's outside law firm while also providing personal legal services to the Executive Chairman and serving as trustee of family trusts. Each item is disclosed and individually defensible; the dollar amounts are small against $17B of revenue. The aggregate reduces the independence of several oversight relationships.

The proxy pay-versus-performance table is the sharpest governance datapoint. CEO compensation actually paid rose from $9.78M (2021) to $27.74M (2025), up 183%, while the UHS TSR index rose from 95 to 163 (+72%) and the self-selected peer index rose from 158 to 284 (+80%). UHS underperformed its own peer group in all five disclosed years, with the gap widening sharply in 2025. The company-selected metric is adjusted diluted EPS, which is both inflated by the supplemental net benefit and mechanically lifted by ~6% annual share retirement.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director between November 2025 and May 2026, spanning a 24% drawdown. In March 2026 all three top executives exercised large option tranches at $138.80 and $152.68 and settled taxes through share withholding rather than open-market sales — mildly constructive, since it is a decision to hold equity, but materially weaker evidence than purchases and also the default administrative mechanism at many companies. Director sales were small (Sussman 674 shares; Nimetz 3,817 shares at ~$223 in November 2025). Code-J transactions by the Executive Chairman on May 27, 2026 were economically neutral paired acquisitions and dispositions consistent with trust transfers.

We score risk factors 3.8: multiple quantified, specific, near-dated downside catalysts, of which the August 2026 trial and the SDP approval cycle are the most immediate.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $215 (+28%)

The market is capitalizing a static, worst-case reading of the OBBBA step-down while ignoring six years of mitigation. The step-down begins in state FY2028 and reaches full effect in 2032; over that window UHS can grow the non-supplemental base at mid-single digits, retire 4-6% of shares annually against $1.298B of remaining authorization, integrate Talkspace into a capacity-light behavioral channel, and benefit from state program redesigns that UHS explicitly anticipates. CMS rulemaking on grandfathered SDP programs could soften the reduction below the disclosed range.

Operationally, the Q2 volume rebound in both segments would, if confirmed in the 10-Q, break the "growth is all rate" reading. Acute SWB improved to 38.6% of same-facility revenue from 40.0%, confirming the contract-labor unwind is largely complete. A CMS-proposed 2.4% FY2027 inpatient increase keeps Medicare updates ahead of a moderating labor environment. Behavioral capacity remains supply-constrained at 74% occupancy with mental health parity enforcement supporting rate.

On this reading, 8.5-9.0x next-year consensus of $24.13 supports $205-$217, and a favorable Cumberland appellate outcome removes an overhang the market is discounting at more than its expected value. Net leverage of 1.7x (2.06x pro forma) gives management room to accelerate repurchases at 7x earnings, which is straightforwardly accretive if the post-OBBBA base holds near $19-20.

### Bear Case — $125 (−26%)

The current multiple is not cheap; it is correctly discounting an earnings stream in which roughly 68% of pretax income derives from a policy transfer that Congress voted to reduce. Q2 2026 demonstrated the transmission channel: revenue beat, volumes reportedly recovered, and guidance still came down because of SDP reimbursement. Consensus is already cutting — down 3.8% for the current year and 4.6% for next year in 30 days — and the cuts predate any OBBBA phase-in. The retroactive recoupment risk and the California Short-Doyle methodology are unquantified and could arrive before FY2028.

Layer on: $0.34-$0.38 of refinancing dilution in FY2027, staged coupon step-ups in 2030 and 2032, an $835M debt-funded acquisition of an asset whose profitability is unverified, the behavioral segment now self-retaining abuse liability with excess coverage down from $250M to $120M, an August 2026 trial where the standing judgment for three plaintiffs exceeds remaining coverage with 43 plaintiffs behind it, and December 2026 related-party lease renewals on two acute hospitals.

If the post-OBBBA base settles near $18 and the market applies 6.5-7.0x for policy risk plus litigation uncertainty, the shares are worth $117-$126. Buyback support is decelerating — Q1 open-market repurchases fell roughly 30% year over year and were executed at $188.54, now 11% underwater — while incremental credit capacity is being directed to M&A rather than the share count.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders have a 9% free cash flow yield, 1.7x leverage and 5.6x EV/EBITDA supporting the downside; new capital has no catalyst against falling estimates and a live binary in August.

- **Base case: $180 (+7.2%).** 6.0x EV/EBITDA on the $2.665B guided midpoint gives EV $15.99B, less $4.58B net debt and $0.14B NCI, equals $11.27B equity, or $185 per share on 60.8M shares; 7.5x next-year consensus of $24.13 gives $181. We round down to $180 to reflect the $0.34-$0.38 refinancing headwind not yet in consensus. This sits below the analyst mean of $193.47 because that mean is a lagging indicator in an active cut cycle.
- **Bull case: $215 (+28%).** 8.9x next-year consensus, requiring confirmed volume growth in both segments in the Q2 10-Q, a favorable Cumberland appellate ruling or a settlement inside insurance limits, and CMS grandfathering guidance that reduces the disclosed OBBBA range.
- **Bear case: $125 (−26%).** 6.9x a post-OBBBA base near $18.10 with litigation and California Short-Doyle overhangs unresolved.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to four dated checkpoints — the Q2 2026 10-Q (same-facility volumes, guidance detail, the Florida directed payment item), the August 2026 Cumberland trial, the September 1, 2026 note maturity and its refinancing terms, and the Q3 2026 Talkspace close with initial contribution disclosure.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**

## Conclusion

UHS presents the hardest version of a value question: a franchise with a real moat in behavioral health, a conservative balance sheet at 1.7x net leverage, roughly 9% free cash flow yield, and a 7.3x trailing multiple that is 70% below the peer median — where the discount is explained by a company-disclosed fact rather than by sentiment. Approximately 68% of FY2025 pretax income came from state Medicaid supplemental payment programs, and Congress has legislated a $432-480M annual reduction to that stream by 2032, worth $5.44-$6.04 per share after tax on a static basis.

The Q2 2026 print converted that risk from a filing disclosure into a guidance cut. Revenue exceeded expectations, management described a volume rebound, and the full-year profit outlook came down anyway because of Medicaid supplemental reimbursement. Consensus has cut current-year EPS 3.8% and next-year 4.6% in 30 days while the shares rallied 19% off the June low. Layered on top are an August 2026 trial where the standing judgment for three plaintiffs exceeds remaining insurance coverage with 43 plaintiffs queued, a $700M refinancing at roughly four times the maturing coupon, an $835M debt-funded acquisition of unverified profitability, and a compensation record showing CEO pay actually paid up 183% since 2021 against five consecutive years of relative TSR underperformance and no insider open-market buying through a 24% drawdown.

Our overall score of 5.05 continues a declining trajectory from 6.8 in February, and the decline is driven by new fundamental information — a guidance cut, negative revisions across every forecast period, and a first adjusted-EPS shortfall — not by the share price. Below approximately $140 the post-OBBBA arithmetic would work at roughly 7.5x a fully-loaded base and the call would change. At $167.87, with estimates falling and a binary trial three weeks out, the correct action is to hold what you own, consider January 2027 puts at fair implied volatility, and wait for the Q2 10-Q to resolve the volume question.
