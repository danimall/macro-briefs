**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Teradata is a 40-year-old enterprise data-analytics vendor mid-way through two overlapping transitions: from perpetual licenses to consumption-based cloud/subscription revenue, and from a legacy on-premises database company to a self-styled "AI and knowledge platform" built around a hybrid deployment differentiator. The core financial reality is a business whose reported revenue has declined three consecutive years ($1,833M → $1,750M → $1,663M) while its recurring base grows modestly on an ARR basis (total ARR +3% to $1,522M, public-cloud ARR +15% to $701M). The company converts this shrinking top line into substantial cash: ~$286M FCF in 2025 on a ~17% margin, guided to ~$310M in FY26, off minimal (~$19M) capex. The screening-tool "distress" flags (Altman Z 1.53, D/E 6.73) are artifacts of a −$1.9B accumulated deficit from the NCR spin-off and years of buybacks; the underlying balance sheet is net-cash after the SAP settlement (~$816M cash vs. $450M term-loan-plus-lease debt).

The dominant recent event is the February 2026 settlement of Teradata's long-running trade-secret litigation against SAP: $480M gross, ~$359M net pre-tax, ~$280M after-tax — roughly 10% of the current $2.9B market cap injected into an already cash-positive company. Paired with a fresh $500M buyback authorization (~$470M remaining), this hands management a substantial capital-return lever. The stock trades at ~6.7x trailing GAAP EPS and roughly 9x forward earnings, with an EV/sales near 2.2x — cheap on nearly every headline metric.

The problem is that cheapness here has a reason. The transformation thesis rests on whether cloud/AI demand can outrun legacy erosion, and the leading indicators are softening: cloud net expansion fell from 117% to 108%, public-cloud ARR growth decelerated from 15% to 13%, and management's own FY26 guide is 2–4% total ARR growth — stabilization, not re-acceleration. Combined with near-total 2025 C-suite turnover, a cluster of insider selling into recent strength with zero open-market buying, 25.7% short interest, and an options market pricing a ±20% move with near-term IV at 99% in backwardation, the setup is genuinely two-sided. We rate TDC a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Teradata (incorporated 1979, public since its 2007 NCR spin-off) is a ~$1.66B-revenue enterprise analytics company headquartered in San Diego with roughly 5,100 employees across ~38 countries. Its franchise is a massively parallel, workload-optimized analytics database (Vantage/ClearScape) serving the largest data consumers in financial services, healthcare, public sector, manufacturing, retail, telecom, and transportation. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. The company reports two segments: Product Sales (recurring plus residual perpetual/hardware) and Consulting Services.

The strategic bet is explicit and differentiated: Teradata is wagering that large regulated enterprises will not go cloud-only, and that hybrid (on-prem plus multi-cloud) deployment retains durable value for data-sovereignty, cost-control, and complex-workload reasons. The company is repositioning around agentic AI — Teradata AI Factory with NVIDIA integration, MCP Server, AgentBuilder, and an Enterprise Vector Store — and claims 150+ AI customer engagements in 2025. Its competitors are better-capitalized and faster-growing: Snowflake, Databricks, and the hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), the last of which are simultaneously Teradata's hosting partners and its rivals.

## Financial Analysis

**Top line: secular decline, ARR stabilization.** Revenue fell 4.5% in 2024 and 5% in 2025 to $1,663M. The decline is concentrated in legacy perpetual/hardware (−26% in 2025) and Consulting (−19%, partly a deliberate shed of low-margin work in favor of partner delivery). Recurring revenue fell only 2%. On an ARR basis the picture is better: total ARR +3% to $1,522M, public-cloud ARR +15% to $701M (46% of total ARR). The disconnect between shrinking reported revenue and growing ARR is a mix/recognition artifact, since cloud consumption recognizes over time rather than upfront.

**Profitability and cash flow are the bright spot.** Operating income held at $205M in 2025 (vs. $209M in 2024), and net income rose to $130M from $114M; diluted EPS was $1.35 vs. $1.16. Gross margin sits around 59–60%, with modest compression from higher (initially lower-margin) cloud mix. The standout is free cash flow: ~$286M in 2025 (~17% FCF margin) on ~$19M capex, a genuinely asset-light software profile. SG&A fell 11% year-over-year, and a 2024 restructuring (~$45–50M cash cost, substantially complete) has permanently lowered the cost base. FY26 FCF guidance is ~$310M.

**Balance sheet: distress flags are false positives.** Altman Z of 1.53 and D/E of 6.73 look alarming but are mechanically distorted by a −$1.9B accumulated deficit (legacy of the NCR spin and years of buybacks) that suppresses book equity to a sliver. Gross debt is modest: a $450M term loan plus finance leases, with a $400M undrawn revolver and, post-settlement, $816M cash, leaving the company net-cash. The sub-1.0 current ratio (0.92) reflects $603M of deferred revenue (prepaid recurring contracts, a favorable liability), not a liquidity problem. Net interest expense is small (~$26M) at a ~4.1% blended rate, with ~90% of the term loan swapped to fixed. The June 24, 2026 8-K (items 1.01/1.02/2.03) most plausibly reflects a refinancing/replacement of the credit facility ahead of its June 2027 maturity, though the underlying document could not be confirmed directly.

**The SAP windfall.** The settlement drove Q1 2026 net income to $335M ($3.47 diluted EPS) and lifted cash to $816M from $493M. Management said it is "evaluating appropriate use of the net proceeds"; the deployment decision is the key near-term catalyst.

**Earnings quality and cadence.** The company has beaten consensus in each of the last three quarters (15.8%, 31.8%, 33.0%), which lends credibility to conservative guidance. But Q1 2026's headline +6% revenue growth (to $444M, gross margin 62.2%) is partly optical, flattered by a 2–3% FX tailwind and timing of upfront on-prem subscription recognition, while Consulting fell another 14%. Management's own 2–4% ARR guide is the more reliable read on the underlying trend than the Q1 print.

## Growth Analysis

The entire growth thesis reduces to a single race: can public-cloud and AI expansion outrun the erosion of legacy on-prem and maintenance ARR? The current evidence says the race is close and tightening. Two leading indicators are moving the wrong way. Cloud net expansion rate fell from 117% to 108% — existing cloud customers are still expanding, but at a decelerating pace as the easy on-prem-to-cloud migration cohort matures. Public-cloud ARR growth decelerated from 15% to 13%. Management's FY26 guide of 2–4% total ARR growth confirms a stabilization posture rather than a re-acceleration.

This does not meet the profile of an inflection stock. There is no revenue growth above 40%, no expanding-margin acceleration, and no positive estimate-revision velocity (estimate-revision data was not available in the sources) that would flag a systematic underestimation of a fundamental turn. Reported revenue is still declining year-over-year on a full-year basis, and the ARR growth that offsets it is decelerating. The AI/hybrid narrative is genuine and differentiated — 150+ AI engagements, NVIDIA integration, an "open and connected" ecosystem (ServiceNow, Salesforce, Fivetran) — but it has yet to show up as re-accelerating net expansion. Until it does, this is a low-single-digit ARR grower with a shrinking legacy tail, not a growth story. The growth-prospects score reflects that: the dimension has already declined in our prior reads (5.8 → 5.0), and the deceleration in the two most important leading metrics justifies a further step down.

## Valuation Assessment

On headline metrics TDC is unambiguously cheap: ~6.7x trailing GAAP EPS (flattered by the SAP gain in the trailing window), roughly 9x on more normalized forward earnings, and ~2.2x EV/sales. Against the IT Consulting & Other Services peer set, the P/E median is 23.8 (ADEA 29.9, DXC 88.2, VYX 23.8, KD 13.3, EPAM 13.3). Teradata trades at a fraction of that median. But the peer set is heterogeneous and littered with impaired/deep-value names (DXC at 88x on collapsed earnings, KD and EPAM both at 13.3x, with KD down 70% and EPAM down 61% from 52-week highs), so the "discount to peers" is less a mispricing signal than confirmation that the market assigns structurally-challenged multiples across this cohort.

The core valuation question is whether cheapness reflects a genuine mispricing or a value trap. The lessons of this environment argue for skepticism toward a low multiple absent an independent catalyst, since cheap has consistently sorted the wrong way. Here the multiple is compressed for identifiable reasons: declining reported revenue, decelerating cloud expansion, and heavy competition from better-funded players. That said, the downside is meaningfully cushioned by three real supports: (1) an FCF yield near 10% (~$310M guided FCF on ~$2.9B market cap), (2) a net-cash balance sheet augmented by ~$280M of SAP proceeds, and (3) a $500M buyback (~$470M remaining) that can retire a meaningful share of the ~94M shares outstanding over time at current prices. The 2024 buybacks at ~$37 are underwater versus today's ~$30, which is a mark against past capital-allocation timing but also implies management views current levels as attractive for repurchase.

We do not treat proximity to any analyst target as a ceiling or a floor here; the guardrail against reading targets as floors applies only to inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples, and TDC is a decelerating grower rather than an inflection name. On balance, valuation is fair-to-modestly-attractive on cash flow but does not offer clear asymmetric upside without evidence that ARR growth re-accelerates. A mid-range score is warranted: cheap enough to limit downside, not cheap enough (given the deteriorating growth trajectory) to force a buy.

## Competitive Landscape

Teradata's differentiation is real but narrow. Its hybrid/on-prem strength addresses a legitimate need among large regulated enterprises for data sovereignty, predictable cost, and complex-workload performance — a defensible position that cloud-native Snowflake and Databricks address less directly. The 10-K's claim of a "resurgence of hybrid" and 150+ AI engagements supports the idea that the niche is not disappearing.

The trajectory, however, is what matters for scoring, and it is deteriorating. Teradata is the legacy incumbent defending share against faster-growing, better-capitalized, higher-multiple cloud players who also happen to be its hosting partners — a coopetition dynamic that gives AWS/Azure/Google leverage over pricing, terms, and platform favoritism. Decelerating cloud net expansion and slowing cloud ARR growth indicate the migration tailwind is maturing while the legacy base continues to erode. The cumulative TSR underperformance versus the proxy peer group (136 vs. 258) is the market's verdict on relative competitive momentum. This is a stable-to-eroding competitive position — level is respectable, trajectory is negative — which supports the low score, consistent with our prior downgrade of this dimension from 5.5 to 4.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Transformation execution.** The single largest risk is that cloud + AI growth fails to outrun legacy erosion. The deceleration in net expansion is the leading tell to monitor each quarter.

**Competitive displacement and hyperscaler dependency.** Better-funded rivals with brand momentum in cloud-native analytics, combined with a partner base that is also a competitor set, create both revenue-mix and pricing risk.

**Revenue predictability and cancellation exposure.** Consumption-based pricing makes revenue lumpier and heavily Q4-weighted. Of $2.03B remaining performance obligations, ~$1.09B carries customer "cancel-for-convenience" terms; only ~$501M is genuinely non-cancelable in the next year.

**Macro and FX.** ~50% of revenue is international; the filings cite elongated decision cycles from tariff/trade uncertainty and elevated rates, plus recurring Argentina Blue Chip Swap losses.

**Governance and controls.** Near-total 2025 C-suite turnover (four of six named executives new) mid-transformation risks loss of institutional knowledge, and a newly deployed 2025 ERP system is still stabilizing and flagged as a potential internal-controls risk. On the positive side, the Ostrander securities class action was dismissed with prejudice (final judgment January 2026, no appeal), clearing a legal overhang.

Most of these risks are at least partially priced given the compressed multiple and 25.7% short interest. But the concentration of them — execution, competitive, cancellation, and governance all live simultaneously — keeps the idiosyncratic risk elevated relative to what a single-digit-P/E software name might otherwise warrant.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 99.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 58.2% — rich by ~41 points, in the 94th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (99.2% near-term vs. 60.4% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent, resolvable event (most likely earnings). ATM IV jumped from 46.0% to 99.2% since early July as the stock fell 11.8% and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.02 to 0.37. Put/call skew of 5.5% shows elevated near-term put demand. The implied one-month move is ±19.9%. The market is not confidently bearish (put/call volume ratio 0.79, OI ratio 0.37 remain call-tilted), but it is pricing a large binary outcome. For a fundamental holder, rich near-term IV in backwardation argues against chasing and favors waiting for post-event vol crush before adding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$40, matching the 52-week high territory).** ARR growth re-accelerates as AI/hybrid demand converts the 150+ engagements into consumption; cloud net expansion stabilizes and turns back up. Management deploys the ~$280M SAP windfall plus the $500M buyback aggressively at ~$30, retiring a meaningful share of the count and compounding FCF-per-share. On ~$310M FCF and a re-rating toward a low-teens FCF multiple as the growth narrative firms, the equity approaches $40, roughly a 31% gain from the current ~$30.48. The 25.7% short interest becomes fuel for a squeeze on any upside surprise.

**Bear Case (~$20–22, near the 52-week low).** Cloud net expansion keeps deteriorating below 105%, legacy erosion accelerates, and reported revenue declines resume after the Q1 optical bounce fades. Consulting continues to shrink, gross margin compresses further on cloud mix, and the market re-rates the FCF multiple lower as terminal-decline fears mount. The SAP cash gets spent on underwater buybacks or a dilutive/ill-timed acquisition. In that scenario the stock revisits the low-$20s, roughly 28–34% downside from current levels.

**Base Case (~$30–32).** Stabilization plays out roughly as guided: 2–4% ARR growth, ~$310M FCF, steady buyback-driven per-share support, and no re-rating in either direction. The FCF yield and net-cash balance sheet limit downside; decelerating growth and competitive pressure cap upside. The stock oscillates around the current price, and total return comes mostly from buyback-driven EPS accretion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence does not support a confident directional call. The valuation and cash-generation profile limit downside, but the decelerating growth metrics, insider selling, and unproven transformation preclude a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the options market is pricing this as the near-term binary), with the SAP capital-allocation decision as the key discrete catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Cloud net expansion rate stabilizes or turns back up (>110%) and public-cloud ARR growth re-accelerates above 15%.
- Total ARR growth exceeds the 2–4% guide on two consecutive quarters, confirming the AI/hybrid demand is converting.
- Management deploys the SAP proceeds into an accelerated buyback at current levels (retiring shares near $30), or into disciplined, accretive M&A rather than a premium-priced deal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cloud net expansion falls below 105% or public-cloud ARR growth drops below 10%.
- Reported revenue decline resumes and accelerates once the Q1 FX/timing benefit laps.
- A material internal-controls deficiency emerges from the ERP stabilization, or the SAP cash is spent on a dilutive, poorly-timed acquisition.

## Conclusion

Teradata is a well-run, cash-generative, net-cash software business that the screening tools mislabel as distressed. It trades cheap on almost every headline metric and carries a ~10% FCF yield with substantial capital-return firepower after the SAP windfall. Those attributes cushion the downside. But the cheapness reflects a genuine, identifiable problem: a decelerating cloud transformation racing against an eroding legacy base, in a market dominated by faster-growing, better-funded competitors. The two most important leading indicators — cloud net expansion (117% → 108%) and public-cloud ARR growth (15% → 13%) — are moving the wrong way, management guides to stabilization rather than re-acceleration, insiders are net sellers with zero open-market buying, and 25.7% short interest with a ±20% implied earnings move confirms that sophisticated money remains skeptical and the near-term outcome is binary.

The recommendation history (HOLD → AVOID → HOLD) and declining score trajectory reflect this genuine uncertainty rather than a stale thesis. We land on HOLD: the balance sheet, FCF, and buyback limit how badly this can go, but the growth deterioration and competitive trajectory prevent a constructive call until ARR expansion shows evidence of re-accelerating. Wait for the next print and for the near-term IV crush before adding, and watch the cloud net expansion rate as the most decision-relevant number.
