**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Winmark Corporation operates a pure-play, asset-light franchise model across five value-oriented resale brands (Plato's Closet, Once Upon A Child, Play It Again Sports, Style Encore, Music Go Round), collecting ~4-5% royalties on $1.68B of system-wide sales without owning a single store. The model produces ~63% operating margins, ~54% FCF margins, near-100% cash conversion, and 98-99% franchisee renewal rates. FY2025 generated $86.1M revenue, $41.7M net income, and ~$44.7M free cash flow on capex of less than $0.2M. The company returns essentially all of it to shareholders, which is why book equity is negative (-$53.7M at year-end 2025), a structural feature of decades of debt-funded special dividends and buybacks rather than a sign of distress (Altman Z-score 6.68).

The investment question is about price, not quality. At ~$400 the stock trades at ~35x trailing and ~34x forward earnings for a business whose core royalty stream grows mid-single-digits organically (royalties +5.8% in FY2025, +8.4% in Q1 2026) with a constrained U.S. unit-growth runway (historical net adds of ~25-30 stores/year). Consensus EPS estimates have drifted lower over the past 90 days (current-year -4.2%, next-year -4.7%), the company has missed EPS in four consecutive quarters, and the franchise system is absorbing two new cost-shifting initiatives (a monthly Software Fee and a Plato's Closet North American Ad Fund) plus a mission-critical POS platform replacement in the same window. Management flagged franchisee-relationship risk explicitly in both the FY2025 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q.

We rate WINA a **HOLD**. The business deserves a premium multiple, and the ~24% pullback from the October 2025 high of $527 to ~$400 has improved the entry. But the combination of a still-elevated multiple, falling estimates, a four-quarter miss streak, modest director selling near the highs with no offsetting open-market C-suite buying, and execution risk on the franchisee-facing initiatives keeps risk/reward balanced. We see no asymmetric edge at the current price and would want either a lower multiple (high-$300s/low-$300s) or evidence the cost-shifting initiatives are landing without hurting renewals before turning constructive.

## Company Overview

Winmark is a franchisor of buy-sell-trade resale retail stores operating on a circular-economy model. The five brands and their 2025 system-wide sales:

- **Plato's Closet** (teen/young-adult apparel): $675.5M system sales, 37.4% of royalty revenue, 526 stores
- **Once Upon A Child** (children's clothing/toys/gear): $543.4M system sales, 30.1% of royalties, 441 stores
- **Play It Again Sports** (used/new sporting goods): $350.0M system sales, 17.3% of royalties, 309 stores
- **Style Encore** (women's apparel): $61.7M, 67 stores
- **Music Go Round** (musical instruments): $51.4M, 35 stores

Winmark owns zero corporate stores. All 1,383 locations (as of Q1 2026) are franchisee-owned, split between the U.S. (1,213) and Canada (165). Revenue is ~89% royalties, with the balance from initial franchise fees, merchandise sales to franchisees, and other income. Franchise agreements run 10 years with an exclusive territory grant and a 2-year post-termination non-compete; renewal rates have held at 98-99% over the past three years.

System-wide sales reached $1.68B in 2025, up from $1.61B in 2024 and $1.59B in 2023. The company employs 87 people and spends roughly $0.2M/year on capex. As of the end of 2025, Winmark completed the run-off of its legacy middle-market equipment leasing segment (begun May 2021), leaving a clean single-segment franchisor.

## Financial Analysis

The margin and cash-flow profile is exceptional. FY2025 revenue grew 5.9% to $86.1M with net income of $41.7M (net margin ~49%) and operating margin of ~63.4%. Operating cash flow was $44.9M against $0.19M capex, producing ~$44.7M of free cash flow (FCF margin ~52%). On a sub-$25M asset base, returns on capital are extraordinary by construction.

Three nuances matter for reading the trajectory correctly:

**The Q1 2026 "decline" is optical.** Total Q1 2026 revenue fell to $20.8M from $21.9M and net income dipped to $9.25M from $9.96M, but this is entirely the disappearance of the leasing segment. FY2025 "other"/leasing income of $2.6M included a one-time $2.2M litigation settlement; Q1 2026 leasing income was $0 versus $2.3M in Q1 2025. Strip the leasing tailwind and the core franchising engine accelerated: royalties grew 8.4% to $19.3M, and franchising operating income rose to $12.4M from $11.4M. A naive year-over-year comparison flags "declining revenue and earnings"; the underlying royalty base strengthened.

**SG&A grew faster than revenue.** SG&A rose 13.7% in FY2025 to $28.4M, driven by higher compensation and a non-recurring expense for third-party software licenses for franchisees tied to the POS modernization. This compressed operating margin from 65.1% to 63.4%. Whether SG&A growth persists or normalizes is a 2026 watch item.

**Negative equity is a capital-return artifact, not distress.** Shareholders' equity was -$53.7M at FY2025 (improving to -$46.2M at Q1 2026) because cumulative dividends and buybacks exceed cumulative retained earnings (accumulated deficit -$73.3M). The company carries $60M of debt: a $30M CIBC term loan at 4.60-4.75% maturing 2029, and $30M of PGIM/Prudential notes at 3.18%, against roughly $10-20M cash. The current ratio is 2.49 and the company is in compliance with all covenants. The Altman Z-score sits comfortably in the safe zone. This is a deliberate "distribute more than you retain" model, funded by reliable FCF and modest leverage.

Capital allocation in 2025 totaled $49.1M in dividends (including a $10.00/share special dividend) plus $2.4M of buybacks, exceeding FCF and funded partly by cash drawdown. The regular quarterly dividend was raised to $0.96 in 2026 from $0.90 (current yield ~1.0%). Only 70,656 shares remain under buyback authorization.

The earnings-delivery record is a concern. Winmark has missed consensus EPS in all four trailing quarters: -1.6% (Q2 2025), -8.1% (Q3 2025), -5.1% (Q4 2025), and -9.2% (Q1 2026). The misses partly reflect the leasing run-off distorting comparisons, but a four-quarter streak warrants reduced confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The core royalty stream is healthy but structurally mid-single-digit. Royalties grew 5.8% in FY2025 and accelerated to 8.4% in Q1 2026, driven by higher franchisee retail sales and net new stores (+28 net openings in 2025, from 55 openings against 27 closures). System-wide sales compounding at ~4-5% supports the toll-booth.

The growth ceiling is the constraint. On paper Winmark cites ~2,800 available territories, but the historical run-rate of ~25-30 net new stores per year against a base of ~1,380 implies low-single-digit unit growth. Future revenue growth therefore comes from same-store system-sales gains, the new cost-shifting initiatives (which mechanically lift the royalty/fee take per location), and incremental unit adds. There were 82 signed-but-unopened agreements at year-end, providing some near-term opening visibility, but new-franchisee development is sensitive to financing conditions and franchisee net worth, both pressured in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

The two new franchisee-facing programs are the most important 2026-27 swing factor for growth. The monthly Software Fee (all brands) and the Plato's Closet North American Ad Fund are near-term royalty/fee positives that lift Winmark's economics per store. If franchisees absorb them without friction, royalty growth can hold above trend. If they perceive the fees as burdensome, renewal and new-development pace could suffer, hitting the model where it is most valuable.

Estimate momentum is negative. The single covering analyst's current-year EPS estimate fell to $11.88 from $12.40 ninety days ago (-4.2%), and next-year to $12.88 from $13.51 (-4.7%). The current-quarter and next-quarter estimates are essentially flat, so the markdowns are concentrated in the full-year figures. This is the opposite of the rising-estimate signal that would support a more aggressive stance.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$400, WINA trades at ~35x trailing EPS of $11.30 and ~34x current-year consensus of $11.88, roughly 31x next-year $12.88. This is a premium multiple for a mid-single-digit grower. The PEG is unfavorable: ~34x forward against ~5-6% organic earnings growth implies a PEG well above 5, even crediting the quality of the cash flows.

Peer comparables in Other Specialty Retail show a median P/E of 17.9 (BBWI 5.3, CHWY 31.3, TSCO 15.4, ULTA 17.9). WINA trades at roughly twice the peer median. The premium is partially justified by Winmark's superior business model: the franchisees, not Winmark, carry inventory, real estate, and operating risk, producing margins and capital efficiency that conventional retailers cannot match, plus a recurring, contractually-supported royalty stream. But twice the peer median is a steep ask when the cheaper peers (TSCO, ULTA) grow faster and the comparison does not flatter WINA on a growth-adjusted basis.

This is not an earnings situation where trailing P/E understates value. Estimates are falling, not rising, and the company is missing rather than beating. The cycle-adjusted-valuation logic that would justify looking through a high trailing multiple does not apply here. The proper way to value WINA is forward earnings power and yield support, and on both measures the current price prices in continued premium status without offering asymmetric upside.

The lone analyst price target of $545 implies ~36% upside, but it rests on a single estimate and sits well above the multiple a decelerating-estimate, four-miss profile typically commands. We treat it as an optimistic data point rather than a reliable floor. At premium multiples, analyst targets often embed the same growth extrapolation as the price and are not independent confirmation.

Our fair-value framing: at a more defensible 28-30x next-year EPS, the stock is worth ~$360-385, modestly below the current price. To justify owning above $400, an investor needs the cost-shifting initiatives to reaccelerate royalty growth into high-single-digits and the multiple to hold, a combination that is plausible but not the base case.

We score valuation **4.5**. The multiple is stretched relative to the growth on offer, estimates are falling, and we cannot identify a specific catalyst that the market is mispricing. A low headline yield does not provide downside support.

## Competitive Landscape

Winmark occupies a defensible niche as the only national, multi-brand franchisor of physical buy-sell-trade resale stores. The moat rests on brand recognition within each category, the proprietary POS/pricing system that standardizes franchisee operations, exclusive territory grants, and an unusually satisfied franchisee base reflected in the renewals. The resale/circular-economy tailwind is structurally favorable, and value retail is somewhat recession-resistant on both the buying side (consumers trading down) and the supply side (consumers selling goods for cash).

The principal competitive threat is online resale disintermediation. eBay, Poshmark, thredUP, Facebook Marketplace, and Amazon compete for both used-goods supply and demand, and brand-direct resale platforms are proliferating. Winmark's physical-store model has structural advantages for certain categories (children's gear, sporting goods, instruments, where try-before-you-buy and immediate cash-out matter) but is more exposed in apparel, its largest category, where Plato's Closet and Style Encore overlap most with digital resale. The company's response has been multi-channel (musicgoround.com, playitagainsports.com, style-encore.com, plus Shopify/eBay/Reverb integration) and now the Plato's Closet Ad Fund. These are reasonable defensive moves but they also represent cost and execution burden on franchisees.

Brand-level competitors for new goods (Dick's, Academy, Guitar Center, Carter's, Walmart, Target) are not direct threats to the resale value proposition but cap the price umbrella resale operators can charge.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on the strength of the resale tailwind and renewal stability, offset by gradual online encroachment in apparel. We score competitive position **6.5**.

## Risk Assessment

**Franchisee cost-shifting and relationship risk (highest priority).** The 3/16/2026 8-K announced the new monthly Software Fee for all locations and the North American Ad Fund for all Plato's Closet locations. Management added an explicit new risk factor in the Q1 2026 10-Q noting franchisees may view these as burdensome, with potential impact on satisfaction, retention, and new-development pace. This directly threatens the renewal rate that underpins the entire valuation.

**POS platform modernization.** Winmark is replacing its legacy proprietary "Data Recycling System" POS platform. The FY2025 10-K added detailed warnings of operational and execution risk, system instability, workflow interruptions, and data/reporting inconsistencies. Because franchisees are required to use this mission-critical system, a botched rollout could disrupt the royalty base. The non-recurring software license expense in FY2025 is the first financial footprint of this initiative.

**Renewal and development dependency.** Roughly 110 (2026), 103 (2027), and 92 (2028) agreements come up for renewal. The model hinges on sustained ~98% renewal; even modest slippage materially impairs future royalties. New-store growth depends on franchisee financing and net worth, both pressured by elevated rates.

**Leverage and refinancing.** $60M debt against negative book equity; the CIBC facility matures 2029. Not a near-term concern given the FCF profile and Z-score, but the negative-equity structure leaves no equity cushion and the company distributes more than it earns in cash.

**Used-merchandise supply.** Franchisee inventory depends on the public selling goods, an irregular, non-contractual supply that is harder to model than wholesale procurement.

**Online disintermediation.** Discussed in Competitive Landscape; a slow-moving structural pressure on the apparel brands.

### Options Market Signal

No options-pricing data was provided. Short interest is elevated at 12.8% of float with 7.3 days to cover, reflecting institutional bearishness centered on the valuation thesis. At this level, short positioning is meaningful but not extreme; a strong renewal print or evidence that the new fees are being absorbed cleanly could force some covering, while continued estimate cuts would validate the shorts. The elevated short interest is a two-sided signal that argues against either complacency or aggressive shorting at the current price.

We score risk factors **5.5**. The business has no near-term solvency or binary regulatory risk, but the concentration of three simultaneous franchisee-facing initiatives (Software Fee, Ad Fund, POS replacement) creates a real, near-dated execution risk to the renewal base that is not fully priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$500-545)

The cost-shifting initiatives land cleanly: franchisees absorb the Software Fee and Ad Fund without friction, the POS modernization improves operations and reporting, and royalty growth holds in the high-single-digits as Q1 2026's +8.4% suggests. Same-store system sales continue to benefit from the resale tailwind and trade-down behavior in a weaker consumer environment. The premium multiple holds or expands as the market rewards a clean, single-segment, high-margin compounder with strong renewals and a shareholder-friendly capital-return policy. At 38-40x a recovering ~$13.50 next-year EPS, the stock reaches the low-$500s, consistent with the $545 analyst target and the October 2025 high.

### Bear Case (~$300-340)

The simultaneous fee increases and POS disruption strain franchisee relations; renewal rates slip from 98% toward the mid-90s and new-store openings slow, pulling royalty growth back toward 3-4%. The miss streak continues, estimates keep falling, and the market re-rates a low-growth franchisor toward a more normal specialty-retail multiple. At 24-26x ~$12.50 EPS the stock falls to the low-$300s, roughly -15% to -25% from current levels. A higher-for-longer rate path compounds the pressure by slowing franchisee development financing and removing any multiple support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (mid-July), which will provide the first read on whether the Software Fee and Ad Fund are affecting renewal and development pace, and the next several quarters of POS rollout commentary.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Royalty growth sustains above 7-8% for two consecutive quarters with renewal rates holding at 98%+, confirming the cost-shifting initiatives are accretive without franchisee attrition.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year consensus stops falling and rises sequentially), reversing the current -4% trend.
- A pullback to the high-$300s/low-$300s (28x or below on next-year EPS), which would create the asymmetric entry currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Renewal rate slips below ~96%, or net store count declines, signaling the fee/POS initiatives are damaging the franchise base.
- A fifth consecutive EPS miss combined with accelerating downward estimate revisions.
- Evidence of a botched POS rollout disrupting the royalty stream (reporting delays, franchisee complaints surfacing in filings).

## Conclusion

Winmark is a high-quality, asset-light franchisor with margins, cash conversion, and renewal stability that few public companies match, and the recent pullback has tempered the valuation concern. The core debate is about price, not business quality, which is excellent. At ~34x forward earnings for mid-single-digit organic growth, with consensus estimates falling, a four-quarter miss streak, modest director selling near the highs and no offsetting open-market C-suite buying, and three simultaneous franchisee-facing initiatives that put the 98% renewal rate at risk, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

We rate WINA a **HOLD**. The premium is partly earned, but the entry price does not offer a margin of safety, and we lack the rising-estimate signal or insider-conviction signal that would support a stronger call. We would turn constructive on either a re-rating into the high-$300s/low-$300s or two quarters of clean execution on the new fee and POS programs alongside stable renewals.
