## Executive Summary

Howmet Aerospace has delivered one of the most impressive financial transformations in industrials over the past three years: revenue up 46%, operating income up 123%, and net margins expanding from 8.3% to 18.3%. The company's investment castings, fastening systems, and engineered structures businesses sit at critical chokepoints in the aerospace supply chain where multi-year OEM qualification processes, limited global capacity, and rising aircraft production rates create durable pricing power. Free cash flow reached $1.43 billion in FY2025 (17.3% margin, 95% conversion of net income), and net leverage has dropped to ~0.8x EBITDA even as the company has returned over $1.4 billion to shareholders through buybacks and dividends.

The valuation is the central tension. At ~55x trailing earnings and ~53x forward FY2026E ($4.67 consensus), HWM trades at 2.4x the A&D peer median P/E of 22.5x. This premium reflects the quality of the franchise, but it also means any disruption to the aerospace production ramp — Boeing production hiccups, tariff impacts on titanium supply, or a broader economic slowdown hitting air travel — would cause a disproportionate derating. The $1.8 billion CAM acquisition (completed April 2026) temporarily doubles leverage and introduces integration risk, though the strategic logic of expanding the fastening platform is sound.

The stock warrants a BUY recommendation based on three factors: (1) analyst estimates are still rising — FY2027 EPS has been revised up 7.7% over 90 days, suggesting the earnings trajectory has not yet been fully captured; (2) the dual-theater defense spending environment adds a second growth vector beyond commercial aerospace; and (3) the company's demonstrated ability to compound margins in a supply-constrained market suggests consensus estimates remain conservative, consistent with HWM's perfect 4-for-4 quarterly earnings beat record.

## Company Overview

Howmet Aerospace is a Pittsburgh-based manufacturer of lightweight, advanced engineered metals serving aerospace, defense, and commercial transportation markets. The company separated from Arconic Inc. in April 2020, retaining the highest-value technology-intensive segments. It operates across four segments:

- **Engine Products** (52% of revenue, 33.3% EBITDA margin): Investment castings and seamless rolled rings for jet engines and industrial gas turbines. This is the crown jewel — single crystal and directional solidification casting capabilities that take competitors years to qualify.
- **Fastening Systems** (21%, 30.4% margin): Aerospace and industrial fasteners installed nose-to-tail on aircraft and engines. The CAM acquisition expands this platform materially.
- **Engineered Structures** (14%, 21.2% margin): Titanium ingots, mill products, forgings, and extrusions for airframes and defense applications.
- **Forged Wheels** (13%, 28.5% margin): Alcoa-branded forged aluminum wheels for commercial trucks, buses, and trailers.

End-market exposure is ~70% aerospace (53% commercial, 17% defense), 15% commercial transportation, 11% gas turbines, and 4% other. RTX Corporation and GE Aerospace each account for approximately 11% of third-party revenue.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue grew 11.1% YoY to $8.25 billion in FY2025, completing a three-year CAGR of 13.4%. Operating leverage has been extraordinary: operating income compounded at 30.6% over the same period.

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $5,663M | $6,640M | $7,430M | $8,252M |
| Operating Margin | 16.2% | 18.1% | 22.0% | 24.8% |
| Net Margin | 8.3% | 11.5% | 15.5% | 18.3% |
| EPS (Diluted) | $1.11 | $1.83 | $2.81 | $3.71 |

The 860 bps of operating margin expansion since 2022 is exceptional for an industrial manufacturer and reflects genuine pricing power: management explicitly states that "product price increases are in excess of material and inflationary cost pass through to our customers." This is the hallmark of a supply-constrained market where Howmet holds near-sole-source positions.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow reached $1.43 billion in FY2025 (up 46% YoY), representing a 17.3% FCF margin with 95% conversion of net income. Capital expenditures are rising ($453M in FY2025 vs. $219M in FY2023) as the company invests in capacity expansion for aerospace and gas turbine products. This is growth CapEx in a supply-constrained market, which should generate strong incremental returns.

### Balance Sheet

Net debt declined to approximately $2.1 billion (~0.8x EBITDA), down from $3.4 billion in 2022. All three major agencies upgraded Howmet to investment grade (BBB+/Baa1) during 2024-2025. The CAM acquisition will temporarily increase leverage to roughly 1.5-1.7x net debt/EBITDA. The company secured a $1.0 billion five-year revolver and a $600 million 364-day facility in February 2026 to provide financing flexibility. Current ratio stands at a healthy 2.13x.

One off-balance-sheet item warrants monitoring: Howmet retains a legacy guarantee on an Alcoa Corporation long-term energy supply agreement expiring in 2047, with potential exposure of up to $1.14 billion. Management considers the risk remote, and Arconic Corporation would indemnify 50% of any liability. A surety bond of $80 million has been obtained.

### Capital Allocation

The company deployed approximately $1.45 billion in share repurchases plus dividends in FY2025, with $1,347 million in buyback authorization remaining. Debt reduction of ~$765 million in principal was also achieved. The dividend ($0.44/share, ~0.2% yield) is token-level and appropriate given the growth profile. Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly and disciplined.

## Growth Analysis

### Earnings Momentum

Analyst estimates are rising across all time horizons:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Revision |
|--------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| Current Year (FY2026) | $4.67 | $4.45 | +4.8% |
| Next Year (FY2027) | $5.65 | $5.24 | +7.7% |

The acceleration in FY2027 revisions (+7.7%) relative to FY2026 (+4.8%) suggests analysts are still in the early stages of modeling the full margin expansion and CAM contribution. The company has beaten consensus EPS in all four reported quarters, with surprises ranging from 3.3% to 9.4%.

### Growth Vectors

1. **Commercial aerospace production ramp**: Boeing and Airbus are increasing production after years of pandemic delays and supply chain constraints. The FAA didn't approve 737 MAX rate increases until October 2025. Each production rate increase mechanically drives higher Howmet content.

2. **Defense spending acceleration**: The dual-theater environment (Middle East and Ukraine) has pushed the US defense budget above $1.5 trillion. Howmet's 17% defense exposure benefits from both increased aircraft production and spare parts demand. European allies accelerating domestic procurement adds an international growth vector.

3. **Gas turbine demand**: The AI infrastructure buildout requires massive power generation capacity. Howmet supplies advanced castings for gas turbines (11% of revenue), and this segment benefits directly from the data center power demand cycle.

4. **CAM acquisition synergies**: Management has not provided specific synergy targets publicly, but the strategic logic of combining complementary fastener product lines with Howmet's distribution and OEM relationships is clear.

5. **Margin expansion runway**: Fastening Systems margins improved from 20.6% to 30.4% in two years. Engineered Structures went from 12.9% to 21.2%. Further room exists as volume leverage continues on a relatively fixed cost base.

### Headwinds

Commercial transportation (15% of revenue) remains weak. Forged Wheels revenue declined 1% in FY2025 and 8% in FY2024. Management expects recovery beginning in H2 2026, but tariff uncertainty and economic softness could delay this.

## Competitive Landscape

Howmet's competitive position is among the strongest in aerospace manufacturing:

**Moat Sources:**
- **Multi-year OEM qualification**: Qualifying new casting, forging, or fastener suppliers takes 3-7 years for aerospace engines. This creates enormous switching costs.
- **Near-sole-source positions**: In investment castings for advanced engine airfoils, Howmet's only peer-scale competitor is Precision Castparts (Berkshire Hathaway). The market is effectively a duopoly.
- **Capacity scarcity**: Industry-wide capacity for specialty aerospace castings and forgings is tight, with multi-year lead times for new capacity additions.
- **Intellectual property**: ~1,020 patents and ~1,590 trademarks covering advanced metallurgical processes.

**Competitive Risks:**
- Precision Castparts has the resources of Berkshire Hathaway behind it, though PCC has been slower to expand capacity.
- VSMPO (Russia) dominates global titanium supply, and geopolitical disruption could benefit Howmet's Engineered Structures segment (domestic sourcing advantage) or hurt it (supply tightness on specific alloys).
- ATI Inc. competes in titanium and specialty alloys but at significantly smaller scale.

The pricing power demonstrated over the past three years — exceeding inflation pass-through to deliver true price-over-cost gains — is the clearest evidence of competitive advantage.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Valuation compression**: At the current forward multiple, any negative revision to the aerospace production outlook, a significant Boeing production disruption, or a broader market derating of growth premiums could cause 15-25% downside without any fundamental deterioration.

2. **CAM integration**: The acquisition approximately doubles leverage and introduces execution risk. If integration costs exceed expectations or synergies are slow to materialize, the market will likely penalize the stock given the premium multiple.

3. **Tariff and trade policy**: Howmet operates in 19 countries and sources globally. While contractual pass-throughs exist, tariff uncertainty creates near-term margin volatility and could slow customer decision-making. The 10-K explicitly flags 2025-2026 tariff executive orders as a new risk factor.

4. **Customer concentration**: RTX and GE Aerospace at ~11% each, plus significant Boeing indirect exposure. Any major customer financial difficulty or production disruption disproportionately impacts results.

5. **CEO succession**: John Plant is 72 years old and has been the dominant strategic force. His departure would create significant uncertainty. No succession plan has been disclosed publicly.

6. **Alcoa guarantee**: The $1.14 billion contingent exposure from the legacy separation is remote but non-trivial.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated near-term uncertainty: ATM IV of 52.6% (1-day tenor) vs. 29.7% historical volatility is a 22.8 percentage point premium, reflecting the post-earnings repositioning environment and the imminent 1-day expiry distortion. The more informative signal is the term structure in backwardation (52.6% near-term → 34.8% at 6-month), with the 6-month P/C volume ratio at 1.77, indicating institutional put buying at the longer tenor. The OI P/C ratio of 1.15 confirms modestly elevated protective positioning. The bull thesis is consensus (short interest at 2.6% of float), and participants are hedging against valuation-driven pullback risk rather than positioning for fundamental deterioration.

- FY2027 EPS reaches $6.00+ (above current consensus) as margin expansion continues and CAM synergies materialize.
- Boeing 737 MAX moves toward 50+/month and Airbus A320 exceeds 75/month.
- Defense spending continues to scale, with European procurement adding incremental demand.

- CAM integration consumes management attention and synergies disappoint.

## Conclusion

Howmet Aerospace is among the highest-quality industrial franchises in public markets, with demonstrated pricing power, exceptional margin expansion, and competitive positions that take competitors years to replicate. The stock is expensive on absolute and relative metrics.

With FY2027 EPS estimates still rising, a perfect quarterly beat record, and structural tailwinds from both commercial aerospace and defense spending, the probability-weighted outcome favors continued outperformance. The 1.5:1 base case risk/reward ratio with manageable downside scenarios supports a BUY recommendation, with the explicit understanding that this position requires active monitoring of Boeing production rates and CAM integration progress. Position sizing should reflect the elevated valuation risk — this is a 2-3% position, not a 5% conviction overweight.
