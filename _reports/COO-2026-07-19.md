**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cooper is a global medical device company built around two very different businesses. CooperVision (~67% of revenue) is a top-three global soft contact lens maker operating in a rational four-player oligopoly, running ~30% segment operating margins and holding a genuinely differentiated asset in MiSight, the only FDA-approved lens for slowing childhood myopia progression. CooperSurgical (~33% of revenue) is an acquisition-assembled fertility and women's-health portfolio that has been a persistent drag: FY2025 segment operating income collapsed 63% on write-offs, and Q2 FY2026 delivered a $271.6M net litigation charge tied to the 2023 LifeGlobal embryo culture-media recall. The GAAP Q2 net loss of $(0.40) badly misrepresents underlying performance; non-GAAP EPS was $1.21, up 26% YoY and above the $1.10 consensus, and CooperVision margins expanded to 30%.

The single most important variable for the next twelve months is the CooperSurgical strategic review. Management confirmed on the June 2026 call it is "advancing discussions with multiple parties," and a divestiture would convert Cooper into a higher-margin, higher-return contact-lens pure-play that plausibly deserves a re-rating from its structurally depressed ROA (3.0%) and ROE (4.6%), returns that are largely an artifact of $5.4B of goodwill and intangibles, not operational weakness. That upside is real but conditional: no deal terms exist, the LifeGlobal litigation (140+ suits, 1,500+ claimants, three uncertified class actions, exposure flagged above insurance) both complicates a sale price and carries additional-reserve risk, and the FY2026 revenue guide was trimmed to $4.28–4.32B.

We rate COO a HOLD (growth_hold). The core vision franchise is executing, the beat track record is clean (four straight beats), and a value-unlock catalyst is live. But estimate revisions have stalled, the litigation is explicitly described by management as escalating rather than resolving, and the stock trades at ~15x forward non-GAAP EPS on a run-rate that is credible but surrounded by a genuine tail. This is a balanced, catalyst-dependent setup rather than an asymmetric buy.

## Company Overview

Cooper (fiscal year ending October 31) operates two segments:

- **CooperVision (~67% of revenue):** Soft contact lenses competing against J&J Vision, Alcon, and Bausch + Lomb. Core families are Biofinity/Avaira (monthly/two-week silicone hydrogel, Biofinity the highest-grossing product), MyDay/clariti (single-use), and MiSight. MiSight is the first and only FDA-approved lens to slow myopia progression in children aged 8–12, with approvals now extended to China (2021) and Japan (August 2025). Growth is driven by the industry mix-shift toward premium single-use and specialty toric/multifocal modalities plus the emerging myopia-management category.
- **CooperSurgical (~33% of revenue):** Fertility (IVF consumables/equipment, donor gametes, genomic testing) and women's health (surgical devices, cryostorage, and the Paragard copper IUD, the only hormone-free IUD in the US). Built largely by acquisition (>600 products).

Products sell in 130+ countries; ~15,000 employees; roughly half of revenue is international. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. Share and price figures reflect the February 2024 four-for-one split.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended October 31, 2025):**
- Revenue $4,092M, +5.1% YoY (CooperVision +5% to $2,744M; CooperSurgical +5% to $1,349M).
- Operating income $683M (16.7% margin), down 3% YoY, the decline driven entirely by CooperSurgical, whose operating income fell 63% to $43M on inventory and long-lived-asset write-offs plus severance from "workforce optimization."
- Net income $375M (9.2% margin), down from $392M. Consolidated gross margin ~66%, down from ~67%.
- Operating cash flow $796M; FCF $434M (10.6% margin) after $362M capex.

**Balance sheet:**
- Total debt ~$2.5B against $8.2B equity; D/E ~0.5, current ratio 1.89, moderate leverage appropriate for a stable-cash-flow device business. Altman Z 2.73 sits in the grey zone.
- Goodwill $3.85B plus intangibles $1.6B equal ~44% of $12.4B assets. This is the reason ROA (3.0%) and ROE (4.6%) look low; on invested capital ex-intangibles the operating business is far more productive. The distinction matters when benchmarking against asset-light peers.

**Q2 FY2026 (ended April 30, 2026):**
- Revenue $1,081.5M, +8% YoY. CooperVision +8% ($724M); CooperSurgical +8% ($358M), with fertility strong at +13%.
- GAAP net loss of $(77.9)M / $(0.40) EPS and an operating loss of $(31)M, entirely a function of the LifeGlobal charge. Excluding it, non-GAAP EPS was $1.21.
- CooperVision operating margin reached 30%.
- Working capital fell from $994M to $495M as ~$550M of term loan (December 2026 maturity) reclassified to short-term. Total debt roughly flat at $2.46B; the company amended and extended $950M of term loans to February 2031 in February 2026, leaving the $550M December 2026 tranche as the only near-term maturity.

FCF conversion is respectable, the core franchise margins are expanding, and the leverage profile is manageable and recently de-risked on maturities. The headline GAAP loss should be looked through; the non-GAAP run-rate is roughly $4.63 (current-year consensus) building toward ~$5.00 next year. We hold financial health at 6.0: a solid, cash-generative franchise, but the grey-zone Z-score, the intangible-heavy return profile, and the unresolved contingent liability keep it below the 7+ range.

## Growth Analysis

CooperVision is the growth engine and it is performing: +8% revenue in Q2 with margins expanding to 30%, driven by durable secular tailwinds, namely the mix-shift into premium single-use and specialty lenses and the myopia-management category where MiSight has first-mover regulatory exclusivity and a fresh Japan approval extending the runway. Fertility within CooperSurgical grew +13% in Q2 on rising infertility rates and higher maternal age, showing the segment's underlying end-markets are healthy even as its execution and litigation overhang muddy the picture.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, most recently +9.3% (June quarter, $1.21 vs. $1.11). The estimate-revision picture is stable-to-soft rather than accelerating. Current-quarter EPS was revised down 5.9% over 90 days (to $1.12) and next-quarter down 2.8%, while current-year and next-year estimates are essentially flat (+0.3% and -0.3% respectively over 90 days). This is a steady mid-to-high-single-digit compounder with a trimmed FY2026 revenue guide, not a revision pattern signaling acceleration. Growth prospects score 6.5: a durable secular demand story in the vision franchise, weighted against the absence of accelerating consensus momentum and the CooperSurgical drag.

## Valuation Assessment

At $71.71, COO trades at ~38x trailing GAAP EPS of $1.87, a figure distorted by the litigation charge and therefore not the right anchor. On forward non-GAAP consensus of $4.63 (FY2026) and $5.00 (FY2027), the stock trades at ~15.5x and ~14.3x respectively. That is a meaningful discount to the Health Care Supplies peer median P/E of ~29.7 (ALGN 29.7, WST 44.1, MMSI 28.8, NVST 60.1, LNTH 25.1).

The discount is partly explained and partly an opportunity. It is explained by the CooperSurgical drag, the LifeGlobal tail risk, and five years of TSR underperformance (indexed 67.1 vs. peer group 104.0). It becomes an opportunity if the strategic review crystallizes value: a sale of CooperSurgical would leave a pure-play vision company running ~30% operating margins that arguably deserves a low-to-mid-20s multiple, lifting both the earnings mix and the reported return profile. Analyst targets bracket this: mean $81.69, median $85.00, high $92.00, low $66.00, with the median implying ~19% upside.

We resist scoring valuation high. Cheapness on a compressed forward multiple is precisely the pattern that has repeatedly proven to be a value trap absent an independent catalyst that actually fires. Here the catalyst (CooperSurgical divestiture) is live but unconfirmed, and the LifeGlobal reserve could yet expand, which both discounts a sale price and caps re-rating. Estimate revisions are flat, not rising, so consensus is not signaling that the market is systematically underestimating an acceleration. We score valuation 5.5: the discount is real and the catalyst gives it optionality, but the setup lacks the confirmed mispricing that would justify a 7+. Prior reports pushed valuation to 7.0; on flat revisions and unrealized catalyst we mark it back toward neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

CooperVision sits in a favorable four-player oligopoly with rational pricing and structural mix-shift tailwinds. MiSight is a genuine moat asset, with first-mover regulatory exclusivity in a myopia-management category with a long runway as childhood myopia prevalence rises globally and approvals expand (China, Japan). The threats are real but slow-moving: larger competitors with deeper R&D/marketing budgets, DTC/telehealth fulfillment disruption, potential low-cost Asian entrants, and refractive-surgery/pharma substitution. Asia Pacific contact-lens sales turned negative (-6% in Q2 on Japan softness), a near-term watch item.

CooperSurgical's competitive position is weaker and deteriorating at the margin. Fertility is fragmented (vs. Vitrolife, Fairfax) with healthy demand, but Paragard's unique hormone-free positioning faces a newly FDA-approved non-hormonal competitor (Miudella), and the LifeGlobal recall has damaged reputation in an emotionally charged, referral-driven category. On balance, competitive position is stable-to-improving in the two-thirds of the company that matters most, offset by erosion in the surgical segment. Score 6.5, consistent with the recent trajectory (down from 7.5) as the surgical headwinds and Japan softness became visible. Competitive-position scores have been a weak recent predictor generally, so we do not lean on this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

**LifeGlobal litigation (dominant near-term risk).** The December 2023 recall of three lots of IVF culture media spawned patient litigation alleging embryo loss. Cooper initially deemed losses immaterial, then reversed in Q2 FY2026 with a $271.6M net charge ($324.1M gross accrued, less $52.5M insurance). As of June 5, 2026: 140+ lawsuits, three uncertified putative class actions, and 1,500+ claimants, with management explicitly flagging exposure above insurance. The trajectory of accelerating cases and rising defense costs makes further charges plausible. This is a class-action-prone, emotionally charged matter with genuine tail risk, and it discounts any CooperSurgical sale price.

**CooperSurgical execution/strategic uncertainty.** The segment was weak before the litigation (FY2025 op income -63%). The strategic review is both the upside catalyst and a source of uncertainty; management confirmed discussions but there are no deal terms.

**Governance yellow flags.** A now-remediated material weakness in CooperSurgical U.S. IT general controls (identified FY2024, remediated October 2025) sits alongside an ongoing multi-year dual-ERP rollout and a cluster of executive/director departures (Item 5.02 8-Ks in Feb 2025, Apr 2025, and two in Dec 2025) during a period of strategic upheaval. Not alarming individually, but collectively they warrant monitoring.

**Other:** the HMRC/Sauflon UK payroll-tax dispute (potential £0–71.7M plus interest, under appeal, no liability accrued); sole-source silicone hydrogel raw-material supply; ~50% FX exposure (a 10% move ≈ $35.5M quarterly operating income swing); tariffs, OECD Pillar Two, and reimbursement pressure on Paragard/fertility.

We score risk factors 4.5. The valuation does not fully reflect the LifeGlobal tail (the accrual could expand) or the binary nature of the strategic review, and the governance/turnover cluster is an additional idiosyncratic overlay. This continues the declining risk trajectory (6.0 → 4.5) as the litigation escalated.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not flashing acute stress but leans mildly cautious. Near-term ATM IV is 35.1% against 31.6% one-year historical vol, modestly rich (spread +3.5%), with a 4.0% put/call skew signaling elevated near-dated put demand. IV has fallen from 39.2% to 35.1% since mid-June as the stock rallied ~5.6%, and the term structure is flat (near ~35.1% ≈ far ~34.8%), indicating uncertainty is spread evenly across tenors rather than concentrated on a single event. The nearest expiry implies a ±9.1% move. Short interest is low at 4.3% of float (1.9 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The market prices moderate, evenly distributed uncertainty consistent with an unresolved litigation overhang and a strategic review with no fixed date, not an imminent binary catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** CooperSurgical is sold, crystallizing value and leaving a ~30%-margin vision pure-play that re-rates from ~15x forward toward a low-to-mid-20s multiple in line with quality peers. The LifeGlobal liability caps near the current accrual and is partly transferred or ring-fenced in a divestiture. MiSight's myopia-management runway (China, Japan, and beyond) compounds. On ~$5.00+ FY2027 non-GAAP EPS at a re-rated ~18–19x, the stock reaches the low-$90s, matching the $92 analyst high.

**Bear case.** The strategic review stalls or produces a disappointing price, the LifeGlobal reserve expands materially beyond $324M as claimant counts keep rising, Japan/Asia contact-lens softness persists, and Paragard cedes share to Miudella. Estimate revisions stay flat-to-negative and the stock derates toward the $66 analyst low, back near its 52-week low of $58.89, roughly 8–18% below current.

**Base case.** The vision franchise compounds high-single-digit revenue with expanding margins, the surgical review drifts without a near-term deal, and the litigation is managed but overhangs the multiple. The stock trades in a range around fair value, with the median $85 target achievable only if the review advances toward a transaction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, tied to (a) the CooperSurgical strategic-review outcome, (b) quarterly LifeGlobal claimant/reserve updates, and (c) fiscal-quarter earnings (next report is the FY2026 third-quarter release, which will be the first period to reflect any post-Q2 litigation or review developments).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed CooperSurgical divestiture at a value-accretive multiple, or credible deal terms disclosed in an 8-K.
- LifeGlobal claimant count and reserve stabilizing (no incremental charge over one to two quarters), removing the tail-risk discount.
- Non-GAAP EPS estimate revisions turning positive (>+3% over 90 days) alongside sustained CooperVision margin expansion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material additional LifeGlobal reserve (a further charge >$100M or a certified class action).
- Strategic review abandoned with no path to value crystallization, combined with continued CooperSurgical revenue/margin deterioration.
- Non-GAAP EPS revisions turning negative >5% over 90 days or a break of the four-quarter beat streak.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release, plus any 8-K disclosure on the strategic review or litigation.

## Conclusion

Cooper is a HOLD (growth_hold). The investment case rests on a high-quality vision franchise trading at a discount to peers with a live value-unlock catalyst in the CooperSurgical review. Those are legitimate reasons to own the stock, and the clean beat record plus expanding CooperVision margins support the underlying earnings power obscured by the GAAP litigation loss. The setup is balanced rather than asymmetric: estimate revisions are flat, the strategic review has no confirmed terms, and management describes the LifeGlobal litigation as escalating with exposure above insurance. The stock's ~15x forward multiple is cheap only if the surgical divestiture fires and the litigation caps near its current accrual, two conditions that remain unresolved. We would move to BUY on a confirmed accretive CooperSurgical transaction or a stabilizing litigation trajectory, and to AVOID on a further material reserve or an abandoned review. Until one of those resolves, the risk/reward supports holding, not adding.
