## Executive Summary

CareTrust REIT is a healthcare-focused REIT that owns 407 properties (37,628 beds/units) across 32 U.S. states and the United Kingdom, with approximately 75% of annualized in-place net rent from triple-net leased skilled nursing facilities and 25% from senior housing. The company has deployed $1.8B in FY2025 at an 8.6% blended stabilized yield and continues significant investment activity into 2026. The recent Moody's investment grade upgrade (Baa3, April 2026) validates the balance sheet discipline and is expected to lower future borrowing costs.

The investment case centers on a structural mismatch between the company's stated 2026 guidance ($1.90-$1.95 NFFO/share) and the likely actual outcome. Guidance assumes zero new investments beyond those made year-to-date as of the February 2026 earnings release, yet the company disclosed $214.8M of post-quarter-end investment activity and a $500M pipeline at that time. Management has indicated continued deal activity since then. This creates a likely upward revision cycle as additional investments are recognized. The 0.7x Net Debt/EBITDA ratio (as defined by the company, which includes forward equity proceeds) against a 4.0-5.0x target represents significant untapped debt capacity that can fund continued accretive growth.

The primary counterarguments are: (1) the stock has appreciated significantly over the past year and trades at ~20.5x forward NFFO, a premium to most healthcare REIT peers; (2) Ensign Group concentration at 23% of annualized contractual rental income creates single-tenant risk; and (3) the aggressive acquisition pace introduces integration risk, particularly with the UK Care REIT portfolio. On balance, the growth visibility and conservative guidance methodology create sufficient margin of safety to warrant ownership.

## Company Overview

CareTrust REIT is a self-administered, publicly-traded REIT that was spun off from The Ensign Group in 2014. The company primarily operates under triple-net leases where tenants bear all property-level operating costs, taxes, and maintenance. CareTrust collects rent and monitors operator financial health.

**Portfolio Composition (as of December 31, 2025):**
- Skilled Nursing Facilities (triple-net): 236 properties, 26,466 beds
- Senior Housing (triple-net): 171 communities, 11,162 units, including 131 UK Care Homes
- Senior Housing Operating (SHOP): 3 communities, 270 units (launched December 2025)
- Real Estate Loans: $899M in mortgage, mezzanine, and preferred equity investments

**Annualized Revenue Mix (Q4 2025 basis):** SNF triple-net ~55%, senior housing triple-net ~23%, SHOP ~1%, interest income ~18%, other interest income ~3%.

**Annualized In-Place Net Rent Mix:** SNF triple-net ~75% ($305.1M of $409.1M), senior housing triple-net ~25% ($104.0M of $409.1M).

**Geographic Concentration (annualized in-place rent):** California (~21%), UK (~17%), Texas (~11%), Tennessee (~11%).

The company operates with just 43 employees for a $5.1B asset base, reflecting the triple-net model's minimal operational overhead.

**Revenue Trajectory:**

- Growth is predominantly acquisition-driven, with organic escalators providing 2-3% annual rent bumps

**Key Profitability Metrics (FY2025):**
- Net income: $320.5M ($1.57/diluted share), +96% YoY
- Normalized FFO: $359.7M ($1.76/share), +17% YoY
- Normalized FAD: $360.0M ($1.76/share), +14% YoY
- Quarterly NFFO progression: $0.42 → $0.43 → $0.45 → $0.47 (accelerating)
- Rent collection: 100% in Q4 2025; 99.7% for full year

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $900M ($400M notes at 3.875% due 2028; $500M term loan at 4.63% due 2030)
- Cash: $198M
- Net Debt to EBITDA: **0.7x** (company-defined metric that includes $339.3M of cash/restricted cash/acquisition escrow and $238.0M of expected ATM forward proceeds)
- Undrawn revolver: $1.2B
- All floating rate exposure hedged via $500M in interest rate swaps (fixing at 3.5%)
- Altman Z-Score: 3.32 (third-party screening metric; Safe Zone)
- No debt maturities before 2028

The 0.7x leverage ratio is the single most important balance sheet datapoint. Against a 4.0-5.0x target, this implies approximately $1.6B-$2.1B of incremental debt capacity before reaching management's ceiling (depending on the target leverage level and EBITDA definition used). Combined with the $1.2B undrawn revolver and ATM equity program, CareTrust has substantial deployable capital, though the exact figure depends on equity market access, share price, and management's willingness to lever toward the upper end of the target range.

**2026 Guidance:**
- NFFO/FAD: $1.90-$1.95/share (midpoint +9.4% YoY)
- Guidance assumes NO new investments, loans, or dispositions beyond those made year-to-date as of the February 2026 earnings release

**Dilution Track Record:** Share count grew from ~155M (2024) to ~225M (2026 guidance), approximately 45% dilution over two years. However, NFFO/share grew from $1.50 (2024) to $1.76 (2025) and is guided for $1.90-$1.95 (2026), demonstrating accretive capital deployment. The spread math works: 8.6-8.8% investment yields funded with 4.3% blended debt costs and equity issued at ~4.5% FFO yield = 400-450bps of positive spread.

**Dividend:** $0.335/quarter ($1.34 annualized), 73% payout ratio on NFAD. Yield of ~3.4% at current price — below the 5.7% peer median, reflecting the growth premium the market assigns.

## Growth Analysis

**Quantified Growth Drivers:**

1. **Acquisition Deployment Acceleration:** $1.8B deployed in FY2025, with significant continued activity into 2026. Management has described a "flywheel" effect where completed deals generate relationship-based referrals for new deals.

2. **Conservative Guidance Creates Revision Tailwind:** The methodology of excluding future investments from guidance creates a likely upward revision dynamic as pipeline investments close. However, the magnitude of any revision depends on timing, financing mix, transaction costs, and whether additional equity issuance is required — factors that prevent a purely mechanical translation of gross yields to per-share earnings.

3. **SHOP Platform Launch:** Three properties launched December 2025 under RIDEA structure. This platform offers higher return potential (operating exposure vs. rent collection) and diversifies the business model. Management has indicated this will remain a small portion of the portfolio (<5%).

4. **Organic Escalators:** Triple-net leases include 2-3% annual rent bumps (CPI-linked or fixed), providing baseline organic growth on existing assets.

5. **Investment Grade Capital Cost Advantage:** The Moody's Baa3 upgrade is expected to lower incremental borrowing costs and expands access to the investment grade bond market, widening the positive spread on new investments.

**Industry Tailwinds:**
- U.S. population 65+ growing (59M+ today, projected to nearly double by 2060)
- SNF supply declining (14,742 facilities in July 2025 vs. 15,600+ in 2016) — favorable supply/demand dynamic
- Medicare Part A rates increasing 3.2% for FY2026
- CMS staffing mandate repealed (December 2025) — removed major cost overhang for SNF operators
- OBBBA legislation permanently extended 20% REIT dividend deduction

## Competitive Landscape

CareTrust competes with Welltower (WELL), Ventas (VTR), Sabra (SBRA), Omega Healthcare (OHI), and private capital (PE, sovereign wealth) for healthcare property acquisitions.

**Competitive Advantages:**
1. **Operator Relationship Network:** The Ensign heritage gives management unique insight into SNF operations. The 43-person team maintains deep operator relationships that produce off-market deal flow — evidenced by the $380M California portfolio described as "meeting the seller's expedited timing demands."

2. **Lower Leverage = Faster Execution:** At 0.7x leverage, CareTrust can close deals rapidly without needing equity raises for each transaction, unlike more levered competitors who must sequence capital.

3. **Size Sweet Spot:** Large enough to deploy $500M+ portfolios but small enough that individual $50-100M deals are material — accessing opportunities below the radar of Welltower/Ventas but above what smaller REITs can fund.

4. **Investment Grade Rating:** As of April 2026, CareTrust joins the investment grade universe, providing access to cheaper unsecured debt markets and signaling financial strength to counterparties.

**Competitive Trajectory: Improving.** The 2025-2026 acquisition pace, UK expansion, SHOP launch, and Moody's upgrade collectively represent a structural improvement in competitive positioning relative to 2023.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Ensign Group Concentration (23% of annualized contractual rental income):** The largest single risk. Ensign is a well-capitalized public company with strong financial performance, which mitigates the risk to some degree, but the single-name exposure is meaningfully above REIT diversification best practices. Loss of Ensign would reduce annualized contractual rental income by ~$92M (with an additional ~$7.6M from Ensign-guaranteed Pennant leases).

**2. Acquisition Pace / Integration Risk:** Deploying $2B+ across 2025-2026 at a rapid clip introduces underwriting risk. The UK Care REIT acquisition (~$595M cash + $291M assumed debt) adds FX risk, regulatory complexity (CQC oversight), and unfamiliar market dynamics. Management's 100% rent collection track record provides some confidence, but the pace leaves limited margin for error.

**3. Interest Rate Environment:** While current debt is well-hedged, continued growth at 8.6-8.8% cap rates requires the spread to remain positive against capital costs. If the 30Y Treasury sustains above 5% and equity prices decline (raising equity cost of capital), the accretion math weakens.

**4. Regulatory Sensitivity — Including Medicaid Risk:** Tenants depend on Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21) includes Medicaid-related provisions that CBO estimates will reduce federal Medicaid outlays by approximately $990B over FY2025-FY2034 through community engagement requirements, provider-tax changes, and state-directed payment modifications. While the CMS staffing mandate was repealed and the OBBBA permanently extended the 20% REIT dividend deduction, the Medicaid funding reductions represent a meaningful headwind for SNF operators dependent on Medicaid reimbursement. State-level actions (Idaho 4% Medicaid cut) and California SB 525 wage mandates ($25/hr by 2028) add further pressure on operator margins.

**5. Immigration Policy / Labor:** The current administration's immigration restrictions could increase labor costs for SNF operators. This is an operator-level risk that indirectly affects CareTrust through tenant financial health.

**6. Share Dilution Continuation:** While recent issuances have been accretive, the ATM program and potential future offerings represent ongoing dilution. If investment yields compress or deals disappoint, dilution becomes destructive to per-share value.

## Investment Thesis

- Investment grade rating attracts additional institutional buyers, compressing the FFO yield
- Continued 8%+ cap rate acquisitions funded at 4-5% cost of capital = durable positive spread

## Conclusion

CareTrust REIT represents a well-managed external growth story with a structural advantage: a fortress balance sheet that enables rapid, accretive acquisitions while competitors are constrained by higher leverage. The stock is not cheap on a headline multiple basis (20.5x forward NFFO), but the growth visibility — driven by conservative guidance methodology, identified pipeline, and demonstrated execution — provides reasonable confidence that actual earnings will exceed stated guidance. The Moody's investment grade upgrade, accelerating deal flow, and favorable industry demographics support continued premium valuation.

The primary risks are the rate environment and Medicaid funding reductions: if the 30Y Treasury remains above 5% and broad REIT multiples compress further, CTRE will not be immune, and OBBBA's estimated $990B in Medicaid outlay reductions over the next decade could pressure tenant operator margins. However, the 9-14%+ FFO growth provides a margin of safety that slower-growth peers (OHI, SBRA, DOC) lack. At $39.48, the stock offers a reasonable entry for investors willing to accept a 3.4% dividend yield in exchange for above-average capital appreciation potential.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The report's upside thesis relied on an unverified ~$990M April 2026 deployment figure and incorrectly stated OBBBA excluded Medicaid cuts; corrected figures (verified $214.8M post-quarter investment activity, $500M pipeline, and ~$990B in estimated Medicaid outlay reductions from OBBBA) moderate the upside case and increase regulatory risk, which may affect the target price range.
