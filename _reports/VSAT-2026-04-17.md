## Executive Summary

Viasat is a vertically integrated satellite communications company operating across commercial broadband (aviation, maritime, fixed) and defense/advanced technologies. The May 2023 Inmarsat acquisition transformed the business into a global multi-band operator with 23 satellites, but also loaded the balance sheet with $3.65B in long-term debt and $3.9B in goodwill/intangibles. The company generated $1.55B in adjusted EBITDA (34.2% margin) on $4.52B in FY2025 revenue, but remains GAAP unprofitable (-$575M net income) and free cash flow negative (-$122M) on a full-year basis.

The stock has appreciated approximately 650% from its April 2025 52-week low of ~$7.95 to $63.88, driven by successful ViaSat-3 satellite launches, an activist campaign proposing business separation, and improving quarterly results. At today's price, VSAT trades above the consensus analyst target of $51 and above the high-end target of $58. The enterprise value of ~$10.7B implies ~6.9x trailing EBITDA — reasonable for a satellite operator, but difficult to justify for one carrying distress-level balance sheet risk, facing structural competition from Starlink, and still burning cash on a GAAP basis. The risk/reward at current levels is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Viasat operates through two segments: Communication Services (~73% of revenue) and Defense & Advanced Technologies (~27%). Communication Services encompasses in-flight connectivity (IFC) for ~4,120 commercial and ~2,000 business aircraft, maritime connectivity for ~14,000 vessels, government SATCOM, and U.S. fixed broadband (~189,000 subscribers at $115 ARPU). The DAT segment develops encryption, cybersecurity, tactical gateways, and satellite payloads for the U.S. DoD and allied governments.

The company's satellite fleet includes 23 GEO satellites across Ka-, L-, and S-bands. The ViaSat-3 program — three high-capacity Ka-band satellites — represents the core growth investment: F1 launched April 2023 (with a reflector anomaly reducing capacity), F2 launched successfully November 2025, and F3 has been delivered to the launch site as of April 2026. Eight additional GEO satellites are under development.

The January 2023 sale of the Link-16 Tactical Data Links business to L3Harris for ~$1.96B partially funded the Inmarsat acquisition ($550.7M cash + 46.36M shares). Shares outstanding diluted from ~74M to ~128.5M — a 73% increase.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue was $4.52B, up 5.5% from $4.28B in FY2024 (which itself reflected the Inmarsat consolidation starting May 2023). Organic growth is low-to-mid single digits. Q4 FY2025 revenue was $1.15B. By sub-segment: commercial aviation grew ~10% in aircraft count with 1,600 in backlog (+18%), DAT grew 17% with $1.6B in new awards, and government SATCOM grew 16%. Maritime declined 8% and fixed broadband declined 19%.

**Profitability:** FY2025 operating loss was $97M and net loss was $575M, driven by $1.04B in depreciation/amortization (satellite fleet and acquired intangibles) and ~$100M in interest expense. Adjusted EBITDA of $1.55B at 34.2% margin is the economically meaningful metric for a capital-intensive satellite business.

**Balance Sheet:** $3.65B in long-term debt against $1.61B cash yields ~$2.04B net debt. Goodwill of $1.62B and intangibles of $2.27B total $3.89B — 25% of total assets. Stockholders' equity has declined to $4.55B with retained earnings turning negative (-$325M). The Altman Z-Score is estimated in the distress zone (~1.0-1.3), below the 1.8 safety threshold.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $908M (+30% YoY) is a genuine positive. CapEx of $1.03B — primarily satellite construction — produced negative FCF of $122M for the full year. Q4 FY2025 generated ~$50M positive FCF, suggesting improvement as ViaSat-3 construction winds down. Management has reduced combined FY2025+FY2026 CapEx guidance by ~$300M. The company redeemed $443M in notes post-quarter and plans $300M of Inmarsat Term Loan B repayment in FY2026.

**Earnings Trajectory:** The last four quarters show improvement — three EPS beats and one miss (the miss was a massive $-1.89 vs. $-0.45 estimate in Q1 FY2025, likely integration-driven). Most recent quarter (Q3 FY2026, December 2025) delivered $0.18 EPS vs. $-0.39 estimated — a 147% beat. Current-year EPS consensus is $1.09 (from $0.46 ninety days ago), but 30-day revisions have turned negative (-14.9% current year, -36.9% next year).

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on several pillars, each with meaningful execution risk:

**Aviation IFC (Strongest):** 4,120 aircraft in service with 1,600 in backlog. The Boeing AERA antenna evaluation (entered April 2026) for 737 MAX, 777X, and 787 linefit could be transformational if selected. The CFO hire from Delta Air Lines signals management's strategic focus here. Airlines increasingly demand high-bandwidth, reliable connectivity — a market projected to grow at mid-teens CAGR.

**Defense (Strongest Secular Tailwind):** DAT revenue grew 17% in FY2025 with $1.6B in new awards, up from ~$0.6B in FY2018. Government spending on resilient space communications, encryption, and cybersecurity provides a strong tailwind aligned with dual-theater defense spending. The December 2025 unified Ka-band government network announcement enhances competitive positioning.

**ViaSat-3 Capacity Expansion:** F2 launched successfully in November 2025. F3 delivered for launch. If both operate at design specifications, they roughly triple the company's Ka-band capacity. If they replicate F1's reflector anomaly, the thesis is severely impaired.

**Maritime (Recovering):** Revenue troughed in Q4 FY2025, with NexusWave platform leveraging ViaSat-3 capacity for competitive offering across ~14,000 vessels.

**Fixed Broadband (Declining):** 189K U.S. subscribers, down year-over-year, with 19% revenue decline. This segment is being managed for harvest, not growth. BEAD subsidies favor terrestrial providers, creating regulatory headwind.

**Growth Estimate Concerns:** While 90-day estimate revisions are strongly positive (+160.5% current quarter, +139.3% current year), the 30-day revisions have turned negative across all periods. Next-year EPS estimates fell 36.9% over 30 days. This deceleration in estimate momentum is a warning sign.

## Valuation Assessment

At $63.88, VSAT's enterprise value is approximately $10.7B ($8.7B market cap + $2.0B net debt). Key multiples:

- **EV/EBITDA:** ~6.9x on trailing $1.55B adjusted EBITDA — in line with GEO satellite peers historically (5-8x range), but at the upper end given the balance sheet risk and competitive headwinds.
- **P/E:** Negative on GAAP basis. On $1.09 current-year consensus EPS, forward P/E is ~59x — extremely expensive and reflects the early stage of the earnings recovery.
- **Price vs. Analyst Targets:** The stock trades at $63.88, above the consensus mean of $51.14, above the median of $50.00, and above the high target of $58.00. Every covering analyst's price target is below the current price.

**Peer comparison:** The communications equipment peer median P/E is 47.9x (excluding negative P/E names). VSAT's forward P/E of ~59x on thin consensus estimates places it at a premium, though the business model is not directly comparable to most peers.

**Carronade's Sum-of-Parts:** The activist argues the combined entity is worth $11B+ ($50+/share for defense alone). If defense generates ~$1.2B revenue at 15% EBITDA margins, a 15x EV/EBITDA multiple on ~$180M EBITDA yields ~$2.7B — well below their estimate. Their $11B figure likely requires aggressive standalone multiples for both segments and assumes the debt burden can be cleanly allocated. A separation would be complicated by the shared satellite infrastructure and could trigger credit downgrades.

The 650% rally to above all analyst targets implies the market is pricing in multiple favorable outcomes simultaneously: successful ViaSat-3 deployment, FCF inflection, activist-driven value creation, and continued defense growth. If any of these falters, the downside is substantial.

## Competitive Landscape

**SpaceX/Starlink** is the defining competitive threat. SpaceX's vertical integration (manufacturing and launching its own satellites) provides an unmatched cost advantage. Starlink has entered aviation (United Airlines deal), maritime, and government markets — each a Viasat core market. Viasat's counter-argument that GEO satellites allocate capacity more efficiently in high-demand zones has theoretical merit but is being tested as Starlink rapidly expands its constellation.

**Aviation IFC** competition includes Starlink Aviation, Gogo, Panasonic Avionics, and SES. Airlines increasingly prefer multi-orbit solutions, which may benefit Viasat's hybrid approach but also commoditizes the offering.

**Maritime** faces similar dynamics — Starlink Maritime offers competitive pricing and is gaining share.

**Defense** is the strongest competitive position. Viasat's encryption and cybersecurity capabilities, classified program access, and decades-long DoD relationships create meaningful barriers. The DAT segment likely commands a wider moat than any commercial segment.

The fixed broadband business has effectively lost the competitive battle to terrestrial alternatives and Starlink in all but the most remote areas.

## Risk Assessment

**Balance Sheet Risk (Critical):** The Altman Z-Score in the distress zone means a single operational disappointment (satellite failure, contract loss, economic downturn) could create refinancing pressure. ~$100M annual interest expense constrains financial flexibility.

**Execution Risk (High):** Three binary outcomes are pending simultaneously — ViaSat-3 F2/F3 performance, strategic review conclusion, and FCF inflection. The F1 reflector anomaly demonstrates that these satellites can underperform. If F2 or F3 disappoints, the capacity growth thesis collapses.

**Competitive Risk (Structural):** Starlink's cost advantage is growing. Each Falcon 9 launch that adds Starlink satellites improves SpaceX's scale economics relative to GEO operators who invest billions in individual satellites with 15+ year lifespans.

**Insider Selling (Alarming):** $381M in insider sales over the past year. At a company with negative net income and distress-zone balance sheet metrics, this volume of insider selling warrants skepticism about management's private assessment of risk.

**Analyst Sentiment Degradation:** Buy recommendations declined (3→2) and Hold increased (1→3). The one Strong Sell has been maintained. Analysts are not chasing the rally.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75-85):** ViaSat-3 F2 and F3 operate at design specifications, doubling or tripling available Ka-band capacity. Aviation IFC backlog converts into revenue at mid-teens growth rates. Boeing AERA antenna evaluation results in linefit selection for 737 MAX/777X/787 — a multi-year revenue annuity. Defense segment sustains 15%+ growth on dual-theater spending. Strategic review results in separation that unlocks defense segment at 15-20x EBITDA. FCF turns sustainably positive, enabling rapid deleveraging. Ligado bankruptcy recovery provides ~$500M+ one-time cash infusion.

**Bear Case ($25-35):** ViaSat-3 F2 experiences similar anomaly to F1, undermining capacity growth thesis. Starlink Aviation gains airline adoption faster than expected, pressuring Viasat's aviation pricing and market share. Strategic review concludes without separation (debt allocation too complex). FCF remains negative as CapEx continues for next-gen satellites. Higher-for-longer rates increase debt servicing costs. Analyst estimates continue declining. The rally reverses as momentum fades and fundamental questions reassert. At $25-35, the stock would still be 3-4x above its April 2025 lows.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No single dated catalyst with high probability of driving the stock materially lower in the near term. Q4 FY2026 earnings (likely May 2026) could disappoint if estimate momentum continues deteriorating, but the recent beat/miss record has been strong (3 of last 4 beats). The strategic review conclusion is the largest binary event — if Carronade's separation thesis is rejected, the "unlock" premium should compress. No date has been announced.

**Timing Window:** The strategic review is ongoing with no announced timeline. Next earnings report likely in May 2026. The ViaSat-3 F3 launch is another binary event within months, with no precise public date.

**Short Interest and Squeeze Risk:** 9.5% SI with 6.1 days to cover. Elevated but not extreme. At 6+ days to cover, a short squeeze is a meaningful risk, especially given the stock's demonstrated momentum. Institutional ownership is likely mixed given the activist angle.

**Borrow and Options Signal:** Near-term IV of 176.8% makes puts extremely expensive. Carrying a short position through options is prohibitively costly. The call-heavy OI (P/C ratio 0.03) confirms speculative bullish positioning that could squeeze further before reversing.

**Downside Target and Risk/Reward:** Bear case target of $25-35 from $63.88 implies 45-60% downside. Bull case of $75-85 implies 17-33% upside. The reward:risk ratio for a short favors the bear side on a probability-weighted basis, but the momentum, squeeze risk, and binary catalyst timing make execution extremely difficult.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental bear case has merit — distress-zone balance sheet, insider selling, price above all analyst targets, competitive threats from Starlink. But no specific, dated catalyst with high probability of triggering a correction exists, the short interest creates meaningful squeeze risk at 6.1 days to cover, and IV is so rich that any hedge is cost-prohibitive. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Conclusion

Viasat has legitimate operational improvements underway — aviation IFC growth, defense contract momentum, ViaSat-3 satellite deployments, and the beginning of a FCF inflection. The business is more interesting than it was 12 months ago. However, the stock has moved from $8 to $64, pricing in the best-case outcome across multiple simultaneous binary variables. At $63.88, VSAT trades above every analyst price target, carries an Altman Z-Score in the distress zone, and management insiders have sold $381M in the past year. The options market's extreme IV confirms this is a momentum/event-driven trade rather than a fundamental value proposition. Recommend AVOID at current levels with re-entry consideration below $48-50 pending resolution of key execution risks.
