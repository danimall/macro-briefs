## Executive Summary

LivaNova is a $3.6B medical technology company operating through two segments — Cardiopulmonary (57% of revenue) and Neuromodulation (43%) — that delivered $1.39B in FY2025 revenue (+10.7% YoY), $199M in operating income (14.4% margin, up from 10.3% in FY2024), and $173M in free cash flow (12.5% margin). The headline net loss of $242M is driven almost entirely by a $365.6M non-cash charge from the SNIA environmental liability ruling in Italy, which obscures what is otherwise the strongest operational year in the company's history. Operating cash flow nearly tripled from $75M in FY2023 to $254M in FY2025, and the company repaid $378M in debt over the past year, reaching a net cash position of $259M.

The FDA granted PMA approval for the aura6000 hypoglossal nerve stimulation system for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) on March 19, 2026. This device is the first approved without a contraindication for complete concentric collapse (CCC) and without requiring pre-implantation drug-induced sleep endoscopy (DISE), giving it a broader addressable population and a simpler surgical pathway than Inspire Medical Systems' competing device. With OSA affecting an estimated 936 million people globally, this represents a multi-billion-dollar market opportunity being added to an already improving base business.

The primary risk is the SNIA environmental liability, currently accrued at ~$395M with Italian Ministries seeking an additional ~$123M. LivaNova's $861M in available liquidity covers the known liability but leaves limited margin for upside revisions. Tariff risk on Italian/German-manufactured Cardiopulmonary products sold into the U.S., ongoing 3T heater-cooler litigation, and the need to build an OSA commercial organization from scratch are additional concerns. At ~17x adjusted FY2025 EPS and a significant discount to the peer median P/E of 44x, the stock compensates investors for these risks while offering substantial upside if OSA commercialization gains traction.

## Company Overview

LivaNova PLC is a UK-incorporated, Nasdaq-listed medical technology company headquartered in London with approximately 3,300 employees across 34 countries. Revenue is split 53% U.S., 19% Europe, and 28% Rest of World.

**Cardiopulmonary (57% of FY2025 revenue, $785M):** Manufactures heart-lung machines, oxygenators, autotransfusion systems, and perfusion tubing/cannulae. The flagship Essenz Perfusion System integrates patient monitoring and sensing technology. Products manufactured primarily in Italy and Germany. This segment grew 14.9% in FY2025 with segment income of $108M (+41%).

**Neuromodulation (43%, $593M):** Built around the Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS) Therapy System for drug-resistant epilepsy and difficult-to-treat depression. VNS devices are manufactured in the U.S. This segment now includes the aura6000 for OSA. Growth of 7.0% in FY2025 with segment income of $216M (+9%).

The former Advanced Circulatory Support segment was wound down in 2024, simplifying the portfolio.

## Growth Analysis

### Base Business Trajectory

The Cardiopulmonary segment has structural tailwinds: aging populations, growing cardiac surgery volumes globally, and technology adoption in perfusion. The Essenz system creates a recurring revenue flywheel through consumables (oxygenators, tubing). At 14.9% growth, this segment is performing well above medtech averages.

Neuromodulation's 7% growth reflects a mature VNS epilepsy franchise. CMS reimbursement expansion to Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes provides incremental volume, but this segment needs the OSA opportunity to re-rate.

### aura6000 OSA

The March 2026 FDA PMA approval for OSA carries three differentiated competitive advantages versus Inspire Medical Systems:

1. **No CCC contraindication.** Inspire's device cannot be used in patients with complete concentric collapse, which affects a meaningful portion of OSA patients. The aura6000's 6-electrode proximal design addresses this population.
2. **No pre-implantation DISE required.** This eliminates a costly ($2,000–$5,000) and time-consuming diagnostic step that creates friction in the Inspire patient pathway.
3. **Broader severity range.** Approved for AHI 15–65.

The OSPREY trial showed a 65% responder rate at 12 months. A next-generation MRI-compatible device with a 15-year rechargeable battery is expected in H1 2027.

Inspire Medical generates ~$800M in annual revenue from a single HGNS product with a more restrictive label, at a market cap of ~$3B. If LivaNova captures even 20% of the estimated addressable surgical OSA market over time, the revenue contribution could be $200–400M annually, representing 15–30% of current total revenue. The timeline is years, not quarters — LivaNova needs to build an OSA commercial organization from scratch, establish payer coverage, and train implanting physicians. The size of the opportunity is meaningful relative to the current $3.6B enterprise value.

### RECOVER DTD — Disappointing

The RECOVER study for VNS Therapy in unipolar depression missed its primary endpoint in 2024. Secondary endpoints were positive, and a CMS reconsideration process has been initiated, but the current non-coverage determination limits the near-term DTD opportunity. This is a negative data point but marginal to the investment thesis given the OSA opportunity's scale.

At approximately 17x adjusted earnings, LIVN trades at a 62% discount to the peer median P/E of 44.3x. This discount reflects three factors: (1) the SNIA liability overhang, (2) the GAAP net loss obscuring operational improvement, and (3) the unproven OSA commercial opportunity.

Analyst consensus targets range from $67 to $90, with a median of $80, implying 23% upside from the current price of $65.18. This range likely reflects varied assumptions about OSA commercialization pace and SNIA resolution.

The valuation discount is excessive if the SNIA liability resolves within the currently accrued range ($395–520M) and OSA commercialization proceeds on a reasonable trajectory. The discount is justified if the liability escalates materially or if OSA adoption disappoints.

A sum-of-the-parts framework: Cardiopulmonary at 3.5x revenue ($2.75B) + Neuromodulation ex-OSA at 3x revenue ($1.78B) + OSA optionality ($0.5–1.5B) - SNIA net liability ($0.5B) = $4.5–5.5B, or $82–100 per share. This is rough but directionally supportive.

## Competitive Landscape

**Cardiopulmonary:** LivaNova holds a leading global position alongside Terumo and Getinge (Maquet). The Essenz system represents genuine technology differentiation in a market where incumbents tend to retain share due to surgeon familiarity and training requirements. Consumables pull-through creates switching costs.

**VNS Therapy (Epilepsy):** Dominant position in neurostimulation for drug-resistant epilepsy. NeuroPace and responsive neurostimulation (RNS) provide an alternative pathway but address different patient profiles. The franchise is mature but defensible.

**OSA:** Inspire Medical has significant first-mover advantage — an established commercial infrastructure, physician relationships, and payer coverage. LivaNova enters with a better label (no CCC contraindication, no DISE) but must build everything from scratch. The competitive dynamic resembles a late entrant with a superior product entering a market that the first mover proved out. History in medtech suggests this can work (e.g., Medtronic entering markets proven by smaller companies) but takes 2–4 years to gain meaningful share.

## Risk Assessment

### SNIA Environmental Liability (HIGH)
The Court of Appeal remand creates uncertainty on final amounts. The 10-K contains going-concern language related to "future unanticipated large liability claims." A worst-case outcome ($500M+) would materially impair the balance sheet and could require dilutive financing.

### Tariff Exposure (MODERATE)
With the U.S. representing 35% of Cardiopulmonary segment revenue (~$275M), incremental tariffs of 10–20% could compress margins by 200–400bps on that revenue stream, or approximately $30–55M in annual margin impact.

### 3T Heater-Cooler Litigation (MODERATE)
Ongoing product liability cases with settlements still occurring as recently as April 2026. The litigation provision decreased by $15.3M in FY2025, suggesting progress, but the 10-K's going-concern warning language indicates tail risk remains.

### OSA Commercial Execution (MODERATE)
Building an OSA commercial organization, securing payer coverage, and training physicians takes time and capital. Any delay in the next-generation MRI-compatible device (expected H1 2027) would slow adoption. Inspire Medical will compete aggressively to defend its position.

### Financial Statement Revisions (LOW)
The 10-K references revisions to previously issued financials. Without full detail, this is a monitoring item rather than an actionable concern.

### Iran Business (LOW)
$10.9M in revenue from Iran with explicit intent to continue operations. Creates reputational and sanctions compliance risk.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 53.5% is rich versus 39.7% historical volatility (13.8pp spread), with IV in the 75th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (53.5% near-term vs 41.1% 6-month), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress or event risk. Near-term put skew of 9.2% shows elevated put demand. The OI put/call ratio of 0.38 is call-heavy, suggesting directional positioning skews bullish despite the elevated near-term IV. The backwardation likely reflects residual uncertainty around the Q4 earnings release (February 25, 2026) aftermath and OSA commercialization announcements. Near-term uncertainty is elevated, but positioning is constructive on the long side, consistent with a wide outcome distribution skewed positively by the OSA catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($85–95)
SNIA resolves within the accrued range, with no material increase from the additional Ministry request. OSA commercialization proceeds on schedule with favorable early payer coverage decisions. Revenue grows 12–14% annually through FY2027 as aura6000 revenue begins contributing $50–100M. Adjusted operating margins expand toward 18–20% on operating leverage. The stock re-rates toward 25x adjusted earnings as GAAP losses normalize and the OSA revenue ramp becomes visible. Fair value range of $85–95 on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.50–5.00.

### Bear Case ($40–48)
SNIA escalates materially (to $500M+), consuming the cash buffer and potentially requiring dilutive financing. Tariff implementation compresses Cardiopulmonary margins. OSA commercialization proves slower than expected — payer coverage delays, physician training bottlenecks, or competitive response from Inspire erodes the differentiation advantage. The stock trades back toward 12x adjusted earnings with reduced earnings power. Floor estimate of $40–48.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $80 within 12–18 months, based on 20x estimated FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$4.00 (implying mid-teens revenue growth and continued margin expansion). This is conservative relative to the 44x peer median but reflects the SNIA overhang discount.

**Bull case target:** $90–95, based on 22–24x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.50 if OSA payer coverage accelerates and SNIA resolves favorably.

**Bear case target:** $42–48 (26–35% downside from current).

**Upside/downside from current ($65.18):** Base case +23%, bull case +38–46%, bear case -26–35%.

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months. Key catalysts: (1) first OSA commercial revenue disclosure and payer coverage announcements (H2 2026); (2) SNIA Court of Appeal implementation ruling (timing uncertain, likely 2026); (3) next-gen MRI-compatible aura6000 launch (H1 2027).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. SNIA liability increases above $550M total (vs. ~$395M currently accrued), requiring dilutive financing.
2. OSA revenue run-rate below $20M annualized by Q4 2026, indicating material commercialization delay.
3. Cardiopulmonary organic growth decelerates below 5% for two consecutive quarters, indicating competitive share loss or tariff-driven demand destruction.

## Conclusion

LivaNova's operational transformation is obscured by a legacy environmental liability. The core business delivered 10.7% revenue growth, 14.4% operating margins, $173M in FCF, and reached a net cash balance sheet in FY2025. The aura6000 FDA approval for OSA adds a large new growth vector with differentiated competitive positioning versus Inspire Medical. At ~17x adjusted earnings — a 62% discount to the peer median — the stock compensates for SNIA liability risk and OSA execution risk. The 4-for-4 earnings beat record, analyst consensus of $80 (23% upside), and improving operational trajectory support a BUY recommendation with a base case target of $80.
