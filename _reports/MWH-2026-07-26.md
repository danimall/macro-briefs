**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SOLV Energy is one of the largest independent U.S. providers of engineering, procurement and construction (EPC), commissioning, operations and maintenance (O&M), and repowering services for utility-scale solar PV, battery storage, and transmission and distribution (T&D) infrastructure. The company IPO'd in February 2026 at $25.00 per share, used the proceeds to eliminate its term debt, and now carries a post-IPO balance sheet with $384.9M of cash and an undrawn $200M revolver. Q1 2026 revenue grew 65.9% year-over-year to $676.8M, gross margin expanded 310 basis points to 17.6%, and Adjusted EBITDA rose 174% to $92.5M. Surging U.S. electricity demand driven by data-center and AI load is making utility-scale solar plus storage the marginal supply of choice, and SOLV's vertical integration into self-perform electrical/T&D (Spartan, SDI, the pending RWE deal) hedges toward grid infrastructure that benefits from load growth regardless of solar-specific incentives.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. First, management concluded disclosure controls were not effective as of March 31, 2026, and one of the four identified material weaknesses is specifically over percentage-of-completion revenue recognition, the single most estimation-intensive accounting method and the core of this company's reported results. With $21.3M of Q1 gross profit coming from favorable change-order/estimate revisions and "vendor recoveries," earnings quality cannot be underwritten with confidence until those controls are remediated. Second, a federal clean-energy tax-credit cutoff around July 4, 2026 is pulling demand forward, creating a near-term booking rush but an unquantified air pocket afterward, and backlog was nearly flat quarter-over-quarter ($8.02B to $8.17B) despite the pre-deadline surge. Third, the Up-C/TRA structure diverts cash to insiders: public Class A holders own only ~57% of economics, a $172.3M-and-growing tax receivable agreement transfers 85% of realized tax benefits to American Securities and other insiders, and a ~$39.5M cash RUA settlement is due December 23, 2026.

The stock has round-tripped its entire post-IPO advance. It ran from the $25 offering to an intraday $48.40 on May 12 (the first public earnings release) and has since retraced to $27.27, roughly 44% off the high and only modestly above the IPO price. On unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, that retrace improves the entry. But a drawdown of this magnitude, layered on a genuine control-weakness disclosure and a policy cliff, means the market is pricing real uncertainty rather than mispricing an obviously good business. We rate MWH HOLD (uncertain) with an upgrade path tied to control remediation and demonstrated post-cliff bookings.

## Company Overview

SOLV Energy provides infrastructure services to the power industry across the project lifecycle: EPC, testing and commissioning, O&M, and repowering of utility-scale solar PV, battery storage, and related T&D assets. Customers are project developers, independent power producers, and utilities. New-construction projects run 12–18 months, typically starting under limited-notice-to-proceed agreements followed by lump-sum EPC contracts, with revenue recognized on a percentage-of-completion (cost-to-cost) basis. O&M is contracted long-term at fixed monthly fees plus time-and-materials corrective work.

In Q1 2026, new construction was 96.1% of revenue ($650.7M), existing-infrastructure/O&M was 3.7%, and other was 0.2%. Within new construction, solar PV and PV-plus-storage was $577.3M, T&D was $50.3M (up ~188% YoY), and standalone battery storage was $23.1M. The T&D growth rate reflects the deliberate pivot toward grid infrastructure that is less exposed to solar-specific incentive policy.

The corporate structure is an Up-C (umbrella partnership C-corp): SOLV Energy, Inc. is a holding company owning ~57.0% of operating entity SOLV Energy Holdings LLC, with Continuing Equity Owners led by PE sponsor American Securities holding ~43% via LLC interests and Class B voting (non-economic) shares. The former parent was Swinerton. Operating subsidiaries include SOLV Energy LLC, SEHV Solutions, CS Energy, SDI (solar predrill/pile foundations, acquired January 2025), and Spartan Infrastructure (electrical transmission self-perform, acquired June 2025). The company consolidated from two reportable segments to one operating/reportable segment in Q1 2026.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials require translation because the GAAP headline is misleading for a company that just IPO'd. Revenue grew 65.9% YoY from $407.8M, driven by ~$246.9M of organic new-construction growth plus ~$26.1M from acquisitions. Gross profit rose to $119.1M from $59.1M (14.5%), with management attributing the expansion to productivity gains, favorable weather, improved pricing, and vendor recoveries.

The GAAP operating loss of $(7.2)M and net loss of $(27.4)M are dominated by non-recurring IPO items: $64.9M of non-cash stock compensation (including a one-time $52.3M charge for the modification/accelerated vesting of legacy MOIC profits-interest awards at IPO) and a $10.7M loss on debt extinguishment. Stripping those out, the underlying business is profitable and growing, up 174% YoY from $34.0M on an Adjusted EBITDA basis. A naive screen reading "net loss plus negative Altman inputs" would misjudge this company; the loss is an artifact of the transition to public status, not operating deterioration.

The balance sheet is the strongest element. Total assets were $1.96B, and the company repaid ~$405.6M of term loans with IPO proceeds, eliminating long-term term debt. Remaining debt is modest equipment financing (~$26M) plus finance leases. The revolver (matures February 2031) was undrawn to $191.5M. Stockholders' equity was $432.5M plus $378.7M of non-controlling interest. This is an appropriate, conservative capital structure for a working-capital-intensive contractor, and it removes refinancing risk from the near-term picture.

The working-capital model deserves scrutiny. SOLV runs negative net working capital: contract liabilities/deferred revenue ($346M) exceed contract assets ($158M), meaning customer prepayments fund operations. This is favorable while backlog is growing but becomes a cash headwind if the pipeline stalls after the tax-credit cliff. Operating cash flow was $14.2M in Q1, down from $20.2M on working-capital swings, the line where any post-cliff demand slowdown would show up first. The company also made $144.8M of distributions to LLC members pre/at IPO in Q1.

Earnings quality is the caveat. Of the gross profit expansion, $21.3M came from favorable change-order/estimate revisions plus vendor recoveries. In a percentage-of-completion business with an unremediated material weakness over exactly that estimation process, the margin trajectory carries more uncertainty than the headline suggests.

## Growth Analysis

The secular demand backdrop is broad-based. Utility-scale solar plus storage is the marginal supply of choice given its low cost and short build cycle. This is supported by external coverage (Forbes, Inside Climate News, Solar Power World) documenting data-center-driven demand and utility-scale solar strength through June–July 2026, which raises the durability of the demand thesis relative to a single-company story.

SOLV's specific growth metrics support the theme: revenue and Adjusted EBITDA growth, an $8.17B total backlog, and remaining performance obligations of $2.46B expected to convert over 12–18 months. Book-to-bill was reported at ~1.2x (per analyst sources). The June 2026 announcements of scaling operations across >4 GW of large-scale solar-plus-storage projects reinforce near-term visibility.

Estimate revisions are broadly positive. Current-quarter EPS was revised +15.9% over 30 days to $0.24; next-year EPS moved +6.8% over 90 days to $1.63. The one soft spot is next-quarter EPS, cut 10.0% over 90 days to $0.45, plausibly the earliest hint of post-cliff booking caution. The single reported quarter as a public filer beat by 67.2% ($0.25 vs $0.15 estimate), but a one-quarter track record provides limited calibration for forward confidence.

The central bull/bear debate is the durability of bookings after the July 4 tax-credit cutoff. The flat QoQ backlog despite a policy-driven pre-deadline rush that reportedly nearly doubled secured pipeline argues that the rush has not translated into net backlog growth at SOLV, or that project completion is offsetting new awards. The T&D/substation/electrical self-perform expansion (Spartan, SDI, and the pending RWE acquisition) diversifies toward grid infrastructure that benefits from data-center load regardless of solar-specific incentives. That diversification is the growth lever most likely to persist through a policy air pocket.

## Valuation Assessment

MWH trades at $27.27, a market cap of ~$3.3B, and a forward/trailing P/E of ~24.9x on the reported EPS basis. Against consensus, next-year EPS of $1.63 implies ~16.7x forward, and current-year $1.39 implies ~19.6x. The utility peer set (MGEE 19.7x, CPK 19.5x, AVA 16.7x, AWR 22.4x, CWT 22.7x; median 19.7x) is not the right comparison: those are regulated utilities with 2–5% dividend yields, betas near 0.5, and mid-single-digit rate-base growth. SOLV is a non-dividend-paying, project-based EPC contractor growing revenue 66%, so a utility multiple is neither a floor nor a ceiling.

On a growth-adjusted basis, ~16.7x forward earnings for a business compounding revenue in the mid-double-digits with expanding margins is not demanding, and the PEG is comfortably below 1.0 if the growth holds. That is the bull's valuation argument. The offset is that these forward estimates rest on earnings quality that cannot yet be trusted: with an unremediated material weakness over the revenue method and Q1 gross profit from estimate revisions and vendor recoveries, the "E" in the P/E carries an unusually wide error band. Discipline requires an independent catalyst before rewarding a cheap-looking multiple, and cheapness in isolation has historically sorted the wrong way; here the multiple is reasonable rather than obviously mispriced.

The analyst set is constructive: 1 strong buy, 9 buy, 1 hold, with a mean target of $47.18 and median $50.00, and a range of $32–$55. Every published target sits above the current price. On a fast-growing stock with a reasonable multiple, we would normally treat consensus targets as floors rather than ceilings. Here we temper that: the targets were largely set before the full round-trip and before the market fully digested the policy-cliff timing, and the next-quarter estimate cut suggests the consensus growth path is being trimmed at the margin. We assign a valuation score of 5.5: the multiple is fair-to-attractive if the numbers are clean, but the earnings-quality overhang prevents scoring cheapness with conviction.

## Competitive Landscape

SOLV is one of the largest independent utility-scale solar/storage EPC contractors in the U.S. Its differentiation is vertical integration: adding self-perform electrical/T&D (Spartan), foundation pile-drilling (SDI), and now substation construction/testing (RWE) reduces subcontractor dependence and captures more scope per project. This improves margin capture and scheduling control in a labor- and equipment-constrained buildout, and it positions the company to compete on the grid-infrastructure side where demand is driven by load growth rather than solar-specific tax credits.

The competitive position is improving on trajectory. The T&D revenue growth and the sequence of self-perform acquisitions indicate share gain into an adjacency the market is only beginning to price. The industry is "highly competitive" per filings, and lump-sum EPC is a margin-thin, execution-sensitive business, so the moat is operational (scale, self-perform capability, safety/quality record, relationships with developers and IPPs) rather than structural. The risk to competitive position is not displacement but the industry-wide demand cadence after the tax-credit cliff, which could compress pricing across all contractors. On balance, SOLV is gaining scope and diversifying its exposure faster than peers, which supports a modestly above-average competitive-position score, tempered because the level of moat protection in commodity EPC contracting is inherently limited.

## Risk Assessment

The risk cluster here is specific, dated, and mostly not yet priced with confidence, which is why this dimension scores low.

**Material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting.** Management concluded disclosure controls were not effective as of March 31, 2026, and identified four separate weaknesses: procure-to-pay/liability recognition/vendor setup/indirect tax accruals; percentage-of-completion revenue recognition (cut-off, estimates-to-complete, contract identification, transaction-price disclosures); fair-value review for goodwill, business combinations, and equity awards; and IT general controls. Remediation is ongoing and unfinished. The second weakness is the most concerning because POC is the company's core revenue method, and a large share of Q1 gross profit came from estimate revisions and vendor recoveries, precisely the areas the weakness touches. Until this is remediated, reported margins and backlog conversion carry elevated restatement risk.

**Policy/tax-credit cliff.** Revenue is highly sensitive to federal renewable incentives. The rush to beat the tax-credit cutoff creates a near-term project pull-forward followed by a potential demand air pocket. The 10-Q flags reduction/elimination/expiration of incentives as a top risk, and the flat QoQ backlog despite the rush is the early tell.

**Up-C / TRA cash leakage.** The TRA obligation transfers 85% of realized tax benefits to American Securities and other insiders, and payments are expected to be significant and rising as LLC units are exchanged. The RUA settlement scales with the share price. These are real cash claims ahead of common holders that headline EBITDA ignores.

**Contract/estimate and concentration risk.** Lump-sum EPC exposes the company to cost overruns, liquidated damages, change-order disputes, and project losses. Backlog "may not be realized" and contracts are cancelable on short notice. Customer concentration and credit risk (paid in arrears, customer bankruptcy/dispute exposure) are standard for the model but material.

**Governance and disclosure.** American Securities retains outsized control via Class B voting shares. An 8-K Item 5.02 (director/officer departure/election) filed May 12, 2026 alongside the first public earnings release could not be retrieved and remains unconfirmed; a leadership change concurrent with the initial public earnings release warrants follow-up given the control-weakness backdrop. No DEF 14A proxy has been filed yet, so compensation and governance detail is unavailable.

**Limited public history.** One quarter as a public filer limits the ability to calibrate forward estimates or trend margins.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** SOLV is a scaled, market-leading beneficiary of data-center and AI-driven electricity load that makes utility-scale solar plus storage the cheapest, fastest incremental supply. The balance sheet is debt-free at the term-loan level, revenue is compounding 66%, margins are expanding to 17.6%, and the backlog plus ~1.2x book-to-bill provide 12–18 months of visibility. The T&D/self-perform pivot (Spartan, SDI, RWE) diversifies toward grid infrastructure that benefits from load growth regardless of solar-specific incentives, hedging the policy cliff. On ~16.7x next-year EPS with a sub-1.0 PEG, the stock is inexpensive for the growth if the numbers are clean, and every analyst target sits above the current price (median $50). If controls are remediated on schedule and post-cliff bookings hold, the round-trip to the IPO price is an attractive entry. Bull target $50–$55, matching consensus median-to-high and implied by ~30x next-year EPS as the market re-rates on demonstrated backlog durability.

**Bear case.** The material weakness over percentage-of-completion revenue is over the exact estimation process that generated $21.3M of Q1 gross profit through change-order revisions and vendor recoveries, and it raises genuine restatement risk. The July 4 tax-credit cliff pulls demand forward, and the flat QoQ backlog despite the industry rush suggests net bookings are not accelerating; a post-cliff air pocket compresses both volume and pricing across the EPC field. The negative-net-working-capital model that funds operations from customer prepayments reverses into a cash drain if the pipeline stalls. The Up-C/TRA structure and the December RUA cash settlement siphon cash away from public holders. If controls slip or bookings soften, the stock re-tests the IPO price and below. Bear target $23–$25 (~8–16% downside), roughly the IPO level and below the $32 low analyst target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The risk/reward is genuinely two-sided. The demand tailwind, balance sheet, and reasonable multiple support the long case; the unremediated revenue-recognition control weakness, the policy cliff, and the cash-leakage structure prevent underwriting conviction with only one public quarter of history.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Remediation of the percentage-of-completion revenue-recognition material weakness, confirmed in a subsequent 10-Q, or auditor sign-off that controls are effective.
- Sequential backlog growth above ~$8.5B and book-to-bill sustained above 1.1x in the first post-July-4 quarter, demonstrating demand durability through the cliff.
- Continued T&D/grid revenue growth above 50% YoY, confirming the policy hedge is working.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any restatement or expansion of the revenue-recognition weakness, or a downward revision to previously reported margins/backlog.
- A post-cliff backlog decline below ~$7.5B or book-to-bill falling below 0.9x, signaling a demand air pocket.
- Operating cash flow turning materially negative on working-capital reversal, or a cut to full-year Adjusted EBITDA guidance.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (the first post-July-4 quarter, expected ~August 2026) is the decisive natural checkpoint: it will show whether the tax-credit rush translated into net bookings and whether control remediation is progressing. The December 23, 2026 RUA cash settlement is a secondary dated item to monitor.

## Conclusion

SOLV Energy is a fundamentally sound, fast-growing business: a scaled utility-scale solar/storage EPC leader with a debt-free balance sheet (at the term-loan level), 66% revenue growth, an $8.17B backlog, and direct leverage to the data-center-driven power buildout. The GAAP net loss is an IPO artifact and should not be mistaken for operating weakness. The stock's full retrace from $48 to $27 improves the entry meaningfully.

What holds us back from a BUY is a cluster of specific, unresolved risks: an unremediated material weakness over the company's core revenue-recognition method (compounded by the fact that estimate revisions and vendor recoveries drove much of the Q1 margin gain), a July 2026 federal tax-credit cliff whose demand aftermath is unknown and whose early tell (flat QoQ backlog despite an industry rush) is not reassuring, an Up-C/TRA structure that diverts substantial cash to insiders ahead of public holders, and only one quarter of public financial history. The options market is pricing a ±22.5% near-term move in backwardation, confirming a high-uncertainty, event-driven name. We rate MWH HOLD (uncertain), with a clear upgrade path: remediated controls and demonstrated post-cliff bookings would convert this into a compelling BUY on a business with a genuine structural tailwind. Until then, the specificity and lack of resolution of the risk cluster argue for patience over conviction.
