## Executive Summary

Matthews International is two-thirds of the way through dismantling and rebuilding itself, and the arithmetic of what remains is now visible enough to value. Over twenty months the company contributed the SGK brand-solutions business into a 40%-owned joint venture (Propelis) for $228.0M of net cash plus $263.0M of JV paper, sold warehouse automation to Duravant for $224.8M cash, exited European roto-gravure and flexographic printing at a $38.4M pre-tax loss with $7.7M of seller financing attached, and bought The Dodge Company (embalming chemicals) for $55.6M net. Revenue ran at $258.6M in the March 2026 quarter — roughly $1.03B annualized against $1.80B in FY2024 — and 83% of it is now Memorialization.

The balance sheet repair is real and should be credited. Total debt fell from $710.8M to $579.2M between September 30, 2025 and March 31, 2026; the $300M 8.625% second-lien notes due October 2027 were redeemed in January at 104.313% and replaced with a $700M revolver and $150M amortizing term loan at a 4.18% weighted rate including swaps. Retiring $25.9M of annual coupon and replacing it with roughly $12.5M implies about $13.4M of pre-tax annual savings against a $12.9M cash redemption premium — a one-year payback. Roughly $295M of revolver capacity is undrawn and the company states covenant compliance.

What the repair has not fixed is cash generation or the trajectory of the operating businesses. H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was −$67.4M and free cash flow −$76.7M, following FY2025 figures of −$23.6M and −$59.4M. Stripping working capital out, Matthews consumed about $29M of cash before any working-capital movement in a half-year that reported $80.0M of adjusted EBITDA — because roughly $22.3M of that EBITDA is a non-cash 40% share of a JV that distributed nothing, while $25.7M of interest, $11.2M of Tesla legal fees and ~$21.6M of strategic-initiative charges were cash. Industrial Technologies, the segment that was supposed to be the growth engine, produced −$7.8M of adjusted EBITDA in H1 FY2026 against +$66.3M for all of FY2023, and revenue recognized under the over-time method fell to 2% of Q2 sales from 13% a year earlier. On August 4, 2026, the CEO of twenty years announced his retirement with no successor named and the portfolio review still unfinished. We move from HOLD 4.8 to HOLD 4.3 on those three developments, and classify the call as uncertain: fair value sits close to spot, and the Q3 FY2026 release scheduled for after the close on August 6 is the first clean read on whether the cash drain reverses.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1850, Matthews now describes itself as operating "through two core global businesses — Industrial Technologies and Memorialization," plus a significant investment in Propelis, with over 4,300 employees in 15 countries (down from 5,400 in 19 countries as of the January 2026 activist-settlement release).

**Memorialization** (77.2% of H1 FY2026 sales, 83% of the March quarter) manufactures bronze and granite memorials, caskets, cremation equipment and mausoleums, and — after Dodge — embalming chemicals. H1 FY2026 sales were $419.4M, up 5.9%, with adjusted EBITDA of $87.8M at a 20.9% margin (Q2: 22.7%). Management attributes the growth to "inflationary price realization" and Dodge, "partially offset by lower sales of caskets, cremation equipment, mausoleums, and cemetery memorials." Four major product lines declining in unit terms simultaneously is consistent with the 10-K's own structural disclosures: rising US life expectancy, a secular shift to cremation, and "an increase in products manufactured offshore, primarily in China."

**Industrial Technologies** ($112.4M H1 sales) combines product identification (marking and coding) with the dry battery electrode (DBE) energy-storage equipment franchise built on roughly two decades of internally developed rotary-processing and calendering technology. Segment EBITDA has gone 13.1% (FY2023) → 8.2% (FY2025) → −6.9% (H1 FY2026), which management attributes to lower engineered-products and product-identification margins plus "customer delays impacting the timing of projects within the energy storage business." Segment goodwill is down to $42.5M against $39.9M of accumulated impairments already recognized in the segment.

**Brand Solutions** is now an equity-method line. Matthews' 40% share of Propelis adjusted EBITDA was $22.4M in the lagged July–December 2025 period; its 40% share of Propelis depreciation, amortization, interest and other items was $34.0M, producing an $11.6M equity-method loss recorded in administrative expense. Grossed up proportionately, Propelis generated roughly $56M of adjusted EBITDA against roughly $85M of D&A/interest — a leveraged, GAAP-loss-making entity that has distributed no cash. Matthews is explicitly not the controlling decision-maker, its interest is transfer-restricted, and results are reported on a three-month lag.

That is a plausible path to positive FY2027 free cash flow without any operating recovery — and it is the single most important item the August 6 print will inform.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth engine in the current configuration.

Memorialization grew 5.9% in H1 on price realization plus Dodge, with volumes falling in caskets, cremation equipment, mausoleums and cemetery memorials. Death rates are the volume driver, cremation mix reduces revenue content per death, and multi-year contracts with large consolidator customers cap price realization on a lag. Dodge is the right structural response — embalming chemicals are consumables indifferent to the burial-versus-cremation decision — but at roughly $43M of pre-acquisition sales it is too small to change the segment's trajectory quickly.

Industrial Technologies has gone from $505.8M of FY2023 revenue to a ~$173M annualized run rate, part divestiture and part order drought. The over-time revenue collapse from 13% to 2% of quarterly sales says the project pipeline converting to revenue has essentially stopped, and Matthews does not control the timing of EV and grid-storage battery capacity additions. World-model context on AI-adjacent industrial demand does not transmit here; DBE demand is a derivative of battery capex, which is soft.

Propelis is a call option on a leveraged pre-media consolidation that currently generates a GAAP loss, distributes nothing, and reports a quarter stale.

Consensus (two analysts) has current-year EPS at $1.09 and next year at $1.24 — 14% growth that is mostly interest savings and cost reduction rather than volume. The revision pattern is mixed and warrants caution: current quarter −12.8% and next quarter −13.2% over 30 days, against current year +9.0% and 90-day next-quarter +30.3%. With two contributors, the near-term cuts and the full-year raise cannot both be structural; the honest read is low-confidence estimates with recent negative near-term drift. This does not qualify as the inflection signal that rising estimates plus accelerating revenue would represent — revenue is contracting and the estimate momentum is ambiguous.

Against that, management's own proxy discloses the adjusted-EPS series: $3.28 (FY21) → $2.88 → $2.88 → $2.17 → $1.26 (FY25). On its own favorably defined metric, earnings power fell 62% in four years. Growth prospects score 3.5.

## Valuation Assessment

Start from what the market is paying. Market capitalization of $853.9M (31.202M shares × $27.365) plus $543.2M of net debt gives an enterprise value of $1,397M. Deducting the $254.2M Propelis carrying value leaves $1,143M attributable to the operating businesses, or **9.9x the ~$115M annualized cash-proximate adjusted EBITDA**. On headline adjusted EBITDA of ~$160M annualized the multiple falls to 8.7x, but that figure includes ~28% non-cash JV earnings and should not be used for leverage or valuation.

On earnings: $27.365 / $1.09 = **25.1x current-year** and / $1.24 = **22.1x next-year** consensus non-GAAP EPS. Trailing GAAP EPS is −$0.79, and the 90.7x "forward P/E" in the data feed simply mirrors the trailing figure — a data artifact, not a valuation input.

Peer context matters here. The closest comparable is Service Corp International at 19.5x with a $11B capitalization, an integrated funeral-and-cemetery footprint, and positive free cash flow. Peer median across the specialized consumer services set is 19.2x (MCW 21.5x, FTDR 19.2x, ADT 9.3x, HRB 6.4x). Matthews trades at a 15–30% premium to that median and to SCI specifically, with contracting revenue, 4.7–5.6x leverage, negative tangible book, and an uncovered dividend. There is no growth or quality differential that justifies a premium to SCI.

The sum-of-parts confirms the same conclusion from a different direction. Using Memorialization annualized adjusted EBITDA of $175.6M (H1 × 2), annualized corporate overhead of $44.7M, zero for Industrial Technologies given negative EBITDA, and Propelis at $254.2M carrying value:

- At 8.0x / −8.0x: $1,405M − $358M + $254M − $543M = **$758M equity ≈ $24.3/share**
- At 9.0x / −9.0x: $1,580M − $402M + $254M − $543M = **$889M equity ≈ $28.5/share**

The current $854M capitalization implies roughly 8.8–9.0x Memorialization EBITDA, zero for Industrial Technologies, and full book value for a transfer-restricted JV stake. That is neither a bargain nor an obvious short. The doubling of H1 EBITDA ignores seasonality, and tax leakage on asset sales has been punitive — the FY2025 SGK gain of $55.1M pre-tax became $6.2M after tax, an ~89% effective rate on that item, while the European sale produced a larger after-tax loss than pre-tax loss.

The 3.73% dividend yield ($1.02 annualized on $27.365, roughly $31.8M of cash per year) provides some valuation floor, but it is not covered by free cash flow and was raised into a negative-FCF half-year, so it should be treated as a management commitment rather than a durable yield.

Two analysts carry targets of $36 and $40 — 32% to 46% above spot. On a stock at 22–25x forward earnings with contracting revenue, analyst targets are not independent confirmation; they reflect the same portfolio-monetization arithmetic as the bull case and are unusually thinly sourced. We use them as the bull-case reference rather than as evidence of mispricing. Valuation score 4.3: fair value approximates spot, with real optionality attached but no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Memorialization is the franchise, and its trajectory is mixed rather than deteriorating. Scale in bronze and granite manufacturing, distribution relationships with funeral homes and cemeteries, and 20.9% segment margins expanding 30bp year over year evidence genuine pricing power. The 10-K's own framing supplies the caution: "markets with ample supply capacity," demand correlated to death rates, prices locked by multi-year contracts with consolidator customers, and growing Chinese import competition. Price is masking volume decline in four product lines at once; that can persist for several years but not indefinitely.

Industrial Technologies is deteriorating on both revenue and margin. Product identification competes against much larger platforms. The DBE energy-storage franchise is technologically differentiated — the February 2026 arbitration decision "clarified the Company's ownership of and rights in certain dry battery electrode technology" — but demand is lumpy, project-based, and currently absent, and the principal counterparty is simultaneously litigating against Matthews on three fronts.

Brand Solutions was surrendered to a bigger platform in exchange for cash plus 40%. The strategic logic (scale response to structural pressure on print and marketing spend) is defensible; the early evidence — a GAAP loss, heavy D&A and interest, no distributions — is not yet supportive.

The clearest external validation of asset value is Duravant paying $224.8M cash for a warehouse-automation unit that generated $1.3M of pre-tax income in H1 FY2025 on $60.9M of net book value. Pieces of this portfolio are worth more to strategic buyers than the consolidated multiple implies. Competitive position 4.5 — a stable-to-improving core franchise, a deteriorating second segment, and a relinquished third.

## Risk Assessment

**The Tesla complex.** Three active arbitrations. The February 13, 2026 interim decision found Tesla established liability on "only a limited subset" of its claims with damages deferred, and imposed a narrow injunction on certain parts in DBE machines (Matthews states approved replacements exist and operations were not materially impeded). Tesla's February 2025 complaint on patent inventorship added trade-secret misappropriation claims in a February 10, 2026 demand; a third demand from September 8, 2025 alleges warranty breach and design defects. Judge Davila confirmed a prior award in Matthews' favor on October 1, 2025; Tesla has appealed to the Ninth Circuit. No loss is accrued and no range is estimable. The cash cost is escalating: $11.2M of legal fees in H1 FY2026 versus $8.6M in H1 FY2025, and $22.2M in FY2025 versus $12.4M in FY2024.

**The $98.5M unbilled contract asset.** Net contract assets were $99.7M at September 30, 2025 and $98.5M at March 31, 2026 — flat — while over-time revenue fell roughly 70%. The balance is "predominantly related to ongoing projects with Tesla." At 11.5% of market capitalization, owed by a counterparty running three arbitrations, this is the largest quantifiable single-point risk in the story. In fairness, no impairment has been recorded, the allowance for credit losses actually declined from $13.5M to $12.6M with only $0.3M charged to expense, and the company states it continues to perform under its contracts. The disclosure gives no conversion timeline; that is the concern, not an assertion of impairment.

**Concentration of book equity in Propelis.** $254.2M, 49.6% of equity, minority, transfer-restricted, currently GAAP loss-making, reported on a three-month lag, with Matthews explicitly not the decision-maker on operations or distributions. Propelis' standalone leverage, covenants and liquidity are not disclosed.

**Leadership vacuum.** The August 4, 2026 CEO retirement announcement named no successor. Bartolacci will support the transition "at least through January 1, 2027." A chairman transition also occurred between January and August 2026 (Garcia-Tunon → Nauman). An open-ended search during an unfinished portfolio review creates strategic drift risk. The alternative reading — an orderly, voluntary retirement at the end of a 29-year career that clears the way for a new mandate — is equally consistent with the language, and no evidence of a forced departure exists.

**Capital-structure concentration.** Revolver and term loan both mature January 31, 2029, secured by a first-priority lien on substantially all assets. One wall, not a ladder.

**Governance tripwire (unverified).** The FY2025 10-K discloses that a change-of-control default could arise if shareholders approve declassification of the Board and a shareholder subsequently wins a proxy contest. Amended and Restated Articles were filed February 19, 2026 alongside Items 5.03 and 5.07. Whether that amendment declassified the Board is not established and should not be assumed; if it did, one leg of a two-part credit tripwire may now be satisfied.

**Structural Memorialization erosion.** Cremation mix and rising life expectancy reduce demand for the highest-content products; Chinese import competition is explicitly disclosed as expected to continue.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case (~$38–42)

The strategic review, publicly running since November 2024 and still "ongoing," concludes with a sale of Memorialization at a strategic multiple. At 11x $175.6M of annualized segment EBITDA the business is worth roughly $1,930M gross; add Industrial Technologies at a modest option value and Propelis at book, deduct $543M of net debt and heavy tax leakage consistent with the SGK precedent, and equity lands in the high $30s to low $40s. Supporting evidence: Duravant paid $224.8M for a unit earning $1.3M of half-year pre-tax income; the board has been substantially reconstituted; Barington's nominations were withdrawn under a January 2026 standstill; J.P. Morgan is advising; the CEO transition removes the last obstacle to a decisive portfolio action. In a lesser version, FY2027 free cash flow turns positive on the $13.4M interest saving, completed restructuring, and $20–30M capex, the Tesla arbitrations resolve favorably and the $98.5M contract asset converts, and the stock re-rates toward the $36–40 analyst range on cash generation alone.

### Bear case (~$18–21)

Memorialization price realization exhausts against locked consolidator contracts while unit volumes keep declining, taking segment EBITDA to $150–160M. Industrial Technologies stays EBITDA-negative as battery capex remains soft. Tesla escalates in the damages phase, legal spend stays above $20M annually, and a reserve is eventually taken against part of the $98.5M contract asset. Propelis requires capital or is marked below carrying value, impairing 49.6% of book equity against already-negative tangible book. Free cash flow stays negative, the dividend is cut, and the succession vacuum stalls the portfolio review. At 7x $175.6M less 7x corporate overhead, Propelis at 75% of carrying value, and $543M of net debt, equity is roughly $564M, or $18/share — 34% below spot.

### Base case (~$27–28)

The SOTP at 8.5–9.0x Memorialization with zero for Industrial Technologies and Propelis at book produces $24–29, centered on approximately $27. That is spot. Q3 shows partial cash-flow improvement without confirming a turn; the strategic review continues without a transaction; the multiple stays roughly where peers sit adjusted for leverage and litigation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Fair value approximates the current price, the decisive information arrives within days of this report, and the mechanics penalize both new longs (rich near-term volatility, negative FCF, uncovered dividend) and new shorts (14% short interest, 8.2 days to cover, live monetization catalyst, thin two-analyst coverage with targets 32–46% higher).

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 results after the close on August 6, 2026 — the first reporting period fully clear of the divestiture noise. Secondary checkpoints: the FY2026 10-K (expected late November 2026), CEO successor announcement (guided before January 1, 2027), and the Q4/FY2026 goodwill test.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Operating cash flow turns positive in Q3 FY2026 with H1-to-Q3 free cash flow improving toward breakeven**, confirming that the −$29M pre-working-capital drain was driven by the identifiable non-recurring items (settlement, incentive payouts, strategic charges) rather than structural cost absorption.
2. **Industrial Technologies returns to positive segment adjusted EBITDA** alongside a rebound in over-time revenue above roughly 8% of quarterly sales, evidencing the energy-storage pipeline converting rather than merely being delayed.
3. **A definitive transaction** — sale of Memorialization or a Propelis monetization at or above the $254.2M carrying value — or **material progress on the Tesla contract asset**, meaning the $98.5M balance declines materially through invoicing and collection rather than through reserve.
4. A price toward **$22–23** on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore a margin of safety against even the 7x bear-case SOTP.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Any reserve, impairment, or write-down against the $98.5M contract asset**, or an adverse damages ruling in the Tesla arbitrations with a quantified accrual.
2. **A dividend cut or suspension**, or any use of the ATM (1,250,000 shares remain authorized) — either would confirm that capital returns were never fundable from operations.
3. **Memorialization segment adjusted EBITDA margin falling below 19%** or segment sales turning negative year over year, indicating price realization has exhausted against declining volumes.
4. **A Propelis carrying-value write-down** or a capital call, given the stake represents 49.6% of book equity against negative tangible book.
5. Departure of the CFO or failure to name a CEO successor by the January 1, 2027 transition date.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report — 3.8 (April) → 4.4 (May) → 4.8 (June), AVOID → AVOID → HOLD — tracked a real improvement: the January redemption of the 8.625% notes, the February refinancing at 4.18%, $131.6M of debt reduction, and Memorialization margins expanding to 22.7% in Q2. We now step back to 4.3, and the reasons are new facts rather than price action (the stock is up 3.8% since the prior snapshot). First, Industrial Technologies has turned EBITDA-negative at the segment level (−$7.8M in H1) with over-time revenue at 2% of quarterly sales, so the segment that carried the growth case is now a cash drain with an absent order book. Second, consensus for the next two quarters was cut 12.8% and 13.2% over thirty days. Third, the CEO of twenty years announced his retirement on August 4 with no successor named and the portfolio review unresolved, which adds execution risk to the one catalyst that supports the bull case.

Management quality stays near the middle. The divestiture program realized genuine value — Duravant's $224.8M for a barely profitable unit is the clearest evidence — and the refinancing has a one-year cash payback. Against that: roughly $96M of dividends across three years of cumulative GAAP losses, a dividend increase into a −$76.7M free-cash-flow half-year, $7.4M of proxy-defense costs across two years, $7.81M of compensation actually paid in a year when the company's own adjusted EPS fell 42% and TSR trailed the peer index by 46 points, and no open-market insider purchase or sale by any officer or director between November 2025 and June 2026. The absence of buying with the stock at $25–27 is a mild negative rather than evidence of a bearish internal view.

At $27.365 the market is paying roughly 9.9x ex-Propelis EV/cash-proximate EBITDA, 22–25x thinly covered forward non-GAAP earnings, and a premium to Service Corp at 19.5x, for a business with contracting revenue, 4.7–5.6x leverage, negative tangible book, half of book equity in an illiquid loss-making JV, and unquantifiable litigation exposure to the counterparty that owes it 11.5% of its market capitalization. The optionality is real and the portfolio is demonstrably worth more in pieces than in aggregate. The price already reflects a fair share of that. Hold existing positions through the August 6 print; commit new capital only on confirmed cash-flow inflection, a definitive transaction, or a price in the low $20s.
