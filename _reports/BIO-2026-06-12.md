**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bio-Rad is two businesses stapled together, and only one of them is operating. The first is a life-science-tools and clinical-diagnostics manufacturer generating ~$2.58B in revenue, ~$375M of free cash flow, and non-GAAP operating margins of roughly 12-13%. The second is a passive ~38% voting stake in Sartorius AG carried at $4.90B as of March 31, 2026. That stake alone approximates the company's entire $6.3B market capitalization, which means the market is assigning close to zero value to the operating business net of a holding-company discount. This structure dominates everything: GAAP net income swung from a $1.84B loss in 2024 to a $760M gain in 2025 to a $527M loss in Q1'26, driven entirely by Sartorius mark-to-market, not operations.

The reason BIO is not simply an AVOID, and the specific reason to hold over a 12-month horizon, is the reported arrival of Elliott Investment Management. Reuters and Bloomberg reported in mid-May 2026 that Elliott built a sizable stake and intends to push the company to address its underperforming shares (as of the research, this is reported via Tier-1 news but not yet confirmed via a 13D filing). The thesis is unusually legible: monetize, collar, or spin the Sartorius position; return capital; and run a margin-expansion program against operating margins that lag peers materially. This is a catalyst with a name, a horizon, and a clear playbook, landing on top of a structurally cheap sum-of-the-parts.

The reason to hold rather than buy is equally concrete. The underlying operating business is deteriorating beneath the GAAP noise: organic revenue fell 4.2% currency-neutral in Q1'26 with both segments declining, gross margin compressed from 53.7% (2024) to 51.9% (2025), and management took $172.8M of impairments on two failed acquisitions. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen 30.9% over 90 days for the current quarter and 12-14% for the full year and next year. The Schwartz family controls 75% of board seats through Class B shares and can block a forced change in control, which caps the probability that Elliott achieves a clean, fast value-unlock. The setup is asymmetric and worth owning, but it is a special-situation hold contingent on an activist process, not a fundamental growth story.

## Company Overview

Bio-Rad Laboratories, headquartered in Hercules, California, operates more than 12,000 products across two reportable segments. Clinical Diagnostics (~60% of sales) supplies test systems, reagents, kits, and quality-control products to clinical labs, with established franchises in quality control, blood typing, diabetes (HbA1c) testing, and immunology. Revenue here is highly recurring because labs standardize on a platform and buy consumables per sample. Life Science (~40% of sales) sells instruments and consumables for separating, purifying, and quantifying proteins and nucleic acids, anchored by Droplet Digital PCR (ddPCR), recently expanded via the ~$258M Stilla Technologies acquisition (closed June 2025) and the QX Continuum launch, plus process chromatography and food-safety testing.

Geographically, the business is roughly 40% US and 60% international, with Europe the largest non-US region. Competitors are far larger and better capitalized: Thermo Fisher, Danaher, Abbott, Roche, Siemens Healthineers, BD, Qiagen, bioMérieux, DiaSorin, and QuidelOrtho. Bio-Rad competes on niche differentiation and razor/razorblade installed-base economics rather than scale.

The defining feature is the Sartorius position. As of Q1'26, BIO held 12,987,900 ordinary (voting) shares plus 9,588,908 preference shares, carried at fair value of $4.90B (down from $5.67B at year-end 2025). There is also a separate €400M collateralized loan to a Sartorius family vehicle (FV ~$359M) and value-appreciation rights. BIO has no board seat, has explicitly tried and failed to obtain the information needed for equity-method accounting, and is a powerless minority holder behind the Sartorius family trust's controlling block.

## Financial Analysis

The headline numbers are unusable without separating operations from the Sartorius mark.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 2,802 | 2,671 | 2,567 | 2,583 | 592.1 |
| Operating income ($M) | 483 | 338 | 269 | 47 | 34.1 |
| GAAP net income ($M) | (3,628) | (637) | (1,844) | 760 | (527.1) |

**Revenue is flat-to-declining and worsening organically.** 2025 sales grew 0.7% (flat currency-neutral). Q1'26 reported +1.1% but -4.2% currency-neutral; the headline was entirely FX from a weak dollar. Life Science fell 4.3% CN and Clinical Diagnostics fell 4.1% CN. Management guided that weak conditions persist through the rest of 2026.

**Operating margin collapsed in 2025** to 1.8% GAAP from ~10% in 2024, driven by $172.8M of impairments: an $81.7M IPR&D write-off on Dropworks (2021 deal) and $91.1M net on Curiosity Diagnostics (2022 deal, discontinued). Both reflect failed M&A. Stripping those out, non-GAAP operating margin runs ~12-13%, which is the relevant baseline and the source of the activist opportunity given peers operate in the 20s-30s.

**Gross margin is eroding:** 53.7% (2024) to 51.9% (2025), pressured by inventory write-offs, higher material costs, and weak fixed-cost absorption. Q1'26 gross margin held roughly flat at 52.3%.

**Cash generation is genuinely solid:** 2025 operating cash flow was $532M and FCF was $375M, a 14.5% FCF margin. This is the one financial line that is not distorted by Sartorius and confirms the operating business, while shrinking, throws off real cash.

**The balance sheet is not distressed.** Current ratio 5.6x, debt/equity 0.42, $1.56B cash and short-term investments against $1.2B of senior notes ($400M at 3.3% due March 2027, now classified current; the remainder at 3.7% due 2032). The Altman Z of 2.95 sits in the grey zone only because the EBIT/assets component is distorted by the impairment-driven operating-income collapse. Management states it has ample liquidity to repay or refinance the 2027 notes, likely at a higher rate.

**Capital allocation is shifting toward buybacks:** $295.5M repurchased in 2025 (1.2M shares) versus $203.6M in 2024, with $237M of authorization remaining. No dividend. The mark-to-market on Sartorius also drives a large deferred-tax liability ($882M at Q1'26); the Q1'26 loss generated a $162.7M tax benefit. Any future monetization of the stake would crystallize a substantial tax bill on the embedded gain, which complicates a clean "sell and return the cash" narrative.

## Growth Analysis

This is the weakest part of the story and the reason the recommendation stops short of a buy. Organic revenue is contracting, and the deterioration is showing up directly in estimates.

Consensus EPS revisions are uniformly negative and accelerating at the front end:
- Current quarter: $1.75, down 28.7% over 30 days and 30.9% over 90 days
- Next quarter: $2.31, down 14.9% over 30 days
- Current year: $9.00, down 12.3% over 90 days
- Next year: $9.72, down 14.3% over 90 days

The earnings track record confirms eroding visibility: after large beats in mid-2025 (+49.1% in Q2'25, +15.7% in Q3'25), the last two prints missed (-8.0% in Q4'25, -5.7% in Q1'26). The combination of declining organic revenue, compressing gross margin, and falling estimates is the opposite of an earnings inflection.

The end-market backdrop is a sector-wide, multi-year post-COVID normalization: tight academic and government research budgets (2025 US federal funding cuts are explicitly cited), soft biopharma and small-biotech demand, and structurally weaker China (diabetes-testing reimbursement cuts, local-content tender restrictions). Newly emphasized in Q1'26 is Middle East conflict hurting EMEA Clinical Diagnostics demand and raising logistics and fuel costs. Peers face the same demand environment, but Bio-Rad is underperforming within it; peers like Illumina are stabilizing while BIO declines organically.

The only structural growth pocket is ddPCR, now expanded via Stilla and QX Continuum, but it is too small to move a flat-to-declining consolidated top line in the near term. The growth thesis that justifies holding is not organic; it is the value-unlock from Elliott's activism reshaping capital allocation and lifting margins from a depressed base.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation case is a sum-of-the-parts, and it is the strongest leg of the thesis.

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Sartorius stake (Q1'26 FV) | ~$4.90B |
| Sartorius family loan (FV) | ~$0.36B |
| Net cash (cash/STI less notes) | ~$0.36B |
| Implied value, Sartorius + balance sheet | ~$5.62B |
| Market cap | ~$6.28B |
| Implied value of operating business | ~$0.66B |

At a ~$6.3B market cap, the market values the operating business at roughly $660M, or under 0.3x sales, for a unit generating ~$375M of annual FCF. Even applying a steep holding-company discount and a deferred-tax haircut to the Sartorius stake, the operating business is priced as if it is worth a small fraction of its cash flow. That is the gap an activist exists to close.

On a conventional basis, the trailing P/E of ~46.9x is meaningless because GAAP EPS is dominated by Sartorius marks; the EPS of $27.85 reflects the 2025 gain, not earnings power. The cleaner lens is the operating non-GAAP estimate of ~$9.00 for the current year, putting BIO at ~32x forward earnings — full for a declining-revenue business, but the multiple is the wrong frame here because the embedded asset value, not the operating P/E, drives the equity.

Peer P/E medians (Illumina 29.4x, Medpace 28.6x) are not comparable because they do not carry a stake equal to their market cap; the negative P/Es at Avantor and Azenta reflect their own impairments. The relevant comparison is the holding-company discount: BIO trades close to the value of its passive stake, which is the definition of a wide discount, and the catalyst to narrow it is now present.

Analyst price targets cluster tightly: low $275, mean $297, median $300, high $320. The current price of ~$292 sits near the mean, consistent with a market that has not yet priced an activist outcome. These targets are not a ceiling here, but they are not a floor either, because the operating estimates underlying them are falling. The valuation score reflects a genuine mispricing of the sum-of-the-parts, tempered by the dual-class obstacle and the deteriorating operating earnings stream that any discount-narrowing must overcome.

## Competitive Landscape

Bio-Rad is a sub-scale niche competitor against far larger rivals. Its defensible positions are in quality control products, blood typing, diabetes (HbA1c) testing, and ddPCR, areas where installed-base economics and switching costs provide stickiness. The recurring consumable revenue in Clinical Diagnostics is the structural quality in the business.

The trajectory, however, is deteriorating, which is what matters for scoring. Organic revenue is declining while peers stabilize, gross margin is compressing rather than expanding, and the company took $173M of impairments on two failed acquisitions that were meant to extend its franchises. In multi-year public tenders, particularly in China, Bio-Rad's lack of scale limits pricing power against Thermo Fisher, Danaher, Roche, and Abbott. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 51.98 versus a peer-group 105.15 quantifies the chronic underperformance: BIO has roughly halved relative to its peer set. The competitive position is stable-to-eroding, not improving, which is why this dimension scores below average.

## Risk Assessment

**Sartorius concentration is the dominant risk.** A passive minority stake worth ~78% of market cap drives GAAP swings of billions independent of operations. BIO has no board seat and cannot obtain information for equity-method accounting; it is a powerless holder behind the family trust's controlling block. The value of the equity is hostage to Sartorius's share price.

**Investment Company Act overhang.** The stake is large enough that BIO discloses it "might be deemed an investment company" under the 1940 Act, relying on a Section 3(b)(1) exemption. A successful challenge would have a material adverse effect and could call into question the validity of contracts. This is also a reason management may eventually be forced to reduce the position, aligning with Elliott's goals.

**Schwartz family dual-class control.** Class B holders elect 75% of the board and can block a change in control. This is the single biggest impediment to a forced value-unlock and the reason the activist path is more likely to be negotiated than hostile.

**End-market and macro.** Constrained research funding, soft biopharma demand, China reimbursement cuts, and Middle East/EMEA disruption all weigh on the top line. With ~60% international sales, FX swings can mask or amplify organic trends, as Q1'26 demonstrated.

**Tax friction on monetization.** An $882M deferred-tax liability and a large embedded gain mean any Sartorius sale crystallizes a substantial tax bill, reducing the net proceeds available for return.

**Governance and management.** No open-market insider buying despite a beaten-down stock; notable executive and director turnover (multiple Item 5.02 filings across 2024-2026, including an April 24, 2026 departure/election); and a board performance metric (operating income) that fell to $47M in 2025. The 2027 notes are now current and will likely refinance at a higher rate.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning does not contradict a constructive special-situation view. Near-term ATM IV is 39.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 40.6%, so implied vol is fair-to-slightly-cheap (spread -1.2%), at the 17th percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew is -21.0% (call-heavy) at the front week and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 0.22 to 0.05 since late May, consistent with the market leaning toward an activist-driven upside catalyst rather than hedging downside. The term structure is flat (39.4% near, 38.0% far), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated on a single near-term event. Short interest is negligible (91 shares), so there is no squeeze dynamic and no informed bearish crowd. The options market is tilted to the upside without pricing an imminent binary, which fits a multi-month activist process.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Elliott forces a negotiated value-unlock: a Sartorius monetization, collar, or spin-out that converts the embedded ~$4.9B asset into returnable capital and/or a clean tracking value, combined with a margin program that lifts operating margins toward the high teens. Even a partial closure of the holding-company discount plus an accelerated buyback against the $237M authorization re-rates the equity. The sum-of-the-parts argues the operating business is being valued at ~$660M against $375M of FCF; correcting that alone supports a materially higher price. A favorable scenario gets the stock toward $360-400, roughly 23-37% upside, if the discount narrows and Sartorius stabilizes or recovers.

### Bear Case
The Schwartz family uses its 75% board control to rebuff Elliott, organic revenue keeps declining (-4% to -5% CN), estimates continue falling, gross margin erodes further, and Sartorius shares drop again (they fell from $5.67B to $4.90B in a single quarter). The Investment Company Act risk and tax friction make monetization slow and value-destructive. In that scenario the stock retraces toward its 52-week low of ~$223, roughly 24% downside, with the operating business worth little and the stake stagnant.

The asymmetry is reasonable but not clean: the bull case requires the activist to overcome a control structure designed to resist it, and the bear case is the continuation of an observable operating decline. That balance is why this is a hold and not a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next two quarterly prints (Q2'26 around late July, Q3'26 around late October) and any Elliott 13D filing or public engagement. The activist process is the clock.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Elliott files a 13D or reaches a public standstill/cooperation agreement that includes concrete steps on the Sartorius stake (monetization, collar, or spin), board changes, or a capital-return commitment.
- Organic (currency-neutral) revenue returns to flat or positive AND consensus EPS revisions stabilize (no further downward moves over a 90-day window).
- Management announces a credible margin-expansion program targeting operating margins toward the mid-to-high teens with quantified savings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The Schwartz family publicly rejects engagement and Elliott exits, removing the catalyst, while organic revenue stays negative.
- Sartorius shares decline materially further, taking the stake value below net debt-adjusted thresholds and eliminating the sum-of-the-parts cushion.
- Gross margin falls below ~50% or additional impairments signal further M&A failure, alongside continued estimate cuts.

## Conclusion

Bio-Rad is a special situation, not an operating turnaround. The operating business is shrinking organically, margins are below peers, and estimates are falling sharply; on those facts alone the stock would be an AVOID. What changes the calculus is the reported Elliott stake and the unusually clean sum-of-the-parts: a ~$4.9B passive Sartorius position trapped inside a $6.3B company, leaving the cash-generative operating business priced near zero. That gap is what an activist exists to close, and the call-heavy options positioning plus negligible short interest suggest the market is leaning toward, but has not yet priced, that outcome.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth (value-unlock) rationale. The 12-month thesis rests on Elliott driving a negotiated monetization of Sartorius, a capital-return acceleration, and a margin program: a specific, named, time-bound catalyst rather than vague optionality. It stops short of a buy because the Schwartz family's 75% board control can blunt the process, the operating business is actively deteriorating, and tax and Investment Company Act friction complicate any clean unlock. Own it for the catalyst, size it as a special situation, and use the upgrade and downgrade triggers above to convert the position once the activist process clarifies in either direction.
