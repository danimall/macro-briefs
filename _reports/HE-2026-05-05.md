## Executive Summary

Hawaiian Electric Industries is a regulated utility holding company serving approximately 95% of Hawaii's population across five island grids. The company is in financial distress following the August 2023 Maui wildfire, carrying $1.92B in remaining settlement liabilities against $1.61B in stockholders' equity. The underlying utility business is operationally functional, generating $391M in operating cash flow in FY2025 and earning slightly above its authorized 9.5% return on Oahu, but the corporate entity faces an existential financing challenge: $1.44B in unfunded settlement installments due over the next three years.

The stock has rallied 45.6% over the past 12 months on settlement progress milestones (class settlement finalized January 2026, credit upgrades, legislative support via Act 258 securitization authority). At $15.06, it trades above the analyst consensus target of $13.75 (high: $14.00) and into a zone where the remaining financing risk provides asymmetric downside. With 14% short interest, zero insider buying, no dividend, and the certainty of substantial equity dilution ahead, the risk/reward profile is unfavorable at current levels.

## Company Overview

HEI operates through its sole remaining segment: Hawaiian Electric Company (and subsidiaries Hawaii Electric Light and Maui Electric). The company sold its bank subsidiary (American Savings Bank) in December 2024 and is divesting its non-regulated clean energy portfolio (Pacific Current). It is pursuing corporate simplification by merging HEI and Hawaiian Electric into a single entity, pending PUC approval.

The utility operates standalone island grids with no interconnection to other systems, requiring higher reserve margins. Approximately 75% of net energy still comes from fossil fuel oil, with 36.8% renewable penetration achieved in 2025 against a 100% by 2045 target. Revenue is regulated under a Performance-Based Regulation framework with decoupling, inflation adjustment, and 98% fuel cost pass-through mechanisms.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement (FY2025):**
- Revenue: $3.09B (down from $3.22B in FY2024, reflecting lower fuel oil pass-through prices; underlying kWh sales grew 2.5%)
- Operating Income: $235M (vs. -$1.71B in FY2024 driven by wildfire charges)
- Net Income: $123M ($0.71 EPS diluted)
- Operating Cash Flow: $391M
- Free Cash Flow: ~$50M (after ~$341M capex)

**Balance Sheet (Dec 31, 2025):**
- Cash: $502M unrestricted + $479M restricted (first settlement installment)
- Long-term debt: $2.29B
- Stockholders' equity: $1.61B (retained deficit: -$666M)
- Wildfire settlement liability: $1.92B ($479M current + $1.44B noncurrent)
- Debt/equity: 4.56x
- Altman Z-Score: 0.88 (deep distress zone, <1.81 threshold)
- Credit ratings: B+/Ba3/B+ (below investment grade; positive outlooks)

**Dilution trajectory:** Shares outstanding grew from 109.7M (2023) to 172.6M (2025) — a 57% increase in two years. Further dilution of 30-55% is virtually certain to fund the remaining $1.44B in settlement payments.

**Liquidity:** $1.57B total available, including credit facility access. Adequate for near-term operations but insufficient for settlement installments 2-4 without additional capital raises.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are constrained by the regulatory environment and the capital-intensive nature of the business:

- **Rate base growth:** The utility needs $350-400M/year in capex for grid modernization, wildfire mitigation, and the Waiau repower project ($847M-$1.15B). However, rate base growth accretes to per-share value only after accounting for dilution.
- **Regulatory mechanisms:** Annual Revenue Adjustment provides inflation-linked revenue growth. EPRM allows interim recovery on approved capital projects. But the PBR framework caps upside at authorized returns (~9.5%).
- **Estimate revisions:** Current year consensus is $1.07 EPS (3 analysts), next year $1.15. Current year estimates are approximately stable over 30 days (-0.9%) but have risen 13% over 90 days — on a 172.6M share base that will likely grow to 220-270M shares within 18 months.
- **Per-share growth math:** Even if utility earnings grow 10% annually, a 40% share count increase transforms that into a per-share decline. On $1.15 next-year EPS and a hypothetical 240M share count post-dilution, EPS drops to approximately $0.83.

The 100% renewable target by 2045 is theoretically a multi-decade growth driver, but execution has slowed (only ~25% GHG reduction achieved against an aspirational 70% by 2030 target), multiple projects have been withdrawn by developers, and federal policy headwinds (potential elimination of renewable tax credits, tariffs on battery storage) add friction.

## Valuation Assessment

At $15.06 per share on $0.71 TTM EPS, HE trades at 21.2x trailing earnings. On FY2026E of $1.07, it trades at 14.1x forward earnings. On FY2027E of $1.15, it trades at 13.1x.

**Key valuation problems:**

1. **Stock trades above every analyst target.** Consensus mean: $13.75; high: $14.00; current price: $15.06. The stock is 7.6% above the highest analyst target. Two analysts rate HOLD, one rates SELL. Zero BUY ratings.

2. **Earnings denominator will shrink.** The forward P/E calculation uses current share count. Post-dilution (likely 220-270M shares), the effective P/E on a fully-diluted basis rises to 17-20x, placing HE at a premium to mainland regulated utilities trading at 14-16x.

3. **No dividend yield.** HE historically paid ~$1/share annually. It has suspended dividends indefinitely and explicitly states it does not intend to resume until settlement is fully funded. For a utility stock, zero yield eliminates a primary valuation support.

4. **Negative retained earnings.** Book value of $1.61B includes a -$666M retained deficit. At current market cap ($2.6B), price/book is ~1.6x — elevated for a below-investment-grade utility with no dividend.

5. **Peer comparison:** Mainland regulated utilities with investment-grade ratings, dividends, and no existential liabilities trade at 14-18x forward earnings. HE at 14x with below-IG ratings, no dividend, pending massive dilution, and bankruptcy risk in its own 10-K disclosure does not warrant a similar multiple.

The stock price reflects full credit for settlement resolution and ignores the dilutive cost of achieving that resolution.

## Competitive Landscape

Hawaiian Electric's monopoly position is structurally strong: each island grid is independent, barriers to entry are extremely high, and regulatory mechanisms provide reasonable earnings visibility. The utility has no direct competitor for grid services.

However, several forces erode the monopoly over time:
- **Distributed solar:** 45% of single-family homes already have rooftop solar. Each incremental solar installation reduces grid dependency.
- **Act 266 (retail wheeling):** Authorizes direct power transactions up to 2 MW, representing a modest but directionally negative competitive development.
- **JERA partnership:** Japan's largest power producer has partnered with the State of Hawaii to support energy transition, potentially providing alternative generation pathways.

The competitive position score reflects the natural monopoly advantage tempered by Hawaii-specific distributed energy penetration that is among the highest in the nation.

## Risk Assessment

**Tier 1 — Existential:**
- **$1.44B financing gap.** The 10-K explicitly states bankruptcy is an alternative if capital raising fails. No financing plan has been publicly announced. The company is "working with financial advisors."
- **Subrogation appeal.** Certain insurers have appealed the dismissal of their subrogation claims. An adverse ruling could delay or complicate settlement payments, which are conditions to final resolution.

**Tier 2 — Material:**
- **Dilution certainty.** At least 30% additional dilution is virtually certain; 50%+ is plausible depending on mix of debt vs. equity. Every day the stock trades higher before the capital raise, the per-share dilution is less severe — but this creates a timing game, not a value thesis.
- **Waiau flood zone.** The newly discovered FEMA flood zone designation for the $1.15B repower project threatens federal DOE financing, may violate HECO siting guidelines, and could add costs the PUC won't allow recovery of.
- **Below-investment-grade lock.** Borrowing costs are elevated. The September 2025 debt issuance carried a 6.0% coupon — roughly 150bps above investment-grade utility debt. This compounds across the ~$350-400M annual capex program.

**Tier 3 — Background:**
- Hawaii mild recession (UHERO forecasts job losses, tourism decline)
- Federal policy risk (renewable tax credit elimination, tariff impacts on batteries)
- Maui continuing disconnection moratorium (collectibility)
- No insider buying despite 60%+ discount to pre-fire levels

**Options Market Signal:**
ATM implied volatility rose from 44.1% to 51.0% over the past month (a 7.0pp increase) while price declined 4.1%. Put/call open interest ratio surged from 0.08 to 0.65 — still below parity but a dramatic shift in positioning toward protective puts. This is consistent with growing institutional concern that the rally above analyst targets is fragile and that a dilutive capital raise announcement could trigger a sharp repricing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($18-20):** Securitization under Act 258 is approved on favorable terms, funding the majority of remaining settlement payments with minimal additional equity dilution. Credit upgrades to investment grade follow within 12-18 months. Dividend reinstatement occurs by 2028. The utility earns its authorized return, Waiau project proceeds, and HE re-rates toward mainland utility multiples (16-18x). In this scenario, the current price is approximately fair value — the upside is limited because the good outcome is largely priced.

**Bear Case ($7-10):** The financing plan requires a larger-than-expected equity raise (>$800M), executed at a discount to market. Or securitization terms are unfavorable (higher rate, shorter tenor), requiring supplemental equity. Subrogation appeal succeeds, delaying settlement and triggering covenant concerns. Waiau project costs escalate. The stock de-rates on dilution announcement, potentially creating a reflexive cycle where lower stock price → more shares needed → more dilution → lower stock price. At $10/share and 250M diluted shares, the stock would trade at roughly 12x normalized earnings — still above distressed utility precedents.

**Base Case ($11-13):** Settlement financing combines securitization ($500-700M) with equity ($500-700M) and some additional debt. Dilution of 35-45% occurs, share count rises to 235-250M. Post-dilution EPS of $0.85-0.95 on FY2027E at 13x = $11-12 fair value. The stock's current premium reflects optionality on a better-than-expected outcome that has a low base rate.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Announcement of the capital raise structure for settlement installments 2-4. This is likely in Q2-Q3 2026 based on the first installment due date and management's stated timeline of "working with financial advisors." Any equity component (highly likely) would pressure the stock. Additionally, BDC NAV marks and credit stress in the current environment could tighten HE's financing options further.

**Timing window:** 2-6 months. The first settlement payment is being made imminently (April 2026 per court hearing), meaning the company must demonstrate financing capacity for installments 2-4 within the next several months to maintain credibility.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 14% of float with 8.2 days to cover. This is elevated and creates meaningful squeeze risk on any positive headline (securitization approval at favorable terms, credit upgrade to investment grade, etc.). The stock demonstrated a squeeze-like move from $10 to $17 in January-February 2026 on settlement milestone news. Position sizing must be conservative.

**Borrow and options signal:** IV at 51% is rich. Put/call OI shifted from 0.08 to 0.65 over the past month — directionally bearish but not yet indicating heavy institutional short positioning through options. The high IV makes puts expensive in absolute terms.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Base case target $11-13 from current $15.06 = 14-27% downside. Upside risk to $17-18 on favorable news = 13-19% upside against shorts. Risk/reward ratio: approximately 1.5:1 to 2:1 depending on entry timing and catalyst specifics.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE at current levels.** While the fundamental thesis supports downside, the 14% SI with 8.2 days to cover creates acute squeeze risk, IV at 51% makes puts expensive, and the stock has demonstrated rapid short-squeeze behavior (Jan 2026 rally). The correct expression is to avoid the long side rather than actively short it. If SI declines below 10% and the stock re-tests $16-17 ahead of the financing announcement, a small short position (1% portfolio) with a $18.50 stop would be more favorable.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** HE would become interesting if: (1) the stock falls below $11 on dilution announcement, creating a post-dilution entry at <13x normalized earnings with the financing risk resolved; (2) securitization is approved on terms that limit equity dilution to <20% of current shares; (3) credit ratings reach investment grade (BB+/Ba1 or above), unlocking cheaper financing for the capex program.

**Timeline:** Headwinds persist through at least Q3 2026, with the financing plan likely announced within 2-6 months and dilution executed within 6-12 months. Dividend reinstatement is unlikely before 2028. The stock may offer value once the financing plan is public and the dilution is quantified — buying into known dilution at a discount is a different proposition than buying ahead of unknown dilution at a premium to analyst targets.

## Conclusion

Hawaiian Electric's operational utility business is functional and structurally sound, supported by monopoly position and regulatory mechanisms. However, the stock at $15.06 prices in settlement resolution without accounting for the dilutive cost of achieving it. The company explicitly acknowledges bankruptcy as a possibility if financing fails, no insider has purchased shares on the open market, the stock trades above every analyst price target, and 30-55% additional dilution is virtually certain. The options market confirms growing concern with a 7.0pp IV increase and dramatic shift in put positioning.

At $15.06 — above the highest analyst target of $14.00 — the stock does not compensate holders for bearing the financing risk, dilution certainty, and execution uncertainty. The risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside: the good outcome is largely priced, while the bad outcomes (larger-than-expected dilution, securitization delays, subrogation complications) retain full downside potential.
