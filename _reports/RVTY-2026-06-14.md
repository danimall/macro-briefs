**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Revvity is the post-2023 reincarnation of PerkinElmer, a ~$2.86B-revenue life-science tools and diagnostics company split roughly evenly between Life Sciences (reagents, instruments, the Signals/ChemDraw software suite, BioLegend antibodies) and Diagnostics (newborn/reproductive-health screening, EUROIMMUN autoimmune testing, China diagnostics). The investment question is whether the stock, at ~$99.66 against a $81.22–$118.30 52-week range, offers asymmetry in either direction. My conclusion is that it does not. This is a fairly-valued, modestly-growing quality compounder where the bearish and bullish vectors roughly cancel.

The prior trajectory on this name has been steadily lower (overall 5.8 → 5.1 → 4.8 across three reports, all HOLD). I am holding the recommendation but pushing back on a further score cut, because the case for incremental deterioration does not survive scrutiny. Three signals offset the genuine headwinds. First, the company has beaten consensus four quarters running (Q4 FY25 +7.4%, Q1 FY26 +2.3%), which is hard to reconcile with a thesis built on falling estimates; the company keeps exceeding the very numbers being revised down, and much of the revision reflects the China Immunodiagnostics divestiture removing revenue rather than demand erosion. Second, the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs means the ~$20M FY25 gross-margin headwind structurally abates going forward, which undercuts the "margins compressing" pillar. Third, the stock has already de-rated to a $81 trough and re-rated ~21% off it on the Q1 beat plus divestiture; much of the bad news is in the price.

What keeps it out of BUY territory is equally concrete: organic growth is genuinely low-single-digit (the company-selected proxy metric, non-COVID organic revenue growth, fell to 1.9% in FY25 from 9.1% in FY23); the Life Sciences Solutions reporting unit carries $4.5B of goodwill with a fair-value cushion of only 10–20%, a realistic non-cash impairment risk within twelve months; and there is no near-term catalyst to force a re-rating. The honest classification is uncertain HOLD at roughly fair value.

## Company Overview

Revvity operates two roughly equal segments. **Life Sciences** (~$1.43B FY25, 50% of revenue) sells reagents, instruments, automation, and software for drug discovery and development. Its franchises include BioLegend (35,000+ antibody/reagent SKUs, its largest acquisition), the Signals software suite (ChemDraw, electronic lab notebook, image analysis), high-content imaging (Opera Phenix), and the Pin-point CRISPR base-editing platform. Software is the segment's growth engine, adding ~$35.6M in FY25 while reagents/instruments were roughly flat. **Diagnostics** (~$1.42B, 50%) centers on reproductive health (newborn/prenatal screening — DELFIA, NeoBase, Vanadis NIPT, EONIS, Revvity Omics), immunodiagnostics (EUROIMMUN autoimmune/allergy platforms, Oxford Immunotec T-SPOT.TB), and emerging-market diagnostics including China infectious-disease screening.

The company employs ~11,000 people, sells in 160+ countries, and generates roughly 60% of revenue and 80% of headcount outside the U.S. It holds leading niche positions (global newborn screening, EUROIMMUN autoimmune testing) but competes against larger, better-capitalized peers (Thermo Fisher, Danaher, Agilent, Bio-Rad, and Roche in diagnostics) on specialization and workflow integration rather than scale.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth is modest and partly mechanical.** FY25 revenue grew 4% reported / 3% organic to $2,856M. Q1 FY26 revenue rose 7% to $711.1M, but that includes ~3pts of FX tailwind, ~1pt from the ACD/Labs acquisition, and an extra fiscal week (FY26 is a 53-week year). Underlying organic demand is low-single-digit, with Reproductive Health (+$20.6M in Q1) and Software the bright spots while Life Sciences instruments/consumables stay sluggish amid cautious pharma/biotech/academic spending.

**Margins compressed, but the driver is partly reversing.** Consolidated gross margin fell 104bps in FY25 (to 54.8%) and a further 200bps YoY in Q1 FY26 (to 54.5%), driven by tariffs, unfavorable FX, and China diagnostic-policy mix. The tariff component (~$25M to COGS / ~$20M gross-margin hit in FY25, mostly on Europe-manufactured product sold into the U.S.) is now structurally set to abate after the IEEPA ruling. Operating margin held far better (down only ~10bps in FY25) thanks to cost containment and a 5%-of-workforce restructuring.

**GAAP earnings are noisy and largely optical.** Net income fell from $693M (FY23, inflated by a divestiture gain and low tax) to $270M (FY24) to $241M (FY25). The decline reflects ~$405M/yr of D&A on $9B of goodwill+intangibles, restructuring, and rising net interest/other expense ($88.4M in FY25 vs $30.6M in FY24 as cash and interest income fell). ROA (2.0%) and ROE (3.3%) look weak precisely because the balance sheet is goodwill-heavy; an analyst relying on a screen would misread this as operating distress.

**Cash flow quality is the real story.** FY25 operating cash flow was $589M; FCF $515M (18% margin) on light ~$74M capex. Q1 FY26 OCF was $126M. Adjusted EPS guidance — $4.90–5.00 for FY25 and $5.20–5.30 pro-forma (ex-China) for FY26 — tells a materially different story than the ~$2.10 GAAP figure. The four consecutive beats reinforce confidence in the adjusted earnings power.

**Balance sheet is sound.** $860M cash (Q1 FY26), ~$3.2B fixed-rate debt including the €500M 2026 Notes maturing July 2026 (to be repaid from cash/revolver), a $1.5B undrawn revolver, and investment-grade ratings. Current ratio 1.68, D/E 0.68. The Altman Z-Score of 2.14 (grey zone) is an artifact of the acquisition-built, goodwill-heavy structure (asset turnover 0.23), not liquidity distress.

**Capital allocation is turning cautious.** FY25 buybacks were aggressive at $820.8M (cutting share count from ~120.6M to ~111.6M), but the Q1 FY26 pace slowed sharply to $79M (from ~$155M in Q1 FY25) with $800.5M still authorized, coinciding with the China divestiture, ACD/Labs purchase, and the July note maturity. This reads as balance-sheet preservation rather than a bearish signal on intrinsic value.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is the central limitation, and the FY23-to-FY25 deceleration is genuine, though one shared across the life-science tools sector amid post-COVID normalization, soft pharma/biotech instrument capex, China headwinds, and NIH/government funding uncertainty.

The growth case rests on mix shift. The deliberate strategy is to grow recurring, higher-margin revenue (Signals/ChemDraw software, ACD/Labs, reproductive-health screening) while pruning policy-exposed hardware and the dilutive China Immunodiagnostics business. The pro-forma EPS guide reset higher even as revenue came down (ex-China), confirming the divested unit was margin-dilutive. Reproductive health is recurring and less cyclical, providing a stable base; software is the durable growth engine.

The four-beat streak (averaging ~3% upside) suggests management is calibrating guidance conservatively and out-executing the consensus whose downward revisions form the bear narrative. I weight the negative estimate revisions (-2.9% on current-year, -3.1% on next-year) as meaningful but partly mechanical (divestiture-driven) and contradicted by realized beats. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a turn; it is a low-growth, steadily-executing profile.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP P/E (~47.7x) the stock looks expensive, but that multiple is meaningless given ~$405M of goodwill amortization crushing GAAP net income. The relevant metric is forward adjusted EPS of ~$5.25 (consensus current-year) / $5.82 (next-year), placing RVTY at ~19x forward / ~17x one-year-out, broadly in line with quality life-science tools peers and not stretched for an 18%-FCF-margin business.

Peer comps are heterogeneous and of limited use: the Health Care Equipment median P/E of 73.4x is distorted by Penumbra (73.4x) and Masimo (126.7x), while Baxter is loss-making and GMED sits at 18.8x. RVTY's forward multiple is more comparable to its true peer set (Thermo, Danaher, Agilent, Bio-Rad), which trade in the high-teens-to-low-20s on forward earnings. On that basis RVTY is fairly valued, not cheap.

Analyst targets argue for modest upside, not danger: mean $113.64 (~+14%), median $110 (~+10%), high $145, low $90 (only ~10% below spot). Sentiment is improving (strongBuy 1→3 over the trailing three months, zero sells among 16 analysts). I do not dismiss this — it caps the downside case — but the targets cluster around models assuming ~5% revenue growth and margin recovery, which is execution-dependent and not yet evident in organic trends. A base case of $95–105 (roughly current price on flat-to-modestly-rising forward earnings) is the most defensible read. The valuation does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify a BUY, nor the overvaluation that would justify an AVOID.

## Competitive Landscape

Revvity competes on specialization rather than scale, and management itself expects industry consolidation to increase the proportion of large competitors. Its defensible positions — global newborn/reproductive-health screening (recurring, less cyclical), the EUROIMMUN autoimmune franchise, and a growing high-margin software/informatics layer — are genuine moats in their niches. The mix-shift toward software and recurring diagnostics is the right strategic response to scale disadvantage.

Trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than deteriorating. The ACD/Labs tuck-in extends the software layer; exiting China Immunodiagnostics removes a margin drag. Against this, instrument/consumable share in a soft capital-spending environment is not gaining, and the company is a price-taker against Thermo and Danaher in overlapping categories. Net, a stable competitive position with a deliberate, credible upgrade path.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment is the single most company-specific risk.** Goodwill ($6.6B) plus intangibles ($2.3B) equals ~73% of total assets. At the November 3, 2025 test, the Life Sciences Solutions reporting unit had fair value exceeding carrying value by only the disclosed margin — the thinnest cushion of six units. Continued softness in life-science instruments/reagents or a higher discount rate could trigger a material non-cash impairment within twelve months. It is non-cash and partly reflected in an already-beaten GAAP optic, but it is a real asymmetric overhang.

**China diagnostics and divestiture execution.** VBP and DRG reimbursement changes have pressured Diagnostics mix; the 10-Q added a new risk factor on the China Immunodiagnostics divestiture (execution risk, costs, disruption).

**Tariffs (now a tailwind).** The IEEPA ruling means the FY25 headwind structurally abates and could produce refunds (no receivable recorded yet). This is a non-modeled forward positive, not a risk.

**End-market funding sensitivity.** Newly emphasized language on U.S. funding/regulatory changes (NIH uncertainty) creating cautious pharma/biotech/academic/government spending.

**Refinancing.** €500M 2026 Notes mature July 2026, addressable with cash/revolver; loss of investment-grade rating would raise costs — low probability given the balance sheet.

### Options Market Signal

The options market does not corroborate a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV (38.4%) sits essentially at one-year historical vol (38.7%), so the market is not pricing elevated event risk, and IV is in the 8th percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew is -21.3% (call-heavy) on the front expiry, the opposite of the put-protection demand expected ahead of a feared downside catalyst. The term structure is in mild contango (38.4% near vs 40.9% at three months), a normal/complacent shape. IV ticked down from 41.8% to 38.4% over the prior two weeks. The 11.8% short interest (5.3 days to cover) reflects institutional skepticism, but the options tape leans constructive, a divergence that cuts against pressing a short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($118–130):** Margin normalization arrives as the tariff headwind reverses post-IEEPA, the China divestiture proves accretive, software/recurring mix continues to lift gross margin, and organic growth re-accelerates toward ~5%. Forward adjusted EPS reaches the upper $5.80s+, supporting ~20x and a return to prior highs. Continued beats (a five-quarter streak) plus improving sentiment drive a re-rating. The tariff-refund recovery provides non-modeled upside.

**Bear case ($82–88, ~-12% to -18%):** The Life Sciences Solutions goodwill cushion fails the next test, triggering a material non-cash impairment that pressures the stock optically. Organic growth stays stuck near 1–2% as pharma/academic funding tightens; China divestiture execution disappoints; the buyback slowdown signals management sees limited near-term value. Estimates keep drifting and the stock retests the April $81–82 trough.

**Base case ($95–105, roughly flat):** Low-single-digit organic growth, gradual margin recovery from tariff abatement offset by mix, ~$5.25–5.50 forward adjusted EPS at ~18–19x. The stock trades sideways around current levels as the market waits for organic growth or impairment resolution. This is the most probable outcome, and it is why the recommendation is HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 FY26 earnings (early August 2026), with the November 2026 goodwill impairment test as the key dated risk event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth re-accelerating above ~5% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the mix-shift is driving real demand rather than FX/acquisition optics.
- Gross margin recovering 150bps+ YoY as the tariff headwind abates and software mix lifts, with forward adjusted EPS revisions turning positive (+5%+ over 90 days).
- A clean pass of the next Life Sciences Solutions impairment test (cushion widening above ~20%), removing the largest asymmetric overhang, ideally with the stock below ~$92 (≤16x forward).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material Life Sciences Solutions goodwill impairment confirming structural overpayment, combined with organic growth falling below 1%.
- China divestiture execution problems or a sharp step-down in pharma/academic funding driving forward adjusted EPS estimates down >10%.
- Loss of investment-grade rating or a buyback suspension signaling balance-sheet stress.

## Conclusion

Revvity is a quality, fairly-valued life-science tools and diagnostics business caught between genuine headwinds (low-single-digit organic growth, thin Life Sciences Solutions goodwill cushion, China policy pressure) and genuine offsets (four consecutive beats, structurally abating tariff drag post-IEEPA, accretive China divestiture, 18% FCF margins, investment-grade balance sheet, improving analyst sentiment with zero sells). The stock has already de-rated to ~$81 and re-rated ~21% off the trough; the most likely path from ~$100 is sideways within a $95–105 band.

The recommendation is HOLD. An AVOID here would require pressing a downside case the market has largely absorbed and the company keeps contradicting with beats; a BUY would require organic acceleration or a positive impairment-test resolution and a lower entry. Neither condition is met. I hold the recommendation but decline to cut the score further; the prior declining trajectory overstated the deterioration, and the current evidence is balanced. The actionable approach for existing holders is to retain for the FCF and the optionality on margin normalization; new capital should wait for either organic re-acceleration confirmation or a pullback toward the low-$90s.
