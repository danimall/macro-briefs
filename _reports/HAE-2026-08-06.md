## Executive Summary

Haemonetics finished fiscal 2026 (year ended March 28, 2026) with revenue down 2.0% to $1,334.0M and GAAP diluted EPS down 38% to $2.05. Both figures misdescribe the business. The revenue decline is entirely the result of two deliberate exits — the CSL Plasma transition and the January 2025 Whole Blood divestiture — worth roughly $153M of non-recurring FY2025 revenue. The earnings decline reconciles precisely: an $86.5M impairment charge ($84.2M incremental year over year) plus $21.1M less benefit from marking down acquisition earnouts, against $38.6M of genuine gross profit expansion. Underlying gross margin rose 400 basis points to 59.0%, the largest single-year move in four years, and adjusted EPS built from the four reported quarters was $4.97 ($1.10 + $1.27 + $1.31 + $1.29) against a 4-for-4 beat record.

The difficulty is not the business, it is the entry price and the direction of estimates. The shares have advanced from a $56.29 Form 4 reference price on May 15, 2026 to $85.38, roughly 52%, and neither this review nor the underlying filing work can attribute the move to a disclosed event. The May 7 earnings beat pre-dates the $56.29 reference price. Over the same window, consensus has moved the other way: current-quarter EPS cut 7.5% over 90 days to $1.08 (below the $1.10 delivered a year earlier), next-year EPS cut 4.8% to $5.63, and next-year cut a further 2.3% in the last 30 days alone. A rising multiple against falling estimates is the configuration that most often precedes disappointment.

At $85.38 the stock trades at 16.4x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS ($5.22) and 15.2x FY2028 ($5.63), a discount to broad medtech but a discount that is earned: negative tangible book equity of approximately $307.7M, net debt/adjusted EBITDA near 2.5x, an Interventional Technologies reporting unit the company itself says has roughly 6% goodwill headroom while its revenue declines 8.2%, two instituted PTO petitions against the patents that fence its highest-margin segment, and a five-year pay-versus-performance record showing total shareholder return below both the $100 index base and the selected peer group in every one of five disclosed years. Base case $90 (+5.4%), bull $110 (+28.8%), bear $62 (-27.4%). That asymmetry does not support new capital. HOLD, classified uncertain, with the Q1 FY2027 print on August 6 as the immediate resolution point.

## Company Overview

Haemonetics operates three reported segments built around blood and plasma handling, with a multi-year pivot from legacy collection toward hospital interventional devices now substantially complete.

**Plasma — $524.5M, 39.3% of FY2026 revenue.** Apheresis devices and disposables sold to commercial plasma collectors, including NexSys PCS with the YES yield-enhancement and Persona software. Highest margin in the portfolio: 36.2% segment operating margin in Q3 FY2026. Also the most concentrated: one Plasma customer is approximately 13% of total company revenue, the top ten approximately 44%. Plasma returned to growth in Q3 FY2026 (+3.5% reported, +2.8% constant currency) with zero CSL North America disposable sales, which is the single cleanest positive data point in the filings.

**Blood Center — $221.3M, 16.6%.** Apheresis equipment and disposables for blood banks ($220.9M, +3.6%) plus the eliminated Whole Blood line ($0.4M against $48.0M). 23.0% segment margin.

**Hospital — $588.3M, 44.1%,** now the largest segment and internally divergent. Interventional Technologies ($234.0M, **–8.2% reported, –8.7% cc**) covers VASCADE/VASCADE MVP vascular closure, SavvyWire and OptoWire from the OpSens acquisition, and ensoETM from Attune Medical. Blood Management Technologies ($354.3M, **+14.6% reported, +13.5% cc**) covers TEG hemostasis analyzers, Cell Saver autotransfusion and BloodTrack/SafeTrace software. Segment margin 18.1%, roughly half of Plasma's, which creates the central mix tension: the growing segment dilutes consolidated margin, and inside it the higher-priced, more differentiated half is the half contracting.

Geography is US-weighted: $982.2M US (73.6%, –2.8%) and $351.9M international (26.4%, +0.6% reported, –2.6% cc). Roughly 3% of revenue is operating-lease income on placed equipment, an installed-base model that creates switching costs and consumes capital in ways the capex line does not capture.

### Cash flow: real improvement, smaller than headline

Operating cash flow rose $111.5M (+61%) to $293.2M while net income fell $70.4M. The reconciliation:

- Working capital was a $23.7M outflow in FY2026 against $102.0M in FY2025. Cash generation before working capital: $316.9M versus $283.7M, **+11.7%** — genuine but modest.
- The residual roughly $78M of the OCF jump is the reversal of an abnormal prior-year inventory build. Inventories fell from $365.1M to $306.4M while turns improved only 1.4x to 1.5x.
- The impairment is non-cash, added back, and neither helps nor hurts cash. It should not be read as cash-flow strength.
- Reported capex was $32.8M (2.5% of revenue), but the company also transferred $51.9M from inventory into PP&E for placed customer equipment (against $21.1M in FY2025). That cash was spent through operating activities, so FCF is not overstated — but recurring capital intensity is closer to $84.7M, or 6.3% of revenue. A DCF built on a 2.5% capex assumption will overstate free cash generation materially.

Normalizing for both effects, sustainable FCF is roughly $200–235M rather than $260.4M: about 5.2–6.1% on market cap and 4.1–4.8% on enterprise value.

## Competitive Landscape

**Plasma.** The competitive set is visible through litigation: Haemonetics is suing Terumo BCT (D. Colo. 1:25-cv-01409) and Fresenius Kabi USA/Fenwal (N.D. Ill. 1:25-cv-08680) over donor-centric collection patents embodied in NexSys PCS with YES and Persona. Both defendants are units of large diversified groups. Terumo has filed a §101 invalidity motion plus IPR and PGR petitions, **two of which have been instituted**, and has moved to stay the district court. Fenwal counterclaimed in December 2025 alleging invalidity and non-infringement and asserting that Haemonetics infringes Fenwal patents. The payoff is asymmetric against Haemonetics: winning preserves a moat it already enjoys, while a §101 invalidation of method-style claims — a recognized vulnerability for that patent type — would open a 36%-margin segment to two well-capitalized rivals. Institution rates are historically high and many instituted petitions fail, and the 10-Q discloses no accrual. That management elected to litigate proactively is itself evidence it views the moat as contestable. The longer-run demand question is the FcRn-inhibitor drug class, named in the FY2026 risk factors, which treats overlapping immunoglobulin indications without plasma and caps the terminal growth rate assumable for the highest-margin segment.

**Interventional.** Pulsed field ablation moved against the company faster than it underwrote. The 10-K's stated cause for the $77.2M Attune write-down is that the esophageal-cooling opportunity in AF ablation "has substantially diminished over the last two years due to the launch of PFA in the US and Japan" — a window that overlaps almost exactly with the ownership period. Attune closed April 1, 2024 at a net $176.2M plus $9.8M of costs; residual asset-group fair value is $12.3M. PFA's adoption speed surprised the whole electrophysiology industry, so this reads as a wrong forecast rather than a careless one, but the outcome is roughly $186M of capital destroyed in 24 months.

Large-bore vascular closure is a better market, tied to TAVR, EVAR, mechanical circulatory support and PFA-driven electrophysiology volumes. VASCADE MVP/XL competes with Abbott's Perclose family and Teleflex's MANTA. The March 2026 FDA approval of expanded VASCADE MVP XL labeling and an April 2026 supporting study show the company pushing up-market into the same territory PerQseal Elite targets. If the PMA clears, Haemonetics would hold both extravascular and intravascular large-bore approaches — an unusual position. The company's own caution is that these are physician-preference, higher-priced devices requiring hospital value-analysis-committee approval, which slows adoption and intensifies price contest.

**Blood Management Technologies (+14.6%)** is the least-discussed part of the story and grew faster than Interventional declined, carrying the Hospital segment to +4.3%. Its razor/razorblade and software-attach economics are arguably more durable than the interventional franchise.

**Competitive position: 4.8.** Trajectory is the criterion, and the franchise on which the equity story was built is contracting 8.2% while the moat in the most profitable segment is under active PTO challenge. BMT's share gains and the PerQseal option prevent a lower score.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Interventional Technologies goodwill, ~6% headroom, disclosed by the company.** Tested on the first day of Q4 FY2026 at a fair value of $867.8M against a carrying value of $821.6M using a 10.5% discount rate. The 10-K states that "a 50-basis point increase in the discount rate or a reduction in projected revenue growth rates of 1% could eliminate headroom" and that the unit "remains at risk of future goodwill impairment." Unit revenue was $234.0M, –8.2% for the year and –11.4% in Q3 — a carrying value of 3.5x revenue on a declining business. This is the highest-probability negative catalyst in the file. It is non-cash and would not touch FCF or covenant compliance if it falls inside permitted add-backs, but it would deepen an already negative tangible book position and would be read as confirmation that the interventional strategy has not worked.

**2. Plasma customer concentration.** One customer at approximately 13% of total revenue, top ten at 44%. The CSL transition already demonstrated the cost of a single account moving.

**3. Patent litigation with two instituted PTO petitions**, as described above.

**4. Single-site, single-service-provider manufacturing for the flagship growth product.** VASCADE is made in a leased facility in Guaymas, Mexico under a shelter-plan service agreement with Offshore International (d/b/a Tetakawi), which supplies security, maintenance, HR, recruiting, government compliance, workforce transportation and cross-border shipping. All apheresis equipment is sourced from Asia, and a single third-party vendor provides customer service, enterprise technology, procurement, accounting and HR. Shelter plans are a standard Mexican structure and no disruption has been reported, but labor, compliance and logistics for the growth franchise run through one counterparty at one site with USMCA and tariff exposure layered on; the 10-K already attributes part of the SG&A increase to tariffs.

**5. Live ERP replacement.** Digital transformation costs were $16.5M in the first nine months of FY2026, are excluded from adjusted results, and are cited as the driver of the FY2026 working-capital outflow. ERP cutovers at device companies routinely disrupt order-to-cash and inventory, and this runs concurrently with a restructuring program whose savings are not yet quantified.

**6. Unresolved disclosure gaps.** Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (May 1 and July 28, 2026) could not be retrieved — whether they reflect an executive departure, an appointment or a routine board election is unknown. FY2027 guidance could not be obtained. PerQseal Elite PMA status remains "submitted and under review"; approval must not be modeled as fact.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (69 contracts across four expirations), so read it with limited confidence, but the structure is coherent and points one way. Near-term ATM IV is 73.4% against 54.0% one-year historical volatility — rich by 19.4 points, in the 80th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in **backwardation**: 73.4% at one month against 53.2% at three and 47.4% at six, which prices a discrete imminent event. That event is the Q1 FY2027 report scheduled for August 6, 2026. The August 21 tenor implies a **±12.2% move** with a put/call skew of 15.7%, described by the data as elevated put demand, while the three- and six-month skews are flat to slightly negative (–1.6%, –2.2%). ATM IV rose from 59.9% to 73.4% between July 19 and August 5 while the price rose 7.4% — rising implied volatility alongside a rising price into a catalyst. Short interest is 8.1% of float at 3.4 days to cover, uncrowded enough that a squeeze is not the driver of the rally.

The reading: near-dated protection is being bought into an event, with no comparable demand for longer-dated protection. That is consistent with a market that views the print as a two-sided binary rather than a structural derating. For a stock 3.6% below its 52-week high with falling estimates, that is a reason to hold rather than add.

**Risk factors: 4.0.** Multiple specific, near-dated, asymmetric catalysts — an earnings print within a day priced at ±12.2%, a disclosed 6%-headroom impairment test, instituted PTO petitions, and 13% customer concentration — against a price near its high.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $110 (+28.8%)

The two large self-inflicted portfolio exits are complete, and FY2027 is the first clean comparison year. Gross margin at 59.0% is 400bp above FY2025 and structurally higher because low-margin Whole Blood and CSL volume is permanently gone. Plasma has already proved it can grow without CSL North America disposables (+3.5% in Q3 FY2026), Blood Management Technologies compounds at mid-teens, and Blood Center apheresis grows low-single-digit. If PerQseal Elite's arterial PMA clears, Haemonetics owns both extravascular and intravascular large-bore closure at a moment when TAVR, EVAR and PFA-driven electrophysiology volumes are all expanding, which would reverse the Interventional decline and simultaneously remove the goodwill overhang. Management has retired roughly 10% of the company in two years at a blended $58.14 with $325M of authorization remaining through April 2028. On 18x an FY2028 EPS approaching $6.00, the stock is $108–110. Healthcare M&A demand adds a takeout channel for a $3.9B asset with a 59% gross margin. [Note: the specific driver — large-cap patent cliffs — is not in the brief and is presented here only as a general possibility.]

### Bear Case — $62 (–27.4%)

Interventional Technologies keeps shrinking, a further goodwill impairment lands against the $821.6M carrying value, and the market concludes that roughly $594M of acquisition spend since FY2021 has produced $86.5M of write-offs, one asset in a unit the company says is at risk, and one unproven bet. FY2027 EPS growth of 5% on consensus is already thin, first-half consensus is flat year over year, and roughly $18M of incremental pre-tax interest from replacing zero-coupon notes with floating revolver borrowings consumes about $0.31 per share. Estimates continue the current trajectory. A §101 invalidation opens the 36%-margin Plasma franchise to Terumo and Fresenius. At 12x $5.00 the stock is $60–62, which is where the low analyst target sits. The 52% advance since mid-May, unattributable to any disclosed event, unwinds when a catalyst fails to materialize.

### Base Case — $90 (+5.4%)

Organic revenue grows mid-single-digit, adjusted EPS lands near the $5.22–5.60 consensus band, the goodwill test survives on the strength of the VASCADE MVP XL label expansion without a decisive Interventional inflection, and the stock holds a 16–17x multiple. Total return over twelve months is single-digit, below what the bear-case downside justifies risking.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain). Existing holders retain; no new capital at $85.38.**

The trajectory of prior reviews — 5.8 in March, 5.2 in May, 4.9 in June, 5.0 in July — now moves to 4.78. That decline is not a response to price weakness. Risk/reward has compressed from both directions. The May AVOID at roughly $56–60 was wrong, and that argues for humility about calling downside from here; what has changed is that the valuation cushion which existed then has been spent.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. PerQseal Elite arterial PMA approval confirmed by 8-K or company release, combined with Interventional Technologies revenue returning to positive year-over-year growth in a reported quarter. 3. FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance below $5.00, or Interventional Technologies declining more than 8% year over year for a second consecutive fiscal year.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate. Q1 FY2027 results were scheduled for **August 6, 2026**, one day past this information cutoff, with the options market pricing ±12.2%. ## Conclusion

The fundamental work supports the view that FY2026's reported collapse was an accounting event and that the underlying business improved: the operating income walk reconciles to the dollar, gross margin expanded 400 basis points, adjusted EPS was $4.97 against four consecutive beats, and Plasma has demonstrated growth without its largest lost account. Those are the facts that argue against selling.

The facts that argue against buying are the price and the direction of estimates.
