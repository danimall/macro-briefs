## Executive Summary

Agnico Eagle is the best-run balance sheet in senior gold mining and, on the operating evidence available, one of the two or three best asset portfolios. Q2 2026 delivered payable production of 855,816 oz (ahead of the company's own 2 July pre-announcement of ~845,000 oz), total cash costs of $1,054/oz, all-in sustaining costs of $1,459/oz against a realized gold price of $4,483/oz, operating cash flow of $2,144M and free cash flow of $1,335M. The company ended the quarter with $3,464M of cash against $197M of company-defined total debt, a net cash position of $3,267M, and a Fitch upgrade into the A rating category. It funded roughly US$677M of Finnish acquisitions and returned a record $625M to shareholders in the same quarter while cash still rose $352M.

The investment question is almost entirely about price paid and gold price assumed, and on both counts the current setup is unattractive rather than compelling. Consensus FY2026 EPS has been cut 11.3% over 90 days to $12.14 and FY2027 cut 11.8% to $12.50; the current-quarter estimate has fallen 19.5% in thirty days alone. The mechanism is transparent: gold retreated from its spike high, and with ~98.6% of FY2025 revenue from gold and a 40% net margin, AEM's earnings are geared to spot with roughly 3:1 leverage. Layered on top is a genuine company-specific negative — the 1 July rock mass movement in the north wall of the Barnat open pit at Canadian Malartic, which the company has quantified at 60,000-80,000 oz of lost H2 2026 production and "up to approximately 150,000 ounces of gold per year" in both 2027 and 2028. At Q2 unit economics that is roughly $450M of annual AISC margin exposure, and the figure sits in the 2 July release rather than the more widely read 29 July results release.

The stock is 30.1% below its 2 March high of $255.24, but it has also rallied 31.2% from its 20 July low of $136.34 in fourteen sessions, including a 6.5% move on 7 August. That drawdown is explained by identifiable fundamental deterioration (gold retracement, an 11-12% cut to forward estimates, a multi-year production reduction at the flagship complex), not by a mispricing we can name. Our base case is $195, bull $250, bear $125 — a probability-weighted expected return of roughly +5% including the 1.0% dividend, with wide dispersion driven by a macro variable we do not forecast. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Agnico Eagle describes itself as Canada's largest mining company and the world's second-largest gold producer, with mines in Canada, Australia, Finland and Mexico, and has declared a cash dividend every year since 1983. Four Canadian cornerstone assets — Detour Lake, Canadian Malartic, Meadowbank and Meliadine — each produce roughly 350,000-700,000 oz/yr and together accounted for approximately 60% of FY2025 sales of 3.4 Moz. Kittilä (Finland), Fosterville (Australia) and the Mexican assets round out the portfolio.

Commodity mix matters more than the geographic story: FY2025 revenue of $11,908M comprised gold $11,742M, silver $105.3M, copper $52.0M and zinc $8.7M. Gold was 98.6% of revenue. The diversification in AEM's portfolio is jurisdictional and asset-count diversification; the equity functions as a low-cost, net-cash, operationally levered proxy on the gold price.

The growth architecture is organic and district-based rather than acquisitive at the producing-asset level. Odyssey underground at Canadian Malartic completed the first phase of shaft sinking at 1,586 m in July 2026, with first production through Shaft #1 targeted for Q2 2027 and a stated path to 1 Moz/yr from the Malartic complex in the early 2030s. Hope Bay in Nunavut was sanctioned on 19 May 2026: 6,000 tpd underground, 4.5 Moz over 11 years at 6.50 g/t, ~435 koz/yr at steady state, first production as early as 2030, $2.4B initial capital plus ~$1.1B sustaining. Three transactions announced 20 April 2026 (Rupert Resources, Aurion Resources, and the purchase of B2Gold's 70% Fingold stake) consolidated ~2,492 km² of the Central Lapland Greenstone Belt around the operating Kittilä mill, with a stated aspiration of ~500 koz/yr from Finland. Management frames the aggregate as 20-30% production growth over the next decade.

**A data-integrity note that materially affects screening.** AEM reports to the SEC under the Multijurisdictional Disclosure System: annual reports on Form 40-F (FY2025 filed 19 March 2026), interim disclosure on Form 6-K. No 10-K, no DEF 14A, and no Section 16 Form 4 filings exist. SEC XBRL company facts for the issuer terminate at FY2013 (net loss $406.5M, total assets $4.96B). Any automated screen sourcing AEM from those endpoints returns twelve-year-old data describing a loss-making $5B-asset company. It is a filing-regime artifact. The practical consequence for this report is that the FY2021-FY2025 statements below are third-party aggregated rather than primary-source verified, and that governance and insider-transaction evidence is genuinely unavailable rather than absent.

Gross margin expanding from 54.9% to 74.5% on roughly flat ounces (FY2025 sales 3.4 Moz; 2026 guidance 3.3-3.5 Moz) is the arithmetic of a fixed-cost-heavy business absorbing a price shock. The same leverage runs in reverse. Q2 realized $4,483/oz against $1,459/oz AISC is $3,024/oz of AISC margin.

**Balance sheet.** Company-reported at 30 June 2026: cash $3,464M, total debt outstanding $197M, net cash $3,267M. The aggregator shows cash and investments $3,478M, total debt $320M, net cash $3,158M — almost certainly a definitional difference (lease liabilities and short-term investments), not a factual conflict about leverage. Either way, net cash of roughly $3.2-3.3B against a $90B capitalization, and a Fitch upgrade from BBB+ into the A category in April 2026 (the exact notch, A- versus A, is reported inconsistently across the issuer release and secondary coverage). Net debt went from $(1,506)M in FY2021 to net cash of $2,553M at FY2025. AEM retired balance-sheet risk during the upcycle rather than levering into it. Covenant and refinancing risk is immaterial by inspection, though we have not read a credit agreement.

**Two cash-flow observations that temper the headline quality.** First, cash conversion has loosened: OCF/net income was 1.53x in FY2025 and 1.26x on a TTM basis. TTM revenue is +22.0% and net income +31.7% versus FY2025, but TTM operating cash flow is only +8.8%. The most plausible drivers are cash taxes on a much larger taxable base plus working-capital absorption, since receivables and gold-in-process inventory carry far more dollars per tonne at $4,400 gold. That reading is inference; the aggregator's TTM stitching could not be reconciled against reported prior-year quarters, so we treat this as a flag for verification against the FY2025 MD&A rather than as established earnings-quality deterioration.

Second, free cash flow growth has stalled by design. TTM FCF of $4,564M is only 3.8% above FY2025 because capex rose to $2,854M from $2,418M. The 29 July guidance raised 2026 capex to $2.6-2.8B from $2.2-2.4B with capitalised exploration unchanged at $290-330M, implying roughly $3.0B of 2026 capital spending, ~24% above FY2025. Quarterly cash flow here should not be annualised: Q1 FCF of ~$732M nearly doubled to $1,335M in Q2. H1 actual FCF of ~$2,067M annualises to ~$4.1B before accounting for the H2 Barnat production loss and $428M of Hope Bay development spend in H2 alone.

**Earnings versus expectations requires care about basis.** Q2 GAAP net income was $1,600M / $3.19 per share while adjusted was $1,541M / $3.07 per share. GAAP exceeding adjusted means the excluded items were net accretive to reported income by roughly $59M ($0.12/share); we could not identify them without the interim statements. The supplied surprise table shows a $3.30 estimate against a $3.05 actual, a 7.7% miss, while secondary coverage reports $3.05 against a $2.89 Zacks consensus, a beat. Both can be true: the revision data shows the current-quarter estimate falling from $3.53 thirty days ago to $2.84 now, so the $3.30 figure is a pre-cut vintage and $2.89 a post-cut one. The honest summary is that the quarter beat costs and cash-flow expectations, missed revenue by ~1.6% ($3.80B versus $3.86B consensus), and landed against a consensus that was itself moving fast.

One reconciliation point on the supplied profile: EPS is listed as $8.86 (FY2025 diluted) alongside a trailing P/E of 15.31, which implies EPS near $11.59. The P/E reconciles to TTM diluted EPS of $11.68, so the EPS field is stale. We use the TTM basis.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term earnings growth is negative and the volume growth is deferred to the 2030s.

**Estimate revisions are the dominant near-term signal, and they are negative.** Current quarter $2.84 (-19.5% over 30 days, -18.2% over 90), next quarter $2.97 (-19.1%, -16.8%), FY2026 $12.14 (-7.3%, -11.3%), FY2027 $12.50 (-11.4%, -11.8%). FY2027 consensus is only 3% above FY2026. Revision velocity of this magnitude, sustained across all four horizons, is a caution rather than an inflection signal — the direction is the inverse of the pattern that precedes re-rating.

**Volume growth is negative in 2027-2028 before it turns positive.** The Barnat impact of up to ~150 koz/yr in each of 2027 and 2028 is ~4.4% of a 3.4 Moz base. Odyssey first production through Shaft #1 arrives Q2 2027 and ramps toward 1 Moz for the Malartic complex in the early 2030s; Hope Bay's first production is "as early as 2030," ramping 319 koz (2030) to 435 koz (2032-2038). Finland's ~500 koz aspiration depends on Ikkari drilling results not yet published. The 20-30% decade growth target is credible and unusually well-specified for the sector, but it does not support a growth multiple over a 12-month horizon.

**Exploration is the genuine growth engine, and it is working.** Q2 intercepts included East Gouldie 5.1 g/t over 14.3 m, a newly defined Artemis zone at Odyssey at 13.7 g/t over 14.6 m, Detour West Pit 2.5 g/t over 62.3 m, Detour West Extension 13.5 g/t over 2.5 m roughly 1.0 km outside the resource pit outline, and Hope Bay Patch 7 at 18.5 g/t over 11.3 m. Since acquiring Hope Bay in February 2021, AEM has drilled 1,239 holes / 522,634 m and converted a care-and-maintenance asset into a sanctioned 400koz+/yr mine in five years. That is value creation through the drill bit rather than through acquisition premiums, and it is the most durable part of the growth case. Externally reported reserve life is "about 15 years" at end-2025, which we could not verify against the company's February 2026 reserve release — a real gap for NAV work.

At $178.37 with ~503-506M diluted shares, market capitalization is $89.7-90.3B and enterprise value roughly $86.5-87.1B after net cash.

The multiple looks moderate, and that is precisely the trap to interrogate. Forward EPS of $12.14-12.50 embeds a realized gold price in the $4,200-4,500 range. Cycle-adjusted, the picture changes sharply. FY2024 EPS was $3.78; FY2025 EPS was $8.86. Scaling from Q2 unit economics, a sustained $3,300/oz realized price removes roughly $1,180/oz across ~3.3 Moz, or about $3.9B pre-tax and ~$5.35 of EPS, putting normalized earnings near $7.00-7.50. On that basis the stock is 24-25x. There is no reading of the data in which AEM is cheap on anything other than peak-cycle earnings.

Against that, the absolute supports are real: 5.1% trailing FCF yield, net cash equal to ~$6.40/share, a dividend covered many times over, and a capital-return framework targeting ~40% of annual FCF (H1 2026 ran at ~48%, $1.0B returned against ~$2,067M of FCF). Sell-side dispersion is extreme — mean target $214.98, median $213.33, high $300, low $87 — which is the honest signature of a stock whose value is a function of one exogenous price. Note also that CIBC's widely cited $304 target has unspecified currency; if Canadian, it is roughly US$222, close to the consensus mean rather than a standout.

We do not treat the mean target (+20.5%) as a floor. That convention applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples; here estimates are falling 11-12% and the targets are being cut alongside them (CIBC trimmed to 304 from 312; Morningstar reduced fair value explicitly on the lower gold price). The stale-target logic runs the wrong way when revisions are negative.

Valuation score of 5.5 reflects a multiple that is defensible but not mispriced, resting on peak margins, with the identified catalyst (higher gold) being macro and two-sided rather than company-specific.

## Competitive Landscape

AEM's competitive position is stable-to-improving on three verifiable dimensions.

**Cost position.** Q2 total cash costs of $1,054/oz and AISC of $1,459/oz sit at the low end of senior-producer cost curves, and FY2026 AISC guidance of $1,400-1,550/oz was reiterated even as capex was raised — cost discipline held while capital intensity rose. The company characterises Hope Bay's projected $1,199-1,214/oz AISC as "below the Company's current peer-leading cost profile," which is the company's own framing and unbenchmarked here.

**Jurisdiction.** Four Canadian cornerstone assets at ~60% of sales, with the balance in Finland, Australia and Mexico, against peer portfolios that include Mali, DRC, Papua New Guinea and Argentina. The trade is structurally lower expropriation and permitting-reversal risk in exchange for higher labour costs and, at Hope Bay, extreme Arctic logistics. Hope Bay's sanction came with a supportive statement from Canada's Minister of Energy and Natural Resources and a C$25M federal commitment to the Hope Bay Wind Project led by the Inuit-owned Kitikmeot Tugliq Limited Partnership — AEM is the site host and offtaking beneficiary there, not the wind asset's owner. Alignment with a national Arctic-sovereignty agenda plausibly reduces permitting risk relative to a comparable project elsewhere, though it is unquantifiable.

**District consolidation.** The Finnish transactions bought a belt rather than producing ounces: ~2,492 km² of contiguous greenstone around an operating mill, with the Fingold split-ownership structure eliminated by acquiring Aurion's 30% and B2Gold's 70% (B2Gold as vendor, not partner). This mirrors the Abitibi playbook that generated AEM's Quebec/Ontario value. Whether Ikkari earns its ~US$2.12B upfront cost depends on drilling not yet published.

The important qualifier: the margin windfall is sector-wide, not company-specific. Wheaton Precious Metals posted record Q2 earnings and cash flow in the same window. AEM's execution differentiates it on cost and jurisdiction, not on the direction of margins.

## Risk Assessment

**Gold price concentration.** 98.6% of revenue from one metal at 40% net margins. A decline in gold from current levels compresses margins and EPS sharply. This is the known variable and it is priced in the sense that everyone can see it, but the magnitude of the operating leverage makes the downside distribution asymmetric relative to a diversified miner.

**Barnat geotechnical failure — the largest company-specific item.** The 1 July rock mass movement occurred in the north wall of the Barnat pit, in an area the company had "previously identified as having weaker geological structures" and which was under enhanced monitoring with safety exclusion zones. No injuries, no equipment damage, no environmental impact — the safety system performed as designed. The economic mitigation was less complete: H2 2026 production reduced by 60,000-80,000 oz, and reduced production of up to ~150,000 oz per year in both 2027 and 2028, against a pit that was to be exhausted by early 2029. At Q2 unit economics that is roughly $454M of annual AISC margin as a labelled sensitivity (150,000 × $3,024), before mitigation, cost reallocation and tax. The 29 July results release referenced only a "preliminary redesign" and 2026 guidance at the low end without restating the 2027-2028 figures; the number an investor most needs sits in the earlier, less-read 2 July release. We draw no inference about motive — the redesign was explicitly still in progress — but models anchored on the Q2 release are likely under-reflecting the multi-year effect. Odyssey and the 1 Moz Malartic pathway are stated to be unaffected.

**Hope Bay execution.** $2.4B initial capital ($428M H2 2026, $797M 2027, $591M 2028, $520M 2029) plus ~$1.1B sustaining. The economics rest on a Preliminary Economic Assessment that includes inferred resources, and previously declared reserves were reclassified as resources in the May 2026 update; only ~66% of mine-plan contained gold is measured and indicated. Detailed engineering was ~67% complete at 30 June. All power is on-site diesel (a new 37 MW plant), and resupply depends on a six-to-eight-week sealift season with the CEO explicitly citing Northwest Passage barge transport as the mechanism keeping cash costs under $1,000/oz. A curtailed sealift season has no substitute. Disclosed sensitivities: a 10% capex change moves IRR 1.62pp and NPV $149M; NPV5% is $2.7B at $3,600 gold (only ~13% above initial capex) versus $4.3B at $4,500. Headline unit costs exclude Nunavut Tunngavik payments (2% of operating profit 2030-2034 rising to 7% 2035-2040), a transparently disclosed definitional exclusion that nonetheless means "below $1,000/oz" understates full cash economics.

**Seismicity** appears in AEM's own forward-looking assumption list and its risk list — a double-listing at LaRonde, Goldex and Fosterville, with an explicit link to community relations at the Quebec mines.

**Governance evidence gap.** No proxy statement and no Form 4s exist under the MJDS regime, and the Canadian management information circular was not retrieved. We have no evidence on executive compensation, pay-for-performance alignment, board independence, related-party transactions, or insider buying and selling. The empty Form 4 result is a regime artifact and must not be read either as a bullish absence of selling or a bearish absence of buying.

**Capital allocation as governance proxy.** The observable record is disciplined: dividend held flat at $1.60 for four consecutive years through the upcycle before rising to $1.80 annualised, net debt converted to net cash, ~40% FCF return policy, and the largest acquisition (Rupert/Ikkari) paid in stock rather than cash — roughly 2.4% of existing share value, equivalent to ~2.3% dilution of pre-existing ownership. The Rupert contingent value right of "up to C$3 per share" has undisclosed trigger and settlement terms and should be modelled as a contingent scenario, not added to diluted shares. Q2 buybacks of 2,235,947 shares at an average $178.86 sit fractionally above today's price; a Normal Course Issuer Bid executes mechanically against a budget, so it is weak evidence of valuation conviction, and if gold retraces those repurchases will look poorly timed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 55.8% against 46.3% one-year realized is rich by 9.5 points and sits at the 80th percentile within the current chain, with a backwardated term structure (55.8% at one week versus 44.3% at six months) that prices a near-dated event. Positioning, by contrast, is tilted toward upside participation: the data source reads the skew as call-heavy at every tenor (-5.4% at one week through -6.5% at six months), put/call volume ratio is 0.29 and open-interest ratio 0.62. Short interest is negligible at 0.9% of float and 1.5 days to cover. The combination — expensive near-dated vol, call-tilted positioning, no bearish crowd — argues against buying optionality here and is consistent with an equity whose next move is a leveraged function of gold rather than of company news. The six-month implied move of ±19.5% is a reasonable frame for the dispersion in our scenario set.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $250 (+40%)

Gold sustains above $4,800-5,000 and central-bank reserve demand persists. Realized price of ~$4,900 against AISC of ~$1,550 lifts FY2027 EPS toward $14.00-14.50, and a trending gold tape carries the multiple to 16-17x. AEM participates with more torque than peers because it has no debt service to fund, no jurisdictional discount to widen, and a buyback authorization with a $2B internal limit against ~$5B of annualised free cash flow. Barnat ounces prove deferrable rather than lost, Odyssey's Shaft #1 starts on schedule in Q2 2027, and early Ikkari drilling validates the ~US$2.12B Finnish outlay.

### Bear Case — $125 (-30%)

Gold retraces to $3,200-3,400 as real yields rise and the reserve-diversification bid pauses. Realized price of ~$3,300 removes roughly $5.35 of EPS, taking FY2027 earnings toward $7.00-7.50, and the multiple compresses with falling estimates to 15-17x. The Barnat redesign lands at the high end of the 150koz/yr impact and the 2027 guidance update, when it comes, cuts consensus again. Capex of ~$3.0B in 2026 and ~$800M of Hope Bay spend in 2027 turn a 5% FCF yield into 2-3% just as the price support disappears. The July low of $136.34 and the 52-week low of $130.04 establish that this equity trades below $140 on a gold retracement even before earnings estimates fully reset; the low sell-side target of $87 marks the tail.

### Base Case — $195 (+9.3%)

Gold holds a $4,200-4,500 range. FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $12.50 at 15.5x, plus net cash. Production sits near the low end of 3.3-3.5 Moz in 2026 and falls ~4% in 2027 on Barnat before Odyssey offsets it; AISC stays inside $1,400-1,550. Dividends and buybacks return roughly 40-48% of ~$4.5-5.0B of free cash flow. Probability-weighting 30% bear / 45% base / 25% bull produces an expected return near +5% including the dividend, with a downside tail three times the base-case upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders should keep exposure — this is the highest-quality vehicle in the sector for expressing a gold view and the balance sheet removes the failure modes that historically destroyed gold-mining equity. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $178.37 after a 31% fourteen-session rally into rich near-dated volatility and falling forward estimates. The classification is uncertain rather than growth- or income-driven: the dominant variable is a commodity price we do not forecast, the dividend yields only 1.0%, and near-term earnings growth is negative.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price.** A decline to $140-148 without fundamental deterioration would put the stock at 11-12x FY2027 consensus with ~6.5% trailing FCF yield and would restore the asymmetry we require; that was the level available on 20 July.
2. **Estimate revisions turning positive.** Two consecutive months of upward FY2027 revisions, reversing the current -11.8% 90-day trend, would confirm that the gold-driven earnings reset has completed.
3. **Barnat resolution.** A completed Barnat redesign that quantifies the 2027-2028 impact materially below 150 koz/yr, delivered with formal 2027 guidance, would remove the largest company-specific overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Realized gold falling below $3,600/oz for a full quarter, which would compress AISC margin materially from Q2 levels and take FY2027 EPS toward $9.
2. FY2026 AISC guidance being raised above $1,550/oz, or 2026 production guided below 3.3 Moz — either would break the "costs contained while price ran" plank.
3. A Hope Bay capital increase above ~$2.7B (a 10%+ overrun, which the company's own sensitivity table maps to ~1.6pp of IRR and $149M of NPV), or a missed or curtailed 2027 sealift season.
4. Any adverse geotechnical event at a second cornerstone asset, or a seismic event at LaRonde/Goldex/Fosterville that suspends production.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 results release in late October 2026, which should carry the completed Barnat redesign and the first clean read on H2 cash flow with Hope Bay spending underway. The Q4 release and year-end reserve statement in February 2027 is the next hard checkpoint for reserve life and 2027 guidance.

## Conclusion

Agnico Eagle earns high marks on the two dimensions that matter most for durable outcomes. Financial health is close to best-in-class: $3.27B net cash, $197M of company-defined debt, a Fitch upgrade into the A category, $7.4B of trailing operating cash flow, and no covenant or refinancing exposure worth modelling. Competitive position is improving through cost leadership at $1,459/oz AISC, jurisdictional quality, and a district-consolidation strategy in Finland that mirrors the Abitibi playbook. Capital allocation has been disciplined in a cycle where gold miners have historically over-distributed or over-acquired.

The constraint is entry arithmetic. Fourteen-and-a-half times FY2027 consensus is only cheap if $4,400 gold is the new normal; on $3,300 gold the same asset base earns closer to $7.00-7.50 and the stock is 24x. Consensus is cutting, not raising — 11-12% off FY2026 and FY2027 over 90 days, with the current quarter down 19.5% in a month. The flagship complex will produce up to 4.4% less gold in each of 2027 and 2028 than plan, and the number quantifying that sits in a release most models did not read. Capital intensity is rising 24% into a $2.4B Arctic build whose reserve base was partly reclassified to resources and whose logistics depend on a single annual sealift window. And the stock has already recovered 31% from its July low into implied volatility at the 80th percentile of its own chain.

Base case $195, bull $250, bear $125. A +9% base-case return against -30% downside is not a distribution that justifies new capital, and the quality of the franchise does not change that arithmetic. We would be buyers in the $140s or on a confirmed turn in forward estimates. HOLD.
