**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Expedia Group is in the middle of a real operating turnaround. The post-2020 technology consolidation, ongoing restructuring (~$163M since Feb 2024), and disciplined direct-marketing spend have driven operating margin from 8.0% (2023) to 12.7% (2025), with Q1'26 reaching a 15.8% operating margin — the highest first-quarter margin in roughly 15 years — on +15% revenue growth and +83% Adjusted EBITDA growth. The B2B segment (Rapid API, white-label, agent tools) is the engine, compounding at +18–22% with gross bookings up 22% in Q1'26. Free cash flow of ~$3.1B (21% margin) funds an aggressive capital-return program: share count is down from 157M (2022) to ~114M, the dividend was reinstated in 2025, and a fresh $5B buyback was authorized in May 2026. Credit is investment-grade with a Moody's outlook upgraded to positive.

The fundamentals support a constructive 12-month view, but three concrete factors prevent a BUY at $243. First, despite a 38.4% Q1 EPS beat, management held full-year guidance unchanged — caution on H2 AI compute costs and macro demand rather than underwriting the Q1 acceleration forward. Second, the stock has already re-rated, trading near ~20.9x EPS, with the consensus split (21 holds vs 15 buys) and the median analyst at HOLD. Third, the AI/agentic disintermediation question is the single largest determinant of the multiple and remains genuinely two-sided: Expedia supplies its inventory into ChatGPT, Claude, and (soon) Gemini, which means the same platforms can disintermediate its booking flow.

We rate EXPE **HOLD (growth_hold)**. The path to an upgrade is specific and checkable: a Q2 guidance raise (early August) plus normalization of front-month implied volatility below ~50% would convert this to a BUY. The thesis is held back by sector timing and an unresolved binary, not by fundamental deterioration, and a lower price on improving fundamentals improves the eventual entry.

## Company Overview

Expedia operates one of the world's largest online travel marketplaces, connecting travelers with ~3.7M lodging properties (including ~2.5M Vrbo alternative accommodations), 500+ airlines, plus car, cruise, insurance, and activities. Three reportable segments:

- **B2C (~62% of revenue):** Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, Travelocity, ebookers, Wotif. FY25 revenue $9.47B (+2%), Adj EBITDA $2.80B. Q1'26 B2C EBITDA $426M (+96%) on marketing leverage.
- **B2B (~33%, the growth engine):** powers travel for airlines, agencies, banks, and corporates. FY25 revenue $4.84B (+18%), Adj EBITDA $1.26B (+22%); Q1'26 gross bookings +22%.
- **trivago (majority-owned, separately listed):** FY25 third-party revenue $417M (+33%).

Business-model mix: Merchant 70% / Agency 22% / Advertising-media-other 8%. Lodging is ~80% of revenue. The high-margin EG Advertising business ($758M, +19%) and trivago are structurally attractive. The moat rests on global supply breadth, a unified post-2020 platform, the One Key cross-brand loyalty program, and first-party data scale.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $11.67B (2022) → $12.84B (2023) → $13.69B (2024) → $14.73B (2025, +8%), accelerating to +15% in Q1'26 ($3.43B), with gross bookings +13% ($35.5B).

**Margin expansion is the standout.** Operating margin moved 8.0% (2023) → 9.6% (2024) → 12.7% (2025). FY25 Adjusted EBITDA was $3.50B (+19%, ~23.8% margin). Q1'26 Adj EBITDA reached $542M (+83% YoY) at a 15.8% margin. The mechanism is clean: revenue growth outpacing a more efficient cost base — disciplined direct marketing in B2C, payments and customer-service automation, and headcount reductions from restructuring.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** FY25 operating cash flow $3.88B, FCF $3.11B (~21% FCF margin). The merchant model is a structural working-capital source — deferred merchant bookings (customer float) grew to ~$15B by Q1'26. Capital return is aggressive: $700M repurchased in Q1'26 at ~$211.79, a new $5B authorization in May 2026 atop $870M remaining, and a reinstated dividend now at $0.48/quarter.

**Balance sheet:** Cash + short-term investments $5.7B (Q1'26); total debt face cut to ~$4.47B after February 2026 repayment of $750M 5.0% notes and the $1.1B 0% convertibles (cash-settled, including a $78M conversion premium). A new $1B 5.5% note (due 2036) was issued in April 2026, and the new $2.5B revolver is unsecured (subsidiary guarantees released). Investment-grade across all three agencies (Moody's Baa2, outlook upgraded to positive; S&P/Fitch BBB stable).

**A note on the Altman Z-score of 0.74:** this "distress" reading is a false signal. It is mechanically depressed by structurally negative working capital (deferred merchant bookings counted as current liabilities) and thin GAAP equity (~$1.28B) from years of buybacks. ROE is 101%, ROA 5.3%, the company generates $3B+ FCF and is investment-grade. The OTA float model should override the standardized screen.

**Quality-of-earnings flag:** Q1'26 reported a GAAP net loss of –$6M, driven by a $155M mark-to-market loss on the Global Business Travel (GBT) equity stake plus FX — non-operating, non-cash items. Operating income was solidly positive at $251M (versus a –$70M loss the prior year). The GBT stake will continue to introduce volatility through "other, net."

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest dimension. Four consecutive beats (Q1'26 +38.4%, Q4'25 +10.4%, Q3'25 +7.3%, Q2'25 +1.5%) and broadly rising estimates: current-year EPS revised +3.0% over 90 days to $19.77, next-year +2.2% to $23.10, current quarter +4.8% to $5.20. These revisions are positive but moderate — not the +30%/90-day velocity that would constitute a standalone strong-BUY signal. They confirm momentum without signaling a dislocation.

B2B is the durable growth vector, compounding mid-to-high teens, helped by the Rapid API and the new exclusive Uber hotel partnership (Expedia supplies lodging inventory into Uber's app). EG Advertising and trivago add high-margin, structurally attractive layers. Global travel spend exceeds $2T with Expedia at a single-digit share, leaving runway.

The offset: management held FY26 guidance flat despite the Q1 beat. That is the most important forward tell in the data. It signals either conservatism that sets up future beats, or genuine caution on H2 AI-compute cost ramp and macro travel demand. With the stock already pricing the margin inflection, the burden is on management to raise, and they declined to.

## Valuation Assessment

At $243, EXPE trades at ~20.9x EPS. Against peers (median P/E 17.5), it sits at a premium to the cruise lines (CCL 12.6x, RCL 17.5x, NCLH 15.8x) and a discount to the asset-light platforms (ABNB 32.1x, HLT 52.1x). The mid-pack positioning is reasonable for a business growing high-single-digit revenue with a real margin inflection, but it is not cheap — the multiple already reflects the EBITDA reacceleration.

The consensus mean target is $286 (~18% upside) with a $409 high and $230 low. I treat these targets with care: the analyst body is split 15 buys / 21 holds, with holds rising over the past three months, so the mean target is not a clean independent confirmation. The stock re-rated from a 52-week low of ~$160 to a $303.80 high before giving back ~20% to current levels — the drawdown is the market repricing AI disintermediation risk, not noise to be dismissed.

The valuation score is held to 5.5 because the asymmetry is no longer compelling at this price. There is no identified near-term catalyst forcing a re-rating, the forward multiple is full relative to high-single-digit revenue growth, and a high-momentum re-rating already occurred. The business quality is genuine, but cheapness is not the case, and a "cheap" framing would be the wrong read given how that signal has behaved in this market.

## Competitive Landscape

Expedia competes with Booking Holdings (the larger, higher-margin rival), Airbnb (expanding into hotels, experiences, flights), Google's vertical search, supplier-direct channels, and emerging AI/agentic entrants. Relative strengths: the fastest-growing B2B platform, high-margin advertising, and Vrbo's whole-home niche. Relative weaknesses: lower international/agency-hotel exposure than Booking and historically lower margins (though the gap is narrowing as platform consolidation pays off).

The competitive trajectory is the crux of the 5.5 score. Margins and B2B share are clearly improving, which argues up. But the structural question — whether AI agents disintermediate the OTA from the booking flow — argues for caution on the moat trajectory. Expedia is positioning offensively: live as an app inside ChatGPT (Oct 2025), ads on ChatGPT (Feb 2026), booking integrations in ChatGPT and Claude, work underway on Gemini, with >30% of self-serve support now AI-handled and agent onboarding cut ~60%. The critical attribution: Expedia supplies its inventory into platforms it does not own. OpenAI's March 2026 pullback from ChatGPT direct checkout was a near-term positive, and CEO Gorin's framing ("if [AI] evolves toward a paid model, that's a space we know well") reframes the threat as a potential channel. The moat trajectory is genuinely two-sided and unresolved, which is why the score sits at the midpoint rather than higher.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AI/agentic disintermediation (defining risk).** Newly elevated 10-K language on the "agentic revolution." Double-edged but unresolved; it is the largest single swing factor for the multiple.
2. **Consumer cyclicality and macro demand.** Travel bookings are directly exposed to a discretionary-spending pullback. Q1'26 already cited adverse Middle East and Mexico events, though management noted US demand was strong with continued momentum.
3. **Tax/transfer pricing.** IRS adjustments ~$244M (2011–13) and ~$431M (2014–16), with 2017–20 under exam; Italian withholding tax ($156M paid Dec 2025, later years open); occupancy/VAT "pay-to-play" disputes that could require prepayment. Offsetting positive: Canada's DST repeal produced a $71M Q1 reversal.
4. **Google/search dependency** raising customer-acquisition costs.
5. **Vrbo regulation** — EU Short-Term Rental Law effective May 2026.
6. **Governance overhang** — Barry Diller controls ~32% of voting power on ~4.6% economics (dual-class), and is also IAC Chairman; CFO transition (Schenkel → Andersen, effective 5/11/26) is in progress but appears orderly.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 89.0% against 46.7% one-year HV (rich, 88th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (1-week 89% > long-term 46%), pricing an imminent binary. IV spiked 30.9pp since the prior snapshot while price rose 9.6%. Put/call skew is balanced (2.7%) and P/C OI fell from 2.06 to 0.81 (less bearish positioning), so this is volatility-of-event pricing rather than directional bearish conviction. For a name I am rating HOLD, the elevated front-month vol reinforces caution against chasing here; for an eventual entry, a normalization of front IV below ~50% would be a favorable timing signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300–340).** B2B keeps compounding 18–22%, the margin inflection continues toward a mid-to-high-teens full-year operating margin, AI distribution (ChatGPT ads, Claude, Gemini) becomes a net-accretive paid channel rather than a threat, and management raises guidance at Q2. The $5B buyback shrinks the share count meaningfully against $3B+ FCF. Re-rating toward the high-quality platform cohort follows. Bull target ~$340.

**Bear case ($175–195).** H2 consumer demand softens, AI agents begin disintermediating the booking flow and compressing the multiple, AI compute costs erode the margin gains, and Google search costs rise. A travel-demand-sensitive name in Consumer Discretionary de-rates toward peer cruise multiples. Bear target ~$185, roughly –24% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Positive 12-month thesis with a specific upgrade catalyst, held back by sector timing, full valuation, and an unresolved AI binary, not by fundamental deterioration.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2'26 results (early August) accompanied by an explicit full-year guidance raise — management underwriting the Q1 acceleration forward rather than holding flat.
- Front-month ATM IV normalizes below ~50% (resolution of the near-term binary the options market is pricing), improving entry timing.
- B2B gross-bookings growth sustained at ≥20% with continued operating-margin expansion through H2 (evidence the AI-compute cost ramp is not eroding the inflection).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of decelerating room-night/gross-bookings growth signaling consumer-demand rollover.
- Concrete evidence of AI-agent disintermediation in the data (declining direct/organic traffic, rising CAC compressing B2C EBITDA).
- A guidance cut, or operating-margin reversal that signals the AI-compute cost ramp is outrunning efficiency gains.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2'26 earnings (early August). Monitor front-month IV and the AI-distribution monetization commentary in the interim.

## Conclusion

Expedia is executing a genuine operating turnaround: record Q1 margins, +83% EBITDA growth, a fast-growing B2B engine, $3B+ FCF, investment-grade credit, and disciplined capital return. The fundamental trajectory is improving, and the recent ~20% pullback from the $303.80 high reflects the market pricing AI risk rather than operating deterioration. At ~20.9x EPS, the margin inflection is already in the price; management declined to raise guidance despite a 38.4% beat; the AI/agentic disintermediation question is unresolved and is the dominant driver of the multiple; and the name sits in Consumer Discretionary, with the options market pricing a near-term binary at 89% front IV. These are concrete, falsifiable blockers to a BUY at $243, and each has a measurable resolution. We rate EXPE HOLD with a clear path to upgrade on a Q2 guidance raise and IV normalization.
