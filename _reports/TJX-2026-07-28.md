**Research Date: July 28, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TJX operates the strongest business model in retail as measured by capital efficiency, competitive durability, and cash generation. The company runs 5,214 off-price stores across four segments, sells brand-name merchandise at 20–60% below full-price retailers, and turns a low-capital, treasure-hunt store format into a 53.9% return on equity. The fiscal 2027 first quarter (ended May 2, 2026) accelerated rather than moderated: net sales rose 9% to $14.3B, comparable-store sales grew 6% (versus +3% a year earlier), diluted EPS climbed 29% to $1.19, and pre-tax margin expanded 170bps to 12.0%, with margin gains across all four segments. Management raised full-year comp and EPS guidance. The comp quality is high, driven by both traffic (transactions) and basket, not just price inflation.

The debate on TJX is the price, not the business. At ~$156, the stock trades at roughly 30x trailing and ~30x forward earnings, a premium to its off-price peer median and near its all-time high. Consensus targets ($177 mean, $175 median, $197 high) imply modest upside, but the low end sits at $125, a wide dispersion that reflects the valuation debate directly. TJX also sits in the Consumer Discretionary sector, which has been a structurally poor source of company-specific alpha; a positive raw return here is mostly beta unless a distinct catalyst is present.

We rate TJX HOLD (growth_hold). The fundamental thesis is intact and arguably improving: margin expansion is being driven by the highest-quality levers (favorable merchandise markon and expense leverage) rather than one-timers, and there is roughly $490M of unrecorded IEEPA tariff-refund optionality that current numbers ignore. But at ~30x forward on a mid-single-digit comp / high-single-digit revenue grower, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would upgrade to BUY on a pullback into the low-$130s or on tangible realization of the tariff refund, and we treat the current consensus targets as a reasonable, not stale, anchor given the premium multiple.

## Company Overview

TJX is the world's leading off-price apparel and home fashions retailer. The model buys opportunistically from ~21,000 vendors across 100+ countries (closeouts, cancellations, overruns, special production, and packaway inventory) and resells at deep discounts to full-price retail. A 1,400-person buying organization, willingness to take partial assortments in small-to-large lots, prompt payment, and no markdown or return concessions make TJX the buyer-of-choice for excess inventory globally.

The four reporting segments (fiscal 2026):
- **Marmaxx (U.S.)** — TJ Maxx, Marshalls, Sierra: $36.6B sales, 15.1% segment margin, 2,748 stores. The largest and most profitable segment.
- **HomeGoods (U.S.)** — HomeGoods, Homesense: $10.2B sales, 12.2% margin (+130bps YoY), 1,042 stores. The fastest-improving segment.
- **TJX Canada** — Winners, HomeSense, Marshalls: $5.6B sales, 13.4% margin, 589 stores.
- **TJX International** — TK Maxx/Homesense in Europe plus TK Maxx Australia: $8.0B sales, 7.0% margin (lowest but rising), 835 stores.

The moat rests on three pillars. First, opportunistic buying scale: TJX can absorb inventory nobody else will take, which makes it structurally advantaged as full-price retail distress and overstocks increase the supply of quality goods available to it. Second, the treasure-hunt format drives rapid inventory turns and frequent visits and is structurally resistant to e-commerce; online is only ~2% of sales, because the discovery experience does not translate to a shopping cart. Third, the model is countercyclical on the supply side and defensive on the demand side; value-seeking, trade-down consumers benefit TJX exactly when full-price retail struggles. Management sees long-term store potential of 7,000 versus 5,214 today (Marmaxx 3,000 vs 2,603; HomeGoods 1,800 vs 1,042), a multi-year unit-growth runway that requires little incremental capital per store.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue grew from $49.9B in fiscal 2023 to $60.4B in fiscal 2026, a steady ~7–8% annual pace. Net income rose from $3.5B to $5.5B, and diluted EPS reached $4.87 (fiscal 2026) versus $4.26 (fiscal 2025). This is compounding, not a cyclical spike.

**Fiscal 2026.** Net sales rose 7% on +5% consolidated comps driven by both higher basket and more transactions. Pre-tax margin reached 12.1% (+60bps), with cost of sales improving to 69.0% of sales on lower freight and lower shrink, and SG&A at 19.1%. Operating cash flow was $6.9B and free cash flow $4.9B (8.1% FCF margin) on $2.0B of capex. The company returned $4.3B to shareholders: $2.5B in buybacks (retiring 18.5M shares) plus $1.8B in dividends.

One normalization matters: fiscal 2026 EPS was flattered by a $419M net gain from a credit-card interchange litigation settlement recognized in SG&A in Q4 (partly offset by ~$198M of related incentive/discretionary expense), a net benefit of roughly $0.14 to diluted EPS. Modeling underlying earnings requires stripping this out. The fiscal 2027 first quarter delivered its margin gains without any comparable one-timer.

**Q1 fiscal 2027 (ended May 2, 2026).** HomeGoods comps accelerated to +9% (segment margin ~12.9%), Marmaxx +6%. The margin expansion came from favorable merchandise markon and expense leverage, the highest-quality drivers. Operating cash flow jumped to $1.1B from $394M a year earlier, boosted by receipt of the interchange settlement cash. On comparability: a non-hedge-accounted derivative swing (+$54M gain in Q1 FY27 versus –$97M loss prior year) flatters the year-over-year comparison, so a portion of the reported acceleration is mark-to-market noise rather than operating gain.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Cash was $5.6B at Q1; financial debt is modest at ~$2.9B, of which a $999M note matures September 2026 and is comfortably covered. There is no commercial paper outstanding, $1.5B of undrawn revolvers, and full covenant compliance. Standardized screens flag a 3.5x debt/equity ratio and an Altman Z of 2.32 ("grey zone"), but these are artifacts of ~$11.3B of capitalized operating leases (ASC 842) and heavy buybacks that have depressed book equity. On financial debt alone (~$2.9B against ~$8B+ EBITDA), TJX is lowly levered with $5.6B in cash. ROE of 53.9%, ROA of 15.4%, and asset turnover of 1.69x reflect a genuinely capital-efficient model, not distress. Management raised the dividend 13% to $1.92 annualized (fiscal 2027), has ~$3.5B of buyback authorization remaining, and plans $2.75–3.0B in fiscal 2027 buybacks. The balance-sheet strength and cash conversion support a high financial-health score; this is the single most reliable dimension for discriminating winners, and TJX scores clearly at the top of it.

## Growth Analysis

TJX has posted four consecutive earnings beats (most recent quarter +15.9%, prior quarters +1.9% to +7.5%), a track record that supports confidence in forward consensus. The near-term acceleration is genuine: comps went from +3% a year ago to +6% in the latest quarter, with traffic gains across all segments and geographies. HomeGoods margin recovery from 10.9% to 12.2% (full year) and 12.9% (Q1) signals the post-pandemic home-goods reset is complete, removing a multi-quarter drag.

The unit-growth runway is the durable long-term driver, with the largest gaps at Marmaxx and HomeGoods. International expansion adds optionality: TK Maxx entered Spain in March 2026, and low-capital equity-method investments (49% MOS JV with Grupo Axo in Mexico, 200+ stores; 35% BFL stake in the Middle East, 100+ stores) seed off-price in emerging markets with an option to increase the MOS stake.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current-year EPS consensus of $5.21 has moved +2.0% over 90 days and 0.0% over 30 days; next-quarter estimates are down 1.9% over 90 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a fundamental acceleration warranting an aggressive growth re-rating. The growth here is high-quality and durable but mid-single-digit at the comp line and high-single-digit at the revenue line, which is what a mature best-in-class retailer should deliver. Growth prospects earn a solid but not exceptional score, and this dimension is used as confirmation of the financial-health read rather than a standalone driver.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$156, TJX trades at roughly 30x trailing EPS of $4.87 and ~30x forward on the $5.21 current-year consensus (~27x on the $5.71 next-year estimate). The dividend yield is only ~1.2%.

Against the Apparel Retail peer group, the multiple looks reasonable relative to the two direct off-price comps but rich relative to the broader set:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield | 52w Discount |
|--------|-----|-----------|--------------|
| ROST (Ross) | 33.4 | 0.8% | -0% |
| BURL (Burlington) | 35.2 | — | -3% |
| GAP | 8.7 | 3.3% | -33% |
| URBN | 14.9 | — | -17% |
| BOOT | 23.8 | — | -30% |
| **TJX** | **~30** | **1.2%** | **~-8%** |
| Peer median | 23.8 | 0.0% | — |

TJX trades below Ross (33.4x) and Burlington (35.2x), which is defensible given it is the largest, highest-margin, and most diversified of the three. But it sits at roughly 1.25x the peer median. The cheaper names, Gap at 8.7x and Urban Outfitters at 14.9x, carry structural or execution discounts, and a low headline multiple among consumer-discretionary retailers is more often a value-trap signal than a bargain. We do not treat those cheaper peers as evidence TJX is expensive; we treat TJX's premium as earned by quality.

The core valuation problem is that the quality is fully recognized. On a PEG basis, ~30x forward against mid-single-digit comp growth and high-single-digit revenue growth (call it ~10–12% EPS growth with buybacks) yields a PEG of roughly 2.5x, not the sub-1.5 that would justify treating consensus targets as stale floors. The multiple already prices continued execution, which makes the risk asymmetric to the downside: beats are expected (limited re-rating), and any comp deceleration or tariff-cost shock derates a rich multiple. The $490M unrecorded IEEPA tariff-refund optionality is real upside not in the numbers, but it is one-time in nature and uncertain in timing, so it supports the story rather than the valuation.

Consensus targets imply ~13% upside; the $125 low sits ~20% below current. Given the premium multiple, we treat these targets as a fair anchor rather than a floor: at ~30x forward, analyst targets tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than lagging it. The valuation score is below neutral, reflecting a stretched multiple with limited asymmetry, not a value-trap concern.

## Competitive Landscape

The off-price channel (TJX, Ross, Burlington) continues to take share from full-price and department-store retail, and TJX is the scale leader. Its structural advantages are strengthening rather than eroding: retail distress in the broader consumer economy increases the supply of distressed and cancelled inventory available to off-price buyers, and a value-seeking, trade-down consumer supports demand. The treasure-hunt in-store model has proven durable against e-commerce; the ~2% online mix reflects a format that does not translate to a cart.

Competitive watch-items from the filings are e-commerce penetration and competitors' adoption of AI/technology in buying and allocation, but there is no evidence of share loss. HomeGoods margin recovery and comp acceleration across all segments indicate the competitive position is stable-to-improving. The trajectory matters more than the level; TJX is a market leader that continues to gain share, which supports a high competitive-position score. This dimension has been a weaker predictor in recent data, so we weight it but confirm it against the stronger financial-health read.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff/trade-policy exposure — the dominant macro variable.** Products are heavily sourced from China, India, and Southeast Asia. Following the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling, a new global tariff executive order was issued, leaving uncertainty on direct/indirect import costs, vendor pricing, and consumer demand. This is genuinely two-sided: the flexible sourcing model has historically absorbed tariff shocks, and off-price can benefit when tariffs create excess or cancelled inventory elsewhere. TJX also estimates it paid ~$490M in IEEPA tariffs now potentially refundable, of which only an immaterial amount had been received as of late May, an off-balance-sheet upside, not a base case.

**Execution risk on opportunistic buying.** The moat depends on merchants judging fashion, price, and quantity correctly with high discretion. Misjudgment produces markdowns or empty shelves. This is the core operating risk of the model.

**Inventory shrink / organized retail crime.** Fiscal 2026 benefited from lower shrink; that tailwind could reverse and pressure margins.

**FX and hedge volatility.** ~22% of revenue is non-U.S. Non-hedge-accounted derivatives create quarterly mark-to-market swings.

**Succession.** The named executive team is uniformly long-tenured and aging (CEO Herrman 65, Executive Chairman Meyrowitz 72, several group presidents 63–66). The off-price buying discipline is hard to teach, so succession execution matters more here than at a typical retailer.

**Seasonality.** Earnings concentrate in the back-half (back-to-school and holiday); disruption in that window is disproportionately damaging.

On balance, the risks are well-understood and largely priced, with the tariff exposure carrying genuine two-sided optionality. There is no binary regulatory or litigation catastrophe on the horizon. The risk-factor score is neutral-to-slightly-positive.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 24.7% versus 19.4% one-year historical vol, modestly rich (spread ~5.2%), but IV percentile within the current chain is only 10%, so the market is not pricing an outsized near-term event. The term structure is flat (24.7% near vs 23.9% far), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a catalyst. Put/call skew is a mild 4.0% (elevated put demand) and the put/call volume ratio of 0.70 is unremarkable; a single unusual print (1,434 October $145 puts at 3.6x open interest) reflects modest downside hedging ~10% out of the money rather than a directional conviction signal. ATM IV rose from 20.7% to 24.7% while the stock slipped 1.8% since the prior snapshot, consistent with routine positioning. Nothing in the options data materially challenges the fundamental thesis; positioning is roughly neutral.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** TJX is a wide-moat, counter-cyclical retailer with accelerating comps, margin expansion driven by high-quality levers, fortress free cash flow, and a long unit-growth runway. The tariff regime that spooks the market is on balance a supply-side tailwind for off-price, and there is ~$490M of unrecorded refund optionality. HomeGoods margin recovery is complete, removing a drag. International (Spain, MOS, BFL) adds optionality at low capital intensity. If comps hold mid-single-digit and margins continue expanding, EPS compounds low-double-digits and the premium multiple sustains. Bull target ~$197 (analyst high), roughly 26% above current, on ~34x the $5.71 next-year estimate plus tariff-refund realization.

**Bear case.** At ~30x forward on a mid-single-digit comp grower, the multiple prices perfection. A tariff-cost shock that the flexible model cannot fully absorb, a reversal of the favorable shrink tailwind, or a consumer-demand cliff in the back-half would derate the stock quickly. Consumer Discretionary is a structurally poor alpha sector, and fiscal 2026 EPS was flattered by a $0.14 interchange one-timer that will not repeat. Bear target ~$125 (analyst low), roughly 20% below current, on ~24x depressed forward earnings.

**Base case.** Comps normalize toward mid-single-digit, margins hold near current levels, and EPS reaches ~$5.71 next year. A ~30x multiple on that yields ~$171. Base target ~$171–175, roughly 9–12% above current including the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is positive and specific (accelerating comps, expanding margins, a long unit runway, and tariff-refund optionality), but the current price already captures most of the quality, leaving balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward.

- **Upgrade to BUY triggers:** (1) A pullback into the low-$130s, compressing forward P/E toward ~25x and restoring asymmetry; (2) tangible realization of a material portion of the ~$490M IEEPA tariff refund recorded as income/cash; (3) a step-change in estimate-revision velocity (current-year consensus rising >5% in 90 days) confirming a durable acceleration rather than a one-quarter beat.
- **Downgrade to AVOID triggers:** (1) Consolidated comps decelerating below +2% for two consecutive quarters; (2) a tariff-driven merchandise-margin compression the sourcing model visibly fails to offset (gross margin down >100bps YoY); (3) a reversal of the shrink tailwind combined with SG&A deleverage that pushes pre-tax margin below 11%.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal-quarter earnings (Q2 FY27, expected ~mid-August 2026), which will test whether the +6% comp and +170bps margin acceleration is sustainable against tougher comparisons, and any development on the IEEPA refund claims.

The score trajectory (7.0 → 7.1 → 7.0 → 6.8) has drifted lower across four consecutive HOLDs, driven mainly by a management-quality markdown (routine insider selling at highs, no open-market buying) even as financial health improved (7.5 → 8.5) on the strengthening balance sheet and cash conversion. New data confirms rather than reverses this pattern: fundamentals are improving, but the case for continuing HOLD unchanged is that valuation, not business quality, is the binding constraint. The recent price action near ~$156 provides only a modest improvement in entry point on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, not enough to clear the bar for a BUY at ~30x forward.

## Conclusion

TJX is a best-in-class, wide-moat, counter-cyclical retailer with accelerating comps, expanding margins driven by high-quality levers, fortress free cash flow, a long unit-growth runway, and genuine tariff-refund optionality. The standardized balance-sheet warning flags are lease-accounting artifacts, not distress. The business deserves its premium, and financial health is unusually strong.

The constraint is price. At ~30x trailing / ~30x forward and near all-time highs, the stock offers ~9–13% base-case upside against ~20% downside to the analyst low, balanced rather than asymmetric, and it sits in a sector where company-specific alpha has been hard to come by. Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating, which does not support an aggressive re-rating call. We rate TJX **HOLD (growth_hold)**, with a clear path to BUY on a pullback into the low-$130s or on realization of the tariff refund.
