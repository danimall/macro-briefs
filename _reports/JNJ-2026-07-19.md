**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Johnson & Johnson has re-rated sharply over the past twelve months, rising 55% from a July 2025 low of ~$163 to ~$253, and reaching a 52-week high of $269.43 in early July 2026 before easing on its Q2 print. The move reflects a genuine improvement in the underlying business: pharma growth reaccelerated to +6% in 2025 despite a ~6.2% company-level drag from STELARA's biosimilar collapse, MedTech's cardiovascular franchise is compounding at mid-teens rates, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance in mid-July to a $100.6B sales midpoint with adjusted operational EPS of $11.50–$11.65. The pipeline is doing its job: DARZALEX, TREMFYA, CAPLYTA, SPRAVATO, and new launches (ICOTYDE, INLEXZO) are outrunning the loss-of-exclusivity cliff, which is the central operating question for the company.

The problem for new capital is that the market has largely paid for this. At ~$253 the stock trades at roughly 21.9x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted operational EPS guidance, a premium to the pharmaceuticals peer median of 20.0x, and a multiple that leaves thin cushion against a still-unresolved talc liability. J&J returned to the tort system in 2025 after its third bankruptcy attempt failed, and it cut its talc reserve from ~$11.6B to ~$3.4B, meaning roughly 74,000 pending ovarian-cancer plaintiffs are now largely unreserved and will be litigated individually through 2026 and beyond. The June 2026 Los Angeles defense verdict is an encouraging early data point, but the range of outcomes remains wide and binary.

Our scoring has drifted lower across four consecutive reads (7.2 → 6.9 → 6.7 → 6.4), driven by declines in competitive position, management quality, and risk factors. This report continues that trajectory to an overall 5.7, but the mechanism is different from the prior reads: the fundamentals have improved (guidance raised, estimates rising), while the valuation cushion has compressed as the price ran. This is a HOLD on a growth basis: a mid-single-digit compounder with a covered 2.1% dividend, in a sound business.

## Company Overview

Post-Kenvue spin (2023), J&J operates two reporting segments:

- **Innovative Medicine (pharma):** $60.4B in 2025, 64% of sales, with a segment income-before-tax margin of 36.9%. Six therapeutic areas anchored by oncology (DARZALEX, CARVYKTI, RYBREVANT, TALVEY, TECVAYLI) and immunology (TREMFYA), with a growing neuroscience franchise (CAPLYTA via the $14.5B Intra-Cellular acquisition, SPRAVATO +57%).
- **MedTech:** $33.8B in 2025, segment IBT margin of only 12.2%. Cardiovascular (+15.8%, powered by Shockwave IVL and Abiomed Impella) is the growth engine; Orthopaedics stagnated at +1.1% and is being separated.

In October 2025 management announced its intent to separate the Orthopaedics business (to be renamed DePuy Synthes) within 18–24 months, shedding a $9.3B, low-growth, low-margin franchise. This continues an active portfolio reshaping: divesting slow growth while acquiring pipeline (Intra-Cellular $14.5B, Shockwave $12.6B, Halda $3.05B, Firefly Bio ~$1B). New products represent roughly 25% of 2025 sales, evidence the R&D engine is refreshing the base.

A distribution note: three wholesalers account for ~48% of gross revenue. This is channel concentration, not end-demand concentration, and is standard for large pharma.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue was $94.2B in 2025 (+6.0%: volume +8.4%, price −3.1%, FX +0.7%), on a 5-year CAGR of 6.7%. Reported net income jumped to $26.8B (diluted EPS $11.03) from $14.1B in 2024, but this headline is misleading. The year-over-year swing is dominated by a ~$12B non-operating item: 2025 included a +$7.0B reversal of the talc reserve, whereas 2024 carried a −$5.1B talc charge. The clean operating gauge is adjusted operational EPS (~$10 in 2025), and segment income before tax rose more modestly to $26.4B from $22.7B. The trailing P/E of 29.3x based on GAAP EPS overstates the multiple; on adjusted operational earnings the stock trades closer to 21.9x forward.

**Margins.** Gross margin is ~68%. The reported ~7.7% operating margin is depressed by how litigation and Other items flow through the income statement; underlying pharma profitability (36.9% segment IBT) is strong. R&D was $14.7B (15.6% of sales, down from 19.4% in 2024, which included a one-time $1.25B IPR&D charge).

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $24.5B and FCF ~$19.7B (FCF margin ~21%). This is high-quality, consistent cash generation and the anchor of the investment case. FCF comfortably covered $12.4B of dividends (63rd consecutive annual increase, raised to $1.30/quarter for 2026) and $6.0B of buybacks.

**Balance sheet.** Cash was $19.7B; total debt rose to $47.9B from $36.6B (funding Intra-Cellular via $9.2B of new notes), lifting net debt to $28.2B from $12.1B. This remains conservative against ~$25B of annual OCF and solidly investment-grade. The Altman Z-Score of 1.59 flagging "distress" is a false positive: the model penalizes intangible-heavy pharma (goodwill $48.8B, intangibles $50.4B) and low asset turnover (0.47), not genuine credit risk. Investment-grade ratings, $24.5B OCF, and 33% ROE contradict any distress reading.

**Earnings quality and surprises.** The four-quarter surprise record shows three narrow beats and one in-line result (Q2 2026 +0.9%, Q1 2026 +0.3%, Q4 2025 −0.1%, Q3 2025 +0.5%). J&J delivers close to consensus with low variance, a mature-compounder profile rather than an inflection story. This calibrates our confidence in forward estimates as high but limits the case for consensus systematically understating earnings power.

Financial health scores 7.0: durable cash generation and manageable leverage, tempered by the accounting noise that clouds the headline and the rising net-debt trajectory from the M&A pivot.

## Growth Analysis

The operating story is credible mid-single-digit compounding with a durable pipeline. Pharma grew 6% in 2025 while absorbing a ~41% STELARA decline to $6.1B (from $10.4B). The offsets are working: TREMFYA +40.5% to $5.2B, DARZALEX +23% (Q1 2026), SPRAVATO +57%, plus CAPLYTA from Intra-Cellular. MedTech cardiovascular +15.8% adds a second growth engine.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest. Current-year EPS moved to $11.66 (+0.7% over 90 days), next-year to $12.83 (+1.0%), and the current quarter to $3.05 (+1.0%). This is broad-based positive momentum, but it is nowhere near the >30% 90-day revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection. J&J is a steady grower whose estimates are drifting up as the pipeline outruns the LOE cliff, not a name where consensus is systematically catching up to a step-change.

The July 15 guidance raise implies roughly high-single-digit adjusted EPS growth. The Orthopaedics separation should be modestly accretive to growth and margins by removing the slowest franchise. The M&A cadence (four deals in 18 months, all pipeline-focused) reinforces durability but at the cost of rising net debt and integration risk.

Growth prospects score 5.5: solid, durable, sector-leading among big-pharma diversifieds, but decidedly mid-single-digit and priced accordingly. The dimension is the most reliable winner-selection signal in our framework, and J&J's growth here is good rather than exceptional, enough to justify a hold but not enough to force a re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$253, J&J trades at ~21.9x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted operational EPS guidance ($11.58) and ~19.7x the 2027 consensus of $12.83. Against the pharmaceuticals peer median P/E of 20.0x, the stock carries a modest premium.

Peer context, with mixed comparability:
- MRK 33.8x, LLY 41.2x (GLP-1 growth premium), PFE 20.0x (6.7% yield reflecting patent-cliff pressure), BMY 15.9x (deep-cliff discount), ZTS 13.3x.

J&J's premium to the median is defensible on quality (diversification, best-in-class balance sheet, a 63-year dividend record) but it does not represent asymmetric upside. The analyst target band is $190–$305 (mean $269.95, median $274), placing the current price ~7–8% below median target. On an inflection stock we would treat consensus targets as floors; here, with growth at mid-single digits and estimate revisions under +1% per 90 days, that principle does not apply. Analyst sentiment is described as "degrading" (hold count rose from 8 to 10 over three months), which argues against extrapolating targets higher.

The prior BUY call (April 2026) captured the re-rating from ~$240; that asymmetry has been realized. The dividend yield of 2.1% is below the peer median of 2.9% and below several peers offering both yield and cheaper multiples. On a growth-adjusted basis, J&J's PEG (~21.9x forward / ~7% adjusted EPS growth ≈ 3.1) is not compelling.

Our framework treats cheapness skeptically: a low multiple alone is not a buy signal, and here the multiple is not even low. There is no identified catalyst forcing a mispricing correction in J&J's favor; the more likely catalysts (talc trial outcomes, IRA rulings) are two-sided or negative. Valuation scores 4.5: the multiple is full for the growth delivered, with the talc tail as an uncompensated risk.

## Competitive Landscape

J&J competes in two distinct arenas and holds strong positions in both, though trajectory is mixed. In pharma, the moat is a deep, diversified pipeline that has demonstrably outrun a $4.3B STELARA revenue loss, a real test that most single-franchise peers would fail. Multiple myeloma (DARZALEX, CARVYKTI) and immunology (TREMFYA) are genuine leadership franchises, and the neuroscience build-out (CAPLYTA, SPRAVATO) adds optionality. New products at ~25% of sales evidence a self-renewing base.

In MedTech, positioning is bifurcated: cardiovascular is gaining share (Shockwave, Abiomed), while Orthopaedics stagnated and is being jettisoned. The divestiture is a net positive for competitive trajectory: it removes a share-losing, low-margin drag.

The offsetting concern is J&J's persistent five-year TSR lag versus its peer composite ($151.55 vs $188.57 on a $100 base; company 5-yr CAGR 8.7% vs S&P 500 14.4%). A market-leading diversified healthcare company that consistently trails its own peer set on total return signals that scale and diversification, while defensive, have not translated into differentiated value creation. Competitive position scores 6.5, down from the 8.5 of a year ago: the moat is intact and the pharma pipeline is executing, but the trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than clearly widening, and confirmation should come from growth prospects rather than this dimension alone.

## Risk Assessment

**Talc litigation — the defining overhang.** The ~$3.4B reserve covers executed settlements and defense costs, leaving the ~74,360 pending ovarian-cancer plaintiffs largely unreserved. The auditor flagged talc as a Critical Audit Matter. This is a genuine binary tail: an adverse causation ruling or a string of large plaintiff verdicts could force reserve rebuilds far above $3.4B. The de-risking of the balance sheet came at the cost of re-risking the outcome.

**Patent cliff.** STELARA fell 41% and SIMPONI biosimilars plus OPSUMIT generics (2026) extend the pressure. The "can new launches outrun LOE" question is answered affirmatively for now, but it is a permanent operating risk.

**IRA / drug-pricing.** XARELTO, IMBRUVICA, INVEGA, and ERLEADA face Medicare negotiation and Part D redesign pressure (price was a −3.1% headwind in 2025). J&J's Janssen unit is litigating the IRA program to the Supreme Court (cert sought December 2025); an adverse ruling would entrench the price headwind.

**Other litigation:** Opioids (~$1.1B remaining), Auris/Fortis $0.8B judgment (paid January 2026), Biosense Webster antitrust (on appeal), securities class action on talc (class certified; SCOTUS cert petition February 2026).

**Execution:** Orthopaedics separation carries timing, cost, and post-spin volatility risk; the rising net-debt trajectory from serial M&A raises integration risk.

Risk factors score 5.0: the talc tort docket is a large, unreserved, multi-year binary that the market prices imperfectly, and the IRA litigation is a second live overhang. In our framework risk_factors carries little recent predictive weight, so we treat this as context rather than a scoring driver, but the specificity and magnitude of the talc tail warrant the below-average mark.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing calm. Near-term ATM IV is 22.7% versus 18.0% one-year historical volatility, modestly rich but at only the 4th percentile within the current chain, and IV has fallen 6.0 points since June 11 as the stock rose 5.3%. Put/call skew is balanced (1.0% near-term), the P/C OI ratio eased to 0.60, and the term structure is flat. The one unusual print, a $255 call for July 24 at 6.7x volume/OI, is mildly bullish and near-dated. Longer-dated skew turns slightly negative (−3.0% at six months), consistent with modest downside hedging around the talc trial calendar, but nothing signals acute stress. The options market corroborates the fundamental read: a low-volatility compounder with a slow-burning tail risk rather than an imminent binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($290–$305):** The pipeline continues to outrun the LOE cliff, DARZALEX/TREMFYA/CAPLYTA sustain double-digit growth, cardiovascular MedTech compounds, the Orthopaedics separation unlocks a cleaner higher-growth RemainCo, and talc trials produce a steady stream of defense verdicts that let the market fade the tail. On ~$13.50+ of 2027 adjusted EPS at a 22–23x multiple, the stock reaches the low-$300s. This requires both operational execution and favorable litigation drift.

**Bear case ($190–$210):** An adverse general-causation ruling or a cluster of large plaintiff verdicts forces a reserve rebuild materially above $3.4B; the IRA Supreme Court case goes against J&J, deepening the price headwind; STELARA/SIMPONI erosion accelerates faster than launches offset. The multiple compresses toward the peer median (20x) or below on ~$11.60 EPS, taking the stock to the low-$200s or the analyst-low $190. This is roughly 15–25% downside from current levels.

**Base case ($260–$270):** Mid-single-digit adjusted EPS growth, guidance delivered, dividend raised again, talc grinding through the tort system without a catastrophic verdict but without full resolution. The multiple holds near 21–22x on ~$12.20 blended forward EPS, plus the 2.1% dividend, roughly 3–7% price upside over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Timeframe: 12 months.**

- **Base case target: $265.** ~21.5x on a blended forward adjusted EPS of ~$12.30, implying ~5% price upside plus a 2.1% dividend.
- **Bull case target: $300** (+18%): pipeline strength plus a benign litigation path support 22–23x on rising 2027 estimates.
- **Bear case target: $200** (−21%): talc reserve rebuild and/or adverse IRA ruling drive multiple compression to the peer median.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A pullback to the low-$220s or below on unchanged fundamentals, restoring the ~10%+ asymmetry to base case (forward P/E toward 19x).
2. Material talc de-risking: a favorable general-causation ruling on appeal, or a sustained run of defense verdicts that lets the market discount the plaintiff docket.
3. Accelerating estimate revisions (>5% over 90 days) confirming the pipeline is compounding faster than modeled.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. An adverse general-causation or bellwether outcome forcing a reserve rebuild materially above $3.4B.
2. An IRA Supreme Court loss combined with accelerating STELARA/SIMPONI erosion that pushes adjusted EPS growth below low-single digits.
3. A dividend-coverage deterioration or a large debt-funded acquisition that stretches the balance sheet beyond conservative levels.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (Q3 2026, ~October), plus the 2026 talc trial calendar and the IRA cert decision. The talc docket is the single most important monitorable and warrants attention at each verdict.

## Management Quality

CEO Joaquin Duato's FY2025 total comp was $32.8M, with "comp actually paid" spiking to $68.3M, a mechanical function of the stock's ~47% total return and PSU vesting, not a discretionary raise. The compensation framework (operational sales, 3-year adjusted operational EPS, relative TSR, FCF) is reasonably aligned. The more meaningful governance data point is the persistent five-year TSR lag versus peers, negative on the company-selected relative-TSR metric in three of the last five years. Board quality is high (Doudna, McClellan, Hewson, Pinto, Joly). Capital allocation has been disciplined (buybacks at sensible levels, a 63-year dividend record, pipeline-focused M&A) though the acquisition cadence is aggressive and has more than doubled net debt. Insider activity is routine only: option exercises paired with sales, RSU vesting, director deferred-share grants, no open-market purchases and no red-flag selling. That neutral signal, combined with the TSR lag, supports a 5.5: a competent, aligned management that has not delivered differentiated returns.

## Conclusion

J&J is a high-quality, defensively-positioned healthcare compounder with a demonstrably self-renewing pharma pipeline, a fortress balance sheet, and $19.7B of durable free cash flow. The operating thesis, new launches outrunning the STELARA cliff, is intact and confirmed by the July 2026 guidance raise. What has changed is price: the stock rose 55% over twelve months and now trades at a premium to its peer median with a below-peer dividend yield, leaving little cushion against the largely-unreserved talc tort docket and a live IRA Supreme Court case.

The prior BUY captured the re-rating; that asymmetry is spent. This is a HOLD for the mid-single-digit compounding and the 2.1% covered dividend, neither a business to avoid nor one offering asymmetric upside at ~$253. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the low-$220s, on meaningful talc de-risking, or on an acceleration in estimate revisions. Absent those, the risk/reward is roughly balanced, and the four-read downtrend in our scoring reflects valuation catching up to a fundamentally sound but fully-priced business.
