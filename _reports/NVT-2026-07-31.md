## Executive Summary

nVent has become one of the purest mid-cap expressions of the electrical build-out behind AI data centers, and the underlying operating data now confirm it. Q1 2026 revenue rose 53.5% to $1,242.0M with organic growth of 34.4%; the Systems Protection segment (enclosures, liquid cooling, switchgear, control buildings) grew organically 50.1%, of which roughly 46 points came from the infrastructure vertical. Infrastructure went from 39.1% to 55.8% of revenue in one year. Q2, reported July 31 and known to me only through third-party coverage, added revenue of approximately $1.47B against $1.26B consensus and adjusted EPS of $1.45 against $1.16, a 24.6% beat, with management citing growth "across every vertical and every geography" and improving short-cycle demand. FY26 organic guidance has moved from 10-13% (February) to 21-23% (May) to 32-34% (July), with adjusted EPS guidance rising from $4.00-4.15 to $5.00-5.10.

The price action between June 22 ($184.34 close, $184.64 intraday high) and July 29 ($133.61) was a 28% derating with no adverse company disclosure in between. That decline coincided with the broader AI-capex scare, higher long-end Treasury yields [unverified], and profit-taking after a roughly fourfold move off late-2025 levels. The Q2 print rebutted the demand-deceleration leg of that repricing and the stock recovered 15% in two sessions. Lower price on better fundamentals improves the risk/reward, which is why my valuation score rises from 4.5 to 5.5 and the overall score from 6.5 to 6.6.

What stops this from becoming a BUY is the arithmetic of what remains. At $153.83 the stock trades at 30.2-30.8x the FY26 adjusted-EPS guide and ~26.8x an FY27 consensus of $5.74 that is still being revised upward. A 28x multiple on $6.00 of FY27 EPS, above consensus and consistent with current revision velocity, produces roughly $168, or about 9% upside. The bear case is not symmetric: gross margin has compressed 290bp with management newly citing "unfavorable product mix," the historically premium Electrical Connections segment saw margin fall 390bp to 24.4%, goodwill and intangibles are 66% of total assets against a disclosed impairment cushion of only 10%, and the largest customer was 11% of FY25 sales in a mix that is tilting toward a small number of very large infrastructure buyers. If order growth normalizes to high single digits, a 21x multiple on $5.00 gives $105. Base +9%, bear -32%, entering two days after a 15% bounce with near-term implied volatility of 64.9% against 45.3% realized. That is a hold.

## Company Overview

nVent designs and manufactures electrical connection and protection products — bus systems, cable management, control buildings, air and liquid cooling, enclosures, power connections and management, and switchgear. It was spun from Pentair in April 2018, is incorporated in Ireland with U.K. tax residency, is managed from London with a U.S. office in Minneapolis, and employed approximately 12,000 people at December 31, 2025, about 48% in the U.S.

Two segments, renamed in 2025:

| Segment | FY2025 revenue | % total | Q1'26 revenue | % Q1'26 | Q1'26 organic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Systems Protection (formerly Enclosures) | $2,592.9M | 67% | $894.8M | 72% | +50.1% |
| Electrical Connections | $1,300.2M | 33% | $347.2M | 28% | +7.9% |

Four transactions in three years reshaped the portfolio: ECM Industries (May 2023, ~$1.1B), Trachte (July 2024, ~$0.7B), the sale of Thermal Management to Brookfield-managed funds (January 2025, $1.7B price / $1,586.8M net proceeds), and the purchase of Electrical Products Group from Avail Infrastructure Solutions (May 2025, $979.6M cash). Thermal Management is treated as discontinued operations for all periods, which is why FY2024 continuing revenue of $3,006.1M sits below previously reported FY2023 totals — a presentation artifact rather than a demand decline. The net effect of the divest-and-buy sequence was to exit a slower-growth thermal business and add enclosures, switchgear and bus systems into the segment now selling to hyperscalers, utilities and OEMs.

Approximately 24% of FY2025 sales were outside the U.S.; Q1'26 Americas revenue was $1,050.2M of $1,242.0M (84.6%).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** On the only two internally consistent comparisons available, FY2025 revenue rose 29.5% to $3,893.1M (organic +12.6%, acquisition +16.3%, currency +0.6%) and Q1'26 rose 53.5% to $1,242.0M (organic +34.4%, acquisition +17.0%, currency +2.1%). Vertical detail for Q1'26: Infrastructure $316.8M → $693.4M (+118.9%), Industrial $278.9M → $317.3M (+13.8%), Commercial & Residential $213.6M → $231.3M (+8.3%). nVent changed its vertical disaggregation categories in Q1'26 and recategorized the prior-year quarter, so the comparison is internally consistent within the 10-Q but does not tie to FY2025 10-K splits.

Adding the externally reported Q2 figure of ~$1.47B, H1'26 revenue of roughly $2.71B compares with an implied H1'25 of about $1.84B, or +47%. A full-year figure in the $5.4-5.6B range is consistent with the guided 32-34% organic plus EPG carryover through the May 1 anniversary and roughly 1.5-2 points of currency.

**Margins — the central tension.** Gross margin is compressing on every disclosed comparison:

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'25 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 40.2% | 37.7% | 38.8% | 35.9% |
| SG&A % sales | 20.5% | 19.9% | 20.5% | 18.3% |
| R&D % sales | 2.2% | 2.0% | 2.2% | 1.8% |
| Operating margin | 17.5% | 15.8% | 16.1% | 15.8% |

Management attributes the erosion to tariff-inclusive inflation in raw materials and labor, capacity investment, and — new in Q1'26 — unfavorable product mix. Operating margin held far better than gross margin because SG&A leverage absorbed most of the hit, but that offset is volume-dependent while the gross-margin drivers (steel, copper, aluminum, tariffs, ramp costs, mix) are not.

The segment split is the more informative disclosure. Systems Protection margin expanded 220bp to 22.7% on 76.1% revenue growth — genuine operating leverage in the data-center-exposed business. Electrical Connections fell 390bp to 24.4%, from 30.0% in FY2024 and 28.3% in Q1'25. Two readings are equally supported: mix is shifting toward a segment that scales well but at a structurally lower margin than the legacy connections franchise, and/or Q1 is the seasonal trough for Electrical Connections (the 10-K discloses demand peaks in Northern Hemisphere spring and summer) with front-loaded digital/selling investment. FY2025 showed a third pattern — both segments down, Systems Protection down more (-140bp vs -130bp) — which argues against a clean structural narrative. Q2 and Q3 segment margins are the resolving data.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 net income of $710.2M is not a recurring figure. Income before taxes from continuing operations was $550.0M, tax $121.5M (22.1% ETR), continuing net income $428.5M; discontinued operations contributed $281.7M (a $435.3M pre-tax disposal gain less $152.7M of tax). Continuing-operations net margin was therefore 11.0%, not the 18.2% a headline screen produces, and headline ROE of ~19% overstates recurring returns by roughly the same proportion. FY2024's 43.9% ETR was itself distorted by $92.8M of non-cash Luxembourg valuation-allowance expense, so the year-over-year "tax improvement" is mostly the absence of a prior-year charge. Any normalized multi-year earnings series requires adjusting both years.

Cash conversion is genuinely good once measured on the right base: FY2025 continuing-operations OCF of $649.0M exceeded continuing net income of $428.5M by $220.5M (1.51x), and company-defined FCF of $561.0M was 1.31x continuing net income.

**Cash flow.**

| ($M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'25 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OCF, continuing | 501.0 | 649.0 | 63.9 | 89.9 |
| Capex | (74.0) | (93.3) | (21.1) | (36.1) |
| FCF (company definition) | 427.5 | 561.0 | 44.4 | 53.8 |

Q1 should not be annualized. It absorbed a $128.3M working-capital build — receivables $138.4M, inventories $42.6M, contract assets/liabilities $38.4M — plus a $50.7M seasonal bonus payout. If FY2026 repeats FY2025's ~14.4% OCF-to-sales ratio on ~$5.5B of revenue, OCF approximates $790M; against capex at Q1's elevated run rate (~$190-200M, including the new liquid-cooling site), FCF lands near $590-600M. That is a scaling exercise, not a forecast: receivable growth on long-cycle projects and cash taxes are both unresolved, and the receivables line is where a project-heavy mix shows up first.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt at 3/31/26 was $1,564.7M against $190.0M cash, for net debt of $1,374.7M. FY2025 EBITDA of roughly $824.6M (operating income $616.8M plus depreciation $60.7M and amortization $147.1M) gives net debt/EBITDA of about 1.67x, against covenants of ≤3.75x (flexing to 4.25x on material acquisitions) and ≥3.00x EBITDA/interest; compliance is affirmed at both 12/31/25 and 3/31/26. The revolver ($600M, expandable by $300M) is fully undrawn. Maturities are back-ended: $10.3M for the rest of 2026, $13.8M in 2027, then $517.2M in 2028 (the 4.550% notes), with $300M 2.750% due 2031, $500M 5.650% due 2033, and a $264.7M term loan at 4.926% due 2030. Current ratio 1.70x.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.46 is a composition artifact. Goodwill ($2,675.5M) plus intangibles ($1,834.7M) equal 66% of total assets, which mechanically depresses asset turnover (0.568) and EBIT/assets (0.090). With 1.67x leverage, an undrawn revolver and $24M of debt due over the next 21 months, solvency risk is low. The same asset structure is a real impairment exposure and a permanent GAAP drag: amortization was $94.7M in FY2024, $147.1M in FY2025, $41.1M in Q1'26 alone with $112.7M scheduled for Q2-Q4 and $136.3M in 2027.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025: $873.3M of gross debt repayment (long-term debt balance down $571.5M after a $275.0M new term-loan draw), $253.1M of buybacks (4.8M shares), $130.4M of dividends, $979.6M for EPG funded from divestiture proceeds. Q1'26: $50.4M of buybacks, $34.2M of dividends. Share count moved from 165.5M basic (FY24) to 161.7M issued at 3/31/26. The dividend was raised to $0.21/quarter in February 2026 ($0.84 annualized, ~0.55% yield, ~17% of the adjusted-EPS guide).

Two constraints worth monitoring: only $96.5M remained of the $500M May 2024 buyback authorization at 3/31/26 (roughly two quarters at the Q1 pace, expiring July 2027, with no new authorization disclosed), and under Irish law distributions come only from nVent Electric plc's statutory distributable reserves, which fell from $2.4B to $2.1B during 2025. At ~$385M of annual distributions that is about five years of headroom — a monitoring item, not a thesis risk, but the direction of travel matters.

## Growth Analysis

The guidance sequence is the most striking piece of evidence available:

| Guidance date | FY26 organic sales growth | FY26 adjusted EPS |
|---|---|---|
| February 2026 | 10-13% (reported 15-18%) | $4.00-4.15 |
| May 2026 | 21-23% (reported 26-28%) | $4.45-4.55 |
| July 2026 | 32-34% | $5.00-5.10 |

The organic guide roughly tripled in five months and the EPS midpoint rose 24.5%. The earnings surprise record corroborates the acceleration: -0.6% (Q4'25), +1.9% (Q3'25), +14.7% (Q1'26), +23.8% (Q2'26). Beat magnitude is expanding, not stable.

Consensus is visibly lagging. FY2026 consensus sits at $4.61 (up 10.2% over 90 days but only 0.4% over 30 days) against a company guide of $5.00-5.10; summing reported Q1 ($1.09) and Q2 ($1.45) actuals with current-quarter ($1.18) and next-quarter ($1.15) estimates already produces $4.87. FY2027 consensus of $5.74 has risen 15.8% in 90 days. Mechanical upward revisions to both years are likely over the next several weeks. This is a positive revision signal, but it falls short of the +30%/90-day threshold that would carry standalone weight, and it arrives on a stock at 30x forward rather than at a trough multiple.

Order-book measures need definitional care. The FY2025 10-K discloses backlog of $2,349.9M at 12/31/25 versus $749.3M a year earlier (+213.6%), attributed "primarily" to the EPG acquisition and data-center growth — the year-over-year percentage is not organic order growth, since YE2024 contained no EPG. The Q1'26 10-Q separately discloses remaining performance obligations of $2,591.0M, with the majority expected within twelve months. Third-party coverage describing a "record backlog of $2.6 billion, up low double digits sequentially" most plausibly maps to that RPO figure. ASC 606 RPO and the company's Item 1 backlog definition are different constructs; the useful signal is the sequential increase on an EPG-inclusive base, not the headline year-over-year.

Three structural supports for durability beyond a single AI theme. First, breadth: Q1 Industrial +13.8% and Commercial & Residential +8.3% grew alongside Infrastructure's +118.9%, and management's Q2 commentary cited stronger short-cycle demand — consistent with Honeywell and Illinois Tool Works also raising on short-cycle orders. Second, capacity: a new liquid-cooling site is slated to begin production in H1 2027 with 200+ employees, which validates both the demand read and the capex-driven margin drag through 2026. Third, management's own framing that growth occurred "across every vertical and every geography" is corroborated by the breadth of Q1 vertical growth, meaning nVent's growth is not resting on a single-company data-center story.

The counter-argument is embedded in the same evidence. A guide that triples in five months indicates management had limited visibility into its own demand curve. The forecasting process that understated 2026 by roughly 22 points of organic growth can overstate 2027, and project-heavy businesses with hyperscaler exposure have historically erred in both directions. All Q2 figures and the July guide are third-party reported; I have not read the Q2 press release or 10-Q.

On stocks at or below 30x forward with rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag; that logic applies here only partially, since 30.2x on the FY26 guide sits at the boundary of where analyst targets stop being independent of the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price. Recommendation distribution is 1 strong buy / 15 buy / 1 hold, and a prior strong-sell was removed — a crowded-long sell side, which limits the upgrade catalyst available from ratings changes.

Valuation score of 5.5, raised from 4.5. The improvement is real and mechanical: the stock is roughly 8% below the level of my prior review while the FY26 adjusted-EPS guide is 11% higher and FY27 consensus is 15.8% higher, taking the forward multiple from roughly 37x to 30x. It is not higher than 5.5 because base-case upside of ~9% against a 30% bear tail is unattractive asymmetry, and because at 21.7x EV/EBITDA the equity requires the current order cycle to persist through 2027 to defend the multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

nVent describes itself as "an enclosures and liquid cooling leader in the U.S. and globally," selling into hyperscalers, utilities, OEMs, panel builders and contractors. The 10-K discloses no market-share figures and explicitly disclaims dependence on any single patent, technology, trademark or brand. Stated differentiators are application engineering, quality and delivery reliability, installation history, new-product velocity, and channel depth across electrical distributors, retail, contractors and OEMs.

That has two consequences. Q1's +50.1% Systems Protection organic growth, with 46 points from infrastructure, is strong circumstantial evidence of at least full participation in a rapidly expanding pool. Whether it represents share gain depends on the underlying market growth rate, which I cannot establish — no independent share data for liquid cooling versus Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider, ABB or Hubbell was obtainable. Growing 50% in a market growing 50% is participation; growing 50% in a market growing 25% is share capture. The distinction matters for how much of the current multiple is defensible.

Structural observations that bear on durability. Regional manufacturing and supply chains are an advantage under a tariff regime, consistent with the disclosure that 2025 tariffs did not materially affect results "due to mitigating actions" despite sourcing from Mexico and China. Channel depth is defensible but slow-compounding and sits awkwardly beside an 11%-of-sales largest customer. The business model is migrating from short-cycle book-and-ship — historically a majority of revenue shipped in the same month as ordered — toward long-cycle engineered projects via Trachte control buildings and EPG switchgear/bus systems, with infrastructure lead times exceeding a year. The balance-sheet fingerprints of that migration are visible: performance bonds, letters of credit and bank guarantees rose from $10.7M (12/31/24) to $75.7M (12/31/25) to $82.6M (3/31/26), and contract assets of $187.9M now appear. Longer-cycle work trades revenue visibility for fixed-price bid risk, subcontractor dependence and working-capital intensity.

A reported Siemens reference architecture for NVIDIA AI data centers that names nVent components is a specification signal of unquantified revenue value from a single source. It does not establish a contract with, or supply relationship to, either party, and no company filing corroborates it.

Competitive position 6.5: trajectory is clearly improving in the segment that matters most (76% growth with 220bp of margin expansion), but the moat is channel- and application-based rather than IP-based, the premium legacy segment is deteriorating on margin, and share gain cannot be distinguished from market participation.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, direction of travel unfavorable.** The largest customer was approximately 11% of FY2025 consolidated sales, identity undisclosed. Systems Protection explicitly sells to hyperscalers, and a shifting vertical mix says nothing about buyer breadth; the plausible read is that concentration rose in 2026. If the 11% account is a broadline electrical distributor it represents thousands of downstream end users and is far less fragile than a single hyperscaler. That cannot be determined from the filings. The FY2026 10-K disclosure is the key checkpoint.

**Margin compression explicitly expected to persist.** The 10-K states that 2025 tariffs were mitigated but that inflationary and tariff-related cost increases are expected "to continue in 2026 and beyond." Raw materials are mild and stainless steel, copper, aluminum, electronic components and paint. A newly emphasized element is the U.S. Trade Fraud Task Force and pending IEEPA tariff litigation heard by the Supreme Court — two-sided, since an adverse ruling for the government could reduce tariff costs while enforcement raises compliance exposure. I have not verified any ruling.

**Goodwill and intangibles at 66% of assets.** Impairment testing uses a uniform 9.5% discount rate and 3.0-3.5% terminal growth over a six-year forecast, with the disclosed sensitivity showing that a 10% decrease in reporting-unit fair values would not have changed the 2025 no-impairment conclusion. That is a thin cushion for reporting units carrying cycle-peak data-center growth assumptions, and the newest goodwill (EPG $439.6M, Trachte) is where write-down risk concentrates. The EPG purchase-price allocation remains preliminary with the company warning finalization could produce material changes; internal-control integration work for EPG is ongoing with no material weakness reported.

**Project execution and order timing.** Management's own risk language: backlog may swing on large multi-year projects, delivery timing is subject to factors beyond its control, and delays "may lead to significant fluctuations in results of operations from quarter to quarter." Fixed-price contract overruns above bid assumptions can produce losses.

**Guidance volatility in both directions.** The same planning process that understated FY26 organic growth by ~22 points can overstate FY27.

**Structural and jurisdictional items.** $75.1M of the $190.0M cash (39.5%) sits in jurisdictions where repatriation is limited by regulation or tax cost. The February 2026 supplemental indenture added guarantors whose combined non-guarantor-adjusted balance sheet holds $22.8M of current assets and $1,600.0M of noncurrent assets (of which $1,592.1M is intercompany receivable) against $1,652.4M of liabilities — standard structure, limited incremental asset coverage. Pillar II's 15% minimum tax has already raised the effective rate.

Risk factors 5.0. Sector cyclicality is priced; what is less priced is the combination of a 66%-intangible balance sheet with a 10% valuation cushion, worsening customer concentration in a mix now dominated by a handful of very large buyers, and demonstrated equity sensitivity — a 28% decline in five weeks on no company news, then a 15% recovery in two sessions.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is bullish and near-term volatility is expensive. ATM IV fell from 80.5% (July 29) to 64.9% (July 31) as the stock rose 15.1%, leaving near-term IV 19.6 points above the 45.3% one-year realized. The term structure is backwardated (64.9% at one month versus 61.1% at six months), and the nearest expiry implies a ±12.3% move. Put/call open-interest ratio halved from 1.01 to 0.50, the volume ratio is 0.45, skew is call-heavy at -4.2%, and the single flagged unusual trade is 1,227 August $175 calls against 309 open interest (4.0x) at 13.8% out of the money.

Options positioning is bullish while I am neutral. That is consistent with post-earnings momentum chasing rather than informed hedging, and the rich near-term IV means an option-based entry is expensive right now. Short interest of 2.8% of float with 1.8 days to cover confirms there is no crowded bear position and therefore no squeeze mechanic to supply upside. For a holder, elevated call premium makes covered-call overwriting reasonably attractive; for a buyer, the backwardation says the market expects continued large moves over the next month, which argues for patience.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $205 (+33%)

Data-center electrical demand is confirmed rather than speculative by nVent's own order book (RPO $2,591.0M, up low double digits sequentially) and the externally reported Q2 revenue beat of ~17%. Systems Protection is compounding at 70-76% with margin expanding 220bp, demonstrating that the growth is profitable rather than bought. The new liquid-cooling plant starting production in H1 2027 adds capacity into a market where nVent is specification-relevant. Growth is broadening — Industrial +13.8%, Commercial & Residential +8.3%, and management citing stronger short-cycle demand across all verticals and geographies — which lengthens the runway beyond a single theme. Consensus FY27 EPS of $5.74 is stale against a company already guiding $5.00-5.10 for FY26 and beating by 24%; $6.40 in FY27 at 32x produces $205, near the top analyst target of $220. Leverage of 1.67x, an undrawn $600M revolver and $24M of maturities through 2027 mean the balance sheet is not a constraint on funding the ramp.

### Bear Case — $105-115 (-27% to -32%)

The equity is a levered claim on hyperscaler order flow at what may be peak growth. Gross margin has fallen 290bp with management now naming unfavorable mix as a driver, and the historically premium Electrical Connections segment lost 390bp of margin — if that does not recover in its seasonally strong quarters, the consolidated story is considerably less benign than the 30bp operating-margin decline implies. Customer concentration is 11% and probably rising. The migration to long-cycle project work has raised performance-security instruments sevenfold and consumed $128.3M of working capital in one quarter, so reported growth converts to cash more slowly than it used to. Goodwill and intangibles at 66% of assets rest on a uniform 9.5% discount rate with a 10% cushion; an order-growth reversal produces both estimate cuts and impairment headlines in the same quarter. Insiders sold ~$14.9M in a nine-day window in May at $165-174 with no offsetting open-market purchases. Buyback capacity is down to $96.5M and requires a new authorization. On $5.00 of FY27 EPS at 21x — a multiple this stock traded at as recently as late 2025 — the price is $105.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive but the base-case return does not compensate for the downside distribution at this entry point.

- **Base case $168 (+9%):** 28x on FY27 EPS of $6.00, above the $5.74 consensus and consistent with current revision velocity, assuming organic growth decelerates from 33% toward the mid-teens.
- **Bull case $205 (+33%):** 32x on $6.40.
- **Bear case $110 (-28%):** 21x on $5.00.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 print (late October 2026), the Q4/FY26 report and FY27 initial guide (February 2027), and the H1 2027 liquid-cooling plant start-up.

**Upgrade triggers (any two would move this to BUY):**
1. Price at or below ~$130, which is roughly 22-23x FY27 consensus and ~26x the FY26 guide, with RPO still rising sequentially. The July 29 low of $130.97 was within a session of that level.
2. Electrical Connections segment margin recovering toward 27%+ in the Q2/Q3 filings and consolidated gross-margin decline narrowing to 100bp or less year over year — this would resolve the mix-versus-seasonality question in favor of seasonality.
3. FY2026 10-K disclosing largest-customer concentration at or below 11%, plus a new buyback authorization of at least $500M, confirming that growth is broadening across buyers and that capital return continues.

**Downgrade triggers (any one would move this to AVOID):**
1. An FY26 or FY27 organic growth guide below the mid-teens, or Systems Protection organic growth decelerating below ~20%.

## Conclusion

nVent is a well-financed operator executing well in a demand environment that its own planning process failed to anticipate. The fundamental trajectory has improved since my last review: Q2 revenue and adjusted EPS beat by ~17% and ~25%, FY26 organic guidance moved to 32-34% from 21-23%, FY27 consensus is up 15.8% in 90 days, net leverage is 1.67x, and FY25 free cash flow of $561M converted at 1.31x continuing net income. The 28% drawdown from the June high was multiple compression driven by AI-capex sentiment, not company deterioration, and the Q2 print refuted the demand-slowdown leg of that repricing. That combination of lower price and higher forward earnings is why the valuation score rises from 4.5 to 5.5 and the overall score to 6.6.

What holds back an upgrade is the residual asymmetry. At 30x the FY26 adjusted guide and 21.7x EV/EBITDA, the price already requires the current order cycle to persist into 2027. Base-case upside of roughly 9% sits against a bear case 28% lower, and the specific unresolved items are consequential: a 290bp gross-margin decline that management now attributes partly to mix, a legacy segment losing 390bp, an 11% largest customer in a mix tilting toward a handful of very large infrastructure buyers, and 66% of assets in goodwill and intangibles carried against a 10% valuation cushion. Adding after a 15% two-day rebound, with near-term implied volatility 19.6 points above realized and a backwardated term structure, is poor entry timing on an otherwise sound franchise.

Hold the position. Add on a retest of the $125-135 area with order momentum intact, or on Q2/Q3 evidence that the Electrical Connections margin decline was seasonal rather than structural. Reduce on any organic-guidance cut or sequential RPO decline.
