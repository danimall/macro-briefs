## Executive Summary

California Resources Corporation is the dominant oil and gas producer in California, operating across the San Joaquin, Los Angeles, and Sacramento basins with 138 MBoe/d of production (78% oil) as of year-end 2025. The company has tripled in size through two acquisitions—Aera (mid-2024) and Berry (December 2025)—and now holds 654 MMBoe in proved reserves. CRC also operates the Elk Hills power plant and is developing a pre-revenue carbon capture and sequestration (CCS) business through its Carbon TerraVault subsidiary, backed by a $500M Brookfield joint venture.

The stock has nearly doubled from its April 2025 lows of ~$34 to $66.87, driven by rising oil prices and the successful integration narrative. At ~15.6x trailing earnings and ~12.5x the current-year consensus estimate of $5.35, CRC trades roughly in line with peer medians but at a substantial discount to the analyst consensus price target of $81.50 (median $84). The investment case rests on: (1) 12% production growth into 2026 from the Berry integration, (2) California crude pricing that realizes a premium to WTI due to the state's structural oil import deficit, (3) $600M in remaining buyback authorization, and (4) free CCS optionality. The primary risks are California's regulatory environment, a $125-135M electricity revenue headwind in 2026, elevated post-acquisition leverage (Altman Z 1.27), and the speculative nature of the CCS business.

## Company Overview

CRC emerged from bankruptcy in October 2020 after being spun off from Occidental Petroleum in 2014. It operates almost exclusively in California, where it holds a near-monopoly position as the state's largest oil and gas producer. The company runs a vertically integrated model: it produces heavy and light crude oil, operates the Elk Hills power plant (both for internal use and external electricity sales), and is developing CCS projects leveraging its extensive knowledge of depleted subsurface reservoirs.

Through the Aera merger (closed July 2024) and the Berry merger (closed December 18, 2025), CRC consolidated California's fragmented E&P landscape. Production grew from ~86 MBoe/d in 2023 to 138 MBoe/d in 2025, with 2026 guidance of 152-157 MBoe/d. The Berry deal also brought CRC its first non-California assets in Utah's Uinta Basin (~15 MBoe/d), though management has indicated these assets "will have to compete for capital" and divestiture remains a possibility.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** Revenue grew 15% in 2025 to $2.91B, driven by the full-year Aera contribution and 14 days of Berry production. However, net income declined to $363M from $376M in 2024 and $564M in 2023. The margin compression is structural: operating costs per Boe rose from $24.51 to $25.42, DD&A jumped from $8.83 to $9.77/Boe as acquired assets carry higher book values, and interest expense increased $19M to $106M from acquisition-related debt. The reported operating margin of -7.2% is misleading—it reflects non-cash items and acquisition costs—but the directional trend of declining profitability per barrel despite rising production is a genuine concern.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $865M and FCF of $543M represent an 18.7% FCF margin, which is solid for E&P. Operating cash flow exceeds net income, indicating good earnings quality. Capital expenditures of $322M in 2025 will rise to $430-470M in 2026 as CRC drills 260 wells (up from 76 in 2025), partially absorbing the production growth benefit.

**Balance Sheet:** Long-term debt stands at $1.28B ($900M at 8.25% due 2029, $400M at 7.00% due 2034), with no near-term maturities. Total liquidity is adequate at $1.4B ($117M cash plus $1.28B revolver availability). However, the debt-to-equity ratio of 2.01x and Altman Z-Score of 1.27 signal elevated leverage. The current ratio of 0.89 is below 1.0, meaning current liabilities exceed current assets. Asset retirement obligations of $1.03B represent a long-tail liability; a 1% increase in inflation assumptions would add $94M.

**Capital Return:** CRC has been an aggressive capital returner, spending $377M on buybacks in 2025 at an average price of $45.29—well below the current stock price. Cumulative buybacks since May 2021 total $1.17B, reducing the share count from over 100M to ~89M. The dividend was raised 5% to $1.62/share annually (2.5% yield), and $600M in buyback authorization remains through December 2027.

## Growth Analysis

**Production Growth:** 2026 guidance of 152-157 MBoe/d represents ~12% growth over 2025, almost entirely from the Berry integration. The 4-rig development program targets entry-to-exit production decline of ~2% (versus ~8% natural decline), with an emphasis on workovers and sidetracks in H1 transitioning to new wells as permit inventory builds under SB 237. The resumption of new well permitting in Kern County (31 permits issued as of January 31, 2026) is a critical enabler.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates have surged from $2.21 ninety days ago to $5.35 today—a 142.5% upward revision. Next-quarter estimates rose 263.5% over 30 days to $1.52. This revision velocity reflects analysts incorporating the Berry production contribution and the elevated oil price environment. The magnitude of the revisions suggests the street was slow to model the combined entity, meaning current estimates may still be conservative.

**Headwinds:** The $125-135M decline in electricity resource adequacy contract revenue in 2026 is material—roughly 4-5% of total operating revenue. With 2025 electricity margin at approximately $195M, this represents a ~60-65% hit to electricity-segment profitability. After tax, this could reduce FCF by $90-100M, partially offsetting the production growth benefit.

**CCS Optionality:** The Carbon TerraVault segment has consumed $246M in cumulative losses with zero revenue. First injection at the Elk Hills cryogenic gas facility is expected in spring 2026, but revenue generation depends on Section 45Q tax credits and California's Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS) credits. The current administration has actively reduced federal CCS grant availability, creating policy risk. Environmental litigation (filed November 2024) adds further uncertainty. This segment contributes optionality but should be valued at zero until revenue is demonstrated.

## Valuation Assessment

CRC trades at ~15.6x trailing EPS of $4.15 and ~12.5x the current-year consensus of $5.35. Against the peer median trailing P/E of 12.7x (CNX 9.2x, MGY 12.7x, CRK 17.4x, SM 3.3x, MUR 43.4x), CRC's valuation is reasonable. The forward multiple of 12.5x is modestly below the peer median when adjusted for CRC's superior production growth trajectory.

The analyst consensus target of $81.50 (range $72-$92) implies 22% upside from the current price of $66.87. With 11 of 12 analysts rating the stock Buy or Strong Buy and zero Sells, the street consensus is firmly bullish. The 2.5% dividend yield is approximately in line with the peer median of 2.1%.

On a FCF basis, $543M in 2025 FCF against a $5.9B market cap yields 9.2%—attractive for an E&P company, though 2026 FCF will be pressured by higher capex and the electricity revenue headwind. Assuming $950M operating cash flow and $450M capex, 2026 FCF of ~$500M would still yield ~8.5%.

The California regulatory discount is embedded in the valuation—CRC's proxy-reported TSR of 211% significantly trails its peer group's 299%. If permitting conditions continue improving and the Uinta Basin assets are monetized or developed, this discount could narrow.

## Competitive Landscape

CRC occupies a unique and defensible niche. As California's largest oil producer in a state that imports approximately 70% of its crude oil needs, CRC's production commands a premium of roughly 2-4% above WTI. The Aera and Berry acquisitions have effectively consolidated the California E&P landscape—few competitors of scale remain, giving CRC operational control and pricing influence.

The state's progressive refinery closures (including the San Pablo Bay Pipeline shutdown) constrain CRC's marketing flexibility but simultaneously tighten local supply and support the crude premium. The structural supply deficit in California means CRC's barrels are more valuable per unit than those produced in the Permian or Bakken.

In CCS, CRC has first-mover advantage with extensive subsurface geological data from decades of E&P operations. The Brookfield JV provides committed capital. However, CCS remains entirely pre-revenue, and competing projects nationally could erode CRC's positioning if 45Q credits are preserved.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory Concentration (Critical):** CRC's near-complete dependence on California operations is the defining risk. SB 237 has eased permitting constraints, but the 10-K warns that "future legislative, regulatory, administrative or judicial actions" could again restrict permitting. California's long-term trajectory is hostile to fossil fuel production. Any reversal of permitting progress would directly impair CRC's 260-well development plan.

**Commodity Price Sensitivity:** Brent averaged $68.22 in 2025, down from $79.84 in 2024. CRC's realized oil price fell $10.40/bbl YoY. The current elevated oil price environment supports near-term earnings, but a normalization toward $70-75 oil would compress margins on CRC's higher-cost, steam-intensive California operations.

**Integration Risk:** The Berry merger closed less than four months ago. Integration of Berry's California and Utah operations, systems harmonization, and workforce optimization are ongoing. A director departure (8-K filed March 16, 2026) and the March 23 financing activity warrant monitoring for integration stress signals.

**Electricity Revenue Cliff:** The guided decline in resource adequacy revenue is a concrete, quantified headwind that will flow directly through 2026 results and is under-discussed relative to its magnitude.

**Leverage:** Post-acquisition debt of $1.28B with an Altman Z of 1.27 and D/E of 2.01x leaves limited margin for error if oil prices decline materially. The 8.25% coupon on the 2029 notes is expensive.

**Insider Activity:** Canada Pension Plan Investment Board sold 3.5M shares ($214M) at $61.10 on March 12, 2026, reducing its position by one-third. Multiple executives sold shares in early March at $62-66, consistent with equity grant vesting and tax withholding rather than conviction-driven selling. No open-market purchases by any insiders were identified.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in steep backwardation (75.6% near-term → 43.4% at 3 months), indicating the market is pricing a near-term catalyst or event risk. However, the near-term IV richness means entry via options is expensive, and the backwardation pattern implies the market expects the current volatility regime to be temporary. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($85-92)
- Oil prices sustained above $90 on continued supply disruptions, driving CRC's realized prices higher. At $90+ oil, CRC's 2026 EPS could reach $6.50-7.00, making the stock trade at ~10x.
- Berry integration proceeds smoothly; the Uinta Basin assets are divested at an accretive valuation, deleveraging the balance sheet.
- SB 237 permitting continues or accelerates, enabling the full 260-well program and beyond.
- CCS achieves first injection in spring 2026, providing a tangible milestone that de-risks the segment and attracts incremental investor interest.
- $600M buyback authorization at current run rate removes 8-10% of float over 12-18 months.

### Bear Case ($45-50)
- Oil prices retreat to $65-70 on de-escalation of Middle East tensions and OPEC+ supply increases. At sub-$70 oil, CRC's cost structure (steam-intensive, high operating costs) compresses margins severely.
- California regulatory environment reverses: permitting delays resume, refinery closures accelerate, and the crude premium narrows.
- Berry integration costs exceed expectations; Uinta Basin assets underperform.
- CCS milestones are delayed by litigation and federal policy changes; Brookfield exercises put rights on 26R reservoir.
- Electricity revenue cliff fully materializes without offsetting operational improvements.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $80** — 15x forward P/E on $5.35 FY2026E EPS, or equivalently, ~13x a modestly elevated FY2026E of $6.15 if oil averages $85+ through the year. This sits between the bull and bear cases and is consistent with the analyst consensus mean of $81.50.

**Bull case target: $92** — analyst high. 14x on $6.50+ EPS with successful Berry integration, sustained high oil prices, and CCS milestones.

**Bear case target: $48** — ~28% downside. Approximately 10x depressed EPS of $4.50-5.00 under $70 oil with full electricity headwind and integration challenges.

**Upside/downside from current ($66.87):** Base case +20%, bull case +38%, bear case -28%. Risk/reward ratio approximately 1.4:1 (base case) to 0.7:1 (bear case), which is acceptable given the strong estimate revision momentum and favorable energy macro backdrop.

**Timeframe:** 6-12 months. Key catalysts: Q1 2026 earnings (likely May 2026) as first full quarter with Berry contribution; CCS first injection (spring 2026); oil price trajectory through blockade resolution or escalation.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Oil prices sustain below $70/bbl for 30+ days—this compresses CRC's margins to levels that make the capital program uneconomic.
2. California permitting activity reverses—if no new permits are issued for 60+ days, the 260-well program is impaired.
3. Q1 2026 production comes in below 148 MBoe/d—would signal Berry integration problems or worse-than-expected base declines.

## Conclusion

CRC offers a combination of near-term earnings growth, aggressive capital return, and a California crude premium in an elevated oil price environment. At ~12.5x forward earnings with a 2.5% yield, the stock is reasonably valued relative to peers, with analyst consensus pointing to 20%+ upside. Current-year EPS revisions up 142% over 90 days suggest the street has not yet fully modeled the combined entity's earnings power at current oil prices.

The risks are real and concentrated: California regulatory dependency, post-acquisition leverage, the electricity revenue cliff, and a pre-revenue CCS segment that has consumed $246M. The CPPIB share sale is a yellow flag. However, at current oil prices, CRC's FCF generation (~8.5% yield) and buyback capacity provide a margin of safety that didn't exist when the stock traded in the mid-$30s a year ago.

BUY with a $80 base case target and $48 stop-loss level, sized at 3-4% of portfolio given the single-state concentration risk and post-acquisition leverage.
