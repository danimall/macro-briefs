## Executive Summary

Morningstar trades at $173 per share, representing a 45% drawdown from its 52-week high of $317. This decline has occurred simultaneously with accelerating fundamental performance: Q1 2026 revenue grew 10.8% YoY, EPS surged 50%, and adjusted operating margins expanded 440bps to 27.7%. The disconnect between improving fundamentals and collapsing share price creates a specific, quantifiable mispricing.

The CRSP acquisition (closed February 2026 for $365M) adds $2.9 trillion in assets linked to CRSP indexes — primarily Vanguard funds — making Morningstar a more credible competitor in the index space alongside FTSE Russell and MSCI. This business generates recurring, asset-based fees at high margins. At the current share price, the market is assigning minimal incremental value to an asset that meaningfully enhances the Morningstar Indexes segment's revenue and margin profile.

The primary risks are PitchBook growth deceleration (organic growth slowed to 4.8% in Q1 from 8.5% in FY2025), elevated post-acquisition leverage (funded indebtedness/EBITDA of approximately 2.0x post-CRSP), ongoing founder selling (~$7-10M/month), and AI disruption risk to the core data business. These are real but quantifiable headwinds that do not justify a 45% price decline when earnings are growing 50% and the fundamental trajectory is improving.

## Company Overview

Morningstar operates across five segments serving the investment industry: Morningstar Direct Platform (34% of revenue, institutional data and analytics), PitchBook (27%, private market data), Morningstar Credit/DBRS (14%, credit ratings), Morningstar Wealth (10%, investment management), and Morningstar Retirement (6%, managed retirement accounts). Revenue is approximately 70% license-based (recurring), 14% asset-based, and 16% transaction-based.

The company's competitive position is anchored by 40+ years of brand equity in independent investment research, proprietary intellectual property (the Morningstar Rating system, style boxes, categories embedded across the advisory industry), and high switching costs from workflow integration. No single customer represents more than 3% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $1.87B (2022) → $2.04B (2023) → $2.28B (2024) → $2.45B (2025) → $2.58B annualized run-rate (Q1 2026). Organic growth has been 7-11% annually, with Q1 2026 at 7.6% organic plus CRSP contribution.

**Margin expansion is the critical story:** Operating margins improved from 8.9% (2022) to 21.5% (2025) to 24.2% (Q1 2026). Adjusted operating margins reached 27.7% in Q1 2026, up 440bps YoY. This reflects operating leverage on a predominantly fixed-cost base as the company grows into its cost structure post-PitchBook investments.

**Cash generation:** $590M operating cash flow and $443M FCF in 2025 (18.1% FCF margin). At the current $6.6B market cap, this implies a ~6.7% FCF yield — attractive for a business growing earnings at double-digit rates.

**Balance sheet concerns are manageable but elevated:** Debt increased from $1.07B to $1.71B with CRSP acquisition financing. Funded indebtedness/EBITDA stands at approximately 2.0x post-CRSP (up from 1.3x at year-end 2025). The company remains in compliance with covenants but has materially less headroom than prior to the acquisition. Net interest expense rose to $13.7M quarterly but remains modest relative to $155.9M quarterly operating income. The Altman Z-Score of 1.46 is misleading for an asset-light subscription business with $443M annual FCF.

**Capital return:** The company repurchased $787M of stock in 2025 and $300M in Q1 2026 alone, reducing shares outstanding ~10% over trailing 12 months. Combined with 8-11% revenue growth and margin expansion, this creates mechanical EPS acceleration: Q1 EPS grew 50% on 10.8% revenue growth. Approximately $400M remains under the $1B buyback authorization as of March 31, 2026 (expires October 2028).

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term earnings trajectory:** Q1 2026 EPS of $2.73 represents 50% growth YoY. Analyst estimates suggest FY2026 EPS in the range of approximately $10-12, reflecting continued margin expansion and share count reduction. Both FY2026 and FY2027 estimates have been revised upward in recent months.

**Growth drivers by segment:**
- Morningstar Credit (+38.4% Q1 2026): Benefiting from issuance market and market share gains as fourth-largest global agency. Cyclical tailwind acknowledged — this won't sustain at 38%.
- Morningstar Indexes (~43% Q1 growth): CRSP contribution is transformative. Approximately $3.2T in assets linked to Morningstar indexes generates recurring fees that scale with AUM growth.
- Morningstar Retirement (+17.9% Q1): Participant growth and asset appreciation driving asset-based revenue.
- PitchBook (+4.8% Q1 organic): Deceleration is concerning. Renewal rates declined from 108% to 103%. Corporate segment showing churn. This requires monitoring but represents one segment of five.
- Morningstar Direct Platform (mid-single digits): Stable recurring revenue base.

**CRSP as structural growth catalyst:** $2.9T in assets linked to CRSP indexes with Vanguard as anchor client creates a high-margin, recurring revenue stream that grows with market appreciation and fund flows. Index revenue jumped from $23M to $32.8M quarterly — a 43% increase — with CRSP contributing approximately two months of revenue in Q1. The combined Morningstar Indexes segment is now on a $130M+ annual run-rate. At scale, index businesses generate 60-70% operating margins (per S&P Global and MSCI benchmarks).

**Buyback contribution:** Through Q1 2026, Morningstar reduced shares approximately 10% over the trailing twelve months. With $400M remaining under the current authorization, the pace of buybacks will likely moderate unless the board approves additional capacity. On stable operating income, continued share reduction generates meaningful EPS growth before any revenue expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $173 per share, Morningstar trades at an attractive discount to financial data and index peers. Based on available analyst estimates, the forward P/E is in the range of approximately 15-17x, depending on which consensus source is used. These multiples compare to:

- Peer median P/E: 16.8x (MarketAxess at 16.8x, DFIN at 30.1x, Virtu at 9.0x)
- S&P Global (SPGI): ~30x forward (comparable data/index business)
- MSCI: ~35x forward (pure index/analytics)
- FactSet (FDS): ~28x forward (data/analytics)

Morningstar trades at or below its closest peer (MKTX at 16.8x) and at 40-55% discounts to the premium data/index peers. Some discount is warranted — MORN has higher leverage, more cyclical exposure (credit ratings), and less established index brand — but a 50%+ discount to the group is excessive given improving margins and the CRSP acquisition.

**Price target derivation:** Applying 18-20x forward earnings yields a range of approximately $190-240, depending on which EPS estimate is used. The analyst median target of $265 implies a more aggressive re-rating toward data/index peer multiples. The base case assumes modest re-rating as the market recognizes improving margins and CRSP contribution are structural.

**Why the market is wrong:** Management explicitly stated that "market sentiment regarding the impact of AI on software and data company growth prospects has driven meaningful recent sector-wide stock price declines, including Morningstar's." The stock is being treated as a generic data company facing AI disruption. In reality: (a) its proprietary ratings methodology and index relationships create barriers AI cannot easily replicate, (b) CRSP/index revenue is asset-based and grows with markets regardless of AI, (c) estimates are being revised upward while the stock falls 45%. The market is pricing sector-level fear into a company with company-specific catalysts.

## Competitive Landscape

**Index business positioning:** Post-CRSP, approximately $3.2 trillion in assets are linked to Morningstar indexes. This places it well above CRSP's prior standalone position, though it remains smaller than FTSE Russell (~$15T linked) and MSCI (approximately $7T+ in ETF and non-ETF AUM linked to its indexes). The Vanguard relationship is the cornerstone — Vanguard's continued use of these indexes (now rebranded "Morningstar Indexes" per April 2026 announcement) validates the durability of this revenue stream.

**Competitive threats:** PitchBook faces intensifying competition from BlackRock-owned Preqin and FactSet's private market expansion. The organic growth deceleration to 4.8% suggests this pressure is materializing. In the broader data business, Bloomberg and FactSet remain formidable. AI tools create incremental competitive risk by potentially commoditizing basic research data, though Morningstar's proprietary methodologies (analyst ratings, style classification, category systems) have significant switching costs.

**Rating agency positioning:** DBRS is the fourth-largest global agency. Regulatory desire for competition (particularly in Europe and Canada) creates a structural tailwind. The 38% Q1 growth reflects both market share gains and cyclical issuance strength.

## Risk Assessment

**PitchBook deceleration is the most material near-term risk.** At 27% of revenue, a sustained slowdown from 8.5% organic to sub-5% would reduce consolidated growth by 1-2pp annually. Renewal rates declining from 108% to 103% suggest pricing power is diminishing. If corporate client churn accelerates further, this segment could approach low-single-digit growth.

**Leverage post-CRSP:** The combination of elevated debt and a buyback program with $400M remaining means a deterioration in operating performance could compress financial flexibility. Interest expense approximately doubled YoY.

**Founder selling:** Joe Mansueto's ongoing diversification under a Rule 10b5-1 plan adopted November 2024 (~50,000+ shares in recent weeks) creates persistent supply. While characterized as planned personal wealth management — he retains over 8M direct shares — it acts as a mechanical headwind on price.

**AI disruption:** The company itself acknowledges this risk, listing AI-related legal, regulatory, and product risks in its 10-K. The CRSP/index business is relatively less exposed (based on contractual licensing relationships and market structure), and the ratings business benefits from regulatory barriers. The most exposed segment is Morningstar Direct Platform, where AI tools could substitute for some research functions.

**Credit ratings cyclicality:** The 38% Q1 growth is unsustainable. A normalization of issuance markets could reduce this segment to low-single-digit growth, removing 3-4pp from consolidated revenue growth.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($250-280, +45-62% upside):**
- Combined Morningstar Indexes business scales to $200M+ revenue at 60%+ margins within 2 years as CRSP relationship fully contributes and asset growth compounds
- Buybacks continue reducing shares meaningfully (subject to board authorization beyond current $400M remaining)
- Margin expansion continues toward 30%+ adjusted operating margins as operating leverage realizes
- Market re-rates toward 20-22x forward earnings (still below data/index peer averages) as AI panic subsides
- Credit ratings business maintains above-market growth via market share gains

**Bear Case ($130-150, -13-25% downside):**
- PitchBook growth decelerates further to flat/negative as Preqin captures institutional clients
- AI tools materially erode value of Morningstar's data products, reducing renewal rates across Direct Platform
- Credit ratings revenue normalizes sharply as issuance markets cool (probable in a rising-rate environment)
- Leverage becomes constraining if operating performance deteriorates, forcing buyback suspension
- Vanguard relationship weakens or migrates to custom/internal index solutions

**Base Case ($212-240, +23-39% upside):**
- PitchBook stabilizes at mid-single-digit organic growth
- Combined Morningstar Indexes segment reaches $140-160M in annual revenue within 18 months
- Margins consolidate at 25-27% adjusted operating
- Buybacks continue but at moderated pace
- Market re-rates to 18-20x forward earnings

## Conclusion

Morningstar at $173 represents a mispriced asset where the market has applied generic AI-disruption fear to a company with company-specific catalysts (CRSP acquisition adding $2.9T in linked assets, ~10% annual share reduction, 50% EPS growth in Q1, and improving fundamental trajectory). At an estimated 15-17x forward earnings with a 6.7% FCF yield and double-digit earnings growth, the stock is cheap relative to both its own history and the financial data/index peer group (16.8x-35x). The primary risk is PitchBook growth deceleration and credit cycle normalization, both partially offset by the CRSP acquisition and margin expansion trajectory. BUY with a 12-18 month horizon targeting $215-225 base case.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrections to remaining buyback capacity ($400M vs. $700M), post-CRSP leverage (2.0x vs. 1.3x), forward EPS estimates (analyst consensus closer to ~$10.3 vs. report's original $11.90), and CRSP-specific revenue potential (Q1 data supports combined Indexes run-rate of ~$130M+, not $150-200M from CRSP alone) collectively reduce the quantified upside and weaken the mechanical EPS acceleration assumptions underpinning the price target.
