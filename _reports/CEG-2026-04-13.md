## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy is the world's largest private power producer following its January 2026 acquisition of Calpine, operating 55 GW of generating capacity anchored by 22 GW of nuclear — the nation's largest fleet by a factor of two. The company sits at the intersection of three structural tailwinds: AI/data center power demand, nuclear energy's policy renaissance, and grid reliability tightening that drove PJM capacity prices up 247-445% in 2025.

The problem is price. At $292 and ~39x trailing earnings, CEG trades at 2.1x the electric utility peer median P/E of 18.6x. This premium implicitly prices in successful integration of a $22B acquisition (which added ~$12.6B in debt), the first-ever commercial nuclear restart at Crane, continued acceleration of data center PPAs, and favorable regulatory outcomes on FERC co-location and PJM market design. The March 2026 investor day disappointed on FY2026 EPS guidance (below consensus) and offered no new data center announcements, triggering a selloff from $317 to $282 in a single session. The stock has fallen 31% from its October 2025 high of $413.

The score trajectory — 7.1 → 6.6 → 6.1 — reflects genuine risk accumulation: the Calpine debt burden roughly doubles leverage, FERC's finding that PJM's behind-the-meter co-location rules are "unjust and unreasonable" threatens the economics of marquee deals, and the current geopolitical environment (oil >$100, elevated gas prices) introduces cost volatility for the newly acquired gas fleet. The competitive position remains exceptional, but the margin of safety has narrowed substantially.

## Company Overview

Constellation Energy was spun off from Exelon in February 2022 as a pure-play competitive generation company. The January 2026 Calpine acquisition transformed it from a nuclear-focused operator into a diversified generation platform spanning five segments:

- **Nuclear (22 GW):** 25 units across 14 stations, 94.7% capacity factor (4pp above industry average), 183 TWh generated in 2025, representing 68% of total supply
- **Natural Gas (~28 GW post-Calpine):** Modern CCGT fleet concentrated in Texas (ERCOT), California, and the Northeast
- **Geothermal (730 MW):** The Geysers — largest U.S. geothermal portfolio
- **Renewables + Hydro (~2.6 GW):** Wind, solar, and the Conowingo dam
- **Battery Storage (~800 MW):** Acquired through Calpine

The customer platform serves ~2.5 million accounts including three-fourths of the Fortune 100, with 204 TWh of retail/wholesale load, 77% C&I power renewal rates, and average customer duration of ~6 years.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue of $25.5B grew 8% YoY. Adjusted operating EPS of $9.39 grew 8.3%, though GAAP net income declined 38% to $2.32B due to $709M in unrealized hedging losses and a $1.76B decline in nuclear PTC revenue. The PTC decline is structurally positive: energy and capacity prices surged high enough (electricity prices +44-65% across regions, PJM capacity +247-445%) that fleet earnings exceeded PTC thresholds. The PTC functions as a floor, and when market prices are above the floor, lower PTC revenue signals higher total profitability.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow recovered to $4.24B in FY2025 from a misleadingly negative FY2024 figure caused by an accounts receivable facility reclassification. FCF of $1.29B represents a 5.0% margin on $25.5B revenue — adequate but not compelling given the growth capex cycle ($3.9B guided through 2027 for Crane restart, nuclear uprates, and co-location infrastructure).

**Balance Sheet — Calpine Debt Is the Central Concern:** Pre-acquisition, long-term debt was $7.3B on $14.5B equity (D/E ~0.5x). The Calpine deal added ~$12.6B in assumed obligations ($7.6B long-term + $5B project financing). Pro forma total debt roughly doubles. Management increased the revolving credit facility from $4.5B to $7.0B and issued $2.75B in new senior unsecured notes. S&P (BBB+) and Moody's (Baa1) both affirmed investment-grade ratings, but the margin of safety in credit metrics has narrowed. The Altman Z-Score of 1.27 is structurally distorted by $12.9B in nuclear decommissioning obligations, so it overstates distress. The Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 (partial) confirms healthy operational fundamentals.

**Capital Return:** The dividend was raised 10% to $1.71 annualized (0.6% yield). A $5B buyback authorization is in place, though only $400M was executed in FY2025 (down from $999M in FY2024), reflecting cash deployment toward the Calpine acquisition.

**Earnings Consistency:** The 4-quarter track record is uneven: 2 beats and 2 misses, including a -83% miss in Q1 2025. The Q1 miss was driven by timing/hedging mechanics rather than operational deterioration, but it reduces confidence in the precision of quarterly estimates.

## Growth Analysis

**20% Base Earnings CAGR through 2029:** Management's target is ambitious but anchored in identifiable drivers:

1. **Data Center PPAs:** 20-year Microsoft deal (Crane, 835 MW), Meta (Clinton, 30 MW uprate), CyrusOne (Freestone 760 MW+ and Thad Hill 400 MW). These represent contracted, long-duration revenue streams, but the pipeline of new announcements has slowed — the investor day produced no new deal disclosures.

2. **Capacity Price Tailwind:** PJM capacity prices surged from low double-digits to $269-333/MW-day. CEG's concentrated PJM position (Mid-Atlantic 33%, Midwest 37%) provides direct exposure. However, proposed PJM market design reforms — including a Reliability Backstop Auction that would allocate costs specifically to data centers — could redistribute this upside away from generators.

3. **Crane Restart:** The first commercial nuclear restart in U.S. history. $1B DOE loan guarantee secured (37.5bp spread, 2055 maturity). The 20-year Microsoft PPA de-risks revenue. But NRC safety review, state permitting, and PJM interconnection approvals remain unresolved. FERC's determination that existing behind-the-meter co-location rules are "unjust and unreasonable" could require Crane to pay for full transmission services, raising costs.

4. **Calpine Synergies:** The combined platform creates cross-selling opportunities (nuclear + gas for C&I customers), operational efficiencies, and natural hedging between baseload nuclear and peaking gas. However, integration of 2,500+ employees, different IT systems, and 72 generation assets introduces meaningful execution risk.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates of $11.60 are up 2.0% over 30 days and 0.9% over 90 days — modestly positive but not the strong upward acceleration seen in companies where the growth thesis is being validated by incoming data. Next-quarter estimates have declined 7.0% (30d) and 11.4% (90d), suggesting near-term softness.

**The Investor Day Gap:** FY2026 EPS guidance came in below the ~$11.60 consensus. Combined with no new data center announcements, this produced the sharpest single-session selloff since the stock's peak. At 39x trailing, tolerance for disappointment is low.

## Competitive Landscape

CEG's competitive position is the strongest element of the thesis and the primary reason to maintain exposure:

**Nuclear Fleet — Structural Moat:** No new commercial nuclear plant has been built in the U.S. in decades. CEG's 22 GW fleet represents decades of cumulative investment, regulatory licensing, and operational expertise that cannot be replicated. The 94.7% capacity factor and 22-day average refueling outage (vs. 33+ industry average) demonstrate operational superiority. License extensions at Clinton (2047), Dresden (2049/2051), and planned Crane renewal (2029) extend asset life by decades.

**Data Center Positioning:** 24/7 carbon-free baseload power is the scarcest resource for hyperscaler data center operators. Wind and solar cannot provide uninterrupted power for AI training clusters. CEG is one of the only companies that can offer terawatt-scale clean baseload under long-duration contracts. The Microsoft, Meta, and CyrusOne deals demonstrate this advantage is monetizable.

**Post-Calpine Diversification:** Adding 28 GW of gas to 22 GW of nuclear creates a natural hedge: nuclear provides baseload at near-zero marginal cost, while gas captures peak pricing in ERCOT and other competitive markets. The combined retail platform provides customer lock-in.

**Risk to Competitive Position:** The FERC co-location ruling threatens a key competitive differentiator. If behind-the-meter data center arrangements must pay full transmission charges, the economics of co-locating at nuclear/gas plants deteriorate, potentially pushing hyperscalers toward utility-served grid connections or on-site generation alternatives.

## Risk Assessment

**Tier 1 — High Impact, Elevated Probability:**

1. **Calpine Integration Execution:** Capital expenditures are guided at $5.7B (2026) and $4.7B (2027). Management distraction from integrating 72 generation assets and 2,500+ employees is real. The 50M shares issued represent 14% dilution, with lock-up expiration creating potential selling pressure from former Calpine stockholders.

2. **FERC Co-Location / PJM Market Design Reform:** Proposed reforms include a Reliability Backstop Auction allocating costs to data centers and mandatory transmission services for co-located loads. These proceedings could meaningfully impair the economics of Crane, CyrusOne, and future co-location deals. Resolution timeline: H2 2026.

3. **Valuation Risk — Premium Requires Perfection:** At current multiples, the stock has no buffer for execution missteps. The investor day selloff demonstrated this: below-consensus guidance and no new deal announcements cost 12% in a week.

**Tier 2 — Meaningful but Manageable:**

4. **Crane Restart — First-of-Kind Risk:** NRC safety review, state permitting, and interconnection approvals remain pending. Cost overruns on nuclear projects are the historical norm. The $1B DOE loan guarantee and 20-year Microsoft PPA provide de-risking, but "significant penalties" for missing contractual timelines are disclosed.

5. **Nuclear PTC Legislative Risk:** The IRA preserved these credits, but they phase out as energy prices rise. Future legislative changes could modify or eliminate them. State ZEC programs are expiring (NJ ended May 2025; IL expires 2027).

6. **Nuclear Fuel Supply Chain:** The Russian uranium import ban creates procurement complexity. CEG is building inventory buffers (~29% of projected capex), but long-term supply diversification remains unresolved.

7. **Gas Fleet Exposure to Elevated Input Costs:** Calpine's 28 GW gas fleet is exposed to natural gas price volatility. With Henry Hub at $6.32/MMBtu and the current geopolitical environment pushing energy costs higher, spark spreads at efficient CCGTs are still healthy, but cost risk has increased.

**Tier 3 — Monitoring:**

8. **Securities Litigation Investigation:** The Schall Law Firm announced an investigation post-investor day. This appears to be an ambulance-chasing response to the stock decline and is typical for high-profile equities with significant price movements. Low probability of material impact.

9. **Nuclear Decommissioning ($12.9B obligation):** A 50bp increase in cost escalation rates adds $2.175B to this liability. NDT fund performance is market-dependent.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Current Assessment: HOLD**

CEG's competitive position is exceptional and the growth drivers are real, but the risk profile has materially worsened since February (score trajectory: 7.1 → 6.6 → 6.1) and the valuation does not compensate for the accumulated risks.

**Upgrade to BUY Triggers:**
1. Stock declines to $240-250 (~18-19x FY2027E), providing sufficient margin of safety for execution risk
2. FERC co-location proceedings resolve favorably, preserving behind-the-meter economics for Crane and CyrusOne
3. A new major data center PPA is announced, validating continued demand acceleration

**Downgrade to AVOID Triggers:**
1. FERC mandates full transmission charges for co-located loads, fundamentally impairing the data center co-location thesis
2. FY2026 EPS guidance is cut below $10.50, signaling integration difficulties or demand deceleration
3. Credit rating downgrade to BBB- or below, indicating leverage has exceeded manageable levels

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late May 2026) and FERC co-location proceedings (expected H2 2026) are the two critical decision points.

## Conclusion

Constellation Energy owns the best competitive asset in the U.S. power sector — 22 GW of nuclear generation that cannot be replicated — and has positioned it directly against the strongest demand catalyst of the next decade in data center power consumption. The 20% earnings CAGR target through 2029 is grounded in visible drivers: capacity price escalation, contracted PPAs, nuclear uprates, and Calpine synergies.

The stock's 39x trailing multiple already embeds significant success. The FERC co-location ruling introduces material uncertainty around marquee deal economics. The investor day produced guidance below consensus and no new data center announcements. The declining score trajectory (7.1 → 6.6 → 6.1) correctly captures this risk accumulation.

At $292, the stock prices in much of the upside without adequately compensating for integration, regulatory, and execution risk. HOLD is the appropriate rating, with a clear upgrade path if the stock provides better entry levels ($240-250) or if FERC proceedings resolve favorably.
