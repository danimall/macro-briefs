## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy is the largest nuclear operator in the United States and, following its January 2026 acquisition of Calpine Corporation, the largest private-sector power producer globally at approximately 55 GW of capacity. The company's 21 GW nuclear fleet is effectively irreplaceable (no new nuclear capacity has been built at scale in the U.S. in decades) and positions it uniquely to serve surging demand from hyperscale data centers requiring 24/7 carbon-free power. Long-term PPAs with Microsoft (Crane/TMI-1 restart), Meta (Clinton), and CyrusOne (Freestone) validate the demand thesis. Management guides >20% annual base EPS growth through 2029.

Three factors complicate the investment case. First, valuation: at ~38x trailing GAAP P/E and ~20.5x forward, CEG trades at roughly 2x the utility peer median P/E of 18.6x, demanding sustained execution to avoid multiple compression. Second, the Calpine acquisition roughly doubled consolidated debt to ~$20B+, creating meaningful integration and leverage risk during a period when rate hike probability is rising. Third, the regulatory environment is turning less favorable — FERC's December 2025 co-location ruling, PJM market design proposals from the National Energy Dominance Council, and potential PTC legislative changes all threaten key revenue pillars. The score trajectory has declined from 7.1 to 6.6 over the past two months, primarily on valuation and management quality concerns; this report continues the HOLD recommendation as these headwinds have not resolved.

## Company Overview

Constellation Energy was incorporated in 2021 as a spinoff from Exelon Corporation and has rapidly established itself as the dominant U.S. clean energy generator. The company operates across five segments — Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, New York, ERCOT, and Other Power Regions — serving approximately 2.5 million customer accounts including three-fourths of the Fortune 100.

**Post-Calpine Fleet (as of January 2026):**
- Nuclear: ~21 GW (94.7% capacity factor, industry-leading)
- Natural Gas: ~23 GW (acquired via Calpine, dispatchable)
- Geothermal: The Geysers — world's largest geothermal complex
- Renewables: Wind, solar, hydro (including Conowingo)
- Battery Storage: Emerging via Calpine's Nova Power project ($591M)

The nuclear fleet is the core competitive moat. These plants operate at near-zero marginal fuel cost, provide baseload emissions-free power, and face an 18-24 month licensing cycle that prevents new competitive entry. With AI data centers requiring continuous carbon-free electricity, nuclear is the only existing technology delivering this at scale.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue has been relatively stable at $23.6-$25.5B over 2022-2025, with profitability improving substantially. Adjusted operating EPS grew 8.3% from $8.67 (2024) to $9.39 (2025), marking the fourth consecutive year of beating guidance midpoint.

GAAP earnings are significantly more volatile due to mark-to-market derivative swings and nuclear decommissioning trust (NDT) fund fluctuations. Net income ranged from -$0.2B (2022) to $3.7B (2024) to $2.3B (2025). The Q1 2025 GAAP EPS miss of -83% ($0.38 actual vs. $2.24 estimate) was driven by non-cash items, not operational deterioration. Investors must focus on adjusted operating earnings to assess the underlying business.

The nuclear PTC revenue decline from $2.08B (2024) to $320M (2025) appears alarming but is a positive signal: the credit phases out as gross receipts rise above thresholds, meaning nuclear plants are earning more in the merchant market. Capacity price increases of 246-445% across PJM — from $51.89/MW-day to $179.79/MW-day in Mid-Atlantic, and $31.09 to $169.50 in Midwest — more than compensate. These are locked-in forward revenues.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

Pre-Calpine, Constellation carried $8.9B in debt against $14.5B in equity and $3.64B in cash. The Calpine acquisition added approximately $12.6B of debt ($7.6B long-term + $5.0B project finance). Post-acquisition consolidated debt approaches $20B+, roughly doubling the pre-deal leverage.

Management has moved quickly to address the balance sheet: $2.3B of Calpine notes exchanged/replaced, $3.75B of term loans and secured notes repaid, the revolving credit facility expanded from $4.5B to $7.0B, and $2.75B in new senior unsecured notes issued. Roughly $1.5B of legacy Calpine corporate debt remains, maturing primarily March 2028. Credit ratings were affirmed at BBB+/Baa1 post-closing.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.27 (technically distress zone) is misleading for a capital-intensive utility with $22.5B in PP&E, $12.9B in decommissioning obligations, and $19.3B in NDT funds. The investment-grade ratings are the more relevant solvency indicator. That said, $20B+ in debt during a rising-rate environment where hike probability has increased to 35-45% is a legitimate concern — higher refinancing costs on the $1.5B March 2028 maturity and future capital needs could compress returns.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow improved to $4.24B in 2025 from -$2.46B in 2024 (the 2024 figure was distorted by accounts receivable facility reclassification). Capital expenditures were $2.95B, yielding $1.29B in free cash flow. The 2026-2027 capex trajectory is steep: $5.7B and $4.7B respectively, inclusive of Calpine integration and growth investments. The $5B share buyback authorization provides flexibility but will compete with debt reduction and growth capex for cash allocation.

Dividends are modest at ~$1.71/share annualized (~0.6% yield), well below the 3.2% peer median, reflecting the growth-oriented capital allocation strategy.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

The 4-quarter track record shows 2 beats and 2 misses. The Q1 2025 miss was driven by GAAP accounting noise, not operational underperformance. The Q3 2025 miss (-3.5%) is more concerning as it occurred on an adjusted basis. This mixed record warrants moderate confidence in forward estimates — the Q1 GAAP volatility is a known feature, but the Q3 adjusted miss suggests visibility is imperfect.

## Growth Analysis

### Data Center Demand Thesis

Constellation has secured three cornerstone data center contracts:
1. **Microsoft (Crane/TMI-1):** 20-year PPA backed by DOE $1B loan guarantee
2. **Meta (Clinton):** 20-year PPA beginning June 2027 with 30 MW uprates
3. **CyrusOne (Freestone):** 380 MW agreement with potential Phase 2 doubling

Over 5,650 MW of clean power commitments are contracted. U.S. electricity consumption hit all-time highs in 2025, and EPRI projects data centers growing from 4-5% to 9-17% of national electricity consumption by 2030.

### Calpine Synergies and Growth

The Calpine acquisition provides:
- 23 GW of natural gas generation for dispatchable capacity (complementing nuclear baseload)
- Retail platform expansion (~62 TWh annual load)
- ERCOT and California market exposure (geographic diversification from PJM concentration)
- Geothermal assets (carbon-free, baseload)

Management projects >20% annual base EPS growth through 2029, driven by contracted deals and Calpine synergies. The 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $11.00-$12.00 (midpoint $11.50) implies 22-28% growth over 2025's $9.39, supported by the first full year of Calpine contribution and ongoing capacity price tailwinds.

### Transmission Constraint Reality

The growth thesis faces a binding physical constraint: U.S. high-voltage transmission construction has collapsed to approximately 322 miles per year, and PJM interconnection queues show a 2.3 TW backlog with only 13% historical completion rates for 2000-2019 projects. The Crane/TMI-1 restart illustrates this — the reactor may be ready by 2027, but PJM has indicated the power may not be deliverable until 2031 due to transmission limitations. This gap between generation readiness and grid deliverability is the key risk to the data center growth narrative.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $282 (current price per analyst data), CEG trades at:
- **38.2x trailing GAAP P/E** — 2.05x the utility peer median of 18.6x
- **~20.5x forward P/E** — on $11.50 midpoint 2026 guidance
- **0.6% dividend yield** — versus 3.2% peer median

The forward multiple compression to ~20.5x partially reflects the >20% EPS growth trajectory, but still represents a meaningful premium to regulated utilities (DUK 18.6x, AEP 17.3x, D 16.9x). The premium is justified by nuclear scarcity, data center exposure, and superior growth — but the degree of premium assumes flawless execution across Calpine integration, TMI-1 restart, FERC regulatory outcomes, and continued data center demand acceleration.

The stock sits 32% below its 52-week high of $412.70 and 50% above its 52-week low of $188.01. The analyst consensus median target of $386 implies 37% upside, but the low target of $272.40 suggests only 3% downside — a narrow range that may not fully reflect the regulatory risks now emerging. The $5B LS Power divestiture proceeds ($5B for 4.4 GW) provide a market-based valuation anchor of approximately $1.14M/MW, though nuclear assets should command a premium over the gas plants being sold.

The March 2026 Investor Day was a modest negative catalyst: 2026 guidance slightly missed consensus ($11.50 midpoint vs. $11.60), and no new data center deals were announced. Repeated incremental disappointments of this kind erode premium valuations over time.

## Competitive Landscape

Constellation's competitive position is the strongest dimension of the investment case. No competitor can build comparable nuclear capacity within a decade, creating genuine scarcity value as data centers bid for 24/7 carbon-free power.

The Calpine acquisition strengthens the competitive position by adding dispatchable natural gas capacity (valuable for grid reliability during renewable intermittency) and expanding the retail platform. The combined 55 GW fleet and 2.5 million customer accounts create an integrated generation-to-retail business with significant scale advantages.

Competitive threats are limited on the nuclear side but more real on gas generation, where merchant competitors and new builds are feasible. The regulatory divestiture of 4.4 GW to LS Power for $5B reduces PJM market concentration but also reduces Constellation's pricing power in its core market.

NRC license renewals at Clinton (through 2047) and Dresden (through 2049/2051) extend the economic life of key assets by decades, reinforcing the durability of the nuclear moat.

## Risk Assessment

### Regulatory Risk (High)

Three concurrent regulatory threats:

1. **FERC co-location ruling (December 2025):** Found PJM's tariff "unjust and unreasonable" for co-located data center load, directing new transmission service rates to be determined in 2026. This directly threatens Constellation's strategy of siting data centers adjacent to generation assets (Freestone/CyrusOne model).

2. **PJM market design proposals:** The National Energy Dominance Council has proposed reliability backstop auctions, data center cost allocation, and residential customer protections from capacity price increases. Any of these could cap the capacity price windfall.

3. **Nuclear PTC uncertainty:** The credit extends through 2032 under OBBBA. While 2025's decline to $320M reflects high merchant prices rather than legislative risk, any repeal or modification would remove the floor that protects nuclear economics in lower-price environments. The current political environment linking energy policy to broader fiscal debates increases this tail risk.

### Calpine Integration Risk (Medium-High)

Integrating 23 GW of natural gas/geothermal generation, a retail platform, and $12.6B of associated debt is operationally complex. The 10-K explicitly warns that integration is "complex, costly and time-consuming." Former Calpine stockholders hold ~14% of shares with lock-up expiration creating potential selling pressure. Significant goodwill will be recorded, subject to future impairment risk.

### TMI-1 Restart Risk (Medium)

This is a first-of-its-kind nuclear restart. The 10-K warns of NRC safety review risk, state permit requirements, federal interconnection challenges, potential cost overruns, and contractual penalties for missing timelines. While the $1B DOE loan guarantee at 37.5 bps over Treasuries provides favorable financing, the $1B commitment is at risk if the restart fails.

### Data Center Demand Cyclicality (Medium)

The 10-K specifically flags that data center demand "may be subject to technological change, shifts in customer behavior, regulatory developments, or changes in capital investment cycles and may not grow at the pace currently anticipated by the market." At ~38x trailing P/E, the stock embeds aggressive demand assumptions.

### Energy Price and Geopolitical Sensitivity (Medium)

With beta of 1.188 and natural gas exposure increasing post-Calpine, CEG is more sensitive to energy price volatility than a pure nuclear play. In the current environment of oil above $100, gas prices at $6.32/MMBtu, and active geopolitical disruption affecting energy markets, fuel cost volatility for the gas fleet could compress margins relative to expectations, while higher power prices benefit the nuclear fleet. The net effect depends on hedging positions and the correlation between gas input costs and wholesale electricity prices.

### Nuclear Fuel Supply (Low-Medium)

Russian LEU import bans and retaliatory export restrictions create ongoing fuel supply uncertainty. Constellation is building inventory as a hedge, but extended supply disruptions would increase fuel costs for the nuclear fleet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$375-410)

PJM capacity prices remain elevated or increase further as reserve margins tighten with data center load growth. Calpine integration executes on schedule, delivering guided synergies and expanding the total addressable market. TMI-1 restart proceeds through NRC review, validating the nuclear restart model and unlocking additional contract opportunities. New data center PPAs are announced through 2026, demonstrating continued demand for carbon-free baseload power. >20% annual EPS growth materializes through 2029, justifying a sustained premium to regulated utility peers. 2026 EPS comes in at or above the $12.00 high end of guidance. At 25x $13+ 2027E EPS, the stock reaches $325-375; if the growth premium expands on deal momentum, $375-410 is achievable.

### Bear Case (~$200-230)

FERC co-location ruling significantly restricts the data center co-location model, reducing Constellation's competitive advantage in serving hyperscale load. PJM market design changes cap capacity prices. Data center demand growth slows as AI capital spending cycles moderate or hyperscalers diversify power sourcing strategies. Calpine integration challenges emerge — cost overruns, systems integration delays, or gas fleet underperformance in a volatile price environment. TMI-1 restart encounters NRC or permitting delays, pushing commercial operation beyond 2028. The premium multiple compresses from ~20.5x to 14-16x on a $11.50 EPS base, yielding $160-185; with modest EPS growth to $12, that range becomes $168-192. Accounting for some growth premium persistence, $200-230 reflects a realistic bear outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. New data center PPA announcements (beyond existing Microsoft/Meta/CyrusOne) demonstrating continued demand acceleration and pricing power
2. 2026 adjusted EPS tracking at or above $12.00 (high end of guidance) through Q2 results, confirming Calpine synergies are on schedule
3. FERC co-location ruling resolves favorably (i.e., rates set at reasonable levels that preserve the economics of co-located generation), or PJM market design proposals are substantially diluted

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FERC co-location ruling or PJM reforms materially impair the data center co-location model (reducing NPV of existing deals by >15%)
2. 2026 adjusted EPS guidance reduced below $10.50 or Calpine integration costs exceed $500M above plan
3. TMI-1 NRC review results in denial or multi-year delay beyond 2029
4. Forward P/E expands above 25x without corresponding upward EPS revisions, indicating pure momentum rather than fundamental support

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August) — the first full quarter with Calpine contribution and the most relevant near-term data point for integration progress. FERC co-location rate determination (expected H2 2026) is the key regulatory catalyst.

## Conclusion

Constellation Energy owns an irreplaceable nuclear fleet positioned at the intersection of AI-driven electricity demand growth and carbon-free power requirements — a structural advantage no competitor can replicate within a decade. The Calpine acquisition creates the world's largest private power producer with meaningful geographic diversification and a complementary generation mix.

Three measurable concerns hold back the investment case: (1) the ~38x trailing / ~20.5x forward multiple demands flawless execution and sustained demand acceleration; (2) post-Calpine leverage of ~$20B+ creates refinancing risk in a rising-rate environment; and (3) FERC and PJM regulatory headwinds are real and quantifiable, with decisions expected in H2 2026 that could materially affect the data center co-location strategy.

The score trajectory decline from 7.1 to 6.6 over two months reflected these accumulating headwinds, and this report finds no material change in either direction since the last assessment. The growth story remains intact but the valuation offers limited margin of safety, and the regulatory catalysts that could resolve uncertainty are 3-6 months away. HOLD is appropriate, with a clear path to BUY if regulatory outcomes are favorable and Calpine integration tracks ahead of plan.
