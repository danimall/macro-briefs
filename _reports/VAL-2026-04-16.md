## Executive Summary

Valaris is one of the world's largest offshore contract drilling companies, operating 13 drillships (12 seventh-generation), 2 semisubmersibles, and 31 jackup rigs across six continents. The company emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in April 2021 and has rebuilt into a profitable operator with $4.67B in backlog, $974M in liquidity, and no debt maturities until 2030. Operating income grew 35% to $477M in 2025 despite flat revenue, demonstrating operational leverage from fleet rationalization.

The investment case is dominated by the pending merger with Transocean (RIG), announced February 9, 2026, at an exchange ratio of 15.235 RIG shares per VAL share. The stock surged ~50% on the announcement (from ~$62 to ~$84 overnight) and has traded in the $87-$105 range since. At $91.67, the stock implies a Transocean share price of ~$6.02, embedding the merger premium. This creates a binary outcome: if the deal closes, VAL shareholders receive Transocean stock; if it fails, the stock likely reverts toward pre-announcement levels ($55-65), representing 30-40% downside. The analyst consensus — 0 Strong Buy, 0 Buy, 7 Hold, 1 Sell, 1 Strong Sell — with a median target of $59 (36% below current price) reflects this binary overhang.

On standalone fundamentals, Valaris is cheap (6.6x trailing, ~29x FY2026E of $3.28 on depressed near-term estimates). The offshore drilling supply backdrop is structurally favorable: the floater fleet has shrunk 45%+ since 2014 with no newbuilds planned. However, the merger agreement restricts capital allocation (no buybacks, limited capex flexibility), and the ARO joint venture with Saudi Aramco represents a significant capital obligation with deferred cash returns. The risk profile is too skewed by the binary merger outcome to recommend accumulation at current levels.

## Company Overview

Valaris provides offshore contract drilling services to international oil and gas companies using drillships, semisubmersibles, and jackup rigs. The company is domiciled in Bermuda and generates 86% of revenue outside the United States. Its customer base includes Petrobras (13% of 2025 revenue), BP, Azule Energy, and Equinor, with the top five customers accounting for 49% of revenue.

The company also holds a 50% equity interest in ARO, a joint venture with Saudi Aramco that owns and operates 9 jackup rigs in the Middle East. Valaris leases 7 additional jackups to ARO. The JV is committed to a long-term newbuild program of up to 18 additional jackups at approximately $300M each, creating a potential contingent obligation of up to $1.1B for Valaris.

On February 9, 2026, Valaris announced a business combination with Transocean under a Bermuda court-approved scheme of arrangement. The combined entity would become the world's dominant offshore driller with approximately $10B in backlog at announcement.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability:** Revenue was essentially flat at $2.37B in 2025 vs. $2.36B in 2024. However, operating income surged 35% to $477M as contract drilling expenses fell 9%, driven by fleet rationalization (retiring 3 semisubmersibles and selling 4 jackups) and completion of expensive reactivation programs. Floater average daily revenue improved 12% to $386K; jackup average daily revenue rose 14% to $138K.

**Reported vs. adjusted earnings:** GAAP net income of $983M ($13.86/share) is heavily inflated by a $523M valuation allowance release on deferred tax assets. Adjusting for this, core earnings are approximately $460M, or roughly $6.50/share. The company carries a $1.33B net deferred tax asset, approximately 25% of total assets, whose realization depends on future profitability in specific jurisdictions.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow improved to $546M (from $355M in 2024). Capital expenditures were $344M, yielding free cash flow of approximately $203M. FCF margin of 9.2% reflects the capital intensity of maintaining and upgrading a large rig fleet; management guides 2026 capex of $425-475M, implying FCF compression absent revenue growth.

**Balance sheet:** Cash of $599M, long-term debt of $1.1B (8.375% Second Lien Notes due April 2030), and a $375M undrawn credit facility provide total liquidity of approximately $974M. No principal payments are due until 2030. Stockholders' equity stands at $3.17B.

**Capital return:** The company repurchased $425M of stock since emergence (shares outstanding declined 6% from 75.1M to 70.6M) at an average price of approximately $49.78 in 2025, well below current levels. The buyback program ($175M remaining authorization) is frozen under the merger agreement.

**Backlog:** Total Valaris backlog of $4.67B (as of February 2026), up 29% from $3.61B a year prior. Floater backlog surged 50% to $3.0B. A $447M Petrobras contract extension for DS-4 was announced April 6, 2026, at an implied day rate of ~$420K, confirming continued demand for 7th-gen drillships.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term softness and medium-term structural support pull in opposite directions.

**Near-term headwinds:** Current quarter EPS estimates have collapsed from $0.11 to -$0.06 over 90 days (−157.7% revision). The company faces floater utilization gaps between contract rollovers — active floater utilization was 71% in 2025 with four drillships idle between programs. Saudi Aramco suspended 37 jackup contracts since early 2024, releasing supply into the market. Oil price weakness (Brent declining from mid-$70s to low-$60s during 2025) creates customer hesitancy on new commitments.

**Structural positives:** The global floater fleet has shrunk 45%+ from the 2014 peak (~280 rigs to ~150), with no newbuilds planned given $500M+ per-unit costs and limited yard capacity. The jackup fleet is aging rapidly — 29% of global jackups are 40+ years old. Valaris's fleet of twelve 7th-generation drillships is among the youngest and most capable in the industry, commanding premium day rates. The backlog provides approximately 2 years of revenue visibility.

**Merger growth thesis:** The Transocean combination would create $10B+ combined backlog and eliminate overhead redundancy. Management has highlighted complementary geographic positioning and fleet composition. Mergers in cyclical industries during price downturns carry integration risk.

**Estimate trajectory:** FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.28 (down 4% over 90 days) steps up to $6.94 for FY2027 (essentially stable over 90 days). The improvement assumes contract rollovers at higher day rates and redeployment of currently idle rigs.

## Competitive Landscape

Valaris operates in an oligopolistic market with meaningful barriers to entry. Building a new drillship costs $500M+ and takes 3-4 years; no newbuilds are currently planned industry-wide. The supply contraction from 2014-2021 removed roughly half of the global floater fleet, creating structural pricing power for remaining operators.

**Competitive advantages:**
1. Twelve 7th-generation drillships represent the industry's highest specification and command premium day rates ($386K average in 2025, trending toward $420K+ on recent awards)
2. Geographic diversity across six continents reduces regional concentration
3. ARO JV provides privileged access to Saudi Arabia, the world's largest offshore drilling market
4. No near-term debt maturities (2030) provide maximum operational flexibility through the cycle

**Competitive risks:**
1. The Transocean merger, if completed, would reduce industry competition — but regulatory scrutiny could delay or block the deal
2. Noble Corp's Diamond Offshore acquisition (2024) created another scaled competitor
3. Saudi Aramco contract suspensions released 37 jackups into the market, depressing utilization
4. Oil price weakness reduces customer urgency to commit to long-term drilling programs

## Risk Assessment

**Merger binary risk (dominant):** The merger agreement restricts Valaris's ability to pursue alternatives, repurchase shares, or make acquisitions until the deal closes or terminates. The deadline is February 9, 2027.

**Oil price cyclicality:** Brent crude declining from mid-$70s to low-$60s through 2025 created contracting deferrals. Management explicitly warns that further oil price declines "could negatively impact demand and pricing for our services." The current geopolitical environment with the Hormuz blockade and oil above $100 should benefit forward contracting and day rates; this is not reflected in the 2025 financial data.

**ARO capital trap:** The ARO JV has never paid a cash dividend. Interest on $401M in notes receivable was paid in kind (not cash) in 2025. Valaris expects to extend the notes' maturity. The contingent obligation for ARO newbuilds could reach $1.1B. Cash realization from this investment is years away.

**Floater utilization gaps:** Four drillships were idle between contracts during 2025. Warm-stacking costs erode margins during these gaps. While contracting activity has improved for mid-2026+, the near-term EPS estimate (-$0.06 for current quarter) reflects this operational drag.

**Tax asset realization:** The $1.33B net DTA depends on generating future taxable income in specific jurisdictions. Any reversal would directly reduce book value.
