**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ONE Gas is a 100%-regulated, pure-play natural gas distribution utility serving ~2.3 million customers across Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas. The business is a textbook capex-into-rate-base growth model: it earns no margin on the gas commodity (passed through at cost), so earnings are a function of rate base, authorized ROE, and recoverable operating expenses. FY2025 was the strongest year in the recent window: net income grew +19% to $264.2M, diluted EPS reached $4.37 (top of the narrowed $4.34–$4.40 guide), and operating income rose +15% to $457.4M, driven by +$116M in new-rate recoveries and continued customer growth (~23,000 connections per year).

The investment question is whether a genuine growth acceleration is underway. Management has stated its long-term 4–6% EPS growth rate is now expected to run above the high end of that range "for the duration of the 5-year plan," describing the change as structural. Three reinforcing tailwinds underpin this: Texas HB 4384 (June 2025), which extends favorable carrying-cost recovery to 100% of Texas capital deployment and reduces regulatory lag in the fastest-growing jurisdiction; interest-rate normalization, which directly benefits a commercial-paper-heavy financing model (each 25bp Fed cut ≈ +$0.025 EPS); and a ~1.5 GW large-load pipeline (data centers, fab/generation projects) that OGS can serve within the regulated model using existing infrastructure, in some cases with customer-funded capital.

We maintain a HOLD. The fundamentals are improving and the large-load optionality is real, but three things keep this from a BUY: the structurally FCF-negative, continuously equity-funded model (diluted shares rose ~8% over two years to 60.5M); authorized ROEs that are middle-of-the-road (9.4% OK, ~9.6% KS, 9.8% TX); and a cumulative TSR that lags the utility peer group by ~40 points, indicating the market has not credited the structural-growth claim. The large-load projects remain in "mid-to-late stages of investment decisions" rather than contracted. The catalyst path is identifiable: the December Utility Week 5-year capex/financial outlook and the first contracted data-center or generation projects. This is a growth hold with a defined upgrade trigger rather than a position to chase here.

## Company Overview

ONE Gas operates through three divisions: Oklahoma Natural Gas (the largest distributor in OK, ~89% of state customers), Kansas Gas Service (~72% of KS), and Texas Gas Service (3rd largest in TX, ~13%). It was spun off from ONEOK in January 2014; the Oklahoma predecessor dates to 1906. The company runs as a single reportable segment and is a member of the S&P MidCap 400.

The earnings model has several structural protections that make cash flows defensive:

- **High fixed-charge revenue**: ~91% of Oklahoma sales revenue (ex-gas cost) comes from fixed monthly charges, with 66% in Texas and 52% in Kansas, insulating earnings from volume and weather swings.
- **Weather Normalization Adjustments** in all three states (Kansas runs year-round) further dampen weather sensitivity.
- **Constructive regulatory mechanisms** that reduce lag: Oklahoma's PBRC (annual rate reviews with an ROE deadband), Kansas GSRS, Texas GRIP, and now Texas HB 4384.
- **Proximity to production**: its three states produce more than one-third of U.S. natural gas, enabling fast, low-capital large-load connections.

## Financial Analysis

The headline revenue line is misleading because it largely reflects pass-through gas costs that net to zero in operating income. The signal is in operating income and net income.

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $2,372.0M | $2,083.6M | $2,427.4M |
| Operating income | $377.6M | $399.0M | $457.4M |
| Net income | $231.2M | $222.9M | $264.2M |
| Diluted EPS | $4.14 | $3.91 | $4.37 |
| Capex + asset removal | $728.7M | $762.1M | $759.5M |

FY2025 operating income growth was driven by +$116.0M from new rates and +$6.6M from residential customer growth, partially offset by +$20.6M depreciation, +$17.0M employee costs, and +$14.7M ad valorem taxes.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt-to-capital (ex-securitization) is 47.6%, with long-term debt-to-capital at 40.9% — comfortably under the 70% credit-facility covenant. Credit ratings are Moody's A3 / S&P A-, both Stable. Adjusted CFO-to-debt is projected at ~19%, the upper end of the range for the current ratings. Senior notes total $2.2B with no maturities until 2029 (after the $250M term loan due September 2026, which reset to 4.48% in February 2026). For a regulated LDC, this is an appropriate and conservative capital structure.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow recovered to $578.8M in 2025 (from $368.4M in 2024; 2023's $939.5M was inflated by Winter Storm Uri gas-cost recoveries). The company is structurally free-cash-flow negative — capex of ~$760M exceeds operating cash flow — and funds the gap with commercial paper and equity. This is normal for a growth-mode regulated utility, but it means continuous reliance on capital markets, and in a higher-for-longer rate environment, the cost of that financing is a live headwind.

**Capital allocation and dilution.** The 2026 capex budget is ~$800M, and the dividend was raised to $0.68/quarter ($2.72 annualized) in January 2026, supporting a yield of roughly 3.4% at the recent ~$80 price. Equity is issued steadily via forward sale agreements and a $300M ATM program; ~40% of the 5-year equity need is already covered via forwards, and ~2.6M shares settled in December 2025 for $205M net. Diluted share count rose from ~55.9M (2023) to ~60.5M (2025), a ~8% increase. EPS still grew despite this dilution, as rate-base returns have outpaced share creep, but the dependence on equity issuance is the key constraint on per-share growth as capex steps up.

The financial-health profile is solid but not pristine: strong ratings, no near-term maturity wall, and disciplined leverage, offset by structural FCF deficits and constant capital-markets dependence. This supports a score of 6.0 — above the peer downside-risk threshold but not high, given the financing model.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has changed character. Management's assertion that EPS growth is moving structurally above the prior 4–6% range "for the duration of the 5-year plan" is the most important development, and it rests on three reinforcing drivers:

1. **Texas HB 4384** extends Texas's favorable 8.209 carrying-cost accounting — previously applied to roughly 25% of capital — to 100% of Texas capital deployment. This directly reduces regulatory lag in the company's fastest-growing jurisdiction. Management is deliberately under-promising the impact, which is constructive.

2. **Interest-rate normalization.** With ~$800M of average commercial paper outstanding to finance rate base ahead of recovery, the CP rate fell to 3.94% at year-end 2025 from 4.77%, a tailwind that has flowed through to recent results. The caveat is that the rate path is two-sided: a higher-for-longer path raises financing costs and dampens this driver.

3. **Large-load / data-center demand.** Management disclosed partnering with customers on ~1.5 GW of utility-scale generation projects across all three states, plus a 200 MW fab/data-center project and an RNG project, all served within the regulated model with modest incremental capex, and in some cases customer-funded capital. The SE Oklahoma pipeline (announced December 2025, ~$120M, 43 miles, >100 Bcf/year for Western Farmers Electric Cooperative's Hugo plant, in service by Q3 2028) is a tangible example.

The data-center demand thesis is corroborated broadly across the regulated-utility complex. OGS's differentiation is capital intensity: unlike electric utilities chasing data centers with multi-year, multi-billion-dollar buildouts, OGS can serve these loads off existing infrastructure and workforce. If even a portion of the 1.5 GW converts to contracted load, the return-on-incremental-capital is high.

The constraints on the growth score: the large-load projects are in "mid-to-late stages of investment decisions," not contracted, so execution risk remains; consensus EPS estimates do not yet appear to reflect the structural-growth claim; and per-share growth still has to outrun ~8%-every-two-years dilution. Growth prospects score 6.3 — the strongest dimension, weighted heavily as the most reliable discriminator, reflecting genuine acceleration tempered by the un-contracted status of the marquee catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

At roughly $80, OGS trades at about 18x trailing FY2025 EPS, with a ~3.4% dividend yield. Against the regulated-utility peer set, this is a middle-of-the-pack multiple — neither the premium of high-load-growth electric names nor a distressed discount. Forward EPS, building in the structural >6% growth claim, would put the multiple around 17x on near-term estimates.

The valuation case is two-sided. The bull argument is a sentiment/fundamental disconnect: net income grew, the structural growth outlook was raised, and yet the stock fell ~10.6% in the month following its early-2026 earnings report and TSR lags the peer group. If the large-load thesis converts and the December 5-year plan formalizes >6% EPS growth, the multiple should re-rate toward the higher-load peers, supporting upside toward the high-$80s/low-$90s.

The bear argument is the more cautionary read. The peer-lagging TSR is not obviously irrational; the market may be correctly discounting (a) un-contracted large-load projects, (b) the dilution drag on per-share growth, and (c) middle-of-the-road authorized ROEs that cap the return on the growing rate base. A compressed-but-not-cheap multiple here does not offer obvious asymmetric upside; the absolute yield provides some floor, but the equity-funded model limits dividend-growth velocity. We also weight the broader caution that "cheap" multiples have been reliably backwards-sorting signals: a modest discount to peers is not, by itself, a reason to upgrade.

On balance, the valuation is fair rather than compelling: there is a plausible mispricing, but it is contingent on a catalyst (the December plan, the first contracted projects) that has not yet landed. Valuation scores 5.8.

## Competitive Landscape

ONE Gas competes primarily against electricity for residential and commercial heating, cooking, and drying loads, where the energy-source choice is typically locked in at equipment installation. In Texas it also faces other LDCs and pipelines for large industrial/transport customers, some served at negotiated below-tariff rates.

The competitive trajectory has two crosscurrents. The favorable one is that the AI/data-center power-demand surge reverses the prior decade's "gas in secular decline" narrative, creating new natural-gas load that OGS is well-positioned to capture given its proximity to production and existing infrastructure. The constructive tri-state regulatory backdrop (pro-economic-growth, pro-gas, reinforced by HB 4384) and the completed Austin System Reinforcement project (largest capital project in company history, delivered ahead of schedule and under budget, +25% Austin winter peak capacity, access to discounted Waha gas) reinforce the position.

The unfavorable crosscurrent is the long-term secular threat of building electrification and decarbonization policy, which is an existential risk to the LDC model over a multi-decade horizon, though not acute in OGS's conservative footprint. OGS is also a slower-growth, lower-ROE, conservatively run LDC; its rate base (~$5.8B combined) is sizeable but not expanding at the pace of the highest-load electric peers.

The prior-report competitive-position decline (6.5 → 5.5) appears to have over-weighted the electrification/peer-lag concern. The large-load thesis and HB 4384 are genuine positives to the trajectory, so we set competitive position at 6.0 — improving at the margin from the prior read but not high, given the un-contracted status of the differentiating opportunity. We confirm this against the growth-prospects signal rather than treating the level alone as conclusive.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that genuinely matter beyond boilerplate:

1. **Regulatory recovery risk** is the central risk. As a 100%-regulated utility, the entire thesis depends on regulators (OCC, KCC, Texas RRC and municipalities) granting timely rate increases and allowed ROEs. Regulatory lag — placing assets in service before they earn a return — is the chronic structural headwind the mechanisms only partly mitigate.

2. **Geographic concentration.** 100% of operations are in three states; adverse political, regulatory, weather, or economic shifts in any of them hit the whole company.

3. **Interest-rate / capital-markets dependence.** The FCF-negative model requires constant debt and equity access; the rate sensitivity ($0.025 EPS per 25bp) is a tailwind now but cuts both ways. The macro rate path is unresolved.

4. **Electrification / decarbonization** — the long-term secular threat, not acute but unbounded.

5. **GHG / methane regulation.** Pending PHMSA rulemakings (leak detection, distribution-pipeline safety) could force material, possibly non-recoverable capex. A January 2025 executive order froze pending rulemakings, providing a near-term pause.

6. **Environmental remediation.** Legacy manufactured-gas-plant liabilities — 12 sites in Kansas (AAO cap raised from $15M to $32M in 2025), 1 in Texas — currently immaterial but capped recovery and open-ended.

7. **Labor.** ~18% unionized; USW contract expires May 2028, IBEW June 2027.

8. **Equity dilution.** The growth is equity-funded; per-share growth depends on rate-base returns continuing to outpace dilution as capex steps up.

Most of these are standard, priced regulated-utility risks. The idiosyncratic items not fully priced are the un-contracted large-load conversion risk and the financing-cost risk under a higher-for-longer rate path. None are acute binary catalysts. Risk factors score 6.0.

### Options Market Signal

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$92).** The December Utility Week 5-year plan formalizes structural >6% EPS growth; the first of the ~1.5 GW large-load projects convert to contracted load with customer-funded capital; HB 4384 carrying-cost recovery measurably reduces Texas regulatory lag; and Fed cuts continue to lower CP financing costs. The peer-lagging TSR closes as the market re-rates the multiple toward higher-load utility peers. EPS approaches the high-$4s on the 5-year plan, and an ~18-19x multiple supports the low-$90s.

**Bear case (~$66).** Large-load projects stall in "investment decision" limbo and never contract; a higher-for-longer rate path raises CP and term-loan financing costs, eroding the rate-sensitivity tailwind; ongoing equity issuance dilutes per-share growth faster than rate base returns; and authorized ROEs compress in unfavorable rate cases. EPS growth reverts to the low end of the 4-6% range, and the multiple de-rates toward ~15x, implying the mid-$60s — roughly -18% from current.

**Base case (~$84).** EPS grows in the 5-6% range as HB 4384 and customer growth flow through, with large-load providing incremental but not transformational upside; the dividend grows in line with earnings; and the multiple holds around 17-18x. Modest total return led by the ~3.4% yield and mid-single-digit EPS growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The position is worth holding for the ~3.4% dividend plus a credible, if un-contracted, large-load growth optionality. We are not chasing it here because the catalyst that would re-rate the stock has not yet landed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The December Utility Week 5-year plan formalizes EPS growth at or above 6% with a quantified large-load contribution, and consensus estimates begin rising toward that path.
- One or more of the ~1.5 GW large-load projects converts from "investment decision" to a signed, in-rate or customer-funded contract, demonstrating execution of the low-capital-intensity model.
- The stock pulls back toward the low-$70s while fundamentals hold, restoring clearer asymmetry (forward yield approaching 4% with the growth outlook intact).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Large-load projects are abandoned or repeatedly delayed without conversion, removing the differentiating thesis.
- A rate case delivers a materially below-expectation ROE or disallowance, or a higher-for-longer rate path pushes CP/term-loan costs high enough to compress EPS while equity issuance accelerates.
- Equity dilution outpaces rate-base returns such that per-share growth turns negative.

**Reassessment timeline:** The December 2025 Utility Week 5-year plan and 2026 guidance is the next natural reassessment point, followed by FY2026 quarterly prints for evidence of large-load contracting and HB 4384 impact.

## Conclusion

ONE Gas is a conservatively run, fully regulated tri-state gas distributor whose growth profile is improving. FY2025 delivered the strongest results in the recent window, and management's structural >6% EPS growth claim rests on three reinforcing tailwinds: HB 4384 carrying-cost recovery, Fed-cut-driven financing relief, and a large-load pipeline served at low capital intensity. These are real and underappreciated, evidenced by a TSR lagging utility peers against improving fundamentals.

The offsets keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY: a structurally FCF-negative, continuously equity-funded model that dilutes per-share growth; middle-of-the-road authorized ROEs that cap rate-base returns; a two-sided rate environment that could reverse the financing tailwind; and large-load projects that remain un-contracted. The absence of any open-market insider buying — only routine vesting and grants — tempers management's optimistic tone, though it is low-signal in isolation. The valuation is fair, not compelling: a plausible mispricing exists, but it hinges on a catalyst (the December plan and the first contracted projects) that has not yet materialized. The score trajectory (5.7 → 6.2 → 5.9, now 5.95) reflects a stable, slowly improving picture with the recommendation unchanged across four reads; the bar to convert to a BUY is the same concrete catalyst path we identify above.
