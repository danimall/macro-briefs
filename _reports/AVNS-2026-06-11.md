**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Avanos Medical is no longer a fundamental equity story. On April 13, 2026, the company signed a definitive merger agreement to be acquired by affiliates of American Industrial Partners (AIP) for $25.00 per share in all-cash, an enterprise value of roughly $1.27 billion. The agreement carries no financing condition and is expected to close in the second half of 2026, with an outside date of January 13, 2027. The stock closed at $24.98 on June 11, 2026, against the $25.00 offer. The gross spread is approximately $0.02, or roughly 0.1%. Every analytical question reduces to one variable: will the deal close on its current terms?

At this spread, the position offers effectively no upside and an asymmetric downside. If the merger completes, holders collect the remaining few cents (an annualized return that is negligible net of any carry). If the deal breaks, most plausibly on antitrust (HSR) friction or a material-adverse-effect dispute triggered by the unresolved Medicare peripheral-RFA coverage threat, the stock would likely revert toward its pre-announcement trading range of $11-12, implying downside in excess of 50%. The market is pricing very high deal certainty, consistent with the absence of a financing condition and a relatively clean regulatory profile (two niche medtech franchises with limited horizontal overlap with a PE acquirer). The implied probability of a break is low, but the payoff is convex against the holder.

The standalone business underneath the deal is a cash-generative niche medtech with a strong Specialty Nutrition Systems (SNS) growth engine, a structurally challenged Pain Management & Recovery (PM&R) surgical-pain line, persistent tariff-driven margin compression, and a now-cleaned-up portfolio after multiple divestitures. That profile is what a value-oriented industrial PE buyer targets. For a public-market holder at the current price, the business fundamentals matter only insofar as they govern deal-completion risk. We rate AVNS HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale: there is no positive 12-month appreciation thesis at $24.98, but the dominant probability path (deal closes at $25) does not warrant forcing a sale either. New money has no reason to enter.

## Company Overview

Avanos Medical (formerly Halyard Health, spun off from Kimberly-Clark in 2014; headquartered in Alpharetta, GA; ~2,287 employees) is a small-cap medical technology company operating two reportable segments:

- **Specialty Nutrition Systems (SNS)** — approximately 62% of segment sales and the clear growth and profit engine. Includes enteral feeding (MIC-KEY tubes, Corpak, each exceeding 10% of consolidated sales) and neonatal solutions (NeoMed), plus the recently acquired Nexus TKO anti-reflux needleless connectors. SNS grew 9.2% in FY2025 and 22.7% in Q1 2026 (with ~19% volume growth).
- **Pain Management & Recovery (PM&R)** — non-opioid pain solutions. Two sub-lines: radiofrequency ablation (RFA: COOLIEF, Trident, ESENTEC, growing ~10%) and surgical pain & recovery (ON-Q, ambIT pumps, Game Ready, declining ~8-11%). PM&R is barely profitable on a consolidated basis and was the subject of a $77M goodwill impairment in Q4 2025.

Avanos holds leading positions in niche categories but competes against far larger players: Boston Scientific in both segments, Stryker/Medtronic/Pacira in pain, and Cardinal Health/Cook in nutrition. It sells primarily through distributors (Medline ~12% of 2025 sales) and GPOs (~29% of global sales). Manufacturing is heavily concentrated in Mexico (1,324 of 2,287 employees) under Maquiladora status, plus Canada.

## Financial Analysis

The financials reflect a company that has shrunk and restructured into a smaller, higher-quality core, with significant non-cash distortion in the GAAP figures.

**Revenue trajectory:** $820M (2022, including subsequently divested businesses) → $673M (2023) → $688M (2024) → $701M (2025, +1.9%). The multi-year decline reflects divestitures (Respiratory Health sold for $110M in 2023; the HA product line and Game Ready rental business sold in 2025). On a continuing-segment basis, the core is growing: segment net sales rose 6.4% in 2025, and Q1 2026 total revenue grew 8.8% YoY to $182.2M.

**GAAP profitability is distorted by impairments.** Operating income was -$61.6M in 2025 and -$396.2M in 2024, driven almost entirely by goodwill/intangible impairments ($77M in 2025; $436.7M in 2024). Adjusted operating profit was positive at $67.1M (2025) and $87.3M (2024). Net loss was -$72.9M (2025) and -$392.1M (2024). Segment operating income, the cleaner read on underlying economics, is healthy and growing: $91.8M (2025) vs. $83.5M (2024) vs. $76.5M (2023). The accumulated deficit of -$775M is a legacy artifact of impairments and the spin structure, not a going-concern signal.

**Margins under pressure.** Gross margin compressed to 50.5% in 2025 from 55.4% in 2024, driven primarily by tariffs on Mexico/Canada/China sourcing and exited low-margin product pricing. This is a recurring structural pressure, not a one-time event, given the Mexico manufacturing concentration.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $74.7M (2025) vs. $100.7M (2024). Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative at -$12.3M (vs. +$25.7M in Q1 2025), driven by a $33.7M draw-down in accrued expenses (bonus/severance payouts), a seasonal/timing effect rather than business deterioration. Cash fell to $65.6M.

**Balance sheet is solid.** Altman Z-Score 3.46 (safe zone), current ratio 2.15, debt/equity 0.38. Total debt is only ~$98M (a term loan), the revolver is undrawn, and the company is in covenant compliance. This balance-sheet strength is one reason the deal lacks a financing condition and one reason AIP found the asset attractive.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely two-sided and is the basis for AIP's value-creation thesis, but it is largely irrelevant to a public holder at the current price.

The SNS engine is performing exceptionally, augmented by the Nexus acquisition (which contributed $5.8M of Q1 revenue). The RFA sub-line within PM&R is growing ~10%. Against that, the surgical-pain franchise (ON-Q, ambIT) is in structural decline at -8% to -11%, and R&D intensity is low and falling — $23.3M in 2025 (~3.3% of sales) versus $27.2M in 2023 — a yellow flag for long-term competitiveness that a private owner would address away from quarterly scrutiny.

Estimate revisions are mixed and now low-signal because consensus coverage has collapsed to a single analyst and the equity is a deal stub. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 33.3% over 90 days while next-quarter estimates rose 37.5% and full-year estimates rose 5.3%, with no 30-day movement. The strong four-quarter earnings beat record (54.1%, 14.6%, 79.7%, 11.1% surprises) confirms operating momentum but no longer informs the equity's return, which is bounded by the $25 offer.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation here is a deal-spread calculation, not a multiple exercise. With an expected close in H2 2026, the annualized return to a successful close is effectively zero net of any borrow or opportunity cost. The forward P/E of ~20x is a residual data artifact; it neither supports nor opposes the position because the equity converts to cash at $25 if the deal closes.

The peer benchmark provided (BridgeBio at a negative P/E) is not a meaningful comparable for a profitable niche medtech and, in any case, is moot for a take-private at a fixed price. The relevant valuation anchors are: (1) the $25.00 deal price as the ceiling, and (2) the ~$11-12 pre-announcement trading range as the downside floor if the deal breaks. AIP's $25 offer represented roughly a 72% premium to the $14.53 close on April 13 and a ~127% premium to the August 2025 lows. The premium is generous relative to standalone fundamentals, which reduces the probability of a competing-bid topping scenario (no realistic "Superior Proposal" upside) given the $37.5M termination fee and no-shop provision.

We score valuation at 5.0: the price offers no asymmetric upside (capped at $25, currently realized) and embeds material asymmetric downside, which is the textbook definition of a non-attractive entry. The cash-generative core is real, so this is not a value trap in the impairment sense, but as a tradeable security the risk/reward is unfavorable to a new buyer.

## Competitive Landscape

Avanos plays in fragmented niches where no single competitor spans its full portfolio, but each product line faces a deeper-pocketed rival. Its moat is brand recognition (MIC-KEY, COOLIEF), clinical efficacy data, and an established distribution/GPO network rather than scale or R&D intensity. The SNS franchise has durable category leadership in enteral and neonatal feeding; the RFA franchise is a credible non-opioid pain player benefiting from the structural shift away from opioids. The surgical-pain line is losing share. Falling R&D spend is a competitive headwind over a multi-year horizon, which is part of why a private owner with a longer time horizon and operational focus is the natural buyer. Trajectory is mixed-to-stable, warranting a 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile bifurcates between deal risk (now dominant) and operating risk (relevant only to the deal-break downside).

**Deal risks:**
- **Non-completion.** Closing conditions include majority stockholder approval and HSR antitrust clearance. The absence of a financing condition removes one major risk. A break would likely send the stock toward the ~$11-12 pre-deal range, implying 50%+ downside.
- **Termination fee and no-shop.** A $37.5M termination fee and no-shop provision cap competing-bid optionality, removing realistic upside above $25.
- **Pendency disruption.** Employee attrition (after heavy management turnover), customer/supplier decision deferral, and ordinary-course covenant constraints during the interim period.

**Operating risks (matter mainly if the deal breaks):**
- **Medicare RFA reimbursement threat.** Several Medicare Administrative Contractors published draft Local Coverage Determinations in October 2025 questioning whether RFA for peripheral nerve pain is "reasonable and necessary." A negative final determination would eliminate Medicare reimbursement in those jurisdictions and directly hit the fast-growing ~$139M RFA franchise. As of early June 2026 the matter remains unresolved and active. This is the most plausible lever for a price renegotiation, though standard MAE clauses typically exclude general regulatory/industry developments, which limits its use as a deal-break trigger.
- **Tariffs / Mexico concentration.** The Mexico manufacturing concentration already compressed gross margin ~500bps. USMCA renegotiation or new tariffs are an ongoing threat.
- **Customer concentration.** Distributor mix is volatile (McKesson was 18% in 2024, dropped below the 10% disclosure threshold in 2025; Medline ~12%), creating pricing leverage against Avanos.
- **Residual goodwill at risk.** $394M of goodwill survived recent impairments by a thin margin — moot if the deal closes.

We score risk factors at 6.0. The dominant risk (deal-break) is low-probability but high-magnitude and is asymmetric against the holder; the operating risks are real but secondary while the merger is in process.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 22.9% to 9.2% between early and mid-May 2026 as the price held near $24.80, the signature of a stock pinned to a cash-deal price: with the equity converting to $25 on close, realized and implied volatility compress toward zero, and the options market prices little remaining uncertainty. The low IV confirms high market-implied deal certainty rather than offering any directional information. Short interest is negligible (3.3% of float, 1.0 days to cover), consistent with a near-dead arbitrage rather than a contested situation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (deal closes at $25)
The merger completes on its current terms in H2 2026. With no financing condition, a generous premium that discourages renegotiation, limited antitrust overlap (a PE buyer with no competing medtech franchises), and an MAE clause that likely excludes the regulatory RFA development, the base case is a clean close. Holders collect $25.00, a near-zero return from $24.98. Realistic ceiling: $25.00.

### Bear Case (deal breaks)
HSR clearance stalls, AIP attempts a price cut citing the Medicare RFA overhang and tariff-driven margin erosion, or the agreement terminates for another reason. The stock reverts toward the pre-announcement $11-12 range, where it traded as recently as late 2025. Downside from $24.98 is approximately 52-56%. This is the low-probability, high-magnitude tail that defines the risk/reward.

The thesis is dominated by deal mechanics. The standalone fundamentals (cash-generative core, SNS growth, structural surgical-pain decline) are AIP's problem now and the basis for the price floor only in the break scenario.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Tied to the merger calendar — stockholder vote, HSR clearance, and expected H2 2026 close (outside date January 13, 2027). Monitor the final Medicare LCD determination as the principal interim wildcard.

**Upgrade triggers (to a deliberate arbitrage long for spread-focused capital):** A widening of the gross spread to a level that compensates for residual deal risk on an annualized basis (for example, the price drifting to $24.50 or below on non-fundamental flow while completion conditions remain on track), combined with continued progress on HSR clearance and a benign or resolved Medicare LCD.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID/exit):** (1) A second-request or extended HSR review signaling antitrust friction; (2) any public indication from AIP of a price renegotiation, most plausibly tied to an adverse final Medicare peripheral-RFA determination; (3) failure to secure stockholder approval; (4) any 8-K disclosing a material breach or termination-related dispute. Any of these would materially raise break probability and shift the asymmetry decisively against holders.

**For new capital:** No reason to initiate at $24.98. The reward is capped at ~$0.02 and the tail risk is ~50%+. For existing holders, the position is a hold-to-close on the dominant probability path, with the above downgrade triggers as the exit discipline.

## Conclusion

Avanos trades at $24.98 against a $25.00 all-cash take-private by American Industrial Partners, with no financing condition and an H2 2026 expected close. The investment question is binary deal completion, and the market is pricing high certainty: a ~0.1% gross spread and IV collapsed to ~9%. There is no standalone appreciation thesis at this price; the reward is effectively realized, and the residual risk is an asymmetric ~50%+ downside to the ~$11-12 pre-announcement range if the deal breaks on antitrust or a Medicare-RFA-driven renegotiation. The underlying business is a legitimately cash-generative niche medtech with a strong SNS growth engine and a clean balance sheet (Z-score 3.46), but those fundamentals govern only the break-scenario floor, not the holder's return.

We rate AVNS **HOLD** (uncertain rationale). Existing holders can hold to close given the high implied completion probability, while monitoring the HSR process and the final Medicare LCD as the key deviation risks. New capital should not enter — the spread does not compensate for the tail, and there is no growth or income thesis to underwrite an outright buy.
