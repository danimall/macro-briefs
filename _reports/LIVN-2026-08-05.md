## Executive Summary

LivaNova reported a second quarter that was strong on the income statement and weak on cash. Revenue of $390.6M grew 10.8% reported and 9.8% constant-currency, beating a ~$380.6M consensus, and adjusted diluted EPS of $1.26 beat the ~$1.09 estimate by roughly 15.6% — the fourth consecutive beat, with an average surprise of approximately 10.3% across the last four quarters. Management raised full-year constant-currency revenue growth guidance to +8-9% (from +7-8% in May and +6-7% in February) and adjusted EPS to $4.30-4.40. Adjusted operating margin expanded 129bp year-over-year to 23.2%, and GAAP gross margin reached 70.1% from 67.8%.

Against that, adjusted free cash flow guidance was cut to $140-160M from $160-180M, attributed to Cardiopulmonary capacity expansion, innovation, and IT investment. First-half adjusted free cash flow reconstructs to roughly $47M, which requires $93-113M in the second half — 2.0 to 2.4 times the first half — to reach the reduced range. Cash conversion implied by guidance is approximately 61% of adjusted net income (~$150M of adjusted FCF against ~$244M of adjusted earnings at a 56M share count). Segment detail contained a second concern: US Cardiopulmonary revenue grew 2.2% in the quarter against Europe's +26.3% reported, after US CP was +7.6% for the half. The double-digit Cardiopulmonary headline currently rests on two of three geographies.

The dominant variable remains unresolved. A $385.0M SNIA environmental liability, recognized following the Italian Supreme Court's March 14, 2025 decision, sits in **current** liabilities — the company's own judgment that a cash demand within twelve months is possible. A Court of Appeal hearing to discuss out-of-court resolution was scheduled for June 24, 2026; the August 5 release makes no mention of a settlement and the liability remains carried at full value. Against $516.6M of cash, payment at the accrued amount would absorb roughly 75% of the balance, partially offset by an Italian tax ruling that confirmed deductibility and produced a €81.8M ($95.4M) discrete benefit. At 18.2x the FY2026 guidance midpoint, the discount to the peer-median 27.2x P/E is explained by this claim rather than by mispricing. Base case $85, bear case $63. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

LivaNova is a UK-domiciled, Nasdaq-listed medical technology company formed from the 2015 combination of Italy's Sorin S.p.A. and US-based Cyberonics, with roughly 3,300 employees and sales in more than 100 countries. Two reportable segments:

**Cardiopulmonary (~57% of Q2 2026 revenue, $221.6M)** — oxygenators, heart-lung machines (the Essenz Perfusion System), cannulae and cardiopulmonary bypass disposables. The model is razor/razor-blade: console placements pull through recurring consumables. Management has cited ten consecutive quarters of double-digit growth.

**Neuromodulation (~43%, $166.9M)** — VNS Therapy for drug-resistant epilepsy, where the company holds the dominant installed base and a replacement-generator annuity, plus two expansion vectors: difficult-to-treat depression via the RECOVER program and CMS coverage-with-evidence-development, and obstructive sleep apnea via the aura6000 proximal hypoglossal nerve stimulation system (PMA granted March 19, 2026; launch expected H1 2027).

Geographic mix in Q2 2026: US $199.4M (51%), Europe $81.8M (21%), rest of world $109.4M (28%).

The commercial franchises are narrow but defensible. Epilepsy VNS carries decades of physician familiarity and a replacement cycle no competitor has replicated at scale, and Q2 growth of +9.5% cc came from volume *and* favorable price — pricing power in a mature medtech category is unusual. Cardiopulmonary is a consolidated oligopoly (LivaNova, Getinge/Maquet, Medtronic, Terumo, Nipro) protected by Class III/PMA and EU MDR regulatory burden plus perfusionist switching costs. The company's current stated constraint in that segment is manufacturing capacity: the Q2 release cites unmet oxygenator demand and a long-term agreement under which LivaNova purchases a critical oxygenator component from Thermo Fisher Scientific. LivaNova is the customer in that arrangement; Thermo Fisher is a component supplier, not a development partner.

### Margins

GAAP operating income fell to $49.5M in Q2 2026 from $54.2M, and to $91.0M for the half from $102.8M, even as revenue grew 12.5%. The drivers are identifiable: $12.4M of other operating expense, $10.5M of ImThera contingent-consideration remeasurement running through R&D, and share-based compensation rising to $25.2M from $17.0M. Adjusted operating income rose to $161.9M for the half from $141.9M, but the adjusted margin improved only ~30bp (21.5% vs 21.2%) because the first quarter's adjusted margin contracted to approximately 19.6% from 20.4% before Q2 rebounded 129bp to 23.2%.

The add-back stack deserves scrutiny. First-half adjustments totaled roughly $70.9M against $91.0M of GAAP operating income: $10.9M purchase-accounting D&A, $10.5M contingent consideration, $24.4M "certain legal and regulatory costs," and $25.2M share-based compensation. Two of those are economically real. Share-based compensation dilutes shareholders. Legal and regulatory costs — 3T Heater-Cooler defense, Saluggia remediation provisions, EU MDR compliance — have appeared as an add-back in every period examined. Stripping both back out gives $112.3M of operating income on $752.8M of revenue, a 14.9% margin that sits much closer to the GAAP 12.1% than to the adjusted 21.5%. The counterargument has merit: SNIA, Saluggia and 3T all stem from pre-2004 Sorin activity and will eventually terminate. But "eventually" is doing significant work in that sentence.

### The GAAP/non-GAAP inversion

Q2 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.93 exceeded adjusted EPS of $1.26 because $110.6M of net "certain tax adjustments" were removed to reach the adjusted figure, reflecting a $90.2M income tax benefit. The driver was an Agenzia delle Entrate ruling confirming deductibility of the recorded SNIA liability, producing a €81.8M ($95.4M) discrete benefit; deferred tax assets rose $87.7M to $198.7M, corroborating the mechanism. The relevant comparison to consensus is non-GAAP against non-GAAP: $1.26 versus ~$1.09. Anyone anchoring to the $1.93 headline is measuring a tax event, not operating performance.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance has been raised twice, which is the strongest thing in the story. But the arithmetic embedded in that guidance points down, not up.

**Revenue.** The FY constant-currency midpoint of +8.5% on FY2025 revenue of $1,388.1M implies ~$1,505.9M. Subtracting first-half constant-currency revenue ($669.4M × 1.104 = $738.8M) leaves ~$767.1M for the second half against H2 2025 of $718.7M — approximately **+6.7% cc, versus +10.4% cc in the first half.** On a reported basis the deceleration is wider, because the FX tailwind that contributed 2.1 points in the first half is guided to ~1 point for the full year, implying it is roughly neutral in H2.

**Earnings.** First-half adjusted diluted EPS was $2.24. The $4.35 FY midpoint leaves **$2.11 for the second half, about 6% below the first half**, on higher revenue. The plausible explanations are OSA launch-preparation spend ahead of H1 2027 commercialization, capacity-expansion operating costs, and a possibly higher adjusted tax rate. Conservatism is a competing explanation, and this management has beaten four consecutive quarters, so guidance discipline is real. But investors extrapolating first-half double-digit reported growth are extrapolating something the company is not guiding to.

**Consensus.** Estimates are stable rather than inflecting: current-year $4.25 (+1.0% over 90 days), next-year $4.63 (+1.3%), with the next-quarter estimate actually cut 4.5% over 90 days. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $4.63 implies +6.4% growth over the FY2026 guidance midpoint. This is not the estimate-revision profile that signals a market systematically underestimating an inflection; it is a high-single-digit compounder with stable expectations. I am explicitly noting that the strong revision-velocity buy signal is **absent** here.

**Optionality.** OSA is the genuine expansion vector. aura6000 received PMA with what the company describes as the first HGNS label lacking complete-concentric-collapse contraindication or warning language, plus MRI compatibility. Twelve-month OSPREY results were published in *Annals of Internal Medicine* on April 20, 2026, and the PolySync programming algorithm reportedly lifted cumulative AHI response from 65% to 84.5%. Two qualifiers matter. Launch is H1 2027, so the P&L carries cost without revenue for at least four more quarters. And the incumbent, Inspire Medical, was already decelerating to a reported ~4-10% FY26 revenue growth guide before aura6000 became commercially available — a pattern more consistent with category-level headwinds (prior-authorization friction, GLP-1 pharmacotherapy as an alternative OSA treatment) than with competitive displacement. Entering a category whose leader is decelerating for reasons unrelated to your entry argues for restraint in capitalizing the option.

**Growth Prospects: 6.4** (down from 6.8). Guidance raised twice is real; the guided second-half deceleration to ~6.7% cc revenue and a ~6% half-over-half adjusted EPS decline, plus a next-year consensus growth rate of 6.4%, cap the score.

The peer median P/E of 27.2x (ITGR 23.2x, LMAT 34.0x, UFPT 27.2x, TMDX 16.5x; TFX's 4,212x is meaningless) implies a 33% discount. The relevant question is whether that discount is a mispricing or a correctly priced claim. My view is the latter, for three reasons.

First, the SNIA liability is a live negotiation with no disclosed outcome and a wide distribution. Full payment absorbs ~75% of cash and would likely require re-drawing the revolver. Settlement at a discount releases cash — but the tax ruling cuts both ways: the €81.8M deferred tax asset scales with the amount actually deducted, so a settlement at, say, €200M rather than €337.8M would reverse roughly €33M of the asset (24% × €137.8M). The liability and the tax asset must be modeled together. Net-net a favorable settlement is clearly positive, just less than the headline suggests.

Second, cash conversion of 61% means the FCF yield is 3.45%, not the ~5.6% an 18x earnings multiple would normally imply. Buyers of medtech at 18x usually get better cash characteristics.

Third, the growth rate does not support a premium re-rating. PEG on FY2027 consensus growth of 6.4% is approximately 2.7. Even on the FY2026 EPS growth rate (roughly +10-13% over an estimated FY2025 adjusted base near $3.85-4.00), PEG is around 1.5-1.8.

Analyst targets: mean $84.80, median $84.00, high $95.00, low $67.00. The mean sits 7.1% above the current price. The consensus-as-floor argument applies to cheap stocks with accelerating estimates; here estimates are flat and the multiple is mid-teens-to-high-teens against high-single-digit growth, so I treat the target distribution as informative context rather than a floor. Recommendation distribution is 2 strong buy / 6 buy / 1 hold / 1 strong sell — a notable single bear.

**Valuation: 5.0** (up marginally from 4.8). The 7.2% single-session decline plus the raised guidance improves the forward multiple from roughly 19.0x to 18.2x. That is a better entry, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

**Epilepsy VNS** is the most defensible asset: dominant installed base, replacement-generator annuity, and Q2 growth of +9.5% cc driven by volume and price. Price realization in a mature franchise implies limited direct substitution pressure. Trajectory: stable-to-improving.

**Cardiopulmonary** presents an unusual disclosure — the company's stated problem in its faster-growing segment is that it cannot make enough product. Unmet oxygenator demand, favorable realized price, ten-plus quarters of double-digit growth, and +23.8% cc European growth are collectively more consistent with a capacity-constrained market in which a competitor has stumbled than with organic market expansion. Two readings are available: a competitor supply or quality disruption has redirected volume (Europe the primary beneficiary), or Essenz is winning console conversions on merit with consumables following. The distinction matters, because share taken from a temporarily impaired competitor is more reversible. The US +2.2% print is the datapoint most consistent with the geography-specific reading — though it is also consistent with management allocating scarce oxygenator output to higher-price or faster-growing geographies, which would make weak US growth a consequence of the supply constraint rather than a demand signal. Neither mechanism is verified.

**OSA** is a share-gain opportunity against a decelerating incumbent, with a differentiated label and mechanism (proximal stimulation, no respiratory sensing lead). Commercially unproven until H1 2027.

**Structural pressures** visible in the company's own disclosures: EU MDR recertification cost, Italian payback claw-backs ($13.3M reserved covering 2019 onward), tariff uncertainty, and hospital capital-budget cyclicality on console placements.

**Competitive Position: 6.4.** Trajectory is improving in two of three franchises, with a genuine unexplained concentration in the European CP result and a stalled US quarter arguing against a higher score.

## Risk Assessment

**SNIA environmental liability — $385.0M, current, unresolved.** The dominant risk. Arising from the environmental legacy of SNIA S.p.A., from which Sorin was demerged in 2004; $365.6M of expense recognized in FY2025 following the March 14, 2025 Corte di Cassazione decision. A Court of Appeal hearing to discuss out-of-court resolution was set for June 24, 2026; the August 5 release contains no settlement disclosure and the liability is carried at full value. Absence of an announcement is not evidence of failure — Italian appellate settlement processes are slow and the hearing may have been adjourned — but it is also not progress.

**Saluggia remediation.** Approximately $41.0M accrued at the low end of a range topping near $54.6M, with additional provisions taken in Q2.

**3T Heater-Cooler.** Approximately 60 claims pending as of May 6, 2026, but the current litigation provision rose from $12.6M to $20.1M in the half with $7.8M of provision increase in the cash flow statement. Thin tail, not closed.

**Supply-chain single point.** The Thermo Fisher component agreement places a supplier on the critical path of the faster-growing segment's growth rate. Supply-chain pressure is a named safe-harbor risk.

**Convertible-note derivative.** The bifurcated conversion feature means GAAP earnings move inversely with the share price. The net derivative liability widened from $47.3M to $95.6M over the half, consistent with the $52.2M non-cash remeasurement charge in the cash flow statement. Above $94.28 the capped call stops offsetting economic dilution.

**Contingent dilution, stated precisely.** Current diluted share count is 56.3M against 55.0M basic. Maximum contingent dilution from the 2029 notes is approximately 4.97M shares ($345M ÷ $69.40), equal to 9.0% of existing basic shares and an 8.3% ownership dilution for current holders. This is conditional on the ~$90.22 conversion trigger being met over the specified measurement period and on the company electing share settlement.

**Governance and controls.** Internal control effectiveness appears as a named safe-harbor risk. Shareholder activism was added as a standalone risk factor in the FY2025 10-K and appears in the August 2026 safe-harbor list, with no named activist identified. FY2025 cumulative TSR of 92.93 against a peer group at 126.87 — roughly −7.1% versus +26.9%, a 34-point shortfall — provides objective context for why such a risk factor might now appear.

**Intangible concentration.** Goodwill plus intangibles of $996.1M equals 75% of book equity. The $13.7M decline in the half is consistent with FX translation; no impairment charge appears.

**Solvency screening.** A reconstructed Altman Z-Score using FY2025 balance-sheet data and current market cap gives a subtotal of 2.755 before the retained-earnings term, which is negative given the cumulative deficit (FY2021 −$135.8M, FY2022 −$86.2M, FY2025 −$242.5M). That places the score in the 1.8-2.8 grey zone rather than the safe zone. Two observations: 1.835 of the 2.755 comes from the market-value term, so the score deteriorates rapidly on a share-price decline; and the EBIT term correctly excludes the SNIA charge, which sits below the operating line.

**Risk Factors: 4.5.** Three unresolved legal/environmental claims, a supplier on the critical growth path, a derivative that generates GAAP losses when the equity rises, and conditional dilution roughly 14% above the current price. These are specific and largely unpriced beyond the multiple discount.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 45.6% against 36.8% one-year realized is rich by 8.8 points, at the 74th percentile within the chain, with the term structure in backwardation (45.6% at one month versus 39.9% at six months). ATM IV compressed from 52.3% on July 19 to 45.6%, so some event premium has drained, but the residual near-term premium after an earnings release already occurred is notable — the likely candidates are the Q2 10-Q filing and any SNIA disclosure it contains. The August 21 tenor prices a ±8.2% move.

Put/call open interest of 3.06 is heavily put-weighted, indicating protection held in size, while the reported skew of −5.3% is characterized as call-heavy at the front tenor. Those two readings are not contradictory: longer-dated protection can coexist with near-dated upside speculation after a strong run. Short interest of 8.6% of float at 5.4 days to cover is moderate — enough to fuel a squeeze on a favorable SNIA headline, not enough to make the bear side crowded.

Net read: the options market is pricing genuine near-term binary risk consistent with an unresolved legal claim, and is not corroborating a directional bull case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $95 (+20%)

The operating business is compounding faster than the multiple implies. Constant-currency revenue growth was raised twice in six months, gross margin reached 70.1%, and adjusted operating margin hit 23.2% in the quarter. Cardiopulmonary is demand-constrained rather than demand-limited — the Thermo Fisher agreement and capacity capex convert that constraint into revenue through 2027. Epilepsy VNS delivers volume and price. aura6000 launches in H1 2027 into a category whose incumbent is stumbling, with a label advantage on complete concentric collapse that expands the addressable population. SNIA settles below the accrual, releasing cash and removing an overhang that has capped the multiple; the Italian tax ruling means the after-tax cost of any settlement is roughly 24% lower than the headline. Applying 20x to a FY2027 EPS of ~$4.75 gives roughly $95; the analyst high of $95.00 and the capped-call cap at $94.28 both point to $95 as the practical bull target.

### Bear Case — $63 (−20%)

The guided second-half deceleration is real, not conservatism: constant-currency growth falls to ~6.7% and adjusted EPS declines ~6% half-over-half. US Cardiopulmonary's +2.2% turns out to be the start of a trend rather than an allocation artifact, and the European +26.3% proves to be a share bolus from a temporarily impaired competitor that reverses. The second-half cash ramp of 2.0-2.4x does not materialize and adjusted FCF lands below $140M, marking the second consecutive reduction and confirming that 61% conversion is structural rather than transitional. SNIA settles at or near the accrual, consuming ~75% of cash and requiring revolver draw, with roughly $33M+ of the deferred tax asset intact but the equity de-rated. Fourteen times a FY2027 EPS of $4.40 gives $62; the analyst low is $67. Call it $63.

### Where I come out

Both cases are live and neither is resolvable from present disclosure. The base case is that the operating business delivers guidance, cash conversion improves seasonally but stays below 70%, and SNIA resolves at a modest discount at an undetermined date. That supports 18.4x FY2027 consensus of $4.63 = **$85**, roughly at the analyst median of $84.00. Upside to base is 7.3%; downside to bear is 20%. That asymmetry does not support new capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** This is the third consecutive HOLD after two BUYs, and the score declines to 5.67 from 6.0. The bar for repeating a rating should rise with repetition, so to be explicit about what is new rather than restated: the adjusted FCF guidance cut to $140-160M, the confirmation in the August 5 release that no SNIA resolution has been reached or recognized four-plus months after the settlement hearing, US Cardiopulmonary decelerating to +2.2%, and the arithmetic showing guidance itself implies ~6.7% cc second-half growth and a ~6% half-over-half adjusted EPS decline. The 7.2% single-session price decline is not the reason for the downgrade — a lower price on unchanged fundamentals improves risk/reward, and I have marked valuation *up* slightly to reflect the better entry. The score reduction comes from the identified cash-flow and segment deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, expected within days, for SNIA and Saluggia footnote language and the exact retained-earnings and revolver-capacity figures. Then the Q3 2026 release (early November) for the first hard read on the second-half cash ramp.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **SNIA resolution below approximately €200M** (or definitive disclosure of a settlement framework), which would release cash net of the associated deferred-tax-asset reversal and remove the multiple cap. This alone would justify re-rating toward 21x.
2. **Adjusted free cash flow of $90M+ in the second half** with capex normalizing, confirming that 61% conversion is transitional and lifting the FCF yield above 4%.
3. **US Cardiopulmonary growth returning above 7%** for two consecutive quarters alongside evidence that European CP growth is retained rather than reversing — this would validate the Essenz-conversion reading over the transient-share-shift reading.
4. A price near **$66-68** with fundamentals intact, which would put the stock at roughly 14.5x FY2027 consensus against high-single-digit growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **A second reduction to adjusted FCF guidance**, or full-year adjusted FCF below $140M.
2. **SNIA settled at or above the $385.0M accrual with cash payment inside twelve months**, requiring revolver draw and eliminating the net-cash framing.
3. **US Cardiopulmonary growth below 3% for a second consecutive quarter** combined with European CP decelerating below 10% cc, which would indicate the segment's growth was a geographic bolus.
4. Any **internal-control deficiency disclosure** in the Q2 or Q3 filings, given that control effectiveness now appears as a named risk.

**Position guidance:** existing holders should own through the 10-Q and the Q3 print. New capital should wait for either SNIA resolution or a price in the mid-$60s. The convertible mechanics create a natural upside brake: the $90.22 conversion trigger and $94.28 capped-call cap sit 14% and 19% above the current price, and above the cap economic dilution is no longer hedged.

## Conclusion

LivaNova is a genuinely improving mid-cap medtech carrying a legacy liability large enough to dominate its equity story. The operating evidence is good: four consecutive adjusted-EPS beats averaging approximately 10.3% surprise, 70.1% gross margin, 23.2% adjusted operating margin in the quarter, guidance raised twice, $95.9M of debt retired, and pricing power in both segments. The problems are equally concrete: adjusted free cash flow guidance cut to $140-160M requiring a 2.0-2.4x second-half ramp, 61% cash conversion, a guided second-half slowdown to ~6.7% constant-currency growth with adjusted EPS declining half-over-half, US Cardiopulmonary at +2.2%, and a $385.0M SNIA claim sitting in current liabilities with no disclosed resolution more than four months after the settlement hearing.
