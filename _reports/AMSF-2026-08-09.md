**Research Date: August 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AMERISAFE is a monoline workers' compensation carrier serving small-to-mid-sized employers in high-hazard industries: construction (47.5% of FY2025 gross premiums written), trucking, logging, agriculture, and maritime. The franchise is genuine: 40 years of proprietary loss data, 93.4% of new voluntary policyholders receiving a pre-quotation on-site safety inspection, 51 open indemnity claims per field case manager, ~1,400 independent agencies with none above 2.0% of in-force premium, and top-ten policyholders at 2.1% of premium. Ten consecutive reserve years have developed favorably, with the 12/31/2015 net reserve of $653.2M re-estimated 36.4% lower. The company carries zero financial debt, an A.M. Best "A" rating, all thirteen 2025 IRIS ratios within usual values, and RBC above minimums.

The reported earnings stream and the underwriting stream have separated. Adding back prior-year development, H1 2026 booked a current-accident-year combined ratio of approximately 104.1% (72.0% loss pick + 30.8% expense + 1.3% policyholder dividend), versus 94.3% reported. Every dollar of reported underwriting profit comes from re-estimating the past. The base for that re-estimation is shrinking: net reserves fell from $583.5M (2022) to $491.8M (6/30/26), gross IBNR collapsed 40.1% in 2025 alone to 10.5% of gross reserves from 17.8% in 2023, H1 2026 releases were $14.9M against $17.4M a year earlier (-14.4%), and net investment income has declined three straight years to $27.0M despite higher market rates because the portfolio is being liquidated to fund distributions. Operating cash flow was $11.1M in FY2025 and -$0.9M in H1 2026 against a ~$30.4M regular dividend run-rate.

Since the prior review the stock has fallen from $31.32 to $29.12 (-7.0%), with price decline and fundamental deterioration moving together. The Q2 print (filed 2026-07-23) showed the combined ratio deteriorating sequentially within 2026 (93.2% → 95.4%), underwriting profit down 37.9% year over year, and operating EPS down roughly 17% on a consistent basis; consensus 2026 EPS has been cut 6.4% in 90 days and 2027 by 3.2%; and the company has now missed four consecutive quarters. At $29.12 the stock is 2.16x a book value that has itself fallen from $15.28 (12/31/23) to $13.49, and roughly 14.4-14.9x forward consensus that is still being revised downward. The 5.5-5.6% yield is covered by GAAP earnings and by statutory dividend capacity, but it is funded from a self-consuming source. We downgrade to REDUCE: base case $30.50 (+4.7%), bull $38 (+30.5%), bear $21.50 (-26.2%), with probability weight skewed toward base and bear.

## Company Overview

AMERISAFE writes one product for one customer type through three insurance subsidiaries (American Interstate Insurance Company and Silver Oak Casualty, Nebraska-domiciled; American Interstate Insurance Company of Texas). The economic logic is that hazardous-industry employers generate less frequent but more severe claims and therefore pay substantially higher rates per payroll dollar; high-touch underwriting and in-house claims handling are intended to convert that rate into above-average returns. Underwriting authority is not delegated, no contingent commissions are paid, and all voluntary policyholders are premium-audited annually against a ~15% deposit collected at inception.

Addressable market: NCCI class codes AMERISAFE underwrites totaled $18.4B of direct premiums in 2024 against FY2025 gross premiums written of $313.9M, roughly 1.7% share, with no more than 5.0% share in any single state and 300+ competitors in workers' comp overall. The largest state is Florida at 16.3% of GPW (up from 13.4% two years earlier), with the top twelve states at 72.3%.

The distribution and policyholder granularity are genuine structural strengths. The pricing environment has changed: the FY2025 10-K states that market conditions are affected by "lower estimated loss costs adopted by a number of states," and that the 72.0% accident-year 2026 loss pick reflects "pressure from continued rate decreases and long-term claim frequency and severity trends, as well as medical inflation." Renewal retention on business elected for renewal slipped to 93.1% (2025) from 94.2% (2024).

Ten points of combined-ratio deterioration in four years, with Q2 2026 at 95.4% against 91.7% a year earlier and Q1 2026 at 93.2%. The deterioration is now occurring within the current year, not just against prior-year comparables.

Reconstructing the current accident year as booked: 72.0% + 30.8% + 1.3% = 104.1% for H1 2026; 72.0% + 30.4% + 0.9% = 103.3% for FY2025. This ties to the disclosed figures (reported net loss ratio 62.2% plus $14.908M favorable development ÷ $152.345M net premiums earned = 9.79 points → 72.0%). Prior-year releases were 57.5% of FY2025 pretax income and 52.4% of H1 2026 pretax income.

AMERISAFE has a documented practice of conservative initial picks, harvested later, and every one of the last ten reserve years has developed favorably. A 104% booked current year may simply be conservatism that gets released in 2028-2030 exactly as the 2018-2020 years were. The bear case does not require the reserving to be wrong; it requires the conservatism margin to have narrowed. The IBNR data supports that concern without proving it.

### The IBNR drawdown

| Gross case loss & DCC | 535,116 | 523,695 | 529,396 |

IBNR fell $42.98M (-40.1%) in 2025 while case reserves rose $5.70M (+1.1%). The reconciliation is exact: -$42.981M + $5.701M - $0.446M (AO) = -$37.726M, the entire change in gross reserves. IBNR as a share of gross reserves went 17.8% → 16.5% → 10.5%.

Two readings are consistent with these numbers, and public disclosure does not distinguish them. The benign reading: IBNR should decline as the tail shortens, and AMERISAFE discloses $105.7M (present value) of purchased annuities that permanently extinguish long-duration claim uncertainty, plus 3,847 claims closed in 2025. The adverse reading: the cushion available for future favorable development is materially thinner. As a sensitivity rather than a forecast, restoring the 2024 ratio of 16.5% would require $101.2M of gross IBNR against the actual $64.3M, a $36.9M gross gap, roughly $30.5M net at the observed 17.3% reinsurance share, or ~$24M after tax, approximately 10% of 6/30/26 equity. The 10-K's own disclosed sensitivity is comparable: a ±30% change in incurred loss development factors moves net reserves ±5.1% (+$24.4M / -$24.0M).

We draw no inference about management intent from the accounting pattern. We do treat the reduced cushion as a genuine reduction in the option value that historically justified a premium book multiple.

### The release engine's mechanics

One-year favorable development as a percentage of opening net reserves: 9.40% (2018), 9.37%, 9.45%, 6.48% (2021), 7.09%, 6.29% (2023), 6.29% (2024). The rate stepped down and stabilized while the base is shrinking. At a constant 6.29% on the 12/31/25 net reserve of $507.5M, 2026 releases would be ~$31.9M against $33.9M in 2025, and less each subsequent year. H1 2026 actuals were $14.9M versus $17.4M (-14.4%). This arithmetic caps earnings power even if reserving remains as conservative as it has been.

In 2025 paid losses exceeded incurred by $31.1M, the signature of a reserve base running off faster than new business rebuilds it. The 2025 cash bridge reconciles exactly: $11.1M operating + $68.4M investing inflow - $61.6M financing = +$17.9M, matching the cash increase from $44.0M to $61.9M. The portfolio's average pre-tax yield is only 3.3%, municipals over one year are 60.4% of holdings, and only 10.5% of fixed maturities mature within a year while 36.2% mature beyond ten. The reinvestment uplift is slow and the shrinkage is fast, with shrinkage currently dominating.

### Earnings quality

Equity securities (ETFs) carried cost of $31.2M against fair value of $64.0M at 6/30/26, a $32.8M embedded unrealized gain, with all mark changes running through net income. In Q2 2026, $8.1M of $18.3M pretax income (44%) was unrealized equity gains. Reconstructing operating EPS: $18.265M pretax - $8.113M unrealized + $0.090M realized losses = $10.242M; at a 21% rate on the removed items → $8.09M ÷ 18.702M diluted shares ≈ $0.43, versus ~$0.52 on the same method a year earlier (-17%). Underwriting profit was $3.562M versus $5.736M (-37.9%); H1 $8.696M versus $13.229M (-34.3%).

This matters for interpreting the reported "beat." GAAP EPS of $0.78 against a $0.53 consensus was widely reported as a ~49% beat, but the consensus for insurers is typically an operating figure excluding equity marks. On matching bases the quarter was a miss and a year-over-year decline. The earnings-surprise record supplied here (four consecutive misses, -4.2%, -16.0%, -9.5%, -16.5%) is consistent with the operating reconstruction rather than the GAAP headline.

A secondary constraint: board policy caps equities at the lesser of 10% of portfolio or 30% of shareholders' equity. The position is 8.3% of portfolio but 25.6% of equity. As equity shrinks from capital returns, the 30% limit could begin to bind, forcing trimming that realizes gains and cash taxes.

The company has no financial debt. A $20M undrawn Frost Bank line was renewed in 2025. The screening figure of "D/E 3.49" is total liabilities to equity ($877.3M / $250.0M = 3.51x at 6/30/26), comprising loss reserves and unearned premium rather than borrowings; financial leverage is zero. An Altman Z-Score of 0.76 has no valid interpretation for an insurance carrier. The one signal within that screen worth keeping is operating cash flow versus net income, which fails and corroborates the runoff dynamic.

The hard constraint on specials: subsidiaries may dividend up to $40.1M to the holding company in 2026 without regulatory approval, against a ~$30.4M regular dividend run-rate plus $7.3M of remaining buyback authorization, leaving roughly $2.4M of ordinary-course headroom. ## Growth Analysis

Premium is growing while economic value is not. GPW: $285.4M (2023) → $294.1M (+3.1%) → $313.9M (+6.7%) → $174.5M in H1 2026 (+6.7%). Net premiums earned grew a faster +10.2% in H1 2026 to $152.3M, aided by ceded premium falling to 5.2% of gross earned from 5.7%. In-force policy count grew 10.2% in 2025.

Where the growth is coming from matters. Florida rose from 13.4% to 16.3% of GPW in two years and Illinois from 5.1% to 8.0%, while Georgia fell 10.9% → 9.4% and Louisiana 7.5% → 5.8%. Florida has seen among the largest cumulative loss-cost reductions of the past decade. Growing fastest in states with falling rates, alongside 10.2% policy-count growth and a rising loss ratio, indicates growth is exposure-driven rather than price-driven. Retention slipping to 93.1% while volume accelerates is the pattern of a carrier winning accounts on price in a softening market.

Severity is also moving. Over the five accident years ended 12/31/2025 there were 91 claims with ultimate value above $1.0M, averaging ~18 per year but ranging from 12 (AY2023) to 25 (AY2025), the highest of the period, which management cited in raising the AY2025 pick from 71% to 72%. The mitigating point: with a $2.0M per-occurrence retention, most severe-claim cost above $2M is ceded, so a severity-frequency spike is primarily a future reinsurance-pricing problem rather than a near-term net-loss problem. Open claims rose to 4,317 at 6/30/26 from 4,024, with Q2 reported claims +19.4%, while average unpaid loss and LAE per open claim fell 12.6% to $149,800, consistent either with a wave of newer, immature claims or with case-reserve adequacy drift.

Consensus reflects the pressure: current-year EPS $1.95 (down 6.4% in 90 days), next year $2.02 (down 3.2%), current quarter $0.49 (down 8.8%). Three analysts cover the name. There is no earnings-inflection signal here; the revision vector is negative on every tenor.

A specific exposure channel is quantified. Premium is a function of audited insured payroll, and the risk factors name tariffs and immigration enforcement as drivers of policyholder activity. Construction plus agriculture is 54.8% of GPW. FY2025 GPW included a -$7.6M reduction from payroll audits and premium adjustments, versus -$3.9M in 2024, a $3.7M adverse swing. If construction and agricultural payrolls contract, premium is lost through audit true-downs without any loss of policy count, invisible in new-business metrics.

## Valuation Assessment

At the frozen basis of $29.12 (close 2026-08-07):

- P/B = $29.12 ÷ $13.49 = **2.16x**
- Trailing P/E on TTM diluted EPS ~$2.48 = **11.7x** — but that EPS includes equity marks
- FY2025 operating EPS ~$2.19 → **13.3x**
- 2026 consensus $1.95 → **14.9x**; 2027 consensus $2.02 → **14.4x**
- Base dividend yield $1.64 ÷ $29.12 = **5.6%**
- Reserve-release-adjusted ROE scenario: FY2025 net income $47.1M less after-tax releases ($33.865M × 0.79 = $26.75M) = $20.4M ÷ ~$254M average equity ≈ **8.0%**

Peer medians in the supplied set are P/E 16.9x and yield 2.9%, but the set is heterogeneous and distorted (EIG at 126.4x; UFCS at 9.6x, STC 14.6x, SAFT 16.9x, PRA 19.6x). AMSF at ~14.4-14.9x forward on falling estimates is not meaningfully cheap against peers trading at similar multiples with stable-to-improving earnings.

The crux is the ROE assumption embedded in 2.16x book. At a genuine ~18% ROE with a 5.6% yield, 2.16x is reasonable. At a normalized ~8% ROE, the implied earnings yield is ~3.7% and 2.16x is expensive. The market has moved partway toward the second view: the multiple has compressed from roughly 3.0x to 2.16x since November 2025 (Form 4 grant prices show $40.21 in Nov 2025, ~$32.53-32.92 in March 2026, $29.04 in Aug 2026). The remaining question is whether it should compress further.

We regard the 8% figure as deliberately harsh: releases are real economic events, ten straight years have developed favorably, and stripping them entirely while leaving a conservative 72% pick in place understates through-cycle earnings power. A reasonable normalized ROE probably sits in the 11-14% range, which supports something in the 1.8-2.1x book area on a stable book, i.e., roughly $24-28 on today's $13.49, or $30-31 if the multiple holds at current levels on a modestly declining book.

The trailing 11.7x P/E is the kind of optically cheap multiple that has historically preceded further underperformance when it sits on inflated earnings and falling estimates. It is not a reason to buy. Analyst targets (low $32, mean $35.67, high $42) sit 10-44% above spot, but with three covering analysts cutting estimates on every tenor, those targets are more likely stale than predictive; the "targets as floors" logic applies to stocks with rising estimates, which this is not.

**Base case $30.50** — roughly 15x the $2.02 next-year consensus, or 2.2x a book value that drifts slightly lower as capital return continues to exceed earnings. +4.7% from spot, ~10% with the dividend.

**Bull case $38** — the AY2026 pick proves conservative again, releases hold near the 6.29% rate, a special dividend returns in Q4 2026, and the multiple re-expands toward 2.7-2.8x book. +30.5%.

**Bear case $21.50** — the current accident year develops in line with the booked 104% rather than favorably, releases decline toward $20-25M, operating EPS settles near $1.60-1.75, and the multiple compresses to ~1.6x a book value near $13.20. -26.2%.

Probability-weighting these at approximately 45% / 25% / 30% gives a price return near +1.8% and a total return near +7% including the dividend, against a cost of equity for a small monoline carrier of roughly 9-10%. That is a less-than-fair expected return with a materially unfavorable left tail.

## Competitive Landscape

The niche is defensible. Large carriers avoid small high-hazard accounts because of premium size, mobile workforces, and service intensity; small carriers lack the actuarial depth for severe-claim tails. AMERISAFE occupies the gap with 40 years of data across 247,000+ claims, in-house claims handling, field safety professionals, and proprietary systems. The pricing benchmark it competes against, state assigned-risk pools, is typically more expensive, giving a persistent value proposition to employers trying to exit the pool. Retention of 93.1% and top-ten policyholders at 2.1% of premium are consistent with sticky, granular relationships.

The cycle is working against that franchise, and the filings say so. Workers' comp has been the industry's most profitable major line for roughly a decade, drawing capital and producing sustained loss-cost reductions in NCCI states. With 300+ competitors and no state share above 5%, AMERISAFE is a price-taker on loss costs and can only manage its loss-cost multiplier and risk selection.

One limitation: independent industry benchmarks (NCCI State of the Line, A.M. Best segment data) were not obtainable. Whether AMERISAFE's ten-point combined-ratio deterioration since 2022 matches, leads, or lags the industry is unverified. This cuts both ways. If the deterioration is a shared cycle, the de-rating is a multiple problem that mean-reverts. If it is company-specific, it is an underwriting-quality problem warranting a permanently lower multiple. The company's own pay-versus-performance disclosure is the closest thing to an external check, and it is unfavorable: cumulative TSR indexed to $100 at 12/31/2020 ended 2025 at 102.32 for AMSF versus 168.70 for its self-selected peer group, +2.3% against +68.7% over five years, with a 32-point relative gap in 2025 alone. Peer-group construction is company-chosen and not a pure like-for-like, but this is the company's audited statement that its chosen comparables have substantially outperformed it.

Management asserts its 30.4% expense ratio is "generally lower than that of our competitors." In absolute terms 30.4% is not low for commercial lines; it is plausibly low for high-touch small-account workers' comp. We did not verify against peer disclosures.

## Risk Assessment

**Reserve adequacy (primary, partially unpriced).** The current accident year is booked above 100% combined, IBNR is at 10.5% of gross reserves versus 17.8% two years ago, and the disclosed sensitivity puts ±5.1% of net reserves (±~$24M, ~9.6% of equity) on a ±30% move in development factors. At 2.16x book, the market still ascribes premium value to a release track record whose cushion has visibly thinned.

**Monoline concentration.** The 10-K states that negative developments in workers' comp are "likely to have a disproportionately adverse effect," with no other line or segment to offset.

**Payroll exposure to construction and agriculture (54.8% of GPW)** through tariffs and immigration enforcement, already visible in the $3.7M adverse swing in payroll-audit adjustments.

**Special-dividend removal.** The most plausible explanation for the ~28% de-rating from $40.21 is the market repricing away special-dividend optionality. The $40.1M subsidiary dividend ceiling against ~$37.7M of regular dividends plus remaining buyback authorization leaves little ordinary-course room.

**Earnings composition.** With 44% of Q2 pretax income from unrealized equity marks, reported EPS is a poor read-through to underwriting, and a market decline would reverse the flattering comparison while potentially forcing trimming into the 30%-of-equity policy limit.

**Reinsurance credit.** Recoverables of $108.4M gross at 12/31/25, of which $44.2M unsecured; the largest single exposure is Hannover Reinsurance Ireland at $41.9M (A+ rated), which is not footnoted as a 2026 program participant, indicating legacy run-off counterparty exposure. Separately, $105.7M (PV) of purchased annuities creates contingent obligations if a provider failed.

**Governance and key-person.** A roughly six-month CFO vacancy (departure 8-K 2025-11-03; Guillermo Ramos effective 2026-05-07) with the CEO serving as interim principal financial officer spanned the FY2025 year-end close, the 40% IBNR reduction, and a ~28% price decline. All thirteen 2025 IRIS ratios were within usual values and a long-tenured SVP & Controller was in place, so this is a bandwidth observation rather than evidence of a control failure. Anti-takeover architecture is heavy for a $539M company: classified board, 66⅔% supermajority provisions, blank-check preferred, Texas business-combination restrictions, plus 10% insurance change-of-control thresholds in Nebraska and Texas, which suppresses the takeover optionality a subscale monoline at 2.16x book would otherwise carry.

**Offsets that genuinely matter.** Zero financial debt, no refinancing cliff, no covenant risk, A.M. Best "A," RBC above minimums, average portfolio credit quality AA− with 0.0% below investment grade, and a 2026 reinsurance program extending to $100M per occurrence with the first layer locked in a multi-year treaty through 12/31/2028. There is no solvency question here. The risk is to earnings power and multiple, not to survival.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is too thin to be informative and should be discounted heavily. Twenty-four contracts across four expirations, near-term ATM IV of 125.0% against 29.4% one-year realized vol, and a prior snapshot showing 2573% IV — the last figure is plainly an artifact, which undermines confidence in the current reading. The 11-day tenor sits after the Q2 report with no identifiable catalyst before expiry, so the 125% print and the resulting backwardation (125% near versus 32.2% at six months) most likely reflect illiquid quotes rather than positioning. The longer tenors, at 43.0% (3m) and 32.2% (6m) against 29.4% realized, price only a modest premium to realized vol. Short interest of 5.4% of float with 3.4 days to cover indicates an uncrowded bearish view, leaving room for the thesis to work but providing no informed-money confirmation. We take no directional signal from options here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($38, +30.5%).** Ten consecutive years of favorable development is a serious track record, and the 72.0% AY2026 pick may be as conservative as prior picks that were harvested at 9%+ release rates. Premium is growing 6.7% with policy count up 10.2%, in-force distribution is exceptionally granular, and the balance sheet carries zero debt with an A rating. The IBNR drawdown is at least partly explained by disclosed structural mechanics ($105.7M PV of purchased annuities and 3,847 claims closed in 2025) rather than by thinning margins. If the workers' comp cycle is the driver, AMERISAFE's deterioration is shared and the multiple compression from 3.0x to 2.16x book has already discounted it. A Q4 2026 special dividend, even at $1.00, would restore part of the total-return proposition that historically supported the premium multiple, and a genuine 18% ROE with a 5.6% yield does not belong at 2.16x book.

**Bear case ($21.50, -26.2%).** Reported profit is entirely release-funded, and the release base shrinks mechanically from $491.8M with each passing year. The current accident year is booked at 104%, the expense ratio is drifting up (29.3% → 30.8%), retention is slipping while growth accelerates in states with falling loss costs, net investment income is declining because the portfolio is being liquidated to pay dividends, and operating cash flow has gone negative. Four consecutive operating misses and estimate cuts on every tenor say the trend is not stabilizing. On a normalized 8-12% ROE with a declining book value, 1.6-1.8x book is the appropriate multiple, and the $40.1M statutory dividend ceiling makes the special-dividend engine hard to restart. Anti-takeover provisions remove the consolidation floor that would otherwise support a subscale monoline.

**Our reading.** The bull case requires the conservatism margin to be intact; the IBNR ratio halving, the release rate stepping down from 9.4% to 6.3%, and the H1 release decline of 14.4% collectively argue it has narrowed. We do not need to assert that reserves are deficient, only that the option value the market is paying 2.16x book for is smaller than it was, and that the earnings base supporting a 5.6% yield is being consumed to pay it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $30.50 (+4.7%), bull $38 (+30.5%), bear $21.50 (-26.2%). Probability-weighted total return of roughly 7% including the dividend, against a required return of 9-10% for a small monoline carrier, with a -26% left tail.

**De-risking plan.** Trim on strength rather than at the current price. The stock has fallen 34.7% from its $47.86 high and is 0.6% above its $28.94 52-week low, so indiscriminate selling into weakness has poor expected value. Use the seasonal Q4 special-dividend declaration window (specials have historically been declared in Q4) as the exit event: a declared special of $1.00 or more would likely lift the stock into the $32-35 range, which is the appropriate place to reduce. Absent a special, reduce on any rally above $33 (2.45x book). We would target retaining no more than a third of a prior full position pending resolution of the AY2026 development question.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.** (1) Two consecutive quarters with the current-accident-year loss pick held at or below 72.0% while the expense ratio stabilizes at or below 30.4%, taking the reported combined ratio back below 92%. (2) Gross IBNR rebuilt to 13% or more of gross reserves in the FY2026 10-K reserve component table. (3) Operating cash flow returning to positive and covering at least 100% of the regular dividend on a trailing-four-quarter basis. (4) Price near $24 (1.75x book) with fundamentals no worse than today, which would restore roughly 25% upside to base case. (5) Clustered open-market insider buying; there has been none across the last 25 Form 4 records, and purchases at 2.16x book by the CEO or new CFO would be a meaningful signal.

## Conclusion

AMERISAFE is a well-run, debt-free specialist with a genuine niche and a decade of conservative reserving behind it. Those attributes are not in dispute. What has changed is that the earnings the market is capitalizing are no longer produced by current underwriting: the current accident year is booked at approximately 104% combined, prior-year releases supply more than half of pretax income, the reserve base funding those releases has shrunk 16% since 2022, gross IBNR has fallen to 10.5% of reserves from 17.8%, net investment income is declining because the portfolio is being liquidated to fund a dividend that exceeds operating cash flow, and shareholders' equity has fallen from $317.4M to $250.0M while underwriting leverage rose 35-45%.

Q2 2026 confirmed rather than arrested the trend: combined ratio 95.4% versus 91.7%, underwriting profit -37.9%, operating EPS -17%, a fourth consecutive miss, and consensus cut 6.4% for the current year. The stock's 7% decline since our last review is matched by that deterioration, and at 2.16x a declining book value with roughly 14.4-14.9x forward earnings on falling estimates, the 5.6% yield does not compensate for a -26% bear case against a +4.7% base case. Score moves to 4.5 from 5.3 and the recommendation to REDUCE. We would revisit constructively near $24, or sooner on evidence that the AY2026 pick is proving conservative and the IBNR cushion is being rebuilt.
