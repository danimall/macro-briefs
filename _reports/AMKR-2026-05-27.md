**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Amkor Technology is experiencing its strongest demand inflection in three years, driven by AI advanced packaging, iOS ecosystem strength, and the nascent ramp of its HDFO data center CPU program with AMD. Q1 2026 revenue grew 27% YoY, utilization improved from the low 50s to the low 70s, and management guided AI packaging revenue to triple in 2026. The $7B Arizona facility — backed by $2.8B in CHIPS Act incentives and the recently issued $1B zero-coupon convertible — positions Amkor as the only U.S.-based advanced packaging provider at scale.

The fundamental challenge is valuation. At $72.32, the stock trades at 42x trailing earnings and ~35x FY26 consensus ($2.08). The stock has moved from $17.79 to $72.32 in twelve months — a 306% appreciation that has compressed the risk/reward from the prior HOLD recommendation. Estimate revisions are strong (+48% current quarter, +19% current year over 30 days), and the 4-quarter beat streak averaging 31% supports confidence in forward numbers. At these multiples, the margin of safety against execution missteps in the $2.5-3.0B 2026 capex program is thin.

The path forward depends on whether gross margins can expand from 14% to "mid-to-high teens" by H2 2026 and whether the revenue trajectory toward $8.5-9.5B by 2028 materializes. Both are plausible given the demand signals, but neither is certain given the capital intensity, Apple concentration (29.8%), and competitive pressure from TSMC's own advanced packaging expansion.

## Company Overview

Amkor Technology is the world's largest U.S.-headquartered outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) provider. The company serves as the critical bridge between chip fabrication and final product assembly, offering turnkey packaging and test services across advanced technologies including 2.5D integration, High Density Fan-Out (HDFO), Co-Packaged Optics, and silicon photonics.

Revenue breaks down by end market: Communications/smartphones (46%, heavily Apple-dependent), Computing/data center (20%, growing fastest), Automotive/Industrial (19%), and Consumer/IoT (15%). Advanced Products represent 83% of revenue, up from 77% in 2023, reflecting the structural shift toward higher-value packaging.

The company operates factories across Korea, China, Japan, Philippines, Malaysia, Singapore, Portugal, Taiwan, and Vietnam, with the Arizona facility under construction for high-volume production beginning 2028-2029.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:**
Amkor's revenue trajectory inflected sharply in Q1 2026 after three years of stagnation:
- FY2022: $7.09B (peak)
- FY2023: $6.50B (-8.3%)
- FY2024: $6.32B (-2.9%)
- FY2025: $6.71B (+6.2%)
- Q1 2026: ~$2.0B (+27% YoY)

Operating margins have compressed to 7.0% (FY2025) from 12.7% (FY2022), driven by the Vietnam ramp costs and lower utilization during the industry downcycle. Gross margins at 14.0% are thin for a company deploying $2.5-3.0B in capex. Management guided H2 2026 gross margins to "mid-to-high teens" as utilization improves and HDFO programs scale — each percentage point improvement on ~$7B revenue adds ~$70M in operating profit.

**Cash Flow and Capital Structure:**
Operating cash flow was $1.10B in FY2025 against $905M capex, leaving $191M in free cash flow (2.8% FCF margin). FY2026 capex guidance of $2.5-3.0B will turn FCF materially negative. This is being funded through:
- $1B zero-coupon convertible notes (May 2026, due 2031)
- $500M Term A Loans (May 2025)
- $500M Senior Notes at 5.875% (September 2025)
- $407M CHIPS Act direct funding + 35% investment tax credit
- $1B undrawn revolver

Post-convertible, total debt will be approximately $2.4B+ against $4.47B in equity (D/E rising to ~0.54). Current ratio at 2.27 provides adequate near-term liquidity, and the zero-coupon structure eliminates cash interest cost on the convertible. Altman Z-Score at 2.84 is in the grey zone — not distressed but not comfortable given the coming capex ramp.

**Earnings Trajectory:**
The earnings beat pattern is strong and accelerating: Q2'25 (+37%), Q3'25 (+18%), Q4'25 (+54%), Q1'26 (+16%). Consensus estimates have been revised sharply upward — current quarter EPS raised 48% in 30 days (from $0.32 to $0.47), and FY26 raised 19% (from $1.75 to $2.08). FY27 consensus sits at $2.45.

## Growth Analysis

Amkor's growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. AI Advanced Packaging Tripling (2026):** Management's most specific near-term catalyst. The Computing end market (20% of 2025 revenue = ~$1.34B) is growing fastest, with HDFO for data center CPUs ramping in H2 2026. The AMD partnership validates this capability for high-performance compute workloads that cannot be served by traditional wirebond.

**2. Arizona Strategic Premium (2028-2030):** The $7B Arizona facility transforms Amkor from a contract manufacturer competing on cost into a strategic national asset with pricing power. No other OSAT has CHIPS Act-funded advanced packaging capacity at this scale. Revenue targets of $8.5-9.5B by 2028 and $11B by 2030 (10-15% CAGR from 2025) depend heavily on this facility's success.

**3. Utilization Leverage:** Moving from low-50s utilization to the low-70s already drove Q1's 27% revenue growth. The fixed-cost structure means further utilization gains flow disproportionately to margins. If utilization reaches 80%+, gross margins could approach 18-20%.

The estimate revision velocity (+48% current quarter) does not quite meet the +30% threshold on forward earnings (FY26 revised +19%). Revenue growth acceleration from -2.9% (2024) to +6.2% (2025) to +27% (Q1 2026 YoY) confirms a genuine inflection rather than a transient improvement.

## Valuation Assessment

At $72.32, AMKR trades at:
- 42.3x trailing P/E (TTM EPS ~$1.71)
- ~35x FY26E P/E ($2.08 consensus)
- ~29.5x FY27E P/E ($2.45 consensus)
- P/B: approximately 4.0x ($4.47B equity, 248M shares)

Against peer medians of 68x P/E (driven by negative-earnings companies like SITM and SMTC), Amkor looks relatively cheap. Against the most comparable profitable peer — Skyworks at 34x with 3.4% yield and negative growth — Amkor at 35x forward with 27% recent revenue growth and accelerating estimates appears reasonable.

However, several factors temper the valuation case:
- The 288% appreciation from the 52-week low was driven by fundamental improvement but leaves limited room for further re-rating at 35x forward.
- FCF will be deeply negative in FY26-27 due to the Arizona capex cycle. DCF models must look through to 2028+ normalized earnings.
- Analyst consensus mean target is $75.50 (4.4% upside) with a $90 high. The stock is trading near consensus already.
- The 12.1% short interest suggests meaningful institutional skepticism at current levels.

The stock trades at approximately 15x 2028E EBITDA assuming $11B 2030 revenue with 15% operating margins is roughly achievable. That's not expensive for a structural growth story, but it requires sustained execution across a $7B capex program over 4 years.

## Competitive Landscape

**Improving:**
- Only U.S.-headquartered OSAT with CHIPS Act funding at scale
- AMD partnership diversifies beyond Apple/Qualcomm customer set
- HDFO/2.5D capabilities validated for AI/HPC workloads
- Geopolitical premium vs. Taiwan-based ASE Technology increasing

**Stable:**
- Apple's packaging needs remain concentrated with Amkor (SiP modules)
- Technology portfolio breadth matches or exceeds ASE
- Vietnam facility adds Southeast Asian capacity option

**Risks:**
- TSMC's CoWoS/InFO dominates NVIDIA AI GPU packaging — the highest-value advanced packaging market segment is captive to TSMC
- Chinese OSATs (JCET) gaining share in non-restricted segments with government subsidy
- Intel's foundry packaging push could compete for same customers

The structural positioning is favorable: AI infrastructure demand is validated across multiple customers. TSMC is capacity-constrained in advanced packaging, creating overflow opportunity for Amkor. The Arizona facility will be the only U.S. site capable of serving defense, automotive, and AI customers requiring domestic production.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Arizona Execution Risk (HIGH):** $7B commitment with no meaningful revenue until 2029. 1-2% margin dilution in 2027 during qualification. If AI demand disappoints or customers choose TSMC's integrated offering, this becomes a stranded asset with depreciation dragging earnings for years.

2. **Apple Concentration (HIGH):** 29.8% of revenue from a single customer. Apple has historically diversified suppliers and has deep relationships with TSMC's advanced packaging. Any reduction in share would be immediately material.

3. **Capital Intensity (MEDIUM-HIGH):** $2.5-3.0B capex in FY26 on $6.7B revenue base. The zero-coupon convertible eliminates interest cost but creates dilution risk (~14M shares at current conversion price). If revenue growth disappoints, the leverage increase becomes problematic.

4. **Thin Margin Buffer (MEDIUM):** At 14% gross margin and 7% operating margin, any utilization decline drops straight to the bottom line. The semiconductor industry's cyclicality has historically produced 20-30% revenue swings — Amkor's trough was just 18 months ago.

5. **Trade Policy (MEDIUM):** 95% of production in Asia exposes Amkor to tariff complexity. While the Arizona facility mitigates this long-term, near-term revenue could be affected by export controls (particularly China operations) or retaliatory tariffs.

## Conclusion

Amkor Technology is executing a strategic transformation from a low-margin commodity OSAT into an AI-enabling packaging provider with a unique U.S. manufacturing footprint. The Q1 2026 results and estimate revision trajectory confirm the fundamental inflection is real. The AMD partnership, Arizona facility, and AI demand convergence create a credible path to $8.5-9.5B revenue by 2028.

At $72.32 and 35x forward earnings, the stock prices in substantial execution success while the capital cycle is just beginning. The 288% appreciation from the 52-week low, 12.1% short interest, consensus targets only 4% above current price, and thin margin buffer all argue against initiating new positions at current levels. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

The stock warrants holding for investors with positions established at lower levels, given the structural growth drivers and strong estimate revision momentum. For new capital, waiting for a pullback to the $55-60 range — or for margin proof-points in Q2/Q3 — would provide a more favorable entry with adequate downside cushion against the Arizona execution risk.
