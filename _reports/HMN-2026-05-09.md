## Executive Summary

Horace Mann Educators Corporation is a multi-line insurer exclusively focused on K-12 educators. The company has completed a decisive earnings recovery — from $0.47 EPS in 2022 (restated under LDTI accounting) to $3.90 EPS in 2025 — driven by disciplined P&C pricing actions, two acquisitions (NTA, Madison National Life), and expansion of higher-margin supplemental and group benefits. At approximately $44.51, the stock trades at approximately 10x 2026 guided core EPS ($4.20-$4.50), below estimated peer multiples, despite demonstrably superior earnings momentum.

The core thesis rests on three legs: (1) P&C profitability has been restored to target (FY2025 combined ratio of 89.7%, with management guiding to low-to-mid 90s including catastrophes for 2026), with further rate adequacy tailwinds; (2) the Supplemental & Group Benefits segment is inflecting as a capital-light growth engine (management has reported strong sales growth, with PFML product expansion underway); and (3) a shareholder-friendly capital allocation framework combining an approximately 3.1% dividend yield with active buybacks ($55.5M remaining authorization). The stock's TSR has underperformed peers (131 vs. 210 cumulative) despite operational improvement, suggesting a rerating opportunity as the earnings trajectory proves sustainable.

Key risks include catastrophe exposure in the P&C book (CA, TX, NC concentrations), legacy commercial claims from 1960s-era policies (though no additional charge was recorded in 2025 and reserves declined), elevated near-term options implied volatility (93.5% 1-week IV vs. 20.3% historical), and regulatory constraints in California auto. These risks are real but quantifiable and, in the case of catastrophes, substantially mitigated by $240M of reinsurance above a $35M retention.

## Company Overview

Horace Mann was founded in 1945 by two teachers and is the largest multiline financial services company serving America's educators. The company operates four segments:

- **Property & Casualty (~47% of revenue):** Personal auto and homeowners for educators. 326K auto and 164K property risks in force, diversified across 50 states.
- **Life & Retirement (~32%):** 403(b) annuities ($5.9B AUM), life insurance ($21.5B in force), and the Retirement Advantage platform. 213K annuity contracts.
- **Supplemental & Group Benefits (~18%):** Individual cancer/heart/hospital/disability plus group life, disability, and supplemental health. 275K individual policies, 875K group covered lives.
- **Corporate & Other (<1% of revenue):** Net investment activity, legacy claims, debt service.

The competitive moat is institutional access: Horace Mann maintains business-to-business relationships in over half of U.S. K-12 school districts, with local agents physically present in schools. This creates distribution advantages that generalist insurers (GEICO, Progressive, State Farm) cannot replicate for this niche. Brand awareness among educators is 35% unaided.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has shifted from defensive repair (2022-2024) to offensive expansion (2025+):

**P&C Segment:** Growth here is primarily earnings-driven rather than top-line. Premium rate adequacy has been achieved in all states except California. The FY2025 P&C combined ratio of 89.7% demonstrates profitability well above break-even, with auto combined ratio at 96.5% and property combined ratio at 78.3% (underlying property combined ratio of 64.3%). Policy counts declined 4.7% (auto) and 1.8% (property) in 2025 as rate actions prioritized profitability; management expects policies to turn positive in H2 2026 as competitors react to hardened pricing with growth initiatives.

**Supplemental & Group Benefits:** This is where the growth thesis is most differentiated:
- Management has reported strong Group Benefits sales momentum
- Individual Supplemental and Life sales growing double digits
- PFML (Paid Family Medical Leave) product expansion leveraging states with paid leave mandates
- Capital-light model drives ROE improvement toward 12-13% target

**Life & Retirement:** Stable contributor with 403(b) market access. Net interest margin improvement continues as new money yields exceed portfolio roll-off. Reinvestment rates of ~5.1% vs. portfolio yield improving from 4.28% to 4.31%.

**Technology Investment:** Major systems modernization (Guidewire, LifePro, Workday, GenAI) with management projecting 25 bps expense ratio improvement in 2026 and additional benefits thereafter. Operating expenses rose 14.8% in 2025 partly due to these investments.

## Competitive Landscape

Horace Mann's niche positioning is both its strength and its constraint. The educator-exclusive focus creates durable competitive advantages:

1. **Distribution moat:** Physical access to school buildings, payroll deduction arrangements, and B2B district relationships are extremely difficult for generalist insurers to replicate. Progressive partners with HMN (through HMGA) rather than competing directly in this channel.

2. **Product specificity:** 403(b) expertise, educator-specific liability coverage (transporting students), supplemental products designed for public employee benefit gaps.

3. **Brand trust:** 35% unaided brand awareness in a population that values institutional endorsement (union relationships, district-level agreements).

**Risks to competitive position:** TIAA and Voya compete aggressively in 403(b). Some states are considering limited-provider 403(b) mandates. In P&C, competitors are beginning to "power up for growth" after the hard market, which could pressure HMN's retention. However, the Group Benefits expansion into PFML products represents genuine competitive differentiation that leverages existing school district access.

## Risk Assessment

**Catastrophe Exposure (Moderate):** 2025 catastrophe losses were $61.7M (improved from $80-95M range in 2022-2024). Reinsurance provides $240M above $35M retention. California wildfire, Texas wind/hail, and hurricane exposure in Gulf/Atlantic states are permanent portfolio features. A severe catastrophe year could consume $60-90M pre-reinsurance. Management's 2026 outlook assumes approximately $90M in catastrophe losses.

**Legacy Commercial Claims (Monitored):** First-time recognition of $20M losses in 2024 from policies issued as early as the 1960s (asbestos, environmental, sexual molestation). Management explicitly states "no reserves for these liabilities prior to 2024" and characterizes estimation as "inherently uncertain." No additional legacy commercial exposure charge was recorded in 2025, and net reserves for these exposures declined to $9.6M from $17.7M. The risk appears contained for now but bears continued monitoring given the inherent uncertainty of decades-old claims.

**California Auto (Moderate):** 15.4% of P&C premiums in a state where regulatory constraints prevent adequate pricing. Management is limiting growth investment; resolution timeline is uncertain.

**Tariff/Inflation Risk to Loss Costs (Moderate):** Newly added 10-K risk factor explicitly addressing tariff-driven parts/materials inflation affecting P&C claims costs. This is a real concern given the current macro environment with persistent inflation.

**Policy Count Decline (Moderate, Temporary):** Auto risks in force declined 4.7% in 2025. This is expected to reverse in H2 2026, but competitive dynamics (peers returning to growth mode) could make volume recovery challenging.

## Conclusion

Horace Mann represents a well-positioned niche insurer trading at a discount to peers despite superior earnings momentum and a capital-light growth vector in Group Benefits. The earnings recovery from $0.47 EPS in 2022 to $3.90 EPS in 2025 is demonstrated fact; the trajectory toward $4.20-$4.50 in 2026 is supported by rate adequacy, investment income tailwinds, and segment-level growth. The approximately 3.1% dividend yield provides downside support while buybacks reduce the share count.

The primary risks — catastrophe exposure, legacy claims, and California regulatory constraints — are quantifiable and manageable within the current reserve and reinsurance structure. The options market signal (extreme near-term IV) likely reflects liquidity constraints or earnings-date positioning rather than informed directional conviction; the 6-month term structure confirms no structural concern.

At current prices, the risk/reward favors buyers: 15-26% upside to fair value targets versus 10-17% downside in a stress scenario. The stock earns a BUY recommendation with standard position sizing (2-3% of portfolio).
