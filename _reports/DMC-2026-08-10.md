## Executive Summary

Del Monte Corporation — the renamed Fresh Del Monte Produce, which changed its corporate name in June 2026 after acquiring the Del Monte brand and select US packaged-foods assets out of Chapter 11 — presents an unusually clean test of whether a cheap multiple on a falling earnings stream is value or a trap. The stock trades at 0.72x reported book value, 0.94x tangible book, and roughly 7.9x the midpoint of a reported FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guide, with a 3.90% dividend yield. Against that, the operating record for the first half of 2026 is unambiguous: adjusted EBITDA of $130.1M versus $170.5M (-23.7%), Q2 adjusted EBITDA of $71.6M versus $102.3M (-30.0%), and GAAP diluted EPS of $0.65 versus $1.82. Those declines occurred *with* roughly 3.2 months of the acquired business in the numbers, contributing $192.7M of revenue and lifting Prepared Foods gross profit from $20.0M to $53.5M. The legacy fresh and banana businesses are shedding gross profit faster than the acquisition adds it.

The mechanism is concentrated in bananas, 30–34% of revenue. Segment gross margin has fallen from 10.0% in FY2023 to 5.9%, then 4.8%, then 3.5% in H1 2026 and 2.3% in Q2 2026. Applying the FY2023 margin to Q2 2026 banana sales of $361.1M would have produced $36.1M of gross profit against $8.4M actual — a $27.7M quarterly gap, on the order of $110M annualized, which exceeds half of the guided full-year adjusted EBITDA. Management's own attribution spans demand (weak North American volume), supply (Asia and Middle East constraints) and cost (per-unit production, procurement, ocean freight, distribution) simultaneously. The cost leg matters for the forward view: the 10-Q states directly that management expects increases and volatility in shipping fuel and fertilizer to be material. A margin recovery thesis that requires those costs to reverse inside two quarters is not supported by current conditions.

Layered on top is a $275.9M transfer-pricing claim across three foreign jurisdictions for tax years 2012–2021 — 19% of market capitalization — against which the company accrued $2.9M in FY2025 and nothing additional in H1 2026, after receiving an unfavorable appellate decision in one jurisdiction on January 16, 2026. Tax authorities have registered real-estate collateral. The non-accrual is a defensible ASC 740 judgment, and such claims routinely settle far below headline, but a 0.94x tangible book multiple does not price a plausible nine-figure cash outflow stacked on a core business in decline. Weighing a base case of roughly $31, a bull case near $42 and a bear case near $20, the probability-weighted return from $30.75 is approximately flat before the dividend against a 35% downside scenario. That asymmetry supports **REDUCE**.

## Company Overview

Del Monte Corporation is a vertically integrated grower, shipper and marketer of fresh produce and, since March 2026, shelf-stable and refrigerated packaged foods, selling in more than 90 countries. FY2025 revenue mix by product: bananas 34%, fresh-cut produce 20%, pineapples 16%, avocados 8%, prepared foods 7%. By geography: North America 58%, Europe 21%, Middle East/North Africa 10%, Asia 9%.

The defining structural feature is farm and logistics ownership. Roughly 52% of fresh produce volume comes from company-controlled farms (81% of pineapples, 47% of bananas), with $655.3M of FY2025 purchases from independent growers. The company owns six refrigerated container vessels plus one chartered, four US port facilities, approximately 11,000 refrigerated containers, 31 distribution centers, 18 fresh-cut facilities and approximately 419 US and 241 Middle East trucks/trailers. Property, plant and equipment was $1,206.4M at June 26, 2026, or 36% of total assets. Employment is roughly 8,562 full-time plus approximately 31,466 daily, seasonal and temporary workers.

This asset intensity cuts both ways. It provides supply security and quality control that asset-light marketers lack, and it is the source of the tangible book value the bull case rests on. It also converts commodity price and volume swings into operating leverage on a fixed cost base that cannot be flexed quickly — which is precisely the banana outcome.

The March 19, 2026 acquisition changed the corporate architecture. Del Monte Foods Corporation II Inc. and 17 affiliates filed Chapter 11 in July 2025; this issuer, previously the *licensee* of the Del Monte trademark for fresh produce, was selected as successful bidder on January 15, 2026 and closed for base cash of $285.0M, total initial consideration of $310.2M, and total ASC 805 consideration of $341.9M once the effective settlement of its own pre-existing $31.7M license intangible is included. Acquired: Del Monte®, S&W®, Contadina® and Take Root Organics® brands, Joyba® bubble tea, four US plants, two in Mexico, one in Venezuela. Net identifiable assets equalled consideration exactly — **no goodwill**. Brand ownership is described as global "subject to certain existing licensing arrangements," and the company states separately that it is not affiliated with Del Monte Asia Pte. Ltd. and certain other regional Del Monte companies, so ownership is not unencumbered worldwide control.

Segments were realigned in Q1 2026 to four reportable units, carving Prepared Foods out of fresh & value-added. Goodwill reporting units were not changed.

Two useful cross-checks on the reported non-GAAP figures. Equity-method income was $8.5M in H1 2026 but only $1.9M in Q2 2026 versus $7.8M in Q2 2025, meaning the H1 contribution was front-loaded into Q1 and an unusual $29.6M January distribution from a liquidating investment fund (carried at $23.0M) sat inside Q1. Second, the widely reported "35.8% EPS beat" on Q2 non-GAAP EPS of $0.72 is a non-GAAP-versus-non-GAAP comparison; GAAP diluted EPS of $0.44 was down 63% year over year, and revenue of $1.22B (+3.1%) missed consensus. The earnings-surprise record shown for this issuer — three beats and one in-line across four quarters, including a 34.5% June-quarter beat — is a non-GAAP record against a single-analyst consensus and should carry limited weight.

### Cash flow and the dividend

FY2025 was a genuinely strong cash year: operating cash flow $245.1M, capex $63.8M, free cash flow $181.3M, against dividends of $57.4M (32% of FCF), buybacks of $29.8M and $71.1M of net debt repayment.

H1 2026: operating cash flow $94.0M versus $159.2M, capex $40.2M, free cash flow $53.8M. Dividends of $28.5M plus buybacks of $20.1M consumed 90% of that. The OCF decline reflects lower net income plus a $71.0M receivables outflow (versus $24.1M), partly period-end collection timing and partly the acquisition, offset by a $49.3M payables inflow.

I am deliberately not annualizing H1 cash flow. Seasonality is shifting: the legacy fresh business historically skewed to H1, while the acquired canned business is Q4-weighted with an annual "pack" in Q2/Q3 that creates material working-capital needs. Working capital rose from $611.5M at year-end to $742.9M at June 26, with inventories up $139.5M to $721.4M and finished goods up from $185.0M to $318.1M. Capex is guided to $60–70M including a global ERP implementation.

On a normalized full-year basis — operating cash flow in the $175–195M range against $65M capex — the $56.6M annual dividend (47.15M shares × $1.20) is covered roughly 1.9–2.2x on free cash flow. The dividend is not the near-term fragility. The restricted-payments basket bears watching, however: the credit agreement permits the greater of 50% of prior-year Consolidated Net Income or $25M plus carryover, and 50% of FY2025 net income of $90.7M is $45.4M, below the annualized dividend. The company relies on carryover and/or the alternative test permitting distributions that keep leverage below 3.50x. Current leverage is well inside that, so this is not a 2026 constraint, but if FY2026 net income lands near an annualized H1 run-rate ($31.2M in H1) the FY2027 basket arithmetic tightens.

### Balance sheet

At June 26, 2026: total assets $3,382.4M; total debt and finance leases $433.4M (from $177.7M at year-end); cash $36.1M; net debt $397.3M; revolver drawn $413.0M at 5.12% (Term SOFR + 1.375%); attributable equity $2,007.4M; NCI $14.7M; retirement obligations $98.9M.

Gross leverage against the $235M guidance midpoint is roughly 1.84x; net leverage 1.69x. Because the acquisition was a Permitted Acquisition, the maximum Consolidated Leverage Ratio stepped up from 3.75x to 4.25x for the quarter ended June 26, 2026 and the three succeeding quarters, reverting thereafter. Amendment No. 3 (July 15, 2026) raised the revolver from $750M to $900M, taking pro forma availability to roughly $519M. Covenant headroom is wide even against the reverting 3.75x test. The caveat is that "Consolidated EBITDA" is contractually defined and may differ from the non-GAAP figure used here; those definitions were not obtained.

Two smaller items are informative about receivable quality. The allowance for advances to growers rose to $27.2M from $12.1M at the start of H1 2025, and gross advances at June 26 were $16.4M current against $20.4M past-due — past-due exceeds current. Separately, $22.2M of VAT receivables were reclassified to non-current because of administrative collection delays, with the company noting it may need appeals or legal action to collect.

**Financial health score: 5.5.** Leverage is genuinely modest for an asset-heavy food business, liquidity is ample, the dividend is covered on normalized cash flow, and the FY2025 Altman Z of 3.9 sits in safe territory. Offsetting: earnings quality is poor (impairments in each of four consecutive periods), the working-capital cycle just got materially larger, receivable quality is degrading at the grower and VAT lines, and a $275.9M contingency sits essentially unaccrued.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story here. Revenue has been range-bound at $4.28–4.44B since FY2022. All of the 2026 revenue increase is acquired: Prepared Foods went from 6% of Q2 2025 sales to 19% of Q2 2026 sales.

The forward arithmetic on the reported guidance is the most important growth datapoint. Management is reported to target FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $230–240M and has raised the Del Monte Foods net sales outlook to approximately $625M. That figure comes from a single secondary report of the July 29 call and was not verifiable against a filing, so all multiples derived from it should be treated as provisional. Taking it at face value: implied H2 adjusted EBITDA is $99.9–109.9M ($230–240M less $130.1M H1 actual), and implied H2 Del Monte Foods revenue is roughly $432M ($625M less the $192.7M recognized March 19–June 26). Even with $432M of incremental acquired H2 revenue at a Prepared Foods gross margin near 17–19%, guided H2 EBITDA is only $100–110M. That embeds continued deterioration in the legacy fresh and banana businesses through the back half.

The acquired business itself is not an earnings engine. From March 19 to June 26 it generated $192.7M of revenue and a **pretax loss of $(2.7)M**, inclusive of a $16.1M impairment of Joyba right-of-use assets after management decided to discontinue bubble-tea production post-closing. The pro forma disclosure quantifies what was bought: pro forma H1 2025 combined net loss of $(55.0)M against actual H1 2025 net income of $89.3M implies the acquired business would have reduced that half's earnings by roughly $144.3M. The zero-goodwill allocation is consistent with paying asset value for working capital, plants and brand rights rather than for an earnings stream.

Reported Prepared Foods gross margin of 18.9% in Q2 2026 is roughly double the consolidated average, which flatters near-term mix. Its durability is unresolved in the filings. Candidate explanations are purchase-accounting effects (a $6.3M inventory step-up, and depressed acquired PP&E basis lowering depreciation), genuine operational improvement of an under-managed asset, and a temporary post-bankruptcy pricing window. Shelf-stable canned vegetables and fruit is a structurally declining US category — the reason the seller was in Chapter 11 — and two new state disclosure regimes bear directly on the acquired portfolio: a Texas warning-label law effective 2027 covering 44 ingredients and a Louisiana QR-code law effective January 2028.

Sell-side estimates are thin and mixed in direction: a single analyst has current-year EPS at $2.25 (+4.7% over 90 days) but next year at $3.15 (-6.5%), the current quarter at $0.46 (-8.0%) and the next quarter at $0.44 (-13.7%). The near-term revisions are negative and the out-year has been cut. With one contributor, this is weak evidence in either direction, but it is not a positive-momentum signal.

**Growth prospects score: 3.8.** Flat organic revenue for four years, adjusted EBITDA declining roughly 20–24%, the only growing segment entirely acquired and marginally profitable, and guidance that implies further core deterioration in H2.

Three arguments support the cheapness:

1. **Asset support.** The equity trades below depreciated tangible book. A portfolio of productive farmland, seven vessels, 11,000 reefer containers, 31 distribution centers and now three owned trade names is being capitalized at less than its carrying value.
2. **Modest EV/EBITDA.** 7.9x is not demanding for a food business with 1.8x gross leverage.
3. **Yield.** 3.90% covered roughly 2x on normalized free cash flow.

Each has a specific rebuttal:

1. Book value is dominated by $1,206.4M of PP&E concentrated in Central American banana and pineapple farmland, and the company has impaired $37.5M of Philippine farms and $10.6M of Costa Rican farms within twelve months. Carrying value has demonstrably not been recoverable at the margin. Central America held $1,053.0M of assets (31% of total) at June 26.
2. The 7.9x rests on an unverified guidance figure that itself implies H2 deterioration. On the H1 annualized *actual* pace ($260M), the multiple is 7.2x; on a further-deteriorating $200M, it is 9.3x. The denominator is the uncertainty, not the multiple.
3. Trailing GAAP P/E of 43x is the honest headline earnings measure, and the gap to the 9.8–13.7x forward multiples is bridged entirely by non-GAAP adjustments for charges that have recurred four periods running.

The single covering analyst carries a $52 target, 69% above the current price. That target is stale — it sits above the 52-week high of $43.58 and appears to predate the H1 deterioration. Per the framework of treating consensus targets as floors only where a stock is cheap against *rising* estimates, this is the opposite case: falling near-term estimates with a target that has not been marked to the operating record. It should not be treated as independent confirmation.

**Valuation score: 5.3.** There is real asset and yield support that argues against a lower score, and the multiple is not stretched. But there is no identified catalyst for the market to reprice, the cheap denominator is falling, and the asset-value floor has already proven porous through two rounds of farm impairments. This is the archetype of cheapness that requires an independent catalyst before it earns credit.

## Competitive Landscape

The company now operates two structurally distinct businesses.

**Fresh produce** is a low-margin, weather- and disease-exposed business with three scaled global players — this issuer, Chiquita and Dole — plus regional specialists. Differentiation comes from proprietary varieties (Pinkglow®, Rubyglow®, Honeyglow®, Bananinis®), owned cold chain and shipping, and retail relationships. One genuine structural advantage stands out: customer concentration is unusually low for packaged food, with Walmart at approximately 7% of FY2025 net sales and the top ten customers at approximately 29%, with no customer at 10% or more. Most branded-food peers carry a single customer above 20%.

That advantage is measured pre-acquisition. Post-acquisition customer concentration has not been disclosed, and canned goods plausibly carry *higher* grocery-retailer concentration than fresh produce. The revenue and asset mix shift (North American assets from $624.7M to $952.7M) is real balance-sheet diversification, but it does not establish improved customer diversification.

The competitive trajectory in the core is negative, and the evidence is company-disclosed rather than inferred:

- **Disease.** Fusarium wilt TR4 was announced as detected in Ecuador in September 2025, Latin America's largest banana producer, after earlier Southeast Asian detections. CORBANA data cited in the 10-K show Costa Rican banana volumes down 21% in H1 2025 from soil-moisture-driven Black Sigatoka. Costa Rica is approximately 26% of the company's banana volumes and 41% of FY2025 PP&E.
- **Capacity rationalization.** Four Costa Rican banana farms were closed in Q2 2026 with a $10.6M PP&E impairment; two Philippine operations were abandoned in Q3 2025 with a $37.5M impairment. Closing farms and writing down basis is management treating part of the problem as structural, not cyclical.
- **Sourcing cliff.** Approximately 94% of Philippine-sourced bananas came from a single grower representing roughly 13% of banana net sales, and that contract expired February 28, 2026. No renewal has been disclosed. The THACO Agri partnership for Vietnam/Cambodia sourcing and a Somalia arrangement via a UAE collaboration appear to be the replacement, unquantified.
- **Avocado oversupply** was cited as depressing per-unit selling prices in Q2 2026.

The genuine positive on the competitive axis is brand consolidation: after decades of split ownership, this issuer now owns the Del Monte trademark globally (subject to existing licenses) rather than licensing it for fresh produce, and management describes cross-selling across fresh, refrigerated and pantry categories under one supply chain. No quantified synergy target has been disclosed in any document reviewed.

**Competitive position score: 4.5.** Trajectory in the segment that drives one-third of revenue is clearly deteriorating on disease, cost and sourcing simultaneously. Brand ownership and low fresh-side customer concentration are real offsets that keep this above the bottom range.

## Risk Assessment

**Transfer pricing — the largest quantified contingency.** Three foreign jurisdictions have proposed deficiencies for 2012–2021 totaling approximately $275.9M including interest and penalties as of the Q2 10-Q, up from $260.6M in the FY2025 10-K. The company accrued $2.9M during FY2025 and states it "has not accrued any additional amounts based upon the proposed adjustments." Procedurally: one jurisdiction's trial set for July 4, 2025 was suspended pending a constitutional remedy; a third jurisdiction issued an unfavorable appeal decision on January 16, 2026, with a further appeal filed February 9, 2026. Tax authorities have registered real-estate collateral with fair values of $8.1M and $33.7M. The claim equals 19.3% of market capitalization and 13.7% of attributable equity. The non-accrual reflects a management judgment that loss is not probable, which is standard and auditable, and such assessments frequently settle at a fraction of headline. But the January 2026 adverse ruling is a data point against the judgment, and the exposure is large enough relative to the equity that even a 40% settlement would consume more than a year of guided EBITDA.

**Banana margin — cyclical or structural, unresolved.** This is the single highest-value open question and it is not answerable from the available evidence. Independent 2026 banana pricing and volume data could not be obtained. The bull reading is that an industry-wide supply shock (TR4, Sigatoka, freight) should eventually lift pricing and repair margins for surviving integrated players. The bear reading is that management's own attribution includes *weak North American demand*, which argues against price recovery, and that the cost drivers — bunker fuel and ocean freight, fertilizer — the 10-Q states management expects those commodity increases and volatility to be material rather than easing.

**Middle East logistics.** H1 2026 included $0.6M of customer claims and $1.7M of other product-related charges from Strait of Hormuz disruption, plus port congestion. Middle East revenue was $105.4M in Q2 (8.6% of sales), and management cited regional supply constraints and geopolitics as banana volume drags. The 10-Q states expected commodity volatility in shipping fuel and fertilizer will be material.

**Tariff whipsaw.** A November 14, 2025 executive order removed tariffs on bananas and pineapples; the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating broad IEEPA tariffs may make the company eligible for refunds of IEEPA duties paid, with quantum and timing unrecognized. The administration has announced intent to levy new duties under Sections 122 and 301. Net direction is unresolved, with the refund possibility a genuine unpriced upside option of undetermined size.

**Covenant reversion.** The 4.25x maximum leverage relief expires after the first fiscal quarter of 2027, reverting to 3.75x. At current leverage near 1.8x this is not binding, but the acquired business's pack cycle peaks working capital in Q2/Q3 and the revolver was raised twice within five months of closing. If adjusted EBITDA lands materially below guidance while inventory peaks, the reverting test becomes the operative constraint.

**Goodwill headroom.** At the FY2025 test the Prepared Foods reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by only 6.8% on $27.1M of goodwill; a one-point discount-rate increase plus a 5% cash-flow reduction would impair the full amount. The dollar exposure is small (0.8% of assets); the signal about that unit's expected earnings trajectory is what matters.

**Governance and control.** The Abu-Ghazaleh family held approximately 30.0% of shares at FY2025 year-end, with the Chairman/CEO holding an irrevocable proxy over all of it, alongside a classified board and Cayman domicile. The Q2 10-Q discloses a $4.7M accrual for historical US income and employment tax withholding on compensation for the CEO's US services, of which the company believes it can enforce reimbursement of only approximately $1.9M — leaving roughly $2.8M borne by shareholders. The amount is financially immaterial; as an internal-control and governance signal about a multi-year compliance lapse involving the controlling shareholder-CEO, it is not.

**Short interest** is 8.6% of float with 3.5 days to cover — elevated but not crowded, and not high enough to create meaningful squeeze mechanics in either direction.

**Risk factors score: 3.5.** The combination of a $275.9M unaccrued claim with an adverse ruling already received, an existential agronomic threat to one-third of revenue, single-grower sourcing that has already lapsed, and geographic concentration of 41% of PP&E in one country is a stack of binary and semi-binary exposures that a 0.94x tangible book multiple does not adequately price.

No options data was available for this issuer, so no options-based positioning read is offered.

## Management Quality

The evidence is genuinely mixed, and the two strongest signals point in opposite directions.

**Positive: insider buying at current levels.** On August 4, 2026, President & COO Mohammed Abbas purchased 12,000 shares on the open market at $29.8921 (approximately $358,705), raising his holdings roughly 20% to 71,930. On June 11, 2026, director Ahmad Abu-Ghazaleh bought 4,000 shares at $29.405, establishing an initial direct position. Open-market purchases by a sitting COO after a 30% drawdown are the highest-quality insider signal available, and these transactions occurred within 3% of the current price. Absolute dollar amounts are small, and neither purchase constitutes a valuation opinion, but the direction is unambiguous.

**Offsetting: a cluster of SVP-level sales on March 6–9, 2026 at $41.46–$42.50**, immediately before the March 19 acquisition close and near the 2026 high. Several exceed plausible tax-withholding sizing — one officer sold 7,395 shares leaving 8,397, another sold 6,297 leaving 3,197. Form 4 footnotes and 10b5-1 designations were not obtained, so post-vest diversification under a pre-established plan cannot be ruled out. The pattern reads as discretionary rather than mechanical.

**Capital allocation** is defensible on the large decisions and weaker on the small ones. Deleveraging FY2022–FY2025 (long-term debt down 68%) was well-timed ahead of a higher-rate regime. Divesting Mann Packing, which contributed $(17.1)M pretax in H1 2025, removed a loss-maker, though the $31.3M recognized consideration is structured as $5.0M over 60 monthly installments plus $14.0M on the fifth anniversary and remains entirely receivable from a private buyer whose credit is unknown. The Del Monte Foods purchase at asset value with zero goodwill is a reasonable structure for a distressed carve-out — it limits overpayment risk, though it also means no cushion against subsequent impairment, and Q2 measurement-period adjustments already brought PP&E down $4.6M and Mexican deferred tax assets down $5.7M. Buybacks in Q2 2026 averaged $34.40 against a current $30.75, so recent repurchases are underwater; $100.15M of the $150M authorization remains.

**Compensation alignment is the weakest link.** CEO FY2025 total compensation was $10,144,565, up 14.5% year over year and up 128% from 2023, while the company-selected performance measure (EBITDA) fell 19.7% from $273.9M to $220.0M and pay-versus-performance net income fell 34.0%. The reconciling variable is total shareholder return: the TSR index rose from 105.78 to 171.17 against a peer index of 91.89. TSR-linked pay is a mainstream design and FY2025 shareholder returns genuinely beat the peer group by a wide margin, so this may be the plan functioning as intended. It nonetheless means FY2025 pay tracked share price rather than operating results in a year when operations weakened materially. CEO PSUs for 2024–2026 pay 0–125% of target while all other participants cap at 100%.

**Management quality score: 4.6.** The COO purchase and the CEO's 5.08M-share direct stake provide real economic alignment. The payroll-tax compliance failure, the pay-versus-operations divergence, the March insider sales at the high, and buybacks executed 12% above the current price offset it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $42 (+37%)

Banana margins prove cyclical. TR4 and Black Sigatoka are industry-wide supply shocks; as competing volumes contract, pricing rises and an integrated producer with 47% owned banana volume captures disproportionate benefit. Restoring banana gross margin to roughly 7% — still 300 bps below FY2023 — adds approximately $68M of annual gross profit on current banana revenue, taking adjusted EBITDA toward $290–300M. First-year acquisition friction rolls off: $6.4M of transaction costs, the $16.1M Joyba write-off, and duplicated overhead do not recur at the same scale. Prepared Foods margins near 18–19% prove operational rather than accounting artifacts, and cross-selling under unified brand ownership generates the first organic revenue growth in five years. Transfer-pricing claims settle at a small fraction of $275.9M. IEEPA refunds arrive. At 8.0x $295M EBITDA, enterprise value of $2,360M less $412M of net debt and NCI implies $41.32 per share, plus a 3.9% dividend along the way.

### Bear Case — $20 (-35%)

Banana deterioration is structural, not cyclical. Disease pressure raises per-hectare costs permanently, the closed Costa Rican and Philippine farms represent the beginning rather than the end of capacity write-downs, the lapsed Philippine sole-grower contract removes 13% of banana revenue without a quantified replacement, and weak North American demand prevents pricing from recovering costs. Elevated bunker fuel, container rates and fertiliser persist through 2027. Prepared Foods margin normalizes downward as the inventory step-up unwinds and the post-bankruptcy pricing window closes, in a structurally declining category now facing state labeling mandates in 2027 and 2028. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA settles near $190M. The transfer-pricing appeals go against the company, forcing an accrual and eventual cash outflow of $100M+. Leverage rises as the 4.25x covenant relief expires, and the dividend is cut to preserve headroom, removing the yield support. At 7.0x $190M, enterprise value of $1,330M less $412M implies $19.47 per share.

### Base Case — $31 (+1%)

FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands within the reported $230–240M range, with H2 at $100–110M as guided. Banana margin stabilizes in the 3–4% range rather than recovering to historical levels, and rather than collapsing further. Prepared Foods contributes roughly $625M of full-year revenue at high-teens gross margin. The transfer-pricing matters remain unresolved through 2027 with no accrual change. The dividend is maintained. At 8.0x $235M, enterprise value of $1,880M less $412M of net debt and NCI implies $31.14 per share, with the 3.90% dividend producing a total return near 4–5% over twelve months.

Probability weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives an expected value of approximately $29.90, roughly 2.8% below the current price before dividends and approximately break-even including them. That is materially below a fair required return for an equity carrying this risk profile, and the distribution is left-skewed.

## Conclusion

Del Monte Corporation is cheap on every static measure and deteriorating on every dynamic one. The static case is real: 0.94x tangible book, 7.9x an EBITDA guide, 1.8x gross leverage, a 3.90% dividend covered roughly 2x on normalized free cash flow, and a sitting COO who bought 12,000 shares in the open market within 3% of today's price. Those facts are why this is a REDUCE rather than an AVOID, and why a full exit at the June low would have been the wrong call.

The dynamic case dominates. Adjusted EBITDA fell 23.7% in the half and 30.0% in the quarter, with the acquisition already in the numbers; the guided H2 of $100–110M implies further core deterioration even with roughly $432M of incremental acquired revenue arriving. Banana gross margin has gone 10.0% → 5.9% → 4.8% → 3.5% → 2.3% across four consecutive periods, and management's own attribution spans demand, supply and cost at once, with the cost leg tied to freight and fertilizer prices that management expects to remain volatile and elevated. Four farms closed in Costa Rica, two operations abandoned in the Philippines, and the sole grower supplying 94% of Philippine volume has an expired contract with no disclosed replacement. Above all of that sits a $275.9M transfer-pricing claim equal to 19% of market capitalization, carrying a $2.9M accrual after one jurisdiction already ruled against the company on appeal.

The probability-weighted return from $30.75 is approximately zero including the dividend, against a bear case 35% lower. Cheapness without a dated catalyst, sitting on a falling earnings base with an unaccrued nine-figure contingency, is not a margin of safety. Existing owners should trim into strength; prospective buyers should wait for the Q3 print to establish whether the banana margin has a floor.
