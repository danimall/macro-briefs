**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Akamai is in the middle of a business-model transition that the market has begun to reprice aggressively. The company reorganized its revenue reporting in Q1 2026 into three categories that make the transition legible: Security ($589.8M, 55% of revenue, +11% YoY), Delivery and other cloud applications ($389.2M, 36%, –7% YoY, the legacy CDN business in structural decline), and Cloud infrastructure services ($94.6M, 9%, +40% YoY, the AI-inference growth engine built on the Linode acquisition). The anchor event was a seven-year, ~$1.8B commitment from a leading U.S.-based frontier model provider (identified by Bloomberg as Anthropic) to run inference workloads on Akamai's distributed edge network. The disclosure drove a +27% single-day move on May 8, 2026, the largest one-day gain in over two decades, and a roughly 40% run from mid-2025 levels.

The tension is that the narrative has moved faster than the fundamentals. GAAP operating margin fell to 11% in Q1'26 from 15% a year earlier, non-GAAP operating margin fell to 26% from 30%, and net income has declined three consecutive years ($548M in 2023 → $505M in 2024 → $452M in 2025), with Q1'26 net income of $106.3M below the prior year's $123.2M. The margin compression is a direct consequence of the cloud/AI build-out: co-location costs up 18%, network build-out up 25%, and rising server/memory costs amplified by hyperscaler-driven component demand. Akamai is spending ahead of revenue that is contracted but not yet recognized. The Anthropic commitment is a commitment, not realized revenue, and management's own risk language notes that a frontier-lab customer's supplier mix can shift within 90-day windows.

The stock has retraced from a $165.45 52-week high to $117.37, roughly halfway between the $87 low and $195 high analyst targets. This is a genuinely two-sided setup. The cloud segment's optionality is real and now partly credible on the back of contracted demand, but so are the margin drag, the customer concentration in the growth engine, the ~$4.14B convertible note load, and a five-year total shareholder return of –17% against a peer group up 158%. We rate the stock HOLD. The AI-inference thesis is the clearest re-rating path Akamai has had in years, but at the current price much of that optionality is already embedded, and the near-term earnings trajectory is deteriorating on GAAP metrics that comp policy is obscuring.

## Company Overview

Akamai operates one of the largest distributed edge platforms in the world (4,300+ points of presence across 130+ countries plus core and distributed compute sites) and reports as a single operating segment. The three-category revenue split introduced in Q1 2026 signals the strategic pivot away from the commoditized content-delivery roots toward higher-value security and cloud compute.

Security is now the largest and healthiest recurring franchise: application security, API security, and Guardicore microsegmentation, growing 11% YoY and representing 55% of revenue. Delivery is the legacy CDN business, in secular decline from price erosion at renewal, large-customer DIY insourcing, and traffic optimization. Cloud infrastructure services is the newest and fastest-growing category (+40% YoY), built on the 2022 Linode acquisition and extended with AI-inference products including Akamai Cloud Inference and the Akamai Inference Cloud on Nvidia Blackwell. The investment case is straightforward to describe but hard to underwrite: a declining legacy line being replaced by a mature grower (security) and an early, capital-hungry grower (cloud/AI) whose payoff is contracted but unproven at scale.

The company is led by co-founder and CEO Dr. Tom Leighton, with CFO Ed McGowan. It carries no dividend, runs a $2.0B buyback authorization (mostly to offset heavy equity comp), and holds ~$1.7B in cash and marketable securities against ~$4.14B of low-coupon convertible notes.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth is low single digits: FY2025 revenue of ~$4.14B, up from $4.03B in 2024 and $3.82B in 2023, as security and cloud gains barely outpace delivery declines. Q1'26 revenue was $1.07B, +6% YoY (+4% constant currency).

Profitability is the central concern. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 40% from 43%, and cost of revenue rose to 44% from 41%, driven by the cloud/AI build-out. This is deliberate spend, but it is real margin erosion happening now for a payoff that must arrive later.

Cash flow remains solid at the operating level. Q1'26 operating cash flow was $312.5M (up from $251.2M, partly aided by tax timing), but capex plus capitalized software runs ~$192M per quarter and is rising as AI infrastructure scales, which compresses free cash flow conversion during the build phase. The convertible senior notes mature across 2027, 2029, and 2033 (coupons 0.25%–1.125%), with $1.15B due September 2027. The leverage is real and drives a low Altman Z-score, but the coupons are cheap, the 2033 tranche ($1.725B) was freshly issued in May 2025 to term out maturities, and the notes trade above par (~$5.16B fair value vs $4.14B principal) on the stock rally, which reduces near-term refinancing pressure while raising dilution risk given conversion prices of $93–$126.

One item materially muddies the earnings picture: stock-based compensation of $128.7M in Q1'26 alone, roughly 12% of revenue. Akamai deliberately shifted its 401(k) match and other compensation to stock in 2025 and added a 2026 retirement-vesting benefit that accelerates SBC recognition. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $1.61 in Q1'26; GAAP diluted EPS was only $0.71 and fell YoY. Investors leaning on non-GAAP metrics should recognize the widening gap is partly a comp-policy artifact, not just an adjustment for one-time items.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and recently softening: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, with a slight miss in Q1'26 (–1.4%) after double-digit beats in mid-2025 (+9.3%, +11.3%). Estimate revisions are essentially flat-to-slightly-negative: current-year EPS at $6.72 is down 2.0% over 90 days, next-year at $7.23 down 4.8% over 90 days, though next-year ticked up 1.3% in the last 30 days. This is not strong positive revision velocity. Consensus is stable-to-marginally-lower, which argues against treating the current price as a coiled spring.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is entirely about mix. Security compounding at ~11% and cloud infrastructure at ~40% must overwhelm a delivery line shrinking ~7%. In Q1'26 the math produced +6% total revenue growth, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to a revenue midpoint around $4.5B on the strength of the contracted cloud demand.

The Anthropic commitment is the durability test. At full ramp it represents ~$257M/year (~6% of guided 2026 revenue), following a separate ~$200M cloud infrastructure deal signed in February 2026. If these deals ramp on schedule, cloud infrastructure could move from 9% of revenue toward a meaningfully larger share within two to three years, and the inference-focused positioning (low-latency, distributed compute complementing centralized hyperscaler training) is a structurally differentiated value proposition rather than a scale race Akamai cannot win. Broad-based industry inference demand supports the argument that this is real and not a single-company story.

The caveats are substantive. First, the commitment is not recognized revenue; it depends on both the customer fulfilling obligations and Akamai deploying infrastructure on time. Second, concentration is increasing precisely in the growth engine: a single frontier-AI customer whose supplier mix shifts quarter to quarter. Third, delivery's decline is not stabilizing; the 10-K flags a large social media customer (context implies TikTok) that reduced traffic via DIY and de-risking of U.S. providers. The security franchise is the most dependable growth contributor and is arguably underweighted in the market's attention relative to the AI narrative. On balance, growth prospects are positive but neither accelerating nor de-risked, which places this dimension modestly above neutral.

## Valuation Assessment

At $117.37, Akamai trades at ~17.5x current-year consensus EPS of $6.72 and ~16.2x next-year $7.23. On a growth-adjusted basis that multiple is not demanding for a company with a mid-single-digit top line and a credible path to reaccelerate via cloud. The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited use: the "Internet Services & Infrastructure" comparables span DigitalOcean at 77.8x, GoDaddy at 11.9x, Verisign at 30.9x, and two loss-making crypto miners, so the 30.9x median P/E is not a clean benchmark. Against its own history and against the quality of the security franchise, ~16–17x forward is closer to fair than cheap or expensive.

The trailing P/E of ~39.7x reflects depressed GAAP earnings (heavy SBC and the build-out drag) and should not anchor the analysis. This is a case where cycle/normalized earnings power matters, but the normalization cuts both ways: if the cloud build-out reaches scale and margins recover, normalized earnings power is higher than trailing suggests; if margins stay compressed through a multi-year investment cycle, the "trough earnings" framing does not hold. The estimate revisions do not support an aggressive cycle-adjusted upgrade; consensus has been trimming, not raising, current-year and next-year numbers.

Analyst targets bracket the setup: low $87, mean ~$159, median $165, high $195. The current price sits below the mean, which superficially implies upside. But treating those targets as floors requires an unambiguous positive-revision backdrop, which is absent; estimates are flat-to-lower over 90 days. The median $165 embeds substantial cloud ramp execution that is not yet in the numbers. A reasonable base case values the equity around $130–$140 on ~18–19x a modestly recovering forward EPS as the cloud mix improves and delivery decline decelerates, roughly 11–19% above current levels but well short of the median target. The upside is real if the Anthropic ramp converts and margins recover; the downside is real if the build-out drag persists without revenue recognition catching up. The absence of a strong independent mispricing catalyst at the current price keeps this dimension near neutral. A meaningfully cheaper multiple with the same fundamentals would improve the case.

## Competitive Landscape

Akamai sits across three competitive fields with divergent trajectories. In security it competes with a broadening field (Cloudflare, Zscaler, and platform vendors) but is winning share in API security and microsegmentation (Guardicore), a genuine strength. In delivery it faces commoditization and DIY insourcing by its largest customers, a structurally deteriorating position. In cloud/AI infrastructure it is a distant challenger to AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud on raw scale, but the distributed-edge network purpose-built for low-latency inference is a differentiated asset, and frontier labs increasingly assemble multi-supplier portfolios rather than picking a single cloud, which opens a seat for Akamai in inference specifically rather than in training.

The competitive-position score has been marked down sharply in prior reports (from 7.5 to 5.5), and that reflects a fair read: the legacy business is losing on price and insourcing, while the growth business is a challenger with a real but unproven angle. The Anthropic and February deals are evidence the inference positioning has commercial traction, but competitive access to servers, memory, co-location space, and power favors the hyperscalers, who can constrain industry supply and raise Akamai's costs. Net trajectory is mixed (strengthening in inference and security, weakening in delivery), which supports a neutral score.

## Risk Assessment

Several idiosyncratic risks are not fully captured in a mid-teens forward multiple:

**Concentration in the growth engine.** The Anthropic deal that anchors the bull case is a single frontier-AI customer whose supplier mix can shift materially within 90-day windows. A slowdown or supplier reallocation would strand capex deployed against contracted-but-uncertain demand.

**Margin drag ahead of revenue.** The cloud build-out is compressing margins now for a payoff that must materialize on schedule. If the ramp slips, the company carries the cost without the offsetting revenue.

**Leverage and refinancing.** The September 2027 maturity depends on market conditions and the stock price at refinancing. The elevated stock keeps conversion in play, creating dilution risk that partly offsets the buyback.

**SBC masking GAAP deterioration.** The deliberate shift of compensation into stock widens the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap and flatters the headline non-GAAP EPS the bull case relies on.

**Leadership transition in the cloud unit.** The COO/GM of the Cloud Technology Group terminated his 10b5-1 selling plan on March 3, 2026, and the 10-K flags succession as a specific risk, worth monitoring given this unit now defines the story.

**Geopolitical and infrastructure risk.** The Q1'26 10-Q added unusually specific language about the U.S.–Israel/Iran conflict and Iranian strikes on Persian Gulf data centers, with ~6% of employees in Israel; plus EU digital-sovereignty/data-localization frameworks and residual PAFACA/TikTok review risk (divestiture finalized January 22, 2026, with a no-liability determination but lingering litigation exposure).

**Track record.** Five-year TSR of –17% against a peer group up 158% is a sobering backdrop; the AI-inference pivot is the bull case for re-rating, but the company has been value-destroying for half a decade.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 179.9% against 1-year historical volatility of 55.5%, a 124-point spread and a 93rd-percentile reading, with a pronounced backwardation term structure (179.9% at one week collapsing to 65.0% at six months). This pattern signals the market expects an imminent binary event, consistent with an earnings print or catalyst dated within days of the snapshot. The one-week put/call volume ratio is elevated (213x on a very small base), while the one-month skew is slightly call-favored (–9.1%) and six-month positioning is near balanced. Short interest is elevated at 15.4% of float (4.7 days to cover), reflecting institutional bearish positioning. Near-term IV is rich and pricing event risk, which argues for patience on entry timing and against chasing; the intermediate-term positioning is more balanced than the headline near-term print suggests. This does not override the fundamental HOLD, but it reinforces that the risk/reward around the imminent event is not asymmetrically favorable.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$165–$195).** The Anthropic and February inference deals ramp on schedule, validating Akamai's distributed-edge platform as the natural home for low-latency AI inference. Cloud infrastructure services grow from 9% of revenue toward the high teens over two to three years, security continues compounding at ~11%, and delivery decline decelerates. As the cloud mix improves, margins recover off the build-out trough, GAAP net income reverses its decline, and the stock re-rates toward 20x+ on rising forward EPS. Contracted demand plus a differentiated inference angle drives the median $165 target and beyond.

**Bear Case (~$87–$100).** The build-out drag persists, delivery declines faster than security and cloud can offset, and the Anthropic ramp slips or the customer reallocates supply, stranding capex. GAAP net income continues to fall, SBC-inflated non-GAAP EPS is increasingly scrutinized, and the September 2027 note maturity becomes a live refinancing concern if the stock retraces. The multiple compresses toward the low end of the analyst range and the five-year track record of underperformance extends. This is roughly 15–26% below current levels.

**Base Case (~$130–$140).** Cloud ramps but not explosively, security compounds steadily, delivery decline moderates but does not stop, and margins bottom without recovering sharply. Forward EPS recovers modestly toward ~$7.50–$8.00 over the next 18 months, supporting ~18–19x and a price in the $130s. This implies 11–19% upside from $117.37, a positive but not compelling risk-adjusted return given the identified downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (rationale: the bull and bear cases are both legitimate and the near-term evidence does not clearly favor either).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected early August 2026) is the key near-term catalyst, with the options market pricing an event even sooner. The cloud infrastructure ramp cadence over the next two to three quarters is the decisive fundamental data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Cloud infrastructure services revenue accelerating above ~50% YoY with evidence the Anthropic deal is converting to recognized revenue on schedule, plus GAAP operating margin stabilizing or expanding off the 11% trough.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turning decisively positive (+10%+ over 90 days on the current-year and next-year lines), reversing the current flat-to-negative trend.
- A pullback toward the high-$90s to low-$100s that restores a more asymmetric entry point with the same or improving fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence the Anthropic/February deals are slipping, being renegotiated, or the customer is reallocating supply.
- Delivery decline accelerating beyond –10% YoY while cloud growth decelerates, breaking the mix-shift math.
- GAAP net income continuing to fall alongside a widening SBC gap and a September 2027 refinancing that appears strained (stock below conversion prices).

## Conclusion

Akamai is a transition story with a credible re-rating catalyst in the contracted ~$1.8B inference commitment. But the fundamentals lag the narrative: GAAP operating margin is at 11%, net income has declined three straight years, SBC is flattering the non-GAAP figures, and consensus estimates are being trimmed rather than raised. The stock has already captured much of the AI-inference optionality in its rally, and at $117.37 it sits near our $130–$140 base case with a bear case in the high-$80s to low-$100s. Elevated short interest and near-term IV signal the market expects imminent volatility, not resolution.

The security franchise is a solid, underappreciated grower; the cloud/AI segment offers real upside if the ramp converts; and the legacy CDN decline plus build-out drag are the clear near-term headwinds. This is a balanced risk/reward that does not clear the bar for a BUY at the current price, nor does it warrant an AVOID given the contracted demand and reasonable forward multiple. We rate the stock HOLD and would become constructive on either a demonstrated cloud-revenue ramp with margin stabilization or a pullback that restores asymmetry.
