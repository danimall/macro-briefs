**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Globalstar stopped being a fundamental equity story on April 13, 2026, when it signed a definitive agreement to be acquired by Amazon.com for $90.00 per share in cash (or Amazon stock, subject to a 40% cash cap), valuing the company at approximately $11.57 billion. Every other data point in the file — the negative trailing EPS, the distress-zone Altman Z-Score, the $621.7M operating cash flow that exceeds revenue, the falling consensus estimates — is now subordinate to that single fact. The stock at $82.64 trades at roughly an 8-9% gross discount to the $90 cash consideration, a merger-arbitrage spread that reflects the long regulatory timeline (expected close in 2027, outside date extendable to April 2028) and the residual risk that the deal does not clear antitrust, FCC, and foreign satellite/telecom approvals.

The setup is unusually clean on the shareholder-approval front. Thermo, the ~57.6% controlling stockholder, executed a written consent approving the merger immediately, eliminating the need for a minority shareholder vote. What remains is regulatory, and the asymmetric break-fee structure (Amazon owes Globalstar approximately $592M if the deal fails on regulatory grounds, versus the $420M Globalstar would owe Amazon) signals which party is more committed. The strategic logic is coherent: Amazon folds a direct-to-device (D2D) satellite network, terrestrial spectrum, and the Apple relationship into Project Kuiper / "Amazon Leo" to challenge Starlink's direct-to-cell ambitions.

For existing holders, this is a hold-to-close situation with a defined ceiling at $90 (less a possible ~$0.85/share milestone adjustment) and a downside to standalone value — likely the $40-55 range — if the deal collapses. The risk/reward is acceptable but not asymmetric enough to justify deploying fresh capital: roughly 9% gross upside over 12-24 months against a 30%-plus drawdown in a break scenario. We rate GSAT HOLD.

## Company Overview

Globalstar operates a Mobile Satellite Services (MSS) business built on a Low Earth Orbit (LEO) constellation plus a global network of ground gateways. The business has four reporting pieces, but one dominates everything:

1. **Wholesale satellite capacity services (63% of FY2025 revenue, 66% in Q1 2026)** — the franchise. Globalstar sells satellite network access to Apple, powering the iPhone's Emergency SOS via Satellite feature launched in November 2022. All strategic and financial dynamics flow through this relationship. Wholesale capacity revenue grew from $145.3M (2024) to $172.7M (2025), up 19%.
2. **Commercial IoT (~10% of revenue)** — asset-tracking modules and chips (STX-3, ST-150, the new RM200M two-way module launched October 2025). Customers include FEMA, the U.S. Army/Air Force, NOAA, BP, and Shell. Subscriber base ~540K average, gross activations up ~50% YoY.
3. **SPOT (~14%)** — consumer satellite messaging/SOS devices, in decline (~222K subs) against Garmin inReach and Iridium.
4. **Duplex (~6%)** — legacy voice, being wound down.

Additional assets include terrestrial spectrum (Band 53/n53, roughly 12 billion MHz-POPs across 12 countries) and the XCOM/Virewirx IP (coordinated multi-point radio and peer-to-peer connectivity), whose purchase option Globalstar exercised in January 2026. The company has roughly 797,000 total MSS subscribers and 477 employees. The moat rests on high barriers to entry: spectrum licenses, satellite and ground capex, and ITU coordination. Competitors include Iridium, Viasat/Inmarsat, ORBCOMM, and the emerging threats of SpaceX Starlink direct-to-cell and AST SpaceMobile.

The company moved from NYSE American to Nasdaq in February 2025 following a 1-for-15 reverse split that reduced share count from ~1.8B to ~128M.

## Financial Analysis

The financials require careful interpretation because the headline figures are distorted by the Apple prepayment structure and standard screens read them backwards.

**Revenue** has grown steadily on Apple's volume: FY2022 $148.5M → FY2023 $223.8M → FY2024 $250.3M → FY2025 $273.0M (+9% YoY). Q1 2026 revenue was $70.1M, up 17% YoY, with wholesale capacity up 26%.

**GAAP profitability is weak but misleading.** FY2025 operating income was just $7.4M (2.7% margin) with a net loss of $8.7M. Q1 2026 showed a net loss of $17.4M, but income from operations turned positive at $8.2M (versus -$8.5M a year earlier). The net loss is driven primarily by $40.6M of non-cash interest (the ASC 606 "significant financing component" recorded on Apple's prepayment) rather than a cash cost. Adjusted EBITDA, the company's preferred KPI, was approximately $136M in FY2025, reflecting heavy non-cash depreciation ($87M) and stock compensation ($23M).

**The cash flow anomaly is the most important thing to understand here.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $621.7M vastly exceeds revenue. This is not earnings; it is Apple's Infrastructure Prepayment ($430.6M received in 2025) recorded as deferred revenue. Net deferred revenue stood at $891.7M as of Q1 2026. The cash flows straight into satellite capex: FY2025 investing outflow was $550M (MDA Space milestone payments of $316M plus $33M to SpaceX). PP&E nearly doubled to $1.3B in FY2025 and reached $1.43B in Q1 2026, with over $1.1B in space-component construction-in-progress.

**The balance sheet looks distressed on a screen but isn't.** The Altman Z-Score of 0.6 and debt/equity of 5.54 would flag bankruptcy risk in any mechanical filter. The reality is different: most of the ~$404M in debt principal is Apple-related — the 2024 Debt Repayment ($221.6M) and 2023 Funding Agreement ($182.1M) — repaid via service-fee offsets from Apple rather than cash. The current ratio is a healthy 2.42 and the minimum-cash covenant is just $30M against $358M in cash (Q1 2026). The -$2.15B retained deficit reflects the troubled pre-Apple history. Standard distress screens get this exactly backwards: a company sitting on $891M of deferred revenue and an $11.6B acquisition bid is not a credit risk.

For the avoidance of doubt: in a deal context, the balance-sheet specifics matter less than the $90 cash floor. They are relevant principally as a description of standalone value if the transaction breaks.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data carries a superficial warning that should be read carefully. Consensus current-quarter and next-quarter EPS have been cut sharply (current quarter -2,408% over 30 days, next quarter -450%), and the revision trend reads "broadly falling." On a standalone basis this would be a meaningful negative signal. In a merger context it is largely noise — analysts are modeling deal-related accounting items and a business that is being acquired, not handicapping organic earnings power. Only one or two analysts are still publishing estimates, and the dispersion confirms low conviction in the standalone numbers.

The underlying operational trajectory remains positive: Q1 2026 revenue +17%, wholesale capacity +26%, IoT gross activations +50% YoY, and the new RM200M two-way module extending the IoT product line. The Extended MSS Network buildout (roughly $2.0B projected spend, $0.7B incurred) is funded by Apple's increased prepayment commitment (raised to ~$1.58B as part of the Amazon deal amendments). First replacement satellites were delivered in April 2026, with the first SpaceX launch scheduled for May 2026.

For the merger thesis, growth durability matters only as the downside floor. If the deal closes, the standalone growth path is irrelevant to the return; the consideration is fixed at $90 less any milestone adjustment. If the deal breaks, the Apple-funded LEO buildout, IoT momentum, and spectrum optionality become the recovery case from a sharply lower price.

## Valuation Assessment

GSAT should be valued as a merger-arbitrage position, not as a going concern. The relevant numbers:

- **Deal floor:** $90.00/share cash (or Amazon stock), subject to a downward milestone adjustment of up to $110M (~$0.85/share on ~129M shares) tied to the HIBLEO-4 replacement-satellite launch and related operational milestones.
- **Current price:** $82.64.
- **Gross spread:** ~$7.36, or ~8.9% to the unadjusted $90; ~8.0% to a $89.15 milestone-adjusted floor.
- **Timeline:** Expected close in 2027, outside date April 13, 2027, extendable to April 2028. Annualized, the spread implies roughly 4-8% depending on the close date, compensating for regulatory risk plus the cash/stock proration mechanics.

The standalone valuation framework (forward P/E, EV/EBITDA versus Iridium and other MSS peers) is now beside the point except as the break-scenario reference. The stock ran from $18 to a $84.69 high over the prior year (a 345% move), driven first by the Apple growth story and then by the Amazon bid. That appreciation reflects fundamental re-rating culminating in a strategic acquisition rather than overvaluation. The relevant question today is the spread to the cash offer, and that spread is modest.

The published analyst price target of $90 (mean, median, high, and low all converging at $90) is simply the deal price, confirming that the Street treats this as a closed-end arbitrage rather than a fundamental call.

The valuation score reflects the bounded nature of the situation: limited upside to a defined ceiling, with the spread already partly compensating for risk. It is neither cheap (the upside is capped) nor expensive (it trades below the cash floor).

## Competitive Landscape

Globalstar's competitive position is the core of why Amazon is paying $11.57B. The company sits in the satellite direct-to-device land grab, holding three scarce assets: an operating LEO constellation, terrestrial spectrum (Band 53/n53 across 12 countries), and the Apple relationship powering iPhone Emergency SOS. Press coverage uniformly frames the acquisition as Amazon bolstering Project Kuiper / Amazon Leo with D2D capability and spectrum to challenge Starlink's direct-to-cell partnership with T-Mobile and AST SpaceMobile.

The moat is real but contested. Barriers to entry — spectrum licenses, satellite and ground capex, ITU coordination — are high. But the competitive threats are well-capitalized: SpaceX's Starlink and AST SpaceMobile are racing toward the same direct-to-device market. The single most important competitive fact is the Apple concentration: 63-66% of revenue from one customer that can terminate the Updated Services Agreements on notice and is not contractually barred from using another satellite network. The Amazon deal substantially de-risks this — Amazon will assume the prepayment agreements and acquire Apple's 20% SPE stake, and Apple consented to and increased its prepayment commitment as part of the transaction. Converting a fragile single-customer dependency into ownership by a deep-pocketed strategic is the strategic value Amazon is buying.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Deal-completion / regulatory risk (now dominant).** The Amazon deal must clear HSR antitrust, FCC, and foreign satellite/telecom/FDI approvals. A break would likely send the stock sharply toward standalone value. This is the central risk, and it is regulatory rather than financial or shareholder-vote driven.

**2. Merger-consideration adjustment.** The $90 is subject to a downward adjustment of up to $110M (~$0.85/share) if Globalstar misses amended operational/satellite milestones — notably the HIBLEO-4 replacement-satellite launch scheduled for May 2026 — before closing.

**3. Cash/stock proration.** Cash is capped at 40% of shares; the balance converts to Amazon stock at an exchange ratio. Holders electing cash may be prorated into AMZN, introducing pre-close exposure to Amazon's share price.

**4. Apple concentration (the underlying business risk).** Apple can terminate on notice. The deal partly neutralizes this, but it remains the key risk in any deal-break scenario.

**5. Execution/satellite risk.** MDA Space has missed delivery milestones (Globalstar is pursuing liquidated damages); a Q1 2025 satellite power-control anomaly caused a $7M uninsured write-off. The new constellation depends on timely SpaceX launches.

**6. Governance/related-party density.** Thermo control, CEO Jacobs's role as controlling stockholder of XCOM/Virewirx (from which Globalstar licensed and bought IP), and a Thermo headquarters lease create related-party entanglement, largely neutralized by the cash-out at close but relevant in a break scenario.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is just 11.3% against a 1-year historical volatility of 69.0% — a -57.7% spread, with IV in the 6th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is flat (near 11.3% ≈ far 12.6%), implying a ±1.7% move over the nearest expiry and ±7.5% over six months. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.1%), and put/call open interest is heavily call-weighted at 0.14. This is the signature of a stock trading on deal mechanics rather than fundamental uncertainty: the market expects the price to grind toward $90 with limited two-sided risk in the near term, and the 6-month implied move (±7.5%) roughly brackets the gross spread plus break tail. The collapsed IV is consistent with a merger-arb situation rather than a directional bet.

Short interest is modest at 6.5% of float, 2.2 days to cover — no crowded positioning either way, consistent with arbitrage capital holding the spread.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90, +8.9%):** The deal closes on roughly the expected timeline. Regulators clear HSR, the FCC, and foreign approvals without material conditions; the asymmetric $592M Amazon reverse-termination fee proves accurate as a commitment signal. Shareholder approval is already secured via Thermo's written consent, so there is no vote risk. Holders capture the full $90 (or the milestone-adjusted $89.15), realizing the gross spread over 12-24 months, an annualized return of roughly 4-8%. The strategic logic — Amazon needs D2D capability, spectrum, and the Apple relationship for Kuiper/Leo — makes a renegotiated higher bid conceivable but not the base case.

**Bear Case ($40-55, -33% to -52%):** Regulators block or impose unacceptable conditions on the transaction, or Amazon walks (collecting the $592M fee partly offsets Globalstar's standalone position but does not support the equity). The stock reverts toward standalone value, which depends on the Apple relationship continuing and the LEO buildout converting deferred revenue into sustained Adjusted EBITDA. At ~$136M Adjusted EBITDA and ~$404M debt principal (against $358M cash, i.e. roughly net-debt-neutral), against MSS-peer multiples and the single-customer overhang, standalone value plausibly sits in the $40-55 range — below the pre-bid trading range adjusted for subsequent operational progress, but well below $90. The downside materially exceeds the upside.

The probability-weighted outcome favors closing — written-consent approval is secured, the strategic rationale is sound, and the break-fee structure signals Amazon's commitment — but the regulatory runway is long and the FCC/foreign-approval path for a Starlink challenger involving spectrum is not trivial.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a hold-to-close arbitrage for existing owners and an unattractive entry for new capital given the bounded upside against a large break-scenario drawdown.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Regulatory clearances (HSR early termination, FCC grant, key foreign approvals) materially de-risk the deal, compressing the implied break probability — at which point the residual spread becomes a near-riskless ~6-8% over a shortened horizon.
- A topping bid or signal of competing strategic interest that raises the effective floor above $90.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Substantive regulatory pushback — a second-request from antitrust authorities accompanied by adverse commentary, FCC opposition tied to spectrum/national-security concerns, or foreign-approval delays that push the outside date toward April 2028.
- A confirmed material milestone miss (HIBLEO-4 launch failure or significant slip) that triggers the $110M downward adjustment and signals broader execution problems that could give Amazon leverage to renegotiate or walk.
- Any indication Apple is reconsidering its consent or the amended prepayment terms.

**Reassessment timeline:** Monitor each regulatory milestone (HSR waiting period, FCC docket activity, foreign filings) and the May 2026 SpaceX launch / HIBLEO-4 milestone. Next natural reassessment is the next quarterly filing and any 8-K disclosing regulatory progress. Expected resolution: a 2027 close, with the spread grinding toward $90 as approvals accumulate.

## Conclusion

Globalstar is a merger-arbitrage position, not a fundamental equity, and should be analyzed as such. At $82.64 against a $90 cash floor, holders are paid roughly 8-9% gross to bear regulatory risk over a 12-24 month runway. The shareholder-approval risk is removed (Thermo's written consent), the strategic rationale is coherent (Amazon needs Globalstar's network, spectrum, and Apple relationship for Kuiper/Leo), and the asymmetric $592M Amazon reverse-termination fee signals genuine commitment. The accounting distress signals — the 0.6 Z-Score, the 5.54 debt/equity, the $621M operating cash flow exceeding revenue — are artifacts of the Apple prepayment structure and should be ignored.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the shape of the payoff: capped ~9% upside to a defined ceiling against a 30%-plus drawdown to standalone value if regulators block the deal, plus a ~$0.85/share milestone-adjustment risk. That is adequate compensation for owners already positioned to close, but it is not the kind of asymmetric setup that warrants fresh capital, particularly given the long regulatory path for a transaction that explicitly aims to build a Starlink competitor on contested spectrum. Existing holders should hold to close and monitor the regulatory milestones; new capital should wait for either a meaningful de-risking event (which would tighten the spread to a near-riskless return) or evidence of a break (which would create a far more attractive entry at standalone value).
