**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ingevity is roughly eighteen months into a radical portfolio simplification driven by activist pressure. The company has divested its industrial specialties/CTO refinery business (closed January 2026, $93.1M, $55.6M gain), sold the road markings line to PPG for $65M (closed April 2026), and is actively marketing its Advanced Polymer Technologies (APT) segment for sale by year-end 2026. What remains — "New Ingevity" — is two franchises: a high-margin, regulation-protected activated-carbon business (Performance Materials, ~53-59% segment EBITDA margin) and Pavement Technologies (Evotherm warm-mix asphalt). The result is a residual profile of ~$370M+ Adjusted EBITDA at 50%+ margins on light capex.

The reported financials are alarming and misleading in equal measure. Three consecutive GAAP net losses (-$5.4M in 2023, -$430.3M in 2024, -$167.1M in 2025) and a stockholders' equity base eroded to $29.7M produce grotesque headline ratios (D/E 54.6x, ROE around -506%, Altman Z 0.7). These are almost entirely non-cash impairments — nearly $490M of goodwill written off (goodwill fell from $527.5M in 2023 to $4.3M in 2025), plus a $109.3M road-markings long-lived asset impairment and the BASF litigation charge. Underneath, Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations has been stable ($353.0M, $376.5M, $373.0M across 2023-2025) and 2025 free cash flow was $273.5M, a 23.4% margin. Actual credit metrics — 2.5x net leverage and 6.2x interest coverage at Q1 2026 — are sound and comfortably inside covenants.

The investment question is whether the market re-rates the cleaner, higher-quality residual business. At $73.26, the stock trades at ~14-15x the reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS range of $4.70-5.20, roughly in line with specialty-chemical peers (median P/E 21.3 on a GAAP basis, but that comparison is distorted). The stock has risen ~60% over the trailing twelve months and ~25% YTD 2026 as the simplification narrative gained traction. We rate the shares HOLD (growth_hold). The core franchise quality and FCF generation justify ownership, but at the current price the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, with the APT sale valuation, secular EV exposure, thin equity cushion, and persistent management churn capping conviction.

## Company Overview

Ingevity was spun out of WestRock in 2016 and is headquartered in North Charleston, SC. Historically a three-segment specialty chemicals company, it is collapsing into two core franchises:

- **Performance Materials — $606.9M FY2025 revenue (52% of continuing-ops sales), the crown jewel.** Hardwood-based activated carbon for automotive gasoline vapor emission control (evaporative emissions canisters). Segment EBITDA margin ~53.8% in 2025, expanding to 59.2% in Q1 2026. Demand is mandated by emissions regulation, and OEMs treat Ingevity as the "low-risk choice" to avoid recall exposure. Top 10 customers (PHINIA, Kayser, MAHLE, Korea Fuel-Tech) are ~90% of segment sales. North America (~50% of segment) is the most profitable region; Asia-Pacific is ~40%, roughly half China. Competitors: Kuraray, Norit, Chinese producers.

- **Performance Chemicals — $400.5M FY2025.** Pavement Technologies (Evotherm warm-mix asphalt, ~$302.6M, the keeper) plus Road Markings (~$97.9M, sold to PPG for $65M in April 2026). Pavement competitors: Nouryon, Arkema, Zydex.

- **Advanced Polymer Technologies (APT) — $160.2M FY2025.** Caprolactone-based polymers from a single UK plant (Warrington). Being actively marketed for sale; process launched January 2026, targeted to close by end of 2026. APT revenue fell 15% in 2025 on Chinese caprolactone competition and weak global industrial demand — the trigger for the full $183.8M goodwill write-off. Competitors: Daicel, Hunan Juren, BASF.

The strategic direction is to shrink to the two highest-quality, most-defensible franchises and eliminate the weakest, most China/tariff-exposed assets. The residual is an attractive materials profile; the open question is whether it re-rates as a cleaner story or gets penalized for concentration.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue (continuing ops):** $1,215.5M (2023) → $1,200.1M (2024) → $1,167.6M (2025). The modest decline is concentrated in APT; Performance Materials held roughly flat despite auto-industry disruption, and Pavement Technologies is cyclical/seasonal.

**GAAP losses are impairment-driven, not operational.** The 2024 loss included a $306.6M Performance Chemicals goodwill impairment and a $308.9M discontinued-ops loss. The 2025 loss included the APT goodwill write-off, the road-markings impairment, and the BASF litigation drag. With goodwill almost entirely eliminated, further goodwill-driven impairments are now a much smaller residual risk (though APT could still see a loss-on-sale against carrying value).

**The underlying business is healthy.** Total 2025 Adjusted EBITDA was $397.5M. FCF was driven by operating cash flow surging to $331.2M (from $128.6M in 2024, when the $100M CTO contract-termination payment and CTO resale outflows weighed). Capex is light (~$40-60M/yr, mostly maintenance), supporting high conversion. Q1 2026 confirmed the trend: net sales +4% to $258.0M, continuing-ops Adjusted EBITDA flat at $91.5M, and GAAP net income of $59.8M boosted by the industrial-specialties gain.

**The balance sheet is the real caveat, though not for the reasons the headline ratios suggest.** Stockholders' equity fell from $631.4M (2023) to $29.7M (2025), recovering slightly to $38.9M at Q1 2026 — from accumulated impairments, not cash burn. Total debt is ~$1.2B ($550M 3.88% Senior Notes due 2028, $518M revolver, ~$98.5M finance leases, ~$39M A/R securitization). The Altman Z of 0.7 and Piotroski of 3 are false distress signals; they key off the impaired equity base and GAAP losses. The relevant credit metrics comfortably clear covenants (max 4.0x leverage, min 3.0x coverage). Current ratio 5.1x.

**Capital allocation is a high-conviction internal bet.** Management paid down the revolver (~$192M net in 2025) while simultaneously buying back stock ($56.3M in 2025, $52.3M in Q1 2026 alone, $245.6M remaining on authorization). No dividend. This aggressive repurchasing runs alongside real leverage and the $113.2M BASF payment made April 2026 — bullish if the turnaround delivers, but it leaves little cushion for error. The March 2026 revolver amendment extended maturity to March 2031 but cut the commitment from $1B to $750M; available capacity fell to $223M at Q1 2026 (from $474M) as cash went to buybacks and working capital.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is more about mix and margin than top-line acceleration. The "New Ingevity" thesis rests on subtracting low-margin, low-growth assets (APT, road markings) so the residual is dominated by 50%+ margin franchises. Reaffirmed 2026 guidance is net sales $1.05-1.15B, Adjusted EBITDA $370-395M, and Adjusted EPS $4.70-5.20 — flat-to-modestly-higher EBITDA on a smaller revenue base, i.e., margin-led.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than uniformly positive. Current-quarter EPS has been cut (-4.1% over 30 days, -11.0% over 90 days), reflecting the road-markings utilization drag and weak PC seasonality. But next-quarter (+3.7% 30d), current-year (+1.6% 30d, +1.9% 90d), and next-year (+3.2% 30d) revisions are rising. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $5.01, next-year $5.94. This is a steady, low-single-digit upward drift with near-term choppiness, not the +30%-in-90-days move that flags a strong buy signal. The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in the numbers — three beats in four quarters (Q1 2026 +45%, Q2 2025 +17.2%, Q3 2025 +7.2%) with one miss (Q4 2025 -10.2%).

The durable growth lever is the hybrid resurgence: hybrids retain evaporative-emissions canisters, and the consumer shift back toward hybrids from pure BEVs directly supports Performance Materials volume. Management is also exploring energy-storage/hard-carbon applications for its activated-carbon platform, but those are unproven and should not be underwritten. Pavement Technologies benefits from a sustainability/recycling tailwind and infrastructure spend but is seasonal (70-75% of PC revenue lands April-September) and government-dependent.

## Valuation Assessment

At $73.26 the stock trades at ~14.6x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($4.95) and ~14.6x consensus current-year $5.01, falling to ~12.3x next-year consensus of $5.94. On EV/EBITDA, roughly $2.5B equity plus ~$1.2B net-of-cash debt against ~$385M 2026 Adjusted EBITDA is approximately 9-9.5x — reasonable for a business with 50%+ core margins and ~23% FCF margins, though appropriately carrying a discount for concentration and secular risk.

Peer comparison is genuinely difficult here because the reported P/E is negative (trailing EPS -$4.15 is GAAP, impairment-distorted). The peer group (KWR 581.6x, MTX 14.9x, FUL 22.0x, IOSP 18.8x, AVNT 21.3x; median 21.3x) is on GAAP earnings for the comparables. Against those normalized multiples, NGVT at ~14-15x adjusted EPS looks modestly cheap, which is defensible given the leverage and EV overhang — the discount is warranted, not a clear mispricing.

Applying the calibration lesson that "cheap" screens have been reliably anti-predictive, we are cautious about rewarding the modest discount as an edge. The valuation support here is real but not a slam-dunk: analyst targets (low $85, mean $89, median $90, high $92) sit 16-26% above spot, and sentiment is improving (the sole hold rating was upgraded). On a stock with reasonable multiples, targets can lag, but NGVT's growth is not accelerating fast enough to treat those targets as firm floors. We assign a valuation score of 6.0 — a modest tilt toward undervaluation, contingent on the APT sale clearing without a large loss and the residual business proving its re-rating case.

The clearest path to upside is a re-rating of the simplified business as a clean ~$370M+ EBITDA, 50%+ margin, light-capex compounder. The clearest cap is that the market may keep discounting for the single-product regulatory dependence and the EV terminal-value question.

## Competitive Landscape

Performance Materials holds a near-oligopolistic, regulation-protected position. Auto OEMs cannot risk emissions-recall exposure, which makes Ingevity's decades-long track record the default choice; competitors exist but face high switching-cost and qualification barriers. The moat is durable in the medium term. Two structural threats define the trajectory: (a) BEV adoption erasing the addressable market long-term (a pure-EV drivetrain needs no evaporative canister), and (b) sealed-tank fuel-system designs that reduce honeycomb content. The near-term hybrid shift is favorable and partially offsets the BEV risk.

Pavement Technologies (Evotherm) rides a sustainability/recycling tailwind but is seasonal and cyclical. APT has been the persistent underperformer — Chinese caprolactone competition (particularly in paint protective film) plus weak industrial/automotive/footwear demand drove its total goodwill write-off. Selling it removes a share-losing, margin-diluting asset.

The competitive-position score was cut from 7.0 to 6.0 in the prior report, and we hold it near there (5.8). The trajectory is two-sided: the core PM franchise is stable-to-improving on margin with a favorable hybrid mix, but the long-term secular question and the concentration that follows the divestitures argue against a high score. This is a strong niche position facing a slow-moving structural headwind, not a widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Secular EV/ICE risk to the crown jewel.** Activated-carbon canister demand is tied to gasoline ICE and hybrid production. A structural shift to BEV eliminates the product entirely. The current hybrid resurgence is a mitigant, and energy-storage applications are exploratory optionality, but this is the dominant long-term thesis question.

2. **Concentration in a simplified portfolio.** After the divestitures, well over half of EBITDA rests on one regulatory-driven auto-emissions product with top-10 customers at ~90% of PM sales. Loss of a major customer or an emissions-standard rollback would be severe.

3. **APT sale valuation.** Management has explicitly warned it may book a loss on sale. Since APT goodwill is already fully impaired, the risk is a further write-down against the already-thin equity base. A cheap or failed sale is a setback.

4. **Thin equity / leverage optics.** Covenant-compliant, but near-zero book equity gives little cushion. Real debt of ~$1.2B against a smaller post-divestiture EBITDA base, in a higher-for-longer rate environment, is a genuine constraint — the $550M notes mature 2028 and the revolver has a springing acceleration feature if >$250M of those notes remain 91 days pre-maturity.

5. **Tariffs/China.** The 2025 APT impairment trigger; retaliatory Chinese tariffs threaten PM and PC margins on US-exported product.

6. **Management churn and governance flags.** A striking cadence of Item 5.02 leadership-change filings (roughly quarterly from late 2024 through mid-2026), a new "leadership transitions" cautionary risk factor in the Q1 10-Q, and a $24.5M "compensation actually paid" figure for the CEO in a GAAP-loss year. The activist (Vision One) presence has driven value-unlocking actions but the churn is a yellow flag.

7. **Seasonality/weather** in Pavement Technologies and government infrastructure dependence.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 47.7% versus 41.7% one-year historical vol (rich by ~6 points), with a backwardated term structure (47.7% 1-month vs 34.1% 6-month) signaling the market expects imminent event stress — most likely the Q2 print and APT sale progress. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.8%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.8%), not signaling directional fear. Short interest is modest at 4.8% of float (3.9 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short. The market is bracing for a catalyst rather than betting against the name, consistent with a HOLD stance, and argues for patience on entry timing into the vol crush after the next print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$90-95):** The APT sale closes cleanly by year-end 2026 without a material loss, leaving a two-franchise business generating ~$270M+ FCF on light capex. The market re-rates from ~9x to ~10-11x EV/EBITDA, and stated stranded-cost eliminations (~$15M targeted by end-2027, against $19-21M of stranded/indirect costs) improve the run-rate. Continued aggressive buybacks at a shrunken share count amplify per-share EBITDA. Hybrid demand supports Performance Materials volumes. Analyst targets become achievable, with upside if the re-rating overshoots. This roughly matches the next-year EPS at ~15-16x.

**Bear Case (~$50-55):** The APT sale closes below carrying value, forcing another write-down against near-zero equity and reigniting balance-sheet concerns. EV adoption re-accelerates and sealed-tank fuel systems compress honeycomb content, pressuring the crown-jewel franchise's terminal value. Pavement/road-markings utilization stays weak (PC segment EBITDA margin collapsed to 1.0% in Q1 2026 from 9.8%), a top customer is lost, or Chinese tariffs bite PM/PC margins. The 2028 notes refinancing looms with reduced revolver capacity. The stock de-rates back toward the low-to-mid $50s, roughly where it traded in late 2025.

**Base Case (~$78-82):** The transformation proceeds broadly as guided, APT exits without major surprise, and the residual business delivers the ~$4.95 EPS midpoint. Modest re-rating plus buyback accretion carries the stock into the high-$70s to low-$80s over 12 months, in line with the low end of analyst targets. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios and reflects a balanced, not asymmetric, setup.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Timeframe: 12 months, anchored to the APT sale close (targeted end-2026) and the sequence of 2026 quarterly prints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- APT sale closes at or above carrying value (no material loss-on-sale), confirming the residual business's clean profile and removing the balance-sheet overhang.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive estimate-revision velocity on current-year and next-year EPS, combined with PC segment margin recovery above high-single-digits.
- A pullback toward the mid-$60s that widens the discount to the residual EBITDA power without any fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- APT sale falls through or closes at a large loss, forcing another impairment against the thin equity base.
- Performance Materials volume or margin rolls over (segment EBITDA margin falling below ~50%), or a top-10 customer loss / adverse emissions-standard change surfaces.
- Net leverage rising above ~3.0x, or a stalled 2028 notes refinancing path.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings print and any 8-K disclosure of the APT sale terms. The near-term options backwardation suggests the market expects a catalyst within weeks.

## Conclusion

Ingevity is a business whose reported financials materially understate its quality. Behind three years of impairment-driven GAAP losses and a near-zero book equity base sits a stable ~$370-395M Adjusted EBITDA, ~$270M FCF, and a Performance Materials franchise protected by auto-emissions regulation running at 53-59% segment margins with light capex. The activist-driven simplification into "New Ingevity" is a coherent value-unlocking strategy, and the residual profile is attractive.

The stock, however, has already appreciated as the market began to reward the narrative, and at ~14-15x reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS it is fairly valued rather than obviously cheap. The remaining upside — a re-rating of the cleaner business plus buyback accretion, with analyst targets 16-26% above spot — is real but balanced against concrete risks: the still-open APT sale valuation, the secular EV question hanging over the crown jewel, a thin equity cushion against ~$1.2B of debt with a 2028 maturity, and a governance profile marked by heavy leadership turnover and an outsized CEO pay figure in a loss year. We rate NGVT **HOLD** and would look to add on a pullback toward the mid-$60s or on confirmation that APT exits cleanly.
