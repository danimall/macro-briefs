## Executive Summary

Virtus is a multi-boutique asset manager whose earnings power is concentrated in one investment style at one affiliate, and that style is in the middle of a severe performance and flow drawdown. AUM fell from $170.7B at June 30, 2025 to $152.2B at June 30, 2026. Net outflows were $(18.9)B in FY2025 — 10.8% of beginning assets — and $(14.1)B in the first half of 2026 alone, roughly 17.6% annualized. The proximate cause is disclosed in the FY2025 10-K: 6% of equity AUM beat its benchmark over one year, 21% over three years, 20% over five. Kayne Anderson Rudnick and Sustainable Growth Advisers together held roughly 92% of equity AUM at year-end, so there is no offsetting affiliate of comparable scale to absorb the damage.

The reported income statement understates the erosion. Reconstructing management fees from the company's own average-AUM and fee-rate tables, open-end funds, retail separate accounts and institutional accounts collectively lost about $21.3M of quarterly fee revenue year over year, offset by roughly $14.1M from closed-end and tender-offer funds — nearly all of that from the Keystone private-credit acquisition plus a step-up in performance fees. Strip the acquisition and the legacy book's management fees are running down 12-13% year over year, not the 4% the headline shows. Adjusted operating margin compressed 520bp to 26.1% as the AUM-linked revenue base fell against a largely fixed people cost structure, and adjusted employment expense ran at 53.5% of revenue excluding a discrete item, at or above the top of the 51-53% guided range.

What has changed since the last two negative assessments of this name is the price, not the fundamentals. The stock has risen from roughly $126 in mid-March to $166.16, while current-year consensus was cut 8.2% over ninety days and next-year 7.0%, and the company missed adjusted EPS in each of the last four quarters. The shares now trade above the analyst mean target of $148 and median of $145, with the sell-side split 1 strong buy / 1 hold / 1 sell / 1 strong sell. On a realistic FY2026 adjusted EPS near $21-22 the multiple is 7.6-7.9x, which looks cheap in isolation and reflects a business the market expects to keep shrinking. A 5.75% dividend covered 2.3x on adjusted earnings and a recourse balance sheet at 0.9x net debt/EBITDA prevent this from being a solvency story, but they do not make the expected return positive. Probability-weighted, we see roughly -9% total return over twelve months. **AVOID.**

## Company Overview

Virtus owns majority or minority economic interests in autonomous investment boutiques and supplies them with distribution, product structuring, fund administration and operational infrastructure. It does not manage money centrally; it monetizes distribution and platform scale.

At June 30, 2026 AUM of $152.2B split into open-end funds $52.1B, institutional $50.4B, retail separate accounts $36.2B (including model portfolios delivered to managed-account sponsors), and closed-end/tender-offer funds $13.5B. By asset class the mix has shifted sharply: equity fell from $96.2B (56.4% of AUM) a year ago to $68.8B (45.2%), while alternatives rose from $14.5B to $19.7B and fixed income was roughly flat at $40.5B. That mix shift is largely a function of equity losses rather than alternatives wins.

The genuine competitive assets are a broad distribution footprint across retail intermediary, managed-account model delivery, closed-end fund, ETF and institutional channels (Q2 total sales of $6.1B, up 9% year over year) and a demonstrated capability in structuring and distributing closed-end and tender-offer vehicles, which is now the delivery mechanism for private credit into the wealth channel. What is absent is any evidence of investment-performance advantage.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and flows.** FY2025 revenue was $852.9M, down 6.0%, on ending AUM of $159.5B, down 8.9%, with net outflows of $(18.9)B against $(10.4)B in FY2024. H1 2026 revenue was $400.9M, down 6% from $428.5M. Q2 2026 GAAP revenue was $201.4M (-4%); adjusted revenue $183.6M (-4%). Average AUM fell 8.2% year over year, so revenue fell less than assets, reflecting the fee-rate mix effect discussed below.

Q2 net outflows of $(5.6)B improved from $(8.4)B in Q1, institutional flows improved to $(0.7)B from $(3.2)B on a single large global listed real estate mandate, ETF flows were +$0.3B and wealth management was positive. Those are the strongest pieces of counter-evidence in the file, and they matter. But the Q2 outflow still equals 3.8% of beginning AUM in a single quarter.

**Retail separate accounts are the acute wound.** AUM fell from $47.4B to $36.2B in twelve months, -23.7%. H1 gross outflows of $9,605M against $2,609M of inflows produced net $(7.0)B versus $(1.5)B in H1 2025, a 4.8x worsening. Annualizing H1 gross outflows against roughly $40B average AUM implies a ~48% gross redemption rate. That is platform-level removal behavior — model sponsors striking a strategy from an approved list — which happens in blocks and takes multiple years of restored relative performance to reverse.

**Margins.** Adjusted operating margin: 31.3% (Q2'25) → 24.0% (Q1'26) → 26.1% (Q2'26). The 520bp year-over-year compression on a 4% adjusted revenue decline demonstrates how violently operating leverage runs in reverse when revenue is AUM-linked and cost is people. Restructuring expense of $3.7M in H1 indicates headcount action is underway; cutting distribution or investment staff at a firm already losing assets has its own cost.

**Fee rate is mix, not pricing power.** Firmwide fee rate rose from 41.3bp to 43.1bp year over year, which reads like pricing strength. The underlying reality: the open-end fund rate fell from 46.7bp to 42.1bp (-9.8%), reflecting ETF growth and intermediary fee pressure in the core mutual fund book, while the closed-end/tender-offer rate jumped from 58.6bp to 86.8bp on Keystone plus performance fees. Firmwide performance fees were 1.2bp in Q2 versus 0.2bp a year earlier — approximately $4.6M in the quarter, of which a meaningful share accrues to affiliate minority holders. Roughly 2.5% of adjusted revenue in Q2 was lumpy and not a run rate. The Q2 fee rate of 43.1bp landed at the bottom of the 43-45bp guided range.

**GAAP is not usable for a single quarter.** GAAP diluted EPS rose 9% to $6.68 in Q2 while adjusted EPS fell 20% to $5.54 and GAAP operating income fell 40% to $27.3M. The company reconciles the gap: $2.65 of realized/unrealized investment gains, $0.50 of minority-interest fair-value adjustments and $0.49 of contingent-consideration fair-value adjustments — about $3.64 per share of non-operating marks on consolidated investment products and seed capital. Q1 was the mirror image at $1.05 GAAP EPS. The GAAP effective tax rate swung from 54% in Q1 to 16% in Q2 on that reversal. Any multiple built on one quarter of GAAP EPS is measuring the seed portfolio. The fair-value gains on contingent consideration and minority interests arise partly because expected affiliate payouts fell — an unfavorable operating signal recorded as an earnings gain. That linkage is an inference from the accounting mechanics rather than a disclosed explanation.

**Cash generation and balance sheet.** FY2025 GAAP operating cash flow was negative $67.2M, which is a consolidation artifact — purchases of investments inside consolidated CLOs and seed vehicles run through operating activities. The parent clearly generates cash: in Q2 the company paid $16.3M of dividends, repurchased $10.0M of stock, repaid $20M of revolver borrowings and still grew cash from $136.6M to $176.2M. For the same reason, distress screens built for industrial balance sheets (an Altman Z near 0.8, D/E near 3.5x) are measuring non-recourse CLO liabilities and should be disregarded here.

Leverage nonetheless increased materially. Gross debt rose 82% year over year to $427.0M; net debt of $250.8M equals 0.9x EBITDA on the credit-agreement definition, implying credit-agreement EBITDA near $279M. That covenant metric excludes contingent consideration of $122.7M (up 228%) and the manager noncontrolling-interest liability of $152.5M (up roughly eightfold). Adding those, debt-and-debt-like claims total $702.2M gross, $526.0M net of cash, or about 1.9x the implied EBITDA. Including $127.3M of redeemable manager NCI takes gross claims to $829.5M, roughly 58% of market capitalization. The manager NCI claims are employee ownership stakes in the affiliates repurchasable at formula multiples, so they contract if affiliate earnings fall — they are not fixed-principal debt. The contingent consideration is not so flexible: $65M is due to the Keystone sellers at the first anniversary and $30M at the second, on a fixed schedule. Working capital fell 50% to $71.6M. Covenant thresholds and the term-loan maturity schedule were not obtainable and headroom is therefore unquantified.

**Capital returns and coverage.** The quarterly dividend was raised 7% to $2.40, or $9.60 annualized — a 5.75% yield at $167.01, covered roughly 2.3x on annualized H1 adjusted net income attributable of about $148M and 1.6x on GAAP. H1 repurchases of $20.5M at an average $139.28 retired about 2.2% of shares, with 662,388 shares (~9.9%) remaining authorized. Buying stock at $139 was sensible capital allocation. The tension is 2027: dividend ~$65M plus buybacks at the current pace ~$41M plus the $65M Keystone deferred payment exceeds the current adjusted earnings run rate, implying buyback moderation, revolver draw or cash drawdown.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here, only a decay-rate question. Organic decay accelerated from 10.8% in FY2025 to ~17.6% annualized in H1 2026. Consensus is moving in one direction: current quarter $6.15 (-5.3% in 30 days, -6.9% in 90), next quarter $6.24 (-6.8%/-8.7%), current year $23.31 (-5.6%/-8.2%), next year $24.56 (-4.0%/-7.0%). The company has missed adjusted EPS four consecutive quarters — by 3.2%, 1.1%, 7.0% and 10.8% — with the magnitude of the miss widening. Estimate revisions are accelerating downward while the misses grow, which is the opposite of the configuration that supports forward-estimate confidence.

The strategic response — Keystone National Group (private credit, ~56% interest) and a 35% stake in Crescent Cove — is coherent and leverages the closed-end/tender-offer distribution capability. It cannot arithmetically offset the legacy decay in any relevant horizon. Keystone brought approximately $2.3B of AUM against a $152B base. Even quadrupling to ~$9B would leave it at ~6% of firmwide assets. In its first full quarter Keystone contributed ~$13.8M of revenue and a net loss of ~$(2.3)M after ~$7.3M of amortization. Total amortization expense is now $20.1M per quarter, up 55%, roughly $80M annualized, and interest expense rose 56% to $7.1M.

The only credible path to a positive growth surprise is a style-cycle reversal: quality-growth managers with small- and mid-cap tilts have systematically lagged benchmarks dominated by mega-cap concentration, and that is cyclical. But with 20-21% of equity AUM beating benchmark on three- and five-year records, the trailing numbers that intermediary gatekeepers screen on would take years to repair, and flow inflection would lag performance inflection substantially.

## Valuation Assessment

At $166.16 against FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS of $19.97, the trailing multiple is 8.3x (the 9.55x trailing and forward figures in the data feed are internally identical and inconsistent with the current price; we use our own computation). On current-year consensus of $23.31 the forward multiple is 7.1x; on next-year $24.56, 6.8x. Both estimates have a demonstrated upward bias — four straight misses, with the last two by 7.0% and 10.8%.

A more defensible earnings base: H1 adjusted EPS of $10.92 ($5.38 + $5.54), with Q2 flattered by ~$4.6M of performance fees and penalized by a $0.43 discrete stock-comp acceleration. Average AUM is still falling sequentially, so H2 should run below H1 absent market appreciation. FY2026 adjusted EPS of $21-22 is a reasonable center, putting the stock at 7.6-7.9x. Carry the decay forward — another 8-10% average AUM decline with continued reverse operating leverage — and FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $18.50-19.50.

Peer context requires care. The stated peer median P/E of 31.7x is not a usable anchor: WisdomTree (39.4x), Acadian (31.7x) and Cohen & Steers (25.7x) are ETF/growth-mix businesses and StepStone's earnings are negative. The one true traditional-active comparable is Artisan Partners at 10.2x with a 7.4% yield. VRTS at 7.1x forward trades at a discount to APAM, and that discount is warranted: VRTS's organic decay accelerated to ~17.6% annualized with 6% of equity AUM beating benchmark, and it carries an 82% year-over-year debt increase plus $275M of contingent and manager-ownership claims outside the covenant metric.

Two market-based checks argue the current price already embeds optimism. First, analyst mean and median targets of $148 and $145 sit 10.9% and 12.7% *below* spot; the high is $172, only 3.5% above. When estimates are being cut, consensus targets tend to be stale on the high side, so this is a ceiling reading rather than a floor. Second, management repurchased stock at an average of $139.28 in H1 and $142.66 in Q2, and granted RSUs at a $126.28 weighted-average grant-date fair value in February. The company's own transaction prices cluster 14-24% below the current quote.

The cheapness is real but reflects the decay rate. A 7x multiple on an earnings stream contracting 10%+ annually with no visible flow catalyst is a discount rate, not a margin of safety. Valuation score: 4.0, down from 6.0 previously — the multiple has expanded roughly 32% since March on an 8% cut to estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

Virtus sits on the wrong side of the active-equity migration and the crowded side of the private-credit pivot.

The performance data establish that this is company-specific rather than merely sector-coincident. Index migration alone does not produce a 48% annualized gross redemption rate in retail separate accounts; management's own attribution of outflows to "quality-oriented equity strategies," repeated three times in the Q2 release, is a performance attribution. The proxy's own peer-group TSR comparison — $100 invested at year-end 2020 worth $88.96 at year-end 2025 versus $136.24 for the selected peer group — confirms 47 percentage points of cumulative relative underperformance within a sector that delivered positive returns.

Structurally, KAR at 36.3% of firmwide AUM and KAR plus SGA at ~92% of equity AUM means one style determines the earnings trajectory. There is no second engine.

On private credit, Virtus's closed-end and tender-offer structuring capability is a genuine differentiated asset, which is why the closed-end fee rate reached 86.8bp. The problem is scale and price paid. Total Keystone consideration of ~$308.2M for ~56% implies ~$550M for 100%, closely matching the $243.7M of goodwill plus $307.0M of intangibles recognized. Against ~$2.3B of AUM that is ~24% of AUM and roughly 10x annualized revenue at the Q2 run rate, versus low-single-digit percentages of AUM for traditional long-only transactions. The largest alternative platforms manage hundreds of billions, hold direct-origination advantages and are building their own wealth-channel distribution. One unresolved item: Keystone's ~$13.8M of quarterly revenue on ~$2.3B of AUM implies an anomalous ~240bp yield, plausibly explained by levered-vehicle economics, servicing fees, fees on committed-undrawn capital or the separately disclosed $1.7B of "other fee-earning assets not included in AUM." It is not reconciled and should not be modeled as a fee rate.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-affiliate, single-style concentration.** KAR was 36.3% of AUM; KAR plus SGA ~92% of equity AUM. This governs everything.

**Self-reinforcing flow dynamics.** 6%/21%/20% of equity AUM beating benchmark on one/three/five years (as of 12/31/2025; not updated for six months of style movement). Model-platform removals are block decisions with multi-year re-entry lags.

**Reverse operating leverage.** 520bp of adjusted margin compression on a 4% revenue decline; comp ratio above guidance. Continued sequential AUM decline means continued margin compression absent cuts.

**Impairment and deferred-payment risk.** $243.7M of goodwill and $307.0M of intangibles from Keystone sit against a $1.11B market cap, and Keystone posted a $(2.3)M net loss in its first full quarter. If tender-offer fundraising disappoints, impairment risk is live; the $75M earnout not being earned is a partial economic offset. The $65M and $30M deferred payments are fixed against AUM-dependent earnings.

**Leverage opacity.** 0.9x covenant leverage excludes ~$275M of contingent and manager-NCI claims (~1.9x on the broader measure). Covenant thresholds and maturity schedule unverified.

**Reporting comparability.** An April 17, 2026 8-K changed the non-GAAP definition to include tax benefits realized on goodwill and intangible amortization, with prior periods recast — so adjusted EPS now excludes amortization expense *and* includes the tax benefit that same amortization generates. Recast Q2 2025 adjusted EPS is $6.91 against a $6.25 figure cited by third-party sources, implying roughly $0.66 of uplift, though we could not verify the original filing. This also means the reported ~9% Q2 miss versus a $6.09 consensus may not be a like-for-like comparison if the consensus was not recast. The change was disclosed and prior periods recast, which is appropriate handling; the effect on comparability is nonetheless real and arrived alongside a large amortization-generating acquisition.

**CIP volatility.** A 54%→16% GAAP tax rate swing in two quarters guarantees recurring headline misreads in both directions.

**Positioning risk to bears.** Short interest is 10.2% of float at 3.1 days to cover — enough to produce a squeeze on any flow or performance surprise, and a reason to avoid rather than fight the tape.

**Insider signal.** A director purchased ~4,050 shares at ~$129.35 (~$524K) on March 12, near the low — the cleanest positive in the record. Against that, the CFO sold ~14,975 shares (~19% of holdings) on August 3, four business days after the Q2 miss, and the head of distribution sold 5,000 shares (~32%) on June 16 at ~$145. Neither Form 4 set distinguishes 10b5-1 plan executions from discretionary sales, both retain meaningful positions, and post-vest diversification is routine at asset managers. We do not infer motive. The relevant observation is that 2026 net insider activity by the two operating executives closest to the earnings trajectory and to the channel losing assets is selling, and it co-occurs with falling estimates rather than rising ones, which is the configuration in which insider sales carry more, not less, information.

No options data was available for this review, so no options-market read is offered.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($200, +20.4%).** Quality and small/mid-cap equity leadership reverses; KAR and SGA relative performance repairs; the sequential flow improvement ($(8.4)B → $(5.6)B) extends and net flows approach breakeven during 2027. Fee-rate mix continues upward as Keystone raises toward $5B+ in tender-offer vehicles at 87bp with performance fees, lifting the blended rate through 45bp. Adjusted EPS holds $24, the ~10% buyback authorization is executed, and the multiple expands to 8.3x on a re-rating toward APAM's ~10x. The 5.75% dividend compounds the return.

**Base case ($140, -15.7%).** Flows remain net negative at $3-5B per quarter through 2027 with SMA attrition continuing at a decelerating but still severe pace. Average AUM declines another 8-10%; adjusted margin holds near 25-26% only through restructuring; FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $19. Applying 7.4x — a warranted discount to APAM for faster decay and higher balance-sheet claims — gives roughly $140, which brackets the analyst median of $145 and the company's own H1 repurchase average of $139.28. Total return including dividend: roughly -10%.

**Bear case ($105, -36.8%).** Decay persists above 15% annualized as additional platforms remove strategies; adjusted EPS falls toward $16; comp-ratio rigidity keeps margins compressing; a goodwill or intangible impairment charge on Keystone forces a GAAP loss quarter; the combination of dividend, buyback and the $65M/$30M deferred payments requires revolver use and the buyback is suspended. At 6.5x on $16, $105 — modestly below the 52-week low of $121.61.

Probability-weighted at 20% bull / 50% base / 30% bear: -14.8% on price, roughly -9% including twelve months of dividends. That is a negative expected return with a wide left tail, and the shares trade above every consensus target except the high.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Q3 2026 earnings (late October) carries fifth-consecutive-miss risk: consensus for the current quarter is $6.15 against a Q2 actual of $5.54 that itself contained lumpy performance fees, and average AUM is entering the quarter below the Q2 average. Secondary catalysts: annual goodwill/intangible impairment testing on Keystone (Q4 2026 or Q1 2027), and the $65M first-anniversary deferred payment (~Q2 2027) against a dividend-plus-buyback commitment that already consumes the adjusted earnings run rate.

**Timing window.** Two to nine months. The earnings catalyst is dated and near; the balance-sheet catalyst is farther out.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 10.2% of float, 443,548 shares, 3.1 days to cover. Not crowded, but not uncrowded either, and the float is small (~6.7M shares) with average daily volume of only ~100,600 shares. A single positive flow datapoint or a style-cycle rotation into quality/SMID could move this name 10% in a session, as it did repeatedly in July.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options data was available for this review, so there is no confirming or contradicting positioning read. The short seller must fund a $9.60 annual dividend — 5.75% of carry at the current price — which is a material drag on any multi-quarter hold.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Target the base case at $140 (-15.7%) with a stop above the July high near $173 (+4.1% from spot); a realistic stop allowing for the name's volatility sits near $185 (+11.3%). Gross reward/risk against the $185 stop is roughly 1.4:1, falling below 1:1 net of dividend carry over a twelve-month horizon. Only the bear case at $105 produces a 3:1 gross ratio, and that requires an impairment or dividend event that is not yet in evidence.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is sound and the catalyst is dated, but 5.75% of dividend carry against a base-case decline of 15.7%, on a $1.1B market cap with ~100,000 shares of daily volume and 10.2% short interest, does not clear a 2:1 asymmetry bar. This is a position to not own, not one to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (any two would move this to HOLD; all three to ACCUMULATE):**

1. **Flow stabilization.** Quarterly net outflows below $2.0B (roughly 1.3% of beginning AUM) for two consecutive quarters, with retail separate account gross outflows falling below $3.0B per quarter from the ~$4.8B H1 pace. This is the single most important datapoint; it appears in the quarterly earnings release AUM tables.
2. **Performance repair.** The share of equity AUM beating benchmark rising above 40% on a one-year basis and above 35% on three years in the next 10-K (February 2027). Below those thresholds, intermediary gatekeepers do not restore approved-list status.
3. **Price.** $125-130, which is roughly 6.5x a normalized $19-20 adjusted EPS and approximates both the March director purchase at $129.35 and the February RSU grant value of $126.28. At that level the ~7.4-7.7% dividend yield plus a covered payout begins to compensate for the decay rate.

**Additional evidence that would upgrade the view:** adjusted operating margin expanding sequentially for two quarters on flat-to-down revenue (proving the cost base can be reset); Keystone AUM disclosed above $5B with fee-earning assets reconciled to revenue; buyback executed at pace against the remaining 662,388-share authorization without revolver draw.

**Evidence that would deepen the negative view:** a goodwill or intangible impairment charge; adjusted employment expense above 55% of revenue for two consecutive quarters; any dividend reduction or buyback suspension; net outflows exceeding $7B in a single quarter; disclosure of covenant amendment or waiver.

**Timeline.** The headwinds are structural on a 12-24 month view. Platform removals reverse on multi-year lags, so even a Q4 2026 style-cycle reversal in quality/SMID equity would not show in flows before late 2027. Next natural reassessment: Q3 2026 earnings, late October 2026.

## Conclusion

The trajectory of this assessment has been consistently negative — 4.9 in March, 4.4 in May, 3.6 now — and the current score decline is driven by two specific developments rather than sentiment. Estimates were cut 8.2% for the current year and 7.0% for next year over ninety days, and the fourth consecutive miss came in at 10.8%, the widest of the streak. Simultaneously the price rose 32% from the March low. A 32% multiple expansion on falling estimates and accelerating organic decay is a worse setup than existed at $126, and the shares now sit 12% above the analyst mean target with the sell-side split evenly between buy and sell recommendations.

The honest counterweights are these: the parent generates cash and grew its balance while paying dividends, buying stock and repaying revolver debt; the 5.75% dividend is covered 2.3x on adjusted earnings; recourse leverage at 0.9x is modest; the closed-end and tender-offer distribution capability is genuine and is producing 87bp fee rates; Q2 flows improved sequentially; and a style-cycle reversal in quality/SMID equity is a live possibility that would eventually help. A director bought half a million dollars of stock near the low, and the CEO holds ~4.2% of the company.

None of that changes the arithmetic. A core business shedding management fees at 12-13% annually cannot be offset by a $2.3B private-credit franchise bought at ~24% of AUM and funded with an 82% increase in gross debt plus $275M of contingent and manager-ownership claims outside the covenant metric. Six percent of equity AUM beating benchmark is close to a worst-case starting point for an equity-centric distributor, and platform removals do not reverse on the timeframe over which the deferred acquisition payments fall due. At 7.1x a falling estimate with a base case near $140 and a bear case near $105, the expected twelve-month total return is negative and the distribution is left-skewed.

**AVOID.** Not a short, given 5.75% of dividend carry against sub-2:1 asymmetry in a $1.1B name with 10.2% short interest and thin volume. Revisit at $125-130 or on two consecutive quarters of net outflows below $2.0B.
