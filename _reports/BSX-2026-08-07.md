## Executive Summary

Boston Scientific has moved from being the fastest-growing large-cap medtech franchise to a company defending its lead product, and the market has repriced it accordingly: the stock is 55% below its September 2025 high of $109.50, having fallen from $91.62 to $75.50 in a single session on February 4, 2026 and ground down to $42.20 by mid-July before recovering to $49.30. The proximate cause is arithmetic rather than narrative. Organic revenue growth was 15.8% in FY2025 and 16.4% in FY2024; it was 9.2% in Q1 2026 (derived from the 8.1% first-half rate) and 7.0% in Q2 2026, with zero acquisition contribution in either 2026 quarter under the company's own definition. Management named the causes in the Q2 10-Q: "increased competition within our Electrophysiology business unit," "a deceleration of certain WATCHMAN procedures," China volume-based procurement in the stone franchise, and "commercial disruption in our sacral neuromodulation franchise." Within electrophysiology, U.S. sales grew 3.2% (to $606M from $587M) while international grew 23.0%, consistent with competitors having gained U.S. commercial traction in pulsed field ablation, the market Farapulse opened in early 2024.

The offsetting evidence is unusually concrete. Adjusted EPS of $0.86 beat consensus by 2.9%, the fourth consecutive beat. Ex the $83M IEEPA tariff refund, Q2 gross margin was 69.2% versus 67.7% a year earlier, roughly 150bp of durable improvement, and SG&A fell to 33.1% of sales from 33.9%. Management moved from zero buybacks in 2024 and 2025 to a $2.0B accelerated repurchase completed in June at an average $50.10, above today's price, with $3.0B still authorized. On August 3, the day the 10-Q disclosing the deceleration was filed, CEO Michael Mahoney purchased 186,240 shares at $48.33 for $9.0M in an open-market transaction, taking his holding to 1,590,024 shares; directors Habiger and Ludwig each bought in two waves, first near $56 in May and again at $45.48–$48.59 in late July and early August. Cluster insider buying with the CEO writing the largest cheque is the highest-quality signal in this dataset.

We rate the shares HOLD. At $49.30 they trade at 14.3x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.44 against a 25.6x health-care-equipment peer median, and our base case of $57 implies 15.6% upside. Three things keep us from adding: FY2027 consensus has been cut 8.7% over 90 days with 7.6pp of that cut arriving in the last 30 days, so the estimate cycle is still moving against the stock; the shares have already recovered 16.8% off the July low, consuming much of the margin of safety that existed a month ago; and the two largest swing factors, U.S. PFA share and the FTC's disposition of the $14.5B Penumbra acquisition, are unquantifiable from available disclosure. We would upgrade to ACCUMULATE at $44–46 or on evidence that U.S. electrophysiology growth has stabilized above 6%.

## Company Overview

Boston Scientific develops and sells single-use interventional medical devices across two reported segments following a Q4 2025 reorganization that combined legacy Cardiology and Peripheral Interventions into Cardiovascular. Revenue is consumed per procedure rather than sold as capital equipment, which produces recurring economics without contractual backlog. Deferred revenue of $691M at June 30, 2026 relates specifically to the LATITUDE patient-management platform and the LUX-Dx II+ cardiac monitor and should not be read as an order book.

Q2 2026 revenue of $5,442M split $1,818M MedSurg (+5.9%) and $3,624M Cardiovascular (+8.3%). Business-unit detail, with growth rates derived from the disclosed dollar figures: Interventional Cardiology & Vascular Therapies $1,333M (+13.2%), Electrophysiology $916M (+9.0%), Cardiac Rhythm Management $585M (−0.8%), Watchman $507M (+4.3%), Interventional Oncology & Embolization $283M (+12.7%), Endoscopy $793M (+7.6%), Urology $684M (+1.1%), Neuromodulation $341M (+12.7%). The United States contributed $3,426M, or 63.0%; Asia-Pacific grew 11.1% to $878M.

R&D was $2,052M in FY2025 (10.2% of sales, up 27% year over year) and $1,069M in the first half of 2026 (10.0%). The company holds investment-grade ratings at all three agencies.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the 2026 deceleration.** FY2022 through FY2025 revenue ran $12,682M → $14,240M → $16,747M → $20,074M, with organic growth of 16.4% in FY2024 and 15.8% in FY2025. First-half 2026 revenue was $10,646M, +9.5% reported and +8.1% organic; Q2 was +7.5% and +7.0%. FY2025's 15.8% organic figure excluded 3.4pp of acquisition contribution, whereas 2026 has none, so the underlying franchise rate is now visible unassisted. Sequential deceleration from roughly 9.2% organic in Q1 to 7.0% in Q2 means the trend has not yet flattened.

**Margins, adjusted for a one-time item.** Reported Q2 gross margin of 70.7% versus 67.7% overstates the improvement. The company's own bridge attributes +2.9pp to "all other," identifying "the benefit recognized in connection with the recovery of previously incurred tariffs" as a driver, and the non-GAAP reconciliation quantifies an $83M pre-tax IEEPA tariff refund ($0.05 per share) booked in cost of products sold. Removing it: gross profit of $3,848M less $83M on $5,442M of sales gives 69.2%, and operating margin falls from 21.6% to 20.1%. Roughly half the reported gross-margin expansion was the refund; the remainder came from mix and lower inventory charges. The 150bp of genuine expansion plus 80bp of SG&A leverage is real operating progress on a 7% revenue base.

**GAAP versus adjusted.** First-half GAAP EPS rose 53.6% to $1.51 while adjusted EPS rose 10.2% to $1.66. The gap is dominated by a $384M discrete tax benefit recognized in Q1 2026 to reflect a change in the anticipated future rate at which capitalized expenses will be recovered, which drove the reported first-half tax rate to negative 1.0% against a continuing-operations rate of 17.4%. Consensus estimates for BSX are stated on the adjusted basis (the $0.84 Q2 estimate against $0.86 actual confirms this), so all forward multiples in this report use adjusted figures, and GAAP EPS of $1.94 (FY2025) is used only where labeled.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $4,534M against $876M of capex for $3,658M of free cash flow, an 18.2% FCF margin, with OCF/GAAP net income of 1.57x reflecting heavy non-cash amortization. First-half 2026 OCF was $1,822M versus $1,827M, flat despite operating income rising from $1,740M to $2,279M. The drag was working capital and tax timing: inventories consumed $310M (versus $44M), payables and accruals $278M (versus $154M), and deferred/prepaid taxes $334M (versus $49M), the last being the cash-flow mirror of the Q1 non-cash tax benefit. We do not annualize this. FY2025 first-half OCF was only 40% of the full year, so a naive doubling of 2026's first half would imply $2,900M of FCF and a 21% decline that seasonality alone argues against; holding FY2025's split constant would imply roughly $3,650M. Neither is a forecast, and first-half cash flow is not yet decision-useful for the full year.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $45,216M, equity $25,172M, cash $539M, current debt $1,709M, long-term debt $10,915M for total debt of $12,624M and net debt of $12,085M. Debt/equity on a debt-only basis is 0.51x. Company-defined covenant leverage was 2.02x against a 4.00x maximum. The current ratio fell from 1.62x to 1.24x, and cash fell from $1,965M to $539M, because the company spent $2,000M on the ASR, $1,600M on MiRus and $523M on Nalu while drawing $1,689M of commercial paper at a 4.06% average yield with 41-day average maturity. That is deliberate, not distress, but it leaves a thin liquidity cushion ahead of the largest financing in company history.

Goodwill of $18,640M and other intangibles of $6,918M total $25,558M, or 56.5% of assets; book equity is almost entirely intangible, the arithmetic consequence of a long acquisition history. Retained earnings, at $7,818M, only turned positive recently (−$750M in 2022). Standard solvency screens that substitute book equity for market equity produce misleadingly weak readings here; recomputed properly on FY2025 balances with market equity, the Altman Z is approximately 3.22, above the 2.99 safe-zone threshold, consistent with 2.02x covenant leverage and investment-grade ratings.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question reduces to whether 7% organic is a floor or a way station. The evidence points both directions.

Supporting a floor: international electrophysiology grew 23.0% in Q2, indicating unexhausted geographic runway for Farapulse; Interventional Cardiology & Vascular Therapies grew 13.2%, Neuromodulation 12.7% and Interventional Oncology 12.7%; Asia-Pacific grew 11.1% in aggregate despite the China VBP hit to stone management. The sacral neuromodulation issue is characterized as "commercial disruption" rather than competitive loss, which may be self-correcting. The 2026 Restructuring Plan approved July 21 targets roughly $500M of gross annual pre-tax savings for $700–800M of charges, though management states a substantial portion will be reinvested. Penumbra, if cleared, adds inorganic revenue in a thrombectomy adjacency.

Arguing for further deceleration: U.S. electrophysiology at 3.2% and U.S. Watchman at 2.9% are the two franchises that produced the 2024–2025 super-cycle, and both are now growing at roughly GDP. Cardiac Rhythm Management is in absolute decline. Urology grew 1.1%. Consensus has moved with the evidence: FY2026 to $3.30 (−2.2% over 90 days) and FY2027 to $3.44 from $3.76 (−8.7% over 90 days, with −7.6% arriving in the last 30 days). At $3.44, FY2027 implies only 4.2% adjusted EPS growth off FY2026. Current-quarter consensus of $0.81 sits below the $0.86 just delivered and has itself been cut 4.0% in 30 days.

The revision pattern is the single most important forward indicator in this file, and it is negative and accelerating. This is estimate momentum running the wrong way at a compressed multiple, which historically resolves through further multiple compression as often as through re-rating. The company also created its own cannibalization problem: it impaired Cryterion cryoablation intangibles in FY2024 explicitly because Farapulse was displacing cryo "in major markets like the U.S." and wrote down POLARx inventory for the same reason. There is no fallback ablation franchise if PFA share is contested.

Against peers with median P/E of 25.6x (Medtronic 21.5x, Becton Dickinson 25.6x, ResMed 18.6x, Edwards 46.5x, IDEXX 41.0x), BSX at 14.3x forward is the cheapest name in the comparable set. Part of that discount is earned: Medtronic pays a 3.6% dividend and BSX pays none, BSX is levering up materially for Penumbra while peers are not, and BSX's growth trajectory is decelerating while ResMed's and Medtronic's are stable. But BSX still grows adjusted EPS at roughly 10% (first-half 2026) against Medtronic's mid-single digits, and a 44% discount to the peer median for that profile is difficult to justify on fundamentals alone.

Two independent, cash-at-risk votes corroborate the mispricing case. The company repurchased 39,922,436 shares at an average $50.10 between May 18 and June 12 (above today's price) with $3.0B of the $5.0B authorization remaining. The CEO committed $9.0M of personal cash at $48.33 on the day the deceleration was formally disclosed, with three directors buying alongside him at successively lower prices. Neither is proof of value, and the May director purchases near $56 were followed by a further 13% decline, but averaging-down by the same individuals is distinguishable from routine portfolio activity.

Our base case is $57, or 16.5x FY2027 consensus of $3.44, a modest discount to ResMed's 18.6x that reflects BSX's leverage, litigation load and absent dividend, and a wide discount to the peer median. Bull case $72 assumes U.S. electrophysiology re-accelerates, Penumbra closes and contributes, and the multiple recovers to 20x on FY2027 EPS revised back toward $3.60. Bear case $40 assumes U.S. EP growth turns negative, FY2027 estimates fall to roughly $3.10, and the multiple holds at 13x, slightly below the $42.20 July low.

One second-order valuation point deserves flagging: because Penumbra's consideration is structured as approximately $11.0B of committed debt with equity as the plug for the remaining ~$3.5B, a lower BSX share price mechanically increases dilution. At $49.30 the stock component implies roughly 71.0M new shares, 4.9% of existing shares and 4.7% post-issuance ownership dilution; at $40 it would be roughly 87.5M shares and 5.7% dilution. The drawdown carries a real transactional cost.

## Competitive Landscape

Pulsed field ablation is the battleground, and BSX moved first at scale. The FY2025 10-K attributes growth in both FY2024 and FY2025 to electrophysiology "led by the continued growth of our Farapulse Pulsed Field Ablation System which launched in the U.S. in early 2024." The Q2 2026 10-Q's phrase "increased competition within our Electrophysiology business unit" is the first filing-level acknowledgment that the first-mover window has narrowed, and the U.S./international split (3.2% versus 23.0%) locates the pressure precisely where competitors would first achieve commercial traction.

We cannot size the share shift. BSX names no PFA competitor in any filing, and no share data is available in this evidence set. That is a material gap: the difference between U.S. EP settling at 3% growth versus reverting to 10% is worth roughly $0.30–0.40 of FY2027 EPS and, at a 16x multiple, $5–6 of share price. Anyone underwriting the bull case is implicitly forecasting a variable that current disclosure does not permit forecasting.

Watchman presents a related but distinct question. Low-single-digit U.S. growth (2.9%) in a franchise the FY2025 10-K described as benefiting from "continued market penetration of LAAC procedures" suggests maturing penetration, competitive entry, or both. The 10-K also added specific, non-boilerplate language on "migration of procedures from inpatient to outpatient, ambulatory surgical centers or office-based settings, and associated changes in coverage, reimbursement, payment terms and provider economics." LAAC and EP are exactly the procedures subject to such shifts, and BSX flagged it before the deceleration appeared.

In China, the structural setup is unfavorable regardless of execution: national tenders "driven primarily by price," processes that "may favor local players," and separate bidding pools for multinationals. BSX's response has been to acquire local capability (Acotec, Anrei Medical, Scivita pending), which addresses market access but does not solve price compression; VBP is now the named cause of Urology's 1.1% quarter.

On the supply side, medtech carries constraints peers outside the sector do not: single-source components requiring FDA validation before substitution, ethylene oxide sterilization under evolving federal and state regulation, and PFAS, where primary manufacturers "have announced that they are discontinuing the supply of such materials," which the company says "could have an adverse impact on our ability to manufacture or supply certain products." PFAS-containing polymers are pervasive in catheters and guidewires; this is an under-discussed input risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Litigation, three new fronts in one quarter.** A securities class action (*Troike v. Boston Scientific*, D. Mass., filed March 5, 2026) alleges false and misleading statements "concerning the Company's financial guidance and anticipated growth in the U.S. electrophysiology business unit," stemming from the February 4 price drop; Indiana Public Retirement System was appointed lead plaintiff, and the July 20 amended complaint added two pension plaintiffs and a Section 20A contemporaneous-trading claim against an unnamed current officer. Four derivative complaints and a books-and-records demand followed. Two of those derivative complaints include allegations concerning battery safety in the Accolade pacemaker family, an allegation only, with no company statement, recall, field action or regulatory communication found; it must not be treated as established, but a battery-safety claim in a pacing line is the category that can escalate. Separately, the JPML consolidated federal spinal cord stimulator injury cases into an MDL in the Central District of California on June 5, 2026, targeting a Neuromodulation franchise growing 12.7%.

The reserve context raises the stakes. BSX is "substantially self-insured with respect to product liability" and states that "the absence of significant third-party insurance coverage increases our potential exposure." Total accrual for probable and estimable legal matters was $282M at June 30, and where a range exists with no more-likely amount, the company accrues the minimum. Litigation-related net charges ran $0 in FY2024, $194M in FY2025 and $76M in Q2 2026 alone. Lenders negotiated a cash-litigation-payment covenant carve-out of $1.160B, of which $1.115B remained at June 30, implying roughly $45M of net payments in the first half, but the size the lenders demanded is itself informative. Offsetting: the Federal Circuit reversed in BSX's favor on July 27, invalidating the University of Texas patent and erasing a $42M verdict plus roughly $7M of interest.

**Penumbra.** A $14.5B acquisition at $374 per share, roughly $11.0B debt-funded, received an FTC Second Request on March 16, 2026. Financing is fully committed (a $3.0B revolver to 2031, a $2.0B 364-day revolver and a $6.0B term loan, all closing-contingent). Adding $11.0B to net debt of $12,085M against an FY2025 EBITDA proxy of $4,981M gives roughly 4.6x before any Penumbra EBITDA or synergies, biased high because Penumbra's financials are absent from available disclosure, but sufficient to establish that the deal consumes most of the covenant headroom a qualified-acquisition designation would provide, against step-downs to 4.50x, 4.25x and 4.00x in subsequent quarters. The Second Request admits two readings: routine depth of review for a large combination in overlapping vascular intervention, or genuine horizontal-overlap concern given BSX's existing peripheral/vascular presence and its cancelled Devoro mechanical thrombectomy program. Available disclosure does not distinguish them.

**MiRus.** BSX discontinued ACURATE TAVR worldwide on May 28, 2025, abandoning U.S. approval. Eleven and a half months later it paid $1.5B (plus a prior $100M) for approximately 33.75% non-voting equity in MiRus plus an exclusive option to acquire its TAVR business for $3.0B more, exercisable only after clinical and regulatory milestones on a system that holds an IDE for its pivotal trial. If BSX declines to fund or exercise, its equity "will be forfeited or reduced by approximately 75 percent." That creates economic pull toward a total path of roughly $4.6B plus sales-based royalties for a pre-approval asset. Equity-method investments rose from $396M to $1,308M, with cost exceeding BSX's share of underlying net assets by $1.311B.

**Tax and controls.** Costa Rica Free Trade Zone benefits and other holidays expire between 2028 and 2034. Gross unrecognized tax benefits were $636M at June 30. On ERP, Item 4 of the Q2 10-Q states that as future phases go live, "we expect the changes to have a material impact on our internal controls over financial reporting," though controls were concluded effective at June 30 and E&Y issued an unqualified ICFR opinion for FY2025.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 99.7% is an artifact of a one-day expiry and should be discounted; the informative tenors are 38.5% (1m), 39.6% (3m) and 40.5% (6m) against 37.4% trailing realized volatility. Options are therefore priced close to fair, with a mild premium for the Penumbra decision and the October print. Skew is balanced (−1.6% at three months, +0.1% at six), which does not corroborate an acute downside bet. The more telling change is positioning: put/call open interest fell from 1.45 on July 19 to 0.72 as the stock rose 12%, meaning protection was unwound into the rally rather than added. Short interest of 1.9% of float with 1.4 days to cover confirms there is no crowded bearish position. The one flag is 3,278 August 21 $46 puts traded against 291 open interest, a modest, specific hedge just below spot. The options market is pricing event risk around the October quarter and the Penumbra decision, not systemic stress, consistent with our balanced fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($72, +46%).** U.S. electrophysiology stabilizes as BSX's next-generation PFA and mapping cycle lands, international EP continues at 20%+, and the 13% growers (Interventional Cardiology, Neuromodulation, Interventional Oncology) carry the mix. The 2026 restructuring delivers a meaningful share of $500M gross savings, extending the 150bp of underlying gross-margin improvement already visible ex the tariff refund. Penumbra clears without material divestiture and contributes revenue and EBITDA, moderating pro-forma leverage below the 4.6x standalone estimate; the remaining $3.0B authorization retires stock in the high-$40s. FY2027 estimates stop falling and revert toward $3.60, and the multiple recovers to 20x, still below the peer median. The CEO's $9.0M purchase and the company's $50.10 ASR mark the low.

**Bear case ($40, −19%).** U.S. EP growth turns negative as competitors compound share, and with cryoablation already cannibalized there is no fallback. Watchman penetration matures while site-of-care migration compresses provider economics. CRM keeps shrinking and China VBP spreads beyond stone management. FY2027 consensus grinds to $3.10 and the multiple stays at 13x. Penumbra closes into that environment at roughly 4.5x leverage with the covenant ladder stepping down, forcing deleveraging that ends the buyback; or the FTC blocks it and eighteen months of strategic capacity is wasted. The SCS MDL and securities class action produce charges materially above the $282M accrual against substantial self-insurance, and MiRus's forfeiture clause pulls another $3.0B into a pre-approval TAVR asset.

**Base case ($57, +15.6%).** Organic growth settles at 6–8%, adjusted EPS reaches roughly $3.30 in FY2026 and $3.44 in FY2027, restructuring savings offset reinvestment and China pricing, Penumbra closes late in 2026 with a deleveraging path, and the multiple recovers modestly to 16.5x as the estimate-cut cycle exhausts.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Base case $57 (16.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.44), +15.6% from $49.30. Bull $72, +46.0%. Bear $40, −18.9%. Probability-weighting 45% base / 20% bull / 35% bear yields roughly +10% expected return over twelve months: positive, but not enough asymmetry to justify adding after a 16.8% recovery off the July low with FY2027 estimates still being cut.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (late October), the Penumbra closing decision (guided second half of 2026), and FY2027 guidance in February 2027.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
1. Price of $44–46 without fundamental deterioration, restoring roughly 25% upside to base case.
2. U.S. electrophysiology growth of 6% or better in a reported quarter, or total organic growth re-accelerating above 8%.
3. FTC clearance of Penumbra without material divestiture, with pro-forma leverage disclosed at or below 4.0x including Penumbra EBITDA.
4. FY2027 consensus stabilizing, a 30-day revision of zero or better after three consecutive negative periods.

**Upgrade to BUY** additionally requires evidence that the 2026 restructuring is converting to adjusted operating-margin expansion rather than being fully reinvested, plus resumption of buybacks against the remaining $3.0B authorization at current prices.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. Q3 organic growth below 5.5%, or U.S. electrophysiology growth at or below zero.
2. FY2027 consensus falling below $3.20 (a further 7% cut).
3. Penumbra closes and covenant leverage is reported above 4.25x with the ladder stepping to 4.00x, or the buyback is suspended to fund deleveraging.
4. Company confirmation of an Accolade field action or recall, or a goodwill impairment in a Cardiovascular reporting unit; the 2026 reset of EP and Watchman forecasts is exactly what makes the April 1 no-impairment conclusion fragile.
5. Adverse ERP-related internal-control finding, or a legal accrual increase exceeding $500M.

**Downgrade to AVOID** on an FTC challenge that BSX chooses to litigate, or on an SCS MDL development indicating exposure materially exceeding the current accrual against substantial self-insurance.

**Reassessment:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October 2026), with an interim review on any 8-K disclosing Penumbra clearance, closing, or FTC action.

## Conclusion

Across three consecutive HOLDs, the score for this name has declined from 7.2 in February to 5.5 in July, and the current data neither reverses nor extends that trend decisively. What has improved since July: the Q2 print delivered a fourth straight beat with 150bp of genuine gross-margin expansion ex the tariff refund and 80bp of SG&A leverage; the CEO committed $9.0M of personal capital at $48.33 alongside three directors averaging down; and the July 27 Federal Circuit reversal removed a $49M legacy liability. What has worsened: organic growth decelerated sequentially from roughly 9.2% to 7.0%, FY2027 consensus was cut a further 7.6% in 30 days, and the shares have recovered 16.8% off the low, so the same fundamentals now come at a 12% higher price than at the prior review.

The valuation case is real: 14.3x FY2027 adjusted EPS against a 25.6x peer median, a 5.1% FY2025 FCF yield, and two independent cash-at-risk buyers in the company and its CEO. But a compressed multiple accompanied by an accelerating negative estimate cycle is the classic configuration in which cheapness resolves through further estimate cuts rather than re-rating, and the two variables that determine which way this resolves, U.S. pulsed-field-ablation share and the FTC's disposition of an $11B-debt-funded acquisition, cannot be forecast from available disclosure. Existing holders should maintain exposure with a $57 base case in view. New capital should wait for $44–46 or for the October print to show U.S. electrophysiology stabilizing.
