**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RadNet is the largest operator of freestanding outpatient diagnostic imaging centers in the United States, running 435 sites across nine states at Q1 2026, alongside a wholly owned AI software subsidiary (DeepHealth). The core imaging business is healthy: revenue grew 11.5% to $2,040M in 2025 and 22% year-over-year in Q1 2026, same-center revenue rose 8.9%, systemwide procedural volumes climbed 10.1%, and PET/CT volumes surged 35% on prostate-cancer and Alzheimer's imaging demand. Adjusted EBITDA reached $300.2M in 2025. GAAP net income to RadNet turned negative at −$18.7M in 2025, and the loss deepened seasonally to −$33.5M in Q1 2026.

The gap between growing EBITDA and negative GAAP earnings is the central analytical question. It resolves into two forces: input-cost inflation (California healthcare minimum wage, higher-cost PET/CT isotope tracers, near-doubling of stock-based comp to $54.6M), and a deliberate, cash-burning build-out of the DeepHealth AI franchise, whose segment operating loss widened to −$32.3M in 2025 from −$14.9M in 2024. DeepHealth's traction is real: annual recurring revenue nearly doubled to $96.9M and its external customer base expanded from roughly 460 to 2,890+ via five acquisitions in eighteen months, but the segment is unprofitable and management expects near-term losses to continue.

At roughly $62 and a ~$5B market cap, the market is capitalizing the AI optionality and EBITDA growth rather than current earnings, evidenced by five-year TSR of 365% against a peer group at 112%. That is a defensible framing when the AI story compounds, but it leaves little cushion. The company carries ~$1.08B of variable-rate term debt at roughly 8% (a 1% SOFR move adds $9.6M of annual interest), an Altman Z-score of 1.42 in the distress zone, goodwill of $1.09B (~28% of assets) after the $264M Gleamer deal, and roughly a quarter of revenue tied to Medicare rates that CMS resets annually. We rate the stock HOLD. The imaging franchise and DeepHealth momentum justify ownership on strength, but the leverage, dilution, reimbursement exposure, and full valuation do not offer asymmetric upside at the current price.

## Company Overview

RadNet operates fixed-site, multi-modality outpatient imaging centers offering MRI, CT, PET/CT, nuclear medicine, mammography, ultrasound, X-ray, and fluoroscopy. Concentration is heaviest in California and the Mid-Atlantic. Most centers are multi-modality, which diversifies revenue per site and positions RadNet as a lower-cost alternative to hospital-based imaging with a "one-stop" offering for referring physicians.

The company reports two segments. Imaging Centers generate roughly 95% of revenue and all of the cash flow. Digital Health (branded DeepHealth) develops radiology informatics: a RIS/PACS stack (eRad, DeepHealth OS cloud platform) and clinical AI for breast, lung, prostate, thyroid, and brain imaging, with 22 FDA clearances and 15 CE marks.

Because most states prohibit the corporate practice of medicine, RadNet does not employ radiologists directly. It contracts with a Consolidated Medical Group (professional corporations owned and controlled by RadNet's own senior management, consolidated as variable interest entities) plus third-party radiology groups. Roughly 100 consolidated centers carry noncontrolling interests, and RadNet runs 12 unconsolidated joint ventures (50 centers) with hospital systems including Cedars-Sinai, Dignity Health, and Providence. Competitive advantages rest on regional scale and density, multi-modality breadth, diversified payor relationships, cost position versus hospitals, and, increasingly, the proprietary AI stack sold to external customers.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has been consistent and accelerating: $1,430M (2022), $1,617M (2023), $1,830M (2024), and $2,040M (2025), with Q1 2026 at $575.6M. The demand backdrop is favorable, driven by an aging population, rising advanced-imaging utilization, and preventive-screening volume.

Profitability tells a different story. Net income to RadNet common fell from +$10.6M (2022) to +$3.0M (2023) to +$2.8M (2024), then to −$18.7M (2025). Operating income dropped from $104.6M in 2024 to $62.0M in 2025, compressing operating margin from 5.7% to 3.0%. The GAAP loss stems from identifiable pressures rather than deteriorating demand:

- California healthcare minimum-wage increases (effective October 2024) plus broad wage inflation.
- Medical-supply cost inflation, up ~22% in 2025 and ~45% in Q1 2026, driven by high-cost PET/CT isotope tracers used in prostate-cancer and Alzheimer's studies. The same trend that drives PET/CT volume growth carries an expensive input.
- Stock-based comp nearly doubling from $29.8M.
- The DeepHealth segment operating loss widening.
- Higher D&A, an $8.6M lease-abandonment charge, and acquisition transaction costs.

Adjusted EBITDA in 2025 (+7.4%) splits Imaging Centers $284.7M and Digital Health $15.5M; Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was $63.3M, up from $46.4M. The imaging engine is generating cash while the software venture consumes it.

On the balance sheet, operating cash flow was $298.8M in 2025 against $213.3M of capex, leaving free cash flow of $85.6M. Cash fell from $767M at year-end 2025 to $455M in Q1 2026, largely funding the Gleamer acquisition. Term-loan debt is ~$1,085M (Barclays ~$961M at roughly 8% effective; Truist ~$124M), with no revolver drawn and ~$323M available; D/E is 2.2. The Altman Z-score sits in the distress zone, but the driver is leverage and thin EBIT/assets rather than illiquidity: the current ratio is 1.76, cash is $455M, the revolver is undrawn, positive FCF continues, and term loans do not mature until 2031. This is an aggressively financed, thin-margin roll-up rather than a solvency crisis, though interest-rate sensitivity is material: each 1% move in SOFR adds $9.6M in annual interest, roughly 11% of last year's FCF.

## Growth Analysis

Two engines are running with opposite economics. The imaging business delivered same-center revenue growth of 8.9% and 10.1% systemwide volume growth in Q1 2026, supported by structural tailwinds in oncology and neurology imaging and preventive screening. PET/CT growth of 35% reflects genuine clinical demand shift, though it carries the isotope-cost headwind noted above.

DeepHealth is the higher-beta component. ARR nearly doubled from $49.8M, the external customer base expanded from ~460 to 2,890+, and the company holds 22 FDA clearances (with two additional 510(k) clearances in June 2026 adding cardiovascular-risk and prior-exam integration to the AI Breast Suite). This traction was built through five acquisitions in eighteen months: Kheiron ($2.3M), See-Mode ($28.9M), iCAD ($110.7M all-stock, adding ~1,500 customers), CIMAR UK ($37M), and Gleamer ($264.1M). The segment remains loss-making and management expects near-term losses to continue as it invests in R&D (~$20M/year non-capitalized) and integration.

Consensus estimate revisions have softened over 90 days: current-year EPS estimates fell 18.4% to $0.52 and next-year fell 6.6% to $0.94, with the current quarter down 11.5%. This does not clear the +30% threshold that would signal a strong buy; the modestly declining revision trend argues for restraint. The growth thesis is durable at the revenue and ARR level, but the path to translating that top-line momentum into GAAP profitability is unproven and, on current estimates, being pushed out rather than pulled forward.

## Valuation Assessment

RadNet trades at a trailing/forward P/E that is not meaningful given the GAAP loss (reported forward P/E of −329x). The relevant lens is EV/EBITDA and the market's capitalization of AI optionality plus EBITDA growth. On ~$5B market cap against ~$300M Adjusted EBITDA and ~$630M net debt (~$1,085M term debt less $455M cash), enterprise value approximates ~$5.6B, implying roughly 19x EV/EBITDA, a full multiple for a business with 3% GAAP operating margins and negative net income.

Peer comparison is complicated by the heterogeneity of "Health Care Services" comps (P/E range from 19.9x at Concentra to 137.8x at Privia, median 40.1x), none of which cleanly maps to a hybrid imaging-plus-AI-software model. The peer set does not offer a clean anchor, so the valuation case rests on the DeepHealth optionality being worth a meaningful premium to the imaging franchise's cash flows.

Two cautions temper the bull framing. First, the equity lessons embedded in our process treat "cheap" as anti-predictive and treat momentum-driven premium multiples with skepticism when estimate revisions are falling rather than rising; here revisions are down 18.4% on the year. Second, the analyst-targets-as-floors principle applies only to stocks that look cheap on rising estimates; RDNT is neither cheap on GAAP nor benefiting from rising estimates, so the consensus targets (low $65, mean $89.75, median $93.50) should be read as optimistic capitalizations of the AI story rather than conservative floors. The stock is roughly 4% below the lowest analyst target, which signals that consensus is running ahead of near-term earnings reality. We score valuation 4.5: not a clear value trap given real growth, but no asymmetric upside at ~19x EV/EBITDA on a levered, loss-making entity where the premium depends on unproven AI monetization.

## Competitive Landscape

The outpatient imaging market is fragmented, with hospitals, private-equity-backed chains, and regional operators competing on price and access. RadNet's advantages are scale and density in regional networks, multi-modality breadth, diversified payor mix, and a cost position below hospital-based imaging. Same-center revenue growth of 8.9% suggests it is holding or gaining share in its core markets.

The differentiated competitive lever is DeepHealth. By selling AI informatics to 2,890+ external customers, including screening sites and what the company describes as the most-used lung-cancer-screening solution in the UK NHS, RadNet is attempting to convert an operating capability into a software moat and a second revenue stream. The strategy has genuine traction (ARR doubling, expanding FDA clearance library) but is capital-intensive and unproven on economics. Competitive risk cuts both ways: RadNet is building a defensible AI position, but larger imaging-software and health-IT vendors could compress the moat, and the segment's continued losses show the market for radiology AI is still being subsidized rather than harvested. We view the trajectory as improving on balance but not yet self-funding.

## Risk Assessment

- **Reimbursement:** ~25% of revenue from Medicare, ~3% Medicaid, ~6% capitation. CMS's CY2026 Physician Fee Schedule final rule (October 2025) resets rates annually; given high fixed costs, cuts flow straight through to margin. The expiring CMS virtual-supervision policy is an additional watch item.
- **Operating leverage:** thin margins mean small volume or rate declines disproportionately hit profit.
- **Leverage and rate sensitivity:** variable-rate debt at ~8%. Term loans mature in 2031, so refinancing is not imminent, but the June 2026 term-loan expansion signals continued debt-funded growth.
- **Impairment:** goodwill jumped to $1.09B (~28% of assets) after Gleamer; there is precedent (a $3.9M Aidence IPR&D write-down in 2023). If AI synergies disappoint, impairment is the most likely negative surprise.
- **Integration:** five AI acquisitions in eighteen months plus continuous imaging tuck-ins strain integration capacity.
- **Digital Health execution:** the segment loses money and management expects that to continue near-term.
- **Governance/structure:** the CEO controls the affiliated physician groups consolidated as VIEs, long-standing and disclosed, but a structural concentration of control. Insiders are net sellers in 2026 with no open-market buying.
- **Cybersecurity:** PHI exposure and ransomware risk, explicitly referencing the 2024 Change Healthcare attack.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 56.9% against 43.7% one-year historical vol (rich by 13.2 points), the term structure is in backwardation (56.9% near vs 51.7% at six months), and the nearest expiry implies a ±14.1% move. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 1.04 to 2.52 since mid-June, and put/call volume is 1.94, indicating elevated hedging or bearish positioning. Combined with 15.1% short interest and 9.8 days to cover, positioning is defensive. One qualifier: the 1-month skew is −9.0% (call-heavy), suggesting some upside speculation is layered on top of the put-heavy open interest. The options market corroborates the caution in our HOLD and flags event risk around the next earnings print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The imaging franchise compounds at high-single-digit same-center growth on secular demand, Adjusted EBITDA continues its climb, and DeepHealth crosses into profitability as ARR (~$97M and doubling annually) scales past the fixed R&D and integration cost base. The market re-rates the software segment on a SaaS-like multiple, the input-cost inflation moderates as CA wage step-ups anniversary, and interest rates ease, relieving the variable-rate burden. In that scenario the stock retraces toward the 52-week high near $86 and analyst upside targets ($93.50 median, $100 high) become achievable. Bull case: ~$90.

**Bear case.** CMS cuts imaging reimbursement, wage and isotope-tracer inflation persist, DeepHealth losses stay wide while integration of five deals consumes management attention, and rates stay higher for longer, pressuring interest coverage. A goodwill impairment against the $1.09B balance forces a headline writedown. Multiple compression on a levered, loss-making name toward the low end of its range takes the stock to ~$50, near the 52-week low. Bear case: ~$50, roughly −20% from current.

**Base case.** The imaging business keeps growing, DeepHealth continues its ARR ramp but stays loss-making through 2026, GAAP results improve seasonally off the Q1 trough but do not turn cleanly positive for the full year, and the stock consolidates in the low-to-mid $60s as the market waits for evidence of AI monetization. Base case: ~$67.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the imaging franchise justifies ownership on strength, but leverage, dilution, reimbursement exposure, and a full valuation prevent a confident BUY, while genuine growth and adequate liquidity argue against an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- DeepHealth segment operating loss narrows materially for two consecutive quarters toward breakeven, with ARR growth sustained above 40% year-over-year.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turn positive (reversing the −18.4% 90-day trend), signaling the market is catching up to a profitability inflection rather than pushing it out.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CMS reimbursement cuts materially exceed expectations, or the CY2026 rate impact compresses imaging EBITDA margin.
- A goodwill impairment charge against the $1.09B balance.
- Interest coverage deteriorates (rising SOFR plus further debt-funded M&A) or a covenant concern surfaces.
- Same-center imaging revenue growth decelerates below mid-single digits, indicating the core engine is stalling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with particular attention to DeepHealth segment loss trajectory, imaging same-center growth, and any commentary on CY2026 Medicare rate impact. The options market's backwardation flags this print as the near-term binary.

## Conclusion

RadNet is a well-run imaging operator wrapped around a fast-growing but unprofitable AI software venture, and the stock's valuation reflects the market crediting the second story more than the first. The imaging engine is healthy (8.9% same-center growth, $300M Adjusted EBITDA, $85.6M FCF), but the GAAP loss, ~19x EV/EBITDA, ~$1.08B of ~8% variable-rate debt, $1.09B of goodwill, and a quarter of revenue tied to annually reset Medicare rates leave little room for error. DeepHealth's ARR doubling to ~$97M is real, but the segment loses money and management expects that to continue near-term, and consensus estimates have been revised down, not up, over the past quarter. Insider selling, distress-zone leverage metrics, and put-heavy options positioning reinforce a posture of patience. We rate RDNT a HOLD at ~$62, would look to upgrade on a DeepHealth profitability inflection or a pullback into the low-to-mid $50s, and would downgrade on a reimbursement shock, impairment charge, or deceleration in the core imaging business.
