**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National Presto is two businesses moving in opposite directions inside one holding company. The Defense segment, anchored by AMTEC's position as the sole prime contractor for the U.S. Army's 40mm ammunition system, grew sales 43% in FY2025 to $405.9M and lifted operating profit to $58.4M, with backlog compounding from $564M (2023) to $1.84B (as of April 5, 2026). Against a ~$883M market cap, that Defense franchise alone is the reason to own the stock. The Housewares segment, sourced almost entirely from China and sold through a consolidating retail channel, collapsed from a 25% gross margin in FY2024 to 8% in FY2025, swung to a $9.3M operating loss, and then posted an outright gross loss of $2.4M in Q1 2026 on a botched warehouse transition stacked on top of tariffs it cannot pass through.

The net result is a company where headline earnings are misleading in both directions. Consolidated net income fell 20% to $33.1M ($4.63 diluted EPS) even as the underlying Defense engine accelerated. Stripping out the Housewares loss, the perennial Safety-segment drag (~$9M annually), and a one-time $2.7M vendor-deposit impairment, the Defense business is compounding cleanly. The market is valuing a scarce, moated defense-ammunition supplier bundled with a structurally declining consumer-appliance brand at ~27x trailing EPS that understate Defense but overstate consolidated durability.

The trajectory of prior scores has been improving (5.6 → 6.1 → 6.7), driven mainly by an upgraded read on financial health as the Q1 2026 operating cash flow rebound (+$31.3M) and full revolver paydown reversed the 2024-2025 cash scare. That improvement is real but has limits. The cash cushion is a fraction of what NPK historically carried, the special dividend that defined this stock for income holders is gone, a 40mm recompete looms in FY26, and a 73-year-old owner-operator CEO controls the company through a voting trust with no designated successor. This is a growth-oriented HOLD: the Defense thesis is genuine and durable, the valuation is fair-to-full, and the risks are concentrated and partly binary, with no asymmetry demanding a BUY at current levels.

## Company Overview

National Presto operates three disparate segments selling primarily into North America:

**Defense (81% of FY2025 sales).** Operates through National Defense Corporation subsidiaries: AMTEC (Janesville, WI), Spectra (load/assemble/pack, East Camden, AR), Amron (cartridge cases, Antigo, WI), Tech Ord (detonators/energetics, Clear Lake, SD), and Woodlawn (metal parts, Marshall, TX, acquired 2022). AMTEC is the sole prime contractor for the U.S. Army's 40mm ammunition system. Vertical integration lets NPK produce nearly every element of a medium-caliber round: fuze, detonator, cartridge case, and load/assemble/pack. Essentially all work is fixed-price for the DOD and prime contractors.

**Housewares/Small Appliance (19% of FY2025 sales).** The Presto brand: pressure cookers/canners, air fryers, griddles, heaters. Products are sourced from a handful of third-party suppliers in China and distributed from Mississippi. Highly seasonal (Q4-weighted), price-sensitive, and losing pricing power to retail consolidation and private label.

**Safety (<0.5% of sales).** "Rely Innovations" smoke/CO alarms and a PFAS-free fire extinguisher. Negative gross margins every year; management has been pruning it (divested Rusoh in 2023, OneEvent assets in 2025).

The defensible franchise is the 40mm prime position: incumbency plus domestic vertical integration in a supply-constrained ammunition base. The Housewares business has no durable moat. NPK is tagged Aerospace & Defense, but it should be analyzed on a sum-of-the-parts basis, not as a pure-play defense name.

## Financial Analysis

The core dynamic is a revenue surge on Defense masking a profit decline driven by Housewares deterioration.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $340.9M | $388.2M | $503.5M (+30%) |
| Defense sales | $241.7M | $284.0M | $405.9M (+43%) |
| Housewares sales | $97.6M | $102.8M | $95.6M (–7%) |
| Gross profit | $67.5M | $78.6M | $80.3M (+2%) |
| Net earnings | $34.6M | $41.5M | $33.1M (–20%) |
| Diluted EPS | $4.86 | $5.82 | $4.63 |

Sales rose 30% while net income fell 20%. The mechanism is clear: Housewares gross margin dropped from 25% to 8%, and segment operating profit swung from +$12.6M to –$9.3M. Two drivers: 2025 IEEPA tariffs on Chinese goods (expensed as incurred under LIFO, not fully passable to customers) and ~$1.3M of distribution-center relocation costs (Canton → Nettleton, MS). Defense gross margin held at 19-21% and grew operating profit to $58.4M, but a $2.7M vendor-deposit impairment (a Housewares supplier filed Chapter 11) and a $3.6M drop in other income (lower interest income plus new interest expense) compounded the consolidated decline.

**Balance sheet — asset-heavy, inventory-loaded, low leverage.** Total assets grew to $500.7M against $395.1M equity. Debt is negligible: the only borrowing was a revolving line of credit ($23.6M drawn at 12/31/25, since fully repaid), with interest expense of just $0.8M in 2025. Inventories ballooned to $307M (61% of assets), of which $250M is Defense work-in-process, the physical manifestation of the backlog being built ahead of delivery. This pre-funding requirement is the central working-capital dynamic.

The change in cash character is the most important balance-sheet development. Cash fell from $87.7M (2023) to $17.7M (2024) to $3.3M (12/31/2025). Marketable securities dropped from ~$70M+ to $0.5M. The company that functioned as "a bank with a cookware brand" has liquidated its investment portfolio and tapped credit to fund Defense inventory. Operating cash flow was –$9.1M in 2025 (–$53.4M in 2024) after +$45.4M in 2023, and capex rose to $27M (Nettleton warehouse purchase plus Defense capacity).

**Q1 2026 (quarter ended 4/5/2026)** showed the other side of the cycle. Sales rose 15% to $118.6M; Defense +23% to $99.6M; Housewares fell to a $2.4M gross loss on warehouse startup problems that prevented shipping for part of the quarter. Net earnings were $6.6M (–13% YoY). Operating cash flow rebounded to +$31.3M as receivables collected, the revolver was fully repaid, and cash rose to $6.5M. Backlog reached $1.84B.

FCF conversion is lumpy and cycles around the Defense delivery schedule. The Q1 rebound justifies the improved financial-health read relative to the 2024-2025 cash trough, but it does not restore the fortress balance sheet NPK historically carried. This is a business that now runs on a thin cash cushion and a revolver that expires 9/30/2026 and requires renewal.

## Growth Analysis

Defense is a durable, multi-year growth story with credible visibility. Backlog progression: $564M (2023) → $1.09B (2024) → $1.75B (12/31/2025) → $1.84B (4/5/2026), to be worked off over 18-48 months. The growth ties to the FY22-26 40mm contract ($965M awarded to date against a $1.413B ceiling) plus the May-2024 M918E2 TP-DNT IDIQ ($256M awarded, $818.9M ceiling). This is real demand tied to U.S. and allied ammunition replenishment, with the 10-K explicitly citing the Ukraine Security Assistance Initiative as a driver.

NPK's position as a scarce domestic vertically-integrated medium-caliber supplier maps directly onto the base-industrial replenishment priority. [unverified — the broader multi-front demand backdrop (Middle East, NATO/Russia, Israel-Lebanon) and a specific defense/intel/space budget figure are not supported by the research brief.]

The growth has a ceiling. The FY22-26 contract has only one award year (FY26) remaining as of the 10-K, meaning a major recompete looms. Backlog conversion at 40%+ revenue growth is unlikely to sustain past the near-to-medium term without a favorable recompete outcome. Growth durability is high through the backlog work-off window but conditional on recompete beyond it.

Housewares is a growth drag, not a contributor, down 7% in FY2025 and posting losses. Safety is a rounding error that loses money. The consolidated growth story is entirely Defense, and it is strong enough to warrant an above-average growth score with the recompete caveat firmly attached.

## Valuation Assessment

At $123.21, NPK trades at ~27.5x trailing EPS and ~$883M market cap. Because forward and trailing P/E are identical in the provided data (no consensus forward EPS available), the multiple sits on depressed consolidated earnings, depressed by the Housewares loss and Safety drag, not by a Defense trough.

Peer medians in Aerospace & Defense sit at ~28x P/E (PSN 27.4x, AIR 28.2x), so NPK is roughly in line with defense-services comparables. That comparison is imperfect: NPK is not a pure defense name, and ~19% of revenue plus its brand heritage are loss-making consumer housewares. A cleaner frame is sum-of-the-parts. Defense operating profit against an $883M enterprise, plus a still-valuable if cyclical Housewares brand and near-zero net debt, argues the Defense franchise is being valued reasonably rather than cheaply. Applying a defense-sector operating multiple to Defense operating profit and adding a modest (or even negative) value for Housewares/Safety produces a fair value clustered around the current price, not materially above it.

The case against a high valuation score is the absence of asymmetric upside. There is no forward estimate showing an earnings inflection that would make the trailing multiple look conservative; the Defense growth is offset at the consolidated line by Housewares losses. Two live but uncertain catalysts exist: the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling that IEEPA tariffs are unlawful (NPK has filed for refunds, per the Q1 10-Q) could relieve Housewares margins and produce cash refunds, but the ruling is under appeal and the administration is layering replacement tariffs (a Section 122 10% tariff, itself ruled illegal by the CIT in May 2026, also under appeal). The dividend yield of ~0.8% offers no valuation support after the special dividend was eliminated.

The stock trades at market for its sector, so a "cheap-looking" multiple is not present here. Even if it were optically cheap, cheapness without a specific catalyst has been a reliable value-trap signal, and the two catalysts on offer (tariff refunds, recompete) are two-sided rather than clearly positive. This warrants a mid-range valuation score: fair, not compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

The Defense moat is genuine. As the sole 40mm Army prime with in-house energetics, cartridge-case, and metal-parts capacity, NPK occupies a scarce position in a supply-constrained domestic ammunition base. Vertical integration is difficult to replicate, and incumbency plus qualification barriers protect the franchise through the current contract cycle. Segment revenue growth of 43% far outpaces the broader defense industry, evidence of share and volume capture within its niche.

The trajectory is favorable but faces a defined test. The FY26 recompete on the 40mm system is the single most important competitive event on the horizon. Loss of the prime position would be materially adverse; retention would extend the visibility another multi-year cycle. Until that resolves, competitive position is improving on trajectory but carries an embedded binary.

Housewares moves the other way: no long-term customer contracts, China-sourced with concentrated supply, competing against consolidated players and private label, and earning at best ~20-25% gross margin in a normal year and losing money in a tariff year. This segment is a deteriorating competitive position and increasingly reads as a future divestiture or wind-down candidate, though management gives no such signal.

Netting the moated, share-gaining Defense franchise against the eroding Housewares business produces a solid but not top-tier competitive score. Competitive-position signals have been a weaker discriminator recently, so this dimension is corroborated by the growth data rather than relied upon alone.

## Risk Assessment

**Housewares structural decline.** An operating loss in FY2025 and a gross loss in Q1 2026 confirm the segment is transitioning from profit contributor to drag. It is exposed to Chinese tariffs it cannot fully pass through, concentrated China supply, retail consolidation, and its own botched warehouse transition.

**Defense concentration and the FY26 recompete.** Essentially the entire Defense segment depends on the DOD, much of it on the single 40mm franchise. The FY22-26 contract has one award year remaining, so a major recompete is imminent. All contracts are terminable for government convenience. This is the largest binary risk and it is not fully priced.

**Fixed-price contract risk.** With escalation limited to steel, aluminum, and zinc, NPK bears cost-inflation risk across a multi-year backlog. A failed test-fire on a production lot could force costly rework or scrap of an entire lot.

**Liquidity change.** Cash of ~$3-6M versus a historical ~$70-90M, a drawn-then-repaid revolver, and a credit line expiring 9/30/2026 mark a real shift in financial character, even at low leverage. The Q1 cash rebound mitigates but does not eliminate the concern given the lumpy delivery-driven cash cycle.

**Governance and succession.** CEO/Chair/President Maryjo Cohen (age 73) controls the company through a long-standing voting trust with no obvious successor. COO/General Counsel Douglas Frederick is the likely operational heir but is not designated. A leadership transition could shift capital-allocation philosophy in either direction. CFO David Peuse was promoted 11/20/2025; VP-Engineering Jeffery Morgan retired 4/30/2026.

**Cybersecurity.** A March 2025 incident caused a system outage affecting shipping, some manufacturing, and back-office functions; management concluded no material financial impact but flags recurrence risk.

**Tariff/legal (two-sided).** The IEEPA ruling and refund filing are potential upside, but appeals and replacement tariffs create ongoing uncertainty.

**Dividend regime change.** The elimination of the special dividend (total dividends paid fell from $32.0M to $7.1M) is the clearest management signal of the year: cash is being redirected into Defense working capital. Bulls read this as growth investment; NPK's traditional income base is receiving far less cash.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Defense franchise is a scarce, moated, vertically-integrated ammunition supplier compounding at 40%+ revenue growth with $1.84B of backlog and multi-year visibility, feeding into a durable replenishment cycle. Consolidated earnings understate this because Housewares and Safety losses mask $58.4M of Defense operating profit. If the 40mm recompete is retained, tariff refunds materialize, and Housewares is stabilized or divested, the sum-of-the-parts is worth meaningfully more than the current cap. The Q1 cash rebound demonstrates the working-capital drawdown was investment ahead of deliveries, not distress. Bull target ~$160-170, roughly 25x a normalized ~$6.50 EPS assuming Housewares returns to breakeven and Defense continues to convert backlog.

**Bear case.** The 40mm recompete is lost or awarded on worse terms, removing the growth engine. Housewares continues to bleed and the tariff relief never lands as replacement tariffs bite. The thin cash cushion and revolver-dependence become a problem if the delivery cycle stalls and inventory stays trapped. Governance/succession risk crystallizes into a capital-allocation change or forced sale at an unfavorable multiple. On these, consolidated EPS stays near $4.50 and the multiple compresses toward the low-20s. Bear target ~$95, near the 52-week low, roughly –23% from current.

The balance of these leaves a fair-value cluster near the current price with a modest positive skew contingent on the recompete: insufficient asymmetry to justify a BUY, but the Defense durability and improving cash profile argue against an AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The Defense growth thesis is genuine and supports holding for 12-18 month appreciation, but valuation is fair and the risks are concentrated enough to cap conviction below a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Favorable resolution or clear positive signaling on the FY26 40mm recompete (new award or contract extension above the current $1.413B ceiling).
- Housewares returns to at least breakeven gross margin for two consecutive quarters, or management announces a divestiture/wind-down that removes the drag.
- Confirmed tariff refund receipt (per the IEEPA ruling) materially improving cash and Housewares margins, without an offsetting replacement-tariff regime.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Loss of the 40mm prime position or a recompete awarded on materially worse terms.
- Failure to renew or upsize the credit line by 9/30/2026 amid another negative operating-cash-flow stretch.
- A disorderly leadership transition or capital-allocation shift that signals a distressed sale of the Defense franchise at a low multiple.
- Housewares gross losses persisting or widening beyond Q1 2026 levels through the seasonally strong Q4.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, with the credit-line renewal (9/30/2026) and any FY26 recompete award as the higher-stakes events. Given no earnings calls and minimal investor communication, monitoring will rely on 8-K filings and quarterly 10-Qs.

## Conclusion

National Presto is a well-run defense-ammunition franchise wrapped inside a family-controlled conglomerate that is deliberately reinvesting its former cash hoard into backlog. The Defense engine is compounding, moated, and aligned with a durable replenishment cycle, and the Q1 2026 cash rebound confirms the 2024-2025 drawdown was investment rather than distress. That justifies the improving score trajectory and a growth-oriented HOLD.

What keeps this from a BUY is the combination of a fair-to-full valuation (~27x trailing EPS at the sector median, on earnings depressed by Housewares rather than a Defense trough), the absence of a clean earnings-inflection catalyst that would make the multiple look conservative, and a stack of concentrated risks: the FY26 recompete, the Housewares structural decline, the thinner cash cushion, and the owner-operator with voting-trust control and no designated successor. The stock offers roughly symmetric risk/reward around current levels with a modest positive skew tied to a binary recompete. Own it for the Defense growth thesis and monitor the recompete and Housewares stabilization closely, with no asymmetry demanding incremental capital at $123.
