## Executive Summary

Heritage closed the $185M all-stock acquisition of Olympic Bancorp (Kitsap Bank) on 31 January 2026, adding roughly 22% to the balance sheet: $954.3M of loans, $1.389B of deposits, $38.1M of goodwill and a $50.3M core deposit intangible. The second quarter of 2026 was the first full combined period, and the headline numbers look good: net interest income $74.8M (+36.1% YoY), NIM 3.99% versus 3.51% a year earlier, nonaccruals down to 0.27% of loans from 0.44%, CET1 12.1%. The acquisition solved a real problem: Heritage's balance sheet was shrinking pre-deal, with FY2025 assets down 2.0%, loans down 0.4%, and $520.3M of prepayments against only $583.3M of new fundings.

Under the surface, the deal has not yet changed the growth arithmetic. Loans grew $964.7M over six months against $954.3M acquired, implying organic growth of roughly $10M; deposits rose $1.118B against $1.389B assumed, implying organic contraction of approximately $270M, which ties to the financing-activities line. The funding tailwind that drove FY2025's 56% earnings recovery is over: the CFO stated on 23 July that spot interest-bearing deposit cost of 1.64% is "the bottom" and he expects gradual increases with CD competition rising. From here, margin expansion depends entirely on asset repricing (portfolio note rate 5.72% versus 6.40% on new production) and on levering an 81.7% loan-to-deposit ratio, against a deposit cost that is turning.

Three things changed our view since the July report, none of them price action. First, consensus EPS has been cut across every horizon (current quarter -4.5%, next quarter -6.8%, current year -1.9%, next year -5.2%) while the stock sits 5% below its 52-week high after a 32% twelve-month advance. Second, the 10-Q filed 7 August shows earnings quality deteriorating at the margin: $1.95M of allowance releases in the half (~$0.04 of EPS) taken while special-mention loans nearly doubled from $71.1M to $125.1M and 86% of new nonaccrual inflow was C&I; a 15.4% effective tax rate dependent on continued LIHTC deployment; purchase accounting running as a net ~$4.74M annualized pre-tax drag; and tangible book per share falling 4.2% to $19.15 partly because buybacks were executed at $26.99. Third, capital returned in Q2 (~$19.9M) exceeded GAAP net income of $17.5M. At 12.7x current-year consensus and 1.55x tangible book, we do not think the shares are expensive; we think the expected return over the next twelve months is below what a small-cap bank equity should deliver given a dated operational risk (late-September core conversion) and negative estimate momentum. Trim into strength.

## Company Overview

Heritage Bank is a relationship commercial bank serving small and medium businesses along the I-5 corridor from Whatcom County, Washington through greater Portland and Eugene, with additional presence in Spokane, Yakima, the Olympic Peninsula and Boise. It reports a single operating segment. Lending concentrates in C&I, owner-occupied CRE, non-owner-occupied CRE and construction, supplemented by a residential book in deliberate runoff: the company originated or purchased no residential loans in FY2025, leaving that $358.8M portfolio to amortize.

The franchise operates under legacy local trade names, including Whidbey Island Bank and, post-acquisition, Kitsap Bank. For a bank of this size a genuine multi-brand community strategy is unusual, and it is plausibly relevant to deposit retention through the systems conversion. Whether the Kitsap brand is permanent is not disclosed.

Post-merger scale at 30 June 2026: total assets $8.431B (from $6.967B at year-end), loans $5.748B, deposits $7.039B, equity $1.110B. Goodwill is $279.0M and other intangibles $47.3M, together 29.4% of equity, up from 26.2%.

## Financial Analysis

**The rate-shock arc.** Diluted EPS went $2.73 (2021) → $2.31 → $1.75 → $1.24 (2024) → $1.96 (2025). NIM went 3.56% (FY2023) → 3.31% (FY2024) → 3.58% (FY2025). This is a textbook liability-sensitive profile: deposits repriced faster than assets into 2024, then the funding side rolled over. The 2024 trough was worsened by $22.7M of securities-sale losses; FY2025 still absorbed $10.7M, selling $152.4M yielding 2.62% and reinvesting $88.2M at 4.89%. FY2025 noninterest income of $21.7M versus $7.5M is almost entirely the absence of the prior year's larger loss rather than fee growth. Heritage is a spread bank with a thin fee franchise.

**Q2 2026 quality of margin.** Reported NIM 3.99%; NIM excluding purchase-accounting accretion 3.90%. Nine basis points of accretion benefit is unusually low for a deal that added 22% to the balance sheet, and the reason is an accounting election: Heritage early adopted ASU 2025-08 effective 1 January 2026, applying PCD-style gross-up treatment to acquired loans that are not credit-deteriorated. Day-one allowance additions were $9.34M ($9.17M on purchased seasoned loans) rather than a discount accreting into future interest income. The mechanical consequence is less earnings sugar in reported NIM than a peer closing a comparable deal under the legacy model. We draw no conclusion about motive: operational simplicity of running one model, and alignment with a standard that becomes mandatory for periods beginning after 15 December 2026, are equally plausible.

The offset runs the other way and is easy to miss. Core deposit intangible amortization was $2.957M in Q2 (versus $302K a year earlier), annualizing to ~$11.83M, against loan accretion of $1.772M/quarter, or ~$7.09M annualized. Net purchase-accounting effect on annualized pre-tax earnings is approximately **-$4.74M**, roughly $0.10/share after tax. Accretion decays; the CDI amortizes on a ten-year schedule. Purchase accounting is currently suppressing reported earnings, and that will persist.

**Funding.** Cost of interest-bearing deposits improved from 1.94% to 1.67% year over year, but management attributes most of it to mix (acquired Kitsap deposits carried a lower cost) plus the three 25bp cuts in autumn 2025. Deposits fell $209.8M (-2.9%) sequentially in Q2; management decomposed this into April tax seasonality, a $67M withdrawal by one commercial relationship that had parked non-operating funds in Q1, and $48.5M of brokered CD runoff allowed to mature because FHLB was cheaper. Adjusted, roughly normal seasonality. FHLB borrowings rose from $20M to $166.25M, all maturing in the first two weeks of July, which is liquidity bridging rather than term leverage. There is no holding-company leverage complex and no covenant risk: junior subordinated debentures are $22.5M, counted as Tier 2.

Rate sensitivity is modestly asset-sensitive: +200bp shocks twelve-month NII +$4.79M (+1.6%); -200bp is -$9.69M (-3.3%). That asymmetry mildly favors Heritage if rates hold or rise and hurts if the Fed cuts.

**Earnings quality items to net out.** 1H 2026 GAAP diluted EPS annualizes to $1.80. Adding back $12.671M of merger charges at the 15.4% effective rate adds ~$0.27. Removing $3.395M of accretion (-$0.07) and $1.951M of provision reversals (-$0.04) gives roughly $2.12. Layering the Q4 expense reset ($56-57M guided quarterly noninterest expense versus $64-65M in Q3) onto a modestly larger revenue base supports something in the $2.30-2.40 range annualized, which is where current-year consensus of $2.34 sits. The Q3→Q4 expense step is worth roughly $0.17/quarter after tax and is the clearest identifiable catalyst.

**Capital and returns.** CET1 12.1%, TCE 9.7%, equity/assets 13.2%. Liquidity of $3.273B covers 119% of $2.75B uninsured deposits. Net charge-offs were 3bp in FY2025 and 3bp annualized in 1H 2026. Against that: TBV/share fell from $19.98 to $19.15 over six months (merger intangibles, a $6.8M widening of AOCI losses, and buybacks above tangible book). HTM gross unrecognized losses of $54.1M sit off the balance sheet entirely; marking them at 21% reduces TBV to ~$18.11. AFS and HTM marks together equal roughly 10.5% of tangible book after tax, though the AOCI opt-out shields regulatory capital.

Q2 capital return: ~$9.8M of dividends plus $10.1M of buybacks at an average $26.99 equals ~$19.9M against $17.5M of net income, a 114% GAAP payout, or ~83% adjusted for merger charges. It is fundable from a 12.1% CET1 position and rational at 1.55x tangible book with weak organic growth, but repurchasing at 1.41x tangible book is dilutive to TBV/share, and it is not repeatable if loan growth reaches the guided mid-single digits.

Annualized Q2 ROA was 0.83% and ROE 6.31%, depressed by $7.5M of merger charges, but even normalized ROTCE of roughly 12% is unremarkable for a bank trading at 1.55x tangible book.

## Growth Analysis

Origination activity is genuinely improving. Commercial commitments closed were $339M in Q2, up 104% from $166M in Q1 and above $248M in Q2 2025. Pipeline was $628M versus $473M a year earlier. Management attributes the demand recovery to fiscal policy and, on the Q1 call, to war-related disruption; those are characterizations, not verified drivers.

Net growth has not followed. Loans grew $26M (+0.4%) in Q2 because prepayments and payoffs rose to $152.4M from $119M, and because production skewed to construction where utilization fell 4.1% sequentially and construction commitments fund over quarters. Six-month arithmetic implies organic loan growth of roughly $10.4M against management's "Heritage-only" figures of +$20M and +$26M; the ~$35M gap is most plausibly runoff inside the acquired Kitsap book, which management corroborated qualitatively ("a few payoffs in the Kitsap portfolio," Kitsap construction loans "meeting their maturity dates"). Heritage paid approximately 1.92x target tangible book for an earning-asset base that is contracting modestly before the systems conversion has occurred.

Guidance for 2H 2026 is mid-single-digit annualized loan and deposit growth, explicitly conditioned on prepayments staying at current elevated levels. That guidance has already been walked down once; an analyst noted the January framing was "upper single digits."

The three margin levers are quantifiable. Asset repricing: 68bp gap between the 5.72% portfolio note rate and 6.40% on new production, though management attributes the Q2 rate increase to the five-year FHLB index moving up rather than to pricing power (new commercial rates fell 45bp in Q1). Balance-sheet leverage: 81.7% loan-to-deposit gives room. Cost reset: the Q4 expense guide. Against those, the CFO's explicit call that deposit costs have bottomed removes the lever that produced FY2025's recovery.

Consensus reflects this balance and is moving down: current year $2.34 (-1.9% over 90 days), next year $2.65 (-5.2%). Six analysts, no upward revisions in either window. The estimate-revision signal here is negative, which we weight as the primary forward indicator.

The dispersion inside the group matters: PFBC at 9.4x and TMP at 7.8x are materially cheaper, WABC at 12.8x, SBSI at 14.4x and CFFN at 13.6x bracket Heritage. There is no discount to exploit here.

The 1.55x tangible book is the harder number. It is not a mispricing. For 1.55x TBV to be cheap, you need either ROTCE moving toward 14-15% (which requires the expense reset and mid-single-digit growth and NIM holding above 4% against rising deposit costs) or a takeout, which is constrained near-term by a CRE concentration ratio sitting at roughly 300% of total risk-based capital.

Consensus targets are $32 low, $34 high, $33 mean, all above spot, implying +11% price appreciation. We treat these as informative but not independent confirmation: they embed the $2.65 next-year figure that has been cut 5.2% in 90 days and is still being revised down. Our base case sits below the analyst low, which is a divergence we own explicitly.

This assumes the Q4 expense reset lands near $56.5M, NIM holds in the 3.95-4.05% band as asset repricing roughly offsets a 10-15bp rise in deposit costs, loan growth reaches low-to-mid single digits, and the provision normalizes to ~$2M/quarter (ending the reserve-release contribution). Total return with the $1.00 dividend: **+8.8%**.

- **Bear $22.50** — 11x on $2.05, equivalently ~1.15x tangible book. Requires deposit costs to rise 25-30bp as CD competition intensifies, special-mention migration to convert (provision to 25-30bp of loans), and the conversion to produce measurable deposit attrition. **-21% including dividend.**

Probability-weighting 60/20/20 gives an expected total return of roughly **+6%**, below the ~9-10% cost of equity we would require for a small-cap bank carrying a dated operational event and negative estimate momentum. Valuation score 5.2. The multiple is defensible rather than mispriced, and the identifiable catalyst (Q4 cost reset) is already inside the consensus number that is being cut.

## Competitive Landscape

The deposit franchise is the genuine asset. Cost of interest-bearing deposits 1.67% with a 1.64% spot rate; noninterest-bearing demand $1.973B or 28.0% of deposits; non-maturity deposits ~84%; **zero brokered deposits**. For a $8.4B bank in a competitive metro footprint, that funding profile is top-quartile and it is what makes the 81.7% loan-to-deposit ratio a lever rather than a liability.

Credit granularity supports the same conclusion: average individual CRE loan ~$1.4M, top ten borrowing relationships ~6.5% of loans at year-end, office exposure 46.1% owner-occupied with 82% recourse to owners. Realized losses of 3bp across multiple years are the strongest evidence in the file.

The competitive weakness is origination capacity. Producer headcount is flat, with management stating hires are netting against retirements of long-tenured bankers, and the disclosed growth playbook is team lift-outs from disrupted Pacific Northwest competitors (a Spokane team added in 2025), supplemented by occasional whole-bank M&A. That is capital-efficient and credible, and analysts from KBW and Raymond James independently raised footprint disruption as an opportunity. But it has not yet produced organic growth: the residential book is in runoff by design, construction balances fell 37.3% in FY2025 as projects converted to payoffs, and the acquired Kitsap book is shrinking.

The five-year record is the uncomfortable datapoint. Indexed TSR from the proxy: HFWA 122.94 at end-2025 versus 160.37 for the company's own selected peer group, with outperformance in only one of five years. Heritage has bought Puget Sound density and a better funding mix while its organic earning-asset base stands still. That is stable-to-modestly-improving positioning, not widening moat. Score 5.6.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Credit is improving in the stock and deteriorating in the flow.** Nonaccruals fell from $20.976M (0.44%) to $15.510M (0.27%) and substandard from 2.4% to 1.8% of loans. But the improvement was exit-driven: $12.956M of payoffs in the half, with the CCO attributing $15.9M of the Q2 substandard decline to three C&I relationships paying off. Meanwhile new nonaccrual additions were $9.376M, of which $8.1M across six loans was C&I, and **special mention nearly doubled from $71.1M to $125.1M**. Combined criticized loans went $187.9M (3.93%) to $230.5M (4.01%). Within classes, NOO CRE special mention rose from $36.0M to $53.7M and OO CRE from $15.0M to $31.7M. The CCO cited "tariff issues, higher labor costs, supply chain issues" in C&I. Part of the increase is mechanical, from onboarding $954M of acquired loans onto Heritage's grading scale, with ~$18M of the Q1 criticized increase from Olympic, but that explains a minority of a $54M half-year move, and Olympic arrived with zero nonaccruals.

**2. Reserve releases into that flow.** ACL/loans went 1.10% → 1.06% → 1.03%, producing $1.951M of provision reversals in the half (~$0.04 of EPS after tax). The CECL mechanics cited (shorter weighted-average life, portfolio mix) are defensible. With ACL coverage of nonaccruals at 383% and charge-offs at 3bp there is cushion. But releasing reserves while early-warning inflow accelerates is low-quality earnings, and the direction reverses if special-mention migrates.

**3. Deposit cost inflection.** The CFO's own words: spot cost of 1.64% is "the bottom," with "gradual increases... from here on" and "pressure on deposits" as market CD rates rise with the Fed on hold. This is the most direct threat to the base case.

**4. Late-September core systems conversion.** The largest near-term operational risk, inside the next quarter. Management has retained branch and commercial teams and reports no negative customer response, while conceding "we'll learn more... when we actually go through the systems conversion." Olympic was excluded from management's ICFR assessment in Q2 (permitted for recent acquisitions), leaving a temporary attestation gap over ~18% of assets.

**5. CRE concentration at the supervisory threshold.** Not exceeded at 12/31/2025 per the 10-K; the CCO disclosed 301% of total risk-based capital at Q1 and "just under 300%" at Q2, attributing the move to fair-value marks reducing the combined capital denominator rather than to CRE growth. Exceeding interagency guidance is not a legal breach but typically triggers heightened CRE risk-management expectations and can create friction on capital actions and future M&A approvals. Sitting at the line while running a >100% GAAP payout is a tension. Within CRE, office is $685.0M (18.3%), of which 24.8% of borrowers are in health care and social assistance, a sub-concentration a top-line office screen would miss.

**6. Tax-rate dependency.** The 14.1% FY2025 / 15.4% 1H26 effective rate reflects a large LIHTC program ($151.0M carrying value, $11.8M of proportional amortization in 1H, $14.0M of unfunded commitments). Reversion to a 21-22% rate would cut net income roughly 8%, or ~$0.19 off a $2.34 base.

**7. Securities marks and provisional accounting.** $54.1M of HTM unrecognized losses sit off the balance sheet; total marks equal ~10.5% of tangible book after tax. Purchase-accounting fair values remain subject to measurement-period adjustment through January 2027.

**8. Insider activity.** No open-market purchases in the last four months. Discretionary sales cluster in a $27.50-$30.00 band as the stock approached its high: a director sold 10,000 shares at $30.00 on 5 August (from 50,719 to 40,719), the CCO 3,234 at $29.92, and the Chief Banking Officer 3,778 at $29.69 leaving 3,099, a large proportional reduction. Aggregate discretionary sales of ~22,300 shares (~$0.64M) against a $1.2B market cap are economically trivial and consistent with routine diversification. We would normally discount this entirely; it carries slightly more weight here because it co-occurs with falling estimates rather than rising ones.

**9. Governance.** A CEO transition (Deuel to McDonald) occurred in the same window as the largest transaction since 2018, with the relevant Item 5.02 8-K (19 December 2025) not retrievable; the transition is established, its terms are not. There has been a cluster of Item 5.02 filings across two years. CFO and CCO continuity is intact, absolute pay is modest (CEO ~$1.35M, CEO/NEO ratio ~1.8x), and the incentive metrics include net charge-offs and the overhead ratio alongside EPS, which is appropriate for a lender.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility moved from 51.1% to 105.1% between 23 July and 7 August while the price fell 1.4%, with put/call open interest of 0.02. We do not treat this as diagnostic. A 105% implied volatility on a $1.2B regional bank whose peer betas run ~0.56 is inconsistent with any plausible fundamental scenario, and near-zero put open interest means there is no hedging demand behind it. The reading is almost certainly a quote artifact from wide spreads on illiquid strikes. No directional inference is drawn.

The more informative positioning signal is short interest: **7.3% of float with 7.1 days to cover**, elevated for a small regional bank and consistent with some sophisticated bearish positioning around the conversion and expense story. It also constitutes squeeze risk against our downgrade: a clean Q4 print with the expense reset delivered could force covering into a thin float.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($37, +28% including dividend)

The expense reset is mechanical and dated: Q3 guided noninterest expense of $64-65M (including ~$6M of residual merger charges) drops to $56-57M in Q4, worth roughly $0.17/quarter after tax. Layer on 68bp of asset repricing against an 81.7% loan-to-deposit ratio, a $628M pipeline up 33% year over year, and commitments closed doubling sequentially, and normalized EPS reaches $2.75 with ROTCE approaching 14%. The deposit franchise (28% noninterest-bearing, zero brokered, 1.64% spot cost) is scarce and would support 1.8-1.9x tangible book in a re-rating. Credit remains pristine at 3bp of charge-offs with 383% ACL coverage of nonaccruals; the special-mention increase proves partly mechanical from acquisition grading. Consolidation across the footprint delivers further team lift-outs at low capital cost. CET1 of 12.1% funds continued buybacks and, eventually, another deal.

### Bear Case ($22.50, -21% including dividend)

Deposit costs rise 25-30bp as CD competition intensifies with the Fed on hold, and the -3.3% NII sensitivity to a -200bp move means a dovish pivot hurts too. NIM stalls below 4%. Special-mention loans, now $125.1M and doubling in six months with C&I supplying 86% of new nonaccrual inflow, migrate to substandard; the provision normalizes toward 25-30bp of loans, removing both the release tailwind and roughly $0.15 of run-rate EPS. The late-September conversion produces measurable deposit attrition in the acquired Kitsap base, which is already shrinking. The LIHTC-driven 15% tax rate reverts toward 22%, costing another 8% of net income. Analysts' unresolved question about cost-save realization is answered unfavorably: legacy expense growth absorbs the merger saves, leaving a structurally higher 2.63%-of-assets expense ratio versus 2.35% for legacy Heritage. EPS settles near $2.05, the multiple compresses to 11x, and the stock finds 1.15x tangible book. Capital return above 100% of earnings is curtailed by a CRE ratio at the supervisory line.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Fourth consecutive quarter of coverage; the prior three were HOLD with scores of 6.4, 6.2 and 5.7. The downgrade rests on new evidence rather than price: consensus cut across all four horizons since the July report, the 10-Q's disclosure of TBV/share erosion and purchase accounting as a net drag, reserve releases taken into a doubling special-mention balance, and capital return exceeding earnings, all with the shares 5% below the 52-week high.

**De-risking plan.** Trim on strength above $30 and complete the reduction ahead of the late-September core conversion, which is the only dated event in the next quarter and is one-sided in its risk profile. Long-term holders seeking the 3.37% yield may retain a half position: the dividend is covered at roughly 43% of normalized $2.34 EPS and is supported by 12.1% CET1. We would not add new capital above $27.

**Targets from the $29.64 basis:** base $31.25 (+5.4% price, +8.8% total return), bull $37.00 (+24.8% price), bear $22.50 (-24.1% price). Timeframe twelve months, anchored to the Q3 print (late October, first read on conversion and deposit costs) and the Q4 print (late January 2027, the clean expense run-rate).

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. Q4 2026 noninterest expense lands at or below $57M with cost of interest-bearing deposits up no more than 10bp from the 1.64% spot, confirming the margin and expense case simultaneously.
2. Special-mention loans decline below $100M with no offsetting increase in substandard, and the provision returns to a build rather than a release.
3. Organic loan growth (Heritage-only, reconcilable to the balance sheet net of acquired runoff) reaches an annualized 4%+ for two consecutive quarters.
4. Price near $25.50-26.00 (≈1.33x tangible book, ≈11x current-year consensus) with fundamentals intact, which would restore roughly 20% to the base case.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Conversion-related deposit attrition exceeding $150M in Q4, or any disclosed conversion incident affecting customer access.
2. Provision expense exceeding 25bp of average loans in any quarter, or nonaccruals returning above 0.50%.
3. A formal supervisory action or written agreement relating to CRE concentration risk management.
4. Suspension of the buyback combined with flat-to-negative loan growth, which would indicate capital is being conserved for credit rather than deployed.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release in late October 2026, which will carry the first commentary on the September conversion and the first post-inflection read on deposit costs.

## Conclusion

Heritage is a well-run, well-capitalized, well-funded small commercial bank whose organic growth engine has been idling for two years and which spent $185M of stock to buy scale it could not originate. The acquired franchise brought excellent deposits and zero nonaccruals, and the Q4 expense reset is a real and dated earnings catalyst worth roughly $0.17 per quarter. Those positives are reflected at 12.7x current-year consensus, 1.55x tangible book, and a price within 5% of the 52-week high after a 32% twelve-month advance.

What tips the balance is the composition of the next twelve months. The funding tailwind that produced the 2025 recovery is over on the CFO's own account. Reported earnings are currently supported by allowance releases, a 15% tax rate dependent on LIHTC deployment, and a payout above 100% of GAAP income funded by excess capital rather than earnings, while tangible book per share declined 4.2% in the half and special-mention loans nearly doubled. Consensus has been cut across every horizon and the only dated near-term event is an operational risk. Expected total return of roughly 6% on a 60/20/20 weighting is below what this risk profile should earn.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength above $30, retain a reduced position only for the covered 3.37% yield, and revisit for a full position in the mid-$25s or on confirmation that the Q4 expense run-rate and deposit costs land where management says they will.
