**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Owens Corning is a North America and Europe-focused branded building products manufacturer operating three segments — Roofing (~44% of sales), Insulation (~37%), and Doors (~21%) — following a 2024–2026 portfolio reshaping that added the $3.2B Masonite doors platform and divested the lower-margin Glass Reinforcements/Composites business. The FY2025 headline is a reported net loss of $(522)M, but this is a "kitchen-sink" result: it absorbs $1,135M of Doors goodwill/tradename impairment plus a $334M loss on the GR discontinued operation. The underlying business still generated $2,268M of adjusted EBITDA and roughly $962M of free cash flow (~9.5% margin). Standardized screens flag OC as distressed (Altman Z 1.6, 0% net margin), which materially misrepresents the cash-generative reality.

The problem is the trajectory, not the current-year optics. Q1 2026 confirmed a deepening downturn: adjusted EBITDA fell 35% YoY to $369M, with all three segments declining and both volumes and pricing turning negative simultaneously (Roofing volumes −14%, Insulation −3%, Doors −12%). Gross margin compressed to 23% from 29%. The Masonite deal appears to be a capital-allocation misstep: a 36% write-down of the purchase price within 18 months, with the remaining $370–380M of Doors goodwill still flagged "at risk" and segment margins halving to 7%. Leverage has become the binding constraint: total debt rose to $5.5B, commercial paper reliance grew from $50M to $380M, and management amended its revolver covenant in February 2026 to carve out the non-cash impairments from the leverage ratio.

On forward estimates ($9.48 FY26, $11.78 FY27), OC trades at roughly 15x current-year and 12x next-year EPS, a discount to the 24.8x building-products peer median. The discount is partly justified by higher leverage and a deteriorating near-term earnings path; consensus estimate revisions are broadly negative for the next-quarter and next-year windows. We see no visible catalyst until U.S. housing starts and roofing/R&R demand inflect, and both legs of the recent portfolio reshaping destroyed value in 2025–26. This is a two-sided setup: real cash generation and a genuine cyclical-recovery option on one side, a decelerating operating base with further impairment and leverage risk on the other. We rate OC a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Owens Corning (SEC CIK 1370946, the post-2006 reorganization successor to the pre-bankruptcy legacy entity, hence the "New" label) manufactures branded building products across three reportable segments recast effective January 1, 2025:

- **Roofing (~$4.44B 2025 sales, ~32% EBITDA margin):** Residential asphalt shingles, oxidized asphalt, roofing components, composite lumber, and glass mat/veil. This is the highest-margin, most defensive segment; demand is anchored by non-discretionary re-roofing and storm activity rather than new construction alone.
- **Insulation (~$3.70B, ~23% EBITDA margin):** Fiberglass batts, loose fill, spray foam, foam sheathing, mineral wool (Paroc), and pipe insulation. Tied to U.S. housing starts and non-residential/industrial activity.
- **Doors (~$2.13B, ~11% and falling toward 7% EBITDA margin):** The Masonite interior/exterior doors business acquired May 2024 for $3.2B, structurally lower-margin and the source of the company's current problems.

OC holds leading share in most core categories, including #1 in North American residential fiberglass insulation and a top-tier position in shingles. Its moat rests on manufacturing scale, vertical integration (glass melting, asphalt), the PINK brand, and broad distribution through home centers, distributors, and contractors. Management has been exiting cyclical/industrial exposure — divesting Glass Reinforcements/Composites (closed April 30, 2026) and the China/Korea building materials business — to concentrate on residential and commercial building products in North America and Europe.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 income statement requires read-through. Revenue from continuing operations was $10.10B, roughly flat versus recast 2024 ($9.85B). The reported net loss of $(522)M compares to +$647M in 2024, but the swing is entirely non-cash and one-time:

- **$1,135M Doors goodwill impairment** ($780M in Q3, $355M in Q4 2025), plus a $39M Doors tradename impairment.
- **$334M net loss from discontinued operations** on the GR divestiture classification.
- A distorted 282% effective tax rate, because the impairments are largely non-deductible.

Stripping these out, the underlying earnings power is intact: adjusted EBITDA of $2,268M (down 8% from $2,468M) and free cash flow of $962M (OCF $1,786M less capex $824M). The GAAP EPS of -$2.24 and negative trailing/forward P/E in the standardized data are artifacts of the impairment year, not the operating base.

The forward story is where caution enters. Every segment declined: Roofing EBITDA −30% (margin 30%→24%), Insulation −26% (25%→19%), Doors −50% (13%→7%). The simultaneous fall in both volume and price is the combination that most reliably signals demand weakness rather than a mix or timing effect.

The balance sheet is the key pressure point. Debt/equity stands at 2.36x, with equity falling from $5.08B to $3.85B on the impairments and further to $3.64B in Q1 2026. The Altman Z-Score of 1.6 nominally sits in the distress zone, but this is mechanically depressed by the equity write-down and impairment-driven EBIT collapse. With ~$1B FCF, $1.5B undrawn revolver plus $1.5B CP capacity, and continued covenant compliance, near-term insolvency is not a realistic concern; the Z-Score here flags elevated leverage and earnings volatility, not bankruptcy risk. The February 2026 covenant amendment excluding the 2025 non-cash impairments from the leverage ratio indicates leverage was pressing against limits.

Capital allocation has been aggressive and, in hindsight, poorly timed. OC repurchased $777M of stock (5.9M shares) in 2025 while raising the dividend, partly funded by CP/debt during a period of falling earnings. Buybacks paused entirely in Q1 2026 (zero shares), signaling that management is now conserving cash — the correct move, but one that removes a support that had propped up per-share metrics.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: over the last four quarters, two beats and two misses, including a −21.2% miss in Q4 2025 and a −3.9% miss in Q3 2025, offset by a +24.5% beat in Q1 2026. Visibility into the current downturn is genuinely low.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis at present. Revenue is flat-to-declining, Q1 2026 volumes are down across all three segments, and the demand backdrop — U.S. housing starts near 1.33M SAAR, high mortgage rates, weak affordability, and R&R pressure — is deteriorating rather than stabilizing. Management now expects roofing replacement demand to "ease," removing the one segment that had provided ballast.

Estimate revisions confirm the negative near-term momentum. Next-quarter consensus has been cut −1.8% over 30 days and −3.5% over 90 days; next-year EPS is down −0.9% over 30 days. Only the current-quarter estimate has ticked up modestly (+1.2% / +3.0%), and the current-year revision is essentially flat (−0.2% / +1.4%). This is not the accelerating positive-revision profile that would signal an earnings inflection; consensus expects the business to bottom rather than reaccelerate near-term.

The growth option, such as it is, is cyclical rather than secular: if U.S. housing starts and R&R recover as rates eventually ease, OC's operating leverage would drive meaningful EBITDA recovery off a depressed 2026 base, and the higher-margin Roofing and Insulation franchises would benefit disproportionately. The timing of that recovery is unknowable and, given the higher-for-longer rate path, likely delayed. The strategic reshaping toward higher-margin residential products is directionally sound long-term, but has destroyed value on both legs so far: a marked-down GR exit into a weak wind/industrial market alongside an overpaid, now-impairing Doors acquisition.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are unusable given the impairment year. On forward consensus — $9.48 FY26 EPS and $11.78 FY27 EPS — OC trades at roughly 15.0x current-year and 12.1x next-year earnings at $142.34. That is a clear discount to the building-products peer median P/E of 24.8x (ALLE 18.3x, WMS 24.8x, WTS 29.4x, SSD 22.7x, AAON 90.6x).

The discount is not a clean bargain. It reflects (a) higher leverage than peers (2.36x D/E, covenant carve-out, $5.5B debt), (b) a deteriorating near-term earnings path with negative estimate revisions, and (c) live impairment risk on the remaining Doors goodwill. The FY26 estimate itself carries downside risk if the Q1 2026 volume-and-price weakness persists; a further EBITDA decline would push the effective forward multiple higher than it appears today. Applying the guidance from our own scoring discipline, a compressed multiple that reflects genuine cyclical and structural pressure is a value-trap warning, not automatically a buy signal, and OC lacks an identified near-term catalyst to close the gap.

Against that, the absolute valuation offers some support. A ~2.3% dividend yield (recently raised despite the downturn) is FCF-covered, and ~$1B of free cash flow against an $11.5B market cap is a ~8.4% FCF yield on trailing figures. The analyst consensus is constructive — median target $168.5, mean $164.6, range $125–$193, with the recommendation mix improving to 2 strong buys / 9 buys / 5 holds. On an inflection stock these targets would function as floors, but OC is not inflecting; with estimates falling, the consensus targets are better read as an anchor for the eventual recovery than as a near-term catalyst.

Net, the valuation is roughly fair-to-modestly-attractive on normalized earnings power but not asymmetrically cheap given the risks. We score it 5.5: the discount is real, but so are the reasons for it.

## Competitive Landscape

OC competes in consolidated, capital-intensive oligopolies where its scale positions are genuine advantages. In roofing it faces GAF and CertainTeed (Saint-Gobain); in insulation, Knauf, Johns Manville, and CertainTeed; in doors, JELD-WEN and a more fragmented, structurally lower-margin field. The Roofing and Insulation franchises are leaders with durable moats — vertical integration, brand, and distribution reach. Q1 2026 showed even Roofing is not immune to cyclical pressure, but its 32% structural margin and re-roof demand mix keep it more defensive than pure new-construction peers.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The core franchises are stable-to-strong, but the Doors segment is the weak leg: margins falling toward 7%, five plant closures announced, and a distribution business divested in February 2026. The impairment tests are effectively the market's verdict that OC overpaid for a structurally weaker position. On balance, the company's competitive position is stable in its high-margin core and deteriorating in Doors, a wash that supports a middling score. Per our scoring discipline, competitive position carries limited recent predictive weight, so we lean on the more reliable financial-health and management signals for the overall call.

## Risk Assessment

**Doors goodwill impairment (the single most important company-specific risk).** The remaining $370–380M of Doors goodwill "continues to be at risk," plus a $156M at-risk tradename. Disclosed sensitivities are tight: a 1% cut to revenue-growth assumptions or a 0.5% EBITDA-margin cut each reduces fair value ~5%. With Doors margins still falling, additional write-downs are plausible.

**Leverage and rating sensitivity.** Growing variable-rate CP exposure and $400M of 3.4% notes are due Q3 2026. A downgrade would raise financing costs and could impair supplier-finance programs. The higher-for-longer rate environment compounds refinancing and CP cost.

**Customer concentration.** Two customers represent 16% and 12% of 2025 net sales (28% combined) and ~20%/16% of receivables, with no long-term volume contracts. Loss or consolidation of a key big-box/distribution partner would be material and could move quickly.

**Cyclical trough with deepening deceleration.** Q1 2026 volume-and-price weakness across all three segments, at a time when management expects roofing replacement to ease, signals the downturn is intensifying, not bottoming.

**Input costs and tariffs.** Asphalt, chemicals, resins, and natural gas (European ops) pressured all segments in 2025–26. A ~$50M IEEPA tariff refund is a possible offset but not yet booked.

**Paroc marine recall.** An $83M liability at Q1 2026 with a further $32M charge taken in Q1; additional ventilation-duct/steel-beam nonconformances remain unquantified.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 49.1% is rich versus 40.7% one-year historical vol (an 8.3pp spread) and sits at the 77th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (49.1% near-term vs 43.0% at six months), consistent with the next earnings print being priced as an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (1.8%) and the put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.42 and 0.23), so there is no directional bearish tilt in positioning; the market is pricing magnitude of move (implied ±11.1% on the nearest expiry), not direction. ATM IV rose 7.6pp since late June even as the stock rallied 7.4%, reinforcing that uncertainty into the coming quarter is elevated. For an owner, this argues for patience on any add until post-print IV crush, and against reading the balanced skew as either confirmation or refutation of the fundamental view.

Short interest is modest at 5.0% of float with 2.5 days to cover: neither a crowded short nor a signal that informed money is aggressively positioned against the name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$185–195).** The FY25 loss is optical; the business generates ~$2.27B adjusted EBITDA and ~$1B FCF, and screens badly overstate distress. Roofing and Insulation are durable, high-margin franchises leveraged to an eventual housing/R&R recovery. As rates ease and starts recover, operating leverage drives EBITDA back toward and above prior peaks, the multiple re-rates toward the peer median (24.8x), and the ~15x forward multiple proves a cyclical-trough entry. Management's negative "compensation actually paid" in 2025 demonstrates genuine alignment, the buyback pause preserves optionality, and the strategic focus on higher-margin residential products pays off once the Doors segment is right-sized. Analyst targets ($168.5 median, $193 high) frame the recovery upside.

**Bear Case (~$105–115).** The downturn is deepening, not bottoming: volume and price falling across all three segments, EBITDA down 35%. The Masonite acquisition is a confirmed capital-allocation error with further Doors impairment likely (7% margins, tight sensitivities, $370M+ still at risk). Leverage is the binding constraint: $5.5B debt, growing CP reliance, a covenant carve-out, and $400M of notes due in Q3 2026 into a higher-for-longer rate path. Negative estimate revisions signal FY26 consensus is at risk, which would push the effective forward multiple higher than it appears. Both legs of the portfolio reshaping destroyed value, and there is no visible catalyst to inflect earnings before a broad housing recovery that may be delayed 12+ months.

The evidence is genuinely two-sided. The cash generation and cyclical-recovery option are real; so is the operating deceleration, the leverage constraint, and the impairment overhang. Neither case dominates at the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The setup lacks the confident-directional evidence for either a BUY or an AVOID. Cash generation and normalized valuation argue against selling; deteriorating volumes, prices, and estimates argue against adding.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Sequential stabilization or reacceleration in segment volumes AND pricing in the Q2/Q3 2026 prints (particularly Roofing and Insulation returning to flat-or-positive volume growth).
- Positive inflection in consensus estimate revisions (next-quarter and next-year turning positive), signaling the cyclical trough is confirmed.
- Evidence the Doors segment has stabilized (margins holding at or above ~10%) with no further goodwill impairment, plus a debt/EBITDA path back below ~2.5x.
- A pullback toward the low-$120s (near the $125 low analyst target), which would restore asymmetric risk/reward on normalized earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further Doors goodwill impairment or a credit-rating downgrade tied to the leverage constraint.
- FY26 EPS estimate cut below ~$8.50 (roughly −10% from current $9.48), or a second consecutive quarter of double-digit volume declines.
- Failure to refinance the $400M Q3 2026 notes on reasonable terms, or renewed reliance on CP funding buybacks/dividend.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which will test whether Q1's volume-and-price weakness was a floor or an ongoing trend. The Q3 2026 note maturity and any Doors impairment update are secondary near-term inputs.

## Conclusion

Owens Corning is a cash-generative, market-leading building products company whose FY2025 GAAP loss badly misrepresents its ~$2.27B adjusted EBITDA and ~$1B FCF. The story that matters is forward, and Q1 2026 confirmed a deepening cyclical downturn with both volume and price falling across all three segments and EBITDA down 35% YoY. The Masonite acquisition has proven a costly misstep — a 36% write-down within 18 months and more goodwill at risk — while leverage has become the binding constraint, evidenced by the covenant carve-out and growing CP reliance. At ~15x forward earnings, OC trades at a justified discount to peers rather than an asymmetric bargain, and estimate revisions are broadly negative with no visible near-term catalyst. The score trajectory (4.7 → 5.2 → 5.0) reflects stabilization without resolution: financial health improved as the market recognized the impairments as non-cash, but management quality declined on the Doors capital-allocation error, and the near-term operating path remains challenged. We maintain a HOLD and would wait for either segment-level volume stabilization plus a positive revision turn (to upgrade) or a low-$120s re-entry, or conversely a further impairment/downgrade (to move to AVOID).
