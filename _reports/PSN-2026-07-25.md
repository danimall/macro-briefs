**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Parsons is a $6.3B market-cap engineering and technical-services provider split roughly evenly between Federal Solutions (defense, intelligence, cyber, space, missile defense, environmental) and Critical Infrastructure (transportation, water, urban development, with heavy Middle East exposure). After four years of double-digit growth that took revenue from $3.66B (2021) to a $6.75B peak (2024), the top line inflected negative in 2025 (–5.7% to $6.36B) and continued lower in Q1 2026 (–4.1% YoY). The stock has fallen 34% from its $89.50 high to $58.73 and touched a 52-week low of $46.88 in late June.

The central analytical question is whether this is an air pocket or a structural break. The evidence points to concentration: the decline is almost entirely one State-Department-linked "confidential contract," a large fixed-price program in the Engineered Systems unit whose second-year option was significantly reduced and is now winding down following a May 29, 2025 State reorganization. Engineered Systems fell 36% in Q1 2026 while Defense & Intelligence grew 14%, Critical Infrastructure grew 3%, book-to-bill hit 1.4x, and backlog reached a record $9.31B. Strip out the confidential program and the franchise is growing. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 10.1% from 9.6% as the lost volume appears to have been low-margin, and FY2025 net income rose YoY to $241M despite the revenue decline.

We rate PSN a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.05, a modest step down from the prior 6.4. The bull case is credible: four insiders (the CEO plus three directors) bought ~$2.1M of stock in the open market near $49-51 in May 2026, the stock sits at the analyst target floor of $58, and the underlying defense/intel demand signal is strong. The offset is real: management is levering the balance sheet (~$275M added in one quarter, debt now $1.51B) and executing five acquisitions in fifteen months to backfill the State revenue with higher-quality intel work, an integration-heavy bet whose payoff is unproven. GAAP EPS fell 18% in Q1 on acquisition costs and amortization, DSO rose to 72 days, and estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative. This is a company in transition where the July 29 Q2 print is the next decisive data point, not a clear-cut mispricing.

## Company Overview

Parsons operates two reportable segments. **Federal Solutions (~51% of revenue)** delivers advanced technology to the U.S. government across cyber operations and electronic warfare, space and missile defense, intelligence/SIGINT, military facility modernization, logistics, and hazardous-material remediation. It splits into two business units: Defense & Intelligence (growing) and Engineered Systems (contracting sharply). **Critical Infrastructure (~49% of revenue)** provides integrated engineering, design, and project management for physical and digital infrastructure globally, split into Infrastructure North America and Infrastructure EMEA. Middle East exposure is roughly 18% of revenue ($272M of $1.49B in Q1 2026).

The company's competitive edge rests on a security-cleared technical workforce, multi-year government contracts, and a large backlog. Parsons is majority employee-owned through an ESOP, which held 50.0M of 107.0M shares outstanding as of Q1 2026, a retention advantage that also functions as a recurring ~$19-20M/quarter dilution source, largely offset by buybacks. Fiscal year ends December 31. The company pays no dividend.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue trajectory is the story. Revenue rose from $3.66B (2021) to $4.20B (2022), $5.44B (2023), and $6.75B (2024) before falling to $6.36B (2025), a 5.7% decline. Net income moved in the opposite direction — $64M (2021), $161M (2023), $235M (2024), $241M (2025) — so profitability improved as the top line fell, indicating the lost volume was low-margin.

Q1 2026 confirmed the pattern. Revenue was $1.49B, down 4.1% YoY. Net income attributable to Parsons fell to $52.9M from $66.2M (–20%), and diluted EPS dropped to $0.49 from $0.60. The GAAP decline was driven by SG&A rising to 18.0% of revenue from 15.7% on acquisition costs and intangible amortization. Adjusted EBITDA rose slightly to $150.9M with margin expanding to 10.1% from 9.6%.

Segment divergence tells the concentration story. Federal Solutions revenue fell 10.0% to $758M, with the Engineered Systems unit down 36% ($255M vs $399M), where the confidential contract sits. Defense & Intelligence grew 14% ($504M vs $443M). Critical Infrastructure grew 3.0% (2% organic).

Forward-looking indicators are strong. Total backlog was $9.31B (up YoY), book-to-bill was 1.4x in Q1 2026 (Federal Solutions itself 1.4x, up from 0.9x a year earlier), and RUPO was $7.1B. The book-to-bill inflection in Federal Solutions means new bookings are outpacing revenue burn even as the confidential contract winds down, which supports the transient-air-pocket interpretation over a structural demand collapse.

The balance sheet has been levered up for the strategic pivot. Total debt rose to $1.51B at 3/31/26 from $1.24B at YE2025, driven by $274M drawn on the revolver plus $330M for the Altamira acquisition. Cash fell to $284M. Net debt of ~$1.23B against $2.75B equity leaves leverage around 2x EBITDA, moderate for a government-services contractor. The capital structure includes $800M of 2.625% convertible notes due 2029 (conversion price ~$94.11, well out of the money), a $450M term loan due 2028, and a $750M revolver due 2030, all with comfortable maturity spacing after the June 2025 refinancing. Goodwill sits at $2.42B and rising with M&A.

Operating cash flow was –$3.7M in Q1, which is seasonally normal for the DSO cycle, but net DSO rose to 72 days (+14 days) on the confidential-contract wind-down and Middle East collection delays, a working-capital watch item. Capital allocation is buyback-only: ~$240M executed cumulatively at an average $58.19 (essentially at today's price), with $90M remaining. Buybacks primarily offset ESOP dilution rather than materially shrinking the share count.

Financial health scores 6.4. The franchise generates real profit and margins are holding, leverage is manageable, and maturities are termed out. But the FCF picture is pressured by rising DSO, debt is climbing to fund M&A, and GAAP earnings are being eroded by acquisition-related charges, a solid but not fortress profile.

## Growth Analysis

The growth debate hinges on one question: can the lost confidential contract be replaced? On the negative side, this was a program exceeding 20% of a customer set, cut after a specific government reorganization, and its wind-down is the primary reason revenue is falling. On the positive side, the rest of the portfolio is growing at healthy rates — Defense & Intelligence +14%, Critical Infrastructure +3% — and the 1.4x book-to-bill plus record backlog suggest demand outside the confidential program is robust.

Management's response is an aggressive M&A pivot toward defense and intelligence, funded by the strong pre-pivot balance sheet: Altamira Technologies (closed 1/14/2026, ~$340M cash plus $45M earnout, adding analytics, SIGINT, cyber, missile warning, space, and IC customers, contributing $39.8M in its stub quarter); Chesapeake Technology International (6/30/2025, $91.5M, all-domain/EW products); TRS Group (1/2025, $36.6M, environmental remediation); and Applied Sciences Consulting (10/2025, $28.2M, Florida water/stormwater). The explicit strategy is to backfill the State-exposed revenue with higher-growth, higher-quality intel and defense-tech work.

Consensus estimates reflect a modest recovery: current-year EPS of $3.31 (revised +2.4% over 90 days) rising to $3.69 next year. Near-term revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative — current-quarter EPS was cut 6.9% over 90 days to $0.76 — which signals no acceleration in the immediate term. The estimate picture shows stabilization, not re-acceleration. The earnings surprise record is favorable (three beats and one miss over four quarters, most recently a 14.1% Q1 2026 beat), which lends some confidence to the current-year number.

Growth prospects score 5.7. The underlying franchise demand is genuine and the backlog is a record, but headline growth remains negative pending the M&A-driven backfill, and the near-term estimate trend does not yet confirm the turn.

## Valuation Assessment

At $58.73 on trailing EPS of ~$2.20, PSN trades at roughly 27x trailing. That trailing figure is depressed by the confidential-contract wind-down and acquisition-related GAAP charges, so forward metrics are more relevant. On consensus current-year EPS of $3.31, the forward P/E is ~17.7x; on next-year $3.69, ~15.9x. For a government-services contractor with a record backlog, 1.4x book-to-bill, and a growing intel/cyber/space mix, mid-to-high-teens forward earnings is a reasonable-to-modestly-attractive multiple, roughly in line with defense-services peers like Booz Allen, CACI, and Leidos, which typically trade 15-20x forward.

The listed peer set (AAR, AeroVironment, Firefly, Intuitive Machines, Hexcel) is a poor comparable: several carry negative or triple-digit P/Es and belong to different subsectors (aerospace hardware, space, composites), making the 64.7x peer-median P/E uninformative for a services firm. The more appropriate benchmark is the government-IT/services group, against which PSN's ~16-18x forward is unremarkable but not stretched.

The stock sits at the analyst target floor: consensus low $58.00, median $69.00, mean $69.18, high $80.00. Trading at the bottom of the sell-side range, with a median target ~17% above spot, argues the downside is largely discounted. This is not a deep-value or clear-catalyst mispricing. The cheapness reflects genuine uncertainty about revenue replacement, and a compressed multiple in the face of declining headline revenue is the value-trap configuration that warrants skepticism absent a catalyst. The catalyst here — the D&I pivot converting backlog to reported growth — is plausible but unproven. Buybacks have been executed at an average $58.19, essentially today's price, a mild signal that the company itself views current levels as fair value rather than a screaming discount.

Valuation scores 6.0. The forward multiple is fair-to-slightly-cheap for the quality and backlog, and the stock is at the target floor, but the recovery case is not yet confirmed enough to warrant a high score, and cheapness on falling headline revenue deserves caution rather than a reflexive buy.

## Competitive Landscape

Parsons competes across a fragmented field spanning niche technical firms to large government-services primes: Leidos, SAIC, Booz Allen, CACI, and KBR on the federal side; AECOM and Jacobs on infrastructure. Its differentiation is the blend of security-cleared federal technology work with global critical-infrastructure engineering, a combination few competitors replicate at scale. The board is deep in defense/intel credentials (former DoD acquisition chief Ellen Lord, former NGA director Letitia Long, former TRANSCOM commander Darren McDew), which supports business development in the highest-value classified segments.

Trajectory is mixed. Defense/intelligence, cyber, and space spending is a structural tailwind — evidenced by +14% D&I growth and the 1.4x federal book-to-bill — consistent with a broader defense budget favoring intel/space priorities. Against that, State Department and foreign-affairs reorganization plus federal efficiency drives (DOGE-style reviews, continuing resolutions, terminate-for-convenience risk) are direct headwinds, evidenced by the confidential-contract wind-down. The M&A strategy is explicitly repositioning the portfolio toward the higher-growth end and away from State-exposed work, which improves the mix if integration succeeds but concentrates $2.1B of goodwill in Federal Solutions and adds execution risk. The fixed-price mix falling to 31.6% from 37% (toward lower-margin but lower-risk cost-plus at 43.4%) also shifts the risk profile.

Competitive position scores 5.8. The moat is real and the intel/cyber positioning is strengthening, but the loss of a >20%-of-a-customer-set program is a genuine share/revenue setback, and the trajectory is transitional rather than clearly improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer and contract concentration** is the central risk. The 10-K discloses one federal customer set exceeded 20% of 2025 revenue, and the second-year option on the large confidential contract was significantly reduced and is winding down after the State reorganization. This single program drives the Engineered Systems and Federal Solutions declines. The specific program is not disclosed and should not be assumed.

**Government budget/appropriations risk** is elevated in the current environment: continuing resolutions, DoD/State reorganizations, federal-efficiency cuts, and shutdowns can defer or cancel backlog, and contracts are terminable for convenience. This sector-wide procurement-reform pressure redistributes value away from civilian/State-exposed work toward defense/intel/space, which cuts both ways for Parsons: its Engineered Systems/State exposure is the loser, its D&I book the winner.

**Middle East collection risk** (recurring DSO delays) and **integration/goodwill risk** (five deals in fifteen months, $2.1B of Federal Solutions goodwill, preliminary ATC purchase accounting) round out the material items. DCAA audit risk is standard for the sector. The Q1 2026 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors since the 10-K.

Two litigation items are watch points with potential upside: a ~$102.5M judgment (plus $34M pre-judgment interest) against Alstom in the Peninsula Corridor dispute is on appeal with oral argument expected in 2026, a possible cash event; and a False Claims Act qui tam case dismissed on summary judgment is under appeal (argument week of May 4, 2026).

Risk factors score 5.2. The concentration risk is specific and material, DSO is rising, and the levered M&A pivot adds integration exposure; these are not fully priced binary comforts, though the stock's drawdown has discounted a meaningful portion of the concentration concern.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75-80):** The confidential-contract loss is a discrete, transient hit that is already priced. Underlying demand is strong — D&I +14%, book-to-bill 1.4x, record backlog — and the Altamira/CTI acquisitions backfill the lost State revenue with higher-quality, higher-growth intel/SIGINT/space work. Margins hold or expand as the mix shifts toward technology content. The four-insider open-market buying cluster near $49-51 signals leadership conviction that the trough is in. Revenue re-inflects positive by late 2026/2027, estimates begin rising, and the stock re-rates toward the analyst high of $80 on ~18-20x $3.69+ forward EPS plus M&A accretion. A favorable Alstom appeal outcome adds a ~$136M cash catalyst.

**Bear Case ($45-50):** The confidential contract is a structural loss of a mega-program that the growing-but-smaller D&I and Critical Infrastructure units cannot fully replace organically, forcing continued reliance on levered, integration-heavy M&A. Debt keeps rising, GAAP earnings stay pressured by amortization and acquisition costs, DSO deterioration and Middle East collection delays crimp free cash flow, and integration missteps or goodwill impairment surface. Federal budget pressure and terminate-for-convenience risk claim additional programs. Revenue stays negative through 2026, estimates get cut, and the stock retests the $46.88 low, roughly 20% below current levels.

The balance of evidence favors the transient interpretation. The book-to-bill inflection, record backlog, margin resilience, and insider buying are hard to reconcile with a broken franchise, but the M&A-dependent replacement path carries genuine execution risk that keeps this a hold rather than a conviction buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold): we see a plausible 12-month appreciation path anchored on backlog conversion and the D&I pivot, but the revenue-replacement thesis needs confirmation before it warrants a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 29, 2026 Q2 earnings report is the near-term decision point, with the FY27 federal appropriations process and subsequent quarterly prints as follow-ons. The Alstom appeal (oral argument expected 2026) is a potential upside cash catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 shows revenue decline moderating toward flat, or total revenue re-inflecting positive, with Federal Solutions book-to-bill sustained above 1.2x.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turn positive (+5% or more over a quarter), confirming the M&A backfill is converting to earnings.
- Evidence that Altamira/CTI are integrating cleanly (segment margin stable-to-up, no goodwill write-down flags), or a pullback toward the low-$50s that widens the risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second material contract loss or a further reduction in the confidential program's scope beyond the current wind-down.
- Goodwill impairment in Federal Solutions or a guidance cut tied to integration shortfalls.
- DSO continuing to climb past ~80 days with operating cash flow deteriorating on a trailing-twelve-month basis, signaling working-capital stress rather than seasonal timing.

**Base case target: $65-68.** Applying ~18x to consensus current-year EPS of $3.31 gives ~$60, and ~17-18x next-year $3.69 gives $63-66; adding modest M&A accretion and the record backlog supports a $65-68 midpoint, roughly 11-16% above the current $58.73 and consistent with the $69 analyst median. Bull case $75-80 (+28-36%). Bear case $46-50 (–15% to –22%, retesting the 52-week low). Timeframe: 12 months, with the July 29 print the first checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Parsons is a quality government-services franchise navigating a concentrated revenue shock. The 34% drawdown from the high reflects a real event, the wind-down of a large State-linked confidential contract, but the underlying business is growing, margins are holding, the backlog is at a record, and the four-insider open-market buying cluster near current levels is a conviction signal that contradicts a broken-business narrative. At ~16-18x forward earnings and sitting at the analyst target floor, the downside appears largely discounted.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is that the recovery is not yet confirmed in the reported numbers: headline revenue is still negative, near-term estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-down, GAAP earnings are pressured by the levered M&A pivot, and DSO is rising. The company is executing a credible but unproven strategy to replace State-exposed volume with higher-quality intel/defense work, and the July 29 Q2 print is the decisive test of whether backlog is converting to growth. With overall score 6.05 and a growth-hold thesis, the risk/reward is balanced-to-modestly-favorable; we would look to upgrade on confirmation of revenue stabilization or a pullback into the low-$50s, and we would prefer to add after the earnings-driven volatility crush rather than into a chain pricing a ±14% move.
