**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Oshkosh is a Wisconsin-based specialty-vehicle manufacturer with leading positions in three distinct end-markets: aerial work platforms and telehandlers (Access/JLG), fire apparatus and refuse vehicles (Vocational/Pierce, McNeilus), and tactical defense plus USPS delivery vehicles (Transport). The business is financially conservative: investment grade across all three agencies, debt/total-capital of roughly 20%, $618M of FY2025 free cash flow, and a twelve-year record of double-digit dividend increases. The durable quality of the franchise and balance sheet is not in dispute.

The near-term operating picture has deteriorated, and the data confirms rather than reverses the declining score trajectory of prior reports. Revenue fell 2.9% in FY2025 to $10.42B as the profit-engine Access segment contracted 13% with margins compressing 440bps. The deterioration accelerated in Q1 2026: revenue was flat at $2.32B but operating income collapsed to $82M (3.5% margin) from $175M (7.6%), and EPS fell to $0.68 from $1.72. Management described conditions as "more challenging and dynamic than anticipated" while maintaining a full-year guide ($10.90 GAAP / $11.50 adjusted EPS) that is explicitly back-half-weighted (~30% of EPS in H1) and partly contingent on a follow-on USPS NGDV order that has not been confirmed. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 17.8% over 30 days, and full-year estimates are drifting down.

The stock has de-rated roughly 22% from its February 52-week high of $180.49 to $141, which improves the entry point and provides valuation and capital-return support. The multiple compression reflects a genuine earnings down-cycle rather than a temporary dislocation. With falling estimates, a ~$200M 2026 tariff drag, a specific NGDV deferred-cost impairment tail (~$125M+), and a guide whose credibility now rests on an aggressive second-half ramp, the setup is balanced. We rate OSK a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Oshkosh operates three reportable segments serving largely separate cycles:

- **Access (43% of FY25 sales, $4.49B):** JLG aerial work platforms and telehandlers plus Jerr-Dan towing/recovery vehicles, sold primarily to equipment-rental companies and contractors. This is the company's profit engine, but it is the most cyclical segment, levered to non-residential construction and rental-company capex. Roughly 78% of company goodwill sits here. FY25 operating margin fell to 11.2% from 15.6%.
- **Vocational (36%, $3.73B):** Pierce/Maxi-Metal fire apparatus, Oshkosh AeroTech airport ground-support equipment, McNeilus refuse/recycling vehicles, IMT cranes, and concrete mixers. The current growth and margin-expansion story, with FY25 operating margin up 270bps to 14.7% and a $6.6B fire-apparatus backlog.
- **Transport (20%, $2.10B):** Oshkosh Defense tactical wheeled vehicles plus the USPS Next Generation Delivery Vehicle (NGDV). Lowest margin (FY25 3.7%); roughly 20% of total company sales go to the U.S. government.

Competitive advantages rest on strong niche brands (JLG, Pierce) with leading share, a $14.5B multi-year backlog, engineered-to-order products with switching costs, and a hybrid direct/dealer distribution model. Principal competitors include Terex/Genie and Caterpillar in Access, Rosenbauer and REV Group in fire/vocational, and the broad defense prime universe in Transport.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $8.28B (FY22) → $9.66B (FY23) → $10.73B (FY24, peak) → $10.42B (FY25, -2.9%). The FY25 decline was driven entirely by Access (-13%, ~-$670M) and the JLTV defense wind-down (-$687M in Transport), partly offset by Vocational (+12.6%) and the NGDV ramp (+$365M).

**Profitability is compressing across two consecutive periods.** Consolidated operating margin fell to 9.0% in FY25 (from 9.4%), gross margin -90bps to 17.5%. Access Q1 operating margin fell to 3.7% from 10.8% on adverse mix and tariff-driven material costs; Vocational fell to 10.3% from 13.6% on fire-apparatus delivery shortfalls (weather, throughput, facility delays) and refuse weakness. The margin bridge is mix- and tariff-driven rather than a pricing failure: Oshkosh captured $36M of price in Q1 and $69M in FY25, but adverse mix and fixed-cost under-absorption swamp those gains.

**Cash flow and balance sheet remain a clear strength.** FY25 operating cash flow was $783M (up $233M, aided by lower tax payments and higher customer advances) and FCF $618M, against modest $165M capex. FCF/net income conversion of ~96% signals high-quality earnings. The balance sheet is conservatively capitalized: total debt $1.10B against $4.53B equity, debt/total-cap ~20%, investment grade (BBB/Baa3/BBB). The headline D/E of 2.22 and the "grey zone" Altman Z (2.54) overstate risk; both are distorted by ~$2B of customer advances and trade payables, which are operating liabilities for an order-backed manufacturer rather than financial leverage. Current ratio 1.94. Liquidity is ample after the March 2026 refinancing into a 5-year, $1.6B revolver maturing 2031.

**Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly.** $278M of buybacks in FY25 (2.3M shares, 7.6M remaining authorized), dividend raised 11.8% to $0.57/quarter (12th consecutive year of double-digit increases, ~1.6% yield). One overhang to monitor: a January 2026 DoD procurement executive order is flagged in the risk factors as potentially limiting defense contractors' ability to repurchase shares or pay dividends.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest dimension and the one most responsible for the declining score trajectory. Revenue is contracting off the FY24 peak, and the forward path is bifurcated. Vocational offers genuine secular tailwinds: a supply-constrained, late-cycle fire-apparatus market where Pierce holds a leading position and a $6.6B backlog still being worked down at favorable pricing. That strength was undercut in Q1 by delivery shortfalls, and refuse-unit backlog is down ~44% YoY, a worrying demand signal within the segment.

Access, the profit engine, is rolling over. FY25 sales fell 13% and segment backlog fell ~30%; non-residential construction is expected to be roughly flat in 2026, and rental customers are cautious. Transport faces the JLTV cliff offset by the NGDV ramp, but NGDV economics are unproven (see Risk Assessment).

The decisive issue is the credibility of the 2026 guide. Management reaffirmed ~$11.0B sales and ~$10.90 GAAP EPS after a Q1 they described as worse than anticipated, with ~70% of EPS pushed to H2. That requires Access price/cost improvement, higher fire-apparatus and NGDV production, better Transport pricing, and an unconfirmed follow-on NGDV order delivering a favorable cumulative catch-up. The estimate-revision data argues against the bull view: current-quarter consensus EPS was cut 17.8% over 30 days and full-year estimates are drifting down 2-3%. This is the opposite of the positive estimate-revision velocity that would support a more constructive growth score. Earnings momentum is negative, and growth visibility is low until the H2 ramp proves out.

## Valuation Assessment

OSK trades at ~15.5x trailing EPS of $10.02 and ~13.0x the FY26 GAAP guide of $10.90 (~10.1x next-year consensus of $14.04). On absolute multiples this looks inexpensive, and the de-rating has reset the entry point.

Against peers, the discount is largely earned. Peer median P/E in Construction Machinery & Heavy Transportation is 20.9x, but the peer set is heterogeneous (Terex 43.3x, Trinity 10.8x, GATX 18.5x). Oshkosh's discount reflects a real cyclical earnings decline and falling estimates rather than a clean mispricing. The valuation framework here cautions against treating a low multiple as a buy signal absent an independent catalyst, and the catalysts on offer (an H2 ramp, an unconfirmed USPS order) are contingent rather than confirmed.

The cycle-adjusted argument cuts against a typical recovery story. This is a stock coming off peak earnings with falling estimates, not a stock at trough earnings with rapidly rising estimates. Trailing EPS of $10.02 may itself overstate near-term earnings power if the H2 recovery disappoints. Normalized mid-cycle earnings power is plausibly in the $11-13 range, supporting a fair value in the high-$140s to mid-$160s at a 13-14x multiple, close to where the stock trades, implying balanced rather than asymmetric upside. The analyst consensus mean of $162 and median of $168 sit ~15-19% above spot, but with estimates falling these targets are at risk of being trimmed rather than treated as floors. The 1.6% dividend yield and buyback provide a downside cushion but do not by themselves justify a high valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

The franchise quality is durable, but trajectory matters more than level, and several lines are softening. In Access, Oshkosh faces pricing pressure from international (notably Chinese) competitors importing below market, and the FY25 loss of the Caterpillar-branded telehandler agreement removed volume. The 30% drop in Access backlog signals share is being defended into a weakening market with higher discounts (+$118M in FY25). Customer concentration is a vulnerability: the Access segment's ten largest debtors are ~27% of consolidated gross receivables, some of them highly leveraged rental companies.

Vocational is the strongest competitive position: Pierce holds leading share in a supply-constrained fire-apparatus market with a long, well-priced backlog and genuine pricing power. Refuse is weakening (-44% backlog). Defense is subject to budget cycles and an increasingly aggressive DoD posture on re-competition and design rights, which erodes the historical incumbency moat. The NGDV is a "show-me" program: large in scale but unproven in economics. The moat is intact in fire apparatus, eroding at the margin in Access, and contested in defense. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-declining, consistent with the prior report's downgrade.

## Risk Assessment

**NGDV/USPS deferred-cost overhang (most company-specific risk).** The contract allows up to 165,000 units over 10 years, but only 51,500 have been ordered. Oshkosh carries $813M of deferred contract costs (engineering $492M, tooling $270M, factory setup $51M) and discloses that deferred costs exceed future profits on existing orders by ~$135M (Dec 2025) / ~$125M (Mar 2026). If the USPS orders fewer units or terminates for convenience, a material impairment is possible. The 2026 guide explicitly assumes a follow-on order that would trigger a favorable catch-up: a single point of failure for both the impairment risk and the earnings ramp.

**Access cyclicality.** The profit engine is in a confirmed downturn (FY25 sales -13%, margin -440bps, backlog -30%) into a flat-to-soft 2026 construction backdrop.

**Tariffs.** Cost $35M in FY25 ($0.42/share), guided to ~$200M in 2026 (+$165M). A February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling generated a $19.7M refund receivable, but Section 232 expansion is offsetting the benefit; net 2026 impact is roughly in line with prior expectations.

**Government dependence and EAC accounting.** ~20-22% of sales to the U.S. government expose the company to re-competition, continuing resolutions, and the January 2026 procurement executive order that could constrain buybacks/dividends. Transport's percentage-of-completion accounting produced unfavorable cumulative catch-up adjustments of -$37.9M (2025) and -$46.9M (2024), a recurring judgment-driven earnings risk.

**Goodwill concentration.** $1.45B goodwill, 78% in JLG/Access, the segment now in cyclical decline. Pratt Miller has already been fully impaired.

These risks are partly priced into the de-rated stock, but the NGDV impairment and the back-half guidance risk are specific, near-term, and asymmetric to the downside, warranting a below-average risk score.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced and does not flag elevated downside fear. Near-term ATM IV of 42.6% sits only modestly above 1-year HV of 38.9% (rich by 3.7pp), with IV in the 2nd percentile of its own chain; uncertainty is low relative to recent history. Put/call skew is a balanced 1.9%, the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.30 to 0.70 over the past month, and the term structure is flat. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.8%. This is consistent with a market that has digested the Q1 miss and is not pricing acute near-term stress. Short interest is unremarkable at 5.0% of float (3 days to cover). The benign options posture argues against an imminent capitulation but offers no contrarian bullish setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175-197).** The H2 ramp materializes: Access price/cost actions stick, fire-apparatus throughput normalizes, the USPS issues a follow-on NGDV order delivering a favorable catch-up, and full-year EPS lands near guide. Estimate revisions turn positive, the multiple re-rates toward the peer median, and the stock recovers toward analyst targets of $162-197. The strong balance sheet, 12-year dividend growth record, and ongoing buybacks provide a floor and amplify per-share recovery.

**Bear Case ($105-120).** The construction cycle stays weak, Access margins remain depressed, the H2 ramp disappoints, no NGDV follow-on order arrives, and the deferred-cost asset takes a ~$125M+ impairment. FY26 EPS comes in well below guide (closer to $9), estimates continue falling, and the stock revisits the FY25 lows around $106-120. The defense executive order constrains capital returns, removing the buyback cushion.

**Base Case (~$150).** Mixed execution: Vocational strength partly offsets Access weakness, the NGDV order eventually arrives but later than hoped, and FY26 EPS lands modestly below guide near $10. The stock grinds in a $140-160 range as the market waits for the cycle to turn, supported by the dividend and balance sheet but capped by negative near-term earnings momentum.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The thesis is genuinely two-sided: a high-quality, cash-generative franchise at a reasonable multiple, set against a confirmed earnings down-cycle, falling estimates, and a credibility-testing guide. We lack sufficient confirmation of the H2 recovery to justify a BUY, and the balance-sheet quality and valuation support argue against an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 results confirm the H2 ramp: Access operating margin recovering toward double digits and consolidated operating margin back above 7%.
- A confirmed follow-on NGDV/USPS order that de-risks the deferred-cost asset and validates the 2026 guide.
- Estimate-revision trend turns positive (current-quarter and full-year consensus stabilizing or rising), with the stock still below ~14x normalized EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive guidance miss or a formal cut to the FY26 guide.
- Recognition of a material NGDV deferred-cost impairment ($100M+).
- Access backlog continuing to fall and refuse weakness spreading, signaling the construction down-cycle is deepening rather than bottoming.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), which will test whether the back-half ramp is on track and whether the follow-on NGDV order has materialized.

## Conclusion

Oshkosh is a financially sound, shareholder-friendly industrial trading at a reasonable multiple after a ~22% de-rating. The data confirms the declining trajectory of recent reports rather than reversing it: revenue is contracting off a peak, margins compressed for two straight periods, Q1 EPS fell ~60% and missed estimates, consensus is being cut, and the full-year guide leans heavily on an aggressive H2 ramp and an unconfirmed USPS order. The cheapness is real but earned: there is no independent catalyst forcing a re-rating, and the specific risks (NGDV impairment, tariff drag, Access cyclicality) are weighted to the downside in the near term. The strong balance sheet and capital returns cushion the bear case but do not create the asymmetric upside a BUY requires. We rate OSK a HOLD and would revisit on confirmation of the H2 recovery or a deeper de-rating that restores genuine margin of safety.
