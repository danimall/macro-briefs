## Executive Summary

Concentra operates the largest occupational health footprint in the United States — 632 stand-alone centers across 41 states and 411 employer-worksite onsite clinics across 45 states as of March 31, 2026. The business works: Q1 2026 revenue grew 13.7% to $569.6M, cost of services improved to 70.1% of revenue from 71.3%, adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 69bps to 21.2%, and management raised full-year guidance across revenue ($2.275-2.375B), adjusted EBITDA ($460-480M) and free cash flow ($215-235M). Credit metrics are comfortable: 3.4x net leverage against a 6.5x covenant, a $434.2M undrawn revolver, and total 2026 maturities of $11.2M with no meaningful wall until the term loan's 2031 balloon and the 6.875% notes' July 2032 maturity.

What has changed since our May BUY is the price, not the business. The stock has advanced from $23.60 on the day of the Q1 report to $31.12, a 32% move, against a 2.1% upward revision to full-year consensus EPS ($1.48 to $1.51) and downward revisions of 4.5-5.5% to the next two quarterly estimates. At 20.6x 2026 consensus EPS, 18.4x 2027, 11.7x guided EBITDA and a 5.65% pre-M&A free cash flow yield, the multiple now discounts the guidance rather than the guidance discounting the multiple. Analyst consensus mean of $33.375 sits 7.2% above the close, with the low target at $28 — a distribution that leaves little room for the stock to work without upward estimate revisions the current data does not yet show.

Three specific items argue against adding here. First, the raised guidance embeds essentially no margin expansion for the final nine months of 2026: implied Q2-Q4 adjusted EBITDA of $349.3M on implied revenue of $1,755.4M is a 19.9% margin, versus 19.8% in the comparable 2025 period, after Q1 delivered +69bps. Second, organic growth is 6.3%, not 13.7%, and the half of the visit base tied to gross hiring flows — employer services, 52% of center visits — grew organic volume 0.7% in Q1 into a labor market that management characterized as "low-hire, low-fire" with employer services volume "still below historical averages." Third, three overlapping physical-therapy billing matters — a DOJ False Claims Act investigation, a California Department of Insurance subpoena, and a qui tam unsealed November 10, 2025 alleging that referral guidelines produced false claims — all target the same practice inside the workers' compensation line that generates roughly 65% of center revenue, with no disclosed reserve and no quantified exposure. Near-term options are pricing a ±8.9% move with ATM implied volatility at 51.2% against 26.7% realized, into a Q2 print scheduled after the close on August 6.

We move to HOLD with a 5.4 overall score, continuing a declining trajectory (6.6 → 6.2 → 5.7 → 5.4) that reflects multiple expansion outpacing earnings revisions rather than operating deterioration.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1979, Concentra separated from Select Medical via IPO in July 2024. It delivers occupational health services in 47 states and the District of Columbia through three segments aggregated into one reportable segment:

- **Occupational health centers** — $519.9M of Q1 2026 revenue (91%), up 9.9% in absolute dollars but down from 95% of the mix a year earlier.
- **Onsite health clinics** — $37.2M (7%), up from 3%, following the June 2025 acquisition of Pivot Onsite Innovations for $55.2M, which added 240+ clinics.
- **Other** — $12.5M (2%): telemedicine, pharmacy, medical compliance administration.

The economics inside the centers segment are the defining feature. Workers' compensation was 46% of Q1 2026 visits but 65% of visit-related revenue, at $213.27 revenue per visit. Employer services (pre-employment physicals, drug screens, clinical testing) was 52% of visits but 33% of revenue, at $96.91 per visit, and management stated on the Q1 call that it carries "much lower contribution margins." Consequently roughly two-thirds of center revenue is priced by state workers' compensation fee schedules rather than negotiated. Concentra is a price-taker on its most profitable service line. Management indicated that 75-80% of annual fee-schedule changes land in Q1, that the long-run average increase has been about 3% per year, that a California increase took effect March 1, 2026, and that Tennessee updates in Q2.

Concentra does not own the medical practices. It operates through affiliated professional medical groups consolidated as variable interest entities — $294.8M of VIE assets (principally receivables) and $65.3M of liabilities at March 31, 2026, with $217.2M owed to Concentra under management agreements. This is the standard corporate-practice-of-medicine structure, and it means much of the receivable base sits in entities controlled contractually rather than owned.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $2,163.4M with adjusted EBITDA of $431.9M (20.0% margin) and consolidated net income of $172.8M. Net income grew only 0.6% versus adjusted EBITDA growth of 14.6%, which is a financing-structure lap rather than an operating problem: the $650M senior notes were issued July 11, 2024 and the term loan closed July 26, 2024, so FY2024 carried roughly five and a half months of the new capital structure against twelve in FY2025. Q1 2025 interest expense of $25.5M implies roughly $103-104M for FY2025 against perhaps $47M of comparable FY2024 interest, an incremental drag near $55M that broadly reconciles the flat net income. The lap reversed in Q1 2026: net income of $52.3M was up 28.7% on 13.7% revenue growth.

**Q1 2026 detail.** Revenue $569.6M; cost of services 70.1% of revenue (from 71.3%); G&A 9.7% (worse than 9.3%), with adjusted G&A at 8.8% versus 8.2% — the deliberate cost of building standalone corporate infrastructure post-separation. Income from operations $95.6M (16.8% margin, from 16.0%); interest expense $26.0M; GAAP diluted EPS $0.39; adjusted EPS $0.40 versus $0.33; effective tax rate 24.9%. Total visits 3,419,091 (+6.7%) on equal business days; revenue per visit $151.47 (+3.1%).

**Cash flow.** Q1 operating cash flow was $21.0M against $11.1M of capex, producing $9.9M of company-defined free cash flow versus negative $4.0M. Q1 is structurally the weakest quarter — accrued payroll fell from $89.5M to $49.3M on the annual incentive payout and accrued interest from $21.4M to $9.8M on the January 15 coupon, so cash interest paid was $36.7M against $26.0M of expense. Annualizing Q1 would be wrong. The full-year FCF guidance of $215-235M reconciles credibly: adjusted EBITDA midpoint $470M, less roughly $105M of cash interest (the $650M notes at 6.875% = $44.7M plus a $940.5M term loan at SOFR plus spread, partially hedged), less capex midpoint $75M, less roughly $65M of cash taxes, approximates $225M before working capital and non-controlling distributions. That implies 48% conversion of adjusted EBITDA, respectable for a leveraged, lease-heavy services business, though note the company's FCF definition excludes business combinations and therefore is measured before the growth strategy consumes cash.

One item to watch in the Q2 10-Q: accounts receivable built $38.6M in Q1 2026 against $21.1M in Q1 2025, an 83% larger build on 13.7% revenue growth. Balance-sheet AR moved from $257.9M to $296.5M, approximately 46.9 days on Q1 revenue. Pivot's direct employer billing terms and growth timing are plausible explanations, so we would not yet call this collections deterioration, but the trend needs to reverse.

**Balance sheet.** At March 31, 2026: cash $61.7M, total assets $2,889.8M, goodwill $1,480.1M (51.2% of assets), intangibles $239.0M, operating lease ROU assets $500.6M against $546.5M of lease liabilities, total debt principal $1,597.2M, total equity including NCI $432.2M. Net debt $1,535.5M against management's stated TTM adjusted EBITDA of $450M equals 3.41x net, consistent with the 3.4x credit-agreement calculation and well inside the 6.5x covenant. Interest coverage was 3.68x on Q1 operating income and 4.64x on Q1 adjusted EBITDA. Hedges fix $300M at 3.829% SOFR and collar another $300M (4.50% cap / 3.001% floor) through February 2028, leaving $640.5M variable at a 3.66% SOFR reference.

Tangible book equity is negative $1,286.9M ($432.2M equity less $1,719.1M of goodwill and intangibles). This is normal for an LBO-and-roll-up lineage and is not a cash risk, but it means there is no asset cushion and a goodwill impairment would be a very large book-equity event. An automated Altman Z-Score reading of ~1.1 on this company is mis-specified; recomputing from the FY2025 balance sheet and current market equity gives roughly 2.19 — grey zone, not distress. The model penalizes goodwill-heavy, lease-heavy service roll-ups by construction. The legitimate balance-sheet questions are 2031-2032 refinancing and goodwill impairment, not near-term solvency.

**Rate sensitivity.** With $640.5M of unhedged variable-rate term loan, a 100bp rise in SOFR costs about $6.4M pretax, roughly $0.04 of EPS or 2.5% of 2026 consensus. [unverified] The company states that a hypothetical 25bp move would not be material to annual interest expense. It is directionally adverse for the deleveraging that management has made a precondition for larger M&A.

## Growth Analysis

**The organic picture is materially softer than the headline, and management disclosed it clearly.** Excluding Nova (closed March 2025) and Pivot (June 2025) from both periods, Q1 2026 revenue grew 6.3% versus 13.7% reported, visits per day grew 2.9% versus 6.7%, workers' compensation visits grew 6.2% with revenue per visit up 1.3%, employer services visits grew 0.7% with revenue up 3.2%, and onsite clinics ex-Pivot grew 20.9%. So roughly 6.3 points of the 13.7% were organic and 7.4 points inorganic. From Q2 2026, Nova is in the base and the reported growth rate converges toward organic plus small tuck-ins.

**Guidance implies the Q1 margin expansion does not repeat.** Implied Q2-Q4 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $349.3M against $329.2M in the comparable 2025 period is +6.1% growth, versus Q1's +17.6%; implied revenue growth is +5.6%; implied margin is 19.9% versus 19.8%, roughly ten basis points. The revenue deceleration is mechanical. The margin flattening is not. Q1 benefited from the easiest weather comparison of the year (management attributed part of the workers' comp volume strength to more ice and snow versus a dry prior-year winter, with the Northeast strongest) while separation-related G&A steps up through the summer technology cutover. Consensus corroborates: full-year $1.51 against Q1 actual $0.40 and Q2/Q3 estimates of $0.42 each leaves roughly $0.27 implied for Q4, essentially flat against the $0.28 delivered in Q4 2025.

The benign reading is conservatism. Management raised adjusted EBITDA guidance by only $10M after a quarter one sell-side analyst characterized on the call as a 10-11% beat versus consensus, and pre-released preliminary 2025 results and 2026 guidance in January — a pattern consistent with setting beatable bars. The four-quarter surprise record supports that: beats of 17.0%, 22.1% and 2.8% against one 2.6% miss.

**Where the durable growth sits.** Onsite clinics went from $64M of revenue in 2024 to an approaching $150M run rate, from 160 sites to 411, and grew 20.9% organically ex-Pivot. Every dollar is negotiated directly with an employer rather than set by a fee schedule, which improves revenue quality and reduces regulatory beta. Management sizes the serviceable market at $15-20B with only a small portion penetrated (an issuer estimate we cannot verify) and identifies "advanced primary care" as the largest white space, enabled by an Epic EMR deployment roughly eighteen months ago. Derived unit math suggests the ex-Pivot growth came mostly from deeper penetration and higher-acuity services at existing sites — onsite count rose 251 while Pivot brought 240+, implying roughly 11 net non-Pivot additions.

Two qualifications. Mix shift is not the same as reduced absolute exposure: center revenue grew 9.9% in dollars, so the fee-schedule-exposed base is expanding, only its share is falling. And onsite revenue is contracted with individual large employers, which may be more concentrated per dollar than the centers business; the company discloses no customer concentration figure for either segment.

**The macro channel matters here more than in most services businesses.** Employer services revenue is a function of gross hiring flows, not net employment. A low-hire, low-fire equilibrium suppresses both hiring and separations, which is precisely what management described ("employer services volume still below historical averages," "quit rates relatively stable or below norm"). Management also noted "total employment continues to grow, especially blue collar," and "there are fewer layoffs compared to prior year." We would expect employer services organic volume to stay near zero through H2 absent a hiring inflection. The offsetting driver management cited is construction-related injury volume, specifically AI data-center buildout, though management conceded onshoring-driven demand "is going to take some time because typically that requires some sort of capital deployment that is not going to happen overnight." Net, we treat the employer services line as flat and the workers' compensation line as share-gain dependent rather than market-growth dependent.

## Competitive Landscape

Occupational health is fragmented and locally delivered. Concentra's 632 centers plus 411 onsite clinics make it the largest operator by locations, and no competitor operates a comparable multi-state combination of stand-alone centers and worksite clinics. That density is what wins national employer RFPs and broker mindshare, and it is the reason acquired assets can be bought below 9x and folded into a platform trading at 11.7x. Nova integration and synergy capture are complete per management, tracking toward the below-7.5x underwritten multiple; Pivot is running ahead of its below-9x target.

The trajectory is mildly improving. Mix is shifting toward employer-paid onsite revenue at 21% organic growth, workers' compensation organic visits grew 6.2% with management attributing part of that to patient satisfaction and account retention (share gains), and the de novo program adds 8-10 centers in 2026 across Arizona, Idaho, Missouri, Illinois, Virginia, South Carolina and Florida.

The structural limit is pricing. On roughly two-thirds of center revenue, price is a legislative outcome averaging about 3% annually, and states can be closed by administration: management said New York adjusted evaluation-and-management codes but not physical therapy rates, leaving the state "still not as attractive as what we want," so Concentra has a pipeline but has not entered. Volume on the high-margin line depends on workplace injury frequency, which the company does not control and which the industry has generally seen decline as employment shifts away from goods production. When wage inflation runs ahead of fee-schedule increases — personnel expense was $322.2M in Q1, 56.6% of revenue — the margin compresses with no offset available.

The onsite market, by contrast, is contested by established employer-clinic specialists, and we have no filing-level or third-party evidence on Concentra's relative share there. Management's claim to be "extremely competitive" via national footprint and the broker channel is plausible and unverified.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Three converging physical-therapy billing matters (highest severity, unquantified).** A DOJ/HHS False Claims Act investigation that began in 2021 with Select and under which Concentra has produced data and made employees available for interviews; a California Department of Insurance subpoena dated February 5, 2024 covering PT billing and coding for commercial and workers' compensation claims; and a qui tam unsealed November 10, 2025 in Santa Clara County alleging that "certain physical therapy referral guidelines resulted in the Company submitting false and fraudulent claims." The common thread is referral guidelines, meaning a clinical-pathway question rather than a coding error. Physical therapy sits inside the workers' compensation line generating about 65% of center revenue, so an adverse outcome requiring changed referral intensity would affect the operating model, not just produce a charge. No reserve is disclosed and no damages figure is public. Mitigants: both the DOJ and the California DOI declined to intervene in the respective qui tam actions, generally a negative signal for relators; the Kane qui tam does not name Concentra; and none of this has affected results across five reported quarters. Critically, how the Select separation agreement allocates this liability is unverified — that allocation could make the exposure materially larger or smaller than the disclosure implies.

**2. Regulated pricing with cost inflation.** Roughly two-thirds of center revenue is fee-schedule set with a lag. Workers' comp organic revenue per visit grew 1.3% in Q1 against personnel expense at 56.6% of revenue.

**3. Labor-market sensitivity.** Employer services, 52% of center visits and described by management as "often the initial point of entry with employer customers," grew organic visits 0.7%. Persistent weakness in the low-margin funnel could eventually feed the high-margin line; no disclosure quantifies that conversion.

**4. Goodwill and negative tangible book.** $1,480.1M of goodwill against $432.2M of total equity; goodwill plus intangibles is 59.5% of assets.

**5. Separation execution.** Functional separation from Select is targeted for the end of summer 2026 ahead of a November deadline, with 95%+ of new FTEs hired. Insourced cost is running above the retired transition-services fees ($3.7M to $1.6M in TSA fees, while adjusted G&A rose 60bps).

**6. Unresolved disclosure gaps.** Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (filed April 14 and July 10, 2026) could not be retrieved. The April filing plausibly corresponds to the previously disclosed retirement of CMO Dr. John Anderson effective year-end 2026. The July 10 officer/director departure or election is unexplained, and if it involves the CEO or the combined President/CFO, the separation-execution and M&A-pipeline conclusions here would require revision. Pivot purchase accounting also remained preliminary ten months after closing with a disclosed warning that adjustments "could be significant," though the amounts are small relative to a $2.9B balance sheet.

**7. Cybersecurity.** The Perry Johnson & Associates transcription-vendor breach generated notices to nearly four million patients and a consolidated class action in E.D.N.Y. naming Concentra among others; the company does not expect materiality and is working with its cyber insurer. Professional and general liability coverage carries a $30.0M annual aggregate limit above retentions, modest for a 632-center footprint and not the relevant coverage for a false-claims resolution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $39 (+25%)

Q2 confirms that Q1's cost-of-services improvement compounds rather than reverses, with the California rate increase (effective March 1) and the Tennessee update landing in full. Management's $10M guidance raise proves conservative and full-year adjusted EBITDA reaches or exceeds the $480M high end. Employer services inflects as construction activity tied to data-center buildout adds injury volume, and onsite revenue crosses a $175M run rate at 20%+ organic growth with the advanced-primary-care offering converting. Year-end net leverage lands "comfortably below three times," which management has explicitly framed as the precondition for a larger transaction — reopening the 7.5-9x acquisition arbitrage at scale in late 2026 or 2027. The multiple holds at 12.5x on roughly $520M of 2027 EBITDA against declining net debt, yielding approximately $40 per share. The litigation cluster resolves as a monetary settlement inside insurance and cash flow, without a change to referral practice.

### Bear Case — $23 (-26%)

Q2 shows the flat-margin guidance was accurate rather than conservative, with adjusted G&A stepping up through the summer technology cutover and cost of services giving back Q1's 120bps. Organic revenue decelerates toward 4-5% as the weather comparison reverses and employer services turns negative in a low-hire, low-fire environment. Workers' comp fee-schedule increases average below 3% while personnel costs run at 4%+. Either the DOJ matter or the California referral-guidelines action reaches a resolution requiring changed clinical pathways, compressing workers' comp revenue per visit and mix. The receivable build proves to be a collections problem rather than growth timing. The multiple compresses to 9.5x on roughly $455M of EBITDA — closer to what Concentra itself pays for assets — producing approximately $22-23. A goodwill impairment, while non-cash, would take book equity toward zero and complicate the credit narrative even at 3.4x leverage.

### Base Case — $34 (+9%)

Organic growth settles at 5-7%, tuck-in M&A adds 2-4 points, and full-year adjusted EBITDA finishes near the $470-475M range. 2027 EBITDA reaches roughly $505M. Applying 11.5x — the current multiple, held flat — against net debt declining toward $1.40B yields about $34.70; 20x 2027 consensus EPS of $1.69 yields $33.80. The two methods converge on $34. Litigation remains disclosed and unquantified without resolution. The onsite segment continues compounding at roughly 20% but is too small at 7-9% of revenue to change the consolidated growth rate within the horizon.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** There is a positive twelve-month thesis with identifiable catalysts — deleveraging toward sub-3x, the onsite compounding, annual fee-schedule increases, and an M&A window reopening in late 2026 — but base-case upside of 9% against 26% bear-case downside does not justify new capital at $31.12 with a ±8.9% event priced for the next session.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Q2 evidence that margins expand rather than flatten.** Specifically: Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin at or above 21.0% (versus the ~19.9% the guidance implies for Q2-Q4) with cost of services at or below 70.5% of revenue, and a full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance raise above $480M. That would falsify the flat-margin read and support 12.5x+ on rising estimates.
2. **Price at $26-27 on unchanged fundamentals**, which would restore roughly 16x 2027 consensus EPS and a 6.7% FCF yield — an entry with a genuine margin of safety against the litigation tail.
3. **Employer services organic visits inflecting above 3%** for a quarter, confirming that the 52%-of-visits funnel has turned and removing the principal organic drag.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Any disclosed reserve, settlement framework, or corporate integrity agreement** arising from the DOJ, California DOI, or Santa Clara County matters that requires changes to physical-therapy referral practice, or a disclosed accrual above $50M.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of organic revenue growth below 4%** with adjusted EBITDA margin contracting year over year, which would break the platform-growth thesis and expose the 3.4x leverage.
3. **Confirmation that the July 10, 2026 Item 5.02 filing involves the departure of the CEO or the President/CFO**, given that the combined President/CFO role concentrates operational and financial authority in one person mid-separation.
4. Accounts receivable days above 55 with a second consecutive outsized build, indicating collections deterioration rather than growth timing.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediate. Q2 2026 results are scheduled after the close on August 6, 2026 — the first quarter with Nova fully in both bases, the first full quarter of the California rate increase, and the quarter containing the Tennessee update. We will re-score on that release. Secondary checkpoints: the Q2 10-Q for receivables, litigation notes, and completion of Pivot purchase accounting; and confirmation of functional separation from Select by end of summer.

## Conclusion

Concentra is a competently run market leader with a working consolidation model, improving revenue mix, comfortable credit metrics and management that has beaten its own guidance in three of the last four quarters. None of that is in dispute. The question is what the equity pays for it. Since our May BUY at $23.60, the share price has advanced 32% while full-year consensus EPS rose 2.1% and the next two quarterly estimates were cut 4.5-5.5%. The stock now trades at 20.6x 2026 consensus, 11.7x guided EBITDA and a 5.65% pre-acquisition free cash flow yield — 30-55% above the multiples at which management itself buys comparable cash flows — with guidance that implies roughly ten basis points of margin expansion over the final nine months, organic revenue growth of 6.3%, half the visit base flat in a low-hire, low-fire hiring environment, and three unquantified physical-therapy investigations converging on the practice that generates most of the profit.

The 5.4 overall score continues a declining trajectory that reflects multiple expansion outpacing earnings revisions. The dimensions that have historically discriminated best — financial health at 6.2 on genuine credit strength, management quality at 5.2 on well-priced buybacks and disciplined M&A offset by sustained director selling with no open-market purchases, and risk factors at 4.5 on the litigation and disclosure stack — sit in the middle-to-lower band. Existing holders should own through the August 6 print given credible guidance and a conservative setting pattern. New capital should wait for either the margin evidence that would justify a higher multiple, or a price in the $26-27 range that pays for the litigation tail.
