## Executive Summary

Reinsurance Group of America is the world's largest life and health reinsurer by revenue, with $4.3 trillion of life reinsurance in force across 26+ countries. The stock trades at $214.59 (as of early 2026), representing approximately 12.1x trailing EPS ($17.69) and 8.2x forward consensus for FY2026 ($26.08). On a trailing basis, this valuation sits at a ~33% premium to the peer median P/E of 9.1x; however, on a forward basis, the 8.2x multiple represents a discount to that same peer median, reflecting the market's caution around capital strain from the Equitable transaction and suggesting potential upside as integration concerns ease.

The primary risk is capital strain from the $32 billion Equitable Holdings transaction closed in July 2025, which prompted Moody's to place a negative outlook on the company's A1 rating. The deal contributed $68M in pre-tax income in 2025, and while the company authorized a new $500M buyback in January 2026, the buyback authorization alone does not confirm that rating-agency capital concerns are fully resolved — repurchases remain subject to market conditions, regulatory requirements, and management discretion.

The stock remains approximately 6.4% below its 52-week high of $229.21. Accelerating Asia Pacific growth, expanding investment yields (4.99%, +17 bps YoY), and a massive new business pipeline ($733.5B face amount produced in FY2025, +45% YoY) provide volume indicators consistent with continued earnings growth, though the translation from face amount production to earnings depends on mix, margins, and capital deployment.

## Company Overview

RGA operates as the world's largest pure-play life and health reinsurer, distinct from property/casualty-focused peers. The company earns revenue through two primary channels: Traditional Reinsurance (mortality/morbidity risk transfer) and Financial Solutions (asset-intensive reinsurance, pension risk transfer, longevity). Geographic breakdown: U.S. and Latin America (51% of net premiums), Asia Pacific (22%), EMEA (19%), and Canada (9%).

The business model benefits from extreme duration — reinsurance treaties often span 10-30+ years, providing embedded earnings visibility. Client relationships are deep and sticky, with 36 clients each generating over $100M in annual premiums and no single client exceeding 10% of revenues. The company maintains AA- (S&P), A+ (A.M. Best), and A1 (Moody's, negative outlook) financial strength ratings.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Trajectory:**
- Total revenue grew from $16.3B (2022) to $23.7B (2025), a 13.3% CAGR. However, net premiums actually declined from $17.8B in 2024 to $17.2B in 2025 due to fewer single-premium PRT transactions, with revenue growth primarily driven by higher net investment income and other revenues.
- Net income expanded from $623M to $1,182M over 2022-2025 (23.8% CAGR)
- Diluted EPS increased 65% YoY from $10.73 to $17.69 in FY2025
- Adjusted operating income before tax rose 12.3% YoY to $1,967M

**Investment Performance:**
Net investment income reached $5.8B in FY2025, up 31.5% from $4.4B in FY2024. Average yield on invested assets (excluding spread business) rose to 4.99%, a 17 bps improvement. The investment portfolio of $134.6B is 94.3% investment grade, though management is deliberately increasing exposure to higher-yielding private assets and CLOs. Credit losses rose from $76M to $181M YoY — a meaningful increase that warrants monitoring, particularly as RGA itself cautions that alternative investment income can fluctuate significantly and that credit/economic stress could materially affect results.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total assets of $156.6B with stockholders' equity of $13.5B. Long-term debt of $5.7B (including $700M subordinated notes issued March 2025 to fund the Equitable deal). Book value per share is approximately $205–$206 (based on $13.461B equity / ~65.5M shares), placing the stock at ~1.04x reported book. RGA's total AOCI was positive $2.682B at year-end 2025 — while $7.0B in gross unrealized AFS losses exists, other AOCI components (primarily discount-rate effects on future policy benefits) more than offset those losses. Excluding all AOCI, book value per share would be approximately $165, placing the stock at roughly 1.30x AOCI-adjusted book. The company generates a 15.7% adjusted operating ROE (excluding AOCI and notable items), indicating strong underlying profitability regardless of book-value framing.

**Capital Return:**
FY2025 shareholder returns totaled $365M ($240M dividends + $125M buybacks). The new $500M buyback authorization in January 2026 represents approximately 3.6% of current market cap. Dividend yield is 1.8%.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow was $4.1B in FY2025 (2024's $9.4B was inflated by PRT transactions). FCF generation is strong and supports both organic growth and shareholder returns.

## Growth Analysis

RGA has multiple compounding growth vectors:

**1. Asia Pacific Expansion:** Adjusted operating income grew 41% YoY to $759M, driven by business growth, a swing from 2024's adverse assumption-review impact to a small favorable result in 2025, and favorable underwriting experience in traditional business. New business production of $75.8B in Asia (up 23% YoY) suggests this region will continue driving top-line growth as insurance penetration increases.

**2. Equitable Deal Contribution:** The $32B transaction contributed $68M in pre-tax income during 2025. The transaction closed July 31, 2025, with an effective date of April 1, 2025. While a simple annualization might suggest approximately $163M in annual run-rate income, this figure should be treated as a model estimate rather than a confirmed steady-state contribution, as the economics of the block may vary over time as it matures.

**3. Pension Risk Transfer:** The global PRT market continues expanding as plan sponsors de-risk. RGA's Financial Solutions segment is a significant beneficiary of this trend.

**4. Rising Investment Yields:** With $45.6B in average invested assets (excluding spread business) earning 4.99% and trending higher, every 10 bps of yield improvement adds approximately $46M in gross pre-tax income (a simplified sensitivity that does not account for reinvestment timing, crediting-rate offsets, or variable investment income volatility).

**5. New Business Pipeline:** Face amount production of $733.5B in FY2025 (up 45% from $505.4B) indicates robust organic volume growth. While face amount is a useful volume indicator, its translation into earnings depends on product mix, pricing margins, cession economics, and capital required per unit of production.

Consensus projects EPS of $26.08 for FY2026 (up 47% from FY2025's $17.69) and $28.87 for FY2027 (+11% growth). The 90-day revision trend for next year's estimates is +3.7%, positive but not at the +30% BUY signal threshold. Near-term 30-day revisions are slightly negative (-1.2% for current year), reflecting typical post-earnings estimate settling rather than fundamental deterioration.

## Competitive Landscape

RGA holds the #1 global position in life and health reinsurance by revenue. This scale advantage compounds through several mechanisms:

**Data superiority:** $4.3 trillion in force across diverse geographies provides unmatched mortality and morbidity data for pricing accuracy. Smaller competitors cannot replicate this actuarial database.

**Relationship depth:** Direct marketing model (not broker-dependent) with 36 clients each generating >$100M annually. Switching costs in reinsurance are high — ceding companies invest years in integrating with a reinsurer's administrative and claims systems.

**Financial strength ratings:** AA-/A+ ratings are essential for large-block transactions. Only a handful of global reinsurers maintain this rating tier, creating a natural oligopoly for the largest deals.

**Threats:** Private equity-backed reinsurers (Athene/Apollo, Resolution Life) are competing aggressively for asset-intensive blocks by offering potentially higher yield assumptions. However, these competitors lack RGA's mortality risk capabilities, limiting competitive overlap to the Financial Solutions segment. The competitive position is stable to improving in Traditional business and facing moderate pressure in Financial Solutions.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Equitable Deal Capital Strain (HIGH):** Moody's negative outlook specifically cites reduced financial flexibility. If capital markets deteriorate or additional large losses emerge, the rating could be downgraded from A1 to A2, potentially limiting future transaction capacity.

**2. UK Mortality Assumptions (MODERATE-HIGH):** The $222M remeasurement loss reflects what RGA describes as "deterioration in mortality improvements and ongoing challenges within the healthcare system" in the UK. Management's characterization suggests this may represent a structural trend rather than a one-time adjustment, though future development remains uncertain. Partial natural hedge exists via longevity business ($24M benefit from same update).

**3. U.S. Claims Experience Deterioration (MODERATE):** U.S. Traditional loss ratio rose from 90% to 93%. While not yet alarming, the trend needs to stabilize.

**4. Credit Loss Acceleration (MODERATE):** Credit losses more than doubled from $76M to $181M as the portfolio shifts toward higher-yielding private assets and CLOs. RGA cautions that alternative investment income can fluctuate significantly and that credit or economic stress could materially affect results. In a recession scenario, this figure could expand materially given the $134.6B portfolio.

**5. Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Approximately $8.2B fair value reduction per 100 bps rate increase on the fixed income portfolio. While flowing through OCI rather than income, sustained unrealized losses compress reported book value and could constrain regulatory capital.

**6. Concentration in Funds Withheld ($8.1B with ~90% from two clients):** Limited control over investment decisions on assets legally owned by ceding companies creates counterparty and portfolio management risk.

## Conclusion

RGA presents a compelling risk/reward at current levels. The stock trades at 8.2x forward earnings for a business generating 15.7% adjusted operating ROE with multiple structural growth tailwinds (Asia Pacific expansion, pension risk transfer, investment yield improvement). The Equitable deal capital strain that prompted Moody's negative outlook is the primary overhang, but this concern is already reflected in the compressed forward multiple and should diminish as organic capital generation rebuilds buffers over the next 12-18 months.

The 33.5% Q4 2025 earnings beat ($7.75 vs $5.81 estimate) demonstrates the earnings power of the combined business is being systematically underestimated by consensus.

BUY with a 12-month base case target of $255, representing approximately 19% upside with 11% downside to bear case. Position sizing should be moderate (2-3%) given the elevated near-term options pricing and pending Q1 2026 earnings catalyst.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The AOCI/book-value analysis has been corrected (ex-AOCI BV is ~$165/share, not higher than reported BV), the Equitable annualized contribution ($163M) is a model estimate rather than a confirmed run-rate, and the forward P/E of 8.2x actually represents a discount (not premium) to peer median — these corrections collectively weaken part of the "undervalued" argument but strengthen the forward-multiple discount thesis.
