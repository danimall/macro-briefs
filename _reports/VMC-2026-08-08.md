## Executive Summary

Vulcan Materials is the largest U.S. producer of construction aggregates, with roughly 10% of national production, 16.6 billion tons of proven and probable reserves across ~310,000 acres, and 425 active aggregates facilities concentrated in states that Woods & Poole projects will absorb 76% of U.S. population growth through 2035. The moat is genuine and mechanically simple: aggregates have a high weight-to-price ratio, transportation can cost more than the material, and new quarry permits in urban and suburban markets take years to obtain when they can be obtained at all. That combination has let Vulcan compound adjusted EBITDA at approximately 12.5% annually from $1,451.3M in 2021 to $2,323.6M in FY2025 while same-store shipment volumes went nowhere. The engine was price and unit cost, not tons.

That engine decelerated sharply in the first half of 2026. Aggregates cash gross profit per ton grew 7% in FY2025, 1.9% in H1 2026, and 1.2% in Q2. In Q2, freight-adjusted price per ton rose $0.86 while freight-adjusted cash cost per ton rose $0.72 — 84% of the price increase was consumed by cost inflation, most of it diesel following the February 28, 2026 Middle East conflict. Consolidated Q2 adjusted EBITDA fell 1% to $654.0M and operating earnings fell 3% to $455.5M on 2.5% revenue growth. Continuing-operations diluted EPS still rose, from $2.43 to $2.47, but reconstruction of the income statement shows pretax earnings *fell* $9.7M; the increase came entirely from the effective tax rate dropping from 22.0% to 20.1% and from a 2% smaller share count. Holding shares at the prior-year 132.9M produces $2.425, below last year's $2.431.

At $284.69 the market is paying 31.1x FY2026 consensus EPS of $9.15, 26.4x FY2027 consensus of $10.79, and 17.4x trailing EV/adjusted EBITDA for a business whose operating profit is currently flat and whose consensus estimates have been cut in every measured period. Management reiterated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $2,400–2,600M, but H1 delivered $1,101.1M (+2.9%), and reaching the midpoint requires H2 growth of +11.6% against H2 2025's $1,253.2M — roughly quadrupling H1's growth rate. Layer on an unreserved LADWP groundwater contribution demand that the Q2 10-Q describes as a "reasonably possible" material near-term loss against a company-wide environmental accrual of $51.4M, plus the September 30, 2026 expiration of IIJA surface transportation authorities now covered only by a stopgap to December 11, and the risk distribution around a full multiple tilts down. We move to REDUCE from three prior HOLDs, with the overall score declining to 5.5 from 6.2.

## Company Overview

Vulcan operates three reportable segments — Aggregates, Asphalt, Concrete — with Aggregates generating approximately 90% of gross profit. FY2025 revenue was $7,941.1M on 226.8 million tons shipped; trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 30, 2026 was $8,115.7M.

Distribution is mostly local: roughly 80% of shipments move by truck directly from quarry to customer. The strategically distinctive remaining share moves by rail, barge or Panamax-class self-unloading ship from remote quarries in the Bahamas, Honduras, Mexico and British Columbia into the Gulf Coast, Eastern Seaboard, California and Hawaii — markets lacking local high-quality stone. That capability is not easily replicated and supports pricing in coastal metros.

Customer concentration is genuinely low: more than 25,000 customers, top five approximately 7% of FY2025 revenue, none above 2%. Geographic concentration is high and static: top ten states approximately 90% of revenue, top five approximately 63%, led by California, Texas, Georgia, Tennessee and Virginia. Historically 40–55% of aggregates shipments are consumed in publicly funded construction, though Vulcan sells to contractors rather than to governments directly.

The portfolio is being pruned rather than expanded. Vulcan completed no business acquisitions in 2025 after deploying $2,310.6M across 2023–2025, and only $75.0M in H1 2026 (a southern Colorado quarry and a Dallas–Fort Worth rail yard). Against that, Q2 2026 saw the sale of the California ready-mixed concrete operations and the U.S. Virgin Islands aggregates and ready-mix business for combined proceeds of $722.1M ($572.1M cash plus a $150.0M note due December 2027 recorded at $139.7M), producing a $13.2M loss, following the Q4 2025 divestiture of the Houston asphalt and paving business at a $42.4M gain. Ronnie Pruitt became CEO effective January 1, 2026, succeeding J. Thomas Hill, with $8.6M of H1 pretax charges tied to the associated organizational changes.

## Financial Analysis

**Historical trajectory.** Revenue grew from $7,315.2M (FY2022) to $7,941.1M (FY2025) while adjusted EBITDA rose from $1,625.6M to $2,323.6M and margin expanded from 22.2% to 29.3%. Net earnings attributable rose from $575.6M to $1,076.7M. Volumes did not participate: tons fell from 234.6M (2023) to 219.9M (2024), recovered to 226.8M (2025) — still 3.3% below 2023 — and the FY2025 increase was attributable to late-2024 acquisitions, with same-store shipments slightly lower. Freight-adjusted price per ton reached $21.98 (+4.3%) and cash gross profit per ton $11.33 (+7%).

**Q2 2026 unit economics — the central fact.**

| Aggregates metric | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tons (M) | 59.3 | 59.9 | +1.0% |
| Freight-adj. price/ton | $22.11 | $22.97 | +3.9% (+4.7% mix-adj.) |
| Freight-adj. cash cost/ton | $10.23 | $10.95 | +7.0% |
| Cash gross profit/ton | $11.88 | $12.02 | +1.2% |

Consolidated gross margin fell 70bp to 29.0%; adjusted EBITDA margin fell 110bp to 30.3%. Cash cost per ton rose 7% but only 3% excluding diesel, implying roughly $0.41 per ton of diesel drag, or about $24.6M on the quarter's volumes. Management's externally referenced figure of nearly $40 million of energy-related cost encompasses liquid asphalt and all segments.

The mechanism is transparent: aggregates production is diesel-intensive at the pit, in on-site haulage, and in delivery. With the Middle East conflict's disruption of energy supplies unresolved, this cost line is exposed to a macro variable Vulcan cannot hedge away, and the Q2 10-Q now names the conflict explicitly in its Known Trends section.

**Guidance arithmetic.** H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $1,101.1M grew 2.9% over H1 2025's $1,070.4M. H2 2025 was $1,253.2M. The FY2026 range therefore requires H2 growth of +3.6% (low end, $1,298.9M), +11.6% (midpoint, $1,398.9M), or +19.6% (high end). Extending H1's growth rate gives approximately $2,390.6M for the year — marginally below the guidance floor. Roughly 5% H2 growth clears the floor. The midpoint is a stretch on current trend.

There are legitimate supports for acceleration: Texas and Southeast rainfall in May and June deferred rather than destroyed volumes, and lost tons hurt unit costs disproportionately in a fixed-cost business; the ~80bp gap between reported (+3.9%) and mix-adjusted (+4.7%) price should narrow as divested lower-price mix rolls off; and diesel could relieve. Against that, the range was set in February 2026 before the conflict began, so reiteration may reflect an unchanged range with materially less cushion rather than confidence in a step-up.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 was strong: operating cash flow $1,813.0M against $1,076.7M of net earnings (1.68x conversion, consistent with ~$724M of DD&A), capex $677.7M, free cash flow $1,135.3M — 48.9% of adjusted EBITDA and a 3.08% yield on the current market capitalization. H1 2026 is weaker: operating cash flow $584.6M, down $8.6M despite net earnings rising $40.1M (a $56.9M adverse deferred-tax swing), with capex up 37% to $370.4M against $750–800M full-year guidance. H1 free cash flow of $214.2M compares to $322.3M, down 33.5%.

Seasonality matters here — H1 is structurally the weaker half and Q3 is normally the strongest. Applying FY2025's seasonal split (H1 operating cash flow was 32.7% of the year) to H1 2026 implies roughly $1,788M of full-year operating cash flow; against $775M of guided capex that is approximately $1,013M of free cash flow, a 2.7% yield. Treat that as a sensitivity rather than a forecast, since it assumes identical working-capital timing and cash taxes. A normalized case tracking the guidance low end puts free cash flow nearer $1.1B, about 3.0%. Either way, the cash yield is thin relative to the multiple.

Working capital quality is clean: days sales outstanding improved to 42.1 from 42.6, receivables over 90 days fell to $22.4M from $30.9M, and the sequential receivable build from $887.7M to $1,100.3M is the ordinary year-end-trough-to-mid-year-peak pattern (up only 2.0% against the comparable June 2025 figure on 2.5% revenue growth).

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total debt $4,364.3M, cash and restricted cash $288.7M, net debt $4,075.6M. Gross leverage 1.9x TTM adjusted EBITDA, 1.7x net — below the stated 2.0–2.5x target. All debt fixed at a 5.04% weighted-average effective rate with 13.2-year weighted-average maturity, an unusually valuable position in a higher-for-longer environment. Undrawn revolver capacity $1,581.5M to November 2029, commercial paper program undrawn, no binding financial maintenance covenants disclosed. Interest coverage is approximately 7.6x (TTM EBIT proxy $1,641.0M against $216.1M net interest). The maturity ladder is undemanding: $400.0M in Q2 2027, then $500M (2029), $750M (2030), balance to 2054. Moody's outlook moved to Positive from Stable during H1 2026, with Fitch and S&P at BBB+/Stable.

Two qualifications on asset quality. Goodwill of $3,780.9M is 23.0% of total assets; with $1,438.5M of other intangibles the combined figure is 31.7% of assets and 61.5% of total equity, leaving derived tangible book equity near $3,269.2M — roughly 11.3x price-to-tangible-book. Accumulated goodwill impairments already total $390.2M, including $86.6M in Concrete in 2024, though the FY2025 test found all units substantially above carrying value. Separately, Vulcan is contingently liable on $1,059.8M of surety bonds and carries $464.5M of discounted asset retirement obligations excluded from the contractual obligations table — contingent rather than financing obligations, but real reclamation claims on future cash.

One data note: a widely circulated Altman Z-Score of 1.92 for Vulcan rests on an operating margin input of −0.5%, which is irreconcilable with audited FY2025 operating earnings of $1,619.6M on $7,941.1M of revenue (20.4% margin). Recomputing the standard formula on the December 2025 balance sheet with correct EBIT gives approximately 4.10 — safe zone. The screen's other ratios (current ratio 2.686, net margin 13.56%, ROA 6.45%, ROE 12.63%) reconcile to the filings. Solvency is not an issue at Vulcan; capital efficiency is merely ordinary.

## Growth Analysis

Structural demand support is credible. The top-ten-state footprint captures the majority of projected U.S. population growth, Vulcan operates in 34 of the 50 fastest-growing MSAs, and in 2025 voters in eleven states approved more than $24B of transportation measures, over $20B of it in Vulcan-served areas. State and local budgets are the more proximate driver of shipments than federal authorization.

Near-term growth, however, is running out of both engines simultaneously. Volumes: +1.0% in Q2, with same-store FY2025 shipments slightly negative. Unit margin: +1.2% in Q2 versus +7% for FY2025. The result is adjusted EBITDA down 1% and operating earnings down 3% in the most recent quarter. Consensus reflects the softening, if mildly: current-quarter estimates are down 3.2% over 30 days, current-year down 0.9% to $9.15, next-year down 0.3% to $10.79. That FY2027 figure implies +17.9% EPS growth off FY2026 — an expectation of substantial cost relief and volume recovery that the H1 data does not yet support.

The Martin Marietta read-across is instructive on what the industry is actually delivering. MLM reported Q2 revenue of $1.95B, up 21.1% to a record, and raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $7.2–7.4B — yet adjusted EPS of $5.00 was *down* from $5.43, with coverage attributing the decline to acquisitions and mix weighing on margins. Vulcan's adjusted EPS rose 5.7%. Neither company grew operating profit per share meaningfully. This is an industry cost-and-energy story, not a Vulcan-specific operating advantage, and it caps the credibility of the "aggregates pricing always wins" formulation for the current cycle. It also narrows the scale gap: MLM's FY2026E revenue base of $7.3B is converging on Vulcan's $8.1B TTM.

Data-center-driven heavy non-residential demand is a real industry tailwind — external coverage cites it repeatedly for Martin Marietta — but Vulcan's own disclosures in the reviewed sections attribute volume support to "healthy public construction activity and large projects" without naming data centers. Treat Vulcan's data-center exposure as an industry theme that is evidenced for the peer and unverified for Vulcan specifically.

The federal funding variable is dated and unresolved. IIJA surface transportation authorities, which carried the Federal-Aid Highway obligation limitation from $66.9B (FFY2022) to $72.1B (FFY2026), expire September 30, 2026. The House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee advanced a five-year reauthorization on May 22, 2026, and Senate leaders included an extension to December 11, 2026 in a bipartisan continuing resolution, so a lapse now looks unlikely. The unresolved questions are the successor program's funding *level* and Highway Trust Fund solvency — CBO concluded on July 12, 2026 that the House bill widens the HTF deficit over five years despite new EV and hybrid fees. Notably, the February 2026 10-K risk factors describe IIJA in the present tense as support "for several years to come" without identifying the expiration date.

Growth Prospects: **5.3**, down from 6.0. Long-run demographics remain intact; the near-term earnings engine is stalled and consensus is drifting lower.

FY2027 EPS $11.50 at 30x. This sits between the analyst median ($337.50) and high ($365).

This breaks the 52-week low of $252.35; the low analyst target of $198 marks a more severe derating.

**Probability-weighted return.** At 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear: 0.55(3.6%) + 0.20(21.2%) + 0.25(−17.5%) = +1.8%, or approximately +2.6% including the dividend. That is materially below a fair required return for an equity with 28% realized volatility, and the downside case is roughly five times the size of the base-case upside. Valuation: **4.0**.

Applying the "analyst targets are floors" logic here would be a mistake. That inference holds for inflection names with rising estimates and multiples below roughly 30x forward. Vulcan has falling estimates in every period and trades above 31x FY2026 consensus, so the mean target and the price likely reflect the same growth extrapolation rather than independent confirmation. The target dispersion ($198 to $365, with one outright sell rating) is itself a signal that the sell side does not agree on what normalized earnings power is.

## Competitive Landscape

Vulcan's position is strong in level and roughly flat in trajectory.

Strengths are durable. The industry is fragmented — approximately 5,000 companies operating 11,000 facilities, with the top ten at ~35% of production and Vulcan alone at ~10%. Competition is local because freight dominates delivered cost, so each permitted quarry is effectively a regional franchise. Permitting is the reinforcing barrier: Item 1A states it is increasingly difficult to permit new sites or expand existing ones in urban and suburban areas, and new sites often take years. Reserves already permitted in growing metros appreciate as a scarce asset. Waterborne distribution into the Gulf Coast, Eastern Seaboard, California and Hawaii from Bahamas, Honduras, Mexico and British Columbia quarries is a capability few competitors possess.

Trajectory concerns are three. First, the perimeter is shrinking: divesting California ready-mix, USVI operations, and Houston asphalt raises margin quality but increases single-product concentration — this is mix improvement, not diversification. Second, MLM's revenue base is converging on Vulcan's while Vulcan's M&A spend has gone to roughly zero, so relative scale is narrowing (though MLM is paying for it in EPS). Third, Item 1A carries a specific substitution risk: recycled concrete and asphalt are increasingly used in urban markets, and expanded use "could cause a significant reduction in the demand for aggregates." Vulcan itself reused 2.5 million tons of reclaimed asphalt pavement and recycled 1.7 million tons of concrete in 2025.

Geographic concentration is the live exposure rather than customer credit. Gulf Coast generated $984.1M of $2,279.8M in Q2 segment sales (~43%), with aggregates revenue there up 9.8% versus East +2.3% and West +6.5%. The growth engine and the weather exposure are the same region — May and June rainfall in Texas and the Southeast compressed Q2 volumes.

Competitive Position: **6.5**.

## Risk Assessment

**1. LADWP / Hewitt Landfill contribution demand — the largest unreserved contingency.** Vulcan's Calmat subsidiary operates under a 2015 Los Angeles Regional Water Quality Control Board cleanup order for the former Hewitt Landfill. On-site remediation is fully accrued; the off-site exposure is not. LADWP built two wellhead treatment systems and alleges Hewitt is one of the primary sources of contamination at one and one of the sources at the other. The 10-Q cites publicly reported capital costs of approximately $92M (NHW) and $245M (NHC), plus an $11.5M six-year groundwater study and $22M for 26 monitoring wells, plus unquantified operation and maintenance. LADWP presented a demand in January 2026 that included actual costs in excess of these estimates. Vulcan states it cannot reasonably estimate a range of loss but that "it is reasonably possible that an estimated material loss could be recognized in the near term."

The referenced amounts are LADWP's costs, not Vulcan's liability. Vulcan is one of potentially several responsible parties and has articulated substantive allocation defenses — relative contribution and duration of contaminants from Hewitt versus LADWP's own property and activities, the cost-effectiveness of the chosen remedy, and identification of other parties. Any share should be a fraction, may be spread over years, and could be partly insured. But the company-wide environmental accrual is $51.4M, so essentially none of this is reserved, and "material" for a company generating $2.35B of adjusted EBITDA plausibly implies a nine-figure charge. This is the item most likely to produce a negative earnings surprise and is nearly invisible to screening data.

**2. Energy cost inflation.** Discussed above; the mechanism runs from energy prices through diesel into cash cost per ton, and the disruption is unresolved.

**3. Federal funding.** September 30, 2026 authority expiration covered by a stopgap to December 11; successor funding level and HTF solvency open.

**4. Calica tax exposure.** Mexico's SAT 2018 audit asserts Calica had no right to mine and denies its cost of goods sold deduction. The 10-Q describes the position as "strictly binary," with an adverse outcome producing a one-time cash outflow and tax expense of approximately $35M including $23M of interest and penalties. Tax year 2019 remains under examination. Calica deferred tax assets of $37.3M carry a full valuation allowance. The July 27, 2026 ICSID decision found Mexico violated NAFTA in several respects but awarded negligible damages and dismissed the ancillary claim (covering the 2022 shutdown and 2024 protected-area decree) on jurisdiction — removing an eight-year recovery option rather than a liability. Vulcan still employed 280 people in Mexico as of January 1, 2026; idle facilities expense rose 61% to $56.2M in 2025, though the filings do not break out the Calica portion.

**5. ERP transition.** A cloud-based ERP replacing significant portions of financial and HR systems runs through Q3 2027, with a concurrent enterprise performance management implementation and modified internal control processes. Disclosure controls were effective at June 30, 2026. Common but real execution and control risk over 18 months.

**6. Corporate alternative minimum tax.** Vulcan expects average adjusted financial statement income above $1B in 2026, subjecting it to the 15% CAMT for the current and all future years, though it does not expect to pay in 2026. Given heavy reliance on percentage depletion and accelerated depreciation, this is a forward cash-tax risk if book-tax differences widen — and Q2's EPS growth already depended on a lower effective rate.

**7. Disclosure lag.** Item 1A of the Q2 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors even as MD&A adds a named military conflict and a quantified new cost headwind and the forward-looking statement list expands to include it. Arguably both facts fall within existing generic categories, but an investor relying on Item 1A alone would miss both the funding cliff and the energy shock.

Risk Factors: **4.3**, down from 5.3, driven by the LADWP language change and the dated funding cliff.

## Management Quality

Capital allocation has shifted deliberately into harvest-and-return mode, and the direction is defensible. Vulcan bought nothing in 2025 and $75.0M in H1 2026 while a peer bought revenue at the cost of declining EPS. Divesting California ready-mix, USVI and Houston asphalt concentrates the portfolio on the 90%-of-gross-profit aggregates franchise. Leverage of 1.9x gross sits below the 2.0–2.5x target, implying roughly $344M of debt headroom to 2.0x and $1,522M to 2.5x, so the balance sheet is not the binding constraint.

Three things temper the score. First, buyback execution shows no valuation timing edge: FY2025 average $283.82, H1 2026 average $283.77, Q2 average $276.69 — all clustered near today's $284.69. H1 repurchases of $399.8M (1.4M shares) were more than tenfold H1 2025's $38.1M, which is a real return of capital, but purchases at the multiple prevailing today are not accretive to intrinsic value if the base case is $295. Only 3,864,057 shares (~3.0% of shares outstanding, ~$1.10B) remain under the February 2017 authorization; at the H1 pace that is exhausted in roughly 14 months, so a new authorization, a step-up in M&A, or a slower pace arrives within four quarters. Second, insider behavior gives no support: no open-market purchases by any officer or director in the reviewed period, and one open-market sale (SVP David Clement, 2,212 shares at $292.29 on June 15, 2026, roughly 20% of his holding). A single sub-$1M sale is close to noise, and large-cap executives rarely buy in the open market, so read this as neutral-to-weakly-negative rather than bearish — but it is not the clustered buying that would argue the stock is cheap. Third, the CEO transition is only seven months old with $8.6M of associated reorganization charges, and the strategy under new leadership has not been tested through a cost cycle.

Incentive design is a genuine positive. Performance measures are adjusted EBITDA, aggregates cash gross profit per ton, and EBITDA economic profit — exactly the metrics currently under pressure. If unit margin stays at +1.2%, payouts should compress. That is alignment rather than metric-shopping. Five-year TSR of $200.02 per $100 invested versus a peer group at $130.99 and the S&P 500 Materials index at $138.79 is substantial relative outperformance, though backward-looking.

Management Quality: **5.8**, a marginal improvement on portfolio discipline offset by buyback timing and the absence of insider conviction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($345, +21%).** The Q2 deceleration is a diesel-and-rain quarter inside a structurally intact franchise. Ex-diesel cash costs rose only 3%, in line with the low-single-digit full-year guide, so if energy normalizes the unit-margin engine reaccelerates mechanically. Weather-deferred Gulf Coast volumes return in the seasonally strongest half; the ~80bp gap between reported and mix-adjusted pricing closes as divested low-price mix rolls off; guidance clears the midpoint. The federal reauthorization passes at a higher obligation limit, state and local measures (>$20B approved in Vulcan-served areas in 2025) fund shipments regardless, and data-center construction adds heavy non-residential volume. Leverage at 1.7x net with $1,581.5M of undrawn revolver funds an accelerated buyback under a refreshed authorization plus bolt-on M&A into permitted reserves. FY2027 EPS of $11.50 at 30x supports the analyst median.

**Bear case ($235, −18%).** The unit-margin engine has met a structural cost wall, and Q2's EPS growth — sourced entirely from a 190bp tax-rate decline and a 2% share-count reduction — masked flat operating earnings. Neither driver repeats at that magnitude: CAMT applies from 2026 forward, and the buyback authorization is 14 months from exhaustion. Energy stays elevated while the Middle East disruption persists, cash gross profit per ton goes flat to negative, and FY2026 lands at or below the $2,400M floor. LADWP produces a nine-figure pretax charge against a $51.4M accrual. ## Conclusion

Vulcan Materials owns the best collection of permitted aggregates reserves in the United States, sells into the fastest-growing metropolitan markets in the country, carries investment-grade credit at 1.7x net leverage with all-fixed-rate debt averaging 13.2 years, and has an incentive structure aligned to the metrics that actually matter in the business. None of that is in question.

What is in question is the price. At $284.69 the market pays 31.1x FY2026 consensus and 17.4x trailing EV/EBITDA for a company whose most recent quarter produced a 1% adjusted EBITDA decline, a 3% operating earnings decline, and unit cash margin growth of 1.2% versus 7% for the prior full year — with the reported EPS increase entirely attributable to a lower effective tax rate and a smaller share count, neither repeatable at that magnitude. Consensus is drifting lower in every period. Reaching the guidance midpoint requires H2 EBITDA growth to roughly quadruple H1's 2.9%. An unreserved contribution demand that the company itself flags as a reasonably possible material near-term loss sits against a $51.4M accrual. Federal surface transportation authority runs on a stopgap through December 11 with the successor funding level unresolved.

Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +2–3% over twelve months, with a base case 3.6% above spot and a bear case 17.5% below, does not compensate for that distribution. **REDUCE.** Trim on strength above $300, keep the name on the watch list, and revisit either on evidence that cash gross profit per ton is compounding again or on a price in the mid-$240s.
