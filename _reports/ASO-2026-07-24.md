**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Academy Sports + Outdoors is a value-positioned, full-line sporting goods and outdoor retailer with 324 large-format stores concentrated in the U.S. South. The stock trades at $47.06, down ~17% over the trailing year and ~25% below its February 2026 high of $62.45, at roughly 7.3x consensus FY2026 EPS ($6.46) and ~6.7x next-year ($7.00). On headline metrics it looks statistically cheap: a low P/E, an Altman Z-score of 4.1, a modest 0.59 debt/equity ratio, and roughly $220–260M of annual free cash flow against a $2.9B market cap. The equity story is fundamentally a per-share compounding thesis: management has retired ~22% of the share count in three years, buying back stock at $50–$58 (above today's price) and pushing $338M of remaining authorization.

The complication is what sits beneath the top-line recovery. Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended May 2, 2026) delivered +6.7% net sales and +2.9% comparable sales, a reversal of FY2025's -1.5% comps. But the growth is manufactured by higher spend per visit: comparable transactions fell -1.5% in Q1 and -4.2% in FY2025. Customer counts are still eroding; the comp is carried entirely by ticket, much of which is tariff-driven price increases rather than genuine demand recovery. Tariffs flipped from a +90bp gross-margin tailwind in FY2025 (price management ahead of cost) to a -110bp headwind in Q1 FY2026 as costs caught up. This is the central margin swing factor, and management's mitigation is unproven quarter to quarter.

We rate ASO a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The de-risked balance sheet (May 2026 refinancing cleared the entire 2027 maturity wall and eliminated variable-rate debt) and disciplined buyback are genuine supports, and the valuation offers a floor. But the traffic erosion, the tariff margin drag, an unproven three-year-old management team deliberately depressing near-term margins to fund store growth, and a sector (mid-market Southern discretionary) facing consumer softness leave the risk/reward roughly balanced. The 20.9% short interest and 165.9% near-term implied volatility indicate the market is pricing real stress into the next earnings print, not an overlooked bargain.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1938 in Texas, Academy operates 324 stores (~70,000 sq ft average) across 21 contiguous states, supported by three distribution centers (Katy TX, Twiggs County GA, Cookeville TN) and an e-commerce platform. The footprint is heavily regional: Texas alone holds 117 stores (~36% of the base), with Georgia, Florida, and Louisiana next. Sales are 100% domestic and skew toward lower- and middle-income Southern consumers.

Merchandise spans four divisions: Outdoors (31% of sales), Apparel (27%), Sports & Recreation (22%), and Footwear (20%), with ~78% national brands (Nike/Jordan, adidas, Under Armour, Columbia, YETI, Carhartt, Stanley) and ~22% private label (19 brands including Magellan Outdoors, BCG, Game Winner). No single brand exceeds 12% of sales, and the largest of ~1,500 vendors is ~12% of purchases, so customer concentration is low and vendor concentration moderate. Firearms and ammunition are ~6% of net sales. The company operates as a single reporting segment.

The positioning is a "value plus one-stop-shop" niche between mass merchants (broader national-brand assortment than Walmart/Target) and higher-end specialty retailers (lower price than DICK'S or Bass Pro). E-commerce reached 11.7% of merchandise sales in FY2025, still a small but growing channel. The C-suite was installed together in 2023: CEO Steve Lawrence (June 2023, former CMO), President Sam Johnson (Oct 2023), CFO Carl Ford (July 2023), CMO Matt McCabe (June 2023).

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue and profit declined from the FY2022 pandemic peak before stabilizing:

| Metric ($M) | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 6,395 | 6,159 | 5,933 | 6,053 |
| Gross margin % | 34.6% | 34.3% | 33.9% | 34.8% |
| Operating income | 847 | 678 | 539 | 512 |
| Operating margin | 13.2% | 11.0% | 9.1% | 8.5% |
| Net income | 628 | 519 | 418 | 377 |
| Diluted EPS | — | $6.70 | $5.73 | $5.54 |

FY2025 revenue rose +2.0% to $6.05B, but the growth came from 24 new store openings (+$142.8M) masking a -1.5% comparable-sales decline. The comp fell on -4.2% comparable transactions, only partly offset by +2.9% average ticket. Traffic, not pricing, is the underlying problem.

Operating margin compressed ~470bps over three years (13.2% → 8.5%). Gross margin improved 90bps to 34.8% (tariff-driven price management plus private-label mix), but SG&A deleveraged from 24.8% to 26.3% on +$109M of growth spend ($84.8M new stores, $13.1M technology). This is deliberate investment depressing near-term margins rather than pure cost inflation.

Cash flow quality is solid. Operating cash flow was $434.8M in FY2025 (down from $528M, largely a working-capital swing from pulling inventory forward ahead of tariffs); FCF was $222M (~3.7% margin), adjusted FCF $263M. The balance sheet is healthy: Altman Z-score 4.1 (safe zone), current ratio 1.89, D/E 0.59, cash ~$330M at year-end, ROA/ROE ~7.1%.

**Q1 FY2026 (ended May 2, 2026).** Net sales rose +6.7% to $1.442B; comparable sales turned positive at +2.9%, led by Outdoors (+11.7%). Comp growth was +4.5% ticket offset by -1.5% transactions. Gross margin fell 80bps to 33.2%, with -110bps directly attributable to tariffs, partly offset by shrink (+20bps) and freight (+10bps). SG&A leveraged 70bps to 28.1% on higher sales and lapping prior-year Jordan Brand launch costs. Net income rose +14.4% to $52.7M; diluted EPS was $0.80 ($0.93 adjusted). E-commerce grew +17.4% to 11.0% of merchandise sales.

**Capital allocation is the equity's engine.** Basic share count fell from ~81.6M (FY2022) to 63.5M at May 2, 2026, roughly -22% in three years. FY2025 repurchases were $199M; Q1 FY2026 added $98.4M at an average $57.79, above the current $47 price. $338M remains under the $700M authorization (through Dec 2027), and the dividend was raised to $0.15/quarter. Against a $2.9B market cap, buyback capacity is material, which is why FY2025 EPS fell only -3% while net income fell -10%.

**Earnings quality.** Zero goodwill/intangible/long-lived-asset impairments in three years despite declining operating results, and management declined to book a tariff-refund receivable even after a favorable February 2026 Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs. Both point to a conservative, non-aggressive reporting culture.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on two legs: the new-store program and the reported comp inflection. The store program is the more durable of the two — 65 stores opened since 2021, ~20–25 per year, averaging ~$13M in annual sales each, with management citing whitespace in-market and in adjacent Southern/Southeastern MSAs. This is a defensible, productive engine and the primary reason revenue stabilized in FY2025 and grew in Q1 FY2026.

The comp inflection is weaker on inspection. Positive comps mask continued transaction declines, a pattern that held across all of FY2025. Higher average ticket is carrying the comp, and a meaningful portion of that ticket lift is likely tariff-driven price increase rather than demand. In a consumer environment where mid-market Southern households — Academy's core — are trading down, a ticket-led comp is fragile. FY2026 guidance of $6.23–$6.355B implies ~3–5% total sales growth, largely dependent on the store program and continued ticket support.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year EPS revised +2.7% over 90 days to $6.46, next-year +1.3% to $7.00, next-quarter +6.9% to $1.30. These are constructive but well below the +30% threshold that would flag a genuine earnings inflection. The revisions confirm stabilization, not acceleration. R&D is not a meaningful line for a retailer; growth investment is instead in stores, distribution, and omnichannel technology, which is running through SG&A and depressing margins today with an unproven return.

Weighing the durable store growth against the hollow comp and fragile traffic, growth prospects score below neutral. The company is not shrinking, but the quality of the recovery is low and the demand backdrop is uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.06, ASO trades at ~7.3x consensus FY2026 EPS and ~6.7x next-year, against a Specialty Stores peer-median P/E of 25.5x. Even acknowledging the peer set is heterogeneous (SIG at 12.9x, SBH at 7.5x, HZO at negative earnings, FIVE at 25.5x, EYE at 31.8x), ASO sits at the low end. The discount is partly justified (declining traffic, tariff margin risk, regional concentration, no growth premium) but is wide enough that a modest re-rating toward even 9–10x forward on stabilized earnings would imply meaningful upside.

The dividend yield is only ~1.2%, so income is not the support; the support is total shareholder yield. Roughly $220–260M of annual FCF plus $338M of buyback capacity against a $2.9B cap is a high combined return-of-capital yield, and management is repurchasing stock aggressively at prices above the current market. On a normalized basis, if comps merely hold and the store program continues, per-share earnings compound faster than net income because of the shrinking share count.

The valuation dimension warrants caution rather than enthusiasm. A low multiple in mid-market discretionary retail has historically been a value-trap signal more often than a mispricing; cheapness alone sorts the wrong way in this sector, and the required independent catalyst here is thin. The buyback is a real catalyst for EPS support, but it does not fix declining traffic; it substitutes financial engineering for demand recovery. Analyst targets (mean $60.05, median $59.00, low $50.00, high $78.00) sit well above spot, but on a stock at a low multiple with a deteriorating demand backdrop and tariff drag, I treat the low target ($50) as more informative than the mean. This is a modest, cheap-but-fairly-priced situation rather than an asymmetric opportunity, which supports a mid-range valuation score, not a high one.

## Competitive Landscape

The sporting-goods and outdoor retail market is fragmented and intensely competitive. Academy faces mass merchants (Walmart, Target), large-format sporting goods (DICK'S), specialty outdoor (Bass Pro/Cabela's), footwear specialists, and increasingly direct-to-consumer brands and marketplaces (Nike DTC, Amazon). Many competitors are larger and national.

Academy's moat is real but narrow: localized merchandising, scale in the value niche, strong Southern brand loyalty, and favorable product allocations from national brands (its 78% national-brand assortment is a genuine differentiator versus mass merchants). The FY2025 gross-margin improvement while comps were negative suggests merchandising discipline relative to peers. But the trajectory is the concern. Comparable transactions have declined for over a year, indicating share-of-wallet or share-of-visit erosion rather than a stable position. The structural threats — vendor DTC disintermediation (Nike selling around retailers) and price transparency from mobile comparison shopping — cut against a retailer whose value proposition depends on national-brand relationships that those same brands are trying to bypass.

The industry-wide nature of the softness (the Tractor Supply comparison, the broad mid-market discretionary pressure) means the pressure is not uniquely a share loss to a specific rival, but Academy's declining transaction count within a soft category still points to a position that is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than improving. Trajectory, which matters more than level, sits below neutral.

## Risk Assessment

The most material ASO-specific risks:

1. **Tariffs — quantified and active.** Significant sourcing from China, Vietnam, Bangladesh, Cambodia, and Brazil. The dynamic has flipped negative, and management's mitigation (sourcing diversification, pricing, vendor negotiation) is unproven quarter to quarter. This is the single most concrete near-term margin threat.

2. **Declining traffic / discretionary sensitivity.** Growth relies on ticket, which is fragile if consumers trade down. The broader mid-market consumer, and Academy's core Southern lower/middle-income customer, is directly exposed to discretionary-spending pressure.

3. **Regional concentration.** ~36% of stores in Texas, heavily Gulf Coast/Southern — hurricane, freeze, and regional-shock exposure that national competitors do not carry.

4. **Execution risk on the growth strategy.** The margin compression is a bet that new stores and omnichannel/tech investment restore growth. If comps stay soft and transactions keep falling, the SG&A deleverage is not temporary investment but structural margin erosion. The three-year-old management team has not yet proven the strategy through a full cycle.

5. **Firearms (~6% of sales).** Regulatory, litigation, and reputational risk, partially eased by the 2025 repeal of the DOJ "zero tolerance" dealer policy.

The May 2026 refinancing removed the largest balance-sheet risk: $500M of 5.875% Senior Secured Notes due 2031 redeemed the $400M 6.00% notes (due Nov 2027) and repaid the $85M variable-rate term loan, and the ABL was extended to 2031. This cleared the entire 2027 maturity wall, eliminated variable-rate exposure, and was executed from a position of strength (Z-score 4.1). That is a clean de-risking event that supports the improving risk-score trajectory.

Netting these, the risk score sits below neutral. The tariff margin drag and traffic erosion are specific, active, near-term catalysts for downside that the market may not have fully priced, and the setup around the next earnings print is stress-loaded.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 165.9% versus 42.8% one-year historical, an extreme spread (~123 percentage points), in the 90th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in sharp backwardation (165.9% at 1 week collapsing to 46.6% at 6 months), which signals the market expects imminent event risk. IV jumped from 94.5% to 165.9% since early June while the stock fell -9.1%. The near-term skew is call-heavy (-14.9%), and the put/call OI ratio (1.22) eased slightly. The combination of elevated near-term IV, rich vs. historical vol, and backwardation says the market is bracing for a large move rather than pricing complacency. For a HOLD, this reinforces waiting for the print to resolve before adding.

Short interest is 20.9% of float with 4.4 days to cover. That is high enough to create squeeze risk on a positive surprise (the small ~63.5M share count and aggressive buyback tighten the float further), but it also confirms that a meaningful cohort of informed investors holds the bearish traffic-and-tariff view. This cuts both ways: a genuine comp/traffic beat could force covering, but the level of short conviction is itself a caution flag.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$62–78).** The refinancing removed the balance-sheet overhang, the store program keeps adding ~$13M/store of productive revenue, comps have inflected positive, and management continues retiring ~7% of the float annually at prices above market. If traffic stabilizes and tariff mitigation succeeds (returning gross margin toward FY2025's 34.8%), FY2027 EPS of ~$7.00+ on a re-rate to 9–11x supports $63–77, aligning with the analyst high of $78. The short interest provides squeeze fuel on any traffic beat.

**Bear Case (~$36–40).** The comp recovery is exposed as a tariff-driven ticket illusion; transactions keep falling as mid-market discretionary pressure persists; tariff margin drag widens beyond 110bps; and the SG&A "growth investment" reframes as permanent margin erosion. EPS compresses toward $5.50–6.00, and the multiple stays at 7x or de-rates further, implying $38–42. At that point the buyback continues but into a genuinely deteriorating business.

**Base Case (~$52).** Comps hold low-single-digit positive on ticket, transactions stay modestly negative, tariff drag persists but is partially mitigated, and EPS lands near the ~$6.46 consensus. Continued buyback supports per-share earnings. A modest re-rate to ~8x on $6.50–7.00 implies roughly $52–55, ~11–17% above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an **uncertain** rationale: the balance-sheet de-risking and buyback discipline are genuine positives, but the traffic erosion, tariff margin drag, and unproven strategy leave insufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings print (Q2 FY2026, expected September 2026) is the natural reassessment point and the event the options market is pricing stress around.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Comparable *transactions* turn positive (not just ticket) for a full quarter — the single most important signal that demand, not price, is driving the recovery.
- Gross-margin tariff drag narrows below ~50bps or reverses, demonstrating successful mitigation.
- Forward P/E compresses below ~6x on stable/rising estimates, widening the margin of safety while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comparable transactions accelerate to worse than -3% alongside a comp miss, confirming demand deterioration behind the ticket facade.
- Tariff drag widens beyond ~150bps of gross margin with no mitigation path, or management cuts FY2026 guidance.
- Any impairment charge or a shift in capital allocation away from buyback (which would remove the core per-share support).

## Conclusion

Academy Sports + Outdoors is a well-run, conservatively financed retailer trading at a genuinely low multiple, with a management team returning capital aggressively and a store program that is productive. The refinancing meaningfully de-risked the balance sheet. Those are real supports and they justify holding rather than avoiding.

But the recovery narrative is weaker than the headline comp suggests. Positive comps are carried by higher spend per visit while customer counts continue to fall, and tariffs have turned from a margin tailwind into a quantified headwind. The core Southern mid-market consumer is precisely the cohort most exposed to discretionary-spending pressure across the retail complex. The short interest and near-term implied volatility confirm the market is pricing stress into the next print. In a sector where a low multiple has historically signaled a value trap more often than a mispricing, the cheapness alone is not a sufficient catalyst.

We rate ASO a **HOLD**. The buyback provides EPS support and a valuation floor, but the traffic-and-tariff overhang caps the upside until the September print clarifies whether the recovery is demand-driven or price-driven. Investors should wait for evidence of positive transaction growth before adding, and should treat a further transaction deterioration alongside a widening tariff drag as the signal to exit.
