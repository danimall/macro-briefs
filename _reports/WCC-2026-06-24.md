**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Wesco International is a scale B2B distributor of electrical, communications/data-center, and utility/broadband products that has become a direct beneficiary of three converging tailwinds it names explicitly: AI-driven data-center buildout, electrification/grid investment, and supply-chain reshoring. The investment story changed character with the Q1 2026 print on April 30, 2026: sales rose 13.8% reported (12.3% organic, the third straight quarter of double-digit growth), adjusted diluted EPS jumped ~53% to $3.37 (beating consensus by ~19%), and both EES and CSS segments expanded margins simultaneously. Data-center sales reached ~$1.4B, up ~70% YoY, and now represent roughly 25% of total revenue. The stock rose ~21% on the print and sits near 52-week highs at ~$350, up 95% over the trailing twelve months.

The bull case is real but the entry point is not asymmetric. FY2025 headline net income actually declined to $640M from $717M in 2024, masked by mix (large, lower-margin hyperscale projects) and the loss of 2024's $122M WIS divestiture gain. Operating cash flow collapsed from $1,101M to $125M, with free cash flow of just $25M, on a ~$935M working-capital build to finance project growth. Q1 2026 OCF rebounded to $221M, which supports the "transitory not structural" read, but cash conversion remains the single most important metric to monitor in a project-heavy growth model. Net leverage at 3.2x is elevated though declining and covenant-compliant.

We maintain a HOLD with a growth bias. The growth and competitive trajectory have genuinely improved and we expect continued appreciation over a 12-month horizon as deleveraging and the refinancing benefit compound. The constraint is valuation: at ~25x trailing and ~22x our normalized forward EPS, near the high end of a parabolic re-rating, the price already discounts much of the re-rating. We would accumulate on pullbacks toward the high-$280s to low-$300s rather than chase at current levels.

## Company Overview

Wesco is one of the largest B2B distributors of electrical, communications/data, utility, and broadband products and supply-chain services globally, ranked #195 on the 2026 Fortune 500. It operates 700+ sites across ~50 countries with ~21,000 employees, ~35,000 suppliers, and ~130,000 customers. The 2020 Anixter merger transformed its scale and is the source of its current segment structure. FY2025 revenue of $23.51B splits across three segments:

- **CSS – Communications & Security Solutions ($9.1B, +18.3%):** the growth engine, a global leader in data center, network infrastructure, and security. Data center solutions drove 16.7% organic growth in 2025 and accelerated to +21.9% organic in Q1 2026.
- **EES – Electrical & Electronic Solutions ($9.0B, +6.7%):** North American leader serving construction, industrial, and OEM customers, with incremental lift from data-center construction and reshoring.
- **UBS – Utility & Broadband Solutions ($5.5B, –4.9%):** the laggard, hit by reduced public-power activity and the 2024 divestiture of the Wesco Integrated Supply business, partially offset by broadband.

The moat is scale-based: SKU breadth, product availability, a global-local footprint, value-added services (kitting, supply-chain management, project deployment), and supplier-rebate economics. Customer concentration is low (top 10 ~15% of sales, none over 5%); supplier concentration is moderate (top 10 ~32% of purchases, none over 6%). The market is fragmented, with thousands of regional competitors alongside Sonepar, Graybar, and Rexel. Wesco's role in the data-center theme is as a distributor/integrator of cabling, connectivity, power systems, and network gear; it sells to hyperscale and colocation builders rather than owning compute or facilities.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 sales grew 7.8% reported / 8.6% organic. The four-year trajectory ($21.4B in 2022 → $22.4B → $21.8B → $23.5B) shows the business absorbed a 2024 air pocket and re-accelerated. Q1 2026 sales of $6.08B grew 13.8% reported / 12.3% organic.

**Profitability.** FY2025 was a two-period story. Reported net income fell to $640M from $717M (2024) and $765M (2023) despite higher revenue, with gross margin compressing ~50bps (COGS 78.9% vs 78.4%) on the heavier mix of low-margin data-center project sales and competitive pricing in public power. Net margin was only 2.7% and operating margin ~5.2%. Q1 2026 reversed the trend: EES adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 8.2% (from 6.9%), CSS to 9.0% (from 7.9%), operating income grew 21.8%, and adjusted EBITDA rose 25.1% to $389M. UBS remained soft (margin 9.6% vs 10.8%). The mechanism behind the improvement is operating leverage as the working-capital-intensive projects booked in 2025 mature into margin-bearing revenue.

**Cash flow.** The dominant watch item. FCF was just $25M (FCF margin ~0.1%). The cause was a ~$935M working-capital build to finance large-project growth: receivables (~$507M impact) and inventory (~$428M impact). Milestone-payment projects consume working capital before generating cash. Q1 2026 OCF (vs $28M in Q1 2025) was helped by payables timing, which supports the transitory interpretation. Whether cash conversion keeps pace with project-heavy growth is the metric to track every quarter.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$5.83B; net leverage was 3.4x at YE2025 (up from 2.9x), improving to 3.2x at Q1 2026. Altman Z of 2.74 sits in the grey zone, D/E is 2.28, and liquidity is ~$3.2B. Goodwill plus intangibles of ~$5.1B (~31% of assets) reflects Anixter, with no impairment indicated. Current ratio is healthy at 2.2. Leverage is elevated for an industrial distributor but within covenant and moving in the right direction.

**Capital allocation.** Priorities are debt reduction, dividends, buybacks, and tuck-in M&A. Buybacks are modest ($75M in 2025, ~$25M/quarter). The dividend was raised to $0.50/quarter (from $0.454), a sub-1% yield. The June 2025 redemption of the 10.625% Series A Preferred (funded by 6.375% 2033 Notes) removed ~$57M/year in preferred dividends, accretive to common EPS. The February 2026 refinancing ($650M of 5.250% Notes due 2031 and $850M of 5.500% Notes due 2034 replacing 7.250% 2028 Notes) lowers the cost of capital, pushes the next significant maturity to 2029, and shifts the portfolio to ~92% fixed-rate. Management guides to "substantial" net income, EPS, and cash-flow benefit.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has moved from aspirational to confirmed in the numbers. CSS organic growth and data-center revenue place Wesco squarely inside the AI-infrastructure capex cycle. This sits within a broad-based theme: data-center demand at the infrastructure layer (cabling, connectivity, power systems, network gear), which Wesco repeatedly cites alongside electrification/grid investment and reshoring. The breadth of that confirmation raises the durability of Wesco's growth versus a single-company story.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current-year EPS consensus of $16.08 is +2.7% over 90 days and next-year $18.84 is +3.1%, while near-quarter estimates have drifted slightly lower (-2.6% over 90 days for the current quarter). This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a violent re-rating; it is steady upward pressure on the out-year supported by a strong beat track record (3 beats, 1 miss in four quarters, including the +19.9% Q1 surprise). Management raised FY2026 guidance to $24.9–25.6B in sales and $15–17 in EPS on the Q1 print.

The growth is not uniform. UBS is a genuine drag, with public-power weakness persisting. The growth is also lower-quality at the gross-margin line — hyperscale project sales carry thinner margins and consume working capital — so the durability of the margin improvement seen in Q1 depends on continued mix discipline and on the deleveraging/refinancing tailwind flowing through to EPS. We score growth prospects 7.5, recognizing the confirmed double-digit top-line momentum and margin improvement while withholding the highest marks because the cash-conversion question and the UBS drag remain unresolved.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$350, Wesco trades at ~25x trailing EPS of $13.05 and ~22x our normalized FY2026 EPS (midpoint of the $15–17 guide, ~$16). On consensus next-year EPS of $18.84, the forward multiple is ~18.6x. Peer medians in Trading Companies & Distributors run materially higher — WSO 31.5x, AIT 30.1x, MSM 31.1x, with a peer-median P/E of 31.1x — so on a headline basis Wesco screens cheap to its group.

The discount is partly justified. Wesco carries higher leverage than the asset-light, lower-debt distributors it trades against, thinner net margins (2.7% vs the high-margin specialty distributors), and a far weaker free-cash-flow profile in FY2025. A distributor whose earnings depend on lumpy, working-capital-hungry project awards deserves a lower multiple than a steady-comp HVAC or industrial-MRO distributor. This is the kind of cheapness that historically marks a value trap rather than a buy signal unless an independent catalyst is present, and we have identified specific catalysts (refinancing/preferred-redemption EPS accretion, deleveraging, potential IEEPA tariff refunds) that argue the discount should narrow over time.

What keeps the valuation score at 5.0 rather than higher is the entry point. The stock has re-rated ~95% over twelve months and sits near its all-time high after a +21% single-day move; much of the re-rating is now in the price. Analyst targets (mean $381, median $395, high $440, low $240) sit above the current price, and the principle that targets lag reality on cheap inflection stocks would normally argue the consensus is stale. But the recent recommendation mix has degraded modestly (a new strong-sell appeared, holds rose), and a $240 low target signals genuine dispersion about the cash-flow and leverage risks. We do not treat the prior 95% run as evidence of overvaluation; appreciation driven by real fundamental improvement is not a reason to be bearish. The valuation discipline is forward-looking: at ~22x normalized earnings with thin FCF and 3.2x leverage, the asymmetry favors waiting for a pullback over chasing strength.

## Competitive Landscape

Wesco's competitive position is improving on trajectory. CSS organic growth materially outpaces the fragmented distribution market, and the combined EES+CSS+UBS portfolio lets Wesco offer integrated data-center solutions — cabling, connectivity, power systems, network gear, and project-deployment services — that few competitors can match at scale. Record backlog in CSS and EES at Q1 2026 indicates strength in the segments tied to the data-center and electrification themes. The scale-based moat (SKU breadth, availability, global-local footprint, supplier rebate economics) is the kind of advantage that compounds in a fragmented market favoring the largest players.

The offset is real: hyperscale projects are lower-margin and working-capital-intensive, so winning them dilutes blended margins and strains cash even as it grows revenue. UBS public-power weakness shows not all end-markets are buoyant, and the largest customers (hyperscalers) have buyer power that compresses distributor economics on the biggest deals. Competition from Sonepar, Graybar, and Rexel is intense in the core electrical business. On balance the trajectory is positive — the company is growing in its fastest-growing end-markets and widening the integrated-solution gap — which supports a competitive-position score of 7.0, up from the level implied when the data-center thesis was still aspirational.

## Risk Assessment

**Working-capital / project-concentration risk (most material).** A dedicated FY2025 risk factor flags that large data-center projects carry milestone payments, liquidated damages, and heavy working-capital intensity. The FY2025 OCF collapse is the live manifestation. A pause or cancellation in data-center capex would hit both growth and cash simultaneously, given that earnings increasingly depend on lumpy, unpredictable large-project awards.

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** $5.83B of debt at 3.2x net leverage in a higher-for-longer rate environment is a double exposure. The OBBBA modification to the EBITDA-based business-interest deduction limit (effective 2026) "could have a material adverse impact" on Wesco's ability to deduct incremental interest, a partial offset to the refinancing benefit.

**Margin and mix.** UBS public-power pricing remains competitive; the gross-margin compression seen in FY2025 could persist if hyperscale mix continues to dominate growth without offsetting operating leverage.

**Supplier rebate dependence.** A "meaningful portion of profitability and cash flow" comes from supplier rebates/volume discounts renegotiable on short notice.

**Tariffs — two-sided.** New "reciprocal tariff regime" language, but the Q1 2026 10-Q discloses the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs, which may entitle Wesco to refunds. The amount and timing are uncertain and nothing has been recorded — unquantified upside optionality.

**Governance/contingencies.** A ~$49M loss contingency is fully offset by a $48.5M insurance recovery (net deductible only); a ~$33.4M Mexican tax assessment received a court decision April 23, 2026 that the company is still evaluating and expects to prevail on. The DDP digital-platform rollout carries execution risk (FY2024 included a $17.8M cloud-software write-off) and touches internal controls.

Several of these risks are partially priced (leverage, cyclicality), but the cash-conversion question is the one that could move the stock if Q2/Q3 OCF disappoints. We score risk factors 5.0, reflecting the genuine working-capital and leverage exposures balanced against the unrecorded tariff-refund optionality and the deleveraging path.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 39.7% sits essentially in line with one-year historical volatility of 40.0% (spread -0.3%, "fair"), and IV percentile within the current chain is just 2%, so the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst. The term structure is in contango (near 39.7% < long-term 44.3%), a normal/complacent shape. Put/call skew of 3.1% indicates mildly elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.50 and OI ratio of 0.27 lean call-heavy, consistent with a stock that has rallied hard. IV fell 2pp since the prior snapshot as the price pulled back ~5%, and the P/C OI ratio dropped from 0.47 to 0.27, indicating fading hedging demand. The ±8.1% implied move on the nearest expiry is unremarkable for a name with 40% realized vol. Net read: no options-implied warning that contradicts the fundamental HOLD; the mild put skew is consistent with our valuation-driven caution rather than a fresh bearish catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$440).** Data-center capex remains durable, CSS sustains 20%+ organic growth, and the margin improvement seen in Q1 holds as projects mature and operating leverage compounds. Cash conversion normalizes (Q1 OCF of $221M is the template), net leverage falls below 3.0x, and the refinancing/preferred-redemption benefits flow through to EPS. FY2026 lands at the top of the $15–17 guide, FY2027 approaches the ~$18.8 consensus, and the multiple holds near 23x on rising estimates plus potential IEEPA tariff refunds. This is the analyst high target of $440, roughly +25% from current.

**Base case (~$395).** Sales reach ~$25.2B (midpoint of guidance), normalized FY2026 EPS lands near $16, and FY2027 EPS approaches $18.8. Applying ~21x to the FY2027 estimate as deleveraging and the refinancing benefit improve earnings quality yields ~$395, near the median analyst target and roughly +13% from current. This requires cash conversion to keep improving and UBS to stop deteriorating.

**Bear case (~$240).** Data-center project awards slow or a hyperscaler pauses capex, exposing the working-capital intensity that gutted FY2025 cash flow. FCF stays thin, the market re-rates the stock toward a high-leverage cyclical multiple (~13–15x trough EPS), and the OBBBA interest-deduction change bites. This is the analyst low target of $240, roughly -32% from current, a meaningful asymmetry that argues against chasing at $350.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth and competitive trajectory justify holding for appreciation over the next 12 months, but the entry point at ~$350 near all-time highs offers limited margin of safety against a -32% bear case.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August), with cash-conversion the headline metric, followed by Q3.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$280s to low-$300s, which would restore a 2:1 reward/risk against the bear case.
- Two consecutive quarters of operating cash flow tracking net income (FCF conversion >70%), confirming the FY2025 working-capital build was transitory.
- Net leverage falling below 3.0x alongside sustained CSS organic growth above 15%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sequential decline in CSS organic growth below mid-single digits, or evidence of a hyperscaler capex pause.
- Operating cash flow staying depressed (<$150M for a second consecutive year on a trailing basis) while leverage rises back above 3.5x.
- Gross-margin compression continuing beyond FY2025 levels with no offsetting operating leverage, signaling the mix shift is structurally dilutive.

## Conclusion

Wesco has demonstrated a genuine 2026 growth-and-margin improvement, anchored in a confirmed, broad-based data-center infrastructure cycle, with deleveraging and a refinancing tailwind improving the per-share economics that the noisy FY2025 headline obscured. Competitive positioning is improving as the company grows in its fastest-growing end-markets. The constraints are equally clear: thin free cash flow, 3.2x leverage, project-cycle lumpiness, and a valuation near 52-week highs that already discounts much of the re-rating. The forward multiple (~22x normalized, ~18.6x on next-year consensus) is reasonable but not cheap once the leverage and FCF profile are accounted for, and the -32% bear case against a +13–25% base/bull case is not the asymmetry we require to chase strength.

We rate WCC a HOLD with a growth bias and an overall score of 6.0, consistent with the improving trajectory across prior reports. The right approach is to accumulate on pullbacks toward the high-$280s to low-$300s rather than add at $350, and to use Q2 cash conversion as the decisive tell for whether this upgrades to a BUY.
