## Executive Summary

Lowe's has spent the past year converting a stalled retail franchise into a partially-diversified building products platform, and the accounting consequences of that decision now dominate the reported numbers in ways that cut in both directions. FY2025 net sales of $86,286M were up 3.1%, but they remain *below* FY2023's $86,377M, and the entire increase is more than accounted for by $2,208M of revenue from the acquired Foundation Building Materials (FBM, $8.8B, October 2025) and Artisan Design Group (ADG, $1.3B, June 2025) businesses. The Retail Home Improvement segment grew 0.5%. Consolidated operating margin fell 74bps to 11.77%, but that headline is misleading: the Retail segment's gross margin *expanded* 53bps in FY2025, and Retail operating margin *rose* to 12.13% in Q1 FY2026 from 11.92%. The consolidated compression is largely the arithmetic of consolidating a distribution business earning roughly 3-4% EBITDA margins against a retailer earning 12%.

The bought growth does not yet convert to per-share earnings. The Other segment lost $70M in FY2025 and $32M in Q1 FY2026 at the operating line, driven principally by intangible amortization scheduled at $397M in FY2026 (roughly $0.54 per share after tax at the 23.9% effective rate). Management's reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $12.25–$12.75 *adjusted* diluted EPS therefore corresponds to implied GAAP EPS of roughly $11.65–$12.20 against FY2025 GAAP of $11.85 — approximately flat GAAP earnings on 7-9% revenue growth. The share repurchase program that reduced the count from 601M to 560M between February 2023 and January 2026 was paused in FY2025 ($0 of program repurchases versus $6,199M in FY2023) and has resumed at only $302M per quarter, roughly 1.0% of market capitalization annually versus about 5% three years ago. The EPS algorithm has structurally changed.

The macro backdrop has deteriorated since the last review. CEO Marvin Ellison described this as the "most difficult housing market since the financial crisis" and identified a sustained sub-6% mortgage rate as the demand unlock. The 30-year rate moved from 6.23% to 6.94% in July following escalation in the Iran conflict, and independent forecasters (Reuters property poll, Zillow) expect subdued turnover and flat prices through 2027. At 17.8x guided adjusted EPS with a 6.1% free cash flow yield, Lowe's is not expensive; at a probability-weighted expected return of roughly 9-10% over 12-18 months against a 19% bear case, with the price up 9.6% since mid-July while estimates ticked down and mortgage rates rose, it is not compelling either. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

As of January 30, 2026 Lowe's operated 1,759 stores and outlets in the United States (approximately 196 million square feet of selling space, roughly 37,000 SKUs per store, 120-plus supply chain facilities) with about 167,000 full-time and 109,000 part-time associates at a $15/hour minimum starting wage. The FY2025 acquisitions added over 540 branch locations in the U.S. and Canada.

Reporting structure now shows one reportable segment, Retail Home Improvement ($84,078M of FY2025 sales, 97.4% of total), with FBM's two operating lines (Ceilings and Wall Systems; Commercial Doors and Hardware) plus ADG's Interior Finishes aggregated into "Other" ($2,208M, 2.6%). Corporate expenses are allocated to the individual operating segments, which matters when interpreting the Other segment's loss.

Retail merchandise divisions in FY2025: Home Décor $31,527M (36.5%), Building Products $26,533M (30.7%), Hardlines $24,185M (28.0%). The largest single category is Appliances at $12,891M, or 14.9% of total sales — a big-ticket, credit-sensitive line. Services revenue (primarily subcontracted installation) rose 31% to $2,542M as ADG's installation model entered the mix.

The strategic rationale is explicit in the filings: ADG expands the Pro offering into a new distribution channel, and FBM accelerates the Total Home strategy through expanded Pro capabilities and trade credit. The underlying problem it addresses is real — Ellison stated on the Q1 call that 60-65% of revenue comes from the DIY customer, implying Pro is only 35-40% of the mix, well below Home Depot's historical Pro weighting.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and comparable sales

| Fiscal year (52 wks) | Net sales | Growth | Comps |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 (Feb 2, 2024) | $86,377M | — | — |
| FY2024 (Jan 31, 2025) | $83,674M | −3.1% | — |
| FY2025 (Jan 30, 2026) | $86,286M | +3.1% | +0.2% |
| Q1 FY2026 (May 1, 2026) | $23,078M | +10.3% | +0.6% |

All three fiscal years were 52 weeks, so the comparison is clean. The composition of Q1 comps is the informative datum: average ticket +1.5%, transactions −0.9%. Four consecutive quarters of positive comps and online sales +15.5% are genuine, but the growth is price and mix, not more customers or more projects. Home Depot's most recent reported quarter showed the same signature (comps +0.3%, ticket +2.4%, transactions −1.6%), which establishes the traffic decline as sector-wide demand rather than Lowe's-specific execution failure.

### Margins: separating mix from operations

FY2025 consolidated: gross margin 33.48% (+16bps), SG&A 19.46% (+72bps of deleverage), D&A 2.25%, operating margin 11.77% (−74bps).

FY2025 Retail segment: cost of sales 66.15% versus 66.68%, so gross margin expanded 53bps to 33.85%. Segment SG&A rose 80bps on employee compensation and benefits at 13.26% of sales (versus 12.94% in FY2024 and 12.50% in FY2023 — 76bps of labor deleverage on flat sales) and advertising at 1.16% of sales versus 0.96% in FY2023, meaning roughly 17% more advertising dollars over two years delivered flat comps. Retail segment operating margin: 12.16% (FY2025) versus 12.51% (FY2024) versus 13.38% (FY2023).

Q1 FY2026 reverses the direction: consolidated gross margin fell 70bps to 32.68%, but the Retail segment operating margin *expanded* to 12.13% from 11.92% while Other lost $32M on $1,754M. The ~600 corporate role reductions announced in February 2026 and the segment gross margin gains are showing up. One quarter is not a trend, and there is a legitimate alternative explanation: the allocation basis for corporate expense may have shifted with the addition of three new operating segments, which could flatter Retail at Other's expense. Full-year FY2026 segment data is the first clean look.

### The acquisitions are GAAP-dilutive at the operating line

Reconciling D&A: FY2025 consolidated $1,941M versus $1,808M in Retail implies ~$133M in Other; added back to the $70M operating loss, that is roughly +$63M of segment EBITDA on $2,208M (~2.9%). For Q1 FY2026, consolidated $566M versus $466M in Retail implies ~$100M in Other, giving ~+$68M on $1,754M (~3.9%). The Q1 GAAP-to-adjusted bridge corroborates: $2.90 GAAP versus $3.03 adjusted is $0.13, roughly $96M pre-tax.

The forward amortization schedule is $397M in FY2026, then $329M annually through FY2030, with $4,067M thereafter on a $5,774M definite-lived base (FBM customer relationships of $3,920M and ADG's $550M each assigned 20-year lives). Amortization is non-cash and does not affect free cash flow, debt service or dividend capacity, so excluding it from an adjusted measure is defensible. But it does mean any comparison of the $12.50 adjusted midpoint to $11.85 of FY2025 GAAP is not like-for-like.

Two caveats argue against concluding the deals are poor. The one-month consolidation lag places FBM/ADG's seasonally weakest construction months (roughly January–March) in Lowe's Q1; the Other segment bears allocated corporate overhead; and acquisition costs were expensed as incurred while the purchase price allocation remains preliminary. Normalized segment profitability is not yet observable.

FY2025 FCF of $7,651M is an 8.9% FCF margin and a 6.1% yield on $125.0B. Dividends consume 34% of FCF and 40% of GAAP net income — comfortable coverage on a $4.80 annualized rate (2.15% yield).

Three composition points. FY2025 OCF was helped by a $703M inventory release and hurt by $645M of other operating liability movement (income taxes payable fell from $491M to $23M; cash taxes paid rose from $1,648M to $2,505M). Lowe's purchased $1.0B of federal transferable clean-energy tax credits in FY2025 (versus $909M and $143M in prior years), so the 23.9% effective rate embeds a purchased benefit dependent on continued transferability provisions. The supplier finance balance was $1,440M, down from $1,511M, so the program is not currently flattering operating cash flow.

### Balance sheet

Total assets rose from $43,102M to $54,144M, concentrated in goodwill ($311M → $3,945M) and intangibles ($277M → $5,908M) — combined $9,853M, or 18.2% of assets. Receivables rose from $94M to $1,090M, a new trade-credit exposure that essentially did not exist before (retail proprietary credit is extended and held by Synchrony Bank).

Total long-term debt was $39,921M including $391M of finance leases, with no commercial paper outstanding and $5.0B of undrawn revolver capacity. FY2025 additions were a $2.0B term loan (4.880%, matures October 2028) and $5.0B of notes issued September 30, 2025 at 3.950%–4.850%. The maturity ladder: FY2026 $2,350M, FY2027 $3,018M, FY2028 $5,005M, FY2029 $1,811M, FY2030 $2,500M, thereafter $25,135M. The September 2025 indenture does not require specified financial ratios, net worth or liquidity levels, and lease agreements contain no material restrictions or residual value guarantees.

Derived coverage: EBIT $10,153M ÷ gross interest of $1,507M = 6.7x. EBITDA of ~$12,347M gives 8.2x interest coverage and 3.23x gross debt/EBITDA (3.15x net). Including the $4,756M operating lease liability, adjusted leverage is roughly 3.41x EBITDAR. FCF after dividends of $5,015M covers FY2026 maturities of $2,350M more than twice. The ratio is overstated relative to a run rate — FY2025 EBITDA captured only ~2.7 months of FBM and ~7 months of ADG.

Shareholders' deficit of $(9,917)M (improved from $(14,231)M; $(9,270)M at May 1, 2026) is the accounting residue of two decades of repurchasing above book value, not accumulated operating losses — the company earned $21.3B over the three years presented. Standard solvency screens keyed to equity (Altman Z, ROE, debt-to-equity) are uninformative here; coverage, the maturity ladder and covenant terms are the correct tools. The deficit is not costless, however: there is no loss-absorption buffer against a $9.85B goodwill and intangible base, and the $25.1B of post-FY2030 maturities carry coupons as low as 3.29-3.88%, so eventual refinancing is a durable interest-expense headwind. Unsecured notes carried at $35,186M had a fair value of $32,070M at May 1, 2026 (~91%), which duration alone explains.

Dilution discipline is clean: roughly 3.9M contingent shares even at maximum PSU vesting (~0.7% of shares outstanding), with only 0.2M anti-dilutive securities excluded.

## Growth Analysis

The honest characterization is that Lowe's has no organic growth and has purchased revenue growth at a cost of roughly 8% of market capitalization plus the suspension of its buyback.

FY2026 guidance of $92–94B implies a $5.7–7.7B increase over FY2025. Annualizing Q1 FY2026 Other segment sales of $1,754M gives a run rate above $7B against the $2,208M captured in FY2025 — roughly $5B of the guided increase is acquisition annualization. On the low end of comp guidance (flat), organic growth is close to zero.

Three forward drivers are real but small relative to the base. Online sales grew 15.5% in Q1. Services revenue grew 31% to $2,542M. And the Pro channel expansion addresses a genuine structural gap: Lowe's 60-65% DIY skew is more exposed to the credit-constrained discretionary consumer than Home Depot's mix, and the acquisitions reach large builders and commercial contractors the store base never served.

Against that, the demand environment is deteriorating rather than stabilizing. The 30-year mortgage rate is against the sub-6% threshold management identified as the unlock. Brokers now expect roughly 4 million existing home sales in 2026; a Reuters property poll expects subdued turnover through 2027; Zillow forecasts flat values and flat sales. Home improvement spending tracks housing turnover (people renovate when they move) and the cost of tappable equity — both impaired. Management flagged more challenges in Q2 than Q1, citing high oil prices.

Estimate revisions confirm no positive inflection: current quarter −4.1% over 90 days, current year −0.9% to $12.49, next year −1.3% to $13.46. The company has beaten adjusted consensus in each of the last four quarters, but by 0.8-2.1% — a managed-expectations pattern that supports confidence in the guide without implying upside surprise.

The supplied peer set is not usable for relative valuation — SBUX at 78.1x, MAR at 41.6x, ROST at 33.4x, BKNG at 21.5x and RCL at 17.5x are asset-light travel, restaurant and off-price models with different capital intensity and cyclicality. The median of 33.4x tells us nothing about a big-box home improvement retailer with 3.2x leverage and flat comps. The only real comparable is Home Depot, and the read-through from HD's most recent quarter (comps +0.3%, adjusted operating margin −120bps) is that the entire category is in a demand trough.

The more useful anchors are absolute. A 6.1% FCF yield with 34% dividend coverage provides real support; the FY2027 forward multiple of 16.6x is below Lowe's own typical 18-20x range in stable periods. But forward EPS growth from $12.49 to $13.46 is 7.8%, giving a PEG of roughly 2.3 — the stock is not cheap relative to the earnings growth it is expected to deliver. And the FCF yield will compress: annualizing the September 2025 debt adds roughly $200-300M of interest expense in FY2026 (Q1 cash interest paid was $751M on the semiannual calendar), partly offset by FBM/ADG EBITDA annualization.

The EV/EBITDA figure of 13.3x overstates the multiple because the denominator lacks a full year of the acquired businesses; on a normalized EBITDA of roughly $13.0–13.5B including full-year FBM/ADG, EV/EBITDA is closer to 12.1–12.6x.

Analyst consensus (mean $262.91, median $270.00, high $300, low $202) sits 18% above spot with 24 buy-or-better ratings versus 10 holds and 1 strong sell, unchanged over three months. On a stock with mildly *negative* estimate revisions and flat GAAP earnings, that consensus target is a lagging indicator rather than a floor: targets function as floors on inflecting names with rising estimates, and this is a name with falling estimates in a deteriorating macro.

My base case is $245, or 18.2x FY2027 consensus of $13.46, roughly the multiple appropriate for flat-to-modestly-growing earnings with a 2.15% yield and no buyback lever. That is +9.8% from spot, +12.0% including the dividend, which places fair value approximately at, to modestly above, the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining industry dynamic is a capital-intensive contest for the large Pro/builder channel, and Lowe's is the follower. Home Depot acquired SRS Distribution in 2024 (widely reported at roughly $18B, unverified against filings), and SRS acquired GMS Inc. for $5.5B, completed September 2025 (tier-3 sourcing, unverified against HD filings). In July 2026 Home Depot created an "Office of Pro Acceleration" to coordinate its contractor businesses against a stated $700B Pro market.

The overlap is direct. FBM's two operating segments are Ceilings and Wall Systems and Commercial Doors and Hardware; GMS distributes drywall, ceilings and steel framing. These assets changed hands roughly one month apart in autumn 2025. Lowe's acquired a platform in a category where its primary competitor had just bought a comparable asset and had a two-year head start on distribution integration through SRS.

Lowe's retains genuine structural assets: national store density supporting same-day availability, private brands (Kobalt, STAINMASTER, allen+roth), an installation services network, and a proprietary credit program that keeps receivables off balance sheet. As a mass retailer it has no customer concentration, a real advantage over the industrial distribution comparables its new segments resemble. The Q1 Retail margin expansion of 21bps against Home Depot's compression is suggestive, though the quarters are not matched and the comparison is directional only.

The trajectory assessment: competitive position is roughly stable, with the Pro gap being closed by capital deployment rather than organic share gain, into a market where the competitor moved first and larger. That justifies a below-average score on a dimension where trajectory matters more than level.

## Risk Assessment

**Housing turnover is the binding constraint and is largely priced.** The stock is down 23.8% from its February high of $293.06 on essentially this factor. Management has been explicit about the threshold (sub-6% mortgages) and external forecasts point the other way through 2027. This is a well-understood risk; the market has repriced accordingly.

**Integration and impairment risk is less priced.** Of FBM's $8,778M purchase price, 94.5% was allocated to goodwill ($3,254M) and intangibles ($5,041M), leaving roughly $483M of net tangible assets. Deloitte designated the $3,920M customer relationship valuation a critical audit matter, citing reliance on management estimates of revenue growth, EBITDA margin and discount rate. The allocation is preliminary and subject to measurement-period adjustment. Management excluded FBM from its FY2025 internal-control-over-financial-reporting assessment under permitted first-year SEC guidance, so FBM's controls are untested by auditor opinion. With negative book equity, an impairment flows straight to earnings with no cushion. The mitigating point: heavy intangible allocation is normal for a services and distribution business with few hard assets and is not itself evidence of overpayment; no impairment existed at the FY2025 annual test.

**Tariffs are two-sided and unquantified.** The subsequent-events note discloses that IEEPA tariffs were declared invalid on February 20, 2026 and that the Court of International Trade ordered CBP on March 4, 2026 to liquidate non-final entries without IEEPA duties. Lowe's states it cannot reasonably estimate the effect on FY2026. Home Depot has applied for refunds; Lowe's has not publicly disclosed whether it has. This is optionality of unknown sign and size: a potential refund asset offset by the loss of a pricing/ticket tailwind and the possibility that duties are reconstituted under Section 232 or 301 authorities, which the IEEPA ruling does not address.

**Transaction declines with a possibly-tariff-derived ticket tailwind.** If part of the +1.5% ticket reflects tariff pass-through and tariffs unwind, the ticket tailwind fades while the −0.9% transaction decline persists. The composition of comps, not the headline, is the metric to watch.

**Labor deleverage** of 76bps over two years on flat sales is structural given a $15 starting wage and ~276,000 associates, though the Q1 margin improvement and the ~600 corporate role reductions suggest stabilization.

**New exposures from the acquisitions:** a limited number of FBM employees are union-represented (a category absent from the legacy non-union retail workforce), and $1,090M of receivables introduces builder credit-cycle exposure that previously sat with Synchrony.

**Governance items:** combined Chairman/President/CEO role; a related-party vendor providing transportation and business services on whose board the CEO sits, with purchases of $237M in FY2025 (~0.4% of cost of sales, properly disclosed, vendor unnamed in the filing). No restatement, auditor change or material weakness in the reviewed 8-K window; Deloitte has audited since 1962.

**Absent risks:** litigation is disclosed as immaterial individually and in aggregate with immaterial accruals; there is no customer concentration; there are no maintenance financial covenants.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.3% against 27.2% one-year realized (a 12.1-point spread, 63rd percentile within the chain) with a backwardated term structure (39.3% near versus 30.3% at six months) prices an imminent event — the Q2 FY2026 report, expected in roughly two weeks. The August 21 tenor implies a 6.3% move. Put/call skew is balanced across tenors (−2.1% to −2.7%), and put/call open interest has fallen from 2.20 to 1.19 since July 21 while the stock rose 9.6%, so protection has been reduced rather than accumulated. Volume put/call of 0.50 and a single unusual print (380 contracts of the August 21 $228 call, 3.3x open interest) tilt marginally constructive into the print. Short interest of 2.2% of float and 3.7 days to cover means no crowded bearish position.

The market is pricing size, not direction, into an earnings binary, and near-term options are expensive relative to realized volatility. For a fundamental holder that argues against adding immediately ahead of the print; for a holder with an existing position, the rich near-term vol makes covered-call overwriting reasonably priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($300, +34.4%)

Mortgage rates break below 6% as the Fed's hawkish faction loses the argument on a contracting labor market, releasing pent-up housing turnover from a roughly 4-million-existing-sale trough. Comps accelerate to +3-4% with transactions turning positive. The Retail segment's Q1 margin inflection (+21bps) proves durable and extends as advertising and labor leverage return. FBM and ADG annualize into full-year seasonality, cross-sell into the small-and-medium Pro base, and deliver the synergies the Total Home thesis assumes, moving Other-segment EBITDA margins from ~3-4% toward the high single digits. Leverage falls below 2.5x on EBITDA growth rather than debt paydown, and the buyback resumes toward $4-5B annually against $10.8B of authorization. An IEEPA refund materializes as a cash windfall. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $14.50 and the multiple re-rates to 21x. This path requires the rate environment to cooperate, which is outside management's control.

### Bear Case ($180, −19.4%)

Housing turnover stays at 4 million sales through 2027 as a higher-for-longer rate path holds — 30-year mortgages above 6.5%, the long end pressured by deficit and AI-related debt supply, and a Fed with three hawkish dissents unable to signal cuts. The ticket tailwind fades as tariffs unwind while transactions continue to decline, pushing comps negative. Labor deleverage resumes. FBM's forecast EBITDA margin proves optimistic, and the preliminary purchase price allocation is followed by a goodwill or customer-relationship impairment that hits earnings with no equity cushion. Interest expense annualization plus rising refinancing coupons compress FCF toward $6.5B. FY2027 adjusted EPS falls to $11.75 and the multiple compresses to 15x. The 52-week low of $199.40 and the low analyst target of $202 sit above this level, so the bear case requires genuine estimate cuts rather than sentiment alone.

### Base Case ($245, +9.8%)

Housing stays flat-to-slightly-worse. Comps land at the low end of guidance (flat to +1%), with FY2026 adjusted EPS at or slightly below the $12.50 midpoint and GAAP EPS approximately flat at $11.7–12.0. FBM/ADG annualize into a full year, moving Other-segment EBITDA to roughly 5-6% as seasonality normalizes and acquisition costs stop recurring, but synergies are gradual. Buybacks continue at $300-400M per quarter, reducing the share count by about 1% annually. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $13.25–13.50, and an 18.2x multiple on $13.46 gives $245. Total return of roughly 12% including the dividend over 12-18 months.

Probability-weighting base at 55%, bull at 20% and bear at 25% gives an expected value of $240, or +7.4% in price and roughly +9.6% with the dividend. That is a fair-to-modestly-positive expected return, not an asymmetric one, and it does not clear the bar for new capital ahead of a print with a 6.3% implied move.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 FY2026 results, expected mid-to-late August 2026 (approximately two weeks out). Full-year FY2026 segment disclosure, expected March 2027, is the first clean look at normalized FBM/ADG profitability.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Comparable-sale transactions turn positive (versus −0.9% in Q1) for two consecutive quarters, confirming that growth is no longer dependent on a possibly-tariff-derived ticket tailwind.
2. Retail Home Improvement segment operating margin sustains above 12.1% for two more quarters while consolidated gross margin stabilizes, converting the Q1 inflection from one datum into a trend.
3. Other-segment operating income turns positive, or implied Other-segment EBITDA margin (segment operating income plus imputed D&A) reaches 6%+, indicating the $10.1B deployment is converting.
4. A price near $190-195 — roughly 14.5x FY2027 consensus and a 7.2% FCF yield — which would restore genuine asymmetry against the bear case without requiring any operational improvement.
5. Buyback pace returning above $750M per quarter, signalling management views leverage as normalized.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $12.25, or comp guidance cut below flat.
2. Any goodwill or customer-relationship impairment charge against the $9,853M goodwill-and-intangible base, or a material measurement-period adjustment to the preliminary FBM allocation.
3. Comps turning negative with transactions declining more than 2%, which would indicate the ticket tailwind has reversed while traffic deteriorates.
4. Retail segment operating margin falling back below 11.9%, reversing the Q1 inflection and confirming that labor and advertising deleverage has resumed.
5. Consensus FY2027 EPS revised below $12.75 (a further ~5% cut from $13.46), which would break the base-case multiple math.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** maintain. Dividend coverage at 34% of FCF is secure, coverage ratios are sound, and there are no maintenance covenants. Rich near-term implied volatility (39.3% versus 27.2% realized) makes covered-call overwriting into the August print a reasonably compensated way to harvest the event premium.

## Conclusion

The score on this name has declined from 5.9 in February to 5.5 in July, and the current analysis extends that modestly to 5.3. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the case for a stronger call in either direction has weakened rather than strengthened. Since the prior review the stock has risen 9.6% (from $203.51 on July 21 to $223.21) while the 30-year mortgage rate rose from 6.23% to 6.94%, next-year consensus was trimmed 1.3%, and no new operating evidence arrived. Price up on marginally worse inputs is a deterioration in risk/reward, and it is the primary reason the valuation dimension moves lower.

The fundamental picture is genuinely mixed. Two facts pull upward: the core retail margin has inflected (+53bps of segment gross margin in FY2025, +21bps of segment operating margin in Q1 FY2026) after two years of deleverage, and the balance sheet is more resilient than any equity-based screen suggests — 6.7x EBIT coverage, $7.65B of FCF, no maintenance covenants, $5.0B of undrawn revolver, and 63% of debt maturing beyond FY2030. At 17.8x guided adjusted EPS, 16.6x FY2027 consensus and a 6.1% FCF yield, Lowe's is priced for the trough it is in. The base case of $245 gives roughly 12% total return over 12-18 months, against a 19% bear case that requires only continuation of the current rate environment. Existing holders should stay; new capital has no urgency ahead of the August print and a better entry near $190-195, or confirmation that transactions have turned positive.
