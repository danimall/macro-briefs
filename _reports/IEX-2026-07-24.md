**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

IDEX Corporation is a decentralized diversified industrial that manufactures mission-critical, highly-engineered components sold into fragmented niche markets where it holds leadership positions and where no single customer exceeds 3% of net sales. The business model is asset-light and cash-generative: FY2025 free cash flow was $617M on 17.8% FCF margin with capex under 2% of sales. The investment debate is narrow and well-defined. The bull case rests on a genuine early-2026 organic inflection — Q1 2026 organic growth of +5% (versus FY25's anemic +1%), with the Health & Science Technologies (HST) segment posting +11% organic on AI data-center power, semiconductor, and space/defense demand, prompting management to raise full-year profit guidance. The bear case is that the stock, near its 52-week high at ~28x adjusted / ~33x trailing GAAP EPS, already prices this inflection while the Fluid & Metering (FMT) and Fire & Safety/Diversified (FSDP) segments remain in a shallow, uneven recovery.

The financial quality here is real and the recent trajectory in our internal scoring reflects it: Financial Health has improved (7.0 → 7.6) as the balance sheet demonstrated ample covenant headroom (net leverage ~1.96x vs. 3.5x max) and strong cash conversion. The same trajectory shows Management Quality and Risk Factors eroding, driven by a broad leadership transition (new CFO after an interim period, departed Chief Strategy Officer, segment reorganization) and a heavy goodwill/intangible concentration (~67% of assets) that becomes a latent impairment risk if FMT/FSDP recoveries stall. The company already took a small $4.3M FMT trade-name impairment in Q1 2026.

We rate IEX a HOLD (growth_hold). This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and we hold the same view for a specific reason: the fundamentals are improving (confirming the direction of our thesis), but the valuation is not offering asymmetric upside. At ~28x forward on rising-but-modestly-revised estimates, the multiple sits at the peer-median level, so an investor is paying full quality price for a franchise whose organic engine is only just re-accelerating from a depressed base. We would become buyers on a pullback into the low-$190s, or on confirmation that HST's AI-driven demand is durable across two-to-three quarters and FMT begins to recover.

## Company Overview

IDEX operates 50+ autonomous subsidiaries under an "8020" operating framework (concentrating resources on the 20% of products and customers that drive 80% of value) and a serial bolt-on acquisition strategy. The portfolio breaks into three reportable segments (FY2025):

- **Health & Science Technologies (HST) — 43% of sales, now the largest and fastest-growing segment.** Precision fluidics, optics, sealing, pneumatics, sintered porous metals (Mott), and diamond tools/optics (Micro-LAM). End markets include life sciences, semiconductor, data-center power, and space & defense. FY25 net sales +15% (+4% organic).
- **Fluid & Metering Technologies (FMT) — 35% of sales, highest-margin (~33% EBITDA).** Positive-displacement pumps (Viking), water/wastewater metering (ADS), energy flow meters, agriculture (Banjo), and specialty valves. FY25 organic flat; softness in chemical, energy, and industrial partly offset by municipal water strength.
- **Fire & Safety/Diversified Products (FSDP) — 22% of sales.** Fire pumps and rescue tools (Hale, Hurst Jaws of Life), paint/colorant dispensing, and BAND-IT stainless banding. FY25 flat, with weakness in Dispensing and international Fire.

The competitive advantage is engineering depth, niche leadership, a short-cycle order book, a decentralized entrepreneurial structure, and a disciplined M&A engine. Geographic mix is balanced (51% U.S. / 49% international). IDEX is a component supplier *into* the AI data-center and semiconductor supply chains, which diversifies its exposure but also makes it indirect and one step removed from the underlying capex signal.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings ($M):** Revenue grew from $3,182 (2022) to $3,274 (2023), was flat at $3,269 (2024), then reached a record $3,458 in 2025 (+5.8% YoY, but only +1% organic). Net income declined over the same period: $587 (2022) → $596 (2023) → $505 (2024) → $483 (2025). Reported diluted EPS fell 3% to $6.41 in 2025; adjusted diluted EPS rose 1% to $7.95.

The 2025 net-income decline is a misleading headline. Operating income actually rose 3% to $699M and gross margin expanded 30bps to 44.5%. The net-income drop came entirely from below-the-line items: interest expense rose 45% to $64M (debt funding the Mott acquisition), acquisition intangible amortization rose to $131M from $107M, and the tax rate rose to 23.7% from 21.1% (lost foreign-source benefits plus OBBBA changes). These are financing and accounting artifacts of the M&A strategy, not operating deterioration.

**Margins:** Operating margin 20.2%, net margin 14.0%, adjusted EBITDA margin 26.8%. FMT is the margin leader (~33%), FSDP ~29%, HST ~26.6%.

**Cash flow quality is excellent.** FY25 operating cash flow was $680M (141% of net income), free cash flow $617M (17.8% FCF margin, 103% of adjusted net income). FCF conversion above 100% signals high-quality earnings and confirms this is a genuinely asset-light compounder.

**Balance sheet:** Total assets $6.93B, of which goodwill and intangibles total ~$4.66B (~67%); goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles alone are 51%. Long-term debt is $1.82B; D/E 0.72; net leverage ~1.96x against a covenant max of 3.5x — ample headroom. Current ratio 2.9. Investment-grade rated (S&P BBB, Moody's Baa2, Fitch BBB+, all reaffirmed stable through Feb 2026). Interest coverage is 13.9x. The Altman Z of 2.66 ("grey zone") is a mechanical artifact of the heavy intangible base, not a distress signal.

**Capital allocation is balanced.** FY25 returned $461M to shareholders ($248M buybacks + $213M dividends; the dividend was raised 3% to $0.71/quarter, continuing a multi-decade growth record). The board authorized an additional $635M buyback in September 2025 (~$850M remaining as of Q1'26). M&A remains the primary growth lever.

**Q1 2026 — a clear positive inflection (10-Q filed 4/29/26):**
- Net sales $886.9M, +9% (+5% organic) — a marked acceleration from FY25's +1% organic.
- HST organic +11%, EBITDA margin +100bps to 26.6%, explicitly driven by AI data-center power, semiconductor, and space/defense.
- Reported EPS $1.61 (+28%); adjusted EPS $2.00 (+14%), beating consensus ($1.79) by ~12%.
- Operating income +21%; interest expense stabilized as Mott anniversaried.
- Management raised full-year profit guidance on AI data-center demand.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats (Q1'26 +11.7%, Q4'25 +1.8%, Q3'25 +4.1%, Q2'25 +2.9%).

## Growth Analysis

The core growth question is durability. FY25 organic growth was weak, and the improvement to +5% in Q1'26 is the crux of the bull case. The driver is concentrated in HST, where the causal chain is: hyperscaler AI capex → data-center power and liquid-cooling buildout → demand for precision fluidics, sintered porous metals, and thermal/flow-control components → HST order acceleration. This chain has broad external confirmation across the AI infrastructure ecosystem, where data-center demand is now visible not only in hyperscaler capex guidance but in downstream hard data across power generation, electrical equipment, and structural components. The breadth of that confirmation argues that the HST tailwind is not a single-company anomaly.

The countervailing signal is that FMT (chemical, energy, agriculture, industrial) and FSDP (Dispensing, international Fire) remain soft, and automotive and industrial businesses within HST "have yet to experience a meaningful recovery." The near-term growth story is a barbell: a genuine secular tailwind in AI-adjacent HST, offset by a shallow cyclical recovery in the roughly 57% of revenue outside HST.

Estimate revisions are directionally positive but modest, which tempers the growth score. Current-year EPS moved to $8.50 from $8.27 ninety days ago (+2.8%), and next-year to $9.25 from $8.97 (+3.1%). Over the last 30 days revisions have been essentially flat (+0.1%). This does not clear the +30%-in-90-days threshold that would flag a powerful inflection signal — IDEX's revision velocity is that of a solid, mid-single-digit compounder catching a tailwind, not a stock at a violent earnings trough. Revenue growth of +9% (with +5% organic) is real but well below the >40% growth that would independently justify treating trailing multiples as meaningless.

R&D and reinvestment run through the M&A engine (Micro-LAM in July 2025, Mott in September 2024) and organic engineering. The growth algorithm depends on continually sourcing, financing, and integrating bolt-ons — a structural dependence that we address in Risk Assessment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $223.27 (market cap ~$16.5B), IEX trades at ~33x trailing GAAP EPS ($6.41) and ~28x on the adjusted ~$8/share run-rate, or ~26x forward on the $8.50 current-year consensus. The forward P/E on next-year's $9.25 is ~24x.

Against peers in Industrial Machinery & Supplies & Components, IDEX is priced at, not below, fair value:

|--------|-----|-----------|
| NDSN | 30.5 | 1.2% |
| MLI | 17.7 | 1.1% |
| ITT | 32.9 | 0.8% |
| LECO | 26.6 | 1.2% |
| FTV | 35.2 | 0.4% |
| **Peer median** | **30.5** | **1.1%** |

IEX's trailing and adjusted multiples sit right at the peer-median 30.5x. The stock is trading in line with its quality-adjusted peers, not at a discount. The 1.3% dividend yield is modestly above the 1.1% peer median but is not itself a valuation support at this multiple.

The cycle-adjusted valuation framework does not rescue the bull case here. That framework applies most forcefully when a stock shows estimate revisions >30% in 90 days or revenue growth >40% with expanding margins — trough conditions where trailing multiples are meaningless. IDEX shows neither: its estimate revisions are +2.8% to +3.1% over 90 days, and revenue growth is +9%. The margin trajectory is favorable (HST EBITDA +100bps, operating income +21% in Q1), which supports the quality, but not to a degree that reprices the stock as a hidden trough. On a PEG basis, ~26x forward against high-single-digit to low-double-digit expected EPS growth yields a PEG well above 1.5, which does not signal asymmetric upside.

A low multiple alone is never a buy signal, and in this system's experience cheap-looking names have tended to be value traps absent a specific catalyst. That caution runs the other way here: IEX is not cheap, so the risk is paying a full multiple for growth that is only beginning to re-accelerate. Analyst targets (low $210, median $242.5, high $261) bracket the current price with the median ~9% above spot, but on a stock already at premium multiples, consensus targets tend to reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation. We do not treat the $242.5 median as a floor here.

The valuation score of 4.8 reflects that the multiple is fair-to-slightly-full for the delivered growth, with no identifiable mispricing to exploit at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

IDEX competes across fragmented niches where no single peer overlaps its full portfolio. It wins on engineering, customization, quality, and delivery rather than scale or price, and it holds leadership positions in most of its markets. The decentralized, entrepreneurial structure and the institutionalized 8020 and M&A playbook are genuine, durable advantages.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. In HST, IDEX is well-positioned as a component supplier into the AI data-center power, liquid-cooling, and semiconductor supply chains, a structural tailwind that is broadening. The Mott (sintered porous metals) and Micro-LAM (advanced optics / laser-assisted machining) acquisitions deepen exactly the capabilities that serve semiconductor and space/defense customers. This is share-gaining, moat-widening activity in the fastest-growing part of the portfolio.

Against that, roughly 57% of revenue (FMT + FSDP) sits in markets in shallow recovery, and there is a candid signal on relative performance: IDEX's three-year TSR (indexed at 95) materially lagged its peer group (191), and the 2023 PSU grant paid out 0% on that relative underperformance. That is not evidence of competitive erosion in its niches, but it does show the company was a relative laggard heading into the 2026 inflection. The competitive-position score of 6.3 reflects a genuinely improving HST franchise weighed against sluggish core-industrial segments and a recent history of relative underperformance.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Goodwill/intangible impairment (highest company-specific balance-sheet risk).** The Q1'26 $4.3M FMT trade-name impairment was minor but illustrates the mechanism: a shortfall in an acquired reporting unit's forecast triggers non-cash charges. The Oct 31, 2025 annual test showed all units "substantially in excess" of carrying value, but WACC assumptions (9.25–10.5%) and market multiples (11–17x) leave the softer FMT/FSDP units more exposed if their recoveries stall.

2. **Acquisition dependence.** With FY25 organic growth of only +1%, the growth algorithm relies on continually finding, financing, and integrating bolt-ons. A higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of that debt-funded strategy, and integration/overpayment risk is structural.

3. **End-market cyclicality and mixed demand.** FMT and FSDP remain soft, and the AI/data-center tailwind is concentrated in HST. A stall in the non-HST recovery would leave the overall organic rate dependent on a single segment.

4. **AI/data-center demand is two-sided.** IDEX explicitly flags AI-driven data-center and semiconductor demand as a growth driver that is "difficult to predict" and volatile. The same tailwind powering the current beat could reverse if AI capex softens. Because IDEX is one step removed from the underlying capex (a component supplier), its visibility into a turn may lag.

5. **FX translation.** ~34% of sales are in non-USD functional currencies; Q1'26 carried a $43.5M negative cumulative translation swing in equity.

6. **Leadership transition.** A new CFO (post-interim period), a departed Chief Strategy Officer, and a segment reorganization all occurred within ~12 months. The 8020/M&A playbook is deeply institutionalized, mitigating strategic-continuity risk, but the concentration of change warrants monitoring.

The risk-factor score of 5.7 reflects that the impairment concentration and the two-sided AI exposure are specific, identifiable risks that are only partially priced, offset by an investment-grade balance sheet and no distress catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term event stress. Near-term ATM IV is 34.8% versus 26.0% one-year historical volatility (rich by ~8.9pp), sitting in the 58th percentile of its own chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (34.8% at 1 month vs. 26.0% at 6 months), consistent with an approaching earnings print. The put/call skew is balanced (+1.2%) and the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.48, so there is no directional bearish tilt; positioning is neutral-to-slightly-constructive. The implied one-month move is ±8.0%. For a prospective buyer, the practical implication is to wait for the post-earnings IV crush rather than pay elevated near-term premium, and to size for an ±8% swing around the next report. Short interest is uncrowded at 3.6% of float (3.3 days to cover), consistent with no strong informed bearish view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (→ $255–280).** The HST AI/data-center inflection proves durable and broadens, organic growth sustains at mid-single-digits or better, FMT/FSDP recoveries firm up, and the M&A engine deploys the ~$850M buyback authorization plus accretive bolt-ons. Adjusted EPS compounds toward $9.25+ (next-year consensus) and beyond, and the market awards a sustained premium multiple (28–30x). A re-rating on a durable HST secular story plus a core-industrial recovery drives the stock toward the high analyst target of $261 and above.

**Bear Case (→ $175–185, roughly −17% to −22%).** AI data-center demand softens or proves lumpy, HST organic decelerates back toward mid-single-digits or lower, and FMT/FSDP recoveries stall. Persistent softness in FMT/FSDP triggers a non-cash goodwill/intangible impairment. The premium multiple compresses toward the low-20s on ~$8.50 EPS, taking the stock into the mid-$180s or below, toward the $210 low analyst target and beyond.

**Base Case (→ ~$235, roughly +5%).** HST continues to grow on AI/semiconductor demand at a moderating but healthy rate, FMT/FSDP recover slowly, and adjusted EPS reaches ~$8.50 this year and ~$9.25 next year. The multiple holds near ~28x forward on adjusted EPS. This produces ~26x on $9.25 ≈ $240, discounted modestly for the balanced risk/reward to a ~$235 base target.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** 12-month horizon.

- **Base case target: ~$235** (≈ +5% from $223.27). Reasoning: ~26x forward on next-year consensus EPS of $9.25 ≈ $240, discounted modestly for the balanced near-term risk/reward and the fair-to-full current multiple.
- **Bull case target: ~$261–280** (+17% to +25%). 28–30x on $9.25+ EPS with a durable HST re-rating.
- **Bear case target: ~$180** (−19%). Low-20s multiple on ~$8.50 EPS plus a possible impairment overhang.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two-to-three consecutive quarters confirming HST organic growth sustaining at ≥8–10%, evidencing durability of the AI/data-center tailwind rather than a single strong print.
2. FMT organic growth turning clearly positive (≥3%) alongside stabilizing FSDP, broadening the growth base beyond HST.
3. A pullback to the low-$190s (≈24x current-year EPS), which would restore asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. HST organic growth decelerating to low-single-digits or negative, signaling the AI tailwind has faded.
2. A material goodwill/intangible impairment charge (beyond the minor Q1 item) indicating an FMT/FSDP reporting unit has fallen below carrying value.
3. Consensus EPS estimate revisions turning negative over a 90-day window.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (approximately late July 2026), which the options backwardation is already pricing, and the following print to confirm HST durability.

## Conclusion

IDEX is a high-quality, cash-generative diversified industrial experiencing a genuine early-2026 organic inflection, concentrated in its HST segment's AI data-center, semiconductor, and space/defense exposure. The financial health is strong and improving: 141% cash conversion, investment-grade balance sheet, ample covenant headroom, and a disciplined balanced capital-return-plus-M&A model. The 2025 "earnings decline" was an accounting and financing artifact, not operating deterioration, and Q1'26's beat-and-raise confirms the direction of the thesis.

The reason this remains a HOLD, for the fourth time, is valuation and breadth. At the peer-median multiple near a 52-week high, the stock already prices the quality and the inflection. Estimate revisions are positive but modest, well short of the velocity that would flag a powerful undervalued inflection, and roughly 57% of revenue sits in segments still in shallow recovery. The goodwill concentration is a latent impairment risk if FMT/FSDP stall, and a broad leadership transition warrants monitoring. We would move to BUY on a low-$190s entry or on multi-quarter confirmation that the HST tailwind is durable and the core industrial franchises are recovering. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced, and the appropriate action is to hold for the growth trajectory while waiting for a better entry.
