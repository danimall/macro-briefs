**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, with nine nitrogen complexes anchored by Donaldsonville, Louisiana (the world's largest, ~40% of CF's ammonia capacity). The investment question is about where in the cycle we are buying, not business quality, which is high. FY2025 net sales rose 19% to $7.08B and diluted EPS jumped to $8.97 from $6.74, with roughly 92% of the revenue increase coming from price (+19% to $372/ton) rather than volume (+1%). The price gains were amplified by transitory foreign supply disruptions in Egypt, Iran, and Russia.

The headline data look strong: consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 115.2% over 90 days (now $18.23), and next-quarter estimates are up 286.1%. Under normal circumstances, revision velocity of this magnitude is among the strongest forward signals available. Here it requires careful framing. These revisions are commodity-price marks riding the same transitory supply outages that drove the 2025 result, not structural earnings growth. A normalization of the foreign supply outages would pull nitrogen prices lower and reverse the revisions. The earnings power is two-sided rather than durable.

Against that, 2026 carries identifiable, quantified headwinds: the November 2025 Yazoo City incident has idled the entire complex through at least Q4 2026 (cutting 2026 ammonia guidance to ~9.5M tons from 10.1M), Henry Hub gas averaged $6.32/MMBtu in early 2026 versus a $3.53 2025 average (gas is 34% of production cost), a dual C-suite transition is underway, and a December 2025 Executive Order created a fertilizer-supply-chain antitrust task force. The stock at ~$110 sits ~22% below its $141.96 March high. The current multiple is low because the market is correctly capitalizing cyclically-peak earnings at a trough multiple. We rate CF a HOLD with a 5.8 overall score, consistent with the declining trajectory of recent reviews.

## Company Overview

CF produces anhydrous ammonia via the Haber-Bosch process and upgrades it into granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate, and industrial products (DEF, nitric acid, urea liquor). The asset base spans six U.S. complexes (Donaldsonville, Port Neal, Yazoo City, Verdigris, Woodward, Waggaman), two in Canada (Medicine Hat, Courtright), and one in the UK (Billingham). Five U.S. plants sit within CF Industries Nitrogen, ~89% owned by CF with CHS holding ~11%. CF also holds a 50% interest in PLNL (Trinidad) and a newly formed 40% interest in the Blue Point low-carbon ammonia JV.

FY2025 segment gross margins: UAN ($921M, 42.6%), Granular Urea ($837M, 47.0%), Ammonia ($682M, 31.3%), Other ($205M, 37.6%), AN ($79M, 18.8%). The competitive core is structurally advantaged North American shale-gas feedstock relative to higher-cost marginal global producers, combined with an unmatched domestic storage and distribution network and an emerging first-mover position in CCS-enabled low-carbon ammonia.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue cyclicality.** Revenue traces the nitrogen cycle: $11.19B (2022 super-cycle peak) → $6.63B (2023) → $5.94B (2024 trough) → $7.08B (2025, +19%). The 2025 rebound added ~$1.06B from price and only ~$84M from volume. Net earnings to common were $1.46B (+19%); diluted EPS was lifted by both higher earnings and a ~10% lower share count.

**Margins.** Gross margin expanded to 38.5% from 34.6%, operating margin 32.5%, net margin 25.4%. ROE of 37.2% and ROA of 12.8% are exceptional, though ROE is flattered by a shrinking equity base from buybacks. The key margin variable is natural gas: cost of gas used rose 38% YoY to $3.31/MMBtu, a ~$316M gross-margin headwind that partially offset pricing. Early-2026 gas costs are roughly double the 2025 average, a material 2026 cost headwind that will compress margins even if nitrogen prices hold.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $2.75B (2024: $2.27B), FCF $1.80B (25.4% FCF margin) after $950M capex (up 83% on Blue Point ramp). CF retired roughly 20% of its share count since 2022 (203M → 162M), completed the $3B 2022 program, and commenced a $2B 2029 authorization, repurchasing $1.37B in 2025. The dividend is $2.00/share (~$326M, 1.88% yield). The buyback pace is throttling as growth capital reallocates to Blue Point; 2026 consolidated capex jumps to ~$1.3B.

**Balance sheet.** $1.98B cash, $3.25B unsecured senior notes (maturities 2034–2044, with 2026 notes refinanced via $1B 5.30% 2035 notes), $750M undrawn revolver, current ratio 3.37. The Altman Z of 1.91 (grey zone) overstates risk; it penalizes a D/E of 2.91 that reflects deliberate buyback-driven equity depletion, not distress. Against ~$2B cash, $1.8B FCF, and a ≤3.75x net-leverage covenant with ample headroom, balance-sheet risk is low. Piotroski 5/5 on measured signals. Financial health is genuinely strong; the qualification is that 2026 earnings face a gas-and-volume squeeze.

The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in near-term consensus: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the Q1 2026 print of $2.89 beating $2.67 by 8.2% and Q4 2025 beating by 24.9%. The one miss (Q2 2025, -10.3%) shows that even in a strong pricing year, quarterly visibility on a commodity is limited.

## Growth Analysis

The revision data demand explicit treatment. Consensus current-quarter EPS is up 48.5% over 30 days and 173.6% over 90 days; next-quarter up 92.0%/286.1%; current-year up 18.0%/115.2% to $18.23; next-year up 66.0% over 90 days to $11.16. Revision velocity of this magnitude normally precedes continued outperformance as consensus catches up to fundamentals.

We do not treat this as a clean BUY signal because of the source of the upward revisions. CF's revenue grew on price, and the price strength was driven substantially by transitory foreign supply outages. The estimate revisions are, in effect, mark-to-market on a nitrogen price spike that is described as "sharp but time-bound." This is the same dynamic that makes commodity producers' forward estimates unreliable predictors at cyclical peaks: the numbers are real and rising right now, but they capitalize a price level that has a high probability of normalizing. A normalization of the foreign supply outages would reverse the supply-disruption premium and pull the revisions back down. The next-year estimate flatlining over 30 days (-0.2%) while the current-year rises sharply is the market's own acknowledgment that the spike is not extrapolated forward.

Durable growth drivers are modest and long-dated. Volume is guided down in 2026 (Yazoo City offline). The Blue Point low-carbon ammonia plant (CF 40% / JERA 35% / Mitsui 25%, ~$3.7B plus ~$550M CF infrastructure) targets ~1.5M tons by late 2029 and uses autothermal reforming at a scale not previously proven for ammonia. The low-carbon ammonia market, by management's own framing, "could take several years to materialize and then ten or more years to fully develop" and "may not develop at all." Donaldsonville CCS went live in July 2025 and earned ~$42M in 45Q credits, with first premium sales completed — genuine first-mover optionality, but not a 2026–2027 earnings driver. We score growth as cyclical recovery from a trough rather than structural expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at 9.9x trailing EPS of $8.97, ~6x the cyclically-inflated $18.23 current-year consensus, and ~9.9x the $11.16 next-year estimate. The Materials peer set (RGLD, AA, BALL, AMCR, IP) carries a 24.0 median P/E, but those are gold, aluminum, and packaging names with little relevance as nitrogen comps.

The compressed multiple is the market correctly applying a trough multiple to peak earnings, the standard treatment of a commodity producer at a cyclical high. Normalizing earnings power illustrates the point: the 2024 trough delivered $6.74 EPS, 2025 $8.97 on transitory price strength, and the 2022 super-cycle peak far higher. A reasonable mid-cycle figure is roughly $7–9. At $110, that is ~12–16x mid-cycle earnings, which is fair-to-slightly-rich for a commodity name, not the bargain the ~6x current-year optic implies. The "cheapness" disappears once you discount the supply-outage-contingent component of forward estimates.

This is the classic value trap structure for cyclicals: the stock looks cheapest at the earnings peak and most expensive at the trough. The asymmetry is two-sided. A worsening of foreign supply outages that spikes nitrogen prices further would make CF look even cheaper and drive upside; a normalization of those outages compresses pricing and the multiple expands on collapsing earnings. With analyst sentiment described as degrading and the stock already ~22% off its high, there is no asymmetric entry here. We score valuation 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's structural edge is low-cost North American shale-gas feedstock relative to higher-cost global marginal producers, reinforced by scale leadership in ammonia and an unmatched domestic logistics network. This edge is real but not static. Record U.S. LNG export growth pulls Henry Hub higher over time, narrowing the feedstock advantage, visible in the doubling of early-2026 gas costs. CF competes against state-owned and government-subsidized producers in China, Russia, the Middle East, Trinidad, and North Africa on delivered price. China currently restricts nitrogen exports; any reversal would pressure global prices and is a key swing factor.

The low-carbon ammonia first-mover position (Donaldsonville CCS live, JERA/Mitsui offtake for Japanese power and steel, EU/UK CBAM as potential premium-demand drivers) is a credible differentiation vector, but it depends on 45Q credits, third-party CCS infrastructure, and policy support that France and Italy are lobbying to dilute for fertilizers. Louisiana's October 2025 indefinite moratorium on new Class VI well applications is a tangible bottleneck for the CCS chain. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on the low-carbon optionality, partially offset by gas-advantage erosion. Score 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

CF carries an unusually dense set of specific, partly-binary risks for a producer of its quality:

- **Commodity supply-outage binary.** A normalization of the transitory foreign outages reverses the supply-disruption premium. CF behaves like the energy complex: it benefits from prolonged disruption and de-rates on resolution.
- **Natural gas squeeze.** A two-sided squeeze develops if foreign outages normalize (lower nitrogen prices) while gas stays elevated (higher costs).
- **Yazoo City.** The incident cut ~600k tons of ammonia and prompted a $25M impairment.
- **Blue Point execution.** ~$3.7B JV using ATR at unproven ammonia scale, ~⅓ imported materials exposed to steel/aluminum tariffs, production not expected until 2029.
- **Antitrust/political overhang.** The fertilizer-supply-chain antitrust task force is a live enforcement risk to monitor.
- **Concentration.** Donaldsonville is ~40% of ammonia capacity.
- **Governance transition** (detailed below) during a major capital cycle.
- **PLNL gas supply.** The Trinidad JV's NGC gas contract expired January 1, 2026 and is on short-term extensions.

Many of these are not fully priced into a multiple that looks low on peak earnings. Risk score 4.8.

### Options Market Signal

The options structure has shifted materially in CF's favor on a near-term basis. ATM IV collapsed from 74.8% to 47.2% over recent days, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.04 to 0.66. The term structure is now flat (47.2% near vs 45.5% far) with balanced skew (-1.0% one-week, -3.7% six-month), implying uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated in an imminent event. IV remains modestly rich versus 42.0% realized (spread 5.2%), and short interest is moderate at 7.3% of float (3.0 days to cover). The market no longer prices an immediate binary, but the embedded commodity two-sidedness keeps IV above historical vol. This is a less attractive setup for selling premium than a week ago and offers no edge for directional positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Foreign nitrogen outages persist and the supply-disruption premium holds or widens. CF's structural gas-cost advantage and 25%+ FCF margin compound through a sustained pricing window; the $18.23 current-year estimate proves achievable and next-year estimates ratchet higher. The $2B buyback and 45Q-credit-supported CCS optionality add to per-share value. Multiple holds at low-double-digits on durable earnings, driving the stock toward the $150 high analyst target.

**Bear case.** A normalization of the foreign supply outages pulls nitrogen prices down, the +115% current-year revisions reverse, and the cyclically-inflated estimates collapse toward mid-cycle. Henry Hub stays elevated, compounding the margin squeeze. Yazoo City stays offline, the antitrust task force escalates, and the C-suite transition introduces execution friction during the Blue Point capital cycle. The stock de-rates toward the low end of analyst targets ($100) and potentially toward the high-$80s/low-$90s if pricing fully normalizes.

The probabilities are genuinely two-sided and tethered to a global nitrogen supply outcome that has repeatedly defied prediction. That balance, more than any company-specific flaw, is why this is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a balanced commodity binary with no asymmetric entry, not a conviction growth or income thesis. The 1.88% dividend yield is too low to anchor an income case, and the growth is cyclical and reversible.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- A decisive persistence or worsening of foreign supply outages that locks elevated nitrogen pricing, combined with a stock pullback toward $90–100 that prices in the gas-cost headwind, restoring genuine asymmetry.
- Henry Hub gas normalizing back toward the $3.50 range while nitrogen pricing holds, removing the 2026 margin squeeze and converting the revisions into durable earnings.
- Yazoo City restart confirmed ahead of schedule plus Blue Point hitting permitting/cost milestones, de-risking the volume and capital cycle.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A normalization of the foreign supply outages that begins reversing nitrogen pricing, the cleanest exit signal, since it directly unwinds the estimate revisions.
- A guidance cut or quarterly miss confirming the gas/volume squeeze materializing faster than priced.
- Escalation of the fertilizer antitrust task force into a formal enforcement action.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (Q2 2026, roughly early August) and ongoing monitoring of the global nitrogen supply situation, which is the dominant near-term variable. The Yazoo City restart timeline (Q4 2026) is a discrete checkpoint.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a high-quality, cash-generative, low-cost producer trading at an optically cheap multiple. The temptation is to read the estimate revisions as a textbook BUY signal. The discipline is to recognize those revisions as mark-to-market on a transitory nitrogen-price spike driven by foreign supply outages, not as structural growth. The low multiple is the market correctly capitalizing peak earnings at a trough multiple, and the 2026 setup carries identifiable headwinds that the headline momentum understates.

The risk/reward is balanced and tethered to a global nitrogen supply outcome that is inherently unpredictable. Existing holders are reasonably positioned to retain for the optionality and the fortress balance sheet; new capital lacks an asymmetric entry. A normalization of foreign outages is the clean exit signal; a sharp pullback toward $90–100 with pricing intact would be the entry to revisit. We rate CF a HOLD at 5.8.

The Q1 2026 EPS was also overstated ($3.98 vs. actual $2.89). These do not change the two-sided cyclical logic but materially recast the central catalyst.
