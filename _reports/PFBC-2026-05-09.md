## Executive Summary

Preferred Bank is a $1.1B market cap relationship-focused commercial bank headquartered in Los Angeles, serving small/mid-sized businesses, real estate developers, and high-net-worth individuals across 15 full-service branch banking offices plus its main Los Angeles office. The bank delivers industry-leading profitability — a 31.5% efficiency ratio versus 55-60% for typical peers — through an extremely lean operating model. At $95.04, the stock trades at approximately 9.0x forward earnings and 1.6x tangible book, offering a 3.2% dividend yield while consensus estimates have been trending higher.

The primary overhang is a $177M nonaccrual exposure linked to an alleged multi-bank fraud by the Cantor Group (Newport Beach real estate firm). According to management commentary, the bank has resolved approximately $57.9M of this exposure at par, and management expects substantial resolution by end of Q2/Q3 2026. The Q1 2026 EPS of $2.53 beat the $2.48 consensus estimate despite a $3.4M interest income reversal on these nonaccrual loans; March NIM of 3.71% (versus reported quarterly 3.57%) reflects the bank's normalized earning power.

The risk/reward setup is favorable: limited downside if management's reported par recovery on loan sales holds, rising estimates, strong capital position (CET1 11.8%), and active buybacks at current prices signal management confidence. Elevated short interest creates potential for forced covering as credit resolution progresses.

## Company Overview

Preferred Bank was incorporated in 1991 as a California state-chartered, FDIC-insured commercial bank. The bank operates from its main office in Los Angeles plus 12 full-service California branch banking offices, two New York branches (Flushing and Manhattan), and one branch in Sugar Land, Texas (Houston suburb), plus a loan production office in Sunnyvale, California.

**Loan Portfolio (Dec 2024):**
- Commercial Real Estate: $2.84B (50.4%)
- Commercial & Industrial: $1.42B (25.2%)
- Residential Real Estate: $790M (14.0%)
- Construction (Residential): $297M (5.3%)
- Construction (Commercial): $287M (5.1%)
- Total Gross Loans: $5.64B

Total assets reached ~$7.6B by early 2026. The bank originally served the ethnic Chinese business community in Southern California and retains meaningful relationships in that network, though it now derives most customers from the diversified mainstream market.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability metrics remain exceptional despite cyclical NIM compression:**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | Q1 2026 (Ann.) |
|--------|--------|--------|----------------|
| NIM | 4.49% | 4.08% | 3.57% (3.71% March) |
| ROA | 2.28% | 1.91% | ~1.74% |
| Efficiency Ratio | 25.9% | 31.5% | ~33% |
| Diluted EPS | $10.52 | $9.64 | $2.53 (qtr) |

The NIM decline from 4.49% to 3.57% reflects two distinct forces: (1) structural deposit repricing — time deposits (53% of funding) have shifted to market rates, and (2) the non-recurring $3.4M interest reversal on Cantor Group loans. Stripping out the interest reversal, Q1 NIM was approximately 3.76%, and March's 3.71% run-rate represents what management views as normalized earning power.

**Balance sheet strength is well above regulatory minimums:**
- CET1: 11.80% (minimum 4.5%)
- Tier 1 Leverage: 11.33% (minimum 4.0%)
- Total Capital: 15.11% (minimum 8.0%)
- Tangible Book Value: $57.86/share (stock at 1.64x TBV)

**Capital return is active and disciplined:** 464K shares repurchased in FY2024 at $73.76 average; ~400K shares in Q1 2026 at $89.90. Quarterly dividend increased to $0.75 from $0.70 in January 2025 (current yield 3.2%). Combined capital return of approximately $50-55M annually on a $130M net income base leaves ample cushion.

**Credit quality — the Cantor Group situation:**
- $177M total exposure (nine loans: $175M CRE, $2M C&I)
- Management-reported average LTV of 52.7% across the exposure
- Per management commentary, $57.9M sold at par (no loss)
- Remaining exposure in bankruptcy resolution; management expects substantial resolution by Q2/Q3 2026
- ACL: 1.27% of loans

**Note on LTV disclosure:** Management has cited an average LTV of 52.7% and appraised collateral of $219M against the $177M in loans. These figures as stated are internally inconsistent — a $177M loan balance against $219M collateral would imply an LTV of approximately 81%, not 52.7%. The 52.7% figure, if accurate, would imply collateral of approximately $336M. Investors should seek clarification from primary company filings. Regardless, management's reported par recovery on a portion of the loans, if confirmed, suggests meaningful collateral protection.

## Growth Analysis

Preferred Bank is not a high-growth story. Loan growth was 7.0% in FY2024, management initially guided high-single-digit growth for 2026 but has moderated expectations due to macro uncertainty. Q1 2026 sequential loan growth was 1.1% (annualized ~4.5%).

**Estimate revisions have been modestly positive:**
- FY2026 consensus: approximately $10.62 EPS
- FY2027 consensus: approximately $11.21 EPS

The revision trajectory is positive but moderate — this is a recovery from NIM trough rather than accelerating growth. EPS growth from FY2024 ($9.64) to FY2026E ($10.62) implies ~10% total growth over two years, driven primarily by NIM stabilization and ongoing buybacks reducing share count.

Growth constraints include: (1) intense deposit competition keeping funding costs elevated, (2) the bank's pricing discipline — management refuses to compete on sub-6% fixed-rate loans, sacrificing volume for margin quality, (3) geopolitical uncertainty cited by CEO Li Yu (Middle East, oil prices, trade policy) dampening client borrowing appetite.

## Valuation Assessment

At $95.04, Preferred Bank trades at:
- 9.0x forward P/E (FY2026E $10.62)
- 9.1x trailing P/E
- 1.64x tangible book value ($57.86)
- 3.2% dividend yield

More appropriate comparisons are California/Western community banks in the $5-10B asset range. High-performing community banks with 1.5%+ ROA typically trade at 10-12x forward earnings and 1.5-2.0x TBV.

At 9.0x forward earnings, the stock prices in significant uncertainty around credit quality and NIM trajectory. With the Cantor resolution proceeding (management-reported par recoveries suggesting limited loss content), the discount should narrow. A re-rating to 10x FY2026 EPS ($10.62) implies a $106 stock — 12% above current levels.

Piper Sandler rates the stock Overweight with a $111 price target. Given rising estimates and resolving credit risk, a return to 10x forward earnings appears achievable within 6-12 months.

A bank earning 18-20% ROE with a 31% efficiency ratio, resolving credit uncertainty, modest estimate increases, and active buybacks, trading at 9.0x forward earnings, is cheap for the quality of the franchise.

## Competitive Landscape

Preferred Bank's competitive position is defined by its operating model:

**Structural advantages:**
1. **Efficiency:** 31.5% efficiency ratio versus 55-60% industry average. This reflects a relationship banking model operating from approximately 16 locations (including main office) that generates $7.6B in assets — approximately $475-500M assets per location.
2. **Community networks:** The ethnic Chinese business community in Southern California provides a valuable origination channel with strong relationship-based retention, though the bank has diversified significantly into mainstream commercial banking.
3. **Deposit-funded model:** Management has stated the bank operates without FHLB borrowings, relying entirely on deposit funding, which demonstrates franchise deposit strength.
4. **Capital flexibility:** 11.8% CET1 provides capacity for both growth and shareholder returns without constraint.

**Competitive pressures:**
- Pricing competition is intense. The Q1 2026 earnings call noted competitors offering fixed-rate loans below 6%, which management considers unprofitable. Preferred is choosing discipline over growth.
- Deposit costs are structurally higher due to heavy CD reliance (53% of deposits). Noninterest-bearing deposits fell ~10% in FY2024 ($787M → $705M).
- The bank's pricing position means it may grow more slowly than peers in a competitive environment, but maintains superior risk-adjusted returns.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Cantor Group Resolution (Moderate — declining probability of loss, but uncertainty remains):**
Mitigants: management-reported 52.7% LTV, approximately $57.9M reportedly sold at par, management timeline for substantial resolution by Q2/Q3 2026. Risks: bankruptcy proceedings introduce timeline uncertainty, competing creditor claims could complicate recovery on specific properties, and broader fraud-related litigation (also affecting Western Alliance and Zions Bancorp) creates complexity. Par recovery on a portion of the loans does not necessarily validate valuations across the entire remaining portfolio, as remaining loans may differ in collateral quality, lien priority, or borrower structure.

**2. CRE Concentration (Moderate-to-Elevated — structural):**
50.4% of loans in CRE, with an additional 5.1% in commercial construction. This is a high-concentration portfolio relative to peers. Southern California multifamily and commercial real estate faces refinancing pressure at higher rates. The broader CRE maturity wave creates systemic risk, and industry-wide past-due rates in CRE remain elevated. While Preferred's conservative underwriting (average LTV well below 65%) provides cushion, the Cantor event may not be the only source of credit stress in a prolonged higher-rate environment.

**3. NIM Trajectory (Low-Moderate):**
March NIM of 3.71% may represent a near-term trough, though this is an analytical assumption rather than a certainty. Deposit competition, rate path uncertainty, and potential further credit reversals could pressure margins. Upside is limited by: (a) CDs maturing in Q2 at 3.89% expected to renew at similar rates, (b) competitive lending environment caps asset yields. Meaningful NIM expansion requires rate cuts (which management considers uncertain) or a less competitive environment.

**4. Short Interest (Monitoring):**
Short interest has been elevated (reported at approximately 10.5% of float with high days-to-cover), reflecting institutional skepticism likely tied to CRE exposure and Cantor Group uncertainty. If credit resolution continues favorably, short covering could accelerate price recovery.

**5. Key-Man Risk:**
Founder-CEO Li Yu has led the bank for 30+ years. His operational discipline is foundational to the efficiency metrics. Succession planning is unclear from available disclosures.

**6. Macro/Geopolitical:**
CEO Li Yu explicitly cited Middle East conflict, oil prices, and trade policy as dampening loan demand. In a prolonged stagflation scenario, credit quality in CRE and C&I could deteriorate beyond the current Cantor exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($108-111, 14-17% upside):**
- Cantor Group resolves entirely at par by Q3 2026 (validating the collateral coverage thesis)
- NIM stabilizes at 3.70-3.75% with no further credit-related reversals
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $10.60-10.80, re-rated to 10x forward = $106-108
- Buybacks reduce share count by 3-4% annually, compounding EPS growth
- Short covering provides additional buying pressure

**Bear Case ($74-80, 16-22% downside):**
- CRE market deterioration produces additional credit problems beyond Cantor
- NIM compresses further on deposit competition or rate hikes
- FY2026 EPS misses at $9.50-9.80 due to elevated provisions
- Multiple compresses to 8x on sector-wide CRE fears = $76-78
- Recession scenario could stress the loan book more broadly

**Base Case ($102-106, 7-12% upside):**
Cantor resolves with minimal loss, NIM stabilizes around 3.70%, FY2026 EPS of $10.50-10.70, gradual re-rating toward 10x forward as uncertainty lifts. Combined with 3.2% dividend = total return of 10-15%.

## Conclusion

Preferred Bank offers an attractive risk/reward at 9.0x forward earnings with a 3.2% dividend yield, trading below both its historical valuation range and appropriate peer multiples. The bank's franchise quality — evidenced by 31% efficiency ratio, 18-20% ROE, and strong capital ratios — is temporarily obscured by the Cantor Group credit event, which management reports is resolving favorably. Rising estimates, active buybacks, and short covering potential provide multiple paths to re-rating. Position sizing should reflect CRE concentration risk and broader macro uncertainty (2-3% portfolio weight), with monitoring focused on credit resolution progress and NIM trajectory through Q2/Q3 2026 earnings.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The report's collateral/LTV math contains an internal inconsistency ($219M collateral on $177M loans implies ~81% LTV, not the reported 52.7%), and key thesis-critical claims (par sales, resolution timeline, short interest) could not be independently verified against primary sources. If the true LTV is ~81% rather than 52.7%, the credit loss cushion is materially thinner than presented.
