## Executive Summary

AMD has executed a strategic transformation over the past 12 months, securing two 6-gigawatt GPU deployment agreements with OpenAI and Meta that validate its position as the credible #2 AI accelerator platform behind NVIDIA. Revenue grew 34% in FY2025 to $34.6B, gross margins expanded to 50%, and free cash flow surged to $5.5B. The "Equity-As-A-Service" warrant model — 320 million shares issued to OpenAI and Meta at $0.01/share tied to deployment milestones — converts AMD's largest customers into aligned shareholders.

The fundamental story is compelling. Analyst estimate revisions are uniformly positive (FY2027 EPS up 7.4% over 90 days to $11.04), semiconductor supply tightness is triple-confirmed through 2027, and AMD's product roadmap (MI450, 5th Gen EPYC, Zen6 Venice) is well-positioned against $725B+ in validated Big Tech AI infrastructure spending. However, the stock has appreciated 264% from its April 2025 trough to ~$354, now trading at approximately 53x FY2026E EPS and 32x FY2027E EPS. With the consensus median target at $300 (15% below current price), execution risks on MI450 ramp, up to 20% dilution from performance warrants, and ongoing export control uncertainty, the risk/reward profile has shifted materially since our prior BUY recommendation. We downgrade to HOLD.

## Company Overview

AMD operates across three segments: Data Center ($16.6B, 48% of FY2025 revenue), Client and Gaming ($14.6B, 42%), and Embedded ($3.5B, 10%). The company is a fabless semiconductor designer relying on TSMC for all leading-edge manufacturing (7nm and below), with assembly/test/packaging through joint ventures with Tongfu Microelectronics affiliates.

AMD competes directly with NVIDIA in AI accelerators (Instinct GPU series), with Intel in x86 CPUs (EPYC server, Ryzen client), and with a fragmented field in FPGAs and embedded processors (legacy Xilinx portfolio). The chiplet architecture design approach and TSMC partnership provide structural cost and performance advantages versus Intel, while the OpenAI and Meta warrant structures create customer alignment that partially offsets NVIDIA's CUDA software ecosystem moat.

The October 2025 acquisition of ZT Systems' design business (~$2B net cost after divesting the manufacturing arm to Sanmina) positions AMD for rack-scale AI infrastructure, a capability required to fulfill the OpenAI and Meta deployment commitments.

FY2025 revenue acceleration was broad-based: Data Center grew 32% to $16.6B, Client and Gaming surged 51% to $14.6B (driven by Ryzen ASP increases of 31%), and Embedded was roughly flat at $3.5B. Gross margin reached 50% despite approximately $440M in net inventory charges from MI308 export control write-downs, implying underlying gross margin of ~51.3%.

Non-GAAP net income was $6.8B versus $4.3B GAAP, with the $2.5B gap primarily attributable to Xilinx acquisition-related intangible amortization ($2.3B annually, declining over time) and stock-based compensation ($1.6B). The effective tax rate of -2.5% in FY2025 benefited from an $853M IRS discrete tax benefit that will not recur.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow from continuing operations reached $6.5B in FY2025 (up from $3.0B in FY2024), with capital expenditures of $974M yielding approximately $5.5B in free cash flow. This represents an FCF/net income ratio of approximately 128%, indicating high-quality earnings conversion.

The balance sheet is conservatively capitalized: $10.6B in cash and short-term investments against $3.3B in total debt, resulting in a net cash position of approximately $7.3B. This provides substantial financial flexibility for R&D investment, share repurchases ($9.4B remaining authorization), and potential strategic acquisitions.

Two balance sheet items warrant monitoring. First, inventory reached $7.9B (up 38% YoY), which management attributes to Data Center product ramp support but which also represents significant exposure to product transition risk and potential export control write-downs. Second, goodwill stands at $25.1B (33% of total assets), primarily from the 2022 Xilinx acquisition. Given the Embedded segment's stagnation ($5.9B revenue in FY2022 declining to $3.5B in FY2025), impairment risk on the associated goodwill warrants ongoing scrutiny.

### Capital Return

AMD repurchased $1.3B in shares during FY2025. There is no dividend. The authorized share count was increased from 2.25B to 4.0B shares in May 2025 to accommodate the warrant structures, signaling the magnitude of potential dilution.

## Growth Analysis

AMD's growth trajectory is anchored by three pillars:

**1. AI Accelerator Revenue ($16.6B Data Center, growing 32% YoY):** The OpenAI and Meta 6-gigawatt GPU deals provide multi-year revenue visibility. Assuming $60-100B total deal value per published estimates, and phased deployment beginning with MI450 series, these two customers alone could drive $15-25B in incremental GPU revenue over 3-5 years. The French exascale supercomputer MOU and expanding enterprise adoption of MI350 provide additional demand vectors.

**2. Client CPU Market Share Gains:** AMD's Ryzen processors continue to gain share against Intel, with ASP increases of 31% in FY2025 reflecting the mix shift to higher-performance products. Intel's persistent manufacturing challenges suggest AMD's x86 CPU share trajectory remains favorable.

**3. Estimate Revision Momentum:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen 2.2% over 90 days to $6.74, while next-year estimates have accelerated to $11.04 (+7.4% over 90 days). The 30-day revision trend is positive across all periods, with acceleration in next-year estimates ($10.28 → $10.77 → $11.04). Rising estimates at an accelerating pace are one of the strongest forward indicators of continued earnings momentum.

Consensus implies approximately 64% EPS growth from FY2026 ($6.74) to FY2027 ($11.04), which requires successful MI450 ramp, continued EPYC market share gains, and no material deterioration from export controls. The growth rate is ambitious but plausible given the contracted hyperscaler backlog.

The Embedded segment remains the growth laggard. At $3.5B (down 41% from FY2022 peak), this Xilinx-derived business has failed to recover to pre-acquisition levels. A sustained recovery would be incremental upside; continued stagnation is already in consensus estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

AMD trades at approximately $354, implying:
- 53x FY2026E EPS ($6.74)
- 32x FY2027E EPS ($11.04)
- ~136x trailing P/E (GAAP EPS $2.61)
- Forward P/E of 136x per Yahoo Finance data (likely reflecting trailing EPS basis)

Against peers, AMD's valuation is mixed. The peer median P/E is 71.2x (heavily influenced by TSM at 171x and ADI at 71x), so AMD at 53x FY2026E appears reasonable on that comparison. However, Micron (MU), a comparable AI beneficiary, trades at 24.4x, reflecting higher cyclical earnings volatility but also significantly more compressed expectations.

The critical valuation question is whether the market is adequately discounting the 320 million performance warrant shares. At $0.01/share exercise price, full vesting would dilute current shareholders by approximately 20%. If we assume 80% probability of substantial vesting (given the contracted nature of the deals), the dilution-adjusted FY2027 EPS falls from $11.04 to approximately $9.40, pushing the FY2027 P/E to ~38x.

At current prices, AMD trades above the consensus median target of $300 by 18% and above the mean target of $300.54 by 18%. The high analyst target of $455 implies 28% upside, but the low target of $225 implies 36% downside. This skew, where the current price already exceeds median consensus, is not characteristic of a favorable entry point.

Much of the AI accelerator upside, the Meta/OpenAI deal optionality, and the EPYC share gains are now embedded in the price. For a BUY, the market would need to be materially wrong about something, and while AI capex validation continues to surprise to the upside, the warrant dilution and export control uncertainties provide offsetting negatives that the current multiple does not obviously discount.

## Competitive Landscape

AMD occupies a strengthening #2 position in AI accelerators, with two structural advantages and one significant disadvantage:

**Advantages:**
1. The warrant model creates deep customer alignment that partially substitutes for NVIDIA's software ecosystem lock-in. OpenAI and Meta, as warrant holders, have financial incentives to deploy AMD hardware even when CUDA compatibility would favor NVIDIA.
2. AMD's chiplet architecture and TSMC partnership provide a structurally competitive cost structure versus Intel's integrated manufacturing and increasingly competitive performance versus NVIDIA at the rack-scale level (with ZT Systems capabilities).

**Disadvantage:**
NVIDIA's CUDA ecosystem remains the dominant software platform for AI development. AMD's ROCm investment is narrowing the gap, but the developer ecosystem disparity means AMD wins primarily with sophisticated hyperscalers who can invest in porting workloads, not the broader enterprise market. This limits AMD's addressable market versus NVIDIA.

The September 2025 NVIDIA-Intel partnership is a new competitive wildcard, potentially combining NVIDIA's GPU ecosystem with Intel's x86 CPU platform, which could erode AMD's competitive advantage in the server CPU market where it has been gaining share.

In the Embedded/FPGA market, AMD (Xilinx) competes with Lattice Semiconductor, Microchip, and Intel's PSG division. The segment's stagnation suggests AMD has not yet realized the cross-selling synergies projected at the time of the Xilinx acquisition.

## Risk Assessment

### Export Control Escalation (High Impact, Medium Probability)
The April 2025 MI308 restrictions resulted in ~$800M gross inventory charges. Newer products (MI350, MI450) face potential additional restrictions. The unprecedented U.S. government expression of expectation to receive 15% of revenue from licensed MI308 sales to China, while not yet codified in regulation, represents a novel regulatory risk with no industry precedent. Further restrictions could eliminate AMD's China market entirely for AI accelerators.

### Warrant Dilution (High Impact, High Probability)
The contracted nature of the OpenAI and Meta deals makes substantial vesting likely. The warrants are liability-classified, creating P&L volatility through fair value remeasurement. The revenue reduction offset from warrant amortization adds complexity to earnings interpretation.

### TSMC Concentration (High Impact, Low-Medium Probability)
All leading-edge AMD products depend on TSMC. No alternative foundry exists for 7nm-and-below manufacturing. Geopolitical risks related to Taiwan, capacity constraints, or natural disasters represent existential supply risk.

### Inventory Execution Risk (Medium Impact, Medium Probability)
Simultaneous product transitions (MI350 → MI450, 5th Gen EPYC → Zen6 Venice) create exposure to write-downs if customer adoption timelines shift or export controls expand.

### Multiple Executive Departures
Multiple Item 5.02 (officer departure/election) 8-K filings between July 2025 and February 2026 indicate above-average executive turnover. While individual departures may be routine, the frequency during a period of rapid organizational change warrants monitoring for signs of institutional knowledge loss or cultural stress.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV at 67.4% is 8.3 percentage points rich to 1-year historical volatility of 59.1%, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 67.4% > long-term 62.1%). This signals the options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty, likely related to the upcoming earnings cycle and macro environment. The 16.6% put skew on the nearest expiry reflects elevated downside hedging demand, while unusual put activity at the $320 strike for May 22 expiry (7.6x volume/OI) suggests institutional positioning for a potential 10% correction. The overall put/call OI ratio of 0.46 is not alarming, but the combination of rich IV, backwardation, and near-term put skew is a cautionary signal for new long entries at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($420-$455, +19-29%)
- MI450 ramp exceeds expectations, with both OpenAI and Meta accelerating deployment timelines
- AI capex cycle extends through 2028 with no deceleration, validating $725B+ aggregate spending
- EPYC server CPU share reaches 30%+ as Intel continues to falter
- Embedded segment recovers to $5B+ as automotive and industrial AI adoption accelerates
- At $11.50+ FY2027 EPS with full warrant adjustment, stock re-rates to 37-40x forward = $425-460

### Bear Case ($200-$250, -29% to -44%)
- MI450 ramp encounters technical delays or yield issues at TSMC
- Export controls expand to cover MI350/MI450, eliminating China revenue entirely
- NVIDIA cuts pricing aggressively, compressing AMD's GPU margins
- Hyperscaler capex cycle decelerates in H2 2026 as ROI scrutiny intensifies
- Warrant dilution fully prices in, consensus revises down to $8-9 FY2027 EPS
- Stock de-rates to 25-28x adjusted FY2027 EPS = $200-250

### Base Case ($300-$340, -4% to -15%)
The most likely outcome over 6-12 months is that AMD executes well on the MI450 ramp but faces periodic volatility from export control headlines, warrant dilution debates, and hyperscaler capex pacing. FY2027 consensus of $11.04 proves achievable, but the dilution-adjusted figure of ~$9.40 is the more appropriate denominator. At 32-36x adjusted FY2027 EPS, fair value sits at $300-340, roughly in line with or slightly below current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### HOLD — Upgrade and Downgrade Triggers

**Upgrade to BUY triggers (any 2 of 3):**
1. Stock pulls back to $270-290 (29-31x dilution-adjusted FY2027E EPS), restoring asymmetric upside
2. MI450 production ramp confirmed on schedule with first hyperscaler deployments verified, removing the largest execution risk
3. Export control clarity: either formal safe harbor for MI450/future products or demonstration that China restrictions are bounded and not expanding

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers (any 2 of 3):**
1. FY2027 EPS estimates revised below $9.50 (currently $11.04), indicating fundamental growth thesis deterioration
2. TSMC signals capacity constraints or pricing increases that compress AMD gross margins below 48%
3. Either OpenAI or Meta materially delays GPU deployment commitments, calling warrant vesting timelines and deal economics into question

**Reassessment Timeline:** The most recent earnings report was on February 4, 2026, so the next quarterly report is likely in late May 2026. The Advancing AI 2026 event on July 22-23 in San Francisco is the next major product catalyst, with MI450 and Zen6 EPYC Venice details expected.

## Conclusion

AMD's fundamental trajectory is the strongest it has been in the company's history. The Meta and OpenAI mega-deals validate AMD as a credible AI accelerator platform, EPYC continues to take CPU market share, and the combination of rising estimates and supply tightness provides favorable multi-quarter tailwinds. The business deserves a premium multiple.

However, the 264% rally from April 2025 lows has pulled the stock above consensus targets, compressing the risk/reward ratio. At current multiples before warrant dilution adjustment, AMD is pricing in near-flawless execution across MI450 ramp, export control navigation, and hyperscaler deployment pacing. Warrant dilution, export control uncertainty, and the elevated inventory position represent material risks that are difficult to fully discount at these levels.

We downgrade from BUY to HOLD. The growth thesis remains intact and this is a name we want to own on pullbacks, specifically in the $270-290 range where the risk/reward recalibrates favorably. Current holders should maintain positions with the understanding that near-term volatility is likely, and new capital should wait for a better entry point.
