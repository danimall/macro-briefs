**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Flowserve is a world-leading flow-control manufacturer of pumps, valves, mechanical seals, and automation for severe-service industrial applications, whose investment story has shifted from industrial cyclical to special situation in transition. Three things changed the trajectory over the past nine months: gross margin expanded to 33.4% in FY25 (up ~590bps over three years), the decades-old asbestos liability was permanently divested in December 2025, and Starboard Value built a stake and went public in May 2026 arguing the company has "significantly underperformed due to poor execution" against favorable secular tailwinds. The stock has risen 76% off its June 2025 low of $45.11 to $81.35.

The complicating factor is that the most recent operating data is soft. Q1 2026 revenue fell 6.7% YoY to $1.07B and bookings fell 6.4%, both pressured by Middle East disruption tied to the Iran conflict and the Strait of Hormuz, plus weaker original-equipment orders. Headline FY25 net income of $346M (EPS $2.64) is also misleading: it was inflated by a $266M Chart Industries merger-termination fee booked in other income while simultaneously absorbing a $140M asbestos divestiture loss and $41M of merger costs. Strip those out and underlying operating income actually fell ~13.5% to $400M. The durable positive is the margin expansion; the durable negative is decelerating top-line momentum.

At ~$81, FLS trades at roughly 30x trailing and ~20x consensus FY26 EPS of $4.06, against a peer-median P/E near 31x. After a 76% run and with estimates drifting slightly lower (current-year consensus down 1.9% over 90 days, current-quarter down 11.7%), the asymmetric upside that existed at $45-55 has largely been realized. We rate FLS a HOLD. The activist campaign provides genuine catalyst optionality and two directors bought stock in the open market in May at ~$65-67, but the offsetting bookings deceleration, one-time-flattered earnings, and a full-ish valuation leave the risk/reward balanced rather than compelling.

## Company Overview

Flowserve manufactures and services flow-control systems used in critical industrial processes across energy, chemical, power generation, and general industries. It operates two segments:

- **Flowserve Pumps Division (FPD)** — ~68% of FY25 sales ($3.24B); highly engineered and pre-configured pumps, mechanical seals, and aftermarket services. FY25 segment operating margin 18.6%, up from 15.2%.
- **Flow Control Division (FCD)** — ~32% of sales ($1.50B); valves and automation. FLS is the #2 global industrial valve supplier. FY25 segment operating margin 11.9%.

The competitive moat rests on a large global installed base that drives recurring, high-margin aftermarket revenue, now ~53% of FY25 sales and ~57% in Q1 2026, providing cyclical ballast, supported by 50+ heritage brands, a network of ~155 Quick Response Centers across ~48 countries, and severe-service engineering expertise. FY25 bookings split: General Industries 34%, Energy 33%, Chemical 19%, Power Generation 14%. About 63% of sales are international.

Management is executing a "3D Strategy" (Diversification, Decarbonization, Digitization) alongside the "Flowserve Business System" and the CORE complexity-reduction program launched in 2024. CEO Scott Rowe leads; Amy Schwetz is CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (FY, $M):**

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 3,615 | 4,321 | 4,558 | 4,729 |
| Gross margin | 27.5% | 29.6% | 31.5% | 33.4% |
| Operating income | 197 | 334 | 462 | 400* |
| Net income | 189 | 187 | 283 | 346 |
| Diluted EPS | — | 1.42 | 2.14 | 2.64 |

\*FY25 operating income fell despite higher gross profit because of two one-time hits: a $140.1M loss on the asbestos divestiture and $41.2M of Chart merger-termination costs. Net income still rose because of the $266M Chart breakup fee in other income.

The margin trajectory is the real, durable story. Gross margin expanded ~590bps over three years (27.5% → 33.4%) on pricing discipline ("selective bidding"), aftermarket mix shift, strategic sourcing, and CORE. That continued into Q1 2026 at 35.6% versus 32.3% a year earlier, though Q1 was flattered by a one-time $30.4M IEEPA tariff refund recognized in cost of sales. Adjusting for that, Q1 gross margin was closer to ~32.7%, still healthy but not the reported figure.

Cash flow and balance sheet are solid. FY25 operating cash flow was $505.9M; FCF was ~$435M (9.2% margin), against modest capex of ~$71M. Cash stood at $760M at FY25 and $792M in Q1 2026, with total liquidity ~$1.38B. Debt is ~$1.55B, comprising 3.50% senior notes due 2030, 2.80% notes due 2032, a term loan, and a revolver. The screening D/E of 2.6 overstates leverage: on net-debt/EBITDA the company is investment-grade and comfortably covenant-compliant. The Altman Z of 2.15 (grey zone) is an artifact of an asset-light, services-heavy industrial with ~$1.39B of goodwill and meaningful treasury stock, not a distress signal. The current ratio is ~2.0 and operating cash flow exceeds net income, both quality markers.

Capital allocation: the dividend was raised to $0.22/quarter (~1.1% yield); FLS bought back $254.9M (4.85M shares) in FY25 with $197.9M of capacity remaining. Buybacks paused in Q1 2026 while the revolver was drawn $150M to fund the pending Trillium acquisition.

The earnings-quality caveat matters for scoring. Investors should normalize FY25: stripping the Chart fee from other income and the asbestos loss from operating income produces a true run-rate earnings power well below the $2.64 headline EPS. Consensus FY26 EPS of $4.06 reflects this normalization plus the margin tailwind, and the four-quarter beat streak (5.2%, 17.2%, 12.0%, 14.9%) gives reasonable confidence in the forward number.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is two-sided. The bull narrative, explicitly endorsed by Starboard, is exposure to reindustrialization, electricity demand growth (the 10-K specifically cites AI-driven power demand), nuclear new-build and life extension, energy security, water/desalination, and decarbonization. The aftermarket mix shift and CORE-driven margin expansion should drive structurally higher earnings power against that demand backdrop. Backlog of $2.9B is supportive, with ~76% expected to convert in 2026 and rising aftermarket mix (42% → 43.4%), which is margin-accretive.

The bear/activist reality is that bookings momentum is decelerating. FY25 bookings rose only 1.1%; Q1 2026 bookings and revenue both declined on Middle East disruption and softer original-equipment orders. Energy bookings declined $194M in FY25. This is precisely the under-execution Starboard is targeting.

Estimate revisions are mildly negative and do not provide the strong positive signal that would support a higher growth score. Next-year consensus EPS fell 1.4% to $4.61 over the same 90-day window. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags an earnings inflection; estimates are stable-to-slightly-down. The implied EPS path ($4.06 in FY26, $4.61 in FY27) suggests ~14% growth, respectable but not exceptional, and dependent on margin progression continuing rather than top-line acceleration. The Trillium Valves acquisition ($490M, closing mid-2026) adds nuclear/power valve exposure aligned with the secular thesis and is incrementally additive to growth once integrated.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer comparables in industrial machinery show a median P/E of 31.2 (Valmont 29.4, Donaldson 23.0, Timken 31.2, SPX Technologies 44.7, with Chart at a negative trailing figure). On a trailing basis FLS sits roughly in line with peers; on forward earnings it is moderately below the peer median, reflecting both the one-time-distorted trailing base and the bookings softness.

The trailing 30x figure overstates richness because FY25 EPS was depressed by the asbestos loss and merger costs, so we anchor on the forward ~20x. That is a reasonable multiple for a margin-improving flow-control franchise, but it is not cheap enough to argue clear mispricing. The asbestos divestiture removes a structural overhang that historically depressed the multiple, providing a legitimate re-rating argument, but the stock has already moved off its low and much of that re-rating appears captured. Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $83, mean $90, median $91, high $100, implying ~11-12% upside to the mean. With estimates drifting slightly lower rather than rising, we do not treat these targets as floors to be revised upward; they are reasonable anchors for a balanced setup.

The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside today. The activist campaign could compress the discount further if it produces a strategic review or operational acceleration, but absent that catalyst, ~20x forward on decelerating bookings and slightly-falling estimates is fair value, not a bargain. We score valuation neutral, treating the in-line-to-slightly-cheap multiple with appropriate skepticism given that cheapness without rising estimates has been a poor predictor of returns.

## Competitive Landscape

Flowserve holds a defensible position in a fragmented flow-control market: #2 global valve supplier in FCD, a large installed base driving recurring aftermarket revenue, and severe-service engineering expertise that creates switching costs in mission-critical applications. The rising aftermarket mix strengthens the recurring-revenue ballast and is margin-accretive. The 155-QRC global service network provides local proximity that low-cost spare-parts replicators cannot easily match.

The trajectory is mixed. Gross margin expansion is evidence of pricing power and mix improvement that outpaces many peers. Against that, the bookings decline and Starboard's explicit execution critique suggest the company is not fully capitalizing on secular tailwinds. The renewed M&A cadence (Trillium $490M, FAMCO $34.5M, prior MOGAS and Greenray) is building out severe-service valves and power/nuclear exposure, which extends the moat into faster-growing end markets but carries integration risk. The Baker Hughes-Chart consolidation signals an active deal environment in industrial/energy equipment, positioning FLS as a potential participant. On balance, the competitive position is stable-to-improving on margins but stalled on volume, a mid-range score.

## Risk Assessment

**Activist outcome (the defining near-term variable).** Starboard's campaign is two-sided: escalation to board seats or a strategic review could unlock value, but it also signals that sophisticated investors believe management has under-executed. We do not have visibility into the path or timeline.

**Cyclical and geopolitical exposure.** Energy, chemical, and general-industry capex drive bookings, which have decelerated. The Q1 2026 10-Q explicitly flags the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz closure as having reduced regional bookings and raised freight costs. Middle East is a meaningful market for FLS, and we cannot forecast the duration of the disruption. A verified, held reopening would relieve the regional headwind; sustained closure or grinding conflict extends it.

**Tariff reversal risk.** The $35.4M IEEPA tariff receivable is non-cash and could reverse if CBP refunds disappoint, which would create an unfavorable adjustment. Steel/aluminum tariffs (50%) remain a cost headwind.

**Integration and M&A.** The renewed deal cadence raises execution risk during a period the activist is already scrutinizing.

**Executive turnover.** A string of Item 5.02 8-Ks (Dec 2025, March 2026), a CHRO change, and an FPD segment-president transition could reflect either disruption or board-driven refresh, worth monitoring given the execution critique.

**Goodwill, pension, FX.** ~$1.39B goodwill (pump unit fair value has "fluctuated"), recurring pension settlement losses (~$5-6M expected 2026, U.S. plan frozen and fully funded), and OCI swings of ±$100-270M from FX translation are all manageable but present.

The most significant structural de-risking is the permanent removal of the asbestos liability, which had previously been projected through 2054, removing a perennial overhang.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and slightly constructive. Near-term ATM IV is 40.4% versus 1-year historical vol of 46.2%, leaving IV cheap (-5.8% spread), and IV sits at the 4th percentile within the current chain, indicating the market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.1%, the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.45, and the put/call OI ratio fell to 0.18 from 0.32 even as the stock rose 16% over the prior month. The term structure is in normal contango (40.4% near vs 44.2% long-term). There is no bearish divergence here; the options market is complacent-to-mildly-positive, consistent with the activist-supported floor. Short interest is moderate at 7.5% of float (3.2 days to cover), neither a squeeze setup nor a strong bear signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$100-110).** The asbestos divestiture re-rates the multiple, Starboard escalation drives operational acceleration or a strategic review, gross margins continue expanding toward the high-30s on CORE and aftermarket mix, Trillium integrates cleanly into the nuclear/power thesis, and Middle East disruption resolves. On ~$4.60 normalized FY27 EPS at a 22-23x multiple, the stock reaches the low-$100s, matching the Street high of $100.

**Bear case (~$60-65).** Bookings deceleration continues, the Middle East/Hormuz disruption persists or worsens, the tariff receivable reverses, Trillium integration stumbles, and the activist campaign stalls without a clear outcome. On ~$3.80 of disappointed FY26 EPS at a de-rated 16-17x, the stock retraces toward the directors' May purchase prices of $65-67 and potentially lower, roughly 20-25% below the current price.

**Base case (~$88-90).** Margins hold and inch higher, bookings stabilize as Middle East disruption eases, the activist keeps a floor under the stock and provides modest upside optionality, and EPS reaches ~$4.40 in FY27. At ~20x, that supports ~$88-90, in line with the analyst mean and median, implying ~9-11% upside plus the ~1% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with the catalyst (activist) and the headwind (bookings/Middle East) roughly balanced, and the valuation no longer offers the asymmetry it did at $45-55.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Starboard secures board seats or a strategic review is announced, providing a concrete value-unlock path; or
- Bookings inflect positive (two consecutive quarters of YoY growth) confirming the secular demand thesis is converting; or
- A pullback toward the directors' purchase zone ($65-70) restores asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The tariff receivable reverses materially and bookings decline accelerates below -10% YoY; or
- The Trillium integration produces margin dilution or a goodwill impairment; or
- The activist campaign collapses without resolution while estimates roll over (current-year consensus falling >5% over 90 days).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (FQ2 2026, expected late July), the mid-2026 Trillium close, and any Starboard escalation or settlement. The Hormuz situation is a live macro input on regional bookings; watch for verified, sustained commercial transit.

## Conclusion

Flowserve has genuinely improved over the past year: gross margins expanded, the asbestos liability is permanently gone, the balance sheet is investment-grade, and an activist is pressing for better execution against real secular tailwinds in power, nuclear, and reindustrialization. The two May insider purchases are a constructive signal at the post-Starboard reset. The trajectory of our scoring (5.7 → 6.4) has been improving, and the underlying franchise quality supports that.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a 76% run off the lows, headline earnings flattered by one-time items, decelerating bookings, estimates drifting slightly lower rather than rising, and a forward multiple (~20x) that fairly captures the improved story. The lower price relative to the February high reflects real operating softness (the revenue decline that triggered Starboard), not market noise, so we do not treat the pullback from $92 as a pure buy-the-dip opportunity. The risk/reward is balanced: ~9-11% to base case, ~22% to the bull case, ~20-25% downside in the bear case. We would become buyers on a pullback toward $65-70 or on a confirmed bookings inflection or activist escalation. At $81, the appropriate posture is to hold and monitor.
