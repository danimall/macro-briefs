## Executive Summary

PBF Energy has just printed the strongest quarter in its corporate history, and the numbers are not marginal. Q2 2026 gross refining margin reached $23.40/bbl against refining operating expense of $8.00/bbl — a $15.40/bbl cash contribution before depreciation, versus $0.39/bbl for full-year 2025. EBITDA excluding special items was $1,218.4M in the quarter against $51.4M in Q1 and $166.5M for all of 2025. Adjusted fully-converted EPS ex-items was $6.22 against a $4.21 consensus, a 47.6% beat. Consensus has chased the print hard: current-year EPS estimates have moved from $5.67 to $16.92 over 90 days (+198.7%), with +100.8% of that arriving in the last 30 days.

The mechanism is exogenous. Q2 benchmark cracks in the 10-Q's own market-indicator table rose 62% to 140% year over year across every region PBF serves — Brent/NYH 2-1-1 at $43.48, LLS/Gulf Coast at $48.66, ANS/West Coast-LA 4-3-1 at $53.28, ANS/West Coast-SF 3-2-1 at $58.27 — with Dated Brent averaging $104.86/bbl. Global product supply is short because refining capacity has been physically damaged and trade routes disrupted; PBF, which operates no upstream production, no retail network, and hedges the crack by policy only lightly, is the highest-beta listed expression of that condition. The same configuration produced adjusted losses of $456M in 2024 and $475M in 2025.

That is the analytical crux. At $61.71 the stock is 3.65x current-year consensus and 6.71x next-year consensus of $9.19 — but reverse-engineering that $9.19 requires roughly $16.80/bbl of gross refining margin in 2027, against $16.07 in the 2023 super-cycle, $7.89 in 2024, and $8.77 in 2025. The forward multiple is cheap only if a war-driven margin regime holds for six more quarters. What does provide genuine support is the balance sheet and book value: net debt fell from $1,620.4M to $855.0M in six months, liquidity exceeds $3.5B with nothing drawn on a $3.5B revolver, and book value per Class A share is $54.06 ($58.15 adjusting after-tax for the $651.3M excess of inventory replacement cost over LIFO carrying value), so the stock trades at 1.14x book (1.06x adjusted). Against that: an unquantified Martinez legal and regulatory tail running to Q1 2028, RFS compliance costs that doubled to $331.3M in a single quarter, a 10% holder that has sold 20.6% of its position into the rally with ~15 million shares remaining, five insiders monetizing roughly $57M in the two days after the print, and no buyback at peak earnings despite $732M of remaining authorization. Base case $68, bull $86, bear $42. HOLD.

## Company Overview

PBF operates six refineries with approximately 1,000,000 bpd of nameplate throughput and a weighted-average Nelson Complexity Index of 12.8: Delaware City (180 kbpd, Nelson 13.6), Paulsboro (155 kbpd), Toledo (180 kbpd), Chalmette (185 kbpd, 13.0), Torrance (166 kbpd, 13.8) and Martinez (157 kbpd, 16.1). Five of six can process discounted heavy and sour crude, which makes light-heavy differentials a primary earnings driver — WTI–WCS widened to $20.25 in Q2 2026 from $10.65 a year earlier, a direct tailwind.

Three structural features determine how the business behaves. First, PBF buys substantially all feedstock from unaffiliated sources on short-term and spot terms and has no exploration and production. Second, it has no retail network and depends on third parties for product outlets, so it captures none of the marketing margin that damps cycle volatility at Marathon or Phillips 66. Third, Torrance plus Martinez give it 323 kbpd of California capacity, roughly a third of the system and the largest independent merchant position in a market that is physically isolated from other PADDs by specification and pipeline geography.

The Logistics segment ($660.5M of assets) is captive: of $188.1M of H1 2026 Logistics revenue, about $181.6M was eliminated in consolidation. PBF also holds a 50% equity-method interest in St. Bernard Renewables (carrying value $859.6M), a renewable diesel JV with Enilive co-located at Chalmette, which contributed +$35.8M of equity income in H1 2026 against –$21.3M a year earlier and provides an elective RIN source. Two disclosed but unvalued options exist: participation in the MACH2 hydrogen hub consortium (DOE selection for up to $750M) on 2,500 acres adjacent to Delaware City, and a real estate portfolio management describes as actively developed. Neither carries a financial statement value; I assign none.

## Financial Analysis

**The two-regime problem.** FY2025 revenue was $29.33B with a GAAP net loss of $158.5M (–$1.39 diluted). That loss understates the operating reality: it was cushioned by an $832.5M gain on Martinez insurance recoveries and burdened by a $313.0M lower-of-cost-or-market inventory charge. Adjusted fully-converted net loss ex-items was $474.6M (–$4.13/share), worse than 2024's $456.1M despite an $0.88/bbl improvement in gross margin, because per-barrel opex rose $0.86 to $8.38 and interest expense more than doubled to $181.6M. Gross refining margin of $8.77/bbl less opex of $8.38/bbl left $0.39/bbl before depreciation of roughly $2.00/bbl, G&A, interest and tax. Operating cash flow was –$78M against $705M of capex.

**Q2/H1 2026.** Revenue was $11,678.3M in Q2 (+56.2% y/y) and $19,582.6M in H1. Income from operations was $1,272.1M; net income attributable to PBF was $906.4M in Q2 and $1,104.7M in H1; GAAP diluted EPS was $7.54 and $9.22. Adjusted FC EPS ex-items was $6.22 and $5.40. Throughput rose 5.7% to 887.3 kbpd, driven almost entirely by the West Coast (269.9 kbpd versus 203.5, +32.6%) as Martinez returned to full operations in May 2026 after fifteen months of impairment following the February 1, 2025 fire.

The distribution matters more than the total. Q1 2026 EBITDA ex-items was $51.4M; Q2 was $1,218.4M. Ninety-six percent of first-half earnings arrived in one quarter, and that quarter combined the highest crack environment in company history with the first full-rate operation of the highest-complexity asset in the fleet in over a year. Any annualization of Q2 is a sensitivity, not a forecast.

**Cash flow quality — two offsetting observations.** H1 operating cash flow was +$1,265.1M against –$470.3M a year earlier. Net working capital was only an $89.0M use, because a $672.5M receivables build was largely offset by a $766.4M increase in accrued expenses. But the accrual build is substantially a price-level effect: inventory-related accruals rose $626.9M (from $1,351.5M to $1,978.4M) with Brent averaging $104.86/bbl. The 10-K states PBF is normally in a net payables position because crude payment terms exceed customer receivable terms, so a decrease in commodity prices generally results in a use of working capital. PBF can therefore post good margins and poor cash flow simultaneously if absolute crude prices fall — a channel independent of crack spreads and not visible in any earnings estimate. Second, H1 book tax expense was $372.5M ($95.6M current, $276.9M deferred) but cash taxes paid were $4.4M. That attribute utilization mechanically generates obligations under the Tax Receivable Agreement, which pays 85% of realized tax benefits to current and former insiders; the recorded liability was unchanged at $168.2M across both period ends despite $1.1B of H1 income, and PBF paid $130.8M under the TRA in January 2025 for the 2023 tax year. Model TRA payments as recurring.

**Balance sheet — the clearest improvement.** At 6/30/26: cash $894.1M, current assets $5,832.4M, current liabilities $4,384.1M (current ratio 1.33 versus 1.21 at year-end); long-term debt carrying value $1,749.1M on $1,800M face, so net debt of $855.0M against $1,620.4M six months earlier. Total equity $6,545.4M on $14,718.7M of assets — total debt to total capitalization about 21%, net debt to net capitalization about 12%. That is conservative leverage for a refiner. Liquidity exceeds $3.5B, with zero drawn on the $3.5B ABL revolver maturing August 2028.

The debt sequence tells the story of distress and repair. In March 2025 PBF issued $800M at 9.875% — emergency pricing. On May 28, 2026 it issued $500M at 7.25% due 2034, and on June 25 redeemed all $801.6M of 6.00% 2028 notes at par with a $2.2M extinguishment loss. Remaining structure is $800M at 9.875%, $500M at 7.875%, $500M at 7.25% — $154.7M of annual cash coupon on $1.8B face, a weighted average of 8.59%. The refinancing cut coupon by roughly $11.8M annually while retiring $301.6M of principal. The obvious remaining lever is the 9.875% tranche, worth up to $79.0M a year; its call schedule is not established here, so treat it as arithmetic, not a dated catalyst.

**Capital returns.** Zero buybacks in H1 2025, Q1 2026 and Q2 2026, with roughly $732.0M of a $1.75B authorization unused. The dividend is $0.275 quarterly — $1.10 annualized, a 1.78% yield costing about $131M a year, comfortably covered at current margins and uncovered at 2025 margins (2025 OCF was –$78M). Management has said it wants a net cash position before setting new repurchase targets. Given net debt of $855.0M and a stated ~$1.5B of cash at end-July, that milestone is close, and reactivation of a $732M program would represent roughly 10% of the market cap.

## Growth Analysis

There are two distinct things happening, and only one is durable.

The cyclical component is enormous and is the source of the estimate revisions. Current-quarter consensus moved from $2.69 to $7.42 (+176.4% over 90 days, +98.2% in 30 days); current year from $5.67 to $16.92; next quarter from $1.55 to $3.40; next year from $3.43 to $9.19 (+168.2%). Revenue grew 56% year over year in Q2 with margins expanding sharply. Mechanically, this is the estimate-revision configuration that historically precedes continued outperformance, and I want to state plainly that the signal is present: 90-day revisions far above +30%, revenue growth above 30%, and a forward multiple well under 30x.

I am discounting it for three specific reasons. First, the revisions are catching up to a spot condition rather than extrapolating a company trend — consensus itself embeds a 46% EPS decline from 2026 to 2027, so the sell side is explicitly modeling reversion rather than durability. Second, the room for further upward revision is limited: solving backward from the $9.19 2027 estimate (119.4M shares, ~25% tax, $155M interest, ~$670M D&A, ~$475M G&A, 325 MMbbl of throughput at $8.30/bbl opex) implies gross refining margin of about $16.80/bbl, versus $16.07 in the 2023 super-cycle. Consensus is not conservative on 2027; it is already at super-cycle margins. Third, the driver is geopolitical. Global refining tightness is a function of damaged refining capacity and disrupted transit; the same conditions that produced the windfall can unwind on verified normalization.

The durable growth components are smaller. Martinez at full rates restores 157 kbpd of Nelson-16.1 capacity permanently — real, but a one-time volume step, not a compounding trend. The Refining Business Improvement program shows evidence: H1 opex of $8.34/bbl against $9.25/bbl a year earlier, with Q2 at $8.00 versus $7.96 despite a 5.7% throughput increase and Martinez restart costs, plus a stated 20% reduction in purchased natural gas and ~$60M of procurement savings. The caveat is that per-barrel opex improves mechanically with throughput, and Q2 natural gas at $2.94/MMBTU versus $3.51 helped. Management is explicit that capital is going to balance-sheet strength and margin capture, not capacity expansion. There is no organic growth engine here beyond the cycle.

- $12/bbl → ~$0.75B EBITDA → roughly break-even EPS after $670M D&A and $155M interest. Stock at 1.14x book is expensive on that.
- $14/bbl → ~$1.4B EBITDA → ~$3.30–3.60 EPS → 17–19x. Fair-to-full.
- $16.80/bbl (2027 consensus) → ~$2.3B EBITDA → $9.19 EPS → 6.7x. Cheap.

So the entire valuation question is the persistent margin regime, and the multiple does not resolve it. What the price does offer is asset support: 1.06x LIFO-adjusted book, with book compounding rapidly if H2 delivers anything near consensus (H2 adjusted EPS of ~$10.8 would carry book toward $63–65 by year-end). That is a real floor mechanism, but it is a floor that erodes if crude prices fall, because the LIFO reserve shrinks with replacement cost.

A high-looking valuation on trough-adjacent trailing data is exactly the configuration that most often turns out to be a value trap in cyclicals, and I am scoring valuation at 5.5 rather than 7+ because the cheapness is conditional on a variable outside management's control, not because the stock has already appreciated. On an unchanged price versus the prior review with materially better fundamentals, the valuation score improves from 4.5 to 5.5; it does not reach the level that would support adding capital.

## Competitive Landscape

PBF's own 10-K states the position accurately: competitors include integrated national and international oil companies with greater resources, proprietary crude production, larger and more complex refineries, retail networks and better trade credit. PBF has none of those. Management prefers to deliver the crack directly to investors rather than hedge it away — a deliberate high-beta choice that explains both the $475M adjusted loss in 2025 and the $753M adjusted profit in Q2 2026.

The differentiated asset is California. With 323 kbpd across Torrance and Martinez, PBF is the largest independent merchant refiner in a state with unique CARB specifications, effectively no pipeline connectivity to other PADDs, and — per management — a structural need to import roughly a third of its gasoline. Two 2025 policy developments materially improved that position and are buried in Item 1A rather than headlined. SBx 1-2 gave the California Energy Commission authority to set a maximum Gross Gasoline Refining Margin with penalties above it; in August 2025 the CEC adopted a resolution pausing that rulemaking for five years while continuing to collect data. PBF then earned $53–58/bbl benchmark West Coast cracks in Q2 2026 in a state that had legislated the power to tax precisely that outcome and chose not to use it. Separately, in June 2025 three Congressional Review Act resolutions revoked the Clean Air Act waivers underpinning ACC II and Advanced Clean Fleets; California is litigating, and if the revocation stands the long-term gasoline demand-destruction path weakens materially. Both are revocable, and neither is a repeal.

Working against the position: RFS is a structural per-barrel tax on merchant refiners. Compliance cost was $680.1M in FY2025 and $609.3M in H1 2026, with the effective RIN basket price rising to $13.78/bbl from $6.14 after the EPA's March 27, 2026 final 2026/2027 rule partially reallocated 2023–2025 small refinery exemptions. On Q2 throughput the cost is roughly $4.10 per throughput barrel — do not confuse that with the $13.78 basket price. This is the clearest justification for a persistent discount to integrated peers, and it moved against PBF in 2026 even as cracks moved for it.

I could not verify specific competitor California capacity-closure announcements, and I make no claim on that arithmetic. Realized Q2 cracks and the structural import requirement are what support the West Coast thesis here.

## Risk Assessment

**Martinez legal and regulatory tail (unreserved, unquantified).** The February 1, 2025 fire generated $1.25B of cumulative insurance proceeds net of $30.0M of deductibles, with management expecting one further similar payment in H2 2026. The legal side is open: active investigations by CalOSHA, the Bay Area Air District (22 notices of violation), Contra Costa County, DOJ, the U.S. Attorney's Office, EPA and the Chemical Safety Board, plus a separate DOJ/USAO/EPA investigation of a November 2022 catalyst release. Seven civil actions are consolidated (including one with 285 plaintiffs, one with 204, one with 195); a January 29, 2026 scheduling order runs discovery and motion practice through Q1 2028. The company states potential liabilities cannot be reasonably estimated and I identified no reserve. Class certification was denied in the lead action in September 2025 and not appealed, which caps the plaintiff population — a genuine mitigant. The material risk is not damages but the possibility of operating restrictions on Martinez as a condition of resolution.

**A second containment event.** The May 8, 2026 Chalmette fire was added to the 10-Q cautionary statements with no dollar quantification, no insurance claim disclosed and no stated regulatory consequence. Gulf Coast throughput actually rose in the quarter, so the direct cost appears modest, but two loss-of-containment events in fifteen months at a company already facing multiple federal and state investigations elsewhere is a regulatory-relationship risk that exceeds the direct dollar cost.

**Live California constraints.** ABx 2-1 authority over minimum product inventories and turnaround pre-conditions remains in force and would raise working capital and reduce maintenance flexibility — relevant given the deliberate deferral of Martinez hydrocracker work into late Q3 and Toledo/Chalmette majors into 2027. The Bay Area Rule 6-5 FCC particulate standard takes effect in 2026, with Martinez compliance dependent on an alternative emissions monitoring system that the filing describes as subject to validation with no assurance.

**Crude price as an independent cash-flow variable.** Because PBF is normally net payables, a decline in absolute crude prices drains working capital even if cracks hold. That is a distinct and under-modeled channel.

**Labor.** USW, IOW and IBEW agreements are scheduled to expire on various dates in 2026 through 2028. Negotiating into the strongest margin environment in company history raises both union leverage and the cost of a stoppage.

**Supply overhang and insider selling.** Form 4s show 10% holder Control Empresarial de Capitales reducing its position from 18,891,698 shares (4/29/26) to 14,992,128 (7/30/26) — 20.6% of the holding — at prices from $42.01 to $74.01, with selling accelerating into strength (489,000 shares on July 30 alone). The residual is 12.65% of Class A shares. The steady round-lot cadence is consistent with programmatic rebalancing of a heavily appreciated concentrated position rather than a fundamental judgment, and the beneficial-owner identity is unconfirmed in filings. Separately, five insiders — former CEO/Chairman Nimbley (~$32.6M), CEO Lucey (~$16.2M) and three SVPs — exercised and sold roughly $57M on August 3–4 at $65–72, days after the print and at the all-time high. Option exercise-and-sell is a weaker signal than open-market selling, and both Lucey and Nimbley retained substantial holdings. But five insiders inside 48 hours, with no open-market purchases anywhere in the reviewed period and no buyback at peak earnings, is not a supportive governance picture.

**Takeover insulation.** The TRA's change-of-control provision requires an immediate discounted present-value payment assuming full utilization; the 10-K states outright that this may deter a sale. Combined with 101% change-of-control puts on all three note series, a 75% supermajority bylaw threshold and blank-check preferred authority, a control-premium outcome is improbable.

**Dilution is not a risk.** Total potential incremental shares of 3,575,728 equal 3.0% of Class A outstanding (2.93% post-issuance ownership dilution).

Neither view is dominant, which is itself informative for a stock whose earnings range across the last two years spans –$4.13 to +$6.22 per share.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $86 (+39%)

Global refining capacity damage is physical and takes years to repair; product inventories rebuild slowly into 2027 while crude trade patterns normalize faster, sustaining both wide cracks and favorable heavy-sour differentials. PBF captures it with Martinez at full rates for the first cycle in two years, turnarounds deferred out of 2026, and West Coast cracks supported by California's structural import requirement with the GGRM penalty rulemaking shelved for five years. 2026 delivers near the $16.92 consensus; 2027 delivers $12+. Net cash is reached in Q3, the $732M buyback reactivates (10% of the cap), the 9.875% notes are refinanced for up to $79M of annual savings, and Venezuelan heavy barrels returning widen differentials further for five heavy-sour-capable plants. Seven times $12.25 = $86; alternatively 1.25x a mid-2027 book value near $70.

### Bear Case — $42 (–32%)

Transit normalizes, OPEC+ supply lands into a market with slowing demand, and cracks retrace toward 2024–25 levels. The reversion hits twice: margins compress and falling absolute crude prices drain the working capital that generated $1,265M of H1 operating cash flow, so cash conversion deteriorates faster than earnings. RIN costs stay near $4/bbl of throughput regardless. An adverse Martinez regulatory resolution imposes operating restrictions or a material reserve; deferred Toledo and Chalmette turnarounds land in 2027 at lower margins. At $12–13/bbl of gross refining margin PBF earns roughly nothing, and the stock trades to 0.75x a book value that has stopped compounding: $42. The 52-week low of $21.46 is what a genuine return to 2025 conditions with Martinez impaired looked like; that is not the modal bear, but it bounds the tail.

### Base Case — $68 (+10%)

Q3 lands strong — cracks were still elevated through July and consensus at $7.42 for the quarter looks achievable given three beats in four quarters — then margins moderate through Q4 and 2027 toward $13–15/bbl as some capacity returns and transit partially normalizes. 2026 finishes near consensus, 2027 delivers $5–6 rather than $9.19. Book value compounds to roughly $63–65 at year-end and $67–68 by mid-2027; the stock holds approximately 1.0x forward book, cross-checked at about 12x a normalized $5.50–6.00. Buyback reactivation provides a bid; the holder overhang and RIN drag cap the multiple.

**Probability-weighted:** 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear gives roughly +5% expected return over twelve months against a 32% downside case. That is fair value with unattractive dispersion.

## Conclusion

PBF is doing what a well-run merchant refiner should do at a cycle peak: running its most complex assets at full rates, deferring turnarounds to capture margin, cutting per-barrel operating cost, retiring high-coupon debt, and rebuilding the balance sheet toward net cash. Net debt of $855M against $6.5B of equity and $3.5B of liquidity is a materially different company from the one that issued paper at 9.875% sixteen months ago. Book value at $54.06 per share, or roughly $58 including the after-tax LIFO reserve, provides an asset floor at 1.06–1.14x that limits how far the equity can fall while margins remain positive.

What the price does not offer is compensation for the specific risks attached. Three point six five times current-year earnings looks like a mispricing until you note that the 2027 consensus behind the 6.7x forward multiple requires roughly $16.80/bbl of gross refining margin — above the 2023 super-cycle and nearly double the 2024–25 average — so the discount is a probability weight on war-driven cracks, not an independent margin of safety.
