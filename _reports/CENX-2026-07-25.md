**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Century Aluminum is a pure-play primary aluminum producer whose earnings have inflected sharply higher, driven less by the LME aluminum price than by a policy artifact: the U.S. Section 232 tariff, which rose from 10% to 25% (March 2025) to 50% (June 2025) and pushed the Midwest Premium from a 2024 average of $427/tonne to $1,295 in 2025 and $2,294 in Q1 2026. Century is a price-taker that captures this premium duty-free at its two U.S. smelters and the European Duty Paid Premium at Grundartangi. Q1 2026 adjusted net income was $171M ($1.63/share) on adjusted EBITDA of $231M, with Q2 guided to $315–335M and management citing a spot-implied ~$400M quarterly run-rate before Mt. Holly's full ramp. The balance sheet has been materially cleaned up: net debt of roughly $220M against ~$611M liquidity, following a 2025 refinancing and the $200M-cash Hawesville sale.

The central issue is durability. The earnings surge rests on the persistence of a 50% tariff, an administration- and litigation-contingent policy. The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs did not touch Section 232, but it underscored how policy-dependent the windfall is. Layered on top are governance flags that cluster uncomfortably: an unremediated material weakness in internal controls tied to Jamalco consolidation (with a resulting non-reliance restatement), broad-based open-market C-suite selling in February–March 2026 with zero purchases, and a ~$327M Glencore sell-down at ~$52 that cut the largest holder/customer/supplier's stake from 36.4% to 30%. Century also has missed EPS estimates in four consecutive quarters, and near-term consensus estimates are falling (current-quarter EPS revised down 14.8% over 90 days).

At $46.21, the stock is down ~34% from its June high of $70.43 but still up ~102% over the trailing year. The forward multiple of ~12.8x looks cheap against a Materials peer median near 29.6x, but the "E" is a peak-cycle number amplified by a transient premium, and the market is discounting that. We maintain a HOLD. The de-risked balance sheet and genuine adjusted earnings power floor the downside, but the tariff dependency, governance cluster, and falling near-term estimates preclude an asymmetric long case. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD; the balance of evidence has not changed enough to warrant a call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Century operates in a single "mine to metal" segment across three smelters: Grundartangi, Iceland (320k tpy, hydro/geothermal-powered, ASI-certified low-carbon "Natur-Al" product), Sebree, Kentucky (220k tpy), and Mt. Holly, South Carolina (230k tpy). Total nameplate capacity is ~770k tpy against 638k tonnes produced in 2025. Vertical integration extends upstream through a 55% JV interest in Jamalco (Jamaica bauxite/alumina, acquired May 2023) and a carbon-anode plant in Vlissingen, Netherlands, which supplies 93–98% of Grundartangi's anode needs.

The business model is a leveraged bet on the spread between the aluminum price (LME + regional premium + value-added premium) and its two dominant costs, electrical power and alumina, which together represent 78–84% of COGS. Century cannot pass through input-cost increases. Its structural edge is geographic and trade-policy positioning rather than scale or cost leadership: duty-free access to the U.S. and EU, the two structurally short aluminum markets, while foreign competitors pay tariffs.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory shows a cyclical business emerging from deep losses:

| $M | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 2,777 | 2,185 | 2,220 | 2,528 |
| Gross profit | 47 | 92 | 185 | 256 |
| Operating income | (150) | 32 | 121 | 158 |
| Net income (to Century) | (14) | (43) | 337 | 42 |
| Operating cash flow | — | 106 | (25) | 185 |
| FCF | — | — | — | 85 |

Two accounting distortions require adjustment. The 2024 net income of $337M was inflated by a $245.9M non-cash bargain purchase gain on Jamalco; underlying 2024 operations were far weaker. The 2025 net income of $42M was depressed by a $94.7M net loss on forward/derivative contracts — mark-to-market losses on LME/MWP hedges as prices spiked, an economic loss on hedges offset by physical gains.

The Q1 2026 inflection is the operative story. GAAP net income surged to $337.5M ($3.23 diluted EPS), but that figure includes a $287.9M one-time Hawesville sale gain and a $33M insurance gain, offset by $65.3M in derivative losses.

The balance sheet is materially de-risked. Total debt was $545.9M at Q1 2026 — $400M of 6.875% senior secured notes due 2032, $85.5M of 2.75% convertible notes due 2028, and a $66M Iceland revolver. Cash of $244M plus $89.5M restricted (Hawesville proceeds) plus revolver availability yields the ~$611M liquidity, and net debt sits below management's <$300M target. The July 2025 refinancing (2032 notes issued, 2028 secured notes redeemed, Grundartangi casthouse facility repaid) cut interest expense. FCF conversion has improved: 2025 FCF of $85M on OCF of $185M.

Two tax-related items add optionality but not near-term earnings. A ~$1.545B federal NOL carryforward (against a ~$471M valuation allowance) means Century pays little cash tax and could release the allowance into GAAP earnings if profitability proves durable — though a Section 382 ownership change (a live risk given Glencore's shifting stake) could impair NOL use. Separately, the Section 45X IRA credit reduces COGS by roughly $25M/quarter, with a $198M receivable accrued; note the phase-out beginning 2031 (25%/year to zero by 2034) under the July 2025 legislation.

Capital allocation is conservative: no dividend, a $43.7M buyback authorization untouched since 2015, and 2026 capex of $170–180M (including insurance-reimbursed Grundartangi repairs, Mt. Holly restart, and Jamalco). Management signaled potential capital returns only after current growth projects conclude.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term volume growth is real and identifiable: Mt. Holly is restarting to a ~230k-tonne run-rate expected in Q3 2026 (adding ~10% to U.S. primary production), and Grundartangi is recovering from transformer failures toward near-full production by end of July 2026, though at reduced amperage until replacement transformers arrive in Q4.

The longer-dated growth optionality is the EGA/Oklahoma greenfield smelter, a 750k-tonne project (EGA 60% / Century 40%) that would be the first new U.S. smelter since 1980 and roughly double U.S. primary output. It is backed by a $500M DOE cooperative agreement, with FID and groundbreaking targeted for end of 2026, contingent on a power deal, additional financing, and a definitive JV agreement. This is high-optionality but early-stage and capital-intensive; it should not be capitalized into a 12-month view.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and net-negative near-term. Current-quarter EPS was revised down 14.8% over 90 days ($2.70 → $2.30), and current-year down 10.1% over 30 days ($10.88 → $9.78). Out-year estimates are more volatile — next-quarter up 19.2% over 90 days but next-year down 20.3% over 30 days. The estimate-revision signal that would support a stronger growth call (sustained +30% velocity on rising revenue) is not present here; the near-term trend is falling. Combined with four consecutive EPS misses (the last three quarters missed by 5–35%), forward visibility is poor. The growth thesis depends heavily on premium durability rather than on unit economics the company controls.

## Valuation Assessment

CENX trades at ~12.8x forward EPS versus a Materials peer median of ~29.6x (CBT 16.1x, SXT 36.6x, KNF 29.6x, HCC 36.0x, SLGN 15.1x). The discount is warranted and reflects the market's assessment that the earnings base is a peak-cycle number amplified by a transient policy premium.

The valuation dimension is the crux. This is closer to a cyclical peak where the "E" is inflated than to a classic cyclical-trough inflection where trailing P/E understates normalized power. Q1 2026 adjusted earnings ($1.63/quarter) rest on a Midwest Premium at $2,294/tonne, up more than 5x from 2024's $427. If the tariff persists at 50%, the ~$9.78 current-year and ~$10.90 next-year consensus figures are achievable and the stock is cheap. If the premium normalizes materially — through tariff exemption, reduction, or a legal challenge — earnings compress toward the mid-cycle run rate that prevailed before the tariff, and the forward multiple on those lower earnings is far less attractive. Analyst price targets ($65 low, $74 mean, $83 high) sit well above the current $46.21, but those targets embed continued premium capture and have not been marked down to match the falling near-term EPS revisions; they should be treated with skepticism given the four-quarter miss streak.

A cheap headline multiple on peak-cycle, policy-dependent earnings is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, absent an independent catalyst confirming premium durability. We do not see that catalyst. We score valuation at 5.0 — neither clearly mispriced upward nor a clear short. The stock is reasonably priced for a two-sided bet on tariff persistence.

## Competitive Landscape

The primary aluminum market is global and historically oversupplied by subsidized Chinese and overseas capacity. Century competes against larger, more vertically integrated players. Its advantage is geographic and trade-policy positioning: duty-free access to the structurally short U.S. and EU markets, capturing the Midwest and European Duty Paid Premiums. Low-carbon Iceland production is a differentiator against carbon-regulation risk, and Jamalco provides a partial natural hedge on alumina.

The current environment is unusually favorable: 50% U.S. tariffs at record-high premiums, an April 2026 executive order that closed valuation loopholes used to circumvent Section 232 on downstream extrusions (boosting value-added demand), a stated 1.4M-tonne global 2026 aluminum deficit with ~2.5M tonnes of Gulf production disrupted, and near-record-low LME inventories. Mt. Holly's restart and Grundartangi's recovery incrementally improve Century's share of U.S. primary output.

The competitive position is stable rather than improving. Century's edge is entirely a function of a policy that it does not control and that could reverse. Its cost position (dependence on volatile market-based power at Sebree, exposed to weather events like Winter Storm Fern in Q1 2026) is not a durable moat. Score: 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff/premium dependence.** This is the dominant risk. Any exemption, reduction, or repeal would directly compress Century's margins. The February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling did not hit Section 232, but it demonstrated that tariff policy is litigation- and administration-dependent. The earnings inflection is a policy windfall of uncertain duration.

**Cost-spread risk.** Century cannot pass through input costs. Power and alumina prices are volatile (alumina spiked to $805/tonne in late 2024 on the Guinea export ban). Weather and Icelandic hydro curtailments recur.

**Related-party concentration.** Glencore was 54% of 2025 sales (47.1% in Q1 2026), a major alumina supplier, a lender, and (post-sell-down) a 30% owner with a board seat. This multi-dimensional entanglement carries conflict-of-interest risk, and the sell-down is a signal about how the best-informed holder views post-rally valuation.

**Unremediated material weakness.** Disclosure controls were "not effective" at both 12/31/2025 and 3/31/2026, tied to Jamalco consolidation, and triggered a non-reliance restatement (8-K Item 4.02, filed 3/3/2026). Net income to Century was unchanged, but this is a genuine, open governance red flag.

**Operational fragility.** The Grundartangi transformer failures (Nov 2024 and Oct 2025) idled roughly two-thirds of that smelter's output; recovery is underway but running at reduced amperage until Q4. Jamalco was hit by Hurricane Melissa and lower bauxite quality. Insurance lags claims by 1–2 quarters.

**Convertible dilution.** The $85.5M 2.75% converts (strike ~$18.74) are deep in the money — if-converted value exceeded principal by $158.8M at 3/31/26, representing ~4.6M dilutive shares into the 2028 maturity.

**EGA execution.** A multi-billion-dollar greenfield with financing, power-contract, and partner-governance risk; Century holds only 40%.

Because several of these risks (tariff reversal, the governance cluster, the earnings-miss streak) are specific, potentially material, and not fully priced given the still-elevated analyst targets, we score risk factors at 3.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 79.9% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 64.3% (a 15.6-point spread, 92nd percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 79.9% > long-term 73.4%), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress into the next earnings print. IV has collapsed from 129.5% on June 18 to 79.9%, coinciding with the ~15% price decline over the same window. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-4.0% at the 3-month tenor) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.33, so options positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the price drop. Short interest at 14.2% of float (3.8 days to cover) reflects elevated institutional skepticism but is not at squeeze-risk extremes. The rich, backwardated IV argues for patience on any entry; the near-term implied move of ±17.7% to ±21.7% means the next print is a two-sided binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$65–83).** The 50% Section 232 tariff persists, the Midwest Premium holds near current levels, and the 1.4M-tonne global deficit plus Middle East supply disruption sustain elevated LME pricing. Mt. Holly and Grundartangi ramp fully into Q3–Q4, lifting volumes ~10–15%. Adjusted EBITDA runs toward the spot-implied ~$400M/quarter. On ~$10–11 of EPS and even a modest re-rate toward peer multiples, the stock reaches the $65–83 analyst target range. The Hawesville put (potential $300M cash event once the data center delivers ≥375 MW IT load) and NOL valuation-allowance release provide additional non-cash upside. Net debt near zero funds the EGA optionality and eventual capital returns.

**Bear case (~$28–35).** A tariff exemption, reduction, or adverse legal development compresses the Midwest Premium back toward historical norms. Earnings revert to the mid-cycle run rate that prevailed before the tariff — an order of magnitude below Q1 2026 adjusted levels. On normalized earnings the current forward multiple looks expensive rather than cheap, the four-quarter miss streak continues, and the governance cluster weighs on the multiple. The stock retraces toward pre-rally levels. Aluminum and power/alumina cost spreads could also compress independently of tariff policy.

The two cases are roughly symmetric around the current price, which is why this is a HOLD. The bear scenario is not a distressed-balance-sheet story — the de-risked capital structure floors the downside above prior cyclical troughs — but the earnings base is genuinely contingent on a policy the company does not control.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmation of tariff durability — a formal extension, statutory codification, or legal resolution that removes the near-term exemption/reduction risk on Section 232, allowing the premium to be capitalized with confidence.
- Positive estimate-revision inflection: current-year and next-year consensus stabilizing and turning up (reversing the -10% to -20% 30-day trends), ideally alongside an earnings beat that breaks the four-quarter miss streak.
- Remediation of the material weakness and evidence the governance cluster was benign (e.g., no further Glencore selling, resumption of insider buying).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A tariff exemption, reduction, or adverse court ruling that compresses the Midwest Premium materially.
- A guidance cut or a fifth consecutive EPS miss with deteriorating spread economics.
- A Section 382 ownership change that impairs NOL use, or a further material governance event (new restatement, accelerated Glencore exit).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural checkpoint, alongside any Section 232 policy development and the Mt. Holly/Grundartangi ramp confirmation in Q3. The EGA FID decision (targeted end of 2026) is a longer-dated input.

## Conclusion

Century Aluminum has genuinely transformed its balance sheet and posted a real earnings inflection, but that inflection is a leveraged bet on the Section 232 tariff-driven Midwest Premium rather than on aluminum prices or company-controlled unit economics. At $46.21, ~34% off the June high but still ~102% higher year-over-year, the ~12.8x forward multiple looks cheap only if the peak-cycle, policy-dependent earnings prove durable. The governance cluster — unremediated material weakness, non-reliance restatement, broad C-suite selling into strength, and the Glencore sell-down — combined with four consecutive EPS misses and falling near-term estimates, caps the case. The de-risked capital structure and ~$611M liquidity floor the downside, producing a balanced, two-sided setup with no asymmetry to justify a stronger call. We maintain **HOLD**, with a preference to reassess after the Q2 print and any Section 232 policy development, and to await a better entry given the rich, backwardated implied volatility.
