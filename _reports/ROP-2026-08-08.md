## Executive Summary

Roper is a portfolio of roughly 30 vertical software and technology-enabled products businesses, 77% of revenue software, generating a 30.8% free cash flow margin on 1.3% capital intensity. The operating record is intact: FY2025 revenue grew 12.3% to $7,902.5M, adjusted free cash flow compounded from $1,598M in 2021 to $2,466M in 2025 (~11.4% annually) on a nearly flat share count, and Q2 2026 marked a third consecutive quarter of modest consensus beats with guidance raised to $22.15–$22.30 of adjusted diluted EPS. At $402.25 the stock trades at 18.1x that guidance midpoint, 16.6x next-year consensus of $24.28, 15.6x EV/EBITDA, and a 6.7–6.9% free cash flow yield — versus roughly 29x FCF per share in 2021.

Three things have changed since the prior review at $330. First, the price is 21.8% higher while consensus estimates rose only 1.2–1.7% over 90 days, so the margin of safety compressed far more than fundamentals improved. Second, the quality of the earnings beat is thinner than the headline: adjusted net earnings grew 2.7% year over year in Q2 while adjusted EBITDA margin fell 130bps to 38.6%, and the +10% adjusted EPS print is reconciled almost entirely by the diluted share count falling from 108.4M to 100.6M. Third, the balance sheet financed that share reduction. Total debt rose from $9,301.0M to $11,319.4M in six months, revolver draw went from $850M to $2,850M against a $3,500M facility, and net leverage moved from roughly 3.0x to ~3.4x. Undrawn revolver capacity fell from about $2,644M to roughly $643M.

The structural question the market is actually debating is whether seat-priced vertical software in knowledge-work professions — timekeepers at Aderant, agency staff at Vertafore, project accountants at Deltek, clinicians at Clinisys and CentralReach — holds its unit count as AI compresses professional headcount. Roper's own Q2 10-Q added the language: new AI offerings may "transform workforce needs and may negatively impact demand for our offerings." That is the mechanism behind a multiple that fell from ~29x to ~17.7x FCF per share while cash flow rose 54%. I cannot resolve it, and neither can the covering analysts, whose most recent published targets ($415 JPMorgan, $417 RBC) imply 3–4% upside after a beat-and-raise. Base case $435 (+8.1%), bull $525 (+30.5%), bear $305 (−24.2%). Probability-weighted return of roughly 5–6% plus a 0.90% dividend is fair value, not an entry signal. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Roper acquires and holds market-leading niche software and instrumentation franchises, operating them on a decentralized model with corporate retaining capital allocation and incentive design. Headcount was approximately 19,400 at year-end 2025 (13,100 U.S.).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Application Software | $4,483.0M | 56.7% | 26.8% | +4.5% |
| Network Software | $1,600.8M | 20.3% | 43.5% | +3.6% |
| Technology Enabled Products | $1,818.7M | 23.0% | 34.5% | +7.1% |
| **Total** | **$7,902.5M** | | **32.0%** pre-corporate | **+4.9%** |

A classification note that matters for any screen: the "Electronic Equipment & Instruments" industry tag is a legacy artifact. The Q2 2026 revenue disaggregation shows software-related revenue of $1,626.1M against $482.8M of hardware product revenue — 77.1% software. The peer set supplied for comparison (Teledyne, Keysight, Trimble, Zebra, Cognex) is a hardware and instrumentation cohort and mis-specifies the business.

Revenue quality is high. Q2 2026 recurring revenue (SaaS, term license, post-contract support) was $1,216.4M, +10.9%, and reoccurring transaction revenue $202.2M, +7.9% — together 67.3% of revenue. The soft line is non-recurring software (perpetual and multi-year licenses, professional services) at $207.5M, +0.1% year over year and +1.6% for the half. That is the line most sensitive to new-logo wins, and its flatness is consistent with continued pressure at Deltek's government-contracting franchise, which the FY2025 10-K explicitly blamed for declining Application Software non-recurring revenue and which is conspicuously absent from Q2's list of growth drivers.

Customer concentration is genuinely low — no customer reached 10% of any segment or of total company revenue in 2025. End-market concentration is a different matter: federal contracting budgets (Deltek), K-12 district budgets (Frontline), autism-therapy reimbursement (CentralReach), higher-education enrollment (Transact/CBORD) and municipal water capital budgets (Neptune) are all government-funded or government-regulated, and the 10-K concedes shutdowns and spending reallocation "has had" an adverse impact.

FY2025 revenue grew 12.3% (organic +5.4%, acquisitions +6.7%, FX +0.2%) with operating margin flat, yet net earnings fell slightly to $1,536.3M from $1,549.3M. The bridge is entirely below the operating line: equity-investment gains fell from 3.3% of revenue to 0.3% (FY2024 contained a $135.6M Certinia gain plus a $96.4M Indicor markup; FY2025 had a $24.0M Indicor markup), and net interest rose from 3.7% to 4.1% of revenue.

Free cash flow is the metric that carries this business. FY2025: operating cash flow $2,540.3M less $47.4M capex and $57.3M capitalized software = $2,435.6M, a 30.8% margin. Two qualifications. The OBBBA repeal of Section 174 domestic R&D capitalization produced approximately $150M of cash tax benefit in 2025 — about 6% of FCF — with the remainder utilized over three to five years, though management guides to a stable go-forward cash tax rate. And working capital contributed less in 2025 than 2024.

For FY2026, H1 adjusted FCF was $1,009.9M. Roper's cash generation is H2-weighted because the Frontline SaaS renewal cycle falls primarily in Q3. Applying FY2025's H1/full-year ratio of 36.9% gives roughly $2,736M; applying FY2025's 8% growth rate gives roughly $2,663M. Call it $2.66–2.74B, a 6.7–6.9% yield on $39.8B of equity value. That range does not attempt to separate the residual Section 174 benefit from the rising cash interest burden, which move in opposite directions.

The GAAP DEPS figure of $11.62 is not economically comparable to anything: it contains an $835.2M pre-tax ($7.86/share after tax) non-cash markup of the Indicor equity investment and a 10.7% effective tax rate driven by Indicor basis adjustments. FY2026 GAAP guidance of $24.78–$24.93 embeds $9.16/share of H1 Indicor gains; stripping them leaves roughly $15.70.

### Leverage: the six-month inflection

| | Dec 31, 2025 | Jun 30, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Total debt | $9,301.0M | $11,319.4M |
| — Revolver drawn | $850.0M | **$2,850.0M** |

| Net debt / net capital | 31.2% | 36.9% |

Against an estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$3.26B, gross leverage is ~3.5x and net ~3.36x, up roughly half a turn from year-end. Every incremental dollar funded buybacks, not acquired cash flow. The covenant is not the constraint — Total Debt/Total Capital of 0.377 against a 0.65 limit leaves large headroom, though note that debt-funded buybacks move this ratio roughly twice as fast as debt-funded M&A because they raise the numerator and cut the denominator simultaneously.

The binding near-term constraint is revolver capacity: $3,500M facility, $2,850M drawn plus $7.0M of letters of credit, leaving roughly $643M undrawn against $705.8M of 2026 maturities and $1,550.1M in 2027. Offsets are a $1,000M accordion, roughly $1.1B of net after-tax Indicor proceeds expected in H2, and roughly $1.7B of implied H2 free cash flow. The plan works; it is simply thinner than a $40B company would normally run, and it makes the Indicor closing a liquidity input rather than a windfall.

Interest expense is the underappreciated drag. Net interest ran $259.2M (FY2024) → $325.0M (FY2025) → $210.7M in H1 2026, with Q2 alone at $111.4M. Senior notes of $8,500M carry roughly a 3.74% weighted average coupon ($318.3M due in 2026 per the contractual table); the revolver at SOFR + 0.920% on $2,850M adds roughly $130–140M annualized. FY2026 net interest of $450–460M is about $130M above FY2025, or roughly $1.03 per share after tax at a 21% rate — 4.6% of the adjusted DEPS midpoint. Coverage fell from 6.9x (FY2025 operating income / net interest) to 5.5x in H1 2026. Declining, not alarming.

### What the buyback actually earned

H1 2026 repurchases were $2,724.1M for 7.865M shares at an average $346.34, funded by $1,061.6M of operating cash flow and $2,000.0M of net revolver borrowings — roughly two-thirds debt. The share reduction from a 106.6M base is +7.9% mechanically. Interest on ~$1,816M of incremental debt at ~4.8% costs ~$87M pre-tax, ~$69M after tax, roughly $0.70 per share on ~99M shares, or −3.1%. Net accretion is therefore approximately +4.8% (about +3.2% on a fully debt-funded assumption). Accretive, but roughly half the headline benefit is consumed by financing cost. The scarce resource created is not EPS; it is the used revolver capacity.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is decelerating gently: FY2025 5.4%, H1 2026 5.3%, Q2 2026 4.9%. Management raised full-year organic guidance to approximately 6% from +5–6%, which requires H2 acceleration. The two software segments — 77% of revenue and the entire strategic premise — grew organically at 4.5% (Application) and 3.6% (Network) in Q2. Technology Enabled Products grew 7.1% organically, the fastest of the three, but also carries the real margin problem.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current quarter +2.4% over 90 days, current year +1.2%, next year +1.6%. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a fundamental inflection; it is a company delivering slightly better than a stable plan. Three consecutive beats of 0.4%, 2.6% and 0.8% confirm sandbagged-but-not-conservative guidance, which raises confidence in the $22.22 current-year and $24.28 next-year consensus without implying upside surprise of consequence.

Three growth mechanisms deserve separate treatment.

**Payments attach.** Transact/CBORD, Procare and Subsplash are all layering embedded payments, which raises revenue per customer and stickiness but dilutes gross margin through interchange pass-through. This is the disclosed cause of gross-margin dilution in both software segments. Reoccurring transaction revenue grew 9.5% in H1 versus 12.6% for recurring — payments is not yet outgrowing the subscription base, so the mix dilution is currently buying less growth than it costs in margin.

**M&A, paused.** Roper deployed roughly $3.5B on acquisitions in 2024 and $3.4B in 2025. H1 2026 acquisition spend was $27.5M — one deal at a $25.0M purchase price. Management frames this as pricing discipline ("With significant capital deployment capacity, we are focused on attractive acquisition targets"), and the FY2025 10-K added "susceptibility to market disruption from AI or otherwise" to the diligence criteria in its acquisition risk factor. The pause removes roughly 6–7 points of inorganic revenue growth from the run rate. Since acquisitions are excluded from guidance, resumption is upside to the top line but would compete with buyback for the same balance-sheet capacity.

**AI as product versus AI as threat.** The FY2025 10-K systematically relabels Aderant, CentralReach, Deltek, Vertafore, ConstructConnect, DAT, Foundry, iPipeline and Subsplash as "AI-enabled," and Reuters framed the July guidance raise as driven by AI software demand. The same filings warn that AI capabilities "could become commoditized" and may "transform workforce needs." Proprietary vertical data plus regulatory workflow depth is a genuine defense against generic models; per-seat pricing in professions facing headcount compression is a genuine exposure. Roper does not disclose the split between per-seat and per-transaction pricing, which is the single disclosure that would most move my confidence in either direction.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | Value at $402.25 |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026E adjusted DEPS ($22.225 mid) | 18.1x |
| P/E on next-year consensus ($24.28) | 16.6x |
| P/E on GAAP-ex-Indicor DEPS (~$15.70) | 25.6x |
| EV / FY2026E adjusted EBITDA (~$3.26B) | 15.6x |
| FCF yield ($2.66–2.74B) | 6.7–6.9% |
| Dividend yield ($3.64 annualized) | 0.90% |

Whether ROP is cheap turns on one accounting judgment. Roper adds back 100% of acquired-intangible amortization — $835M pre-tax, $6.53 per share after tax, 29.4% of adjusted EPS — for a company that deployed $8,960M on acquisitions in 2023–2025. On adjusted EPS the stock is 18.1x; on GAAP earnings excluding the Indicor mark it is 25.6x. The defensible case for the add-back is that customer-relationship intangibles amortizing over 10–15 years do not require replacement capex the way plant does, and Roper's disclosed churn is low enough that acquired relationships plausibly outlive their schedules.

Free cash flow is the honest tiebreaker, and it is supportive without being decisive: adjusted FCF per share compounded from roughly $15.0 in 2021 to $22.79 in 2025, about 11% annually, on a nearly flat share count. The implied multiple on that measure fell from roughly 29x to 17.7x. FCF also charges nothing for acquisition capital, and Roper has historically spent all of it plus incremental debt on M&A, so the fully distributable figure is lower than the headline. If one credits only organic growth of 5% as free, the arithmetic is roughly 6.8% cash yield plus 5% growth, or a high-single-digit to low-double-digit return before any multiple change — an equity risk premium of about [unverified] points over the 10-year.

The peer table supplied (median P/E 31.6) is not usable as a direct anchor because it is a hardware cohort. On the more comparable GAAP-ex-Indicor basis ROP's 25.6x sits roughly 19% below that median, but the businesses differ enough that I would not underwrite a target off it.

What the market repriced is worth stating explicitly, since the stock is 26.4% below its 52-week high after a roughly 42% peak-to-trough decline. The company's own proxy quantifies the gap: cumulative TSR from a December 31, 2020 base of 100 reached 106 for Roper against 258 for its disclosed peer group, while the company-selected performance measure, adjusted free cash flow, rose 54.3%. Operations compounded; the multiple contracted. The compression has three plausible components: (1) an AI-driven reassessment of the durability of seat-based vertical software revenue, which the company now names in its own risk factors; (2) a higher discount rate applied to a 3.4x-levered compounder; and (3) organic deceleration plus a paused M&A engine, which together cut the growth rate embedded in the old multiple roughly in half. Component (2) is cyclical and reversible; (1) is unresolvable from outside; (3) is partly a management choice.

I do not think the current price is a mispricing I can prove. At 16.6x next-year consensus with 6.8% cash yield the stock is priced for mid-single-digit organic growth continuing and no re-rating — a reasonable central case rather than an obviously wrong one. Base-case fair value of $435 comes from 18x next-year consensus $24.28 (equivalently ~16x FY2027E FCF per share of roughly $27.5), which brackets the analyst median of $420 and mean of $440. That is +8.1% from spot, plus 0.90% dividend. The two most recent published target changes, JPMorgan to $415 and RBC to $417, imply 3–4% and confirm that the sell side sees the same modest gap. Analyst targets deserve treatment as floors only on cheap, accelerating stories; here estimates are rising 1–2%, not 30%, so the target cluster is informative rather than stale.

## Competitive Landscape

Roper's stated moat is niche leadership plus workflow embeddedness: mission-critical systems of record in fragmented markets where "no single company competes with us over a significant number of product lines." The evidence is consistent — 67.3% recurring and reoccurring revenue, negative net working capital because customers prepay (deferred revenue of $1,707.8M with no matching current asset), no customer above 10%, gross margin stable at 69.2% for four years.

The trajectory question is more mixed than the level. Both software segments decelerated in Q2, non-recurring software revenue was flat, and the composition of margin compression differs by segment in a way most screens will miss. Network Software's operating margin fell 290bps to 41.0%, but its gross margin *improved* to 84.3% from 83.2%; the entire decline sits in SG&A, which the 10-Q attributes to the cost and amortization profiles of the 2025 acquisitions (Subsplash, Convoy). That is purchase accounting, not competitive erosion. Technology Enabled Products is the reverse: its 210bp operating decline runs through gross margin (56.9% from 58.6%) on water-meter input cost pressure at Neptune, with only $6.0M of quarterly segment D&A, and the 10-Q separately flags "an expected decline in our water meter technology business." One segment's compression is accounting; the other's is operational and tied to a physical-input cost cycle.

The cyclical software assets — DAT in freight matching and ConstructConnect in pre-construction — were both cited as Q2 growth leaders, which is a positive read given soft freight and construction conditions. Roper also disclosed a receivables purchase agreement at Outgo (freight payments within DAT) under which factored receivables are sold non-recourse and derecognized, with a $10.1M retained beneficial interest; collections on that interest ($4.5M in H1) appear in investing activities but are added back to adjusted FCF. Immaterial today, worth watching if Outgo scales.

On AI, the competitive position is genuinely two-sided and I will not pretend otherwise. Regulatory workflow depth and proprietary vertical data (lab result formats at Clinisys, government contract accounting rules at Deltek, P&C rating logic at Vertafore) are hard for a general model to replicate and are compliance-audited. Seat-count exposure in the same accounts is the offsetting risk. Roper also disclosed dependence on third-party model providers "such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft" as *suppliers* it pays, warning that rising provider pricing may not be passable to customers — a gross-margin risk that scales with AI feature adoption.

## Risk Assessment

**AI-driven seat compression (structural, unresolvable from outside).** The Q2 10-Q's new language — "New AI offerings may disrupt our offerings or transform workforce needs and may negatively impact demand" — is the bear case in the company's own words. It is also the most credible explanation for a multiple that halved while cash flow rose. Partially priced at 16.6x forward; not fully resolvable until several quarters of seat-based renewal data emerge.

**Revolver capacity and the Indicor dependency.** Roughly $643M of undrawn revolver against $705.8M of 2026 maturities means the H2 plan depends on the Indicor instrumentation sale to AMETEK closing. Roper is a 43.4% passive minority holder; CD&R controls the process; the transaction (~$5.0B enterprise value, ~$1.3B pre-tax / ~$1.1B net proceeds to Roper) remains subject to regulatory approval. A delay would not create distress — Roper still generates ~$1.7B of H2 FCF and has a $1,000M accordion — but it would force a choice between buyback pace and balance-sheet repair.

**Floating-rate exposure.** $2,850M drawn at SOFR + 0.920%. Each 25bp of rate change moves interest cost by roughly $7M pre-tax annually on the current draw, and the credit-agreement spread grid leaves roughly 38bp of downside on a rating downgrade (~$11M annually on the current draw). This is a modest earnings input.

**Q4 2026 goodwill test.** Goodwill of $21,341.2M exceeded stockholders' equity of $19,881.5M at year-end 2025 and was roughly 62% of total assets; goodwill plus intangibles is 87.2% of assets, leaving tangible equity of approximately negative $12.0B. Twenty-five reporting units range from $17.5M to $3,371.9M in goodwill, and the FY2025 annual test was conducted qualitatively for all of them. The 10-K's own trigger list includes market-capitalization declines. Qualitative-only testing is permitted and normal where headroom is wide, and the units are individually profitable and growing, so ample headroom is the most likely explanation. But the recently acquired units (Transact, Procare, CentralReach, Subsplash) are conceded to carry "higher inherent risk," and the Q4 test is a dated event. An impairment would be non-cash — it would not touch operating cash flow, adjusted FCF, adjusted EBITDA or adjusted DEPS — but it would hit GAAP earnings, equity and the debt-to-capital ratio, and would likely be read as validation of the AI bear case.

**Government end-market exposure.** Deltek's non-recurring line appears to remain under pressure, consistent with flat consolidated non-recurring software revenue. Frontline, CentralReach and Transact carry district, reimbursement and enrollment budget risk respectively.

**Neptune input costs.** Water-meter cost pressure is operational and hits a segment with almost no amortization to blame.

**Reported dilution mechanics.** H1 2026 equity grants roughly doubled in share count at trough prices (0.845M RSUs at $352.88 versus 0.403M at $601.02; 0.544M options versus 0.271M), with stock compensation expense rising to $108.2M from $82.7M. Separately, 2.533M awards were excluded as antidilutive in Q2 (versus 0.705M a year earlier) and would re-enter the diluted count on a price recovery. Performance RSUs granted in H1 total 0.157M at target with a maximum 200% payout, so the maximum incremental issuance above target is 0.157M shares, about 0.16% of the 98.9M outstanding — immaterial to capitalization but a partial offset to the buyback if the stock recovers.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.4% against 28.9% one-year realized is fair, and the 3rd-percentile IV reading within the current chain plus a flat term structure (31.4% at one month, 32.0% at three, 31.4% at six) indicates the market is pricing no dated binary. The nearest expiry implies a ±5.0% move. Put/call open interest of 0.22 is heavily call-tilted, while the one-month put/call volume ratio of 1.60 and the source's characterization of the 4.1% one-month skew as elevated put demand point to short-dated hedging around the August 21 expiry; by the same sign convention, the three- and six-month readings (−2.0% and −3.4%) reverse that tilt further out. Short interest of 6.9% of float and 4.8 days to cover is moderate and uncrowded. Read together: no informed positioning is signaling a near-term dislocation, and optionality is cheap in absolute terms for anyone wanting to own the Q3 print or the Indicor close through options rather than equity. The ATM IV comparison to the prior snapshot (509.9% → 31.4%) reflects a data artifact in the earlier reading, not a volatility collapse, and I place no weight on it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $525 (+30.5%)

Free cash flow per share compounds from roughly $27 toward $31–32 by FY2027 as organic growth holds at 6%, payments attach scales, and roughly $1.1B of Indicor proceeds plus H2 free cash flow restore revolver capacity above $2B. Management continues retiring stock with the remaining $2,775.9M authorization (7.0% of market cap, and explicitly excluded from guidance, so it is upside to guided EPS), and resumes M&A at prices reflecting the sector's derating rather than 2021 multiples. Q3 guidance of $5.75–$5.80 is delivered, FY2026 adjusted DEPS lands at or above $22.30, and AI features convert into pricing rather than seat erosion — DAT, ConstructConnect and the "AI-enabled" relabeling become a monetization story with disclosed evidence. On that record the multiple recovers toward 21x next-year earnings, or roughly 19x FCF per share, giving $520–530. The CEO's $4.5M open-market purchase and the 8.99M shares retired at $358.79 are the corporate and personal expressions of this case.

### Bear Case — $305 (−24.2%)

Seat-based renewals begin to reflect AI-driven headcount compression at Roper's professional-services customers. Organic growth slides from 4.9% toward 2–3%, non-recurring software revenue stays flat or declines, and adjusted EBITDA margin keeps giving back 100–150bps a year on payments mix and Neptune input costs. Interest expense at $450–460M and rising, plus a used revolver, leaves the buyback dependent on Indicor proceeds that slip past year-end on regulatory review. The Q4 2026 goodwill test forces a quantitative assessment and a write-down at one of the 2024–25 units, confirming that the acquisition prices behind an 87%-intangible balance sheet were set in a different growth regime. Adjusted EPS growth without buyback support falls to low single digits, and the market applies 13x to roughly $23 of earnings — $300, close to the 52-week low of $305.96.

### Base Case — $435 (+8.1%)

Organic growth holds in the 5–6% band with H2 helped by the Frontline renewal cycle, margin compression moderates as the 2025 acquisition amortization annualizes, and FY2026 adjusted DEPS lands near the $22.225 midpoint with FY2027 approximating the $24.28 consensus. Indicor closes in H2, restoring capacity; buyback continues at a slower pace once the revolver is repaid. The multiple holds around 18x forward earnings and roughly 16x FCF per share. Total return of approximately 9% including the dividend.

Probability weighting of 50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear gives roughly +5.5% price return plus 0.90% dividend — approximately fair, which is what a HOLD means.

## Conclusion

Roper remains a high-quality cash machine: 30.8% FCF margin, 1.3% capital intensity, 67% recurring and reoccurring revenue, no customer above 10%, and a management team that bought back 8.99M shares at $358.79 — 11% below today's price — while the CEO added $4.5M of personal capital on the open market. The company's own proxy shows the five-year problem was multiple compression, not execution: cumulative TSR of 106 against a peer group's 258 while adjusted free cash flow rose 54%.

What holds me at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE is arithmetic, not doubt about the franchise. The stock is 21.8% higher than at my last review while consensus estimates rose 1.2–1.7%, so the gap to a defensible $435 base case has narrowed to about 8%, and the two most recent published sell-side targets imply 3–4%. The quality of the reported growth deteriorated in the same period: adjusted net earnings grew 2.7% with margin down 130bps, and three-quarters of the +10% EPS print came from a share count reduction that was two-thirds financed on the revolver, leaving roughly $643M of undrawn capacity and net leverage half a turn higher. Net accretion from that program is closer to +4.8% than the +7.9% the share count implies. And the central question — whether seat-priced vertical software in professions facing AI-driven headcount compression holds its units — is now written into Roper's own risk factors and cannot be settled from outside.

A 6.8% free cash flow yield plus 5% organic growth is a fair proposition, and existing holders are compensated for waiting. New capital has better entry points: $340–355 restores double-digit expected returns, and evidence of non-recurring software returning to growth alongside organic acceleration to 6% would justify paying up. Until one of those arrives, own it if you own it.
