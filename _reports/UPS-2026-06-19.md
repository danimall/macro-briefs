**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UPS is roughly halfway through a deliberate, self-imposed shrinkage of its business. Under CEO Carol Tomé, the company is intentionally cutting more than 50% of its largest customer's volume — Amazon, which accounted for 10.6% of 2025 revenue — on the bet that shedding low-margin e-commerce pieces, taking out ~$3B of annual network cost, and pivoting toward healthcare logistics, SMBs, and international will lift consolidated margins over time. The strategy has a coherent logic but has not yet worked at the consolidated level. Revenue fell to $88.7B in 2025 (−2.6% YoY) and declined again in Q1 2026 (−1.6%), while operating margin compressed from 13.1% in 2022 to 8.9% in 2025 and to 6.0% in Q1 2026. U.S. Domestic operating margin collapsed to 3.6% in Q1 2026 as cost removal lagged volume loss.

The central financial vulnerability is the dividend. In 2025, UPS paid $5.4B in dividends against $4.77B of free cash flow, a ~113% payout of FCF before $1.0B of buybacks. The buyback was suspended in Q1 2026 to preserve cash. The dividend gap is being funded with debt issuance ($4.2B of senior notes in 2025) and sale-leasebacks ($434M of gains, ~$0.30 of EPS), neither of which is sustainable. The roughly 6% yield is the equity's primary support, and the market is already pricing meaningful cut risk. A ~$1.2B Driver Choice separation charge lands in 2026, mostly in Q2.

We rate UPS AVOID with an overall score of 4.4. This is the decisive year for the strategy: the Amazon reduction completes by June 2026, the bulk of the restructuring charges hit, and the targeted cost savings must materialize. Until there is evidence that cost takeout is catching up to volume loss — visible in stabilizing consolidated operating margin and FCF coverage of the dividend returning above 1.0x — the risk/reward favors avoidance. The 12-month catalysts skew negative (dividend-cut risk, restructuring charges, falling margins) against a thesis whose payoff requires patience the recent track record has not rewarded.

## Company Overview

UPS is the world's largest integrated package delivery company, operating in over 200 countries through three segments:

- **U.S. Domestic Package** (~67% of revenue, $59.5B in 2025) — the largest but lowest-margin segment. GAAP operating margin was 6.6% in 2025.
- **International Package** (~21%, $18.6B) — the highest-margin segment at 15.5% in 2025, though margins compressed ~230bps on tariff/de minimis disruption to the China-U.S. lane.
- **Supply Chain Solutions** (~12%, $10.6B) — forwarding, healthcare logistics, and digital businesses (Roadie, Happy Returns, UPS Capital). The only segment with improving margins (10.1% in 2025, up 280bps).

The defining strategic fact is the deliberate Amazon exit. UPS is targeting a >50% reduction from 2024 Amazon volume levels by June 2026, trading volume for yield. The supporting moves are a pivot to healthcare logistics (now >$11B revenue, targeting #1 globally), SMBs (now >30% of U.S. volume), and international, alongside a "Network of the Future" automation and facility-consolidation program (93 buildings closed in 2025, 23 more in Q1 2026, 50+ identified).

UPS's moat is its integrated air-plus-ground network with global density and brand. That same network is a fixed-cost burden when volume contracts, which is the dynamic in play. Average revenue per piece rose 6.6% in 2025 and 7.7% in Q1 2026, partially offsetting volume declines of −7.0% and −7.7% respectively, but not enough to hold consolidated margins.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (multi-year decline):**
- 2022: $100.3B → 2023: $91.0B → 2024: $91.1B → 2025: $88.7B (−2.6%)
- Q1 2026: $21.2B (−1.6% YoY)

**Profitability erosion:**
- Net income: $11.5B (2022) → $6.7B (2023) → $5.8B (2024) → $5.6B (2025)
- Operating margin: 13.1% (2022) → 8.9% (2025) → 6.0% (Q1 2026)
- Q1 2026 EPS fell 27% YoY to $1.02 GAAP; U.S. Domestic operating profit fell 47% to $515M.

The strategy's premise is "revenue quality over volume." Yield gains are real, but they have not arrested margin compression because cost takeout is lagging volume loss and one-time transition costs (Ground Saver insourcing then USPS handoff, MD-11 retirement, weekend Europe delivery) are dragging.

**Cash flow and the dividend — the central concern:**
- 2025 operating cash flow: $8.45B (down from $10.1B in 2024); FCF: $4.77B.
- 2025 dividends paid: $5.4B, before $1.0B of buybacks. Total cash returned ($6.4B) far exceeded FCF.
- GAAP payout ratio ~100% ($6.56 declared per share vs. $6.56 EPS in 2025).
- Q1 2026: dividends $1.35B vs. FCF ~$1.2B (OCF $2.2B less ~$1.0B capex) — again roughly a full payout.
- Buybacks suspended in Q1 2026 (zero vs. $1.0B in Q1 2025).

The mechanism matters: dividends > FCF → the shortfall is plugged by debt issuance and asset sales → debt rises and the balance sheet gradually weakens → the dividend itself becomes the marginal funding decision. The $434M of 2025 sale-leaseback gains and a $109M tax valuation allowance reversal flatter reported earnings; on a recurring basis the coverage is thinner than the headline payout ratio suggests.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt rose to $24.1B (2025) from $21.3B (2024); equity is $16.2B (reported D/E ~4.5x). Altman Z of 2.07 sits in the grey zone. Pension/postretirement obligations of $6.7B require ~$1.3B in annual contributions, an ongoing cash drain. Liquidity is adequate ($5.8B cash, $3.0B undrawn revolvers, clean commercial paper). The FCF-to-dividend gap is the key structural vulnerability, not near-term liquidity.

**Earnings quality:** UPS beat Q1 2026 on EPS ($1.07 adjusted vs. ~$1.03 expected) but only reaffirmed — did not raise — FY2026 guidance (~$89.7B revenue, ~9.6% margin), and the stock fell ~4% on the print because investors wanted a raise. The beat masks a 27% YoY profit decline and the U.S. Domestic margin collapse. Non-GAAP add-backs ($794M to operating expenses in 2025) widen the gap between GAAP and adjusted figures.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term consolidated growth story. Revenue has declined for three of the last four years and is guided to fall again in 2026. The growth thesis is entirely about mix and timing: if UPS can complete the Amazon exit, remove the stranded network cost, and let healthcare/SMB/international yield carry the average, consolidated margins should eventually expand even on a smaller revenue base. SCS margin improvement (+280bps to 10.1%) and healthcare logistics surpassing $11B are the early supporting data points, alongside the Frigo-Trans and Andlauer Healthcare acquisitions and new Asian air hubs (Philippines late-2026, Hong Kong 2028).

This is a margin-recovery thesis dressed as growth, and the recovery has repeatedly slipped. Cost takeout has lagged volume loss in every reporting period observed, and 2026 layers ~$1.2B of Driver Choice separation charges onto the transition. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (current-quarter EPS +6.1% over 90 days, next-year +0.5%), but the magnitude is far below the threshold that would signal the market is systematically underestimating a turn — these are routine, low-single-digit upticks, not a step-change. Current-year EPS estimates were flat to slightly negative over 30 days (−0.1%).

Growth prospects score 4.0. The business is shrinking, the recovery is timing-dependent and has consistently disappointed, and the only segment with genuine momentum (SCS) is too small to offset the U.S. Domestic decline near-term.

## Valuation Assessment

UPS trades at ~16.0x trailing/forward EPS ($6.56 EPS, $104.86 price) and ~13.1x next-year consensus ($8.00). The dividend yield is ~6.2%. Against the Air Freight & Logistics peer median P/E of 26.9x, UPS looks cheap, and screens close to FedEx (18.0x).

The discount is warranted, not a mispricing. The peer median is inflated by asset-light forwarders and 3PLs (CHRW 38.7x, GXO 43.5x, EXPD 26.9x) that are not comparable to UPS's capital-heavy integrated network; FedEx at 18.0x is the only true comparable, and UPS trades at a deserved discount to it given the steeper margin decline and the dividend question. The next-year P/E of ~13.1x rests on $8.00 EPS that assumes the margin recovery materializes — the same recovery that has slipped repeatedly. Using more conservative 2026 numbers (consensus ~$7.13 current-year EPS, before the $1.2B Driver Choice charge fully resolves in adjusted terms), the multiple is closer to ~14.7x on numbers that may prove optimistic.

The yield provides apparent support, but a yield this elevated relative to the peer median of 1.1% is the market pricing cut risk, not offering a free coupon. If the dividend is cut to restore FCF coverage, the yield support evaporates and the multiple has no obvious floor above the low-$80s 52-week low. A low P/E here reflects structural pressure and a contested payout, the profile of a value trap rather than asymmetric upside.

Analyst targets span $75 (low) to $135 (high), with a $115 median. The wide dispersion captures the two-sided nature of the strategy. We do not treat the median as a floor here: UPS is a declining-earnings story where the cheap multiple reflects real risk, not an inflection stock with rising estimates and a cheap multiple. Valuation scores 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

UPS competes with FedEx, DHL, USPS, regional carriers, freight forwarders, and, most consequentially, customers who can insource, with Amazon the defining example. Amazon built its own delivery network, became UPS's largest customer and a competitor simultaneously, and UPS is now retreating from that relationship rather than defending it on price. The strategic choice is defensible, but it confirms that UPS's pricing power over its single largest account had eroded to the point where the volume was no longer worth carrying.

The trajectory of the competitive position is the key issue. Five-year cumulative TSR of 74.7 against a peer group at 136.9 shows the market has been skeptical of the strategy, and the underperformance is severe. The integrated network moat is real and durable, but it is being deliberately under-utilized during the transition, and the fixed-cost base makes the moat a liability while volume falls. The SCS healthcare pivot is a genuine, improving niche, but it is small relative to the deteriorating U.S. Domestic segment. Net, the competitive trajectory is flat-to-declining: the company is choosing a smaller, theoretically higher-quality footprint, but the market has not yet seen evidence the trade pays off. Competitive position scores 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Dividend sustainability (most acute).** FCF no longer covers the dividend. The payout is being funded with debt and asset sales while earnings fall. A cut would remove the equity's main support. The buyback pause already signals cash preservation.

2. **Amazon de-risking execution.** Cutting >50% of the largest customer's volume requires removing stranded network cost as fast as revenue is shed. This has lagged in every period so far. ~1M more pieces/day of Amazon volume exit by end-2026.

3. **Restructuring charge load.** The ~$1.2B Driver Choice separation program (mostly Q2 2026) is a large, novel cost event, layered on accelerated depreciation and facility-closure charges running through 2027.

4. **Tariffs / trade policy.** De minimis changes and tariffs crushed the China-U.S. lane and pressured International margins (−230bps). The Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs (Feb 20, 2026), and UPS is filing for ~$500M in refunds as a customs intermediary — currently unrecognized, a potential positive cash wildcard but uncertain.

5. **Goodwill impairment.** The Global Freight Forwarding ($877M goodwill) and Healthcare Logistics & Distribution ($738M) units have only limited excess of fair value over carrying value; an interim test was already required for GFF in Q4 2025. A modest discount-rate increase or cash-flow cut could trigger impairment.

6. **Operational shock — Louisville MD-11 crash.** The fatal Nov. 4, 2025 cargo crash drove the MD-11 fleet retirement ($182M charge), created capacity constraints that raised Q1 2026 third-party lease/charter costs, and carries NTSB design-inquiry (May 2026 hearings) and litigation tail risk. Management calls impacts immaterial.

7. **Labor.** Teamsters master agreement runs to July 2028 (no near-term cliff), but the pilots' IPA contract became amendable Sept. 1, 2025 and is in active negotiation. Embedded wage increases (+$567M in 2025) are structural.

These risks are largely two-sided and material relative to the current valuation, which is why risk factors score 4.5 rather than lower: the dividend and execution risks are partly priced (the elevated yield, the depressed multiple), but the concentration of dated negative catalysts in 2026 keeps the score below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 28.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 30.8%, a fair-to-slightly-cheap reading (spread −2.6%, IV percentile 7%). Put/call skew is balanced (−0.2%), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.46 is not signaling unusual bearish positioning. The term structure is in normal contango (28.2% near vs. 33.4% at six months), reflecting complacency rather than acute event fear. ATM IV fell from 34.4% to 28.2% over the prior three weeks as the stock drifted down 2.2%, releasing uncertainty rather than building it. The options market is not confirming an imminent bearish catalyst; it reads the situation as a slow grind rather than a binary event, consistent with the dividend question being a 2026-long overhang rather than a single dated trigger. Short interest is low at 3.3% of float (3.9 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Amazon exit completes by June 2026 and the stranded cost finally comes out, lifting consolidated margin back toward the ~9.6% FY2026 guide and beyond. The $3B annual cost program lands, SCS healthcare compounds toward #1 global share, yield gains (revenue per piece +7.7%) carry the average on a smaller, higher-quality base, and the ~$500M tariff refund provides a cash bonus. The dividend holds, the yield re-rates as coverage is restored, and the stock works back toward the $135 high target. This is the "post-Amazon world" thesis.

**Bear case.** Cost takeout continues to lag volume loss, as it has in every recent period. Consolidated margin stays below 7%, the $1.2B Driver Choice charge and ongoing restructuring depress 2026 FCF further, and the dividend is cut to restore coverage, removing the equity's primary support. Goodwill impairment hits SCS, the Louisville litigation tail surprises, and the stock retraces toward the $75 low target and the low-$80s 52-week low. Revenue keeps shrinking with no margin payoff to show for it.

The pivotal variable is timing of cost takeout versus volume loss, and the track record on that question is poor. The bear case does not require anything to go wrong beyond a continuation of the current trend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. **Dividend coverage restored.** Trailing-twelve-month FCF covering the declared dividend above 1.0x with the buyback resumed, demonstrating the payout is self-funded rather than debt-financed. This is the single most important condition.
2. **Consolidated operating margin stabilizing and inflecting.** Two consecutive quarters of U.S. Domestic operating margin recovering above ~5% and consolidated margin holding at or above the ~9.6% FY guide, evidence that cost takeout has finally caught up to volume loss.
3. **Valuation reset with a real catalyst.** Either the stock derates toward the high-$70s/low-$80s (where the ~7%+ yield and trough multiple offer genuine support even with a partial dividend cut priced), or a clean post-Amazon margin print confirms the turn at a reasonable multiple.

**Timeline:** The headwinds persist through at least the back half of 2026. The cost-savings payoff, if it comes, should be visible in H2 2026 to H1 2027 results. The next natural reassessment is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which will show the Driver Choice charge, the completed Amazon volume reduction, and whether margins have begun to inflect. We would revisit the rating then.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The most plausible dated negative catalyst is a dividend cut announcement, which most often accompanies a Q4/full-year capital-allocation review (typically January–February) rather than a fixed mid-year date, plus the Q2 2026 earnings print (late July). Neither is a clean, single-day binary; the dividend question is a slow-developing 2026 overhang. There is no precise dated trigger that reliably moves the stock materially lower within a defined window.

**Timing window:** Diffuse. The thesis resolves over the H2 2026 to H1 2027 period as margin recovery either materializes or does not. A short would carry a ~6% annual dividend cost against the borrow, plus margin, a meaningful negative carry against a thesis that may take 6–12 months to play out.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** This is an uncrowded short with room, but the low short interest also indicates informed investors are not positioned bearishly. Squeeze risk is modest, but the dividend carry is the dominant friction.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options are not confirming a bearish thesis, and positioning is neutral despite the weak fundamentals, which is a warning sign for shorts. The market is pricing a slow grind, not an imminent decline.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Downside to the $75 analyst low (~−28% from $104.86) on a dividend cut; upside risk includes a successful margin inflection or the ~$500M tariff refund driving a re-rate toward $135 (~+29%). With the dividend carry, balanced options positioning, and no clean dated catalyst, the reward:risk on an active short is roughly 1:1, below the 2:1 bar.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental case is real, but the absence of a clean dated catalyst, the heavy dividend carry, and balanced options positioning make this an avoid-to-own situation rather than an active short. The thesis is a shrinking company with a stretched payout, not an imminent collapse.

## Conclusion

UPS is a deliberately shrinking business midway through a strategy whose payoff is timing-dependent and has repeatedly slipped. Revenue has fallen for three of four years, consolidated operating margin compressed to 6.0% in Q1 2026, and U.S. Domestic margin collapsed to 3.6%. The dividend, the equity's main support, is being funded above free cash flow with debt and asset sales, the buyback is paused, and a ~$1.2B restructuring charge hits in 2026. The cheap headline multiple reflects this structural pressure rather than a mispricing; the next-year P/E of ~13.1x rests on a margin recovery that the recent track record does not yet justify.

The current data does not support continuing a hold: there is no specific 12-month growth catalyst, the dated catalysts skew negative (restructuring charges, dividend-cut risk, ongoing margin compression), there is no insider buying despite a derated stock and 6% yield, and executive churn has accelerated. We rate UPS AVOID. We would revisit after Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which will reveal whether the completed Amazon exit and cost program have finally begun to inflect margins, or whether the dividend coverage gap forces a payout decision.
