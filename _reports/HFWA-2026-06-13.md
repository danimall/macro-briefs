## Executive Summary

Heritage Financial is a $1.2B-market-cap community bank headquartered in Olympia, Washington, operating 65 branches along the I-5 corridor and Puget Sound following the January 2026 acquisition of Olympic Bancorp (Kitsap Bank). The investment story is a post-trough earnings recovery layered on top of an unusually clean acquisition. Net interest margin expanded to 3.96% in Q1 2026 from 3.44% a year earlier, net income rose 36% year-over-year to $18.9M, and the acquired Olympic book improved Heritage's combined credit and funding profile simultaneously: zero acquired nonaccruals, lower deposit cost, lower criticized-loan ratio. The franchise asset is a low-cost, granular deposit base, with noninterest-bearing deposits at 28.5% of total and a 1.71% cost of interest-bearing deposits, both strengthened by the deal.

The bear-case offset is that the stock has already priced much of this. HFWA is up 24% over twelve months and trades at $29.04, effectively at its 52-week high of $29.12 and at a 14.2x trailing P/E versus a peer median near 12.8x. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised down across every horizon over the past 90 days (current quarter -5.4%, current year -3.0%, next year -3.0%), the Q1 print absorbed elevated integration costs, and the bank's non-owner-occupied CRE concentration has crossed the 300% regulatory guidance line to 301%. The Chief Credit Officer has explicitly flagged rising special-mention and substandard balances in the C&I book tied to tariff, labor, and supply-chain stress, a deterioration currently masked at the aggregate level by the pristine acquired book.

The setup is a credible re-rating thesis with a defined catalyst, the late-September 2026 system conversion that unlocks a Q4 expense step-down to roughly $55M per quarter, but at a price that no longer offers asymmetric upside. The base case sits within a few percent of current levels, the consensus target band ($30-32) implies 3-10% upside, and the falling estimate trend cuts against the recovery narrative in the near term. We rate HFWA a HOLD with a growth bias and would become more constructive on a pullback toward tangible book or on evidence the C&I credit drift is contained.

## Company Overview

Heritage Bank is a relationship-driven commercial community bank serving small- and medium-sized businesses and their owners across Washington, the greater Portland/Eugene area of Oregon, and Boise, Idaho. The company reports a single segment, commercial banking, and earns the bulk of its income from net interest income. As of March 31, 2026, total assets stood at $8.50B (up 22% from $6.97B at year-end 2025), loans receivable at $5.72B, total deposits at $7.25B, and stockholders' equity at $1.12B. Certain branches operate under the Whidbey Island Bank and Kitsap Bank names.

The defining franchise characteristic is deposit quality. Brokered deposits are 0.67% of the total. In a competitive deposit market where management has acknowledged raising its top CD rate to defend "excess dollars," this low-beta funding base is the core of the bank's economics and is the principal reason NIM is expanding rather than compressing. The Olympic/Kitsap acquisition reinforced this advantage rather than diluting it, which is uncommon in community-bank M&A.

The footprint concentrates Heritage geographically in the Pacific Northwest, which is both a coherent operating market and a source of single-sub-economy exposure to regional real estate values and employment.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and margin.** Q1 2026 net income was $18.9M, or $0.48 diluted EPS, up 36% from $13.9M ($0.40) a year earlier. The diluted EPS of $0.48 came in slightly above the $0.46 consensus estimate (a 3.4% surprise), even as it absorbed $5.2M of merger and integration costs and $2.1M of intangible amortization. NIM expanded to 3.96% from 3.72% in Q4 2025 and 3.44% in Q1 2025. Stripping out purchase-accounting accretion and a one-time 5bp nonaccrual interest recovery, core NIM was approximately 3.87%, still a genuine improvement. NII rose 28.9% year-over-year to $69.2M. Loan yield was 5.73% against a falling 1.71% cost of interest-bearing deposits. The balance sheet is asset-sensitive (+200bp shock = +2.2% NII; -200bp = -3.7% NII), a modest headwind into a year where the rate path skews toward cuts or, at minimum, no further hikes.

**The earnings cycle.** Heritage came through a meaningful trough. Diluted EPS ran $2.73 (2021), $2.31 (2022), $1.75 (2023), $1.24 (2024 trough), $1.96 (2025), with the trough driven by NIM compression during the rate-hike cycle. The 2025-2026 recovery is a real recovery in NIM and net income, supported by the accretive deal. This is the legitimate bull anchor: the trailing 14.2x P/E understates normalized earnings power if NIM reaches the guided 4.0% and the expense base normalizes.

**Expense trajectory and the catalyst.** Q1 noninterest expense was $56.6M (+36.7% YoY). Management guides $64-65M per quarter in Q2 and Q3 (carrying remaining merger costs), then a step-down to $56-57M in Q4 post-conversion, with an ex-merger run-rate of roughly $55M. The system conversion is scheduled for late September 2026 and is the single most important earnings catalyst. Once integration costs roll off, run-rate EPS should improve materially, all else equal. The mechanism is direct: integration spending suppresses 2026 earnings, the conversion ends the bulk of it, and Q4 becomes the first clean quarter.

**Credit quality.** Headline metrics are strong. Nonaccrual loans are $15.0M, just 0.26% of loans (improved from 0.44% at year-end), net charge-offs are 0.04% annualized, and NPAs are 0.19% of total assets. The ACL/loans ratio fell to 1.06% from 1.10%, reflecting the high quality of the acquired book, which produced a $1.03M provision reversal in the quarter.

**Capital and liquidity.** Total capital ratio is 13.5%, CET1 12.2%, leverage 10.3%, comfortably well-capitalized. The tangible common equity ratio declined to 9.6% from 10.1% on merger goodwill and intangibles, which is expected and still healthy. Available liquidity is $3.2B; uninsured deposits fell to 39.1% from 41.1%. Goodwill is $279M and the core deposit intangible $50.3M (10-year amortization). AOCI carries a -$33.0M loss and held-to-maturity securities hold ~$50.8M of unrecognized losses (fair value $617M vs $668M amortized cost), a reminder of unrealized rate risk, but not a forced-sale liquidity threat.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend held at $0.24/share (~$8.3M/quarter, ~49% payout of Q1 EPS), supporting a 3.3% yield. Roughly 797k shares remain on the buyback authorization, with management signaling potential Q2 2026 repurchases.

## Growth Analysis

The growth path has two components. The mechanical component is the integration: the Olympic deal added $1.55B in assets, ~$945-954M in loans, and $1.39B in deposits, and the Q4 expense normalization converts integration drag into run-rate earnings. The organic component is loan demand, which is recovering: the commercial pipeline grew to $631M (up 35% quarter-over-quarter and up from $460M a year ago), which management attributes partly to post-tax-legislation activity. Management guides mid-single-digit annualized loan growth over the next couple of quarters. Q1 organic loan growth excluding Olympic was a modest +$20M as 2025 construction payoffs worked through, but prepayment pressure that dogged Heritage is abating.

The tension is that the forward earnings setup is being marked down even as the structural story improves. Consensus EPS revisions are negative across every horizon: current quarter -8.7% (30-day) and -5.4% (90-day), next quarter -7.3%/-6.7%, current year -4.7%/-3.0%, next year -4.1%/-3.0%. The 30-day moves are larger than the 90-day moves on the near-term quarters, meaning the downward revision is accelerating, not stabilizing. This is the opposite of the rising-estimate trend that would justify a more aggressive rating. The most likely reconciliation is that analysts are pulling forward the recognition of elevated 2026 integration expense and the C&I credit drift while the underlying NIM and conversion thesis remains intact for 2027. Growth is real but front-loaded with cost drag and currently being de-rated by the sell side.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.04, HFWA trades at 14.2x trailing EPS of $1.96 and roughly 12.2x the current-year consensus of $2.39, falling to ~10.4x on next-year's $2.79. Against the regional-bank peer median P/E of 12.8x, Heritage trades at a modest premium on trailing earnings and broadly in line on forward. The peer set (PFBC 9.4x, TMP 7.8x, WABC 12.8x, SBSI 14.4x, CFFN 13.6x) shows wide dispersion, and Heritage's dividend yield of 3.3% sits just below the 3.4% peer median.

The cycle-adjusted case is the strongest argument for the stock. On normalized post-conversion earnings power (NIM at the guided 4.0%, an expense run-rate near $55M, and a full year of Olympic accretion), the forward multiple compresses toward 10-11x, which is reasonable for a clean PNW deposit franchise. The forward P/E sits below the implied earnings growth rate into 2027.

What caps the valuation score is the combination of three facts that argue against treating the analyst target as a floor. First, the stock is at the top of its 52-week range ($21.32-$29.12), with the current price $0.08 below the high, so there is no compressed-multiple or pullback discount to exploit. Second, estimates are falling, not rising; the framework that would let us discount near-term consensus applies to rising-estimate names, and HFWA's revisions run the wrong way at -3% to -9%. Third, the consensus target band of $30-32 implies only 3-10% upside, and analyst targets are not reliable floors when a stock has already reached the upper end of its range against deteriorating near-term estimates. A bank trading at a peer-premium trailing multiple, at its high, with falling estimates does not offer the asymmetric mispricing required for a high valuation score. The forward earnings power is genuine but largely recognized.

## Competitive Landscape

Heritage's competitive position is stable-to-improving. The low-cost deposit base is a durable structural advantage versus rate-chasing competitors and credit unions; blended deposit cost held near 1.68-1.71% even as deposit competition for excess dollars intensified. The Olympic deal added Puget Sound scale (16 Kitsap branches) and improved both funding cost and credit quality. Management is opportunistically recruiting commercial banking teams amid disruption across other PNW banks, though headcount is roughly flat as long-tenured bankers retire.

The trajectory argument is supported by NIM expansion outpacing peers and a recovering commercial pipeline. The offset is that five-year total shareholder return has lagged the peer group (index 122.9 vs 160.4), reflecting the depth of the 2023-2024 earnings trough. The franchise is sound and gaining modest share through M&A and team hires, but it is a price-taker on rates and exposed to a single regional economy, which limits how far the competitive score can rise.

## Risk Assessment

**C&I credit drift — the most important forward risk.** The Chief Credit Officer twice volunteered that special-mention and substandard loans have risen proportionately in the C&I book year-over-year, tied to economic uncertainty, tariffs, higher labor costs, and supply-chain issues. It is not concentrated in one industry. Aggregate criticized loans look stable at 3.9% only because the clean acquired Olympic book masks the underlying C&I drift. The pristine headline metrics (NPAs 0.26%, NCOs 0.04%) currently obscure a management-acknowledged emerging deterioration. If the broader consumer and small-business environment softens in the second half, this is where it surfaces first.

**CRE concentration above guidance.** Non-owner-occupied CRE to total capital rose to 301%, just above the 300% regulatory guidance threshold, mechanically driven by fair-value capital marks from the merger rather than new lending. Total CRE is ~64% of loans; office is the largest collateral type at 18.4% ($675.7M, of which 46% is lower-risk owner-occupied). Crossing this line can attract heightened regulatory scrutiny and constrain future CRE growth and M&A until it normalizes.

**Integration execution.** The core system conversion is not until late September 2026, so the company carries elevated expenses and integration risk (customer/employee retention) through Q3, with cost-save realization back-end loaded to Q4. Any slippage in the conversion timeline pushes the catalyst out and extends the earnings drag.

**Rate and securities risk.** The bank is asset-sensitive, so a faster-than-expected easing cycle pressures NII (-3.7% on a -200bp shock). HTM securities carry ~$50.8M of unrecognized losses and AOCI is -$33.0M, manageable absent forced sales.

**Geographic concentration** in WA/OR/ID ties the bank to a single regional economy.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 32.5% against a 29.3% one-year realized vol, modestly rich, in the 10th percentile within the current chain, so the market is not pricing acute near-term event risk. The structure is in contango (1-week 32.5% rising to 3-month 44.9%), a normal-to-complacent shape. The notable feature is an elevated near-term put/call skew of 125%, indicating some demand for downside protection into the next several weeks, consistent with the falling-estimate backdrop and the CRE/credit overhang. Short interest is 7.0% of float with 10.7 days to cover, moderate, not a squeeze setup. The options market and the fundamentals broadly agree: a defensible franchise with identifiable downside risks and no imminent binary catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The 2024 earnings trough ($1.24 EPS) is behind the company, and a clean recovery is underway. NIM expanded to 3.96% and management guides 4.0% by year-end on loan repricing and balance-sheet leverage. The Olympic deal was accretive on all three vectors (funding cost, credit quality, and scale), a rare community-bank outcome. The late-September conversion ends the bulk of integration spending, stepping expenses down from $64-65M to ~$55M in Q4 and re-rating run-rate EPS. On normalized earnings power, the stock trades near 10-11x, cheap for a low-cost deposit franchise. A recovering commercial pipeline ($631M, +35% q/q) supports mid-single-digit loan growth, and a 3.3% dividend plus buyback capacity provides a floor. If NIM hits 4% and expenses normalize as guided, 2027 EPS approaching $2.80+ supports a re-rating toward the mid-$30s.

**Bear case.** The stock is at its 52-week high after a 24% run, estimates are falling 3-9% across all horizons with the near-term cuts accelerating, and a quiet C&I credit deterioration that the acquired book is currently hiding sits beneath the headline metrics. CRE concentration has breached the 300% regulatory line. If the second-half environment softens, C&I criticized balances migrate to nonaccrual, provisioning rises, and the NIM-to-4% glide path stalls just as integration costs peak in Q2-Q3. The asset-sensitive balance sheet loses NII if the Fed cuts. In that scenario, the stock de-rates toward tangible book in the low-$20s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (late July) and the late-September conversion milestone.**

The score sits at 6.15, just below the threshold that would compel a BUY, and the gap to BUY is concrete rather than rhetorical. Three specific, falsifiable conditions prevent a BUY at $29.04: (1) the stock is $0.08 below its 52-week high with no pullback discount, and the consensus target band of $30-32 implies only 3-10% upside, insufficient asymmetry; (2) consensus EPS estimates are being revised down across every horizon (current year -3.0%, next year -3.0%, current quarter -5.4% over 90 days with the 30-day cut accelerating to -8.7%), the inverse of the rising-estimate signal that would justify a long; and (3) the Chief Credit Officer's explicit acknowledgment of rising C&I special-mention and substandard balances, combined with CRE concentration breaching the 300% regulatory guidance line, are unpriced downside risks that the pristine headline credit metrics actively obscure. A genuine BUY requires bear-case downside contained near 15% and a catalyst inside 60 days; the conversion catalyst is ~3-4 months out, and the bear case (de-rate to tangible book, low-$20s) implies ~25%+ downside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) a pullback toward tangible book value (low-$20s) on no fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetric upside; (2) two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising consensus EPS estimates confirming the recovery is being recognized rather than marked down; (3) the late-September conversion completing on schedule with the Q4 expense step-down to ~$55M confirmed and C&I criticized balances stabilizing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) C&I special-mention/substandard balances migrating into nonaccrual, pushing NPAs above ~0.60% or net charge-offs above ~0.25% annualized; (2) the conversion slipping past Q4 2026, extending the integration cost drag; (3) NIM reversing below ~3.7% if the bank's asset-sensitivity bites on faster rate cuts, breaking the 4% guidance glide path.

## Conclusion

Heritage Financial is a soundly run Pacific Northwest community bank executing a genuinely accretive acquisition through a clean internal CEO succession, with a real NIM-driven earnings recovery and a defined late-September catalyst that should normalize expenses and lift run-rate EPS. The franchise asset, a low-cost, granular, low-beta deposit base, is durable and was strengthened by the Olympic deal. None of that is in dispute.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is price and timing, not business quality. The stock has run 24% to the top of its range, trades at a peer-premium trailing multiple, and faces a falling-estimate trend that contradicts the near-term recovery narrative, while two unpriced risks, the quiet C&I credit drift and the breached CRE concentration line, sit beneath pristine headline metrics. The asymmetric upside required to underwrite a long has largely been recognized by the market. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward tangible book or on evidence that estimate momentum has turned and the C&I deterioration is contained. For existing holders, the 3.3% yield, solid capital, and the Q4 expense catalyst justify continuing to hold through the conversion.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report's premise that Q1 2026 was a consensus "miss" is incorrect — actual diluted EPS of $0.48 beat the $0.46 consensus by 3.4% (the ~$0.57 figure was a stale/wrong estimate). The "optical miss masks core strength" framing in the executive summary, growth, valuation, and conclusion sections rests on a factual error and may warrant revisiting.
