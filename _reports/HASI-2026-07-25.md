**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HASI is an internally-managed specialty investor in energy-transition real assets, managing ~$16.4B in Managed Assets across behind-the-meter distributed solar/storage/efficiency (~50% of the on-balance-sheet Portfolio), grid-connected utility-scale wind and solar (~34%), and fuels/transport/nature projects (~16%). The business earns money four ways: net investment income on its ~$7.6B balance-sheet Portfolio (~9.2% yield, ~16-year weighted asset life), equity-method income from project JVs structured with preferred-return "flip" mechanics, gain-on-sale from securitizations, and asset-management/broker-dealer fees. The company is not a bank or a traditional REIT; standard bankruptcy and quality screens do not apply, which is why financial-health scoring tools return errors on it.

The central analytical fact is that GAAP earnings mislead here. Q1 2026 printed a GAAP net loss of $(0.57) per share, driven by a $(79.3)M equity-method loss of which ~$97M was a single non-cash HLBV (Hypothetical Liquidation at Book Value) timing artifact that management states reverses in a later period. Underneath, the same quarter delivered record results: Adjusted EPS of $0.77 (+20% YoY, ~11% consensus beat) and Adjusted ROE of 15.7%, the highest ever. Adjusted EPS has compounded steadily from $1.88 (2021) to $2.70 (2025), roughly 10% annually. The dividend, comp plan, and cash generation are all keyed to these adjusted figures rather than GAAP.

We rate HASI a HOLD (income orientation). The durable return here is the ~4.4% dividend, well-covered by ~$939M of Cash Available for Reinvestment, plus mid-single-digit adjusted-earnings growth aided by the KKR CarbonCount JV, which is lifting ROE through fee income without proportional balance-sheet expansion. What caps the equity: forward EPS estimates are broadly falling (current-quarter -3.5%, next-quarter -5.2% over 90 days), the cost of debt is rising (5.7% to 6.1% YoY) as a $600M 3.375% note reprices to 6%+ in June 2026, federal clean-energy tax-credit policy is the dominant and binary sector risk, and the first visible credit crack has appeared. The stock at ~$89x trailing GAAP P/E is meaningless; on ~$2.99 current-year Adjusted EPS it trades at ~12.8x, a fair-to-slightly-cheap multiple for a ~10% grower with elevated policy risk.

## Company Overview

HASI (formerly Hannon Armstrong, renamed HA Sustainable Infrastructure Capital in July 2024) provides capital to decarbonization projects. Its ~$16.4B of Managed Assets comprise the ~$7.6B on-balance-sheet Portfolio, ~$7.3B in off-balance-sheet securitization trusts, and ~$1.5B in co-investment structures. The Portfolio spans 600+ assets averaging ~$12M each, sourced from a single origination pipeline of >$6.5B built on long-standing relationships with developers, utilities, and energy-service companies.

The moat is programmatic origination (recurring, structured deal flow that a balance-sheet lender without those relationships cannot replicate) combined with structuring expertise across preferred/flip equity, securitization, and green-bond issuance (its proprietary "CarbonCount" carbon-per-dollar metric even ratchets some borrowing margins). The company operates under a 1940 Act investment-company exemption, which constrains how it holds assets, and reports as a single segment with the CEO as chief operating decision-maker.

About ~$1.6B of the behind-the-meter book is mezzanine loans to bankruptcy-remote SPEs of residential-solar companies, the most credit-sensitive slice given documented installer distress industry-wide.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth.** GAAP annual revenue rose from $239.7M (2022) to $319.9M (2023), $383.6M (2024), and $400.5M (2025). Q1 2026 revenue grew 14.3% YoY to $124.2M, with interest/rental income +24%, gain-on-sale +22%, and fees +39%. The top line has compounded double-digits consistently.

**GAAP vs. economics.** HASI accounts for ~$4.25B of equity-method investments (49 positions) under HLBV, which allocates GAAP income based on shifts in each partner's claim on book value as tax attributes flow to tax-equity investors, not on cash. Q1 2026's $(73.7)M GAAP net loss was driven by an investee executing an investment-tax-credit sale agreement that temporarily boosted tax-equity investors' capital accounts, reducing HASI's HLBV allocation. Full-year GAAP net income has been positive: $148.8M (2023), $200.0M (2024), $184.5M (2025). The economically relevant figures — Adjusted EPS and Cash Available for Reinvestment — tell a cleaner story.

**Cash generation.** TTM Adjusted Cash from Operations plus Other Portfolio Collections was ~$1.16B, with ~$939M of Cash Available for Reinvestment after dividends. This liquidity engine covers the dividend comfortably, which GAAP EPS obscures.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets ~$8.2B; debt-to-equity 1.6x, within the 1.5–2.0x target and below the 2.5x board limit. Roughly 100% of debt is fixed-rate or hedged, a meaningful strength as rates stay higher-for-longer. Liquidity is ~$2.3B (unrestricted cash $124M, $1.8B undrawn revolver maturing April 2028, $250M delayed-draw, $125M CP capacity).

**Debt structure and cost of capital.** Funding relies on green senior unsecured notes (2026–2036 maturities, coupons 3.375% to 6.75%) plus $1.1B of junior subordinated notes (rating agencies treat 50% as equity, reducing common dilution). In Q1 2026 HASI issued $600M junior subs at 7.125% and $400M of 6.0% 2036 notes, and redeemed its 8.0% 2027 notes early at an $18M premium, the main driver of a $35M / 53% YoY jump in interest expense. Average cost of debt rose from 5.7% to 6.1% YoY. The near-term item to watch is the $600M 3.375% senior note maturing June 2026, which reprices into a ~6%+ environment. That compresses the spread between the ~9.2% portfolio yield and the rising cost of debt, the core economics of the business.

Financial health scores a 6.0: the hedged, fixed-rate posture, staggered maturities, and strong liquidity are genuine strengths, and cash generation supports the dividend. The offsets are the rising cost of capital, structural reliance on continuous debt/equity market access for a long-dated illiquid asset base, and the accounting opacity that makes standard resilience metrics inapplicable.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth vector is Adjusted EPS compounding at ~10% supported by the KKR CarbonCount Holdings 1 (CCH1) JV. HASI and KKR each committed $1.5B (investment period through December 2027), with each having funded ~$792M by Q1 2026. HASI earns 1% upfront origination fees plus 0.5–1.0% ongoing asset-management fees through its broker-dealer/asset-manager subsidiaries. This is the asset-light lever behind the record Adjusted ROE — it lets HASI originate beyond its balance sheet and build a fee stream, though as a 50% JV owner it eliminates half the fees in equity-method income. The pace of the remaining ~$700M each through the investment period is the growth metric to track.

The earnings track record supports moderate confidence: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, with beats of 10.6% (Q1 2026), 2.5%, and 14.0% and a single 6.3% miss (Q2 2025). Management delivers on the adjusted metrics it steers by.

The near-term drag is the estimate-revision picture. Current-year Adjusted EPS consensus of $2.99 is roughly flat over 90 days (+0.9%), but the near-term windows are deteriorating: current quarter -3.5% and next quarter -5.2% over 90 days, both continuing to erode over 30 days. Next-year consensus of $3.30 implies ~10% growth but has slipped modestly (-0.8% over 30 days). Earnings momentum is mildly negative in the windows that matter most for the next two to three quarters, which is the opposite of the accelerating-revision signal that would justify a growth-driven upgrade. Growth prospects score a 5.8: the multi-year compounding story and KKR lever are real, but the near-term revision velocity is negative and the growth is mid-single to low-double-digit.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing GAAP P/E of ~88.9x is not meaningful given the HLBV-driven GAAP volatility; ignore it. On the metric the business is actually run against, current-year Adjusted EPS of ~$2.99 implies a forward multiple of ~12.8x, and next-year $3.30 implies ~11.6x. That is a fair multiple for a company compounding adjusted earnings ~10% with a ~4.4% dividend, but it does not indicate mispricing.

Peer context is imperfect because HASI has no clean public comp; the specialty-finance basket (FFIN 18.1x, CNO 20.0x, IBOC 11.3x, MC 23.2x, RDN 7.9x; median 18.1x) is heterogeneous and dominated by banks and insurers with different risk profiles. HASI's forward adjusted multiple sits below the peer median, but that discount is defensible given the accounting opacity, dominant policy/tax-credit dependence, rising cost of capital, and off-balance-sheet equity-method exposure. The dividend yield exceeds the peer median 2.6% and offers absolute yield support.

Analyst targets (low $41, mean $49.3, median $50, high $57) sit meaningfully above the $38.37 price. On a stock with rising estimates I would treat consensus targets as floors, but here estimates are falling in the near term, so the targets are more likely to be revised toward price than the reverse. The valuation is a fair multiple on a fair grower with a specific, binary policy risk that could impair the pipeline. Valuation scores a 4.8. A "cheap"-looking multiple often marks a value trap without an independent catalyst, and that applies here: there is no near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple higher while policy risk overhangs and revisions drift lower.

## Competitive Landscape

HASI is a fairly unique public vehicle — a pure-play, internally-managed energy-transition capital provider with programmatic origination relationships that are hard to replicate. The KKR partnership is institutional validation of that origination engine and moves the company toward a hybrid balance-sheet plus asset-management model that scales ROE. Its structuring toolkit (preferred/flip equity, securitization, green bonds with the CarbonCount metric) is differentiated.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving at the platform level, but the industry variable dominates: the durability of IRA-era tax credits and tax-equity structures directly determines pipeline economics. Rising rates compress the yield-to-cost spread. The residential-solar segment faces well-documented installer distress. Competitive position scores a 6.0 — the origination moat and KKR-driven platform expansion are genuine and the ROE trajectory is positive, but the structural exposure to a single policy regime caps how much credit the moat deserves.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is federal clean-energy policy. HASI's model depends on the investment/production tax-credit regime and tax-equity partnership structures; any rollback, phase-out, or change to IRA-era credits would impair deal economics and pipeline. This is the single most important thread for the name and a likely driver of the elevated 11.6% short interest.

Secondary risks: (1) the GAAP-vs-economics disconnect can produce headline losses that pressure the stock on misreads and requires investor trust in management's non-GAAP assumptions; (2) off-balance-sheet opacity — ~54% of the Portfolio is non-consolidated equity-method, and the Rule 3-09 disclosure showed significant unconsolidated investees (Palmetto HASI Holdings, Daggett Renewable HoldCo) posting large underlying net losses ($(452)M aggregate 2025) on depreciation and hedge marks, with visibility one quarter in arrears; (3) refinancing/rate risk on the June 2026 note; (4) residential-solar counterparty concentration; (5) the first visible credit crack — two same-borrower receivables migrated from Performance Category 1 to Category 2 in Q1 2026 on project-specific technical challenges, lifting the allowance to $67M (still tiny; 98% of Portfolio remains Category 1, no non-accruals, 10-year realized loss 0.08%); and (6) contingent guarantees (~$53M Jupiter working-capital, ~$104M JV energy-efficiency maturing Aug 2026).

Risk factors score a 4.8. The policy risk is specific and binary rather than a generic sector truism, the credit crack and senior commercial-leadership departure (Chief Revenue & Strategy Officer transitioning to advisor) are early-warning items, and the accounting complexity is a genuine analytical hazard, not diffuse noise. These are partly but not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.3% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 30.5% (7.8pp spread), indicating the market prices elevated uncertainty into the coming months. Put/call skew is balanced (0.1%) at the front and only modestly negative further out (-3.5% at 6 months), so there is no acute directional bearish tilt in options despite the short interest. IV has risen from 32.5% to 38.3% since late May while the stock fell 8%, consistent with an approaching catalyst (earnings, policy developments) rather than complacency. The flat term structure and balanced skew suggest the options market is not confirming a strong directional thesis in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($46–50).** HASI compounds Adjusted EPS at ~10% toward ~$3.30 next year while the KKR CCH1 JV scales fee income and lifts ROE further, the HLBV timing loss reverses cleanly, the June refinancing wall is absorbed without material spread compression, tax-credit policy stabilizes, and the dividend continues rising. A re-rating to ~14–15x forward adjusted EPS plus dividends gets to the low-$50s, near consensus median. Requires estimate revisions to turn positive.

**Bear case ($28–31, ~19–27% downside).** A material rollback or phase-out of IRA tax credits impairs the pipeline and deal economics; the rising cost of capital compresses the yield-to-cost spread faster than expected; the residential-solar mezzanine book or the emerging Category-2 credit deteriorates further; and continued negative revisions plus a headline GAAP loss pressure the multiple. Compression to ~9–10x forward adjusted EPS with policy overhang gets to the high-$20s.

**Base case (~$40–42).** Adjusted EPS grows mid-single to low-double digits, the dividend covers, ~12.8x forward adjusted holds roughly steady, and the stock delivers largely its dividend yield plus modest capital appreciation over twelve months. The risk/reward is roughly balanced, which is why this is a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (typically late July/early August, then early November) and any material federal clean-energy tax-credit policy development, whichever comes first. The KKR CCH1 funding pace and the June 2026 refinancing execution are the near-term operational checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Near-term Adjusted EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current-quarter and next-quarter revisions inflect from -3.5%/-5.2% to positive over a 90-day window).
- Clarity that IRA-era tax credits and tax-equity structures are durable, removing the dominant policy overhang.
- KKR CCH1 funding accelerates toward the remaining ~$700M each with visible ROE accretion, and the yield-to-cost spread stabilizes despite the June refinancing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material adverse change to federal clean-energy tax credits that impairs the pipeline.
- Credit deterioration spreads beyond the single Category-2 borrower — allowance rising materially above $67M, additional migrations, or any non-accrual.
- A dividend cut or coverage strain, or Cash Available for Reinvestment declining materially below the ~$939M level.
- Cost of debt rising materially above the ~6.1% level such that the portfolio-yield-to-cost spread compresses below the level needed to sustain ~10% adjusted-earnings growth.

## Conclusion

HASI is a legitimate specialty energy-transition compounder producing record Adjusted ROE (15.7%), ~10% Adjusted EPS growth, a well-covered ~4.4% dividend, and a fully-hedged fixed-rate debt book. The GAAP loss headline misleads; the economics are sound. The trajectory of the score (5.7 to 5.9) reflects improving financial-health recognition, and the recent 8% price decline on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals modestly improves the entry point rather than signaling deterioration.

What holds it back from a buy: forward estimate revisions are negative in the near-term windows, the cost of capital is rising as low-coupon notes reprice, the multiple is fair rather than cheap, and the dominant risk of federal tax-credit policy is specific and binary with no near-term resolution catalyst. The origination moat and KKR lever are real, but the equity offers limited asymmetric upside until estimate revisions turn and policy risk clears. Own it for the yield and the steady adjusted-earnings compounding; do not chase it for capital appreciation. **HOLD** (income orientation), overall score 5.5.
