**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SkyWest is the largest regional airline operator in the United States, flying roughly 2,370 daily departures under the United Express, Delta Connection, American Eagle, and Alaska brands. It carries no consumer-facing brand risk and competes by supplying contracted "lift" to the four major carriers, with about 84% of revenue under Capacity Purchase Agreements (CPAs) where the major bears fuel and ticket-price risk. The 2024-2025 stretch was a powerful earnings recovery: revenue rose 15% to $4.06B in 2025, operating income climbed to $618M, net income reached $428M, and diluted EPS hit $10.35. That recovery came from utilization, with block hours up 14.7% on a roughly flat aircraft count, after a 2022-2023 captain shortage that had constrained flying.

The forward case is harder. The Q1 2026 print reported $2.50 EPS, essentially flat year-over-year, but the headline masks a 53% collapse in the SkyWest Airlines + SWC operating-segment profit (to $25.5M from $54.3M) driven by an 11.9% jump in salaries and wages from rising pilot attrition and training costs. Net income held flat only because the leasing segment grew 24% to $82.3M and a one-time 5.6% tax rate provided a discrete benefit; pre-tax income actually fell roughly 11%. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut 16.9% for the current quarter and 5.1% for the full year over 90 days. The utilization tailwind that drove the recovery is largely spent, and the labor cycle that powers this business is turning.

At ~9x trailing and forward earnings, SKYW screens cheap against a passenger-airlines peer median P/E of 22.1x, and the balance sheet has deleveraged from $2.94B to $1.85B of long-term debt since 2022. The cheapness reflects genuine earnings risk: the stock trades at a discount because the easy growth is over and the margin headwind is materializing in real time. We rate SKYW a HOLD, classifying the rationale as uncertain — the valuation support and capital-return discipline are real, but the deteriorating operating-segment quality and falling estimates do not support a confident BUY at this point in the cycle.

## Company Overview

SkyWest operates two segments. The operating segment (SkyWest Airlines plus SWC) flies the aircraft, employs the crews, and earns CPA and prorate revenue. The leasing segment owns and finances the E175 fleet and leases regional jets and engines to third parties, functioning as a captive aircraft-financing arm whose returns are largely insulated from operating volatility.

The revenue mix is the key to understanding the business. Roughly 84% of FY2025 revenue came from CPAs, under which the major airline pays fixed fees per block hour, departure, and aircraft, and bears fuel and ticket-price risk; about 93% of code-share operating costs are reimbursed at pre-determined rates. The remaining ~16% comes from prorate agreements and SWC charter, where SkyWest controls pricing but bears fuel and operating risk. The prorate slice is the fastest-growing (+33.5% in 2025) and the highest-margin, but it is also the least contractually secure, terminable on 30-180 days' notice.

SkyWest's structural edge is multi-partner diversification. Most regional competitors (Endeavor, Envoy, PSA, Piedmont, Horizon) are wholly owned by a single major; SkyWest serves four. The binding industry constraint over the past several years has been pilot supply, not aircraft or demand, which is why earnings swung so violently down in 2022-2023 and up in 2024-2025. A persistent constraint is the major-airline scope clause that caps regional aircraft at 76 seats, limiting growth options.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year recovery is genuine and well-documented:

| Revenue | $3.00B | $2.94B | $3.53B | $4.06B (+15%) |
| Operating income | $181M | $104M | $495M | $618M |
| Net income | $73M | ~$34M | $323M | $428M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $7.77 | $10.35 |
| Block hours | — | 1.14M | 1.29M | 1.48M (+14.7%) |
| Long-term debt | $2.94B | $2.56B | $2.14B | $1.85B |

FY2025 operating cash flow was $940M with reported free cash flow near $908M, flattered by the fact that net capex was only $32M (most aircraft purchases are debt-financed and flow through investing and financing, not operating capex). The balance sheet improved to roughly 53% equity / 47% debt, with cash and marketable securities of $707M at year-end 2025 and $627M at Q1 2026. ROE of 15.6% is healthy for an asset-heavy carrier. The Altman Z-Score of 1.48 (nominally "distress") is a false positive here: it is dominated by a low current ratio of 0.65, but the aircraft debt is secured, ownership costs are contractually reimbursed by investment-grade partners, and the company is actively deleveraging with strong FCF.

The Q1 2026 quarter is where the analysis tightens. Revenue grew 6.8% to $1.01B and net income held flat, but the composition deteriorated. Growth decelerated sharply: block hours +3.1% (versus +14.7% in 2025), departures +1.1%, passengers -0.6%. The earnings-surprise record is strong (four consecutive beats, including 13.1% in Q1 2026), which lends some confidence to near-term consensus, but the underlying segment trend is the more important signal.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has changed character. The 2024-2025 surge was a one-time utilization normalization as the captain shortage eased and SkyWest returned to full schedules requested by its partners. With aircraft count roughly flat and attrition rising, organic growth has slowed to ~3% in Q1 2026. Future growth now depends on net new E175 deliveries — a 68-firm-aircraft order book (~$2.24B) plus 44 delivery positions, financed 75-85% with debt. That path is lower-margin and more capital-intensive than the recovery just completed.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration. Current-quarter consensus has been cut 16.9% over 90 days (to $2.74 from $3.29), the full-year number is down 5.1% (to $10.90), and next year is down 2.1% (to $11.99). The 30-day trend for the current quarter is still negative (-2.3%), so the cuts are ongoing rather than stabilizing. Negative estimate momentum of this kind is one of the more reliable forward indicators, and it argues against rewarding growth here. The leasing segment is the quiet exception — it delivered $302M of FY2025 pre-tax profit (more than the operating segment's $263M), is far less volatile, and is structurally growing. Adjacent optionality (the CRJ550 conversion program, SWC commuter authority, engine-leasing JV) is real but small and unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

SKYW trades at ~9x trailing and forward earnings, well below the passenger-airlines peer median P/E of 22.1x. The relevant peers are a heterogeneous mix: Air Lease at 7.0x (the closest comparable for the leasing piece), Sun Country at 22.1x, Alaska at 75.6x on depressed earnings, and JetBlue and Allegiant at negative multiples. The blended median overstates how cheap SKYW truly is, because the most comparable peer (AL, a lessor) trades at a similar single-digit multiple, and the depressed/negative peers inflate the median.

The case for a re-rating rests on the leasing segment deserving a higher multiple than a pure regional operator and on the deleveraging continuing. The case against it is that earnings estimates are falling, the operating-segment margin is compressing on a turning labor cycle, and the trailing P/E is not a trough multiple — this is roughly mid-cycle earnings power that the market is appropriately discounting for forward risk. A low P/E by itself is not a buy signal, and here the compression reflects a real forward headwind rather than a clean mispricing. The analyst consensus mean target of $121.50 (low $95, high $150) implies meaningful upside, but those targets are stale relative to the 90-day estimate cuts and should be treated cautiously. We assign a valuation score of 5.5 — cheap on the screen, but the cheapness is earned by the cycle risk, not an obvious mispricing with an identifiable catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

SkyWest's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-favorable. Its scale and four-partner diversification are durable advantages over single-major captive carriers, and the January 2026 CPA extensions (40 E175 with United, 13 E175 with Delta, now running into 2033-2038) materially de-risk the two largest relationships, which together represent ~72% of aircraft. The E175 is the only sub-76-seat dual-class jet still in production after Bombardier ended the CRJ line, giving SkyWest a position in the fleet-refresh cycle that few competitors can match.

The trajectory is mixed. The binding constraint — pilot supply — is turning against the industry as majors resume hiring, which drove the Q1 2026 attrition uptick and operating-margin compression. Scope clauses cap the addressable fleet, and supplier concentration (two airframers, one engine maker) limits flexibility. SkyWest's pilot-pipeline investments (Contour stake, debt guarantees, United Aviate pathway) and CRJ550 program are sensible offsets, but the near-term competitive dynamic of competing with the majors for pilots while supplying lift to them is the structural tension that determines margins.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Pilot attrition reversal — already materializing.** The Q1 2026 operating-segment profit decline is directly attributable to higher attrition and training costs. This is the single most important variable, and it just moved the wrong way. It is the central risk not fully reflected in trailing data.
2. **Customer concentration.** United (~45%) plus Delta (~28%) is ~72% of aircraft. The January 2026 extensions de-risk this into the 2030s, a genuine positive.
3. **Embraer/Brazil tariff exposure (new).** The 10-K states recently enacted US tariffs have had a significant impact on the cost of non-US-manufactured E175 components imported from Brazil, directly threatening the economics of the $2.24B order book.
4. **No cybersecurity insurance (new).** SkyWest disclosed it previously carried cyber insurance but currently does not, leaving it self-insured against a breach.
5. **Heavy committed capex.** 68 firm E175 plus 44 delivery positions, 75-85% debt-financed, re-levers the balance sheet and exposes the company to financing-cost and residual-value risk on $5.8B of PP&E.
6. **Prorate terminability.** The higher-growth, higher-margin revenue is the least contractually secure (30-180 days' notice).
7. **Scope-clause / in-sourcing and government-shutdown sensitivity** round out the structural set.

Several of these risks are specific and recently surfaced (attrition trend, tariffs, lapsed cyber coverage) and are not fully priced into a 9x multiple, which is why the risk score sits just above the midpoint despite the strong contractual revenue base.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.1% sits essentially on top of one-year historical volatility of 36.9% (fairly priced), and IV has fallen 6.9 points (from 44.0%) as the stock rallied 13.9% off the early-June low. The put/call skew is mildly elevated at 3.8% on the near tenor with a 2.00 put/call volume ratio, indicating some defensive hedging into the next earnings window, but the put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.98 to 0.51, suggesting bearish positioning has been unwinding rather than building. Short interest is light at 4.3% of float (3.2 days to cover). The options market is pricing routine uncertainty around an upcoming catalyst, not directional conviction, and does not override the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$120-135).** The pilot-attrition uptick proves transitory as the broader major-airline hiring cycle moderates, and the operating segment re-stabilizes margins. The leasing segment continues compounding at low-volatility ~$300M+ pre-tax, and the market begins valuing the hybrid operator-plus-lessor at a higher blended multiple. CPA extensions and the E175/CRJ550 fleet program drive measured, debt-funded growth. Continued buybacks (~21% share-count reduction since 2022, $137.8M remaining) compound EPS. At 11-12x normalized $11-12 EPS, the stock approaches the $121.50 consensus mean.

**Bear Case (~$78-80).** Pilot attrition accelerates as majors keep hiring, driving sustained operating-segment margin compression. The leasing segment and tax timing can no longer mask the deterioration, and reported EPS follows estimates lower. Tariffs erode E175 fleet-renewal economics while debt-financed deliveries re-lever the balance sheet into a higher-for-longer rate environment. Multiple stays at ~8x on falling earnings, implying high-$70s, near the 52-week low of $77.89.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The valuation support and capital-return discipline are real, but the deteriorating operating-segment quality, exhausted utilization tailwind, and falling estimates do not justify a confident growth or income call in either direction. There is no dividend, so this is not an income hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating-segment profit stabilizes or recovers for two consecutive quarters, with salaries-and-wages growth decelerating below revenue growth — direct evidence the attrition headwind has peaked.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year consensus revised up rather than down over a 90-day window).
- Clear evidence the leasing segment is being re-rated, or a fleet/financing decision that materially improves the debt-funded growth math (e.g., favorable resolution of E175 tariff exposure).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of double-digit operating-segment profit decline without a one-time tax or leasing offset.
- Loss or material renegotiation of a United or Delta CPA, or a meaningful prorate termination.
- A guidance cut or estimate revisions accelerating to the downside (next-quarter consensus cut by >10% over 30 days).
- Balance-sheet re-leveraging that pushes long-term debt materially back above $2.5B without commensurate earnings growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) is the natural checkpoint — it is the cleanest test of whether the Q1 operating-margin compression was a one-quarter attrition spike or the start of a sustained labor-cycle headwind.

## Conclusion

SkyWest is a well-run, deleveraging, share-count-shrinking regional operator with a genuinely high-quality captive-leasing segment and a screen-cheap ~9x multiple. The trajectory data shows a HOLD → BUY → HOLD path with valuation and growth scores fading and risk scores improving; the current data confirms the fade rather than reversing it. The decisive issue is that the 2024-2025 utilization recovery is largely complete, the operating segment's Q1 2026 profit fell on re-emerging pilot attrition (masked only by leasing gains and a one-time tax rate), and consensus estimates are being cut 5-17% over 90 days. This is a low-multiple value-trap setup that requires an independent catalyst to reward, and that catalyst is not yet visible. We maintain a HOLD and would revisit on evidence the attrition headwind stabilizes or estimate revisions turn positive.
