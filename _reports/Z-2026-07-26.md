**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zillow reached an operating milestone in FY2025: its first GAAP net profit in years ($23M), on revenue of $2.58B (+16%), with adjusted EBITDA margin rising to 24% and adjusted free cash flow of $420M. Q1 2026 extended the momentum: revenue $708M (+18%), net income $46M, adjusted EBITDA margin 26%. Rentals grew +42% and mortgage origination volume nearly doubled (+97%). The balance sheet is strong: net cash, only non-recourse mortgage warehouse debt, current ratio 3.13, and an undrawn $500M revolver. On the surface this is a business inflecting to durable profitability.

The stock tells a different story. Class C shares have fallen roughly 62% from the July 2025 level and now sit at ~$30.77, near the 52-week low of $29.23 and below the ~$46-48 average price at which management repurchased $626M of stock in Q1 2026 alone. The market is pricing three concerns that the headline numbers obscure: (1) revenue growth is being driven entirely by monetization per visit while traffic and unique users are declining (−3% each in Q1'26), raising the risk of AI-driven search disintermediation eroding the top of the funnel faster than monetization can compensate; (2) the fastest-growing segment, Rentals, rests on the Zillow-Redfin syndication deal that the FTC and five state attorneys general are litigating, with trial expected around August 2026; and (3) near-term consensus EPS estimates are falling (current-quarter revised −14.3% over 90 days).

We rate Z a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The valuation gap to consensus (current price ~45% below the $56 median target) and the balance sheet argue against an AVOID, while the binary FTC trial, declining traffic, negative estimate revisions, and complete absence of insider conviction buying argue against a BUY. The situation resolves on identifiable catalysts within the next two to four months. Until the FTC trial outcome is known and the traffic-versus-monetization divergence is clarified, the asymmetry is not compelling enough to size a position on either side.

## Company Overview

Zillow operates the most-visited U.S. residential real estate app and website, anchored by a database of ~173 million homes and the Zestimate valuation model (1.8% median error on listed homes). The portfolio spans Zillow, Trulia, StreetEasy, HotPads, Zillow Home Loans (ZHL), Zillow Rentals, Zillow New Construction, Follow Up Boss (CRM), ShowingTime, dotloop, and Zillow Closing.

The revenue model combines advertising and transaction facilitation across four segments (FY2025):
- **Residential (66%, $1.70B)** — Premier Agent, the rebranded "Zillow Preferred" pay-when-you-close program, Zillow Showcase, plus SaaS tools.
- **Rentals (24%, $630M)** — the growth engine; multifamily advertising and rental applications.
- **Mortgages (8%, $199M)** — ZHL purchase originations sold into the secondary market.
- **Other (2%, $50M)** — display advertising.

The strategic thesis is the "integrated transaction" (Enhanced Markets): funneling high-intent movers through touring → agent → financing in a unified experience, with more than 40% of connections now running through Enhanced Markets. Management estimates roughly 80% of U.S. transactions involve an agent using at least one Zillow product, against a stated TAM of ~$202B.

A structural feature the reader must internalize: Class C shares (ticker Z) are nonvoting. Founders Rich Barton and Lloyd Frink control ~57.7% of voting power through Class B super-voting shares. Holders of Z own economic claims on the business with no governance rights.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is bifurcated between a strong balance sheet and a thin, add-back-dependent income statement.

| Revenue | $1,945M | $2,236M | $2,583M (+16%) | $708M (+18% YoY) |
| Net income | −$158M | −$112M | +$23M | +$46M (vs $8M) |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | $498M (22%) | $622M (24%) | $182M (26%) |
| Gross margin | 78% | 76% | 74% | 73% |
| Operating CF | — | $428M | $368M | $200M |
| Adj. FCF | — | $309M | $420M | $127M |

**Segment momentum (Q1 2026):** Rentals +42% ($183M; multifamily +57%), Mortgages +56% (origination volume +97% to $1.57B), Residential +8% (revenue-per-visit +12%, not traffic). For FY25, Rentals grew +39% and ZHL origination volume rose +52% to $4.75B.

**The critical operating tension.** Visits fell −3% and unique users −3% YoY in Q1 2026, and visits grew only +3% in FY25. Revenue growth is coming from monetization per visit (Residential revenue-per-visit +12%) and Enhanced Markets conversion, not audience expansion. This is the fulcrum of the investment case. A business that raises revenue per user while the user base contracts can compound for a while, but the runway depends on how much pricing and attach the funnel will bear before it hits a ceiling, and on whether AI-driven search tools accelerate the traffic decline.

**Balance sheet.** This is the strongest part of the story. Altman Z-Score 5.6 (safe zone), current ratio 3.13, debt/equity 0.16, equity/assets 0.86. The only debt is non-recourse mortgage repo lines ($335M) funding ZHL, plus a new undrawn $500M Goldman revolver (Jan 2026). All prior convertible notes are settled. Cash and investments stood at $788M in Q1'26, down from $1.3B at year-end 2025 primarily because of buybacks.

Two caveats temper the balance-sheet framing. Goodwill of $2.82B represents ~54% of total assets, a meaningful impairment risk if growth stalls or a segment (Rentals under adverse FTC outcome) deteriorates. And the FY25 GAAP profit of $23M is thin relative to $390M of stock-based compensation add-backs (~15% of revenue). Adjusted EBITDA (24-26% margins) and adjusted FCF ($420M FY25) are the more reliable measures of underlying earnings power; the headline net income does not yet reflect a comfortable margin of safety.

A latent tailwind: the company carries ~$1.8B of federal NOLs, and management has flagged that the deferred-tax valuation allowance could reverse "within the next several quarters," which would produce a large one-time non-cash GAAP benefit.

**Earnings surprise record.** Over the last four quarters, Zillow beat once (Q1'26 +14.2%), was in-line once (Q3'25 +0.2%), and missed twice (Q4'25 −4.4%, Q2'25 −7.3%). This is not the consistent-beat pattern that would justify high confidence in current consensus; visibility is moderate at best.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth vectors are Rentals and Mortgages. Rentals compounding near +40% off a $630M base is the clearest secular story; multifamily advertising demand and rental application monetization are structurally underpenetrated. ZHL nearly doubling origination volume validates the integrated-transaction thesis: as Zillow converts more high-intent buyers into financing customers, it captures a second monetization event per transaction. Residential, the largest segment, is growing single digits and depends on continued revenue-per-visit gains.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and net-negative in the near term. Current-quarter EPS consensus was cut −14.3% over 90 days (to $0.45), and the current quarter shows a +0.0% 30-day revision. Current-year ($2.30) and next-year ($3.01) estimates are marginally higher over 90 days (+3.4% and +1.0%) but flat-to-slightly-down over 30 days. This does not clear the bar for the strong inflection signal (rising estimates + accelerating revenue at a reasonable multiple) that would justify a BUY. Revenue growth is solid at +18%, but the near-term earnings momentum is deteriorating rather than accelerating, and the estimate cuts co-occur with declining traffic, so the two signals reinforce each other rather than diverge favorably.

The single most important growth question is whether monetization can outrun traffic erosion. Zillow's 10-K explicitly names AI-driven search and assistant tools as a threat to portal traffic, and the −3% visits/users trend makes that threat tangible rather than hypothetical. If AI search compresses the top of the funnel faster than Enhanced Markets and ZHL attach can lift revenue per remaining user, the model's growth ceiling arrives sooner than consensus assumes.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$30.77 and a trailing/forward P/E of ~127x on $0.09 GAAP EPS, headline valuation is not the right lens, because GAAP earnings are depressed by SBC add-backs and the recent profitability inflection. On consensus EPS of $2.30 (current year) and $3.01 (next year), the stock trades at ~13.4x and ~10.2x forward earnings. On adjusted FCF of $420M against a $5.5B market cap, the FCF yield is ~7.6%. Those are undemanding multiples for a business growing revenue at 18% with 24-26% EBITDA margins and net cash.

Peer comparison is of limited use here — the sector screen (BZ 13.2x, BIDU negative, NBIS 76.8x) is a heterogeneous grab-bag of Chinese internet names and an AI infrastructure company, none of which are true real-estate-portal comparables. The more relevant anchor is the analyst target set: consensus median $56, mean $55.83, low $37, high $76. The current price sits ~17% below even the low target and ~45% below the median. On the estimate-revision-supported basis, that gap looks like meaningful upside.

We do not, however, rate valuation highly. A "cheap" multiple has been a value-trap warning far more often than a buy signal, and cheapness must be paired with an independent catalyst before it is rewarded. The catalyst that would close the gap — favorable FTC trial resolution plus evidence that monetization can sustainably outrun traffic decline — is unresolved and binary. The stock is cheap on forward earnings, but the forward earnings themselves are contingent on the Rentals segment surviving the antitrust challenge intact and on the monetization-versus-traffic divergence resolving favorably. That contingency is why the market has marked the stock down ~62%. Analyst targets here are not reliable floors: they were set before the estimate cuts and before the FTC trial date crystallized, and the wide dispersion ($37 to $76) signals genuine disagreement rather than a firm base. We assign a 5.5: cheap enough to reject AVOID, but not cheap enough relative to the binary risk to reward as asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Zillow's competitive position is defined by a battle over control of listing data and the homebuyer relationship, and the trajectory has recently softened from clearly dominant to contested. The brand moat is real (Zillow is searched more than "real estate"), as are the proprietary data/Zestimate assets, 224 patents, and the largest rental listings marketplace. Enhanced Markets and ZHL attach are widening the monetization funnel where competitors cannot easily follow.

Three forces cut against the trajectory. First, Zillow's "listing access standards" (banning listings publicly marketed before hitting the MLS/Zillow, aimed at Compass "private exclusives") make it both a competitive aggressor and a legal target. The scorecard leans slightly Zillow's way (Compass lost its preliminary injunction and voluntarily dismissed its antitrust suit in March 2026, and Zillow won a court order against MRED (Chicago) to restore its listing feed), but the fight is ongoing across multiple fronts including CoStar/Homes.com IP claims and Compass ethics complaints in 26 states. Second, CoStar's well-funded Homes.com is a persistent portal challenger. Third, and most structurally, the −3% traffic decline suggests the audience moat is eroding at the margin even as monetization improves, which is why we mark competitive position down from the prior 7.0 to 5.0. A market leader whose audience is contracting scores lower than a challenger gaining share.

The NAR commission settlement (2024) is reshaping how buyer-broker commissions are negotiated, an ongoing structural change to the economics of Zillow's agent-advertising customers that adds uncertainty to Residential monetization durability.

## Risk Assessment

**Antitrust / FTC (dominant, binary, dated).** The FTC and five state AGs are suing Zillow and Redfin over the Feb 2025 $100M rentals syndication deal. In July 2026 a judge denied the FTC's motion to deem the deal presumptively unlawful, a modest procedural win for Zillow, but the case heads to trial around August 2026. An adverse ruling could unwind Zillow's rentals syndication advantage, directly threatening the +42%-growth segment. This is the single largest catalyst and it resolves within the analysis horizon.

**AI search disintermediation.** The 10-K explicitly flags AI-driven search/assistant tools reducing portal traffic. The already-negative visits/users trend makes this a live, measurable risk rather than a distant one.

**Zillow Offers legacy litigation.** A certified securities class action (9th Circuit affirmed certification Jan 2026) and derivative suits over the iBuying wind-down. No accruals booked; loss "reasonably possible" but not estimable, an unquantified tail.

**New investor securities suit (June 2026)** alleging Zillow mischaracterized the Redfin syndication deal's antitrust risk, which reinforces the litigation overhang.

**Housing-market and rate dependency.** Total transaction value grew only ~2-3%; Zillow is outgrowing a weak market via share gains, but further housing deterioration hits all segments simultaneously. ZHL adds interest-rate, secondary-market, and GSE-dependency risk.

**Goodwill impairment.** $2.82B of goodwill (~54% of assets) is vulnerable if growth stalls or the Rentals segment is impaired by an adverse FTC outcome.

**Governance.** Dual-class founder control (~57.7% voting) with Class C carrying zero votes.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 73.8% versus 1-year historical vol of 46.6%, rich by 27 points and in the 89th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (73.8% near-term vs 64.8% long-term), consistent with the market anticipating a discrete near-term event (the FTC trial and the next earnings print). The put/call OI ratio has risen sharply from 0.70 to 1.94 since mid-June, and the put/call volume ratio is 1.57, indicating net defensive/bearish positioning. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±16.5%. Put/call skew is only −2.2% (balanced), so the demand is for two-sided protection rather than a pronounced crash bet. The market expects a large move soon and is unsure of direction, which aligns with our HOLD framing: the setup is binary, not directional, until the FTC trial resolves.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Zillow is a net-cash, FCF-generative platform compounding revenue at 18% while inflecting to GAAP profitability. Rentals (+42%) and Mortgages (+97% origination volume) are secular growth engines with long runways. At ~10-13x forward earnings and a ~7.6% FCF yield, the stock is deeply discounted, ~45% below the $56 median analyst target. Favorable litigation momentum (Compass dismissal, FTC's failed early-ruling bid) plus a potential ~$1.8B NOL valuation-allowance reversal could re-rate the equity. If the FTC trial resolves favorably and monetization-per-visit continues to outrun traffic decline, the stock recovers toward the mid-$50s. Bull case: $56-60 (consensus median to upper range), ~80-95% upside.

**Bear Case.** Revenue growth is a monetization illusion masking a contracting audience; AI search accelerates the −3% traffic decline; the FTC wins at trial and forces an unwind of the Redfin rentals syndication, gutting the fastest-growing segment. Goodwill impairment follows. The thin GAAP profit reverses, buybacks (executed at $46-70 while the stock trades at ~$31) continue to destroy value, and estimate cuts deepen. Bear case: $22-25 (near prior lows / on impaired Rentals growth), ~20-28% downside.

**Base Case.** The FTC trial produces an ambiguous or settled outcome that neither destroys Rentals nor fully removes the overhang; monetization gains partly offset flat-to-declining traffic; revenue grows low-to-mid teens. The stock re-rates modestly toward the low-$40s as the litigation cloud thins and the profitability inflection holds. Base case: ~$40, ~30% upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The valuation discount and balance-sheet strength preclude an AVOID; the binary FTC trial, declining traffic, negative near-term estimate revisions, and pure insider selling preclude a BUY. The trajectory (5.7 → 5.8) has been mildly improving, driven by the financial-health upgrade as GAAP profitability arrived, but competitive position deteriorated as traffic declined, a divergence that captures why this is a two-sided hold rather than a directional call. Two consecutive HOLDs is not yet a stale pattern, and the current data supports continuing it because the dominant catalyst (FTC trial ~August 2026) has not resolved.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Favorable FTC trial outcome (or benign settlement) that preserves the Rentals syndication economics, removing the segment's binary risk.
- Stabilization or return to positive growth in visits/unique users for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the traffic decline is not accelerating under AI-search pressure.
- Current-quarter and current-year estimate revisions turning positive alongside sustained +15%+ revenue growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse FTC ruling forcing unwind of the Redfin rentals deal, with a Rentals growth deceleration below +15%.
- Traffic decline accelerating beyond −5% YoY, signaling AI disintermediation outrunning monetization.
- A goodwill impairment charge or a reversal of the FY25 GAAP profit.

**Reassessment timeline:** The FTC trial (~August 2026) and the Q2 2026 earnings print are the natural reassessment points, both within the next one to two months. Given the options market is pricing a ±16.5% near-term move, the situation should clarify meaningfully by early autumn 2026.

## Conclusion

Zillow is a fundamentally improved business trading at a discounted forward multiple, which is precisely what makes it dangerous to score without discipline. The GAAP profitability inflection is real but thin and add-back-dependent; revenue growth is being manufactured from a shrinking audience; and the fastest-growing segment sits under a binary antitrust trial that resolves within months. Management's aggressive buybacks at $46-70 into a stock now near $31 have destroyed near-term value, and the complete absence of open-market insider purchases at multi-year lows offers no conviction signal from those with the most information; the entire C-suite sold (mechanically) in May-June 2026. The options market is pricing imminent stress and two-sided uncertainty.

We rate Z a HOLD. The ~45% discount to consensus and the balance sheet keep it off the AVOID list; the unresolved FTC trial, negative near-term estimate revisions, declining traffic, and weak management-alignment signal keep it off the BUY list. This is a stock to reassess after the August FTC trial and the next earnings print, when the binary that currently dominates the setup will resolve.
