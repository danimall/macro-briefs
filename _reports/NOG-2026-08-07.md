## Executive Summary

Northern Oil and Gas is the largest publicly traded dedicated non-operator in US onshore E&P: 301,797 net acres, 1,195 net wells, an average 10.2% working interest across more than 100 operating partners, and 64 employees running $2.08B of annual oil and gas sales at $1.24/Boe of G&A. The structural cost advantage is real and quantified. The problem is what the model has produced lately. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA was $1,628.8M against $1,619.1M in FY2024, growth of 0.6%, and roughly $48.6M of that was a non-recurring net litigation benefit from a North Dakota operator settlement. Normalized, EBITDA declined modestly while production grew 9%. Widening oil differentials ($5.53/Bbl versus $3.88) and collapsing gas realizations (79% of Henry Hub versus 93%) consumed the entire volume gain.

The per-share arithmetic is the crux of the call. FY2025 EBITDA of $1,628.8M against ~98M average shares is about $16.60 per share. A reasonable FY2026 estimate of $1.5–1.6B against ~107M shares is roughly $14.50, down about 12%, and that decline occurred while the company deployed $464.6M on the Utica package from Infinity and roughly US$253M on the Duvernay entry from Parallax, funded partly by an 8,288,289-share March offering at approximately $27.50. Acquisitions are growing barrels; they have not yet grown cash flow per share.

At $20.95 the stock is 2.9x FY2025 EBITDA, 4.9x the seven-analyst FY2027 consensus of $4.27, 1.24x a book value that has already absorbed $971M of ceiling-test impairments, and yields 8.6% on a $1.80 dividend covered roughly 2.3x by organic free cash flow. Those are genuinely low multiples. The reason we are not upgrading is the hedge structure: NOG has sold 15.74 MMBbl of 2027 WTI swaptions at $69.44, 10.81 MMBbl of 2028 at $65.81, plus short calls of 5.74/4.61/1.72 MMBbl across 2027–2029, all exercisable at the counterparties' option. In the sustained-high-oil scenario that would justify re-rating a 2.9x E&P, counterparties exercise and NOG is locked into below-market realizations into 2029. In the falling-oil scenario, they do not exercise and NOG receives no floor from that layer. That is capped upside without matched downside protection, and it is not visible in any screen. **HOLD**, on income grounds.

Both matter to the thesis: the first because net leverage is 1.56x, the second because the hedge policy is currently the dominant driver of reported results.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported GAAP numbers do not describe the business.** FY2025 net income was $38.8M on $2,475.7M of revenue against $520.3M on $2,225.7M in FY2024. Q1 2026 showed a net loss of $522.8M (−$5.31/share) on total revenues of $5.0M. Two mechanics explain it. First, full-cost ceiling-test impairments: $702.7M in FY2025 and $268.3M in Q1 2026, $971.0M across five quarters, all non-cash and driven by a trailing-twelve-month average of first-day-of-month SEC prices. Second, NOG does not use hedge accounting, so unsettled mark-to-market runs through the revenue line — the Q1 2026 unsettled derivative loss alone was $521.4M as the March Hormuz-driven oil spike moved the book from a $121.6M net asset to a $398.2M net liability. Note that the supplied profile fields are internally inconsistent (EPS of $0.39 alongside a trailing P/E of −3.18, which implies roughly −$6.59); the GAAP loss is the operative figure, and neither is analytically useful here.

**Operating performance.** The Adjusted EBITDA series disclosed in the proxy is the cleaner read: $543.0M (2021), $1,086.3M (2022), $1,428.3M (2023), $1,619.1M (2024), $1,628.8M (2025). Reconstructing FY2025 from 10-K line items gives $1,617.3M, within 0.7%, so the disclosed figure holds. Growth of 0.6% on 9% production growth is the fact that matters, and it becomes a slight decline once the $81.7M settlement receipt less $33.1M of associated legal expense is removed.

Price capture explains it: realized oil $59.20/Bbl versus $71.59; $64.35 versus $71.48 including settled hedges; realized price per Boe with hedges $44.82 versus $49.21. Gas realization fell to 79% of Henry Hub from 93%, and to 72% in Q1 2026 from 100% a year earlier.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,505.3M against $1,251.7M of capitalized spend ($919.2M drilling and development, $328.0M acquisitions), leaving $253.6M of free cash flow and roughly $581.6M excluding acquisitions. Shareholder returns totalled $230.4M: $173.4M of dividends and $57.0M of buybacks at an average of $29.25, now about 28% below the current price.

Q1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $323.6M from $407.4M on lower realizations, against $739.7M of capitalized spend including $513.9M of acquisitions. The gap was funded with the $227.9M March equity offering and $175M of net revolver draws. Q2 capex stepped down sharply to $190–200M, and management flagged a strong free cash flow quarter.

**Balance sheet.** $2,578.0M of debt: $653.0M revolver (SOFR+175–275bp, November 2030), $700M 3.625% converts (April 2029), $500M 8.75% notes (June 2031), $725M 7.875% notes (October 2033). No bond maturity before April 2029 after the October 2025 tender of the 8.125% 2028 notes extended weighted average maturity to 5.4 years. Net debt of $2,541.0M is 1.56x FY2025 EBITDA against a 1.0x stated target and a 3.50x covenant. Liquidity was approximately $1.2B at 3/31/26, and lenders raised the borrowing base from $1.8B to $1.975B in a February 2026 wildcard redetermination — third-party collateral validation that argues against reading the 1.49 Altman Z-Score literally, though that increase partly reflects the larger Utica asset base rather than higher per-unit value. Book equity of $1,784.4M equals about $16.87 per share.

The $426.5M working capital deficit is largely the $262.4M current derivative liability, and the covenant's current-ratio definition includes unused revolver commitments while excluding ASC 815 items, so it does not signal a breach.

**Financial health score: 5.0.** Maturity profile, liquidity, and covenant compliance are genuinely solid. Against that: leverage 56% above the stated target and likely higher after Duvernay cash, a retained earnings deficit, $971M of impairments, and a dividend-plus-buyback commitment ($192M plus $243M authorized) that exceeds plausible 2026 organic free cash flow of roughly $400–450M once the ~$180M annual ground-game run rate is included. The company cannot fund all three without incremental borrowing above target leverage.

## Growth Analysis

Headline growth is real and low quality. Q1 2026 total production rose 10% year over year while **oil volumes fell 6%** (6,621 MBbl) and gas rose 33%. Q2 oil was guided to 67.5–68.25 Mbo/d against roughly 73.6 Mbo/d in Q1, a 7–8% sequential decline. Oil was 81% of Q1 oil-and-gas sales, so revenue-weighted growth is materially worse than the Boe figure. Realized price per Boe excluding hedges fell 15% to $40.45.

In Q2 approximately 7,000 Boe/d was shut in by operators across April, May and part of June, concentrated on Novo assets in Culberson County, Texas and Eddy County, New Mexico, because deeply negative Waha realizations offset otherwise strong oil prices. Roughly three net turn-in-lines slipped to Q3. Williston (+4.0%) and Uinta (+11.5%) outperformed internal expectations, partially offsetting.

Acquisitions supply the growth. Utica adds 40% of a package where **Infinity, not NOG, is the operator and controls development pace**; from closing to 3/31/26 it contributed $3.5M of revenue and $0.2M of operating income. Duvernay adds roughly 4,000 Boe/d of light oil, ~75,000 net acres and 500-plus gross locations, with US$45M of 2026 and US$50M of 2027 capex, under a joint development agreement carrying **multi-year drilling commitments** — the first structural erosion of the model's stated flexibility.

Estimate revisions do not support an inflection call. Current-quarter consensus is $0.97, down 4.0% over 30 days but up 10.2% over 90; next quarter $0.97, −7.4%/+4.3%; next year $4.27, +2.2%/+2.6%. The "current year" figure of $3.07 (−18.6% over 90 days) rests on a single analyst and should not be weighted. Net read: near-term trims, out-year stability. The +30% revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection is absent.

Earnings delivery has been consistent: four consecutive beats (18.5%, 5.8%, 7.9%, 7.7%), with Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.13 against a $1.05 estimate. But $1.13 compares to $1.37 a year earlier, down 18%. The company beats a declining bar.

**Growth prospects score: 4.6.**

Peer context: the E&P comparable set shows a median P/E of 6.2x (CRK 6.2x, CNX 4.4x, with TALO, CRGY and CRC all negative on trailing earnings). NOG at 4.9x forward sits modestly below the gas-weighted names and is not an outlier. On EV/EBITDA, 2.9–3.1x is a discount to the 3.5–4.0x range non-op and diversified small-cap E&Ps have commanded, but the discount has identifiable causes rather than being unexplained.

The analyst target range ($25 low, $36 high, $30.89 mean, $29 median) sits 19% to 72% above spot. These are not independent confirmation. The brief documents a TipRanks-summarized Sell rating carrying a $29 target — a combination that indicates stale numbers rather than a coherent bullish view. Johnson Rice cut to Hold on May 20 and Raymond James cut to Outperform on May 21 with a $35 target, and the rating distribution has been static at 1 strong buy / 3 buy / 4 hold / 1 sell for four consecutive months. Targets that have not been marked to the current hedge and differential reality are a floor only if the underlying earnings assumptions hold.

What would make the multiple wrong? Two specific levers. First, the July 10 board increase to a ~$243M repurchase authorization, roughly 11.6M shares or 10.9% of the count at $20.95, with Q2 repurchases already executed at an average $20.37 — accretive at 1.24x book if funded from free cash flow. Second, the Q2 capex step-down to $190–200M from Q1's $739.7M, which converts a high-realization quarter into visible free cash flow.

What keeps us from scoring valuation higher: per-share EBITDA is falling roughly 12% year over year, and the short swaption and call layer means the bull-case oil scenario does not flow through to 2027–2029 realizations. A cheap multiple on eroding per-share earnings power with capped upside participation is the definition of a discount that deserves to persist until one of those two conditions changes.

**Valuation score: 6.0.** Cheap, with an identifiable but partly structural reason for the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

The non-op edge is transactional, not geological: 64 employees, more than 100 operating relationships, $1.24/Boe G&A, and an explicit strategy of buying small positions at a discount to the contiguous acreage larger producers pursue. Operator concentration improved, with the top six falling to 46% of Q1 2026 oil and gas sales from 54%, though the single largest operator remained in the 10–15% disclosure band in both periods.

Three areas of erosion deserve weight. First, the model's stated advantage of freedom from minimum drilling obligations is now qualified by the Duvernay joint development agreement's multi-year commitments and by Infinity's control of Utica development. Second, wellbore-only interests, where NOG does not hold the underlying leasehold, carry no right to participate in future wells, making reserve replacement structurally dependent on continued acquisition activity. Third, the 10-K concedes that falling oil prices reduce both the number of well proposals received and the proportion NOG elects — growth is pro-cyclical in a way an operator's is not.

The Waha shut-ins are the clearest demonstration that a third party can unilaterally reduce NOG's revenue regardless of how many basins it holds. Geographic diversification (Q1 2026 Boe: Permian 39%, Williston 28%, Appalachia 26%, Uinta 7%) and operator diversification are different measures and should not be conflated.

**Competitive position score: 5.2.** Real cost advantage, deteriorating trajectory on optionality and revenue-weighted mix.

## Risk Assessment

**Hedge structure (most material).** Q1 2026 unhedged realized oil was $66.32/Bbl but $62.00 after settled hedges, a $4.32 cost. Q2 realized hedge losses were $85–90M. Management states H2 2026 hedge P&L should be de minimis at current strip, which addresses the near term but is silent on the 2027–2029 short optionality: 15.74 MMBbl of 2027 WTI swaptions at $69.44, 10.81 MMBbl of 2028 at $65.81, 1.00 MMBbl of 2029 at $70.00, plus short calls at $80.51/$77.32/$85.00 and short Henry Hub swaptions of 28.6/37.5/30.3 Bcf. The 2027 oil swaption volume alone is roughly 43 Mbbl/d against guided oil production near 68 Mbo/d. In fairness, selling this optionality generated premium that subsidized NOG's floors, and the volumes are a minority of expected production; the premium received was not disclosed, so no judgment on pricing is warranted.

**Gas realizations and the mix.** 72% of Henry Hub in Q1 2026, negative Waha cash prices, and Waha basis swaps at −$0.84 to −$1.01/MMBtu that provide partial rather than full protection. Appalachian reserves are 1% oil.

**Further impairments.** Management explicitly warns additional ceiling-test charges are possible. The trailing-average mechanic cuts both ways: sustained higher post-March oil raises the ceiling over time.

**Leverage drift and dividend coverage.** 1.56x versus a 1.0x target, before Duvernay cash of roughly US$171M and Q2 buybacks. Dividends of ~$173M (FY2025 basis) are covered ~1.5x by organic free cash flow of ~$581.6M; full buyback utilization would require debt.

**Operator dependence, Dakota Access (Record of Decision pending; Williston is 28–30% of production), and new Canadian FX/tax/egress exposure** through NOG Energy Canada round out the specific list. No Canada- or FX-specific risk factor had been added as of the Q1 filing because the agreement post-dates it.

**Short interest.** 21.1% of float, 4.8 days to cover. This cuts both directions: it means sophisticated capital shares the bear view, and it means any positive catalyst produces violent upside. It substantially raises the cost of expressing a negative view.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 50.1% is rich against 45.7% realized, with the term structure in backwardation (50.1% at 13 days versus 45.9% at six months) and an implied move of ±8.1% on the August 21 expiry. Skew is call-heavy at the front month (−7.6% on the source convention) with a put/call open interest ratio of 0.50, but flips to put-heavy (+10.4%) at the September 18 tenor, which also carries the highest implied volatility in the chain at 56.9% and a 15.9% implied move. Read together: the front month is positioned for upside, while hedging demand concentrates around the Q3 print and the September macro calendar. Since the July 22 snapshot, IV eased 1.6pp while the price fell 10.2% and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.44 to 0.50 — modest de-risking rather than panic. The absence of front-month put demand does not confirm the bear case, and the September put skew does not confirm the bull case; the combination is consistent with a HOLD.

**Risk factors score: 4.4.** The hedge-structure cap, gas realization deterioration, and leverage drift are specific, quantified, and only partially priced.

## Management Quality

The team is stable (no Item 5.02 event since December 2024), there are no restatements, and disclosure controls were reported effective. Alignment evidence is mixed but net modestly positive.

Positive: Director Bahram Akradi, the largest insider holder, bought 25,760 shares on the open market at $19.3999 on June 22, 2026 (~$500K), taking holdings to 1,713,444 — the only open-market purchase in the retrieved Form 4 window, executed within 7% of the current price. No discretionary open-market sales appear; the March and February dispositions are code-F tax withholding. CEO compensation actually paid for FY2025 was negative $3.47M under SEC methodology, and 45,239 TSR restricted stock units were forfeited in Q1 2026 for missed market metrics, so the plan has teeth in both directions.

Counterweights: CEO O'Grady adopted a Rule 10b5-1 plan on March 30, 2026 to sell 53,748 shares (roughly 20% of his position) through June 2027, adopted near $27.51. Under the standard I apply, a single pre-arranged plan of that size alongside record aggregate results is routine wealth management rather than a bearish signal, but it does dilute the insider-buy read. FY2025 repurchases at $29.25 are approximately 28% underwater. The 2026 sequence — issuing 8,288,289 shares at ~$27.50 in March, then repurchasing 2.95M at $20.37 in Q2 — is accretive per share, but that is a consequence of price movement rather than demonstrated timing skill. FY2025 TSR was −38.1% against −2.2% for the compensation peer group, roughly 36 points of single-year underperformance, though cumulative TSR since 2020 remains ahead (297 versus 243).

**Management quality score: 5.6.**

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($30, +43%).** Hormuz disruption persists, WTI holds in the $85–95 range, Waha basis normalizes and the ~7,000 Boe/d of shut-ins return. FY2027 EBITDA reaches $1.6–1.75B even after swaption exercise caps a portion of realizations. Q2's capex step-down proves durable, free cash flow funds the full $243M authorization plus the ground game, share count falls toward 100M, and net leverage returns to 1.2x. At 3.5x EV/EBITDA the equity is worth roughly $29–31. The 21.1% short interest amplifies the move.

**Base case ($22.50, +7.4%).** WTI averages $75–85, gas realizations improve modestly, FY2026 EBITDA lands near $1.5–1.6B and FY2027 similar as swaptions are exercised on 2027 volumes. Dividends of ~$190M and partial buybacks are funded from free cash flow; net debt holds near $2.6–2.7B and leverage stays 1.4–1.6x. At 3.25x the equity is $22–23. Total return including the 8.6% dividend is mid-teens.

**Bear case ($14, −33%).** Hormuz transit verifies as reopened, OPEC+ completes the rollback of voluntary cuts, and WTI reverts to $60–65. NOG receives no protection from the swaption layer because counterparties do not exercise below strike. EBITDA compresses toward $1.1B, ceiling-test impairments resume, leverage exceeds 2.3x, the buyback stops and the dividend comes under review. At 4.0x trough EBITDA on ~$2.9B of net debt, the equity is $14. The $17.18 52-week low, set in early July with oil already elevated, indicates how quickly this range can be revisited.

Probability-weighted at 30/45/25, price return is roughly +8%, and total return with the dividend is approximately +15% with wide dispersion. That is a fair-to-modestly-positive expectation, not the asymmetry required for a BUY or the negative expectation that would justify REDUCE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income). Timeframe: 12 months, with the Q3 print (late October 2026) as the first checkpoint.**

- Base case target **$22.50** (+7.4%): 3.25x EV/FY2026E EBITDA of $1.55B less pro forma net debt of ~$2.65B across ~107M shares.
- Bull case **$30.00** (+43%): 3.5x on $1.65B with net debt reduced to ~$2.5B.
- Bear case **$14.00** (−33%): 4.0x on $1.1B with net debt at ~$2.9B and a suspended buyback.
- Dividend contributes ~8.6% annually at the current $1.80 rate, covered roughly 2.3x by organic free cash flow.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of oil volumes flat-to-higher sequentially (Q3 above the 67.5–68.25 Mbo/d Q2 guide) alongside the ~7,000 Boe/d of Waha shut-ins returning to production;
2. Net debt/EBITDA moving toward 1.2x while the company executes at least half the $243M authorization from free cash flow rather than revolver draws;
3. Disclosure in the Q2 or Q3 10-Q showing the 2027–2028 short swaption and call volumes materially reduced or restructured, removing the cap on the high-oil scenario;
4. Price toward $17–18 with the dividend intact, which would push the base-case return above 25% and restore the margin of safety.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if:**
1. WTI sustains below $65 for a quarter, driving another ceiling-test impairment and net leverage above 2.0x;
2. Any reduction to the $1.80 dividend, which is the primary reason to own the shares at this multiple;
3. Q3 capex reverts above $300M without a corresponding oil-volume increase, indicating the Q2 free-cash-flow quarter was a timing artifact;
4. Confirmation of an adverse Dakota Access outcome affecting Williston takeaway.

**Verification gaps to close at the Q2 10-Q filing:** post-Duvernay net debt and ending share count; the FY2026 production guidance range (only the $0.9–1.1B capex budget is filing-confirmed); reconciliation of the conflicting third-party Q2 revenue figures ($670.8M versus $745.2M); the Utica purchase-price allocation, which assigns 100% of $464.6M to oil and gas properties despite the transaction including pipeline, water and treatment entities; and whether the Duvernay contingent consideration of up to CA$25M settles in cash or stock.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory (6.6 → 5.7 → 5.7 → 5.6 → 6.4 → 5.4) has been declining, and this report lands at 5.1. The decline is not a function of the 7% price move since the July report. Q2 actually delivered on several fronts: adjusted EPS of $1.13 beat the $1.05 estimate for a fourth consecutive quarter, revenue rose 16.8% year over year, capex stepped down to $190–200M, the repurchase authorization was raised to ~$243M, and the largest insider bought stock at $19.40. A 7% lower price on that news improves risk/reward at the margin, which is why the valuation score moves up from 5.5 to 6.0.

What holds the overall score down is a fundamental issue rather than a price one. Aggregate EBITDA has been flat for two years, was negative in FY2025 excluding the litigation settlement, and is spread across a share count roughly 9% larger. Per-share EBITDA is declining about 12% year over year despite ~$700M of 2026 acquisition spending. Meanwhile the equity's ability to participate in the one macro scenario that would fix the multiple — sustained oil above $80 — is contractually capped through 2029 by short optionality that a screen will never surface, while the same layer provides no floor if oil falls.

The offsetting evidence is legitimate and is why this is not a REDUCE: no bond maturity before April 2029, $1.2B of liquidity, a borrowing base that lenders raised in a falling-price environment, confirmed covenant compliance, an 8.6% dividend, a buyback authorization worth 10.9% of the shares at a 1.24x book multiple, and 21.1% short interest that makes any positive surprise violent. Existing holders are being paid to wait. New capital should wait for either the removal of the 2027–2029 upside cap or a price near $17–18. **HOLD.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the dividend was corrected to ~$173M (FY2025) at ~1.5x coverage against organic FCF (not "$192M... covered ~2.3x"), a materially weaker income-support figure than several places in the report assert; the HOLD (income) thesis leans heavily on that coverage claim.
