## Executive Summary

PACCAR is a premium heavy truck manufacturer trading at $116.07, sitting 12% below its 52-week high of $131.88 after a post-earnings selloff on April 28, 2026. The stock has appreciated 33% over the past 12 months as the market prices in a cyclical recovery from the 2025 trough — deliveries fell 22%, truck gross margins compressed from 13.9% to 7.5%, and EPS declined from $7.90 to $4.51. The recovery thesis is credible: freight spot rates are up 20%, build slots are full through Q2 with majority filled for H2, and the EPA 2027 emissions standard creates a prebuy incentive over the next 12-18 months.

However, the stock's current 20.3x forward P/E (on $5.71 FY2026E EPS) already embeds substantial recovery expectations and sits at a 35% discount to peer median P/E of 38.3x — a persistent discount that reflects PACCAR's cyclical earnings volatility rather than mispricing. Three consecutive quarterly earnings misses (Q3'25 through Q1'26) demonstrate that the recovery path is bumpier than consensus expects. At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced: upside exists if the prebuy cycle materializes strongly and margins normalize above 12%, but downside is meaningful if the macro cycle deteriorates under tariff/inflation pressure or if credit quality in the Financial Services segment continues to worsen.

The score of 6.7 reflects a genuinely good business at a reasonable but not compelling valuation, with identifiable catalysts that have not yet been confirmed by execution. The specific concern preventing a BUY: PACCAR has missed EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters by cumulative $0.11, $0.07, and $0.02 — a pattern indicating consensus may still be slightly ahead of realized performance. Until a clean beat confirms the margin recovery trajectory (likely Q2 2026 reporting in July), the probability-weighted return from current levels does not meet the threshold for a high-conviction BUY.

## Company Overview

PACCAR designs, manufactures, and distributes Class 5-8 commercial trucks under the Kenworth (U.S.), Peterbilt (U.S.), and DAF (Europe) brands. The company operates through three segments: Truck (68% of 2025 revenue), Parts (24%), and Financial Services (8%). With 10 manufacturing plants across 7 countries, $28.4B in FY2025 revenue, and a $61B market cap, PACCAR is the second-largest heavy truck OEM in North America behind Daimler.

90%+ of trucks sold in the U.S./Canada are manufactured domestically, providing a structural cost advantage under Section 232 tariffs (25% on imported trucks effective November 2025). The company's premium brand positioning, build-to-order model, and high-margin aftermarket parts business differentiate it from volume-oriented competitors.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2025 revenue of $28.4B represents a 15.5% decline from FY2024's $33.7B peak, driven by a 22% drop in truck deliveries (144,200 vs. 185,300). Q1 2026 revenue was $6.8B with deliveries of 33,100 units — annualizing to approximately $27.2B, suggesting the absolute trough may have passed.

**Margin dynamics:** Truck gross margins collapsed from 13.9% (2024) to 7.5% (2025) on volume deleverage, rising per-unit costs, and a $350M EC litigation charge. Q1 2026 showed initial recovery with consolidated gross margins at 13.1%, guided to 13.5% for Q2 — implying truck-specific margins approaching 8-9%. Full normalization to 12-14% truck margins requires deliveries above 40,000/quarter.

**Cash flow generation:** Even in a trough year, PACCAR generated $3.67B in FCF on $4.4B operating cash flow, representing a 6.0% FCF yield at current market cap. This speaks to the asset-light premium model and the Parts segment's countercyclical stability.

**Parts segment resilience:** $6.87B revenue (+3% YoY) with 24.3% pre-tax margins ($1.67B income) — nearly double the Truck segment's $870.8M. Parts now contributes over half of manufacturing pre-tax income during downturns, providing earnings floor protection.

**Balance sheet:** $9.5B cash/securities against A+/A1 ratings. The 1.3x D/E ratio is distorted by $15.7B in Financial Services debt (self-funding loan portfolio), not manufacturing leverage. The Altman Z-Score of 1.07 is a false signal from this captive finance structure.

**Capital return:** $2.72/share total dividends in FY2025 ($1.32 regular + $1.40 extra). At current price, the regular yield is 1.1%; including the extra dividend, effective yield is 2.3%. Management has historically paid extra dividends in strong years — the FY2024 payout was $4.17/share.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term recovery catalysts:**
1. **EPA 2027 prebuy:** The 35mg NOx standard effective January 2027 will increase per-truck costs. Historical precedent (EPA 2002, 2007, 2010) shows 20-40% pre-buy demand spikes in the 12-18 months preceding regulatory changes. Build slots being full through Q2 with majority filled for H2 is early evidence.
2. **Freight market recovery:** Over-the-road spot rates up 20%, contract rates improving. Fleet profitability recovering → capital allocation to truck replacement.
3. **Section 232 share gains:** North American build share reached 31.8% in Q1 2026 (vs. 29.9% in FY2025), above the company's 30% target. The tariff structure creates structural pricing support and cost disadvantage for import-dependent competitors.

**Medium-term growth path:** Management guides FY2026 U.S./Canada heavy-duty industry at 230,000-270,000 units (midpoint 250K) vs. 232,800 in 2025 and a long-term average of ~267K. PACCAR's $5.71 FY2026E consensus EPS implies 27% growth from $4.51, with FY2027E at $6.81 (+19%).

**Estimate revision momentum:** Positive but modest — current quarter EPS revised up +3.7% (30 days), next quarter +4.5%. Full-year revisions are +1.1-1.2%. This is constructive but falls well below the +30% threshold that signals strong forward momentum.

**Parts organic growth:** Guided 3-6% annually regardless of truck cycle, driven by expanding installed base (every truck sold adds 10-15 years of aftermarket demand). International expansion (350+ TRP stores in 99 countries) extends the addressable market.

## Valuation Assessment

At $116.07, PCAR trades at:
- 24.8x trailing EPS ($4.68 TTM)
- 20.3x FY2026E ($5.71)
- 17.0x FY2027E ($6.81)
- ~16x normalized mid-cycle EPS (~$7.00-7.50 based on historical margins at average volumes)

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 38.3x (WAB, CMI, CAT) suggests PCAR trades at a 47% discount on trailing earnings. However, this comparison is misleading — PACCAR's earnings are at a cyclical trough while CAT (46.2x) and CMI (37.2x) are at or near peak earnings, making their P/E ratios optically high for different reasons. On a normalized basis, the valuation gap narrows significantly.

**Historical context:** PACCAR typically trades at 12-16x mid-cycle earnings during troughs and 18-22x during recoveries. At 17x FY2027E, the stock is pricing in recovery but not fully pricing a strong prebuy cycle.

**DCF perspective:** Assuming mid-cycle FCF of $4.5-5.0B (FY2023 peak was higher), a 9% cost of equity, and 3% terminal growth implies fair value of $125-140/share — upside of 8-21%.

At 20.3x next year's earnings with estimates rising modestly (+4-5%), the stock offers potential appreciation if the prebuy materializes but limited margin of safety if the recovery disappoints.

## Competitive Landscape

PACCAR's domestic manufacturing footprint (Ohio, Texas, Washington) means 90%+ of U.S.-sold trucks avoid the 25% import tariff. Competitors with Mexican manufacturing exposure (Daimler/Freightliner, TRATON/Navistar) face higher structural costs, driving market share gains to 31.8% in Q1 2026 vs. 29.9% full-year 2025.

**Premium positioning intact.** Kenworth and Peterbilt command price premiums through build-to-order customization, driver comfort features, and fuel efficiency (Peterbilt 579EV, PACCAR MX engines). DAF won International Truck of the Year for its XG+ platform.

**Parts distribution scale advantage.** 21 parts distribution centers (added Columbus PDC in 2025), 2,200+ dealer locations, AI-driven demand forecasting, and same/next-day delivery capability create switching costs and logistics barriers that protect 24%+ margins.

**Vulnerability:** PACCAR is a pure-play truck company. Competitors like Caterpillar and Cummins have broader diversification across construction, mining, and power generation. In a severe freight recession, PACCAR has no offsetting segments.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical demand risk (HIGH).** Three consecutive quarterly misses demonstrate the challenge of predicting cycle timing. If tariff uncertainty slows economic activity, the expected prebuy may be delayed or muted. The wide industry guidance range (230K-270K) reflects genuine uncertainty.

**Financial Services credit deterioration (MODERATE-HIGH).** Past-due accounts doubled to 2.4% in FY2025 (Brazil: 4.6%, U.S.: 1.8%). Loss provisions up 65%. Loan modifications rose 18% to $1.175B. If freight rates reverse or tariff-driven recession materializes, collateral values (used trucks) and borrower ability to pay could deteriorate simultaneously. The $22.8B portfolio represents real balance sheet risk.

**Tariff uncertainty (MODERATE — two-directional).** Section 232 on trucks is a clear positive. But tariffs on steel/aluminum/components raise input costs, and IEEPA tariff legality is pending Supreme Court review. If broad tariffs slow economic activity enough to reduce truck demand, the share-gain benefit is partially offset by lower absolute volumes.

**EC litigation tail risk (LOW-MODERATE).** $950M already charged; settlements ongoing in multiple jurisdictions. Additional charges possible but likely diminishing in magnitude.

**Leadership transition risk (LOW-MODERATE).** New CFO (June 2025), new President (January 2026), multiple new division heads. Generational change during a cyclical recovery creates execution risk, though the conservative Pigott culture provides continuity.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty: 36.4% ATM IV vs. 26.6% HV (9.8pp rich), with a backwardated term structure (36.4% near-term > 28.5% 6-month). This backwardation typically signals pricing of an imminent event or resolution of uncertainty — consistent with the post-earnings repricing (PCAR reported Q1 on April 28, the stock dropped 6% that day). The put/call OI ratio of 0.69 leans slightly bullish. The IV spike from 30.8% to 36.4% on May 7 with minimal price movement suggests institutional hedging activity rather than directional positioning.

Section 232 drives sustained share above 32%. Parts grows 6%+. FY2027 EPS reaches $7.50+, and the stock re-rates to 20x = $150. Financial Services credit stabilizes as used truck values recover.

**Base Case ($125-130, +8-12%):** Moderate recovery unfolds per guidance. FY2026 EPS of $5.50-5.80 (in line with consensus). Gradual margin improvement through the year. Stock converges toward analyst median target of $125 over 6-9 months.

## Conclusion

PACCAR is a high-quality cyclical industrial at the early stages of a recovery, trading at a valuation that embeds recovery expectations but does not fully price a strong prebuy cycle. The 6.7 overall score reflects genuine quality (premium brand, dominant parts business, Section 232 structural advantage) moderated by near-term execution uncertainty (three consecutive misses, credit deterioration, leadership transition). At 20.3x FY2026E with modest estimate revision momentum (+3-5%) and an unconfirmed margin recovery trajectory, the stock needs at least one clean earnings beat to justify higher conviction. The options market's backwardated term structure and elevated IV confirm that near-term uncertainty is real. HOLD with a positive bias, awaiting Q2 confirmation.
