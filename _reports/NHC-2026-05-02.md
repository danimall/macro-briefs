## Executive Summary

National HealthCare Corporation is a post-acute and senior care operator running 80 skilled nursing facilities, 26 assisted living facilities, hospice/homecare agencies, and behavioral health hospitals across 9 Southeastern and Midwestern states. The company has delivered extraordinary operational improvement over the past three years: revenue grew from $1.03B (FY2022) to $1.47B (FY2025), operating income quadrupled from $31.9M to $128.4M, and free cash flow reached $148.6M (10.1% margin).

On April 21, 2026, NHC announced a $560M acquisition of 35 properties it currently leases from NHI (National Health Investors), expected to close July 1, 2026. This transaction eliminates ~$39.7M in annual cash rent, removes the governance conflict between overlapping boards, and resolves a lease dispute that was an existential risk to the business. The stock rallied ~8% on the day ($160.61 prior close to $173.63 close, intraday high $184.08) and now trades at $174.86.

The primary risk preventing a higher-conviction rating is the financing mechanics of the $560M acquisition. NHC will need to raise substantial debt against a balance sheet that currently carries only $32.5M, moving leverage from 0.42x D/E to likely 1.5-2.0x. This is manageable given $185M operating cash flow and $39.7M rent elimination, but the specific terms, interest rates, and any equity component remain undisclosed. A secondary risk is the $121.6M self-insurance reserve, which generated $17.6M in unfavorable development in FY2025.

## Company Overview

NHC operates across the full continuum of senior care with a distinctive regional concentration strategy. The company differentiates on quality: 62.5% of its SNFs are rated 4-5 stars on CMS metrics versus 38.6% for the industry, with an average rating of 3.83 versus 2.95 nationally. This quality positioning supports a favorable payor mix (31% Medicare, 30% Medicaid, 27% private pay, 12% managed care) and premium rate capture.

The business model has low capital intensity ($36.4M capex on $1.47B revenue = 2.5%) and high labor intensity. The critical operational achievement of the past two years has been normalizing post-COVID agency staffing: from an estimated $30M+ peak to $4.7M in FY2025, with Q3 2025 at just $1.2M. This $25M+ annual tailwind is substantially realized and should be viewed as a durable margin reset.

The $560M NHI acquisition transforms NHC from a partial lessee (32 of 80 SNFs leased from NHI) to a full owner of its operating footprint. Post-close, NHC will own 75 SNFs and lease only 8 from third parties. This vertical integration eliminates a $39.7M annual cash outflow, creates unencumbered asset value for future borrowing capacity, and removes the single largest business risk (lease dispute/renewal uncertainty).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $1.029B → $1.088B → $1.252B → $1.470B (FY2022-2025). Three-year CAGR of 12.7%, driven by the White Oak acquisition ($228M revenue contribution) and same-facility organic growth of 8.4%. Occupancy improved sequentially each year: 87.9% → 88.6% → 89.7%.

**Margin expansion:** Operating margin improved from 3.1% (FY2022) to 8.7% (FY2025). The primary driver is labor cost leverage: salaries/wages as a percentage of revenue declined from 62.4% to 60.7%, with agency costs plummeting 67% annually for two consecutive years. Non-GAAP adjusted EPS grew from $3.55 to $6.65 over FY2023-2025.

**Cash flow quality:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $185.1M exceeds net income of $120.0M by 54%, confirming earnings quality. FCF of $148.6M represents a 10.1% margin on revenue. The company paid down $97M of the White Oak acquisition debt within one year of closing while simultaneously paying $38.7M in dividends and $14.7M in buybacks.

**Balance sheet (pre-acquisition):** Total debt of $32.5M, cash of $92.8M, unrestricted marketable securities of $163M (including $129.6M in NHI stock at 9/30/25 market value), stockholders' equity of $1.069B. Altman Z-Score of 3.8 (safe zone). Current ratio of 1.82.

**Pro-forma balance sheet (post-acquisition):** Assuming $560M funded via ~$450M new debt + $92M cash + $18M from NHI stock sale proceeds, debt would rise to ~$480M. Against FY2025 EBITDA of ~$170M (operating income $128.4M + D&A ~$42M), plus $39.7M rent elimination = ~$210M pro-forma EBITDA, implying leverage of ~2.3x. At 6% interest on $450M new debt, annual interest expense would be ~$27M, well covered by $39.7M rent savings alone.

## Growth Analysis

NHC has three distinct growth vectors:

1. **Organic rate escalation:** Medicare rates increased 3.2% net for FY2026. Private pay rates increased 6.8% in FY2025. The Medicaid supplemental payment decline ($20.2M → $12.7M → $7.2M) is partially offset by base rate increases of 3.5%.

2. **Occupancy recovery:** At 89.7%, occupancy remains below pre-COVID levels for the SNF industry (~92-93%). Each percentage point of occupancy on 10,329 beds generates approximately $10-15M in incremental revenue at minimal variable cost.

3. **Accretive acquisition execution:** The White Oak acquisition demonstrated NHC's ability to integrate operations successfully. The NHI property acquisition is operationally zero-risk (NHC already operates these facilities) and financially accretive from day one via rent elimination.

The hospice/homecare segment (Caris Healthcare, 10.5% of revenue) faces a CMS-proposed 6.4% rate cut for FY2026. This is a headwind but manageable given the segment's relatively small contribution.

Forward EPS trajectory: GAAP EPS of $7.67 (trailing) includes unrealized equity gains. Adjusted EPS of $6.65 (FY2025) should step up to approximately $8.50-9.00 in FY2027 post-acquisition (adding ~$39.7M rent savings pre-tax less ~$27M interest expense = ~$13M net pre-tax benefit, or ~$0.65/share after tax, plus organic growth of 5-8%). This implies 22.6x trailing compresses to approximately 19-20x FY2027 adjusted earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $174.86, NHC trades at 22.8x trailing GAAP EPS ($7.67) and approximately 26.3x FY2025 non-GAAP adjusted EPS ($6.65). The market cap is $2.73B against FY2025 EBITDA of ~$170M (16.0x). These multiples are not cheap in absolute terms.

Post-acquisition economics materially change the picture:
- Pro-forma EBITDA of ~$210M implies 15.1x EV/EBITDA (adding $450M debt to market cap, netting cash)
- Pro-forma adjusted EPS of ~$8.50-9.00 (FY2027E) implies 19-20x forward P/E
- FCF yield of ~5.5% on current market cap (using FY2025 $148.6M as base, growing to ~$160-170M post-acquisition after debt service)

The peer group for comparison is limited given NHC's private-company-dominated sector. Among publicly traded senior care operators, Ensign Group (ENSG) trades at ~25x forward P/E with similar growth characteristics. Sabra Health Care REIT (SBRA) at 12-13x FFO captures only the real estate, not the operating leverage NHC demonstrates.

The market may be mispricing NHC: the acquisition announcement created a one-day spike followed by consolidation, suggesting the market has not fully modeled the pro-forma earnings power ($39.7M rent elimination minus financing cost = $12-13M net annual benefit, growing as debt amortizes and rates potentially decrease). The stock's 85% appreciation over the past year prices in the operational turnaround but may not fully capture the capital structure optimization.

## Competitive Landscape

**Quality differentiation:** 62.5% of facilities at 4-5 stars versus 38.6% industry average. This quality premium supports preferred network inclusion with managed care organizations and enables above-market rate increases.

**Internal talent pipeline:** The 24-month Administrator in Training program has produced 59 of 80 facility leaders. This reduces administrator turnover (a key driver of facility performance) and creates a sustainable competitive advantage in an industry with chronic leadership shortages.

**Scale benefits:** 80 facilities across 9 states provide purchasing leverage, shared services efficiencies, and regulatory expertise that smaller operators cannot match. The post-acquisition asset base of ~$2B in owned real estate provides significant collateral value.

**Labor market advantage:** NHC's reduction of agency staffing by 67% annually for two years demonstrates superior workforce management. Zero unionized employees across 15,278 staff. The CMS staffing rule suspension through 2034 removes a regulatory overhang that would have disproportionately pressured smaller operators.

**Occupancy trajectory:** Improving from 87.9% to 89.7% while the industry average recovery has been slower, suggesting market share gains or superior discharge/readmission management.

## Risk Assessment

### Financing Risk (Near-term, High)
In a 4.42% 10Y environment, acquisition financing for a mid-cap healthcare operator likely runs 5.5-7.0%. At the high end, $450M at 7% = $31.5M annual interest, nearly offsetting the $39.7M rent savings in year one. Amortization and rate normalization would improve this over time, but year-one accretion could disappoint if rates are punitive.

### Self-Insurance Reserve Volatility (Ongoing, Moderate)
NHC operates two captive insurance subsidiaries. Claims are lumpy and difficult to predict. A bad year could add $20-30M in unexpected expense.

### Government Reimbursement (Structural, Moderate)
61% Medicare/Medicaid revenue creates perpetual rate risk. Medicaid supplemental payments already declining ($20.2M → $7.2M over three years). CMS sequestration continues through 2030.

### Concentration in NHI Stock ($129.6M)
Post-acquisition, NHC may sell this position (it becomes a non-strategic holding), but any forced liquidation at an unfavorable time could impact book value. This also creates GAAP earnings volatility via unrealized mark-to-market.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term IV of 34.3% sits 3.8pp above 1-year historical vol (30.4%), with term structure in backwardation (near-term 34.3% > long-term 31.6%). This is consistent with the market pricing execution risk around the July 1 acquisition close. The 12-day implied move of ±5.5% (~$165-$185 range) brackets current price action. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.3%, suggesting no directional consensus among options participants. Short interest at 6.0% with 5.8 days to cover is modestly elevated but not extreme.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($210-225, +20-29%)
Acquisition closes on schedule at sub-6% financing. Pro-forma EBITDA runs to $210-220M by FY2027. Occupancy continues improving toward 92%. Organic revenue growth of 6-8% compounds through rate increases and managed care expansion. Agency staffing costs remain negligible. NHC sells NHI stock post-close, using proceeds for debt reduction. The market re-rates NHC to 25x forward adjusted EPS ($8.75) = $219.

### Bear Case ($135-145, -17-23%)
Financing comes at 7%+ due to credit market stress, compressing year-one accretion to near-zero. Self-insurance reserves develop unfavorably ($25M+ charge). Medicaid rate increases lag wage inflation, compressing margins. The market applies a leverage penalty given the step-change from 0.42x to 2.0x+ D/E. At 18x a more conservative $7.75 adjusted EPS = $140.

### Base Case ($195-210, +12-20%)
Acquisition closes on schedule. Financing at 5.5-6.5% provides meaningful year-one accretion ($8-12M net pre-tax). Occupancy reaches 90.5% by FY2027. Revenue grows organically at 5-7%. Agency costs stable at $4-5M. Adjusted EPS of $8.50-9.00 by FY2027. Market applies 23x multiple = $195-207.

## Conclusion

NHC warrants a BUY rating with 2-3% portfolio sizing. The business has delivered four consecutive years of accelerating profitability and cash flow improvement. The $560M NHI property acquisition eliminates $39.7M in annual rent, removes the company's largest single risk factor (lease dispute), and creates a fully-owned asset base with significant collateral value. Pro-forma economics compress the effective forward multiple to 19-20x on conservative estimates, with upside to 25x if the market rewards the integrated owner-operator model.

The primary risk is financing a $560M acquisition for a $2.7B market cap company in a tight credit environment. Position sizing reflects this uncertainty: the stock deserves exposure given the quality of the underlying business and the catalytic nature of the transaction, but full conviction awaits financing term disclosure. The July 1, 2026 close date provides a near-term de-risking event that should either confirm the thesis or signal need for reassessment.
