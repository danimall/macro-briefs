## Executive Summary

Keurig Dr Pepper is executing one of the most complex corporate transformations in Consumer Staples history. Having closed the $18.4B JDE Peet's acquisition on April 1, 2026, the company simultaneously announced plans to separate into two independent publicly traded companies — a pure-play North American refreshment beverage company and the world's largest global coffee company. The stock trades at $28.56, down 21% from its 52-week high of $35.94 and near levels that imply approximately 12x forward earnings on consolidated 2026 guidance.

The investment thesis hinges on whether the planned separation (targeting readiness by end-2026) can unlock a sum-of-parts valuation premium. The refreshment beverage business is thriving: 28.2% operating margins, double-digit revenue growth, and the Dr Pepper brand gaining share against Pepsi. A standalone beverages entity could reasonably command a 20-24x P/E, implying meaningful upside from current levels. However, the magnitude of execution risk (integration, spin-off, new financing structures, credit agency scrutiny), combined with U.S. Coffee secular decline and near-term liquidity strain, creates a genuinely uncertain outcome distribution that prevents higher-conviction positioning.

At current prices, KDP offers a specific, identifiable catalyst (separation) with a defined timeline (end-2026 readiness) that could deliver 15-25% upside if executed successfully. This catalyst, combined with a 3.2% dividend yield and the Q1 2026 earnings beat demonstrating continued operational momentum, justifies holding the position through the transformation window rather than exiting at what may prove to be trough valuation.

## Company Overview

Keurig Dr Pepper operates across three segments: U.S. Refreshment Beverages (63% of 2025 revenue), U.S. Coffee (24%), and International (13%). The portfolio includes 125+ brands spanning carbonated soft drinks (Dr Pepper, 7UP, Canada Dry, A&W), water (Core Hydration), energy (GHOST), coffee (Green Mountain, Keurig single-serve system), and numerous other categories.

The April 2026 JDE Peet's acquisition added global coffee brands including Douwe Egberts, L'OR, Peet's Coffee, Jacobs, Senseo, and Tassimo, creating the world's largest pure-play coffee company. Combined 2026 revenue guidance stands at $25.9-26.4B. The company employs a hybrid distribution model combining Direct Store Delivery (DSD), warehouse direct, and concentrate-to-bottlers.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Growth Drivers:**
1. GHOST energy brand integration — contributed 3.8pp to consolidated volume/mix in 2025, with the energy category growing mid-teens
2. Dr Pepper market share gains in U.S. CSD (reportedly surpassing Pepsi in some share metrics)
3. JDE Peet's consolidation adding ~$9B in revenue from April 2026 forward
4. International expansion through JDE Peet's global distribution

**Growth Challenges:**
1. U.S. Coffee in secular decline: K-Cup volumes -4.8%, appliances -19.9%. Household penetration appears to have peaked.
2. Green coffee price inflation (+20%) compressing margins and driving volume elasticity
3. Tariff headwinds on brewers (manufactured in Asia) and imported ingredients
4. GLP-1 weight loss drugs cited as emerging consumption headwind for beverages

**Estimate Trajectory:**
- Current year (2026) EPS: $2.29 (flat vs. 30 days ago, +5.3% vs. 90 days ago)
- Next year (2027) EPS: $2.53 (+9.4% vs. 90 days ago)
- Current quarter estimate: $0.54 (down 5.8% in 30 days — negative near-term momentum)

The 90-day revision trend is modestly positive (+5-9% for full-year estimates), but the 30-day negative revision in the current quarter suggests analysts are absorbing JDE Peet's integration costs. This does not constitute the >30% estimate revision velocity that would signal a strong BUY.

**Separation as Growth Catalyst:**
The planned spin-off is the primary growth catalyst. A standalone refreshment beverage company (ex-coffee) growing at 8-12% organically with 28%+ operating margins would likely trade at a meaningful premium to current consolidated multiples. The coffee company, as the world's largest pure-play, could attract a dedicated investor base despite near-term volume headwinds. Combined sum-of-parts could exceed consolidated value by 15-30%.

## Competitive Landscape

**U.S. Refreshment Beverages (Improving Position):**
- Dr Pepper has been the top-performing major CSD brand in the U.S. for multiple years
- GHOST acquisition provides meaningful entry into the high-growth energy category alongside Monster (distributed by KO's bottling network) and Red Bull
- Core Hydration gaining share in premium water
- Hybrid distribution model (DSD + warehouse + concentrate) provides flexibility that pure concentrate models lack
- Competitive trajectory: improving

**U.S. Coffee (Deteriorating Position):**
- Keurig single-serve faces competition from Nespresso, ready-to-drink formats, and cafes
- Appliance volume decline of -19.9% suggests household penetration may be stalling
- Pod volumes declining despite price increases — classic demand elasticity signal
- Competitive trajectory: deteriorating

**Global Coffee (Post-JDE Peet's):**
- Combined entity becomes world's largest pure-play coffee company
- JDE Peet's has strong positions in Western Europe (Douwe Egberts, Jacobs, L'OR)
- Scale advantages in procurement (green coffee purchasing power)
- However, JDE Peet's organic growth was entirely price-driven (-4% volume offset by +20% pricing in 2025)
- Russia exposure (6% of JDE Peet's revenue) creates ESG and sanctions risk

## Risk Assessment

**High-Priority Risks:**

1. **Separation Execution (Probability: material).** The planned spin-off requires tax opinions, SEC filings, standalone audited financials, maintaining investment grade for both entities, and Board approval. The convertible preferred holders (KKR/Apollo) have consent rights and pro forma leverage caps (4.0-4.25x). Any delay or cancellation would likely send the stock to $24-25.

2. **Credit Downgrade (Probability: 30-40%).** A downgrade would raise borrowing costs, disrupt the commercial paper program, and create forced selling from investment-grade-only mandates. The 0.64 current ratio and $3.1B short-term debt create refinancing pressure.

3. **Coffee Secular Decline Acceleration.** If K-Cup volumes decline at -5% annualized and appliance volumes at -20%, the coffee business value erodes materially. The spin-off could crystallize this value destruction by forcing standalone disclosure.

4. **Dilution from Convertible Preferred.** The $4.5B in convertible preferred stock (4.75% coupon) held by KKR and Apollo converts to common stock under certain conditions. This represents approximately $213M in annual preferred dividends reducing free cash flow available to common shareholders, plus conversion dilution risk.

5. **Goodwill/Intangible Impairment.** $1.1B of brand intangibles have zero headroom at current discount rates. A 50bp rate increase implies ~$130M in additional impairments. In an elevated rate environment, further non-cash charges are probable.

**Options Market Signal:**
- Near-term IV at 24.6% vs. 28.0% historical volatility = options are cheap (-3.4pp discount)
- Put/call OI ratio of 0.34 = low structural put demand relative to calls
- Call-heavy skew (-3.1%) suggests the options market is marginally bullish
- Flat term structure indicates no specific event being priced beyond normal uncertainty
- Cheap IV relative to HV suggests the market is not positioning for further downside shocks, which is modestly supportive for holding

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33-36, +15-26% upside):**
- Separation executes on schedule (end-2026 readiness)
- Refreshment beverages business re-rates toward peer multiples (20-22x)
- GHOST brand scales to $500M+ revenue, validating energy category entry
- Credit agencies affirm investment grade ratings for both entities
- 20-22x on ~$1.50 beverage EPS = $30-33 for beverages alone; coffee adds $3-5/share

**Bear Case ($22-24, -16-23% downside):**
- Separation delayed or cancelled due to integration difficulties or credit constraints
- Credit downgrade triggers forced selling and higher borrowing costs
- Coffee volume decline accelerates, requiring additional impairments
- Convertible preferred conversion dilutes common shareholders 10-15%
- KKR/Apollo exercise governance rights that constrain management flexibility

**Base Case ($31-33, +9-15% upside over 12 months):**
- Separation proceeds with some delays (mid-2027 vs. end-2026)
- Operational performance continues at current trajectory (beverages strong, coffee flat to declining)
- Credit ratings maintained at current level (no upgrade, no downgrade)
- Dividend maintained; buybacks remain suspended through separation
- Gradual re-rating as market gains confidence in separation path

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**

2. Credit agency removes negative watch/CreditWatch without downgrade

**Reassessment Timeline:**

## Conclusion

KDP presents a genuine growth catalyst (corporate separation) with a defined timeline that could unlock 15-25% upside from current levels. The U.S. Refreshment Beverages business is performing at peer-leading levels, and the stock's current ~12.5x forward P/E embeds a significant execution discount relative to KO (24.9x) and PEP (24.5x). The 3.2% dividend yield provides income support while waiting.

However, the execution complexity is extraordinary: simultaneous integration of an $18.4B acquisition, preparation for a corporate separation, management of $4.5B in convertible preferred stock with sophisticated governance provisions from KKR/Apollo, credit rating pressure, and secular decline in the coffee segment. The current ratio of 0.64 and elevated short-term debt create near-term liquidity pressure atypical for a Consumer Staples company.

This is a HOLD — specifically a growth-catalyst hold where the identified catalyst (separation) could deliver re-rating within 12-18 months, but the execution uncertainty and current financial strain prevent the conviction level required for a BUY recommendation. The stock merits holding for investors already positioned, with potential upgrade as the separation timeline becomes more concrete through formal regulatory filings and credit agency resolution.
