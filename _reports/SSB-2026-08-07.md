## Executive Summary

SouthState is one of the better-run mid-cap Sunbelt regionals: $68.9B of assets across Florida, the Carolinas, Texas, Georgia, Colorado, Alabama and Virginia, generating a 1.36% return on average assets, a 17.6% return on average tangible common equity, and 6bp of annualized net charge-offs in Q2 2026. It also owns something few $50–100B banks own — a correspondent banking and capital markets division serving 1,200-plus community financial institutions nationwide, plus specialty verticals (Corporate Billing transportation factoring, SBA 7(a) origination-and-distribute, equipment finance) that carry different credit cycles from the core book. Fee income grew 14.1% in the first half against flat net interest income, which is the correct direction of travel for a bank facing spread compression.

The central analytical question is whether core earnings are replacing the purchase-accounting accretion from the January 1, 2025 Independent Bank Group acquisition fast enough. On the evidence, yes: loan discount accretion into interest income fell from $125.3M in H1 2025 to $71.8M in H1 2026 (−42.7%), yet adjusted net income excluding after-tax accretion rose from roughly $358.6M to $400.4M (+11.7%), and on a per-share basis from about $3.52 to $4.10 (+16.4%). That calculation is derived, not disclosed, and uses each period's blended effective tax rate — but the direction survives reasonable alternative assumptions. It is the strongest argument against the view that SouthState's earnings are an accounting artifact.

Three facts argue the other way, and all are equally verifiable. First, adjusted net income was $230.0M in Q2 2026 versus $233.8M in Q2 2025 — a 1.6% decline; adjusted EPS rose ~2.6% solely because diluted shares fell 4.2%. Second, commercial real estate concentration rose from 271.8% to 282.6% of Bank Tier 1 capital plus allowance in six months while construction and land development went 35.2% to 40.4%, and allowance coverage thinned from 1.20% to 1.15% of loans even as quarterly provision expense tripled from $5.1M to $15.9M. Third, trailing-twelve-month loan modifications for borrowers in financial difficulty rose roughly eight-fold to about $335.7M, concentrated in non-owner-occupied CRE including roughly $135.3M with about seven months of deferred payments — while nonaccruals *fell*. Payment deferrals keep loans accruing and outside the nonperforming bucket. At 1.84x tangible book and 1.9% below its 52-week high, the market is not pricing a CRE credit event.

We move to HOLD with an income/capital-return rationale. The stock has advanced 13% since our June review while consensus estimates drifted slightly lower, compressing the margin of safety to the point where the probability-weighted return (~7% including dividends) sits at or modestly below a reasonable cost of equity for a CRE-concentrated bank.

## Company Overview

SouthState Bank Corporation is a Winter Haven, Florida financial holding company operating a single national bank subsidiary. At December 31, 2025 the company reported $67.2B of assets, $48.6B of loans, $55.1B of deposits, $9.1B of equity and 342 branches across eight states, with 6,190 full-time employees (up from 4,986). By June 30, 2026 assets stood at $68.91B, loans $50.85B and deposits $56.35B. The company redomiciled from South Carolina to Florida in Q3 2025 — a legal and naming change, not a combination.

The loan book is commercial-real-estate-centric: $28.0B of CRE (58%), $10.5B of residential (21%), $9.2B of C&I (19%) and $1.0B of other consumer (2%) at year-end, with roughly 78.5% of loans carrying real estate as primary or secondary collateral. That single fact drives most of the risk analysis below.

The defining corporate event was the January 1, 2025 acquisition of Independent Bank Group, which added approximately $13.1B of loans and $15.2B of deposits, extended the footprint into Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, Austin and Colorado, lifted goodwill from $1.92B to $3.09B, and increased diluted shares from roughly 76.8M to 101.5M. Integration is finished: merger, branch consolidation and severance expense ran $92.4M in H1 2025, $20.9M in Q3, $4.5M in Q4, and zero in both Q1 and Q2 2026. GAAP and adjusted results now converge, which removes an earnings-quality overhang and makes the current run rate readable.

Management's stated strategy is unchanged and explicit: organic in-footprint growth plus opportunistic team hiring, supplemented by strategic mergers or branch expansion within or contiguous to the footprint. This is a serial acquirer by design and should be underwritten as one.

## Financial Analysis

**Standard screening tools do not work on this issuer, and we discarded them.** Altman Z-Score is constructed for non-financial manufacturers using working capital and sales-to-assets turnover — neither is meaningful for a depository whose liabilities are its product. Separately, one XBRL aggregation returned total assets of roughly $3.17B against the $67.2B in the audited balance sheet, an obvious tag error. Every figure below comes from the filings, the July 23 earnings release, or arithmetic on those inputs. The appropriate solvency lens is regulatory capital, tangible common equity, liquidity and credit coverage.

**Profitability (Q2 2026).** Net income $230.0M; diluted EPS $2.35; ROAA 1.36%; ROE 10.2%; ROATCE 17.6%; company-reported efficiency ratio ~50%. Our simple GAAP calculation — $357.7M expense ÷ ($575.9M net interest income + $96.7M fee income) — gives 53.2%; the gap reflects tax-equivalent revenue and the exclusion of intangible amortization in the company's convention. Both are defensible; they are not the same ratio.

**The revenue reality the EPS line conceals.** Net interest income was $575.9M in Q2 2026 against $577.9M in Q2 2025 — slightly *lower* despite loans growing $3.6B (8%) year over year. The quarterly path: $577.9M (Q2'25) → $599.7M (Q3'25) → $581.1M (Q4'25) → $561.6M (Q1'26) → $575.9M (Q2'26), with NIM at 3.78% in Q2 against roughly 4.06% in Q3 2025. That is accretion fade plus three 2025 Fed cuts working through asset yields faster than deposit costs. Management is externally reported to have guided to a stable 3.75%–3.80% NIM through 2027 with mid- to high-single-digit loan growth; we could not retrieve the transcript, so treat that as secondhand characterization rather than filed guidance.

**Operating leverage, stated honestly.** H1 2026 operating expense of $717.3M rose 3.7% against H1 revenue (NII plus operating fee income) of $1,334.4M, up 3.1%. That is slightly negative operating leverage for the half, even though Q2 improved sequentially on lower OREO and loan-related costs. FY2025's efficiency improvement from 56.9% to 53.1% was substantially accretion-assisted.

**The accretion arithmetic.** FY2025 recognized $258.6M of loan discount accretion; at a 23.22% effective rate that is $198.6M after tax, or roughly $1.96 per share on 101.5M average diluted shares — about 25% of FY2025 GAAP EPS of $7.87. H1 2026 recognized $71.8M; at a 22.79% rate that is $55.4M after tax, or about $0.57 for the half (~$1.13 annualized), roughly 12% of H1 diluted EPS of $4.64. The tailwind has already shrunk by roughly $0.83 per share annualized, and $185.9M of unamortized discount remains against $259.5M at year-end. Note the distinction: the $73.6M decline in the balance is not the $71.8M flow into income — the balance also moves on early payoffs and charge-offs.

**Capital.** CET1 went 12.62% (12/31/24) → 11.36% (12/31/25) → 11.12% (6/30/26); total risk-based 14.96% → 13.84% → 13.49%; Tier 1 leverage 10.04% → 9.26% → 9.39%; TCE 8.8% → 8.7%. The 150bp CET1 decline reflects the Independent deal plus buybacks. At 11.12%, CET1 sits roughly 412bp above the 7.0% buffer-inclusive threshold — a real cushion, on a clear downtrend.

**A tangible-book sensitivity.** AOCI was −$310.1M at June 30, and the held-to-maturity portfolio carried $314.7M of unrealized loss not reflected in equity. Marking HTM after tax: $5,694.0M tangible common equity − ($314.7M × 0.772) = $5,451.1M, or $56.21 per share versus the reported $58.72 — a ~4.3% overstatement on a fully-marked view, moving P/TBV from 1.84x to 1.93x. This is illustrative; SouthState has opted out of AOCI inclusion in regulatory capital as permitted, and HTM securities held to maturity never realize the mark.

**Funding — the nuance that matters.** Total deposits grew $1.20B in H1 2026. Brokered channels grew by approximately the same amount: brokered time roughly $1.7B → $2.3B and brokered interest-bearing checking/MMDA roughly $2.0B → $2.6B. Non-brokered deposits were approximately flat; excluding reciprocal deposits as well, the residual in-market base declined by roughly $0.3B, though rounded disclosures make that figure carry real estimation error (plausibly flat to −$0.6B). Cash fell from $3.17B to $2.35B and a $300M FHLB advance appeared where none existed at year-end; the loan/deposit ratio rose from 88% to 90%.

Management's account is deliberate substitution — letting higher-cost local deposits run off in favor of cheaper brokered funding — and the evidence supports it: total deposit cost was flat at 1.76% sequentially in a quarter when deposits grew, which is what successful cost-driven substitution looks like. The competing reading, that the in-market franchise is not growing and wholesale funding is filling the gap, cannot be dismissed, and the two are not mutually exclusive. Noninterest-bearing deposits were 24% of the mix at year-end, down from 27%. Uninsured deposits improved to 37.8% from 39.8%. This is margin management with a franchise-growth question attached, not a liquidity problem.

**Financial health score: 6.7.** Capital and liquidity are comfortably adequate, returns are top-quartile, and charge-offs are minimal. The score is held below 7 by the CET1 downtrend, the unmarked HTM loss equal to ~4.3% of tangible book, allowance coverage thinning while provision triples, and the fact that all first-half funding growth came from wholesale channels.

## Growth Analysis

Loans grew 8% year over year and about 9.3% annualized in H1 2026. Operating noninterest income rose 14.1% to $196.8M, with correspondent banking and capital markets the cited driver. Net investment in direct financing leases more than doubled from roughly $94.3M to $199.6M in six months — small absolutely, fast-growing and higher-yielding. Roughly $519M of SBA loans were pooled into trading securities in H1, confirming an origination-and-distribute engine rather than a pure portfolio lender.

Set against that: net interest income was flat year over year on an 8% larger loan book, and adjusted dollar earnings declined 1.6%. Consensus expects $9.48 for the current year and $10.38 for next (+9.5%), and 13–14 covering analysts have nudged estimates down modestly — current quarter −0.4% over 30 days and −0.7% over 90; next quarter −1.5% and −1.3%; next year −0.5% and −0.8%; current year essentially flat. There is no inflection signal here. The earnings surprise record is four consecutive beats, but the magnitude is decelerating sharply: +16.1%, +7.5%, +2.0%, +1.3%. That deceleration is consistent with the accretion fade compressing the cushion between guidance and delivery.

The mechanics of per-share growth deserve precision. Q2 adjusted EPS rose 2.6% on a 1.6% decline in adjusted dollars because the share count fell 4.2% (102.0M → 97.677M). Check: 0.984/0.958 − 1 = +2.7%. In H1, approximately 2.5M shares were repurchased at an average near $99.58 against a 2026 authorization of 5.56M shares (~5.6% of the year-end count), and shares outstanding fell from 99.14M to 96.97M. Buybacks vastly outweigh equity-compensation dilution: roughly 864,567 RSUs plus about 133,447 of maximum performance upside and 22,519 options total roughly 998,014 shares — about 1.03% of current shares, or ~1.02% post-issuance ownership dilution.

The forward earnings bridge over the next two years therefore looks like: roughly $1.13 per share of annualized accretion decaying toward zero, offset by mid-to-high-single-digit loan growth, low-teens fee growth, ~4% annual share retirement, and any NIM stabilization. Credit is the swing factor: normalizing net charge-offs from 6bp to a 25bp run rate on $50.85B costs roughly $96M pretax, about $74M after tax, or $0.76 per share. That is the principal risk to the $10.38 consensus for next year.

**Growth prospects score: 5.6.** Volume and fee growth are real; earnings growth is currently financial engineering plus one-time cost elimination, with a two-year accretion headwind still running and estimates drifting lower.

## Valuation Assessment

At $108.355:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/Book ($94.17) | 1.15x |
| P/Tangible book ($58.72) | 1.84x |
| P/TBV, HTM-adjusted ($56.21) | 1.93x |
| P/E on current-year consensus $9.48 | 11.4x |
| P/E on next-year consensus $10.38 | 10.4x |
| P/E on Q2 annualized $9.40 | 11.5x |
| P/E on FY2025 GAAP $7.87 | 13.8x |
| Dividend yield ($2.64 annualized) | 2.44% |
| Gross shareholder yield (div + ~4.2% retirement) | ~6.6% |

Against regional-bank peers at a 12.7x median P/E and 2.4% median yield (CFR 14.2x, WTFC 13.1x, UMBF 11.8x, ZION 10.3x, ONB 12.7x), SouthState trades at a ~10% earnings discount with a materially better return profile — 17.6% ROATCE and 1.36% ROAA are above the group. That combination is the bull case in one line: a premium-return bank at a discount multiple.

Two adjustments dull it. First, the ROATCE is accretion-assisted; a fully ex-accretion ROATCE would be several points lower, which narrows the quality gap that justifies paying 1.84x tangible book. Second, the annualizations flatter: Q2 contained zero merger or severance charges, 6bp of charge-offs against a provision line running at $15.9M and rising, and mortgage banking income that fell $3.4M sequentially on seasonality. A normalized case assuming accretion decays to zero over two years and charge-offs migrate to 20–25bp puts run-rate EPS meaningfully below $9.40 before offsetting core growth and further buyback benefit.

The stock is 1.9% below its 52-week high of $110.47 and 28% above the 52-week low of $84.48, having advanced 16.6% over twelve months. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument — the relevant point is that price rose 13% since mid-June while consensus estimates edged lower, so the discount to peers has narrowed on a stale-earnings base. Consensus targets are $105 low / $119.57 mean / $120 median / $130 high, implying −3.1% to +20.0% with the mean at +10.4%. Analyst targets function as floors on cheap, upwardly-revising inflection stocks; here estimates are drifting down and the mean target sits only 10% above spot, so we treat the range as a fair-value band rather than a floor. Analyst sentiment is flagged as degrading (one hold added over three months, now 12 buy-equivalents and 2 holds).

Our base case: 11.5x on a credit-normalized $10.00 next-year EPS (haircutting the $10.38 consensus for charge-off migration) = $115; alternatively 1.84x a mid-2027 tangible book of roughly $64 (13% growth) = $118. We take $118. Bull case $133 (13x on $10.75 with NIM holding at 3.75–3.80% and modification balances curing). Bear case $86 (roughly 1.35x tangible book, near the 52-week low) on a CRE credit event that converts modifications into charge-offs and forces provision building.

Probability-weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives +4.3% price return, or roughly +6.7% including the dividend. That is at or modestly below a reasonable cost of equity for a bank with 58% CRE concentration.

**Valuation score: 5.6.** Fair, defensible, and no longer asymmetric. A discount multiple on cyclically-flattered earnings is not the same thing as a margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's competition discussion is generic. What the filings establish is more useful.

The correspondent banking and capital markets division — 1,200-plus community financial institutions, credit unions and money managers served with fixed income distribution, hedging, loan brokerage, bond accounting, safekeeping and asset/liability consulting — is the most defensible piece of the franchise. It is capital-light, relationship-embedded, national in addressable market irrespective of branch footprint, and it supplies low-cost correspondent deposits. Structurally, it makes community banks SouthState's customers rather than only its competitors. Very few $50–100B banks have anything at this scale. Complementary assets include SouthState Securities Corp. (Memphis broker-dealer), Corporate Billing (transportation and automotive factoring, Decatur, Alabama), SBA 7(a) gain-on-sale, and SouthState PCM in wealth.

The footprint post-Independent spans Florida, the Texas Triangle, the Carolinas, Georgia and Colorado — among the strongest demographic corridors in the country.

Headwinds are shared across the industry and visible in the numbers: noninterest-bearing deposits fell from 27% to 24% of the mix in a year; deposit costs are sticky at 1.76% on the way down from Fed cuts; and the cleanest expression of competitive pressure is net interest income flat year over year on 8% loan growth. The in-market deposit franchise did not grow in H1.

One tailwind is strategic rather than operational: the 10-K observes that the DOJ appears to be assessing bank mergers under a looser standard than the 2024 Banking Addendum implied, and that supervisory intensity has decreased. For a serial acquirer with a stated M&A strategy, that is an opening — and symmetrically it raises the probability of another sizeable stock-funded deal, with the dilution and integration risk that implies.

Also worth registering: mix diversification and concentration are moving in opposite directions. Fee income grew 14.1% and the specialty verticals are scaling, while CRE-to-capital rose to 282.6% and construction to 40.4%. Each measure establishes only what it measures.

**Competitive position score: 6.4.** A genuinely scarce fee franchise and a premium footprint, offset by flat spread revenue and a core deposit base that is not growing.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate concentration — the dominant risk.** The interagency screening metrics moved the wrong way in H1: CRE (multifamily plus non-owner-occupied non-farm nonresidential plus construction and land development) went 271.8% → 282.6% of Bank Tier 1 plus allowance against the 300% guidance level, and construction/land development 35.2% → 40.4% against 100%. At roughly 11 percentage points per half, the 300% screen could be approached within about twelve months, pushed by two forces at once: CRE-led loan growth raising the numerator and buybacks reducing Tier 1 in the denominator. The 300% level is a supervisory indicator, not a hard cap — crossing it triggers heightened expectations around stress testing, risk limits, allowance levels and capital. Management retains flexibility to slow CRE origination, shift mix, or moderate buybacks. It is nonetheless a real constraint on the compounding math and it is not visible on any screen.

**The modification signal.** Trailing-twelve-month loans modified for borrowers experiencing financial difficulty totaled roughly $335.7M at June 30, 2026 against roughly $41.3M a year earlier — an eight-fold increase, overwhelmingly commercial non-owner-occupied CRE, including approximately $135.3M of "other-than-insignificant payment delay" (about 0.77% of that asset class) with roughly seven months of deferred payments. Modified loans on nonaccrual rose from about $10.1M to about $51.6M. Simultaneously nonaccruals fell to $270.6M and nonperforming assets to 0.42% of assets. Mechanically, a payment deferral keeps a loan current and out of both buckets, so rising modifications alongside falling nonaccruals is consistent with stress being deferred rather than resolved. These aggregates were compiled by summing 10-Q sub-line items and should be treated as approximate.

Three competing explanations are plausible and we cannot adjudicate among them: these may be prudent, recovery-maximizing workouts on well-collateralized cash-flowing CRE bridging rate resets; the H1 2025 base is artificially depressed because acquired Independent loans entered the modification disclosure population only after acquisition-date accounting; and the company reports most modified balances remain current. Contradicting the bearish read, criticized and classified commercial credits *improved* in H1, from $3,129.3M (8.49% of commercial loans) to $2,969.5M (7.70%), reversing a Q4 2025 spike. We draw no conclusion about management intent from the accounting treatment. This is the single most important item to monitor in the Q3 and Q4 prints.

**Thinning coverage against rising provision.** Allowance to loans went 1.31% (6/30/25) → 1.20% (12/31/25) → 1.15% (6/30/26); allowance plus unfunded commitment reserve 1.35% → 1.30%. Provision rose $5.1M → $6.6M → $10.8M → $15.9M across four quarters against $8M of Q2 charge-offs. Adding the remaining $185.9M of acquired-loan discount to the $586.7M allowance gives $772.6M, or 1.52% of loans — but that discount embeds a rate/yield component as well as credit, so 1.52% overstates true loss-absorbing capacity by an amount the disclosures do not permit isolating. The directionally valid point is that combined coverage converges downward toward the stated allowance as the discount amortizes. Rising provision is also the arithmetically necessary consequence of 9%-plus annualized loan growth under CECL, independent of any credit view.

**Macro overlay.** The rate path is the transmission channel through which CRE stress would surface. Higher-for-longer policy rates support asset yields but pressure CRE debt-service coverage and property values simultaneously — precisely where the modification data would show up first — while any labor-market softening would be the channel to consumer and small-business credit. The specific timing and magnitude of any such macro stress is not established in the sources provided and is not something we can quantify here; the point is that SouthState's accretion runoff and the broader CRE refinancing cycle overlap in time. [unverified — specific rate levels, payrolls prints, and credit-spread figures are not in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**Other named risks.** Regulation II debit interchange — the 10-K states a finalized cap reduction "would likely materially decrease" noninterest income; unresolved. Geographic and catastrophe concentration in Florida and the Southeast, including property-insurance cost inflation that pressures CRE coverage independently of rates. Basel III endgame applies at $100B; at $68.9B and growing organically plus by acquisition, SouthState is on a trajectory toward it. Two regulatory items moved favorably: the OCC's December 23, 2025 proposal to raise the heightened-standards threshold from $50B to $700B, and the FDIC's deferral of the Bank's resolution-plan submission to 2027 — both proposals, not final rules. The February 2024 cybersecurity incident litigation received final court approval on June 24, 2026 with payment from cyber insurance in Q3 — genuine de-risking. A July 13, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K (director/officer departure or election) could not be retrieved; a senior credit or finance leadership change would be materially relevant to the modification question.

**Risk factors score: 5.0.** The specific, monitorable downside catalyst — CRE modification migration into charge-offs while concentration rises toward a supervisory screen and coverage thins — is not reflected in a 1.84x tangible book multiple 1.9% below the 52-week high.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 27.2% against 24.5% one-year realized — a 2.7-point spread that is fair, with IV in only the 33rd percentile of the current chain. Term structure is flat (27.2% near, 27.9% at six months), put/call skew is balanced at −1.5%, and the nearest expiry prices a ±4.7% move. Put/call open-interest data returned 0.00 and is not interpretable. The apparent collapse in ATM IV from 66.0% (June 17) to 25.2% (August 7) alongside a 12.9% price gain is almost certainly a stale or thinly-traded prior reading rather than a genuine 41-point volatility crush; we do not read a signal from it. Short interest is 3.5% of float with 3.4 days to cover — uncrowded, meaning informed capital is not positioned for the CRE thesis either. The takeaway: the options market is pricing an ordinary regional bank with no near-term catalyst, which is consistent with our view that the credit question is unresolved rather than imminent, and inconsistent with any claim that a repricing is being anticipated.

## Management Quality

The scoring here is forward-looking, and the evidence is mixed.

**Constructive.** The Independent integration was executed cleanly — merger and severance charges to zero within six quarters. The dividend went $0.54 → $0.60 → $0.66 (declared July 2026, payable August 14), a ~28% payout on annualized H1 EPS. H1 buybacks averaged $99.58, below the current price. Combined gross shareholder yield is roughly 6.6%. Tangible book per share grew 13% year over year. The February 2025 sale-leaseback paired a $229.3M pre-tax gain against a $228.8M securities restructuring loss — textbook balance-sheet repositioning that reset forward earning-asset yields without a capital hit; securities interest income rose from $178.0M to $188.9M in the half. The offsetting consequence is a permanent $70.3M increase in FY2025 occupancy expense from the new leases, so the economics turn on whether the reinvestment yield uplift exceeds the capitalized lease cost over time — which the disclosures do not settle. We infer no motive from the transaction structure.

**Cautionary.** CEO compensation rose 22.7% to $7.63M in 2025 while the proxy's own pay-versus-performance table shows cumulative company TSR of 147 against a peer-group 153 — modest underperformance on management's chosen benchmark. The primary disclosed performance measure, "Adjusted PPNR less net charge-offs," rose 69% to $1,217M in a year when net interest income included $258.6M of acquisition accretion. Whether the compensation committee neutralizes accretion is unverified; that verification determines whether this is a real alignment issue or a non-issue, and we raise it as a diligence question rather than a conclusion.

On insider activity: David R. Brooks, the former Independent chairman/CEO now on the SouthState board, sold roughly 14,300 shares in late April at ~$98.39 and roughly 30,800 shares across August 3–5 at ~$107.92–$110, retaining 341,686 in one reported account. The natural reading is post-merger diversification by a founder paid in stock — a pattern following virtually every stock-funded bank merger — and it is not on its own evidence of a negative view. More notable is the absence: across the 30 most recent Form 4 transactions reviewed, no open-market purchases by C-suite executives, with the remainder grants, vesting and tax withholding. A 30-transaction window is weaker evidence than a full-period search, but no insider buying with the stock within 2% of a multi-year high is a mild negative rather than a neutral.

Finally, repurchasing at ~1.84x tangible book is arithmetically tangible-book-dilutive per share relative to not repurchasing; TBV/share grew 13% despite the buybacks, from retained earnings and mark accretion, not because of them. Both statements are true.

**Management quality score: 5.9.** Competent operators with disciplined integration and generous, well-timed capital return, marked down for the comp-versus-TSR divergence on an accretion-boosted metric and the absence of insider buying near highs.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (20% probability, $133 target, +22.7%)

Ex-accretion core earnings are compounding at double digits and will fully absorb the remaining ~$186M of discount runoff. NIM stabilizes in the 3.75–3.80% band as deposit costs finally reprice down, restoring positive year-over-year net interest income growth in H2 2026. Fee income keeps growing low-to-mid teens off the correspondent, capital markets and equipment finance verticals, which requires no incremental capital and no incremental CRE. The H1 improvement in criticized and classified credits proves to be the true signal and the modification balances cure. The 5.56M-share authorization retires another 3–4% of the count. Applying 13x to $10.75 of 2027 earnings — a modest premium to the 12.7x peer median, justified by a mid-teens ex-accretion ROATCE — gives $133. A well-executed accretive acquisition in a demonstrably looser merger-review environment is optional upside.

### Bear Case (25% probability, $86 target, −20.6%)

The $335.7M of CRE modifications is deferred loss, not prudent workout. Seven months of payment deferral on non-owner-occupied CRE ends with sponsors unable to refinance, modifications migrate to nonaccrual (already visible: $10.1M → $51.6M), and charge-offs move from 6bp toward 40–60bp. Allowance at 1.15% is too thin for a 58%-CRE book, forcing a build that consumes two to three quarters of earnings; provision expense tripling on flat charge-offs suggests management already sees this. CRE concentration crosses 300%, drawing heightened supervisory expectations that curtail both loan growth and buybacks — removing the only current source of per-share growth, since adjusted dollar earnings are already declining. Accretion runs off on schedule with nothing left to replace it. At 1.35x a stagnant tangible book, the stock trades to $86, near its 52-week low.

### Base Case (55% probability, $118 target, +8.9%)

Modifications produce elevated but manageable losses, with charge-offs normalizing to 20–25bp — a roughly $0.76 per-share drag that partly offsets core growth. NIM holds near 3.75%, loan growth moderates to mid-single digits as CRE concentration approaches the screen, fee income grows low double digits, and buybacks continue at a slower pace. ## Conclusion

SouthState is a good bank at a fair price with one unresolved question that the price does not account for. The quality is real: 1.36% ROAA, 17.6% ROATCE, a scarce national correspondent franchise serving 1,200-plus institutions, a premium Sunbelt footprint, and roughly 6.6% of market capitalization returned annually. The purchase-accounting concern is largely answered — ex-accretion core EPS grew about 16% year over year, so core earnings are outrunning the fading tailwind.

What has changed since our June review is the risk/reward, not the business. The stock is up 13% while consensus estimates drifted slightly lower and the earnings-surprise cushion narrowed from 16.1% to 1.3% over four quarters. Adjusted dollar earnings declined 1.6% year over year with all per-share growth coming from share retirement. And the credit file now contains a specific, monitorable warning that no headline metric captures: roughly $336M of CRE modifications, up eight-fold, concentrated in non-owner-occupied CRE with about seven months of deferred payments, while allowance coverage thinned to 1.15%, provision expense tripled, and CRE concentration climbed to 282.6% of Bank Tier 1 plus allowance on a path toward the 300% supervisory screen. At 1.84x tangible book (1.93x adjusted for the unmarked HTM loss) and 1.9% below the 52-week high, none of that is priced.

The probability-weighted return of roughly 7% including the dividend is approximately fair, not compelling. Hold for the capital return and the franchise; add in the mid-$90s, or after the Q3 and Q4 10-Qs show modification balances stabilizing. If instead modifications keep building while allowance coverage thins, the multiple compresses toward book and the exit is at a materially worse price than today's.
