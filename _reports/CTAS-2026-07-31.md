## Executive Summary

Cintas closed FY2026 with the best operating results in its history. Revenue grew 8.9% to $11,264.8M (organic +8.3%), gross margin reached an all-time-high 50.7%, operating margin expanded to 23.1%, and diluted EPS rose 11.6% to $4.91. Free cash flow was $1,881.2M, a 16.7% FCF margin, with operating cash flow converting at 1.14x net income. Leverage is minimal: $2,428.1M of senior notes at a 4.1% average coupon against $3,119.4M of EBITDA (0.69x net), interest coverage of 24.5x, and a fully undrawn $2.0B revolver signed March 27, 2026. Return on equity of 40.7% on 1.11x asset turnover is exceptional for a route-delivery business, and gross margin expanded in every service line for a third consecutive year. This is a high-quality compounder operating well.

The investment question is entirely about price and one unresolved binary. At $206.79 the stock trades 42.1x trailing GAAP EPS, 38.1x the FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $5.43, 34.0x FY2028 consensus of $6.09, and 27.4x EV/EBITDA, for a 2.27% free cash flow yield and a 0.88% dividend. Guided adjusted EPS growth of 8.5-11.3% against a 38x forward multiple is a PEG near 3.8. A 2.3% cash yield on a business decelerating from 8.3% to a guided 7.0-8.3% workday-adjusted organic growth leaves little margin for multiple compression. The FY2026 10-K, filed July 29, quantified the UniFirst risks for the first time: a **$350.0M termination fee payable by Cintas to UniFirst** under certain circumstances, and management's expectation of "approximately $2.8 billion in additional indebtedness" taking consolidated debt to roughly $5.2B. Both parties received an FTC second request on June 11, 2026.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on CTAS, and the composition of that call has changed rather than the conclusion. Since the prior review the fundamentals improved (a beat-and-raise Q4, record margins, FY2027 guidance that leans on operations rather than repurchases) while the risk/reward compressed: the shares are up roughly 14% from $181.37 on July 5, taking the forward multiple from about 33x to 38x, and the 10-K put hard numbers on deal risk that previously existed only as an announcement. Base case $210 (+1.6%), bull $245 (+18.5%), bear $155 (-25.0%); the bear level is near where the stock actually traded on May 13, 2026, roughly two-and-a-half months ago.

## Company Overview

Cintas is a North American route-based business services company serving, per the 10-K, "more than one million businesses of all types and sizes." Over 95% of revenue is route servicing (a Cintas employee physically visits a customer site on a recurring schedule), recognized over time under a single performance obligation whose performance period "generally corresponds with the monthly invoice period."

Two accounting policies define the economics. First, Cintas owns the rented inventory: "uniforms and other rental items in service" of $1,276.2M is carried at cost less straight-line amortization over 18-30 months for uniforms and 8-60 months for mats, mops, towels and linens. Second, Cintas capitalizes incremental sales commissions: $400.7M of contract costs at May 31, 2026 ($96.0M current, $304.7M non-current), amortized over an eight-year weighted average with $105.4M of amortization in FY2026. Management's own accounting judgment therefore assumes customer relationships measured in years.

**FY2026 segments:**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform Rental & Facility Services | $8,621.6M | 76.5% | $2,077.0M | 24.1% | 23.5% |
| First Aid & Safety Services | $1,391.9M | 12.4% | $353.4M | 25.4% | 24.2% |
| All Other (Fire Protection $929.1M, Uniform Direct $322.1M) | $1,251.3M | 11.1% | $191.2M | 15.3% | 16.7% |
| Corporate (UniFirst transaction expense) | — | — | $(15.1)M | — | — |
| **Total** | **$11,264.8M** | **100%** | **$2,606.5M** | **23.1%** | **22.8%** |

Growth comes from three sources: pricing and penetration on existing routes, new-customer additions (including conversion of in-house programs), and tuck-in M&A — 30 individually immaterial businesses in FY2026 for $164.5M cash, $144.9M of it goodwill, with 21 of the 30 landing in the All Other segment (of which Fire Protection is the primary component).

## Financial Analysis

**Three-year trajectory:**

| Operating margin | 21.6% | 22.8% | 23.1% |
| Net income | $1,571.6M | $1,812.3M | $2,000.0M |
| Diluted EPS | $3.79 | $4.40 | $4.91 |
| Operating cash flow | $2,068.5M | $2,165.9M | $2,276.3M |
| Capex | $409.5M | $408.9M | $395.1M |
| Free cash flow | $1,659.0M | $1,757.0M | $1,881.2M |

Capex fell to 3.5% of revenue while revenue grew 8.9%; the route network is being leveraged, not rebuilt. Cash conversion of 1.14x (OCF/net income) is high-quality: $640.9M of non-cash addbacks (D&A $512.8M, SBC $128.1M) against a $422.1M working-capital drag concentrated in receivables (−$135.2M, growing 9.7% against 8.9% revenue growth, roughly in line), in-service uniforms (−$137.6M) and capitalized contract costs (−$174.0M). Growth consumes working capital here; that is the model, not an accrual problem.

**Balance sheet and the current-ratio red herring.** Total assets $10,529.1M, equity $5,139.9M, cash $289.0M. Debt is four tranches of senior notes: $1,000.0M at 3.70% (now current, maturing FY2027), $400.0M at 4.20% (2028), $800.0M at 4.00% (2032), $236.6M at 6.15% (2037). Net debt $2,139.1M, net debt/EBITDA 0.69x, total debt/equity 0.47x, total liabilities/equity 1.05x. A standardized screen circulating on this name reports debt/equity near 2.05, which cannot be reproduced from the audited balance sheet on any definition; use the filing figures.

The current ratio fell from 2.09x to 1.43x, which looks alarming and is not. The FY2026 cash flow statement shows zero debt proceeds and zero debt repayments; the entire move is the $1.0B of 3.70% notes reclassifying from long-term to current. Back that out and the current ratio is 2.28x. The maturity is nonetheless real cash, and it lands in the same fiscal year as roughly $2.9B of estimated UniFirst cash consideration, which is why the ~$2.8B of incremental debt and a bridge facility exist. The classification is benign; the aggregate FY2027 financing task is large. FY2027 guidance already embeds net interest rising to ~$105.0M from $101.2M, "primarily a result of the amortization of bridge loan financing expenses."

**Capital allocation.** FY2026: $701.5M dividends paid ($1.80/share declared, +15.4%), $952.1M of buybacks ($777.8M open-market at an average $196.38 plus $174.3M of tax withholding), $164.5M of tuck-ins. Total shareholder return of $1,653.6M equals 88% of free cash flow. Diluted shares fell 1.0% to 406.2M. An October 28, 2025 $1.0B authorization is **entirely unused**, and post-year-end repurchases through July 29 were only $48.4M at an average $199.70 — a sharp deceleration from a $778M annual open-market pace. Cintas is conserving liquidity for the merger. Because diluted EPS is the company-selected compensation measure, this removes one of two EPS levers and means guided 8.5-11.3% adjusted EPS growth rests almost entirely on operations. That makes the guide higher-quality even as it removes a support.

**Two margin-quality caveats.** Estimated legal reserves fell from $55,541K to $5,650K, a ~$49.9M swing. If that was released to income rather than paid out, it flattered FY2026 operating margin by up to ~44bp on $11.26B of revenue. The filings reviewed do not distinguish the two, and reserve releases on resolved matters are routine, so this should be treated as an open question rather than an allegation. Separately, the 20.2% effective tax rate — which FY2027 guidance assumes unchanged — includes a 2.1 percentage-point benefit from stock-based compensation windfalls. That benefit is share-price dependent. A drift toward ~22% is worth roughly 2.5% of EPS, which happens to be the same order as one quarter's guidance beat.

## Growth Analysis

FY2027 guidance: revenue $12.10-12.25B (+7.4% to +8.7% reported, +7.0% to +8.3% workday-adjusted on 261 versus 260 workdays), adjusted diluted EPS $5.36-5.50 (+8.5% to +11.3% over FY2026 adjusted $4.94), tax 20.2%, net interest ~$105M. Guidance explicitly excludes UniFirst, future acquisitions, future buybacks, and assumes constant FX.

The honest read is mild deceleration: the **top** of the FY2027 workday-adjusted range (8.3%) matches FY2026's actual organic growth. External commentary characterized the $12.18B revenue midpoint as roughly in line with, or slightly below, expectations; the shares' up-to-6.7% move on July 15 was driven by the margin and EPS print, not the revenue outlook.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current quarter +2.3% over 30 days, current year +1.1%, next year +1.1% (to $5.49 and $6.09). This is a business tracked by consensus, not a fundamental turn being systematically underestimated. The earnings-surprise record deserves attention: **one beat and three misses in the last four quarters** (−0.8%, −0.9%, −1.8%, then +2.9%). For a company whose franchise rests on execution consistency, three consecutive small misses before the Q4 beat suggests estimates had caught up to, and briefly overtaken, delivery. The Q4 "beat" is also a non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP comparison ($1.29 adjusted versus a ~$1.23 pre-release expectation); GAAP EPS was $1.26.

**UniFirst is the growth swing factor.** UniFirst adds roughly $2.5B of annual revenue on externally reported (unverified) figures, against Cintas's $8.6B Uniform Rental segment — meaningful scale in the core. But Cintas has published no verified synergy target, FY2027 guidance excludes the deal entirely, and Item 1A explicitly acknowledges the transaction "may not be accretive to earnings, may not improve our financial position, may not enhance our ability to reduce leverage." Route-density overlap in shared metros is the classic source of laundry-plant consolidation savings, and it is also precisely what an antitrust regulator examines. There is no publicly available pro forma EPS path, so the deal cannot be underwritten as growth — only as optionality with a quantified downside ($350M fee plus deal costs).

Longer term, the addressable-market argument (converting in-house uniform and facility programs) is real, and Cintas's own risk factors cut both ways: Item 1A lists "loss of customers due to outsourcing trends" and the possibility that "customers and prospects may decide to perform certain services in-house."

## Competitive Landscape

The filings disclose three competitive mechanics: price competition ("if existing or future competitors seek to gain or retain market share by reducing prices, Cintas may be required to lower prices"), competition for acquisition targets (which "can increase the price for acquisitions and reduce the number of available acquisition candidates"), and in-sourcing.

The financial evidence for a widening moat is strong. Gross margin expanded in all three service lines simultaneously (Uniform Rental 50.0% from 49.3%, Other 52.9% from 52.4%, Q4 consolidated 51.0% matching the all-time high) while revenue grew 8-9% organically. Simultaneous price realization and margin expansion across an entire portfolio is more consistent with route-density scale advantage and pricing power than with a commoditizing market. First Aid & Safety at 25.4% operating margin is now the highest-margin segment, up 120bp — a segment where Cintas cross-sells onto existing routes at low incremental delivery cost. The absence of any customer-concentration risk factor in Item 1A is a disclosure choice rather than proof, but for a one-million-customer route business it is consistent with genuinely granular revenue.

One counter-datapoint: **All Other operating income was flat in dollars at $191.2M versus $191.6M despite 9.2% revenue growth**, compressing margin from 16.7% to 15.3%. D&A rose to $27.5M from $22.5M and 21 of 30 tuck-ins landed there, with segment goodwill rising from $188.1M to $276.0M — most plausibly acquisition-integration drag and step-up amortization in Fire Protection (revenue +13.7%), compounded by a 2.0% decline in Uniform Direct Sales. The alternative reading is genuine pricing pressure in sprinkler and alarm inspection, which the segment note does not allow me to rule out. At 11% of revenue and 7% of operating income it does not change the case, but it is the single place margins went backwards.

The UniFirst combination is a horizontal merger between two of the largest U.S. uniform rental operators, leaving Vestis (the 2023 Aramark spin), Alsco and a long tail of regional launderers. I have no sourced market-share data for any participant, so the competitive-position conclusions here are inferences from Cintas's own filings. Consolidating the largest remaining independent public competitor removes both the biggest acquisition target and a bidder from a model that has depended on a supply of independent operators.

## Risk Assessment

**1. FTC second request — the dominant open item.** Cintas confirmed via the July 15 press release that on June 11, 2026 UniFirst shareholders approved the deal and "we and UniFirst each received a request for additional information (the 'second request') from the U.S. Federal Trade Commission. Both of these developments were expected." A second request is a formal in-depth investigation. It widens the outcome set to clearance, clearance conditioned on divestitures of overlapping plants and routes in specific metros, litigation, or abandonment triggering the $350.0M fee (17.5% of FY2026 net income). Second requests are common in large horizontal deals and most resolve with remedies rather than blocks; the existence of one is not evidence of intent to sue. I could not verify the FTC's substantive theory, any timing agreement, or any divestiture discussions — this is an explicit and material verification gap that no amount of financial analysis resolves. Management expects closing in the second half of calendar 2026.

**2. Pro forma leverage and the FY2027 financing stack.** Against standalone FY2026 EBITDA, the ~$5.2B pro forma consolidated indebtedness is 1.67x, before any UniFirst EBITDA — comfortably investment-grade-consistent leverage. But it stacks with the $1.0B note maturity and ~$2.9B of estimated cash consideration in the same fiscal year. The balance sheet can absorb this; the question is the cost and the loss of the buyback.

**3. Multiple compression.** At 38x forward with 2.27% FCF yield, a 20% de-rating requires no operational failure — only a change in what the market pays for high-quality duration. The stock traded at $161.16 on May 13, 2026, roughly 22% below spot, on fundamentals that were not materially different from today's.

**4. Earnings-quality sensitivities.** The 20.2% tax rate assumes continued stock-comp windfall benefits (2.1pp) that depend on the share price; normalization toward 22% costs ~2.5% of EPS. The ~$49.9M legal-reserve decline may have flattered margin by up to 44bp if released to income. The sole Critical Audit Matter is the $218.5M insurance reserve (workers' comp, auto and general liability), where the 10-K states plainly that "these changes in estimates may be material" and prior-claim development runs through selling and administrative expenses — a recurring channel of operating-margin volatility. The reserve rose only 5.1% against 8.9% revenue growth, so there is no evidence of a current problem, only structural sensitivity.

**5. Labor, unionization, tariffs.** The cost base is route drivers and plant workers; benefit contributions were $144.3M (Partners' Plan/ESOP/401k) plus $4.9M Canada and $15.1M SERP, all rising. Item 1A calls out "costs and possible effects of union organizing activities" explicitly. Tariff language was substantially expanded, including the admission that "competitive conditions or contractual arrangements may limit our ability to pass increased costs on to customers in a timely manner, or at all."

**6. Macro sensitivity.** Revenue scales with customer headcount and locations; Item 1A names "higher levels of unemployment" first among adverse economic factors. A rise in unemployment reduces garments in service per customer, so the volume side of the algorithm is the channel most exposed to a labor-market slowdown. Cintas's small-business customer base is more exposed to a credit-tightening cycle than the headline economy suggests.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 29.9% against 23.2% one-year realized volatility is rich (a 6.7-point spread), with the term structure in backwardation (29.9% at 1m and 30.3% at 3m versus 27.4% at 6m) — the market is pricing near-dated event risk, consistent with FTC newsflow and the Q1 FY2027 print. The put/call volume ratio of 1.19 and open-interest ratio of 1.08, up from 0.73 at the prior snapshot, show put open interest accumulating. The data source characterizes the skew as balanced, so I draw no directional inference from it: the market is paying up for volatility around a known catalyst without an obvious directional bet. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.1%, rising to 7.8% at three months and 13.1% at six — a distribution wide enough to encompass both the bull and bear cases below.

One reported change should be discounted: an ATM IV reading of 188.3% in the delta series is irreconcilable with the 29.9% current snapshot and is almost certainly a chain artifact rather than a genuine volatility event.

Short interest of 4.1% of float with 5.4 days to cover is uncrowded — no squeeze dynamic, and no informed bearish consensus either. For a HOLD at a premium multiple, rich near-term IV argues that selling covered calls into the FTC window is better compensated than adding to the position.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $245 (+18.5%)

Cintas is one of a small number of businesses that compounds mid-to-high single-digit revenue with annual margin expansion and almost no capital intensity (capex 3.5% of revenue), producing 40.7% ROE. FY2027 guidance excludes buybacks, acquisitions and UniFirst — every one of which is upside. The FTC clears the deal with modest metro divestitures in the second half of calendar 2026; Cintas adds roughly $2.5B of revenue onto existing route density, and the combination proves accretive within 18 months as plant consolidation flows through. The $1.0B unused authorization resumes in FY2028 once leverage retraces toward 1x. Estimates keep drifting up (+1.1% on both years over 90 days), and the market continues to pay 40x for a business with no customer concentration, 24.5x interest coverage and a demonstrated ability to price above cost inflation. $245 is 40.2x FY2028 consensus of $6.09, near the high end of the recent multiple band and just below the $250 street high.

### Bear Case — $155 (-25.0%)

The multiple does the damage. A 2.27% FCF yield re-rates toward 3%, which at unchanged FY2027 earnings is a 28.5x multiple and roughly $155, close to the $160 street low and only about 4% below the actual May 13, 2026 low of $161.16. The catalyst set is available: the FTC litigates or the deal is abandoned, costing $350M plus transaction expense and removing the strategic narrative; the tax rate normalizes toward 22% (−2.5% EPS); prior-year insurance development turns adverse; small-business employment softens and garments-in-service per customer fall, taking workday-adjusted organic growth below 6%. Three consecutive small misses before Q4 show how little cushion exists between consensus and delivery. Nothing in the balance sheet breaks; this is a de-rating, not a solvency event.

### Base Case — $210 (+1.6%)

FY2027 lands near the guidance midpoint of $5.43 adjusted, the FTC clears with remedies late in calendar 2026, and the multiple holds around 38-39x as investors continue to pay a premium for quality but decline to expand it further with buybacks paused and leverage rising. $210 is 38.7x FY2027 adjusted EPS. This is a total return of roughly 2.5% including the 0.88% dividend, with a 25% downside tail — a distribution that justifies owning but not adding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 FY2027 results (quarter ending August 31, 2026, typically reported late September) for the first read on whether workday-adjusted organic growth holds in the 7-8% range and whether gross margin extends past 50.7%. The FTC process is the second and larger clock, with management guiding to a second-half calendar 2026 close.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. **Price:** a decline to $170-178, which is 31-33x the FY2027 guide midpoint of $5.43 and a ~2.7% FCF yield — the level at which the premium is paid for demonstrated compounding rather than for multiple expansion.
2. **Deal resolution with economics:** FTC clearance (with or without contained divestitures) accompanied by a quantified, published synergy target and a pro forma accretion path. The 10-K currently warns the transaction "may not be accretive," and FY2027 guidance excludes it entirely; converting UniFirst from optionality into modellable earnings is worth a multiple point.
3. **Growth reacceleration:** workday-adjusted organic growth printing above 8.5% for two consecutive quarters, or FY2027 EPS revisions exceeding +5% over 90 days (versus +1.1% today), evidence that the deceleration implied in guidance is conservatism rather than the trend.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. Workday-adjusted organic growth falling below 6.0% in any quarter, or consolidated gross margin declining year over year — the two pillars of the 38x multiple.
2. FTC litigation to block, or termination of the merger agreement triggering the $350M fee, while the shares still trade above 36x forward earnings. Deal failure alone is survivable; deal failure at an unchanged premium multiple is not.
3. Adverse prior-year insurance-claim development or a tax rate above 22% causing FY2027 adjusted EPS to guide or track below $5.30, combined with the buyback remaining paused — the scenario where neither operating nor financial levers support the EPS algorithm.

**What to do in the meantime:** hold the position. Near-term implied volatility of 29.9% against 23.2% realized makes covered-call writing into the FTC window reasonably compensated. Do not add at 38x forward earnings ahead of an unresolved antitrust review.

## Conclusion

Cintas earned its FY2026 results: 8.9% revenue growth to $11.26B, all-time-high 50.7% gross margin, 23.1% operating margin, $1.88B of free cash flow, 0.69x net leverage, 40.7% ROE, and margin expansion in all three service lines. The FY2027 guide of 8.5-11.3% adjusted EPS growth is higher-quality than usual because it excludes buybacks — the growth has to come from operations, and on this track record it probably will.

None of that is the binding constraint. At $206.79 the market is paying 38x forward earnings and accepting a 2.27% free cash flow yield, for a business guiding to slower organic growth than it just delivered, with its largest-ever acquisition under an FTC second request, a $350M reverse termination fee, $2.8B of incremental debt coming, and its buyback authorization deliberately idle. The base case is a roughly flat twelve months; the bear case is a level the stock traded at only a couple of months ago.

Scores decline modestly from the prior review (5.9 from 6.2), driven entirely by valuation and risk: financial health and competitive position remain strong and the growth engine is intact, but a 14% move in the shares since early July against +1.1% estimate revisions, plus newly quantified deal exposure in the 10-K, compress the asymmetry. **HOLD.** This is a business worth owning at the right price, and $207 is not it. The place to become more constructive is $170-178 with FTC clearance in hand.
