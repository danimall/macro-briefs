**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Intercontinental Exchange owns one of the most durable cash-generation engines in the financial-infrastructure industry: 13 regulated exchanges, six clearing houses, the NYSE listings franchise, a $2.4B fixed-income data business, and an end-to-end mortgage-technology platform. FY2025 marked the 20th consecutive year of record net revenue ($9.93B, +7% YoY), with net income up 20% to $3.32B, adjusted EPS up 14% to $6.95, and a 60% adjusted operating margin. Recurring revenue is now 51% of the total, up from 34% a decade ago. On fundamentals alone this is a top-decile business.

The stock does not reflect that. ICE trades at ~$139.65, roughly 26% below its $189.35 52-week high, and has underperformed the S&P 500 sharply over the trailing year despite record operating results. The market is discounting three specific concerns: (1) the ~$2B commitment to Polymarket plus crypto-custody ventures that sit outside ICE's core and carry regulatory and reputational risk; (2) still-thin GAAP profitability in Mortgage Technology; and (3) sensitivity of clearing float income to a Fed easing cycle. Consensus EPS estimates are drifting lower — the current-quarter estimate has been cut 7.3% over 90 days — which is the clearest counterweight to the "cheap on record earnings" thesis. This is negative near-term earnings momentum layered on a structurally strong business.

At ~17x the $8.03 current-year consensus and ~16x the $8.73 next-year estimate, ICE is priced well below its data/exchange peer cohort (CME 22.4x, NDAQ 26.1x, MCO 31.7x, MSCI 34.0x). The gap looks like a mispricing of durable recurring cash flow, and Goldman's Buy rating (PT cut to $180) plus a 15-buy/1-hold analyst tilt support upside toward the low-$180s. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of falling near-term revisions, unresolved strategic-capital questions, and options markets pricing an imminent stress event (near-term IV in backwardation at 39.6%). We would upgrade on an estimate-revision turn or a pullback into the low-$120s where the risk/reward becomes clearly asymmetric.

## Company Overview

ICE operates three segments off a shared technology and data backbone:

**Exchanges (55% of FY2025 net revenue, ~74% operating margin).** The global energy futures franchise anchored by Brent crude, plus 800+ oil products, global natural gas/TTF, environmental/carbon markets, agricultural softs (coffee, cocoa, sugar, cotton), financial futures (rates, MSCI/FTSE indices), and the NYSE, where ~70% of the S&P 500 is listed and which holds ~75% of global ETF AUM (~$10.1T). Energy revenue reached $2.18B (+16%) on record volumes (+14%); total futures volume rose 13% and open interest 17%.

**Fixed Income & Data Services (24%, 39% GAAP / 45% adjusted margin).** Evaluated pricing on 3M+ securities, ICE Data Indices, ICE Bonds execution, CDS clearing (ICE Clear Credit, $24.9T notional), and connectivity. Annual Subscription Value is $1.99B, +8.3% YoY, a leading indicator of recurring growth.

**Mortgage Technology (21%).** The Encompass origination platform, MSP servicing, MERS, and Simplifile, built through the ~$11.4B Ellie Mae (2020) and ~$11.8B Black Knight (2023) acquisitions. The segment reached GAAP operating breakeven in 2025 ($14M vs. −$170M in 2024), with a 41% adjusted margin obscured by ~$779M/yr of acquisition-intangible amortization.

The moat rests on self-reinforcing liquidity in benchmark contracts (Brent, TTF, soft commodities), the NYSE listings network, a proprietary internally-built technology stack, and a systemically-important, regulator-entrenched clearing franchise. The lift in recurring revenue from 34% (2014) to 51% (2025) has materially de-risked the earnings stream.

The quality of the business is not in question. Adjusted FCF of $4.19B against a market cap of ~$80B is a ~5.2% FCF yield on a franchise growing high-single-digits with 60% adjusted margins. Six consecutive earnings beats confirm forecasting reliability: Q4'25 EPS $1.71 (beat), Q1'26 EPS $2.35 (beat +2.9%) on record ~$3B net revenue.

The balance sheet carries one critical caveat. Screening tools flag an Altman Z of 0.68 ("distress") and D/E of 3.73, but these are artifacts. ICE's balance sheet is grossed up by ~$76.8B of clearing-member cash margin deposits and guaranty funds, which appear as matched current assets and current liabilities and have nothing to do with corporate solvency. The Piotroski 5/5, 60% adjusted margins, and $4.2B adjusted FCF are the accurate read. Actual corporate debt is $19.6B, investment-grade, at a 3.7% weighted-average cost with ~14-year weighted maturity, and being paid down post-Black Knight.

Capital allocation has normalized after the 2023–24 buyback pause taken to deleverage: $1.3B repurchased in 2025 (7.7M shares), a new $3.0B authorization effective January 2026, and the dividend raised to $0.52/quarter from $0.48. Total FY25 capital returned was $2.40B. The dividend yield is modest at ~1.5%; the return thesis rests on growth plus buybacks rather than yield.

The one item that genuinely warrants caution is float income. A portion of clearing and CDS revenue is net interest income earned on collateral; interest income already declined in 2025, and a continued Fed easing path compresses this line. This is the same mechanism pressuring CME and NDAQ, and it is a real, non-trivial headwind to consolidated growth.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is mid-single-digit recurring (Exchanges and FI&DS both guided to mid-single-digit recurring-revenue growth for 2026), with transaction/volume upside layered on top when volatility is elevated. ASV at +8.3% and open interest at +17% support the recurring base. The energy franchise benefits from strong global hedging demand, which drove record energy volumes in FY2025 regardless of price direction — a structural positive for ICE's transaction revenue.

Three swing factors matter for the growth trajectory:

**Mortgage Technology recovery.** The GAAP-positive turn in 2025 indicates Black Knight synergies are materializing. Further upside is rate-dependent — origination volume remains suppressed by elevated mortgage rates. A sustained easing in the long end of the curve would help; a prolonged high-rate environment caps the recovery.

**U.S. Treasury clearing.** The SEC's February 2026 approval of ICE Clear Credit to add Treasury clearing (cash live, repo targeted Q4 2026) opens a large adjacent TAM in response to the SEC's clearing mandate. This is credible but faces entrenched incumbent DTCC/FICC, and adoption is unproven.

**Prediction markets / crypto.** The ~$1.6B Polymarket stake and ICE Digital Trust crypto custody are optionality plays into event-based markets and real-time data. Higher-risk, higher-reward, and outside ICE's core competency.

The near-term concern that keeps Growth Prospects mid-range is estimate momentum. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut from $1.98 to $1.84 over 90 days (−7.3%), and the current-year estimate is essentially flat (+0.2%). This is mild negative drift rather than the +30% velocity that flags a strong buy signal. The full-year estimate holding roughly flat while the near-quarter falls suggests timing/mix effects (transaction-revenue seasonality, float compression) rather than a structural break, but it does undercut any case for aggressive near-term re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$139.65, ICE trades at ~17.4x the $8.03 current-year consensus and ~16.0x the $8.73 next-year estimate. Against the peer cohort in Financial Exchanges & Data — CME 22.4x, NDAQ 26.1x, MCO 31.7x, MSCI 34.0x (peer median 31.7x) — ICE sits at a substantial discount despite comparable or superior recurring-revenue mix and margins.

The discount is partly explained by legitimate factors: mortgage cyclicality that peers lack, float-income exposure, and the market's skepticism about the ~$2B allocated to Polymarket/crypto. But the magnitude of the gap looks excessive for a business compounding net income 20% with 60% adjusted margins and a 51% recurring base. On the specific mispricing test the valuation framework demands: the market is discounting ICE's recurring cash flow at a multiple normally reserved for cyclical transaction-heavy exchanges, when the recurring share of ICE's revenue now rivals the pure data names trading at 30x+.

Applying a conservative 20x — still below the 22.4x CME comparable and well below the data-peer median — to the $8.73 next-year estimate yields ~$175, roughly in line with the $180 median analyst target and Goldman's revised $180 PT. The analyst spread runs from $159 low to $240 high on a $180 median. Because ICE trades at a compressed multiple relative to its growth and estimate revisions are stable-to-flat rather than collapsing, the ~$180 consensus should be treated as a reasonable near-term anchor rather than a ceiling.

The valuation score is held to 6.3 for a specific reason: cheapness alone has been an unreliable, often anti-predictive signal, and here the discount coexists with falling near-term revisions and an unresolved strategic-capital question. The cheapness is real, but it needs a catalyst — a revision turn, a mortgage-volume recovery, or clarity on Polymarket returns — to convert. Without one, ICE can remain cheap.

## Competitive Landscape

ICE occupies an oligopolistic industry with high barriers to entry, liquidity network effects, and recurring data revenue. Its diversification across energy, ags, rates, equities, fixed income, and mortgage means it does not depend on volatility in any single market — a structural advantage versus more concentrated peers. Secular tailwinds include demand for independent data, electronification of fixed income and mortgage, passive/indexation growth, and regulation-driven transparency.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than clearly widening. Energy franchise share is strong and growing. Mortgage Technology is consolidating share as it integrates Black Knight. But two fronts warrant caution. Treasury clearing pits ICE against a dominant incumbent (FICC), and success is unproven. EMIR 3.0's Active Account Requirement could pull euro short-term rate clearing away from ICE Clear Europe toward EU CCPs — a genuine share-erosion risk on a specific product line. The score of 6.5 reflects a strong-but-not-widening moat with two identifiable challenges to specific revenue pools.

The prior report trajectory shows Competitive Position stepping down from 8.5 to 6.5. That reset reflects the accumulating regulatory pressure (EMIR 3.0, Basel III Endgame, market-data fee scrutiny) and the contested nature of the Treasury-clearing and prediction-market expansions rather than any erosion in the core franchise. The current data confirms that reset: the core is intact, the incremental growth vectors are contested.

## Risk Assessment

**Clearing-house / CCP risk (structural).** ICE custodies $81.2B of member margin/guaranty funds and holds $82.6B of sovereign collateral ($72.9B U.S. Treasuries). A major clearing-member default or sharp collateral decline could impose losses beyond ICE's ~$381M skin-in-the-game and default insurance. Adding Treasury clearing inside the same ICE Clear Credit legal entity as CDS clearing introduces cross-contamination risk, which management flags explicitly.

**Regulatory pressure on multiple fronts.** Basel III Endgame (raises client-clearing costs), EMIR 3.0 (euro-clearing migration risk), EU energy-market intervention (TTF price caps), Bank of England CCP reforms (higher UK costs), SEC scrutiny of high-margin market-data and connectivity fees, new equity tick-size rules (compliance November 2026), and EU/UK ESG-data regulation. None is individually fatal, but the cluster caps multiple expansion and is a live overhang.

**Mortgage cyclicality.** Transaction-based mortgage revenue is levered to origination volume, still depressed by elevated rates. A prolonged high-rate environment caps recovery; GAAP segment profitability is nascent.

**New-venture execution and reputational risk.** The Polymarket stake (prediction markets under rising scrutiny) and crypto custody sit outside ICE's core; Bakkt (going-concern doubt, written down to $9M) is a cautionary precedent for ICE's ventures into adjacent digital markets.

**Goodwill/intangibles.** $30.6B goodwill plus $15.4B intangibles; a mortgage-tech impairment remains possible if the recovery stalls.

**Float-income sensitivity.** Falling rates reduce net interest income on collateral; interest income already fell in 2025.

The Risk Factors score of 5.5 reflects that these are largely known and partly priced (the discount to peers embeds some of them), but the Polymarket capital allocation and the Treasury-clearing/EMIR outcomes are genuine binaries with asymmetric potential that the market has not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing an imminent stress event. Near-term ATM IV is 39.6% against 24.0% historical volatility (rich by 15.6 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (39.6% near-term versus 31.0% at six months), which signals the market expects near-term volatility to exceed longer-dated, typical ahead of a catalyst (the next earnings print). Put/call skew is balanced (2.6%), and the six-month tenor shows negative skew (−3.7%), implying no pronounced downside hedging demand further out. Short interest is negligible at 2.1% of float (1.9 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The net read: the market is bracing for a near-term move but not positioning heavily bearish, consistent with a stock digesting falling near-term estimates rather than pricing structural collapse. The rich near-term IV also means options are an expensive way to express a directional view here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($180–200).** ICE is mispriced at 16–17x forward on a franchise with 51% recurring revenue, 60% adjusted margins, and $4.2B FCF. As mortgage origination recovers with any easing in rates, Treasury clearing gains adoption, and the market gains confidence that Polymarket is optionality rather than value destruction, the multiple re-rates toward the peer median. Applying 22x (in line with CME) to $8.73 next-year EPS yields ~$192; the $240 street high assumes fuller mortgage recovery plus Treasury-clearing traction. Buybacks under the fresh $3.0B authorization retire shares at a depressed price, accelerating per-share growth.

**Bear Case ($110–120).** Estimate revisions keep drifting lower, float income compresses through a Fed easing cycle, the mortgage recovery stalls in a higher-for-longer environment, and the Polymarket/crypto commitment proves a low-return distraction that continues to weigh on sentiment. EMIR 3.0 migrates euro clearing away and market-data fee scrutiny caps the highest-margin revenue line. The stock de-rates toward 14x on $8.03, roughly $112, and the multiple discount to peers proves permanent rather than a mispricing. This is a meaningful ~20% downside from current levels.

**Base Case (~$168).** The recurring franchise compounds mid-single-digits, mortgage recovers gradually, Treasury clearing builds slowly, and the market grants a partial re-rating to ~19x on $8.73 as near-term revisions stabilize. This is roughly the midpoint between the analyst low ($159) and median ($180).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is a 12-month re-rating from a compressed multiple as recurring cash flow, mortgage recovery, and buybacks compound, but the near-term estimate drift and unresolved strategic-capital questions prevent a confident BUY at the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-quarter or current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive over a 30–60 day window (reversing the −7.3% 90-day drift), OR
- The stock pulls back into the low-$120s (near the $121.79 52-week low), where 15x on $8.03 forward EPS offers clearly asymmetric risk/reward, OR
- Mortgage Technology posts two consecutive quarters of accelerating transaction revenue confirming a durable origination recovery.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut on the next earnings call, or a break in the six-quarter beat streak, OR
- A material Polymarket/crypto write-down or evidence the ~$2B is generating negative returns, OR
- EMIR 3.0 implementation confirms euro-clearing migration away from ICE Clear Europe with quantified revenue impact, OR
- Estimate revisions accelerate to the downside (30-day cut exceeding the 90-day cut across multiple periods).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release and 2026 guidance update, which the options market is already pricing as a stress event. Treasury-clearing repo launch (targeted Q4 2026) is a secondary catalyst.

## Conclusion

ICE is a high-quality, cash-compounding financial-infrastructure franchise trading at a genuine discount to its data/exchange peers after a ~26% drawdown that its record operating results do not justify on their own. The disconnect between fundamentals (20th straight record revenue year, six consecutive beats, 60% adjusted margins, ~5.2% FCF yield) and stock performance is the central opportunity, and at 16–17x forward EPS versus a 31.7x peer median, the market is discounting durable recurring cash flow at a cyclical multiple.

The reasons this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY are specific and current: near-term estimate revisions are drifting lower (−7.3% on the current quarter over 90 days), float income faces compression through a Fed easing path, and ~$2B of capital sits in unproven prediction-market/crypto ventures whose returns the market is right to question. The options market is pricing an imminent move via near-term IV backwardation. None of these deteriorates the franchise, but together they mean the cheapness needs a catalyst — a revision turn or a deeper pullback — to convert into realized upside. We would become buyers on positive estimate-revision velocity or on weakness toward the low-$120s. Until then, hold with a base-case fair value of ~$168 and monitor the next guidance update closely.
