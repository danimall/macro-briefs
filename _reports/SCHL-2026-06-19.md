**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Scholastic has delivered a 122% rally over the trailing twelve months, from a $18.70 low to $42.83, driven less by operating improvement than by a balance-sheet monetization and an aggressive share-count reduction. The investment question is no longer whether the business is improving (it is largely flat) but whether the current price fairly captures a de-risked, moat-protected core franchise against two structurally declining segments and low-quality reported earnings. The evidence points to a fairly-valued, two-sided situation rather than a clear directional opportunity.

The pivotal event of the year was the December 2025 sale-leaseback of the SoHo headquarters ($386M) and the Jefferson City distribution center ($95M) for $452.4M net, which booked a $118.2M pre-tax gain and funded full repayment of the $250M revolver taken on to acquire 9 Story Media in 2024. Long-term debt is now $0. That genuinely de-risks a balance sheet that the prior FY25 10-K showed in Altman grey-zone territory (Z 1.89, D/E 2.06x). The cost was $279M of new operating lease liabilities at a steep 10.4% implied rate and ~$23.7M of incremental annual rent. The reported nine-month net income of +$47.3M is entirely the real-estate gain; underlying operations ran a $36.2M operating loss and burned $39.1M of operating cash over the same period (seasonality drives Q1/Q3 losses, with Q2 and Q4 carrying the year, but the cash burn is still elevated versus the prior year's +$17.3M).

We rate SCHL a **HOLD**. The bull case rests on a real moat: the curated, in-school book fairs and book clubs channels that produced $130.7M of segment operating income at 13.6% margins (essentially the entire company's profit), plus an owned IP catalog (Dog Man, Hunger Games, Wings of Fire) now feeding the 9 Story content flywheel. The bear case rests on the structural decline of Education Solutions (-14% in 9M FY26), persistent Entertainment losses (-$16.5M in 9M FY26 with impairment risk), and the fact that management is manufacturing per-share earnings by retiring a large portion of the share count rather than growing the underlying business. At roughly 16x next-year consensus EPS, in line with the closest comp, Wiley, at 15.5x, the stock is neither cheap on quality-adjusted earnings nor demanding. The risk/reward spread of roughly -28% (bear) to +18% (bull) does not justify a directional position in either direction.

## Company Overview

Scholastic is the world's largest publisher and distributor of children's books, with a century-plus operating history and distribution into 135+ countries. The business runs four reportable segments:

1. **Children's Book Publishing & Distribution (~60% of revenue, the profit engine)** — School Reading Events (the iconic book fairs and book clubs sold directly into schools) plus the Trade channel (retail/online), home to owned franchises including Dog Man, Captain Underpants, Hunger Games, Wings of Fire, and the US Scholastic editions of Harry Potter. FY25 segment operating margin was 13.6%.
2. **Education Solutions (~19% of revenue)** — Supplemental and core classroom curriculum, classroom magazines, and literacy programs. Structurally declining.
3. **Entertainment (~4% of revenue)** — Built on the June 2024 acquisition of 9 Story Media Group (Toronto-based content producer, includes Brown Bag Films). Currently loss-making.
4. **International (~17% of revenue)** — Canada, UK, Australia/NZ, Asia, and export/foreign rights.

Voting control rests with Class A stock majority-owned by the Estate of the late Richard Robinson, voted by Iole Lucchese (Board Chair, EVP, Chief Strategy Officer, and President of Scholastic Entertainment) as special executor. The Estate can elect four of five directors; common holders elect one. This is the defining governance feature of the equity.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year earnings record shows an operating collapse that has not recovered:

| Metric (FY, $M) | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,642.9 | 1,704.0 | 1,589.7 | 1,625.5 |
| Operating income | 97.4 | 106.3 | 14.5 | 15.8 |
| Net income | 80.9 | 86.3 | 12.1 | (1.9) |

Operating income fell roughly 85% from FY23 to FY24 and has hovered near breakeven since, producing a small net loss in FY25 (-$0.07 EPS). The dispersion within FY25 is the key fact: Children's Book P&D earned $130.7M of operating income, the entire company's profit, offset by Education Solutions ($6.3M, down 60% YoY), Entertainment (-$12.1M), International (-$1.0M), and unallocated overhead (-$108.1M).

For the nine months through February 28, 2026, revenue of $1,105.8M was down 1.0% YoY, with a seasonal operating loss of -$36.2M (vs. -$37.7M prior year). The underlying run-rate operating income remains close to the ~$15M annual figure of FY24-FY25. Nine-month operating cash flow was negative $39.1M, hurt by ~$38M of higher tax payments on the gain, a UK pension contribution, and elevated severance ($7.6M). A model keying on trailing EPS ($1.87 diluted, 9M) would materially overstate earnings quality.

**Balance sheet.** The de-leveraging is genuine and material. The 9 Story acquisition ($193.7M, June 2024) was funded with $250M of revolver debt, which the December sale-leaseback fully repaid. The trade-off is $279M of new operating lease liabilities and ~$23.7M/year of incremental rent, capitalized at a 10.4% borrowing rate that management itself attributes to its "higher leverage position at the time." Scholastic converted owned, appreciated real estate into a 15-20 year fixed obligation to fund de-leveraging and buybacks.

**Earnings surprise track record.** The recent surprise pattern is better than the bearish narrative implies, and we weight it explicitly: the most recent reported quarter came in at -$0.15 versus a -$0.37 estimate (+59.7%), and the December 2025 quarter, the year's profit driver, beat at $2.57 versus $2.11 (+22.0%). Two consecutive beats undercut any premise that the next quarterly report will "reveal" hidden operating deterioration. They do not, however, signal organic acceleration; they reflect a combination of cost discipline (SG&A down $7.0M ex-rent in 9M FY26) and a lower share count flattering per-share figures.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth prospects are poor, and this is the weakest pillar of the story. Revenue has been flat-to-down for three years (FY24 $1,589.7M, FY25 $1,625.5M, 9M FY26 -1.0%). Two of four segments are in retreat:

- **Education Solutions (-11.8% FY25, -14.0% 9M FY26)** is in a secular, not cyclical, decline. Districts are shifting toward evidence-based "science of reading" core curricula and away from the supplemental materials Scholastic has historically sold, against a backdrop of tight school funding. Scholastic's supplemental-heavy mix is on the wrong side of this transition, and it competes against larger basal-textbook publishers with bigger sales forces.
- **Entertainment** is loss-making (-$16.5M operating loss in 9M FY26, including $5.2M of film/TV development impairments) and depends on streaming greenlights that have been delayed in a consolidating, capacity-constrained market.

The only genuine engine is Children's Book P&D, whose results swing on franchise release timing (FY26 was affected by Dog Man shifting from Q3 to Q2). The retail/trade book market is described by management as "soft" and expected to remain so in FY26.

**Estimate revisions cut both ways.** Over 90 days, consensus moved up sharply — current quarter +25.6%, current year +26.9%, next year +22.5%. We do not dismiss this as the system's history cautions against, but we read it correctly: most of the upward revision reflects the mechanical EPS benefit of a sizable share-count reduction plus the cost takeout, not a reacceleration of the underlying business. The more recent 30-day trend has turned negative across the board (current quarter -10.6%, current year -3.5%, next year -4.6%), and the next-quarter estimate is down 25.3% over 90 days. Near-term earnings momentum is softening even as the annual figures got marked up earlier in the year.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.83 against consensus next-year EPS of $2.67, SCHL trades at ~16.0x forward earnings. Within the publishing peer set, that is in line with the closest comparable, Wiley (15.5x), and well below the peer median of 31.8x (skewed by NYT 31.8x, News Corp 33-38x, IAC 24.5x — businesses with stronger digital/subscription growth profiles).

The forward multiple looks reasonable until two adjustments are applied. First, the next-year EPS base is engineered: it rests on a share count that has fallen from ~28.2M (May 2024) to ~20.9M (February 2026) and lower post-tender. Stripping the financial engineering, underlying operating income is closer to $15M annually — a quality that does not warrant a growth multiple. Second, the trailing "profit" is an accounting artifact of the real-estate gain. On quality-adjusted earnings power, the stock is roughly fairly valued, not cheap.

A low headline multiple on a stagnant operating base with two structurally declining segments is a value-trap signature, and we decline to reward the apparent discount in the absence of an organic catalyst. The catalysts that exist are technical rather than fundamental: continued buyback accretion, an undersubscribed $40 tender suggesting holders see value above that price, and 21.2% short interest creating squeeze potential. These support a HOLD floor; they do not constitute a fundamental mispricing that argues for a BUY.

We anchor a base case at ~$41-43 (in line with the sole covering analyst's $41 mean target and current spot), a bull case at $50-52 (continued buyback accretion plus Entertainment reaching breakeven and a Children's Book hit cycle), and a bear case at $30-32 (Education decline accelerates, a goodwill/intangible impairment crystallizes, or the buyback pace slows). The resulting -28%/+18% spread is symmetric-to-slightly-negative and does not favor a directional position.

## Competitive Landscape

The book fairs and book clubs channels are a genuine moat. Scholastic holds near-exclusive, curated, in-school physical distribution that Amazon and mass-market retailers cannot easily replicate, reinforced by an owned IP catalog that generates recurring bestseller economics and now feeds the 9 Story production flywheel. At 13.6% segment margin and $130.7M of operating income, this is the franchise that justifies the equity.

The trajectory, however, is flat-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving. The FY25 10-K acknowledges that "customer-specific curation tools" from online distributors are eroding the channels' differentiation, and that the model depends on continually recruiting younger (millennial/Gen-Z) sponsor-teachers. In Education, Scholastic is on the losing side of the science-of-reading transition. In Entertainment, 9 Story is sub-scale in a consolidating market. The net competitive trajectory across the portfolio is roughly neutral: a durable core slowly defended against share erosion, dragged by a structurally challenged adjacent segment.

## Risk Assessment

Company-specific risks that are not fully priced:

1. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** $200.3M goodwill plus $81.7M intangibles, largely from 9 Story, against a segment running operating losses and $5.2M of recent development impairments — a live watch-item across seven reporting units.
2. **Education secular decline.** A structural, not cyclical, headwind on ~19% of revenue.
3. **9 Story integration and Canadian tax-credit dependency.** The segment must remain Canadian-controlled (Scholastic holds 100% economic but only 25% voting interest) to preserve the production tax credits it relies on for financing. The 10-K added a dedicated risk factor on the inability to "sustain, manage or effectively execute" the strategy.
4. **Customer concentration.** Top 5 trade customers represent ~75% of US trade and ~16% of total revenue.
5. **Tariff exposure.** Management warns COGS will rise in FY26, "especially in the book fairs channel." A potential ~$9M IEEPA tariff refund is a modest offsetting positive not yet recognized.
6. **Governance.** Estate control voted by a sitting executive, with 5-year TSR of 66.04 vs. a peer group at 371.54 — severe long-term underperformance and minimal minority-holder influence over any value-unlock.

Partially offsetting: the de-levered balance sheet ($0 long-term debt) removes the financial-distress risk the grey-zone Z-score flagged a year ago, and the undersubscribed $40 tender suggests downside support near that level.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 48.4% sits modestly rich to one-year historical volatility of 42.9% (a 5.5pp spread), but the IV percentile within the current chain is low (12%), the term structure is flat (~48-49% near and far), and put/call skew is balanced at 1.1%. The market is pricing an implied move of roughly ±11.6% with no directional tilt and uncertainty evenly distributed across tenors. The reported spike in one delta snapshot to >1000% IV is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative. The cleaner signal is the short-interest structure: 21.2% of float short with 6.8 days to cover. This is a two-sided tell: it reflects bearish positioning on the secular and earnings-quality concerns, but on a thinning, controlled float it also creates meaningful squeeze risk that caps the case for shorting.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($50-52)
The Children's Book P&D moat compounds: a hit cycle (Dog Man, a new Collins or Pilkey title) lifts Trade, book fairs absorb tariff costs through curation and pricing, and the owned IP catalog monetizes across 9 Story film/TV and merchandising as Entertainment reaches breakeven. Continued buybacks against a thinning float (and a controlling Estate that is not a seller) compound per-share earnings. Cost restructuring sustains margin. The IEEPA tariff refund and easing rate pressure are minor tailwinds. At ~16x a rising EPS base, the stock re-rates toward $50.

### Bear Case ($30-32, ~-28%)
Education's secular decline accelerates and drags total revenue negative; Entertainment losses persist and a goodwill/intangible impairment crystallizes; the trade market stays soft; and the EPS engineering runs its course as buyback capacity is consumed by the fixed lease obligation and modest free cash flow. With underlying operating income stuck near $15M and the real-estate gain non-recurring, the market re-rates the stock to a low-quality-earnings multiple in the low-$30s.

The two cases are roughly balanced. The de-leveraging and the moat protect the downside; the secular drag and engineered earnings cap the upside. Neither dominates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q4/FY26 report (fiscal year ends May 31; FY26 results expected July 2026), which carries the year and will reveal full-year segment trends, the FY27 buyback posture, and any impairment decision. Monitor the CEO transition signaled by the January 29, 2026 Warwick letter agreement.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Children's Book P&D revenue and margin re-accelerate (segment operating margin sustained above 14%) AND Entertainment narrows to breakeven, signaling organic profit growth rather than share-count arithmetic.
- A valuation reset toward the low-$30s on no fundamental deterioration, restoring a >2:1 reward/risk asymmetry.
- Evidence the Education decline is stabilizing (revenue decrement narrowing below mid-single digits) or that 9 Story IP monetization is generating material, profitable greenlights.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material goodwill/intangible impairment that exposes Entertainment as uneconomic.
- FY26 free cash flow turning structurally negative once the real-estate gain rolls off, with the buyback program forced to slow or pause.
- Total revenue declining more than mid-single digits with the book fairs/clubs moat showing share loss (segment margin compressing below 11%).
- A governance action by the controlling Estate that disadvantages minority holders.

## Conclusion

Scholastic is a stable, newly de-levered, moat-protected business whose equity story has shifted from operating turnaround to balance-sheet monetization and share-count reduction. The book fairs and clubs franchise is a durable asset generating the company's entire profit; the long-term debt is now $0; and two recent earnings beats and a thinning, partly squeezed float provide technical support. Against that, Education Solutions is in secular decline, Entertainment is loss-making with live impairment risk, reported profits are an accounting artifact of a one-time real-estate gain, and per-share earnings are being manufactured rather than grown. At ~16x next-year EPS — in line with its closest comp and below a higher-growth peer median — the stock is fairly valued, with a -28%/+18% risk/reward that argues for neither buying nor shorting at $42.83 after a 122% run.

This is a defensible **HOLD**. The improving score trajectory (4.4 → 5.5) is confirmed by the de-leveraging rather than reversed, but the de-risking is now reflected in the price, and the absence of an organic growth catalyst keeps this short of a BUY. We do not see structural impairment severe enough to call AVOID — the core franchise is intact and the balance sheet is clean — nor a mispricing large enough to call BUY. **Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).**
