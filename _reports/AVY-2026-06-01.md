**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Avery Dennison is the global scale leader in pressure-sensitive label materials, paired with a higher-growth digital-identification franchise (Intelligent Labels/RFID) that, until recently, supplied the multi-year growth narrative. The investment problem today is that the growth engine has stalled. Organic Intelligent Labels sales decelerated from high-single-digit growth in 2024 to low-single-digit in 2025 and to a low-single-digit decline in Q1 2026, driven by tariff-induced softness in apparel volumes (apparel being a major early RFID vertical). Management's response is to defer the thesis to a promised second-half-2026 ramp, which has not been confirmed by any operational data.

The core Materials Group (~69% of sales) remains a steady, high-return cash engine: gross margin around 28.8%, ROE above 30%, and roughly $700M of adjusted free cash flow. The balance sheet screens distressed (Altman Z 1.57, D/E 3.93x) but this is a screening artifact: book equity is depressed by accumulated buybacks, debt is investment-grade and well-laddered, and interest coverage is comfortable (EBIT ~$1.06B vs. $135M interest). The genuine concerns are different: flat organic revenue masked by FX and a Q1 2026 segment reclassification that moved RFID revenue into Materials, consensus EPS estimates falling 2-5% over 90 days, buybacks doubled to $572M in 2025 while the company issued €500M of notes and bought Taylor Adhesives for ~$390M, and a former-CEO selling ~$25M of stock.

At ~$159, AVY trades at roughly 16x current-year consensus EPS of $10.02 and ~14x next-year $11.17, a discount to the packaging peer median P/E of ~26.6x, but the peer comparison is imperfect (those are commodity containerboard/flexible-packaging names with different growth and cyclical profiles). The multiple is not stretched, but it is not a bargain either, because forward estimates are drifting lower and the catalyst that would justify a re-rating (IL re-acceleration) remains unproven. This is a HOLD with no strong directional conviction until the second-half IL data either confirms or refutes the bull thesis.

## Company Overview

Avery Dennison operates two reportable segments:

- **Materials Group (~69% of sales; ~$6.1B FY25):** The core franchise, a leading global producer of pressure-sensitive (self-adhesive) label materials, graphics/reflective films, and performance tapes, sold to converters serving food, beverage, home/personal care, pharma, logistics, and durables. Brands include Fasson, JAC, Mactac, and Avery Dennison. Principal competitors: UPM, Fedrigoni, Lintec, 3M, Orafol, tesa, Nitto Denko.
- **Solutions Group (~31% of sales; ~$2.8B FY25):** The higher-tech segment focused on digital identification: ultra-high-frequency RFID inlays/tags ("Intelligent Labels"), apparel brand embellishments/tags, Vestcom shelf-edge media, and loss-prevention/brand-protection. Competitors: Checkpoint (CCL), R-pac, SML, Tageos.

The company has ~35,000 employees across 200+ facilities in 50+ countries. Roughly 69% of sales are international, ~40% from emerging markets, and ~58% of the workforce sits in Asia Pacific. The strategic thesis is to leverage Materials' converter-channel access and materials-science R&D ($137M in 2025) to drive item-level RFID adoption, connecting physical objects to digital identity. Competitive moats: scale, process-engineering R&D, global distribution density, brand strength, and a leading installed position in item-level RFID. No single customer represents ≥10% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Net sales grew ~1% to $8.86B in FY25 from $8.76B in FY24 and $8.36B in FY23 (FY22 was higher at $9.04B; sales dipped in 2023 on customer destocking and recovered through 2024). Organic growth has been roughly flat, with raw-material-deflation-driven price reductions offsetting volume gains. Q1 2026 sales rose 7% to $2.30B, but ~5 points of that was FX and only ~1 point was organic; the underlying volume picture is weak.

**Profitability.** Gross margin held steady at ~28.8% (FY25 gross profit $2.55B), supported by productivity, material re-engineering, and >$60M/year of restructuring savings. Net income was $688M in FY25, down slightly from $705M in FY24, with diluted GAAP EPS of $8.79. ROE of ~30.7% and ROA of ~7.8% reflect a capital-efficient model. Q1 2026 net income was $168M (GAAP EPS $2.19 / adjusted $2.47). The effective tax rate rose to 30.1% in Q1 2026 from 26.7% on a discrete foreign tax-group ruling charge; full-year is guided to "high-20s%," a modest EPS headwind.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $881M in FY25 (down from $939M on higher incentive comp and tax payments); adjusted FCF was ~$707M (a ~5.9% FCF yield on the ~$12.0B market cap). Capital allocation has turned increasingly aggressive:
- Share repurchases more than doubled to $572M (3.2M shares) in FY25 from $248M in FY24; $526M remained on the $750M April 2025 authorization. Buybacks slowed sharply to $61M in Q1 2026, suggesting opportunistic discipline.
- The dividend rose ~7% to $0.94/quarter in 2025 and again +6% to $1.00/quarter in April 2026, supporting a ~2.5% yield, well-covered by FCF.
- Share count fell to ~76.5M from ~80.4M.

**Balance sheet.** The screening Altman Z of 1.57 ("distress zone") is a false positive. AVY deliberately runs a leveraged, equity-light balance sheet; large accumulated treasury stock depresses book equity to $2.24B, mechanically inflating D/E to 3.93x. Total debt of $3.73B is investment-grade, well-laddered, and covenant-compliant (revolver max leverage 3.5x, well below the limit; a temporary step-up to 4.0x was triggered by the Taylor Adhesives acquisition). Financial health is solid, not distressed. The legitimate concern is the direction of travel: ~$739M of average variable-rate borrowings and €500M of new notes (Sept 2025) raise interest expense into a flat-revenue, elevated-rate environment, and the buyback program is now partly debt-funded.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is the crux of the AVY thesis, and it has deteriorated. Intelligent Labels, the segment that justified a premium narrative, has gone from high-single-digit organic growth in 2024, to low-single-digit in 2025, to a low-single-digit decline in Q1 2026. The cause is tariff-driven softness in apparel and discretionary volumes; AVY's direct tariff exposure is small, but the indirect demand drag hit a low-single-digit rate across Q2-Q4 2025 and persisted into Q1 2026.

Two facts complicate the picture further. A Q1 2026 segment reclassification moved certain RFID revenues from Solutions into Materials "to better align with growth strategy." This optically inflated Materials' reported growth (+11%) while obscuring the underlying IL trend, and forces analysts to track IL on a company-wide organic basis to see the real trajectory. Separately, the entire bull case now hinges on a promised second-half-2026 IL ramp that has no operational confirmation. That is a forecast, not a fact.

Consensus estimate revisions corroborate the deceleration: current-quarter EPS estimates fell 2.0% over 30 days and 4.5% over 90 days; current-year fell 2.3%; next-year fell 1.6%. This is broadly negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the positive-revision signal that would support a constructive call. The earnings beat track record is consistent but tepid (3 beats, 1 in-line over four quarters, with surprises of just 0.1-1.1%), reflecting management's habit of guiding to delivery rather than upside.

The Wiliot investment ($75M minority stake, completed April 2026) positions AVY as the preferred commercial partner for ambient-IoT/"Physical AI" battery-free sensing tags, extending the digitization roadmap into cold-chain/sensing. This is a forward option, not a 2026 earnings driver; AVY is the channel partner, not the platform owner. The Taylor Adhesives deal (~$390M, Oct 2025) bolts flooring adhesives into Materials but is immaterial to consolidated results so far.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward P/E of ~17.9x (per the structured data, on trailing-equivalent EPS) sits at a meaningful discount to the packaging peer median P/E of ~26.6x.

The peer comparison requires care. The listed comparables, Amcor (25.7x), International Paper (negative earnings), Packaging Corp (26.6x), and Smurfit Westrock (57.2x), are commodity containerboard and flexible-packaging names with different cyclical and margin profiles. AVY's higher-return, asset-light, specialty model arguably deserves a premium to commodity packaging, but its current flat-organic-growth reality argues against one. The cleaner framing is that AVY trades roughly in line with its own historical mid-teens forward multiple, which is appropriate for a low-single-digit organic grower with a stalled premium segment.

The cycle-adjustment argument does not rescue the valuation here. Estimates are falling, not rising, and there is no earnings trough to normalize from. Trailing EPS of $8.79 reflects roughly mid-cycle earnings power, not a depressed base. A re-rating toward the analyst mean target of $200 (vs. spot ~$159) requires the IL ramp to materialize and revisions to turn positive. Until that happens, the multiple is fair, not cheap.

Analyst targets span $175 (low) to $221 (high), mean $200, median $201, with the rating distribution 2 Strong Buy / 7 Buy / 2 Hold. The current price sits below even the low target, which superficially suggests upside, but those targets embed the second-half IL ramp that has not been confirmed. Given falling estimates, I treat the consensus targets as optimistic rather than as a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

AVY's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-favorable. In pressure-sensitive label materials, it is the scale leader with high capital and technical barriers to entry, defending share against UPM, Fedrigoni, Lintec, and 3M. The customer base is fragmented (no customer ≥10%), but ongoing converter consolidation pressures pricing over time. In item-level RFID, AVY is the market leader against Checkpoint, SML, and Tageos, and the Wiliot partnership signals an attempt to lead the next wave (ambient IoT/sensing) ahead of competitors.

The trajectory is mixed. Materials held up as a relative outperformer on margins and scale in Q1, but the Solutions/IL drag is company-specific to AVY's retail/apparel mix, and the long-term RFID adoption curve has clearly stalled. The moat is intact; the question is whether the growth optionality the market once paid for is durable or whether RFID adoption is structurally slower than the bull case assumed. The Q1 2026 reclassification of RFID into Materials makes the underlying competitive trajectory harder to monitor and warrants skepticism toward headline segment growth.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Intelligent Labels deceleration (the central risk).** The deceleration from high-single-digit growth to decline undermines the multi-year thesis. The promised 2H-2026 ramp is the make-or-break metric; failure to materialize would compress both estimates and the multiple. Goodwill of $2.27B (grown by Taylor Adhesives) passed its 2025 qualitative impairment test but is vulnerable to a sustained Solutions/RFID slowdown.
2. **Tariff/trade-driven demand drag.** The indirect demand hit to apparel/discretionary volumes is the dominant near-term swing factor. The Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling could relieve some duties but introduces a period of trade-policy instability.
3. **FX translation.** With 69% international sales, FX is material (+$29M FY25 sales, +$99M Q1 2026). Euro strength helps; INR/BRL weakness hurts.
4. **Customer credit.** A large Materials Group customer filed prepackaged Chapter 11 in January 2026. Management expects full prepetition collection, but it signals stress in the converter base.
5. **Leverage into rising rates.** Rising interest expense on variable-rate debt, with buybacks now partly debt-funded against flat organic revenue. Investment-grade commitment caps further leverage flexibility.
6. **Capital-allocation quality.** Doubling buybacks while issuing notes and acquiring is EPS support layered on muted organic growth. The Q1 slowdown to $61M is a constructive sign of discipline.
7. **Other:** Dutch DB-to-DC pension conversion by 2028 (compensation cost), 10 environmental PRP sites, EU CSRD reporting from 2028.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sending a measured, non-directional signal. Near-term ATM IV of 29.1% is rich versus 1-year realized volatility of 23.0% (a 6pp premium), but the IV percentile is only 26% within the current chain, and put/call skew is balanced (-0.6%), implying the elevated IV reflects general uncertainty rather than a directional bearish bet. The term structure is flat (29.1% near vs. 27.4% far), with uncertainty spread evenly rather than concentrated on a near-term catalyst. The put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.79 to 9.11 since early May, alongside a 5.2% price decline, a sharp build in put open interest that suggests downside hedging into the second-half IL data window. Short interest is low (3.4% of float, 3.2 days to cover), so the bearish positioning is uncrowded but also modest. Net read: the market is hedging the IL-ramp uncertainty, not pricing a collapse.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$200-221)
Materials Group continues compounding cash at ~30% ROE while the second-half-2026 Intelligent Labels ramp materializes as apparel volumes normalize post-tariff and the Wiliot partnership extends the RFID/sensing TAM into cold-chain and grocery. Estimate revisions turn positive, the stock re-rates toward the historical mid-teens multiple on a re-accelerating EPS base toward $11-12, and buybacks at depressed prices accelerate per-share growth. At 18-20x ~$11.17 next-year EPS, that supports $200-221, matching the analyst mean-to-high range.

### Bear Case (~$130-140)
The IL ramp disappoints, apparel weakness persists, and RFID adoption proves structurally slower than the bull case assumed. Estimates continue falling, the goodwill from Taylor Adhesives faces impairment scrutiny, rising interest expense compresses net income, and the multiple de-rates toward 12-13x on flat-to-declining organic EPS near $10. That implies ~$130-140, roughly 12-18% downside.

### Base Case (~$170-180)
Materials carries the company, IL stabilizes but does not meaningfully re-accelerate in 2026, estimates settle near current levels, and the stock recovers modestly toward 16-17x ~$10.50-11.00 blended forward EPS as buyback support and the dividend provide a floor. That implies ~$170-180, roughly 7-13% upside, with the dividend adding ~2.5%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month HOLD; the directional outcome is genuinely two-sided and hinges on data not yet available.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmed Intelligent Labels organic re-acceleration (back to mid-single-digit+ growth on a company-wide basis) in the Q2 or Q3 2026 prints, accompanied by EPS estimate revisions turning positive (+5% or more over 90 days).
- Price weakness toward ~$140-145 (≈12-13x forward EPS) absent fundamental deterioration, which would create asymmetric upside on the Materials cash engine alone.
- Tangible Wiliot/sensing revenue contribution or a major IL customer win that broadens the adoption base beyond apparel.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Continued IL declines through 2H 2026 with management pushing the ramp into 2027, plus a goodwill impairment charge or a guidance cut.
- A breach toward covenant leverage limits or a downgrade of the investment-grade rating.
- Material deterioration in Materials margins (gross margin sustained below ~27%) signaling pricing erosion from converter consolidation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), which should provide the first read on the promised second-half IL ramp. The full reassessment comes with Q3 2026 results, when the ramp must be visible in the numbers or the bull thesis is effectively dead.

## Conclusion

Avery Dennison is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise trading at a non-demanding ~16x forward earnings, with a comfortable balance sheet that screens worse than it is. That is not enough to make it a buy today, because the asset that justified a growth premium (Intelligent Labels) is contracting, consensus estimates are drifting lower, and the constructive case rests on a second-half-2026 ramp that has no operational confirmation. The price decline from ~$199 to ~$159 over the past year reflects genuine fundamental softening (decelerating IL, falling estimates, flat organic revenue), not just sentiment, so the lower price is not automatically a better entry.

The score has declined from 6.6 in February to a current 5.6, driven by the growth and management/governance deterioration; today's data confirms rather than reverses that trend. With balanced risk/reward (~7-13% base-case upside vs. ~12-18% bear-case downside), a well-covered ~2.5% dividend providing a floor, and a dated catalyst window in 2H 2026, the appropriate posture is to wait. **HOLD.** The bar to upgrade is confirmed IL re-acceleration with positive estimate revisions; the bar to downgrade is a failed ramp with impairment or guidance risk. Until the second-half data arrives, there is insufficient evidence for a confident call in either direction.
