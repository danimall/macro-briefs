## Executive Summary

ING Groep produced the best quarter of its post-rate-cycle era in Q2 2026. Net result of €1,947m beat a company-compiled consensus of €1,830m by 6.4%; profit before tax rose 23% year-on-year and 29% sequentially; total income grew 10%; fee income reached €1,278m (+14% YoY); net core lending grew €15.2bn and net core deposits €15.9bn; and return on tangible equity printed 17.0% for the quarter. Management raised 2026 total income guidance to above €24.5bn, lifted 2027 to above €26bn (a more than €1bn increase), raised the RoTE targets to >15% and >16% respectively, and raised full-year net interest income guidance to "up to €17bn." CET1 stood at 13.1%, the loan-to-deposit ratio improved to 97.7% from 100.0% at year-end, and risk costs ran at 15 basis points of average customer lending — below the company's own stated through-the-cycle average. The stock has responded: up 46% over twelve months and within 3% of its 52-week high.

The complication is that the market has already paid for this. Working from third-party aggregator data on ING's IFRS filings, tangible book value per share was €16.68 at 30 June 2026. At $35.22 and EUR/USD of 1.1437 (derived from Reuters' 30 July reference of $1 = €0.8744), the ordinary-share equivalent is €30.79, putting the shares at roughly 1.85x tangible book. A Gordon-growth cross-check at 16% RoTE, a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth supports 1.86x — meaning the current multiple is approximately fair only if ING sustains the 2027 RoTE target through the cycle. At 15% RoTE the same framework supports 1.71x; at 14% RoTE and an 11% cost of equity, 1.38x. That is not much cushion, particularly since the three quarters preceding Q2 delivered an implied average RoTE of about 13.7% (derived from the 14.5% four-quarter rolling figure against Q2's 17.0%).

Two structural facts should temper enthusiasm. First, absolute earnings have not grown: net income fell from €7,287m in FY2023 to roughly €6,432m on a trailing basis to June 2026, a decline of 11.7%, while EPS rose 12.7% from €2.04 to €2.30. The entire gap is share count, down about 18.4% over that span and 25% since FY2021. The current €1.0bn buyback is worth roughly 1.1% of a derived €89.7bn market capitalization — a far weaker per-share tailwind than the ~6.3% annual share-count reduction that carried EPS through 2022–2025. Second, risk costs at 15bps must eventually normalize; a move to 30bps costs roughly €1.2bn of pre-tax profit, about 11% of a guidance-delivered 2027 base case, and management identified the ECB rate outlook as among the key questions for the upcoming quarters. **HOLD.** Existing holders are paid roughly 4-5% in dividends and buybacks to wait for the guidance to prove itself; new capital has no asymmetry at 1.85x tangible book with the analyst consensus mean target ($33.83) sitting below spot.

## Company Overview

ING Groep N.V. is the Amsterdam-listed holding company for ING Bank N.V., a European G-SIB with roughly €1.16 trillion of assets at 30 June 2026, more than 60,000 employees and customers across more than 100 countries. Shares list in Amsterdam and Brussels; ADRs trade on the NYSE.

The holding-company structure matters for the distribution thesis. ING's own risk enumeration flags "regulatory restrictions on dividends and distributions (also among members of the group)" — the holdco funds its €0.40 interim dividend and its buyback by upstreaming capital from the ECB-supervised operating bank. That channel is subject to supervisory discretion, and it is the specific transmission mechanism through which any prudential intervention would reach shareholders.

Segment income (aggregator data on ING's IFRS reporting, € millions) tells a more honest story than the headline growth rate:

| Segment | FY2023 | FY2025 | TTM Jun'26 | FY23→TTM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retail Netherlands | 5,001 | 4,968 | 5,276 | +5.5% |
| Retail Belgium | 2,683 | 2,674 | 2,790 | +4.0% |
| Retail Germany | 3,152 | 2,991 | 3,104 | −1.5% |
| Retail Other | 4,233 | 4,908 | 5,156 | **+21.8%** |
| Wholesale Banking | 7,057 | 7,009 | 7,171 | +1.6% |
| Corporate Line | 450 | 484 | 303 | — |
| **Sum** | **22,576** | **23,034** | **23,800** | **+5.4%** |

The three home retail markets generated €10,836m in FY2023 and €10,633m in FY2025 — a 1.9% two-year decline. Wholesale Banking was flat. Effectively all growth over 2023–2025 came from "Retail Other" (Spain, Italy, Poland, Australia, Romania and adjacent markets). Only in the trailing period to June 2026 did the home markets return to growth. ING's model in the growth markets is digital-first, branch-light deposit gathering — 377,000 new mobile primary customers in Q2 alone, with management naming the Netherlands, Germany and Spain — monetized through mortgages and investment products.

**A note on data quality.** ING is a foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K, 10-Q, DEF 14A or Section 16 Form 4s. The FY2025 20-F (filed 26 February 2026) exists but its text was not retrievable in this cycle, so the risk-factor and legal-proceedings discussion below draws on ING's issuer-authored 30-item risk enumeration in its 30–31 July press releases rather than the Item 3.D section itself. Multi-year financials come from a third-party aggregator with three identified internal inconsistencies (FY2021–22 net income irreconcilable with the same source's EPS; a "revenue" line that does not tie to the segment sum; an implausible dividend-yield column). Share counts are derived by dividing common equity by book value per share. Insider transactions and executive compensation are structurally unobservable through SEC channels — draw no conclusion from their absence.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 2026 was above trend, not a new baseline.** If the four-quarter rolling RoTE is 14.5% and Q2 alone was 17.0%, the prior three quarters averaged (4 × 14.5 − 17.0) ÷ 3 = 13.67%. Q2 therefore ran roughly 330bps above the preceding run-rate, with risk costs simultaneously below the through-the-cycle average and PBT up 29% sequentially. Annualizing the quarter (€1,947m × 4 ÷ ~2,909m shares = €2.68 EPS → 11.5x) flatters the multiple; the trailing €2.30 EPS at 13.4x is the defensible anchor.

**The earnings-versus-EPS divergence is the central finding.**

| | FY2021 | FY2023 | FY2025 | TTM Jun'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EPS (€) | 1.53 | 2.04 | 2.12 | **2.30** |
| Net income (€m) | 5,951* | 7,287 | 6,327 | 6,432 |
| BVPS (€) | 13.38 | 14.37 | 16.62 | **17.27** |
| TBVPS (€) | 13.09 | 14.04 | 16.12 | **16.68** |
| Common equity (€m) | 52,066 | 51,240 | 49,698 | 50,246 |
| Derived shares (m) | 3,891 | 3,566 | 2,990 | **2,909** |

\*FY2021–22 net income figures are internally inconsistent with the same source's EPS and are not relied upon.

Common equity was flat to slightly down in absolute euros (€51,240m → €50,246m) while TBVPS rose 19%. Per-share book growth was largely a share-count phenomenon. Returning capital a franchise cannot profitably deploy is correct allocation, but it changes how "EPS growth" should be read: 2023–2025 was capital return layered on declining absolute earnings. The 2026 inflection — income +10% YoY, PBT +23%, guidance raised twice-over — is the first evidence in three years that absolute earnings growth has resumed, and it is two quarters old.

**Balance sheet: fast expansion, thinning simple leverage.** Total assets rose from €1,054,405m at 31 December 2025 to €1,160,759m at 30 June 2026, +€106.4bn (+10.1%). Only about 32% of that was customer lending (+€33.9bn); trading assets (+€26.9bn) and securities (+€20.5bn) contributed €47.4bn. Deposits grew €51.8bn (+7.2%), faster than loans, which is why the loan-to-deposit ratio improved to 97.7%. Net loan growth of 4.70% over six months annualizes to ~9.4%, independently corroborating management's stated "9% annualised" retail lending growth — a cross-check that raises confidence in the aggregator's recent series.

Simple equity/assets fell from 5.48% (FY2021) to 4.71% (FY2025) to 4.33% at June 2026. The binding constraint is CET1/RWA at 13.1%, not this ratio, and trading gross-ups inflate the denominator while carrying low RWA density. But the direction — buybacks shrinking equity while the balance sheet grows 10% in six months — is a genuine limit on further distribution. Long-term debt has grown 70.6% since FY2021 (€108,499m → €185,079m), faster than deposits (+25.2%); much of that is likely MREL build required of a G-SIB, but it means more of the liability stack reprices at market spreads.

**Cash flow metrics do not apply here.** The aggregator shows negative operating cash flow in every year (FY2025: −€6,380m) and a "FCF margin" of −30.97%. Under IFRS, growth in customer loans sits within operating activities; a lender adding €33.9bn of loans in six months mechanically reports a large operating outflow. Capex was €351m against €1.05tn of assets. Any screen penalizing ING on free cash flow — or applying an Altman Z-Score calibrated on non-financial manufacturers — is mis-specified. The appropriate solvency measures are CET1 (13.1%), loan-to-deposit (97.7%), risk costs (15bps) and RoTE, and all currently read well.

**Earnings visibility is good.** Four consecutive beats on ADR consensus: +5.2%, +5.8%, +6.6%, +2.4%. Estimate revisions are positive but modest — current-year ADR EPS up 1.7% in 30 days and 2.0% in 90 days to $2.82; next year up 1.8%/3.1% to $3.19. This is steady positive momentum, not the >30% revision velocity that signals a mispriced inflection. The one soft spot: next-quarter consensus was cut 5.0% to $0.72 (single-analyst coverage, low signal value).

## Growth Analysis

Guidance implies acceleration relative to what the franchise has actually delivered. Against a FY2025 segment-income base of €23,034m, >€24.5bn in 2026 implies ~+6.4%, and >€26bn in 2027 a further ~+6.1%. From the trailing €23,800m to >€26bn is +9.2% over roughly eighteen months — versus about +1.0% per annum from the home markets and Wholesale Banking over 2023–2025.

The decomposition between rate, volume and fee drivers is not disclosed, and it determines how durable the upgrade is. Management's framing points toward rates: NII guidance was raised to "up to €17bn" against >€24.5bn of income, so NII remains roughly 69% of the revenue base, and Reuters reported van Rijswijk "saying higher interest rates helped support earnings." Notably, in April 2026 the same CEO guided that higher-rate NII benefits "would be largely offset by lower income elsewhere, including hedging-related revenues" — a caution that July's upgrade overtook. That cuts two ways: current guidance may itself be conservative, or management's revenue-mix sensitivity modelling has limited precision.

The volume-and-fee reading is equally supportable from the disclosed facts. Fee income grew 14% YoY (Retail +16%, Wholesale +11%) and was the line management specifically singled out for upgrade. Retail lending grew €12.1bn (+9% annualised), of which mortgages were €7.1bn across the Netherlands, Germany, Italy and Australia. Retail deposits grew €16.7bn (+10% annualised). Active investment-product customers rose 110,000 in the quarter. Under this reading the upgrade is materially more rate-resilient. The evidence does not permit a decomposition, so the honest position is that a meaningful portion of the 2027 target is levered to the ECB path.

Two headline growth numbers require adjustment. Assets under management grew 27% YoY to €322bn, but ING states this was "partially supported by the full consolidation of Polish asset manager TFI, after acquiring the remaining stake" — the organic component is not quantified, and the word "partially" implies the majority was organic without establishing it. The Singular Bank transaction in Spain is a ~40% minority investment, not an acquisition: secondary reporting indicates ING is buying from Warburg Pincus, which held 93% and would retain roughly 53%, leaving ING the junior shareholder. Price, control rights, accounting treatment (equity-method versus consolidation) and CET1 impact are all undisclosed; closing is targeted for Q1 2027. No earnings contribution should be modelled.

## Valuation Assessment

Multiple bases must be reconciled. The market-data feed reports trailing and forward P/E of 10.46 on EPS of $3.266, which does not tie to the IFRS trailing EPS of €2.30 ($2.63 at 1.1437) that the filing-derived data support. Using the more internally consistent basis:

| Metric | Derivation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary-share equivalent | $35.22 ÷ 1.1437 | €30.79 |
| Market cap | 2,909m × €30.79 | ~€89.6bn (~$102bn) |
| P/E (TTM, €2.30) | 30.79 ÷ 2.30 | **13.4x** |
| P/E (FY2026 ADR consensus $2.82) | 35.22 ÷ 2.82 | **12.5x** |
| P/E (FY2027 ADR consensus $3.19) | 35.22 ÷ 3.19 | **11.0x** |
| P/TBV | 30.79 ÷ 16.68 | **1.85x** |
| P/B | 30.79 ÷ 17.27 | **1.78x** |
| Buyback yield | €1.0bn ÷ €89.6bn | **1.1%** |
| Dividend yield | TTM DPS €1.291 ÷ €30.79 | **~4.2%** (feed reports 2.18%; conflict unresolved) |

The dividend figure requires care. ING declared a €0.40 interim for H1 2026 with a final to follow; the aggregator's TTM DPS of €1.291 implies ~4.2%, while the market-data feed shows 2.18%, most likely a single-payment artifact. A defensible forward cash yield is roughly 3.5-4.2%, plus ~1.1% from buybacks.

On the earnings multiple, ING is not expensive: 11.0x next-year ADR consensus against a European bank sector that has re-rated but generally trades 8-11x forward. The premium is justified by RoTE guidance of >15%/>16% against a sector running closer to 12-13%. On the capital multiple, the picture is different. The Gordon check — P/TBV = (RoTE − g) ÷ (CoE − g) — supports 1.86x at 16% RoTE, 10% cost of equity and 3% growth; 1.71x at 15% RoTE; and 1.38x at 14% RoTE with an 11% cost of equity. Those inputs are assumptions, not disclosures, but the sensitivity is the point: at 1.85x the shares are fairly priced for full delivery and de-rate roughly 10-25% on reversion toward the RoTE the three pre-Q2 quarters actually produced.

**Scenario arithmetic (18-month view, guidance basis).** Base case: income €26.0bn less opex €13.0bn = €13.0bn pre-provision profit; risk costs at 25bps on ~€790bn average lending = €1.98bn; PBT €11.0bn; applying FY2025's implied 27.8% tax-and-minorities rate gives net ~€7.9bn; on ~2.75bn shares after further buybacks, EPS ~€2.88, or 10.7x at the current price. Downside case: income stalls at €24.0bn, opex still €13.0bn, risk costs normalize to 30bps (€2.4bn) → PBT €8.6bn → net ~€6.2bn → EPS ~€2.25 at 13.7x, with a smaller buyback providing less per-share support.

Analyst positioning corroborates the fair-value read rather than an undervaluation: mean target $33.83 and median $35.00 both sit at or below the $35.22 close, with a $37.50 high and $29.00 low. Coverage is thin (three recommendations, and one strong-sell appeared this month against two holds a month prior). On a stock trading at a full capital multiple, targets clustered at spot are not evidence of a floor.

Valuation score: 5.0. Reasonable on earnings, full on book, no identified mispricing, and no cushion for RoTE reversion.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. What is working: digital-first deposit gathering at genuine scale, with 377,000 new mobile primary customers in one quarter and €15.9bn of net core deposit inflows; a funding position improving rather than stretching (loan-to-deposit 100.0% → 97.7% in six months); and capital-efficient origination in Wholesale Banking, where management reduced risk-weighted assets while growing the book. ING completed another significant risk transfer securitization in Q2 delivering €1.0bn of RWA relief — standard European capital management in which ING retains the loans and pays a third party to absorb a loss tranche, but not free (the protection premium reduces net revenue) and leaving a different retained risk profile.

What is not working: two-thirds of retail income sits in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany, where income declined 1.9% from FY2023 to FY2025 against intense competition, and Wholesale Banking income was flat across a 100-country franchise. Deposit share in the growth markets is being bought — management attributed inflows to "successful deposit gathering campaigns" and noted higher marketing expenses — and campaign deposits typically price above relationship balances, with that cost embedded in NII rather than disclosed. Growth is also concentrating in secured residential mortgages across four specific housing markets (Netherlands, Germany, Italy, Australia): capital-efficient and low loss-given-default, but geographically concentrated exposure to housing and refinancing cycles, with no country-level LTV or arrears data available.

On wealth management — the sector's consensus fee-income answer — ING is buying capability rather than building it (Singular Bank minority stake, TFI buy-in). Peers across Europe are pursuing the same route, which bids up prices for these franchises and makes the undisclosed Singular consideration material to judging the allocation.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest-rate dependence is the dominant exposure.** NII is roughly 69% of guided 2026 income, and the guidance upgrade embeds a higher-for-longer ECB assumption. Reuters identified the outlook for ECB policy as "among the key questions for the upcoming quarters." The two-sided nature of this is what makes it a genuine risk rather than a directional bet — higher policy rates support the spread, but the same macro conditions that would force the ECB to reverse are the conditions that normalize credit costs.

**Risk costs at 15bps are a benefit that must reverse.** Management explicitly states this is below the through-the-cycle average (the actual through-the-cycle figure is not quantified in what was available). Normalization to 30bps costs roughly €1.2bn pre-tax, about 11% of a guidance-delivered 2027 PBT.

**Holding-company distribution dependency.** Item (13) of ING's risk enumeration cites regulatory restrictions on distributions "also among members of the group." The dividend and buyback depend on ECB-supervised upstreaming from ING Bank N.V. ING's CET1 target and formal distribution policy were not verifiable, so the surplus above requirement — and therefore the runway for further buybacks — cannot be quantified. This is the single most decision-relevant unresolved item for the capital-return case.

**Resolution and conduct risk.** Item (16) covers "write down and conversion powers in relation to our securities" under bank recovery and resolution regimes. Item (17) covers litigation and enforcement including "claims by customers or stakeholders who feel misled or treated unfairly" — historically a large and lumpy category for European retail banks with Dutch and German mortgage and savings franchises. No ING-specific enforcement action surfaced in 2026 searches, but the 20-F legal-proceedings section was not readable, and absence of search results is not evidence of a clean docket.

**Geopolitical exposure is named by management.** The risk list covers Russia/Ukraine, "other existing or emerging military conflicts," trade restrictions, and fiscal uncertainty in Europe and the United States. On the results call van Rijswijk said customer activity "remained resilient even as bombings continue in the Middle East" — an unusually direct acknowledgement of active conflict as a business variable.

**Governance observability.** As of 31 July 2026 the statutory Executive Board comprises van Rijswijk (CEO), Lerner (CFO) and Cesaroni (CRO) — two of three seats recently refreshed, with Cesaroni's appointment requiring an EGM one day after results. The EGM was convened solely for that appointment (no capital raise, no charter amendment). Predecessor identities and departure circumstances were not verified, so no inference is drawn. Separately, no compensation, proxy or insider-transaction data is obtainable through SEC channels for a 20-F filer, which caps how much the management-quality dimension can be evidenced in either direction.

**Management quality, on what is observable,** reads reasonably well: four consecutive beats, guidance raised rather than defended, CET1 held at 13.1%, and a coherent shift of capital from buyback toward lending. At 13.4x trailing earnings the buyback's earnings yield is 7.5%, against a >16% marginal return on lending — the observable pattern of a smaller €1.0bn programme alongside 9%-annualised loan growth and SRT-freed RWA is consistent with management reaching that conclusion. The offsetting point: repurchasing at 1.6-1.85x tangible book mechanically reduces TBVPS, so part of reported per-share book growth is being given back. Buyback execution to 10 July: 14,960,000 shares at an average €26.23, €392.4m or 39.24% of the programme complete; the full €1.0bn retires roughly 36m shares, about 1.24% of outstanding.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing shows no anticipated stress and no crowded bearish view. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 22.5% against 27.2% one-year realized — cheap by 4.7 points, in the 10th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is flat (22.5% near versus 21.5% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a catalyst. Put/call volume ratio is 0.19 and open-interest ratio 0.12 — call-dominated, with the nearest expiry pricing only a ±3.3% move. Short interest is effectively nil (0.0% of float, 937,491 shares, 0.2 days to cover), so there is neither informed bearish positioning nor squeeze fuel.

The one nuance worth noting is the January 2027 tenor: −6.7% put/call skew and a 0.40 put/call volume ratio against a 12.8% implied move, versus balanced positioning in the front months. Longer-dated protection is being bid more than near-dated, which is consistent with hedging the 2027 delivery and ECB-path risk rather than any near-term event. For a holder, cheap near-term volatility means collar or covered-call structures are inexpensive relative to realized vol; for a prospective buyer, the absence of any priced downside means the market is not offering a discount for the reversion risk in the valuation section.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($42, +19%).** The guidance upgrade is predominantly volume- and fee-driven rather than rate-dependent: fee income compounds from 14% growth, the 377,000 quarterly primary-customer additions convert into mortgages and investment products, and "Retail Other" continues taking share in Spain, Italy, Poland and Australia while the home markets return to the +3% trajectory visible in the trailing period. ING delivers >€26bn income and >16% RoTE in 2027 on €13bn of opex, generating roughly €7.9bn of net income and ~€2.88 EPS. Additional buyback programmes are announced alongside results — plausible given ING's pattern — lifting the total shareholder yield back above 5%. The market sustains 1.85-1.95x on a tangible book approaching €18.5-19, supporting €35-37 per ordinary share, or $40-42 at unchanged FX. A weaker dollar adds to the ADR mechanically.

**Bear case ($27, −23%).** The ECB reverses as European growth deteriorates, NII compresses from the "up to €17bn" guide, and the hedging-related offsets management flagged in April 2026 reassert themselves. Income stalls near €24bn against €13bn of opex while risk costs normalize from 15bps toward 30-40bps in a recession — the Dutch, German, Italian and Australian mortgage concentration transmits housing weakness directly. RoTE reverts toward the 13.7% the three pre-Q2 quarters delivered; on the Gordon framework that supports roughly 1.4x tangible book, or about €24.5-25 per share, $28 at unchanged FX. A smaller buyback provides less per-share support, and an ECB supervisory constraint on upstreaming — the mechanism ING's own risk list names — would compound it by hitting the dividend.

**Base case ($37, +5% over 12 months, plus ~3.5-4% dividend).** FY2026 lands at or slightly above the >€24.5bn income guide, producing roughly €2.55-2.60 of EPS. At 12-12.5x that is €31-32.5, or $35.5-37.2 at 1.1437. Total return of roughly 8-9% including the dividend is close to a bank cost of equity — fair, not compelling. Probability-weighting 50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear yields about +2% of price return plus the yield, or 5-6% total. That is a HOLD, not an ACCUMULATE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis for continued ownership is the roughly 4-5% combined dividend-plus-buyback yield on a franchise with improving funding (97.7% loan-to-deposit), a 13.1% CET1 ratio and four consecutive earnings beats. The thesis against adding is the absence of margin of safety at 1.85x tangible book with the consensus mean target below spot.

**Base case:** $37 within 12 months — 12-12.5x FY2026 EPS of ~€2.55-2.60 at EUR/USD 1.1437. Upside +5% plus dividend.
**Bull case:** $42 (+19%) — 2027 targets delivered, multiple sustained near 1.9x a higher tangible book.
**Bear case:** $27 (−23%) — RoTE reverts to ~13.7%, risk costs normalize to 30-40bps, multiple compresses to ~1.4x TBV.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of RoTE at or above 15% *with* risk costs at or above 25bps — this would demonstrate that the 2027 target survives credit normalization rather than depending on a below-trend provisioning environment.
2. Disclosure of ING's CET1 target confirming meaningful surplus above requirement, accompanied by a new buyback programme materially larger than €1.0bn (i.e., total shareholder yield restored above 6%).
3. A price near $29-30 (roughly 1.55-1.60x current tangible book, close to the low analyst target) on unchanged guidance — that would restore the asymmetry that 1.85x does not offer.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. 2026 or 2027 income guidance cut, or NII guidance reduced below €17bn — this would confirm the rate-dependent reading of the upgrade.
2. Risk costs above 35bps for two consecutive quarters, or any disclosed deterioration in Dutch, German, Italian or Australian mortgage arrears.
3. Any ECB supervisory restriction on distributions or upstreaming from ING Bank N.V., or a dividend or buyback suspension.
4. Confirmation of a material conduct or AML enforcement matter — a category ING's own risk enumeration flags and that could not be verified in either direction here.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October/early November 2026) is the first natural checkpoint, and the key question is whether Q2's 17.0% RoTE was above-trend or a new baseline — the four-quarter rolling figure should move toward 15% if the latter. Two ancillary items to resolve before then: ING's stated CET1 target and distribution policy, and the FY2025 20-F risk-factor and legal-proceedings text, neither of which was obtainable in this cycle. The Singular Bank close (targeted Q1 2027) should disclose consideration and accounting treatment.

## Conclusion

ING's Q2 2026 was a strong quarter delivered by a well-capitalized, well-funded bank whose management raised two years of guidance and has beaten consensus four times running. The operating evidence — 17.0% quarterly RoTE, 13.1% CET1, 15bps risk costs, 97.7% loan-to-deposit, 377,000 new primary customers, 9%-annualised lending growth — is genuinely good, and the fact that the stock is up 46% over twelve months is no argument against it.

The argument against adding is arithmetic. At $35.22 the shares trade at roughly 1.85x tangible book, which a Gordon-growth framework supports only on delivery of the >16% 2027 RoTE target; the three quarters preceding Q2 ran closer to 13.7%, and the same framework supports 1.38-1.71x on that range. Absolute net income remains about 12% below its FY2023 peak, with all EPS growth since attributable to a 25% share-count reduction that has decelerated to roughly a 1.1% annual buyback yield. Risk costs are running below management's own through-the-cycle average, and management has identified the ECB rate path as a key open question for the coming quarters. Consensus targets bracket the current price rather than sitting above it, and options markets price no downside at all — near-term implied volatility is below realized and the put/call ratio is 0.19.

Own the position for the yield and the operating momentum. Wait for either credit normalization to prove the RoTE target durable, or a price closer to $29-30, before committing new capital.
