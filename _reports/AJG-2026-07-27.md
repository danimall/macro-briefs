**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Arthur J. Gallagher & Co. is the world's third-largest insurance broker, a capital-light, fee-and-commission business with a high-quality economic profile: 36.5% adjusted brokerage EBITDAC margins, ~$1.79B of free cash flow, and a diversified client base where the largest single client is ~1% of revenue. The company has compounded revenue from $8.55B (2022) to $13.94B (2025) through a disciplined roll-up model (~780 acquisitions since 2002), capped by the $13.8B AssuredPartners deal that closed in August 2025. It is a well-run franchise with a durable niche-specialization moat.

The problem for 2026 is that both engines of the equity story face headwinds at the same time. First, the insurance cycle has turned soft: US commercial property rates fell ~7% in Q1 2026 and industry-wide global property rates fell 12% in Q2 2026 (Marsh). Because brokerage commissions are a percentage of premium, premium deflation compresses organic growth directly — AJG's organic growth has decelerated to 5% from 7% in 2024, and management flagged that further property declines could shave up to a full point off organic. Second, 2025 earnings were flattered by ~$363M of interest income earned on the ~$14B raised in December 2024 to pre-fund AssuredPartners; that tailwind reverses in 2026, mechanically compressing reported margins (Q1 2026 operating margin fell to 21.9% from 23.5%, roughly $143M of which was the lost prior-year interest income). Core brokerage margin is still expanding ~50 bps, but the optical comp is negative through 2026.

At ~$248, the stock trades at roughly 40x trailing GAAP EPS ($5.74) and ~18.8x forward adjusted EPS ($13.20 consensus). The stock is down ~21% from its 52-week high of $315.48 and well below the $280 December 2024 equity-raise price. Estimate revisions are drifting negative across the current-year window, the recent earnings track record is mixed (two misses, one in-line, one beat over four quarters), and the recommendation has been HOLD across five consecutive reports with a declining score trajectory (6.8 → 5.5). None of the near-term evidence argues for upgrading; equally, this is a durable franchise trading closer to fair value than to distress, so the case for AVOID is weak. HOLD, with the soft-market turn and AssuredPartners synergy realization as the swing factors.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1927 and headquartered in Rolling Meadows, IL, Gallagher operates in ~130 countries through three segments:

- **Brokerage (87% of 2025 revenue):** Retail (75% of brokerage), wholesale (13%), and reinsurance via Gallagher Re (12%). The business places insurance and reinsurance and earns commissions (percentage of premium), fees, and supplemental/contingent revenues from carriers. Gallagher assumes no underwriting risk; capital is provided by third-party underwriting enterprises.
- **Risk Management (13%, Gallagher Bassett):** Third-party claims administration, roughly 59% workers' comp, 34% liability/auto, 7% property, with ~95% of revenue from clients unaffiliated with the brokerage arm.
- **Corporate:** Debt, acquisition costs, FX, and legacy clean-energy tax-credit investments.

The competitive advantages are real: deep niche and practice-group specialization (74% of retail revenue), proprietary data and analytics tools (Gallagher Drive, SmartMarket, RISX-FACS, Blueprint), extreme client diversification (top 10 clients ~3% of revenue), and a long-tenured, family-anchored sales culture that supports the acquisition model.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue grew 21% to $13.94B in 2025, and Q1 2026 revenue was $4.76B (+28% YoY). Organic growth was only 5%; the balance is acquisitions. 2025 net income was $1.49B; adjusted EPS was $10.69 (+6%), while reported diluted EPS fell to $5.74 (−12%), driven by $894M of brokerage intangible amortization and $200M+ of transaction/integration costs. Adjusted brokerage EBITDAC margin expanded 145 bps to 36.5%.

**The one-time interest-income distortion.** 2025 results contained the ~$363M of interest income noted above, held in money-market accounts until the August 2025 close. This reverses in 2026. Q1 2026 brokerage margin fell ~360 bps YoY largely because Q1 2025 included $143M of this income. The 2025→2026 margin compression is therefore partly mechanical rather than fundamental — core brokerage margins are still expanding ~50 bps — but it also means reported 2026 comparisons will look weak regardless of operating performance.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$13.1B against stockholders' equity of $23.3B. Goodwill rose from $12.3B to $22.6B and intangibles from $4.5B to $10.7B, both AssuredPartners-driven. Interest expense rose to $639M in 2025 from $381M. The headline Altman Z-Score of 0.79 ("distress") is a false signal, distorted by ~$27B of fiduciary assets/liabilities (client premium and claim funds held in trust) that inflate the liability base of any broker. The company is investment-grade rated and was in compliance with all debt covenants. A naïve balance-sheet screen badly misreads this.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $1.93B in 2025 (down from $2.58B in 2024, reflecting a $750M Willis Re earnout payment and working-capital timing), with FCF ~$1.79B. The model is capital-light (capex ~$145M). The dividend was raised to $0.70/quarter for 2026 (~$719M annualized). Gallagher resumed buybacks in Q1 2026, repurchasing 1.4M shares for ~$310M at ~$221, its first repurchases in years — a mild signal that management views the post-pullback price as attractive.

The gap between reported EPS ($5.74) and adjusted EPS ($10.69) is large (~$4.95/share). Much of that gap — intangible amortization — is a recurring cost of the M&A model rather than a truly one-off item, so investors are underwriting an adjusted-earnings story that leans heavily on add-backs. That is not disqualifying for a serial acquirer, but it caps how high the financial-health score can go given the leverage now embedded post-AssuredPartners.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story splits into two components with very different quality.

**Organic growth is decelerating.** Organic was 7% in 2024, 6% in FY2025, and 5% in Q1 2026. The mechanism is direct: commissions are a percentage of premium, and the industry is in a broadening soft market. CIAB data showed US commercial P/C rate increases decelerating through 2025 (+4.2% → +1.6% by Q3); property fell ~7% in Q1 2026, and Marsh reported global property rates −12% in Q2 2026 (−19% in India). Casualty is the one line still firming, mostly in the US on elevated litigation/loss trends. This is a cyclical, industry-wide phenomenon rather than a company-specific problem — Marsh's consolidated organic was +6% in Q2 2026, broadly comparable — but it is a real headwind for the next several quarters.

**Acquisitive growth remains the engine.** AssuredPartners (10,900 employees) and Woodruff Sawyer ($1.2B) are the recent large deals. Management targets ~$160M run-rate synergies from AssuredPartners by end-2026 against ~$575M of integration cost over three years. The pipeline remains full (40+ term sheets, up to ~$10B deployable over two years), and management notes deal multiples are now falling, which favors a well-capitalized acquirer. Structural growth pockets include the E&S market for complex risks, including data centers and AI infrastructure, though these are small relative to the base.

The 24th consecutive quarter of double-digit adjusted EBITDAC growth is a genuine achievement, but it is increasingly M&A-driven. With organic growth decelerating and the interest-income tailwind reversing, 2026 will be the year the roll-up model has to work harder to sustain the compounding story. Consensus current-year EPS of $13.20 has been revised down slightly over 90 days (−0.1%), and current-quarter estimates are down 2.3% over 90 days. Next-year estimates ($14.87) are flat-to-marginally-positive. Earnings momentum is negative-to-neutral.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$248 the stock trades at the multiples noted above. The trailing GAAP multiple is misleading given the intangible-amortization drag, so forward adjusted is the right lens. On that basis AJG is priced roughly in line with its own history and its closest peers — Marsh McLennan and Aon typically trade in the high-teens-to-low-20s forward multiple range, and Gallagher's premium-broker quality supports a similar rating.

This is not a cheap stock with an identifiable mispricing, nor a trough-earnings situation where trailing multiples understate normalized power. It is a high-quality compounder trading near fair value into a decelerating organic-growth environment with negative near-term estimate revisions. Consensus price targets span $225 (low) to $388 (high), mean $281, median $279 — the mean/median imply ~13% upside, but those targets have not been reset downward for the full extent of the Q2 property-rate decline, and the low target sits below the current price. AJG's multiple offers little cushion if organic growth slips further.

The buyback at ~$221 tells you management thought the stock offered value ~11% below the current price. At $248, the margin of safety has partly closed. There is no specific catalyst to argue the market is mispricing the stock to the upside; the base case is a compounder growing adjusted EPS in the high-single-digits with a multiple near fair value. That warrants a below-average valuation score, with risk skewed toward multiple compression on any organic-growth disappointment rather than toward re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Gallagher's competitive position is stable-to-solid, with a durable moat rooted in niche specialization, proprietary analytics, and extreme client diversification. It sits behind Marsh McLennan and Aon in scale but ahead of the broader consolidator field, and its acquisition machine has proven repeatable across two decades and 780 deals. The falling deal multiples cited by management are a competitive tailwind for a disciplined, well-capitalized buyer as PE-backed consolidators face a higher cost of capital.

The trajectory question matters more than the level. The soft market is industry-wide, and AJG's diversified platform (retail, wholesale, reinsurance, benefits, TPA) is better positioned to weather it than smaller mono-line brokers; its 5% organic is broadly in line with peer results, and there is no evidence of share loss. The prior report's downgrade of competitive position from 8.0 to 6.0 reflects the cyclical organic deceleration and the integration burden of digesting AssuredPartners, not a structural erosion of the moat. I hold competitive position at 6.0: the moat is intact, but the near-term growth environment and the sheer size of the integration temper the trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Soft market / premium deflation (dominant, currently materializing).** The single most important near-term risk is already playing out. If the soft market broadens into casualty, organic growth pressure intensifies. This is the central bull/bear variable.

**AssuredPartners integration.** The largest deal in company history, adding 10,900 employees, ~$575M integration cost, and unquantified assumed liabilities. Risk factors were explicitly modified to call out IT-systems integration, broker retention, and unknown liabilities. Execution risk here is elevated for the next two-to-three years.

**Leverage and financing.** ~$13.1B debt, a portion floating-rate. A credit downgrade would raise financing costs and strain the acquisition-dependent model. Interest expense already rose ~$258M YoY.

**IRS micro-captive (IRC 831(b)) investigations.** Under IRS promoter investigation since 2013, plus a criminal investigation into micro-captive underwriting enterprises (company states it is "not a target"). Low-probability, high-severity, unquantified — a genuine tail risk.

**Contingent/supplemental revenue volatility.** ~$790M in 2025, less predictable than base commissions and reversible if carrier loss ratios deteriorate.

**Succession/governance.** Multi-generational family leadership concentrates succession risk (explicitly cited). A March 12, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K (officer/director change) is unconfirmed in detail and worth monitoring alongside the long-tenured CFO.

I score risk factors at 5.0. The soft-market headwind is real but partly priced; the integration and leverage risks are material and not fully reflected; the IRS criminal investigation is a genuine unquantified tail. This is a balanced-to-slightly-cautious risk profile, not a clean bill of health.

The term structure is in backwardation (40.8% near-term vs 31.9% at six months), and ATM IV jumped from 27.3% to 40.8% over three weeks while the price drifted −1.8%, indicating the market is pricing the next earnings report. Put/call skew is balanced (−1.4% near-term, steepening to −6.7% at six months), and put/call OI is a benign 0.39. Short interest is low at 3.1% of float (4.2 days to cover); this is not a crowded short. The market expects a volatile earnings reaction (implied move ±8.4% at the nearest expiry) but is not positioned aggressively bearish, consistent with a HOLD into a binary print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$300, ~+21%):** The soft market proves shallow and short, casualty firming offsets property deflation, AssuredPartners synergies ($160M) land on schedule and lift core brokerage margins, and the M&A pipeline converts at attractive falling multiples. Organic growth stabilizes at 5–6%, adjusted EPS compounds high-single-digits toward $14.87 in 2027, and the multiple holds at ~20x forward. The interest-income comp headwind laps by end-2026 and reported margins re-expand, restoring the double-digit adjusted EBITDAC growth narrative.

**Bear case (~$195, ~−21%):** The soft market broadens into casualty, organic growth slips below 4%, AssuredPartners integration stumbles on broker attrition or unknown liabilities, and the reversal of the interest tailwind combined with rising interest expense drives visible margin compression. Negative estimate revisions accelerate, the multiple compresses toward the mid-teens on a decelerating-compounder re-rating, and the IRS criminal investigation resurfaces as a headline risk. This maps to roughly the stock's early-2026 lows near $190–$205.

**Base case (~$270, ~+9%):** Organic growth holds at ~5%, AssuredPartners synergies substantially deliver, adjusted EPS reaches ~$14.87 in 2027, and the multiple stays near ~18–19x forward. Modest upside plus a ~1.1% dividend, with the 2026 optical comp headwind capping near-term re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Horizon: 12 months, with the primary reassessment point at each quarterly print given the live soft-market turn and the imminent earnings event the options market is pricing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic growth reaccelerates above 6% for two consecutive quarters, or property rate declines demonstrably bottom (industry rate-change data turning less negative).
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (+3% or more over 90 days), signaling the interest-income comp headwind is fully absorbed and core margins are visibly re-expanding.
- Price pullback toward the ~$205–$220 buyback zone, restoring the margin of safety management itself acted on.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Soft market broadens into casualty and organic growth falls below 4%, with management guiding lower.
- Evidence of AssuredPartners integration failure — material broker attrition, a synergy guide-down below $160M, or disclosed assumed liabilities.
- A credit-rating downgrade, or the IRS micro-captive criminal investigation escalating to name the company as a target.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (implied move ±8.4%), and the year-end 2026 update on AssuredPartners synergy run-rate.

## Conclusion

Gallagher is a high-quality, capital-light broker with a proven roll-up model, a durable moat, and strong free cash flow. It is also a compounder trading near fair value at ~18.8x forward adjusted EPS into a broadening soft insurance market that pressures its commission base, while absorbing a mechanical 2026 margin/EPS comp headwind and the integration of the largest acquisition in its history. Estimate revisions are drifting negative, the recent earnings track record is mixed, and the recommendation has been HOLD across five prior reports with a declining score. Organic deceleration, the interest-income roll-off, and negative revision momentum all argue against an upgrade, but the franchise quality, intact moat, and reasonable multiple keep it well clear of AVOID. Wait for organic growth to stabilize or for a pullback toward the ~$205–$220 zone where management itself was buying. **HOLD.**
