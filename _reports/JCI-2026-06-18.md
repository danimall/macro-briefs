**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Johnson Controls has completed its transformation into a pure-play commercial buildings company, and the operating data now confirms the strategic bet is working. Following the divestiture of Residential & Light Commercial HVAC to Bosch for ~$5.6B net after-tax (closed July 2025) and a segment realignment into three regional units, the company is showing a genuine operating inflection: Q2 FY26 revenue grew 8% to $6.14B, gross margin expanded to 36.8%, SG&A fell ~230 bps to 22.8% of sales, and segment EBIT compounded double-digits across all three regions (Americas +14%, EMEA +53%, APAC +42%). The signal underneath all of it is backlog of $20.0B at 3/31/26 (+26% YoY) and orders +30% YoY, concentrated in large data-center thermal-management projects. Management raised FY26 adjusted EPS guidance to ~$4.85 in May on data-center cooling demand.

The fundamental story is solid, but the price has caught up to it. JCI trades at 44x trailing earnings and roughly 30x the FY26 adjusted guide, a premium to the Building Products peer median of 22.9x and well above the stock's own history. The shares have advanced 38% over the past year and now sit near the 52-week high ($146.68 vs. $149.38). Estimate revisions are positive but modest (+3.0% on current-year EPS over 90 days), not the +30% velocity that would signal the market is systematically underestimating an inflection. The Danaher-trained CEO Joakim Weidemanis brings real operating discipline and the $5.0B accelerated share repurchase at an average $114.43 was well-timed, but capital allocation alone does not change the valuation math at the current level.

The overhang that prevents a higher rating is the AFFF/PFAS firefighting-foam litigation. The nationwide public-water-systems class is settled and paid ($750M), but the residual exposure (15,900+ individual personal-injury actions, 363+ municipal cases, 50 putative class actions, and 35+ state attorney-general suits in the South Carolina MDL) is explicitly unquantifiable by the company. This is the asymmetric risk the market is pricing through a perennial discount to operating quality. We rate JCI a HOLD with a growth bias: the order momentum supports the position, but the entry point does not offer the asymmetry required for a BUY.

## Company Overview

Johnson Controls is a global leader in commercial building technology (commercial HVAC, industrial refrigeration, building controls and management, fire detection and suppression, and security systems) sold and serviced largely through a direct installation channel that creates a durable recurring service and retrofit installed base. Marquee brands include YORK, Metasys, Ansul, Frick, Sabroe, Simplex, Grinnell, and the Tyco fire/security franchise. The OpenBlue platform layers AI and analytics onto building systems.

The company is Irish-domiciled (Cork) and reports three regional segments effective April 1, 2025: Americas (~67% of sales, $15.8B FY25), EMEA (~21%, $5.0B), and APAC (~12%, $2.8B). FY25 revenue mix was 68% Products & Systems and 32% Services, with stated strategy aimed at growing higher-margin recurring service revenue. The company employs roughly 87,000 people.

JCI competes in a consolidated commercial-buildings oligopoly against Honeywell, Siemens Smart Infrastructure, Schneider Electric, Carrier, Trane Technologies, Vertiv, and Daikin. Its differentiators are the direct field channel and installed base, breadth across HVAC, fire, security, and controls under one roof, and leverage to secular demand drivers: data-center cooling (including liquid cooling), decarbonization, building-performance regulation, and mission-critical verticals.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins (continuing operations).** FY25 revenue was $23.6B (+3% reported, +6% organic), with gross margin expanding to 36.4% from 35.2%. The drop from FY23's $26.8B reflects portfolio reshaping (the ADT and R&LC divestitures), not demand erosion. Segment EBITA in FY25 was Americas $2.88B, EMEA $649M, APAC $476M.

Q2 FY26 continuing diluted EPS was $0.99 versus $0.71 a year earlier, with 1H FY26 continuing EPS of $1.90. The margin leverage is consistent with the Weidemanis operating playbook landing alongside a favorable demand cycle.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $2.55B on continuing operations against capex of only $434M, an asset-light model with high FCF conversion. Net debt declined to $8.8B at 3/31/26 from $9.5B, with net-debt/cap of 39.5%. Ratings are investment-grade and stable (S&P BBB+, Moody's Baa1). Cash on hand is $698M.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.47 reads as "distress zone," but this is a mechanical artifact, not an operating signal. It is driven by $16.6B of goodwill on a $37.9B balance sheet plus the structurally high financial leverage (D/E 2.93) typical of a roll-up built from Tyco and York legacy assets. Operating metrics contradict the distress reading entirely: ROE 27.2%, ROA 9.3%, net margin 14.9%, current ratio 1.17, and a covenant cushion of $13.5B actual equity against a $3.5B minimum. A reader should weight the investment-grade ratings, rising FCF, and expanding margins over the Z-Score.

**Earnings quality.** FY25 net income of $3.29B is inflated by a $1.79B after-tax gain on the R&LC sale. The relevant earnings base is continuing operations. The company has beaten consensus in each of the past four quarters (5.4%, 4.9%, 3.9%, 2.7%), a consistent but decelerating-magnitude beat pattern that supports reasonable confidence in current estimates without implying a large upside surprise.

**Capital allocation.** JCI returned the entire R&LC proceeds via the $5.0B accelerated share repurchase completed in February 2026: 43.7M shares, roughly 22% below today's price. Approximately $4.5B remains authorized. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.40 ($1.51/yr FY25), a thin ~1.1% yield that is amply covered.

## Growth Analysis

The single most important leading indicator is the order book, and it is strongly positive. Within Q2, Americas orders grew +40% and Products & Systems orders +57%. The mechanism is direct: hyperscaler and colocation data-center construction is driving demand for applied HVAC and thermal management (including liquid cooling), which converts to JCI orders, then to backlog, then to revenue over the following several quarters. APAC Applied HVAC is growing 20%+ on the same data-center driver.

This is the cleanest expression of the broad-based data-center buildout within JCI's universe. JCI competes most directly with Vertiv and Trane in data-center cooling, and the order data shows it is participating in the cycle rather than ceding share.

Estimate revisions corroborate the direction but with modest velocity: next-year EPS revised +3.7% over 90 days. This is positive earnings momentum, not the +30% revision velocity that would mark a stock the market is systematically misjudging. The growth is real and broad-based, but consensus is broadly tracking it rather than chasing it.

Beyond data centers, secular tailwinds support a multi-year mid-single-digit organic growth base: decarbonization retrofits, EU and US building-performance regulation, and mission-critical verticals (hospitals, pharma, advanced manufacturing). The restructuring program targets $500M of annual savings by FY27 against $400M of cost, providing a margin-expansion lever independent of volume.

## Valuation Assessment

At $146.68, JCI trades at 44x trailing EPS of $2.63 and roughly 30x the FY26 adjusted EPS guide. Against the Building Products peer set, the comparison is unfavorable on trailing metrics: Trane 35.1x, Carrier 46.5x, Lennox 22.9x, Masco 18.3x, Allegion 18.3x, with a peer median of 22.9x. JCI sits at the upper end of the group, closest to Trane and Carrier, the two peers with the strongest data-center and HVAC exposure.

The cycle-adjustment argument applies partially. The trailing P/E overstates expense because FY25 was a transition year, and the forward multiple of ~30x on the adjusted guide is the more honest figure. But even on forward earnings, JCI is not cheap. The PEG, using consensus next-year EPS of $5.76 versus current-year $4.89 (implying ~18% growth), sits around 1.7x on forward earnings, above the 1.5x threshold that would flag mispricing. The stock has appreciated 38% over the past year, and while prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, the relevant question is whether the current price misprices forward value. It does not appear to. The order momentum is visible to the market, the guidance raise is public, and the multiple reflects it.

The analyst consensus reinforces a balanced read: median target $155, mean $154.90, range $111-$180. Current price sits ~7% below median. On a stock with positive but modest estimate revisions trading at 30x forward, the analyst target functions as a reasonable anchor rather than a stale floor; this is not the cheap-inflection profile where targets lag reality, nor the >50x premium where targets and price reflect the same optimism. The ~7% to median is a fair-value-plus call, not asymmetric upside.

We score valuation 4.5. The multiple is full relative to peers and growth, the PFAS overhang justifies part of the discount the market does not apply, and there is no specific reason to conclude the market is mispricing the stock to the upside at this level.

## Competitive Landscape

JCI operates in a consolidated oligopoly and its competitive trajectory is improving on two fronts. First, the direct field channel and installed base generate recurring service and retrofit revenue that competitors selling through distribution cannot replicate as efficiently, and management is deliberately growing the higher-margin services mix (32% of FY25 revenue). Second, the company is gaining traction in data-center thermal management, the most attractive growth pocket in commercial buildings, where it competes head-to-head with Vertiv and Trane. The +57% Products & Systems order growth and +40% Americas orders are evidence of share participation in a fast-growing segment, not erosion.

The breadth of the portfolio (HVAC, fire, security, controls, and the OpenBlue digital layer) is a genuine differentiator for large mission-critical projects that want a single integrated supplier. Margin trajectory supports the improving-position read: gross margin expanded ~120 bps in FY25 and continued higher in 1H FY26, while SG&A leverage suggests the operating culture is tightening.

The principal competitive risk is the same as the principal demand risk: commercial construction cyclicality, credit availability, and the durability of the data-center capex cycle. JCI's position within the group is improving; the open question is the cycle, not the company. We score competitive position 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**PFAS / AFFF litigation (the dominant tail risk).** Tyco Fire Products and Chemguard settled the nationwide public-water-systems class (fully paid, final $415M December 2024). The residual exposure is large and explicitly unquantifiable, nearly all consolidated in the South Carolina MDL. New suits continue to be filed (Perdue AgriBusiness, May 2026). The company recorded ~$131M of insurance recoveries in 1H FY26 but states it cannot estimate loss beyond the water-systems settlement. This is the structural reason for the perennial valuation overhang and is the item most capable of producing a material, hard-to-model liability. MDL bellwether outcomes are the key monitoring point.

**Environmental and asbestos.** The Marinette, WI PFAS matter carries $175M in environmental reserves with an active Wisconsin DOJ enforcement action being settled. Asbestos net liability is a modest, well-reserved $62M.

**Tax and structural.** Irish domicile creates Section 7874 inversion-recharacterization risk; there is a $6.3B deferred-tax valuation allowance and $1.9B of uncertain tax positions. The effective tax rate is rising under OECD Pillar Two (Q2 FY26 17.1% vs. 5.2% prior year), a headwind to reported EPS growth.

**Execution and macro.** The $400M restructuring and segment reorganization are proceeding amid unusually heavy executive turnover (frequent Item 5.02 filings through 2025-2026), a continuity watch item. Tariffs and supply-chain inflation have been largely mitigated to date. The data-center cycle that is driving order growth is also the concentration risk if hyperscaler capex decelerates.

We score risk factors 4.5. The PFAS tail is specific, material, and unquantifiable, and the rising tax rate plus reorg-related turnover are real near-term frictions. These are not fully priced given how strong the operating story reads.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options structure is in steep backwardation (1-week ATM IV 177.7% vs. long-term 32.7%), with the front-week reading distorted by a single expiry one day out; treat the 177.7% print as a thin-options artifact rather than a clean signal. The more informative tenors (1m IV 30.5%, 3m 36.2%, 6m 32.7%) are only modestly elevated against 1-year historical vol of 28.0%. Put/call OI ratio is low at 0.31 and skew is balanced-to-slightly-negative in the back months (-4.9% at 3m and 6m), indicating no concentrated bearish positioning. Short interest is low at 2.0% of float (2.6 days to cover). The options and positioning data do not contradict the fundamental view; the market is pricing ordinary uncertainty, not imminent stress at the relevant tenors.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($180).** The data-center cooling cycle proves durable, backlog continues converting at +20%+ growth, and the Weidemanis operating playbook drives sustained margin expansion past the FY27 restructuring target. EMEA and APAC EBIT growth holds, FY27 adjusted EPS approaches $6.00, and the PFAS overhang clears through manageable settlements as MDL bellwethers come in below feared levels. At 30x a $6.00 number, the stock reaches ~$180, matching the Street high. This requires both cycle durability and PFAS de-risking.

**Bear case ($111).** The data-center capex cycle decelerates, order growth reverts toward GDP, and a PFAS bellwether or AG settlement materializes at a multi-billion-dollar scale the market has not modeled. The premium multiple compresses toward the peer median on a flat-to-down adjusted EPS, taking the stock to the Street low of $111, roughly 24% downside from current levels.

**Base case ($155-160).** Backlog converts at a moderating but healthy mid-teens pace, margins expand on restructuring savings, FY26 adjusted EPS lands near the guide and FY27 reaches ~$5.40-5.60. The multiple holds near 28-30x as the data-center thesis stays intact but PFAS uncertainty persists. This delivers ~$155-160, roughly in line with the analyst median and ~7-10% above current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A meaningful PFAS de-risking event — a structured settlement of the personal-injury/AG claims at a quantifiable, market-absorbable level, or favorable MDL bellwether outcomes — that removes the overhang. This is the single most likely catalyst for a re-rating.
- A pullback to the low-$130s (sub-27x forward) that restores asymmetry while order momentum stays intact (backlog growth holding >20% YoY).
- Estimate revision velocity accelerating to >10% on forward EPS, signaling the market is underestimating the inflection rather than tracking it.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Backlog growth decelerating below +10% YoY or order growth turning negative, signaling the data-center cycle has rolled over.
- A PFAS bellwether or AG settlement at a scale (>$2-3B) that the balance sheet must absorb without insurance offset.
- Gross margin reversing below 36% with no offsetting volume, indicating the operating leverage story is unwinding.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q3 FY26, expected early August 2026), with backlog and order growth the primary data points to watch, and any MDL bellwether scheduling as the secondary catalyst. The score trajectory has declined from 6.6 to 5.7 across three reports, driven mostly by valuation compression in the scoring as the multiple expanded. New data partially reverses that trend on the operating side, since the order inflection is stronger than at the prior review, but the higher price offsets it on valuation, keeping the call at HOLD with a growth bias rather than upgrading.

## Conclusion

Johnson Controls is delivering a genuine operating inflection: data-center cooling demand is driving the order book, segment EBIT is compounding double-digits across all three regions, and a disciplined new CEO is converting the portfolio simplification into margin leverage. The business quality and growth durability are improving, and the buyback was a well-timed use of divestiture proceeds. These are the reasons to own the stock.

The reasons not to add at $144 are equally concrete. The multiple is full at 44x trailing and ~30x forward, a premium to peers that the modest estimate revisions do not justify as a mispricing, and the stock sits ~7% below an analyst median that functions as fair value rather than a stale floor. The unquantifiable PFAS litigation tail is a structural overhang the market prices through a perennial discount that operating strength alone will not remove. The asymmetry that would justify a BUY is not present at this entry point.

We rate JCI a HOLD with a growth rationale. The order momentum supports the position for investors who already own it; new capital should wait for either a PFAS de-risking event or a pullback toward the low-$130s before stepping up.
