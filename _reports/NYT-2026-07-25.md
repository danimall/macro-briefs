**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The New York Times Company has completed its transformation into a subscription-first digital media franchise, and the FY2025 and Q1 2026 results confirm the model is working better than the market has been willing to credit. Total revenue grew 9.2% to $2,825M in 2025 and accelerated to +12% in Q1 2026, driven by digital-only subscription revenue (+14.3% in 2025, +16.1% in Q1) and digital advertising (+20% in 2025, +31.6% in Q1). Operating margin expanded from 13.6% to 15.3% (19.5% adjusted), the company is debt-free with roughly $1.17B of cash and securities, and it generated $550M of reported free cash flow. Total subscribers reached a record ~13.08M by Q1 2026 against an explicit 15M-by-2027 target. The company has beaten consensus EPS in all four of the last quarters, most recently by 27.5%.

The offsetting question is generative AI, and it is genuinely two-sided. The 10-K states in expanded language that GenAI "has negatively impacted, and we expect will continue to negatively impact" referral traffic and top-of-funnel demand as users migrate from link-based search to zero-click AI answers. At the same time, NYT is monetizing content licensing to digital platforms (a growing line within affiliate/licensing revenue) and is the lead plaintiff in the copyright suit against Microsoft/OpenAI, where core copyright and trademark-dilution claims survived a motion to dismiss and the July 2026 sanctions motion suggests the case is hardening toward trial rather than settlement. The outcome could set industry precedent on training-data copyright, a binary catalyst that is difficult to handicap.

Our recommendation is HOLD (growth_hold). The bundle-driven subscriber engine, pricing power (ARPU rose to $9.77), and pristine balance sheet support continued double-digit digital growth and justify ownership for its 12-month appreciation potential. At ~30x trailing and ~22x next-year EPS on estimates that are stable-to-modestly-rising, the stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced. After a 35% run over the trailing year and a pullback from the April high of $87 to ~$71.65, the risk/reward is balanced. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the current data confirms the improving trajectory without providing the specific catalyst or valuation cushion that would justify an upgrade.

## Company Overview

NYT operates as a single reportable segment (reclassified in Q3 2025) built around its flagship general-interest news product and a bundle of interest-specific products: The Athletic (sports), Games (Wordle/Connections/Crossword), Cooking, Audio, and Wirecutter. The strategic core is the bundle, which drives multi-product engagement, higher ARPU, and lower churn, a differentiated model versus single-product news peers.

The subscriber base runs ~12.78M at year-end 2025 (a company record), rising to ~13.08M total / 12.52M digital-only by Q1 2026. International subscribers are ~26% of the digital-only base and content reaches 234 countries. The company holds the most Pulitzer prizes of any organization and the largest print circulation among U.S. seven-day newspapers. It employs ~6,000 FTEs, of which more than 3,000 are journalists.

Revenue mix is recurring-heavy: subscription ~69%, advertising ~20%, and affiliate/licensing/other ~11%. Print subscribers have fallen to ~570K of the base, and the story is the mix shift: digital growth is now more than offsetting secular print declines (print subscription −3.2%, print advertising −5.4% in 2025).

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is one of the strongest in publishing.

**Revenue and margins.** Total revenue rose from $2,308M (2022) to $2,825M (2025). Operating profit rose 22.9% to $431.6M in 2025 with the operating margin expanding to 15.3% from 13.6%. Q1 2026 operating margin jumped to 12.7% from 9.2% a year earlier. Adjusted operating margin was 19.5% and net margin 12.2% for 2025, with ROE of 16.9%. Adjusted diluted EPS was $2.46 (+22.4%), against GAAP diluted EPS of $2.09.

**ARPU and pricing power.** Digital ARPU rose to $9.68 for 2025 and $9.77 in Q1 2026, driven by promo-to-full-price graduation and price increases on tenured subscribers rather than discount-driven volume. Bundle ARPU ($12.67) sits well above the blended level, and the deliberate migration of subscribers into the bundle (bundle/multiproduct +24% to 6.48M while news-only fell 27% to 1.47M in 2025) is margin-accretive.

**Balance sheet.** The company is debt-free, holding ~$1.17B in cash and marketable securities at year-end 2025 with a $400M undrawn revolver extended to 2030. The Altman Z of 2.92 ("grey zone") is a model artifact: it reflects an asset-light, low-leverage business with a large equity/pension base and modest asset turnover, not credit risk. The reported debt/equity of 1.47 is likewise misleading; it captures operating and pension liabilities, not financial debt.

**Cash flow quality — normalize.** Operating cash flow was $584.5M (+42%) and reported FCF $550.5M (19.5% FCF margin). Two items flatter this figure: roughly $65M from OBBBA R&D immediate expensing and ~$33M from a one-time College Point land sale, both of which management explicitly flags as largely non-recurring beyond FY2026. The OBBBA benefit continues at ~$60M in 2026 but is not expected to recur after. Normalized underlying FCF is closer to ~$450–475M. This normalization matters for any DCF or FCF-yield framing and is the single most important adjustment for this name.

**Capital allocation.** Policy is to return ≥50% of FCF via dividends and buybacks over 3–5 years. The dividend was raised 28% to $0.23/quarter in February 2026; 2025 buybacks were ~$165M under a $350M authorization (approved Feb 2025) with ~$291M remaining as of Q1 2026. Share count is drifting down (163.2M basic in 2025 vs 166.9M in 2022). This is disciplined, but the yield is thin (~1.3%) and buybacks at ~30x trailing earnings are neither aggressive nor obviously value-accretive.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is durable and the momentum is intact. The 15M-subscriber-by-2027 target implies continued double-digit digital growth from the current base, and Q1 2026 added ~310K net digital subscribers sequentially. Digital advertising acceleration to +31.6% in Q1 is a standout for a legacy publisher, reflecting premium first-party-data targeting and brand-safe adjacency. Licensing revenue is a newer, quietly growing lever tied to commercial agreements with third-party digital platforms.

Estimate revisions confirm positive earnings momentum without signalling a violent re-rating. Current-year EPS consensus has risen +6.1% over 90 days to $2.88, and next-year to $3.22 (+2.9% over 90 days). These are steady upgrades, not the >30% velocity that would flag a trough-to-recovery re-rating candidate. The four-quarter beat streak (27.5%, 1.0%, 9.5%, 15.7%) supports confidence in the current consensus.

The counterweight is structural. GenAI is already eroding referral traffic, the top-of-funnel that historically fed subscription conversion. The bundle strategy is the defense: by cultivating direct habit and app engagement, NYT reduces reliance on search referral. Whether that defense fully offsets the disintermediation of the open web over a multi-year horizon is the central unresolved question for the growth thesis. For a 12-month view, the subscriber and margin trajectory remains firmly positive.

## Valuation Assessment

At $71.65 the stock trades at ~30x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.09), ~29x adjusted 2025 EPS ($2.46), ~25x current-year consensus ($2.88), and ~22x next-year consensus ($3.22). Against the Publishing peer set (median P/E 24.5), NYT trades at a premium on trailing metrics but roughly in line on next-year earnings, appropriate for a company with faster, higher-quality digital growth and a cleaner balance sheet than News Corp (33.4x), Wiley (15.5x), or Scholastic (18.0x).

Earnings are growing steadily off a healthy base, so trailing and forward metrics both apply. On a PEG basis, ~22x next-year against low-double-digit forward EPS growth puts PEG near ~1.7, fair rather than cheap. Normalized FCF against an ~$11.5B market cap implies a ~4% FCF yield, adequate but not compelling.

Consensus targets (mean $83.44, median $82, high $95, low $66) sit ~16% above the current price, offering genuine upside to the midpoint. Because estimate revisions are moderate rather than explosive, these targets are more credible as fair-value anchors than as stale floors that consensus will chase materially higher within weeks. The stock's underperformance versus its own chosen peer group (5-year TSR 142 vs peer 230) despite excellent operating results reflects the market's discount for the AI-disruption overhang and the temporary tax/land inflation of reported cash flow. That discount is warranted, which is why cheapness alone does not make this a buy. We score valuation 5.5 — fairly priced with modest upside but no identified mispricing that offers asymmetric return.

## Competitive Landscape

NYT is the best-positioned traditional publisher to survive AI disruption. Its bundle drives direct engagement and habit, reducing dependence on referral traffic; its trusted brand is a differentiator in an era of AI-generated misinformation; and it has the balance sheet and legal resources to both litigate against and license to AI companies. Within a structurally challenged industry, its relative winner status is clear: hundreds of local papers are suing OpenAI/Microsoft but lack NYT's scale, direct-subscription model, and liquidity.

The trajectory, however, has softened, which is why the competitive-position score has come down from a prior 7.5 to 5.5 across recent reports and we hold it there. The threat is no longer hypothetical: the 10-K now names AI chatbots and aggregators from Apple, Alphabet, ByteDance, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Perplexity, and X as direct competitors, and states that referral erosion is already occurring. The competitive question has shifted from "can NYT gain digital share" (yes, it is) to "can the bundle grow direct engagement fast enough to outrun the decay of the open-web funnel." On advertising, NYT continues to lose structural share to Google/Meta scale platforms and is a small player in short-form video, though its first-party-data premium ad business is growing. The moat is real and the company is executing, but the competitive environment is deteriorating even as NYT's own share within traditional publishing improves.

## Risk Assessment

**Generative AI disintermediation (structural, elevated).** The dominant risk, sharpened in the latest 10-K. Zero-click AI answers reduce the referral traffic that historically converted to subscriptions. This is a slow-burn threat that the bundle partially defends against but does not neutralize.

**AI copyright litigation (binary).** NYT v. Microsoft/OpenAI (consolidated MDL, SDNY) had its core copyright and trademark-dilution claims survive dismissal; the July 2026 sanctions motion alleging evidence concealment/destruction suggests escalation toward trial. NYT v. Perplexity (filed Dec 2025) is in early motion practice. NYT breaks out GenAI litigation costs as a special item ($13.3M in 2025, $4.2M in Q1 2026), manageable in size, but the outcome is a binary, precedent-setting catalyst that could swing sentiment sharply either direction.

**Platform dependency.** Discovery, referral, and app-store subscription rails depend on Apple and Alphabet, whose algorithm and fee changes NYT cannot control.

**Labor timing (near-term wildcard).** The NewsGuild contract (~23% of FTEs) expired Feb 28, 2026, and the Drivers/Machinists contracts (~3%) expired March 30, 2026, with negotiations open as of the filings. Given NYT's history of Guild and Tech Guild actions, a work stoppage or costly settlement is a plausible near-term headline not reflected in the smooth financials. Employee costs are the dominant, sticky cost base.

**Pension.** ~$225.9M pension liability plus ~$166M unfunded non-qualified plans and ~$60M multiemployer withdrawal liability. De-risked and not a solvency issue, but a persistent non-operating noise source subject to discount-rate/mortality volatility.

**Dual-class control.** The Ochs-Sulzberger trust holds ~95% of Class B and controls ~70% of the board; Class A holders elect only 30% and cannot force a change of control. This protects editorial independence but limits M&A optionality and minority influence.

**Disclosure reduction.** From Q1 2026, NYT stopped reporting subscriber sub-categories, reducing analyst visibility into the news-only decline and bundle migration.

We score risk 5.2, reflecting the combination of a structural AI overhang that is only partially priced, a binary litigation outcome, and a live near-term labor situation, against an otherwise low idiosyncratic risk profile (debt-free, diversified subscriber base).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 44.5% is rich versus 29.5% one-year historical vol (a 15-point spread, 72nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (44.5% near-term vs 34.6% six-month), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with an upcoming earnings print and the labor/litigation overhang. The 1-month implied move is ±9.6%. ATM IV jumped from 28.0% to 44.5% over the past month on essentially flat price, a signal the market is bracing for a catalyst. Put/call OI is roughly balanced at 1.13 and skew is flat near-term (0.1%) but turns modestly positive at three months (6.7% put skew) before flipping negative at six months (−5.5%). The elevated near-term put/call volume ratio (7.86) is noisy but leans defensive. The options market is not confirming a strong directional bear thesis, but it is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty. For a HOLD, the rich IV argues against chasing and favors patience on any entry until post-print vol normalizes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$95).** The bundle drives NYT to and past its 15M-subscriber-2027 target on continued double-digit digital growth; ARPU keeps climbing on price increases and mix shift; digital advertising sustains 20%+ growth; and the AI copyright litigation resolves favorably (a settlement or ruling that establishes a licensing regime NYT can monetize at scale). Margins expand toward the low-20s and next-year EPS beats $3.22 meaningfully, re-rating the multiple. This maps to the analyst high target of $95, ~33% above current.

**Bear case (~$60).** GenAI disintermediation accelerates faster than the bundle can defend, referral-driven conversion decays, and subscriber-add growth slows below the 15M path. The OpenAI suit produces an adverse or ambiguous ruling that undermines the licensing-revenue thesis. Labor negotiations produce a costly settlement or work stoppage. Normalized FCF becomes visible as the OBBBA and land-sale benefits roll off, disappointing investors anchored to the reported $550M. The multiple compresses toward the peer median on next-year earnings, taking the stock toward $60, ~16% below current.

**Base case (~$80).** Digital growth continues in the low-double-digits, margins grind higher, subscriber adds track roughly toward target, and the AI litigation remains unresolved but non-catastrophic. EPS reaches ~$3.20 next year and the stock trades at ~25x, roughly $80, about 12% above current and consistent with the consensus mean/median cluster of $82–83.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings print (near-term subscriber-add and digital-ad trajectory), resolution or escalation of the NewsGuild/Drivers labor negotiations, and any procedural milestone in the OpenAI MDL. The rich near-term IV and backwardation imply the market expects a catalyst within weeks.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$60s (near the analyst low of $66), where forward P/E compresses below ~20x and the ~4% normalized FCF yield turns to a genuine valuation cushion.
- A favorable, monetizable resolution of the OpenAI/Microsoft litigation (settlement establishing a scaled licensing regime), removing the primary overhang.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +15% over 90 days combined with subscriber-add re-acceleration above the ~310K/quarter Q1 pace, indicating the 15M target is being cleared with room to spare.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained deceleration in net digital subscriber adds below ~150K/quarter, or digital subscription revenue growth falling below ~8% YoY, signalling the AI-disintermediation bear thesis is materializing.
- An adverse OpenAI ruling that undermines both the copyright leverage and the licensing-revenue thesis.
- A prolonged labor work stoppage or a settlement that materially inflates the sticky employee cost base and compresses margins.
- Consensus current-year and next-year estimates turning negative on revision.

## Conclusion

NYT is a high-quality, debt-free franchise executing a successful digital transition with expanding margins, growing capital returns, and a defensible bundle model that positions it as the relative winner in a structurally challenged industry. The four-quarter beat streak and steadily rising estimates confirm the operating momentum, and the improving trajectory in our scores (financial health up, valuation modestly improved) reflects genuine fundamental progress rather than price action alone.

The stock has declined from ~$87 in April to ~$71.65, and on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals that improves the risk/reward at the margin. This is a ~18% drawdown from the high, not a dislocation, and the reasons the market applies a discount — the AI overhang, the binary litigation, the normalization of reported cash flow, and a live labor situation — are legitimate and only partially priced. At ~22x next-year EPS with ~12% upside to the consensus base case and ~16% downside to the bear, the return profile is balanced. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it is met but not exceeded: the company is worth owning for its 12-month growth thesis, but there is no asymmetric entry here and no specific catalyst that would justify an upgrade to BUY at the current price. We rate NYT HOLD with a base-case target of ~$80.
