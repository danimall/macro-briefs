## Executive Summary

Stryker's second-quarter 2026 report resolved the most important open question from the spring: the March 11 cybersecurity incident was disruptive but not structurally damaging. Q2 net sales of $6,589M grew 9.4% reported and 9.0% organic, adjusted operating margin expanded 170 basis points to 27.4%, and adjusted diluted EPS of $3.69 rose 17.9% and beat published non-GAAP consensus of $3.46-$3.49 by roughly 6%. That followed a first quarter in which derived adjusted EPS of $2.60 *declined* 8.5% year over year and adjusted operating margin compressed to approximately 21.1% on idle production time. The cyber drag on gross margin fell from roughly 100 basis points at the H1 level to 30 basis points in Q2 alone, the cleanest evidence of sequential normalization.

The problem is what management then guided to. FY2026 organic growth of 8.3%-9.3% against H1 actual organic of 5.8% implies H2 organic of roughly 10.6%-12.4%, or 160 to 340 basis points above the 9.0% Stryker just posted in its strongest quarter. On earnings, guided adjusted EPS of $14.95-$15.10 less H1 actual $6.29 implies H2 of $8.66-$8.81 versus $7.66 a year earlier, or +13.1% to +15.0%. The identifiable bridge exists (Inari backorders clearing "by the end of Q3" per CEO Kevin Lobo, cyber-delayed capital equipment shipping from an elevated backlog, and the Mako RPS partial-knee and Pangea trauma launches) but it requires all three to land. Consensus for the current quarter has already been trimmed 2.4% over 30 days and 3.2% over 90 days even as the full-year figure held at $15.01.

At $338.285 the shares trade at 22.5x the guidance midpoint, 20.2x next-year consensus of $16.76, 35.1x trailing GAAP EPS of $9.65, and 30.3x FY2025 free cash flow of $4,283M. Against a base case of $372 (22.2x FY2027 consensus), a bull case of $440 and a bear case of $278, probability-weighted return is roughly 6-7% including the 1.04% dividend. That is approximately fair for a high-quality MedTech compounder and insufficient to justify adding capital ahead of the October 31 annual goodwill test on the Peripheral Vascular reporting unit, whose fair value exceeded carrying value by only 12% at the last measurement date. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Stryker operates two reportable segments. MedSurg and Neurotechnology generated $15,647M of FY2025 sales (62.3% of total) across Instruments, Endoscopy, Medical and Vascular. Orthopaedics generated $9,469M (37.7%) across Knees, Hips, Trauma and Extremities, and the newly created Ortho Tech business. The Q1 2026 reorganization combined Mako, power tools, cutting accessories and enabling technologies into Ortho Tech, moving $518M of goodwill from MedSurg and Neurotechnology to Orthopaedics on a relative-fair-value basis and creating a new reporting unit while recomposing the Instruments and Joint Replacement units. Segment count is unchanged; the reporting-unit reshuffle matters because it resets the goodwill testing perimeter.

The stated capital allocation priority, repeated verbatim in the FY2025 10-K and Q2 2026 10-Q, is acquisitions first, dividends second, share repurchases third. That ordering explains the balance sheet: goodwill of $19,584M plus net other intangibles of $5,742M equals $25,326M, or 52.8% of $47,930M total assets, leaving tangible book equity at negative $1,338M.

Growth is a volume-and-mix story, not a pricing story. FY2025 organic growth of 10.3% decomposed to +9.9% volume and +0.4% price; H1 2026 decomposed to +5.6% volume and +0.2% price. The United States was 75.7% of FY2025 sales and 75.3% of Q2 2026 sales.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory.** Net sales grew from $20,498M (FY2023) to $22,595M (FY2024, +10.2%) to $25,116M (FY2025, +11.2%), with constant-currency growth of 10.7% in each of the last two years. Adjusted operating margin expanded from 24.2% to 25.3% to 26.3%. The FY2024 GAAP operating margin dip to 16.3% is fully explained by $977M of impairments: a $456M goodwill write-off on Spine ($273M at the October 2024 annual test plus $183M allocated to the held-for-sale disposal group) and a $362M estimated loss on held-for-sale classification.

**The Q2 margin figure requires care.** The 10-Q gross-profit bridge attributes +260 basis points of Q2 gross-margin expansion to a $158M reversal of 2025 tariffs. Management excluded that item from adjusted results; the reconciliation removes $158M from gross and operating income, $133M of net earnings and $0.34 of EPS. So the headline 27.4% adjusted operating margin and $3.69 adjusted EPS are clean of the reversal. GAAP operating margin of 25.2% is not: stripping the $158M gives ($1,659 − $158) ÷ $6,589 = 22.8%. Any year-over-year GAAP margin comparison that ignores this overstates improvement by roughly 240 basis points.

**Cash generation is high quality but not cheap.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $5,044M against free cash flow of $4,283M, implying capex of $761M, or 3.0% of sales, a capital-light manufacturing model. FCF margin of 17.1% exceeded the 12.9% GAAP net margin, confirming operating cash flow above net income. H1 2026 FCF of $1,474M is up 39.7% year over year, but the 10-Q attributes the improvement primarily to working-capital timing, and H1 is seasonally depressed (accrued compensation fell $490M as prior-year bonuses were paid). Doubling H1 gives $2,948M versus FY2025 actual of $4,283M; applying FY2025's H1-to-full-year ratio gives roughly $6.0B, which is too aggressive with cyber remediation continuing. The defensible statement is FY2026 FCF of $4.3B to modestly above, and the interim data does not narrow that.

**Leverage is conservative.** Total debt fell from $15,859M at December 31, 2025 to $14,942M at June 30, 2026, achieved by repaying $1,000M of 3.500% senior notes in March, partly offset by $250M of commercial paper against a $3,000M program. Net debt of $11,466M against LTM adjusted operating income of $6,792M is 1.69x. LTM interest coverage on GAAP EBIT is 9.2x. Current ratio is 2.16x, covenants are in compliance, and $2,910M of facility capacity is undrawn against a $3,000M revolver maturing February 2030. The maturity ladder is flat: $1,000M in 2026, $3,988M across 2027-2028, $4,256M across 2029-2030.

Two commonly cited screening figures are wrong and worth correcting. A "debt-to-equity of 1.13" is total liabilities divided by equity ($25,424 ÷ $22,420); actual total debt to book equity was 0.71x at year-end and 0.62x at June 30. An Altman Z-score of 2.22 substitutes *book* equity for market equity in the fourth term; using the actual $129.7B market value against $25,424M of liabilities lifts that term from 0.529 to 3.061 and the score from 2.22 to approximately 4.75, comfortably in the safe zone. Stryker is not a moderate-distress credit. The legitimate observation the screen surfaces (asset-heaviness from serial acquisition, reflected in the low 0.525 asset-turnover input) stands independently.

**Returns on capital.** NOPAT of $5,726M (LTM adjusted EBIT $6,792M taxed at the 15.7% H1 adjusted rate) against invested capital of $35,454M gives ROIC of 16.2%; excluding goodwill, 36.1%. On a GAAP-EBIT basis at the 28.1% FY2025 effective rate, ROIC is 11.2%. The 500-basis-point spread between those two figures is the price of acquired growth: adjusted EBIT excludes $732M of annual purchased-intangible amortization, which is a real economic cost of the M&A strategy even if non-cash.

**The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is widening.** Adjusted net earnings exceeded GAAP by $901M (28.5%) in 2023, $1,707M (57.0%) in 2024 and $2,021M (62.3%) in 2025. The 2025 gap decomposes to tax matters $660M, purchased-intangible amortization $581M, other acquisition and integration $299M, structural optimization $140M, inventory step-up $131M and impairments $120M. Only roughly $712M is non-cash by construction. A company that reports "special" charges of this magnitude every year for three consecutive years is describing a recurring cost of its operating model.

**A data-basis note.** The supplied surprise table appears offset by one quarter: the row labeled 2026-06-30 shows actual EPS of $2.60 against a $3.01 estimate (-13.7%), which reconciles to derived Q1 2026 adjusted EPS, not to Q2's reported $3.69. Two rows also carry identical $4.44/$4.47 values. The usable read: Stryker historically beats by 0.7-0.8%, missed materially only in the cyber-affected quarter, and beat by roughly 6% in Q2 2026. Similarly, the profile's forward P/E and trailing P/E are both reported as 34.97, which is internally inconsistent with guided EPS growth; I use guidance and consensus for all forward multiples.

## Growth Analysis

Q2 2026 organic growth was led by Orthopaedics at 8.6% organic, with Trauma and Extremities +11.9% and Ortho Tech +10.3%. International organic growth was 8.9%, led by Australia, Germany, Canada, South Korea, Japan, India and Brazil. Mako surpassed 2.5 million cumulative procedures across 47 countries and posted its best second quarter, with the handheld Mako RPS partial-knee system now in full commercial rollout, Incompass total ankle launched, and Pangea trauma plating in limited European release ahead of a Q4 2026 full rollout.

Two lines are decelerating. Hips grew only 2.9% reported and 2.6% constant currency in Q2, with international hips down 0.8% in constant currency, the weakest franchise in the portfolio. More consequentially, Vascular fell 1.8% in constant currency, with US Vascular down 6.7% to $250M from $268M. Q2 2026 is the first clean like-for-like comparison for the Inari platform, which closed in February 2025 and distorted the H1 base (H1 US Vascular shows +12.5%, essentially calendar effect). Management attributes the shortfall to a plant and supply issue with backorders reaching manageable levels by the end of Q3, not to lost demand.

The forward math is the binding constraint. Weighting H1 2025 revenue at 47.3% of FY2025, solving 0.473(5.8%) + 0.527(X) for the guided FY range yields required H2 organic of 10.6% at the low end, 11.5% at the midpoint and 12.4% at the high end. On earnings, implied H2 adjusted EPS of $8.66-$8.81 against $7.66 a year ago requires 13.1%-15.0% growth versus H1's 5.4%. Management narrowed rather than raised guidance, with CFO Preston Wells citing "better visibility," which means they had roughly a month of Q3 data when they set it. That is a meaningful vote of confidence, but consensus for the current quarter has still been cut 2.4% in 30 days, and full-year consensus of $15.01 sits marginally *below* the guidance midpoint of $15.025.

Longer term, next-year consensus of $16.76 implies 11.7% EPS growth off the current-year figure, consistent with the company's high-single-digit organic sales aspiration plus 50-100 basis points of annual margin expansion plus modest share count reduction. That is a credible durable algorithm. The medium-term risks to it are the proposed Medicare cuts for large joint replacement procedures, which analysts pressed on the Q2 call and which management expects to be softened by compromise (magnitude and effective date unverified), and the disclosed structural mix headwind as lower-gross-margin MedSurg outgrows Orthopaedics.

Management has said repurchases will resume in H2, but a materially larger authorization would be required to change the capital-return arithmetic.

Fair value on this evidence is $350-$380. Current price sits at the low end of that band, a discount that does not clear a BUY bar.

## Competitive Landscape

The durable advantages are the robotics installed base and the breadth of the hospital relationship. Mako at 2.5 million cumulative procedures creates switching costs at the account level for knees specifically, and the RPS partial-knee launch extends that franchise into a procedure class where Stryker previously had thinner coverage. Selling capital equipment, implants, disposables and clinical communication into the same accounts supports the stated goal of growing at the high end of MedTech, which Stryker has done: 10.3% organic in FY2025 against a peer set where Smith+Nephew cut its revenue outlook on orthopaedic weakness in early August 2026 (headline only; not independently verified).

Segment operating margins now favor Orthopaedics: 34.0% versus MedSurg and Neurotechnology at 28.1% in Q2 2026, having been near-identical in FY2025 (29.8% and 29.9%). The disclosed expectation that lower-gross-margin MedSurg grows faster is therefore a genuine mix headwind, offset so far by SG&A leverage: Q2 SG&A fell to 33.8% of sales from 34.5% on spend discipline and lower acquisition-related charges.

The competitive question sits in Vascular. Stryker has deployed roughly $5.5B into Peripheral Vascular in fifteen months: Inari at $4,810M net of cash in February 2025, and AVS at $706M in May 2026 ($435M net cash plus contingent consideration carried at $271M against a $400M maximum). The AVS allocation is telling: $404M of the $706M went to in-process R&D with no developed-technology or customer-relationship intangibles, meaning it is a development-stage asset in intravascular lithotripsy, a category where an established competitor has been commercial for years. The first clean organic quarter for the Inari platform was -6.7% in the US. Management's plant-issue explanation is plausible and specific, but the combination of a large acquired platform posting negative organic growth and a pre-revenue follow-on purchase into the same category is the least attractive part of the portfolio.

The Spine exit is a useful precedent in both directions. Management took roughly $1B of charges to leave a structurally challenged category and sold the disposal group to Viscogliosi Brothers in April 2025, evidence of willingness to admit a bad position. It also demonstrated that a Stryker reporting unit can move from no disclosed impairment risk to a $456M write-off within one annual test cycle when the competitive environment shifts. Residual consideration on that sale can still increase by up to $57M or decrease by up to $245M, an open exposure skewed roughly 4:1 to the downside.

## Risk Assessment

**Peripheral Vascular goodwill (October 31, 2026 test).** At the October 31, 2025 test, the Peripheral Vascular reporting unit's fair value exceeded carrying value by 12% on $3,203M of goodwill; every other reporting unit exceeded by at least 100%. Disclosed sensitivities show a +100bps discount-rate change producing a 14% fair-value decline and a $198M charge, while a -100bps cut to long-term revenue growth produces zero impairment. That asymmetry cuts against the intuitive reading: a transient supply disruption plausibly causes no charge, while a rise in the unit's cost of capital does. The $198M charge itself is 0.15% of market capitalization and 3.0% of FY2025 adjusted operating income, a headline event rather than a solvency event. Its significance would be as confirmation that $5.5B of capital was deployed at the top of a category.

**Cybersecurity remediation.** The disclosure sequence ran from an Item 8.01 filing on March 11, 2026 to an Item 1.05 materiality determination in the April 9 8-K/A. Stryker has quantified the margin effect (-100bps H1 gross margin, -30bps in Q2) but has not disclosed a total dollar impact on revenue or earnings. H1 structural optimization and other special charges rose to $213M from $52M, with "Other charges" of $93M in H1 and $70M in Q2 alone; the 10-Q does not attribute that line to cyber, and the Q1 reorganization is an equally plausible driver. Management concluded disclosure controls were effective at June 30, 2026 with no change to internal control over financial reporting, a meaningful absence of a control failure given the incident's scale. Third-party attribution claims (Iran-linked hacktivists; 200,000 systems wiped) are unconfirmed by any company filing. Remediation costs continue through 2026.

**Tax structure.** The FY2025 GAAP effective rate of 28.1% versus an adjusted rate of 15.1% reflects a $(718)M charge for intra-group intellectual-property transfers. H1 2026 carried $(75)M of the same, with a 15.7% adjusted rate. A move to a 21% effective rate would reduce adjusted earnings by roughly 6.3%, or about $0.94 per share against the $15.025 guidance midpoint, the single largest identifiable threat to the earnings base. Direction is genuinely uncertain: the OECD's January 2026 administrative guidance introduced safe harbors that appear favorable to US-headquartered multinationals but must be legislated domestically. Separately, German assessments of $754M plus $11M expected for 2010-2017 stand against a total uncertain-tax-position reserve of $403M across all jurisdictions, with Stryker intending to defend its positions and pursue offsetting foreign tax credits. At roughly 0.6% of market capitalization this is a cash-flow item, not an equity-story item.

**Structural.** Negative tangible book equity of $1,338M; goodwill and intangibles at 52.8% of assets; contingent consideration balance rising from $518M to $756M; cash held outside the US jumping from 20% to 51% in six months with no explanation offered in the 10-Q, which interacts with the domestic buyback commitment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.4% sits 4.0 points above 27.4% trailing realized volatility, with a backwardated term structure (31.4% at 13 days versus 29.2% at six months) and a ±4.8% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Positioning does not corroborate a directional bearish read: put/call volume is 0.39, put/call open interest 0.51 (call-tilted), and near-term skew is 1.2%, described as balanced. Put/call open interest did rise from 0.18 to 0.51 since mid-July, so some protection was added into and after the print, but the absolute level remains call-heavy. Options are modestly rich for buyers here, which argues against paying for near-term upside optionality and mildly favors covered-call overlays for existing holders.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($440, +30.1%).** H2 organic delivers within the guided 10.6%-12.4% range as Inari backorders clear by the end of Q3, cyber-delayed capital equipment ships from an elevated backlog, and Mako RPS, Incompass and Pangea contribute. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at or above $15.10, FY2027 estimates move above $16.90, Vascular returns to growth and the October 31 test passes without charge. The Medicare joint-replacement proposal is softened as management expects. A new and larger buyback authorization replaces the stale 2015 mandate. On restored confidence the multiple re-rates toward 26x FY2027, still below Stryker's 2024 level and far below ISRG and EW, giving 26 × $16.90 = $439.

**Bear case ($278, -17.8%).** H2 organic comes in at 8-9% rather than the required 10.6%-12.4%, meaning FY2026 organic lands below the 8.3% low end and adjusted EPS near $14.60. Vascular weakness proves demand-driven rather than supply-driven, the October 31 test produces a nine-figure charge, and a higher discount rate compounds it. The Medicare cut lands closer to proposal than compromise, Pillar 2 legislation pushes the effective rate toward the high teens, and hips deceleration spreads. FY2027 estimates are cut to roughly $15.45 and the multiple compresses to 18x: 18 × $15.45 = $278, at the 52-week low of $281.

**Base case ($372, +10.0%).** H2 organic runs 9-10.5%, delivering FY2026 organic near or slightly below the 8.3% low end with adjusted EPS at $14.90-$15.05, close enough that the year reads as a hit. Vascular stabilizes but does not accelerate. FY2027 consensus holds near $16.76 and the multiple holds at the current 22.2x forward: 22.2 × $16.76 = $372, plus 1.04% dividend for an 11% total return.

Weighting base 50%, bull 20% and bear 30% gives an expected value of $357 and a probability-weighted 12-month total return of approximately 6.7%. That is at or slightly below a reasonable cost of equity for a name of this quality in a 4.6% ten-year yield environment.

**On the score trajectory.** Scores have fallen from 7.2 in February to 6.3 in July, with management quality down 2.5 points and growth prospects down 1.8. Q2 arrests the operational component of that decline (margin expansion of 170 basis points, organic growth restored to 9.0%, debt down $917M) but does not reverse the two dimensions that drove it. Management quality remains constrained by the absence of any open-market insider purchase, roughly $98M of May 2026 sales (dominated by a founding-family director's $96.8M diversification, which carries little informational content, alongside the chief legal and chief HR officers reducing positions), compensation metrics that contain no return-on-capital measure for a company whose first capital-allocation priority is acquisitions, zero repurchases in H1 with the stock 15-25% off its high, and a $1,033M authorization dating to March 2015. Growth prospects remain constrained by the H2 arithmetic. The stock is 5.7% higher than at the prior review on genuinely better fundamentals; the multiple absorbed the improvement, leaving risk/reward roughly unchanged. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and it is a HOLD because the base case return is fair rather than because the evidence is thin.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Twelve-month horizon, anchored to the late-October Q3 print and the October 31 annual goodwill test.

- **Base case target: $372** — 22.2x FY2027 consensus of $16.76, holding the current forward multiple as estimates roll forward. +10.0% from $338.285, +11.0% with the dividend.
- **Bull case target: $440** — 26x FY2027 EPS of $16.90 after H2 delivery at the guidance high end. +30.1%.
- **Bear case target: $278** — 18x a cut FY2027 estimate of $15.45. -17.8%.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:** (1) Q3 organic growth prints at or above 10.5%, validating the H2 bridge; (2) US Vascular returns to positive year-over-year growth, confirming the supply explanation and de-risking the $3,203M goodwill balance; (3) the board authorizes a new repurchase program of at least $5B (roughly 3.9% of market capitalization) with repurchases actually executed; (4) the shares trade to $300-$310, which would place them at roughly 18x FY2027 consensus and restore double-digit expected return in the base case.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:** (1) FY2026 organic guidance is cut below 8.3% at the Q3 print; (2) the October 31 test produces a Peripheral Vascular impairment accompanied by a downward revision to that unit's growth outlook rather than a pure discount-rate explanation; (3) US Vascular declines for a second consecutive quarter after management's end-of-Q3 backorder commitment; (4) the adjusted effective tax rate guides above 18% on legislated Pillar 2 implementation, which would remove roughly $0.90-$1.00 of EPS.

**Reassessment date:** late October 2026 (Q3 earnings), with the October 31 annual impairment test result disclosed in the FY2026 10-K in February 2027.

## Conclusion

Stryker is a high-quality compounder trading at a fair price with an unusually well-specified near-term test. The business emerged from a material cyberattack with margins expanding, organic growth back to 9.0%, leverage at 1.69x net debt to adjusted EBIT, 9.2x interest coverage and ROIC of 16% on adjusted EBIT. The Mako installed base at 2.5 million cumulative procedures is a genuine moat, and Stryker is outgrowing a stumbling orthopaedic peer set.

Three things prevent a more constructive rating. First, guidance requires H2 organic growth of 10.6%-12.4% against the 9.0% just delivered, and quarterly consensus is already drifting down. Second, at 22.5x guided adjusted earnings, 35.1x trailing GAAP and 30.3x free cash flow, the price does not compensate for that execution requirement; expected return is roughly 6-7% against 18% downside if the bridge fails. Third, the capital-allocation record in Peripheral Vascular, where $5.5B has been deployed into a platform now shrinking organically in the US with a 12% goodwill cushion facing an October test, does not support paying up.

Existing holders should stay. New capital has a better entry available near $300-$310, or a cleaner signal available in late October.
