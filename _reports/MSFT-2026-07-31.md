## Executive Summary

Microsoft closed FY2026 (June 30, 2026) with revenue of $331.8B, up 17.8%, operating income of $155.2B, up 20.8%, and GAAP diluted EPS of $17.95, up 31.6%. The operational headline is Azure: full-year growth of 41% took the business above $100B in annual revenue for the first time, and fourth-quarter growth *accelerated* to 43% in constant currency from 40% in Q3, with management guiding to 45% for Q1 FY2027. Acceleration at that revenue base is difficult to manufacture, because converting remaining performance obligations into revenue requires physically delivered capacity. Alongside it, Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud grew 17% on only 6% seat growth, and paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats rose from above 20 million in April to above 30 million in June. Consensus estimates are rising modestly (+0.8% for FY2027, +2.6% for FY2028 over 90 days) and the company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters, most recently by 9.5%.

The financial character of the company changed at the same time. Cash additions to property and equipment roughly doubled to $115.9B, lifting capex intensity from 22.9% to 34.9% of revenue. Operating cash flow rose $46.8B to $182.9B, yet free cash flow fell roughly 6% to about $67.0B from $71.6B. Contractual obligations reached $743.8B, of which $241.9B is due in FY2027 — $59B more than FY2026 operating cash flow — including $169.0B of largely take-or-pay purchase commitments equal to 51% of FY2026 revenue. Earnings quality also requires attention: the OpenAI recapitalization dilution gain added $0.67 to GAAP EPS (excluded from non-GAAP), while a $3.2B Q4 gain on the Anthropic stake appears to sit *inside* adjusted results under Microsoft's stated non-GAAP definition, which excludes OpenAI marks only. At a 19% tax rate that is roughly $0.35 per share against a $0.50 Q4 adjusted-EPS beat.

At $463.62 the stock trades at 25.8x FY2026 GAAP EPS, 26.8x FY2026 adjusted EPS, 23.8x FY2027 consensus of $19.50, and 20.1x FY2028 consensus of $23.08 — a PEG of about 1.3 on 18% forward EPS growth. Those multiples are not demanding for a business compounding revenue at 16-18% with 46.8% operating margins. The free-cash-flow yield of 1.95% on declining FCF is less comfortable, and two FY2027 accounting changes (building useful lives extended from 15 to 25 years; more future datacenter leases classified as operating rather than finance leases) mean forward EPS and capex figures are not comparable to FY2026 on a like-for-like basis. The stock has recovered from $349.20 in late June to $463.62, roughly 18% above the average price at which Microsoft itself repurchased shares in March. That combination of improving operations, degrading cash conversion, a rebuilt entry price, and a large single-counterparty obligation concentration supports holding rather than adding.

## Company Overview

Microsoft reports in three segments. **Productivity and Business Processes** generated $140.0B (42% of revenue, +16%) at a 60.0% operating margin, driven by Microsoft 365 Commercial (+16%, cloud portion +17% on 6% seat growth with the balance from revenue-per-user expansion tied to Copilot and E5), Microsoft 365 Consumer (+24%), LinkedIn (+11%) and Dynamics 365 (+18%). **Intelligent Cloud** generated $137.8B (42%, +30%) at a 41.3% operating margin, with server products and cloud services up 31% and Azure up 41%; the on-premises server-license line grew only 1%. **More Personal Computing** generated $54.1B (16%, −1%) at a 26.6% margin, the only declining segment: Windows and Devices −1%, Xbox −7% with hardware −29%, search advertising +9%. Consolidated Microsoft Cloud revenue rose 27% to $214.4B, with Microsoft Cloud gross margin declining to 66%. Commercial remaining performance obligations reached $678B, up 84%.

The strategic architecture shifted twice in seven months. The October 2025 OpenAI recapitalization converted the for-profit arm into a public benefit corporation, removed the profit cap, and gave Microsoft a 27% stake while ending Microsoft's exclusive compute rights. The April 2026 agreement went further: OpenAI may now license its models on any cloud, Microsoft no longer pays OpenAI a share of Microsoft AI revenue, and what OpenAI owes Microsoft is now subject to a fixed ceiling rather than running indefinitely. Microsoft retains licensed IP rights to OpenAI models and infrastructure. In parallel, Anthropic's Claude reached general availability on Microsoft Foundry, hosted on Azure and billed through Azure commitments, and Microsoft is building its own models and silicon. The repositioning is toward being the neutral infrastructure and distribution layer for whichever frontier models win, monetized through Azure consumption. Microsoft is simultaneously an investor in, supplier to, and competitor of both leading labs; Anthropic and OpenAI are model owners and Azure commercial counterparties, not Microsoft products.

## Financial Analysis

| ($M except EPS) | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 331,839 | 281,724 | +17.8% |
| Gross margin | 225,465 | 193,893 | +16.3% |
| Gross margin % | 67.9% | 68.8% | −0.9pt |
| Operating income | 155,237 | 128,528 | +20.8% |
| Operating margin | 46.8% | 45.6% | +1.2pt |
| Other income (expense), net | 10,697 | (4,901) | +15,598 |
| Net income | 133,749 | 101,832 | +31.3% |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $17.95 | $13.64 | +31.6% |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $17.28 | $14.13 | +22.3% |

**Operating leverage is real and driven by expense restraint.** Operating expenses rose only 7% ($4.9B) against 18% revenue growth: R&D +9% to $35.6B (11% of revenue from 12%), sales and marketing +4% to $26.7B (8% from 9%), G&A +10% to $8.0B. The AI buildout runs through cost of revenue — Intelligent Cloud cost of revenue rose 44% against 30% segment revenue growth — so gross margin compressed 90bp while operating margin expanded 120bp. That is a favorable outcome, but the mechanism is opex discipline rather than unit economics improvement, and opex growth of 7% against 18% revenue growth is not indefinitely repeatable.

**Non-operating income did meaningful work.** Other income swung $15.6B, from −$4,901M to +$10,697M. The 10-K discloses $6.5B of net FY2026 gains from OpenAI investments versus $4.8B of net losses in FY2025 — an $11.3B swing, about 72% of the total — primarily the dilution gain from the recapitalization. Net of tax, OpenAI contributed $4,963M to net income and $0.67 to diluted EPS, and is excluded from non-GAAP. Excluding OpenAI, other income still improved from −$138M to +$4,167M, meaning roughly $4.3B of additional non-operating, largely mark-to-market income sits inside adjusted results. Contemporaneous reporting identifies a $3.2B Q4 gain on the Anthropic stake against a ~$600M OpenAI investment loss in the same quarter. Because Microsoft's stated non-GAAP definition excludes OpenAI marks only, the Anthropic gain appears to remain in adjusted EPS: at a 19% effective tax rate and ~7.44B diluted shares that is approximately $0.35 per share against a Q4 adjusted-EPS beat of $0.50 ($4.74 versus $4.24 consensus, comparing adjusted to adjusted). I have not read the Q4 press-release reconciliation, so treatment could differ; the tax rate and share count are estimates. This is a disclosure-comparability observation, not an accounting allegation, and the direction reverses in years when private AI marks are negative, as they were in FY2025.

**Cash conversion deteriorated.** Operating cash flow rose 34% to $182.9B. Investing outflows rose $66.9B to $139.5B, driven by a $51.4B increase in additions to PP&E (to ~$115.9B) and a $22.2B increase in "other investing primarily to facilitate the purchase of components." Free cash flow, measured conventionally as OCF less cash capex, was approximately $67.0B in FY2026 versus $71.6B in FY2025 — down 6% — with FCF margin falling from 25.4% to 20.2%. Q4 alone showed capex plus finance leases of $41B (+69%) against free cash flow of $19.64B (−23%). Dividends of $27.0B and buybacks of $16.7B totalled $43.7B, or 65% of FCF, versus a far lower share of a larger FCF base historically.

The $22.2B component-financing item deserves flagging. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q disclosed $17.8B of other receivables related to facilitating server-component purchases (from $8.2B) plus $11.5B of restricted investments pursuant to a supplier agreement. This is economically part of the AI buildout but sits outside capex, so it is invisible to standard capex-intensity and FCF screens. Whether it carries third-party credit exposure or is straightforward supply prepayment is unresolved from the filings read.

**The balance sheet remains strong in absolute terms and thinner at the margin.** Cash and short-term investments of $76.8B are down from $94.6B. Long-term debt principal is $46.1B with only $9.25B due in FY2027; interest expense of $3.05B against $155.2B of operating income is trivial coverage. Equity and other investments rose from $15.4B to $36.3B, consistent with the OpenAI revaluation plus the Anthropic position. Unearned revenue is $75.7B, $73.0B recognizable within four quarters.

The commitment stack is where the strain shows. FY2027 contractual obligations of $241.9B — $169.0B purchase commitments, $32.4B leases, $29.8B construction, $10.7B debt principal and interest — exceed FY2026 operating cash flow of $182.9B by $59B. Total lease obligations including imputed interest reached $443.5B, against roughly $306B on a comparable basis at March 31 (on-balance-sheet undiscounted payments of ~$109.5B plus ~$196.6B of leases signed but not commenced); the two disclosures may differ in scope and discounting, so the implied sequential increase should be treated as indicative. Purchase commitments are described as primarily datacenter open purchase orders and take-or-pay contracts, so a meaningful portion is non-discretionary near-term. Much of this overlaps with rather than adds to planned capex, and management states liquidity and capital-markets access are sufficient. FY2027 is likely to involve external financing.

**Financial health score: 6.8.** Near-zero net leverage, 46.8% operating margins, and $182.9B of operating cash flow are among the most resilient financial profiles in the market. Against that: FCF declining, capex intensity at 34.9%, FY2027 obligations exceeding operating cash flow, an unquantified component-financing exposure, an unresolved $28.9B IRS proposed adjustment, and a risk factor that now explicitly names continued access to capital and Microsoft's own credit ratings as conditions of the strategy.

## Growth Analysis

Azure is the durable engine. Q4 acceleration to 43% constant currency against roughly 40% consensus, and Q1 FY2027 guidance of 45% versus 41.4% consensus. Q1 FY2027 revenue guidance of $89.85–90.95B (+16% at midpoint) also exceeded the $89.66B consensus. Management guided to further capex growth in FY2027 citing demand signals, and to positive free cash flow.

Commercial RPO is the metric most likely to be misread. External reporting attributes to a January 2026 Microsoft statement that roughly 45% of the then-$625B RPO was tied to OpenAI — approximately $281B with a single pre-IPO counterparty. I could not locate a June 30, 2026 concentration figure in the filings; the 10-Q's "no customer above 10% of revenue" is a revenue test that says nothing about RPO. Microsoft stated that Q4's 8% sequential RPO growth was driven by commitments from clients other than AI model developers. That is a genuine diversification signal for incremental bookings, but a change in the source of *sequential* growth does not establish that absolute concentration has fallen.

Copilot monetization is working through price rather than units. The balance of Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud growth came from revenue per user. GitHub Copilot has 50 million users, and the new E7 bundle has "hundreds of enterprise customers" buying "millions of seats." ARPU-led growth is high-margin and capital-light, which is attractive; it is also more finite than seat growth and more exposed to the price competition and commoditization that Item 1A now names directly. Microsoft discloses no Copilot revenue, churn or utilization, so seat counts cannot be converted into revenue durability.

Consensus embeds continued compounding: FY2027 EPS of $19.50 (+12.8% on FY2026 adjusted) and FY2028 of $23.08 (+18.4%). Ninety-day revisions are positive but small — a supportive signal, well short of the velocity that would change a rating on its own. Part of FY2027 EPS growth will come from the depreciation-life extension rather than operations, and the magnitude has not been quantified.

The drags are concentrated and identifiable. More Personal Computing fell 1% for the year and 4.4% in Q4; Windows OEM sales fell 7% in Q4 against estimated PC shipments down 4.2%, so Microsoft underperformed the unit market, with MD&A noting elevated channel inventory. Xbox fell 7% for the year and 10% in Q4, hardware down 29%, with an unquantified impairment, job cuts, and four studios spinning out. Server products grew 1%. These segments are roughly 16% of revenue and 9% of segment operating income, so they cap consolidated growth without threatening the thesis.

**Growth prospects score: 7.2.**

A note on the supplied data: the profile reports trailing EPS of $13.64 with a P/E of 26.87. Those are inconsistent — $13.64 is FY2025 GAAP EPS, while 26.87x corresponds to FY2026 adjusted EPS of about $17.25. I use the filed FY2026 figures.

The supplied Systems Software peer set is not usable for relative valuation: PANW at 243.7x, CRWD with negative earnings, NOW at 61.4x, FTNT at 56.2x, TWLO at 315.3x, median 243.7x. Microsoft at 23.8x forward looks dramatically cheap against that median, but those companies are one-tenth to one-thirtieth of its scale with different GAAP earnings quality. The relevant comparison set is the hyperscaler cohort. On that basis Microsoft is fairly priced, not obviously mispriced.

Three considerations argue against a high valuation score despite an undemanding P/E. First, the earnings base is flattered. FY2026 GAAP EPS includes $0.67 from the OpenAI dilution gain, and adjusted EPS appears to include roughly $0.35 of Q4 Anthropic mark — non-cash, unrealized, and reversible if private AI valuations retrace. Second, the FY2027 denominator is not clean: extending building useful lives from 15 to 25 years reduces depreciation on a rapidly growing PP&E base with no cash effect, and the benefit has not been sized. Third, on cash the stock is expensive: a 1.95% FCF yield on FCF that declined 6% is a full price, and the lease reclassification will shift future datacenter payments from financing into operating outflows, mechanically reducing conventionally-measured FCF in FY2027.

The bull argument on valuation is that the multiple compressed while the business accelerated — down 19% year-to-date through July 29 against an S&P 500 up 7%, while Azure moved from 40% to 43% growth. That divergence was the buying opportunity, and Microsoft itself repurchased into it at monthly average prices of $465.49 in January, $402.72 in February and $391.53 in March, with FY2026 repurchases up 28% to $16.7B (from $13.0B). At $463.62 the stock is roughly 18% above the March average and has recovered most of the drawdown. Consensus targets (mean $561.58, median $550.00, range $400–$870) imply upside, but at 23.8x forward with an $870 high target the dispersion mainly reflects differing views on AI monetization rather than independent confirmation.

**Valuation score: 5.8.** Reasonable relative to growth, unattractive relative to cash generation, with a forward earnings base whose comparability is compromised.

## Competitive Landscape

Azure at above $100B trails AWS and exceeds Google Cloud, and its growth rate accelerated in the quarter — the strongest evidence in the file that Microsoft's infrastructure position is improving rather than eroding. The Foundry strategy converts model competition into Azure consumption: Claude runs natively on Azure, billed through Azure, drawing down existing Microsoft commitments. Whether OpenAI, Anthropic or Microsoft's own models win, the compute is a candidate to run on Azure. That is a coherent response to losing exclusivity.

The offsetting trajectory items are real. April 2026 de-exclusivity removed the structural advantage of being the only place to run the leading models, and capped what Microsoft can collect from OpenAI. Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell to 66% as mix shifts toward lower-return infrastructure — the core bear framing, articulated by one manager as the market deciding Azure is "not a very exciting business," and more precisely stated as Microsoft converting high-margin software dollars into lower-return infrastructure dollars. Item 1A newly warns that expanding vertically-integrated capabilities including proprietary models and silicon "could increase our cost structure, reduce margins." There is an internal capacity conflict: chips allocated to Microsoft's own model research are chips not sold to Azure customers, and the risk factor concedes Microsoft may "modify capacity allocations, deployment priorities, pricing." Windows OEM underperformed the PC unit market. Xbox is in structural retreat against the 2023 Activision acquisition still named in the acquisition-goodwill risk factor. Open-weight model competition is named explicitly as a commoditization vector.

Prior reports marked competitive position down from 9.0 to 6.5. Azure acceleration and non-lab-driven sequential RPO growth argue that markdown slightly overshot on the infrastructure leg; the exclusivity loss, margin mix shift and consumer/gaming decline confirm it on the others. **Competitive position score: 6.8.**

## Risk Assessment

**Single-counterparty RPO concentration.** Since April 2026 OpenAI may buy compute on any cloud. The FY2026 risk factor language is a direct hedge: strategic partners "may adjust their purchasing decisions, deployment strategies, workloads, or anticipated use," and "expected consumption or anticipated demand may not materialize, may be delayed or reduced, or may decline over time." OpenAI raised $122B in March 2026 at an $852B valuation, so near-term solvency is not the concern; renegotiation and multi-cloud diversion are. The current concentration percentage is a verification gap.

**Capacity overbuild and impairment.** Item 1A now states that "overestimation of demand or misalignment of capacity investments may result in underutilization of infrastructure and may lead to impairment of assets on our balance sheet," and that AI cost structures may not decline while pricing may fall through "competition, commoditization, or other market forces." Against $115.9B of annual capex and $443.5B of committed lease obligations, that is the largest quantifiable tail. Management's stated mitigation is the ability to slow GPU and CPU investment if demand weakens.

**Financing exposure.** FY2027 obligations against FY2026 operating cash flow, combined with a risk factor referencing Microsoft's credit ratings and continued access to capital, point toward debt issuance. Microsoft's balance sheet absorbs this easily.

**Tax.** IRS Notices of Proposed Adjustment for 2004–2013 seek an additional $28.9B plus penalties and interest on intercompany transfer pricing, with 2014–2017 still under audit. Microsoft disagrees and believes allowances are adequate. Unresolved and material relative to a single year's FCF.

**Accounting comparability.** The 15-to-25-year building life extension and the finance-to-operating lease reclassification both flatter the two most-watched FY2027 metrics — EPS and "capex plus finance leases" (guided to ~$175B versus ~$190B previously). Lease classification is determined by contract terms under ASC 842, not preference, and a genuine shift toward shorter-term or colocation contracts would justify operating treatment and represent a real reduction in commitment per site; 25 years is within peer norms for buildings. I am not inferring motive. The verifiable points are that the ~$15B reduction is not a $15B spending reduction, and that FY2027 will not be comparable to FY2026 on a like-for-like basis.

**Other.** AI training/inference/output copyright claims are newly named in Item 1A; export-control fragmentation is live (Claude's Foundry general availability could not be deployed by European enterprises); power availability, permitting delays, and "increasingly coordinated opposition to infrastructure development" are materially expanded risk language; the Xbox impairment amount is undisclosed. Item 3 is a bare cross-reference to Note 14, which was not read, so specific litigation accruals are unexamined. On the positive side, the Musk v. Altman action naming Microsoft as co-defendant and seeking $150B was resolved for the defendants on May 18, 2026 on statute-of-limitations grounds, removing a material contingency, though the underlying conduct questions were not adjudicated and appellate status is unconfirmed.

**Options Market Signal.** The headline readings need decomposition. Near-term ATM IV of 59.6% with a 93rd-percentile chain reading and a backwardated term structure sits entirely on the July 31 expiry — one day to expiry, implied move ±0.5%, immediately after the earnings gap. That is same-day pinning mechanics rather than a signal about imminent stress. Beyond the front expiry, one-month IV is 29.1%, three-month 31.5% and six-month 29.8% against one-year historical volatility of 31.4% — options are priced at or slightly below realized vol, which is not the profile of a market pricing an unpriced event. The 9.0% headline put/call skew that the data source labels elevated put demand is driven by the 42.7% one-day figure; at one month it is 2.1% and at three and six months it reads −0.3% and −2.7% (the sign convention for negative values is not defined, so I draw no directional conclusion there). Put/call volume of 0.41 and open-interest ratio of 0.36 are call-heavy on a ratio basis. All ten unusual-activity lines are near-the-money July 31 or early-August contracts with volume/OI multiples of 14x–72x, consistent with same-day flow around the gap. Short interest of 1.2% of float and two days to cover means no crowded bearish position. Net read: no options-market challenge to the fundamental view, and no cheap-vol entry advantage either.

**Risk factors score: 5.3.** The sector-level AI-capex-ROI risk is partly priced — that is what the 19% year-to-date drawdown was. The items that are specific, quantified and less clearly priced are the RPO concentration, the $28.9B tax exposure, the impairment tail on a $443.5B commitment stack, and the FY2027 comparability break.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $600 (+29%)

Azure sustains 45%+ growth through FY2027 as the guide implies, and the capacity that $116B of capex bought converts into revenue at incremental margins that stabilize Microsoft Cloud gross margin near 66%. Copilot seats compound from 30 million toward 50 million-plus, extending the ARPU expansion that already produced 17% Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud growth on 6% seat growth, with E7 adding a second price tier. Non-model-developer RPO growth continues, progressively diluting the OpenAI concentration and reframing the $678B backlog as broad-based. FY2027 free cash flow turns positive-growing as guided, resolving the central bear objection, and the market re-rates a 16-18% revenue grower with 47% operating margins to 26x FY2028 EPS of $23.08 — $600. The 2026 drawdown was a multiple event during which management increased repurchases 28%; the fundamental trajectory never broke.

### Bear Case — $350 (−24.5%)

AI capex ROI disappoints across the hyperscaler cohort. Microsoft's own risk factor scenario plays out: demand overestimation leads to underutilized infrastructure and asset impairment against $443.5B of lease commitments and $169B of near-term take-or-pay purchase obligations. OpenAI, now free to buy compute anywhere and preparing an IPO, renegotiates or diverts a portion of a roughly $281B commitment, and RPO growth reverses. Free cash flow stays flat or declines a second year while $241.9B of FY2027 obligations forces debt issuance. Investors discount adjusted EPS for the private-AI marks inside it and discount FY2027 EPS for the depreciation-life change, so the effective multiple on clean earnings is higher than 23.8x. Multiple compresses to 17-18x on FY2027 EPS of $19.50 — $332-351, roughly the June 2026 low of $349.20.

### Balance of evidence

The strongest disconfirming evidence against my HOLD is Azure's acceleration combined with a 23.8x forward multiple and rising estimates — that combination has historically preceded further re-rating. The reason it does not convert to a BUY here is that the price already reflects most of the recovery (a 31% move off the June low of $349.20, including 8-9% on the print, to 16.5% below the high), the forward earnings base is compromised by two unquantified accounting changes and by non-cash investment marks inside adjusted EPS, and the cash yield is under 2% on FCF that declined. Compounding at 16-18% revenue with 47% operating margins deserves a premium multiple; it does not obviously deserve a higher one than it already carries while the capital-intensity question is unresolved.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 FY2027 results, late October 2026 — the first print with the new depreciation and lease conventions, the first test of the 45% Azure guide, and the first opportunity to see FY2027 capex and FCF quantified rather than paraphrased.

**Base case (12 months): $505**, or 22x FY2028 consensus EPS of $23.08, implying +9% from $463.62. The 22x multiple is a modest discount to the current 23.8x on FY2027, reflecting a haircut for the portion of FY2027-28 EPS growth attributable to the depreciation-life extension rather than operations. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios above.

**Bull case: $600 (+29%)** — 26x FY2028 EPS of $23.08 on sustained 45% Azure growth and confirmed FCF inflection.
**Bear case: $350 (−24.5%)** — 18x FY2027 EPS of $19.50 on AI-capex derating and RPO concentration stress.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q1 or Q2 FY2027 disclosure quantifying FY2027 free cash flow growth above FY2026's ~$67.0B on a like-for-like basis, with the depreciation and lease-classification effects separately sized.
2. A disclosed reduction in OpenAI's share of commercial RPO — from the reported ~45% toward 35% or below — accompanied by continued total RPO growth.
3. A price of $400 or below with Azure growth still at or above 40% constant currency, which would restore a mid-teens forward multiple and a ~2.3% FCF yield on unchanged operations.
4. Clustered open-market purchases by C-suite executives, which have been absent through a 19% drawdown.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Azure constant-currency growth decelerating below 38%, or Q1 FY2027 Azure falling short of the 45% guide by more than 3 points.
2. Commercial RPO declining sequentially, or disclosure of a renegotiation, deferral or reallocation of OpenAI commitments.
3. A datacenter or capacity-related asset impairment charge, or FY2027 free cash flow guidance revised to flat-to-down.
4. Microsoft Cloud gross margin falling below 63%, indicating the infrastructure mix shift is compressing unit economics faster than opex leverage can offset.

## Conclusion

**HOLD** at $463.62, overall score 6.3.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score is unchanged from July despite a strong quarter — the composition shifted rather than the level. Prior reports marked competitive position down from 9.0 to 6.5 and management quality from 8.0 to 5.5. The FY2026 10-K and Q4 print partially reverse the first: Azure accelerating to 43% at above $100B scale, with 45% guided and Q4 sequential RPO growth coming from non-model-developer clients, is evidence that the infrastructure franchise is strengthening, and I raise competitive position to 6.8. The filing confirms the second: a compensation plan built on revenue, operating income, Azure revenue and relative TSR with no identifiable capital-efficiency or free-cash-flow measure, in a year when revenue grew 18%, operating income grew 21%, and free cash flow fell 6%, is an alignment gap during the most capital-intensive period in the company's history. CEO total compensation rose from $48.5M in FY2023 to $96.5M in FY2025 while the proxy's own measure showed company TSR of 255 against peer-group TSR of 276. No executive bought stock on the open market during the drawdown. Financial health moves modestly lower to 6.8 on FCF decline, 34.9% capex intensity, and FY2027 obligations exceeding operating cash flow, offset by near-zero net leverage.

What would make this a BUY is a cleaner read on cash. Microsoft is executing well enough that at $400 or on a demonstrated FY2027 free-cash-flow inflection, the case would be straightforward. At $463.62 — 18% above the company's own March repurchase average, 1.95% FCF yield, roughly $0.35 of the last quarter's $0.50 beat from a non-cash private-equity mark, and two accounting changes that flatter FY2027 optics by an amount not yet sized — the appropriate action is to own it and wait for the October print rather than add.
