**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Peabody Energy is the largest U.S.-based pure-play coal producer, with a diversified portfolio spanning Australian seaborne thermal and metallurgical coal plus U.S. thermal mines, principally in the Powder River Basin. The investment case has deteriorated through the first half of 2026. After windfall years in 2022-2023, the company has rolled over into a sharp cyclical downturn: 2025 operating income swung to a loss of $80M from $445M in 2024, full-year net loss to common was $53M (-$0.43 diluted EPS), and Q1 2026 extended the trend with a $32.4M net loss and a 43% YoY decline in Adjusted EBITDA. The Seaborne Metallurgical segment posted negative EBITDA in Q1 as the Centurion longwall hit commissioning problems that have pushed full production rates to H2 2026.

The defining idiosyncratic overhang is the terminated Anglo American acquisition. Peabody declared a Material Adverse Change after the March 2025 Moranbah North ignition event, walked away in August 2025, and now faces ICC arbitration in London with unspecified damages and a disputed deposit. That collapse also removed the company's primary inorganic growth leg, leaving Centurion, currently behind schedule, as the main organic vector. Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration: current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 137.6% over 90 days, current-year consensus down 69.7% to $0.82, and the company missed three of the last four quarters, including a 350% miss in Q1 2026.

The relative bright spot is the balance sheet. With only ~$335M of debt against $492M of cash and $868M of total liquidity, Peabody is not a solvency story; the Altman Z-score of 2.6 sits in the grey zone, and the opportunistic $225M 0.50% convertible due 2031 priced in May extends maturities at a near-zero coupon. Adequate liquidity does not offset negative earnings, falling estimates, and the absence of an identifiable catalyst over the next two-to-three quarters. We rate BTU AVOID at $25-26. The prior trajectory of small score improvements does not survive the Q1 print and the wholesale estimate cuts that followed it.

## Company Overview

Peabody operates 17 active mining complexes and sold 122.0 million tons of coal in 2025 across four reportable segments:

- **Seaborne Thermal** (NSW, Australia; export-focused low-sulfur, high-Btu): $908.5M revenue, $222.2M Adjusted EBITDA in 2025.
- **Seaborne Metallurgical** (Queensland/NSW plus Shoal Creek, Alabama; coking and PCI coal): $1,036.6M revenue, $56.4M Adjusted EBITDA.
- **Powder River Basin** (Wyoming surface mines; thermal coal to U.S. utilities): $1,153.0M revenue, $175.8M Adjusted EBITDA. This is the volume workhorse at 84.5M tons (~69% of tonnage) but earns roughly $2/ton.
- **Other U.S. Thermal** (Illinois, Indiana, New Mexico, Colorado): $707.3M revenue, $71.4M Adjusted EBITDA.

The seaborne segments contributed ~53% of mining Adjusted EBITDA in 2025, U.S. thermal ~47%; metallurgical coal was ~27% of revenue. Peabody controls roughly 2.0 billion tons of proven and probable reserves. The competitive position rests on scale, portfolio diversification across thermal and met and across U.S. and seaborne markets, and long-lived low-cost surface assets in the PRB. Those advantages sit against structural decline in thermal coal demand and the cyclicality of seaborne pricing.

## Financial Analysis

The downturn is well-documented in the income statement:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4,982M | $4,947M | $4,237M | $3,862M |
| Operating income | $1,382M | $1,075M | $445M | -$80M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | ~$1,845M | $1,364M | $872M | $455M |
| Net income to common | — | — | $371M | -$53M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $2.70 | -$0.43 |

The 2025 loss was driven by lower seaborne realized prices (Seaborne Thermal revenue/ton -20%, Seaborne Met revenue/ton -17%), the non-recurrence of a 2024 Shoal Creek insurance recovery of $109.5M, and $78.9M of costs tied to the terminated Anglo acquisition, including roughly $46.7M of bridge-loan commitment and duration fees. Adjusted EBITDA fell 47.8% YoY. No impairment was taken in 2025, though management flags $60-64M of Other U.S. Thermal assets as sensitive to customer concentration.

Q1 2026 continued the slide. Revenue rose 4% YoY to $973.3M on volume and mix, but net loss to common was $32.4M (-$0.27 diluted) versus +$34.4M a year earlier. Seaborne Metallurgical posted negative segment EBITDA of -$7.0M as Centurion commissioning issues drove costs per ton up 20%. Operating cash flow fell to $30.0M from $119.9M.

On the balance sheet, the company is in better shape than the income statement. Cash was $492.5M at Q1 2026 (down from $575M at year-end 2025, $700M at year-end 2024, and $1.3B in 2022). Total debt is only ~$335M (the $320M 2028 convertible plus finance leases). D/E is 0.62, current ratio 1.85, and the Altman Z-score of 2.6 sits in the grey zone. Solvency is not the near-term issue; EBIT/assets of -1.4% is.

The structural obligations off the balance-sheet face are the real liability burden: ~$755M of asset retirement obligations, ~$997M-$1.0B of surety bonds, $844M of restricted cash and collateral supporting bonding, ~$1.0B of take-or-pay rail and port commitments (terms up to 19 years), and $110M+/year of legacy postretirement, black-lung, and reclamation cash outflows (~$1.7B+ cumulative). These do not threaten near-term liquidity, but they consume cash through cycle troughs.

Free cash flow was -$77.7M in 2025 (capex of $411M against operating cash flow of $334M). The company guides ~$340M of capex for 2026, pays a $0.075/quarter dividend (~$37M/year, ~1.2% yield), and has repurchased $530.8M of stock under a $1.0B authorization (23.8M shares, $469.6M remaining), though buybacks slowed sharply in 2025. Capital returns are gated by a surety-agreement minimum-liquidity test (greater of $400M or bonding shortfall) and a 1.5x net-leverage covenant.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the case and the most direct driver of the AVOID. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut hard across every near-term horizon:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Revision |
|--------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Current Quarter | -$0.29 | $0.77 | -137.6% |
| Next Quarter | $0.68 | $1.06 | -36.1% |
| Current Year | $0.82 | $2.70 | -69.7% |
| Next Year | $3.80 | $3.91 | -3.0% |

Estimate revisions of this magnitude are among the strongest forward indicators available, and here they point in the wrong direction. The earnings-momentum signal that would support a buy thesis — rising estimates and accelerating growth — is absent.

The organic growth story rests on Centurion, the Queensland underground longwall met mine that began longwall production in February 2026 but hit mechanical, electrical, and adverse roof/strata conditions in commissioning. Management now targets full longwall rates only in H2 2026. If Centurion reaches design rates on schedule, the next-year consensus of $3.80 becomes plausible, but the Q1 negative-EBITDA print and the history of timeline slippage warrant skepticism. The inorganic growth leg disappeared with the Anglo termination.

Optionality exists but is early-stage and unquantifiable today: rare-earth/critical-minerals recovery from PRB coal (a $6.25M Wyoming Energy Authority pilot award in February 2026) and surplus land/resource monetization ($39.5M of 2025 Corporate EBITDA, more than double 2024). Neither moves the 12-month thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless given negative TTM earnings (-25.4x reported), so the relevant question is whether forward earnings power justifies the current $25-26 price. On current-year consensus of $0.82, the stock trades at ~31x. On next-year consensus of $3.80 — which embeds a full Centurion ramp and a seaborne price recovery — the stock trades at ~6.8x, in line with the energy peer median P/E of 7.5x. That is the bull's anchor.

The problem is the reliability of the $3.80 number. The same analysts who set the $3.80 next-year figure have been revising near-term numbers down sharply. A forward multiple is only as good as the estimate behind it, and Peabody's estimates are deteriorating, not stabilizing. Applying the cycle-adjusted lens correctly cuts against the stock here: this is a downward move with negative revision velocity, not a trough recovery with rising estimates.

Peer benchmarks confirm there is no clear discount to exploit. The energy peer set shows wide dispersion (CVI -72.7x, KNTK 19.3x, PARR 6.2x, STNG 7.5x, TALO -3.4x), with a median P/E of 7.5x and median yield of 1.3%. Peabody's ~1.2% yield is roughly in line and its forward multiple on credible (current-year) estimates is above the median. The 1.2% dividend provides minimal downside support.

Analyst price targets (low $30, mean $34.3, high $40) sit above the current price, and sentiment is nominally "improving," but these targets lag a rapidly deteriorating estimate base. With the stock at ~31x current-year earnings and estimates falling, proximity to a $30 low target is not evidence of mispricing; it is evidence the targets have not fully caught up to the Q1 reset. There is no specific, identifiable reason the market is wrong on the downside here, which is what a high valuation score would require. The valuation score is low.

## Competitive Landscape

Peabody competes in two structurally distinct markets. In seaborne met coal, demand tracks global steel and pig-iron output — declining in China under anti-involution policies and steel export quotas, growing in India, with electric-arc-furnace steelmaking a long-term threat to coking-coal demand. In thermal, Peabody competes against natural gas, wind, and solar; U.S. coal generation share is ~16% and structurally declining, though 2025 saw a cyclical uptick on higher gas prices, and Q1 2026 seaborne thermal pricing was supported by Middle East/Strait of Hormuz disruptions elevating LNG prices.

Industry-wide retrenchment by banks and insurers from thermal coal raises Peabody's bonding and financing costs, a disadvantage versus non-coal energy peers, though shared with coal competitors Arch, Warrior Met, and Alpha Met. The company's diversification across thermal/met and U.S./seaborne is a relative differentiator within coal. The March 2026 NSW "Coal Industry 2026-50" policy, which bars new greenfield coal mines and ends government coal-exploration investment while allowing extensions, constrains long-term seaborne thermal growth where Peabody's NSW assets sit. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-deteriorating: a diversified leader operating in markets with secular and policy headwinds, having just lost its largest planned expansion.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by idiosyncratic, not-yet-resolved overhangs that justify a low score:

1. **Anglo arbitration (most material).** Anglo initiated ICC arbitration in London in September 2025 alleging wrongful termination, seeking unspecified damages plus the disputed portion of Peabody's deposit (Anglo returned $29M of $75M). The MAC argument is plausibly strong — the asset caught fire and closed — but arbitration outcomes are unpredictable and the damages could be material. This is the single biggest swing factor.

2. **Centurion execution.** The negative Q1 segment EBITDA shows the ramp is behind plan; a further slip past H2 2026 directly undermines the next-year earnings recovery the valuation depends on.

3. **Surety-agreement expiry Dec. 31, 2026.** The company's own risk language states that failure to maintain bonding could result in inability to continue as a going concern. The agreement expires at year-end 2026, a concrete dated item to monitor.

4. **Seaborne price volatility.** Met and thermal pricing drive the swing in profitability; markets remain volatile and tied to China steel policy, India demand, and global LNG.

5. **Dilution overhang.** The 2028 converts are in-the-money (~$232M above par, ~16.8M potential shares) and the new 2031 convert adds further potential dilution, though capped-call structures mitigate.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 96.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 60.4% (rich by 35.7 points, 82nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (96.1% near-term versus 59.3% at six months). ATM IV rose 13.5 points (82.6% to 96.1%) as the price fell 12.6% since early June, and the put/call OI ratio more than doubled (0.29 to 0.68). The near-dated skew is call-heavy (-35.5%), consistent with hedged or speculative upside positioning rather than directional conviction, but the elevated near-term vol and rising put OI signal a market braced for a near-term binary. Short interest is 15.5% of float with 3.5 days to cover — elevated bearish institutional positioning, but not at squeeze-risk levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Seaborne met and thermal prices have shown a Q1 2026 rebound (premium HCC +17% QoQ, Newcastle thermal supported by Middle East disruption). If Centurion reaches full longwall rates in H2 2026 and pricing holds, Seaborne Met returns to positive EBITDA and next-year consensus of $3.80 becomes achievable, putting the stock at ~6.8x, in line with the peer median. The balance sheet is genuinely strong, the 0.50% 2031 convert is opportunistic refinancing at a near-zero coupon, and a favorable Anglo arbitration outcome plus deposit recovery would remove a large overhang. REE/critical-minerals optionality offers a non-coal value lever aligned with U.S. policy. Bull target $40 (~9-10x next-year EPS on a clean ramp and arbitration win).

### Bear Case
Estimates are still falling, not stabilizing. Centurion slips again, seaborne prices roll back over on weak China steel demand, the Anglo arbitration goes against Peabody with material damages, and the surety-agreement renewal at year-end 2026 proves costly amid coal-financing retrenchment. FCF stays negative, cash continues to draw down from $492M, and the stock re-rates toward the low end of its range. Bear target ~$18 (near the post-Q1 lows, roughly -29% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID, so the relevant framework is re-entry criteria and timeline rather than a price target.

**Re-entry criteria** — any one of the following would prompt a reassessment toward HOLD or BUY:
- **Centurion confirmed at full longwall rates** with Seaborne Met returning to positive segment EBITDA in a reported quarter (watch the Q2 and Q3 2026 prints).
- **Estimate revision trend turns positive** — current-year and next-quarter consensus stabilizing or rising for two consecutive months.
- **Anglo arbitration resolved favorably** (dismissal, immaterial damages, or full deposit recovery), removing the largest idiosyncratic overhang.
- **Surety-agreement renewal completed** on acceptable terms well ahead of the Dec. 31, 2026 expiry.

**Timeline:** The headwinds are likely to persist through at least H2 2026. The two natural reassessment points are the Q2 2026 earnings release (Centurion ramp progress, segment EBITDA, updated estimates) and any procedural milestone in the ICC arbitration. The surety expiry sits at year-end 2026.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: There is no single dated catalyst with high conviction. The nearest candidates are the Q2 2026 earnings release (likely to show continued Seaborne Met weakness if Centurion is still ramping) and the Dec. 31, 2026 surety-agreement expiry. Neither is a clean, dated trigger with a known asymmetric outcome — the surety renewal is more likely to be completed routinely than to fail, and the Anglo arbitration has no near-term decision date.

**Timing window**: The earnings deterioration is already largely reflected in falling estimates and a stock that has fallen 12.6% in two weeks. A short here would be betting on continued estimate cuts rather than a discrete event, a slow, uncertain catalyst.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is already 15.5% of float with 3.5 days to cover. The crowd is meaningfully short, which limits remaining downside capacity and raises squeeze risk on any positive surprise (a Centurion ramp confirmation, a favorable arbitration headline, or a seaborne price spike on Middle East disruption).

**Borrow and options signal**: The 96.1% near-term ATM IV against 60.4% HV makes options expensive, and the call-heavy near-dated skew (-35.5%) shows the options market is not confirming a clean bearish setup. Elevated near-term IV raises carrying cost on any options-based short.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear target ~$18 (-29%) against meaningful upside risk from a coal-price spike or arbitration win that could move the stock +30-50% given the call-heavy positioning. With 15.5% SI already in and rich, call-skewed options, the risk/reward is below the 2:1 threshold required for an active short.

**Verdict**: **CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis is directionally correct on near-term fundamentals, but short interest at 15.5% of float, the absence of a clean dated catalyst, and call-heavy options positioning make the remaining downside small relative to squeeze risk. Avoid owning the stock; do not short it here.

## Conclusion

Peabody is a financially solvent company in a deepening earnings downturn with no near-term catalyst to reverse it. The balance sheet is adequate and the 2031 convertible was well-executed, but adequate liquidity does not justify ownership when TTM earnings are negative, every near-term consensus estimate is being cut sharply, the company has missed three of its last four quarters, the primary organic growth project is behind schedule, and a material arbitration overhangs the equity. The valuation looks cheap only on a next-year estimate that the same analysts are revising downward at the front end. We rate BTU **AVOID** at $25-26. The appropriate posture is to wait for confirmed Centurion ramp progress, stabilizing estimates, or resolution of the Anglo arbitration before re-engaging.
