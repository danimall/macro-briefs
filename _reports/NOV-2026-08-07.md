## Executive Summary

NOV reported a 86% EPS beat in Q2 2026 ($0.31 versus $0.17 consensus) and the stock has done nothing with it — shares closed at $19.06 the day after the release, versus $20.47 the day before, and sit at $19.83 today. The market's reaction is the correct one. Adjusted EBITDA of $283M includes roughly $40M of expected US tariff refunds recorded inside cost of revenue following the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling, of which only $17M had been received in cash by June 30. Excluding it, adjusted EBITDA was $243M against $252M in Q2 2025, on revenue 2% lower. A further $20M of gains on fixed-asset sales sat in pre-tax other items. The underlying quarter was flat, not inflecting.

Beneath that flat consolidated line there is real divergence worth owning an opinion on. Energy Equipment delivered its highest quarterly adjusted EBITDA margin since the segment was formed — 15.3% excluding the refund allocation, versus 13.1% a year earlier, a 218bp improvement driven by project mix and execution, with subsea flexible pipe running a 135% trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill and backlog 28% above prior year. Energy Products & Services moved the other way: 12.1% ex-refund versus 14.2%, a 212bp deterioration entirely concealed by the reported 14.8% figure. Consolidated SG&A rose to 15.8% of revenue in the first half from 13.9%, a 183bp deterioration on 2.4% lower revenue, with corporate costs up 51% year over year in FY2025 and a $(72)M corporate drag in a Q1 that produced only $47M of total operating profit.

The decisive near-term variable is orders, not margins. Capital-equipment backlog fell to $4.08B at June 30 from $4.30B a year earlier and $4.34B at year-end. Book-to-bill has printed 73% (Q4'25) and 74% (Q2'26); management guides 90-100% for full-year 2026, implying roughly 105-125% across the second half. That is a large step-up, and it is the single cleanest catalyst-or-disappointment in the name. At 7.7x our FY2026E EV/EBITDA (roughly 8.0x on an ex-refund basis) and 15.6x next-year consensus EPS of $1.27, the stock is priced approximately where the balance sheet and a stable 2026 justify. We model +6% to base case, +31% to bull, −27% to bear, producing a probability-weighted 12-month total return near 6% including the ~2.3% dividend. HOLD, with the rating explicitly contingent on the October Q3 order print.

## Company Overview

NOV is the largest independent equipment and technology supplier to the global energy industry, operating in 57 countries from 503 locations with 31,605 employees. "Independent" carries specific commercial meaning: the company has deliberately resisted vertical integration, selling technology to the integrated majors (SLB, Halliburton, Baker Hughes), their smaller competitors, national oil companies, drilling contractors and shipyards alike. Management frames this as driving end-market fragmentation — by arming everyone, NOV prevents any single service company from holding a proprietary technology advantage, which expands the addressable customer set and, per the 10-K, avoids customer concentration in most businesses.

Two segments. **Energy Products and Services** (FY25 revenue $3,977M, 45%) is the shorter-cycle, transactional business: drill bits, downhole tools, completion tools, artificial lift, tubular inspection and coating, solids control, digital solutions (Max platform, wired drill pipe), drill pipe, and composite pipe and structures. **Energy Equipment** (FY25 $4,934M, 55%) is longer-lead capital equipment — land rigs, offshore drilling packages, top drives, managed-pressure drilling, frac pumps and coiled tubing units, heavy-lift cranes and wind-installation vessel equipment, process systems and FPSO topsides, subsea flexible pipe — plus aftermarket, which was 37% of segment revenue in Q2.

The installed-base aftermarket annuity is the most durable part of the franchise, reinforced by post-Macondo regulatory preference for OEM maintenance of critical safety equipment. Adjacent-market optionality spans geothermal, offshore wind, carbon capture, and now precision manufacturing for advanced nuclear developers. Capital intensity is genuinely low for an industrial of this scale: FY25 capex of $375M on $8,744M of revenue, 4.3% of sales, with FY26 guided to $340-370M.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue declined 1.4% to $8,744M against a 6.6% drop in the average worldwide rig count (1,821 versus 1,950) — modest relative outperformance management attributes to offshore capital-equipment demand. Below the revenue line the deterioration was severe: GAAP operating income fell 44% to $494M (5.6% margin from 9.9%) and net income fell 77% to $145M. Three separable causes.

**Real margin compression.** Gross margin fell 246bp, from 22.7% to 20.2%. Energy Products & Services adjusted operating margin fell from 11.7% to 8.4%, with tariffs cited in MD&A.

**Cost creep.** SG&A rose $69M (+6.1%) on revenue that fell $126M — 13.8% of sales versus 12.8%. Corporate costs rose 51%; the corporate/eliminations drag widened from $(207)M to $(276)M. This continued into 2026 at an accelerated pace.

**Tax structure, which is the largest single driver.** FY2025 effective tax rate was 59.7% versus 23.6% in FY2024, with the valuation allowance growing from $266M to $352M on foreign tax credit carryforwards and losses in non-benefit jurisdictions. Q4 2025 produced a GAAP net loss of $(78)M on a $147M quarterly tax provision despite $267M of adjusted EBITDA. Management guides 2026 to 34-36%; 1H26 actual was 29.2%. A ~35% structural rate on an asset-light industrial carrying a $639M accumulated deficit means real cash tax paid in high-rate jurisdictions with limited US shelter. Cash taxes in 1H26 were $79M. There is a reverse-direction possibility: an earnings-mix shift back toward usable-attribute jurisdictions could release valuation allowance and produce a one-time GAAP benefit, which is precisely what happened in FY2023 when a $373M tax benefit lifted net income to $993M on $651M of operating income.

**Cash flow deteriorated in the first half, and the seasonality reconciliation matters.** 1H26 operating cash flow was −$9M versus +$326M in 1H25, a $335M swing driven by working capital: receivables −$111M, inventories −$170M, contract assets −$70M, accrued liabilities −$60M. Derived 1H26 free cash flow was −$155M ($9M OCF outflow plus $146M capex). Cash fell from $1,552M to $1,164M while NOV returned $227M ($130M buybacks, $97M dividends) — first-half shareholder returns were funded from the balance sheet.

The seasonal offset is real but demanding. Derived 2H25 free cash flow was +$717M (2H25 OCF of $925M less $208M capex). Management guides FY26 conversion of 40-50% of adjusted EBITDA; against our FY26E adjusted EBITDA of roughly $997M (~$957M ex-refund) that implies $400-500M full-year FCF, requiring 2H26 FCF of approximately +$555M to +$655M — below what NOV actually delivered in 2H25. Achievable on precedent, but only if the deliberately elevated Middle East inventory buffers unwind and milestone billings land. If they do not, the discretionary supplemental dividend is the designed release valve, and it has already been cut once.

**The balance sheet is the strongest part of the file.** Total debt $1,706M ($1,092M of 3.95% notes due 2042, $497M of 3.60% notes due 2029, $117M other) against $1,164M cash gives net debt of $542M, or 0.53x FY25 adjusted EBITDA. The $1.5B revolver is undrawn and extended to September 2030. The sole referenced covenant, debt-to-capitalization ≤60%, sits at 23.9%. Current ratio 2.42x. Senior notes carry at $1,589M against a $1,345M fair value, a ~15% discount reflecting below-market coupons rather than credit stress. Two genuine qualifications: $747M of the cash sits at foreign subsidiaries, constraining repatriation for buybacks, and $909M of letters of credit are outstanding as performance obligations on international equipment contracts — not debt, but a contingent exposure equal to roughly 15% of company equity and a structural reason NOV must preserve investment-grade standing.

The Altman Z-score of 2.82 in the "grey zone" is a poor solvency guide here. It is depressed by EBIT/total assets of 4.4% (a cyclical earnings problem, genuine) and a negative retained-earnings term reflecting write-offs from the 2015-2020 cycle. A 23.9% debt-to-cap ratio and an undrawn revolver through 2030 are the decision-relevant facts. NOV's problem is earnings power, not solvency.

## Growth Analysis

The most important structural finding in this file is that the demand transmission from oil price to NOV revenue has broken down. In Q2 2026 WTI averaged $95.75, up 48.2% year over year, while the average worldwide rig count was 1,760 — down 1.0% year over year, down 3.9% sequentially, and roughly 10% below the 2024 average of 1,950. NOV revenue declined 2%. The company's own risk factors name part of the mechanism: successful new technology can reduce equipment demand because operators achieve equivalent output from fewer rigs. The rest is budget behavior — operators treated the war-driven spike as temporary and did not raise capital budgets, a read supported by WTI already back to $89.31 by July 24.

There is a counter-signal. As of July 24, the North America rig count was 791, roughly 12% above the Q2 average of 704, consistent with the +5% to +7% Q3 revenue guide for Energy Products & Services. Whether that response persists depends on whether the geopolitical premium holds long enough to enter budget cycles.

**Order intake is the binding constraint.** Capital-equipment backlog of $4.08B is down 5% year over year and 6% from year-end. Q2 orders were $474M, up 13% year over year, but only 74% of revenue burned. Backlog composition — 57% offshore, 94% international — means the equipment franchise is levered to NOC and offshore sanctioning rather than US shale, and only about 29% converts to revenue in the remainder of 2026. Remaining performance obligations of $4,443M measure a different scope (contracts with original duration over one year, excluding royalty, right-to-invoice and sub-one-year work) and should not be added to backlog.

**Where growth is actually occurring.** Subsea flexible pipe posted record segment EBITDA on a 135% TTM book-to-bill with backlog up 28%, and is nearing capacity constraints — sizable new orders are largely scheduled for 2028 delivery, with added capacity anticipated in early 2029 following the $200M, three-year Açu, Brazil expansion announced in March. This is simultaneously the clearest evidence of pricing power in the portfolio and a physical ceiling: incremental demand converts to price and margin, not volume, until 2029. Drill pipe recorded its strongest first-half bookings in more than a decade with backlog roughly doubled. Digital Services posted a fourth consecutive quarter of year-over-year growth, with wired drill pipe services revenue nearly doubling. US drill bit revenue set a quarterly record and US drilling-motor rental revenue was the strongest in over six years.

**Where it is not.** The $40M goodwill impairment against the Renewables reporting unit from the October 2025 quantitative test is hard evidence that the offshore-wind equipment thesis has deteriorated versus underwriting. Equity income from unconsolidated affiliates swung from +$36M in FY24 to −$16M in FY25, with the largest investee's sales down ~30% on OCTG pricing pressure and down 29% again in 1H26 — third-party corroboration of pipe-market weakness that does not appear in NOV's own revenue line.

**Estimate revisions are genuinely mixed and should not be read as a single signal.** Current-year consensus rose to $0.87 from $0.77 ninety days ago (+12.8%) and from $0.79 thirty days ago (+10.1%) — the Q2 beat flowing through. But next-quarter estimates fell 9.5% over thirty days to $0.29, and next-year consensus is flat-to-down at $1.27 (−1.0% over ninety days). The revision pattern says the market marked up 2026 for a one-time refund and left 2027 alone. That is not the inflection profile that would warrant treating rising estimates as a buy signal — the improvement is concentrated in a non-recurring item and does not extend into the out-year where the order-driven thesis would show up.

Earnings visibility is poor: one beat and three misses across the last four quarters, including a −107.9% surprise in Q4 2025 and −68.1% in Q1 2026. That record argues for discounting rather than extrapolating consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing GAAP EPS is unusable. Second-half 2025 diluted EPS was −$0.09 (FY25 $0.39 less 1H25 $0.48), putting LTM EPS near $0.27 and the trailing P/E near 77x. Enterprise and cash-flow metrics are the honest basis.

At the $19.83 price basis and 357.3M shares, equity value is $7,085M. Adding $1,706M of debt and deducting $1,164M of cash gives an enterprise value of $7,627M.

Our FY26E EBITDA embeds a Q4 assumption of $270-290M anchored to Q4 2025's $267M actual and the 10-K's statement that Q4 is seasonally strongest. Management has issued no full-year EBITDA guidance; every multiple above inherits that assumption.

**Peer context.** The Oil & Gas Equipment & Services median P/E of 19.6x is distorted by Kodiak Gas at 88.7x and Cactus at 55.0x. The more comparable names are Weatherford at 15.8x and Oceaneering at 11.8x, with Archrock at 19.6x. NOV at 15.6x next-year sits in line with Weatherford and above Oceaneering; at 22.8x current-year it is above both. There is no discount to correct here. A structurally 34-36% tax rate also means EV/EBITDA flatters NOV relative to lower-taxed peers, so the equity-level comparison is the fairer one.

**What would make this cheap.** A 7.7x EV/EBITDA multiple is at the low end of mid-cycle oilfield-equipment ranges, and a 1.14x book multiple with $542M of net debt provides asset support. The remaining $326.6M buyback authorization equals roughly 4.6% of shares outstanding at current prices, expiring around April 2027, and the company has reduced share count 6.5% in eighteen months. Short interest at 13.3% of float with 5.8 days to cover means an order inflection would meet forced buying.

**What keeps it from being cheap.** FY25's headline 12.3% FCF yield on $876M of actual free cash flow was substantially a working-capital release (inventories fell from $1,932M to $1,799M, cash taxes were low, and OCF exceeded adjusted EBITDA). The FY26 guide of 40-50% conversion is a sharp step down and yields ~6.3%, which is unremarkable for a business with declining backlog. There is no identified mispricing catalyst independent of the order inflection management is guiding to. Analyst consensus sits at a $22.03 mean and $22.00 median with a $17-26 range, and 12 of 21 ratings are hold or worse — this is not a stock where the sell side lags an accelerating fundamental picture.

We score valuation 4.3. The multiple is defensible but the asymmetry is unfavorable at the current price, and cheapness alone without a demonstrated catalyst has repeatedly been a poor entry signal in this sector.

## Competitive Landscape

NOV's structural position is genuinely differentiated. As the largest independent equipment provider it benefits when the industry fragments and when NOCs and local-content startups require equipment they cannot manufacture. Local-content mandates function as a tailwind rather than a barrier. The OEM installed base funnels aftermarket work reinforced by regulatory preference for OEM maintenance, and Energy Equipment aftermarket was 37% of segment revenue in Q2.

Three specific read-throughs from the filings complicate the picture.

**The IP moat has a defined expiry and is losing in court.** The 10-K states patent expirations range from 2025 to 2044 and acknowledges that competitive products with different designs have been successfully developed and marketed by others. More concretely, on September 29 and October 7, 2025 the district court issued rulings in the Halliburton, Ulterra and Varel drill-bit royalty cases whose effect is that NOV cannot collect royalties after the date each licensee stopped paying. NOV has appealed to the Federal Circuit (Nos. 26-1256, 26-1266) and carries $140M of net royalty receivables in other assets against a gross claim near $258M.

**Renewables is impaired rather than merely soft.** NOV describes itself as the leading equipment and technology provider for purpose-built offshore-wind installation vessels — a leading share of a shrinking pool, per the October 2025 goodwill test.

**The pipe value chain is under real pricing pressure**, evidenced independently through the equity-method investee's ~30% sales decline.

Against those, the Energy Equipment margin record and the subsea flexible pipe position — capacity-constrained with 2028 delivery slots — are evidence of improving competitive standing in the parts of the portfolio that matter most for 2027-2029 earnings. Net trajectory: mixed, with genuine improvement concentrated in long-cycle equipment and genuine erosion in IP monetization, renewables and OCTG-adjacent lines. Score 5.3, unchanged.

A commercial nuance deserves flagging without overclaiming: NOV is simultaneously a supplier to and litigant against Halliburton, Baker Hughes and Schlumberger Technology Corp. Large oilfield companies routinely litigate with counterparties they also trade with, the dispute concerns legacy Grant Prideco licensing rather than current products, and there is no filing evidence of order loss attributable to the litigation. It is a latent channel-conflict risk, not a demonstrated one.

## Risk Assessment

**Middle East concentration, quantified by management.** The region is roughly 15% of total revenue. Q3 guidance assumes conditions consistent with Q2 and regional activity improving 10-15% sequentially, risked to the low end; failure would cost approximately $20-25M of quarterly EBITDA, or 8-10% of a quarterly base. Q1 2026 already demonstrated the mechanism — logistics delays pushed shipments and produced an 8.6% adjusted EBITDA margin quarter. Management describes logistics as constrained and costly, and offshore regional activity remains below pre-conflict levels.

**Hormuz status is unresolved in the primary record.** The Q2 10-Q, filed July 29, refers to "the closure of the Strait of Hormuz" having materially tightened oil and gas fundamentals. External reporting indicates a reopening around mid-June into a fragile state with tanker traffic hitting a five-week low in mid-July. No NOV filing establishes current condition. Any thesis depending on sustained closure or a durable oil premium is unsupported by primary sources. The proposed Hormuz-bypass midstream projects (Iraq-Syria Kirkuk pipeline, Fujairah terminal expansion) are pre-FID with no NOV orders disclosed — optionality, not evidence.

**Backlog erosion against a demanding second-half target** is the highest-probability source of a negative repricing. A sub-80% Q3 book-to-bill makes the 90-100% full-year guide unreachable and puts 2027 Energy Equipment revenue at risk. Capital-equipment orders are lumpy enough that a single FPSO topsides or subsea award can move a quarter by 20 points in either direction.

**Tariffs are a persistent cost, not a resolved one.** Q2 tariff expense was roughly $30M versus $10M a year earlier — approximately $80M of annualized incremental cost and $120M annualized in total — because tariffs were reintroduced under alternative authorities after the IEEPA ruling. The 10-K specifically flags Section 232 steel tariffs against fixed-price, multi-year NOC contracts where input inflation cannot be passed through.

**Balance-sheet contingencies.** Goodwill of $1,584M plus $438M of intangibles against $6,205M of equity, with the Renewables unit already written down. Tax disputes aggregate roughly $130M of exposure (Denmark transfer pricing $51M paid and under appeal, IRS 2017 restructuring $48M if lost, Canada CRA $31M assessed) plus $56M of unrecognized tax benefits. The Russia business was deconsolidated in Q1 2025 with a sale agreement signed in 2022 still awaiting government approvals. Disclosed FX exposure maps a 10% move to roughly $47M of net income.

**Governance items.** Bayardo holds combined Chairman and CEO titles following an internal promotion from CFO/COO; whether a lead independent director is designated was not verified. NOV has underperformed its self-selected compensation peer group in each of the five disclosed years — indexed TSR 123.36 versus 181.73 on a 2020 base of 100, a ~32% relative shortfall — while FY2025 PEO compensation actually paid rose to $15.33M in a year net income fell 77%. The mitigating fact is that FY2024 CAP was negative $0.67M, so the mark-to-market mechanism has real downside; the 2025 increase largely marks up previously written-down awards. Separately, adjusted EBITDA is the company-selected incentive metric and the $40M tariff refund flowed into it without adjustment. The refund reverses costs previously expensed through the same metric, so symmetric accounting is at least as plausible an explanation as metric management; we draw no inference about motive. The practical point is that the reported metric requires adjustment before comparison.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 38.7% against 37.0% one-year realized is fair — the market is pricing no dislocation, and IV has compressed from 44.9% on July 19 as the earnings event resolved. Term structure is flat (38.7% near versus 37.0% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a near catalyst, which is somewhat at odds with our view that the October Q3 order print is the binary. The mixed positioning signal deserves note: put/call skew of 6.5% indicates elevated put demand at the near tenor, but put/call open-interest of 0.08 and volume ratio of 0.10 are heavily call-tilted and the OI ratio has fallen from 0.18 since mid-July. Read together with 13.3% short interest and 5.8 days to cover, the structure is consistent with directional short exposure expressed in stock rather than options, and modest call accumulation for the order-inflection scenario. Neither confirms nor contradicts our HOLD; it argues that the options market sees a range-bound stock into the next print, and that a positive order surprise would meet thin put protection and meaningful short-covering demand.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $26 (+31%)

The second-half order inflection lands. Energy Equipment book-to-bill exceeds 100% in Q3 and Q4, backlog turns up from $4.08B, and management's "broadening investment cycle across offshore production, offshore drilling, international unconventional development and higher-value digital technologies" is validated in bookings rather than commentary. Subsea flexible pipe continues at 135% book-to-bill with the Açu expansion pre-selling 2029 capacity at premium pricing. Middle East activity recovers 10-15% sequentially, recovering the $20-25M quarterly EBITDA at risk. Inventory buffers unwind, delivering 2H26 FCF near $600M and validating the 40-50% conversion guide. SG&A normalizes as the Q1 stock-compensation charge and restructuring roll off, recovering 100bp+ of the 183bp first-half deterioration. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $1.15B; at 8.5x EV/EBITDA that implies $9,775M EV less $542M net debt, or $25.9/share. The remaining 4.6% buyback authorization and 13.3% short interest amplify the move. Optional upside: a Federal Circuit reversal recovers some of the $140M royalty receivable ($0.39/share pre-tax), or a valuation-allowance release produces a one-time GAAP tax benefit.

### Bear Case — $14.50 (−27%)

Q3 book-to-bill prints below 80% for a third consecutive quarter, the 90-100% full-year guide is abandoned, and 2027 Energy Equipment revenue guidance moves down. Middle East logistics stay constrained, inventory buffers remain on the balance sheet, and FCF conversion misses the 40-50% band — forcing a further cut to the supplemental dividend, which already fell from $0.21 to $0.09. The short-cycle segment's 212bp underlying margin deterioration persists as NAM rig-count improvement fades with the oil premium (WTI $89.31 in late July versus $95.75 Q2 average). SG&A creep continues, holding consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin near 11%. The Federal Circuit affirms the adverse royalty rulings and NOV writes down the $140M receivable. Renewables takes a second impairment. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA settles near $950M; at 6.0x that implies $5,700M EV less net debt, or $14.4/share — near the level the stock traded in October 2025.

### Base Case — $21 (+6%)

2026 finishes roughly where it started: adjusted EBITDA near $1.0B, FY27 near $1,075M as Energy Equipment margin gains offset flat-to-down volumes, book-to-bill lands in the mid-80s to low-90s, and FCF conversion comes in at the low end of the 40-50% guide. At 7.5x FY27E EBITDA of $1,075M that gives $8,063M EV less $542M net debt, or $21.05/share — corroborated by 16.5x next-year consensus EPS of $1.27 ($20.96). Buybacks retire 3-4% of shares, adding modestly to per-share value.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: approximately +4% price return plus ~2.3% dividend, or roughly 6% total over twelve months. That is below the bar for ACCUMULATE, particularly in a sector where our selection record has been persistently negative relative to the market and where the resolving catalyst is genuinely two-sided.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders should maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $19.83.

**Reassessment date: the Q3 2026 report, expected late October 2026.** That print carries the order number, the first clean read on 2H cash conversion, and the first SG&A comparison uncontaminated by the Q1 stock-compensation charge.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 Energy Equipment book-to-bill above 100% with capital-equipment backlog turning up from $4.08B. This is the primary trigger and would support base-case EBITDA above $1.1B for FY27.
2. Q3 free cash flow above +$250M with inventories declining sequentially from the June 30 level, demonstrating that the Middle East buffers unwind and the 40-50% conversion guide is achievable.
3. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin above 13% with no tariff-refund or asset-sale contribution, plus SG&A back below 14.5% of revenue.
4. A price below $16.50 on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the stock near 6.5x FY27E EV/EBITDA and roughly 0.95x book — a level at which the balance-sheet floor does the work.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 book-to-bill below 80%, or abandonment of the 90-100% full-year guide. This invalidates the 2027 revenue basis of the base case.
2. Reduction or elimination of the 2027 supplemental dividend, or FY26 FCF conversion guided below 40% — either signals the working-capital release did not materialize.
3. Middle East revenue disruption exceeding management's $20-25M quarterly EBITDA sensitivity, or a renewed Hormuz closure that halts regional shipments.
4. A further goodwill impairment beyond Renewables, or an adverse Federal Circuit ruling combined with a write-down of the $140M royalty receivable.

**What would falsify our HOLD in the bullish direction most quickly:** a single large subsea flexible pipe or offshore production award in Q3 sufficient to push segment book-to-bill above 110%. Order lumpiness means this can happen without any macro change, and 13.3% short interest means the price response would be disproportionate.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory here has been 4.6 → 4.3 → 4.3 → 4.7 → 4.7 → 4.7, with the recommendation moving from AVOID to HOLD. The Q2 print does not change the classification, and we resist the temptation to read the 86% EPS beat as confirmation of an inflection. Strip the $40M tariff refund and $20M of asset-sale gains and the quarter delivered $243M of adjusted EBITDA against $252M a year earlier on 2% less revenue. What did improve is worth respecting: Energy Equipment's 218bp underlying margin gain and the subsea flexible pipe franchise running a 135% book-to-bill into capacity constraints are the parts of NOV that will define 2028-2029 earnings power, and both are genuinely strengthening.

We nudge the overall score to 4.9 from 4.7, reflecting confirmed balance-sheet resilience (0.53x net leverage, 23.9% debt-to-cap, undrawn revolver to 2030) and a clearer view of ex-refund earnings power, offset by the 183bp first-half SG&A deterioration, negative first-half free cash flow, and a capital-equipment backlog down 5% year over year against a full-year guide requiring 105-125% book-to-bill in the second half. Valuation stays low at 4.3: 7.7x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 15.6x next-year consensus are defensible but offer no discount to the comparable peers (Weatherford 15.8x, Oceaneering 11.8x), and the ~6.3% FCF yield at the guidance midpoint compares to 12.3% on FY25 actual free cash flow that was substantially a working-capital release. Management quality scores 4.5 on the absence of open-market insider buying at 1.14x book, five consecutive years of TSR underperformance versus a self-selected peer group, and cost creep that widened the corporate drag 51% year over year — partly offset by disciplined FY25 buybacks at an average $13.82 and a 6.5% share-count reduction in eighteen months.

The stock is approximately fair. The October order print resolves the question in one direction or the other, and the asymmetry around that print — +31% bull, −27% bear, roughly +6% base — does not justify committing new capital ahead of it. HOLD, classified as uncertain rather than as a growth or income thesis, because the evidence on the decisive variable is genuinely two-sided and NOV has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters.
