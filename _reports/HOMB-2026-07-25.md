**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Home BancShares is the holding company for Centennial Bank, a ~$23 billion-asset Sun Belt commercial bank that ranks among the most profitable and efficient regional banks in the country. FY2025 delivered a 2.10% ROA and a 40.88% efficiency ratio against a peer norm of 55-65%, with net income up 18.2% to $475.4 million and EPS of $2.41. The bank's net interest margin expanded 24 basis points to 4.51% during a rate-cutting cycle because its deposit-funded, liability-sensitive balance sheet repriced funding costs down faster than asset yields, aided by proactive paydown of the $700 million BTFP advance and $160 million of subordinated notes. Capital is strong at 16.30% CET1 and 19.06% total risk-based, and credit is clean: net charge-offs collapsed to 0.02% of loans in 2025 from 0.41% in 2024, with allowance covering non-performing loans 350%.

The investment question is price rather than business quality, which is high and improving. The stock has re-rated roughly 21% off its mid-May 2026 low near $25.68 to $30.98, driven by the record Q2 2026 print (adjusted EPS $0.64, record revenue $295.1 million) and a July dividend increase. At ~12.3x forward EPS and roughly 2x tangible book ($15.32/share), the valuation now sits below the peer-median P/E of 14.7 on an absolute basis while embedding a well-deserved quality premium on a price-to-book basis. Consensus targets of $30 low / $32 median / $36 high imply only modest upside to the midpoint from here. Estimate revisions are positive but shallow (current-year EPS +1.9% over 90 days), which places HOMB in the category of a steady quality compounder rather than an earnings-inflection story.

We rate HOMB a HOLD (growth-hold). The franchise is worth owning for its efficiency moat, tangible-book compounding, and disciplined M&A optionality into Tennessee, and CEO John Allison's $2.7 million open-market purchase at ~$27 in April 2026 is a strong conviction signal from the most-informed insider. The recent run has compressed the risk/reward: after appreciating back toward its 52-week high, the stock offers a fair-not-cheap entry, with commercial real estate concentration (53.2% of loans in CRE plus construction) and large-borrower dependency ($8.1 billion, or 51.6% of loans, to relationships above the $40 million lending limit) as the principal overhangs. We would add on a pullback toward the mid-$27s where the CEO was buying.

## Company Overview

Home BancShares operates through Centennial Bank, headquartered in Conway, Arkansas, running 218 branches across five states as of year-end 2025: Arkansas (75), Florida (78), Texas (59), Alabama (5), and one branch in New York City, with loan-production offices in California, Virginia, and Maryland. The business is standard spread banking: $892 million of net interest income in 2025 earned on a ~$15.7 billion loan book and ~$4.1 billion securities portfolio, funded by ~$17.5 billion in deposits and modest FHLB borrowings, plus ~$198 million of fee income from deposit charges, interchange, trust, and mortgage banking.

Three attributes distinguish HOMB from a plain-vanilla community bank. First, it is a serial acquirer (23 banks acquired since 1999, 18 since 2010), making M&A an explicit, core strategic lever rather than an occasional event. Second, it operates specialty lending niches: Centennial Commercial Finance Group, a ~$2.0 billion national commercial finance franchise, and Shore Premier Finance, a ~$1.22 billion book financing high-end recreational marine vessels. Third, its operating efficiency and top-decile profitability (2.10% ROA versus the ~1.0-1.2% regional norm) give it a cost-of-capital and acquisition-currency advantage in a consolidating industry.

The company is founder-led. Chairman and CEO John W. Allison is the driving force behind the franchise and remains a significant shareholder (~5.8 million shares). This concentration of authority (loans above $40 million require sign-off from Allison and the Vice Chairman) is both a discipline and a key-man dependency.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a strong year on nearly every operating metric. Net income of $475.4 million rose 18.2% from $402.2 million; diluted EPS of $2.41 rose from $2.01. ROE of 11.6% (ROTCE ~17.9%) places HOMB well above peer profitability. HOMB converts a substantially larger share of revenue into pre-provision profit than most regional banks, which run efficiency ratios of 55-65%.

The margin story is the most differentiated feature of the year. The cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 40 basis points to 2.68%, outpacing the modest decline in asset yields. Management engineered part of this by paying off the BTFP advance in late 2024, redeeming $140 million of 5.50% subordinated notes in July 2025, and repurchasing $20 million of 2032 sub notes at a gain. This is a liability-sensitive, funding-advantaged book positioned favorably for continued rate cuts, the reverse of the pressure many asset-sensitive peers face.

Balance sheet growth was measured and organic: loans grew 6.2% to $15.69 billion (all organic in 2025 — $727.5 million legacy plus $194.2 million CFG), and deposits grew 1.9% to $17.48 billion, with ~23% noninterest-bearing. Capital is dramatically above regulatory minimums: leverage 14.09%, tangible equity/tangible assets 13.36%. Tangible book value per share rose to $14.60 from $12.68, and reached $15.32 by Q2 2026.

Credit quality is clean and improving. Non-performing loans fell to 0.54% of loans from 0.67%. The 2024 charge-off elevation was a deliberate Q4 2024 "asset quality cleanup," concentrated in $50.5 million of Texas losses, rather than ongoing deterioration. The allowance stands at 1.90% of loans. Provision expense dropped to $20.9 million from $48.1 million (the prior year included a $33.4 million hurricane reserve for Helene/Milton).

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and disciplined: $158.9 million in dividends (33% payout) and $81.4 million of buybacks in 2025 (2.89 million shares at ~$28.13), with 17.1 million shares still authorized. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.23 in July 2026.

One methodological note: standard industrial-company screening tools misfire badly on banks. An Altman Z-Score of 0.41 ("distress zone") and a reported "D/E of 4.33" are artifacts of applying manufacturing models to a bank whose deposits are its raw material, not distressed leverage. HOMB's real financial condition — 16.3% CET1, 2.1% ROA, $475 million net income — is the opposite of distress.

Given resilient cash generation, fortress capital, and clean credit, we score Financial Health 7.5 — the single most reliable winner/loser discriminator in our framework, and here it clearly favors the name. The score is not higher because the AFS unrealized loss position ($223 million gross, plus HTM fair value ~$100 million below carrying) and the CRE concentration are genuine embedded exposures, not zero.

## Growth Analysis

HOMB's growth is a blend of steady organic loan generation (mid-single-digit) and periodic M&A. The pending/completed Mountain Commerce Bancorp acquisition — signed December 8, 2025, completed Q2 2026 — marks the company's entry into Tennessee, now management's stated primary future M&A target market alongside Texas, Arkansas, Florida, and Alabama. The deal is small (Knoxville-based) but strategically meaningful as a beachhead, carrying a $12.7 million Q2 merger expense and a November 2026 systems conversion expected to generate ~$5.5 million in annual cost saves.

Q2 2026 was a record quarter: net income $119.3 million ($128.1 million adjusted, +8.4% linked-quarter), record total net revenue $295.1 million (+10.6% QoQ), and adjusted EPS $0.64 beating the $0.62 consensus. Adjusted efficiency improved to 40.46% and adjusted ROA held at 2.09%. Estimate revisions are positive but modest — current-year EPS revised +1.9% over 90 days to $2.51, next-year +0.3% to $2.60. This is durable, not accelerating, momentum. It does not clear the +30%-in-90-days threshold that would flag a strong standalone buy signal; HOMB is a quality compounder growing EPS in the high-single-digit range, not an earnings-recovery story.

The earnings surprise record is mixed-to-modest: over the trailing four quarters, one beat (Q2 2026 +2.8%), two in-line, and one miss (-1.8% in Q4 2025). Management tends to deliver close to consensus rather than routinely blow through it, which caps how much extra confidence to place in forward estimates. We score Growth Prospects 6.0 — directionally positive, supported by NIM tailwind from falling rates, the Tennessee M&A optionality, and clean organic loan growth, but not a top-tier growth engine. We weight this alongside Financial Health rather than as a standalone driver, given that recent-window growth signals in our framework separate winners only thinly.

## Valuation Assessment

At $30.98, HOMB trades at ~12.3x the $2.51 current-year consensus EPS and ~11.9x the $2.60 next-year estimate, versus a Regional Banks peer-median P/E of 14.7. On price-to-earnings alone the stock screens slightly cheap. On price-to-tangible-book (~2.0x at $15.32 TBV), it carries a clear premium to the sub-1.5x that lower-ROA peers command — a premium justified by its 2.1% ROA and ~18% ROTCE, but a premium nonetheless.

The peer table underscores the point. Against ABCB (13.7x), UBSI (12.6x), HWC (14.7x), and AUB (16.8x), HOMB's forward multiple is at the low end of the group despite delivering the best profitability metrics in the set. That gap is the bull's argument: a bank earning 2.1% ROA at 12.3x forward earnings is arguably underpriced relative to peers earning ~1.0-1.2% ROA at similar or higher multiples. The counterweight is that HOMB carries above-average CRE and office/hotel concentration, which the market discounts, and that the current dividend yield of 2.76% sits slightly below the peer-median 2.8%, offering no absolute-yield support edge.

Cheapness alone is not a reason to be constructive — in the current regime, a low multiple frequently marks a value trap rather than a mispricing, and we require an independent catalyst before rewarding it. Here the identifiable catalysts are modest: the November 2026 MCBI conversion, continued NIM benefit from Fed cuts, and buyback support. These are incremental, not transformational. Analyst consensus frames only ~3% upside to the $32 median and ~16% to the $36 high case, and because HOMB is a steady grower rather than an inflection stock, those targets are more likely to act as a fair-value band than as a stale floor that analysts will chase upward.

We score Valuation 5.5. The forward multiple is reasonable relative to quality, but the ~21% run off the May low has closed most of the gap between price and intrinsic value, leaving the risk/reward roughly balanced at $31. A more attractive entry sits in the mid-$27s, where the CEO was buying and where the price-to-book premium compresses meaningfully.

## Competitive Landscape

HOMB's competitive advantage is structural cost efficiency. A ~41% efficiency ratio means it earns more pre-provision profit per dollar of revenue than nearly any comparable bank, which in turn lowers its cost of capital and enhances its acquisition currency. In a consolidating Sun Belt banking market, that lets HOMB acquire smaller community franchises, impose its cost discipline, and accrete earnings — the MCBI/Tennessee deal being the newest instance. The specialty niches (national commercial finance, marine finance) add non-geographic diversification and higher-yielding assets that a typical community bank cannot replicate.

The trajectory question matters more than the level. The prior report cycle marked competitive position down from 7.5 to 6.5, reflecting the maturing of the efficiency edge (which is now widely recognized and priced) and the reality that HOMB competes against both larger, cheaper-funded national banks and granular local relationship lenders. We hold at 6.5. The moat is stable and genuine, but it is not visibly widening; organic loan growth of 6% is respectable but not share-gaining at a pace that would justify a higher score. The Tennessee expansion is a modest positive for the trajectory if the conversion and cost-save targets are realized, but it is early.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is commercial real estate concentration. Roughly 74.1% of loans are real-estate-secured, and CRE plus construction/land development equals 53.2% of the total book ($8.35 billion funded CRE). Within that, office exposure is $1.01 billion (12.1%) and hotel $1.16 billion (13.9%), with $2.19 billion of unfunded construction/land commitments. Collateral is geographically concentrated in Florida ($2.67 billion) and Texas ($2.32 billion). Office and hotel are the two CRE segments most exposed to secular and cyclical stress, and a Santa Monica office building and a Gunter, TX apartment complex already dominate the OREO balance. Sector-wide office/CRE stress is the primary industry overhang, and while HOMB's low charge-offs, 350% NPL coverage, and 16% CET1 provide substantial cushion, this is the area where a downturn could most meaningfully dent an otherwise clean profile.

Second is borrower concentration: $8.1 billion, or 51.6% of loans, is committed to relationships whose total debt exceeds the $40 million in-house lending limit. A single large credit event carries more impact than at a granular consumer bank. Third is geographic and hurricane risk — ~84% of real estate loans sit in AR/FL/TX/AL/NY, and the 2024 $33.4 million Helene/Milton reserve is a concrete example of catastrophe exposure. Fourth is integration risk on MCBI (a newly added 10-K risk-factor section) and the $1.40 billion of goodwill that would face impairment if acquisitions underperform. Fifth is the securities-book unrealized loss ($223 million AFS gross, HTM ~$100 million below carrying) that would pressure tangible equity if rates reverse — manageable given the capital cushion but not trivial. Sixth is key-man risk around CEO Allison.

None of these is a near-term binary catalyst. They are chronic exposures that the market broadly prices, which is why we score Risk Factors 5.5 rather than lower — but the CRE/large-borrower concentration is real enough, and asymmetric enough, that it caps the score below the level of a granular, diversified consumer lender.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 33.2% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 22.0% (an 11.2-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (33.2% near-term versus 22.0% at six months), which typically signals the market pricing imminent event stress — consistent with the July 15 earnings just past and continued post-print positioning. Put/call skew is -19.4% (call-heavy), and the deeper-tenor skew is also negative (-23.1% at three months), indicating no elevated demand for downside protection. For a HOLD, this is a benign-to-mildly-constructive signal: the options market is not pricing a directional collapse. The rich near-term IV argues against buying premium here and favors patience on entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36).** HOMB compounds tangible book at a mid-teens ROTCE, sustains its ~41% efficiency moat, and continues to benefit from falling funding costs as the Fed cuts, holding NIM near 4.5%. The Tennessee expansion via MCBI proves out — November conversion delivers the cost saves and establishes a beachhead for further Sun Belt roll-up. Buybacks at ~$28 average and a growing dividend return capital while EPS grinds toward $2.60+. Credit stays clean, and the market re-rates the shares toward peer P/E (14.7x on $2.60 = ~$38, tempered to ~$36 for CRE concentration). This delivers ~16% upside plus a ~2.8% yield.

**Bear Case (~$24, roughly -22%).** Office and hotel CRE stress materializes in the concentrated Florida/Texas book, one or more of the large (>$40 million) relationships deteriorate, and charge-offs revert toward the 0.41% seen in 2024. Provision expense rises, EPS growth stalls, and the price-to-book premium compresses back toward 1.5x tangible book (~$23). A rate reversal simultaneously pressures the liability-sensitive NIM and deepens the securities-book unrealized loss, eroding tangible equity. In this scenario the stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $25.50 and potentially below.

The base case sits between these: HOMB is a high-quality bank at a fair price, with limited near-term catalysts to force a re-rating and real (but well-reserved) CRE exposure to cap the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale — a quality franchise worth owning through the cycle for tangible-book compounding and M&A optionality, but not worth chasing at $31 after a 21% run.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the mid-$27s (the CEO's April purchase level), where the forward P/E compresses toward ~11x and the price-to-book premium narrows, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity accelerating beyond the current shallow +1.9% (e.g., a clean Q3 2026 beat with NIM holding above 4.5% and MCBI cost saves confirmed on the November conversion).
- A second confirmed CRE-quality all-clear — office/hotel NPLs stable or declining through 2026 — that would de-risk the concentration overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net charge-offs reverting above ~0.25% of loans, or a specific large (>$40 million) relationship moving to non-accrual, signaling that the concentration risk is crystallizing.
- Goodwill impairment on MCBI or a materially over-priced follow-on acquisition (>2.5x tangible book) that suggests M&A discipline is slipping.
- A dividend or buyback cut, which would flag capital or credit stress not yet visible in trailing data.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release (mid-October) and the November 2026 MCBI systems conversion, which together will test both the NIM trajectory and the cost-save realization.

## Conclusion

Home BancShares is a genuinely high-quality regional bank — top-decile efficiency, 2.1% ROA, fortress 16.3% CET1 capital, clean credit, and a NIM that expanded into a rate-cut cycle. The trajectory of prior scores (declining from 6.9 to 6.4 over recent cycles) reflected not fundamental deterioration but the stock's earlier price weakness and a maturing competitive edge; the fundamentals have if anything improved, with Q2 2026 setting revenue records and the CEO buying $2.7 million of stock in the open market at ~$27. The current-quarter data confirms the quality, not any crack in the thesis.

The constraint is valuation. After a ~21% recovery from the May low, HOMB trades at ~12.3x forward EPS and ~2x tangible book, a fair-to-full price that prices in the quality and leaves only modest upside to the ~$32-33 consensus band. The CRE/office/hotel and large-borrower concentrations are well-reserved but real, and there is no near-term catalyst compelling enough to justify chasing the shares here. We rate HOMB a HOLD (growth-hold) and would become a buyer on a pullback into the mid-$27s, where the risk/reward turns asymmetric and where the most-informed insider was accumulating.
