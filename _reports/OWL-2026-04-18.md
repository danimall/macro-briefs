## Executive Summary

Blue Owl Capital is a $307B AUM alternative asset manager built around permanent capital vehicles, primarily non-traded BDCs, net lease real estate, GP minority stakes, and digital infrastructure. The company has delivered strong top-line growth (~25% CAGR in revenue from 2022-2025) and generated $1.2B in free cash flow in FY2025. At $9.85 per share, the stock trades at roughly 11x forward distributable earnings, with a 9.1% dividend yield, and sits 53% below its 52-week high.

The core problem is an active and escalating private credit redemption crisis. In Q1 2026, $5.4B in redemption requests hit two of Blue Owl's flagship retail BDCs — OCIC at 21.9% of fund NAV and OTIC at 40.7% — far exceeding the 5% quarterly redemption cap. Moody's placed OCIC on negative outlook. OBDC II is being wound down. Blue Owl's stock decline (-53% from peak) materially exceeds peer drawdowns, and short interest sits at 19.8% of float. Earnings estimates are falling across all periods (current year down 7.8% over 90 days), and the GAAP earnings picture is distorted by $673M in equity-based compensation that drives persistent dilution — share count has grown 51% since 2022.

The combination of active redemption stress on Blue Owl's primary fundraising channel, deteriorating earnings momentum, extreme balance sheet leverage (D/E 2.91x, goodwill 68% of total assets), and a $1.8B Tax Receivable Agreement liability make this an unattractive risk/reward at current levels. The dividend is covered by FCF and C-suite insiders bought $7M+ in stock in December 2025, but those purchases were at ~$15 — 52% above today's price — and preceded the worst of the redemption crisis.

## Company Overview

Blue Owl Capital was formed through the 2021 SPAC-driven merger of Owl Rock Capital (private credit) and Dyal Capital Partners (GP minority stakes). The firm operates three platforms:

- **Credit ($157.8B AUM, 61% of FRE revenue):** Direct lending through publicly-traded and non-traded BDCs (OBDC, OCIC, OTIC, OTF), plus alternative credit (Atalaya) and investment grade credit (KAM).
- **GP Strategic Capital ($69.1B AUM, 24% of FRE):** Minority equity stakes in alternative asset managers and professional sports franchises.
- **Real Assets ($80.6B AUM, 15% of FRE):** Net lease real estate ($45.9B), digital infrastructure ($17.1B via IPI Partners acquisition), and real estate credit ($17.5B).

Approximately 84-85% of management fees derive from permanent capital vehicles, which was historically Blue Owl's primary competitive advantage — predictable, long-duration fee streams. The Q1 2026 redemption crisis is testing whether "permanent" describes the legal structure or actual investor behavior.

Blue Owl has completed five significant acquisitions since 2022 (Oak Street, Prima Capital, KAM, Atalaya, IPI Partners), adding $57B+ in AUM and loading the balance sheet with $8.51B in goodwill and intangibles — 68% of total assets.

| Fee-Related Earnings (Non-GAAP) | — | — | $1.25B | $1.50B |
| Distributable Earnings (Non-GAAP) | — | — | $1.13B | $1.31B |

Revenue grew 25% in FY2025, and Non-GAAP FRE grew 19.4%. GAAP net income, however, *declined* 28% to $78.8M. The gap between GAAP and Non-GAAP is extraordinary: $1.31B in Distributable Earnings versus $78.8M in GAAP net income — a 16:1 ratio. The key adjustments are equity-based compensation ($673.5M, up 115% YoY), intangible amortization ($359M), and a new Services Agreement ($233.6M in G&A).

While some of these are genuinely non-cash, equity-based compensation is dilutive. The share count has grown from 433M (2022) to 655M (2025), a 51% increase in three years. Investors valuing OWL on DE multiples must account for this persistent dilution.

FRE margin compressed from 59.4% in 2024 to 58.3% in 2025, reflecting investment in headcount, distribution, and new product launches.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $1.26B in 2025 (+26% YoY), and free cash flow was $1.20B (41.7% FCF margin). The dividend ($0.90/share × ~655M shares ≈ $590M) is covered roughly 2x by FCF. This is the strongest element of the financial profile.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is fragile:

- **Debt/Equity:** 2.91x — very high for an asset-light business
- **Total debt:** ~$3.37B (senior notes + $860M revolver draw)
- **Goodwill & intangibles:** $8.51B (68% of $12.47B total assets)
- **Cash:** $194.5M + ~$1.6B revolver availability
- **Retained earnings:** Negative $1.61B, worsening (from -$1.14B in 2024)
- **TRA liability:** ~$1.8B — a structural cash drain requiring Blue Owl to pay 85% of tax savings to pre-IPO holders

The Altman Z-Score of 0.66 formally indicates deep distress. While this partly reflects the business model (high goodwill from acquisitions), the balance sheet has almost no margin for error if fee streams contract.

### Earnings Quality

Trailing EPS is $0.10 (GAAP), producing the 93x trailing P/E in the data. On a Non-GAAP DE per share basis (~$0.86 in FY2025), the stock trades at roughly 11.5x, which appears cheap — but only if you accept that the ~$700M annual equity-based compensation does not represent real economic cost. The persistent dilution suggests it does.

The earnings surprise track record is mediocre: 1 beat, 2 in-line, 1 miss over the last four quarters. The Q1 2025 miss (-9.5%) coincided with the beginning of the current decline.

## Growth Analysis

### Positives

- AUM grew from $157B (2023) to $307B (2025), roughly doubling in two years.
- 2025 was a record fundraising year: $56.3B in new capital commitments.
- $28.4B in AUM was not yet fee-paying as of year-end 2025 — representing ~$326M in embedded annualized management fees once deployed.
- Digital infrastructure (IPI acquisition, $17.1B AUM) diversifies the platform into AI/data center demand.
- The private wealth distribution channel hit record quarterly flows in late 2025.

### Negatives

- **Estimate revisions are uniformly negative.** Current year EPS estimate has fallen from $0.97 to $0.90 over 90 days (-7.8%). Next year has fallen from $1.14 to $1.03 (-9.6%). The 30-day revision rate is accelerating for next year (-5.9%), indicating deterioration is not stabilizing.
- The redemption crisis directly threatens the core fundraising channel. If retail investors lose confidence in non-traded BDCs, Blue Owl's primary capital formation mechanism is impaired.
- OBDC II is being wound down, removing a growth vehicle.
- Organic AUM growth is difficult to disentangle from acquisition-driven growth. Of $42.98B in AUM additions in 2024, a substantial portion came from Prima, KAM, and Atalaya.

The key question is whether Blue Owl can continue raising capital at scale in a semi-liquid private credit market experiencing its first real stress test. The company claims this is temporary (driven by market-wide concerns around AI disruption of software companies and geopolitical stress), but the magnitude of redemptions — 41% of OTIC — suggests something more structural.

The discount to peers reflects three factors: (1) the redemption crisis, (2) ongoing share dilution, and (3) balance sheet leverage. The stock is 53% below its 52-week high of $21.08 and 24% above its 52-week low of $7.95. The $7.95 low was reached in early April 2026, suggesting the market tested a floor recently.

## Competitive Landscape

Blue Owl competes with Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Ares, and other large-scale alternative managers. Its competitive differentiators include:

1. **Permanent capital concentration:** Higher proportion of permanent/evergreen vehicles than most peers
2. **GP minority stakes:** A unique strategy where Blue Owl invests in other alternative managers — few direct competitors
3. **Early mover in retail non-traded BDCs:** Built distribution infrastructure targeting private wealth channels

However, the current crisis disproportionately affects Blue Owl. Its OCIC and OTIC redemption rates substantially exceed peer vehicles. Blackstone and Cliffwater chose to honor redemptions above the 5% floor, while Blue Owl capped at 5%. This creates reputational damage — retail investors who cannot access their capital lose trust in the platform.

The Zacks Industry Rank for the investment management sector is 210 out of 244 (bottom 14%), indicating broad sector headwinds. Within this weak sector, OWL carries a Zacks Rank #5 (Strong Sell).

The launch of private credit CDS instruments by JPMorgan and Barclays creates a new mechanism for shorting the private credit market broadly. This introduces reflexive dynamics: CDS widening validates bear theses, attracting more shorts, potentially forcing redemptions and realized losses. Blue Owl, as the most visible company experiencing redemption stress, is particularly exposed to this feedback loop.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Private Credit Redemption Crisis (CRITICAL — Active)
If Q2 2026 redemption requests remain elevated (>15% of NAV), the "semi-liquid" model faces a credibility challenge that could structurally impair fundraising across the non-traded BDC channel.

### 2. Balance Sheet Fragility
The balance sheet has no capacity to absorb a sustained revenue decline given its leverage and goodwill concentration.

### 3. Share Dilution
Dilution is persistent and structural — it funds compensation, acquisition earn-outs, and TRA payments. At the current pace, it erodes per-share economics even as headline AUM grows.

### 4. Software Lending Concentration
OTIC's elevated redemption rate is specifically linked to fears about AI disruption of software companies. Management says software is only 8% of total AUM, but the concentration in OTIC specifically is the problem.

### 5. Governance
Controlled company under NYSE rules. Principals hold 80% voting power. Exempt from independent board requirements. CEO compensation nearly tripled from $10.1M to $29.2M over three years while GAAP net income declined.

### 6. Falling Estimates
The revision trend is accelerating, not stabilizing, across all periods.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 63.9% is substantially above 42.5% historical volatility — a 21.3pp spread. The term structure is in backwardation (63.9% near-term > 61.2% at 6 months), indicating the market prices imminent stress rather than a medium-term event. OI put/call ratio of 1.78 confirms institutional protection positioning. Call-heavy near-term volume (0.28 P/C volume ratio) may reflect speculative rebound bets, but structural OI positioning is bearish.

## Investment Thesis

- $28.4B in undeployed fee-paying AUM activates, adding ~$326M in annualized management fees.

- Private credit CDS instruments accelerate reflexive selling dynamics.
- AUM declines as redemption outflows exceed new fundraising.
- Credit quality deterioration in software/tech lending portfolio leads to realized losses.
- Balance sheet fragility forces either a dividend cut or dilutive capital raise.
- Stock trades to 6-8x reduced DE estimates (~$0.70-0.80) = $4.20-$6.40.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Q2 2026 redemption reporting (expected July-August 2026). If OCIC and OTIC redemption requests remain elevated (>15% of NAV), it confirms structural impairment of the semi-liquid model and removes the "one-time stress" narrative. Secondary catalyst: March 31 NAV marks entering the reporting pipeline in May-June 2026 could reveal credit quality deterioration.

**Timing window:** 3-5 months (Q2 2026 earnings and redemption reporting in July-August). Reasonably tight for a short thesis with identified catalysts.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 19.8% of float with 3.7 days to cover. Elevated but below the 25% squeeze-risk threshold. Institutional ownership is substantial. The 19.8% SI means the bearish view is already partially expressed, limiting remaining downside relative to a fresh short. However, the stock is not heavily crowded at multi-year lows — it still trades 24% above the $7.95 trough.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options IV is rich (63.9% vs 42.5% HV), increasing the cost of put-based short expressions. The term structure backwardation indicates the market expects near-term resolution of uncertainty in one direction or another. Carrying costs for a direct short are moderate given the SI level.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $5-7 (30-50% downside from $9.85). Bull case to $14-16 (42-62% upside). This produces a reward:risk ratio of roughly 0.7-1.2:1 — the upside risk materially exceeds the remaining downside given the stock has already declined 53% from its high. The 9.1% dividend yield also creates carry cost for shorts.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is directionally correct — the redemption crisis is real, the balance sheet is fragile, and estimates are falling. But the stock has already priced substantial distress (53% from highs), short interest is elevated at 19.8%, the dividend creates carry cost, and the reward:risk ratio is below 2:1. The correct expression is to avoid owning the stock rather than actively shorting it. A short would become more attractive if the stock rallied to $12-14 on temporary positive news (ceasefire extension, one quarter of calmer redemptions) without underlying fundamentals improving.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of redemption requests below 10% of NAV across OCIC and OTIC, demonstrating the semi-liquid model has stabilized.
2. Earnings estimates stabilize or inflect upward — specifically, 30-day revision trend turns positive.
3. D/E ratio declines below 2.0x, either through debt paydown or equity accumulation.

**Timeline:** The headwinds are likely to persist for at least 6-12 months. The redemption crisis will take multiple quarters to resolve. Balance sheet repair takes longer. Share dilution is ongoing. A potential re-entry point would be mid-to-late 2027 if conditions (1) and (2) above are met, likely at a significantly lower price that compensates for the remaining structural risks.

## Conclusion

Blue Owl Capital has built a large alternative asset management platform, reaching $307B in AUM with competitive positioning in permanent capital vehicles, GP minority stakes, and digital infrastructure. The dividend is supported by $1.2B in FCF, and insider buying signals management conviction.

These positives do not overcome the active and worsening redemption crisis. The 41% redemption request at OTIC represents a genuine stress test of the "permanent capital" model that constitutes Blue Owl's core competitive advantage. The balance sheet carries $8.5B in goodwill against $2.2B in equity, and the launch of private credit CDS instruments by major banks creates new reflexive dynamics that disproportionately affect the most visible redemption-stressed platform.

At $9.85, the stock may appear cheap on Non-GAAP metrics, but the GAAP earnings reality ($0.10 EPS), ongoing dilution, balance sheet fragility, and active redemption crisis present a risk profile that is not adequately compensated by the yield. Recommendation: **AVOID**.
