## Company Overview

TopBuild was spun out of Masco in June 2015, headquartered in Daytona Beach, Florida, and described itself as the largest installer and distributor of insulation and related building products in North America. FY2025 net sales were **$5,409.1M** across two reportable segments:

- **Installation Services** — $3,182.9M of segment sales, $589.5M segment operating profit (18.5% margin). Insulation installation plus, following the July 2025 Progressive acquisition, commercial roofing; also glass and windows, gutters, garage doors, closet shelving, fireplaces.
- **Specialty Distribution** — $2,523.3M of segment sales including $297.1M intercompany, $323.0M segment operating profit (12.8% margin). Building and mechanical insulation, accessories, gutters, shelving, roofing materials distributed to contractors, dealers, and lumber yards.

The vertical integration was mechanical and real: $53.9M of intercompany profit was eliminated in FY2025 consolidation, up from $49.8M in 2024. Structurally, the business was asset-light — net property and equipment of $291.6M against $5.41bn of revenue — with the moat resting on local branch density and jobsite proximity rather than fixed capital.

End-market mix shifted sharply in FY2025: residential sales fell **9.3%** to $3,126.7M while commercial/industrial rose **21.3%** to $2,282.4M, lifting C&I from 35.3% to **42.2%** of the total. Insulation and accessories remained 80.1% of revenue at $4,335.4M (down 2.6%), with a new $215.8M roofing line entirely attributable to Progressive.

Two disclosure facts define the counterparty profile. Customer concentration was unusually low for a housing-levered business: largest customer roughly **3.1%** of sales, top ten roughly **10.6%**. Supplier concentration ran the other way — a handful of fiberglass manufacturers (Owens Corning, Johns Manville, Knauf, CertainTeed) supplied the dominant product category.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth was entirely inorganic.** FY2025 net sales rose 1.5% to $5,409.1M. Management's own decomposition in MD&A: **+8.8pp acquisitions, +0.8pp price, −8.1pp volume**, which sums to the reported +1.5pp. Installation Services sales fell 3.4% with volume contributing roughly **−11.2pp**. The $391M of revenue recognized from 2025 deals (Progressive $215.8M, SPI $157.8M, others $17.3M) carried the headline.

**Margins compressed at every line and in both segments.**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Δ '24→'25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 30.87% | 30.49% | 29.01% | −148bp |
| SG&A / sales | 13.96% | 13.86% | 14.37% | +51bp |
| Operating margin | 16.92% | 16.63% | 14.64% | −199bp |
| Net margin | 11.83% | 11.68% | 9.65% | −203bp |

Adding back all disclosed one-timers — $12.5M of branch-optimization charges in cost of sales, $2.0M of SG&A severance, and the $11.4M SPI inventory step-up — still leaves Specialty Distribution at 13.5% (down 160bp) and Installation at 18.7% (down ~100bp). The compression is operating deleverage on falling volume plus mix dilution from lower-margin roofing and mechanical distribution, not a purchase-accounting artifact. The disclosure does not permit separating the two.

**Cash generation was high quality.** Operating cash flow of $756.3M against $521.7M of net income (1.45x) was driven by $169.4M of D&A (including $95.3M intangible amortization), $16.6M stock comp, and $9.4M of impairment, not working-capital harvesting. Working capital contributed only **+$25.7M**. Cash taxes of $186.9M matched $181.9M of book expense; cash interest of $88.5M ran below $103.8M of expense. Capex was $59.4M, producing **free cash flow of $696.9M** (12.9% of sales, down from 15.1% in FY2023) and FCF/EBITDA of 72.5% on derived EBITDA of $961.3M.

**The balance sheet was transformed in twelve months.** Total debt principal rose from $1,387.5M to $2,871.9M while cash fell from $400.3M to $184.7M, taking net debt from ~$987M to **$2,687M** — **2.80x** FY2025 EBITDA against a 3.75x covenant maximum, with interest coverage of 9.3x on EBITDA (7.6x on operating profit) versus a 3.00x minimum and $933.9M of undrawn revolver. Goodwill rose to $3,045.2M and net intangibles to $1,351.6M, together **66.6% of total assets**, leaving tangible book equity at **−$2,081M**. Debt maturities were back-loaded: $62.5M annually 2026–2028, then $462.5M in 2029 and $971.9M in 2030, across $400M 3.625% notes (2029), $500M 4.125% notes (2032), $750M 5.625% notes (2034), and a $1,221.9M term loan at 4.97%.

The Altman Z reading circulating in screening tools is unreliable here: recomputed on December 31, 2025 inputs it is ~3.49 using the stale $9.9bn market cap, ~2.43 substituting book equity, and ~4.09 at the $505 deal-implied equity value. The model's tangible-asset orientation fits a goodwill-heavy, asset-light distributor poorly. The direct solvency evidence — 9.3x coverage, 1.94x current ratio, 0.95 turns of covenant headroom, $697M of FCF — was sound. Leverage tripled, but this was not a company near distress. Financial health scores 4.8 rather than higher because the FCF and coverage strengths are now inside a materially more levered acquirer and no longer accrue to a separate security.

**Earnings trajectory into 2026 deteriorated.** Reported quarterly EPS: $5.31 (Q2'25, +3.2% surprise), $5.36 (Q3'25, +0.1%), $4.50 (Q4'25, **−1.8% miss**), $3.75 (Q1'26, +1.5%). Q1 is seasonally weakest, but the sequence of near-consensus prints on declining absolute earnings — three beats and one miss across four quarters, with average surprise magnitude under 2% — indicates estimates were being reset downward rather than exceeded. This supports the late-cycle reading of the deal timing.

## Growth Analysis

The cleanest available read on underlying trajectory is the Note 15 pro forma, which places Progressive and SPI in both periods: FY2025 pro forma revenue **$6,164.1M and net income $575.9M** versus FY2024 pro forma of **$6,431.0M and $681.9M** — like-for-like declines of **4.1%** and **15.5%**. Adjusting for identified one-timers does not reverse the direction.

Against that, the commercial/industrial line grew 21.3%, consistent with the demand drivers both QXO and third-party investors emphasize: aging housing stock, energy-efficiency codes raising insulation content per unit, and data-center construction. TopBuild's own December 2025 investor-day targets were $9–10bn revenue and $1.7–2.0bn adjusted EBITDA by 2030 with cumulative 2026–2030 FCF of $4.2–5.0bn — management projections requiring both a residential recovery and continued M&A, never independently validated, and now superseded by the acquisition.

The macro backdrop for the residential leg is not established in the provided sources beyond the company's own housing-starts-linked disclosures; specific mortgage-rate, application-demand, list-price, and mortgage-lender-financing claims are [unverified] and are omitted here. Goodwill impairment testing at TopBuild was explicitly geared to "estimated U.S. housing starts," and the FY2025 test escalated from qualitative to quantitative. The commercial/industrial and data-center legs are genuinely strong, but they carry different economics — fixed-price long-duration contracts, surety bonding, retainage of $81.6M, contract assets of $74.0M — and Progressive's isolation as a standalone third reporting unit with $442.3M of goodwill removed the ability to offset weakness there against cushion elsewhere.

The decisive point for scoring: whatever growth the platform delivers from here accrues to QXO shareholders. There is no BLD instrument through which to participate. Growth prospects score 3.5 — the operating trajectory was negative organically, and forward participation is zero.

Remaining performance obligations of $579.6M (half from Progressive) are not backlog and the company does not describe them as such: they represent roughly 10.7% of annual sales, cover primarily longer-duration commercial work, and structurally exclude most of the residential business that generated 57.8% of FY2025 revenue.

The transaction terms do permit a valuation frame on the business as sold:

- **$17.0bn total value** against FY2025 as-reported EBITDA of $961.3M = **17.7x**, versus QXO's stated **14.9x** pre-synergy and 11.8x post-synergy. The gap is entirely the difference between GAAP-derived EBITDA and QXO's acquisition-annualized non-GAAP measure (~$1.14bn on ~$6.2bn of annualized sales, ~18.4% margin). - **$505 ÷ FY2025 diluted EPS of $18.28 = 27.6x trailing GAAP earnings.**
- **$505 ÷ FY2025 FCF per diluted share of $24.42 = 20.7x trailing free cash flow.**
- Reconciliation check: $505 × 28.069M shares = $14.17bn equity, plus $2.69bn net debt = $16.86bn, consistent with the ~$17bn headline.

That is a full-cycle price on peak-margin, acquisition-annualized EBITDA for a business whose organic volumes were contracting at a high single-digit rate and whose pro forma net income had fallen 15.5%. Two readings are available and the evidence does not adjudicate between them: a trough purchase of a category leader ahead of recovery, or a late-cycle purchase on normalizing margins. Q1 2026's $3.75 of EPS, down sequentially for a second quarter, tilts modestly toward the latter.

Peer benchmarking is not informative here. The supplied comparables — American Airlines, Ryder, StandardAero, Chart Industries, Flowserve — share only a market-cap band and an "Industrials" GICS label; two carry negative P/Es. The 27.8x peer median has no bearing on an insulation installer/distributor, and the profile's 17.0x forward and 19.9x trailing multiples are computed off the invalid stale price.

Valuation scores 2.5. Cheapness against a stale print is a data artifact, not a mispricing, and apparently cheap prices with no verifiable catalyst tend to sort the wrong way. Here there is not even a security to buy at that price.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive assessment is the one dimension that holds up, and it is why the score is 6.0 rather than lower. TopBuild was the #1 insulation installer and distributor in North America, uniquely owning both layers — capturing an intercompany margin that consolidation eliminates and providing dual routes to market. Customer concentration of ~3.1% (largest) and ~10.6% (top ten) is unusually granular for a homebuilder-levered business. QXO's own CEO cited TopBuild's "industry-leading adjusted EBITDA margin of approximately 18%" and said QXO planned to replicate TopBuild's practices, including deploying its "special OPS" teams — the acquirer publicly conceding the target was the better operator.

Offsetting that: supplier bargaining power sits with four fiberglass manufacturers; installers such as Installed Building Products compete directly in residential insulation; Beacon (now QXO) and SRS/Home Depot compete in roofing distribution; Builders FirstSource competes in lumber and components. Segment margins declined in both segments even after one-timer add-backs, a trajectory signal that matters more than level.

One framing correction: the C&I mix shift from 35.3% to 42.2% reduces single-family-cycle exposure but does **not** constitute customer diversification — concentration was already low and there is no evidence it improved. Commercial roofing introduces new concentration vectors instead: larger individual projects, bonding capacity constraints, retainage, and longer collection cycles, with revenue recognized on a cost-to-cost measure of progress that PwC designated a critical audit matter.

Industry structure supports the consolidation logic. Per the Webb Analytics 2025 report cited by HousingWire, 2025 was the busiest year of the past decade for building-materials M&A by facilities acquired even as total deal count fell 30% — four of 120 reported deals accounted for 85% of facilities. Consolidation is concentrating into a handful of megadeal buyers, and TopBuild's asset became a scarce one. That scarcity was monetized at $505; it is not available to new capital.

## Risk Assessment

**Instrument risk is now the dominant risk.** The security has been extinguished with settlement mechanics unverified. Election results, applied proration factors, aggregate cash paid, QXO shares issued, and the price on the relevant measurement date are all unconfirmed from filings. The completion 8-K, Form 25, and Form 15 were not read. A holder cannot determine from available evidence exactly what was received.

**Realized-consideration risk.** Because 55% of consideration was a fixed 20.2 QXO shares with no disclosed collar, post-signing risk transferred to TopBuild holders. Signing-implied QXO value was $25.00; QXO traded at **$13.46** on July 30, 2026, after a >20% July decline and a −30.4% twelve-month return. Pure-stock election: 20.2 × $13.46 = **$271.89** (−46.2% versus headline). Blended at the announced 45/55: $227.25 cash + 11.11 shares × $13.46 = **$376.79** (−25.4%). These are sensitivities on an observed price, not settlement facts, and QXO's July weakness had non-deal contributors including higher rates and oil.

**Residual-exposure risk (QXO).** The identified causal channel for QXO's decline is capital structure and integration risk. QXO has closed Beacon (~$11bn, 2025), Kodiak (~$2.25bn, April 1, 2026) and TopBuild (~$17bn) inside roughly eighteen months, with the TopBuild consideration financed in stock and debt. Illustrative dilution: ~$14.2bn equity consideration × 55% ÷ $25.00 ≈ 312M QXO shares against a pre-deal count near 725M inferred from third-party market-cap data — a ~43% share-count increase, taking legacy holders to roughly 70% ownership. This is illustrative only; the pre-deal count is inferred, not filed.

**Change-of-control put on $1.65bn of notes.** Each of the three series carried a repurchase offer at **101% of principal plus accrued interest**. At December 31, 2025 the 3.625% notes were marked 97.1 and the 4.125% notes 95.25, giving holders of $900M of paper an economic incentive to tender. If triggered and fully tendered, roughly **$1.67bn** of refinancing or cash would have been required at close on top of the ~45% cash equity portion. Standard indentures often condition the put on a Change of Control *Triggering Event* including a ratings downgrade; the indentures were not read, and whether the put was triggered is unknown. This is a plausible contributor to the capital-structure concerns cited by QXO investors.

**Legacy operating risks that transfer with the assets.** Note 5 discloses **$762.0M of accumulated goodwill impairment** already recorded in the Installation reporting unit against $1,960.0M gross goodwill — this business has been written down before, and impairment testing is geared to housing starts. Amortization steps up to **$142.7M in 2026** from $95.3M. The allowance for credit losses rose 57% from $18.5M to $29.1M with bad-debt provision of $14.5M versus $6.5M in 2023, though this cannot be attributed between genuine contractor credit deterioration and reserves against $236.2M of acquired receivables. Immigration-enforcement and I-9 audit risk received prominent Item 1A treatment for a 14,707-employee, labor-intensive installer. Thirty multi-employer pension plans include the Carpenters Pension Trust Fund for Northern California in the PPA red zone, CBA expiring June 30, 2027. Progressive and SPI — roughly 7% of assets and 7% of revenue — were excluded from the FY2025 ICFR assessment, a permitted first-year scope limitation rather than a control deficiency.

Risk factors score 3.0. The binary event has already resolved, but the specific downside — receiving 25–46% less than the headline value — is unresolved and unverifiable from the current evidence set.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (for the residual QXO exposure, not for BLD)

QXO bought the best operator in insulation at a cyclical trough. FY2025 volumes fell ~8% because single-family starts fell, not because share was lost — commercial/industrial grew 21.3% and now represents 42.2% of sales, with data-center construction a durable, well-corroborated demand driver. $300M of synergies by 2030 against an acquisition-annualized $1.14bn EBITDA base takes the effective multiple to 11.8x. Vertical integration extends across roofing and lumber; the combined platform has more than 450 TopBuild locations and claims #1 or #2 positions in insulation, roofing, waterproofing, and regional lumber. If starts recover in 2027–2028 on structural undersupply and pent-up demand, the assets were bought cheaply and QXO's July derating is the entry. Buybacks executed at $295–$397 ahead of a $505 exit were value-accretive to remaining holders.

### Bear Case

The price was paid on peak-margin, acquisition-annualized non-GAAP EBITDA — 17.7x on as-reported figures — while the organic engine decelerated: pro forma revenue −4.1%, pro forma net income −15.5%, operating margin −199bp with both segments down even after add-backs, and Q1 2026 EPS down sequentially for a second quarter. The residential recovery required for the 2030 targets remains gated on U.S. housing starts, the same variable to which the impairment model is levered. QXO now carries the combined leverage of Beacon, Kodiak, and TopBuild, ~43% incremental share count, up to ~$1.67bn of potentially puttable notes at 101%, and 66.6%-intangible acquired assets in a reporting unit with $762.0M of prior impairment precedent, tested against housing starts. Impairment would be non-cash and would not breach an EBITDA-based leverage covenant, but it would reduce reported equity in a structure where tangible book is already deeply negative. Amortization steps up $47.4M in 2026 alone. For the TopBuild holder specifically, the fixed 20.2 ratio meant the "$505, 23.1% premium" described value at signing, not value delivered.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For existing holders — disposition, not investment.**

1. **Determine what you actually hold.** Confirm from your custodian statement whether the position settled as $505 cash, a mix of cash plus QXO shares, or QXO shares only. The announced structure capped cash at 45% in aggregate with proration; QXO retained the right to increase the stock portion if stock elections exceeded 55%.
2. **If the residual is QXO common stock, treat it as a new, unrelated position requiring fresh underwriting.** It is a highly levered serial acquirer that has closed more than $13bn of acquisitions in the eleven months preceding the TopBuild announcement (per QXO's April 2026 release), is down >30% over twelve months, and derated >20% in July on capital-structure and integration concerns. Nothing in TopBuild's operating record underwrites QXO's balance sheet. The specific items to verify before deciding to hold: post-close net leverage and covenant headroom; whether the $1.65bn of legacy TopBuild notes were put at 101%, refinanced, or left outstanding; the final QXO share count and any preferred or warrant overhang; and disclosed integration cost versus the $300M synergy target.
3. **Do not hold on the strength of TopBuild's operating quality.** The 18% adjusted EBITDA margin, ~3.1% customer concentration, and $697M of FCF were real, and they are now consolidated into a much larger, more levered entity.

**Re-entry criteria for BLD as a security: none exist.** Re-entry would require the security to be reconstituted — a spin-off, carve-out IPO, or separate listing of the insulation platform. No such transaction has been announced or is contemplated in any available disclosure. There is no price at which to re-enter.

**Timeline.** Remove BLD from the active coverage universe pending confirmation of deregistration. If QXO announces a divestiture, spin-off, or separate financing of the insulation business, reinitiate at that point.

**Documentation gaps to close if a holder needs certainty.** The merger completion 8-K, the final election and proration results, Form 25/Form 15, and the note indentures (Exhibits 4.2, 4.4, 4.6 to the FY2025 10-K) were not retrieved. Each materially affects what was received and what obligations transferred.

## Conclusion

**AVOID. Overall score 4.1**, down from 4.5 in July and 6.0 in March — a trajectory that reflects the disappearance of the instrument rather than progressive operating deterioration, though the operating trend was genuinely negative: organic volume −8.1%, pro forma revenue −4.1%, pro forma net income −15.5%, operating margin −199bp, and Q1 2026 EPS down sequentially for a second quarter.

The mechanical facts govern. The registrant under TopBuild's CIK is now QXO Insulation, LLC; trading stopped after June 30, 2026; the canonical price snapshot fails validation with no authoritative price and unknown security status; analyst coverage has halved and no price targets are published. There is no BLD equity to buy, hold, or short, and no forward participation in the platform's economics. The June 30 close of $354.53 and the derived $9.94bn market cap are stale artifacts that do not reconcile with the announced consideration and should not anchor any decision.

The business that was sold was a legitimate category leader — largest insulation installer and distributor in North America, ~18% adjusted EBITDA margin acknowledged as best-in-class by its own acquirer, low customer concentration, $697M of clean free cash flow, and covenant headroom of roughly 0.95 turns. QXO paid 17.7x as-reported FY2025 EBITDA (14.9x on its acquisition-annualized measure) and 27.6x trailing GAAP earnings for it, at a point when organic volumes were contracting and the residential recovery the 2030 targets assumed remains gated on U.S. housing starts. Whether that proves a trough purchase or a late-cycle one is QXO's question now.

For the TopBuild holder, the operative arithmetic is that the fixed 20.2 exchange ratio transferred post-signing risk to the seller: at QXO's $13.46 on July 30, a pure-stock election delivered roughly $272 and a blended election roughly $377 against a $505 headline. Those figures are sensitivities, not settlements, and the settlement detail remains unverified. Any capital still attached to this position is now exposed to a levered serial acquirer that requires its own underwriting, and that exposure should be held only on an affirmative QXO thesis, not on inertia from a TopBuild thesis that no longer has a security attached to it.
