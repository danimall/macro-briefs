**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SL Green is Manhattan's largest office landlord, a self-managed REIT concentrated almost entirely in midtown Manhattan trophy office. The investment case is unusually two-sided, and after working through the data I land on a HOLD. The asset base is inflecting positively: Q1 2026 produced the single largest first-quarter leasing volume in the company's 28-year history (929,264 sf), occupancy is 94.4% and rising, and a new secular demand driver — AI tenants leasing large premium blocks (Harvey AI 185,326 sf at One Madison, Clay 163,095 sf at 11 Madison) — is showing up in the leasing pipeline. Against this, the capital structure is levered and refinancing-exposed: ~$10.8B of total economic debt (consolidated plus SLG's share of JV debt), a weighted-average consolidated maturity of just 3.2 years, and ~$2.9B of obligations due in 2027.

The market has already absorbed much of the bear case. The stock is down 22% over the trailing twelve months and sits at $44.74, below the consensus mean price target of $47.61 and median of $46.00. Sell-side ratings remain net-constructive (2 strong buy, 6 buy, 9 hold, 1 sell) with an improving sentiment trend, and short interest is elevated at 20.1% of float. A depressed price below fair-value consensus, a crowded short, and operating data that is inflecting up is not the profile of a stock to actively avoid. It is also not a clean buy, because the leverage and 2027 maturity wall create real refinancing risk that one strong leasing quarter does not resolve.

The decisive factor against a bearish call is management's demonstrated capital-markets access. In March 2026 SLG closed a $1.65B refinancing of One Madison Avenue (the largest U.S. office CMBS issuance in twelve months) and extended its corporate revolver and a new $750M term loan out to 2030/2031. These contemporaneous facts directly rebut the thesis that the 2027 wall is unmanageable. The call is HOLD: the operating recovery and the levered, refinancing-dependent capital structure roughly offset, and the bear view is already in the price.

## Company Overview

SL Green, formed in 1997, is a vertically integrated REIT that owns, develops, leases, manages, finances, and special-services Manhattan office assets. As of 3/31/26 it held interests in approximately 45 buildings totaling ~26.9M sf (33 consolidated, 12 unconsolidated JVs), plus a 2.5M-sf non-core portfolio. The flagship is One Vanderbilt, the supertall tower beside Grand Central that houses the company's HQ and the SUMMIT observation deck, carried at a $4.7B gross JV valuation after a 2025 sale of a 5% stake to Mori Building.

Three segments drive results. **Real Estate** is the core owned/JV office portfolio (~94% leased). **Debt & Preferred Equity (DPE)** includes mezzanine lending plus the new SLG Opportunistic Debt Fund (initial close Nov 2024; $1.3B committed, $303.6M funded), where SLG acts as GP/manager earning fees and promote — a capital-light, counter-cyclical pivot to distressed-CRE credit. **SUMMIT** is the high-margin observation-deck experience, now relaunching a premium "Ascent" tier and expanding internationally to Paris.

The competitive position rests on midtown scale, trophy asset quality, deep relationships with foreign institutional capital (National Pension Service of Korea, Mori, Hines, PGIM, Vornado, Allianz, RXR), and a reputation as the go-to operator for premium midtown space.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and GAAP earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $1.003B, up 13% YoY. The company is GAAP-unprofitable, driven by depreciation and impairments: a net loss of -$96.9M in 2025, with the Q1 2026 net loss widening to -$84.4M (-$1.20/sh) from -$21.1M (-$0.30) a year earlier. For a REIT, GAAP net income is not the operative measure — FFO and cash flow are. The collapse in current-year consensus EPS (from -$2.22 to -$6.31) is a GAAP figure driven by impairment and depreciation marks contributed by only one or two analysts. It is not evidence of operating deterioration, and it would be inconsistent to lean on it as a core bearish pillar after declaring GAAP EPS the wrong lens. The forward-looking estimate that matters more — next-year EPS — was revised +7.7% over the trailing 90 days.

**FFO.** Normalized FFO/share has trended down over a multi-year window: $6.58 (2021) → $6.76 (2022) → $5.48 (2023) → $5.24 (2024) → $5.48 (2025). Q1 2026 FFO was $64.6M ($0.84/sh), down from $106.5M a year earlier. The headline 39% YoY drop materially overstates the underlying change. The prior-year quarter benefited from ~$35M of one-time items (a $25M loan-loss recovery and $10M of CMBS interest). Normalizing the base to ~$71.5M puts the clean YoY decline closer to ~10%. Quarterly FFO is also genuinely lumpy — Q4 2025 FFO was $131.9M, roughly 2x Q1 2026 — so judging the franchise on a single quarter is misleading. The multi-year downtrend from $6.76 toward ~$5.48 is real and reflects the office cycle, but it is not a cliff.

**Cash flow.** This is the legitimate concern. Full-year 2025 operating cash flow was only ~$83M, and Q1 2026 operating cash flow turned negative at -$17.6M. The dividend is being supported partly by asset sales and JV distributions rather than recurring operations. The common dividend was cut ~20% YoY (Q1'26 declared $0.618/sh vs. $0.773/sh), prudent given the coverage picture.

**Leverage.** Consolidated debt is $4.77B (up from $4.04B at YE25 after the $768M Park Avenue Tower acquisition), 86% fixed/hedged, weighted-average rate 5.25%, weighted-average maturity 3.21 years. SLG's share of unconsolidated JV debt is $5.997B, putting total economic leverage at ~$10.8B against ~$3.5B book equity and a ~$3.2B market cap. The Altman Z-score of ~1.1 nominally flags distress, but that model is manufacturing-oriented and a poor fit for a high-leverage REIT; the real risk is refinancing, not insolvency. Liquidity is thin at ~$0.6B (3/31/26: $418M undrawn revolver + $169M cash), down from ~$782M at YE25.

**Capital allocation.** Aggressive recycling under a $2.5B 2026 disposition program: 10 East 53rd sold for $312.2M, a 49% stake in 346 Madison sold to Mori, 7 Dey St condos held for sale at $220.5M, 690 Madison sold in Q1'26 for a $15.3M gain. Proceeds fund deleveraging, refinancing, and selective trophy acquisitions.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is the leasing recovery, covered above: a record first quarter at ~$105/sf across 51 Manhattan office leases, with new-lease mark-to-market modestly positive (~$103/sf new vs. ~$102 prior). The new demand vector is AI: Harvey AI and Clay join earlier large premium leases (Pinterest, IBM, Sigma). These are tenants leasing SLG space, not partnerships or products — AI firms are now a material source of large-block premium demand in exactly the trophy segment SLG owns.

The Debt Fund is a quieter growth lever. SLG is monetizing the distressed-CRE cycle as a lender/manager, with Q1'26 fee income of $20M (up from $12.3M), plus promote upside. This is capital-light and counter-cyclical to the owned portfolio.

The constraint on growth is the macro and rate backdrop. Refinancing JV debt at current rates (vs. legacy ~3% coupons like One Vanderbilt's 2.95%) compresses JV cash flow, and the multi-year FFO downtrend reflects that pressure. Growth here means stabilization and gradual recovery in FFO/share, not a return to the $6.76 peak in the near term.

## Valuation Assessment

SLG screens as unprofitable on GAAP (negative trailing/forward P/E), so peer P/E comparison is not meaningful. The Office REIT peer set is itself a mix of positive and negative reported P/Es (HIW 31.2x, CDP 23.4x, KRC 18.7x, CUZ -839.6x, DEI -70.9x), confirming the sector is earning depressed-to-negative GAAP results across the board. Hudson Pacific's 2026 core FFO guidance of just $1.10–$1.18 underscores how compressed office-REIT earnings are sector-wide.

On the metrics that matter for a REIT, the stock is neither clearly cheap nor clearly expensive. At $44.74 against ~$5.48 normalized FFO/share, SLG trades at roughly 8x trailing FFO — a discount to its own history, reflecting cycle and leverage risk. The 5.4% dividend yield (post-cut) sits below the peer median of 6.3%, consistent with the market pricing some continued coverage uncertainty. Book equity of ~$3.5B against a ~$3.2B market cap implies the market is valuing the equity near book — neither a deep value-trap discount nor a premium.

The stock trades below consensus targets with an improving sentiment trend. After a 22% decline, the valuation-compression phase appears largely complete relative to where the Street sees fair value. This supports a HOLD: the downside from leverage and the 2027 wall is substantially in the price, but the absence of a clear catalyst for FFO re-acceleration and the thin liquidity prevent a higher valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

The Manhattan office market is bifurcating sharply. Trophy/Class-A space near transit (Grand Central, Park/Madison corridors) is tightening with rising rents and falling availability, while commodity older stock languishes. SLG is concentrated in the recovering premium segment and is capturing the new AI demand vector. Its structural edges — midtown scale, trophy quality, foreign-capital JV access, and a growing fee/credit platform — are real and improving at the margin. The 5-year TSR of 104 vs. a peer group at 65 confirms SLG has outperformed office-REIT peers through the cycle.

SLG's competitive position is stable-to-improving within a structurally challenged asset class. The risk is that the bifurcation thesis is well understood and the premium-segment recovery is partly priced. Peers Vornado (a JV partner at 280 Park), RXR, and Hudson Pacific face similar dynamics; SLG's scale and capital-markets access differentiate it on the financing side specifically.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing / maturity wall — the dominant risk.** The ~$2.9B due in 2027 breaks down as $936M consolidated property mortgages, a $300M term loan, and ~$1.45B (SLG share) of JV debt, including 245 Park Ave ($885.8M SLG share, June 2027), 1515 Broadway ($385.7M, March 2027), Worldwide Plaza ($300.6M, Nov 2027), and 220 E 42nd St ($253.2M, Dec 2027). Much is non-recourse, but refinancing at higher rates compresses JV cash flow and could require equity infusions. The counterweight is the demonstrated capital-markets access detailed in the Executive Summary; the 2027 wall is a live risk being actively managed, not an unaddressed cliff.

**Concentration.** ~100% NYC office. NYC fiscal/political conditions (management has flagged the Mamdani administration and a proposed pied-à-terre tax), sublease supply, and macro/tariff uncertainty all hit SLG directly.

**JV / off-balance-sheet exposure.** $6B of JV debt and negative-book-value JVs (~$223M with implicit funding commitments). Foreclosures are occurring: the lender foreclosed on the 650 Fifth Ave retail JV (April 2026); 11 West 34th St JV debt has been past maturity since Feb 2023; one DPE loan has been in default/non-accrual since 2025. These are real but contained, mostly non-recourse events.

**Cash flow / dividend coverage.** Negative Q1 operating cash flow and a dividend partly funded by asset sales create coverage risk if leasing momentum stalls. The 20% dividend cut already reflects management's recognition of this.

**Interest-rate sensitivity.** A 100bp move adds ~$5.5M consolidated + ~$4.1M JV-share annual interest.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in contango (near-term ATM IV 48.0% < 6-month 50.8%), a normal-to-complacent shape that does not signal an imminent binary event. IV is rich relative to 1-year historical vol of 37.2% (a 10.8pp spread), and the put/call skew is modestly elevated at 3.2% — consistent with some hedging demand but not extreme. The most informative cross-current is the 20.1% short interest against this benign options structure: bears are positioned heavily in the equity, but the options market is not pricing acute downside. With 7.4 days to cover and a price already below consensus targets, any positive catalyst (a clean Q2 FFO print, additional asset sales at firm pricing, a further refinancing) carries asymmetric squeeze risk to the upside. This reinforces the HOLD rather than AVOID conclusion.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The Manhattan trophy office recovery is real and accelerating, with record Q1 leasing, 94.4% occupancy, and a new demand driver in AI tenants. Management has demonstrated capital-markets access, the dividend has been right-sized, and the Debt Fund adds a capital-light, counter-cyclical fee stream. Pay-for-performance alignment is strong, with the CEO's "compensation actually paid" collapsing to $959K in 2025 as equity marked down with the stock. At a ~8x FFO multiple, below consensus targets, with a crowded 20.1% short base, the stock could re-rate meaningfully if FFO stabilizes and the 2027 wall is refinanced on acceptable terms. In a bull scenario, the stock approaches the $70 analyst high.

**Bear Case.** The multi-year FFO downtrend ($6.76 → ~$5.48) reflects structural pressure that one leasing quarter does not reverse. Operating cash flow is negative, liquidity is thin, and economic leverage sits against a far smaller market cap. The 2027 maturity wall must be refinanced at materially higher rates than the legacy ~3% coupons, compressing JV cash flow and potentially requiring equity infusions. JV foreclosures are already occurring. In a bear scenario where rates stay high and leasing momentum fades, the stock revisits the $36 analyst low (-20% from current).

The two sides roughly offset. The asset base is improving while the capital structure is levered and refinancing-dependent, and management is actively and successfully managing that structure. This supports a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (mid-July), which will test the "FFO cadence" thesis against a cleaner comparison, and progress on the 2027 maturity refinancings through H2 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 FFO/share stabilizes at or above ~$1.30–$1.40 quarterly on a clean basis, confirming the Q1 weakness was timing rather than deterioration, AND operating cash flow returns to positive.
- One or more of the 2027 JV maturities (1515 Broadway, 245 Park) is refinanced on terms that preserve JV cash flow, removing the dominant overhang.
- Completion of the $2.5B disposition program at or above marked values, restoring liquidity above ~$1B.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A 2027 maturity is missed or refinanced only via a dilutive equity infusion, OR a material recourse exposure crystallizes from a JV foreclosure.
- Occupancy reverses below ~92% or leasing volume falls materially below the trailing run-rate, signaling the trophy recovery has stalled.
- A further dividend cut or evidence the dividend is being funded entirely by asset sales.

## Conclusion

SL Green is a genuinely balanced situation, and the call is HOLD. The bear case — declining multi-year FFO, negative operating cash flow, a dividend cut, high economic leverage, and a real 2027 wall with live JV foreclosures — is offset by concrete, contemporaneous positives: record Q1 leasing, rising occupancy, AI-tenant demand, the completed One Madison refinancing, extended corporate maturities, and best-in-class TSR.

Three points keep this from being an AVOID. First, the headline 39% YoY FFO decline normalizes to ~10% after stripping ~$35M of prior-year one-time items. Second, the GAAP EPS estimate collapse is a depreciation/impairment artifact, the wrong lens for a REIT, while the forward-looking next-year estimate improved +7.7%. Third, the stock trades below consensus targets with net-constructive ratings and a crowded short base — the bearish view is substantially priced, and squeeze risk is asymmetric to the upside on any positive catalyst. The leverage and refinancing exposure prevent a bullish lean; the active, successful liability management and inflecting operating data prevent active avoidance. Hold, and reassess at Q2 earnings and on the 2027 refinancing path.
