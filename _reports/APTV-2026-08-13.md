## Executive Summary

Aptiv completed the spin-off of Electrical Distribution Systems into Versigent PLC (NYSE: VGNT) on April 1, 2026, converting a ~$20.4B three-segment automotive supplier into a ~$12.7B two-segment company. Two quarters of post-spin financials are now visible, and they tell a more divided story than the separation thesis implied. Engineered Components — the connector and interconnect business benchmarked against Amphenol, TE Connectivity and Molex — delivered a 17.6% adjusted operating margin in Q2 (up 250bps year over year) and 67% of total adjusted operating income on 54.5% of segment revenue. Intelligent Systems, the segment sold as the high-technology growth engine, saw its Sensors and Compute product line (roughly 42% of total company revenue) decline 1.6% in Q2 and 0.9% in H1 while gross margin fell from 19.0% to 18.5%. The growth line inside that segment, Software and Services, grew 10.9% but is only $152M per quarter, or 4.6% of revenue.

The August 4 guidance reset was the proximate cause of a 16.6% single-session decline (from $57.23 to $47.72 on 16.96M shares against 2.72M average volume). FY2026 revenue guidance of $12.6-12.8B implies H2 revenue of $6,294-6,494M against H1 of $6,306M — flat to +3% into a seasonally weaker half, with management attributing the reset to China. Consensus followed: the current-quarter estimate fell 16.9% in 30 days to $1.33, current-year fell 7.5% over 90 days, and next-year fell 3.6% to $6.53. Adjusted EPS of $1.63 beat the $1.42-1.46 non-GAAP consensus, so the problem was the forward view rather than the quarter.

What holds the stock up is valuation support and an unusually clean insider signal. At $48.655 the shares trade at 7.4-8.5x next-year adjusted consensus, ~6.6-7.0x EV/EBITDA, and 1.16x book. The remaining $1,744M buyback authorization equals 17.3% of market cap and is materially more accretive here than at the $61.59 Q2 average or the $65.84 June average at which it was executed. Between August 5 and 11, three insiders bought $8.06M of stock in the open market: director Paul Meister ($5.00M at $47.33), Chair and CEO Kevin Clark ($2.50M at $48.89), and director Sean Mahoney ($0.55M at ~$50.25). Clark's purchase equals roughly 13% of his FY2025 total compensation. We rate the shares HOLD: probability-weighted expected return is positive (~+14%) but dispersion is wide, the decisive evidence on working capital and China does not arrive until the Q3 report, and the discount to peers narrows considerably once earnings bases are aligned.

## Company Overview

Post-spin Aptiv describes itself as an industrial technology company spanning automotive, commercial vehicle, aerospace and defense, telecom and datacom, and diversified industrial end markets. It reports two segments:

**Engineered Components** ($1,800M Q2 net sales, $317M adjusted operating income, 17.6% margin, 27.6% gross margin) supplies connection systems, high-performance interconnects, and cable management and protection. Its named competitors are Amphenol, Molex and TE Connectivity. The June 2026 agreement to acquire Optical Fiber Packaging Ltd. for approximately $18M is small in dollars but signals intent to shift end-market mix toward telecom and datacom, where the interconnect peer group earns its multiples.

**Intelligent Systems** ($1,501M Q2 net sales, $156M adjusted operating income, 10.4% margin, 18.5% gross margin) supplies sensors, compute platforms and software, and houses Wind River. Competitors include Bosch, Denso, Valeo, Mobileye, Harman, Gentex, Aumovio, and for Wind River, Nutanix and Red Hat.

The parent is incorporated in Jersey and tax-resident in Switzerland (Schaffhausen), with a maximum combined Swiss effective corporate rate of ~15%. That structure carries a specific capital-return constraint: a 35% Swiss withholding tax applies to dividends and repurchases unless funded from qualifying capital contribution reserves. Aptiv obtained a tax ruling confirming creation of a new material qualifying reserve after the prior reserves transferred with EDS, but concedes it "may be depleted over time." No common dividend is paid.

## Financial Analysis

### Reported results are better than the underlying business

| Continuing operations | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $3,274M | $3,199M | $6,306M | $6,186M |
| Operating income | $367M | $325M | $641M | $614M |
| Operating margin | 11.2% | 10.2% | 10.2% | 9.9% |
| Interest expense | $(62)M | $(92)M | $(146)M | $(185)M |
| Diluted EPS | $1.40 | $1.21 | $2.01 | $0.55 |
| Adjusted segment OI | $473M (14.4%) | $410M (12.8%) | $829M (13.1%) | $789M (12.8%) |

The revenue variance analysis in the 10-Q is the decisive disclosure. Q2's $75M increase decomposed into volume net of contractual price reductions +$37M, FX +$27M, and commodity pass-through +$11M. For H1, the $120M increase decomposed into volume net of price reductions **–$12M**, FX **+$115M**, and commodity +$17M. First-half organic volume was negative; essentially all reported growth was currency translation.

The 160bps of Q2 adjusted margin expansion decomposes similarly. Management cites favorable FX of +$35M (primarily Mexican peso) and $15M of lower SG&A, offset by $54M of contractual price reductions net of recoveries. The derivatives note shows $18M of pre-tax hedge gains reclassified into cost of sales in Q2 2026 versus $2M in Q2 2025, a $16M year-over-year tailwind worth roughly 49bps of sales. FX and hedge-gain timing therefore account for approximately $51M of the $63M increase in adjusted segment operating income. The residual operational contribution net of the $54M price headwind is genuine and management-controlled, but the repeatable portion of the headline margin gain is smaller than 160bps. The peso exposure is two-sided: a stronger peso adds to translated operating income while raising dollar labor cost in Aptiv's largest workforce geography, where statutory minimum wage rose 13% effective January 2026 after 12% in 2025 and 20% in 2024.

### The debt work was the most value-accretive action of the year

Aptiv's total debt carried at $5,354M at June 30 against a fair value of $4,493M, a 16.1% discount created by long-dated low-coupon paper ($1,500M at 3.10% due 2051; $920M at 4.15% due 2052). Using the ~$1,920M dividend received from Versigent, Aptiv retired $2,054M of notes in H1, including an April tender in which $1,446M of principal was extinguished for $1,377M of cash for a $55M GAAP gain. Quarterly interest expense fell from $92M to $62M, roughly $120M annualized pre-tax, about $0.41 per diluted share after tax at the 14% Q2 rate.

The $44M pre-tax Q2 gain on extinguishment equals ~$0.207 per diluted share, or ~$0.178 after tax. Ex-gain Q2 GAAP continuing EPS was approximately $1.22 against the reported $1.40, relevant when annualizing.

### Free cash flow is the open question

| H1, continuing operations | 2026 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $82M | $531M |
| Capital expenditures | $(278)M | $(267)M |
| Free cash flow (derived) | **–$196M** | **+$264M** |

A $460M swing, driven by working capital: receivables –$314M, inventories –$230M, other assets –$204M, with a net –$731M use from changes in operating assets and liabilities. On the balance sheet, AR rose from $1,910M to $2,434M (+27.4%) on ~2% revenue growth; inventories rose $1,789M to $2,038M.

At least three factors are mechanical or seasonal rather than demand-driven. First, Aptiv now sells to Versigent as a third party (approximately $201M in Q2 alone, or 6.1% of net sales) which at 60-day terms creates roughly $130-135M of newly external receivables with no December 31, 2025 counterpart. Second, December 31 balances are seasonally depressed given disclosed North American and European December shutdowns, so a June-versus-December comparison overstates deterioration. Third, the FY2025 10-K already stated inventories had "increased substantially from customary levels" at both December 31, 2025 and 2024 because of customer production volatility and cancellations, so the overhang predates H1 2026, though it worsened by $249M.

Derived metrics: DSO ~67.7 days against stated average terms of 60; inventory ~74.2 days. Elevated for a just-in-time operator, not distressed. We are not annualizing –$196M, but we also cannot exclude the alternative reading that inventory is building ahead of a China-driven volume decline, which would be consistent with the guidance cut. This is the single item that most needs re-underwriting at Q3.

### Balance sheet

Cash $761M (down from $1,575M), total debt $5,354M, net debt ~$4,593M (management-confirmed "$4.6 billion"). Liquidity of $3,271M including a $1,998M undrawn revolver to March 2030. Annualizing H1 adjusted EBITDA (adjusted segment OI $829M + $280M depreciation = $1,109M) gives ~$2,218M and net leverage of ~2.07x; a normalized FY figure of $2.05-2.15B implies ~2.1-2.2x against a 3.5x covenant. Comfortable.

Less comfortable: goodwill of $3,937M plus intangibles of $1,875M equal 66.4% of the $8,753M equity base, leaving tangible book of ~$14.17 per share against a $48.655 price. Net deferred tax assets of $1,447M sit in other long-term assets after a $294M valuation allowance was added in FY2025 on Pillar Two guidance. The $500M of 6.875% junior subordinated notes due 2054 permit interest deferral for up to 20 semi-annual periods, but any deferral bars all dividends and repurchases, a structural constraint on capital return that plain-vanilla peer comparisons miss.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the file, and it is where our score has moved most.

**Organic volume is negative.** H1 volume net of contractual price reductions was –$12M. FY2026 guidance against a derived FY2025 continuing-operations baseline of roughly $12.4-12.5B implies +1% to +3%, much of it currency.

**The declining half is the larger half.** Sensors and Compute at ~$1.36B per quarter is 42% of company revenue and shrank 1.6% in Q2 with gross margin down 50-80bps. The 10-K adds specific, non-boilerplate language: "certain of our OEM customers have recently announced delays in or changes to their software-defined vehicle investment strategies amidst reduced expectations for future consumer demand." Software and Services grew 10.9% but at $152M per quarter needs several years of compounding to matter. Restructuring is aimed at the weak segment: H1 charges were $12M in EC versus $28M in IS.

**Regional mix is adverse.** Q2 revenue by manufacturing location: North America $1,193M (+8.6%), EMEA $1,180M (–6.0%), Asia Pacific $863M (+6.7%). FY2025 global vehicle production rose 4% but only 1% on an Aptiv-weighted basis, with China +10% against North America –2% and Europe –1%. Growth is concentrated in the region where domestic Chinese OEMs are taking share from Aptiv's legacy Western customers; the 10-K names General Motors' 2025 China restructuring explicitly.

**Estimate revisions confirm the direction.** All four windows are negative: current quarter –16.9% (30d), current year –3.6%/–7.5%, next year –3.9%/–3.6%. Analysts cut Q3 hard ($1.60 to $1.33) while holding Q4 nearly flat ($1.66 to $1.63), which reads as treating the reset as timing rather than a structural reduction in run-rate earnings. If Q4 follows Q3 down, the current-year and next-year numbers have further to fall.

One inconsistency in the consensus data deserves flagging: H1 actual adjusted EPS of $3.34 ($1.71 + $1.63) plus Q3 and Q4 estimates of $1.33 and $1.63 sums to $6.30, against a stated current-year figure of $5.74. We therefore treat forward earnings as a range rather than a point.

**Unconfirmed upside optionality:** a reported $300M robotics and drone revenue target from the August 4 call appears in a single secondary source and is not disclosed in the 10-Q or 10-K. It is thematically consistent with the 30%-weighted "software and adjacent market revenue" compensation metric, but we assign it no value.

**Backlog does not exist in the conventional sense.** The 10-Q states plainly: "There are no contracts for production parts outstanding beyond one year. Aptiv does not enter into fixed long-term supply agreements." The only disclosed remaining performance obligation is ~$176M of software and services contracts with ~55% converting within twelve months, covering a product line that is 4.6% of revenue. Contract liabilities are $74M, down from $90M. Management's "bookings" figures are estimates of lifetime awarded business against which the 10-K carries a dedicated risk factor titled "We may not realize sales represented by awarded business."

Peer medians in Automotive Parts & Equipment are P/E 14.4x and yield 2.0% (ALV 14.4x/2.9%, LEA 11.5x/2.6%, BWA 34.9x/1.0%, GNTX 12.7x/2.0%, DORM 19.6x/nil).

The headline comparison of 7.4x forward versus a 14.4x peer median overstates the discount because the bases differ. Peer figures are trailing GAAP. On annualized H1 GAAP continuing earnings of $4.02, Aptiv trades at 12.1x, between Lear at 11.5x and Autoliv at 14.4x. Adjust further for the $0.18 after-tax debt-extinguishment gain in Q2 and for H2 seasonality (the 10-K discloses July/August European and July North American production reductions, December shutdowns, and reduced Q3 output for model-year launches) and the GAAP-equivalent multiple is closer to 13-14x, essentially in line with peers. The cheapness lives entirely in the adjusted numbers, which exclude ~$208M of annualized amortization (roughly $0.86 per share pre-tax) and restructuring. Aptiv also pays no dividend against a 2.0% peer median.

Where the discount is genuine is on enterprise value to EBITDA. At 6.8-7.2x normalized, Aptiv trades below both the auto-supplier group and, far more so, the interconnect franchises Engineered Components competes with. If EC's telecom/datacom mix shift takes hold and Sensors and Compute stabilizes, a sum-of-parts argument at 9-10x consolidated EBITDA is defensible and worth roughly $78-88 per share. That is the bull case, and it requires the segment that is currently shrinking to stop shrinking.

Two other valuation supports and one caveat. Share count already fell from 212.7M at year-end to 207.6M at July 31, and repurchases continued at $46M between July 1 and August 3. Motional carries at $1,121M ($222M common + $899M preferred), roughly 11% of market cap or $5.40 per share, with no requirement for further Aptiv funding after the April 2024 restructuring; a monetization would be a real catalyst, a markdown a real charge. Neither is observable.

**On analyst targets:** the range is $55 low, $78 high, $66.61 mean, $65.50 median. Every published target sits above the current price. We do not treat these as floors. The standard for that treatment is rising estimates and a cheap multiple; here estimates are falling in all four windows, hold ratings tripled from 1 to 3 in a month, and the targets predate or barely postdate the guidance cut. We expect the mean to migrate toward the low end.

## Competitive Landscape

Aptiv now plays two different games with divergent trajectories.

Engineered Components is improving. Adjusted operating margin rose 250bps to 17.6% and gross margin 180bps to 27.6%. It carries the company: $317M of the $473M Q2 adjusted operating income on 54.5% of segment revenue. Its margin still sits below best-in-class interconnect peers, and the OFP acquisition plus explicit "telecom and datacom" positioning are attempts to close that end-market mix gap. Joseph Massaro, the long-tenured former CFO, runs it as Vice Chair and President, a sensible allocation of internal credibility.

Intelligent Systems is deteriorating on the metrics that matter. Sensors and Compute is declining with falling gross margin, in the industry's most crowded competitive field, against Bosch, Denso, Valeo and Mobileye plus, per the 10-K, "new and disruptive entrants from outside the traditional automotive supply industry, particularly in countries such as China." Wind River is benchmarked against Nutanix and Red Hat, enterprise infrastructure vendors selling to buyers Aptiv does not know, and already absorbed a $648M goodwill impairment in Q3 2025.

Customer concentration is worsening on a like-for-like basis. The 10-K's 56% top-10 figure (pre-spin) and the 10-Q's 44% (continuing operations) are different scopes and must not be compared. On the comparable continuing-operations basis, top-10 concentration rose from ~40% in Q2 2025 to ~44% in Q2 2026. The end-market mix is shifting toward non-automotive, but the concentration data itself is moving the wrong way; mix shift alone does not establish diversification.

Structural supplier economics remain unhelpful: requirements contracts with no minimum purchase obligation, termination for convenience, annual price step-downs of 1-3% (the $54M Q2 / $87M H1 headwind), and, outside of copper, which departed with EDS, limited commodity pass-through. Aptiv retains a 35.3 million pound copper hedge (~$215M notional), but the 10-K states copper exposure "primarily affects our Electrical Distribution Systems segment," so this cost sensitivity is structurally reduced post-spin.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment tail (material, partially mitigated).** $3,937M of goodwill remains, most of it in Intelligent Systems, the segment whose largest product line is shrinking with compressing margin. In Q1 2026 the Wind River reporting unit was folded into the larger Intelligent Systems reporting unit, which mechanically tests Wind River's carrying value inside a unit containing the profitable Sensors and Compute business and reduces standalone impairment sensitivity. Aptiv disclosed step-one tests both before and after the realignment with no impairment at Q1 2026. The realignment is a defensible reflection of post-spin management structure; the reduced sensitivity is a consequence. The 10-K nonetheless warns "it is possible that we could incur additional charges in the future."

**China (the stated guidance driver).** Domestic Chinese OEMs taking share from Aptiv's legacy Western customer base is a structural, not cyclical, headwind. Aptiv-weighted production growth of +1% against global +4% quantifies the misalignment.

**Working capital / cash conversion.** Unresolved until Q3, as discussed.

**Spin-off tax status.** Aptiv did not seek an IRS ruling on Section 355 treatment, relying on non-binding tax opinions. If the distribution were held taxable, subsidiaries and US shareholders could face significant liabilities. Versigent indemnifies under the Tax Matters Agreement, but Versigent is a freshly levered wiring-harness company carrying ~$2.1B of new debt at 6.125-6.375% coupons, and is simultaneously a ~6% Aptiv customer (~$201M of Q2 revenue). Aptiv has no obligation on Versigent's debt; the exposure is counterparty and receivable, plus indemnity credit quality. Low probability, high severity.

**Deferred tax assets.** $1,447M net, following a $294M valuation allowance addition. Swiss DTA recoverability remains under monitoring.

**Mexico labor and tariffs.** Minimum wage +13% for 2026 with a possible 48-to-40 hour work week phase-in from January 2027, against a footprint the 10-K concedes is prompting "a strategic review of more cost-competitive jurisdictions." US tariffs of at least 10% on all-country imports since April 2025 were "not significant during 2025" but future impact "may be material" for a company whose North American supply runs through Mexico.

**Capital-return constraints.** The 35% Swiss withholding tax absent qualifying capital contribution reserves (a new reserve was confirmed by ruling but "may be depleted over time"), and the junior subordinated notes' deferral-linked prohibition on any dividend or repurchase.

**Other:** non-US pension underfunding of $128M ($423M including statutory retirement obligations), $76M warranty accrual with reasonably possible excess of zero to $40M, cybersecurity risk spanning safety-critical products, and ~64% of revenue generated outside the US.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.6% sits below 43.6% trailing realized volatility (a –4.0pp spread) at the 7th percentile of the current chain, and ATM IV collapsed from 50.0% on July 22 to 39.6% now: event risk around the print has been discharged rather than reset higher. The term structure is flat (39.6% near, 40.8% at six months), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly rather than concentrated in a near-term binary. Put/call open interest doubled from 0.37 to 0.71 as the price fell 16.7%, so protection was accumulated through the decline even as forward-looking volatility cheapened. The one-week tenor carries an elevated put-demand skew reading of 7.8%, while the one-, three- and six-month tenors do not. Short interest of 7.3% of float at 4.7 days to cover is moderate, neither crowded nor a source of squeeze fuel. The composite reading: no crisis is priced, options are not expensive for anyone wanting to express a view, and nothing in the structure contradicts a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $78 (+60%)

Engineered Components is a genuine interconnect franchise mispriced inside an auto-supplier wrapper. It already produces 67% of adjusted operating income at a 17.6% margin, expanding 250bps, with the OFP acquisition and telecom/datacom positioning shifting mix toward the end markets where Amphenol, TE and Molex earn double the multiple. Sensors and Compute stabilizes as regulation-driven radar and vision content resumes and OEM software-defined-vehicle programs restart; Software and Services compounds from 10.9% growth toward relevance. The $120M annualized interest saving is permanent, net leverage of 2.1x supports aggressive deployment of the $1,744M authorization at prices 21-26% below where it was executed in Q2, and share count falls 8-10%. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $6.50 at a 12x multiple, or 9x EV/EBITDA on $2.25B against a reduced share count. A Motional monetization or Hyundai buy-in adds up to $5.40 per share of carrying value. Three insiders committing $8.06M of personal capital at $47-50 is the most credible external validation available.

### Bear Case — $38 (–22%)

The separation revealed a good interconnect business attached to a decelerating sensors and compute business that holds most of the goodwill, which is why no re-rating has occurred despite adjusted EBITDA margin rising to 18.7% from 17.1%. Organic volume is already negative before China deteriorates further; the Q3 estimate cut of 16.9% is the first of several as analysts extend the reset into Q4 and FY2027. Roughly $51M of the $63M adjusted operating income gain was FX and hedge-gain timing that reverses if the peso and copper curves flatten, while $87M of annual price step-downs recur regardless. H1 free cash flow of –$196M proves partly structural rather than mechanical, forcing a choice between the buyback and the balance sheet. An Intelligent Systems impairment follows the Wind River precedent. On $5.40 of FY2027 adjusted EPS at 7x, or 6x EV/EBITDA on $1.95B, the equity is worth $35-38, and tangible book of $14.17 provides no floor.

### What we conclude

The distribution is genuinely two-sided. Probability-weighted at 20% bull / 50% base / 30% bear, expected return is approximately +14%, which is positive but does not clear the margin-of-safety bar for adding capital ahead of a print that could resolve either way. The prior three HOLD ratings rested on uncertainty about what the spin would reveal. That uncertainty is now resolved, and the answer is mixed: EC is better than expected, IS is worse. The overall score moves from 5.4 to 5.0 to reflect confirmed organic stagnation, negative revisions across all windows, and the guidance cut, partially offset by the insider cluster and the improved entry multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (hold_rationale: uncertain)

- **Base case: $57 (+17%).** 9.2x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $6.20 (consensus $6.53 haircut 5% for continued negative revisions), cross-checked at 7.5x EV/EBITDA on $2.15B less $4.6B net debt over ~205M shares = $56.
- **Bull case: $78 (+60%).** 12x on $6.50, or 9x EV/EBITDA on $2.25B over a reduced share count. Matches the high published target.
- **Bear case: $38 (–22%).** 7x on $5.40, cross-checked at 6x EV/EBITDA on $1.95B. Eighteen percent below the August low of $46.16, consistent with an IS impairment plus a deeper China reduction.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the Q3 2026 print (late October/early November) as the decisive checkpoint.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
1. Q3 operating cash flow turns positive with receivables and inventory growing slower than revenue — specifically, DSO back below 62 days and inventory days below 70.
2. Sensors and Compute returns to year-over-year growth, or Intelligent Systems adjusted operating margin recovers above 10.5% for two consecutive quarters.
3. FY2027 consensus stops falling — a flat-to-positive 30-day revision on the next-year line, which currently sits at –3.9%.
4. Price at or below $42, which at unchanged FY2027 consensus implies 6.4x forward adjusted earnings and ~6.1x EV/EBITDA.

**Upgrade to BUY** requires the above plus evidence that buyback pace has accelerated at these prices (Q3 repurchases materially above the $325M H1 run-rate at an average below $52) and confirmation that the Swiss qualifying capital contribution reserve supports that pace.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID if any of:**
1. FY2026 revenue guidance is cut again, or FY2027 consensus falls more than 8% from the current $6.53.
2. Q3 free cash flow from continuing operations is negative again, taking nine-month FCF below –$200M with no articulated working-capital recovery path.
3. An Intelligent Systems goodwill impairment is recorded, or the reporting-unit test discloses a fair-value cushion under 15%.
4. Sensors and Compute revenue decline accelerates beyond 4% year over year.
5. Buybacks are suspended for liquidity, covenant, or Swiss-reserve reasons.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings. Between now and then the monitorable items are Q4 estimate migration (currently anchored at $1.63 while Q3 was cut 16.9%), any 8-K disclosing further Motional or StradVision valuation events, and continued Form 4 activity, particularly whether the August buyers add.

## Conclusion

Aptiv is statistically inexpensive on enterprise value to EBITDA, roughly in line with peers on GAAP earnings, and carrying an unusually strong insider signal alongside a buyback authorization worth 17% of its market capitalization. Those are real supports, and they are why we do not rate the shares REDUCE after a 45% decline from the 52-week high.

They are not sufficient for a buy. Organic volume is negative, the segment representing 42% of revenue is shrinking with compressing gross margin and holding most of the goodwill, roughly 80% of the year-over-year adjusted operating income improvement traces to currency and hedge-gain timing, first-half free cash flow was negative by $196M, and consensus earnings estimates are falling in every forecast window. The company just reduced its own revenue outlook citing China, a headwind the 10-K describes as structural rather than cyclical. Cheapness in the absence of an earnings catalyst is the recurring failure mode in this industry, and Consumer Discretionary auto suppliers have been a reliably poor place to be early.

The next real information arrives with the Q3 report: whether working capital reverses, whether Sensors and Compute stabilizes, and whether the estimate cuts stop at Q3 or extend into FY2027. Existing holders have adequate compensation for waiting, with insiders alongside them. New capital should wait for that resolution or for a price near $42, where the forward multiple compresses to roughly 6.4x and the margin of safety does the work the fundamentals currently cannot.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the current-year consensus figure of $5.74 is internally inconsistent with H1 actuals plus Q3/Q4 estimates (implying ~$6.30), so the "8.5x current-year" valuation support is unreliable; and the 52-week high used in the Conclusion is $88.93 (a ~45% decline to $48.66 is correct), but note the peer-multiple parity conclusion rests on GAAP annualizations that are themselves sensitivities, not forecasts.
