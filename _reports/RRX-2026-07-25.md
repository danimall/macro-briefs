**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Regal Rexnord has completed a multi-year transformation (the Rexnord PMC merger in 2021, the Altra Industrial Motion acquisition in March 2023, and the divestiture of its industrial motors and generators business in April 2024), leaving a three-segment industrial company that makes products to power, transmit, and control motion. The operating story since the trough is constructive: operating margin recovered from 6.0% in 2023 to 11.5% in 2025, long-term debt fell from $6.4B to $4.76B over two years, and the highest-value segment (Automation & Motion Control) grew organic sales +12.1% in Q1 2026 on data-center and aerospace/defense demand.

The problem is price. The stock has risen roughly 38% over the trailing twelve months and 53% year-to-date 2026, re-rating to ~49x trailing and ~20x forward earnings on a thesis that is substantially a 2027 event. The company itself frames 2026 as a bridge year: the data-center order book ($735M booked in 2025, ~85% converting to revenue in 2027) does not meaningfully hit the P&L until next year, tariff pressure is not expected to reach margin-neutrality until end-2026, and Q1 2026 free cash flow turned negative as the receivables-securitization boost that flattered FY25 operating cash flow reversed. Layered on top is a humanoid-robotics narrative that is a genuine call option but does not appear as a disclosed revenue stream in any SEC filing.

We maintain HOLD, consistent with the prior four reports, but with an improving underlying trend acknowledged. Scores have moved from 4.8 (March) to 5.35 (current) as deleveraging, margin recovery, and backlog visibility all firmed. The reason we do not upgrade to BUY is valuation: the market is already paying for flawless 2027 execution, a leadership transition that hands the "prove-it" burden to a CEO unproven at RRX, and a refinancing schedule of high-coupon notes stretching to 2033. Estimate revisions are flat, not accelerating, so there is no positive earnings-momentum signal to override the stretched multiple. We would upgrade on either a pullback that restores asymmetry or hard evidence (Q2, then Q3) that data-center backlog is converting on schedule and free cash flow is normalizing toward the ~$650M full-year guide.

## Company Overview

Regal Rexnord is a Milwaukee-based industrial manufacturer with ~28,700 employees (roughly 20,000 outside the US). The business is largely purchase-order-driven with limited long-term volume commitments, which gives it inherent revenue variability. Three segments:

- **Industrial Powertrain Solutions (IPS)** — $2,594.1M FY25 sales (44% of total). Bearings, couplings, gearboxes, clutches, brakes, integrated powertrain sub-systems. The highest-margin segment (~42% gross margin, ~13% operating margin).
- **Automation & Motion Control (AMC)** — $1,689.8M (28%). Conveyor systems, precision/servo motors, controls, drives, linear actuators, aerospace components, and power-management products (E-Pods, automatic transfer switches, paralleling switchgear) that serve data centers. The highest-growth, highest-strategic-value segment and the engine of the current re-rating.
- **Power Efficiency Solutions (PES)** — $1,650.6M (28%). Fractional-to-5HP motors, blowers, and air-moving subsystems, primarily for residential and commercial HVAC. The most cyclical and weather-sensitive segment.

RRX is one of few US-based vertically integrated suppliers of the "motion control stack" (motors, gearing, actuators, controls, and switchgear) at a time when China dominates rare-earth and low-cost motion components. This scarcity in a domestic-supply-chain framing is the core of the bull thesis.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory:**

| Year | Revenue | Op. Income | Op. Margin | Net Income | Interest Exp. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $5,217.9M | $690.4M | 13.2% | $488.9M | $87.2M |
| 2023 | $6,250.7M | $377.1M | 6.0% | -$57.4M | $431.0M |
| 2024 | $6,033.8M | $630.0M | 10.4% | $196.2M | $399.7M |
| 2025 | $5,934.5M | $680.8M | 11.5% | $279.5M | $349.2M |

The FY25 revenue decline of 1.6% was entirely attributable to the industrial-motors divestiture (-2.8%); organic sales rose +0.8%. The underlying story is margin expansion and deleveraging rather than shrinkage. IPS gross margin rose to 42.1% on synergy capture, and PES operating margin jumped to 12.7% from 9.9% on restructuring savings. The 2023 net loss reflected Altra integration costs, a $57.3M goodwill impairment in industrial motors, and a spike in interest expense post-acquisition.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue $1,479.1M, +4.3% (organic +1.6%, FX +2.7%). AMC organic sales grew +12.1% on data-center and discrete-automation strength, with aerospace/defense orders +76% and medical +53% per company reporting. PES fell -10.3% organically on residential-HVAC weakness. EPS rose to $0.97 from $0.86, and interest expense fell to $80.5M from $90.2M on deleveraging.

**Cash flow — the key watch item.** FY25 operating cash flow was $990.8M, up $381M YoY, but roughly $372M was a one-time boost from selling receivables under a new $400M securitization facility, not organic improvement. Reported FCF was $893.1M. In Q1 2026, operating cash flow collapsed to $14.9M from $102.3M and free cash flow turned slightly negative, as the working-capital effect reversed and inventory built. Full-year FCF is guided to ~$650M. Investors must strip the securitization effect out to assess underlying cash generation; the trajectory from here to the August print is the single most important near-term financial signal.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$4.7B, D/E 2.03x, net leverage ~3.6x adjusted EBITDA. An Altman Z-Score of 1.7 flags the "distress zone," but this is a false positive: the reading is driven by ~$6.6B of acquisition goodwill and ~$3.4B of intangibles (together ~72% of $13.9B assets) depressing asset-productivity ratios, not by liquidity stress. The genuinely healthy signals are a 2.15x current ratio, full covenant compliance, and a $1.33B undrawn revolver. Deleveraging is real and deliberate: long-term debt fell $6.4B (2023) → $5.45B (2024) → $4.76B (2025), with $709M of net debt repayments in 2025. The company refinanced its $1.1B 6.05% 2026 notes in February 2026 via a new $850M term facility plus revolver. Remaining senior notes ($1.25B 6.05% due 2028, $1.1B 6.30% due 2030, $1.25B 6.40% due 2033) carry high coupons reflecting the 2023 rate environment, making refinancing a multi-year overhang rather than a near-term solvency issue.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend held flat at $0.35/quarter (~$93M/yr, ~0.65% yield). There were no buybacks in 2025 (versus $50M in 2024), with capital prioritized to debt reduction and $145M remaining on the authorization. This is the right choice given the leverage, but it means per-share support is thin at current levels.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three industrial themes: data-center power and thermal management, factory and discrete automation, and — optionally — humanoid robotics. AMC is the vehicle for the first two, delivering strong aerospace/defense and medical order momentum.

The data-center leg is the most concrete and filing-verifiable element. The Q1 10-Q discloses $735M of data-center orders booked in 2025, with ~85% of the $911.2M total remaining performance obligations scheduled to convert to revenue in 2027. Management has guided to roughly $900M of 2027 data-center revenue, up from ~$130M in 2025. If that converts, data center reaches a meaningful share of company revenue and materially lifts AMC's already-superior mix. Data-center demand has been confirmed across power and electrical-equipment suppliers, which argues the underlying capex wave is broad-based rather than a single-company story.

Two constraints temper the growth score. First, timing: the data-center ramp is a 2027 event, and 2026 is explicitly a bridge year. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $10.64, and estimate revisions are flat — current-quarter EPS was trimmed -3.5% over 90 days, current-year -0.4%, with no accelerating positive velocity in any window. That matters, because the strongest forward buy signal (>30% upward revision velocity) is absent here. Second, PES cyclicality: residential HVAC weakness partially offsets AMC's strength and keeps blended organic growth in the low single digits.

The humanoid-robotics angle is a narrative call option, not a fundamental. RRX's SEC filings do not quantify or prominently feature a robotics business. The claim that motion-control content could be 40-60% of a humanoid's bill of materials comes from an analyst thesis, not disclosed revenue. RRX is a potential component supplier to robotics OEMs, not a robotics product company. We assign this zero weight in base-case growth and treat it strictly as upside optionality.

## Valuation Assessment

At $212.74, RRX trades at ~49x trailing and ~20x forward FY26 consensus EPS of $10.64. The peer set in Electrical Components & Equipment shows wide dispersion — Acuity at 21.2x, NXT at 31.2x, Powell at 56.7x, Generac at 80.4x, Vicor at 99.7x — with a peer-median P/E of 56.7x. On a headline basis, RRX looks cheaper than the median peer, but that comparison is misleading given the heterogeneity of the group and the fact that several peers (Vicor, Generac) are themselves elevated on the same thematic optimism.

The relevant question is whether the current price offers asymmetric upside on current and forward fundamentals. It does not clearly. The forward multiple of ~20x is not stretched for a margin-expanding industrial, but it already discounts substantial 2027 execution — the data-center backlog conversion is priced in, not a hidden option. Estimate revisions are flat, so there is no consensus-catch-up dynamic to power a re-rating; the market has already done the re-rating (+53% YTD). For the estimate-revision-as-buy-signal logic to apply, estimates need to be rising while the stock lags, and here neither condition holds.

We are cautious about treating the ~49x trailing multiple as a reason to reward "cheapness" — the forward multiple is the correct lens — but we are equally cautious about crediting a growth premium the current data does not yet support. The 2026 bridge year, negative Q1 FCF, and flat revisions mean the stock is priced for a future that has to be delivered, with limited margin of safety if the 2027 ramp slips. Analyst targets ($240 low, $260 mean/median, $300 high) sit 13-41% above spot, but on a stock that has already run and where estimates are not rising, we read those targets as reflecting the same 2027 extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation. We score valuation below neutral: the price is defensible but offers no clear asymmetric edge today.

## Competitive Landscape

RRX's competitive trajectory is the strongest dimension of the story. As one of few US-based vertically integrated suppliers of the full motion-control stack, it has scarcity value in a domestic-supply-chain narrative precisely as China's dominance of rare-earth magnets and low-cost motion components becomes a strategic vulnerability for customers. AMC's organic growth, aerospace/defense order growth, and the data-center order book are direct evidence of share gains and mix improvement in high-value end markets.

The offsets are real. The 10-K acknowledges some competitors are larger and better-capitalized, and the business faces persistent OEM cost-down pressure and customer vendor-consolidation trends. PES competes in a commoditized, weather-sensitive residential-HVAC market where pricing power is limited. On balance, the trajectory is improving: the mix is shifting toward the highest-margin, highest-growth segment, and the moat in the integrated stack is widening, which supports an above-average competitive score even as the level of the moat is not fortress-grade.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and refinancing.** High-coupon debt with cash flow the company explicitly flags as potentially insufficient to service it; a rating downgrade triggers note step-ups of up to 2.00%. Deleveraging is progressing well, but leaves limited margin for a demand shock, and the 2028-2033 maturity wall is a multi-year overhang.

**Rare-earth magnet supply.** China's export controls on rare-earth magnets have created, in the company's words, "persistent challenges sourcing rare earth magnets, particularly for products serving the medical and defense markets" in AMC — a direct threat to the highest-growth, highest-margin segment.

**Tariffs and USMCA.** India reciprocal/penalty tariffs (Aug 2025) are flagged as a probable cost. Most Canada/Mexico imports are USMCA-compliant and exempt, but a renegotiation could remove that. A potential offset: the US Supreme Court ruled Feb 20, 2026 that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized, and RRX as importer-of-record may be entitled to refunds, though it has recorded no benefit given uncertainty.

**Goodwill impairment.** In the 2025 annual test, two of five quantitatively-tested reporting units had fair value exceeding carrying value by less than 10%, a thin cushion against a material non-cash impairment if forecasts weaken.

**Cash-flow quality and the bridge year.** The thesis requires FCF to recover to ~$650M and net leverage to fall below ~2.0x by end-2027. Slippage in the 2027 ePOD ramp would break the valuation.

**Leadership transition.** Louis Pinkham, CEO since 2019 and architect of the transformation, is being succeeded by Aamir Paul (effective no later than July 1, 2026, from a senior North America role at Schneider Electric per external reporting). The automation/electrification background fits the pivot, but the new CEO inherits peak valuation and high execution expectations and an unproven-at-RRX prove-it burden.

**Legacy product liability.** A subsidiary acquired in 2007 faces numerous claims that sub-fractional motors caused fires in third-party ventilation units; unquantified, and the company cannot rule out a material effect.

The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 59.2% > six-month 53.8%), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the August earnings binary. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$300).** Data-center backlog converts on schedule, lifting AMC mix and blended growth; IPS margins hold near 42% gross; PES stabilizes as HVAC cycles up; deleveraging drives net leverage below 2.0x, cutting interest expense and de-risking the balance sheet; the robotics call option begins to attract real revenue attribution; and the IEEPA tariff refund provides a one-time cash boost. On $13.49 next-year consensus EPS at a ~22x multiple, this supports the high-end analyst target.

**Bear case (~$150, roughly -30%).** The 2027 ePOD ramp slips, negative Q1 FCF proves the start of a working-capital drag rather than a one-quarter reversal, PES weakness deepens with a housing-led consumer slowdown, rare-earth supply constraints throttle AMC's best products, a sub-10%-cushion reporting unit tips into a material goodwill impairment, and the new CEO stumbles on integration or capital allocation. At ~15x a stressed ~$10 EPS, the stock derates toward $150.

**Base case (~$232, +9%).** The data-center backlog converts largely on schedule but the benefit is a 2027 event; 2026 delivers low-single-digit organic growth with FCF recovering toward the ~$650M guide; deleveraging continues; and the multiple holds near ~21x forward on ~$10.64 FY26 consensus EPS blending into rising 2027 estimates. Modest appreciation, driven by earnings growth rather than multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Given the HOLD recommendation:

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 results showing free cash flow normalizing toward the full-year guide (i.e., Q2 OCF meaningfully positive and working-capital reversal contained), confirming FY25 quality concerns are resolved.
- Concrete evidence the data-center backlog is converting on schedule for 2027 (management reaffirming or raising the 2027 revenue framing with shipment milestones), combined with positive estimate-revision velocity.
- A pullback toward the mid-$170s (~16x forward), which would restore asymmetric upside to the analyst target range.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any slippage in the 2027 ePOD ramp, or negative FCF persisting through Q2 without a credible one-time explanation.
- A goodwill impairment on the thin-cushion reporting units, or a credit-rating downgrade triggering note step-ups.
- PES organic decline accelerating beyond -10% alongside a deteriorating HVAC/housing backdrop, or AMC growth decelerating below high-single-digits on rare-earth supply constraints.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings on August 5, 2026 is the next natural checkpoint — it tests both backlog conversion commentary and cash-flow normalization. A second confirmation at Q3 would be required before an upgrade given the 2027-weighted nature of the thesis.

## Conclusion

Regal Rexnord is a successfully deleveraging, margin-expanding industrial with an improving competitive position in the motion-control stack and a real, filing-verified data-center backlog. The trajectory across our last four reviews has been positive — financial health, growth, and risk scores have each risen a full point since March — and the operating turnaround is credible. But the equity is priced for that improvement and then some. At ~20x forward earnings after a 53% year-to-date run, the market is paying today for a 2027 backlog conversion, discounting a bridge year with negative Q1 free cash flow, and crediting a robotics option that does not exist in the financials. Estimate revisions are flat, so there is no earnings-momentum catalyst to force further re-rating, and the options market is pricing a large, uncertain move into the August print.

We maintain **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale. The path to BUY is clear and monitorable: proof of cash-flow normalization and on-schedule data-center conversion, or a pullback that restores margin of safety. Neither is in hand today. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, held not from inertia but because the fundamentals and the price have converged: a good business at a full price, where the risk/reward is balanced pending the next two earnings prints.
