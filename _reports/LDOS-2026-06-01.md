**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Leidos is one of the largest U.S. government-services and defense-technology contractors, generating $17.17B of FY2025 revenue with 87% coming from the U.S. government and roughly half from the Department of War (formerly DoD) and the Intelligence Community. The business is high-quality on the metrics that matter for a govcon: $1.625B of FY2025 free cash flow (~9.5% FCF margin), operating cash flow above net income, ROE of 29.7%, and a $48.4B backlog with a strong Q1 FY2026 book-to-bill ($3.3B of bookings on $4.40B revenue). Management has run a disciplined capital-return program ($900M of buybacks plus $211M of dividends in FY2025), and total shareholder return has materially outpaced the peer index (186.3 vs. 127.3).

The stock has fallen about 38% from its November 2025 high of $205.77 to $127.80, and roughly 13% since our last review. This drawdown is large enough that the burden falls on the bull case to explain what sellers are missing, and the answer is that they are partly right. The decline coincides with genuine near-term fundamental softening, not just price noise: Q1 FY2026 diluted EPS declined to $2.56 from $2.77 a year earlier, current-quarter consensus has been cut 5.1% over 90 days, and the company added $1.7B of debt (to $6.3B total) to fund the $2.4B Entrust acquisition. Goodwill now sits above 50% of total assets. The longer-dated picture is intact (full-year guidance was raised, backlog is at record levels, and the portfolio is being cleaned up), but the near-term earnings trajectory is negative.

We rate LDOS a HOLD with a growth lean. At ~10-11x forward EPS and a ~10% FCF yield, the valuation offers real support and asymmetric optionality if the company executes through Entrust integration and the budget environment stabilizes. What prevents an upgrade to BUY is specific and checkable: negative near-term estimate momentum, a just-spiked leverage position against a 4.50x covenant ceiling, and the "Prioritizing the Warfighter in Defense Contracting" Executive Order, which can restrict the buyback/dividend model that underpins the per-share growth story.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1969 (the former SAIC) and headquartered in Reston, VA, Leidos employs roughly 47,000–50,000 people, 53% of whom hold U.S. security clearances. The company is a prime or subcontractor on long-cycle programs spanning defense, intelligence, health, civil, and homeland security. Effective fiscal 2026, Leidos realigned into four segments, with the following Q1 FY2026 mix:

- **Intelligence & Digital** (~$1.51B, ~34% of revenue, ~9.6% operating margin): cyber, digital modernization, mission software, AI/ML.
- **Health** (~$1.19B, ~27%, ~24% operating margin): VA disability exams (managed health services), health IT/EHR, FAA air traffic, life sciences. This is the disproportionate profit engine.
- **Homeland** (~$0.82B, ~19%, ~4% margin in Q1): air-traffic automation, security detection (30,000+ units in 120+ countries), CBP/TSA, and now commercial energy engineering. Margin was compressed in Q1 by Entrust deal costs.
- **Defense** (~$0.88B, ~20%, ~7% margin): hypersonics, maritime/undersea autonomy, radar/sensors, airborne ISR.

The contract mix is well-balanced: ~44% cost-reimbursement/FPIF, ~43% firm-fixed-price, ~13% T&M. Leidos competes against Booz Allen, CACI, SAIC, GDIT, Accenture Federal, Peraton, Parsons, and the primes (LMT, NOC, RTX, L3Harris). Its moat rests on scale, clearances, incumbency, and agency relationships.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** has compounded at a steady mid-single-digit pace: $14.40B (FY22) → $15.44B (FY23) → $16.66B (FY24) → $17.17B (FY25, +3%). Q1 FY2026 revenue of $4.40B was +3.7% YoY. This is a steady grower, not a high-growth story.

**Profitability** recovered strongly from a FY2023 trough. FY2023 net income was just $199M, distorted by a $596M non-cash goodwill impairment of the Security Enterprise Solutions (SES) unit; FY2025 net income to Leidos was $1.448B (+15% YoY), with operating margin expanding to 12.3% from 11.0%. ROE was 29.7% and ROA 10.8% — high returns, partly a function of an equity base kept lean by buybacks.

**Cash flow quality is the standout.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.75B and free cash flow $1.625B against modest capex (~$125M). OCF exceeding net income indicates clean, accrual-light earnings, a meaningful positive for a contractor with large EAC (estimate-at-completion) judgments. The earnings-surprise record supports forward confidence: three straight beats (Q3 FY25 +10.3%, Q4 +3.8%, Q1 FY26 +5.9%).

**The balance sheet changed materially in Q1 FY2026.** To fund the Entrust acquisition, debt rose from $4.6B to $6.3B (via $1.4B of new senior notes plus commercial paper), goodwill jumped to $8.09B, and cash fell to $457M. The company remains covenant-compliant (leverage covenant ≤3.75x, stepping up to 4.50x post-acquisition), and ~$1.6B of annual FCF supports a deleveraging path. The balance sheet is less resilient than a quarter ago, and the goodwill concentration (with a prior impairment on record) is a watch item. The Z-Score of 2.82 sits in the grey zone, and D/E of 1.74 reflects a deliberately leveraged, goodwill-heavy structure rather than distress.

**Capital allocation** has been disciplined and shareholder-friendly: $400M open-market buybacks plus a $500M accelerated repurchase in FY2025, $200M of buybacks in Q1 FY2026, and a dividend raised to $0.43/share from $0.40. Share count fell from ~131M to ~126M YoY.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is solid but unspectacular, and it has bifurcated. The structural/long-term picture is positive: Q1 net bookings of $3.3B (vs. $2.1B a year prior), total backlog of $48.4B, RPO of $19B, and management raising full-year revenue, EPS, and cash-flow guidance. The NorthStar 2030 strategy targets five pillars — space & maritime, energy infrastructure, digital modernization & cyber, mission software, and managed health services — and the Entrust acquisition is a deliberate bet on energy/grid modernization that diversifies revenue toward investor-owned utilities, away from pure federal spend.

The near-term picture is the offset. Q1 FY2026 EPS was pressured by Entrust deal costs, a $23M UK pension buy-out settlement loss, and higher interest expense. Consensus estimate revisions confirm soft momentum: the current-quarter estimate has been cut 4.3% over 30 days, the full-year estimate is roughly flat (+0.1% over 90 days at $12.34), and only the next-year estimate has firmed (+2.0% to $13.20). This is the opposite of the high-velocity upward revision pattern that signals an earnings inflection. The OBBBA tax benefit (~$91M lower FY2026 cash taxes) and the SES divestiture into a JV provide tailwinds, but they do not offset the near-term cost and interest drag visible in current-quarter estimate cuts.

## Valuation Assessment

At $127.80, LDOS trades at ~11.5x trailing EPS ($11.14) and ~10.4x current-year consensus EPS ($12.34), falling to ~9.7x next-year EPS ($13.20). FCF yield is ~10% ($1.625B against a ~$16.3B market cap).

The peer table requires care because the cohort is heterogeneous:

| Ticker | P/E | Comparability |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| RBA | 48.3 | Auctioneer — not comparable |
| AMTM | 38.6 | Different mix/profile |
| CACI | 21.1 | Pure govcon, higher growth |
| SAIC | 13.2 | Closest pure-govcon comp |
| KBR | 9.7 | Closest pure-govcon comp |
| **LDOS** | **~10-11x fwd** | — |

Against the headline peer median of 21.1x, LDOS looks deeply discounted. Against its truly comparable pure-govcon peers — SAIC at 13.2x and KBR at 9.7x — Leidos is fair-to-slightly-cheap, not a screaming bargain. The discount to SAIC is partly justified by SAIC's cleaner balance sheet and the absence of LDOS's just-incurred leverage and EO overhang.

The valuation offers genuine support: a ~10% FCF yield on a mid-single-digit grower with a record backlog is not a value trap. But the cheapness is not asymmetric upside in isolation, because the mechanism that converts that FCF into per-share value — buybacks and dividends — is precisely what the "Warfighter" Executive Order targets. The market is pricing real risk, not mispricing the business. We score valuation 6.8: above neutral on absolute cheapness and FCF support, but capped by the overhang and the lack of an upward-revision catalyst.

Analyst consensus targets (low $160, mean $186, high $225) sit 25-75% above the current price, but with current-quarter estimates being cut and the stock continuing to fall, these targets are lagging on the downside and should be treated as stale rather than as a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

Leidos's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving, with a clear concentration of profit in the Health segment (~27% of revenue at ~24% margins, well above the 4-10% margins in other segments). The durability of VA managed-health-services write-ups is the key swing factor for consolidated profitability and a concentration to monitor.

Management is conducting sensible portfolio surgery: acquiring an energy-engineering platform (Entrust) while divesting the chronically weak SES security-detection unit into a JV with Analogic (an Altaris portfolio company), retaining 41.5%. This removes a recurring impairment risk and reshapes the former Commercial & International segment constructively.

The sector faces structural pressure: the shift toward multiple-award IDIQ vehicles, GSA schedules, and OTAs increases competition and compresses pricing, while bid protests delay awards. The FAR overhaul (EO 14275) could expand commercial/non-traditional competition during the transition. Offsetting this, the administration has not signaled cuts to defense or homeland security, and Leidos is positioned in priority areas (hypersonics, space, maritime autonomy, cyber, AI, health IT).

## Risk Assessment

This is the dimension that most constrains the rating, and several risks are specific rather than sector boilerplate:

1. **The "Warfighter" Executive Order (the differentiated risk).** Future DoW contracts, including renewals, are expected to include provisions that can restrict stock buybacks and dividends during periods of "underperformance," and tie executive comp to on-time delivery and domestic-investment criteria. If the Secretary of War deems Leidos underperforming, the board must submit a 15-day remediation plan or face enforcement. This directly threatens the capital-return model that has driven Leidos's peer-beating TSR, and that model is what makes the cheap multiple valuable to equity holders.

2. **Negative near-term earnings momentum.** A net unfavorable $9M change in contract estimates in Q1 (versus +$30M favorable a year prior), with write-downs hitting Homeland and Defense margins, is a reminder that EAC adjustments swing quarterly results.

3. **Just-spiked leverage.** The post-Entrust debt and goodwill increase sits against a 4.50x covenant ceiling, with cash down to $457M. The deleveraging path exists but the cushion is thinner.

4. **Government concentration and shutdown risk.** 87% U.S.-government revenue; a DHS shutdown that began ~Feb 2026 was resolved only April 30, 2026, directly exposing the Homeland segment. Continuing resolutions limit new-program starts.

5. **DOJ antitrust criminal investigation.** A grand jury subpoena (open since Aug 2022) relates to three 2021-22 Intelligence Group procurements. Outcome and penalties are not estimable; debarment is a low-probability, high-severity tail.

6. **Goodwill impairment risk.** Goodwill exceeds 50% of assets post-Entrust; the SES unit has already taken a $596M impairment.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in backwardation (near-term ATM IV 39.6% vs. long-term 35.0%), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress rather than complacency. IV is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility (39.6% vs. 30.1%, a 9.6-point spread), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.8%. Put/call volume is elevated (3.17 near-term, and 8.50 at the 45-day tenor), though open-interest skew is balanced-to-call-heavy (P/C OI 0.54), and the recent collapse in the P/C OI ratio (1.54 → 0.54) suggests some bearish positioning has unwound. The market is hedging near-term risk into a window that likely includes budget/EO clarity and the next earnings print, consistent with our HOLD and with the near-term estimate cuts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (toward ~$185-225)

A high-quality govcon with ~10% FCF yield re-rates as near-term costs anniversary. The drivers: Entrust deal costs and the one-time UK pension charge roll off, restoring EPS growth; the SES JV closes in 2H FY2026, removing impairment risk and lifting blended margins; the energy-infrastructure diversification (Entrust) opens a higher-multiple commercial revenue stream; the backlog converts at a healthy book-to-bill; and the budget environment stabilizes. If the Warfighter EO proves less restrictive in practice and buybacks continue compounding per-share metrics, a re-rating toward 14-15x on ~$13.20+ next-year EPS supports $185-200, with $225 if the energy pivot earns a growth premium. This aligns with the existing analyst high.

### Bear Case (toward ~$105-115)

Near-term estimate cuts continue and the multiple stays compressed at the bottom of the govcon range. The Warfighter EO meaningfully restricts buybacks/dividends, removing the per-share-growth lever and forcing a de-rate to a pure-services multiple. Budget dysfunction (further CRs or shutdowns) defers awards; Homeland and Defense EAC write-downs recur; the DOJ matter escalates; or leverage pressures capital returns as the company prioritizes deleveraging. At ~9x on a flat-to-down ~$12 EPS, the stock trades to roughly $108-115, about 10-15% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean). Reassessment horizon: 6-12 months.**

This is a growth-oriented hold: there is a specific 12-month thesis for appreciation (multiple re-rating + backlog conversion + Entrust accretion + SES cleanup), but near-term momentum is negative and the EO overhang is unresolved, which is why it does not yet warrant a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-quarter and full-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (e.g., +5% or more over a 90-day window), confirming the near-term earnings trough is past.
- Resolution or clear de-risking of the "Warfighter" EO such that the buyback/dividend program is confirmed unconstrained on the DoW book.
- Leverage falls back toward ~3.5x and the SES JV closes on schedule, restoring balance-sheet cushion and lifting blended margins.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut, or two consecutive quarters of negative contract-estimate adjustments materially hitting margins.
- The DOJ antitrust matter escalates toward charges or debarment exposure.
- Leverage breaches toward the 4.50x covenant ceiling, or capital returns are formally suspended/restricted under the EO.

**Next natural reassessment point:** the Q2 FY2026 earnings release, which will show whether near-term estimate cuts stabilize and how Entrust integration and deleveraging are progressing.

## Conclusion

Leidos is a fundamentally sound, cash-generative government-services franchise trading at a reasonable ~10-11x forward EPS with a record backlog and a disciplined capital-allocation history. The ~38% drawdown from the high has improved the risk/reward, and the valuation offers genuine downside support. This is not a clean inflection setup: near-term EPS declined YoY, current-quarter consensus is being cut, leverage just spiked against a tightening covenant ceiling, and the "Warfighter" Executive Order directly threatens the buyback/dividend mechanism that converts the company's strong FCF into per-share value. The market is pricing real, specific risks rather than mispricing the business.

The combination of a fair-but-not-asymmetric multiple, negative near-term earnings momentum, and an unresolved policy overhang on the capital-return model holds us at HOLD. We would upgrade on positive estimate revisions and clarity on the EO; we would downgrade on a guidance cut, recurring contract write-downs, or escalation of the DOJ matter. The asymmetry is not yet compelling enough to step in front of the near-term softening.
