## Executive Summary

Duolingo's income statement and its demand curve are now telling different stories, and the gap is wide enough to drive the recommendation. Q2 FY2026 reported revenue of $298.454M grew 18.3%, but total bookings of $289.054M grew only 7.8% reported and **6% in constant currency** — the first quarter in the company's public history in which bookings came in *below* revenue. Because subscription revenue is recognized ratably, reported growth is the amortized residue of contracts written in 2025. The deferred-revenue bridge confirms the handoff: deferred revenue rose $8.897M in H1 2026 versus $58.293M in H1 2025, removing roughly $49.4M of prior-year cash tailwind. Constant-currency bookings growth has fallen approximately 26 points in four quarters, from ~32% for FY2025 to 6% in Q2 2026.

Management's guidance ratifies the convergence rather than disputing it. FY2026 guidance of ~11% bookings growth and ~16% revenue growth implies H2 revenue of $613.2M against $554.6M, or **+10.6%**, with a quarterly path of +26.5% (Q1) → +18.3% (Q2) → ~+12% (Q3E) → ~+9% (Q4E). The raised 26.5% adjusted EBITDA margin target still represents roughly 300bp of contraction from FY2025's 29.5%, and the "closer to 70%" full-year gross margin guide implies second-half gross margin near 67.6% against 72.6% in Q2 — a ~500bp step-down as AI inference costs scale with Video Call and related feature rollouts. Most consequentially, the 11% bookings guide requires H2 bookings of $688.4M; with Q3 guided at ~$307M, Q4 is implied at roughly $381M, a ~24% sequential step-up versus roughly 10% in the prior year.

The offsetting facts are real and keep this out of AVOID territory. Duolingo carries $1,416.6M of cash and investments against no borrowings — $30.32 per share, or 23% of the price. H1 free cash flow was $226.4M. DAUs grew 23% to 58.7M with roughly 43M users holding week-plus streaks and 15M holding year-plus streaks at year-end 2025, a habit asset that a chat interface does not obviously replicate. The board authorized $400M of repurchase capacity in February 2026 and executed it with visible price sensitivity, buying 5.5x more shares in April at $97.81 than in June at $112.27. But at $132.83 the stock trades above the consensus mean target of $124.79 and median of $125, with 18 of 24 covering analysts at Hold, next-year estimates cut 3.0% over 90 days, and a new strong-sell rating appearing this month. The asymmetry has inverted: our base case is roughly 4% above spot against a bear case 32% below. We downgrade to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Duolingo operates a freemium mobile learning platform built around 250-plus language courses delivered to more than 130 million monthly users, with Math, Music, and Chess integrated into the same app. Monetization runs through Super Duolingo (the core ad-free subscription with a family plan), Duolingo Max (a higher-priced AI-feature tier available to only "a portion" of the user base per the 10-K), advertising, in-app purchases, and the Duolingo English Test (DET). The company employed more than 900 people including over 430 engineers at year-end 2025 and is headquartered in Pittsburgh.

The stated moat rests on brand-driven organic acquisition, data scale (nearly 2 billion exercises daily feeding hundreds of quarterly A/B tests), and habit lock-in through streaks. Two elements deserve skepticism. First, intellectual property provides no barrier: the 10-K discloses exactly two patent applications, both narrowly directed at DET question generation. Second, the Competition section names no competitor at all, framing the contest as one against inertia — defensible given share, but it leaves the filing without a stated view on the AI-native threat that dominates the equity debate.

The DET is the most structurally defensible asset in the company: accepted by 6,100-plus programs including every Ivy League school, MIT, Stanford, and 24 of the top 25 U.S. undergraduate programs by international enrollment. It is also 4.0% of FY2025 revenue, flat in Q2 2026 and down 3% in H1. The asset most resistant to AI substitution contributes nothing to growth; 86.5% of Q2 revenue comes from the consumer subscription most exposed to free AI alternatives.

## Financial Analysis

### The trailing figures are unusable without adjustment

FY2025 revenue reached $1,037.6M (+38.7%) with operating income of $135.6M (13.1% margin) and adjusted EBITDA of $305.9M (29.5%). Reported net income of $414.1M, however, includes a **$231.7M tax benefit** driven by a one-time Q3 2025 release of the deferred tax asset valuation allowance worth $256.7M net. Pretax income was $182.4M; a notional 25% rate yields normalized net income near $137M. The provided trailing P/E of 16.23 on EPS of $8.57 therefore reflects a tax windfall, not earnings power — the $5.95 EPS print in the September 2025 quarter is the tell. Any screen using reported FY2025 ROE (30.7%), ROA (20.8%), or net margin (39.9%) overstates the business by roughly threefold.

The balance-sheet conclusions survive the distortion. At 6/30/2026: cash $1,180.9M, short-term investments $133.0M, long-term investments $102.7M for $1,416.6M total, no disclosed borrowings, total liabilities $664.2M of which $505.1M is deferred revenue, equity $1,409.7M, current ratio 2.61.

Three items require adjustment before comparing periods. First, GAAP operating income was flattered by roughly **$10.9M** from a Type III modification to the final two Founder Award tranches held by one founder, which reversed $8.217M of previously recognized stock compensation and produced a net SBC *benefit* of $6.982M against $3.963M of expense a year prior. This explains the otherwise contradictory pattern of GAAP operating income rising 1.7% while adjusted EBITDA fell 1.7% — because adjusted EBITDA adds SBC back, a negative addback neutralizes the benefit. On a like-for-like basis Q2 operating income would have been approximately $10.9M lower. Second, stripping that line out, H1 SBC rose from roughly $57.5M to roughly $78.6M, **+37% against revenue +22%**, with $412.0M of unrecognized RSU cost to be expensed over about three years (~$137M/year). Third, the effective tax rate moved from 3.6% to 26.9%, which accounts for most of the EPS decline.

### Cash flow: strong headline, three quality offsets

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $239.0M (+21.8%) and free cash flow $226.4M (+19.6%) on $12.6M of capex. Q2 alone produced $78.6M of FCF versus $86.3M, **down 8.9%**. Three adjustments matter:

1. Accounts receivable fell from $162.8M to $131.0M, contributing **+$31.8M** to H1 OCF. A falling receivable from app-store processors is partly a symptom of slowing bookings, not a durable cash source.
2. The deferred-revenue engine has stalled (+$8.9M vs +$58.3M).
3. Cash taxes are near zero and about to become real: $0.768M paid in H1 2026 versus $11.7M a year prior, while $21.3M of deferred tax expense was recognized and the net DTA declined from $227.3M to $206.0M. The GAAP rate is already 24-27%; as the DTA depletes, cash taxes converge toward it. On roughly $190M of pretax income that is $45-50M of eventual annual cash-tax drag not present in today's FCF.

Normalizing: H1 FCF less the AR release, annualized, gives roughly **$389M**. Charging full-year stock compensation of ~$140-150M against that yields owner earnings near **$240-250M**, falling toward $200M as cash taxes normalize.

## Growth Analysis

The engagement metrics are improving and the monetization metrics are not. That divergence is the whole analysis.

**Engagement.** DAU growth went 52.7M/+30% (Q4 2025) → 57.0M/+21% (Q1 2026) → 58.7M/+23% (Q2 2026). Sequential Q1→Q2 improved to +3.0% from +1.3% a year prior. Management expects DAU growth above 20% for the balance of the year and has reiterated a 100 million DAU target for 2028. However, Q2 DAU benefited from a **one-time "Streak Revival" campaign** that reactivated millions of dormant users, and management explicitly said future growth must come from retention and learning outcomes rather than promotions. MAU growth of 10% is the binding long-run constraint: DAU/MAU has already climbed to 41.7% from 37.2%, and DAU cannot outgrow MAU indefinitely.

**Monetization.** Paid subscribers went 12.2M (12/31/25) → 12.7M (6/30/26), **+0.5M net adds in H1 2026 against approximately +1.37M in H1 2025** (using the 10-K's disclosed +28% growth to derive the prior base). Incremental subscribers per incremental DAU fell to roughly **8.3% from 19.0%**. Subscription bookings per paid subscriber (period-end proxy) fell 5.5% to $19.71; revenue per DAU fell 3.9% to $5.08. These are crude period-end calculations without cohort data, and conversion lags — if Streak Revival cohorts convert on a normal curve the ratio partially reverses. But the direction is corroborated by the bookings line itself, which does not depend on the derivation.

**Non-subscription lines are shrinking in aggregate.** Advertising +2%, DET ~0% (H1 –3%), in-app purchases –23% (H1 –17%). Together $39.163M versus $41.081M, **–4.7%** — while DAUs grew 23%. Advertising revenue per DAU fell 17%, from $0.432 to $0.359. Duolingo added 11 million daily users and extracted less total advertising and transactional revenue from a larger base than a year ago.

**Rule-of-40 framing** captures the debate cleanly: on guided revenue growth, 16% + 26.5% = 42.5 (passes); on guided bookings growth, 11% + 26.5% = 37.5 (fails); on Q2 constant-currency bookings, 6% + 26.5% = 32.5 (fails clearly). Which denominator you use determines whether this is a decelerating compounder or a stalling one.

**Estimate revisions are negative**, which matters for how much weight to place on consensus as a floor. Next-quarter estimates are down 3.4% over 30 days and 4.0% over 90; next-year estimates down 2.1% and 3.0%. The powerful upward-revision signal that justifies looking through elevated multiples on inflection stocks is absent here — the revisions run the other way.

The dilution is not hypothetical: Q2 diluted weighted-average shares were 50.031M against 46.695M basic, and 3,453 thousand of awards remain outstanding (7.39% of existing shares).

The 12.3x EV/normalized-FCF figure is the strongest bull argument and deserves fair treatment. It is also the least durable: it excludes stock compensation entirely, benefits from a near-zero cash tax rate that will converge upward as the $206M DTA depletes, and rests on a deferred-revenue balance that has stopped growing.

On the >30% drawdown question — the stock is 64% below its $371.63 high and roughly 60% below where it traded a year ago — the burden is on the bull to identify what sellers are missing. We do not think they are missing much. The sellers are pricing a genuine monetization break visible in bookings, subscriber net adds, ARPDAU, and every non-subscription revenue line, plus the possibility that free frontier-model tutors and translation wearables compress the category. The company itself disclosed in its 10-K that an April 2025 AI memo "may have contributed to unfavorable publicity, adverse impacts on the Company's brand and social media presence, and a deceleration in user growth." That is an issuer linking a management communication to a growth deceleration in a risk factor, for a business whose Item 1 attributes growth primarily to word-of-mouth.

The prior-appreciation point cuts against the stock here rather than for it. Since our July 23 review the shares are up roughly 10% (from $120.91) while the Q2 print delivered the first bookings-below-revenue quarter, a Q3 revenue guide of ~$302M below the ~$304M consensus, and a guided second-half margin step-down. Higher price on modestly worse fundamentals is a narrower margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

AI is simultaneously the dominant bear narrative and a measurable near-term margin tailwind. Q2 gross margin held at 72.6% specifically because per-unit third-party AI costs fell, and management is routing non-frontier workloads to open-source models. Duolingo is a customer of external model providers and cloud infrastructure, not a model developer, so commoditization of inference lowers its cost base. That tailwind appears to be reversing within the guidance window: the implied H2 gross margin of ~67.6% is consistent with inference costs scaling as Video Call reaches a much larger base.

The threat case has three distinct forms per the 10-K: generative AI lowering entry barriers such that a new entrant achieves rapid scale; thousands of free language apps; and real-time translation wearables reducing interest in language learning altogether. The third is demand destruction against which product execution offers no defense. With two narrow patent applications, there is no legal barrier.

The counter-case is behavioral rather than technological. What Duolingo sells is habit: roughly 43M DAUs with week-plus streaks and 15M with year-plus streaks at year-end 2025. Nobody maintains a 400-day streak with a chatbot. This is real, and it is why DAU growth remains above 20% while bookings decelerate to 6%. The problem is that habit without conversion is a cost center — advertising costs rose 43.5% and sales and marketing 35% in Q2 to acquire users whose marginal propensity to pay is roughly half the prior cohort's.

Where the competitive position has clearly deteriorated is monetization breadth. Every line that would demonstrate a widening franchise — advertising, DET, in-app purchases — is flat or shrinking, leaving the company more concentrated in the single revenue stream most exposed to substitution than it was a year ago.

## Risk Assessment

**Platform concentration.** Q2 2026 revenue processed: Apple 59.6%, Google 23.2%, Stripe 11.9% — 94.7% through three payment and distribution counterparties, with agreements terminable on 30 days' notice without cause and take rates of 15-30%. Receivables concentration is higher still (Apple 65.5%). Apple plus Google fell only from 85.5% to 82.8% year over year; this is not diversification. To be precise: these are distribution and payment counterparties that collect from and remit on behalf of millions of individual consumer customers, not customers themselves.

**Guidance risk at Q4.** The FY26 bookings guide requires H2 bookings of $688.4M against $618.8M. With Q3 guided at ~$307M, Q4 is implied near $381M, a ~24% sequential step-up versus roughly 10% in the prior year. Duolingo's holiday and New Year promotional seasonality is genuinely pronounced, and management may have visibility into pricing or plan-mix actions not yet disclosed, so this is not automatically a warning. But it is the single most consequential unverified assumption in the story, and a miss resets the entire growth framing.

**Infrastructure.** The AWS us-east-1 outage on October 20, 2025 took Duolingo offline for more than 12 hours. For a product whose retention mechanic is a daily streak, outages destroy the precise variable management is paying employees to grow.

**Regulatory.** EU AI Act substantive obligations were scheduled from August 2, 2026 (a proposed extension to December 2027 was not final at the 10-K date), with fines up to 7% of worldwide turnover. There is also an office in China with prior temporary app-availability disruptions, and no FX hedging — relevant given FX contributed roughly 2 points of tailwind to reported Q2 bookings.

**Structural monetization ceiling.** The 10-K states as policy that learning content will not be placed behind a paywall: "Anyone can download the Duolingo App... and complete any and every course without paying." This forecloses the most obvious lever for repairing conversion.

**Governance.** Class B carries 20 votes per share; directors, officers, and 5% holders held 76.3% of voting power at 12/31/2025. Class A holders have negligible governance leverage.

**Legal.** A ~$5M accrual in Q2 2026 for proposed legal settlement charges, with the remainder expected to be insurance-funded and characterized as immaterial; G&A legal and accounting fees rose $5.4M in the quarter.

**Short interest.** 20.7% of float short, 7.2 days to cover. This is two-sided: it validates that sophisticated capital shares the bearish view, and it creates real squeeze risk on any upside surprise. It is the primary argument against a more aggressive negative rating.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 54.6% sits 12.2 points below one-year realized volatility of 66.8%, at the 25th percentile of the current chain, with contango into 61.9% at six months and a call-heavy skew of –8.5%. Put/call open interest fell from 1.61 to 0.94 since July 23 as the price rose 7.7% — bearish positioning was unwound into strength. The structure reads as near-term complacency following the resolution of the August 5 earnings event, with longer-dated tenors still pricing substantial uncertainty (14.4% implied move by late September, 33% by January). The September 25 tenor carries an unusually high put/call volume ratio of 5.25, worth monitoring as possible positioning ahead of the Q3 print. For holders who want to retain exposure while de-risking, cheap near-dated volatility makes protective puts or stock-replacement structures efficient rather than selling outright. The options market is not corroborating the fundamental caution at the near end, which is a divergence we flag rather than resolve.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $195 (+46.8%)

Q2's DAU acceleration marks the trough in the demand cycle, and bookings follow with a two-to-three-quarter lag. The Streak Revival cohorts retain and convert on a normal curve, restoring incremental conversion toward prior levels. Duolingo Max expands from "a portion" of the base toward general availability, lifting ARPU; Video Call drives Max attach; the DET recovers as international-student policy normalizes. Bookings reaccelerate to 15%+ in 2027 on FY27 adjusted EBITDA near $400M; an 18x EV/EBITDA multiple plus ~$1.7B of net cash produces roughly $187, and multiple expansion on restored growth carries it to $195. The 20.7% short base amplifies the move. Supporting evidence: four consecutive EPS beats, an EBITDA margin guide *raised* mid-year, gross margin resilience from AI cost deflation, opportunistic buyback execution with $329.7M remaining, and a director purchasing 5,000 shares at $99.76 in March.

### Bear Case — $90 (–32.2%)

Constant-currency bookings growth of 6% is the run rate, not the trough. Subscriber net adds continue at half the prior pace; ARPU declines as promotional pricing and shorter-duration plans are used to defend volume; the non-subscription lines keep shrinking; H2 gross margin compresses ~500bp on scaling inference costs as guided; the Q4 bookings step-up embedded in guidance is missed. FY27 adjusted EBITDA lands near $300M and the multiple compresses to 9x for a mid-single-digit grower with 10%+ of revenue in stock compensation. That gives roughly $88 per share, near the April low of $87.89 and the low analyst target of $80. The AI-native competitive threat and translation-wearable demand destruction are the tail that makes recovery from that level slow rather than sharp.

### Our Base Case — $138 (+3.9%)

Bookings stabilize at 9-11% as engagement gradually converts, FY27 revenue reaches roughly $1.32B with adjusted EBITDA near $345M at a 26% margin. A 14.5x EV/EBITDA multiple gives EV of $4.99B; adding net cash grown to roughly $1.60B and dividing by ~47.5M shares yields $138. Probability-weighting bear 40% / base 37% / bull 23% produces an expected return of approximately **–0.7%** — essentially zero against realized volatility near 67%. A fair risk-adjusted return on an asset with this volatility profile would require materially more than that.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base $138 (+3.9%), bull $195 (+46.8%), bear $90 (–32.2%).

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the Q3 print in early November. The stock has recovered roughly 10% from its post-earnings low and trades above the consensus mean target of $124.79 with estimates falling — this is the better half of the range implied by our scenarios. For holders retaining core exposure, size it to no more than a token position and use the cheap near-dated implied volatility (54.6% versus 66.8% realized) to buy downside protection through the Q3 report rather than selling the entire position. Do not add on weakness above $110.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD.** (1) Constant-currency total bookings growth of 9% or better in Q3 2026, confirming the 6% print was a trough rather than a run rate. (2) Paid subscriber net additions of 0.9M or more in H2 2026 (versus 0.5M in H1), evidencing conversion of the DAU base. (3) A price of $110-115 or below on unchanged guidance, which restores a 25%+ margin of safety to our base case and coincides with the zone where the company itself bought stock aggressively.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE.** Aggregate non-subscription revenue (advertising + DET + in-app purchases) returning to growth, combined with reaffirmed 26.5%+ adjusted EBITDA margins and reported gross margin holding above 70% in H2 — that combination would indicate the monetization break is repairing rather than widening.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) Q4 2026 bookings materially below the ~$381M implied by the FY26 guide, which would break the guidance framework and put the bear case in play. (2) Constant-currency bookings growth falling below 3%. (3) Any material change to the Apple or Google distribution terms, or a take-rate increase. (4) SBC continuing to grow faster than revenue into FY2027 alongside decelerating bookings — the combination converts a cash-generative business into one where equity holders fund growth they do not receive.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 FY2026 report, expected early November 2026, is the decisive checkpoint: it tests the ~+12% revenue guide, delivers the first clean read on constant-currency bookings without Streak Revival distortion, and reveals whether the Q4 bookings hurdle remains credible. A secondary checkpoint is any 8-K disclosing changes to the $400M repurchase program's pace — accelerated buying below $110 would signal management shares our view on where value sits.

## Conclusion

Duolingo remains a high-quality asset with a fortress balance sheet, genuine habit-based engagement, and a product organization that has repeatedly beaten quarterly estimates. Those attributes justify continued attention and argue against shorting. They do not justify owning the shares at $132.83, where the metric management is compensated on — bookings — is growing 6% in constant currency, subscriber net additions have fallen roughly 63%, every non-subscription revenue line is flat or shrinking, the guided second half embeds a ~24% sequential Q4 bookings step-up against ~10% in the prior year, and consensus estimates are drifting down while the price has risen 10% since our last review.

The scoring trajectory (6.9 → 6.5 → 6.6 → 6.1 → 6.0, now 5.3) has been driven by competitive position and growth prospects deteriorating faster than financial health has improved. The Q2 print did not arrest that pattern; it documented it in the bookings line and in management's own guidance. With a base case only 4% above spot against 32% downside to the bear case and near-zero probability-weighted return, the appropriate action is to reduce exposure and wait for either a materially lower price near $110-115 or hard evidence — Q3 constant-currency bookings above 9% and H2 net subscriber adds above 0.9M — that engagement is converting to revenue. **REDUCE.**
