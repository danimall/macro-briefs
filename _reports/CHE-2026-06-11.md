**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chemed is a two-segment holding company combining the nation's largest hospice operator (VITAS, ~64% of revenue) with the dominant national plumbing and drain-cleaning brand (Roto-Rooter, ~36%). The two businesses share no operational synergies; corporate exists to allocate capital and select management. The model has historically produced high returns on capital (FY25 ROE 27.1%, ROA 17.2%, asset turnover 1.64) with minimal leverage. That quality profile is intact, but the underlying earnings trend has turned in the wrong direction.

The central tension is that headline revenue growth is concealing a real profitability problem. FY2025 revenue rose 4.1% to $2.53B, but net income fell 12.2% to $265.2M and operating income declined to $338.2M from $366.5M, compressing net margin to 10.5% from 12.4%. Q1 2026 confirmed the trend: revenue +1.6%, net income -7.6%, with diluted EPS essentially flat at $4.84 only because the share count shrank roughly 7% year-over-year. Both segments saw net income decline in the quarter. VITAS is growing but absorbing acuity-mix and cost headwinds; Roto-Rooter is seeing volume contraction (plumbing jobs -6.4%, drain cleaning -12.0%) offset by double-digit price increases, a strategy with a finite runway.

The stock is down about 22% over the trailing twelve months and trades at ~15.8x forward earnings against a defensive, low-beta (0.54) profile. Management has responded by buying back stock aggressively ($197.7M in Q1 2026 at ~$395, versus $29.8M a year earlier at ~$595), a high-conviction corporate bet that the market is mispricing the business. We respect the quality and the capital discipline, but estimate revisions are flat (90-day current-year EPS +1.4%, with the current quarter revised down marginally over 30 days), there is no near-term earnings inflection, and insiders are net sellers personally even as the company buys. We rate CHE a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the downside is well-protected by the balance sheet and buyback, but there is no identifiable catalyst to drive a re-rating, and the earnings trajectory is negative.

## Company Overview

Chemed operates two unrelated businesses under a deliberately decentralized holding-company structure:

**VITAS Healthcare** is the largest U.S. provider of end-of-life hospice and palliative care, delivered through interdisciplinary teams of physicians, nurses, aides, social workers, clergy, and volunteers. Over 95% of VITAS revenue comes from Medicare and Medicaid on a fixed per-diem basis, which means VITAS bears essentially all cost inflation. The business is heavily concentrated in Florida, including its two largest programs in South Florida. It competes in a fragmented market against small community and not-for-profit hospices, hospital programs, and home-health agencies, with scale and Medicare certification as its primary barriers.

**Roto-Rooter** is the leading U.S. provider of plumbing, drain cleaning, excavation, and water restoration, reaching more than 90% of the U.S. population through company-owned branches, independent contractors, and franchisees. The Roto-Rooter trademark, in use since 1935, is among the most recognized service marks in the country and represents a genuine brand moat. Demand is weather-sensitive and partly discretionary.

The company employs roughly 15,811 people and carries a market capitalization of approximately $5.65B.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is declining while revenue grows.** Cost of revenue grew faster than revenue, compressing net margin by roughly 190 basis points. This is the single most important fact in the file, and it is obscured by the revenue headline. The three-year revenue CAGR is near 5.8%.

**Q1 2026 confirmed the deceleration.** Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 17.7% from 18.8%.

**Segment divergence is the real story.** VITAS Q1 revenue rose 3.1% on 2.2% average-daily-census growth and a 2.6% Medicare rate increase, with admissions up 6.9% and segment net income up 4.4%. The headwinds, however, are visible: an acuity/level-of-care mix shift cut revenue growth by 120 basis points, continuous-care revenue fell 26.4%, average length of stay dropped sharply to 102.7 days from 118.7, and gross margin slipped to 22.5% from 23.2% on higher variable patient-care costs. Roto-Rooter is deteriorating more clearly: Q1 revenue -0.9%, net income -10.4% ($35.8M vs $39.9M), after-tax margin down to 15.1% from 16.7%. Job counts fell (plumbing -6.4%, drain cleaning -12.0%, water restoration -11.7%) while pricing rose 12–14%. The segment is leaning on price to offset volume losses, which works until brand-supported pricing power exhausts.

**Balance sheet remains conservative.** Debt/equity is 0.57, Altman Z-Score is 3.59 (safe zone). Long-term debt was zero at year-end 2025; the company drew its revolver to $91.2M by Q1 2026 to fund buybacks and tuck-in acquisitions. A new five-year $450M revolving credit facility (April 2026, expandable by $250M) carries comfortable covenants (leverage <3.5x, interest coverage >3.0x) against minimal current borrowing.

**Cash flow is lumpy but sound.** FCF turned slightly negative in 2025 (-$30.1M) on elevated capex ($62.8M) and working-capital timing, not a structural issue; Q1 2026 operating cash flow rebounded to $88.2M from $32.7M. VITAS receivables are distorted by a roughly $62M biweekly Medicare payment whose timing straddles period-ends, which produces noisy quarterly cash-flow optics.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed and, on a net-income basis, currently negative. VITAS provides the structural tailwind: U.S. demographics favor rising hospice demand, and Q1 admissions growth confirms volume momentum. But the industry-wide trend toward shorter lengths of stay and higher-acuity care is a durable margin headwind, not a transient one. VITAS's length of stay has dropped meaningfully, and the per-diem reimbursement model means rate increases have historically lagged true cost inflation. Growth in census does not translate cleanly into earnings growth when the patient mix shifts toward higher cost-to-serve cases.

Roto-Rooter is the more concerning leg. The volume declines across plumbing, drain cleaning, and water restoration point to genuine softness in discretionary home-services demand. The price-over-volume strategy delivered roughly flat revenue but a 160-basis-point margin contraction and a 10.4% net income decline. A continuation of falling job counts with diminishing pricing headroom would turn this from a flat-revenue story into a declining-earnings story.

Estimate revisions reflect this stall. Consensus current-year EPS is $24.35, revised up just 1.4% over 90 days and flat over 30 days; the current quarter was revised down marginally (-0.1% over 30 days). Next-year consensus of $26.91 implies roughly 10.5% EPS growth, but a meaningful portion of that is buyback-driven rather than operating growth. There is no estimate-revision inflection here; the sharp positive revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings turn is absent. This is a steady-to-decelerating business, not an accelerating one.

## Valuation Assessment

CHE trades at ~15.8x forward earnings and ~23.2x trailing, with a 0.55% dividend yield and a 0.54 beta. The forward multiple is reasonable for a defensive, high-ROE compounder, and below where the stock traded a year ago. The peer-comparable set provided is not analytically useful — BridgeBio (a loss-making biopharma at -18.1x P/E) shares the healthcare sector label but nothing of CHE's business model — so we anchor valuation on absolute earnings power and the company's own history.

On forward consensus, the stock is neither cheap nor expensive. Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly: low $400, high $500, mean $446.50, median $443. The current price of $425.69 sits below the mean target, implying modest upside to consensus, but the analyst community is split (1 buy, 3 holds) and has not been revising targets upward. The cycle-adjusted argument that would justify a high valuation score is absent here. Earnings are declining, not recovering from a depressed base, so the trailing P/E of 23.2x is, if anything, flattered by a still-elevated denominator that is shrinking.

The bull-case valuation argument rests on the company's own behavior: management deployed buybacks at 6.6x the prior-year pace at depressed prices, even tapping the revolver. That is a deliberate "the market is wrong" stance, and at ~15.8x forward for a 27% ROE business with a fortress balance sheet, the downside is well-supported. The offsetting fact is that no executive is buying personally despite the depressed price, and the 5-year cumulative TSR in the proxy collapsed to 81.7 versus a peer group at 182.3. The valuation is fair, with the buyback providing a floor and the lack of earnings momentum capping the upside. We score valuation a 6.0 — supportive but not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

VITAS holds the leading national position in a fragmented and consolidating hospice market. Scale, Medicare certification, and Certificate-of-Need barriers in some states are real advantages, and the aging-demographics tailwind is durable. The competitive position is stable-to-improving on volume but pressured on economics by the structural shift to higher-acuity, shorter-stay care that the whole industry faces.

Roto-Rooter's brand is a genuine moat: a 90-year-old service mark with national emergency-service availability. But two new competitive threats are emerging. First, private equity has made significant investments in home-services and plumbing roll-ups, intensifying an already fragmented market. Second, changes in AI and search algorithms threaten Roto-Rooter's lead-generation model, which depends heavily on online discovery. Combined with the Q1 volume declines, the segment's competitive trajectory looks like it is flattening rather than improving. The prior trajectory shows this dimension already de-rated from 7.5 to 6.5 across recent reports; the latest data supports keeping it at 6.0 rather than reversing the decline.

## Risk Assessment

**VITAS Medicare dependence is the dominant risk.** With >95% of VITAS revenue government-funded on a fixed per-diem basis, the segment bears all cost inflation, and Medicare hospice is a frequently cited target for spending cuts. A 2% Medicare sequestration runs through federal FY2027. The Medicare per-beneficiary cap ($35,361 for the year ending September 2026) creates program-level exposure; VITAS recorded a $2.4M cap adjustment in Q1.

**False Claims Act and audit overhang.** The hospice model attracts qui tam and OIG scrutiny. VITAS settled a major DOJ FCA case in 2017 (corporate integrity agreement concluded 2023), and a $50.3M OIG repayment demand from 2022 was reduced to de minimis by an ALJ in February 2025 with deposits refunded, though the structural exposure persists.

**Cybersecurity — newly materialized.** The FY2025 10-K disclosed an October 24, 2025 cyberattack in which a threat actor accessed a significant amount of Protected Health Information at VITAS, the first successful breach of the company's systems. Resulting class actions were consolidated and settled for a non-material, insured amount, but management warns attacks are ongoing and a future breach could carry greater consequences.

**Roto-Rooter demand softness and PE competition** as discussed above, plus Florida hurricane concentration at VITAS and tariff exposure (steel for cabling machines, pharmaceuticals for VITAS) that VITAS cannot pass through under fixed reimbursement.

**CMS Special Focus Program** to flag poor-performing hospices was finalized in 2023 and halted February 2025 pending litigation; reinstatement could damage referrals for any flagged program.

These risks are largely known and at least partially priced, but the cyber breach and the Roto-Rooter volume erosion are genuinely newer developments that have not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

The options market has de-risked. ATM implied volatility fell from 41.6% to 22.6% (a 19-point drop) between late April and mid-May 2026, while the price rose 14.2%, and the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 1.95 to 0.39. This indicates resolved near-term uncertainty and a shift toward bullish or neutral positioning following the Q1 report; the market is not pricing imminent stress. Short interest is modest at 5.6% of float with 2.9 days to cover, consistent with no concentrated bearish conviction. The options signal is mildly supportive of the floor thesis but does not point to a catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
At ~15.8x forward earnings, CHE is a defensive, low-beta business with 27% ROE, a fortress balance sheet (D/E 0.57, near-zero net leverage at year-end), and demographic tailwinds at VITAS. Management is making a high-conviction bet on its own stock, retiring shares at 6.6x the prior pace at depressed prices and tapping the revolver to do so, which mechanically supports EPS and signals confidence. If Roto-Rooter volume stabilizes as discretionary spending recovers and VITAS census growth continues to outpace the acuity-mix drag, earnings reaccelerate and the multiple re-rates back toward its historical range. Bull-case target ~$500 (the high analyst target, ~20x forward EPS of ~$25 on stabilizing earnings), roughly +17% from current.

### Bear Case
Both segments are seeing net income decline, and EPS is being propped up by buybacks rather than operating growth. If Roto-Rooter's volume erosion continues and pricing power exhausts, the segment shifts from flat to declining. VITAS margins remain squeezed between fixed Medicare reimbursement and rising care costs, with sequestration and cap risk live through FY2027. A Medicare reimbursement disappointment, a CMS Special Focus reinstatement, or a renewed FCA action would compound the pressure. Bear-case target ~$365–400 (the 52-week low to low analyst target, ~15x on stagnant-to-declining EPS), roughly -6% to -14% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026), with particular attention to Roto-Rooter job-count trends and VITAS gross margin.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Roto-Rooter job counts stabilize or return to growth (plumbing and drain-cleaning volumes flat-to-positive YoY) without further margin erosion, OR VITAS gross margin recovers toward 23%+ as acuity-mix headwinds abate.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turn positive and accelerate (>5% over 90 days), signaling a genuine earnings inflection rather than buyback-supported optics.
- A pullback toward the high-$300s that would restore clearer asymmetry given the buyback floor.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Roto-Rooter pricing power breaks — revenue declines despite continued double-digit price increases, indicating brand-supported pricing has hit its ceiling.
- A material adverse Medicare reimbursement change, CMS Special Focus reinstatement affecting VITAS programs, or a new FCA action with quantified exposure.
- Net margin compression continues toward single digits with consolidated net income declining for three consecutive quarters.

## Conclusion

Chemed is a well-run, low-leverage, high-return holding company whose two businesses are both currently delivering declining net income while revenue grows. The headline numbers flatter the reality: consolidated EPS is being held flat by an aggressive buyback, VITAS faces a durable acuity-mix margin drag against fixed reimbursement, and Roto-Rooter is masking volume contraction with double-digit price increases that cannot continue indefinitely. The balance sheet and the company's own buyback provide a solid floor near current levels, and at ~15.8x forward the valuation is fair. But there is no earnings inflection, estimate revisions are flat, insiders are net sellers personally, and there is no identifiable catalyst to re-rate the stock over the next twelve months. This is a defensive name to hold for those already invested, not one to add to without a clearer turn in either segment's profitability. We rate CHE a HOLD.
