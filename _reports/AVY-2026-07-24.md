**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Avery Dennison is a global leader in pressure-sensitive label materials and digital identification, generating ~$8.86B in FY2025 revenue across two segments: a high-margin, scale-advantaged Materials Group (~69% of sales) and a lower-margin, growth-oriented Solutions Group (~31%) that houses the Intelligent Labels/RFID franchise. The company is a durable free-cash-flow compounder (~$707M adjusted FCF in 2025) with a shareholder-friendly capital-return profile: mid-teens historical dividend CAGR (raised again to $1.00/quarter in April 2026, ~2.5% forward yield), $572M of buybacks in 2025, and 30.7% ROE. The balance-sheet "distress" screens (Altman Z 1.57, D/E 3.93) are artifacts of an intentionally buyback-shrunk book equity, not solvency stress: interest coverage is roughly 7x, the company sits well below its 3.5x net-leverage covenant with no revolver drawn, and it remains committed to investment grade.

The growth thesis and the valuation depend on the same lever, and that lever is currently weakening. Intelligent Labels/RFID, the sole above-GDP organic growth avenue, decelerated from high-single-digit growth in 2024 to low-single-digit in 2025 and turned negative (low-single-digit decline) in Q1 2026. The strategic narrative has inverted: the "boring" Materials Group (organic +2%, segment OI +10% in Q1) is carrying the company while the Solutions "growth" segment posted a 14% decline in operating income on apparel softness and investment drag. Consensus EPS estimates have drifted modestly lower across every window (current year -0.7%, next year -1.1% over 90 days), and 2026 faces two known non-operating EPS headwinds: a tax rate stepping up to the high-20s (from mid-20s) and the normalization of temporarily depressed 2025 incentive-comp accruals.

At ~$155 the stock has de-rated ~22% from its February 2026 high near $199.54 and trades at ~15.5x forward EPS (~$10.02 current-year consensus). That is a reasonable multiple for a franchise of this quality, and the dividend-yield-support case has merit, but there is no identified catalyst to re-rate the multiple until RFID re-inflects, which is program-driven and lumpy. The setup is balanced: downside is cushioned by yield, FCF, and buybacks, but upside requires a growth reacceleration that the most recent data point contradicts. We rate AVY a HOLD on an income basis, constructive on weakness into the low-$140s or on evidence of an Intelligent Labels rollout re-acceleration.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1935 and headquartered in Mentor, Ohio, Avery Dennison operates two reportable segments:

- **Materials Group (~$6.09B FY2025, ~69% of sales):** Global provider of pressure-sensitive label materials (Fasson, JAC), graphics and reflective films (Mactac), and performance tapes/adhesives. A scale-advantaged "razor" business selling to a highly fragmented converter channel, with FY2025 segment adjusted operating margin ~15.1% (~$922M). The October 2025 ~$390M Taylor Adhesives acquisition (Georgia flooring adhesives) was folded into this segment's high-value categories.
- **Solutions Group (~$2.76B FY2025, ~31% of sales):** Information and branding solutions including Intelligent Labels (RFID inlays/tags), apparel embellishments, price ticketing, and Vestcom shelf-edge media. Lower margin (~10.4%) but historically the growth vector.

The business is heavily international (~69% of sales outside the U.S., ~40% emerging markets, ~58% of the ~35,000 employees in Asia Pacific). No customer exceeds 10% of sales, and the fragmented base is a genuine competitive strength. The moat rests on scale, materials-science R&D ($137M in 2025), process engineering, and converter-channel access. Key competitors include UPM, Fedrigoni, Lintec, and Flexcon in labels; 3M and Orafol in graphics/tapes; and Checkpoint (CCL), SML, and Tageos in RFID/Solutions.

A Q1 2026 segment reclassification moved certain RFID product revenue from Solutions into Materials (reflecting RFID inlay materials increasingly sold through the Materials channel), which will slightly complicate segment comparisons going forward.

Revenue is essentially flat and margins are stable, the profile of a mature, cash-generative compounder rather than a grower at present.

(2023 net income was depressed by the Adasa RFID patent settlement and Argentine peso remeasurement charges.)

Organic growth stalled at ~0% in 2025 as raw-material deflation-driven price reductions offset volume gains, a pricing-pass-through effect rather than a demand collapse. Q1 2026 showed the now-characteristic bifurcation: total sales +7% (of which ~+5pp was FX), organic +1%; Materials Group re-accelerated (organic +2%, segment adjusted OI +10% to $254M) while Solutions Group weakened (organic -1%, segment adjusted OI -14% to $58.5M) on apparel softness and growth-investment drag. Q1 net income was $168.1M (EPS $2.18 vs. $2.09), with the effective tax rate jumping to 30.1% from 26.7% on a discrete foreign tax-reserve charge; full-year 2026 tax guidance was raised to "high-twenty percent."

Capital allocation remains disciplined. The dividend was raised ~7% in 2025 and a further ~6% to $1.00/quarter in April 2026, extending a mid-teens dividend-growth history; ~$572M of buybacks in 2025 (3.2M shares) reduced the share count from ~80.4M to ~76.5M. FCF of ~$707M comfortably covers ~$288M of dividends.

The balance sheet warrants a caveat. The Altman Z of 1.57 and D/E of 3.93 are artifacts of an aggressively buyback-shrunk book equity ($2.24B) against $3.73B debt. In practice, ROE is 30.7%, interest coverage is roughly 7x (~$135M interest against ~$925M pretax income), the company sits well below its 3.5x (4.0x post-acquisition) net-leverage covenant, carries no drawn revolver, and remains committed to investment grade. This is a deliberately levered, capital-return-oriented compounder, not a distressed balance sheet. The relevant watch item is interest-expense creep: FY interest rose $117M→$135M, and Q1 2026 interest was up ~15% YoY following the September 2025 €500M 2035 notes (4.0%), on top of ~$739M average variable-rate borrowings.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is the crux of the debate, and it is currently working against the bulls. Intelligent Labels/RFID, the company's clearest above-GDP avenue, has decelerated sharply: high-single-digit organic growth in 2024, low-single-digit in 2025, and a low-single-digit decline in Q1 2026. Because the bull case rests almost entirely on secular RFID adoption (item-level digital identity for inventory accuracy and loss prevention), this deceleration is the single most important thread. RFID growth is program-driven and lumpy, dependent on the timing of large retailer rollouts, and is currently cyclically depressed by apparel weakness that tariff policy has amplified.

Management's response is the ~$75M minority investment in Wiliot (completed April 2026), a "physical AI"/ambient-IoT platform, positioned as an option to extend RFID into battery-free sensing (temperature, location) for food and pharma cold chains. This diversifies away from apparel dependency, but it is early-stage, a minority stake rather than owned technology, and immaterial to near-term numbers.

Beyond RFID, the growth outlook is modest. Materials Group is a GDP+ industrial-materials business with strong FCF conversion; its Q1 re-acceleration is encouraging but is not a structural growth driver. Consensus reflects the muted picture: current-year EPS estimates of $10.02 (down 0.7% over 90 days) and next-year $11.15 (down 1.1%), with no meaningful directional shift and no signal approaching the estimate-revision velocity that would flag a reacceleration. The earnings-surprise record is unremarkable (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss over four quarters, all within ~2.2%), consistent with a stable, well-modeled business.

A quality-of-earnings nuance further tempers 2026 growth: management flagged that 2025 EPS benefited from temporarily depressed incentive-comp accruals that normalize (rise) in 2026, alongside the higher tax rate. Both are non-operating headwinds that could make 2026 look softer than the underlying business.

AVY trades right at the peer median P/E of 15.5x while carrying a superior return profile (30.7% ROE, ~15% Materials margins, durable FCF), arguably a modest quality discount. The dividend-yield-support argument has merit: at ~2.5% forward versus a ~1.7% five-year average, the yield sits toward the wide end of its historical range, which is the basis for the external "~24% discount to fair value" dividend-yield-theory call.

A re-rating requires a catalyst, and the one that matters, RFID re-inflection, is absent from the most recent data. A low multiple on a de-rated compounder is not by itself a buy signal, and cheapness without a catalyst has historically marked value traps more often than bargains. AVY is fairly priced for a business with flat organic growth and a weakening growth engine. The analyst consensus targets (mean ~$199.6, median ~$204.5, range $175–$218) imply substantial upside, but those targets embed a growth reacceleration that has not materialized and are likely to drift toward the price if RFID stays soft. We do not treat those targets as reliable floors here, because the setup lacks the rising-estimate momentum that would validate them.

Fair value on ~$10.50 normalized EPS at ~15.5–16x supports a base case around $165, roughly 6% above current price plus the ~2.5% yield. That is a reasonable total-return profile but not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Materials Group competes on scale and process technology in a fragmented, slowly consolidating converter market. Customer consolidation is a gradual pricing-pressure risk, but AVY has managed it historically, and the segment's Q1 re-acceleration and stable ~28.8% gross margins indicate no near-term erosion. This is a durable, sticky franchise with high FCF conversion.

Solutions Group / Intelligent Labels is the strategic battleground, and the competitive trajectory there is the concern. The RFID adoption thesis remains structurally intact over a multi-year horizon, but the 2024→2026 deceleration demonstrates that AVY's growth in this category is program-timing-dependent and currently cyclically depressed. Competitors (Checkpoint/CCL, SML, Tageos) are active in RFID, and the Wiliot investment is an acknowledgment that the apparel-centric RFID base needs diversification into food/grocery and ambient-IoT use cases. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-weakening: the core Materials moat is intact, but the growth vector the market cares about has lost momentum, which explains the trajectory of prior score reductions in this dimension (7.5 → 6.0).

## Risk Assessment

1. **RFID/Intelligent Labels deceleration (the growth-thesis risk).** The entire re-rating case hinges on re-inflection; H2 2026 program pipeline is the key evidence to watch.
2. **Tariff/trade-policy whipsaw.** Direct exposure is modest, but the indirect apparel-demand hit is real: management estimated a low-single-digit sales decline in apparel across Q2–Q4 2025. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize presidential tariffs may bring relief but injects near-term trade-policy instability.
3. **Large Materials customer Chapter 11 (Jan 2026).** Management expects to collect prepetition receivables but flagged spread risk; collection is a thread to follow.
4. **2026 EPS headwinds.** Tax rate stepping to high-20s and normalization of temporarily depressed 2025 incentive-comp accruals are both non-operating drags.
5. **FX and emerging-market exposure.** ~69% international; FX was a ~$29M 2025 tailwind and ~$99M Q1 2026 tailwind, favorable to reported optics now but volatile (Argentina, India, Brazil cited).
6. **Interest-expense creep and rate sensitivity.** ~$739M average variable-rate borrowings; interest up ~15% YoY in Q1 2026. In a higher-for-longer rate environment this is a persistent, if manageable, headwind.
7. **Sustainability regulation double-edge.** EU CSRD (disclosure from 2028) and California climate rules raise compliance costs; anti-plastic/EPR rules could pressure pressure-sensitive-label demand but also create demand for recyclable products.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign. Near-term ATM IV of 30.5% is modestly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 24.1% (a 6.4pp spread), but the IV percentile within the current chain is low at 22%, put/call skew is balanced at 2.3%, and the term structure is in normal contango (30.5% near vs. 32.8% long-dated). The put/call OI ratio collapsed from 9.11 to 0.77 since early June, removing what had been heavy downside positioning. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.0%. Short interest is low at 3.9% of float (3.3 days to cover). Nothing in the options or short-interest data signals informed bearish positioning ahead of a catalyst, consistent with a stable, un-crowded name rather than one priced for a sharp move.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($200+):** RFID re-inflects in H2 2026 as new retailer programs roll out and the Wiliot-enabled food/pharma adjacency begins contributing, pushing Solutions Group organic growth back toward high-single-digit. Tariff relief following the SCOTUS ruling restores apparel demand. Materials Group continues its high-margin re-acceleration. EPS recovers to ~$11.50+ and the multiple re-rates toward 17–18x on renewed growth confidence, supported by continued double-digit dividend growth and buybacks. This is essentially the consensus-target case (mean ~$199.6, median ~$204.5).

**Bear Case (~$130):** RFID deceleration proves structural rather than cyclical: apparel weakness persists, retailer rollout timing stays soft, and competitive pressure from CCL/SML/Tageos compresses margins. Tariff instability continues to depress demand. The 2026 tax and incentive-comp headwinds make reported EPS look flat-to-down, and estimate revisions turn decisively negative. Absent growth, the multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range (~13x on ~$10 EPS), implying ~$130, roughly 16% below current price. The yield cushion limits but does not prevent a further de-rating.

**Base Case (~$165):** Organic growth stays modestly positive, Materials carries the company while Solutions stabilizes without re-inflecting, EPS reaches ~$10.50 for 2026, and the multiple holds near 15.5–16x. Total return is the ~2.5% yield plus low-single-digit price appreciation, a fair, unexciting outcome for a quality compounder without a catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** Reassessment timeline: the H2 2026 earnings prints (Q2 and Q3), which will reveal whether Intelligent Labels re-inflects and whether the tax/incentive-comp headwinds land as flagged.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Intelligent Labels/RFID organic growth returns to positive and preferably mid-single-digit or better in Q2/Q3 2026, indicating new retailer program rollouts.
- Consensus current-year EPS estimates turn positive (rising, not the current -0.7% 90-day drift), confirming a fundamental turn.
- Price weakness into the low-$140s that pushes the forward yield above ~2.8% and the forward P/E below ~14x while fundamentals hold, an entry where the yield support and cheapness become asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- RFID declines accelerate (mid-single-digit or worse) or Solutions Group operating income posts a second consecutive double-digit decline, signaling structural rather than cyclical impairment.
- 2026 EPS guidance is cut materially below ~$10, or estimate revisions turn sharply negative (>3% over 90 days).
- A dividend-coverage deterioration (FCF falling well below ~$500M) or a leverage breach approaching the 3.5x covenant.

Given three consecutive HOLD ratings with a declining score trajectory (6.6 → 6.2 → 5.6), the bar for continuing the call unchanged is appropriately high. The current data does not reverse that trend: organic growth remains flat, RFID has weakened further, and estimates continue to drift lower. But the ~22% de-rating from the February high has improved the yield-support and valuation cushion, keeping the risk/reward balanced rather than negative. The price decline itself is not the reason for caution; the growth-engine deceleration is.

## Conclusion

Avery Dennison is a well-run, cash-generative materials-science franchise with a strong economic profile (30.7% ROE, ~$700M FCF, disciplined capital return) that is trading at a fair multiple after a meaningful de-rating. The company will very likely continue compounding capital returns through dividends (mid-teens historical growth, ~2.5% forward yield) and buybacks, which cushions downside and supports a base-case total return in the mid-single digits.

What is missing is a catalyst. The Intelligent Labels/RFID engine, the only element of the business capable of re-rating the stock, has decelerated from high-single-digit growth to outright decline, and the strategic narrative has inverted, with the mature Materials Group now carrying a Solutions Group whose operating income fell 14% in Q1. Estimates are drifting lower, and 2026 carries known tax and incentive-comp headwinds. Until RFID demonstrates re-inflection, AVY is a capital-return compounder trading at fair value rather than a mispriced growth story. We rate the stock a HOLD on an income basis, with a constructive lean on weakness into the low-$140s or on the first credible evidence of an Intelligent Labels rollout re-acceleration.
