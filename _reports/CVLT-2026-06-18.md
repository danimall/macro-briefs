**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Commvault has transformed from a legacy backup-and-recovery vendor into a cyber-resiliency platform built around ransomware recovery, with FY2026 revenue of $1,183.7M (+19% YoY), subscription revenue of $768.3M (+30%), and SaaS ARR of $400.2M (+42%). The business generates high-quality cash: $237.2M free cash flow at a ~20% FCF margin, 81% gross margins, and a Piotroski F-Score of 5/5. It is a structurally sound software franchise riding the secular tailwinds of data growth, cloud migration, and ransomware defense, sitting in Information Technology systems software, the one sector where company selection has historically generated positive risk-adjusted returns.

The stock is down 32% over the trailing twelve months and 37% below its 52-week high of $200.68, almost entirely traceable to one event: the January 27, 2026 report that net new ARR came in at $39M against a ~$45M target, which triggered a 31% single-session decline and a wave of analyst downgrades. Management framed the miss as a SaaS-mix optical artifact (SaaS net revenue retention decelerated from 127% to 121-122%, diluting reported ARR), but a securities class action covering April 29, 2025 – January 26, 2026 alleges the guidance was materially misleading, and DA Davidson publicly questioned the cannibalization narrative. A CFO transition occurred in the same window, with the Chief Accounting Officer signing the Q3 10-Q as Principal Financial Officer before Gary Merrill (formerly Chief Commercial Officer) assumed the CFO role.

The fundamental case is genuinely two-sided. The bankruptcy-zone Altman Z-Score of 1.16 is a false positive driven by buyback-zeroed book equity (cash ≈ debt, $7.5M stockholders' equity against a $1.47B accumulated deficit), not distress. But the stock has already recovered roughly 27% off its April low to $127, while consensus estimates have merely stabilized (no upward revisions in 30 days) rather than re-accelerated, and insiders did not buy the dip. The valuation no longer prices the worst case, and the litigation, decelerating net retention, and management instability are unresolved. We rate CVLT HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident call in either direction at the current price.

## Company Overview

Commvault sells data protection and cyber-resilience software under a "protect, secure, and recover" framing, with its flagship Commvault Cloud platform (incorporating the former Metallic SaaS offering) addressing hybrid and multi-cloud environments. The fiscal year ends March 31. Market capitalization is approximately $5.4B at $127.10 per share, on roughly 41.3M shares outstanding.

The FY2026 revenue mix tells the strategic story:
- **Subscription: $768.3M (65% of revenue, +30%)** — term-based licenses $435.3M (+18%) plus SaaS $333.0M (+52%).
- **Customer support: $320.4M (+4%)** — recurring but maturing, with term-based support growing while perpetual support declines.
- **Perpetual license: $43.2M (–22%)** — deliberately wound down.
- **Other (professional) services: $51.7M (+21%).**

Roughly 90% of revenue flows through indirect channels (resellers, distributors, OEMs, marketplaces), and approximately 47% is international. The company reports as a single segment. Blended gross margin is ~81%, with term-license at ~98%, SaaS at ~64%, and support at ~82%. Named competitors in the 10-K are Rubrik, Cohesity, and Veeam, alongside the hyperscalers' native tooling.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory and profitability.** Revenue has compounded durably: $784.6M (FY23) → $839.2M (FY24) → $995.6M (FY25) → $1,183.7M (FY26), a ~19-21% growth band that has held. ARR reached $1,121.6M (+21%), subscription ARR $989.3M (+27%), and SaaS ARR $400.2M (+42%). GAAP operating income was $74.0M (6.3% margin) and net income $70.7M. The FY24 net income figure of $168.9M was flattered by an $85.3M valuation-allowance release, so FY25 and FY26 reflect a normalized higher tax rate; a year-over-year comparison of GAAP net income alone overstates the deceleration.

**Cash generation is the standout.** Operating cash flow was $244.7M and free cash flow $237.2M, an ~20% FCF margin that exceeds GAAP net income by a wide margin because of deferred-revenue dynamics and ~$120M+ of annual stock-based compensation. On the current ~$5.4B market cap, the FCF yield is approximately 4.4%, reasonable for a 19%-growth software business.

**The balance sheet requires careful reading.** Cash stands at ~$900M against $900M of 0% convertible notes due 2030, leaving the company net-debt-neutral. Stockholders' equity is just $7.5M with an accumulated deficit of –$1.47B. This is the cumulative result of $1.25B+ of buybacks over five years retiring equity faster than retained earnings accrue, not financial distress. The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 lands in the "distress zone" purely because the equity-to-assets and equity-to-liabilities terms collapse toward zero, a financial-engineering artifact. The current ratio of 1.95 and the Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 (positive net income, ROA, OCF; OCF exceeding net income; healthy current ratio) are the accurate read on operating health.

**Capital allocation has been buyback-heavy.** FY26 repurchases totaled $446.1M (4.2M shares at an average $106.91), of which $117.7M was a concurrent buyback funded by the convertible-note proceeds. The 0% coupon notes (conversion price $236.88, capped calls lifting the effective dilution cap to $357.56) were cheap financing to retire stock without near-term cash interest. The board re-authorized an additional $250M in April 2026. There is no dividend.

**Earnings track record.** The company beat in three of the last four quarters: +15.6% (Mar 2026), +17.0% (Dec 2025), –5.1% (Sep 2025), +2.2% (Jun 2025). The two most recent prints were strong beats on the bottom line; the EPS line held up even as the ARR KPI disappointed. The discipline gap is between accounting earnings (beating) and the forward-bookings KPI the market prices the stock on (missing).

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is the subscription transition, led by SaaS at +52% revenue and +42% ARR. The secular backdrop is favorable: data volumes, ransomware threats, AI workloads, and cloud migration all expand the addressable market for cyber-resilient data protection. Three of Commvault's named competitors are growing fast, which confirms a real, broad-based demand shift rather than a single-company story.

The complication is the deceleration inside the growth numbers. SaaS net revenue retention fell from 127% to 121-122%, and SaaS ARR growth slowed from 68% to 42%. The mechanism management offers is a mix shift: as SaaS replaces term-license and the company sells longer-duration term deals, reported net new ARR is diluted even as underlying demand holds. The DA Davidson critique pushes back: Commvault had previously shown upside in both SaaS and term-license simultaneously, so a sudden cannibalization claim reads as a demand or execution problem wearing an accounting explanation.

Estimate revisions land in the middle of this debate and do not rescue the bull case. Consensus moved up over 90 days (current year $4.83 → $5.24, +8.5%; next year $5.67 → $6.09, +7.3%) but has been flat over the last 30 days (0.0% across all periods). The strongest forward signal — estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days alongside accelerating revenue — is not present here. Revisions are positive but stable, consistent with a market that has digested the miss and is waiting for the next two prints rather than systematically re-rating the stock upward. The next net-new-ARR disclosure is the data point that resolves whether net new ARR re-accelerates back toward management's targets.

## Valuation Assessment

At $127.10, the trailing P/E of ~80x is misleading; it reflects a tax-normalized GAAP base, not the cash earnings power. The more relevant frame is the forward multiple on consensus: $5.24 current-year EPS implies ~24.3x forward, and $6.09 next-year EPS implies ~20.9x forward. On a ~20% growth, 20%-FCF-margin software business, that is not demanding. The PEG on next-year growth is well below 1.5x.

Peer comparison is muddied by sector heterogeneity. The systems-software comparables (PATH 17.6x, QLYS 19.9x, ATEN 53.5x, PRGS 16.2x, NABL negative) carry a peer median P/E of 19.9x, but most of these are slower-growing or troubled (PATH down 47% from its high, PRGS down 52%, NABL loss-making). On forward earnings CVLT at ~21-24x sits at a modest premium to the median, which its superior growth and FCF generation arguably justify. Rubrik and Cohesity, the more directly comparable cloud-native cyber-resilience peers, are not included in the peer benchmark set provided, so a direct multiple comparison against the cohort that matters most is not supported by the available data.

The reason this is not a high valuation score is that the asymmetry has narrowed. The stock has recovered from ~$72 in April to $127, a ~27% move, while the fundamentals that would justify a re-rating (re-accelerating net new ARR, stabilizing SaaS NRR) have not yet confirmed. The analyst consensus mean target of $135.20 (median $133, range $100–$175) sits only ~6% above the current price, and on a stock with an open litigation overhang and decelerating net retention, the consensus targets are not a clean "floor" to treat as conservative. The market has already moved the price back to fair, leaving limited margin of safety. A pullback toward the low-$100s, or a clean print showing net new ARR back near target, would shift this toward a buy. At $127 the price embeds a recovery that has not been demonstrated.

## Competitive Landscape

Commvault is a mid-cap incumbent in an intensely competitive, fragmented data-protection market. Its advantages are breadth of workload coverage and a long enterprise track record; its disadvantage is that Rubrik and Cohesity were cloud-native from inception and carry stronger brand momentum in exactly the SaaS segment where Commvault's reported growth just decelerated. Cohesity's merger with Veritas's NetBackup business created a better-capitalized scaled competitor, and Veeam dominates the mid-market.

The competitive read on the ARR miss is the crux. If the deceleration in SaaS net revenue retention reflects normal maturation and a genuine accounting mix effect, the moat is intact and the franchise is gaining absolute share in a growing market. If it reflects share loss to cloud-native rivals at renewal, the trajectory is deteriorating in a way trailing revenue does not yet show. The acquisitions of Satori Cyber (data/AI security, ~$28M, Aug 2025), Clumio, and Appranix signal Commvault buying cloud-native and AI-security capability to defend the SaaS battleground, which is the right strategic response but unproven in its effect. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-pressured: the company is still growing share in absolute terms, but the trajectory in the contested segment is the open question.

## Risk Assessment

**ARR-miss litigation.** A securities class action in D.N.J. alleges guidance materially omitted how SaaS mix and longer-duration term licenses would dilute ARR. The lead-plaintiff deadline is July 17, 2026. Settlement risk is real but financially bounded given the company's cash position; the larger cost is credibility and management distraction.

**Channel concentration.** Partner A is 32% of total revenue (and ~25% of AR); Partner B is 11%. These are distribution aggregators rather than end customers, but disruption of Partner A would be materially adverse. This concentration is high relative to most software peers.

**Management instability.** The CFO transition coincided with the worst quarter. Multiple Item 5.02 8-Ks (Oct/Dec 2025, Apr 2026) confirm elevated turnover during a credibility crisis.

**ARR as both comp metric and litigation subject.** ARR is an executive incentive metric and the disputed KPI, creating a governance tension worth weighting. CEO FY25 total comp was $18.8M ($42.1M comp-actually-paid on the prior stock run).

**Convertible-note mechanics.** Conversion can trigger above ~$308, with capped calls offsetting dilution only to $357.56. The company's own disclosure notes the structure may encourage short selling.

**Security incident.** A "non-routine security matter" cost $1.8M in legal/consulting expense in Q2 FY26; public reporting tied Commvault to a nation-state Azure intrusion, though that corroborating detail was limited in the available sources and specifics should be treated as not fully confirmed. For a cyber-resilience vendor, any breach carries outsized reputational risk.

**SaaS margin mix.** SaaS gross margin (~64%) is structurally below term-license (~98%), so blended gross margin faces gradual pressure as SaaS scales, with Azure infrastructure costs partly fixed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.3% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 59.9% (a –12.6% spread), placing implied vol cheap and in the 19th percentile of the current chain; the options market is not pricing acute near-term event risk into the July expiry, and entry-timing for hedged positioning is favorable. Put/call skew is balanced (–0.8%), and the put/call OI ratio collapsed to 0.04 from 0.16 a month prior, indicating bearish positioning has unwound as the stock recovered. The 3-month tenor carries a higher 54.8% IV (±18.8% implied move), consistent with the next earnings print being the volatility catalyst. The reported 472.9% ATM IV in the delta snapshot is a corrupted thin-options artifact and should be disregarded. The options market is neither confirming nor contradicting a bearish thesis: positioning is balanced and complacent, consistent with a stock that has round-tripped the panic.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The ARR miss was an optical SaaS-mix artifact, not a demand break. Net new ARR re-accelerates toward management's targets in the next two prints; SaaS NRR stabilizes near 120%+; the franchise continues compounding revenue at ~19% with 20% FCF margins. On normalized cash earnings the stock is cheap relative to its growth and FCF profile, the litigation settles for a bounded sum, and the new CFO restores credibility. Re-rating toward the high end of the analyst range ($175) implies ~38% upside. The cheap IV and balanced options positioning suggest the market is not crowded against the recovery.

**Bear Case.** The deceleration in SaaS net revenue retention reflects genuine share loss at renewal to better-capitalized cloud-native rivals (Rubrik, Cohesity/Veritas). The cannibalization narrative is the cover story the DA Davidson critique alleges. Net new ARR continues to disappoint, the litigation drags through 2026, management turnover signals deeper execution problems, and the stock retests its April low near $72–$100. The low analyst target is $100, ~21% below current.

The two cases are genuinely balanced at $127. The stock no longer prices the disaster scenario, but the fundamentals that would confirm the bull case (re-accelerating net new ARR, stabilizing NRR) have not arrived, and insiders did not buy the dip; all recent transactions are routine 10b5-1 vesting-related sales, with no open-market buying, a neutral-to-mildly-negative tell during a 31% drawdown.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report (Q1 FY2027, expected late July/early August 2026) and the litigation lead-plaintiff deadline are the near-term resolving events. The net-new-ARR disclosure is the single most important data point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net new ARR re-accelerates to or above management's target (~$45M+) for one to two consecutive quarters, AND SaaS net revenue retention stabilizes at or above ~120%.
- A pullback toward the low-$100s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring an asymmetric forward multiple (~17-18x forward EPS) against ~19% growth.
- Resumption of upward estimate revisions (current-year EPS revised above $5.24 over a 30-day window) confirming consensus is again chasing reality.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive net-new-ARR miss or a further decline in SaaS NRR below ~118%, confirming the demand/competitive-deterioration thesis rather than a one-off mix effect.
- Material adverse development in the securities litigation (e.g., denial of a motion to dismiss with a large quantified exposure) or evidence the ARR-disclosure issue extends beyond the disputed period.
- Loss or material disruption of Partner A, or a gross-margin decline below ~78% signaling SaaS-mix pressure outpacing scale benefits.

## Conclusion

Commvault is a high-quality, cash-generative, growing cyber-resilience software franchise whose stock was cut sharply on a single ARR miss and now carries unresolved litigation, a CFO transition, and a decelerating SaaS net-retention rate. The balance-sheet "distress" signal is a false positive from buyback-driven negative book equity; solvency is not the issue. The genuine question is whether the net-new-ARR shortfall was an optical SaaS-mix artifact or the first visible sign of competitive deceleration against better-capitalized cloud-native rivals, and that question is not yet answerable from the available data.

The stock has already recovered ~27% off its April low while estimates have only stabilized and insiders have not bought, leaving the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric at $127. The forward multiple (~21-24x) is reasonable but not cheap enough to compensate for the open litigation and execution risks, and the consensus target sits only ~6% above the current price. We rate CVLT **HOLD**. The next net-new-ARR disclosure and the litigation lead-plaintiff deadline will resolve the core debate; a clean re-acceleration or a pullback to the low-$100s would move this to a buy, while a second miss or a deeper NRR decline would move it to avoid.
