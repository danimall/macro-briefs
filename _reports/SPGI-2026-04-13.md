## Executive Summary

S&P Global is a $125B+ market cap provider of credit ratings, benchmarks, data, and analytics across capital markets, energy, and automotive verticals. The company's core competitive advantage—regulatory-embedded positions in ratings (64% operating margin) and indices (69% operating margin)—generates $5.1B in annual free cash flow on just $195M of capex. All five business segments posted margin expansion in 2025, operating margins reached 42.2% company-wide, and management has returned $15.1B to shareholders over the past three years through buybacks and dividends.

The stock has pulled back materially in early 2026, trading at ~$422 against a consensus mean target of $539 and a range of $480-$625. The decline is driven primarily by AI disruption fears, particularly concerns that advanced language models could erode the value of Market Intelligence's data analytics and research products. These fears are partially justified for Market Intelligence (32% of revenue, 20% margin) but substantially overstated for Ratings (31% of revenue, 64% margin), Indices (12% of revenue, 69% margin), and Energy (15% of revenue, 41% margin), which derive their value from regulatory status, benchmark embeddedness, and contractual reference pricing respectively.

The mid-2026 Mobility spin-off provides a specific, near-term catalyst for value crystallization. At ~21.5x current-year earnings ($19.62 consensus), SPGI trades at a meaningful discount to its peer group median near 35x—a discount that appropriately reflects some AI risk but overweights it relative to the structural durability of the ratings and indices franchises. Estimate revisions have been modestly negative (-1.5% over 90 days for current year) but have stabilized over the last 30 days.

## Company Overview

S&P Global operates through five segments following the 2022 IHS Markit merger:

**S&P Global Ratings** (31% of revenue, ~46% of segment operating profit): Duopoly with Moody's controlling ~80% of the global credit ratings market. NRSRO regulatory status creates an effective barrier to entry. 64% operating margin. Revenue split 52% transaction (tied to issuance volumes) / 48% non-transaction (recurring surveillance fees).

**Market Intelligence** (32% of revenue, ~15% of segment profit): Data, analytics, and workflow tools including Capital IQ Pro. 83% subscription revenue. 20% operating margin, suppressed by $588M in annual acquisition-related intangible amortization from IHS Markit.

**S&P Global Commodity Insights** (15% of revenue, ~14% of segment profit): Platts price assessments and upstream data. 88% subscription. 41% operating margin. Benchmark prices embedded in physical commodity contracts.

**S&P Dow Jones Indices** (12% of revenue, ~19% of segment profit net of NCI): Operates the S&P 500 and other benchmarks. $5.48T in ETF AUM linked to S&P DJI indices. 69% operating margin. 65% of revenue is asset-linked fees—a direct exposure to global equity market levels.

**Mobility** (11% of revenue, ~6% of segment profit): Automotive data and analytics. 22% operating margin. Slated for spin-off as a standalone public company by mid-2026.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Growth

Revenue grew from $11.2B (2022) to $15.3B (2025), an 11% CAGR driven by all segments. The 2024 surge (14% growth) was powered by a Ratings bonanza—billed issuance volumes surged 54% on refinancing activity. Growth moderated to 8% in 2025 as the Ratings cycle normalized, but the trajectory remains positive.

Adjusted diluted EPS compounded at 10.1% annually: $12.36 (2022) → $14.06 (2023) → $16.23 (2024) → $18.20 (2025). Consensus estimates project $19.62 for FY2026 and $22.07 for FY2027, implying 8% and 12.5% growth respectively. These estimates have been stable over the past 30 days after modest negative revisions over 90 days (-1.5% for current year, -1.0% for next year).

### Margin Expansion

Operating margin expanded to 42.2% in 2025 from 39.3% in 2024 and 32.2% in 2023. The improvement reflects the completion of IHS Markit merger cost absorption (~$133M in 2024 falling to $0 in 2025) and operating leverage across all segments.

| Segment | 2023 Margin | 2024 Margin | 2025 Margin |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Ratings | 56% | 62% | 64% |
| Indices | 66% | 68% | 69% |
| Commodity Insights | 36% | 39% | 41% |
| Market Intelligence | 16% | 19% | 20% |
| Mobility | 18% | 19% | 22% |

Net margin reached 31.4% in 2025.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Free cash flow was $5.14B in 2025 ($5.65B operating cash flow minus $195M capex, minus ~$315M of capitalized software), representing a 33.5% FCF margin. Capital intensity is negligible at 1.3% of revenue.

Management has been aggressive on capital returns: $15.1B over three years (2023-2025), comprising ~$11.6B in buybacks and ~$3.5B in dividends. Share count declined 4.3% from 318.9M to 305.1M over this period. A new $5B buyback authorization was approved in November 2025, and the quarterly dividend was raised to $0.97/share ($3.88 annual, ~0.9% yield at current prices).

### Balance Sheet

Total debt is $13.1B against $1.75B in cash. Debt-to-equity of 0.80 is manageable for a business generating $5.7B in annual operating cash flow. The debt maturity profile is well-laddered: $718M within 1 year, $2.5B in 1-3 years, $3.3B in 3-5 years, $6.5B beyond 5 years. The only financial covenant (indebtedness-to-cash-flow ≤ 4:1) has never been exceeded.

The balance sheet carries $52.7B in goodwill and intangibles (86% of total assets), a legacy of the $44B IHS Markit acquisition. This depresses ROA (7.9%) and ROE (15.4%) relative to underlying economics. On a tangible basis, returns on capital are substantially higher.

CME Group holds a put option to sell at least 20% of its stake in S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC at fair value, carried at $4.9B in redeemable noncontrolling interest.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

Three beats in four quarters, with a Q4 2025 miss of -2.6%. The miss is worth monitoring but does not constitute a pattern break. The average surprise over the trailing four quarters is +2.0%, suggesting consensus estimates are reasonably well-calibrated with a slight upward bias.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Growth Drivers

**Ratings issuance cycle:** Billed issuance reached $4.3T in 2025 (+11% YoY). Elevated interest rates, widening spreads from the Hormuz crisis and trade uncertainty, and 45-50% recession probability create a mixed outlook. Refinancing activity driven by maturing debt provides a floor, but new issuance could slow if credit conditions tighten. AI-related capital raising was specifically cited as a new issuance driver.

**Index AUM growth:** $5.48T in ETF AUM linked to S&P DJI indices. This revenue stream (65% of Indices revenue) is a direct function of global equity market levels and passive investing flows. Secular growth in passive investing supports the long-term trajectory, though market drawdowns would compress revenue proportionally.

**IHS Markit synergy completion:** The integration is effectively complete. The company achieved $600M+ in cost synergies (versus the original $600M target), and ongoing operating leverage should continue driving margin expansion, particularly in Market Intelligence and Mobility.

**Private markets expansion:** The $1.8B acquisition of With Intelligence (November 2025) targets the private markets data space, a key growth adjacency as private credit and alternatives expand. This is a direct response to the structural threat that private credit growth poses to the traditional ratings business.

### Medium-Term Catalysts

**Mobility spin-off (mid-2026):** The separation of Mobility ($1.75B revenue, 22% margin) should allow RemainCo to trade at a multiple more reflective of its higher-margin ratings/indices/commodity insights profile. Mobility Global Investor Day on May 12, 2026 will establish the standalone valuation narrative. Management allocated $303M in annual intangible amortization to Mobility, meaning RemainCo's reported margins should improve post-separation.

**Vitality Index:** Management's proprietary metric tracking revenue from products launched in the last three years. This measures the innovation pipeline's commercial success, and the 10-K indicates this is a key internal KPI.

### Long-Term Structural Tailwinds

Global capital markets growth, expansion of rated debt in emerging markets, continued secular shift to passive investing, and increasing demand for ESG/climate data all support mid-to-high single-digit revenue growth over the cycle. Commodity Insights benefits structurally from the current geopolitical environment—Platts benchmark pricing becomes more valuable when commodity markets are volatile and supply chains are disrupted.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$422, SPGI trades at approximately:
- 21.5x current-year consensus EPS ($19.62)
- 19.1x next-year consensus EPS ($22.07)
- ~82x trailing GAAP EPS (distorted by non-recurring items; adjusted EPS is the relevant metric)
- ~23.2x trailing adjusted EPS ($18.20)

The analyst consensus target is $539 (median $550), implying 28% upside from current levels. The low target of $480 sits 14% above the current price; the high target of $625 is 48% above.

Relative to peers in the financial data/analytics space, SPGI at ~21x forward earnings trades at a substantial discount to the peer median near 35x. Moody's (MCO), the closest comparable, typically trades at a 5-15% premium to SPGI due to its slightly higher organic growth rate but narrower business mix. At current levels, SPGI's discount to both its own historical multiple and the peer group is wider than average.

The discount reflects legitimate AI disruption concerns, but the market appears to be applying a single discount factor across the entire business rather than differentiating between segments with vastly different vulnerability profiles. Ratings and Indices together constitute ~43% of revenue and ~65% of segment operating profit. These businesses face minimal AI disruption risk and warrant premium multiples.

A sum-of-the-parts framework suggests:
- Ratings at 25x operating profit: ~$76B
- Indices at 30x operating profit (net of NCI): ~$39B
- Commodity Insights at 20x operating profit: ~$19B
- Market Intelligence at 15x operating profit: ~$22B
- Mobility at 12x operating profit: ~$5B
- Less: Net debt ($11.4B), NCI ($4.9B)
- **Implied equity value: ~$146B, or ~$478/share** on 305M diluted shares

This is conservative—it applies a significant AI-risk discount to Market Intelligence and Mobility while still yielding 13% upside from current levels. At peer-median multiples, the SOTP would exceed $600/share.

## Competitive Landscape

SPGI's competitive position varies significantly by segment:

**Impregnable:** Ratings (duopoly with MCO, NRSRO regulatory barrier), Indices (S&P 500 is the world's most important equity benchmark, with natural monopoly characteristics)

**Very Strong:** Commodity Insights/Platts (one of two dominant price reporting agencies, benchmarks embedded in physical contracts)

**Strong but Contested:** Market Intelligence (faces Bloomberg, LSEG Refinitiv, FactSet, and emerging AI-native competitors), Mobility (faces increasing competition)

The AI disruption debate centers on Market Intelligence, where the risk is real. Advanced language models can process financial data, generate research, and build analytical frameworks that partially replicate what Capital IQ Pro and similar products offer. However, several factors mitigate this risk: (1) workflow integration creates meaningful switching costs; (2) proprietary datasets (not just analytics) are the core value proposition; (3) regulated financial institutions require auditable, compliant data sources; (4) the 10-K notes the company is actively investing in AI-enabled products using its own data advantage.

Businesses that sell data and benchmarks are less vulnerable to AI disruption than businesses that sell analysis. SPGI's highest-margin businesses fall in the former category.

## Risk Assessment

### AI Disruption (Moderate-High, Concentrated)
The most prominent risk and the primary driver of the stock's underperformance. The 10-K explicitly warns that "third parties could use our data with AI tools to create their own insights and potentially supplant our products and services" and that "pervasive data leakage could render our capabilities redundant over time." This risk is concentrated in Market Intelligence (32% of revenue). Ratings, Indices, and Commodity Insights are structurally insulated by regulatory status, benchmark embeddedness, and contractual reference pricing. A 20% revenue erosion in Market Intelligence (a severe scenario) would reduce total company revenue by ~6% and operating profit by ~3%, manageable given the margin profile of the remaining businesses.

### Bond Issuance Cyclicality (Moderate)
Ratings transaction revenue (~16% of total company revenue) is directly tied to debt issuance volumes. Oil above $100 on the Hormuz blockade, rising credit spreads, and 45-50% recession probability create headwinds for new corporate issuance. Refinancing of maturing debt provides a floor, and widening spreads paradoxically increase the value of credit ratings to investors. Net effect: modestly negative near-term, neutral-to-positive medium-term.

### Mobility Spin-Off Execution (Moderate)
Expected mid-2026 but faces standard execution risks: timing delays, stranded costs, loss of synergies, tax complications, and the risk that combined post-separation market caps undershoot the pre-separation value. The $42M in executive leadership transition costs in 2025 suggests meaningful organizational restructuring is already underway.

### Regulatory Intensification (Low-Moderate)
DORA, EU AI Act, ESG ratings regulation, and expanding benchmark rules all increase compliance costs. However, regulatory complexity reinforces SPGI's moat—incumbents with the scale to absorb compliance costs benefit relative to smaller competitors.

### CME Put Option (Low-Moderate)
CME Group's put option on its S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC stake ($4.9B redeemable NCI) represents a contingent cash obligation. Exercise would require significant cash deployment but is manageable given annual FCF.

### Credit Environment Deterioration (Elevated)
Credit cascade probability at 58-68% based on five independent stress channels (private credit redemptions, public credit outflows, AI-driven leveraged loan divergence, private credit fund bonds at year-lows, and blockade-driven energy mark-to-market losses) creates genuine headwind for Ratings transaction revenue and Market Intelligence subscription renewals. SPGI partially offsets this through countercyclical demand: spreads widening increases demand for credit analysis, risk tools, and price discovery.

### Options Market Signal

Short interest is minimal at 1.2% of float with 1.6 days to cover, indicating no significant bearish institutional positioning. The stock's ~28% discount to consensus mean target and the tight analyst range (all above current price) suggest the selloff has been driven by broad thematic selling (AI fear) rather than fundamental deterioration. No elevated put activity or unusual options signals are present to contradict the fundamental thesis.

- At 23-24x FY2027 EPS of $22.07: $507-$530

## Conclusion

S&P Global at ~$422 represents a high-quality franchise trading at an uncharacteristic discount to both its own history and the peer group. The AI disruption fear driving the selloff is legitimate for Market Intelligence (32% of revenue, 20% margin) but substantially overstated for the three highest-margin businesses (Ratings, Indices, Commodity Insights), which together generate ~65% of segment operating profit behind regulatory, benchmark, and contractual moats.

The financial profile is exceptional: 42% operating margin expanding across all segments, $5.1B in annual FCF on minimal capex, well-laddered debt, and consistent capital returns. The Mobility spin-off in mid-2026 provides a specific catalyst for value realization. The Q4 2025 earnings miss (-2.6%) and modestly negative 90-day estimate revisions warrant monitoring but do not constitute a fundamental deterioration pattern against three prior beats and stable 30-day estimates.

At 21.5x current-year earnings with a 2:1 to 3:1 risk/reward skew to the base and bull cases, SPGI is a **BUY**. Accumulate at current levels, with additional conviction warranted if the Mobility Investor Day on May 12, 2026 establishes a compelling standalone narrative.
