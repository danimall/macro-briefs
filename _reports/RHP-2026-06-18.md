**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ryman Hospitality Properties owns the most differentiated asset class in lodging REITs: nine large-box, group-oriented convention resorts (~11,869 rooms) anchored by five Gaylord properties, each with 400,000+ square feet of meeting space, plus the Grand Ole Opry and Ryman Auditorium entertainment franchise. The "everything-under-one-roof" convention format is hard to replicate, group business books years in advance, and the brand assets are irreplaceable. This is a quality franchise, and the most consequential recent development is positive: Q1 2026 net definite group room nights booked rose 37.9% (same-store +18.1%), the strongest first-quarter group bookings production since 2018, which drove a full-year guidance raise.

The complication is that the headline financials overstate the organic story. Total Q1 2026 revenue grew 13.2% YoY, but most of that came from the June 2025 Desert Ridge acquisition ($73.9M of revenue in its first full quarter). Strip Desert Ridge out and same-store Total RevPAR grew only 2.8%, with same-store occupancy falling 2.0 points to 67.7% and same-store group rooms traveled down 4.1%. ADR strength (+5.1%) confirms pricing power, but volumes are softer than the headline suggests, and consensus EPS estimates have been drifting down (current-quarter estimate cut 8.4% over 90 days). The forward-booking acceleration and the realized same-store occupancy decline point in opposite directions; resolving that tension is the central question for the stock.

After a 26% rally over the trailing year to ~$122, the stock trades at roughly 32x trailing earnings and ~3.9% yield, near the top of the $112-137 analyst target range, and CBRE downgraded to Hold in May on valuation. The franchise quality and booking trajectory justify continuing to own RHP; the valuation, the acquisition-flattered organic picture, the leveraged balance sheet repricing higher, and a late-cycle consumer backdrop argue against adding at this level. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

RHP operates two segments. **Hospitality** (~88% of Q1 2026 revenue) comprises five Gaylord resorts (Opryland in Nashville, Palms in Orlando, Texan in Dallas, National near Washington DC, Rockies in Denver) and two JW Marriott resorts (Hill Country in San Antonio, acquired 2023; Desert Ridge in Phoenix, acquired June 2025 for ~$865M), plus two small overflow hotels. All hotels are managed by Marriott under long-term management agreements; RHP owns the real estate but does not operate the properties.

**Entertainment / Opry Entertainment Group (OEG)** (~12% of revenue) is ~70% owned by RHP, with Atairos and NBCUniversal as minority partners. It includes the Grand Ole Opry, Ryman Auditorium, WSM-AM, Ole Red and Category 10 branded venues, Block 21 in Austin, festivals, and recently managed amphitheaters (Ascend, CCNB).

The strategic model is consolidation of trophy group-convention assets (Hill Country 2023, Desert Ridge 2025) combined with a >$1B multi-year reinvestment program in existing properties (Opryland meeting-space expansion, Texan and Hill Country renovations), funded by REIT-level leverage and retained cash flow above the distribution requirement.

## Financial Analysis

**Top-line and cash-flow metrics are growing robustly on a reported basis.** Q1 2026 total revenue reached $664.6M, up 13.2% YoY; Hospitality revenue rose 17.6%. Operating income was $137.8M (+18.7%), net income to common $70.5M (+11.9%), diluted EPS $1.03 versus $1.00. The REIT cash-flow metrics that matter most grew faster: Adjusted EBITDAre $219.3M (+18.2%) and Adjusted FFO $156.1M (+19.2%). Operating cash flow nearly doubled to $169.2M from $98.2M, partly on working-capital timing.

**Organic quality is more muted.** Same-store ADR rose 5.1%, but same-store occupancy fell 2.0 points to 67.7% and same-store group rooms traveled fell 4.1%. Same-store Total RevPAR grew only 2.8%. Property-level dispersion was wide: Opryland (+16.5% revenue) and Palms (+10.5%) were strong, while Gaylord National (-8.2%, occupancy -9.4 points, partly Winter Storm Fern), JW Marriott Hill Country (-9.0%), and Gaylord Texan (-3.5%, under renovation) were weak. Desert Ridge contributed $73.9M of revenue and $24.3M of operating income with a $863.95 Total RevPAR, well above the same-store $497.95, an early sign the acquisition is accretive.

**The balance sheet is leveraged, consistent with the asset class.** Total debt is ~$3.97B against total equity of $0.77B. The low Altman Z-Score that screens flag is a function of REIT leverage and a large depreciation base, not a solvency signal. 88% of debt is fixed-rate after swaps at a weighted-average 6.4%, with no maturities until July 2028. Liquidity is comfortable: $424M unrestricted cash plus $930M of revolver availability. Recent liability management extended the maturity profile but raised the cost: the company redeemed $700M of 4.75% 2027 notes and replaced them with $700M of 5.75% 2034 notes in March 2026, expanded the revolver to $850M, and raised the permitted net-leverage covenant to 7.25x in January. Interest expense rose 18% YoY. This is prudent maturity management at a structurally higher cost of debt.

**Earnings delivery is mixed.** Over the last four quarters RHP posted two beats and two misses: Q1 2026 beat by 26.5% and Q3 2025 by 20.2%, but Q4 2025 missed by 8.9% and Q2 2025 by 6.0%. The lumpiness reflects the event- and weather-driven nature of the business and argues against high confidence in any single quarter's consensus.

**The dividend is substantial but consumes most distributable cash.** At $1.20/quarter (~$304M annualized) against AFFO running near $156M in a strong Q1, coverage is adequate on a full-year basis but leaves limited cushion alongside $350-450M of guided 2026 capex. The ~3.9% yield is below the 6-7% offered by transient-heavy lodging peers, reflecting the market's preference for RHP's growth and quality.

## Growth Analysis

The bull case rests on the booking acceleration. Total net definite group room nights booked rose 37.9% YoY in Q1 2026 (same-store +18.1%), the strongest first-quarter group production since 2018, and group room nights on the books for all future years are up 2.0% at ADRs up 5.4%. This reverses the more cautious group-booking commentary in the FY2025 10-K and drove the full-year guidance raise. Group business books years ahead, so a strong booking quarter improves revenue visibility well into 2027-2028.

Two offsets temper the growth read. Realized same-store occupancy fell 2 points even as bookings accelerated, so the forward pipeline has not yet shown up in trailing volumes; the gap between booked and traveled room nights is the tension to watch. Consensus EPS estimates are drifting lower: the current-quarter estimate was cut 8.4% over 90 days and the current-year estimate 2.6%, while only the next-year estimate is rising (+5.7%). The near-term estimate revision pattern is mixed-to-negative and does not support a re-rating thesis driven by accelerating bookings.

OEG is a smaller, more volatile growth lever. Segment operating income fell 58.8% YoY in Q1 against a tough comp (the Opry's 100-year celebration plus weather). Expansion continues — Ascend and CCNB amphitheater management, Category 10 Las Vegas opening late 2026, a Universal Orlando CityWalk location in late 2027 — but the segment is event-driven and thin-margined.

Acquisitions (Hill Country, Desert Ridge) plus the >$1B reinvestment program are the clearest structural growth driver, and Desert Ridge's above-portfolio RevPAR suggests the consolidation strategy is working. The constraint is that this growth is capital-intensive and increasingly debt-funded at higher coupons.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$122, RHP trades at ~32x trailing EPS of $3.77 and roughly 29x the current-year consensus of $4.29. On the REIT-relevant metric, the stock is more reasonably priced: annualizing Q1 AFFO of $156M against a ~$7.8B equity value implies a mid-teens P/AFFO multiple, in line with quality lodging REITs. Peer P/E comparisons are noisy given negative earnings at PK and PEB and a 107x figure at SHO; the cleaner comparables (APLE 22.3x, DRH 25.1x) suggest RHP trades at a premium that its superior asset quality and growth visibility partly justify but do not fully explain.

The dividend yield of ~3.9% sits above the DRH/SHO 3.1% but well below APLE (6.1%) and PK (7.2%), placing RHP at the growth end of the sector. That is appropriate, but it limits the yield-support argument that anchors valuations elsewhere in lodging.

The stock has already closed most of the gap to fair value. The consensus mean target is $124 and median $125, both essentially at the current price, with a $112-137 range. CBRE downgraded to Hold in May explicitly on valuation, noting consensus implied only single-digit upside after strong TSR outperformance. With near-term estimates drifting down and the stock at the upper end of the analyst range, recent revision action points to downside rather than stale targets waiting to be revised up. We score valuation below neutral: the price reflects the good news, and the asymmetry is no longer attractive.

## Competitive Landscape

RHP's moat is real and improving at the margin. The Gaylord large-box convention format — 400,000+ square feet of meeting space combined with lodging, F&B, retail and spa under one roof — cannot be easily replicated given the land, cost, and operational complexity involved, and there are few comparable assets and few buyers or sellers in the segment. Group meetings book years in advance, giving RHP better forward visibility than transient-dependent hotels. The Grand Ole Opry and Ryman Auditorium are irreplaceable cultural assets with no substitute.

The company has outperformed its closest benchmark, the FTSE NAREIT Lodging Resorts index, on total shareholder return (cumulative TSR 160.06 vs peer-group 137.83). Consolidation of trophy assets (Hill Country, Desert Ridge) deepens the moat in a segment with limited supply. The trajectory is one of widening scale advantage, which supports a 7/10 competitive score.

The structural dependence on Marriott as third-party manager is the principal qualifier: RHP's operating results hinge entirely on Marriott's execution, with embedded covenants (mortgage debt capped at 75% of FMV, operating-profit-to-debt-service ≥1.2x on pooled hotels). This is a contractual constraint rather than a competitive erosion, but it means RHP does not control the operating lever directly.

## Risk Assessment

**Group/convention demand cyclicality.** The entire thesis rests on corporations and associations committing to large meetings years ahead. Cancellations, attrition, and macro/geopolitical uncertainty hit results directly, and management's own forward-looking statements flag geopolitical uncertainty, inflation, and trade-policy effects on group customers. Group budgets are a discretionary line item that can soften in a downturn.

**Concentration.** With only nine hotels, a single property's renovation, weather event, or local-market softness moves consolidated results, as Q1 demonstrated. Gaylord National's DC-area exposure (-8.2% revenue, -9.4 points occupancy) is a specific vulnerability to government and association travel and budget dynamics.

**Leverage and rising financing costs.** ~$4B of debt is repricing higher (4.75%→5.75% on the latest refinancing), interest expense is up 18% YoY, and the leverage covenant was deliberately loosened to 7.25x. At higher-for-longer rates, the leveraged model carries a structurally rising cost of capital that compresses per-share growth.

**OEG put rights.** The ~30% OEG minority holders (Atairos/NBCUniversal) hold put rights that could require RHP to fund a buyout in cash or stock (~4.4M dilutive shares in the if-converted EPS calculation), a latent capital-structure overhang.

**Same-store occupancy decline.** The 2-point same-store occupancy fall and 4.1% drop in group rooms traveled, even as bookings accelerated, is the cleanest near-term risk that the headline growth masks softer organic volumes.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 24.3% is roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 23.6% (fair, 0.7% spread) and sits at the 5th percentile within the current chain; the options market is pricing complacency, not stress. The term structure is in contango (24.3% near vs 29.0% long-term), normal. The near-term put/call skew of 5.0% indicates modestly elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio of 4.50 is high, but the put/call open-interest ratio is low at 0.27 and has fallen sharply from 1.22 since early May, alongside a 13.6% price rise; net positioning has turned less hedged as the stock rallied. There is no options signal of an imminent negative catalyst; the modest near-term put skew is consistent with ordinary downside protection on a stock near its highs.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$137, +12%).** The 37.9% booking surge converts into traveled room nights through 2026-2027, lifting same-store occupancy back toward the low 70s and validating the guidance raise. Desert Ridge continues to run above-portfolio RevPAR, the reinvestment program lifts rates at Opryland and the renovated Texan/Hill Country, OEG comps normalize, and AFFO compounds at a high-single-digit rate. The scarcity value of the asset class supports a premium multiple, and the stock re-rates to the top of the analyst range.

**Bear Case (~$95-100, -18% to -22%).** A weaker consumer/group-budget backdrop materializes in H2 2026, group attrition rises, and the booked-vs-traveled gap resolves toward the weaker traveled figure rather than the stronger booking number. Gaylord National's DC weakness persists, rising interest expense compresses AFFO per share, and the stock derates toward peer lodging multiples as the growth premium erodes. A weak quarter or a guidance walk-back would accelerate the move.

**Base Case (~$124-125, roughly flat).** The booking strength and same-store occupancy softness roughly offset, AFFO grows in the mid-single digits, the dividend is maintained, and the stock tracks sideways near consensus fair value while the market waits to see whether the forward pipeline converts. The ~3.9% yield provides modest total-return support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The franchise quality and booking trajectory justify continuing to own the position; the valuation, acquisition-flattered organic picture, and leveraged balance sheet repricing higher argue against adding at ~$122.

**Base case target: $124-125** — approximately current consensus mean/median, derived from a mid-teens P/AFFO multiple on ~$600M of full-year AFFO, with the booking strength and same-store occupancy decline offsetting. Roughly flat from current.

**Bull case target: $137** (+12%) — top of the analyst range if booked room nights convert and same-store occupancy recovers.

**Bear case target: $95-100** (-18% to -22%) — peer-lodging multiple if the consumer/group backdrop weakens and AFFO per share compresses on rising interest expense.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the next two quarterly prints (early August 2026 and early November) the key checkpoints for whether bookings convert to traveled room nights.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Same-store occupancy recovers to ≥70% with same-store Total RevPAR growth above 5%, confirming the booking pipeline is converting.
- Current-year EPS/AFFO estimate revisions turn positive over a 90-day window.
- A pullback to the low-$100s that restores a double-digit margin of safety to the bull case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store group rooms traveled deteriorates further (worse than -4% YoY) for two consecutive quarters, signaling group-budget contraction.
- A guidance cut on the full-year outlook, particularly if driven by group cancellations/attrition rather than weather.
- Net leverage approaching the 7.25x covenant, or a dividend reduction.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026), with interim monitoring of Gaylord National's DC trajectory and any movement on the OEG put rights.

## Conclusion

Ryman owns the best collection of group-convention assets in lodging, with a genuine moat, strong pricing power, and a positive forward-booking trajectory. Those qualities support continuing to hold the shares but, at ~$122, do not support adding. The headline growth is heavily flattered by the Desert Ridge acquisition while same-store occupancy fell, near-term consensus estimates are drifting lower, the balance sheet is repricing higher, and the stock sits at the top of its analyst range after a 26% run, with CBRE already downgraded on valuation. The asymmetry that would justify a BUY is no longer present; the asymmetry that would justify an AVOID has not yet appeared. We rate RHP HOLD, with the August and November prints as the decisive tests of whether the booking surge converts into realized occupancy or fades into a weaker demand environment.
