## Executive Summary

Entegris supplies the consumable materials and contamination-control products that flow through semiconductor fabs on a per-wafer basis — CMP slurries and pads, deposition precursors, specialty gases, liquid and gas filtration, FOUPs and reticle pods, and fluid-handling hardware. After three years in which reported revenue went nowhere ($3,282M in 2022 to $3,196.6M in 2025, obscured by three divestitures and an alliance termination), the business has inflected. Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 5.0% to $811.9M, Q2 FY2026 grew 11.5% to $883.2M with non-GAAP EPS of $0.93 against an $0.83 non-GAAP consensus, and Q3 guidance of $905-935M implies roughly 13-14% year-over-year growth at the midpoint. Advanced Purity Solutions (APS) led, with segment margin reported at 30.3% versus 24.1%. Consensus EPS has risen 7.5% for FY2026 and 6.1% for FY2027 over 90 days, with the last two quarters beating by 13.1% and 11.0%.

Three things temper the print. First, in January 2026 Entegris extended PP&E useful lives, reducing FY2026 depreciation by approximately $72.9M — roughly $0.20 per diluted share — and that benefit flows through GAAP earnings, non-GAAP EPS, and segment margins, but not through Adjusted EBITDA or operating cash flow. In Q1 FY2026 the split was visible: Adjusted Operating Income +12%, non-GAAP EPS +28%, Adjusted EBITDA +2% with margin down 0.7pp to 27.8%. Second, the effective tax rate was 1.1% in Q1 (7.1% for FY2025); each 500bp of normalization is roughly $0.13 per share. Third, the recovery is being led by the fab-construction-linked products (fluid handling, FOUPs, "Equipment and Engineering" customer revenue +20.6% in Q1) that caused the 2025 trough, which makes 2026 growth more cyclically contingent than the "predominantly unit-driven or recurring" framing suggests. Materials Solutions (MS) — which carries $3,631.3M of the company's $3,947.6M goodwill and the lower segment margin — grew segment profit just 1% in Q1.

At $137.715 the equity is $21.0B; adding $3.25B of net debt gives an enterprise value near $24.25B, or roughly 27x trailing Adjusted EBITDA of $891.6M and about 24x an illustrative FY2026 EBITDA near $1.0B. Consensus puts FY2026 EPS at $3.67 (37.5x) and FY2027 at $4.70 (29.3x), a PEG close to 1.0 on next-year growth but one that leans on the depreciation change, a sub-10% tax rate and falling interest expense as much as on operating leverage. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and I am comfortable that the bar is met: fundamentals have improved since the June review while the price is 23% lower than the June 18 level and 26% below the 52-week high of $186.94, so risk/reward is better — just not asymmetric enough to add at a multiple that already embeds multi-year acceleration, on 3.65x net leverage, negative tangible book equity, and worsening customer and geographic concentration.

## Company Overview

Entegris is a Delaware entity formed in 2005 from the Entegris/Mykrolis merger, with roots in Fluoroware (1966). It acquired ATMI in 2014 and CMC Materials in July 2022 — the transaction that defines the current balance sheet. It sells to fabs, OEM equipment makers, chemical and gas suppliers, and wafer growers. TSMC is a customer (16% of FY2025 net sales), not a partner. Danaher's Pall unit competes in filtration; Merck KGaA (EMD), Air Liquide, Linde, Qnity Electronics, Anji Microelectronics and Mersen compete in materials.

Two segments (FY2025): Materials Solutions, $1,406.7M of sales and $276.6M of segment profit (19.7%), covering deposition precursors, CMP slurries and pads, post-CMP cleans, selective etch, ion-implant gases and graphite; and Advanced Purity Solutions, $1,799.1M and $426.4M (23.7%), covering liquid microcontamination control, gas purification, microenvironments (FOUPs, EUV reticle pods) and fluid management.

Management's stated growth engine is content per wafer, with molybdenum interconnect at advanced nodes as the sharpest expression: Entegris can supply the precursor, the CMP slurry and pad, the post-CMP clean, the selective etch and the filtration for a single material transition. Engineering, research and development spending has risen to $329.0M, or 10.3% of sales, from 7.9% in 2023 — a rising R&D burden on a flat revenue base, which is either investment ahead of the transition or evidence that the transition is expensive to win.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY2025 net sales fell $44.6M to $3,196.6M, decomposed by management as −$33.9M divested Pipeline & Industrial Materials, −$14.2M volume, +$3.5M FX — approximately flat organically. Gross margin fell 1.5pp to 44.4%, attributed to plant performance and higher depreciation, which had climbed from $135.4M (2022) to $205.3M (2025) as the Kaohsiung, Colorado Springs and Korea capital cycle landed in cost of sales. FY2025 also carried $29.7M of restructuring (versus $3.9M in 2024), with $4.1M more in Q1 FY2026 — a multi-quarter program.

Q1 FY2026 gross margin was 46.9% versus 46.1%, GAAP operating income $141.6M (17.4%), diluted GAAP EPS $0.60, non-GAAP EPS $0.86. Reported depreciation fell to $34.1M from $49.9M despite new asset additions.

**The useful-life change is the single most important accounting fact.** The disclosed FY2026 effects are approximately $72.9M less depreciation, allocated +$52.4M to gross margin, −$11.4M to ER&D expense, and −$9.1M to ending inventory. The Q1 actual was $19.0M less depreciation, about $8.0M of net income and roughly $0.05 per share — about 26% of the $0.19 year-over-year non-GAAP EPS increase. Because the company's non-GAAP definition adds back only acquired-intangible amortization and special items, the benefit is inside non-GAAP EPS. Adjusted EBITDA adds depreciation back, so it is unaffected; operating cash flow is unaffected. The Q1 wedge quantifies the issue: Adjusted Operating Income $192.0M (+12%), Adjusted EBITDA $226.1M (+2%), Adjusted EBITDA margin 27.8% versus 28.5%. On the depreciation-insensitive measure, profitability margin was still going backwards year over year on 5% revenue growth.

The change may well be appropriate — a 5-to-10-year life for semiconductor-materials manufacturing equipment is short, the company documents a usage-and-obsolescence analysis, and guided FY2026 capex of ~$250M versus $299.2M spent in 2025 is consistent with longer economic lives. Note also that the primary incentive-compensation measure is Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of net sales, which the change does not touch. I draw no inference about motive. The practical implication for a valuation analyst is narrower: use EV/Adjusted EBITDA as the clean multiple, and discount roughly $0.20 of FY2026 non-GAAP EPS as non-operational. The same caution applies to the reported Q2 APS margin of 30.3% versus 24.1% — the current-period figure embeds the benefit and the year-over-year comparison is therefore not fully like-for-like.

**Tax.** Effective rates were 8.8% (FY2024), 7.1% (FY2025) and 1.1% (Q1 FY2026), driven by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's §163(j) and FDII/GILTI changes, releases of unrecognized tax benefits and income mix. On roughly $400M of annualized pretax income and 153.2M diluted shares, each 500bp of rate is about $20M, or $0.13 per share. Normalization from ~5% toward the low teens is a $0.20-0.26 annual headwind invisible in operating-margin commentary. With 82% international sales and ~$200M of interest expense, a permanently below-statutory rate is plausible; the discrete benefit releases are what should not recur.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $695.4M against $299.2M of capex, giving $396.2M of free cash flow (12.4% margin) and covering $235.6M of net income roughly 3x, with the gap explained by $205.3M depreciation, $184.4M intangible amortization and share-based compensation. Q1 FY2026 OCF of $183.0M and capex of $41.5M produced $141.5M of FCF, which should not be annualized: accrued interest payable rose $40.6M on semiannual coupon timing (cash interest paid was only $4.6M) and cash taxes were $2.1M versus $19.2M. Normalizing those two items gives roughly $125M of OCF, and capex is heavily back-half weighted against the ~$250M guide. A reasonable FY2026 band is $470-530M of FCF against $61M of dividends — a step up from FY2025, and a 2.2-2.5% yield on a $21.0B market capitalization. Recurring excess-and-obsolete inventory charges ($13.9M in Q1'26, $11.8M in Q1'25) are a real cost of a consumables business through technology transitions.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/28/2026: cash $442.7M, par debt $3,695.0M, goodwill $3,947.6M, intangibles $860.7M, equity $4,049.2M, current ratio 3.21. Net debt $3,252.3M against TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $891.6M is 3.65x. The stack is a $400M term loan B due 2029 (SOFR+175, 5.43%), $1,600M 4.75% secured notes due 2029, $895M 5.95% unsecured due 2030, $400M 3.625% unsecured due 2029 and $400M 4.375% unsecured due 2028, a weighted coupon near 4.95%. Nothing matures before 2028. On 2026-04-29 the revolver was upsized to $750M and extended to April 2031; it was undrawn at both period ends, though $567.0M was drawn and repaid intra-year in 2025 and $65.0M in Q1 2026, indicating active working-capital use. Coverage is 4.44x on FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA/interest and 2.28x on GAAP operating income/interest.

Two structural points. Tangible book equity is approximately −$900M ($3,953.4M equity less $3,946.7M goodwill and $906.9M intangibles at 12/31/2025) — a normal artifact of the CMC purchase accounting, but it means the equity cushion is earnings power, not assets. And only $400M of prepayable term loan remains; once retired, further gross deleveraging requires bond tenders, so the path to management's ~3x year-end target runs through cash accumulation and EBITDA growth. On the FY2026 FCF band, 3.0-3.2x by year-end is achievable but is a target, not a covenant.

A screening note worth correcting: a distress-zone Altman Z of 1.68 circulates for ENTG, but that calculation substitutes book equity for the market-equity/total-liabilities term. With standard inputs — market equity ~$21.0B over total liabilities of $4,397.1M — X4 contributes about 2.87 and Z lands near 3.83, in the safe zone. At the 2025 low of $62.92 the same formula gives roughly 2.30, grey zone. The score is dominated by the share price; the economically meaningful terms are the low EBIT/assets (5.5%) and asset turnover (0.38), which reflect genuine capital intensity plus a goodwill-heavy denominator. Extending the Piotroski screen to all nine signals gives about 5/9, not "strong": ROA fell, gross margin fell, asset turnover slipped and share count rose.

## Growth Analysis

The inflection is documented and accelerating. Q1 FY2026 revenue +5.0% on +$37.5M of volume; Q2 FY2026 +11.5% to $883.2M with non-GAAP EPS $0.93 versus $0.83 non-GAAP consensus; Q3 guidance $905-935M, above the just-delivered quarter and reported as roughly 4% above consensus. On the reported 11.5% Q2 growth, Q2 FY2025 revenue was near $792M, so the $920M Q3 midpoint implies roughly 13-14% year over year (my arithmetic on a rounded growth rate). Estimate revisions confirm: current quarter +6.4% over 90 days, FY2026 +7.5% to $3.67, FY2027 +6.1% to $4.70. The revision magnitudes are solidly positive but well short of the +30% threshold that marks a violent trough-to-recovery re-rating; this is a steady upgrade cycle, not a step function, and the estimate table appears to predate the Q2 print, so near-term numbers should rise further.

Composition matters more than the headline. APS, the segment that fell 3% in FY2025 on "a decline in facilities-based capital expenditure investments," is leading: +24% segment profit in Q1, +17% revenue in Q2. The "Equipment and Engineering" customer category rose 20.6% in Q1 ($112.9M to $136.2M). MS, the segment with the CMP slurries, deposition precursors and specialty gases that anchor the content-per-wafer thesis, grew segment profit 1% in Q1. So the near-term upcycle is disproportionately fab-construction beta, and the structural story — molybdenum integration across five adjacent product lines, 2nm liquid filtration in production, HBM and advanced packaging — has yet to show up in MS segment economics. The 10-K also concedes less exposure to back-end assembly and test, which caps participation if packaging grows faster than front-end.

Visibility is thin by design. Order-to-delivery is short, contract liabilities were only $34.4M at 3/28/2026 (down from $49.9M), and the company discloses no backlog or remaining performance obligation. There is no contractual cushion to lean on if fab construction pauses.

The demand environment supports the direction. Zacks/Yahoo commentary in late July 2026 was constructive going into Q2 on AI-driven demand and improving wafer-fab spending, which is consistent with a multi-quarter consumables ramp, and Entegris' per-wafer exposure should compound as advanced-node process steps multiply. I score growth 6.5: two confirming prints plus a guide above the delivered quarter, tempered by the cyclical composition of the recovery, a stagnant MS, and no order visibility.

On that basis ENTG screens in line to cheap, but the comparison is between trough denominators and tells you little. The EPS-based multiples look friendlier than the EBITDA multiple because three non-operating levers are pulling: approximately $0.20 from the depreciation estimate, roughly $0.05-0.10 from falling interest expense as the term loan amortizes, and a tax rate in the low single digits. Strip the depreciation benefit and normalize tax toward 12%, and FY2026 EPS is closer to $3.20-3.30, putting the stock at 42-43x. That is the number to hold in mind when the sell-side quotes 37x.

What would make the market wrong here? A durable move to mid-teens revenue growth with MS participating, EBITDA margin above 29-30%, net leverage at 3x and FCF above $550M would justify 24-26x EBITDA and support the $166 mean analyst target (+20.5%). That is a credible path — but it is the path already embedded, not a discount to it. The analyst-target-as-floor principle applies to cheap inflection stocks; at 37.5x current-year and 24x EBITDA, targets reflect the same acceleration assumption as the price rather than independent confirmation. The dispersion in the target range ($115 low to $205 high, a 78% spread) and the split ratings (1 strong buy, 5 buy, 4 hold, 1 sell) reflect exactly this disagreement about the multiple rather than about business quality.

Valuation score 5.0, up modestly from the prior review's 4.5. The improvement is entirely the arithmetic of a 23% lower price than mid-June against higher estimates; the multiple itself still offers no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is real and mechanical: products are specified into customer process recipes, requalification is customer-gated and slow, no single product platform exceeds 3% of net sales, and no global competitor spans the full portfolio. Roughly 1,600 of ~7,700 employees are in engineering and R&D with about 4,400 active patents. The regional manufacturing footprint (Kaohsiung Science Park, Colorado Springs with up to $77.0M of CHIPS support, a Korea Technology Center, plus expansions in Billerica, Yonezawa, Toronto, Chaska, JangAn, Aurora and Burnet) is itself a moat component in a business where local qualification and supply assurance matter — and is also the source of the depreciation load that compressed FY2025 gross margin.

The trajectory is more mixed than the level. Customer concentration is worsening and is disclosed as worsening: TSMC moved from 11% of net sales in 2023 to 16% in 2024 and 2025; top-ten customers from 43% to 48% to 50%; Taiwan is 23% of FY2025 and 26% of Q1 FY2026 revenue. Supply agreements typically carry no long-term purchase commitments, with "limited or no contractual recourse." Part of the increase is a shrinking denominator from the EC and PIM divestitures and part is genuine Taiwan growth (+18% in Q1), and a rising share from the leading-edge foundry is also a quality signal. What is not defensible is calling the portfolio streamlining "diversification" when the measured direct concentration is flat-to-worse. The 10-K is unusually explicit about mechanism: consolidation and localization may increase customer bargaining power and produce pricing pressure, more restrictive commercial terms, longer payment cycles, and demands for dual sourcing or rapid qualification of alternatives. Customer-mandated dual sourcing is the specific threat to the specified-in stickiness.

China is 21% of FY2025 sales (18% in Q1 FY2026) and is targeted by policy on both sides. Export controls have reduced sales, customers have moved to domestic sourcing, and two named competitors — Cobetter Filtration and Anji Microelectronics — are precisely the local substitutes that policy has incentivized. Governments also provide incentives for state-backed customers to buy local. Separately, Qnity Electronics appears in Entegris' own MS competitor list; I have not verified its scale or lineage, but if it represents a newly independent electronic-materials competitor of meaningful size in CMP and deposition, that is a material change to the competitive set in the segment carrying $3.63B of goodwill and the weaker growth. Competitive position 6.0 — a durable franchise with a deteriorating bargaining position on the customer side.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer and geographic concentration.** TSMC at 16%, top ten at 50%, Taiwan at 26% of Q1 revenue, with no long-term purchase commitments. A Taiwan disruption is a low-probability, extreme-impact event that no valuation absorbs; a TSMC capex pause is a higher-probability, high-impact one.

**MS goodwill.** Goodwill is $3,947.6M, of which $3,631.3M sits in Materials Solutions. MS required a quantitative test at 8/31/2025 (APS was tested qualitatively), and the disclosed sensitivity is only that a 50 basis point change in revenue CAGR, gross margin, discount rate or terminal growth would not produce an impairment. A 50bp disclosure is a floor on headroom, not headroom itself, and no impairment was recorded in 2024 or 2025. Still, the exposure is concentrated exactly where growth is weakest. If MS does not participate in this recovery, an MS impairment is the most plausible large non-cash charge on the horizon.

**Tax normalization.** A move from ~5% toward the low teens costs $0.20-0.26 per share annually and is invisible in operating commentary.

**Tariffs and trade.** The Q1 10-Q discloses that in February 2026 the Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs, with a refund process of uncertain timing and scope; new tariffs were then imposed under other authorities and Section 301 investigations opened across a broad range of products and partners. One specific exposure: foreign rules of origin may treat non-U.S.-manufactured Entegris output as U.S.-origin, exposing it to retaliation.

**PFAS.** Much of the product identity is fluoropolymer-based (FluoroPure, PTFE/PFA fluid handling). The 10-K warns that suitable PFAS replacements may not exist at similar performance and cost, "or at all." For this company that is more fundamental than its boilerplate placement suggests.

**Leverage and single-source inputs.** 3.65x net leverage, negative tangible book, and single- or limited-source dependencies on filtration membranes, polymer resins, engineered abrasives, specialty chemicals and petroleum coke, where requalification is customer-gated.

**Sector positioning.** Semiconductor indices have run hard into record positioning, and this is a high-beta consumables name inside that trade. A cohort-wide multiple compression would hit ENTG's 24x EBITDA harder than its fundamentals.

**Unresolved items.** Q2 FY2026 figures come from secondary reporting on 2026-08-04; the 10-Q was not available, so Q2 GAAP results, Adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow and updated leverage are unknown. "Semi Distributor and Other" revenue fell 28.4% year over year in Q1 ($70.0M to $50.1M) while every other category grew, with no explanation in the filing. The FY2025 10-K and Q1 10-Q describe different lower bounds for the new useful-life ranges, though the quantified effects agree.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 81.4% against 64.8% one-year realized is rich by 16.5 points, and the nearest expiry (16 days) still prices a ±14.2% move even though the Q2 print has already occurred — the market is pricing continued two-sided uncertainty rather than resolution. The near-term put/call skew of 5.1% is characterized as elevated put demand; the three- and six-month tenors carry negative skew values whose direction I will not assert without a stated sign convention. The term structure is in contango (81.4% near versus 84.1% at six months), which is the normal, non-panic configuration, and near-term IV sits at only the 8th percentile within the current chain.

The more informative change is positional: put/call open-interest ratio has moved from 0.06 on 2026-06-18 to 0.46 now, while ATM IV collapsed from 254.9% to 81.4% and the price fell 23.6% from $178.77. Hedging demand has been built up during the drawdown from a base of essentially none. Short interest is modest at 7.2% of float with 2.3 days to cover, so there is neither a crowded short nor squeeze support. For a HOLD, the takeaway is that options are not cheap enough to argue for buying upside exposure here, and premium selling into an 81% IV against 65% realized is the better-compensated side of this surface. Nothing in the options data contradicts the fundamental view.

Risk factors score 5.0: several of these exposures (MS impairment cushion, tax normalization, PFAS, rules-of-origin treatment) are specific, quantifiable in direction, and not visibly reflected in a 24x EBITDA multiple.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $190 (+38%).** The molybdenum transition converts into MS revenue in FY2027, lifting the weaker segment's growth and margin toward APS levels. Revenue grows mid-teens through FY2027 to roughly $4.0B, Adjusted EBITDA margin reaches 29-30% (~$1.18B), net leverage falls below 3.0x, and FCF exceeds $550M. FY2027 EPS beats the $4.70 consensus toward $5.10-5.20. At 25x EBITDA and ~$2.7B of net debt that is $187-195 per share, consistent with the $205 high analyst target. Requires MS participation and a low-teens or better tax outcome.

**Base case — $155 (+12.5%).** Revenue grows 12-13% in FY2026 to ~$3.57B and ~9-10% in FY2027 to ~$3.9B; Adjusted EBITDA margin holds near 28-29% (~$1.13B in FY2027); net debt falls to roughly $2.85B on $470-530M of FCF less $61M of dividends. At 23x FY2027 EBITDA, equity is about $23.1B, or $151 per share; on FY2027 consensus EPS of $4.70 the same target is 33x. Call it $155, roughly 7% below the $166 mean analyst target.

**Bear case — $97 (−30%).** Fab construction spending pauses in H2 FY2026 or FY2027, APS growth reverts, and MS stays flat. FY2027 EBITDA settles near $1.02-1.05B; the cohort derates and ENTG trades at 17-18x EBITDA. With net debt near $3.1B that is $14.8-15.8B of equity, or $97-104 per share. Layer on an MS goodwill impairment (non-cash, but a signal that would extend the derating) and tax normalization costing $0.20-0.26, and the 2025 trading range of $62.92-$109.53 becomes the reference frame rather than the exception.

The distribution is roughly symmetric in magnitude (+38% / −30%) with a base case of +12.5%. That is a hold, not a buy. It becomes a buy either at a lower price or on evidence that resolves the two open questions — MS participation and the durability of Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion excluding the depreciation change.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Reassessment point: the Q3 FY2026 report (late October / early November 2026), which will be the first quarter fully reflecting the new CFO and the first to test the $905-935M guide.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Adjusted EBITDA margin above 29% for two consecutive quarters** with the year-over-year improvement not explainable by the ~$18M/quarter depreciation benefit — that is, Adjusted EBITDA growth converging toward non-GAAP EPS growth rather than trailing it by 26 points as in Q1.
2. **MS segment profit growth above 10% year over year**, confirming that content-per-wafer economics (molybdenum, CMP, advanced deposition) are converting and simultaneously widening the disclosed goodwill headroom in the segment holding $3.63B of it.
3. **Price at or below $115-120** (approximately 20x forward EBITDA and 24-25x FY2027 consensus EPS) with FY2027 estimates still at or above $4.70 — the level at which the base case offers 30%+ upside against the same bear case. Note that $115 is also the low end of the published analyst target range.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A Q4 FY2026 or Q1 FY2027 revenue guide below the prior quarter's actual**, indicating the fab-construction leg of the recovery has rolled over — the same mechanism that produced the FY2025 APS decline.
2. **An MS goodwill impairment charge, or disclosure that the quantitative test's headroom has narrowed** below the 50bp sensitivity band.
3. **Net leverage failing to reach 3.2x or better by the Q4 FY2026 report**, which would mean FCF came in materially below the $470-530M band and that the deleveraging narrative underpinning the multiple has stalled — with only $400M of prepayable term loan left as a lever.
4. **TSMC or top-ten concentration rising further alongside evidence of price concessions** (gross margin down year over year ex-depreciation benefit), confirming the customer-bargaining-power mechanism the 10-K describes.

**Monitoring items without a defined threshold:** the Q2 FY2026 10-Q, to verify Adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow and leverage against the secondary reporting used here; the effective tax rate trajectory; any explanation of the 28.4% decline in "Semi Distributor and Other" revenue; and open-market insider purchases, which have been absent from the last 25 Form 4 line items.

## Conclusion

Entegris is a genuinely good franchise emerging from a flat three years into a real revenue acceleration, and the June-to-August decline from $178.77 to $137.72 has improved the entry arithmetic materially while estimates rose. Two consecutive beats, a Q3 guide above the delivered quarter, APS margin expansion, an extended and upsized revolver, no maturities before 2028, and a credible path from 3.65x to roughly 3.0x net leverage are all supportive.

The reasons not to add are specific rather than atmospheric. Roughly $0.20 of FY2026 EPS and part of the reported segment-margin improvement come from a January 2026 depreciation estimate change; on the measure that change does not touch, Q1 Adjusted EBITDA grew 2% with margin down 0.7pp. The tax rate of 1.1% cannot persist and costs $0.13 per share for every 500bp of normalization. The recovery is being led by the fab-construction-linked products that caused the 2025 trough, while the segment carrying 92% of the goodwill grew segment profit 1%.
