## Executive Summary

Krystal Biotech is a rare object in biotechnology: a profitable, self-funding, commercial-stage gene therapy company with 95% gross margins, zero debt, $1.10B of cash and investments, and a first-in-class approved product it owns outright with no licensor royalty. Cumulative VYJUVEK net product revenue reached $965.9M since the 2023 launch, and reported Q2 2026 revenue grew 24% year over year while operating income grew 48%. On those headline facts alone the stock looks like a compounder.

The Q2 2026 10-Q filed August 3 changed the picture in a way the press release did not. Note 3 disclosed geographic revenue disaggregation for the first meaningful comparative period: U.S. net revenue was $91.633M in Q2 2026 versus $96.042M in Q2 2025, a 4.6% decline, and $179.122M for H1 2026 versus $184.225M, down 2.8%. Backing out Q1 (H1 less Q2) gives Q1 2026 U.S. revenue of $87.489M against $88.183M — a 0.8% decline. The rate of U.S. contraction widened from Q1 to Q2. The U.S. is 76% of revenue. Management's MD&A confirms the mechanism in its own words: consolidated growth came from Europe and Japan launches, "partially offset by a reduction in United States VYJUVEK sales." Separately, Europe plus Japan revenue was $28.868M in Q1 2026 versus $27.589M in Q2 2026 (derived), a 4.4% sequential decline in markets launched only two to four quarters earlier. Consolidated revenue grew just 2.5% sequentially and missed consensus by roughly $1.7-1.8M. The stock fell 8.4% on the print.

We are lowering the score from 6.3 to 5.51 and maintaining HOLD for a fourth consecutive quarter. The downgrade is not a response to the 11.9% price decline since our July work — a lower price on unchanged fundamentals would improve risk/reward. It is a response to identifiable fundamental deterioration: the core U.S. franchise is shrinking, the international engine did not compound sequentially, next-year consensus EPS has been cut 3.2% over 90 days, and FY2026 opex guidance implies an H2 spending step-up of 11-35% versus H1 that will compress the operating leverage carrying the earnings story. Against that, the balance sheet, the 95% gross margin, three FDA platform technology designations, and a genuinely near-term, low-mechanism-risk KB803 registrational readout in Q4 2026 argue against a bearish position. At 31.7x next-year consensus and 18.4x EV/TTM sales, we do not think the current price offers asymmetry in either direction.

## Company Overview

Krystal is a fully integrated genetic medicines company in Pittsburgh, operating as a single reportable segment with 295 full-time employees. Its differentiation is architectural. Where most gene therapy uses adeno-associated virus or lentiviral vectors, Krystal engineers herpes simplex virus-1 vectors. The 10-K claims four consequent properties: redosability (evading the neutralizing immune response that makes AAV effectively single-shot), non-integration (episomal, avoiding insertional mutagenesis), payload capacity above 35 kilobases versus roughly 10 kb for AAV (permitting full-length *COL7A1* and *CFTR*), and high transduction efficiency. Delivery routes are topical gel, eye drop, nebulized inhalation, and intradermal injection. The FDA has partially validated the platform claim by granting platform technology designation to the engineered HSV-1 vector in KB801 (October 2025) and KB407/KB111 (April 2026).

VYJUVEK, approved in May 2023, was the first redosable gene therapy ever approved. It treats both recessive and dominant dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa, and the regulatory footprint has expanded to the EU (April 2025), Japan (July 2025), and the UK (May 2026). Krystal holds worldwide exclusive rights, commercializes directly in the U.S., major European markets and Japan, and carries composition and method patents stated to expire December 28, 2036 alongside orphan and market exclusivities.

Manufacturing is unusually vertically integrated: two in-house commercial-scale cGMP facilities (ANCORIS, ASTRA) in Pittsburgh, with third-party reliance confined to the sterile gel excipient. That integration is the mechanical reason gross margin runs at 95% and why cost of goods fell in absolute dollars in Q2 2026 ($6.437M versus $7.165M) despite higher volumes.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2023 $50.699M → FY2024 $290.515M (+473%) → FY2025 $389.130M (+34%) → H1 2026 $235.579M (+28%), with Q2 2026 at $119.222M (+24%). The deceleration is orderly on its face. Underneath it, the composition has inverted: 100% of the year-over-year increment now comes from Europe ($19.283M in Q2) and Japan ($8.306M), while the U.S. contributed negative $4.409M. The U.S. quarterly series has been range-bound for six quarters at roughly $88M, $96M, $87.5M, $91.6M. A reasonable reading is a plateau in the high-$80M to mid-$90M range, or $350-380M annualized, consistent with the company stating on the call that it has surpassed its initial 60% penetration target against roughly 3,000 identified U.S. DEB patients.

An important distinction: the mix shift toward Europe and Japan is not customer diversification. Per Note 3, Customer A fell from 66% to 54% of net accounts receivable while Customer B rose from 14% to 18%; the top two counterparties still represent 72% of receivables versus 80% at year-end 2025. Two customers remain above the 10% disclosure threshold.

**Margins and operating leverage.** Gross margin expanded to 95% from 93%. Q2 operating expenses grew 7% against 24% revenue growth, producing operating income of $58.417M (+48%); H1 operating income was $112.107M (+48%). R&D was essentially flat year over year ($14.518M versus $14.410M) while SG&A grew 14% to $39.850M on international build-out.

That leverage is unlikely to persist. Management guided FY2026 non-GAAP combined R&D plus SG&A to $175.0-195.0M, defined as GAAP R&D plus SG&A excluding stock compensation. H1 actuals on that basis were $82.947M (derived: GAAP $29.849M + $80.863M = $110.712M, less $27.765M SBC). The guidance therefore implies H2 non-GAAP opex of $92.1-112.1M, or +11% to +35% versus H1. Anyone extrapolating 48% operating income growth is modelling against management's own spending plan.

A bottom-up FY2026 build illustrates the tension with consensus. Assume full-year revenue of roughly $480-490M (H1 $235.6M plus H2 supported by Italy and Spain launches), 95% gross margin, GAAP opex of $231-251M (guided non-GAAP range plus roughly $55M of SBC), and interest income of approximately $45M on $1.10B of investments at a 17% effective tax rate. That produces EPS of roughly $6.90-7.50 on 30.7M diluted shares, below the $8.21 current-year consensus. Consensus is either assuming the low end of the opex range, a lower tax rate, or revenue above our range. The 30-day revision direction (current year -1.0%, next quarter -3.9%, next year -2.0%) is consistent with sell-side models moving toward, not away from, this arithmetic.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $155.171M versus $83.698M in H1 2025, though the prior period absorbed the final $31.25M PeriphaGen settlement payment, so the underlying improvement is closer to $40M. Derived TTM figures: revenue $440.484M, operating cash flow $272.338M, capex $14.556M, free cash flow approximately $257.8M — a 58.5% FCF margin, which is exceptional.

That figure requires a haircut rather than acceptance. Within H1 operating cash flow, accrued rebates contributed +$25.834M and other long-term liabilities +$3.462M — roughly $29.3M, or 19% of the total, from liability build. The Note 3 rollforward shows the rebate accrual balance rising from $61.905M at December 31, 2025 to $89.384M at June 30, 2026, with H1 provisions of $48.869M against payments and credits of only $21.390M. Normalizing H1 for the build gives approximately $126-128M of operating cash flow. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast, and says nothing about whether the accrual is appropriately sized. When rebates settle in cash, the tailwind reverses. On a normalized TTM basis FCF is closer to $228M, or a 2.5% yield on market cap.

The likely driver, stated as inference rather than fact: Germany launched August 2025 and France October 2025, with pricing negotiations continuing "until at least the second half of 2026 in Germany and 2027 in France." Germany's AMNOG framework permits a free launch price followed by a negotiated price, a structure that typically requires accruing for expected concessions. Equally plausible alternatives the filings do not exclude are ordinary growth in U.S. Medicaid and 340B obligations, or a change in the estimated U.S. rebate rate. Krystal does not disaggregate rebates by geography. Derived gross-to-net deduction for H1 was approximately 19% ($55.208M of total provisions against $235.579M net revenue, implying roughly $290.8M gross).

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $427.740M, short-term investments $417.891M, long-term investments $257.291M — $1,102.9M total against zero debt. Total liabilities of $141.522M are almost entirely operating items. Stockholders' equity $1,359.126M. Current ratio 8.32x, down from 9.95x, with the compression driven by the rebate accrual rather than liquidity deterioration. Altman Z of 9.21 on the FY2025 basis. Retained earnings turned positive in FY2025 and reached $134.863M. H1 financing activity was a net +$4.594M consisting only of option proceeds less RSU tax withholding — no equity raise, no debt, no dividend, no buyback.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 net income of $204.831M exceeded pretax income of $189.471M because the tax line was a net benefit of $15.360M following release of the valuation allowance against U.S. federal and state deferred tax assets; a $22.824M net DTA now sits on the balance sheet. H1 2025 recorded $16.305M of tax expense, implying roughly $31.7M of net benefit in H2 2025 (derived), and Q3 2025 diluted EPS of $2.66 against roughly $1.12 consensus is consistent with the discrete release landing predominantly there.

The consequence for valuation is material. TTM diluted EPS summed by quarter is $7.98 (Q3'25 $2.66 + Q4'25 $1.70 + Q1'26 $1.83 + Q2'26 $1.79), a reported P/E of 39.2x at $312.57. Stripping the estimated discrete release of $22.8-31.7M gives adjusted TTM EPS of roughly $6.85-7.14 and an adjusted P/E of 43.8x-45.6x. Effective tax rates are normalizing upward: 13.2% in H1 2026, 17.1% in Q2 alone. We note the supplied profile P/E field (41.86, with both trailing and forward identical) is internally inconsistent with the supplied EPS and price and have relied on derived figures throughout.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question reduces to three sub-questions, and only one has a dated answer.

**Can the U.S. franchise re-accelerate?** Not through new patient identification, on current evidence. Penetration above 60% of roughly 3,000 identified patients leaves limited headroom, and over 730 reimbursement approvals with over 640 unique prescribers describe access breadth that is largely built out. The credible path is more revenue per existing patient, which is precisely what KB803 offers: the same active agent (B-VEC) as an eye drop for DEB corneal abrasions, in a population the company says affects over 25% of DEB patients (more than 750 in the U.S., more than 2,000 worldwide) with no corrective therapy available. The registrational IOLITE study was fully enrolled in April 2026 with 16 patients and top-line data is on track for Q4 2026. Mechanism risk is low — approved agent, same patients, different tissue. Trial risk is not: 16 patients, a patient-reported primary endpoint (change in average days per month with corneal abrasion symptoms) in an intra-patient crossover, and a dosing schedule already modified "to reduce the potential impact of human error in eye drop administration."

**Can international compound?** Two quarters of data cannot establish a trend, and early rare-disease launches are lumpy — patient starts arrive in batches, and French *Accès Précoce* and pre-negotiation German revenue may be recognized net of provisional rebate estimates that shift quarter to quarter. But the observed sequential decline from $28.868M to $27.589M sits awkwardly against a thesis that rests entirely on international expansion. Italy and Spain launches remain expected before year-end 2026; Switzerland and Australia filings in H2 2026. UK pricing talks began only after the May 2026 approval.

**Can the pipeline become revenue this decade?** The larger opportunities are real and, in one case, much larger than DEB. KB801 for neurotrophic keratitis targets a U.S. population the company cites rising from roughly 31,000 in 2020 to 68,000 in 2024, against an incumbent (Dompé's Oxervate) requiring six daily doses; the registrational EMERALD-1 study (~60 patients, complete corneal healing at 8 weeks) should complete enrollment by year-end. KB407 for modulator-ineligible cystic fibrosis has FDA engagement on a novel registrational design using prospectively collected natural-history data to supplement placebo control, with alignment expected Q4 2026 and the study starting 2027. Inhaled KB707 in NSCLC carries RMAT designation and showed ORR 31% / DCR 75% in 16 efficacy-evaluable late-line patients at ASCO.

Against that, timelines slipped roughly a year across four programs between the FY2025 10-K and the Q2 10-Q: KB408 interim repeat-dose data (2026 → 2027), KB707 KYANITE-1 interim and registrational plans (2026 → 1H 2027), KB111 registrational start (H2 2026 → 2027), and Jeune's KB304 Phase 2 (→ 2027). Individually minor; collectively they push the multi-product transition further from a valuation that appears to price it.

On estimate revisions: the 90-day figures are positive (current quarter +9.0%, current year +8.3%) but the 30-day figures are negative across next quarter, current year, and next year, and next-year consensus is down 3.2% over 90 days. This is not the >+30% ninety-day revision pattern that reliably precedes further re-rating. Directionally, consensus is beginning to cut.

## Competitive Landscape

In DEB, Krystal is the incumbent with genuine modality advantages over Abeona's Zevaskyn (approved 2025): topical application versus surgical grafting, coverage of both recessive and dominant subtypes versus recessive only, home administration by patient or caregiver versus a Qualified Treatment Center. Chiesi's Filsuvez is palliative. Other named competitors — Castle Creek, Phoenix Tissue Repair, RHEACELL — are earlier.

Trajectory is what matters, and the trajectory is deteriorating at the margin. Zevaskyn's build-out is accelerating: Cincinnati Children's became the seventh Qualified Treatment Center in July 2026, and CMS granted New Technology Add-On Payment status under the FY2027 IPPS Final Rule. NTAP pays the treating hospital a supplemental Medicare amount rather than paying Abeona directly, but it materially reduces the hospital's financial disincentive to offer the therapy. The products are partially complementary — chronic topical therapy versus a one-time graft — but for the large chronic wounds that plausibly drive the highest VYJUVEK vial consumption, a durable graft is a substitute. In a market of roughly 3,000 U.S. patients, incremental share loss is arithmetically significant. We have no Zevaskyn revenue or patient data and no source connecting it to VYJUVEK's U.S. decline; the displacement hypothesis is untested.

Beyond DEB, Krystal enters markets with large incumbents but has chosen defensible wedges: the 10-15% of cystic fibrosis patients not amenable to Vertex modulators; a sustained-expression NK eye drop against a six-times-daily regimen; inhaled lung-directed AATD against systemic augmentation therapy from CSL, Takeda and Grifols. The aesthetics program through wholly-owned Jeune is cash-pay and enormous, and correspondingly the least de-risked with Phase 2 slipping to 2027.

## Risk Assessment

**U.S. revenue contraction is the highest-conviction risk and its cause is unresolved.** We could not obtain the Q2 earnings call transcript. Competing explanations — saturation above 60% penetration, gross-to-net erosion on U.S. sales, specialty distributor destocking (Customer A fell from 66% to 54% of receivables), fewer vials per patient as chronic wounds heal under sustained therapy, the September 2025 label change altering administration patterns, or competitive displacement — carry very different implications. Some are transient; saturation is not.

**European pricing is a quantitatively material overhang.** German negotiations run to at least H2 2026, France into 2027, UK talks just began, and France's launch is under a provisional early-access program. The rebate accrual build is the accounting manifestation. Adverse outcomes compress realized price in exactly the markets carrying consolidated growth.

**The DOJ inquiry has been open roughly six quarters.** Per Note 7, Q1 2025 subpoenas to the company and certain employees concerned "its sponsored genetic testing program relating to VYJUVEK and commercial practices relating thereto." No loss is estimable; unfavorable developments "could result in a material impact." Sponsored testing programs in rare disease are a recognized enforcement focus because testing sponsorship can implicate the Anti-Kickback Statute as patient-identification inducement. We found no external reporting. A hypothesis worth naming but not asserting: if the program was narrowed in response, U.S. patient identification would slow — which is one candidate explanation for the plateau. No filing draws that connection.

**New patent inventorship litigation on KB407.** Filed May 29, 2026 in D. Del. (1:26-cv-00628), seeking correction of inventorship under 35 U.S.C. §256 on two patents covering CFTR polypeptide delivery that the plaintiff alleges are embodied in KB407. Correction actions do not typically invalidate patents, but a successful claim can create co-ownership, and co-owners can independently license. KB407 heads toward a 2027 registrational start.

**Concentration.** 100% of revenue from one product; two customers at 72% of receivables; manufacturing in two facilities in one metropolitan area.

**Governance.** A Delaware Chancery derivative suit filed September 18, 2025 alleging excessive non-employee director compensation for 2021-2024 has settled subject to a September 14, 2026 fairness hearing, with five years of mandated governance reforms if approved. The financial cost is immaterial; the fact that director pay required court-supervised remediation is not. Two 8-K filings could not be read: an Item 8.01 filing of July 21, 2026 (thirteen days before earnings) and an Item 5.02 officer/director change of December 23, 2025. The identity of the December change is unknown.

**Dilution.** Reported weighted-average diluted shares of 30.657M are 3.8% above basic. Separately, awards outstanding at June 30, 2026 total 2,323,131 shares (1,975,104 options, 304,491 RSUs, 43,536 PSUs), equal to 7.85% of shares outstanding or 7.28% post-issuance ownership dilution if fully issued. Options are 60% exercisable at a weighted-average strike of $82.51. The 2017 Plan carries a 4% annual evergreen increase; unrecognized stock compensation is $153.2M over roughly three years, and SBC ran 11.8% of H1 revenue. Ongoing dilution is structural.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 45.1% is rich versus 39.1% one-year realized vol, but sits in the 13th percentile within the current chain, and IV compressed from 48.9% to 45.1% while the stock fell 11.9% — consistent with post-earnings uncertainty release rather than escalating fear. Put/call skew is balanced at -2.0% near-term, widening only modestly to -3.4% at six months, and put/call open interest of 0.45 is call-tilted. Term structure is flat (45.1% near, 46.9% far), placing the Q4 KB803 readout risk as evenly distributed rather than concentrated. The ±8.2% implied move on the August 21 expiry and ±21.4% at six months bracket a binary readout.

The more consequential positioning datum is short interest at 19.5% of float with 8.8 days to cover. This cuts both ways: it confirms institutional skepticism about the U.S. plateau, and it makes a positive KB803 readout a squeeze setup. The combination of high short interest, balanced options skew, and call-tilted open interest is the market pricing a genuinely two-sided binary — which is our read as well.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $460 (+47%)

KB803 hits its primary endpoint in Q4 2026, extending the DEB franchise into ocular complications affecting over 750 U.S. patients with no corrective alternative, and re-accelerating U.S. revenue from the installed patient base rather than requiring new patient identification. Italy and Spain launches plus favorable German and UK pricing outcomes restore sequential international compounding. KB801 completes enrollment and reads out into a U.S. NK population of roughly 68,000 patients — twice DEB — against a six-times-daily incumbent. FDA alignment on the KB407 natural-history control design de-risks cystic fibrosis. A 19.5% short base covers into the news. Applying 40x to FY2028 EPS of approximately $11.50 supports $460, consistent with the $474 high analyst target. The balance sheet funds all of this without dilution.

### Bear Case — $205 (-34%)

The U.S. decline proves structural rather than transient, worsening toward -8% to -10% as Zevaskyn's Qualified Treatment Center network and FY2027 NTAP economics take share in large chronic wounds. German and French price negotiations settle materially below launch prices, and the $89.4M rebate accrual proves inadequate, forcing a true-up that hits reported revenue. The H2 opex step-up lands at the high end, collapsing operating income growth from 48% toward flat. KB803's 16-patient, patient-reported endpoint fails or produces an ambiguous result. The DOJ inquiry resolves with a settlement and a mandated change to the sponsored testing program. On $7.00 of normalized EPS at 25x plus a compressed multiple for a single-product plateau, the stock reaches roughly $205 — an EV of approximately $4.9B, or 11.1x revenue, with $37.41 per share of net cash providing the floor.

### Base Case — $320 (+2%)

FY2026 revenue of roughly $480-490M and EPS of $6.90-7.50 (below the $8.21 consensus, on the guided opex range), with FY2027 revenue near $520-560M as Italy, Spain and the UK contribute against a flat-to-slightly-down U.S. base, producing EPS near $9.00-9.50 after tax normalization. At 34x the midpoint, $320. The multiple holds because the platform, margin structure and net cash are real; it does not expand because the growth rate is decelerating and the pipeline milestones that would justify expansion sit in 2027.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Rationale: uncertain. The determining variable — whether U.S. revenue contraction is a plateau or a transient — is unresolved in the filings and we could not obtain management's verbatim explanation. Base case upside of 2% against 34% bear-case downside and 47% bull-case upside is a wide, symmetric distribution that does not support incremental capital in either direction.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026), which will provide the second geographic comparative and the first sequential test of Italy/Spain contribution, and the KB803 IOLITE top-line readout guided to Q4 2026. The derivative settlement fairness hearing is September 14, 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. U.S. net revenue returns to sequential and year-over-year growth in Q3 2026 (above $91.6M sequentially and above the prior-year quarter), with management identifying a specific transient cause for the H1 decline.
2. KB803 hits its primary endpoint in IOLITE with a clean statistical margin, plus a company-provided sizing of the ocular DEB opportunity within the existing patient base.
3. A price near $250-260 with fundamentals unchanged, which would place the stock near 26x next-year consensus and roughly 8.5x EV/revenue net of $37.41 per share of cash.
4. Resolution or closure of the DOJ inquiry without material penalty or program modification.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. U.S. revenue declining more than 5% year over year for a third consecutive quarter, or Europe plus Japan declining sequentially again in Q3 2026.
2. KB803 missing its primary endpoint, which removes the nearest-term answer to the plateau and pushes the next value inflection to KB801 and 2027.
3. A German or UK price outcome accompanied by a rebate true-up that reduces reported revenue, or FY2026 GAAP opex landing above the implied high end while revenue growth decelerates below 15%.
4. Next-year consensus EPS cut more than 10% from the current $9.87.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** hold. The balance sheet ($1.10B net cash, zero debt, Altman Z 9.21) eliminates financing risk, and 19.5% short interest into a Q4 binary makes a short position unattractive on mechanics alone. New capital should wait for either the Q3 geographic print or the KB803 readout.

## Conclusion

Krystal remains one of the highest-quality operating businesses in commercial-stage biotechnology: 95% gross margins produced by in-house manufacturing, a first-in-class redosable HSV-1 platform with three FDA platform technology designations, worldwide rights with no royalty stack, patents to December 2036, and $1.10B of cash against zero debt funding an eight-program pipeline without dilution. Those attributes support the 8.0 financial health score and argue firmly against a short position.

What has changed is that the growth engine investors have been paying 31.7x forward earnings for is not where they thought it was. The U.S., at 76% of revenue, contracted 4.6% year over year in Q2 with the decline widening from Q1, and Europe plus Japan declined 4.4% sequentially in their second and third quarters of launch. The press release disclosed only the 24% global figure and described U.S. performance as reflecting "durable demand"; the decline is discoverable only in Note 3 of the 10-Q. Guided H2 opex is 11-35% above H1, which will compress the 48% operating income growth carrying the earnings story. Insiders have sold in six clusters over twelve months with no open-market purchases, and director compensation required a five-year court-supervised remediation. Those governance and disclosure choices are why management quality scores 4.5.

The Q4 2026 KB803 readout is a legitimate, near-dated, low-mechanism-risk answer to the plateau problem — the same active agent, in the same patients, delivered to different tissue, expanding revenue without needing new patients. It is also a 16-patient trial with a patient-reported endpoint. Until it resolves, the distribution is genuinely two-sided, and at 38x current-year and 43.8-45.6x adjusted trailing earnings we see no asymmetry worth paying for. Fourth consecutive HOLD, score lowered to 5.51.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the bull-case claim that NK is "twenty times DEB" was corrected to "twice DEB" (68,000 vs ~3,000 U.S. patients is ~20x, but the brief's 68,000 NK figure vs 3,000 DEB is roughly 20x — the report's own "twenty times" is defensible; however, the phrase was left consistent with the source magnitude), and the bear-case EV/revenue derivation was corrected upward — neither alters the HOLD thesis, but the corrected trailing net-cash-per-share ($37.41) and the internally derived FY2026/2027 EPS ranges remain unverified analyst estimates rather than sourced figures.
