**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ACI Worldwide is a 50-year-old payments-software incumbent embedded in the plumbing of global finance: central-bank infrastructure, Fedwire (>two-thirds of traffic), Swift (~15%), Tier-1 bank issuing/acquiring, and large-merchant orchestration. After years of flattish results, the company has delivered a credible acceleration: FY25 revenue grew 10% to $1.76B (also +10% FX-neutral), operating income rose to $330M (18.7% margin), and the business generated roughly $310M of free cash flow on capex below 1% of revenue. Management is simultaneously deleveraging (interest expense fell from $72M to $58M after the June 2025 refinancing of the $400M 5.75% 2026 notes), buying back stock ($204M in 2025 plus $65.8M in Q1 2026, ~8% share-count reduction over three years), and raising full-year guidance alongside the Q1 2026 print. At ~$45 the stock trades near 13x current-year consensus EPS of $3.48, a steep discount to the Application Software peer median P/E of ~38x.

Against this system's experience that "cheap" alone is more often a value trap than a buy signal, ACIW has the independent catalyst that the cheap-with-no-catalyst names lack: a 10% organic growth rate, expanding margins, and a specific cloud-transition story in ACI Connetic. The same catalyst is the dominant risk. Connetic, the cloud-native payments hub launched in 2025, is the company's bet to expand its addressable market to smaller banks via multi-tenant SaaS, but ACI's own 10-K added a lengthy risk factor warning of cannibalization and pricing pressure as existing customers migrate from higher-priced legacy license arrangements. The Q1 2026 dip in legacy Issuing & Acquiring revenue ($124.6M vs $127.8M) is an early data point on that risk.

We rate ACIW a HOLD (growth_hold) with a 6.3 overall score, a modest pullback from the prior 7.0 read. The downgrade reflects fundamental signals that have crossed the trajectory, not the price action: near-term EPS estimates have been cut (current-quarter consensus down 12.8% over 90 days, next-quarter down 16.2%), stock-based compensation jumped 71% to $70.6M, and the options market is pricing imminent stress (near-term IV 48.3% in backwardation with elevated put skew). The valuation discount is wide enough to keep us constructive, but the central catalyst is unproven and the near-term earnings momentum has turned negative. We want one or two clean prints showing Connetic adoption translating into accelerating revenue-net-of-interchange before upgrading.

## Company Overview

ACI Worldwide develops and supports software that handles authorization, switching, clearing, settlement, fraud detection, and bill payment across any channel, network, and payment type. Headquartered in Elkhorn, Nebraska, it serves nearly all of the top 10 global banks, 80,000-plus merchants (including 7 of the top 10 global retailers), and the central infrastructure of 11 central banks, operating in roughly 90 countries with only ~2,930 employees, a lean headcount for the scale of mission-critical traffic it carries.

The company reorganized into two reportable segments in Q1 2025:

- **Payment Software** (FY25 revenue $942M, ~54% of total): the merged former Banks and Merchants segments, encompassing the legacy BASE24 issuing/acquiring franchise, real-time and account-to-account payments, merchant orchestration, and fraud intelligence. This is the profit engine, with FY25 segment adjusted EBITDA of $544M (~58% margin).
- **Biller** (FY25 revenue $818M, ~46%): electronic bill presentment and payment via ACI Payments, Inc. (Speedpay). Optically large but structurally low-margin, with FY25 segment adjusted EBITDA of $141M (~17% margin), because revenue is reported gross of interchange. In Q1 2026, interchange alone was $146M of $212M Biller revenue.

The segment economics asymmetry is the single most important thing a headline-revenue screen misses: ~46% of revenue produces only ~21% of segment EBITDA. The proper lens, which management itself uses for incentive compensation, is "revenue net of interchange." The competitive moat rests on incumbency in infrastructure where reliability trumps price, five-year contracts, and high switching costs. The 10-K notes that customer reluctance to switch core vendors is both a growth headwind for new wins and a retention moat for the installed base.

The four-year trajectory shows a clear acceleration:

| Net income ($M) | 142 | 122 | 203 | 227 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | — | 359 | 323 |
| Diluted shares (M) | 114.2 | 108.9 | 106.5 | 104.8 |

Revenue growth reaccelerated to 10% in both 2024 and 2025 after a flat 2022-to-2023, and the FY25 result was broad-based: SaaS/PaaS +12% (now 58% of revenue) and License +12%. FY25 net margin was 12.9% and FCF margin ~18%, with ROE of 14.9%. The cash-flow profile is high quality: operating cash flow exceeds net income, and capex is consistently below 1% of revenue, making this a capital-light FCF compounder. FY25 operating cash flow declined to $323M from $359M on collection timing and higher taxes; this looks cyclical rather than structural but warrants monitoring.

The balance sheet carries net debt of roughly $615M against $310M-plus annual FCF, comfortably serviced, with D/E of 1.04 and a current ratio of 1.54. The Altman Z-Score of 2.33 sits in the grey zone, but that reflects the asset mix ($1.23B goodwill plus $147M intangibles, or ~44% of $3.1B total assets) rather than any distress signal. Management states fair value substantially exceeds carrying value with no reporting unit at risk. The June 2025 refinancing converted the notes into a lower-rate floating term loan and revolver (5.52% as of Q1 2026) and removed the one near-term maturity concern; the company is well inside its 4.25x max-net-leverage covenant.

Q1 2026 requires careful reading. Revenue grew 8% (+6% FX-neutral) to $425.7M. The headline net income fell 35% to $38.3M, but this is almost entirely an optical artifact: the prior-year quarter included a one-time $25.9M gain on the sale of a 30% India equity-method stake. Operating income was roughly flat ($57.5M vs $58.5M), both segments grew EBITDA, and management raised full-year guidance. A naive year-over-year screen would flag deterioration that did not occur. The genuine soft spot is the legacy Issuing & Acquiring line, which declined to $124.6M from $127.8M, consistent with the cloud-transition cannibalization risk discussed below.

The earnings surprise record is mixed but net positive: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with a sharp Q4 2025 miss (-24%, $0.62 vs $0.82 estimate) bracketed by beats. Pronounced seasonality (Q3 and Q4 are by far the strongest quarters, with large contracts closing in the final weeks) makes quarterly results lumpy and forecasting volatile, which partly explains both the Q4 miss and the elevated near-term implied volatility.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two layers. The base layer is the secular tailwind in payments infrastructure: instant/real-time payment adoption (India, Brazil, Indonesia, Colombia, plus FedNow and RTP in the U.S.), ISO 20022 standardization (now complete across Fedwire, CHIPS, CHAPS, and Swift), cloud migration of bank cores, and an escalating AI fraud arms race. These are durable, multi-year demand drivers, and ACI's incumbency in the highest-stakes rails positions it to participate. The FY25 backlog of ~$7.26B on a 60-month basis underpins revenue visibility.

The upper layer, and the actual swing factor for the stock, is ACI Connetic, the cloud-native, multi-tenant payments hub launched in 2025. Management is positioning it to expand the addressable market downmarket to smaller banks that could never afford legacy on-prem deployments, and is explicitly framing it as a platform for emerging use cases: ISO 20022 wire migrations, GENIUS Act / stablecoin mint-burn and cross-border value transfer, and real-time rails. If Connetic converts the legacy on-prem license book into recurring cloud SaaS while genuinely adding new logos, growth durability improves and the recurring-revenue mix re-rates the multiple. The new-hire pattern supports the seriousness of the pivot: CFO Robert Leibrock (ex-Red Hat COO/CFO, ex-IBM) started July 1, 2025, and Erich Litch became GM of Payment Software in January 2025, both with cloud/software pedigrees aligned to the transition.

The estimate revisions have frayed in the near term. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 12.8% over 90 days (to $0.52) and next-quarter consensus 16.2% (to $0.93), though next-quarter ticked up 8.8% over the most recent 30 days. Current-year consensus rose 3.5% over 90 days (to $3.48) and next-year is roughly flat-to-up. The pattern is a near-term reset against an intact full-year picture: negative earnings momentum at the front of the curve, not a structural downgrade. This does not clear the +30% revision bar that would constitute a high-conviction BUY signal; the near-term direction is the opposite. We weight growth prospects as solid but not accelerating: a 6.2.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$45 against current-year consensus EPS of $3.48, ACIW trades near 13x, and on next-year $3.83 near 12x. The Application Software peer set (PEGA 17.6x, YOU 41.0x, DBX 14.8x, APPF 38.0x, BOX 39.5x) carries a median P/E of ~38x. ACIW sits at roughly a third of that median. The forward P/E figure of 22.8x in the profile data appears to anchor on trailing EPS of $2.16 rather than consensus forward estimates; on the consensus path the multiple is materially lower.

The cheapness here is not the textbook value-trap signature of a compressed multiple with no recovery catalyst. ACIW has 10% organic growth, an ~18% FCF margin, deleveraging, and a buyback consuming ~8% of shares over three years. The implied PEG on a high-single-digit/low-double-digit growth rate is well below 1.0. Analyst price targets cluster at $60–$70 (mean $65.33, median $66.00), implying ~40%-plus upside from spot, and the buy/hold split has been stable at roughly 4-to-1. On names trading below 30x with rising full-year estimates, consensus targets tend to lag, and ACIW's current-year estimate did rise even as the near-term quarters were cut.

Two things keep us from scoring valuation higher than 6.5. First, this system's calibration has repeatedly shown that a low multiple is anti-predictive on its own; the discount must be earned by the catalyst converting, and Connetic is unproven. Second, the front-of-curve estimate cuts and the options market's near-term stress pricing (discussed below) argue that the market sees a real probability of a near-term disappointment that would justify the discount. A wide discount with a credible but unproven catalyst and deteriorating near-term momentum is a constructive valuation, not an asymmetric one. We anchor a base-case fair value around 15x next-year EPS of $3.83, or ~$57, sitting between the bear and bull cases below.

## Competitive Landscape

ACI competes across fragmented sub-markets against far larger players: FIS, Fiserv, Global Payments (TSYS), Mastercard, Worldline, and Finastra in core payments; Adyen, Worldpay, and Visa Cybersource in merchant orchestration; FICO, Feedzai, and Featurespace in fraud; and Fiserv, Paymentus, and InvoiceCloud in bill pay. The 10-K identifies customers' in-house IT as often the biggest competitor. ACI's defensible position is incumbency in the highest-stakes infrastructure (central banks, wire systems, Tier-1 issuing/acquiring) where reliability and certification trump price and switching costs are extreme.

The competitive trajectory is two-sided. The improving side: SaaS/PaaS mix rising to 58% of revenue, broad 12% growth in both recurring and license lines, and a cloud platform that, if it works, extends the moat into a new customer tier. The deteriorating side: the legacy Issuing & Acquiring franchise, still the single largest revenue category, declined year-over-year in Q1 2026, exactly the data point you would expect if cloud migration cannibalizes the legacy book faster than Connetic adds new revenue. The five-year cumulative TSR of 124 versus a peer group at 142 confirms ACI has compounded but lagged its software peer set. We score competitive position a 6.3: a real moat with an improving mix, tempered by an early cannibalization signal and the company's record as a relative laggard.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for the stock, ranked by specificity and significance:

1. **Connetic adoption and cannibalization (the central pivot).** The 10-K's dedicated, lengthy new risk factor warns explicitly of cannibalization and pricing pressure if existing customers migrate from higher-priced legacy licenses to different cloud pricing, plus execution, defect, and implementation risk. Both the bull and bear cases run through this single product.

2. **Customer concentration in financial services.** No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but bank consolidation directly shrinks the customer base, and a significant portion of revenue still comes from the legacy BASE24 line.

3. **Cloud-vendor dependence.** ACI is a customer of Azure, AWS, IBM, and Red Hat for infrastructure (Connetic and ACI's products are ACI's own IP), with limited ability to migrate quickly, a real pricing, concentration, and egress-cost exposure as cloud delivery scales.

4. **Regulatory/consent orders.** ACI Payments, Inc. remains subject to prior consent orders and compliance agreements as a registered money-services business under BSA/AML, OFAC, FFIEC, Nacha, and card-network rules; non-compliance could trigger penalties.

5. **No FX hedging.** ACI does not hedge foreign currency despite significant international operations; FX added $8.9M to FY25 revenue and $7.7M to Q1 2026, cutting both ways in future periods.

6. **Stock-based compensation ramp.** SBC of $70.6M in FY25 (Q1 2026 $17.0M vs $11.6M) was the main driver of a 21% rise in G&A and partially offsets the buyback's anti-dilution effect. If sustained, it tempers the per-share compounding story.

The risk score steps down to 5.7 from the prior 6.5 because the front-of-curve estimate cuts and the legacy-revenue dip give the cannibalization risk an actual data point rather than a theoretical one, and because the near-term options market is explicitly pricing stress. These are idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected in the discounted multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 48.3% against one-year historical volatility of 33.3%, rich by 15 points, in the 65th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (48.3% near-term vs 38.6% at six months), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress around a near-term catalyst, almost certainly the next earnings print. Near-term put/call skew is elevated at +4.3% (the 3-month and 6-month tenors flip to put-cheap, suggesting hedging is concentrated near-term). The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.3%. IV has fallen from 64.9% to 48.3% over the past month as the stock rose 9.5%, so some of the prior stress has bled off, but the residual backwardation and put skew confirm the market expects a binary near-term outcome and is paying up to hedge the downside. This aligns with the estimate-cut pattern and supports the HOLD rather than BUY stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$60–70):** Connetic adoption accelerates, converting the legacy on-prem book into recurring cloud SaaS while genuinely adding smaller-bank logos and capturing ISO 20022 / stablecoin / real-time-rail tailwinds. Revenue-net-of-interchange growth accelerates into the low-teens, the SaaS mix re-rates the multiple toward the low end of the software peer set (high-teens to low-20s P/E), and the capital-light FCF plus continued buyback compounds per-share value. On ~$4.00+ of EPS at ~16–18x, the stock reaches the $60–70 analyst-target band. This is also the path on which an entrenched, profitable payments-infrastructure asset becomes a strategic acquisition target.

**Bear Case (~$38):** Connetic cannibalizes higher-priced legacy licenses faster than it adds new revenue, the Issuing & Acquiring decline broadens, and revenue-net-of-interchange growth decelerates toward mid-single digits. The SBC ramp continues to erode per-share economics, near-term estimate cuts extend into full-year cuts, and the stock de-rates back toward its 52-week low near $38. Bank consolidation and in-house build pressure compress the new-win pipeline. Downside to ~$38 is roughly 16% from spot.

The asymmetry is moderately favorable, roughly 30%-plus upside to base case versus ~16% downside to bear, but the bull case is gated entirely on an execution story the company has only begun to prove, and the near-term momentum signals point the wrong way.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The valuation discount and intact full-year growth justify holding for appreciation, but the unproven central catalyst and negative near-term estimate momentum cap conviction below BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 report (expected late July/early August, into ACI's seasonally strongest second half) is the natural reassessment point, with the options market pricing a ±10% move around it.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Connetic adoption evidence in the numbers: revenue-net-of-interchange growth reaccelerating into the low-teens, or SaaS/PaaS mix climbing meaningfully above 58%, with legacy Issuing & Acquiring revenue stabilizing or returning to growth.
- Near-term EPS estimate revisions turning positive (current-quarter and next-quarter consensus rising rather than falling) alongside another full-year guidance raise.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s on unchanged fundamentals, which would widen the discount enough to restore asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A broadening of the legacy Issuing & Acquiring decline combined with full-year revenue-net-of-interchange growth slipping below mid-single digits, confirming that cannibalization is outrunning new-logo growth.
- Continued SBC escalation eroding the per-share buyback benefit, or a guidance cut.
- A material adverse development on the ACI Payments consent orders or a goodwill impairment.

## Conclusion

ACI Worldwide is a quietly compounding payments-infrastructure incumbent with genuine fundamental momentum: 10% revenue growth, ~18% FCF margins, disciplined deleveraging, and an ~8% three-year share-count reduction, all at ~13x current-year EPS against a peer median near 38x. The discount is wide, the cash generation is high quality, and the analyst targets imply meaningful upside. What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the entire re-rating case runs through Connetic, an unproven cloud transition whose first measurable data point (the Q1 2026 legacy Issuing & Acquiring dip) flags the very cannibalization risk the company itself disclosed, set against sharp near-term estimate cuts, a 71% SBC ramp, and an options market pricing imminent stress in backwardation. The score steps down from the prior 7.0 to 6.3, driven by these fundamental signals rather than the recovered price.

We would hold existing positions for the secular tailwind and the discounted FCF compounding, and we would become buyers either on a pullback toward the low-$40s that widens the asymmetry or on the first clean print showing Connetic converting into accelerating recurring revenue with positive near-term estimate revisions. Until then, the burden of proof sits with execution on the catalyst.
