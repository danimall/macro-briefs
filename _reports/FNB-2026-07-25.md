**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

F.N.B. Corporation is a $50B-asset Pittsburgh-based super-community bank that just closed a record fiscal 2025 (net income to common +23% to $565M, diluted EPS $1.56) and posted record Q2 2026 revenue of $463M with EPS up 17% year over year. Credit quality is pristine (NPLs 31bps, net charge-offs 19bps, reserve coverage ~420% of non-performers), capital is at a record (CET1 11.4%), and management is buying back stock at an accelerated pace near tangible book. This is a well-run regional executing cleanly.

The problem is the trajectory of forward earnings, not the trailing print. On the Q2 call FNB cut its full-year 2026 net interest income guidance to $1.485B–$1.515B and guided expenses to the high end of $1.01B–$1.02B, citing intense deposit competition and one-month SOFR volatility pressuring loan yields. The two-year NIM tailwind (+10bps in 2025 even as the Fed eased 75bps, helped by the well-timed November 2024 securities restructuring) is fading. Consensus responded: EPS estimates are down across every window (current year -2.5%, next year -3.8% over 90 days), BofA downgraded the stock on July 20, and analyst sentiment is degrading. The stock nonetheless sits near a 52-week high at $19.05, roughly 1.6x tangible book.

We rate FNB a HOLD (income-oriented). The bank is financially sound and returns capital sensibly, and the 2.8% dividend is covered by a conservative ~31% payout. But it now trades at fair value against decelerating earnings, with no identifiable re-rating catalyst and an M&A growth lever hobbled through roughly 2029 by outstanding fair-lending consent orders. The risk/reward is balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable; we would want a pullback toward tangible book or a stabilization in NII guidance before turning constructive.

## Company Overview

FNB operates primarily through First National Bank of Pennsylvania, with $50.2B in total assets and 355 branches across seven states plus D.C. The footprint runs deliberately from the Mid-Atlantic (Pittsburgh, Cleveland, Baltimore, D.C.) into higher-growth Southeastern MSAs (Charlotte, Raleigh-Durham, Piedmont Triad, Charleston). The business splits into three segments: Community Banking (the core lending/deposit franchise), Wealth Management ($14.9B assets under administration), and Insurance.

FNB positions itself as a super-community bank taking share in metro markets historically dominated by money-center and larger regional players, differentiating through a proprietary digital stack (eStore, Common App, and an "Insight 360" AI aggregation tool going live end-2026) and a diversified fee engine. Its structural advantages are modest but real: a granular low-cost deposit base (26% non-interest-bearing), geographic exposure to faster-growing Southeastern markets, and a demonstrated organic-plus-acquisition growth history (Yadkin 2017, Howard Bancorp, UB Bancorp). CEO Vincent Delie serves as combined Chairman, President and CEO.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a genuine record with positive operating leverage:

- **Net income available to common $565.4M** (+23% YoY), diluted EPS $1.56 (+22.8%), operating EPS $1.59 (+14.4%). Net income has stepped up from $439M (2022) → $485M (2023) → $465M (2024) → $565M (2025).
- **Total revenue ~$1.8B** (+10.5%). Net interest income $1.40B (+9.0%); non-interest income a record $369.3M (+16.7% reported, +5.4% operating).
- **NIM (FTE) 3.19%**, up 10bps YoY despite the Fed cutting 75bps. The mechanism: cost of funds fell 23bps (interest-bearing deposit costs down 31bps to 2.65%), outrunning a 13bp decline in earning-asset yields. The November 2024 securities restructuring (sold $231M yielding 1.41%, reinvested at 4.78%) was a meaningful tailwind.
- **Profitability:** ROA 1.15%, ROTCE 14.4%, efficiency ratio 54.8% — solid for a bank this size.
- **Balance sheet:** Loans +2.5% to $34.8B (consumer +8.4% led by residential mortgage; commercial -1.1% on deliberate CRE reduction). Deposits +4.5% to $38.8B; loan/deposit ratio 89.7%.
- **Capital:** Record CET1 11.36% (up from 10.58%), TCE/TA 8.89%. Tangible book value/share $11.87 (+13.2%). Book value/share $18.92.
- **Cash flow / capital allocation:** Operating cash flow $482M, FCF $376M. Dividend $0.48/share held flat (payout ~31%). Repurchased $50M (3.3M shares) at avg $14.92 in 2025.

Standardized health screens (Altman Z, D/E) are not meaningful for a bank where leverage is structural. The relevant markers all point to a sound, well-reserved institution: CET1 11.4%, NPLs 0.30%, ACL/NPL coverage 418%, NCOs 0.20%. Capital-structure cleanup reinforces this: all $111M preferred redeemed (2024), ~$475M debt retired in 2025, no scheduled debt maturities in 2026, LCR 2.3x, 14 months of cash on hand at the parent.

Q2 2026 (reported 7/16) confirmed the operating strength: net income ~$149M, TBV/share $12.24 (+10% YoY), CET1 steady at 11.4%, loans to $35.8B (+7.5% annualized, C&I ~8%), non-interest income +6.5% to $97M (capital markets +16%, wealth +8%), efficiency ratio improved to 53.7%. Credit stayed clean.

The offsetting signal is forward earnings visibility. The FY2026 NII guidance cut, with expenses at the high end of range, is the fulcrum. Goodwill of $2.48B (~37% of equity) means the more conservative valuation anchor is tangible book, which is why the stock at ~$19 is best framed as ~1.6x TBV rather than "near book value."

## Growth Analysis

The trailing growth is real but the forward path is flattening. FY2025 EPS growth of +22.8% and Q2 EPS +17% reflect the NIM tailwind and fee diversification that are now fading. The clearest forward evidence is estimate direction: current-quarter EPS revised -5.4% over 30 days, next-quarter -3.0%, current-year -2.5%, and next-year -3.8%. This is broad-based negative revision momentum across every horizon, driven by the same deposit-competition and short-rate dynamics management flagged.

The estimate revision signal that typically precedes re-rating (>30% upward over 90 days on an inflecting name) is the mirror image here: estimates are moving down, not up. FNB's own recent earnings pattern reinforces the deceleration: after beating by 8.8% (Q3 2025) and 14.2% (Q4 2025), it missed by 1.8% (Q1 2026) and came in-line, missing by 0.8%, in Q2 2026. Visibility has narrowed.

The organic growth levers remain: continued Southeastern share gains, C&I loan growth (~8% in Q2), fee income from wealth and capital markets, and the Insight 360 digital rollout. But the acquisition lever — historically a core part of FNB's growth model — is constrained by the fair-lending consent orders through roughly 2029, since CRA rating and fair-lending status gate merger approvals. That shifts the burden of growth onto organic execution at precisely the moment NIM is under pressure. Net, growth prospects are positive but modest and decelerating, warranting a below-average score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $19.05 the stock trades at ~11.2x trailing EPS ($1.56) and ~11.3x forward on the current-year consensus of $1.69, at ~1.6x tangible book value ($12.24) and roughly 1.0x book ($18.92). The 2.8% dividend yield is in line with peers.

Peer context tempers any cheapness argument. The Regional Banks peer median P/E is 13.7x, so FNB screens optically cheap on a headline basis — but the closest-comparable value names cluster near FNB: UBSI 12.6x, PB 13.0x, ABCB 13.7x, with GBCI the outlier at 22.8x. FNB is roughly at the low end of that band, appropriate given falling estimates and the M&A constraint. A low multiple here reflects genuinely decelerating forward earnings, which is exactly the value-trap pattern that has historically sorted the wrong way. Cheapness without a catalyst is not a buy signal.

Analyst targets bracket the stock tightly — low $19.00, mean $20.94, median $21.00, high $22.00 — implying only single-digit upside to consensus, and the sentiment trend is degrading. On an inflecting name we would treat targets as stale floors; here, with estimates falling and one house cutting its rating, the target cluster is more likely a fair-value ceiling than a floor. The multiple already embeds FNB's above-peer execution history (cumulative TSR 215.8 vs. peer 152.7).

Where is the market wrong? We do not see a specific mispricing to underwrite. The valuation score sits below neutral: the stock is priced as a steady, well-run compounder, not a bargain, and the near-term earnings direction argues against paying up.

## Competitive Landscape

FNB competes against larger regionals (PNC, KeyCorp, Truist, Huntington, Fifth Third) in overlapping metros, alongside community banks, credit unions, and increasingly non-bank/fintech entrants; the 10-K explicitly flags GENIUS Act stablecoin disintermediation as an emerging risk. Its differentiation is a proprietary digital platform used to deepen primary-bank relationships and a deliberate tilt toward higher-growth Southeastern MSAs.

Trajectory is roughly stable. On the positive side, FNB is winning share in the Carolinas, has grown consumer and C&I while deliberately shrinking CRE/office, and carries an above-peer TSR that suggests the market has rewarded execution. On the cautious side, the 2026 NIM/funding pressure is a sector-wide dynamic that FNB is not escaping (its NII guidance cut mirrors peer commentary), and the consent-order M&A constraint removes a lever competitors retain. Deposit-cost competition is compressing the deposit-franchise advantage that drove 2025's NIM expansion. Overall the competitive position is holding, not clearly widening — a neutral score.

## Risk Assessment

The most distinctive idiosyncratic risk is the **DOJ / North Carolina redlining consent orders**. In February 2024 FNBPA resolved allegations of Fair Housing Act, ECOA/Reg B, and NC UDAP violations from 2017–2021 (partly as successor to acquired Yadkin Bank) covering the Charlotte and Winston-Salem areas. The orders run a minimum of five years, require loan-subsidy payments, and can constrain M&A, since fair-lending status and CRA rating gate bank-merger approvals. For a company whose growth has historically leaned on acquisitions, this is an under-appreciated strategic limiter running through roughly 2029.

Other watch-items:
- **CRE concentration:** $12.3B (35% of loans), $8.5B non-owner-occupied, ~18% of that office. Management is actively de-risking (CRE down ~$431M in 2025, another $129M in Q2 2026), disclosed granularity is reassuring (42% of office exposures <$5M), and NPLs actually fell 34% in 2025 — but office remains the sector's key refinancing watch-item.
- **Uninsured deposits:** ~23% uninsured, better than the failed 2023 banks; liquidity buffers strong (uninsured coverage 139%, $18.3B unused wholesale capacity).
- **NII/margin pressure:** the dominant near-term concern, now confirmed in the guidance cut.
- **$50B asset threshold:** crossing it triggers heightened resolution-planning and enhanced regulatory costs.

De-risking has partly offset these: Moody's revised its outlook from negative to stable on Feb 12, 2026, and the preferred/debt cleanup improved the funding profile. Most of these risks are known and at least partly priced, which keeps the risk score near neutral rather than low.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 22.5% sits slightly below one-year historical volatility of 25.2% (fair-to-cheap), and IV has collapsed from 41.6% to 22.5% since the mid-June snapshot as the Q2 earnings event resolved. The term structure is in contango (near 22.5% vs. long-dated 27.0%), a normal/complacent shape. Put/call skew of 3.3% shows modestly elevated put demand, consistent with the 11.5% short interest (6.0 days to cover) — institutional positioning leans mildly bearish. Nothing in the options signal contradicts a neutral fundamental view; the market is not pricing a near-term binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$22, +15%):** Deposit competition eases as the rate environment stabilizes, NIM holds at the guided 3.25% or better, and fee diversification (wealth +8%, capital markets +16% in Q2) continues to drive positive operating leverage. Pristine credit lets reserves run off favorably; accelerated buybacks near tangible book (~$80M in 1H 2026, ~$250M remaining) compound tangible book value per share. EPS reaccelerates toward the $1.89 next-year consensus and the multiple holds, taking the stock to the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear case (~$16-17, -11% to -16%):** Deposit-cost competition and SOFR volatility compress NIM below the guided range, expenses land at or above the high end, and EPS stalls near $1.65-1.70 with no growth. The M&A constraint keeps FNB reliant on a decelerating organic engine, the multiple compresses toward tangible book on further negative revisions, and CRE/office refinancing produces a modest uptick in charge-offs. The stock re-rates back toward the low-$16s where it traded in early 2026.

**Base case (~$20, +5%):** Steady execution — NIM roughly flat, modest loan and fee growth, continued buybacks — delivers low-single-digit EPS growth, and the stock tracks tangible book accretion plus the dividend for a total return in the mid-single digits.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an income orientation over a 12-month horizon. The 2.8% dividend is well-covered (~31% payout) and buybacks near tangible book support the floor, but decelerating earnings and a full valuation cap appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY2026 NII guidance stabilizes or is revised upward, with consensus EPS revisions turning positive over a 90-day window.
- A pullback toward tangible book (~$15-16, roughly 1.2-1.3x TBV) that restores an asymmetric entry.
- Confirmation that the consent orders will lapse on schedule (~2029) without extension, restoring M&A optionality.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NIM breaks below ~3.10% with continued NII guidance cuts.
- CRE/office charge-offs rise materially above the current 19bp run-rate, or NPLs move above ~50bps.
- Dividend coverage deteriorates (payout ratio rising toward 50%+) or buybacks are suspended.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q3 2026, mid-October), with the FY2026 NII guidance trajectory as the key data point.

## Conclusion

FNB is a financially sound, conservatively run super-community bank that just delivered record trailing results with clean credit and record capital. The score trajectory (6.0 → 6.9 → 6.2) captured the improving financial health and fading valuation, and the latest data confirms that split: financial health remains a genuine strength while the earnings-growth engine is decelerating. The Q2 NII guidance cut, high-end expense trajectory, broad-based negative estimate revisions, and the BofA downgrade collectively mean the bank is now compounding more slowly than 2025's headline suggested, and the stock at ~1.6x tangible book and ~11x forward on falling estimates already reflects fair value.

We maintain HOLD (income). The dividend and buyback support the downside near tangible book, but with no re-rating catalyst, a constrained M&A lever, and sector-wide margin pressure that FNB is not escaping, the risk/reward is balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable at the current price. We would become constructive on a pullback toward tangible book or a stabilization in the NII outlook.
