## Executive Summary

DaVita is the second-largest U.S. kidney dialysis provider with ~2,657 outpatient centers, ~200,500 patients, and an estimated 36% domestic market share. The business generates highly recurring revenue from a life-sustaining treatment (patients typically require three in-center sessions per week) and operates in a duopoly with Fresenius Medical Care. Full-year 2025 revenue reached $13.6B (+6.5% YoY), and management guided FY2026 adjusted EPS of $13.60–$15.00, implying 26–39% growth driven by revenue per treatment improvements, lower debt costs, and continued share count reduction.

The valuation is compelling on the surface: ~10.7x the midpoint of 2026 EPS guidance, with a 52-week high of $159 and current price near $150. The financial structure carries concentrated, difficult-to-underwrite risks. Debt stands at $10.2B against negative stockholders' equity of -$651M. Nearly all profit derives from ~11% of patients covered by commercial insurance, and multiple policy vectors (ACA premium tax credit expiration, Medicaid restructuring, payor plan design changes) threaten that concentration. Short interest at 20.2% of float signals meaningful institutional skepticism. Until the commercial payor mix trajectory and leverage sustainability become clearer, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

DaVita operates two reportable segments: U.S. Dialysis (~86% of consolidated revenue) and Ancillary Services (~14%), which includes Integrated Kidney Care (IKC), international operations across 14 countries (585 centers), clinical research, and transplant software. Revenue per treatment averaged $409.56 in 2025. The payor mix is the economic fulcrum: ~89% of patients are on government programs (primarily Medicare), generating ~68% of U.S. dialysis revenue at rates well below commercial. The ~11% of patients on commercial insurance generate ~32% of revenue and substantially all of the profit margin above break-even.

The company's capital allocation strategy is aggressive share repurchases funded partly through debt. In 2025, DaVita repurchased $1.79B in stock (174% of free cash flow), reducing diluted shares outstanding by 13% to 75.9M. Berkshire Hathaway remains the largest shareholder at ~40%+ of outstanding shares (~30.1M shares), subject to a 45% ownership cap agreement that requires periodic trimming as DaVita shrinks its share count.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins:** Revenue grew from $11.6B (2022) to $13.6B (2025), a 5.6% compound annual rate. Growth in 2025 was driven by $18.24/treatment revenue improvement (+4.7%), international expansion (+37.8% YoY), and IKC growth (+7.5%). Treatment volumes declined 1.1% in 2025 due to elevated mortality from a severe flu season and missed treatments. Management expects approximately flat volumes in 2026.

GAAP operating income fell 2.2% to $2.04B in 2025, though adjusted operating income (excluding $50M in cybersecurity and international legal charges) rose 5.7% to $2.09B. Net income declined 20.2% to $747M, driven by $110M in incremental interest expense (+23.4%), a higher effective tax rate (29.1% vs. 22.9%), and $46M in Mozarc Medical investment losses. Patient care costs per treatment rose 5.9%, reflecting phosphate binder administration costs and ~3.8% clinical wage inflation.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow was $1.89B, free cash flow $1.02B. Both declined from 2024 ($2.02B and $1.16B respectively). The company spent $1.79B on repurchases — 74% more than FCF — funding the gap through incremental borrowing.

**Balance sheet:** Long-term debt reached $10.2B, up from $9.2B in 2024. Cash was $676M. Stockholders' equity is negative $651M after the company retired treasury shares, taking retained earnings from $1.53B to -$328M. The debt-to-adjusted-EBITDA ratio remains within the 3.0x–3.5x covenant band. Near-term maturities are light ($82M in 2026), but $4.59B comes due in 2029–2030. Total future interest obligations on existing facilities: $2.93B. Weighted average effective interest rate improved slightly to 5.51% after 2025 refinancing activity.

**Earnings trajectory:** Adjusted EPS grew 11.4% in 2025 to $10.78, entirely driven by the 13% share count reduction (adjusted net income was roughly flat). The 2026 guidance implies another 26–39% jump, driven by: (1) revenue per treatment growth, (2) lower interest expense from refinancing, (3) reduced Mozarc losses, and (4) continued buybacks. Q4 2025 EPS of $3.40 beat consensus by 6.7%.

## Growth Analysis

DaVita's organic growth story is modest. Treatment volumes are flat to slightly declining. Revenue per treatment benefits from rate increases and the phosphate binder TDAPA inclusion, but the TDAPA is set to expire at end of 2026, creating uncertainty about the permanence of this benefit.

The primary growth driver is financial engineering: leveraged share repurchases reducing the denominator of EPS. From 87.3M diluted shares in 2024 to 75.9M in 2025, with further buyback activity continuing in early 2026. This works mechanically as long as cash flows remain stable and debt markets remain accessible.

International operations provide genuine organic growth (76 net new centers in 2025, 37.8% revenue growth), but from a small base (~10% of revenue) and at lower margins than the U.S. business.

Estimate revisions tell a mixed story. Current-year (FY2026) EPS estimates rose 10.7% over 90 days to $14.12, reflecting the strong Q4 and guidance. But the current quarter estimate fell 5.2% over 90 days to $2.33, suggesting near-term softness. Full-year estimates are stabilizing over the last 30 days (+0.3%).

GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, and early-stage xenotransplantation research represent long-duration competitive threats that could slow the growth of the ESRD patient population. These are not near-term concerns but represent a structural uncertainty over the next 5–10 years.

## Valuation Assessment

At $150.05, DVA trades at ~10.7x the $14.12 consensus for FY2026 and ~9.2x the $16.40 estimate for FY2027. This is significantly below the peer median P/E of 65.3x, though the peer group (Brightspring, Chemed, RadNet, Hims & Hers, Sotera Health) is heterogeneous and not directly comparable. More meaningfully, the trailing P/E of ~15.8x on $9.51 TTM EPS is modest for a business with this level of recurring revenue and market position.

The analyst consensus target is $151.71 (median $156), with a range of $126–$190. This narrow range around the current price reflects the consensus HOLD stance (6 holds, 1 buy, 1 strong sell among 8 analysts).

The stock is 5.9% below its 52-week high of $159.42 and 48.6% above its 52-week low of $101.00. The January 2026 trough near $101 coincided with Berkshire's share sale and broader market weakness; the subsequent 21% earnings-day surge reflects the market pricing in the strong 2026 guidance.

The valuation is attractive in absolute terms if 2026 guidance is achievable and the commercial payor mix holds. At $14.30 midpoint EPS and a 12x multiple, fair value is $172. At 15x, it's $215. The bear case — payor mix deterioration plus refinancing risk on the 2029–2030 maturities — justifies values closer to $100–110 (7–8x distressed earnings).

## Competitive Landscape

DaVita and Fresenius Medical Care collectively control an estimated 70%+ of the U.S. outpatient dialysis market. Barriers to entry are substantial: regulatory compliance (Medicare certification, state licensing), capital requirements, established physician/medical director relationships, and geographic network density. New center openings by competitors have been limited.

The primary competitive threats are structural rather than from direct competitors: (1) payor-driven plan design changes that shift patients from commercial to Medicare earlier; (2) non-traditional kidney care entrants targeting pre-dialysis and home dialysis segments; (3) CMMI value-based payment models that could change the economics of dialysis provision; and (4) long-duration technology risks from GLP-1s reducing CKD progression and xenotransplantation.

DaVita's IKC business (managing ~66,000 patients in risk-based arrangements) positions the company to capture value if the industry shifts toward outcomes-based payment. The international expansion provides geographic diversification, though at lower margins.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial payor mix (CRITICAL):** This is the single risk that defines DVA's investment profile. The expiration of enhanced ACA premium tax credits (under OBBBA or other legislative action) would directly reduce the number of patients with commercial coverage. Management's 2026 guidance explicitly identifies this as a headwind. California AB 290 (capping reimbursement for patients receiving charitable premium assistance) provides a template that other states could replicate. The post-*Marietta* legal environment gives payors more latitude to design plans that limit ESRD coverage. Any 2–3 percentage point shift in commercial patient mix would materially compress margins.

**Leverage (HIGH):** The strategy of funding buybacks in excess of FCF through debt only works if cash flows remain stable. A 15–20% decline in EBITDA (plausible under adverse payor mix scenarios) would push leverage above covenant limits and potentially impair the refinancing path for 2029–2030 maturities. Interest expense consumed $579M in 2025 (77% of net income).

**Cybersecurity (MODERATE):** The April 2025 ransomware attack caused $25M in direct costs, disrupted billing cycles, and led to PHI/PII exfiltration. Management expects ongoing remediation costs in 2026. Regulatory fines and litigation from the data breach represent open-ended tail risk.

**TDAPA expiration (MODERATE):** Phosphate binder reimbursement under the TDAPA is currently set to expire at year-end 2026. If permanent bundling occurs at inadequate rates, the impact on revenue per treatment would be material.

**Flat treatment volumes (MODERATE):** Mortality remains elevated vs. pre-pandemic levels. GLP-1s and SGLT2 inhibitors may slow the pipeline of new ESRD patients over time, though this is a multi-year dynamic.

The 23rd percentile IV rank within the current chain implies no unusual event pricing in the near term. The 20.2% short interest (6.9 days to cover) is the more significant market signal. This level of structural short positioning, despite the stock being within 6% of 52-week highs, indicates that meaningful capital is betting against the durability of earnings or the capital structure. Incremental negative news on commercial payor mix or debt markets could trigger accelerated selling; conversely, continued earnings delivery and short covering could provide mechanical upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175–$190):** DaVita delivers at the high end of 2026 guidance ($15.00 EPS), commercial payor mix remains stable as ACA subsidies are extended or partially preserved, continued buybacks reduce shares to ~68M by year-end 2026, and the TDAPA is extended or permanently included at adequate rates. At 12–13x $15 EPS, the stock reaches $180–$195. Short covering provides additional mechanical support. International growth continues at 20%+ and begins contributing meaningful margins.

**Bear Case ($95–$110):** ACA enhanced premium tax credits expire without replacement, commercial patient mix declines 2–3 percentage points, and the TDAPA expires at inadequate permanent rates. EBITDA declines 15–20%, pushing leverage above 4.0x and impairing the 2029–2030 refinancing path. Earnings compress to $9–$10 per share. At 10–11x distressed earnings, the stock reaches $90–$110. Berkshire reduces its position further, removing the largest source of perceived floor support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Congressional extension or replacement of enhanced ACA premium tax credits providing multi-year visibility on commercial payor mix
2. TDAPA extension confirmed beyond 2026 at economically adequate rates
3. Stock pullback to $125–$130 (9x FY2026 consensus) providing sufficient margin of safety against the leverage and payor mix risks

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Quarterly commercial payor mix declining below 10% of patients
2. Leverage exceeding 4.0x adjusted EBITDA
3. Management withdrawing or materially reducing 2026 EPS guidance

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report (likely late April/early May 2026 for Q1 FY2026) will provide the first data point on 2026 commercial payor trends and TDAPA contribution. The legislative trajectory of ACA premium tax credits under OBBBA should become clearer by mid-2026.

## Conclusion

DaVita is a high-quality operating franchise with life-sustaining, recurring revenue, a duopoly market position, and significant scale advantages, housed in a financial structure that amplifies both upside and downside. If 2026 guidance is delivered, the stock is inexpensive at ~10.7x. The 90-day estimate revision trend (+10.7% for FY2026) supports confidence in the near-term earnings trajectory, and the 3-of-4 quarterly beat record is constructive.

The negative equity balance sheet and the fragility of the commercial payor mix — the single variable that determines whether this is a $10 or $15 EPS business — create a risk profile that prevents a higher-conviction recommendation. At current levels, the stock is priced for execution in line with guidance. HOLD until either the legislative/regulatory backdrop clarifies the commercial payor trajectory (upgrade trigger) or valuation compresses to provide adequate margin of safety against the known risks.
