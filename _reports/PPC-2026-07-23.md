**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pilgrim's Pride is one of the largest protein companies globally, a vertically-integrated chicken producer (with U.K. pork and lamb) that is 82.2% owned by JBS S.A. The stock has declined roughly 40% from its August 2025 high of $50.56 to $28.43, and the reason is straightforward: the U.S. broiler cycle has turned. Q1 2026 U.S. gross profit fell 49% year-over-year as U.S. sales price per pound dropped 4.7% against industry ready-to-cook production up 3.4%. Consolidated operating margin compressed to 3.6% from 9.1% a year earlier, and net income fell 66% to $101M. This is the classic supply-response phase of the poultry cycle: record egg sets and chick placements are lifting output faster than still-solid demand can absorb, pressuring prices even as feed costs stay cheap.

The valuation optics are misleading in both directions. The 7.6x trailing P/E capitalizes near-peak 2025 earnings ($4.54 diluted EPS) and is not a real discount: consensus has cut current-year EPS to $2.78 (down 29% in 90 days), which puts the forward multiple closer to 10x. That is roughly fair for a commodity cyclical without a dividend, carrying a controlling-shareholder overhang, a recurring ~$160-170M/year antitrust litigation drag, and a fresh December 2025 Trump food-sector antitrust executive order of unquantified scope. The balance sheet is genuinely sound (Altman Z 3.29, ~$3.1B in laddered notes maturing 2031-2034, no near-term wall), but the $2.0B in special dividends paid in 2025 drained cash from $2.04B to $640M just as earnings rolled over, a JBS-directed cash upstreaming that leaves minority holders with less cushion at exactly the wrong point in the cycle.

We rate PPC a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The business is sound, but there is no asymmetric edge here: estimate revisions are still falling across every window, options markets are pricing imminent stress, and there is no open-market insider buying to signal a bottom. A compressed multiple on peak earnings has historically been a value-trap signal in this sector, not a buy signal, until the cycle turns. Reassess at the July 29-30 Q2 print, which will show whether U.S. pricing discipline is stabilizing as supply grows.

## Company Overview

Pilgrim's Pride operates ~63,000 employees across three reportable segments:

- **U.S.** (~59% of sales, $11.0B in 2025): fresh and prepared chicken to foodservice, retail, and frozen-entrée customers, including the Just Bare® and Pilgrim's® brands. It is the highest-margin segment (10.7% operating margin in 2025) and the one most exposed to the current cycle turn.
- **Europe** (~29%, $5.4B): U.K./Ireland/France/Netherlands operations spanning fresh poultry, pork, lamb, ready meals, and prepared foods (Pilgrim's Food Masters, Moy Park). Much is retailer private label. Lower margin (5.1%) but improving on restructuring and pricing pass-through.
- **Mexico** (~11%, $2.1B): commodity chicken, the most affordable local protein. Historically the most volatile segment (7.9% margin in 2025).

The core structural advantage is vertical integration (control of feed mills, hatcheries, growing, and processing), which lets PPC manage food safety, yields, and margins better than fragmented competitors. In the U.S. it competes with Tyson, Wayne-Sanderson, Perdue, and Koch Foods. The defining feature relative to peers is JBS's 82.2% ownership, which provides global procurement scale, shared corporate services (SAP, tax sharing), and related-party synergies, but also concentrates control and subordinates minority interests.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a near-peak year followed by a sharp early-2026 margin reset.

| Metric | 2023 (trough) | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $17.4B | $17.9B | $18.5B (+3.5%) | $4.53B (+1.6% YoY) |
| Gross profit | $1.12B | $2.31B | $2.36B | $345M |
| Operating margin | 3.0% | 8.4% | 8.7% | **3.6%** (vs 9.1% Q1'25) |
| Net income (attr.) | $322M | $1.086B | $1.082B | $101M (vs $296M) |
| Diluted EPS | — | $4.56 | $4.54 | $0.43 (vs $1.24) |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | $2.21B | $2.27B | $308M |

The 2023 trough (net income $322M) versus the 2024-25 peak (~$1.08B) shows the amplitude of this cycle: earnings can swing 3x. The Q1 2026 data is the operative signal, not the FY2025 annual pull. U.S. gross profit collapsed as pricing fell against rising industry supply and higher live-operations, grower-pay, and utility costs. Mexico gross profit fell 44% on commodity price declines. Only Europe grew (+3.5%).

Cash generation is high-quality: $1.37B operating cash flow in 2025 (down from $1.99B in 2024 on working-capital build and the absence of prior-year one-time items), with $141M OCF in Q1'26. ROE of 29.4% and ROA of 10.5% in 2025 are peak-cycle figures that will compress as earnings normalize.

The balance sheet is sound. Roughly $3.1B in unsecured senior notes mature 2031-2034 (coupons 3.5%-6.875%), ~$1.1B of undrawn revolver, and $542M cash at Q1'26. Debt-to-equity is 1.81 and the Altman Z of 3.29 sits in the safe zone. There is no refinancing cliff. Management is actively managing liabilities, having tendered $250M of 6.25% 2033 notes in April 2026 at 105.7 ($264M cost).

The capital-allocation story is the key financial fact. In 2025, PPC paid $2.0B in special dividends ($6.30/share in April, $2.10/share in September), funded from the cash pile built in 2024. This drained cash from $2.04B to $640M and cut equity from $4.24B to $3.68B. Given JBS's 82.2% stake, this was overwhelmingly a parent-directed upstreaming of cash. Capex is stepping up sharply: $711M in 2025, guided to $900-950M in 2026, with Q1'26 capex already $235M (+140% YoY) for growth, efficiency, and portfolio conversion. The combination of a drained cash position, rising capex, and a down-cycle in earnings means free cash flow will be materially tighter in 2026 than the headline history suggests.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term growth thesis; the direction of estimates is clearly negative. Consensus EPS revisions have moved sharply lower across every window:

|--------|-------------|---------|--------------|

| Next Quarter | $0.89 | $1.22 | -27.4% |
| Current Year | $2.78 | $3.93 | -29.3% |
| Next Year | $3.16 | $4.03 | -21.7% |

The earnings surprise record confirms deteriorating visibility: after two beats in mid-2025 (+6.7%, +7.7%), PPC missed by 11.4% in Q4'25 and by 27.6% in Q1'26. A streak of beats followed by widening misses is a signature of a cyclical rolling over faster than analysts model.

The medium-term picture is a commodity cycle, not secular growth. Revenue growth has been low-single-digit (2025 +3.5%), and the swing variable is the spread between feed costs and protein prices, which is currently compressing on the pricing side. Feed remains the offsetting positive: corn, soy, and wheat sit below prior-year levels with ample stocks, cushioning the cost side. Europe is the one relative bright spot (restructuring benefit, GBP tailwind, pricing pass-through), and the capex-funded conversion of a commodity plant to U.S. retail is a modest mix-improvement lever. None of this offsets a poultry cycle in its supply-glut phase. Growth prospects score low: the strong-estimate-revision buy signal that would apply to an inflection stock is present here in reverse, with revisions falling ~30% over 90 days.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing 7.6x P/E is the wrong anchor. It capitalizes 2025 peak earnings of $4.54, and the business is entering the down-leg of its cycle. On the more relevant forward basis (consensus FY26 EPS of $2.78) the multiple is approximately 10.2x, and on next-year $3.16 it is ~9.0x. Neither is a compelling discount for a commodity protein processor without a dividend, carrying controlling-shareholder and litigation overhangs.

Peer comparison reinforces this. Packaged Foods & Meats peer medians are ~15.7x P/E and 3.4% yield. PPC's ~10x forward P/E is below the median, but the appropriate comparable within the group is Cal-Maine (CALM) at 5.5x, another commodity-protein cyclical (eggs) that trades at a structural discount to branded, dividend-paying staples like Campbell's (11.2x, 6.8% yield) and Lamb Weston (21.1x). PPC deserves a discount to the branded names because its earnings are commodity-driven and lumpy, and it pays no regular dividend. Against CALM's 5.5x, PPC's forward multiple is not obviously cheap for the same class of business.

A low P/E on a cyclical peak is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, absent a specific catalyst for the market being wrong. We do not see one. The stock's ~40% decline is the market correctly pricing the cycle turn, not an overreaction: the Q1'26 U.S. gross-profit halving and the still-falling estimate stream are the fundamental deterioration that justifies the move. The 90d estimate cuts are larger in percentage terms than the further downside implied by any modest multiple compression, meaning the price has largely tracked fundamentals rather than overshooting them.

Analyst targets frame a wide range: low $30, median $31.5, mean $33.56, high $42, against the current $28.43. With estimates falling and sentiment degrading (a recent buy-to-hold downgrade left the coverage at 7 holds, 2 buys/strong buys, 0 sells), we treat the median (~$31.5, ~11% upside) as the realistic base rather than the mean. Because estimates are being cut, not raised, the analyst-targets-as-floors principle does not apply; these targets are more likely to drift down toward spot than up. We score valuation at neutral: not stretched, but not offering asymmetric upside at a peak-earnings-capitalized multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-favorable at the structural level and neutral on trajectory. Vertical integration and JBS-scale procurement should let PPC defend margins better than smaller peers (Perdue, Koch, regional processors) through the down-cycle; the industry supply glut and price weakness are sector-wide dynamics, not company-specific share loss. USDA data (record egg sets, higher chick placements, improved hatchability and liveweights) confirms the production increase is an industry phenomenon.

Chicken retains its position as the value protein of choice amid elevated beef prices and strained consumer budgets, which supports demand even as pricing softens. Europe is genuinely improving on restructuring and pricing discipline. Customer concentration is a modest structural pressure: the two largest customers are ~16.8% of consolidated sales, and retail consolidation pressures pricing over time. We do not see PPC gaining or losing meaningful share; the story is a cyclical margin reset that affects all vertically-integrated producers similarly. The trajectory is stable rather than improving or deteriorating, which supports a middling competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity cyclicality (dominant, now materializing).** The 2023-to-2025 earnings swing (3x) shows the amplitude, and Q1'26 confirms the down-cycle has begun on the pricing side. The key monitorable is whether industry supply discipline returns before the feed-cost cushion erodes.

**Avian influenza.** HPAI H5 is detected across multiple U.S. states and described in filings as "increasingly common." Outbreaks can force culls, export bans, and demand shocks; African swine fever is a tail risk for U.K. pork.

**Antitrust litigation — structural and recurring.** The Broiler Antitrust Litigation has cost PPC $706M cumulatively ($22.9M in Q1'26 alone), with litigation-settlement expense of $163M in 2025 and $167M in 2024. The balance-sheet accrual rose to $191M at Q1'26; Phase 2 motions to dismiss were denied in February 2025 and discovery with "Broiler Opt-Outs" continues. This is a semi-permanent drag on GAAP earnings that adjusted EBITDA strips out; watch the widening gap between GAAP and adjusted figures. The December 2025 Trump food-sector antitrust executive order directing DOJ/FTC investigations is a fresh, unquantified overhang disclosed in PPC's own filings.

**Controlling-shareholder / governance risk.** JBS's 82.2% ownership permits appointment of up to 8 of 10 board seats and means capital-return and strategic decisions serve the parent first, with the 2025 $2.0B special dividend as direct evidence. The *City of Miami Beach Fire and Police Pension Fund v. JBS Wisconsin Properties* derivative suit (Delaware Chancery, filed July 2025) alleges directors breached fiduciary duties via a 2024 charter amendment benefiting JBS at minority expense; a tentative settlement was reached April 10, 2026 (terms pending). A Tax Sharing Agreement (effective 12/30/2024) folded PPC into JBS USA's consolidated tax group.

**Mexican tax disputes.** The Tyson de México/Provemex assessment is ~$269.5M under appeal with $88.2M accrued as a probable loss (Tax Court reaffirmed against PPC March 2026). A separate Avícola 2010 matter carries $18.1M accrued, and a U.K. HMRC review has a hearing set for early 2027 (no accrual).

**FX exposure.** A 10% GBP move equals ~$100M net-asset swing; a 10% MXN move ~$64-79M. EUDR (effective December 2026) raises European compliance costs.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 50.7% versus 30.2% one-year historical volatility, rich by 20.4 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (50.7% near-term vs 40.0% at six months), which typically signals an anticipated event. The nearest expiry prices a ±11.1% implied move, consistent with the July 29-30 Q2 print. Put/call skew is elevated at 18.2% and IV has risen 16 points (34.7% to 50.7%) since mid-June as the stock fell 7.1%, indicating hedging demand into the report. The market expects a volatile Q2 and is leaning toward downside protection.

Short interest is low at 2.8% of float (4.0 days to cover), meaning this is not a crowded bear thesis and there is no squeeze setup: informed investors are not aggressively short, but neither are they defending the name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36-42):** The broiler cycle bottoms faster than feared as industry producers pull back supply into 2H 2026, restoring pricing discipline. Feed costs stay low (ample corn/soy/wheat stocks), widening the margin spread on any pricing recovery. Europe continues to improve on restructuring and GBP strength. Chicken's value-protein positioning holds demand firm against expensive beef. Q2 estimates ($0.66-0.89) prove to be the trough, and estimate revisions turn positive within two quarters, driving a re-rate toward the analyst high of $42 as normalized EPS recovers toward $3.50-4.00. In this scenario, the $2.0B of 2025 special dividends looks like well-timed capital return, and the sound balance sheet supports the ramp in growth capex.

**Bear Case ($22-24):** Industry supply continues to outrun demand through 2026 as high placements and improved livability keep production elevated, driving U.S. pricing further below the five-year average. Soft foodservice traffic and slowing retail growth (1.3% vs 2.8% prior year) compress volumes. Feed-cost relief proves temporary, squeezing the spread from both sides. Estimates keep falling toward and below $2.50, and the ~$900-950M capex step-up combined with the drained post-dividend balance sheet pressures free cash flow. The Trump antitrust EO produces a material investigation, and the Provemex tax assessment resolves unfavorably. The stock derates toward ~9x on further-reduced estimates, implying $22-24.

**Base Case ($30-32):** The cycle grinds through a shallow trough with Q2 near the bottom. Normalized EPS settles around $2.78-3.16, the litigation and governance overhangs persist as known-and-priced drags, and the stock holds a ~10x multiple on stabilizing estimates. Modest upside to the analyst median (~$31.5) but no re-rate absent a clear pricing recovery.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the business is sound but there is no asymmetric edge at $28 with estimates still falling and no bottoming signal.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 29-30, 2026 Q2 earnings call is the immediate catalyst. It will show whether U.S. pricing is stabilizing as industry supply grows, and whether Q2 marks the estimate trough. The options market is pricing a ±11.1% move around it.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive quarters, or the Q2 print beats the reduced $0.66-0.89 range and management signals U.S. pricing has stabilized (a genuine cycle bottom).
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives (none currently; the only recent open-market transaction was a small CFO tax-related sale at $43.52 in February 2026).
- A pullback toward $22-24 (the bear-case level), where forward valuation would offer genuine asymmetry against a normalizing earnings base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 U.S. gross profit declines further sequentially with no pricing stabilization, and FY26 EPS estimates fall below $2.50.
- The December 2025 antitrust EO advances to a formal, material investigation, or the Provemex tax matter (~$269.5M exposure) resolves adversely beyond the $88.2M accrued.
- Free cash flow turns negative on the capex step-up combined with continued margin compression.

## Conclusion

Pilgrim's Pride is a financially sound, well-run poultry producer caught in the down-leg of the broiler cycle. The ~40% share decline correctly reflects a real margin reset rather than an overreaction to unchanged fundamentals. The trailing 7.6x P/E capitalizes peak 2025 earnings; on trough-normalizing forward estimates the ~10x multiple is roughly fair for a no-dividend commodity cyclical carrying an 82.2% JBS controlling stake, a recurring litigation drag, and a fresh antitrust EO of unquantified scope.

We rate PPC a HOLD. There is no asymmetric opportunity here: estimates are still falling, options markets are pricing imminent stress, and no insider is buying the decline. The balance sheet is solid and the business is not impaired, but a compressed multiple on peak earnings is not a buy signal until the cycle turns. The July 29-30 Q2 print is the decisive input; a confirmed pricing bottom would move us constructive, while further U.S. price deterioration and sub-$2.50 estimates would move us to AVOID. For now, the risk/reward is balanced with a modest downward tilt while the cycle finds its floor.
