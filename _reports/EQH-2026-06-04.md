**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Equitable Holdings is a diversified U.S. retirement, life and asset-management company whose reported financials are difficult to read at face value. FY2025 showed a $1.38B GAAP net loss and negative stockholders' equity, which is why the screen-level trailing and forward P/E both print as -13.2x. Those figures are products of LDTI market-risk-benefit (MRB) accounting on variable-annuity guarantees and two deliberate de-risking transactions executed in 2025 (the RGA quota-share cession, booked at a $1.1B loss, and the Venerable novation at a $499M pre-tax loss). The economic business is sound: Q1 2026 produced $472M of non-GAAP operating earnings (+12% YoY), operating EPS of $1.62 versus $1.30, and a trailing-twelve-month operating ROE of 29.8% against modest holdco leverage of roughly $3.84B on $310B of assets.

The investment case has been reshaped by the March 26, 2026 announcement of an all-stock "merger of equals" with Corebridge Financial (CRBG). A new parent will be renamed Equitable Holdings, with close expected by end-2026 (outside date December 26, 2026). This converts the EQH thesis from a standalone valuation exercise into a hybrid merger-arbitrage and pro-forma-combined-entity situation. At ~$40, the stock trades below Q1 2026 buyback prints ($46.51 average), below the entire published analyst target range ($51 low, $63 high, $57.92 mean), and at roughly 5.7x current-year operating EPS consensus of $7.06.

We rate EQH a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The 12-month thesis is concrete: a dated merger catalyst, demonstrated operating earnings power, and a valuation that already discounts considerable bad news. What keeps us from a higher conviction call is the combination of falling consensus estimates (current-year EPS revised down 6.2% over 90 days), genuine merger-completion risk (a $475M termination fee, a 75% AllianceBernstein advisory-consent condition, six-state regulatory approval, and votes from both shareholder bases), an all-stock structure whose value now floats with Corebridge's share price, and an options market pricing imminent stress.

## Company Overview

EQH operates three reportable segments (reorganized effective July 1, 2025):

- **Retirement** — the core engine, holding ~$192B of the company's ~$310B in assets and generating $396M of Q1 2026 operating earnings (up from $380M). Products include the Structured Capital Strategies (SCS) registered index-linked annuity, EQUI-VEST 403(b)/457(b) tax-exempt products, Investment Edge, and institutional spread-lending products.
- **Asset Management** — its ~68% economic interest in AllianceBernstein (AB), a publicly traded global manager with $838.6B AUM. EQH consolidates AB fully via control of the general partner. Q1 2026 operating earnings of $140M.
- **Wealth Management** — the Equitable Advisors retail platform, $131B in assets under administration, expanding through the Stifel Independent Advisors acquisition ($4.5B of advisory assets added in Q1 2026). Q1 2026 operating earnings of $55M.

A Corporate & Other bucket holds run-off blocks: the demutualization-legacy Closed Block, Individual Life, Employee Benefits, and legacy variable annuities (GMxB Legacy).

The differentiating feature is vertical integration: EQH manufactures (Retirement, Life), distributes (Wealth Management), and manages assets (AB), which lets it capture fees across the value chain and feed AB captive AUM. Insurance financial-strength ratings are A (AM Best) / A+ (S&P) / A1 (Moody's) at Equitable Financial Life, with holdco credit at bbb+/A-/Baa1.

## Financial Analysis

The single most important interpretive point is that the headline loss is not distress. The −$74M stockholders' equity attributable to Holdings at year-end 2025 and the $1.38B net loss derive from two sources: (1) MRB fair-value swings on variable-annuity guarantees that flow through net income and OCI as rates and the company's own credit spread move, and (2) the two voluntary 2025 de-risking transactions. AOCI sits at −$6.3B, driven almost entirely by rate-driven unrealized losses on the available-for-sale bond portfolio rather than credit deterioration. Standard solvency ratios (Z-Score, debt/equity on negative equity) are uninformative for a life insurer with this accounting structure, which is why financial-health screens return "insufficient data."

The operating picture is materially stronger than the GAAP optics suggest:

- Q1 2026 net income attributable to Holdings of $621M (versus $63M in Q1 2025), diluted EPS of $2.14.
- Non-GAAP operating earnings of $472M (+12% YoY); operating EPS $1.62 versus $1.30.
- Trailing-twelve-month operating ROE of 29.8% (GAAP ROE was −15.2% due to the noise above).
- Net investment income rose to $1.28B on higher yields and balances; total revenue of $4.23B was down YoY mainly because the RGA cession shrank the life block.
- Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $499M.

Capital return has been aggressive. A $1.0B buyback authorization was added in February 2026, leaving ~$1.9B remaining. The company repurchased 3.2M shares at an average $46.51 in Q1 2026, and share count has fallen from ~321M (2024) to ~281M. The quarterly common dividend was raised to $0.27. Shares acquired at $46.51 now trade near $40, so recent repurchases are underwater, though they reduce the share base ahead of the merger.

The most important structural constraint is dividend capacity. Equitable America paid a $1.7B extraordinary dividend approved by Arizona in 2025, but Equitable Financial (NY) had zero ordinary dividend capacity in both 2025 and 2026. Holdco cash therefore leans on Equitable America distributions and AB. Statutory surplus is also flattered by more than $1.4B from five NY/AZ permitted accounting practices (SSAP 108 hedge deferrals; SA 68/69 book-value treatment), which are legitimate but mean "true" NAIC-basis capital is lower than the reported figure.

## Growth Analysis

Operating earnings momentum is positive at the segment level. Retirement operating earnings rose, the Stifel acquisition is scaling Wealth Management, and net investment income is benefiting from higher reinvestment yields. Consensus operating EPS of $7.06 for the current year and $8.79 for next year implies roughly 24% forward growth.

Two facts temper this. First, consensus EPS estimates are falling across every horizon: current quarter −8.6% over 90 days, current year −6.2%, next year −3.1%. The 30-day moves remain negative (current year −2.2%), so the deceleration is recent and persistent, not a one-time reset. Second, AB had a soft quarter: $7.1B of net long-term outflows and $21.2B of market depreciation, partly offset by growth in Alternatives/Multi-Asset (now ~$200B average AUM, up from $168B) and Private Wealth (+$20B). Active-to-passive pressure is a named risk in the filings.

The dominant growth variable is now the Corebridge combination. The strategic logic in a capital-intensive industry is scale: lower hedging costs, more diversified liabilities, and improved access to private-credit asset origination. Whether that logic translates into per-share value depends entirely on the exchange ratio and pro-forma ownership split, which are not disclosed in the 10-Q and require the definitive merger 8-K and joint S-4 to assess. Until those economics are public, the growth thesis is partly contingent on deal terms we cannot yet evaluate.

## Valuation Assessment

On operating earnings, EQH looks cheap: ~5.7x current-year operating EPS and ~4.5x next-year $8.79. Those multiples are not comparable to the −13.2x GAAP figure, and they sit below the financials peer-group median P/E of 12.1x. The published analyst targets all sit 27–57% above the current price, and the buy-side recommendation distribution is bullish (3 strong buy, 10 buy, 0 hold/sell). The stock also trades below recent buyback prints.

Peer comparison illustrates the dispersion in this group. First Horizon trades at 12.1x, Webster at 11.9x, Globe Life at 10.4x, Jefferies at 18.2x, and Corebridge itself at 57.2x with a −29% drawdown from its 52-week high. The peer set is heterogeneous, so a single multiple anchor is not meaningful. On operating earnings power and absolute yield (3.0%), EQH screens cheap; on GAAP optics it screens as a loss-maker. The truth is closer to the former.

The valuation case is real but not unencumbered. The all-stock structure means EQH's market value now moves with Corebridge's share price, so the standalone operating-earnings framing is incomplete. The analyst targets predate full clarity on deal economics, and falling estimates push fair value lower at any given multiple. We score valuation a 6.5: there is identifiable asymmetric upside (below buyback, well below targets, cheap on operating earnings) but the merger converts a clean discount into an arbitrage situation, and the negative revision trend caps how aggressively we credit the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

EQH is a top-tier U.S. annuity writer with a differentiated SCS/RILA franchise and an integrated manufacturer-distributor-manager model that few peers replicate. It competes against Corebridge (now its merger partner), Brighthouse, Jackson, Lincoln, Athene/Apollo, and Prudential in annuities, while AB competes across the full asset-management complex.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The core Retirement franchise is stable-to-improving, with growth in spread-based products and rising net investment income from higher yields. AB is the softer link, with net long-term outflows in Q1 and structural active-to-passive pressure, partially offset by alternatives and private-wealth growth. The Corebridge combination would create greater scale in a business where size lowers hedging costs and improves private-credit origination access, a strategic rationale the market will scrutinize once economics are disclosed.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger completion risk** is now the dominant idiosyncratic driver. Conditions include majority shareholder votes from both companies, HSR clearance, insurance regulatory approvals in Arizona, Colorado, Missouri, New York, Texas, and Vermont, S-4 effectiveness, Section 351 tax treatment, and consent from clients representing 75% of AB-type advisory fees (an Investment Advisers Act assignment hurdle most insurance mergers do not face). The termination fee is $475M either direction. Pre-closing covenants restrict EQH's strategic flexibility. This is a binary, dated event resolving by end-2026.

**Market-sensitivity / guarantee risk.** Variable-annuity guarantees (GMDB/GMIB/GWBL) create large tail exposure to equity declines and rate moves, with ~$15.4B net amount at risk on GMxB Legacy alone. The CTE98-targeted hedge program drives significant GAAP net-income volatility ($580M net derivative gains in Q1 2026, $2.4B embedded-derivative swings).

**Liquidity / dividend constraint.** Zero ordinary dividend capacity at Equitable Financial (NY) under Reg 213 concentrates upstreaming reliance on Equitable America and AB. The intercompany structure is intricate (an EQH facility with $705M drawn, a $600M Equitable America–to–Holdings loan in March 2026).

**Investment / credit risk.** The $84.6B AFS book carries $6.4B of gross unrealized losses (rate-driven). Commercial mortgages have a 67% average LTV, with $3.0B (17%) maturing in 2026 and office at 20% of the $23B mortgage book; non-accruals rose to $49M from $11M quarter-over-quarter. Growing private credit ($19.9B) and CLO consolidation expose EQH to tightening NAIC structured-security RBC rules effective year-end 2026.

**Regulatory.** NAIC AG 55, VM-22 PBR, and the vacated 2024 DOL fiduciary rule create background uncertainty for an annuity-centric distributor.

We score risk factors a 4.5, reflecting specific, dated binary events (merger close/fail) and an options market actively pricing stress.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing event risk. Near-term ATM IV is 66.1% against 31.8% trailing realized volatility (a 34.3-point premium, 71st percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (66.1% at one month versus 40.2% at six months). Put/call open interest is 5.77 with a 19.2% put skew, and ATM IV jumped 18.6 points in a single session (47.5% to 66.1%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.6%. This profile is consistent with the merger pendency plus the June 18 expiry and does not contradict a HOLD; it confirms that the market expects a sizeable, near-term move and is paying up for downside protection. We would treat the rich front-end IV as a reason to favor patience over chasing the stock here. Short interest is negligible (effectively 0% of float), so the move the market fears is not a short-driven one.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The GAAP loss masks a business earning a 29.8% operating ROE with operating EPS up 12% YoY. At ~$40, the stock trades at ~5.7x current-year operating EPS, below recent buyback prints and well under the $51-63 analyst target range. The Corebridge merger closes by end-2026 at favorable terms, creating a larger, more diversified retirement and asset-management platform with lower hedging costs and stronger private-credit origination. Estimates stabilize as the de-risking transactions roll off and net investment income compounds. Continued buybacks against a shrinking share count amplify per-share value. Re-rating toward the analyst mean of ~$58 implies ~44% upside.

### Bear Case

The all-stock structure ties EQH's value to Corebridge, which trades at a steep discount to its own 52-week high; if CRBG weakens or the exchange ratio disappoints, EQH falls with it. Falling consensus estimates (current year −6.2% in 90 days) prove to be the start of a longer earnings reset rather than noise. The merger faces a high condition bar (six-state regulatory approval, 75% AB advisory consent, both shareholder votes); a break triggers the $475M fee and removes the primary catalyst. CRE office rollover ($3.0B maturing in 2026) and rising non-accruals pressure investment income, and the zero NY dividend capacity constrains holdco cash. A renewed equity or rate shock magnifies GMxB hedge volatility. In a deal-break-plus-estimate-cut scenario, the stock revisits the low-to-mid $30s (the 52-week low was $35.20).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the definitive merger S-4/joint proxy (which will disclose the exchange ratio and pro-forma ownership), the Q2 2026 earnings release, and the regulatory-approval cadence ahead of the targeted end-2026 close.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Publication of merger economics (exchange ratio / pro-forma ownership) on terms that imply per-share value at or above the current analyst mean, combined with stabilizing consensus EPS revisions (current-year revisions turning flat-to-positive over a 60-day window).
- Clearing of the AB 75% advisory-consent condition and HSR, materially de-risking completion.
- Evidence that operating ROE holds at or above the mid-20s% range and AB net flows turn neutral-to-positive across a quarter.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Termination of the Corebridge merger (with the $475M fee paid) absent a credible standalone re-rating path.
- Acceleration of negative estimate revisions (current-year EPS cut by more than another 10% over a quarter) signaling a genuine earnings reset rather than accounting noise.
- A material deterioration in CRE/office (non-accruals climbing materially above the current $49M and rising LTVs on the 2026 maturity wall) or a renewed GMxB hedge-loss event that impairs statutory capital and dividend capacity.

## Conclusion

EQH is a fundamentally sound insurer trading at a deep discount to operating earnings power, with a dated merger catalyst and a price below both recent buyback levels and the full analyst target range. Those are the ingredients of a constructive 12-month thesis, which is why we view this as a growth-oriented HOLD. The reasons we stop short of a buy are specific and current: consensus estimates are falling across every horizon, the all-stock merger ties EQH's value to Corebridge while introducing a high-bar completion process, the exchange-ratio economics that determine per-share value are not yet public, and the options market is pricing imminent stress. The economic business deserves more credit than its GAAP optics imply, but the path to realizing that value runs through a merger whose terms and approval we cannot yet underwrite. We would accumulate on clarity around deal economics and stabilizing estimates rather than into front-end event risk.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Overall score 5.9/10.**
