## Executive Summary

Lumentum is now a fundamentally different company than the one described in its last audited annual report. FY2025 (ended June 28, 2025) produced $1,645.0M of revenue, a $180.1M operating loss, negative $104.7M of free cash flow, and positive net income of $25.9M only because of a $198.0M income tax benefit that included a $153.1M UK valuation-allowance release. Three quarters later, the March 2026 quarter delivered $808.4M of revenue (+90.1% YoY), 44.2% GAAP gross margin, $174.5M of GAAP operating income (21.6% margin), and $144.2M of net income. Quarterly GAAP net income progressed $4.2M to $78.2M to $144.2M through FY2026. Management guided the June quarter to $960–1,010M of revenue and $2.85–$3.05 of non-GAAP EPS, roughly +105% revenue growth at the midpoint against a $480.7M year-ago base. The driver is a supply-constrained position in merchant lasers (EMLs, narrow-linewidth lasers and pump lasers) feeding AI data-center optics, corroborated in the filings by purchase obligations more than doubling to $1,795.6M and nine-month capex rising 60.6% to $284.5M.

The problem is price. At $809.78 the shares carry ~43x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $18.73 and roughly 25x EV to FY2026E revenue of ~$2,993M on a properly diluted share base. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $18.73 already assumes substantial further growth beyond the June-quarter run rate. For the current price to be supported by a normal-cycle 25x multiple on a component supplier past its peak growth, earnings would need to reach roughly $32 per share (about 1.7x consensus FY2027), which is a bet on a second doubling beyond what the sell-side already models. That bet is being made into 38% two-customer revenue concentration (worsened from 32% a year ago), zero disclosed backlog or RPO ($9.5M of deferred revenue and customer deposits, $6.9M of which is an acquired below-market contract liability), a live Bureau of Industry and Security and Department of Justice inquiry stemming from the company's own December 2023 voluntary self-disclosure, and roughly 11.2M shares of pending convertible dilution equal to ~12.2 percentage points of ownership.

We rate LITE HOLD. Fundamentals have improved materially since our last review: free cash flow turned positive, the convertible overhang is now cash-covered at 1.43x, and next-year estimates rose 3.3% in 30 days. Expected return from $809.78 is approximately fair. Our probability-weighted 12-month value is ~$853 (+5%), built from a $900 base, $1,250 bull and $450 bear. The June-quarter print was scheduled for release hours after this report's information cutoff with a two-day implied move of ±10.7% and one-month implied move of ±16.8%, near-term implied volatility of 144.3% against 93.3% realized, in backwardation. That is not a setup in which to initiate a full position.

## Company Overview

Lumentum designs and manufactures optical and photonic chips, components, modules and subsystems. Spun out of JDS Uniphase in August 2015, it has been assembled through acquisition: Oclaro (2018, indium phosphide lasers and coherent components), NeoPhotonics (2022, telecom transmission lines), and Cloud Light Technology (November 2023), which supplies the datacom transceiver franchise now carrying much of the AI module revenue.

The portfolio spans four commercially distinct areas: merchant laser chips and photonic components (EMLs, photonic integrated circuits, photodiodes, ultra-narrow-linewidth lasers for coherent applications, pump lasers for optical amplifiers, VCSELs); 800G/1.6T datacom transceivers and coherent pluggables; optical transport subsystems including ROADMs, wavelength-selective switches and optical circuit switches; and industrial/consumer lasers.

Beginning Q1 FY2026 the company consolidated from two reportable segments to one under a unified management team with the CEO as chief operating decision maker. Disclosure is now limited to a two-line product split: Components $533.3M (66.0%) and Systems $275.1M (34.0%) in Q3 FY2026, versus 70.7%/29.3% a year earlier. This eliminated visibility into cloud/AI economics versus the industrial and consumer drag at precisely the point mix was changing fastest, a factual reduction in transparency, with the company's stated reason (reorganization) the plausible explanation.

Manufacturing spans owned wafer fabs and assembly/test sites in the US, Thailand, China, UK, Slovenia and Japan, supplemented by contract manufacturers in Thailand, Taiwan, Malaysia and the Philippines. Headcount was ~10,562 at June 28, 2025. IP position: ~1,020 US patents, ~1,100 foreign patents.

### Cash flow quality — improving, with three real qualifications

Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow was $388.4M versus $62.3M; capex $284.5M versus $177.1M; derived free cash flow +$103.9M versus −$114.8M, or 5.2% of nine-month revenue. FY2025 full-year FCF was −$104.7M. The direction is unambiguous.

Three offsets matter. First, non-cash add-backs are large: SBC $129.4M, intangible amortization $102.2M, depreciation $91.2M. Second, working capital is a growing drag: receivables rose 76.6% to $441.6M and inventory 34.6% to $632.8M, a net ~$195M nine-month use partially offset by payables. At 90%+ revenue growth this is mechanically normal, but it means reported FCF will trail reported earnings for as long as growth persists. Third, cash taxes are artificially low: $41.1M paid against a $58.9M book provision, with a full valuation allowance still standing on US federal/state deferred tax assets despite three profitable quarters. Cash interest was $12.8M on $3.2B of debt at coupons of 0.375%–1.50%. Both normalize upward.

Nine-month pre-tax income of $285.5M was flattered by a one-time $27.5M escrow settlement tied to the Cloud Light acquisition indemnity, approximately 9.6% of the total. Separately, the warranty reserve rose to $25.0M from $14.4M, with $9.8M of the nine-month provision specifically attributed to Cloud Light legacy products. The benign reading is warranty accruals scaling with a doubled revenue base; the company's own "legacy products" language points toward field-quality issues in the acquired transceiver line, partially recovered from escrow.

### Capital structure: the analytical crux

Standard screens read this balance sheet as distressed. They are wrong, but the real risk is specific rather than absent.

At March 28, 2026 Lumentum carried $3,198.4M of convertible principal across four series (0.50% 2026 Notes, 0.50% 2028 Notes, 1.50% 2029 Notes, 0.375% 2032 Notes) plus $98.4M of yen term loans. All series were reclassified to current liabilities because the stock traded above 130% of conversion price for 20 of 30 trading days, collapsing the current ratio from 4.37 to 1.14. Any screen reading that as a liquidity crisis is reading a symptom of the stock having quadrupled. Similarly, an Altman Z-Score computed on FY2025 book equity produces a "distress" reading; substituting market equity and the March 2026 balance sheet produces Z ≈ 10. Neither number is decision-useful, because 9.4 of the 10 comes from a market-equity term reflecting a ~21x sales valuation; the model is circular here.

The economically meaningful disclosure is that the notes carry a fair value of $19,074.7M against $3,183.4M of carrying value. Roughly $15.9B of equity claim sits inside instruments booked at $3.2B, which no conventional debt-to-equity or EV calculation captures.

Deleveraging has been active. September 2025: repurchased $581.1M of 2026 Notes principal for $843.1M cash, funded by $1,254.7M of net 2032 Note proceeds. April 7, 2026: privately negotiated exchanges retired ~$474.6M of principal for ~5.7M shares with no cash outflow. Conversion requests of $500.8M principal were received as of April 30. Remaining principal derives to ~$2,223M ($52.0M 2026, $68.8M 2029, ~$837.5M 2028, $1,265.0M 2032) against cash and short-term investments of $3,172.3M (1.43x coverage), plus a $400M undrawn revolver and positive year-to-date FCF. The conversion wave is fundable.

Management nonetheless escalated its own language, stating in the Q3 10-Q that if holders convert a significant portion within a short period, liquidity "could adversely impact our ability to continue as a going concern." This is conditional drafting, not an auditor qualification, and the arithmetic above suggests coverage is adequate. It should be quoted accurately rather than dismissed.

Two structural changes deserve attention. On December 19, 2025 Lumentum entered a $400M **senior secured** revolver maturing 2030, with quarterly covenants (secured net leverage ≤3.25x, interest coverage ≥3.00x) and a springing maturity 91 days before the final convertible maturity if liquidity/leverage tests are not met. The company moved from covenant-light unsecured convertibles to granting a security interest over substantially all assets. Neither covenant is close to binding at current EBITDA, but the springing maturity creates a linkage that becomes relevant if the equity de-rates and the notes fall out of the money.

### Dilution: the number the headline market cap omits

The $63.2B headline cap implies ~77.8M shares — common outstanding after the April exchange, excluding both the NVIDIA preferred and all note dilution. Net-share-settlement at $809.78 adds approximately 11.2M shares from the remaining series (2028 ~5.4M, 2032 ~4.5M net of capped calls, 2026 ~0.5M, 2029 ~0.9M). Adding 2.9M NVIDIA preferred (1-for-1 convertible) and ~3.4M outstanding equity awards gives ~95M fully diluted shares, cross-checked against the 96.2M weighted diluted count Lumentum itself reported for Q3.

Stated both ways: the 11.2M conversion shares equal +14.0% of the 80.3M as-converted base, reducing existing holders' proportional ownership from 100% to ~87.8%, approximately 12.2 percentage points of ownership dilution. The 2032 Notes' capped calls (cost $102.0M, cap $268.24) provide only a fixed ~$542M offset against a ~$4,207M excess conversion value at current prices, leaving roughly 87% of that series' dilution unhedged. Dilution is price-dependent and shrinks if the stock falls; the company also retains the right to settle excess conversion value in cash, substituting outflow for dilution.

Fully diluted equity value is therefore ~$77.2B, and enterprise value ~$76.4B, approximately 22% above the headline figure.

## Growth Analysis

The demand case is credible and filing-corroborated on the supply side. Management (via externally reported commentary, August 7, 2026) describes an EML supply-demand gap above 30%, tighter pump-laser constraints, allocation decisions among customers, and narrow-linewidth and pump products "effectively sold out for the foreseeable future." Q3 shipment growth: narrow-linewidth lasers >120% YoY, pump lasers +80% YoY. The 800G→1.6T transition is a content tailwind because 200G EMLs carry roughly twice the ASP of the 100G parts used in 800G. Management expects optical scale-up — connectivity inside and between AI racks — to inflect from 2027, with optical intensity potentially rising at least three times, and characterizes that opportunity as larger than scale-out.

The filing-based evidence of management's own conviction is the capacity build: purchase obligations of $1,795.6M versus $837.6M at fiscal year-end, construction in progress $248.4M versus $152.3M, gross machinery and equipment $1,059.7M versus $848.8M, and a $38.0M operational fab acquired in Greensboro, NC (with a ~15-month transitional wafer-manufacturing arrangement for the unnamed seller, accounted for as agent and recognized net at $0.4M in Q3).

Two disciplines apply. Purchase obligations are commitments to suppliers, cancellable or reschedulable per the 10-Q, and constitute a bet rather than a backlog. And there is no customer backlog to underwrite the growth: deferred revenue plus customer deposits total $9.5M, of which $6.9M is an acquired below-market contract liability. The 10-K states backlog is not indicative of revenue and that a significant share of revenue arises from vendor-managed inventory arrangements with unpredictable customer utilization timing. Forward visibility rests on guidance and customer forecasts.

Estimate momentum is positive but moderate rather than explosive: current quarter +10.2% over 90 days, next quarter +10.0%, next year +3.3% over 30 days and +3.8% over 90 days, current year flat at −0.2%. Four consecutive beats (average +9.6%) support confidence in guidance delivery. The revision velocity does not reach the >30%/90-day threshold that marks a systematically underestimated inflection; consensus has largely caught up.

The context is a broad, multi-company AI optics upcycle — Marvell +147.6% YTD, LITE +127.4%, Coherent +81.1%, Ciena +72.7% against a 16.9% sector — which raises the durability of the demand thesis relative to a single-company story. It does not make individual valuations self-justifying.

## Valuation Assessment

The profile's trailing and forward P/E of 142.66 is an artifact anchored on FY2025 GAAP EPS of $0.37 and should be discarded. Meaningful metrics:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Price / FY2026E non-GAAP EPS ($8.23 consensus) | ~98x |
| Price / FY2027E non-GAAP EPS ($18.73 consensus) | ~43x |
| EV / FY2026E revenue (~$2,993M) | ~25.5x |
| Headline mkt cap / FY2026E revenue | ~21.1x |

FY2027 consensus EPS of $18.73 represents +128% growth over FY2026's ~$8.23, giving a PEG well below 1. On that arithmetic the stock looks cheap. The qualification is that a PEG built on a single year of triple-digit inflection growth is not a durable multiple anchor, and consensus already embeds a second large step-up in earnings delivered from capacity that is still under construction.

A useful reverse test: for $809.78 to be justified by a 25x multiple — reasonable for a cyclical optical component supplier past its peak growth phase — earnings must reach approximately $32 per share, or ~$3.0B of net income on ~95M shares. At a 30% non-GAAP net margin that implies roughly $10B of revenue, more than triple FY2026E. The current price therefore embeds not just consensus FY2027 but continued compounding well beyond it.

Peer context is weak because the cohort is heterogeneous: Ciena at 137x P/E, F5 at 31.9x, Motorola Solutions at 36.8x, Viasat and Viavi loss-making. Forward price/sales is the more usable comparison: LITE ~10.9x versus Marvell 13x, Ciena 7.6x, Coherent 6.4x, sector 6.5x. LITE carries a premium to every named optical peer including the one NVIDIA reportedly also funded.

Analyst targets: mean $1,125.93, median $1,121, low $800, high $1,400 — mean +39% from spot, with the low target essentially at the current price. Sell-side skews positive (5 strong buy, 15 buy, 4 hold; Barclays upgraded to Overweight around July 20). On a stock at ~43x forward earnings, however, targets and price largely reflect the same growth extrapolation and are not independent confirmation.

Valuation score of 5.0 reflects the balance: the multiple is not indefensible against near-term growth, but there is no identified mispricing and no margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Lumentum's defensible position sits in merchant lasers. Reliability, performance and customer manufacturing-yield qualification create genuine switching friction, and the >30% EML supply gap gives it allocation power and pricing leverage, visible in non-GAAP gross margin expanding 540bp sequentially to 47.9%. Morgan Stanley names Lumentum, Corning and Coherent as the clearest beneficiaries of the copper-to-optical shift inside data centers.

The weaker position is in transceiver modules, where it competes with Coherent, Applied Optoelectronics and low-cost Asian module makers, and where the $9.8M Cloud Light legacy warranty provision suggests execution has not been clean. The FY2025 10-K states plainly that "some of these competitors are also our customers for certain of our products." Management's 1.6T ramp includes initial integration of internally sourced CW lasers, and stated intent to broaden into photonic ICs, photodiodes, laser drivers and possibly TIAs. Each step up the value chain converts a merchant laser customer into a rival. In a market with a 30%+ supply gap that conflict is largely theoretical; it becomes real when supply loosens, which is exactly when Lumentum would least want to lose merchant volume.

The NVIDIA relationship requires precision. Per the Q3 10-Q, NVIDIA paid $2.0B cash on March 2, 2026 for ~2.9M shares of Series A Convertible Preferred at $695.31, roughly the prevailing market price, consistent with arm's-length terms. The instrument converts 1-for-1 at holder option, participates in dividends, votes as-converted except on director elections, and carries no redemption rights. No commercial supply agreement, volume commitment, capacity reservation, board seat, standstill or related-party revenue is disclosed. Multiple tier-3 outlets report NVIDIA made a comparable $2B investment in Coherent, which would be consistent with NVIDIA securing supply breadth across competing laser suppliers rather than anointing a winner. Neither the possible commercial arrangement nor the Coherent investment is verified in Lumentum's filings; both are flagged as gaps. The "NVIDIA-backed" label is best read as capital plus a signal about the optical bottleneck, not as a contracted moat.

Sector beta is extreme and correlation dominates fundamentals over short windows: Applied Optoelectronics moved LITE +5% on July 14 and −7% on July 15 with no company-specific news, and Corning's in-line July guidance took Coherent, Lumentum and Ciena down together.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is worsening.** Q3 FY2026: two customers at 26% and 12% of revenue (38% combined) versus 17% and 15% (32%) a year earlier; nine-month FY2026 24% and 16% (40%). Gross receivable concentration moved from 13%/11% to 25%/10%, meaning one counterparty's payment behavior drives a quarter of receivables. Systems mix rose from 29.3% to 34.0% and ship-to geography is spread across the US 23.0%, Thailand 21.9%, Hong Kong 16.8%, Mexico 12.8% and China 8.7%, but ship-to locations reflect contract-manufacturer sites, and the direct concentration measure moved the wrong way. Customers are unnamed in every filing.

**Export controls with an open investigation.** Lumentum received inquiries from BIS and the DOJ in August 2024 following its own December 2023 voluntary self-disclosure (supplemented April 2024) and remains unable to predict the outcome. With 81% of FY2025 revenue shipped outside the US and Hong Kong plus China at 25.5% of Q3 revenue, this is an unquantified tail.

**Demonstrated cyclicality.** FY2024 revenue fell 23.1% and gross margin compressed to 18.5% on network-equipment destocking, with excess-capacity charges and E&O write-downs. The industrial business saw segment profit fall from $152.7M (FY2023) to $12.1M (FY2025). If AI optics demand normalizes, the $1,795.6M of purchase obligations and the current inventory build become excess-capacity liabilities on the same mechanism.

**Convertible timing and dilution.** ~$2.22B of principal must be settled in cash on holders' timetable, and ~11.2M shares (~12.2pp of ownership) will be issued at current prices. Capped-call protection stops at $268.24.

**Supplier concentration.** One contract manufacturer accounted for 19% of net inventory purchases in Q3 and nine-month FY2026, with sole- or limited-source arrangements acknowledged for certain components.

**Governance verification gap.** An 8-K filed July 30, 2026 under Item 5.02 (Departure/Election of Directors or Certain Officers) could not be retrieved. Circumstantial evidence points to a board addition (a July 15 director grant to a holder of only 1,313 shares) and the CEO was publicly confirmed in role on July 27, but prior Item 5.02 filings on January 5, 2026, December 15, 2025 and November 24, 2025 indicate a period of board and officer turnover. Unresolved.

**Unreported results.** The June-quarter and full-year FY2026 report was scheduled for August 11, 2026 after the close, after this report's information cutoff. No FY2026 10-K exists. All Q4 figures cited are guidance or consensus.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 144.3% against 93.3% one-year realized (a 51-point spread, 94th percentile within the chain), with the term structure in steep backwardation — 144.3% at two days, 96.7% at one month, 88.6% at three months, 88.2% at six months. One notable print: 988 contracts of September 4 $800 puts against 110 open interest (9.0x), a near-the-money hedge or bet around the earnings window.

Reading: the market expects a genuine binary at the print, with no directional consensus. Short interest of 12.5% of float at 1.8 days to cover reflects an active but easily covered short base, adding squeeze potential to an upside surprise. For a prospective buyer, initiating ahead of a ±11–17% priced move with no margin of safety is poorly compensated; for an existing holder, the call-heavy skew and stable OI structure do not argue for exiting.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $1,250 (+54%)

Q4 delivers at or above the $1,010M high end and management guides FY2027 above consensus. Capacity additions convert the >30% EML supply gap into shipped revenue; non-GAAP gross margin pushes through 50% on 200G EML mix at ~2x the ASP of 100G parts; the scale-up cycle inflects from 2027 as optical content inside AI racks multiplies. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS lands at $21–22 against consensus $18.73, and the market applies 55–58x on the view that FY2028 grows another 40%+. Cash generation clears the remaining $2.22B of convertibles without stress, cash taxes stay low on remaining NOLs, and a US valuation-allowance release adds a further non-cash EPS benefit. Requires: sustained hyperscaler capex, no share loss to second sources qualified during allocation, and no adverse BIS/DOJ outcome.

### Bear Case — $450 (−44%)

AI optics order rates normalize as capacity across Lumentum, Coherent and Applied Optoelectronics arrives simultaneously. The FY2024 pattern repeats in compressed form: the allocation premium disappears, the doubled inventory ($632.8M) and $1,795.6M of supplier commitments turn into E&O and excess-capacity charges, and gross margin retraces toward the mid-30s. FY2027 EPS lands near $12 rather than $18.73 and the multiple compresses to 35x, giving ~$420–455. Concentration accelerates the mechanism — a single 26% customer trimming orders moves revenue immediately, with no backlog to cushion. An adverse export-control resolution or a step down in the largest customer's allocation are the specific accelerants. The stock's own recent trading (a low near $594 on July 29 before recovering toward $920 in early August and back to ~$810) demonstrates the speed of such repricing.

### Base Case — $900 (+11%)

Q4 lands within guidance, FY2027 revenue reaches the consensus-implied range and non-GAAP EPS $18–19, and the multiple holds in the 47–50x range as investors extend the scale-up thesis into FY2028 while discounting deceleration. Convertibles are retired through a mix of cash and shares with ~12pp of ownership dilution absorbed. Concentration and export-control risks persist unresolved but unrealized.

**Probability-weighted 12-month value:** 45% base ($900) + 25% bull ($1,250) + 30% bear ($450) = **~$853, or +5% from $809.78.** That is approximately fair risk-adjusted return, with the distribution skewed by a −44% left tail against a +54% right tail, an asymmetry that argues for waiting rather than initiating.

## Conclusion

Lumentum's operating inflection is real and verifiable in the filings: revenue up 90.1% year over year in the March quarter, GAAP operating margin at 21.6% versus a −10.9% full-year FY2025 figure, nine-month free cash flow of +$103.9M against −$114.8M a year earlier, and $3.17B of cash and short-term investments covering the remaining ~$2.22B of convertible principal 1.43x.
