**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lennox International is a pure-play North American HVACR manufacturer that, since divesting its European operations in late 2023, derives roughly 93% of revenue domestically across two segments: Home Comfort Solutions (residential, 64% of FY2025 sales) and the higher-margin Building Climate Solutions (commercial/refrigeration, 36%). The business generates best-in-class economics: 33.4% gross margin, 20% operating margin, ~69% ROE, and investment-grade ratings (Baa1/BBB stable). On a screening basis, it looks like a quality compounder.

The central tension is that nearly all of Lennox's earnings growth from 2022 to 2025 came from price, not volume. Gross margin expanded ~680bps over that span on price/mix, while unit volumes fell — residential (HCS) volumes dropped 17% in FY2025 and 21% in Q1 2026, offset by 9% favorable price/mix. On 3/20/2026, Lennox and roughly half a dozen peers controlling >90% of the U.S. market were named in a Sherman Act §1 price-fixing class action (E.D. Michigan) alleging coordinated above-inflation increases exceeding 50% since 2020. The conduct under attack is the same pricing power that drove the margin story, making the antitrust suit and the durability of pricing two readings of a single fact.

The stock has fallen ~28% from its July 2025 high of $689 to ~$498, and now trades at ~22x trailing/forward EPS — below the building-products peer median of 25.5x. The forward-looking data cuts against a bearish posture: consensus EPS estimates are rising across every period (current year $24.32, +0.7% over 30 days; next year $26.71, +0.5% over both 30 and 90 days), the most recent quarter beat consensus by 4.8%, and the sell-side mean/median target of $570 sits ~14% above spot with even the low target ($450) only ~10% below. The conclusion is a HOLD: genuine offsetting risks (volume erosion, litigation, tightening cash flow) against a substantially de-rated price, rising estimates, and a strengthening commercial/aftermarket mix, with no directional edge in either direction.

## Company Overview

Lennox operates two segments after the 2023 European divestiture:

- **Home Comfort Solutions (HCS)** — residential furnaces, air conditioners, heat pumps, packaged systems, indoor air quality, and parts/supplies. FY2025 sales $3.34B (64% of total), 21.8% segment margin. Roughly 75% is sold through a direct-to-dealer network (independent installers plus company-owned Lennox stores). This direct model builds a large installed base that drives higher-margin replacement and service pull-through over time.
- **Building Climate Solutions (BCS)** — light commercial rooftop units, VRF, refrigeration (Heatcraft Worldwide), plus installation, service, energy monitoring, and HVAC recycling. FY2025 sales $1.85B (36%), 23.4% segment margin. This is the faster-growing, higher-margin, less weather/rate-cyclical segment.

Demand is split between more-stable replacement and cyclical new construction, and is highly weather-seasonal. Key raw materials are steel, copper, and aluminum (partially hedged). No single customer is ≥10% of revenue.

The profitability is high-quality at the margin level, but the composition of growth matters. FY2025 revenue fell 3% as a 13% volume decline was offset by 9% favorable price/mix and ~1% from the Duro Dyne/Supco acquisition. Q1 2026 sharpened the picture in both directions: total sales rose 6%, but HCS volumes fell 21% (offset by +9% price/mix), and BCS grew 38% with segment profit +63%, lifted by Duro Dyne and strong commercial volumes (+17%).

Margin durability is now being tested. Q1 2026 gross margin fell 90bps as ~300bps of product-cost inflation and factory under-absorption (from low residential volumes) outpaced ~250bps of price/mix benefit. This is the first quarter where the pricing engine did not fully offset cost and volume headwinds at the gross line.

Cash flow tightened materially. Operating cash flow fell to $758M in 2025 (from $946M) on a $217M working-capital build; FCF was $639M (12.3% margin). Q1 2026 OCF was a thin $16M, partly seasonal but also reflecting the volume environment. Total debt rose to $1.39B at year-end 2025 and $1.52B by Q1 2026 (commercial paper up to $361M), driven by the $554M Duro Dyne/Supco acquisition and prior buybacks. Interest expense more than doubled YoY in Q1 ($15.2M vs $6.2M).

The balance sheet optics (Altman Z of 2.11 in the grey zone, D/E reading ~2.5x, equity of only $1.16B) reflect an engineered capital structure, not distress. Equity was negative in 2022 after years of buybacks. Against that, ROE is ~69%, ROA ~20%, ratings are investment grade and stable, the net-leverage covenant cap is 3.5x with comfortable headroom, and there is no near-term maturity wall ($300M 2027 notes at 1.7%, $500M 2028 notes at 5.5%, $300M term loan due 2027). The Q4 2025 LIFO→FIFO accounting change added $107M to retained earnings (effective 1/1/23) and provides a modest, non-operational EPS tailwind.

The shift that matters going forward: buybacks slowed from $482M in FY2025 to $20M in Q1 2026 as cash tightened and the acquisition was funded with debt. With falling residential volumes, rising interest expense, and a richer dividend, the buyback firepower that supported per-share earnings is diminished.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algebra is the crux. Reported revenue and EPS growth from 2022 to 2025 was almost entirely price-driven while units declined. That is a durable formula only if pricing power persists. Three forces now bear on it:

1. **Refrigerant transition (R-410A → A2L/R-454B):** A regulatory-driven product-cost-up cycle that justified 2024-2025 price increases but pulled demand forward via 2024 pre-buys, leaving 2025-2026 volumes depressed by destocking, sticker shock, and rate-sensitive residential demand.
2. **Mix shift toward BCS and aftermarket:** The genuine positive. BCS is growing (+38% in Q1), higher-margin, and less cyclical. The Duro Dyne/Supco deal deepens a higher-margin, less-cyclical parts/supplies platform, a sensible counter to equipment-volume cyclicality. Successful execution here structurally de-risks the volume problem in residential.
3. **Estimate revisions are rising, not falling.** This argues against a bearish read and deserves explicit weight. Despite the Q1 volume decline and the March antitrust filing, consensus is moving up: next quarter $7.91 (+0.6% over 30 days, +1.1% over 90 days). Eighteen analysts are nudging numbers higher after seeing both the volume miss and the litigation. The earnings track record reinforces this: 3 beats in 4 quarters, including a Q1 2026 print of $3.35 vs $3.20 estimate (+4.8%). The volume weakness was already in the numbers, and the company still beat.

The revisions are modest in magnitude — fractions of a percent, well short of the +30% velocity that would signal a genuine inflection — but unambiguously positive in direction. The market already knew volumes were weak, priced it, and the sell-side is not extrapolating either the volume decline or the antitrust filing into materially lower forward earnings. That tempers, rather than confirms, a deterioration thesis.

Lennox commands a premium to the pure cyclicals (MAS, ALLE at ~17x) and a discount to high-growth WMS and the outlier AAON (98.7x, itself an antitrust co-defendant). At ~22x with ~69% ROE, 20% operating margins, and a growing higher-margin commercial/aftermarket mix, the multiple is neither stretched nor obviously cheap; it sits roughly fair for the quality, discounted for the dual overhang of volume and litigation.

The full sell-side target distribution argues against treating the stock as overvalued: mean and median both $570, high $650, low $450. Even the most bearish analyst target sits above a structural-impairment thesis. The consensus distribution points up, not down, and a HOLD is the call most consistent with it.

The valuation case is not a clean buy, however. The 22x multiple rests on EPS that is itself a price-driven artifact. If pricing power normalizes — whether from demand elasticity, competitive response, or litigation-driven scrutiny — earnings power compresses and the multiple re-rates against falling estimates simultaneously. That two-sided risk is why the valuation score is a 5.5 rather than higher: the price is reasonable on current numbers, but the durability of those numbers is genuinely uncertain.

## Competitive Landscape

Lennox competes in a consolidated oligopoly. The antitrust plaintiffs allege the named manufacturers — Carrier, Trane, Daikin, Bosch, Rheem, AAON, and Lennox — control >90% of the U.S. equipment market. That concentration is what enabled multi-year, above-inflation pricing, which is both the source of margin expansion and the basis of the legal challenge.

Lennox's competitive position is mixed in trajectory. The direct-to-dealer model and large installed base create a durable replacement annuity in residential, and the equipment manufacturing business is high-barrier. But residential volumes are eroding faster than peers on a unit basis, and the parts/supplies and commercial-service businesses (where Lennox is expanding via Duro Dyne/Supco) are lower-barrier and more competitive. The mix shift toward BCS and aftermarket is the right strategic move and is improving the trajectory at the margin, which supports a 6.0 — a stable-to-modestly-improving competitive position, with the residential volume erosion as the principal offset.

## Risk Assessment

**Antitrust class action (most material).** The 3/20/2026 E.D. Michigan suit (and at least six related filings, consolidation underway) alleges Sherman Act §1 price-fixing from January 2020 to present, with defendants accused of using AHRI data and ACHR News pricing announcements to coordinate the price increases. Lennox cannot estimate possible loss and warns an adverse outcome "could have a material adverse impact." The threat is both direct (damages, which in antitrust can be trebled) and indirect: even absent a finding, regulatory and litigation scrutiny could constrain future price increases, and price is currently Lennox's only growth lever. The matter is disclosed only in Legal Proceedings, not yet elevated to a standalone risk factor, and the suit is at the pleading stage with years of process ahead. This is a real overhang but one that is now well-documented and broadly known.

**Volume/demand deterioration.** Residential volume erosion is driven by refrigerant-transition pre-buy payback, high system prices, and soft rate-sensitive residential demand. If pricing normalizes before volumes recover, margins are exposed.

**Tariffs/Mexico concentration.** $268M of PP&E in Mexico; the Q1 10-Q flags a new Section 122 10% global tariff (post the Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling) plus ongoing Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs, with USMCA up for review in 2026. These raise input costs.

**Other.** Single-location manufacturing and key-supplier concentration; commodity exposure (partially hedged, 87.6M lbs Cu/Al notional); long-tail liabilities ($169M warranty reserve, warranties up to 20 years; ongoing asbestos/environmental charges ~$11M in 2025). Tightening cash flow and reduced buyback capacity remove a support for the per-share earnings algebra.

The risk score of 4.5 reflects that the antitrust matter is a genuine binary with asymmetric downside that is only partially priceable, combined with the volume/pricing fragility. It is not lower because the price has already de-rated ~28% and the suit is early-stage with no near-term resolution.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 44.1% versus 1-year historical volatility of 34.8% — rich by ~9.2pp — and the term structure is in backwardation (44.1% at 1 month vs 38.1% at 6 months), which typically signals an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is +5.6% (elevated put demand) and the put/call volume ratio is 3.0, though the P/C open-interest ratio of 0.71 is more balanced and fell from 1.34 a month ago as the stock dropped 8%. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.4%. Traders are hedging a near-term event (most plausibly Q2 earnings or antitrust headlines), with rich IV making long-premium hedges expensive. This points to a two-sided, event-driven setup rather than a clean directional signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$570-650):** Pricing power proves durable, the antitrust suit resolves without material liability or is settled at manageable cost, and the refrigerant-transition volume air-pocket reverses as destocking completes and replacement demand normalizes. BCS and the Duro Dyne/Supco aftermarket platform continue compounding at high margins, structurally de-risking residential cyclicality. Estimates that are already rising continue to climb, and the stock re-rates toward the peer median on a recovering volume base. Sell-side mean target $570 (+14%); high $650 (+30%).

**Bear Case (~$430-450):** The antitrust litigation, demand elasticity, or competitive response forces pricing to normalize while residential volumes remain depressed. Because ~100% of recent earnings growth came from price, margin compression hits a richly-embedded earnings base, estimates reverse, and the multiple de-rates simultaneously. Cash flow stays tight, buyback support fades, and EPS falls. Downside to the mid-$400s — roughly the 52-week low of $434 and the analyst low target of $450, ~10-13% below spot.

The bear-case downside (~$430-450) is barely below the current price and essentially at the 52-week low, while the bull case offers materially more room. The market has already done much of the work of pricing the volume and litigation risk. That asymmetry, combined with rising estimates and a consensus target above spot, disqualifies a bearish AVOID and supports a balanced HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the offsetting positives (de-rated price, rising estimates, mix shift, recent beat) and negatives (antitrust overhang, volume erosion, pricing dependence, tightening cash flow) are genuinely balanced, and neither side carries a clear edge over a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- HCS residential volumes inflect positive (or decline narrows to single digits) on consecutive quarters, confirming the refrigerant-transition air-pocket is clearing while pricing holds — restoring volume-plus-price growth rather than price-only.
- Antitrust matter resolves or de-escalates materially (dismissal at pleading, narrow settlement, or clear signal of immaterial exposure), removing the overhang on the pricing engine.
- Estimate revision velocity accelerates above ~+10% on a 90-day basis alongside a pullback toward the low-$400s, creating clear forward valuation support.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence that pricing power is breaking — sequential gross-margin compression beyond the Q1 90bps decline driven by price (not just cost), or guidance/commentary signaling competitive price erosion.
- The antitrust matter escalates (DOJ/FTC interest, class certification, or a credible damages estimate that management can no longer characterize as unquantifiable), elevating from Legal Proceedings to a standalone risk factor.
- A guidance cut or a string of negative estimate revisions reversing the current upward trend.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July) for volume/margin trajectory; ongoing antitrust docket developments (consolidation, motions to dismiss) over the next 6-12 months.

## Conclusion

Lennox is a high-return HVAC franchise whose quality metrics (33% gross margin, 20% operating margin, ~69% ROE, investment-grade balance sheet) are largely intact, but whose growth has become entirely dependent on a pricing lever that is simultaneously the subject of a multi-defendant antitrust class action and increasingly tested by collapsing residential volumes. That tension is real and unresolved.

What disqualifies a bearish call is that the market has already repriced most of the bad news: the stock is down ~28% from its high to ~22x earnings (below the peer median), the bear case lands only ~10% below spot and near the 52-week low, estimate revisions are rising across every period even after the antitrust filing and the volume miss, the most recent quarter beat consensus, and consensus targets sit ~14% above the current price. What disqualifies a bullish call is the genuine binary in the antitrust litigation, the fragility of price-only earnings, tightening cash flow, reduced buyback capacity, and the absence of any insider open-market buying despite the drawdown. The strengthening BCS/aftermarket mix is the most encouraging structural development and the key thing to monitor. On balance this is a HOLD: a fairly-valued, two-sided situation where the documented risks are substantially in the price and the forward data does not point decisively in either direction. The trajectory (6.3 → 6.7 across prior reports) reflected improving financial-health and growth scores; the antitrust filing and the volume acceleration in Q1 introduce enough new uncertainty to hold the overall score roughly flat rather than extend the prior improvement.
