**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tractor Supply has fallen 51% from its 52-week high of $63.99 to $31.22, with most of the damage concentrated in the period since the April 21 Q1 FY2026 print and the subsequent wave of analyst downgrades. The stock now trades at roughly 15.4x trailing and forward earnings, versus a specialty-retail peer median near 31x, and yields 3.1% on a covered dividend. The drawdown is large enough that the burden of proof shifts to identifying what, specifically, the market is repricing, and the evidence points to valuation compression on a previously premium-rated franchise rather than the onset of structural collapse.

The franchise is intact. Tractor Supply is the largest rural-lifestyle retailer in the U.S. with 2,641 stores, a defensible small-format rural footprint that big-box and pure-online competitors find unattractive, roughly two-thirds of sales in needs-based consumable categories, $740M of free cash flow, and investment-grade ratings (Baa1/BBB, both stable, covenant-compliant). The leverage optics (D/E 3.24) are an artifact of the sale-leaseback model and a buyback-shrunken equity base, not financial distress. What has deteriorated is the growth algorithm: FY2025 comps of +1.2% and Q1 FY2026 comps of +0.5% are increasingly driven by ticket (+1.6%) rather than traffic (-1.0%), operating margin compressed from 9.9% (FY24) to 6.5% (Q1 FY26), and the company has missed EPS in three of the last four quarters.

The call is HOLD. The bear inputs are real but substantially priced after a 51% decline to a half-peer-median multiple. Downside from here is cushioned by the dividend and IG balance sheet, and there is a genuine, under-appreciated tariff-refund optionality following the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling. Against that, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, the central pet-category debate (cyclical vs. structural) is unresolved, and there is no confirmed catalyst within the next 60 days. The setup warrants patience and monitoring of the items below.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1938 and headquartered in Brentwood, TN, Tractor Supply operates 2,641 stores across 49 states (2,435 Tractor Supply plus 206 Petsense), serving recreational farmers, ranchers, and rural-lifestyle ("Out Here") customers, supplemented by e-commerce (TractorSupply.com, Petsense.com, Allivet.com) and a mobile app. The business reports as a single segment.

Merchandise mix (FY2025): Livestock, Equine & Agriculture 27%; Companion Animal (pet) 24%; Seasonal & Recreation 24%; Truck, Tool & Hardware 15%; Clothing, Gift & Décor 10%. The competitive moat rests on small-format stores in rural towns outside metro markets (locations that lack the density to attract Home Depot/Lowe's-scale boxes or efficient last-mile e-commerce) combined with a high mix of needs-based, consumable, usable, edible ("C.U.E.") categories that drive recurring, relatively recession-resistant traffic. The chain compounded net sales at roughly 7.9% over 2020–2025 (2,105 → 2,602 stores) and management argues meaningful whitespace remains.

Strategic initiatives include Project Fusion remodels, garden-center "side lot" transformations, Final Mile delivery, the Allivet online pet pharmacy (acquired December 2024 for ~$140M), and an expansion of pet services to ~700 stores by year-end 2026 aimed at reaccelerating the soft Companion Animal category.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth has decelerated.** Net sales rose from $14.20B (FY22) to $15.52B (FY25), with YoY growth of +2.5%, +2.2%, and +4.3%. The FY25 acceleration was driven mostly by new stores plus Allivet (~3.1 points); underlying comps were only +1.2%. Q1 FY26 revenue grew +3.6% to $3.59B, but comps were +0.5%, composed of +1.6% transaction value and -1.0% transaction count. Growth is coming from price and mix, not from winning more shopping trips, which is a quality-of-growth concern.

**Margins are compressing on SG&A deleverage.** Gross margin has held up (FY24 36.3% → FY25 36.4%; Q1 flat at 36.2%), aided by cost management and EDLP, partially offset by tariffs and delivery/transportation costs. Operating margin fell from 9.9% (FY24) to 9.5% (FY25) and more sharply to 6.5% in Q1 FY26 (from 7.2%). The mechanism is fixed-cost deleverage against weak comps compounded by an accelerated new-store cadence (40 stores opened in Q1 FY26 vs. 15 a year earlier), which front-loads pre-opening and ramp costs. Net income was roughly flat in FY25 (~$1.10B) and fell 8.3% in Q1 FY26 to $164.5M ($0.31 EPS vs. $0.34).

**Cash flow and balance sheet remain sound.** FY25 operating cash flow was $1.64B against $895M capex, leaving ~$740M FCF (FCF margin ~4.8%). The headline D/E of 3.24 and Z-Score of 2.48 (grey zone) overstate risk: they reflect ~$4.2B of operating-lease ROU assets/liabilities from the sale-leaseback model and an equity base depressed by $6.5B of cumulative treasury stock. ROE of 42.5% and ROA of 10% confirm a capital-light, lease-funded model rather than distress. Investment-grade ratings (Baa1/BBB stable) and Q1 covenant compliance (fixed-charge coverage ≥2.0x; leverage ≤4.0x) corroborate health. Funded debt rose from $1.78B at YE25 to $2.14B at Q1 FY26 as the revolver was drawn to $590M.

**Capital allocation is aggressive but worth monitoring.** FY25 returned $848.5M ($361M buybacks + $488M dividends); the dividend was raised to $0.24/quarter in February 2026, with ~$1.0B buyback authorization remaining. FY26 guidance: ~$675–725M net capex, ~$375–450M buybacks. The company is partly funding shareholder returns and accelerated capex with revolver borrowings while EPS declines, which is defensible given the IG balance sheet but a line item to watch if comps stay soft.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is muted but not broken. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $2.13 and next-year at $2.31, implying low-single-digit earnings growth off a depressed base. This is not an inflection story: estimate revisions are flat-to-negative (-2.8% CY and -4.1% next year over 90 days; essentially flat over 30 days), so the strong-revision-velocity signal that would argue for a BUY is absent. The earnings-surprise record reinforces caution: three misses and one in-line print over the last four quarters, with the two most recent misses widening (-8.9% in Q4, -11.2% in Q1). That pattern signals deteriorating near-term visibility, not improving momentum.

The unit-growth engine is intact (roughly 100 openings per year against management's claimed whitespace), and the Allivet pet pharmacy plus the ~700-store pet-services rollout are direct, controllable responses to the Companion Animal softness. If comps recover toward management's reaffirmed 1–3% target, the store-growth-driven deleverage reverses and the recently opened stores ramp into profitability, a plausible path to margin recovery. The bear case is that pet weakness is structural (declining dog ownership, rising pet-ownership costs) and persists for several years, in which case the new stores remain a drag. The truth is likely in between: management attributes part of the pet softness to "category shifts and unfavorable product mix," which is self-correctable via merchandising, but the macro overlay (cautious, trip-consolidating consumer) is real and not in management's control.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.22, TSCO trades at ~15.4x trailing and forward earnings against a specialty-retail peer median of 31.3x (ULTA 17.9x, CHWY 31.3x, BBWI 5.3x, WINA 35.6x, a heterogeneous set). The dividend yield of 3.1% is more than triple the peer median 1.0% and provides downside support. Consensus targets span $36 (low) to $60 (high), with a mean of $46.04 and median of $45.00, implying roughly 44–47% upside to the mean, though those targets have been cut hard (Piper Sandler $51→$36, TD Cowen $53→$38) and likely still lag the deceleration.

The valuation has already de-rated to reflect the slowdown. A franchise that historically commanded a premium multiple now trades at half the peer median, which means the market has substantially priced the comp deceleration, the margin compression, and the pet-category overhang. This is the core reason the name is a HOLD rather than an AVOID: there is no evidence the multiple is stretched, and the compression does not reflect a value trap with no recovery catalyst. The moat, FCF, and balance sheet are intact, and a tariff-refund optionality exists. Equally, it is not a clean BUY: at ~15.4x forward with flat-to-negative revisions and three EPS misses, the stock is reasonably priced for a low-growth, traffic-challenged retailer, not obviously cheap relative to the earnings power it is currently delivering. The forward P/E sits modestly below the implied earnings growth rate, which keeps the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. I score valuation 6.0: supportive enough to remove the avoidance case, not compelling enough to drive a buy on its own.

## Competitive Landscape

Tractor Supply's rural small-town footprint remains its key differentiator, insulating most of its store base from direct big-box overlap and from efficient last-mile e-commerce. Management asserts it is gaining share in farm & ranch even as the broader category contracts, and the two-thirds C.U.E. mix provides recurring traffic. Gross margin resilience (flat-to-expanding through the downturn) supports the claim that pricing power and category positioning are holding.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-pressured rather than improving. The pet vertical (~24% of sales) faces structural questions and direct competition from Chewy/PetSmart/Petco; the Allivet acquisition and pet-services build are defensive moves to expand TAM and protect against online pharmacy competition, with execution still unproven. The negative transaction-count trend is the clearest competitive yellow flag: it indicates the company is monetizing existing trips rather than acquiring new ones, which over time can signal share stagnation even if dollar comps hold. I score competitive position 5.5: a strong, defensible niche with a stable moat but a flattening share trajectory in its most pressured category.

## Risk Assessment

**Pet-category structural risk** is the central bear thesis. Pet ran below company average in Q1 on softer demand, category shifts, and unfavorable mix. If the structural read (declining dog ownership, cost inflation) dominates, the drag persists for years; if cyclical/mix-driven, merchandising and the services rollout can correct it. Unresolved.

**Tariffs cut both ways.** They are currently depressing gross margin (the 10-K states tariffs "have impacted our costs and margins"; TSCO sources heavily from Asia and Central America). But the Q1 10-Q subsequent-events footnote discloses that on February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court ruled certain IEEPA tariffs exceeded presidential authority, and CBP launched a refund-claims process (CAPE) in April 2026. This is a potential cost reversal and one-time recovery not yet in numbers: genuine optionality skewed to the upside, though unquantified and uncertain in timing.

**Margin deleverage from store growth** is partly self-inflicted and management-controllable: the Q1 margin drop is significantly driven by deliberately accelerating openings into a downturn. This reverses if comps recover and persists if they don't.

**Other risks**: weather/seasonality dependence (management advises judging on "halves, not quarters"); discretionary/big-ticket exposure to a cautious consumer; and a candid, company-specific ESG/DE&I disclosure tied to the July 2024 decision to roll back carbon and DE&I goals, which the 10-K acknowledges could affect index inclusion, morale, and the share price. Funding buybacks, dividends, and accelerated capex partly via the revolver while EPS declines is a monitoring item, not yet a solvency concern.

Most of these risks are disclosed and priced after the 51% drawdown, which supports a risk-factors score of 5.5: neither benign nor pointing to a specific unpriced catastrophic catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface is sending a noisy near-term signal that should be read carefully. Near-term ATM IV of 131.5% sits far above 31.1% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (131.5% at 1 week vs. 46.3% at 6 months). The 1-week implied move is only ±1.8–2.4%, however, and the elevated front-end IV collapsed from 291.7% to 110.7% over the prior month while price rose 2.1%, consistent with a thin, event-driven near-dated chain rather than informed directional positioning. The put/call OI ratio of 0.44 (down from 0.81) and a modest 4.5% put skew do not indicate aggressive bearish hedging. Net read: the market is pricing some near-term uncertainty but not a directional crash, and the IV spike is largely artifact. This neither confirms nor refutes the fundamental HOLD; it argues against treating the front-end vol as a meaningful bearish tell.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Pet weakness proves cyclical/mix-driven, and the Allivet plus 700-store services rollout reaccelerates the category through 2026. Comps recover toward the 1–3% target, the accelerated store base ramps into profitability, and operating margin rebuilds toward ~9%. The IEEPA tariff refund materializes as a one-time gross-margin tailwind. Estimate revisions inflect positive, and the multiple re-rates from ~15.4x toward a mid-cycle ~18–20x on recovering earnings. Bull target ~$48–52 (analyst high $60 reflects a fuller recovery), roughly +54–66% from current.

**Bear case.** Pet weakness is structural and persists for several years; traffic continues to decline; the accelerated-store deleverage does not reverse, and operating margin stalls near 6.5–7%. EPS estimates continue to drift lower, and the stock holds its compressed multiple on lower earnings. Bear target ~$26–28, roughly -10% to -17% from current, with the dividend and IG balance sheet limiting further downside absent a dividend cut.

**Base case.** Comps grind near the low end of guidance, margin recovers partially as store cadence normalizes, the tariff refund provides modest upside, and the multiple holds. Base target ~$36–40 (in line with the cut analyst targets and the low end of the consensus range), roughly +15–28% from current over 12 months. The downside (~10–17%) is cushioned and the upside (~15–28% base, more in the bull) is real but contingent, a balanced setup that nonetheless lacks the confirmed near-term catalyst or positive revision momentum required to buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026, expected mid-to-late July 2026). Management explicitly advises judging the business on halves, so the H1 read will be the first clean look at whether comps are stabilizing and whether the tariff refund is quantifiable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Comps reaccelerate above +2% with positive transaction count, signaling traffic recovery rather than ticket-only growth.
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive (+30-day and +90-day) alongside an operating-margin recovery back above ~8%, confirming the store-growth deleverage is reversing.
- The IEEPA tariff refund is quantified as a material gross-margin tailwind, converting the disclosed optionality into a dated catalyst.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Pet-category comps deteriorate further with explicit management confirmation of structural (not mix) drivers, and estimate revisions accelerate downward (>5% additional 90-day cut).
- Operating margin compresses below ~6% with no comp recovery, indicating the accelerated capex is permanent margin destruction rather than a timing drag.
- Any dividend cut or covenant pressure, or a material step-up in revolver reliance that signals the capital-return program is no longer self-funding.

## Conclusion

Tractor Supply is a high-quality, investment-grade rural retailer going through a genuine soft patch, and the market has priced much of that soft patch. The 51% drawdown to ~15.4x forward earnings — half the peer median, against a 3.1% covered dividend and $740M of FCF — removes the case for active avoidance: there is no value trap with no recovery catalyst, no solvency risk, and the moat is intact. At the same time, three consecutive EPS misses, flat-to-negative estimate revisions, declining store traffic, and an unresolved pet-category debate remove the case for a buy. There is no confirmed catalyst inside 60 days, and the strongest forward signal (estimate-revision velocity) is neutral-to-negative.

This is a balanced hold with genuine uncertainty. Downside is limited (~10–17%) and cushioned by the dividend and balance sheet; upside (~15–28% base, more if the tariff refund and comp recovery break right) is real but contingent on developments not yet visible in the numbers. I rate TSCO **HOLD**, with the next earnings report as the natural reassessment point and the specific upgrade/downgrade triggers above as the monitoring framework.
