## Executive Summary

SolarEdge has executed a genuine operational recovery from a near-death experience. Revenue has grown from $901.5M in FY2024 to $1,184.4M in FY2025 (+31.4%), Q1 2026 revenue rose 41.5% year over year to $310.5M, gross margin recovered from 8.0% to 22.0%, the quarterly net loss narrowed to $57.4M from $132.1M in Q4 2025, and the company retired $342.25M of convertible notes in cash at maturity in September 2025 without issuing a single share. Europe — the market that collapsed from $1,903.8M of revenue in FY2023 to $317.6M in FY2025, an 83.3% decline — grew 116.2% in Q1 2026. Battery revenue reached $94.9M, or 30.6% of total revenue, on 331 MWh shipped.

The problem is what sits underneath the margin. Both the FY2025 10-K and the Q1 2026 10-Q state that excluding Section 45X Advanced Manufacturing Production Tax Credits, SolarEdge would have reported a **gross loss** — the 10-Q says so explicitly "for both periods presented." That constrains the credit to exceed $196.3M in FY2025 and $68.3M in Q1 2026, i.e. more than 22.0% of quarterly revenue, or above roughly $275M annualized at the current run rate. The 10-K states the credits are "expected to be phased out by the end of 2031" and warns that reductions "may include transitioning into a gross loss." Separately, H.R.1 eliminated the Section 25D residential homeowner credit at the end of 2025 and pulled the 48E/45Y sunset forward to 2027, which sets up demand pull-forward into 2026–27 followed by an air pocket. SolarEdge is therefore in the position of receiving the supply-side subsidy while its customers lose the demand-side ones, on a clock.

The second issue is cash-flow quality. FY2025 operating cash flow of +$104.3M included a $164.8M increase in trade payables; Q1 2026 OCF of +$24.4M included a $132.6M increase. Excluding those single lines, the figures are −$60.5M and −$108.2M. Days payable outstanding expanded from roughly 100 days at 12/31/25 to roughly 152 days at 3/31/26, against a base where two contract manufacturers do most of the manufacturing and one holds 50.8% of the payables balance. The underlying cash loss before working capital shrank from about −$118M for FY2025 to about −$24M in Q1 2026. Reported OCF cannot be annualized in either direction.

At ~$41 the equity carries an enterprise value of roughly $2.29B on FY2025 revenue of $1.18B (1.94x) or annualized Q1 revenue of $1.24B (1.85x), 6.05x book, and 26x a next-year consensus EPS of $1.56 that has *fallen* 5.1% over 90 days. Twenty-one of twenty-six covering analysts sit at Hold, four at Sell or Strong Sell, one at Buy, and the median target of $40.00 is below the current price. We maintain AVOID.

## Company Overview

SolarEdge sells module-level power electronics for photovoltaic systems: DC power optimizers, string inverters designed to work with those optimizers, residential and commercial batteries, EV chargers, and a monitoring platform. It now reports a single operating segment after exiting every adjacency it built — LCV e-Mobility (final disposal completed February 11, 2026), the battery-cell division (last South Korean plant sold September 4, 2025; Korean subsidiary liquidation begun December 2025), and the PV tracker business (divested April 2025). Goodwill and intangibles total just $57.3M, or 2.6% of total assets, so there is little remaining acquisition carrying value to impair.

The installed base is roughly 142.5 million optimizers and 6.2 million inverters. That footprint is both the durable asset (replacement and attach channel, monitoring data) and the source of a $321.8M warranty liability, since optimizers carry 25-year limited warranties.

Manufacturing is asset-light and now U.S.-weighted: inverters in Texas, optimizers and inverters in Florida, batteries in Utah, with residual capability at Sella 1 in Israel and in Vietnam. Manufacturing in China, Mexico, and Hungary has been discontinued. The company relies mainly on two contract manufacturers. Sales run through distributors and large installers under agreements that, per the 10-K, carry no long-term purchase commitments and are "generally cancellable by either party after a relatively short notice period," leaving the company with "limited visibility as to end-customer demand." That structure is the mechanism behind the 2023–24 destocking collapse and it has not changed.

The sector classification in the data feed — Information Technology / Semiconductor Materials & Equipment — is misleading. The peer set supplied (COHU, PDFS, ICHR, VECO, PLAB) has a median P/E of 188.9 and bears no operational relationship to residential solar hardware. SolarEdge's economics are driven by U.S. and European residential solar demand, net-metering policy, and tax-credit design, not by semiconductor capital equipment cycles.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** $1,963.9M (2021) → $3,110.3M (2022) → $2,976.5M (2023) → $901.5M (2024) → $1,184.4M (2025). FY2025 revenue is 38.1% of the 2022 peak. The FY2025 recovery was almost entirely American: U.S. revenue rose 89.2% to $718.2M (60.6% of total), contributing +$338.6M against consolidated growth of $282.9M — meaning non-U.S. regions were net negative in aggregate. Europe was flat at −1.5%.

Q1 2026 changes that geographic picture: Europe $113.5M (+116.2%), U.S. $158.1M (+19.7%), non-U.S. mix up to 49.1% from 39.8%. One unexplained divergence: inverter units fell 29.8% to 50,500 while optimizer units rose 15.4% to 2,438,400, lifting optimizers per inverter to 48.3 from 29.4. That is consistent with a mix shift toward larger commercial systems or with the "Single SKU" software-defined platform changing shipment composition; it could also reflect optimizer sales into the installed base or channel timing. The filings do not explain it, and we would not assume inverter share loss without more evidence.

**Gross margin.** FY2025 gross profit $196.3M (16.6%) versus −$877M (−97.3%) in FY2024. Q1 2026 gross profit $68.3M (22.0%) versus $17.5M (8.0%). The Q1 bridge attributes the 14.0-point improvement to +7.8 points of fixed-cost absorption on higher volume, +6.5 points of direct-cost improvement (mix, higher U.S. content, AMPTC, partly offset by higher U.S. manufacturing cost), +2.7 points of lower warranty expense, and −2.9 points from lapping prior-year restructuring gains. The absorption component is genuinely operational and repeatable if volume holds. The AMPTC component inside the +6.5 is subsidy-dependent. The +2.7 warranty contribution came from a −$21.6M favorable change in estimate, following a −$26.5M favorable change in FY2025. Field-reliability improvement on newer hardware generations is a legitimate basis for releasing reserves — settlements of $28.9M exceeded the $14.5M accrual in the quarter, consistent with older cohorts working through claims — but this is a management estimate on a 25-year obligation across 142.5 million units, and we would not extrapolate further releases.

**Operating results.** FY2025 operating loss −$301.7M, net loss −$405.4M, diluted EPS −$6.88. The net loss includes a $58.9M non-cash reclassification of cumulative translation adjustment out of AOCI triggered by the Korean subsidiary liquidation. That is recycling of previously recognized currency translation, not a cash cost; excluding it, the net loss was approximately −$346.5M. It nonetheless affects book-value composition and any net-income-based screen.

Q1 2026: operating loss −$55.0M, net loss −$57.4M, −$0.95 per share on 60.5M weighted diluted shares. Cost discipline is visible: R&D $50.2M (−19.1%), S&M $27.4M (−13.3%), G&A $36.4M (+20.7%). The G&A increase is a $13.4M net doubtful-debt provision versus an $8.1M net reversal a year earlier, a ~$21.5M swing, not overhead creep. Headcount in R&D/S&M/G&A fell to 1,641 from 2,157 while operations/production headcount rose to 1,935 from 1,804. Stock-based compensation remains heavy at $92.5M in FY2025 (7.8% of revenue) and $19.9M in Q1 2026 (6.4%), with $140.0M unrecognized through February 2030.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/26: cash $512.4M, restricted cash $41.0M, marketable securities $29.3M, inventories $596.8M (≈224 days of COGS), net receivables $222.7M, prepaid and other current $414.5M, total assets $2,260.5M. Total liabilities $1,849.8M against equity of $410.7M (4.51x), accumulated deficit −$1,491.0M. Interest-bearing debt is $331.9M of notes plus $18.3M of finance leases = $350.2M, producing **net cash of +$191.5M** (+$232.5M including restricted cash). The next maturity is the 2.25% 2029 convertible on July 1, 2029.

A standardized screen returned an Altman Z-Score near 1.0 and a debt/equity ratio of 5.1 alongside long-term debt of $18.5M — internally inconsistent inputs that omit the $337M convertible principal. Re-derived from the filings, the low Z-Score is driven by negative EBIT relative to assets and the accumulated deficit, not by leverage or illiquidity. This is a persistent-unprofitability problem, not a near-term solvency problem, and the distinction matters for how one sizes any position.

Items that loom large against a $410.7M equity base: warranty obligations $321.8M (78% of equity); a "government authorities" receivable of $207.0M (50% of equity), up $45.3M in one quarter, largely unmonetized 45X credits plus VAT, with the 10-Q warning that failure to sell credits "could result in delays between 18–24 months" in realizing value; non-cancelable purchase obligations of $297.2M carrying a $24.3M loss provision; and a new Israeli campus lease with a $274.2M 15-year non-cancelable commitment not yet commenced (67% of equity). Management paid a $28.8M modification fee ($26.2M in Q1 2026) to shrink that lease — prudent right-sizing at real cash cost, but the residual commitment is still large relative to today's revenue.

**Tax structure.** FY2025 pretax income was +$181.8M domestic versus −$572.9M foreign: the U.S. manufacturing profit and 45X credits land in the U.S. entity while the Israeli parent absorbs R&D and the losses. Foreign NOLs of $2,056.2M sit behind a $455.9M valuation allowance and are unlikely to shield income earned in the U.S. Do not model a deferred-tax release as an earnings tailwind.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth engines are visible and two of them are policy-conditioned.

**Storage attach is the most credible organic driver.** Battery revenue of $94.9M in Q1 2026 (+126% YoY, 331 MWh, +86.8% in energy terms) is 30.6% of total revenue versus 19.1% a year earlier. This is structurally supported rather than subsidy-supported: California NEM 3.0 and similar reviews in Arizona, Hawaii, and North Carolina cut export compensation, which *improves* the economics of pairing storage with solar and favors vendors with integrated DC-coupled offerings. New commercial products (CSS-OD at 102.4 kWh and 197 kWh, scalable to ~4 MWh) extend the addressable market, though the 10-K cautions these batteries are "relatively new to the market" and servicing experience "is still developing" — a forward warranty risk on an unproven line.

**European normalization off a destroyed base.** Europe at $113.5M in Q1 2026 remains a fraction of the FY2023 run rate. Recovery from destocking can continue for several quarters purely on channel normalization. Germany is actively discussing reduced residential feed-in tariffs, which caps the medium term.

**U.S. domestic content as a share-gain mechanism.** H.R.1 raises the 48E domestic-content threshold from 45% to 50% effective January 1, 2026, then +5 points annually through 2029. U.S.-manufactured SolarEdge product is advantaged versus imports in that channel. This is the most underrated positive in the file, and it is conditional, because the 10-K also discloses continued reliance "on suppliers in China for certain key subcomponents," which is exactly what puts SolarEdge's own Prohibited Foreign Entity / FEOC compliance in question.

Against that: H.R.1 eliminated the Section 25D residential homeowner credit at the end of 2025, removing the primary economic driver of the U.S. cash/loan residential market, and shortened the 48E/45Y window to a December 31, 2027 placed-in-service deadline with a 12-month construction-start rule. The predictable shape is safe-harbor pull-forward through 2026–27 and a demand air pocket after. That shape is not visible in a trailing or even a one-year-forward multiple.

**Estimate revisions are two-directional and the header is misleading.** Current quarter went from −$0.04 to $0.00 over 90 days, next quarter from $0.18 to $0.25 (+35.1%), current year from $0.08 to $0.09. But **next-year consensus fell 5.1% over 90 days to $1.56**. Near-term estimates are rising as the recovery beats a low bar; the out-year estimate that actually anchors the valuation is deteriorating. This does not qualify as the inflection signal that a uniform 30%+ upward revision across all periods would represent, and the earnings track record (three beats, then a 50.1% miss in the March 2026 quarter) argues for modest confidence in the forward path.

Externally reported Q2 2026 guidance of $325–355M revenue with near-breakeven operating income (source: a June 22 third-party article, unconfirmed against a company release) would imply ~10% sequential growth at the midpoint. If that guidance is on a non-GAAP basis, it must be compared with non-GAAP expectations; GAAP breakeven at these opex levels would be a much larger step.

Under 2x EV/sales for a business growing revenue 41% is not obviously expensive on its face. There is no defensible earnings-based anchor: 26x next year requires the company to swing from −$0.95 quarterly EPS to $1.56 annual EPS, on a consensus that is drifting lower, in a year that includes the 48E construction-start deadline.

**On the drawdown.** The stock is 49.3% below its May 29 high of $81.25 while up 79% from its 52-week low of $23.01 and up 60.6% over twelve months. Fundamentals have not changed since the May 7 Q1 report — no guidance cut, no new impairment, no covenant issue. The $40 → $81 melt-up in May–June was substantially policy-headline sector beta (an 89.4% post-earnings move in one month, per third-party reporting), and it has round-tripped to roughly where the stock traded before. We are not marking down scores for the price decline; the decline removed froth rather than revealing deterioration. That also means the current price is not a discount to any fundamental anchor; it is simply the pre-squeeze level.

**Convertible mechanics now shape the asymmetry.** The 2029 notes convert at $34.32 into 9,819,347 shares. The company holds capped calls struck at $34.32 with a cap of $48.84. From the disclosed $164.278M excess of if-converted value over principal at 3/31/26, the implied quarter-end share price was $51.05 — above the cap. At $41 the stock is back inside the hedged band, where the calls economically offset conversion dilution. Above roughly $49, incremental appreciation begins creating unhedged dilution; and holders gain an early conversion right if the stock closes at or above $44.62 (130% of the conversion price) for 20 of 30 trading days in a quarter. Dilution from the notes alone would be +16.1% to share count, equivalent to 13.9% ownership dilution post-issuance; including all 4,547,263 antidilutive award shares, maximum contingent dilution is +23.6% to share count / 19.1% ownership dilution. Neither figure is the current reported diluted count of 60.5M weighted shares, since all instruments are antidilutive against losses. The practical effect is a soft ceiling on the bull case in the high $40s.

Sell-side positioning corroborates the absence of an edge: mean target $45.25 (+9.8%), median $40.00 (−0.6%), range $26–$100, with 21 Holds against 1 Buy and 4 Sell/Strong Sell, unchanged for four months. When the median target equals the price and the distribution is that wide, the market is expressing genuine uncertainty rather than a mispricing we can arbitrage.

## Competitive Landscape

SolarEdge's stated competitive concern is specific and in its own filing: "competition from Chinese state owned or financed companies pose pricing pressure on us and may exacerbate regional market volatility," with "low-cost Asian manufacturers" entering the U.S., Australia, and Europe. Against microinverter competition (Enphase, unnamed in the filing) and emerging MLPE providers, SolarEdge's differentiation is the DC-optimized architecture, the 142.5-million-unit installed base, and the monitoring platform.

The customer channel is consolidating violently, and the filings name it: SunPower acquiring Sunder Energy (September 2025) and Ambia Solar (November 2025); Solaris Assets acquiring substantially all of Sunnova's assets (September 2025); PosiGen Chapter 11 (November 2025); Freedom Forever Chapter 11 (April 2026). These are customers and channel partners, not suppliers. Their distress is simultaneously a bad-debt problem — the credit-loss allowance rose from $17.2M to $30.5M in one quarter — and a concentration problem, with the 10-K warning that consolidation means "we may increase our reliance on a small number of customers." One customer was 18.6% of FY2025 revenue, up from 12.9%; the top three were 35.8% of revenue and 45.5% of trade receivables.

Q1 2026 concentration readings improved (one customer at 16.4% of revenue, two at 22.5% of AR versus 35.9%), and the geographic mix rebalanced meaningfully. But single-quarter concentration figures are volatile and not directly comparable to annual figures, and the annual measure worsened in FY2025. Geographic rebalancing does not by itself establish reduced customer concentration; those are different measures, and one quarter is not a trend.

Trajectory assessment: mixed to modestly negative. Storage attach and the domestic-content regime are share-gain vectors. Chinese price competition, the inverter unit decline, and a European business still 83% below its 2023 peak are not. Supplier dependency is severe — two contract manufacturers, one at 50.8% of payables, with the 10-K noting SolarEdge's orders "represent a relatively small percentage of their overall revenues," so fulfilling them "may not be considered a priority."

## Risk Assessment

**1. 45X dependency, with both an expiry date and a live eligibility question.** Management states plainly that gross profit becomes a gross loss without the credits, and that they are expected to phase out by end-2031. More immediate: H.R.1 introduced Prohibited Foreign Entity / FEOC requirements for Section 45X effective for projects beginning on or after January 1, 2026. IRS Notice 2026-15 (February 12, 2026) provided safe-harbor relief, but the 10-K warns that the "impending Notice of Proposed Rule and Final Rule on this same topic expected later this year could create challenges for the Company to meet the FEOC requirements" — while disclosing continued reliance on Chinese suppliers for certain key subcomponents. An adverse final rule would attack 100% of reported gross profit. This is the single largest unpriced binary.

**2. Demand-side policy cliff.** 25D eliminated at end-2025; 48E/45Y to a December 31, 2027 placed-in-service deadline. Pull-forward, then air pocket.

**3. Cash-flow quality and supplier terms.** DPO at ~152 days against a concentrated CM base cannot expand indefinitely. If payables merely stopped growing at the Q1 rate, quarterly OCF would be roughly −$100M before further improvement in the underlying loss.

**4. Working-capital chokepoint on the credits.** The government-authorities receivable at $207.0M and growing means the income-statement benefit is outrunning cash conversion, and monetization occurs at a discount to face.

**5. Estimate-driven margin support.** Two consecutive favorable warranty re-estimates (−$26.5M, −$21.6M) flatter gross margin while credit-loss provisioning burdens opex. An investor tracking only gross margin misses the offset.

**6. Israel concentration.** 279 employees (13% of the Israeli workforce) called to reserve duty in FY2025 and 162 (6.8%) in Q1 2026; direct military conflict with Iran in March–April 2026; the 10-K discloses "pressure being placed on our customers not to engage in business with us due to our affiliation with Israel." FX sensitivity: ~$7.0M per 10% EUR move, ~$6.0M per 10% NIS move, on 45.2% non-USD revenue in Q1 2026.

**7. Litigation.** The securities class action settled at $55M, substantially insurance-funded, with final approval scheduled August 24, 2026 — net cash impact modest but the gross-up inflates both sides of the balance sheet. Unresolved: Ampt LLC (D. Del., seeking to enforce an agreement valued at $54M over five years, trial November 1, 2027) and Stellantis (Turin injunction seeking €100,000/day over the e-Mobility exit, appeal decision pending).

**8. Tariffs, two-sided and unquantified.** SCOTUS invalidated certain IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026 with a CBP refund portal opened April 20; SolarEdge "may be eligible" but has recorded no adjustment. Simultaneously new Section 122 tariffs of 10–15% took effect February 24, 2026 for 150 days.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing an imminent, large, two-sided event. Near-term ATM IV of 140.0% against 93.9% one-year realized volatility is a 46-point premium at the 85th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (140.0% at one week, 121.2% at one month, 103.0% at three months, 100.7% at six months). The nearest expiry implies a ±15.4% move, consistent with a Q2 print in the first week of August. Put/call volume is 3.31 and open-interest ratio 2.66, indicating heavy put positioning, though the put/call skew of −1.7% at the front is characterized as balanced and only steepens to −4.9% at six months.

Traders are buying near-dated protection into the print rather than expressing a durable directional bearish view, and the vol is expensive relative to realized. We have no informational edge into the event; the market is charging a large premium for a genuinely uncertain outcome. It also cuts against shorting, since 20.1% of float is already short at 4.3 days to cover and the stock rose 89.4% in the month after the last earnings report.

## Management & Governance

Executive turnover is the most striking governance fact. The CFO seat has held four people in under two years: Ronen Faier (interim, August 2024) → Ariel Porat (August 2024) → Asaf Alperovitz (March 2025) → Maoz Sigron (effective May 31, 2026, with Alperovitz departing "to pursue another professional opportunity outside of the industry"). CEO Zvi Lando resigned in August 2024, replaced by Shuki Nir in December 2024; the General Counsel also turned over. A further 8-K with Item 5.02 was filed June 25, 2026, contents unverified.

There is no restatement, no material weakness, and no auditor issue disclosed, and disclosure controls were assessed as effective at 3/31/26. We are not inferring any accounting concern from the turnover. The signal is continuity risk in exactly the functions where estimation judgment matters most: warranty reserves, credit-loss provisioning, 45X credit recognition and monetization, and working-capital management — layered on an ERP transition substantially completed in Q2 2025.

Compensation is unflattering on relative terms. CEO Nir's FY2025 total compensation was $5.06M while "compensation actually paid" was $12.50M — the highest single-year figure in the five-year table, exceeding any 2021–22 boom year — against company TSR of 9.04 versus peer-group TSR of 47.80 (indexed), and 4.26 versus 32.23 in FY2024. The mechanism is equity granted at the bottom: FY2025 PSU grants carried a weighted-average grant-date fair value of $6.39 and RSUs $17.69, then appreciated. That is the intended function of equity compensation, and no awards were repriced; Q1 2026 PSU grants were struck at $37.76, closing the window. But the effect is that management captured substantial value from recovery off a self-inflicted collapse. Performance metrics are revenue, **non-GAAP** operating income, relative TSR, and stock price — with no measure tied to gross margin excluding 45X, free cash flow excluding working capital, or return on capital. In a business whose entire reported gross profit is a credit, that metric set can be satisfied without building durable unsubsidized economics.

Capital allocation shows real discipline in places: $342.25M of notes repaid in cash at maturity with no equity issued; the portfolio rationalization completed; $28.8M paid to shrink an oversized headquarters lease. Insider activity is thin but not negative — one open-market purchase across the reviewed history (director Avery More, 30,000 shares at $13.70 in March 2025, superbly timed), followed by ~$99.5K of trimming in May 2026 while retaining 295,379 shares. There is no pattern of executive open-market buying, which at $11 would have been the more meaningful signal. Governance improves at the 2026 annual meeting when the classified board sunsets to annual elections.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($68, +66%).** The 48E construction-start deadline drives a genuine 2026–27 demand pull-forward; revenue reaches ~$1.7B with battery attach continuing above 30% of mix; fixed-cost absorption (+7.8 points in Q1 alone) plus U.S. manufacturing scale pushes unsubsidized gross margin above zero before the credits step down; operating income turns positive in H2 2026; the FEOC final rule clears SolarEdge's U.S. production, and the rising domestic-content thresholds become a share-gain mechanism against Chinese-supplied competitors. On $1.7B of revenue at 2.5x EV/sales plus net cash, the equity is worth roughly $70, before allowing for unhedged dilution above $48.84. A 20.1% short base and 4.3 days to cover would amplify any upside surprise.

**Bear case ($24, −41%).** The FEOC proposed and final rules disqualify some or all of SolarEdge's 45X eligibility, or the credits are reduced without an offsetting cost reduction, and the company returns to the negative gross margin its own risk factor contemplates. 25D elimination bites U.S. residential volumes as the pull-forward exhausts; the post-2027 48E air pocket arrives; European normalization stalls on feed-in tariff cuts; another installer bankruptcy adds to the $30.5M allowance; payables can no longer be stretched and quarterly operating cash flow prints near −$100M, consuming the $191.5M net cash cushion within roughly two years. On ~$1.0B of revenue at 1.2x EV/sales plus residual cash, the equity is worth roughly $23.

**Base case ($40, −2%).** FY2026 revenue of $1.35–1.40B (Q1 $310.5M, Q2 ~$340M, H2 ~$700M on pull-forward), gross margin holding 21–23% with continued 45X support, GAAP operating loss narrowing toward breakeven by Q4 but not clearly positive, operating cash flow near zero on flat payables. At 1.5–1.8x EV/sales on $1.4B plus $191.5M net cash, the equity is worth $38–44. The math: $1.4B × 1.65x = $2.31B EV; + $191.5M net cash = $2.50B; ÷ 60.8M shares = $41.

The base case sits at the current price. That is the core of the AVOID: there is no positive expected return to the most likely outcome, the bear case is a legislated and regulatory risk rather than a speculative one, and the bull case is capped in the high $40s by convertible mechanics until it clears them.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Q2 2026 earnings, expected in the first week of August 2026 (implied by the 140% IV on the August 7 expiry). Secondary and better-defined: the Section 45X FEOC/PFE Notice of Proposed Rule and Final Rule expected later in 2026, which the 10-K itself flags as potentially creating compliance challenges. A third: the August 24, 2026 final approval hearing on the $55M securities settlement (immaterial to cash).

**Timing window.** The earnings catalyst is days away but direction is not predictable. The company beat consensus in three of the last four quarters, and the stock rose 89.4% in the month following the May 7 print. The FEOC rulemaking has no fixed date within 2026.

## Conclusion

SolarEdge has stabilized. Revenue is growing 41% year over year, Europe has recovered, storage attach is at 30.6% of mix and compounding, the underlying pre-working-capital cash loss has narrowed from roughly $118M annualized to $24M in a quarter, and the balance sheet carries net cash of $191.5M with no maturity until July 2029. Management repaid $342M of notes in cash without dilution and paid $28.8M to shrink an oversized lease commitment. This is not a distressed company in the solvency sense, and the low Altman score circulating in screens reflects negative EBIT and a $1.49B accumulated deficit rather than leverage or liquidity risk.

It is also a company whose entire reported gross profit is a federal manufacturing credit with a legislated 2031 end date and an unresolved eligibility test, whose reported operating cash flow is substantially a payables build against a supplier holding 50.8% of that balance, whose largest end market just lost its homeowner tax credit, and whose out-year earnings consensus is falling even as near-term estimates rise. At ~$41 the shares trade at 1.9x EV/sales, 6.0x book, and 26x a deteriorating next-year estimate, with the sell-side median target below the current price and 21 of 26 analysts at Hold.

The May–June run to $81 was policy-headline sector beta, and its reversal to $41 removed froth rather than revealing new deterioration — we have not marked down the thesis for the price decline. The reversal also means the current price is the pre-squeeze level, not a discount to anything. Our base case is $40, essentially spot, against a $24 bear case rooted in specific regulatory and legislative mechanics and a $68 bull case capped near-term by convertible economics above $48.84. **AVOID**, and do not short it.
