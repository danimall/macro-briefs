**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EnerSys is a ~125-year-old global leader in industrial stored-energy systems (batteries, chargers, power conversion equipment, and enclosures) sold into industrial and infrastructure end markets, not consumer or EV applications. The FY2026 result (year ended March 31, 2026) was a record on the top line ($3.75B, +3.7%) and on adjusted EPS ($10.56, +4%), with a strong cash-flow year ($547.6M operating cash flow, $467.6M FCF) and a clean balance sheet at 1.1x net leverage. The Energy Systems segment (43% of sales, +7.8%) carries an AI-data-center demand tailwind, and Specialty (+12.1%, defense-led) is benefiting from rising global defense budgets. These offset a cyclical Motive Power downturn (-3.6%, organic volume -8%) in forklifts and material handling.

The defining feature of the story is the company's dependence on the IRC Section 45X advanced-manufacturing production tax credit. The FY26 benefit was $158.6M, booked as a reduction of cost of goods sold. Stripping it out, net income was $135.0M versus $293.6M reported — the credit is roughly 54% of net income. Reported gross margin of 29.3% becomes 25.1% ex-45X. The underlying business is growing (adjusted EPS ex-45X +15% to $6.41), but reported earnings, margins, and the buyback program (FY26 returned $409M, exceeding FCF) are materially propped up by a federal subsidy whose future is subject to legislative change. The credit survived near-term (management guides to $42–47M in Q1 FY27), but any narrowing, sourcing disqualification, or eventual phase-out would hit reported EPS hard.

The stock has appreciated ~173% over the trailing twelve months, from the low-$80s to ~$228. After that move it trades at ~29.5x trailing GAAP EPS ($7.70) and ~35x adjusted-ex-45X EPS ($6.41). How an investor capitalizes the 45X stream (durable subsidy or fading windfall) largely determines whether the stock is cheap or expensive. With the favorable AI-data-center narrative now broadly recognized, the multiple expanded, and a binary policy dependency unresolved, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate ENS a HOLD, with the June 11 Investor Day and clarity on 45X durability as the key reassessment points.

## Company Overview

EnerSys operates four reportable franchises against FY2026 revenue of $3.75B:

- **Energy Systems — $1,651M (43% of sales), +7.8% YoY.** UPS, telecom/broadband, data centers, utility power, switchgear, and enclosures. Adjusted operating margin expanded 210 bps to 8.8%, driven by AI data-center and industrial demand. This is the growth engine and the segment most exposed to the structural AI-power buildout.
- **Motive Power — $1,431M (38%), -3.6% YoY (organic volume -8%).** Forklift, AGV, and material-handling batteries. Historically the highest-margin segment (~14% adjusted), but margins compressed 170 bps on lost volume leverage, freight, and tariffs. Tariff-driven customer project pauses and weak EMEA auto weighed on the year.
- **Specialty — $665M (19%), +12.1% YoY.** Aerospace & defense, premium auto/Class-8 SLI, and medical. Adjusted operating margin jumped to 9.3% from 6.2%, helped by the Bren-Tronics defense acquisition ($206M, July 2024).
- **New Ventures — nascent.** BESS for commercial real estate and EV fast charging, pre-scale and reported within Corporate/Other.

The competitive moat rests on proprietary Thin Plate Pure Lead (TPPL) technology (not patent-protected but capital-intensive to replicate), a deep installed base, and a global direct sales/service network serving 10,000+ customers across 100+ countries, with no customer exceeding 10% of revenue. Principal competitors include East Penn, Exide/Stryten, Clarios, SAFT, EaglePicher (in A&D), and power-systems players such as Vertiv, Delta, and OmniOn, plus Chinese cell producers.

A leadership and strategy reset is underway. Shawn O'Connell was promoted to CEO in May 2025 after a 14-year internal climb, has launched the "EnerGize" strategic framework, and hosted an NYSE Investor Day on June 11, 2026. A May 28, 2026 business-unit realignment shifted Keith Fisher's title to "President, Network & Infrastructure," signaling a re-segmentation around the fastest-growing data-center/communications franchise. CFO Andrea Funk remains in place as a continuity anchor.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (FY24 → FY26):**
- Revenue: $3,582M → $3,618M → $3,751M. The FY26 +3.7% growth decomposed into +3% price, +2% FX, +1% M&A, and -2% organic volume — pricing and mix carried the year against soft underlying volumes.
- GAAP net income: $269M → $364M → $294M (-19% YoY in FY26).
- GAAP diluted EPS: $7.70 vs. $8.99 prior (-14%); adjusted diluted EPS $10.56 (+4%).

The headline net income decline is largely optical. FY25 carried an unusually large 45X true-up, inflating the prior-year base. The cleaner read is adjusted EPS ex-45X of $6.41 in FY26 versus $5.58 in FY25 (+15%), which captures underlying earnings power growing at a healthy clip.

**The 45X dependency.** The credit pays $35/kWh on cells, $10/kWh on modules, and 10% on electrode materials, generating real cash today but introducing a binary policy dependency for tomorrow.

**Margins.** GAAP operating margin slipped to 11.4% from 12.8% on $51M of restructuring (vs. $14M prior) and $10.8M of accelerated stock comp. Adjusted operating margin held roughly flat at 14.4% vs. 14.6%. The mix shift — higher-value Energy Systems and Specialty offsetting cyclical Motive Power — is structurally favorable.

**Cash flow.** FY26 operating cash flow of $547.6M (vs. $260.3M in FY25, which was depressed by a $192M prepaid-tax swing) and FCF of $467.6M (12.5% FCF margin). FCF conversion ran ~159% of net earnings, and primary operating capital fell to 22.2% of sales (170 bps improvement). Capex was $80M (down from $121M), with FY27 capex guided to only ~$70M — light capital intensity consistent with the gigafactory ambition being deprioritized.

**Balance sheet.** Cash of $438.7M, total debt ~$1,120M, net debt of $684M = 1.1x adjusted EBITDA (down from 1.3x), well inside the 4.0x covenant. Interest coverage is 12.5x, current ratio 2.66, ROE 15.4%, ROA 7.3%. The company refinanced into a $1.0B revolver maturing 2030 in September 2025; the only fixed maturity of note is $300M of 6.625% senior notes due 2032. There is no refinancing cliff.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks ran at 2.4x the prior year and exceeded FCF; share count fell from ~39.8M to ~37.4M (basic), with ~$876M remaining under authorization. The dividend was raised to $0.2625/quarter, a modest payout with ample coverage. The buyback effectively monetizes the 45X windfall into share-count reduction while the credit lasts — sensible if the credit is durable, less so if it fades and the buyback was funded against a temporary subsidy.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats (Q4 FY26 +5.6%, Q1 FY26 +0.3%, with two mid-single-digit beats in between). Current-quarter EPS estimates were revised +7.7% over 30 days, though full-year revisions are more muted (+3.9% over 90 days, current year), and next-year estimates ticked down 1.9% over 30 days.

## Growth Analysis

The thesis is diversification across structurally divergent end markets. Two tailwinds offset one cyclical headwind:

1. **AI/data-center power.** EnerSys explicitly describes data centers as "in the midst of a growth cycle driven by AI." A lithium data-center solution and BESS for warehouse operators both moved into customer commissioning — early evidence the pivot is materializing rather than aspirational. This connects to the broad infrastructure-layer demand confirmation visible across the power and electrical-equipment supply chain.

2. **Defense.** Rising global defense budgets, with EMEA outpacing the US, are driving Specialty's growth, augmented by Bren-Tronics. The demand backdrop is supportive.

The headwind is Motive Power cyclicality, pressured by tariff-driven project pauses and weak EMEA auto. The encouraging note is sequential order improvement in Motive Power and Transportation in Q4, which management frames as a potential FY27 swing factor.

The estimate-revision picture is positive but not the explosive inflection that would warrant a stronger growth call. Current-quarter EPS revisions of +7.7% (30d) and current-year +3.9% (90d) reflect genuine momentum but fall well short of the +30%-in-90-days threshold that signals a true earnings inflection. This is a steady mid-single-digit organic growth business (ex-45X earnings power +15%, but flattered by buybacks and mix) with a real but moderate data-center kicker, not a hyper-growth name. FY27 Q1 guidance of $915–955M revenue and $2.70–2.90 adjusted EPS implies continued modest growth.

Growth durability is supported by the breadth of the data-center theme across the supply chain — power generation, transmission, electrical equipment, and now industrial power systems all show confirming demand signals, which argues against a near-term deceleration at the infrastructure layer even as the chip layer has shown isolated warnings. EnerSys participates as a picks-and-shovels supplier of UPS and backup power, not as a chip or cloud player.

## Valuation Assessment

At $227.95, ENS trades at ~29.5x trailing GAAP EPS ($7.70) and ~19x consensus current-year EPS ($12.01 estimate). On the cleaner ex-45X adjusted basis ($6.41), the multiple is ~35x. The forward P/E of ~29.6x reflected in the data is consistent with this.

The peer set offers little direct support. The Electrical Components & Equipment comparables (Sensata 154x, Mercury negative, Acuity 21.2x, Powell 56.7x, Vicor 99.7x) show enormous dispersion and a distorted median of 99.7x driven by depressed-earnings names — not a usable benchmark. EnerSys at ~29.5x trailing sits in the lower-to-middle range of that scattered set, but the comparison is not meaningful given how different the business models and earnings bases are. The more disciplined frame is the company's own normalized earnings power.

The central valuation question is how to capitalize the 45X stream. If 45X is treated as a durable, multi-year subsidy embedded in the cost structure, then ~19x current-year EPS is reasonable for a diversified industrial with a data-center tailwind and strong FCF. If 45X is treated as a fading windfall, the relevant number is ~35x ex-45X earnings — a premium multiple for a business growing organic volume at -2% and reliant on price and mix. The truth sits between, but the market is currently paying close to the favorable end of that range.

Two cautions weigh on the valuation score. First, the 173% twelve-month move has compressed the gap to fair value; the easy re-rating from the data-center recognition has largely happened. Second, a "cheap" headline multiple here is misleading precisely because over half the reported earnings rest on a policy credit; that is the profile of a value-trap risk, not a margin-of-safety setup. Analyst targets (median $265, mean $247, high $280) sit above the current price, but on a name already at a ~30x trailing multiple with a binary policy overhang, those targets reflect the same favorable narrative as the price rather than independent confirmation of mispricing.

We assign a below-neutral valuation score. The asymmetry is not compelling at current levels: meaningful upside requires both 45X durability and a data-center acceleration that the multiple already partly anticipates, while the downside includes a credible 45X-impairment scenario that would reset reported earnings sharply lower.

## Competitive Landscape

EnerSys holds a defensible incumbent position in industrial stored energy. The TPPL technology and integrated systems-plus-service model differentiate it from commodity cell suppliers, and the global direct sales/service network creates switching friction in mission-critical applications (UPS, telecom backup, defense). No customer exceeds 10% of revenue, which limits concentration risk.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. In Energy Systems and Specialty, the company is gaining ground in structurally growing markets (data center, defense), and the segment realignment toward "Network & Infrastructure" suggests management is organizing around its winning franchises. In Motive Power, the company is the lead-acid/TPPL incumbent navigating a customer transition toward maintenance-free lithium, racing against cell integrators sourcing Asian chemistry. EnerSys's differentiation in lithium is integration and service rather than cell-chemistry leadership — a tenable but not dominant position. The plant closures (Tijuana, Monterrey, São Paulo) and Richmond, KY consolidation are partly a response to this transition, optimizing footprint while reshoring to capture 45X and de-risk sourcing.

The five-year TSR of 193.7 lagged the peer group (272.1), which frames both the opportunity and the burden on the new management team. The competitive position is improving at the franchise level (data center, defense) but contested in the legacy core, netting to a modestly above-neutral score.

## Risk Assessment

1. **IRC Section 45X policy risk — the defining risk.** ~54% of net income and a large share of buyback funding depend on the federal production tax credit. FY26 risk factors flag the benefit as "not guaranteed." The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) modified energy credits and added foreign-entity-of-concern sourcing restrictions, with new IRS Notice 2026-15 MACR guidance in March 2026. Any future reduction, sourcing disqualification, or phase-out would hit reported EPS hard. This is a binary, policy-dependent overhang that the current multiple does not appear to discount.

2. **Gigafactory/DOE funding uncertainty.** Lithium growth depends on partner Verkor's proposed South Carolina gigafactory and a $199M DOE award (Jan 2025) that was paused by executive order and remains subject to renegotiation. FY27 capex of only ~$70M implies the gigafactory build is not an imminent commitment — reducing capital and execution risk but also capping the lithium growth story.

3. **Motive Power cyclicality and tariffs.** The largest legacy segment is in a volume downturn. Tariffs raise input (copper, antimony) and freight costs; ~25% of revenue carries lead-indexed pricing with a 6–9 month lag.

4. **Technology transition risk.** Customers shifting from flooded lead-acid to lithium/TPPL is driving plant closures. Slow conversion would cede share to lithium integrators.

5. **FX and commodity exposure.** ~40% of sales are non-US (euro dominant); lead is the key commodity. FY26 carried a $7.4M FX loss and a $9.7M UK pension settlement charge.

6. **Tax-structure complexity.** The effective rate jumped to 15.5% (from 10.5%) on valuation-allowance changes, a Swiss IP transfer, and OECD Pillar 2. Reliance on a low-taxed Swiss subsidiary creates ongoing rate volatility.

7. **ERP implementation.** A multi-year ERP rollout has previously caused shipping delays — an operational execution watch item.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-mildly-favorable and does not contradict the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 44.7% is rich versus 1-year historical vol of 37.1% (a 7.6pp spread), but IV sits at only the 9th percentile of its own chain and has fallen from 48.5% a month ago — uncertainty is easing, not building. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.6%), and the put/call volume and OI ratios (0.09 and 0.11) are low, indicating no meaningful protective-put demand or bearish positioning. Short interest is modest at 4.5% of float with 2.6 days to cover. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±9.8%, consistent with a stock that has been volatile post-earnings but is not pricing a specific imminent catalyst. The flat term structure (44.7% near ≈ 45.4% far) suggests evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a binary event premium.

The lack of fundamental binary risks priced into the options market is itself part of the risk-score story: the 45X policy overhang is a genuine future catalyst that options positioning does not appear to compensate for, which is why we score risk factors below neutral despite calm near-term positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The data-center power tailwind is real and durable, Energy Systems compounds high-single-digit growth, Motive Power orders inflect positively in FY27 (Q4 showed sequential improvement), and Specialty sustains defense-driven double-digit growth. 45X proves durable through budget cycles, allowing continued aggressive buybacks against $467M+ of FCF on a shrinking ~37M share count. The June Investor Day delivers credible long-term targets, the multiple holds at ~30x, and EPS compounds toward $13–14. In this scenario the stock reaches the analyst high near $280, roughly +23% from current.

**Bear Case.** 45X is narrowed or phased out in a future budget cycle, resetting reported earnings toward the ex-45X level (~$6.41 adjusted, ~$135M GAAP net income). The buyback, having been funded above FCF against the subsidy, becomes harder to sustain. Motive Power weakness persists, the lithium transition cedes share, and the multiple compresses toward the mid-teens on a de-rated earnings base. The low analyst target of $176.43 (-23% from current) approximates this outcome; a sharper 45X impairment combined with multiple compression could take the stock lower still.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a directional call: the two scenarios are roughly symmetric in magnitude (+23% to the high target, -23% to the low target), and the pivotal variable — 45X durability — is a policy question the analyst cannot handicap with confidence. The fundamental business is sound, the balance sheet is clean, and cash generation is strong, but the current price already reflects the favorable narrative without discounting the binary overhang.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (rationale: uncertain).** This is a quality business with strong cash flow and a real tailwind, offset by a binary policy dependency and a multiple that already prices the good news. The trajectory supports this: the prior two reports rated BUY before downgrading to HOLD in May, with the valuation sub-score declining from 6.5 to 5.0 as the stock re-rated. New data confirms that downgrade — the stock has held its gains, growth is steady but not inflecting, and the 45X overhang remains unresolved.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clear evidence at or after the June 11 Investor Day that 45X is durable through the medium term (multi-year guidance that incorporates the credit with management conviction), removing the central overhang.
- Motive Power organic volume returns to positive YoY growth (vs. -8% in FY26), confirming the cyclical inflection and broadening the growth base beyond data center/defense.
- A pullback toward the high-$170s to $190 (the analyst low and below), which would restore asymmetry by compressing the forward multiple toward the low-20s on a fully recognized data-center franchise.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A budget-cycle development that narrows, disqualifies (FEOC/MACR sourcing), or sets a phase-out path for 45X, which would reset reported earnings and undermine buyback funding.
- Two consecutive quarters of Energy Systems organic growth decelerating below mid-single digits, signaling the data-center tailwind is fading rather than building.
- Free cash flow conversion falling below ~80% of net income alongside continued buybacks above FCF, indicating the capital-return program is no longer self-funding.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the post-Investor-Day disclosure and the Q1 FY27 earnings release (mid-2026), which together should clarify both the strategic framework and near-term 45X realization.

## Conclusion

EnerSys is a well-run, financially healthy industrial franchise with a credible AI-data-center demand tailwind, defense-driven Specialty growth, and a clean balance sheet generating strong free cash flow. The new CEO's "EnerGize" reset and segment realignment around the data-center franchise are constructive, and the reshoring strategy hedges against the company's biggest dependency. These are genuine positives.

The dependency itself is the constraint on conviction. Roughly 54% of reported net income and a large share of the buyback program rest on the 45X production tax credit, a policy variable that survived near-term but cannot be handicapped with confidence over a multi-year horizon. After a 173% twelve-month run, the stock trades at ~29.5x trailing GAAP EPS and ~35x ex-45X adjusted earnings, a multiple that already incorporates the favorable narrative while leaving the binary overhang largely undiscounted. The risk/reward is balanced: meaningful upside requires both 45X durability and continued data-center acceleration, while a credible downside scenario involves 45X impairment resetting earnings sharply. We rate ENS a HOLD and would look to the June Investor Day and subsequent 45X clarity, or a pullback toward the high-$170s to $190, as the catalysts for a more decisive call.
