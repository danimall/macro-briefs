**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dentsply Sirona is the world's largest diversified manufacturer of professional dental products, but the investment case has little to do with that market position and everything to do with a balance sheet under strain and a business in secular revenue decline. FY2025 net sales fell to $3.68B (down 3.0% reported, 4.3% constant currency), the company posted its fourth consecutive annual net loss (−$598M), and the Altman Z-score sits at 1.11, squarely in the distress zone. Cumulative goodwill impairments of roughly $1.6B since 2022 are the arithmetic behind the losses, an admission that the 2016 Sirona merger and 2020 Byte acquisition destroyed enormous value. The equity, at a ~$2.8B market cap sitting on top of ~$2.0B of net debt, is effectively a levered option on a turnaround that has yet to show traction.

The turnaround toolkit is now visible: a 2026 restructuring plan targeting ~$120M of annualized savings, R&D lifted from ~4% to ≥5% of sales, a diversified (non-exclusive) Patterson distribution agreement, and the elimination of the common dividend in Q1 2026 to preserve cash. The most constructive data point in the file is a June 2026 cluster of open-market purchases by five independent directors near $10/share — real insider capital committed at multi-year lows. Balanced against that are the tells of a company that came close to a liquidity/covenant squeeze in 2025: $550M of 8.375% junior subordinated notes, a $435M bridge loan, amended debt covenants in December 2025, and a CEO currently signing filings as both principal executive and principal financial officer.

We rate XRAY AVOID. The stock has rallied roughly 49% off its May 2026 low of $9.40 to $14.05 without any confirming fundamental inflection — Q1 2026 accelerated the deterioration (constant-currency sales −6.7%, Americas −10.7% cc, CTS swinging to an operating loss). Estimate revisions are broadly flat-to-negative, the earnings track record is three misses in the last four quarters, and another goodwill impairment is a live risk at the April 1 annual test. This is a mediocre business in a cyclically weak end-market with a stressed capital structure; the recent price move has removed the deep-value cushion without removing the distress.

## Company Overview

Dentsply Sirona operates in ~140 countries with a 139-year history, generating roughly two-thirds of sales internationally (heavy European manufacturing base — Germany, Sweden, Switzerland) and selling about two-thirds of dental products through third-party distributors. Customer concentration is meaningful: Henry Schein was 13% of FY25 net sales and Patterson represented 11% of receivables. The company reports four segments (FY2025 net sales $3.68B):

- **Essential Dental Solutions (EDS)** — $1,469M, the profit engine at $514M segment adjusted operating income. Consumables (endodontics, restoratives, preventives): the highest-margin, most defensible business.
- **Connected Technology Solutions (CTS)** — $1,036M in equipment, imaging, and CAD/CAM (CEREC). Only $52M segment adjusted operating income in FY25, and it turned to a −$6M operating loss in Q1 2026, the clearest sign of competitive and cyclical stress.
- **Orthodontic and Implant Solutions (OIS)** — $850M, down 12.6%, including SureSmile aligners and the discontinued Byte DTC franchise.
- **Wellspect Healthcare** — $325M in urology/enterology catheters, +6.6% growth with ~$102M segment operating income. Structurally different from dental, defensive, and the one clear bright spot.

The core consumables (EDS) and Wellspect generate real segment profit. The losses come from non-cash impairments and a cost structure the 2026 plan is trying to shrink. Wellspect is a plausible divestiture candidate that could materially de-lever, and is worth monitoring as a potential catalyst.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is that of a distressed turnaround.

**Revenue is in secular decline.** $3.92B (2022) → $3.97B (2023) → $3.79B (2024) → $3.68B (2025). Q1 2026 deteriorated further: reported sales flat at $880M but −6.7% constant currency, with the Americas at −10.7% cc.

**Four consecutive net losses:** −$950M (2022), −$132M (2023), −$910M (2024), −$598M (2025). Retained earnings have swung from +$456M (2022) to an accumulated deficit of −$1.56B. The mechanism is serial impairment: $650M goodwill/intangible impairment in 2025 and $1,014M in 2024. Goodwill has fallen from $2.69B (2022) to $1.15B (2025), with $1.59B of accumulated impairment losses. CTS and OIS goodwill has been substantially written off.

**Margins are compressing.** Gross margin fell to 50.0% in 2025 (from 51.6%) and to 48.5% in Q1 2026 (−450 bps YoY), driven by unfavorable CAD/CAM and implant mix, pricing pressure, and tariff costs on Europe-made equipment sold into the U.S. The tariff exposure is structural given the European manufacturing footprint.

**Cash flow is the redeeming feature but it is thinning.** Operating cash flow fell from $461M (2024) to $235M (2025); FCF was $104M (2.8% margin). Cash dropped from $326M at 12/31/25 to $190M at 3/31/26, a $136M decline in a single quarter driven partly by front-loaded restructuring and debt repayment.

**The capital structure carries clear stress signals.** Net debt is ~$2.0B and net-debt-to-cap deteriorated to 59.9% (from 48.9%). D/E is 3.05. In sequence: a $435M bridge loan in March 2025 to cover commercial paper; $550M of 8.375% junior subordinated notes due 2055 issued in June 2025 (a high coupon, deeply subordinated instrument typical of a borrower shoring up liquidity); amended debt covenants with private-placement noteholders and revolver lenders on 12/24/2025; and elimination of the $0.16 quarterly dividend starting Q1 2026. A $2.1B deferred-tax valuation allowance signals management does not expect to generate taxable income to use those assets, an internal tell about forward earnings expectations. There is $228M of debt due within a year and $53M of trapped cash in Russia.

The narrative that holds these facts together: the company approached a covenant/liquidity squeeze in 2025 and is actively de-risking. The dividend cut, the junior sub notes, and the covenant amendments are consistent pieces of that story. Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of refinancing and the discount rate applied in impairment models, a double headwind for a levered company with intangibles one forecast revision from another write-down.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth outlook is negative. The demand backdrop is a recession in discretionary dental capital equipment: clinicians are deferring financed equipment purchases amid high rates and macro uncertainty, which hits CTS and implants hardest, and most acutely in the U.S. Distributor inventories are already below historical averages, so there is limited restocking tailwind, and the deliberate shift away from distributor-held inventory plus new non-exclusive Patterson agreements will increase near-term sales volatility rather than provide a growth lever.

Estimate revisions do not support a growth inflection. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been revised down 7.1% over 90 days to $0.35; current-year sits at $1.42 (−0.9% over 90 days). Next-quarter and next-year revisions are modestly positive (+1.4% and −0.3%), so the picture is flat-to-slightly-negative, not the +30% revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection. The company-selected performance metric, adjusted EPS, has declined every year: 2.82 → 2.09 → 1.83 → 1.67 → 1.60.

The two credible internal growth levers are the ≥5%-of-sales R&D reinvestment (which takes years to convert to revenue) and Wellspect, which is insulated from the dental capex cycle. Long-term dental demand drivers — aging demographics, aesthetic dentistry, DSO consolidation, digital workflows — remain intact per the 10-K, but they are not translating into current results, and the CAD/CAM franchise that should have captured the digital-workflow tailwind is instead ceding share to lower-cost intraoral scanners.

## Valuation Assessment

On GAAP, XRAY has negative trailing earnings, so headline P/E is meaningless. The relevant frame is forward earnings power and enterprise value. On consensus FY26 EPS of $1.42 and next-year $1.55, the stock trades at ~9.9x current-year and ~9.1x next-year — optically cheap. But the "E" here is adjusted EPS on a declining trajectory, and the enterprise value tells the more honest story: ~$2.8B equity plus ~$2.0B net debt gives an EV near $4.8B against FY25 revenue and thin, still-declining margins. On FCF of $104M, the equity yields roughly 3.7%, inadequate for the leverage and business risk carried.

The peer set (Health Care Supplies) trades at a median P/E of ~60x, but that median is distorted by names with depressed or negative earnings (NEOG, BLFS) and premium compounders (ICUI 77x, NVST 60x), and is not a clean comparable for a levered distressed name. XRAY's low absolute multiple reflects structural impairment risk, not a mispricing — the classic value-trap signature. A compressed multiple that reflects genuine distress does not earn a high valuation score.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $9.00, mean $13.375, median $13.50, high $17.00. The stock sits above the mean and median, which argues the near-term upside consensus sees is limited and the stock is already trading toward the middle-to-upper end of what the sell side will underwrite. On a name like this — negative GAAP earnings, distressed balance sheet, no earnings inflection — analyst targets are not floors that get chased higher; they reflect a wide, uncertain distribution around a turnaround with binary outcomes. We assign a valuation score of 4.5: cheap on adjusted forward EPS, but the cheapness is compensation for real distress, and the recent rally has consumed most of the deep-value margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Dentsply's competitive advantage rests on brand-portfolio breadth, a large installed base, distribution reach, and the DS Core cloud platform. In consumables (EDS) this moat remains reasonably intact and profitable. The problem is trajectory in the segments that matter for growth. The 10-K cites "competitive pressures including pricing" in CTS/CAD/CAM, where segment operating income collapsed to $52M in FY25 and turned negative in Q1 2026. This is a market leader losing profitability to lower-cost intraoral scanners — the worse of the two competitive positions (a leader ceding share, versus a challenger gaining it).

OIS revenue fell 12.6%, compounded by the Byte DTC wind-down (aligner sales suspended October 2024, discontinued for new patients January 2025), which carried ~$187M of 2024 impairments and ongoing refund and litigation obligations. The channel is also in transition: the deliberate reduction of distributor-held inventory and the shift to non-exclusive Patterson agreements diversify the U.S. channel but at the cost of near-term volatility and reduced restocking support. Wellspect is the exception — a growing, defensive continence-care franchise — but it is a ~$325M business inside a ~$3.7B company and does not offset the erosion in the dental core.

## Risk Assessment

Idiosyncratic risks here are numerous and several are unquantified:

1. **Further impairment risk.** Management explicitly warns of possible additional write-downs; Q1 2026 disclosed that OIS and CTS indefinite-lived intangibles "continue to approximate carrying value." The April 1 annual goodwill test, run against a depressed stock price and cut forecasts, is a live catalyst for another impairment that would further dent thin equity.
2. **Leverage and refinancing.** Z-score 1.11, D/E 3.05, net-debt-to-cap 59.9%, 8.375% new notes, December 2025 covenant amendments, and $228M due within a year keep capital access and covenant headroom as active risks in a higher-for-longer rate environment.
3. **Litigation overhang.** Two waves of securities class actions — the 2022 revenue-recognition/distributor-rebate matter (class certified July 2025, partial summary judgment briefing complete January 2026) and the 2024 Byte-related litigation (survived motion to dismiss January 2026, now in discovery) — plus derivative suits. A German criminal tax investigation (2016–2017 intercompany loans) and an IRS dispute over a $451M 2016 stock redemption are both potentially material and unquantified.
4. **Governance instability.** An extraordinary cadence of Item 5.02 director/officer departure filings (multiple in 2025 and into 2026), and a CEO currently serving as both principal executive and principal financial officer — no permanent CFO during a leverage-stressed turnaround. This is the single biggest execution risk.
5. **Customer/channel concentration.** Henry Schein at 13% of sales and the Patterson channel transition amplify sales volatility.
6. **Tariffs.** Structural margin headwind from manufacturing dental equipment in Europe for U.S. sale.

Several of these are not fully priced given the low absolute equity value and the recent rally, which supports a low risk score of 3.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market has shifted from defensive to relatively sanguine over the past month. ATM IV fell from 68.1% to 57.3% as the stock rallied, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 2.06 to 0.36 — the bearish positioning that dominated in June has unwound. Near-term put/call skew is −5.1% (call-heavy) and the P/C volume ratio is 0.47. This reflects near-term optimism riding the rally, not confirmation of a fundamental turn. IV remains rich to 1-year historical vol (57.3% vs 45.6%), and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±16.0%, so the market still prices meaningful event risk. The 3-month tenor shows a positive +8.4% skew (put-heavy) and a 29.2% implied move, indicating hedging demand builds around the next earnings window. Short interest at 16.3% of float (4.9 days to cover) confirms sustained institutional bearishness underneath the recent optimism.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$17, matching the high analyst target)

The 2026 restructuring delivers its ~$120M of annualized savings, margins stabilize and recover off the Q1 48.5% gross-margin trough, and R&D reinvestment plus DS Core traction slow the CAD/CAM bleed. The Americas equipment cycle bottoms as rates ease, and constant-currency sales inflect toward flat. A Wellspect divestiture (or partial monetization) at a healthy multiple materially de-levers the balance sheet, removing the distress discount and re-rating the equity toward 12x forward EPS. The June director purchases near $10 prove prescient. Upside is roughly +21%.

### Bear Case (~$9, near the low analyst target)

The dental capital-equipment recession persists, constant-currency declines continue through 2026, and the April goodwill test triggers another impairment. Margin compression from tariffs and mix does not reverse, FCF thins further, and covenant headroom becomes a live issue requiring another dilutive or expensive financing. Litigation (the two securities classes, the German probe, the IRS matter) produces a material adverse outcome. The equity re-rates toward $9, roughly −36% from current levels. Given the leverage, the tail below $9 is real.

The distribution is genuinely two-sided, but the recent rally has skewed the near-term reward/risk unfavorably: ~+21% to the bull case against ~−36% to the bear case, with the bear case carrying a heavier probability weight given the ongoing revenue deterioration and unresolved balance-sheet stress.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The nearest identifiable catalysts are the Q2 2026 earnings report (likely late July/early August, given the May 5 Q1 print) and the April 1 annual goodwill test (already passed for this cycle, but a residual write-down could surface in subsequent filings). A Q2 miss consistent with the 3-of-4 recent-miss pattern and continued Americas weakness could reverse the recent rally. But there is no single hard-dated event (debt maturity wall, binary regulatory ruling) that forces a near-term repricing.

**Timing window**: Q2 earnings within roughly one to two months provide a defined window; beyond that the thesis is a slow grind on continued revenue decline, which is a poor structure for a short given carry.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is elevated at 16.3% of float with 4.9 days to cover. This is meaningful but below the >25% level that would make the short crowded. The recent unwinding of the put/call OI ratio and the rally off the low demonstrate the squeeze dynamic a fresh short faces — the stock has already run hard against the shorts.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options positioning has turned call-heavy near-term (−5.1% skew, P/C volume 0.47) and IV has compressed as the stock rallied, so the options market is not currently confirming a bearish thesis in the front month. The 3-month tenor shows some put demand (+8.4% skew), but the near-term signal argues against pressing a short into optimism.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: A bear-case target of ~$9 implies ~36% downside, against squeeze/turnaround-optimism upside that could plausibly retest $17 (~+21%). That is roughly 1.7:1 reward-to-risk against a recently-run stock with elevated short interest — below the 2:1 bar and into unfavorable squeeze territory.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental case for downside is real, but the setup fails the short test: no hard-dated forced-selling catalyst, elevated short interest that just demonstrated a squeeze, and reward/risk below 2:1 after the rally. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID.

**Re-entry criteria**: We would revisit constructively on (1) two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or positive constant-currency revenue, particularly a bottoming of Americas CTS; (2) evidence of margin recovery, with gross margin recovering above 50% and the restructuring savings visibly reaching the operating line; (3) a concrete de-leveraging event, such as a Wellspect divestiture that brings net-debt-to-cap back below ~45% and removes covenant risk; (4) appointment of a permanent, credible CFO ending the CEO-as-PFO arrangement; or (5) a price re-set back toward the June $10 level, where the director-purchase cohort committed capital and the deep-value margin of safety was more intact.

**Timeline**: The headwinds — dental capital-equipment demand recession, tariff pressure on European-made equipment, refinancing cost in a higher-for-longer rate environment, and litigation overhang — are likely to persist through at least the next 12 months. The restructuring savings take multiple quarters to reach the P&L, and a genuine revenue inflection requires the equipment cycle to turn, which is rate-dependent and not yet in evidence.

**Score trajectory context**: XRAY has scored 4.3 (Mar) → 3.2 (May) → 3.4 (Jun), with the recommendation moving HOLD → AVOID → AVOID. New data neither confirms a reversal nor deepens the decline decisively: Q1 fundamentals worsened while the stock rallied off its low, and the June director-buying cluster is a genuine positive that argues against pushing the score materially lower. The overall score of 3.6 reflects a modest firming versus June, driven almost entirely by management-quality alignment (negative comp-actually-paid, director open-market buying) rather than any improvement in the operating or balance-sheet picture. The AVOID stands: the recent price move has removed the deep-value cushion without removing the distress.

## Conclusion

Dentsply Sirona is a distressed turnaround with the profile of a global healthcare leader. The core consumables and Wellspect businesses generate real profit, and the board has committed personal capital near the lows. But the equity trades on top of ~$2.0B of net debt, with a 1.11 Z-score, four straight annual losses, a declining top line accelerating to −6.7% constant currency in Q1 2026, compressing margins, thinning FCF, and a CEO doubling as CFO amid relentless executive churn. Estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, the earnings track record is three misses in four quarters, and another impairment is a live risk.

The rally off the May low has run ahead of any fundamental confirmation, leaving reward/risk unattractive (~+21% to bull, ~−36% to bear) and removing the deep-value margin that briefly existed near $10. We rate XRAY **AVOID**, one to revisit only on demonstrated revenue stabilization, margin recovery, concrete de-leveraging, and management stabilization, or at a materially lower entry price.
