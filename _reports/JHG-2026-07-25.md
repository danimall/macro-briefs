**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The central fact governing this report is that Janus Henderson has been taken private. The merger with the consortium led by Trian Fund Management (Nelson Peltz) and General Catalyst — via the Jersey-incorporated Jupiter Company Limited — closed on June 30, 2026 at $52.00 per share in cash, and the shares were delisted from the NYSE the same day. Public shareholders were cashed out. There is no longer a tradeable JHG equity, so the standard framework of market cap, beta, forward return, and price targets no longer applies. The $51.95 "current price" in the data feed is a stale reflection of the near-arb close-to-deal-price convergence, not a live quote.

The deal path produced a positive outcome for holders who were in the stock when the merger was announced. The original agreement, signed December 21, 2025, offered $49.00/share. By June 18, 2026, all regulatory approvals and the required client-consent threshold (≥80% of revenue run-rate — asset managers must re-paper advisory contracts on a change of control) were obtained, and the price was raised to $52.00, a 6.1% bump. Shareholders approved and the transaction closed on schedule. The final buyer group broadened beyond the original disclosure to include Qatar Investment Authority, Lunate Capital, Sun Hung Kai & Co., and MassMutual, with committed debt financing from an eight-bank syndicate. CEO Ali Dibadj stays on, and the dual London/Denver headquarters is retained.

The underlying business is healthy but its 2025 headline results overstate normalized earnings power. Reported net income doubled to $815.9M and diluted EPS reached $5.23, but that surge leans on roughly $390M of extraordinary hedge-fund performance fees (up from $70.4M) and a $136.7M non-cash FX reclassification benefit. Adjusted EPS was $4.78, and stripping the one-offs leaves underlying growth in the mid-single to low-double-digit range. The Guardian Life mandate powered AUM to a record $493.2B but at low fee margins, compressing the blended fee rate. Because the equity is delisted, no meaningful forward-looking investment recommendation is possible; the rating is retained as a HOLD placeholder for a completed, settled transaction.

## Company Overview

Janus Henderson is an independent global active asset manager, Jersey-incorporated with headquarters in London and a substantial U.S. operating base in Denver. It runs as a single reportable segment across four capabilities — Equities, Fixed Income, Multi-Asset, and Alternatives — serving three client channels: Intermediary (~49% of AUM), Institutional (~31%), and Self-directed (~20%). The firm traces its lineage back roughly 92 years.

At December 31, 2025, AUM was up 30% year-over-year. The growth was driven by +$46.5B from the Guardian partnership, +$49.7B market performance, +$8.3B FX, and $56.5B net inflows. Investment performance is credible in aggregate — 65% of AUM beat benchmark on 1/3/5-year horizons, 67% on 10-year — but the mix is uneven. Fixed income (90–93% beating on 3–10-year), Multi-Asset (96–98%), and Alternatives (100%) are strong. Equities, still the largest capability at roughly $257B, is the soft spot at 46–54% across periods.

The revenue model is roughly 70% asset-based management fees, supplemented by performance fees, shareowner servicing, and distribution/12b-1 fees. Earnings are directly leveraged to market levels and net flows, which is why the 2025 performance-fee spike matters for interpreting the reported numbers.

## Financial Analysis

On the metrics disclosed in the 10-K filed February 25, 2026, financial health was solid. The Altman Z-score of 3.46 sits in the safe zone, debt-to-equity was 0.42, the current ratio was 4.18, ROE was 17.8%, and ROA was 11.0%.

For FY2025 versus FY2024: revenue was $3,097.3M, up 25%, but heavily flattered by performance fees of $460.0M versus $70.4M — a +$389.6M swing that came almost entirely from a handful of hedge funds. Management fees, the durable base, grew a more modest 11% to $2,168.3M on higher average AUM. Operating income rose 51% to $976.8M, with operating margin expanding to 31.5% from 26.1% (adjusted operating margin 36.0%). Net income doubled with diluted EPS of $5.23 (adjusted $4.78), aided by the FX reclassification of accumulated translation on liquidated entities — a low-quality, one-off item.

Operating cash flow was $719.5M (versus $694.6M), with minimal capex (~$9M), consistent with a capital-light model. The balance sheet held $1.25B cash against only $395.5M long-term debt (5.45% 2034 Senior Notes) and $5.1B equity. Goodwill and intangibles of $4.15B represented roughly half of assets, a latent impairment risk in a market drawdown.

The 2025 headline earnings are not a run-rate. Excluding the extraordinary performance fees and the FX reclass, the underlying business grew mid-single to low-double digits. Fixed-income fee margins compressed sharply from 26.2 to 19.8 bps because the large Guardian book carries low fees, dragging the total average fee margin down from 48.6 to 45.2 bps. AUM growth did not translate proportionally into revenue.

Prior to the merger signing, capital allocation included roughly $249M of dividends and $266M of buybacks in 2025. Both were suspended upon signing the merger agreement, a routine step for a pending take-private.

## Growth Analysis

For a going-concern analysis this section would matter; for a delisted equity it is moot. Directionally, the growth picture was mixed before the deal closed. Net inflows and the Guardian mandate, RBA bolt-on (agreed January 23, 2026), and 2024 build-out in ETFs and private credit (Tabula, NBK Capital Partners, VPC) show a firm expanding its capability set. But the growth is dilutive to fee rate: the Guardian book grew fixed-income AUM from $82.7B to $155.8B at low margins, and equities — the largest and highest-fee capability — carries the weakest relative performance.

Consensus estimate revisions were pointing the wrong way before the delisting: current-year EPS was revised down 2.4% over 90 days, next-year down 3.0%, and both current and next quarter down roughly 4.2–4.5% over 90 days. Q1 2026 also missed (actual $0.90 versus $0.97 estimate, -7.5%), breaking a three-quarter beat streak. Falling estimates against inflated 2025 comparables reflect the normalization of performance fees rather than a structural collapse, but the momentum was negative. With the equity delisted, these estimates are now academic.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is largely irrelevant now — the market cleared at $52.00/share in cash. Holders received a defined outcome. The relevant "return" was the arbitrage from the deal price to close, and the price bump from $49.00 to $52.00 was a positive signal, likely tied to the broadened investor syndicate and strong (if inflated) 2025 results.

For historical context, the trailing P/E near 10.3x and forward P/E near 11.0x looked optically cheap, but the trailing figure sits on peak, performance-fee-inflated earnings. On the $4.78 adjusted 2025 EPS the multiple was closer to ~10.9x, and on a normalized run-rate that strips the peak performance-fee year it would be higher still. The peer set is heterogeneous and not directly comparable: TPG at 120.6x, Blue Owl at 81.5x (alternatives managers with different fee/earnings structures), and Ares Capital at 11.8x (a BDC). JHG's discount to the peer median reflected its exposure to secular active-fee compression, not an obvious mispricing.

A buyer paying ~$8B+ of equity value at $52 was underwriting the platform, distribution reach, and the optionality of a technology/AI transformation freed from quarterly public scrutiny, not this year's peak fee print. Given the resolved outcome and the low-quality earnings base, the valuation dimension scores near neutral; there is no live asymmetric upside to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

JHG competes in a structurally challenged niche: active management under secular fee pressure from passive/ETF migration and robo/AI-driven advice. Its differentiation is investment performance (strong ex-equities) and a diversified global, multi-capability platform. Its largest capability, equities, has the softest relative performance, and the Guardian-driven AUM growth came at structurally lower fees. Net flows were positive, which is better than most active-only peers, but the blended fee-rate erosion signals the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than improving.

The strategic logic of going private is explicit in management commentary: escape public-market quarterly scrutiny to make long-duration investments in AI transformation, technology, private markets, and distribution. General Catalyst was brought in for the AI/tech angle, QIA and Sun Hung Kai for global distribution and capital. This fits a broader wave of asset-manager consolidation seeking scale and public/private convergence. The repositioning will play out in private hands, invisible to public markets.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk — deal completion — has resolved favorably. Shareholders were paid. Residual risks now attach to creditors and the private entity rather than to any equity holder.

The most material forward change is the leverage profile. JHG warned in the 10-K that it "expect[s] to incur a significant amount of debt to finance the proposed Merger," with potential for a ratings downgrade and a Change-of-Control Repurchase Event on the 2034 Notes. The eight-bank financing syndicate confirms substantial acquisition debt is now on or around the business. The clean 0.42x pre-deal D/E overstates the go-forward balance-sheet resilience; a future AUM or market downturn would bite harder against acquisition leverage. This is relevant to bondholders, not to former equity holders.

Structural asset-management risks persist for the private company: passive/ETF fee compression, AUM sensitivity to markets, equity-concentration and key-person dependency, performance-fee volatility (demonstrated by the 2025 swing), private-credit valuation subjectivity, UK DB pension exposure, FX (24% of AUM non-USD against a GBP cost base), and heavy multi-jurisdiction regulation. Execution risk on the Aladdin platform migration (triggering an $8.1M impairment and $13.6M accelerated amortization in 2025) is multi-year.

The Trian conflict of interest — the largest existing shareholder (~20.7%, two board seats since 2022) was also the lead acquirer — was mitigated by a Special Committee advised by Goldman Sachs and Wachtell, and the price bump plus a resounding shareholder vote suggests the process reached a fair outcome. From a public-equity standpoint, idiosyncratic risk is now effectively fully realized and priced, which is why the risk-factor dimension scores high on an "already resolved" basis — a mechanical consequence of the equity ceasing to exist rather than a durable business quality.

## Investment Thesis

This does not affect anyone who held the common stock through close.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Because the transaction has closed and the shares are delisted, standard price-target and exit-criteria mechanics do not apply. For completeness:

- **Base case outcome (realized):** $52.00/share cash, received at close on June 30, 2026. This is the terminal value; there is no ongoing position to target.
- **Upgrade triggers:** None applicable. There is no security to upgrade. The only path to a re-listed public JHG would be a future IPO or spin-out by the private owners, which would be evaluated as a new security if and when it occurs.
- **Downgrade triggers:** None applicable to former equity holders. Creditor-relevant deterioration (ratings downgrade, AUM-driven covenant stress) would be monitored in the credit market, not the equity market.
- **Reassessment timeline:** No natural equity reassessment point exists. Any data feed still quoting JHG at ~$51.95 should be disregarded as stale; the cash consideration was $52.00 and the listing is terminated.

The rating is retained as a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale strictly as a bookkeeping convention for a merger that has settled. It should not be read as an active recommendation to hold a security — no such security trades.

## Conclusion

Janus Henderson is a completed take-private, closed June 30, 2026, with the public equity delisted from the NYSE. The business itself is fundamentally sound — record AUM, positive net flows, credible investment performance outside equities, a capital-light model, and modest pre-deal leverage — but its 2025 headline earnings are inflated by non-recurring performance fees and the FX reclass benefit, and its blended fee rate is compressing under the low-margin Guardian mandate. As a private company, it now carries materially higher acquisition leverage relevant to bondholders.

For the equity investor, there is no forward return to underwrite, no valid current price, and no basis for a BUY or AVOID call on a delisted name; the recommendation is a HOLD placeholder for a settled transaction. Former shareholders realized $52.00 in cash. Any structured data still presenting JHG as a live public equity with a market cap, beta, and price targets is stale and should be treated as invalid.
