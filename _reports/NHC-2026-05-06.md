## Executive Summary

National HealthCare Corporation has executed one of the most impressive financial turnarounds in the post-acute care sector, growing net income from $22M (2022) to $120M (2025) while expanding operating margins from 5.1% to 8.4%. The company now trades at approximately 22x trailing GAAP EPS ($7.67) and 25.5x trailing non-GAAP operational EPS ($6.65), with a $560M acquisition of its formerly leased NHI properties expected to close July 1, 2026, subject to customary closing conditions including HSR clearance. This acquisition eliminates approximately $40M in annual NHI-specific rent, and management has stated it expects the transaction to be accretive to both earnings and cash flow.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) continued organic growth from occupancy recovery (89.7% heading toward pre-pandemic ~92-93%), favorable demographics (baby boomers entering prime skilled nursing age), and annual rate increases across payer categories; (2) the NHI acquisition creating earnings accretion once fully financed, though the magnitude depends on financing terms and purchase-accounting depreciation not yet disclosed; and (3) NHC's superior operational quality (3.83 star rating vs. 2.95 industry average) providing durable competitive advantage in a consolidating sector. The primary risk is the near-term balance sheet transformation from $33M to potentially $350-400M in debt, though $185M in annual operating cash flow provides ample coverage.

## Company Overview

National HealthCare Corporation operates one of the largest independently-owned post-acute care platforms in the Southeastern and Midwestern United States. The company manages 80 skilled nursing facilities (10,329 beds), 26 assisted living facilities, 9 independent living facilities, 3 behavioral health hospitals, 34 homecare agencies, and 33 hospice agencies across 9 states. Revenue mix is diversified across Medicare (31%), Medicaid (30%), Managed Care (12%), and Private Pay/Other (27%).

NHC differentiates through a continuum-of-care model that captures patients across the acuity spectrum, from independent living through skilled nursing and hospice. The company's 1991 spinoff of National Health Investors created a REIT landlord relationship that persisted for 35 years; the pending $560M acquisition of those properties completes vertical integration and eliminates this structural complexity.

Net patient revenue grew 42.9% cumulatively from 2022-2025 (total net operating revenue, which includes other revenue, grew approximately 39.8% over the same period), driven by the White Oak acquisition ($228M full-year 2025 contribution), occupancy recovery (87.9% → 89.7%), and composite per-diem rate increases of 4.0% annually.

**Margin Expansion:**

Total costs as a percentage of revenue declined from 94.9% (2023) to 91.6% (2025). Labor costs — 61% of revenue — dropped from 62.4% to 60.7%, reflecting a 66.6% reduction in agency nursing expenses ($9.3M savings in 2025). Operating margins expanded from 5.1% to 8.4%. This margin improvement is partially structural (permanent reduction in agency reliance, scale from White Oak) and partially cyclical (agency nursing costs likely near a sustainable floor).

**Cash Flow Quality:**

Operating cash flow of $185M against net income of $120M (1.54x conversion ratio) signals high-quality earnings. Free cash flow of $149M after $36.4M in maintenance capex provides substantial capital deployment capacity. The company used this to pay down $97M in debt during 2025, bringing long-term obligations from $130M to $33M.

**Balance Sheet — Pre-Acquisition:**

- Current ratio: 1.82
- Total liabilities-to-equity: 0.42 (total liabilities of $451.9M vs. equity of $1.075B; actual funded debt-to-equity is far lower at approximately 3.7%, reflecting only $40M in credit facility debt against $1.075B equity)
- Cash + unrestricted securities: $256M ($93M cash + $163M securities)
- Undrawn $50M credit facility
- Altman Z-Score: 3.53 (safe zone)

**Balance Sheet — Post-Acquisition (Estimated, Assuming July 2026 Close):**

NHC has not disclosed the financing structure for the $560M acquisition. Assuming NHC deploys $150-200M from existing cash/securities and finances the remainder through new debt, net new borrowings could be approximately $350-400M. This would bring total debt to an estimated ~$385-435M. However, the actual financing mix, terms, and covenants remain undisclosed. Management has stated the transaction is expected to be accretive to both earnings and cash flow, but specific accretion estimates depend on financing costs, purchase-accounting depreciation on acquired real estate, and opportunity cost of deployed cash and securities — none of which have been quantified in public filings.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Drivers:**

1. **Occupancy Recovery:** Current 89.7% vs. pre-pandemic industry norms of 85-87% and NHC's own historical levels above 91%. Each 1 percentage point of occupancy improvement on NHC's owned and leased SNF beds (approximately 9,350 licensed beds; operational beds may be lower) at a blended per-diem of approximately $380 generates an estimated $13-14M in incremental annual revenue at high incremental margins.

2. **Rate Increases:** Medicare +3.2% effective October 2025, hospice +2.6%, state Medicaid increases in Tennessee ($3M) and South Carolina ($4.2M). Private pay rates increased 6.8% in 2025. While future rate increases are subject to annual rulemaking, contract negotiations, and state budgets, the historical trend has been consistently positive across most payer categories.

3. **Demographic Tailwind:** The 75-84 age cohort (prime skilled nursing entry age) is entering its fastest growth period as baby boomers age in. This provides secular demand growth for the next 10-15 years.

4. **Homecare/Hospice Expansion:** This segment generates 19.5% pretax margins on $154M in revenue — materially higher than the 9.2% margin in inpatient services. Organic agency openings create high-ROIC growth.

**Inorganic Growth:**

White Oak (Aug 2024, $216M for 15 SNFs) was successfully integrated and immediately accretive. The NHI acquisition ($560M for 35 properties) is lower-risk operationally since NHC already operates these facilities — the change is from tenant to owner. Beyond these, the skilled nursing acquisition market is described as "tight amid fierce competition," potentially limiting near-term M&A opportunities.

**Growth Rate Estimate:**

Base case organic revenue growth of 4-6% annually (rate increases + occupancy gains) plus full-year contribution from NHI properties and earnings accretion from rent elimination suggests non-GAAP EPS of $8.00-8.50 in FY2026 (20-28% growth), though this estimate is sensitive to acquisition financing terms and purchase-accounting assumptions. Growth normalizes to 8-12% thereafter.

## Valuation Assessment

At $169.41, NHC trades at:
- 22.1x trailing GAAP EPS ($7.67)
- 25.5x trailing non-GAAP operational EPS ($6.65)
- ~20-21x estimated FY2026 non-GAAP EPS ($8.00-8.50)

**Peer Comparison:**

The health care facilities peer set is highly heterogeneous. SEM at 14.2x is a lower-growth diversified operator. ASTH at 76.4x and USPH at 50.3x reflect specialty growth premiums. ACHC has negative earnings. Peer median of 50.3x is skewed by these outliers and not directly comparable to NHC's business mix.

A more appropriate comp is the broader skilled nursing/senior living operator universe. Operators with NHC's quality metrics, growth profile, and balance sheet strength typically trade at 15-22x forward earnings. NHC at ~20-21x forward is at the upper end of this range but justified by: (a) superior quality ratings, (b) above-industry occupancy, (c) active margin expansion, and (d) the NHI acquisition providing a visible earnings step-up.

Valuation is not cheap, but not stretched. The 20-21x forward multiple on an 8-12% medium-term growth rate implies a PEG of ~1.8-2.5x, which is reasonable for a healthcare services company with defensive revenue characteristics and secular demographic tailwinds. The $560M acquisition provides a specific catalyst for earnings accretion that the current multiple may not fully reflect, given the complexity and recency of the announcement.

## Competitive Landscape

NHC operates in a fragmented market with "hundreds of operators" and no single dominant player. Competitive advantages include:

**Quality Differentiation:** 62.5% of NHC's facilities rated 4-5 stars vs. 38.6% industry-wide. Average rating of 3.83 vs. 2.95. This quality premium drives: (a) higher managed care contract rates, (b) preference in hospital discharge planning, (c) higher private pay census mix, and (d) lower regulatory scrutiny.

**Barriers to Entry:** Certificate of Need requirements in most operating states prevent new facility construction by competitors. This creates a natural supply constraint that benefits existing operators as demand grows demographically.

**Vertical Integration:** Post-NHI acquisition, NHC will own substantially more of its operating facilities, significantly improving operational flexibility and eliminating landlord-tenant friction. As of December 31, 2025, NHC operated 80 SNFs (43 owned, 29 leased, 8 managed). The NHI transaction includes 32 SNFs, though four Florida SNFs within the master lease are subleased to a third-party operator rather than operated by NHC. The precise post-close ownership breakdown will depend on remaining lease and management arrangements. The continuum-of-care model (SNF → assisted living → homecare → hospice) captures patients throughout their post-acute journey.

**Administrator Development:** NHC's proprietary 24-month Administrator-in-Training program has produced 59 of its 80 facility administrators. This creates a management pipeline unavailable to competitors reliant on external hiring.

**Competitive risks:** Increasing payor consolidation and vertical integration by large health systems could pressure reimbursement. Not-for-profit competitors have tax and fundraising advantages. Rising professional liability claims affect the entire industry.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Acquisition Financing and Leverage (HIGH):**
Financing terms have not been disclosed. In a 5.5-6.0% interest rate environment, if the full $560M were debt-financed, annual interest expense would be approximately $31-34M; if partially offset by cash/securities deployment, interest on $350-400M of new debt would be approximately $19-24M. Additionally, purchase-accounting depreciation on acquired real estate will reduce GAAP earnings. While coverage ratios should remain comfortable, the loss of balance sheet optionality is real. Any operational misstep would be amplified by leverage.

**2. Self-Insurance Claims Deterioration (MODERATE):**
Reserves grew $18M in 2025 to $122M, with $17.6M in "unfavorable claims activity" representing ~15% of net income. The long-tail nature of professional liability claims means current reserves may prove inadequate. No cap on maximum claims exposure exists.

**3. Government Reimbursement Dependency (MODERATE):**
61% of revenue from Medicare/Medicaid subjects NHC to political and budgetary risk. Medicare sequestration (2% cut) extends through 2030. Medicaid rates depend on state fiscal health. However, the aging demographic creates powerful political pressure to maintain funding.

**4. Agency Staffing Tailwind Exhaustion (LOW-MODERATE):**
The 66% annual reductions in agency nursing costs are approaching a floor. Future margin expansion must come from occupancy leverage and rate increases rather than labor cost reductions.

**5. CMS Minimum Staffing Rule — Deferred, Not Eliminated:**
Suspended until October 2034 under current law, and CMS has since issued an interim final rule rescinding part of the minimum staffing rule. However, future developments may alter implementation, and the rule could re-emerge under a future administration, potentially requiring significant additional hiring. NHC's current staffing levels may already exceed proposed minimums given its quality orientation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($200-215, +18-27%):**
- NHI acquisition closes on schedule at favorable financing terms (<5.5%)
- Occupancy continues recovering toward 91-92% (adds $28-42M revenue at high incrementals)
- FY2026 non-GAAP EPS reaches $8.50+
- Market re-rates to 23-25x forward on improved earnings visibility and owned-asset model
- Hospice/homecare segment grows 10-15% organically

**Bear Case ($130-140, -23-17%):**
- Acquisition financing terms worse than expected (>6.5% cost, restrictive covenants)
- Self-insurance claims continue deteriorating, requiring $25M+ reserve additions
- Occupancy stalls or reverses due to labor shortages or competitive dynamics
- Medicare/Medicaid rate increases below inflation, compressing margins
- Market applies 16-17x multiple reflecting increased leverage and execution risk

## Conclusion

NHC warrants a BUY recommendation based on: (1) demonstrated operational excellence with expanding margins and superior quality metrics, (2) a specific, identifiable near-term catalyst in the NHI property acquisition that management expects to be accretive to earnings and cash flow, (3) secular demographic tailwinds with the 75-84 age cohort entering rapid growth, and (4) reasonable valuation at ~20-21x forward earnings on a mid-teens growth trajectory. The primary risk — increased balance sheet leverage — is manageable given $185M in annual operating cash flow and the elimination of approximately $40M in NHI-specific rent payments. The stock's 80% appreciation over the trailing 12 months reflects genuine fundamental improvement, and the forward setup remains attractive given the acquisition catalyst has yet to close.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The $15-20M earnings accretion estimate from the NHI acquisition is not substantiated by disclosed filings; actual GAAP accretion depends on undisclosed financing terms and purchase-accounting depreciation, which could materially reduce the estimated benefit and affect the FY2026 EPS target underpinning the price target.
