## Executive Summary

Huntington Bancshares closed two major acquisitions (Veritex in October 2025, Cadence Bank in February 2026) that expand its footprint from ~1,000 branches in 14 states to ~1,400 branches across 21 states with $275B+ in total assets. At $16.33, the stock trades at approximately 12x forward earnings and 3.8% dividend yield — below the peer median P/E of 13.6x and at the low end of its historical range — while consensus targets range from $18–$22.50 with a median of $20.

The market is discounting integration risk and the recent Q1 2026 GAAP EPS miss ($0.25 reported, driven by $263M in acquisition-related expenses). On an adjusted basis, Q1 came in at $0.37, beating consensus. NIM expanded to 3.24%, fee income diversification accelerated (capital markets +60%, payments +21%), and management raised its 2027 ROTCE target to 18–19%. The $3B buyback authorization approved in Q1 2026 represents approximately 9% of current market cap.

The primary risks are execution-related: simultaneous integration of two acquisitions while transitioning to Category III regulatory status, Texas CRE concentration (27% of CRE post-Veritex), and negative estimate revision trends reflecting near-term uncertainty. These risks appear over-discounted at current valuation. The stock offers asymmetric upside if integration proceeds on plan, with a clear re-rating path toward 14–15x earnings as execution risk fades through 2H 2026.

## Company Overview

Huntington Bancshares, organized in 1966 with banking roots to 1866, operates two primary segments: Consumer & Regional Banking (~66% of consolidated net income) and Commercial Banking (~51% of consolidated net income — percentages sum to more than 100% due to negative Treasury/Other segment contribution). The bank is differentiated by its "Fair Play" consumer banking philosophy — no overdraft fees under $50, 24-hour grace periods, standby cash lines — which has driven deposit market share gains in key Midwest markets (44% in Columbus OH, 28% in Akron OH).

The Cadence Bank merger (closed February 1, 2026) is the defining event for HBAN's next 2–3 years. It adds $54B in assets, $37B in loans, and $44B in deposits with significant Texas, Southeast, and Gulf Coast exposure. Combined with the $12B Veritex deal (Dallas-Fort-Worth focus), HBAN has shifted from a Midwest-centric bank to a super-regional with a footprint spanning the Midwest, the South, and Texas across 21 states.

Post-merger (Q1 2026), key metrics: total assets $285.4B, CET1 10.2%, ACL coverage 1.78% of total loans and leases, insured deposits ~70%, and liquidity coverage ratio 118%.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers:**

1. **Cadence synergies:** Cost savings from branch rationalization, system consolidation, and headcount optimization are expected to materialize primarily in 2H 2026 and 2027. Revenue synergies from cross-selling capital markets, payments, and treasury management products into Cadence's client base are early-stage but cited as strong.

2. **Fee income diversification:** Capital markets revenue hit a record in Q1 2026 (+60% YoY ex-acquisitions). Payments grew 21%, wealth management 19%. Fee income now represents approximately 27% of total revenue, trending toward 30%+, which reduces NII cyclicality.

3. **Buyback accretion:** At the guided $550M pace for 2026 and $1.1–1.2B for 2027, share repurchases should contribute 2–3% annual EPS accretion (at current prices, $550M buys ~34M shares, or 1.7% of outstanding).

4. **Operating leverage:** The efficiency ratio target of <57% by 2027 (from 59.9% in 2025) implies $400–500M in expense savings on the combined platform.

**Estimate trajectory:** Current year consensus is $1.44 (declining from $1.47 ninety days ago), and next year is $1.89 (from $1.92). The 2–6% negative revision trend reflects near-term caution around merger costs and NIM compression risk. However, the path from $1.44 to $1.89 (+31% YoY growth) demonstrates the market expects substantial earnings accretion from synergy realization.

If HBAN achieves its 18–19% ROTCE target on the combined ~$285B asset base, normalized earnings power is $1.85–$2.00/share — implying current pricing reflects only 8.5–8.8x FY2027 earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Huntington's $285B asset base positions it in a strategically advantageous middle ground. It has the scale to offer capital markets, treasury management, and specialty commercial products (healthcare, technology, distribution finance) that sub-$100B regionals cannot, while retaining relationship-oriented service that differentiates it from JPM/BAC/WFC.

The Texas expansion is strategically sound — Texas is among the highest-growth banking markets in the U.S., with strong population and commercial lending demand. HBAN enters with significant Texas deposits and relationships across Dallas, Houston, Austin, and San Antonio.

The "Fair Play" philosophy is a genuine differentiator for deposit gathering. The bank's Outstanding CRA rating (highest possible) and 44% Columbus deposit share confirm execution quality on the consumer side.

Key competitive risk: the transition to Category III regulation (triggered by >$250B assets) will increase compliance costs by an estimated $100–200M annually. This is a known cost that must be offset by scale economies from the combined platform.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration Execution (HIGH):** Two simultaneous acquisitions of this scale are rare. System conversions, cultural integration, and customer retention are all live risks through year-end 2026. Historical base rate for major bank merger success is approximately 65–70%, but HBAN's track record with FirstMerit (2016) and prior TCF integration (2021) provides management credibility.

**Texas CRE Concentration (MODERATE):** CRE jumped from $11B to $15.2B, with Texas going from 4% to 27% of the portfolio. Dallas and Houston CRE markets have elevated vacancy rates (office particularly). The 3.7% ACL coverage on CRE and active reduction of construction exposure mitigate this, but a sharp Texas downturn would be felt disproportionately.

**Regulatory Transition (MODERATE):** Category III triggers LCR/NSFR compliance, annual stress tests, resolution planning, and SCCL. Management has been building infrastructure since the Cadence announcement, but unexpected compliance costs or enforcement actions during the transition are possible.

**NIM Compression (LOW-MODERATE):** Management guided NIM to "low end" of range due to deposit competition. The balance sheet is modestly asset-sensitive (+2.5% NII in +200bp), so a rate hold environment is roughly neutral. A rapid cutting cycle would compress NIM. The Fed held rates at 3.50%–3.75% at its April 29, 2026 meeting, with the next FOMC decision scheduled for June 16–17, 2026.

**Credit Quality Trends (LOW-MODERATE):** While NCOs remain low at 0.26%, nonaccrual loans rose meaningfully in Q1 2026 (NAL ratio 0.71% vs. 0.62% at year-end), driven by acquired Cadence and Veritex loans. Management attributed this to loan portfolio marks, but further deterioration would warrant monitoring. The ACL adverse scenario implies needing $800M in additional reserves if unemployment reaches 8.4%.

**Moody's Negative Outlook (LOW-MODERATE):** A downgrade from Baa1 to Baa2 would increase wholesale funding costs by approximately 10–15 bps on new issuance. At ~$35B in wholesale funding, this translates to $35–50M in annual cost — meaningful but manageable.

**Macroeconomic Sensitivity:** The Midwest/industrial footprint creates sensitivity to manufacturing weakness and tariff impacts.

## Conclusion

HBAN presents a compelling risk-reward for investors willing to underwrite 12–18 months of integration execution. The stock trades at a meaningful discount to peers on P/E (11.3x vs. 13.6x peer median) and relative to its own earnings growth trajectory (31% EPS growth FY2026 to FY2027). The 3.8% dividend yield provides income while waiting.

The market is mispricing this stock because simultaneous integration of two acquisitions creates headline noise (GAAP EPS miss in Q1, declining estimate revisions) that obscures the underlying earnings power trajectory. Adjusted metrics are tracking to plan, the capital position is robust, and the $3B buyback authorization signals management's confidence in cash generation.

The risks preventing a higher-conviction call are: (a) the 12-month earnings estimate trajectory is negative (revisions of -3% to -6% over 90 days), which typically signals near-term underperformance even when the longer-term thesis is intact; (b) the macroeconomic environment (tariff/trade policy uncertainty, elevated credit stress) creates a hostile backdrop for banks with increased CRE exposure; (c) no insider open-market purchases to confirm management's expressed frustration with stock performance; and (d) at ~1.7x tangible book value, the stock is not deeply discounted on asset-based metrics, meaning the thesis relies primarily on earnings growth and re-rating rather than a margin of safety on book value. These risks warrant standard position sizing (2–3%) rather than outsized conviction, but they are insufficient to overcome the fundamental undervaluation at 11.3x forward earnings with a credible path to 30%+ earnings growth.

**Rating: BUY** with a 12-month base case target of $19.50 and a 2-3% portfolio position.
