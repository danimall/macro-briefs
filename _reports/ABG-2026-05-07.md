## Executive Summary

Asbury Automotive Group is the 5th-6th largest publicly-traded franchised auto retailer in the US, operating 223 franchises across 171 dealership locations in 15 states. The company trades at 7.0x forward earnings with a trailing FCF yield above 15%, generating $18B in annual revenue. CEO David Hult purchased $1.03M in stock at ~$205 in March 2026, and the company repurchased $147M in shares in Q1 alone.

However, the cheapness reflects real fundamental deterioration. Same-store new vehicle GPU has declined 18% over 18 months (from $3,734 to $3,061), Q1 2026 EPS missed estimates by 5.3%, and consensus estimates are declining across all time horizons (-5% to -9% over 90 days). The company faces a particularly acute tariff headwind: 40% of new vehicle revenue derives from imports subject to 25% tariffs, with another 32% from luxury brands with significant import exposure. At 3.2x net leverage (upper end of the 2.5-3.5x target range) heading into a period of margin normalization and potential demand destruction, the balance sheet provides limited cushion.

The stock sits 27% below its 52-week high of $274.50, with 11.3% short interest and options in backwardation. The core question is whether parts & service growth (48% of gross profit, growing 5-10% same-store) and aggressive buybacks can offset the structural new vehicle margin headwind and tariff risk. Until earnings stabilize or tariff resolution provides clarity, the risk/reward remains unclear.

## Company Overview

Asbury Automotive Group operates as a franchised automotive retailer with revenue derived from new vehicles (53%), used vehicles (29%), parts & service (14%), and F&I products (4%). The company also operates TCA (Total Care Auto), a captive F&I product provider that manufactures extended service contracts, GAP protection, and prepaid maintenance.

**Brand composition:** Toyota/Lexus (30% of new vehicle revenue), Ford/Lincoln (14%), Honda/Acura (10%), with luxury representing 35% (up from 30% a year ago following portfolio optimization). The geographic footprint spans 15 states with concentrations in Massachusetts (Herb Chambers acquisition, July 2025), Maryland/Virginia (Koons, acquired December 2023), Florida, Texas, and Georgia.

**Strategic priorities include:** (1) completing Tekion DMS migration by fall 2026, (2) rolling TCA across all platforms including Herb Chambers, (3) aggressive share buybacks funded partly by divestiture proceeds, and (4) shifting the brand mix toward luxury/import through a buy-and-divest portfolio strategy.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Revenue grew from $14.6B (2022) to $18.0B (2025), entirely through acquisitions. Same-store revenue declined 9% in Q1 2026. Q1 revenue of $4.1B annualizes to approximately $16.5B, suggesting organic contraction.

**Profitability deterioration is the dominant financial trend.** Net income declined from $997M (2022) to $492M (2025) despite revenue growing 23% over that period. Adjusted EBITDA fell from $1.34B to $1.01B. The primary driver is new vehicle GPU compression:

| Period | Same-Store New Vehicle GPU | YoY Change |
|--------|---------------------------|------------|
| FY 2024 | $3,734 | — |
| FY 2025 | $3,342 | -10.5% |
| Q1 2026 | $3,061 | -18.0% vs Q1 2025 |

Domestic brand GPU is declining fastest (-17% in FY2025, another -16% in Q1 2026), with imports (-14% then -19%) not far behind. This is structural normalization from pandemic-era pricing power, compounded by rising inventories (days supply rose from 44 to 55).

**Positive offsets exist but are insufficient to arrest the decline:**
- Parts & service: 48% of gross profit, ~58% margins, growing 5-10% same-store
- Used vehicle GPU: rose from $1,517 (2024) to $1,847 (Q1 2026), +22% in 18 months
- F&I per vehicle retailed: stable at $2,200-$2,300
- FCF: $589M in FY2025 (15.5% FCF yield at current market cap)

**Balance sheet:** Total long-term debt of $3.57B plus $2.0B floor plan, against $3.89B equity. Net leverage at 3.2x sits at the upper bound of the company's 2.5-3.5x target. The Altman Z-Score of 2.45 places ABG in the "grey zone." Significant debt maturities include $525M (2028) and $600M (2030). Recurring goodwill/franchise impairments ($117-150M annually) signal that portions of the acquired portfolio persistently underperform acquisition prices.

**Capital allocation:** Management is aggressively buying back stock — 678K shares ($147M) in Q1 2026 alone versus 433K shares ($100M) for all of FY2025. At current prices, the remaining $453M authorization could retire ~12% of shares outstanding. This partially offsets EPS pressure from margin compression.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is negative. Same-store revenue declined 9% in Q1 2026, with same-store new vehicle units down 11% and used down 12%. Domestic units fell 18%. Consensus estimates reflect this deterioration:

| Horizon | Current EPS Est | 90-Day Prior | Change |
|---------|----------------|--------------|--------|
| FY 2026 | $25.85 | $27.23 | -5.1% |
| FY 2027 | $29.49 | $31.59 | -6.7% |

The 30-day revision trend for the current quarter is -4.1%, suggesting analysts haven't finished cutting. Growth catalysts are limited and execution-dependent:

1. **Tekion completion (fall 2026):** Cloud-based DMS should drive long-term efficiency, but the transition is currently a $8M/quarter cost headwind with productivity losses at converted stores.
2. **TCA full rollout:** Once deployed across Herb Chambers (targeted 2026), this converts third-party F&I commissions to captive recurring revenue. However, TCA segment revenue actually declined 24% in 2025, raising execution questions.
3. **Buyback math:** At current pace ($600M/year), EPS could benefit 6-8% annually from share count reduction alone.
4. **Parts & service secular growth:** Vehicle complexity, aging fleet, and recall volumes provide mid-single-digit organic growth in the highest-margin segment.

Against these, the primary growth headwind is structural: the industry is normalizing from a period when $0 discounts, limited inventory, and demand surge created abnormally high new vehicle margins. The return toward historical 4-5% new vehicle margins (currently ~5.9% at ABG, down from 7.3%) has further to run.

The stock is cheap by any absolute valuation metric. The question is whether this reflects a value trap (continued margin normalization eroding earnings toward $20-22/share) or a mispricing of the stable cash flows from parts & service plus aggressive buyback support. At 7x a potentially trough-adjacent earnings level with a $589M FCF base, the downside appears limited — but the catalyst for re-rating is unclear.

Analyst consensus target is $236 (mean $235.67, median $230), implying 15-18% upside. The range of $212-$312 is wide, reflecting uncertainty. The low target of $212 is only 6% above current price, suggesting limited downside in the base case but also that ABG could trade sideways for an extended period.

## Competitive Landscape

ABG occupies a defensible position within franchised auto retail, with two structural advantages: (1) TCA as a captive F&I provider (shared only with Lithia among public peers), creating recurring revenue and higher attachment rates, and (2) over-indexing to Toyota/Lexus (30% of revenue) — the highest-demand, most resilient brand franchise in the US market.

The luxury mix shift (30% → 35% of new vehicle revenue) through portfolio optimization is strategically sound — luxury margins are more durable, and luxury buyers are less rate-sensitive. However, 25% tariffs disproportionately impact luxury imports (BMW, Mercedes, Land Rover), partially offsetting this advantage.

Parts & service is the competitive moat. At 48% of gross profit with 58% margins, this segment is inherently recurring (vehicles require maintenance regardless of new vehicle demand) and benefits from vehicle complexity (more systems = higher service ticket). ABG's scale advantage creates procurement leverage on parts and labor efficiency.

Direct-to-consumer threats (Tesla, Rivian) have not meaningfully impacted franchised dealers to date. Franchise protection laws remain intact in most states, and the service-heavy model creates customer relationships that pure direct sellers lack.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff exposure (CRITICAL):** 25% tariffs on imported automobiles directly impact 40% of ABG's new vehicle revenue. Secondary effects compound: parts supply chain disruption affecting service operations, price increases suppressing demand, and potential further margin compression if dealers absorb costs to maintain volume.

**Margin normalization trajectory:** Q1 2026 shows no signs of bottoming. Domestic GPU declining 16% YoY in a quarter when inventories rose from 44 to 55 days supply suggests further pressure as OEMs push volume incentives. Historical mid-cycle new vehicle margins of 4-5% vs. current 5.9% implies another 15-20% GPU decline is possible.

**Leverage at cycle turning point:** 3.2x net leverage leaves limited flexibility if earnings decline further. Each 100bps in margin compression on $18B revenue = ~$180M EBITDA impact, pushing leverage toward 4.0x+ and potentially triggering covenant pressure on the $1.75B revolving credit facility.

**Tekion execution risk:** IT transformations of this magnitude (enterprise-wide DMS replacement) carry meaningful operational risk. Management disclosed "learning curve" productivity losses in Q1 2026 with $8M in direct costs. Full conversion by fall 2026 is ambitious given only 38 of 171 stores migrated by year-end 2025.

**Interest rate sensitivity:** With $3.57B in long-term debt plus $2.0B floor plan, a 100bps rate increase adds ~$26M in annual interest expense (on variable-rate portion). Refinancing upcoming maturities may cost more than existing rates.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($265-290):**
- Tariff relief (tariffs rolled back or exempted for autos) → demand recovery
- Tekion transition completes on time, driving $40-60M in annual SG&A savings
- Parts & service continues 5-10% organic growth, offsetting new vehicle margin normalization
- Aggressive buybacks (12% of float remaining in authorization) provide EPS support
- FY2027 EPS reaches $29-30 on stabilized margins + buyback benefit → 9x multiple = $265
- 10x re-rating if tariff risk resolves = $290

**Bear Case ($155-170):**
- Tariffs fully implemented, 40% import revenue faces demand destruction
- New vehicle GPU normalizes to pre-COVID levels (another 15-20% decline)
- FY2026 EPS falls to $22-23, FY2027 to $24-25 (vs. current $29.49 estimate)
- Leverage climbs toward 4.0x, constraining buybacks and increasing interest costs

**Base Case ($210-225):**
- Tariffs remain but demand impact is moderate (some models exempt, pricing absorbed partially)
- New vehicle GPU declines another 5-10% before stabilizing in H2 2026
- FY2026 EPS approximately $24-26, FY2027 $27-29
- Buybacks partially offset margin headwinds
- 8.0x forward earnings on $27-28 = $216-224

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of stable or improving same-store new vehicle GPU
2. Tariff resolution or specific auto exemption that removes the 40% import revenue overhang
3. Stock below $175 (6.0x forward earnings) where the FCF yield exceeds 20% and downside protection from asset value becomes dominant

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS estimate falls below $23 (implying continued acceleration in margin compression)
2. Net leverage exceeds 3.8x for two consecutive quarters (signal of deteriorating coverage)
3. Same-store parts & service revenue growth turns negative (breach of the profit stability thesis)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) will reveal whether Q1's -11% same-store unit decline was weather/one-time or represents a demand shift. Also monitor tariff policy developments through May-June 2026.

## Conclusion

ABG is objectively cheap at 7x forward earnings with strong FCF generation and insider buying near current levels. The stock has sold off 27% from its 52-week high, and the market may be overweighting near-term headwinds. However, the fundamental trajectory is negative: estimates are falling, margins are compressing, volume is declining, and tariff risk represents a material overhang on 40% of new vehicle revenue. Elevated leverage (3.2x) at what may be the early stages of a cyclical downturn creates asymmetric risk that the valuation discount does not fully compensate.

The Parts & Service segment and the buyback program provide a floor, but they are insufficient to reverse the earnings trajectory alone. Waiting for evidence of GPU stabilization or tariff resolution before committing capital is the prudent approach. HOLD with a bias toward buying on further weakness below $175 or on clear evidence of margin bottoming.
