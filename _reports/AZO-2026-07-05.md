**Research Date: July 5, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AutoZone is the leading retailer and distributor of automotive replacement parts in the Americas, operating 7,856 stores and running one of the most disciplined capital-return engines in large-cap retail. The core investment question today is whether the ~28% drawdown from the September 2025 high of $4,388 to the current $3,159 is a fundamental break or a valuation reset on a business that is, by its own operating metrics, re-accelerating. The evidence points to the latter: Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended May 9, 2026) delivered net sales of $4.84B (+8.4%, the fastest growth since Q2 FY2023), EPS of $38.07 (+7.7%, a ~3.9% beat), and domestic commercial growth of +10.4%. The stock fell because sales slightly missed the top line on a weather-driven mid-quarter slowdown and management flagged a ~$30M Q4 LIFO charge, a reaction disproportionate to the fundamental information.

AutoZone's Altman Z-Score of 1.52 and negative stockholders' equity of –$2.78B are manufactured by 25+ years of buybacks ($39.8B repurchased, 156M shares retired), not financial distress. Real credit metrics are healthy: 2.5x adjusted debt/EBITDAR, 5.1x interest coverage, investment-grade ratings, and ~36% adjusted ROIC even mid-investment-cycle. Reported gross-margin "declines" (–142bps YTD) are almost entirely non-cash LIFO charges tied to tariff-driven cost inflation; underlying merchandise margin is stable-to-improving, and LIFO inverts to a tailwind as tariffs normalize or refunds arrive.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is disciplined rather than skeptical. The valuation has compressed to ~18x forward EPS against a 29x peer median, which is genuinely attractive for a durable compounder, and a director bought stock in the open market days after the selloff. But estimate revisions are only modestly positive (+1.7% current-year over 90 days, well short of the velocity that would flag a systematic underestimation), the commercial mix-shift dilutes blended margins as it grows, the tariff/refund situation is a genuine two-sided wildcard, and the Consumer Discretionary sector carries a structurally negative alpha profile that raises the bar for any single-name BUY. The company is worth holding for a 12-month appreciation thesis, with upgrade contingent on a clean comp re-acceleration and confirmation that ROIC re-inflects.

## Company Overview

AutoZone, founded in 1979 and headquartered in Memphis, sells automotive replacement parts and accessories through 7,856 stores: 6,766 in the U.S., 933 in Mexico, and 157 in Brazil. It reports as a single operating segment and serves two customer bases:

- **DIY (retail):** Walk-in consumers, still the majority of sales.
- **DIFM/Commercial:** Delivery of parts and commercial credit to garages, dealers, service stations, and fleets, now operating in 6,356 domestic stores and representing ~31.7% of domestic sales.

Ancillary assets include ALLDATA (diagnostic and shop-management software) and the Duralast private-label brand, a key margin and differentiation lever. AutoZone does not perform installation, positioning it purely as a parts seller.

The structural demand drivers management tracks are miles driven (+1.1% TTM) and average vehicle age (12.8 years, a record; ~43% of U.S. vehicles are 7+ years old). These support a slow, durable, non-cyclical demand base. Downturns can help DIY as consumers defer new-vehicle purchases and repair older cars.

*FY2024 contained a 53rd week (~$366M sales, $87M op profit), inflating the base and worsening FY2025 optics.

FY2025 was a soft year, but FY2026 is re-accelerating. Q3 FY2026 delivered operating profit +6.6%, net income +5.4%. YTD 36-week sales rose +8.3% to $13.74B. The comp cadence within the quarter softened — domestic comps landed at 4.1%, below expectations — attributable to unseasonably cool weather (parts fail more frequently in temperature extremes).

**Margins and the LIFO distortion.** Reported gross margin was 52.2% in Q3 (–77bps) and 51.9% YTD, and the decline is entirely LIFO. The LIFO reserve tripled from $83M (Aug 2025) to $260M (May 2026), a direct read-through of tariff-driven cost inflation flowing through the P&L. On a pre-LIFO basis, underlying merchandise margin is improving. When tariff inflation normalizes or reverses (via refunds), the LIFO charge inverts to a tailwind, and the current optical margin pressure unwinds. Operating margin holds ~19%, net margin ~13%.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow runs ~$3.1B/yr; FY2025 FCF was ~$1.79B. Since 1998, AutoZone has repurchased 156.0M shares for $39.8B, shrinking the count from ~22.8M (FY2021) to ~16.3M today — a ~28% reduction in five years and the primary EPS growth engine. Buybacks moderated in FY2025 ($1.5B vs. $3.2B FY2024) as management prioritized record capex, then reaccelerated in FY2026 ($1.3B YTD, plus ~$174M through June 5). The board added $1.5B of authorization in October 2025; ~$0.8B remained at quarter-end. Capex is rising sharply ($797M FY2023 → $1.1B FY2024 → $1.33B FY2025), funding accelerated store growth (304 net new in FY2025, ~365 planned FY2026), hubs/mega-hubs, and new distribution centers.

**Balance sheet.** The Altman Z-Score and negative equity are entirely a function of aggressive buybacks, a deliberate capital-structure choice common among high-ROIC compounders (Home Depot, McDonald's). The relevant credit metrics are healthy: adjusted debt/EBITDAR 2.5x (management's IG-holding target), interest coverage 5.1:1 (covenant ≥2.5:1), total debt ~$9.0B. The current ratio of 0.88 reflects a negative-working-capital retail model funded by supplier terms: AP/inventory of 114%, with $5.6B of AP under supplier-financing (reverse-factoring) arrangements. Adjusted after-tax ROIC of ~36% (TTM) is exceptional, though down from 55% (FY2023) as the capex cycle temporarily inflates the invested-capital denominator ahead of the revenue new assets will produce.

The financial-health score of 6.5 reflects genuinely strong cash generation, disciplined leverage against a stated IG target, and best-in-class returns, offset by the reverse-factoring dependency on credit ratings and the temporary ROIC compression during the investment cycle. On this downside-filter dimension, AutoZone clears the bar for avoiding losers.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three legs. First, the commercial engine: domestic DIFM grew +10.4% in Q3 and +11.6% YTD, versus domestic same-store of ~4.1%. This validates the multi-year mega-hub strategy (deep local inventory plus fast delivery aimed at closing the parts-availability gap that drives commercial share) and suggests AutoZone is taking share, likely from a weakened Advance Auto Parts. Second, unit growth: planned FY2026 openings against a rising capex base set up multi-year sales layering as new stores and DCs mature. Third, the structural demand base: record average vehicle age and steady miles driven support durable, low-cyclicality volume.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but do not reach the velocity that flags systematic underestimation. Current-year consensus stands at $151.11 (+1.6% 30d, +1.7% 90d); next year at $175.49 (+0.6% 90d). These are constructive but well below the +30% threshold that would independently support a high-conviction growth call. The earnings-surprise record is mixed — two beats and two misses over four quarters (Q3 +3.9%, Q2 +0.8%, Q1 –5.5%, Q4 FY2025 –5.1%) — which tempers confidence in near-term consensus and argues for treating estimates as a base case rather than a floor.

Growth prospects score 6.5. The commercial share-gain story is real and the demand base is durable, but blended-margin dilution from mix-shift, the mid-quarter comp softening, and revision velocity below the inflection threshold cap the score. This dimension is the most reliable winner-selector in the framework, and AutoZone earns a solidly positive but not exceptional mark.

## Valuation Assessment

At $3,159, AutoZone trades at ~21x trailing EPS and ~18x forward on the next-year consensus. The peer median in Automotive Retail is 29.3x (ORLY 29.3, CVNA 39.6, KMX 30.7, MUSA 21.5, PAG 13.1). The dispersion is wide, but the most relevant comparable — O'Reilly, which runs a nearly identical share-gaining, buyback-driven, commercial-focused model — trades at 29.3x. AutoZone at ~18x forward against ORLY at 29x is a meaningful discount for a business of comparable quality and superior long-run capital-return discipline (353% 5-year TSR vs. 152% peer group).

The valuation case rests on three supports. First, the multiple has genuinely compressed: the drawdown was a valuation reset, not a fundamental break, given the EPS beat and fastest sales growth in three years. Second, reported margins understate operational health because the LIFO drag is non-cash and reversible; on normalized (pre-LIFO) earnings power the stock is cheaper than the headline multiple implies. Third, there is an unbooked tariff-refund optionality — following the Supreme Court's February 2026 IEEPA invalidation, AutoZone filed for refunds of tariffs already paid but has conservatively booked $0 benefit.

I resist reading the compressed multiple as pure opportunity, however. The lessons of the current regime are that cheapness has been anti-predictive as a standalone signal, and that a low multiple requires an independent catalyst before it earns a high score. AutoZone has one (commercial share gains plus LIFO reversal), but the catalyst is gradual rather than dated, and the drawdown means sophisticated sellers acted on something — most plausibly the deceleration in the comp trend and the recognition that FY2025 was structurally soft. The valuation score of 6.8 reflects a real discount to a high-quality peer with identifiable normalization catalysts, discounted for the absence of a hard, near-term re-rating trigger. Analyst targets (low $3,200, high $4,800, median $3,989) place the current price just below the low end of the target range, which on a name with positive-but-modest revisions is supportive but not a floor guarantee.

## Competitive Landscape

AutoZone operates in a rational oligopoly: AutoZone, O'Reilly, and Advance Auto Parts on retail, plus dealers, jobbers, and independents on commercial. The moat rests on a dense store/hub/mega-hub network that delivers parts availability (the #1 purchase driver), the Duralast private label, and enormous purchasing/supplier-financing scale. The competitive trajectory is improving: double-digit commercial growth indicates share capture in the DIFM battleground where O'Reilly has historically led and Advance Auto has struggled operationally. AutoZone's mega-hub investment is aimed at closing the availability gap that drives commercial wins.

The long-run overhang is EV penetration and improved parts durability reducing failure frequency, but the transition is slow and the ICE installed base ensures a decades-long runway. The near-term structural headwind is that commercial carries lower gross margins than DIY, so the mix-shift driving growth dilutes blended margins — offset by higher asset turns and incremental volume. Competitive position scores 7.0: an improving trajectory with demonstrated share gains, weighed against the recent-window observation that competitive-position scores alone have been anti-predictive, so I confirm the mark against the growth and financial-health evidence rather than lean on it in isolation.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Tariffs and trade policy (lead strategic risk).** AutoZone directly imports ~13% of purchases, with additional indirect exposure via domestic vendors. The February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA invalidation created a two-sided wildcard: refund optionality (unbooked) against re-imposed tariffs under other authority. Net exposure remains genuinely uncertain.

2. **Weather and consumer sensitivity.** Demand is sensitive to miles driven, fuel prices, and deferred maintenance in downturns. Within the broader consumer backdrop, mid-market discretionary spending is decelerating, though auto parts skew defensive.

3. **Commercial competition and margin mix.** Continued growth requires taking share from entrenched competitors, and success dilutes blended margin.

4. **Credit-rating dependency.** The entire model — cheap debt for buybacks plus $5.6B reverse-factoring — hinges on investment-grade ratings. A downgrade would raise borrowing costs and could unwind supplier financing.

5. **ERP transition.** A multi-year system replacement began going live in Q2 FY2026; execution and internal-control risk is flagged in the 10-Q.

6. **FX translation.** International comps swing on the peso (+16.1% FY2024 → –3.2% FY2025 → +15.0% YTD FY2026); constant-currency remains solidly positive (~+2.6% YTD).

Risk factors score 6.0. Most sector risks (cyclicality, EV secular decline) are permanently priced and slow-moving. The idiosyncratic items that could move the stock and are not fully priced are the tariff/refund two-sidedness and the ERP go-live. Neither is a binary near-term catalyst, so the score sits in the middle rather than the low end.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is constructive-to-neutral and does not challenge the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 28.9% sits essentially at 1-year historical vol of 27.5% (spread +1.4%, "fair"), with IV in the 2nd percentile of its current chain — the market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty, and the recent IV compression from 36.1% (late May, post-earnings) to 28.9% signals resolved event risk. Put/call skew is –3.6% (call-heavy), and the put/call volume ratio of 0.93 is balanced. The elevated put/call OI ratio of 1.64 reflects longer-dated hedging rather than acute bearishness. The term structure is flat (28.9% near vs. 30.7% far), consistent with evenly distributed, non-acute uncertainty. Net read: the options market corroborates a stabilizing, non-distressed setup after the May drawdown.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($4,200–4,800):** Commercial share gains sustain double-digit growth, comps normalize as weather headwinds pass, LIFO reverses to a tailwind as tariff inflation abates and refunds arrive, and the multiple re-rates toward the ORLY-comparable 29x. Buybacks continue compounding EPS on a shrinking share base. On normalized earnings power and a re-rating, the stock recovers toward and beyond its prior high.

**Bear case ($2,600–2,800):** Comp deceleration continues, the broader consumer cliff materializes and depresses even defensive auto-parts demand, re-imposed tariffs keep cost inflation elevated with no refund benefit, and blended-margin dilution from commercial mix combines with the capex cycle to keep ROIC and margins depressed. A credit-rating scare would compound. The multiple stays compressed at ~16–17x on flat-to-declining EPS.

The base case sits between these: gradual comp normalization, continued commercial-led growth in the high-single-digits, LIFO drag persisting into FY2026 before easing, and a modest multiple recovery toward ~20x. AutoZone is a high-quality compounder caught in a temporary weather/tariff-optics air pocket, trading at a genuine discount to its closest peer, but without the estimate-revision velocity or dated catalyst that would justify a high-conviction upgrade.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive 12-month growth bias (growth_hold).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Domestic comps re-accelerate above ~5% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the weather-driven Q3 slowdown was transient.
- Current-year or next-year EPS estimate revisions accelerate above +5% over 90 days (toward the systematic-underestimation threshold), or a material tariff-refund benefit is recognized.
- Adjusted ROIC re-inflects off the ~36% TTM trough as new stores/DCs mature, confirming the investment cycle is converting to returns.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Domestic comps turn negative or commercial growth decelerates below ~5%, signaling share-gain stall or demand deterioration.
- A credit-rating downgrade or supplier-financing disruption that raises the cost of the buyback-and-reverse-factoring model.
- Evidence the ERP transition is impairing operations or internal controls.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q4 FY2026 report (late September 2026), which will show whether the comp trend normalized and how the guided ~$30M Q4 LIFO charge and any tariff-refund resolution flow through. Watch the tariff-refund docket and any credit-rating commentary in the interim.

## Conclusion

AutoZone is a durable, share-gaining compounder whose operating fundamentals re-accelerated in Q3 FY2026 even as the stock fell ~28% from its September high on a weather-driven comp softening and reversible tariff/LIFO margin optics. The balance-sheet "distress" flags are false positives manufactured by 25 years of disciplined buybacks, and the ~18x forward multiple is a real discount to the 29x commanded by its closest peer, O'Reilly. The case for owning it is sound.

The case against upgrading to BUY today is disciplined: estimate revisions are positive but modest, the mixed 2-of-4 beat record tempers confidence in consensus, the tariff/refund wildcard is genuinely two-sided, blended-margin dilution accompanies the commercial growth, and the Consumer Discretionary sector carries a structurally negative alpha profile that raises the bar for a single-name BUY. Improving risk/reward on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals argues against lowering the score further despite the price decline, and the setup is closer to a buy than an avoid. Rating: HOLD, growth-hold rationale, with a clear path to upgrade on comp re-acceleration and ROIC re-inflection.
