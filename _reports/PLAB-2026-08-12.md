## Executive Summary

Photronics is a merchant photomask manufacturer whose reported financials have been stable at the revenue line and steadily deteriorating everywhere else. Revenue has sat in an $825-890M band for four consecutive fiscal years. Over the same window, gross margin fell from 37.7% (FY23) to 31.3% (Q2 FY26) and operating margin from 28.4% to 20.1%, with Q3 FY26 guided to 18-20%. That is roughly 1,000 basis points of operating-margin erosion with no revenue decline to explain it, translating to a one-third reduction in the quarterly operating-profit run rate. Asked directly what could be done if softness persists, the CFO answered that there are "very little levers we can pull… Most of our cost is fixed." That statement anchors the downside case.

The demand mechanism explains why the AI capital-expenditure cycle has not helped. Photomask consumption tracks semiconductor design releases (tape-outs), not wafer starts — a point both the MD&A and management's prepared remarks make explicitly. Management attributed the Q2 FY26 shortfall to fabs running at utilization rates so high they cannot accommodate additional design releases, OEMs prioritizing existing products over new ones, and memory price surges delaying consumer-electronics launches. A cycle concentrated in a handful of very-high-volume leading-edge designs, at nodes where Photronics does not compete (its "high-end" is 28nm and below; its most advanced U.S. site is qualified at 7nm; the 10-Q defines state of the art as 4-5nm and EUV), generates little mask demand while crowding out the mainstream tape-outs that produce $376M of annual revenue. The company's own proxy pay-versus-performance table prices this: a PLAB TSR index of 245 against a peer index of 618 for FY2025, after tracking peers in line through FY2023.

We are moving the rating from AVOID to REDUCE, a modest upgrade on the scale rather than a change of view, and lowering the composite score marginally to 4.4 from 4.5. The upgrade reflects that the tail risks do not threaten solvency: cash and short-term investments of $637.7M against $3.9M of debt, a current ratio of 5.37 and an Altman Z of roughly 5.3 leave no distress question. The score decline reflects the only material change since the last review: the stock is 15% higher at $33.30 versus $28.86 with no confirming fundamental data, and the shares now sit inside a September window containing both the Q3 FY26 report (guided to a third consecutive quarter of margin compression, from a management team that has produced double-digit single-day declines on fiscal-Q2 guidance in each of the last two years) and the September 4 lead-plaintiff deadline in *Cooper v. Photronics*. Base/bull/bear of $29/$42/$21 probability-weights to roughly $30, about 9% below spot.

## Company Overview

Photronics manufactures photomasks — quartz or glass plates carrying microscopic circuit images used as the master template for transferring patterns onto silicon wafers and display substrates. It is one of the largest independent (merchant) suppliers, competing both against other merchants and against the captive in-house mask shops of large integrated device manufacturers and foundries. Eleven manufacturing facilities: Taiwan (3), China (2), South Korea (1), United States (3), Europe (2), with roughly 1,908 employees and one reported operating segment.

Two end markets. Integrated circuits were 70% of Q2 FY26 revenue ($147.5M of $209.9M), split between "high-end" (28nm and below, $56.7M) and "mainstream" (above 28nm, $90.9M). Flat panel display was 30% ($62.4M), split between high-end (G10.5+, AMOLED, LTPS; $52.8M) and mainstream ($9.6M). FY25 revenue by origin: Taiwan $283.8M, China $221.0M, South Korea $158.5M, United States $148.9M, Europe $34.2M. Non-U.S. revenue was 82% of the total.

The corporate structure materially affects every per-share calculation. Photronics consolidates two joint ventures — PDMC in Taiwan and PDMCX in Xiamen, China, the latter 49.99%-held by Dai Nippon Printing — at 100% of revenue and profit while owning 50.01% of each. In FY2025, consolidated net income of $190.2M produced $53.8M (28.3%) attributable to noncontrolling interests and $136.4M to Photronics shareholders. Noncontrolling interests carry $452.2M of the $1,693.5M of total equity.

Backlog is structurally minimal — one to two weeks for IC, two to three weeks for FPD. Substantially all revenue is recognized over time using a cost-input method, and the company elects the ASC 606 practical expedient not to disclose remaining performance obligations. Contract liabilities of $25.6M at May 3, 2026 are not backlog and are not described as such. There is no order-book metric for this business, which constrains both management's forecasting and an outside analyst's visibility.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY22 $824.5M, FY23 $892.1M (+8.2%), FY24 $866.9M (−2.8%), FY25 $849.3M (−2.0%), 1H FY26 $435.0M (+2.8%). Q3 FY26 guidance of $207-215M against a derived Q3 FY25 of $210.4M is essentially flat year over year. If Q4 FY26 matches Q4 FY25's $215.8M, FY26 lands near $861M — a fourth straight year inside the same band. That is a scenario built from the guidance midpoint, not company guidance.

**Margins.** This is the operative issue.

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | 1H FY26 | Q2 FY26 | Q3 FY26 guide |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 37.7% | 36.4% | 35.3% | 33.2% | 31.3% | — |
| Operating margin | 28.4% | 25.6% | 24.5% | 22.3% | 20.1% | 18-20% |

Operating income was $253.1M in FY23 and $208.2M in FY25; the Q2 FY26 quarterly figure of $42.2M annualizes to roughly $169M. The annualization is a sensitivity — Q1 is seasonally strongest ahead of Chinese New Year and Q4 typically firms — but the direction is unambiguous. Management attributes compression to higher labor, benefits, material and manufacturing costs, plus negative operating leverage on a fixed base and product mix. Two internal cost lines are moving in opposite directions: SG&A rose to 9.9% of revenue in Q2 FY26 from 8.6% a year earlier, while R&D fell 35% to $5.4M in 1H FY26 from $8.3M, now running near 1.2% of revenue against 1.9% in FY25. For a company whose strategy is migrating down technology nodes, a one-third R&D cut is a poor trade.

**Earnings quality.** GAAP results are dominated by non-operating currency translation of intercompany and cross-currency balances (New Taiwan dollar, Korean won). Q2 FY25 carried a $31.1M FX loss (GAAP EPS $0.15 vs non-GAAP $0.40); Q1 FY26 a $12.9M gain ($0.74 vs $0.61); Q2 FY26 a $7.9M gain ($0.54 vs $0.42). A 10% adverse move in non-functional currencies would have produced a $66.8M net unrealized pre-tax loss at May 3, 2026. Separately, FY25 GAAP EPS of $2.28 included roughly $0.28 per diluted share from a $16.751M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release that cut the FY25 effective rate to 14.2% from 25.7%; FY26 is running at 19.4%. Adjusted for FX only, non-GAAP diluted EPS has been flat at $2.04 (FY23), $2.05 (FY24), $2.01 (FY25), with 1H FY26 at $1.03. One point in the company's favor: the non-GAAP definition strips out only FX and its tax and NCI effects — no stock compensation, restructuring or amortization add-backs. That is more conservative than most semiconductor peers.

**Balance sheet.** Cash of $511.5M plus short-term investments of $126.2M equals $637.7M against total debt of $3.864M (a single embedded finance lease on an FPD lithography tool component). Total liabilities of $237.4M against total assets of $1,930.8M. Current ratio 5.37, debt/equity 0.18, FY25 ROE 16.2%, ROA 10.5%. Recomputed Altman Z from FY25 inputs lands near 5.3 using retained earnings in the second term, higher with market equity in the fourth. Solvency is not a question here.

The cash geography is. On the May 28 call, the CFO stated that $477M of the $637.7M sits inside the joint ventures Photronics owns at 50.01%. A defensible attributable net-cash figure is therefore $637.7M − $477.0M + $238.6M (50.01% of the JV-held balance) − $3.9M ≈ $395M, or 20.4% of market capitalization, versus the headline $633.8M (32.7%). The Q2 FY26 10-Q added new language asserting that Photronics controls the JV boards, can approve distributions through ordinary governance, cannot be blocked by minority shareholders, and that Chinese SAFE requirements affect timing and net proceeds rather than access. Taken at face value, that resolves *access*. It does not change *ownership*: 49.99% of every distributed dollar belongs to the minority partners. The addition of that paragraph reads as a response to investor scrutiny of trapped cash; a routine disclosure enhancement following auditor or comment-letter input is an equally consistent explanation.

**Cash flow and the capital-expenditure step-up.** This is the largest change in the story.

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | 1H FY26 | FY26 guide |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $302.2M | $261.4M | $247.8M | $144.3M | — |
| Capex payments | $131.3M | $130.9M | $188.1M | $93.4M | ~$330M |
| Free cash flow | $170.9M | $130.5M | $59.7M | $50.8M | — |

Operating cash flow has fallen three consecutive years while capex has risen. Management reaffirmed ~$330M of FY26 capex payments on May 28 — a 75% step-up on FY25 and 2.5x FY23-24, equal to roughly 38% of implied FY26 revenue. If full-year operating cash flow lands in the $250-280M range consistent with FY24-25, FY26 free cash flow runs roughly −$50M to −$80M. That is a scenario with stated inputs, and it is financeable given the cash position; it is not existential.

There is internal tension in the $330M figure worth checking against the Q3 10-Q. Reaching it requires ~$236.6M of payments in 2H FY26, yet the 10-Q discloses $172.0M of capital commitments plus $39.3M of accrued capital-equipment liabilities and states the company will fund $199.0M of its $211.3M of committed and recognized obligations *over the next twelve months* — a window extending seven months past fiscal year-end. Either substantial not-yet-placed orders get paid inside two quarters, or FY26 capex lands below guidance and slips into FY27. The latter would improve FY26 free cash flow.

**The depreciation sequencing problem.** Depreciation has not yet inflected — $39.7M in 1H FY26 versus $40.2M in 1H FY25 — while construction in progress rose to $147.5M. Machinery and equipment of $2,182.5M gross against $1,656.5M of accumulated depreciation means much of the existing base is largely written down. If FY25 and FY26 capex of roughly $518M enters service over 7-10 year lives, incremental annual depreciation of approximately $52-74M arrives in FY27-28 against a current base near $79M. Management guides initial Allen, Texas revenue "late in fiscal 2026" with meaningful contribution "in 2027 and beyond," and Korea initial revenue "by the end of fiscal 2027." The depreciation therefore lands before the revenue, adding a mechanical second leg to margin compression independent of demand.

**Capital allocation and share count.** Photronics repurchased 5.0M shares for $97.4M in FY2025 at an average of $19.52 — good execution in hindsight. Buybacks then stopped entirely: zero in Q1 or Q2 FY26 with $27.6M of authorization remaining, while share count rose from 57,633K to 58,152K on equity-plan issuance. Not buying in the $40s-$50s was correct. It also removed the only mechanical bid under the stock. Not-yet-issued incremental awards (676,982 RSUs and 19,250 options at a $9.51 strike) total 696,232 shares, 1.20% of existing shares and 1.18% post-issuance dilution — immaterial. The 2025 plan authorizes up to 5.0M shares, which is capacity, not current dilution.

One data note: the supplied quote fields show forward P/E equal to trailing P/E at 11.67, which does not reconcile with $33.30 against FY25 GAAP EPS of $2.28 (14.6x) or against consensus. We use our own computed multiples throughout.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has moved sharply and then stabilized. Over 90 days, current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 22.4%, next-quarter 23.0%, current year 15.8% and next year 14.4%. Over 30 days, revisions are +2.1%, +0.8%, +0.8% and −0.2% — flat, which is stabilization at the reset level rather than positive momentum. Any characterization of these estimates as "broadly rising" reflects the 30-day window in isolation and misses the reset. Current-year consensus of $1.86 is 7.5% below FY25 non-GAAP of $2.01; next-year $2.02 merely returns to the FY25 level. Consensus embeds three years without earnings growth.

The earnings-surprise record broke in the most recent print: three consecutive beats of 11.8-31.7% followed by a 23.7% miss ($0.42 against $0.55). More relevant, the miss was against management's own non-GAAP guidance of $0.49-0.55 issued in February — a non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP shortfall of 19% at the midpoint. Two consecutive fiscal-Q2 reports have produced double-digit single-day declines on guidance disappointment (−36.4% on May 28, 2026; −13.2% on May 28, 2025). The plausible mechanism is systematic over-extrapolation of the pre-Chinese-New-Year Q1 build into Q2; the alternative, that one-to-three-week backlog makes precise quarterly guidance structurally impossible, argues for wider ranges rather than exonerating the process. Either way, forward guidance from this team warrants a discount.

Where growth could come from:

- **FPD is genuinely working.** Q2 FY26 FPD revenue rose 13.3% year over year to $62.4M, with high-end up 21.1% — described by management as one of the strongest display quarters in company history. A newly installed Korea mask writer entering G8.6 AMOLED production carries higher layer counts and ASPs, with wider adoption expected later in the calendar year. Management also noted that high-end consumer-electronics launch schedules for smartphones and smartwatches in Western markets remain on track despite memory pricing.
- **Capacity and node extension.** Allen, Texas is producing qualification masks with initial revenue targeted late FY26; the dual purpose is upgrading nodes Allen cannot currently serve while shifting high-end-of-mainstream work out of Boise so Boise can absorb higher-ASP orders. Boise is qualified at 7nm with stated intent to go below. Korea equipment installs begin later in FY26 to extend to 8nm and below, with initial revenue by end of FY2027.
- **Captive outsourcing and regionalization.** Management's stated strategy depends on captive mask shops choosing to outsource more, a decision outside its control that historically tracks captive utilization rather than merchant pricing.
- **Delayed tape-out recovery.** Lee noted "a lot of tape-out have happened since beginning of May." If fabs digest AI capacity and mainstream design activity resumes, mask demand follows with a lag.

Against that: IC high-end fell 4.5% year over year and 20.5% sequentially in Q2; IC mainstream, the largest line at $376.2M in FY25, fell 8.2% in FY25 and a further 2.9% in 1H FY26; Taiwan revenue fell 13.3% year over year to $65.0M; and related-party revenue fell 27% to $28.6M. The growth case rests on FPD plus two facility ramps whose revenue arrives in FY27-28 alongside their depreciation.

## Competitive Landscape

Photomask supply splits between captive shops inside large IDMs and foundries — which produce the bulk of leading-edge and EUV masks internally — and the merchant market. Photronics competes against Compugraphics, Dai Nippon Printing (outside Taiwan and China), Hoya, LG Innotek, SK-Electronics, Taiwan Mask Corporation, Tekscend Photomask, and the Chinese entrants Shenzhen Newway and Shenzhen Qingyi.

The Chinese competitors matter disproportionately because they compete in mainstream IC, Photronics' largest and weakest line. This is the most plausible structural explanation for persistent gross-margin erosion: price and cost pressure concentrated where Photronics has the most revenue exposure and where differentiation is thinnest. The FY25 10-K's own warning about "accelerated erosion of selling prices" during slowdowns supports the reading.

On trajectory — which matters more than level — the picture is mixed but net negative. Deteriorating: mainstream IC declining, high-end IC down sequentially and year over year, top-two customer concentration widening to 29% from 27% in each of the two prior years (Customer A 16%, B 13%, C 8%), R&D cut 35%, and a technology position one to two full generations behind the leading edge with no evident commercial EUV capability. Management's diversification narrative emphasizes geography and technology node; the geographic mix is indeed shifting, but that measures something different from counterparty concentration, which worsened by 200bp.

Improving: FPD high-end, where China and Korea are described as competitive strongholds and the G8.6 AMOLED transition raises layer counts and ASPs; U.S. onshore positioning as regionalization advances ("we like to be the main photomask supplier in the United States"); and Boise's 7nm qualification.

The counterparty relationship with DNP is unusual and worth flagging: DNP is simultaneously the 49.99% partner in PDMCX, a named competitor outside Taiwan and China, and most probably the unnamed related party generating $28.6M of Q2 FY26 revenue (13.6%) and $33.3M of receivables — an identification the 10-Q text does not confirm.

## Risk Assessment

**Wrong-side-of-the-cycle exposure (structural, partially priced).** Mask demand tracks design breadth, not wafer volume. Management's stated causes of the Q2 shortfall include elevated fab utilization preventing acceptance of new design releases, OEMs continuing production on current designs while delaying new ones, memory price surges delaying consumer launches, and geopolitical uncertainty — specifically the U.S.-Iran conflict cited by management. The linkage between the macro overhang and PLAB's order flow is real.

**Negative operating leverage with no levers (largely unpriced beyond FY26).** The 10-Q warns that lower revenue could produce excess capacity, materially affecting operating margins or causing impairment write-offs. With ~$928M of net PP&E and depreciation rising toward a possible $130-150M, an FY27 demand air pocket would compound.

**Securities litigation (unpriced; dated).** At least eight plaintiffs' firms have issued releases; the lead case is *Cooper v. Photronics*, No. 26-cv-01069 (D. Conn.), class period December 10, 2025 to May 27, 2026, lead-plaintiff deadline September 4, 2026. Allegations center on creating a false impression of reliable revenue visibility while minimizing post-holiday seasonality, and on undisclosed bottlenecks in the high-end design-release pipeline. These are allegations in a complaint, not established facts, and no company filing reviewed discloses them — Note 12 of the June 11 10-Q reports only ordinary-course claims. The Q3 FY26 10-Q is the natural venue for first disclosure. For a $1.94B company with $637.7M of cash, the financial magnitude is unlikely to be existential; the disclosure event and any associated management distraction are the near-term risk.

**Governance (unpriced).** Thirteen Item 5.02 8-Ks between January 2024 and April 2026, including three in January 2026. The CEO seat is ambiguous: the February 2026 proxy names Dr. Lee as FY25 principal executive officer; an April 8, 2026 Form 4 lists George Macricostas as "CEO"; the May 28 call listed Macricostas as Executive Chairman with no participant carrying the CEO title; the 10-Q is signed by Eric Rivera as "President, Chief Financial Officer." Combining President and CFO in one officer with no clearly identified sitting CEO, at a company executing its largest capital program in years, justifies a discount. Compensation metrics are all absolute (revenue, gross margin, operating income, net income) with none relative or return-based, and FY2025 PEO compensation actually paid of $5.5M was the highest in the five-year table while revenue and gross margin both declined.

**Insider selling (informational, now historical).** Seven insiders disposed of 361,144 shares for ~$15.70M between March 19 and April 17, 2026 at $35.80-$47.77, including the Executive Chairman (121,194 shares, 26.6% of his position, in a single day), the President/CFO (23.4%), the SVP/COO of IC operations (31.0%), the founder (to zero), a director and the corporate controller. Zero open-market purchases. Mitigating context is real: roughly 30,000 of Lee's shares came from same-day exercises of $8.60-$10.75 options nearing expiry (only 19,250 options remain outstanding); insiders sold well below the $53.51 peak; early April is a conventional post-earnings window; and the stock had roughly tripled from the $19.52 FY25 buyback average. Against that, Lee stated on the May 28 call that the post-Chinese-New-Year slowdown was apparent "in the end of February," before the selling began. Whether the sales executed under pre-existing Rule 10b5-1 plans is unresolved — the 10-Q states only that no plans were adopted or terminated during the quarter, which is a different question. We draw no inference about intent. The breadth is what matters for scoring management alignment: essentially the entire senior team plus the founder inside a 30-day window, with no offsetting purchases.

**DNP put/call on PDMCX (conditional).** If either party's ownership falls below 20.0% for more than six consecutive months, the exiting party may sell and the other may buy at the exiting party's share of net book value, closing within three business days of approvals. Each side held approximately $177.1M of net investment at May 3, 2026. Read literally, this is a conditional right triggered by a dilution event, not an at-will put, and it is not a balance-sheet liability. It nonetheless defines a potential ~$177M cash call, 9% of market capitalization, and the trigger is symmetric.

**Geographic concentration.** Two Chinese facilities ($221.0M FY25 revenue, $123.3M in 1H FY26) and three Taiwanese facilities ($283.8M) in a seismically active region, against a backdrop of the U.S. Section 232 semiconductor import investigation, export-administration rules that could bar non-U.S. sites from supplying restricted entities, and U.S.-China technology friction as flagged in the FY25 10-K risk factors (expropriation and repatriation constraints).

The Q2 FY26 10-Q states there were no material changes to the FY25 risk factors, which is striking given that a 36% share-price collapse and multiple class actions followed within weeks.

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 55.8% against 84.8% one-year realized (a −28.9pp spread, 9th percentile within the chain), while the September 18 tenor prices 83.8% with a ±21.6% implied move and the December tenor 80.2% with ±37.3%. For a holder trimming exposure, cheap near-dated volatility argues that protection is more efficient than it has been in months. The reading locates the risk in September rather than August.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $42 (+26% from $33.30)

Q2 FY26 was a mix accident inside a demand deferral rather than a permanent reset. High-end IC fell 20.5% sequentially; a normal high-end quarter recovers several hundred basis points of gross margin, and FY25's 35.3% is closer to through-cycle than Q2's 31.3%. Tape-outs resumed from early May, and as fabs digest AI capacity, deferred mainstream design releases arrive with a lag. FPD compounds independently: high-end display revenue up 21.1% year over year, the G8.6 AMOLED transition lifting layer counts and ASPs, and Korea and China positioned as strongholds. The $330M capex program buys 7nm-and-below capability, U.S. onshore share as regionalization advances, and a real option on captive outsourcing — with initial Allen revenue late FY26 and Korea by end-FY27. The balance sheet funds all of it: $637.7M of cash, $3.9M of debt, Altman Z above 5. If FY27 revenue reaches $950M at a 24% operating margin, attributable EPS is roughly $2.24; at 18x, $40, with three analysts already at $41-44. Resumption of the $27.6M remaining buyback authorization at $33 would be a credible signal that management views the shares as cheap.

### Bear Case — $21 (−37%)

Margin compression is structural, and management said as much: the cost base is fixed, there are few levers, and the pressure is concentrated in mainstream IC where Chinese merchants compete directly on price and where Photronics has $376M of exposure. Gross margin has fallen in each of the last four years. Depreciation from $518M of FY25-26 capex adds an estimated $52-74M of annual cost in FY27-28 — 6-9% of revenue — while Allen and Korea revenue is still ramping, mechanically compressing operating margin below the 18-20% Q3 guide even if demand holds. FY26 free cash flow runs roughly −$50M to −$80M, the buyback is suspended, and there is no dividend, so nothing supports the stock mechanically. Consensus already embeds three years of no earnings growth; the risk is that $2.02 for FY27 is still too high. Guidance credibility is poor after two consecutive fiscal-Q2 blowups. Governance is opaque, litigation is pending, and 28.3% of consolidated profit belongs to minority partners. At 15% attributable operating margin on $830M of revenue, attributable NOPAT is roughly $72M; at 10x plus $395M of attributable net cash, the equity is worth about $19, near the $19.81 fifty-two-week low.

### Base Case — $29 (−13%)

## Conclusion

Photronics is a solvent, cash-rich, structurally challenged business trading at roughly fair value on normalized earnings. The balance sheet is genuinely strong and precludes distress. The operating story is weak: four years of flat revenue, 640bp of gross-margin erosion, a CFO who states plainly that the cost base cannot flex, and a $330M capital program whose depreciation arrives one to two years ahead of its revenue. The headline valuation screen flatters the equity by crediting 100% of joint-venture cash and profit to shareholders who own 50.01% of it; corrected, the stock trades near 12-13x attributable operating profit and 17-18x forward non-GAAP earnings for a business consensus expects to earn no more in FY27 than it earned in FY23.

Photomask demand tracks the breadth of new chip designs, not the volume of wafers. A capital cycle concentrated in a small number of leading-edge accelerator and memory designs — at nodes Photronics does not serve — suppresses rather than stimulates its order book, because fabs running at capacity defer new tape-outs. Management said this explicitly. The company's own proxy shows the market has been pricing it since FY2024.

At $33.30, after a 15.7% rally on no new fundamental information, the distribution is unfavorable: roughly 13% downside to a $29 base case, 37% to a $21 bear case, against 26% upside if margins recover and the Allen and Korea ramps land on schedule. A September window containing both a report from a management team that has misjudged this specific quarter twice running and a securities-litigation milestone is the wrong time to be adding. **REDUCE.**
