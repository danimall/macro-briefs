**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Integer Holdings is one of the largest medical-device contract development and manufacturing organizations (CDMOs), supplying components, subassemblies, and finished devices to large-cap OEMs. The company's fortunes track OEM demand cycles and inventory behavior rather than end-patient demand, and roughly 49% of 2025 revenue came from three customers (Abbott, Boston Scientific, Medtronic). The business grew revenue at a mid-to-high-single-digit pace through 2025 ($1,854M, +8%), anchored by a 17% expansion in its Cardio & Vascular franchise. On a normalized operating basis, 2025 was a solid year: operating income rose ~6% and the headline net income decline to $103M from $120M was largely a one-time, non-cash $46.7M convertible-note inducement charge.

In Q1 2026, revenue was essentially flat (+0.5% to $439.6M) while gross margin fell to 24.9% from 27.5% and operating margin fell to 7.2% from 11.3%, a textbook fixed-cost-absorption problem from soft production volumes. Management attributes the volume shortfall to lower-than-anticipated market adoption of three new products simultaneously, a coincidence it calls "highly unusual." Inventory built from $254M to $284M in the quarter, consistent with demand running below plan. Consensus estimates for the current year have been revised down 6.3% over 90 days, and current-quarter estimates down 10.2%, confirming that the market is repricing a genuine execution or destocking stumble rather than a temporary blip.

Layered on top of the fundamental soft patch is a cluster of special-situation events: a formal Board strategic review announced in April 2026 (explicitly weighing sale, merger, or standalone), an activist cooperation agreement in March 2026, a securities class action filed in December 2025, and a CEO handover to Payman Khales in October 2025. At ~$97.50, the stock trades roughly halfway back from its October 2025 crash low of $62 toward its former $116 high, implying the market already discounts a reasonable probability of a takeout. The result is a binary, two-sided setup with no clean fundamental catalyst independent of the review outcome, which is why we rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

Integer, headquartered in Plano, Texas, operates as a pure-play medtech supplier under the Greatbatch Medical and Lake Region Medical brands. The business is organized into three product lines:

- **Cardio & Vascular (C&V):** $1,107M in 2025 (~60% of sales), +17% YoY. Covers interventional cardiology, structural heart, peripheral and neurovascular, electrophysiology, and vascular access. This is the growth engine, supported by new-product ramps and tuck-in coating acquisitions.
- **Cardiac Rhythm Management & Neuromodulation (CRM&N):** $669M, +1% YoY. Batteries, capacitors, filtered feedthroughs, leads, and finished IPG systems for pacemakers, ICDs, and neurostimulators. A mature franchise growing roughly at market, dragged by a planned wind-down of a legacy spinal-cord-stimulation IPG customer.
- **Other Markets:** $78M, −27% YoY, dominated by the deliberate exit from Portable Medical, expected to finish in 2026.

The company operates globally (US, Mexico, Ireland, Malaysia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, Uruguay), employs ~11,000, and holds 731 patents, though the 10-K notes no single patent is material. Backlog was ~$675M at year-end 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth has been steady:** $1,376M (2022) → $1,597M (2023) → $1,717M (2024) → $1,854M (2025). Growth reflects a blend of organic ramps and acquisitions (Precision Coating $153.5M, VSi Parylene $24M, Biocoat $15M in 2025).

**Full-year 2025 profitability held up.** Gross margin rose modestly to 27.0% from 26.7%; operating income reached $221M at an 11.9% margin. The net income decline looks worse than the underlying reality: it was driven by the non-cash inducement charge on the 2028 note exchange plus $6.1M of FX losses. Diluted EPS from continuing operations was $2.89 versus $3.40.

**The deterioration is in Q1 2026.** On flat revenue, gross margin fell ~260 bps and operating margin fell ~410 bps, with operating income down 35.7% YoY. This is the mechanism to watch: Integer carries a large fixed manufacturing base, so a volume shortfall compresses margins quickly. The inventory build reinforces the demand-below-plan read.

**Cash flow is capital-intensive but positive.** 2025 operating cash flow was $196M against $91M capex, yielding ~$105M free cash flow (FCF margin ~5.7%). Q1'26 operating cash flow slipped to $24.7M from $31.3M. Management guides 2026 capex to $95–105M.

**Balance sheet sits in a grey zone.** At year-end 2025: cash $17M (thin), long-term debt $1,185M, equity $1,747M, D/E ~0.95, current ratio 3.32. Goodwill ($1,111M) plus intangibles ($825M) equal 57% of total assets, the legacy of an acquisition-driven strategy and a source of impairment risk if end-markets deteriorate. The Altman Z-Score of 2.29 places the company in the grey zone. Credit-agreement net leverage rose from 2.7x at year-end to 3.0x in Q1'26 against a 5.0x covenant cap, with interest coverage near 14x. The direction of leverage is the wrong one as EBIT softens.

**Debt structure is manageable and largely fixed.** The stack includes $1.0B of 2030 converts (1.875%, $150.96 conversion, well out of the money), $116M of 2028 converts (2.125%, $87.20 conversion, near the money), a $91M term loan, and an $800M revolver ($65M drawn at Q1'26). Roughly 88–92% of debt is fixed after the 2030 issuance, and interest expense fell 23% in 2025 as variable debt was swapped for cheaper fixed-rate converts, a genuine positive.

**Capital allocation is aggressive relative to the operating backdrop.** No dividend. A $200M buyback was authorized in November 2025; $50M was executed in 2025 and another $50M via a February 2026 ASR at an average $84.80, leaving $100M. Repurchasing stock and drawing the revolver while cash is thin and EBIT is softening is a defensible but forward-leaning stance.

## Growth Analysis

The secular backdrop for the C&V franchise is attractive: structural heart, electrophysiology/AFib, and neurovascular are among the faster-growing pockets in medtech, and Integer's coating tuck-ins deepen its capability set. The 17% C&V growth in 2025 is real and reflects share gains within these end markets.

The near-term growth trajectory has broken down. Both the 10-K and Q1'26 10-Q flag that 2026 growth will be pressured by the multi-product adoption shortfall. Whether this is idiosyncratic execution or a symptom of broader OEM destocking, the filings point to the former. Consensus reflects the reset: current-year EPS estimates fell to $6.08 from $6.49 ninety days ago, next-year to $6.82 from $7.23 (−5.6%), and current-quarter to $1.38 from $1.54. The 30-day revision trend has stabilized (essentially flat), suggesting the initial cut has been absorbed, but there is no positive revision momentum to underwrite a growth-driven re-rating.

This is the mirror image of a strong-inflection buy signal: estimates are down, not up. The growth thesis rests on a 2026 volume recovery that is not yet visible in the data, so we score growth prospects below neutral.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$97.50, ITGR trades at ~24.6x trailing EPS of $2.89 and ~16x forward on the $6.08 current-year consensus. The forward multiple looks reasonable against a Health Care Equipment peer median P/E of 34.0 (TMDX 16.5, LMAT 34.0, LIVN 41.1, UFPT 27.2, ENOV negative). But the peer set is heterogeneous, and Integer's CDMO business model (margin-thin, customer-concentrated, capital-intensive) warrants a structural discount to higher-margin finished-device OEMs. The company's 5.7% FCF margin and mid-single-digit organic growth do not support a premium multiple.

The analyst target set brackets the current price closely: low $84, median $99.50, mean $101.20, high $115. The stock trades essentially at the median, and the tight dispersion around current price is the market's read that this is a special situation with a bifurcated outcome. The high target of $115 approximates a takeout scenario; the low of $84 approximates a "continue standalone, fundamentals soft" scenario.

A compressed multiple is not itself a buy signal when it reflects genuine near-term deterioration and an absence of positive estimate momentum. This is closer to a value-trap-adjacent situation than a mispricing: cheapness paired with falling estimates and a binary catalyst sorts poorly. We do not treat proximity to consensus targets as a floor here because the estimate revisions are negative; the conditions for treating targets as stale-and-rising are absent. We score valuation at neutral: the forward multiple provides some support, but there is no identified independent catalyst that makes the market demonstrably wrong on the standalone business.

## Competitive Landscape

Integer's moat is real but customer-dependent. The CDMO industry is highly fragmented, with hundreds of niche players; Integer differentiates on end-to-end capability breadth, global manufacturing scale, and quality/regulatory systems (ISO 13485, FDA). Its position upstream of large-cap OEMs means it participates in medtech's secular growth without carrying direct clinical-adoption risk, but it also inherits OEM inventory-cycle volatility and recurring customer-driven price reductions.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. C&V share gains are a genuine positive. Against that, the CRM&N franchise is mature and market-growth-limited, and FY2025 total shareholder return lagged the medtech peer group (index 96.6 versus peer 116.3). The Q1'26 margin miss and the multi-product adoption stumble suggest execution friction at exactly the moment Integer needs its growth engine to deliver. The customer concentration is the structural ceiling on how strong this competitive position can score: any in-sourcing, dual-sourcing, or program loss at Abbott, Boston Scientific, or Medtronic would materially reset the trajectory. We hold the score at neutral, reflecting a stable-to-slightly-eroding position.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is elevated and not fully priced:

1. **Customer concentration (49–52% from three customers).** OEM inventory rebalancing can swing sales, and most large customers lack minimum purchase requirements.
2. **The multi-product adoption miss.** Management's own "highly unusual" framing suggests it does not fully understand the demand shortfall, which raises the risk that the 2026 recovery is delayed.
3. **Securities class action (new).** *Pembroke Pines Pension Fund v. Integer Holdings* (SDNY, filed December 2025, amended April 2026), with a motion-to-dismiss deadline in May 2026. Integer denies wrongdoing, but this is an open-ended overhang.
4. **Strategic-review uncertainty.** The review has no timeline and no assurance of outcome. A "continue standalone" resolution would likely disappoint a market that has already priced some takeout probability.
5. **Margin/fixed-cost-absorption risk**, demonstrated in Q1'26.
6. **Balance-sheet tension:** thin cash, leverage rising to 3.0x, 57% of assets in goodwill/intangibles, buybacks partly revolver-funded.
7. **Tariff/geopolitical exposure:** significant Mexico and Ireland manufacturing; 47% of sales outside the US.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 71.6% versus 48.6% one-year historical vol, rich by a 23-point spread and in the 88th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (71.6% near-term versus 58.2% at six months), which typically signals a near-dated binary event; the strategic review and next earnings print are the obvious candidates. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±15.7%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (−0.8% near-term, widening to −3.2% at six months), so the market is pricing a large move without a strong directional lean, consistent with a two-sided takeout-versus-standalone outcome. Short interest at 15.8% of float (10.1 days to cover) reflects meaningful bearish positioning but also creates squeeze potential on a positive review outcome.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($115+).** The strategic review concludes with a sale or merger at a premium. A scaled, attractively positioned CDMO with sticky OEM relationships, C&V exposure to structural heart and electrophysiology, and coating-technology tuck-ins is a logical target for a larger CDMO or private equity. In this scenario the current fundamental soft patch is secondary, and the short interest amplifies the upside on resolution. Absent a deal, a 2026 volume recovery restores operating margin toward 12%, EPS re-accelerates toward $7+, and the stock re-rates on normalized earnings.

**Bear Case ($84 and below).** The review ends with "continue standalone" while the multi-product adoption miss persists and OEM destocking continues. Fixed-cost absorption keeps margins depressed, estimates drift further, the securities litigation survives a motion to dismiss, and leverage tightens flexibility. In this outcome the stock retraces toward the low target of $84, roughly 14% below current price, with further downside toward the October 2025 crash zone if fundamentals worsen.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as **uncertain**: the risk/reward is genuinely two-sided and hinges on an event outcome we cannot handicap with confidence, not on a stable fundamental thesis in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Announcement of a definitive sale/merger at a premium above ~$115, or credible signals the review is advancing toward a transaction.
- Q2 2026 results showing volume recovery, operating margin recovering above ~10%, and positive estimate revisions turning current-year consensus back up.
- Evidence the three-product adoption issue is resolving, with the C&V franchise reaccelerating toward mid-teens growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Strategic review concludes as "continue standalone" with no fundamental improvement.
- A second consecutive quarter of margin compression (gross margin sustained below ~25%) or a further step-down in guidance.
- Net leverage rising above ~3.5x on softening EBITDA, or a goodwill/intangible impairment charge.
- Adverse development in the securities class action (motion to dismiss denied) combined with deteriorating operations.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, together with any interim disclosure on the strategic-review process. Given the options-implied ±15.7% move and near-term backwardation, a resolution in either direction is plausibly within the next one to two quarters.

## Conclusion

Integer is a structurally attractive, scaled medtech CDMO whose current market value is dominated by an event-driven overlay rather than by normalized fundamentals. Full-year 2025 operating performance was solid once the one-time convertible charge is stripped out, but Q1 2026 revealed a real volume/margin stumble, estimates have been cut across every window, and a cluster of governance and legal stress events (strategic review, activist truce, securities litigation, CEO transition) all landed within roughly six months. At ~$97.50 the stock already discounts a meaningful probability of a takeout, so the upside from a deal is partly priced while the downside from a standalone outcome amid soft fundamentals is material. With no fundamental catalyst independent of the review, balanced options positioning around a large implied move, and negative-to-stabilizing estimate momentum, the appropriate posture is to hold and monitor the review outcome and the Q2 volume trajectory. Trajectory context supports this: the score has oscillated (5.2 → 6.5 → 5.2) and the recommendation has been HOLD three times running; the Q1 margin miss and negative revisions confirm the growth deterioration flagged in the prior cycle rather than reversing it. We maintain HOLD.
