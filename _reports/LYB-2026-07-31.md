## Executive Summary

LyondellBasell's reported financials now span two incompatible worlds within nine months. FY2025 produced a $738M net loss, $1,251M of impairments (including the complete write-off of remaining goodwill in O&P-EAI and Advanced Polymer Solutions), a dividend that consumed 459% of free cash flow, and a September 2025 renegotiation of the revolver's maximum leverage covenant. Q2 2026 produced revenue of $9,177M (+19.8% YoY), operating income of $1,543M, and adjusted EBITDA of roughly $2,060M once the $734M European divestiture loss and $74M impairment are added back. O&P-Americas alone generated $1,183M of EBITDA in the quarter, exceeding that segment's entire FY2025 result of $1,144M.

The cause is identifiable and largely external. The Middle East conflict that began in late February 2026 removed a large block of crude-linked, naphtha-based petrochemical capacity from the global cost curve. Polyolefin prices are set at the margin by naphtha crackers; LYB's US Gulf Coast crackers run 75–80% ethane priced off domestic natural gas. A wider crude-to-US-gas spread mechanically widens LYB's North American margin, and its oxyfuels business (16% of revenue) carries a second oil-linked leg through gasoline blending economics. The 10-Q's own outlook attributes conditions to the Middle East and states that the return of conflict-impacted supply is "likely extending into 2027." Management is telling investors, correctly, that current earnings power is not mid-cycle.

Consensus already models the unwind: FY2026 EPS of $8.48 falls to $6.47 in FY2027, and the quarterly path steps down from $4.30 actual in Q2 to $2.83 in Q3 and $1.84 in Q4, with 30-day revisions of -13.7% and -15.3% on those two quarters even as 90-day revisions remain positive. At 7.4x FY26 and 9.7x FY27 consensus, with a 4.4% yield now covered at current earnings power, the stock is not expensive. But enterprise value of ~$30.6B spans 5.8x to 12.8x EBITDA across the plausible normalized range, leverage restrictions bar dividend increases at 4.0x and buybacks beyond dilution offset through 2027, H1 2026 free cash flow was negative $56M, and no insider has bought stock in the open market while a director-affiliated entity sold ~385,000 shares near a local high. This is a fairly priced levered bet on the crude-to-gas spread holding, and it belongs in the hold bucket rather than either extreme.

## Company Overview

LyondellBasell is a Dutch-incorporated commodity and intermediate chemicals producer operating from London, Rotterdam, Houston, and Hong Kong. Despite the "Specialty Chemicals" sector tag, the FY2025 10-K describes operations that "consist primarily of large processing plants that convert large volumes of liquid and gaseous hydrocarbon feedstocks into plastic resins and other chemicals," with products that "tend to be basic building blocks." Only Advanced Polymer Solutions (11% of FY2025 revenue) and Technology (~2%) resemble specialty economics. Benchmarking LYB against specialty-chemical multiples and margin profiles systematically misjudges the business.

FY2025 segment results (revenue / EBITDA): O&P-Americas $9,801M / $1,144M; O&P-EAI $10,227M / -$457M; Intermediates & Derivatives $9,069M / $878M; Advanced Polymer Solutions $3,472M / -$651M; Technology $549M / $180M. Product mix: polyethylene 24%, polypropylene 19%, oxyfuels 16%, olefins and co-products 14%, compounding 11%.

Market positions are strong on paper — largest North American polypropylene producer, largest European PE and PP producer, world's second-largest PP producer, world's largest oxyfuels producer — but LYB is only third in North American ethylene (6.2 Mt/yr), third in North American PE, and third in European ethylene. Competition in the core business is disclosed as being "based on price and, to a lesser extent, on product quality, product delivery, reliability of supply." Customer concentration is genuinely low: no customer exceeded 10% of revenue in 2023, 2024, or 2025.

The portfolio has been materially reshaped. Houston refining ceased in February 2025 and is in discontinued operations. The European propylene-oxide joint venture with Covestro was permanently closed. Four European O&P sites — Berre l'Etang, Münchsmünster, Carrington, and Tarragona, roughly 25% of O&P-EAI capacity — were sold to Munich-based turnaround investor AEQUITA effective 1 May 2026. A Brindisi polypropylene unit is slated for closure.

## Financial Analysis

**The descent, measured on a consistent basis.** Headline revenue fell from $49,439M (FY2022) to $30,153M (FY2025), but that comparison includes the now-discontinued refinery. On continuing operations the decline was $33,336M (2023) → $33,394M (2024) → $30,153M (2025), or -9.6% over two years, driven by roughly -8% price, -4% volume, +2% FX. Earnings fell far more, which is what operating leverage does at a commodity trough: operating income $5,101M → $2,725M → $1,918M → -$420M; EBITDA $6,296M → $4,504M → $3,460M → $1,126M. Stripping impairments, EBITDA fell from $4,409M (2024) to $2,377M (2025), an underlying -46% rather than the -67% the reported figures imply. Total equity fell from $12,930M (2023) to $10,082M (2025).

**Impairments are non-cash but not costless.** Charges escalated $507M (2023) → $949M (2024) → $1,251M (2025), of which $972M in 2025 was the full write-off of goodwill in O&P-EAI and APS, triggered in Q3 2025 by the "prolonged downturn in, and outlook for, the European petrochemical and global automotive industries... combined with the sustained decline in our market capitalization." Residual goodwill is now $705M, so further goodwill risk is modest in dollar terms. Two mechanical consequences matter for screens: the write-offs depress reported EBIT, ROA, ROE, and the Altman EBIT/assets term without touching cash or covenants (Altman Z of 1.61 on FY2025 data is largely an impairment artifact); and lower carrying value reduces future depreciation, flattering go-forward returns. D&A fell from $1,522M (2024) to $1,390M (2025) to ~$1,378M annualized in H1 2026.

**Q2 2026 in detail.** Revenue $9,177M, operating income $1,543M, reported EBITDA $1,252M, GAAP diluted EPS $1.71, non-GAAP EPS excluding identified items $4.30 against a $3.45–3.56 consensus (a 21–25% like-for-like non-GAAP beat). The $2.59 gap between GAAP and non-GAAP is dominated by the $734M divestiture loss (~$2.27/share pre-tax) and the $74M impairment. Adjusted EBITDA of ~$2,060M is the cleanest read of the quarter's earnings power under those conditions. Note that trailing EPS still shows -$2.48 in most data feeds, which predates the Q2 print and reconciles to LTM through Q1 2026 — screens using it will misread the company badly.

**Cash flow is the weakest link, in both directions.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $2,262M looked respectable against a net loss, but $687M came from receivables release and $945M from inventory liquidation, offset by a $768M payables reduction — a one-time working-capital harvest, not recurring earnings. H1 2026 ran the film backwards: OCF of $483M against $539M of capex produced -$56M of free cash flow despite $1,543M of Q2 operating income, because rising prices forced a $1,692M receivables build and $521M inventory build against $721M of payables. Excluding those swings, H1 2026 operating cash generation was approximately $1,975M. The Q2 receivables should convert in Q3, so H2 cash flow should look materially better even if margins merely hold — but H1 FCF should not be read as a run rate in either direction, and the H2 disclosure of planned Clinton downtime plus oxyfuels seasonality argues against annualizing any single quarter.

Cash fell $809M in H1 2026 to $2,630M, absorbing $448M of dividends, the $310M closing contribution to the divested European businesses, and $539M of capex.

**Leverage depends entirely on the denominator.** At 30 June 2026: total debt $12,910M, cash $2,630M, net debt $10,280M, equity $10,680M, current ratio 1.64. Reported LTM EBITDA of $1,685M gives 6.1x. Adding back $1,219M of H2 2025 impairments and $823M of H1 2026 charges gives adjusted LTM EBITDA of $3,727M and **2.76x** — the fairest single characterization of trailing leverage. Annualizing H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA ($5,286M) gives 1.9x; annualizing Q2 alone ($8,240M) gives 1.2x, which is a sensitivity rather than a forecast and should be discarded as a valuation anchor. Liquidity is adequate: the $3,750M revolver is fully undrawn, commercial paper outstanding is zero, covenants were met, and the September 2026 €500M note maturity (carried at $569M) is to be funded from cash. Two details cut the other way — the US receivables facility was downsized from $900M to $700M in June 2026, and long-term debt carried at $11,204M has a fair value of $9,602M (a ~14% discount that is largely a duration effect on 20–35 year 3.4–4.2% coupons, since the year-end 2025 gap was a comparable 13%, and cannot be attributed to credit deterioration without spread data).

**Self-help is real.** The April 2025 cash improvement plan targeted $600M of annualized savings and delivered $800M by year-end, with $500M more targeted in 2026 for $1.3B cumulative. Headcount fell ~7% to ~18,970. Capex is budgeted at ~$1.2B for 2026 versus $1,878M in 2025, achieved by deferring the Flex-2 propylene expansion; H1 capex of $539M tracks that budget. This discipline is why the company reached the 2026 upcycle with an intact revolver.

Financial health scores 4.5: adequate liquidity and 2.8x adjusted leverage offset by negative H1 FCF, purchased covenant relief, a dividend already halved, four consecutive years of impairments and declining equity, and $1,461M of current maturities into a higher-for-longer rate path.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term earnings momentum is strong and forward structural growth is negative. Those two statements are not in tension — the first is price, the second is volume and capacity.

Revenue grew 19.8% YoY in Q2 2026 on price, not volume. Capacity is shrinking by design: ~25% of O&P-EAI capacity sold, the European PO joint venture closed, Brindisi PP slated for closure, the Houston refinery gone. Growth capital has been deferred: Flex-2 postponed, the MoReTec-2 final investment decision postponed, and the 50%-owned Houston plastic-waste sorting facility fully impaired at $74M with the 10-Q citing "the slower than anticipated pace of our circularity investments in the U.S." MoReTec-1 (Wesseling, 50 kt/yr, 2027 startup) continues, and sustainability-linked spending remains ~15% of the capital budget, so this is a slowdown rather than abandonment — but for anyone underwriting a circular-economy growth thesis, the write-off contradicts it directly.

Consensus itself prices the unwind. FY2026 EPS of $8.48 is up 11.8% over 90 days but down 6.1% over 30 days; FY2027 of $6.47 is up 13.2% over 90 days and down 6.0% over 30 days. The near-quarter cuts are sharper: Q3 from $3.28 to $2.83 (-13.7%), Q4 from $2.17 to $1.84 (-15.3%). The sequential path — $4.30 actual, then $2.83, then $1.84 — reflects the April ceasefire, Clinton planned downtime, and guided Q3 operating rates of 85% in O&P-Americas, 70% in European O&P-EAI (down from 75% guided for Q1), and 85% in I&D. A European operating rate cut to 70% during record pricing is the single most informative demand datapoint in the file.

Demand-side context is unhelpful. Q2 China revenue fell 21% YoY to $378M while total revenue rose 19.8%. The China decline most plausibly reflects weak Chinese demand and/or self-sufficiency from new domestic capacity, and it is a negative datapoint about end-market strength. European and North American end-market demand for polyolefins — which feed packaging, automotive compounds, and construction — remains a constraint on volume recovery even if the price shock persists. [The specific macro claims about Chinese PMI, ECB policy, and US mortgage rates are not supported by the research brief and have been removed.]

The one genuine forward lever is capacity rationalization. LYB's own closures plus reported delays to two Chinese petrochemical projects would improve the 2027+ supply picture materially if the deferrals become cancellations. There is no evidence yet to resolve that.

Growth prospects: 4.5. The estimate-revision signal here fails the inflection test — the 90-day positive revisions are a war-price artifact, the 30-day trend is negative, and the forward year is guided down 24% from the current year.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing multiples are meaningless (GAAP LTM EPS is negative and stale). The relevant frame is forward earnings and normalized EBITDA.

**On consensus earnings:** $62.49 against FY2026 EPS of $8.48 is 7.4x; against FY2027 of $6.47 it is 9.7x. Peer median P/E is 24.0x, but that peer set (IFF 24.0x, DD 147.7x, PPG 16.9x, RPM 20.7x, ALB negative) is heterogeneous and mostly genuine specialty businesses with different margin structures and cyclicality. PPG at 16.9x is the closest analogue, and LYB deserves a substantial discount to it: PPG's earnings are coatings-formulation driven, LYB's are a spread. Commodity polyolefin producers historically trade at 6–10x peak earnings and 12–20x trough earnings, so 7.4x on what is plausibly a peak year is unremarkable rather than cheap.

**On enterprise value:** EV is approximately $30.6B ($20.2B equity + $10.28B net debt + $0.11B redeemable NCI). Against adjusted LTM EBITDA of $3,727M that is 8.2x. Against H1-2026-annualized adjusted EBITDA of $5,286M it is 5.8x. Against the ~$2.4B underlying FY2025 level it is 12.8x. The market is currently paying roughly a mid-cycle EBITDA of $3.8–4.2B, which sits between the two poles and is a defensible place for it to be.

**Yield support:** the $0.69 quarterly dividend annualizes to ~$892M, or 4.4% at the current price, versus a 2.1% peer median. Against FY2025 FCF of $384M that would still be 232% coverage, but FY2025 was the trough; against H2 2026 cash conversion of the Q2 receivables the dividend is covered comfortably. The covenant bars increases at 4.0x leverage and the 10-K retains "could be forced to lower or suspend our dividends" language written into the same filing that disclosed the cut, so the yield is support, not a guarantee.

**Analyst frame:** mean target $66.94 (+7%), median $65 (+4%), range $48–$95. The distribution is 3 strong buy / 3 buy / 9 hold / 2 sell / 1 strong sell — genuinely divided, with sell-side ratings roughly stable over three months. On a stock at 7.4x forward, analyst targets could in principle be treated as stale floors; here they are not, because the same analysts are cutting near-quarter estimates by 14–15% while holding annual numbers, which means the targets embed the normalization they are modeling.

My own scenario build:
- **Bear ($45, -28%):** the conflict premium fully unwinds, crude-to-gas normalizes, EBITDA reverts toward $2.4–2.8B, leverage returns above 3.5x and approaches the 4.0x dividend restriction, and the dividend comes under question again. Roughly 6.5–7x EV/EBITDA on $2.6B implies equity near $7–9B, but the November 2025 low of $41.58 was set with worse fundamentals than today's, so $45 rather than the mechanical output is the realistic floor.
- **Base ($66, +6%):** earnings settle near FY2027 consensus of $6.47 at 10x, or mid-cycle EBITDA of $3.9B at 7.5x EV/EBITDA against declining net debt. Both routes converge near $65–68, consistent with the analyst mean and median.
- **Bull ($88, +41%):** the crude-over-gas spread persists into 2027 as management's own outlook implies, EBITDA runs $4.5–5.0B, H2 2026 and 2027 free cash flow takes net debt below $9B, and the multiple holds at 6.5–7x EV/EBITDA. Equivalently, ~10x on $8.48 of FY26 earnings.

Probability-weighting these at roughly 35% bull / 40% base / 25% bear gives an expected price return near +8% plus a 4.4% dividend. That is adequate, not asymmetric, and the fan is unusually wide. Valuation scores 5.0. The cheapness on FY26 numbers is real but it is cheapness on windfall earnings, which is the classic configuration of a value trap in this sector; the offset is that the macro conditions producing the windfall have not normalized.

## Competitive Landscape

The durable advantage is feedstock, not product. Ethane comprised 75–80% of North American cracker feedstock in 2024–2025, and LYB states it can produce up to ~90% of US ethylene output from NGLs. Naphtha was 70% of European feedstock in 2025 (up from 60%), with only about half displaceable. Feedstock and energy together are roughly 70% of cost of goods sold on a three-year average. LYB is therefore structurally long the crude-to-US-gas spread on its American assets and short it on its remaining European assets — the single lens that explains both the 2023–2025 collapse and the 2026 inflection.

The 2026 evidence of that bifurcation is broad and consistent: LYB and Dow benefiting, Alpek raising guidance, Japanese ethylene operating rates at a record low on naphtha disruption, Chinese state refiners cutting throughput by more than 1 mb/d, naphtha-based converters in South Asia squeezed. This is an industry pattern rather than a company claim.

Trajectory is the problem. Europe is structurally impaired even at cyclical highs: O&P-EAI generated roughly $302M of EBITDA on $2,648M of Q2 revenue excluding the divestiture loss — an ~11% margin at what should be peak — while the Q3 operating rate was guided down to 70%. Advanced Polymer Solutions produced -$651M of EBITDA in FY2025 with its goodwill fully written off, tied to a weak global automotive market. China revenue is contracting. US revenue at $3,573M was 39% of the Q2 total, so geographic concentration in North America increased rather than diversified.

Two structural qualifiers on the headline capacity positions. A material share comes from joint ventures where LYB explicitly lacks control (Saudi Arabia, China, Poland, South Korea, Thailand, Mexico), contributing roughly 1.8 Mt of PP, 1.6 Mt of olefins, and 760 kt of PE pro rata. And the US Covestro propylene-oxide arrangement gives Covestro an in-kind entitlement to 680 kt/yr of PO production rather than an ownership percentage, with the parties not sharing marketing — a tolling relationship, not equity-proportional profit sharing. Headline capacity overstates economic control of output.

The bright spot is Technology: $549M of FY2025 revenue at $180M of EBITDA (~33% margin), $73M of EBITDA in Q2 2026 alone, backed by ~5,000 patents and licensing relationships that feed catalyst supply into the same joint ventures. It is the highest-quality, least cyclical earnings stream and at ~2% of revenue too small to change the consolidated character of the business.

Competitive position: 4.5. The North American cost advantage is genuine and currently being monetized; the overall franchise is shrinking, its European and automotive-linked positions are the largest in structurally contracting markets, and the sale of a quarter of O&P-EAI capacity closed during a price spike that temporarily flattered those assets.

## Risk Assessment

**Windfall reversal is the dominant risk and is the inverse of a disclosed risk factor.** The 10-K warns that a sustained low crude price relative to US natural gas would compress the NGL advantage. The entire Q2 margin gain is that risk running in LYB's favor. Management expects conflict-affected supply to return "extending into 2027," which is a runway, not a permanence. A normalization of crude back toward pre-conflict levels while US gas stays firm would reverse the entire Q2 margin gain. This is a macro variable outside management's control, and it is the largest single determinant of the equity's value. [The specific claims about Brent above $90, strikes on US assets in Kuwait and Bahrain, and a Damietta vessel attack are not supported by the research brief and have been removed.]

**Capital-return constraints through 2027.** The September 2025 revolver amendment raised the maximum permitted leverage ratio and accepted restrictions barring dividend increases at leverage ≥4.00:1.00 and repurchases except to offset dilution. A borrower does not pay for covenant relief it expects not to need; the alternative reading is routine pre-emptive flexibility ahead of a known trough, and both are consistent with an undrawn revolver and met covenants. What is factual is that the constraint exists, is binding on capital returns, and runs through 2027. Shareholders approved a 34.0M-share authorization (~10.5% of shares) in May 2026 that is essentially untouched — optionality rather than a program.

**Dividend risk is not fully extinguished.** FY2025 dividends of $1,764M against $384M of FCF forced the February 2026 halving to $0.69/quarter, saving ~$872M annually. The 10-K retained suspension language after the cut. Coverage now depends on H2 2026 receivables conversion and on the spread holding.

**Obligations that do not flex down.** Purchase obligations approximate 20% of annual COGS with a 6-year weighted-average term; take-or-pay commitments including Polish joint-venture off-take approximate 5% of COGS with a 14-year weighted-average term. These amplify downside operating leverage in a trough.

**Unquantified tails.** A 2024 proposed class action in Kansas names LYB and eight other industry defendants on plastics-recyclability claims spanning public nuisance, antitrust, unfair competition, consumer protection, and unjust enrichment. EU ETS free-allowance reduction, the Single-Use Plastics Directive, and packaging regulation raise European cost. The pension deficit was $863M at year-end 2025 with a US expected long-term return assumption of 7.25%, on the optimistic side. LIFO lower-of-cost-or-market risk on $1.6B of pools was extinguished by the price rise and returns if prices normalize.

**Governance and reporting notes.** The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows five-year TSR of -33.6% against a peer group at +22.4%, with average NEO compensation actually paid falling from $7.75M (2023) to $1.33M (2025) — equity-linked pay absorbing the shareholder experience, which is the design working. One caution: 2026 EBITDA-linked awards will likely pay well for reasons unrelated to management action. Separately, structured extractions of the 2026 proxy return a prior CEO's name and $0 in principal-executive-officer compensation for FY2022–FY2025; both are parsing errors (Peter Vanacker has been CEO since May 2022) and should not be read as disclosure.

**Insider activity.** Across February–June 2026, activity was overwhelmingly routine grants and tax withholding. One open-market executive sale (General Counsel Kaplan, 10,000 shares at $74.16, ~9.4% of his position) is weak evidence either way. More notable: AI Investments Holdings, an Access Industries entity affiliated with director Lincoln Benet, sold ~385,390 shares on 9 March 2026 at $67.03–68.90 (~$26.3M) plus a 464,610-share gift disposition, leaving roughly 7.0M shares across associated accounts. No insider bought in the open market. Given the buyback restriction and the recent dividend cut, absence of buying is not a strong negative — but there is nothing on the bullish side of the ledger either.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 44.1% sits essentially on top of one-year realized volatility of 44.2% and in the 9th percentile of the current chain — post-earnings vol crush, with ATM IV down 6.4 points from 50.4% two days earlier as the price rose 3.3%. The term structure is in contango (44.1% at one month, 45.4% at three, 49.0% at six), which is normal-to-complacent. Two details are worth flagging. Put/call open interest moved from 0.68 to 1.51 across the print, and put/call volume ran 1.53–1.91 in the front expiry, including 1,534 August $58 puts against 318 open interest. And the six-month tenor carries a skew reading of -6.4% against a balanced -0.2% at one month. Read together: hedging demand is building specifically in the December window, which is the period in which conflict-affected supply is expected to begin returning and in which Q3/Q4 earnings step down to the guided $2.83 and $1.84. The options market is not pricing near-term distress; it is paying for protection against the normalization the fundamental analysis identifies as the central risk. Short interest of 4.9% of float and 2.2 days to cover means the bearish view is uncrowded, so there is no squeeze mechanic supporting the stock.

Risk factors: 4.5. The reversal risk is partly priced (the stock is 26% below its March high and consensus models FY27 down 24%), but leverage constraints, fixed take-or-pay obligations, the litigation tail, and renewed dividend risk stack on top of a macro binary.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $88 (+41%)

The crude-to-US-gas spread stays wide through 2027 because Middle East supply does not durably return. LYB's North American assets, running 75–80% ethane, capture the full spread while naphtha-based competitors in Asia and Europe remain rate-constrained; oxyfuels adds a second oil-linked leg. Adjusted EBITDA runs $4.5–5.0B in 2026 and holds near $4B in 2027. Q3 receivables convert, H2 free cash flow turns sharply positive, the September 2026 note is retired from cash, and net debt falls below $9B, taking leverage under 2.5x and removing the covenant overhang. The $1.3B cost program and the removal of loss-making European assets mean a structurally higher trough than 2025's. At 7x EV/EBITDA on $4.5B with $9B of net debt, equity is worth roughly $22.5B, or ~$70; at 10x FY26 earnings of $8.48, $85. Capacity rationalization — LYB's own closures plus deferred Chinese projects — proves permanent, and 2027 supply/demand tightens without the war.

### Bear Case — $45 (-28%)

The April ceasefire pattern repeats and holds: Middle East volumes return, crude normalizes relative to US gas, and the entire Q2 margin gain reverses within two quarters. Consensus already points this direction — Q3 estimates cut 13.7% in thirty days, Q4 cut 15.3%, FY27 EPS 24% below FY26 — and the guided Q3 European operating rate of 70% during record pricing shows demand cannot absorb the volume. Underlying oversupply from global capacity additions, which the 10-K names as a core profitability driver, was never resolved; the windfall came from supply removal elsewhere. EBITDA reverts toward $2.4–2.8B, net debt/EBITDA returns above 3.5x, the 4.0x dividend restriction becomes live, and the $892M dividend is questioned again with the 10-K's suspension language already in place. Demand compounds the problem: Chinese contraction with LYB's China revenue already -21% YoY, weak European demand, and softening US end-markets hitting packaging, automotive compounds, and construction. Fixed take-or-pay obligations with a 14-year weighted-average term do not flex down. At 6.5–7x on $2.6B of EBITDA against $10.3B of net debt, equity approaches the mid-$40s, near the analyst low of $48 and above the $41.58 low set eight months ago on worse fundamentals.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The decisive checkpoint is the Q3 2026 report in late October, which will show (a) whether the Q2 receivables build converted to cash, (b) O&P-Americas EBITDA under post-ceasefire conditions with Clinton downtime, and (c) whether the guided 70% European operating rate held. The September 2026 €500M note retirement from cash is a secondary confirmation. Monitor Middle East supply-return developments continuously, since the return of conflict-impacted supply is the trigger for the normalization path management expects to extend into 2027.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA of at least $1.6B with positive H2 free cash flow and net debt declining toward $9.0B — evidence the windfall converts to cash and deleveraging rather than working capital.
2. FY2027 consensus EPS revisions turning positive from the current $6.47 (i.e., analysts extending rather than fading the spread), together with confirmed cancellation rather than deferral of announced Asian capacity projects.
3. Price below ~$53 with fundamentals intact, which would put the stock at roughly 8x FY27 consensus and near 5x EV/adjusted-LTM-EBITDA — a level at which the bear scenario is substantially pre-paid.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. O&P-Americas quarterly EBITDA falling back below $500M, indicating the crude-to-gas spread has compressed to pre-conflict economics.
2. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA returning above 3.5x, or any disclosure that the 4.00:1.00 covenant restriction on dividend increases has become live.
3. H2 2026 free cash flow failing to turn positive despite the Q2 receivables release, which would indicate the earnings improvement is not converting.
4. Any change to the dividend, in either direction, that signals the board no longer views $0.69/quarter as sustainable.

**Position framing for existing holders.** The 4.4% dividend plus roughly 6% to my base case is a reasonable carry while the spread question resolves, and the September note retirement plus H2 cash conversion are near-term catalysts on the positive side. I would not add here; the March high of $83.94 and the June low of $52.65 within four months demonstrate how violently this equity re-prices on headlines it does not control.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** LyondellBasell delivered a genuine operational and financial improvement in Q2 2026 — a 21–25% non-GAAP beat, revenue up 19.8%, adjusted EBITDA near $2.06B, one segment out-earning its own prior full year — and it entered the upturn with a much better cost base than it had in 2024, having delivered $800M of savings against a $600M target, cut capex 36%, halved a dividend that was consuming 459% of free cash flow, and paid roughly $410–460M in cash to shed a quarter of the capacity in a segment that lost $684M at the operating line. Adjusted trailing leverage of 2.76x with an untouched $3,750M revolver makes solvency a low-probability concern.

The problem is that the earnings driving the improvement are a spread, not a franchise, and the spread widened because a war removed competitors' feedstock. Management says so in its own outlook language, consensus is cutting the next two quarters by 14–15% while modeling FY27 EPS 24% below FY26, the December options tenor is where hedging demand is concentrated, and no insider has bought a share in the open market while a board-affiliated entity sold near a local high. The unresolved question — whether normalized EBITDA is closer to $2.4B or $5.3B — spans an EV/EBITDA range of 5.8x to 12.8x on today's enterprise value, and no filing in the record resolves it. That is what an uncertain hold looks like: a fairly valued, better-managed, still-levered cyclical whose next 40% move in either direction will be decided by the return of Middle East supply rather than by anything in Houston.

This is the fourth consecutive hold on LYB and the overall score has drifted from 5.6 in May to 4.6 now. The direction of that drift is right: growth prospects improved on the Q2 print but the forward path is guided down, competitive position continues to weaken as the franchise shrinks into a contracting European and automotive end-market, and the valuation case has narrowed as the price recovered 19% off the June low. Nothing in the current data supports either upgrading to a buy on a war premium or shorting a 4.4%-yield producer into a live oil shock.
