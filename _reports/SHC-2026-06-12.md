**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sotera Health is a leading global provider of outsourced, regulation-mandated sterilization and lab-testing services for the medical-device, pharmaceutical, and food-safety industries. The business is high quality on most operating dimensions: roughly 51% adjusted EBITDA margins, mid-single-digit constant-currency revenue growth, recurring contracted demand, and the only vertically integrated global gamma sterilization platform via the Sterigenics-Nordion combination. The franchise sits in an oligopolistic, high-barrier market where shipping economics, regulatory qualification lock-in, and Cobalt-60 supply integration create durable switching costs.

The 2026 story is largely about overhangs lifting. Warburg Pincus and GTCR completed full exits of their stakes by mid-May 2026, taking the public float to roughly 90% from a sponsor-controlled structure, and the securities class action was dismissed with prejudice and affirmed by the Sixth Circuit. Both developments remove recurring pressures that capped the stock, even as the secondary supply explains why shares traded near the sponsors' ~$15-16 exit prints. The stock is up about 36% over the trailing twelve months and trades at ~$16.39, against a 52-week range of $10.80-$19.85.

The reasons this is not yet a buy are concentrated in two areas. First, ethylene oxide (EO) tort litigation is unreserved and effectively self-insured: the company books no reserve for future EO matters, its environmental insurance is exhausted and current policies exclude EO, and it lost its insurer duty-to-defend appeal in March 2026. A single adverse verdict above the greater of $162.6M or 30% of EBITDA, if unstayed for 60 days, would trigger an event of default. Second, deleveraging from 3.2x net leverage is real but gradual, and estimate revisions are flat rather than rising. The combination produces a reasonable forward multiple on a quality asset whose downside is genuinely binary. Maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

Sotera Health operates three segments serving non-discretionary, regulation-driven end markets:

- **Sterigenics** (FY25 revenue $755.8M, +8.3%; 54.6% segment margin): terminal sterilization via gamma, ethylene oxide, and E-beam. This is the core franchise and the primary EO-litigation exposure.
- **Nordion** (FY25 revenue $187.6M, +8.2%; 57.3% margin): global supplier of Cobalt-60 and gamma irradiation systems. Combined with Sterigenics, it makes SHC the only vertically integrated global gamma sterilization provider. Revenue is lumpy quarter-to-quarter because of Co-60 harvest and shipment timing.
- **Nelson Labs** (FY25 revenue $220.2M, –3.9%; 33.3% margin): outsourced microbiological and analytical lab testing. This is the current soft spot, declining on a fall-off in higher-margin expert advisory and consulting revenue.

The moat is structural: regulatory and customer qualification lock-in (a device maker cannot casually switch sterilization providers without re-validating), proximity-based facility networks (shipping product for sterilization is costly), and vertical Co-60 integration. The principal long-tail competitive threats are in-housing by large OEMs and alternative modalities (X-ray, FDA-approved vaporized hydrogen peroxide as of 2024), though adoption of alternatives remains early.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded steadily: $1,003.7M (2022) → $1,049.3M (2023) → $1,100.4M (2024) → $1,163.6M (2025, +5.7%). Q1'26 revenue was $280.0M, up 10% reported but 6.5% constant currency. The gap matters: Q1's headline was flattered by FX (+3.5 points) and Nordion Co-60 harvest timing (the +29% Nordion segment growth is a timing artifact, not run-rate). Constant-currency growth and the soft Nelson Labs trend (–0.7%, segment income –11.5%) are the truer read, and the FY26 guide of +5-6.5% constant currency is the right anchor.

Profitability is strong. FY25 GAAP net income was $77.9M (up 75% from $44.4M), and adjusted EBITDA was $593.8M at a ~51% margin, which is exceptional for a services business. Adjusted net income was $245.4M. The gap between GAAP and adjusted is large and recurring, driven by amortization, stock-based comp (~$31M), and EO litigation/professional fees (~$46M plus $65M of settlements in 2025). Investors should treat the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge as partly structural rather than purely transitory, given the ongoing legal spend.

Cash flow is the franchise's best attribute. FY25 operating cash flow was $287.2M, capex $138M (down from $179M), producing roughly $149M of free cash flow (~13% FCF margin). Capex is now ramping again, guided at $175-225M for FY26, including ~$51M for environmental/EO facility upgrades and capacity expansion, which will compress near-term free cash flow.

The balance sheet is where the picture turns cautious. Total assets are $3.24B, with goodwill of $1.10B (34% of assets) and a retained deficit of –$505.5M, a legacy of the 2022 $233.6M Willowbrook settlement loss. Total debt is roughly $2.24B (Term Loan $1.42B due 2031 at SOFR+2.50%; Secured Notes $750M at 7.375% due 2031; finance leases ~$97M). Net debt is about $1.92B, putting net leverage at 3.2x. The widely cited Altman Z-Score of 0.9 ("distress zone") is misleading: it reflects LBO-legacy leverage and the retained deficit, not operating distress. The business carries a 2.46x current ratio, $316M cash, and roughly $900M+ liquidity including an undrawn $600M revolver.

The deleveraging tailwind is genuine. The weighted-average interest rate fell to 6.23% in Q1'26 from 7.65%, via the September 2025 repricing (Amendment No. 6) plus a $75M paydown. FY26 interest expense is guided to $135-145M. A 1% SOFR move equals roughly $10.2M of annual interest impact, partly hedged by a $400M swap to 2027.

FY26 guidance, reaffirmed at Q1: revenue $1,233-1,251M, adjusted EBITDA $632-641M, adjusted EPS $0.93-1.01.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying growth is steady but unremarkable: mid-single-digit constant currency, with Sterigenics carrying the load and Nelson Labs detracting. The earnings record is reassuring on visibility: three beats and one in-line across the last four quarters (Q2'25 +14.5%, Q3'25 +17.8%, Q4'25 +6.2%, Q1'26 in-line at $0.18). That pattern argues for confidence in the FY26 adjusted EPS guide.

The estimate revision picture does not support a buy. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $0.97 (essentially flat over 30 days, +1.0% over 90 days), and next-year at $1.06 (+1.6% over 90 days). These are stable, not rising. The strong forward-momentum signal that would justify an upgrade — estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days combined with revenue acceleration — is absent. SHC is a steady compounder and should be valued as such.

Growth optionality exists in three places: a Nelson Labs recovery (currently the drag), continued Sterigenics pricing and volume durability (the recurring analyst question at recent conferences), and capacity additions funded by the elevated FY26 capex. None of these is a near-term catalyst with a measurable threshold, which keeps the growth case in "durable but slow" territory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $16.39 against FY26 adjusted EPS guidance, SHC trades at roughly 16-18x forward adjusted earnings. The trailing GAAP P/E of ~40x is not the right lens given the large, recurring GAAP-to-adjusted bridge; the franchise should be evaluated on adjusted earnings and free cash flow. On EV/EBITDA, an enterprise value of roughly $6.6B (market cap ~$4.7B plus net debt) against FY26 adjusted EBITDA is about 10.3x — reasonable for a ~51% margin, wide-moat services business.

Peer comparison is messy, as the Health Care Services cohort is heterogeneous (RDNT and HIMS show negative P/Es; CHE at 24.1x; CON at 18.5x; VCYT at 45.1x), with a peer median P/E of 24.1x. On forward adjusted earnings, SHC's ~16-18x sits below the median, and that discount is explainable: it reflects 3.2x leverage and the unreserved EO litigation tail rather than inferior business quality. The discount is warranted but not excessive.

The valuation score reflects a balanced setup. The stock cleared the sponsors' exit prints and the secondary overhang is gone; the multiple is undemanding for the franchise; and analyst targets (low $16.00, mean $20.11, median $20.00, high $24.00) imply ~23% upside to the mean. But the litigation tail caps how much premium the market will pay, and flat estimate revisions mean there is no re-rating catalyst pulling the multiple higher in the near term. This is a 6.0, not higher: the asymmetry is favorable on the business but truncated by a genuine binary.

## Competitive Landscape

Sterilization is an oligopolistic, high-barrier market dominated by SHC and STERIS-affiliated competitors. SHC's competitive position is improving on trajectory, which supports a 7.5 here. The vertical Co-60 integration through Nordion is unique and not easily replicated: Co-60 is sourced from a limited set of Canadian and Russian reactors with multi-year licensing cycles. Customer qualification lock-in and proximity-based facility economics raise switching costs meaningfully; a device maker must re-validate its product release process to change sterilization providers.

The threats are real but slow-moving. In-housing by large OEMs is constrained by the capital and regulatory burden of running sterilization facilities. Alternative modalities (X-ray and vaporized hydrogen peroxide) could reduce Co-60/EO dependence over a multi-year horizon, but adoption is early. The recurring analyst focus at RBC, Jefferies, and Goldman conferences in mid-2026 is the durability of Sterigenics pricing and volume, which is the right question; FY25 +8.3% Sterigenics growth supports durability against the distant modality-shift question.

## Risk Assessment

**Ethylene oxide tort litigation is the defining risk and it is asymmetric.** Sterigenics faces individual EO injury and property suits across California (~140 Vernon claims, trials January and April 2027), Georgia (~470 personal injury plus ~305 property claims in Atlanta), Illinois (~10 remaining Willowbrook), and New Mexico (state AG, trial July 2026). The history is sobering: a 2022 Willowbrook jury awarded $363M, after which SHC settled 880 claims for $408M plus additional batch settlements of $30.9M and $34M in 2025. The company books no reserve for future EO matters (it deems losses "not probable" and a range of loss "not estimable"), meaning any future settlement or judgment hits earnings and cash unexpectedly.

**Insurance is exhausted and EO is excluded.** The environmental policy covering EO is exhausted, current policies exclude EO, and SHC lost its insurer duty-to-defend appeal (Seventh Circuit reversal, March 2026; Illinois Supreme Court holding that pollution exclusions apply regardless of permits). EO defense and settlement costs are effectively self-funded, so any large adverse verdict flows straight to cash and equity with no insurance backstop.

**Covenant and appellate-bond risk.** A future adverse EO judgment could require a large appellate bond, straining liquidity. A judgment above the greater of $162.6M or 30% of EBITDA, if unstayed for 60 days, would be an event of default under the credit facilities, with cross-default to the notes. This is the channel by which a litigation loss becomes a balance-sheet event.

Mitigating this materially: the 2025-2026 Georgia causation wins improve the litigation risk picture versus the 2022 Willowbrook shock. In October 2025 the court granted specific-causation summary judgment for Sterigenics in pool cases; on October 31, 2025 the Court of Appeals vacated the unfavorable Phase 1 general-causation ruling; and on March 30, 2026 the trial court granted summary judgment for defendants in five remanded pool cases (plaintiffs appealing). The scientific-causation defense is gaining traction, though California and New Mexico remain open.

**Other risks:** supply concentration (single U.S. EO supplier; Co-60 from limited reactors with ~20% historically Russian, exposed to sanctions and 2026-2028 licensing renewals); regulatory tightening (EPA NESHAP/FIFRA on EO emissions, with a two-year White House exemption granted July 2025 now subject to a January 2026 lawsuit seeking to invalidate it); Nelson Labs weakness; and 3.2x leverage with covenants restricting dividends and M&A.

The risk score of 4.0 reflects a genuine, unreserved, self-insured binary tail that the market only partially prices, offset by the recent Georgia causation progress that lowers the near-term probability of a Willowbrook-scale shock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($22-24).** Sterigenics pricing and volume durability holds, Nelson Labs recovers from its advisory-revenue drag, and net leverage falls from 3.2x toward the high-2s as free cash flow services debt. The EO causation defense continues to win, materially de-risking the tail and allowing the forward multiple to expand toward the peer median (~24x). With the PE overhang and securities litigation both resolved and float at ~90%, the equity re-rates on improved governance and reduced tail risk. At the high end of the analyst target range (high $24.00), the stock reaches roughly 22-23x FY27 adjusted EPS of ~$1.06.

**Bear Case ($11-13).** An adverse EO verdict in New Mexico (July 2026) or California (2027) materially above prior settlement scale revives the Willowbrook shock, with no insurance backstop and a covenant-default trigger above the $162.6M/30%-of-EBITDA threshold. Even absent a catastrophic verdict, a string of settlements at unreserved levels compresses cash flow and the multiple. Nelson Labs continues to erode and Sterigenics pricing decelerates. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $11, a ~30% loss from current levels.

**Base Case ($18-19).** Steady mid-single-digit constant-currency growth, FY26 adjusted EPS lands within guidance, leverage grinds down, and EO litigation produces neither a clean resolution nor a catastrophic loss but rather continued contained settlements and incremental causation wins. The stock works modestly higher toward the median analyst target as the overhang-removal narrative carries it, at roughly 18-19x forward adjusted EPS.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD with a 12-month reassessment horizon, anchored on quarterly earnings and the New Mexico and California trial calendars.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable New Mexico verdict or settlement in mid-2026 that materially de-risks the EO tail, combined with continued Georgia causation wins surviving appeal.
- Net leverage falling below 3.0x with adjusted EBITDA tracking the upper half of guidance, plus 90-day estimate revisions turning meaningfully positive.
- A confirmed Nelson Labs inflection (segment revenue returning to positive YoY growth for two consecutive quarters).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse EO judgment approaching or exceeding the covenant threshold, raising appellate-bond and event-of-default risk.
- Sterigenics constant-currency growth decelerating below ~3% or evidence of accelerating modality substitution (X-ray, vaporized H₂O₂) eroding volumes.
- FY26 guidance cut, or net leverage rising rather than falling.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2'26, early August 2026) and the New Mexico trial (July 2026) are the near-term checkpoints. The California 2027 trials are the larger binary further out.

## Conclusion

Sotera Health is a high-quality, wide-moat, cash-generative sterilization franchise trading at a reasonable ~16-18x forward adjusted EPS and ~10x forward EV/EBITDA, now meaningfully de-risked on governance after the complete PE sponsor exit (float to ~90%) and the dismissal of the securities class action. The recent Georgia causation wins are a genuine positive on the litigation front, and new data confirms rather than reverses the improving trend.

What holds the recommendation at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of an unreserved, self-insured EO litigation tail with a covenant-default trigger, flat estimate revisions, and only gradual deleveraging. The downside is genuinely binary: a single adverse verdict in New Mexico or California could revive a Willowbrook-scale shock with no insurance backstop, and that asymmetry, not the business quality, is what caps the score. This is a growth_hold: a durable franchise worth owning for steady compounding and overhang-removal, where the bar for a buy is a measurable reduction in the litigation tail or a leverage/estimate inflection that is not yet present. Maintain HOLD.
