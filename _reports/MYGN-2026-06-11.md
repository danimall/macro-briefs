**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Myriad Genetics is a Salt Lake City molecular-diagnostics company in the middle of a difficult, cash-consuming turnaround. The business pioneered hereditary cancer testing and retains a recognizable brand and broad community-medicine commercial reach, but the financials show a company whose earnings power has collapsed: revenue declined 2% to $824.5M in FY25, GAAP net loss was $365.9M (EPS −$3.95) including $319.4M of goodwill and intangible impairments, free cash flow was −$13.8M, and the Altman Z-Score sits at 1.08, squarely in the distress zone. Equity fell from $701M to $337M in a single year, and goodwill collapsed from $286M to $47M after two consecutive impairment events tied to the company's own declining internal valuations of its Women's Health and Mental Health units.

The central fundamental problem is structural, not transitory. UnitedHealthcare dropped coverage of multi-gene pharmacogenetic panels including GeneSight (effective 2025), and management states explicitly that the negative revenue, profit, and cash-flow impact "will continue in 2026 and thereafter." This drove the Mental Health impairment and directly impairs roughly a fifth of the revenue base, with the risk that other payers follow. Layered on top is a July 2025 OrbiMed term loan ($125M at a 10.2–10.4% floating rate, maturing 2030, secured by substantially all assets) carrying a minimum trailing-12-month revenue covenant that ratchets from $615M at end-2025 to $974M by end-2029. FY26 guidance of $860–880M leaves a wide gap to that 2029 requirement, and the covenant escalates faster than the company's own growth plan. Successful execution of the Precise MRD and FirstGene launches is a requirement to avoid a covenant breach that could accelerate the debt.

We rate MYGN AVOID with an overall score of 3.83. The bull case requires a new management team (nearly the entire C-suite turned over in 2025) to execute two late-entry product launches into competitive markets, recover a structurally impaired reimbursement franchise, and keep cash burn contained while servicing 10% debt. Near-term liquidity is adequate ($124.4M cash plus $75M undrawn), so this is not an imminent bankruptcy. But the multi-year solvency math, the chronic GAAP losses, the ongoing dilution, and the absence of any C-suite open-market buying leave the equity as a high-beta (1.64) leveraged bet whose risk/reward we do not find attractive at $4.62.

## Company Overview

Myriad develops and sells molecular diagnostic tests across three product pillars after a 2026 reporting reorganization:

- **Cancer Care Continuum (CCC)** — roughly 60% of revenue ($120.2M in Q1'26). Combines the former Hereditary Cancer ($372M FY25) and Tumor Profiling ($122M FY25) segments. Includes MyRisk Hereditary Cancer (the flagship, expanded to 63 genes in November 2025), the FDA-approved BRACAnalysis CDx and MyChoice CDx companion diagnostics, Prolaris (prostate), Precise Tumor, and the newly launched Precise MRD (molecular residual disease).
- **Prenatal Health** — roughly 21% of revenue. Foresight carrier screening, Prequel NIPT, SneakPeek (a consumer early-gender test from the Gateway acquisition), and the pipeline FirstGene multi-modal prenatal screen.
- **Mental Health** — roughly 19% of revenue. Essentially the single GeneSight pharmacogenomic test for psychiatric medication selection.

The company operates as a single reportable segment. Each test runs in only one CLIA facility (Salt Lake City; South San Francisco; Mason, OH for GeneSight; San Diego for SneakPeek), which concentrates operational and disaster risk. Stated competitive advantages are a trusted brand, broad reach into community medicine rather than only academic centers, and a large clinical-evidence base. Headcount is approximately 2,700.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has stagnated.** The trajectory runs $678M (2022) → $753M (2023) → $838M (2024) → $824.5M (2025, −2% YoY). The 2025 decline came from the GeneSight/UnitedHealthcare coverage loss and the EndoPredict divestiture, with volume up only ~1%. Q1'26 returned to modest top-line growth (+2.3% to $200.4M), helped by Mental Health revenue/test up 15% and volume up 7%, and CCC volume up 13%, but Prenatal volume fell 12%.

**Profitability is deeply negative.** FY25 net loss was −$365.9M (EPS −$3.95) against −$127M in 2024, with an operating margin of −47%. The loss was dominated by $319.4M of impairments ($234.7M goodwill plus roughly $82M of intangibles) hitting Women's Health and Mental Health. These are non-cash, but they reflect management materially downgrading the internal value of those businesses. Q1'26 net loss was −$34.1M, against a near-breakeven Q1'25 that had benefited from a one-time $29.3M tax item; another $5.4M impairment in Q1'26 wrote Women's Health goodwill to zero.

**Cash flow and balance sheet are eroding.** Operating cash flow was barely positive in FY25 (+$1.8M) but FCF was −$13.8M. Q1'26 used $15.7M in operations, and cash fell from $149.6M at year-end to $124.4M at March 31, a $25M draw in one quarter. Q1 is seasonally weak, but the burn is real. Goodwill now stands at $47.1M (Mental Health $29.8M, International $17.3M, Women's Health zero). The current ratio of 2.49 indicates adequate near-term liquidity; the Z-Score of 1.08 is driven entirely by negative EBIT relative to assets (−0.55), so the distress signal is about earnings power, not an imminent liquidity wall.

**Capital structure carries an embedded growth requirement.** The OrbiMed term loan replaced the prior ABL in July 2025, with up to $75M more available. With FY25 revenue at $824.5M and FY26 guidance at $860–880M, the company must grow revenue roughly 18% by 2029 against a covenant that rises faster than its guided trajectory. There are no dividends or buybacks; cash is funding operations and the Precise MRD and FirstGene launches.

## Growth Analysis

FY26 guidance calls for revenue of $860–880M (about +6% at the midpoint), adjusted gross margin of 68–69%, and adjusted EBITDA of $37–49M (about +43% at the midpoint). The adjusted EBITDA figure excludes stock-based compensation, impairments, and D&A, so GAAP remains a loss. The company has a respectable recent beat history on EPS (three beats in four quarters, including large beats in mid-2025 driven by small absolute numbers), but the most recent quarter (Q1'26, reported 5/6/26) missed at −$0.09 against a −$0.07 estimate, and the stock fell about 20% in a single session despite reaffirmed guidance, a sign of fragile investor confidence.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and does not support an inflection thesis. The current-quarter EPS estimate was cut sharply (−520% over 30 days to −$0.06), the current-year estimate is roughly flat (+1.5% over 30 days, −19% over 90 days), and the next-year estimate is down 3% over 30 days and 11% over 90 days. The one bright spot is the next-quarter estimate (+77% over 30 days). This is not the +30% sustained upward revision velocity that signals a fundamental inflection; it reads as a business stabilizing modestly off a low base while the longer-horizon estimates drift lower.

The growth thesis rests on two unproven, late-entry launches. Precise MRD launched commercially with select community oncologists in March 2026 into a market where Natera and Guardant are entrenched, well-funded leaders. FirstGene is advancing through the CONNECTOR study toward full launch. MyChoice CDx received FDA approval as a companion diagnostic for Zejula (niraparib) in ovarian cancer in March 2026, a genuine positive, though incremental. The core hereditary-cancer franchise continues to show the classic diagnostics dynamic of volume up, price down (FY25 hereditary cancer revenue/test fell 5% while volume rose 7%). The growth needed to satisfy the escalating covenant depends on these launches winning clinical utility and reimbursement against incumbents, which is a meaningful execution hurdle rather than a base case.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward P/E of approximately 30.6x sits on a thin GAAP-loss-adjacent earnings base; there is no meaningful trailing P/E because the company is loss-making. Applying the inflection framework does not rescue the valuation: revenue growth is mid-single-digit, not the 40%+ that would justify ignoring trailing metrics, and the estimate-revision velocity is negative on the longer horizons. This is a stagnant-revenue, loss-making business, so the high-multiple-on-trough-earnings argument does not apply.

Peer comparison is of limited help given the heterogeneity of the diagnostics/biotech complex; the supplied comparable (BridgeBio at a negative P/E) is itself unprofitable, which shows that this cohort is valued on pipeline and revenue trajectory rather than earnings multiples. On an enterprise basis, with a ~$436M market cap plus net debt (roughly $125M term debt against $124M cash, so near-neutral net debt), the company trades around 0.5x revenue. That looks optically cheap, but a sub-1x revenue multiple is appropriate, not anomalous, for a diagnostics business with declining-to-flat revenue, negative operating margins, structural reimbursement impairment in a fifth of the base, and a covenant that requires growth the plan does not deliver. The compressed multiple reflects genuine impairment risk rather than a mispricing the market has missed.

Analyst targets span $4.00 low to $10.00 high, with a $6.25 mean and $5.75 median, against a current price of $4.62. The wide dispersion (a 2.5x spread from low to high) reflects the binary, launch-dependent nature of the story rather than a confident base case. We do not treat the mean target as a floor here; on a loss-making, covenant-constrained name with negative longer-horizon revisions, consensus targets are not independent confirmation of upside. We assign a valuation score of 4.0: the multiple is not egregiously stretched, but it reflects structural impairment with no identified near-term catalyst to close the gap to fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

In hereditary cancer, its historical stronghold, Myriad retains brand recognition and broad community reach but faces continuous price erosion and competition from large reference labs and molecular-diagnostics specialists. The volume-up, price-down pattern in hereditary cancer indicates a mature franchise defending share rather than expanding it.

MRD is the key growth battleground, and Myriad is a late entrant (March 2026) against Natera and Guardant, which have established assays, larger clinical-evidence bases, and existing reimbursement. Myriad's differentiation is its community-medicine commercial channel and an ultra-sensitive tumor-informed assay, but it must still prove clinical utility and win coverage against incumbents who got there first. The PATHOMIQ AI prostate-cancer license (enhancing Prolaris) and the SOPHiA GENETICS liquid-biopsy collaboration are sensible bolt-ons, but they are partnerships Myriad licenses rather than proprietary platforms, and they do not change the competitive math materially.

The Mental Health franchise is the clearest negative trajectory: a single product (GeneSight) facing a structural reimbursement loss that management says persists indefinitely, with the risk of additional payer defections. The competitive position is stable-to-deteriorating in the core and unproven in the growth vectors, which supports a below-average score on trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and material, not generic sector truisms:

1. **GeneSight reimbursement loss is structural.** UnitedHealthcare dropped coverage across commercial, exchange, and certain managed Medicaid plans, and management states the impact persists into 2026 and beyond. Other payers could follow, which would deepen the impairment in roughly a fifth of revenue.

2. **The covenant is the sleeper solvency risk.** A sustained shortfall on the MRD/FirstGene launches risks a breach of the escalating minimum-revenue covenant that could accelerate the secured debt.

3. **Goodwill is nearly exhausted.** Two consecutive impairment events drove goodwill from $286M to $47M. Limited remaining downside, but the pattern signals management's internal DCFs of these units keep falling.

4. **Reimbursement/PAMA pressure.** The next PAMA data-reporting window (May–July 2026) could reprice Medicare reimbursement lower; one payer represents 13% of receivables.

5. **Wholesale management turnover and dilution.** Nearly the entire C-suite changed in 2025 (new CEO 4/30/25, new CFO 8/16/25), and shares outstanding rose from 80.6M (2022) to 94.4M (Q1'26), with a new equity plan (~8.6M shares) and ESPP expansion seeking approval, meaningful continued dilution on a sub-$5 stock.

6. **Litigation tail.** A qui tam False Claims Act suit (Carolina Urology Partners) was unsealed in January 2025 and served in April 2025; DOJ intervened to defend the FCA's constitutionality. No accrual is recorded, but the company has a history of large settlements (Crescendo $45.25M in 2022; a $77.5M securities settlement in 2023 that depleted D&O insurance).

### Options Market Signal

No listed options sentiment data was provided. The relevant positioning signal is short interest at 9.3% of float with 4.2 days to cover (8.55M shares short). That is elevated but well below the squeeze-risk threshold, indicating bearish positioning exists but is not crowded. Combined with high realized beta (1.64) and a recent ~20% post-earnings drop, the equity is trading in a distressed, high-volatility regime where price action is dominated by launch-execution and reimbursement headlines rather than steady fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
A new, financially aligned management team executes the Precise MRD and FirstGene launches into commercial traction, MyChoice CDx and the Zejula companion approval drive incremental CDx volume, Mental Health continues to recover (Q1'26 revenue/test +15%), and revenue inflects toward the high end of guidance and beyond. Cost-per-test falls as the consolidated automated lab footprint scales, adjusted EBITDA reaches the $37–49M guided range and grows, the company stays comfortably onside its covenant, and the equity re-rates as a turnaround that worked. In this scenario the stock could reach the upper end of the analyst range near $7–10 as cash burn turns to positive FCF and the covenant overhang lifts.

### Bear Case
GeneSight reimbursement remains permanently impaired and other payers follow, MRD fails to win share against Natera and Guardant, FirstGene slips, revenue stalls near $850–870M, and the company approaches the escalating covenant from the wrong side by 2027–2028, forcing a dilutive raise or a debt renegotiation on unfavorable terms. Continued GAAP losses, cash burn, and dilution compound. In this scenario the equity revisits its 52-week low near $3.53 or lower, given the negative operating leverage and the secured-creditor priority ahead of common.

The asymmetry is not attractive. The bull case requires multiple things to go right simultaneously over a multi-year window, and the bear case is supported by management's own language on GeneSight and by the covenant math. The base case is a stabilizing but still-unprofitable business whose equity stays range-bound between roughly $3.50 and $6 while the launch story plays out, with limited reason to own it ahead of evidence.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: There is no specific, dated near-term catalyst that would force the stock materially lower. Q2'26 earnings (expected early August) could disappoint, and the PAMA data-reporting window (May–July 2026) is a potential negative, but neither is a hard binary with a defined large downside. The covenant risk is real but resolves over a 2027–2029 window, far too distant for a short.

**Timing window**: The covenant constraint is multi-year. Carrying a short for 18–36 months against borrow and squeeze risk while waiting for a covenant problem to crystallize is not viable.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 9.3% of float with 4.2 days to cover. This is elevated but below the >20% squeeze-risk threshold; the position is not crowded, but it also means informed investors have not piled in on the short side.

**Borrow and options signal**: No options data provided to confirm rich put skew or unusual put activity. Absent a clear options confirmation and with management reaffirming guidance, the bearish thesis lacks a near-term accelerant.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: The plausible near-term downside is toward the 52-week low of $3.53 (about −24%), against meaningful upside risk from a launch data readout (AACR, ASCO GU, SGO in 2026), a payer-coverage win, or acquisition interest at a depressed valuation. That asymmetry is below 2:1, and the company's history of being an episodic takeover/turnaround story raises squeeze and event risk.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE**. The bearish view is grounded, but it is a distressed turnaround without a dated near-term catalyst and with event/squeeze risk. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria** — we would revisit constructively if:
1. The company posts two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow with revenue tracking toward the top of guidance, demonstrating the covenant gap is closing on the company's own cash generation rather than on adjusted metrics.
2. Precise MRD secures broad commercial-payer reimbursement or demonstrates clear share gains against Natera/Guardant, and/or GeneSight regains a major payer (reversing the UnitedHealthcare structural loss).
3. Estimate revisions turn decisively positive across the current-year and next-year horizons (currently flat-to-negative), confirming a genuine earnings inflection rather than stabilization off a low base.
4. Management deleverages or refinances the OrbiMed facility on terms that remove the escalating-covenant overhang, and insider conviction appears in the form of C-suite open-market buying (only a single ~$28K director purchase exists today).

**Timeline**: We expect the headwinds — structural GeneSight reimbursement loss, cash burn, the covenant constraint, and competitive MRD entry — to persist through at least 2026–2027. The next natural reassessment point is Q2'26 earnings (early August 2026), with the FY26 covenant test at year-end 2026 a key checkpoint. Until there is evidence the launches are gaining reimbursed traction and FCF is turning positive, the equity remains a leveraged bet we prefer to watch rather than own.

## Conclusion

Myriad Genetics is a recognizable diagnostics brand wrapped around a financially distressed turnaround. Revenue has stagnated, GAAP losses and cash burn are ongoing, equity has halved on impairments, and the company carries an expensive secured loan with a revenue covenant that escalates faster than its own growth plan. The most important fundamental risk, the GeneSight reimbursement loss, is structural by management's own admission, and the growth that would satisfy the covenant depends on two late-entry launches winning against entrenched, better-capitalized competitors. Near-term liquidity means there is no imminent default, and a new, genuinely pay-aligned management team is a modest positive. But the risk/reward does not justify ownership: the bull case needs many things to break right over a multi-year window, and the bear case is supported by the company's own disclosures. We rate MYGN **AVOID** with an overall score of 3.83. It is not an active short — there is no dated near-term catalyst and the position is not crowded — but it is a stock to leave alone until the launches show reimbursed traction, free cash flow turns positive, and the covenant overhang is resolved.
