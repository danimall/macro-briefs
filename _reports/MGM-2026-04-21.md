## Executive Summary

MGM Resorts International is a $10.2B market cap global gaming operator trading at $39.79, approximately 52x trailing earnings and 21.5x FY2026E consensus EPS of $1.85. The stock has rallied 36% from its April 2025 lows but remains 2% below its 52-week high. This rally has occurred against a backdrop of deteriorating fundamentals: Las Vegas Strip revenue declined 4% in FY2025 (RevPAR down 6.5%), operating income has fallen 47% over two years, and analyst EPS estimates have been cut 15-23% across all time horizons over the last 90 days. The company's Altman Z-Score sits at 0.65, deep in distress territory, driven by a lease-heavy capital structure that creates $2.15B in annual non-negotiable financial obligations.

Free cash flow of $1.46B (8.3% margin) is the strongest feature of the financial profile, but this must fund ~$575M/year in Osaka equity calls through 2028, ~$1B in maintenance CapEx, and whatever share repurchase activity management continues to prioritize over balance sheet repair. Barry Diller's IAC has accumulated ~24% of outstanding shares and is still buying, creating a strategic overhang that could resolve positively (takeout premium) or negatively (governance conflict). With consumer confidence at record lows, oil above $95, and Las Vegas visitor traffic down 8% in 2025, the macro environment is hostile to discretionary consumer spending, and MGM's fixed-cost structure amplifies the downside in any further deterioration.

## Company Overview

MGM operates through four segments: Las Vegas Strip Resorts (~48% of revenue, including Bellagio, MGM Grand, Aria, The Cosmopolitan), Regional Operations (~22%, including MGM National Harbor, Borgata), MGM China (~25%, MGM Macau and MGM Cotai), and MGM Digital (~4%, LeoVegas and related online operations). The company also holds a 50% stake in BetMGM, a sports betting and iGaming joint venture with Entain plc.

MGM does not own its domestic real estate. All domestic gaming facilities are leased from VICI Properties under triple net leases requiring approximately $1.8B in annual rent, with the tenant responsible for taxes, insurance, maintenance, and capital expenditures. This structure was created through the 2022 sale of MGM Growth Properties to VICI. The company is also building an integrated resort in Osaka, Japan (43.5% equity stake, JV with ORIX), with ~$2.3B in remaining equity commitments through 2028.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue has grown from $13.1B (2022) to $17.5B (2025), a 10.1% CAGR, but growth decelerated sharply: from 23.1% (2023) to 6.7% (2024) to 1.7% (2025). The composition of the 2025 result is concerning. Las Vegas Strip revenue declined 4%, the first such decline since the post-COVID recovery. RevPAR fell from $245 to $229 (down 6.5%), driven by ADR compression ($260 → $249) and occupancy slippage (94% → 92%). Rooms revenue dropped 9%.

The growth that did occur came from MGM China (+11%, benefiting from continued Macau visitor recovery) and MGM Digital (+19%, off a small base). Regional operations were approximately flat.

Profitability has deteriorated materially:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|
| Operating Income | $1.89B | $1.49B | $1.00B |
| Operating Margin | 11.7% | 8.6% | 5.7% |
| Net Income (attributable) | $1.14B | $0.75B | $0.21B |
| Net Margin | 7.1% | 4.3% | 1.2% |
| EPS (diluted) | ~$3.18 | ~$2.41 | ~$0.74 |

Operating income declined 47% over two years. Contributors include a $279M goodwill impairment (Empire City + Push Gaming), $93M in additional Empire City write-downs, a $186M increase in D&A from completed capital projects, and a $288M FX loss on USD-denominated debt held by a foreign subsidiary. Adjusted EBITDA was more stable at $2.43B, up 0.6% YoY, but the mix shifted unfavorably, with Las Vegas Strip EBITDAR declining $249M while MGM China made up most of the offset.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $2.53B in 2025, up from $2.36B the prior year. Free cash flow of $1.46B represents an 8.3% margin. CapEx was $1.07B, guided to $950M-$1.05B for 2026. Cash flow quality is solid — the conversion rate of approximately 7x net income to OCF is high, though it reflects the low quality of reported earnings rather than exceptional cash generation.

### Balance Sheet

The Altman Z-Score of 0.65 sits deep in the distress zone (<1.8). This is structurally driven by the lease-heavy model, which creates massive right-of-use lease liabilities and a stockholders' equity-to-total-assets ratio of only 5.9%. Long-term debt is $6.23B (including $2.5B at MGM China), with $2.06B in cash. Net debt is approximately $4.2B. Debt-to-equity is 15.7x.

The critical number is $2.15B in annual fixed financial obligations: $1.8B in rent (with annual escalators) plus ~$350M in interest. Add ~$1B in CapEx and ~$575M/year in Osaka equity calls, and total committed cash outlays reach approximately $3.7B against $2.53B in operating cash flow. The arithmetic only closes because of timing flexibility on CapEx and the expectation that operations remain healthy.

Stockholders' equity has shrunk from $4.83B (2022) to $2.43B (2025), a 50% reduction in three years. Retained earnings declined from $4.79B to $2.11B despite positive net income — the difference is aggressive buybacks that exceeded earnings. Share count fell from 409M to 275M over the same period (33% reduction).

### Earnings Trajectory

The quarterly earnings record is mixed: 2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 significant miss (Q3 2025: -40.2% surprise) over the last four quarters. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative:

- Current quarter: -22.6% over 90 days ($0.66 → $0.51)
- Current year: -15.3% over 90 days ($2.18 → $1.85)
- Next year: -12.1% over 90 days ($2.65 → $2.32)

The 30-day revisions show deceleration (-12.6% current quarter, -3.4% current year), suggesting the pace of cuts may be slowing, but estimates are still moving lower.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are limited and uncertain across all segments:

**Las Vegas Strip:** Visitor volume declined 8% in 2025. MGM's response — launching all-inclusive packages at Luxor and Excalibur from $330/night — signals management expects continued softness. Caesars is pursuing similar tactics, confirming this is industry-wide. The macro environment (record-low consumer confidence, elevated energy costs, rising inflation) works against discretionary Las Vegas spending.

**Macau:** MGM China revenue grew 11% in 2025 as Macau visitor arrivals increased 15%. Recovery from the post-COVID/anti-junket disruption continues, but all six concessionaires are competing aggressively for the same mass-market customer pool. The Macau concession expires December 31, 2032, with the government having a redemption option starting 2031.

**Digital:** LeoVegas grew 19% to $654M but the segment's EBITDAR loss widened from -$77M to -$90M. BetMGM turned profitable ($60M income vs. -$110M loss), but lowered its 2026 revenue outlook. One digital reporting unit's fair value exceeds carrying value by only 7% on $341M in goodwill, near the impairment threshold.

**Osaka:** The integrated resort under construction won't generate revenue until 2029-2030 at the earliest. Remaining equity commitment of ~$2.3B carries construction cost inflation risk. Japan has no demonstrated market for integrated resorts at this scale.

Consensus expects EPS to recover from $0.74 (2025) to $1.85 (2026E) to $2.32 (2027E), implying a near-threefold recovery in net income. This requires Las Vegas Strip stabilization, continued Macau growth, reduced impairment charges, and better FX outcomes, none of which are confirmed by current data.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.79, MGM trades at:

- **52x trailing EPS** ($0.76)
- **21.5x FY2026E EPS** ($1.85) — assuming estimates hold
- **17.2x FY2027E EPS** ($2.32)
- **~4.2x EV/EBITDA** on a $2.43B EBITDA base (depends on lease liability treatment)

Against the peer median P/E of 21.9x, MGM appears roughly in line on forward estimates. However, GAP (12.2x) and Penske (11.0x) offer significantly cheaper discretionary exposure, and TXRH (26.2x) and NCLH (21.9x) have cleaner growth trajectories. The peer set is heterogeneous — gaming operators trade differently from retailers and restaurants — but the comparison illustrates that MGM's forward multiple is not discounting the downside risks embedded in the estimate revision trajectory.

The analyst consensus median target is $43.00, with a range of $30.00-$62.00. At $39.79, the stock is already 93% of the way to the median target, leaving only 8% upside to consensus. With 3 sell ratings out of 22 analysts, the distribution is not uniformly constructive.

The valuation case requires believing that (a) the $0.76 trailing EPS is a trough, (b) estimates stop falling, and (c) the Las Vegas Strip stabilizes. None of these conditions are confirmed by current data.

## Competitive Landscape

**Las Vegas Strip:** MGM and Caesars dominate the Strip, but the market is softening. Both operators resorting to all-inclusive promotional pricing simultaneously confirms the visitor volume decline is structural for now. Regional competition is intensifying: tribal gaming expansion in Florida, new commercial gaming in multiple states, and three new integrated resort licenses in New York that will feature live-dealer table games, directly threatening MGM's Northeast properties (Borgata, Empire City/Yonkers). MGM's withdrawal from the NYC casino race was a permanent loss of access to the most valuable new gaming market in the U.S.

**Macau:** All six concessionaires compete for the mass-market customer base after the elimination of gaming promoters. MGM China holds a smaller market share than Sands China or Galaxy Entertainment. The concession renewal process adds long-term uncertainty.

**Digital:** BetMGM competes against DraftKings and FanDuel, which have larger market shares and greater promotional spending capacity. The 10-K identifies prediction markets (e.g., Polymarket) as an emerging competitive threat.

**M&A:** Reports of a potential $7B bid by Tilman Fertitta for Caesars would, if completed, create a combined competitor with significant Strip and regional scale advantages.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Consumer Spending Deterioration (HIGH).** Michigan consumer sentiment at 47.6 (record low), inflation accelerating, oil above $95. The 8% visitor volume decline in 2025 occurred before the current escalation in energy costs and geopolitical disruption. Further deterioration is the base case.

**2. Fixed Obligation Structure (HIGH).** A 15-20% revenue decline would create a severe cash squeeze given the ~$3.7B in committed annual outlays against $2.53B OCF. The leases contain financial covenants requiring 6 months to 2 years of rent as collateral if breached.

**3. Osaka Execution Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH).** Construction cost inflation risk on the remaining $2.3B commitment in a market with no proven demand. This is an illiquid, foreign-currency-denominated obligation that cannot be easily unwound.

**4. Estimate Trajectory (HIGH).** Estimates have fallen 12-23% across all horizons over 90 days and are still declining. The Q3 2025 miss (-40.2%) demonstrated the earnings volatility inherent in the business.

**5. FX Exposure (MEDIUM).** The $288M FX loss in 2025 was material. With yen credit facilities for Osaka and CNY revenue from Macau, significant currency risk persists.

**6. Governance/IAC Overhang (MEDIUM).** IAC at ~24% with a 25% voting cap creates uncertainty. This could resolve as a strategic positive (takeout, activism) or negative (board conflict, strategic disagreement).

**7. VICI Shortfall Guarantees.** MGM guarantees shortfall on ~$6.0B in VICI property-level debt. If property values decline below debt levels, MGM is liable.

### Options Market Signal

Implied volatility is elevated: near-term ATM IV of 49.3% versus 36.7% historical vol, a 12.6pp premium. The term structure is in backwardation (49.3% near-term vs. 35.9% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing a near-term catalyst or stress event rather than sustained uncertainty. Put/call skew is elevated at 8.4%, confirming directional put demand. The OI put/call ratio of 0.57 is moderate, suggesting institutional hedging rather than speculative put buying. The implied move of ±3.7% on the nearest expiry reflects substantial uncertainty around the current price level. Short interest at 15.8% of float (4.9 days to cover) confirms meaningful institutional bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($50-55, ~26-38% upside)
- Las Vegas Strip stabilizes in H2 2026 as promotional tactics restore occupancy and RevPAR bottoms
- Macau recovery continues at double-digit rates, lifting consolidated EBITDA toward $2.7-2.8B
- BetMGM generates $100M+ in annual distributions
- IAC/Diller's accumulation results in strategic transaction or M&A premium
- Share buybacks continue to support EPS growth despite flat/declining EBITDA
- Osaka proceeds on schedule and on budget, creating a long-dated growth option

### Bear Case ($24-28, ~30-40% downside)
- Consumer recession drives Las Vegas visitor volumes down an additional 10-15%, compressing Strip RevPAR below $200
- Fixed obligations create cash flow crisis as committed outlays exceed declining OCF
- Osaka construction costs escalate; yen strengthens against USD, increasing the equity call
- FY2026 EPS prints below $1.50
- Additional digital goodwill impairment ($341M at risk)
- VICI shortfall guarantees triggered by declining property valuations

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Continued estimate revisions lower ahead of Q1 2026 earnings (expected late April / early May 2026). If Las Vegas Strip trends don't improve, the next earnings print could miss the current $0.51 estimate, triggering another leg down in estimates and repricing the forward P/E.

**Timing window:** 2-6 weeks until Q1 2026 earnings.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 15.8% of float with 4.9 days to cover is meaningful but not extreme. The stock is only 2% below its 52-week high after a 36% rally, suggesting shorts have been punished recently. IAC's continued buying creates a structural bid that limits downside velocity and increases squeeze risk.

**Borrow and options signal:** The backwardated IV structure and elevated put skew (8.4%) confirm bearish positioning. However, rich IV means put options are expensive, reducing risk/reward for directional put strategies. The borrow may be elevated given the SI level.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target of $28-30 implies 25-30% downside from current levels. Bull case risk is $50-55 (26-38% upside). This produces a risk/reward ratio of approximately 0.8:1 to 1.2:1, below the 2:1 threshold required for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is directionally correct — deteriorating fundamentals, expensive valuation, negative estimate momentum — but IAC's 24% ownership creates a structural floor, the 15.8% SI means the short is already moderately crowded, and the risk/reward is unfavorable given the recent 36% rally. The correct action is to avoid owning, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** (1) Las Vegas Strip RevPAR stabilizes or improves for two consecutive quarters. (2) Estimate revisions flatten or turn positive. (3) Stock retreats to $28-30, where the forward P/E on stabilized estimates (~$2.00 EPS) would approach 14-15x, providing a margin of safety. (4) Resolution of IAC/Diller strategic intentions (takeout bid, board seats, or exit).

**Timeline:** The consumer spending headwinds and Las Vegas Strip softness are likely to persist for at least 2-3 more quarters given the current macro trajectory. Osaka will remain a cash drain through 2028. Reassess after Q2 2026 earnings (expected July-August 2026) for evidence of Strip stabilization.

## Conclusion

MGM Resorts trades at 52x trailing earnings and 21.5x forward estimates that are still falling. The core Las Vegas Strip business is declining for the first time since COVID, with management resorting to promotional all-inclusive pricing. The balance sheet carries $2.15B in annual fixed obligations with minimal equity cushion (Altman Z-Score 0.65), and the company has committed $2.3B more to an unproven Japanese casino project. Free cash flow of $1.46B is real but consumed by structural obligations. IAC's accumulation at 24% creates optionality but also governance uncertainty. With consumer confidence at record lows, energy costs elevated, and estimate revisions uniformly negative, the risk-reward at current prices is unfavorable. AVOID.
