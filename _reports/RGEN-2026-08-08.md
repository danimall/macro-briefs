## Executive Summary

Repligen has delivered the operating recovery bulls have waited three years for. Q2 2026 revenue of $204.1M grew 12% reported and 13% organic, adjusted operating margin expanded 470 basis points to 16.7%, adjusted EPS of $0.54 beat consensus by 18%, and management raised FY2026 organic growth guidance to 10.5–13.5% with adjusted EPS to $2.03–$2.09. That is the fourth consecutive beat, averaging roughly 14% above consensus. Gross margin has moved from 43.3% in FY2024 to 54.8% in H1 2026 as the company consolidates a manufacturing footprint of about 19 sites. Three of four franchises are compounding at 11–50%.

The problem is price. At $163.90 the stock trades at 79.6x the midpoint of FY2026 *adjusted* EPS guidance, 62x next-year consensus of $2.64, roughly 11.0x enterprise value to guided FY2026 revenue, and about 53x FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA. FY2025 free cash flow of $91.7M is a 0.99% yield on market capitalization. Consensus annual estimates have risen only about 3% over 90 days while the shares have risen 59% from the May 14 low of $100.99 and 23% since late July — the multiple, not the earnings stream, did the work. And the recovery being capitalized is exactly that: FY2026 guidance of roughly $824M would be the first year above the FY2022 peak of $801.5M, a cumulative 2.8% over four years, or about 0.7% annually. Nearly half of revenue — Filtration & Fluid Management at $95.7M of $204.1M product revenue — grew 1.0% year over year, and European product revenue fell 6.1%.

Two further items compress the margin of safety. First, internal control over financial reporting was not effective at December 31, 2025, and the material weaknesses in IT general controls and in business-process controls over **inventory valuation and the financial statement close** remained unremediated at June 30, 2026, following restatements of five 10-Qs spanning Q1 2023 through Q2 2024 plus items in the FY2023 10-K. The account with the acknowledged deficiency is the account producing the gross-margin recovery. Second, the pending $1.5B BioLife Solutions acquisition (announced July 21, 2026, targeted Q4 2026 close) consumes roughly $545M of cash, issues about 6.98M shares (a 12.4% increase in share count; existing holders diluted to 89.0% ownership), and on reproducible arithmetic is approximately EPS-neutral in year one — while shifting the balance sheet from roughly $210M net cash to roughly $300M net debt against $600M of convertible notes whose principal must be settled in cash by December 15, 2028. We move to REDUCE from HOLD: base case $168 versus a bear case of $115.

## Company Overview

Repligen supplies the consumables and hardware used to manufacture biologic drugs — Protein A affinity ligands, OPUS pre-packed chromatography columns, XCell ATF cell-retention devices, single-use flow paths, filtration membranes and process-analytical instruments. Customers are large biopharmaceutical companies, CDMOs and integrators. The company does not develop drugs. It reports as one bioprocessing business and, notably for impairment purposes, as **one reportable segment and one reporting unit**.

Four franchises, with FY2025 and Q2 2026 detail:

| Franchise | FY2025 revenue | FY2025 growth | Q2 2026 | Q2 YoY | H1 2026 YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Filtration & Fluid Management | $402.8M | +8.0% | $95.7M | **+1.0%** | +2.5% |
| Chromatography | $153.2M | +24.7% | $51.9M | +11.3% | +17.7% |
| Process Analytics | $81.2M | +37.0% | $25.8M | +31.2% | +40.4% |
| Proteins | $97.4M | +30.9% | $31.4M | **+50.1%** | +29.0% |

FY2025 revenue was $738.3M, up 16.4%. H1 2026 was $398.4M, up 13.3% reported and roughly 12% organic. Management defines its addressable market at roughly $13B within an approximately $20B bioprocessing market, putting Repligen at about 6% of its own defined TAM — a specialist competing against Danaher (Cytiva), Thermo Fisher, MilliporeSigma and Sartorius, all of which the 10-K concedes can bundle products and outspend Repligen on development and sales.

The economic moat is designed-in specification: consumables written into customers' regulatory filings and validated processes are costly to switch once a drug reaches commercial manufacture. Repligen does not disclose a recurring-revenue percentage or installed base, so this is inference from product architecture rather than a reported metric.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: recovery, not compounding

The revenue path is the canonical bioprocessing destocking shape: $801.5M (FY2022 COVID peak) → roughly $639M (FY2023) → $634.4M (FY2024 trough) → $738.3M (FY2025) → $813–835M guided (FY2026). The midpoint of guidance, about $824M, exceeds the 2022 peak by 2.8% four years later. That does not make the current re-acceleration fake — 13% organic growth off the trough is genuine and above the mid-to-high-single-digit growth typical of the bioprocessing market. It does mean investors underwriting a premium multiple are underwriting the *durability* of a cyclical recovery, not a demonstrated multi-year compounding record.

### Margins: real improvement, obscured by GAAP noise

Gross margin: 43.3% (FY2024, depressed by roughly $36.1M of inventory and restructuring-related charges routed through cost of goods) → 52.3% (FY2025) → 53.9% in Q2 2026 versus 51.0%, and 54.8% in H1. FY2026 guided adjusted gross margin is 53.7–54.2%.

Operating results diverge sharply by measure:

| Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025 | GAAP | Adjusted |
|---|---|---|
| Operating margin | 6.8% vs 7.6% | 16.7% vs 12.0% |
| Operating income | $13.98M vs $13.90M | $34.0M vs $21.9M |
| Net income | $5.01M vs $14.87M | $30.8M vs $21.2M |
| Diluted EPS | $0.09 vs $0.26 | $0.54 vs $0.37 |

Three separable items drive the gap. A contingent-consideration swing dominates: Q2 2025 carried a $7.94M benefit (mark-down of the Tantti earnout) against a $2.31M charge in Q2 2026, a $10.2M swing equal to 5.5 points of operating margin by the company's own reconciliation. The Q2 effective tax rate was 64.6% versus 18.4%, attributed to a discrete item on the Polymem divestiture (H1 ETR was 16.2%; FY2026 guided adjusted rate is about 22%). And H1 included a $13.66M loss on the Polymem sale plus roughly $9.5M per quarter of intangible amortization.

A GAAP-only screen reads Q2 net income down 66% and concludes the business is deteriorating. The opposite is happening operationally. But the adjusted bridge is unusually long: $20.0M of add-backs against $14.0M of GAAP operating income, including restructuring that has recurred since 2023, acquisition and integration costs since 2021, and "transformation costs" across 2025–2026. Durable earnings power sits between the two measures — closer to adjusted after excluding amortization, but with a normalized allowance for the restructuring and integration spend that is the running cost of an acquisitive model. One point in management's favor: the $2.949M of Q2 transformation costs is net of a $0.8M benefit from selling previously reserved inventory ($1.7M in H1), which the company removes from adjusted operating income rather than pocketing. That same mechanic flatters GAAP gross margin by roughly 40–60 basis points.

### Cash flow and balance sheet

FY2025 operating cash flow was $117.4M against $25.7M of capex, giving $91.7M of free cash flow. H1 2026 produced $61.1M of operating cash flow and $11.0M of capex for $50.0M of free cash flow. FY2024's higher $175.4M of operating cash flow was substantially a working-capital release, including a $56.9M inventory decrease of which roughly $36.1M related to prior restructuring charges; FY2025 reversed that as inventory rebuilt. H1 2026 absorbed a $20.2M inventory build inside a $34.9M unfavorable working-capital swing, with inventory rising from $170.5M to $186.6M in six months. We do not annualize H1 free cash flow. As a labeled sensitivity: if H2 working capital is neutral and adjusted EBITDA lands near the guided approximately $171M, free cash flow could reach $110–130M after roughly $22M of capex and about $6M of cash coupon — contingent on the inventory build stopping.

At June 30, 2026: cash $606.8M plus marketable securities $203.7M equals $810.4M of liquidity against $600.0M principal of 1.00% convertible notes due December 15, 2028 (carrying value $551.0M, effective rate 4.39%, conversion price $203.06, roughly 19% out of the money and not conditionally convertible in Q3 2026). Net cash is therefore about $210M. There is no bank debt, no revolver, and no disclosed financial covenants. Operating lease liabilities total $139.9M. Total equity is $2,112.0M against $1,104.2M of goodwill and $357.6M of net intangibles, leaving roughly $650M of tangible equity.

Two convert mechanics matter. Repligen may call the notes for cash on or after December 18, 2026, and on conversion must pay at least the lesser of $1,000 per note and the daily conversion value in cash — principal is a cash obligation, with only the above-par excess settleable in stock. A triggered conversion feature would reclassify principal to current liabilities, which the risk factors flag as a material reduction in net working capital.

Capital efficiency is unimpressive relative to the multiple. FY2026 guided adjusted operating income of roughly $131M against approximately $1.9B of invested capital (equity plus debt less cash) is a pretax return near 7%.

## Growth Analysis

Three engines are working. Process Analytics grew 31.2% in Q2 and 40.4% in H1, aided by the 908 Devices PAT portfolio purchase. Chromatography grew 11.3% and 17.7%. Proteins grew 50.1% and 29.0%. Management characterizes order momentum from Q1 as continuing into Q2 with "sequential acceleration."

Two offsets deserve equal weight. Filtration & Fluid Management, at 46.9% of Q2 product revenue, grew 1.0%. Part of that is the March 30, 2026 Polymem divestiture, which the company quantifies at roughly one point of total-company reported headwind concentrated in this franchise, so organic Filtration growth is meaningfully above the reported figure — but it is not derivable from disclosed data, and Filtration is the most capital-equipment-exposed and most directly contested franchise. Europe, at 32% of Q2 revenue, declined 6.1% while APAC and rest-of-world grew 43.3% and North America 17.3%. A European decline may be a demand signal or order timing; disclosed data does not resolve which. [unverified — no world-model or macro source in brief]

The Proteins surge is not diversification. Protein A ligands are sold into chromatography resin manufacturers — a small number of OEM counterparties that overlap with named competitors. The 10-K warns that if Repligen products compete with those of its largest customers, "these customers may place fewer orders." Direct concentration measures are flat to slightly worse: no customer exceeded 10% of revenue in FY2025 or H1 2026, but one customer represented 10% of trade receivables at June 30, 2026 versus none at December 31, 2025. A single quarter-end receivable concentration can be shipment timing, but it is not evidence of improving diversification.

Estimate revisions are supportive but modest, not an inflection signal: current-year consensus $2.06 (+3.1% over 30 days, +3.2% over 90), next-year $2.64 (+3.6%, +3.4%). Current-quarter estimates were cut 10.5% over 90 days and next-quarter 2.6%, indicating phasing rather than deterioration given the annual raises. This is a +3% revision profile, not the +30% velocity that signals systematic underestimation.

### The BioLife acquisition

BioLife Solutions supplies biopreservation media used to freeze, store and ship cell therapies — consumables embedded in approved regulatory filings, the same specified-in model applied to cell therapy. FY2025 BioLife revenue was $96.2M, up 17.0% from the post-divestiture $82.3M base, at a 64.6% gross margin, with a GAAP operating loss of $16.6M, SG&A at 47% of revenue, $20.1M of operating cash flow, $10.6M of capex-adjusted free cash flow, $33.0M of cash and zero long-term debt.

Consideration is $11.25 cash plus 0.1442 RGEN shares per BioLife share, valuing BioLife at $31.00 per share, a 6.2% premium, with at least $20M of first-year cost savings targeted. Derived arithmetic: roughly 48.4M BioLife shares implies about $545M of cash and 6.98M Repligen shares (+12.4% share count; ownership dilution to 89.0%, excluding unquantified rollover of BioLife equity awards). The exchange ratio appears fixed with no disclosed collar; at the implied reference price of $136.96, the stock component was worth about $956M, and at $163.90 it is worth roughly $1.14B, raising effective consideration to about $1.69B, or 17.6x trailing sales versus 15.6x at announcement. Repligen is paying roughly 15–18x sales using equity valued at about 9x sales on the reference-price basis — partially justified by BioLife's higher growth and 11 points more gross margin, but EV/sales-dilutive on the equity component.

Year-one accretion is not obvious. Using an estimated $20M of BioLife adjusted EBITDA (unverified; derived from $20.1M of operating cash flow against a $16.6M GAAP operating loss), plus $20M of synergies, less roughly $3M of depreciation and $17–19M of forgone investment income on $545M at H1's realized 3.2% yield, incremental after-tax contribution is roughly $15M. Against $117M of standalone adjusted net income on 63.7M pro forma shares, that produces approximately $2.07 versus $2.06 standalone. The deal is unambiguously accretive to revenue growth and gross margin; the EPS claim likely rests on a later-year basis, a higher BioLife non-GAAP EBITDA definition than we can verify, or synergies above $20M. The Form S-4 pro formas are the resolving document.

## Competitive Landscape

Repligen competes as a specialist against four integrated platform vendors that its own risk factors describe as better capitalized, able to bundle, and able to outspend on development and marketing. Its moat rests substantially on trade secrets and know-how rather than patents — the 10-K names these as "among the most important elements of our proprietary position as it relates to many of the products that currently account for a majority of our revenue." Trade-secret protection is durable until independent development occurs, and it offers no litigation remedy against that outcome.

The Protein A disclosure is specific and adverse: patents held by others directed toward Protein A "have expired or have been invalidated in litigation proceedings," which may induce Repligen "to sell Protein A at lower prices and may erode our market share." The franchise growing 50% is the franchise with a disclosed, active IP-decay path and an OEM channel populated by competitors.

Trajectory reads mixed rather than clearly improving. Process Analytics and Chromatography are gaining, and management asserts market outperformance (plausible at 13% organic versus mid-single-digit market growth, though we obtained no independent third-party market data). Against that, the largest franchise is flat, Europe is contracting, and the company holds roughly 6% of its own defined addressable market against scale players. Competitive position scores 5.5.

BioLife extends the specified-in model into cell therapy with commercial-stage, approved-therapy consumables at 64.6% gross margin — the strongest strategic argument for the deal. The counterweight is Repligen's own new risk-factor language, added in the Q2 10-Q, conceding "the risk that the parties have overestimated the size or trajectory of the cell therapy market and BioLife's market position." Companies rarely volunteer that about a market they just paid $1.5B to enter.

## Risk Assessment

**Unremediated material weaknesses (highest-conviction concern).** ICFR was not effective at December 31, 2025; disclosure controls were not effective at June 30, 2026. The revenue-recognition weakness was remediated, but the IT general controls weakness (logical access and change management, which renders dependent automated and business-process controls ineffective) and the business-process weakness over **inventory valuation and the financial statement close** remain open. Five 10-Q/A amendments filed November 18, 2024 restated Q1 2023 through Q2 2024 plus FY2023 items. The bull case rests on gross margin moving from 43.3% to 54.8% — recorded in the very account with the acknowledged deficiency, and including benefits from selling previously reserved inventory. FY2025 statements were audited and management asserts interim statements are fairly stated; we allege no misstatement. We do flag elevated measurement uncertainty on the *precision* of the margin trend and elevated restatement tail risk, executed simultaneously with an S-4 registration and the consolidation of a second public company's ledgers.

**BIS export-control self-disclosures.** The 10-K states Repligen "may have exported certain products without a required export license in apparent violation of U.S. export control laws" and has submitted voluntary self-disclosures to the Bureau of Industry and Security. No accrual, penalty range or timeline is disclosed; Item 3 reports no material proceedings. Voluntary disclosure typically mitigates penalties substantially, but the tail is open and unquantified.

**Balance-sheet transition.** Post-close liquidity of roughly $300M (before H2 free cash generation) against $600M of converts due December 2028 requiring cash settlement of principal. Manageable, and callable for cash from December 18, 2026, but it removes the optionality that funded the tuck-in strategy.

**M&A track record.** Polymem was sold March 30, 2026 for $3.6M net with a $13.7M loss on sale. Tantti has paid $5.2M of a maximum $54.5M undiscounted earnout with $3.0M of fair value remaining, implying performance well below acquisition-case targets. The 908 Devices PAT purchase has absorbed $13.8M of transaction and integration costs on a $69.9M price. A second consecutive multi-year restructuring program, running to end-2027, began in Q1 2026 ($4.2M H1 charges).

**Goodwill — smaller risk than the balance sheet implies.** Goodwill plus intangibles of $1,461.8M equals 49.6% of assets and 69.2% of equity, and BioLife will add well over $1.1B. But with one reporting unit, the ASC 350 test compares total-company fair value (about $9.2B) to carrying value (about $2.1B) — roughly a 4.4x cushion. Near-term goodwill impairment would require market capitalization to fall toward book value. Finite-lived intangibles are tested separately and more exposed, with about $38M of annual amortization already in the base case.

**Other:** 39.5% of FY2025 revenue in foreign currency; the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating IEEPA tariffs creates two-sided policy uncertainty; a single-source supplier constraint on XCell ATF systems.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is bullish to the point of complacency and offers no confirmation of downside protection. Put/call open-interest ratio is 0.06 and volume ratio 0.03 — the lowest readings in any name we track. Since the July 24 snapshot, ATM IV fell 17.7 points from 63.6% to 45.9% while the price rose 22.8%, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 1.99 to 0.06: hedges bought ahead of the deal and the print were closed out afterward. IV at 45.9% against 46.5% trailing realized is fairly priced with a flat term structure and a 19th-percentile IV reading within the chain, so the market prices no near-term regime break. The nearest expiry implies ±7.1% over 12 days; the November tenor implies ±20.8%.

Two divergences are worth holding. Short interest of 10.3% of float and 5.8 days to cover indicates real institutional bearish positioning that the options market is not corroborating — protection has been sold into strength precisely as the multiple reached its highest point of the recovery. Absolute IV near 46% on a 60x-earnings name means the option market expects a wide distribution while pricing no directional bias; for holders trimming, near-dated calls are not expensive relative to realized volatility.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $205 (+25.1%)

Bioprocessing demand recovery has multiple years to run and Repligen outgrows the market at 13–15% organic. Filtration's 1.0% print is largely the Polymem divestiture drag plus a tough comparison, and reaccelerates to high single digits in 2027. Footprint consolidation and the 2026–2027 restructuring lift adjusted operating margin from the guided 15.7–16.0% toward 20%. BioLife delivers 17%+ growth at 64.6% gross margin, synergies exceed $20M, its standalone adjusted EBITDA proves materially above our $20M estimate, and the deal is genuinely accretive from year one. Estimate revisions continue at +3–5% per quarter as the beat streak extends to eight. FY2028 pro forma adjusted EPS approaches $2.90–3.00; at 68–70x the stock reaches $200–210. Remediation completes in the FY2026 10-K, removing the governance discount, and the BIS matter closes without material penalty.

### Bear case — $115 (−29.8%)

Filtration's flatness proves structural rather than divestiture-driven as Cytiva and Sartorius bundle against a specialist, and Europe's 6.1% decline extends. Total organic growth decelerates to high single digits in 2027. The Proteins surge decelerates as expired and invalidated third-party Protein A patents invite price competition in the fastest-growing, highest-mix franchise. BioLife integration absorbs management attention while ITGC and financial-close controls remain deficient; integration costs run at 908 Devices-like ratios; a restatement or further control adverse opinion resets the multiple. Post-deal net debt of roughly $300M against a $600M December 2028 maturity removes acquisition optionality and forces either a refinancing at materially higher coupons than the 1.00% cash rate or free cash flow diversion. A derating to 40x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $2.55 gives $102, essentially the May 2026 low. Bear target $115 allows for partial multiple support.

### Base case — $168 (+2.5%)

Organic growth lands at the middle of guidance in 2026 and moderates to 10–12% in 2027 as Filtration stabilizes but does not accelerate. Adjusted margin expands 100–150 basis points annually. BioLife closes in Q4 2026, is EPS-neutral in 2027 and modestly accretive in 2028. Pro forma FY2027 adjusted EPS of roughly $2.75 at 61x equals $168. Control weaknesses are remediated on the FY2026 10-K but the discount persists through the S-4 and first consolidated quarters.

The distribution is unfavorable: 25 points of upside against 30 points of downside, with the base case at the current price and zero yield support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** The change to REDUCE reflects a specific development: since late July at roughly $133, the shares have risen 23% while consensus annual estimates rose approximately 3%. The improvement in operating results is real and we credit it; the entry point has deteriorated faster than the fundamentals have improved.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than around a dated catalyst. The stock is 6.8% below its 52-week high, positioning is one-sided long (put/call OI 0.06), and IV is fairly priced, so exiting into liquidity now carries little timing cost. For holders unwilling to exit fully, reduce to a half-weight position and hold the remainder through the Q3 print and the BioLife close, recognizing that the Form S-4 pro formas are the first document capable of validating or refuting management's accretion claim. Prospective investors should wait.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Price at or below $128–135, which puts pro forma FY2027 adjusted EPS of roughly $2.75 at 47–49x — a multiple consistent with a mid-teens grower carrying an open control deficiency.
2. Form S-4 pro formas or the deal close demonstrating BioLife standalone adjusted EBITDA materially above $20M and clear FY2027 EPS accretion of 5%+.
3. Reported Filtration & Fluid Management growth above 6% for two consecutive quarters, with European product revenue returning to growth.
4. FY2026 Form 10-K reporting ICFR effective with all material weaknesses remediated.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Any new restatement, adverse auditor opinion on ICFR, or additional material weakness identified in the FY2026 10-K or the S-4 process.
2. Disclosure of a quantified BIS penalty or accrual exceeding roughly $25M, or the initiation of a formal enforcement proceeding.
3. FY2027 organic growth guidance below 8%, or a reduction in FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance.
4. Post-close liquidity below $200M combined with any convert-refinancing action at a coupon above 6%.

**Reassessment timeline.** Three concrete decision points: the Form S-4 filing and its pro forma financials (expected before the Q4 close); the Q3 2026 print in late October, which tests whether Filtration and Europe inflected; and the BioLife close plus first consolidated guidance, expected Q4 2026 to early 2027. We will revisit formally at the Q3 print.

## Conclusion

Repligen is a good business at a demanding price with an unresolved accounting-control problem in the account that produces its headline improvement. The Q2 print was strong on every operating measure we track — 13% organic growth, 470 basis points of adjusted operating margin expansion, an 18% EPS beat, a fourth consecutive beat, and raised full-year guidance — and we do not dispute the direction of the recovery.

But at $163.90 the market pays 79.6x FY2026 adjusted EPS, 62x FY2027 consensus, 11.0x EV/revenue and roughly 101x FY2025 free cash flow for a company whose FY2026 guided revenue is 2.8% above its FY2022 peak, whose largest franchise grew 1.0%, whose European revenue fell 6.1%, whose internal controls over inventory valuation and financial close remain deficient, and which is about to spend $545M of cash and issue 12.4% more shares on a deal that our arithmetic shows as approximately EPS-neutral in year one while flipping the balance sheet to net debt ahead of a $600M cash-settled maturity. Consensus estimate revisions of roughly +3% do not support a 59% move off the May low.

Base case $168, bull $205, bear $115. Probability-weighted expected return is approximately zero with no dividend and 46% realized volatility. Trim.
