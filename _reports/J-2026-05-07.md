## Executive Summary

Jacobs Solutions is trading at $122.42, representing a forward P/E of ~16.9x against full-year FY2026 consensus of $7.23 and next-year estimates of $8.26. This compares favorably to select construction & engineering peers, though direct comparisons require careful peer selection given Jacobs' professional-services orientation. The discount reflects legitimate concerns about elevated post-acquisition leverage and GAAP results distorted by separation charges, Amentum mark-to-market losses, and PA-related compensation charges. Underlying operating metrics are stronger: segment margins expanding (I&AF from 7.6% to 8.4% over two years; PA from 20.5% to 22.0%), backlog at $27B (+22% YoY), and data center/AI infrastructure work growing 100%+ off a meaningful base.

The most recent quarter (Q2 FY2026, reported May 5) delivered $1.75 adjusted EPS vs. $1.67 consensus (+5% beat), raised full-year guidance for the second consecutive quarter, and showed backlog accelerating. The stock declined 9.6% on the report due to a GAAP net loss of -$45.88M driven predominantly by PA acquisition and compensation costs, creating what appears to be a sentiment-driven buying opportunity. Analyst consensus targets cluster around $155-160, implying 27-30% upside. With improving segment mix from full PA ownership and AI infrastructure tailwinds still in early stages, the path to $140-155 over 12 months appears well-supported by fundamentals.

## Company Overview

Jacobs Solutions is a $14.4B market cap professional services firm headquartered in Dallas, operating through two segments following its September 2024 separation of Critical Mission Solutions and Cyber & Intelligence businesses (merged with Amentum):

**Infrastructure & Advanced Facilities (I&AF)** — ~89% of revenue ($10.76B FY2025). End-to-end engineering, design, program management, and lifecycle services across transportation, water, energy, life sciences, advanced manufacturing, and cities & places.

**PA Consulting** — ~11% of revenue ($1.27B FY2025, now fully owned after March 20, 2026 buyout). UK-based innovation and transformation consultancy serving defense, health, and public sector clients at ~22% operating margins. PA was already a consolidated segment prior to full ownership; the buyout eliminates the redeemable noncontrolling interest and increases Jacobs' attributable economics.

The business model is capital-light (PP&E $312M against $12B revenue), predominantly cost-reimbursable (68% of contracts), and employs approximately 47,000 people across 40+ countries. U.S. federal government exposure has declined to 8% of revenue, with the majority of work tied to state/local and private-sector infrastructure investment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:** Continuing operations grew from $10.85B (FY2023) to $11.50B (FY2024, +6.0%) to $12.03B (FY2025, +4.6%). Q2 FY2026 gross revenue of $3.695B was up 27.0% YoY, though adjusted net revenue growth was approximately 8.8% YoY — the difference driven by pass-through revenue on cost-reimbursable contracts. Backlog of $26.97B provides significant forward revenue visibility, though the company cautions that backlog is subject to cancellation, scope changes, and is not necessarily indicative of future revenues. Approximately $6.77B is expected to convert within the next fiscal year.

**Profitability:** Segment operating margins have improved steadily — I&AF from 7.6% to 8.4%, PA from 20.5% to 22.0% over two fiscal years. GAAP results are severely distorted: FY2025 net income of $289M (vs. $806M prior year) was depressed by $227M Amentum stock losses, $58.8M separation charges, and a 39.7% effective tax rate inflated by non-deductible items. Company-adjusted EPS of $6.12 excludes recurring amortization and large transaction/compensation adjustments; FY2026 guidance of $7.10-$7.35 (midpoint $7.23, +18% growth) and FY2027 consensus at $8.26 (+14%) reflect the expected underlying trajectory.

**Cash Flow:** FCF of $607M in FY2025 (5.0% margin) on operating cash flow of $687M. Capital return was aggressive: $754M in buybacks plus $153M in dividends ($907M total), more than the full FCF — funded partly by balance sheet capacity. However, H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $103.4M (adjusted FCF of $92.6M for six months after adding back PA incentive-award payouts), reflecting near-term working capital and transaction-related cash flow volatility. The $600M+ annual FCF run-rate may take several quarters to re-establish.

**Balance Sheet:** This is the primary area of concern. Long-term debt stood at $2.24B at FY2025 end, with debt-to-equity of approximately 0.61x. Following the PA buyout in March 2026, Q2 FY2026 long-term debt rose to $4.08B with stockholders' equity of $3.29B, implying debt-to-equity of approximately 1.24x. Cash of $1.37B at Q2 FY2026 implies net debt of approximately $2.71B. Net leverage relative to adjusted EBITDA depends on the annualized denominator used; the company reported Q2 FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $327M and six-month adjusted EBITDA of $630M.

To fund the PA acquisition, Jacobs issued $800M of 4.750% notes due 2031 and $500M of 5.375% notes due 2036 in March 2026, with proceeds used to fund PA cash consideration and repay revolver/term-loan borrowings. The $750M 2025 Term Loan (maturing March 2027) remains a refinancing event, though the successful March 2026 bond issuance demonstrates continued capital markets access.

**Altman Z-Score of 2.08** places the company in the "grey zone." This score should improve as PA earnings fully consolidate and leverage declines from operating cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

Four distinct growth vectors support the forward outlook:

1. **AI/Data Center Infrastructure (fastest growing):** Data center work grew 100%+ YoY in Q2 FY2026. Currently ~3-4% of revenue directly, with total AI infrastructure ecosystem at ~10% growing 40%+ annually. Management describes this as "still in early stages" with the engineering/design phase of data center buildout being a critical bottleneck that Jacobs is positioned to serve.

2. **Infrastructure Super-Cycle:** IIJA, CHIPS Act, and IRA funding continues flowing to state/local projects. Water infrastructure (Jacobs' leading practice), transportation, and grid modernization represent multi-year demand drivers. Backlog growth of 22% YoY provides hard evidence of pipeline conversion.

3. **PA Consulting Full Ownership:** Full ownership eliminates the redeemable noncontrolling interest and increases Jacobs' attributable economics from PA's 22% operating margins (vs. I&AF at 8.4%). The advisory capability combined with engineering depth creates a differentiated offering versus pure-play competitors. Management expects at least $20M per year in transaction cost synergies within 24 months.

4. **Separation Restructuring:** Jacobs disclosed $165-200M in expected gross annualized pre-tax savings from separation restructuring activities, which were expected to be substantially completed by the end of calendar 2025. Additionally, PA Consulting restructuring activities (expected to produce $50-65M in annualized savings) were substantially completed per the FY2025 10-K. Much of these savings are likely already embedded in current run-rate results and FY2026 guidance; the incremental contribution to future earnings beyond consensus is uncertain.

Estimate revisions support this trajectory: FY2026 consensus has risen 1.4% in 30 days, FY2027 up 2.9%. These are modest revisions consistent with a gradually improving narrative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $122.42, Jacobs trades at:
- 16.9x forward FY2026E EPS ($7.23)
- 47.4x trailing P/E (based on reported $2.58 GAAP — heavily distorted by one-time items)
- ~14.8x P/E on FY2027E ($8.26 consensus, ~14% growth)
- ~9.5x EV/EBITDA on estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA

Peer comparisons require careful selection. Specialty electrical/mechanical contractors with AI/data-center momentum (EME, FIX, PWR) trade at significantly higher multiples — Yahoo Finance data suggests forward P/Es ranging from approximately 27x to 44x — but they are not direct professional-services comparables. More relevant peers include AECOM (ACM) and Tetra Tech (TTEK), which trade at forward P/Es in the 18-22x range. Against this professional-services peer set, Jacobs' 16.9x forward P/E represents a modest discount that reflects its elevated leverage and GAAP earnings noise.

Analyst consensus target of approximately $156 (average) implies 27% upside. Yahoo Finance data shows a low analyst target of approximately $126, only slightly above the current price. This suggests limited further de-rating risk from current levels absent fundamental deterioration.

Applying a conservative 16x multiple to FY2027 EPS of $8.26 yields $132. At 18x FY2027E ($8.26) — in line with professional-services peers — the stock reaches $149.

## Competitive Landscape

Jacobs occupies a differentiated position in the construction & engineering services market. The combination of deep technical engineering capability (I&AF) with strategic advisory (PA Consulting) is unusual — most competitors are pure-play engineers (AECOM, Tetra Tech, WSP) or pure-play consultants (McKinsey, Bain). This integration allows Jacobs to capture projects from strategy through implementation.

The contract mix is deliberately conservative: 68% cost-reimbursable with virtually zero fixed-price at-risk construction. This reduces earnings volatility and execution risk versus peers with material construction exposure.

Voluntary turnover at 8.2% is healthy for professional services, suggesting the company can retain technical talent in a competitive market. With approximately 47,000 employees across 40+ countries, scale provides advantages in pursuing large, complex multi-year programs.

The data center/AI infrastructure opportunity is creating a competitive advantage for firms with power engineering, water cooling, and site design capabilities. Jacobs' breadth across these disciplines positions it as a one-stop provider for hyperscalers, though competition from AECOM and others is intensifying in this space.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage Risk (Primary Concern):** The $750M 2025 Term Loan maturing March 2027 creates a refinancing event. FCF generation capability of $600M+/year provides reasonable debt service coverage, though H1 FY2026 cash flow was negative due to transaction-related outlays.

**GAAP Distortion Creates Continued Headline Risk:** Q2 FY2026 included $214.9M in charges for subsidiary-level compensation agreements (related to the PA transaction) and $120.4M in PA transaction costs — significantly larger than the $142.1M previously disclosed as unrecognized. These charges have now been recognized but will continue distorting YoY comparisons. Each earnings report risks market overreaction to reported "losses" that mask improving underlying operations.

**Government Spending Policy:** While U.S. federal exposure is declining (8%), broader infrastructure funding (IIJA, CHIPS Act) faces political uncertainty. The 10-K explicitly notes risk from "the change in the federal administration and subsequent reductions in government spending," and Q2 FY2026 risk language includes possible changes to IIJA amounts and directives to reduce federal spending. State/local budgets supported by multi-year federal appropriations provide mitigation, but the risk should not be dismissed given the company's own cautionary language that loss of federal agencies as customers "could have a material adverse effect."

**Joint Venture Legal Exposure:** The undisclosed "unfavorable interim ruling" against a consolidated JV (50% interest) creates potential for additional charges. Without disclosure of the matter's size, this remains an open risk.

**Goodwill:** $4.78B in goodwill at FY2025 (42.5% of assets). At Q2 FY2026, goodwill was $4.76B on total assets of $11.94B (~39.9%). Because PA was already a consolidated segment, the buyout of the remaining stake did not create a large new goodwill step-up. Any sustained decline in market cap below ~$12-13B could trigger impairment testing.

## Conclusion

Jacobs Solutions at $122.42 presents a favorable risk/reward setup. The stock trades at 16.9x forward earnings — low relative to its own history and a discount to professional-services peers — while underlying operations are improving: backlog growing 22%, segment margins expanding, AI infrastructure work doubling, and improving mix from full PA ownership. The market is penalizing the stock for elevated leverage and GAAP distortion from the separation/PA acquisition, both of which are temporary conditions with defined resolution paths.

The position is suitable for investors with a 12-18 month horizon who can tolerate near-term GAAP headline volatility and are comfortable with the leveraged capital structure. Position sizing should reflect the leverage risk (2-3% allocation). The primary monitoring variables are backlog trajectory, cash flow normalization, and the 2027 term loan refinancing outcome.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrections to the restructuring-savings thesis (programs substantially completed by calendar 2025, limiting incremental EPS accretion beyond consensus), peer valuation comparisons (specialty contractors are not direct peers; discount to professional-services peers is more modest), and near-term cash flow weakness (H1 FY2026 negative operating cash flow) collectively reduce confidence in the magnitude of upside, though the directional thesis remains intact.
