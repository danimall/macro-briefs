**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Gaming & Leisure Properties is a self-managed, gaming-focused triple-net-lease REIT — the second-largest of two public gaming REITs behind VICI. It owns the real estate under 69 casinos and related facilities across 20 states (100% occupied) and leases them back to gaming operators under long-dated master leases where tenants bear all property costs. The model produces a high-margin, predictable rent stream: 2025 revenue of $1,594.8M (+4.1% YoY), AFFO of $1,120.1M (+5.6%), adjusted EBITDA of $1,466.9M, and a covered dividend now yielding roughly 7.0% at $44.59.

The defining feature of the investment case is tenant credit, not GLPI's own balance sheet. Roughly 97% of cash rent comes from five tenants, and the dominant tenant, PENN Entertainment, supplies about 36% of revenue. PENN's regional casinos remain cash-generative, but at the consolidated operator level PENN carries deep financial stress: negative operating margin, negative free cash flow, and a debt/equity ratio near 6.8x driven by its Interactive/ESPN Bet losses. GLPI has engineered substantial contractual protection (corporate guarantees, cross-collateralization, leverage-linked coverage triggers, low default-coverage floors), and PENN paid 100% of rent in 2025 with reported coverage of 1.86x–2.12x. The risk is a multi-year deterioration or a PENN restructuring/change-of-control event, not near-term non-payment. A second, rising concern is Bally's, now GLPI's #2 credit exposure after the $700M Lincoln acquisition exercised in February 2026, also a leveraged, recently-merged operator.

We rate GLPI a HOLD on an income basis. The stock is cheap on absolute metrics (~14x earnings vs. a 22.4x specialized-REIT peer median) and the dividend is well-covered by AFFO, but the discount is the market's pricing of tenant credit risk and the company's structural dependence on continual capital-markets access to fund growth. With the stock down ~11% from its 52-week $49.95 high and the dividend yield doing most of the work, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. This is a yield-bearing hold for income-oriented portfolios, not a source of meaningful capital appreciation in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

## Company Overview

GLPI was spun off from PENN Entertainment (then Penn National Gaming) in November 2013 and remains structurally tied to PENN. The strategy emphasizes regional, drive-to gaming markets (lower volatility and lower capex than Las Vegas Strip destination resorts) and a differentiated role as a flexible development financier and lead capital partner using delayed-draw term loans, construction funding, and tribal loans. This niche generates higher headline yields (8–12% cap rates) than peer net-lease activity, at the cost of more construction, counterparty, and enforceability risk.

The portfolio comprises interests in 69 facilities across 20 states (~6,600 acres). The tenant roster: PENN (34 facilities), Bally's (15 properties plus Chicago development plus the new Lincoln acquisition), Caesars (6), Boyd (4), Cordish (3, private), and smaller/newer tenants including Hard Rock-managed Rockford, Strategic Gaming, and American Racing (Tioga Downs). Leases run long-dated (expirations 2031–2122) with fixed or CPI-linked escalators, typically capped near ~2%. Most master leases carry corporate guarantees and cross-collateralization, and the UPREIT structure lets sellers defer taxes by taking OP units, a useful acquisition tool.

This is an externally-light, deal-driven shop with only 20 full-time employees. Founder Peter Carlino (age 79) has run the company as CEO/Chairman since inception; Brandon Moore was promoted to President in September 2024 as apparent successor.

A growing share of the portfolio flows through as financing-receivable/sales-type lease income and non-cash accretion ($28.4M accretion plus $22.5M straight-line/deferred adjustments in 2025), a function of long lease terms relative to asset lives. This is why standardized screens that read GLPI's revenue as zero produce a misleading Altman Z-Score of 1.25 ("distress"); the figure is a leveraged-REIT artifact, not a real solvency signal. AFFO and cash rent collected ($1.505B, 100%) are the cleaner anchors.

Margins and returns are strong: only ~$393M of operating expense (of which ~$266M is non-cash depreciation), ROE ~17%, ROA ~6.6%. Operating expenses fell in 2025, helped by a $28.6M reduction in the CECL credit-loss provision. The Piotroski signals (positive NI, ROA, OCF, OCF>NI) are all favorable.

Capital allocation in 2025 was active: completed funding for PENN's M Resort tower ($150M @ 7.79%), Joliet relocation ($130M @ 7.75%), Casino Queen Baton Rouge ($111M @ 9.0%), Bally's Chicago ($201.6M @ 8.5%), and the Sunland Park acquisition ($183.75M @ 8.16%). The dividend was raised 2.6% to $0.78/quarter ($3.12 annualized). Funding came from $1.29B of new debt and $402.8M of equity (forward sales settled near $48–49).

The balance sheet is sound: ~$7.2B debt, ~87% fixed-rate, only ~13% variable (revolver plus term loan at ~5.02% weighted), $1.76B revolver capacity available, and compliance with all covenants (max 60% debt/asset value, min 1.5x fixed-charge coverage). In August 2025 the company refinanced $975M of 5.375% notes due 2026 with new 5.25%/2033 and 5.75%/2037 notes, proactive maturity management at only a modest $3.8M extinguishment charge.

The earnings track record is uneven quarter-to-quarter: two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, including a -29.5% miss in Q2 2025 and a -1.8% miss in Q1 2026, offset by +17.5% and +19.1% beats in the intervening quarters. This is partly the lumpiness of financing-lease accounting and one-time items (a $6.3M executive severance charge in 2025). It argues for anchoring on AFFO trend rather than single-quarter EPS.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is entirely external and capital-markets-dependent. With the 90% REIT distribution requirement leaving near-zero retained earnings and only 20 employees, per-share growth hinges on two conditions: issuing equity above NAV and raising debt at spreads below incremental cap rates of 8–9%+. At the current yield and a stock near $45, the accretion math still works, but it is fragile to a sustained stock decline or rate spike.

The development pipeline is both the visible growth engine and a visible risk. GLPI has committed billions to projects it does not control: Bally's Chicago (up to $940M; only $201.6M funded; completion ~late 2026), Bally's Lincoln call right ($700M, exercised February 2026), Live! Virginia ($467M), Dry Creek/Caesars Republic Sonoma ($225.3M), Ameristar Council Bluffs ($150M), PENN Aurora relocation ($225M), and the Ione tribal loan ($110M). As these draws fund, they convert into incremental rent at attractive yields, supporting mid-single-digit AFFO-per-share growth.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive. Current-quarter consensus rose +4.6% over 90 days, next-quarter +5.2%, and current-year EPS sits at $3.20 (vs. $3.21 ninety days ago, essentially flat). Next-year consensus is $3.33. These revisions confirm steady compounding, not acceleration. There is no estimate-revision BUY signal here; the +30%-over-90-days velocity that flags a fundamental inflection is absent, as expected for a stable net-lease REIT.

The contractual escalators (capped near ~2% CPI-linked) limit organic same-store growth, so external acquisition and development funding carry the growth burden. This is a durable but rate-sensitive model: in a higher-for-longer environment, the equity cost of capital rises and the accretion spread compresses.

On headline multiples GLPI looks cheap — roughly 0.63x the peer-median P/E and a yield ~250bps above the peer median. The relevant question is whether that discount reflects mispricing or a justified risk premium. We read it as largely justified. The discount compensates for: (1) tenant concentration with PENN in operator-level distress; (2) rising Bally's exposure to another leveraged operator; (3) the capital-markets-dependent growth model that is unusually rate-sensitive; and (4) construction and tribal-enforceability risk in the development book. EPR, the closest comparable (cyclical, single-tenant-exposure experiential net-lease REIT), trades at 18.3x and a 6.3% yield, framing GLPI's discount as wider than EPR's but in the same risk neighborhood.

The most useful valuation anchor is the dividend yield. At the $3.12 annualized payout covered by ~$3.96 of AFFO per share (~$1.12B / ~283M shares), the payout ratio is ~79% of AFFO — comfortably covered, with the dividend providing a yield floor. Analyst price targets bracket the stock between $46 (low) and $62 (high), with a $54.52 mean and $53 median, implying ~19% upside to the median. We treat the analyst targets with some skepticism: this is not a high-momentum name where targets lag reality, and the stock has spent the past year oscillating between $41 and $50 well below those targets, suggesting the discount is sticky rather than transient.

We assign a valuation score of 6.0, modestly above neutral. The cheapness is real and yield-supported, but cheapness alone is not a buy signal, and we do not see a discrete catalyst to close the gap. A material re-rating requires either evidence that tenant credit is improving (a PENN deleveraging or ESPN Bet turnaround) or a sustained decline in long rates, neither of which is in hand.

## Competitive Landscape

GLPI competes directly with VICI Properties, which is larger, carries a lower cost of capital, and owns marquee Las Vegas Strip assets (Caesars Palace, Venetian, MGM Grand). GLPI differentiates by focusing on regional/drive-to assets and by acting as a flexible development financier and lead capital partner, a higher-yield, higher-risk niche VICI pursues less aggressively. The competitive position is stable but not improving relative to VICI: GLPI's higher cost of capital structurally limits its ability to win the largest, lowest-risk deals, pushing it toward higher-yield, higher-risk sourcing (construction funding, tribal loans).

The lease portfolio's structural protections are a genuine competitive strength in tenant-credit management. Bally's and Casino Queen leases include dynamic coverage triggers that tighten to 1.35x when the tenant parent's net leverage exceeds 5.5x, and the Casino Queen default-cure mechanism lets GLPI fold assets into the corporate-guaranteed Bally's Master Lease II. This is a landlord actively engineering around weaker credits, a moat in underwriting discipline rather than asset quality.

The structural industry question is whether iGaming, sports betting, and newly emphasized prediction markets (federally regulated exchanges that can operate even in states banning iGaming) cannibalize the regional brick-and-mortar foot traffic underpinning tenant coverage ratios. Percentage rent is only ~4.8% of cash income, limiting GLPI's direct exposure to revenue cannibalization, but a sustained decline in tenant operating performance would eventually pressure coverage and credit quality across the portfolio.

## Risk Assessment

**Tenant concentration and PENN distress (primary risk).** The 10-K's first risk factor is dependence on PENN. Its consolidated credit profile is weak: negative operating margin (~-9.7%), negative free cash flow (-$139.5M), D/E ~6.8x, and a distressed-zone Z-Score, driven by its Interactive/ESPN Bet segment. The regional casino base remains cash-generative. The threat is a multi-year deterioration or a restructuring/change-of-control event that could disrupt the master leases. Mitigants are substantial (corporate guarantees, cross-collateralization, technical-default landlord protections, 1.1x default-coverage floors), but the magnitude of the concentration is the reason the stock carries a discount.

**Development funding and counterparty risk.** GLPI has committed billions to projects it does not control, with cost-overrun and tariff exposure explicitly flagged for Bally's Chicago. Tenants can decline funding, and construction delays could push out the rent ramp.

**Bally's exposure rising.** Between the Bally's master leases, Tropicana LV ground lease, Chicago development, and the Lincoln acquisition, Bally's is becoming the #2 credit exposure, and Bally's is a leveraged, recently merged (Standard General, Feb 2025) operator.

**Tribal-loan enforceability.** The Ione ($110M) and Dry Creek loans carry sovereign-immunity risk; GLPI may be unable to foreclose or enforce customary remedies, a newer, higher-risk asset class.

**Capital structure and rate sensitivity.** The 90% distribution requirement means growth depends on continual debt/equity issuance. A sustained stock decline or rate spike would choke the accretion engine while the $1.25B ATM program ($886.7M remaining) and OP-unit issuance continue diluting holders. In the current higher-for-longer rate path, refinancing cost and the yield-driven valuation are both pressured.

**Key-man and leadership transition.** CEO Carlino is 79 with disclosed key-man risk; a 12/8/2025 officer departure tied to a $6.3M severance charge signals leadership transition in progress, with Moore positioned as successor.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no warning. Near-term ATM IV of 19.7% sits essentially in line with 18.2% one-year historical vol (spread +1.5%, "fair"), at the 3rd percentile of the current chain; the market is not pricing elevated event risk. Put/call skew is balanced (0.6%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.44. The term structure is in normal contango (near 19.7% vs. long-term 22.8%). Since the prior snapshot, IV fell 1.7pp as the price declined 5.6%, and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.71 to 0.96, a modest tilt toward defensiveness consistent with the recent drawdown but well short of a bearish positioning signal. The 6.1% short interest (8.3 days to cover) is unremarkable and reflects no concentrated bearish bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($53–62).** Tenant credit stabilizes or improves: PENN's ESPN Bet losses narrow and its regional base sustains coverage, removing the overhang that drives the peer discount. The development pipeline (Bally's Chicago, Lincoln, Live! Virginia) funds on schedule at 8–9% yields, adding accretive rent. A decline in long rates re-rates the yield toward the peer-median 4.5–6%, compressing the discount. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the $53 analyst median and potentially the $62 high, with the dividend adding ~7% annually. This requires both tenant-credit improvement and a friendlier rate environment.

**Bear Case (~$38–40, roughly -12% to -15%).** PENN's distress deepens into a restructuring or change-of-control event that disrupts the master leases despite contractual protections, or Bally's leverage triggers tighten coverage and force lease renegotiation. A higher rate path raises GLPI's equity cost of capital, breaks the accretion math on the development book, and forces dilutive ATM issuance below NAV, while simultaneously pressuring the yield-driven valuation lower. The dividend yield would need to rise toward ~8% to clear the market, implying a low-$40s-to-high-$30s price.

The base case sits between: steady ~4–5% AFFO-per-share growth funded by the development pipeline, a covered and modestly growing dividend, and a discount that neither widens materially nor closes. This is a total-return profile dominated by the yield with limited capital appreciation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The thesis rests on the well-covered dividend and stable cash-rent collection, not on expected capital appreciation. We do not see a discrete catalyst to close the peer discount, nor a fundamental deterioration that would justify a sell, so the position is held for yield.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- PENN consolidated credit improvement — return to positive consolidated free cash flow or a deleveraging event (e.g., ESPN Bet/Interactive segment turnaround or divestiture) that removes the tenant-credit overhang.
- A sustained decline in long-end rates (10Y materially lower) that re-rates the yield toward the peer median, combined with continued accretive pipeline funding.
- Price dislocation to the low-$40s on unchanged fundamentals, pushing the yield above ~7.5% with AFFO coverage intact, which would shift the risk/reward to clearly favorable.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any PENN rent shortfall, lease renegotiation, or restructuring/change-of-control event that breaches the master-lease protections.
- Bally's coverage triggers tightening to 1.35x (parent net leverage >5.5x) with evidence of stress, or a Bally's payment issue.
- A dividend cut, or AFFO coverage falling below ~1.1x of the payout.
- Forced ATM issuance well below NAV to fund pipeline commitments, signaling the accretion engine has broken.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the associated tenant-coverage disclosures; monitor PENN's quarterly results as the dominant credit input. The Bally's Chicago completion (~late 2026) and the funding cadence of the Lincoln acquisition are the near-term operational checkpoints.

## Conclusion

GLPI is a well-run, high-margin, covenant-compliant gaming net-lease REIT with a covered dividend and a disciplined, accretive external-growth model. The 100% occupancy, 100% rent collection, and proactive maturity management reflect genuine operational quality, and the multiple is a real discount to the specialized-REIT peer median. The recent pullback from the 52-week high on unchanged fundamentals modestly improves the entry yield.

The discount is the market's pricing of the central risk: tenant credit, where PENN sits in operator-level distress and Bally's exposure is rising to a second leveraged operator. The lease engineering (guarantees, cross-collateralization, leverage-linked coverage triggers) is designed to absorb that risk and has done so, but it does not eliminate the tail of a tenant restructuring, and the company's structural dependence on continual capital-markets access makes the growth model unusually rate-sensitive in the current environment. The combination of a yield-supported floor and a genuine, non-trivial credit overhang produces a balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward.

We rate GLPI a HOLD for income-oriented investors, with the covered dividend doing most of the work and limited capital appreciation expected absent tenant-credit improvement or a friendlier rate path. Overall score: 6.05.
