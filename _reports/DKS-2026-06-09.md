**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dick's Sporting Goods is two businesses bolted together by the September 2025 acquisition of Foot Locker. The DICK'S core remains one of the best-executing specialty retailers in the U.S.: comps accelerated to +6.0% in the quarter ended May 2, 2026, segment margin held near 11%, and the vertical-brand plus experiential big-box strategy continues to win premium product allocation and share. The Foot Locker business — roughly 2,500 mostly mall-based sneaker stores across 20 countries — is a lower-margin, fashion-driven, internationally complex turnaround asset that has just posted its first positive comps in about three years (+0.6% proforma) but is still near break-even and shedding stores rapidly (85 net owned-store closures in a single quarter, with WSS shrinking from 143 to 100 locations).

The reported earnings decline (diluted EPS from $14.05 to $9.97) is almost entirely an accounting and transition artifact: ~$3.61/share of acquisition charges plus roughly 12% share dilution from a stock-funded deal, not a deterioration of the franchise. The investment question is narrow and binary: will the Foot Locker turnaround create value exceeding the $500–750M of integration charges and the dilution already absorbed? The balance sheet is sound — the Altman Z-Score of 2.0 and 3.14 debt/equity are artifacts of ~$9.9B of capitalized operating leases, not funded debt (actual funded debt is only ~$1.9B against $1.5B+ annual operating cash flow, with investment-grade ratings and an undrawn $2B revolver).

At ~$216 the stock trades at roughly 15x the $14.31 current-year consensus EPS — a discount to its quality on a sum-of-parts basis if Foot Locker inflects, but a fair-to-full multiple if the turnaround stalls and a goodwill writedown follows. With estimate revisions modestly negative on the current quarter, no insider buying near these levels, and the decisive catalyst (back-to-school Foot Locker comps, H2 gross-margin recovery, Fast Break scaling to 250 stores) still two-plus quarters away, the risk/reward does not support a high-conviction call in either direction. We rate DKS a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Dick's is the leading U.S. specialty sports retailer and, since September 2025, a global sports and sneaker retailer. The company now reports two segments:

- **DICK'S Business** (~$14.1B FY net sales, ~11.1% segment margin): DICK'S Sporting Goods, Golf Galaxy, Public Lands, Going Going Gone!, the experiential House of Sport and Field House formats, Golf Galaxy Performance Centers, and the GameChanger youth-sports SaaS platform. 888 stores.
- **Foot Locker Business** (~$3.1B partial-year FY net sales; ~$8B full-year run-rate, near break-even): Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS, atmos. ~2,500 stores across ~20 countries.

The core moats are genuine: vertical (private) brands at ~13% of DICK'S sales carrying 700–900 bps higher gross margin per management, deep brand-partner relationships (Nike above all), experiential formats that win premium allocation, and a growing high-margin DICK'S Media Network plus GameChanger SaaS. Foot Locker adds global scale and makes the combined entity one of the most important wholesale partners for athletic brands worldwide. Founder Edward W. Stack and family control a majority of voting power through Class B super-voting shares; Lauren Hobart is CEO, with Stack as Executive Chairman retaining operational control over merchandising and the Foot Locker initiative (the two serve jointly as Chief Operating Decision Maker).

The headline distortion is entirely Foot Locker. Revenue jumped 28% while operating income fell because the consolidated number absorbs (a) Foot Locker's structurally lower margins, (b) a $217.9M inventory write-down/liquidation, and (c) $164.2M of merger/integration costs. Stripping those out, the DICK'S core grew comps +4.5% and expanded segment profit to $1.57B (11.1% margin). The core franchise is healthy.

**Most recent quarter (ended May 2, 2026):** Net sales +62.7% to $5.16B (+$1.79B from Foot Locker). DICK'S core comps +6.0%, broad-based across footwear, apparel, team sports, golf, and trading cards. Foot Locker segment profit turned positive at $17.5M, with proforma comps +0.6%. Consolidated operating margin compressed to ~8.7% from 11.5%; core DICK'S segment margin dipped 66 bps to 10.7% on mix (lower-margin trading cards), higher shipping, and tech/talent investment. A one-time $174.5M litigation/settlement benefit ($150M interchange-fee settlement + $24.5M lease-termination gain) flattered net income by roughly $1.45/share, so the underlying EPS quality this quarter was weaker than the headline.

**Balance sheet and capital structure:** The Altman Z-Score of 2.0 (grey zone) and 3.14 debt/equity overstate risk because the denominator carries ~$9.9B of capitalized operating leases under ASC 842. This is a lease-heavy retailer, not a debt-laden one. Actual funded debt is ~$1.9B ($400M 2029 notes at 4.0%, $750M 2032 at 3.15%, $750M 2052 at 4.10%), no revolver or commercial paper borrowings, a $2.0B undrawn facility, investment-grade ratings (Baa2 / BBB), and ~$1.0B cash. Current ratio 1.53; ROE 15.3%; ROA 4.9%. The acquisition was funded mostly with stock ($2.1B / 9.6M shares), preserving the balance sheet while creating the ~12% dilution that depressed EPS.

**Capital allocation:** Dividend raised 3% to $1.25/quarter ($414M paid FY2025); $342M of buybacks in FY2025 ($141M in Q1) against a fresh $3.0B authorization. Capex guided up to ~$1.4–1.5B in FY2026 (House of Sport/Field House build-out, Foot Locker Fast Break remodels, new Fort Worth DC). Near-term FCF will be pressured by elevated capex plus integration charges; the $400M FY2025 FCF figure understates normalized generation but is the relevant number while the bet is being absorbed.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. The DICK'S core is in its strongest comp phase in years against a discretionary backdrop where many peers are struggling, validating the experiential-format and vertical-brand strategy. Structural tailwinds include women's sports, the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and the 2028 Olympics. Vertical brands at 700–900 bps margin premium and the DICK'S Media Network are durable margin levers.

Consolidated growth optics are dominated by Foot Locker integration. Reported FY2026 guidance of net sales $22.1–22.4B and GAAP diluted EPS $13.27–14.27 (non-GAAP ~$13.50–14.50) carries a heavy turnaround dependency. The low end of comp guidance was raised for both banners while the high end held — net cautious, but not a cut. Fast Break remodel pilots are posting double-digit comps, scaled to ~100 stores in Q1 and targeted at 250 by back-to-school, which management has named as the inflection point.

Estimate revisions are mixed and slightly negative on the near term: the current quarter has been cut 10.3% over 90 days (to $3.77) and 3.4% over 30 days, while next-year consensus is down only 2.8% over 90 days and up 1.0% over 30 days. This is a business in transition with estimates drifting modestly lower as the Street recalibrates for dilution and mix. The earnings surprise record is 3 beats and 1 miss over four quarters, with the most recent reported quarter an actual $3.45 vs $2.90 estimate (a +19.1% beat).

Two genuine unmodeled options exist on the upside: potential IEEPA tariff refunds (the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that those tariffs were unauthorized; DKS is treating refunds as an unrecognized gain contingency, zero in guidance), and synergy guidance ($100–125M) that looks conservative against the combined entity's ~$8B Foot Locker purchasing scale and Nike-allocation leverage.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$216, DKS trades at ~15x the $14.31 current-year consensus and ~13.2x the $16.31 next-year consensus. Against the Specialty Stores peer median P/E of 11.7, DKS carries a premium, but the peer set is heterogeneous and lower-quality (ASO at 9.3x, SIG at 11.7x, SBH at 6.7x are value/cyclical names; FIVE at 24x and EYE at 26.9x are growth names with their own issues). On a quality-adjusted basis, a 13–15x forward multiple for a share-gaining core retailer with a real turnaround option is not stretched, but it is not cheap either, because the multiple already embeds a partial Foot Locker recovery.

The cleaner frame is sum-of-parts. The DICK'S core alone, generating ~$1.57B segment profit at 11% margin and gaining share, would command roughly 16–18x standalone, supporting most of the current enterprise value. That leaves Foot Locker as a near-free option with binary outcomes: if back-to-school comps inflect and synergies build, the combined entity re-rates toward DA Davidson's $260 target (~15-16x a normalized ~$16–17 EPS plus synergy capture); if the turnaround stalls and a goodwill/intangible writedown follows, the stock de-rates toward the high-$160s to low-$180s.

The estimate-revision picture does not support treating consensus targets as floors here: revisions are modestly negative on the near term, and the stock trades only ~21% below the consensus median target of $262 with no inflection catalyst yet confirmed. We assign a Valuation score of 5.5: fairly priced for the current information set, with the asymmetry roughly balanced rather than skewed favorably. The wide analyst dispersion ($169 low to $300 high) reflects the binary nature of the Foot Locker bet.

## Competitive Landscape

The DICK'S core competitive position is improving, and we score Competitive Position at 6.8, continuing the upward trajectory in prior reports (6.5 → 7.0). Comp acceleration while discretionary retail broadly struggles, expanding vertical-brand penetration, the high-margin retail media network, and experiential formats that secure premium allocation all point to widening share and a deepening moat in the U.S. The Foot Locker acquisition makes the combined entity one of the most important athletic wholesale partners globally, a lever to deepen Nike and other brand allocations.

The offsetting concern is that the acquisition grafts a structurally weaker, mall-exposed, internationally complex, fashion-trend-dependent business onto a high-performing domestic one. Foot Locker's competitive position in much of its fleet is deteriorating — the net owned-store closures in Q1 (WSS down 30% in one quarter) signal that management found more dead weight than initially flagged. Europe is explicitly lagging North America. The net competitive trajectory is positive on the core and uncertain on the acquired business, which caps the score below 7.

## Risk Assessment

We score Risk Factors at 4.5 — specific, partly binary downside risks exist that are not fully reflected in the multiple:

1. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** The deal added $618M goodwill plus $710M indefinite-lived trade-name intangibles carried at fair value "approximating carrying value," with management explicitly warning of possible future impairment. A disappointing back-to-school 2026 likely triggers a sizeable non-cash writedown (Foot Locker itself took a $110M goodwill impairment in the comparable pre-acquisition quarter). This is the cleanest asymmetric downside.

2. **Nike concentration.** Nike is ~31% of total merchandise purchases with no long-term supply contract, and the combined entity is now even more Nike-dependent through Foot Locker's footwear mix. Any adverse change in Nike allocation or terms would be highly material.

3. **Foot Locker turnaround execution.** Total acquisition charges guided to $500–750M against only $100–125M of medium-term synergies. The accelerating store-closure pace is a near-term sales drag even if it pulls profitability forward; WSS may be heading toward a wind-down.

4. **Margin mix dilution.** Foot Locker's ~24–28% gross margin and the fast-growing but low-margin trading-card category both pressure consolidated margin. The H2 gross-margin recovery thesis rests on vertical-brand and supply-chain execution.

5. **Securities litigation.** The In re Dick's Sporting Goods Securities Litigation survived (partially) a motion to dismiss in April 2026 on inventory-statement claims tied to 2022–23 disclosures — a manageable but unresolved tail.

6. **Dual-class control.** The Stack family controls the vote; other shareholders cannot force change, and activist pressure on dual-class structures is a noted risk.

Consumer-cycle exposure is also relevant: DKS sits squarely in discretionary retail. The core's +6% comps argue the consumer is holding for now, but a discretionary downturn would hit both segments.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 45.3% is rich versus 35.6% one-year historical vol (a 9.7pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (45.3% near vs 42.7% at three months), pricing imminent stress around the June 18 expiry. The skew is call-heavy (-7.0% put/call skew, put/call volume ratio 0.24), but the put/call open-interest ratio jumped to 2.35 from 0.93 over the prior month, indicating a build in put open interest underneath a call-tilted near-term flow. Short interest is elevated at 13.3% of float with 7 days to cover. The composite read is two-sided positioning: bearish institutional shorts and rising put OI against call-heavy near-term volume. IV has compressed 11.6pp since mid-May (post-earnings de-risking), so the entry timing on optionality is less attractive than it was a month ago. None of this overrides the fundamental call; it confirms the show-me, binary character of the setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$260–300):** Back-to-school 2026 confirms the Foot Locker inflection — Fast Break scales to 250 stores at double-digit comps, proforma Foot Locker comps turn solidly positive, and synergies exceed the conservative $100–125M guide. Core DICK'S sustains mid-single-digit comps, H2 gross margin recovers on vertical brands, IEEPA tariff refunds materialize as an unmodeled cash benefit, and the dilution-depressed EPS rebases toward $16–17+ as charges roll off. The stock re-rates toward 15–16x normalized earnings, the $260 DA Davidson target, with upside to $300 if synergy capture surprises.

**Bear case (~$169–185):** Foot Locker's turnaround stalls, Europe continues to lag, and store closures accelerate into a partial wind-down. A goodwill/intangible writedown of several hundred million dollars follows a weak back-to-school. Margin recovery disappoints, a discretionary consumer slowdown pressures both banners, and consensus EPS drifts lower. The multiple compresses toward 11–12x on de-rated earnings, taking the stock to the high-$160s to mid-$180s, roughly 14–22% below current.

The base case sits between these: the core carries the company while Foot Locker grinds toward break-even-plus over several quarters, EPS recovers gradually as charges abate, and the stock trades in a wide range around current levels until back-to-school resolves the binary.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Back-to-school 2026 Foot Locker proforma comps turn solidly positive (>+2%) with Fast Break at/near 250 stores, confirming the inflection.
- H2 consolidated gross margin recovers toward 35%+ with core DICK'S segment margin back above 11%, and current-year EPS revisions turn positive.
- A pullback toward the high-$170s to low-$180s on unchanged fundamentals, improving the sum-of-parts risk/reward to clearly asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or back-to-school Foot Locker comps turning negative, signaling the turnaround is failing.
- Core DICK'S comps decelerating below +2% (loss of the franchise that anchors the valuation), or Nike materially reducing allocation.
- FY2026 guidance cut, or next-year consensus revisions falling more than ~5% as the Street prices a stalled integration.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next two earnings reports — the August (Q2) print and especially the back-to-school-inclusive Q3 print — are the decisive catalysts. The thesis should resolve within 6–9 months.

## Conclusion

Dick's pairs a high-quality, share-gaining core retailer with a large, dilutive, margin-diluting bet on the Foot Locker turnaround. The balance sheet is genuinely sound (the leverage screen is a lease-driven false positive), and the reported EPS decline is a transition artifact rather than franchise deterioration. The value-creation question is unresolved and binary: integration charges of $500–750M plus ~12% dilution against $100–125M of guided synergies and a turnaround that has only just shown its first positive comp in three years. Estimate revisions are modestly negative on the near term, there is no insider buying near current levels, options positioning is two-sided with rich near-term IV, and the decisive back-to-school catalyst is still two-plus quarters out. With goodwill-impairment risk live and asymmetric, and the stock fairly priced at ~15x current-year earnings, the evidence does not support a confident call in either direction. We rate DKS a HOLD and would revisit on either a confirmed Foot Locker inflection (upgrade) or a turnaround failure/impairment (downgrade).
