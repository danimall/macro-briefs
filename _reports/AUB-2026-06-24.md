**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Atlantic Union Bankshares is a $37.3 billion-asset Richmond, Virginia regional bank that has just completed the largest deal in its history: the all-stock acquisition of Sandy Spring Bancorp (closed April 1, 2025), which added more than 50 branches in Northern Virginia, the D.C. metro, and Maryland and expanded the balance sheet roughly 50%. The first clean quarter with Sandy Spring fully integrated (Q1 2026) showed the franchise's combined earnings power: net interest income up 70% to $312.4 million, NIM (FTE) of 3.85%, net income to common up 154% to $119.2 million, and adjusted operating EPS of $0.89, a ~1% beat. The integration appears to have been executed competently: systems converted, goodwill finalized, an unqualified auditor review, no internal-control changes.

The complication is that the headline numbers overstate underlying run-rate momentum. Roughly $32.9 million of the quarter's net interest income was purchase-accounting accretion (up from $12.6 million a year earlier), a benefit that mechanically inflates NIM and EPS and decays on a fixed schedule. Strip that out and the core earnings trajectory is more pedestrian. Two further items temper the story: commercial real estate concentration of ~$16.7 billion (60% of loans, 272% of total capital, near the 300% supervisory threshold), and $39.5 million of net charge-offs on acquired purchased-credit-deteriorated loans recorded as measurement-period adjustments, indicating some Sandy Spring credits were worse than the original marks.

At ~$40.81 (near a 52-week high of $42.18), AUB trades at ~17x trailing EPS of $2.03 and ~11x consensus 2026 EPS of $3.73 (the spread reflects the large step-up in run-rate earnings from the full-year Sandy Spring contribution). That forward multiple is below the regional-bank peer median of 13.7x, which is computed on a trailing basis and not directly comparable; on a trailing basis AUB at ~17x sits above the peer median. With estimate revisions essentially flat-to-slightly-negative over 90 days, a benign-but-watchful credit picture, and a freshly initiated capital-return program supporting the equity, this is a HOLD. The 3.8% dividend yield and well-covered payout provide a floor; the absence of an asymmetric valuation entry and the accretion-runoff headwind cap the upside case. We rate AUB HOLD with an uncertain rationale.

## Company Overview

Atlantic Union Bankshares is the holding company for Atlantic Union Bank, a commercial bank operating across Virginia, Maryland, Washington D.C., and North Carolina, serving roughly 800,000 customers. It runs two reportable segments — Wholesale Banking (commercial/CRE lending, treasury management, equipment finance, capital markets, wealth/trust) and Consumer Banking (retail deposits, home loans, brokerage) — supplemented by non-bank subsidiaries in equipment finance, investments, and capital markets. As of Q1 2026 the company reported ~$27.9 billion in loans held for investment and ~$30.4 billion in deposits.

The company has pursued a consolidator strategy in the mid-Atlantic, using stock-funded M&A (American National, prior deals, and now Sandy Spring) to build scale in affluent markets. The Sandy Spring transaction (0.900 AUB shares per Sandy Spring share, ~41 million shares issued, ~$1.28 billion value) densified AUB's presence in Northern Virginia and the D.C. metro and materially expanded the wealth management franchise (AUM +~125% YoY). AUB is now the largest regional bank headquartered in Virginia, competing against super-regionals (Truist, PNC, Wells Fargo) above it and community banks below it. Its differentiation is local commercial relationships, a low-cost core deposit base (22.5% noninterest-bearing), and a now-scaled fee-generating wealth business.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 was the first full-run quarter with Sandy Spring integrated, and the year-over-year comparisons are dominated by the deal:

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net interest income | $312.4M | $184.2M | +70% |
| NIM (FTE) | 3.85% | 3.45% | +40 bps |
| Net income to common | $119.2M | $46.9M | +154% |
| Diluted EPS | $0.84 | $0.52 | +62% |
| Adjusted operating EPS | $0.89 | $0.57 | +56% |
| Noninterest income | $54.8M | $29.2M | +88% |
| Provision for credit losses | $2.7M | $17.6M | -85% |

**Margin and accretion.** NIM of 3.85% is healthy, supported by a 29 bps drop in cost of funds to 1.94% (the Fed cut 75 bps Sept–Dec 2025, and AUB ran off brokered deposits). The accretion benefit declines on a fixed schedule as the acquired loan book amortizes, so the core, ex-accretion margin is the variable to monitor. Falling rates compound this: AUB is now modestly asset-sensitive (+10.2% NII over 12 months in a +300 bps shock, -8.8% in a -300 bps shock), so the rate environment that lowered funding costs also pressures forward NII.

**Profitability and capital.** ROA annualized to roughly 1.3% and ROTCE is robust on ~$2.83 billion of tangible common equity. Capital ratios improved year-over-year and are comfortably above well-capitalized minimums: CET1 10.21%, total capital 14.01%, leverage 9.31%, TCE/TA 8.03% (up from 7.39%). Management estimates ratios would improve under proposed Basel III endgame rules.

**Credit quality.** Currently benign at the headline: NPAs 0.36% of loans (down from 0.42%), net charge-offs 0.02% annualized, ACL/loans 1.15%, allowance-to-nonaccrual coverage ~298%. The caveat is the $39.5 million of net charge-offs on acquired PCD loans recorded as measurement-period adjustments (and $101.2 million of charge-offs excluded from the initial ACL at acquisition under GAAP). These did not flow through the P&L provision, but they reveal that some acquired Sandy Spring credits — concentrated in CRE — deteriorated beyond the original fair-value marks. The headline NPA decline was partly a function of resolving these acquired problem loans.

**Securities and AOCI.** Accumulated other comprehensive loss of -$278.5 million (widened from -$256 million) reflects ~$322 million of unrealized losses on the $5.1 billion AFS book (largely agency MBS and munis). This is rate-driven duration risk, not credit, and it is manageable against strong liquidity, but it sits as an embedded mark in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Liquidity.** Strong. Liquid assets are 36.8% of total assets; ~$14.6 billion of unused borrowing capacity (FHLB, Fed Discount Window, fed funds lines); management claims ability to cover ~156% of uninsured/uncollateralized deposits. Uninsured deposits are ~$10.8 billion, with ~68.4% of total deposits insured or collateralized — adequate deposit-stability metrics in a sector still sensitive to the 2023 failures.

**Capital return.** The dividend was raised 8.8% YoY to $0.37/quarter, and a new $250 million buyback was authorized May 5, 2026 (no repurchases occurred during 2025). The Bearing Insurance Group divestiture (May 1, 2026) generates a ~$32.3 million pre-tax gain expected in Q2 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question hinges on separating M&A arithmetic from underlying momentum. The 70% NII and 88% fee growth are predominantly the Sandy Spring step-change, not organic acceleration. Going forward, three growth levers matter:

1. **Synergy capture and operating leverage.** The integration is complete (systems converted Q4 2025, goodwill finalized), so the cost-saving thesis should now be visible in the efficiency ratio. If realized synergies hold, the combined entity earns more per share than the standalone companies would have.

2. **Fee diversification.** Wealth/fiduciary fees jumped to $20.2 million from $6.7 million with AUM +125%, adding capital-light recurring revenue. This is the highest-quality piece of the growth story and improves the business mix beyond spread income.

3. **Organic expansion.** Management is pushing into North Carolina/South Carolina and announced a fintech partnership (Spiral, June 2026) for consumer savings. These are early-stage and not yet material.

Against these, the accretion runoff is a structural drag. Consensus reflects this tension: 2026 EPS of $3.73 and 2027 EPS of $4.01 imply ~7.5% forward growth, but estimate revisions over the trailing 90 days were negative (current year -0.9%, next year -1.0%), with only a marginal 30-day uptick. This is not a revision signature that would warrant treating the stock as a re-rating candidate. The earnings-surprise record is solid (3 beats in 4 quarters, including a +33% Q2 2025 beat), but the most recent quarter (Q1 2026) was only a +1.1% beat as the deal-comparison tailwinds normalized. Growth prospects are positive but modest, and we weight the negative revision trend and accretion decay against the synergy upside, scoring this dimension at 5.6.

## Valuation Assessment

AUB trades at ~$40.81, near its 52-week high of $42.18 and up ~32% from the year-ago low. On trailing EPS of $2.03 the multiple is ~17x; on consensus 2026 EPS of $3.73 it is ~10.9x; on 2027 EPS of $4.01 it is ~10.2x. The large gap between trailing and forward multiples is real and reflects the full-year Sandy Spring contribution rather than a cyclical trough, so the forward figure is the relevant anchor.

Against the regional-bank peer set, the picture is unremarkable:

|--------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| HWC | 14.7 | 2.8% | -5% |
| ABCB | 13.7 | 0.9% | -2% |
| HOMB | 11.5 | 3.0% | -10% |

| **Peer median** | **13.7** | **2.8%** | — |

AUB's ~11x forward sits below the peer median, but the comparison is muddied because the peer P/Es are reported on a trailing basis while AUB's forward figure normalizes for the full-year Sandy Spring contribution. On a comparable trailing basis AUB at ~17x is above the group. The cleaner read is that AUB trades roughly in line with the group once accretion-flattered earnings are normalized down. Its 3.8% dividend yield is at the high end of the peer range, providing yield support. There is no specific catalyst forcing a re-rating: estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, the accretion tailwind is decaying, and the CRE concentration is a discount factor rather than a premium one.

A note on the analyst-target framework: the consensus mean target is $44.25 (low $40.00, high $46.00, median $45.00), implying roughly 8% upside to the mean. On a stock with rising estimates, those targets would function as floors. Here, with estimates flat and the stock near its high, the targets more likely reflect fair-value consensus than understated upside. We do not treat them as a strong tailwind.

Cheapness in a regional bank with above-peer CRE concentration and decaying accretion earnings is more value-trap-adjacent than asymmetric-opportunity. We score valuation 5.4 — fair, with yield support but no identifiable mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

AUB's competitive position has improved structurally through the Sandy Spring deal. It is now the largest Virginia-headquartered regional bank, with dense branch coverage in some of the wealthiest U.S. banking markets (Northern VA, D.C. metro, Maryland). The expanded wealth franchise gives it a more diversified, capital-light revenue stream that smaller community competitors cannot match. The low-cost, granular deposit base (22.5% noninterest-bearing, ~68% insured/collateralized) is a genuine funding advantage.

The trajectory is positive on balance, but bounded. AUB sits in a competitive squeeze between the national super-regionals (Truist, PNC, Wells Fargo) that out-scale it and community banks that out-localize it. Its consolidator model works only as long as it can keep finding accretive stock-funded deals, and the larger it gets, the harder that becomes. The CRE concentration that drove much of the loan growth is also a competitive liability in a downturn. The fee-diversification progress and scale gains are real; we score competitive position 6.0, reflecting improving-but-not-dominant standing.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration (the dominant risk).** The CRE-to-total-capital ratio of 272% sits near the 300% supervisory threshold, and total CRE exposure grew 93% over 36 months on the Sandy Spring addition. Within the ~$16.7 billion book, ~$1.47 billion is office (suburban, average loan ~$2.1 million), $7.2 billion non-owner-occupied CRE, $2.3 billion multifamily. Management emphasizes granularity (median CRE loan ~$317K) and suburban/non-CBD focus, but the concentration makes AUB more sensitive to a CRE downturn than the average regional bank. Further CRE growth or capital erosion could attract heightened regulatory attention. With the higher-for-longer rate path raising commercial refinancing costs and office values under pressure, this concentration is a live, not theoretical, risk.

**Acquired-credit quality.** The $39.5 million of PCD net charge-offs as measurement-period adjustments, and $101.2 million excluded from the initial ACL, indicate the acquired CRE book carries credit risk beyond the original marks. This is the clearest tell that the deal's credit underwriting is not yet fully de-risked.

**Earnings quality / accretion runoff.** The ~$33 million/quarter of accretion is inflating NIM and EPS and will decline. Core ex-accretion margin trajectory is the key forward variable.

**Interest-rate risk.** Modest asset-sensitivity means falling rates pressure NII; the AFS unrealized loss in AOCI is rate-driven duration risk.

**Goodwill/intangibles.** $1.75 billion goodwill plus ~$300 million intangibles equals ~40% of equity; impairment is a tail risk if integration economics disappoint.

**CFO transition.** Longtime CFO Robert Gorman is transitioning out under a documented agreement; Alexander Dodd was appointed EVP & CFO (Feb 2026) and signed the Q1 10-Q. A planned succession rather than a red flag, but a new CFO inheriting a freshly doubled balance sheet warrants monitoring.

**Emerging exposures.** NDFI loans of $247 million (0.89% of loans) — a sector under industry-wide regulatory scrutiny — characterized by management as granular PE-fund facilities with conservative advance rates. Small but worth tracking.

Many of these risks are partly priced, but the CRE concentration near the regulatory line combined with evidence of acquired-credit deterioration represents idiosyncratic downside that is not fully reflected at a near-52-week-high valuation. We score risk factors 5.2.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 47.1% to 11.8% between late May and late June while the stock rose 8.4%. The earlier elevated IV was likely event-driven, and its subsequent collapse reflects resolved near-term uncertainty rather than a directional signal — options are now pricing complacency. Short interest is moderate at 8.2% of float with 9.3 days to cover, which is neither a meaningful bearish tell nor a squeeze setup. The options market is not flagging an imminent catalyst in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Sandy Spring integration is complete and competently executed, and the combined franchise earns materially more per share than the standalone companies. Synergy capture drives the efficiency ratio lower; fee diversification provides recurring, capital-light revenue; credit holds benign; and the capital-return phase ($250M buyback, 8.8% dividend hike) supports the equity. If accretion runoff is offset by organic loan growth and synergy realization, core earnings grow into the multiple, and the stock re-rates toward the high end of analyst targets. Bull case ~$46 (high analyst target, ~11x 2027 EPS plus yield).

**Bear case.** Accretion decays faster than core earnings grow, NIM compresses as the asset-sensitive book reprices lower in a falling-rate scenario, and CRE credit normalizes upward from today's 0.02% net charge-off run-rate — particularly in the acquired office and non-owner-occupied book where marks have already proven optimistic. CRE concentration near the 300% threshold constrains growth or invites regulatory friction. A goodwill question surfaces if integration economics disappoint. In that scenario the stock derates toward ~$32 (below the peer-median multiple on lower core earnings), roughly -22% from current.

The base case sits between these: modest mid-single-digit earnings growth, benign-but-watchful credit, a well-supported dividend, and a multiple that holds roughly where it is. Net, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — we lack the evidence for a confident directional call. The thesis is balanced: a quality regional franchise with a freshly initiated capital-return program, against accretion-flattered earnings, above-peer CRE concentration, and flat estimate revisions, all at a near-52-week-high price with no valuation discount.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Core, ex-accretion NIM stabilizes or expands over two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the run-rate earnings are durable as accretion decays.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (>+5% on current-year EPS over 90 days) alongside evidence of synergy-driven efficiency-ratio improvement.
- The stock pulls back to the low-to-mid $30s (~9-10x forward, a clear peer discount) on no fundamental deterioration, restoring an asymmetric entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE net charge-offs accelerate materially (e.g., annualized NCOs above 0.25% concentrated in office/non-owner-occupied), or NPAs reverse higher toward 0.75%+.
- CRE-to-capital breaches the 300% supervisory threshold without offsetting capital build, attracting regulatory constraint.
- A goodwill impairment, dividend coverage strain, or further large PCD measurement-period charge-offs signaling the acquired book is worse than marked.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (which will include the ~$32.3 million Bearing Insurance gain and a cleaner read on core ex-accretion margin), followed by Q3 2026 for credit-quality trend confirmation. The trailing trajectory — overall score drifting from 6.1 to 6.0 with growth prospects falling from 6.8 to 6.0 — reflects the recognition that the deal-driven growth comparisons are now lapping and the accretion tailwind is fading. The current data confirms rather than reverses that drift: revenue is strong but increasingly normalized, and revisions are flat-to-negative. We hold the score modestly lower at 5.7.

## Conclusion

Atlantic Union Bankshares is a well-capitalized, profitable regional bank that has successfully digested a transformational acquisition, delivering a 3.85% NIM, 10.21% CET1, an 8.8% dividend increase, and a first-in-cycle $250M buyback. The franchise is genuinely stronger and more diversified than it was a year ago. The equity offers no asymmetric edge today: headline growth is flattered by decaying purchase-accounting accretion, CRE concentration sits at 272% of capital near the regulatory line with evidence that acquired credits required additional marks, and estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative. At ~17x trailing / ~11x forward near a 52-week high and roughly in line with peers on normalized earnings, the stock is fairly valued. The 3.8% dividend and well-covered payout provide a floor, and the capital-return program is a genuine positive, but neither creates a compelling upside case.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Own it for the yield and franchise quality if already held; the absence of a valuation discount and the accretion-runoff headwind argue against adding here. Look for either a pullback into the low-to-mid $30s or confirmation that core ex-accretion earnings are stabilizing before upgrading.
