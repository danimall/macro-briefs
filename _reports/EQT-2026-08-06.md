## Executive Summary

EQT is the largest US natural gas producer by volume and, since the July 2024 Equitrans merger, the only Appalachian producer of scale that owns the gathering and long-haul transmission systems moving the bulk of its own molecules. Two things have changed since our last review, and they point in opposite directions.

The first is balance-sheet quality, which improved faster than we modeled. Total debt fell from $7,800.3M at December 31, 2025 to $5,655.7M at June 30, 2026, with $2,122.9M of principal retired in the first half (a ~$1.4B March tender across the 3.90% '27, 4.50% '29 and 6.375% '29 notes, a $344.9M redemption of the 6.50% '27s, and $392.9M of 3.125% '26s at maturity). Net debt of $5,542.9M is approximately 0.9-1.0x our FY2026 adjusted EBITDA scenario, revolver covenant utilization is 16.4% against a 65% ceiling, and first-half interest coverage was 14.8x. Ratings sit at Baa3 (positive outlook), BBB and BBB. The stated $5.0B long-term debt target is essentially reached. Separately, the July 21 print raised FY2026 volume guidance by roughly 90 Bcfe to 2,375-2,450 Bcfe *while cutting* capex guidance $25M, attributed to compression investments shallowing base decline. Raising volumes on lower capital is the highest-quality guidance revision an E&P can deliver.

The second is earnings momentum, which is deteriorating. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut 30.1% over 90 days (to $0.51 from $0.73) and 26.6% in the last 30 days alone; current-year consensus is down 14.2% to $4.14 and next year down 7.6% to $4.37. The mechanism is straightforward: EQT has hedged only ~18-21% of 2H26 volumes and ~16% of 2027, and Q2 realized pricing was $2.65/Mcfe against an in-basin differential of −$0.67 guided to widen to −$0.75/−$0.65 in Q3. Every $0.25/Mcf move in gas is worth roughly $0.58 in annual diluted EPS after hedges and tax. The stock's decline from the March 27 high of $68.24 (about 25%, though note the 52-week high in the vendor feed is $68.24) is a rational response to a lower forward strip, not a market error we can name.

At $51.19 EQT trades at 12.4x current-year and 11.7x next-year consensus, roughly 6.1-6.4x FY2026E EBITDA on our scenario, and a ~7% free cash flow yield attributable to EQT. That is fair-to-modestly-cheap for a low-cost, investment-grade producer with a genuine demand-side option in PJM power and LNG. It is not the asymmetry required to override falling estimates in a sector where cheapness has repeatedly been a trap. We maintain **HOLD** with a base case of $58 (+13%), a bull case of $72 (+41%) and a bear case of $41 (−20%).

## Company Overview

EQT holds 28.0 Tcfe of proved reserves (26,416 Bcf gas, 272 MMbbl liquids) as of December 31, 2025, of which 20,581 Bcfe is proved developed. Ninety-three percent sits in the Marcellus, concentrated in Pennsylvania (17,253 Bcfe) and West Virginia (9,927 Bcfe). Acreage totals ~2.26 million gross acres with 4,264 gross productive wells. Roughly 90% of the production mix is dry gas, giving EQT the cleanest cost structure and the highest gas-price beta among large-cap producers, and the least liquids cushion when Henry Hub weakens.

Three segments: Upstream, Gathering (~1,995 miles of pipeline, ~650,000 horsepower of compression) and Transmission (~950 miles of FERC-regulated interstate pipe, 5.0 Bcf/d capacity, 44 Bcf of working gas storage). Equity-method interests in the Mountain Valley Pipeline complex — Mainline, Southgate and Boost — carry at $3,802.8M at June 30, 2026, with EQT's Series A and Series C stakes lifted from 49.3% to 53.2% in March 2026 via a $213.9M purchase of Consolidated Edison's affiliate interests. Headcount is 1,523.

The integration story requires precision. Upstream provides 73% of Gathering throughput / 76% of Gathering revenue and 69% of Transmission throughput / 61% of Transmission revenue. The midstream businesses are predominantly captive — valuable for capital coordination, system-pressure optimization and takeaway control, but not revenue diversification away from gas prices. FY2026 third-party midstream revenue is guided at $600-700M, which is the honest measure of non-commodity revenue. Similarly, the $10,868.1M of remaining performance obligations at June 30 is 75.6% affiliate ($8,218.9M) and eliminated in consolidation; only ~$2,635M is third-party. EQT does not define RPO as backlog, and it should not be read as contracted external revenue.

The 29% headline revenue decline is mostly a derivative artifact: the prior-year quarter carried a $720.0M unrealized gain versus $44.6M this year. Commodity sales fell 5.3% on an 11.7% volume increase because realized pricing dropped $0.16/Mcfe. Adjusted EBITDA actually rose 3.9% year-over-year. The reported "miss" was one cent on adjusted EPS ($0.39 vs $0.40 consensus); the GAAP $0.34 is not the basis consensus was set against.

**Cost position is the durable asset.** Total per-unit operating cost was $1.03/Mcfe in Q2 (down from $1.08), with lease operating expense at $0.10 and depletion $0.95. All-in cash-plus-depletion cost of roughly $1.98/Mcfe against a $2.65 realization means EQT still cleared roughly $0.67/Mcfe in one of the weakest pricing quarters on record, running 2-3 rigs and 2-3 frac crews against a ~6.97 Bcfe/d program. That is the reason the equity is a call option rather than a distressed situation.

**Cash flow, with the appropriate haircuts.** First-half GAAP operating cash flow of $4,103.1M includes a $373.7M working-capital tailwind as winter-priced receivables collected (AR fell from $1,458.0M to $835.1M); EQT's own adjusted figure is $3,729.4M. Neither should be annualized — 1H realized $3.85/Mcfe versus Q2's $2.65, and the first half is structurally the strong half for a dry-gas Appalachian producer. Company-defined free cash flow was $2,398.8M in 1H26, $2,161.2M attributable to EQT; Q2 alone was $453.7M / $329.7M attributable. Cash taxes are currently negligible ($518.3M of first-half tax expense was $491.6M deferred), a real near-term tailwind that reverses and should not be capitalized.

**Our FY2026 sensitivity (not guidance).** Taking the volume guidance midpoint (~2,412 Bcfe) less 1H actuals leaves ~1,160 Bcfe in 2H. At NYMEX ~$3.10, a −$0.70 differential and Btu uplift, 2H realizations land near $2.70/Mcfe, implying 2H adjusted EBITDA around $2.1B and FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $5.9-6.2B ($5.4-5.7B attributable). On that basis net debt/EBITDA is ~0.9x, and FY2026E FCF attributable is $2.3-2.7B against remaining capex of $1.35-1.56B — a ~7-8% FCF yield on the current market capitalization. At $2.60 NYMEX the EBITDA estimate falls roughly $500M and the yield compresses toward 5%.

The $0.66 annual dividend costs ~$413M (1.29% yield), consuming about 17% of estimated attributable FCF. Coverage is not in question; the yield offers no valuation support.

**One structural claim to carry.** EQT consolidates PipeBox LLC, in which a Blackstone Credit affiliate holds Class B units purchased for $3.5B. Available cash is distributed 60% to Blackstone / 40% to EQT until a "Base Return" is achieved. Distributions were $355M in FY2025 and $238.0M in 1H26, guided to $430-470M for FY2026, yet the disclosed remaining Base Return moved only from ~$3.41B (December) to ~$3.4B (June). The natural reading is that the hurdle accretes at approximately the rate of current distributions, making this economically closer to a participating preferred than to common equity — though the figures are rounded to $0.1B, Blackstone contributed a further $98.4M in the period, and we have not read the JV agreement, so the accretion rate is unverified. The practical discipline: use adjusted EBITDA *attributable to EQT* ($1,067.0M in Q2, 11% below consolidated) and treat the ~$3.4B as a debt-like claim when computing enterprise value.

**Financial health: 7.0.** Rapid, verified deleveraging into an investment-grade structure with enormous covenant headroom, a well-covered dividend and a cost structure that generates cash at trough pricing. Marked down from higher only for the Blackstone claim, ~$590M of undiscounted LNG vessel lease commitments not yet on balance sheet, and the fact that coverage on Q2 alone (5.8x) is materially thinner than the first-half figure.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is modest and capital-efficient rather than expansive. FY2026 guidance of 2,375-2,450 Bcfe compares with 2,382 Bcfe produced in FY2025 — call it 0-3% growth, with Olympus Energy (closed July 2025) contributing 96 Bcfe in the first half. Reserve replacement was 103% organic (2,445 Bcfe added against 2,382 produced), 177% including Olympus, with zero PUDs older than five years and 2,380 Bcfe converted to proved developed in 2025. This is disciplined maintenance-mode operation, not a growth machine.

The genuine growth thesis is on the demand side, and it is about *price realization* rather than volume. EQT's core problem has never been cost; it is that roughly half its gas is priced against a chronically oversupplied local market (the 10-K discloses ~4.3 Bcf/d of firm takeaway against ~6.7-7.0 Bcfe/d of production, with 49% of volumes reaching markets outside Appalachia). Three developments attack that:

1. **CPV Shay (announced July 21, 2026).** A ten-year definitive agreement to supply 325,000 Dth/d to CPV's ~2 GW gas-fired plant in Doddridge County, WV, with **pricing linked to PJM power prices**. EQT is the gas seller; CPV develops and owns the plant. The volume is ~4.7% of current production; the novelty is converting a slice of output from a depressed in-basin index to a scarce-power index. Management's language ("another long-term gas supply agreement") implies prior deals of this type, but we could not verify the size or terms of earlier agreements — the cumulative contracted power-demand book is not established.
2. **LNG.** A new five-year, 0.5 MTPA offtake with an unnamed Asian integrated energy company from 2028, projected by EQT to add ~$45M to 2028 FCF at recent strip. This sits alongside 4.5 MTPA of 20-year offtake (3.0 MTPA as early as 2030, 1.5 MTPA in 2031) and up to 2.0 MTPA of tolling — roughly 0.6 Bcf/d or ~9% of production, of which 1.0 MTPA offtake and 2.0 MTPA tolling relate to projects that have **not reached FID**.
3. **Takeaway.** MVP Southgate has all key approvals (FERC Virginia March 2026, North Carolina June 2026) at $370-430M cost; MVP Boost is filed with FERC. Both carry a **mid-2028** expected in-service date per the 10-Q, notwithstanding press-release language about accelerating $85M of contributions to complete construction by year-end 2026 — treat mid-2028 as the filing fact. Two firm gas sales agreements for up to 1.2 Bcf/d beginning 2027 are **conditional on Williams' Transco Southeast Supply Enhancement entering service** and are excluded from EQT's contractual commitments table. That is ~18% of production whose premium-market realization depends on a third party's construction and permitting.

The near-term arithmetic is unambiguous and negative. Consensus for the current quarter has been cut from $0.73 to $0.51 in 90 days; current year from $4.82 to $4.14; next year from $4.73 to $4.37. Next-year consensus ($4.37) implies only 5.6% EPS growth off the reduced current-year base ($4.14). This is a gas-price revision, not a company-execution failure, but it is what the equity earns on.

**Growth prospects: 5.3.** Capital efficiency is improving (compression-driven decline mitigation, longest lateral in shale history at 29,000+ feet, 9% capex underspend in Q2), and the PJM/LNG contracting strategy is the right answer to the basin's structural discount. But the volume trajectory is flat, the price realization improvements land in 2027-2030 and depend partly on third parties, and consensus earnings are being revised down across every horizon.

Enterprise value at $51.19: $32.02B equity plus ~$5.60B net debt (adjusting June 30's $5,542.9M for the July 15 repayment of $115M of 7.75% debentures and the $77M revolver draw for Blackline) = **~$37.6B**. Treating the Blackstone Base Return as debt-like gives ~$41.0B.

The peer comparison cuts both ways. EQT is at a 35% discount to the E&P peer median P/E of 19.2x, but that median is populated by oil-levered names (PR 22.4x, OVV 19.2x, CTRA 15.0x) whose commodity mix and TPL's royalty structure are not comparable. Against Expand Energy at 6.5x, EQT carries a substantial premium — defensible on owned midstream, MVP equity, cost structure and ratings, but it means EQT is not the cheap gas name in its own subsector.

The reserve anchor: FY2025 PV-10 was $25,594M at the SEC 12-month average price of $2.749/Mcf, and $29,798M on five-year strip. EV therefore sits $8-15B above proved PV-10, which the market implicitly assigns to third-party midstream, MVP earnings, undeveloped inventory beyond proved (~4,000 gross locations per management, a figure that includes probable and possible resource), and gas-price optionality above strip. Note two cautions: the PV-10 increase from $9,844M (2024) to $25,594M (2025) is overwhelmingly the SEC reference price, not value creation; and upstream PV-10 is already net of gathering and transmission costs paid to EQT's own midstream, so midstream value cannot be layered on at full freight.

Analyst targets span $52 (low) to $81 (high), mean $67.68, median $68.00, with sentiment improving (strong buys 3 → 4; holds 6 → 4 over three months). The current price sits *below* the low target of $52. Ordinarily we treat consensus targets as lagging on inflection names with rising estimates. That condition is absent here — estimates are falling 5-30% depending on horizon — so the $67.68 mean is best read as a strip-lagging artifact that will be revised down if gas stays soft, not as a floor.

**Valuation: 6.3.** The multiple is reasonable and the FCF yield is real, but the E in the P/E is a direct function of a gas curve we cannot forecast, and a compressing multiple on falling estimates is not the same as mispricing. We require an independent catalyst before paying up for cheapness in this sector; the catalysts here (Transco SSE, MVP in-service, power contracting scale) sit in 2027-2028.

## Competitive Landscape

EQT's structural advantages are specific and verifiable. Per-unit operating cost of $1.03/Mcfe with $0.10 lease operating expense is best-in-class; running 2-3 rigs against ~6.97 Bcfe/d demonstrates capital intensity peers cannot match. Owned gathering internalizes what other Appalachian producers pay away and enables the system-pressure and compression work that just produced a volume raise on lower capital. Equity ownership of 53.2% of MVP Series A/C gives physical access to Southeast markets competitors must contract for. And EQT has demonstrated willingness to curtail into weak prices — Q2 outperformance was partly attributed to *lower-than-expected* price-related curtailments — an option that producers with heavier debt or firmer midstream commitments cannot always exercise.

Trajectory is improving on two axes (cost per unit down year-over-year, market access expanding through Southgate approvals and PJM-linked contracting) and unchanged-to-negative on one (90% dry gas means no liquids cushion; FY2025 realizations were gas $3.13/Mcf, NGLs ex-ethane $38.04/bbl, ethane $8.01/bbl, oil $49.08/bbl). Basin-level relief arrives from four directions in 2027-2028 — Southgate (+0.55 Bcf/d firm to EQT), Boost, Transco SSE, and local gas-fired power for data centers. If several land, Appalachian basis narrows structurally and the ~51% in-basin exposure stops being punitive. If they slip, volume growth hits the same wall it always has.

One caution on how EQT's contracting is discussed publicly: a revenue-mix shift toward power and LNG counterparties does not by itself establish customer diversification. The 10-K states no dependence on a single customer, but we have no concentration table confirming the direction of travel. And the Blackline Midstream acquisition ($77M, closed July 21) is backward integration into a channel EQT already supplies ~60% of, with company-projected ~$15M average annual FCF over 2027-2031 — sensible and small, not strategically material.

**Competitive position: 6.7.**

## Risk Assessment

**1. Deliberate under-hedging is the dominant risk.** Per the July 14 hedge table, EQT holds 125 MMDth for Q3 2026 and 108 MMDth for Q4 — roughly 18-21% of guided volumes. FY2027 hedges total 387 MMDth ≈ 1.06 Bcf/d, ~16% of a comparable program. The 2027 structures are thinner than they look: long $3.00 puts are paired with **sold $2.50 puts** on the same volumes, so below $2.50/Dth the protection is exhausted, and fixed-price swaps at $3.16 on 65-66 MMDth cap upside in Q2/Q3 2027. The 10-Q's own sensitivity confirms how little exposure is transferred — a ±10% NYMEX move shifts the derivative book only $117-124M. Our estimate: $0.25/Mcf on ~2,400 Bcfe, net of hedges and at the disclosed 23.6% blended tax rate, is roughly $0.58 per diluted share annually.

**2. In-basin basis.** Q2 basis was −$0.67/Mcf; FY2026 guidance is −$0.55 to −$0.35 with Q3 guided at −$0.75 to −$0.65. Half of production prices against a congested local market until new takeaway lands.

**3. Third-party execution risk on the growth legs.** The conditional 1.2 Bcf/d of firm sales requires Transco SSE in service. MVP Southgate and Boost are mid-2028 per the 10-Q. Non-FID LNG projects underpin 1.0 MTPA of offtake and 2.0 MTPA of tolling in the book.

**4. Fixed-rate midstream contracts.** 95% of contracted firm transmission capacity is under negotiated rates with limited or no cost-escalation adjustment — nominally locked revenue against costs that are not.

**5. Legacy Equitrans regulatory exposure.** Rager Mountain drew a PHMSA notice of probable violation proposing a $939,000 penalty (October 2025) and four second-degree misdemeanor criminal charges against Equitrans, L.P. from a Pennsylvania grand jury (July 2025); the Pratt field carries an open PA AG complaint and PUC/PHMSA investigations. The dollar amounts are immaterial to a $32B company. The relevant risk is permitting friction and standing in the two states holding 96% of proved reserves.

**6. Structured-equity leakage.** The Blackstone 60/40 sweep against a ~$3.4B claim that is not visibly amortizing means EQT receives 40% of a meaningful midstream asset base's cash flow for an extended period. This is disclosed, guided, and already deducted from EQT's "attributable" metrics — the risk is to analysts using consolidated figures.

**7. Capital-allocation timing.** No repurchases in Q2 despite $1.4B remaining under an authorization that **expires December 31, 2026**, with the stock at $50.15 in early July. Cumulative buybacks since December 2021 total only $622.1M. Debt reduction was the chosen use of cash. Defensible against the $5.0B target and a probable ratings upgrade; it also means management's own capital actions did not corroborate a cheapness view at the lows.

The Q2 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors from the FY2025 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 38.9% sits 8.3 points above 30.6% one-year realized (66th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation — 38.9% at one week against 33.5% at six months — consistent with near-term event risk being priced. The 1.9% implied move on the one-week expiry is mechanical and should be discounted; the informative tenors are the three-month (8.1% implied) and six-month (15.1%) figures, both of which price a wide gas-driven distribution. Put/call open interest of 1.41 shows accumulated downside protection, while the data source characterizes current skew as call-heavy (−21.5%) and put/call volume at 0.79 leans to calls — recent flow is positioned for upside against a standing book of puts. Since the prior snapshot ATM IV fell from 41.3% to 38.9% while the price rose 3.6% and put/call OI eased from 1.51 to 1.41, a modest de-risking. Short interest is low at 3.8% of float and 2.5 days to cover, so there is neither crowded bearish positioning nor squeeze fuel. Net read: the options market corroborates our fundamental framing — elevated near-term uncertainty tied to the gas curve, no priced credit or solvency concern, and no directional conviction worth overriding fundamentals.

**Risk factors: 5.0.** The commodity beta is intrinsic to a 90%-dry-gas producer and is largely reflected in a ~25% drawdown, but EQT's ~80% unhedged posture amplifies that beta beyond peers by management choice, the 2027 collar structure has a hole below $2.50, and two of the three growth legs depend on other companies' construction schedules.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $72 (+41%)

Gas prices normalize toward the $3.25-3.50 strip on LNG export ramp and gas-fired power demand; FY2027 EBITDA attributable reaches $6.5-7.0B against essentially no incremental capital. Compression-driven decline mitigation proves durable, letting EQT hold 2,400+ Bcfe with 2-3 rigs and pushing FCF attributable above $3B (~9-10% yield at today's cap). Moody's upgrades from Baa3 to Baa2, lowering the cost of capital. Transco SSE enters service in 2027, activating the conditional 1.2 Bcf/d of premium firm sales and narrowing in-basin basis toward the guidance low end. The PJM-linked contracting model scales beyond CPV Shay, converting a growing share of output from a −$0.67 discount to power-parity pricing. With debt at target and the December 2026 authorization expiry forcing a decision, management redirects $1.4B+ into repurchases at 6-7x EBITDA. At 7.5x EV/EBITDA on $6.75B attributable EBITDA less ~$5.5B net debt, the equity is worth ~$72. The premium-strike option grants ($90/$95/$100) sketch management's own version of this case.

### Bear Case — $41 (−20%)

Henry Hub settles below $2.75 through 2027 as Appalachian and Haynesville supply outruns LNG start-ups. With ~80% of volumes unhedged and 2027 protection exhausted below $2.50/Dth, FY2027 EBITDA attributable falls toward $4.5-4.8B and FCF toward $1.2-1.5B, taking the yield to 4-5% and leverage back above 1.2x. Q3 basis at the guided −$0.75 forces heavier price-related curtailments, so the volume raise does not convert to cash. Transco SSE slips past 2027 and MVP Southgate's mid-2028 date drifts, deferring the premium-market thesis. The Blackstone sweep continues absorbing $430-470M annually with the Base Return not amortizing. Consensus, which has already cut the current year 14.2%, cuts another 15%, and the multiple that looked like 11.7x forward turns out to be 14-15x on realized earnings. At 6.0-6.5x on $4.8B attributable EBITDA the equity is worth $41 — below the 52-week low of $47.94.

The honest weighting: neither case turns on EQT's execution, which has been good. Both turn on a gas curve where we hold no informational advantage. That is precisely the condition under which a HOLD is the correct answer rather than a hedge.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026), which will test whether the raised volume guidance survives Q3 basis of −$0.75 to −$0.65 and whether the compression-driven decline benefit persists past its typically front-loaded window. Two other dated checkpoints: the buyback authorization expiring December 31, 2026, and any Moody's action on the Baa3 positive outlook.

**Base case: $58 (+13% from $51.19).** Math: 13.3x next-year consensus EPS of $4.37; cross-checked at 7.0-7.3x EV/EBITDA on $5.5B FY2026E EBITDA attributable less ~$5.6B net debt, and at a 7% yield on ~$2.5B ($4.00/share) of attributable FCF. Bull $72 (+41%), bear $41 (−20%). The reward/risk to base is unfavorable (0.65:1); to bull versus bear it is roughly 2:1. That asymmetry does not clear our BUY bar in a sector where our cheap-multiple calls have historically underperformed.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. **Estimate momentum turns.** Current-year consensus stops falling and revises up for two consecutive months (from $4.14), or forward 12-month EPS revisions turn positive. This is the single condition we weight most heavily.
2. **Price below $46** with the balance sheet intact (net debt ≤$5.8B, leverage ≤1.1x), which would put the stock at ~10.5x next-year consensus and a ~9% FCF yield — the level at which cheapness compensates for commodity beta.
3. **A named catalyst converts:** Transco SSE confirmed in service (activating the 1.2 Bcf/d conditional firm sales), a Moody's upgrade to Baa2, or a disclosed increase in PJM-linked/data-center supply volumes beyond CPV Shay to a cumulative 1.0 Bcf/d or more with terms verifiable in filings.

**Downgrade to AVOID if any of:**

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Overall score 6.05, a modest improvement from 5.7 in mid-July that reflects new, verified information rather than price action — the stock is roughly flat versus our last review. What changed is the June 30 balance sheet ($2,146.7M of net debt eliminated in six months, leverage at ~0.9-1.0x, 16.4% covenant utilization, coverage at 14.8x for the half) and the quality of the July 21 guidance revision (volumes up ~90 Bcfe, capex down $25M, driven by decline-rate mitigation rather than incremental drilling). Financial health and capital efficiency are demonstrably better than we credited a month ago.

What has not changed, and what caps the call, is that consensus EPS is falling across every horizon — 30.1% for the current quarter, 14.2% for the current year, 7.6% for next year over 90 days — because roughly 80% of 2026-2027 volumes are exposed to spot gas by design. The prior trajectory decline from 7.0 (May 19) to 5.7 (July 19) was the correct read on that dynamic, and the estimate data confirms it is still in force. Growth prospects stay at 5.3 and risk factors at 5.0 on that basis; financial health rises to 7.0.

At 11.7x next-year consensus, ~6.1-6.4x FY2026E EBITDA and a ~7% attributable FCF yield, EQT is priced fairly for what it is: the lowest-cost large-scale US gas producer, now investment-grade with covenant headroom to spare, holding a genuine but back-loaded option on converting Appalachian discount gas into PJM power and Gulf Coast LNG pricing. The base case offers 13% upside against 20% downside. That is a position to own, not one to build. The trigger that would change our mind is specific and monitorable: a turn in earnings revisions, or a price in the mid-$40s with the balance sheet intact.
