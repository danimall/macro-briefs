**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TD SYNNEX is the largest pure-play global IT distributor, formed by the 2021 merger of SYNNEX and Tech Data, with FY2025 revenue of $62.5B and a market cap of ~$22.8B. The investment case has shifted in the last two quarters from thin-margin distribution beta to AI-infrastructure beneficiary. The lever is Hyve Solutions, the custom server/storage business serving hyperscale and AI-compute customers, which the company elevated to a standalone reportable segment in Q1 FY2026. In that quarter Hyve generated roughly 32% of consolidated operating income on roughly 13% of revenue at a 7.23% operating margin, multiples of the ~2% margin in core distribution. Q1 FY2026 consolidated results showed revenue +18.1% YoY, operating income +18.5%, net income +95%, GAAP diluted EPS +104%, and non-GAAP EPS +69%.

The market is repricing the stock for this mix shift, and consensus is catching up rather than running ahead. Current-year non-GAAP EPS estimates have risen 16.5% over 90 days to $17.04, and next-year estimates are up 18.5% to $19.07, while the company beat by 41.6% in the most recent quarter. At $283.81, SNX trades at ~16.7x current-year and ~14.9x next-year non-GAAP EPS, below the technology-distributor peer median P/E of 17.8x despite a materially higher growth rate, a PEG near 0.6. Buybacks have been executed at $108–156 per share against the current price, and share count is down ~16% since FY2022.

We rate SNX a BUY with a 12-month base-case target of $305 (~7.5% upside), a bull case of $343, and a bear case of $221. The thesis carries genuine offsets: roughly a quarter of revenue rides on two terminable OEM agreements (Apple ~12%, HP ~10%), one Hyve hyperscaler customer is ~11% of total revenue, ~71% of Hyve's revenue is recognized net (which flatters reported margins), and the model depends on supplier-finance and AR-securitization programs staying open and cheap in a higher-for-longer rate environment. These are the reasons the score sits at 6.4 rather than 7+. They are watch items, not thesis-killers, and they are at least partly reflected in a forward multiple that has stayed reasonable as the stock has doubled.

## Company Overview

TD SYNNEX aggregates more than 200,000 SKUs from approximately 2,500 OEMs and resells to a fragmented base of more than 150,000 resellers — VARs, MSPs, integrators, and retailers. The business is high-volume and low-margin (FY2025 gross margin 6.99%, operating margin 2.26%, net margin 1.3%), with high asset turnover (1.83x) and a tight cash conversion cycle (16 days at FY2025 year-end). It employs ~24,000 full-time co-workers plus ~6,000 temporary, headquartered in Fremont, CA and Clearwater, FL.

As of Q1 FY2026 the company reports four segments: Americas distribution, Europe distribution, APJ distribution, and Hyve Solutions. Two product portfolios cut across distribution — Endpoint Solutions (PCs, mobile, peripherals, print) and the higher-margin Advanced Solutions (data center, hybrid cloud, security, networking, servers, software). Hyve designs and contract-manufactures custom infrastructure for hyperscale and AI-compute customers under customer-owned procurement models. Hyve is the strategically pivotal, higher-margin business and the focus of the current re-rating.

## Financial Analysis

The income statement understates the underlying business momentum because of a mix shift toward revenue recognized net (software, cloud, SaaS, and Hyve's customer-owned procurement model). Approximately $2.8B (~5%) of FY2025 reported revenue growth was suppressed by this shift, which simultaneously lifts gross margin. Reported FY2025 revenue was $62.5B (+6.9%), net income $828M (+20%), GAAP diluted EPS $9.95, non-GAAP EPS $13.19, operating income +18.5% to $1.41B, operating cash flow $1.53B, and free cash flow $1.39B (2.2% FCF margin). Cash stood at $2.4B.

Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended 2/28/26) saw gross margin expand to 7.30% from 6.87% and operating margin to 2.85% from 2.10% on revenue of $17.16B (+13.2% constant currency). Operating income improvement was broad: APJ +182%, Europe +59%, Hyve +66%. Hyve generated $2.15B of revenue (+24%) at a 7.23% operating margin (up from 5.42%), contributing $155M of the $489M consolidated operating income. A $22.4M ($0.28) one-time gain on the sale of equity securities was included in GAAP but excluded from non-GAAP.

Two mechanical points qualify the margin story. First, ~71% of Hyve sales are now recognized net under customer-owned procurement, which lifts Hyve's reported margin by ~262 bps and means reported Hyve revenue dramatically understates underlying volume. Second, the net-vs-gross shift is the silent driver of consolidated margin expansion. Investors comparing headline revenue growth understate true momentum, but they should also recognize that part of the margin gain is presentational rather than operating leverage.

On the balance sheet, total debt is ~$4.7B (Q1 FY2026): $3.6B Senior Notes (maturities 2026–2035) plus a $0.75B term loan, against $700M of 1.75% Senior Notes due August 2026 covered by cash and liquidity. D/E of 3.05x and an Altman Z-score of 2.65 (grey zone) screen as elevated, but for a distributor these headlines mislead — the leverage is largely working-capital financing (trade payables ~$18B, AR securitization, supplier-finance programs of $3.4B). At quarter-end nothing was drawn on the $3.5B revolver or the $1.5B AR securitization facility, the company was covenant-compliant, and Piotroski signals were 5/5 on available metrics (positive NI, ROA, OCF; OCF > NI; current ratio 1.21).

The genuine financial risk is funding-market access, not solvency. Q1 used ~$896M of operating cash to fund a ~$1.5B inventory build (DIO rose from 53 to 62 days), and AR discount fees nearly doubled YoY to $20.8M from $12.0M. The model is healthy when growing but dependent on supplier-finance and AR-securitization programs remaining open and cheap. A higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of that financing at the margin. Financial health is solid but not pristine: strong FCF and accretive capital returns are offset by working-capital intensity and financing dependence.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile carries an inflection-grade signal. Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 18.1% with EPS more than doubling, and consensus estimate revisions are broad and positive: current-quarter EPS up 20.3% over 90 days, current-year up 16.5% to $17.04, and next-year up 18.5% to $19.07. The 41.6% beat in the most recent quarter (versus a 1.7% beat the prior quarter) signals the visibility of forward estimates is improving toward the upside. While 90-day revisions of +16-18% sit below the strongest inflection thresholds, the combination of ~18% revenue growth, rising estimates across all four reported periods, and a standalone-segment disclosure that newly reveals the Hyve profit engine constitutes a durable positive growth case rather than a single-quarter pop.

The structural driver is Hyve's exposure to hyperscale and AI-compute infrastructure spending, which puts SNX in the ODM/EMS competitive set (Jabil, Celestica, Quanta, Wiwynn) riding the AI capex wave. The core distribution business is separately benefiting from an enterprise refresh cycle (Windows refresh, AI PCs) and a mix shift toward higher-margin Advanced Solutions, security, software, and services. UBS flagged SNX (alongside Jabil) in June 2026 as a likely "beat-and-raise" on AI demand.

Two qualifiers temper the score. The reported revenue growth is flattered by the net-vs-gross shift, so underlying unit growth is harder to verify than the headline implies. And next-year non-GAAP EPS growth (~12% over the current year) decelerates from the current-year pace (~29% over FY2025), consistent with a normalization off an exceptional Q1. The mechanism that drives durability is Hyve's mix climbing as a share of operating income; the risk is that the same mix is concentrated and contract-light.

## Valuation Assessment

At $283.81, SNX trades at ~16.7x current-year non-GAAP EPS and ~14.9x next-year, a PEG near 0.6 on current-year growth. The peer median P/E for technology distributors is 17.8x (CDW 15.7, ARW 15.9, NSIT 19.3, PLUS 17.8, AVT 34.6). SNX therefore trades roughly in line with or slightly below peers on a trailing-multiple basis while delivering materially higher near-term EPS growth and a higher-margin AI-infrastructure mix that peers (other than the EMS names) lack.

A low multiple absent a catalyst is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal. Here there is a specific, dated catalyst (Q2 FY2026 results June 25), an estimate-revision tailwind that consensus has not finished pricing, and a structural margin-mix shift that is newly visible via the standalone Hyve segment. On inflection stocks with reasonable multiples (forward P/E well below 30x, PEG well below 1.5), analyst targets tend to lag and to revise upward as consensus catches up. The mean analyst target of $269.81 sits ~5% below the current price and the sentiment trend is labeled "degrading," but the median target is $298 (above current), the high is $320, and the buy/hold distribution improved over the past quarter (strong buys rose from 3 to 4, holds fell from 2 to 1). The mean target's lag versus the median is consistent with consensus being slow to mark up a stock that has just delivered a +104% EPS quarter.

Base-case fair value of $305 applies 16x to next-year non-GAAP EPS of $19.07, a modest premium to the current next-year multiple, justified by the AI-mix re-rating, and consistent with the $298 median target. The bull case of $343 (~18x next-year) approaches the $320 high analyst target. The bear case of $221 (~13x current-year) reflects multiple compression toward the low end of distributor peers if Hyve growth disappoints, and sits near the $200 analyst floor.

## Competitive Landscape

SNX is one of three scaled pure-play IT distributors alongside Ingram Micro and Arrow, competing also against Westcon-Comstor, ScanSource, ALSO, and Esprinet. The distribution moat is scale, vendor breadth, supplier-financing capacity, and logistics density; the underlying business is commoditized and thin-margin, where the competitive level is unremarkable but the trajectory is improving as mix shifts toward Advanced Solutions, security, software, and services.

Hyve competes against ODM/EMS players (Jabil, Celestica, Flex, Quanta, Wiwynn) for hyperscale infrastructure builds, and its trajectory is favorable as the segment grows into the AI-infrastructure spend wave. The structural caveat is that Hyve operates under customer-owned procurement models with limited or no volume guarantees and end-of-life protection, so its position depends on remaining a preferred build partner to a small number of hyperscalers rather than on durable switching costs. Tariffs are largely passed through to customers, a structural advantage that insulates margins from trade-policy volatility. On balance, the competitive position is stable-to-improving, with the improvement concentrated in the higher-margin, higher-concentration Hyve business.

## Risk Assessment

The risks specific to SNX are concentration, accounting optics, and financing dependence rather than sector cyclicality (which is permanently priced into distributor multiples).

**Supplier concentration.** Apple and HP Inc. together represent roughly a quarter of revenue across two OEMs on short-term, non-exclusive, terminable agreements. A shift to direct sales or a material agreement change at either would move results.

**Customer concentration in Hyve.** One customer was ~11% of total revenue in FY2025 (12% in FY2024). Hyve's systems-design business carries heavy customer concentration, requires working-capital and infrastructure investment, and often has no volume guarantees. A single hyperscaler pullback could swing consolidated results, the flip side of the AI growth story driving the re-rating.

**Margin and accounting optics.** With 6–7% gross margins and partly fixed costs, small swings in vendor rebates, price protection, or net-vs-gross mix move operating income materially. The recent margin gains lean meaningfully on the net-revenue presentation shift, which is presentational rather than fundamental operating leverage.

**Working-capital and financing dependence.** Supplier-finance ($3.4B), AR securitization ($1.5B capacity), and receivables sales ($2.0B sold) fund the business. AR discount fees nearly doubled YoY, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises this cost. Tightening of any of these programs would pressure liquidity.

**Other.** Goodwill and intangibles of ~$7.9B (90%+ of equity) from the Tech Data merger carry no impairment to date but represent a stress point. A recurring pattern of cyber intrusions (2021, 2022, 2023, plus a 2024 partner breach) has not been deemed material but is persistent. International revenue (48%) carries tariff/geopolitical and China exposure. A legacy French Apple antitrust matter (€24.9M fine, paid) is being appealed but deemed remote.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 47.0% against 30.0% one-year historical volatility — rich, with the term structure in backwardation (47.0% near vs 43.5% long-dated), which prices imminent event risk around the Q2 FY2026 report on June 25. The nearest-expiry implied move is roughly ±6–11%. Positioning is not defensive: the put/call volume ratio is 0.11, the put/call open-interest ratio is 0.05, and skew is balanced at 1.8%, indicating call-heavy, constructive options positioning into the print rather than put-side hedging that would contradict a bullish fundamental view. (A reported 317.4% ATM IV in the change snapshot is treated as a corrupted thin-option artifact and disregarded.) The market is pricing a meaningful earnings event but leaning bullish, consistent with the beat-and-raise thesis while flagging that a Q2 miss would be sharply repriced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($343):** Hyve continues to compound revenue at 20%+ with operating margins above 7%, lifting its share of consolidated operating income above one-third and structurally re-rating SNX toward the EMS/AI-infrastructure cohort rather than the distributor cohort. The enterprise refresh cycle (AI PCs, Windows) and Advanced Solutions mix shift support core distribution. Q2 delivers another beat-and-raise on June 25, consensus marks next-year EPS toward $20+, and the multiple expands to ~18x next-year. Accretive buybacks against a $1.1B authorization further support per-share metrics.

**Bear Case ($221):** A single hyperscaler customer pulls forward or reduces Hyve orders (no volume guarantees), exposing the lumpiness and contract-light nature of the AI exposure. The market re-recognizes that margin expansion was substantially driven by net-vs-gross presentation, not durable operating leverage. Rising AR discount and supplier-finance costs in a higher-for-longer environment compress the financing arbitrage. The multiple compresses toward 13x current-year EPS, and the stock retraces toward the analyst low of $200.

The base case ($305) sits between these: Hyve growth normalizes but stays healthy, core distribution delivers GDP-plus growth with continued mix improvement, and the stock re-rates modestly to 16x next-year EPS as consensus catches up to the inflection. The reasonable forward valuation tilts the risk/reward to the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base-case target: $305** — 16x next-year non-GAAP EPS of $19.07, consistent with the $298 median analyst target; ~7.5% upside from $283.81.

**Bull-case target: $343** — 18x next-year non-GAAP EPS; ~21% upside; approaches the $320 high analyst target.

**Bear-case target: $221** — 13x current-year non-GAAP EPS ($17.04); ~22% downside; near the $200 analyst floor.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the first checkpoint at Q2 FY2026 results on June 25, 2026 (UBS-flagged beat-and-raise candidate) and subsequent quarterly prints confirming Hyve's growth and margin trajectory.

**Upside/downside from current ($283.81):** +7.5% to base, +21% to bull, −22% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Hyve revenue growth decelerates below ~10% YoY or its operating margin compresses below ~6% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the AI-infrastructure tailwind is fading or a hyperscaler customer is pulling back.
2. Current-year or next-year consensus EPS estimates turn negative (downward revisions) over a 60–90 day window, reversing the present revision momentum.
3. A material adverse change in supplier terms (loss of, or a direct-sales shift by, Apple or HP) or a tightening/repricing of AR-securitization or supplier-finance programs that materially raises financing cost or constrains working-capital capacity.

## Conclusion

TD SYNNEX is transitioning from a thin-margin IT distributor to a recognized AI-infrastructure beneficiary, and the financials are confirming the shift: Q1 FY2026 EPS more than doubled, Hyve now contributes roughly a third of operating income at a 7%+ margin, and consensus EPS estimates have risen 16–19% over 90 days. At ~15–17x forward non-GAAP earnings (PEG ~0.6) versus a peer median of 17.8x, the valuation is reasonable for the growth and is supported by accretive buybacks executed well below the current price. The principal risks — supplier and customer concentration, net-vs-gross accounting optics, and working-capital financing dependence in a higher-for-longer environment — are real and keep the overall score at 6.4 rather than higher, but they are watch items rather than thesis-breakers and are partly reflected in a multiple that has stayed reasonable as the stock doubled. With a near-term catalyst on June 25, constructive options positioning, and a favorable subsector backdrop, we rate SNX a **BUY** with a 12-month base-case target of $305.
