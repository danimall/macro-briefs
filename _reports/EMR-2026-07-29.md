**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Emerson Electric has completed a multi-year portfolio transformation, shedding its consumer/climate businesses (Copeland to Blackstone for ~$14B, InSinkErator for $3.0B) and reinvesting in process automation, measurement, and industrial software. The result is a higher-margin, more cash-generative business: gross margin expanded from 49.0% in FY23 to 52.8% in FY25, adjusted EBITA margin reached 26.0%, and free cash flow ran at 18.0% of sales ($3.2B). The March 2025 buy-in of the AspenTech minority stake for $7.2B completes a decade-long software build-out, adding recurring asset-optimization software and an industrial-AI layer on top of a dominant valve and measurement installed base (Fisher, Rosemount).

The near-term picture is mixed. FY25 underlying revenue grew only 3% (+2.5% price, +0.5% volume), and Q2 FY26 underlying sales rose just 0.5%, dragged by AspenTech software-renewal timing (~2 points) and Middle East disruption (~1-2 points). Underneath the soft headline, the U.S. grew 9% underlying, Test & Measurement grew 12% on aerospace/defense and semiconductor demand, and backlog sits at ~$9.5B with roughly three-quarters converting within twelve months. The bull case rests on a process-industry cycle inflection that JPMorgan flagged in its July 2026 upgrade; the bear case rests on China weakness (-7% to -13%), FX drag from 59% international exposure, and the price-led revenue mix that limits organic acceleration.

At ~$147 (recently $151.81), EMR trades at roughly 23x the midpoint of FY26 adjusted EPS guidance ($6.45-6.55) and ~35x trailing GAAP EPS. That forward multiple sits below the peer median of 46.5x, but the peer set (AME 34x, ROK 47x, ETN 38x) is a heterogeneous group and Emerson's mid-single-digit underlying growth does not command a premium. Estimate revisions have been flat across every window, and the company has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (by small margins). This is a quality franchise at a fair price with no identifiable near-term catalyst for re-rating, which supports a HOLD. We maintain the HOLD call that has been in place across the prior three reports.

## Company Overview

Emerson is a St. Louis-based industrial automation and software company with fiscal year ending September 30. Following a November 2025 reorganization "upon completion of the transformation," it reports five segments across three groups:

- **Software & Systems (~$5.9B):** Control Systems & Software (DeltaV, Ovation, AspenTech, industrial AI) and Test & Measurement (National Instruments automated test platforms).
- **Intelligent Devices (~$12.4B):** Sensors (Rosemount, Micro Motion) and Final Control (Fisher, ASCO, Bettis valves/actuators/regulators).
- **Safety & Productivity (~$2.1B):** professional mechanical/electrical/plumbing tools.

End markets skew to process industries — energy, LNG, power, chemicals, life sciences — with semiconductor and aerospace exposure via NI. The competitive moat rests on installed-base razor/razorblade dynamics in valves and measurement, where Fisher and Rosemount are category leaders, plus a growing recurring-software layer through AspenTech. The company competes against ABB, Siemens, Honeywell, Schneider Electric, and Yokogawa in automation, and Keysight and Rohde & Schwarz in test.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Continuing-operations revenue moved from $15.2B (FY23) to $17.5B (FY24, mostly the NI acquisition) to $18.0B (FY25). Adjusted diluted EPS was $6.00 in FY25 (+9% YoY). GAAP EPS from continuing operations was $4.03, up 43% as prior-year NI purchase-accounting drags rolled off. FY26 guidance calls for net sales up ~4.5%, underlying up ~3%, GAAP EPS $4.79-4.89, and adjusted EPS $6.45-6.55.

**Cash flow.** FY25 operating cash flow was $3.7B (20.4% of sales), on light capex of ~2.4% of sales. FY26 guidance targets OCF of $4.0-4.1B and FCF of $3.5-3.6B, implying an FCF yield of roughly 4.2% on the current market cap.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt rose to $13.1B at 9/30/25 from $7.7B, funding the $7.2B AspenTech buy-in via ~$4.2B commercial paper plus €500M/$500M/€500M notes. Total-debt-to-capital rose to 39.3% and net-debt-to-net-capital to 36.2%. Interest coverage remains healthy at 8.6x GAAP (11.6x adjusted), and management explicitly guides to deleveraging. Goodwill ($18.2B) plus intangibles ($9.5B) equal ~$27.6B, about 66% of the $42B asset base — a direct consequence of the NI and AspenTech deals.

**On the Altman Z-Score.** A screening Z-Score of 1.36 flags "distress," but this is a mechanical artifact, not a real signal. It is depressed by the large goodwill/intangible base inflating assets, the temporary commercial-paper spike pushing the current ratio to 0.88, and an EBIT/assets input reading zero (data error). A business generating $3.2B FCF at 8.6x interest coverage with investment-grade ratings is not in distress. We do not penalize financial health on this number.

Financial health scores a 6.5: excellent cash generation and coverage, but the leverage taken on for AspenTech and the deferred deleveraging keep it short of a 7+ until net debt visibly declines.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying growth has been mid-single-digit and price-led; FY25 volume grew only 0.5%, so the 3% underlying figure was almost entirely price. Adjusting Q2 FY26 for the ~2-point AspenTech software-renewal timing drag and ~1-2 points of Middle East disruption, underlying demand is running closer to 3-4%, which is respectable but not accelerating.

Bright spots exist. Backlog of ~$9.5B, with ~75% converting within twelve months, provides genuine second-half visibility. JPMorgan's July 2026 upgrade argued that H2-growth-pace concerns overlook this backlog and an improving process-industry cycle, and a new refinery-workflow software launch (July 2026) supports the digitalization narrative.

Offsetting these: China underlying sales are down 7-13% and AMEA is soft broadly; Europe is weak; FX remains a headwind; and AspenTech's upfront term-license recognition creates recurring quarterly lumpiness that obscures the trend.

Estimate revisions are the decisive tell here. Consensus EPS has been essentially flat across every window — current-year $6.51 (+0.2% over 90 days), next-year $7.17 (+0.3% over 90 days). There is no positive revision velocity to signal an earnings inflection the market is missing. The strong-BUY estimate-revision signal that would justify treating analyst targets as floors is absent. Growth prospects score a 5.5: solid but decelerating momentum, with the process-cycle inflection still a thesis rather than a data point in the estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward multiple sits below the peer median of 46.5x, but that comparison flatters Emerson: the peer set is heterogeneous (AME 34x, ROK 47x, WWD 47x, NVT 56x, ETN 38x), and several peers carry higher secular growth (ETN's data-center exposure, NVT's electrical franchise) that Emerson's mid-single-digit underlying growth does not match. On a growth-adjusted basis, EMR is roughly fairly valued, not cheap.

The dividend yield of ~1.5% modestly exceeds the peer median of 0.6% and provides some support, but it is not high enough to anchor a valuation case on its own. The FCF yield of ~4.2% is respectable for a compounder but not a bargain.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $104, high $203, mean $164, median $165. The mean implies ~11% upside, but the wide dispersion reflects genuine disagreement on the process-cycle timing rather than a clear consensus of undervaluation. Because estimate revisions are flat and the stock is not at a cyclical trough with earnings tripling, the "analyst targets are floors" principle does not apply — this is a stable-growth industrial, not an inflection name.

The valuation dimension scores a 4.5. A below-peer-median forward multiple is not, by itself, evidence of mispricing — it reflects the market's accurate read that Emerson grows slower than the higher-multiple names in its cohort. There is no specific, identifiable catalyst for the market to re-rate the stock higher, and flat estimates give no support to a cheapness thesis. A high valuation score requires demonstrating why the market is wrong; here the market's pricing looks reasonable.

## Competitive Landscape

Emerson's competitive position is stable-to-improving, differentiated by the combination of a dominant hardware installed base and a fast-growing software/AI layer. Fisher (control valves) and Rosemount (measurement) are category leaders with sticky, high-margin aftermarket and consumables revenue. The AspenTech acquisition adds recurring asset-optimization software that positions Emerson to monetize industrial digitalization, decarbonization, LNG, and grid investment.

Test & Measurement's growth in aerospace/defense and semiconductor test demonstrates the NI franchise is competing effectively against Keysight and Rohde & Schwarz. Against ABB, Siemens, Honeywell, and Schneider in core process automation, Emerson holds share rather than gaining it aggressively; the U.S. strength is partly cyclical.

The prior report's trajectory marked competitive position down from 7.5 to 6.5, reflecting that price-led revenue growth and soft international markets indicate a franchise holding position rather than expanding share. New data neither reverses nor deepens that trend materially. We hold competitive position at 6.5: a genuine moat with a growing software layer, but not clear share gains.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs and trade policy.** Tariffs diluted gross margin ~0.8 points in Q2 FY26, offset by price actions. The unusual wrinkle: the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in February 2026 that IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs, and CBP opened a refund portal in April 2026. Emerson has booked zero refund benefit, creating un-modeled upside optionality if refunds materialize.

**Software-renewal lumpiness.** AspenTech's upfront term-license recognition creates a ~$(0.09) EPS and 2.5-4.5-point segment-margin headwind in recent quarters. This is timing, not demand loss, but it makes Software & Systems results choppy and depresses headline underlying growth.

**Geographic exposure.** Middle East conflict and China weakness weigh on underlying sales; 59% international exposure carries FX risk.

**Leverage and integration.** The $13.1B total debt represents elevated leverage for the sector, with a management commitment to deleverage that must be executed. Integration of AspenTech and NI carries execution risk, and the narrower, higher-growth portfolio "may encounter more volatility," per the company's own filings.

**Structural.** Cyclical process/capital-spending exposure; asbestos litigation (reserved through 2065, ~$122M — immaterial); AI competitive and regulatory risk (newly expanded risk factor).

Risk factors score 5.5. There are no binary near-term events with catastrophic downside, and much of the known risk (China, tariffs, software lumpiness) is priced. The elevated leverage and the earnings-miss track record keep this below a 7, while the tariff-refund optionality provides modest asymmetric upside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 44.4% versus 31.5% one-year historical volatility — rich by ~13 points, with the term structure in backwardation (44.4% near-term vs 30.9% six-month). This signals the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with the late-July/early-August earnings window. The put/call skew is balanced at -2.6%, put/call volume ratio a low 0.25, and put/call OI ratio 0.39 — positioning is neutral-to-slightly-bullish, not bearish. Short interest is negligible at 2.1% of float. The prior-snapshot ATM IV reading of 308% was a corrupted thin-options artifact; the current 44.4% is the credible figure. Options show no directional bearish signal, but rich near-term vol argues against chasing entry immediately ahead of the print; better to wait for the post-earnings volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$180-200):** The process-industry cycle inflects favorably, backlog converts, U.S. strength broadens, China stabilizes, and Test & Measurement sustains double-digit growth. AspenTech renewal timing normalizes, lifting Software & Systems margins. Tariff refunds materialize as un-modeled upside. Multiple holds at ~25-28x on ~$7.15 next-year adjusted EPS, plus continued dividend growth and buybacks. This is the JPMorgan thesis playing out.

**Bear case (~$105-120):** China weakness deepens, Middle East and European softness persist, the process cycle stalls, and price-led growth fades as pricing power tops out. Software renewals disappoint on demand rather than timing. Deleveraging slows and higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of refinancing the $4.2B commercial-paper stack. Multiple compresses to ~16-18x on flat earnings — near the analyst low target of $104.

**Base case (~$160):** Underlying growth holds at 3-4%, backlog delivers on second-half visibility, adjusted EPS lands near the top of the guide and grows to ~$7.15 next year. The stock re-rates modestly toward the analyst mean of $164 on cycle-improvement sentiment, supported by ~$2.2B of capital return. This implies roughly 8-10% total return including the dividend over twelve months.

The risk/reward is roughly balanced. The bull and bear cases are both plausible and hinge on the same unresolved variable — process-cycle timing — with no estimate-revision momentum to tip the odds. That balance defines this as a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is a stable, high-quality compounder with process-cycle optionality, best owned for its ~$3.2B FCF, 68-year dividend-increase streak, and second-half backlog visibility, rather than for a near-term catalyst.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q3 FY26 earnings report (early August 2026), followed by FY26 full-year results in November. The August print tests whether backlog conversion and U.S. strength offset China/Middle East drag.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive by +3% or more over a 90-day window, confirming the process-cycle inflection JPMorgan describes is entering the numbers.
- Underlying (organic) growth re-accelerates above 4% with volume, not just price, contributing meaningfully — signaling durable demand rather than pricing power alone.
- A pullback toward the mid-$120s (near the 52-week low of $122.64), which would compress the forward multiple below 20x and restore asymmetric upside; or confirmed materialization of IEEPA tariff refunds as booked benefit.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- China underlying declines accelerate beyond -15% while Europe/Middle East fail to recover, breaking the international revenue base.
- Deleveraging stalls with net-debt-to-capital rising above ~40% amid higher refinancing costs, or a dividend/buyback cut.
- Two or more consecutive quarters of adjusted EPS misses combined with a guidance cut, signaling the transformation-era growth premium is unwarranted.

## Conclusion

Emerson has become the automation-and-software franchise management set out to build: high-margin, cash-generative, and anchored by category-leading valve, measurement, and test businesses plus a recurring AspenTech software layer. The financial engine is strong across margins, free cash flow, and disciplined capital return. The obstacle to a higher rating is that the growth is mid-single-digit and price-led, estimate revisions are flat, the company has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (albeit narrowly), and the ~23x forward multiple already reflects the quality without offering an asymmetric entry. The process-cycle inflection is a credible thesis but not yet visible in the estimates.

We rate EMR a **HOLD** with an overall score of 5.6, consistent with the modest downward trajectory across the prior three reports (6.3 → 6.0 → 5.9). New data neither confirms an accelerating deterioration nor supports an upgrade; it reinforces a fairly valued, well-run industrial with balanced risk/reward. The stock is worth holding for its capital return and second-half optionality; adding requires either positive estimate-revision confirmation of the cycle turn or a pullback toward the mid-$120s.
