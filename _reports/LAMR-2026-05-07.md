## Executive Summary

Lamar Advertising is the largest operator of billboards in the United States, with approximately 159,300 displays across 45 states. The company operates as a REIT structured as an UPREIT, generating $846.7M in AFFO in FY2025 ($8.33/share) and paying a well-covered 4.5% dividend yield. Q1 2026 showed organic growth acceleration to 3.9% and AFFO/share of $1.72 (+7.5% YoY), with management guiding pacings "at the top end" of full-year expectations.

The stock currently trades at $141.15, within 1% of its 52-week high, while consensus EPS estimates are declining across all timeframes (current year down 4.0% over 90 days, next year down 5.2%). The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty, with 1-week IV at 40.9% vs. 21.1% realized volatility, in steep backwardation. Short interest at 10.4% of float with 7.3 days to cover confirms institutional skepticism about whether the current price is sustainable given macro headwinds to advertising demand. At ~17x trailing AFFO, the stock is fairly valued for a high-quality infrastructure REIT but offers limited margin of safety against the current negative estimate revision cycle and stagflationary macro environment.

## Company Overview

Lamar Advertising Company, founded in 1902 and headquartered in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, is one of the two largest outdoor advertising companies in North America. The company operates three segments: Billboard (~89% of revenue), Logo Signs (~4%), and Transit (~7%). Its billboard portfolio includes approximately 5,500 digital displays that generate roughly 33% of billboard net revenues despite representing only 3.5% of total display count — a ratio that underscores the digital conversion opportunity.

The company converted to REIT status in 2014 and adopted an UPREIT structure in 2022, enabling tax-efficient acquisitions using partnership units. Its revenue base is approximately 79% local advertising (no single customer exceeds 2% of billboard revenue), providing meaningful diversification relative to peers that skew toward national campaigns.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:** FY2025 revenue totaled $2.27B (+2.7% reported; +2.1% organic). The organic growth rate accelerated into Q1 2026 at 3.9%, driven by strong demand from both local and national customers. Total Q1 2026 revenue was $528M (+4.5% reported).

**AFFO Generation:** FY2025 AFFO was $846.7M ($8.33/share), growing 3.4% YoY. Q1 2026 AFFO was $177.5M ($1.72/share, +7.5% YoY). Annualizing Q1 implies ~$6.88/share for 2026, though seasonal weighting (Q2/Q3 are typically stronger) suggests the full-year run-rate is higher. The analyst consensus for FY2026 is $5.67 EPS (GAAP), which maps to an AFFO estimate closer to $8.50-8.70 when adding back non-cash items.

**Margins:** Adjusted EBITDA margin was 46.7% in FY2025 and expanded to a Q1 2026 rate that grew EBITDA 7.7% on 4.5% revenue growth, demonstrating operating leverage as digital conversion reduces variable costs.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of ~$3.42B against trailing EBITDA of ~$1.06B produces leverage of 3.2x, well within the 4.5x secured debt covenant and conservative for the REIT sector. The September 2025 refinancing ($400M at 5.375% maturing 2033) laddered the maturity profile effectively. Liquidity stands at $701.5M as of Q1 2026.

**Capital Allocation:** In 2025, Lamar deployed: $655.9M in dividends ($6.45/share, +14.2% YoY), $191.1M in cash acquisitions, $147.6M in UPREIT unit-funded acquisitions (Verde Outdoor), $180.8M in capex ($90.9M of which was digital investment), and $150M in buybacks at ~$108/share average.

**Dividend Coverage:** AFFO of $846.7M vs. dividends of $655.9M = 1.29x payout coverage. Sustainable, though the 2026 guided minimum of $6.40/share (vs. $6.45 in 2025) reflects a small step-down following the one-time Vistar Media gain in 2025.

## Growth Analysis

**Digital Conversion Runway:** With only 5,500 digital boards out of 159,300 total displays, and digital generating 33% of billboard revenue, Lamar has a long conversion runway. Digital capex increased 50% in 2025 to $90.9M. Each digital conversion multiplies per-face revenue by approximately 4-5x, making this the company's primary organic growth lever.

**Programmatic OOH:** Currently only 2% of the outdoor business but growing as advertisers integrate digital OOH into programmatic campaigns. This channel bridges outdoor's impact with digital's measurement and targeting capabilities.

**Organic Growth Trajectory:** The acceleration from 2.1% organic growth in FY2025 to 3.9% in Q1 2026 is encouraging. Management's comment about pacings "at the top end" of guidance implies this trend is continuing into Q2 2026.

**Limitations:** This is fundamentally a low-single-digit organic revenue growth business. Consensus expects $5.67 EPS in FY2026 and $6.08 in FY2027, roughly 7% annual growth. The growth story is incremental digital conversion and pricing power within a regulated, supply-constrained asset base. Estimate revisions are currently negative across all timeframes (FY2026 down 4.0%, FY2027 down 5.4% over 90 days), signaling that the market is pricing in some demand softening.

## Valuation Assessment

At $141.15 per share:
- Trailing P/E: 24.5x ($5.77 EPS)
- Forward P/E: 24.5x (consensus ~$5.77)
- Forward P/AFFO: ~17x (on estimated FY2026 AFFO of ~$8.30-8.50/share)
- Dividend yield: 4.5%
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 14-15x

Relative to outdoor advertising peers, LAMR commands a premium: Clear Channel Outdoor (CCO) trades at significant discount but carries far more leverage and operational risk; Outfront Media (OUT) trades at lower multiples reflecting a weaker competitive position. The premium is warranted by LAMR's superior execution, lower leverage, and stronger digital conversion profile.

Two factors constrain upside from here: (1) The stock is trading within 1% of its 52-week high and at the analyst consensus mean target of $140.80 (median $145). (2) Estimates are declining, not rising — the current year has been revised down 4.0% in 90 days. At a premium multiple with negative estimate momentum, the stock is priced for execution to plan with no margin of safety for disappointment.

The analyst target range of $128-$150 implies 9.3% downside to the low and 6.3% upside to the high from current levels. This is not an attractive risk/reward distribution for a new position.

## Competitive Landscape

Lamar's competitive position is exceptional and improving:

1. **Regulatory moat:** The Highway Beautification Act and local zoning ordinances effectively prohibit new billboard construction in most markets. Existing permits are non-reproducible assets that appreciate over time as demand grows against fixed supply.

2. **Scale advantage:** Largest US billboard operator with dominant positions in small/mid-size markets where the fragmented landlord base and local relationships create switching costs. Regional managers averaging 34 years of tenure means deep local market knowledge.

3. **Digital conversion leadership:** With 5,500 digital boards and growing, Lamar offers advertisers programmatic reach that smaller operators cannot match. Scale attracts national campaigns to programmatic OOH inventory.

4. **UPREIT acquisition engine:** The 2022 UPREIT conversion enables tax-deferred acquisitions from private billboard owners, a structural advantage that drives consolidation inflows toward Lamar over non-UPREIT competitors.

5. **Financial strength vs. peers:** At 3.2x leverage vs. CCO's much higher debt load and OUT's weaker coverage metrics, Lamar has more flexibility to acquire and invest through cycles.

The main competitive concern is the company's own acknowledgment that "the pool of suitable acquisition candidates is dwindling," which limits the external growth algorithm that drove historical outperformance.

## Risk Assessment

**Macro Advertising Demand:** In a stagflationary environment with potential recession risk, advertising budgets are vulnerable to cuts. Outdoor has historically been more resilient than other media in downturns due to lower cost-per-impression, but is not immune. The Q1 2026 filing specifically added tariff/trade risk language, suggesting management is alert to this.

**Estimate Revision Cycle:** Current estimates are declining at -4% to -5.4% across all timeframes. This negative momentum typically persists for 2-3 quarters once established. The Q1 2026 beat (103.4% surprise) may partially offset this, but with only 2 analysts covering, the estimate sample is thin and subject to volatility.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** As a REIT with $3.42B in debt and a 4.5% dividend yield, LAMR competes with fixed-income instruments for yield-seeking capital. Rising rates reduce the relative attractiveness of LAMR's yield and increase future refinancing costs.

**Family Control:** The Reilly family controls 63% of voting power with 15% economic interest. This eliminates takeover premium potential and creates governance risk, though historical capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly.

**Short Interest at 10.4%:** Elevated short positioning (7.3 days to cover) suggests institutional bearish conviction, likely reflecting concerns about advertising cyclicality at current valuations.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress: 1-week ATM IV at 40.9% vs. 21.1% historical vol represents a 19.8pp premium. The term structure is in steep backwardation (40.9% near-term vs. 21.7% at 6-month), indicating the market expects a near-term event to resolve uncertainty, likely related to the next earnings release or macro developments affecting ad spending. Put/call OI ratio of 1.06 is essentially neutral, and skew is balanced at 1.8%, suggesting the options market is pricing uncertainty in both directions rather than directional conviction. The elevated short-term IV argues against initiating new positions before the near-term catalyst resolves.

- Digital conversion accelerates, driving margin expansion toward 48-50% EBITDA margins

- Interest rate environment compresses REIT valuations broadly
- Organic growth decelerates back below 2% as macro headwinds hit local advertisers

- Stock reverts to October 2025 levels ($117-120)

## Conclusion

Lamar Advertising is a high-quality business with a genuine regulatory moat, a visible digital conversion growth lever, and a well-managed balance sheet. The 4.5% dividend yield is well-covered at 1.29x by AFFO and provides income support. Q1 2026 results were strong, with organic growth accelerating and AFFO beating expectations by 103%.

The specific reason this does not warrant a BUY at $141.15: the stock is trading at the top of its 52-week range ($142.39 high) while consensus estimates are declining at -4% to -5.4% across all forward periods. This price-vs-estimate divergence is unsustainable — either estimates must stabilize/rise to justify the price, or the price must correct. With the stock at the analyst consensus mean target ($140.80), there is no asymmetric upside that compensates for the downward estimate momentum, elevated short interest, and macro risk to advertising demand in a stagflationary environment.

For existing holders, the 4.5% dividend yield, 3.2x leverage, and accelerating organic growth provide sufficient reason to maintain the position. The business generates reliable cash flow with modest growth, and the dividend provides compensation for holding through uncertainty.
