## Executive Summary

Texas Pacific Land Corporation is among the most financially exceptional businesses in public markets: 74% operating margins, 33% ROE, zero net debt, and $498M in free cash flow generated from 114 employees managing 882,000 surface acres in the Permian Basin. The company's zero-capex royalty model, emerging data center optionality (evidenced by a $42.5M land sale arrangement in Q1 2026 and a partnership with Eric Schmidt's Bolt Data & Energy), and scarce, advantaged land position make it irreplaceable.

The fundamental challenge is valuation. At $402, TPL trades at ~58x trailing earnings and ~44x FY2026E forward earnings versus a sector median of 10.9x. While the premium is partially justified by the royalty model's superior economics, the stock requires continuous high-teens or better earnings growth to avoid multiple compression. The 90-day estimate revisions have been positive, but recent 30-day revisions have reportedly turned negative, signaling that near-term momentum may have stalled. With oil prices facing potential moderation as geopolitical conditions evolve and Permian rig counts declining approximately 13% YoY in 2025, the next 2-3 quarters present execution risk on the growth trajectory needed to sustain this valuation.

The stock has declined 26% from its February 2026 high of $547, creating improved entry dynamics, but not enough to justify a BUY given the remaining valuation stretch and near-term earnings deceleration risk.

## Company Overview

TPL is a land and royalty company, not an oil producer, despite its sector classification. The business operates through two segments: Land and Resource Management (62% of FY2025 revenue, $491M) encompassing oil/gas royalties, easements, and land sales; and Water Services (38%, $307M) covering water sourcing, produced water royalties, and related infrastructure.

The company owns approximately 882,000 surface acres and 224,000 net royalty acres concentrated in the Permian Basin. Revenue generation requires no capital expenditure on wells — TPL collects royalties as operators drill on or through its land. Every pipeline, power line, road, and facility that crosses TPL acreage generates an easement fee.

An emerging "NextGen" business adds optionality: data center development partnerships (Bolt Data & Energy, co-founded by Eric Schmidt, received a $50M minority investment from TPL), produced water desalination technology (10,000 bbl/day facility nearing first inlet barrels mid-2026), and renewable energy evaluations.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $632M (FY2023) → $706M (FY2024) → $798M (FY2025) → $237M Q1 2026. Revenue grew 13% in FY2025 despite a 15% decline in WTI, driven by 29% production volume growth (from 26.8 MBoe/d to 34.6 MBoe/d), supported in part by the $450.7M NRA acquisition completed in November 2025. Q1 2026 revenue of $237M represents 21% YoY growth and a quarterly record, though it includes $20.9M of land sale revenue from a financing arrangement related to a data center power-generation project. Excluding that item, Q1 revenue was approximately $216M, or roughly $864M annualized.

**Margins:** Operating margins remain at 74% — extraordinary for any business, though slightly compressed from 84% in FY2022 as the higher-cost Water segment grows as a revenue share. Net margins of 60% reflect minimal overhead on a 114-person workforce generating $7M revenue per employee.

**Balance sheet:** Effectively debt-free. The $500M credit facility established October 2025 remains entirely undrawn, and TPL had no credit-facility borrowings at year-end 2025 or March 31, 2026. Cash position of $145M is down from $725M in 2023 due to the acquisition spree, but the company generates approximately $500M annually in FCF to rebuild reserves.

**Cash flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $546M. TPL's company-defined free cash flow was $498M. The company's FCF reconciliation subtracts $59.5M in purchases of fixed assets (primarily water infrastructure and desalination investments) from operating cash flow, yielding approximately 91% FCF/OCF conversion. While the oil and gas royalty business requires virtually no capital expenditure, the growing Water Services segment is increasingly capital-consuming.

**Capital allocation:** FY2025 dividends of $148M ($2.13/share, ~0.6% yield) plus a largely unused $250M buyback authorization. Management has articulated a $700M target cash balance, above which excess FCF will be returned to shareholders. The Horizon Kinetics board appointment to the acquisitions committee suggests additional M&A is planned.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term drivers (12-18 months):**
- Royalty volume growth: Q1 2026 production reached 37.1 MBoe/d (+19% YoY), driven by the 17,306 NRA acquisition. Full-year run-rate benefit still materializing.
- Water services expansion: 54% revenue growth over two years (FY2023-2025). Produced water volumes generally correlate with basin-wide production, though they remain subject to individual operator development decisions on and around TPL's land.
- Easement revenue: Pipelines and infrastructure continue being built across TPL acreage.
- Consensus estimates: FY2026E EPS of $9.10 (+31% YoY from $6.97) and FY2027E of $10.12 (+11% YoY). Recent 90-day revision trends have reportedly been positive, though near-term 30-day revisions appear to have turned negative based on available market data.

**Medium-term optionality (2-5 years):**
- Data center/AI: The Q1 2026 land sale arrangement ($42.5M aggregate contractual consideration to a power generation developer, with $20.9M recognized at closing and the remainder structured as a financing receivable through 2046) represents an early validation of TPL's land for non-energy purposes. CEO Glover cited "virtually every major hyperscaler and AI lab" evaluating large-scale plans in Texas. Management sees potential for "multiple multi-gigawatt energy campuses" on their acreage, though the economics of repeatable revenue at this scale remain to be demonstrated.
- Desalination: 10,000 bbl/day R&D facility approaching first operations (cumulative spending of $48.3M as of Q1 2026, with $41.3M capitalized). If commercially viable, addresses a 20M+ bbl/day produced water market in the Permian facing regulatory constraints on injection. Potential to provide data center cooling water in a closed-loop system.
- Additional NRA acquisitions: Credit facility and board composition signal continued inorganic growth.

**Growth risks:**
- Permian rig count declined approximately 13% YoY in 2025 (from 296 to 257 average weekly horizontal rigs). While production volumes held due to efficiency, a sustained drilling downturn would slow royalty growth.
- Oil price sensitivity: TPL is fully unhedged. Every $10/bbl WTI decline reduces annual revenue by approximately $50M.
- Near-term estimate revision trends may reflect analyst concern about oil price trajectory as geopolitical conditions evolve.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the investment case strains. At $402 and FY2025 diluted EPS of $6.97:

- **Trailing P/E:** ~58x
- **Forward P/E on FY2026E ($9.10):** ~44x
- **Forward P/E on FY2027E ($10.12):** ~40x
- **EV/EBITDA (trailing):** ~40x
- **P/FCF (trailing):** ~56x
- **Peer median P/E:** 10.9x (Coterra 15x, EQT 11x, Devon 13x, APA 9x, EXE 7x)

TPL trades at 4-5x the P/E multiple of its classified peer group. This premium is substantially justified by:
1. Zero-capex royalty model (vs. E&P capital intensity of 30-50% of revenue)
2. 74% operating margins (vs. E&P margins of 25-40%)
3. Reduced production decline risk — while TPL does not bear drilling capex or operating costs, its royalty revenues remain tied to underlying well production and third-party operator decisions regarding future development
4. Data center optionality is genuinely valuable and has no analog in E&P peers
5. Scarcity premium — no other public vehicle offers this exposure

However, even granting a 3x premium to peers on business quality grounds, fair value on trailing earnings would imply ~33x P/E = ~$230. Using FY2027E earnings of $10.12 at a justified 40x premium multiple = $405 — essentially the current price.

The stock is fairly valued if FY2027 estimates hold and the market maintains its premium multiple. It is cheap only if data center revenue scales materially (not yet in estimates) or if oil prices remain elevated and production growth accelerates beyond current forecasts. Any negative near-term estimate revision trend removes confidence that earnings will surprise to the upside in the immediate term.

**Why not BUY despite a 7.0 overall score:** At 44x forward earnings, the stock has minimal margin for error — a single quarter of estimate misses would likely trigger 10-15% downside from current levels. The 26% decline from the February high already reflects some de-rating, but at 44x forward P/E the stock still requires growth acceleration, not deceleration, to sustain its level. EIA's May 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook projected Brent near $106/b in the near term but a full-year 2026 average of approximately $95/b and $79/b in 2027, suggesting meaningful price moderation over the forecast horizon. A BUY requires either (a) entry below $350 where the valuation provides adequate margin of safety, or (b) estimate revisions turning positive again, confirming growth re-acceleration.

## Competitive Landscape

TPL occupies a scarce, deeply advantaged competitive position. There is no direct public comparable. The 882,000 acres cannot be replicated — they were assembled in 1888 from railroad land grants and have been held for 138 years. TPL itself states it has "few direct peers." While TPL's land position gives it significant negotiating leverage with operators, pipeline companies, and infrastructure developers working across large portions of the Permian Basin, the company acknowledges that any owner of comparable property may be a competitor in its Land and Resource Management segment, and that its water market is "highly competitive," including other landowners, water supply/transfer companies, and produced-water companies.

Competitive advantages include:
- **Irreplaceable land position:** Scale cannot be recreated in the Permian
- **Zero-capex royalty model:** Operators bear all drilling and completion costs; TPL bears none
- **Multi-decade revenue streams:** Easements provide long-duration recurring income
- **Emerging data center positioning:** Combination of cheap gas, excess land, water resources, and proximity to fiber routes creates a compelling value proposition for hyperscalers
- **Operating leverage:** 114 employees generating $798M revenue means incremental revenue falls almost entirely to profit

The competitive position is stable-to-improving as data center optionality adds a new dimension to the land's value that was not present 2-3 years ago.

## Risk Assessment

**Oil price sensitivity (HIGH):** TPL is fully unhedged. If geopolitical de-escalation progresses and Brent moderates toward $79-95 (consistent with EIA's 2026-2027 projections), royalty revenue would decline meaningfully from current run rates.

**Rig count decline (MODERATE):** Q1 2026 showed further deterioration to 222 rigs (approximately 23% below Q1 2025). While production has held due to efficiency gains, a sustained capex pullback from operators would slow volume growth on TPL's NRA.

**Third-party operator dependence (MODERATE-HIGH):** TPL produces nothing itself. Its royalty revenues depend entirely on decisions made by E&P operators regarding drilling activity on and around its acreage. TPL explicitly acknowledges that as wells age, production may decline absent additional operator investment, and that it has no control over these decisions.

**Valuation risk (HIGH):** At 44x FY2026E and 40x FY2027E, any earnings disappointment carries outsized price impact. A single quarter miss likely triggers 10-15% downside.

**Technology risk (MODERATE):** Cumulative spending of $48.3M on desalination with no commercial revenue yet. Data center partnerships are early-stage. Neither is in current estimates, but market price likely embeds some optionality value.

**Customer concentration (MODERATE):** 40% of revenue from three customers. While these are major investment-grade operators unlikely to disappear, their capital allocation decisions directly control TPL's royalty volumes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($500-550, +25-37%):** Data center development accelerates, with 2-3 additional land sales or long-term lease agreements announced in 2026. Oil prices remain elevated ($105+) as geopolitical disruptions persist. FY2026 EPS reaches $10+ (vs. $9.10 consensus) on volume growth from the NRA acquisition and higher oil prices. Desalination facility achieves commercial operations, opening a new addressable market. Market re-rates to 50x on confirmed NextGen revenue streams.

**Bear Case ($300-330, -18-25%):** Oil prices decline to $79-90 on de-escalation, Permian operators cut activity further, and FY2026 EPS comes in at $7.50-8.00 (below current consensus). Data center timeline delays. Market compresses multiple to 38-40x on slower growth. Stock retests the $300 support established in late 2025.

**Base Case ($420-450, +4-12% over 12 months):** FY2026E EPS of $9.10 achieved, FY2027 estimates of $10.12 hold, and the market maintains 44-45x forward P/E as data center optionality remains in early stages but credible. Modest total return including 0.6% dividend yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines below $350 (38-39x FY2026E), providing adequate valuation margin of safety for a premium asset
2. Near-term estimate revisions turn positive again (indicating growth re-acceleration), particularly if driven by above-consensus Q2 2026 results
3. A second confirmed data center transaction of material size (>$25M) demonstrating the revenue stream is repeatable

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS estimates decline below $8.00 (indicating sustained earnings deterioration)
2. Oil prices decline below $85 for 60+ days without offsetting volume growth
3. Key customer reduces Permian activity by 20%+ or exits TPL acreage

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected early August 2026 for Q2 results) will reveal whether Q1's 21% YoY growth trajectory is sustainable and whether the data center revenue pipeline is materializing.

## Conclusion

TPL is one of the highest-quality businesses in public markets by almost any operational metric. The combination of 74% operating margins, minimal capex requirements on its royalty business, a scarce and advantaged land position, and emerging AI/data center optionality creates a compounding machine with few peers. The fundamental quality justifies a significant premium to traditional energy valuations.

However, at 44x FY2026E earnings with potential negative near-term estimate revisions and oil price uncertainty from possible geopolitical de-escalation, the risk/reward at $402 is balanced rather than asymmetric. The stock requires growth to accelerate or data center revenue to materialize in estimates to drive meaningful upside from here. The correct posture is to hold existing positions (capturing the structural compounding) while waiting for either a valuation pullback or estimate acceleration to add exposure. This is a $350 BUY, not a $400 BUY.
