## Executive Summary

Bio-Rad is two businesses bolted together and priced as one. The first is a $2.58B-revenue life-science tools and clinical-diagnostics franchise that has not grown since 2022: FY2025 net sales of $2,583.2M sit 7.8% below FY2022's $2,802.2M, and H1 2026 revenue of $1,243.1M was +0.5% reported but **−3.0% currency-neutral**, with Q2 Life Science down 5.1% currency-neutral and Clinical Diagnostics up just 0.3%. The second is a passive 38%-of-ordinary-shares holding in Sartorius AG, marked at $5,295.2M on 30 June 2026, plus a €400M loan to the Sartorius family's holding vehicle carried at $369.9M. The stake alone equals 59.7% of Bio-Rad's total equity capitalization, and the company discloses that it has no board designee at Sartorius and has "tried and failed" to obtain the information required to apply the equity method.

The bull argument is arithmetic: strip out $6,079.9M of pre-tax net financial assets from a total equity capitalization of ~$8,900M (26.74M Class A plus Class B shares at $332.88, not the $7.2B Class A-only figure most screens carry) and the operating business is implied at ~$2,820M, or 6.5x an estimated $433M of operating EBITDA. That is cheap against mid-cap tools comparables. The problem is that this discount is not new, is not narrowing, and has no mechanism to close. Class A holders elect only 25% of the board; Class B, ~70% of single-class voting power, elects the rest. The proxy shows indexed TSR of 51.98 against a self-selected peer group at 105.15 — a 53-point gap that has widened every year of the disclosure — while CEO target compensation has been essentially flat at $7.5M–$8.7M throughout. Deduct the full $982.2M deferred tax liability that a monetization would crystallize and the implied operating multiple becomes 8.7x, which is not a discount worth underwriting.

Since the last review the stock has risen 9.4% (from $304.36 on 19 July) and 40.6% from the 13 May low of $236.73, and now trades above the consensus mean and median targets with an analyst split of one strong buy, three holds and one strong sell. The Q2 non-GAAP EPS beat ($2.62 vs $1.77, +48.4%) came on flat reported / −1.9% currency-neutral revenue, so it was a cost and below-the-line beat, not a demand beat — and both forward quarters were revised **down** over 90 days (Q3 −11.0%, next quarter −4.7%) even as the arithmetic of the beat lifted the full-year figure. On 31 July the company initiated its third consecutive restructuring program, $80–90M of pre-tax charges for $30–35M of net savings by end-2027. Base case $305 (−8.4%), bull $395 (+18.7%), bear $240 (−27.9%). **AVOID** at this price; not a short.

## Company Overview

Bio-Rad develops, manufactures and distributes more than 12,000 life-science research and clinical-diagnostics products from Hercules, California, selling directly in over 36 countries with roughly 7,450 employees, of whom ~790 are in R&D. Two segments: Life Science, $1,021M in FY2025 (~40% of sales), serving academic, government, industrial and biopharma customers into a management-estimated $19B served market; and Clinical Diagnostics, $1,562M (~60%), supplying several thousand products supporting more than 300 diagnostic tests into an estimated $16B served market. Geographic mix in H1 2026 was 39% U.S. / 61% international.

The moat claim rests on a razor/razor-blade installed base in Diagnostics — quality controls (the largest Diagnostics line) and blood typing — plus technology differentiation in droplet digital PCR, supported by the broadest digital-PCR instrument portfolio and more than 12,000 peer-reviewed publications. The June 2025 Stilla Technologies acquisition ($257.7M total consideration, including $28.5M initial contingent consideration with up to $50.0M payable in cash on milestones through 2027) consolidated a ddPCR competitor and underpins the QX700 series.

The Sartorius relationship is the dominant balance-sheet fact and the most commonly misread part of the story. Bio-Rad holds 12,987,900 ordinary and 9,588,908 preference shares of a company whose ordinary majority is held by the Sartorius family trust. Bio-Rad is a minority financial holder, not a partner or operator. Separately, Bio-Rad is the **lender** on a €400M collateralized loan to Sartorius-Herbst Beteiligungen II GmbH, which used the proceeds to buy trust interests from a trust beneficiary; the collateral is an interest in that trust, non-tradable until trust termination in mid-2028, with the loan due January 2029 at a 1.5% coupon against Bio-Rad's own 3.3%/3.7% debt cost.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2022 $2,802.2M → FY2023 $2,671.3M (−4.7%) → FY2024 $2,566.5M (−3.9%) → FY2025 $2,583.2M (+0.7%) → H1 2026 $1,243.1M (+0.5% reported, −3.0% currency-neutral). The reported-versus-constant-currency gap is the weak dollar, and the same weak dollar inflates the international cost base, which management cited as the driver of higher H1 2026 SG&A. The nuance that screens miss: reported EMEA revenue rose 4.6% in H1 2026 to $436.0M, yet MD&A states currency-neutral EMEA sales **declined** in both segments. U.S. revenue fell 4.6% to $485.3M.

**Margins.** GAAP operating income collapsed from $482.6M (17.2% of sales) in FY2022 to $47.2M (1.8%) in FY2025, but the FY2025 figure absorbs $172.8M net of purchased-intangible impairments — $81.7M on the Dropworks IPR&D asset and a $127.7M Curiosity Diagnostics IPR&D write-off net of $36.6M of recycled translation gains. Adding those back gives ~$220M, or 8.5% of sales, still below FY2024's 10.5%. The more durable read is gross margin: GAAP consolidated 53.7% → 51.9% (FY2024 → FY2025); non-GAAP 55.0% → 53.3%. H1 2026 stabilized at 52.7% GAAP (flat year over year), with Q2 at 53.1%.

That stabilization matters because FY2026 guidance calls for **non-GAAP gross margin of 54.0%–54.5%**, which requires meaningful second-half improvement over the H1 run rate. Management attributed the Q4 2025 trough (52.5% non-GAAP) to expedited freight, overtime and service costs following the U.S. government shutdown, plus "slower-than-expected progress on our procurement initiatives," and characterized these as "execution-related rather than structural." That is an assertion. It is the single largest execution risk inside 2026 guidance and it resolves in the Q3 and Q4 prints.

**Reported earnings are not a performance measure.** The fair-value line on the Sartorius stake and the SHB loan has swamped operations for five years: a $2.66B loss in FY2024, a $900.4M gain in FY2025, a $280.2M loss in H1 2026, and a $458.0M gain in Q2 2026 alone — which produced GAAP diluted EPS of $13.85 for the quarter. Cumulative GAAP net income from FY2022 through H1 2026 is approximately −$5.50B, and the reconciliation holds: retained earnings fell from $9,898.2M (12/31/2022) to $8,020.6M (6/30/2026), a $1,877.6M decline that matches the sum of FY2023–H1 2026 net results within rounding, with buybacks flowing to treasury stock. The cumulative loss reflects mark-to-market on a marketable minority holding, not cash burn.

The usable operating series is non-GAAP: FY2025 net income $271M / $9.92 diluted EPS versus FY2024 $291M / $10.31. Note the direction — non-GAAP EPS is declining, and consensus has it at $9.64 for FY2026 and $10.03 for FY2027, meaning consensus does not expect Bio-Rad to regain FY2024 earnings power until beyond 2027.

**A data inconsistency worth naming.** The quoted trailing and forward P/E of 53.34 cannot be reconciled with the quoted EPS of $27.85 ($332.88 / $27.85 = 12.0x). Neither field is usable for valuation. The defensible anchors are consensus non-GAAP EPS of $9.64 (34.5x) and $10.03 (33.2x), and the sum-of-parts work below.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $532.2M, capex $157.6M, free cash flow $374.6M — a 138% conversion of non-GAAP net income, with FY2026 guided at $375M–$395M. Two disciplining points. First, roughly 20–25% of that is not from the tools business: FY2025 "Other income, net" was $90.3M (portfolio interest plus the annual Sartorius dividend, $17.6M in H1 2025), so stripping it from the $385M guidance midpoint leaves ~$295M of operations-attributable FCF, before crediting back the ~$49M of interest expense that is a genuine cash cost of the debt. Second, H1 2026 OCF fell to $206.8M from $246.4M on lower cash received from customers ($1,242.7M vs $1,304.9M) and a $37.7M inventory build. I will not annualize a half-year: Q4 is seasonally the largest quarter ($693M of revenue in Q4 2025, $165M of OCF), and H1 2025 was 46% of full-year OCF. On that pattern H1 2026 is consistent with roughly $450M of full-year OCF — modestly below FY2025.

**Balance sheet (6/30/2026).** Cash $517.0M plus short-term investments $1,101.5M = $1,618.5M against total debt of $1,203.7M carrying ($400M 3.3% notes due March 2027, now current; $800M 3.7% due 2032). A $200M revolver is undrawn through February 2029. Current ratio 5.62, D/E 0.42, stockholders' equity $7,173.9M, deferred tax liability $982.2M. Refinancing the 2027 notes at, say, 5.5% instead of 3.3% adds ~$8.8M of annual pre-tax interest, about 2.8% of FY2025 non-GAAP operating income — a cost, not a thesis.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.95 ("grey zone") is an artifact here: its EBIT/assets input of 0.004 uses post-impairment GAAP EBIT in the numerator and includes ~$6.0B of Sartorius stake plus loan in the denominator, assets that generate no EBIT by construction. Substituting FY2025 non-GAAP operating income ($312.6M) over operating assets excluding the stake and loan ($4,533.9M) gives 0.069. The liquidity and leverage components remain informative and are strong. Similarly, the Piotroski "OCF exceeds net income = false" flag inverts because FY2025 net income embeds a $900.4M non-cash gain; OCF of $532.2M comfortably exceeds non-GAAP net income of $271M. The honest characterization of the operating business's economics is FY2025 non-GAAP operating income over operating assets: 312.6 / 4,533.9 = **6.9% pre-tax** — high-single-digit returns on the deployed capital that actually produces revenue.

**Financial health: 5.8.** Resilience is genuinely high — $1.62B of liquid assets against $1.20B of debt, a $5.3B daily-marked equity holding, an undrawn revolver, and no covenant or maturity pressure. Efficiency is poor: more than half the balance sheet earns a low-single-digit dividend yield and produces no operating income, gross margins have eroded ~180bp on a non-GAAP basis since FY2024, H1 cash generation declined, and the €400M loan carries a 1.5% coupon against a 3.3–3.7% cost of funds, with the company itself disclosing that the collateral "may be insufficient" and that the borrower "will likely have no other assets from which to repay."

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance was cut from +0.5%/+1.5% currency-neutral revenue growth (February) to **−3% to +0.5%** (1 May), a ~2.25-point reduction at the midpoint, and reiterated on 4 August. Management attributed the cut principally to the Middle East conflict; the Q2 10-Q adds MD&A language absent from the FY2025 10-K, stating that escalating regional conflict "has adversely affected demand and logistics in the EMEA region, particularly within our Clinical Diagnostics segment," and expects that to continue through 2026.

The other drags are company-specific. Process chromatography grew over 20% in FY2025 and is guided to decline approximately mid-teens in FY2026, with ~50bp of impact on non-GAAP operating margin, because a customer changed manufacturing strategy after vaccine-policy expectations shifted by geography. Management conceded the commercial portfolio is "over-indexed to vaccines compared to the average of the bioprocess industry" and occupies "a niche position in the polishing step," with revenue concentrated in a small number of commercial therapeutics. Recovery is guided flat-to-low-single-digit in 2027 and mid-single-digit thereafter. On the demand side, academic institutions are "focused on maintaining staffing levels and sustaining ongoing research rather than purchasing capital equipment," and U.S. revenue fell 4.6% in H1.

Against that: core Life Science excluding process chromatography is guided to grow nearly 4% in 2026; quality controls are guided to mid-single digits; QX700 placements are winning both qPCR conversions and competitive displacements; Stilla is now expected accretive by mid-2026, six to twelve months ahead of the initial view; and the new restructuring targets $30–35M of net savings by end-2027 (a ~2.6-year simple payback on $80–90M of charges).

**On the estimate-revision signal.** The headline reads "estimates broadly rising," and full-year FY2026 consensus did move from $9.00 to $9.64 over 30 days (+7.1%). The composition matters: Q1 actual $1.89 + Q2 actual $2.62 + Q3 estimate $2.23 + next-quarter estimate $2.91 = $9.65, so essentially the entire full-year increase is the arithmetic of the Q2 beat flowing through, while the current-quarter estimate was cut 11.0% and the next-quarter estimate 4.7% over 90 days. The Q2 beat itself came on flat reported and −1.9% currency-neutral revenue, and the four-quarter record is two beats and two misses. This is not the revision velocity on accelerating revenue that marks a genuine earnings inflection; it is a cost-driven quarter re-based into a full-year number while forward quarters are trimmed.

**Growth prospects: 3.5.** Four consecutive years without growth, negative currency-neutral revenue guidance for a fifth, the fastest-growing product line reversing on a single customer's policy-driven decision, R&D down to 10.7% of sales from 11.5%, and consensus EPS in FY2027 still below FY2024. The offsets are real but small relative to the base.

## Valuation Assessment

Start with the share count, because the standard screen is wrong. The $7.2B market cap corresponds to 21,676,980 Class A shares only. Class A and Class B participate equally in earnings, so total equity capitalization is 26,738,018 × $332.88 = **$8,900M**.

Net financial assets at the 30 June 2026 marks:

| Item | $M | Per share (26.74M) |
|---|---|---|
| Sartorius stake | +5,295.2 | +198.04 |
| SHB loan at fair value | +369.9 | +13.83 |
| Cash + short-term investments | +1,618.5 | +60.53 |
| Total debt (carrying) | −1,203.7 | −45.02 |
| **Pre-tax net financial assets** | **6,079.9** | **227.38** |
| Deferred income tax liability | −982.2 | −36.73 |
| **After full deferred tax** | **5,097.7** | **190.65** |

Operating EBITDA: FY2025 non-GAAP operating income of $312.6M (12.1% × $2,583.2M) plus roughly $120M of D&A excluding purchased-intangible amortization = **~$433M**, or $16.20 per share.

At $332.88 the market is therefore paying, for the operating business:
- $332.88 − $227.38 = **$105.50/share, or 6.5x operating EBITDA** (pre-tax framing, ~1.09x sales)
- $332.88 − $190.65 = **$142.23/share, or 8.7x operating EBITDA** (full deferred-tax framing, ~1.46x sales)

Neither bookend is "correct." The deferred tax liability is not a near-term cash obligation, but it is precisely the liability that crystallizes in the one scenario — a taxable disposition of the stake — that would close the holdco discount. Any bull case that requires monetization must therefore be priced on something closer to the full-tax framing, where the operating business is at 8.7x. Two further caveats: I could not verify what portion of the $982.2M DTL relates specifically to Sartorius, and the $94.2M of equity securities inside short-term investments may overlap with the stake (a ~1.1% potential double-count).

Peer context is awkward because the group's reported P/Es are distorted by impairments: AVTR −11.9x, CRL −50.0x, AZTA −9.3x, TECH 77.8x, MEDP 29.9x, median 77.8x. On an EV/EBITDA basis the mid-cap tools cohort generally sits around 9–10x for comparable or slightly better growth. So Bio-Rad's operating business is at the low end of the range on the pre-tax framing and roughly in line on the after-tax framing. On headline earnings, 34.5x FY2026 and 33.2x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS for a business with declining EPS is only defensible via the sum-of-parts.

The valuation case therefore reduces to whether the holdco discount closes. Three facts argue against underwriting it. First, it has persisted for more than a decade and widened over five years of disclosed TSR underperformance (indexed 51.98 vs peers 105.15). Second, the governance structure gives outside holders no mechanism: Class A elects 25% of directors, Class B roughly 70% of single-class votes and 75% of the board, and the risk factor states plainly that the Schwartz family "are able to elect a majority of our directors, effect fundamental changes in our direction and control matters affecting us." Third, no filing contains a commitment to monetize. The one conditional path the company itself discloses is the Investment Company Act exemption: if an adverse Section 3(a)(1)(C) determination appeared likely, "we may modify our position in Sartorius AG." That is real, but it is contingent on an adverse regulatory posture the company says it is "comfortable" it will avoid.

Finally, price context. The shares trade at $332.88 against a consensus mean target of $321 and median of $315, with a range of $280–$370 and a recommendation distribution of 1 strong buy / 3 hold / 1 strong sell. Citigroup downgraded to Neutral on 7 April 2026. Consensus targets can be stale on cheap, accelerating names; that is not this setup — estimates for the forward quarters are being cut, the multiple on headline earnings is above 30x, and spot is already through the mean.

**Valuation: 4.3.** A structural discount with no controllable catalyst, already narrowed by a 40.6% rally, with spot above consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

Bio-Rad names its competitors specifically: Becton Dickinson, Danaher, Merck KGaA, Qiagen and Thermo Fisher in Life Science; Abbott, Becton Dickinson, bioMérieux, Danaher, DiaSorin, Werfen, QuidelOrtho, Roche, Siemens Healthineers, Thermo Fisher and Tosoh in Diagnostics. The filings concede the scale problem directly — competitors with "greater financial resources... better equipped to license technologies... fund research and development, manufacturing and marketing efforts, or to source high-demand materials." At $2.58B of revenue against several competitors an order of magnitude larger, and with R&D falling to 10.7% of sales, that gap widens rather than narrows.

One disclosure deserves more attention than it gets: "Many public tenders have become more competitive due to governments lengthening the commitments of their public tenders to multiple years, which reduce the number of tenders in which we can participate annually. Because the value of these multiple-year tenders is so high, our competitors have been more aggressive with their pricing." Longer tender cycles with higher contract value structurally advantage scaled bidders and reduce the number of shots a sub-scale player gets per year. Bio-Rad also flags that it may be excluded from certain public tenders in China, India and Russia for lack of local manufacturing.

Where the position is genuinely defensible: digital PCR (instrument breadth, assay menu, publication base, QX700 taking share from both qPCR and direct competitors, with Stilla removing one competitor from the field), clinical quality controls, and blood typing. Where it is deteriorating: currency-neutral revenue is falling faster than the larger tools players have generally reported, and two acquired development platforms from the 2021–2022 vintage — Dropworks and Curiosity Diagnostics — were written off entirely in December 2025, the latter removing an in-house molecular-diagnostics development effort from Clinical Diagnostics. The risk-factor language was amended to acknowledge experience-based failure ("which we have experienced," "as they have occurred in the past").

The sharpest competitive datapoint is internal to the story: Sartorius reported approximately +7.7% constant-currency H1 2026 revenue growth and confirmed guidance, while Bio-Rad's own operations declined 3.0% currency-neutral. Bio-Rad holders own a recovering European bioprocess franchise passively, at ~60% of market cap, attached to a contracting tools business.

**Competitive position: 4.0.** Two defensible niches inside a franchise losing relative ground on scale, pricing structure and R&D intensity.

## Risk Assessment

**Sartorius concentration and mark volatility.** The stake plus loan equal 63.8% of total equity capitalization. Bio-Rad's own risk factor states its share price "may change significantly based upon changes in the market value of our position in Sartorius AG, independent of the actual performance of our business." Each 10% move in the stake is roughly ±$19.80 per Bio-Rad share pre-tax. The 30 June mark of $5,295.2M is the last verifiable figure; the stake's value on 5 August is unknown, and part of the 9.4% rally since 19 July may simply be Sartorius appreciation rather than any re-rating of Bio-Rad's operations. This cuts both ways and is the single largest driver of near-term price outcomes.

**Investment Company Act.** The company discloses it "might be deemed to be an 'investment company' under Section 3(a)(1)(C)" and relies on the 3(b)(1) "primarily engaged" exemption, warning that an adverse determination "could also call into question the validity of all contracts to which the Company is a party." Low probability, severe consequence, and — unusually — the disclosed remedy is stake modification, making this a two-sided binary.

**SHB loan collateral.** The company states that declines in Sartorius ordinary shares "have reduced the value of the collateral," that collateral "may be insufficient to cover the repayment of the Loan if the decline in value continues," and that SHB "will likely have no other assets from which to repay." The pledged trust interests are not tradable before trust termination in mid-2028 and in enforcement "may have to be sold with a significant discount." Carrying value is $369.9M, 4.2% of total equity capitalization.

**H2 2026 gross-margin step-up.** Guidance of 54.0%–54.5% non-GAAP gross margin against an H1 run rate near 52.7% GAAP requires a material second-half improvement that depends on procurement initiatives management already missed once, in Q4 2025.

**Governance.** Dual-class control with Class A electing 25% of directors, a Delaware forum-selection provision, family board representation, flat CEO compensation ($8.70M/$7.53M/$7.46M/$8.29M/$8.00M for 2021–2025) across a period in which the proxy's own indexed TSR fell to 51.98 against peers at 105.15, and a company-selected performance measure (GAAP operating income) that fell 90.6% from 2021 to 2025.

**Other.** Middle East EMEA diagnostics disruption guided through 2026; process chromatography customer and vaccine-policy concentration; China at mid-single-digit revenue share with tender-access restrictions; Q2 effective tax rate jumping to 31.5% from 23.2% partly on unnamed new legislation, with $88.9M of gross unrecognized tax benefits and open tax years from 2012 in five jurisdictions; a disclosed history of material weaknesses in internal control (controls concluded effective at 6/30/2026); execution risk on a third consecutive restructuring.

**Elliott.** The Wall Street Journal reported on 17–18 May 2026 that Elliott had built a "sizable" stake, picked up by Reuters and Bloomberg citing the same reporting. No size was disclosed, and there is no Elliott 13D or 13G on EDGAR — the most recent Schedule 13D/A on Bio-Rad dates to April 2022. The position may be sub-5%, held in derivative form, or reported only on a 13F. I treat this as an unconfirmed report, not a catalyst of known size, and note that even a confirmed activist faces a board 75% of which is elected by Class B.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 56.0% against 37.8% one-year realized — an 18.2-point premium — with the term structure in backwardation (56.0% at 15 days, 44.2% at 43 days, 38.1% at 134 days) and an implied move of ±9.7% on the 21 August expiry. Implied vol rose 8.9 points (47.1% → 56.0%) while the stock rose 9.4% since 19 July, and open interest is call-tilted at 0.77 put/call with balanced near-term skew. Rising implied volatility *after* an earnings release, alongside a rising price and call-tilted open interest, is unusual; the most straightforward reading is that participants are paying up for upside optionality around a possible discrete corporate development, which the activist reporting and the disclosed conditional path to stake modification make plausible. Two implications: near-term options are expensive to own on either side, and the configuration is a specific warning to anyone considering the short. This does not change the fundamental assessment; it does argue against expressing it with a short position.

**Risk factors: 4.0.** The Sartorius mark is transparent and arguably priced, but the H2 gross-margin step-up, the collateral adequacy disclosure, the Investment Company Act contingency, and the governance structure are all live, and several are two-sided.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($395, +18.7%)

The sum-of-parts gap closes, or partially closes. Sartorius continues its recovery (+7.7% constant currency in H1 2026) and appreciates 15%, lifting the stake's contribution by roughly $30 per Bio-Rad share pre-tax. The restructuring delivers $30–35M of net savings, Stilla continues running ahead of plan, procurement initiatives land and FY2026 non-GAAP gross margin hits the 54.0–54.5% guide, taking operating EBITDA toward $470M. Activist pressure — public rather than board-level, given the voting structure — pushes management toward a defined action on the Sartorius position or a firmer capital-return commitment, and the operating business re-rates to 10x EBITDA. Combining a $212 after-tax financial-asset contribution with $176 of operating value gives approximately $388, with an announcement premium taking it toward $395–400.

### Bear Case ($240, −27.9%)

Sartorius gives back 20% of its value (−$40 per Bio-Rad share pre-tax), and the H2 gross-margin step-up fails as it did in Q4 2025, holding operating EBITDA near $400M. Currency-neutral revenue lands at the low end of the −3% to +0.5% guide, with EMEA diagnostics disruption persisting and process chromatography recovery slipping into 2028. Restructuring charges of $80–90M land ahead of savings, the March 2027 notes are refinanced at a higher coupon, no action is taken on the stake, and the operating multiple compresses to 5.5x. That is roughly $161 of after-tax financial assets plus $82 of operating value = $243, essentially a retest of the 13 May low of $236.73.

### Base Case ($305, −8.4%)

Financial assets held at the 30 June marks net of the full deferred tax liability ($190.65/share) plus 7.0x operating EBITDA of $433M ($113.4/share) = $304. Revenue lands near the guidance midpoint, gross margin improves but undershoots the 54.0% floor, savings begin arriving in 2027, and the holdco discount persists because nothing in the governance structure changes. This sits below the consensus median of $315 and mean of $321, and reflects a stock that has already priced most of the plausible good news over the last twelve weeks.

Reward-to-risk from $332.88 is 18.7% up versus 27.9% down, or 0.67:1.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There is no dated downside catalyst. Q2 was reported on 4 August with a 48.4% non-GAAP EPS beat and reiterated guidance. The next scheduled events are the Q3 print in late October/early November — where the gross-margin step-up gets its first real test — and a deferred Investor Day with no announced date. A short here is a bet on the absence of good news rather than the arrival of bad news.

**Timing window.** Approximately three months to the Q3 print. Over that window the dominant driver of the share price is not Bio-Rad's operations but Sartorius's share price, which is recovering. Carrying a short whose primary exposure is to a third party's improving European bioprocess business is a poor structure.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** The reported short interest figure (31 shares) is clearly a data artifact and cannot be used. Average daily volume is 280,738 shares against a Class A float reduced by 1.9M shares of buybacks since Q1 2024 and by concentrated family ownership — thin enough that a positive headline moves the stock violently, as the single-day gain on 18 May 2026 (following the Elliott reports) demonstrated.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market is actively hostile to the short. Near-term implied volatility of 56.0% against 37.8% realized makes put protection expensive, open interest is call-tilted at 0.77, and implied volatility *rose* 8.9 points after earnings alongside a 9.4% price gain — positioning consistent with participants paying for upside around a possible corporate action. An Elliott 13D filing, a Sartorius monetization announcement, or an Investment Company Act-driven stake modification would each produce a sharp adverse move.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear case $240 (−27.9%) against a bull case of $395 (+18.7%) on a catalyst that could arrive without warning. ## Conclusion

Bio-Rad's overall score of 4.3 continues a trajectory that has run 6.1 → 5.6 → 5.0 → 4.8 → 4.4 → 4.3, and the flatness of this step deserves explanation. The operating picture is marginally better than at the last review: Q2 non-GAAP EPS beat by 48%, full-year consensus rose 5–7%, guidance was reiterated rather than cut again, a quantified $30–35M savings program is in place, and Sartorius — 60% of the market capitalization — is growing. Those improvements were offset by a 9.4% price advance that carried the stock above the consensus mean and median targets, 40.6% above the May low, and near a 52-week high. Fundamentals modestly better, price materially higher, net risk/reward worse.

The recommendation moves from HOLD to AVOID on price and structure rather than on any single new deterioration. The specific reasons: currency-neutral revenue is negative for a fifth year with negative guidance for a fifth; the Q2 beat was a cost and below-the-line event on flat revenue, and both forward quarters were revised down; the FY2026 gross-margin guide requires a step-up management already missed once; the sum-of-parts discount is 6.5x on a pre-tax framing that ignores the tax a monetization would trigger and 8.7x on the framing that does not, the latter roughly in line with tools peers that grow; and the discount has widened, not narrowed, across five years in which the company's own proxy shows a 53-point TSR gap to its selected peer group, with a governance structure that gives outside holders no mechanism to force change.

The offsetting possibility is genuine and is why this is an AVOID rather than a short: an activist escalation, an Investment Company Act-driven stake modification, or continued Sartorius appreciation could each move the shares sharply higher, and the options market appears to be paying for exactly that optionality. Do not own the shares at $332.88; do not sell them short either. Revisit at $260–275, or on delivered gross margin and a filed action on the Sartorius position.
