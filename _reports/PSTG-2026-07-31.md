## Executive Summary

Everpure enters fiscal 2027 with the best top-line momentum in its history as a public company and the worst input-cost environment. FY2026 (ended February 1, 2026) revenue reached $3,662.8M, up 15.6%, marking the second consecutive year of acceleration off the +2.8% near-stall in FY2024. Remaining performance obligations grew 40% to $3.7B against 14% the prior year, deals over $5M reportedly grew 80% year over year, and the company began shipping under a royalty-based flash design win with a major hyperscaler that exceeded internal forecasts in its first year. Free cash flow was $615.7M on $880.1M of operating cash flow, against zero debt and $1.5B of cash and marketable securities.

The countervailing evidence is equally specific and comes from the company's own filing. Management states component costs "increased significantly" in FY2026, anticipates continued volatility through FY2027, implemented product price increases in Q1 FY2027, and guides product gross margin to decline sequentially in that quarter. It has separately disclosed that it "recently increased our partner incentive compensation arrangements which we expect to negatively impact revenue," an unquantified, self-created headwind to reported FY2027 revenue. Subscription net dollar retention fell from 117% to 113%, and subscription ARR growth decelerated from 21% to 16%. The memory cycle that is enriching NAND and DRAM suppliers works against Everpure, which buys flash; the analogue is Apple raising Mac prices up to 18% on memory-cost pressure. Everpure sits on the same cost-absorbing side of that trade, with a partial structural hedge in DirectFlash, which writes software to raw NAND and, per the filing, "requires less NAND" than SSD-based designs.

Valuation is where the call resolves. At $75.06 on 343.0M diluted shares, market capitalization is roughly $25.7B and enterprise value roughly $24.2B net of the $1.5B securities portfolio. That is 6.6x trailing revenue, 39x FY2026 free cash flow, and 38x FY2026 non-GAAP operating profit of $635M. Stock-based compensation of $481.7M (13.2% of revenue) consumed essentially all of the $615.7M of free cash flow through $342.5M of buybacks and $271.7M of cash spent withholding employee tax shares, and diluted share count still rose from 342.7M to 343.0M. The headline 2.4% FCF yield falls to roughly 0.5% after SBC. That leaves little valuation cushion for the guided gross-margin decline. We maintain HOLD, with a base case of $80 (+6.6%), a bull case of $104 and a bear case of $48.

## Company Overview

Everpure sells an integrated enterprise storage and data management platform pairing proprietary software with vertically integrated flash hardware. The architecture rests on four elements disclosed in Item 1 of the FY2026 10-K: **Purity**, a single operating environment spanning block, file and object protocols across all product lines; **DirectFlash Modules**, where the company writes directly to raw NAND rather than purchasing commodity SSDs (a 300TB DFM released in 2025 is described as up to 7x the capacity of the largest SSDs); **Evergreen**, the non-disruptive upgrade model with independently replaceable controllers and flash modules that underpins the subscription business; and **Pure1/Everpure Fusion** for telemetry and storage-as-code control.

Product families span FlashArray (//ST, //XL, //X, //C, //E, and the new //RC20 for edge deployments), FlashBlade (//S, //E, and //EXA launched March 2025 for GPU-cloud and HPC workloads), Everpure Cloud (software-only on AWS and Azure), and Portworx for Kubernetes data management. Consumption models are Evergreen//One (outcome-SLA storage-as-a-service) and Evergreen//Flex (customer-owned hardware, pay-as-you-go). The rebrand to Everpure, announced February 23, 2026, is positioned around data management rather than flash storage alone, supported by the pending 1touch acquisition of data intelligence and governance tooling.

Scale: over 14,500 customers, presence in roughly 64% of the Fortune 500, certified NPS of 84 at December 31, 2025, approximately 6,400 employees (headcount +8% in FY2026), and more than 3,000 issued patents and applications. Named competitors are Dell EMC, NetApp, Hitachi Vantara, HPE, IBM and Huawei, plus public cloud providers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2023 $2,753M → FY2024 $2,831M (+2.8%) → FY2025 $3,168M (+11.9%) → FY2026 $3,663M (+15.6%). Product revenue grew 16% to $1,972M; subscription services grew 15% to $1,691M (46% of total). U.S. revenue grew 12% to $2.5B; rest-of-world grew 25% to $1.2B, now roughly a third of the total.

**Margins.** Gross margin was 70% in both FY2025 and FY2026, with product GM improving 66% → 67% and subscription GM flat at 74%. GAAP operating income was $114.8M (3.1% of revenue), up from $85.3M. GAAP net income was $188.2M (5.1%), up from $106.7M. Non-GAAP operating profit, per the Q4 call, was $635M at a 17.3% margin — a figure that must not be compared against the GAAP 3.1%, since the gap is almost entirely stock compensation plus amortization and restructuring.

The SBC arithmetic is the central financial fact. Summing the disclosed lines — product COR $16.2M, subscription COR $34.2M, R&D $238.0M, S&M $104.2M, G&A $89.1M — yields **$481.7M, or 13.2% of revenue, roughly 4.2x GAAP operating income**. R&D grew 20% (to $963M including SBC, 26% of revenue), faster than revenue, with management explicitly directing incremental spend at DFM density and at "increasing the operational scale of our supply chain partners to support large production deployments for our hyperscaler customer."

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow rose 17% to $880.1M; capex was $264.3M (7.2% of revenue, flat as a ratio to FY2025 despite the absolute increase); free cash flow was $615.7M, a 16.8% margin. OCF exceeded GAAP net income by 4.7x, consistent with $481.7M of SBC, $147.8M of D&A and deferred revenue growth. This is a genuinely cash-generative business at the pre-SBC level.

**Balance sheet.** $1.5B of cash and marketable securities ($854.9M in cash and equivalents), **zero outstanding borrowings**. The prior facility's $100M drawn balance was repaid and terminated June 10, 2025, replaced by an undrawn $500M five-year unsecured revolver maturing June 2030 with a ≤3.5:1 net leverage covenant, in compliance at year-end. Current ratio 1.6. Stockholders' equity $1,445.7M against an accumulated deficit of $1,180.8M.

Screening artifacts deserve explicit handling. An Altman Z-Score of 1.86 and a reported debt/equity of 2.23 look alarming but are not measuring leverage here: the D/E figure is computed on total liabilities, which for a subscription-transitioning storage vendor are dominated by deferred revenue, a customer prepayment rather than a creditor claim. The Z-Score's EBIT/assets term is 0.025 because GAAP EBIT is SBC-suppressed, and its retained-earnings term is negative from pre-FY2023 losses. A company with $1.5B of investments, no debt, an undrawn revolver and $616M of FCF is not a solvency risk. The honest bearish reading of the same inputs is different and worth stating: a 3.1% GAAP operating margin is the more conservative picture of earnings power, and $482M of annual SBC is a real cost borne by shareholders.

**Two items that temper the financial health score.** First, trade receivables rose from $680.9M to $944.8M (+38.8%) against 15.6% revenue growth — from 21.5% to 25.8% of annual revenue, roughly 16 days of implied DSO expansion. This compares a period-end balance to a full-year flow, so it is a proxy rather than a true DSO, and Q4 is structurally the largest quarter (roughly 30% of the year at ~$1.1B), which inflates any year-end snapshot. Faster international growth, where the filing flags collection difficulty, and the hyperscaler royalty's different billing rhythm are plausible benign explanations; extended terms to close large "franchise" deals is a less benign one. Operating cash flow still rose 17%, so this is a quality-of-receivables item to monitor rather than evidence of recognition trouble. Quarterly detail was not obtainable. Second, inventories rose 77% from $42.8M to $75.9M on strategic tariff-mitigation purchases — small in absolute terms, large proportionally, and relevant given falling-NAND-price scenarios.

**Capital allocation.** FY2026 repurchases were 5.6M shares at an average $60.93 for $342.5M, with an incremental $650M authorized during the year ($400M in December 2025) and $329.0M remaining. Separately, 4.2M shares were withheld for employee tax withholding at a cash cost of $271.7M. Combined cash out of $614.2M equals essentially 100% of free cash flow, and diluted shares still rose to 343.0M (basic 325.8M → 328.5M). Buybacks are functioning as dilution offset rather than per-share accretion. A mechanical mitigant: FY2026's strong share price both raised the cash cost of tax withholding and reduced shares retired per dollar, so in a flatter price year the same dollars would retire more stock. No dividend.

**A GAAP earnings event to segregate.** MD&A discloses a full valuation allowance on U.S. deferred tax assets and states that "sufficient positive evidence may become available where we will release all or a portion of the valuation allowance within 12 months," producing a decrease to income tax expense. Against a $1.18B accumulated deficit, a release could be large. It would be non-cash, one-time, and operationally meaningless, and screens will read it as an earnings beat. Separately, the FY2026 tax provision fell 12% to $36.1M despite pretax income rising 52%, attributed to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's removal of Section 174 domestic R&D capitalization. That is a genuine cash-tax benefit, but legislative rather than operational.

## Growth Analysis

**The metrics point in two directions, and management is steering toward the favorable one.**

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription ARR | $1,657.8M | $1,924.3M (+16%) |
| ARR growth, prior year | +21% | +16% |
| Subscription NDR | 117% | **113%** |
| Total RPO | ~$2.6B | **$3.7B (+40%)** |

RPO growth of 40% is the strongest single datapoint in the filing, and management has elevated it to the headline KPI (CFO Tarek Robbiati: "the RPO is really the best measure of latent revenue and momentum that we have"). Three scope limits apply before accepting it. RPO is not backlog: it comprises deferred revenue plus non-cancelable amounts to be invoiced, explicitly **excludes cancelable product orders**, and only **$228.5M** of the $3.7B is non-cancelable product orders. The company expects roughly 45% to convert within twelve months, so the durable multi-year committed portion is closer to $2.0B.

ARR deceleration from 21% to 16% is attributed to "longer term renewals of our Evergreen subscription offerings." That is mechanically coherent — annualizing contract value at a point in time understates value when terms lengthen — and is corroborated by the reported 80% growth in deals above $5M. The 4-point NDR decline is not explained by contract-length mix in the same way, because NDR measures cohort expansion net of contraction and churn. Softer per-customer expansion is a live alternative reading. A related filing inconsistency: MD&A credits "Evergreen//Forever" with driving RPO growth, but that offering appears nowhere in the Item 1 product portfolio, so its terms and economics cannot be verified.

**The hyperscaler line is the growth swing factor.** Shipments began in FY2026 on what the filing calls an "industry-first Flash design win with a major hyperscaler," exceeded the annual forecast, and more shipments are anticipated in FY2027. The economics are royalty-based: Everpure licenses functional IP and recognizes revenue when *supply-chain partners* ship *third-party hardware* to the customer, classified within product revenue. Externally reported call commentary put hyperscaler gross margin at 75-85%; the 10-K says only that royalties contributed to the 1-point product GM improvement "to a lesser extent" than mix, so treat the 75-85% range as unconfirmed. The customer is never named, and press association with a specific hyperscaler was not verified.

The structure is asymmetric. Everpure earns royalty-like revenue without manufacturing, yet Item 1A states it must "ensure a sufficient supply of flash to support our hyperscaler customer" and "may be obligated to fulfill NAND flash purchase commitments if our hyperscale customer reduces its demand." Non-cancelable purchase obligations total $565.8M with $418.8M due within twelve months — 15.4% of FY2026 revenue and roughly 92% of FY2026 FCF, though not disaggregated by counterparty or component. Capex explicitly includes "investments to scale operations in support of our hyperscale customer."

**Two disclosed FY2027 headwinds.** Management guides product gross margin to decline sequentially in Q1 FY2027 on component costs and to "normalize on a full year basis," and warns that the Q1 price increase "may result in reduced sales or the loss of customers." The channel-incentive disclosure — increased partner incentive compensation the company expects to reduce revenue — is unquantified and would be missed entirely by a standard screen.

**Unverified Q1 FY2027.** A single third-party source reports Q1 revenue +35.2% to $1.1B with non-GAAP operating margin up 450bp to 15%. The 10-Q filed June 5, 2026 could not be retrieved. If accurate, that would be a sharp acceleration and almost certainly hyperscaler-royalty-driven, but it is unconfirmed and should not be annualized in a business with disclosed back-end-loaded seasonality and royalty-timing lumpiness. We do not model it as a base case; it is the principal upside variable for the next print.

| Trailing non-GAAP P/E | [unverified — actual: N/A] |
| Headline FCF yield | 2.4% |
| FCF yield after SBC ($134M) | ~0.5% |

We do not have a company-disclosed non-GAAP EPS figure or a consensus estimate in the supplied data, so a trailing non-GAAP P/E cannot be reliably constructed here.

Against legacy storage peers this is a wide premium: NetApp and Dell trade in the low-to-mid teens on forward EPS and at a fraction of Everpure's revenue multiple. Against infrastructure software comparables the gap narrows. The premium is defensible on quality grounds — 70% gross margin, 46% subscription mix, a growth rate roughly 2-3x the named incumbents, no debt — but that is an argument for a premium, not for asymmetric upside from here.

Two adjustments make the multiple less forgiving. First, on a per-share basis, FY2026's free cash flow sustained the share count rather than shrinking it; after SBC, owner earnings are closer to $134M and the yield is roughly 0.5%. Second, the guided sequential product GM decline and the unquantified channel-incentive contra-revenue create a real path to FY2027 estimate cuts, which at ~40x EV/FCF is the multiple's principal vulnerability. Premium-multiple infrastructure names have shown through the 2025-2026 software derating that a correct sector thesis does not protect against multiple compression when a cost or growth surprise lands.

At 38x EV/operating profit, the target and the price reflect the same growth extrapolation, so the "analyst targets are floors" logic does not apply here. The single third-party target located ($80, from a July 7, 2026 note) is not a consensus range, and the supplied analyst dataset contained no targets; we report it as a single data point rather than a distribution.

**Base case:** FY2027 revenue of ~$4.35B (+19%, hyperscaler royalty ramp partly offset by the channel-incentive drag) at a 17% non-GAAP operating margin gives ~$740M of operating profit; adding ~$60M interest income and taxing at 20% yields ~$640M, or ~$1.86-1.90 per diluted share. At 42-43x — the middle of this stock's historical range — that is **$80**, roughly 6.6% above spot. That arithmetic, more than any qualitative judgment, drives the HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

Everpure's own risk factors concede that the data storage market "has not experienced substantial growth in the past few years." Its 16% growth is therefore predominantly share capture from Dell EMC, NetApp, HPE, Hitachi Vantara, IBM and Huawei. That is higher-quality growth than riding a rising market, and also more fragile, because it depends on continuing to displace entrenched vendors with deep bundling and discounting capacity.

The core thesis is disk-to-flash conversion, and the strategically important shift is that in an AI-constrained data center, power and rack space have become the binding constraint. That converts Everpure's efficiency claims — 2-5x less power and space, 5-10x less labor, at least 50% lower TCO versus competitive all-flash configurations, 80% power/space reduction for FlashArray//E versus disk — from a cost argument into a capacity argument. These are company claims, not independently verified. The hyperscaler design win is the most credible third-party validation available, since a hyperscaler adopting DirectFlash over internally designed storage is a meaningful endorsement.

The memory cycle cuts both ways. Everpure is a NAND buyer and is on the losing side of the current shortage, which is why the Q1 FY2027 price increase and the guided product GM decline exist. Three offsets: DirectFlash's lower NAND consumption per usable terabyte is a relative cost hedge against SSD-integrating competitors; HDD vendors reportedly sold out of 2026 capacity to AI data centers removes the cheap-disk alternative for enterprise buyers, accelerating flash conversion; and industry forecasts of NAND flipping from undersupply to surplus in 2H 2027 would turn the FY2027 headwind into an FY2028-29 tailwind. The counter-reading matters: a NAND surplus also cheapens commodity SSDs, narrowing DirectFlash's relative advantage, and hyperscalers with in-house storage teams capture the most benefit from cheap NAND.

Two competitive-pressure tells sit inside the filing. Management devoted unusually pointed 10-K language to characterizing rivals' consumption offerings as "a financing model tied to equipment rather than to a hardware-independent storage service" — a characterization of Dell APEX, HPE GreenLake and NetApp Keystone that we cannot verify. And the increase in partner incentive compensation, disclosed with an expected negative revenue impact, reads as a defense of channel mindshare against vendors that can bundle. Public cloud remains the structural substitution risk the filing names directly, creating the tension that hyperscalers are simultaneously the largest new revenue opportunity and the most credible long-term substitute.

Portworx in Kubernetes data management is the least-discussed asset and the one most adjacent to the "data management" identity the rebrand claims. The pending 1touch acquisition (data discovery and governance, expected to close in Q2 FY2027) extends that identity, but the purchase price, consideration mix and any earn-out are undisclosed, so no dilution estimate is possible.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Component cost inflation with a quantified near-term margin effect.** Product gross margin is guided down sequentially in Q1 FY2027, with full-year "normalization" the stated expectation. The price increase carries an explicit volume-loss warning. This is the highest-probability source of a negative surprise in the next two prints.

**2. Hyperscaler concentration with asymmetric structure.** Royalty-like revenue economics paired with inventory-like downside: $565.8M of non-cancelable purchase obligations ($418.8M within twelve months), plus the disclosed possibility of being left holding NAND commitments if the customer reduces demand. The concentration itself is unquantified — we did not obtain the customer-concentration footnote, and this remains the highest-priority open check. A shifting revenue mix does not establish diversification.

**3. Unquantified channel-incentive revenue drag.** Disclosed as expected to reduce revenue, with no size given.

**4. NDR deceleration.** 117% → 113%. Still healthy for infrastructure, but not explained by the contract-length mix that accounts for the ARR slowdown.

**5. Receivables quality.** +38.8% against +15.6% revenue growth, roughly 16 days of implied DSO expansion, unresolvable without quarterly detail.

**6. SBC and per-share economics.** $481.7M annually, with 100% of FCF absorbed by buybacks plus tax withholding and share count still rising. The compensation plan's measures — Revenue, Non-GAAP Operating Profit, TCV Subscription Sales, NPS — contain no per-share, ROIC or FCF-per-share metric, so dilution is not directly penalized.

**7. Governance and management turnover.** Classified board, blank-check preferred authorization, no action by written consent, no shareholder-called special meetings, board-amendable bylaws and a Delaware exclusive-forum provision — a defensively structured package. Both the CFO and CRO were hired in FY2026; NEO average total compensation rose from $5.49M to $14.54M, most plausibly new-hire inducement equity, which we could not confirm.

**8. Supply-chain single-sourcing and tariffs**, with named China component exposure and no long-term purchase agreements for several key components.

**9. Multiple compression.** At a ~0.5% post-SBC FCF yield, the terminal-multiple sensitivity is high. The AI-infrastructure tape has been supportive — the shares rose 8.4% over the supplied price window — but that support is a beta the stock also loses on the way down.

**10. Verification gaps that should temper confidence in either direction.** No primary-source Q1 FY2027 data (10-Q retrieval failed); no insider transaction data (Form 4 lookup failed, likely a PSTG→"P" remapping artifact — absence of data is not absence of activity); ten 8-K filings through June 12, 2026 unread; no primary earnings transcript; 1touch price undisclosed; customer-concentration footnote unread.

No options data was supplied for this name, so no options-implied read is available.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($104, +39%).** The hyperscaler royalty stream compounds through FY2027 — the unverified Q1 +35% revenue figure proves directional — and a second design win is announced, converting Everpure from a storage vendor into an IP licensor with 75-85% incremental gross margin on capex-light revenue. Component costs pass through cleanly and full-year gross margin normalizes at 70% as guided. RPO's $2.0B durable committed base converts on schedule and franchise deals continue growing at the reported 80% clip, keeping revenue growth above 20% while non-GAAP operating margin expands toward 18.5%. NAND flips to surplus in 2H 2027, turning the FY2028 cost line into a tailwind while power and rack constraints keep accelerating disk-to-flash conversion. On ~$2.16 of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS at 48x, the shares reach $104.

**Bear case ($48, −36%).** Memory cost inflation persists longer than the "normalize on a full year basis" guidance assumes; the Q1 price increase costs volume in a competitive market where Dell and HPE can absorb margin to hold accounts; the channel-incentive drag proves larger than expected. Product gross margin settles 300bp below FY2026 while the channel drag holds revenue growth to single digits at ~$4.0B. NDR slips below 110%, confirming that the ARR deceleration was expansion softness rather than contract-length mix. Hyperscaler shipments push right, triggering an inventory or NAND purchase-commitment write-down against the $418.8M of near-term obligations. On ~$1.45 of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS at a derated 28-32x, the shares trade to $45-50. A valuation-allowance release flatters GAAP EPS in the same year, masking the operating deterioration for a quarter or two.

**Base case ($80, +6.6%).** Revenue of ~$4.35B (+19%), non-GAAP operating margin roughly flat at 17%, non-GAAP EPS ~$1.88, valued at 42-43x. Growth is real and share gains continue, but the multiple already discounts them and the near-term gross-margin path is the wrong direction for a stock at ~40x free cash flow.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

The score moved from 5.3 in March to 6.9 in May (HOLD both times), reflecting the recognition that the balance sheet screens were misleading and that FCF generation is genuine. We are reversing part of that improvement, from 6.9 to 6.0. The shares are roughly where the July inference placed them, so this is not a reaction to price action. Three items drive it: the valuation score falls from 6.5 to 4.5 because at $75.06 the stock carries ~39x EV/FCF with a ~0.5% post-SBC owner yield, well above the FY2026 buyback average of $60.93 at which management itself was buying; the risk score falls because the guided product gross-margin decline, the unquantified channel-incentive contra-revenue and the $418.8M of near-term NAND-linked purchase obligations are all specific, near-dated and unpriced; and management quality moves to 5.5 because 100% of FCF was absorbed neutralizing dilution while the incentive plan contains no per-share metric, with no insider transaction data available to offset. Financial health remains strong at 7.0, marked down modestly for the receivables build and the SBC-adjusted cash generation. Growth and competitive position hold at 7.0 each — the RPO acceleration and hyperscaler validation are real.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Q2 FY2027 results:** product gross margin holding at ≥66% with full-year total gross margin reaffirmed at ~70% despite component costs, demonstrating that the Q1 price increase passed through without volume loss.
2. **A second hyperscaler design win disclosed,** or the customer-concentration footnote plus two consecutive quarters of sequential royalty growth with no NAND commitment write-down.
3. **Price below ~$60** with FY2027 estimates intact, which would restore asymmetry.
4. **Clustered open-market insider buying** once Form 4 data becomes retrievable under the new "P" ticker.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Full-year FY2027 product gross margin guided below 65%, or total gross margin below 68%.
2. Subscription NDR below 110% or subscription ARR growth below 12%.
3. Any inventory or NAND purchase-commitment write-down, or a disclosed reduction in hyperscaler shipment expectations.
4. Receivables continuing to outgrow revenue by more than 15 percentage points on a quarterly basis with operating cash flow conversion deteriorating.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 FY2027 earnings is the decisive event — it is the first print to contain both the full-quarter effect of the price increase and a clean read on hyperscaler royalty run-rate. Secondary checkpoints are the 1touch close (Q2 FY2027, price and consideration mix still undisclosed) and any FY2027 valuation-allowance release, which should be excluded from run-rate earnings when it appears.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Everpure is a better business than its GAAP income statement suggests and a more expensive stock than its free cash flow multiple suggests once $482M of annual stock compensation is charged against the same cash. The growth story is credible and improving — two consecutive years of revenue acceleration to 15.6%, RPO up 40%, an 80% increase in deals above $5M, and a hyperscaler royalty arrangement that monetizes IP without manufacturing. The balance sheet is genuinely strong: zero debt, $1.5B of securities, an undrawn $500M revolver, and covenant compliance, with the alarming Altman Z-Score and 2.23 debt/equity both artifacts of deferred revenue and a legacy accumulated deficit rather than leverage.

What prevents a BUY is the intersection of price and the next two quarters. The company has told investors, in its own filing, that component costs rose significantly, that product gross margin will decline sequentially in Q1 FY2027, that it raised prices and may lose customers as a result, and that it increased channel incentives in a way it expects to reduce revenue. Net dollar retention fell four points. At 6.6x revenue and 39x free cash flow, none of that is discounted. Our base case of $80 offers 6.6% upside against 36% downside in the bear case, an asymmetry that argues for owning the position already held and waiting for the next print rather than adding at $75.

For investors positioned in AI infrastructure, Everpure is a legitimate second-derivative holding with a real hyperscaler validation and a structural NAND-efficiency hedge, and it sits on the cost-absorbing side of the current cycle rather than the supplier side. That cost exposure is the central question for the next two quarters.
