**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Acushnet owns the two most valuable franchises in golf equipment and footwear: Titleist, the #1 golf ball used on 72% of worldwide professional tours, and FootJoy, the #1 shoe on the PGA Tour for eight decades and the #1 glove. The business is a genuine premium-brand moat: disciplined two-year product cycles, 850+ active U.S. ball patents, owned manufacturing, and a "pyramid of influence" model that converts tour validation into amateur demand at premium prices. The structural backdrop is favorable: U.S. on-course participation has risen for eight consecutive years and worldwide rounds played sit ~22% above 2019.

The problem is price against fundamentals. The stock has appreciated 35% over the past twelve months to $97.60 and trades at roughly 31x trailing and ~25x forward earnings, against revenue growth that has settled into the low single digits (+4.1% in FY2025, +4.8% constant-currency in Q1 2026) and core-segment operating income that declined. The Titleist equipment segment, the most profitable part of the company, saw operating income fall 10.6% in FY2025 even as its sales rose 5.9%, as the company spent more on advertising, selling, fitting networks, R&D, and absorbed tariffs to defend its franchise. Forward estimates are mixed (next-quarter EPS revised down 8.2% over 90 days), and the company has missed consensus in all four of the last quarters.

The headline net-income declines (−12% FY2025, −18% Q1 2026) are largely optical, distorted by prior-period one-time gains and current-period charges; adjusted EBITDA and constant-currency revenue both grew. That nuance prevents the situation from being a clear sell, but it does not turn a high-quality, slow-growth franchise at a premium multiple into a buy. Three specific overhangs cap the upside case: the USGA/R&A golf-ball distance rollback (effective 2028 for elite play, potentially 2030 across the whole game) that could force a redesign of the most profitable category; ongoing tariff margin compression; and a ~50.6% controlled-company ownership structure. We rate GOLF a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Acushnet is the global leader in performance-driven golf products, organized into three reportable segments for FY2025:

- **Titleist golf equipment (~62% of sales, $1,596M):** golf balls ($821M) plus clubs, wedges, and putters ($775M). Pro V1/V1x balls, GT drivers, T-Series irons, Vokey wedges, Scotty Cameron putters.
- **FootJoy golf wear (~22%, $570M):** shoes, gloves, outerwear, apparel.
- **Golf gear (~10%, $245M):** bags, headwear, travel gear.
- Plus non-reportable premium apparel (KJUS), PG Golf (recycled balls), and the Titleist Performance Institute.

The customer is the "dedicated golfer": avid, skill-biased players who pay premium prices. Consumables (balls and gloves, ~40% of sales) provide recurring revenue; durables (~60%) drive cyclical upgrade demand. Custom-logo balls (25–30% of ball sales) tie a meaningful slice of revenue to corporate spending, which adds discretionary-cycle sensitivity. The company employs ~7,300 people and runs a ~200-person R&D operation.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth — steady but moderating to low-single-digits.** FY2022 $2.27B → FY2023 $2.38B → FY2024 $2.457B → FY2025 $2.559B (+4.2% constant currency). Q1 2026 net sales were $753M (+7.1% reported), with Titleist equipment +8.9% and Golf gear +10.8%, but FootJoy only +1.7% reported and −1.3% constant currency as footwear volumes declined.

**Profitability — GAAP earnings declined, but the comparison is distorted.** Net income attributable fell from $214M in FY2024 to $188.5M in FY2025; Q1 2026 net income was $81.4M vs $99.4M, EPS $1.36 vs $1.62. Both comparisons are dominated by one-timers: FY2024 carried a $17.7M non-cash PTO policy benefit, Q1 2025 included a $20.9M non-cash Lionscore deconsolidation gain, and FY2025 absorbed a $17M loss on debt extinguishment plus $13.7M of restructuring. Adjusted EBITDA rose modestly to $410M (16.0% margin) from $404M (16.5%). A model relying on GAAP net income alone overstates deterioration.

**The real concern is core-segment margin compression.** Gross margin slipped from 48.3% (FY2024) to 47.7% (FY2025), with Q1 2026 at 47.2% vs 47.9%, driven by tariffs. Titleist equipment segment operating income fell 10.6% to $244.9M in FY2025 even as segment sales rose 5.9%. The mechanism: rising advertising/promotion, selling, fitting, and R&D spend plus tariff costs outran gross-profit gains. The open question is whether this is cyclical (new-product launch investment ahead of the Pro V1 cadence) or a structural rise in the cost of maintaining leadership against well-funded competitors.

**Cash flow — deteriorating but explainable.** Operating cash flow declined from $372M (FY2023) to $245M (FY2024) to $194M (FY2025), driven by ERP investment and working-capital funding. Capex was $74M in FY2025, guided to ~$95M for 2026, plus ~$25M of capitalized ERP costs. Capital intensity is rising, which compresses near-term free cash flow.

**Balance sheet — moderate leverage, thin cash.** Altman Z-Score 2.59 (grey zone), D/E ~2.0, current ratio 2.38, ROE 23.9%, ROA 8%. Total debt was ~$950M at YE2025, rising to ~$1.15B by Q1 2026 as the revolver was drawn to $627.6M to fund the seasonal H1 working-capital build (receivables jumped from $217M to $505M). Cash is only ~$48–51M, ~95% held offshore. The 2025 refinancing issued $500M of 5.625% notes due 2033, redeemed $350M of 7.375% 2028 notes, and extended the $950M revolver to 2030, lowering the coupon but raising gross debt. $657.5M is variable-rate; a 100bp move is $6.6M pretax.

## Growth Analysis

Acushnet's growth profile is mature single-digit, supported by a genuine structural tailwind in golf participation but constrained by already-dominant category share. The 10-K states the company holds dominant share in balls, shoes, and gloves, limiting incremental share gains. Growth therefore depends on: (1) premium price/mix, (2) the durability of post-COVID participation, and (3) modest geographic expansion (EMEA and Rest of World are growing double-digits, offsetting persistent Korean weakness, −5.3% FY2025 and −7.6% Q1 2026).

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and does not support an inflection call. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose +16.4% over 90 days (seasonal recovery), but next-quarter estimates fell −8.2% and current-year fell −2.2% over the same window. The overall revision trend skews downward, signaling negative near-term earnings momentum on most tenors. This is the opposite of the +30%-over-90-days inflection signal that would warrant aggressive forward-multiple treatment; here, the dominant near-term estimates are drifting lower against a stock that has already re-rated. Combined with four consecutive quarterly EPS misses (−5.5%, −2.3%, −6.2%, −6.3%), forward visibility is modest and the bar for management to clear consensus is not low.

A multi-year transition looms with the golf-ball distance rollback, which could force a redesign of Titleist's most profitable category beginning 2028 (elite) and potentially 2030 (whole game). This is a growth headwind in the form of a regulatory event: the cost of re-developing an entire ball lineup, with competitive-reset risk.

## Valuation Assessment

At $97.60, GOLF trades at ~31x trailing EPS of $3.11 and ~25x current-year consensus of $3.96, ~24x next-year $4.14. The dividend yield is ~1.0%. This is a premium to the Leisure Products peer median P/E of 15.5x, though the peer set is heterogeneous and distorted by negative-earnings names (Brunswick −39.7, Polaris −8.9). The cleaner comparables — Thor at 15.5x, Yeti at 25.5x, Mattel at 9.3x — frame GOLF at the top of the range.

The premium is partly justified by quality: Acushnet earns a 23.9% ROE, generates positive free cash flow, returns capital aggressively, and owns a defensible moat that the toy and RV makers lack. But the valuation guardrail matters here. Revenue grows low-single-digits, forward estimates are mixed-to-falling, and the trailing period is not a cyclical trough, so the cycle-adjustment logic that would justify a high multiple does not apply. At ~24-25x forward earnings on ~3-4% revenue growth and compressing core margins, the multiple already discounts the quality. The PEG, on any reasonable growth assumption, exceeds 3x.

Analyst price targets reinforce the lack of asymmetry: consensus mean $96.00, median $97.00, high $100.00, low $87.00 — the stock trades essentially at the median target with only ~2% upside to the high and ~11% downside to the low. All seven covering analysts rate the stock HOLD, with zero buys. Because this is a premium multiple with mixed-to-falling estimates rather than a cheap stock with rising estimates, the analyst targets here function as a realistic ceiling, not a stale floor that consensus will revise upward.

A premium multiple on a slow grower with deteriorating forward estimates is the opposite of an asymmetric-upside setup. We score valuation 4.5 — the quality prevents a lower score, but there is no identified mispricing to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

Acushnet's competitive position is the strongest dimension and is stable-to-improving in its core franchise. The moat rests on tour validation, premium fitting networks, the ball-patent portfolio, owned manufacturing, and a disciplined launch cadence. Competition is intense and well-capitalized (Callaway/Topgolf, TaylorMade, Ping, Srixon, Bridgestone in equipment; Nike, Adidas, Ecco in footwear), but Acushnet competes on performance and validation rather than price, which insulates the premium balls/clubs franchise.

The trajectory is more nuanced. The core ball/club franchise is holding and modestly gaining. FootJoy is softer, as apparel and footwear are more exposed to fashion cycles and discounting. The rising spend required to maintain leadership (the FY2025 segment operating-income decline) suggests the cost of defending the moat is increasing, which tempers what would otherwise be a clear-cut high score. On balance, this remains a wide-moat franchise with structural participation tailwinds, scored 7.0.

The golf-ball rollback could reset the competitive field. As market leader with the deepest patent portfolio and R&D resources, Acushnet argues it is best-positioned in a more rules-constrained environment, since historically complexity favors the incumbent with the most engineering depth. That is a reasonable bull point, but it remains unproven over a multi-year horizon.

## Risk Assessment

1. **USGA/R&A golf-ball distance rollback (most company-specific structural risk).** The Overall Distance Standard change takes effect for elite play in Jan 2028, with recreational players able to use current balls until Jan 1, 2030; a Jan 2026 proposal floated a single 2030 date across the whole game. This could force redesign of Titleist's most profitable category. Multi-year overhang, not yet resolved.

2. **Tariffs — actively compressing margins, with a refund wildcard.** Substantially all FootJoy footwear is made in Vietnam; gloves and some balls in Thailand; inputs from China. The tariff regime hurt FootJoy gross profit by $2.8M in Q1 2026 alone. Offsetting upside: the Feb 2026 SCOTUS ruling on IEEPA tariffs and the April 2026 CBP refund portal — Acushnet has filed claims but conservatively recognized no refund asset, leaving an unbooked potential one-time benefit.

3. **Controlled-company / Magnus ownership (~50.6%).** Magnus controls the board and dividend policy; interests may diverge from minority holders. Magnus has held the stake as effectively its only asset and at times pledged shares, a latent overhang if it needs liquidity, with a 35% third-party change-of-control threshold in the credit agreement.

4. **Thin domestic liquidity, rising leverage, seasonal revolver dependence.** Cash is largely offshore, the revolver is drawn at the seasonal peak, and gross debt is climbing. Manageable given strong cash generation, but limited cushion if a discretionary-spending downturn coincides with the ball-rollback transition.

5. **FX.** >40% of sales in foreign currencies while ~80% of foreign COGS is USD-denominated; managed via forward hedges that dampen both downside and upside.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term event risk. Near-term ATM IV is 92.4% against 27.7% one-year historical volatility, in the 82nd percentile, with the term structure in steep backwardation (92.4% near-term vs 25.0% long-term). This signals the market is pricing imminent, single-event stress, most likely tied to the immediate June 18 expiry (1-week implied move ±7.9%). Beyond the near-term, the picture normalizes: the 1-month and 3-month tenors sit at 23.8% and 25.0% IV, near historical vol, with balanced put/call skew (−0.0%) and a low OI put/call ratio of 0.14, which fell sharply from 1.51 in mid-May as the stock rallied 16.9%. The collapse in protective put positioning alongside the rally suggests bearish hedging has unwound rather than intensified. Short interest is elevated at 12.6% of float with 7.2 days to cover, a modest squeeze-risk factor on any positive surprise but also a reflection of skepticism about the premium valuation. Net read: the market is not pricing structural collapse, but the rich near-term vol argues against chasing into a binary near-term event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Acushnet is a wide-moat, market-leading franchise in a structurally growing sport. Post-COVID participation has stepped up durably, supporting recurring consumables revenue. The GAAP earnings decline is optical, with adjusted EBITDA and constant-currency revenue both growing, and the company aggressively returns capital (share count down from 72M in 2022 to 58.5M, dividend raised to $0.255). Unbooked tariff-refund optionality is a one-time catalyst not in current numbers. As the ball-rollback's best-positioned incumbent, a rules-constrained environment could entrench leadership. Bull case: estimates re-accelerate, tariff refunds materialize, and the multiple holds at ~27x forward $4.20 → ~$113 (+16%).

**Bear Case.** The premium multiple discounts a slow-growth profile with mixed-to-falling forward estimates and four straight earnings misses. Core Titleist equipment operating margins are compressing as the cost of defending the franchise rises. Tariffs continue to bite, the ball rollback forces costly lineup redesign, and the controlled-company structure caps strategic optionality. A consumer-discretionary slowdown coincides with thin domestic liquidity and a seasonally drawn revolver. Bear case: multiple de-rates to ~18-19x forward earnings → ~$76 (−22%).

The base case sits between: estimates stay flat-to-soft, margins stabilize but do not expand, and the multiple modestly compresses toward 23x forward $4.10 → ~$94 (−4%). Risk/reward is balanced and does not favor adding at current prices.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain — insufficient evidence for a confident directional call; quality is real but the premium valuation, mixed-to-falling estimates, and multi-year regulatory overhang offset it).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (early August 2026), with the Pro V1 launch cadence and any tariff-refund recognition as interim catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Core Titleist equipment segment operating margin stabilizes or expands for two consecutive quarters, AND forward EPS estimates turn positive on 30- and 90-day revision windows.
- Material IEEPA tariff refund recognized (one-time cash/earnings benefit currently unbooked), or clear evidence the ball-rollback transition entrenches rather than disrupts Titleist's lead.
- A pullback toward the low-$80s (~18-19x forward earnings) that restores valuation asymmetry on the unchanged quality.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Constant-currency revenue growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters, signaling participation normalization rather than a durable step-up.
- Gross margin falls below ~46% on sustained tariff pressure with no offsetting price/mix, or core-segment operating income declines accelerate.
- A forced or strategic Magnus stake sale triggering change-of-control provisions, or a dividend cut signaling capital-return strain.

## Conclusion

Acushnet is a high-quality business: the dominant premium franchise in a structurally healthy sport, with a defensible moat, strong returns on capital, and disciplined shareholder returns. The trajectory in our scoring has been gently improving (5.3 → 5.6), and the latest data confirms rather than reverses that: financial health is resilient once one strips out the optical GAAP-earnings noise, and management's capital allocation remains aligned. But the case for owning the stock at $97.60 is not asymmetric. The shares trade at ~24-25x forward earnings on low-single-digit revenue growth, with forward estimates drifting lower on key tenors, four consecutive earnings misses, compressing core-segment margins, and a multi-year golf-ball distance-rollback overhang that the market has not been forced to price. All seven covering analysts rate it HOLD, and the stock sits at the median target with limited room to either side.

We would own this name through a cheaper entry or a confirmed margin/estimate inflection, neither of which is present today. We rate GOLF a HOLD and would revisit on a pullback toward the low-$80s or evidence that core Titleist economics are reaccelerating.
