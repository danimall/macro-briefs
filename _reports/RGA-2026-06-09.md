**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Reinsurance Group of America is one of three or four dominant global life and health reinsurers, with $4.3 trillion of life reinsurance in force, $164.1B in total assets, and a diversified franchise spanning traditional mortality/morbidity reinsurance and a fast-growing Financial Solutions book (longevity, asset-intensive annuity/coinsurance, pension risk transfer). The business is performing well: Q1 2026 adjusted operating EPS of $6.97 beat consensus (~$6.10) by roughly 14%, revenue rose ~22% YoY to $6.49B, and adjusted operating ROE reached 15.2% (16.2% ex-notable items). Consensus EPS estimates are rising across every horizon, the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters (twice by double digits), and the entire published sell-side target range sits above the current price.

This assembles a value-with-momentum profile: ~7.4x forward consensus EPS ($27.07 current year, $29.02 next year), ~1.0x book value, a 15%+ return on equity, and a 1.8% dividend recently raised. An independent review of this analysis correctly notes that the scenario arithmetic (base ~$235, bull $261-288, bear $170-180) implies a roughly 2:1 to 3:1 reward/risk skew and asks why this is not a BUY.

The honest answer, and the basis for maintaining HOLD with a bullish lean, is that the book-value "floor" the bull case rests on is being eroded in real time, and a meaningful portion of the recent earnings strength is cyclical rather than structural. Q1 2026 comprehensive income was negative $37M, and gross unrealized losses on the fixed-maturity portfolio grew from $7.0B at year-end 2025 to $8.5B, suppressing reported book value per share even as GAAP net income hits records. With rates more likely to stay elevated than to fall in the current macro backdrop, the "cheap at 1.0x book with a rising floor" framing overstates the downside support. A genuine ratings-downgrade binary tail (collateral/termination triggers), the lumpiness of annual assumption reviews (a ~$222M UK mortality charge pushed EMEA Traditional to a full-year loss in FY2025), and near-term options-implied stress make this a constructive accumulate-on-weakness name rather than a high-conviction long at $201.

## Company Overview

RGA is an insurance holding company incorporated in Missouri and headquartered in Chesterfield, MO. It operates through nine geographic segments (U.S. & Latin America, Canada, EMEA, Asia Pacific) split into two business lines:

- **Traditional reinsurance**: individual and group life, health, disability, and critical illness. Premiums are earned over multi-decade treaty durations, and profitability hinges on whether actual mortality/morbidity claims track the pricing assumptions. Management explicitly identifies mortality/morbidity as its single most significant risk.
- **Financial Solutions**: longevity reinsurance, asset-intensive (annuity/coinsurance), pension risk transfer (PRT), capital/financial reinsurance, and stable-value products. Profitability here is driven by investment spreads and fee income rather than claims experience.

The competitive moat rests on underwriting and mortality expertise, fast quote turnaround, global diversification, deep cedant relationships, and the capital and ratings strength needed to execute large structured transactions. Principal competitors are Munich Re, Swiss Re, SCOR, Hannover Re, and Pacific Life Re, with Athene and Global Atlantic increasingly active in the asset-intensive segment. Leadership transitioned to CEO Tony Cheng (who rose through RGA's Asia operations) from prior CEO Anna Manning, with Axel André as CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Top-line and earnings momentum are strong.** Revenue grew from ~$16.3B (2022) to ~$23.7B (2025); net income rose to $1.18B (2025) from $717M (2024) and $902M (2023). Q1 2026 delivered net income to shareholders of $330M, diluted EPS of $4.98 (vs. $4.27 in Q1'25), and adjusted operating EPS of $6.97. Adjusted operating income before tax was $611M vs. $485M (+26%), described by management as broad-based across regions. TTM GAAP ROE was 9.7%; adjusted operating ROE was 15.2%.

**Growth drivers.** The Equitable Holdings transaction (closed July 31, 2025) added a 75% quota share of ~$12B of in-force individual life liabilities, contributing $39M to Q1'26 pretax income and driving large growth in U.S. Financial Solutions. Net investment income rose to $1.70B in Q1'26 from $1.23B, helped by a growing asset-intensive base ($37.2B U.S. / $38B Asia, both up sharply YoY) and higher variable investment income; investment yield ex-spread reached 4.93% (+29bps YoY). A portion of this lift, variable investment income and elevated reinvestment yields, is cyclical and mean-reverting, not a permanent step-up.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total equity was $13.38B; book value per share approximately $204 on 65.5M shares. Long-term debt was $6.1B at an average 5.40% cost. Operating cash flow was robust at $2.87B in Q1'26. The dividend was raised to $0.93/quarter (from $0.89), and a $500M buyback was authorized in January 2026, but only $50M was repurchased in Q1; management is prioritizing capital deployment into new transactions over buybacks.

**A critical earnings-vs-book-value divergence.** Despite record GAAP net income, Q1'26 total comprehensive income was negative $37M: $1.66B of unrealized AFS investment losses (rising rates) were only partly offset by a $1.31B discount-rate benefit on reserves. Gross unrealized losses on the fixed-maturity portfolio grew from $7.0B (YE25) to $8.5B (Q1'26). These are largely rate-driven and held-to-recovery, and the simultaneous discounting benefit on long-dated reserves means the economic picture is healthier than reported book equity. But the practical effect is that book value per share is not compounding at anything like the operating ROE while rates stay elevated, which is the central reason the bull case's "1.0x book floor" support is weaker than it looks.

A note on screening artifacts: the Altman Z-Score (~0.33) and D/E (~10.6x) that surface on standard health screens are not meaningful for a reinsurer, whose balance sheet is dominated by policyholder liabilities and a high-quality (94.3% investment-grade) ~$107B fixed-maturity portfolio. Piotroski signals were uniformly positive.

## Growth Analysis

The structural demand backdrop is favorable. Two tailwinds are evident in the filings: surging demand for asset-intensive and longevity/PRT solutions as primary insurers seek capital relief (RGA's asset-intensive base nearly doubled YoY in both the U.S. and Asia), and higher reinvestment yields lifting spread income. Asia Pacific remains a key growth engine, consistent with CEO Cheng's background.

Estimate revisions are positive across all horizons, a genuine forward signal: current-year consensus rose +3.8% over 30 days and +3.6% over 90 days to $27.07; next-year rose +1.3% over 90 days to $29.02. This is steady, broad-based upward drift. It is not the +30%-velocity acceleration that forces a near-term re-rating: the next-quarter estimate is essentially flat (+0.8%/30d, -0.6%/90d), and current-quarter revisions are slightly negative over 90 days. The earnings track record (3 beats / 1 miss; +33.5% and +14.2% beats but a -16.1% miss in Q2'25 on the UK mortality charge) confirms both the underlying strength and the lumpiness. RGA is a steady compounder with a transaction-and-spread growth engine, and that characterization matters for how much multiple expansion to expect.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$201 and ~$27 of current-year operating EPS, RGA trades at roughly 7.4x forward earnings and approximately 1.0x book value with a 15%+ adjusted operating ROE. On a forward operating-earnings basis it is inexpensive in absolute terms.

The peer comparison is genuinely heterogeneous, which limits its precision. The same-sector comps are predominantly P&C and mortgage reinsurers (Everest 6.8x, RenaissanceRe 4.8x, MGIC 8.1x, SiriusPoint 5.5x; median 6.8x) whose businesses, cash-flow durations, and cycle exposures differ materially from RGA's long-duration life book. On the headline P/E metric, RGA's ~7.4x forward sits modestly above the peer median of 6.8x, so the "deep discount to the complex" framing does not hold cleanly. The more relevant anchor is the historical 1.0-1.3x book range for quality life reinsurers; at ~1.0x, RGA is at the low end of fair, which provides some support but not a wide margin of safety.

The reason I score valuation 7.0 rather than higher is the book-value dynamic discussed above. A 1.0x-book multiple is only a true floor if book value is compounding; with comprehensive income negative and unrealized losses growing, that floor is being held down by rate moves outside management's control. On normalized earnings power and a re-rating toward ~8.5x forward, a base case around $235 (+17%) is defensible; the entire sell-side target range ($212 low, $252 mean, $261 median, $288 high) sits above spot, which supports upside but should be read alongside the recurring assumption-review volatility that periodically interrupts the compounding story.

## Competitive Landscape

RGA operates in a concentrated global life-reinsurance oligopoly where scale, mortality data, and A-range ratings are real barriers to entry. The trajectory is improving: the company is winning large asset-intensive and longevity blocks (Equitable, and a near-doubling of the asset-intensive base) and its underwriting franchise remains differentiated. The offsetting dynamic is intensifying competition for those same large blocks from Athene, Global Atlantic, and others, which pressures returns, plus growing reliance on third-party/sidecar capital (the sponsored, non-consolidated Ruby Re vehicle, ~$4.1B ceded recoverable) and modco/funds-withheld structures that introduce counterparty and asset-control nuances. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-improving, warranting a 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Mortality/morbidity assumption risk (core).** FY2025 EMEA Traditional posted an operating loss (~$106M) driven by an ~$222M UK mortality assumption update. Q1'26 loss ratios crept higher (U.S. Traditional 91.7% vs 91.0%; Asia Pacific Traditional 85.5% vs 84.0%) on less favorable claims experience. Annual Q3 assumption reviews are a recurring source of single-segment volatility.
2. **Interest-rate / AOCI drag.** Negative comprehensive income and growing gross unrealized bond losses pressure book value and statutory capital. Largely held-to-recovery and economically offset by reserve discounting, but a real near-term headwind given the elevated-rate macro environment.
3. **Ratings sensitivity (binary tail).** Many derivative and reinsurance contracts contain termination/collateral triggers if debt ratings fall below BBB+/Baa1 or financial strength below A-/A3. A downgrade would raise funding costs and could force collateral posting; this is the single most important external variable and a genuinely binary, not-fully-priced risk.
4. **Counterparty/funds-withheld concentration.** In U.S. Financial Solutions, ≥90% of funds withheld is concentrated in two clients; RGA bears investment performance on cedant-owned assets without direct control.
5. **Integration/transaction risk** from large asset-intensive deals and the sponsored Ruby Re vehicle.
6. **Regulatory/capital** (Reg XXX/A-XXX, PBR, Bermuda/Pillar II global minimum tax) raising reserve-financing and tax costs.

Risk factors were unchanged between the FY2025 10-K and the Q1 2026 10-Q. The mortgage/credit book is well-positioned: 91.7% U.S. CRE loans, modest watch list, low non-accruals, and a manageable 15% office exposure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 32.6% against 23.5% one-year historical vol (rich, +9.1pp), the term structure is in backwardation (32.6% near-term vs 25.0% six-month), and put/call skew is elevated at 9.7%. ATM IV rose 5.7pp over the past week even as the price ticked up 2%. This configuration of backwardation, rising near-term vol, and put skew indicates the market is pricing imminent stress into the June 18 expiry, coinciding with the June 16-17 FOMC. For a rate-sensitive balance sheet with growing unrealized losses, this near-term caution is consistent with the fundamental view that the entry is better on weakness than at $201 into a binary rate event. It does not change the medium-term thesis but argues against pressing the position right here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** RGA is a structurally advantaged life/health reinsurer compounding earnings at a 15%+ adjusted operating ROE, with a multi-year demand tailwind in asset-intensive and longevity reinsurance, a successful Equitable integration adding to embedded value, and rising consensus estimates. At ~7.4x forward and ~1.0x book, modest multiple normalization toward ~8.5x plus continued estimate revisions supports $261-288 (+30-43%).

**Bear case.** A meaningful portion of recent strength is cyclical (variable investment income, elevated reinvestment yields) and mean-reverts; annual assumption reviews periodically deliver multi-hundred-million-dollar charges (UK mortality FY2025); the book-value "floor" is being eroded by AOCI losses while rates stay elevated; and the ratings-downgrade trigger is a low-probability, high-severity tail. In this path, the stock drifts toward ~$170-180 (-12-15%), with book value providing partial but not rising support.

**Reconciliation with the upgrade challenge.** The reward/risk skew is favorable on paper. The reasons it remains a HOLD rather than a BUY are specific and source-backed: (1) the book-value floor central to the bull case is being suppressed, with comprehensive income negative and unrealized losses growing $1.5B in one quarter, so the downside support is softer than 1.0x book implies; (2) RGA's forward multiple sits above the peer median P/E, so this is not a deep-discount mispricing; (3) the +3.6%/90d revision is steady-compounder drift, not the kind of acceleration that re-rates a stock quickly, and the underlying beats lean partly on cyclical investment income; and (4) the ratings-trigger binary tail and assumption-review lumpiness are legitimate reasons a quality compounder trades at a discounted multiple. The current price (~$201) sits between a preferred accumulation zone (~$185-190) and the base-case target (~$235), so the asymmetry, while positive, is not compelling enough at this entry to override these frictions. This is a constructive HOLD with a defined upgrade path, not a hedged BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the June 16-17 FOMC (for the rate/AOCI trajectory), the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), and the Q3 annual assumption review (the historical source of segment volatility).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward $185-190 (~1.0x or below book on a stabilizing book value), restoring a wider margin of safety; or
- Comprehensive income turning positive and gross unrealized losses contracting for two consecutive quarters, confirming the book-value floor is compounding rather than eroding; or
- A clean Q3 assumption review (no material adverse charge) alongside continued positive estimate revisions, demonstrating the lumpiness risk is contained.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any rating-agency action placing RGA's financial strength on negative watch or below A-/A3, given the contractual collateral/termination triggers;
- A material adverse assumption charge exceeding ~$250M that drives a quarterly miss and negative full-year revisions;
- Loss-ratio deterioration sustained across multiple regions (beyond the modest Q1 creep) signaling structural mortality/morbidity mispricing.

## Conclusion

RGA is a well-run, structurally advantaged life/health reinsurer generating record operating earnings and a 15%+ adjusted operating ROE at an undemanding ~7.4x forward multiple, with rising estimates and a favorable scenario skew. The case for upgrading to BUY is real and was given full consideration. It is held back by two concrete, source-documented frictions: the book-value floor that anchors the bull thesis is being eroded by AOCI/unrealized-loss dynamics (negative comprehensive income, $8.5B of unrealized bond losses) in an elevated-rate environment, and a meaningful slice of the recent beats is cyclical investment income rather than durable franchise growth, alongside a genuine ratings-trigger binary tail and recurring assumption-review lumpiness. At ~$201, between the preferred accumulation zone (~$185-190) and base case (~$235), the entry is constructive but not compelling enough to issue a high-conviction long in a sector where the bar for that conviction is appropriately high.

**Recommendation: HOLD (bullish lean).** Accumulate toward $185-190 or on confirmation that book value is compounding; upgrade to BUY on either condition.
