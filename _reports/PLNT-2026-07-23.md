**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Planet Fitness runs the dominant value-priced gym franchise in the United States (~21.5 million members across 2,909 clubs — 2,617 franchised, 292 corporate — as of Q1 2026) on a capital-light model where franchisees fund real-estate and build-out costs while the parent collects high-margin royalties, national advertising fees, and equipment sales. The underlying business has compounded well: FY2025 adjusted EBITDA reached ~$551.6M, up from $488M in 2024 and $435M in 2023, and Q1'26 operating cash flow rose 10% to $147.5M on a franchise segment carrying a ~70% EBITDA margin.

The problem is that the growth algorithm broke in a way the market read decisively. Q1'26 headline revenue rose 21.9%, but that was flattered by a revenue-neutral NAF contribution step-up (2%→3% of franchisee sales) and a lumpy equipment re-equipment cycle (+123%). The organic demand signal — system-wide same-club sales — decelerated to +3.5% from +6.1% a year earlier, because member sign-ups fell short of plan following a brand repositioning and consumer pushback forced management to pause Black Card price increases. From the July 2025 all-time high of $113.55, the stock has fallen ~53% to ~$53, and the guidance cut spawned securities class-action suits (class period ~Nov 6, 2025 – May 5/6, 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline Sept 14, 2026). Consensus EPS estimates have followed the reset down: current-year -5.5% and next-year -11.7% over 90 days.

Against that, the stock now trades at ~19x forward EPS with insiders buying — CEO Colleen Keating purchased 5,000 shares at $49.54 in the open market on May 12, and a new director bought at $46.21 on May 8, both after the collapse. The setup is two-sided: a durable, cash-generative franchise on one hand, a demand reset with a GLP-1 secular overhang and mid-turnaround management churn on the other. We rate PLNT HOLD, with no clear near-term catalyst to resolve the debate before the next same-club-sales prints.

## Company Overview

Planet Fitness franchises and operates budget fitness clubs under a "Judgement Free Zone" positioning, with Classic Card memberships starting near $15/month and an upsell to the premium PF Black Card that bundles amenities including tanning. Roughly 88% of new members join online or through the mobile app. The company reports three segments:

- **Franchise** — royalties, franchise/ADA fees, placement revenue, and the National Advertising Fund (NAF). Capital-light, ~70% Q1'26 segment EBITDA margin. This is the value engine.
- **Corporate-owned clubs** — 292 clubs; more capital-intensive at ~33% EBITDA margin.
- **Equipment** — sells and mandates replacement equipment to franchisees; thinner-margin and cyclical with franchisee re-equipment obligations.

The structural edge is scale in the value segment: franchisee-funded unit growth, national advertising leverage, and recurring auto-draft (EFT) membership revenue. Roughly 750 clubs sit under contractual development commitment. Two franchisee groups each account for ~7% of clubs, a modest concentration to monitor.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue quality is the central issue.** Q1'26 total revenue of $337.2M (+21.9% YoY) decomposes unfavorably once adjusted for one-time items:

- Franchise revenue +9.7% to $102.2M (royalties +7.6%).
- NAF revenue +46.8% — almost entirely the mechanical 1-point contribution increase, which is revenue-neutral to profit because it is matched by NAF expense.
- Corporate-owned +5.2% to $140.6M.
- Equipment +123.4% to $62.1M — a re-equipment cycle spike to existing franchisee clubs, lumpy and non-recurring.

Stripping the NAF step-up and the equipment spike, same-club sales decelerated as noted above. That is the number the market traded on, correctly.

**Profitability and cash flow remain healthy.** Net income was $51.8M (+23%), diluted EPS $0.65 vs $0.50, net margin ~15%. Adjusted EBITDA rose 19.5% to $139.9M; operating cash flow held against modest capex (~$25.5M/quarter). The current-earnings picture is fine; the forward algorithm is what deteriorated.

**The balance sheet is structurally unusual but not distressed.** Stockholders' equity is –$482.8M and accumulated deficit is –$1.107B. This is the deliberate output of a whole-business securitization plus aggressive buybacks, not operating weakness. The company carries ~$2.51B of securitized fixed-rate senior notes (2019/2022/2024/2025 series), bankruptcy-remote at the Master Issuer level and secured by substantially all U.S. revenue-generating assets, plus $75M undrawn variable funding notes. Liquidity is adequate: $375M cash, ~$195M marketable securities, $81M restricted cash, undrawn VFNs, and covenant compliance. Interest expense rose 25.8% YoY to $33.0M as the December 2025 refinancing raised both principal and blended coupon (new notes 5.27%–5.65% vs older sub-4% paper), a slow structural headwind as low-coupon 2019 notes roll off. Refinancing walls of ~$923M (2029) and ~$397M (2030) are manageable but rate-sensitive.

**Capital allocation timing was poor.** The company executed a $350M accelerated share repurchase in Dec'25/Jan'26 at a VWAP of $108.76 — near the all-time high, funded by a refinancing that raised interest costs — just before the stock halved. Share count fell from ~84.3M to ~79.1M. A fresh $500M program has $450M remaining. On a levered, negative-equity structure, buybacks amplify per-share outcomes in both directions; here the timing destroyed value.

**Two claims sit ahead of common holders.** A ~$415.8M Tax Receivable Agreement liability (85% of realized tax benefits paid to pre-IPO owners), with a change-of-control acceleration estimated at ~$330M, is both a cash claim and a takeover deterrent.

The financial-health picture nets to average: real cash generation and manageable liquidity, offset by rising interest cost, a negative-equity cushion, and rapid-amortization covenant triggers tied to system-wide sales and debt-service coverage that make a prolonged member stall a debt concern, not just an equity one. Financial health improved from prior reads (4.5 → 6.0) but does not warrant a top-tier score because the leverage removes the equity buffer precisely when demand is softening.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis reset. The same-club-sales deceleration reflects two simultaneous pressures: member sign-ups below internal expectations following a brand repositioning, and consumer resistance to Black Card price increases that forced a pause on pricing initiatives. Both legs of the classic same-store algorithm (new members plus price/mix) softened at once.

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration rather than a data blip. Over 90 days: current quarter -8.4%, next quarter -10.2%, current year -5.5%, next year -11.7%. The 30-day revisions have flattened (current year -0.1%, current quarter +0.6%), which suggests the worst of the cutting may be behind, but the trend remains down. Estimates are falling into a demand miss — the opposite of the +30% signal that would argue for aggressive re-rating.

The structural unit-growth pipeline (~750 clubs committed) provides a multi-year royalty tailwind independent of same-club trends, and the value proposition is defensive in a consumer downturn — a $15 membership is among the last discretionary items households cut. But the company's own franchisee financing support (SOFR+4% to +5.5% promissory notes, up to ~$30M committed) is a stress indicator in a model that depends on franchisees self-funding development. Growth prospects score below neutral: the reported top line overstates momentum, estimates are falling, and the re-acceleration path (unpausing pricing, brand-repositioning payoff) is unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$53 and consensus FY26 EPS of $3.20, PLNT trades at ~19x forward earnings, down sharply from the multiple it commanded near the highs. The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited use — OSW at 33x, PRKS at 16.6x, FUN with negative earnings — so the median P/E of 33.1 is not a clean benchmark. Against its own history and its high-margin, recurring-revenue franchise model, ~19x forward is not demanding.

The valuation is not a clear bargain, though, and the calibration lesson that cheapness sorts the wrong way without a catalyst is directly relevant here. The forward multiple compressed because forward estimates are falling — a low multiple on declining estimates is a value trap unless members re-accelerate. Analyst targets span a wide $45–$133 (mean $66.5, median $60.5), and the $133 high (and reported unconfirmed $175 Jefferies target) reflect the pre-reset franchise-quality thesis; the $45 low prices continued deceleration. There is a plausible mispricing case — the market may be extrapolating one weak quarter of an execution-and-pricing miss into secular GLP-1 substitution — but there is no dated catalyst to force that revaluation, and estimates would need to stop falling first. On balance the valuation is fair rather than asymmetric, warranting a neutral score.

## Competitive Landscape

Planet Fitness retains the dominant position in the value gym niche, with scale advantages in national advertising and franchisee-funded expansion that budget peers cannot match. The competitive trajectory, however, is no longer clearly improving. The 10-K flags the specific squeeze that materialized: "we may face increased competition if we increase our price," and Black Card pushback confirmed it. The company also notes rising demand for digital member-management and content, requiring incremental spend to stay competitive against boutique, digital, and home-fitness alternatives.

The overarching question is secular demand durability in the GLP-1 era. The bull view holds that affordable gyms complement weight-loss drugs (users want to preserve muscle and tone); the bear view is substitution. The Q1'26 deceleration is the first data point the market is reading through that lens, likely prematurely — management attributes the miss to brand-repositioning execution and pricing, not drug substitution. The competitive position remains strong in level but its trajectory has flattened, so this scores at neutral rather than the higher marks warranted a year ago (down from 7.0 to 5.0 in prior reads).

## Risk Assessment

**Demand/member-growth reset (acute).** The SSS deceleration and paused pricing are the crux. If sign-ups do not re-accelerate, the multiple stays compressed and the debt covenants (rapid-amortization triggers tied to system-wide sales) become a live concern.

**GLP-1 secular overhang.** The 10-K now explicitly names weight-loss medications as a demand risk. Hard to quantify, and it caps the multiple the market will pay regardless of near-term execution.

**Leverage and rising rates.** The securitized debt against negative equity, refinanced at higher coupons, is already compressing the interest line. A rapid-amortization event would choke cash to the parent.

**Litigation.** The securities class actions are not yet quantified but an overhang.

**Management instability.** CFO seat turned over (interim CFO Fitzgerald signed the Q1'26 10-Q; permanent CFO Priyadarshi hired mid-2026), executing a brand repositioning during finance-suite flux. A related-party board arrangement (a director-franchisee holding ~10.5% of a compliance-software vendor to PF clubs) is small-dollar but worth flagging.

**Consumer sensitivity.** The value proposition is defensive in a downturn, but the low-income core is exposed to inflation and any dues increase risks accelerating attrition — the exact dynamic that just played out with Black Card.

Given the stack of live risks — a demand reset not yet resolved, an unquantified securities suit, a covenant-triggering leverage structure, and a secular GLP-1 question — risk factors score below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 63.1% is rich versus 49.9% historical vol (IV percentile 88%), and the term structure is in backwardation (63.1% near vs 54.7% long-dated), consistent with the next earnings print testing whether the SSS deceleration continues. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±14.0%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-0.7% near-term, steepening to -4.5% at six months), and put/call volume (0.19) and OI (0.07) ratios are low, so positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the setup. IV rose 7.3pp since mid-June alongside a 6.3% price gain, indicating the market is bracing for a catalyst rather than fading the recovery. For a prospective buyer, the rich IV argues against paying up for optionality now and favors patience into the vol crush after the next report.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The franchise engine is intact and capital-light: FY25 adjusted EBITDA ~$551.6M compounding, ~70% franchise segment margins, ~$147.5M quarterly operating cash flow, and a ~750-club development pipeline that generates royalty growth independent of same-club trends. The Q1'26 miss is execution-and-pricing (fixable) rather than secular substitution. Insiders bought the dip — CEO at $49.54, a new director at $46.21. At ~19x forward on a durable, high-margin model, a re-acceleration in members or an unpause of pricing re-rates the stock toward the low-to-mid-$60s consensus and potentially higher. Bull target ~$85 [unverified — actual: N/A].

**Bear case.** The demand reset is the leading edge of GLP-1 substitution and a maturing budget-gym category, and the pricing pause caps the mix lever indefinitely. Estimates keep falling (next-year already -11.7% over 90 days), the negative-equity/levered balance sheet offers no cushion and rising interest cost compounds, and the securities litigation and management churn add friction during the wrong window. The $350M buyback at $108.76 signals capital-allocation misjudgment. Continued sub-4% SSS drags the multiple toward the $45 analyst low. Bear target ~$45 (-15% from ~$53).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report is the decisive checkpoint — same-club-sales trajectory, sign-up trends, and any commentary on restarting Black Card pricing. The Sept 14, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline and subsequent litigation developments are secondary monitors.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- System-wide same-club sales re-accelerate above ~+5%, or management credibly restarts Black Card pricing without attrition spiking.
- Estimate revisions turn positive across two consecutive quarters (reversing the current -5.5% CY / -11.7% NY trend).
- A pullback toward the low-$40s with fundamentals intact, which would restore clear asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-club sales decelerate further toward or below +2%, confirming the GLP-1/substitution bear case rather than an execution miss.
- Evidence of a rapid-amortization covenant approaching, or a franchisee-health deterioration (rising promissory-note support, unit-closure acceleration).
- A material adverse development in the securities class action, or continued C-suite turnover.

**Base-case reference:** ~$60 (roughly consensus median), reflecting a stabilized ~+3–4% SSS at ~18–19x a modestly rebased forward EPS — call it ~13% above the current ~$53, insufficient asymmetry to warrant a BUY given the falling-estimate backdrop.

## Conclusion

Planet Fitness is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise business whose stock halved on a real but likely fixable demand reset, layered with a genuine but unquantifiable GLP-1 secular overhang. The score trajectory (5.4 → 6.0 → 5.7 → 5.5) captures the tension: financial health improved as the market understood the negative equity is a securitization artifact rather than distress, while competitive position and growth prospects declined as the same-club deceleration and paused pricing revealed the growth algorithm under pressure. New data does not resolve the debate in either direction — estimates are still falling (a headwind), but 30-day revisions have flattened and insiders bought near the lows (a support).

At ~19x forward the valuation is fair but not asymmetric, and there is no dated catalyst to force a revaluation before the next same-club-sales print, which options positioning suggests will be volatile (±14% implied). This is our fourth consecutive HOLD; the bar for continuing it is met because the risk/reward is two-sided and the key uncertainty — whether members re-accelerate — is not yet observable. We rate PLNT **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale, and would want to see either a same-club-sales re-acceleration or a pullback into the low-$40s before turning constructive.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: forward P/E was corrected from ~17x to ~19x (Phase 1 forward P/E 19.49 on EPS basis; ~$53 / $3.20 CY est ≈ 16.6x, but the vendor forward multiple is ~19x), making the "not demanding" valuation modestly less attractive than stated.
