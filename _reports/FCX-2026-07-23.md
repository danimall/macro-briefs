**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Freeport-McMoRan is one of the world's largest copper producers, with a differentiated asset base spanning U.S. mines, South America, the Grasberg copper-gold district in Indonesia, and a growing downstream smelting/refining footprint. The investment case rests on two pillars: structural copper demand from electrification, grid buildout, and data-center power, against a supply-constrained new-mine environment; and a uniquely strong U.S. position (~70% of domestic refined copper) that lets FCX capture COMEX/tariff premiums that peers cannot. Both pillars remain intact. Gold has become a large secondary earnings lever — at ~$4,889/oz realized, by-product credits drove PTFI to negative unit cash costs of –$3.53/lb in Q1 2026.

The problem is timing and price. The Grasberg Block Cave mine, suspended after the September 2025 mud rush, faces more than a delay: the restart revealed a higher ratio of "wet drawpoints" requiring physical chute modifications, capping Blocks 2 & 3 at ~60% of capacity until roughly mid-2027 and pushing full-capacity restoration to end-2027. Management cut 2026 copper sales guidance to ~3.08B lbs (from 3.4B) and now describes forecasts as "inherently more variable." Consensus EPS for the current year fell ~7.6% over 90 days and the next-quarter estimate dropped ~21.6%. The stock trades at ~34x trailing and ~23x forward EPS on the recovering estimate, meaning the market is already paying for the volume normalization that carries execution risk over the next 18 months.

We rate FCX a HOLD (growth_hold). This continues a HOLD run and reflects a declining score trajectory (6.4 in April to 5.6 now). The declines in our growth and management dimensions are grounded in specific fundamental developments — the Grasberg ramp deferral and the Indonesia ownership step-down — not price action. The stock is a reasonable core holding for investors who want copper-supercycle and gold optionality, but the current entry does not offer the asymmetric upside required for a fresh BUY. We would upgrade on evidence the ramp is running ahead of the revised schedule, or on a pullback toward the low-$50s where the recovery torque is less fully priced.

## Company Overview

FCX operates four mining divisions plus downstream processing:

- **U.S. copper (7 operations):** Morenci (72%-owned), Bagdad, Safford/Lone Star, Sierrita, Miami, Chino, and Tyrone. Lower ore grades offset by leaching/technology programs; long reserve lives (Bagdad >80 years).
- **South America:** Cerro Verde, Peru (55.08%) and El Abra, Chile (51%).
- **Indonesia:** PT Freeport Indonesia (48.76%-owned, FCX-operated) runs the Grasberg district, one of the world's largest copper-gold deposits and, at normal rates, among the lowest-cost operations globally.
- **Molybdenum:** Climax and Henderson (Colorado), plus by-product moly.
- **Downstream:** Atlantic Copper (Spain), U.S. rod/refining, and the newly commissioned PTFI smelter + precious-metals refinery in Indonesia, making PTFI a fully integrated refined copper and gold producer as of 2025.

The core competitive advantage is scale plus the irreplaceable Grasberg orebody, whose gold by-product credits deliver negative cash costs, layered over a U.S. footprint that supplies ~70% of domestic refined copper. CEO Kathleen Quirk leads following the succession from Richard Adkerson, who remains Chairman.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (most recent reported):**
- Revenue **$6.23B**, up 9% YoY from $5.73B — driven entirely by price, since Grasberg volumes collapsed.
- Net income to common **$881M** ($0.61 diluted EPS), up from $352M ($0.24), though roughly $0.04 was a net credit tied to the Grasberg insurance settlement.
- Operating cash flow **$1.5B**; capex **$1.0B**.
- Realized prices: copper +30% YoY ($5.78/lb), gold +59% ($4,889/oz), moly +16% ($25.21/lb).

The volume damage is severe. Indonesia copper sales fell to 82M lbs (from 290M) and gold to 116k oz. Grasberg Block Cave extraction dropped to 4,400 t/day from 93,600 t/day. Consolidated copper production fell to 662M lbs from 868M. The Q1 revenue increase existed only because price gains more than offset the volume loss, a favorable but reversible mix.

**Balance sheet (Mar 31, 2026):**
- Total debt $9.4B (5.2% weighted average rate, ~8-year duration); cash $3.7B.
- Net debt of $2.4B excluding $3.2B of PTFI downstream-processing project debt — inside the $3–4B net-debt target.
- No senior note maturities in 2026; $1.3B in 2027. $3.0B undrawn FCX revolver.
- The Altman Z-Score near ~1.8 reflects a capital-intensive miner with $41B PP&E and $12B noncontrolling interests, not a distress signal. Liquidity and coverage are strong. The May 20, 2026 financing activity (8-K Items 1.01/1.02/2.03) is consistent with a bond issuance and/or revolver amendment ahead of the 2027 maturity.

**Capital allocation:** Base + variable dividend ($0.15/qtr); $2.9B remaining on a $5.0B buyback, of which only $93M was repurchased in Q1 (at $54.25). The framework directs up to 50% of post-capex, post-NCI free cash flow to shareholders, subject to the net-debt cap. Management's measured buyback pace during Grasberg uncertainty is prudent capital preservation rather than stock defense.

**2026 guidance:** ~3.08B lbs copper / 650k oz gold sales (cut from 3.4B lbs / 0.8M oz); operating cash flow ~$8.7B (at $6.00 copper, $4,500 gold, $25 moly); capex $4.3B. Idle-facility/restoration charges of ~$1.3B flow through as non-inventoriable costs and should be normalized out of headline comparisons.

Earnings quality is solid but the beat streak requires context: the company has beaten consensus in four consecutive quarters (Q1 2026 by 23.1%, Q4 2025 by 62.0%), but recent beats partly reflect insurance timing (the $0.7B gain collected April 2026) and price, not operational normalization.

## Growth Analysis

The demand thesis is genuine and broad-based. Copper touched all-time highs (~$6.28/lb LME) in January 2026 on the structural deficit narrative — electrification, grid investment, data centers/AI — against constrained new supply. This is a sector-wide repricing across the complex, not a single-company story.

The company-specific growth path has been pushed to the right. The revised Grasberg ramp is ~65% of capacity in H2 2026 (from 85% prior), ~80% by mid-2027, and full by end-2027. This is management's own schedule, and it carries an unusual technical risk: the wet-drawpoint problem is a changed ore-handling environment requiring physical chute modifications, not a simple restart delay. The recovery torque is real (each incremental Grasberg pound comes with high-value gold credits at current prices), but it is delayed and less certain than a standard mine restart.

Estimate revisions capture the tension. Over 90 days, current-quarter estimates fell 8.2%, next-quarter fell 21.6%, and current-year fell 7.6%, the ramp deferral hitting the model. Over 30 days, estimates have edged back up (current quarter +5.1%, current year +2.1%) as copper firmed. Next-year estimates are essentially flat (+0.2% over 90 days, +3.1% over 30 days). This is a re-rate downward on volume followed by stabilization on price, not the +30% revision pattern that signals systematic under-estimation. We do not apply the inflection-BUY signal here — the near-term revision trend is net negative over the meaningful window.

The longer-dated growth pipeline is attractive and mostly brownfield: El Abra (+700M lbs/yr potential, EIS submitted March 2026), Bagdad doubling (~$3.5B, +200–250M lbs/yr, potential H2 2026 decision), Kucing Liar reserves expanded ~20% to ~8B lbs Cu / 8M oz Au (first production ~2030), and ~300M lbs/yr targeted from leaching innovation. These are real but multi-year options that do not change the 12-month picture.

## Valuation Assessment

At $65.00 with $2.83 current-year and $4.05 next-year consensus EPS, FCX trades at ~23x current-year and ~16x next-year earnings, versus ~34x trailing on depressed $1.52 TTM EPS. The trailing multiple overstates expensiveness because trailing earnings absorb the Grasberg volume collapse; forward metrics are the correct lens. On EV/EBITDA, one aggregator estimate puts FCX at ~7.6x, a discount to Southern Copper, which per the same secondary framing trades at ~39x forward P/E — the standard bull framing that FCX offers similar copper leverage plus gold credits and U.S. tariff exposure at a lower multiple. (These peer multiples come from a single tier-3 source and should be treated as directional.)

The valuation is not stretched, but it is not cheap enough to compel a fresh purchase. The ~16x next-year multiple prices in a substantial portion of the Grasberg recovery, which per management is "inherently more variable" and not complete until end-2027. Analyst targets (mean $71.11, median $74.50, high $82, low $30) bracket the stock modestly above spot, with the wide low end reflecting the commodity and execution tail. The consensus mix is constructive (6 strong buy, 13 buy, 3 hold, 1 sell) and stable.

We score valuation a 5.0. The equity-lessons framework treats a "cheap" print as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, and the material caution here is that the apparent forward discount rests on volume assumptions with genuine execution risk over the next 18 months, plus a structural ownership dilution in 2042. This is a fair-value setup, not asymmetric upside. Cycle-adjusting for a normalized Grasberg at current copper/gold prices would justify a higher fair value, but that normalization is exactly what carries the risk. We do not treat prior appreciation (the stock is up ~42% over twelve months) as a negative; the caution is forward risk versus current price, not where the stock has been.

## Competitive Landscape

FCX's competitive position is strong and broadly stable, with the U.S. tariff dynamic a genuine and underappreciated tailwind. A 50% Section 232 tariff on semi-finished/derivative copper took effect August 2025, and a potential refined-copper tariff (15% in 2027 rising to 30% in 2028) is under review. Domestic-sale requirements (25% of cathode/concentrate in 2027, rising to 40% by 2029) are not expected to hurt FCX given it already sells most U.S. output domestically (76% as rod, 24% as cathode in Q1). The company is positioned to capture COMEX premiums that import-dependent peers cannot.

Against Southern Copper, FCX offers similar copper exposure at a lower multiple, plus Grasberg's gold by-product credits and unmatched U.S. domestic exposure. Growth optionality is brownfield/low-incentive-price, attractive versus greenfield-dependent competitors.

The trajectory has softened at the margin, which is why we hold this dimension at 6.5 rather than the 7.5 of early reports. The Grasberg disruption temporarily removes the lowest-cost, highest-torque asset from the production base, and the Indonesia post-2041 ownership step-down to ~37% (from 48.76%) structurally dilutes long-term claim on the crown jewel. Neither erodes the moat, but both trim the trajectory relative to prior expectations.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Grasberg ramp-up (dominant risk).** A standardized read sees "earnings beat / higher copper" and misses that the crown-jewel asset won't be fully restored for ~18 more months, with real technical execution risk. Management frames it as deferral, not resource loss, which is likely correct, but the timeline is contingent.

2. **Indonesia sovereign/IUPK risk.** PTFI operating rights expire 2041. The February 2026 MOU would extend life-of-resource but steps FCX ownership down to ~37% beginning 2042. The extension is still contingent on an amended IUPK and definitive agreements. On April 10, 2026 Indonesia issued PTFI tax assessments on 2022 audit exceptions (contested).

3. **Commodity leverage (double-edged).** ~$220M OCF per $0.10/lb copper; ~$50M per $100/oz gold. Earnings are highly geared; the recent copper pullback illustrates the downside.

4. **Input-cost inflation.** The late-February 2026 Middle East conflict drove volatile increases in diesel, sulfur/sulfuric acid, and consumables, lifting U.S. mine unit costs to ~$3.02/lb.

5. **FCPA matter — materially resolved.** The SEC declined enforcement on March 17, 2026, removing a tail risk. DOJ/foreign exposure is theoretically open but materially diminished.

We score risk factors 5.0. The Grasberg execution timeline and the not-yet-finalized Indonesia terms are specific, unresolved, asymmetric catalysts that are only partially priced. Commodity leverage is inherent to the sector and largely priced.

ATM IV jumped to 112.3% (from 64.9% a month ago) against 1-year historical vol of 50.9% — a 61.4pp rich spread, with 85th-percentile IV and a sharp backwardation (near-term 112.3% vs. 48.4% six-month). This configuration reflects positioning around the imminent Q2 print rather than a directional bearish signal; the call-heavy skew is mildly constructive, but the elevated IV argues against buying premium here and signals the market expects a large move on the earnings catalyst. The IV spike is a timing caution, not a thesis input.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($82+):** Copper's structural deficit holds, prices stay near recent highs, and Grasberg ramps at or ahead of the revised schedule. Gold near $4,900/oz makes each recovered Grasberg pound doubly valuable via negative cash costs. The U.S. tariff regime lets FCX capture premiums peers cannot, and the brownfield pipeline (Bagdad, El Abra, Kucing Liar) delivers low-cost growth. On normalized volumes and current prices, forward EPS re-rates well above $4.05 and the stock closes the discount to Southern Copper. Analyst high target $82.

**Bear case ($30–50):** Copper corrects on Chinese demand weakness or a global slowdown, gold retraces, and the Grasberg ramp slips again on the wet-drawpoint problem, the "inherently more variable" language proving prescient. Estimates get cut further, the 2042 Indonesia dilution weighs on long-term value, and input-cost inflation squeezes margins. Analyst low target $30 reflects this tail; a realistic bear is $45–50.

**Base case ($68–72):** Copper holds in a broad range, the Grasberg ramp proceeds roughly on the revised schedule, and forward earnings recover toward the $4.05 next-year consensus. The stock tracks the analyst mean of ~$71, modestly above spot, offering total return in the high-single digits plus the ~1% dividend, a fair but unexciting risk/reward at the current entry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly Grasberg ramp disclosures and the finalization (or not) of the Indonesia IUPK amendment.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence in Q3/Q4 2026 disclosures that Blocks 2 & 3 are running above the ~60%/~65% revised path, or that the wet-drawpoint chute modifications are resolving faster than mid-2027.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s, where the recovery torque is materially less priced and forward P/E compresses below ~13x next-year EPS.
- Finalized Indonesia definitive agreements removing the extension contingency without terms worse than the MOU.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further Grasberg ramp deferral or a widening of the wet-drawpoint problem beyond Blocks 2 & 3.
- Copper falling and holding below ~$4.50/lb, which would drop OCF meaningfully given the ~$220M-per-$0.10/lb sensitivity.
- Deterioration in the Indonesia negotiation (failure to secure IUPK amendment, or ownership terms worse than the ~37% step-down).

## Conclusion

FCX is a high-quality copper-gold producer with real structural tailwinds and a differentiated U.S. tariff advantage, but the near-term setup is balanced. The crown-jewel Grasberg asset will not run at full capacity until end-2027, the restart revealed a genuine technical complication, and the stock at ~16x next-year and ~23x current-year earnings already prices a substantial portion of the recovery. The declining score trajectory reflects these fundamental developments — the ramp deferral cutting our growth read, the ownership step-down and absence of open-market insider buying tempering management alignment — rather than price movement.

We rate FCX a **HOLD (growth_hold)**. There is a credible 12-month appreciation path if copper cooperates and the ramp proceeds, which is why we hold rather than avoid; there is not the asymmetric upside that would justify a fresh BUY at $65. Investors seeking copper-supercycle and gold optionality can hold a core position, but incremental capital is better deployed on a pullback toward the low-$50s or on confirmation the Grasberg ramp is beating its revised schedule.
