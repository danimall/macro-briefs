## Executive Summary

Goldman Sachs is operating at the highest profitability level in its recent history. EPS more than doubled from $22.87 in FY2023 to $51.32 in FY2025, ROE expanded from 7.5% to 15.0%, and the firm returned $16.8B to shareholders through buybacks and dividends. The decisive exit from consumer banking — which cumulatively cost the firm an estimated $5B+ in pre-tax losses — is nearly complete, leaving a cleaner, more focused institution built around the dominant Global Banking & Markets franchise and a growing Asset & Wealth Management platform with $3.6T in AUS.

The challenge is that the stock, at $935, already reflects much of this improvement. At 17.2x forward earnings and 2.6x tangible book, GS trades near the upper end of its historical valuation range. More importantly, the current macro regime — oil above $95, CPI likely breaching 4%, consumer confidence at record lows, and a 50-55% recession probability — poses direct risks to capital markets activity, which constitutes ~71% of GS revenues. Options markets are pricing elevated near-term stress (34.7% IV vs. 27.1% HV, backwardation in term structure), and the credit-equity divergence observed across broader markets suggests institutional caution about the durability of the capital markets cycle.

We maintain GS at HOLD with a growth orientation. The firm's competitive position and earnings trajectory are genuinely strong, and proposed G-SIB surcharge reforms could free several billion dollars of excess capital. But with cyclical sensitivity high, quarterly EPS estimates declining near-term, and geopolitical risk elevated, the risk/reward at current levels does not justify a BUY. We would upgrade at $820-850, where the valuation discount to tangible book and forward earnings provides a more adequate margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Goldman Sachs is a leading global financial institution with $1.81T in total assets and three operating segments:

**Global Banking & Markets (GBM)** — 71% of net revenues ($41.5B in FY2025). Includes investment banking advisory ($4.7B, +34% YoY), equity and debt underwriting, FICC ($14.5B, +9%), and Equities ($16.5B, +23%). The segment earned $17.6B in pre-tax income with a 16.4% ROE.

**Asset & Wealth Management (AWM)** — 29% of net revenues ($16.7B). Manages $3.6T in AUS with $420B in alternatives. Management fees of $11.5B (+11% YoY) and record $168B in long-term fee-based net inflows. Pre-tax income of $4.1B at 12.5% ROE, below management's 17-19% target.

**Platform Solutions** — 0.3% of revenues ($151M). Effectively being wound down. The Apple Card program is being transitioned to another issuer over ~24 months, with the GM Card already sold.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Trajectory

The 3-year financial transformation has been substantial:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Net Revenues | $46.3B | $53.5B | $58.3B |
| Diluted EPS | $22.87 | $40.54 | $51.32 |
| ROE | 7.5% | 12.7% | 15.0% |
| ROTE | 8.1% | 13.5% | 16.0% |

EPS growth was driven by three factors: (1) IB fee recovery (+21% to $9.3B in FY2025), (2) elimination of consumer banking losses (Platform Solutions swung from -$997M to +$151M pre-tax), and (3) aggressive buybacks that reduced diluted shares from 356M to 313M over two years.

The earnings beat record is strong. Over the last four quarters, GS beat consensus by an average of 9.8%, with the widest beat in Q4 2025 (+16.5%). Q1 2026 EPS of $17.55 beat the $16.99 estimate by 3.3% — a narrower beat, consistent with the capital markets environment becoming less favorable.

### Balance Sheet and Capital

Total assets of $1.81T represent 14.5x leverage on $125.0B of shareholders' equity. Book value stands at $357.60/share, tangible book at $335.49/share. The CET1 ratio declined from 15.0% to 14.3%, driven by $51.1B growth in standardized credit RWAs to $642.8B. Total standardized RWAs reached $727B.

Net interest income surged 68% to $13.6B, benefiting from the rate environment. Average daily VaR was $90M with zero exceptions in FY2025 — reflecting well-managed market risk exposure for a balance sheet of this size.

Unsecured long-term debt totals $285.5B with a weighted average maturity of ~6 years. This funding structure provides adequate liquidity runway but represents a meaningful cost of capital.

### Capital Return

The firm returned $16.8B to common shareholders in FY2025: $12.4B in buybacks and $4.4B in dividends. The dividend was raised 12.5% to $4.50/quarter ($18.00 annualized), yielding 1.9% at the current price. A $40B repurchase authorization with no expiration is in place. The dividend payout ratio fell from 45.9% (2023) to 27.3% (2025), reflecting earnings growth outpacing dividend increases and providing ample room for continued buyback acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term: Mixed Signals

Current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised down 3.0% over the past 30 days ($14.24 → $13.80), and next-quarter estimates declined 2.0%. This is consistent with a capital markets environment under pressure from geopolitical uncertainty, rising energy costs, and consumer weakness. April CPI likely exceeds 4.0%, and the Fed is firmly on hold at 3.64% — conditions that compress equity issuance volumes and elongate M&A timelines.

However, full-year FY2026 estimates are rising: +1.6% over 30 days to $59.26, with FY2027 at $65.37 (+0.5% over 30 days, +1.4% over 90 days). This divergence — near-term down, full-year up — reflects analyst expectations that capital markets activity will recover in H2 2026. The IB backlog was "significantly higher" at year-end 2025 vs. year-end 2024, driven by advisory and debt underwriting, providing visibility for a rebound.

### Medium-Term: AWM Scaling

The AWM segment is the multi-year growth engine. Management targets:
- Alternative AUS: $750B by 2030 (from $420B today)
- Credit alternatives: $300B by 2028
- AWM pre-tax margins: ~30% (from ~25%)
- AWM ROE: 17-19% (from 12.5%)

Progress toward these targets is measurable: alternative AUS grew 20% in FY2025, the firm raised $115B in third-party alternative commitments, and two acquisitions (Industry Ventures at $665M, Innovator Capital Management at ~$2.0B) expand the alternatives platform. There is also $5.24B in unrecognized incentive fees (up from $3.77B two years ago) that will flow into revenue as fund vintages crystallize.

The T. Rowe Price strategic partnership (up to $1B equity investment) targets retirement and wealth market solutions — a channel that could accelerate AUS growth beyond organic fundraising.

### Regulatory Tailwind

Proposed March 2026 capital reforms from federal bank regulators would reduce CET1 requirements for Category I/II banks by an estimated 4.8%, replace the dual risk-based capital framework with a single "expanded risk-based" ratio, and index G-SIB surcharge coefficients to GDP with a 1.2x reduction factor. For Goldman, which faces a rising G-SIB surcharge trajectory (3.0% → 3.5% → potentially 4.0% by 2028), this reform could free several billion dollars of excess capital for deployment or shareholder return. This remains a proposal and is subject to political and rulemaking timelines, but the directional intent from regulators is unambiguous.

Further multiple expansion requires sustained execution above 15% ROE, which is uncertain in a deteriorating macro environment.

The consensus mean target of $933 is essentially flat with the current price. The median of $960 offers 3% upside. The low target of $729 implies 22% downside; the high of $1,050 implies 12% upside. This is a tight consensus range for a stock with this level of cyclicality.

## Competitive Landscape

Goldman maintains a dominant competitive position in its core businesses:

- **Advisory**: $1.6T in announced M&A volume in 2025, +67% YoY. Consistently ranked #1 or #2 globally.
- **Equities**: $16.5B in revenues (+23%). The prime brokerage and derivatives franchise benefits from structural demand for financing and hedging, particularly during volatile periods. Equities financing revenues of $7.2B (+31%) reflect growing prime brokerage balances.
- **FICC**: $14.5B (+9%). Diversified across rates, credit, commodities, and FX. Commodities exposure provides a structural hedge against the energy-driven inflation regime.
- **Alternatives**: $420B in AUS, growing at ~20% annually. The integrated platform (origination + distribution + balance sheet) differentiates GS from pure-play alternative managers like Blackstone and Apollo, though the fee rate is under modest pressure (58 bps vs. 62 bps in 2023).

The primary competitive risk is cyclical: during downturns, GBM revenues compress 20-40% from peak levels, and the firm lacks the deposit-funded lending income base that helps JPMorgan maintain earnings stability. The AWM scaling strategy is explicitly designed to build a larger, more durable fee base that reduces this cyclicality over time, but it remains a multi-year work-in-progress.

## Risk Assessment

### Macro/Cyclical Risk (High)

Goldman's revenue composition is heavily leveraged to capital markets activity. With recession probability at 50-55%, consumer confidence at record lows, oil above $95, and inflation likely exceeding 4%, the conditions for sustained IB fee growth are deteriorating. M&A pipelines tend to pause during periods of elevated policy uncertainty, and the current geopolitical regime — ceasefire near expiration in the Iran conflict, tariff escalation — creates precisely this type of uncertainty. The 3.0% decline in current-quarter EPS estimates over the past 30 days reflects this near-term pressure.

### G-SIB Surcharge / Capital Constraint

The firm is now in the 4.0% G-SIB surcharge threshold range based on FY2025 data, up from 3.5% for 2026 and 3.0% for 2025. Without regulatory reform, this escalation trajectory constrains the firm's ability to grow the balance sheet and return capital simultaneously. The March 2026 reform proposals are a significant positive catalyst if adopted but remain uncertain. This represents a binary risk/reward: reform adoption disproportionately benefits GS; failure to adopt constrains the firm's capital return and growth ambitions.

### Credit Risk

Commercial real estate nonaccrual loans rose from $800M to $1.08B. Real estate loans in GBM grew from $38.4B to $49.8B. In an environment of rising rates and potential recession, credit losses could accelerate. The $20.3B in Level 3 assets (1.1% of total assets) adds valuation uncertainty, though Level 3 derivative losses of $1.7B in FY2025 were manageable relative to the firm's earnings power.

### Apple Card Wind-Down

The ~24-month transition timeline introduces execution risk. The firm still holds ~$20B of credit card loans classified as held-for-sale plus $71B of cancellable credit card lines. While the $2.26B markdown and $2.48B reserve release largely cleared the financial impact, timing delays or disputes with Apple could generate incremental losses.

### Options Market Signal

Options on GS are pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 34.7% sits 7.6pp above the 27.1% 1-year realized volatility, placing IV at the 68th percentile of its range. The term structure is in backwardation (34.7% near-term vs. 29.2% at 6-month), indicating the market expects stress to concentrate in the immediate future. Put/call skew of 4.6% on the nearest expiry reflects elevated downside hedging demand, though the OI put/call ratio of 0.79 suggests this is hedging activity rather than directional positioning. The 1-month tenor shows a 3.28 put/call volume ratio — abnormally elevated, likely reflecting institutional portfolio protection around the ceasefire expiration and macro deterioration. This options profile confirms the thesis that near-term downside risk is elevated, supporting the decision to wait for a more attractive entry point rather than buy at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($1,050-$1,100)

IB backlog converts at or above historical rates through H2 2026. G-SIB surcharge reform is adopted, freeing $3-5B of excess capital for accelerated buybacks. AWM scaling proceeds on-target with $450B+ alternatives AUS by year-end 2026. Geopolitical de-escalation restores capital markets activity. Equities franchise continues to benefit from elevated vol and client hedging demand. FY2026 EPS reaches $62-65, and the stock re-rates to 17-18x forward on improved ROE visibility. Implies ~$1,080 at 17x $63.50 FY2026E.

### Bear Case ($720-$780)

Recession materializes in H1 2026. IB revenues decline 25-30% from FY2025 levels as M&A and issuance pipelines freeze. Credit losses escalate in commercial real estate. G-SIB surcharge reform is delayed or diluted. AWM suffers net outflows from alternative funds as institutional allocators pull back. FY2026 EPS falls to $45-48. Stock de-rates to 15-16x on reduced confidence in the earnings recovery. Implies ~$720 at 15x $48.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers** (HOLD → BUY):
1. Price declines to $820-850 (2.45-2.53x tangible book), providing adequate margin of safety for cyclical risk
2. G-SIB surcharge reform adopted in substantially current form (creates $3-5B capital release catalyst)
3. Two consecutive quarters of IB fee growth ≥10% YoY, confirming cycle durability despite macro headwinds

**Downgrade triggers** (HOLD → AVOID):
1. FY2026 EPS estimates decline below $52 (below trailing year), indicating earnings peak has passed
2. CET1 ratio falls below 13.5%, constraining capital return capacity
3. AWM experiences net outflows for two consecutive quarters, undermining the fee-based growth thesis

**Reassessment timeline**: Q2 2026 earnings (expected mid-July 2026). This will provide the first full-quarter read on IB pipeline conversion under the current macro stress conditions and the first update on G-SIB reform progress following the comment period.

## Conclusion

Goldman Sachs has executed one of the more successful strategic pivots in recent banking history — exiting consumer banking, restoring ROE from 7.5% to 15.0%, and building a $3.6T AUS platform with growing fee-based revenues. The competitive franchise in investment banking and markets is the strongest in a generation, with a record IB backlog and equities revenues at all-time highs.

The concern is timing and price. At 17.2x trailing earnings and 2.6x tangible book, the stock prices in much of the improvement. The macro environment is turning hostile for capital markets activity — oil above $95, inflation likely exceeding 4%, recession probability above 50%, and geopolitical uncertainty elevated. Options markets are pricing near-term stress (backwardation, elevated IV, high put skew), and near-term EPS estimates are declining.

The firm warrants a growth-oriented HOLD. The fundamental trajectory is strong and the AWM scaling story is credible, but the cyclical risk embedded in a $287B market cap financial institution at the late stage of a capital markets recovery cycle argues against paying full valuation today. We would become more constructive at $820-850 or upon adoption of G-SIB surcharge reform.
