## Executive Summary

MGM Resorts trades at $37.41 with a trailing P/E of 53x on $0.76 in trailing EPS, reflecting the collapse of GAAP earnings from $1.14B (2023) to $206M (2025) despite stable Adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.4B. The stock sits 9% below its 52-week high of $40.94, carried there by a rally from late-November lows near $30 that accompanied Barry Diller's IAC accumulating a ~24% stake. The fundamental picture has deteriorated: EPS estimates have been revised down 19-53% over the past 90 days across all forecast periods, the most recent quarter missed by 9%, and the quarter before that missed by 40%.

The core problem is structural: MGM's sale-leaseback model creates $1.8B/year in fixed rent obligations plus $350M in interest expense, meaning $2.2B in fixed charges must be covered before a dollar reaches shareholders. With Las Vegas Strip revenue declining 4% in 2025 and visitor volumes down 8%, this leverage works in reverse. The company generates $1.5B in FCF today, but the margin of safety between operational cash generation and fixed obligations is narrower than the market appreciates, particularly in a consumer downturn environment with gasoline above $5/gallon and savings rates at 3.6%.

The 16.8% short interest and options market pricing (50% near-term IV vs. 36% realized, backwardation structure) confirm that sophisticated participants share concerns about near-term downside. While IAC's strategic involvement and aggressive buybacks ($1.2B in 2025, 33% share reduction since 2022) provide downside support, they do not constitute a sufficient growth catalyst to justify holding at current valuations given the deteriorating earnings trajectory.

## Company Overview

MGM Resorts operates 13 Las Vegas Strip properties (Bellagio, MGM Grand, Aria, Cosmopolitan, Mandalay Bay), regional U.S. casinos (Borgata, National Harbor), two Macau properties under concession through 2032, and a digital segment including the 50%-owned BetMGM joint venture and LeoVegas platform. Revenue mix: Las Vegas Strip 48%, Regional 22%, MGM China 25%, Digital 4%.

The company operates an asset-light structure, having sold its real estate to VICI Properties and leasing it back under triple-net leases. This freed capital for buybacks and growth investments but created a permanently levered operating structure with minimal balance sheet flexibility.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $17.5B in 2025 (+2% YoY), with Q1 2026 posting a record $4.45B (+4.2%). Growth is bifurcated: Macau (+11%) and Digital (+19%) growing while Las Vegas Strip declined 4% in 2025 before showing early recovery signs in Q1 2026.

**Profitability Collapse:** Operating income fell from $1.89B (2023) → $1.49B (2024) → $1.00B (2025). Net income: $1.14B → $747M → $206M. Key drivers: $279M goodwill impairment (Empire City), $288M FX losses, $186M increase in depreciation. Adjusted EBITDA remains stable at $2.43B, but GAAP margins compressed from 11.7% to 5.7%.

**Balance Sheet:** Altman Z-Score of 0.65 (deep distress), Debt/Equity of 15.68x. Total debt $6.3B, equity only $2.4B supporting $41.4B in assets. Fixed obligations: ~$1.8B/year rent + ~$350M/year interest = $2.15B before any operating expenses. Cash position: $2.1B, current ratio 1.23x.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow $2.53B, free cash flow $1.46B (8.3% margin). FCF yield on market cap is approximately 15% — optically attractive but misleading given the debt load and lease obligations. On an enterprise basis including capitalized leases, the yield is far lower.

**Share Buybacks:** Shares reduced 33% from 413M to 277M since 2022. $1.6B remains on the current authorization. This is the primary shareholder value creation mechanism currently operating.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects have materially weakened over the past 90 days:

- Current quarter EPS estimates revised down 28.7% (from $0.89 to $0.63)
- Next quarter estimates revised down 52.5% (from $0.55 to $0.26)
- Current year estimates revised down 18.7% (from $2.53 to $2.06)
- Next year estimates revised down 11.6% (from $2.61 to $2.31)

The market is systematically reducing its expectations for MGM's earnings power across all time horizons.

**Las Vegas:** The 8% visitor volume decline in 2025 (Canadian visitors -17.4%, driven by geopolitical boycott) is the primary concern. Q1 2026 shows early recovery with convention business driving improvement, but continued consumer weakness suggests the recovery may stall.

**Macau:** Growing at 11% with improving win rates (25.5%), this is the brightest segment. However, it carries concession risk (government can redeem from 2030), FX exposure, and China policy uncertainty.

**BetMGM:** Revenue guidance cut to $2.9-3.1B from $3.1-3.2B. Active users fell 9% YoY. Growth decelerated from 68% to 4% in one year. The venture is now profitable and distributing cash ($135M to MGM in 2025), but the growth narrative has collapsed.

**Japan (Osaka):** Potentially transformative ($800M-$1B EBITDA potential) but won't open until ~2030. Remaining commitment: $2.3B. Construction and FX risk are substantial. This is a distant option with significant near-term capital drain.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.41, MGM trades at:
- 53x trailing P/E (reflecting GAAP earnings collapse)
- ~18x forward P/E on $2.06 current-year estimates
- ~16x forward P/E on $2.31 next-year estimates
- ~6.5x EV/Adjusted EBITDA (on ~$16B enterprise value including debt)
- ~6.5x FCF yield on market cap

Versus peer WYNN at 27.8x P/E, MGM appears expensive on trailing earnings but cheaper on forward estimates. The problem: forward estimates have been revised down 19-53% over 90 days, meaning the "cheap on forward" narrative relies on estimates that are still declining. The low trailing P/E of the peer median (27.8x) compared to MGM's 53x reflects MGM's specific earnings deterioration, not sector cheapness.

The 6.5x EV/EBITDA is reasonable for gaming, but this metric obscures the capital structure risk. At 15.68x D/E with no dividend and declining earnings, the equity residual is highly sensitive to operational performance. A 15% revenue decline would consume most FCF after fixed obligations — this non-linear risk is not captured in simple multiples.

**IAC's purchases at $36-37** provide a valuation floor of sorts, but IAC is a strategic buyer with a multi-year horizon and restructuring capabilities unavailable to passive equity holders.

## Competitive Landscape

MGM holds strong competitive positioning in Las Vegas (largest operator on the Strip by property count) and a meaningful Macau position. However, competitive headwinds are mounting:

- Three new integrated resorts licensed in New York (directly impacting Borgata and Empire City catchment)
- Florida/Seminole tribal gaming expansion
- Prediction markets (Kalshi) emerging as threat to BetMGM
- All-inclusive pricing trend across Las Vegas suggests industry-wide margin pressure

The company's competitive moat in Las Vegas is real (prime Strip locations are irreplaceable assets, even if leased), but the conversion of that positioning into earnings growth is impaired by the fixed-cost structure and consumer spending environment.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Consumer spending downturn (HIGH probability, HIGH impact).** With savings rates at low levels and gasoline prices elevated, discretionary travel and gaming spending face sustained headwinds. The 8% visitor decline in 2025 may not recover if the consumer environment deteriorates further through H2 2026.

2. **Fixed obligation coverage risk (MODERATE probability, EXTREME impact).** The $2.15B/year in fixed charges against $2.53B operating cash flow leaves a thin buffer. A recession reducing operating cash flow by 25% would breach the coverage threshold. The Z-Score of 0.65 correctly identifies this structural fragility.

3. **Estimate revision momentum (ACTIVE, confirmed).** All four periods show 90-day downward revisions of 12-53%. Two of the last four quarters missed estimates.

4. **Japan capital drain.** $2.3B remaining commitment to a project that won't generate cash until 2030+. In an environment where core U.S. operations face headwinds, this represents misallocated capital.

5. **Macau political risk.** The concession can be redeemed from 2030 without compensation. This creates an approaching binary risk for 25% of revenue.

### Options Market Signal

The P/C OI ratio moved from 0.57 to 1.05 over three weeks while the stock fell 6%, indicating put accumulation. The 1-week expiry at 50.1% IV prices a ±3.1% move ($36.25-$38.57 range). The backwardation structure (50.1% near-term vs. 38.5% longer-term) specifically prices imminent stress rather than structural long-term uncertainty. This is consistent with the elevated short interest (16.8%, 4.6 days to cover) and confirms institutional bearish positioning.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Consumer earnings releases likely confirm spending weakness, pressuring all Consumer Discretionary including gaming. More directly: Q2 2026 earnings (July/August timeframe) where the current-quarter estimate of $0.63 faces the same dynamics that produced the -9% and -40% misses in recent quarters. Las Vegas summer season revenue data through July will be the key fundamental catalyst.

**Timing window:** 2-4 months. The most actionable window is between now and Q2 earnings. The consumer weakness thesis should manifest in Las Vegas visitation data and gaming revenue reports (monthly Nevada gaming revenue data, published with ~45 day lag).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 16.8% SI with 4.6 days to cover is elevated but not extreme squeeze territory. IAC's ~24% stake and active buying creates an offsetting demand floor that limits downside velocity. Squeeze risk is moderate — a sudden positive catalyst (IAC bid, oil decline driving consumer relief) could force rapid covering.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options confirm bearish positioning directionally. Borrow should be available given 256M share float.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case target $28-30 represents 20-25% downside. Upside risk to $42-44 on IAC activity or consumer recovery = 13-19% upside. Risk/reward approximately 1.3-1.9:1 — below the 2:1 threshold for active shorting given the IAC floor risk.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is directionally correct (deteriorating earnings, consumer headwinds, leverage risk), but IAC's ~24% stake and active buying creates a floor that limits downside. The risk/reward is insufficient for an active short given squeeze potential from a strategic holder who has demonstrated willingness to buy at current levels. Avoid owning, but don't short against a strategic acquirer.

## Conclusion

MGM Resorts presents a fundamentally challenged picture at current prices: declining GAAP earnings, negative estimate revision momentum across all periods, extreme financial leverage, and exposure to a deteriorating consumer spending cycle. The stock's 53x trailing P/E and ~18x forward P/E (on estimates still being revised downward) do not offer adequate compensation for the structural risks.

The IAC/Diller stake provides optionality and a soft floor, and the aggressive buyback program has been genuinely accretive, but these are insufficient to offset the fundamental headwinds.

This is a Consumer Discretionary name with extreme leverage in an environment where multiple independent signals confirm consumer spending weakness. The appropriate position is to avoid ownership and reassess after fundamental evidence of an earnings trough emerges.
