**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Liberty Media has completed a multi-year simplification into a pure-play global motorsports company. Following the December 15, 2025 Liberty Live Split-Off, FWONK is no longer a tracking stock; it is the sole common stock of Liberty Media Corporation, and its asset base is two crown-jewel franchises: Formula 1 (100% owned through Delta Topco) and MotoGP (~84% owned, acquired July 3, 2025 for ~$3.66B). Both are effectively global monopolies on premier-tier motorsport, monetized through media rights, race promotion fees, and sponsorship, with a "significant majority" of contracts paid in advance with annual escalators. The result is unusual revenue visibility: roughly $2.87B of contracted-but-undelivered performance obligations remain for 2026, $3.44B for 2027, $3.0B for 2028, and ~$7.0B across 2029–2033.

The operating business is growing. Q1 2026 revenue was $711M (+59% YoY), F1 segment revenue rose 53% to $617M with F1 Adjusted OIBDA doubling to $172M, and consolidated Adjusted OIBDA reached $181M (+148%). The headline multi-year "revenue collapse" from ~$12.5B (2023) to ~$3.65B (2024) is an artifact of serial split-offs (SiriusXM, Atlanta Braves, Liberty Live), not operating deterioration. The single most important forward catalyst, the 5-year exclusive Apple TV US streaming deal that replaced ESPN beginning in 2026, is tracking ahead of expectations on early viewership with no meaningful fan backlash.

We rate FWONK HOLD with a positive 12-month growth bias. The franchise quality is high and the cash-flow visibility is genuine, but four specific factors prevent a BUY at the current price: a sharp negative current-quarter EPS revision (-61.1% over 30 days, -66.4% over 90 days) that reflects the materializing event-cancellation risk; the $475M 2.25% convertible due 2027 with a conversion price (~$82.98) below the current price, creating a near-certain dilution/conversion overhead; the redeemable MotoGP minority (~$674M and growing) that converts into a multi-year cash-and-stock buyout obligation; and a valuation that already embeds part of the sum-of-the-parts re-rating thesis without a clean catalyst inside the next 60 days. The stock has also declined ~18% from its 52-week high while the operating story improved, which sharpens the risk/reward but does not by itself justify a BUY given the offsetting overhangs.

## Company Overview

Liberty owns two assets:

- **Formula 1 (100%)** — Delta Topco holds the exclusive, long-dated commercial rights to the FIA Formula One World Championship. Revenue is split across race promotion fees (circuit hosting fees plus the directly promoted Las Vegas Grand Prix), media rights fees (broadcast licensing plus F1 TV subscriptions), and sponsorship. The defining financial characteristic is advance payment with contractual annual escalators. The 2026 calendar is 22 events, reduced from a planned 24 after Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were dropped due to Middle East conflict. F1's fan base is ~800M+, reported to be growing ~12% YoY in a young, affluent, global demographic.

- **MotoGP (~84%)** — Acquired July 3, 2025 for ~$3.66B (€3.12B). MotoGP Sports Entertainment Group (formerly Dorna Sports) holds exclusive commercial rights to the FIM Grand Prix World Championship plus adjacent series (World Superbike). The business model matches F1's — media rights, race promotion, sponsorship — but is smaller and more Europe-centric. 2026 calendar: 22 events.

The strategic logic is ownership of the sport rather than participation in it. Both championships are monopolies over their categories, with multi-year, advance-paid, escalating contracts. The value-creation thesis behind MotoGP is applying F1's commercial playbook — US media-rights monetization, hospitality, digital — to a second flywheel.

Management is led by CEO Derek Chang, with Brian J. Wendling as CAO/PFO and Renee Wilm as Chief Legal/Administrative Officer. John Malone is now classified as a "10% Owner" in March 2026 Form 4 filings versus "Chairman of the Board" in December 2025, consistent with stepping back from the board chairmanship. Chase Carey, former F1 CEO, remains a director.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (10-Q filed May 7, 2026):**
- Total revenue $711M vs. $447M prior-year Q1 (+59%), driven by an extra F1 event recognized in the quarter (3/22nds vs. 2/24ths of season fees) plus the first inclusion of MotoGP.
- F1 segment revenue $617M (+53% YoY); F1 Adjusted OIBDA $172M (doubled from $85M).
- MotoGP segment $94M revenue, $16M Adjusted OIBDA, $24M operating loss driven by $40M of purchase-accounting amortization.
- Consolidated Adjusted OIBDA $181M (+148% from $73M).
- Operating income $64M vs. ($67M) loss prior year (prior year burdened by $50M Concorde incentive payments and $11M acquisition costs).
- Net earnings $53M; EPS $0.23 basic.
- Operating cash flow $357M (vs. $391M; decline is working-capital timing).

**Balance sheet (March 31, 2026):**
- Total assets $15.9B. Goodwill $6.96B + intangibles $4.96B = ~$11.9B, or ~75% of assets, reflecting acquisition premiums on F1 and MotoGP.
- Total debt ~$5.0B: F1 Senior Loan Facilities $3.34B (wtd avg 5.39%), MotoGP Credit Facilities $1.15B (4.99%), plus the $475M 2.25% Convertible Senior Notes due 2027 (conversion price ~$82.98). Cash $1.33B. All subsidiary debt is non-recourse to Liberty and secured at the operating-company level.
- Equity $7.73B; ~$2.3B variable-rate / $2.7B fixed-rate, with $2.2B of F1 interest-rate swaps hedging.
- Interest expense $68M in Q1, up $20M YoY, reflecting the incremental F1 term loans used to fund MotoGP.

The reported multi-year revenue decline ($12.5B in 2023 to $3.65B in 2024, with a ~$2B 2024 net loss) is entirely the product of split-offs and discontinued-operations reclassification, not business deterioration. A standardized data pull misreads this as a collapsing company. The continuing operation — F1 plus MotoGP — is growing, and Q1 2026 segment OIBDA confirms it.

The earnings beat/miss record is noisy because of the seasonality of event recognition and acquisition accounting: Q1 2025 +162.6% surprise, Q2 2025 +128.4%, Q3 2025 -11.0%, Q4 2025 in line. The large positive surprises reflect timing of event-fee recognition rather than a durable beat cadence; visibility on any single quarter is low even though the annual contracted backlog is high.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs, each with concrete support:

1. **US media-rights step-up (Apple).** The Apple TV deal replaced ESPN's prior 7-year arrangement at materially higher value. Management commentary (sourced via The Hollywood Reporter, AppleInsider, MacRumors) indicates viewership is up versus ESPN and the feared streaming-only fan backlash did not materialize. This validates the higher-value US contract and supports the media-rights escalator thesis for the contract term.

2. **MotoGP integration and monetization.** A full year of MotoGP consolidation lifts revenue and OIBDA in 2026, and the longer-term case is applying F1's commercial discipline to MotoGP's smaller base. The near-term GAAP optics are muddied by $40M/quarter of purchase-accounting amortization, which depresses MotoGP operating income while the Adjusted OIBDA is positive.

3. **Contracted escalators.** The advance-paid, escalating contract structure delivers compounding revenue with limited volume risk. The performance-obligation backlog is the quantitative anchor for the durability of the growth.

The principal qualifier is the current-quarter estimate revision. Consensus current-quarter EPS was cut to $0.25 from $0.64 thirty days ago and $0.74 ninety days ago — a -61.1%/-66.4% revision driven primarily by the cancelled Bahrain/Saudi F1 events and postponed MotoGP Qatar event shifting fee recognition out of the period. Full-year estimates are more stable (current year $1.78, +8.9% over 30 days; next year $2.13, +1.3%), indicating the cut is timing-driven rather than a structural deterioration. The signal favoring a BUY would be a large positive revision combined with accelerating revenue; here the near-term revision is sharply negative even if the annual picture holds, which removes the strongest forward indicator from the bull side.

## Valuation Assessment

At $87.14 (last close $89.32), FWONK trades at ~38.6x trailing P/E and roughly 49x current-year consensus EPS of $1.78, falling toward ~41x on next-year $2.13. Headline P/E is the wrong lens given the seasonality of event recognition and the heavy non-cash purchase-accounting amortization on MotoGP; the cleaner metrics are consolidated Adjusted OIBDA growth and the sum-of-the-parts view.

The bull-case valuation is a sum-of-the-parts dislocation. Morgan Stanley named FWONK its Top Pick in US Media & Entertainment in May 2026 with a $117 price target (lowered from $120 but ~34% above the current $87.14), arguing the stock trades at roughly a 25% discount to the aggregate value of the F1 teams alone, despite Liberty owning the entire sport plus MotoGP. The analyst distribution is constructive: 14 buy/strong-buy versus 2 hold, a mean and median target of $114.50, low $97, high $135. With the consensus mean ~31% above spot and the low target ~11% above spot, the analyst floor sits meaningfully above the current price.

Two factors temper the valuation. First, the $475M convertible at ~$82.98 sits below the current price; conversion is likely and adds dilution that the headline share count understates. Second, the redeemable MotoGP minority (~$674M, re-measured to fair value through retained earnings) is a structured future cash-and-stock obligation, with up to 50% settleable in FWONK shares. Both are real claims on equity value that the simple multiple does not capture. The valuation is favorable but not asymmetric at this price; a portion of the re-rating is already in the stock, which has held in the high-$80s/low-$90s through 2026 rather than de-rating to a distressed multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is the strongest dimension. Both F1 and MotoGP are global monopolies over their categories — there is no competing premier open-wheel or premier motorcycle championship of comparable stature. The moat is reinforced by long-dated commercial rights (F1 via the Concorde Agreement with teams and FIA arrangements; MotoGP via the $1.65B, 36-year FIM Agreement), advance-paid escalating contracts, and a growing, affluent, global fan base. The closest public comparables are TKO (UFC/WWE) and Live Nation, both built on live-sports scarcity and pricing power; Morgan Stanley prefers FWONK and TKO within this cohort.

The trajectory is improving rather than merely stable: rising US popularity (aided by "Drive to Survive") turned F1 media rights into the object of bidding wars, culminating in the higher-value Apple deal; Cadillac entered as F1's 11th team in 2026, expanding the grid and franchise interest; and MotoGP adds a second monetization vector. The main competitive caveats are upstream — the dependency on renewing the Concorde and FIM commercial agreements on favorable terms, which is an existential rather than a market-share risk.

## Risk Assessment

- **Event cancellation triggering minimum-event clauses.** The Q1 2026 10-Q rewrote and expanded the event-cancellation risk factor to explicitly name the cancelled 2026 Bahrain and Saudi F1 GPs and the postponed MotoGP Qatar GP, all tied to the Iran conflict. Broadcast contracts contain fee-reduction clauses below minimum event counts (typically 14–16 for F1, 16–20 for MotoGP). With 2026 F1 at 22 events, the floor is not breached, but this is a live, materializing risk that already drove the current-quarter estimate cut. Further calendar attrition would pressure media-rights fees directly.

- **Convertible note conversion.** The $475M 2.25% notes due 2027 convert at ~$82.98, below the current price. Conversion is likely and dilutive.

- **MotoGP minority overhang.** The 16% Rollover Sellers hold redeemable put rights (~$674M, growing with FMV) recorded outside permanent equity, exercisable in tranches (1/3 after year 3, 2/3 after year 5, remainder after year 6), settleable up to 50% in FWONK shares. Acquisition accounting remains preliminary. This is a multi-year cash/dilution obligation easily missed in a simple model.

- **FX.** MotoGP's functional currency is the Euro; a 10% EUR/USD move shifts MotoGP quarterly revenue ~$9M. Q1 2026 comprehensive loss of ($24M) was driven by a $96M negative FX translation swing.

- **Leverage and restricted payments.** ~$5B debt with rising interest expense; restricted-payment tests can block cash upstreaming from F1/MotoGP to the parent if leverage ratios are exceeded. The mitigant is that all subsidiary debt is non-recourse to Liberty.

- **Asset concentration.** The entire equity story rests on two assets; any reputational, regulatory, or competitive damage to either sport is highly impactful.

- **Governance complexity.** The Malone-complex structure (shared services, overlapping directors, ~$5M/quarter reimbursed G&A, aircraft time-sharing) is a disclosed related-party governance risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 31.6% versus 23.9% one-year historical volatility — rich by ~7.7 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (31.6% at one month vs. 28.7% at six months), which signals the market is pricing near-term event uncertainty. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±5.7%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.1% at one month, steepening to -4.3% at six months), and the put/call OI ratio of 7.10 looks alarming in isolation but the put/call volume ratio of 0.07 indicates the open interest is dominated by static/hedging positions rather than fresh directional put buying. The composite read: the market is pricing an identifiable near-term catalyst window (consistent with the event-cancellation/estimate-cut dynamic) with elevated but not panic-level downside skew. This supports patience on entry rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$117–135).** F1 plus MotoGP is a scarce, monopoly-grade live-sports asset with advance-paid, escalating contracts and ~$16.3B of contracted backlog through 2033. The Apple deal validates a step-up in US media-rights value and is tracking ahead of plan. A full year of MotoGP consolidation plus commercial-playbook execution lifts OIBDA, and the sum-of-the-parts discount closes as the market re-rates the pure-play. Morgan Stanley's $117 supports the base of the bull range; the high target of $135 implies ~55% upside on full re-rating.

**Bear case (~$72–78, roughly -12% to -19%).** Geopolitical calendar attrition continues, pushing F1 toward minimum-event fee-reduction thresholds and pressuring media-rights revenue. The convertible converts dilutively, the MotoGP put obligation forces cash/share settlement, and FX translation continues to drag comprehensive income. With ~75% of assets in goodwill/intangibles and ~$5B of debt, any impairment or leverage-driven restriction on upstreaming cash compresses the multiple back toward the low-$70s.

The base case sits between these. The franchise is high quality and growing, but the near-term setup lacks a clean positive catalyst inside 60 days, and the convertible plus minority overhangs cap the asymmetry. This is a growth-oriented hold: own it for the 12-month re-rating, but the current-quarter estimate cut and dilution mechanics argue against a high-conviction add at this price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (early August 2026) and the 2026 race-calendar resolution over the summer.**

**Why not a BUY at this price (specific and falsifiable):** The single strongest forward indicator — earnings-estimate momentum — is currently negative, not positive. Current-quarter consensus EPS was cut 61.1% over 30 days and 66.4% over 90 days, driven by cancelled F1 events that triggered the very minimum-event broadcast clauses management newly highlighted in the 10-Q. That is a materializing downside risk with a measurable financial channel, not a generic concern. Compounding it, the $475M convertible at ~$82.98 trades in-the-money against the current price, making dilution a near-certainty rather than a tail risk, and the ~$674M redeemable MotoGP minority is a structured cash-and-stock claim re-measured through retained earnings. A BUY requires either (a) the current-quarter revision to stabilize/reverse with full-year estimates rising, or (b) a price closer to the $97 analyst floor that compensates for the dilution overhangs. Neither condition is met today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Current-quarter EPS revision stabilizes or turns positive while full-year consensus rises further, confirming the cut was purely event-timing.
2. The 2026 calendar stabilizes at ≥20 F1 events with no further cancellations and explicit confirmation that media-rights fees are not reduced under minimum-event clauses.
3. Resolution of the convertible (conversion or refinance) that removes the dilution uncertainty, OR a pullback toward the $97 analyst-low/low-$80s area that prices in dilution while preserving sum-of-the-parts upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Further race cancellations pushing F1 below the ~14–16 event minimum-event threshold or triggering disclosed broadcast fee reductions.
2. A goodwill/intangible impairment (given ~75% of assets are intangible) or a leverage breach that restricts cash upstreaming from F1/MotoGP to the parent.
3. Evidence the MotoGP integration is underperforming — Adjusted OIBDA flat-to-negative ex-amortization across consecutive quarters, or an unfavorable revision to the preliminary purchase-price allocation.

## Conclusion

FWONK is a high-quality, monopoly-grade live-sports franchise that has been simplified into a clean motorsports pure-play with genuine, contractually-backed revenue visibility and an improving competitive position. The operating results are strong, the Apple deal is a validated US media-rights catalyst, and the sell-side sees ~31% upside to a $114.50 mean target. We share the constructive 12-month view on the franchise.

We stop short of a BUY because the near-term setup is unfavorable on three concrete, measurable dimensions: a sharply negative current-quarter estimate revision tied to materializing event-cancellation risk, an in-the-money convertible that makes dilution near-certain, and a redeemable MotoGP minority that is a structured future claim on equity. The valuation already embeds part of the re-rating and offers no clean 60-day catalyst. HOLD with a growth bias; we would move to BUY on stabilization of near-term estimates and calendar certainty, or on a pullback toward the analyst floor that better compensates for the dilution overhangs.
