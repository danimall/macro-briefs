**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UGI Corporation is a Pennsylvania-based energy holding company with four segments: regulated gas Utilities, fee-based Midstream & Marketing, European LPG distribution (UGI International), and AmeriGas Propane. The investment case rests on a deliberate simplification strategy under CEO Robert Flexon: sell the volatile, lower-multiple commodity businesses (European LPG, the PA electric utility) and reweight the portfolio toward regulated gas and fee-based midstream to earn a higher, more stable multiple. Adjusted EPS has climbed steadily from $2.84 in FY2023 to $3.32 in FY2025, and the ~$1.50 annual dividend (~4% yield) rests on roughly 50 consecutive years of increases. That combination anchors an income-oriented hold.

The complications are specific and near-term. A $700M 5.00% convertible senior note due June 2028 hit its early-conversion trigger as of March 31, 2026, is deeply in the money (conversion price ~$27.60 vs. $37.33 spot), and has been reclassified to current maturities, creating a live choice between ~6–7M incremental dilutive shares or a meaningful cash settlement. Simultaneously, the West Reading, PA explosion litigation escalated when the PA Public Utility Commission filed a formal complaint on March 18, 2026, alleging pipeline-safety violations and seeking civil penalties, with exposure unquantified. Consolidated leverage sits at ~2.2x debt/equity, normal for a regulated utility but a headwind in a higher-for-longer rate environment where negative free-cash-flow financing models carry rising costs.

We rate UGI a HOLD with an income-hold rationale and an overall score of 5.0, essentially flat versus the prior two reports (5.1 in May, 5.0 in June). The valuation is cheaper than the peer median (~12.8x vs. ~16.1x P/E), but a cheap multiple in this environment is more reliably a value-trap flag than a buy signal absent a discrete catalyst. The portfolio-reshaping thesis is credible but multi-year, and whether divestiture proceeds go to deleveraging or dilution offset is unresolved. We would upgrade on evidence that the simplification is re-rating the multiple; we would downgrade on a quantified West Reading liability or a dividend-coverage deterioration.

## Company Overview

UGI operates through four reportable segments, with FY2025 adjusted net income contributions as follows:

- **Utilities (regulated):** UGI Utilities (PA natural gas plus a small electric utility) and Mountaineer Gas (West Virginia). ~$237M adjusted net income. This is the intended growth engine via rate-base investment in constructive jurisdictions.
- **Midstream & Marketing:** Energy Services — gathering/processing, pipelines, storage, peaking, electricity generation, and a growing RNG/renewables platform. ~$269M adjusted net income, the largest single contributor. Largely fee-based.
- **UGI International:** European LPG distribution, now concentrated in France after aggressive divestitures. ~$242M adjusted net income, euro-denominated.
- **AmeriGas Propane:** The largest U.S. retail propane distributor, mid-turnaround. ~$36M adjusted net income FY2025 versus -$23M FY2024.

The strategic direction is to shrink the commodity-exposed, lower-multiple pieces (European LPG, PA electric) and reweight toward regulated gas plus fee-based midstream. The company divested Italy, Austria, the UK cylinder business, Hawaii propane, and Romania, and has the Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland, and Slovakia held for sale. In April 2026 it signed a definitive agreement to sell the PA electric utility for ~$470M, moving Utilities toward a pure-play gas profile, with a targeted close around Q2 FY27.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP net income is structurally volatile and largely uninformative.** Reported net income swung from $1,073M (FY2022) to -$1,502M (FY2023, a large impairment year) to $269M (FY2024, including ~$192M AmeriGas goodwill impairment) to $678M (FY2025, $3.09 diluted GAAP EPS). These swings are driven by unrealized commodity-derivative marks and divestiture/impairment charges. Any valuation must run off adjusted figures.

**Adjusted EPS tells the operating story:** FY22 $2.90 → FY23 $2.84 → FY24 $3.06 → FY25 $3.32. That is modest, steady growth (~4.6% CAGR from FY22 to FY25).

**FY2025 fundamentals:** Revenue ~$7.29B; adjusted net income ~$728M; operating cash flow ~$1,227M; capex ~$837M, implying roughly $390M of free cash flow before the FY2026 capex step-up. ROE ~14.2%.

**H1 FY2026 (six months ended March 31, 2026):** GAAP net income $817M (vs. $854M prior year); adjusted net income $745M vs. $779M; adjusted diluted EPS $3.35 vs. $3.58. Segment bridges: Utilities +$14M on a PA base-rate increase; AmeriGas +$130M adjusted improvement (heavily tax-driven, not yet a proven operational turnaround); Midstream & Marketing -$69M on lower investment tax credits against a strong prior-year comparison. The year-over-year adjusted-EPS decline of $0.23 warrants attention: the improvement narrative is uneven across segments, with the largest positive contributor (AmeriGas) flattered by tax items and the largest fee-based segment (Midstream) down.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt ~$7.0–7.1B; equity rose from ~$4.78B (Sep-25) to ~$5.42B (Mar-26); D/E ~2.2x; current ratio ~0.89. The frequently-cited Altman Z-Score of ~1.17 ("distress" zone) is a false alarm for a regulated utility with stable cash flows, ~$2.1B liquidity, covenant compliance, and a five-decade dividend record. The screen is misleading, but it correctly directs attention to the two genuine balance-sheet issues: the June-2028 convertible now conversion-eligible, and consolidated leverage.

**Earnings surprise record is mixed.** The most recent quarter (Q2 FY26, period ended March 31, 2026) missed by 12.9% ($2.09 actual vs. $2.40 estimate), the prior quarter missed by 1.3%, and the two seasonally weak fiscal-Q4/Q1 shoulder quarters beat on smaller absolute numbers. Two beats and two misses across four quarters signals moderate, not high, forward visibility.

## Growth Analysis

UGI's growth is a rate-base-and-reshaping story, not an organic-demand story. FY2026 capex is guided up to ~$1,053M (from $844M), concentrated in Utilities (~$629M) and Midstream renewables/RNG (~$217M). That capex, funneled into constructive PA/WV rate cases, is the primary driver of the targeted mid-single-digit adjusted-EPS growth.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed rather than a clean signal. The current-quarter estimate improved +30.6% over 30 days, but this is a seasonally small loss quarter ($-0.12), so the percentage move is mechanically large on a tiny base and not meaningful. The next-quarter estimate deteriorated -15.4% (30d) and -29.3% (90d), and the current-year estimate fell -6.8% over 90 days to $2.81, with next year at $3.33, down modestly. Read together, forward estimates are drifting sideways-to-lower on the meaningful full-year lines. This does not meet the threshold of a genuine earnings-inflection signal (the +30% current-quarter move is a small-base artifact, and revenue is not accelerating from a depressed level).

The reshaping strategy is the real growth lever, but it is subtractive in the near term: selling the PA electric utility and exiting most of Europe shrinks the earnings base even as it improves quality. Whether this converts to per-share growth depends entirely on how proceeds are deployed — debt paydown improves coverage but not EPS, while buybacks (the new 8M-share, four-year authorization from February 2026) offset dilution but have been modest so far (~$23M in H1 FY26). We score Growth Prospects 4.8: modest, quality-improving, but subtractive and lacking accelerating momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

UGI trades at ~12.8x trailing/forward EPS with a ~4% dividend yield, versus a Gas Utilities peer median of ~16.1x P/E and ~3.4% yield. On the surface, that is a ~20% multiple discount plus a superior yield.

The discount, however, is substantially explained rather than mispriced. Relative to the peer set:
- **NFG (10.3x, -15% off 52w high)** trades lower still, reflecting the market's willingness to discount E&P-adjacent commodity exposure.
- **SWX (26.7x), NJR (16.1x), OGS (17.0x), SR (15.6x)** are cleaner regulated-utility profiles commanding higher multiples.

UGI's discount to the median reflects three specific, defensible drags: (1) higher consolidated leverage than the cleaner peers; (2) the commodity-exposed LPG contribution (AmeriGas plus UGI International) that the market multiples at a lower rate; and (3) the West Reading and convertible overhangs. A cheap multiple has been a reliable value-trap flag in this regime, and cheapness without a discrete catalyst is not a buy signal. The catalyst that could close the gap — a completed simplification that leaves a pure-play regulated-gas-plus-midstream entity — is real but 12–24 months out, gated on regulatory approvals for the electric-utility sale (targeted Q2 FY27) and completion of the remaining European exits.

Analyst targets ($40 low, $45 high, $43 mean, $44 median) sit 7–20% above spot. On an income-hold basis, the ~4% yield plus low-single-digit EPS growth supports a total-return profile roughly in line with the low end of that range. We do not treat these targets as floors here because UGI is a slow compounder where the estimates are drifting sideways rather than an inflection stock with rising estimates and a sub-1.5 PEG, so the analyst range reflects the same modest-growth math as the price. Valuation score: 5.3 — mildly cheap, but the discount is earned, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The regulated Utilities segment holds monopoly gas franchises in constructive PA/WV jurisdictions — a genuine moat with predictable rate-base returns, though also subject to affordability scrutiny and, in UGI's case, a reputational overhang in its home state (West Reading) at the same time it seeks rate increases. Midstream competes in the Appalachian/Marcellus basin on fee-based gathering and an emerging RNG niche, a stable but not share-gaining position. AmeriGas is the #1 U.S. retail propane distributor in a fragmented, consolidating market where route density matters but secular demand is flat-to-declining. In Europe, LPG faces the most aggressive decarbonization and electrification pressure (EU EPBD), which is why management is exiting most of it.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving-in-quality but not share-gaining. The simplification improves the mix (higher weighting to the moated regulated business) without changing the underlying growth ceiling. Competitive Position score: 5.2 — a solid moat in the core, offset by secular headwinds in two of four segments and an underperforming shareholder-return record (proxy TSR 126.4 vs. peer group 171.9), which signals the market is discounting execution.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific and not fully priced:

1. **June-2028 convertible, now conversion-eligible.** This is a live, quantifiable event: ~6–7M potential incremental diluted shares (~3% dilution) or a meaningful cash outflow. A dedicated $300M revolver exists to cash-settle, and the cluster of May–July 2026 financing 8-Ks (Items 1.01/1.02/2.03) is consistent with active refinancing around this maturity. Manageable, but a near-term overhang either way.

2. **West Reading, PA explosion litigation and regulatory action.** The March 2023 explosion caused seven fatalities. UGI believes it is largely insured, but the exposure is unquantified and carries reputational and regulatory-relationship risk in the jurisdiction where it is simultaneously seeking rate increases.

3. **Secular LPG decline and FX.** European and U.S. propane volumes face structural conservation and electrification pressure; euro translation adds volatility.

4. **Weather sensitivity.** ~60–65% of volumes fall in the Oct–Mar heating season; a warm winter directly hits results.

5. **AmeriGas turnaround execution.** FY2025's return to positive adjusted income is early, and the H1 FY26 improvement was substantially tax-driven, not operational.

Risk Factors score: 4.6 — the convertible and West Reading are specific, semi-binary items with asymmetric downside that the market is only partially pricing.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term ATM IV of 30.3% is rich relative to 22.6% one-year historical vol (a 7.7-point spread, 74th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (30.3% near-term vs. 25.3% at six months), with a 4.6% put-call skew indicating elevated near-term put demand. The market is pricing near-term event risk with an implied move of ±8.6% at the nearest expiry. IV collapsed from 126.3% on June 17 to 30.3% on July 25 as the price recovered +11%, so the extreme prior reading was likely a thin-options artifact around a specific event that has since passed. Put-call OI ratio fell to 0.06 (very call-tilted on open interest), and the low put/call volume ratio (0.21) argues against heavy directional bearish positioning. Net read: the options market flags near-term event risk but is not signaling a structurally bearish stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($44–48).** The simplification executes cleanly: the PA electric sale closes on schedule, the remaining European LPG exits complete, and proceeds retire debt while the February 2026 buyback authorization offsets convertible dilution. The residual entity — regulated gas plus fee-based midstream — re-rates toward the 16–17x peer multiple. On ~$3.40+ normalized adjusted EPS at 16x, that supports the mid-$40s to high-$40s, plus a growing ~4% dividend. AmeriGas proves an operational (not just tax-driven) turnaround, and the West Reading matter resolves within insurance limits.

**Bear Case ($30–33, ~11–20% downside).** The West Reading PUC complaint produces a material uninsured penalty and damages the regulatory relationship into the next rate case. The convertible settles in a way that dilutes without a clear offset. LPG secular decline and a warm winter compress earnings, and the current-year estimate cuts (-6.8% over 90 days) prove to be the start of a trend rather than noise. Leverage in a higher-for-longer rate environment raises financing costs on the stepped-up ~$1.05B capex plan. The multiple stays discounted, and the stock reverts toward its 52-week low of $31.62.

**Base Case ($40).** The dividend holds and grows modestly, adjusted EPS compounds low-single-digits toward ~$3.40, the convertible is managed without a disruptive event, and West Reading resolves largely within insurance. The multiple stays near current levels as the market waits for the simplification to complete. At ~13x $3.40 EPS plus dividend, ~$40 is the most-likely 12-month outcome: modest appreciation plus yield, roughly a high-single-digit total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold). 12-month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The PA electric-utility sale closes and management commits divestiture proceeds primarily to debt reduction, lowering D/E toward ~2.0x, OR the buyback pace accelerates meaningfully (>$100M/year) to demonstrably offset convertible dilution.
- Current-year and next-year adjusted-EPS estimates turn positive (revision inflection) alongside evidence the AmeriGas improvement is operational rather than tax-driven, across a full heating season.
- Multiple re-rating begins — the stock sustains above ~14x forward EPS as the market prices the cleaner post-simplification entity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The West Reading PUC matter produces a quantified, materially uninsured liability, or evidence of a damaged regulatory relationship ahead of the next PA rate case.
- Dividend coverage deteriorates — free cash flow falls short of the dividend on a sustained basis as the stepped-up capex plan runs, or management signals dividend pressure.
- Adjusted EPS declines beyond the modest H1 FY26 slippage, confirming the -6.8% current-year revision trend as a durable deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal quarterly report and any 8-K detailing final convertible settlement terms; the West Reading discovery/complaint progression; and the PA electric-utility sale regulatory approval milestones (targeted close ~Q2 FY27).

## Conclusion

UGI is a leveraged, four-segment gas utility executing a credible but multi-year simplification designed to close the gap between its ~4% adjusted-EPS growth and a shareholder-return record that has lagged peers. The ~4% dividend with ~50 years of increases and stable adjusted-EPS execution justify holding for income. The ~20% multiple discount to the peer median is earned — reflecting leverage, LPG secular decline, and two specific overhangs (the conversion-eligible June-2028 convertible and the escalating West Reading regulatory complaint) — rather than a mispricing that warrants a BUY. Forward full-year estimates are drifting sideways-to-lower, not inflecting, so the estimate-revision signal does not favor a stronger call. We maintain HOLD at an overall score of 5.0, consistent with the prior two reports, and would revisit on evidence that the simplification is re-rating the multiple or, conversely, on a quantified West Reading liability or dividend-coverage strain.
