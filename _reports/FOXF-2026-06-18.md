**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fox Factory is a premium performance-products franchise (FOX suspension and shocks, Marucci baseball/softball, Race Face/Easton bike components) that ran a debt-funded acquisition strategy into the top of its end-market cycle and is now working through the consequences. The business itself remains competitive: it holds defensible niche positions, maintains OEM design-in relationships with BRP, Polaris, Ford, and Toyota, and gained share in powersports during the downturn. The problem is the capital structure. FOXF carries roughly $695M of debt at a 6%+ weighted-average rate against $53.9M of cash and a cyclically depressed earnings base, and it required covenant relief in May 2026 (the Sixth Amendment) that raised maximum net leverage to 5.0x, a signal it was approaching a breach.

The investment question is whether those risks are already in the price. The stock has fallen ~84% from its 2021 peak (TSR index 160.9 → 16.5 versus a peer group that roughly held at 83.1), the equity now trades at ~12x current-year consensus EPS ($1.43) and ~10x next-year ($1.77) against a peer median near 24x, the last two quarters beat consensus by +115% and +36% against a reset bar, and the entire visible sell-side, including the most bearish $20 target, sees upside from the current $17.64. An AVOID rating here would be issuing a bearish view that the market has substantially discounted.

This is also not a BUY. Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $16.1M, residual SSG goodwill of $83.6M would be fully impaired by a 1.5% WACC increase, and the deleveraging plan depends on a demand recovery that has not arrived. The risk/reward is two-sided: meaningful reverse-operating-leverage upside if demand and margins recover, against real solvency stress if they do not. We rate FOXF HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Fox Factory designs and manufactures premium suspension, shock, and performance components across three segments:

- **Powered Vehicles Group (PVG, ~33% of sales):** FOX-brand shocks for off-road trucks, side-by-sides, ATVs, snowmobiles; Marzocchi motorcycle suspension (Ducati, BMW, Triumph). OEM and aftermarket channels.
- **Aftermarket Applications Group (AAG, ~32%):** Lift kits, vehicle upfitting (Rocky Ridge, Outside Van, Black Widow), and Method Race Wheels, leveraging chassis-supply relationships with Ford, GM, and Stellantis.
- **Specialty Sports Group (SSG, ~35%):** Bike suspension and components (FOX, Race Face, Easton, Marzocchi), about 22% of total sales, manufactured primarily in Taiwan, plus Marucci baseball/softball equipment, acquired in 2023.

Customer concentration is moderate: the top 10 customers represent 35% of sales, with Ford alone at ~12% (declining from 15% in FY24). The franchise has real moats: brand equity validated by professional athletes (Olympics, MLB, Baja 1000), proprietary technology (Live Valve electronic suspension), and OEM design-in relationships that create switching costs.

## Financial Analysis

The headline FY2025 result was a net loss of $544.6M, driven almost entirely by a $557.3M non-cash goodwill impairment that cut goodwill from $639.5M to $83.6M. This write-off is backward-looking; it recognizes in accounting what the market already knew about the debt-funded 2023 Marucci/Custom Wheel House deals (~$700M) executed at peak valuations.

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,602M | $1,464M | $1,394M | $1,467M |
| Operating income | $247M | $160M | $58M | -$523M |
| Net income | $205M | $121M | $6.6M | -$545M |
| Gross margin | 33.2% | 31.7% | 30.4% | 30.2% |
| Operating cash flow | — | $179M | $132M | $61M |

The deterioration predates the impairment. Operating income collapsed from $247M (FY22) to $58M (FY24) on declining revenue and margin compression, and operating cash flow fell roughly two-thirds in two years. This is the genuine concern, not the non-cash charge.

The more constructive read comes from the most recent quarter. Q1 FY2026 (ended April 3, 2026) revenue grew 3.9% to $368.7M, and operating income turned positive at $6.0M. The net loss of $15.0M (-$0.36/share) included a $10.0M divestiture loss and $11.9M of interest expense; the operating business is no longer losing money. PVG was the standout: sales +17.4%, EBITDA +57%. The offset is cash conversion: Q1 operating cash flow was negative $16.1M (versus +$0.7M prior year), and gross margin slipped 200bps to 28.9% on tariffs and mix.

The balance sheet is the central issue. The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 sits deep in the distress zone. Total debt of ~$695M (Term Loan $519M + revolver $176M) compares to $659M of equity (D/E ~1.49) and only $53.9M of cash, with a 6.04% weighted-average variable rate. FY2025 free cash flow was $27M against this debt load. The revolver is heavily drawn ($176M of $500M), so liquidity is adequate but not comfortable.

The clearest tell is the financing sequence. The October 2025 Fifth Amendment pushed maturities to 2030. The May 2026 Sixth Amendment then granted covenant relief: raising maximum net leverage to 5.00x (stepping down to 4.00x by mid-2028), setting minimum interest coverage at 2.50x, and raising interest margins (up to SOFR+2.75%) during a "Covenant Relief Period" through Q2 2028. Companies do not negotiate covenant relief from a position of strength; this strongly implies FOXF was near breaching its leverage covenant. The relief extends the runway but does not eliminate the risk. The deleveraging plan still requires a demand recovery to convert EBITDA into the free cash flow needed to service 6%+ debt.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is best understood as cyclical recovery potential rather than secular expansion. The end-market is in a discretionary downturn (high rates, elevated vehicle prices, and dealer destocking, with SSG/bikes down 8.7% in Q1) that is largely sector-wide rather than a FOXF share-loss problem. The segment divergence in Q1 illustrates the dynamic: PVG strong (+17.4% sales, +57% EBITDA) on powersports and auto-aftermarket momentum, while AAG EBITDA fell 33% and SSG EBITDA fell 25% on tariffs, mix, and bike-channel destocking.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and warrants caution. The current-quarter estimate was cut 48.9% over 90 days (to $0.18 from $0.36), and the full-year estimate slipped 2.6% over 30 days. That is negative near-term earnings momentum. But the next-quarter estimate rose 10.7% over 90 days and next-year rose 3.5%, suggesting analysts see a trough forming rather than continued deterioration. The earnings surprise record reinforces this: the two most recent quarters beat by +115.3% (Q1 FY26: $0.18 vs $0.08) and +36.1% (prior quarter: $0.20 vs $0.15), against a depressed bar. The prior two quarters missed (-58.7% and -10.4%), so the sequential improvement is the signal worth watching.

Consensus sees current-year EPS rising to $1.77 next year, roughly 24% growth off a trough. If demand recovers and the cost-optimization program (Transformation Committee, portfolio divestitures, PSUs newly tied to FCF/ROIC) delivers, the reverse operating leverage on a fixed-cost manufacturing base is meaningful. That recovery is plausible but not yet visible in the cash flow statement.

## Valuation Assessment

On forward earnings, FOXF screens cheap. At $17.64 against consensus current-year EPS of $1.43, the stock trades at ~12.3x, falling to ~10x on next-year's $1.77. The peer median P/E is 24.1x.

The peer set is heterogeneous, with multiples ranging from 10.5x to 49.2x, so the median is a loose anchor. FOXF trades closer to the levered, lower-multiple names (SMP, LCII) than the premium-multiple ones, appropriately, given its balance sheet. A discount to the median is warranted; the question is whether ~12x forward is too cheap or about right for a company carrying distress-zone leverage.

The valuation must be framed on an enterprise basis, because this is a levered equity stub. EV is ~$1.5B (~$793M equity + ~$695M debt less $53.9M cash, i.e. ~$641M net debt) against FY2025 segment Adjusted EBITDA of ~$226M (sum of segments before corporate), roughly 6.5x on this basis. That is not expensive for the franchise quality, but the equity captures only the residual after debt, which makes the stub highly sensitive to small changes in EBITDA. A modest EBITDA recovery flows disproportionately to equity holders; a modest decline does the reverse and pressures the covenant.

A low multiple on its own is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal; cheapness sorts the wrong way absent a catalyst. FOXF does have potential catalysts (cost program, divestitures, demand recovery, possible tariff refunds from the SCOTUS IEEPA ruling), but none are yet confirmed in the cash flows. The forward multiple compression is real but reflects genuine solvency risk, not pure mispricing. A base case lands near the current price: if the trough holds and deleveraging proceeds, the stub re-rates toward the low-$20s (consistent with the sell-side mean of $22.33 and median of $22.00); if cash flow stays negative and a dilutive raise or further impairment hits, the stub compresses toward $13. The symmetric range supports a balanced valuation score rather than a clearly cheap or clearly stretched one.

## Competitive Landscape

FOXF holds premium positions across fragmented niches: bike suspension versus RockShox/SRAM, X-Fusion, and Manitou; off-road versus Bilstein, King Shocks, and ICON; baseball versus Easton/Rawlings and Wilson (DeMarini/Louisville Slugger). Brand strength and OEM design-in relationships are durable advantages; these are not commodity products, and the switching costs in OEM suspension programs are real.

The current downturn is a cyclical, sector-wide phenomenon rather than a competitive erosion story. FOXF gained share in powersports during the period, and the structural problem is the balance sheet, not the brands. That distinction matters for the competitive score: trajectory in the core franchise is stable-to-improving even as the financial structure is stressed. The main competitive risk is Taiwan manufacturing concentration in bikes, where a September 2025 CBP Withhold Release Order against Giant Manufacturing raises spillover scrutiny, and the discretionary nature of the products, which amplifies cyclicality.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and covenant pressure — the dominant risk.** Debt is secured by substantially all assets, the revolver is heavily drawn, and cash is only $53.9M. Negative Q1 operating cash flow means the deleveraging story currently runs in reverse. If demand does not recover, the path to a dilutive equity raise or distressed refinancing is real.

**Residual goodwill.** Management disclosed that a 1.5% WACC increase would fully impair SSG's remaining $83.6M goodwill. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, another impairment is plausible; non-cash, but a further hit to the equity base and a negative signal.

**Tariffs.** A margin headwind and the explicit trigger for the FY2025 impairment. A new Section 122 10%→15% global tariff took effect February 24, 2026. The SCOTUS IEEPA ruling creates an unquantified refund opportunity the market may not be pricing.

**Discretionary demand.** High-end products exposed to rates, vehicle costs, and dealer destocking. A consumer cliff in H2 2026 would directly pressure the recovery thesis.

**Strategic-review risk.** A new Q1 10-Q risk factor explicitly flags that the cost-optimization and portfolio-review program may fail or disrupt the business, an escalation in management tone.

**Resolved overhang.** The 2021–2023 securities class action was dismissed with prejudice on February 10, 2026, and derivative suits were dismissed in May 2026, removing a litigation cloud.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated uncertainty with a bearish tilt, but not panic. Near-term ATM IV of 43.2% sits below one-year historical volatility of 54.2% (an 11.1% cheap spread) and at just the 5th percentile of the current chain; single-name vol is cheap relative to realized, which favors patient entry if the demand recovery materializes. The put/call skew of 13.0% reflects elevated put demand consistent with the leverage concern, and the term structure is in contango (43.2% near versus 70.6% at three months), so the market sees the bigger risk further out, likely around the Q2 print and covenant testing. Short interest of 10.8% of float with 5.8 days to cover confirms meaningful institutional bearishness, but it is not crowded enough to create a strong squeeze setup on its own. The implied move on the nearest expiry (1m: ±22.9%) signals the market expects a binary outcome at the next earnings event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($26)

The franchise is intact and gaining share; PVG is already inflecting. The cost program and activist-driven Transformation Committee deliver margin recovery, demand stabilizes as rate pressure eases, and free cash flow turns positive. Deleveraging proceeds, the covenant runway proves more than adequate, and the stub re-rates with significant operating leverage. Potential tariff refunds add unmodeled upside. The stock reaches the high analyst target of $26 (~47% upside).

### Bear Case ($13)

The demand recovery stalls, operating cash flow stays negative, and the company is forced into a dilutive equity raise or distressed refinancing. A 1.5% WACC increase triggers another SSG goodwill impairment. Tariffs continue compressing margins, and the covenant relief proves insufficient. The stub compresses toward the 52-week low of $13.08 (~26% downside).

### Base Case ($20-22)

The trough holds, the cost program delivers incremental margin, and deleveraging proceeds slowly but steadily without a forced raise. The stub re-rates modestly toward the sell-side mean of ~$22 over 12-18 months as cash conversion improves. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios and offers roughly 13-25% upside from current levels, balanced against the ~26% bear-case downside.

The asymmetry is two-sided. The bear case is largely discounted (stock down ~84% from peak, ~12x forward, below the most bearish analyst target), which is why this is not an AVOID. The solvency risk is real and the cash flow is currently negative, which is why this is not a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026, expected early August 2026) is the critical checkpoint; it will reveal whether operating cash flow has turned positive and whether the leverage trajectory is improving against the relaxed 5.0x covenant.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow, confirming the deleveraging plan is funding itself.
- Net leverage trending toward 4.0x with the demand recovery broadening beyond PVG into AAG and SSG.
- A confirmed tariff refund or resolution that materially improves margins and provides cash to pay down the revolver.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A covenant breach or amendment seeking further relief, signaling the runway is closing.
- A dilutive equity raise announced at distressed levels.
- A second goodwill impairment (SSG), or operating cash flow remaining negative for two more quarters.
- Q2 revenue growth turning negative YoY, indicating the trough was not the bottom.

## Conclusion

Fox Factory is a quality franchise carrying a balance sheet it cannot comfortably afford at a cyclical trough. The leverage is severe (Altman Z of 1.16, negative Q1 operating cash flow, covenant relief that signals near-breach), and that risk alone keeps the stock out of BUY territory. The bearish case is substantially in the price: the stock has lost ~84% of its value from the 2021 peak, trades at ~12x forward earnings against a 24x peer median, beat its last two prints against a reset bar, and sits below even the most bearish analyst target of $20. The operating business has stopped losing money, PVG is inflecting, and an activist-driven self-help program is forming.

When positive and negative roughly cancel (meaningful reverse-operating-leverage upside against real solvency stress, a de-rated equity stub with sell-side upside against negative cash conversion) the honest call is HOLD. We classify this as an "uncertain" hold rather than a conviction hold: the next two quarters of cash flow data will determine whether this is a recovering trough or a deteriorating balance sheet. We would wait for confirmation of positive operating cash flow before paying for the recovery, and we would exit on any sign the covenant runway is closing.
