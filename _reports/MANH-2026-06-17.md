**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Manhattan Associates is a debt-free, high-return supply-chain and omnichannel commerce software company in the middle of a cloud transition that is going well operationally but is masked by the headline numbers. Cloud subscriptions grew 21% in FY2025 and accelerated to 24% in Q1 2026; remaining performance obligations (RPO, the contracted backlog) reached roughly $2.3B, up ~24% year over year. These are the forward indicators that matter for a ratable-revenue software model. The business generates a 34.6% FCF margin ($374M on $1,081M revenue), carries zero debt, and earns roughly 70% on equity. On forward adjusted EPS consensus of $5.36 (current year) and $5.95 (next year), the stock at ~$139 trades near 26x and 23x respectively, below the 35x application-software peer median, and well off the ~$220 sustained levels of mid-2025.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and testable. First, two consolidated securities class actions (Section 10(b)/Rule 10b-5) covering the July 2024–February 2025 period are live; the motion to dismiss was argued April 9, 2026 and the ruling is pending, with the company explicitly unable to estimate potential loss. A denial keeps the overhang in place and raises settlement risk. Second, services revenue — 47% of the business — declined ~4% in FY2025 on "customer budgetary constraints," and the company sells large (>$1M) projects on 9–12+ month cycles into retail and logistics verticals that are exposed to macro spending pullbacks. Because cloud revenue is recognized ratably over 5+ years, a softening in new bookings would not show in current revenue but would compound into future periods. Third, at a PEG near 2.4 the multiple already prices the quality; the re-rating case requires backlog conversion and litigation lift that are not yet in the numbers.

The stock has fallen ~37% from its sustained 2025 highs. That drawdown reflects genuine, identifiable concerns: a sharp deceleration to ~4% headline revenue growth, the late-January 2026 guidance disappointment, the litigation, wholesale leadership turnover, and a rising effective tax rate, rather than indiscriminate volatility. The cloud/RPO durability is the offset, and it is real, but it is contingent on the litigation and macro picture clearing. That balance supports holding the quality while waiting for those two binaries to resolve.

## Company Overview

Manhattan Associates develops, sells, deploys, and services software that manages supply chains, inventory, warehouse operations, transportation, labor, and retail point-of-sale and omnichannel commerce. Customers span retailers, wholesalers, manufacturers, and logistics providers. The flagship is **Manhattan Active®**, a cloud-native, "versionless," continuously updated SaaS suite. Headquartered in Atlanta with a large R&D center in Bangalore, the company employs ~4,390 people.

The FY2025 revenue mix: Services 47% ($503M), Cloud subscriptions 38% ($408M), Maintenance 12% ($130M), Hardware 2% ($25M), and Software license 1% ($15M). Cloud now represents ~96% of total software revenue. Geographically, the Americas contribute ~75% of revenue, EMEA ~20%, and APAC the balance. Gartner and ARC Advisory Group recognize the company as a market leader in supply-chain execution and omnichannel. The competitive edge rests on deep domain depth, mission-critical embeddedness in customer operations, multi-year non-cancelable cloud contracts (5+ years), and an integrated portfolio whose versionless, zero-downtime architecture is a genuine technical differentiator against legacy-encumbered rivals.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet and cash generation are the strongest part of the story. FY2025 operating cash flow was $389M (+32% YoY), with $329M cash, zero debt, and no credit facilities. The Altman Z of 3.32 places the company in the safe zone; the reported D/E of 2.67 is an artifact of deferred revenue and lease liabilities, not borrowings. Net income was $220M and GAAP diluted EPS $3.60; on a non-GAAP/adjusted basis the trailing four quarters summed to roughly $5.12 (Q2 2025 $1.31, Q3 $1.36, Q4 $1.21, Q1 2026 $1.24). Return on equity near 70% reflects the asset-light, buyback-heavy capital structure.

Revenue was up 4% in FY2025, after a clear deceleration: ~21% (2023), ~12% (2024), ~4% (2025). The deceleration is partly mechanical. The high-margin legacy streams are shrinking by design — license -2%, maintenance -6%, services -4% — as the model pivots to ratable cloud. With legacy license and maintenance now a combined 13% of revenue, their drag on the headline is diminishing, and cloud's 21–24% growth will increasingly dominate. Operating margin was 25.9% (up from 25.1%) in FY2025 but compressed to 23.0% in Q1 2026 on higher sales-and-marketing spend.

Two quieter items weigh on reported EPS. The effective tax rate rose from ~18% (FY2024) to ~23% (FY2025) to ~29% (Q1 2026) as excess stock-comp tax benefits shrank, a structural EPS headwind unrelated to operations. And capital returns have turned aggressive: $274.5M of buybacks in FY2025 plus $150M in Q1 2026, with the board raising authority five-fold from $100M to $500M in March 2026. Buybacks now exceed FCF in some quarters, drawing cash from $329M to $226M in a single quarter. With zero debt and ~$374M FCF this remains sustainable, but it is consuming the cash cushion faster than it is being replenished, which warrants monitoring if bookings stumble.

Earnings visibility is good: four consecutive beats (Q2 2025 +13.9%, Q3 +13.4%, Q4 +5.7%, Q1 2026 +9.1%). That track record supports confidence in current consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth engine is the cloud subscription line and the backlog behind it. Because cloud revenue is recognized over 5+ year contracts, the RPO trajectory is a better read on demand than the 4% reported revenue growth; the contracted-but-unrecognized revenue is compounding faster than the income statement shows.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not a trough-recovery signal: current-year EPS consensus is up 2.8% over 90 days, next-year up 2.0%. This is a steady-growth profile, not an earnings trough recovering steeply. EPS growth is muted — roughly flat-to-mid-single-digit current year versus the prior $5.12, accelerating to ~11% next year — held back by the rising tax rate and margin investment more than by demand.

The swing factor is services (47% of revenue), which is tied to large capital projects with 9–12+ month sales cycles. FY2025 services weakness in the Americas was attributed to "customer budgetary constraints that shifted services work to future periods." Q1 2026 services returned to ~+4% growth, suggesting stabilization, but the macro backdrop — risk of an enterprise-capex and consumer-spending pullback, with retail (the largest vertical) in ongoing disruption — keeps this line vulnerable. The mechanism matters: a slowdown in new project bookings would not dent current revenue but would feed through ratable recognition into slower cloud growth in 2027 and beyond. The long-run TAM expansion thesis (supply-chain digitization, omnichannel, and now agentic AI through "Manhattan Active Agents") is intact and supportive, but it is a multi-year tailwind rather than a near-term catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

On GAAP trailing EPS of $3.60 the stock screens at ~39x, but that overstates the multiple — software companies are valued on adjusted earnings, and on forward adjusted consensus the stock at ~$139 trades at ~26x and ~23x. Against the application-software peer median P/E of 35.0 (GWRE 63.8, YOU 41.0, BSY 35.0, DOCU 28.9, PEGA 17.6), MANH is below the median and roughly in line with the higher-quality, profitable names. On that basis it is not expensive.

The honest qualifier is PEG. On ~11% next-year EPS growth, 23–26x forward implies a PEG near 2.2–2.4. That is a full multiple for the EPS growth being delivered, even for a debt-free, 34%-FCF-margin compounder with a genuine moat. The bull bridge runs through RPO: if backlog converts at 24% and the legacy drag fades, revenue and EPS re-accelerate and the multiple is justified on a 2027 view. The bear bridge is that the multiple already discounts that conversion, leaving little asymmetry if services bookings soften.

One specific catalyst could close the gap: resolution of the securities litigation. A motion-to-dismiss grant would lift an overhang that has compressed the multiple since early 2025. That is a real, dated trigger, but it is binary and could resolve in either direction. Treating cheapness alone as a buy signal would be a mistake here; the discount to peers is partly earned by the litigation, the leadership reset, and the macro-sensitive services line. The valuation supports holding the quality, not paying up for an asymmetric entry that is not yet present.

## Competitive Landscape

Manhattan competes against far larger players — Oracle, SAP, and Infor in ERP; Blue Yonder/Panasonic, E2open, Körber, Relex, and SAS in supply-chain execution and planning; Aptos, Oracle, Salesforce, and Jumpmind in point-of-sale. Its differentiation is depth in supply-chain execution and the versionless, cloud-native, zero-downtime architecture, which spares customers the upgrade burden that legacy competitors impose. RPO growth and cloud reaching 96% of software revenue are evidence that the company is winning new logos and expanding within its base, not just retaining it. The multi-year non-cancelable cloud contracts entrench the relationships once won.

The competitive trajectory is improving on the dimension that matters: the cloud transition is being executed successfully against larger but more legacy-bound rivals. The longer-term battleground is shifting to embedded and agentic AI, where Manhattan is investing through "Manhattan Active Agents." It is a consumer of third-party LLMs rather than a foundation-model developer, which keeps capital intensity low but creates dependence on hyperscaler/model pricing (cloud infrastructure obligations are ~$188.5M over five years). The risk to the moat is scale: well-capitalized incumbents consolidating the space could out-invest a $1.1B-revenue specialist over time. For now, the share and wallet evidence points to a widening, not narrowing, position.

## Risk Assessment

**Securities litigation (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** Two putative class actions (Prime, filed 2/25/2025; City of Orlando Police Pension Fund, 4/15/2025), consolidated 5/2/2025, allege false/misleading statements between July 24, 2024 and February 7, 2025, the window that ends with the late-January 2025 services/guidance disappointment and stock collapse. A derivative suit (Ayers v. Capel, 9/22/2025) names current and former officers including the prior CEO. This is a binary, near-term event with asymmetric downside if the motion to dismiss is denied.

**Macro-sensitive services and bookings.** Services and new cloud bookings depend on large discretionary capital projects with long sales cycles. With enterprise-capex and consumer-spending risk present, a bookings slowdown would compound into future cloud growth through ratable recognition.

**Leadership transition.** New CEO Eric Clark and new CFO Linda Pinne both arrived around the litigation window; longtime CEO Eddie Capel moved to a director seat. CEO comp rose to $16.7M (from ~$10.8M) including a $3.0M signing bonus and $6.5M of hire-related stock. Turnaround under new management introduces execution risk alongside the possibility of a clean-slate re-rating.

**Tax-rate and capital-return drift.** The rising effective tax rate is a structural EPS drag. Buybacks now run ahead of FCF in some quarters, drawing the cash balance down.

**Concentration and FX.** Single line of business (100% supply-chain software); large India cost base with Rupee exposure and no natural in-market revenue hedge.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options surface shows elevated stress, though it must be read carefully. Near-term ATM IV is 101.6% against 38.8% one-year historical vol, but that figure sits on the 1-day-to-expiration June 18 contract and is heavily distorted by expiry pin mechanics; the 1-month IV is 49.3% and the 6-month is 51.8%, both ordinary for a software name. The published term structure is in backwardation (near-term 101.6% > long-term 51.8%), but stripping the front-week expiry the 1m-to-6m segment is roughly flat-to-mildly-upward (49.3% to 51.8%). The more meaningful tells are the put/call volume ratio of 2.43 and the rise in put/call open-interest ratio from 0.20 to 0.53 over two weeks, alongside a 12% price decline — increased put demand consistent with hedging into the litigation ruling and a softer macro tape. The implied move on the front expiry is ±4.4%. The options market is leaning cautious near-term, which corroborates the HOLD. It is not pricing a structural collapse (the 6-month skew is only -4.0%).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The cloud transition is working, and the income statement understates it. Cloud +24% and RPO +24% are the durable forward indicators; as legacy license/maintenance (now 13% of revenue combined) fades toward zero, headline revenue re-accelerates toward the cloud rate. The business is debt-free with a 34.6% FCF margin and ~70% ROE, and the board's five-fold buyback expansion at ~$140–150 (down from $220) retires shares at sensible prices. At ~23–26x forward adjusted EPS versus a 35x peer median, a successful motion-to-dismiss grant removes the overhang and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. Bull case: **$220–240** (toward the prior sustained highs and the $240 Street high), as RPO converts and the litigation clears.

### Bear Case
The 4% headline reflects genuine demand softening, not just mix. Services budget constraints deepen with an enterprise-capex and consumer pullback, new bookings slow, and ratable recognition pushes the pain into 2027. The motion to dismiss is denied, sustaining the overhang and raising settlement risk. The rising tax rate and margin investment keep EPS growth muted, exposing the full PEG. Bear case: **$105–115** (roughly -20% to -25%), a ~20x forward multiple on flat-to-modest EPS with the litigation unresolved.

### Base Case
Cloud and RPO continue compounding in the low-to-mid 20s, services stabilizes but does not boom, the litigation resolves without catastrophic loss over the next several quarters, and the multiple holds near current levels. Base case: **~$155–165**, roughly 28–30x current-year and ~26x next-year adjusted EPS, modest upside that does not yet clear the bar for a BUY given the binary overhang.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** A positive 12-month growth thesis exists — RPO and cloud compounding at ~24%, a fortress balance sheet, and a moderated multiple — but it is gated on two binaries (litigation, macro-sensitive services) that prevent a higher-conviction call today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. The securities class-action motion to dismiss is **granted**, or the matter is resolved without a material, unquantified liability — removing the binary overhang.
2. **New cloud bookings and RPO growth hold at or above ~20%** for two consecutive quarters while services returns to positive growth, confirming the demand base through the macro test.
3. The stock trades toward **$120–125** on unchanged fundamentals, compressing forward adjusted P/E toward ~22x and restoring genuine asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Motion to dismiss is **denied** and the company signals a material settlement reserve, or guidance is cut.
2. **RPO growth decelerates below ~12%** or cloud subscription growth falls below mid-teens, indicating the demand engine is slowing.
3. Operating margin compresses below ~21% on sustained S&M deleverage without a bookings payoff.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the pending MTD ruling (argued April 9, 2026, decision expected in coming months) and the Q2 2026 earnings report. Reassess on either.

## Conclusion

Manhattan Associates is a genuinely high-quality business — debt-free, 34% FCF margins, a defensible cloud-native moat, and a backlog growing 24% — trading at a reasonable ~23–26x forward adjusted EPS that is below its peer median. In a stronger macro setting and without the litigation cloud, that combination would support a BUY, particularly given that supply-chain software sits in the one area where this framework has shown durable selection skill.

What holds the call at HOLD is concrete and falsifiable. The securities class-action motion-to-dismiss ruling is pending with unquantified potential loss, a binary that can move the stock in either direction. Services revenue, 47% of the business and already softening on customer budget constraints, is exposed to an enterprise-capex and consumer pullback that would compound through ratable recognition into future cloud growth. And at a PEG near 2.4, the multiple is full for the ~11% EPS growth being delivered, so the re-rating depends on backlog conversion and litigation lift that the numbers do not yet show. The ~37% drawdown from 2025 highs reflects these real concerns, not indiscriminate selling. The right action is to hold the quality and wait for the litigation and bookings data, with a clear path to upgrade if the MTD is granted or the stock approaches $120–125 on intact fundamentals.
