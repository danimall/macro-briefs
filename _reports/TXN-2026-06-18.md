**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Texas Instruments is the world's largest broad-based analog and embedded processing semiconductor company, and the stock is in the middle of one of the clearer fundamental inflections in large-cap semis. After a deliberate six-year capex super-cycle that depressed gross margins from 68.8% (2022) to 57.0% (2025) by loading new 300mm fabs with under-absorbed depreciation, the company is now passing the trough. Capex is guided down roughly 50% to $2-3B in 2026 from ~$4.6-4.8B/year, free cash flow margin has recovered from 9.6% (2024) to 23.6% on a trailing basis at Q1'26, and Q1'26 revenue accelerated to +19% YoY with EPS +31%. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up +19.9% for the current year and +16.4% for next year over the past 90 days, revision velocity that typically signals the market is catching up to a genuine operating turn.

The difficulty is that the stock has already priced most of this. TXN is up roughly 76% YTD in 2026 and +52.7% over the trailing year, trading at $301.88 against current-year consensus of $7.69 and next-year $8.94, about 39x and 34x forward, respectively. The 51.6x trailing multiple is meaningless at a cyclical trough, but even on forward-normalized numbers the stock is fully valued for a company guiding ~12-19% revenue growth: PEG sits well above 1.5. The mean analyst target ($293.51) is below the current price, the sell-rating count has risen to two (with no strong-sell in the most recent reporting period), and the near-term options curve is backwarded with ATM IV at 105% (91st percentile) versus 40% historical, signaling the market is pricing imminent event risk.

This is a high-quality franchise mid-recovery that has run ahead of its margin of safety. The fundamental thesis is intact and improving; the entry point is poor. We rate TXN a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: own it for the FCF re-rating that is still unfolding, but the risk/reward at $302 does not support adding.

## Company Overview

Texas Instruments generated $17.68B in revenue in 2025 across two reportable segments plus "Other":

- **Analog (79% of revenue, ~$14.0B)**: power (regulators, battery management, switches) and signal chain (amplifiers, data converters, interface, sensing). This is the profit engine at 38.6% operating margin in 2025.
- **Embedded Processing (15%, ~$2.7B)**: microcontrollers, processors, wireless connectivity, radar. Margin was depressed at 11.3% in 2025 because of the under-loaded LFAB (Lehi, Utah) fab, but it is inflecting fast: Embedded operating profit grew +205% YoY in Q1'26 as loadings rise.
- **Other (~6%, ~$979M)**: DLP, calculators, custom ASIC.

End markets after the 2025 realignment: Industrial (33%), Automotive (33%), Personal Electronics (21%), Data Center (9%), Communications (3%). Industrial, Automotive, and Data Center are the strategic growth priorities.

The competitive moat rests on four pillars TI itself articulates: in-house manufacturing scale (300mm wafers cost ~40% less than 200mm and provide "geopolitically dependable capacity"), catalog breadth (>80,000 products), direct market reach (>80% of revenue via TI.com, >100,000 customers, ~half of revenue from outside the top-50 accounts), and product/customer longevity. The business is explicitly managed for long-term free-cash-flow-per-share growth rather than revenue.

Leadership is stable: Haviv Ilan has been CEO since 2023 and is now also Chairman; CFO Rafael Lizardi is long-tenured, and all executive officers have more than five years of tenure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue cycle.** Revenue fell from $20.0B (2022) to a $15.6B trough (2024), then recovered to $17.7B (2025, +13%). Q1'26 accelerated to +19% YoY ($4.83B), and the Q2'26 guide of $5.0-5.4B implies continued double-digit growth. Industrial and data center led the Q1 reacceleration.

**Gross margin compression is cyclical and self-inflicted, and it is reversing.** Gross margin moved 68.8% → 62.9% → 58.1% → 57.0% (2022-2025). The cause is not demand or pricing collapse but fixed-cost absorption: TI built three US 300mm fabs (Sherman TX, Richardson TX, Lehi UT), and the heavy depreciation runs through cost of goods while the fabs are under-loaded. Net PP&E rose to $12.3B in 2025, with accumulated depreciation jumping to $5.7B by Q1'26 from $4.2B a year earlier. As loadings rise, the operating leverage re-engages: Q1'26 gross margin held at 58.0% while volumes grew, and Embedded operating profit more than tripled.

**The FCF inflection is the thesis.** FCF/revenue went 9.6% (2024) → 16.6% (2025) → 23.6% TTM at Q1'26. Trailing FCF rose from $1.7B to $4.4B (+154%). The mechanism: capex guided down to $2-3B in 2026 from ~$4.6-4.8B/year, with depreciation drag peaking and fabs absorbing more volume. Operating cash flow has stayed above 40% of revenue throughout the cycle, so the cash-generation engine never broke; it was the capex outflow that suppressed FCF.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Long-term debt nearly doubled from $8.2B (2022) to $13.5B (2025), funding the fab build, with interest expense up to $543M. Current ratio is 4.35, D/E ~1.13, ROE 30.7%, ROA 14.5%. The Altman Z of 2.65 sits in the "grey zone" but reflects capital intensity, not distress, which is normal for a fab-heavy manufacturer. TI returned $6.48B to shareholders in 2025 ($5.0B dividends + $1.48B buybacks), and the dividend was raised again (Q1'26 declared $1.42 vs $1.36).

One item to monitor: in 2025, dividends ($5.0B) exceeded FCF ($2.9B), with the gap bridged by debt and cash during the capex peak. This is sustainable specifically because FCF is recovering sharply; the math flips comfortably positive in 2026 as capex halves and FCF margin rises. It is the reason debt climbed, not a distress signal, but it leaves less of a cushion than the headline cash-return figure implies.

**Policy tailwind.** CHIPS Act benefits totaled $335M of cash benefit in 2025 and $555M in Q1'26 alone. Going forward, a 35% investment tax credit applies to qualifying manufacturing placed in service after Dec 31, 2025, plus up to $1.6B of direct funding for the three 300mm fabs. The OBBBA (July 2025) raises the ITC and allows expensing, and TI expects a lower effective tax rate and lower cash taxes in 2026+. This is a real, multi-hundred-million-dollar annual cash benefit that the market sometimes underweights.

**Earnings track record.** The recent surprise pattern is mixed (two beats, two misses over the last four quarters) but the most recent (Q1'26) was a large +21.6% beat ($1.68 vs $1.38 estimate), consistent with the inflection narrative. The two prior small misses (-2.8%, -1.6%) came at the cycle trough.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has three legs. First, the cyclical recovery in analog: industrial and automotive content is rebounding from a deep inventory correction, and TI's broad catalog and direct reach capture that recovery at high incremental margin. Second, the data-center tailwind: TI's data-center revenue grew ~90% YoY in Q1'26, driven by power management, signal chain, and rack power/thermal content into AI servers. This is GPU-adjacent rather than GPU-competitive; TI sells the analog content around accelerators, giving differentiated AI-infrastructure exposure without hyperscaler-capex single-point dependence. The 800V DC power transition (EVs plus data center) is a multi-year analog content driver. Third, the Embedded re-leverage as LFAB loads up, which is the highest-incremental-margin part of the portfolio today.

The estimate revision data confirms the inflection: current-quarter EPS revised +22.8% over 90 days, +19.9% for the full year, and +16.4% for next year. Combined with revenue growth above 19%, this meets the threshold of a genuine fundamental inflection rather than a single-quarter beat. The caveat is that revision momentum has flattened in the most recent 30 days (+0.0% to +0.8%), suggesting consensus has largely caught up: the 90-day re-rating is mostly behind us, not ahead.

Silicon Labs ($231/share all-cash, ~$7.5B EV, expected close 1H 2027) marks a shift toward inorganic growth after years of organic-only discipline, bolstering Embedded connectivity/IoT where TI has historically been weaker. It is strategically logical but adds debt-funded integration risk on top of an already-doubled debt load.

## Valuation Assessment

At $301.88, TXN trades at ~39x current-year consensus ($7.69) and ~34x next-year ($8.94). The 51.6x trailing P/E reflects trough earnings and should be disregarded. On the relevant forward numbers, the comparison is nuanced:

| Metric | TXN | Peer Median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (trailing/peer-reported) | 51.6x | 61.4x |
| Forward P/E (next-year consensus) | ~34x | — |
| Dividend yield | 1.86% | 1.4% |

The peer median of 61.4x is distorted by ADI (61x), MPWR (114x), and MCHP (423x, itself at a deep cyclical trough). QCOM at 22x and NXPI at 29x are the more comparable analog/mixed-signal names. On that basis, TXN at ~34x next-year is at the higher end of the peer band, justified partly by its superior franchise quality, FCF profile, and CHIPS-supported domestic manufacturing.

The problem is absolute, not relative. A company guiding ~12-19% revenue growth and ~16-20% near-term EPS growth at 34x forward carries a PEG well above 1.5; the multiple already discounts the FCF inflection. The estimate-revision-as-buy-signal logic that applies to cheap inflection stocks (forward P/E <30x or PEG <1.5) does not cleanly apply here: the stock has re-rated ~76% YTD and is no longer cheap relative to the rising estimates. The mean analyst target ($293.51) is below the current price, and the median ($300) is essentially at it; analyst targets are functioning as ceilings here, not lagging floors, the expected pattern when a stock has already run to fair value rather than being a mispriced cyclical trough.

A reasonable base case: ~36x on next-year EPS of $8.94 = ~$322, roughly 7% above current. The bull case requires both the data-center growth rate and the FCF margin to durably exceed expectations, supporting a $400 high target. The bear case (a demand stall that re-under-loads the new fabs, or China-driven analog price erosion) supports the $200 low target, ~34% below current. The asymmetry from $302 is unattractive: limited base-case upside against a material drawdown if the cyclical recovery falters or the AI-content narrative cools.

## Competitive Landscape

TI's structural position is strong and arguably strengthening on the manufacturing axis. In-house, US-centric 300mm capacity is increasingly a strategic asset as customers seek supply-chain resilience and as CHIPS incentives reward domestic production. Breadth, direct reach, and catalog longevity give durable pricing and customer-stickiness advantages in fragmented analog and embedded markets, where competitors include Analog Devices, ON Semiconductor, STMicroelectronics, Infineon, NXP, and Microchip.

The competitive trajectory is two-sided, which is why this dimension scores good-but-not-great. The clear positive is the data-center content ramp and the 300mm cost advantage. The clear negative is the structural threat from subsidized Chinese domestic analog and MCU suppliers. TI's 10-K explicitly flags China "actively promoting and reshaping its domestic semiconductor industry," and mature-node analog/MCU is precisely the segment where Chinese champions are gaining share with government support. With ~50% of TI's revenue shipped into China, this is a genuine long-term share and pricing risk in its single largest shipping geography, partially offsetting the geopolitical-capacity advantage. The net trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving, not unambiguously widening.

## Risk Assessment

**China concentration is the dominant risk.** ~20% of revenue comes from China-headquartered customers, but ~50% of revenue is from products shipped into China, an acute exposure to tariffs, export controls, and trade retaliation. The same dynamic creates the structural domestic-competition threat described above. This is the central two-sided debate for the stock and is only partially priced.

**Operating leverage cuts both ways.** The heavy fixed-cost base that is now re-leveraging margins upward would cut sharply the other way if a demand downturn re-under-loads the new fabs. Management names margin variability as a risk.

**Capex ROI.** ~$24B deployed over a decade is a multi-year bet on secular semiconductor content growth; if demand disappoints, returns on the US fabs lag.

**Capital-return cushion.** Dividends exceeded FCF in 2025, bridged by debt. This normalizes in 2026 as FCF recovers, but it leaves a thinner cushion and is the reason debt doubled. The Silicon Labs deal will be funded with additional debt on top of this.

**Governance — pay-vs-performance.** TI's 5-year cumulative TSR (121.78) materially lagged its peer group (258.38); peers more than doubled while TI roughly held flat over the measurement window, reflecting the 2023-25 trough. CEO comp stayed in the ~$19-23M range. "Comp actually paid" did decline in 2025 ($17.4M vs $25.0M), so the pay-for-performance mechanism is functioning, but the TSR gap is a legitimate shareholder concern.

**Insider activity.** All recent insider transactions are routine option-exercise-and-immediate-sale (code M→S), standard comp monetization, not discretionary selling, and not a bearish signal given the record results. There are no open-market purchases either, so management is not providing a conviction-buying signal. Director Mark Blinn fully exited (April 2026) and Director Craighead trimmed, modest negatives consistent with board rotation. The net read is neutral-to-slightly-cautious, the main drag on the Management Quality score alongside the TSR-lag governance flag.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing a near-term warning that should temper any urge to chase. Near-term ATM IV is 105.1% versus 39.7% historical volatility, a 65pp rich spread at the 91st percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (1-week 105% vs 6-month 51%), which the market uses to price imminent, specific event risk. ATM IV jumped from 47.4% to 105.1% over the past six weeks while the price rose 11.5%, so volatility is being bid up into the rally rather than fading. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (1.9%) and short interest is low (2.3% of float, 2.5 days to cover), so this is not a crowded-short or extreme-bearish setup, but the elevated near-term IV says the market expects a sharp move and is uncertain about direction. This argues against adding at $302 and favors waiting for the implied event to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The FCF inflection continues and accelerates: capex stays at $2-3B, fabs load up, gross margin re-leverages back toward the low-60s, and FCF margin sustains above 23%. The data-center analog content ramp (+90% YoY) proves durable and broad-based, driven by the 800V DC transition. CHIPS ITC and OBBBA tax benefits lower cash taxes meaningfully. FCF-per-share compounds, the dividend grows comfortably within FCF, and the market awards a premium FCF multiple. Silicon Labs closes cleanly and strengthens Embedded connectivity. EPS reaches the high-$9s/low-$10s, and the stock re-rates to ~$400.

**Bear case.** The analog recovery stalls or proves shallower than the Q1 reacceleration implied, re-under-loading the new fabs and re-compressing margins through fixed-cost deleverage. Subsidized Chinese domestic analog/MCU competition erodes share and pricing on the ~50% of revenue shipped into China. Tariff or export-control escalation hits the China shipping base. The capital-return cushion stays thin while debt rises further to fund Silicon Labs. The stock, having priced the recovery at 34x forward, de-rates toward $200 (~34% downside) as the multiple normalizes against decelerating growth.

The base case sits between: the recovery is real but largely priced, EPS reaches ~$8.94 next year, and the stock holds near the high-$290s to low-$320s, modest appreciation, with the realized return depending heavily on whether the data-center growth rate sustains the premium multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: own it for the FCF re-rating that is still unfolding, but do not add at current levels.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2'26 earnings (guided to $5.0-5.4B revenue, $1.77-2.05 EPS), which will test whether the +19% revenue acceleration and the data-center ramp are sustaining. The Silicon Labs close (1H 2027) is a longer-dated structural checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to ~$250 or below (roughly 28x next-year EPS) without fundamental deterioration, restoring a margin of safety.
- Evidence that data-center analog revenue is sustaining >50% YoY growth across multiple quarters, supporting a structurally higher growth rate and premium multiple.
- Gross margin re-leveraging back above 60% with capex confirmed in the $2-3B range, validating the FCF-per-share compounding story ahead of consensus.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revenue growth decelerating below ~5% YoY or a guidance cut, signaling the recovery has stalled and fabs are re-under-loading.
- Gross margin re-compressing below ~55% on weak loadings.
- Concrete evidence of China-driven analog price/share erosion (declining China shipping revenue with margin pressure), or a material tariff/export-control escalation.
- Debt-funded Silicon Labs financing combined with a dividend that remains above FCF into 2027.

## Conclusion

Texas Instruments is a high-quality franchise passing the trough of a deliberate capex super-cycle, with a genuine and improving FCF inflection: capex halving to $2-3B, FCF margin recovering to 23.6% TTM from 9.6%, confirmed +20% estimate revisions, and a differentiated data-center analog tailwind growing ~90% YoY. The growth and competitive fundamentals justify ownership.

The constraint is price. At $302, after a ~76% YTD run, the stock trades at ~34x next-year EPS with a PEG well above 1.5, a mean analyst target below the current price, and a near-term options curve pricing imminent stress. The recovery is largely in the price, leaving roughly 7% to a base case against ~34% downside to the bear case, unattractive asymmetry. The dividend-above-FCF dynamic and rising debt (further pressured by the Silicon Labs financing) warrant monitoring. We rate TXN a HOLD and would look to add only on a pullback toward $250 or on confirmation that the data-center growth rate is structurally elevated enough to support the premium multiple.
