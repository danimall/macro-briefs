**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ZoomInfo Technologies (recently reticketed from ZI to GTM) is a B2B go-to-market intelligence platform that generates substantial free cash flow — roughly $389M in 2025 at a ~31% margin — but is now contracting. FY2026 guidance was cut to $1.185–1.205B, a ~4% year-over-year decline at the midpoint, and net revenue retention sits at ~90%, meaning the installed base shrinks net of expansion. Management's own 10-K language attributes part of this to "AI-driven seat compression": as AI agents make individual sales reps more productive, customers reduce seat counts at renewal. That is a direct, structural mechanism eroding a seat-based revenue model, and it is the central reason the stock has fallen ~71% over the trailing year to $2.83 and ~77% from its 52-week high of $12.51.

The valuation looks superficially cheap at ~6.7x trailing earnings, well below the peer-group P/E median of 22. The reason it is not a buy is that the equity is a junior, leveraged claim. ZoomInfo carries ~$1.33B of partly variable-rate debt and a Tax Receivable Agreement obligation of roughly $2.7B — more than triple the entire $834M market cap — payable to pre-IPO owners, with change-of-control acceleration that drains cash and makes a clean take-private uneconomic. The "30% FCF margin, cheap on cash flow" thesis rests on a business that is shrinking, sits behind a multi-billion-dollar related-party payable, and faces a credible threat that LLM providers commoditize its core data asset at near-zero marginal cost.

Capital allocation compounds the concern. The company repurchased roughly $1B of stock across 2024–2025 — partly drawing on the revolver — at prices far above today's level, then added a $1B buyback authorization in February 2026 even as fundamentals deteriorated. The founder-CEO's 2025 compensation jumped to ~$33.3M (compensation actually paid ~$35.3M) in a year when indexed total shareholder return fell to 21.09 against a peer-group 258.44. Two workforce reductions within twelve months (6% in June 2025, ~20% in May 2026) signal that the upmarket "reaccelerate growth" plan has not worked and that management is now in defend-the-margin mode. We rate GTM AVOID.

## Company Overview

ZoomInfo operates a subscription database of business contact and company information used by sales, marketing, and recruiting teams, serving 35,000+ paying customers and integrating into Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics, and Marketo. Its moat is the depth, breadth, and accuracy of its data, fed partly by a "contributory network" (including the freemium ZoomInfo Lite product) where users share contact data in exchange for credits.

Management has rebranded around "GTM" (GTM Workspace, GTM Studio, Copilot) and is repositioning from a data vendor toward an "AI-ready go-to-market platform," pivoting upmarket toward enterprise customers. The corporate structure is a holding-company artifact of the Up-C IPO (ZoomInfo Technologies Inc. on top of ZoomInfo Intermediate / ZoomInfo Holdings LLC), which is the source of the Tax Receivable Agreement discussed below.

## Financial Analysis

The cash engine is genuine; the top line is not growing. Revenue progressed ~$1.10B (2022) → $1.24B (2023) → ~$1.21B (2024) → ~$1.25B (2025, roughly flat). Q1 2026 revenue of $310M (+1.5% YoY) beat guidance, but the full-year cut is the operative signal. Net revenue retention below 100% means downgrades and churn exceed expansion, so the existing book is contracting before any new logo wins.

Profitability remains intact. GAAP net income was ~$124M in 2025, adjusted operating income margin runs ~35%, and free cash flow conversion is efficient. The problem is what sits above the equity in the capital structure:

- **Debt:** First-lien facilities and senior notes, partly variable-rate, with $150M of revolver availability and restrictive covenants. Interest-rate exposure is a live headwind in a higher-for-longer environment.
- **Tax Receivable Agreement:** Owed to pre-IPO owners (85% of certain tax benefits), with present-value acceleration on change of control or early termination.
- **Goodwill:** ~$1.69B dominates the asset base. Stockholders' equity fell from ~$1.69B (2024) to ~$1.51B (2025). The 10-K explicitly names sustained stock-price and market-cap declines as a goodwill impairment trigger — a condition now realized, so a non-cash write-down is plausible in upcoming filings (it would dent book equity, not cash).

The Altman Z-Score near 0.79 nominally places GTM in the distress zone, but that reflects heavy goodwill, high leverage, and a depressed market cap rather than operating cash distress. The real risks are impairment and refinancing, not near-term insolvency.

The earnings surprise record is consistently positive — four straight beats (7.6%, 11.9%, 8.3%, 7.6%) — which lends some confidence to near-term EPS estimates. But estimate revisions are negative across the board: next-year EPS has been cut 4.6% over 90 days, current-year down 0.5%, next quarter down 1.6%. Beating a falling bar is not a growth signal.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here. The forward guide is a revenue decline, and the primary driver of the deceleration is structural rather than cyclical by management's own description. The 10-K added or sharpened several AI-related risk factors in the 2026 filing:

- **AI-driven seat compression:** As AI agents raise per-user productivity, customers reduce seat counts at renewal, hitting revenue even where the value delivered is unchanged. This is a direct mechanism for falling NRR, and management cited "AI-driven customer confusion" as a Q1 2026 headwind.
- **LLM commoditization:** Management warns that LLM providers could incorporate business contact information, company intelligence, and market insights directly into their models at "substantially lower costs… or even at no incremental cost." That threatens the economic basis of selling access to a curated data layer.
- **AIO replacing SEO:** AI-powered search answers users without routing them to websites, threatening ZoomInfo's historically large inbound-lead funnel and raising customer-acquisition costs.

Layered on top is tightening data-broker regulation, which is structurally negative for a data-centric model: California's Delete Act DROP platform launched January 1, 2026, allowing consumers to delete their data from all registered brokers in a single request; ~20 states now have comprehensive privacy laws; Virginia's HB638 proposes a $100k registration fee. Higher opt-out rates directly erode the data asset that is the differentiator.

The Markaaz AI data partnership (announced ~November 2025) is an attempt to bolster the AI narrative, but it is a data arrangement, not an acquisition, and its financial impact appears immaterial. The growth picture is a contracting base, a structural disruption vector, and a regulatory drag, with no offsetting catalyst of comparable size.

## Valuation Assessment

GTM screens far below the Interactive Media & Services peer median of 22 (QNST 10.9, YELP 10.4, CARS 22.0, TRIP 75.6, SSTK negative). A cheap multiple alone is not an investment case, and here it reflects structural impairment rather than mispricing. The equity trades cheaply because the market is pricing a contracting top line, a disruption threat the company itself flags, the debt load, and a TRA that ranks ahead of equity holders. A free-cash-flow yield on a shrinking, structurally threatened asset is a different proposition than the same yield on a stable one.

The capital-structure point is decisive for any sum-of-the-parts or take-private framing. At ~$834M of equity value plus ~$1.33B of debt, enterprise value is ~$2.16B against ~$389M of FCF — roughly 5.6x EV/FCF, which looks inexpensive until the TRA is layered in. The TRA, with change-of-control acceleration, means a sponsor contemplating an LBO inherits a liability larger than the equity check, calculated on present-value assumptions that can exceed actual cash benefits. That structurally junior position is why the equity is cheap and why the cheapness is not a catalyst.

Analyst targets span $2.50 to $15.00 (mean $5.17, median $4.00), and the recommendation mix has deteriorated — buy ratings collapsed from 4 to 0 over the past two months, with holds at 13, sells at 3, and strong sells at 4. The wide target dispersion and the high-end $15 figure reflect the bull case that AI fears are overdone and the cash flow underwrites a recovery; the cluster of sells and the median near $4 reflect the structural-decline view. We weight the latter. We assign valuation a 4.0 — the multiple is low, but the discount is a value trap absent a credible re-acceleration catalyst, and the TRA caps any acquisition optionality.

## Competitive Landscape

ZoomInfo's competitive trajectory is deteriorating. It competes with free sources (LinkedIn, company websites, government records), in-house databases, point-solution data vendors, and increasingly with CRM incumbents — Salesforce, Microsoft/LinkedIn, Oracle, HubSpot — that can bundle data into platforms customers already own and price flexibly. Generative AI can synthesize publicly available and licensed B2B data at near-zero marginal cost, which simultaneously commoditizes the data layer, compresses seat-based revenue, and disintermediates the SEO-driven lead funnel.

The contributory-network moat (data shared by users in exchange for credits) is durable in normal conditions, but it weakens if opt-out regulation raises deletion rates and if customers shrink seat counts, reducing the contribution base. The upmarket enterprise pivot is the company's response, but two RIFs in twelve months indicate it has not yet produced growth. Trajectory, not level, drives the score: a data leader losing share of value to bundled CRM platforms and to LLMs warrants a 3.0.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are large and specific, several of them not fully reflected in the already-depressed price:

- **TRA acceleration (most underappreciated):** The obligation against the market cap, with change-of-control acceleration, drains future cash, deters acquisition, and subordinates the equity to a related-party payable to pre-IPO insiders.
- **Structural AI disruption:** Seat compression and LLM data commoditization are named risk factors that map directly onto the falling NRR.
- **Goodwill impairment:** Explicitly tied to sustained market-cap decline, now realized. A write-down would not touch cash but would further reduce book equity.
- **Leverage and refinancing:** Partly variable-rate debt with covenants in a higher-for-longer rate environment.
- **Governance and material-weakness history:** A prior internal-control material weakness was disclosed; classified board, supermajority provisions, founder designation rights, and a relatively junior internally-promoted CFO (made permanent only in August 2025) compound the concern.
- **Delisting / reverse-split risk:** At ~$2.83, a sustained sub-$3 (or sub-$1 on any further leg down) price raises a practical listing-standard concern, not yet confirmed in filings.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 69.3% versus 1-year historical volatility of 65.9% — modestly rich, with the implied move on the nearest expiry at ±12.5%. The put/call skew of 13.1% indicates elevated put demand, consistent with bearish positioning, and short interest is 16.3% of float at 3.0 days to cover. The term structure is in contango (long-dated vol 85.6% > near-term), which reads as relative near-term complacency against a more uncertain six-month horizon. The P/C open-interest ratio collapsed from 4.62 to 0.05 since mid-May while the price fell 28.7%; the heavy protective put structure that existed before the guidance cut has rolled off, so the options market is no longer pricing an imminent acute catalyst even as the fundamental decline continues. This is a slow-bleed setup, not a single-event collapse.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The business throws off ~$389M of FCF at a ~31% margin and trades at ~6.7x trailing earnings and ~5.6x EV/FCF before the TRA. If AI fears are overdone and the upmarket pivot stabilizes NRR back toward 100%, the cash flow underwrites a sharp re-rating; the $15 high target implies multiples of the current price. A director's open-market purchase of 27,500 shares at ~$3.60 in May 2026 is a small vote of confidence near the lows. Aggressive buybacks at depressed prices could shrink the share count meaningfully. Bull target: $7–8.

### Bear Case
NRR at ~90% plus an FY revenue-decline guide signal a structurally shrinking base, and management's own seat-compression language suggests the decline accelerates as AI adoption deepens. LLM commoditization and tightening data-broker regulation erode the core data asset over multiple years. The TRA subordinates the equity and forecloses a clean take-private rescue. Continued buybacks funded partly by debt, plus the CEO package amid ~80% relative underperformance, point to weak capital discipline. A goodwill impairment further dents book equity. Bear target: $1.75–2.00.

The base case is continued slow erosion: low-single-digit annual revenue decline, FCF gradually compressing as the base shrinks, no near-term catalyst to reverse NRR, and the equity range-bound to lower as the TRA and leverage cap any upside. We see fair value around $2.50–3.00, roughly the current price, with asymmetric downside if the AI-driven decline proves structural.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** There is no single dated event likely to drive a sharp leg down. The fundamental deterioration (declining revenue, ~90% NRR, AI seat compression) is gradual and already partly priced. The next earnings report and any goodwill impairment disclosure are incremental rather than binary catalysts. The most concrete near-term events are the next quarterly print (likely August 2026) and any further guidance revision.

**Timing window:** Diffuse. The bear thesis plays out over 6–18 months as renewals reset seat counts lower; there is no near-dated forcing event to compress the carry.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest is 16.3% of float at 3.0 days to cover — elevated but not extreme. The buyback authorization (~$1.1B+ remaining) is a live source of demand that could squeeze the stock on any positive surprise, and a $1B+ authorization against an $834M market cap is a meaningful overhang for shorts.

**Borrow and options signal:** Put skew and rich near-term IV confirm a bearish lean, but the collapse of the protective put OI ratio indicates the acute downside positioning has already unwound; the options market is not pricing an imminent catalyst. The ±12.5% near-term implied move is modest. Carrying a short here means paying for time without a trigger.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear target ~$1.75–2.00 (roughly 29–38% downside) against squeeze risk to $7–8 on any stabilization or buyback-driven move (well over 100% upside risk). That is worse than 2:1 against the short. The active-buyback overhang and absence of a dated catalyst make this unattractive to short despite the weak fundamentals.

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE** — the business is structurally challenged but slowly declining, not acutely collapsing; there is no dated catalyst, the buyback authorization creates real squeeze risk, and the risk/reward favors avoiding rather than shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria:** We would reconsider on (1) net revenue retention recovering above 95%, signaling the seat-compression mechanism has stabilized; (2) a credible resolution or quantification of the TRA that removes the change-of-control overhang and subordination concern; (3) evidence the upmarket enterprise pivot is producing net new ARR growth rather than two consecutive workforce reductions; or (4) the stock pricing in liquidation-grade pessimism (sub-$2) while FCF holds near $350M+, which could create a genuine cash-on-cash floor even within a declining business.

**Timeline:** The AI-driven and regulatory headwinds are structural and multi-year; we expect the revenue-decline and NRR-pressure backdrop to persist through at least FY2026–FY2027. We would reassess at the next quarterly print and on any 8-K addressing the TRA, a goodwill impairment, or further guidance revision.

## Conclusion

ZoomInfo is a cash-generative business whose equity is the wrong place to express that fact. The free cash flow is real, but it accrues behind the debt load and a Tax Receivable Agreement that exceeds the market cap more than threefold, and it is generated by a top line that management now guides to shrink with net revenue retention stuck below 100%. The company itself names AI-driven seat compression and LLM data commoditization as risk factors, which converts the cheap multiple from a value opportunity into a value-trap warning absent a recovery catalyst. Capital allocation — buybacks above current prices, a fresh authorization, and the CEO package in a year of ~80% relative underperformance — undermines confidence that the cash will be deployed in shareholders' interest. The thesis is not shortable given the absence of a dated catalyst and the buyback overhang, but it is one to avoid owning. We rate GTM AVOID.
