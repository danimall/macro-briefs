## Executive Summary

Yelp's Q2 2026 report resolved the central question that has hung over the stock for two years: whether rising cost-per-click could continue to offset falling ad clicks. It cannot. In FY2025, ad clicks fell 7% while average CPC rose 10%, producing +3% advertising revenue growth. In Q2 2026, clicks fell 5% and CPC rose just 1%, and the 10-Q attributes even that 1% to Services taking a larger share of mix, disclosing "modest decreases in average CPC in Services categories." On a like-for-like basis, both volume and price are now declining. Total advertising revenue fell 3%; Services advertising, the entire growth thesis, grew 0.07% year over year ($240.963M versus $240.796M); Restaurants, Retail & Other fell 10% and has now declined for roughly two and a half years.

Against that, the balance sheet and the cash flow statement remain the bull case. Enterprise value of approximately $1.393B ($1.387B equity + $100M drawn revolver − $94.1M cash) against LTM adjusted EBITDA of $354.5M is 3.9x. LTM free cash flow of $297.4M is a 21.4% yield on market capitalization; normalizing the cash-tax benefit (cash taxes paid were $6.56M in the first half of 2026 versus a $25.0M book provision, as Yelp consumes deferred tax assets that fell from $116.1M to $91.6M) implies a durable figure closer to $260-280M, still an 18.7-20.2% yield. Gross leverage is roughly 0.28x against a 3.75x covenant ceiling, so the structure does not face a refinancing wall.

The problem is that the cash flow does not currently reach shareholders. Management is pausing repurchases "through the remainder of 2026" to repay a revolver drawn at 5.30%, after spending $291.9M in FY2025 at an average $33.29 per share (23% above today's price) and $271.2M cash plus $30.0M of retention expense on Hatch, an asset that was 81.3% goodwill on allocation. The combination of a structurally shrinking core, negative estimate revisions (next-quarter consensus cut 12.6% in 30 days), no capital return until 2027, and a capital-allocation record that has converted cash into goodwill and above-market repurchases produces roughly zero probability-weighted expected return from $25.56. We rate the shares REDUCE, a step toward neutral from two prior AVOID ratings; the licensing evidence is genuine and the leverage is not a threat, but the shares are not yet a fair-return proposition.

## Company Overview

Yelp operates a single-segment local commerce platform monetized almost entirely through performance advertising: 95% of FY2025 revenue, with 99.5% of Q2 2026 revenue ($373.5M of $375.5M) generated in the United States. The core asset is a corpus of 330.2M cumulative reviews as of 12/31/2025 (+7% year over year), of which 300.3M appear on business pages, plus 8.392M active claimed business locations (+8%). Headcount was 5,168 at year-end, fully remote since 2022.

Revenue splits three ways. Services advertising (home services, auto, professional) was $947.6M in FY2025, +8%. Restaurants, Retail & Other was $443.7M, −6%. Other revenue — subscription products (Yelp Guest Manager, Yelp Host, Yelp Receptionist, Hatch), data licensing (Places API, Insights API, AI API) and transactions — was $73.7M, +17%.

Pricing is set by a CPC auction whose algorithms, per the 10-K, are designed to "maximize ad clicks" and dynamically reallocate advertiser budgets around predicted traffic weakness. Revenue is therefore clicks × CPC, and Yelp controls neither the click supply (organic search traffic) nor advertiser budgets. Distribution runs through Local (still nearly half of advertising revenue, businesses with fewer than ten locations), Multi-location, Self-serve, and resale partners including Thryv. Off-platform, Yelp syndicates to Facebook and Bing, partners with DoorDash on transactions, and licenses APIs to Apple (Maps and Siri), Audi, BMW, Sprinklr and Chatmeter.

The 10-K names Google as "our primary competitor," alongside TripAdvisor, TikTok, Instagram, Reddit, DoorDash, OpenTable, Resy, Angi and Thumbtack, and concedes competitors have "greater name recognition, longer operating histories, substantially greater market share."

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 versus first-half-2026 discontinuity is the file's most important fact.**

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | 6M-2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,412.1M | $1,465.0M | $737.0M (+1%) |
| Net income | $132.9M | $145.6M | $49.4M (−28%) |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $358.0M | $369.2M | $170.8M (−8%) |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | 25.4% | 25.2% | 23.2% |
| Operating cash flow | $285.8M | $372.0M | $133.6M |
| Free cash flow | $248.5M | $323.7M | $106.2M (−20%) |

Revenue grew 1% while net income fell 28% and adjusted EBITDA fell 8%. The GAAP gap is instructive because it opened *against* a tailwind: stock-based compensation fell from $34.8M to $25.7M in Q2, a $9.0M GAAP benefit, yet net income still fell $12.4M. The bridge: amortization step-up (Hatch added $55.0M of intangibles over a 4.9-year weighted life, ~$11.2M annually, on top of RepairPal's $53.6M; D&A rose 24% in FY2025 to $50.1M); $3.5M of Q2 acquisition and integration costs ($7.9M for six months); sublease income collapsing from $3.662M to $724K as legacy office subleases roll off; interest income reversal as cash plus securities fell from $319.4M to $94.1M with the entire securities portfolio liquidated, now compounded by interest expense on $100M at 5.30%; and a 33.6% first-half effective tax rate ($25.0M on $74.4M pretax) versus FY2025's 28.6% and management's own 22-26% guide, driven by $8.4M of discrete items tied to equity-compensation tax shortfalls and interest on uncertain tax positions.

That last item deserves emphasis because it is self-reinforcing. When equity vests below grant-date value — all 1.832M outstanding options are underwater at a $36.90 strike with zero aggregate intrinsic value at 6/30/2026, and 6.374M nonvested RSUs/PRSUs carry a $33.82 average grant-date fair value — Yelp books tax-deduction shortfalls that mechanically raise the effective rate. The falling share price is depressing reported EPS and simultaneously weakening retention through underwater equity, at the moment the company needs an AI product pivot executed.

**Balance sheet.** Six months transformed a fortress into an adequate structure. Cash fell from $216.1M to $94.1M, marketable securities from $103.3M to zero, current ratio from 2.99 to 1.75. Goodwill rose from $135.8M to $354.7M and net intangibles from $49.0M to $92.1M; combined, $446.8M is 45% of total assets and 69% of stockholders' equity ($644.5M). The revolver went from undrawn to $100M. Sources and uses reconcile: OCF $133.6M plus a $100M net draw funded $263.6M of net acquisition outlay and $174.0M of cash buybacks, with the ~$225M liquidity drawdown covering the balance.

Covenants are not the binding constraint. The $325M facility (expiring 4/28/2028, Wells Fargo administrative agent) requires total leverage ≤3.75x (up to 4.25x for a period after significant acquisitions) and interest coverage ≥3.00x. At $100M drawn against ~$354.5M LTM adjusted EBITDA, gross leverage is ~0.28x; net debt is ~$5.9M. Yelp was in compliance with $220.7M available. The facility is secured by first-priority liens on substantially all domestic assets, including certain domestic intellectual property.

**Free cash flow quality.** LTM FCF of $297.4M is down ~8% from FY2025's $323.7M, and first-half FCF fell 20% year over year. Two distortions flatter 2026: the $18M-plus cash-tax gap described above, and the absence of the interest income that previously supported other income. We treat $260-280M as the normalized annualized figure and would not annualize any single quarter.

**Earnings surprise history is not a reliable confidence input here.** Q2 adjusted EPS of $0.62 against a reported $0.40 consensus (a 55.9% "beat" in the surprise table) is anomalous for a company with this profile and most likely reflects thin or stale sell-side estimates, as only four to eight analysts contribute to the annual lines. The economically relevant comparison is that adjusted EPS of $0.62 declined roughly 7% from $0.67 a year earlier *despite* a 13.8% reduction in share count, meaning underlying earnings fell far more than 7%. The market read it that way: on 8/7/2026 the stock opened at $27.11, traded to $29.23, and closed at $25.56, a 12.6% intraday round trip in which the initial reaction to the headline beat was fully faded on 2.13M shares versus 1.15M average volume.

## Growth Analysis

The click/price decomposition reconciles cleanly and tells the whole story:

| Period | Ad clicks | Average CPC | Advertising revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | +6% | 0% | — |
| FY2025 | −7% | +10% | +3% |
| Q4 2025 | −8% | +6% | −2% |
| Q2 2026 | −5% | +1% | −3% |

FY2025: 0.93 × 1.10 = 1.023 against a reported +3%. Q2 2026: 0.95 × 1.01 = 0.960 against a reported −3%. For two years, price absorbed volume contraction. That capacity is exhausted.

**FY2026's apparent stabilization is acquired.** The FY2025 10-K warned full-year 2026 revenue "may be slightly down." Current guidance is reported at approximately $1.47B — a figure sourced only to secondary reporting (StockStory, 2026-08-06) that we could not verify against a company document and treat as unconfirmed. Taking it at face value: $1,470M less first-half actual $737.0M implies second-half revenue of ~$733M against H2-2025 actual of $736.0M, or −0.4%. Hatch contributed $14.1M in the first half from a February 2 close and had a ~$35M annual run rate in June, implying ~$18-19M in the second half and ~$32-33M for the year. Organic FY2026 therefore approximates $1,437M versus $1,465M, roughly −1.9%, with organic second-half revenue near −3%. The direction is independently confirmed by the 10-Q's primary-source statement that Q3 revenue will "decrease slightly" year over year alongside a "year-over-year decrease in adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter."

FY2025's Services strength was itself partly acquired: the 10-K discloses that RepairPal (November 2024, $80.0M) contributed nearly two-thirds of the year-over-year increase in Services paying advertising locations and roughly two percentage points of total advertising revenue growth.

**Two genuine positives.** First, advertiser attrition is moderating: total paying advertising locations were 496K in Q4 2025 (−5% year over year) but 510K in Q2 2026 (−1%), with Services at 259K (flat) and RR&O at 251K (−2%). Second, the licensing and subscription pivot is producing measurable results. Other revenue grew 98% in Q2 to $33.024M. The 10-Q states that data licensing demand was "robust," including from a partnership with OpenAI, that Yelp ratings and reviews began powering ChatGPT's local experience, and that a Request-a-Quote integration with ChatGPT will launch "in the coming weeks." Yelp Host reached a 2.4M annualized call run rate in July 2026, more than tripling from January. Yelp Assistant drove ~10% of all Request-a-Quote projects, with total RAQ projects up ~10%.

**But the arithmetic of the pivot is unforgiving.** Other revenue must grow from $73.7M to a $250M run rate by end-2028 — roughly +$59M per year. Advertising, at $1,391M in FY2025 and declining ~3%, sheds ~$40M per year. Net growth on a $1,465M base is therefore ~1.2% annually *if the target is achieved in full*. If advertising decline accelerates to −6%, total revenue shrinks even with the licensing target met. Hatch's $35M ARR is ~14% of the $250M target, so the target is substantially dependent on further acquisition or on organic subscription scaling that has not yet been demonstrated at size.

**Estimate revisions confirm the direction.** Current-quarter consensus fell 5.0% in 30 days, next quarter 12.6%, next year 2.6% over 90 days. Current-year consensus of $1.89 against trailing EPS of $2.24 implies a ~16% decline in 2026; the 2027 consensus of $2.53 implies a 34% rebound that requires buyback resumption, tax-rate normalization and revenue stabilization simultaneously. That 2027 estimate is doing a great deal of work.

## Valuation Assessment

**Reproducible multiples at $25.56:**

- Market capitalization: 54,263,621 × $25.56 = **$1.387B**. (The profile field of 54.99M shares implies $1.406B; we use the 7/31/2026 filed count.)
- Enterprise value: $1.387B + $100.0M − $94.1M = **$1.393B**
- LTM (H2-2025 + H1-2026): revenue $1,473.0M; adjusted EBITDA $354.5M; net income $126.5M; FCF $297.4M
- **EV/LTM adjusted EBITDA: 3.9x** | **P/LTM FCF: 4.7x** | **LTM FCF yield: 21.4%** | **Normalized FCF yield: 18.7-20.2%**
- Trailing P/E 11.4x; current-year consensus $1.89 → 13.5x; next-year consensus $2.53 → 10.1x

**Peer context.** The stated Interactive Media & Services median P/E of 22.0 is not usable: TripAdvisor at 75.6x sits on depressed earnings and Shutterstock is loss-making. The informative comparables are ZoomInfo at 6.7x and QuinStreet at 10.9x. ZoomInfo matters most — it is the other structurally challenged proprietary-data business in the set, and it trades at a *lower* multiple than Yelp while sitting 73% below its 52-week high. When a data asset's distribution is disintermediated, the multiple does not find a floor at 11x. Every name in the peer set is 14-73% below its 52-week high, indicating a sector-wide derating of small-cap internet on AI-disruption grounds rather than a Yelp-specific dislocation.

**What the current price implies.** On normalized FCF of $270M and a 12% cost of equity, a perpetuity model solves as follows: 3% annual FCF decline → $1.80B, or ~$33/share; 5% decline → $1.59B, ~$29; 8% decline → $1.35B, ~$25. At $25.56 the market is pricing roughly a 7-8% perpetual decline in free cash flow. That is a demanding assumption, and it is the strongest argument for the shares.

We do not translate it into a high valuation score because the discount-rate framing assumes cash flow accrues to shareholders, which it currently does not. Repurchases are suspended through 2026 and expected to resume in 2027; there is no dividend. The two most recent large capital deployments were $291.9M of FY2025 repurchases at an average $33.29 (23% above today's price) and $301M all-in for an asset recorded 81.3% as goodwill. Between now and 2027, the ~20% FCF yield is a corporate statistic rather than a shareholder return, and the reinvestment risk is material given the stated strategy of "pursuing strategic acquisitions through a combination of cash and financing."

We score valuation 5.8: the absolute cheapness is genuine and bounds downside, but this has the structure of a compressed multiple on an impaired franchise without a near-term mechanism to convert cheapness into return.

Analyst targets bracket the price at $21 low, $32 high, $26.86 mean and $27.00 median — approximately fair, with the recommendation distribution (2 buy, 6 hold, 1 sell, 1 strong sell) unchanged for four months. On a name with falling estimates and no premium multiple, we treat consensus targets as neither a floor nor independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Yelp's business converts free organic search traffic into monetizable ad clicks. When Google answers a query in-page, the click never occurs, and an auction designed to "maximize ad clicks" has nothing to auction.

The 10-K states directly that Google AI Overviews and AI Mode "reduced the prominence of links to our platform and may be having a negative impact on our traffic." Traffic metrics corroborate: FY2025 app unique devices 28.009M (−2%), desktop 37.399M (−6%), mobile web 59.729M (−7%), with the 10-K noting traffic remains below pre-pandemic 2019 levels. (Desktop methodology was revised in 2025 to exclude home-page-only visits with 2024 restated, so that series is not comparable to older disclosures.) Externally, AI Overviews now appear in roughly 43% of searches per July 2026 reporting, and multiple large publishers are weighing blocking Google's crawlers over AI-driven traffic loss.

Apple is a second, already-realized loss: the 10-K states that traffic and app downloads driven by Apple Maps decreased following changes to how Yelp content displays in Apple Maps. Apple simultaneously pays to license Yelp Places API data.

Yelp also discloses discovering "AI-powered products using content originally from our platform without our permission," while conceding it has "only limited patent protection for our core business." The moat is a data asset with a weak legal perimeter.

The strategic response — licensing the corpus to AI search providers — has genuine merit and is being executed (OpenAI live, ChatGPT local experience powered, RAQ integration imminent). But it trades owned-and-operated advertising economics, where Yelp controls the auction and captures high margin, for negotiated fees with counterparties of far greater bargaining power. Economic terms of the OpenAI agreement are not disclosed in any filing, and licensing revenue is not broken out from the $33.024M Other line. There is also a plausible path where OpenAI, having licensed the content, builds an advertising layer and becomes a competitor as well as a licensee; that specific claim rests on a single speculative press report and should not be treated as established.

A further tension sits in the filings unresolved. Yelp discloses that its 2024 paid-search initiative to acquire Services projects raised project volume and clicks at lower CPCs but "did not drive our desired returns in advertiser retention or ad budget increases," and the 2026 plan nonetheless calls for *increased* paid traffic acquisition, substantially purchased from the company it is suing. Either the funnel has been redesigned so acquired traffic now converts (Yelp Assistant at ~10% of RAQ projects, RAQ up ~10%, paying locations improving to −1%), or organic decline has removed the alternative. The disclosures do not distinguish.

There is also a low-concentration/high-concentration distinction. No individual customer represented 10% or more of consolidated revenue, and credit quality is improving (allowance for credit losses down to $11.8M from $13.8M; six-month write-offs $19.955M versus $23.485M). That is genuine advertiser diversification. It does not diversify traffic sourcing, which remains extremely concentrated on Google.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill and deferred tax assets.** Goodwill of $354.7M — 81.3% of it created in the February 2026 Hatch transaction — sits against $644.5M of book equity and a $1.387B market capitalization. The last annual test (8/31/2025) found no impairment and the 10-Q identified no triggering events through 6/30/2026, but the 10-K explicitly ties impairment risk to "declines in our stock price or market capitalization." Separately, net deferred tax assets of $115.5M at year-end ($91.6M non-current at 6/30/2026) carry explicit valuation-allowance language; sustained deterioration in profitability projections could force a non-cash charge. Uncertain tax positions total $53.8M.

**Revenue visibility.** Short-term deferred revenue was $8.573M and remaining performance obligations $74.1M (~5% of annual revenue), with the RPO figure explicitly excluding contracts with original terms of one year or less. Neither figure represents contracted backlog. There is essentially no contractual cushion against advertiser churn.

**Regulatory.** EU Digital Services Act penalties reach 6% of worldwide turnover and UK Online Safety Act penalties 10%; erosion of CDA 230 protections would raise moderation liability for a business whose core asset is user-generated content.

**Retention.** All outstanding options are underwater; nonvested RSUs/PRSUs carry a $33.82 average grant value against $25.56. The 10-K independently flags a compensation shift from equity toward cash and the risk that cash compensation is less effective at retention. Unrecognized SBC is $192.4M over ~2.5 years.

**Unresolved items.** $5.3M of the RepairPal holdback is retained through 12/31/2026 pending resolution of claims by Yelp, with ~$5.0M of indemnifiable legal expenses incurred in 2025 (of which $3.5M was recovered from the holdback); nature and magnitude are undisclosed. Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (4/29/2026, 6/10/2026) covering officer or director changes were not read; a CTO transition is inferred from Form 4 patterns but unverified. Hatch purchase accounting remains preliminary.

**Short interest.** 26.9% of float short, 9.04M shares, 6.5 days to cover. This cuts both ways: it signals that a substantial cohort of informed capital holds the structural-impairment view, and it makes any positive surprise mechanically violent. It also makes active shorting unattractive at this level, which is one reason we prefer REDUCE to a full AVOID.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 62.5% against 43.3% trailing realized volatility is rich by 19 points, with the term structure in backwardation (62.5% at 12 days, 54.7% at 40 days, 50.8% at 103 days), so the market is pricing near-term stress rather than a distant catalyst. Put/call volume ratio of 3.81 (4.60 on the August 21 tenor) with elevated put demand in the skew, against a more balanced put/call open interest ratio of 1.20, indicates fresh directional put buying layered on top of a modestly put-tilted existing book rather than a long-standing hedge. The ±10.4% implied move on the August 21 expiry, arriving *after* the August 7 print, is unusual and consistent with continued disagreement about the trajectory rather than resolved uncertainty. Options positioning corroborates the fundamental caution; it does not by itself drive the rating.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $34, +33%)

The licensing pivot works and the market has mispriced a stabilizing annuity. Paying locations have already improved from −5% to −1% year over year, indicating advertiser churn has bottomed; with churn stabilized, CPC pricing power partially returns. Other revenue compounds toward the $250M end-2028 target as OpenAI's ChatGPT local experience, the Request-a-Quote integration, Yelp Host (2.4M annualized calls, tripled since January) and Hatch ($35M ARR) scale, while SBC falls toward the stated sub-6%-of-revenue goal by end-2027, expanding margins. Revenue returns to ~+3% ($1,510M) with adjusted EBITDA margin recovering to 24.5% ($370M); the multiple re-rates to 5.0x EV/EBITDA as the terminal-decline assumption is relaxed, giving EV of $1,850M and roughly $34 per share. Buybacks resume in 2027 at a ~20% FCF yield against 26.9% short interest, and the Google antitrust case (N.D. Cal., trial September 2028) becomes a free option.

### Bear Case (target $18, −30%)

AI Overviews expand beyond 43% of searches, click supply contracts faster, and the pricing offset stays absent. Advertising declines 5-8% annually; licensing fees prove smaller and less durable than owned-and-operated ad economics because Yelp has no bargaining power over Google, Apple or OpenAI. Revenue falls to ~$1,400M with adjusted EBITDA margin compressing to 20.5% ($287M) as paid traffic acquisition costs rise. The multiple compresses toward ZoomInfo's 6.7x P/E territory — 3.5x EV/EBITDA gives EV of $1,005M, equity of ~$999M, or ~$18.40. A goodwill impairment against $354.7M, and/or a deferred-tax valuation allowance, adds a non-cash but sentiment-relevant charge. Management deploys 2027's cash into another Hatch-scale acquisition rather than repurchases.

### Base Case (target $26.50, +3.7%)

Revenue holds roughly flat at ~$1,470M on acquired contribution against ~−2% organic; adjusted EBITDA margin settles at ~23% ($338M); the multiple stays near 4.3x, giving EV of $1,453M and ~$26.65 per share. Probability-weighting base at 50%, bull at 20% and bear at 30% yields an expected return of approximately −0.4% — effectively zero, with a 30% downside tail against a 33% upside tail. That distribution does not compensate for the absence of capital return through 2026.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Trim existing exposure into strength rather than at the current print; the 26.9% short interest and rich near-term implied volatility make squeeze risk real, and the August 7 intraday high of $29.23 demonstrates how quickly this name can gap. Target de-risking on rallies into the $28-30 range, which corresponds to the 4.7-5.0x EV/EBITDA the bull case requires. Residual exposure, if retained, should be sized as a small position held for the 2027 buyback resumption and the licensing optionality, not as a core holding.

**Base case target: $26.50** (4.3x EV/EBITDA on $338M adjusted EBITDA, less $6M net debt, on 54.3M shares) — +3.7% from $25.56.
**Bull case target: $34** — +33%.
**Bear case target: $18** — −30%.
**Timeframe:** reassess at the Q3 2026 report (early November 2026), where the 10-Q has already pre-announced a year-over-year revenue decline and a year-over-year adjusted EBITDA decline.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or better:**
1. Ad clicks decline narrowing to −2% or better *with* average CPC growth of +3% or better on a like-for-like basis (not mix), which would demonstrate the auction has regained pricing power.
2. Services advertising returning to positive year-over-year growth on an organic basis, with paying locations flat-to-positive.
3. Management resuming repurchases earlier than 2027, or disclosing quantified data-licensing revenue and contract duration that supports the $250M Other-revenue target without further acquisition.
4. Clustered open-market insider buying — currently absent across 25 Form 4 filings from March to August 2026, with the CEO's 10b5-1 plan sized at approximately his full reported direct holding of 938,934 shares.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Advertising revenue decline accelerating beyond −6% year over year, or adjusted EBITDA margin falling below 21%.
2. A goodwill impairment charge against the $354.7M balance, or establishment of a valuation allowance against the $91.6M non-current deferred tax asset.
3. Another acquisition of Hatch scale or larger funded with cash and revolver while repurchases remain suspended.
4. Normalized free cash flow falling below $230M, which would take the yield below 17% and remove the principal support for the shares.

**Reassessment timeline:** next natural decision point is the Q3 2026 report in early November 2026, with the August 21 options expiry as a nearer-term volatility marker.

## Conclusion

Yelp presents a legitimate tension rather than a straightforward short. The cash generation is real (~$260-280M normalized, an 18.7-20.2% yield), leverage is negligible (0.28x gross, ~$6M net debt against $220.7M of undrawn revolver), the licensing pivot is producing verifiable results (Other revenue +98%, OpenAI live, Host calls tripled), and advertiser attrition has demonstrably moderated (paying locations −1% versus −5% two quarters earlier). At 3.9x EV/EBITDA the market already discounts roughly a 7-8% perpetual decline in free cash flow.

What tips the balance to REDUCE is the conjunction of three facts. The mechanism that sustained the top line — CPC inflation absorbing click contraction — has stopped working, and Services advertising growth has reached zero. Reported FY2026 stability is acquired, with organic revenue running roughly −2% and management's own 10-Q guiding Q3 revenue and adjusted EBITDA lower year over year. And the ~20% free cash flow yield does not reach shareholders until 2027 at the earliest, in a company whose last two large capital deployments were repurchases at an average $33.29 and $301M all-in for an 81%-goodwill asset, with no insider having committed new capital at these prices.

Probability-weighted expected return is approximately zero against a 30% downside tail. Existing holders should use rallies toward $28-30 to reduce; prospective buyers should wait for either evidence that like-for-like CPC has stabilized alongside a narrowing click decline, or a price in the high teens where the terminal-decline assumption becomes conservative rather than merely plausible.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Q2 2026 EPS consensus was $0.40 (not $0.32), so the "beat" magnitude, while still large, is somewhat less anomalous than portrayed — but this does not change the direction of the thesis.
