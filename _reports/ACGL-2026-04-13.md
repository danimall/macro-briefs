## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group is a Bermuda-domiciled S&P 500 specialty insurer/reinsurer with approximately $26.9 billion in capital, operating across three segments: Insurance (47% of net premiums written), Reinsurance (46%), and Mortgage (6%). The company generated $4.4 billion in net income in 2025 on $19.9 billion in revenue, compounding book value per share at 22.6% during the year to $65.11. Free cash flow reached $6.1 billion, yielding a 30.7% FCF margin. The stock trades at approximately $96.28, or roughly 1.48x book value and ~10.2x forward earnings.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a structural investment income floor of $1.625 billion annually from a $47.4 billion asset base, which will persist or grow in the current rate environment; (2) the mortgage segment's extraordinary profitability (14.6% combined ratio) providing a countercyclical earnings buffer; and (3) disciplined capital return, with $1.9 billion in buybacks executed in 2025 and management signaling willingness to return nearly 100% of generated capital if growth opportunities are limited. The primary risks are Bermuda's new 15% corporate income tax (effective tax rate nearly doubled from 7.7% to 14.7%), softening property catastrophe reinsurance rates (down 10-20% at January 2026 renewals), and catastrophe event tail risk. These are meaningful but quantifiable headwinds against a company delivering 20%+ ROE with a conservatively leveraged balance sheet.

## Company Overview

Arch Capital Group Ltd. writes specialty property & casualty insurance and reinsurance globally, with operations in the U.S., Bermuda, U.K., Europe, Canada, and Australia. The company's three-segment structure is a key differentiator:

- **Insurance Segment** (~47% NPW): Specialty P&C lines including property, commercial auto, workers' compensation, commercial multi-peril, and other liability. North America accounts for approximately 73% of segment premiums. The MCE Acquisition (U.S. MidCorp and Entertainment businesses from Allianz, closed August 2024) expanded this segment meaningfully.

- **Reinsurance Segment** (~46% NPW): Property catastrophe, casualty, specialty, and marine/aviation reinsurance written worldwide. This segment produced the highest underwriting income in 2025 despite California wildfire losses.

- **Mortgage Segment** (~6% NPW): U.S. primary mortgage insurance, credit risk transfer (CRT), and international mortgage insurance/reinsurance. Arch MI is one of six private mortgage insurers approved by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.

The company was added to the IAIG (Internationally Active Insurance Groups) list in August 2024, subjecting it to additional regulatory oversight. Market capitalization is approximately $34.5 billion.

The balance sheet is conservatively leveraged. The 10.1% debt-to-capital ratio is among the lowest in the industry. The Altman Z-Score of 0.95 is irrelevant for an insurance company — the model was designed for manufacturing firms and penalizes the liability structures (loss reserves, unearned premiums) that are core to an insurer's business model. The Piotroski F-Score of 4/4 is a more appropriate quality signal.

### Capital Allocation

Arch repurchased $1.9 billion of common shares in 2025, retiring approximately 5.6% of shares outstanding. Since inception, the company has repurchased 455 million shares for $7.8 billion. The remaining authorization was $1.1 billion at year-end 2025. Management has stated explicitly that if growth opportunities remain limited in 2026, they may distribute nearly 100% of generated capital to shareholders. With $6.1 billion in annual FCF, this implies continued aggressive buybacks in the $1.5-2.0 billion range.

### Earnings Quality

Four consecutive quarterly beats averaging 14.2% surprise magnitude. The most recent quarter (Q4 2025) beat by 12.7% ($2.98 vs. $2.64 estimate). This consistent outperformance increases confidence in the $9.40 current-year consensus estimate, which itself has been revised upward by 0.8% over the past 90 days. Favorable prior-year reserve development of $600 million in 2025 ($43M insurance + $322M reinsurance + $235M mortgage) was a meaningful contributor. The sustainability of reserve releases is a legitimate question — as benign pre-2020 accident years are exhausted, this tailwind may decelerate, though the mortgage segment's releases are driven by secular trends in housing credit quality (weighted average FICO 749).

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Drivers

1. **Net Investment Income:** With the Fed rate path likely to remain elevated (3.64%+ SOFR with rate hike probabilities rising, not declining), Arch's investment portfolio will continue generating substantial float income. Each 25bp change in portfolio yield translates to roughly $119 million in annual income.

2. **Insurance Segment Expansion:** The MCE Acquisition added U.S. MidCorp and Entertainment lines, driving 13.4% insurance NPW growth. Integration costs ($75M transaction costs, $193M intangible amortization in 2025) will dissipate, improving the segment's contribution.

3. **Mortgage Segment Stability:** $1.0 billion of underwriting income from this segment has been delivered for four consecutive years. The in-force portfolio's credit quality (749 FICO, 2.17% delinquency) is strong.

### Growth Headwinds

1. **Reinsurance Rate Softening:** Property catastrophe and short-tail excess-of-loss rates declined 10-20% at January 2026 renewals. Reinsurance NPW was already down 1.7% in 2025 due to non-renewals. Arch's cycle management discipline means they'll reduce capacity when pricing is inadequate, which protects margins but limits top-line growth.

2. **Bermuda Tax Impact:** The step-up from 7.7% to 14.7% effective tax rate absorbs roughly $209 million annually, reducing net earnings growth relative to pre-tax growth.

### Estimate Trajectory

Consensus estimates of $9.40 for the current year and $10.10 for next year represent 7.4% EPS growth, reflecting the dampening effect of softer reinsurance pricing partially offset by investment income growth and buybacks. The 90-day revision trend is modestly positive, consistent with the earnings beat pattern but tempered by industry pricing dynamics.

## Valuation Assessment

At $96.28, Arch trades at:

- **~1.48x book value** ($65.11 BVPS)
- **~10.2x current-year EPS** ($9.40 consensus)
- **~9.5x next-year EPS** ($10.10 consensus)
- **~5.6x trailing FCF** ($6.1B / ~$34.5B market cap)

Analyst consensus targets a mean/median of $110 (14.3% upside) with a range of $95-$125. The $95 low target is essentially at the current price, suggesting the most bearish analyst views are fully reflected.

The valuation is compelling relative to the P&C peer group. Specialty reinsurers typically trade at 1.2-2.0x book depending on cycle position and ROE. Arch's 20%+ ROE justifies the upper end of that range. On an earnings basis, the roughly 10x forward P/E is a discount to the S&P 500 and below the specialty insurance median of approximately 12-14x. The FCF yield of 17.7% is extraordinary for a company with this earnings quality and stability.

The slight markdown from the prior valuation score of 8.5 reflects the stock's appreciation from the low-$90s to $96 while reinsurance pricing has softened, reducing the gap between price and intrinsic value. The fundamental math remains attractive: a company generating 20%+ ROE, buying back 5-6% of shares annually, and growing book value at 15-20%+ should not trade at 10x earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Arch competes with Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, RenaissanceRe, Everest Group, and AXIS Capital in reinsurance; with large commercial insurers in specialty insurance; and with MGIC, Radian, Essent, and NMI in mortgage insurance.

The three-segment diversification is Arch's most distinctive competitive feature. Few global (re)insurers combine P&C insurance, reinsurance, and mortgage insurance under one roof. The mortgage segment provides a high-margin, relatively uncorrelated earnings stream that allows Arch to absorb catastrophe losses in P&C while maintaining overall profitability.

Arch's cycle management is well-documented. When reinsurance rates soften, the company reduces exposure and redeploys capital to higher-returning lines or buybacks. The current signaling — reducing reinsurance capacity at January 2026 renewals while committing to return nearly 100% of capital — is consistent with this disciplined approach.

The elevated geopolitical risk environment (Hormuz disruption, Gulf infrastructure damage, shipping rerouting) creates structural support for P&C insurance and reinsurance pricing, particularly in specialty lines covering marine, energy, and political risk. Post-conflict insurance normalization typically takes 1-3 years. Arch's specialty positioning provides optionality to deploy capacity into these lines as they harden.

## Risk Assessment

### Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (HIGH)

The single largest structural risk. Bermuda's 15% CIT under OECD Pillar II nearly doubled Arch's effective tax rate. While substance-based tax credits partially offset the impact (reducing corporate expenses from $119M to $57M), the trajectory of OECD implementation may push rates higher. Every incremental percentage point of effective tax rate reduces EPS by approximately $0.12 at current pretax income levels. This is a permanent structural change.

### Catastrophe Event Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH)

Modeled peak zone exposure is $1.9 billion (Florida Tri-County windstorm, 1-in-250 year), representing approximately 8% of tangible equity. A major multi-event catastrophe year could reduce book value by 15-20%. The current geopolitical environment adds uncertainty — while man-made catastrophe losses from Middle East infrastructure destruction have been largely absorbed by 2025 results, any escalation targeting Gulf ports or facilities could trigger additional claims.

### Reinsurance Rate Cycle (MEDIUM)

If rate softening continues into 2027, the reinsurance segment's margins will compress. However, Arch's demonstrated ability to shrink capacity during soft markets and redirect capital to buybacks limits the earnings impact. The floor is set by investment income and mortgage underwriting, which together cover roughly 55-60% of recent annual net income.

### Reserve Adequacy (MEDIUM)

$24.5 billion in net loss reserves with a Monte Carlo 90th percentile of $29.0 billion implies $4.5 billion of potential adverse development ($11.98/share pre-tax). Casualty reserves face social inflation risk. International casualty pricing tracking below loss trends is a concerning leading indicator. Favorable development may decelerate as older benign accident years are exhausted.

### GSE Reform Risk (LOW-MEDIUM)

The January 2025 amendment to GSE preferred stock purchase agreements signals possible Fannie/Freddie privatization. Any change to the private mortgage insurance requirement would fundamentally impair the mortgage segment. However, this risk has existed for decades without materializing, and PMIERs compliance requirements (phasing in through September 2026) suggest continued reliance on private MI for the foreseeable future. The mortgage segment's RDS loss estimate of $931M (4.1% of tangible equity) bounds the downside.

### Macroeconomic Sensitivity

Arch benefits from higher interest rates through investment income but faces indirect exposure to economic slowdowns through premium volume and insured loss severity. The current inflationary environment raises claims costs in long-tail lines. However, the company's specialty positioning and disciplined underwriting largely insulate it from broad commercial premium pressure.

### Options Market Signal

Short interest at 1.7% of float with 3.4 days to cover is negligible, consistent with broad institutional confidence in the name. No meaningful contrarian signal from the short side.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$115-125, 19-30% upside)

- Rate hardening resumes due to a major catastrophe event or continued geopolitical disruption, driving reinsurance margin expansion
- Net investment income grows to $1.8B+ as the portfolio rolls into higher yields
- Share buybacks at $1.5-2.0B annually compound per-share metrics at 5-6%/year
- Bermuda tax credits prove more durable than feared, keeping effective rate at 13-14%
- Book value per share reaches $75+ by year-end 2026, and the stock re-rates to 1.5-1.6x book (~$112-120)

### Bear Case (~$80-85, 12-17% downside)

- A severe multi-event catastrophe year ($3B+ insured losses) depletes surplus and requires capital raising
- Reinsurance rates decline another 15-20%, compressing the segment to breakeven underwriting
- OECD Pillar II implementation pushes effective tax rate above 18%
- Reserve development turns adverse in casualty lines, adding 3-5 points to the combined ratio
- Book value per share stagnates at $60-65, and the stock de-rates to 1.25x book

## Conclusion

Arch Capital is a high-quality specialty insurer/reinsurer trading at a valuation that does not adequately reflect its earnings power, diversification, or capital return program. The 20%+ ROE, 30%+ FCF margin, and 22.6% annual book value compounding are being valued at roughly 10x earnings and 1.5x book — discounts to both the peer group and the company's own profitability profile. The Bermuda tax step-up is a real and permanent headwind that reduces after-tax earnings by roughly $200 million annually, but this is already reflected in the forward estimates. The softening reinsurance cycle will pressure one segment's margins while the investment income floor and mortgage income provide substantial earnings stability. Geopolitical disruption, particularly around the Persian Gulf, extends the insurance hardening cycle in specialty lines and reinforces structural demand for reinsurance capital.

The score trajectory has improved from 7.9 to 8.1, and the current assessment at 8.0 reflects a minor valuation adjustment as the stock has appreciated while forward pricing has softened. The fundamental thesis is unchanged and reinforced by Q4's earnings beat, continued aggressive buybacks, and rising estimate revisions. BUY with a base case target of $110.
