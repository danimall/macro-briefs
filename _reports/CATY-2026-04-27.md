## Executive Summary

Cathay General Bancorp is a $24.2 billion-asset bank holding company serving Chinese-American communities through 63 branches concentrated in California and New York. The bank reported a strong Q1 2026 with EPS of $1.29 (beating consensus by 8.4%), driven by NIM expansion to 3.43% as time deposit repricing continues to flow through the income statement. Full-year 2026 consensus EPS of $5.50 represents 21% growth over 2025's $4.54, with positive estimate revisions across all forecast periods over the past 30 and 90 days.

The investment case rests on a well-defined NIM recovery cycle: $3 billion of CDs maturing at a 3.62% weighted average provide continued repricing tailwind, and new loan originations at 6.12-6.15% support yields. The bank is returning capital aggressively — a new $150 million buyback program was authorized in Q1, the dividend was raised 11.8%, and shares outstanding have declined 4.7% since 2023. At ~11x forward earnings with a 2.8% yield, valuation looks reasonable relative to the 13.6x peer median.

The principal concerns are CRE concentration at 287% of risk-based capital (approaching the 300% regulatory threshold), the unique tariff vulnerability of CATY's import/export-oriented client base, and the absence of insider buying despite multiple executives selling shares. The stock has rallied 27% over 12 months and sits within 2% of its 52-week high, compressing the margin of safety. The NIM improvement thesis is largely consensus at this point, and the options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty (52% IV vs. 26.3% HV). This combination of improving fundamentals, reasonable valuation, concentrated risks, and limited price discount supports a HOLD with an income orientation.

## Company Overview

Cathay General Bancorp operates Cathay Bank, a California state-chartered commercial bank founded in 1962 and headquartered in Los Angeles's Chinatown. The bank's competitive identity is built on deep relationships within Chinese-American communities — approximately 78% of its ~1,268 employees are of Asian descent, many speaking Chinese dialects, which creates genuine customer stickiness that generic regional banks cannot replicate.

The loan book is dominated by commercial real estate ($10.6 billion, 52.4% of gross loans), followed by residential mortgages ($6.1 billion, 30.1%) and commercial loans ($3.2 billion, 15.9%). Geographic concentration is pronounced: 46% of CRE/construction loans are in California, 35% in New York. The bank maintains a small Hong Kong branch ($322 million in loans) and representative offices in Beijing, Shanghai, and Taipei.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Recovery

CATY's earnings hit a cyclical trough in 2024 (EPS $3.95, net income $286 million) as deposit repricing lagged rate hikes — interest expense surged from $118 million in 2022 to $661 million in 2024. The recovery is now well underway:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 Ann. |
|--------|------|------|------|-------------|
| Net Income ($M) | $354 | $286 | $315 | ~$348 |
| Diluted EPS | $4.86 | $3.95 | $4.54 | $1.29 (Q1) |
| ROA | 1.56% | 1.22% | 1.33% | ~1.4% |
| ROE | 13.6% | 10.2% | 10.9% | ~11%+ |
| Efficiency Ratio | 47.0% | 51.4% | 43.4% | 40.4% |

The Q1 2026 efficiency ratio of 40.4% (36.9% adjusted) is exceptionally strong for a community bank, reflecting operating leverage as NII grows while expenses remain controlled.

### NIM Dynamics

NIM is the central financial story. The trajectory from 3.04% (2024) to 3.30% (2025) to 3.43% (Q1 2026) reflects deposit repricing working through a time-deposit-heavy funding base. Specific mechanics:

- Cost of interest-bearing deposits fell from 3.85% (2024) to 3.29% (2025) to 2.96% (Q1 2026)
- $3 billion of CDs maturing in 2026 carry a 3.62% weighted average cost — these will reprice lower
- Time deposits comprise 46.5% of total deposits, so the repricing runway remains substantial
- Partially offsetting: loan yields declined from 6.26% (2024) to 6.01% (Q1 2026) as floating-rate loans repriced with Fed cuts

Management guided 2026 NIM of 3.40%-3.50% assuming no additional rate cuts. This implies NIM roughly flattens from Q1 levels, which is plausible given that asset-side repricing partially offsets continued deposit-side benefit.

### Balance Sheet

Capital ratios are strong: Tier 1 capital of 13.27%, total risk-based capital of 14.93%, both well above regulatory minimums. Tangible book value per share reached $30.95 in Q1 2026, up 9% YoY. The bank has minimal long-term debt ($119 million in trust preferred securities relative to a $24.2 billion balance sheet).

Loan growth was soft in Q1 at 0.2% linked-quarter, against full-year guidance of 3.5%-4.5%. Management attributed this to deliberate caution in an uncertain environment. Deposit growth was -1% in Q1 (seasonal plus brokered deposit decline), against full-year guidance of 4%-5%. Both trajectories imply back-loaded growth, which introduces execution risk.

Free cash flow generation is solid at $364 million in 2025 ($369 million OCF less $5 million capex), supporting the robust capital return program.

### Capital Return

The capital allocation story is unambiguously positive:
- Shares outstanding declined from 72.6 million (2023) to 69.2 million (2025), a 4.7% reduction
- New $150 million buyback program authorized in Q1 2026
- Dividend increased 11.8% to $0.38/quarter ($1.52 annualized, ~2.8% yield)
- Payout ratio is conservative at approximately 33% of earnings

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth is moderate. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $5.50 represents 21% growth over FY2025's $4.54, with FY2027 at $5.78 (+5.1%). The FY2026 jump is largely mechanical — NIM recovery plus share count reduction — rather than fundamental franchise expansion. By FY2027, CATY returns to low-single-digit earnings growth.

Estimate revisions are positive across all timeframes:
- Current year: $5.50, up 4.9% over 30 days, up 3.4% over 90 days
- Next year: $5.78, up 1.8% over 30 days, up 0.8% over 90 days

The acceleration in near-term revisions (30-day changes exceeding 90-day changes for the current quarter and year) reflects the Q1 beat flowing through to annual estimates. The Q1 earnings surprise of 22.7% (per vendor data) was the strongest in at least four quarters, following two consecutive misses.

Organic growth opportunities are constrained by the bank's niche focus. Total addressable market expansion is limited to new Chinese-American community branches and potentially adjacent Asian-American demographics. Pacific Rim banks from Taiwan, Hong Kong, and China continue to open LA-area branches, adding competitive pressure in the core market.

## Competitive Landscape

CATY occupies a defensible niche as the second-largest Chinese-American bank in the U.S., behind East West Bancorp (EWBC, $76 billion in assets). The competitive moat derives from cultural affinity, multilingual service, and multi-decade community relationships. These are genuine switching costs — a Chinese-American small business owner who has banked with Cathay for 20 years and conducts business in Mandarin faces real friction in moving to a generic regional bank.

The moat's trajectory is mixed. The community relationships provide stability but the addressable market is finite. CATY's 5-year TSR of 176.7% has lagged its peer group's 213.1%, suggesting the niche positioning is generating below-peer returns over longer periods. Meanwhile, Pacific Rim banks continue to expand in Southern California, directly competing for the same customer base with sometimes lower pricing.

The Q1 2026 efficiency ratio is a competitive strength — well below regional bank averages — but reflects cost discipline rather than revenue acceleration. Loan growth of 0.2% in Q1, while partly deliberate, also signals limited pricing power in a cautious market.

## Risk Assessment

### CRE Concentration (High)
CRE loans at 287% of risk-based capital approach the 300% regulatory threshold. While the Q1 2026 ratio declined 9 points, this remains the bank's single largest vulnerability. The mitigants are real — 49% weighted average LTV, granular $2 million average loan size, 25% owner-occupied — but a severe CRE downturn in California or New York would disproportionately impact CATY. Office exposure at 13% of CRE ($1.44 billion) is the most vulnerable subsegment. The CECL downside scenario projects $97 million in additional reserves under stress (unemployment 8.4%, CRE prices -21%).

### Tariff and Trade War Exposure (High, Unique)
This risk is distinctive to CATY among U.S. banks. The client base of Chinese-American businesses, many involved in import/export trade, faces direct margin compression from tariffs. Management explicitly cited "geopolitical tensions and uncertainty" in pulling back on Q1 loan growth. The 10-K risk factors discuss retaliatory tariffs reducing client revenues and margins. This is a binary risk that could impair credit quality across the loan book if U.S.-China trade relations deteriorate further.

### Uninsured Deposits (Moderate)
$10.19 billion in uninsured deposits (approximately 49% of total) is elevated in a post-SVB environment. The bank maintains substantial available liquidity (FHLB $8.85 billion credit line, $1.28 billion Fed Discount Window capacity, $1.64 billion unpledged securities), providing meaningful coverage. Community banking relationships provide stickier deposits than SVB's venture capital base, but the ratio warrants monitoring.

### Insider Activity (Moderate Negative)
Executive Chairman Cheng sold ~$1.5 million in shares (November 2025-January 2026). CEO Liu sold ~$239K in April 2026. Vice Chairman Tang and a Director also sold. No open-market purchases by any insider. While some sales relate to RSU exercises and tax withholdings, the absence of any buying by C-suite or board members near the 52-week high is a moderate negative signal.

### CFO Transition (Low-Moderate)
Long-tenured CFO Heng Chen (2003-2026) retired in March 2026. Successor Albert Wang appeared competent in the Q1 earnings call. Chen remains as Special Advisor through year-end 2026, providing continuity. The transition adds modest execution risk during a period of improving but complex NIM dynamics.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 52.0% versus 26.3% historical volatility represents a significant premium — the options market is pricing in roughly double the typical volatility over the next month. The backwardated term structure (52% near-term vs. 27% at 6 months) signals the market expects a specific near-term event to resolve uncertainty, likely related to the post-earnings digest and potential tariff developments. Put/call skew at 20.4% indicates elevated put demand. This options profile is consistent with a stock where fundamentals are improving but tail risks remain unresolved.

Short interest is low at 3.4% of float with 2.6 days to cover, suggesting limited crowded positioning on either side.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$65, +20%)
- NIM reaches the high end of the 3.40-3.50% guided range as CD repricing accelerates
- Loan growth catches up in Q2-Q4, hitting the 3.5-4.5% guided range
- Credit quality continues improving (NPA ratio below 50 bps)
- Tariff fears prove overstated as trade tensions stabilize
- Buyback program completes, reducing share count another 3-4%
- Multiple re-rates toward peer median of 13.6x as CRE concerns fade → 13x FY2026 EPS of $5.50 = $71.50

### Bear Case (~$42, -23%)
- Tariff escalation directly impairs loan quality among import/export clients, pushing NCOs above 30 bps
- CRE stress concentrates in California office and New York multifamily
- Loan growth stalls as management maintains defensive posture
- NIM expansion halted by competitive deposit pricing pressure
- CRE concentration ratio crosses 300% trigger, attracting regulatory scrutiny
- Multiple compresses to 8x on deteriorating credit outlook → 8x FY2026 EPS of $5.00 = $40

### Base Case (~$58-60, +7-10%)
NIM stabilizes at 3.40-3.45%. Loan growth reaches 3-3.5% (below guidance). Credit quality remains manageable with NCOs around 15-20 bps. Buyback program reduces share count 2-3%. EPS comes in at $5.40-5.50. The stock trades at 10.5-11x forward, reflecting improving earnings but persistent CRE/tariff risk concentration. Total return including the 2.8% dividend yield reaches approximately 10-13% over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $48-50 (8.7-9.1x forward earnings), providing sufficient margin of safety against CRE/tariff risks
2. Two consecutive quarters of loan growth above 3% annualized combined with net charge-offs below 15 bps, demonstrating that tariff fears are not impairing the franchise
3. CRE concentration ratio declines below 270% of risk-based capital, reducing regulatory overhang

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Net charge-offs exceed 30 bps for any single quarter, suggesting tariff/CRE stress is materializing in credit losses
2. NPA ratio reverses above 80 bps
3. CRE concentration ratio exceeds 295%, approaching the 300% regulatory threshold
4. Uninsured deposit outflows exceed 5% in any quarter

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (July 2026) — this will be the first full quarter capturing any tariff-related credit deterioration and will provide critical visibility on whether Q1's soft loan growth was prudent caution or demand weakness.

## Conclusion

Cathay General Bancorp is executing well through a NIM recovery cycle, with improving efficiency, solid capital returns, and positive estimate revisions. The Q1 2026 beat was convincing, and the mechanical tailwind from CD repricing provides earnings visibility through at least year-end 2026. At 9.9x FY2026 earnings with a 2.8% yield, the stock offers fair value for a bank of this quality and risk profile.

The limiting factors for a stronger recommendation are: (1) the stock's proximity to its 52-week high after a 27% run, reducing the margin of safety; (2) CRE concentration near regulatory thresholds; (3) tariff risk that is genuinely differentiated for this bank given its Chinese-American import/export client base; and (4) insider selling without offsetting purchases. The improving NIM story is largely consensus, reflected in the current price and analyst targets. The dividend yield and ongoing buyback provide income support, making this a reasonable hold for existing shareholders, but the risk-reward profile at current prices does not warrant fresh capital commitment.
