## Executive Summary

Flagstar Bank is a $87.5 billion-asset institution in the middle of a complex turnaround led by Joseph Otting (former OCC Comptroller) and backed by Steven Mnuchin's capital. The bank emerged from its 2023-2024 crisis — which produced $2.4B in goodwill impairment, $1.1B in net losses in FY2024, and material weakness disclosures — and posted its first profitable quarter in Q4 2025. Q1 2026 continued with modest profitability ($0.04 adjusted EPS), and management guides $0.60-$0.65 adjusted EPS for full-year 2026.

At $13.58/share, FLG trades at approximately 0.73x tangible book value ($7.8B TBV on 416.8M shares = ~$18.72/share TBV), which prices in significant probability of further credit deterioration. The bull case rests on classified loan resolution ($9.7B classified loans declining from $11.5B peak), NIM recovery from the current 1.89% toward a normalized 2.5%+, and eventual re-rating toward 1.0x TBV as profitability proves durable. The bear case centers on $2.98B in non-performing assets (3.41% of total assets), $11.2B in multi-family loans repricing through 2027 into an elevated rate environment, and the structural damage from NYC rent regulation that constrains borrower cash flows.

The 12-month thesis is positive: management guidance implies a 4-5x acceleration in EPS from Q1's run-rate, Moody's upgraded deposit ratings to investment grade (Baa3) in April 2026 with a positive outlook, and 89% of multi-family loans that repriced in 2025 remain current. However, falling consensus estimates (current quarter revised -32% over 90 days), persistent negative earnings momentum, and the idiosyncratic concentration in NYC rent-regulated multi-family create enough uncertainty that this remains a HOLD with a growth thesis rather than a conviction BUY.

## Company Overview

Flagstar Bank, National Association is the successor to New York Community Bancorp (NYCB), which consolidated New York Community Bank, Flagstar Bancorp, and FDIC-resolved Signature Bank assets before experiencing a near-existential CRE credit crisis in 2023-2024. Following a $1B+ capital raise led by Mnuchin's Liberty Strategic Capital in March 2024, the company installed turnaround management, sold its mortgage servicing operations, eliminated its holding company structure (October 2025), and began a multi-year restructuring of its loan portfolio.

The bank operates ~340 locations across nine states, concentrated in the greater New York/New Jersey metro area with additional presence in the upper Midwest, Florida, and West Coast. Its loan book is dominated by multi-family ($29.0B, 48% of total) and commercial & industrial ($15.2B, 25%), with meaningful CRE exposure ($9.3B, 15%).

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability trajectory:** FLG lost $1.118B in FY2024 and $177M in FY2025. The loss narrowed 84% year-over-year as provision expense dropped from $1.092B to $184M and net charge-offs fell from $892M (1.16% of loans) to $351M (0.55%). Q4 2025 was the first profitable quarter; Q1 2026 continued with $21M net income.

**Net interest margin:** NIM compressed from 2.99% (FY2023) to 1.89% (FY2025). This reflects the cost of replacing low-rate deposits lost during the crisis with more expensive brokered and wholesale funding. As brokered deposits have been reduced 76% (from $10.2B to $2.4B) and the loan book reprices, NIM is expected to expand. The pace depends on how quickly CRE/multi-family payoffs occur versus new C&I originations ramping.

**Capital position:** CET1 at 12.83% and total capital at 16.23% provide substantial buffers — nearly 2x the well-capitalized minimums. Tangible book value is approximately $18.72/share (equity of $8.1B less $381M intangibles, divided by 416.8M shares). The stock's 0.73x P/TBV discount reflects credit risk uncertainty.

**Liquidity:** Total available liquidity of $27.1B ($18.8B on-balance sheet plus $8.3B additional capacity) covers uninsured deposits of $13.5B by 2.0x. Deposit flight risk, which was the proximate cause of peer failures (SVB, First Republic), appears well-managed.

**Balance sheet shrinkage:** Total assets contracted from $114B (2023) to $87.5B (2025) as a deliberate de-risking strategy. Multi-family loans declined 15% YoY, CRE 21%. The bank is replacing these with C&I growth ($5.8B originations in FY2025).

## Growth Analysis

**Forward earnings trajectory:** Management guides $0.60-$0.65 adjusted EPS for FY2026. Consensus for FY2027 is $1.56/share (14 analysts), implying the turnaround gains significant momentum as credit costs normalize and NIM expands.

**Key growth driver — C&I expansion:** The strategic pivot toward commercial & industrial lending produced $5.8B in originations and $8.3B in new/increased commitments in FY2025. C&I now represents 25% of the loan book and is growing while CRE/multi-family shrinks. This diversification, if sustained, addresses the structural concentration risk.

**Estimate revision headwind:** Current quarter EPS estimates have been revised down 32% over 90 days (from $0.10 to $0.07). Full-year 2026 estimates declined 11% (from $0.55 to $0.49). Next year estimates declined 6% (from $1.66 to $1.56). This negative revision trend is a clear caution signal — consensus is becoming less optimistic about the recovery pace.

**Multi-family repricing as dual catalyst:** $3.9B in multi-family loans reprice in 2026 and $7.3B in 2027. If borrowers sustain payments at higher rates (89% of 2025 reprices are current), classified assets decline and provisions stay low. If they cannot, further credit deterioration extends losses. This is the fundamental binary that determines whether FLG reaches management's targets.

## Valuation Assessment

At $13.58/share:
- **P/TBV:** ~0.73x ($18.72 TBV)
- **Forward P/E on FY2026 guidance midpoint ($0.625):** 21.7x
- **Forward P/E on FY2027 consensus ($1.56):** 8.7x
- **Dividend yield:** 0.3% (minimal; capital retained for rebuild)

Peer regional banks trade at median 13.5x P/E with 3.1% yields. FLG's current-year earnings multiple is premium to peers, but its FY2027 multiple of 8.7x would represent a substantial discount. The relevant question is whether FY2027 earnings materialize.

On a price-to-book basis, healthy regional banks trade at 1.0-1.5x TBV. FLG's 0.73x implies the market assigns roughly 30% probability of further material impairment to book value. If credit resolution proceeds as management projects and the bank earns its way to normalized ROE (8-10%), a re-rating to 0.9-1.0x TBV ($16.85-$18.72) is reasonable over 12-18 months. That represents 24-38% upside from current levels.

The analyst consensus mean target of $15.69 (median $16.00) implies 16% upside. The high target of $17.00 implies 25% upside.

However, the forward earnings multiple is not yet cheap enough to compensate for execution risk. At ~22x FY2026 guidance, you are paying a full multiple for earnings that haven't been proven sustainable.

## Competitive Landscape

FLG's competitive position is uniquely constrained by its legacy portfolio. No other major U.S. bank carries comparable concentration in NYC rent-regulated multi-family loans ($13.9B, 88% of its NY multi-family book). This concentration creates a problem that management must work through rather than a sustainable advantage.

The C&I pivot represents an attempt to build a more durable franchise, but FLG is competing against well-established commercial banks with decades of relationship banking in its footprint. The private banking operation (~20 branches) provides some differentiation for high-net-worth deposit gathering.

The Moody's upgrade to investment-grade deposit ratings (Baa3, April 2026) is competitively meaningful — it reduces the bank's cost of funding over time and enables participation in certain institutional deposit channels previously unavailable. The positive outlook suggests further upgrades are likely if execution continues.

TSR of 49% versus peer group's 211% over the comparable period demonstrates the competitive handicap the bank operates under. The gap quantifies how much value destruction the crisis caused relative to peers who avoided it.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risk — NYC multi-family credit resolution:** $2.26B in multi-family non-accruals (up 29% YoY) with $6.5B in interest-only loans entering amortization by end of 2026. The NYC Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act (2019) structurally limits rent increases, creating a squeeze between rising operating costs/debt service and capped revenue. If rates remain above 5% on the long end, more borrowers face unsustainable debt service coverage ratios.

**Single-name concentration:** A single borrower bankruptcy drove $566M in non-accruals in early 2025. While this portfolio auction closed in January 2026, the incident demonstrates that individual borrower failures can materially impact quarterly results.

**NIM recovery uncertainty:** The 1.89% NIM is well below the ~3% needed for sustained profitability at current scale. Expansion depends on: (a) lower-cost deposit growth replacing brokered deposits, (b) C&I loan growth at attractive spreads, and (c) the CRE/multi-family book repricing without credit deterioration. Each factor carries execution risk.

**Macro headwinds:** With 30Y rates above 5% and the current rate environment suggesting rates stay elevated or potentially rise further, the multi-family repricing wave faces a hostile backdrop. Higher-for-longer rates directly increase the probability of additional credit deterioration.

**ACL coverage declining:** Allowance coverage of non-accruals fell from 46% to 35%. If non-accruals don't resolve as expected, the bank may need additional provisioning that would depress earnings.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($16-18, +18-33%):** Multi-family repricing proceeds without significant credit deterioration. NIM expands toward 2.3-2.5% as brokered deposits run off and C&I growth continues. FY2026 hits guidance, FY2027 accelerates toward $1.50+. Rating agencies upgrade further. Stock re-rates toward 0.9-1.0x TBV. Capital returns commence in late 2026/early 2027. The Otting/Mnuchin "OneWest playbook" executes successfully.

**Bear Case ($9-10, -26-34%):** Elevated rates cause a second wave of multi-family distress. More single-borrower failures emerge. Non-accruals rise above 5% of loans. Additional provisioning eliminates profitability. NIM recovery stalls as CRE payoffs outpace C&I growth. Estimates continue falling. Stock de-rates toward 0.5x TBV or below, revisiting 2024 crisis lows.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Estimate revisions stabilize or turn positive — specifically, FY2026 EPS consensus stops declining and begins rising above $0.55
2. Q2 2026 NIM expands above 2.10%, confirming deposit cost normalization
3. Non-performing assets decline below 2.5% of total assets for two consecutive quarters

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Non-accrual loans exceed $3.5B (20% above current level), indicating repricing wave is generating new distress
2. Management withdraws or materially reduces FY2026 guidance below $0.50 adjusted EPS
3. FY2027 consensus EPS falls below $1.20, implying normalization is delayed beyond the current investment horizon

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). This quarter will show the first full impact of 2026 multi-family repricing, NIM trajectory, and whether C&I growth offsets CRE runoff.

## Conclusion

Flagstar Bank presents a legitimate turnaround story with identifiable catalysts (NIM expansion, credit normalization, potential capital returns) and meaningful upside if execution continues (24-38% to TBV parity). The management team has relevant turnaround experience, capital ratios are strong, and the Moody's upgrade validates improving credit trends.

However, the investment case faces three specific headwinds that prevent a higher-conviction recommendation: (1) consensus estimates are actively declining (-11% on FY2026, -6% on FY2027 over 90 days), indicating the market is pricing in execution disappointment; (2) non-performing assets are still rising in absolute terms (3.41% of total assets, up from 2.62% prior year); and (3) the macro environment of elevated long-term rates directly undermines the multi-family repricing thesis by increasing borrower stress.

The 12-month growth thesis is specific: if FLG achieves its $0.60-$0.65 EPS guidance and non-performing assets begin declining in absolute terms during H2 2026, the stock should re-rate toward $15-16 (0.85-0.90x TBV), representing ~11-18% upside from current levels with identifiable quarterly checkpoints. The thesis depends on rate-sensitive credit outcomes in a hostile rate environment, which keeps this as a monitored HOLD rather than a conviction position.
