## Executive Summary

Freeport-McMoRan enters 2026 with the world's largest publicly traded copper reserve base (112.3 billion pounds), production normalizing after an unprecedented operational disruption, and copper prices at all-time highs near $6/lb. The September 2025 Grasberg mud rush destroyed ~44% of Indonesian copper output and ~50% of gold output for the year, compressing 2025 earnings to $1.52/share. This depressed base creates the forward opportunity: management guides to $8B operating cash flow at $5/lb copper and $4,000/oz gold, and at current spot prices ($6+/lb copper, $5,000+/oz gold), that figure could approach $12B. Consensus estimates for FY2026 EPS of $3.05 and FY2027 of $4.02 imply the stock trades at ~22x and ~17x forward earnings respectively, a substantial compression from the optically distorted ~45x trailing multiple.

The February 2026 Memorandum of Understanding for a life-of-resource extension of PTFI's operating rights in the Grasberg minerals district is the most important strategic development in several years. It de-risks the single largest source of value in FCX's portfolio — Grasberg's underground operations produce copper at a net cash credit of $0.55/lb after gold by-product credits — and extends the asset's economic life well beyond 2041. Combined with leaching technology innovations targeting 300M lbs in 2026, a $3.5B Bagdad expansion, and the El Abra mill project, FCX has a visible production growth path of 15-20% over the next five years.

The principal risk is Grasberg Block Cave restart execution. The Q2 2026 restart timeline is management guidance, not confirmed fact. Insurance coverage caps at $1.0B with a $0.5B deductible. Securities class actions, an SEC subpoena, a DOJ inquiry, and an FCPA investigation create legal and reputational overhang. At 45x trailing earnings, any further delay in Grasberg normalization would compress cash flow estimates and likely trigger a sharp re-rating. These risks are real but priced into a stock that has already doubled from its April 2025 lows.

## Company Overview

Freeport-McMoRan is the world's largest publicly traded copper producer, operating mining assets across three continents. The company produced approximately 3.4 billion pounds of copper in 2025 and holds reserves sufficient for multiple decades of production.

**Operating Segments:**

- **Indonesia (Grasberg minerals district):** 48.76%-owned PT Freeport Indonesia. At normal rates, Grasberg's underground mines produce ~1.7B lbs copper and ~1.3M oz gold annually. The 2025 mud rush reduced output to 1.0B lbs copper and 937K oz gold. PTFI now operates a fully integrated smelter and precious metals refinery in East Java, eliminating export dependency.

- **United States (7 copper mines):** Including Morenci (largest U.S. copper mine), Bagdad, Safford/Lone Star, Sierrita, and others. Produced 1.3B lbs copper in 2025. FCX produces ~70% of total U.S. refined copper.

- **South America:** Cerro Verde (Peru, 55%-owned) and El Abra (Chile, 51%-owned). Produced 1.06B lbs copper in 2025.

- **Molybdenum and Downstream:** Climax and Henderson mines (Colorado), Atlantic Copper (Spain smelter/refinery), U.S. rod and refining operations.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow fell to $5.6B in 2025 from $7.2B in 2024, driven by lower Indonesia volumes and $1.3B in working capital uses. Capital expenditures were $4.5B, yielding free cash flow of approximately $1.1B (4.3% FCF margin). Shareholder returns totaled ~$970M ($864M dividends + $107M buybacks).

The 2026 outlook is substantially stronger. At current spot prices (~$6/lb copper, $5,000+/oz gold), the sensitivity analysis implies:
- Additional copper revenue: $0.10/lb × $330M = ~$3.3B incremental OCF from the $1/lb above plan
- Additional gold revenue: ~$750M+ from gold at $5,000+ vs. $4,000 assumption
- Potential 2026 OCF: **$11-12B** at current spot

Against $4.3B capex and $0.9B idle/restoration costs, this implies potential free cash flow of **$5-7B** — a 5-7% yield on the current $101B market cap, before any incremental shareholder return programs.

### Balance Sheet

Net debt stands at $2.3B (excluding PTFI downstream facility debt), well below management's $3-4B target range. Total debt is $9.4B at a weighted average rate of 5.2%, with 93% fixed and average duration of ~8 years. No senior note maturities fall in 2026; the nearest is $1.3B in 2027. Liquidity is strong: $3.8B cash plus $4.85B revolver capacity. Current ratio of 2.29 provides ample cushion.

Environmental obligations ($2.0B) and asset retirement obligations ($3.8B) are meaningful long-duration liabilities, with ~$0.9B annual spending expected. The Altman Z-Score of 1.84 sits in the grey zone, driven by the capital-intensive nature of mining and elevated debt, but this is structural for the industry rather than a sign of financial distress.

## Growth Analysis

FCX's growth operates on three timescales:

**Near-term (2026-2027): Grasberg normalization.** Management expects 85% of normal Grasberg rates restored by H2 2026, with full normalization in 2027. At full run-rate, the production recovery alone, holding prices constant, represents ~$3-4B in incremental revenue.

**Medium-term (2026-2030): Leaching technology and Bagdad expansion.** Leaching innovations have already reached a 240M lb annual run rate from existing stockpiles. These are low-cost incremental pounds from already-mined material. The Bagdad expansion (adding 200-250M lbs/year) needs ~$4/lb incentive pricing — well below current spot.

**Long-term (2028-2035+): Kucing Liar and El Abra.** Kucing Liar (~$4B additional investment through 2033, production ~2030) unlocks new Grasberg-district resources. El Abra mill project could add 700M+ lbs/year. Together with the life-of-resource extension, FCX has a visible path to 15-20% production growth over the next decade.

The structural copper demand thesis — electrification, EVs, grid build-out, data center power infrastructure, defense — is supported by supply-demand analysis showing growing deficits. New large copper deposits take 15-20 years to develop. This supply inelasticity is the fundamental reason copper has reached all-time highs.

## Competitive Landscape

FCX's competitive position is among the strongest in global mining:

1. **Reserve base:** 112.3 billion pounds of recoverable copper reserves provide decades of production without needing to acquire new assets. This is unmatched among publicly traded copper producers.

2. **Cost position:** Grasberg's net cash cost after gold by-product credits is negative ($0.55 credit per pound). U.S. operations run at $2.06/lb net cash costs. Consolidated costs of $1.75/lb provide significant margin at $5-6/lb copper.

3. **Domestic dominance:** ~70% of U.S. refined copper production positions FCX as the primary beneficiary of Section 232 tariffs and any domestic sourcing mandates. The proposed 25% domestic cathode requirement in 2027 (potentially 40% by 2029) creates a structural demand floor for FCX's output.

4. **Technology leadership:** Autonomous haulage at Bagdad (first major U.S. mine), leaching technology innovations unlocking incremental production from existing stockpiles, and PTFI's newly operational smelter/PMR demonstrate operational capability.

5. **Barriers to entry:** New copper mines take 15-20 years from discovery to production. Capital requirements for greenfield development exceed $5B for world-class deposits. Permitting complexity in the U.S. is increasing.

## Risk Assessment

### Operational Risks

**Grasberg restart execution** is the dominant near-term risk. The restart timeline is subject to completion of mud removal, infrastructure repair, and safety barrier installation. A three-month delay would shift ~500M lbs of copper and ~300K oz of gold into 2027, reducing FY2026 EPS by an estimated $0.60-0.80.

**Indonesian regulatory risk** is structural. The new March 2025 regulation requiring 100% of export proceeds deposited in Indonesian banks for 12 months constrains capital flexibility. PTFI's ownership may decline to ~37% post-2041 under the life-of-resource extension terms. Separatist violence in Central Papua (55+ incidents in 2025) creates ongoing security concerns.

### Legal and Regulatory Risks

Multiple concurrent investigations create overhang:
- SEC subpoena and DOJ inquiry regarding PTFI smelter disclosures
- FCPA investigation at PT Smelting (66% PTFI-owned)
- Securities class action and derivative suits post-mud rush
- Director departure (March 2026 8-K) requires monitoring

None of these have resulted in charges or material settlements to date, but the combination is unusual and could produce adverse outcomes.

### Commodity Price Risk

FCX's operating leverage works in both directions. At $3.50/lb copper (a 30% decline from current levels), operating cash flow would decline by approximately $5B from the spot-implied $12B, compressing to $6-7B — still covering capex but leaving minimal free cash flow.

### Capital Intensity

$4.3B capex in 2026, with $10B+ in growth projects over the next decade (Kucing Liar, Bagdad expansion, El Abra). These projects carry execution risk and long payback periods. The Bagdad expansion's $4/lb incentive price requirement is currently met, but a cyclical downturn could make these projects uneconomic.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 58.6% is meaningfully rich to 45.0% historical volatility, with IV at the 76th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (58.6% near-term vs. 44.3% at 6-month), indicating the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty related to Grasberg restart timing and Q1 2026 earnings. Put/call OI ratio of 0.43 and volume ratio of 0.25 are heavily call-skewed, consistent with bullish positioning. The 1-month put/call skew of 2.1% (roughly neutral) suggests institutional participants are not hedging aggressively. Summary: elevated short-term uncertainty but directionally bullish positioning, consistent with near-term execution risk around a structurally positive setup.

## Conclusion

Freeport-McMoRan presents a world-class copper producer with depressed trailing earnings, a clear production recovery catalyst, and the strongest commodity price environment in the company's history. The Grasberg life-of-resource extension MOU addresses the longest-standing structural concern about asset duration. Estimate revisions are strongly positive (FY2027 up 32.6% in 90 days), the balance sheet is clean, and capital allocation discipline is intact.

The risks are concentrated in execution — specifically, whether the Grasberg Block Cave restarts on the guided Q2 2026 timeline. Active regulatory investigations add headline risk. At $70, the stock has already doubled from its post-mud-rush lows, reducing the margin of safety relative to the bear case. On a 12-18 month horizon, the combination of production normalization, spot commodity prices well above plan assumptions, and 20%+ long-term estimate revision momentum supports a BUY recommendation with a $80-85 base case target. Position sizing should reflect the commodity cyclicality and concentration risk in Grasberg — 2-3% of portfolio is appropriate.
