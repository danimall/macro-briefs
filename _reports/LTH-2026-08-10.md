## Executive Summary

Life Time operates 195 large-format "athletic country clubs" across 31 U.S. states and one Canadian province, generating $866.0M of revenue in Q2 2026 (+13.7%) at a 28.5% adjusted EBITDA margin. The operating record over the last four quarters is strong and consistent: four straight EPS beats, adjusted EBITDA up 16.8% in Q2, two FY2026 guidance raises (revenue now $3,350-3,375M, adjusted EBITDA $940-955M), net leverage down to 1.4x, and upgrades to BB from both Fitch (April 21) and S&P (June 25). The equity has responded, rising roughly 60% year-to-date from approximately $27 at year-end 2025.

Two features of that record deserve more weight than the headline growth rate. First, growth is overwhelmingly price and mix rather than members: center memberships rose 1.2% year-over-year to 860,041 while center count rose 6.0%, so memberships per club fell from 4,618 to 4,410 (-4.5%). Revenue per center membership rose 11.8% to $993 per quarter. Management's explanation — deliberate reduction of insurance-subsidized "qualified" memberships, more individuals per family membership, and newer clubs hitting target economics with fewer memberships — is supported by visits per membership rising from ~140 to ~148 and by 122M total visits in 2025 (+7%). But the mix benefit is arithmetically finite, several third-party administrator contracts expire in 2026, and comparable center revenue growth has already stepped down from +15.3% (2023) to +12.2% to +11.1% to a guided +7.9-8.3%.

Second, the growth program is not self-funded on a conventional basis. Operating cash flow of $408.4M in H1 2026 against capex of $523.3M produced conventional free cash flow of -$114.9M; FY2025 was -$21.0M. FY2026 guided capex of $1,165-1,210M is a 31-36% step-up, part-funded by ~$400M of sale-leasebacks. That is a disclosed, coherent capital-recycling strategy, but each property can only be sold once, and the transaction permanently converts owned assets into fixed rent. The operating lease liability is $2,765.5M and grew $126.7M in six months, with Q2 rent up 13.3% to $94.3M. At $42.97 the stock trades 24.6x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS of ~$1.75, 21.2x next-year consensus of $2.03, 27.2x guided GAAP EPS that is itself declining 3.5% year-over-year, and 11.5x EV/2026E adjusted EBITDA. That set of multiples is defensible but not cheap, and it prices in a second half that management has guided to grow adjusted EBITDA roughly half as fast as the first.

## Company Overview

Life Time positions itself deliberately outside the gym category. A typical club runs 90,000+ indoor square feet with indoor and outdoor pools, tennis and pickleball courts (800+ dedicated courts, 5.9M participations in 2025), basketball, group fitness and cycle studios, LifeSpa and medi-spa, LifeCafe, childcare and Kids Academy, recovery amenities and co-working lounges, staffed by 220-260 employees. Total headcount exceeded 44,000 at year-end 2025 and 52,000 at the July 2026 seasonal peak, including 11,100+ certified fitness professionals.

Memberships are month-to-month with no long-term contracts, across base, signature, qualified (discounted, often subsidized by third-party administrators including medical insurers) and on-hold ($15/month) tiers. Membership dues and enrollment fees were 72.2% of center revenue in H1 2026; in-center revenue — personal training, spa, café, camps, events, supplements — was 27.8%. The member base skews affluent: median household income $160,000, roughly 1.4x the surrounding trade area, 73% homeowners, ~60% in couples or family memberships.

Adjacent concepts include Life Time Work (15 co-working locations), Life Time Living (four wellness residences), MIORA longevity clinics (eight locations, up from one 2024 pilot), ARORA 55+ programming, 25+ owned athletic events, and LTH supplements.

The real-estate model is central. Since 2015, ~71% of centers are leased, including ~84% of centers opened after 2015. Management targets $25-30M of net invested capital per new location — gross cost net of landlord construction reimbursements and net sale-leaseback proceeds — with an average portfolio cash-on-cash return targeted "in excess of 30%" after a three-to-four-year ramp. At year-end 2025, 29 centers were under three years old and 17 were under construction.

## Financial Analysis

| Revenue | 2,216.6 | 2,621.0 | 2,995.3 | 3,362.5 |
| Growth | +21.6% | +18.2% | +14.3% | +12.3% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 536.8 | 676.8 | 825.2 | 947.5 |
| Margin | 24.2% | 25.8% | 27.5% | 28.2% |
| GAAP net income | 76.1 | 156.2 | 373.7 | 360.5 |
| Adjusted net income | — | — | 325.5 | 398.0 |

The margin recovery has largely completed. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded ~870bp in 2023, ~170bp in each of 2024 and 2025, and guidance implies ~70bp in 2026. Revenue growth has decelerated in every year since 2023.

**FY2025 GAAP earnings were inflated by items that do not repeat.** Net income of $373.7M included roughly $106M pre-tax of non-recurring benefit: ~$54.6M of CARES Act employee retention credits, ~$38.6M of net Zurich business-interruption proceeds, and a $12.8M net gain on sale-leasebacks. Adjusted net income was $325.5M. This is why FY2026 GAAP net income is guided *down* 3.5% to $358-363M while adjusted net income is guided *up* 22.3% to $394-402M. Any comparison to consensus must be basis-matched; the divergence runs opposite to the usual non-GAAP pattern.

**The interest tailwind is largely spent.** Net interest expense fell from $148.1M (FY2024) to $82.3M (FY2025) to $68.5M (TTM June 2026). The 2024-to-2025 decline of ~$66M pre-tax was roughly $0.21 per diluted share at a 27% rate, a large share of adjusted EPS growth that cannot repeat at that scale with net leverage already at 1.4x. FY2026 guidance is $59-63M net of $28-30M of *capitalized* construction interest, implying gross interest nearer $90-95M. Capitalizing construction-period interest is standard GAAP, but it supports roughly $0.09 of adjusted EPS (~5% of the $1.75 midpoint) and returns through depreciation as the build program matures.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $408.4M (+7.6%) against $523.3M of capex and $200.2M of sale-leaseback proceeds, yielding company-defined free cash flow of $85.3M (from $153.8M) and conventional FCF of -$114.9M. Life Time's own FCF definition adds back sale-leaseback and land-sale proceeds; it should not be compared against peers' conventional FCF. FY2026 capex guidance sums to $1,165-1,210M (growth $885-910M, maintenance $140-150M, modernization/technology $140-150M) against ~$400M of planned sale-leasebacks. Management does not guide operating cash flow. Applying the FY2025 half-year split suggests a defensible $880-940M band, giving conventional FCF of roughly -$250M to -$310M, before noting that cash income taxes were raised to $103-105M from $80-83M on sale-leaseback gains, and that seven of fourteen 2026 openings land in Q4 with their pre-opening costs.

A more useful lens for the existing estate: ~$910M of operating cash flow less maintenance and modernization capex of ~$290M gives roughly $620M, a 6.5% yield on market capitalization. That represents what the mature portfolio produces, and it is also the figure that only matters if you treat growth capex as discretionary, which management does not.

**Balance sheet (June 30, 2026).** Total assets $8,358.2M; liabilities $5,056.0M; equity $3,302.2M. Cash $223.6M plus $34.6M restricted. Total debt $1,497.8M, net debt $1,290.2M, net debt/TTM adjusted EBITDA 1.4x (from 1.8x). Liquidity $855.7M including $632.1M undrawn on the revolver. Goodwill $1,235.4M and intangibles $180.7M, unchanged and unimpaired, leaving tangible book equity near $1,886M.

The lease book is where the leverage sits. Operating lease liabilities of $2,765.5M against $2,596.4M of right-of-use assets. Adding leases to net debt gives $4,056M, or 4.3x 2026E adjusted EBITDA; on an EBITDAR basis (adding back ~$381M of guided rent) the multiple is 10.3x EV including leases. Neither framing is alarming, but the 1.4x headline understates the fixed-charge burden of the model.

A standardized screen returned an Altman Z of 1.47 on FY2025 data. Recomputing on June 2026 balances and current market cap gives approximately 1.76. The drivers are structural rather than solvency-related: retained earnings only turned positive in H1 2026 (-$46.9M to +$142.6M) after 2020-2021 losses and the pre-IPO recapitalization; asset turnover of 0.38 reflects $2.6bn of ROU assets and $3.8bn of PP&E against ~$3.2bn of revenue; working capital is structurally negative (-$227.5M) because subscription revenue is collected in advance with essentially no receivables ($26.3M against $866M of quarterly revenue). Against 1.4x net leverage, $855.7M of liquidity and two 2026 upgrades, the screen is not measuring distress. It is correctly flagging low asset turnover and negative conventional free cash flow, the same features that make the company dependent on continued net-lease market access.

## Growth Analysis

| | Jun-2025 | Jun-2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Center memberships | 849,643 | 860,041 | +1.2% |
| Total centers | 184 | 195 | +6.0% |
| Quarterly revenue / center membership | $888 | $993 | +11.8% |
| Memberships per center (derived) | 4,618 | 4,410 | -4.5% |

The same pattern held annually: memberships +1.3% (812,062 to 822,380) against club count +5.6% (179 to 189), with memberships per club falling ~4.1%. Annual revenue per center membership rose $2,810 → $3,160 → $3,531, roughly 12% per year.

Management's account is checkable and largely holds: family memberships are ~60% of the base at roughly 1.83 individual members each; visits per membership rose from ~140 to ~148 on 122M total visits; and the company is deliberately shrinking qualified memberships administered through medical insurers, "which have significantly lower average dues." Engagement is rising, which is inconsistent with quiet member attrition masked by price.

The forward question is what happens when the mix tailwind exhausts. At $3,531 of annual revenue per membership growing ~12%, the model requires sustained pricing power on an increasingly full-price base. Several qualified-membership contracts expire in 2026 — costless while management is choosing to shed that volume, but asymmetric if a slow-ramping large-format club later needs volume and the channel is gone. Comparable center revenue growth is guided to 7.9-8.3% for 2026, having been raised from 6.9-7.5% in May, so near-term momentum is better than management itself expected three months ago. The multi-year direction is nonetheless down.

Unit growth is stepping up from ~10 net openings to 14, with the 2026 class at ~1.3M square feet, nearly double each of the 2024 and 2025 classes, mostly large-format ground-up construction. That is the growth engine and also the execution risk: longer lead times, permitting, construction-cost and tariff exposure, and a three-to-four-year ramp per site.

**Second-half guidance embeds a real slowdown.** Against reported H1 results, guidance midpoints imply H2 revenue of $1,707.8M (+11.8% versus H1's +12.8%), H2 adjusted EBITDA of $474.3M (+12.2% versus +17.5%), and H2 adjusted EPS of roughly $0.84 (+14.5% versus +28.2%). Much of this is mechanical: seven of fourteen openings in Q4 carrying pre-opening expense (already +69% in H1 to $4.1M), rent, D&A and payroll ahead of revenue, with guided FY D&A of $337-345M against $164.0M in H1 confirming the step-up. Management has beaten and raised twice, so the bar may be beatable. But at 24.6x forward adjusted earnings, the guided deceleration is the near-term risk.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year consensus $1.74 (+2.5% over 90 days), next year $2.03 (+3.9%). This is a steady grind higher consistent with a company beating conservative guidance, not the sharp revision velocity that reliably precedes further re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

Working from the frozen $42.97 basis and 223,217k shares outstanding at June 30:

- Market cap $9,591M; plus net debt $1,290M = EV ex-leases $10,881M
- **EV / 2026E adjusted EBITDA ($947.5M) = 11.5x**
- **Forward adjusted P/E = $42.97 / $1.75 = 24.6x**; on next-year consensus $2.03, **21.2x**
- **Forward GAAP P/E = $42.97 / $1.58 = 27.2x**, on GAAP earnings guided down 3.5%
- Owner-earnings proxy (OCF less maintenance/modernization capex) ≈ 6.5% yield
- Lease-inclusive EV / EBITDAR ≈ 10.3x

Peer comparison is of limited use here. The stated Leisure Facilities median P/E of 33.2 is set by OneSpaWorld; Planet Fitness trades at 17.0x on a fundamentally different unit model (low-hundreds of dollars of annual revenue per member versus Life Time's $3,531), Six Flags is loss-making and United Parks has no meaningful multiple. Life Time occupies a price tier with no scaled listed comparable. The more informative anchors are its own history and its growth-adjusted multiple: PEG on next-year consensus growth of 16.7% is approximately 1.27; on current-year adjusted growth of ~21%, approximately 1.18. Neither is stretched.

What tempers that is the quality of the earnings being capitalized. Adjusted net income excludes $54-58M of guided share-based compensation, a real cost that lifted basic weighted-average shares 3.1% year-over-year (215.6M to 222.2M). Roughly $0.09 of adjusted EPS comes from capitalized construction interest that reverses through depreciation. And conventional free cash flow is negative, so there is no cash-yield floor under the multiple; the 6.5% owner-earnings yield only exists if you assume away $885-910M of growth capex.

The re-rating path matters for calibration. Derived from the proxy TSR index (149.75 at FY2025 on an $18.00 IPO baseline, cross-checking against three other years and an externally reported ~$7.26bn mid-June market cap), the sequence is roughly $27 at year-end 2025 → ~$32.50 in mid-June → $42.97 now. Adjusted EPS is guided to grow ~21%; the share price is up ~60%. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, and the re-rating is defensible against two guidance raises, two credit upgrades, deleveraging and the removal of sponsor overhang. The multiple has done most of the work, so from here returns depend on earnings delivery rather than further re-rating.

**Scenario values.**
- **Base $47** — 23x next-year consensus $2.03, cross-checked at 11.5x EV/EBITDA on 2027E adjusted EBITDA of ~$1,060M less ~$1,400M net debt over ~229M shares. **+9.4%.**
- **Bull $57** — comparable center revenue holds near 8%, 2026 class ramps ahead of the three-to-four-year schedule, adjusted EPS reaches ~$2.25 at a 25x multiple; equivalently ~13x EV/EBITDA. **+32.6%.**
- **Bear $32** — consumer discretionary weakness pulls comparable revenue toward 3-4%, adjusted EBITDA stalls near $980M, multiple compresses to ~9.5x EV/EBITDA (equivalently ~17x on $1.85 EPS). **-25.5%.**

Probability-weighting 50/25/25 gives roughly +6.5% expected return, approximately fair, and below the bar for staged accumulation.

Consensus sits well above this: mean target $54.43, median $53.50, range $39-64, with 14 covering analysts now showing two strong buys, twelve buys and zero holds after the last remaining hold was upgraded. Analyst targets are a reasonable floor when a stock looks cheap against improving estimates. Here the stock trades at 21-25x forward on 12-17% adjusted growth with negative conventional cash generation, and the gap between $54 and my $47 base is largely explained by whether one capitalizes adjusted EPS and company-defined free cash flow at face value or discounts for share-based compensation, capitalized interest and growth capex. I discount them.

## Competitive Landscape

The replication barrier is the strongest part of the story and it is quantifiable. A 2026-vintage club averages roughly 93,000 indoor square feet (1.3M sq ft across 14 openings) with pools, courts, spa, restaurant and childcare in affluent metro trade areas. Very few operators can underwrite, permit, build and staff that facility, and fewer can do fourteen a year. The 10-K also describes a "halo effect": landlords value Life Time as an anchor tenant, which converts into better lease terms and larger tenant-improvement allowances, extended further by Life Time Work and Life Time Living.

The competitive set the company names is broad — health clubs (Equinox, Bay Club, Invited, LA Fitness, 24 Hour), YMCAs, boutique and value studios, racquet centers, country clubs, residential and condominium amenity centers, digital fitness, home equipment, co-working providers and luxury apartments. Life Time competes for a share of affluent households' discretionary lifestyle budget rather than within a defined gym category. That breadth is a moat against direct replication and an exposure to substitution from a wide set of alternatives.

Where the disclosure falls short is unit economics. Management targets "in excess of 30%" average cash-on-cash return on $25-30M of net invested capital after ramp. A mature club at ~4,400 memberships × ~$3,531 generates roughly $15.5M of revenue (a large-format 2026 club might reach $18-20M), which would require $7.5-9.0M of annual club-level cash flow to clear 30% on $25-30M — roughly 45-55% of revenue. Against a company-wide adjusted EBITDA margin of 28.5% *after* corporate overhead but also *after* rent, a four-wall margin of that magnitude is difficult to reconcile. Two explanations are possible and I cannot distinguish them from the filings: the numerator may be measured before the rent created by the sale-leaseback while the denominator is net of the sale-leaseback proceeds, counting the same transaction twice; or large-format clubs may genuinely produce $20M+ on net invested capital nearer the low end, with landlord reimbursements doing more work than assumed. The 10-K does not define the numerator and no club-level P&L is disclosed. For a company deploying ~$900M of growth capex a year, this is the single most consequential unresolved item in the file. I present it as an inference, not as evidence the target is overstated.

Industry support for the demand thesis is real in the company's own metrics: 122M visits (+7%), Dynamic Personal Training sessions +18%, Dynamic Stretch +34%, pickleball participations 5.9M, MIORA scaling one to eight locations. Whether GLP-1 weight-loss drugs are a net tailwind (drug users seeking resistance training and body-composition support, which MIORA is positioned to serve) or a headwind (pharmacological substitution for fitness spend) is not addressed in any source reviewed. That question is open in both directions.

## Risk Assessment

**Master-lease cross-default concentration.** The FY2025 10-K discloses 55 properties held under 15 master leases with 14 lessors, averaging ~3.7 properties per lease. A default at one property can constitute default across the pool, and remedies typically cannot be cured property-by-property, converting idiosyncratic single-club underperformance into portfolio-level event risk. The exposure grows with every sale-leaseback. It is contingent on default, which is remote at 1.4x net leverage with $855.7M of liquidity, but the specific cross-default language and the size of the largest individual master lease are not disclosed.

**Fixed rent against cancellable revenue.** With ~71% of centers leased, $2,765.5M of lease liabilities and ~$378-384M of 2026 rent, operating leverage runs both ways. Memberships are month-to-month; leases run decades. Deferred revenue is only $58.0M — enrollment fees and prepaid dues, not a contracted backlog — and the company discloses no remaining performance obligations. Revenue visibility rests entirely on retention behavior.

**Consumer exposure through the affluent housing channel.** The member base is 73% homeowners with $160,000 median household income. A downturn in affluent discretionary spending works against a $3,531/year membership. Nothing in Q2 results shows this transmitting yet — comparable revenue guidance was raised, not cut — but the mechanism is direct and the sector has historically been a source of negative surprise. [unverified — the research brief contains no macro data on mortgage rates, application demand, or metro-level home-clearing prices]

**Capital-markets dependence.** A disruption that closed the net-lease bid would force either slower openings or revolver/debt funding. The H1 2026 vintage already sold below appraised value: five properties with $207.0M net book value for $201.0M gross proceeds against $209.8M estimated fair value, recognizing a $2.0M gain versus a $12.8M net gain across FY2025. Selling below appraisal in exchange for lower ongoing rent is economically rational, not distressed, but terms are tightening at the margin.

**Qualified-membership contract expirations.** Several third-party administrator arrangements expire in 2026. Non-renewal looks costless while management is shrinking the channel; it removes a demand cushion that cannot easily be rebuilt.

**Key-person dependence.** Bahram Akradi founded the company in 1992 and is Founder, Chairman and CEO; the 10-K names him specifically as a risk factor with no publicly identified successor. Two Item 5.02 8-Ks dated March 11 and May 20, 2026 could not be retrieved, so whether either relates to senior succession is unresolved. A CFO employment agreement for Erik Weaver became effective May 4, 2026, and a May 20 equity grant to Rachael A. Wagner suggests a new director was elected.

**Operational liability breadth.** Childcare, aquatics and swim lessons, medi-spa, physical therapy and chiropractic, lab testing at MIORA, and a member-management system capturing member photographs — a biometric-privacy exposure in states such as Illinois. Center operations expense grew 12.3% in Q2, roughly in line with revenue, so cost control has held, but with 33,000+ part-time employees across 31 minimum-wage regimes there is little slack.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 50.1% sits 13.5 points above 36.6% one-year realized volatility, at the 70th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (50.1% near versus 44.5% at six months) and a ±8.0% implied move on the August 21 tenor. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.2%. The structural read is a split: put/call open-interest ratio of 6.77 is very high, while put/call *volume* is 0.04, meaning essentially all recent flow is in calls. The most consistent interpretation is that a substantial protective put position is held in size while current trading interest is directional upside, with elevated near-term vol pricing event size rather than direction. For a prospective buyer, rich near-term volatility is unfavorable entry timing; for an existing holder, the cost of protection is elevated. This corroborates the fundamental read that the near-term distribution around Q3 results is wide.

Short interest is 6.2% of float at 3.3 days to cover, uncrowded, neither a squeeze setup nor a bear-consensus signal.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($57, +32.6%)

Life Time is the only scaled operator in a price tier with no listed competitor, and the barrier to replicating a 93,000-square-foot resort-format club in an affluent trade area is genuine. Engagement is rising: 122M visits (+7%), 148 visits per membership, personal training sessions +18%, stretch +34%. Management has beaten consensus four consecutive quarters (by 12.0%, 10.3%, 52.0% and 32.8%) and raised full-year guidance twice in 2026, including lifting comparable center revenue growth to 7.9-8.3% from 6.9-7.5%. The balance sheet has genuinely improved: net leverage 1.4x from 1.8x, $855.7M of liquidity with an undrawn revolver, two ratings upgrades to BB, and a retained-earnings deficit cleared. The unit pipeline steps from ten net openings to fourteen at nearly double the square footage, so if the 2026 class ramps to $18-20M of revenue per club, 2027-2028 earnings power is materially above consensus. At 11.5x EV/2026E EBITDA and a PEG near 1.2-1.3, the multiple does not require heroic assumptions, and $427M of remaining buyback authorization can retire ~4.4% of shares. Analyst consensus at $54.43 mean implies the sell side underwrites this path.

### Bear Case ($32, -25.5%)

Unit-level demand is flat. Center memberships grew 1.2% against 6.0% club growth, and memberships per club have fallen 4-5% annually for two years. The revenue engine is price and mix: revenue per membership up 11.8%, driven partly by deliberately purging low-dues insurance-subsidized members, a benefit that is arithmetically finite and whose contracts expire in 2026. Comparable center revenue growth has already halved from 15.3% to a guided 8.1%, and second-half guidance implies adjusted EBITDA growth of 12.2% against 17.5% in the first half. Conventional free cash flow is negative (-$115M in H1), capex is stepping up 31-36% to $1.17-1.21bn, and the funding relies on selling clubs into a lease book already at $2,765.5M with 55 properties exposed to master-lease cross-default. Two of the adjusted-EPS drivers — the $66M interest-expense decline and ~$0.09 of capitalized construction interest — are non-repeatable or deferred. The >30% cash-on-cash return claim cannot be reconciled with a 28.5% company-wide margin struck after rent, and no club-level P&L is disclosed. Incentive compensation keys off adjusted EBITDA and stock price with no return-on-capital or free-cash-flow metric, so management is paid on the size of the EBITDA base rather than the efficiency of the $900M/year deployed to build it. Executives sold in a cluster on July 31 at $44.80-45.09, above the current price. If affluent discretionary spending softens, revenue is cancellable in a month and rent is not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Base case $47 (+9.4%), bull $57 (+32.6%), bear $32 (-25.5%); probability-weighted 50/25/25 gives roughly +6.5% expected return over twelve months. That is approximately fair compensation and below the threshold for adding capital, particularly with near-term implied volatility rich to realized and the second half guided to decelerate. Existing holders have a coherent reason to stay; new capital has no urgency.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Center membership growth accelerating above 3% year-over-year for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating that growth is not exclusively price and mix. This is the single most important disconfirming datapoint for the bear case.
2. Disclosure that defines the numerator of the ">30% cash-on-cash return" target, or club-level economics for the 2023-2024 vintages that reconcile against a sub-30% company margin. Resolving this gap would justify a materially higher multiple on the growth program.
3. Price below approximately $36-37 on unchanged guidance — roughly 18x next-year consensus $2.03 and 10x EV/EBITDA — which would restore a 25%+ margin of safety to the $47 base.
4. Open-market insider purchases (as distinct from exercise-and-sell) by the CEO or CFO, or a buyback that reduces the share count net of option-exercise issuance.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Comparable center revenue growth falling below 5% in any quarter, or a downward revision to the 7.9-8.3% full-year range.
2. Sale-leaseback proceeds falling materially short of the ~$200M H2 target, or transactions executed at wider discounts to appraised value than the $201.0M/$209.8M H1 vintage, evidence the funding channel is narrowing.
3. Net leverage rising above the stated 2.00x ceiling, or a revolver draw to fund construction.
4. Center membership *declines* year-over-year, which would invalidate management's mix explanation.
5. A senior succession event involving Akradi without an identified internal successor.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October/early November) are the natural checkpoint; they test the guided H2 deceleration, the pace of the seven Q4 openings, the H2 sale-leaseback execution, and whether qualified-membership contract expirations show up in the membership count. The full-year print in February 2027 will provide the first clean read on the 2026 class ramp and on FY2027 capex intentions.

## Conclusion

Life Time is a well-run operator of a genuinely hard-to-replicate physical format, in the middle of a credible unit-growth program, with a balance sheet that has improved measurably over the last eighteen months. The Q2 result and the second guidance raise were good, and the four-quarter beat record supports confidence in near-term consensus.

The rating is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE for three specific reasons.
